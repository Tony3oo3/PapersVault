## Problem

When combining multiple LoRA adapters for joint content-style generation, unstructured low-rank weight updates cause undesired feature entanglement between content and style attributes. Existing solutions (ZipLoRA, B-LoRA, ComposLoRA) either depend on model-specific architectural analysis or require post-training coefficient optimization to achieve disentanglement, leaving no unified, model-agnostic mechanism that learns orthogonal adaptation modules directly during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252596/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single content image and a single style image as visual references, combined with a text prompt in the template 'a <c> in <s> style' where <c> and <s> are trigger words for content and style respectively. Training uses one image per instance with 500 steps at rank r=64, and the output is a single fused image that simultaneously preserves the content subject's identity and the style image's visual characteristics. The framework is demonstrated on SDXL, SD3, and FLUX.1-dev backbones without architecture-specific modifications.

## Method

QR-LoRA replaces the standard LoRA AB decomposition with a structured QR-based parameterization: the pretrained weight matrix is first decomposed via SVD to extract a rank-r core component, which is then QR-decomposed into a fixed orthogonal matrix Q and a fixed upper-triangular matrix R. During fine-tuning, both Q and R remain frozen while only a task-specific residual matrix ΔR (initialized to zero) is trained, halving the trainable parameters compared to conventional LoRA. The orthogonal Q basis naturally enforces feature independence across tasks, so content and style ΔR matrices exhibit near-zero cosine similarity (max < 0.2) without any explicit regularization. Merging is reduced to a simple weighted sum ΔR_cs = λ_c·ΔR_c + λ_s·ΔR_s, eliminating the need for post-training coefficient search or model-specific block selection.

## Results

On SDXL, QR-LoRA achieves the highest scores across all four similarity metrics: DINO-S 0.694, DINO-C 0.776, CLIP-S 0.707, and CLIP-C 0.709, outperforming ZipLoRA, B-LoRA, and StyleAligned. User study ratings reach 4.07/5 on SDXL, 4.14/5 on SD3, and 3.96/5 on FLUX.1-dev, consistently surpassing all baselines. On SD3 and FLUX.1-dev, QR-LoRA improves over the naive DreamBooth-LoRA merge baseline by 0.036–0.051 in DINO/CLIP similarity and by over 1.0 point in user ratings, confirming model-agnostic effectiveness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QR-LoRA outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric across all three backbone models. On SDXL, it leads ZipLoRA by +0.018 (DINO-S), +0.064 (DINO-C), +0.021 (CLIP-S), and +0.041 (CLIP-C), with a user-study margin of +0.94 over ZipLoRA and +0.40 over StyleAligned. Against B-LoRA, the gains are +0.005 (DINO-S), +0.036 (DINO-C), +0.038 (CLIP-S), and +0.063 (CLIP-C). On SD3 and FLUX.1-dev, QR-LoRA surpasses the naive DreamBooth-LoRA merge by 0.036–0.051 in similarity scores and by 1.08–1.21 points in user ratings, demonstrating that the QR-based orthogonal decomposition provides meaningful disentanglement benefits beyond simple weight addition regardless of the underlying diffusion architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

QR-LoRA builds directly on LoRA's low-rank adaptation paradigm but departs from the dominant 'train independent LoRAs, then merge' strategy (ZipLoRA, ComposLoRA, CMLoRA) by redesigning the parameterization from the ground up. It repurposes SVD-based initialization ideas from PISSA and SVDiff for core information extraction, then applies QR decomposition to construct a fixed orthogonal basis (Q) and upper-triangular base (R), training only a residual ΔR. Unlike B-LoRA, which exploits SDXL-specific architectural properties for style-content separation, QR-LoRA is explicitly model-agnostic, validated across SDXL, SD3, and FLUX.1-dev.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By halving trainable parameters relative to standard LoRA and enabling simple element-wise addition for multi-attribute merging (replacing complex coefficient optimization), QR-LoRA offers a practical, architecture-agnostic recipe for disentangled customization that could be adopted broadly in the PEFT-for-diffusion community. The initialization overhead (1–5 minutes for SVD+QR) is a one-time cost but may matter in large-scale or multi-LoRA pipelines. Open questions include whether the ΔR-only training strategy generalizes to tasks beyond binary content-style fusion (e.g., multi-concept or temporal/video customization) and whether the MoE-style extension to many ΔR matrices preserves the disentanglement guarantees at scale.
