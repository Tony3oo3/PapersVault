## Problem

Existing underwater NeRF methods assume a static, homogeneous illumination source (e.g., sunlight) and cannot handle the inhomogeneous, dynamic lighting produced by co-moving artificial lights on autonomous underwater vehicles operating in deep or turbid water. These co-moving lights interact with the scattering medium to create strong backscatter cones and extreme brightness variation across views, breaking photometric consistency and making standard NeRF reconstruction fail. No prior method jointly recovers a clean scene representation free of both illumination and medium effects without requiring radiometric calibration or explicit knowledge of light sources.

## Input / Output

The method consumes calibrated multi-view RAW images (captured with a GoPro Hero9 Black in a dome port housing) and camera poses from underwater SfM (Refractive COLMAP). Because artificial lighting in dark water produces extreme dynamic range and varying shutter speeds across views, inputs are processed in RAW with Bayer-pattern masking and normalized to a common exposure level following RawNeRF. The model outputs a novel-view-synthesis NeRF, a clean scene representation with both light and medium effects removed, a per-pixel medium backscatter field, and a surface illumination field that can be visualized independently.

## Method

The architecture combines three components: a global NeRF MLP (with multiresolution hash encoding and spherical-harmonics view direction) that predicts per-sample density and reflectance color in world coordinates; a local illumination field MLP that co-moves with the camera and takes the camera-frame position and surface normal (derived from the density gradient) as input to predict a per-channel light intensity factor α; and a volumetric medium rendering formulation with constant attenuation, backscatter, and medium color as single optimizable scalars. The key insight is that the 3D illumination pattern is fixed relative to the camera, so a local MLP can memorize the cumulative light distribution without parameterizing individual light sources, their poses, or intensity profiles. During rendering, α modulates both the object color and the medium backscatter term, and the full pipeline is trained end-to-end with a RawNeRF-style stop-gradient loss on RAW data, requiring no calibration of lights or medium parameters.

## Results

The paper is primarily qualitative, with visual comparisons in Figure 4 across five datasets (Four Lights, Color Checker, Tank, DarkGS, Beyond Nerf: Underwater) showing that the proposed method successfully removes both co-moving light and medium effects to recover a clean scene representation. The only quantitative metric is a color-checker ablation (Table 1) measuring L² norm in linear color space (0–255, lower is better): the 3-channel illumination field achieves 11.879 on synthetic medium data versus 12.780 for the 1-channel variant, while the 1-channel variant is slightly better in-air (9.554 vs. 10.143). On the real medium dataset, the difference is negligible (28.944 vs. 30.077), which the authors attribute to calibration and pose noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper explicitly notes that 'there are virtually no competitor methods to compare against' for this largely unsolved problem, so all comparisons in Figure 4 are qualitative. DarkGS [33] requires a human-in-the-loop light calibration on a calibration board and models only a single known light source, whereas the proposed method trains directly on the provided data with no calibration and handles arbitrary numbers of lights. SeaThru-NeRF [9] achieves partial color restoration but produces heavy artifacts because it cannot model the co-moving light cone, leaving unlit regions unrecoverable. Beyond Nerf: Underwater [32] assumes a single ideal point light at the camera center, a restriction the proposed method drops entirely. In all visual comparisons, the proposed method produces the cleanest scene reconstruction with the fewest artifacts, while the baselines show visible light-cone patterns, color casts, or dark regions corresponding to areas never illuminated.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: underwater NeRF reconstruction (SeaThru-NeRF, Tang et al.) which assumes a distant, homogeneous light source, and co-moving-light NeRF (DarkGS) which handles unknown lights but only in air. It departs from both by introducing a non-parametric, camera-attached illumination field that jointly models arbitrary co-moving light configurations and a scattering medium without any radiometric calibration, building on the volumetric rendering formulation of SeaThru-NeRF and the RAW-data pipeline of RawNeRF. The closest prior work, Beyond NeRF Underwater [32], restricts to a single ideal point light at the camera center, whereas this paper removes that constraint entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first calibration-free approach to recover a clean scene representation from underwater captures with unknown, spatially distributed co-moving lights, which is the dominant imaging scenario for AUVs operating below the photic zone. The paper honestly identifies key limitations: shadows from co-moving lights are not modeled, and the method requires sufficient variation in camera-to-scene distance and pose diversity to disentangle medium attenuation from object color and illumination from reflectance. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to dynamic scenes, whether the illumination field can be extended to handle occlusion-based shadows, and whether the single-parameter medium model suffices for strongly inhomogeneous water columns.
