## Problem

Content-style decomposition (CSD) from a single image enables recontextualization and stylization, but existing CSD methods (B-LoRA, UnZipLoRA) are designed exclusively for diffusion models, leaving autoregressive (VAR) models unexplored for this task. Additionally, no publicly available benchmark dataset with quantifiable metrics exists for evaluating CSD performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250507/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and a structured text prompt of the form 'A photo of a <content> object in <style> style' as input. It produces two independently usable representations — a content embedding and a style embedding — which at inference time are combined with new prompts to generate recontextualized images (preserving subject identity in new environments) or stylized images (applying the extracted style to new subjects). The underlying VAR backbone (Switti or Infinity) operates on VQ-VAE token maps across 10 scales from 1×1 up to 10×10.

## Method

CSD-VAR extends textual inversion on pretrained VAR models with three key mechanisms. First, a scale-aware alternating optimization strategy assigns style loss to scales {1,2,3,10} and content loss to scales {4,…,9} (determined empirically via DINO/CSD-S ablation), optimizing the two embeddings in separate iterations to prevent gradient mixing. Second, an SVD-based rectification step constructs a content subspace from LLM-generated subconcepts embedded via CLIP, then projects the style embedding onto this subspace and subtracts the projection to enforce orthogonality and reduce content leakage. Third, augmented Key-Value memory pairs are prepended to the self-attention layers at specific scales (scale 1 for style, scale 4 for content) to capture information that textual embeddings alone cannot represent. All components are trained with Adam at lr=1e-3 for 200 steps on a single A100, with no modifications to the pretrained VAR weights.

## Results

On the CSD-100 dataset, CSD-VAR(Infinity) achieves the highest content alignment scores (CSD-C 0.660, CLIP-I 0.795), while CSD-VAR(Switti) leads in style alignment (CSD-S 0.564, DINO 0.521) and text alignment (CLIP-T 0.332). Both VAR backbones outperform all baselines across every reported metric. The user study with 100 participants (7,500 responses) confirms these quantitative gains, with CSD-VAR receiving the highest preference in content alignment, style alignment, and prompt adherence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSD-VAR outperforms all three baselines on every metric where both methods report scores. Against B-LoRA [8], CSD-VAR(Switti) improves CSD-C by 0.080 (0.603 vs 0.523), CLIP-I by 0.162 (0.754 vs 0.592), CSD-S by 0.088 (0.564 vs 0.476), DINO by 0.175 (0.521 vs 0.346), and CLIP-T by 0.054 (0.332 vs 0.278). Against Inspiration Tree [50], the margins are even larger: CSD-C +0.106, CLIP-I +0.179, CSD-S +0.160, DINO +0.168, CLIP-T +0.075. DreamBooth [38] is evaluated separately for content (DreamBooth-C) and style (DreamBooth-S); CSD-VAR(Switti) beats DreamBooth-C on CSD-C (0.603 vs 0.594) and CLIP-I (0.754 vs 0.721), while CSD-VAR(Switti) beats DreamBooth-S on CSD-S (0.564 vs 0.537) and CLIP-T (0.332 vs 0.289). Notably, DreamBooth exhibits overfitting (lower text alignment), and B-LoRA/Inspiration Tree suffer from content leakage in style outputs, which CSD-VAR mitigates via SVD-based rectification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the content-style decomposition (CSD) task—previously explored only in diffusion-model settings via methods like UnZipLoRA and B-LoRA—to the Visual Autoregressive (VAR) framework, which generates images via next-scale prediction rather than iterative denoising. It builds directly on textual inversion [9] as the optimization backbone and on VAR's multi-scale token prediction paradigm [47], introducing a scale-aware alternating optimization strategy grounded in the empirical observation that early scales encode style while later scales encode content. The SVD-based rectification and augmented K-V memory are novel additions that have no direct counterpart in prior diffusion-based CSD work, repurposing subspace projection and prefix-style conditioning to address content leakage and identity preservation within the autoregressive setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that VAR's hierarchical scale structure can be exploited for content-style disentanglement, this work opens a path toward CSD in a model family that is increasingly competitive with diffusion models in both quality and efficiency. The introduction of CSD-100, the first dedicated benchmark for this task, addresses a clear gap in the literature and provides a standardized evaluation protocol. However, the dataset is small (100 images), training is limited to 200 steps on a single GPU, and the authors acknowledge struggles with intricate subject details—suggesting the method's robustness at scale remains an open question. Future work could explore whether the scale-content/style correspondence generalizes across VAR architectures and whether CSD-100 can be expanded into a training corpus for learned decomposition.
