## Problem

Visual Place Recognition (VPR) systems can be exploited to track individuals' daily movements from social media images, yet no prior work has specifically addressed privacy protection for VPR. Existing adversarial perturbation methods fail to simultaneously satisfy the three critical requirements of real-world VPR privacy: black-box transferability (adversary models are unknown), visual imperceptibility, and near-real-time performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252653/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (the user's intended post photo) and a set of positive reference images retrieved from a VPR database. The input image is resized to match the fine-tuned ViT's input size for saliency prior extraction. The output is a protected image where perturbations are spatially concentrated in saliency-critical regions and spectrally restricted to high-frequency components (via 8×8 DCT blocks with a frequency threshold of 12), ensuring the result is visually indistinguishable from the original while disrupting VPR feature matching.

## Method

VPR-Cloak introduces a saliency-aware prior generated by a fine-tuned DINOv2-based ViT (using adapter strategy) that identifies decisive regions for place recognition. The core optimization, SAP-PO, operates in two stages: first, spatial-domain perturbation optimization maximizes feature distance between the perturbed image and positive reference samples using a randomly selected surrogate VPR model from a pool (ensuring black-box transferability); second, frequency-domain optimization applies DCT to isolate and refine only high-frequency perturbation components while preserving low-frequency structures essential for human perception. The total loss balances a normalized L2 feature distance term (privacy) against a PSNR term (visual fidelity), and at each iteration the perturbation is clipped to saliency-aware bounds (±P·ε) to concentrate modifications in critical regions. This two-domain strategy achieves a 15× runtime speedup over MultiANDA while outperforming it in protection success rate across all evaluated VPR models.

## Results

On the Nordland dataset (most challenging, with seasonal variation), VPR-Cloak (Ours_Crica) achieves 87.9% ΔR@1, 84.8% ΔR@5, and 79.2% ΔR@10 against the Crica VPR model, substantially outperforming ANDA (63.1/61.3/51.0) and MultiANDA (66.0/62.3/52.3). Averaged across all models on Nordland, VPR-Cloak reaches 70.9% ΔR@1, 77.0% ΔR@5, and 76.0% ΔR@10, compared to 55.0/59.1/49.2 for ANDA and 59.5/64.7/56.5 for MultiANDA. In terms of runtime, VPR-Cloak requires only 0.036 s per image, achieving a 4.7× speedup over ANDA (0.169 s) and a 15× speedup over MultiANDA (0.551 s). Image quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) also favor VPR-Cloak, e.g., on Pitts30k Ours_Sela achieves PSNR 32.00 and LPIPS 0.082 versus 29.84 and 0.144 for ANDA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline family is ANDA / MultiANDA (Fang et al., CVPR 2024), which approximates the posterior distribution of adversarial perturbations via asymptotic normality of stochastic gradient ascent, with MultiANDA adding a deep-ensemble strategy for improved transferability. VPR-Cloak (Ours_Crica) wins decisively on protection success rate across all five datasets: for example, on Tokyo247 it achieves 82.2% ΔR@1 versus 49.8% (ANDA) and 52.0% (MultiANDA), a gap of roughly 30 percentage points. On Pitts30k the advantage is 69.5% vs. 50.8%/52.6%, and on Nordland 87.9% vs. 63.1%/66.0%. The cross-surrogate variant Ours_Sela also outperforms both baselines on most model–dataset pairs, confirming strong black-box transferability (e.g., 73.2% ΔR@1 on Pitts30k-Sela vs. 17.7% for ANDA). In runtime, VPR-Cloak is 4.7× faster than ANDA and 15× faster than MultiANDA (0.036 s vs. 0.169 s and 0.551 s), making it the only method suitable for near-real-time deployment. Image quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, Avg Epsilon) are also consistently better for VPR-Cloak, indicating that the larger protection gains come without sacrificing visual fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by framing privacy protection specifically for Visual Place Recognition, a task the authors argue has no prior dedicated work. Methodologically, it repurposes several existing ideas: the saliency-aware prior draws on DINOv2 with adapter-based fine-tuning (following Houlsby et al. and Sela), the frequency-domain optimization leverages the Fourier-robustness insight from Yin et al., and the gradient-based perturbation loop is a standard projected-gradient-descent variant. The key departure from prior adversarial-privacy work (ANDA, MultiANDA, SparseFool, PGD_0) is the joint use of a spatial saliency mask to concentrate perturbations on VPR-critical regions and a DCT-based high-frequency refinement to preserve human-perceptible low-frequency structure, addressing transferability, imperceptibility, and speed simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By being the first to formalize and solve VPR-specific location privacy, this work opens a practical defense avenue for social-media users whose geolocatable photos can be crawled and matched by commercial or open-source VPR systems. The validation on Google and Microsoft Bing APIs demonstrates that the protection generalizes beyond academic benchmarks to real deployment targets, and the 15× runtime advantage over MultiANDA makes near-real-time use feasible. Open questions include whether the saliency prior (which requires fine-tuning DINOv2 per VPR model family) can be made fully model-agnostic, how the approach extends to video- or 3D-based place recognition, and whether the limited surrogate-model pool (five open-source VPRs) adequately represents the diversity of future black-box systems.
