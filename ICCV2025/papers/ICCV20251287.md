## Problem

Existing encoder-decoder motion forecasting models process historical and future trajectories with separate modules, causing the decoder to receive outdated agent information and inconsistent feature representations when predicting far-future trajectories. This limits accuracy at longer prediction horizons, particularly for endpoint predictions. The paper asks whether a unified, decoder-only autoregressive model that treats historical and future steps identically can overcome these limitations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251287/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 5 seconds of historical agent trajectories (50 steps at 10 Hz) with position, velocity, and motion vectors, plus vectorized road geometry encoded as polylines with semantic categories and inter-lane relations. It produces K=6 future trajectories spanning 6 seconds (60 steps), each with (x, y) position and heading at every time step, along with a probability distribution over modes. Positions are parametrized as a mixture of Laplace distributions and headings as von-Mises distributions, and all spatial reasoning uses a query-centric relative coordinate frame anchored to the current sub-trajectory endpoint.

## Method

DONUT replaces the encoder-decoder split with a single autoregressive decoder that processes trajectories in sub-trajectory chunks of 10 time steps (1 s). At each step, a proposer module predicts the next sub-trajectory and an additional overprediction (the sub-trajectory after that), inspired by multi-token prediction in LLMs; a refiner module then corrects both using an updated reference point at the predicted endpoint. The factorized attention stack—temporal self-attention over prior sub-trajectory tokens, cross-attention to nearby map tokens, social attention to other agents, and mode attention across K hypotheses—ensures the model always conditions on up-to-date scene context. Overpredictions are supervised during training but discarded at inference, encouraging the model to anticipate longer horizons. The reference coordinate frame is re-anchored to each predicted endpoint before the next decoder step, enabling consistent relative encodings throughout the unrolling process.

## Results

On the Argoverse 2 single-agent forecasting benchmark (test set), DONUT achieves a b-minFDE₆ of 1.79, setting a new state-of-the-art among non-ensemble methods and outperforming the previous best non-ensemble method DeMo (1.84) by 0.05. DONUT also achieves the best non-ensemble minFDE₆ of 1.16 (vs. DeMo's 1.17) and a competitive minADE₆ of 0.63. The ablation study (Table 1) shows that the decoder-only architecture alone improves b-minFDE₆ from 1.874 (QCNet baseline) to 1.838, and adding overprediction plus refinement further reduces it to 1.807 on the validation set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DONUT outperforms all 11 non-ensemble baselines on the primary b-minFDE₆ metric, achieving 1.79 versus DeMo's 1.84 (the previous best non-ensemble) and QCNet's 1.91 (its direct encoder-decoder baseline). It also wins on minFDE₆ (1.16 vs. DeMo's 1.17) and MR₁ (0.54, tied with SEPT*), while DeMo retains a slight edge on minADE₆ (0.61 vs. 0.63) and MR₆ (0.13 vs. 0.14). Among ensemble methods, DONUT's b-minFDE₆ of 1.79 is competitive with QCNet* (1.78) but trails SEPT* (1.74) and DeMo* (1.73). On unimodal K=1 metrics, DeMo substantially outperforms DONUT on minFDE₁ (3.74 vs. 4.06) and minADE₁ (1.49 vs. 1.66), which the authors attribute to the inherent bias of unimodal metrics toward mean predictions. The ablation confirms that the decoder-only architecture alone (without overprediction or refinement) already improves b-minFDE₆ from 1.874 to 1.838 over the QCNet baseline, with the full model reaching 1.807 on validation.

## Relation to Prior Work

DONUT departs from the dominant encoder-decoder architecture in motion forecasting (exemplified by QCNet, MTR, and DeMo) by adopting a unified, autoregressive decoder-only model inspired by the success of LLMs. It builds directly on QCNet's query-centric scene encoding and map encoder, but replaces the separate agent encoder and recurrent decoder with a single module that processes historical and future sub-trajectories identically. The overprediction strategy is a direct adaptation of multi-token prediction from LLM training (Gloeckle et al., ICML 2024), repurposed to encourage the model to anticipate longer temporal horizons during trajectory forecasting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoder-only framing offers a cleaner conceptual foundation for trajectory prediction, eliminating the train/inference inconsistency of recurrent decoders that switch between learned embeddings and output embeddings. The finding that overprediction and refinement are individually ineffective but synergistic is practically useful for practitioners designing auxiliary objectives. However, the evaluation is limited to the Argoverse 2 single-agent benchmark, and the minADE6 metric is slightly worse than the encoder-decoder baseline, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to multi-agent prediction, other datasets, or scenarios requiring higher spatial granularity. The paper also does not explore whether the decoder-only advantage persists at larger model scales or with different sub-trajectory lengths.
