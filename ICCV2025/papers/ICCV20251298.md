## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models frequently fail at concept composition, producing missing objects, attribute leakage, and concept entanglement. Prior analyses relied on synthetic data or class-to-image settings, leaving the causal factors for poor concept responses in large-scale T2I tasks under-explored. This paper demonstrates through controlled experiments that once model size and dataset scale reach sufficient thresholds, data distribution imbalance becomes the dominant factor limiting concept composition ability.

## Input / Output

The method operates within a standard DiT-based diffusion pipeline: text prompts (average ~100 words) are encoded by a T5 text encoder and injected as conditioning, while 512×512 RGB images are encoded into latent space via the Stable Diffusion VAE. The IMBA loss is computed at the token/region level within the latent representation, allowing different spatial regions of the same image to receive different reweighting factors based on the concepts they contain. Evaluation uses VQA-based success rate (via GPT-4V) in addition to CLIP Score across three benchmarks.

## Method

The core contribution is the IMBA loss, an online concept-wise equalization loss that dynamically reweights the diffusion training objective. The key insight is that the IMBA distance — the L-γ norm of the difference between the ground-truth noise and the unconditional diffusion prediction (‖ε − ε_θ(x_t, φ, t)‖) — is theoretically proportional to 1/φ(c_i), the inverse frequency of the concept, making it a self-calibrating proxy for data imbalance without any offline statistics. During training, this distance (computed with stop-gradient) multiplies the standard conditional diffusion loss, effectively upweighting rare concepts and downweighting frequent ones at the region level. The final loss combines the reweighted conditional loss with the unconditional loss using a fixed ratio λ=0.9, replacing the original random condition-dropout. The approach requires only a few lines of code modification to the loss function, is compatible across diffusion model variants (DDPM, flow matching), and is orthogonal to training-free inference methods.

## Results

On LC-Mis, the proposed IMBA loss (trained from scratch) achieves 62.89% VQA success rate, a 16.7-point improvement over the 46.21% baseline, and 0.3121 CLIP score. On the newly introduced Inert-CompBench, IMBA loss reaches 57% VQA versus 44% for the baseline (a 13-point gain). On T2I-CompBench, the method leads on all sub-metrics: Color (0.7067), Shape (0.5151), Texture (0.6861), and Spatial (0.2518), compared to the baseline's 0.5812, 0.4307, 0.6188, and 0.1966 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against the baseline (standard diffusion loss), the training-free A&E method [5], and a fine-tuning variant of IMBA loss. IMBA loss from scratch decisively outperforms A&E on all VQA-based metrics: 62.89% vs 48.42% on LC-Mis and 57% vs 44.5% on Inert-CompBench, while A&E edges out IMBA only on LC-Mis CLIP score (0.3198 vs 0.3121). The authors attribute A&E's CLIP advantage to its attention-based guidance boosting overall image-text alignment, but note A&E cannot generate concepts the base model has never learned, making it far weaker on attribute leakage (Shape, Color, Texture). Fine-tuning with IMBA loss (3+1 epochs) also improves over the baseline (51.82% vs 46.21% LC-Mis VQA) but lags behind from-scratch training (62.89%), suggesting inert concepts require longer exposure to the balanced loss. On T2I-CompBench, IMBA loss leads every sub-metric over both A&E and the baseline, with the largest gap on Spatial composition (0.2518 vs 0.1998 for A&E).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: (1) synthetic studies of concept composition in diffusion models (Okawa et al., Wiedemer et al., Chang et al.), which used simple shapes and class-conditioned settings, and (2) long-tail data balancing methods from classification (Cui et al., Park et al.), which rely on offline frequency-based reweighting. The key departure is moving from sample-level or class-level reweighting to an online, concept-region-level reweighting derived from the model's own conditional-unconditional prediction gap (IMBA distance), making it applicable to multi-concept text prompts where no single class label exists. Unlike training-free inference-time methods (Attend-and-Excite, structured diffusion guidance), IMBA loss operates at training time and is not bounded by the base model's representational capacity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that data distribution balance—not dataset scale or model size beyond a threshold—is the dominant factor for concept composition ability is practically significant for anyone training large-scale T2I models, as it redirects effort from data collection to distribution management. The IMBA loss's extreme simplicity (a few lines modifying the loss function, no offline preprocessing) makes it immediately adoptable across diffusion architectures. However, the causal analysis relies on controlled experiments with specific non-co-occurring concept pairs and a single DiT architecture at relatively modest scales (100M–1B), leaving open whether the conclusions hold for production-scale models (10B+), autoregressive generators, or video/3D generation. The limited improvement from fine-tuning on Inert-CompBench also suggests that truly inert concepts may require fundamentally different interventions beyond reweighting.
