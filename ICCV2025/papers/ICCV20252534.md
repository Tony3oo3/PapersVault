## Problem

Autoregressive visual generation inherits next-token prediction from NLP, but the optimal prediction unit for 2D images is unclear—individual patches lack the semantic coherence of words, and the quantization process discards fine detail. Additionally, standard AR training relies on teacher forcing, which exposes the model only to ground-truth contexts and causes cascading errors at inference (exposure bias).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252534/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label (ImageNet) and Gaussian noise as input and produces a 256×256 or 512×512 RGB image. Internally, the image is encoded by a continuous VAE (downsampling factor f=16) into a 16×16×4 latent, which is partitioned into prediction entities—by default 8×8 cells on a 2×2 grid. At inference, generation proceeds autoregressively: each cell is synthesized from a fresh Gaussian noise sample via flow matching, conditioned on all previously generated clean cells.

## Method

xAR generalizes next-token prediction to next-X prediction, where X is a flexible entity (individual token, k×k cell, non-local subsample, coarse-to-fine scale, or entire image) and the prediction objective is continuous entity regression via flow matching rather than discrete token classification. The key training innovation is Noisy Context Learning (NCL): instead of conditioning on ground-truth preceding entities (teacher forcing), the model is trained to predict each entity X_n by regressing the flow-matching velocity, conditioned on all previous entities corrupted with independently sampled noise time steps t_i ∈ [0,1]. This exposes the model to imperfect contexts during training, aligning the training distribution with inference and mitigating exposure bias. The default configuration uses 8×8 cells (capturing both local structure and regional semantics) with unconstrained random noise schedules, and the architecture is a Transformer operating on the continuous VAE latent without any VQ-VAE quantization.

## Results

On ImageNet-256, xAR-H (1.1B) achieves a state-of-the-art FID of 1.24 with IS 301.6, Precision 0.83, and Recall 0.64, outperforming the previous best (REPA†, FID 1.42) by 0.18 FID without relying on DINOv2 or guidance interval sampling. The lightweight xAR-B (172M) achieves FID 1.72, surpassing DiT-XL/2 (675M, FID 2.27) by 0.55 FID while running 20× faster (9.8 vs. 0.5 images/sec). On ImageNet-512, xAR-L (608M) sets a new SOTA FID of 1.70, beating DiT-XL/2 (3.04) by 1.34 FID and VAR-d36 (2.63) by 0.93 FID.

## Baselines & Comparisons

xAR-H (1.1B) achieves the best FID (1.24) among all compared methods on ImageNet-256, beating the previous SOTA REPA† (1.42, which uses DINOv2 + guidance interval sampling) by 0.18 FID, RAR-XXL (1.48) by 0.24 FID, MaskBit (1.52) by 0.28 FID, and MAR-H (1.55) by 0.31 FID. The efficiency advantage is striking: xAR-B (172M) outperforms DiT-XL/2 (675M) by 0.55 FID while being 20× faster at inference (9.8 vs. 0.5 img/s), and xAR-H runs 2.2× faster than REPA†. On ImageNet-512, xAR-L (608M) achieves FID 1.70, surpassing DiT-XL/2 (3.04) by 1.34 FID, DiMR-XL/3R (2.89) by 1.19 FID, VAR-d36 (2.63) by 0.93 FID, and REPA† (2.08) by 0.38 FID. Notably, xAR does not require vision foundation models (DINOv2) or advanced sampling strategies (guidance interval sampling), both of which REPA† depends on. In ablation comparisons (Table 3), xAR-B with cell prediction (FID 2.48) outperforms LlamaGen-L with token prediction (3.80), PAR-L with subsample prediction (3.76), DiT-L/2 with image prediction (5.02), and VAR-d16 with scale prediction (3.30), all with fewer parameters (172M vs. 310–458M).

## Relation to Prior Work

xAR unifies and extends several prior autoregressive visual generation paradigms—vanilla next-token AR (VQGAN, LlamaGen), next-scale prediction (VAR), and flow-matching-based generation (SiT, MAR)—under a single 'next-X' framework where X is a flexible prediction entity. It builds directly on MAR's use of continuous VAE latents (avoiding VQ-VAE quantization) and flow matching as a regression objective, but departs from both by (1) introducing cell and subsample groupings as new prediction entities and (2) replacing teacher forcing with Noisy Context Learning, which conditions each AR step on noisy rather than clean preceding entities. Conceptually, xAR subsumes flow matching (X = entire image) and VAR (X = scale) as special cases while adding the cell entity that empirically outperforms all alternatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: xAR-B (172M) outperforms DiT-XL (675M) by 0.55 FID while running 20× faster, and xAR-H achieves a new SOTA FID of 1.24 on ImageNet-256 without relying on vision foundation models (DINOv2) or guidance interval sampling—both of which the previous best (REPA) required. The Noisy Context Learning strategy offers a general, principled remedy for exposure bias in any autoregressive generative model, potentially transferable to video, text-to-image, or other sequential generation tasks. Open questions include whether the cell-prediction advantage persists at higher resolutions or in conditional (text-guided) settings, and whether NCL can be combined with other AR acceleration techniques like parallel decoding.
