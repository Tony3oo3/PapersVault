## Problem

The cosine-based softmax loss is widely used for discriminative feature embedding, but its temperature parameter—which scales the bounded cosine similarities into meaningful logits—must be manually tuned in an ad-hoc, trial-and-error manner. No principled, task-agnostic method exists to automatically determine the proper temperature during training, and the optimal value varies across datasets, class counts, and feature dimensions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and its class label (during training) and produces a class prediction along with a per-sample optimized temperature κ. Unlike a fixed hyperparameter or a single learned scalar, κ is computed independently for each training sample via a greedy line search over 20 equally-spaced candidates in log scale on [e⁻², e⁵], then fed into the loss to drive back-propagation. The backbone CNN (e.g., ResNet-50) and linear classifier weights are updated end-to-end, while κ itself is not subject to gradient-based learning.

## Method

The core contribution is a least-squares geometric criterion for optimizing κ. The paper shows that the softmax coefficients solve an optimization problem (Eq. 9) analogous to projecting the L₂-normalized feature x̃ onto the convex hull spanned by the L₂-normalized classifier vectors {w̃_c} (Eq. 8). By substituting the softmax coefficients into the projection error, the authors obtain a closed-form criterion E_κ^LS (Eq. 10) that measures how well the softmax-weighted combination of classifiers approximates the feature; minimizing this over κ via simple line search yields κ*. This approach is robust to high feature dimensionality (unlike the von Mises-Fisher probabilistic alternatives) and requires no extra training procedure beyond standard back-propagation. The optimized κ* is shown to act as a lower bound of the feasible temperature range: smaller κ ≈ κ* produces compact intra-class features and high-confidence models useful for OOD/MISS detection, while larger κ > 2κ* yields diverse features beyond the classifier subspace that improve transfer-learning generalization.

## Results

On ImageNet (C=1000, ResNet-50), the proposed LS approach achieves 77.27% top-1 accuracy with an optimized κ*=12.83, slightly outperforming the standard softmax baseline (77.12%). The LS method substantially outperforms alternative temperature estimation strategies: rule-of-thumb (3.36%), maximum likelihood (0.10%), and end-to-end trainable κ (75.51%). For OOD/MISS detection, the optimized κ* yields AP scores of 0.9421 (z_max) and 0.9094 (‖x‖₂) for MISS and 0.9019/0.8564 for OOD, surpassing the softmax baseline (0.9092/0.7804 and 0.8673/0.7236). In transfer learning (linear probe on CUB-200), features trained with larger κ=60 reach 75.63%, significantly exceeding the softmax-pretrained baseline of 69.05%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The LS approach decisively outperforms the three alternative temperature estimation methods on ImageNet: rule-of-thumb collapses to 3.36% (κ*=65525, far too large due to high dimensionality d=2048), maximum likelihood yields only 0.10% (κ*=0.14, too small to produce discriminative features), and end-to-end trainable κ reaches 75.51% (κ*=120.8, too large at later epochs). Against the standard softmax baseline, the LS-optimized κ* achieves comparable or slightly better accuracy: +0.15% on ImageNet (77.27 vs 77.12), +0.24% on Food-101 (83.27 vs 83.03), but −0.07% on CIFAR-10 (95.49 vs 95.56) and −0.81% on CIFAR-100 (78.26 vs 79.07). The key advantage emerges in downstream utility: at κ*, the model achieves substantially better OOD/MISS detection AP (e.g., 0.9421 vs 0.9092 for z_max on MISS), while larger κ values (e.g., κ=60) produce features that transfer far better via linear probe (75.63% on CUB-200 vs 69.05% for softmax). For long-tailed classification, the LS-optimized κ* consistently outperforms softmax (e.g., 49.16% vs 48.51% on ImageNet-LT, 67.75% vs 66.76% on iNaturalist2018), with the gap widening for larger κ values that degrade tail-class performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the geometric connection between softmax and projection onto a convex hull of classifier vectors, drawing from sparse-representation classification [41], to formulate a least-squares criterion for temperature estimation in cosine-based softmax loss. It departs from prior work on cos-softmax temperature—which has been manually tuned in face recognition (ArcFace, CosFace) and analyzed in contrastive learning (uniformity, equilibrium)—by providing an automatic, per-sample estimation method grounded in supervised classification geometry. The probabilistic (von Mises-Fisher KDE) formulation is explored but shown to fail in high dimensions, positioning the geometric approach as the viable alternative. Unlike post-hoc calibration methods [1, 17], the temperature is optimized in-vivo during training without end-to-end gradient flow through κ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, low-overhead mechanism (20-candidate line search per sample) to replace ad-hoc temperature tuning in cos-softmax, and more importantly, a clear interpretive framework: κ* acts as a lower bound below which features become too compact (hurting discriminativity) and above which features gain diversity useful for transfer learning. The finding that larger κ dramatically improves linear-probe transfer (e.g., CUB-200: 69→76%) while smaller κ aids OOD/MISS detection gives practitioners a principled knob for trading off specialization vs. generalization. Limitations include evaluation restricted to ResNet CNNs, the per-sample line search adding modest overhead, and the 'cosify' experiment showing that retrofitting large-κ behavior onto a standard-softmax-pretrained model is harder than small-κ behavior, suggesting the classifier-subspace bias is not easily overcome.
