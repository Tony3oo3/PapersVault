## Problem

Smartphones typically carry only wide-angle lenses, yet telephoto shots produce a distinctive lens-compression effect (background enlargement, foreground diminishment, subject size preservation) that digital zoom cannot replicate. The problem of controlled, targeted lens compression and perspective adjustment for wide-angle-to-telephoto translation has not been explicitly addressed in the literature, with the closest prior work (Dolly-Zoom generation) offering no direct control over the amount of compression.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251880/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a single 512×512 RGB image captured with a wide-angle lens plus user-specified pixel coordinates (x, y) marking the subject whose apparent size must be preserved. It outputs a same-resolution telephoto-lens image where background content is enlarged and foreground content is diminished relative to the subject. The approach is focal-length-agnostic: a single 2× compression model can be applied iteratively to simulate 2^p× focal-length changes (p ∈ ℤ⁺).

## Method

Wide2Long decomposes lens compression into four sequential modules: (1) depth-based layering via MiDaS splits the image into 20 depth layers; (2) layer-wise inpainting using an FFC-based model fills occluded regions with composite context from all shallower layers; (3) inter-layer redundancy reduction applies SSIM-thresholded sigmoidal masks to suppress repeated inpainted content across adjacent layers; (4) layer scaling and flattening resizes each layer by ρ_n = e^{γ(n_f − n)} and superimposes them with the subject layer as pivot. Four pipeline parameters (γ, λ, T, α) are predicted by a small convolutional network (I2P) trained on paired wide-angle/telephoto images, making the compression amount data-driven rather than fixed. The key insight is that the exponential depth-dependent scaling factor, combined with learned inpainting and redundancy-removal parameters, faithfully emulates the optical ray geometry of moving the camera backward while increasing focal length.

## Results

On the W2L test dataset (100 image pairs, 2× focal length change), the Wide2Long pipeline achieves SSIM 0.768, PSNR 19.873 dB, and LPIPS 0.139, outperforming the Dolly-Zoom baseline of Shih et al. (SSIM 0.735, PSNR 17.682, LPIPS 0.147) and a U-Net baseline (SSIM 0.183, PSNR 6.29, LPIPS 0.715). On the CMDP portrait dataset (41 samples, 2× focal length), W2L achieves SSIM 0.716, PSNR 20.38 dB, and LPIPS 0.117, matching or exceeding state-of-the-art portrait distortion correction methods. The pipeline also generalizes to 4× and 8× focal length changes via iterative application.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the U-Net encoder-decoder baseline (trained with SSIM loss for 800 epochs), W2L dominates across all metrics by a wide margin (SSIM 0.768 vs. 0.183; PSNR 19.87 vs. 6.29 dB), confirming that a generic image-to-image translation network is highly ineffective for this structured geometric task. Against Shih et al. [15] (Dolly-Zoom via context-aware layered depth inpainting), W2L wins on all three metrics (SSIM +0.033, PSNR +2.19 dB, LPIPS −0.008), and additionally provides direct control over the compression amount rather than requiring brute-force frame selection. On the CMDP portrait dataset, W2L trails Wang et al. [21] (3D-GAN inversion) on SSIM (0.716 vs. 0.747) but surpasses it on PSNR (20.38 vs. 17.52 dB) and LPIPS (0.117 vs. 0.167), and also outperforms Fried et al. [6] on PSNR and LPIPS while being slightly behind on SSIM (0.716 vs. 0.724). Overall, W2L is competitive with or superior to all baselines, with the largest gains over the naive U-Net and consistent improvements over the Dolly-Zoom approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

The authors explicitly position targeted lens compression as a problem not previously addressed, carving new ground between two adjacent literatures: Dolly-Zoom generation (Shih et al., Liang et al.), which produces uncontrolled sequences of zoomed/dolled frames, and portrait barrel-distortion correction (Fried et al., Wang et al.), which handles only a sub-case without foreground/background scaling. Rather than building on a single prior architecture, W2L repurposes off-the-shelf components—MiDaS for monocular depth, an FFC-based inpainting model, and a small CNN for parameter estimation—into a structured four-stage pipeline (depth layering, inpainting, redundancy reduction, scaling) whose geometric logic mirrors the optical ray diagrams of focal-length change. The closest conceptual ancestor is Shih et al.'s layered depth inpainting, but W2L departs by introducing explicit inter-layer redundancy removal and a learnable exponential scaling law to achieve *targeted* compression rather than free-form view synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is clear: enabling telephoto-equivalent shots from a single wide-angle smartphone camera without hardware changes, and the pipeline's focal-length-agnostic design (iterating 2× steps for 4×, 8×, etc.) makes it broadly applicable. A key limitation the authors acknowledge is that the pipeline cannot hallucinate content that would be newly revealed by the backward camera movement, capping its fidelity in scenes with significant occlusion. Open questions include whether the depth-estimation error from MiDaS propagates catastrophically in complex multi-plane scenes, whether the fixed N=20 layer discretization is optimal, and whether the approach extends to non-integer or sub-2× focal-length ratios. The new 600-pair dataset (real + synthetic) also provides a useful benchmark for future work on this task.
