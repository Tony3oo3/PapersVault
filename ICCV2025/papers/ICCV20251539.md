## Problem

Existing artist-reminiscent mesh generation methods produce non-editable outputs, while sketch-based 3D editing methods either regenerate the entire shape (causing inconsistency in unedited regions) or rely on implicit representations that cannot produce compact triangle meshes. There is no method that enables interactive, localized editing of artist-style triangle meshes through simple 2D sketch modifications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251539/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D sketch bitmap where line strokes are color-coded (black for existing geometry, red for regions to add or delete) and an optional existing 3D mesh, and produces a 3D triangle mesh represented as a tokenized sequence of vertex coordinates with control tokens, plus an automatically regenerated sketch reflecting the updated mesh. The sketch is encoded by a frozen RADIO 2.5-h image encoder, and the mesh is tokenized using MeshAnythingV2's adjacent-triangle tokenizer, which groups vertices into subsequences of adjacent triangles separated by <split> tokens.

## Method

MeshPad decomposes sketch-conditioned mesh editing into two subtasks: addition (autoregressive generation of only the new mesh regions corresponding to red strokes, using causal attention in an OPT backbone) and deletion (bidirectional-attention classification that predicts a binary keep/remove label per vertex). The key architectural contribution is a vertex-aligned speculative decoder that, given the x-coordinate token of a vertex, predicts the y and z coordinates in a single forward pass, reducing generation from 3 sequential steps per vertex to 1. The speculator is jointly trained with the OPT backbone (rather than trained against a frozen transformer) so that the backbone learns to encode sufficient context into its hidden states for the speculator to succeed. Training data for both operations is generated self-supervisedly by sampling nested volumes from ground-truth meshes to create partial mesh pairs, with synthetic sketches produced via Canny edge detection on multi-view normal and depth renderings.

## Results

On ShapeNet, MeshPad achieves a Chamfer Distance of 6.20 (×0.001), LPIPS of 0.1790, CLIP similarity of 95.85, and FID of 9.38, outperforming all baselines across every metric. On the IKEA dataset, it achieves CD 6.78, LPIPS 0.1837, CLIP 96.67, and FID 29.67. In the binary perceptual study, 90%+ of participants prefer MeshPad over all baselines for both generation and editing, with the vertex-aligned speculator enabling interactive editing in 1–5 seconds per step.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeshPad is compared against two state-of-the-art sketch-conditioned 3D generation methods: LAS (SDF-based) and SENS (occupancy-grid-based), each also evaluated with MeshAnythingV2 post-processing (LAS-MA, SENS-MA). MeshPad wins on all four metrics (CD, LPIPS, CLIP, FID) against every baseline on both ShapeNet and IKEA. Against the strongest baseline SENS, MeshPad reduces CD from 8.95 to 6.20 on ShapeNet (≈31% improvement) and from 8.76 to 6.78 on IKEA, while also achieving substantially lower LPIPS (0.1790 vs. 0.2753) and FID (9.38 vs. 81.88). The MeshAnythingV2 post-processing variants (LAS-MA, SENS-MA) actually degrade CD and LPIPS relative to their raw counterparts, indicating that the marching-cube artifacts in LAS/SENS outputs are not well handled by the mesh tokenizer. In the editing task, MeshPad achieves the highest unary ratings (4.2–4.3/5) and is preferred by 91.9–96.5% of participants in binary comparisons, whereas baselines suffer from inconsistency in unedited regions because they regenerate the entire shape.

## Relation to Prior Work

MeshPad builds directly on MeshAnythingV2's OPT backbone, triangle-sequence tokenizer, and pre-trained weights, extending the autoregressive mesh generation paradigm from full-shape synthesis to partial, local editing. It departs from prior sketch-conditioned 3D methods (LAS, SENS) that operate on implicit representations (SDF, occupancy grids) by working natively with artist-reminiscent triangle meshes, and from prior mesh generation work (PolyGen, MeshGPT, MeshAnything) by decomposing editing into addition and deletion subtasks rather than regenerating the entire shape. The vertex-aligned speculator adapts the speculative decoding idea from NLP [55] to mesh token sequences, exploiting the fixed 3-token-per-vertex structure to predict y,z coordinates given x in a single pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decomposition of editing into addition and deletion is a practical training insight: it eliminates the need to collect real mesh-editing sequences, enabling supervised learning from standard 3D shape datasets. The vertex-aligned speculator provides a 2.2× speedup that makes interactive editing feasible (1–5 seconds per edit), a meaningful step toward usable 3D content creation tools. However, the context-length limitation of the transformer restricts output to a few thousand triangles, far below the millions needed for production graphics, and the method has only been validated on ShapeNet-scale categories. Open questions include whether the add/delete decomposition generalizes to more complex multi-step editing sessions and whether token compression can bridge the gap to production-quality mesh complexity.
