## Problem

Multi-modal large language models can only process a finite number of frames per inference, so a single prediction on a long video may miss the key visual evidence needed to answer correctly. While the correct answer appears in the set of multiple predictions with high frequency (Pass@10 > 78% on Video-MME), simply selecting the majority answer leaves a ~15% gap to the coverage upper bound, indicating that a more informed selection strategy is needed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251655/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a long video (ranging from ~3 minutes to over an hour) and a multiple-choice text question as input. It produces a single multiple-choice answer by generating 10 predictions, each based on 32 frames sampled via a bin-wise strategy (one random frame per uniform segment). An external text-only LLM (Deepseek-R1 or Llama-3.3-70B) is additionally queried for question categorization and complementary answer generation, but the video model itself is never retrained or fine-tuned.

## Method

The approach is a training-free, test-time scaling pipeline with two stages. First, bin-wise visual context sampling divides the video into 32 uniform segments and randomly selects one frame per segment for each of 10 predictions, ensuring even temporal coverage while maintaining a fixed inference cost. Second, a self-reward score selects the best prediction by linearly combining three components: (1) a frequency score measuring option prevalence, (2) a marginal confidence score computed as the maximum inter-token logit gap (top-1 minus top-2) across all samples yielding the same answer, which filters out low-confidence predictions, and (3) an adaptive contextual voting score that generates a complementary answer via an external LLM—using segment-wise video narration and clue-guided reasoning for global questions, or uncertainty-guided temporal self-refocus (localizing the key segment by the highest 'Yes' logit on a complementary localization question) for local questions. The final answer is the prediction maximizing S = S^f + α·S^mc + β·S^v, with α=1 and β=3 by default.

## Results

The proposed self-reward selection method consistently improves three MLLMs across seven long video benchmarks without any additional training. With Llava-Video-7B, the method achieves +4.28% on Video-MME (66.52 vs. 62.24 baseline), +5.89% on MLVU (73.38 vs. 67.49), and +4.97% on CG-Bench (39.67 vs. 34.70). With InternVL-2.5-8B, the method reaches 42.17% on CG-Bench and 46.61% on LVBench, surpassing proprietary models like GPT-4o (44.9% CGB, 34.7% LVB) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (37.8% CGB, 33.1% LVB) on LVBench. The Pass@10 upper bound (e.g., 78.74% on Video-MME for Llava-Video) confirms substantial headroom, and the method narrows the gap between single-inference accuracy and this coverage ceiling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is evaluated as a plug-and-play inference-time strategy on three base MLLMs (VideoLlama2-7B, InternVL-2.5-8B, Llava-Video-7B), each compared against its own single-inference baseline and a majority-voting ablation. The proposed self-reward score outperforms both the baseline and majority voting on all seven benchmarks for all three models, with the largest gains on Llava-Video (+4.28% V-MME, +5.89% MLVU) and InternVL-2.5 (+5.54% CG-Bench, +4.39% LVBench). Against proprietary models, InternVL-2.5 + Ours surpasses GPT-4o on LVBench (46.61 vs. 34.7) and Gemini 1.5 Pro on LVBench (46.61 vs. 33.1), while remaining below them on V-MME and V-MMMU. Gains are smaller for VideoLlama2 (16 frames/sample) than for the 32-frame models, attributed to its limited per-sample visual context and weaker narration/instruction-following capability. The method also outperforms random selection by +12.3% on average when choosing among divergent predictions, confirming that the self-reward score provides meaningful signal beyond chance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the repeated-sampling self-improvement paradigm from LLM inference (Brown et al., 'Large Language Monkeys') to the video domain, replacing text-level resampling with visual context sampling—generating multiple predictions from different frame subsets of the same video. It departs from the dominant line of work on long video MLLMs (Video-XL, LongVU, Slow-fast Llava, IG-VLM) that focuses on architectural or token-compression changes to process more frames in a single pass, instead proposing a training-free test-time strategy that keeps each inference at a fixed frame budget. The self-reward scoring mechanism draws on reward-model-based Best-of-N selection from LLM literature but introduces a novel marginal-confidence term and an adaptive contextual voting component tailored to the distinction between global-reasoning and local-perception video questions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a practical, training-free recipe that consistently improves three open-source video MLLMs by 3–6% across seven long-video benchmarks, narrowing the gap between single-pass accuracy and the Pass@N upper bound. However, a substantial gap (10–20%) between the method's accuracy and Pass@10 remains, indicating that the selection mechanism is far from optimal and leaving open the question of whether better scoring or larger sample budgets can close it. The 10× inference cost and dependency on an external 70B-class LLM for question categorization and narration limit immediate deployment, and the method's gains shrink for models with fewer frames per sample (e.g., VideoLlama2 at 16 frames), suggesting the approach is most beneficial when the base model already has reasonable per-sample quality.
