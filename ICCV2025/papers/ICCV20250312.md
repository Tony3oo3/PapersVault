## Problem

Existing human image animation methods either rely solely on 2D skeleton poses, which lack 3D geometry cues (depth, normals) and produce incoherent results, or introduce 3D parametric models like SMPL that are cumbersome to operate, prone to misalignment with pose sequences, and unable to capture fine details such as hands and clothing. The paper asks whether 3D geometry cues can be efficiently enriched directly from 2D poses without requiring a separate, rigid 3D model pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single static human reference image and a sequence of 2D skeleton poses as the only user-provided inputs, and produces a high-resolution animated human video. Internally, a low-resolution first stage jointly generates per-frame RGB, depth, and normal maps; only the depth and normal maps are carried forward to the high-resolution second stage (the low-res RGB is discarded). The video diffusion backbone is Stable Video Diffusion, so outputs inherit SVD's native resolution and temporal length constraints.

## Method

DreamDance is a two-stage diffusion pipeline. Stage 1, the Mutually Aligned Geometry Diffusion Model, jointly denoises concatenated RGB, depth, and normal latents in a single UNet (initialized from Stable Diffusion v1.5) conditioned on the reference image via a reference-UNet cross-attention and on target poses via a zero-conv pose encoder; a geometry attention module cross-attends queries from each modality against concatenated keys/values from all three modalities to enforce mutual alignment, and a temporal attention module smooths across frames. A multi-domain classifier-free guidance scheme applies separate guidance scales per modality. Stage 2, the Cross-domain Controlled Video Diffusion Model, builds an SVD-ControlNet where a cross-domain controller encodes pose, depth, and normal maps through domain-specific convolutions and fuses them via the same geometry attention before injecting into the frozen SVD backbone; a dropout-based robust conditioning strategy randomly zeros control signals across time and modality to mitigate error accumulation from Stage 1. Training follows a three-step curriculum (independent modalities → geometry attention → temporal attention) on the newly constructed TikTokDance5K dataset of 5K dance videos annotated with pose, depth, and normal maps.

## Results

On the TikTok benchmark dataset, DreamDance achieves the best performance across all six metrics: L1 2.89E-04, PSNR 29.90, SSIM 0.798, LPIPS 0.233, FID-VID 19.86, and FVD 153.07. On the proposed TikTokDance5K test set, it similarly leads with FVD 158.24 and FID-VID 20.37, outperforming the next-best method (AnimateAnyone) by roughly 10% in FVD. The most notable margin is in FID-VID on TikTok, where DreamDance (19.86) beats MagicAnimate (21.75) and Champ (26.14), indicating substantially better distributional fidelity of generated videos.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamDance outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric on both the TikTok benchmark and the proposed TikTokDance5K test set. Against the closest competitor Champ (which uses SMPL-rendered depth/normal maps), DreamDance reduces FVD from 170.20 to 153.07 (≈10% improvement) and FID-VID from 26.14 to 19.86 on TikTok, while also achieving higher SSIM (0.798 vs 0.773) and lower LPIPS (0.233 vs 0.235). Compared to AnimateAnyone and MagicAnimate, which rely solely on 2D pose or densepose, DreamDance improves FVD by 11–15% and FID-VID by 9–24%, confirming that the enriched 3D geometry cues (depth + normal) generated by the Mutually Aligned Geometry Diffusion Model provide substantially stronger guidance. The GAN-based MRAA and the frame-by-frame DisCo lag far behind on video-level metrics (FVD 284–293 vs 153), highlighting the advantage of the temporal video diffusion backbone (SVD ControlNet) over sequential or warping-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamDance sits at the intersection of diffusion-based human image animation (AnimateAnyone, MagicAnimate) and 3D-guided animation (Champ), but departs from both in a key way: rather than relying on a parametric 3D model (SMPL) to produce geometry cues, it trains a dedicated diffusion model to jointly generate depth and normal maps directly from 2D poses and a reference image. The architecture inherits the reference-UNet feature injection and zero-convolution pose encoding from AnimateAnyone, and the ControlNet-style adapter pattern from SVD, but introduces a geometry attention mechanism and multi-domain CFG to handle the multi-modality (RGB + depth + normal) generation within a single diffusion process. The two-stage pipeline (low-res geometry generation → high-res video synthesis) is a deliberate departure from single-stage approaches, motivated by the need to decouple geometry enrichment from appearance synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the SMPL motion generation step, DreamDance offers a more user-friendly and flexible pipeline where users only need to provide 2D skeleton poses, which can be easily manipulated with open-source tools. The TikTok-Dance5K dataset (5K annotated dance videos with pose, depth, and normal maps) fills a gap in publicly available resources for this task. However, the method is limited to human-centric animation, the two-stage design introduces potential error accumulation (mitigated but not eliminated by the dropout strategy), and generalization to non-dance motions, longer sequences, or diverse body types remains untested. Open questions include whether the geometry diffusion stage can be distilled or unified with the video stage, and whether the approach extends to multi-person or object-centric animation.
