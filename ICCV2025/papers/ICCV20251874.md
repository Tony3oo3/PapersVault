## Problem

Existing diffusion-based posterior sampling methods for image restoration approximate the intractable noisy likelihood score using the Tweedie estimate, introducing an uncontrolled approximation error that can push the reverse diffusion process off the learned data manifold. This paper identifies that the error becomes severe when the forward operator interacts with high-frequency image components (e.g., high-pass filtering), and provides the first rigorous characterization of this approximation gap under distributional assumptions on natural images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251874/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded observation y = A(x_0) + z (where A is a known forward operator and z is Gaussian noise with σ=0.05) along with a pretrained unconditional diffusion model, and produces samples from the posterior p(x_0|y) via a modified reverse diffusion process. It operates on 256×256 RGB images and handles both linear operators (Gaussian blur, motion blur, high-pass deconvolution) and nonlinear operators (haze). No retraining is required; the pretrained score network is used as-is, with the only additional computation being a frequency-domain mask applied to the measurement at each step.

## Method

FGPS exploits the observation that diffusion models generate images hierarchically from low to high frequencies by applying a time-varying low-pass filter k_t to the measurement y, so that early reverse-diffusion steps only see coarse frequency content while later steps progressively incorporate finer details. The noisy likelihood score is approximated as ∇_{x_t} log p(C_t y | μ_{0|t}), where C_t is a circulant matrix representing the filter and μ_{0|t} is the Tweedie estimate, yielding a tractable gradient computed via backpropagation through the score network. The frequency curriculum {τ_t} is data-dependent (exponential for face datasets, uniform for diverse ImageNet), and the step size S_t is scheduled with a cosine decay. Under a Gaussian data model with power-law power spectral density, the paper derives closed-form expressions for the true conditional score and the FGPS approximation, proving that the approximation gap is orders of magnitude smaller than DPS for high-pass forward operators.

## Results

On the FFHQ dataset, FGPS achieves FID 10.02, LPIPS 0.124, PSNR 18.79, and SSIM 0.739 on the high-pass filter task, dramatically outperforming DPS (FID 121.57) and DSG (FID 17.81). On motion deblurring (FFHQ), FGPS reaches FID 20.48 and PSNR 25.48, beating DPS (FID 27.15) and DSG (FID 32.74). On ImageNet, FGPS leads all baselines across every task, e.g., FID 49.25 on motion blur vs. 77.74 for DPS and 107.25 for DSG. For the nonlinear dehazing task, FGPS achieves FID 9.37 and LPIPS 0.066, surpassing even the supervised AOD-Net (FID 33.35).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FGPS clearly dominates on the high-pass filter task, where DPS collapses (FID 121.57 on FFHQ, 111.73 on ImageNet) and even DSG (FID 17.81/110.85) struggles, while FGPS achieves FID 10.02/24.44 — a 12× improvement over DPS on FFHQ. On motion deblurring, FGPS beats DPS by ~7 FID points on FFHQ (20.48 vs. 27.15) and by ~28 FID points on ImageNet (49.25 vs. 77.74), and also outperforms DSG on both datasets. On the simpler Gaussian blur task (FFHQ), DPS retains a slight edge in FID (21.69 vs. 21.89) and PSNR (26.01 vs. 25.44), but FGPS is competitive and wins on ImageNet (FID 56.46 vs. 98.02 for DPS). MCG consistently performs worst across all tasks (FID >150 on FFHQ, >290 on ImageNet), confirming that manifold-constrained gradients are ineffective for these problems. For the nonlinear dehazing task, FGPS (FID 9.37, LPIPS 0.066) outperforms DSG (FID 10.55), DPS (FID 24.24), and even the supervised AOD-Net (FID 33.35), demonstrating that the frequency curriculum generalizes beyond linear operators.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Diffusion Posterior Sampling (DPS) [9], which approximates the intractable noisy likelihood score by replacing the denoising distribution with a Dirac delta centered at the Tweedie estimate. The key departure is introducing a time-varying low-pass filter on the measurements, motivated by the observation that diffusion models generate images hierarchically from low to high frequencies (connecting to the inverse heat dissipation interpretation [30] and spectral autoregression [15]). Unlike MCG [10] and DSG [42], which impose strong structural assumptions on the data manifold (linearity or clean-manifold membership of μ_{0|t}) to control approximation error, FGPS controls the error through a frequency curriculum that is data-adaptive and requires no manifold assumptions. The theoretical contribution—providing the first exact characterization of the approximation gap under Gaussian data with power-law spectral density—fills a gap left by prior works that only offered loose upper/lower bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and practically relevant failure mode: existing SOTA diffusion-based restoration methods (DPS, MCG, DSG) produce completely incoherent outputs when the forward operator is a high-pass filter, a regime that arises in real imaging systems like phase contrast microscopy. The frequency curriculum is a simple, easy-to-implement modification that addresses this failure without retraining, and the theoretical analysis gives practitioners a principled way to understand when and why posterior sampling approximations break down. Limitations include the theoretical analysis being restricted to linear inverse problems with Gaussian data assumptions, and the frequency schedule still requiring dataset-dependent tuning (exponential vs. uniform). Open questions include whether the approach extends to highly nonlinear forward operators beyond dehazing, whether the frequency curriculum can be learned end-to-end, and whether the spectral assumptions hold for video or 3D data.
