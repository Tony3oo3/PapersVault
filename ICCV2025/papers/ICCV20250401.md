## Problem

Existing PnP-based pose estimation methods predict only the front surface of an object, ignoring the back surface and interior volume. This limits the density of 2D-3D correspondences available for pose solving, leaving potential geometric information unused. The paper asks whether simultaneously predicting both surfaces and densely sampling between them can yield more accurate poses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250401/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes a 256×256 cropped RGB image (from a 2D detector) and outputs a 128×128 feature map with 49 channels: 24 channels encoding 8-level continuous codes for the front surface (x, y, z × 8 levels), 24 channels for the back surface, and 1 channel for the object mask. At inference, the predicted continuous codes are binarized and decoded into 3D coordinates, which are then densely interpolated between front and back surfaces so that multiple 3D points share each 2D pixel, producing the ultra-dense correspondence set fed to RANSAC-PnP. For the RGB-D variant, depth is used only in a post-hoc FoundationPose refinement step.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-part pipeline: (1) a CNN (ResNet-34 or EfficientNet-B4, adapted from ZebraPose) that simultaneously regresses front and back surface coordinates via a novel Hierarchical Continuous Coordinate Encoding (HCCE), where each coordinate component is encoded into 8 levels of continuous codes generated by iterative mirroring of the previous level, eliminating the dark/light stripe boundaries that hinder binary-code learning; and (2) a dense interpolation step that uniformly samples n = ⌊‖q₁−q₂‖/d̄⌋ points along the line segment between each front-back 3D pair, creating ultra-dense 2D-3D correspondences. Training uses a histogram-based hierarchical loss: a separate error histogram is computed per coordinate component (x, y, z) per surface, and the loss weights at each encoding level are dynamically adjusted from these histograms to guide learning from coarse to fine levels. RANSAC-PnP is constrained to sample at most one 3D point per 2D pixel per iteration (150 iterations) to avoid degenerate solutions from the shared 2D projections.

## Results

On the seven BOP core datasets, HCCEPose(BF) achieves a BOP score of 77.3% on RGB 6D localization, outperforming ZebraPose (74.9%) by 2.4% and GPose (72.9%) by 4.4%. In the 2D segmentation task, it reaches 65.6%, surpassing ZebraPose (61.9%) by 3.7%. When trained on RGB but tested on RGB-D data, the method scores 84.7%, a 4.7% improvement over the previous SOTA GDRNPP (80.5%). The method wins on 6 out of 7 individual datasets in RGB 6D localization, losing only marginally on HB (91.9% vs. ZebraPose's 92.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ZebraPose (CVPR 2022), the primary SOTA RGB-based baseline, HCCEPose(BF) wins on 6 of 7 datasets in RGB 6D localization (losing only 0.3% on HB) and on all 7 datasets in 2D segmentation, with the largest gains on ITODD (+3.8% 6D, +5.1% seg) and T-LESS (+3.5% 6D, +4.7% seg). Against GPose (2023), the method consistently outperforms across all datasets in RGB 6D localization, with the core average improving from 72.9% to 77.3%. In the RGB-D setting, HCCEPose(BF) surpasses GDRNPP on 6 of 7 datasets (core: 84.7% vs. 80.5%), with a particularly large margin on ITODD (73.4% vs. 58.8%), but trails PFA on TUD-L (94.4% vs. 96.3%). In 2D segmentation, the method dominates DLZDet by a wide margin on every dataset (e.g., +19.6% on IC-BIN, +27.4% on ITODD), reflecting the benefit of jointly predicting front and back surface coordinates alongside the mask.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the surface-coordinate-based PnP pipeline established by CDPN, GDR-Net, and Pix2Pose, and builds directly on ZebraPose's hierarchical binary surface encoding, from which it adapts the network architecture. The key conceptual departure is twofold: (1) predicting both front and back surface coordinates and densely sampling the interior to construct 'ultra-dense' 2D-3D correspondences, and (2) replacing ZebraPose's hierarchical binary codes with Hierarchical Continuous Coordinate Encoding (HCCE) that uses mirroring to generate multi-level continuous codes, eliminating the stripe-boundary learning difficulty observed in binary encodings. The multi-histogram weight adjustment for the loss function is a targeted fix for the fact that ZebraPose's single-histogram strategy degrades performance when applied to continuous codes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that incorporating back-surface and interior geometry into 2D-3D correspondences yields consistent (if modest) gains across all seven BOP core datasets, suggesting that the front-surface-only assumption in prior work leaves exploitable information on the table. The HCCE encoding is a practical, drop-in improvement over hierarchical binary encoding that practitioners working on coordinate-based pose estimation can adopt. A significant open limitation is that the method requires a separate network per object, which restricts scalability to open-world or category-level settings; the authors explicitly note that a unified multi-object model is infeasible with the current approach. Whether the ultra-dense correspondence idea transfers to category-level or novel-object pose estimation remains unexplored.
