## Problem

Unsupervised 3D object detection relies on pseudo bounding boxes from clustering to initialize self-paced training, but these pseudo boxes inevitably contain coordinate-level inaccuracies (especially for small, distant objects) that accumulate and degrade the final model. Existing methods treat all pseudo box coordinates equally during training, failing to account for the varying reliability of individual coordinates within a single box.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251997/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw LiDAR point clouds (and optionally co-registered RGB images in fusion settings) and produces dense point-wise 3D bounding box predictions with 7 coordinates (x, y, z, length, width, height, orientation) per point, alongside a matching 7-dimensional uncertainty vector. The uncertainty is computed at the coordinate level rather than the box or scene level, meaning a single box can have high uncertainty on its orientation while having low uncertainty on its position. No ground-truth 3D boxes are used during training; they are reserved exclusively for evaluation.

## Method

UA3D introduces an auxiliary detection branch with a reduced channel configuration (γ=0.5× the primary) attached to an intermediate Set Abstraction layer of the PointNet++ backbone, creating two detectors with different capacities. Uncertainty is estimated as the L1 prediction discrepancy between the primary and auxiliary detectors at each of the 7 box coordinates, capturing fine-grained disagreement that reflects pseudo-box inaccuracy. During training, the per-coordinate detection loss is divided by exp(U_i) and augmented with a penalty term λ·U_i, so coordinates with high estimated uncertainty receive proportionally lower loss weight while a regularization term prevents the model from uniformly inflating uncertainty. This uncertainty-regularized loss is applied symmetrically to both detectors, and the total loss combines them with equal weight (μ=1), enabling the framework to selectively discount unreliable pseudo-box coordinates while preserving the influence of accurate ones throughout iterative self-training.

## Results

On nuScenes (LiDAR-based, 10 self-training rounds), UA3D achieves 19.6 AP_BEV and 10.5 AP_3D, surpassing the state-of-the-art LiSe by +3.9% AP_BEV and +1.5% AP_3D. On Lyft (LiDAR-based, 10 rounds), UA3D reaches 62.1 AP_BEV and 57.9 AP_3D, outperforming LiSe by +2.3% AP_BEV and +1.8% AP_3D. The largest gains appear in the long-range (50–80 m) range, where pseudo boxes are most noisy, confirming that coordinate-level uncertainty regularization is most impactful where label quality is lowest.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UA3D is compared against three unsupervised 3D detection baselines: MODEST (CVPR'22), OYSTER (CVPR'23), and LiSe (ECCV'24). On nuScenes (LiDAR-based, 10 rounds), UA3D outperforms LiSe by +3.9 AP_BEV (19.6 vs. 15.7) and +1.5 AP_3D (10.5 vs. 9.0), and surpasses MODEST by +7.8 AP_BEV and +3.9 AP_3D. On Lyft (LiDAR-based, 10 rounds), UA3D beats LiSe by +2.3 AP_BEV (62.1 vs. 59.8) and +1.8 AP_3D (57.9 vs. 56.1), with the largest margin in the 50–80 m range (+8.3 AP_BEV, +4.1 AP_3D). The same hyper-parameters tuned on nuScenes transfer directly to Lyft, confirming cross-dataset generalizability. Under LiDAR–image fusion settings, UA3D also consistently leads LiSe (e.g., +1.5 AP_BEV and +0.7 AP_3D on nuScenes; +2.5 AP_BEV and +1.7 AP_3D on Lyft), demonstrating compatibility with multi-modal baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-paced unsupervised 3D object detection paradigm established by MODEST, OYSTER, and LiSe, which all rely on clustering-based pseudo boxes that inevitably contain noise. The key departure is the introduction of an auxiliary detector whose prediction discrepancy with the primary detector serves as a learnable, coordinate-level uncertainty signal, replacing the direct use of noisy pseudo labels. Unlike prior uncertainty estimation approaches (regression-based, ensemble, MC Dropout) that the authors benchmark against, the dual-detector discrepancy mechanism avoids overfitting while remaining computationally efficient (single forward pass). The method is built on PointRCNN/PointNet++ but is presented as a plug-in module compatible with various baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The coordinate-level uncertainty regularization addresses a persistent and underexplored failure mode in self-paced 3D detection: not all box coordinates are equally noisy, yet prior methods treat the entire pseudo box uniformly. The ablation showing coordinate-level substantially outperforms box-level and point-cloud-level uncertainty provides a clear design principle for future work. Limitations include evaluation on only two datasets and a single backbone family (point-based), leaving open whether the approach transfers to voxel-based or transformer-based detectors. The auxiliary detector also adds training overhead, and the method's dependence on the self-paced paradigm limits immediate applicability to other unsupervised settings.
