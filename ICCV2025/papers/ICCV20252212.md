## Problem

Action recognition models achieve strong performance on standard benchmarks, but it remains unclear whether they can transfer high-level motion concepts (e.g., 'punching') to unseen fine-grained contexts (e.g., 'punching person') within the same distribution. Existing zero-shot, base-to-novel, and domain generalization evaluations test across different distributions or novel classes, but do not isolate whether a model truly understands the core motion or merely memorizes context-specific cues. This paper formalizes and systematically measures this intra-dataset motion transferability gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252212/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

Unimodal models take video clips as input and produce a single coarse action class label via a fixed classification head, so they can only be evaluated on known coarse classes. Multimodal models take video clips plus text action descriptions (coarse or fine captions) and match video embeddings to text embeddings in a shared space, enabling evaluation on both known and unknown fine-grained classes without retraining. The evaluation protocol trains on one set of fine classes and tests on both the known set and a disjoint unknown set that shares the same coarse classes, reporting absolute drop and harmonic mean between known and unknown accuracies.

## Method

The paper introduces three benchmark datasets (Syn-TA, K400-TA, SSv2-TA) each structured with a coarse-to-fine hierarchy where fine classes are split into two disjoint sets sharing identical coarse classes, enabling controlled known-vs-unknown context evaluation. Syn-TA is a synthetic Blender-rendered dataset with 3D objects performing motions against varied backgrounds, designed to eliminate object and background biases and isolate pure motion understanding. The key proposed method is a disentanglement strategy applied to EZ-CLIP that adds two parallel branches near the encoder output—one for coarse concepts and one for fine-grained contexts—with residual connections injecting fine features into the coarse branch after each block, forcing the model to learn complementary representations. Thirteen state-of-the-art models (8 unimodal CNNs/transformers, 5 multimodal CLIP-based) plus three domain generalization methods are evaluated under this protocol, revealing consistent performance drops from known to unknown contexts and from coarse to fine motions.

## Results

Across all three datasets, every model exhibits a substantial performance drop from known to unknown contexts, with absolute drops (D_abs) typically exceeding 20% for coarse motions. AIM achieves the highest harmonic mean on Syn-TA (82.17) and SSv2-TA (58.18) for coarse actions, while FROSTER leads on K400-TA (80.42). For fine motions (multimodal models only), EZ-CLIP achieves the best harmonic mean on K400-TA (78.47) and SSv2-TA (39.70), while ActionCLIP leads on Syn-TA (53.85). The disentanglement strategy applied to EZ-CLIP improves coarse HM by ~2–3 points on Syn-TA and SSv2-TA (e.g., 67.96→70.69 on Syn-TA Set 1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among unimodal models, AIM dominates Syn-TA (HM 82.17) and SSv2-TA (HM 58.18), outperforming the next-best X3D (71.79 / 53.05) by 10+ points, while FROSTER (multimodal) leads K400-TA (HM 80.42) ahead of AIM (76.29) and EZ-CLIP (75.43). For fine motions, ActionCLIP achieves the best Syn-TA HM (53.85), EZ-CLIP leads K400-TA (78.47) and SSv2-TA (39.70), and FROSTER is competitive on K400-TA (80.81). Domain generalization methods (VideoDG, STDN, CIR) consistently underperform dedicated action recognition models, with CIR showing the worst unknown-context accuracy (9.59% on Syn-TA, 12.66% on K400-TA). Notably, all 13 models suffer large absolute drops (D_abs 20–65%) from known to unknown contexts, confirming that no current architecture reliably transfers high-level motion concepts to novel fine-grained settings. The disentanglement strategy applied to EZ-CLIP yields modest but consistent gains (+2.7 to +3.2 HM) on Syn-TA and SSv2-TA, suggesting that leveraging fine-context cues during training partially mitigates the transferability gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a distinct evaluation niche between zero-shot action recognition (which tests unseen classes), domain generalization (which tests cross-distribution shifts), and base-to-novel transfer (which splits by frequency). It departs from all of these by introducing a coarse-to-fine intra-dataset hierarchy that specifically probes whether a model can transfer a high-level motion concept (e.g., 'punching') to novel fine-grained contexts (e.g., 'punching person') within the same distribution. The disentanglement strategy builds directly on EZ-CLIP's architecture, adding dual-branch heads with residual connections to leverage fine-grained features for coarse reasoning. The synthetic Syn-TA dataset extends the use of Blender-rendered 3D scenes (previously used for controlled studies) into a structured action-recognition benchmark with a coarse-to-fine class hierarchy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This benchmark exposes a persistent and quantified gap in motion transferability that prior evaluation protocols (zero-shot, domain generalization, base-to-novel) do not capture, providing the field with a diagnostic tool to measure whether models truly understand motion or merely memorize context-specific patterns. The finding that a bias-free synthetic setting is harder than real-world datasets, and that model scale does not improve temporal reasoning, challenges common assumptions about what drives generalization. Open questions include whether the disentanglement strategy generalizes beyond EZ-CLIP and to larger model families, whether the coarse-to-fine hierarchy can be extended to more complex action taxonomies, and whether closing the known-to-unknown gap requires fundamentally new architectures or simply better training signals.
