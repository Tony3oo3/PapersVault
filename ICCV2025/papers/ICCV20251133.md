## Problem

Establishing dense pixel-level correspondences between objects from different categories based on functional similarity (e.g., matching a kettle spout to a bottle neck for 'pour-with') remains unsolved. Existing self-supervised features handle within-category matching but degrade across categories, while VLMs can identify functional parts but cannot perform fine-grained spatial correspondence. No established task definition, benchmark dataset, or scalable training method existed for this cross-category dense functional correspondence problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251133/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of segmented object images at 224×224 resolution and a function text prompt (e.g., 'pour-with') as input, and produces dense per-pixel feature embeddings that encode functional correspondence, plus an optional binary functional part mask. The images are assumed to be pre-segmented objects rather than full scenes, and the function prompt conditions the entire correspondence computation, restricting matches to functionally relevant parts rather than the whole object.

## Method

The approach distills complementary knowledge from VLMs and self-supervised features into a single function-conditioned MLP trained on top of frozen DINOv2-B features and CLIP text embeddings. Training uses two contrastive objectives: a functional part contrastive loss (infoNCE) that pulls features of VLM-pseudo-labeled functional parts across objects together while pushing non-functional regions apart, and a multi-view spatial contrastive loss that enforces view-invariant structural encoding to prevent mode collapse within a part. Pseudo-labels are generated by prompting CogVLM with GPT-4-derived part descriptions on multi-view renders of Objaverse assets, then aggregating 2D bounding boxes onto 3D point clouds for multi-view consistency. The spatial loss operates on multi-view pairs of the same 3D asset, while the functional loss operates on cross-category pairs sharing a function, jointly encoding both semantic and structural information in the learned feature space.

## Results

On the real evaluation dataset, the full model (with mask loss) achieves a Normalized Dist of 0.153, PCK@23p of 0.501, Best F1@23p of 0.808, and AP@23p of 0.730, outperforming all baselines across every metric. On the synthetic evaluation dataset, the same model reaches Normalized Dist 0.172, PCK@23p 0.480, Best F1@23p 0.774, and AP@23p 0.684. Notably, the model is trained entirely on synthetic data yet generalizes to real-world images, with real-dataset metrics consistently exceeding synthetic-dataset metrics. The closest baseline (CogVLM+DINO) achieves PCK@23p of 0.440 on the real dataset, a 6.1-point gap behind the proposed method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baseline families on every metric in both the synthetic and real evaluation datasets. Against pure self-supervised features (DINOv2, SD, SD-DINO), the full model improves PCK@23p by 9.3 points on the real dataset (0.501 vs. 0.408 for DINOv2) and by 19.2 points over SD (0.501 vs. 0.309), confirming that self-supervised features alone struggle with cross-category correspondence. The strongest baseline is CogVLM+DINO, which adds VLM-grounded functional part bounding boxes to DINOv2 features; it achieves PCK@23p of 0.440 and AP@23p of 0.561 on the real dataset, still trailing the proposed method by 6.1 and 16.9 points respectively, and is roughly 50× slower at inference. ManipVQA-based baselines perform worse than CogVLM+DINO, with prompting by function name (ManipVQA-F) yielding notably lower scores than prompting by part name (ManipVQA-P), highlighting the difficulty of zero-shot affordance grounding from function descriptions. The ablation rows further show that neither functional-only nor spatial-only contrastive loss is sufficient: functional-only collapses spatial structure (PCK@23p drops to 0.336 on real), while spatial-only lacks functional semantics (PCK@23p 0.472 vs. 0.501 for the full model).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of self-supervised correspondence learning (DINOv2, Stable Diffusion features) and vision-language model grounding (CogVLM, ManipVQA), arguing that each is individually insufficient for cross-category dense functional correspondence. It departs from Lai et al.'s sparse functional keypoint formulation by defining a dense, pixel-level correspondence task, and from pure self-supervised approaches by introducing function-conditioned contrastive learning that distills VLM pseudo-labels into a dedicated feature extractor. The method builds on DINOv2's emergent correspondence properties and multi-view contrastive learning (Dense Object Nets) while adding a functional semantic axis via CLIP text conditioning and infoNCE-based part-level contrastive objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a genuine gap by introducing the first dense cross-category functional correspondence task with both synthetic and real-world benchmarks, providing the community a standardized evaluation that previously did not exist. The practical implication for robotics is significant: the learned features enable efficient transfer of manipulation demonstrations across visually dissimilar objects (e.g., kettle → bottle) without per-object re-annotation. Key limitations include the assumption of pre-segmented objects, reliance on 3D assets for both training data generation and ground-truth annotation, and the fact that the evaluation protocol (3D alignment-based) may not fully capture the difficulty of open-world functional reasoning. Open questions include whether the approach scales to functions with no clear single-part correspondence and whether the pseudo-labeling pipeline can be extended to video or in-the-wild settings without 3D geometry.
