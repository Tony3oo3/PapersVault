## Problem

Partially Relevant Video Retrieval (PRVR) requires encoding diverse temporal contexts within long untrimmed videos to match text queries, but existing methods face a fundamental accuracy-efficiency trade-off: exhaustive clip modeling (MS-SL) captures rich multi-scale contexts at high memory cost, while local clip modeling (GMMFormer) is efficient but cannot represent complex, extended events. The paper asks whether a fixed, compact set of prototypes can simultaneously preserve diverse video contexts and remain searchable via text queries without sacrificing either accuracy or storage efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251892/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes untrimmed videos (sampled at 3 fps, encoded via CLIP-L/14 or ResNet152+I3D) and natural language text queries (encoded via CLIP or RoBERTa) as input. Each video is compressed into a fixed number of prototypes (default 30) regardless of video length, yielding constant per-video storage (~0.09 MB vs. 1.01 MB for MS-SL). At inference, the text query is compared against all stored prototypes across the database, and the video whose prototype achieves the highest cosine similarity is returned as the retrieval result.

## Method

The framework uses a dual-branch (frame + clip) architecture where the clip branch applies exhaustive clip modeling to generate multi-scale clip features, which are then aggregated into L^p learnable prototypes via K iterations of cross-attention (no self-attention between prototypes, ensuring independent event capture). To compensate for the implicit nature of prototype aggregation, the paper introduces dual reconstruction: a cross-modal task where only the retrieved prototype reconstructs masked text tokens (aligning prototypes with query semantics via infoNCE), and a uni-modal task where all prototypes reconstruct frame features via an MLP decoder (preserving full visual context). A video mixing strategy concatenates two videos and applies binary cross-entropy on the retrieved prototype's attention distribution to provide weak supervision for focusing on query-relevant segments, while an orthogonal loss encourages semantic diversity among prototypes. The overall objective combines standard contrastive retrieval loss with these four auxiliary terms.

## Results

With the CLIP-L/14 backbone on TVR, the proposed method achieves R@1=34.7, R@5=60.0, R@10=70.1, R@100=94.4, and SumR=259.2, outperforming the strongest PRVR baseline MS-SL (SumR=248.2) by 11.0 points while using only 0.09 MB per video versus MS-SL's 1.01 MB (a 91% memory reduction). On ActivityNet Captions with the same backbone, it reaches SumR=192.3 vs. GMMFormer's 186.9. With the ResNet152+I3D+RoBERTa backbone, it achieves SumR=185.1 on TVR and 147.4 on ActivityNet Captions, surpassing GMMFormer (176.6 and 146.0) and MS-SL (172.4 and 140.1). On QVHighlights (reorganized for PRVR), it attains R@1=22.6 and SumR=226.6, exceeding MS-SL (20.4, 222.5) and GMMFormer (18.2, 211.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 13 compared baselines across every dataset and backbone setting. Against the two primary PRVR competitors, it surpasses MS-SL [2] by +3.9 R@1 and +11.0 SumR on TVR (CLIP-L/14) while reducing per-video memory from 1.01 MB to 0.09 MB, and beats GMMFormer [5] by +5.3 R@1 and +16.0 SumR on the same setting. With the weaker ResNet152+I3D+RoBERTa backbone, it still leads GMMFormer by +1.5 R@1 and +8.5 SumR on TVR, and outperforms DL-DKD [3] (+1.0 R@1) and PEAN [4] (+1.9 R@1). On QVHighlights, it exceeds the prototypical T2VR method TMVM [21] by +2.1 R@1 and +9.0 SumR, and beats MS-SL [2] by +2.2 R@1. The paper also shows that its streamlined cross-attention-only prototype generator outperforms both Perceiver [58] (−9.7 SumR) and Slot-attention [57] (−5.4 SumR) on TVR, confirming that simpler architectures are better suited for PRVR. Notably, GMMFormer's local-biased encoding underperforms more severely with the stronger CLIP backbone (29.4 vs. 30.8 R@1 on TVR), whereas the proposed method benefits from CLIP's richer representations, suggesting that unconstrained exhaustive clip modeling better exploits foundation-model features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two competing lines of PRVR research: MS-SL's exhaustive clip modeling, which captures diverse temporal scales at high memory cost, and GMMFormer's Gaussian-constrained local encoding, which is efficient but limited to short contexts. It repurposes the prototypical learning paradigm from slot attention and perceiver (originally developed for object-centric learning and general perception) as a compression mechanism that aggregates exhaustive clip features into a fixed number of prototypes. The key architectural departure is a deliberately simplified cross-attention-only prototype generator, which the authors show outperforms the more complex Perceiver and Slot Attention designs for this retrieval task, combined with dual reconstruction objectives and a video-mixing weak-supervision strategy not previously applied to PRVR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 90% memory reduction (0.09 MB vs. 1.01 MB for MS-SL) while simultaneously improving retrieval accuracy addresses a core deployment bottleneck for large-scale video search systems, making diverse-context encoding practically viable. The observation that GMMFormer's local-biased encoding underperforms with strong foundation-model backbones like CLIP suggests that constraint-heavy inductive biases may actively hinder the exploitation of richer pre-trained representations—an insight relevant beyond PRVR. Open questions include whether the fixed prototype count can be made adaptive per video, how the approach scales to hour-long videos, and whether the video-mixing weak supervision can be replaced by cheaper alternatives.
