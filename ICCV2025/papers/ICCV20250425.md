## Problem

While LoRA-based fine-tuning can drive video diffusion models toward specific camera motions or object movements, fusing multiple LoRAs for compound trajectories suffers from instability (abrupt direction changes) and the conventional adapter-scale coefficient fails to provide linear, fine-grained control over motion amplitude. Prior LoRA fusion work targets high-level properties like style and subject, leaving low-level spatial controllability—precise camera trajectory and motion strength—largely unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250425/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, an optional input image (for image-to-video), and one or more scalar scaling values S ∈ [s, 1] that encode camera trajectory amplitude or object motion speed. Each S is mapped through a Fourier positional embedding into a single scaling token appended to the DiT token sequence; for multi-LoRA fusion, k scaling tokens are concatenated and each LoRA's attention is restricted to its own token. The output is a 49-frame video at 480×720 with the specified camera path and/or motion strength, generated via 50-step DDIM sampling with classifier-free guidance scale 6.

## Method

LiON-LoRA rethinks LoRA fusion through three principles. First, it exploits the empirical orthogonality of camera LoRA features in shallow transformer blocks (mean cosine similarity ≈ 0.06 ± 0.06) to guarantee decoupled low-level control. Second, it enforces norm consistency by post-normalizing each LoRA's output (ΔŴ_i = α/‖ΔW_i‖ · ΔW_i) so that independently trained LoRAs contribute proportionally during fusion, eliminating the dominance of high-norm modules. Third, it replaces the global adapter scale λ with a dedicated scaling token E = linear(γ(S))—where γ is a multi-frequency Fourier embedding—concatenated to the DiT sequence, enabling linear, explicit control of motion amplitude without degrading base-model generalization. For training-free multi-LoRA fusion, each LoRA's self-attention is restricted to attend only its own scaling token within an independent subspace; hidden states are then averaged while scaling tokens are spatially concatenated, preserving decoupled control across arbitrary camera-motion combinations.

## Results

On basic camera poses (Table 1), LiON-LoRA achieves the best scores across all four metrics: RotErr 0.776, TransErr 0.167, ATE 0.295, and FVD 136.0, improving over the strongest baseline DimensionX-S* by 36.5% in RotErr, 16.9% in TransErr, 17.8% in ATE, and 29.6% in FVD. On complex fused camera poses (Table 2), LiON-LoRA again leads in TransErr (0.197), ATE (0.345), and FVD (172.8), though CamI2V retains a slight edge in RotErr (0.924 vs. 1.044). The method achieves these results with only 100 training samples and 4,000 fine-tuning iterations, compared to 10k+ iterations typically required by explicit camera-injection methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LiON-LoRA outperforms all five baselines on basic camera poses across every metric. Against the closest competitor DimensionX-S* (re-implemented S-Director on the same CogVideoX backbone), LiON-LoRA reduces RotErr from 1.223 to 0.776 (−36.5%) and FVD from 193.3 to 136.0 (−29.6%), demonstrating that the scaling token and norm-consistency fusion yield both more accurate trajectories and higher visual quality. Compared to explicit camera-injection methods (CameraCtrl, CamI2V), LiON-LoRA achieves lower trajectory errors while also producing substantially better FVD (136.0 vs. 218.9 and 294.6), indicating that the LoRA-based implicit control avoids the overfitting and hallucination issues of rigid pose conditioning. On complex fused trajectories (Table 2), LiON-LoRA wins on TransErr, ATE, and FVD against all baselines, but CamI2V retains a narrow RotErr advantage (0.924 vs. 1.044), suggesting that rotation estimation from SfM remains slightly more favorable for that method under multi-motion combinations. MotionCtrl and CameraCtrl, which rely on explicit motion or camera inputs, show the largest gaps in FVD (290.9 and 218.9 vs. 136.0), reflecting quality degradation from their heavier training pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper rethinks LoRA fusion specifically for low-level spatial control in video diffusion, departing from prior LoRA composition work (K-LoRA, ZipLoRA, Multi-LoRA) that targeted high-level properties like style and subject. It builds on CogVideoX's DiT architecture and the LoRA parameter-efficient fine-tuning paradigm, but introduces three principled modifications—orthogonality analysis in shallow layers, norm normalization, and a scaling token mechanism—that address why vanilla LoRA fusion produces unstable camera trajectories. Compared to explicit camera-injection methods (CameraCtrl, CamI2V) and prior LoRA-based camera control (DimensionX, AnimateDiff), LiON-LoRA positions itself as a training-efficient middle ground that avoids the generalization degradation of explicit pose conditioning while achieving finer-grained control than implicit LoRA scaling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical efficiency is notable: 100 training samples and 4,000 iterations suffice for competitive camera control, versus 50,000+ steps for explicit-injection baselines, making controllable video generation accessible with minimal annotation. The orthogonality finding in shallow VDM layers provides a theoretical lens for understanding when LoRA fusion is decoupled versus coupled, which could inform future multi-task PEFT design beyond camera control. Limitations include validation on a single base model (CogVideoX), a 3DGS-based rendering pipeline for training data that adds complexity, and camera primitives limited to three basic motion types. Open questions include whether the norm-consistency and orthogonality properties generalize to other VDM architectures (e.g., MMDiT-based models like HunyuanVideo) and whether the scaling token approach extends to more complex 4D scene dynamics.
