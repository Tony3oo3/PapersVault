## Problem

Projection-based Zero-Shot CIR models built on CLIP are efficient but struggle to interpret textual modifications, while LLM-based methods that understand instructions well are computationally expensive, slow, and often rely on proprietary models. The paper argues that the performance gap stems specifically from the instruction-following capability inherent in LLMs, which is absent in retrieval-oriented VLMs, and asks whether this capability can be distilled into compact projection-based architectures without requiring LLMs at inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252690/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a reference RGB image and a free-form textual modification sentence (e.g., 'change the color to red') and produces a single composed embedding in CLIP's text embedding space. Target images are pre-encoded by the CLIP visual tower and cached; retrieval is performed via cosine similarity between the composed embedding and all cached target embeddings. The entire inference pipeline uses only the CLIP-based projection model (153M–429M parameters) with no LLM involvement, making it deployable in latency-sensitive or privacy-constrained settings.

## Method

DistillCIR builds on Pic2Word's architecture (CLIP visual tower → 3-layer MLP projection → CLIP text tower) and trains it with three jointly optimized contrastive losses. First, a frontier LLM (Claude 3.5 Sonnet / GPT-4o) extends CC3M image-caption pairs into triplets with a structured three-step reasoning chain (identify changeable terms → brainstorm modification → derive modified caption), providing both the composed loss (L_com) and a reasoning distillation loss (L_rea) that aligns the (image, modified caption) pair with the LLM's reasoning text using learnable prompt tokens to disambiguate semantics. Second, an open-source MLLM (LLaVA-Phi3-mini) is separately trained on the same triplets as an embedding model, and its instruction-aware caption embeddings are distilled into the projection model via a feature-level contrastive loss (L_fea) with a linear projection head. The student is trained with LoRA on both CLIP towers and full fine-tuning of the MLP projection module, achieving state-of-the-art ZS-CIR performance across four benchmarks while remaining compact and LLM-free at inference.

## Results

On the CIRR test set (ViT-L backbone), DistillCIR achieves 32.34% R@1, 63.58% R@5, and 74.29% R@10, surpassing the strongest projection-based baseline Slerp by 1.40% in R@1 and the LLM-based CIReVL by 7.79% in R@1. On CIRCO, DistillCIR reaches 18.67% mAP@5, 20.33% mAP@10, and 22.82% mAP@25, outperforming Slerp by 0.21% mAP@5 and CIReVL by 0.10% mAP@5. Notably, DistillCIR uses only 429M parameters (comparable to other projection-based models) while CIReVL requires 12.5B and MCL requires 7.4B, and no LLM is needed at inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DistillCIR outperforms all eight baselines on both CIRR and CIRCO. Against the strongest projection-based method Slerp, DistillCIR gains +1.40% R@1 on CIRR and +0.21% mAP@5 on CIRCO, while matching Slerp's Rs@3 (91.83 vs. 92.31). Against LLM-based methods, the advantage is more pronounced: DistillCIR beats CIReVL by +7.79% R@1 on CIRR and MCL by +6.12% R@1, despite using 29× fewer parameters than CIReVL (429M vs. 12.5B) and no LLM at inference. On CIRCO, DistillCIR edges out CIReVL by +0.10% mAP@5 and MCL by +1.00% mAP@5. Against earlier projection-based baselines, gains are substantial: +8.44% R@1 over Pic2Word, +8.10% over SEARL, +7.30% over LinCIR, +5.94% over KEDs, and +6.74% over Context-I2W on CIRR R@1. On GeneCIS (Table 2), DistillCIR achieves 16.83% average R@1, surpassing CIReVL by 0.98% and Pic2Word by 5.68%, confirming its instruction-following strength on condition-sensitive retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two ZS-CIR lineages: projection-based models built on CLIP (Pic2Word, SEARLE, Slerp, LinCIR) and LLM-augmented methods (CIReVL, MCL) that invoke large language models at inference or as text encoders. Its key conceptual departure is the argument that LLM-based methods' advantage stems specifically from instruction-following capability rather than raw multimodal power, and that this capability can be distilled into a compact projection-based architecture. The dual-stream distillation strategy—combining a reasoning-level contrastive loss (inspired by chain-of-thought prompting) with a feature-level alignment loss from a separately trained MLLM (LLaVA)—is the novel architectural contribution, building on Pic2Word's projection module and LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating LLM dependency at inference while matching or exceeding 12B-parameter LLM-based baselines, DistillCIR offers a practical path to instruction-aware CIR in deployment-constrained settings (e.g., commercial search where proprietary LLM calls are prohibited). The instruction-following framing provides a useful diagnostic lens for the field: it suggests that future projection-based CIR models should explicitly target semantic understanding of modifications rather than merely fusing multimodal features. Open questions include whether the attention-map proxy for instruction awareness generalizes as a quantitative metric, whether the reliance on frontier LLMs (Claude 3.5/GPT-4o) for data synthesis limits reproducibility, and whether the distilled instruction awareness degrades on out-of-distribution modification types not well-represented in CC3M-derived triplets.
