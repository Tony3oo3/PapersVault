## Problem

Existing motion prediction methods commit to a single deterministic trajectory or dense video, ignoring the inherently multimodal and stochastic nature of real-world scene dynamics. This makes them ill-posed for representing the full space of possible outcomes and prevents direct access to uncertainty or interaction structure. The paper asks whether one can directly model the distribution of local motion conditioned on sparse, targeted interactions ('pokes') rather than rendering a single future.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251309/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (processed at 448² resolution by the encoder), a variable set of sparse pokes (each a 2D point paired with a 2D motion vector, ranging from 0 to 128 during training), and arbitrary off-grid query points. It outputs a full 2D Gaussian Mixture Model (with full, non-diagonal covariance matrices) per query point, giving direct access to multimodal motion distributions. Downstream, this yields dense flow fields (via parallel mean prediction or autoregressive sampling at 64² or 128² grids) and moving-part segmentation masks via KL-divergence thresholding between conditional and unconditional distributions.

## Method

The Flow Poke Transformer (FPT) treats each poke and query point as a token in a transformer, using Fourier embeddings for poke displacements and RoPE for arbitrary-precision positional encoding that supports off-grid, sparse flow supervision from optical tracking. Queries attend only to themselves and pokes (not to other queries) during self-attention, enabling parallel evaluation of many query distributions in one forward pass; a separate cross-attention to DINOv2-R-initialized ViT image tokens injects visual context. The output head directly parameterizes an N-component GMM with full 2×2 covariance matrices (via Cholesky factorization with soft-clipped positive diagonals), extending GIVT's diagonal-covariance formulation. Training minimizes negative log-likelihood under a novel query-causal attention mask—a teacher-forcing variant that nests poke sets (|P⁰|⊂|P¹|⊂…⊂|P^Np|) and reduces self-attention complexity from O(Np²·Nq²) to O(Np²+Np·Nq). Adaptive instance normalization (AdaIN) conditions the model on camera-staticity to handle open-world video without restricting training to static-camera clips.

## Results

On TalkingHead-1KH face motion generation, FPT (zero-shot, generic) achieves EPE of 7.64 with 1 poke, substantially outperforming Motion-I2V (29.08) and beating the face-specific InstantDrag (9.24), with LPIPS of 0.16 vs. 0.18 and 0.35 respectively. On Drag-A-Move articulated objects, the zero-shot model achieves EPE 12.74 (vs. Motion-I2V's 33.27), while the fine-tuned model reaches EPE 3.57, PCK 0.834, and mIoU 0.572 for moving part segmentation—surpassing in-domain DragAPart (EPE 9.69, mIoU 0.273) and PuppetMaster (EPE 9.62, mIoU 0.112) by wide margins. The model also demonstrates well-calibrated uncertainty, with predicted standard deviation correlating to true EPE at Pearson ρ=0.64.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against InstantDrag (face-specific, trained on CelebV-Text), FPT wins on EPE at every poke count (e.g., 7.64 vs. 9.24 at 1 poke; 2.51 vs. 7.29 at 100 pokes) and on LPIPS for warped images, but is slightly behind on PCK at low poke counts (0.150 vs. 0.193 at 1 poke), catching up and surpassing it at 100 pokes (0.264 vs. 0.212). Against Motion-I2V (generic, zero-shot), FPT dominates on both benchmarks: EPE 7.64 vs. 29.08 on faces and 12.74 vs. 33.27 on articulated objects. Against the in-domain baselines DragAPart and PuppetMaster on Drag-A-Move, the zero-shot FPT is competitive on EPE (12.74 vs. 9.69/9.62) and already wins on mIoU for segmentation (0.287 vs. 0.273/0.112); after 30k steps of fine-tuning, FPT outperforms both by a large margin (EPE 3.57, PCK 0.834, mIoU 0.572). The KL-divergence-based segmentation score is a key differentiator: without it, FPT still achieves mIoU 0.415, well above prior SOTA, but the divergence-based approach nearly doubles the score to 0.572.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GIVT autoregressive transformer framework (which parameterizes outputs as GMMs with diagonal covariances) to full 2×2 covariance matrices, repurposing it for continuous motion distribution prediction. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant line of poke-conditioned motion work (ipoke, DragAPart, PuppetMaster, Motion-I2V, InstantDrag) that commits to a single RGB video or flow realization, instead directly modeling the conditional distribution p(f(q)|P, I) as an explicit GMM. The query-causal attention mechanism is a novel training technique that reduces computational complexity when training with nested poke sets, and the use of AdaIN to condition on camera staticity is a practical solution to a data-quality challenge in open-world video training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making motion distributions directly accessible rather than requiring sampling from a black-box generative model, FPT enables downstream capabilities (uncertainty quantification, multimodal reasoning, KL-divergence-based part segmentation) that are architecturally unavailable to prior methods. The 25 ms per-prediction latency and 160k predictions/sec throughput make this practical for interactive and real-time applications. Key limitations include poor generalization to stylized content (cartoons), inconsistent shadow handling, and the 2D-only formulation; open questions include whether the distributional approach scales to 3D scenes or longer temporal horizons, and whether the GMM parameterization is sufficient for highly complex multimodal motion spaces.
