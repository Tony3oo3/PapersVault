## Problem

Language-guided dexterous grasp generation achieves strong performance on seen object categories but fails to generalize to unseen categories in the open world, due to the huge semantic gap between high-level natural language instructions and low-level hand joint configurations. This paper formalizes the open-set language-guided dexterous grasp task and identifies that the core difficulty is bridging this language-to-action gap without category-specific training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252143/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a scene point cloud (merged from five RGB-D cameras), a rendered RGB image (textured via Paint3D for MLLM processing), and a free-form language command. GPT-4o first parses the command and image into structured key cues (object category, intention, contacting part, and a discretized six-direction grasp vector in world coordinates). The final output is a dexterous hand pose parameterized as global rotation, translation, and joint angles for Shadow Hand or Leap Hand, along with an intermediate per-point affordance scalar field indicating graspable regions.

## Method

The core contribution is a Generalizable-Instructive Affordance: a per-point scalar map representing the union of all graspable regions that share the same semantic intention, contact part, and direction, smoothed via Gaussian filtering. This affordance is coarse enough to generalize across categories (aligned with category-agnostic cues) yet fine enough to guide high-DOF hand placement. Two cascaded conditional flow matching models are trained independently: Affordance Flow Matching (AFM) uses PointNet++ + CLIP + Perceiver IO Transformer to denoise an affordance map conditioned on language and direction, and Grasp Flow Matching (GFM) generates the hand pose conditioned on the predicted affordance, language, and direction, with losses on pose regression, hand chamfer distance, and fingertip positions. A non-parametric test-time optimization stage then refines the generated pose using affordance contact constraints, penetration penalties, and joint limits, avoiding the training instability that penetration losses cause in the generative stage.

## Results

On the open-set dexterous grasp benchmark (Open Set A), AffordDexGrasp achieves FID 0.231, Chamfer Distance 3.81, Top-1 R-Precision 0.480, success rate 45.1%, Q1 0.0531, and penetration 0.293 cm, outperforming all baselines on every intention-consistency and quality metric. On Open Set B, the method further improves to FID 0.162, CD 2.95, Top-1 0.532, success rate 38.9%, Q1 0.0352, and penetration 0.361 cm. In the close-set setting (Table 3), AffordDexGrasp reaches FID 0.0286, CD 1.13, Top-1 0.779, Q1 0.0562, and penetration 0.193 cm, again leading all competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AffordDexGrasp outperforms all five full-pipeline baselines on every intention-consistency metric (FID, CD, Top-1/2/3 R-Precision) and every quality metric (Q1, penetration) across both open-set splits. The closest competitor, DexGYS [51], is surpassed by a margin of 0.066 FID and 0.163 Top-1 on Open Set A, and 0.196 FID and 0.238 Top-1 on Open Set B, while also achieving lower penetration (0.293 vs 0.362 cm on A; 0.361 vs 0.691 cm on B). Contact-based methods (ContactGen [28], Contact2Grasp [19]) degrade severely on unseen categories, with Top-1 R-Precision dropping to 0.164–0.257, confirming that fine-grained contact maps do not generalize. SceneDiffuser [12] and GraspCVAE [40] show moderate generalization but remain 0.07–0.14 FID and 0.09–0.24 Top-1 behind AffordDexGrasp. In the ablation comparison of optimization strategies (Table 2), the proposed affordance-guided optimization outperforms ContactNet [17] on FID (0.231 vs 0.349), CD (3.81 vs 12.3), and Top-1 (0.480 vs 0.279), though ContactNet achieves slightly lower penetration (0.124 vs 0.293 cm), indicating a quality–penetration trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends language-guided dexterous grasp generation (DexGYS, SemGrasp, Text2Grasp) by introducing an intermediate 'generalizable-instructive affordance' representation that sits between fine-grained contact maps (ContactGen, Contact2Grasp) and coarse object part segmentation (PartSLIP). The generative backbone is conditional flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022), applied in two cascaded stages: Affordance Flow Matching for affordance prediction and Grasp Flow Matching for pose generation, with a Perceiver IO Transformer as the interaction module. A key departure from prior data-driven approaches is the use of GPT-4o as an MLLM for intention pre-understanding, extracting structured cues (category, part, direction) before feeding them to the generative models, and a non-parametric affordance-guided test-time optimization that replaces learned refinement networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: dexterous hands with 16+ DOF have far fewer open-set language-guided methods than parallel grippers, and the high cost of dexterous hand data collection makes open-set generalization essential. The clean ablation showing that contact maps overfit to seen categories while part segmentation is too coarse to guide high-DOF hands validates the affordance-as-bridge hypothesis. However, real-world validation is limited to 10 attempts per object with a single arm-hand configuration, and the multi-stage pipeline (MLLM → AFM → GFM → optimization) introduces compounding error risk and latency that remain unaddressed. Open questions include whether the affordance representation transfers to multi-object scenes, dynamic manipulation, or truly novel object geometries beyond tabletop settings.
