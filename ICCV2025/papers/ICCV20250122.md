## Problem

Selective State Space Models (SSMs) offer linear-complexity sequence modeling, but point cloud videos lack consistent spatio-temporal ordering, fundamentally conflicting with SSMs' unidirectional scanning paradigm. Existing approaches like Mamba4D rely on temporally sequential scanning, which restricts each point's interaction to preceding frames and suffers from long-range attenuation of semantically similar but temporally distant points, while also discarding local geometric structure during serialization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250122/cropped_page0_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unordered point cloud video sequence of T frames × N points (e.g., 24–36 frames, 2048 points per frame) with 3D coordinates and learned features, and produces either a global action class label (for recognition on MSR-Action3D and NTU RGB+D) or per-point 4D semantic labels (for segmentation on Synthia 4D). The input is consumed as a raw 4D point set with no pre-imposed spatial or temporal ordering; the model internally constructs the ordering via prompt-guided clustering and Hilbert sorting before feeding the serialized sequence into the SSM backbone.

## Method

UST-SSM unifies spatio-temporal modeling through three synergistic components. First, Spatio-Temporal Selection Scanning (STSS) uses a lightweight PointNet-based prompt network to assign K semantic categories to points, clustering spatio-temporally distant but semantically similar points together; within each cluster, 3D Hilbert curve sorting preserves local geometry, and inter-cluster chronological ordering maintains motion continuity. Second, Spatio-Temporal Structure Aggregation (STSA) reconstructs disrupted local neighborhoods via 4D KNN (spatial coordinates + learned temporal embeddings) and propagates features through attention-inspired exponential pooling, compensating for geometric detail lost during serialization. Third, Temporal Interaction Sampling (TIS) applies multi-stride sampling (stride-1 aggregation followed by stride-2 downsampling) to expand the temporal receptive field and reuse non-anchor frames, enabling each anchor frame to access features from all preceding frames. The serialized sequence is processed by a bidirectional Mamba (ST-SSM) block interleaved with STSA, followed by an MLP head for classification or segmentation.

## Results

On MSR-Action3D, UST-SSM achieves 94.77% accuracy with 24 frames and 95.12% with 36 frames, outperforming the best Transformer (LeaF, 93.84% at 24 frames) and the prior SSM method (Mamba4D, 93.23% at 36 frames). On NTU RGB+D, UST-SSM reaches 92.1% cross-subject and 97.8% cross-view accuracy, surpassing PST-Transformer (91.0%/96.4%) by 1.1% and 1.4% respectively. On Synthia 4D for 4D semantic segmentation, UST-SSM attains 84.06% mIoU with 3 frames, edging out PST-Transformer (83.95%) and Mamba4D (83.35%). In terms of efficiency, UST-SSM uses only 0.138M parameters and 2.403 GB GPU memory (36 frames, batch 32), roughly half the memory of PST-Transformer (4.072 GB) while delivering 4% higher accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UST-SSM outperforms all compared baselines across all three datasets. On MSR-Action3D (24 frames), it beats the strongest Transformer baseline LeaF (93.84%) by 0.93% and the best CNN PointCMP (93.27%) by 1.5%, while also surpassing the closest SSM competitor Mamba4D (92.68%) by 2.09%. On NTU RGB+D, UST-SSM leads PST-Transformer by 1.1% in cross-subject (92.1% vs. 91.0%) and 1.4% in cross-view (97.8% vs. 96.4%), and exceeds P4Transformer by 1.9% in cross-subject. On Synthia 4D, the improvement over PST-Transformer is marginal (+0.11% mIoU) but the gap over Mamba4D is more substantial (+0.71%). Notably, Transformer-based methods (P4Transformer, PST-Transformer) suffer significant accuracy drops at 36 frames (82.81% and 91.15% respectively), whereas UST-SSM continues to improve (95.12%), highlighting its advantage in long-sequence modeling. In efficiency, UST-SSM uses 52% fewer parameters and 41% less GPU memory than PST-Transformer while achieving 4% higher accuracy on the 36-frame setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the selective state space model (Mamba) lineage from 3D point clouds (Mamba3D, PointMamba) and the direct predecessor Mamba4D to a unified 4D spatio-temporal setting for point cloud videos. The key conceptual departure from Mamba4D is replacing its temporally sequential scanning with prompt-guided semantic clustering (STSS), motivated by the observation that temporally distant but semantically similar points suffer from long-range attenuation in unidirectional SSMs. The Hilbert-curve intra-cluster ordering is borrowed from Point Transformer V3, and the segmentation experiments build on P4Transformer as a backbone, replacing its attention layers with the proposed ST-SSM block.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The linear-complexity SSM backbone with only 0.138M parameters outperforming quadratic-complexity Transformers (0.289–0.834M parameters) by 4–12 accuracy points while using less GPU memory is a practically significant efficiency story for long-sequence point cloud video understanding. The prompt-guided clustering strategy for organizing unordered 4D points into SSM-compatible sequences is a reusable idea that could transfer to other unordered-to-sequential modeling tasks. Open questions include whether the lightweight prompt network generalizes to unseen action categories or very long sequences, whether the fixed number of prompt clusters (K=8 or 16) is optimal across domains, and whether the approach extends to other 4D tasks beyond action recognition and semantic segmentation.
