## Problem

Existing AI weather forecasting models assume all atmospheric variables are available synchronously, requiring full retraining from scratch when new variables (e.g., satellite-derived aerosol data) are introduced—a process that can take weeks on hundreds of GPUs. This paper introduces incremental weather forecasting as a new paradigm and tackles the catastrophic forgetting problem that arises when expanding a pre-trained model to accommodate additional variables without degrading performance on the original ones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250405/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a spatiotemporal atmospheric state on a 721×1440 grid (0.25° resolution) comprising 65 upper-air channels (Z, Q, U, V, T each at 13 pressure levels from 50 to 1000 hPa) and 5 surface channels (u10, v10, t2m, msl, sp at a single level), producing 6-hour-step predictions up to 120 hours (5 days) ahead. In the incremental setting, the initial stage trains on only the 65 upper-air channels, while the incremental stage adds the 5 surface channels; the model must predict all 70 channels jointly at inference. Ablation experiments use a coarser 128×256 grid at 1.5° resolution.

## Method

VA-MoE augments a standard Transformer backbone with per-variable Channel-Adaptive Experts (CAEs) alongside a Shared Expert. Each CAE receives a one-hot variable index embedding that is channel-wise multiplied with the input features, passed through an MLP and TopK gating to select the most relevant spatial channels for that variable type, and then processed by a variable-specific expert sub-network. During incremental training, all pre-trained CAEs and the encoder/decoder segments corresponding to old variables are frozen, while newly added CAEs, the shared expert, and the index encoder are trained on the expanded variable set—preventing catastrophic forgetting without auxiliary regularization losses. The training objective combines a dynamic prediction loss that assigns learnable per-channel weights (prioritizing fast-transient fields like temperature over slow-changing fields like geopotential) with a reconstruction loss that enforces the encoder-decoder path to handle feature encoding/decoding, freeing the intermediate Transformer blocks to focus on learning atmospheric dynamics. The full model has 665M parameters, of which only 137M are trainable in the incremental stage.

## Results

On the ERA5 dataset (0.25°, 721×1440), VA-MoE achieves RMSE of 1.03 K for T2M and 1.41 m/s for U10 at 72h, competitive with GraphCast (0.94 K, 1.51 m/s) and superior to Pangu-Weather (1.09 K, 1.63 m/s) and FourCastNet (1.02 K, 2.08 m/s). For upper-air variables, VA-MoE attains 134.63 m²/s² RMSE on Z500 at 72h and 0.76 K on T500, outperforming Pangu-Weather (167.90, 0.94) and IFS (154.08, 0.98) while remaining slightly behind GraphCast (125.42, 0.87). The incremental variant (VA-MoE-IL) trains only 137M new parameters on 20 years of data yet retains upper-air accuracy within ~1% of the full model, confirming minimal catastrophic forgetting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VA-MoE outperforms Pangu-Weather, FourCastNet, ClimaX, and IFS across nearly all surface variables and horizons, with the largest gains on U10 (1.41 vs. 1.63–2.08 m/s at 72h) and V10 (1.46 vs. 1.68–2.11 m/s). Against GraphCast, VA-MoE is slightly worse on T2M (1.03 vs. 0.94 K at 72h) and Z500 (134.63 vs. 125.42 m²/s²) but wins on U10, V10, MSL, and SP at 72h and 120h. For upper-air T500, VA-MoE achieves 0.76 K at 72h, beating Pangu-Weather (0.94), IFS (0.98), and FuXi (0.99) while trailing GraphCast (0.87) by 0.11 K. The incremental variant (VA-MoE-IL) trained on only 20 years of data with 137M new parameters degrades by less than 0.1 K on T2M and 0.06 m/s on U10 relative to the full model, demonstrating that freezing pretrained experts effectively prevents catastrophic forgetting. In the architecture ablation (Table 4, 1.5°), VA-MoE (665M) outperforms ViT+MoE (1113M) on Z500 by ~10% at 72h (139.02 vs. 169.61 m²/s²), confirming that channel-adaptive routing is more parameter-efficient than uniform MoE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Mixture-of-Experts paradigm from the incremental/continual learning literature (Expert Gate, Lifelong-MoE, MoE-Adapter) to a fundamentally different axis of expansion: rather than adding new classes or tasks, it adds new atmospheric variables to a weather forecasting model. It departs from existing data-driven weather forecasters (Pangu-Weather, GraphCast, FourCastNet, Stormer) that assume a fixed, synchronous set of input variables and require full retraining when the variable set changes. The channel-adaptive expert with variable index embeddings and TopK gating is a novel mechanism not present in prior MoE weather models (e.g., EWMoE), which use uniform or learned routing without explicit variable-type specialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial for operational meteorology: eliminating the need for full retraining (Pangu-Weather required 64 days on 192 V100 GPUs per cycle) when new observational data streams become available. However, the experimental scope is narrow—only one incremental stage (5 upper-air → 5 surface variables) on a single dataset (ERA5), and the 'incremental' setting is somewhat constrained since the surface variables are known in advance. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multiple sequential incremental stages, handles truly novel variable types (e.g., aerosol, CO2), and generalizes to regional or high-resolution forecasting. The paper also does not ablate the dynamic prediction loss or the reconstruction loss separately, leaving their individual contributions unclear.
