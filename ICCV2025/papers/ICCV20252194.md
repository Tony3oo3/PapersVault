## Problem

Existing task-oriented dexterous grasping methods rely on conditional generative models trained on fixed object categories and struggle to generalize to unseen objects due to large shape variations and delicate joint-level parameters. The core gap is the lack of a mechanism to transfer fine-grained grasping knowledge (which part to contact, how fingers should be arranged) across object categories without requiring new annotations for each unseen object.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252194/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form natural language task instruction (e.g., 'hold the kettle to pour some tea') and a 3D point cloud of the target object (2048 surface points) as input. It additionally renders an RGB image of the object from a selected viewpoint for MLLM-based affordance inference and GLIP-based part localization. The output is a full MANO hand pose G = (T, R, Θ) with 3D translation, SO(3) rotation, and 16 joint angles, which must be physically stable (low simulation displacement), collision-free, and semantically aligned with the instructed contact part.

## Method

G-DexGrasp is a three-stage retrieval-augmented generation pipeline. First, GPT-4o infers the affordance type and contact part name from the instruction and rendered image, GLIP localizes the part in 3D, and K-means clustering over the AffordPose dataset retrieves a generalizable prior Γ = (P, Θ̂, C) comprising the localized contact part, a representative intrinsic pose, and a Gaussian contact-map distribution. Second, a part-aware CVAE (PointNet++ encoder over the contact-part point cloud, conditioned on part size, one-hot cluster ID, and rest-object centroid) predicts extrinsic hand parameters, trained with reconstruction, KL, and penetration losses. Third, a two-stage differentiable optimizer refines the pose: the first stage adjusts only extrinsics using prior-guided contact and contact-map losses to escape poor local minima, while the second stage fine-tunes all parameters with the full objective including force closure, bidirectional penetration, joint limits, and self-penetration terms.

## Results

On unseen object categories, G-DexGrasp achieves 71.6% Part Accuracy (vs. 42.5–46.5% for baselines) and 4.27 cm simulation displacement (vs. 7.61–9.93 cm), demonstrating substantial gains in both semantic alignment and grasp stability. The solid intersection volume drops to 2.94 cm³ on unseen objects (vs. 7.98–10.55 cm³ for baselines), and the perceptual score reaches 3.93/5 (vs. 0.88–1.72). With ground-truth part segmentation (Ours*), Part Accuracy further improves to 96.3% on unseen categories, confirming the transferability of the retrieval-based prior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

G-DexGrasp outperforms all three baselines on every metric for unseen object categories. Against AffordPose [14], the largest gap appears in Part Accuracy (71.6% vs. 42.5%) and simulation displacement (4.27 vs. 9.93 cm), indicating that affordance-type conditioning alone cannot transfer to novel shapes. Against GrabNet† [35] (BERT-encoded text instructions), G-DexGrasp improves Part Accuracy by 26.1 points and reduces intersection volume from 11.07 to 2.94 cm³. Against GraspTTA† [15], which uses a more advanced contact-consistency network, G-DexGrasp still leads by 25.1 points in Part Accuracy and 3.34 cm in simulation displacement. On seen categories the margins are smaller but consistent: G-DexGrasp achieves the best or near-best values across all five metrics, with the only exception being Perceptual Score where AffordPose edges ahead slightly (3.84 vs. 3.72). The Ours* variant with ground-truth part segmentation pushes Part Accuracy to 96.3% on unseen objects, suggesting that the remaining gap is largely attributable to part-segmentation quality rather than the grasping pipeline itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) paradigm from NLP for 3D dexterous grasping synthesis, departing from prior task-oriented grasping methods (AffordPose, Text2Grasp, SemGrasp) that rely on a single conditional generative model to handle all objects and instructions. It draws on the generalizable-parts concept (GAPartNet) and contact-map-based intermediate representations (ContactOpt, ContactGrasp) to define what constitutes a transferable grasping prior—affordance type plus contact part geometry—across object categories. The CVAE generation stage builds on the part-conditioned architectures in DexGraspNet and ContactOpt, while the refinement stage extends differentiable force-closure optimization (DexGraspNet, ContactOpt) with a novel prior-distribution regularization term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underexplored gap: task-oriented dexterous grasping that generalizes to unseen object categories without retraining, which prior conditional-generative approaches struggle with. The RAG formulation is scalable—the grasping database can be extended with new interaction types without retraining the generation network—making it a practical recipe for building increasingly capable grasping systems. However, the approach depends on open-vocabulary 3D part segmentation for unseen objects (currently handled via GLIP + depth projection or manual annotation), and the authors acknowledge that tasks requiring very delicate hand postures remain underrepresented in the training data, limiting applicability to fine manipulation.
