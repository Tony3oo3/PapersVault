## Problem

Existing diffusion-based 3D pre-training methods (e.g., PointDif) rely on 3D diffusion models trained on limited-size 3D datasets, which constrains their ability to learn robust and generalizable representations. This paper investigates whether the rich semantic knowledge encoded in large-scale text-to-image diffusion models, particularly Stable Diffusion, can be transferred to the 3D domain to overcome this data bottleneck.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251800/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D point clouds (1,024 points grouped into 64 KNN-based patches of 32 points) and their corresponding rendered RGB images (30 evenly-spaced views per ShapeNet object). In Stage I, noisy rendered images are fed into the frozen SD UNet with point-cloud-derived tokens as cross-attention conditions; in Stage II, noise-free rendered images serve as input while point-cloud tokens condition feature extraction from the SD down-sampling layers. The final output is a pre-trained 3D transformer backbone whose features are aligned to SD intermediate representations, ready for fine-tuning on classification, segmentation, or detection heads.

## Method

PointSD employs a two-stage strategy. In Stage I, the SD model's CLIP text encoder is replaced by a trainable 3D encoder that projects point clouds into token sequences H; these tokens replace text features in the UNet's cross-attention layers (only W_k and W_v are optimized while W_q and the UNet remain frozen), establishing a point-to-image diffusion model trained with a standard denoising loss at high time steps (t ∈ [500, 1000]). In Stage II, inspired by VPD, noise-free rendered images are passed through the frozen point-to-image model with point-cloud tokens as conditions, and features are extracted from the down-sampling layers (rather than up-sampling layers, to avoid low-level texture contamination). A separate 3D backbone and a transformer projector are then trained to minimize L2 distance between projected 3D features and the extracted SD features. A patch-level mixing augmentation (combining two point clouds and stitching their rendered images) is applied with 50% probability in both stages to increase sample diversity and force the backbone to learn discriminative representations.

## Results

On ScanObjectNN PB-T50-RS, PointSD achieves 90.08% accuracy, surpassing the closest diffusion-based method PointDif (87.61%) by 2.47% and the single-modal SOTA Point-FEMAE (90.22%) by a narrow margin. On ModelNet40, PointSD reaches 93.7% classification accuracy, outperforming Point-MAE (93.2%) and Point-BERT (92.7%). For few-shot learning (5-way 20-shot), PointSD achieves 99.0% accuracy, and for 3D object detection on ScanNetV2, it attains 42.4 AP@0.5, outperforming TAP (41.4) and the 3DETR baseline (37.9). On part segmentation (ShapeNetPart), PointSD scores 86.1% mIoU_I, slightly behind I2P-MAE (86.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PointSD substantially outperforms the closest diffusion-based baseline PointDif across all three ScanObjectNN settings (95.18 vs. 93.29 on OBJ-BG, 93.63 vs. 91.91 on OBJ-ONLY, 90.08 vs. 87.61 on PB-T50-RS), validating the hypothesis that a large-scale pre-trained SD model overcomes the data limitations of a 3D diffusion model. Against the label-free cross-modal methods I2P-MAE and ACT, PointSD wins on OBJ-BG (95.18 vs. 94.15 and 93.29) and OBJ-ONLY (93.63 vs. 91.57 and 91.91), but trails I2P-MAE by 0.03% on PB-T50-RS (90.08 vs. 90.11). Compared to the label-using SOTA ReCon*, PointSD ties on OBJ-BG and OBJ-ONLY (both 95.18 and 93.63) but falls 0.55% short on PB-T50-RS (90.08 vs. 90.63) and 0.4% on ModelNet40 (93.7 vs. 94.1), while using roughly half the parameters (22.1M vs. 43.6M). On part segmentation, PointSD (86.1% mIoU_I) lags behind I2P-MAE (86.8%) and Joint-MAE (86.3%), which the authors attribute to their patch-level reconstruction supervision versus PointSD's object-level alignment. For 3D object detection on ScanNetV2, PointSD achieves the best AP@0.5 of 42.4, beating TAP (41.4) and MaskPoint (40.6) despite pre-training on the more challenging ShapeNet domain rather than ScanNetV2 itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

PointSD extends the diffusion-based 3D pre-training paradigm introduced by PointDif, which conditions a 3D diffusion model's denoising on extracted 3D features. The key departure is replacing the purpose-trained 3D diffusion model with the frozen Stable Diffusion model, swapping its CLIP text encoder for a 3D encoder so that point clouds directly condition image denoising via cross-attention. For feature extraction, the paper adapts VPD's strategy of feeding noise-free images through SD to obtain semantic features, but substitutes class-based text prompts with point-cloud-derived tokens. This positions PointSD between CLIP-based cross-modal methods (ULIP, ACT, ReCon, I2P-MAE) and single-modal 3D diffusion methods, leveraging a large-scale 2D generative model without requiring category labels during pre-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the rich semantics encoded in a large-scale text-to-image diffusion model can be transferred to 3D representation learning without training a dedicated 3D diffusion model, offering a practical recipe for overcoming the 3D data bottleneck. However, the approach depends on rendered multi-view images as an intermediate representation, which may limit applicability to raw or sparse point clouds (e.g., LiDAR scenes). Part segmentation performance lags behind methods using patch-level reconstruction, suggesting that object-level alignment alone is insufficient for dense prediction tasks. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to larger 3D datasets, whether newer diffusion backbones (SDXL, video diffusion) yield further gains, and whether the rendered-image dependency can be eliminated.
