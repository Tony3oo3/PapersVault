## Problem

Current driving world models are built on video diffusion architectures that specialize in visual generation but cannot naturally incorporate other modalities such as action sequences, making seamless integration of world modeling and end-to-end planning in a single differentiable framework largely unresolved. This paper asks whether autoregressive transformers, which natively handle sequential multi-modal data, can unify both driving simulation and trajectory planning into one sequence modeling problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251517/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes front-camera images (900×1600, resized to 288×512) that are pre-tokenized by a VQ-VAE into 576 or 2304 discrete tokens per frame, alongside frame-to-frame relative driving actions (Δx, Δy, Δθ) quantized into 128 bins per component. These are interleaved frame-by-frame into a single driving language sequence. The model outputs future image tokens (decoded back to video via the VQ-VAE decoder) and future action tokens (unbinned and integrated via 2D transformation matrices to recover absolute trajectories), all produced by a single next-token prediction pass.

## Method

DrivingGPT formulates driving as next-token prediction over a unified multimodal vocabulary of size D+3M (16,384 image tokens + 3×128 action bins), where image tokens from VQ-VAE and action tokens from component-wise binning are interleaved per frame. A key design choice is using frame-to-frame relative actions rather than long-horizon absolute trajectories, preventing the causal model from simply copying history actions. The architecture is a Llama-like 110M-parameter transformer with SwishGLU FFN layers and frame-wise 1D rotary position embeddings applied to both image and action tokens, trained with standard cross-entropy loss. This single objective simultaneously learns world modeling p(z_t | z_<t, q_<t) and end-to-end planning p(q_t | z_≤t, q_<t) as sub-tasks, requiring no auxiliary supervision or separate heads. At inference, guided sampling masks foreign-modality logits to prevent cross-modal token confusion, and long videos are generated autoregressively in 16-frame chunks conditioned on the previous 8 frames.

## Results

On the NAVSIM end-to-end planning benchmark, DrivingGPT achieves a PDMS of 82.4, outperforming the ResNet-50+MLP baseline (77.8) and matching the state-of-the-art LAW planner (82.7) without any auxiliary supervision. For action-conditioned video generation on navtest, DrivingGPT achieves FVD 142.61 and FID 12.78, substantially outperforming the fine-tuned SVD baseline (FVD 227.54, FID 24.03) and CogVideoX (FVD 848.87, FID 31.78). In long-video generation (64 frames), DrivingGPT maintains FVD 506.95 and FID 20.45 while SVD degrades to FVD 1079.28 and FID 46.45, demonstrating the autoregressive architecture's advantage in temporal consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For video generation, DrivingGPT (FVD 142.61, FID 12.78) decisively outperforms both the fine-tuned SVD baseline (FVD 227.54, FID 24.03) and CogVideoX (FVD 848.87, FID 31.78) on navtest, with the gap widening dramatically at 64 frames where SVD's FVD nearly doubles to 1079.28 while DrivingGPT stays at 506.95. For end-to-end planning, DrivingGPT (PDMS 82.4) beats the ResNet-50+MLP baseline by 4.6 points and is within 0.3 points of LAW (82.7), the strongest no-auxiliary-supervision baseline, despite using only compressed VQ-VAE tokens rather than raw pixels. DrivingGPT trails TransFuser (84.6) by 2.2 PDMS points, but TransFuser relies on auxiliary supervision (perception and prediction losses) that DrivingGPT explicitly avoids. In the tokenizer ablation, LlamaGen (rFVD 68.40, rFID 5.67) is selected over OpenMAGVIT2 (rFVD 96.32, rFID 6.70) and Cosmos Tokenizer (rFVD 178.50, rFID 27.12) as the best visual tokenizer. The ablation replacing predicted actions with copied history actions shows DrivingGPT truly learns driving: copying the longitudinal action x drops PDMS from 82.4 to 53.5, confirming the model is not merely extrapolating past maneuvers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant diffusion-based paradigm for driving world models (GAIA-1, Vista, Drive-WM) by adopting an autoregressive transformer with next-token prediction, drawing on the LlamaGen/Llama architecture and VQ-VAE tokenization from the image generation literature. The key conceptual departure is treating driving as a unified 'language' that interleaves discrete image tokens and quantized action tokens, enabling a single model to simultaneously perform world modeling (future frame prediction) and end-to-end planning (trajectory generation) — a unification no prior driving world model has attempted. GAIA-1 is the closest prior work in using autoregressive next-token prediction for driving, but it only predicts visual tokens and relies on a separate diffusion decoder, whereas DrivingGPT jointly predicts both modalities within one vocabulary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a compact 110M-parameter model trained from scratch (no pretrained visual encoder) can achieve planning performance (PDMS 82.4) comparable to state-of-the-art pixel-based planners while also producing high-quality action-conditioned videos, suggesting that the autoregressive unification is a viable and efficient path toward differentiable model-based planning. However, the planning results are only on par with existing methods rather than clearly surpassing them, and the evaluation is limited to open-loop, front-camera-only, no-ego-status settings. Open questions include whether this framework scales to multi-sensor inputs (LiDAR, surround cameras), whether it can support true closed-loop model-based planning with search, and whether the data-quality-over-quantity finding generalizes beyond NAVSIM's curated scenarios.
