## Problem

Existing amodal segmentation methods cannot accept textual user queries or reason about implicit scene purposes, while reasoning segmentation methods like LISA are limited to predicting only visible regions and fail under complex occlusions. No dataset or model exists that jointly performs amodal shape completion and language-grounded reasoning in a single conversational framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and a free-form text question that does not directly name target objects, requiring the model to infer which objects are relevant. It outputs a natural-language answer embedding [SEG] tokens at object mentions, along with paired visible and amodal segmentation masks for every referenced object. A single answer can reference multiple objects simultaneously, and the system supports multi-round conversation.

## Method

AURA builds on LLaVA-7B-v1-1 (fine-tuned with LoRA) for language understanding and SAM ViT-H for visual feature extraction, connected through a Prompt Encoder that refines [SEG] token embeddings into segmentation prompts. Two occlusion-specific modules are the key architectural contribution: an Occlusion Condition Encoder that transforms the refined embedding into an occlusion-aware embedding and predicts a scalar occlusion rate, and a Spatial Occlusion Encoder that takes both predicted visible and amodal masks and produces a spatial map (visible=1, occluded=2, background=0) enforcing geometric consistency between them. Training uses a composite loss combining cross-entropy for text, cross-entropy plus Dice for both mask types, MSE for occlusion rate, and cross-entropy for the spatial occlusion map. The AmodalReasonSeg dataset (3,143 images, 35,494 QA pairs) is generated semi-automatically from COCOA-cls using ChatGPT-4o with human cross-checking.

## Results

On the AmodalReasonSeg dataset, AURA achieves the best performance across all four metrics: 47.76 gIoU and 47.32 cIoU for amodal masks, and 51.31 gIoU and 55.38 cIoU for visible masks. The closest MLLM-based competitor, PSALM, scores 46.81/47.03 (amodal) and 50.64/55.19 (visible), yielding AURA a margin of +0.95 gIoU on amodal and +0.19 cIoU on visible. Traditional amodal segmentation methods (VRSP, C2F-Seg, SD-Amodal) trail significantly, with the best (SD-Amodal) reaching only 20.87 amodal gIoU, highlighting the advantage of language-guided reasoning for target-specific segmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AURA outperforms all nine baselines on every metric. Against the strongest MLLM-based competitor PSALM (ECCV'24), AURA gains +0.95 amodal gIoU (47.76 vs 46.81) and +0.19 visible cIoU (55.38 vs 55.19), with the gap widening on visible gIoU (+0.67). Compared to GLaMM (CVPR'24), AURA leads by +2.01 amodal gIoU and +1.24 visible cIoU. The earlier reasoning-segmentation methods LISA and LLM-Seg (both CVPR'24) are substantially behind, scoring 23.05 and 24.62 amodal gIoU respectively, roughly 23–25 points below AURA, largely because they segment only visible regions and handle a single object per query. Traditional amodal segmentation methods (VRSP, C2F-Seg, SD-Amodal) perform worst (≤20.87 amodal gIoU) since they cannot interpret textual questions and must segment all objects indiscriminately. The ablation baseline (AURA without occlusion encoders) scores 43.55 amodal gIoU, confirming that the Occlusion Condition Encoder and Spatial Occlusion Encoder contribute +4.21 gIoU on amodal masks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: amodal segmentation (VRSP, C2F-Seg, SD-Amodal) and LLM-based reasoning segmentation (LISA, LLM-Seg, GSVA, PSALM). It departs from both by introducing a unified task that requires simultaneously understanding a user's implicit question and predicting complete amodal masks, rather than either predicting amodal shapes without language interaction or reasoning about visible regions only. Architecturally, it repurposes SAM's ViT-H backbone and mask decoder alongside LLaVA-7B with LoRA, adding two purpose-built occlusion encoders (a global Occlusion Condition Encoder and a spatial Spatial Occlusion Encoder) that have no direct counterpart in either prior line of work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a genuine gap by providing the first dataset and method for amodal reasoning segmentation, enabling interactive, question-driven prediction of occluded object shapes in daily-life scenes. However, the dataset is relatively small (3,143 images, 35K QA pairs) and derived from a single source (COCOA-cls), limiting generalizability claims. The performance gains over the strongest MLLM baselines (PSALM, GLaMM) are modest (~1 gIoU), and the method's reliance on a 7B–13B LLM with SAM-ViT-H makes real-time deployment impractical. Open questions include whether the occlusion encoders transfer to video or 3D settings, whether multi-round conversation truly improves amodal prediction, and whether the task generalizes beyond COCO-derived indoor/outdoor scenes.
