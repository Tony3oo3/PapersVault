## Problem

Learned image compression models achieve strong rate-distortion performance but suffer from high computational complexity (excessive parameters, FLOPs, and decoding latency) that severely limits practical deployment. Knowledge distillation has proven effective in many vision domains, yet its application to LIC remains underexplored due to unique challenges: the VAE-based architecture, pronounced channel-wise energy compaction in intermediate features, and the need to simultaneously manage reconstruction quality and bitrate regularization.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images as input and produces a compressed bitstream plus a reconstructed image, following the standard LIC encoder-quantizer-decoder pipeline with a hyper-prior entropy model. Training uses 256×256 patches from Flickr2W, while evaluation spans three datasets at different resolutions: Kodak (768×512), Tecnick (1200×1200), and CLIC professional validation (2K). The student model KDIC is constructed by pruning the S2CFormer-Hybrid teacher, reducing first-stage channels from 192 to 128, block counts from {3,8,8} to {3,5,5}, and FFN expansion factors from 4 to 2.

## Method

The paper proposes SMoDi (Stage-wise Modular Distillation), which decomposes the LIC pipeline into six independent sub-tasks (three encoding stages and three decoding stages, split at resolution-change nodes) and trains each student stage against the corresponding frozen teacher stage, avoiding error propagation and gradient degradation from joint training. Two key design choices enable effective transfer: (1) Teacher-Guided Student Model Construction, a pruning-like strategy that builds the student by selectively removing blocks, channels, and FFN width from the teacher to maintain architectural consistency and avoid cross-architecture capacity gaps; and (2) Implicit End-to-end Supervision, which replaces explicit channel-wise feature alignment with the end-to-end RD loss, allowing the student to adaptively learn energy compaction patterns and regulate bitrate without requiring matched channel counts. The same stage-wise principle is applied to the SCCTX entropy model, distilling the channel-context and parameter-aggregation modules sequentially. A theoretical analysis via Jacobian determinants and KL divergence shows that stage-wise training keeps latent distribution errors uncorrelated and bounded, whereas joint training causes errors to accumulate across layers.

## Results

KDIC achieves the best BD-rate across all three evaluation datasets: −13.71% on Kodak, −12.63% on CLIC, and −16.64% on Tecnick, outperforming all compared LIC methods. In terms of model complexity, KDIC uses only 47.61M parameters and 1.80T FLOPs, the lowest among all compared methods, while also achieving the fastest decoding latency (0.350 s) and highest training throughput (36.78 samples/s). Compared to the teacher model S2CFormer, KDIC reduces parameters by 40% and FLOPs by 57% while incurring only a 0.25–0.57% BD-rate degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KDIC outperforms all six compared LIC methods on every BD-rate metric across all three datasets. Against MLIC++ (116.48M params), KDIC achieves a 1.74% BD-rate improvement on Kodak while using 59% fewer parameters and 32% fewer FLOPs. Against TCM-L (75.89M params), KDIC gains 3.67% BD-rate on Kodak with 37% fewer parameters and 52% fewer FLOPs. FTIC, despite competitive BD-rate on Kodak (−12.94%), suffers from a decoding latency exceeding 10 seconds versus KDIC's 0.350 s, making it impractical for real-time use. CCA is the closest competitor on CLIC (−11.40% vs −12.63%) but still trails KDIC by 1.23% BD-rate while using 36% more parameters. MambaVC has a similar parameter count to KDIC (47.88M vs 47.61M) but lags by 5.6% BD-rate on Kodak and has 5.6× lower training throughput. The paper also reports that KDIC outperforms the traditional VTM-21.0 codec by 13.71%, 12.63%, and 16.64% BD-rate on Kodak, CLIC, and Tecnick respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies knowledge distillation—well-established in classification, detection, and super-resolution—to learned image compression for the first time, building directly on S2CFormer as the teacher architecture and ELIC's SCCTX entropy model as the distillation target. It departs from conventional KD in two key ways: (1) a stage-wise modular decomposition that treats each encoder/decoder stage as an independent sub-task rather than performing end-to-end distillation, and (2) a teacher-guided pruning strategy for student construction that preserves architectural consistency, avoiding the capacity gap that plagues cross-architecture distillation in low-level tasks. The implicit end-to-end supervision mechanism replaces explicit feature alignment, motivated by the energy compaction phenomenon unique to LIC latent representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first successful KD framework for LIC, this work opens a practical path to deploying high-quality learned compressors on resource-constrained hardware, achieving 40% parameter and 57% FLOPs reduction with under 0.6% BD-rate degradation. The generalizability demonstrated across three distinct architectures (S2CFormer, S2C-Conv, TCM-L) suggests the framework could be broadly applicable to future LIC models. Open questions include whether the stage-wise approach extends to video compression, whether the theoretical justification (KL divergence argument based on Jacobian determinants) holds under less restrictive assumptions, and whether the efficiency gains compound when combined with other compression techniques like quantization or architectural search.
