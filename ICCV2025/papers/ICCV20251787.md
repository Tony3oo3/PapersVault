## Problem

Decoder-only transformer architectures for feed-forward novel view synthesis (e.g., LVSM) suffer from an inherent feature alignment problem: source and target tokens become indistinguishable across all layers, wasting computational capacity on source information that is ultimately discarded and making the model vulnerable to noise and compression artifacts. Additionally, publicly available 3D scene datasets contain fewer than 100,000 scenes, leaving most in-the-wild scenes out-of-distribution, and naively augmenting training with diffusion-generated synthetic data degrades rather than improves reconstruction quality due to generation artifacts.

## Input / Output

The model takes 2 sparse RGB input views (256×256 for Re10K/ACID, 256×448 for DL3DV) along with their camera poses encoded as Plücker coordinate patches. Source tokens are formed by concatenating image patches with Plücker patches through a shared linear embedder, while target tokens use only Plücker coordinates. The network produces novel view RGB images one at a time (6 target views at test time), with the target patch size matching the source patch size p×p×3.

## Method

The core architectural contribution is the Token-Disentangled (Tok-D) transformer block, which introduces an indicator variable (δ=1 for target, δ=0 for source) that is embedded and projected into layer-specific scale (σ) and bias (μ) parameters, modulating source and target tokens independently at each layer. The extended Tok-D Plus variant applies pre-modulation (scale + shift) before the self-attention and MLP sub-layers and post-modulation (scale only) after them, inspired by DiT, further separating the two token streams. For synthetic data, the authors use CAT3D to generate multi-view samples but deliberately assign the real conditioned image as the reconstruction target and the diffusion-generated views as sources, forcing the model to always produce photorealistic outputs and making it robust to diffusion artifacts. Training uses 8 H100 GPUs, 100K iterations, batch size 64, AdamW with cosine-free linear LR schedule, and EMA (rate 0.99), with no gradient clipping required.

## Results

On in-distribution scene synthesis at 256 resolution, the Tok-D transformer achieves PSNR 30.02 / SSIM 0.919 / LPIPS 0.058 on RealEstate10K, 29.47 / 0.846 / 0.086 on ACID, and 21.55 / 0.643 / 0.208 on DL3DV, outperforming the previous SOTA (LVSM) by 1.13 dB, 0.28 dB, and 1.64 dB PSNR respectively. Notably, the method is trained on only 8 H100 GPUs yet surpasses LVSM trained with 64 GPUs by 0.2 dB on RealEstate10K. When augmented with synthetic data from CAT3D, the method further improves cross-dataset performance (e.g., DL3DV PSNR rises from 21.18 to 21.27), whereas LVSM degrades under the same synthetic data augmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Tok-D transformer outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the closest competitor LVSM (ICLR'25), the gains are +1.13 dB PSNR on RealEstate10K, +0.28 dB on ACID, and +1.64 dB on DL3DV, with LPIPS reduced by roughly 45–47% relative. The gap widens substantially against geometry-based feed-forward methods: MVSplat (ECCV'25) trails by 3.63 dB on Re10K and 4.01 dB on DL3DV, while DepthSplat (CVPR'25) lags by 2.58 dB on Re10K and 2.50 dB on DL3DV. PixelSplat (CVPR'24) and GPNR (CVPR'23) are further behind, with GPNR missing DL3DV results entirely. A key differentiator is robustness to synthetic data: when CAT3D-generated views are added to training, LVSM's PSNR drops (e.g., 28.89→28.49 on Re10K), whereas the Tok-D method maintains or slightly improves (30.02→29.97), confirming that token disentanglement mitigates artifact propagation from synthetic sources.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LVSM (ICLR 2025), a decoder-only transformer for feed-forward NVS that removes 3D inductive biases, and identifies a fundamental inefficiency in its design: source and target tokens become feature-aligned across all layers, wasting computational capacity and propagating source artifacts into the target. The proposed Tok-D transformer block addresses this by applying layer-wise modulation (inspired by DiT's conditioning mechanism) that explicitly distinguishes source from target tokens at every layer. For the data side, the paper departs from procedural synthetic data (Blender-based, as in LRM-Zero and MegaSynth) by using CAT3D, a multi-view diffusion model, to generate training scenes, and introduces a training scheme where the conditioned real image serves as the reconstruction target, making the model robust to diffusion artifacts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles the core bottleneck in feed-forward NVS: fewer than 100K scenes exist across all public multi-view datasets, making real-world scenes out-of-distribution. Demonstrating that diffusion-generated synthetic data can *improve* (rather than degrade) NVS quality—provided the architecture disentangles source/target tokens and the training scheme avoids learning from artifact-laden targets—opens a practical path to scaling NVS training data. However, the gains from synthetic data remain modest compared to adding real data (Table 3), and the approach is validated only on scene-level reconstruction at 256 resolution with 2 input views, leaving open whether the token disentanglement principle transfers to higher resolutions, dynamic scenes, or object-level tasks. The finding that LVSM's performance *drops* with synthetic data while Tok-D improves is a useful diagnostic for the broader community building on decoder-only NVS architectures.
