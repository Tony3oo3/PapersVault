## Problem

Existing learning-based ISP hyperparameter optimization methods rely solely on unimodal image data, limiting their ability to capture richer semantic and quality context needed for task-specific tuning. No prior work has leveraged multimodal large language models to provide interpretable textual priors for ISP tuning, and existing preference-alignment approaches require extensive human-labeled data that is impractical to prepare for ISP hyperparameter spaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252422/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a RAW image (e.g., 4096×2304 from an IMX766 sensor or simulated COCO RAW) and a task-related prompt as input. The MLLM generates a textual description conditioned on the prompt, which is then encoded alongside the image via CLIP. The output is a vector of ISP hyperparameters (20 for the synthetic ISP, 114 for the Qualcomm Spectra 580 ISP) that, when applied to the RAW image, produce an optimized RGB image evaluated by downstream task metrics (mAP for detection/segmentation, SSIM/PSNR for IQA).

## Method

The framework operates in two stages. In the training stage, an MLLM (Q-Instruct) generates task-specific text descriptions, which are encoded with the image via CLIP and fused through a Multimodal Fusion Adapter (MF-Adapter) comprising image/text adapters, self-attention, and cross-attention to produce a joint embedding. A PPO-based RL agent (Actor-Critic MLP) then predicts ISP hyperparameters, receiving reward from downstream task metric differences. In the fine-tuning stage, a Dynamic Pair Generation Module (DPGM) uses two agents with different weight initializations to produce action pairs, an evaluation function selects accepted/rejected actions based on task metrics, and a combined PPO+DPO loss (L_D-DPO) aligns the policy toward preferred hyperparameters without any human-annotated preference data.

## Results

On MSCOCO object detection, MLLM-ISP (Fine-tuning) achieves mAP@0.5 = 0.653, mAP@0.75 = 0.482, and mAP@0.5:0.95 = 0.454, outperforming the strongest baseline RL-SepISP (0.640 / 0.460 / 0.431) by +1.3, +2.2, and +2.3 points respectively. On instance segmentation, the same model reaches mAP@0.5 = 0.632, mAP@0.75 = 0.401, and mAP@0.5:0.95 = 0.373, again surpassing all baselines. For image quality assessment on the IMX766 dataset with the Qualcomm Spectra 580 ISP, MLLM-ISP (Fine-tuning) attains SSIM = 0.863 and PSNR = 34.26 dB, approaching the expert-tuned ground truth (SSIM = 1.000) while substantially exceeding default parameters (SSIM = 0.814, PSNR = 29.62 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MLLM-ISP (Fine-tuning) achieves the best score on every reported metric across both object detection and instance segmentation, beating the strongest prior method RL-SepISP [32] by 1.3 mAP@0.5 points (0.653 vs. 0.640) and 2.2–2.6 points on mAP@0.75 and mAP@0.5:0.95 for detection, and by 0.2 / 2.6 / 2.6 points for segmentation. The training-only variant (without DPO fine-tuning) already surpasses RL-SepISP on mAP@0.75 and mAP@0.5:0.95 for both tasks, though it trails by 0.6 points on detection mAP@0.5, confirming that the DPG-DPO refinement contributes a consistent additional gain. Against earlier RL-based methods (DRL-ISP [30], AdaptiveISP [37]), the improvement is more substantial: over 25 mAP@0.5 points over DRL-ISP and roughly 10 points over AdaptiveISP. Among learning-based (non-RL) baselines, MLLM-ISP outperforms Sequential-tuned [23] by 2.5 mAP@0.5 points and Attention-aware [22] by 4.3 points on detection. For IQA on the Qualcomm Spectra 580 ISP, MLLM-ISP (Fine-tuning) improves PSNR by 4.6 dB over default parameters and narrows the gap to expert-tuned settings, while on the synthetic ISP it raises PSNR from 13.73 to 20.65 dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-based ISP hyperparameter optimization line (RL-SepISP, AdaptiveISP, DRL-ISP) by introducing a multimodal input channel: MLLM-generated textual descriptions (via Q-Instruct) are fused with image embeddings through a CLIP-based adapter before feeding a PPO agent. It also repurposes DPO from the LLM alignment literature, replacing human-annotated preference pairs with dynamically generated pairs from two differently-initialized agents, eliminating the need for manual labeling. Conceptually, it carves new ground by being the first to treat MLLM text as a structured prior for low-level image processing rather than high-level understanding, bridging the gap between MLLM research (Q-Instruct, PixelLLM) and ISP tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work opens a promising direction for leveraging the rich implicit knowledge of MLLMs in low-level vision pipelines, potentially reducing reliance on expert-tuned ISP parameters across diverse downstream tasks. However, the empirical gains over the strongest RL baseline (RL-SepISP) are modest (e.g., +1.3 mAP points on detection), and most experiments use a synthetic 20-parameter ISP rather than full hardware pipelines, leaving open whether the approach scales to the 114+ parameter Qualcomm ISP in practice. The added MLLM inference cost at training time and the sensitivity to MLLM quality (Table 4 shows only small differences across models) are practical concerns. A key open question is whether the textual priors provide genuine task-specific guidance or merely act as a regularizer that happens to help.
