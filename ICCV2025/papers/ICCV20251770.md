## Problem

Current vision-language models rely on whole-image descriptions that overlook fine-grained object attributes and spatial relationships, making it difficult to reason about complex scenes. Existing approaches to improve this either depend on labor-intensive manual annotations or expensive closed-source models for generating hierarchical descriptions, limiting scalability. The paper addresses the need for a self-contained, scalable pipeline that can automatically produce multi-granularity scene annotations without external model dependencies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251770/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and recursively decomposes it into hierarchical sub-regions via a Region Proposal Network (with optional OCR for text regions), retaining only regions above a confidence threshold. During training, the model jointly processes the full image and k randomly selected sub-regions (k=5 optimal) through a shared visual encoder. Outputs are structured hierarchical scene descriptions (merged from child to parent via object-name matching) and up to five diverse VQA pairs per image covering objects, relationships, style, scene context, and details.

## Method

OURO employs a two-stage self-bootstrapped pipeline built on Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct with LoRA (rank 8). In the data generation stage, an RPN recursively segments images into sub-regions; a VLM generates a description for each region, and child descriptions are merged into parent descriptions via object-name matching to form a comprehensive hierarchical caption, from which multi-faceted VQA pairs are generated. In the training stage, the model processes the full image alongside k sub-regions in parallel, optimizing a joint loss combining a captioning cross-entropy term and a VQA loss, so that shared visual-language representations benefit from both generation and reasoning objectives. This self-bootstrapping loop—where the model's own outputs become its training supervision—yields a 1.4M-sample augmented dataset and produces consistent gains across 20 benchmarks without any external annotation or closed-source model calls.

## Results

On General VQA (Table 1), OURO-7B achieves 66.2 on OKVQA, 80.8 on VQAv2, 70.4 on VizWiz, 77.7 on GQA, 77.0 on VSR, 87.0 on ScienceQA, and 51.6 on IconVQA, outperforming the Qwen2-VL-7B base model on all metrics except ScienceQA (87.0 vs. 95.4). On Doc-oriented VQA (Table 2), OURO reaches 93.5 on DocVQA, 84.1 on ChartQA, 79.1 on InfoVQA, 52.5 on DeepForm, 56.2 on KLC, and 72.0 on WTQ, surpassing GPT-4o on five of six benchmarks. On Scene Text-centric VQA (Table 3), OURO attains 85.3 on TextVQA, 80.2 on AI2D, 77.0 on STVQA, and 90.2 on ESTVQA, setting new state-of-the-art among open-source models. On image captioning (Table 4), OURO scores 136.2 on COCO Caption, 88.0 on Flickr30K, and 89.7 on TextCaps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its base model Qwen2-VL-7B, OURO improves OKVQA by +8.3 (66.2 vs. 57.9), VQAv2 by +5.3 (80.8 vs. 75.5), VizWiz by +5.7 (70.4 vs. 64.7), and GQA by +0.4 (77.7 vs. 77.3), though it trails on ScienceQA (87.0 vs. 95.4). Compared to Monkey-7B, OURO gains +4.9 on OKVQA, +9.2 on VizWiz, +17.0 on GQA, and +17.6 on ScienceQA, while matching VQAv2 (80.8 vs. 80.3). Against SPHINX-7B, OURO leads on every reported metric, with particularly large margins on VizWiz (+30.5) and GQA (+15.1), though it narrowly loses on IconVQA (51.6 vs. 52.7). In Doc-oriented VQA, OURO surpasses GPT-4o on DocVQA (93.5 vs. 92.8), InfoVQA (79.1 vs. 66.4), DeepForm (52.5 vs. 38.4), KLC (56.2 vs. 29.9), and WTQ (72.0 vs. 46.6), but falls slightly short on ChartQA (84.1 vs. 85.7). On Scene Text-centric VQA, OURO outperforms Monkey-7B by +17.7 on TextVQA (85.3 vs. 67.6) and +17.6 on AI2D (80.2 vs. 62.6), and beats Qwen2-VL-7B by +3.1 on TextVQA and +15.7 on STVQA (77.0 vs. 61.3). On image captioning, OURO achieves the best Flickr30K score (88.0) among all compared models, edging out Monkey-7B (86.1) and Qwen-VL-Chat-7B (81.0), while Monkey retains a slight edge on TextCaps (93.2 vs. 89.7).

## Relation to Prior Work

OURO builds directly on Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct as its base architecture, fine-tuned via LoRA, and departs from prior fine-grained scene understanding methods (FlexCap, Monkey, Visual CoT) that rely on external closed-source models or large-scale manual annotations to generate hierarchical descriptions. The key conceptual departure is the self-bootstrapping loop: a simple RPN provides region proposals, and the model itself recursively generates multi-level scene descriptions and VQA pairs, eliminating the dependency on external annotators or proprietary APIs. This positions OURO as a lightweight, self-contained alternative to the weight-mixing and multi-model integration strategies used by SPHINX and mPLUG-Owl2, while retaining the hierarchical decomposition idea explored by Monkey and CAPSFUSION but making it fully autonomous.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-bootstrapping pipeline offers a practical, scalable recipe for improving multimodal scene understanding without incurring the cost of manual annotation or API calls to frontier models, which is significant for groups with limited compute or data budgets. However, the current random sub-region selection lacks interpretability and may miss question-relevant areas, and the recursive description process can produce overly long, redundant captions—both acknowledged limitations that constrain real-world deployment. An open question is whether the gains generalize beyond the Qwen2-VL family and LoRA fine-tuning, since all results come from a single base model and a single parameter-efficient adaptation recipe. The approach also raises broader questions about how far self-generated supervision can push a model before error compounding in the recursive annotation loop degrades quality.
