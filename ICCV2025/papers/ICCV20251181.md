## Problem

Existing human image animation methods remain constrained to coarse-grained motion synthesis and fail to simultaneously achieve fine-grained holistic control (subtle facial expressions combined with body movements), multi-scale adaptability from portraits to full-body views, and long-term temporal coherence for unseen regions such as garment textures on the back. These limitations reduce expressiveness and robustness for real-world deployment in film, advertising, and gaming.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251181/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes one or multiple reference images (portrait, upper-body, or full-body) and a driving video as input, optionally accepting a speech signal in place of the driving video for audio-driven lip-sync mode. During training, video clips of 25–121 frames are resized to a 960×640 pixel area while preserving aspect ratio; at inference, 73-frame segments are generated sequentially with the last latent of each segment initializing the next. The driving video is decomposed into face crops (224×224), rendered 3D head spheres, and 2D-projected 3D body skeletons, which serve as the control signals injected into the diffusion process.

## Method

DreamActor-M1 builds on a pre-trained MMDiT backbone (Seaweed) with Flow Matching, replacing the conventional ReferenceNet with a token-concatenation scheme where reference and video latents are patchified, concatenated, and interacted via 3D self-attention and spatial cross-attention within each DiT block. The core novelty is a hybrid control signal design that decouples three motion aspects: implicit facial representations (face motion tokens from a pre-trained encoder injected via cross-attention for expression), 3D head spheres (color-coded spheres controlling head pose and rotation independently), and 3D body skeletons with bone-length normalization (for torso and limb articulation without imposing a fixed body shape). A complementary appearance guidance protocol generates pseudo multi-view references from the single input to fill information gaps in unseen regions during long-form sequential generation. Training follows a three-stage progressive schedule—first body and head control only, then adding the face encoder with frozen backbone, and finally full fine-tuning—on 500 hours of diverse video spanning dancing, sports, film, and speeches at mixed scales.

## Results

On the collected body-animation dataset, DreamActor-M1 achieves FID 27.27, SSIM 0.821, PSNR 23.93, LPIPS 0.206, and FVD 122.0, outperforming the best baseline (DisPose) by 5.74 FID, 0.017 SSIM, 1.94 PSNR, 0.042 LPIPS, and 22.7 FVD. On the standard TikTok benchmark, it reaches FID 38.64 and FVD 131.3, again leading all competitors. For portrait animation on the collected dataset, it attains FID 25.70, PSNR 28.44, and FVD 110.3, surpassing the strongest portrait baseline (Act-One) by 4.14 FID, 3.37 PSNR, and 24.9 FVD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamActor-M1 outperforms all eight baselines across every reported metric on both evaluation datasets. In body animation, it beats DisPose (the strongest body baseline) by 5.74 FID and 22.7 FVD on the collected dataset, and by 9.18 FID and 27.5 FVD on TikTok; the gap over Animate Anyone, Champ, and MimicMotion is even larger (up to 12.94 FID over Champ on the collected set). In portrait animation, it surpasses Act-One (the closest competitor) by 4.14 FID, 3.37 PSNR, and 24.9 FVD, and leads SkyReels-A1 by 4.96 FID and 23.5 FVD. The margins are most pronounced on PSNR and FVD, indicating substantially better per-frame fidelity and temporal coherence. No baseline is competitive on any individual metric, and the advantage widens on the more diverse collected dataset relative to the narrower TikTok benchmark, suggesting stronger generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MMDiT architecture (Esser et al., ICML 2024) and initializes from the Seaweed pretrained image-to-video DiT (Lin et al., 2025), departing from the dominant ReferenceNet paradigm used by Animate Anyone, Animate Anyone 2, and MimicMotion in favor of a token-concatenation approach where reference and driving tokens interact through shared self-attention. Conceptually, it unifies two previously separate research threads—portrait animation (EMO, X-Portrait, SkyReels-A1) and body animation (Animate Anyone, Champ, MimicMotion)—into a single DiT framework with hybrid control signals that decouple facial expressions (implicit face latents), head pose (3D spheres), and body motion (3D skeletons with bone-length adjustment). The complementary appearance guidance via pseudo multi-reference frames extends the single-reference paradigm to address information decay in long-form generation, a problem only partially addressed by prior multi-reference methods like MSTed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By addressing fine-grained holistic control, multi-scale adaptability (portrait through full-body), and long-term temporal coherence simultaneously, this work provides a practical template for production-grade human animation systems. The progressive training strategy and the decoupled hybrid control design offer reusable architectural patterns for future DiT-based animation research. However, significant limitations remain: no dynamic camera control, no physical interaction modeling, instability in bone-length adjustment for edge cases, and no support for multi-character or non-humanoid animation—areas that will be critical for real-world deployment in film and gaming.
