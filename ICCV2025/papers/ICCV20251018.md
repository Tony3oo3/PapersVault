## Problem

Existing hallucination mitigation methods for MLLMs either impose heavy inference-time computational overhead (decoding strategies) or introduce distribution mismatches by relying on external LLMs for output rewriting or out-of-domain training data. The paper identifies that hallucinations predominantly emerge at the early stages of text generation and propagate through subsequent outputs, yet no prior method targets this early-onset propagation pattern with in-domain, annotation-free preference learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251018/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image and a text prompt, then iteratively samples multiple candidate sentences from the current model, extracting noun entities via SceneGraphParser and validating their presence using two open-vocabulary detectors (GroundingDINO and YOLO World) in cross-checking mode. The output is a LoRA-fine-tuned MLLM (LLaVA-v1.5-7B/13B) that produces hallucination-reduced captions; during training, the context is progressively extended by appending verified non-hallucinated sentences, so the model learns to discriminate at the sentence level where hallucinations first appear rather than over full captions.

## Method

SENTINEL performs sentence-level early intervention through three stages: (1) in-domain candidate bootstrapping, where multiple sampled outputs are parsed into scene graphs, objects are extracted, and a dual-detector cross-check (GroundingDINO + YOLO World) classifies each sentence as hallucinated, factual, or uncertain; (2) context-aware preference data generation with Iterative Contextual Bootstrapping (ICB), which pairs context-coherent non-hallucinated positives (y_w+) with hallucinated negatives (y_l) and iteratively appends verified positives to the generation context to enrich training diversity; (3) a context-aware DPO (C-DPO) loss that conditions the policy on the accumulated context c but excludes c from gradient computation, so the model learns to discriminate hallucinated from factual sentences at the point where hallucinations first manifest. The entire pipeline is model-agnostic, requires no human annotations or external LLMs for rewriting, and preserves the model's intrinsic output distribution by using only in-domain sampled data.

## Results

On Object HalBench (7B), SENTINEL reduces response-level hallucination from 52.7% (baseline) to 4.3% and mention-level from 28.0% to 2.6%, a >90% reduction. On AMBER, CHAIR drops from 8.4 to 2.9 and the Hallucination Score from 35.5 to 14.6. Crucially, SENTINEL preserves or improves general capabilities: VQAv2 stays at 78.4%, TextVQA at 58.2%, while ScienceQA improves from 66.8% to 69.2% and MM-Vet from 31.0 to 32.6, whereas competing methods like TPO (MM-Vet 25.7) and RLAIF-V (MM-Vet 29.9) suffer significant degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SENTINEL outperforms all ten baselines on Object HalBench for the 7B model, achieving 4.3% response-level and 2.6% mention-level hallucination versus the previous SOTA TPO (5.6% / 3.2%) and a large gap over decoding-based methods like VCD (51.3%), OPERA (45.3%), and DoLa (44.0%). Among preference-learning baselines, SENTINEL substantially beats HA-DPO (37.0% Resp.), POVID (33.4%), and RLAIF-V (7.8%), while also surpassing CLIP-DPO on AMBER CHAIR (2.9 vs. 3.7) and Hallucination Score (14.6 vs. 16.6). On general capabilities, SENTINEL is the only method that matches or exceeds the unmodified baseline on VQAv2 (78.4 vs. 78.5), TextVQA (58.2 vs. 58.2), ScienceQA (69.2 vs. 66.8), and MM-Vet (32.6 vs. 31.0), whereas TPO drops MM-Vet to 25.7 and RLAIF-V to 29.9. For the 13B model, SENTINEL again leads all baselines on hallucination metrics (3.3% Resp., 1.9% Ment., 11.7 Hal.) while maintaining or improving general scores (ScienceQA 72.8 vs. 71.6 baseline, MM-Vet 36.2 vs. 36.0). The paper also shows that combining SENTINEL's data with HA-DPO's GPT-generated dataset (Table 5) further reduces hallucination to 8.0% Resp. while boosting MM-Vet to 33.5, demonstrating complementarity with existing preference-learning pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

SENTINEL extends the DPO framework [Rafailov et al., NeurIPS 2023] by incorporating generation context into the preference loss (C-DPO), shifting the intervention point from whole-response to sentence-level discrimination. It departs from prior preference-based hallucination mitigation methods (HA-DPO, POVID, RLAIF-V, TPO) that rely on external LLMs like GPT-4 for data curation or output rewriting, instead bootstrapping in-domain preference pairs through iterative self-sampling validated by cross-checking two open-vocabulary detectors (GroundingDINO and YOLO World). The Iterative Contextual Bootstrapping strategy, which progressively appends verified non-hallucinated sentences to the generation context, is a novel data-construction mechanism not present in prior DPO-based hallucination work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical demonstration that hallucinations concentrate in early generated sentences and propagate forward provides a concrete, actionable principle for designing mitigation strategies, and the 90%+ hallucination reduction while preserving (and even improving) general capabilities addresses a key trade-off that has limited prior methods. The finding that in-domain, style-consistent data outperforms GPT-4-rewritten data is a practically useful insight for the broader preference-learning community. However, the method is evaluated only on LLaVA-v1.5 (7B/13B), and the authors acknowledge it cannot handle spatiotemporal hallucinations in video MLLMs, leaving open whether the sentence-level early-intervention principle generalizes to longer-form or multi-modal-sequence generation.
