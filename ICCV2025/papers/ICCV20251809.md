## Problem

Existing backdoor attacks, whether using universal trigger patterns or sample-specific perturbations, remain vulnerable to trigger-inversion defenses (e.g., Neural Cleanse, BTI-DBF) that can recover the trigger and enable backdoor removal via fine-tuning. Moreover, even stealthy attacks produce perceptible pixel-level perturbations that fail human visual inspection. No prior attack simultaneously evades model-level detection and remains imperceptible to human observers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251809/cropped_page5_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/GTSRB, 224×224 for ImageNet-12) and a designated target label, and produces a poisoned ResNet model plus a trained autoencoder. The autoencoder maps Gaussian-distributed seed samples (σ=5.0) to per-pixel trigger maps in [0,1] via a sigmoid-constrained output, which are then element-wise multiplied with the FFT amplitude spectrum of each image. The shallow backdoor additionally requires a set of unique colors (one per class) and random target labels to generate four-corner patch triggers.

## Method

SPD employs a two-phase training procedure: first clean supervised training for generalization, then joint backdoor injection where the model f and autoencoder g are optimized simultaneously. The deep backdoor samples from a class-specific Gaussian distribution, encodes the sample through the autoencoder to produce a diverse trigger, and injects it by element-wise multiplication with the FFT amplitude spectrum (preserving phase), followed by IFFT to recover the pixel-domain backdoor sample. The shallow backdoor uses traditional patch-based triggers with a unique color per class, deliberately covering all classes rather than a single target, which breaks the statistical-anomaly assumption exploited by model-level detectors like MM-BD and BTI-DBF. The joint loss combines clean accuracy, shallow backdoor loss, deep backdoor loss, and an L2 pixel-space stealthiness term weighted by α, ensuring backdoor samples remain nearly indistinguishable from clean inputs.

## Results

SPD achieves near-perfect attack success rates across all three datasets: 99.91% ASR on CIFAR-10, 99.98% on GTSRB, and 100% on ImageNet-12, while also attaining the highest clean accuracy on CIFAR-10 (94.38%) and ImageNet-12 (94.30%). In terms of stealthiness, SPD dramatically outperforms all baselines with SSIM of 0.999 and PSNR of 50.75 on CIFAR-10, 0.995 and 42.24 on GTSRB, and 0.998 and 47.64 on ImageNet-12. Against model-level detection, SPD achieves 0% Detection Success Rate on CIFAR-10 for all three detectors (NC, MM-BD, BTI-DBF), and 0–10% DSR on GTSRB and ImageNet-12, while most baselines are detected at 80–100%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On attack effectiveness, SPD matches or exceeds all nine baselines on ASR across all datasets, and achieves the highest CA on CIFAR-10 (94.38% vs. 93.58% for WaNet) and ImageNet-12 (94.30% vs. 94.26% for Ftrojann); on GTSRB, Bpp edges out SPD by 0.28% in CA. On stealthiness, SPD's advantage is overwhelming: its PSNR of 50.75 on CIFAR-10 exceeds the next-best Ftrojann (40.47) by over 10 dB, and its SSIM of 0.999 far surpasses WaNet (0.974) and Ftrojann (0.990). Against model-level detection, SPD achieves 0% DSR on CIFAR-10 for NC, MM-BD, and BTI-DBF, whereas BadNets, Blended, Ftrojann, and SSBA are detected at 100% by at least one method; on ImageNet-12, SPD's 10% DSR under BTI-DBF is the lowest among all attacks. On input-level detection, SPD attains the lowest average AUC and F1 scores across STRIP, TeCo, and IBD-PSC on all three datasets, with AUC values near 0.5 (random chance) on CIFAR-10, indicating effective evasion.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPD repurposes two well-studied backdoor attack paradigms—frequency-domain triggers (Ftrojann, FIBA) and pixel-domain patch triggers (BadNets)—into a novel dual-layer architecture where the visible, all-class shallow backdoor acts as a 'firewall' to shield the invisible, single-class deep backdoor. The deep backdoor departs from prior frequency-domain attacks by modulating the *intensity* of the amplitude spectrum via an autoencoder-encoded Gaussian sample rather than replacing the amplitude component outright, and from sample-specific attacks (SSBA, IAD, LIRA) by drawing triggers from a parametric distribution instead of per-sample optimization. The firewall metaphor is borrowed from network security, but its application to backdoor defense evasion is new in the DNN literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that model-level detection methods (NC, MM-BD, BTI-DBF) rely on the assumption that backdoor classes are few and statistically anomalous, and that an all-class shallow backdoor can systematically break this assumption—a useful insight for both attack and defense research. However, the white-box threat model (adversary fully controls training) limits real-world applicability, and the shallow backdoor's visible patches remain a potential vulnerability to human inspection or input-level defenses. Open questions include whether the dual-backdoor strategy generalizes to black-box settings, whether more sophisticated detectors that search for *multiple* backdoors simultaneously can uncover the deep layer, and whether the autoencoder-based trigger generation scales to larger, more diverse datasets.
