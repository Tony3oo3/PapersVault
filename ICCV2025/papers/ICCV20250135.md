## Problem

Existing VLM-based zero-shot domain adaptation methods for object detection address either domain bias (distribution shift between source and target) or detection bias (insufficient instance-level localization), but not both simultaneously. Manually crafted prompts fail to capture foreground/background contextual attributes, and learnable prompts trained only on source-domain data inadvertently amplify domain bias, creating a trade-off that has remained unresolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250135/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images resized to 600×1067 pixels and structured text prompts (image-level domain descriptors, positive class-specific prompts, and negative background prompts) as input. It outputs standard Faster R-CNN detection results: bounding boxes with regression offsets and class probabilities computed via cosine similarity between CLIP proposal embeddings and text embeddings. During inference on target domains, the URE transformation is disabled, so only the original image features and optimized prompts are used.

## Method

UPRE jointly optimizes textual prompts and visual representations through three coupled components. The Multi-view Domain Prompt (MDP) prepends learnable context vectors to static human-defined prompts in three views (image, positive, negative) to capture both domain priors and instance-level detection knowledge. The Unified Representation Enhancement (URE) extends AdaIN from global to per-patch learnable mean and deviation parameters, transforming source-domain features into pseudo target-domain representations that capture spatially varying style shifts. Two multi-level losses coordinate training: Relative Domain Distance (RDD) aligns image-level multi-modal representations by matching the relative displacement between source and pseudo-target embeddings to the corresponding text-embedding displacement, while Positive-Negative Separation (PNS) applies distinct cross-entropy losses to foreground and background proposals to capture diverse instance contexts. Training proceeds in two stages on source data only: prompt and enhancement learning (5k iterations), followed by detector fine-tuning with frozen MDP/URE (100k iterations).

## Results

On the Diverse Weather Conditions benchmark (Daytime Clear → four adverse-weather targets), UPRE achieves mAP of 40.0 (Daytime Foggy), 41.5 (Night Clear), 19.8 (Night Rainy), and 34.5 (Dusk Rainy), improving over the strongest baseline UFR by 0.4–1.3 mAP points and over the Faster R-CNN baseline by 7.1–8.5 points. On Cross-City Scenarios (Cityscapes → BDD100K/KITTI), UPRE reaches 28.7 mAP on BDD100K and 74.3 AP on KITTI car, outperforming the best prior method (OA-DG) by 1.5 and 1.3 points respectively. On Virtual-to-Real transitions (Sim10K → three real datasets), UPRE attains 47.9, 37.8, and 61.9 mAP on Cityscapes, BDD100K, and KITTI, surpassing the next-best method by 0.9–2.2 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UPRE outperforms all eight baselines across every evaluation setting. Against the non-VLM baselines Faster R-CNN and S-DGOD, gains are largest (7.1–8.5 and 3.2–6.5 mAP points respectively), confirming the benefit of VLM-based prompt learning for ZSDA. Among VLM-based methods, UPRE edges out the closest competitors UFR (by 0.4–1.3 mAP) and PDD (by 0.6–1.3 mAP) on the weather-condition benchmark, with the margin widening to 1.5–2.2 mAP on virtual-to-real transitions where UFR is not evaluated. Against PODA* (the direct ZSDA predecessor using global AdaIN style transfer), UPRE improves by 0.8–2.8 mAP, with the largest gain on Night Clear (+2.8), suggesting per-patch enhancement handles spatially varying styles better. DAI-Net* is outperformed by 0.5–4.5 mAP depending on the setting, and OA-DG by 0.6–3.5 mAP. Notably, on the challenging Night Rainy target where all methods struggle (mAP < 20), UPRE still leads by 0.6–3.0 points, and per-class gains on small/occluded objects (bike +4.2, rider +4.2) highlight the contribution of the positive-negative separation strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLM-based zero-shot domain adaptation line initiated by CLIP-GAP and PODA, which use manually crafted prompts and global style transfer (AdaIN) to simulate target domains. It departs from prior prompt-driven methods by retaining static human-defined prompts alongside learnable ones (rather than replacing them), and from detection-bias-focused methods (e.g., DetPro, domain-aware detection heads) by jointly optimizing both prompt representations and visual features in a single framework. The per-patch learnable mean/variance enhancement generalizes AdaIN's global style parameters to spatially varying transformations, and the multi-view prompt (image/positive/negative) extends single-prompt designs to capture foreground-background context separately.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete trade-off in ZSDA detection—methods that reduce domain bias tend to worsen detection bias and vice versa—by proposing a unified training objective that mitigates both simultaneously. However, all experiments are confined to driving-scene datasets (Cityscapes, BDD100K, KITTI, Sim10K), leaving open whether the multi-view prompt and per-patch enhancement generalize to non-driving domains or higher-resolution settings. The reliance on CLIP's zero-shot text-image alignment as the semantic backbone also constrains the approach to categories well-represented in CLIP's training data, and the 'zero-shot' label is somewhat loose since source-domain images with full annotations are still required.
