## Problem

No existing 3D shape matching method simultaneously achieves global optimality, geometric consistency (neighbourhood preservation), and practical scalability. Prior approaches either ignore geometric consistency (functional maps, deep methods), rely on NP-hard formulations like QAP or integer programming that do not scale, or enforce consistency only at limited points (e.g., SpiderMatch only at curve intersections). This paper closes the gap by presenting the first formalism that is provably globally geometrically consistent, globally optimal, and efficiently solvable in practice.

## Input / Output

The method takes two 3D triangular surface meshes (source X and target Y) of equal genus as input. The source mesh is decomposed into one surface cycle per triangle, and the target is treated as a directed shape graph. The output is a vertex-to-vertex correspondence from X to Y that preserves all neighbourhood relations (Definition 4). For quantitative evaluation, shapes are decimated to 1000 triangles, and matching costs are computed from deep features (ULRSSM features) or axiomatic features depending on the baseline. The LP relaxation of the resulting ILP is solved with Gurobi and empirically always yields integral solutions.

## Method

The source shape is represented as a collection of surface cycles (one per triangle) that partition the mesh and are glued at opposite edges. For each surface cycle, a product graph with the target shape graph is constructed, where finding a cyclic shortest path corresponds to matching that cycle. The key innovation is coupling these independent product graphs: coupling constraints (matrix L) enforce that opposite edges of neighbouring cycles are matched consistently, and injectivity constraints (matrix S) ensure each surface cycle edge is matched exactly once. Together with the incidence matrices (P), these form a constraint matrix H that casts the problem as a minimum-cost circulation flow on a directed hyper product graph. The resulting integer linear program (GeCo3D) is solved via LP relaxation, which the authors empirically observe is always tight, yielding globally optimal and geometrically consistent matchings without requiring branch-and-bound or heuristics.

## Results

On five 3D shape matching benchmarks (FAUST, SMAL, DT4D-H intra/inter, BeCoS), the method achieves the lowest mean geodesic error across all datasets, ranging from 0.024 (DT4D Intra) to 0.056 (BeCoS). In terms of geometric consistency (Dirichlet energy), the method dramatically outperforms all baselines, achieving 0.46–0.77 versus 0.81–13.6 for competitors, confirming that global geometric consistency yields substantially smoother correspondences. The method also scales best among geometrically consistent approaches, handling shapes with roughly twice as many vertices as the next-fastest competitor (DiscoMatch).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all four accuracy baselines (ULRSSM, SmoothShells, DiscrOpt, SpiderMatch) on every dataset in mean geodesic error, with the largest margins over SmoothShells (e.g., 0.027 vs. 0.379 on FAUST) and DiscrOpt (0.027 vs. 0.110 on FAUST). Against the closest competitor SpiderMatch, gains are modest in geodesic error (e.g., 0.024 vs. 0.027 on DT4D Intra) but the method wins on all five datasets. The most striking advantage is in Dirichlet energy (geometric consistency): the method achieves 0.46–0.77 across datasets, while SpiderMatch scores 0.81–2.1, ULRSSM 2.1–4.1, and DiscrOpt 1.1–13.6, indicating that global geometric consistency produces far smoother, more neighbourhood-preserving correspondences. In runtime, the method and SpiderMatch scale best among geometrically consistent approaches, but SpiderMatch's scalability degrades sharply when using a denser curve (SpiderMatch geo.) required for true global geometric consistency, whereas the LP relaxation of the proposed formulation remains tight and fast. The method also generalises to planar graph matching (WILLOW) and partial-to-full shape matching (SHREC'16) as proof-of-concept applications.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the product graph formalism of Lähner et al. [48] (originally for 2D-to-3D contour matching) to the 3D-to-3D setting by representing the source shape as a collection of surface cycles (one per triangle) rather than a single self-intersecting curve as in SpiderMatch [67]. The key conceptual departure from SpiderMatch is the use of multiple coupled cycles with explicit coupling constraints at opposite edges, which enforces geometric consistency globally rather than only at curve intersection points. The formulation also generalises the triangle-to-triangle ILP of Windheuser et al. [89] into a more structured hyper product graph flow problem that is empirically solvable via LP relaxation, whereas the original ILP required expensive branch-and-bound or heuristic solvers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first 3D shape matching method that simultaneously achieves global optimality, full geometric consistency (Definition 4), and practical scalability, filling a gap identified in Table 1 where no prior method satisfied all three properties. The empirical observation that the LP relaxation is always tight despite the constraint matrix not being totally unimodular is a significant practical finding, though it remains unproven and the authors explicitly leave the question of a polynomial-time algorithm open. Limitations include the allowance of inside-out flips and the possibility of multiple cycles collapsing to a single target vertex, which the authors suggest could be addressed via a symmetric formalism. The work also opens a path toward integrating geometric consistency as a hard constraint into learning-based shape matching pipelines, which currently ignore neighbourhood preservation.
