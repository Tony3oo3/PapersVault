## Problem

Existing generative novel view synthesis methods face a fundamental trade-off: image-based diffusion models handle large viewpoint changes but produce temporally inconsistent results, while video-based models ensure smoothness but fail at large spatial extrapolation. Additionally, most approaches are locked into fixed input/target view configurations and require post-hoc NeRF or 3DGS distillation to fuse inconsistent samples into a coherent 3D representation, limiting their practicality for diverse real-world NVS tasks.

## Input / Output

The model accepts any number of input views (1 to 32 or more) with full camera intrinsics and extrinsics, plus arbitrary target camera poses, and outputs novel view images or temporally ordered trajectory videos up to ~30 seconds with seamless loop closure. Training operates on 576×576 RGB images with a fixed context window T (8 or 21 frames), but at inference the model is cast as a flexible 'P-in Q-out' renderer where P and Q need not match the training-time M and N, enabling arbitrary input-target configurations without retraining.

## Method

SEVA builds on Stable Diffusion 2.1's latent U-Net, inflating 2D self-attention in low-resolution blocks to 3D self-attention across the view axis and adding 1D self-attention via skip connections (870M→1.3B params), with an optional 3D-convolution temporal pathway (→1.5B params) for ordered-frame smoothness. Training uses a two-stage curriculum (T=8 for 100K steps, T=21 for 600K steps) with random input-frame sampling and small subsampling strides to jointly cover small and large viewpoint changes, plus shifted SNR to better remove input information. At inference, a two-pass procedural sampling strategy first generates anchor frames using all inputs, then fills target chunks: nearest-neighbor chunking for set NVS and uniform-stride interpolation chunking for trajectory NVS. For very long trajectories (Q≫T), a memory bank of previously generated anchors with spatial-neighbor lookup replaces temporal-neighbor reuse, dramatically improving long-range 3D consistency without any explicit 3D representation.

## Results

On large-viewpoint set NVS (Table 3), SEVA achieves state-of-the-art PSNR across all five datasets, improving over CAT3D by +1.17 dB on RE10K (27.95 vs 26.78), +1.31 dB on CO3D (21.88 vs 20.57), and +1.20 dB on Mip-NeRF360 (17.82 vs 16.62). On small-viewpoint set NVS (Table 2), SEVA leads on object datasets (30.30 PSNR on OO3D, 31.53 on GSO) and achieves +6.0 dB over prior methods on LLFF with P=3. On trajectory NVS (Table 5), SEVA sets new SOTA on the O split with P=3, reaching 27.34 PSNR on RE10K and 19.99 on DTU, with the optional temporal pathway providing a further +0.2 dB boost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEVA outperforms CAT3D [8], the prior SOTA, by 0.3–1.3 dB PSNR across all large-viewpoint set NVS datasets (Table 3), confirming the paper's headline claim of +1.5 dB in the best setting. Against regression-based baselines, SEVA dominates: it beats DepthSplat [31] by 4–7 dB on large-viewpoint tasks (e.g., 15.30 vs 10.42 on CO3D) and MVSplat [16] by 5–10 dB on small-viewpoint set NVS. Among diffusion-based image models, SEVA surpasses ReconFusion [7] by 1.2–2.1 dB and ZeroNVS [13] by 2.8–8.5 dB on large-viewpoint set NVS. On trajectory NVS (Table 5), SEVA outperforms ViewCrafter [9] on the O split (P=3) by 0.8–4.3 dB, though ViewCrafter retains a slight edge on the V split (P=1) due to its high-resolution training. In the semi-dense regime (P=32), SEVA trails the specialized Long-LRM [17] by 1.7 dB on T&T but matches or exceeds it on DL3DV. SEVA also outperforms SV3D [18] on object datasets (19.25 vs 19.28 on OO3D is a near-tie, but 20.65 vs 20.38 on GSO) and 4DiM [12] on RE10K (17.99 vs 17.08 on the D split).

## Relation to Prior Work

SEVA builds directly on the Stable Diffusion 2.1 latent U-Net architecture and adopts the 3D self-attention inflation strategy from CAT3D and MVDream to enable multi-view conditioning. It departs from prior diffusion-based NVS methods (ReconFusion, CAT3D, ViewCrafter) in two key ways: it avoids explicit 3D representations (NeRF/3DGS) entirely, eliminating the need for post-hoc distillation, and it unifies set NVS and trajectory NVS—tasks that prior works address in separate models with fixed input configurations—into a single generalist model with flexible P-in Q-out sampling. The two-pass procedural sampling strategy and spatial-neighbor memory bank for long trajectories are novel inference-time mechanisms not present in prior diffusion-based NVS pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single diffusion model can handle both large-viewpoint generation and temporally smooth trajectory synthesis across 1–32+ input views without 3D distillation, SEVA simplifies the NVS pipeline for real-world deployment and removes a major engineering bottleneck. The comprehensive 10-dataset benchmark and open-source release provide a useful reference point for the field. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly dense captures (hundreds of views), whether the 576×576 resolution constraint can be lifted without architectural changes, and whether the two-pass sampling strategy generalizes to multi-object or dynamic scenes where static camera assumptions break down.
