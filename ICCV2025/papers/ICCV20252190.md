## Problem

Global completeness in XAI—requiring attributions to sum to the model's response—is too weak as a standalone criterion for faithful explanations, since it can be trivially satisfied by post-hoc normalization without any genuine explanatory power. The paper argues that promoting completeness locally within subregions of the attribution map, in a soft and flexible manner, is a more meaningful guiding principle for producing faithful attributions.

## Input / Output

Given an RGB image and a target class label, SLOC produces a pixel-level attribution map of the same spatial dimensions as the input. The method operates in a true black-box setting: it only requires forward passes of the model on masked versions of the input (where patches are replaced with a black-image baseline), and never backpropagates gradients through the model. The attribution map is initialized and then refined over ~500 gradient descent steps, with binary patch-level masks (Bernoulli probability p) defining the sub-maps over which local completeness is enforced.

## Method

SLOC introduces the completeness gap—a soft, per-sub-map measure of how far the sum of attributions in a subregion deviates from the model's response to the corresponding masked input—and minimizes this gap simultaneously across a diverse set of randomly generated patch masks. The full loss combines the mean squared completeness gap with L1 sparsity and Total Variation smoothness regularization, and is optimized via gradient descent on the attribution map alone (model outputs on masked inputs are treated as constants). The gradient update for each exposed element is the normalized completeness gap, ensuring that after a single step with learning rate 1, a single sub-map would exactly satisfy local completeness; with multiple overlapping masks, elements receive accumulated updates weighted by each mask's gap. Three variants are evaluated: SLOC (dynamically tuned p per image), SLOC_xp (fixed p calibrated per model), and SLOC_m (which additionally monitors the INS-DEL difference metric during optimization to select the best-performing attribution map).

## Results

On ImageNet with DenseNet-201, SLOC_m achieves the best faithfulness scores across all eight perturbation-based metrics, with POS=11.61 (lower is better), NEG=76.54, DEL=9.39, INS=65.79, NPD=64.93, IDD=56.4, AIC=79.21, and SIC=78.35, outperforming the next-best method (Ablation-CAM) by 5.6 points on POS and 14.5 points on NPD. On ViT-Small, SLOC_m extends this lead with POS=14.83, NEG=81.81, and IDD=58.04, surpassing DIX by 3.9 points on POS and 16.1 points on NPD. On the FunnyBirds benchmark, SLOC achieves the highest overall score (0.78 for ResNet-50, 0.88 for ViT-Base), and on ImageNet-Seg segmentation it is competitive with DIX (mIoU 0.56 vs. 0.66) while matching or exceeding all other methods on mAP and PA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLOC dominates all 14 baselines on the primary faithfulness evaluation (Table 1, DenseNet-201 on ImageNet), winning on every metric; the closest competitor is Ablation-CAM, which trails SLOC_m by 5.6 points on POS (17.24 vs. 11.61) and 14.5 points on NPD (50.44 vs. 64.93). Among learning-based perturbation methods, LTX is the strongest baseline (POS 16.24, NPD 54.84) but still lags SLOC_m by 4.6 points on POS and 10.1 on NPD, while DIX (POS 13.36, NPD 49.59) and RISE (POS 18.42, NPD 44.33) are further behind. On ViT-Small (Table 2), SLOC_m extends its lead over DIX (the best non-SLOC method) by 3.9 points on POS and 17.5 points on NPD, and over GAE and T-Attr by similar margins. On the FunnyBirds benchmark, SLOC achieves the top overall score for both ResNet-50 (0.78 vs. EP's 0.73) and ViT-Base (0.88 vs. DIX/T-Attr at 0.87). The one area where SLOC does not lead is segmentation on ImageNet-Seg, where DIX achieves the best mIoU (0.66 vs. SLOC's 0.56), though SLOC remains competitive on mAP (0.80 vs. 0.84) and PA (0.77 vs. 0.82), and the authors note that higher segmentation accuracy does not necessarily reflect better explanatory quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLOC sits within the learning-based perturbation family (alongside LTX, Meaningful Perturbation, and Extremal Perturbations) but departs from these methods in a key conceptual way: rather than learning perturbations that maximize or minimize the model's prediction, it formulates explanation as minimizing a 'completeness gap'—a soft, local version of the conservation property originally associated with Integrated Gradients and Deep Taylor Decomposition. The paper explicitly argues that global completeness is too weak a standalone criterion (a flat map can be trivially normalized to satisfy it), and repurposes the idea as a per-subregion guiding signal. Unlike LTX, SLOC requires no learned explainer network and no gradient backpropagation through the model, operating in a true black-box setting by treating masked-input model outputs as constant nodes in the computation graph.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a widely held assumption in XAI—that enforcing global completeness is a meaningful quality criterion—and shows that a soft, local version used as an optimization objective can outperform both completeness-satisfying methods (IG, DIX) and non-completeness methods (Grad-CAM, RISE) across faithfulness, part-level, and segmentation benchmarks. Practically, the black-box, gradient-free-through-model design makes SLOC applicable to proprietary or API-only models where backpropagation is unavailable. Open questions include whether the local completeness principle generalizes to non-image modalities (text, audio), whether the optimization's sensitivity to mask probability p and number of sub-maps can be better characterized theoretically, and whether the method's reliance on multiple forward passes per example limits its scalability to very large models.
