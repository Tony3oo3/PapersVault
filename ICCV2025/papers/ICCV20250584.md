## Problem

SAM's zero-shot segmentation degrades on camouflaged objects because image-only features cannot distinguish targets from visually similar backgrounds, and existing multimodal augmentation strategies (depth maps, manual text, hand-crafted prompts) are labor-intensive. Even when VLMs automatically generate multimodal cues, domain shift causes the resulting prompts to highlight only local regions rather than the full object, degrading downstream segmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250584/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (no auxiliary depth, text, or point annotations) and outputs a binary segmentation mask of the camouflaged target. Internally, the image is consumed twice: by BLIP's image encoder for caption generation and image-text alignment, and by SAM's ViT-H image encoder for feature extraction. The SAM mask decoder receives three structured inputs—a dense mask-prompt embedding, a refined image embedding, and an auxiliary sparse embedding—rather than the standard single image-embedding-plus-prompt configuration.

## Method

The pipeline first uses BLIP to auto-generate a caption, extracts the first noun via spaCy for target category, then produces an initial Grad-CAM prompt from BLIP's cross-attention weights and gradients. The key contribution, the Adaptive Refinement Module (ARM), employs k shared-weight two-layer convolutional adapters that extract and fuse features from both BLIP's image encoder and each transformer layer of SAM's image encoder; the final adapter output modulates BLIP's self-attention weights to expand the local prompt into a complete object mask, while intermediate adapter outputs are injected into subsequent SAM transformer layers to fine-tune the image encoder. An auxiliary sparse embedding is constructed by concatenating a Mamba-encoded text vector with BLIP's image embedding, providing additional semantic context. The refined mask prompt, the augmented image embedding, and the auxiliary embedding are jointly fed to SAM's mask decoder to produce the final segmentation.

## Results

On COD10K, the proposed method achieves the best Sm (0.909), Fβmax (0.885), Eφmax (0.955), and M (0.020), outperforming the second-best method (ZoomNeXt) by 1.6% in Sm and 2.4% in Fβmax. On CHAMELEON, it attains the highest Sm (0.932) and Fβmax (0.922), while on NC4K it leads in Sm (0.906) and Fβmax (0.897). On CAMO, the method achieves the best Sm (0.887) but trails ZoomNeXt by 0.3% in Fβmax (0.883 vs. 0.886) and by 0.7% in Eφmax (0.935 vs. 0.942). Overall, the method achieves the top or near-top result on 13 out of 16 metric-dataset combinations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 16 baselines on Sm across all four datasets, with the largest margins on COD10K (+1.4% over ZoomNeXt) and CHAMELEON (+0.4% over ZoomNeXt). On Fβmax, it leads on CHAMELEON (+0.3%), COD10K (+2.1%), and NC4K (+0.6%), but trails ZoomNeXt by 0.3% on CAMO. On Eφmax, ZoomNeXt retains the edge on CHAMELEON (0.977 vs. 0.964), CAMO (0.942 vs. 0.935), and NC4K (0.948 vs. 0.947), while the proposed method wins on COD10K (0.955 vs. 0.951). For the M metric (lower is better), ZoomNeXt achieves slightly lower values on CHAMELEON (0.017 vs. 0.023), CAMO (0.042 vs. 0.046), and NC4K (0.028 vs. 0.033), but the proposed method is best on COD10K (0.020 vs. 0.018 is actually ZoomNeXt; the paper reports 0.020 for Ours). Among SAM-based baselines (COMPrompter, DSAM), the proposed method achieves substantially higher scores, e.g., +2.4% Sm and +5.9% Fβmax over COMPrompter on COD10K, confirming that the ARM-based prompt refinement and auxiliary embedding provide meaningful gains over prior SAM adaptation strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAM-based COD line (SAM-COD, WS-SAM, SAM-adapter, MCASAM) by replacing manual or sparse prompts with fully automated multimodal prompts generated by BLIP, and introduces the Adaptive Refinement Module (ARM) that simultaneously refines those prompts and fine-tunes SAM's image encoder via lightweight adapters. It departs from prior multi-modality COD work (HPGT, ACUMEN, MMCPF) that still required human annotation or verification of textual/depth data, by leveraging a pretrained VLM end-to-end. The Grad-CAM-based prompt generation and CLIP-ES-inspired attention refinement are borrowed from prior interpretability and weakly-supervised segmentation literature, but their combination with a VLM-generated noun cue and the dual-purpose ARM is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a cost-effective, annotation-free pipeline (BLIP caption → noun extraction → Grad-CAM prompt → ARM refinement → SAM decode) can match or exceed purpose-built COD architectures, suggesting that foundation-model prompt engineering may reduce the need for task-specific network design in underperformed scenes. However, the approach inherits BLIP's captioning quality as a bottleneck, the spaCy noun-extraction heuristic is fragile for atypical targets, and the single-GPU, batch-size-1 training limits scalability claims. Open questions include whether ARM generalizes to other underperformed SAM scenes (shadow, medical) and whether the Mamba-based auxiliary embedding outperforms simpler text encoders at scale.
