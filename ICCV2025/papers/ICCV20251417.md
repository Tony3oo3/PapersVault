## Problem

Existing adversarial-based anti-personalization methods for text-to-image diffusion models only deter unauthorized use, rendering images permanently unusable for legitimate authorized personalization. Moreover, none of these methods provide a verifiable mechanism to trace copyright directly from the copyrighted image itself, and they cause irreversible visual degradation. The paper asks whether a single framework can simultaneously resist unauthorized personalization, support authorized restoration, and enable copyright traceability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251417/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a copyright watermark image of the same spatial dimensions as inputs. Both are decomposed via discrete wavelet transform (DWT) into LL, LH, HL, and HH sub-bands before being fed into the invertible network. The forward process outputs a copyrighted image (visually close to the original but embedding watermark features) and a key image; the backward process, given the copyrighted image and key, losslessly restores the original image and extracts the watermark. No fixed perturbation bound is imposed—perturbation magnitude is determined dynamically by the loss function.

## Method

The framework is a sequence of N invertible compound coupling modules (following the RealNVP architecture) that operate in the DWT frequency domain rather than pixel space. The key architectural innovation is a novel information exchange mechanism within each coupling module that uses squeeze-and-excitation blocks to modulate cross-flow between the original-image and watermark frequency streams, motivated by the finding that low-frequency sub-bands are most robust against diffusion-model personalization. Training uses a contrastive learning-based adversarial loss: the copyrighted image's VAE latent representation is treated as an anchor, pulled toward the watermark's representation and pushed away from the original image's representation, ensuring the diffusion model captures watermark features rather than facial identity. A secondary adversarial loss directly minimizes the anchor-to-watermark distance to guarantee traceability in unauthorized outputs. Because the network is strictly invertible, authorized users holding the key can perfectly recover the original image, enabling legitimate personalization without quality loss.

## Results

On VGGFace2, the proposed method achieves an FID of 300.35 (seen prompt) and 314.71 (unseen prompt) for unauthorized personalized generation, substantially outperforming all baselines (next best: ASPL at 214.41/216.95). The method also achieves FDFR of 0.1887/0.2350 and ISM of 0.4611/0.4963, indicating moderate face disruption. For copyright traceability, the extracted watermark achieves a Normalized Cross Correlation of 0.9929 across 200 images. Under authorized personalization, restored images achieve PSNR of ∞ and SSIM of 0.9861, enabling near-lossless recovery of the original.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method achieves the highest FID (300.35/314.71) among all baselines, exceeding ASPL (214.41/216.95) by ~86 points and Wm-AdvDM (160.12/151.68) by ~140 points, indicating the strongest deviation of unauthorized generated images from the original distribution. However, the method underperforms ASPL and FSMG on ISM (0.4611 vs. 0.3208/0.2956), meaning it is less effective at disrupting facial identity consistency in generated images. On FDFR, the method (0.1887) is competitive with FSMG (0.2037) and MetaCloak (0.2062) but lower than Wm-AdvDM (0.0712) and ASPL (0.1637). The key differentiator is that the proposed method is the only one simultaneously providing unauthorized personalization resistance, authorized personalization support, and direct copyright traceability from the copyrighted image itself (NC = 0.9929), whereas Wm-AdvDM can only trace from generated outputs and other baselines offer no traceability. The method also maintains consistent performance under unseen prompts (FID increases from 300 to 315), unlike MetaCloak and Wm-AdvDM which degrade significantly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the RealNVP invertible neural network architecture (Dinh et al., ICLR 2017) for copyright protection, a domain where it had not previously been applied. It departs from the adversarial anti-personalization line (AdvDM, Anti-DreamBooth, MetaCloak, Wm-AdvDM) by adding two capabilities those methods lack: direct watermark traceability from the copyrighted image itself and lossless restoration for authorized users. The compound coupling module introduces a squeeze-and-excitation-based information exchange mechanism (Hu et al., CVPR 2018) to enhance cross-frequency interaction, and the contrastive learning adversarial loss borrows from the VAE-latent-space formulation used in Wm-AdvDM and Salman et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in copyright protection: existing methods force a trade-off between anti-personalization, traceability, and authorized usability, whereas this framework claims to satisfy all three simultaneously through invertibility. However, robustness remains a concern—Gaussian filtering drops FID from 300 to 176, and visual quality (PSNR 30.7) lags behind Wm-AdvDM (35.3). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond face images to arbitrary content, how it performs against more sophisticated purification pipelines, and whether the invertible constraint limits the achievable protection strength compared to unconstrained adversarial optimization.
