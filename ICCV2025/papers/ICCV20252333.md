## Problem

Low-light object detection suffers from severe noise and contrast degradation, yet existing RAW-based approaches either bin and quantize 4-channel Bayer data into 3-channel RAW-RGB (losing sensor information), rely on computationally expensive multi-stage ISP parameter search, or require auxiliary normal-light references during training. No lightweight, self-adaptive ISP exists that directly processes unquantized Bayer RAW images in an end-to-end manner aligned with detection objectives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252333/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 4-channel Bayer RAW images (RGBG format) at 400×600 resolution as input and produces a 3-channel enhanced RGB image that is fed into a RetinaNet detector (ResNet-18/50 backbone) to output bounding boxes with class labels. The ISP plugin itself is extremely lightweight (~0.5 MB total parameters) and sits as a differentiable front-end before the frozen-pretrained backbone, with the nonlinear component using 8th-order polynomial bases and the linear component operating on a 3×4 camera parameter matrix augmented by attention-generated spatial maps.

## Method

Dark-ISP decomposes the conventional ISP pipeline into two trainable sub-modules: a linear component that combines a fixed camera calibration matrix (white balance × binning × color correction) with Local-Global Attention over dual-stream features to produce a pixel-wise adaptive 3×4×H×W transformation, and a nonlinear component that applies a set of non-convex polynomial bases (orders 0–8, each passing through (0,0) and (1,1)) with pixel-wise coefficients predicted by a small 3×3 convolution network, explicitly encoding the tone-mapping prior of stretching dark regions while compressing bright areas. A Self-Boost Regularization loss computes a closed-form pseudo-target matrix from the nonlinear output and enforces directional consistency (cosine distance) between its row vectors and the linear component's rows, promoting synergy between the two stages without requiring paired RAW-sRGB data. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a compound loss of detection supervision plus the Self-Boost term (λ=10⁻², activated after warmup epochs), using ImageNet-pretrained ResNet weights and 15 epochs of SGD training.

## Results

On the LOD dataset with a ResNet-50 backbone, Dark-ISP achieves 70.4 mAP on Bayer RAW input, outperforming the strongest baseline FeatEnHancer (67.0 mAP) by 3.4 points. With ResNet-18, Dark-ISP reaches 64.9 mAP versus 63.4 mAP for FeatEnHancer. On the NOD dataset, Dark-ISP leads on both Sony (31.5 mAP) and Nikon (29.9 mAP) cameras, and on SynCOCO it attains 23.1 mAP, consistently surpassing all baselines across the three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dark-ISP outperforms all four main baselines across all three datasets. The closest competitor is FeatEnHancer [28], which Dark-ISP surpasses by 1.5 mAP on LOD (ResNet-50), 1.2 mAP on NOD-Sony, 1.1 mAP on NOD-Nikon, and 0.7 mAP on SynCOCO. LIS [10] (RAW-RGB based) trails Dark-ISP by 2.5 mAP on LOD-ResNet50 and by 3.2 mAP on NOD-Sony, confirming that quantizing Bayer RAW to three channels loses critical sensor information. RAW-Adapter [15] underperforms Dark-ISP by 4.2 mAP on LOD-ResNet50 and 2.6 mAP on NOD-Sony. SID [6], a two-stage denoising method, achieves only 64.7 mAP on LOD-ResNet50, showing that noise removal alone is insufficient without task-aligned ISP adaptation. Notably, the default camera ISP on Bayer RAW already reaches 67.3 mAP on LOD, suggesting substantial untapped potential that Dark-ISP's learnable pipeline further exploits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by decomposing the conventional camera ISP pipeline into a trainable linear sensor-calibration module (white balance, binning, CCM) and a nonlinear tone-mapping module, then optimizing both end-to-end for detection. It departs from prior RAW-to-detection methods (LIS, RAW-Adapter) that quantize Bayer into 3-channel RAW-RGB, and from ISP parameter-search approaches (GenISP, AdaptiveISP, DynamicISP) that rely on expensive multi-stage training or reinforcement learning. The nonlinear component repurposes the coefficient-based curve-fitting idea from Zero-DCE but replaces the generic quadratic with an explicit set of non-convex polynomial bases grounded in low-light tone-mapping physics, while the linear component introduces local-global attention for content-aware adaptability. The Self-Boost Regularization, which uses the nonlinear output as a pseudo-target to guide the linear mapping via cosine-distance alignment, is a novel inter-module coupling mechanism not seen in prior ISP-for-perception work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Dark-ISP offers a practical, lightweight (≈0.5 MB total) drop-in plugin that lets a standard detection backbone consume unquantized Bayer RAW directly, avoiding the information bottleneck of RGB or RAW-RGB inputs without requiring paired normal-light references or complex parameter search. Its demonstrated cross-camera generalization (Sony and Nikon) without retraining suggests the physics-informed decomposition captures sensor-agnostic structure. Open questions include whether the polynomial-basis nonlinear module scales to higher dynamic ranges or multi-exposure settings, whether the Self-Boost mechanism transfers to other downstream tasks (segmentation, tracking) the authors mention, and whether the approach remains effective when the detection backbone is not ImageNet-pretrained. The modest parameter count also raises the question of whether further capacity could yield larger gains or whether the physics priors are already near-optimal for this task.
