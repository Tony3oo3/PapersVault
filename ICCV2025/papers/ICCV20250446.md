## Problem

Existing volumetric body models like COAP rely on large MLP decoders (256–512 neurons per layer), creating a severe computational bottleneck that saturates 24 GB GPUs with modest query batches and makes them impractical for optimization-based applications requiring frequent body queries. Additionally, COAP represents the body as a step occupancy function, yielding ill-defined gradients far from the iso-surface and limiting its utility in tasks requiring smooth distance approximation such as scene-constrained motion synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250446/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given SMPL shape coefficients β and pose parameters θ, the model first converts the parametric mesh into a point cloud partitioned into K kinematic-chain body parts, each canonicalized by its bone transformation and encoded by a shared PointNet into a local latent code z_k. For any 3D query point x, the model outputs a signed distance value d̃(x) by fusing per-part neural SDF predictions (aggregated via min) with an analytic bounding-box SDF for points outside all part bounding boxes, producing a globally consistent, differentiable SDF over the entire body volume.

## Method

The core contribution is the Neural Blend Weights (NBW) Generator, which replaces large fixed MLP decoders with compact 64-neuron MLPs whose weights are dynamically predicted per body part. For each layer l, the weight matrix W_k^l is expressed as a base matrix plus a linear combination of R learned shape-specific matrices, modulated by blending coefficients v_k^l computed from the PointNet latent code z_k via independent linear layers. This design, inspired by ResFields, preserves expressiveness while drastically reducing memory and compute. At inference, each local decoder predicts a signed distance for the canonicalized query point, predictions are aggregated via a min operation, and an analytic SDF handles distant queries outside all bounding boxes. The model is trained with an L2 loss on both the sign and magnitude of the SDF over points sampled inside bounding boxes and near the mesh surface, achieving ~10× faster inference and 6× lower GPU memory than COAP while improving accuracy.

## Results

On the PosePrior benchmark, VolumetricSMPL achieves 94.67% mean IoU (94.25% surface, 95.10% uniform), surpassing COAP (94.31%) and LEAP (75.98%) while running ~10× faster (15 ms vs. 140 ms) and using ~6× less GPU memory (3.1 GB vs. 18.7 GB). It is the first model in this comparison to provide a true SDF (MSE 3.7×10⁻⁵) rather than a binary occupancy. In downstream tasks, VolumetricSMPL delivers a 500× speedup in human-object interaction optimization (4.28 ms vs. 2128.2 ms per iteration), 3.4× faster egocentric mesh recovery (0.61 s vs. 2.08 s per frame), and 7× faster / 20× lower-memory scene-constrained motion synthesis.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LEAP [43], VolumetricSMPL dominates on every axis: 94.67% vs. 75.98% mean IoU, 15 ms vs. 79 ms inference, and 3.1 GB vs. 7.7 GB GPU memory. Against COAP [42], the closest competitor, VolumetricSMPL achieves a marginal IoU gain (94.67% vs. 94.31%) while being ~10× faster and ~6× more memory-efficient; it also provides a smooth SDF where COAP offers only a binary occupancy, enabling differentiable collision losses. In the human-object interaction task (Table 3), replacing the per-step object SDF recomputation of Wang et al. [59] with VolumetricSMPL's SDF yields a 500× per-iteration speedup (4.28 ms vs. 2128.2 ms) and lower object penetration (5.73% vs. 6.33%), at the cost of a slightly larger contact distance (3.96 cm vs. 3.20 cm). In egocentric mesh recovery (Table 4), VolumetricSMPL matches COAP's MPJPE (86.63 vs. 86.74 mm) while enabling 10× larger batch sizes and 3.4× faster inference. For self-intersection resolution (Table 6), VolumetricSMPL converges in fewer iterations (7.37 vs. 9.75) with fewer residual triangle collisions (68 vs. 74) and 3× lower memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

VolumetricSMPL builds directly on COAP's compositional encoder (kinematic chain partitioning, PointNet-based part encoding, canonical-space local decoders) but departs in two key ways: it replaces COAP's 256-neuron MLP decoders with a Neural Blend Weights (NBW) generator that produces compact 64-neuron MLPs via dynamic blending of learned weight matrices, and it models the body as a Signed Distance Field rather than a step occupancy function. The NBW mechanism is repurposed from ResFields, which originally composed learned weights for temporal signal fitting, here adapted to feed-forward volumetric body modeling. The paper also positions itself against LEAP and imGHUM, noting their reliance on even larger decoders or observation-space learning that limits integration with SMPL-based pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving 10× faster inference and 6× lower GPU memory while matching or exceeding COAP's accuracy, VolumetricSMPL removes the primary computational barrier that has limited the adoption of volumetric body models in optimization- and learning-based applications. The SDF representation provides smooth, informative gradients that materially improve downstream tasks like scene-constrained motion synthesis (90% fewer collisions) and self-intersection resolution. Open questions include whether the NBW approach generalizes beyond the SMPL shape space (e.g., to SMPL-X with hands/face or to imGHUM-style observation-space models), and whether the compact 64-neuron decoders remain sufficient for more complex articulations or clothed bodies.
