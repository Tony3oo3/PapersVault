## Problem

Existing ambient lighting normalization (ALN) methods assume single white light sources or homogeneous color fields, failing to handle the complex chromaticity shifts, self-shadows, and material-dependent reflectance variations produced by multiple colored directional lights. No large-scale dataset captures these multi-colored lighting scenarios with paired ambient-lit references, and current restoration architectures lack explicit mechanisms to disentangle illumination from reflectance under non-uniform color lighting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252625/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image captured under non-homogeneous multi-colored direct lighting (up to three programmable RGB lights with variable intensity, position, and orientation) and produces a single ambient-normalized RGB image with uniform diffuse white lighting. The CL3AN dataset provides 24 MP (1920×1440) triplets of colored-lit, white-lit, and ambient-lit images with perfect pixel alignment; the method is also evaluated on AMBIENT6K at 1280×960. Internally, the input RGB image is decomposed into HSV channels, where the Value channel drives the luminance branch and the Hue-Saturation pair drives the reflectance branch.

## Method

RLN² is a Retinex-inspired CNN with two parallel streams that independently compensate the luminance (L) and reflectance (R) components of the input image, guided by the HSV cylindrical decomposition: the V channel provides illumination priors for the L-branch while H and S encode chromaticity for the R-branch. The encoder performs joint RGB downsampling and Haar-DWT spectral decomposition, and a Cross-Domain Feature Fusion Attention (CDFFA) module uses dot-product similarity between low-frequency spectral features and guidance-channel features to compute softmax-weighted relevance coefficients that filter outlier features. The decoder concatenates refined features with high-frequency spectral information and fuses them with wide-context features from an ImageNet-pretrained ConvNeXt backbone via cross-attention, followed by channel-attention-based refinement. Training uses a multi-stage resolution curriculum with L1 loss, Adam optimizer (lr=2e-4), cosine scheduling, and gradient clipping, achieving state-of-the-art PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS on both CL3AN and AMBIENT6K at competitive computational cost (22.72 GMACs for the full variant).

## Results

On the proposed CL3AN dataset (1920×1440 px), RLN²-Lf achieves the best restoration fidelity with PSNR 20.523, SSIM 0.746, and LPIPS 0.208, outperforming the closest ALN-specific competitor IFBlend (PSNR 20.370, SSIM 0.720, LPIPS 0.228) while using ~15% fewer MACs (22.72G vs. 26.01G). On the AMBIENT6K benchmark (1280×960 px), RLN²-Lf again leads with PSNR 21.712, SSIM 0.825, and LPIPS 0.120, surpassing IFBlend (21.443, 0.819, 0.128). Even the lightweight RLN²-S variant (3.95G MACs) outperforms RetinexFormer (4.86G MACs) on CL3AN, and RLN²-Sf (4.24G MACs) matches IFBlend's perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.128) on AMBIENT6K at roughly 1/6 the computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RLN²-Lf outperforms IFBlend [65], the strongest ALN-specific baseline, on all three metrics on both datasets: +0.153 dB PSNR and −0.020 LPIPS on CL3AN, +0.269 dB PSNR and −0.008 LPIPS on AMBIENT6K, while reducing computational cost by ~15% (22.72G vs. 26.01G MACs). Against general-purpose IR methods, RLN²-Lf leads by a wide margin on CL3AN (e.g., +1.05 dB over NAFNet, +1.96 dB over HINet), confirming that task-specific ALN design is critical under multicolor lighting. On AMBIENT6K, the gap narrows (RLN²-Lf leads Restormer by +0.57 dB and MPRNet by +0.77 dB), but RLN² still wins. The lightweight RLN²-Sf (4.24G MACs) matches IFBlend's LPIPS on AMBIENT6K (0.128) at roughly 1/6 the FLOPs, and already surpasses RetinexFormer [9] on CL3AN PSNR (20.128 vs. 18.649) despite similar MACs. The paper observes that ALN favors architectures with extensive receptive fields (HINet, MPRNet) over local-attention transformers (SwinIR, Uformer, Restormer), and that frequency-domain access (SFNet, IFBlend, RLN²-Sf/Lf) consistently improves perceptual quality over RGB-only pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Ambient Lighting Normalization (ALN) task established by IFBlend [65] and the AMBIENT6K benchmark from white-aligned multi-source lighting to multi-colored (RGB) direct lighting, a setting the authors identify as previously unaddressed. Architecturally, RLN² draws on the Retinex decomposition framework [43] and frequency-domain image restoration (Haar-DWT, as in SFNet [12]), but introduces a novel CDFFA module that uses HSV-channel guidance to explicitly separate reflectance and illumination streams—a design choice motivated by the observation that single-stream end-to-end models conflate chromatic illumination shifts with material reflectance. The work also positions itself against the broader image restoration literature (SwinIR, Restormer, NAFNet, MambaIR) by benchmarking them under the new colored-lighting conditions, revealing systematic failure modes absent in white-light settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CL3AN dataset fills a genuine gap: no prior benchmark captures the interaction of multiple colored directional lights with diverse materials (conductors, dielectrics, varying roughness/transparency) alongside a diffuse ambient reference, making it a valuable resource for the ALN and broader illumination-invariant representation community. The finding that leading restoration architectures produce texture leakage and color distortion under colored lighting—despite strong performance on white-light ALN—highlights a fragility in current methods that practitioners deploying ALN as a preprocessing step (e.g., for neural image editing) should be aware of. Open questions include whether the HSV-guided decomposition generalizes to in-the-wild multi-illuminant scenes without controlled studio capture, and whether the approach scales to video or 3D relighting pipelines where temporal/geometric consistency adds further constraints.
