## Problem

Large multi-modal models (LMMs) that support segmentation suffer from two coupled failures: inaccurate segmentation caused by the LLM's inability to embed precise object attributes (position, color, identity) into the <seg> token, and hallucinated comprehension because local text descriptions are never explicitly grounded to the corresponding image regions. The paper shows that segmentation accuracy is positively correlated with the latent semantics carried by the <seg> token, motivating a design that strengthens both the semantic content of that token and the region-to-text alignment.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a free-form text instruction (e.g., 'segment the red bus closest to the white car'). The global image is encoded at full resolution by both a semantic encoder (InternVL2) and a pixel encoder (OMG-Seg); local regions extracted from segmentation masks are re-encoded at 448×448. Output is an autoregressive sequence of interleaved <seg> tokens (decoded into binary masks by a pixel decoder), local image feature tokens, and region-specific text descriptions wrapped in <p>...</p> markers, producing grounded conversations that pair each mask with its visual crop and description.

## Method

LIRA introduces two components. (1) The Semantic-Enhanced Feature Extractor (SEFE) fuses high-level semantic features from a pre-trained LMM encoder with fine-grained pixel features from a segmentation encoder via multi-head cross-attention (pixel features as queries, semantic features as keys/values) followed by residual addition and concatenation, enriching the <seg> token with accurate object attributes. (2) The Interleaved Local Visual Coupling (ILVC) paradigm constructs training sequences as mask–region–text triplets: after each <seg> token, the corresponding local image crop (448×448, extracted via GT mask in training or decoded mask at inference) is encoded and fed back into the LLM to generate a region-specific description, explicitly coupling visual evidence to text. Training is two-stage: first aligning the pixel encoder to the LLM, then jointly fine-tuning the fusion module, text projector, and LLM (via LoRA) with a combined next-token prediction loss and mask loss (cross-entropy + Dice).

## Results

LIRA-8B achieves an average of 78.2% across eight comprehension benchmarks and top performance in 5 out of 8 standard segmentation evaluation settings on RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg. On RefCOCOg, the 8B model reaches 78.4% (val) and 78.2% (test), surpassing the previous best OMG-LLaVA-8B (70.7%/70.2%) by over 7 points. Critically, co-training with segmentation data causes only a 0.2% drop in comprehension performance (75.2% vs 75.3%), compared to a 14.3% drop for OMG-LLaVA, demonstrating that LIRA preserves comprehension while adding segmentation. On Grounded Conversation Generation, LIRA-8B achieves 38.4 CIDEr (val) and 28.1 AP50, outperforming OMG-LLaVA-8B (36.2 CIDEr, 26.9 AP50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LIRA-8B outperforms the strongest prior segmentation-supporting model OMG-LLaVA-8B by 6.2 points on RefCOCO Val (81.8 vs 75.6), 10.7 points on RefCOCO+ Val (76.3 vs 65.6), and 7.7 points on RefCOCOg Val (78.4 vs 70.7), while also substantially improving comprehension (POPE: 88.1 vs 80.0; MMB-en: 81.1 vs 47.9). Against GLaMM (which used the much larger GranD pretraining set), LIRA-8B leads on all three RefCOCO variants (81.8/76.3/78.4 vs 79.5/72.6/74.2). PSALM retains a narrow edge on RefCOCO (83.6 vs 81.8 Val), but the authors note PSALM uses 100 queries per object, an advantage not matched by LIRA's single-query protocol. On comprehension-only benchmarks, LIRA-8B matches or exceeds InternVL2-8B (its backbone) on most metrics (VizWiz 71.5 vs 62.9, VQAv2 80.4 vs 79.8) while adding full segmentation capability. The co-training comparison in Table 5 is particularly striking: LIRA-2B loses only 0.2% average comprehension accuracy when adding segmentation data, whereas OMG-LLaVA drops 14.3% (e.g., MMBench 47.9 vs 67.5, AI2D 42.9 vs 61.7). On gRefCOCO zero-shot, LIRA-8B (40.9 cIoU val) surpasses all prior zero-shot methods including OMG-LLaVA (39.3) and LaSanA (38.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

LIRA extends the embedding-as-mask paradigm introduced by LISA [19] and the unified visual-encoder approach of OMG-LLaVA [69], building its architecture on InternVL2 [8] for semantic encoding and OMG-Seg [22] for pixel-level decoding. The key conceptual departure is the explicit exploitation of the complementarity between visual comprehension and segmentation: SEFE fuses high-level semantic features with pixel-level segmentation priors via cross-attention, while ILVC introduces a mask-region-text interleaved training paradigm that prior methods (which only use binary <mask, text> pairs) did not employ. This positions LIRA as a method that treats segmentation accuracy and hallucination reduction as two sides of the same coin—fine-grained visual understanding—rather than as independent objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is that co-training with segmentation data causes only a −0.2% comprehension drop versus −14.3% for OMG-LLaVA, suggesting that the SEFE+ILVC design can preserve general visual understanding while adding pixel-level capabilities—a key concern for practitioners deploying unified models. However, the AttrEval top-1 logit accuracy of 25.7% reveals that the model's internal attribute inference remains weak, and the ~15% inference overhead for grounded conversation generation limits real-time applicability. Open questions include whether the interleaved local coupling paradigm generalizes to video or multi-image settings, and whether the <seg> token's latent semantic structure can be further exploited for zero-shot attribute reasoning.
