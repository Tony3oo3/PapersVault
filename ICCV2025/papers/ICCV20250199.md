## Problem

Reflow, the standard technique for straightening Rectified Flow inference trajectories, relies on deterministic couplings built from pre-trained model-generated images, introducing a distribution gap between synthetic and real images and accumulating errors across iterations. The paper argues that true trajectory crossings are exponentially rare in high-dimensional space (Theorem 1), and that the real source of curvature is 'approximate crossing'—statistical similarity of intermediate states that confuses the velocity field. This motivates a method that straightens trajectories without depending on pre-generated images.

## Input / Output

At training time, the method consumes raw dataset images (CIFAR-10 at 32×32, AFHQ cat/dog at 64×64, 128×128, 256×256) and standard Gaussian noise; the encoder reparameterizes the noise conditioned on each image to form an 'optimized coupling' before the velocity field is trained. At inference, a single Gaussian sample is reparameterized by the encoder and integrated through the ODE in 1, 5, or 10 Euler steps to produce a generated image. The encoder is deliberately tiny (fewer than 1/20 of the velocity-field parameters), so the added inference cost is modest (~0.13 s per 512 images over RF at NFE=1).

## Method

VRFNO is a joint training framework coupling a lightweight VAE-style encoder with a neural velocity field (UNet or NCSNv2). The encoder maps each image X₁ to a mean μ and variance σ², which reparameterize a random Gaussian ε into an optimized noise X₀ = ε·σ² + μ; a KL-divergence term keeps (μ, σ²) near the standard Gaussian to preserve diversity. The key architectural addition is a Historical Velocity Term (HVT): at each training step the model receives the stop-gradient velocity predicted at the previous time step (t − Δt), exploiting Theorem 2's result that velocity differences between trajectories exceed state differences, thereby disambiguating 'approximately crossing' intermediate states. Training uses a two-stage loss—MSE on the velocity field first, then LPIPS added in a second stage—without any distillation or adversarial component. The optimized couplings (noise-image pairs satisfying ‖v_θ(X_t) − (X₁−X₀)‖ ≤ ε) replace Reflow's deterministic couplings, eliminating the distribution gap while retaining the data-reuse benefit through the encoder's subspace concentration effect.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (32×32), VRFNO achieves FID=4.50, KID=2.73, and IS=9.59 in single-step generation, substantially outperforming 1-RF (FID=379) and 2-RF (FID=11.97). In 5-step and 10-step settings, VRFNO reaches FID=4.03 and FID=3.36 respectively, matching or exceeding CAF and 2-RF. On AFHQ at 256×256, VRFNO achieves FID=27.04 (cat) and FID=27.37 (dog) in one step, compared to 2-RF's FID=171.84 and 189.82, demonstrating a ~6× improvement in image quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VRFNO decisively outperforms 1-RF and 2-RF (Reflow) across all step counts: at 1 step, FID drops from 379 (1-RF) and 11.97 (2-RF) to 4.50, and KID drops from 428 and 8.66 to 2.73. Against the closest competitor CAF, VRFNO achieves a lower FID at 1 step (4.50 vs. 4.81) and 10 steps (3.36 vs. 3.77), while tying at 5 steps (both 4.03); VRFNO also reports KID values that CAF does not. VRFNO ties TraFlow at FID=4.50 for 1-step generation but additionally reports KID=2.73 and IS=9.59, whereas TraFlow omits these metrics. Compared to Shortcut Model (SM, FID=4.93 at 1 step), VRFNO achieves a 0.43 FID improvement. On AFHQ at 256×256, VRFNO's one-step FID of 27.04 (cat) and 27.37 (dog) is roughly 6× better than 2-RF's 171.84 and 189.82, confirming the advantage generalizes beyond CIFAR-10.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Rectified Flow (RF) and its Reflow operation, but departs from RF's core explanation for trajectory curvature. The authors show via Theorem 1 that exact trajectory crossings are exponentially rare in high-dimensional space (P ~ O(e^{-c(n×n)})), arguing instead that curvature arises from the model's inability to distinguish statistically similar intermediate states. To address this, VRFNO introduces a Historical Velocity Term (HVT) for trajectory disambiguation and replaces Reflow's deterministic couplings (which require a pre-trained model and introduce distribution gap) with a VAE-style encoder that optimizes noise via reparameterization, drawing on the reparameterization trick from β-VAE and the multi-time-scale training philosophy of distillation methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VRFNO offers a principled alternative to Reflow that avoids the distribution gap and error accumulation inherent in iterative reflow, while achieving competitive one-step FID (4.50 on CIFAR-10) without distillation. The HVT mechanism is lightweight and could be adopted in other flow-based or ODE-based generative frameworks. However, the method still requires an encoder at inference time (adding ~75% overhead in one-step sampling), the 'optimized coupling' definition is somewhat ad hoc, and the two-stage training with LPIPS lacks theoretical justification. Open questions include whether the approach scales to high-resolution text-to-image generation, conditional generation, and whether the encoder's subspace-concentration effect (analogous to data reuse) generalizes beyond the tested resolutions.
