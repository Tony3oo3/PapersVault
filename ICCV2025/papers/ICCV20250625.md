## Problem

Existing object-centric video prediction methods (SlotFormer, OCVP) model objects primarily through appearance features, overlooking explicit motion dynamics such as position, velocity, and acceleration. This omission limits their ability to capture complex physical interactions and causes prediction divergence on longer sequences or more complex scenes, as the model must implicitly infer motion from appearance changes alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250625/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of N video frames (default 6; 128×128 for synthetic MOVi/OBJ3D, 1280×1920 for Waymo) and autoregressively generates H future frames (default 8, tested up to 18 for long-term generalization). Internally, each frame is decomposed into permutation-invariant object slots via a pretrained SAVi encoder and a kinematics vector (2D center-of-mass position, velocity, and acceleration) via a CNN-based kinematics encoder; the final output is reconstructed frames plus per-slot object masks.

## Method

OCK introduces an explicit Object Kinematics vector K_t = [x_pos, x_vel, x_acc] computed from object centers of mass extracted by the slot encoder, where velocity is the scaled difference of consecutive positions (with learnable λ) and acceleration is the difference of consecutive velocities. Two transformer variants integrate kinematics with slots: Joint-OCK concatenates them and processes through a standard self-attention encoder, while Cross-OCK uses cross-attention with slots as queries and kinematics as keys/values (with a temperature parameter τ for attention calibration). Kinematics can be fed analytically (predicting next-frame position via x_pos + x_vel·δ and propagating velocity/acceleration) or empirically (relying solely on gradient-based learning from the current frame). Training is two-stage: first a SAVi slot encoder is pretrained, then the OCK transformer is trained autoregressively with a combined L2 object-slot reconstruction loss and a frozen-decoder image reconstruction loss, without any task-specific kinematics supervision.

## Results

On the six synthetic datasets (OBJ3D, MOVi-A through MOVi-E), Cross-OCK achieves the best LPIPS on 5 of 6 datasets (e.g., 0.123 on MOVi-A, 0.236 on MOVi-D) and the best PSNR on MOVi-A (27.576) and MOVi-D (22.338), while Joint-OCK leads on OBJ3D PSNR (35.125) and MOVi-C PSNR (21.038). On the real-world Waymo Open Dataset, Cross-OCK achieves PSNR 25.979, SSIM 0.728, and LPIPS 0.220, substantially outperforming SlotFormer (PSNR 19.127, LPIPS 0.714). Notably, OCVP-Seq and OCVP-Par diverge entirely on MOVi-D and MOVi-E, whereas both OCK variants maintain stable predictions across all complexity levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OCK (both Joint and Cross variants) outperforms all three baselines across every dataset and metric. Against G-SWM [21], OCK gains roughly 2–4 dB in PSNR and 0.1–0.2 in LPIPS on complex MOVi datasets (e.g., Cross-OCK 22.338 vs. G-SWM 20.567 PSNR on MOVi-D). Against SlotFormer [44], the improvement is more pronounced on complex scenes: Cross-OCK achieves 21.040 PSNR vs. 19.482 on MOVi-C and 22.338 vs. 20.675 on MOVi-D, with LPIPS dropping from 0.534 to 0.376 and 0.332 to 0.236 respectively. Against OCVP-Seq/Par [37], OCK not only achieves higher scores on MOVi-A/B/C but also avoids the complete divergence that OCVP suffers on MOVi-D and MOVi-E, where OCVP's predictions collapse entirely. On the real-world Waymo dataset, Cross-OCK improves PSNR by ~6.9 dB over SlotFormer (25.979 vs. 19.127) and reduces LPIPS by 0.494 (0.220 vs. 0.714), while OCVP-Par diverges outright.

## Relation to Prior Work

OCK extends the SlotFormer [44] and OCVP [37] lineage of transformer-based object-centric video prediction by introducing explicit Object Kinematics (position, velocity, acceleration) as a structured input alongside learned object slots. While SlotFormer and OCVP rely solely on implicit appearance features extracted via Slot Attention [22] to model dynamics, OCK augments these with analytically or empirically computed kinematic states derived from object centers. This departs from G-SWM [21], which models interactions through graph neural networks over appearance-only representations, by making motion an explicit, permutation-equivariant attribute that the transformer can attend to directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical finding is that OCVP, a recent SOTA, completely diverges on the most complex MOVi datasets (D, E) while OCK maintains stable predictions, suggesting that purely appearance-based slot representations are insufficient for long-horizon prediction in complex scenes. The kinematics are computed via simple numerical differences of object centers without any task-specific loss, making the approach lightweight and interpretable. However, the authors acknowledge a significant gap in slot-based encoders for in-the-wild data, and the kinematics are limited to 2D position/velocity/acceleration without rotation or scaling, leaving open whether richer 3D kinematic representations would further improve real-world generalization.
