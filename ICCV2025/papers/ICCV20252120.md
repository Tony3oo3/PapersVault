## Problem

Reconstruction-based anomaly detection suffers from the 'identical shortcut' problem, where anomalous information leaks from conditioning inputs and accumulates through iterative denoising, skewing generation toward anomalous manifolds. Prior remedies (e.g., VPOT, LafitE) impose conditions as time-invariant priors, creating a trade-off: excessive anomaly suppression erodes normal details, while insufficient suppression lets anomalies persist. The paper identifies the missing ingredient as a time-variant condition mechanism that adaptively prioritizes anomaly suppression in early timesteps and detail restoration in later ones.

## Input / Output

For single-modality settings, the method takes a 256×256 RGB image, encodes it via VAE into a 32×32×3 latent, and produces a reconstructed normal latent plus an anomaly score map computed from L1-norm and perceptual distance (Gaussian-smoothed, kernel=5). For cross-modal face anti-spoofing (CeFA), infrared images (channel-duplicated to 3, normalized to [-1,1]) serve as the generation target while CLIP tokens extracted from the RGB modality provide conditioning. Inference runs 10 timesteps starting from t₀=0.5 with a curvature-based mask ratio of 0.4 to select which spatial tokens receive low-dimension guidance.

## Method

DTG operates on a rectified-flow (straight-path) formulation of Flow Matching built on InstaFlow/Stable Diffusion 1.5. Top-down Trace Distillation learns a low-dimension mapping ω(·) implemented as a multi-expert quad-tree 2D wavelet decomposition (M=8 paired lowpass/highpass filters, level l=2, kernel length K=6) that spatially compresses the latent by 2^l while preserving global structure; it is trained with Skinhorn Distance (using Perlin-noise-filled DTD textures as anomaly proxies), wavelet reconstruction loss, and coefficient constraints enforcing unit ℓ₂ norm and √2 sum. Bottom-up Velocity Alignment then injects velocity features extracted from a finetuned low-dimension UNet encoder into the high-dimension UNet via KV-Injection Attention (concatenating low-dim tokens into key and value projections), while an Alignment Mask zeros out attention scores for randomly sampled (training) or top-curvature (inference) tokens, forcing those spatial regions to reconstruct details solely from the anomaly-robust low-dimension global context. Training proceeds in three stages: learn ω, finetune the low-dim UNet on distilled pairs, then finetune the high-dim UNet with the frozen low-dim encoder as a conditional guide.

## Results

On MVTec-AD (15 categories), DTG achieves a mean AUROC_cls/AUROC_seg of 99.0/98.4, tying with VPOT and surpassing the prior best diffusion method DiAD (98.5/97.6) by 0.5/0.8. On VisA (12 categories with more complex scenarios), DTG reaches 96.6/98.3, outperforming VPOT (94.2/98.9) by 2.4 in AUROC_cls, with particularly strong gains on multi-instance categories (average +4.1 AUROC_cls). On the cross-modal CeFA face anti-spoofing benchmark, DTG achieves 2.4% ACER under Cross-Ethnicity protocol, outperforming the second-best SpoofTrace (5.0%) by 52% relative reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MVTec-AD, DTG ties with VPOT (99.0/98.4) on mean AUROC but shows larger per-category gains, e.g., +4.6 AUROC_cls over DiAD on Cable and +9.3 over DiAD on Capsule, where the temporal debiasing guidance is most beneficial. On VisA, DTG (96.6/98.3) clearly outperforms VPOT (94.2/98.9) by 2.4 AUROC_cls, with the largest margin on multi-instance categories (Macaroni 2: +12.7 AUROC_cls over VPOT), while slightly trailing VPOT by 0.6 in AUROC_seg. Non-diffusion baselines like PatchCore (99.1/98.1 on MVTec) remain competitive on detection but lack pixel-level localization capability in multi-class settings. On CeFA, DTG (2.4% ACER Cross-Ethnicity) substantially outperforms SpoofTrace (5.0%) and PSMM-Net (3.5%), though SpoofTrace retains an edge on the Cross-PAI protocol (0.9% vs. 2.0% ACER). Overall, DTG achieves the best or tied-best results across all three benchmarks, with the most pronounced advantages on categories with complex structural anomalies and multi-instance scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Flow Matching and Rectified Flow frameworks (Lipman et al., Liu et al.) and uses InstaFlow as its base generative architecture, but departs fundamentally from prior diffusion-based anomaly detection methods (VPOT, LafitE, DiAD) that treat conditioning as a time-invariant prior. Where those methods attempt to suppress anomalous information in the condition itself—either by learning vague prototypes or narrowing initial-state gaps—DTG instead introduces a time-varying guidance mechanism: a low-dimensional, anomaly-robust sub-trace distilled via learnable wavelet filters provides velocity features that are injected into the main generation process through KV-Injection Attention. This reframes the 'identical shortcut' problem from one of condition purification to one of velocity-field alignment across dimensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine and well-identified trade-off in reconstruction-based anomaly detection: over-suppressing anomalies erodes normal detail, while under-suppressing lets anomalies leak into reconstructions. The time-varying condition mechanism is a conceptually clean resolution, and the extension to cross-modal face anti-spoofing (CeFA) demonstrates broader applicability. However, the practical gains are modest on MVTec-AD (+0.3 AUROC_cls) and the method's complexity—three training stages, learnable wavelet kernels, a frozen conditional encoder, and a separate high-dimension UNet—raises questions about scalability and ease of adoption. Open questions include whether the wavelet-based distillation generalizes beyond 2D images, whether the alignment mask ratio can be made adaptive per sample, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D anomaly detection.
