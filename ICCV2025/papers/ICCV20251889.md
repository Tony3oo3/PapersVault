## Problem

Existing camera-LiDAR fusion strategies cannot simultaneously achieve computational efficiency, long-range global modeling, and retention of complete scene information: dense local fusion is short-sighted, sparse global fusion discards background context, and quasi-dense global fusion remains window-limited. While linear-complexity operators like Mamba and linear attention offer a path to efficient global fusion, the paper shows that naively substituting them into existing frameworks degrades performance, revealing that height information loss during discrete voxel quantization causes multi-modal alignment errors that sequential processing amplifies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251889/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 6-camera multi-view images at 704×256 resolution and a 32-beam LiDAR point cloud voxelized at [0.3, 0.3, 0.25] m within a [-54, 54] m × [-5, 3] m spatial range. It produces 3D bounding boxes with category labels and orientations, evaluated via mAP and NDS on nuScenes. Notably, fusion occurs in two complementary coordinate systems—raw 3D image-plane space (LiDAR projected onto camera planes) and BEV space (camera features lifted via LSS)—rather than a single unified representation.

## Method

The core contribution is a Hybrid Mamba Block (HMB) that performs dense global fusion by combining a Local Mamba (aggregating features within w×w non-overlapping spatial regions) and a Global Mamba (serializing all multi-modal features via a Hilbert curve over 3D coordinates and processing them bidirectionally), achieving linear-complexity interaction across the entire scene. To make this work, the paper introduces Height-Fidelity LiDAR Encoding, which computes voxel centroids via ScatterMean on raw point coordinates in continuous 3D space at every downsampling stage (rather than averaging discrete grid indices) and applies a conflict test to exclude generated voxels that would overlap with neighbors, thereby preserving precise height information critical for camera-LiDAR alignment. The fusion pipeline first aligns modality distributions through a shared HMB, then performs cross-modal information exchange in both image-plane space (LiDAR voxels projected onto camera feature grids) and BEV space (camera features lifted via LSS and merged with point cloud features), before a detection head operates on the fused BEV representation. The key insight is that sequential Mamba processing amplifies small alignment errors far more than attention-based methods, making height-fidelity encoding a prerequisite rather than an optional refinement.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, MambaFusion-Base achieves 75.0 NDS and 72.7 mAP, setting a new state-of-the-art among camera-LiDAR fusion methods. On the nuScenes test set, it reaches 75.9 NDS and 73.2 mAP. The Lite variant achieves 74.0 NDS / 71.6 mAP (val) at 5.4 FPS, while the Base variant runs at 4.7 FPS — approximately 50% faster than IS-FUSION (3.2 FPS) and 62% faster than SparseLIF (2.9 FPS). The method also demonstrates superior robustness under sensor degradation (e.g., 74.2 NDS under LiDAR-camera timestamp misalignment vs. 71.8 for UniTR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MambaFusion-Base outperforms all 16 compared methods on nuScenes validation NDS (75.0), with the closest competitor being SparseLIF at 74.6 NDS and IS-FUSION/DAL at 74.0 NDS. On validation mAP, it edges out IS-FUSION (72.7 vs. 72.8, a 0.1 gap) while clearly surpassing SparseLIF (72.7 vs. 71.2) and UniTR (72.7 vs. 70.5). On the test set, MambaFusion-Base leads all methods reporting test results, achieving 75.9 NDS / 73.2 mAP versus IS-FUSION's 75.2 / 73.0 and DAL's 74.8 / 72.0. In terms of speed, the Base model (4.7 FPS) is ~50% faster than IS-FUSION (3.2 FPS) and ~62% faster than SparseLIF (2.9 FPS), while the Lite model (5.4 FPS) is the fastest among all methods reporting FPS. Under robustness stress tests (Table 8), MambaFusion maintains 74.2 NDS under LiDAR-camera timestamp misalignment, outperforming UniTR (71.8) and BEVFusion (70.3), and achieves 72.3 NDS with only the front camera preserved, surpassing all baselines. The method also improves end-to-end planning when plugged into FusionAD, reducing displacement error from 0.81 to 0.70 and collision rate from 0.12 to 0.10.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as the first to achieve 'Dense Global Fusion' in camera-LiDAR 3D detection by replacing the quadratic-complexity window-based Transformer (as in UniTR) with a pure linear-complexity Mamba-based architecture. It builds directly on Mamba's selective state space model for sequential processing, LION for the LiDAR backbone, and VMamba for camera tokenization, while adopting UniTR's overall pipeline structure. The key conceptual departure is the identification of height-information loss in discrete voxel quantization as the root cause of multi-modal misalignment, addressed through a novel height-fidelity encoding that computes voxel coordinates in continuous 3D space. The Hybrid Mamba Block, which combines local and global Mamba modules serialized via Hilbert curves, represents a new architectural primitive for 3D multi-modal fusion that is distinct from prior window-based or object-centric attention approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that pure linear operations can match or exceed Transformer-based fusion in camera-LiDAR 3D detection while being 1.5× faster, which is practically significant for real-time autonomous driving deployment. The diagnostic finding that naive Mamba substitution degrades performance due to height quantization errors—rather than a fundamental limitation of SSMs—provides actionable guidance for future work integrating linear sequence models into 3D perception. Open questions include whether the height-fidelity encoding generalizes to other sensor pairs (e.g., radar-camera), whether the Hilbert curve serialization remains effective at larger spatial extents, and whether the approach extends to multi-frame temporal fusion without additional architectural changes.
