## Problem

Existing vision-based semantic scene completion methods construct unified voxel features that entangle geometry and semantics, causing occluded regions at different depth positions to share identical 2D semantic information and producing ambiguous or erroneous semantic segmentation. This paper tackles the problem by explicitly decoupling the SSC objective into separate geometric and semantic sub-goals, each addressed by a dedicated voxel construction pathway guided by SAM shape priors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252654/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of stereo RGB images (left and right views; 1226×370 on SemanticKITTI, 1408×376 on SSCBench-KITTI-360) and produces a 3D semantic voxel grid of size 256×256×32 covering a 51.2 m × 51.2 m × 6.4 m scene at 0.2 m resolution, where each voxel is assigned one of 19–20 semantic class labels. A frozen pre-trained ViT-B SAM model is used as a feature and mask source throughout, and the network is trained end-to-end with a combined loss of depth supervision, semantic affinity, geometry, and class-weighted cross-entropy terms.

## Method

SDFormer builds two parallel voxel representations and fuses them via adaptive cross-attention. The Semantic Constructor (SC) encodes the relationship between the left image and SAM image-encoder features using a Semantic Calibration Affinity (SCA) module—six transformer layers with multi-head self-attention that produce relative position coefficients for projecting aligned semantic pixels into a 3D semantic voxel via iterative depth-hypothesis sampling. The Geometric Refiner (GR) first estimates dense depth through stereo matching with an EfficientNetB7 backbone, then refines semantic placement using two SAM-driven components: Learnable Mask Generation (LMG), which applies random box prompts to SAM to obtain saliency-weighted shape mask features, and Shape Segments Interactive (SSI), which fuses these shape features with view-transformation contextual features via a max/average-pooling gating mechanism. The final voxel is obtained by cross-attention between the two channels, reducing semantic errors from depth ambiguity while preserving geometric accuracy.

## Results

On the SemanticKITTI hidden test set, SDFormer achieves 45.30% IoU and 17.42% mIoU, outperforming all camera-based baselines including HTCL-S (44.23% IoU, 17.09% mIoU) and CGFormer (44.41% IoU, 16.63% mIoU). On SSCBench-KITTI-360, SDFormer reaches 46.99% IoU and 20.59% mIoU, surpassing BRGScene (45.73% IoU, 17.56% mIoU) and Symphonies (44.12% IoU, 18.58% mIoU), and even exceeding LiDAR-based SSCNet in mIoU. At close range (12.8 m) on the SemanticKITTI validation set, SDFormer attains 26.45% mIoU, narrowing the gap with point-cloud methods like SSCNet (20.02% mIoU at 12.8 m).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDFormer outperforms the strongest temporal baseline HTCL-S (which uses 5-frame input) by +1.07 IoU and +0.33 mIoU on the SemanticKITTI hidden test, while using only a single stereo pair. Against the single-frame baseline BRGScene, SDFormer gains +1.96 IoU and +2.06 mIoU, confirming the benefit of the dual-voxel construction. On SSCBench-KITTI-360, SDFormer leads all camera-based methods in mIoU (20.59%) but trails CGFormer slightly in IoU (46.99% vs. 48.07%), indicating that the geometric channel is marginally weaker on this dataset while the semantic channel is stronger. Compared to the earlier VoxFormer-T, SDFormer improves mIoU by a substantial +4.01 points (17.42 vs. 13.41), largely attributable to the SAM-assisted semantic voxel. At close range (12.8 m) on the validation set, SDFormer (26.45% mIoU) still lags behind HTCL-S (27.37% mIoU) by ~0.9 points, but at 51.2 m it edges ahead (17.18% vs. 17.13%), suggesting the dual-channel design is more effective for long-range scene completion.

## Relation to Prior Work

SDFormer extends the camera-based 3D semantic scene completion line initiated by MonoScene and advanced by VoxFormer, BRGScene, and OccFormer, which all construct a single unified voxel representation from 2D image features. The key architectural departure is the dual-channel decomposition: rather than one voxel carrying both geometry and semantics (where occluded regions inherit ambiguous shared 2D semantics across depth), SDFormer builds a dedicated Semantic Constructor voxel guided by SAM shape priors and a separate Geometric Refiner voxel from stereo matching, then fuses them via cross-attention. The Geometric Refiner's stereo-matching and BEV integration directly follows BRGScene, while the Semantic Constructor repurposes SAM's frozen image encoder and mask decoder as a shape-prior source—a use of SAM in 3D scene understanding that goes beyond the 2D segmentation or 3D reconstruction settings explored in prior SAM-based work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-channel voxel decomposition offers a principled way to address the well-known occlusion-induced semantic ambiguity in camera-based SSC, and the practical integration of a frozen SAM encoder as a shape prior is a reusable pattern for other 3D perception tasks. However, the performance gains over strong baselines (HTCL-S, CGFormer) are modest—roughly 0.3–0.5 mIoU on SemanticKITTI and KITTI-360—and the ablations show each SAM-dependent component (SCA, LMG, SSI) contributes only 0.6–0.8 mIoU individually, raising questions about whether the added complexity and inference cost of running SAM are justified over simpler learned shape features. Open questions include whether the dual-channel approach generalizes to multi-view or video settings, whether the frozen SAM encoder can be replaced by a lighter learned alternative without significant loss, and how the method scales to denser voxel grids or higher-resolution inputs.
