## Problem

Cross-domain medical image segmentation suffers from both performance degradation due to domain shift and fairness bias across demographic subgroups. Existing UDA methods achieve fairness but with clinically limited accuracy (IoU < 40%), while emerging ADA methods boost performance yet entirely ignore fairness. No prior paradigm simultaneously addresses both challenges, forcing an undesirable trade-off between equitable outcomes and model effectiveness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251786/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled fundus images from the target domain paired with their sensitive attribute labels (race, ethnicity, or gender) encoded as text prompts (e.g., 'A photo of a fundus and the race is Black'). Only 5% of target samples are selected for expert annotation, producing pixel-level segmentation masks for cup and rim regions. The final output is a fine-tuned TransUNet segmentation model that generalizes across demographic subgroups with reduced bias.

## Method

The framework has two core stages. First, VLM-attribute Learning uses a CLIP ViT-B/16 model with contrastive learning to align fundus image features with sensitive-attribute text descriptions; the language encoder is frozen while the vision encoder is trained, yielding dense vision-based attribute features that capture semantic correlations between visual content and protected attributes. Second, the FairAP selection strategy operates in three steps: (1) Fair Quota allocates equal sample budgets per subgroup, (2) Attribute Representation applies k-means++ clustering within each subgroup and selects nearest-to-centroid samples, and (3) Polysemy Representation selects samples near inter-racial cluster boundaries to capture cross-attribute diversity. The selected 5% of target samples are expert-annotated and used to fine-tune a TransUNet segmentation model via standard cross-entropy loss, producing a target fair model that simultaneously improves overall accuracy and reduces subgroup disparities.

## Results

On the FairDomain benchmark (Race attribute), FairAP achieves ES-Dice of 0.828 and IoU of 0.747 for cup segmentation, and ES-Dice of 0.711 and IoU of 0.667 for rim segmentation, all with statistical significance (p<0.05). For rim segmentation, FairAP improves ES-IoU by ~20% (0.592 vs. 0.369 for DAFormer-FIA) and IoU by ~30% (0.667 vs. 0.369) over the best fairness-aware DA method. Among ADA methods, FairAP leads on all four overall metrics for both cup and rim, with the largest margin over STDR on rim ES-Dice (+2.3 pp) and ES-IoU (+2.2 pp).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FairAP outperforms all eight baselines on every overall metric for both cup and rim segmentation. Against the strongest DA method (DAFormer-FIA [41]), the gains are most dramatic on rim: ES-Dice improves from 0.521 to 0.711 (+19 pp) and IoU from 0.369 to 0.667 (+30 pp), while cup ES-Dice rises from 0.796 to 0.828 (+3.2 pp). Among ADA methods, FairAP edges out the closest competitor Detective [61] by +1.4 pp ES-Dice and +0.7 pp IoU on cup, and by +1.1 pp ES-Dice and +0.8 pp IoU on rim. The margin over STDR [47] is slightly larger on rim (ES-Dice +2.3 pp, ES-IoU +2.2 pp), reflecting FairAP's fairness-aware quota allocation. DAFormer [12] and PixMatch [28] are substantially outperformed, with DAFormer actually degrading below the target-domain baseline on rim (IoU 0.202 vs. 0.342). All improvements are statistically significant (paired t-test, p<0.05), and the same pattern holds for the Ethnicity attribute (Table 3), where FairAP achieves ES-Dice 0.836 (cup) and 0.784 (rim), again leading all DA and ADA baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established threads: fairness-aware medical imaging (pre/in/post-processing), unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA methods like PixMatch, DAFormer, and the fairness-aware DAFormer-FIA from FairDomain), and active domain adaptation (ADA methods like MHPL, DML-Core, STDR). It departs from prior fairness work by repurposing CLIP's contrastive vision-language alignment to create continuous, semantically rich attribute representations in the visual feature space, replacing the simplistic 0/1 categorical encodings used in FairDomain. The FairAP selection strategy then combines k-means++ clustering for attribute representativeness with a novel 'polysemy' criterion (samples near cluster boundaries of other subgroups) for active sample selection, a formulation not present in prior ADA or fairness literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to demonstrate that fairness and performance need not be traded off in cross-domain medical segmentation: by actively selecting a small (5%) diverse and attribute-balanced subset for expert annotation, the method simultaneously improves ES-Dice/ES-IoU fairness metrics and overall segmentation accuracy over both UDA and ADA baselines. The practical implication is significant for clinical deployment, where annotation budgets are limited and equitable performance across demographic subgroups is a regulatory and ethical requirement. Key limitations include evaluation on a single benchmark (Harvard-FairDomain, fundus images only), a fixed set of three demographic attributes, and reliance on CLIP's pre-trained representations for medical imagery; whether the VLM-attribute alignment generalizes to other modalities (CT, MRI) or more complex attribute hierarchies remains open.
