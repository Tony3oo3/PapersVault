## Problem

No publicly available eye-tracking dataset exists for CT scans, leaving 3D scanpath prediction in medical imaging unexplored. Existing scanpath predictors are designed for 2D images and cannot model the unique volumetric behaviors of radiologists, such as back-and-forth slice navigation and spatial integration across depths, making them inadequate for CT interpretation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252170/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 3D CT volume at 96×96×96 resolution and produces a sequence of up to N=400 fixations, each a 4-tuple (x, y, z, t) encoding 3D spatial coordinates and fixation duration. The spatial output is a probability distribution over H×W×D+1 positions (the extra position being a learned 'stop' token for sequence termination), while durations are sampled from a learned Gaussian via the re-parameterization trick, giving the output a probabilistic character.

## Method

CT-Searcher is a transformer-based architecture that uses a frozen Swin UNETR encoder to extract 3D voxel features, which are projected into a token sequence augmented with a special 'stop' token and 3D sinusoidal positional encoding (adapted from 2D by splitting the embedding dimension across x, y, z axes). A 6-layer transformer encoder composes 3D-aware representations, and a 6-layer decoder with N learnable gaze queries attends to these representations to produce decoded features. Spatial prediction applies a fully-connected layer followed by matrix multiplication with the encoded features and softmax to yield 3D fixation probability maps, while duration prediction uses the re-parameterization trick to regress mean and log-variance parameters. Training uses a combined cross-entropy loss (for spatial maps, framed as classification over H×W×D+1 positions) and L1 loss (for durations), with a pretraining step that converts 2D CXR gaze data from EGD and REFLACX into synthetic 3D fixations by flipping x, fixing z at the mid-slice, and mapping y to the slice dimension, followed by Gaussian augmentation of coordinates.

## Results

On CT-ScanGaze (5-fold cross-validation), CT-Searcher achieves a ScanMatch score of 0.1466 (w/o duration) and 0.1170 (w/ duration), substantially outperforming the strongest baseline GazeformerISP (0.0828 / 0.0711). In MultiMatch, CT-Searcher scores 0.7859 on position and 0.5003 on duration, compared to GazeformerISP's 0.7354 and 0.3375. On spatial metrics, CT-Searcher attains CC 0.1706, KLDiv 3.645, and NSS 1.1422, all leading the baselines. The lowest SED (174±18) further confirms superior temporal alignment with ground-truth scanpaths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CT-Searcher outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest baseline GazeformerISP [13], CT-Searcher improves ScanMatch by ~77% (0.1466 vs 0.0828), MultiMatch Position by ~7% (0.7859 vs 0.7354), MultiMatch Duration by ~48% (0.5003 vs 0.3375), and reduces SED by ~27% (174 vs 238). On spatial metrics, CT-Searcher achieves CC 0.1706 vs GazeformerISP's 0.1104 and KLDiv 3.645 vs 5.023, indicating markedly better alignment of predicted fixation heatmaps with ground truth. Gazeformer [50] suffers from consistency issues due to its separate MLP heads for x, y, and duration prediction, yielding a high KLDiv of 23.332. HAT [80] struggles with the limited CT-ScanGaze training data (ScanMatch only 0.0171), while PathGAN [4] performs worst overall across all metrics, confirming that GAN-based approaches are ill-suited for the high-dimensional 3D search space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 2D scanpath prediction literature (PathGAN, Gazeformer, GazeformerISP, HAT) to the 3D volumetric setting of CT imaging, where radiologists exhibit fundamentally different search behaviors such as back-and-forth slice navigation and depth-wise spatial integration. Architecturally, CT-Searcher follows the DETR-style learnable-query transformer decoder paradigm established by Gazeformer, but replaces the 2D encoder with Swin UNETR and introduces a 3D sinusoidal positional encoding adapted from the 2D formulation. The pretraining pipeline repurposes existing 2D medical gaze datasets (EGD, REFLACX) by converting CXRs to CTs and mapping 2D fixations into 3D coordinates, a creative but domain-gap-laden strategy to overcome the small size of the new CT-ScanGaze dataset (909 volumes vs. 6,202 in COCO-Search18).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CT-ScanGaze fills a critical gap as the first publicly available CT eye gaze dataset, unlocking multiple underexplored tasks in volumetric medical imaging including 3D scanpath prediction, explainable report generation, and radiology training. The cross-radiologist evaluation alleviates concerns about individual style overfitting, but the dataset's small scale (909 volumes, 2 radiologists) and the still-low absolute ScanMatch scores (~0.15) suggest the task remains far from solved. Open questions include whether the 2D-to-3D pretraining strategy generalizes to other modalities (MRI, ultrasound), whether more radiologists or longer sessions would substantially improve model performance, and how well these predicted scanpaths transfer to clinical decision-support or collaborative CAD systems.
