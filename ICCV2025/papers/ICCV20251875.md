## Problem

Existing text-based person search benchmarks (CUHK-PEDES, ICFG-PEDES, RSTPReid) are heavily biased toward routine actions like walking and standing, failing to capture the full spectrum of real-world pedestrian behaviors. Meanwhile, video anomaly detection datasets suffer from limited scale (<500k frames), coarse binary annotations, and severe long-tail distributions where anomaly keyframes are underrepresented. This paper introduces text-based person anomaly search, a new task that requires locating a specific pedestrian engaged in either normal or anomalous behavior via natural language, bridging the gap between person retrieval and fine-grained anomaly understanding.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language query describing a pedestrian's appearance, specific action (e.g., 'lying on the ground', 'playing soccer'), and background context, and retrieves the matching image from a candidate pool of pedestrian images. The PAB benchmark provides 1,013,605 synthesized training image-text pairs (generated via Realistic Vision V4.0 diffusion model with Qwen2-VL re-captioning) and 1,978 real-world test pairs extracted from OOPS! videos. Unlike traditional person search where any image of the same identity counts as correct, anomaly search requires the top-ranked image to match both identity AND the specific behavioral state described in the text.

## Method

The Cross-Modal Pose-aware (CMP) framework integrates three components: (1) a Pose-aware Image Encoder that extracts OpenPose keypoint maps, processes them through a Pose Conv module, and fuses pose features into a Swin-B image encoder via multi-head cross-attention to explicitly encode behavioral posture; (2) Identity-based Hard Negative Mining (IHNM) that exploits the paired normal/anomaly structure of PAB to construct hard negatives sharing the same pedestrian identity and background but differing only in action, forcing the model to learn discriminative behavioral features; (3) a cross-modal modeling module combining three losses — Image-Text Matching (last 6 BERT layers as cross encoder), bidirectional contrastive learning, and Masked Language Modeling — optimized jointly. The model (230.8M parameters) is initialized from X-VLM weights and trained for 30 epochs with AdamW, achieving 84.93% R@1 on PAB and 55.23% R@1 on the out-of-distribution UCC test set.

## Results

On the proposed PAB benchmark, CMP trained on the full 1M image-text pairs achieves 84.93% R@1 and 91.66% mAP, outperforming the strongest baseline X-VLM (81.95% R@1, 89.86% mAP) by +2.98% R@1. With only 0.1M training pairs, CMP still leads all baselines at 83.06% R@1 and 90.41% mAP. On the out-of-distribution UCC test set (extracted from UCF-Crime), CMP(1M) reaches 55.23% R@1 and 44.35% mAP, surpassing the next-best method RaSa (54.12% R@1, 39.71% mAP) by +1.11% R@1 and +4.64% mAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMP outperforms all eight baselines on PAB under both 0.1M and 1M training settings. At 0.1M, CMP (83.06% R@1) beats the strongest baseline X-VLM (81.95%) by +1.11% R@1 and RaSa (80.79%) by +2.27% R@1; the gap widens to +2.98% R@1 over X-VLM when scaling to 1M data. On the OOD UCC test set, CMP(0.1M) ties RaSa at 54.12% R@1 but surpasses it by +3.42% mAP (43.13 vs. 39.71), while CMP(1M) extends the lead to 55.23% R@1 and 44.35% mAP. Zero-shot X-VLM (71.94% R@1) substantially outperforms all task-specific person search methods (IRRA 30.59%, CLIP 47.57%), indicating that large-scale vision-language pre-training provides a strong prior, yet fine-tuning on PAB data still yields a consistent +10–13% R@1 gain across all methods. The ablation confirms both PE (+0.66% R@1) and IHNM (+1.01% R@1) contribute independently, with their combination yielding the full +1.46% improvement over the X-VLM baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of text-based person search (CUHK-PEDES, ICFG-PEDES, RSTPReid) and video anomaly detection (UCF-Crime, ShanghaiTech, UBnormal), departing from both: it moves person search beyond appearance to require fine-grained behavioral matching, and it moves anomaly detection beyond binary video-level tags to text-queried, frame-level retrieval. Methodologically, it builds on X-VLM as the initialization backbone, Swin Transformer for image encoding, and BERT-based cross-encoding with CLIP-style contrastive learning, while introducing pose-aware cross-attention (via OpenPose) and identity-based hard negative mining as task-specific components. The data pipeline repurposes diffusion models (Realistic Vision V4.0) and MLLMs (Qwen2-VL) for large-scale synthetic training data generation, a strategy inspired by prior synthetic-data work in person re-identification (MALS, Tan et al.) but applied here to the anomaly-behavior domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PAB benchmark (1.01M training pairs, 1,978 real test pairs) fills a concrete gap: no prior dataset supports text-queried retrieval of specific pedestrian behaviors, and existing anomaly datasets are limited to binary labels or <500k frames. The deliberately elevated 3:2 anomaly-to-normal ratio and 1,600 anomaly action types make it a useful resource for training behavior-sensitive retrieval models. However, the significant OOD performance drop (84.93% R@1 on PAB → 55.23% on UCC) reveals that synthetic training data does not fully bridge the domain gap to unseen real-world anomaly distributions, and the small real-world test set (989 pairs) limits statistical confidence. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more diverse anomaly taxonomies, whether the synthetic-to-real gap can be closed with better generation or domain adaptation, and how the method performs under the occlusion and viewpoint challenges typical of surveillance footage.
