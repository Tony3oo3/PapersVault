## Problem

Vision-language models like CLIP inherit and amplify spurious correlations from pretraining data, causing skewed zero-shot predictions (e.g., associating camels exclusively with deserts). Existing debiasing methods either require predefined bias categories, external visual data, or sacrifice overall accuracy when removing biased components. The paper addresses the gap of a fully data-free, task-agnostic debiasing approach that identifies biases dynamically without fixed categories or auxiliary images while preserving the model's general classification performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a set of K text prompts (formatted as 'A photo of a <class>') as input, with no external images or labeled data required. At inference, it produces a class label via argmax over inner products in a projected embedding space. A key detail is that during the offline debiasing stage, an LLM is prompted with only the class labels to generate scene descriptions encoding spurious attributes, and the learned output is a linear projection matrix P (of dimension d×d) applied to both image and text embeddings at test time.

## Method

PRISM operates in two stages. In Stage 1, an LLM is prompted with class labels to extract potential spurious attributes and generate controlled scene descriptions for each (attribute, class) group, exploiting the LLM's learned co-occurrence statistics to surface biases without predefined categories. In Stage 2, a novel Latent space Debiasing (LD) loss—a contrastive-style objective with two terms—trains a linear projection P: the intra-class inter-attribute term minimizes distance between embeddings of the same class under different spurious attributes (enforcing invariance), while the inter-class intra-attribute term applies a margin-based penalty to keep embeddings of different classes with the same spurious attribute separated. The key insight is that spurious correlations present in the text modality of CLIP's aligned embedding space can be leveraged to debias the image modality, eliminating the need for external visual data. A lighter variant, PRISM-mini, skips optimization entirely and computes P as a closed-form orthogonal projection against the text embeddings of the spurious attributes, trading some debiasing strength for zero training cost.

## Results

On Waterbirds, PRISM achieves 84.2% worst-group (WG) accuracy and 93.6% overall accuracy, outperforming all data-free baselines and even the best image-based method (FairerCLIP: 78.1% WG, 85.1% Acc). On CelebA, PRISM reaches 84.0% WG and 86.9% overall accuracy, setting the best trade-off among data-free methods. The lighter variant PRISM-mini, which requires no optimization, still achieves 69.5% WG on Waterbirds and 82.6% WG on CelebA, ranking second among all data-free approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among data-free methods, PRISM decisively outperforms all baselines on both datasets: on Waterbirds it improves WG by 15.4 points over the next-best data-free method (Orth-Cali, 68.8%) and by 39.0 points over RoboShot (45.2%); on CelebA it leads by 1.4 points over RoboShot (82.6%) and by 7.9 points over Orth-Cali (76.1%). Against image-based methods, PRISM surpasses WiSE-FT (65.9% WG) and both DFR variants (51.9%/65.9% WG) on Waterbirds while maintaining competitive overall accuracy (93.6% vs. 97.6%/96.1%). On CelebA, FairerCLIP [7] edges out PRISM on both WG (86.1% vs. 84.0%) and Acc (88.0% vs. 86.9%), but requires auxiliary images for debiasing, whereas PRISM is fully data-free. PRISM-mini, which skips the optimization stage entirely, still beats all data-free baselines on Waterbirds WG (69.5%) and matches RoboShot on CelebA WG (82.6%), making it a strong low-resource alternative.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRISM carves out a new niche at the intersection of three existing debiasing paradigms for VLMs: feature-space projection (as in Orth-Proj and FairerCLIP), prompt-based bias identification (as in RoboShot and BendVLM), and contrastive representation learning. Its key conceptual departure is making the entire pipeline data-free and task-agnostic by using an LLM to dynamically discover spurious attributes from class labels alone, rather than relying on predefined bias categories or external image data. The novel LD loss repurposes contrastive learning principles (intra-class invariance, inter-class separation) specifically for debiasing the joint embedding space, which prior projection methods like Orth-Proj handled only through fixed orthogonalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The data-free, task-agnostic property makes PRISM broadly applicable to any CLIP-based zero-shot task without task-specific bias annotations or auxiliary datasets, addressing a practical bottleneck in deploying debiased VLMs. The empirical confirmation that text-domain spurious correlations mirror image-domain ones (Table 2) provides a useful theoretical justification for text-only debiasing. However, the reliance on a linear projection limits applicability to complex non-linear biases, and the strong dependence on LLM quality (Table 3: 58.9% WG with Llama 3.2 70B vs. 84.0% with GPT-4o) raises questions about robustness in settings where only smaller models are available. Open questions include whether non-linear projections or iterative refinement could close the remaining gap to image-based methods like FairerCLIP on harder bias structures.
