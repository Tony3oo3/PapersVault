## Problem

Existing reward models for text-to-image generation (CLIP, BLIP, PickScore, ImageReward, HPSv2) inherit a fundamental flaw from contrastive pre-training: they assume equivalence between image and text information content, causing them to assign lower scores to high-quality images with rich details than to simpler images that merely match the prompt. This 'reward scoring paradox' means that using these models to optimize state-of-the-art diffusion models like SD3.5 actually degrades output quality by steering generation toward visually sparse, aesthetically deficient images.

## Input / Output

The reward model takes a generated RGB image and a text prompt (either a basic or LLM-refined version) and outputs a scalar quality score. The ICT model computes a containment score via CLIP's dual-encoder (text encoder dot image encoder divided by temperature), while the HP model scores images using only the image encoder plus an MLP head. For diffusion model optimization, the reward functions are applied to LoRA adapter weights of SD3.5-Large-Turbo, with 32,000 non-repetitive prompts from the Pick-High dataset driving the DRaFT-K fine-tuning loop.

## Method

The core insight is replacing bidirectional text-image alignment with a one-directional 'image-contains-text' (ICT) objective: a threshold-based score min(CLIP(I,P)/θ, 1) caps alignment at 1 so that additional visual detail beyond the prompt is never penalized, and refined-prompt scores are further scaled by text-text CLIP similarity. The ICT model is fine-tuned from CLIP using MSE loss against these ICT labels plus an in-batch negative-sample loss with sigmoid-based soft weighting to handle false negatives. A second-stage HP model, trained with margin ranking loss on image triplets using only the image modality, captures aesthetic and detail quality once ICT reaches its ceiling. The combined ICT-HP reward (product of both scores) is then used as a differentiable objective in DRaFT-K to optimize SD3.5-Turbo LoRA adapters, and the entire pipeline is supported by the newly constructed Pick-High dataset of 360K high-quality image triplets generated via LLM chain-of-thought prompt refinement.

## Results

On the Pick-High/PickAPic_v2 preference prediction benchmark, the proposed ICT-HP model achieves 88.84% average pairwise accuracy, surpassing PickScore (79.04%) by ~10% and HPS_v2 (72.88%) by ~16%. On the I3>I2 and I3>I1 comparisons (distinguishing high-quality from medium/low-quality images), ICT, HP, and ICT-HP all reach 100% accuracy, while the best baseline (PickScore) only achieves 75.37% and 86.94% respectively. On the GenEval benchmark for downstream generation quality, the ICT-optimized SD3.5-Turbo achieves the highest mean score (0.71) and best counting accuracy (0.70), while ICT-HP achieves the best two-object (0.88) and color attribution (0.58) scores. The HP model also yields the best JPEG compressibility (334.86) and aesthetic score (6.448) on Parti-Prompts, slightly surpassing SD3.5-Large and FLUX.1-dev.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The ICT-HP model outperforms all existing reward models on average preference prediction accuracy (88.84% vs. PickScore 79.04%, HPS_v2 72.88%, ImageReward 63.81%, CLIP 60.30%), with the largest gains on distinguishing high-quality images (I₃>I₂: 100% vs. 75.37% for PickScore). However, on the I₂>I₁ comparison (distinguishing medium from low quality), PickScore (74.80%) still edges out ICT-HP (66.42%), suggesting the ICT objective is specifically designed for the high-quality regime. On the GenEval generation benchmark, ICT-optimized SD3.5-Turbo achieves the best mean (0.71) and counting (0.70), while ICT-HP leads on two-object (0.88) and color attribution (0.58); notably, PickScore-optimized SD3.5-Turbo degrades below the unoptimized baseline (0.66 vs. 0.69 mean), and CLIP optimization causes complete training collapse (0.13 mean). On Parti-Prompts, the HP model achieves the highest JPEG compressibility (334.86) and aesthetic score (6.448), surpassing both SD3.5-Large (374.80/6.307) and FLUX.1-dev (270.58/6.436) on aesthetic quality. In human evaluation (Table 5), ICT-HP achieves a 0.714 win rate against PickScore-optimized outputs and 0.673 against the base model, confirming the quantitative gains are perceptually meaningful.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CLIP's dual-encoder architecture (Radford et al., 2021), fine-tuning it with a novel one-directional 'image-contains-text' (ICT) objective rather than the standard bidirectional contrastive alignment. It departs from the lineage of human-preference reward models (PickScore, HPSv2, ImageReward) that inherit CLIP/BLIP's alignment bias, arguing that their scoring mechanism fundamentally penalizes information-rich images. For diffusion model optimization, the paper adopts DRaFT-K (Clark et al., 2024) as the gradient-based fine-tuning procedure, applying the new reward signals to Stable Diffusion 3.5-Turbo via LoRA adapters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The information-theoretic decomposition (I(v) = I(v;t) + I(v|t)) provides a clear mechanistic explanation for why alignment-based reward models fail on modern high-fidelity generators, a problem that becomes increasingly critical as diffusion models outgrow the era where text-image alignment was the bottleneck. The practical demonstration that CLIP-based reward optimization causes outright training collapse (GenEval mean dropping from 0.69 to 0.13) underscores the urgency of rethinking reward design for SOTA models. Open questions include whether the threshold-based ICT mechanism generalizes to video generation or 3D synthesis, whether the HP model's image-only aesthetic signal can be further decomposed into interpretable sub-dimensions, and whether the approach transfers to non-diffusion architectures like autoregressive image generators.
