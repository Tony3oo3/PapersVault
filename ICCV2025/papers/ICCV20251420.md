## Problem

Metalens-based ultra-compact cameras produce images with spatially varying optical aberrations and sensor noise that are difficult to correct with uniform-PSF assumptions or require precise calibration and large paired datasets. While pretrained diffusion models offer strong natural image priors for restoration, they lack fine-grained control over the inference process, leading to hallucinated artifacts that do not exist in the original scene. The paper addresses both the degradation modeling gap and the controllability gap simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 400×400 pixel RGB image captured by the authors' ~1 mm³ MetaCamera (metalens + miniaturized CMOS sensor) as input and produces a restored high-quality RGB image as output. At inference, the model runs a single denoising step (k=1) conditioned on a text prompt describing imaging quality (not scene content), and stores the resulting latent codes; a tunable decoder then blends positive and neutral-path latents with a scalar α to produce outputs at varying diffusion intensities, enabling on-the-fly trade-offs between structural fidelity and perceptual sharpness without re-running the network.

## Method

DMDiff builds on SD-Turbo (a one-step distilled Stable Diffusion) with LoRA fine-tuning on the VAE encoder and UNet. A Spatially Varying Degradation-Aware Attention (SVDA) module computes per-patch FWHM from simulated PSFs and per-patch MUSIQ no-reference quality scores, then feeds these degradation maps through a small attention network that modulates the LoRA adaptation matrices, allowing the model to apply region-specific corrections. Training uses three prompt-conditioned paths: a positive path (degraded→GT, generating high-frequency detail), a neutral path (degraded→edge-preserving-filtered GT, preserving low-frequency structure), and a negative path (GT→degraded, learning the metalens degradation signature and generating pseudo training pairs). At inference, the positive and neutral latent codes are linearly combined with a tunable weight α before VAE decoding, giving users a continuous knob between conservative reconstruction and vivid detail enhancement.

## Results

On the combined DIV2K/Flickr2K/WED test set, DMDiff (α=0.75) achieves the best PSNR of 30.31 dB and competitive SSIM of 0.8731, while the higher-diffusion setting (α=1.05) yields the best perceptual scores: LPIPS 0.1485, DISTS 0.1356, MUSIQ 51.85, and CLIP-IQA 0.4460. The method outperforms the strongest transformer baseline (SwinIR, PSNR 29.46) by ~0.85 dB in PSNR and reduces LPIPS from 0.2462 to 0.1705. Against the closest diffusion-based competitor (SeeSR-finetuned, PSNR 25.38), DMDiff gains nearly 5 dB in PSNR and 0.034 in SSIM while also improving all no-reference quality metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMDiff dominates all seven baselines on the majority of metrics. Against SwinIR (the strongest non-diffusion baseline), DMDiff gains 0.85 dB PSNR and reduces LPIPS by 0.076, though SwinIR retains a marginal SSIM advantage (0.8786 vs 0.8731). Compared to the closest diffusion-based method SeeSR (finetuned, 50-step), DMDiff achieves a substantial 4.9 dB PSNR improvement (30.31 vs 25.38) and 0.034 higher SSIM while also surpassing it on LPIPS (0.1705 vs 0.2100) and DISTS (0.1499 vs 0.1566); at α=1.05, DMDiff further leads on MUSIQ (51.85 vs 44.71) and CLIP-IQA (0.4460 vs 0.4005). OSEDiff and S3Diff, both single-step diffusion methods, trail DMDiff by 10+ dB in PSNR (19.35 and 22.01 respectively), indicating that the SVDA-guided LoRA fine-tuning and multipath training are critical for metalens-specific restoration. The traditional baselines (Wiener deconvolution, Two-step PSF correction) are far behind, confirming that model-based approaches cannot handle the spatially varying, sensor-noise-coupled degradation of a real metalens. Neural nano-optics, the closest physics-based method, is outperformed by DMDiff on PSNR (30.31 vs 29.25), LPIPS (0.1705 vs 0.2001), and all no-reference quality scores, demonstrating the advantage of learned degradation modeling over calibrated PSF-based correction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SD-Turbo (adversarial diffusion distillation) as its one-step diffusion backbone, extending the line of prior-driven diffusion restoration methods (SeeSR, OSEDiff, S3Diff) that leverage pretrained generative priors rather than large paired datasets. It departs from prior metalens restoration work (Wiener deconvolution, neural nano-optics, two-step PSF correction) by eliminating the need for precise PSF calibration, instead learning spatially varying degradation through the proposed SVDA module that combines simulated FWHM with no-reference quality scores. The multipath (positive/neutral/negative prompt) training strategy and instantly tunable decoder represent a new architectural contribution not present in prior single-path diffusion restoration approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Metalens-based cameras are a critical enabling technology for ultra-compact imaging in AR/VR, biomedical, and implantable applications, but their spatially varying aberrations have been a persistent barrier to practical deployment. This work demonstrates that a one-step diffusion model with degradation-aware attention can reconstruct high-fidelity images from a ~1 mm³ metalens camera without tedious optical calibration, validated on real hardware. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to larger-aperture or multi-metalens arrays, whether the pseudo-data augmentation strategy scales to more diverse real-world degradations, and whether the one-step constraint (k=1) limits ultimate reconstruction quality compared to multi-step inference.
