## Problem

Tubular structure segmentation in medical images faces three intertwined challenges: structures vary widely in size and topology, public dataset annotations are often sketchy or incomplete, and existing methods either fuse vesselness maps only via shallow concatenation or enforce skeleton growth without adequately suppressing the noise that overgrowth introduces. No prior framework simultaneously addresses multi-scale adaptability, effective vesselness integration at deep feature levels, and a principled balance between recall-driven growth and precision-driven suppression under partial labels.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a pair of co-registered inputs: the original medical image (3D CT volume with HU clipping, or 2D RGB retinal image) and a precomputed vesselness filter map of identical spatial dimensions. It outputs a per-voxel/per-pixel binary segmentation mask of the tubular structure. For CT tasks the vesselness map is generated with Frangi-style filters; for airway and coronary tasks where adjacent structures cause interference, the vesselness branch is omitted and only the image is fed through the network.

## Method

HarmonySeg fuses the image and vesselness modalities through a Shallow-and-Deep Fusion (SADF) module: a Deep Mutual Query (DMQ) applies bidirectional cross-attention between the deepest encoder features of both modalities to capture global vessel context, while a Shallow Query (SQ) uses self-attention with average/max pooling on shallow features to highlight tiny vessel candidates. A Deep-to-Shallow Decoding (D2SD) strategy then progressively decodes from deep to shallow scales, replacing standard convolutions with parallel stacked flexible-convolution blocks that provide diverse receptive fields without the grid artifacts of dilated convolutions. Training is governed by a Growth-Suppression Balance (GSB) loss: the growth side combines a relaxed Dice+CE supervision (using a bounding-box ROI to tolerate missing labels) with a connectivity loss that soft-skeletonizes the prediction and penalizes disconnected components via endpoint reconnection; the suppression side adds a Gaussian-kernel spatial-prior loss and a MixUp equivalence loss to constrain overgrowth. The final objective weights growth and suppression terms with a tunable λ, enabling the model to recover missing vessel segments while keeping false positives in check.

## Results

On the primary hepatic vessel segmentation (HVS) task, HarmonySeg achieves 66.79% Dice, 72.04% clDice, 9.60 HD, and 67.17% F1-score, outperforming the second-best method DSC-Net (63.57% Dice) by 3.22% in Dice. On the external validation set (HVS-External), HarmonySeg reaches 77.76% Dice and 80.61% clDice, surpassing DSC-Net by 1.93% and 3.07% respectively. On retinal vessel segmentation (DRIVE), HarmonySeg attains the best clDice (82.03%) and HD (6.51) but trails DSC-Net by 0.52% in Dice. On airway tree segmentation, HarmonySeg achieves the highest branch detection (95.0%) and tree length detection (92.3%), while on coronary artery segmentation it obtains the best Dice (83.24%) and HD (26.42).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the primary HVS task, HarmonySeg outperforms all five baselines on Dice (66.79% vs. 63.57% for DSC-Net, 61.24% for Kirchhoff et al., 60.15% for nnU-Net) and clDice (72.04% vs. 71.80% for Kirchhoff et al.), with the largest margin over nnU-Net (+6.64% Dice). On HVS-External, HarmonySeg leads in Dice (77.76% vs. 75.83% for DSC-Net) and clDice (80.61% vs. 77.54%), though DSC-Net achieves slightly better HD (3.15 vs. 3.55) and ASSD (1.02 vs. 1.40). On DRIVE (retinal vessels), HarmonySeg wins on clDice (82.03% vs. 81.16% for DSC-Net) and HD (6.51 vs. 6.68) but loses by 0.52% in Dice to DSC-Net (81.85%). On airway tree segmentation, HarmonySeg substantially outperforms all baselines in branch detection (95.0% vs. 91.1% for Zheng et al.) and tree length detection (92.3% vs. 80.7% for Qin et al.), but sacrifices precision (91.1% vs. 98.9% for Zheng et al.), reflecting a recall-precision trade-off. On coronary artery segmentation, HarmonySeg achieves the best Dice (83.24% vs. 82.96% for U-Net++) and HD (26.42 vs. 27.22), with a marginally higher AHD (0.88 vs. 0.82 for U-Net++).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the nnU-Net backbone with three targeted modifications for tubular structure segmentation: a cross-attention-based fusion module (SADF) that treats vesselness maps as an independent auxiliary modality rather than a simple input channel, a progressive deep-to-shallow decoding strategy with flexible receptive-field convolutions, and a growth-suppression balanced loss that generalizes topology-preserving losses (clDice, skeleton recall) to handle incomplete annotations. It departs from prior vesselness-integration work (which typically concatenates filter outputs with images) by performing deep mutual querying between image and vesselness features, and from prior topology losses by explicitly balancing skeleton growth against contextual noise suppression via spatial priors and MixUp constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GSB loss addresses a practically important gap: most topology-preserving losses assume complete ground-truth labels, yet real tubular structure datasets (e.g., LiVS, MSD8) often have sketchy or missing annotations. The ablation showing that naive vesselness concatenation actually degrades performance relative to no vesselness at all validates the need for deeper fusion mechanisms. Limitations include the inapplicability of vesselness filtering to certain tubular structures (airways, coronary arteries), where the SADF module is disabled, and the reliance on a hand-crafted Frangi filter rather than a learned alternative. Open questions include whether the vesselness prior can be learned end-to-end and whether the GSB framework transfers to other incomplete-label segmentation tasks beyond tubular structures.
