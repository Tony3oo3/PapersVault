## Problem

Existing T2V identity customization methods train a customized T2I prior via image reconstruction and then inject it into a T2V model, creating a tuning-inference gap because the T2I and T2V distributions diverge. This gap causes both inferior ID fidelity and degradation of the original model's motion dynamics and semantic prompt-following capability, making generated videos appear static and fail to match text descriptions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of reference face images (as few as one) and an arbitrary text prompt describing the desired scene, action, and appearance. The reference images are consumed only through a frozen face-recognition backbone (ResNet-100) to extract identity embeddings, not as pixel-level reconstruction targets. The output is a full video generated by the underlying T2V model (HunyuanVideo or AnimateDiff) with the target identity consistently injected across frames, supporting both DiT-based and UNet-based backbones as well as style-specific LoRA variants.

## Method

PersonalVideo replaces the conventional image-reconstruction objective with a non-reconstructive reward framework that directly supervises videos generated from pure noise by the T2V model, thereby bridging the tuning-inference gap. Two differentiable rewards are combined: an Identity Consistency Reward (ICR) that minimizes the cosine distance between reference face embeddings and those of a randomly sampled generated frame, and a Semantic Consistency Reward (SCR) that aligns the HPSv2 image-text score distributions of the target model's outputs with those of the frozen original model via KL divergence, preserving dynamics and prompt-following. To reduce overfitting with few references, 50 LLM-generated prompts covering diverse motions, appearances, and backgrounds are sampled during training (simulated prompt augmentation). Finally, an Isolated Identity Adapter (LoRA on Q/K/V projections) is injected only in the later denoising steps, where appearance details are refined, so that early-step motion and layout generation remain undisturbed.

## Results

On 1000 generated videos across 20 identities with 50 prompts, PersonalVideo achieves the best score on every metric: Face Similarity 62.35 (vs. 53.22 for ConsisID), Dynamic Degree 17.80 (vs. 15.22 for ConsisID), FVD 1272.32 (lower is better; vs. 1325.89 for DreamBooth), Temporal Consistency 0.9935, CLIP-T 26.30, and CLIP-I 76.48. The face-similarity gap over the strongest baseline (ConsisID) is approximately 9 points, while the dynamic-degree gap over MagicMe (11.88) is nearly 6 points, confirming that identity injection no longer comes at the cost of motion or prompt following.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PersonalVideo outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against DreamBooth (the T2I reconstruction baseline), it gains ~20 points in Face Similarity (62.35 vs. 42.62) and ~32 points in CLIP-I (76.48 vs. 44.27), while also improving Dynamic Degree by ~4 points, confirming that the tuning-inference gap severely hurts both identity and motion. Against MagicMe (the closest T2V customization baseline), PersonalVideo leads by ~12 points in Face Similarity and ~6 points in Dynamic Degree, showing that MagicMe's image-reconstruction supervision degrades motion the most. Against IDAnimator, which requires thousands of high-quality human videos for fine-tuning, PersonalVideo surpasses it by ~18 points in Face Similarity and ~300 in FVD, while needing only a few static reference images. Against ConsisID (the strongest encoder-based baseline), PersonalVideo still leads by ~9 points in Face Similarity and ~2.6 points in Dynamic Degree, though the gap in CLIP-I is smaller (76.48 vs. 74.58). In the user study, PersonalVideo also receives the highest human preference across Identity Fidelity, Text Alignment, Dynamic Degree, and Overall Quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant reconstructive paradigm in both T2I and T2V identity customization (DreamBooth, MagicMe, ID-Animator), which trains on image reconstruction objectives and then transfers the learned prior to video generation. Instead, it repurposes the identity-supervision idea from encoder-based T2I methods (PuLID, LCM-Lookahead) by applying it as a direct reward on frames sampled from the T2V model's own generated videos, thereby eliminating the distribution mismatch between tuning and inference. The Isolated Identity Adapter, which injects identity only in later denoising steps, is a novel architectural choice motivated by the observation that motion is formed early while appearance details are refined late. The Semantic Consistency Reward, which aligns the score distribution of the customized model with the original via KL divergence, has no direct analogue in prior T2V customization work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Identity-specific video generation is a high-demand application in AIGC (film, social media, personalized content), and the tuning-inference gap identified here is a fundamental limitation of the current reconstructive approach that likely affects all methods in this family. The practical advantage of requiring only a single reference image while maintaining robustness is significant for real-world deployment. However, the method still requires per-identity training (not zero-shot), and the evaluation is limited to 20 identities with 50 prompts; open questions include scalability to multi-person scenes, longer video durations, and whether the SCR generalizes beyond the HPSv2 reward model. The demonstrated compatibility with community style LoRAs suggests broad practical utility within the existing T2V ecosystem.
