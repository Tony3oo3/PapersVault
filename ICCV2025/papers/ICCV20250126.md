## Problem

Existing feed-forward semantic 3D reconstruction methods suffer from two coupled limitations: pixel-wise Gaussian prediction creates substantial redundancy in overlapping regions, and per-primitive compressed semantic features (e.g., 512→64 dims) incur irreversible information loss that degrades fine-grained scene understanding. No prior method simultaneously eliminates this geometric redundancy and preserves full semantic expressiveness without inflating storage.

## Input / Output

The method takes V unposed RGB images (trained at 256×256) along with their camera intrinsics as input, requiring no extrinsics or depth maps. It outputs a dual-field 3D representation: a fine-grained instance-aware radiance field with 8-dim learnable instance features on all retained Gaussians, and a coarser semantic feature field with full 512-dim uncompressed features on only 1/64 of the primitives (downsampled by factor 8). From this representation, the model synthesizes novel-view RGB images and performs open-vocabulary 3D segmentation by clustering instance features and matching them against text prompts.

## Method

SpatialSplat uses a MASt3R-initialized ViT encoder-decoder with DPT heads to predict per-pixel Gaussian parameters (position, opacity, rotation, scale, SH) plus importance scores and feature vectors from unposed images. A Selective Gaussian Mechanism multiplies a learned importance score β into each primitive's opacity during alpha blending, then applies a BCE loss with L1 regularization to push β toward binary values, discarding ~35% of redundant primitives at inference. The key architectural insight is a dual-field semantic decomposition: a fine-grained field with low-dim (8-dim) instance features trained via contrastive loss against SAM-generated 2D masks captures inter-instance geometry, while a coarse field with uncompressed 512-dim features on uniformly downsampled primitives preserves full semantic information without per-primitive compression. Training is end-to-end with a weighted sum of photometric (MSE+LPIPS), importance, contrastive, and semantic MSE losses, requiring no 3D supervision.

## Results

On ScanNet, SpatialSplat achieves 0.5593 mIoU and 0.7814 accuracy for open-vocabulary 3D segmentation (source view), outperforming the SOTA feed-forward method LSM (0.5141 mIoU, 0.7719 Acc) by ~4.5% mIoU. For novel view synthesis, SpatialSplat reaches 25.46 dB PSNR and 0.8045 SSIM, surpassing LSM (24.12 dB, 0.7961 SSIM) while using only 40% of the storage (25.58 MB vs. 63 MB) and 65% of the Gaussian primitives (87.7K vs. 131K). On the out-of-distribution Replica dataset, SpatialSplat further extends its lead over LSM with 0.5131 mIoU vs. 0.5024 and 22.09 dB PSNR vs. 17.61 dB on the hardest scene.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpatialSplat outperforms LSM [15], the primary SOTA feed-forward semantic 3DGS method, across all metrics: +4.5% mIoU (0.5593 vs. 0.5141), +1.3 dB PSNR (25.46 vs. 24.12), and −0.048 LPIPS (0.2046 vs. 0.2526), while using 60% fewer parameters. Against the per-scene optimization baselines, SpatialSplat surpasses NeRF-DFF [23] by +2.1% mIoU and +3.0 dB PSNR, and dramatically outperforms Feature-3DGS [52] (+6.0% mIoU, +7.5 dB PSNR), which degrades severely with only two input views. The 2D baseline L-Seg [26] achieves comparable mIoU (0.5541) but provides no 3D geometry or novel view synthesis. NoPoSplat [46] achieves marginally better PSNR (25.70 vs. 25.46) and SSIM (0.8159 vs. 0.8045) but lacks any semantic capability, making it a non-semantic reference rather than a direct competitor. On the OOD Replica dataset, SpatialSplat extends its advantage over LSM by up to +4.5 dB PSNR (22.09 vs. 17.61 on room0), confirming stronger cross-dataset generalization.
