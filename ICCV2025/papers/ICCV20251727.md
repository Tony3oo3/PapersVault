## Problem

Masked Autoregressive (MAR) models enable parallel token decoding for image generation, yet their reliance on bidirectional self-attention fundamentally prevents the use of standard KV caching, creating a computational bottleneck that negates their expected efficiency advantage over sequential autoregressive models. No prior caching mechanism has been designed to accommodate MAR's non-causal, full-attention architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251727/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on class-conditional image generation at 256×256 resolution, where a single class label is expanded into 256 image tokens (via KL-16 tokenizer) plus 64 prepended cls tokens. LazyMAR is applied during the iterative multi-step decoding process (32 or 64 steps), intervening at the feature level within the transformer's self-attention and MLP layers rather than modifying the input or output format. The method is training-free and plug-and-play, requiring no architectural changes to the underlying MAR model.

## Method

LazyMAR introduces two complementary caching mechanisms that exploit distinct redundancies in MAR decoding. Token Cache leverages the observation that most tokens exhibit near-identical representations across adjacent decoding steps: it computes all tokens through the first three layers, then uses cosine similarity between current and cached features to select which tokens to skip (reusing cached values) versus recompute, skipping ~84% of tokens on average. Condition Cache exploits the finding that the residual between conditional and unconditional outputs in classifier-free guidance is ~35.5× more stable than either branch individually, allowing the unconditional pathway to be approximated from the cached residual plus the current conditional output, halving per-step computation. A periodic cache-reuse-refreshment strategy (every K steps) performs full computation to prevent progressive error accumulation, and the entire framework is training-free and applicable to any MAR model without modification.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, LazyMAR-H with 64 decoding steps achieves a 2.83× speedup over the original MAR-H (GPU latency drops from 1.74s to 0.75s) while incurring only a 0.10 FID increase (1.69 vs. 1.59) and a marginal 0.1 IS improvement (299.2 vs. 299.1). At 32 decoding steps, LazyMAR-H reaches 3.63× speedup with FID 1.94, still outperforming the original MAR-H at 32 steps (FID 1.92, 1.64× speedup) in both speed and quality. The method is training-free and plug-and-play, requiring no architectural modifications or additional training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against the original MAR model at various decoding step counts. LazyMAR-H,64 (2.83× speedup, FID 1.69) substantially outperforms MAR-H,32 (1.64× speedup, FID 1.92) in both speed and quality, and also beats MAR-H,16 (2.41× speedup, FID 4.49) by a large margin in FID (1.69 vs. 4.49) while achieving higher speedup. Against non-MAR baselines, LazyMAR-H,64 achieves the best FID (1.69) and IS (299.2) among all methods listed, surpassing DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.27, IS 278.2), LDM-4 (FID 3.60, IS 247.7), LlamaGen-XXL (FID 3.09, IS 253.6), MaskGIT (FID 6.18, IS 182.1), and MAGE (FID 6.93, IS 195.8). Notably, LazyMAR-H,64 also outperforms the smaller MAR-L,64 (FID 1.82, IS 296.1) and MAR-B,64 (FID 2.32, IS 281.1) in both efficiency and generation quality, demonstrating that the caching framework enables a larger model to run faster than a smaller one without quality loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the MAR-Diffusion framework [18], which uses bidirectional attention for parallel token decoding but consequently cannot leverage the KV caching that makes causal autoregressive models efficient. The authors draw conceptual inspiration from KV caching in LLMs and feature caching in diffusion models (DeepCache, Faster Diffusion), but repurpose these ideas to address the specific structural incompatibility of bidirectional attention with incremental computation. The key departure is identifying two distinct redundancy sources unique to MAR's multi-step parallel decoding—token-level similarity across adjacent steps and the small residual between conditional/unconditional CFG outputs—and designing separate caching mechanisms for each, rather than applying a single uniform caching rule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAR models were introduced to overcome the sequential bottleneck of autoregressive image generation, yet their bidirectional attention silently negates the expected efficiency gains; LazyMAR addresses this practical gap with a 2.83× speedup that is training-free and plug-and-play, making it immediately applicable to any existing MAR model. The evaluation is limited to ImageNet class-conditional generation at 256×256, leaving open whether the caching strategies transfer to text-to-image MAR models, higher resolutions, or other bidirectional architectures. A broader open question is whether the periodic refreshment interval and the early-layer similarity threshold can be made adaptive rather than fixed, potentially improving the speedup-quality tradeoff further.
