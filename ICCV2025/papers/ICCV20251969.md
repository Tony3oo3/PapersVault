## Problem

Existing motion-conditioned video generators fine-tuned on real-world videos fail to capture internal, part-level dynamics (e.g., a drawer sliding, a tail oscillating) because natural videos confound part motion with overall object translation, camera movement, and background changes. No prior model provides a general, category-agnostic 'foundation' prior for how individual object parts move and deform in response to external stimuli.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251969/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single 256×256 RGB image of an object and up to 5 sparse drags, where each drag is a 2D point trajectory specifying a start location on the object and its per-frame displacement across N rendered frames. It outputs an N-frame video at 256×256 resolution depicting physically plausible part-level motion. Drags are encoded as multi-resolution spatial maps (6 channels per drag per frame) marking the origin, current, and final positions, and the model is trained at 256×256 despite SVD's native 1024×576 training resolution.

## Method

Puppet-Master fine-tunes Stable Video Diffusion with three architectural modifications: (1) FiLM-style adaptive layer normalization modules that modulate U-Net feature maps at multiple resolutions using drag encodings (scale + shift), (2) drag tokens regressed via MLPs from drag endpoints and internal diffusion features, appended to the CLIP image token in cross-attention to restore spatial awareness, and (3) all-to-first attention, a drop-in replacement for spatial self-attention where every noised frame queries only the first frame's key and value, creating a direct shortcut to the clean reference image. Training uses a curated synthetic dataset (Objaverse-Animation-HQ, 10k clips, plus Drag-a-Move) where animations are filtered via point-cloud displacement metrics and GPT-4V realism assessment, and drags are sampled per independently moving sub-model to emphasize part-level dynamics. The synthetic-only training data eliminates confounding factors present in real videos, enabling zero-shot generalization to real-world images.

## Results

On the Drag-a-Move benchmark, Puppet-Master achieves the best PSNR (24.41), SSIM (0.927), and LPIPS (0.085) among all video generators, and the lowest Motion Error (12.21/3.53), where the second value (all foreground points) is markedly lower than the first (drag origins only), confirming part-level rather than whole-object motion. On the out-of-domain Human3.6M benchmark (zero-shot, no real-video fine-tuning), Puppet-Master leads on PSNR (17.59), LPIPS (0.155), and FVD (454.76), outperforming DragNUWA (17.52 / 0.158 / 466.91) despite being trained exclusively on synthetic 3D renderings. The only metric where Puppet-Master trails is FVD on Drag-a-Move (246.99 vs. 180.27 for DragAPart-Original), reflecting a trade-off between temporal diversity and fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Puppet-Master outperforms all three video-generator baselines (DragNUWA, DragAnything, Image Conductor) on every metric for both Drag-a-Move and Human3.6M, with the largest gaps on LPIPS (0.085 vs. 0.172/0.296/0.585 on Drag-a-Move) and FVD (246.99 vs. 281.49/468.46/1138.89). The critical differentiator is the second Motion Error value (all foreground points): Puppet-Master scores 3.53, comparable to the image-based DragAPart (3.71–3.73), while the video baselines score 15.41–27.51, confirming that they move the entire object rather than individual parts. Against DragAPart (the closest competitor), Puppet-Master wins on PSNR (24.41 vs. 23.41/23.78), the first ME value (12.21 vs. 14.17/14.34), and all Human3.6M metrics (PSNR 17.59 vs. 15.14/15.25; FVD 454.76 vs. 683.40/549.64), but trails on FVD for Drag-a-Move (246.99 vs. 180.27/189.10), likely because DragAPart generates frames independently without temporal consistency constraints. On the zero-shot Human3.6M test, Puppet-Master surpasses even DragNUWA (the strongest video baseline) by 0.07 dB PSNR and 12.15 FVD, demonstrating that synthetic-only training transfers effectively to real-world objects including humans, animals, and articulated objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

Puppet-Master extends the drag-conditioned video generation line (DragNUWA, DragAnything, Image Conductor) by repurposing Stable Video Diffusion with new drag-encoding modules (adaptive layer normalization and cross-attention drag tokens) and an all-to-first attention mechanism to address appearance degradation during out-of-domain fine-tuning. It departs from prior drag-based methods that fine-tune on real videos—where confounding factors like camera motion and background changes cause models to shift whole objects rather than animate individual parts—by training exclusively on curated synthetic 3D renderings from Objaverse. The drag encoding scheme is directly inherited from DragAPart, but the video-generation setting and the all-to-first attention shortcut are novel architectural contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a model fine-tuned solely on synthetic 3D animations generalizes zero-shot to real-world images and outperforms both real-data-trained baselines and Sora (qualitatively) at capturing part-level dynamics, challenging the assumption that Internet-scale real video is necessary for learning object motion. The all-to-first attention mechanism is a simple, portable fix for a common failure mode (appearance/background degradation) when fine-tuning diffusion models on out-of-distribution data, making it broadly applicable. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-object scenes, higher resolutions, and longer video horizons, and whether the synthetic-to-real gap can be further closed for more complex deformable objects.
