## Problem

Existing 3D geometry compression methods (GPCC, VPCC, Draco, DeepSDF) are designed for individual models or temporally linked sequences and rely on high-resolution voxelized point clouds, making them inefficient and inapplicable to large sets of diverse, unrelated 3D mesh models. No prior method can compress thousands of structurally varied meshes into a few megabytes while preserving fine geometric details.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250221/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of N irregular 3D mesh models (scaled to [-1,1]³) as input and produces a single compressed bitstream (as small as ~2 MB for 1000 models) that decompresses back into the full set of meshes. Internally, each mesh is converted to a 4D TSDF-Def tensor of uniform size K×K×K×4 (default K=128), and the neural representation reduces each to a small embedded feature of K'×K'×K'×C (K'=4, C=16) plus shared decoder parameters, all entropy-coded via Huffman coding. The framework also supports dynamic addition of new models by optimizing only their embedded features against a frozen decoder.

## Method

NeCGS operates in two stages. First, each mesh is represented as a TSDF-Def tensor that augments a standard TSDF with a per-grid-point 3D deformation vector, optimized via differentiable Deformable Marching Cubes (DMC) with an L1 penalty on deformations to keep most grids at zero offset; this allows uniform low-resolution (K=128) representation of diverse shapes without the resolution inflation that plain TSDF requires. Second, a quantization-aware auto-decoder (5 cascaded PixelShuffle upsampling modules) regresses each 4D tensor from its small embedded feature, exploiting local geometric similarity both within each shape and across different shapes to remove redundancy. Training uses a joint loss combining global MAE, surface-near weighted MAE (via a TSDF-threshold mask), and SSIM, with differentiable quantization applied to both features and parameters. The quantized features and decoder weights are then Huffman-coded into the final bitstream.

## Results

NeCGS achieves a compression ratio of up to ~900× on the DT4D dataset (500 animal models, 683.80 MB) while maintaining minimal geometric distortion, and a minimum compression ratio of 300× across all four datasets. On the Thingi10K dataset (1000 CAD models, 335.92 MB), the entire set is compressed to just 2.064 MB. In terms of efficiency, NeCGS has the fastest decompression time (98.95 ms per model) among all compared methods, and its compression time (10.01 h per set) is substantially faster than VPCC (39.34 h) though slower than per-model codecs like Draco (0.06 h) and GPCC (0.62 h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeCGS outperforms all five baselines on rate-distortion performance, with the advantage growing substantially at high compression ratios (≥300×). Against GPCC and Draco (per-model codecs), NeCGS achieves far higher compression ratios because it exploits cross-shape redundancy via a shared auto-decoder, whereas these methods compress each model independently. Compared to VPCC (the only other set-level codec), NeCGS is ~4× faster in compression (10.01 h vs. 39.34 h) and ~7.7× faster in decompression (98.95 ms vs. 762.87 ms), while also achieving lower distortion at equivalent compression ratios. Against the learning-based PCGCv2, NeCGS achieves better RD curves on all four datasets, particularly on the diverse Mixed and Thingi10K sets where PCGCv2's point-cloud-based representation struggles. The adapted QuantDeepSDF baseline, which uses an MLP to regress SDFs, is outperformed by NeCGS because the MLP's limited receptive field cannot effectively encode thousands of diverse shapes, whereas NeCGS's convolutional auto-decoder with per-shape embedded features handles category diversity more robustly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant point-cloud-based compression paradigm (GPCC, VPCC, Draco, PCGCv2) by adopting an implicit tensor representation, and extends the TSDF formulation with per-grid deformation (TSDF-Def) inspired by recent deformable marching cubes methods. It also moves beyond single-model or temporally-linked sequence compression (DeepSDF, VPCC) by introducing a shared quantization-aware auto-decoder that exploits local geometric similarity both within and across diverse, unrelated shapes. The entropy-coding stage (Huffman) follows standard learned-compression practice, but the combination of a uniform-resolution implicit representation with a cross-shape shared decoder for set-level compression is the novel architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NeCGS addresses a practically important but previously unaddressed setting: compressing large libraries of diverse, unrelated 3D models (e.g., asset stores, AR/VR content) into a few MB while preserving fine geometric detail, and it supports incremental addition of new models without retraining. Key limitations include the high per-shape TSDF-Def optimization cost (~10 h for a full set) and the reliance on a fixed resolution that trades off detail against compression ratio. Open questions include whether the shared-decoder approach scales to tens of thousands of highly heterogeneous shapes, whether more advanced entropy models (arithmetic coding, learned entropy models) can close the remaining gap, and whether the method extends to textured or animated geometry sets.
