## Problem

Self-attention blocks in Vision Transformers catastrophically fail to train when their skip connections are removed, while other components (FFN, CNNs) degrade only modestly under the same condition. No prior work had provided a theoretical explanation for why self-attention is uniquely dependent on skip connections, nor a principled mechanism to improve the conditioning of self-attention inputs beyond relying on the skip connection alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250649/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (e.g., 224×224 for ImageNet-1K, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) and produces class labels. The key unusual step is that after converting images into a token matrix X ∈ R^{n×d}, a Token Graying pre-processing (SVD or DCT-based singular value amplification) is applied to X before patch embedding, modifying the token conditioning without changing the downstream architecture or output format.

## Method

The paper proves that the condition number of a linear self-attention output embedding without skip connection is bounded by the cube of the input matrix's condition number (κ ≤ C·(σmax/σmin)³), whereas the FFN's condition number is only linearly bounded, explaining the asymmetric sensitivity to skip removal. It further shows that adding the identity (skip) connection dramatically reduces the output condition number. To exploit this insight, the authors propose Token Graying: applying SVD (or its cheaper DCT approximation) to the input token matrix, normalizing singular values to (0,1], raising them to a power ε ∈ (0,1] to amplify small components, then reconstructing the matrix. The DCT variant achieves O(nd·log(nd)) complexity versus O(nd·min(n,d)) for SVD, adding negligible training overhead while improving SAB output conditioning across all layers and yielding consistent Top-1/Top-5 gains on supervised (ViT, Swin, CaiT, PVT) and self-supervised (MAE) benchmarks.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, DCT Token Graying (DCTTG) consistently improves Top-1 accuracy across five ViT architectures: ViT-S (80.2→80.4%), ViT-B (81.0→81.3%), Swin-S (81.3→81.6%), CaiT-S (82.6→82.7%), and PVT V2b3 (82.9→83.0%). On the smaller Tiny-ImageNet dataset with ViT-Tiny, gains are more pronounced: softmax attention Top-1 improves from 43.0% to 44.8% (+1.8%) and Top-5 from 67.3% to 69.7% (+2.4%). In the self-supervised MAE setting, DCTTG lifts fine-tuned ViT-Base Top-1 from 83.0% to 83.2% and Top-5 from 96.4% to 96.6% on ImageNet-1K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCTTG is evaluated against five baseline architectures on ImageNet-1K, and it wins on every comparison. Against the standard ViT [Dosovitskiy, ICLR 2021], DCTTG adds +0.2% Top-1 for ViT-S and +0.3% for ViT-B. Against Swin Transformer [Liu, ICCV 2021], the gain is +0.3% Top-1 (81.3→81.6%) with Top-5 unchanged. Against CaiT [Touvron, ICLR 2022], DCTTG yields +0.1% Top-1 and +0.2% Top-5. Against PVT v2 [Wang, ECCV 2022], the improvement is +0.1% on both Top-1 and Top-5. In the self-supervised MAE setting [He, CVPR 2022], DCTTG improves fine-tuned ViT-Base by +0.2% Top-1 and +0.2% Top-5. The gains are modest (0.1–0.3%) on large-scale ImageNet-1K but substantially larger (+1.8% Top-1, +2.4% Top-5) on the smaller Tiny-ImageNet dataset, suggesting the conditioning benefit is most impactful when data and model scale are limited.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of transformer architecture design and numerical optimization theory. It builds on the empirical observation that skip connections are indispensable in transformers (Vaswani et al., He et al. 2023) and the conditioning-based optimization literature (Saratchandran et al.), but departs by providing a formal proposition showing that self-attention's output embedding condition number scales as the cube of the input condition number, whereas FFN scales linearly. The Token Graying method repurposes classical signal-processing tools (SVD, DCT) as a pre-conditioning step for input tokens, a novel application in the ViT training pipeline. The controlled comparison with ConvMixer isolates the conditioning pathology to the self-attention mechanism specifically, rather than to depth or architecture in general.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical explanation for a long-standing empirical mystery—why skip connections are so critical in self-attention blocks but far less so in FFN blocks—which could inform future architecture design (e.g., designing inherently well-conditioned attention variants). However, the practical contribution (Token Graying) yields only modest gains (0.2–0.5% Top-1), and the DCT-based method introduces sensitivity to quantization in low-precision training. Open questions include whether the conditioning framework can guide the design of attention mechanisms that are well-conditioned without relying on skip connections, and whether the theoretical bounds tighten or change under multi-head attention and non-linear (softmax) activations beyond the empirical validation provided.
