## Problem

Small-scale LVLMs (<10B parameters) exhibit confirmation bias: when presented with an incorrect initial answer, they rationalize the error rather than detecting and correcting it. This stems from SFT training on predominantly positive, user-aligned data that rewards dialogue coherence over critical reassessment. Existing reflection methods (REVERIE, Mulberry) train models to justify answers but do not explicitly teach them to overturn wrong ones.

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image paired with a text question (multiple-choice or open-ended). The output is a structured three-part sequence: an initial direct answer (a₀), a reflection rationale (r) that reviews and analyzes the initial response, and a final corrected answer (a_f). During training, a₀ is deliberately set to incorrect values (traversed over all wrong options for multiple-choice tasks) to create adversarial correction-required samples, while a_f is always the ground-truth answer.

## Method

ReCoT follows a two-stage SFT-DPO paradigm. In the Reflective Fine-Tuning (RFT) stage, the model is trained on adversarial samples where the initial answer is incorrect, with a selective loss masking strategy: cross-entropy loss is computed only on the reflection rationale and final answer, explicitly excluding the erroneous initial answer to prevent the model from memorizing mistakes. In the Consistency Direct Preference Optimization (Consist-DPO) stage, preference pairs are constructed by identifying cases where the model's reflection contradicts its final answer (e.g., the rationale identifies an error but the answer remains unchanged), and a standard DPO loss is applied to prefer consistent corrections over inconsistent ones. This two-stage design ensures the model both learns to detect and fix errors (RFT) and maintains logical coherence between what it reflects on and what it ultimately answers (Consist-DPO).

## Results

ReCoT improves accuracy across all three benchmarks for all models: Mulberry-7B reaches 95.14% on ScienceQA, 2262.40 on MME, and 59.87% on MMStar, while Mulberry-2B reaches 86.51%, 2070.58, and 50.20% respectively. The most dramatic gains appear in the Correction Success Rate (CSR) metrics: Mulberry-7B's CSR_self jumps from 22.29% to 75.00% on ScienceQA and from 17.22% to 26.86% on MMStar, while CSR_ctrl rises from 31.69% to 94.46% on ScienceQA. REVERIE-1.5-7B, which had near-zero self-correction ability (CSR_self = 0.00%), achieves 58.11% CSR_self and 86.53% CSR_ctrl on ScienceQA after ReCoT training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares ReCoT against SFT-trained baselines (with and without reflective prompting) for two model families: REVERIE-1.5-7B and Mulberry (2B and 7B). On REVERIE-1.5-7B, ReCoT wins decisively on all CSR metrics (e.g., CSR_self on ScienceQA goes from 0.00% to 58.11%) and improves accuracy by 3.27 points on ScienceQA and 2.54 points on MMStar, while MME score improves modestly (1827.89 → 1841.34). On Mulberry-2B, ReCoT outperforms the SFT+reflection baseline by 1.53 points on ScienceQA accuracy, 201.58 points on MME score, and 3.80 points on MMStar accuracy, with CSR_ctrl on MME jumping from 6.11% to 65.63%. Mulberry-7B shows the largest absolute gains: ReCoT achieves 95.14% ScienceQA accuracy (vs. 93.21% for SFT+reflection), 2262.40 MME score (vs. 2249.81), and 59.87% MMStar accuracy (vs. 57.20%), with CSR_self on ScienceQA reaching 75.00% versus 22.29% for the baseline. In all cases, ReCoT strictly dominates the SFT baselines on both accuracy and correction metrics, with the largest relative improvements observed in the smaller REVERIE-1.5-7B model, which had essentially no self-correction ability before training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of reflective instruction tuning for LVLMs (REVERIE, Mulberry, R3V) by identifying that existing reflection training actually reinforces confirmation bias rather than teaching genuine correction. It repurposes the DPO framework (Chen et al., 2024) in a novel way: instead of aligning with human preferences, Consist-DPO enforces logical coherence between a model's self-reflection and its final answer. The two-stage SFT-DPO paradigm departs from prior work that treats reflection and correction as a single objective, explicitly separating format learning (RFT with selective loss masking) from consistency enforcement (DPO).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that models explicitly trained with reflection rationales (REVERIE, Mulberry) still rationalize incorrect answers rather than correcting them is a practical warning for anyone deploying LVLMs in safety-critical or iterative reasoning settings. The proposed CSR and RCS metrics provide a concrete evaluation framework that the field lacked for measuring self-correction quality. Key open questions remain: whether the approach scales to larger models (>10B), how to dynamically select between direct answering and reflective correction based on question difficulty, and whether the 10k-sample DPO optimum generalizes across architectures. The paper's limitation to small models and its acknowledgment that no single response strategy universally outperforms others suggest this is a starting point rather than a final solution.
