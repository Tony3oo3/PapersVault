## Problem

While NeuS, VolSDF, and HF-NeuS each employ different strategies to map implicit function values to volume densities for unbiased surface reconstruction, no prior work has provided a unified theoretical framework explaining their relationships or the general conditions for unbiasedness. This paper addresses that gap by systematically characterizing the mapping function space and establishing when a density mapping guarantees first-order unbiased surface reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252023/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of calibrated multi-view RGB images of a single object as input and produces a continuous 3D neural implicit function (typically an SDF, but also raw fields or generalized winding numbers) whose zero level set defines the reconstructed surface. Training is supervised via photometric loss between rendered and reference images, optionally augmented with an Eikonal regularization term. Evaluation is performed on the DTU and OmniObject3D benchmarks using Chamfer distance, with all training conducted without mask supervision.

## Method

The paper first proves (Theorem 1) that modeling volume density σ, accumulated transmittance T, and color weights w are mutually equivalent representations, all derivable from one another. It then derives the necessary and sufficient condition for first-order unbiasedness: at the surface intersection point, σ'(t*) = σ²(t*). Building on this, the authors propose a general family of density mappings σ(f) = c·s·(f')^(k+1)·h(s·f·(f')^k), where h is a positive decreasing kernel function, s is a learnable scale, and k is a fixed exponent. They show that NeuS (logistic kernel, k=0), HF-NeuS (logistic kernel, k=0), and VolSDF (Laplace kernel, k=0) are all special cases of this family, and that VolSDF achieves unbiasedness when its scale parameter is tied to f' rather than treated as free. The framework is instantiated with new algebraic and softplus kernels, different exponent values (k=0,1,2), and alternative implicit functions (SDF, raw fields, generalized winding numbers), yielding seven case studies evaluated on benchmark datasets.

## Results

On the DTU dataset, UNIS-C3 (softplus kernel) achieves the best mean Chamfer distance of 0.777×10⁻³, outperforming NeuS/VolSDF (0.857×10⁻³) by approximately 9.3%. UNIS-C1 (unbiased VolSDF with Laplace kernel) also improves over the baselines to 0.778×10⁻³. On OmniObject3D, UNIS-C3 achieves the lowest mean Chamfer distance of 3.047×10⁻³, beating NeuS (3.094×10⁻³) and VolSDF (3.277×10⁻³). The raw implicit function variant (UNIS-C5) performs worst (0.895×10⁻³ on DTU), confirming that SDF with Eikonal regularization is preferred.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: NeuS (C0, logistic kernel) and VolSDF (Laplace kernel with learnable α). On DTU, UNIS-C1 (the unbiased VolSDF variant) reduces mean Chamfer distance from 0.857 to 0.778×10⁻³, a 9.2% improvement, confirming that fixing α = −2sf′(t) rather than treating it as a free parameter eliminates the bias in VolSDF. UNIS-C3 (softplus kernel) achieves the best overall DTU score of 0.777×10⁻³, marginally beating UNIS-C1. On OmniObject3D, UNIS-C3 (3.047×10⁻³) outperforms both NeuS (3.094×10⁻³) and VolSDF (3.277×10⁻³), while UNIS-C1 (3.192×10⁻³) beats VolSDF but trails NeuS slightly. The new algebraic and softplus kernels (C2, C3) perform comparably to the logistic kernel (NeuS), demonstrating that the specific kernel choice is less critical than satisfying the unbiasedness condition. UNIS-C5, which drops the SDF and uses a raw implicit function without Eikonal regularization, performs worst on both datasets, validating the importance of the SDF constraint.

## Relation to Prior Work

UNIS unifies three prominent neural implicit surface methods—NeuS, VolSDF, and HF-NeuS—by showing their seemingly different density mapping strategies (modeling color weights, volume density, and accumulated transmittance, respectively) are all special cases of a single parametric family σ(f) = c·s·(f')^(k+1)·h(s·f·(f')^k). The paper departs from prior work by treating the implicit function f as a general signed function rather than restricting to SDFs, and by introducing new kernel functions (algebraic, softplus) beyond the logistic and Laplace kernels already in use. It also extends the framework to generalized winding numbers as an alternative implicit representation, broadening the scope beyond the SDF-centric literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a clear theoretical criterion (σ'(t*) = σ²(t*)) for unbiased density mapping and shows that VolSDF's original mapping can be trivially corrected to achieve first-order unbiasedness, which practitioners can adopt immediately. However, the empirical gains are modest—different kernel choices yield nearly identical Chamfer distances, suggesting the specific kernel shape matters less than the unbiasedness condition itself. Open questions include extending the framework to semi-transparent objects, open (non-watertight) surfaces via UDFs, and incorporating high-frequency SDF decomposition (à la HF-NeuS) within the unified formulation.
