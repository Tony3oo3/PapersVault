## Problem

Conventional score-level fusion in whole-body biometric recognition (face, gait, body) applies fixed or globally learned weights that ignore per-sample quality variation and the heterogeneous score distributions produced by different modalities. This leads to suboptimal performance when some modalities are degraded (occlusion, low resolution, off-angle views), as the fusion cannot dynamically shift reliance toward higher-quality cues. The paper tackles the joint problem of quality-aware, learnable score fusion that adapts to both modality-specific score distributions and input quality without retraining the underlying biometric backbones.

## Input / Output

The method consumes precomputed similarity score matrices S ∈ R^{T×N} from N frozen biometric models (e.g., AdaFace for face, BigGait for gait, CAL/CLIP3DReID for body), where T is the total number of gallery templates and N is the number of modalities; Euclidean-distance models are converted to similarity via 1/(1+Euc(q,g)) before a BatchNorm layer. The QE additionally ingests intermediate backbone features I_n ∈ R^{L×U×P_n×d_n} (mean and std over patches, reduced to R^{L×2d_n}) to produce a scalar quality weight w_n per modality. The output is a fused score matrix S' ∈ R^{1×T} used for 1:1 verification (TAR@FAR) and 1:N open-set search (FNIR@FPIR), with L=8 frames sampled per query tracklet during training.

## Method

QME comprises three coupled components trained in two stages. First, a modality-specific Quality Estimator (QE) is trained with a pseudo-quality loss L_rank that uses the ranking position of the query among gallery templates as a proxy label (no human annotation), predicting a sigmoid quality weight w_n from pooled intermediate backbone features. Second, with the QE frozen, a Mixture of Score-Fusion Experts layer is trained: Z=2 experts (3-layer MLPs) each receive the concatenated, BatchNorm-normalized score matrix and produce a fused score S_z, while a router N_r maps the QE quality weights to expert assignment probabilities p_z (p_1=w_n, p_2=1−p_1), so that the expert prioritizing the high-quality modality dominates. The training objective is a novel score triplet loss L_score = ReLU(S'_nm) + ReLU(m − S'_mat) that simultaneously suppresses non-match scores below zero and pushes match scores above a margin m, directly optimizing the decision-threshold metrics (TAR@FAR, FNIR@FPIR) rather than relative distances alone.

## Results

On CCVID, QME (CAL-QE) achieves 94.1% Rank-1, 90.8% mAP, 76.2% TAR@1%FAR, and 12.3% FNIR@1%FPIR, outperforming the best fusion baseline (Farsight: 92.0% Rank-1, 91.2% mAP, 73.9% TAR, 13.9% FNIR). On the more challenging MEVID dataset, QME (AdaFace-QE) reaches 55.7% Rank-1, 28.2% mAP, 32.9% TAR@1%FAR, and 64.6% FNIR@1%FPIR, surpassing Z-score normalization (54.1% Rank-1, 27.4% mAP) and BSSF (53.5% Rank-1, 27.4% mAP). On BRIAR (the largest benchmark), QME achieves 84.5% TAR@0.1%FAR and 41.2% FNIR@1%FPIR under Face-Included Treatment, and 67.9% TAR@0.1%FAR and 64.1% FNIR@1%FPIR under Face-Restricted Treatment, leading all baselines in both settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QME consistently outperforms all 16 baselines across all four datasets. On CCVID, QME (CAL-QE) improves Rank-1 by +2.1 over Farsight (94.1 vs. 92.0) and by +2.3 over BSSF (94.1 vs. 91.8), while also achieving the best TAR (76.2 vs. 73.9) and lowest FNIR (12.3 vs. 13.9). On MEVID, where face quality is severely degraded, QME gains +1.6 Rank-1 over Z-score (55.7 vs. 54.1) and +2.2 over BSSF (55.7 vs. 53.5), with a notable +2.2 TAR improvement (32.9 vs. 30.7). On LTCC, QME edges out BSSF by +0.3 Rank-1 and +0.5 mAP (73.8/39.6 vs. 73.5/39.1), and on BRIAR it leads all methods in both Face-Included (84.5% TAR vs. 84.0% for Weighted-sum) and Face-Restricted (67.9% TAR vs. 64.9% for RHE) treatments. QME also surpasses the SoTA multimodal model SapiensID on CCVID (94.1 vs. 92.6 Rank-1) and LTCC (73.8 vs. 72.0 Rank-1), demonstrating that the score-fusion strategy itself provides substantial gains beyond what a single end-to-end model achieves. The gains are largest on challenging datasets (MEVID, BRIAR Face-Restricted), confirming that quality-guided expert routing is most beneficial when input quality varies significantly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Mixture of Experts architecture from NLP (Switch Transformers, Shazeer et al.) for biometric score-fusion, replacing the standard input-driven router with a quality-estimator-guided mechanism. The Quality Estimator component adapts the intermediate-feature extraction strategy from CAFace (Kim et al., NeurIPS 2022) to a multi-modal setting, while the pseudo-quality loss eliminates the need for manual quality annotations by distilling ranking signals from pretrained backbones. The score triplet loss modifies the classic FaceNet triplet loss to directly constrain non-match score magnitudes, aligning training with verification and open-set search metrics. Positionally, the work sits at the intersection of the score-fusion literature (Z-score, likelihood-ratio, SVM-based methods) and whole-body biometric systems (Farsight, SapiensID), proposing a learnable, quality-aware alternative to fixed-rule fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's modularity—requiring no retraining of biometric backbones and operating purely on score matrices—makes it a practical drop-in for existing multi-modal deployment pipelines where sensor quality is unpredictable. A key open question is whether the pseudo-quality loss generalizes beyond the two-expert, two-to-three-modality setting tested here; scaling to more experts or modalities (e.g., thermal, audio) remains unexplored. The paper also acknowledges that in scenarios where one modality is consistently weak (e.g., face on LTCC), fusion can underperform the best single model, suggesting that quality-aware gating alone may not fully resolve modality imbalance. Broader applicability to other fusion problems (e.g., multi-sensor fusion in robotics) is implied but not demonstrated.
