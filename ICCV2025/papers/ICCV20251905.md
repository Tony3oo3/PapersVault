## Problem

Direct text-to-3DGS generation for unbounded real-world scenes requires jointly modeling multi-view images and scene-specific camera poses, but prior methods that extend 2D generative models to this joint task suffer from instability due to the modality gap between images and poses. This instability forces reliance on external stabilization models (e.g., SDS++ refinement, Dust3R distillation), preventing a self-contained single-model pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251905/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an arbitrary text prompt as input and produces a pixel-aligned 3D Gaussian Splatting scene (position, opacity, covariance, color per pixel across K=8 views at 320×512 resolution) along with the corresponding multi-view images and camera poses represented as Plücker rays. It also supports a camera-conditioned mode where user-specified camera trajectories replace the generated poses, enabling controlled multi-view image synthesis. The video backbone (Mochi) operates in latent space, so the actual generation happens on VAE-encoded image latents before decoding.

## Method

VideoRFSplat introduces a dual-stream architecture where a dedicated rectified-flow pose generation transformer is side-attached to a pretrained Mochi video model, with the two streams interacting only through cross-attention communication blocks at intermediate layers to minimize modality interference. The key sampling innovation is an asynchronous timestep strategy that decouples pose and image denoising schedules (t_R = max(t_I − δ, 0)), allowing the lower-dimensional pose modality to denoise faster and thereby reducing the mutual ambiguity that causes divergence in synchronized sampling. A modified classifier-free guidance term exploits the fact that a near-noisy pose acts as an unconditional signal, enabling the clearer pose to better guide image generation. A feed-forward 3D CNN Gaussian Splat decoder with attention layers reconstructs the 3DGS from the generated latents and rays, trained with L1+LPIPS losses on both target and source views. The entire system is trained end-to-end with a conditional flow-matching loss that accepts independent timesteps per modality, eliminating the need for any post-hoc SDS refinement.

## Results

On the MVImgNet validation set, VideoRFSplat achieves FID-10K of 30.33 and CLIPScore of 33.0, outperforming SplatFlow (FID 34.85, CLIP 31.43) and Director3D (FID 39.55, CLIP 30.48) without any SDS++ refinement. On DL3DV, it reaches FID-2.4K of 73.69 and CLIPScore of 32.5, again surpassing both baselines without refinement. On the out-of-domain T3Bench benchmark, VideoRFSplat attains the lowest BRISQUE (32.2) and NIQE (4.20) scores along with the highest CLIPScore (33.6), beating even the SDS++-refined variants of SplatFlow and Director3D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoRFSplat outperforms all direct text-to-3DGS baselines without relying on SDS++ post-hoc refinement. Against the two primary competitors—SplatFlow [20] and Director3D [35]—it achieves lower FID (30.33 vs. 34.85 on MVImgNet; 73.69 vs. 79.91 on DL3DV) and higher CLIPScore (33.0 vs. 31.43; 32.5 vs. 30.06) even without refinement, while their SDS++-augmented variants still trail on FID. On T3Bench, VideoRFSplat (BRISQUE 32.2, NIQE 4.20, CLIP 33.6) surpasses both SplatFlow w/ SDS++ (32.4, 4.24, 33.2) and Director3D w/ SDS++ (32.3, 4.35, 32.9). Older SDS-based methods (DreamFusion, Magic3D, LatentNeRF, SJC, Fantasia3D, ProlificDreamer) are substantially worse on T3Bench, with BRISQUE scores ranging from 61.5 to 92.8 versus VideoRFSplat's 32.2. For camera-conditioned generation, VideoRFSplat outperforms MotionCtrl [76] and CameraCtrl [21] on FID-8K (43.07 vs. 69.04/62.97), translation error (0.063 vs. 0.109/0.088), rotation error (0.4223 vs. 0.5648/0.5176), and CLIPScore (31.1 vs. 29.8/30.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of direct text-to-3DGS generation initiated by SplatFlow and Director3D, which jointly model multi-view images and camera trajectories from text. It departs from their channel-concatenation or sequential-generation designs by introducing a dual-stream architecture that side-attaches a dedicated rectified-flow pose model to a pretrained video generation backbone (Mochi), communicating only through cross-attention blocks. The key methodological departure is asynchronous sampling—decoupling pose and image timesteps so poses denoise faster—replacing the synchronous denoising assumed by prior joint models. By leveraging a video model's inherent multi-frame coherence and eliminating the SDS++ refinement step that SplatFlow and Director3D depend on, the paper positions itself as a self-contained alternative to the external-model-dependent pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that VideoRFSplat removes the per-scene SDS optimization bottleneck and external model dependencies (Dust3R, additional 2D refiners) that have made prior direct-generation methods complex and fragile, enabling a single end-to-end model for real-world scene generation. The asynchronous sampling insight—that modality-specific denoising speeds reduce mutual ambiguity in joint generation—is a generalizable principle applicable beyond 3DGS to other multi-modal joint generation tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer video sequences or more complex unbounded scenes, whether the dual-stream design generalizes to other backbone architectures beyond Mochi, and how well it handles dynamic or interactive scene generation.
