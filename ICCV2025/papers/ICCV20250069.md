## Problem

Existing image stitching methods face a fundamental trade-off: spatially adaptive warps achieve parallax-tolerant alignment but introduce unnatural distortion, while shape-preserving warps produce natural panoramas at the cost of alignment accuracy. Learning-based approaches improve alignment robustness but largely neglect visual naturalness, and no prior method simultaneously achieves accurate alignment and structure preservation in an unsupervised paradigm.

## Input / Output

The method takes two overlapping RGB images (resized to 440×440 during training) and an externally estimated homography (from UDIS++ or VGG+RANSAC) as input. It produces two bidirectionally warped images that are blended via average fusion into a final stitched panorama, along with the intermediate bidirectional optical flow fields. The pipeline is designed to handle large parallax and reduce the proportion of invalid (hole) pixels compared to unidirectional warping.

## Method

PixelStitch operates in two stages. First, a homography decomposition algorithm splits the estimated homography H into two equal transformations H* = H₁ = H₂ (solved via eigenvalue decomposition so H = (H*)²), projecting both images onto a virtual midplane to distribute projective distortion evenly. Second, a RAFT-based network predicts bidirectional optical flow fields between the coarsely aligned images, which are refined by Projection-Weighted Natural Coefficients (PN-Coef) that smoothly attenuate flow magnitude in non-overlapping regions based on each pixel's projected position along the line connecting image centers. Training is fully unsupervised, using a composite loss combining SSIM-based alignment, a content-selection-mask matching term for stable convergence, a bidirectional consistency constraint to prevent drift, and structure-preservation terms (smoothness and multi-scale flow uniformity) to suppress distortion.

## Results

On the UDIS-D dataset, PixelStitch achieves an average mSSIM of 0.874, outperforming the strongest baseline PixelAlign (0.858) by 1.6 points and the next-best traditional method ELA (0.774) by 10 points. The advantage is most pronounced on the Hard subset (0.800 vs. 0.784 for PixelAlign and 0.642 for ELA), indicating superior robustness to challenging parallax. On 18 traditional datasets, PixelStitch achieves the best mSSIM on 16 out of 18, with notable margins such as 0.920 vs. 0.822 on carpark and 0.911 vs. 0.768 on temple.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PixelStitch outperforms all six baselines on the UDIS-D benchmark, with the largest gains over feature-based methods: +23.4 mSSIM over LPC (0.640), +21.3 over GES-GSP (0.661), and +14.8 over APAP (0.726). Against the closest learning-based competitor PixelAlign [11], PixelStitch leads by 1.6 mSSIM on average (0.874 vs. 0.858), with the gap widening on the Hard subset (0.800 vs. 0.784). On the 18 traditional generalization datasets, PixelStitch achieves the best mSSIM on 16 of 18, but PixelAlign retains an edge on two scenes (train: 0.757 vs. 0.647; garden: 0.818 vs. 0.705), suggesting PixelAlign's supervised pixel-level training still helps in certain texture-rich or low-parallax scenarios. Among traditional methods, ELA is consistently the strongest baseline, yet PixelStitch surpasses it by 8–20 mSSIM across most traditional datasets. The ablation in Table 1 further confirms that removing the bidirectional consistency loss (L_bic) drops average mSSIM from 0.874 to 0.763, highlighting its critical role in stabilizing the bidirectional warp.

## Relation to Prior Work

PixelStitch sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: traditional feature-based stitching (APAP, ELA, LPC, GES-GSP) that relies on hand-crafted features and spatially adaptive warps, and recent learning-based unsupervised methods (UDIS++, PixelAlign) that predict deformation fields via CNNs. It departs from prior learning-based stitching by introducing a bidirectional pixel-wise warp (as opposed to the unidirectional warps in PixelAlign and UDIS++) and a homography decomposition step that projects both images onto a virtual midplane before residual flow estimation. The optical flow backbone is RAFT, and the unsupervised training paradigm follows the reconstruction/consistency-based objectives from the UDIS++ and PixelAlign lineage, but the specific combination of midplane projection, PN-Coef weighting, and bidirectional consistency constraints is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the long-standing trade-off in image stitching between parallax-tolerant alignment and structural naturalness, showing that distributing deformation across two views (bidirectional warp) with a geometrically motivated midplane can improve both simultaneously. The virtual midplane concept offers a clean geometric interpretation that could inform future multi-view or 3D-aware stitching pipelines. However, the method is limited to two-image stitching, depends on the quality of the initial homography estimate, and the quantitative gains over the strongest baseline (PixelAlign) are modest (~1.6 mSSIM on UDIS-D). Open questions include whether the bidirectional formulation extends naturally to multi-image panoramas and whether the PN-Coef mechanism generalizes to scenes with significant depth variation beyond the planar assumption.
