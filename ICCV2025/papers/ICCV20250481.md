## Problem

Text-driven 3D human motion generation relies almost exclusively on 3D motion capture data, which is expensive to acquire, limited in scale and diversity, and constrained by controlled collection environments. While 2D human videos offer a vast, accessible source of motion covering a wider range of activities, they cannot be directly repurposed for 3D generation because 2D motion entangles camera movement with the human's actual 3D motion. The paper addresses how to effectively leverage this abundant 2D data to improve the quality and diversity of text-to-3D-motion synthesis.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language text prompt as input and produces a full 3D human motion sequence expressed as SMPL pose parameters (via SMPLify fitting). Internally, the pipeline operates on a decomposed representation: 2D local joint motion (J−1 joints per frame, normalized by bounding box) and 2D root velocity per view, rather than raw 3D joint positions. Training uses 97.63 hours of 2D motion extracted from EgoExo4D and Motion-X++ videos for pretraining, and 28.59 hours of 3D motion from HumanML3D for multi-view fine-tuning with V=4 virtual cameras.

## Method

The core insight is to reformulate 3D motion generation as multi-view 2D motion generation with a root-decoupled representation, separating local joint motion from global root movement to avoid the camera-motion entanglement problem inherent in 2D video data. A transformer-based diffusion model (8 decoder layers, 4 heads, 512 hidden units) is first trained as a single-view 2D local motion generator on large-scale text-2D-motion pairs. It is then extended into a Multi-view Diffusion model by inserting a multi-view attention layer into each transformer block and adding a root velocity head, trained on synthetic multi-view 2D sequences projected from 3D data with cameras tracking the character root. Following the ControlNet strategy, all original 2D layers are frozen during this fine-tuning stage, so learning focuses exclusively on view consistency and global movement. At inference, the model generates multi-view 2D local motion and root velocities, which are triangulated into 3D local joint positions while root velocities are accumulated over time to recover the 3D global trajectory, yielding a complete 3D motion sequence.

## Results

On HumanML3D, Motion-2-to-3 achieves the best FID of 0.321 among all compared methods, outperforming OMG (0.381), MLD (0.473), MDM (0.544), and MAA (0.774). However, it trails the strongest baselines on R-Precision (0.697 vs. OMG's 0.784 and MLD's 0.772) and Diversity (9.286 vs. MLD's 9.724), which the authors attribute to not directly optimizing in the 3D feature space used by the evaluation metrics. On novel out-of-distribution text prompts, the method substantially outperforms MDM and MLD in user study, achieving a 51.43% Best Motion Rate (vs. ~24% for both baselines) and 84.49% Top-2 Motion Rate (vs. 60.68% and 54.83%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MDM [70], Motion-2-to-3 wins on every reported metric: FID drops from 0.544 to 0.321, MM Dist from 5.566 to 3.579, and R-Precision rises from 0.611 to 0.697. Against MLD [13], the method achieves a clearly better FID (0.321 vs. 0.473) but lags on R-Precision (0.697 vs. 0.772), MM Dist (3.579 vs. 3.196), and Diversity (9.286 vs. 9.724). Against OMG [38], the FID advantage is modest (0.321 vs. 0.381) while R-Precision is notably lower (0.697 vs. 0.784). Against MAA [4], the method dominates on FID (0.321 vs. 0.774) and Diversity (9.286 vs. 8.230). In the ablation-style comparisons (Table 3), the 2D-to-3D lifting strategies—MotionBERT (FID 23.292), MAS (FID 11.893), and the 2D-condition variant (FID 0.877)—all fall well short of the proposed multi-view diffusion approach, confirming that joint multi-view generation with root-velocity prediction is critical for both realism and global motion coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MDM's transformer-diffusion architecture for motion generation and MAS's multi-view 2D-to-3D triangulation pipeline, but departs from both in critical ways. Where MAS independently generates per-view 2D motions and enforces consistency via a post-hoc triangulation block, Motion-2-to-3 introduces a unified multi-view diffusion model with cross-view attention layers trained end-to-end on synthetic multi-view data, following the ControlNet-style strategy of freezing pretrained layers and training only new components. The key conceptual departure is the root decoupling formulation—separating local joint motion from global root velocity—which is inspired by the 2D-to-3D object generation literature (Zero1to3, MVDream) but adapted to human motion to handle the entanglement of camera and body movement inherent in 2D video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a fundamental data bottleneck in text-to-motion generation: 3D mocap datasets are small, expensive, and limited in motion diversity, while 2D human videos are abundant and cover a much wider range of activities. The practical finding that 2D video data outperforms monocular 3D reconstruction annotations (Table 3) provides a clear recipe for practitioners seeking to scale motion generation beyond curated mocap. Open questions include whether the approach scales to the much larger 2D video corpora available in the wild, whether the transformer-diffusion architecture is optimal (the authors note GPT-style alternatives), and how to extend to hand motion and object interactions that remain challenging for mocap systems.
