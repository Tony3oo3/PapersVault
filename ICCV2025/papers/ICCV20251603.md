## Problem

Encoder-based text-to-image customization methods require training data with multiple images of the same object under diverse poses, backgrounds, and lighting, but no such large-scale dataset exists because real-world images lack object-identity annotations. Existing encoder-based approaches either train on single-image or limited multi-view data (hurting identity preservation) or rely on expensive per-object optimization at test time. This paper addresses the data bottleneck by synthesizing a high-quality multi-image customization dataset and training a new encoder-based model on it.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model accepts 1–3 reference images of a target object (any resolution, processed through the same diffusion backbone) and a free-form text prompt describing a new scene or composition. The number of reference images at inference can differ from training because the shared-attention conditioning is agnostic to sequence length. The output is a single generated image of the object placed in the prompted scene, produced in one forward pass without per-object fine-tuning.

## Method

The pipeline has three components. First, SynCD is generated by running a depth-conditioned FLUX DiT model in parallel over N prompts per object, with a Masked Shared Attention (MSA) mechanism that lets each image's features attend to the foreground object regions of all other images (while masking out their backgrounds and cross-image text tokens); for rigid objects, Objaverse 3D assets provide depth maps and cross-view correspondence, and pairwise feature warping is applied during early denoising steps to enforce multi-view geometric consistency. Second, the customization model is fine-tuned (via LoRA on FLUX or U-Net backbones) so that reference-image features are concatenated with the target's features along the sequence dimension in every attention block, allowing the target to attend to fine-grained reference details rather than a compressed embedding. Third, at inference the text and image classifier-free guidance vectors are each rescaled to the minimum of their norms before being weighted by separate strength parameters, which prevents the overexposure artifacts that arise from naively summing high-magnitude guidance at large scales.

## Results

On the DreamBooth evaluation set (30 objects, 25 prompts), Ours (3B) achieves the best GeometricScore of 0.838, outperforming JeDi (0.780), Emu-2 (0.740), and IP-Adapter Plus (0.675). Ours (3B) also leads on MDINOv2-I for background-change prompts (0.822) and TIFA (0.863). Ours (12B) ties JeDi at 0.780 GeometricScore while producing higher-fidelity images with greater viewpoint and background diversity. In human evaluation, Ours (1B) is preferred over JeDi 68.2% of the time overall, and Ours (3B) over Emu-2 66.7% of the time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ours (3B) clearly outperforms all baselines on the combined GeometricScore: 0.838 vs. JeDi (0.780), Emu-2 (0.740), MoMA (0.730), BLIP-Diffusion (0.714), IP-Adapter (0.704), OminiControl (0.685), IP-Adapter Plus (0.675), and Kosmos (0.679). Against the strongest baseline JeDi, Ours (3B) gains +0.058 GeometricScore and +0.051 on MDINOv2-I (background change), while Ours (12B) ties JeDi at 0.780 but produces visibly higher-fidelity images with more diverse viewpoints. At the 12B scale, Ours (12B) substantially outperforms both IP-Adapter (12B) (0.780 vs. 0.639) and OminiControl (12B) (0.780 vs. 0.685), indicating the benefit of the SynCD training data and shared-attention conditioning over prior 12B-scale approaches. In pairwise human evaluation, Ours (1B) is preferred over JeDi 68.2% of the time overall (80.9% on photorealism), Ours (3B) over Emu-2 66.7% overall, and Ours (12B) over OminiControl 58.0% overall, confirming the quantitative gains translate to perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends encoder-based text-to-image customization methods (IP-Adapter, JeDi, Emu-2) by addressing their core data bottleneck: the absence of large-scale multi-image datasets with diverse poses, backgrounds, and lighting for the same object. It repurposes the Masked Shared Attention mechanism from multi-view generation work (Zero123++, StoryDiffusion) for two distinct purposes—synthesizing consistent multi-image training data and conditioning the customization model on reference images—while adding 3D asset priors from Objaverse and depth conditioning for rigid-object consistency. The inference contribution (norm-normalized classifier-free guidance) departs from standard guidance rescaling by jointly scaling text and image guidance vectors, a simple but effective fix for overexposure at high image guidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The publicly released SynCD dataset (~95K objects, 2–3 images each) provides a practical resource that the customization community has lacked, and the ablation showing that merely fine-tuning an existing IP-Adapter Plus on SynCD already yields substantial gains underscores the dataset's value independent of architectural changes. The shared-attention conditioning and normalized inference are lightweight, model-agnostic techniques likely to be adopted by subsequent work. Key limitations include single-object focus, reliance on 3D assets for rigid-object consistency, and the gap between synthetic and real-world object diversity; extending to multi-object scenes, deformable objects without 3D priors, and video generation are natural next steps.
