## Problem

Existing point cloud frame interpolation (PCI) methods treat intermediate frame estimation as a deterministic single-pass prediction, causing geometric distortion and motion trajectory hopping in scenes with large or non-uniform object motion. While diffusion models have shown promise in 3D shape generation and video interpolation, their potential for point cloud time-series interpolation—where coupled motion inference and geometric reconstruction must be solved simultaneously—remains unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes two consecutive LiDAR point cloud frames (each R^{3×N}) and a continuous intermediate timestamp n ∈ (0,1) as conditional inputs, producing a single interpolated point cloud frame at time n. During inference, three intermediate frames are generated between every pair of input frames at 2.5 Hz temporal resolution; 4096 points are sampled for training and 8192 for inference. The nuScenes evaluation uses data downsampled from 20 Hz to 10 Hz to stress-test large-motion interpolation.

## Method

DiffPCI formulates PCI as a conditional denoising diffusion process: a forward Markov chain progressively adds Gaussian noise to the ground-truth intermediate frame, and a learned reverse denoiser (conditioned on both boundary frames and the timestamp) iteratively recovers the clean frame, trained via a PCI-specific variational lower bound that reduces to a Chamfer-distance regression objective. The denoiser's core is a dual-branch full-scale motion estimator that encodes each boundary frame with a DGCNN backbone augmented by dense cascading across EdgeConv layers to preserve multi-scale geometric cues, then applies local Point Transformer and self/cross-attention to compute soft correspondences and bidirectional scene flow. An adaptive selection module warps both input frames along the estimated flows to produce four candidate intermediate frames, dynamically choosing a central and a remedial frame based on the interpolation time n, and fuses them via k-NN clustering with an MLP-weighted softmax. Training additionally employs two self-supervised regularization losses that supervise each branch's warped frames against the ground truth, providing gradient signals for the motion estimator beyond the final interpolation loss. The model uses 20 diffusion steps during training but only 3 at inference for efficiency.

## Results

On nuScenes (the most challenging large-motion dataset), DiffPCI achieves an average CD of 0.84 and EMD of 165.04, outperforming the next-best method FastPCI (CD 1.11, EMD 173.64) by 24% in CD and 5% in EMD. On Argoverse 2, DiffPCI records CD 0.67 and EMD 56.79, and on KITTI odometry, CD 0.50 and EMD 53.67, both leading all baselines on average metrics. The paper reports a 27% CD reduction and a 25.5 absolute EMD reduction over PointINet on nuScenes, with the largest gains appearing in Frame-2 (the midpoint frame) where large inter-frame motion is most pronounced.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffPCI outperforms all five baselines on average CD and EMD across all three datasets. Against the closest competitor FastPCI, DiffPCI improves average CD by 0.27 (nuScenes), 0.06 (Argoverse 2), and 0.08 (KITTI), with EMD gains of 8.6, 1.16, and 0.11 respectively; however, on Argoverse 2 Frame-2, FastPCI marginally edges out DiffPCI on CD (0.77 vs. 0.79). Versus the dual-branch PointINet, DiffPCI achieves substantially larger margins (CD 0.84 vs. 1.55 on nuScenes), confirming that the diffusion-based iterative refinement and full-scale encoder address the large-motion failures of direct bidirectional flow warping. Against the neural-field-based NeuralPCI, DiffPCI leads by 0.31 CD and 14.07 EMD on nuScenes, showing that explicit motion estimation with diffusion denoising surpasses implicit 4D field interpolation in high-dynamic scenes. The scene-flow baselines GMSF and DiffSF (used as linear-interpolation references) are consistently the weakest, with GMSF's backward branch performing particularly poorly (CD 2.48 on nuScenes), highlighting the importance of the dual-branch design and the diffusion denoising loop over single-pass flow estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffPCI extends the diffusion model paradigm from 3D shape generation (Luo & Hu, PVD, LION) and scene flow estimation (DiffSF, DifFlow3D) to the specific task of point cloud frame interpolation, which prior methods (PointINet, NeuralPCI, FastPCI) treated as a deterministic single-pass or iterative optimization problem. The motion estimator backbone is adapted from DiffSF, while the bidirectional flow strategy and k-NN point fusion follow PointINet. The key conceptual departure is formulating interpolation as a progressive denoising process with a PCI-specific variational lower bound, combined with a full-scale dense-cascading DGCNN encoder and a dual-branch adaptive selection module designed for large, non-uniform motions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to apply diffusion models directly to point cloud frame interpolation, offering a probabilistic alternative to deterministic flow-based methods that struggle with large and irregular motions in autonomous driving. The practical relevance is clear for high-frequency LiDAR applications where sensor rates are limited. However, the paper's own conclusion acknowledges open questions around occluded scenarios and generalization to unseen environments, and the evaluation is confined to three driving datasets. The claimed 860% EMD reduction on nuScenes appears inconsistent with the reported table values (~13% over PointINet), which warrants caution when interpreting the magnitude of improvement.
