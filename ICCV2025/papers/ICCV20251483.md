## Problem

Existing 3D plane recovery methods from monocular images formulate the task as 2D pixel-level segmentation, relying solely on 2D convolutional or attention-based features while neglecting the inherent 3D spatial structure of planar surfaces. This 2D-centric formulation is fundamentally insufficient for accurately characterizing real-world 3D configurations, particularly because planar surfaces possess holistic geometric properties that cannot be fully captured from pixel-level cues alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251483/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (256×192 in experiments) as input and produces N global plane instances, each with a 3D parameter (surface normal and camera-to-plane distance), a per-pixel binary segmentation mask, and a rendered depth map. Pretrained monocular depth (Metric3D) and surface normal (Omnidata) estimates are consumed as geometric priors at the primitive level, with learned residuals added to refine metric accuracy. The segmentation is performed in 3D space over planar primitives and then splatted back to the 2D image plane, rather than being predicted directly from 2D feature maps.

## Method

PlaneRAS introduces a two-stage pipeline: (1) a reconstruction module that initializes M local planar primitive queries (48×64 grid of 2D anchors) and iteratively refines them via deformable cross-attention with multi-scale backbone features, decoding each into a 3D planar primitive (center, normal quaternion, scale, opacity); and (2) an aggregation module where N global queries (20 centroids) serve as 3D cluster centers, with local primitives assigned via dot-product similarity to produce instance-level segmentation directly in 3D space. A custom plane kernel replaces the radial Gaussian decay of 2D Gaussian Splatting with a product-of-sigmoids formulation that maintains near-constant opacity across the primitive surface, preserving geometric integrity for walls and floors. The 3D segmentation probabilities are then splatted onto the image plane to yield per-pixel masks. Pretrained monocular depth and normal estimates are integrated as additive priors on the primitive parameters, enabling direct transfer of 3D geometric knowledge. Training is supervised end-to-end with L1 depth loss, cosine normal loss, and a bipartite-matching aggregation loss covering classification, parameter regression, mask, and per-plane depth terms.

## Results

On ScanNet, PlaneRAS (Swin-B) achieves the best plane segmentation with VI=0.593, RI=0.950, and SC=0.883, outperforming the previous best (PlaneRecTR Swin-B: VI=0.651, RI=0.943, SC=0.866). On NYUv2, it reaches VI=0.987, RI=0.926, SC=0.771, again surpassing all baselines. For plane parameter estimation, PlaneRAS reduces normal error to 7.77° and offset error to 106.61 mm, a 24.05% and 35.44% reduction over PlaneRecTR (10.23°, 165.13 mm). On NYUv2 depth accuracy, PlaneRAS achieves the best RMSE (0.488) and δ₂/δ₃ (97.8/99.3), though it trails PlaneRecTR on Rel (0.165 vs 0.157) and δ₁ (71.6 vs 74.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PlaneRAS (Swin-B) outperforms all seven baselines on every segmentation metric (VI, RI, SC) on both ScanNet and NYUv2. The closest competitor is PlaneRecTR (Swin-B), which PlaneRAS surpasses by 0.058 in VI (0.593 vs 0.651) and 0.017 in SC (0.883 vs 0.866) on ScanNet, and by 0.058 in VI (0.987 vs 1.045) and 0.026 in SC (0.771 vs 0.745) on NYUv2. PlaneAC is the second-closest on ScanNet (VI 0.658 vs 0.593) but trails by a wider margin on NYUv2 (VI 1.015 vs 0.987). PlanePDM, despite its boundary-aware design, is clearly outperformed (VI 0.839 vs 0.593 on ScanNet). Earlier methods (PlaneNet, PlaneRCNN, PlaneAE, PlaneTR) show substantially larger gaps, with VI values 0.1–0.7 higher. On NYUv2 depth accuracy, PlaneRAS wins on RMSE (0.488 vs 0.547 for PlaneRecTR) and relaxed-threshold accuracy (δ₂: 97.8 vs 94.2), but concedes the strictest metric δ₁ (71.6 vs 74.2) and Rel (0.165 vs 0.157) to PlaneRecTR, which the authors attribute to over-smoothing from pretrained depth priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

PlaneRAS departs from the dominant 2D query-based paradigm (PlaneNet, PlaneRCNN, PlaneTR, PlaneRecTR) that treats plane recovery as pixel-level segmentation on image feature maps. It borrows the iterative deformable-attention refinement from GaussianFormer, the dot-product aggregation mechanism from Mask2Former, and the bipartite-matching loss from PlaneRecTR, but reorganizes them around an explicit 3D planar-primitive representation. The key conceptual shift is performing instance segmentation in 3D space (clustering primitives by geometric similarity) and then splatting back to 2D, rather than segmenting directly in the image plane. Additionally, the explicit 3D parameterization enables direct injection of pretrained monocular depth and normal priors (Metric3D, Omnidata), a capability unavailable to purely 2D methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that lifting plane recovery into an explicit 3D representation space yields consistent gains across segmentation, parameter regression, and depth metrics, and that pretrained geometric priors plug in naturally once the representation is 3D. The plane kernel (sigmoid-based flat opacity replacing Gaussian decay) is a simple, reusable design choice for any splatting-based planar reconstruction. However, the method remains single-view and susceptible to domain shift, and the limited primitive count (48×64) causes visible boundary perturbations; extending to multi-view settings and scaling primitive counts are natural next steps. The approach also opens a path toward unifying feed-forward plane recovery with the richer geometric tools available in offline reconstruction.
