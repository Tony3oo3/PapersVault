## Problem

Existing OOD detection methods operate on raw activations or logits without distinguishing between directions that are critical for the classification decision and those that are largely ignored by the training objective. This paper identifies that these two types of directions have complementary and sometimes conflicting utility for OOD detection: the classification objective 'taints' decisive directions for ID/OOD separation, while insignificant directions can interfere with activation shaping methods, yet neither is exploited in a principled way by prior work.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained CNN (ResNet-50, MobileNetV2, or DenseNet-101) with a linear classification head, an RGB test image, and a small random subset (10%) of the training set for computing cosine similarities. The SVD of the classification head weight matrix W ∈ R^{c×n} is computed once offline; at inference, the activation vector a ∈ R^n is projected into two orthogonal subspaces. The output is a single scalar OOD score (lower = more likely OOD) formed as a weighted product of two component scores, which can be thresholded for a binary ID/OOD decision.

## Method

ActSub decomposes the classification head weight matrix W via SVD (W = UΣV^T) into a decisive subspace (top-k right singular vectors) and an insignificant subspace (remaining vectors), where k is automatically selected to balance the expected norms of the two projected activation components. For the insignificant subspace, a cosine-similarity score (extending NNGuide) measures how far the test activation is from training activations in that subspace, capturing 'untainted' features that behave like a random network and are discriminative for Far-OOD. For the decisive subspace, an activation shaping method (e.g., SCALE) is applied to the projected activation before computing an energy score, which reduces interference from insignificant directions and improves Near-OOD detection. The final ActSub score is the product of the two component scores raised to a tunable power λ, combining the complementary strengths of each subspace.

## Results

On the standard ImageNet-1k ResNet-50 OOD benchmark (iNaturalist, SUN, Places, Textures), ActSub achieves an average AUC of 96.74% and FPR@95 of 15.14%, outperforming the strongest baseline SCALE (95.71% AUC, 20.05% FPR) by +1.03% AUC and −4.91% FPR. On the more challenging OpenOOD benchmark, ActSub with SCALE reaches SotA for both Near-OOD (84.24% AUC, 52.60% FPR) and Far-OOD (96.96% AUC, 14.29% FPR), surpassing SCALE by +2.88% AUC on Near-OOD. The method generalizes across backbones: on MobileNetV2 it achieves 95.37% average AUC, and on CIFAR-10/100 with DenseNet-101 it reaches 96.87%/89.35% AUC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ActSub outperforms all 22 baselines on the average AUC/FPR across the four standard OOD datasets. Among distance-based methods, the S→ component (cosine similarity on the insignificant subspace) beats fDBD by +0.64% AUC and −6.87% FPR on average, with a particularly large margin on Textures (98.33% vs. 92.12% AUC). Among logit/energy methods, the −E·S→ variant surpasses NNGuide by +1.05% AUC and −2.31% FPR, and outperforms CoRP (92.61% AUC) by +0.26% AUC. Among activation shaping methods, the full ActSub (S↔) beats SCALE by +1.03% AUC and −4.91% FPR, and outperforms the next-best DDCS (95.55% AUC) by +1.19% AUC. On the OpenOOD benchmark, ActSub with SCALE achieves SotA for both Near-OOD (84.24% AUC vs. SCALE's 81.36%) and Far-OOD (96.96% AUC vs. SCALE's 96.53%), with the Near-OOD gap being the most pronounced (+2.88% AUC, −7.16% FPR). The individual S← component (activation shaping on the decisive subspace alone) already beats SCALE on iNaturalist, SUN, and Places but lags on Textures, which the combined S↔ score recovers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the null-space analysis of Cook et al. [8] by replacing the strict null space of the classification head with a broader set of 'insignificant' directions identified via SVD of the weight matrix, and introduces a second, previously unexplored implication: that these directions interfere with activation shaping methods by causing inconsistent pruning. The decisive-subspace score builds directly on the energy formulation of Liu et al. [36] and the cosine-similarity scoring of NNGuide [41], while the paper explicitly distinguishes its SVD-based decomposition from PCA-based methods by showing that variance-based and decision-relevance-based directions are fundamentally different. The combination of a distance score on the insignificant subspace with an energy score on the shaped decisive subspace is the paper's core methodological contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that directions barely affecting the classifier output are more discriminative for OOD than the decisive directions provides a principled explanation for why raw activations are suboptimal for OOD detection and offers a practical, post-hoc remedy requiring no retraining. The complementary behavior—insignificant subspace excels on Far-OOD while the decisive subspace (with activation shaping) excels on Near-OOD—gives practitioners a clear diagnostic for which regime they are in. Limitations include the dependence on training-set activations for the cosine-similarity score, the requirement of a linear classification head, and evaluation restricted to image classification; extending to non-linear heads, other modalities, and tasks like segmentation remains open.
