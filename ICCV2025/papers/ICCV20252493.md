## Problem

Real-world multi-view, multi-lighting datasets with ground-truth geometry are scarce and limited in scale, making it difficult to train and evaluate modern learning-based photometric stereo and relighting methods. Existing datasets either lack dense lighting coverage, multi-view capture, or high-precision 3D shapes, while synthetic datasets suffer from an inherent domain gap with real-world lighting complexities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252493/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each physical object (up to 15×15×15 cm) is captured from 270 views (6 cameras × 45 turntable rotations) under 53 lighting conditions per view, yielding 57,240 LDR images per object that are exposure-bracketed into 14,310 HDR images at 5328×4608 resolution. The 1,637 lighting conditions comprise 1,620 one-light-at-a-time (5×5 LED patches), 8 environment maps, 8 linear patterns, and 1 full-on illumination. Outputs include per-image foreground masks, calibrated camera poses, and sub-millimeter 3D meshes, all aligned to a common coordinate frame.

## Method

The authors build a custom near-field lightstage (80×80×80 cm cube) with 24,576 independently PWM-controlled LEDs on all six faces, 6 Basler machine-vision cameras (70 mm focal length), and a programmable turntable, enabling synchronized capture of multi-view, multi-lighting images in approximately 45 minutes per object. Camera calibration uses a 12×9 chessboard (mean reprojection error 0.045 px), LED positions are calibrated via an obsidian sphere with joint optimization of sphere center and per-face rigid transforms, and color correction uses an X-Rite ColorChecker. Foreground masks are generated by SAM2 with automated prompts derived from background subtraction and morphological operations, while camera poses are estimated from ARTags via COLMAP to avoid cumulative turntable rotation errors. The paper then benchmarks 13 state-of-the-art methods across single-view photometric stereo (MAE), multi-view photometric stereo (Chamfer distance, F-score), and OLAT/non-OLAT relighting (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS), revealing that uncalibrated methods generally outperform calibrated ones under near-field lighting and that current relighting methods still struggle with indirect lighting and complex shadows.

## Results

On the single-view photometric stereo benchmark (Table 3), Uni-MS-PS achieves the best average MAE of 12.99° across 6 objects, closely followed by SDM-UniPS (13.13°), while the worst performer PS-FCN (uncalibrated) reaches 34.75°. For multi-view photometric stereo, SuperNormal outperforms RNb-NeuS with a mean Chamfer distance of 0.475 mm vs. 0.698 mm and F-score of 0.788 vs. 0.599. In OLAT relighting, GS³ slightly edges out NRHints (PSNR 32.46 vs. 32.11, LPIPS 0.088 vs. 0.120), and in non-OLAT relighting, GaussianShader substantially outperforms TensoIR (PSNR 30.94 vs. 27.00, LPIPS 0.117 vs. 0.180).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among single-view photometric stereo methods, Uni-MS-PS [24] and SDM-UniPS [28] are the top performers (avg. MAE 12.99° and 13.13°), both uncalibrated methods that handle the near-field LED lighting without assuming distant point sources. Calibrated methods (PS-FCN-cal, PS-Transformer, GR-PSN) perform noticeably worse, with PS-Transformer [27] and GR-PSN [32] reaching 25.69° and 22.33° respectively, confirming that directional-lighting assumptions are incompatible with the near-field lightstage setup. PS-FCN in uncalibrated mode [12] is the weakest overall (34.75°), while its calibrated variant (16.07°) is mid-pack. For multi-view PS, SuperNormal [11] clearly beats RNb-NeuS [10] on both Chamfer distance (0.475 vs. 0.698 mm) and F-score (0.788 vs. 0.599), with RNb-NeuS producing discontinuous surfaces on complex objects like BIRD and FOX. In relighting, GS³ [8] marginally outperforms NRHints [68] under OLAT conditions, while GaussianShader [30] substantially surpasses TensoIR [31] under non-OLAT (environment) lighting by ~4 dB in PSNR and ~0.06 in LPIPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

OpenSubstance extends the lightstage-based capture tradition established by OpenIllumination [44] and OpenSVBRDF [50], but scales it dramatically: 24,576 independently controlled LEDs (vs. ~142 in OpenIllumination), 270 views (vs. 72), and 187 objects with ground-truth geometry from a ZEISS ATOS scanner. It departs from prior studio datasets by simultaneously providing dense OLAT sampling (1,620 conditions per object), near-field environment and linear lighting, and high-precision 3D meshes, a combination no single prior dataset offers. The benchmarking protocol draws on the DiLiGenT evaluation framework for photometric stereo and extends it to multi-view PS and relighting tasks using recent neural methods (3DGS-based, NeRF-based, and transformer-based).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With 2.4 million HDR images across 187 objects, 270 views, and 1,637 lighting conditions, OpenSubstance fills a critical gap between small studio benchmarks (DiLiGenT: 10 objects, 96 OLATs) and uncontrolled in-the-wild datasets, providing the scale and ground-truth fidelity needed for reliable training and evaluation of photometric stereo and relighting methods. The benchmarking reveals that uncalibrated PS methods consistently outperform calibrated ones under near-field lighting, a practical insight for practitioners working with real lightstages. Key limitations include the inability to capture geometry for non-rigid or extremely thin objects, a 15 cm size cap, and the absence of automatic focusing; the authors are actively growing the dataset (~10 objects/day) with the stated goal of eventually supporting AIGC and generative tasks.
