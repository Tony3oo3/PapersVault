## Problem

Synthesizing realistic aerial images from ground street-view images is hindered by two compounding challenges: the drastic viewpoint difference creates a large domain gap that causes generated satellite images to be realistic yet content-inconsistent with the actual street scene, and dense urban occlusion (tall buildings, trees) severely limits the visible range of any single street-view, making it impossible to reconstruct the full satellite footprint from one image alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250710/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes equirectangular panoramic street-view images at 1024×512 pixels (true north aligned to the center column) and produces 512×512 aerial/satellite images centered on the same location. No text prompts, camera intrinsics, or depth maps are required; the geometric BEV transformation is a fixed lookup computed purely from the equirectangular projection. In multi-to-one mode, multiple street-view panoramas from neighboring capture points are merged into a single unified BEV before conditioning the diffusion model.

## Method

SkyDiffusion first applies a Curved-BEV transformation that replaces the standard flat ground-plane assumption with an upward-curved surface (z = d_norm⁴ × λ), enabling mapping of upper street-view features like distant rooftops and roads that flat BEV discards; the mapping from 3D points to panorama pixel indices is derived analytically from equirectangular projection geometry, requiring no camera calibration or depth estimation. For dense urban scenes, a Multi-to-One strategy merges BEV projections from multiple street-view cameras into a unified satellite coordinate frame, resolving overlaps by selecting the nearest camera's mapping. The BEV image is then encoded by a small conditional encoder with spatial attention (to suppress geometric distortions) and injected into a Stable Diffusion v1.5 backbone via ControlNet-style zero-convolution layers and replicated encoder/middle-block modules. Training uses the standard denoising diffusion loss with the BEV embedding as the sole conditioning signal (no text), and inference employs 50-step DDIM sampling with classifier-free guidance scale 9.0.

## Results

On CVUSA, SkyDiffusion achieves FID 29.18, SSIM 0.168, PSNR 14.58, and LPIPS 0.635, outperforming the best baseline (ControlNet: FID 32.45, SSIM 0.149) by 10.1% in FID and 12.8% in SSIM. On CVACT, it reaches FID 36.48 and SSIM 0.118, beating the closest competitor Instr-p2p (FID 49.62, SSIM 0.116) by 26.5% in FID. On the urban VIGOR-Chicago dataset, SkyDiffusion attains FID 45.29 and SSIM 0.186, improving over ControlNet (FID 53.27, SSIM 0.170) by 14.98% in FID and 9.41% in SSIM. Across all three datasets, the paper reports an average SSIM increase of 8.67% and FID reduction of 21.50% over state-of-the-art methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SkyDiffusion outperforms all nine baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Among GAN-based methods, X-Seq [26] and CDTE [37] produce the worst results (FID > 120 on CVUSA), while CUT [23] is the strongest GAN baseline but still trails SkyDiffusion by over 2.5× in FID on CVUSA (72.83 vs. 29.18). Among diffusion-based competitors, ControlNet [50] is the closest on CVUSA (FID 32.45) and VIGOR-Chicago (FID 53.27), while Instr-p2p [2] is closest on CVACT (FID 49.62); SkyDiffusion beats these by 10–26% in FID and 7–13% in SSIM. GPG2A [1], which also uses BEV guidance, achieves FID 58.80 on CVUSA—nearly double SkyDiffusion's 29.18—suggesting that the curved BEV transformation and multi-to-one mapping provide substantial gains over prior BEV-based approaches. Aerial Diff [9] and I2I-Turbo [24] generate realistic images but lack content consistency, reflected in their high LPIPS scores (0.855 and 0.685 on CVUSA vs. 0.635 for SkyDiffusion).

## Relation to Prior Work

SkyDiffusion builds directly on the ControlNet conditioning architecture (zero-convolution injection of spatial features into a frozen diffusion backbone) and on prior geometry-guided BEV mapping methods (Shi et al. 2023, Wang et al. 2023) that project street-view panoramas onto a flat ground plane. The key conceptual departure is replacing the flat ground-plane assumption with a curved BEV surface (z ∝ d⁴), which captures upper-scene features (distant roads, building facades) that flat-plane methods discard, and extending the mapping from one-to-one to a multi-to-one strategy that fuses multiple street views into a unified satellite-coordinate BEV. Among prior cross-view synthesis methods, GAN-based approaches (X-Seq, SelGAN, CDTE) and text-guided diffusion (Aerial Diff, GPG2A) are positioned as either producing inconsistent content or requiring auxiliary semantic/textual inputs that SkyDiffusion avoids.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in enabling rapid aerial imagery synthesis for disaster response, low-altitude UAV planning, and historical satellite gap-filling—scenarios where real satellite captures are unavailable or delayed. The multi-to-one BEV strategy is a natural but underexplored solution to the occlusion problem in dense urban scenes, and the G2A-3 dataset opens application-specific benchmarks that prior cross-view retrieval datasets (CVUSA, VIGOR) do not cover. Limitations include the inability to consistently generate building rooftop details (which are invisible from street level), reliance on paired training data, and the fact that the curved-BEV gain over standard BEV, while consistent, is moderate (≈6 FID points on CVACT). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to truly unseen geographic regions without retraining and how it scales to very large urban extents requiring many street views.
