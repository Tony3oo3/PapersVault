## Problem

Existing text-to-layout generation methods are either closed-vocabulary with fixed object categories or rely on proprietary LLMs (e.g., GPT) for open-vocabulary generation, introducing opacity, high cost, and latency. Moreover, LLM-based approaches tend to produce unrealistic object aspect ratios and unnatural bounding box placements, limiting their utility in controllable image generation pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250582/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt describing a natural scene and produces a layout as a set of (description, bounding box) tuples, where each bounding box is a 4-tuple (x0, y0, x1, y1) normalized to [-1, 1] relative to the layout dimensions, making the output aspect-ratio agnostic. The number of objects N is not fixed a priori but is determined by the description set extracted from the prompt. Inference runs in ~5 ms per sample with 15 DDIM steps on a single GPU.

## Method

LayouSyn decomposes text-to-layout generation into two stages: (1) a lightweight open-source LLM (Llama-3.1-8B) extracts an open-vocabulary description set with object counts from the prompt, and (2) a novel aspect-aware diffusion Transformer (LDiT, ~18M params) generates bounding boxes in continuous coordinate space conditioned on the prompt, descriptions, and aspect ratio. The LDiT architecture modifies standard DiT blocks by adding cross-attention between the global text-prompt tokens and local object-description/bounding-box tokens, improving alignment between global and local conditions. A key technical contribution is a noise schedule scaling factor (s=2.0) integrated directly into α̅_t to prevent rapid information destruction in the low-dimensional bounding box space during early denoising steps. The model is pretrained on ~3.5M grounded layouts from GRIT and finetuned on COCO-GR and NSR-1K, using DDPM training with 100 steps and DDIM sampling with 15 steps at inference.

## Results

On the NSR-1K benchmark, LayouSyn (GRIT pretraining) achieves 92.58% spatial reasoning accuracy and 58.94% GLIP spatial detection accuracy (exceeding the ground-truth layout score of 57.20%), along with 95.14% numerical accuracy and 99.23% recall. On the COCO-GR layout quality evaluation, LayouSyn achieves an L-FID of 3.07, outperforming LayoutGPT (GPT-3.5) by 12.5% and LayoutGPT (GPT-4o-mini) by 5.6%. The model requires only ~18M parameters and generates a layout in ~5 ms with 15 DDIM steps on a single GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LayouSyn (GRIT pretraining) outperforms LayoutGPT (GPT-4o-mini) on numerical accuracy (95.14% vs. 77.51%), numerical recall (99.23% vs. 86.84%), and spatial accuracy (92.58% vs. 92.01%), though it trails slightly on spatial GLIP detection (58.94% vs. 60.49%). Against LLM Grounded Diffusion, LayouSyn achieves substantially higher spatial accuracy (92.58% vs. 72.46%) and spatial GLIP (58.94% vs. 27.09%), while LLM Grounded Diffusion edges ahead on numerical precision (84.36% vs. 77.62%). Compared to LLM Blueprint, LayouSyn dominates on numerical accuracy (95.14% vs. 38.36%) and spatial accuracy (92.58% vs. 73.52%), though LLM Blueprint reports higher numerical precision (87.21% vs. 77.62%). Against the finetuned Llama-3.1-8B baseline, LayouSyn improves spatial accuracy by ~6 points (92.58% vs. 86.64%) and spatial GLIP by ~6 points (58.94% vs. 52.93%), while also achieving much higher numerical accuracy (95.14% vs. 70.84%). On the COCO-GR L-FID metric, LayouSyn (3.07) beats LayoutGPT (GPT-3.5) at 3.51 and the finetuned Llama-3.1-8B at 13.95, confirming superior layout quality with a lightweight open-source LLM for description extraction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture and DDPM formulation, repurposing them from image generation to the low-dimensional bounding-box coordinate space for layout generation. It departs from the dominant LLM-based paradigm (LayoutGPT, LLM Blueprint, LLM Grounded Diffusion) that relies on proprietary models like GPT-4o for open-vocabulary layout generation, instead decomposing the task into a lightweight open-source LLM (Llama-3.1-8B) for description extraction and a novel aspect-aware diffusion Transformer for geometric layout synthesis. The noise schedule scaling (Eq. 3) addresses a specific challenge of diffusion in low-dimensional spaces that prior layout diffusion works (Dolfin, LayoutDM) handled differently, and the cross-attention mechanism between global prompt tokens and local description tokens is a targeted architectural modification to the standard DiT block.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By replacing proprietary LLMs with a lightweight open-source model plus a small (~18M parameter) diffusion Transformer, LayouSyn makes open-vocabulary scene layout generation more accessible, transparent, and cost-effective for controllable image generation pipelines. The LLM-initialization-plus-DDIM-inversion trick (Sec. 5.1) offers a practical recipe for combining coarse LLM predictions with diffusion refinement in just 15 steps, which could generalize to other structured generation tasks. Key limitations remain: the method does not model depth or occlusion (acknowledged as future work), spatial reasoning accuracy plateaus around 92–93%, and the pipeline still depends on GLIGEN for downstream image synthesis, coupling its end-to-end quality to that model's capabilities.
