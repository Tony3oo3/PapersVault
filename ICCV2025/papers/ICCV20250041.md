## Problem

Existing PAN-sharpening methods assume perfect pixel-wise alignment between PAN and MS images and rely on per-pixel reconstruction losses, which produce spectral distortion, double edges, and blurring when real-world cross-modality misalignment (from sensor placement, acquisition timing, and resolution disparity) is present. Prior alignment strategies use fixed-scale convolutions or self-similarity aggregation that cannot dynamically adapt to varying misalignment patterns across datasets.

## Input / Output

The network takes a high-resolution PAN image (4H×4W×1) and a low-resolution MS image (H×W×C_ms, where C_ms=4 for QB/GF2 and 8 for WV3), which is first upsampled by a factor of 4 to match PAN resolution. The output is a residual HRMS prediction (4H×4W×C_ms) added to the upsampled LRMS input, following a residual learning formulation. At inference, the modality selection is fixed to MS mode, so the network always produces the final HRMS image.

## Method

PAN-Crafter is a U-Net-based encoder-decoder with two key mechanisms. First, Modality-Adaptive Reconstruction (MARs) enables a single network to jointly reconstruct HRMS (MS mode) and a multi-channel PAN replica (PAN mode) via learnable channel-wise modulation parameters, using PAN's high-frequency details as auxiliary self-supervision to sharpen spatial fidelity. Second, Cross-Modality Alignment-Aware Attention (CM3A) performs bidirectional local attention within a k×k window (k=3): in MS mode, queries built from LRMS features attend to both MS key-value pairs (self-attention) and PAN key-value pairs (alignment-aware attention), while PAN mode reverses this direction. This replaces fixed positional embeddings with modality-aware feature priors derived directly from the input images, enabling adaptive compensation for local geometric shifts without predefined spatial encodings. Training duplicates each sample across both modes with an L1 loss weighted by λ=1.0, and the architecture uses C=128 feature channels with ResBlocks at high-resolution stages and cascaded ResBlock+AttnBlock at lower stages.

## Results

On the WV3 dataset, PAN-Crafter achieves the best scores across all metrics: HQNR 0.958, Ds 0.027, Dλ 0.016, ERGAS 2.040, SCC 0.988, SAM 2.787, Q8 0.922, PSNR 37.956 dB, and SSIM 0.976. It runs 50.11× faster than CANConv (0.009 s vs. 0.451 s) and uses only 0.63× the memory of DCPNet (1.711 GB vs. 7.213 GB). On the unseen WV2 satellite dataset (zero-shot, trained only on WV3), PAN-Crafter again leads all metrics with HQNR 0.942, ERGAS 4.169, SCC 0.924, SAM 5.078, and PSNR 29.276 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAN-Crafter outperforms all nine baselines on every reported metric across WV3, GF2, QB, and the unseen WV2 dataset. Against the strongest recent CNN baseline CANConv (CVPR 2024), it improves HQNR from 0.951 to 0.958 and SAM from 2.927 to 2.787 on WV3 while running 50.11× faster (0.009 s vs. 0.451 s). Compared to the diffusion-based PanDiff (TGRS 2023), PAN-Crafter achieves higher HQNR (0.958 vs. 0.952) and lower SAM (2.787 vs. 3.058) with 328× faster inference; against TMDiff (TGRS 2024) the speedup is 1111×. Versus DCPNet (TGRS 2024), which uses a dual-task strategy, PAN-Crafter surpasses it on all quality metrics (e.g., PSNR 37.956 vs. 37.009) while using only 0.63× the GPU memory (1.711 GB vs. 7.213 GB). On the zero-shot WV2 test, PAN-Crafter leads by a clear margin over the next-best method PanDiff (HQNR 0.942 vs. 0.932; PSNR 29.276 vs. 28.964 dB), confirming that its modality-consistent alignment generalizes without dataset-specific tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAN-Crafter extends the line of deep PAN-sharpening methods that attempt to handle cross-modality misalignment (SIPSA, LAGConv, CANConv) by replacing their fixed-scale or self-similarity-based alignment with a bidirectional, modality-aware attention mechanism (CM3A) that operates within local windows, borrowing the windowed-attention design from Slide-Transformer. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) Modality-Adaptive Reconstruction (MARs) introduces a dual-task formulation where the same network jointly reconstructs HRMS and a multi-channel PAN replica, using PAN sharpness as auxiliary self-supervision—a strategy not previously applied in PAN-sharpening; and (2) CM3A replaces fixed positional embeddings with modality-derived feature priors (concatenating downsampled PAN or MS images into Q/K/V), enabling the attention to adapt to local geometric shifts without explicit displacement estimation. The overall U-Net backbone with residual blocks follows standard PAN-sharpening architectures (PanNet, S2DBPN, DCPNet), so the contribution is primarily in the alignment and training strategy rather than the network topology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving SOTA quality while being 50× faster than CANConv and 328× faster than PanDiff with lower memory, making it a viable alternative to diffusion-based methods for real-time or resource-constrained remote sensing pipelines. The zero-shot generalization to the unseen WV2 satellite (trained only on WV3) suggests the modality-consistent alignment strategy captures sensor-agnostic geometric corrections rather than dataset-specific patterns. An acknowledged limitation is the slightly lower Dλ on WV3, reflecting a deliberate trade-off where CM3A in MS mode prioritizes spectral fidelity to the LRMS input over PAN-HRMS correlation. Open questions include whether the local-window approach (k=3) suffices for larger parallax-induced shifts in very high-resolution or oblique imagery, and whether the MARs auxiliary task generalizes to hyperspectral settings with many more bands.
