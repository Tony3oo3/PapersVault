## Problem

Existing diffusion-based PBR material decomposition methods require 30–50 denoising steps and separate models per material property, making them too slow for interactive use and preventing end-to-end training with task-specific losses. Because these methods supervise predicted noise rather than final material outputs, they cannot incorporate perceptual or re-render losses that enforce physical consistency across jointly estimated albedo, metallic, and roughness maps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250768/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For 2D decomposition, the model takes a single RGB image of an object under unknown illumination and simultaneously outputs albedo, metallic, and roughness maps in one forward pass (millisecond-scale on an A100). For 3D decomposition, the input is a textured mesh with only an RGB texture; it is rendered from six orthogonal viewpoints, processed by the multi-view model, backprojected to UV space, and refined to produce complete PBR material UV maps in approximately 3 seconds total. The RM channel is packed as (1, roughness, metallic) in image space and (0, roughness, metallic) in UV space to match the rendering pipeline's expectations.

## Method

SuperMat fine-tunes Stable Diffusion's UNet with two structural expert branches (one for albedo, one for RM) that share all preceding layers, adding only 2.23% extra parameters while enabling simultaneous multi-material prediction. The critical enabler is correcting the DDIM scheduler to use the trailing timestep (t=T) instead of the default leading setting (t=1), which aligns the timestep signal with the actual noise level and makes true single-step inference viable. This single-step formulation allows end-to-end training where the fully denoised material maps are directly supervised with a VGG-based perceptual loss and a re-render loss that renders predicted materials under a novel environment map and compares the result to ground-truth relighting. The multi-view extension (SuperMatMV) adds a 3D self-attention layer conditioned on camera extrinsics (following MVDream) for cross-view consistency. For 3D objects, a UV refinement one-step diffusion model inpaints missing regions and harmonizes overlapping backprojected patches, completing the full pipeline in three inference steps.

## Results

SuperMat achieves state-of-the-art PBR material decomposition with albedo PSNR of 27.68, metallic PSNR of 25.48, roughness PSNR of 24.25, and relighting PSNR of 27.66, outperforming the best prior method (StableMaterialMV) by 3.12 dB on albedo and 8.83 dB on metallic. Inference time is 0.07 s per material map on an A100 GPU, roughly 7.6× faster than StableMaterial (0.53 s) and ~99× faster than IntrinsicAnything (6.93 s). The multi-view variant SuperMatMV maintains comparable quality (albedo PSNR 27.56) at 0.09 s, and the full 3D pipeline completes PBR material estimation for a textured model in approximately 3 seconds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SuperMat achieves the best score on every reported metric (albedo, metallic, roughness, and relighting PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS) against all six baselines. The largest quality gap is on metallic PSNR, where SuperMat (25.48) surpasses the strongest baseline StableMaterial (20.29) by 5.19 dB, and on relighting PSNR where it leads StableMaterialMV (23.14) by 4.52 dB. Among diffusion-based methods, StableMaterialMV is the closest competitor on albedo (24.56 vs. 27.68 PSNR) but still trails by 3.12 dB; IntrinsicAnything reports only albedo and is the slowest at 6.93 s per prediction. On speed, SuperMat (0.07 s) is 7.6× faster than StableMaterial (0.53 s), 21× faster than IID (1.45 s), and ~99× faster than IntrinsicAnything (6.93 s), while only IIR (0.04 s) and Derender3D (0.16 s) are faster but at substantially lower quality (albedo PSNR 21.94 and 20.97 respectively). The multi-view variant SuperMatMV (0.09 s) further improves metallic and roughness over the single-view model while maintaining near-identical albedo quality, confirming cross-view consistency gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

SuperMat builds directly on Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion framework and the material decomposition paradigm established by MaterialFusion/StableMaterial, which reframed PBR estimation as conditional image generation. The key departure is twofold: architecturally, it replaces separate per-material diffusion models with structural expert branches (adding only 2.23% of UNet parameters) to predict albedo and RM simultaneously; and procedurally, it corrects the DDIM scheduler's leading-to-trailing timestep setting to enable true single-step inference, which in turn unlocks end-to-end training with perceptual and re-render losses—something prior multi-step diffusion methods could not support due to prohibitive backpropagation cost. For the 3D extension, it departs from optimization-based approaches (SDS, differentiable rendering) in favor of a deterministic backprojection-plus-UV-refinement pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reduction from 30–50 denoising steps to a single inference step (milliseconds vs. seconds) makes high-quality PBR decomposition viable in interactive and production pipelines, and the ~3-second 3D material estimation pipeline is practical for game and film asset creation. The end-to-end training paradigm—enabled by the scheduler fix—is the more transferable insight, as it allows task-specific losses (perceptual, re-render) to directly supervise the final output rather than intermediate noise predictions, a pattern applicable to other diffusion-based estimation tasks. Limitations include reliance on six orthogonal views for 3D coverage, the need for paired ground-truth material maps during training, and the separate UV refinement model adding pipeline complexity. Open questions include whether single-step quality degrades on more complex multi-material scenes or real-world captures with extreme lighting, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the three PBR channels to full BRDF parameters.
