## Problem

Aligning a retrieved (inexact) CAD model to an object in a single image requires estimating a full 9-DoF pose, but existing methods either depend on extensive scene-level pose annotations that limit them to a narrow set of categories, or rely on foundation features that are sensitive to texture variation and cannot distinguish symmetrical object parts. This paper tackles the gap of zero-shot, category-agnostic 9-DoF inexact CAD alignment without any pose annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251550/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image with a bounding box around the target object, a closely matching CAD model (obtained via ROCA's retrieval system), and known camera intrinsics. It outputs a 9-DoF pose in camera coordinates, parameterized as a 6D rigid transformation in SE(3) plus an anisotropic scaling vector in R³. The CAD model may be textureless or differ in shape from the object, and the method additionally consumes a SAM segmentation mask and a DepthAnything metric depth map internally for correspondence lifting and scale disambiguation.

## Method

The pipeline is coarse-to-fine. For coarse alignment, a lightweight 2-layer MLP adapter transforms DINOv2 penultimate-layer features into a geometry-aware space, trained self-supervised on ShapeNet renderings with a combined NOC-prediction loss and a geometry-consistent triplet loss that enforces multi-view consistency while separating symmetrical parts. The fused feature (concatenation of original DINOv2 and adapted features) is used for nearest-neighbor matching between the 2D image feature map and a 3D voxel grid built from multi-view CAD renderings, yielding 2D-3D correspondences solved via RANSAC with metric depth. For fine alignment, the pose is refined through dense differentiable rendering in NOC space (rather than RGB or feature space), minimizing a combined loss of NOC alignment, silhouette matching via SoftRasterizer, and metric depth consistency, which provides texture invariance and stable gradients for per-pixel optimization.

## Results

On ScanNet25k (9-DoF, NMS alignment accuracy), the method achieves 23.1% mean category-wise and 30.1% mean instance-wise accuracy, surpassing the supervised ROCA baseline (+2.7% Cat., +1.6% Inst.) and the weakly supervised DiffCAD by +4.3% (vs. DiffCAD-Err) on DiffCAD's split. On the newly introduced SUN2CAD benchmark with 20 unseen categories, the method reaches 24.5% Cat. / 17.6% Inst. accuracy, outperforming the supervised SOTA SPARC by +12.7% and DINOv2-based matching by +12.9%. In the inexact 6-DoF setting on ScanNet25k, the method achieves 32.2% Cat. / 38.0% Inst., beating the adapted FoundationPose by +7.3% Cat. / +6.9% Inst.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ROCA (supervised, CVPR 2022), the method wins on both category-wise (+2.7%) and instance-wise (+1.6%) NMS accuracy on ScanNet25k, making it the only weakly supervised approach to surpass this supervised baseline. Against SPARC (supervised, BMVC 2022), the method loses on ScanNet25k seen categories (−4.2% Cat., −3.8% Inst.) but decisively wins on the unseen SUN2CAD benchmark by +12.7% Cat., demonstrating superior zero-shot generalization. Against DiffCAD (weakly supervised, ACM TOG 2024), the method outperforms both the error-based selection (+4.3% Cat.) and mean-hypothesis (+9.2% Cat.) metrics on DiffCAD's own test split. Against FoundationPose (weakly supervised, CVPR 2024), the method wins in the inexact 6-DoF setting by +7.3% Cat. / +6.9% Inst. on ScanNet25k and by +14.2% Cat. / +10.3% Inst. on SUN2CAD, while also outperforming the adapted 9-DoF version in 5 of 9 classes. Against raw DINOv2 features used for zero-shot matching, the method achieves +12.9% Cat. / +10.3% Inst. on SUN2CAD, validating the benefit of the geometry-aware feature adapter.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DINOv2 foundation features and the NOC (Normalized Object Coordinate) framework, but departs from prior CAD alignment work in two key ways. Unlike supervised methods (ROCA, SPARC) that require pose-annotated training data, or weakly supervised DiffCAD that needs synthetic scene renderings with 9-DoF annotations, this work trains only a lightweight 2-layer MLP adapter via self-supervised triplet and NOC-prediction losses on unposed CAD renderings. It also diverges from recent 6-DoF foundation-feature methods (FoundationPose, Freeze) by operating in NOC space for dense alignment rather than RGB or feature space, making it texture-invariant and compatible with inexact, textureless CAD models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to perform 9-DoF CAD alignment without any scene-level pose annotations significantly lowers the barrier to deploying alignment in new categories, which is critical for scalable applications like AR/VR content creation. The introduction of SUN2CAD (20 unseen categories) provides a much-needed benchmark for evaluating zero-shot generalization beyond the 9 categories in ScanNet25k. However, the method still trails the supervised SOTA (SPARC) by 3.8% on seen categories and struggles with heavily occluded or cropped objects, suggesting that the geometry-aware feature space may not fully capture complex scene context. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large model databases and whether the self-supervised adapter can be further simplified or made category-agnostic in its training data.
