## Problem

Existing financial reasoning benchmarks are predominantly text-only, limited in domain coverage, or restricted to multiple-choice formats that diverge from authentic financial problem-solving. There is no comprehensive multimodal benchmark that simultaneously challenges MLLMs on visual perception of complex financial visuals, domain-specific knowledge reasoning across diverse subdomains, and multi-step precise numerical computation in a bilingual setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251428/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each question pairs a bilingual text prompt (English or Chinese) with one or more financial images (tables, bar charts, line charts, ownership structure charts) rendered purely as pixel images rather than structured text, preventing text-only shortcuts. For 2,150 questions, two contextually adjacent distractor images from the same source report are mixed with the ground image to simulate real-world visual filtering. The expected output is a precise numerical answer evaluated with a strict 0.2% error tolerance (enforcing correct units, percentages, and decimal places), or executable Python code in the PoT setting whose execution result is checked against ground truth.

## Method

FinMMR is constructed by (1) re-annotating and transforming 2,150 questions from existing text-based financial benchmarks (MMMU, FinanceMath, CodeTAT-QA, CodeFinQA, DocMath-Eval) into multimodal form by rendering all tabular data as images, and (2) building a novel CRRQA subset of 2,150 questions from 90 authorized Chinese financial research reports using Qwen-VL-Max for question generation. Difficulty is stratified via a heuristic formula combining operator count, code lines, and parenthesis pairs in the Python solution. Evaluation employs three prompting strategies (IO, CoT, PoT) across 15 SOTA MLLMs, with GPT-4o-mini for answer extraction and strict numerical matching. Three mitigation pipelines are proposed: a two-stage visual filtering-then-reasoning pipeline to handle distractor images, a knowledge augmentation system using a 3,133-function financial Python library with MLLM-instructed retrieval queries and MLLM-as-judge for top-3 selection, and a visual-parser-plus-reasoner model collaboration where GPT-4o converts tables to structured text for downstream LRM reasoning.

## Results

On the FinMMR Hard subset, the best-performing model (Claude 3.7 Sonnet with 64K extended thinking) achieves only 53.00% accuracy (IO) and 51.40% (PoT), while OpenAI o1 reaches 48.40% (CoT) and GPT-4o reaches 47.80% (PoT). Across all 15 evaluated MLLMs, the mean accuracy drops from 73.33% (Easy) to 54.05% (Medium) to 38.14% (Hard) under CoT prompting. PoT prompting yields a modest 1.44 pp improvement over CoT on the Hard subset (37.64% vs. 36.20% mean), while extended thinking provides a 2.20 pp gain for Claude 3.7 Sonnet at the cost of ~12× more tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary MLLMs, Claude 3.7 Sonnet with 64K extended thinking leads on the Hard subset (53.00% IO, 51.40% PoT), outperforming OpenAI o1 (48.40% CoT) by ~4.6 pp and GPT-4o (47.80% PoT) by ~3.6 pp. Llama 4 Maverick (17B, open-source) surprisingly matches GPT-4o on Hard CoT (48.70% vs. 45.40%), while Qwen2.5-VL-72B (72B) trails at 43.30% CoT / 46.20% PoT. Smaller open-source models (Gemma 3 27B, Mistral Small 3.1, Pixtral Large) drop below 25% on Hard, highlighting a sharp capability gap. In the model-collaboration experiment (Fig. 4), GPT-4o as Visual Parser + DeepSeek-R1 as Reasoner achieves 86.72% on 1,160 tabular QA instances, surpassing Claude 3.7 Sonnet alone (83.53%) and GPT-4o alone (80.60%). Knowledge augmentation (RAG with a 3,133-function financial library) adds 2.76–4.31 pp across all four tested MLLMs, with Gemini 2.0 Flash Thinking gaining the most (+4.31 pp, from 78.71% to 83.02%), nearly closing the gap to Claude 3.7 Sonnet without augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of multimodal reasoning benchmarks and financial domain evaluation. While text-based financial numerical reasoning benchmarks (FinQA, CodeTAT-QA, CodeFinQA, FinanceMath, DocMath-Eval) and general multimodal benchmarks (MMMU, MathVista, FAMMA) exist, none simultaneously provide image-centric financial reasoning with exact numerical answers, bilingual coverage, and 14-subdomain breadth. FinMMR repurposes existing text-based financial QA by rendering tables as images and removing textual table data, and introduces the novel CRRQA dataset constructed from Chinese financial research reports with distractor images. The evaluation methodology draws on CoT and PoT prompting, and the proposed visual filtering pipeline and knowledge augmentation via a 3,133-function financial library are practical extensions of RAG and multi-stage reasoning ideas applied to this specific domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FinMMR establishes a rigorous, challenging evaluation standard (best model: 53% on Hard) that exposes concrete failure modes—visual perception, knowledge reasoning, and numerical computation—of current MLLMs in a high-stakes domain. The finding that distractor images cause >10% accuracy degradation and that a simple two-stage filtering-reasoning pipeline recovers most of the gap (64.73% → 71.56%) provides actionable guidance for deploying MLLMs in real-world document-heavy settings. Open questions include whether PoT prompting can yield gains on open-source reasoning-enhanced models, how to prevent RL-based reasoning training from degrading foundational capabilities (as seen with QVQ-72B), and whether model collaboration (MLLM parser + LRM reasoner) can be made cost-effective at scale.
