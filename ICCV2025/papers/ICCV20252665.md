## Problem

Existing text-and-image-to-image (TI2I) methods are typically constrained to a single aspect of visual guidance (e.g., style, object, or background) and degrade in quality when handling complex multi-reference instructions. Additionally, no comprehensive benchmark exists for evaluating unified TI2I tasks where a single instruction incorporates multiple reference aspects simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252665/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target text prompt, R reference images (each with its own reference prompt), and produces a single generated or edited image. It operates on MM-DiT-based backbones (SD3.5-large with 4096 vision tokens and 333 text tokens per layer) without any fine-tuning. The reference images are encoded into vision tokens and their prompts into textual tokens, which are then processed through the existing diffusion pipeline at each timestep.

## Method

The key insight is that in MM-DiT architectures, textual tokens implicitly aggregate visual context from vision tokens through multimodal attention, effectively becoming 'contextual tokens' that carry reference information. TF-TI2I exploits this via Contextual Token Sharing (CTS), which concatenates these enriched textual tokens from all references into the generation pipeline instead of full vision tokens, reducing computational overhead from (2R−1)× to roughly half. Reference Contextual Masking (RCM) then restricts each reference's contribution by binarizing attention scores between the instruction and reference vision tokens (using Otsu thresholding) to prevent mutual interference. Finally, a Winner-Takes-All (WTA) module assigns each output vision token exclusively to its most relevant reference based on attention saliency, mitigating distribution shifts that arise from the training-free modification.

## Results

On the proposed FG-TI2I Bench, TF-TI2I achieves the best CLIP-Text (CP) scores across all five single-reference sub-tasks (29.3–30.7) and the best Image Reward (IR) scores (0.67–1.30), outperforming Emu2, OmniGen, StyleAlign, and MasaCtrl. On DreamBench (customization), TF-TI2I attains the highest CP of 33.1 (vs. 31.5 for OmniGen) but trails OmniGen on CLIP-Image (79.1 vs. 80.1). On Wild-TI2I (editing), TF-TI2I achieves the best CP of 30.4 but a lower CLIP-Directional Score (0.115) compared to TIS (0.210) and MasaCtrl (0.209), reflecting a trade-off between prompt alignment and reference fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TF-TI2I consistently outperforms Emu2 [50] on every FG-TI2I sub-task metric (CP, DI, IR), with CP gains of 1–3 points and IR gains of 0.3–1.0, confirming that Emu2's object-centric training design limits its generalization to texture, action, and background references. Against the single-reference methods OmniGen [57], StyleAlign [22], and MasaCtrl [3], TF-TI2I wins on CP and IR across all sub-tasks but trails on DINO Similarity (DI) by 3–14 points, as the training-free approach encodes references at a semantic level rather than copying low-level pixel details. On DreamBench, TF-TI2I surpasses OmniGen on CP (33.1 vs. 31.5) and DreamBooth (30.5) but falls slightly short on CP-I (79.1 vs. 80.1 for OmniGen). On Wild-TI2I, TF-TI2I leads in CP (30.4 vs. 29.3 MasaCtrl, 29.0 TIS) but lags significantly on CDS (0.115 vs. 0.210 TIS, 0.209 MasaCtrl), indicating that the method prioritizes prompt compliance over preserving the original image's directional features during editing. Overall, TF-TI2I achieves SOTA on 12 of 18 FG-TI2I metrics, with the remaining 6 losses concentrated in DINO-based reference-alignment scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MM-DiT architecture (SD3, FLUX) and the broader line of training-free image conditioning methods (share-attention approaches like MasaCtrl and StyleAlign, single-aspect methods like TF-ICON and ZSTAR). It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it discovers that textual tokens in MM-DiT implicitly absorb visual context through multimodal attention, enabling a Contextual Token Sharing mechanism that replaces the expensive vision-token concatenation of share-attention methods with a much cheaper text-token concatenation; and (2) it introduces RCM and WTA modules specifically designed to handle multi-reference interference, going beyond the single-reference or single-aspect scope of most prior training-free methods. Unlike from-scratch multi-modal models (Emu2, Kosmos-G) that are constrained by limited TI2I training data, TF-TI2I repurposes an existing T2I backbone without any fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, multi-reference TI2I capability is practically important because it lets users leverage state-of-the-art T2I models (SD3.5, FLUX) for complex compositional generation—combining object, texture, action, and background references in a single instruction—without any additional training cost. The FG-TI2I Bench fills a genuine evaluation gap for unified multi-aspect TI2I, which prior benchmarks (DreamBench, EditBench) did not cover. However, the authors acknowledge that mutual interference between references persists due to the limited precision of RCM (which relies on Otsu binarization of attention scores rather than explicit semantic segmentation), and the method trades off low-level reference fidelity (lower DINO similarity) for instruction compliance. Open questions include whether the implicit-context learning property generalizes beyond MM-DiT architectures and whether more precise masking strategies can reduce cross-reference contamination.
