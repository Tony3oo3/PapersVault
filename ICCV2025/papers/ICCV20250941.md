## Problem

Physics-based Shape-from-Template methods for deforming fabric reconstruction suffer from depth ambiguity: the projection step during rendering loses information along the viewing direction, causing the optimizer to crumple the cloth in-plane rather than displace it in depth. Existing approaches (φ-SfT, PG-SfT) produce visually plausible but geometrically inaccurate reconstructions, and their erroneous geometry precludes reliable SVBRDF and lighting estimation from a single monocular video.

## Input / Output

The method requires a monocular RGB video, a template mesh providing the ground-truth geometry of the first frame, and per-frame segmentation masks (obtainable via Segment Anything). It outputs per-frame 3D vertex positions of the deforming fabric, a set of SVBRDF textures (diffuse, roughness, metallic) at 472×472 resolution generated by a trainable decoder that blends 16 overlapping 128×128 patches, and an environment map at 512×256. The projective texture mapped from the first frame is kept fixed during geometry optimization, and no normal map is reconstructed due to ambiguities from a single viewpoint.

## Method

The pipeline has three optimization phases: (1) projective texture mapping via differentiable rasterization (nvdiffrast), (2) geometry reconstruction by optimizing mass-spring cloth parameters (stretching Y, bending B, shearing S) and per-vertex external forces using backward Euler integration, and (3) SVBRDF and environment-map estimation via differentiable ray tracing (nvdiffrecmc). The key contribution is two regularization terms added to the SfT loss: an energy regularization that penalizes the internal cloth deformation energy (with stop-gradient on stiffness parameters to prevent them from being driven to zero) to suppress unrealistic crumpling, and a force regularization that removes the component of external forces perpendicular to the viewing direction, thereby encouraging displacement along the depth axis where image gradients carry no information. Geometry optimization starts with three frames and adds one frame every five epochs, while appearance estimation uses a staged schedule that first constrains the environment map to grayscale before gradually enabling color over 2000 total epochs.

## Results

On the φ-SfT dataset (9 real-world cloth scenes), the proposed method achieves a mean L₂ Chamfer distance of 3.33 × 10⁻⁴ m², reducing the error by a factor of 2.64× compared to φ-SfT (8.80) and 2.92× compared to PG-SfT (9.71). Geometry reconstruction takes 29.9 min on average (Nvidia A100), which is 36× faster than φ-SfT (1105 min) but ~15× slower than PG-SfT (2.0 min). The appearance estimation phase adds 31.9 min on average to recover SVBRDF textures (diffuse, roughness, metallic) and an environment map from the same monocular video.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: φ-SfT (physics-based SfT with a complex continuum-mechanics cloth model) and PG-SfT (neural surrogate cloth simulation). On L₂ Chamfer distance, the proposed method outperforms φ-SfT by 2.64× (3.33 vs. 8.80) and PG-SfT by 2.92× (3.33 vs. 9.71) on average, with per-scene wins in 7 out of 9 scenes against both baselines. In terms of runtime, the proposed method is 36× faster than φ-SfT (29.9 min vs. 1105 min) but approximately 15× slower than PG-SfT (29.9 min vs. 2.0 min), trading speed for substantially better geometric accuracy. The ablation study confirms that the two novel regularization terms (energy and force) are critical: removing the energy regularization alone degrades the mean Chamfer distance from 3.33 to 23.59, while removing the force regularization increases it to 5.96, with the largest regressions in scenes where wind blows along the viewing direction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Shape-from-Template (SfT) line of work for cloth reconstruction, building directly on φ-SfT's physics-based deformation framework but replacing its complex continuum-mechanics model with a simpler mass-spring system, and on PG-SfT's neural surrogate approach by reverting to explicit differentiable physics. The key methodological departure is the introduction of two regularization terms (energy and force) specifically designed to counteract the depth ambiguity inherent in monocular projection, which prior SfT methods did not explicitly address. For the appearance estimation phase, the paper repurposes the trainable texture decoder from ROSA [30] and the differentiable ray tracer nvdiffrecmc [24], combining them with the reconstructed geometry in a pipeline that no prior monocular SfT method has attempted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple mass-spring model with targeted regularizations can outperform both heavier physics simulations and neural surrogates for cloth SfT, reducing Chamfer distance by 2.64× while running 36× faster than φ-SfT. More broadly, it shows that sufficiently accurate dynamic geometry enables SVBRDF and environment-map estimation from a single monocular video of a deforming object—a combination previously thought to require multi-view or RGBD data. Limitations include the reliance on projective texture mapping (which fails for specular materials like silk), residual geometric discrepancies in some scenes, and the fact that evaluation is confined to a single 9-scene dataset, leaving open whether the regularization weights generalize across cloth types and motion regimes.
