## Problem

Existing 3D head avatar reconstruction methods focus exclusively on facial geometry and texture, ignoring the dynamic interplay between hands and face that conveys cognitive states like pondering. No prior work simultaneously produces a detailed, textured head avatar with physically plausible non-rigid deformations induced by hand contacts from a single monocular video, as hand-induced facial deformations lack established priors and are highly under-constrained from RGB alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252636/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB video (approximately 1,000 frames per sequence, captured on iPhone 15 Pro) as its sole visual input, supplemented by off-the-shelf depth estimates (Depth Anything V2) and 2D facial/hand keypoints for tracking. It outputs a per-scene neural implicit head avatar with photorealistic texture, a MANO hand mesh, and a contact-induced non-rigid deformation field represented as a compact set of PCA parameters scaling learned blendshapes. The method is optimization-based (per-scene), not a feed-forward network, and requires no LiDAR depth at reconstruction time.

## Method

The framework operates in two stages. In preprocessing, separately tracked FLAME and MANO meshes are jointly aligned in a unified coordinate system using a depth order loss (enforcing correct relative depth between face and hand pixels) and a contact regularization term (pulling fingertip vertices toward nearby facial vertices). In reconstruction, a neural implicit avatar following the IDR paradigm (canonical geometry, rendering, and deformation MLPs) is optimized per scene, with a dedicated non-rigid deformation network that predicts contact-related blendshapes and per-frame contact parameters from a 30-dim latent code. The key insight is constraining the deformation space via a PCA basis extracted from the DECAF hand-face interaction dataset, reducing the problem to estimating a compact set of PCA coefficients rather than a full spatial offset field. A physics-inspired contact loss penalizes face occupancy at hand surface points to prevent interpenetration, while a regularization term suppresses deformation in non-contact regions, together ensuring physically plausible results.

## Results

On the synthetic dataset, the proposed method achieves a Normal Consistency of 75.06, Chamfer Distance of 2.74, F5 of 10.22, and F10 of 33.20, substantially outperforming both baselines across all four metrics. The Chamfer Distance is reduced by approximately 6.6× compared to NDR (19.14) and 6.7× compared to Morpheus (18.245), while F5 and F10 scores are roughly 19–33× higher than the best baseline. On real iPhone-captured videos, the method operates on RGB input alone (no LiDAR depth), whereas both baselines require RGB-D sensor data, yet still produces more refined geometric details and physically plausible contact deformations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two state-of-the-art dynamic surface reconstruction methods: NDR [5] (Cai et al., NeurIPS 2022) and Morpheus [24] (Wang et al., CVPR 2024). The proposed method decisively outperforms both on every metric in the synthetic evaluation: NC improves from 52.35/53.53 to 75.06, CD drops from 19.14/18.245 to 2.74, and F5/F10 jump from 0.54/1.93 (NDR) and 0.31/1.14 (Morpheus) to 10.22/33.20. Qualitatively, NDR fails to produce valid hand shapes or facial expressions, while Morpheus shows improved facial reconstruction but still struggles with large hand motion variations and articulated hand shapes. A key practical advantage is that the proposed method requires only monocular RGB input on real videos, whereas both NDR and Morpheus depend on LiDAR-derived depth maps from an iPhone 15 Pro. The paper also notes that both baselines are particularly under-constrained when processing dynamic sequences with significant head rotations and diverse hand articulations, whereas the proposed method's contact-aware deformation and PCA prior maintain coherent hand-face spatial relationships.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the IDR-style neural implicit avatar framework (canonical-space occupancy + deformation network + ray marching) to jointly model hand-face contact deformations, a component absent from prior head avatar methods (FLARE, PointAvatar, BakedAvatar) that treat the face in isolation. For the interaction modeling, it departs from single-image hand-face deformation predictors (DECAF, DICE, NePHIM) by operating on video sequences and producing full photorealistic avatars rather than coarse mesh displacements. The key methodological departure is the use of a PCA basis extracted from the DECAF interaction dataset to regularize an otherwise under-constrained non-rigid deformation field, combined with a physics-inspired contact loss that has no direct analogue in prior avatar reconstruction work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Hand-face interactions occur roughly 50 times per hour and convey cognitive states (pondering, uncertainty) critical for telepresence and VR, yet no prior method simultaneously delivers detailed person-specific geometry, photorealistic texture, and physically plausible contact deformations from a single monocular video. The introduced synthetic dataset with PBD-simulated deformations provides a much-needed ground-truth benchmark for evaluating contact-region reconstruction. Key open questions remain: the contact loss cannot model skin pulling or friction, material properties (skin, muscle, fat) are unmodeled, and per-scene optimization is too slow for real-time use—addressing these would be necessary for production avatar pipelines.
