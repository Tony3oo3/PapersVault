## Problem

Video Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) are computationally expensive at inference, especially for high-resolution or long-duration clips, and prior acceleration methods (step distillation, fixed caching schedules) either require retraining or apply a uniform compute budget regardless of video content. The paper identifies that not all videos are equally complex—some achieve reasonable quality with fewer denoising steps than others—yet no existing method adapts its caching schedule per-video to exploit this variance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251976/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

AdaCache is a plug-and-play inference-time module applied on top of a pretrained video DiT (Open-Sora, Open-Sora-Plan, or Latte). It takes a text prompt and optionally a reference image (for text+image-to-video) and produces a video clip at the baseline's native resolution and duration (e.g., 480p-2s, 512×512-2.7s, or 720p-2s). The method operates entirely in latent space during the denoising loop, requiring no architectural modification or retraining of the underlying DiT.

## Method

AdaCache caches residual computations (spatial-temporal attention, cross-attention, and MLP outputs) within DiT transformer blocks and reuses them across subsequent denoising steps. At each recompute point, it computes an L1 distance metric between the current residual and the previously cached one, then maps this distance through a codebook of basis cache-rates to determine how many future steps will reuse the cached value—higher change yields more frequent recomputation. A Motion Regularization (MoReg) component estimates a noisy motion score from latent frame differences and a motion gradient across diffusion steps, scaling the distance metric so that high-motion content automatically receives more compute. A single common caching schedule is enforced across all layers to maintain denoising stability, and the entire pipeline is training-free and content-adaptive per video.

## Results

On the VBench benchmark, AdaCache-fast achieves up to 3.70× speedup (Open-Sora-Plan, 512×512-2.7s at 150-steps) with VBench 75.83% vs. 80.39% baseline, while AdaCache-slow reaches 2.20× speedup with VBench 80.50% (slightly above baseline). On Open-Sora (480p-2s, 30-steps), AdaCache-fast delivers 2.24× speedup with VBench 79.39% (vs. 79.22% baseline), and AdaCache-slow achieves 1.46× with VBench 79.66%. In the higher-quality 720p-2s setting (100-steps), AdaCache achieves a 4.7× speedup (latency 89.53s vs. 419.60s) with VBench 83.40% vs. 84.16% baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaCache consistently outperforms all three training-free baselines (Δ-DiT, T-GATE, PAB) on speedup while maintaining or improving generation quality. Against PAB (the strongest prior method), AdaCache-fast achieves 2.24× vs. 1.34× speedup on Open-Sora with VBench 79.39% vs. 76.95%, and 3.70× vs. 1.45× on Open-Sora-Plan. Against T-GATE, AdaCache-fast delivers roughly 2× the speedup (e.g., 2.24× vs. 1.10× on Open-Sora) with a 1.78-point VBench advantage. Δ-DiT reports no latency numbers and shows severe quality degradation (e.g., VBench drops from 77.40% to 52.00% on Latte). The motion-regularized variant (AdaCache-fast w/ MoReg) recovers most of the quality gap at the fastest setting (e.g., VBench 79.48% vs. 79.39% on Open-Sora, 79.30% vs. 75.83% on Open-Sora-Plan) at only a ~0.14× speedup cost. In a user study, AdaCache is preferred over PAB 70% of the time at comparable latency, and is perceived as indistinguishable from the unaccelerated baseline 41% of the time.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaCache extends the line of training-free caching methods for diffusion models (DeepCache, PAB, Delta-DiT, T-GATE, FORA) by replacing their fixed or heuristically-chosen caching schedules with a content-adaptive one driven by a per-video residual-change metric. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) the caching rate is decided dynamically per video generation via a codebook lookup on a distance metric, rather than being a static hyperparameter, and (2) it introduces Motion Regularization, a video-specific signal that modulates the schedule based on estimated latent motion—something absent from image-focused caching methods. The 'not all videos are created equal' framing connects to broader content-adaptive generation literature (region-based editing, adaptive step-count methods) but applies it to the full DiT compute graph rather than a spatial subset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 4.7× plug-and-play speedup across multiple open-source video DiTs (Open-Sora, Open-Sora-Plan, Latte) without retraining is a practically significant efficiency win that lowers the barrier to high-resolution, longer video generation. The observation that caching can actually *improve* VBench scores over the unaccelerated baseline (AdaCache-slow: 79.66 vs. 79.22) hints at a mild regularization effect, though this is not deeply analyzed. Open questions include whether the adaptive schedule generalizes to substantially longer videos (beyond 2–4 s), to architectures with heterogeneous per-layer costs, and whether the motion-regularization heuristic (noisy latent frame differences) remains reliable as video length and temporal complexity grow. The method also does not reduce the total number of denoising steps, so it is orthogonal to—and potentially stackable with—step-distillation or consistency-model approaches.
