## Problem

Existing few-shot counting and detection (FSCD) methods represent support exemplars as spatially collapsed prototypes via global average pooling, which discards the geometric and structural layout of the exemplar. This object-centric design causes significant performance degradation on non-object patterns—such as object parts, textures, or abstract geometric repetitions—where spatial structure is the primary matching cue rather than high-level semantics. The paper generalizes the task from object detection to arbitrary pattern detection and introduces a new dataset (RPINE) to evaluate this broader setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250708/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and one or more support exemplars, each specified by a bounding box on the image. The exemplar's box is used to crop a template feature from the backbone's feature map via RoIAlign, preserving the exemplar's spatial extent at the feature resolution (128×128 after upsampling from the ViT-H's native 64×64). At each feature-map location, the model outputs a presence score and four regression parameters (Δx, Δy, α_w, α_h) that shift and scale the exemplar's box to produce a predicted pattern box; low-confidence boxes are filtered by NMS, with an optional SAM decoder refinement step.

## Method

TMR (Template Matching and Regression) replaces prototype-based matching with channel-wise 2D cross-correlation between a RoIAlign-cropped template feature and the frozen SAM-ViT/H image feature map, preserving the exemplar's spatial structure. The resulting template-matching feature is concatenated with the original feature map and fed to a minimal prediction head consisting of a single 3×3 convolution plus linear projections for both box regression and presence classification—no cross-attention or multi-scale FPN. Box regression is support-conditioned: predicted boxes are parameterized as shifts and exponential scalings of the exemplar's own width and height, enabling the model to adaptively resize detections relative to the given exemplar. Training uses BCE loss on presence (with a margin around ground-truth centers) and gIoU loss on box parameters, with only ~19M trainable parameters on top of the frozen backbone, yielding 3.04T FLOPS versus 4.72–5.08T for prior FSCD methods.

## Results

On the new RPINE dataset (one-shot), TMR achieves AP 33.59, AP50 64.05, and AP75 30.52, substantially outperforming the best prior method GeCo (AP 23.33, AP50 45.93) by over 10 AP points. On FSCD-LVIS unseen split (three-shot), TMR reaches AP 22.71 and AP50 39.68, nearly doubling GeCo's AP of 11.47. On FSCD-147 (three-shot), TMR achieves AP 44.43 and AP50 73.83, matching or slightly exceeding GeCo (AP 43.42, AP50 75.06). TMR also uses fewer FLOPs (3.04T) than PseCo (5.08T) and GeCo (4.72T) with only 19M trainable parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On RPINE, TMR decisively outperforms all seven baselines, with the largest gap over GeCo (AP 33.59 vs 23.33, +10.26) and PseCo (AP 33.59 vs 23.18, +10.41), confirming that prototype-based matching fails on non-object patterns. On FSCD-LVIS unseen split, TMR (AP 22.71) more than doubles GeCo (AP 11.47) and PseCo (not reported), indicating strong generalization to unseen object categories. On FSCD-147, TMR is essentially on par with GeCo in the three-shot setting (AP 44.43 vs 43.42) and slightly ahead in one-shot (AP 43.15 vs 43.11), while clearly surpassing C-DETR (AP 19.14 one-shot) and DAVE (AP 19.46 one-shot). In cross-dataset evaluation, TMR trained on FSCD-147 transfers to RPINE with AP 26.21 versus GeCo's 19.47, and TMR trained on RPINE transfers to FSCD-147 with AP 41.39 versus GeCo's 36.99, demonstrating that template matching generalizes better than prototype matching across domains. The SAM decoder refinement helps on FSCD-147 (+7 AP) but hurts on RPINE (AP drops from 33.59 to 29.66), revealing that edge-based post-processing is counterproductive for non-object patterns.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant prototype-based paradigm in few-shot counting and detection (FSCD), where methods like C-DETR, PseCo, and GeCo spatially pool exemplar features into single vectors, losing geometric structure. Instead, TMR repurposes classic template matching—a technique from early computer vision—by performing channel-wise cross-correlation between a RoIAlign-cropped exemplar feature and the image feature map, preserving the 2D spatial layout. The architecture is deliberately minimalistic (a few 3×3 convolutions and linear projections on a frozen SAM-ViT/H backbone), contrasting with the cross-attention and multi-stage pipelines of prior FSCD methods. The paper also carves new ground by introducing RPINE, a dataset that extends evaluation beyond object-centric patterns to non-object and part-level repetitions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is twofold: RPINE provides the first densely annotated benchmark for multi-pattern, non-object detection, and TMR's simplicity (19M trainable params, 3.04T FLOPS) makes it a lightweight baseline for pattern detection tasks. A notable open question the paper surfaces is how to combine foundation-model knowledge (e.g., SAM) with detection mechanisms that are not biased toward object-level edges, since the SAM decoder actively degrades performance on non-object patterns. The strong cross-dataset generalization of TMR over prototype-based methods suggests that structural matching may be a more transferable inductive bias than semantic prototypes, a direction worth exploring further.
