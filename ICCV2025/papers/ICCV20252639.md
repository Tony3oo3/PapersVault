## Problem

RGBT multispectral vision tasks follow a case-by-case paradigm where each task requires manually customized two-stream models with handcrafted fusion modules, introducing artificial inductive bias, modality bias from ImageNet initialization of the thermal branch, and a data bottleneck from scarce annotated RGBT data. No generalized foundation model existed to learn modality-invariant representations from large-scale RGBT data in a self-supervised manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252639/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes paired RGB and thermal images, patchifies them into non-overlapping patch embeddings (768-d vectors), and applies complementary masking (90% ratio by default) so that masked patches in one modality are unmasked in the other. During pre-training, the shared ViT encoder produces modality-invariant latent representations; for downstream tasks, RGB and thermal features are concatenated along the channel dimension (2C) and fed into standard task heads (ViTDet, UperNet, LoFTR) without any additional fusion module.

## Method

M-SpecGene adopts a Siamese masked autoencoder with a shared-weight ViT encoder, a cross-attention layer for complementary information propagation, and two modality-specific decoders that independently reconstruct masked RGB and thermal pixels. The key innovation is the GMM-CMSS progressive masking strategy: a Cross-Modality Structural Sparsity (CMSS) metric quantifies per-patch information density using the ratio of cosine similarity to structural variance between RGB and thermal patch embeddings; a 3-component Gaussian Mixture Model is fit to the overall CMSS distribution and updated epoch-by-epoch; and a sampling function progressively shifts unmasked patches from high-information-density (foreground/object) regions to low-information-density (background) regions over training. This easy-to-hard, object-centric curriculum alleviates the information imbalance inherent in RGBT data, where thermal images lack textures and objects are smaller and less salient than in ImageNet.

## Results

On FLIR and LLVIP object detection, M-SpecGene (ViT-B) achieves mAP of 44.7 and 65.3 respectively, surpassing the previous best (TIRDet 44.3 on FLIR, CrossFormer 65.1 on LLVIP). On MVSeg semantic segmentation, M-SpecGene (ViT-B) reaches 63.02 mIoU, outperforming the best prior method DPLNet (57.90) by over 5 points. For cross-modality feature matching on LLVIP, M-SpecGene (ViT-S) achieves AUC@10° of 48.2, exceeding LoFTR (46.2) and all detector-based methods. On salient object detection, M-SpecGene (ViT-B) attains S-measure of 0.935 on VT1000 and 0.891 on VT821, setting new state-of-the-art across all three VT benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M-SpecGene (ViT-B) achieves the best FLIR mAP (44.7) and LLVIP mAP (65.3), edging out TIRDet (44.3) and CrossFormer (65.1) respectively, while also leading on mAP50 for FLIR (84.8 vs. 82.8 for vanilla MAE). On KAIST pedestrian detection, M-SpecGene (ViT-S) surpasses UniRGB-IR (ViT-B) by 1.47% on the ALL set (27.28 vs. 25.21 MR⁻²), and outperforms all 12 prior methods on 7 of 9 evaluation subsets. For semantic segmentation on MVSeg, M-SpecGene (ViT-B) achieves 63.02 mIoU, a 5.12-point gain over DPLNet (57.90) and 6.56 points over UniRGB-IR (56.46). In feature matching, M-SpecGene (ViT-S) outperforms LoFTR at all angular thresholds (e.g., 48.2 vs. 46.2 at 10°) and dramatically surpasses detector-based methods like RIFT and ReDFeat which score near zero. On salient object detection, M-SpecGene (ViT-B) beats LSNet and UniRGB-IR on VT821 (S: 0.891 vs. 0.877/0.881) and VT1000 (S: 0.935 vs. 0.924/0.939), though UniRGB-IR retains a slight edge on VT5000 S-measure (0.906 vs. 0.892). The one notable loss is on the small-scale FMB segmentation dataset where MRFS (61.2 mIoU) outperforms M-SpecGene (ViT-B) (60.1), which the authors attribute to FMB's limited 280-sample validation set favoring complex fusion modules.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the masked autoencoder (MAE) framework [14] from single-modality RGB to the RGBT multispectral setting, introducing a Siamese architecture with a shared-weight ViT encoder and modality-specific decoders. It departs from the dominant case-by-case RGBT paradigm—where each downstream task relies on handcrafted cross-attention, spatial attention, or graph-based fusion modules—by proposing that a self-supervised foundation model can learn modality-invariant representations that make such modules unnecessary. The closest prior work, UniRGB-IR [66], also targets unified RGB-IR processing but still requires an adapter-tuning design and a handcrafted fusion module; M-SpecGene eliminates both. The GMM-CMSS progressive masking strategy is a novel departure from information-aware masking methods that operate on single-modality cues (e.g., InfMAE's gray-value-based masking), instead exploiting cross-modality structural sparsity as a joint information-density signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single pretrained encoder plus simple channel concatenation can match or exceed task-specific fusion architectures across four diverse RGBT tasks, this work challenges the field's assumption that complex handcrafted fusion modules are essential for multispectral perception. The RGBT550K dataset (548K curated samples from 41 sources) addresses a real data bottleneck and provides a reusable resource for the community. However, several open questions remain: performance on smaller or more specialized benchmarks (e.g., FMB segmentation, VT821 saliency with ViT-S) lags behind methods with task-specific fusion, raising questions about whether the foundation-model paradigm fully subsumes handcrafted inductive biases for all task regimes. It is also unclear how the approach generalizes beyond the RGB-thermal pair to other multispectral combinations (e.g., RGB-NIR, RGB-hyperspectral) or whether the GMM-CMSS strategy transfers to modalities with different information-imbalance profiles.
