## Problem

Existing LLM-based mesh methods like LLaMA-Mesh suffer from two critical limitations: (1) token-length constraints force discarding most long mesh sequences, leaving only ~31k trainable samples and violating LLM scaling law principles, and (2) direct text serialization of full meshes causes the model to lose inherent 3D spatial structure such as vertex-face connectivity and local semantic coherence. The paper asks how to simultaneously scale up trainable mesh data and preserve 3D structural information within a text-serialized LLM framework.

## Input / Output

The model accepts natural language text prompts and text-serialized 3D meshes in OBJ format, where vertex coordinates are quantized to integers in [0,64] and sorted by z-y-x order, with faces sorted by their smallest vertex index, all flattened into a single token sequence compatible with standard LLM tokenizers (no vocabulary expansion needed). Full-mesh inputs are restricted to 800 faces via planar simplification to fit within the 8192-token context window. Outputs include generated text-serialized meshes, fluent textual descriptions of input meshes, and multi-turn dialogue responses that reference mesh topology and geometry.

## Method

MeshLLM introduces a Primitive-Mesh decomposition strategy that partitions full meshes into localized subunits using two complementary approaches: KNN-based clustering (yielding 1500k+ samples, ~50× larger than LLaMA-Mesh) and semantic segmentation via 3DSAMPart (yielding 100k+ high-quality samples with meaningful part boundaries). On top of this expanded dataset, the framework designs four progressive training tasks: vertex-face prediction (inferring face connectivity from vertex coordinates to learn topology), mesh assembly (reconstructing full meshes from local Primitive-Mesh components to capture global spatial relationships), mesh understanding (generating text descriptions from mesh data), and mesh generation (producing meshes from text prompts). Training follows a three-stage progressive schedule on LLaMA-8B-Instruct with full fine-tuning: Stage 1 on KNN Primitive-Meshes, Stage 2 on semantic Primitive-Meshes, and Stage 3 on full-mesh generation/understanding tasks, with 30% mixing of prior-phase and UltraChat data to prevent catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

On mesh generation (ShapeNet, 4 categories), MeshLLM achieves COV of 47.33–49.85 and FID of 35.70–42.39, substantially outperforming LLaMA-Mesh (COV 19.53–38.98, FID 49.83–65.29) and matching or approaching MeshXL (COV 46.16–51.42, FID 33.55–42.64). On mesh understanding, MeshLLM scores BLEU-1 0.763, CIDEr 1.753, and CLIP similarity 0.391, versus LLaMA-Mesh's 0.483, 0.397, and 0.124 respectively — a 58% relative improvement in CIDEr. MeshLLM is the only method evaluated that simultaneously supports generation, understanding, and interactive dialogue.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaMA-Mesh (the closest same-paradigm baseline), MeshLLM wins decisively on every metric: COV improves by 20–28 points (e.g., 47.33 vs. 19.53 on Chair), FID drops by 7–30 points, and KID drops by 7–21 points. On mesh understanding, CIDEr jumps from 0.397 to 1.753 (4.4×) and CLIP similarity from 0.124 to 0.391 (3.1×). Against MeshXL (a dedicated encoder-based mesh generator), MeshLLM is broadly comparable — slightly better on Table (COV 49.26 vs. 47.74, FID 39.59 vs. 42.64) and Bench FID (36.63 vs. 41.80), but slightly behind on Bench COV (49.38 vs. 51.42) and Lamp FID (35.70 vs. 33.55). Against PolyGen (class-conditional), MeshLLM is far superior on all categories except 1-NNA, and uniquely supports text-conditional generation, understanding, and dialogue. The key differentiator is that MeshLLM is the only method combining all three capabilities (generation + understanding + dialogue) in a single LLM-based framework without requiring a separate 3D encoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

MeshLLM is a direct methodological extension of LLaMA-Mesh, which first demonstrated that LLMs can parse and generate 3D meshes in text-serialized OBJ format without additional encoders. The key departure is the Primitive-Mesh decomposition strategy (KNN clustering + semantic segmentation via 3DSAMPart), which addresses LLaMA-Mesh's critical data-scale bottleneck (31k → 1500k+ samples) and its loss of 3D structural information. The vertex-face prediction and mesh assembly training tasks are novel additions that explicitly teach the LLM topological and spatial reasoning, going beyond LLaMA-Mesh's single text-mesh alignment objective. The text serialization pipeline (quantization, PolyGen-style sorting) is inherited from prior work, positioning MeshLLM as an engineering and training-strategy contribution within the established text-serialized mesh paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50× data scale expansion through simple KNN decomposition is a practical recipe that any LLM-based 3D pipeline could adopt, and the progressive local-to-global training paradigm offers a general template for injecting structural awareness into text-based 3D models. However, the approach remains constrained to 800-face meshes, the available 3D corpus is still orders of magnitude smaller than NLP corpora, and fine-grained generation or iterative refinement is not yet possible. Open questions include whether incorporating additional modalities (e.g., rendered images) or reinforcement learning could close the gap to encoder-based methods like MeshXL, and whether the Primitive-Mesh strategy generalizes to higher-polygon-count or textured meshes.
