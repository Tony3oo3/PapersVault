## Problem

Existing video-based multimodal LLMs are designed and evaluated almost exclusively on single-video tasks, leaving their ability to recognize fine-grained similarities and differences across sets of similar videos largely unexplored. No dedicated benchmark exists to test cross-video reasoning over action attributes, poses, object interactions, and emotional states within video sets, despite this capability being central to real-world applications like skill evaluation, anomaly detection, and procedural summarization.

## Input / Output

The model receives a set of four video clips (each ~55 s on average, uniformly sampled at 16 frames at 224×224 resolution) concatenated into a 64-frame input, along with a text question and a list of answer options (2 for binary, 4 for multiple-choice). Outputs include a predicted answer (evaluated by accuracy) and free-form similarity/difference captions describing commonalities and variations across the video set. The dataset spans 3,783 sets with 76,859 Q&A pairs covering nine aspects and 27 subcategories, with clips ranging from a few seconds to over two minutes.

## Method

VidSetReasoner explicitly models cross-video reasoning by encoding each of the four videos with the frozen InternVideo2.5 encoder (16 frames at 224×224, yielding 16×4096 max-pooled local features plus one 4096-dim global feature per video), projecting these into LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct's token space via a small linear layer, and feeding them as prefix tokens so the LLM reasons jointly over all videos. The model is fine-tuned with LoRA on LLaMA and the linear projection for three epochs (lr 2×10⁻⁴) on an automatically generated 72,000-pair training set, where GPT-4 produces Q&A pairs and similarity/difference captions from clip-level descriptions (InternVideo2.5 captions, InstructBLIP frame descriptions, Whisper transcripts) using 12 predefined templates. The key design choice is treating multi-video features as a unified prefix rather than processing videos independently, enabling the LLM to perform explicit cross-video comparison during generation.

## Results

On the VideoSetDiff evaluation set (4,859 Q&A items over 783 four-video sets), VidSetReasoner achieves 68.6% overall accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline InternVideo2.5 (66.8%) by 1.8 points and GPT4o (58.6%) by 10.0 points. The caption+GPT-4 pipelines (VideoChat2, VideoLLaMA2, TimeChat, InternVideo2, InternVideo2.5) cluster between 56.6% and 57.2%, while direct multi-video QA without GPT-4 assistance drops sharply for most models (e.g., TimeChat falls to 32.7%). VidSetReasoner also leads on both recognition (70.6%) and reasoning (66.5%) sub-metrics, and on binary questions (74.2%) versus multiple-choice (62.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VidSetReasoner (68.6%) outperforms every baseline on overall accuracy. The closest competitor is InternVideo2.5 in direct multi-video QA mode (66.8%), which VidSetReasoner surpasses by 1.8 points; the gap widens to 10.0 points over GPT4o (58.6%) and 11.4–12.0 points over the caption+GPT-4 pipelines (56.6–57.2%). A striking finding is that for most video-based LLMs (VideoChat2, VideoLLaMA2, TimeChat, InternVideo2), direct multi-video QA actually underperforms their own caption+GPT-4 counterparts by 4–24 points, indicating that these models lack native cross-video reasoning ability. TimeChat is the weakest direct-QA model at 32.7%, barely above the Blind GPT-4 baseline (51.7% is the no-video upper bound; TimeChat is below it). VidSetReasoner is the only model that simultaneously leads on both recognition (70.6%) and reasoning (66.5) sub-metrics, whereas GPT4o and the caption pipelines each dominate one sub-metric but lag on the other.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of multi-video comparison work (SVIP, StepDiff) from pairwise, narrow-domain settings to four-video sets spanning both instructional and general activities, and introduces the first benchmark explicitly targeting fine-grained similarity/difference reasoning across video sets. It also departs from the dominant single-video paradigm in multimodal LLM evaluation by requiring models to jointly attend to multiple clips and reason about cross-video attributes. The proposed VidSetReasoner repurposes existing components (InternVideo2.5 encoder, LLaMA-3.1-8B, LoRA) with a simple prefix-token mechanism for multi-video input, rather than introducing a fundamentally new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even strong models like GPT4o and InternVideo2.5 achieve only ~59–67% accuracy on multi-video similarity/difference QA, far below human-level performance, and that direct multi-video input often underperforms caption-then-reason pipelines—highlighting a fundamental gap in how current video LLMs handle cross-video reasoning. This opens concrete research directions: incorporating multi-video reasoning into pretraining, improving fine-grained recognition of subtle cues (facial expressions, sub-action ordering, viewpoint changes), and scaling training data for set-level video understanding. The practical applications (skill coaching, anomaly detection, procedural summarization) are well-motivated, but the current state of the art remains insufficient for deployment.
