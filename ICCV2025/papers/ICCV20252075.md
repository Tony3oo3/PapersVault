## Problem

DUSt3R's pointmap representation unifies geometry estimation and matching in 3D space but produces ambiguous correspondences in dynamic regions, where corresponding pixels across views yield inconsistent 3D coordinates. Existing dynamic reconstruction pipelines rely on separate auxiliary modules (optical flow, 2D trackers) that introduce domain gaps and accumulated errors, leaving a gap for a single network that jointly handles geometry, explicit matching, and motion understanding without external components.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252075/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts either a pair of 288×512 RGB images (pairwise mode) or a temporal window of 12 video frames (sequential mode), processed by a ViT-Large encoder and ViT-Base decoder. Beyond standard pointmaps, the third head (Head_3) outputs, for each pixel in the second view, the 3D coordinates of its corresponding pixel in the first view under the first view's coordinate frame, enabling explicit 3D correspondences. Dynamic masks are derived by thresholding the L2 difference between Head_2 and Head_3 outputs at 3× the median displacement, and downstream tasks (tracking, depth, reconstruction) are handled by flexibly constructing input pairs around a keyframe.

## Method

POMATO extends the DUSt3R/MonST3R architecture with two key additions: (1) a third regression head (Head_3) that learns explicit pointmap matching by predicting, for each pixel in view 2, the 3D coordinate of its counterpart in view 1 under view 1's frame, supervised by Euclidean distance loss on ground-truth correspondences from 2D tracking annotations; and (2) a temporal motion module consisting of two multi-head self-attention + FFN blocks inserted into the DPT head along the temporal dimension, promoting scale consistency across frames. Training is two-stage: Stage 1 trains Head_3 on pairwise images with a frozen encoder, and Stage 2 introduces the temporal module on 12-frame sequences with frozen decoders, randomly sampling among tracking, depth, and reconstruction tasks. The explicit matching representation enables dynamic mask estimation without auxiliary optical flow, and the self-attention-based temporal module (unlike autoregressive approaches) allows comprehensive interaction across all frames in the window.

## Results

On 3D point tracking (APD, higher is better), POMATO achieves 29.79 average APD at 12-frame length and 27.20 at 24-frame length, surpassing MonST3R (24.03 / 21.70) by 23.3% and 21.4% respectively, and outperforming SpatialTracker (24.17 / 22.42) which uses ground-truth camera intrinsics as extra input. For video depth estimation (per-sequence scale), POMATO achieves 0.085 Abs Rel and 93.3% δ<1.25 on KITTI, beating the online CUT3R (0.118 / 88.1) and the GA-based MonST3R (0.168 / 74.4). On camera pose estimation, POMATO attains the best RPE-rot of 0.509° on TUM, improving over MonST3R (1.142°) by 55.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

POMATO decisively outperforms all DUSt3R-family baselines on 3D point tracking: it beats MonST3R by +5.76 APD (avg L-12), MASt3R by +11.22, and DUSt3R by +10.53, with the largest gap on PStudio (+8.09 over MonST3R) where dynamic human motion is most challenging. Against SpatialTracker, a dedicated 3D tracking network that receives ground-truth intrinsics, POMATO still wins on PointOdyssey (+12.74) and ADT (+9.93) at L-12, though it trails on PStudio (−5.82), suggesting SpatialTracker's explicit depth prior helps in multi-person studio settings. On video depth (per-sequence scale), POMATO surpasses the online CUT3R on all three benchmarks (e.g., KITTI Abs Rel 0.085 vs 0.118) and matches or exceeds GA-based MonST3R on KITTI (0.085 vs 0.168) while being slightly behind on Sintel (0.416 vs 0.378). For pose estimation, POMATO achieves the best ATE on TUM (0.020) and the best RPE-rot on both TUM (0.509°) and Bonn (1.782°), improving RPE-rot over MonST3R by 55.4% on TUM, though it lags MonST3R on Bonn ATE (0.037 vs 0.025) due to limited motion in that indoor dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

POMATO is a direct architectural extension of DUSt3R's pointmap representation and MonST3R's dynamic-scene geometry estimation, inheriting the ViT-Large/ViT-Base encoder-decoder backbone and initializing from MonST3R checkpoints. Its key departure is the introduction of an explicit pointmap matching head (Head_3) that learns dense 3D correspondences between views, replacing the rigid-transformation assumption that makes DUSt3R ambiguous in dynamic regions. The temporal motion module follows Video Depth Anything's design of inserting transformer blocks into DPT heads, but applies self-attention across all frames simultaneously rather than the autoregressive blocking used by Spann3R and CUT3R. Conceptually, the paper unifies what prior pipelines handled with separate optical-flow or 2D-tracking auxiliaries into a single end-to-end network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for external matching modules and global alignment, POMATO offers a practical efficiency gain (0.7 s vs. 5.8 s for MonST3R on 6 frames) while matching or exceeding their accuracy, making feed-forward dynamic 3D reconstruction more accessible for real-time applications. The pointmap matching representation also enables dynamic mask estimation as a byproduct, removing a common source of domain gap in multi-module pipelines. Open questions include whether the 12-frame temporal window can be extended to longer sequences without degradation, how well the method generalizes beyond the synthetic training distributions (PointOdyssey, Carla, etc.) to in-the-wild dynamic scenes, and whether the approach scales to more complex non-rigid deformations.
