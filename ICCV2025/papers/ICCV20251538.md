## Problem

Event-based camera (EBC) object detection has been dominated by approaches that either construct complex event-specific representations or design specialized architectures, operating under the assumption that frame-based detectors are fundamentally incompatible with sparse, asynchronous event data. This paper challenges that assumption by asking whether mainstream, state-of-the-art image detectors can be adapted to EBC data with minimal modifications rather than requiring domain-specific redesign.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251538/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes event streams from Prophesee automotive sensors, converted into stacked 2D histogram frames of shape (20, H, W) — 2 polarity channels × 10 time bins of 5 ms within a 50 ms window — at 256×320 (Gen1) or 384×640 (1Mpx) resolution. Sequential frames are processed with persistent RNN memory across time steps, enabling detection of objects that become event-invisible when stationary. Output is standard COCO-format bounding boxes with class labels (cars, pedestrians, two-wheelers), evaluated via mAP.

## Method

EvRT-DETR follows a two-stage latent space adaptation procedure. First, RT-DETR is trained from scratch on individual event frames treated as conventional images using standard supervised training. Second, the trained RT-DETR is frozen and lightweight ConvLSTM modules are inserted at three encoder feature scales (E3, E4, E5) via residual connections with learnable ReZero-style scaling factors and projection matrices for dimensionality reduction. The ConvLSTM choice provides theoretically unbounded temporal memory, critical for maintaining detection of static objects that cease generating events. Only the inserted temporal modules are trained in stage two, adding as few as 2.3 M parameters (5.4% of the base model) while achieving state-of-the-art mAP on both Gen1 (+2.3) and 1Mpx (+1.4) benchmarks.

## Results

EvRT-DETR-B achieves state-of-the-art 52.7% mAP on Gen1 (+2.3 over the previous best ERGO-12 at 50.4%) and 50.1% mAP on 1Mpx (+1.4 over the previous best SAST-CB at 48.7%). The lighter EvRT-DETR-T variant also surpasses all prior methods, reaching 52.3% mAP on Gen1 and 49.9% mAP on 1Mpx. Notably, even the image-only RT-DETR-B baseline (without temporal adaptation) achieves 47.6% mAP on Gen1 and 45.2% mAP on 1Mpx, already competitive with specialized EBC architectures. Inference times are 12.7 ms (Gen1) and 18.8 ms (1Mpx) for EvRT-DETR-B on an NVIDIA T4 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvRT-DETR-B outperforms all 12 compared baselines on both Gen1 and 1Mpx. On Gen1, it surpasses the previous best ERGO-12 (50.4%) by +2.3 mAP and SAST-CB (48.2%) by +4.5 mAP; on 1Mpx, it beats SAST-CB (48.7%) by +1.4 mAP and AEC/GET-T (48.4%) by +1.7 mAP. The image-only RT-DETR-B (no temporal module) already matches or exceeds several specialized EBC methods (e.g., RVT-B at 47.2% Gen1, S5-ViT-B at 47.4% Gen1), demonstrating that the core RT-DETR architecture transfers well to event data. In terms of speed, EvRT-DETR-B (12.7 ms Gen1, 18.8 ms 1Mpx) is slower than RVT-B (10.2/11.9 ms) and S5-ViT-B (8.2/9.6 ms) but substantially faster than ERGO-12 (69.9/100.0 ms) and ASTMNet (35.6/72.3 ms). The lighter EvRT-DETR-T (8.4 ms Gen1, 12.5 ms 1Mpx) achieves near-identical accuracy to the Base variant while running faster than RVT-B on Gen1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant paradigms in EBC object detection—constructing complex event representations (ERGO-12, Time Surfaces) and designing specialized temporal architectures (RVT, S5-ViT, ASTMNet)—by instead applying latent space adaptation principles (LoRA, ReZero) to a frozen mainstream detector. It builds directly on RT-DETR (CVPR 2024) as the spatial backbone and inserts lightweight ConvLSTM modules at the encoder's multiscale feature levels, drawing on the RVT model's use of ConvLSTM for temporal memory. The conceptual lineage connects parameter-efficient fine-tuning in NLP/vision with event-based vision, positioning adaptation of existing detectors as a viable third direction alongside representation engineering and architecture design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a standard RT-DETR trained on simple stacked 2D histogram frames already matches specialized EBC methods challenges the field's assumption that event data fundamentally requires domain-specific representations or architectures, potentially redirecting research effort toward adaptation rather than redesign. The I2EvDet framework's demonstrated generalizability across RT-DETR and YOLOX suggests a reusable recipe for adding temporal capability to any image detector, with applications beyond EBC to other asynchronous or sparse temporal visual streams. Open questions include whether the fixed 50 ms window is truly sufficient versus exploiting full event asynchrony, whether the approach scales to higher-resolution or longer-sequence settings, and whether more complex representations (e.g., ERGO-12) combined with this adaptation could yield further gains.
