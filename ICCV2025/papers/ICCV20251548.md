## Problem

SMPL-driven 3DGS human avatars rely on the current frame's pose to predict non-rigid deformation, which fails to capture the complex one-to-many mapping from pose to appearance during complex motions. This global skeletal condition overlooks local details such as garment deformations far from the skeleton and cannot distinguish different appearances that correspond to the same pose at different moments in a motion sequence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251548/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB video frames (24 training / 6 testing views on DNA-Rendering; 4–5 training views on I3D-Human) along with per-frame SMPL(-X) pose parameters and camera intrinsics/extrinsics. It outputs an animatable set of 3D Gaussian primitives initialized from SMPL template vertices, which can be rendered at novel views and novel poses in real time (~45 FPS on I3D-Human) via differentiable splatting.

## Method

SeqAvatar introduces a hierarchical motion context with two complementary conditions: a coarse skeleton motion embedding f_ΔP derived from axis-angle pose differences across a temporal sequence, and a fine-grained point-wise velocity embedding f_V sampled from a motion template field that stores per-vertex velocities computed via LBS warping of the SMPL template. To enhance robustness, a Spatio-Temporal Multi-Scale Sampling (STMS) strategy expands the receptive field both spatially (aggregating velocities of τ nearest template vertices per Gaussian) and temporally (sampling pose/velocity sequences at multiple progressively increasing intervals to capture long-term trends and inter-frame details). An MLP conditioned on the Gaussian position, current pose, and both motion embeddings predicts per-Gaussian non-rigid offsets in position, scale, and rotation quaternion. The deformed canonical Gaussians are then warped to observation space via standard LBS with learned weight offsets and rendered through differentiable splatting, optimized with a combined photometric, perceptual, mask, and isopose/isocovariance loss.

## Results

On the I3D-Human dataset, SeqAvatar achieves the best PSNR across all four sequences (31.81, 31.98, 31.53, 33.62 dB), outperforming the strongest 3DGS baseline GauHuman by 1.3–3.4 dB and the NeRF-based Dyco by 0.4–2.1 dB. On DNA-Rendering, the method leads all six sequences in PSNR (e.g., 30.81 vs. 27.96 for GauHuman on 1_0206_04) and LPIPS (e.g., 39.47 vs. 50.42). For novel pose rendering on I3D-Human, SeqAvatar scores 30.28 PSNR / 0.9583 SSIM, surpassing Dyco (30.09 / 0.9569) and all 3DGS baselines, while rendering at ~45 FPS compared to Dyco's ~0.7 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GauHuman [22], SeqAvatar wins on every metric across both DNA-Rendering and I3D-Human, with PSNR gains of 1.3–3.4 dB and LPIPS reductions of up to 19 points (e.g., 34.02 vs. 53.67 on I3D-Human ID3_1). Against 3DGS-Avatar [56], the improvement is consistent but slightly smaller (0.9–2.6 dB PSNR), with the largest gap on DNA-Rendering sequence 2_0019_10 (34.97 vs. 32.06 PSNR). Against GART [31] (evaluated only on DNA-Rendering), SeqAvatar leads by 1.5–3.3 dB PSNR and 8–15 LPIPS points. Against the NeRF-based Dyco [8], SeqAvatar achieves higher PSNR (by 0.4–2.1 dB) and SSIM on I3D-Human while rendering roughly 60× faster (~45 FPS vs. ~0.7 FPS); Dyco's LPIPS is slightly lower on ID1_2 (25.75 vs. 27.84), indicating marginally better perceptual quality in that specific case, but SeqAvatar dominates on the other three sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

SeqAvatar extends the 3DGS-based human avatar line (3DGS-Avatar, GART, GauHuman) by introducing a hierarchical temporal motion context—coarse skeleton pose differences (ΔP) plus fine-grained point-wise vertex velocities (V)—that prior explicit 3DGS methods entirely lacked. It also builds on Dyco's (NeRF-based) observation that pose sequences carry useful temporal information, but departs from Dyco by exploiting the explicit Gaussian primitive structure to sample per-point velocities from a SMPL template motion field, resolving the circular dependency that would arise from using dynamically updated Gaussian positions. The spatio-temporal multi-scale sampling strategy further differentiates the work by aggregating motion cues across multiple temporal intervals and spatial neighborhoods, a design not present in either the 3DGS or NeRF baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: SeqAvatar achieves ~45 FPS real-time rendering while matching or exceeding the quality of NeRF-based Dyco (~0.7 FPS), closing the long-standing speed-quality gap for temporally-aware human avatars. The hierarchical motion context addresses the one-to-many pose-to-appearance mapping problem that limits current SMPL-driven 3DGS methods, particularly for loose garments and complex motions. However, the authors acknowledge that Gaussian splatting inherently introduces slight blur compared to NeRF's volumetric integration, and that velocity cues derived from the coarse SMPL mesh (rather than dense surface tracking) may cap fine-scale garment deformation accuracy—both open challenges for future work.
