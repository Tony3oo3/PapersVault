## Problem

Coarse-to-fine dense matching pipelines remain computationally expensive because their refiners require high-dimensional features, can only correct errors within local windows, and are not effectively co-optimized with other components. This forces reliance on heavy feature extractors (e.g., DINOv2 in RoMa) and complex global matchers (e.g., Gaussian-process-based in DKM) to compensate for weak refinement, inflating parameter counts, memory, and inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250055/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images at arbitrary resolutions and produces dense pixel correspondences at 1/2 resolution along with a per-pixel certainty map. Internally, a feature pyramid is built at 1/16, 1/8, and 1/4 resolutions, and the refinement pipeline iteratively refines matches from 1/16 up to 1/2 across three stages. Training uses 800×608 crops; ArgMatch+ recovers full resolution by appending RoMa's last two convolutional refinement modules without additional training.

## Method

ArgMatch introduces an adaptive refinement gathering pipeline with three co-designed components: (i) a content-aware offset estimator that autoscales CV sampling windows via flow-gradient-based geometric scale and modulates CV decoding using a self-correlation volume (SV) computed from the sampled patch, replacing the need for highly distinctive features; (ii) a locally consistent match rectifier that regresses noisy matches as a weighted combination of neighbors, where weights fuse spatial correlation, feature similarity, and confidence scores, enabling correction of large errors without global matching; and (iii) a locally consistent upsampler that applies the same neighbor-gathering principle across resolutions to avoid bilinear artifacts at depth discontinuities. An adaptive gated gathering mechanism selectively integrates offset updates and routes backpropagated gradients to earlier stages when errors exceed the local window, while the local consistency property prevents gradients from crossing depth boundaries. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a lightweight feature extractor (depthwise separable + dilated convolutions, U-Net, CBAM) and a GMFlow-style global matcher using Flash Attention, achieving SOTA-competitive accuracy at ~90% fewer parameters and 73% less inference time than RoMa.

## Results

On MegaDepth, ArgMatch (38.3M params) achieves AUC@5°=61.2%, AUC@10°=75.9%, AUC@20°=85.9% with 270ms inference time and 2.30GB memory, compared to RoMa (415M params) at 62.6/76.7/86.3% with 1557ms and 14.8GB — a 90% parameter reduction, 73% speedup, and 84% memory saving. On dense matching (PCK@0.5px), ArgMatch+ reaches 60.2%, surpassing RoMa (58.9%) and DKM (56.2%). On visual localization (InLoc), ArgMatch+ achieves 58.6/78.8/89.4% pose accuracy on DUC1, outperforming RoMa (54.5/78.8/89.4%) and all other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest dense matcher RoMa (415M params), ArgMatch (38.3M) trails by only 1.4% on MegaDepth AUC@5° (61.2 vs 62.6) while using 90% fewer parameters, 73% less inference time (270ms vs 1557ms), and 84% less memory (2.30GB vs 14.8GB). On HPatches, ArgMatch actually surpasses RoMa at the 3px threshold (72.8 vs 72.4 AUC). ArgMatch+ (with RoMa's final refinement modules) matches or exceeds RoMa on InLoc visual localization (58.6 vs 54.5 on DUC1 at 0.25m/2°) and on dense matching PCK@0.5px (60.2 vs 58.9). Against DKM (72.3M), ArgMatch is slightly better on MegaDepth (61.2 vs 60.4 AUC@5°) with 3.5× less memory and 3.5× faster inference. All semi-dense baselines (LoFTR, ASpan, ELoFTR, XFeat) are outperformed by 5–11% on MegaDepth AUC@5°, and the sparse baseline LG trails by over 10%. On IMC 22, ArgMatch+ ranks second (84.2 mAA@10°) and ArgMatch third (83.2), behind RoMa (85.5) but ahead of DKM (82.6).

## Relation to Prior Work

ArgMatch sits at the intersection of optical flow refinement (RAFT's correlation-volume decoding, GMFlow's global matching) and dense geometric matching (PDC-Net+, DKM, RoMa), but deliberately departs from the prevailing assumption that high-dimensional features and heavy global matchers are necessary for competitive accuracy. Its core departure is a redesign of the refinement pipeline itself: content-aware CV modulation replaces the pursuit of sharper features, locally consistent rectification (inspired by KPA-Flow/GAFlow) handles errors beyond the CV window, and an adaptive gated gathering mechanism replaces the gradient-stopping tricks used in DKM and RoMa. The global matcher follows GMFlow's cross-attention formulation, and the upsampler extends RAFT's pixel-shuffle with semantic weighting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 38M-parameter model achieves within ~1° AUC of RoMa's 415M-parameter model while using 84% less memory and 73% less inference time, making dense geometric matching viable on resource-constrained hardware. The conceptual contribution—that refinement pipeline design, not feature dimensionality, is the primary efficiency bottleneck—reframes how practitioners should approach dense matcher design. Open questions remain: the paper acknowledges hallucinated matches in occluded regions due to local-consensus regression, suboptimal certainty-map learning from noisy MegaDepth ground truth, and the unresolved one-to-many ambiguity in coarse stages that the gated mechanism only partially addresses.
