## Problem

Diffusion models lack a direct, lightweight mechanism to control the level of detail (granularity) in their outputs. While the model can produce varying detail levels across different images, its uniform generative process offers no simple way to adjust how much detail is rendered in specific regions of the same image or to globally shift toward richer or smoother results without retraining or architectural modifications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251263/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts any standard diffusion model input (text prompt, reference image, or structural control signal such as pose/depth/canny) augmented with a single scalar ω, a spatial omega mask at VAE-downsampled resolution (H/f × W/f), or a temporal omega schedule over denoising steps. The output is the generated image or video with the desired granularity applied globally, per-region, or per-stage. No additional training data, reference images, or auxiliary networks are required beyond what the base model already consumes.

## Method

Omegance scales the predicted noise ε_θ(z_t, t) by a factor ω at each reverse-diffusion denoising step, modifying the effective SNR trajectory without altering the network or noise schedule. When ω < 1, less noise is removed per step, retaining high-frequency components that the model interprets as richer textures and more complex structures; when ω > 1, more aggressive denoising suppresses high-frequency detail for smoother outputs. The technique is scheduler-agnostic: for DDIM and Euler it directly multiplies the noise prediction, while for flow-matching schedulers a mean-preserving variant (subtracting the batch mean before scaling) prevents unwanted color shifts. Spatial granularity control is achieved via an omega mask M(i,j) that assigns distinct ω values to different image regions, and temporal control via an omega schedule S(t) that modulates ω across denoising stages to independently adjust early-stage layout complexity versus late-stage texture richness.

## Results

Using 1,000 prompts from DiffusionDB with SDXL as the base model, Omegance's detail suppression setting (∏=6.0) achieves the best FID (157.47) and IS (13.82) among all tested configurations, outperforming the SDXL baseline (FID 162.18, IS 13.23). Detail enhancement (∏=−6.0) maintains CLIP score (32.81) and Q-Align (4.67) on par with the baseline while boosting PickScore from 0.1468 to 0.1601. In a user study with 101 participants, Omegance achieves 93.94% average rank accuracy for granularity control and 81.38% positive preference for output quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Omegance (∏=6.0, detail suppression) outperforms FreeU on FID (157.47 vs. 167.22), IS (13.82 vs. 12.25), CLIP (32.70 vs. 31.76), and PickScore (0.1149 vs. 0.0967), while matching on Q-Align (4.64). Against the Cosine scheduler baseline, Omegance wins decisively on every metric, with FID dropping from 182.06 to 157.47 and Q-Align rising from 2.88 to 4.64. The Rescaled scheduler is similarly dominated: Omegance improves FID by 5.8 points (163.29→157.47), IS by 2.94 (10.88→13.82), and CLIP by 3.9 (28.80→32.70). For detail enhancement, Omegance (∏=−6.0) achieves the highest PickScore (0.1601) and Q-Align (4.67) among all settings, surpassing the SDXL baseline on aesthetics while preserving text-image alignment (CLIP 32.81 vs. 32.88). Overall, Omegance dominates all three external baselines (FreeU, Cosine Sch., Rescaled Sch.) across the majority of metrics, confirming that a single noise-scaling parameter can match or exceed methods requiring architectural modifications or scheduler redesigns.

## Relation to Prior Work

Omegance repurposes a fundamental but previously underexplored operation in diffusion models—scaling the predicted noise during denoising—as a single interpretable parameter for granularity control. It departs from architecture-specific quality-enhancement methods like FreeU (which adjusts U-Net backbone and skip-connection features) and global CFG modifications (SAG, PAG) by being architecture-agnostic and supporting spatially/temporally varying control via omega masks and schedules. The method also contrasts with noise-schedule redesign approaches (cosine, rescaled schedulers) that require fixed global adjustments, and with RLHF-based fine-tuning (DPOK, ImageReward) that incurs costly retraining. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of diffusion denoising dynamics (early layout vs. late detail) and variance manipulation, showing that the SNR trajectory can be locally and temporally modulated without altering the forward process or model weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Omegance provides a training-free, near-zero-overhead knob for controlling detail granularity that works across U-Net and DiT architectures, multiple schedulers (DDIM, Euler, flow matching), and both image and video generation—making it immediately deployable in existing pipelines. Its spatial and temporal control (omega masks and schedules) opens the door to region-specific and stage-specific detail manipulation that text prompts alone cannot express. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that Omegance does not inherently improve base-model generation quality; it redistributes existing detail rather than creating new information. Open questions include how to automatically infer optimal omega values or schedules from user intent, and whether the approach transfers to other generative paradigms (e.g., autoregressive or GAN-based models).
