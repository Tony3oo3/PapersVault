## Problem

Existing adversarial purification methods (e.g., DiffPure, LM) are designed around the assumption that perturbations are additive and small-scale, making them significantly less effective against non-additive perturbations such as blur, occlusion, and geometric distortion. When forcibly applied to non-additive perturbations, these methods suffer from semantic drift because the effective additive difference can be large enough to span multiple feasible solutions from different classes. The paper asks whether adversarial purification can be extended to handle general non-additive perturbations where the transformation type is known but its parameters are unknown.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 32×32 RGB adversarial image, a pretrained EDM diffusion model, and a known transformation function f (e.g., convolution kernel, patch placement, or flow-field) whose parameters ε are unknown. It jointly optimizes both the underlying clean image x and the perturbation parameter ε over T=500 alternating Adam steps, producing a purified image and estimated perturbation parameters. For non-differentiable transformations (e.g., patch occlusion), an auxiliary neural network is trained on synthetic (x_adv, x, ε) tuples to approximate f during optimization.

## Method

NAPPure models the adversarial image generation as x_adv = f(x, ε) and disentangles the clean image and perturbation parameters by maximizing a three-term objective: (1) an image likelihood term approximated via EDM's ELBO (denoiser matching loss), (2) a perturbation prior via an energy-based potential function φ(ε) that penalizes large deviations from the identity element, and (3) a Gaussian reconstruction term ‖x_adv − f(x, ε)‖² that constrains solutions to be valid under the known generation process. The image and parameter are updated alternately with separate learning rates, and a single noise sample per step is used for efficiency. A key theoretical result shows that under additive perturbations with a uniform prior, the inner optimization over ε is analytically solvable, causing NAPPure to exactly degenerate into the standard LM purification objective. For composite transformations, NAPPure-joint introduces learnable interpolation weights w∈[0,1] per component to stabilize joint optimization.

## Results

On GTSRB, NAPPure achieves an average robust accuracy of 73.93% across four perturbation types (Conv, Patch, Flow, Add), substantially outperforming DiffPure (47.61%), LM (38.71%), and adversarial training AT (37.30%). On CIFAR-10, NAPPure reaches 66.94% average robust accuracy versus 57.82% for DiffPure and 44.88% for LM. The type-agnostic variant NAPPure-joint, which composites all four transformations simultaneously, still achieves 59.29% on GTSRB and 59.72% on CIFAR-10, exceeding all baselines under most attack types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NAPPure dominates all baselines on GTSRB under non-additive perturbations: it achieves 86.91% robust accuracy on Conv (vs. 61.52% DiffPure, 53.32% LM), 74.22% on Patch (vs. 46.29% DiffPure, 13.67% LM), and 51.37% on Flow (vs. 21.88% DiffPure, 8.79% LM). Under additive attacks, NAPPure (83.20%) slightly trails LM (79.07%) but exceeds DiffPure (60.74%), confirming the theoretical degeneration result that NAPPure subsumes standard purification. On CIFAR-10, NAPPure leads on Patch (76.75% vs. 69.73% DiffPure) and Add (82.81% vs. 79.10% DiffPure), though DiffPure remains competitive on Conv (59.38% vs. 66.40% NAPPure) and Flow (23.06% vs. 48.24% NAPPure). Adversarial training (AT) is consistently the weakest defense, with only 37.30% average robust accuracy on GTSRB, highlighting that retraining-based approaches struggle with non-additive threats. The type-agnostic NAPPure-joint variant, while 14.64% below NAPPure on GTSRB, still surpasses DiffPure by 11.68% on average, demonstrating practical value when the exact perturbation type is unknown.

## Relation to Prior Work

NAPPure extends the adversarial purification paradigm established by DiffPure and LM, which use diffusion models to push perturbed images back toward the data manifold. The key conceptual departure is jointly optimizing both the clean image and the perturbation parameters, rather than only the image, and the paper proves that standard purification (LM) is a degenerate special case when the transformation is additive. The framework repurposes EDM as the likelihood estimator and introduces an energy-based prior on perturbation parameters plus a reconstruction term tied to the known transformation function, making it a principled generalization rather than an ad-hoc extension.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Non-additive perturbations (blur via optical films, occlusion via printed patches, geometric distortion) are physically more realistic and arguably easier for attackers to deploy than pixel-level additive noise, yet prior purification methods fail catastrophically on them. The framework's plug-and-play design for new transformation types and its composite-attack handling (NAPPure-joint) offer a practical path toward type-agnostic purification. However, experiments are limited to 32×32 images on two small datasets, the type-agnostic variant loses 15–20 points over the type-specific one, and scalability to higher-resolution or more complex transformations remains untested.
