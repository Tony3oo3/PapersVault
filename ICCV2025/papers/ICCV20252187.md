## Problem

Existing 3DGS-based semantic scene understanding methods attach features to free Gaussians via differentiable rendering, producing noisy segmentation and messy instance-level Gaussian selections. The root causes are threefold: unbounded Gaussians expand excessively across views creating semantic artifacts, α-blending rasterization ambiguously assigns features to overlapping Gaussians, and feature-similarity-only selection ignores 3D spatial constraints, pulling in Gaussians from non-adjacent objects with similar features.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images of a 3D scene along with SAM ViT-H instance masks and OpenCLIP ViT-B/32 language features extracted per instance. At inference, it accepts either a 2D click coordinate (back-projected via depth map) or an open-vocabulary text prompt (encoded by CLIP) as the query signal. The output is a structured representation where each anchor stores its voxel position, size, a 3-D distilled semantic feature, a 512-D CLIP language feature, and k=5 associated Gaussians; downstream tasks consume this to produce clean binary instance masks, edited scenes (via LaMa inpainting), or physics simulation results (via PhyGaussian/MLS-MPM).

## Method

AG²aussian introduces an anchor-graph structure that organizes semantic features and regulates Gaussian primitives through a three-stage pipeline. In Stage 1, a three-layer multi-resolution voxelization (4× size ratio, minimum 200³) places anchors at voxel centers, each governing k=5 child Gaussians whose positions and scales are constrained within the parent voxel via offset vectors and sigmoid-scaled relative sizes; training combines 3DGS reconstruction loss with intra-voxel spatial regularization, depth distortion loss (from 2DGS), and SAM-guided contrastive learning (intra-mask smoothing + inter-mask contrastive). In Stage 2, intra-voxel and inter-voxel edges form a sparse anchor graph, and a Gaussian-weighted graph Laplacian propagation (Dirichlet energy with τ=0.05 kernel) smooths anchor features while preserving boundary sharpness. In Stage 3, Union-Find clustering groups anchors into object instances, and CLIP features are attached via IoU-and-feature-distance matching against SAM masks. At query time, region-growing traverses graph edges with weight >0.90 (click) or seeds from CLIP cosine similarity (text), yielding clean, spatially coherent Gaussian selections.

## Results

On the LERF-OVS dataset, AG²aussian achieves a mean IoU of 88.33% and mean Boundary IoU of 73.58% for click-based object query, outperforming the strongest baseline OpenGaussian (73.46% mIoU, 58.33% mBIoU) by 14.9 and 15.3 points respectively. For open-vocabulary text query, the method reaches 54.35% mIoU and 49.43% mBIoU, surpassing OpenGaussian (44.17%, 38.87%) by 10.2 and 10.6 points. The ablation study (Table 2) confirms that both the graph Laplacian propagation loss and the graph-based region-growing segmentation contribute meaningfully, with the full model achieving 54.35% mIoU versus 42.72% without either component.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AG²aussian outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against OpenGaussian [39] (the strongest competitor), the gains are most pronounced on click query: +14.9 mIoU (88.33 vs. 73.46) and +15.3 mBIoU (73.58 vs. 58.33), indicating substantially cleaner instance boundaries. On text query, the improvement over OpenGaussian is +10.2 mIoU (54.35 vs. 44.17) and +10.6 mBIoU (49.43 vs. 38.87). SAGA [3] is competitive on click mIoU (80.05) but lags significantly on boundary quality (63.15 mBIoU) and is far weaker on text query (12.99 mIoU). GaussianGrouping [44] is consistently the weakest baseline, with mIoU below 41 on click and 36 on text, likely due to its convex-hull-based selection strategy. In physical simulation (Figure 6), the clean Gaussian selection enables realistic object dragging, whereas SAGA and OpenGaussian fail to move the object (inner Gaussians not selected) and GaussianGrouping leaves messy surrounding artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3DGS semantic understanding line (LEGaussian, Feature3DGS, SAGA, GaussianGrouping, OpenGaussian) by replacing free Gaussians with an anchor-graph structure inspired by Scaffold-GS's anchor-voxel organization. The key departure is twofold: (1) semantic features are attached to anchors that spatially constrain their associated Gaussians via multi-resolution voxelization, and (2) a Gaussian-weighted graph Laplacian propagation refines anchor features across spatially connected neighbors, addressing the feature inconsistency and boundary ambiguity that plague free-Gaussian approaches. The contrastive learning loss is adopted from OpenGaussian, and the depth distortion loss from 2DGS, but the graph-based propagation and region-growing query mechanism are novel contributions specific to this work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Clean instance-level Gaussian selection is a prerequisite for downstream 3DGS editing and simulation tasks, yet prior methods produce noisy selections with extra surrounding Gaussians and missing interior ones. The anchor-graph structure offers a principled way to enforce spatial compactness while enabling semantic propagation, and the validation across four applications (click query, text query, object removal, physical simulation) demonstrates practical utility beyond segmentation metrics. Limitations include sensitivity to glass/metal objects due to feature blending and dependence on SAM's segmentation quality, which can introduce over-segmentation. Open questions include scalability to large-scale scenes, handling of reflective surfaces, and whether the graph propagation can be extended to temporal or dynamic settings.
