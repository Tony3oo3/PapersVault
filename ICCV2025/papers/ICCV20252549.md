## Problem

Geospatial Foundation Models pretrained on RGB satellite images require adaptation to handle multispectral data, but existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods like LoRA apply uniform low-rank updates that entangle RGB geometric information with new spectral radiometric information, failing to exploit the pretrained parameters for their intended purpose. The paper identifies that the linear arithmetic of self-attention mixes heterogeneous information types (geometry vs. radiometry) through shared projection matrices, limiting both the utility of pretrained weights and the integration of new spectral channels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252549/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multispectral satellite images with 6–13 spectral channels (resized to 224×224), decomposing them into RGB channels processed by the frozen pretrained GFM and additional spectral channels processed via a pixel-set encoder that computes spectral indices (NDVI, NDTI) and their summary statistics (mean, std, quartiles, min/max, 0.1/0.4/0.6/0.9-quantiles) over 10% of randomly sampled pixels per patch. Outputs are class labels for classification tasks (So2Sat, Brick Kiln, ForestNet) or pixel-wise masks for semantic segmentation (MADOS, BurnScars), with the latter using a trainable UPerNet decoder on top of the adapted encoder features.

## Method

DEFLECT introduces three architectural components: (1) an Untangled Patch Embedding (UPE) layer that separately computes RGB embeddings via the pretrained patch embedder and spectral embeddings via a parameter-light pixel-set encoding of spectral index statistics; (2) an Untangled Attention (uAtt) module that replaces standard attention in a subset of layers (e.g., layers 3, 5, 7, 11 for ViT-Base), using frozen pretrained Q/K/V matrices for RGB-to-RGB products and new low-rank (rank-16) matrices for all cross-modal and spectral-to-spectral attention terms; and (3) an embedding deflection constraint that forces the norm of the uAtt displacement to equal the norm of the original pretrained attention displacement, preserving the global latent-space structure while redirecting trajectories based on spectral information. Only 0.2–0.9% of encoder parameters are tuned (vs. 2–5% for LoRA/SLR), and the method is composable with LoRA rather than mutually exclusive.

## Results

DEFLECT achieves an average performance of 64.2 (Scale-MAE), 64.5 (DINO-MC), and 58.5 (Cross-Scale MAE) across five geospatial datasets, while tuning only 0.2–0.9% of encoder parameters. This matches or exceeds full fine-tuning (oracle) on several dataset–model pairs, e.g., MADOS with Scale-MAE (50.5 mIoU vs. 46.2) and ForestNet with Cross-Scale MAE (41.6 mF1 vs. 36.0). Compared to low-rank baselines (LoRA, SLR) that tune 2.1–5.7% of parameters, DEFLECT uses 5–10× fewer trainable parameters while delivering equal or higher average scores. Standard deviations across three runs are consistently low (≤1.2), indicating stable convergence unlike competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA [21], DEFLECT wins on average performance for all three GFMs: 64.2 vs. 63.1 (Scale-MAE), 64.5 vs. 45.9 (DINO-MC), and 58.5 vs. 53.2 (Cross-Scale MAE), while tuning 0.2–0.9% of parameters versus LoRA's 2.1–5.4%. The gap is most dramatic with DINO-MC, where LoRA's MADOS mIoU collapses to 5.7±3.4 while DEFLECT maintains 51.6±0.1. Against SLR [50], DEFLECT similarly outperforms on average (64.2 vs. 63.4, 64.5 vs. 47.5, 58.5 vs. 55.2) with roughly 10× fewer tuned parameters. Against the selective baselines BitFit [62] and NormTuning [64], DEFLECT leads by 7–13 points in average performance across all models, with the largest margins on Scale-MAE (64.2 vs. 56.9/57.1) and Cross-Scale MAE (58.5 vs. 55.8/55.0). Notably, DEFLECT also surpasses the full-finetuning oracle on specific configurations (MADOS/Scale-MAE: 50.5 vs. 46.2 mIoU; ForestNet/Cross-Scale MAE: 41.6 vs. 36.0 mF1), suggesting that the embedding deflection constraint preserves useful pretrained structure that end-to-end fine-tuning can destroy.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEFLECT departs from the dominant low-rank adaptation paradigm (LoRA, SLR) by arguing that the linear arithmetic of self-attention entangles heterogeneous information types (RGB geometric vs. multispectral radiometric), making shared projection matrices suboptimal. It draws on the dynamical-systems view of ViTs (Karkar et al.) to formalize 'embedding deflection' as a norm-constrained trajectory deviation, and repurposes pixel-set encoding with hand-crafted spectral indices (NDVI, NDTI) for the auxiliary embedding. Rather than building on a single prior PEFT method, it synthesizes domain-specific inductive biases (untangled patch embedding, untangled attention) that are orthogonal to and combinable with low-rank techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For the geospatial ML community, DEFLECT offers a practical path to adapting RGB-pretrained GFMs to multispectral sensors with <1% tuned parameters and notably more stable performance across models than LoRA/SLR, which can collapse on certain model-dataset combinations. The norm-constraint on embedding displacement is a clean mechanism for preserving pretrained spatial structure while injecting spectral information. Open questions include whether the hand-crafted spectral indices generalize to hyperspectral or multi-temporal settings, whether the higher FLOPs cost is acceptable at scale, and whether the approach transfers to non-ViT GFMs or to tasks beyond classification/segmentation.
