## Problem

CNN encoders in visual reinforcement learning are highly sensitive to input resolution, require extensive hyperparameter tuning, and focus on pixel-level features rather than the underlying environment dynamics. While representation learning has been explored through auxiliary contrastive losses (CURL) or autoencoders (SAC-AE), these approaches add computational overhead and still rely on CNN backbones. The encoder architecture itself has remained largely unexplored as a lever for improving both performance and sample efficiency in model-free visual RL.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251999/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder accepts stacked grayscale frames (typically 4 history frames as input channels) at arbitrary spatial resolutions (84×84, 100×100, 128×128, 48×48, up to 224×224) without any resizing or retraining. It outputs a fixed-dimensional representation vector via channel-wise max pooling, which is resolution-independent by construction. This representation is then consumed by standard RL algorithms (PPO, A2C, Efficient Rainbow, SAC) to produce discrete or continuous actions, making the FNO encoder a direct drop-in replacement for the CNN in any existing model-free RL pipeline.

## Method

The paper replaces the CNN image encoder with a Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) consisting of an initial affine projection to 128 channels, three successive Fourier layers, and a final channel-wise max pooling layer. Each Fourier layer performs element-wise multiplication in the Fourier domain (capturing global, low-frequency structure via a learned low-pass filter) combined with a 1×1 convolution in physical space (adding local detail), followed by GeLU activation and optional 3×3 max-pool downsampling. Because the FNO learns its parameters directly in Fourier space, it is discretization-invariant: the same trained weights produce valid representations at any input resolution, enabling zero-shot domain adaptation from low-resolution training to high-resolution inference. The key insight is that FNOs implicitly approximate the PDEs governing environment dynamics, yielding 'physics-informed' representations that improve both reward and sample efficiency without requiring auxiliary encoders, contrastive losses, or any modification to the base RL algorithm's hyperparameters.

## Results

On the Atari 100K benchmark, the FNO encoder achieves a median Human-Normalized Score (HNS) of 26.11%, improving over Efficient Rainbow (16.14%) by 1.36× and CURL (17.53%) by 1.49×, with a 6.04× improvement in mean HNS over Efficient Rainbow. The method outperforms the CNN-based Efficient Rainbow in 20 out of 26 games and achieves superhuman performance in Freeway, Jamesbond, and Krull. On the CARLA lane-keeping task (D4RL), FNO-PPO reaches a mean normalized score of 81 (and 100±0.03 in best runs), while in the offline setting (1% DQN Replay), FNO-modified R-BVE outperforms Decision Transformers by 2.89×. The FNO encoder also demonstrates zero-shot resolution invariance, maintaining ~26% median HNS across 84×84, 100×100, and 128×128 image inputs without retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Efficient Rainbow (the primary base algorithm), FNO achieves a 1.36× improvement in median HNS (26.11 vs 16.14) and a 6.04× improvement in mean HNS, winning in 20 of 26 games. Compared to CURL (which adds momentum contrast to Efficient Rainbow), FNO achieves superior performance in 13 tasks versus CURL's 9, with a median HNS of 26.11 vs 17.53. In the offline RL setting (1% DQN Replay), FNO-modified R-BVE outperforms Decision Transformers by 2.89× (e.g., 555.1 vs 267.5 on Breakout, 110.7 vs 15.4 on Qbert) and also beats CQL and QR-DQN across all four evaluated games. On CARLA lane-keeping, FNO-PPO surpasses the CNN-PPO baseline in both final reward and sample efficiency, reaching a perfect normalized score of 100±0.03 in best evaluation runs. The method also demonstrates a unique advantage over all CNN-based baselines: zero-shot generalization to different image resolutions (84×84, 100×100, 128×128) without retraining, whereas CNN-based methods (Rainbow, Efficient Rainbow, CURL) fail entirely at non-standard resolutions. Notably, FNO does not require additional hyperparameter tuning, auxiliary encoders, or offline pretraining data, unlike CURL (which needs a momentum encoder) or SGI (which requires offline datasets and multiple auxiliary models).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Fourier Neural Operators (FNO), originally designed to learn PDE solution operators in infinite-dimensional function spaces (Li et al., ICLR 2021), as a drop-in image encoder for model-free visual RL, replacing the CNN backbones in PPO, A2C, Rainbow, and SAC. It departs from prior RL representation-learning efforts (CURL, SAC-AE, SGI) that all retain CNN encoders and require auxiliary models, offline pretraining data, or additional hyperparameter tuning; here the FNO encoder is entirely online, uses no auxiliary networks, and requires no hyperparameter changes to the base RL algorithm. The authors frame this as the first major architectural change to the image encoder in online model-free visual RL since DQN, positioning FNO as a physics-informed alternative that implicitly captures the underlying differential equations governing environment dynamics rather than pixel-level features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The resolution invariance property—zero-shot generalization from 48×48 training images to 224×224 inference images without retraining—is a practically valuable and theoretically grounded consequence of Fourier-space parameterization, with potential implications for any vision-based RL system facing sensor upgrades or rendering changes. However, the grayscale-only limitation (the authors could not find a working method to combine RGB channels) significantly constrains real-world applicability, and the paper leaves open whether FNO encoders transfer to model-based RL, robotics, or other neural-operator variants. The finding that standard augmentations like random cropping actively degrade FNO performance suggests the learned representations are fundamentally different from CNN features, an observation that warrants deeper investigation into what FNO actually encodes.
