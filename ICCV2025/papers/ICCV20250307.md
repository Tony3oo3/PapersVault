## Problem

Multimodal LLMs lack fine-grained document grounding and referring capabilities due to two key shortcomings: (1) existing parsing annotations from OCR tools are inaccurate and fail to extract semantically coherent text from complex layouts, and (2) current instruction-tuning datasets only cover basic referring tasks (region-level OCR, summarization) without supporting grounding or integrating spatial awareness into dialogue and reasoning. No comprehensive benchmark exists to systematically evaluate these capabilities across document types.

## Input / Output

The model accepts high-resolution document images (posters, charts, PDFs) that are dynamically tiled into up to 16 patches plus a thumbnail, along with text queries and optionally user-selected regions converted to bounding box coordinates (discretized to integers 0–999) for referring tasks. Outputs are structured as grounded text wrapped in <ocr> tags with corresponding <bbox x1,y1,x2,y2> coordinates, supporting four response formats: brief grounded answers, grounded reasoning chains, grounded open-ended responses, and plain text answers. During inference, predicted bounding boxes are overlaid on the original document image for interactive visualization.

## Method

DOGR-Engine constructs 2.1M multi-granular parsing annotations (word/phrase/line/paragraph/full-page) using a Re-rendering Strategy for posters and charts (pixel-wise subtraction of re-rendered text blocks) and a Merge Strategy for PDFs (combining MinerU's reading-order layout with PyMuPDF's exhaustive extraction). GPT-4o then generates 700K instruction-tuning samples with grounded text in <ocr><bbox> format, using a Post-annotating Strategy for PDFs where the document image (not text) is fed to GPT-4o to reduce token cost, and PyMuPDF retrieves bounding boxes for extracted text. The DOGR model pairs InternViT-300M-448px (with pixel shuffle for efficient high-resolution tiling) and Qwen2-7B-Instruct in a three-stage training pipeline: pre-aligning the projector on LLaVA-558K, pre-training on 2.1M parsing data plus DocStruct4M for text reading, and fine-tuning on 2M diverse instruction-tuning samples to activate grounding and referring in dialogue and reasoning.

## Results

On DOGR-Bench (IoU threshold 0.5), DOGR achieves 73.0 F1-all on grounded answering (Ga), 52.5 on grounded reasoning (Gr), and 57.1 on grounded open-ended answering (Go), substantially outperforming the best competitor Gemini 2.5 Flash (38.8, 25.4, 21.5 respectively). On the referring task (Rt), DOGR reaches 60.3% accuracy vs. 43.0% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B and 40.7% for Gemini 2.5 Flash. On DocLocal4K, DOGR attains 86.64 IoU@0.5 (ALL) for text localization and 77.88 BLEU-4 for recognition, surpassing DocOwl-1.5 (80.38 / 70.63) and all general-purpose MLLMs by a wide margin. On 10 traditional document benchmarks, DOGR outperforms IXC-2.5-7B by +5.2 on WTQ and +25.0 CIDEr on VisualMRC while remaining competitive overall.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DOGR dominates all 10 compared baselines on every F1-all grounding metric in DOGR-Bench, with the largest gap over Gemini 2.5 Flash (38.8 F1 on Ga) by +34.2 points, and over GPT-4o (8.8 F1 on Ga) by +64.2 points. On the referring task (Rt accuracy), DOGR (60.3%) beats Qwen2.5-VL-7B (43.0%) and Gemini 2.5 Flash (40.7%) by 17.3 and 19.6 points respectively. On DocLocal4K, DOGR outperforms DocOwl-1.5 (the only other model pre-trained with bounding-box parsing data) by +6.3 IoU on ALL localization and +7.3 BLEU-4 on ALL recognition, while general-purpose MLLMs like Qwen2.5-VL-7B and GPT-4o score below 30 on both metrics. On the 10 traditional document benchmarks, DOGR surpasses IXC-2.5-7B on WTQ (58.8 vs. 53.6) and VisualMRC (332.5 vs. 307.5 CIDEr) but trails slightly on DeepForm (70.8 vs. 71.2) and TabFact (84.5 vs. 85.2), confirming that the grounding-focused training does not significantly degrade general document understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two largely separate research threads: MLLMs for visual document understanding (UReader, TextMonkey, InternVL, IXC) that handle content comprehension but lack spatial grounding, and MLLMs for grounding/referring on natural images (Kosmos-2, Shikra, Ferret, GLaMM) that cannot adapt to document layouts. Prior document-specific attempts (mPLUG-1.5, Kosmos-2.5, Fox) rely on OCR-tool-derived bounding boxes and support only basic localization or region-level OCR, without integrating grounding into multi-turn dialogue or reasoning. DOGR departs from these by using GPT-4o to generate grounded instruction-tuning data directly from multi-granular parsing annotations, and by formulating seven distinct task types that combine grounding and referring in both input and output, rather than treating them as isolated sub-tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically important finding is the stark gap between document content understanding and spatial grounding: even top proprietary models (GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro) achieve >75% accuracy on grounded answering yet score below 10 F1_all on the grounding component, indicating that current MLLMs fundamentally cannot localize text in documents despite reading it correctly. DOGR-Bench provides the first standardized evaluation for this capability, which will be essential as document AI assistants move toward interactive, region-aware interfaces. Open questions include whether the grounding gap can be closed by scaling alone or requires architectural changes, and whether the GPT-4o-assisted data generation pipeline generalizes to document types beyond posters, charts, and PDFs.
