## Problem

Existing dexterous grasp generation methods, trained on limited datasets, fail to generalize to the unbounded diversity of real-world objects and environments. More critically, they lack an evolutionary mechanism to iteratively refine grasping strategies from experience (both successes and failures) while maintaining physical plausibility, and diffusion-based approaches are too slow (hundreds of timesteps) for real-time preference-driven adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251112/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an object point cloud O ∈ R^{N×3} as input and produces dexterous grasp poses parameterized by 24 joint angles, a 3D global translation, and an SO(3) global rotation. Unlike standard diffusion-based grasp generators requiring 100+ sampling steps, EvolvingGrasp operates in 2–8 steps via a consistency model, and the evolutionary loop iteratively collects preferred/non-preferred pose samples (from simulation or human feedback) to fine-tune the model via LoRA, progressively shifting the output distribution toward human-preferred grasps.

## Method

EvolvingGrasp combines two key components: (1) Handpose-wise Preference Optimization (HPO), an extension of DPO that formulates grasp preference alignment as a posterior probability optimization problem, allowing asymmetric numbers of preferred and non-preferred samples and driving the model to reinforce successful grasps while suppressing failures; and (2) a Physics-Aware Consistency Model (PCM) that distills a pre-trained diffusion teacher into a few-step consistency student while enforcing three physical constraints (surface pulling force, external-penetration repulsion, self-penetration repulsion) during both distillation training and guided sampling. The physics-aware distillation loss augments the standard consistency distillation objective with constraint losses on the predicted x̂₀, and during sampling, constraint gradients modify the denoising mean to steer trajectories toward physically feasible poses. This architecture achieves a 30× speedup over prior diffusion-based grasp generators while enabling efficient iterative preference fine-tuning with minimal backpropagation steps.

## Results

On the 8-step setting, EvolvingGrasp achieves the best Suc.6 across all four datasets: 65.4% (DexGraspNet), 80.3% (MultiDex), 64.4% (RealDex), and 60.8% (DexGRAB), outperforming the strongest baseline DexGraspAnything by 11.8, 8.1, 29.8, and 4.3 points respectively. Penetration is also lowest on DexGraspNet (15.9 mm), RealDex (21.8 mm), and DexGRAB (22.3 mm). The method achieves a 30× speedup over DexGraspAnything (2.71 s vs. 32.91 s per batch), and even the 2-step variant (0.73 s) surpasses all baselines on Suc.6 while running 45× faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvolvingGrasp (8-step) outperforms all five baselines on Suc.6 across every dataset, with the largest margins over DexGraspAnything on RealDex (+29.8 pts) and DexGraspNet (+11.8 pts). On penetration, EvolvingGrasp achieves the lowest values on DexGraspNet (15.9 vs. 21.5 mm), RealDex (21.8 vs. 23.1 mm), and DexGRAB (22.3 vs. 28.6 mm), but DexGraspAnything retains a slight edge on MultiDex (9.6 vs. 12.3 mm). The efficiency advantage is dramatic: at 2.71 s per batch, EvolvingGrasp is ~12× faster than DexGraspAnything (32.91 s) and ~14× faster than UGG (38.34 s), while the 2-step variant runs in 0.73 s—still beating all baselines on Suc.6. Among the non-diffusion baselines, UniDexGrasp is the fastest (0.46 s) but trails EvolvingGrasp by 28–40 Suc.6 points, and GraspTTA, despite moderate speed (10.41 s), achieves the worst Suc.6 on three of four datasets. The ablation (Table 2) confirms that the physics-aware distillation (PGD) and sampling (PGS) constraints each contribute 4–8 Suc.6 points, and HPO adds a further ~1.5 points on top.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) preference optimization for generative models, extending DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) and Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., 2024) from image/text generation to dexterous grasp pose generation via the novel Handpose-wise Preference Optimization (HPO); (2) consistency model distillation (Kim et al., 2023; Lu & Song, 2024), which compresses multi-step diffusion sampling into few-step inference; and (3) physics-aware dexterous grasping (Zhong et al., 2025 / DexGraspAnything), whose three physical constraints (surface pulling, external penetration, self-penetration) are adopted here. The key departure from prior work is the 'evolutionary' framing: rather than a one-shot generation, the model iteratively refines its grasp distribution from accumulated success/failure feedback, and HPO relaxes DPO's paired-sample requirement to allow unequal numbers of preferred and non-preferred poses. The paper is, to the authors' knowledge, the first to bring DPO-style preference alignment into the dexterous grasping domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 30× speedup (0.73 s vs. 32–38 s for prior SOTA) makes preference-aligned dexterous grasping viable for real-time robotic deployment, as validated on a physical Shadow Hand. The demonstration that a deliberately degraded model (trained on noise-perturbed data) can be evolved beyond the original model's performance through iterative HPO fine-tuning suggests a practical pathway for robots to self-improve in the field without retraining from scratch. Open questions include whether the evolutionary loop scales to multi-object or cluttered scenes, how many preference iterations are needed for convergence in novel environments, and whether the physics-aware consistency distillation generalizes to hands with different kinematic structures beyond the 24-DOF Shadow Hand used here.
