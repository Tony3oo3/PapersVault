## Problem

Long-form video understanding with MLLMs is hampered by massive temporal and spatial redundancy that overwhelms limited context windows. Existing key-content extraction methods rely heavily on semantic priors (CLIP retrieval, dense captioning), making them sensitive to query specificity and prior-model errors. This paper identifies optical flow as a previously overlooked motion prior that can reduce redundancy more robustly and independently of user-provided details.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251438/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a long-form video (up to 2 hours) and a text query, operating as a training-free plug-in on top of a base video MLLM (e.g., LLaVA-Video-Qwen with 8k context, 336px resolution, 64 initial frames). It outputs a standard text answer. The key operational detail is that intra-frame token pruning increases the effective sampling frame count to maintain the same visual token budget as the unpruned model, so the LLM sees the same number of tokens but with higher information density per token.

## Method

Flow4Agent introduces two modules that exploit optical flow at different granularities. The Temporal Granularity Optimization (TGO) module first transforms frames to HSV space and computes inter-frame MSE to coarsely detect scene boundaries, then refines boundaries using coarse SeaRAFT optical flow (4 iterations) over a 3-frame window; it then selects representative events via SigLiP semantic scoring with a p-value constraint (<0.05) that minimizes the number of selected events while ensuring coverage. The Motion Token Pruning (MTP) module computes fine-grained SeaRAFT optical flow (12 iterations) between consecutive frames, compensates for global camera motion via homography estimation, multiplies by a U2-Net salient mask, and retains only tokens corresponding to the top 50% flow-magnitude pixels. This coarse-to-fine motion strategy reduces inter-frame redundancy through event-level clustering and intra-frame redundancy through pixel-level token selection, requiring no dense captions or query-specific retrieval.

## Results

Flow4Agent (7B) achieves state-of-the-art 0-shot accuracy across all six video understanding benchmarks: 84.0% on NextQA, 61.4% on EgoSchema, 69.6% on Perception-Test, 71.4% on MLVU, 60.4% on LongVideoBench, and 64.7% on VideoMME (overall). On the long-video subset of VideoMME (30–60 min), Flow4Agent scores 54.2%, outperforming LLaVA-Video by 3.6% and LongVA (224k context) by 6.6%. As a 7B open-source model, Flow4Agent surpasses GPT-4V on EgoSchema (61.4% vs. 55.6%), LongVideoBench (60.4% vs. 59.1%), and VideoMME overall (64.7% vs. 60.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Flow4Agent outperforms the strongest open-source 7B baseline, Apollo, by +2.3 on Perception-Test (69.6 vs. 67.3), +0.5 on MLVU (71.4 vs. 70.9), +1.9 on LongVideoBench (60.4 vs. 58.5), and +3.4 on VideoMME overall (64.7 vs. 61.3). Against its base model LLaVA-Video, Flow4Agent gains +4.1 on EgoSchema (61.4 vs. 57.3), +3.6 on VideoMME-Long (54.2 vs. 50.6), and +2.2 on LongVideoBench (60.4 vs. 58.2), confirming the plug-in nature of the method. On long-video benchmarks, Flow4Agent surpasses LLaVA-OneVision by +7.5 on VideoMME-Long (54.2 vs. 46.7) and LongVA (224k context) by +6.6 (54.2 vs. 47.6), demonstrating that motion-guided redundancy removal is more effective than simply extending context. Compared to key-content extraction methods built on GPT-4 (VideoAgent, VideoTree, LVNet), Flow4Agent as a 7B model still leads on NextQA (84.0 vs. 71.3–75.6) and EgoSchema (61.4 vs. 54.1–61.1). Notably, Flow4Agent exceeds GPT-4V on EgoSchema (+5.8), LongVideoBench (+1.3), and VideoMME overall (+4.0), highlighting the benefit of explicit motion priors over purely semantic retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

Flow4Agent builds on the LLaVA-Video architecture as its base MLLM but departs fundamentally from the dominant paradigm of semantic-prior-driven keyframe extraction (e.g., VideoAgent, VideoTree, LVNet) that relies on CLIP retrieval or dense captioning to identify relevant video content. Instead, it introduces optical flow as a motion prior for LLM-based video understanding for the first time, using coarse flow (Sea-RAFT with few iterations) for temporal event segmentation and fine-grained flow for intra-frame token pruning. This positions the work at the intersection of classical video analysis (optical flow, HSV-based shot detection) and modern multimodal LLM pipelines, repurposing a well-studied signal in a context where it had not been directly applied.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-in nature of Flow4Agent makes it immediately applicable to a wide range of existing video MLLMs without retraining, and the consistent gains across LLaVA-NeXT, LLaVA-OneVision, Qwen2-VL, and LLaVA-Video (7B and 72B) demonstrate broad practical utility. A key limitation is that the method still depends on a semantic prior (SigLIP) for event selection, and the optical flow computation adds non-trivial inference latency, which may constrain real-time applications. Open questions include whether motion priors can be learned end-to-end within the MLLM rather than computed externally, and whether the approach degrades gracefully for videos with minimal motion (e.g., static presentations or screen recordings).
