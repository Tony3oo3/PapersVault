## Problem

Visual-only speech recognition (lip reading) is hampered by viseme ambiguity—where distinct phonemes produce nearly identical lip shapes—and coarticulation effects that blur phoneme boundaries. Existing approaches either predict words directly from visual features (incurring high error rates) or depend on large-scale self-supervised pre-training on thousands of hours of unlabeled video, making them data-hungry and less interpretable. This paper asks whether an explicit phoneme-level intermediate representation, combined with a fine-tuned LLM, can resolve these ambiguities with far less labeled data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252313/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 16 temporally sampled video frames of a speaker's face, cropped to a 224×224 region centered on the lips via MediaPipe face detection, with 50% random pixel masking applied during training for regularization. The first stage outputs a variable-length sequence over 39 English phonemes plus a CTC blank symbol; the second stage maps that phoneme sequence to a natural-language sentence. The phoneme output is an explicit, interpretable intermediate that can be inspected independently of the final text.

## Method

VALLR decomposes lip reading into two independently trained stages. Stage 1 uses a ViT-base encoder to extract per-frame features from the 16 face crops, followed by a six-layer 1D-convolutional adapter that downsamples the temporal dimension from 1568 to 8 timesteps, and a CTC head that predicts the phoneme sequence without requiring frame-level alignment. Stage 2 fine-tunes a Llama 3.2-3B LLM via LoRA on phoneme-to-sentence pairs generated from WikiText using the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary, learning to reconstruct coherent words from (potentially noisy) phoneme inputs. The key design choice is constraining the LLM's role to phoneme→sentence reconstruction rather than visual→text translation, which eliminates the need for expensive visual-text alignment and reduces the visual model's target space to just 38 classes. This yields SOTA WER of 18.7% on LRS3 using only 30 hours of labeled video—99.4% less than the next best approach—without any self-supervised pre-training.

## Results

On LRS3, VALLR achieves a WER of 18.7% using only 30 hours of labelled video data and no self-supervised pre-training, outperforming all supervised-only baselines (best: VTP at 40.6%) and all self-supervised pre-training methods (best: USR at 21.5%). On LRS2, the method achieves 20.8% WER with just 28 hours of training data, beating fully supervised baselines (Auto-AV-SR at 27.9%) and several self-supervised approaches (ES3 at 26.7%, Sub-Word at 22.6%), though it trails USR (15.4%) and RAVEn (17.9%) which use 1,759 hours of unlabelled pre-training data. The LLM ablation shows that scaling from GPT-2 Small (0.12B, WER 33.9%) to Llama 3.2-3B (3.0B, WER 18.7%) yields a 15.2-point WER reduction, confirming the critical role of LLM capacity in phoneme-to-sentence reconstruction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On LRS3, VALLR (18.7% WER) decisively outperforms all fully supervised public-data baselines, beating the best (VTP, 40.6%) by 21.9 points, and surpasses every self-supervised pre-training method including USR (21.5%), RAVEn (23.1%), LLaMA-AVSR (24.0%), and Whisper (25.5%), despite using no unlabelled pre-training data at all. The method also outperforms the end-to-end Sub-Word approach [41] (30.7%) by 12 points, confirming the benefit of the explicit phoneme intermediary. On LRS2, VALLR (20.8%) beats Sub-Word (22.6%), ES3 (26.7%), and Auto-AV-SR with 818 hrs (27.9%), but trails USR (15.4%) and RAVEn (17.9%), both of which leverage 1,759 hours of unlabelled audio-visual pre-training. Against non-public-data methods on LRS3 (LP at 12.5%, ViT-3D at 17.0%), VALLR remains competitive given it uses 99.4% less labelled data, but does not match their absolute performance. The two-stage design is validated by the end-to-end ablation: removing the phoneme intermediary and training ViT+LLM directly yields 25.6% WER, a 6.9-point degradation, confirming that the phoneme bottleneck is essential for data efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revives the phoneme-based lip-reading line of work (El-Bialy et al., Thangthai et al.) that was previously outpaced by end-to-end sequence-to-sequence and CTC approaches, but modernizes it by coupling a ViT+CTC phoneme predictor with a LoRA-fine-tuned LLM for phoneme-to-sentence reconstruction. It departs from the dominant self-supervised pre-training paradigm (AV-HuBERT, LiteVSR, Lip2Vec) by demonstrating that a compact supervised phoneme intermediate representation, combined with an LLM trained on text-only phoneme-sentence pairs, can match or exceed methods requiring thousands of hours of unlabeled video. Unlike recent LLM-based lip-reading pipelines (VSP-LLM, personalized lip reading) that map raw visual features directly into LLM text embeddings, this work constrains the LLM's role to the well-defined phoneme→sentence task, avoiding expensive visual-text alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in data efficiency: achieving 18.7% WER on LRS3 with only 30 hours of labeled video and no self-supervised pre-training challenges the assumption that large-scale unlabeled video is necessary for competitive V-ASR, which is important for resource-constrained or privacy-sensitive deployments. The modular two-stage design also offers interpretability—phoneme-level errors are explicit and localizable—addressing hallucination concerns critical for accessibility applications. However, the approach remains limited by inherent visual ambiguity (e.g., /s/ vs /z/, homophones like 'your' vs 'you're'), and it is unclear whether the phoneme-intermediate strategy generalizes to languages with larger phoneme inventories or to noisy, in-the-wild conditions beyond curated TED/BBC footage.
