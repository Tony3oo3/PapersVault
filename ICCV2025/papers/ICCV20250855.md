## Problem

While pre-trained models are widely used as backbones for downstream tasks, it remains unclear how their latent spaces structure domain-specific versus class-specific information, and how to exploit this structure for domain generalization without access to domain labels. This paper investigates whether diffusion model features, trained with a class-agnostic denoising objective, naturally encode domain-specific variations (photographic styles, textures, spatial compositions) that can complement standard classifiers to improve generalization to unseen domains.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces a class label. At inference, the image is processed by two frozen encoders in parallel: a ResNet-50 classifier backbone (Φ) for class features and a diffusion model (DiT or SD-2.1) for domain features. The diffusion features are used only to assign the image to its nearest pseudo-domain cluster centroid (learned during training), which is then transformed and concatenated with the ResNet features before the final classification head—no domain labels are needed at test time.

## Method

GUIDE operates in two stages. First, it performs unsupervised pseudo-domain discovery by extracting features from a frozen diffusion model (Ψ), applying K-Means++ clustering to partition the feature space into K pseudo-domains, and using each cluster centroid as a compact domain representation. Second, it augments a standard ERM classifier by concatenating each sample's ResNet-50 features (Φ) with the RBF-kernel-ridge-regressed pseudo-domain centroid (mapped from Ψ's space to Φ's space via a transformation T updated on a logarithmic schedule). The key insight is that diffusion models' class-agnostic denoising objective causes their latent spaces to separate domain-specific variations (style, texture, spatial composition) rather than class identity, making them ideal complementary signals. DiT (transformer-based) excels at capturing global stylistic differences, while SD-2.1 (U-Net-based) captures fine-grained spatial variations, and the best-performing variant (GUIDE-BEST) selects the superior diffusion feature space per dataset.

## Results

On the DomainBed test suite (5 datasets: VLCS, PACS, OfficeHome, TerraIncognita, DomainNet), GUIDE-BEST achieves the highest average test accuracy of 66.3% without using domain labels at any point, outperforming the ERM baseline (63.7%) by +2.6% and the strongest label-free method MIRO (65.9%) by +0.4%. The largest per-dataset gains are on TerraIncognita (+4.3% over ERM, reaching 51.3%) and PACS (+3.3% over ERM, reaching 87.1%). When combined with ERM++ training strategies, GUIDE++ further improves to 89.2% on PACS and 53.6% on TerraIncognita, surpassing ERM++ by +5.4% and +6.6% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GUIDE-BEST (66.3% avg) outperforms ERM (63.7%) by +2.6% and is the best method that uses neither multi-layer features nor domain labels. Among label-free methods, GUIDE-BEST beats MIRO (65.9%) by +0.4%, LatentDR-SA (65.7%) by +0.6%, AdaClust (64.9%) by +1.4%, and Mixup (63.4%) by +2.9%. Among domain-adaptive classifier frameworks specifically, GUIDE-BEST surpasses DA-ERM (64.1%) by +2.2% and AdaClust (64.9%) by +1.4%. On individual datasets, GUIDE-BEST wins on PACS (87.1 vs. 86.3 for SagNet/LatentDR-Pool), TerraIncognita (51.3 vs. 50.4 for MIRO), and DomainNet (45.9 vs. 45.1 for LatentDR-SA), but trails MIRO on VLCS (78.5 vs. 79.0) and OfficeHome (68.6 vs. 70.5). Notably, GUIDE achieves these gains with default DomainBed hyperparameters and a single-layer feature extractor, whereas most competing methods require extensive hyperparameter tuning, multi-layer features, or explicit domain labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

GUIDE extends the pseudo-domain augmentation framework of Dubey et al. [16] and Thomas et al. [66] (AdaClust), which concatenate discovered domain representations with input features for a domain-adaptive classifier. The key departure is the source of pseudo-domain representations: rather than learning a separate domain prototype network or relying on explicit domain labels, GUIDE extracts features from frozen pre-trained diffusion models (DiT, SD-2.1) and clusters them unsupervised via K-Means++. This positions the work as the first to exploit frozen diffusion latent spaces in an unsupervised manner for domain generalization, contrasting with prior diffusion-for-DG approaches that fine-tune diffusion models for synthetic data augmentation or require test-time access.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a useful empirical taxonomy of how different pre-training objectives (contrastive, masked reconstruction, self-distillation, denoising) shape the domain-vs-class structure of latent spaces, offering practitioners guidance on which frozen features to pair with a classifier for robustness. The finding that diffusion features naturally separate domains without any class-driven incentive is a conceptual insight that could inform future foundation model design. Limitations include the heuristic choice of cluster count K, the computational overhead of a second feature extractor, and the fact that gains over ERM are modest (1.9–4.3%); open questions include whether combining multiple diffusion architectures or adapting the clustering to dataset-specific structure could yield larger improvements.
