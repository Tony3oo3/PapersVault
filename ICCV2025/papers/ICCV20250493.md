## Problem

4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS) achieves photorealistic dynamic scene rendering but requires millions of 4D Gaussians, each carrying 161 parameters (144 of which are 4D spherical harmonics coefficients), resulting in multi-gigabyte storage that is impractical for AR/VR and other resource-constrained deployments. No prior work had developed a systematic framework to compress 4DGS representations while preserving rendering quality and real-time speed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250493/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Training consumes multi-view video sequences with synchronized camera poses (Technicolor at 2048×1088, Neu3DV at 2704×2028 half-resolution) and produces a compact 4D Gaussian model stored in FP16 with zip delta compression. At inference, the compressed model accepts a scalar time query and a camera viewpoint to render a novel view via temporal slicing, projection, and differentiable rasterization. The final artifact is roughly 190× smaller on Technicolor and 125× smaller on Neu3DV compared to the original 4DGS while sustaining comparable FPS.

## Method

MEGA replaces the 144-parameter 4D spherical harmonics color with a DC-AC decomposition: a 3-parameter per-Gaussian DC color plus a shared three-layer MLP AC predictor that takes stop-gradient 3D position, view direction, time, and DC color to output the final RGB, yielding an ~8× per-Gaussian parameter reduction. To reduce the total Gaussian count, a temporal-viewpoint aware deformation MLP (with frequency positional encoding) predicts per-Gaussian position, scale, and rotation offsets that expand each Gaussian's effective action range, while an opacity-based entropy loss pushes spatial opacity toward binary values and periodic pruning removes near-zero-opacity Gaussians, raising the rendering participation rate from under 50% to ~75%. Training uses the standard photometric loss (L1 + SSIM) plus the entropy term, with half-precision optimization; the final model is serialized in FP16 and further compressed via zip delta encoding for an additional ~10% size reduction.

## Results

On the Technicolor dataset, MEGA achieves a PSNR of 33.57 dB, DSSIM₁ of 0.0442, DSSIM₂ of 0.0204, and LPIPS of 0.1014, while rendering at 83.14 FPS with only 32.45 MB of storage for 50 frames. This represents a 190× storage reduction compared to the original 4DGS (6107.07 MB) while simultaneously improving PSNR by 1.5 dB and rendering speed by 50%. On the Neural 3D Video dataset, MEGA achieves a 125× compression ratio (25.05 MB vs. 3128.00 MB for 4DGS) with near-identical quality (PSNR 31.49 vs. 31.57 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MEGA decisively outperforms the primary baseline 4DGS [46] on all quality metrics (PSNR +1.5 dB, DSSIM₁ −0.009, DSSIM₂ −0.006, LPIPS −0.018) while reducing storage by 190× (6107 MB → 32.45 MB) and increasing rendering speed by 50% (55 → 83 FPS). Against the strongest Gaussian-based competitor STG [23], MEGA achieves a 0.22 dB PSNR advantage and 37% less storage (32.45 vs. 51.35 MB), though STG retains an edge on DSSIM₁ (0.0404 vs. 0.0442), LPIPS (0.0846 vs. 0.1014), and FPS (141.73 vs. 83.14). Compared to the NeRF-based HyperReel [1], MEGA gains 0.87 dB in PSNR, renders 20× faster (83 vs. 4 FPS), and uses half the storage. MEGA also surpasses Deformable 3DGS [44] and E-D3DGS [2] on all quality metrics and storage. On the Neu3DV dataset, MEGA matches 4DGS quality almost exactly (PSNR 31.49 vs. 31.57) at 125× lower storage, but still trails STG [23] on PSNR (31.49 vs. 32.04) and LPIPS (0.0568 vs. 0.0440).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 4DGS [46], which represents dynamic scenes as anisotropic 4D Gaussians with 4D spherical harmonics for view- and time-dependent color. The key departure is replacing the 144-parameter SH coefficients with a 3-parameter per-Gaussian DC color plus a shared lightweight MLP (AC predictor), drawing an analogy to DC/AC decomposition in electrical engineering. The entropy-constrained deformation field extends the deformable 3DGS line of work [2, 44, 45] by coupling a temporal-viewpoint deformation network with an opacity entropy loss that actively prunes redundant Gaussians, whereas prior deformable methods did not explicitly constrain Gaussian count. The compression pipeline (FP16 + zip delta) is deliberately simple, distinguishing it from learned 3DGS compression approaches [11, 20, 34, 42] that target static scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~190× storage reduction (7.79 GB → 32 MB on Technicolor) while simultaneously improving PSNR over the uncompressed 4DGS baseline makes 4DGS practically deployable on resource-constrained AR/VR headsets, which was previously infeasible. The finding that only ~6% of 4DGS Gaussians are active at any given time, and that the deformation + entropy loss combination raises participation to ~75%, reveals a fundamental inefficiency in the original formulation that other 4DGS variants may share. Open questions include whether the shared AC predictor generalizes to longer sequences or more extreme viewpoint changes, whether the approach extends to other 4DGS variants (e.g., 4D-rotor GS [10]), and whether the per-scene training requirement can be relaxed toward feed-forward or streaming settings.
