## Problem

Existing VLM jailbreak methods either require white-box access for adversarial optimization or depend on manually crafted pipelines that lack diversity and scalability. There is no automated, black-box framework that can autonomously generate diverse, contextually rich multimodal jailbreak prompts at scale, limiting comprehensive safety evaluation of VLMs against realistic adversarial threats.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251419/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single harmful text goal (e.g., one of 100 sampled from AdvBench's 520 dangerous/illegal activity goals) as input and produces an adversarial image-text pair as output. The attacker VLM emits a structured JSON containing three fields—analysis (CoT reasoning), image prompt, and text prompt—where the image prompt is fed to Stable Diffusion 3 Medium to synthesize the jailbreak image. The attack operates in a multi-turn setting where the attacker VLM retains full conversation history while the victim VLM only sees the current image-text input, and the breadth-depth exploration (N_b=7, N_d=3) generates up to 21 candidate pairs per goal.

## Method

IDEATOR leverages an open-source VLM (MiniGPT-4 with Vicuna-13B) as a red-team agent that iteratively crafts jailbreak strategies through structured JSON outputs, guided by a system prompt that configures it as a red team assistant with in-context learning exemplars. The key mechanism is a breadth-depth exploration strategy: multiple parallel attack streams (breadth) each undergo iterative refinement (depth) where the attacker VLM analyzes the victim's prior response via Chain-of-Thought reasoning in the analysis field, then generates refined text and image prompts for the next round. A diffusion model (Stable Diffusion 3 Medium) converts the generated image prompts into visually subtle jailbreak images that employ typographic manipulations and cartoon-style visuals to conceal malicious intent. The entire pipeline is training-free and black-box, requiring only API-level access to the victim VLM, and the combined image-text modality achieves higher ASR (94%) with fewer queries (5.34) than either modality alone.

## Results

IDEATOR achieves a 94% attack success rate (ASR) on AdvBench's 100 harmful-behavior samples against MiniGPT-4, matching the best white-box method (UMK, 94%) while being fully black-box and training-free, with an average of only 5.34 queries per successful jailbreak. Transferred to other VLMs, IDEATOR's samples yield ASRs of 82% (LLaVA), 88% (InstructBLIP), and 75% (Chameleon), far exceeding the black-box baseline MM-SafetyBench (46%, 29%, 22%). On the VLJailbreakBench challenge set (3,654 samples), the most robust evaluated model, Claude-3.5-Sonnet, still suffers a 19.65% ASR, while GPT-4o reaches 46.31%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDEATOR matches the strongest white-box baseline UMK (94% ASR) while operating entirely in a black-box, training-free setting, a regime in which no prior method reaches above 66%. Against the text-only GCG baseline (50%), IDEATOR gains 44 percentage points; against the vision-adapted GCG-V (85%), it gains 9 points; and against VAJM (68%), it gains 26 points. The only other black-box and training-free method, MM-SafetyBench, achieves 66% ASR on the target model, so IDEATOR outperforms it by 28 points. In cross-model transfer (Table 2), IDEATOR's samples generated on MiniGPT-4 achieve 82%/88%/75% ASR on LLaVA/InstructBLIP/Chameleon, roughly doubling or tripling MM-SafetyBench's transfer ASRs of 46%/29%/22%, confirming that IDEATOR's contextually rich image-text pairs generalize far better than manually crafted typographic attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the red-teaming paradigm from LLMs (Chao et al.) to the multimodal setting by using a VLM itself as the adversarial agent, combined with a diffusion model for image generation. It departs from prior VLM jailbreak work in two key ways: unlike white-box methods (GCG, VAJM, UMK) that require gradient access and produce semantically meaningless adversarial perturbations, IDEATOR is black-box and training-free; unlike manually crafted pipelines (MM-SafetyBench, FigStep, Visual-Roleplay) that rely on human-engineered templates, IDEATOR autonomously generates diverse, contextually rich image-text pairs through iterative CoT reasoning. The breadth-depth exploration strategy is a novel scheduling mechanism that balances diverse attack discovery with iterative refinement, drawing on the multi-arm bandit intuition of exploring multiple strategies simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VLJailbreakBench (3,654 samples, 12 safety topics, 11 VLMs) fills a critical gap: prior benchmarks either target explicit harmful content or focus on text-only transfer, leaving multimodal adversarial robustness largely unmeasured. The finding that even GPT-4o (46.31% ASR) and Claude-3.5-Sonnet (19.65% ASR) remain vulnerable to sophisticated multimodal jailbreaks challenges the perception that frontier commercial models are adequately protected. Key limitations include the benchmark's relatively small scale, the dependence on attacker model quality (weakly aligned but capable VLMs are needed), and the fact that the challenge set was generated using GPT-4o-mini, which inflates its ASR. Open questions include whether defenses can generalize across the diverse attack strategies IDEATOR discovers, and whether the benchmark can be expanded with automated filtering at scale.
