## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation methods for medical image segmentation rely on parametric updates (BN-centric or full-model fine-tuning) that are ineffective for LN-based foundation models like MedSAM, risk catastrophic forgetting in large architectures, and incur heavy computational costs from repeated forward/backward passes through the image encoder. This paper shows that directly refining the image embedding (latent refinement) can achieve the same posterior-maximization objective as parametric updates under MedSAM's no-skip-connection architecture, while being forgetting-free and computationally efficient.

## Input / Output

The method takes a medical image (retinal fundus RGB or spinal cord MRI) together with a fixed bounding box prompt as input and produces a binary segmentation mask of the region of interest. The bounding box prompt is used both to condition the initial MedSAM inference and to construct the K-random pair graph for the DAL-CRF loss, restricting co-occurrence regularization to the prompted region. Each latent vector corresponds to a 16×16 receptive field in the input space (L=256 intensity vectors), which is the granularity at which distributional statistics are computed.

## Method

The key theoretical insight is that MedSAM's architecture (no skip connections between encoder E_θ and decoder D_φ) implies Y ⊥ X | Z, so the MAP objective factorizes into log P(Y|Z) + log P(Z|X), and optimizing the latent embedding Z directly is equivalent to parametric updates. To maximize the latent fidelity term log P(Z|X) without invoking the image encoder, the paper proposes a Distribution-Approximated Latent CRF (DAL-CRF) loss that measures MMD between empirical distributions of intensity vectors in each latent vector's receptive field, weighted by an exponential Frobenius-norm similarity in latent space, encouraging discriminative latent representations that retain input-space statistics. The segmentation likelihood log P(Y|Z) is implicitly maximized via a foreground-only entropy minimization loss with a high confidence threshold (α=0.95) that also serves as implicit volumetric regularization. The refined latent is fed directly to the frozen mask decoder without re-running the image encoder, yielding ~7× GFLOPs reduction compared to parametric-update baselines while achieving ~3% Dice improvements across three multi-center datasets.

## Results

On three multi-center medical image segmentation datasets (optic cup, optic disc, and spinal cord gray matter), the proposed latent refinement method achieves average Dice scores of 90.84 (OC), 84.19 (OD), and 59.49 (SCGM), representing approximately 3% improvement over direct inference (87.77, 80.57, 56.67). The method requires only 97.3 GFLOPs during adaptation, a roughly 7× reduction compared to the most efficient parametric-update baseline (TENT at 743.5 GFLOPs) and up to 58× less than MEMO (5646.5 GFLOPs). The method also achieves the best Average Surface Distance in most settings, e.g., 7.77 vs. 8.33 for direct inference on OC with looseness prompts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed latent refinement method outperforms all six baselines across all three datasets. Against normalization-based TTA methods, the gains are most pronounced: over PASS (84.05/71.81/54.80), the method improves by +6.79/+12.38/+4.69 Dice on OC/OD/SCGM, and over TENT (86.24/78.08/54.34) by +4.60/+6.11/+5.15. Against full-update methods, the advantage is more moderate but consistent: +1.89/+4.40/+2.01 over MEMO and +1.89/+4.40/+2.68 over CRF-SOD. Notably, several baselines (TENT, InTEnt, PASS) actually degrade below direct inference on SCGM, confirming the paper's argument that BN-centric or full-parameter updates are poorly suited to LN-based foundation models. The computational advantage is dramatic: 97.3 GFLOPs versus 743.5–5646.5 GFLOPs for baselines, a 7–58× reduction, because the method avoids any forward/backward pass through the ViT image encoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant TTA paradigm for medical segmentation (BN-centric partial updates like TENT/InTEnt/PASS, or full-model updates like MEMO/CRF-SOD) by proposing latent refinement—optimizing the image embedding Z directly while freezing all model parameters. Theoretically, it builds on the conditional independence structure inherent to MedSAM's encoder-decoder architecture (no skip connections) to show that refining Z achieves the same factorized MAP objective as parametric updates. Methodologically, it repurposes the latent refinement concept from neural image compression and extends CRF-based regularization from the label-input space (Veksler's CRF-SOD) to the latent-input space, replacing Gaussian-kernel pairwise potentials with MMD-based distribution comparison over receptive fields.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~7× GFLOPs reduction is practically significant for deploying foundation medical segmentation on resource-constrained edge devices, and the forgetting-free property eliminates a key failure mode of parametric TTA on large models. However, the approach is tightly coupled to MedSAM's specific architecture (no skip connections, bounding-box prompts, ViT-B encoder), and generalization to other foundation models (e.g., 3D SAM variants, multi-modal encoders) or to architectures with skip connections remains unexplored. The single-step optimization and 2D-only evaluation also leave open whether the gains hold under multi-step refinement or in volumetric settings.
