## Problem

Cross-architecture knowledge distillation (CAKD) between heterogeneous model families (CNNs, ViTs, MLPs) remains underexplored, and the pioneering method OFA requires architecture-tailored projection modules (depth-wise separable convolutions for CNNs, attention blocks for ViTs/MLPs) that introduce parameter overheads up to 3× the student model size. The paper asks whether CAKD can be achieved with a simple, architecture-agnostic objective that extracts shared knowledge without these costly, bespoke designs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251414/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 on CIFAR-100, 224×224 on ImageNet-1k) and outputs class labels. Distillation operates on the penultimate-layer 1-D embeddings of both teacher and student, which are always dimensionally uniform regardless of architecture type, eliminating the need for architecture-specific feature reconciliation. A lightweight AAD module (two FC layers with BatchNorm and GeLU) aligns the student embedding dimension to the teacher's at training time and is discarded at inference, so the student produces standard class predictions with no additional overhead.

## Method

RSD formulates knowledge transfer as redundancy suppression: it constructs a D×D Pearson correlation matrix P between batch-normalized teacher and student penultimate-layer embeddings, then drives the diagonal to 1 (cross-architecture invariance maximisation) and the off-diagonal to 0 (feature decorrelation), effectively extracting the architecture-agnostic commonality while suppressing teacher-specific patterns. A lightweight Architecture-Agnostic Knowledge Decoupler (AAD) module buffers the student's internal representations from direct RSD dictation, preserving student-exclusive inductive biases (e.g., CNN locality) that would otherwise be overridden. The full training objective is simply CE + λ·RSD_loss, implementable in ~8 lines of PyTorch, with no architecture-specific operations, memory banks, or multi-stage feature access. This contrasts with OFA, which projects intermediate features from four network stages through separate architecture-tailored projectors into logit space, incurring quadrupled parameter complexity.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, RSD achieves an average gain of +10.69% over the student baseline across 12 heterogeneous teacher-student pairs, substantially outperforming OFA (+7.47%) and all other baselines. The largest individual margin is 6.70% on ConvNeXt-T→DeiT-T (82.46% vs. 75.76% for OFA). On ImageNet-1k, RSD achieves an average gain of +2.34% over 15 pairs, edging out OFA (+2.20%) and KD (+1.55%), with the most notable win being ConvNeXt-T→ResMLP-S12 at 78.41% vs. OFA's 77.53%. RSD also uses up to 10× fewer extra parameters than OFA while delivering these gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RSD outperforms all 13 baselines on both CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-1k. Against the primary cross-architecture baseline OFA [22], RSD leads by an average of 3.22% on CIFAR-100 (e.g., 83.92% vs. 80.54% for Swin-T→ResNet18) and 0.14% on ImageNet-1k, while using up to 10× fewer extra parameters. Among logit-based methods, RSD surpasses KD [25] by 7.52% and DKD [78] by 7.53% on CIFAR-100, and by 0.79% and 1.05% on ImageNet-1k respectively. Among feature-based methods, RSD dominates FitNets [49] (which sometimes collapses, e.g., 24.06% on ConvNeXt-T→Swin-P), CC [45], RKD [44], and CRD [53] by wide margins on CIFAR-100. On the same-architecture ImageNet-1k evaluation (Table 8), RSD (72.18% for ResNet34→ResNet18; 73.08% for ResNet50→MobileNetV1) also beats OFA (72.10%), SDD [65] (71.14%/72.24%), DKD [78] (71.70%/72.05%), and DIST [26] (72.07%/73.24%), confirming its generality beyond the cross-architecture setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from OFA [22], the pioneering cross-architecture distillation method, by rejecting its architecture-specific projectors and logit-space projection in favour of a simple Pearson-correlation-based loss applied directly to penultimate-layer embeddings. Conceptually, it repurposes redundancy reduction principles from unsupervised feature learning (Barlow [3], Deco & Parra [11]) and the Information Bottleneck framework [54, 55] to the distillation setting, framing cross-architecture knowledge transfer as suppression of architecture-exclusive information. The lightweight AAD decoupler module is a deliberate contrast to OFA's per-architecture projection networks, drawing instead on the one-size-fits-all adaptor idea from FitNets [49] but applied to a fundamentally different objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RSD provides a simple, parameter-efficient baseline (up to 10× fewer extra parameters than OFA) that outperforms the state of the art on both CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-1k across diverse CNN/Transformer/MLP pairs, making cross-architecture distillation more accessible and practical. However, the method is limited to 1-D penultimate embeddings, which precludes direct extension to spatially demanding tasks like detection, and its gains are less prominent on ImageNet than CIFAR-100. Open questions include whether the redundancy suppression principle can be extended to 2-D feature maps, whether it generalises to other modalities, and how to reduce the observed sensitivity to the balancing hyperparameters λ and κ.
