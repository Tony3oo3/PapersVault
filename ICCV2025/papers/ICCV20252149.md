## Problem

Existing continual deepfake detection methods treat stability (retaining prior forgery knowledge) and plasticity (adapting to new forgeries) as inherently conflicting, relying on replay-based strategies that store historical data and still suffer from accumulative forgetting due to inter-task distribution shifts. These approaches also neglect generalization to unseen forgery domains, treating it as secondary to the stability-plasticity trade-off. The paper asks whether stability and plasticity can be jointly improved by explicitly preserving the model's generalization capacity during incremental updates.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 299×299 RGB face crops (extracted via RetinaFace) and outputs a 2-class real/fake probability vector. A key operational constraint is that during incremental stages, only 25 real and 25 fake videos per dataset are available (no replay buffer), and the Xception backbone and classifier remain frozen throughout — only the lightweight watermark generator (a small conv net plus a shared watermark tensor) is updated. This means the model adapts to new forgery types by modifying input-level perturbations rather than internal representations.

## Method

GPL comprises two components operating on a frozen Xception backbone. (1) Hyperbolic Visual Alignment (HVA): a lightweight watermark generator overlays learnable, sample-specific perturbations (attention map × shared watermark) on input images, then a contrastive loss in the Poincaré ball pulls incremental-sample features toward the nearest same-class K-means cluster center (computed from the base set) while pushing them away from opposite-class centers, using hyperbolic distance to exploit hierarchical structure. (2) Generalized Gradient Projection (GGP): the base-aligned gradient is computed via KL divergence between frozen and learnable watermark outputs; if the angle between the new-task gradient and this base gradient exceeds 90°, the conflicting component is orthogonally projected out, preventing updates that would erode established generalization boundaries. Together, these mechanisms eliminate the need for backbone retraining or historical data storage while simultaneously improving accuracy (92.14% AA) and reducing forgetting (1.42% AF) over replay-based SOTA.

## Results

On the four-dataset continual deepfake detection protocol (FF++ → DFDCP → DFD → CDF), GPL achieves an average accuracy (AA) of 92.14% and average forgetting (AF) of 1.42%, outperforming the best replay-based baseline DMP (AA 89.99%, AF 4.08%) by 2.15% in accuracy and 2.66% in forgetting reduction. On unseen forgery domains (DFD and CDF tested after training only on FF++ and DFDCP), GPL reaches 82.84% average accuracy versus 64.46% for DFIL, an 18.38% improvement, while using only 1% of the trainable parameters of the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPL outperforms all five baselines on both AA and AF across the four-dataset sequence. Against the strongest replay-based competitor DMP (AA 89.99%, AF 4.08%), GPL gains +2.15% AA and reduces AF by 2.66 points. DFIL (AA 85.49%, AF 7.01%) and CoReD (AA 81.21%, AF 11.42%) show progressively larger gaps, with CoReD suffering severe forgetting on the final CDF test set (80.78% vs. GPL's 92.16%). The general continual-learning baselines DGR (AA 75.45%) and LwF (AA 76.68%) are substantially weaker, with LwF dropping to 41.78% on DFDCP after the DFD stage. On unseen forgery generalization (Table 5), GPL achieves 82.84% average accuracy on DFD and CDF versus 64.46% for DFIL, a 18.38% absolute gain, while training only the lightweight watermark generator (~1% of DFIL's trainable parameters).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Visual Reprogramming paradigm (Cai et al., NeurIPS 2024) to continual deepfake detection, repurposing a lightweight watermark generator as the sole trainable component while keeping a frozen Xception backbone. It departs from the dominant replay-based continual deepfake detection line (DFIL, CoReD, DMP, HDP) by eliminating historical data storage entirely, instead relying on hyperbolic contrastive alignment (drawing on Poincaré-ball embeddings from Nickel & Kiela) and a gradient projection mechanism reminiscent of EWC-style regularization. The key conceptual departure is reframing the stability–plasticity trade-off as a generalization problem: rather than treating retention and adaptation as competing objectives, the authors argue both are jointly improved by preserving the model's ability to generalize to unseen forgeries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The replay-free, parameter-efficient design (only ~1% of baseline parameters trainable) addresses a practical bottleneck in deployment: storing historical deepfake samples raises privacy and storage concerns that are non-trivial in real-world forensic pipelines. The 18.38% accuracy gain on unseen forgery domains is the most practically significant result, suggesting that generalization preservation during incremental training transfers to truly novel manipulation techniques. However, the evaluation is confined to four datasets, a single backbone (Xception), and binary real/fake classification; whether the hyperbolic alignment and gradient projection strategy generalizes to multi-class forgery typologies, video-level detection, or substantially different architectures remains open. The modest hyperbolic-vs-Euclidean gap (~3% AA) also raises the question of whether the geometric contribution is essential or merely complementary to the watermarking mechanism.
