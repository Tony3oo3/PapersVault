## Problem

State-of-the-art deep learning methods for 2D reassembly fail on real-world fresco fragments due to three coupled limitations: they cannot scale to irregular shapes with many contour points (e.g., PuzzleFusion runs out of memory), they do not jointly exploit geometric and textural cues, and they require large annotated datasets unavailable in heritage domains. ReassembleNet targets all three gaps simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252104/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes m unordered 2D fresco fragment images with arbitrary irregular polygonal boundaries as input. Keypoints are first extracted via Harris Corner Detector, then reduced to a fixed set of k=20 selected keypoints per piece. The output is a per-piece 2D translation vector and a rotation encoded as [cos θ, sin θ] (following Zhou et al. for continuity), from which the final reassembled arrangement is recovered by averaging the polygon center and rotation across each piece's keypoints.

## Method

ReassembleNet combines three components: (1) a learnable keypoint selector that frames contour sparsification as a graph problem, using a Graph Transformer followed by a top-k pooling layer gated by a learnable projection vector, pre-trained unsupervised to preserve both area and perimeter of the original shape; (2) multi-modal per-keypoint features fusing geometric attributes (boundary curvature, tangent edge angle) with global texture (ResNet-18 on the full piece) and local texture (ResNet-18 on 32×32 patches centered at each keypoint); and (3) a DDIM diffusion process that iteratively denoises piece poses (translation + rotation), where the denoising network uses sparse self-attention within each piece and standard self-attention across pieces to predict the clean pose X̂₀. A semi-synthetic dataset (5000 samples) generated by adding erosion and random rigid transforms to the PairingNet segmentation pipeline is used for pre-training, then fine-tuned on the 97-sample RePAIR training split to bridge the sim-to-real gap.

## Results

On the RePAIR benchmark, ReassembleNet with learnable keypoint selection and fine-tuning achieves the best overall performance: RMSE rotation of 32.91° and RMSE translation of 17.18 mm, with a Q_pos of 0.21. This represents a 57% improvement in RMSE rotation and 87% improvement in RMSE translation over the second-best method (Greedy Geom Match, 76.99° / 135.95 mm). Even without fine-tuning, ReassembleNet with learnable keypoint selection (47.61° / 19.16 mm) substantially outperforms all other learnable baselines. The scalability ablation confirms that k=20 keypoints is the optimal trade-off, with k=25 exceeding 40 GB GPU memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReassembleNet (learnable KP + fine-tuning) decisively outperforms all five baselines on both RMSE rotation and translation. Against the strongest non-learnable baseline, Greedy Geom Match [36], it reduces RMSE rotation from 76.99° to 32.91° (−57%) and RMSE translation from 135.95 mm to 17.18 mm (−87%). Among learnable methods, DiffAssemble [26] struggles because it approximates irregular shapes as square patches and relies on a regular grid, yielding RMSE rotation of 123.42° and translation of 280.76 mm—nearly 4× worse than ReassembleNet. PairingNet [40] performs poorly (91.54° / 364.62 mm) because its pair-matching loss aligns contour points, which is unsuitable for eroded or gapped fresco fragments. PuzzleFusion [18] is excluded entirely as it is computationally intractable on the highly irregular RePAIR shapes due to processing all contour keypoints without any reduction mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReassembleNet builds on the DDIM diffusion framework for iterative pose denoising and extends the semi-synthetic fresco fragmentation pipeline from PairingNet by adding erosion and random transformations to better model realistic break patterns. It departs from prior diffusion-based reassembly methods (DiffAssemble, PuzzleFusion) that either restrict pieces to regular grid patches or process all contour vertices, making them intractable for complex irregular shapes. The key conceptual departure is framing keypoint selection as a graph sparsification problem solved by a learnable Graph Transformer with pooling, rather than relying on fixed heuristics like Farthest Point Sampling or processing all points exhaustively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical pathway toward automating fresco reassembly in archaeological workflows, where manual reconstruction can span years. The learnable keypoint selector offers a generalizable strategy for reducing computational complexity on irregular shapes, potentially applicable beyond frescos to other 2D reassembly domains. However, the fixed keypoint count (k=20) and non-rotation-equivariant texture backbone are acknowledged limitations, and the evaluation is confined to a single benchmark (RePAIR, 24 test puzzles), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to 3D reassembly or to domains with fundamentally different fragment statistics.
