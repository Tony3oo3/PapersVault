## Problem

Feature-level fusion in collaborative perception is bottlenecked by two compounding degradation sources during feature acquisition: domain gaps arising from heterogeneous LiDAR hardware and varying sensor mounting conditions across agents, and temporal misalignment introduced by communication transmission delays. Existing approaches either disrupt physical validity when mixing domains (e.g., DI-V2X) or bias motion modeling toward background regions (e.g., FFNet), leaving both issues inadequately addressed simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The ego agent ingests its current LiDAR point cloud, while each collaborative agent transmits compressed multi-scale BEV features from two consecutive frames (at t−τ and t−τ−ΔT) along with motion information, where τ is the transmission delay. The backbone is PointPillar with a 0.4 m × 0.4 m BEV grid; the final output is 3D car bounding boxes evaluated at IoU thresholds of 0.50 and 0.70. CDAM operates only during training, while PTAM and IFAM run at both training and inference time.

## Method

DATA comprises three modules. CDAM reduces domain gaps via (i) Proximal-region Hierarchical Downsampling, which partitions nearby objects into inner/outer concentric bounding-box regions and applies Farthest Point Sampling at different ratios (β_in=0.6, β_out=0.8) to balance point density while preserving contours, and (ii) an Observability-constrained Discriminator that uses a foreground estimator to compute per-location observability maps, weights the adversarial BCE loss only over common-observable regions, and employs a Gradient Reversal Layer for end-to-end domain-invariant feature learning. PTAM performs two-stage progressive temporal alignment: the collaborative agent predicts an intermediate feature using unit temporal scaling (ξ=1), then the ego agent computes an adaptive scaling factor ξ via an MLP that fuses a motion-difference context vector with sinusoidal delay encodings, applying bilinear feature warping to recover the current-time feature; a multi-window self-supervised strategy with two offset window partitions (size l=16) supervises both intermediate and final predictions. IFAM separates foreground/background via the shared foreground estimator, enhances structural details with specialized difference convolutions (central, horizontal, vertical, angular), and verifies enhanced features against pillar-encoding height-semantic cues using channel-shuffle + group-convolution attention to suppress spurious targets before fusing all agents' refined BEV features through a shared 1×1 convolution.

## Results

On the real-world DAIR-V2X-C dataset (zero latency), DATA achieves 79.94% AP50 and 65.48% AP70, outperforming the closest SOTA (HEAL) by 0.94% AP50 and 2.36% AP70. On the simulation datasets V2XSIM and V2XSET, DATA reaches 88.91%/77.69% and 89.53%/74.98% (AP50/AP70), surpassing MRCNet by 3.58%/7.87% and 4.53%/8.67% respectively. Under 500 ms communication delay on DAIR-V2X-C, DATA retains 75.58% AP50, exceeding FFNet (72.19%) and CodeFilling (71.50%) by 2.61% and 4.08% AP50.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DATA outperforms all 13 baselines on every dataset where numbers are reported. On DAIR-V2X-C, the closest competitor is HEAL (79.00/63.12), which DATA surpasses by 0.94% AP50 and 2.36% AP70; CodeFilling (79.90/61.06) is competitive on AP50 but trails by 4.42% AP70. On V2XSIM, DATA leads MRCNet (85.33/69.82) by 3.58% AP50 and 7.87% AP70, and FFNet (85.56/68.64) by 3.35% AP50 and 9.05% AP70. On V2XSET, the margin over MRCNet (85.00/66.31) is 4.53% AP50 and 8.67% AP70. Under 500 ms latency, DATA (75.58% AP50) beats FFNet (72.19%) and CodeFilling (71.50%) by 2.61% and 4.08% respectively. For pose-error robustness on V2XSIM/V2XSET, DATA retains 66.53–67.22% AP70 at σ_local=0.6 m and 71.17–75.23% AP70 at σ_head=1.0°, while baselines such as MRCNet, FFNet, Where2comm, and V2X-ViT degrade by over 30% (local) and 9% (heading) AP70.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two previously treated-separately challenges in collaborative perception: domain alignment (addressed by DI-V2X's instance mixing, V2X-ViT's heterogeneous embeddings) and temporal alignment (addressed by FFNet's global flow, CoBEVFlow's RoI-based prediction). Rather than building on a single prior method, DATA repurposes adversarial domain adaptation (GRL-based, from unsupervised domain adaptation literature) and optical-flow-style feature warping into a unified two-stage pipeline that explicitly separates raw-data density balancing (PHD) from feature-level domain invariance (OD), then layers progressive temporal compensation on top. The IFAM module extends standard BEV fusion with instance-level structural enhancement and height-semantic verification, a departure from prior work that treats fused features as a monolithic representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's core insight—that domain and temporal misalignment degrade feature quality with cumulative effects throughout the collaborative network, and that alignment must happen at the acquisition phase rather than being corrected post-hoc at fusion—reframes how practitioners should think about CP system design. The systematic three-module decomposition (align domain → align time → enhance semantics) provides a clear blueprint that other CP architectures can adopt. Open questions include whether the adversarial training component (OD) remains necessary when agents share identical hardware, how the approach scales beyond two agents with heterogeneous sensor suites (e.g., camera + LiDAR), and whether the two-stage temporal compensation introduces compounding errors under extreme delay or high-velocity scenarios not fully explored in the experiments.
