## Problem

Standard object detectors excel at identifying discrete entities but cannot recognize complex events that depend on compositional relationships, inter-object correlations, and contextual semantics (e.g., a person operating multiple fishing rods, or a worker operating machinery without a helmet). Bridging this gap traditionally requires either training specialized event-recognition models on extensive labeled datasets or fine-tuning detectors on task-specific data, both of which incur prohibitive annotation costs, sacrifice generalizability, and produce opaque black-box decisions.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes RGB images along with a natural-language event scenario description, which is used to generate detection prompts for the open-vocabulary detector. The detector outputs structured entity features (category labels, bounding boxes, confidence scores) that are transformed into a feature vector capturing entity counts, spatial relationships, and attribute distributions. The final output is a binary event label (0/1) accompanied by a human-readable symbolic expression (e.g., a logical rule over entity counts) that explicitly explains the recognition decision, providing full interpretability without any gradient-based training.

## Method

SymbolicDet is a training-free, plug-and-play framework that combines two synergistic components: (1) a Symbolic Logic Search module that uses an evolutionary algorithm (population initialization, crossover, mutation, fitness evaluation) to explore a space of mathematical operators {+, −, ×, ÷, max, min} and logical operators {∧, ∨, ¬} over structured entity features extracted from any open-vocabulary detector; and (2) an LLM-guided reasoning module that employs a three-layer structured prompt space—scene context initialization, chain-of-thought guidance, and contextual feedback integration—to inject world knowledge and direct the evolutionary search toward semantically meaningful expressions. At each iteration, the best-performing symbolic expression is fed back to the LLM, which generates new candidate patterns informed by its commonsense understanding of visual event semantics, while the symbolic search framework objectively evaluates and grounds these suggestions in empirical performance. This bidirectional interaction accelerates convergence over the combinatorial expression space while preserving full interpretability, as the final event rule is an explicit, human-readable logical formula operating solely on detector outputs.

## Results

On the UCSD Ped2 benchmark, SymbolicDet achieves 98.7% AUROC in a fully training-free setting, matching Background-Agnostic (98.7%) and within 1.0% of the SOTA DMAD (99.7%). Across the ERA aerial video dataset, the framework yields the largest gains on Sport events (+23.16% AUROC with APE, reaching 90.29%) and BALL events (+39.55%, reaching 94.91%). On the self-collected Helmet-Mac dataset, APE+SymbolicDet improves from 67.41% to 83.18% AUROC (+15.77%), and on Multi-rods Fishing from 66.82% to 75.16% (+8.36%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On UCSD Ped2, SymbolicDet (98.7%) ties Background-Agnostic (98.7%) and outperforms SD-MAE (95.4%) by 3.3 points, VALD-GAN (97.74%) by ~1.0 point, and MAMA (98.2%) by 0.5 points. It trails the two strongest baselines—FastAno (99.3%) and DMAD (99.7%)—by 0.6 and 1.0 points respectively, all while requiring no task-specific training data. The critical distinction is that all six compared methods require supervised or self-supervised training on the target dataset, whereas SymbolicDet operates entirely training-free by leveraging a pre-trained open-vocabulary detector plus LLM-guided symbolic search. On the ERA and self-collected datasets (Table 1), the framework's gains over the raw detector baselines are substantially larger (up to +39.55% on ERA BALL), demonstrating that the symbolic reasoning layer adds the most value when the underlying detector has not been fine-tuned for the specific event.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of neuro-symbolic AI and LLM-guided search, but does not build on a single prior method. It repurposes open-vocabulary detectors (APE, GLIP, YOLO-World) as frozen perception backends and layers an evolutionary symbolic regression module on top, guided by LLMs (qwen-series) operating in their native text domain. The key conceptual departure from prior neuro-symbolic work is treating existing detectors as modular, off-the-shelf perception units rather than requiring custom neural-symbolic integration, and using LLMs as reasoning guides for an external symbolic search rather than as direct visual reasoners or optimization targets. This positions the work as a plug-and-play enhancement layer rather than a new end-to-end architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, detector-agnostic design offers a practical path to event understanding in safety-critical domains (construction, surveillance) where interpretability and rapid deployment matter, and the discovered symbolic rules provide transparent reasoning that black-box event classifiers cannot. However, the symbolic expressions discovered are largely simple counting and threshold comparisons, raising open questions about whether the framework scales to genuinely complex compositional or temporal events. The approach also depends heavily on the underlying detector's quality and the LLM's ability to generate useful search directions, and has only been validated on binary event classification with relatively small datasets, leaving generalization to open-world, multi-class, or video-based event understanding unexplored.
