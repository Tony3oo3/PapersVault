## Problem

Existing multi-modal remote sensing foundation models (e.g., SkySense) train separate backbone networks for each data modality, resulting in redundant parameters and inefficient utilization. Additionally, prevalent self-supervised pre-training strategies designed for natural images fail to account for the complex multi-subject semantic distribution within a single remote sensing image, where different regions may contain buildings, forests, ponds, and land simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250997/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes geographically aligned multi-modal satellite data with vastly different ground sample distances: high-resolution optical RGB at 2048×2048, Sentinel-2 multi-spectral (10 bands) at 64×64, and Sentinel-1 SAR (2 bands) at 64×64, each accompanied by multi-temporal sequences (avg. 65 S2 and 10 S1 images per location). The unified backbone outputs spatially aligned feature tokens whose resolution is modality-dependent—HR optical features are progressively downsampled while MS and SAR features retain their native resolution—enabling direct cross-modal fusion for downstream heads producing classification labels, segmentation masks, detection boxes, or change maps.

## Method

SkySense V2 replaces SkySense's three separate backbones (1.26B params) with a single 4-stage hierarchical transformer (665M params) that fully shares parameters across modalities, using SwinV2B blocks in stages 1–2 and vanilla transformer blocks in stages 3–4. An Adaptive Patch Merging module after each stage selectively downsamples HR optical tokens by 2×2 while preserving MS/SAR token resolution via a linear projection, maintaining spatial alignment across modalities. Learnable modality-specific prompt tokens (4 per modality per stage) are prepended to stages 3–4 to inject modality identity through attention without breaking parameter sharing. A Mixture-of-Experts module (8 experts, top-1 routing) replaces FFN layers in the last 6 blocks to scale capacity efficiently. For pre-training, the paper introduces Query-based Semantic Aggregation Contrastive Learning (QSACL), which uses m learnable queries to cross-attend over multi-crop view features, aggregating semantically consistent features before applying contrastive loss—addressing the multi-subject problem inherent to RS images—combined with the inherited MGCL, GCPL, and dense Image-Text Alignment losses under a DINOv2-style EMA teacher-student framework.

## Results

SkySense V2 achieves state-of-the-art results across all 16 evaluation datasets. On scene classification, it reaches 98.34% OA on AID (TR=20%), 96.42% on RESISC-45 (TR=10%), 89.13% mAP on BEN-S2 (TR=10%), and 66.65% Top-1 on fMoW-S2, outperforming SkySense by 0.66–2.27 points. On semantic segmentation, it attains 71.87 mIoU on iSAID and 95.86 mF1 on Potsdam; on object detection, 79.50 mAP50 on DIOR and 55.96 mAP on FAIR1M; on change detection, 94.83 F1 on LEVIR-CD and 65.29 F1 on OSCD. Overall, SkySense V2 surpasses its predecessor SkySense by an average of 1.8 points across all tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SkySense V2 outperforms all 21 compared baselines across every evaluated task and dataset. Against its direct predecessor SkySense [20], it gains an average of 1.8 points, with the largest margin on change detection (OSCD: 65.29 vs. 60.06 F1, +5.23) and fMoW-S2 temporal classification (66.65 vs. 64.38 Top-1, +2.27). Among prior SOTA methods, MA3E [30] achieves the highest AID accuracy at TR=50% (99.04) but does not report results on most other datasets; SkySense V2 matches or exceeds it on all comparable settings. SatMAE [13] and SatMAE++ [41] are the strongest MIM-based baselines, yet SkySense V2 surpasses them by 3.3–5.4 points on scene classification and 2.0–4.9 points on segmentation. Contrastive-learning baselines (GASSL [3], SeCo [38], CACo [37], DINO-MC [58]) trail by 5–10+ points, confirming the advantage of the unified multi-modal pre-training with QSACL. On multi-modal tasks (Table 3), SkySense V2 consistently leads SkySense by 0.5–1.6 mIoU/accuracy points, with the largest gain on PASTIS-MM with GEP input (86.7 vs. 85.8).

## Relation to Prior Work

SkySense V2 is a direct architectural and methodological upgrade to SkySense (CVPR 2024), inheriting its teacher-student pre-training framework, MGCL loss, GCPL, and ~21M-image multi-modal training corpus. The key departure is replacing SkySense's three separate backbones (Swin-H, ViT-L, ViT-L) with a single unified hierarchical transformer that uses Adaptive Patch Merging to handle heterogeneous spatial resolutions, plus modality-specific prompt tokens (inspired by register tokens and visual prompt tuning) to preserve feature diversity under full parameter sharing. The novel QSACL loss departs from standard DINOv2-style contrastive learning by inserting learnable queries that aggregate semantically consistent features across augmented views before computing contrastive objectives, addressing the multi-subject structure of RS images that single-subject natural-image SSL does not capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified backbone reduces parameter count from 1.26B to 665M while improving average performance by 1.8 points over SkySense across 16 datasets and 7 tasks, making multi-modal RS foundation models more practical to deploy and scale. The QSACL formulation offers a principled way to adapt contrastive pre-training to the distributed-semantic nature of RS imagery, a gap that has persisted since DINOv2-based methods were ported to remote sensing. Open questions include whether the unified design generalizes to additional modalities (e.g., hyperspectral, LiDAR), whether the MoE scaling strategy can be pushed further without degrading multi-modal alignment, and how language grounding (mentioned as future work) would interact with the current purely visual pre-training objective.
