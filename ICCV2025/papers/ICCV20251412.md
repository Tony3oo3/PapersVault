## Problem

Spatial intelligence is bottlenecked by the scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and annotated 3D datasets, while existing solutions each suffer critical drawbacks: simulation-based data has a sim-to-real gap, AI-generated 3D assets lack scene-level realism, and sensor-captured datasets are expensive, small-scale, and domain-restricted. Meanwhile, web-scale 2D image datasets with rich annotations remain largely untapped for spatial understanding tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251412/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes single-view RGB images (from COCO or Objects365-v2) along with their existing 2D annotations (segmentation masks or bounding boxes). It produces metric-scale 3D point clouds with per-point RGB, scale-calibrated depth maps, predicted camera intrinsics and extrinsics, and 3D-lifted annotations (segmentation labels and 3D bounding boxes). Unlike multi-view or LiDAR-captured datasets, the resulting point clouds are partial single-view reconstructions, and the 3D scenes span scales from ~1 m to several hundred meters, far exceeding the ~10 m range typical of indoor datasets like ScanNet.

## Method

The pipeline integrates four components: (1) MoGe for relative depth estimation to capture fine-grained local geometry, (2) Metric3D v2 for metric depth estimation to recover correct global scale, (3) a scale-calibration step that computes a per-image scaling factor s = mean(d_metric)/mean(d_relative) over valid pixels and applies it to the relative depth map, and (4) camera parameter prediction using Wild-Camera for intrinsics and PerspectiveFields for extrinsics (per-pixel upward vectors and latitude to construct a rotation matrix aligning the scene to a z-up world frame). Each 2D pixel is then back-projected into 3D via the calibrated depth and camera matrices, and original 2D annotations (COCO masks directly, or SAM-generated masks for Objects365-v2 bounding boxes) are lifted into 3D segmentation labels and 3D bounding boxes. This dual-depth strategy—combining geometric fidelity from relative depth with metric accuracy from metric depth—distinguishes the approach from prior 2D-to-3D methods like SpatialRGPT (which lacks fine-grained geometry) and SpatialBot (which omits camera parameters), yielding scale-authentic, appearance-realistic 3D data at web scale.

## Results

Pre-training on the synthetic COCO-3D dataset yields substantial gains across all evaluated 3D tasks. For semantic segmentation, SpUNet's mIoU on ScanNet jumps from 31.09% (ScanNet-only training) to 62.48% (+31.39) with COCO-3D pre-training, while PTv2 and Uni3D also improve by +4.77 and +3.69 mIoU respectively. For instance segmentation, mAP increases from 24.30% to 28.64% (+4.34), and referring instance segmentation mIoU rises from 26.10% to 32.47% (+6.37). On 3D LLM tasks, the 1.3B LL3DA model with COCO-3D pre-training achieves BLEU-4 of 15.8 on ScanQA, matching or surpassing 7B-parameter models fine-tuned specifically on the task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's comparisons are structured as training-strategy ablations (COCO-3D only, ScanNet only, COCO-3D pre-train + ScanNet fine-tune) across multiple backbone architectures rather than competing methods. COCO-3D pre-training consistently outperforms ScanNet-only training across all three semantic segmentation backbones: SpUNet benefits most dramatically (+31.39 mIoU), while PTv2 (+4.77) and Uni3D (+3.69) show more modest but consistent gains. For instance segmentation (Uni3D + Mask3D), the +4.34 mAP gain is smaller, and zero-shot transfer from COCO-3D to ScanNet is weaker (mIoU drops from 19.33 to 10.10), indicating domain gap remains for direct transfer without fine-tuning. On 3D LLM tasks (Table 5), COCO-3D pre-training yields uniform improvements: +4.06 C@0.5 on ScanRefer, +1.26 C@0.5 on Nr3D, and +3.44 C on ScanQA. In the 3D QA comparison (Table 7), the 1.3B LL3DA with COCO-3D pre-training (C=85.2, B-4=15.8) surpasses the 7B Vicuna-based LLaVA-3D (C=91.7, B-4=14.5) on BLEU-4 and matches it on C, demonstrating that data quality/scale can compensate for model size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the three dominant paradigms for 3D data acquisition—simulation engines (e.g., Isaac Gym), AI-generated 3D assets (text-to-3D), and sensor-captured datasets (ScanNet, Structured3D)—by proposing a '2D-to-3D data lifting' pipeline that converts existing web-scale 2D image datasets into metric-scale 3D representations. Methodologically, it integrates MoGe (relative depth) and Metric3D v2 (metric depth) with a simple averaging-based scale calibration, combined with Wild-Camera (intrinsics) and PerspectiveFields (extrinsics), to produce fully annotated 3D point clouds. The closest prior efforts, SpatialRGPT and SpatialBot, generate 3D data from 2D inputs but omit full camera parameters or fine-grained geometry; this paper's key distinction is the systematic combination of scale-invariant and scale-aware depth with complete camera calibration to preserve both local detail and real-world metric scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By releasing COCO-3D (~122K scenes) and Objects365-v2-3D, the paper provides the first ~2M-scene spatial dataset spanning 300+ categories in diverse in-the-wild conditions, dramatically exceeding existing scene-level 3D benchmarks (ScanNet: 1.5K, Structured3D: 3.5K). This opens a low-cost pathway to scaling spatial intelligence without specialized hardware, potentially enabling the 'ImageNet moment' for 3D perception. However, the authors acknowledge that interactive 3D environments and vision-language-action (VLA) applications remain unexplored, and the zero-shot transfer gaps (e.g., referring segmentation) highlight that synthetic-to-real domain differences in scene scale, object distribution, and view completeness are not yet fully resolved.
