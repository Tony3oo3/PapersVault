## Problem

Existing public face video datasets suffer from three critical limitations: limited total duration (most under 300 hours), a quality-quantity trade-off (e.g., VoxCeleb2 offers 2400h but only 224×224 resolution), and insufficient ethnic diversity with severe underrepresentation of Asian faces. These gaps hinder the training of high-resolution, diverse face video generation backbones and leave open questions about the minimum data scale needed for effective fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251195/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 270,043 video clips (all exceeding 512×512, with 46.5% at 1080×1080 or higher) paired with synchronized speech audio, DWPose facial keypoints, and detailed text captions covering ethnicity, gender, age, appearance, emotion, actions, environment, and lighting. For generation, T2V models consume text prompts while I2V models consume a reference face image plus optional text, producing temporally consistent talking face videos. Audio-driven variants additionally condition on speech audio, with lip-sync quality verified via a retrained SyncNet score.

## Method

The core contribution is a rigorous multi-stage data pipeline: raw videos (>1080p, ~2000h) from crowdsourced interviews and vlogs are cropped to face+shoulder, filtered by FaceXFormer (age >22) and OpenCV (face ≥256×256, single face), cleaned via OCR-based subtitle detection, black-border removal, and manual exclusion of hands/noise, with CodeFormer applied to the <4% of remaining blurry clips. Annotations are generated by PLLaVA using prompt-based captioning (sampling 3 non-consecutive frames per video) and then double-checked by 100+ annotators over six months against ISO 2859 standards. For empirical validation, the authors fine-tune five T2V backbones (AnimateDiff, Latte, OpenSora, EasyAnimate, CogVideoX) and five I2V backbones (SVD, I2VGen-XL, ConsistI2V, EasyAnimate, CogVideoX) on DH-FaceVid-1K versus four public datasets, and conduct a data-scale ablation with CogVideoX at 2B and 5B parameters across 100h–1000h subsets, finding ~600h optimal for 2B models and full-dataset superiority for 5B models.

## Results

On Text-to-Video generation, CogVideoX trained on DH-FaceVid-1K achieves the best overall performance with FVD=98.01, FID=11.73, and CLIPSIM=0.3801, outperforming the same model trained on CelebV-Text (FVD=129.59, FID=15.82) by 24% in FVD and 26% in FID. On Image-to-Video generation, CogVideoX on DH-FaceVid-1K reaches FVD=92.31, FID=10.69, and CLIPSIM=0.3814, again the best across all model-dataset combinations. The dataset's advantage is consistent: every one of the five evaluated models (AnimateDiff, Latte, OpenSora, EasyAnimate, CogVideoX) achieves its best scores when trained on DH-FaceVid-1K rather than any public dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is dataset-driven: the same five T2V models (AnimateDiff, Latte, OpenSora, EasyAnimate, CogVideoX) and five I2V models (SVD, I2VGen-XL, ConsistI2V, EasyAnimate, CogVideoX) are fine-tuned on each of five datasets, and DH-FaceVid-1K wins on FVD and FID for every single model in both tasks. CogVideoX [71] is the strongest backbone overall, achieving FVD=98.01 (T2V) and FVD=92.31 (I2V) on DH-FaceVid-1K, beating the next-best CelebV-Text-trained CogVideoX by 24% and 25% in FVD respectively. Among T2V baselines, the DiT-based Latte [37] (FVD=140.01) and OpenSora [79] (FVD=117.88) are competitive but still trail CogVideoX; the UNet-based AnimateDiff [22] (FVD=176.13) lags most. For I2V, SVD [2] (FVD=132.92) and I2VGen-XL [76] (FVD=154.35) show that UNet-based architectures converge faster but plateau at higher FVD than DiT-based CogVideoX (FVD=92.31). ConsistI2V [46] (FVD=152.92) and EasyAnimate [63] (FVD=95.27) fall between these extremes. The paper also notes that DiT-based models (Latte, CogVideoX) consistently outperform UNet-based models (AnimateDiff, SVD) in final quality, though UNet models require less training compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the face video generation data landscape rather than extending a specific methodological lineage. It departs from prior public datasets (HDTF, CelebV-HQ, CelebV-Text, VoxCeleb2, TalkingHead-1KH) by simultaneously addressing their three core limitations—insufficient duration (<300h), quality-quantity trade-offs, and ethnic underrepresentation—through a crowdsourced collection pipeline with rigorous multi-stage filtering and annotation. The empirical evaluation repurposes existing generation backbones (CogVideoX, SVD, AnimateDiff, Latte, OpenSora, EasyAnimate, I2VGen-XL, ConsistI2V) as test vehicles rather than proposing new architectures, positioning the contribution squarely as a data resource and scaling study.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DH-FaceVid-1K (1,200h, 270K clips, 20K+ identities) is the largest publicly available face video dataset to date and directly addresses the critical underrepresentation of Asian faces (~80% of the dataset) that has constrained model generalization. The scaling-law analysis (RQ1) provides actionable guidance—approximately 600h for 2B-parameter DiT backbones, with 5B models benefiting from the full dataset—while the backbone comparison (RQ2) empirically confirms DiT architectures outperform UNet-based ones for face video generation at the cost of higher compute. Open questions include whether the 80% Asian composition, while correcting a prior bias, introduces its own representational skew, and whether the primarily talking-head content limits applicability to more dynamic facial animation tasks. The authors note plans to expand demographic coverage (Indonesia, Vietnam) and release additional pre-trained models.
