## Problem

Existing camera-controlled video diffusion models suffer from two critical limitations: they significantly degrade in generating dynamic content after incorporating camera control, and they are restricted to short single clips that cannot be extended with new camera trajectories in the same scene. These constraints limit both the types of scenes that can be generated (constrained to largely static content) and the spatial range users can explore, diminishing practical utility for interactive scene navigation.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a starting RGB image, a user-specified camera trajectory (extrinsic and intrinsic matrices converted to per-pixel Plücker embeddings of shape 6×h×w per frame), and an optional text prompt. For sequential scene exploration, it additionally conditions on the last n clean frames from the previously generated clip. Outputs are video clips at 384×640 resolution (32-step Euler sampling), with the ability to chain multiple clips into a coherent sequence. The base model is a ~3B parameter transformer-based latent video diffusion model.

## Method

CameraCtrl II introduces three key components. First, it constructs the RealCam dataset from real dynamic videos with camera annotations estimated via VGGSfM, applying metric depth calibration (RANSAC-based scale alignment) and camera trajectory distribution balancing to address arbitrary SfM scales and long-tailed trajectory types. Second, it injects camera control through a single lightweight patchify layer at the initial DiT layer, element-wise adding camera features to visual features before the first transformer block—avoiding the over-constraining of deeper layers that suppresses dynamics. Joint training on both camera-labeled and unlabeled videos (using zero Plücker embeddings for the latter) enables camera classifier-free guidance at inference. Third, for extended scene exploration, it performs clip-wise autoregressive generation: previous clip frames are kept clean and concatenated with noised current-clip tokens along the sequence dimension, with a binary mask channel distinguishing conditioning from target tokens, and loss computed only on the new clip. A global reference frame (first frame of the initial trajectory) is used for all relative pose calculations to prevent geometric drift across clips.

## Results

In the I2V setting, CameraCtrl II achieves an FVD of 73.11, more than 2× lower than Cami2v (154.46) and CameraCtrl (199.53), while its Motion Strength of 698.51 is roughly 5× higher than both baselines (129.53 and 133.37). Camera control accuracy is also superior, with TransErr of 0.1527 and RotErr of 1.58, compared to 0.1984/1.92 (Cami2v) and 0.2812/2.81 (CameraCtrl). Geometric consistency reaches 88.70, well above Cami2v (76.53) and CameraCtrl (52.12). In the T2V setting against AC3D, CameraCtrl II (3B DiT) achieves FVD 641.23 vs. 987.34, Motion Strength 574.21 vs. 162.21, and Geometric Consistency 85.00 vs. 69.20.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CameraCtrl II dominates all three baselines across every reported metric. Against Cami2v in the I2V setting, it reduces FVD by 53% (73.11 vs. 154.46), increases Motion Strength by 4.4× (698.51 vs. 129.53), and improves Geometric Consistency by 12.2 points (88.70 vs. 76.53). Against CameraCtrl, the gains are even more pronounced: FVD drops by 63%, Motion Strength rises by 5.2×, and Geometric Consistency jumps from 52.12 to 88.70, indicating that prior camera-control methods severely suppress scene dynamics. In the T2V comparison with AC3D, CameraCtrl II (3B DiT) achieves 35% lower FVD (641.23 vs. 987.34), 3.5× higher Motion Strength (574.21 vs. 162.21), and 15.8 points higher Geometric Consistency (85.00 vs. 69.20). The Appearance Consistency metric (0.8893) also confirms that CameraCtrl II maintains the best cross-clip visual coherence, outperforming Cami2v (0.8104) and CameraCtrl (0.7784) in sequential generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends CameraCtrl's camera-controlled video diffusion framework, retaining the Plücker embedding representation but replacing the dedicated camera encoder with a lightweight patchify layer injected only at the initial DiT layer. It departs from the broader line of camera-controlled video generation work (MotionCtrl, CamCo, Cami2v, AC3D, VD3D) in two fundamental ways: training on a newly curated dynamic video dataset (RealCam) with SfM-estimated camera parameters rather than static-scene datasets like RealEstate10K, and introducing clip-wise autoregressive generation to enable sequential scene exploration beyond a single short clip. The joint training strategy with unlabeled data and camera classifier-free guidance is borrowed from the text-conditioning literature but applied here to the camera modality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By addressing the two core limitations of prior camera-controlled video models—degraded dynamics and restricted spatial range—this work makes interactive scene exploration a practical use case for video diffusion, moving beyond one-shot clip generation toward navigable digital environments. The RealCam dataset and the clip-wise extension recipe are reusable components for future camera-controlled generation research. Open questions include how far sequential generation can be extended before geometric and appearance consistency degrade, whether the approach generalizes to fully autonomous (non-user-specified) camera paths, and whether the distillation-based speedup can achieve real-time interactivity.
