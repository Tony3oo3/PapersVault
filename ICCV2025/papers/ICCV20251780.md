## Problem

Existing SLAM benchmarks face a fundamental accuracy-diversity tradeoff: MoCap-based ground truth achieves mm accuracy but is restricted to small indoor rooms, while GPS/SfM-based methods scale to diverse outdoor scenes but introduce cm-level errors that bias evaluation. Additionally, the standard ATE metric does not account for scene scale, making it impossible to fairly compare SLAM performance across trajectories with different object distances.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251780/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides synchronized monocular RGB (rendered from Insta360 360° camera at 60 FPS), stereo RGB (ZED X at 1920×1200, 60 FPS global shutter), and IMU streams across 365 sequences totaling 2.1M frames. Ground truth is derived from a separate rendered view of the same 360° camera that sees AprilTag calibration boards, while the user-view shown to SLAM/NVS methods contains no visible boards. The proposed IOF metric additionally consumes ZED X depth maps to fit a parametric depth distribution (mixture of Gaussians/Gammas, 1–8 components selected by BIC) and computes a deterministic induced-optical-flow score in the range 0–100% (Flow AUC).

## Method

The ground-truth pipeline places 50 AprilTag boards (≈20 m coverage radius) in a new scene in ~5 minutes, then films with an Insta360 X4 360° camera that is post-processed into a user-view and a ground-truth view. Board relative poses are estimated via PnP across all visible board pairs per frame, aggregated with median-distance outlier rejection, optimized globally with g2o pose-graph optimization, and optionally snapped to a common plane. Camera poses are initialized by multi-board PnP (transforming all visible markers into a common reference frame) and then refined by Bundle PnP—a nonlinear least-squares optimization (Ceres/Levenberg-Marquardt) that jointly minimizes marker reprojection error over board and camera poses while respecting the fixed 3D marker geometry on each board. For evaluation, the paper introduces the Induced Optical Flow (IOF) metric, which re-projects pixels from the ground-truth pose to the estimated pose at sampled depths drawn from a fitted parametric distribution, then converts the resulting flow magnitudes into a scale-aware Flow AUC score via Umeyama Sim(3) and Kabsch SO(3) alignment.

## Results

The proposed ground-truth pipeline achieves a mean ATE of 2.88 mm against MoCap validation, outperforming COLMAP (11.4 mm) by nearly 4× across 9 sequences. On the SLAM benchmark (Table 6), even the best method (DPV-SLAM) drops from 92.15 composite score on easy scanning to 0.75 on hard indoor videos, with Flow AUC falling below 1% for all methods on hard indoor/outdoor sequences. For NVS, COLMAP fails on 5 of 6 scenes entirely, while the proposed calibration-board poses always succeed and yield higher PSNR (e.g., 32.26 vs 23.28 for Splatfacto on Window-Night).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's ground-truth pipeline outperforms COLMAP on all 9 MoCap-validated sequences, achieving 2.88 mm mean ATE versus COLMAP's 11.4 mm (up to 26.1 mm on individual sequences). Among the five SLAM/VO baselines evaluated on Princeton365, DPV-SLAM [20] is the strongest overall, edging out DPVO [38] by ~1–10 points in composite score across difficulty levels (e.g., 42.11 vs 32.53 on hard scanning). ORB-SLAM3 [7] is competitive on outdoor sequences (28.65 composite on easy outdoor) but suffers from low coverage (51.88% on easy scanning, 8.19% on easy indoor). LEAP-VO [11] is the weakest, tracking only ~10% of frames on indoor/outdoor videos and scoring below 7.5 composite everywhere. COLMAP [29] is evaluated only on scanning videos and trails DPV-SLAM by 23 points on easy scanning; it also fails entirely on 5 of 6 NVS scenes, whereas the proposed calibration-board poses always produce valid reconstructions with higher PSNR and lower LPIPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in SLAM benchmark construction by resolving the long-standing accuracy-diversity tradeoff that has constrained prior datasets. MoCap-based benchmarks (TUM RGB-D, EuRoC) achieve mm accuracy but are confined to one or two rooms, while GPS/RTK-based benchmarks (KITTI, Michigan NCLT) cover large distances but offer only cm-level accuracy and 4-DoF camera motion. The authors repurpose the 360° camera paradigm—previously used for immersive video—into a dual-view ground-truth collection system (user-view for evaluation, ground-truth view for calibration boards), combined with a novel Bundle PnP optimization that achieves mm accuracy without MoCap infrastructure. The Induced Optical Flow metric departs from the standard ATE framework by incorporating scene-scale-aware depth distributions, addressing a known limitation of absolute-error metrics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Princeton365 is likely to become a standard SLAM evaluation benchmark given its combination of 365 diverse sequences, full 6-DoF motion, mm-level ground truth, and multi-modal outputs (monocular, stereo, IMU) at a scale (2.1M frames, 26 km) far exceeding prior accurate benchmarks. The IOF/Flow AUC metric addresses a genuine gap in how SLAM performance is compared across scenes of different scales, and its adoption could reshape how the community reports results. However, only 56.1% of frames carry ground-truth pose (the rest are outside the ~20 m calibration zone), which limits evaluation to start/end segments of long trajectories. Open questions include whether the IOF metric generalizes to scenes without depth sensors, whether the 20 m ground-truth zone is sufficient for very large outdoor scenes, and whether the dataset's diversity (285 object scans, 40 indoor, 40 outdoor) is balanced enough to avoid over-representing certain failure modes.
