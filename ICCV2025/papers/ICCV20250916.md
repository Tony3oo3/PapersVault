## Problem

Existing diffusion-based forward and inverse rendering methods suffer from two critical limitations: multi-step inference is computationally expensive, and independently trained forward and inverse models exhibit cycle inconsistency when applied sequentially—decomposed intrinsic maps often fail to faithfully reconstruct the original image. No prior work unifies both directions under a single-step, cycle-consistent framework spanning indoor and outdoor scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250916/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For inverse rendering, the model takes an RGB image plus a fixed text prompt (e.g., 'normal', 'albedo') that acts as a channel switch to select which intrinsic map to produce; for forward rendering, it accepts any subset of the five intrinsic channels (albedo, normal, roughness, metallicity, irradiance) concatenated as input along with a BLIP/BLIP-2-generated scene description. All processing occurs in VAE latent space at reduced resolution, and the single-step design (fixing timestep to T with Gaussian noise) yields a 50× speedup over multi-step baselines while remaining non-deterministic. For video, the same image model is extended via pseudo-3D convolutions and iterative windowed inference without any video-specific fine-tuning.

## Method

Ouroboros fine-tunes two pre-trained RGB↔X latent diffusion UNets (freezing all modules except the UNet) to perform single-step denoising from the maximally noisy state at timestep T, using Gaussian noise rather than the zero-noise initialization of E2E to preserve the inherent ambiguity of intrinsic decomposition. Task-specific losses are applied after VAE decoding: angular loss for normals, an affine-invariant loss for irradiance (absorbing scale/shift ambiguity), and MSE for albedo, roughness, metallicity, and RGB. A cycle-consistency phase then jointly optimizes both models with L_cycle = |X − X̃|² + |I − Ï|², where X̃ and Ï are the second-pass reconstructions, enabling self-supervised training on unannotated real images (MSCOCO, Flickr30k) and improving bidirectional coherence. For video inference, 2D 3×3 convolutions are replaced with 1×3×3 pseudo-3D kernels and spatial-temporal attention is applied over flattened multi-frame patches, with overlapping windows blended via a weighted latent interpolation (γ=0.1) to maintain temporal continuity without any video-specific training.

## Results

On albedo estimation, Ouroboros achieves PSNR of 20.71 (Hypersim), 25.38 (MatrixCity), and 22.07 (InteriorVerse), outperforming all baselines in most metrics. For normal estimation, it reaches mean angular error of 11.98°/18.12°/9.58° and 80.71%/76.21%/83.54% pixels within 11.25° across the three datasets. In forward rendering, Ouroboros achieves PSNR of 18.09/21.57/15.79 versus RGB↔X's 16.37/9.24/13.70, with the largest gap on MatrixCity (12.3 dB improvement). All results are obtained with single-step inference, yielding a 50× speedup over multi-step diffusion baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ouroboros outperforms RGB↔X [79] (its direct predecessor) on nearly all metrics across all three datasets, with the most dramatic gains on MatrixCity: albedo PSNR jumps from 12.61 to 25.38 and forward-rendering PSNR from 9.24 to 21.57. The only metric where Ouroboros trails RGB↔X is Hypersim albedo LPIPS (0.19 vs. 0.11), suggesting slightly less perceptual fidelity in that specific setting. Against non-diffusion baselines (Zhu et al. [85], Kocsis et al. [31], IntrinsicAnything [11], Careaga & Aksoy [7]), Ouroboros dominates by large margins—e.g., 12.8 dB PSNR advantage over Zhu et al. on MatrixCity albedo. For normal estimation, Ouroboros beats StableNormal [73] (10.73° vs. 9.58° mean error on InteriorVerse) and E2E [47] (15.87° vs. 9.58°) while also surpassing Lotus [21] on MatrixCity (18.12° vs. 25.06°). The single-step inference (vs. multi-step diffusion in all baselines) provides a 50× speedup without sacrificing accuracy, making it the only method to combine SOTA quality with real-time-friendly latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

Ouroboros builds directly on RGB↔X [79], which first unified inverse and forward rendering under a single latent diffusion framework, and on E2E [47], which demonstrated that multi-step diffusion models can be collapsed to single-step inference via end-to-end fine-tuning. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of CycleGAN-style [86] cycle consistency between the two independently trained models, ensuring that decomposed intrinsics faithfully reconstruct the original image and vice versa. Additionally, the paper extends the image-based model to video via pseudo-3D convolution (inspired by VDM and FLATTEN) without any video-specific retraining, a training-free transfer that prior diffusion-based rendering methods have not explored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50× inference speedup while maintaining state-of-the-art decomposition and synthesis quality makes diffusion-based inverse/forward rendering practically viable for interactive applications like relighting and object insertion, where multi-step sampling was previously prohibitive. The cycle consistency mechanism also enables self-supervised training on unannotated real-world images (MSCOCO, Flickr30k), reducing dependence on expensive synthetic renderings with paired ground truth. The primary open question, acknowledged by the authors, is data quality: current public datasets contain unreliable intrinsic maps and lack accurate lighting information, capping the achievable fidelity. Future work on procedurally generated, fully controlled synthetic scenes could unlock the full potential of this framework.
