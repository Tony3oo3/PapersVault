## Problem

While loss weighting strategies and architectural refinements have been explored to accelerate diffusion model training, the fundamental role of the noise schedule in determining how computational resources are allocated across noise levels remains underexplored. The paper identifies that the choice of noise schedule has been largely ad hoc and asks whether strategically concentrating training on mid-range noise intensities (around log-SNR = 0) via the schedule itself is more effective than adjusting loss weights.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251234/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes class labels (ImageNet) or text prompts (CC12M) as conditioning signals and produces generated images at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution. Images are center-cropped and encoded to compressed latents of 32×32×4 or 64×64×4 before being fed into the DiT-B backbone. During inference, the paper aligns sampled SNRs to the cosine schedule across all methods to isolate the training-phase effect of the noise schedule, using DDIM with 50 steps and multiple classifier-free guidance scales.

## Method

The paper reframes noise schedule design as a probability distribution design problem over log-SNR (λ), showing via importance sampling that the sampling distribution p(λ) is theoretically equivalent to the noise schedule λ(t). By specifying a target p(λ) that concentrates probability mass around λ = 0 (the signal-noise transition point), the inverse CDF directly yields the noise schedule. The authors propose four concrete schedules—Laplace, Cauchy, Cosine Shifted, and Cosine Scaled—that all increase sampling density near λ = 0, with the Laplace schedule (scale b = 0.5) achieving the best FID of 7.96 on ImageNet-256, a 26.6% improvement over the cosine baseline. A key empirical finding is that modifying p(λ) to reallocate FLOPs to mid-range noise levels outperforms equivalently motivated loss weighting schemes (Min-SNR, Soft-Min-SNR) under the same 500K-iteration budget, and the benefit generalizes across prediction targets (ε, x₀, v), resolutions, and datasets.

## Results

On ImageNet-256 (500K iterations, DiT-B), the proposed Laplace noise schedule achieves FID-10K of 7.96 at CFG=3.0, improving over the Cosine baseline (11.06) by 2.89 points (26.6% relative reduction). Cosine Scaled (s=2) and Cauchy (μ=0, γ=0.5) also outperform the baseline, reaching 8.04 and 8.14 respectively at CFG=2.0. On ImageNet-512, Laplace achieves FID-10K of 9.09 versus 11.91 for Cosine, and on CelebA-64 the improvement is 7.94 vs 10.07 at 100K iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Laplace schedule outperforms all six baselines on ImageNet-256. Against the Cosine baseline [35], Laplace reduces FID-10K from 11.06 to 7.96 at CFG=3.0 (−2.89). It also beats the loss-weighting methods Min-SNR [17] (10.43) and Soft-Min-SNR [9] (10.66) at CFG=3.0, demonstrating that reallocating compute via the noise schedule is more effective than adjusting loss weights. Compared to EDM [25] (11.56) and FM-OT [31] (11.98), Laplace achieves substantially lower FID under the same 500K-iteration budget. Cosine Shifted [23] (11.13) performs worse than the unshifted Cosine baseline, confirming that shifting away from λ=0 is detrimental. The Cosine Scaled variant (s=2) reaches 8.04 at CFG=2.0, nearly matching Laplace's 7.96 at CFG=3.0, suggesting the specific distributional form matters less than concentrating probability mass near log-SNR=0.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes noise schedule design in diffusion models as a probability distribution design problem over log-SNR, building on the unified ELBO formulation from VDM++ (Kingma & Gao, 2023) and the cosine schedule from Improved DDPM (Nichol & Dhariwal, 2021). It departs from the loss-weighting line of work (Min-SNR, Soft-Min-SNR) by arguing that directly modifying the sampling distribution p(λ) is more effective than adjusting w(λ), even though the two are theoretically equivalent. The approach also subsumes and generalizes prior schedule modifications like the shifted cosine (Hoogeboom et al., 2023) and EDM's Gaussian noise-intensity sampling (Karras et al., 2022) under a single importance-sampling lens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, implementable recipe (Laplace schedule with b=0.5) that yields ~26% FID improvement over cosine at matched compute, and the unified p(λ) framework gives practitioners a principled way to reason about where to allocate training FLOPs. Limitations include evaluation only at DiT-B scale and 500K iterations, no exploration of inference-time schedule optimization, and the need for resolution-dependent hyperparameter tuning (e.g., b=0.75 at 512×512). Open questions include whether the log-SNR=0 concentration principle transfers to video, 3D, or language diffusion, and how to jointly optimize training and sampling schedules.
