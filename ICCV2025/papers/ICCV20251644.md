## Problem

Neural parametric models (NPMs) have achieved strong results for humans and animals but require large-scale 3D datasets with paired deformations, which do not exist for leaves. Leaves further present unique challenges: extreme shape diversity across species, high flexibility that defies a common skeleton, and the absence of any existing NPM for leaf geometry. This paper addresses both the data scarcity and the structural mismatch between conventional skinned models and leaf morphology.

## Input / Output

For training, the shape space consumes 2D leaf silhouette images (segmented from scanned photos), while the deformation space consumes paired 2D base shapes and 3D-scanned deformed meshes from the newly created DeformLeaf dataset (~300 pairs). For reconstruction, the model accepts point clouds, depth maps, or RGB-D observations of individual or multiple leaves, which are converted to SDF grids and segmented per-leaf before fitting. The output is a 3D deformed leaf mesh with a UV-mapped texture, parameterized by three latent codes (shape z_s, deformation z_d, texture z_t), enabling both instance-wise reconstruction and generative modeling via latent manipulation.

## Method

NeuraLeaf disentangles a leaf's 3D geometry into a 2D base shape and a 3D deformation, each governed by a separate latent code. The base shape is represented as a neural SDF (MLP conditioned on z_s) trained on 2D leaf images, then converted to a mesh; texture is generated by a CycleGAN mapping the binary mask to a UV texture map. For deformation, the paper introduces a skeleton-free skinning model using up to 1,000 control points (far exceeding the ~20–30 typical for articulated bodies), where a skinning-weights decoder (conditioned on z_s) predicts per-vertex weights and a transformation decoder (conditioned only on z_d) predicts rigid transforms per control point, ensuring shape-independent deformation. Deformation training proceeds in two stages: first optimizing chamfer distance with a novel deformation-mapping loss that aligns latent norm to deformation magnitude, then generalizing to unpaired shapes via boundary-length and face-angle losses. Fitting to observations uses 3D-convolutional encoders for latent code inversion followed by gradient-based refinement of the latent codes against a bidirectional chamfer loss.

## Results

On the DeformLeaf test set (30 single-leaf point clouds), NeuraLeaf achieves a C-ℓ₂ of 2.1 mm and normal consistency (NC) of 0.973, substantially outperforming all baselines. The closest competitor, the human-oriented NPM extended to leaves, reaches 15.1 mm C-ℓ₂ and 0.961 NC, while the PCA-based parametric leaf model and B-spline model achieve 32.7 mm and 26.7 mm C-ℓ₂ respectively. For multiple-leaf reconstruction from RGB-D observations, NeuraLeaf with shared shape latents achieves 31.74 mm C-ℓ₂ and 0.894 NC, compared to 57.29 mm and 0.856 NC for the NPM baseline with the same sharing strategy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuraLeaf decisively outperforms all three baselines on both C-ℓ₂ and NC for single-leaf reconstruction. Against the PCA-based parametric leaf model [4], NeuraLeaf reduces C-ℓ₂ from 32.7 mm to 2.1 mm (≈15× improvement) and improves NC from 0.924 to 0.973, while also supporting correspondence-free and template-free fitting that PCA does not. Against the B-spline model [11], NeuraLeaf achieves a 12.7× reduction in C-ℓ₂ (26.7 → 2.1 mm) and a 0.016 NC gain, at the cost of higher inference time (55 s vs. 18 s). Against the human-oriented NPM [30] extended to leaves, NeuraLeaf reduces C-ℓ₂ from 15.1 mm to 2.1 mm (≈7× improvement) and improves NC from 0.961 to 0.973, while also being correspondence-free (the NPM baseline requires correspondences). In the multiple-leaf setting, NeuraLeaf with shared shape latents (31.74 mm, 0.894 NC) nearly halves the C-ℓ₂ of the NPM baseline with the same sharing strategy (57.29 mm, 0.856 NC), and dramatically outperforms PCA (1311.3 mm) and B-spline (159.34 mm).

## Relation to Prior Work

NeuraLeaf extends the neural parametric model (NPM) paradigm established for humans and animals (e.g., NPMs [30], SPAMs [31]) to the domain of leaves, where prior work relied on PCA-based morphable models [4] or B-spline curve representations [2, 11] that lacked deformation modeling. The key conceptual departure is the biological insight that flattened leaves approximate a 2D plane, enabling a disentangled representation where the 2D base shape is learned from large-scale 2D image datasets via a neural SDF, while 3D deformation is captured by a novel skeleton-free linear blend skinning model with up to 1,000 control points—contrasting with the skeletal structures used in articulated-object NPMs. Texture generation is cast as a CycleGAN-based image-to-image translation from mask to UV map, and the deformation mapping loss (L_map) is a new regularization that aligns latent norm with geometric deformation magnitude.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NeuraLeaf provides the first neural parametric model for leaves and introduces DeformLeaf, the first 3D leaf deformation dataset with dense correspondences, filling a critical data gap that has hindered data-driven leaf modeling. The disentangled 2D-shape/3D-deformation representation has practical implications for agriculture (leaf area measurement, growth monitoring) and CG (deformation transfer across species), and the skeleton-free skinning with many control points opens a direction for modeling fine-grained, highly flexible deformations beyond leaves. Limitations include the limited species diversity in DeformLeaf (~300 pairs) and the difficulty of optimizing texture codes for fine-grained appearance, leaving open questions about scalability to broader botanical diversity and photorealistic rendering.
