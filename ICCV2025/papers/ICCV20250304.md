## Problem

Existing VLA models cannot achieve second-level real-time decision-making in complex 3D combat environments: RL-based approaches require extensive reward engineering and trial-and-error, while VLM-based agents (Cradle, VARP) relying on ultra-large models like GPT-4o incur 60–90 second latencies that are incompatible with real-time gameplay. Additionally, no benchmark exists to systematically evaluate a model's combat understanding (perception, comprehension, and tactical reasoning) in 3D action role-playing games.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250304/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes real-time game video frames sampled at over 60 FPS (with redundant frames removed to reduce inference load) and produces a JSON-formatted action sequence containing a [action] field (e.g., 'press space', 'left mouse button') and an [explanation] field describing the tactical rationale. A special <TRUNC> token terminates generation early, so only the action tokens preceding it are parsed and translated into pyautogui keyboard/mouse commands. The action vocabulary comprises 10 discrete inputs (wsad movement, shift, space, r, 1, left/right mouse button) that can be combined.

## Method

CombatVLA is a 3B model built on Qwen2.5-VL-3B, trained via a three-stage progressive learning paradigm: (1) coarse-grained video-level AoT tuning where actions are loosely aligned to video segments, (2) fine-grained frame-level AoT tuning where actions are precisely aligned to k preceding frames, and (3) truncated AoT tuning where the explanation is reordered after the action and a <TRUNC> token enables early stopping. Training uses full-parameter SFT with a composite loss combining standard language modeling, a priority-aware action alignment loss, and a modality contrastive loss that pulls or pushes visual and action EOS embeddings based on whether the highest-priority action category matches. The action tracker collects human video-action pairs in the background, and the truncated inference strategy reduces per-inference latency to 1.85 s with a single model call, achieving a 50× speedup over VARP.

## Results

On CUBench, CombatVLA-3B achieves the highest average score of 63.61, surpassing the second-best Gemini-2.0-flash (57.90) by 5.71 points and its backbone Qwen2.5-VL-3B (55.87) by 7.74 points. In the high-level reasoning sub-task, CombatVLA scores 69.71, outperforming Claude3.5-Sonnet (55.43) by 14.28 points. In task-level practical tests, CombatVLA achieves a 50× speedup over VARP (1.85 s vs. 90.23 s per inference) with only 1 model call versus 10, and attains a higher combat task success rate than human players on several hard and very-hard tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CUBench, CombatVLA-3B (63.61 avg) outperforms all closed-source VLMs including Gemini-2.0-flash (57.90), GPT-4o-0513 (57.29), and Claude3.5-Sonnet (55.56), with the largest margin in the reasoning sub-task (69.71 vs. 55.43 for Claude3.5-Sonnet, +14.28). It also surpasses its own backbone Qwen2.5-VL-3B (55.87) by 7.74 points, confirming the benefit of AoT training. However, CombatVLA trails Gemini-1.5-pro on gathering (60.83 vs. 64.44) and GPT-4o-0513 on comprehension (60.29 vs. 66.67), indicating that the AoT paradigm primarily strengthens action reasoning rather than low-level perception. In task-level practical tests, CombatVLA achieves 1.85 s latency with 1 model call, compared to Cradle (61.68 s, 5 calls) and VARP (90.23 s, 10 calls), yielding a 50× speedup over VARP and reducing API costs to 1/10. On hard and very-hard combat tasks, CombatVLA surpasses both Cradle and VARP in success rate and even exceeds the average human player, while Cradle performs worst due to its reliance on explicit in-game text prompts and VARP shows poor cross-game generalization on Sekiro.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLA paradigm (RT-2, OpenVLA, DeeR-VLA) from robot manipulation and UI navigation to real-time 3D combat, where second-level response and high-resolution perception are critical. It departs from prior game-agent work (Cradle, VARP) that relies on ultra-large VLMs like GPT-4o with 60–90 s latency, instead using a 3B Qwen2.5-VL backbone with an action-of-thought (AoT) data format inspired by Chain-of-Thought. The modality contrastive loss adapts the visual-action embedding alignment from He et al. [15], and the progressive video→frame→truncated training schedule is a novel curriculum design not seen in prior VLA training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CombatVLA provides the first efficient (1.85 s, single call) VLA for 3D ARPG combat and introduces CUBench, the first benchmark for combat understanding, along with a reusable action tracker for collecting video-action data. The 50× speedup over VLM-based agents and the demonstration that a 3B model can outperform GPT-4o on domain-specific combat reasoning suggest that task-specific fine-tuning of small models can rival much larger general-purpose models in latency-sensitive settings. Open questions include generalization beyond the two tested games, whether the truncated-AoT strategy transfers to other action domains, and whether the approach scales to open-world scenarios without pausing the game during inference.
