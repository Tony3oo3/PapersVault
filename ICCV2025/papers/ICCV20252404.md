## Problem

Generating long, interactive videos that explore an imagined 3D scene along user-specified camera paths requires maintaining scene consistency over extended sequences, especially when revisiting previously seen regions. Existing outpainting-based methods accumulate errors in 3D reconstruction, while multi-view/video-based methods are limited to small context windows of recent frames due to quadratic attention cost, causing severe long-term inconsistencies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252404/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and a user-specified sequence of camera poses as input, generating M=4 novel view images autoregressively at each step for interactivity. Beyond the generated image sequence, the method maintains a persistent surfel-indexed view memory (a set of surfels each storing 3D position, surface normal, radius, and indices of past views that observed it) that grows over time and enables retrieval of geometrically relevant context views. The generator is conditioned on K retrieved reference views (K=4 or 17) along with Plücker embeddings of both reference and target camera poses.

## Method

The core contribution is Surfel-Indexed View Memory (VMem), a plug-and-play module that geometrically indexes past views using surfels rather than relying on temporal recency or camera proximity. For reading, surfels are rendered from the target viewpoint as splats with their stored view indices as attributes; the top-K most frequently appearing indices across rendered pixels are selected to retrieve the most relevant past views. For writing, an off-the-shelf point map estimator (CUT3R) estimates geometry of newly generated views, which is downsampled and converted to surfels with normals computed from neighboring pixel cross-products and radii scaled by depth and viewing angle; new surfels are merged into the existing index by proximity and normal similarity. The retrieved views condition a diffusion-based image-set generator (SEVA, fine-tuned with LoRA for K=4 efficiency) to synthesize novel views. This geometric retrieval strategy achieves comparable quality to using K=17 views with only K=4 context views, yielding a 12× inference speedup (4.2s vs 50s per frame on RTX 4090).

## Results

On the proposed cycle-trajectory protocol (RealEstate10K), VMem (K=17) achieves LPIPS 0.304, PSNR 18.15, SSIM 0.377, and Tdist 0.165, substantially outperforming all baselines. The lightweight VMem (K=4) variant achieves PSNR 15.72 and Tdist 0.392 while delivering a 12× speedup (4.2 s vs. 50 s per frame on an RTX 4090). On the standard RealEstate10K benchmark (Table 1), VMem (K=17) reaches PSNR 14.09 and LPIPS 0.452 at the ≥200th frame, and VMem (K=4) achieves PSNR 18.49 and LPIPS 0.287 at the 50th frame, both leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VMem outperforms all seven baselines on the cycle-trajectory protocol (Table 2). Against SEVA (K=17), the backbone generator, VMem (K=17) improves PSNR from 11.82 to 18.15 (+6.33 dB) and Tdist from 0.492 to 0.165, while the lightweight VMem (K=4) still achieves PSNR 15.72 and Tdist 0.392, beating SEVA on 5 of 6 metrics with only 4 context views. ViewCrafter and SEVA (K=17) report identical numbers in Table 2, suggesting ViewCrafter's results were reproduced via the SEVA backbone. GenWarp and MotionCtrl trail VMem (K=4) by 4.6 and 6.6 dB in PSNR respectively, while Look-out degrades most severely (PSNR 8.41, LPIPS 0.809). On the standard RealEstate10K benchmark (Table 1), VMem (K=4) leads all methods at the 50th frame (PSNR 18.49, LPIPS 0.287), and VMem (K=17) leads at the ≥200th frame (PSNR 14.09, LPIPS 0.452), with GeoGPT and PhotoNVS not evaluated on the cycle protocol. On out-of-domain Tanks-and-Temples (Table 3), VMem (K=4) again leads on PSNR (14.11) and Tdist (0.387), confirming generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

VMem builds directly on SEVA as its backbone generator, positioning itself within the multi-view/video-based autoregressive novel view synthesis lineage (GeoGPT, LookOut, MotionCtrl, SEVA). It departs from this lineage by replacing the fixed temporal context window with a geometrically-aware retrieval mechanism: rather than conditioning on the most recent K frames, it retrieves the K most spatially relevant past views via a surfel-indexed memory. This contrasts with outpainting-based methods (SceneScape, ViewCrafter, GenWarp) that accumulate 3D reconstruction errors, and with concurrent memory-based approaches (Genie, WorldMem, StarGen) that use distance- or FoV-based retrieval without explicit occlusion modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play surfel memory module offers a practical path toward long-term consistent interactive scene exploration, with a 12× inference speedup (4.2s vs 50s per frame on RTX 4090) that moves the task closer to real-time interactivity. The proposed cycle-trajectory evaluation protocol exposes a critical blind spot in existing benchmarks—trajectories that revisit previously observed regions—making it a useful addition for the field. Open questions include scalability to very large open-world scenes, robustness when the off-the-shelf point map estimator (CUT3R) produces poor geometry, and whether the approach generalizes to outdoor or highly dynamic scenes beyond the indoor-focused evaluation.
