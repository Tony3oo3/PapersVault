## Problem

Existing debiasing methods for vision-language models are tailored to specific downstream tasks and require annotated datasets with sensitive attribute labels, which undermines the zero-shot generalizability that makes VLMs like CLIP valuable. These approaches also face practical barriers: overfitting to narrow tasks, poor transferability across modalities, and regulatory constraints on collecting sensitive data. The paper asks whether one can debias a VLM in a fully task-agnostic manner by specifying only the target bias concept (e.g., 'gender bias') without any downstream task annotations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two inputs at training time: (1) a bias text corpus of ~2,550 short prompts (e.g., 'A photo of a male', 'A portrait of a woman') generated by an LLM and encoded by the frozen CLIP text encoder, and (2) a generic vision dataset aggregated from public benchmarks (UTKFace, LFW, FACET) whose images are encoded by the frozen CLIP image encoder. At inference, the debiased image embedding is produced by replacing the bias adaptor output with random Gaussian noise, yielding a bias-neutral embedding in the original CLIP feature space that can be directly plugged into zero-shot classification (cosine similarity with text prompts), retrieval (ranking by similarity), or image editing (StyleCLIP optimization).

## Method

ZSDebias places three lightweight 2-layer MLP adaptors (bias, neutral, reconstruction) on top of frozen CLIP image embeddings, structured as a variational autoencoder. The key mechanism is using Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) with an RBF kernel as a representational similarity regularizer: the bias adaptor is trained to maximize CKA with top-k ensembled bias text embeddings (semantically matched per image), while the neutral adaptor is trained to minimize CKA with the bias representation, promoting statistical independence. A swap-based reconstruction loss (inspired by feature translation) forces both subspaces to carry complementary information, and an ELBO term preserves the original embedding geometry. At inference, the bias component is simply replaced with N(0,I) noise, so all downstream tasks operate on the bias-neutral embedding alone—no task-specific fine-tuning or sensitive labels are ever needed.

## Results

On CelebA zero-shot classification, ZSDebias achieves the lowest worst-group/average accuracy gap across all baselines: 1.68% with CLIP ResNet-50 (WG 83.16%, Avg 84.85%) and 2.42% with CLIP ViT-L/14 (WG 78.18%, Avg 80.60%), while maintaining competitive average accuracy. On FairFace zero-shot retrieval, ZSDebias reduces MaxSkew@1000 to 0.1238 (RN50) and 0.1676 (ViT-L/14), outperforming DBP and DEAR on the ViT-L/14 backbone but trailing CLIP-clip, which achieves the lowest skew at the cost of substantially degraded classification accuracy (Gap of 21.43% on ViT-L/14).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CelebA, ZSDebias achieves the best (lowest) accuracy gap on both backbones—1.68% (RN50) and 2.42% (ViT-L/14)—beating ConAdapt (6.99%/5.44%), CLIP-clip (4.01%/21.43%), DBP (7.43%/10.35%), B2T (11.76%/11.45%), and RoboShot (9.29%/17.35%). However, ZSDebias trades some average accuracy: ConAdapt reaches 91.43% Avg (RN50) and 89.89% Avg (ViT-L/14) versus ZSDebias's 84.85% and 80.60%, respectively, because ConAdapt uses task-specific annotated data. On FairFace retrieval, ZSDebias (MaxSkew 0.1238 RN50 / 0.1676 ViT-L/14) outperforms DBP (0.1296/0.2802) and DEAR (0.1254/0.2076) on both backbones, but is surpassed by CLIP-clip (0.0776/0.0334), which removes bias features more aggressively at the cost of a 21.43% classification gap on ViT-L/14. The key distinction is that ZSDebias requires no sensitive-attribute annotations or task-specific prompts, whereas all compared baselines need at least one form of downstream supervision or task-specific prior knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLM debiasing line of work (CLIP-clip, DBP, ConAdapt, B2T, DEAR) by removing the dependency on task-specific annotated datasets, building most directly on Chuang et al.'s (DBP) prompt-based approach but eliminating even the need for downstream task context. Its core technical novelty is repurposing Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA), previously used only as an evaluation metric for representational similarity, as a training regularizer to drive VAE-based disentanglement of image embeddings into bias and neutral subspaces. The bias text corpus—curated from LLM-generated demographic descriptors—serves as a proxy for sensitive-attribute supervision, replacing the labeled data that prior methods require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: by requiring only a target bias name and public image datasets, ZSDebias sidesteps GDPR constraints, annotation costs, and task-specific overfitting that limit existing debiasing methods in real-world deployment. However, the evaluation is confined to demographic (gender) bias on two face-centric datasets (CelebA, FairFace), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to non-demographic biases (e.g., object-background spurious correlations), multiple simultaneous biases, or non-face domains. The method also trades some average accuracy for fairness (e.g., 84.85% vs. 91.43% for ConAdapt on CelebA ResNet-50), and the retrieval results do not uniformly dominate all baselines, suggesting the task-agnostic formulation still has room to close the gap with fully supervised approaches.
