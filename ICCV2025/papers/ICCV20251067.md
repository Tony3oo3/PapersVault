## Problem

Existing 3D reconstruction methods—visible multi-view stereo, structured light, photometric stereo, and shape from polarization—rely on strong assumptions about illumination conditions and material optical properties, and visible polarization is fundamentally limited by specular-diffuse ambiguities. This paper shows that long-wave infrared (LWIR) polarization, dominated by black-body emission, is free from these ambiguities and can serve as a robust, material-independent cue for detailed 3D shape recovery in steady state without active heating or cooling.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view thermal AoLP images (captured by rotating a wire-grid polarizer at 0°, 45°, 90°, and 135° in front of a 320×256 FLIR Boson 320 thermal camera), binary silhouette masks, and calibrated camera poses (estimated via ArUco markers on an adjacent visible camera and transformed to the thermal frame). It outputs a 3D mesh extracted from the learned SDF zero-level set along with per-vertex surface normals. Objects must be kept warm during capture to ensure the emission component dominates over reflection, and the low spatial resolution of the thermal sensor (320×256) is a practical constraint on achievable detail.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution is a unified polarization observation model showing that in the LWIR spectrum, the observed Stokes vector reduces to the emission component alone (s = s_e), with the Mueller matrix determined solely by the surface normal's zenith angle and the refractive index via Kirchhoff's law. This makes the Angle of Linear Polarization (AoLP) a direct, material-independent measurement of the surface normal's azimuth angle. For reconstruction, an 8-layer MLP with positional encoding (dimension 10) and skip connections represents the SDF, optimized via the IDR differentiable rendering framework. The loss combines a tangent space consistency (TSC) term—enforcing that the estimated normal is orthogonal to the projected tangent vector t(φ) = r₁cosφ − r₂sinφ derived from each view's AoLP—plus a silhouette cross-entropy loss and an Eikonal regularization. Training uses Adam (lr=0.001) for 50 epochs with batch size 4096, halving the silhouette weight every 10 epochs, taking ~2 hours on a single RTX A6000.

## Results

The paper evaluates on 7 real-world objects (ceramic owl, glass containers, black tumbler, vase, bottle, etc.) captured from 20–30 views, using Chamfer distance (geometric error) and mean angular error (surface normal error) as metrics. The proposed thermal polarimetric method outperforms both baselines on all objects in both metrics, with the smallest errors highlighted in magenta in Figures 4 and 5. Qualitatively, the method successfully recovers fine details such as concave features in transparent vases/bottles, relief patterns on glass containers, and surface normals on low-reflective black surfaces where visible polarization fails. No explicit numerical values are tabulated; the quantitative comparison is embedded in the figure annotations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: MVAS [Cao et al., CVPR 2023] using visible-light polarization, and IDR [Yariv et al., NeurIPS 2020] using thermal intensity images. Against MVAS, the proposed method wins on all objects because visible AoLP is corrupted by the specular-diffuse mixture, producing noisy azimuth estimates and wavy surface artifacts (e.g., the black tumbler's white lid is unrecoverable in visible polarization, while LWIR AoLP provides a clean signal). Against IDR with thermal input, the proposed method wins because IDR relies solely on intensity for silhouette constraints and does not exploit surface normal information, resulting in swollen or blurred shapes (e.g., concave details in the vase and bottle are lost, and the container lid has a large hole). The key advantage is that LWIR AoLP is analytically equivalent to the surface normal's azimuth angle and is independent of material properties, providing a robust normal cue that neither visible polarization (material-dependent) nor thermal intensity (no normal information) can match.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the tangent-space-consistency (TSC) loss and neural implicit surface framework from visible-light Multi-View Azimuth Stereo (MVAS, Cao et al. CVPR 2023) and IDR (Yariv et al. NeurIPS 2020) into the long-wave infrared (LWIR) polarization domain. The key conceptual departure is a theoretical result: because LWIR emission dominates over reflection in typical environments, the observed polarization reduces to a pure emission term governed by Kirchhoff's law, eliminating the specular-diffuse ambiguity that forces visible-light SfP methods to rely on material assumptions or multi-view disambiguation. This positions the work not as a refinement of visible polarization but as a fundamentally cleaner observation model that sidesteps the core difficulty of the visible-light problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that LWIR AoLP is material-invariant and illumination-independent opens a practical path to 3D reconstruction of transparent, translucent, and heterogeneous objects that defeat visible-light methods, without the active heating/cooling cycles required by thermal photometric stereo or heat-conduction approaches. However, the method assumes emission dominates reflection (failing near hot sources or in cold environments), is less reliable on metallic or very rough surfaces, and currently requires a mechanically rotating wire-grid polarizer rather than a snapshot sensor. The experimental validation, while consistent, covers only seven objects in a single controlled setup, leaving open questions about scalability to complex scenes, outdoor conditions, and the impact of higher-resolution thermal sensors.
