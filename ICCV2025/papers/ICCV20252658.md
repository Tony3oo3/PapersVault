## Problem

Existing forest point cloud segmentation methods rely on multi-step hand-crafted pipelines or non-differentiable clustering, limiting their adaptability across diverse forest types, sensor modalities, and structural complexities (dense overlapping canopies, multi-scale trees, variable point densities). No unified end-to-end framework existed that could jointly perform individual tree instance segmentation and semantic segmentation on complex, unstructured forest scenes while generalizing across regions and sensors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252658/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw 3D LiDAR point clouds using only spatial coordinates (no RGB, intensity, or return attributes), voxelized into a sparse tensor and processed in cylindrical blocks of 16 m radius to handle the large spatial extent of forest scenes. It produces per-point individual tree instance masks with confidence scores, plus three-class semantic labels (ground, wood, leaf), all generated jointly by a single transformer decoder.

## Method

ForestFormer3D uses a sparse 3D U-Net (SpConvUNet) backbone to extract voxel-wise features, which feed a six-layer transformer query decoder that concurrently predicts instance and semantic masks. Three key innovations distinguish it: (1) ISA-guided query point selection, where two parallel MLP heads produce a 5D instance-discriminative embedding and a binary tree/non-tree classification, and farthest point sampling is applied in the embedding space over tree-only voxels to yield high-coverage, low-background query points; (2) one-to-many association during training, which exploits the known spatial positions of selected queries to directly match each predicted mask to its GT instance, eliminating Hungarian matching and reducing false positives; (3) score-based block merging at inference, where all predicted masks across overlapping cylindrical blocks are globally ranked by confidence and NMS removes lower-scoring duplicates, with masks near crop boundaries discarded to avoid split-tree artifacts. The total loss combines BCE, Dice, and IoU-targeted score losses for instances with cross-entropy for semantics, summed across all six decoder layers, plus auxiliary binary and discriminative losses for the query-selection heads.

## Results

On the FOR-instanceV2 test set, ForestFormer3D achieves 82.8% F1 and 92.4% precision for individual tree segmentation, surpassing the second-best method (OneFormer3D at 73.1% F1) by 9.7 percentage points, and attains the highest coverage (Cov) of 81.2%. For semantic segmentation, it reaches 86.2% mIoU (ground 96.9%, wood 67.7%, leaf 94.1%), slightly below ForAINetV2_R16 (87.5% mIoU). On the unseen Wytham woods dataset, ForestFormer3D leads with 75.0% F1 and 66.9% Cov, while on LAUTx it ranks second to TreeLearn (90.5% vs. 92.1% F1) but still substantially outperforms all other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ForestFormer3D decisively outperforms OneFormer3D (the closest transformer-based baseline) on FOR-instanceV2, gaining +9.7 pp in F1 (82.8 vs. 73.1) and +20.4 pp in precision (92.4 vs. 72.0), while maintaining comparable recall (75.0 vs. 74.3). Against ForAINetV2 (both R8 and R16 variants), ForestFormer3D improves F1 by 10.4 and 12.0 pp respectively, with a notable precision advantage (+8.3 pp over R16), though ForAINetV2_R16 retains a slight edge in semantic mIoU (87.5 vs. 86.2). TreeLearn, designed for ground-based LiDAR, performs poorly on the mixed-sensor FOR-instanceV2 set (50.6% F1) due to sparse stem points in ULS data, and is excluded from the Yuchen region entirely. On the unseen Wytham woods dataset, ForestFormer3D leads all methods in F1 (75.0) and Cov (66.9), beating ForAINetV2_R16 by +9.9 pp F1 and OneFormer3D by +25.3 pp F1. On LAUTx (MLS data), TreeLearn narrowly edges out ForestFormer3D in F1 (92.1 vs. 90.5) and Cov (85.2 vs. 79.1), but ForestFormer3D still surpasses all other baselines by a wide margin (next best: ForAINetV2_R16 at 85.0% F1). SegmentAnyTree and the older Point2tree/TLS2trees methods are consistently outperformed across all three test sets.

## Relation to Prior Work

ForestFormer3D builds directly on the OneFormer3D transformer decoder architecture (Kolodiazhnyi et al., CVPR 2024), adopting its query-based mask prediction, six-layer decoder, and loss formulation, while replacing the Hungarian one-to-one matching with a one-to-many association strategy. It inherits the cylindrical cropping strategy from ForAINet (Xiang et al., 2024) for handling large forest scenes but departs fundamentally from the bottom-up clustering paradigm used by ForAINet and TreeLearn, introducing a fully differentiable end-to-end pipeline. The ISA-guided query point selection is a novel contribution that combines instance-discriminative 5D embeddings with semantic binary classification to outperform both random learnable queries and raw FPS, addressing a known weakness of prior query-based 3D instance segmentation methods in unstructured natural scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FOR-instanceV2 dataset (8× more trees than FOR-instance, spanning boreal, temperate, and tropical forests with multiple sensor modalities) fills a critical gap in forest LiDAR benchmarks and is publicly released, enabling broader community progress. The strong generalization to unseen forests (Wytham woods, LAUTx) and across sensor types (ULS, MLS) suggests the unified framework could serve as a practical baseline for operational forest inventory. Open questions include whether the slight mIoU trade-off in semantic segmentation (−1.3 pp) can be resolved, whether the method scales to even larger or denser tropical canopies, and how incorporating additional LiDAR attributes (intensity, return number) might further improve performance.
