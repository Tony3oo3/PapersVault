## Problem

Single-view 3D reconstruction faces a persistent trade-off: CNNs capture fine local geometry but lack global topological awareness, while Transformers and SSMs model long-range dependencies at the cost of fine detail. Additionally, using the same RGB features for both geometry and texture decoding causes texture signals to interfere with SDF optimization, destabilizing reconstruction in highly textured regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252415/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image, camera pose, and 2D/3D bounding boxes as input, and additionally consumes a depth prior extracted by a separate pretrained depth network at reduced resolution (H/s × W/s). The output is a continuous SDF field and view-dependent color for arbitrary 3D query points, rendered into pixel colors via NeRF-style volume rendering with adaptive point resampling near surfaces.

## Method

Dual-S3D employs a hierarchical dual-path encoder: the primary path first extracts local features with residual CNN blocks, then applies a windowed selective SSM mixer (Mamba-style: channel split, Conv1D, selective scan with input-dependent dt/A/B/C/D, concat, project back) followed by Transformer blocks for cross-window global fusion. An auxiliary path extracts depth priors from a pretrained network and dynamically fuses them with RGB features via a learnable scalar weight, decoupling geometric and visual cues. Training follows a curriculum: an early geometry stage relies on depth features to stabilize SDF learning, then a later texture stage gradually introduces RGB features. Decoding uses an MLP to predict SDF values converted to volume density via a Laplace function, combined with a NeRF-style rendering MLP for view-dependent color, regularized by an Eikonal loss and refined by PDF/CDF-based point resampling.

## Results

On 3D-FRONT, Dual-S3D achieves a mean Chamfer Distance of 6.60, F-Score of 80.85, and Normal Consistency of 0.901, outperforming the strongest baseline SSR (CD 10.45, F-Score 71.36, NC 0.854) by 36.8%, 13.3%, and 5.5% respectively. On the real-world Pix3D dataset, Dual-S3D records CD 19.90, F-Score 63.70, and NC 0.806, again leading all baselines. The paper reports overall improvements of 51% in CD, 33.6% in F-Score, and 10.3% in NC over prior methods. In terms of efficiency, the SSM-based encoder reduces FLOPs by 41.1% and parameters by 34.5% compared to a ViT-Base encoder, with inference time dropping from 3.51 s to 1.52 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dual-S3D outperforms all four implicit-reconstruction baselines (MGN, LIEN, InstPIFu, SSR) on every metric across both 3D-FRONT and Pix3D. The closest competitor, SSR, is beaten by 3.85 CD points (10.45→6.60) and 9.49 F-Score points (71.36→80.85) on 3D-FRONT, and by 1.89 CD and 3.99 F-Score on Pix3D. Against the diffusion-based Zero-1-to-3 and the generative Shape-E (Table 5), Dual-S3D also leads on CD, F-Score, and NC, indicating that its SSM-CNN encoder captures shape and texture more precisely than large-scale pretrained 2D priors. In the cross-dataset transfer experiment (trained on 3D-FRONT, tested on Pix3D), Dual-S3D still surpasses SSR and InstPIFu on all five metrics (CD, F-Score, PSNR, IoU, NC), demonstrating stronger portability. The efficiency comparison shows the SSM mixer uses 41.1% fewer FLOPs and 34.5% fewer parameters than a ViT-Base encoder while achieving 2.3× faster inference (1.52 s vs. 3.51 s on an RTX 3090).

## Relation to Prior Work

Dual-S3D sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the progression from CNN-only encoders (MGN, LIEN, InstPIFu) to Transformer-based global context modeling in single-view 3D reconstruction, (2) the recent adoption of selective state-space models (Mamba, MambaVision) as efficient alternatives to quadratic-complexity self-attention, and (3) NeRF-style volume rendering for implicit surface decoding. The paper's key architectural departure is a hierarchical CNN→SSM pipeline within a single encoder, combined with a dual-path design that routes precomputed depth features through a separate branch to decouple geometric and textural signals before SDF optimization—a strategy borrowed from cross-modal fusion literature but applied here specifically to stabilize SDF training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-path decoupling of depth and RGB features offers a practical recipe for reducing texture-induced interference in SDF optimization, a known pain point in implicit reconstruction. The 41% FLOP reduction over a ViT-based encoder makes SSM-based 3D reconstruction more accessible on consumer hardware. However, the authors acknowledge that performance degrades on real-world data (Pix3D) relative to synthetic (3D-FRONT), and the cross-dataset transfer experiment (Table 4) shows substantial metric drops, leaving open the question of how well the framework generalizes under data scarcity. Future work on semi-supervised or self-supervised training to close the synthetic-to-real gap is a natural next step.
