## Problem

Standard mass-spring (MS) hair models are computationally cheap but suffer from instability, excessive sagging, and loss of global strand structure, while more accurate models like Discrete Elastic Rods (DER) are too expensive for real-time simulation of dense hair (10k+ strands). The paper addresses this gap by augmenting the MS formulation with minimal additional physics to recover global structural integrity without sacrificing the efficiency that makes MS attractive for interactive applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251814/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a discretized hair strand geometry (N particles per strand), a corresponding ghost rest-shape configuration, external force fields (e.g., wind), and a pre-computed signed distance field of the head for collision response. It produces per-particle positions and velocities at each time step, driving real-time rendering of 1k–15k strands at 60+ FPS on a consumer GPU (NVIDIA GTX 3080 Ti) with peak memory under 1 GB. The Eulerian hair-hair interaction stage operates on a 128³ dynamic grid that moves rigidly with the head mesh, and the entire pipeline is implemented in C++/CUDA.

## Method

The AMS model augments the standard MS edge/bending/torsion springs with a one-way biphasic coupling to a rigid ghost rest-shape: an integrity spring (κ_I) penalizing point-to-point displacement between each particle and its ghost, and an angular spring (κ_α) penalizing the angle between the real segment and its ghost counterpart. Both are connected in parallel per particle-ghost pair and use coupling constants several orders of magnitude below local spring stiffness, so they preserve global shape without over-constraining local dynamics. Because the ghost is one-way (rigid), only the N real particles are solved per step via a heptadiagonal backward-Euler system, halving the matrix size relative to the 2N−1 coupled particles in Selle et al.'s MS. Hair-hair interactions use a two-stage hybrid Eulerian/Lagrangian scheme: hair segments are rasterized into a dynamic FLIP/PIC volume for coarse velocity transfer, followed by detailed Lagrangian collision resolution. Hair-solid collisions are handled via SDF-based velocity projection and positional correction, and non-Hookean responses (e.g., roller-coaster-level deformation) are captured by parametrizing the integrity coupling nonlinearly.

## Results

The AMS framework achieves real-time simulation of dense hair on a consumer GPU (NVIDIA GeForce GTX 3080 Ti) with peak memory of only 0.58 GB. Headline performance includes 14,718 strands at 67 FPS under wind, 7,528 strands at 156 FPS for hair-face contact, and 10,298 strands at 114 FPS for complex object interaction. For the 10,422-strand wig asset, per-frame simulation time is 1.9 ms, and for 1024 strands it drops to 0.04 ms — roughly 30× faster than the hybrid Cosserat-MPM baseline [7] which reports 1.2 ms for the same strand count.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the hybrid Cosserat-MPM model of Hsu et al. [7], AMS is dramatically faster: 0.04 ms per frame for 1024 strands versus 1.2 ms reported by [7], a ~30× speedup, while scaling to 10,422 strands in just 1.9 ms. Additionally, [7] requires a dedicated sag-free initialization procedure to prevent artifacts, whereas AMS inherently prevents sagging through its integrity coupling without any special initialization. Against the neural interpolation approach of CT2Hair [19], AMS wins on interaction fidelity: in the hollow-sphere experiment (Figure 8), the neural method (128 guide strands + interpolation) produces visible interpolation artifacts and object penetration because it cannot account for new scene geometry or updated head poses, while AMS with 10,298 fully simulated strands accurately resolves hair-solid contact. In ablation comparisons against standard mass-spring (MS), AMS preserves global strand shape at initialization where MS collapses or sags, and remains stable at spring constants orders of magnitude lower than what MS requires, enabling larger time steps and real-time execution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical mass-spring formulation for hair (Selle et al., 2008) by replacing the two-way coupled ghost particles and altitude springs with a one-way biphasic coupling to a frozen rest-shape, adding integrity and angular springs that preserve global strand structure without the numerical stiffness that plagues traditional MS systems. It adopts the heptadiagonal matrix decomposition of Jiang et al. (2020) for efficient solving and borrows the hybrid Eulerian/Lagrangian interaction scheme of McAdams et al. (2009) for hair-hair collisions. Conceptually, the paper positions AMS as a middle ground between the simplicity of MS and the physical fidelity of Discrete Elastic Rods, arguing that a carefully designed augmentation to a cheap model can close much of the visual gap without the computational overhead of DER or the data-dependence of neural approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for real-time applications: simulating 10,000+ strands at 60+ FPS on a consumer GPU with under 1 GB of VRAM makes dense, strand-level hair feasible in games and interactive avatars, a regime previously limited to guide-strand interpolation or expensive DER solvers. The paper also demonstrates that physics-based grooming and interaction (trimming, hand contact, roller-coaster forces) remain physically coherent in ways that neural interpolation methods cannot guarantee, since the simulated strands carry embedded dynamic information. Limitations include the approximation of real-world physics (no full compliance with continuum mechanics), the reliance on a pre-computed head SDF for solid collisions, and the open question of whether the biphasic coupling generalizes to other thin-structure simulations such as cloth, which the authors flag as future work.
