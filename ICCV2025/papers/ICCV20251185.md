## Problem

Mamba-based vision models must flatten 2D images into 1D sequences and compute hidden states recursively, introducing sequential latency and limiting spatial modeling. This makes them lag behind attention-based transformers on vision understanding tasks despite their linear complexity advantage. The paper asks whether the Mamba operator can be parallelized to break sequential constraints while preserving linear computational cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251185/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image, partitions it into 4×4 pixel patches to form N tokens, and processes them through four hierarchical stages with progressive downsampling (halving spatial size, doubling channels). For classification it outputs a single class label; for detection it produces bounding boxes with class labels via Mask R-CNN; for segmentation it outputs per-pixel class masks via UperNet. The key architectural detail is that hidden states are computed by spatial aggregation over the 2D feature map rather than sequential scanning, so no explicit 1D ordering of patches is required.

## Method

PVMamba parallelizes the SSM hidden-state computation through three steps: (1) parallelization replaces the recursive dependency h_k = Ā_k·h_{k-1} + B̄_k·x_k with a direct spatial aggregation h_k = Σ_{w∈Ω} P_Ω(Ā)·x_w over a set of sampled input pixels; (2) alignment simplifies the transition matrix Ā from R^{L×C} to R^{L×1} so it can be spatially aligned with the 2D feature map; (3) aggregation reformulates the cumulative product of transition matrices as a difference (Ā_b − Ā_a), enabling a weighted sum over sampled locations. Two DSA variants are used: Lo-DSA applies a K_l×K_l average pooling over neighboring pixels in shallow stages for local modeling, while Sa-DSA predicts K_d learnable sampling offsets per token via a lightweight linear layer and retrieves features with differentiable bilinear interpolation in deep stages for long-range modeling. A layer-multiplexing strategy reuses sampling points across stacked Sa-DSA layers to prevent training collapse from dense sampling and reduce computational overhead.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification, PVMamba-T achieves 83.9% Top-1 accuracy with only 24M parameters and 4.5G FLOPs, PVMamba-S reaches 84.2% with 40M/7.4G, and PVMamba-B attains 84.8% with 89M/16.1G. On COCO object detection (Mask R-CNN 1×+MS), PVMamba-T/S/B achieve 47.1/48.6/49.7 box AP and 42.6/43.7/44.5 mask AP. On ADE20K semantic segmentation (UperNet), PVMamba-T/S/B reach 48.2/50.8/51.4% mIoU, consistently outperforming prior Mamba and transformer backbones at comparable model cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PVMamba-T (83.9%) outperforms VMamba-T (82.2%) by 1.7% and LocalVMamba-T (82.7%) by 1.2% on ImageNet-1K, while also surpassing MLLA-T (83.5%) and InternImage-T (83.5%) by 0.4% with fewer parameters (24M vs. 25M/30M). At the small scale, PVMamba-S (84.2%) matches InternImage-S (84.2%) while using fewer FLOPs (7.4G vs. 8.0G) and fewer parameters (40M vs. 50M), and beats VMamba-S (83.5%) by 0.7%. At the base scale, PVMamba-B (84.8%) trails InternImage-B (84.9%) by a marginal 0.1% but outperforms VMamba-B (83.9%) by 0.9% and DAT-B (84.0%) by 0.8%. On COCO detection (1×+MS), PVMamba-T achieves 47.1 AP, surpassing VMamba-T (46.5) by 0.6 AP and LocalVMamba-T (46.7) by 0.4 AP; PVMamba-S reaches 48.6 AP vs. VMamba-S (48.2) and LocalVMamba-S (48.4). On ADE20K segmentation, PVMamba-T (48.2% mIoU) edges out VMamba-T (48.0%) and LocalMamba-T (47.9%), while PVMamba-B (51.4%) leads VMamba-B (51.0%) by 0.4%.

## Relation to Prior Work

PVMamba builds directly on the Mamba/SSM lineage (Mamba [8], VMamba [31], ViM [71]) but departs from the dominant strategy of improving 2D-to-1D scanning patterns (e.g., bidirectional scans, quadtree partitioning in LocalMamba [22]). Instead, it parallelizes the SSM hidden-state computation entirely by replacing recursive state propagation with a spatial aggregation operator, drawing conceptual inspiration from deformable convolutions (DCN [3], DCNv2 [72], InternImage [53]) for the dynamic sampling mechanism. The hybrid design—local pooling in shallow layers and deformable spatial-adaptive sampling in deep layers—creates a bridge between CNN-style local processing and attention-style global modeling within a single SSM framework, a combination not previously explored in vision Mamba architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the sequential bottleneck, PVMamba offers a hardware-friendly parallel pipeline that avoids the complex associative-scan and hardware-optimization tricks required by standard Mamba, making SSM-based vision backbones more accessible and easier to deploy. The layer multiplexing technique addresses a practical training-stability issue (collapse from dense sampling) that would otherwise limit the adoption of deformable operators in SSM contexts. Open questions remain: whether the parallel formulation preserves the long-range causal modeling that makes SSMs attractive for video and multi-modal tasks, and whether the approach scales beyond the modest 0.5–1.5% gains observed over VMamba to larger model regimes or new task settings.
