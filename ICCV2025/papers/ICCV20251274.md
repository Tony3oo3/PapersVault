## Problem

Existing visual speech recognition systems rely exclusively on visual input, yet human lip-reading naturally incorporates peripheral cues such as speech context, accumulated task experience, and the ability to filter irrelevant signals. This paper introduces the concept of 'peripheral information'—categorized into contextual guidance, task expertise, and linguistic perturbation—and investigates how systematically integrating these multi-level external cues can improve VSR in challenging real-world conditions where visual signals are degraded or ambiguous.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251274/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a lip-centered video clip (processed at 25 fps by a pretrained AV-HuBERT encoder) along with structured text prompts containing contextual guidance (speech title, description, speaker info), a task-aware instruction with an explicit output constraint phrase, and optionally injected linguistic perturbation tokens (random tokens or natural-text fragments). Visual features are 2× downsampled and linearly projected into the Llama 3.1 8B embedding space, then concatenated along the temporal axis with the tokenized text embeddings. The output is an autoregressively generated speech transcript, preceded by a mandatory reasoning phrase (e.g., 'After carefully consideration, the identified sentence is') that enforces an explicit self-monitoring step before transcription.

## Method

The framework has two stages: (1) multimodal encoding, where a pretrained AV-HuBERT visual encoder extracts spatiotemporal lip-motion features that are downsampled and projected into the LLM's embedding space, and peripheral information is formatted as structured text and tokenized; (2) LLM decoding, where Llama 3.1 8B generates the transcript autoregressively. The key architectural contribution is Synergy LoRA, a parameter-efficient adaptation module applied to the query and value projections of all attention layers. It consists of a dedicated visual-specific LoRA path (rank 96) that independently adapts visual features, plus a Mixture-of-Experts LoRA module (8 experts, rank 4 each) for the full multimodal sequence, where all experts share the same A matrix but have distinct B matrices selected by a softmax-gated router. This design lets the dominant visual modality be processed first without interference, while the MoE dynamically routes textual peripheral information to specialized experts, enabling hierarchical utilization of cues with varying relevance. Training uses standard negative log-likelihood fine-tuning on LRS3 (1,759 h unlabelled + 433 h labelled), with linguistic perturbation injected during training to build robustness against irrelevant signals.

## Results

On LRS3-TED, the proposed method achieves a WER of 22.03% with full peripheral information (contextual guidance + task expertise + linguistic perturbation), outperforming the best prior method trained on comparable data (Haliassos et al., 22.3%). Without peripheral information, the model still achieves 24.92% WER, surpassing LLM-based baselines (Cappellazzo et al., 25.3%; Yeo et al., 25.4%). On the more challenging AVSpeech dataset, the full configuration reduces WER from 46.24% (no peripheral info, Synergy LoRA) to 40.23%, demonstrating consistent gains in real-world scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's method (22.03% WER) outperforms all prior approaches trained on the same 1,759 h unlabelled + 433 h labelled LRS3 data, including the closest competitor Haliassos et al. [15] (22.3%) and LLM-based methods Cappellazzo et al. [7] (25.3%) and Yeo et al. [46] (25.4%). Even without peripheral information (24.92%), it surpasses Prajwal et al. [34] (24.3% with extra audio) and matches or exceeds Cappellazzo et al. [7] and Yeo et al. [46]. The method does not compete with systems trained on massive non-public datasets (Chang et al. [8] at 12.8% on 100k hours; Serdyuk et al. [37] at 17.0% on 90k hours), which operate in a fundamentally different data regime. On AVSpeech, the full configuration (40.23%) consistently outperforms the no-peripheral-info baseline (46.24%) by 6.01 WER points, and Synergy LoRA provides an additional 4.35-point improvement over vanilla LoRA (44.58%) under the same full-peripheral-info setting, confirming the architectural advantage in handling multi-level information.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-based VSR line of work (Cappellazzo et al. [7], Yeo et al. [46]) by introducing peripheral information—contextual guidance, task expertise prompts, and linguistic perturbation—as auxiliary textual inputs alongside visual features. The adaptation mechanism builds directly on LoRA [16], but departs from standard single-module LoRA by proposing Synergy LoRA, which combines a visual-specific adaptation path with a Mixture-of-Experts module that shares A matrices across experts while routing B matrices. Conceptually, the paper draws on context-aware ASR literature [4, 6, 17, 20] but broadens the notion of 'context' to include task-relevant cognitive priors and even irrelevant noise, framing them under a unified 'peripheral information' taxonomy that has no direct precedent in VSR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper opens a new axis of improvement for VSR beyond visual feature engineering, suggesting that readily available metadata (titles, descriptions, speaker info) and structured prompting can yield consistent gains across datasets. However, the absolute improvement over the strongest comparable baseline (22.03% vs. 22.3% on LRS3) is modest, and the approach's practical value depends on the availability of peripheral information, which is not guaranteed in all deployment scenarios. Open questions include whether the gains persist with smaller LLMs or without the specific prompt engineering for task expertise, and whether the linguistic perturbation strategy generalizes to non-English or low-resource settings. The Synergy LoRA design, while effective here, remains untested in other multimodal adaptation contexts.
