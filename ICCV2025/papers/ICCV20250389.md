## Problem

Unified image restoration methods either require task-specific architectures that fail to generalize across degradation types, or depend on paired training datasets that impose closed-set constraints. The paper addresses the gap of achieving high-quality restoration across diverse, unseen degradations in a truly dataset-free, zero-shot manner without any prior knowledge of the corruption type.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250389/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image and a short text prompt (≤20 words) auto-generated by a multimodal LLM describing the scene content. It operates in the 4×-downsampled latent space of a pretrained VAE, and outputs a restored full-resolution RGB image. No paired clean image, degradation label, or task identifier is required at inference time.

## Method

LD-RPS performs controllable posterior sampling within a pretrained latent diffusion model, guided by MLLM-generated text embeddings for semantic consistency. A lightweight Feature and Pixel Alignment Module (F-PAM) — comprising a frozen VAE decoder plus degradation-learning convolutions with channel attention — is trained at test time to map generated latent features into the degraded image domain, bridging both the latent-to-pixel space gap and the clean-to-degraded domain gap. The posterior sampling gradient combines a composite distance loss (MSE, perceptual, and adversarial terms) with a quality loss enforcing luminance and chrominance constraints. A recurrent refinement strategy then re-initializes the reverse diffusion from the previous iteration's output (partially re-noised to γT steps), progressively reducing artifacts and color casts across multiple passes.

## Results

On LOLv1 (low-light enhancement), LD-RPS achieves PSNR 17.45, SSIM 0.804, and NIQE 5.52, outperforming the best zero-shot baseline ZERO-IG (PSNR 17.22, SSIM 0.794) and the posterior sampling baseline TAO (PSNR 15.84, SSIM 0.757). On RESIDE (dehazing), LD-RPS reaches PSNR 21.45, surpassing TAO (18.38) and GDP (13.15) by 3.07 and 8.30 dB respectively. On Kodak24 (denoising), LD-RPS obtains PSNR 28.64 and SSIM 0.841, exceeding TAO (27.72 / 0.815) while remaining competitive with task-specific zero-shot methods like ZS-N2N (30.14 / 0.862).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two posterior sampling baselines, LD-RPS consistently wins: on LOLv1 it beats GDP by 0.93 dB PSNR and TAO by 1.61 dB; on RESIDE it surpasses GDP by 8.30 dB and TAO by 3.07 dB; on Kodak24 it exceeds TAO by 0.92 dB (GDP is inapplicable to denoising). Against task-specific zero-shot methods, LD-RPS is competitive but not always dominant: on LOLv1 it edges out ZERO-IG by 0.23 dB PSNR while ZERO-IG achieves lower LPIPS (0.184 vs. 0.277); on RESIDE it beats YOLY by 0.96 dB PSNR but YOLY has lower LPIPS (0.108 vs. 0.177); on Kodak24, ZS-N2N leads by 1.50 dB PSNR. Supervised unified methods (DiffUIR, AirNet, PromptIR) achieve higher full-reference scores (e.g., DiffUIR 21.36 dB on LOLv1) but are closed-set and cannot generalize to unseen degradations, whereas LD-RPS operates fully zero-shot and dataset-free. On no-reference metrics (PI, NIQE), LD-RPS matches or exceeds even the supervised DiffUIR, suggesting comparable perceptual quality without any training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

LD-RPS extends the posterior sampling framework for image restoration (GDP, Fei et al. 2023) by moving from pixel-space to latent-space diffusion and replacing the linear degradation operator (y = Ax + B) with a learnable Feature-and-Pixel Alignment Module (F-PAM) that implicitly models complex, unknown degradations. The test-time degradation adaptation paradigm is inherited from TAO (Gou et al. 2024), whose loss formulation directly informs the F-PAM training objective. The key conceptual departure is the recurrent bootstrap mechanism, which re-initializes the reverse diffusion from a previously restored image rather than from pure noise, a strategy not present in either GDP or TAO. Additionally, the use of an MLLM to generate task-blind semantic prompts for classifier guidance is a novel integration point absent from prior posterior sampling methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot, dataset-free nature of LD-RPS addresses a practical bottleneck in unified restoration: the need for large paired datasets covering all degradation types, which is both costly and inherently closed-set. The recurrent refinement strategy offers a general-purpose mechanism for improving diffusion-based restoration quality without retraining, potentially transferable to other inverse problems. However, the approach remains computationally expensive (multiple full diffusion passes plus MLLM inference per image), and its reliance on a frozen pretrained diffusion model means it inherits that model's biases (e.g., preference for certain content categories). Open questions include whether the recurrent mechanism scales to video restoration, whether the F-PAM can be distilled into a single-pass model, and how performance degrades under severely compound degradations beyond the tested combinations.
