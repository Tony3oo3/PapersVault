## Problem

Existing data attribution methods for text-to-image generative models operate only at the whole-image level, treating each training image as an indivisible unit, and are not scalable to models with billions of parameters. This paper tackles the more granular and practically useful question: which specific regions within training images most strongly influence a given region of a generated image, enabling copyright tracing, error diagnosis, and targeted correction at the source.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250514/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The AR detector takes three inputs: a training image X, a generated image Q with a bounding box R_Q specifying the region of interest, and the text prompt p used to generate Q. It outputs a single bounding box R_X (four-dimensional coordinates) localizing the most influential region in X, paired with a scalar attribution score S_X indicating the degree of influence. A notable design constraint is that the visual query (R_Q from Q) and the target image (X) come from different visual domains—generated versus real—necessitating separate image backbones rather than a shared one.

## Method

The core contribution is an Attribution Region (AR) detector built on CountGD, an open-world counting and detection model, modified with two Swin Transformer backbones (one for the training image, one for the generated query image), a BERT-Base text encoder, and two linear adapter modules (F and F̃) that project target and query tokens into a shared space while enhancing attribution scores. Training proceeds in two phases: Phase 1 trains the full detector on positive samples with standard classification and regression losses to learn region localization; Phase 2 freezes all components except the adapters and fine-tunes them on batches mixing one positive with k=4 negative samples using focal loss plus an orthogonality regularization term on the adapter weights. Ground-truth region-level attributions are established via a customization pipeline (Break-a-Scene) that fine-tunes Stable Diffusion on a masked exemplar region, creating a direct, verifiable link between the generated concept and its source region—bypassing the intractable problem of measuring influence by removal.

## Results

On the COCO-type dataset, the proposed AR detector achieves 0.7278 Recall@10 (a 226.2% improvement over the second-best baseline ICPE at 0.2231) and 0.1095 Recall@1 (+34.0% over ICPE). On the more challenging LVIS-type dataset with 477 base classes, the method still attains 0.3516 Recall@10 (+111.2% over ICPE) and 0.0968 Recall@1 (+55.9%). In terms of localization, the method achieves 0.89 box accuracy on COCO and 0.72 on LVIS, outperforming the few-shot detectors ICPE (0.86/0.62) and VFA (0.83/0.64).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all four baselines across every metric on both datasets. Against the image-level attribution baselines, AbC (Attribution-by-Customization) and AbU (Attribution-by-Unlearning), the gap is largest: on COCO, the method achieves 0.7278 Recall@10 versus 0.2079 (AbC) and 0.1452 (AbU), and on LVIS it reaches 0.3516 versus 0.1061 and 0.0813 respectively, confirming that whole-image similarity scores are poorly suited to region-level tasks. Against the few-shot object detection baselines, ICPE and VFA, the method still leads by a wide margin (e.g., 0.4620 vs. 0.1752 Recall@5 on COCO), with the advantage attributed to the AR detector's ability to leverage both textual and visual prompts, whereas ICPE and VFA rely solely on image-based queries. On box localization accuracy, the method edges out ICPE (0.89 vs. 0.86 on COCO; 0.72 vs. 0.62 on LVIS) and VFA (0.89 vs. 0.83; 0.72 vs. 0.64), indicating that the two-adapter fine-tuning in Phase 2 improves both scoring and spatial precision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the open-vocabulary detection architecture of CountGD (itself built on GroundingDINO) as an Attribution Region detector, adding a second image backbone for the query image and two linear adapters for score calibration. Conceptually, it departs from the data attribution literature (influence functions, TRAK, Attribution-by-Customization, Attribution-by-Unlearning), which treats each training image as an indivisible unit, by reframing attribution as a regional detection-and-scoring problem. The training data pipeline borrows the Break-a-Scene customization paradigm to create ground-truth region-level attributions, a use of customization that prior attribution work has not exploited.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Region-level attribution opens practical avenues—copyright tracing, artifact diagnosis, and targeted training-data repair—that whole-image methods cannot address, as demonstrated by the noise-sensitivity and occlusion-repair experiments. However, the evaluation relies entirely on a synthetic dataset where ground truth is established through per-concept model customization, leaving open whether the learned detector generalizes to attribution in real, large-scale training sets where no such controlled setup exists. Scalability to the billions of images in production text-to-image models, and the transferability of scores across different generative backbones, remain significant open questions.
