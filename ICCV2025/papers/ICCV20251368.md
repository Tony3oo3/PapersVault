## Problem

CLIP-based OOD detection methods struggle with hard OOD samples near the InD/OOD boundary, where images are more densely packed than labels in feature space, causing boundary OOD samples to align with InD labels. Fine-tuning CLIP to resolve this ambiguity has been hindered by the lack of suitable near-boundary OOD training data, and the potential of foundation models (MLLMs and diffusion models) for task-oriented data generation remained largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single RGB image and outputs a binary InD/OOD decision via a score S(x) computed as the ratio of summed CLIP similarities to InD labels versus negative labels, compared against a threshold η. During the generation phase, the pipeline consumes InD images (ImageNet-1k, 50k subset) along with MLLM-extracted contextual prompts and a frozen ResNet50 Energy-score model to iteratively produce synthetic OOD images. The final detector adds less than 1% parameters over NegLabel and incurs under 2 ms per-image inference overhead.

## Method

SynOOD is a three-stage pipeline. First, an MLLM (LLaVA) extracts contextual elements from each InD image, which serve as prompts for an iterative inpainting diffusion process (Stable Diffusion 2, strength 0.6, 20 timesteps); at each iteration, the Energy score of a frozen ResNet50 classifier provides a gradient that is backpropagated through the diffusion model to update the initial noise ε, progressively shifting the image from InD toward the OOD boundary while preserving visual similarity (a Skip Gradient approximation reduces cost). Second, a projection layer is trained on top of the frozen CLIP image encoder using a contrastive CLIP loss over a combined dataset of InD images and synthetic OOD images paired one-to-one with negative labels. Third, a subset (~5.5k of 11k) of negative label text features is made learnable and fine-tuned with SGD against the synthetic OOD images, reducing the semantic gap between InD and negative labels to an appropriate distance. The two fine-tuning stages are performed sequentially (not jointly) to avoid overfitting and interference, and the projection layer is excluded during the label fine-tuning phase for better generalization.

## Results

On the ImageNet-1k benchmark with four OOD test sets (iNaturalist, SUN, Places365, Texture), SynOOD achieves an average AUROC of 97.01% and FPR95 of 14.27%, improving AUROC by 2.80% and reducing FPR95 by 11.13% over the strongest prior method NegLabel (94.21% AUROC, 25.40% FPR95). The largest gains appear on Places365 (AUROC 97.37 vs. 91.64 for NegLabel) and Texture (AUROC 95.29 vs. 90.22), where high intra-class variability makes boundary discrimination hardest. On the OpenOOD benchmark, SynOOD outperforms NegLabel on both near-OOD (77.55 vs. 75.47 AUROC) and far-OOD (96.21 vs. 94.30 AUROC) while using only 50k training images versus the full 1.2M ImageNet set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SynOOD outperforms all 23 compared baselines on average AUROC and FPR95 across the four standard OOD test sets. Against the closest competitor NegLabel [22], SynOOD gains +2.80% AUROC (97.01 vs. 94.21) and reduces FPR95 by 11.13 points (14.27 vs. 25.40), with the largest per-dataset improvements on Places365 (+5.73 AUROC) and Texture (+5.07 AUROC). Against the other strong CLIP-based methods—AdaNeg (96.66 AUROC, 18.92 FPR95) and CSP (95.76 AUROC, 17.51 FPR95)—SynOOD leads by 0.35 and 1.25 AUROC respectively, while cutting FPR95 by 4.65 and 3.24 points. On the OpenOOD benchmark, SynOOD surpasses NegLabel on both near-OOD (77.55 vs. 75.47 AUROC) and far-OOD (96.21 vs. 94.30 AUROC) despite training on only 50k images versus the full ImageNet. Single-modal vision methods (MSP, ODIN, Energy, ReAct, etc.) trail substantially, with the best (ReAct) reaching only 92.95 average AUROC, confirming the advantage of CLIP-based multi-modal detection. The method also generalizes across backbones, beating NegLabel by 2.83 AUROC on ResNet50, 2.26 on ViT-B/32, and 2.80 on ViT-B/16.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NegLabel framework [22], which introduced negative labels for CLIP-based OOD detection, by addressing its key weakness: near-boundary OOD samples that visually resemble InD data. While prior synthetic OOD generation methods (NPOS [49], DreamOOD [10]) create outlier data from scratch or from latent spaces, SynOOD instead uses an MLLM (LLaVA) to extract contextual elements from InD images and an iterative inpainting diffusion process (Stable Diffusion 2) guided by OOD gradient feedback to produce boundary-aligned samples. The gradient-based noise refinement borrows the Skip Gradient trick from Chen et al. [5], and the overall detection scoring follows the NegLabel formulation exactly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that foundation models can be repurposed as data generators specifically for OOD detection, offering a practical recipe (MLLM prompt extraction → iterative inpainting → gradient-guided refinement) that requires only ~50k InD images and adds <1% parameters. A notable limitation is the multi-model pipeline (LLaVA + Stable Diffusion 2 + ResNet50) needed at generation time, and the finding that fine-tuning too many negative labels (>7k) degrades performance reveals a delicate balance between near-boundary sensitivity and far-OOD robustness. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-ImageNet domains, whether the generation cost is prohibitive for large-scale deployment, and whether simpler generative strategies could achieve similar boundary alignment.
