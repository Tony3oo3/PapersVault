## Problem

Existing WSI-level MIL pretraining methods face two critical limitations: multimodal approaches (e.g., TANGLE, MEDELEINE) require expensive auxiliary modalities like transcriptomics or IHC staining that limit scalability, while unimodal approaches (e.g., Intra) tend to overfit staining artifacts. Additionally, all prior methods produce non-interpretable WSI-level deep embeddings that cannot reveal the pathology concepts driving predictions, unlike how pathologists actually reason about diagnoses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251391/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes H&E whole slide images (gigapixel) tiled into 448×448 patches at 20× magnification (0.5 μm/px), encoded by the CONCH vision encoder, and optionally paired gene expression data. It produces two WSI-level embeddings: a 512-dimensional deep embedding from the ABMIL branch and a compact (C=20 or 30) interpretable concept embedding where each dimension corresponds to a named pathology concept. For unsupervised prediction, a pathologist-driven heuristic sums per-class concept activations; for supervised prediction, linear classifiers are trained on either or both embeddings.

## Method

GECKO pretrains a dual-branch MIL by contrastively aligning a WSI-level deep embedding with a WSI-level concept embedding. The concept prior is derived by computing cosine similarity between CONCH patch features and text embeddings of LLM-generated task-specific pathology concepts (one set per class), yielding an N×C interpretable matrix. The deep-encoding branch uses ABMIL (2-layer MLP projector + gated attention) to aggregate patch features, while the concept-encoding branch selects top-K salient patches, applies MLP-Mixer layers for cross-patch/cross-concept contextualization, computes gated concept attention scores, and linearly scales the concept prior (preserving interpretability) before average pooling. Training uses a symmetric CLIP contrastive loss with false negative elimination to align the two embeddings in a shared latent space, requiring no WSI-level labels—only task-specific class names to build the concept bank. When gene data is available, TANGLE's gene encoding branch is seamlessly integrated as an additional modality in the contrastive objective.

## Results

Under full supervision (linear probing), GECKO-ensemble achieves AUC of 97.6 (LUAD vs. LUSC), 86.4 (EBV+MSI), 86.5 (MSI), 86.6 (EBV), and 77.2 (HER2) using only WSI data, outperforming the unimodal Intra baseline on 4 of 5 tasks. When gene data is added, GECKO-ensemble reaches 97.9, 87.1, 89.4, 87.4, and 78.4 AUC, exceeding TANGLE (the prior multimodal SOTA) on 4 of 5 tasks. In the zero-label unsupervised setting, GECKO-Zero achieves 83.4 AUC on EBV+MSI and 60.6 on HER2, surpassing MI-Zero by 21.5 and 28.4 points respectively, and outperforming ConcepPath-Zero on all five tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the unimodal Intra baseline [14], GECKO-ensemble wins on 4 of 5 tasks under full supervision (e.g., +2.9 AUC on EBV+MSI, +2.6 on MSI, +0.7 on EBV) and also outperforms Intra in the few-label setting across all tasks. Against the multimodal TANGLE [14], GECKO-ensemble with gene data exceeds TANGLE on 4 of 5 tasks (e.g., +2.8 AUC on MSI, +1.7 on EBV+MSI, +3.1 on HER2), while matching on LUAD vs. LUSC. In the zero-label unsupervised setting, GECKO-Zero outperforms MI-Zero [23] by 10–30 AUC points on four tasks (e.g., 83.4 vs. 61.9 on EBV+MSI, 60.6 vs. 32.2 on HER2) and is comparable on LUAD vs. LUSC (95.0 vs. 96.6). GECKO-Zero also surpasses ConcepPath-Zero [42] on all five unsupervised tasks (e.g., +9.2 on EBV+MSI, +14.2 on HER2). In the ablation comparison (Table 4), GECKO significantly outperforms PANTHER [30] and Giga-SSL [20] in the WSI-only few-label setting (e.g., 96.4 vs. 91.2 on Lung k=10). Against TITAN [5], GECKO loses by 0.5 AUC on Lung (k=10) but gains 6.7 AUC on the challenging EBV+MSI task, despite TITAN being pretrained on >100K paired samples versus GECKO's ~800 WSIs.

## Relation to Prior Work

GECKO extends the line of WSI-level MIL pretraining (Giga-SSL, Intra, TANGLE, MEDELEINE) by replacing the need for expensive auxiliary modalities (transcriptomics, IHC stains) with a task-specific concept prior derived from LLM-generated pathology descriptions and VLM embeddings. Architecturally, it borrows the ABMIL deep-encoding branch from TANGLE and the linear-mapping interpretability principle from SI-MIL, but departs from both by introducing a dual-branch contrastive objective that aligns a deep WSI embedding with a linearly aggregated concept embedding. Unlike prior concept-based methods (ConcepPath, Concept MIL) that operate at the patch level or require supervision, GECKO is the first to leverage concepts for unsupervised WSI-level pretraining, and unlike TITAN or PANTHER which rely on large-scale paired corpora or GMM prototyping, it requires only ~800 WSIs and no external text data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GECKO addresses a practical bottleneck in computational pathology: multimodal pretraining methods like TANGLE and MEDELEINE depend on costly clinical profiling (RNA-seq, additional stains) that limits scalability across existing WSI archives. By showing that a WSI-only pretraining method can match or exceed multimodal baselines while simultaneously producing pathologist-interpretable slide-level concept embeddings, the paper offers a more deployable and clinically transparent alternative. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the three TCGA datasets and five tasks evaluated, whether the CONCH dependency limits transferability, and whether the task-specific concept bank can be expanded toward pan-cancer zero-shot prediction as the authors envision. The reliance on a single VLM (CONCH) for both deep features and concept prior extraction also raises questions about robustness to feature extractor choice.
