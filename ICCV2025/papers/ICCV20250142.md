## Problem

State-of-the-art video diffusion models require thousands of GPUs and millions of GPU hours for training, and inference for a single second of video takes several minutes. The root cause is that most video LDMs inherit the 2D VAE latent space from image diffusion (8× spatial downsampling), which is far more than necessary for video given its inherent temporal and spatial redundancy. This paper asks whether video can be encoded into an extremely compressed latent space that captures only motion, with content supplied separately as a condition image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250142/cropped_page0_idx9.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a text prompt and a condition image (the middle frame, generated by an off-the-shelf text-to-image model such as PixArt-α or SDXL) and produces a 16-frame video clip at up to 1024×1024 resolution. The condition image is consumed through two parallel encoders (OpenCLIP ViT-H for semantics and SD2.1-VAE for spatial content) whose features are concatenated with T5 text tokens and cross-attended into the noisy latent. For longer videos, the last frame of a generated clip is recursively fed back as the next condition image, enabling zero-shot extension to 32 or 48 frames.

## Method

The core contribution is Reducio-VAE, a 3D variational autoencoder that compresses video into a 4096× downsampled latent space (f_t=4, f_s=32, |z|=16) by using the middle frame as a content reference: the decoder fuses content feature pyramids (at H/8×W/8 and H/4×W/4) via cross-attention, so the latent only needs to encode motion variables. This yields a 64× smaller latent than SDXL-VAE while improving PSNR by over 5 dB. On top of this, Reducio-DiT is a DiT-XL model with full 3D attention that conditions on the joint text-image representation through cross-attention, trained progressively at 256², 512², and ~1024² resolutions. A key ablation finding is that spatial compression (not temporal) is the dominant source of redundancy when a reference frame is available, and that combining both semantic and content encoders outperforms either alone. The entire system trains in 3.2K A100 GPU hours and generates a 16-frame 1024² clip in 15.5 seconds on a single A100.

## Results

Reducio-DiT (1.2B params) achieves a VBench score of 81.39, FVD of 318.5 on UCF-101, and 291.9 on MSR-VTT, outperforming all compared text-to-video LDMs. In terms of efficiency, it generates a 16-frame 1024×1024 video in 15.5 seconds on a single A100 GPU (0.0650 videos/sec), a 16.6× speedup over Lavie, and requires only 3.2K A100 GPU hours for total training versus >368K for CogVideo and >56K for Show-1. The underlying Reducio-VAE achieves 35.88 PSNR and 0.94 SSIM on Pexels reconstruction, surpassing SDXL-VAE by over 5 dB in PSNR while using a 64× smaller latent space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against text-to-video baselines, Reducio-DiT achieves the best FVD on UCF-101 (318.5) and MSR-VTT (291.9), beating the closest competitor Lumiere (332.49) by 14 points and MicroCinema (342.86) by 24 points; it also leads on VBench (81.39 vs. 78.93 for Show-1). On efficiency, Reducio-DiT is 5.3× faster than Lavie at 256² and 16.6× faster at 1024², while CogVideo, Show-1, and MicroCinema all run out of memory at 1024² resolution. Training cost is dramatically lower: 3.2K A100 hours versus >368K for CogVideo, >56K for Show-1, and >6K for MicroCinema. In the image-to-video setting (Table 2), Reducio-DiT outperforms DynamiCrafter on UCF-101 FVD (318.5 vs. 429.2) but trails it on MSR-VTT (291.9 vs. 234.7); it also beats VideoComposer (576.8) and I2VGen-XL (571.1) on UCF-101. For VAE reconstruction (Table 1), Reducio-VAE surpasses SDXL-VAE by 5.3 dB in PSNR and OmniTokenizer by 8.8 dB, though it scores worse on UCF-101 rFVD (65.17 vs. 23.68 for SDXL-VAE) due to the low visual quality of UCF-101 source frames.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DiT/PixArt-α architecture for the diffusion backbone and draws conceptual inspiration from LaMD's factorized motion-latent decomposition, but pushes the compression far beyond prior work. It departs from the dominant paradigm of reusing 2D VAEs (SD-VAE, SDXL-VAE) for video by arguing that the spatial redundancy in video—especially when a content frame is available—allows a 64× smaller latent space without quality loss. The factorized text-to-image-then-image-to-video pipeline follows the line of MicroCinema and Emu Video, but Reducio-VAE's extreme 4096× overall downsampling (f_s=32, f_t=4) is a significant escalation over the 4×8×8 or 8×8×8 factors used by concurrent video VAEs like OmniTokenizer and Cosmos-VAE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.2K A100-hour training cost and 15.5-second 1024² inference time represent a dramatic efficiency win that could democratize high-resolution video generation beyond well-funded labs. The ablation finding that spatial redundancy dominates temporal redundancy (when a reference frame is provided) is a useful design principle for future video VAEs. However, the approach is currently limited to 16-frame clips with modest motion magnitude, trained on relatively small datasets (400K Pexels + 5M internal), and the UCF-101 rFVD is actually worse than some baselines due to low input quality—raising open questions about whether the compression strategy generalizes to longer, more dynamic videos at scale.
