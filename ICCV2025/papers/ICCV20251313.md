## Problem

Existing diffusion-based multi-view generation methods rely on a single fixed reference view to condition all other views, causing severe consistency degradation when the target view has minimal overlap with the reference (e.g., generating a back view from a front view). No prior work has explored autoregressive generation for multi-view synthesis, where each new view can leverage all previously generated views as cumulative reference, nor has a unified AR framework been established to handle heterogeneous conditions (text, image, shape, camera pose) simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model accepts any combination of a text prompt (encoded by FLAN-T5 XL), a reference RGB image, a 3D mesh (sampled as 8,192 surface points with normals, encoded by a Michelangelo shape encoder), and camera poses (as Plücker ray embeddings). Images are tokenized by a 2D VQ-VAE into discrete codebook indices, and the model generates views sequentially at 256×256 resolution, with views arranged in a circular configuration (e.g., 4 views at 90° angular intervals). The output is a sequence of multi-view consistent images where each view is conditioned on all preceding views in the generation order.

## Method

MV-AR is built on a Llama-based autoregressive Transformer that generates multi-view VQ-VAE tokens sequentially, with each new view conditioned on all preceding views. Four condition injection modules handle different modalities: Split Self-Attention (SSA) zeros out the self-attention output at text token positions to prevent image tokens from corrupting text guidance; Plücker rays serve as Shift Position Encoding (SPE) added directly to token embeddings to encode camera geometry; an Image Warp Controller (IWC) uses cross-attention between reference image features and camera rays to produce warped overlap features inserted token-by-token in a residual fashion; and shape tokens from a pre-trained 3D encoder are pre-filled between text and start tokens. Training employs a progressive strategy—first learning text-to-multiview, then randomly dropping and combining additional conditions with linearly increasing probability—and a Shuffle Views augmentation that randomly permutes view order, expanding each object into n(n-1)/2 training sequences to mitigate overfitting from limited high-quality 3D data.

## Results

On the Google Scanned Objects (GSO) benchmark for image-to-multi-view generation, MV-AR achieves the highest PSNR (22.99) and second-highest SSIM (0.907) among all compared methods, with LPIPS of 0.084 (third best). For text-to-multi-view generation, MV-AR attains an IS of 8.00 and CLIP-Score of 29.49, surpassing MVDream (IS 7.49, CLIP 28.71) while achieving a comparable FID of 144.29 (vs. MVDream's 141.05). The ablation study confirms that removing the Shuffle Views augmentation degrades performance substantially (FID rises from 144.29 to 173.51, PSNR drops from 22.99 to 18.27).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two strongest diffusion-based baselines, MV-AR is competitive but not uniformly dominant: it edges out Wonder3D on PSNR (22.99 vs. 22.82) and SSIM (0.907 vs. 0.892) but trails on LPIPS (0.084 vs. 0.062), and it slightly loses to Era3D on SSIM (0.907 vs. 0.911) and LPIPS (0.084 vs. 0.071) while winning on PSNR (22.99 vs. 22.73). Against the remaining three baselines (RealFusion, Zero123, SyncDreamer), MV-AR achieves clear and substantial wins across all three metrics, with PSNR improvements of 3.1–7.7 dB. In the text-to-multi-view setting, MV-AR outperforms the AR baseline LLaMA-Gen on all metrics (FID 144.29 vs. 146.11, IS 8.00 vs. 5.78, CLIP 29.49 vs. 28.36), and it surpasses MVDream on IS and CLIP-Score while being marginally worse on FID (144.29 vs. 141.05). Overall, MV-AR matches or exceeds the best diffusion-based methods on pixel-fidelity metrics (PSNR, SSIM) while showing a slight trade-off in perceptual quality (LPIPS), which the authors attribute to the model's stricter enforcement of low-level feature consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant diffusion-based paradigm for multi-view image generation (MVDream, Zero123++, SyncDreamer, Wonder3D, Era3D), which models all views jointly via cross-view attention or tiled single-pass synthesis. Instead, it repurposes the LlamaGen autoregressive image model [38]—itself built on the Llama transformer architecture and VQ-VAE tokenization—into a sequential multi-view generator where each view is conditioned on all preceding views. The conditioning modules draw on established components: Plücker-Ray Embedding from SPAD [15] for camera poses, FLAN-T5 for text, and Michelangelo [47] for shape, but the key architectural departure is the Image Warp Controller (IWC) and Split Self-Attention (SSA), designed specifically to make in-context conditioning work within an AR framework rather than a diffusion U-Net.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MV-AR establishes the first unified X-to-multi-view model that handles text, image, and shape conditions simultaneously, and demonstrates that sequential AR generation can match diffusion-based methods on multi-view consistency—particularly for distant views where reference overlap is minimal. However, the evaluation is constrained to 30 objects at 256×256 resolution on a single benchmark (GSO), and the LPIPS trade-off (worse perceptual quality despite higher PSNR/SSIM) suggests the model over-enforces low-level feature matching. Open questions include whether the AR approach scales to higher resolutions, more views, or open-domain scenes, and whether the Shuffle Views augmentation generalizes beyond the controlled Objaverse setting. The authors themselves flag the need for causal 3D VAE tokenizers and unified generation-understanding as critical next steps.
