## Problem

Existing stylized motion generation methods either duplicate diffusion backbones for content and style (as in SMooDi's ControlNet-style architecture) or are restricted to motion-only style references, making them complex, computationally expensive, and inflexible. There is no unified framework that simultaneously controls motion content and style within a single diffusion backbone while accepting style cues from diverse modalities such as text, image, video, and audio.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250474/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt describing the desired action (content) and a style reference from any supported modality—motion sequence, text label, image, video clip, or audio clip—and produces a stylized 3D human motion sequence in root-velocity representation. Non-motion style inputs are encoded via ImageBind into a unified multi-modal feature space, from which the most semantically similar motion features are retrieved to serve as the actual style condition for the diffusion process. The content and style data use consistent root-velocity motion representations from HumanML3D.

## Method

STYLEMOTIF builds on a pre-trained Motion Latent Diffusion (MLD) backbone and injects style through a style-content cross fusion mechanism that replaces SMooDi's zero-initialized ControlNet layers with a parameter-free statistical transformation. Specifically, the mean and variance of content features at a chosen block are used to normalize the style features (an AdaIN-like cross-normalization), which are then added back scaled by a learnable ratio γ, requiring no additional learnable parameters. The style encoder is pre-trained in a VAE manner on both HumanML3D (for content priors) and 100STYLE (for style priors), then frozen during diffusion training. Multi-modal alignment is achieved by contrastive learning between motion encoder features and ImageBind text features, creating a shared embedding space where any modality's features can retrieve corresponding motion style features at inference time. The fusion is applied only once after the m-th block during denoising to avoid distorting content while effectively introducing style.

## Results

STYLEMOTIF achieves 77.65% SRA, 1.551 FID, 4.354 MM Dist, 0.586 R-Precision, 7.567 Diversity, and 0.097 Foot Skate Ratio on motion-guided stylization, outperforming SMooDi (72.42% SRA, 1.609 FID, 4.477 MM Dist, 0.571 R-Precision, 9.235 Diversity, 0.124 Foot Skate Ratio). On text-guided stylization, STYLEMOTIF scores 56.71% SRA, 0.603 FID, 3.684 MM Dist, 0.690 R-Precision, 9.101 Diversity, and 0.101 Foot Skate Ratio, beating MLD+ChatGPT (4.82% SRA, 0.614 FID, 4.313 MM Dist, 0.605 R-Precision, 8.836 Diversity, 0.131 Foot Skate Ratio). On motion style transfer, STYLEMOTIF scores 68.81% SRA, 1.375 FID, and 0.094 Foot Skate Ratio, outperforming MLD+Aberman (61.01% SRA, 3.892 FID, 0.338 Foot Skate Ratio), MLD+Motion Puzzle (67.23% SRA, 6.871 FID, 0.197 Foot Skate Ratio), and SMooDi (65.15% SRA, 1.582 FID, 0.095 Foot Skate Ratio).

## Relation to Prior Work

STYLEMOTIF builds on the Motion Latent Diffusion (MLD) backbone for content generation and on ImageBind for multi-modal feature alignment, but departs sharply from the dominant ControlNet-style paradigm (exemplified by SMooDi and MulSMo) that duplicates an entire diffusion network to handle style conditioning. Instead of maintaining a second set of diffusion weights with zero-initialized skip connections, the paper replaces style injection with a parameter-free statistical cross-normalization (analogous to AdaIN) applied once at a single block, drawing on the well-established observation that feature mean/variance encode style. The multi-modal extension is novel in applying ImageBind's unified embedding space to motion stylization, using contrastive alignment between a motion style encoder and frozen ImageBind text features to enable retrieval-based style transfer from images, video, and audio.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The architectural simplification—eliminating a duplicated diffusion backbone—reduces training and inference overhead while matching or exceeding the quality of heavier baselines, making stylized motion generation more accessible for production pipelines (the authors are at Roblox). The emergent multi-modal stylization capability (e.g., a duck image guiding 'Duckfoot' gait) opens a practical path for animators to specify style from diverse references without motion capture. However, the evaluation is limited to two datasets (HumanML3D, 100STYLE) and a small set of baselines, the multi-modal path relies on retrieval from a finite motion feature pool rather than direct generation, and it remains unclear how well the single-block fusion generalizes to longer sequences or more complex multi-style compositions.
