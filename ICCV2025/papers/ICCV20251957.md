## Problem

Most VLM fine-tuning methods (prompt tuning, adapter tuning) refine text or visual representations in isolation, overlooking the fused cross-modal interaction that actually drives the final prediction. In CLIP, this fused information is buried within the similarity computation and is not directly accessible for targeted adaptation, leaving a gap in how fine-tuning can exploit the decision-making stage itself.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (processed by a ViT-B/16 vision encoder into a D-dimensional L2-normalized feature vector) and K text prompts of the form 'a photo of a [class]' (encoded into a K×D matrix of L2-normalized text features). The output is a class label determined by summing the adapted rational matrix (K×D) along the feature dimension, where adaptation is governed by a learned K×D mask produced by a single attention layer. The mask is initialized to all-ones so that the first forward pass exactly reproduces the original CLIP prediction.

## Method

RAda extends the rational matrix concept (originally from classical linear classifiers) to CLIP by defining R as the element-wise product of the normalized image feature and text features, yielding a K×D matrix whose column sums give the logits. A lightweight multi-query attention layer is attached at the very end of the VLM: the image features, text features, and rational matrix each serve as separate queries (with distinct linear projectors), while the rational matrix serves as both key and value; the three attention outputs are averaged to produce the mask M. The adapted logits are computed as the sum of M∘R (Hadamard product) over the D dimension, and a regularization term ‖M−1‖² is applied when encoders are frozen to preserve zero-shot generalization. The method is encoder-agnostic and requires only a few lines of code beyond baseline CLIP, operating exclusively at the final output stage without modifying intermediate features, which makes it applicable across full fine-tuning, efficient fine-tuning (frozen encoders), and test-time training (entropy minimization on unlabeled data) settings.

## Results

In the EFT setting (frozen encoders, 16-shot base-to-novel), RAda achieves 77.94% harmonic mean (HM) across 11 datasets, improving over the CLIP baseline (71.70%) by 6.2pp while raising base accuracy from 69.34% to 82.16% without degrading novel-class accuracy. In the FFT setting, RAda-FT attains 61.3% average OOD accuracy (vs. 60.2% for FLYP and 60.3% for LP-FT) with 81.4% ImageNet ID accuracy. In TTT (unlabeled test data only), RAda† reaches 64.26% OOD average, outperforming PromptAlign (63.55%) and TPT (60.81%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAda outperforms all prompt-tuning baselines (CoOp 71.66, CoCoOp 75.83, ProGrad 76.16, KgCoOp 77.00) and CLIP-Adapter (76.67) in EFT HM, with the largest margin over CoOp (+6.3pp). Against the closest fused-representation competitor MaPLe [21], RAda trails by 0.6pp in EFT (77.94 vs 78.55) but surpasses it by ~2pp in TTT (64.26 vs 62.31) and is applicable in FFT where MaPLe is not. RAda-FT leads all FFT baselines in OOD average (61.3% vs FLYP 60.2%, LP-FT 60.3%, L2-SP 57.8%) while FLYP retains a 1.2pp edge in ID accuracy (82.6 vs 81.4). In TTT, RAda† (64.26%) beats PromptAlign (63.55%), CoOp+TPT (62.84%), and TPT (60.81%), achieving the best OOD average without requiring ImageNet pretraining. When combined with complementary strategies, RAda+Adapter reaches 80.08% HM in EFT, surpassing MMA (79.87%) and DePT+MaPLe (79.52%), confirming orthogonality with existing adapter/prompt paradigms.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAda extends the rational matrix concept from classical linear classifiers [5] to the VLM setting, where the text encoder's output plays the role of classifier weights and the image encoder's output serves as the feature vector. It departs from the dominant fine-tuning paradigms—prompt tuning (CoOp, CoCoOp, MaPLe) and adapter tuning (CLIP-Adapter, MMA)—which modify intermediate features within individual modality encoders, by instead operating on the fused cross-modal representation at the final decision-making stage. The sequential training strategy is borrowed from LP-FT [23], and the entropy minimization objective for test-time training follows the TPT/PromptAlign line of work, but RAda is the first to unify these settings under a single encoder-agnostic mechanism applied to the rational matrix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a conceptually clean and practically lightweight alternative to multi-layer prompt/adapter tuning, with a ~12× reduction in FLOPs compared to MaPLe while remaining competitive in accuracy. Its encoder-agnostic design broadens applicability beyond ViT-based architectures, and its versatility across FFT, EFT, and TTT settings makes it a broadly useful building block. However, RAda alone does not achieve state-of-the-art in EFT, and its strongest results come from combining with existing prompt or adapter methods, suggesting it is complementary rather than a replacement. Open questions include whether the rational matrix perspective transfers to pretraining, non-classification VLM tasks (captioning, VQA), and whether the attention-based mask can be further simplified or made input-independent.
