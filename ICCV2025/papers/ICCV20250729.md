## Problem

Existing red-teaming methods for text-to-image models require white-box gradient access, rely on computationally expensive per-prompt discrete optimization, and produce semantically meaningless adversarial prompts that are trivially blocked by perplexity-based filters and blacklist word filters. This makes them impractical for scalable, real-world safety evaluation of T2I safety mechanisms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250729/cropped_page0_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a benign text prompt (truncated to 50 tokens) and produces an adversarial suffix of up to 15 tokens, which is concatenated to form the full adversarial prompt. The suffix generator (Llama-3.1-8B) operates in a black-box setting where only the generated images from the target T2I model are observed—no internal gradients or parameters are accessed. The generated adversarial prompt must simultaneously satisfy two evasion constraints: low perplexity (measured by an auxiliary LLM) and absence of any tokens from a predefined unsafe word blacklist.

## Method

AutoPrompt employs an alternating optimization-finetuning pipeline: in the optimization phase, a frozen LLM generates adversarial suffixes token-by-token via stochastic beam search (k=12 candidates, b=4 beams), minimizing a unified jailbreak constraint that combines CLIP alignment to unsafe images and unsafe concept words with a perplexity penalty from an auxiliary LLM; in the fine-tuning phase, the LLM is supervised on high-priority (successful jailbreak, low objective) suffix pairs from a replay buffer. A dual-evasion strategy is embedded in the optimization: an auxiliary LLM (Qwen2.5-7B) scores token-level log-probabilities to enforce human readability, while banned-token penalties suppress both individual blacklisted tokens and multi-token combinations that reconstruct prohibited words. A prior suffix (e.g., 'and a beautiful girl's body with') is prepended to benign prompts to provide contextual guidance for the LLM's generation. This yields a zero-shot, transferable suffix generator that requires no per-prompt optimization at inference time and generalizes across unseen prompts and heterogeneous T2I architectures.

## Results

On the I2P dataset, AutoPrompt achieves the highest Red-teaming Success Rate (RSR) across most safe T2I models under a blacklist word filter: 61.5% on ESD and 70.5% on SLD-MAX for nudity, and 73.81% on ESD and 65.98% on AdvUnlearn for violence. In terms of prompt naturalness, AutoPrompt achieves an average perplexity of 0.167 (×10³) with Qwen2.5-7B, roughly 1/70th of Ring-A-Bell (11.646 ×10³) and 1/28th of P4D-Union (4.599 ×10³), confirming substantially higher human-readability. The method also achieves the lowest blocking rate (2%) against the blacklist word filter, compared to 87% when banned-token penalties are removed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Ring-A-Bell, AutoPrompt dominates by a wide margin on every model and category, with RSR values of 30–70% versus Ring-A-Bell's near-zero performance (0–2.5%), confirming that the genetic-algorithm-based discrete optimization is essentially ineffective under a blacklist filter. Against UnlearnDiffAtk, AutoPrompt wins on 7 of 8 model-category pairs (e.g., 61.5% vs 18.5% on ESD-nudity, 65.98% vs 35.05% on AdvUnlearn-violence), but loses on SLD-MAX-violence (20.66% vs 28.51%). Against P4D-Union, the comparison is more mixed: AutoPrompt wins on ESD-nudity (61.5% vs 41.5%), SLD-MAX-nudity (70.5% vs 62.5%), AdvUnlearn-nudity (30.5% vs 9.5%), ESD-violence (73.81% vs 67.86%), and AdvUnlearn-violence (65.98% vs 60.82%), but trails on Receler-nudity (36.5% vs 41.5%), SLD-MAX-violence (20.66% vs 30.99%), and Receler-violence (43.97% vs 67.24%). Across all baselines, AutoPrompt's key differentiator is its dramatically lower perplexity (0.167 vs 2.776–11.646 ×10³) and near-zero blocking rate, making it the only method that simultaneously achieves high RSR while remaining human-readable and filter-resistant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of T2I red-teaming (Ring-A-Bell, P4D, UnlearnDiffAtk), which relies on white-box gradient access and per-prompt discrete/continuous optimization to produce semantically meaningless adversarial prompts. Instead, it repurposes the LLM jailbreak literature's adversarial-suffix concept—previously applied to text-only LLMs—into a black-box framework for diffusion models, using a fine-tuned Llama-3.1-8B as a suffix generator trained via an alternating optimization-finetuning loop. The dual-evasion strategy (auxiliary LLM perplexity scoring plus banned-token penalties) is a novel combination not present in prior T2I attack methods, which typically produce prompts that are either gibberish or contain explicit blacklisted terms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical, scalable black-box tool for auditing T2I safety mechanisms without requiring model internals, and its zero-shot transferability to unseen prompts and commercial APIs (Leonardo.Ai, Midjourney) highlights that current safety filters remain fragile against natural-language adversarial suffixes. A key open question is whether T2I safety mechanisms can be made robust against LLM-generated, human-readable adversarial prompts without degrading legitimate generation quality. The paper also calls for standardized, automated safety evaluation benchmarks, noting that the lack thereof has left the efficacy of existing defenses inadequately quantified.
