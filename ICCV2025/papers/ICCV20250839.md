## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting overfits when trained on sparse views, with test-view quality degrading after roughly 2000 iterations while training-view quality continues to improve. Ensembling is a well-known strategy for improving generalization but has not been explored in the 3DGS context, and the straightforward approach of training multiple independent 3DGS models is computationally expensive and produces models that lack sufficient diversity for effective ensembling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250839/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3–10 posed RGB images (depending on dataset and experimental setting) and a target camera pose as input, producing a single rendered RGB image at the novel viewpoint. Gaussian parameters are initialized from COLMAP point clouds shared across all compared methods, and MVImgNet images are resized to 512 on the longest side. During training, pseudo views are generated via SLERP interpolation between training camera poses to provide self-supervised regularization signals without requiring any additional ground-truth data such as depth maps or multi-view stereo reconstructions.

## Method

SE-GS jointly trains two 3DGS models: a Δ-model that is dynamically perturbed and a Σ-model that serves as the final ensemble used at inference. Every 500 training iterations, the Δ-model is perturbed by adding Gaussian noise to the 3D positions, 6D rotation representations, scales, and opacities of only those Gaussians whose 2D splat projections overlap pixels with high uncertainty scores, where uncertainty is computed as the pixel-wise standard deviation across a buffer of S=3 pseudo-view renderings stored at different training steps and locally smoothed with a 5×5 kernel. The Σ-model is regularized to match the perturbed Δ-model's renderings at M=24 pseudo views via a combined L1 and D-SSIM photometric loss, and a co-pruning strategy removes discrepant Gaussians between the two models to reduce the Gaussian count. This self-ensembling is entirely self-supervised and incurs negligible overhead because perturbed models are generated by parameter noise rather than separate training runs, and only the Σ-model is retained for inference.

## Results

SE-GS achieves the best PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across all four datasets (LLFF, DTU, Mip-NeRF360, MVImgNet) in the sparse-view setting. On LLFF with 3 views, SE-GS reaches 20.79 PSNR / 0.724 SSIM / 0.183 LPIPS, outperforming the closest competitor CoR-GS (20.45 / 0.712 / 0.196) and vanilla 3DGS (19.22 / 0.649 / 0.229). On DTU with 3 views, the PSNR gain over 3DGS is 0.59 (19.24 vs. 17.67), and on MVImgNet with 5 views, SE-GS achieves 27.88 PSNR versus 26.48 for 3DGS. SE-GS also demonstrates superior efficiency, using only 86,742 Gaussians and 1,781 MB memory versus 3,516,780 Gaussians and 14,857 MB for 3DGS, while training faster (29.47 vs. 23.17 iter/s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SE-GS outperforms all 10 baselines on every metric across all four datasets. Against the strongest competitor CoR-GS (which trains multiple 3DGS models with cross-model regularization), SE-GS gains +0.34 PSNR on LLFF-3v, +0.59 on DTU-3v, +0.39 on Mip-NeRF360-12v, and +0.26 on MVImgNet-5v, while also achieving lower LPIPS in every setting. Compared to vanilla 3DGS, the PSNR improvement ranges from +0.26 (MVImgNet-5v) to +1.57 (DTU-3v), and SE-GS consistently beats 3DGS on all metrics in all configurations. Depth-assisted methods (DNGaussian, FSGS) are less stable: FSGS drops below 3DGS on LLFF-9v (25.31 vs. 25.44 PSNR), and DNGaussian underperforms 3DGS on LLFF-6v and 9v, whereas SE-GS never regresses. NeRF-based methods (Mip-NeRF, DietNeRF, RegNeRF, FreeNeRF, SparseNeRF) are uniformly outperformed by 3DGS-based approaches, confirming the advantage of explicit Gaussian representations for few-shot NVS. The generalizable MVSplat model is significantly weaker than per-scene optimized methods (e.g., 17.33 vs. 19.24 PSNR on DTU-3v), highlighting the gap between generalizable and scene-specific radiance field modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) with a self-ensembling mechanism inspired by temporal ensembling and consistency regularization from the broader ensemble learning literature. It directly builds on CoR-GS, which demonstrated that co-regularizing multiple independently trained 3DGS models improves sparse-view NVS, but departs from it by replacing expensive multi-model training with dynamic perturbation of a single Δ-model guided by rendering uncertainty. Unlike depth- or MVS-based approaches (DNGaussian, FSGS) that inject noisy auxiliary signals, SE-GS operates entirely in a self-supervised manner on pseudo views, requiring no additional ground-truth data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-ensembling framework offers a practical and efficient remedy for 3DGS overfitting in sparse-view settings, a common scenario in real-world capture pipelines where dense view coverage is infeasible. The finding that the Σ-model is both more accurate and more efficient (fewer Gaussians, lower memory, faster training) than vanilla 3DGS makes the approach attractive for deployment. Open questions include whether the uncertainty-aware perturbation strategy generalizes to dynamic or unbounded scenes, and the authors note that an outlier identifier could further improve robustness by filtering unreliable perturbed models during regularization.
