## Problem

Current slide-level foundation model (SLFM) fine-tuning frameworks are limited to single-task, single-cancer-site, and unimodal (WSI-only) settings, failing to exploit complementary information from transcriptomics, clinical records, and inter-task semantic relationships. This results in suboptimally fine-tuned models that under-utilize the rich, multi-modal, multi-annotated data available in modern pathology datasets.

## Input / Output

The method consumes WSIs (divided into 256×256 patches at 0.5 μm/pixel, with 1536-dim features extracted by Gigapath's frozen DINOv2 encoder; patches from multiple WSIs per patient are concatenated into a single bag) paired with bulk transcriptomics data (331 gene pathways spanning 4,987 genes from the Xena database). It produces a 256-dim combined feature vector per patient, which is then decoded via linear probing into a cancer subtype label (evaluated by balanced accuracy) or a survival risk score (evaluated by C-index via a Cox Proportional Hazards model). Training targets are text embeddings generated by the CONCH text encoder from structured clinical tabular data, rather than discrete class labels or continuous survival times.

## Method

ModalTune introduces a Modal Adapter that injects transcriptomics features into a frozen Gigapath LongNet slide encoder via alternating cross-attention modules (Multi-Modal Feature Injectors and Extractors) at each block, with learnable [task] prompts enabling task-specific feature interactions without modifying backbone weights. The transcriptomics encoder compresses gene expression into 64 pathway tokens using a sparse MLP followed by an MLP-Mixer for intra- and inter-pathway mixing. A key design choice is unifying all downstream tasks (general, survival, subtype) into a single embedding space by encoding clinical metadata as text and processing it with the CONCH text encoder, so training minimizes a single KL-divergence loss between the combined multi-modal feature and the corresponding text embedding regardless of task type or count. This eliminates per-task loss functions and leverages semantic relationships between tasks and cancer sites, enabling a pan-cancer variant that pools all sites into one model.

## Results

On the four TCGA cancer types (BRCA, GBMLGG, NSCLC, RCC), ModalTune achieves an overall balanced accuracy of 0.954 for cancer subtype prediction and a C-index of 0.750 for survival prediction, outperforming the best single-task baseline by +1.4% and +1.9% respectively. Compared to the standard image-only SLFM (Gigapath Tuned), ModalTune improves subtype accuracy by +4.6% and survival C-index by +9.6%. On out-of-distribution datasets (COADREAD, BLCA), ModalTune Pan-Cancer outperforms the fine-tuned Gigapath (cat) by +25% in subtype and +8.3% in survival, while remaining competitive with fully supervised baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ModalTune outperforms all WSI-only baselines (Gigapath LP, ABMIL, TransMIL, Gigapath Tuned) on both tasks across all four cancer types, with the largest gap over Gigapath (Tuned) at +4.6% subtype accuracy and +9.6% survival C-index overall. Among genomics-only baselines, S-MLP is the strongest (0.917 subtype, 0.736 survival), but ModalTune still leads by +3.7% and +1.4% respectively. Against multi-modal baselines, ModalTune beats the best (ABMIL KP at 0.941 subtype, S-MLP/ABMIL cat at 0.736/0.732 survival) by +1.3% and +1.8%, and surpasses the fully-tuned multi-modal Gigapath (cat/KP) by +2.8% in classification and +7.0% in survival, demonstrating that the Modal Adapter's cross-attention fusion is more effective than simple concatenation or Kronecker-product late fusion. On OOD datasets, ModalTune Pan-Cancer outperforms the fine-tuned Gigapath (cat) by a large margin (+25% subtype on COADREAD, +8.3% survival on BLCA) while remaining within 2.4% of a fully supervised model on subtype and actually exceeding it by 1.2% on survival, highlighting the generalizability advantage of the pan-cancer training recipe.

## Relation to Prior Work

ModalTune builds directly on the Gigapath slide-level foundation model (a LongNet-based MIL aggregator) as its frozen backbone, and adapts the ViT-Adapter paradigm from natural image processing to inject multi-modal features via cross-attention at multiple hidden layers without modifying pre-trained weights. It departs from prior multi-modal pathology fusion methods (MCAT, SurvPath, Pathomic Fusion) that rely on early concatenation or late Kronecker-product merging, instead using a bidirectional cross-attention adapter that preserves the slide encoder's representations. The text-embedding unification of tasks via a CONCH text encoder and KL-divergence alignment draws on prompt distillation ideas from vision-language models (PromptKD), but is the first to apply this to unify heterogeneous pathology tasks (classification, survival) and cancer sites into a single training objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to jointly address multi-modal, multi-task, and pan-cancer fine-tuning of SLFMs, offering a practical recipe for deploying foundation models on smaller, multi-site pathology datasets where per-task, per-site fine-tuning is infeasible. The pan-cancer variant's ability to generalize to unseen cancer types (COADREAD, BLCA) while remaining competitive with fully supervised models is practically significant for clinical deployment on rare or emerging cancer types. Key open questions include the computational cost of multi-layer cross-attention, the convergence mismatch across cancer sites in the pan-cancer setting (where early stopping for one site underfits another), and whether the framework transfers cleanly to newer SLFMs like TITAN beyond the limited supplementary validation.
