## Problem

RL fine-tuning of diffusion models is prone to reward hacking, where generated images achieve high reward scores but deviate from the true data distribution with reduced diversity and quality. Existing mitigations—early stopping, fixed-weight KL regularization, and dense temporal rewards—fail to effectively balance exploration and distributional fidelity across the heterogeneous denoising steps, leaving the problem largely unsolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251963/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on latent diffusion models (Stable Diffusion v1.4/v1.5/v2.1-turbo/XL) fine-tuned via LoRA, taking a text prompt and random Gaussian noise in latent space as input and producing a 512×512 RGB image. A key operational detail is that the number of denoising steps is not fixed: a lightweight perception model evaluates each intermediate noised latent and triggers early termination when the predicted denoising quality (γ_t) reaches a threshold, reducing the total steps by up to 40% during training.

## Method

AdaEnt introduces entropy of the denoising policy as a principled exploration metric, implemented through a dynamic, step-aware variance head η_{θ,φ}(x_t, t) that modulates the DDIM sampling variance at each step. A binary-classification perception model f(x_t) predicts γ_t ∈ [0,1] for each intermediate latent, which adaptively weights the entropy term (1−γ_t)H(p_θ) against the KL-regularization term γ_t·KL(p_θ‖p_pre) in the PPO objective—favoring diversity exploration in early denoising steps and distributional fidelity in later steps. The paper proves three theorems: convergence of the entropy-enhanced policy, monotonic increase of global entropy during training, and systematic decrease of entropy along the denoising trajectory. When γ_t ≈ 1, the denoising process is dynamically halted, providing a hyperparameter-free early-stopping criterion that simultaneously reduces computation and alleviates reward hacking.

## Results

On the Simple-animals benchmark (SDv14, aesthetic-score reward), AdaEnt achieves the highest mean Inception Score (24.34 vs. 22.95 for DDPO, 22.29 for DPOK, 21.88 for TDPO), mean ImageReward (1.1212 vs. 1.0707, 1.0496, 0.8453), and mean ClipScore (0.3050 vs. 0.3019, 0.3046, 0.3010), while matching the best PickScore (21.41). AdaEnt is the top performer on 8 out of 12 reported metric values (#Top1 = 8), compared to 1–2 for all baselines. The dynamic early-stop mechanism reduces total denoising steps by ~87% (e.g., 0.242M vs. 1.843M for DDPO) and cuts training time from 11.98 h to 5.52 h with negligible GFLOPs increase.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaEnt consistently outperforms all four baselines on the primary quality-diversity metrics. Against DDPO, AdaEnt gains +1.39 in mean Inception Score (24.34 vs. 22.95) and +0.050 in mean ImageReward (1.121 vs. 1.071), while PickScore is essentially tied (21.41 vs. 21.37). Against DPOK, the Inception Score gap widens to +2.05 (24.34 vs. 22.29) and ImageReward improves by +0.072, indicating that DPOK's fixed-weight KL regularization over-constrains exploration. TDPO (dense-reward) shows the most instability—its ImageReward minimum drops to 0.005 and mean Inception Score falls to 21.88—while AdaEnt maintains stable, high scores across all metrics. D3PO (human-feedback-based) underperforms on every metric, with mean ImageReward of only 0.896 and no usable Inception Score data. Across SDv15 and SDv21 backbones (Table 2), AdaEnt again leads on #Top1 (5 vs. 0–2) and is the only method that successfully trains on SD-XL without reward collapse.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL fine-tuning lineage for diffusion models established by DDPO (MDP formulation of denoising) and DPOK (KL regularization against a reference policy), and leverages the DDIM η-parameter as a learned, step-aware dynamic variance rather than a fixed hyperparameter. It departs from prior work by introducing entropy as a theoretically-grounded exploration term with convergence guarantees, and by coupling it with an adaptive perception model that dynamically balances exploration (entropy) and distributional fidelity (KL) at each denoising step. The dynamic step-alignment mechanism, which uses the same perception model to trigger early stopping, is a practical departure from fixed-step or externally-predicted-step approaches like AdaDiff.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reward hacking remains a persistent and practically damaging failure mode in RL fine-tuning of diffusion models, and this paper offers a principled, plug-and-play remedy that simultaneously improves reward scores, preserves diversity, and reduces training/inference cost by up to 40% through adaptive early stopping. The theoretical guarantees (global entropy increase, monotonic entropy decrease across denoising steps) provide a clear mechanistic explanation for why the method works, which is rare in this area. Open questions include whether the entropy-adaptive framework generalizes beyond image generation (e.g., video, 3D), whether the lightweight perception model scales to higher-resolution or longer-horizon denoising, and whether the approach can be combined with dense-reward methods like TDPO for further gains.
