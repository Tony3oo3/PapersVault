## Problem

In semi-supervised multi-organ segmentation, complex anatomical structures (overlapping organs, large size variations) produce many unreliable pseudo-labels that introduce confirmation bias in voxel-wise contrastive learning (VCL). Existing remedies either discard unreliable voxels entirely or push them away from complementary-label prototypes, but neither approach lets unreliable voxels fully benefit from VCL's core advantages of intra-class aggregation and inter-class separation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251692/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D medical volumes (D×H×W) from CT or MRI, split into a small labeled set S^l and a large unlabeled set S^u (M≫N), and produces per-voxel class predictions (C×D×H×W) for multiple abdominal organs simultaneously. Training uses two parallel 3D U-Net networks under the Cross Pseudo Supervision framework, where each network's pseudo-labels supervise the other, and a feature head (MLP) extracts per-voxel embeddings for contrastive objectives.

## Method

DVCL exploits the observation that unreliable voxels, while lacking discriminative class boundaries, still form coherent clusters in feature space: close voxels (KNN neighbors) likely share a semantic class, while distant voxels (K'-farthest outsiders) likely belong to different classes. For each unreliable voxel acting as a query, the method pulls its neighbors into the same cluster and pushes outsiders away, using a log-likelihood loss with a computationally tractable upper bound that avoids summing over all unreliable features. Two stabilizing strategies are added: a Neighbor Partitioning Strategy (NPS) that assigns higher affinity to mutually-confirmed neighbors (relevant) versus one-directional neighbors (ordinary), and a Query Detection Strategy (QDS) that excludes outlier queries whose mutual-neighbor count falls below a threshold. The full training objective combines supervised loss, CPS consistency loss, a high-quality contrastive loss (HqCL) for reliable voxels using InfoNCE with a memory bank, and the DVCL loss for unreliable voxels.

## Results

On four multi-organ segmentation datasets (FLARE 2022, AMOS, MMWHS, BTCV) under 10% and 50% labeled ratios, DVCL achieves the best Mean Dice across all settings. Headline numbers include 84.2% on FLARE 2022 (10%), 71.4% on AMOS (10%), 86.3% on MMWHS (10%), and 52.9% on BTCV (10%). Compared to the second-best method, DVCL improves Mean Dice by 2.06% (FLARE 2022, 10%), 5.55% (AMOS, 10%), 2.08% (MMWHS, 10%), and 2.76% (BTCV, 10%), with gains of 1.70%, 1.74%, 1.29%, and 3.39% at the 50% ratio respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DVCL outperforms all 13 baselines on every dataset and label ratio. The largest margin is on AMOS (10%), where DVCL (71.4%) surpasses the runner-up CLD (65.8%) by 5.55 points, reflecting the benefit of distance-aware contrastive learning on highly variable organ shapes. Among contrastive-learning baselines, U²PL (82.1% FLARE 10%) and BaCon (82.0%) are the closest competitors, but DVCL still leads by 2.1 and 2.2 points respectively, confirming that pushing unreliable voxels away from complementary labels is less effective than the proposed neighbor-pull/outsider-push strategy. UGPCL, which discards unreliable pseudo-labels entirely, underperforms even the non-contrastive CPS baseline on FLARE 2022 (79.6% vs. 79.3%), validating the paper's argument that discarding unreliable voxels wastes valuable information. On BTCV (10%), DVCL (52.9%) beats MagicNet (50.2%) by 2.7 points, and on MMWHS (50%) it leads CLD (86.9%) by 1.3 points. The Dice curves (Fig. 5) further show that DVCL converges on difficult organs (e.g., LAG) at least 2000 iterations earlier than competing methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Cross Pseudo Supervision (CPS) framework with two parallel 3D U-Net networks and adopts the entropy-based selection module from U²PL to partition pseudo-labels into reliable and unreliable groups. Its key departure is from the dominant 'complementary label' strategy (U²PL, BaCon, CCL), which pushes unreliable voxels away from the prototype of the class they are least likely to belong to. The authors argue this one-directional repulsion prevents unreliable voxels from benefiting from intra-class aggregation, and instead propose a distance-aware contrastive loss that simultaneously pulls feature-space neighbors together and pushes distant outsiders apart, augmented by a neighbor partitioning strategy and an outlier query detection strategy for stability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in semi-supervised multi-organ segmentation where unreliable pseudo-labels at organ boundaries and overlapping regions degrade performance on small or anatomically complex organs (e.g., adrenal glands, pancreas). The consistent 2–5% Dice gains over the strongest baselines across four datasets and two label ratios demonstrate that fully exploiting unreliable voxels through distance-aware clustering is more effective than discarding them or using complementary labels. Open questions include whether the distance-aware formulation generalizes beyond the CPS + 3D U-Net setup, whether the neighbor/outsider hyperparameters (K, K′, queue sizes) transfer across organ counts and imaging modalities, and whether the approach extends to 2D or sparse-label settings.
