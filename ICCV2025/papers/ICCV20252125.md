## Problem

Diffusion models achieve strong image-to-image translation results but require many iterative denoising steps, hindering real-time use. While flow matching and bridge matching offer alternatives without explicit noising, the stochastic bridge matching variant has seen minimal exploration beyond low-resolution pixel-space applications, leaving open whether it can scale to high-resolution images and diverse translation tasks with single-step inference.

## Input / Output

The method consumes source images (masked images for object removal, RGB images for depth/normal estimation, composite foreground-background images for relighting, or images paired with 2D light maps for controllable relighting and shadow generation) and produces the corresponding target-domain image in a single forward pass. All processing occurs in the VAE latent space, with a random cropping strategy applied during training to handle high-resolution inputs without excessive memory cost. The U-Net backbone is initialized from SDXL weights, and conditioning variables (e.g., light maps) are injected by channel concatenation with the latent.

## Method

LBM applies the bridge matching framework in a VAE latent space: given paired source-target latents z_0 and z_1, a stochastic interpolant z_t = z_0(1−t) + z_1·t + σ√(t(1−t))·ε is constructed, and a U-Net is trained to regress the SDE drift (z_1 − z_t)/(1−t). Two key design choices enable single-step inference: (1) a discrete timestep distribution concentrating training on 4 equally spaced timesteps that match inference, and (2) an auxiliary LPIPS pixel loss on the decoded prediction that accelerates domain shift. The ablation study reveals that the stochasticity parameter σ > 0 (making the process an SDE rather than an ODE) consistently outperforms the zero-noise flow matching limit, because the intrinsic randomness allows the model to reach a wider diversity of target samples. A conditional variant extends the drift network to accept an additional conditioning input (e.g., a light map), enabling controllable relighting and shadow generation.

## Results

On the RORD object-removal task (52k images), LBM achieves FID 26.29, Local FID 27.91, fMSE 1314.58, PSNR 22.38, and SSIM 69.06 using a single neural function evaluation (NFE), outperforming all baselines that require 50 NFEs. For normal estimation, LBM achieves the best average rank of 1.4 across four datasets (NYUv2, ScanNet, i-Bims, Sintel), with mean angular errors of 15.5° (NYUv2), 14.1° (ScanNet), 16.9° (i-Bims), and 32.2° (Sintel). On image relighting, LBM achieves FID 12.79 and fMSE 1173.02, beating the best competitor (INR Harmonization, FID 13.86) while using only 1 NFE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For object removal, LBM (1 NFE) outperforms all four baselines on every metric: it beats Attentive Eraser (the strongest competitor at 50 NFEs) by 3.41 FID, 5.24 Local FID, 714.46 fMSE, 1.45 PSNR, and 3.37 SSIM, while being 50× faster. For normal estimation, LBM achieves the best average rank (1.4) across 12 competitors, beating Lotus-D (rank 2.4) and Diff.-E2E-FT (rank 2.8) on NYUv2 (15.5° vs 16.2°/16.5° mean angular error) and ScanNet (14.1° vs 14.7°), and achieving the best 30° threshold on all four datasets. For image relighting, LBM wins on FID (12.79 vs 13.86 for INR Harmonization), Local FID (12.83 vs 14.21 for Harmonizer), and fMSE (1173.02 vs 1480.01 for INR), though it trails slightly on PSNR (23.24 vs 23.49 for Harmonizer and INR). Notably, IC-Light performs worst (FID 20.88) partly because it requires a separate background estimation step that introduces artifacts. The ablation further shows LBM outperforms flow matching (σ=0) at all inference step counts, confirming the benefit of the stochastic bridge formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stochastic interpolant / bridge matching framework (Albergo et al. 2023; Peluchetti 2023; Shi et al. 2024) from pixel space to a VAE latent space, enabling scalability to high-resolution images. The closest prior work, I2SB (Liu et al. 2023), applied bridge matching to super-resolution and inpainting but was restricted to low-resolution pixel-space operations. The key conceptual departure is the demonstration that the stochastic component (σ > 0) is not merely a generalization of flow matching but actively improves generative quality, positioning LBM as a strictly more expressive transport mechanism than its deterministic ODE counterpart.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving single-step (1 NFE) inference with competitive or SOTA quality across six diverse image-to-image tasks is practically significant for real-time applications where iterative diffusion sampling is prohibitive. The finding that stochasticity in the transport process improves FID over flow matching challenges the implicit assumption that deterministic transport is sufficient for high-quality generation. A notable limitation is the requirement for pre-existing paired couplings between source and target distributions, which restricts applicability to unpaired settings; whether the latent bridge matching framework can be extended to unpaired or weakly-supervised translation remains an open question.
