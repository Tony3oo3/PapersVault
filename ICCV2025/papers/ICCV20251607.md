## Problem

Homomorphic encryption (HE) can protect face templates from reconstruction attacks, but existing HE-based biometric template protection methods are hundreds of times slower than unprotected systems because HE operations are not tailored to the structure of real-valued face templates on a hypersphere. This makes large-scale identification (e.g., 1M+ identities at an airport) impractical, as computational cost grows linearly with database size and each matching score requires expensive homomorphic multiplications.

## Input / Output

The system takes an RGB face image, extracts a 512-dimensional unit vector template via a pretrained AdaFace (IResNet101) feature extractor, and returns either the identity identifier of the closest enrolled template (if the matching score exceeds a threshold τ) or a 'reject' decision. Operationally, the system uses a two-server architecture: a local server holds the public key and encrypted database, while a separate key server holds the secret key and performs decryption to find the maximum score. The query template is not encrypted (only the database is), which is appropriate when the primary threat is database compromise rather than query privacy.

## Method

IDFace introduces two techniques to make HE-based template protection efficient for large-scale identification. First, an almost-isometric transformation T_α maps each 512-dim real-valued unit vector to a ternary vector in {−1, 0, 1}^d by retaining only the top α components (by absolute value) and replacing them with their signs, reducing inner product computation to additions and subtractions only—eliminating all homomorphic multiplications. Second, a space-efficient encoding packs m transformed templates into a single HE message slot using positional encoding with base p > α, exploiting the bounded range [−α, α] of inner products between ternary vectors to maximize SIMD parallelism and minimize ciphertext count. The database encryption scheme encrypts column vectors of the template matrix, and identification requires only 2(β−1) ciphertext additions per query (where β controls the sparsity of the query template), achieving 126 ms identification over 1M encrypted templates with only 2× overhead versus plaintext matching.

## Results

IDFace with CKKS and β=63 identifies a face from a 1M-identity encrypted database in 0.126s, achieving only 2× overhead over unprotected identification (0.063s) while using 4.125GB storage. At β=341 (highest accuracy), identification takes 0.753s with 6.6GB storage. On accuracy benchmarks, IDFace(341,341) achieves 99.78% on LFW, 99.09% on CFP-FP, 97.97% on AgeDB, and 98.27% TAR@1e-3 on IJB-C verification, with less than 1% degradation versus the plain AdaFace model in most settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDFace dominates all HE-based baselines on identification speed for 1M identities. Compared to HERS(128) [20], the closest prior HE-based method at the same storage budget, IDFace(63) CKKS is 97.6× faster (0.126s vs 12.3s) with identical 4.125GB storage, while IDFace(341) CKKS is 16.3× faster (0.753s vs 12.3s) at only 60% extra storage. Against MFBR-ID [4], the other multiplication-free approach, IDFace(63) CKKS is 4.5× faster on identification (0.126s vs 0.545s), 23.5× faster on enrollment (72s vs 1641s), and requires 31.8× less storage (4.125GB vs 132.1GB). Older methods like [17] (290,605s) and [7] (5,037–6,210s) are orders of magnitude slower, making them impractical for real-time use. On accuracy, IDFace(341,341) loses less than 1% on LFW (99.78% vs 99.82%), CFP-FP (99.09% vs 99.24%), and AgeDB (97.97% vs 98.00%) relative to the plain AdaFace model, and only 0.12% on IJB-C verification TAR@1e-3 (98.27% vs 98.39%), confirming that the efficiency gains come at negligible accuracy cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

IDFace directly extends the HERS [20] database encryption framework (ENC_DB / IP_DB) by inserting two template-level transformations before encryption: an almost-isometric ternary quantization T_α that converts real-valued unit vectors into {0, ±1} vectors, and a space-efficient encoding that packs multiple templates into a single HE message slot. This departs from the entire prior line of HE-based biometric template protection (Boddeti [7], Bauspieß [6], Drozdowski [17], Choi [11], Bassit [3,4]) where HE was applied to raw real-valued templates, forcing expensive homomorphic multiplications and wasting message-slot capacity. The paper also notes a conceptual link to IronMask [40], which uses the same top-α sign extraction as a decoding step for fuzzy commitment, but repurposes it here as an almost-isometric distance-preserving map rather than a collision-facilitating one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing identification over 1M encrypted templates to 126 ms (CKKS, β=63) with only ~2× overhead versus unprotected matrix-vector multiplication, IDFace makes HE-based face template protection practically deployable in latency-sensitive settings such as airport or building-entry identification, a regime where prior HE-based methods were hundreds of times too slow. The almost-isometric transformation is of independent interest and could be adapted to other angular-metric biometrics or privacy-preserving retrieval tasks. Key limitations include the passive-attacker-only threat model, a small but non-zero accuracy drop on IJB-C identification (up to ~3% at β=63), and dependence on the specific hypersphere/angular-metric structure of modern face encoders; extending to active adversaries and to non-angular metrics remains open.
