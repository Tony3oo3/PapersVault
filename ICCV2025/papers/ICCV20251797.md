## Problem

Existing diffusion-based image editing methods rely on textual instructions to specify edits, which fails for complex or compound edits that are difficult to describe in natural language (e.g., unnamed artistic styles, multi-edit combinations). Additionally, standard CLIP-based evaluation metrics assess only whether an edit is applied globally, ignoring whether the unedited regions of the source image are preserved, forcing researchers to fall back on costly human evaluations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251797/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For exemplar-based editing, the method takes a reference image pair (input and its edited counterpart, channel-concatenated into a single tensor) and a separate query image, producing an edited query image via a diffusion model. For automated evaluation, it takes any image pair and either a text instruction or a second reference pair, outputting a scalar cosine similarity score (EC2T or EC2EC). The visual encoder operates on 768-dimensional token sequences, and the edit embedding is projected through a trainable linear layer plus LayerNorm to align with the diffusion model's text-conditioning space.

## Method

EditCLIP modifies CLIP's visual encoder to accept a channel-concatenated pair of input and edited images, then trains it via contrastive learning against a frozen CLIP text encoder on 313k instruction-based editing triplets from InstructPix2Pix. The resulting edit embedding directly encodes the transformation between the two images rather than their individual content, as confirmed by attention visualizations that focus on edited regions. For exemplar-based editing, this embedding replaces the text prompt in InstructPix2Pix's diffusion pipeline, with the diffusion model fine-tuned using a standard noise-prediction loss plus an LPIPS term to preserve the query image's layout. For evaluation, two metrics are defined: EC2T (cosine similarity between an edit embedding and a text instruction embedding) and EC2EC (cosine similarity between two edit embeddings), both of which capture both the applied edit and structural preservation in a single scalar.

## Results

On TOP-Bench-X, EditCLIP achieves the best LPIPS (0.233) and the highest EC2EC score (0.477) among all exemplar-based methods, while running at 1.8 s per sample—tied with IP2P and 200× faster than VISII (370 s). In the user study, EditCLIP wins on both edit quality and input preservation against every baseline (winning rates >50%). For automated evaluation, the proposed EC2T and EC2EC metrics achieve the highest Pearson correlation with human judgment (0.256/0.104 and 0.372/0.157 respectively), outperforming CLIP (0.209/−0.028), Directional CLIP (0.186/−0.023), and S_visual (0.240/−0.023).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against IP2P (text-based reference), EditCLIP achieves lower LPIPS (0.233 vs. 0.295) and higher EC2EC (0.477 vs. 0.418), confirming better structural preservation and edit fidelity, though IP2P leads on text-based metrics (CLIP 0.226 vs. 0.216) since it conditions directly on text. Against VISII, EditCLIP dramatically outperforms on LPIPS (0.233 vs. 0.518) and EC2EC (0.477 vs. 0.313) while being 200× faster (1.8 s vs. 370 s), though VISII scores higher on S_visual (0.832 vs. 0.761). Against PromptDiffusion, EditCLIP wins on LPIPS (0.233 vs. 0.620), EC2EC (0.477 vs. 0.204), and runtime (1.8 s vs. 5.5 s), but PromptDiffusion leads on S_visual (0.906). Against the SOTA InstaManip, EditCLIP achieves better LPIPS (0.233 vs. 0.383), higher EC2EC (0.477 vs. 0.383), and is 8× faster (1.8 s vs. 14.9 s) while using only 5.9% of the parameters. In the evaluation-metric comparison (Table 2), EC2T and EC2EC both outperform CLIP, Directional CLIP, and S_visual in Pearson correlation with human judgment on both edit quality and preservation, with EC2EC showing the strongest overall alignment (0.372 edit, 0.157 preservation).

## Relation to Prior Work

EditCLIP directly extends CLIP's contrastive pre-training paradigm by modifying the visual encoder to accept channel-concatenated image pairs, learning an implicit edit representation aligned with textual instructions. For exemplar-based editing, it departs from prior approaches (VISII, PromptDiffusion, InstaManip) that either optimize textual tokens from exemplars or rely on large VLMs to verbalize edits, instead producing a purely visual embedding that plugs into InstructPix2Pix's diffusion pipeline as a drop-in replacement for the text encoder. For evaluation, it moves beyond CLIP score and directional CLIP similarity—which compare global image embeddings—by encoding the transformation itself, capturing both the applied edit and structural preservation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual utility of EditCLIP embeddings—as both a generation conditioning signal and an automated evaluation metric—addresses two persistent pain points in image editing research: the linguistic bottleneck for complex/compound edits and the reliance on costly human studies for quality assessment. The EC2T and EC2EC metrics showing higher Pearson correlation with human judgment than existing CLIP-based metrics could accelerate iterative development of editing pipelines. However, training is limited to the IP2P dataset (lacking removal and deformation edits), and the approach inherits IP2P's modality gap, leaving open whether the representation generalizes to fundamentally different edit types or to video/3D domains.
