## Problem

Transformer-based multi-task learning architectures suffer from fixed network capacity and rigid structures that cannot adapt to varying task-specific needs across network depths. Existing dynamic network approaches like Recon mitigate negative transfer by directly converting shared parameters into task-specific ones, but this causes parameter inefficiency, excessive computational overhead, and severe overfitting in transformers. The paper addresses the underexplored problem of how to adaptively expand existing pre-trained multi-task networks without duplicating network branches.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (from NYUD-v2, PASCAL-Context, and Taskonomy) and produces multiple dense prediction outputs simultaneously: semantic segmentation maps, depth maps, surface normal maps, edge maps, scene parsing maps, and saliency maps. It operates as a plug-in module on top of pre-trained ViT backbones (ViT-T through ViT-L), where the input is consumed as a sequence of N tokens of hidden dimension p at each transformer layer, and the outputs are per-task dense maps decoded from the modulated/expanded token representations.

## Method

DTME-MTL decomposes the token space at each transformer layer via SVD of the uncentered covariance of shared tokens, splitting it into a range space (high-variance eigenvectors) and a null space (low-variance eigenvectors) using a variance-proportion threshold r. Task-specific gradients are then projected onto both subspaces, and conflicts are categorized: range-space conflicts (g_R,i · g_R,j ≤ 0) are resolved by inserting lightweight affine modulators (W⊙T + b) that rotate/scale existing tokens, while null-space conflicts (g_N,i · g_N,j ≤ 0) are resolved by concatenating new task-specific tokens that participate in self-attention. This distinction is grounded in transfer learning theory—range-space conflicts indicate the pretrained network already has relevant interpretative capacity (fixable by modulation), whereas null-space conflicts signal genuinely missing features (requiring expansion). The entire procedure adds only 0.2–0.3% parameters and is applied dynamically during training, with early-stage expansion yielding the best results.

## Results

On Taskonomy (11 tasks, ViT-B), DTME-MTL achieves a multi-task performance gain of +4.67% Δm, the only positive value among all compared methods, while every optimization baseline shows negative Δm (ranging from −5.01% for Nash-MTL to −26.11% for GradDrop). The improvement requires only a 0.118% increase in parameters. On NYUD-v2, DTME-MTL (TM+TE) reaches 38.27 mIoU for semantic segmentation with just 0.24% additional parameters, substantially outperforming Recon (31.92 mIoU, 23.34% extra params) and approaching the single-task baseline (39.35 mIoU). When integrated into state-of-the-art architectures, DTME-MTL boosts InvPT's semantic segmentation from 79.03 to 81.91 mIoU on PASCAL-Context and TaskPrompter's from 55.30 to 56.36 mIoU on NYUD-v2, with <0.05% parameter overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DTME-MTL decisively outperforms all ten multi-task optimization baselines on Taskonomy: it is the only method with positive Δm (+4.67%), while the best optimizer (Nash-MTL) still degrades performance by −5.01% and GradDrop is worst at −26.11%; MGDA fails to converge entirely. Against the dynamic architecture Recon [22] on NYUD-v2, DTME-MTL achieves 38.27 mIoU vs. Recon's 31.92 mIoU (a 6.35-point gap) while using 0.24% extra parameters versus Recon's 23.34%, demonstrating that parameter-level duplication causes severe overfitting in transformers. When plugged into existing SOTA architectures, DTME-MTL consistently improves them: InvPT gains +0.82 mIoU on NYUD-v2 and +2.88 mIoU on PASCAL-Context, while TaskPrompter gains +1.06 mIoU on NYUD-v2 and +0.12 mIoU on PASCAL-Context, all with <0.05% parameter overhead. MTFormer [62] is listed as a reference architecture but no DTME-MTL augmentation is reported for it, so no direct head-to-head comparison is available. Overall, DTME-MTL wins every direct comparison, confirming that token-space conflict resolution is both more effective and more parameter-efficient than gradient manipulation or parameter duplication strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

DTME-MTL carves new ground at the intersection of multi-task optimization (PCGrad, CAGrad, MGDA) and dynamic network architectures (Recon), but departs from both by operating entirely in token space rather than parameter space or loss-weight space. It repurposes the conflicting-gradient diagnostic from PCGrad and the SVD-based variance decomposition from PCA literature, applying them to transformer token representations rather than weight matrices. The key conceptual departure from Recon is treating tokens as the unit of adaptation: range-space conflicts are resolved by affine modulation of existing tokens, while null-space conflicts trigger the insertion of new task-specific tokens, avoiding the parameter duplication that Recon performs at the weight level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of DTME-MTL (0.2–0.3% parameter overhead, ~1 hour SVD computation) makes it a practical add-on for existing transformer MTL stacks like TaskPrompter and InvPT, and the consistent gains across NYUD-v2, PASCAL-Context, and Taskonomy suggest broad applicability to dense-prediction MTL. The finding that Recon's parameter-level conflict resolution actively degrades transformer performance (worse than the joint baseline) while token-level resolution improves it challenges the assumption that more task-specific parameters always help. Open questions include whether the SVD-based range/null split generalizes to non-dense tasks (classification, detection), how the method behaves with very large token counts (e.g., high-resolution ViTs), and whether the expansion timing sensitivity can be made more robust.
