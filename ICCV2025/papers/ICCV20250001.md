## Problem

Existing 3D shape vertex descriptors, including recent foundation-model-based methods like Diff3F, are unable to disambiguate between left and right symmetric parts of a shape, causing left-right ambiguities in shape matching and part segmentation. While chirality has been studied in the image domain, no method in the shape analysis literature extracts chirality-aware per-vertex feature descriptors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250001/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an untextured 3D mesh (vertex set and edge set) plus a category label for diffusion guidance. It renders N multi-view images, textures them via StableDiffusion with ControlNet conditioned on depth and normal maps, then processes both the original and horizontally-flipped textured images through frozen DINO-V2 and StableDiffusion feature extractors. The output is a single scalar chirality value per vertex in [-1, 1], where the sign encodes left vs. right assignment, obtained as the first component of a normalized projected encoding.

## Method

The pipeline generates chirality vertex feature pairs by extracting 2D foundation model features (DINO-V2 + StableDiffusion) from both original and horizontally-flipped textured multi-view renderings, then projecting both sets back onto mesh vertices to obtain pairs (F_v, F̄_v) that differ only in left-right information. A small encoder g_Φ with a linear projection layer A and a decoder h_Ψ is trained unsupervised to map each vertex feature to a chirality scalar χ_v = [Ag(F_v)]₁ / ‖Ag(F_v)‖₂. Training uses four losses: a dissimilarity loss maximizing ‖χ − χ̄‖, an invertibility loss preventing trivial encoder solutions, a total variation loss for spatial smoothness along mesh edges, and a fifty-fifty loss balancing the number of vertices assigned to each half. The key insight is that horizontal image flipping creates a controlled chirality perturbation in the 2D features, allowing the network to isolate the left-right component without any supervision.

## Results

On the left-right disentanglement task (Table 1), the proposed chirality feature χ_DINO+SD achieves 91.84% accuracy on BeCoS, 94.09% on BeCoS_h (humanoids), and 84.19% on BeCoS_a (four-legged animals), substantially outperforming all baselines which cluster around 50–54% (Diff3F, DINO+SD, Zhang et al.) or 72–90% (Liu et al.). On shape matching (Table 2), augmenting Diff3F with the chirality feature improves accuracy from 20.25% to 22.73% and reduces error from 5.44 to 4.72 on TOSCA-a, and from 26.40% to 27.32% accuracy (error 1.69→1.02) on SHREC'19. The method also generalises to partial shapes (CP2P) and anisotropic meshes (FAUST/SCAPE) without retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's chirality feature decisively outperforms all feature-based baselines on left-right disentanglement: Diff3F [16], raw DINO+SD [40,49], and Zhang et al. [65] all hover near chance level (50–54%), confirming that standard 2D foundation model features carry no usable left/right signal. The axiomatic symmetry-axis method of Liu et al. [33] is the strongest baseline, reaching 72–90% depending on dataset, but still trails the proposed method by 10–25 points (e.g., 90.45 vs. 94.76 on SMAL, 80.84 vs. 95.51 on TOSCA). For shape matching, the chirality feature is used as an augmentation to Diff3F rather than a standalone method; it improves over Diff3F alone (20.25→22.73 acc on TOSCA-a; 26.40→27.32 acc on SHREC'19) and surpasses the unsupervised point-cloud methods DPC [29] (30.79 acc on TOSCA-a) and SE-ORNet [12] (33.25 acc on TOSCA-a) in error metric (4.72 vs. 3.74 and 4.32 respectively, though DPC/SE-ORNet report higher accuracy at the cost of larger error). The supervised 3D-CODED [21] is only available for human shapes and achieves 2.10% accuracy on SHREC'19, far below the proposed approach. Overall, the method wins on every dataset and every metric reported in both the disentanglement and matching experiments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Diff3F, which aggregates DINO-V2 and StableDiffusion features from multi-view renderings to produce semantic vertex descriptors for untextured 3D shapes. The key departure is that Diff3F and all prior vertex descriptors (including DINO+SD and fine-tuned variants) are blind to left-right chirality, causing symmetric parts to be indistinguishable. The authors introduce a chirality disentanglement stage that exploits the fact that horizontally flipping rendered images inverts chirality, then trains a lightweight autoencoder to extract a per-vertex scalar that separates left from right. In the broader literature, chirality has been studied in 2D (visual chirality, mirror detection, facial analysis) but, as the authors note, no prior method extracts chirality-aware vertex features in 3D shape analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Left-right ambiguity is a persistent and practically important failure mode in shape matching and part segmentation that existing descriptors cannot resolve; this paper provides a clean, unsupervised fix that plugs into the Diff3F pipeline and generalizes to partial and anisotropic shapes. The approach's reliance on 2D foundation models means it inherits their occlusion sensitivity and requires mesh connectivity for the total-variation loss, limiting direct point-cloud applicability. Open questions include whether 3D-native chirality extraction could avoid rendering artifacts, whether the method extends beyond bilateral symmetry to more complex symmetry groups, and whether the chirality signal can be made robust to the close-proximity and touching-part failures the authors acknowledge.
