## Problem

Hyperspectral image restoration methods are typically designed for a single degradation type, requiring separate models for denoising, deblurring, dehazing, etc. Existing all-in-one restoration frameworks developed for RGB images fail to account for the unique spectral structure of HSIs, leading to spectral distortions. No universal framework exists that can handle diverse, unknown HSI degradations while preserving spectral fidelity across tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251473/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded HSI tensor of shape H×W×B (e.g., 64×64×31 for natural scenes or 64×64×100 for remote sensing) and produces a restored HSI of the same shape. A degradation predictor first classifies the corruption type, which selects the appropriate textual prompt; the model is trained jointly on seven tasks (Gaussian denoising, complex denoising, Gaussian deblurring, super-resolution, inpainting, dehazing, band completion) and evaluated on two additional generalization tasks (motion deblurring, Poisson denoising). The output is generated via a 3×3 convolution followed by an image-level residual connection.

## Method

MP-HSIR is a three-level hierarchical encoder-decoder built from Prompt-Guided Spatial-Spectral Transformer Blocks (PGSSTBs), each combining sliding-window spatial self-attention, global spectral self-attention across all bands, and a prompt-guided local spectral self-attention that uses learnable spectral prompts (P_S) to provide universal low-rank spectral patterns as prior knowledge for spectral reconstruction. At each skip connection, a Text-Visual Synergistic Prompt (TVSP) module fuses a CLIP-derived textual prompt (selected by a degradation predictor) with a learnable visual prompt via cross-attention, injecting degradation-specific semantic guidance at the pixel level. The spectral prompt enables any local spectral feature to be represented as a linear combination of a small set of universal patterns, improving generalization across degradation types. The framework is trained end-to-end with L1 loss using AdamW, with separate training runs for natural-scene and remote-sensing HSI datasets due to domain differences.

## Results

Across 7 all-in-one HSI restoration tasks (Gaussian denoising, complex denoising, Gaussian deblurring, super-resolution, inpainting, dehazing, and band completion), MP-HSIR consistently achieves the best PSNR/SSIM among all all-in-one methods. Headline gains include 51.53 dB PSNR on inpainting (ICVL, mask rate 0.9) vs. 46.38 for PromptIR, 42.41 dB on dehazing (Eagle) vs. 41.03 for the best task-specific method MB-TaylorFormer, and 56.48 dB on band completion (ARAD) vs. 50.26 for MambaIR. In generalization tests, MP-HSIR reaches 40.06 dB on motion deblurring (few-shot, 5% data) and 38.56 dB on Poisson denoising (zero-shot), surpassing the best task-specific baselines (39.28 and 37.92 dB respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MP-HSIR outperforms all six all-in-one baselines (AirNet, PromptIR, PIP, HAIR, InstructIR, PromptHSI) across every one of the 7 evaluated tasks. The most dramatic wins are in inpainting (51.53 vs. 46.38 PSNR over PromptIR, +5.15 dB), dehazing (42.41 vs. 40.86 over HAIR, +1.55 dB), and band completion (56.48 vs. 51.31 over InstructIR, +5.17 dB). Against task-specific SOTA, MP-HSIR surpasses the best dedicated methods in inpainting (51.53 vs. 45.79 Restormer), dehazing (42.41 vs. 41.03 MB-TaylorFormer), and band completion (56.48 vs. 50.26 MambaIR), while remaining competitive in denoising (41.62 vs. 41.92 LDERT, −0.30 dB) and deblurring (48.07 vs. 47.66 MLWNet, +0.41 dB). In the generalization setting, MP-HSIR achieves 40.06 dB on motion deblurring with only 5% fine-tuning data, beating the fully-trained task-specific MLWNet (39.28 dB), and 38.56 dB on zero-shot Poisson denoising, exceeding LDERT (37.92 dB). Notably, InstructIR and PromptHSI show severe degradation on deblurring (34.05 and 32.52 dB respectively), indicating their inability to distinguish HSI-specific degradation types, whereas MP-HSIR maintains robust performance across all tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two research threads: prompt-based all-in-one image restoration (PromptIR, PIP, InstructIR, HAIR) and spatial-spectral transformer architectures for HSI (SST, SERT). It departs from prior all-in-one methods by introducing a spectral prompt that encodes universal low-rank spectral patterns as prior knowledge, a component absent from RGB-oriented prompt frameworks. The text-visual synergistic prompt (TVSP) repurposes CLIP embeddings and learnable visual prompts in a cross-attention fusion, extending the VLM-based dual-prompt idea (e.g., [1], [42]) with an explicit HSI domain adaptation layer. Unlike PromptHSI, which applies textual prompts to a single composite degradation mode, MP-HSIR handles nine distinct tasks with a unified architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap: deploying a single model for unknown HSI degradations avoids maintaining multiple specialized networks, reducing storage and management costs in remote sensing pipelines. The spectral prompt concept—learning a small set of universal low-rank patterns that remain consistent across degradation types—is a potentially transferable idea for other spectral imaging domains. Open questions include whether the frozen CLIP textual embeddings generalize to spectral ranges or sensor configurations outside the training distribution, and whether the degradation predictor's accuracy bottleneck limits end-to-end performance on truly novel corruption types.
