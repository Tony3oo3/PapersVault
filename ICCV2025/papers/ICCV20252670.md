## Problem

Existing privacy-preserving action recognition (PPAR) methods suffer significant performance degradation when transferring across video domains with different lighting, viewpoints, or environmental conditions. This paper is the first to systematically challenge the cross-domain transferability of PPAR models, showing that a privacy-preserving model trained on normal videos cannot maintain its privacy-utility trade-off on videos captured in dark environments or from drone perspectives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252670/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 16-frame video clips at 128×128 resolution (frame sampling rate 4) from a labeled source domain (with action and privacy labels) and an unlabeled target domain, producing both an anonymized video and an action class prediction. Target domain data is accessed only in an unsupervised manner during training, and target-domain privacy labels are used exclusively for evaluation. For cross-dataset settings where target privacy labels are unavailable, a separate VISPR image dataset provides privacy supervision during the adversarial training phase.

## Method

GenPriv introduces ST-VAE, a VQ-VAE with two separate codebooks (2048 static, 16384 dynamic entries) that decouples video features into static (spatial, frame-level 2D CNN) and dynamic (temporal, 3D CNN) components. The key insight is that privacy-sensitive information (face, skin color, clothing) resides primarily in static features, so privacy removal is targeted at the static codebook via adversarial training with a privacy predictor, while an EMA-based selective deactivation strategy removes codes that are privacy-critical but action-irrelevant. Three auxiliary losses guide the decoupling: a Spatial Consistency Loss (triplet structure) enforces static features to be invariant to temporal order, a Mutual Information Loss (NT-Xent) minimizes static-dynamic dependency, and a Temporal Alignment Loss (domain classifier + GRL) makes dynamic features domain-invariant. Training follows a three-phase protocol: identity initialization, two-step iterative minimax adversarial learning (alternating updates of anonymizer, action classifier, and privacy predictor), and evaluation with a re-initialized privacy predictor on anonymized target data.

## Results

On the TP-HMDB→TP-UCF transfer benchmark, GenPriv achieves 87.91% Top-1 action recognition accuracy on the target domain, surpassing the second-best method (SPAct at 72.22%) by 15.69 percentage points. On the reverse TP-UCF→TP-HMDB direction, GenPriv reaches 80.55% Top-1, outperforming SPAct (74.26%) by 6.29 points. Privacy preservation metrics (cMAP and F1) remain comparable to baselines, with GenPriv achieving cMAP of 67.42 and F1 of 0.519 on TP-HMDB→TP-UCF, indicating that the large action-recognition gains do not come at the cost of weaker privacy protection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenPriv decisively outperforms both VITA [43] and SPAct [9] on target-domain Top-1 accuracy in both transfer directions: 87.91% vs. 65.50% (VITA) and 72.22% (SPAct) on TP-HMDB→TP-UCF, and 80.55% vs. 72.73% (VITA) and 74.26% (SPAct) on TP-UCF→TP-HMDB. The multi-stage DANN variants (VITA+DANN, SPAct+DANN) offer only marginal improvements over their base methods and are substantially outperformed by GenPriv, confirming that post-hoc domain adaptation on anonymized data is insufficient. On privacy metrics, GenPriv achieves cMAP of 67.42 and F1 of 0.519 on TP-HMDB→TP-UCF, which is comparable to SPAct (66.47 / 0.570) and slightly lower than VITA (70.60 / 0.587), indicating that the large action-recognition gains are not purchased at the expense of privacy protection. The paper also reports consistent top-left trade-off positioning on the V-Kinetics-NECDrone and V-HMDB-ARID benchmarks (Fig. 5), where GenPriv achieves the highest target-domain Top-1 while maintaining competitive cMAP/F1 scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenPriv sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: adversarial privacy-preserving action recognition (VITA, SPAct, STPrivacy) and domain-adaptive action recognition (TA3N, T-CoN, SAVA, CoMix). The static/dynamic feature decoupling architecture is directly inspired by Wei et al.'s disentanglement-based domain adaptation [41], but is repurposed here so that privacy-sensitive content is isolated in the static branch and adversarially removed, while action-relevant dynamics are preserved and domain-aligned. Unlike prior PPAR methods that operate on a single domain, GenPriv is the first to explicitly formulate and benchmark the cross-domain transferability of privacy-preserving models, and unlike prior domain adaptation methods, it simultaneously enforces privacy constraints during the adaptation process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing three transfer benchmarks (TP-UCF↔TP-HMDB, V-Kinetics-NECDrone, V-HMDB-ARID) and showing that existing PPAR methods degrade substantially under domain shift, this paper highlights a practical gap that will matter as privacy-preserving video systems are deployed across heterogeneous environments. The decoupling insight—that privacy lives in static appearance while action lives in dynamics—provides a clean architectural principle that could inform future privacy-preserving video models beyond action recognition. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer videos or higher-resolution inputs, whether the adversarial privacy removal generalizes to unseen privacy attributes without retraining, and whether the multi-component training pipeline (initialization + two-stage adversarial + re-initialized evaluator) can be simplified for practical deployment.
