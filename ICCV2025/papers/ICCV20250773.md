## Problem

Existing street Gaussian splatting methods fail to model the diverse motion patterns of dynamic objects: supervised approaches impose a binary static/dynamic split, while self-supervised methods optimize the scene holistically without distinguishing motion speeds (e.g., pedestrians vs. vehicles). This lack of explicit motion modeling leads to blurry reconstructions of dynamic objects and suboptimal scene decomposition in complex driving scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250773/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The EMD module is a plug-and-play component that takes as input a set of 3D Gaussian primitives (position, scale, rotation, opacity, spherical harmonics) and a timestamp, and outputs deformed Gaussian parameters at that timestamp. It is integrated into existing frameworks (S3Gaussian, DeSiRe-GS for self-supervised; StreetGaussian, OmniRe for supervised) without altering their overall pipeline. In supervised settings, EMD additionally refines tracked 3D bounding box poses and SMPL body parameters through the same dual-scale mechanism.

## Method

EMD introduces two key components: (1) Motion-aware Feature Encoding, which concatenates multi-frequency spatial positional encoding, an adaptive temporal embedding (a learnable matrix W progressively sampled from 30 to 150 entries via bilinear interpolation during training), and a per-Gaussian learnable latent vector z_k ∈ R^32 to capture individual motion characteristics; and (2) a Dual-scale Deformation Framework where a coarse network predicts large-scale displacements (e.g., vehicle translations) and a fine network, conditioned on the coarse-offset position, predicts local deformations (e.g., articulated movements). The final deformed parameters are the sum of coarse and fine offsets for position/scale/opacity, and quaternion composition for rotation. Training includes a local smoothness regularization on Gaussian embeddings (KNN-based) and zero-anchoring losses on predicted deformations, making the module compatible with both self-supervised and supervised street Gaussian splatting pipelines.

## Results

On the Waymo-D32 self-supervised benchmark, S3Gaussian+EMD achieves 32.50 PSNR / 0.933 SSIM / 0.082 LPIPS for full-image scene reconstruction, improving over S3Gaussian (30.69/0.900/0.121) by +1.81 PSNR and over 3DGS (28.47/0.876/0.136) by +4.03 PSNR. For vehicle-specific reconstruction, the gain is even larger: 29.04 PSNR vs. 26.23 for S3Gaussian (+2.81). On the DeSiRe-GS comparison across Waymo and KITTI, EMD lifts scene-reconstruction PSNR to 34.15 (Waymo) and 34.13 (KITTI) while maintaining ~32 FPS. In supervised settings, StreetGaussian+EMD reaches 35.41 PSNR (1-cam) and OmniRe+EMD reaches 33.89 PSNR full-image / 25.97 PSNR human, and novel-trajectory FID drops from 83.48 to 45.11 at 0.5 m offset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMD consistently outperforms all self-supervised baselines. Against S3Gaussian, EMD gains +1.81 PSNR (full) and +2.81 PSNR (vehicle) on scene reconstruction, and +0.78 PSNR on vehicle novel-view synthesis. Against DeSiRe-GS, EMD improves Waymo scene-reconstruction PSNR from 33.61 to 34.15 and LPIPS from 0.204 to 0.130, while KITTI PSNR rises from 33.94 to 34.13; the speed cost is modest (36→32 FPS on Waymo, 41→32 FPS on KITTI). In the supervised setting, StreetGaussian+EMD beats StreetGaussian by +0.80 PSNR (1-cam) and +0.14 PSNR (3-cam), and OmniRe+EMD surpasses OmniRe by +1.32 PSNR full-image and +1.61 PSNR on the human category. EMD also substantially outperforms NeRF-based baselines (S-NeRF, EmerNeRF, SUDS) and other GS methods (PVG, 3DGS, MARS, NSG, StreetSurf) in Table 2, with the largest margins over S-NeRF (+14.48 PSNR on Waymo) and NSG (+10.07 PSNR). For novel-trajectory synthesis, S3Gaussian+EMD nearly halves the FID of S3Gaussian at every offset (45.11 vs. 83.48 at 0.5 m; 90.20 vs. 134.38 at 1.5 m), indicating markedly better generalization to unseen camera paths.

## Relation to Prior Work

EMD sits at the intersection of 4D Gaussian Splatting and street-scene reconstruction, building directly on the deformation-field paradigm of 4DGS and the self-supervised static-dynamic decomposition of S3Gaussian and DeSiRe-GS. It departs from prior street Gaussian methods (S3Gaussian, DeSiRe-GS, StreetGaussian, OmniRe) by identifying that they treat all dynamic objects with a uniform motion model, ignoring the continuous spectrum of speeds (pedestrians vs. vehicles). The key conceptual departure is the introduction of per-Gaussian learnable motion embeddings combined with a hierarchical coarse/fine deformation network, packaged as a framework-agnostic plug-and-play module rather than a new end-to-end pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means EMD can immediately benefit any existing street Gaussian splatting method without architectural redesign, lowering the barrier for practitioners working on autonomous driving simulation. The novel trajectory synthesis results (FID nearly halved at 0.5 m offset) suggest that explicit motion modeling is not just a rendering-quality improvement but a prerequisite for simulating out-of-distribution camera paths like lane changes, which is critical for closed-loop testing. Open questions include whether the dual-scale decomposition generalizes to scenes with extreme speed variation (e.g., cyclists alongside trucks), whether the progressive temporal embedding schedule is optimal, and whether the approach scales to longer sequences or more objects without the Gaussian embedding regularization becoming a bottleneck.
