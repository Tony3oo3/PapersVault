## Problem

Existing CLIP-based prompt learning methods struggle on fine-grained datasets because global methods treat visual features holistically, missing subtle inter-class discrepancies, while local methods either discard non-discriminative attributes or treat all attributes uniformly. The key unaddressed gap is that attributes contribute to recognition to varying degrees—some are shared across multiple classes (non-individualized) while others are exclusive to single classes (individualized)—and no prior method dynamically allocates attention based on this differential discrimination ability.

## Input / Output

At training, the method consumes 16-shot RGB images per class (encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-B-32 image encoder) and class names (tokenized and encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder with a learnable M-vector text prompt prepended). At inference, a single test image's visual feature is compared via cosine similarity against prompted textual features of all C classes to produce a class label. The only learned parameters are the text prompt vectors, two MLP encoders (E_s, E_d), a U-Net transfer model, and an MLP decoder D; all CLIP encoders remain frozen.

## Method

CaPL decomposes visual features into non-individualized and individualized attribute representations via a Brownian Bridge Diffusion Model (BBDM) that models the transition from shared attributes to the full visual feature conditioned on class-specific attributes, trained by reconstructing the original feature from a random diffusion timestep. A granule learning module then constructs visual granules under two causal inference strategies: (1) factual intervention decorates each of K individualized attributes (extracted via learnable attribute queries) with all non-individualized attributes to form focused supervision signals, and (2) counterfactual intervention swaps non-individualized and individualized attributes across images in a batch to simulate alternative contexts and improve generalizability. The text prompt is optimized to recognize these granules through cross-entropy losses on both attribute-level and class-level predictions, plus a reconstruction loss ensuring the decoder produces faithful granules. Training proceeds iteratively: the BBDM disentanglement module is first optimized, then the text prompt and granule learning module are jointly trained with the disentanglement module frozen.

## Results

On the base-to-new generalization task across 11 datasets, CaPL achieves an average harmonic mean of 84.34% (Base 86.37%, New 82.40%), outperforming the strongest baseline CoCoLe (H=82.70%) by 1.64%. The gains are most pronounced on fine-grained datasets: Flowers102 (H=91.75% vs. 88.60% for CoCoLe, +3.15%), StanfordCars (H=82.01% vs. 79.57%, +2.44%), and FGVC Aircraft (H=45.32% vs. 43.25%, +2.07%). On cross-dataset transfer (Table 2), CaPL achieves 70.45% average accuracy, surpassing CoCoLe (68.91%) by 1.54%, and on cross-domain generalization (Table 3), it reaches 62.70% average, leading CoCoLe (61.85%) by 0.85%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CaPL outperforms all 10 baselines on the average harmonic mean across 11 datasets. The closest competitor is CoCoLe [57] (local prompt learning via conceptual codebook), which CaPL surpasses by 1.64% in average H (84.34 vs. 82.70); the gap widens to 3.15% on Flowers102 and 2.07% on FGVC Aircraft, confirming the advantage of differentiated attribute perception over uniform attribute encoding. Among global prompt methods, ProMetaR [36] (H=80.49) and LoGoPrompt [42] (H=79.03) are the strongest, yet both trail CaPL by 3.85% and 5.31% respectively, highlighting the limitation of holistic feature processing for fine-grained tasks. Among local methods, CPL [58] (H=81.08) and HPT [48] (H=80.23) are competitive on coarse-grained datasets but fall 3.26% and 4.11% short of CaPL on average, with the largest deficits on fine-grained benchmarks. The zero-shot CLIP [39] baseline (H=71.70) and the original CoOp [61] (H=71.66) are substantially outperformed, with CaPL gaining over 12% in harmonic mean. On cross-dataset transfer, CaPL leads CoCoLe by 1.54% average accuracy and achieves the best result on 9 of 10 target datasets; on cross-domain generalization, it attains the top score on 3 of 4 ImageNet variants (ImageNet-S, -A, -R) and second-best on ImageNet-V2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CLIP-based prompt learning by introducing a causal inference framework that departs from both global methods (which treat features holistically) and local methods (which either discard or uniformly weight attributes). It builds on CoOp's learnable text prompt formulation [61] and repurposes Brownian Bridge Diffusion Models [29]—originally designed for image-to-image translation—as a mechanism for disentangling visual features into shared (non-individualized) and class-specific (individualized) attribute representations. The key conceptual departure is the 'visual granulation' technique, where factual and counterfactual interventions construct synthetic supervision signals by recombining attributes across images, a strategy not previously applied to prompt learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The causal perspective provides a principled explanation for why fine-grained recognition fails under standard prompt learning: attributes contribute unequally, and homogeneous shared attributes can create spurious correlations that hurt generalization. The consistent improvements across 15 datasets (especially 2–3% harmonic mean gains on Flowers102, StanfordCars, and FGVC Aircraft) validate that differentiated attribute perception is a meaningful axis of improvement. However, the BBDM-based disentanglement module introduces substantial training overhead, and the paper acknowledges that constructing more accurate granules remains an open problem. Whether the causal framing generalizes beyond few-shot settings or to other foundation models (e.g., SAM, LLaVA) is unexplored.
