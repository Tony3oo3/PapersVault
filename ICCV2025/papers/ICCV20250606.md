## Problem

Automatic quad mesh generation remains challenging because traditional field-guided methods rely on local geometric features or manual constraints, producing layouts that fail to capture global shape semantics. Existing learning-based approaches are further limited by small training datasets, mesh-based architectures that break on non-manifold geometries, and unresolved ambiguities in frame field representation that hinder stable training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250606/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an arbitrary triangle mesh (including non-manifold and marching-cubes-extracted surfaces) as input, samples a point cloud with normals from it, and predicts a per-point frame field encoded as polyvector coefficients (complex polynomial c₀, c₁) in the local tangent space. The predicted frame field drives a three-step extraction pipeline (frame-guided deformation, QuadriFlow isotropic quad remeshing, deformation transfer) to produce the final quad mesh. The neural network is connectivity-agnostic, operating solely on point coordinates and normals rather than mesh topology.

## Method

NeuFrameQ decomposes frame field prediction into two complementary stages: (1) a PTv3 point-cloud transformer regresses the polyvector representation of the unit direction field, using a 2-RoSy-aware exponential loss with permutation matching to handle the inherent symmetry of frames; (2) a DDIM-based conditional diffusion model predicts log-magnitudes given the predicted direction, with attention-based encoder/decoder layers enforcing invariance under the four-direction permutation. The polyvector representation (converting the frame {u,−u,v,−v} to polynomial coefficients c₀=−(u²+v²), c₁=u²v²) provides a unique, ambiguity-free encoding in the tangent space. Trained on a large-scale dataset of ~270k quad meshes from Objaverse and related sources, the connectivity-agnostic point-cloud formulation generalizes to non-manifold and neural-field-derived geometries that mesh-based methods cannot process.

## Results

On frame field prediction (DFaust), NeuFrameQ achieves a mean angle error of 5.316° and magnitude error of 0.0811, outperforming LDF (6.987°, 0.0995) and IntrinsicVH (6.623°, 0.1043). On end-to-end quad remeshing (QuadWild-300), the method produces pure quad meshes with the fewest singularities across all four test models (e.g., 40 vs. 266 for Instant-Meshes on 'lock'; 114 vs. 467 on 'lego') while achieving the best chamfer distance on 'lock' (0.035×10⁻⁵) and 'lego' (0.011×10⁻⁵). The Scaled Jacobian scores are competitive (0.952–0.982), confirming good element quality despite the anisotropic deformation step.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On frame field prediction (DFaust), NeuFrameQ clearly outperforms both prior learning-based methods: LDF [9] (6.987° angle / 0.0995 mag) and IntrinsicVH [13] (6.623° / 0.1043), achieving 5.316° / 0.0811. For end-to-end quadrangulation on QuadWild-300, the comparison is more nuanced. Against Instant-Meshes [23], NeuFrameQ produces pure quad meshes with dramatically fewer singularities (e.g., 40 vs. 266 on 'lock', 114 vs. 467 on 'lego'), though IM achieves slightly lower chamfer distance and higher Scaled Jacobian on some models due to its quad-dominant (not strictly quad) output. Against QuadriFlow [21], NeuFrameQ wins on chamfer distance for 'lock' (0.035 vs. 0.116) and 'lego' (0.011 vs. 0.030) but loses on 'nicolo' (0.757 vs. 0.536) and 'igea100k' (0.869 vs. 0.292), with SJ scores within 0.01–0.03. Against QuadWild [33], NeuFrameQ consistently achieves lower chamfer distance across all four models (e.g., 0.035 vs. 0.587 on 'lock', 0.011 vs. 0.633 on 'lego') while maintaining comparable or better Scaled Jacobian, indicating that the learned frame field yields more faithful geometric recovery than feature-line-driven remeshing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the frame-field-based quadrangulation framework of Panozzo et al. [30] by replacing hand-crafted energy minimization with learned frame field prediction, building directly on the pioneering neural approaches of Dielen et al. [9] and Gao et al. [13]. The key departures are threefold: (1) a connectivity-agnostic point-cloud representation (using PTv3) that handles non-manifold meshes, in contrast to the mesh-based spiral networks and intrinsic heat networks of prior work; (2) a principled decomposition of frame field prediction into direction regression and direction-conditioned magnitude diffusion, addressing the ill-posed nature that single-stage regression cannot; and (3) a large-scale 270k-mesh dataset that enables generalization far beyond the SMPL-topology-limited training sets of earlier methods. The polyvector representation from Diamanti et al. [8] is adopted to resolve frame ambiguity, and the remeshing stage replaces the fragile MIQ solver with QuadriFlow for practical robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NeuFrameQ addresses a critical gap in contemporary 3D content pipelines: neural field reconstruction methods (NeRF, 3D Gaussian Splatting, LRM, etc.) produce triangle meshes that lack the structural regularity of artist-crafted quad meshes, and no prior method could automatically bridge this gap at scale with semantic awareness. The 270k-mesh dataset and the connectivity-agnostic formulation make the approach broadly applicable to real-world 3D assets, including non-manifold CAD models. However, the quad mesh quality remains bottlenecked by the isotropic remeshing step (QuadriFlow), and the authors acknowledge that performance is largely defined by dataset quality. Open questions include whether both prediction stages can be unified into a single generative model, and whether learned frame fields can guide more advanced remeshing algorithms beyond current field-aligned approaches.
