## Problem

Existing controllable scene generation for autonomous driving faces a fundamental trade-off: full-sequence diffusion models cannot react to real-time environmental changes because they denoise the entire future at once, while next-token prediction models lack goal-state awareness, making precise control over safety-critical scenarios (e.g., directing a collision) infeasible. Additionally, public driving datasets are dominated by safe, ordinary behaviors, so learned models struggle to generate rare corner cases like cut-ins, sudden braking, and collisions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a vectorized scene representation: an agent tensor (x, y, heading, velocity, dimensions for up to A agents over T timesteps), a map tensor (lane coordinates and types), and optionally a goal point. Agents are encoded via MLP into unified tokens, while the map is compressed into fixed-length tokens through Perceiver IO. Output is a sequence of future agent tokens generated in sliding chunks rather than as a single fixed-length sequence, with each chunk containing historical context, frames under denoising, and optional goal tokens at varying noise levels.

## Method

Nexus introduces decoupled diffusion by assigning independent noise levels to each token across agent, temporal, and denoising-step axes (tri-axial masking), unifying full-sequence diffusion with next-token prediction into a single training objective. During training, a random noise matrix is applied to the agent tensor and the DiT learns to reconstruct the full sequence from these soft-masked tokens, enabling low-noise goals to condition generation without architectural changes. At inference, a noise-aware scheduling strategy (pyramidal or trapezoidal) slides a chunk through time: at each denoising step, zero-noise tokens are popped out and new high-noise frames are pushed in, allowing real-time overwriting of agent states for reactivity. The DiT backbone uses map cross-attention for lane-following, AdaLN-conditioned temporal/spatial transformer blocks for agent interactions, and 2D rotary positional encoding over both physical time and denoising steps. A classifier-guidance post-processing step enforces behavioral constraints (collision separation, on-road pulling, trajectory smoothing) at each denoising step.

## Results

On nuPlan conditioned generation (8-second future from 2-second history), Nexus achieves an ADE of 1.28, off-road rate of 6.89%, collision rate of 1.62%, and instability of 4.63, outperforming all baselines. With Nexus-Data and classifier guidance (Nexus-Full), ADE drops further to 1.12 and collision rate to 1.56%. On free exploration, Nexus reduces collision rate to 2.61% (Nexus-Full: 2.10%) and instability to 3.23 (Nexus-Full: 2.14). On the Waymo Open Sim Agent validation set, Nexus scores 61.9 on the composite metric versus 55.8 for SceneDiffuser.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Nexus outperforms all six baselines on the primary ADE metric, achieving 1.28 versus SceneDiffuser's 5.99 (−4.71), GUMP's 1.93, CTG's 3.33, and Diffusion Policy's 7.80. On collision rate, Nexus (1.62%) dramatically beats SceneDiffuser (11.78%), Diffusion Policy (14.92%), and GUMP (7.85%), and also improves over Diffusion Planner (3.47%) and CTG (4.80%). The closest competitor is Diffusion Planner, which achieves a slightly lower ADE (1.36) and much lower instability (M_k 2.07 vs. 4.63), but Nexus wins on collision rate (1.62% vs. 3.47%) and off-road rate (6.89% vs. 8.01%). In free exploration, Nexus achieves the lowest collision rate (2.61%) among all methods, while Diffusion Planner leads on instability (1.73). Nexus also generates 8-second scenes in 2.79 s, faster than SceneDiffuser (5.34 s), GUMP (5.59 s), and Diffusion Policy (6.59 s), while matching IDM's speed (2.16 s) with far superior quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

Nexus builds on the diffusion transformer (DiT) architecture for structured scene generation, but departs from the two dominant paradigms in driving scene synthesis: full-sequence diffusion (SceneDiffuser, Diffusion Policy) that denoises an entire trajectory at once, and autoregressive next-token prediction (GUMP, TrajEnglish) that rolls out frames sequentially. The key conceptual move is a 'tri-axial mask modeling' formulation that unifies noise-axis masking (diffusion) and time-axis masking (next-token prediction) by assigning independent noise levels to each token, drawing inspiration from Diffusion Forcing. This decoupled noise design, combined with pyramidal/trapezoidal scheduling, is the paper's architectural departure from prior work, enabling simultaneous goal conditioning and real-time reactivity that neither paradigm achieves alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in autonomous driving: the inability to generate diverse, safety-critical scenarios at scale, which limits data-driven approaches to long-tail cases. Nexus-Data (540 hours of adversarially generated corner cases) is the largest such collection to date, and the demonstrated 20% closed-loop planning improvement via synthetic data augmentation shows a concrete path to reducing real-world data collection costs. Open questions include whether the vectorized-only representation can be extended to richer modalities (the NeRF rendering attempt is preliminary), how the approach scales to denser urban scenes with more agents, and whether the adversarial data generation pipeline generalizes beyond the specific attack patterns (cut-in, collision) it was designed for.
