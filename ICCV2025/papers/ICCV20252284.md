## Problem

Image and image-text embedding models are evaluated through disjointed, task-specific protocols (retrieval, zero-shot classification, linear probing, fine-tuning, MLLM proxies), leaving it unclear whether a model strong at one capability generalizes to others. No existing benchmark provides a unified, comprehensive evaluation spanning the full spectrum of visual embedding abilities, including under-explored dimensions like visual text understanding, compositionality, interleaved encoding, and multilinguality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252284/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark feeds frozen embeddings from 50 models (vision-only, CLIP-style, and MLLM-based) into 130 evaluation tasks. Inputs include RGB images, text queries, interleaved image-text sequences, and high-resolution document images; for models without native interleaved encoding, a simple sum of text and image embeddings is used. Outputs are per-task scores (nDCG@10, accuracy, NMI, Spearman correlation) aggregated into 8 category averages, with a lightweight MIEB-lite subset of 51 tasks reducing runtime by ~82% while preserving 0.992 Spearman correlation with full MIEB rankings.

## Method

MIEB organizes 130 tasks into 8 categories—Retrieval (45), Document Understanding (10), Linear Probing (22), Clustering (5), Zero-shot Classification (23), Compositionality Evaluation (7), Vision-centric QA (6), and Visual STS (9)—spanning 38 languages. Key design choices include: Visual STS, which renders existing STS text pairs as images to probe OCR capability via Spearman correlation; few-shot (16-shot) logistic regression linear probing validated as rank-stable across k∈{8,…,256}; compositionality evaluation via image-text pair classification with confounded negatives (e.g., WinoGround, ARO); and multilingual retrieval across 55 subtasks. MIEB-lite is constructed by iteratively removing tasks with pairwise model-performance correlations above 0.45–0.5, then clustering remaining tasks via UMAP+HDBSCAN into 17 interpretable groups to ensure balanced category coverage. The benchmark is fully reproducible with versioned model and dataset specifications, and new models can be added in under 5 lines of code.

## Results

On MIEB (130 tasks, 38 languages), no single model dominates all 8 task categories. Voyage-multimodal-3 (MLLM) achieves the highest overall Mean(m) of 62.5, excelling in Document Understanding (71.1 nDCG@5), Visual STS English (81.8 Spearman), and multilingual Visual STS (70.4). Among open-source models, E5-V leads with Mean(m) 58.2, achieving state-of-the-art multilingual retrieval (66.6 nDCG@10) and compositionality (46.3 accuracy). CLIP-style encoders (e.g., SigLIP-so400m) dominate linear probing (84.6) and zero-shot classification (70.8), where MLLM-based models underperform by 10–25 points on fine-grained tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Voyage-multimodal-3 (closed-source MLLM) leads overall (Mean(m) 62.5) and is best in Document Understanding (71.1 vs. 62.7 for E5-V) and Visual STS English (81.8 vs. 79.3), but trails SigLIP-so400m in linear probing (71.3 vs. 84.6) and zero-shot classification (58.2 vs. 70.8). E5-V (open-source MLLM) is the strongest open model overall (58.2) and dominates multilingual retrieval (66.6 nDCG@10 vs. 59.2 for SigLIP-multilingual and 54.4 for VISTA), compositionality (46.3 vs. 40.4 for SigLIP), and vision-centric QA (51.9 vs. 46.3 for SigLIP). SigLIP-so400m-patch14-384 is the best CLIP-style encoder, winning retrieval (40.8), linear probing (84.6), and zero-shot classification (70.8), but scores only 40.2 on multilingual retrieval and 41.4 on multilingual Visual STS. EVA02-CLIP-bigE-14-plus achieves the highest clustering score (92.4 NMI) and competitive zero-shot classification (70.8), yet lags in document understanding (32.3) and multilingual tasks (27.8, 28.2). Open-CLIP ViT-bigG leads single-modality retrieval (41.5) and clustering (85.6) but drops to 28.0 on multilingual retrieval. VLM2Vec (LoRA/Full) scores 50.5/50.4 overall, trailing E5-V by ~8 points, though it matches E5-V on vision-centric QA (62.0/62.1). VISTA(m3) achieves 54.4 average multilingual retrieval with low variance (0.9), outperforming Open-CLIP (31.4) and EVA02-CLIP (31.0) by over 20 points. DINOv2 and MoCo-v3 (self-supervised, no text) perform near-random on Visual STS (Spearman 12.98 and 14.31), confirming that text supervision is essential for visual text understanding. BLIP/BLIP-2 appear in the top-5 for vision-centric QA despite being absent from other categories, suggesting that including images in contrastive fine-tuning benefits spatial reasoning tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

MIEB directly extends MTEB (the Massive Text Embedding Benchmark) by porting its codebase, leaderboard infrastructure, and evaluation philosophy to the image and image-text domain. It consolidates previously fragmented benchmarks—CLIP Benchmark, UnED, M-BEIR, and task-specific retrieval/classification suites—into a single unified protocol spanning 130 tasks across 8 categories. The paper carves new ground by introducing task formulations absent from prior image benchmarks, notably Visual STS (rendering textual similarity pairs as images to probe OCR), Compositionality Evaluation with confounders, and Document Understanding as a retrieval task, addressing gaps the authors identify in existing evaluation protocols.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that no single embedding model dominates all task categories, combined with the >99% correlation between Visual STS scores and MLLM performance on OCR/TextVQA benchmarks, provides a practical, low-cost proxy for selecting vision encoders without training full MLLMs. The revelation that text-supervised models like CLIP possess previously unacknowledged OCR capabilities, while self-supervised models (DINOv2, MoCo) perform near-randomly on visual text, reframes how the community should think about what contrastive language-image pretraining actually encodes. Open questions include whether MLLM-based embedding models can recover fine-grained visual discrimination (where CLIP-style models still lead) and how to improve compositionality, where all models score lowest. MIEB-lite (51 tasks, 82% compute reduction) makes the benchmark accessible for iterative model development.
