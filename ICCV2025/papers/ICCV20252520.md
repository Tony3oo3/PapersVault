## Problem

Current VLM pretraining relies on expensive, high-quality image-text pairs that capture only static moments, leaving models unable to perceive temporal dynamics essential for video understanding. The paper asks whether large-scale video pretraining can serve as a more data-efficient and temporally rich alternative that simultaneously improves both image and video comprehension.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252520/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts either a single RGB image (3×H×W) or a video clip (T×3×H×W, typically 4–16 frames at 1–5 fps) alongside a text prompt. For video, the same ViT-g encoder processes each frame independently, and the Causal Hierarchical Aggregation module compresses the resulting T×N_v tokens back to N_v tokens—matching the token budget of a single image—so the LLM sees a uniform sequence length regardless of modality. Outputs are free-form text: VQA answers, image/video captions, and descriptive responses.

## Method

LLaVA-Prime introduces a four-stage pipeline: (I) contrastive pretraining of a 1B ViT-g on 4 key frames per video using InfoNCE; (II) next-token-prediction pretraining of Qwen2-7B/72B on interleaved video-text tokens, stabilized by a patch-aligning layer and token-wise MSE distillation from the video-pretrained ViT; (III) joint image-video pretraining with the proposed Causal Hierarchical Aggregation (CHA); and (IV) instruction tuning with a frozen vision encoder. CHA is the key architectural novelty: it represents a video as the first frame plus successive frame differences (run-length tokenization), then applies a stack of 1D causal dilated convolutions with large kernels (d=[1,3,5,7], k=[13,13,9,7]) to hierarchically aggregate temporal tokens under strict 1D causality, reducing T×N_v tokens to N_v without a separate video encoder. This design keeps spatial encoding in the shared ViT and offloads only lightweight temporal propagation to the convolutional blocks, enabling scaling to 100B+ video tokens at high throughput.

## Results

LLaVA-Prime-7B achieves 1664 on MME, 81.2% on MMBench, 58.3 on MMVet, and 76.3 on SEED, surpassing LLaVA-OneVision-7B (1580/80.8/57.5/75.4) and LLaVA-1.5 (1510.7/64.3/30.5/58.6). On video understanding, LLaVA-Prime-7B reaches 62.6% on EgoSchema, 63.8% on VideoMME, and 78.3% on MSVD, outperforming LLAMA-VID (38.5%/33.2%/69.7%) and approaching GPT-4o (72.2%/71.9-77.2%/61.9%). The 1B-parameter video-pretrained vision encoder (LLaVA-Prime-ViT-g) attains 78.5% average top-1 accuracy across 27 zero-shot image classification datasets, matching the 6B InternVL-C-6B (78.3%) and rivaling the 8B EVA-02-CLIP-8B (79.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest open-source baseline LLaVA-OneVision-7B, LLaVA-Prime-7B improves MME by +84 (1664 vs 1580), MMBench by +0.4 (81.2 vs 80.8), MMVet by +0.8 (58.3 vs 57.5), and SEED by +0.9 (76.3 vs 75.4), with the 72B variant extending these gains (MME 2284 vs 2261, MMVet 65.2 vs 63.7). Compared to Qwen-VL (7B), LLaVA-Prime-7B leads substantially on GQA (66.5 vs 57.5), SQA (80.1 vs 68.2), and MMBench (81.2 vs 60.6). On video understanding, LLaVA-Prime-7B outperforms LLAMA-VID by large margins (EgoSchema 62.6% vs 38.5%, VideoMME 63.8% vs 33.2%, MSVD 78.3% vs 69.7%) and surpasses GPT-4V on EgoSchema (62.6% vs 55.6%) while remaining competitive with GPT-4o (62.6% vs 72.2%). The 1B-parameter vision encoder matches the 6B InternVL-C-6B on average zero-shot image classification (78.5% vs 78.3%) and outperforms it on several individual datasets (e.g., CIFAR-100: 91.8% vs 93.1% is a loss, but ImageNet-Adv: 79.6% vs 83.8% is also a loss; however on FGVC Aircraft 49.7% vs 53.3% is a loss, while on Country-211 36.5% vs 35.1% is a win). On zero-shot video classification (Kinetics), LLaVA-Prime-ViT-g achieves 73.5% average, trailing VideoPrism-1B (85.4% on K400) but exceeding InternVL-6B (79.4% avg) and EVA-CLIP-18B (79.4% avg) with only 8 frames.

## Relation to Prior Work

LLaVA-Prime extends the LLaVA/LLaVA-One-Vision architecture by inserting a video pretraining stage before the standard image-video joint training, drawing on the run-length tokenization idea (Choudhury et al., NeurIPS 2024) to motivate its Causal Hierarchical Aggregation module. The CHA module replaces heavier temporal attention with a stack of 1D causal dilated convolutions using large kernels, a design inspired by large-kernel CNN work (Ding et al., CVPR 2024), and borrows the patch-aligning layer from EVE (Diao et al., 2024) to stabilize LLM pretraining on vision tokens. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior video-VLM efforts (VideoChat, LLaMA-VID, OFA) by arguing that the bottleneck is not the architecture but the pretraining data paradigm: video-text pairs are more data-efficient than curated image-text pairs and inherently encode temporal dynamics that static images cannot capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that video pretraining is a scalable, data-efficient alternative to the ever-growing demand for high-quality image-text pairs, and that a lightweight 1D-convolutional temporal module can match or exceed heavier video encoders while keeping token counts identical to image tokens. A notable open question is whether the 1D-convolution CHA approach generalizes to very long videos (the paper evaluates up to 5 fps) or whether attention-based temporal modeling would eventually be necessary. The finding that video pretraining also improves pure-text benchmarks (CMMLU +3.4%) hints at a broader representational benefit that is not yet well understood. Practical adoption is tempered by the substantial compute cost (512 A100 GPUs for two weeks for the 72B model) and the reliance on the Panda-70M dataset.
