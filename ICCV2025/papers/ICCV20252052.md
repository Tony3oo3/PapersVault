## Problem

End-to-end autonomous driving methods achieve very low open-loop collision rates (as low as 0.1%) yet remain below 35% success rate on closed-loop benchmarks, revealing a critical gap between trajectory fitting and safe closed-loop control. The paper identifies two underexplored causes: planning queries rely on sparse, single-granularity waypoint supervision that fails to provide fine-grained control signals, and planning queries lack a mechanism to selectively retrieve image features relevant to the predicted trajectory, forcing them to rely on global attention without geometric context.

## Input / Output

The model consumes six-camera RGB images at 640×352 resolution along with target points and high-level commands (ego status is deliberately excluded from inputs and used only as a training supervision signal). It simultaneously outputs 3D object detections, motion predictions, a vectorized online map, and a rich set of planning waypoints spanning three types (temporal at 2 Hz and 5 Hz, spatial at 2 m and 5 m intervals, and driving-style across three speed bands) for each of 48 modalities. At inference, lateral control is derived from dense spatial waypoints and longitudinal control from the highest-scoring driving-style waypoints, with a fallback to temporal waypoints when current speed deviates from the selected style.

## Method

HiP-AD introduces a unified decoder that processes 900 agent, 100 map, and 480 planning queries (10 granularities × 48 modalities) through three iterative interaction modules: temporal cross-attention with stored queries, collaborative self-attention with a geometric distance-weighted attention map, and a task-specific deformable aggregation that samples image features. The key architectural novelty is Planning Deformable Attention (PDA), which projects future waypoints onto multi-view images at predefined heights and learns spatial offsets to sample local features in the physical neighborhood of the trajectory, replacing the global attention used by prior unified transformers. For planning, the paper proposes hierarchical multi-granularity waypoints (temporal, spatial, and driving-style) with diverse sampling strategies; queries of different granularities within a modality are summed into a fused query that regresses all waypoint types, and an align-matching strategy designates one reference granularity group to determine the best modality, broadcasting the match to all other groups so gradients flow consistently. Training is two-phase (12 epochs without the driving-style head, then 6 epochs with it) using a ResNet-50 backbone, 6 decoder layers, and AdamW with cosine annealing on 8×NVIDIA 4090 GPUs.

## Results

On the closed-loop Bench2Drive benchmark, HiP-AD achieves a Driving Score of 86.77 and a Success Rate of 69.09%, substantially outperforming the previous best method DriveTransformer-Large (63.46 DS, 35.01% SR) by over 20 points in Driving Score and 34 points in Success Rate. On the open-loop nuScenes validation set, HiP-AD reports an average L2 error of 0.56 m and a Collision Rate of 0.07%, matching the best collision rate while remaining competitive in L2 (vs. 0.33 m for DriveTransformer-Large and DiFSD-S). In multi-ability evaluation on Bench2Drive, HiP-AD reaches a mean ability score of 65.98%, nearly doubling the 38.60% of DriveTransformer-Large, with particularly large gains in Merging (50.00% vs. 17.57%) and Overtaking (84.44% vs. 35.00%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiP-AD decisively outperforms all ten baselines on the closed-loop Bench2Drive benchmark, with the largest gap over DriveTransformer-Large (the prior state-of-the-art): +23.3 Driving Score and +34.1 percentage points in Success Rate. Against the sparse-query methods (SparseDrive, DiFSD), the advantage is even more pronounced in Success Rate (69.09% vs. 16.71% and 21.00% respectively), indicating that the multi-granularity planning and planning deformable attention are critical for safe closed-loop behavior. On the open-loop nuScenes evaluation, HiP-AD ties DriveTransformer-Large and DiFSD-S on Collision Rate (0.07%) but trails them in average L2 (0.56 m vs. 0.33 m), suggesting the closed-loop-oriented design trades some trajectory precision for safer driving. In multi-ability scoring, HiP-AD leads every individual scenario (Merging, Overtaking, Emergency Brake, Give Way, Traffic Sign) by a wide margin, with the mean ability score of 65.98% versus 38.60% for DriveTransformer-Large. The only metric where HiP-AD is not the best is Comfortness (19.36 vs. 48.63 for SparseDrive), which the authors attribute to aggressive but necessary maneuvers (e.g., emergency braking) that are required to achieve the high Success Rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiP-AD extends the unified single-decoder paradigm established by DriveTransformer and VAD, but departs in two key ways: it replaces global image attention with a planning deformable attention (PDA) that uses waypoint geometry to sample local multi-view features, and it introduces a multi-granularity planning query representation that decomposes trajectories into temporal, spatial, and driving-style waypoints at multiple sampling densities. The geometric attention mechanism in the collaborative interaction module is adapted from SparseBEV, while the sparse query projection and vectorized map representation follow the SparseDrive/VAD lineage. The waypoint decomposition builds on CarLLaVA's time/space conditioning but extends it with driving-style waypoints and a multi-granularity fusion strategy with align-matching for training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the well-known open-loop-to-closed-loop performance gap in E2E-AD, showing that richer planning supervision (multi-granularity waypoints) and geometry-guided feature extraction (PDA) can nearly double closed-loop success rate on Bench2Drive (69% vs. 35% for the prior best). The dramatic reduction in ego hesitation (timeout from 41.8% to 1.8%) suggests that sparse supervision is a key bottleneck for closed-loop behavior learning. However, the comfortness score is notably lower than several baselines, and the authors acknowledge that real-world validation and handling of rear-approaching vehicles remain open challenges. The method's complexity (480 planning queries across 10 granularities and 48 modalities) raises questions about scalability and practical deployment.
