## Problem

Existing visual style prompting methods for text-to-image diffusion models suffer from content leakage, where poses, layouts, and objects from the reference image are unintentionally transferred into the generated result alongside the intended style. Training-based approaches (fine-tuning, textual inversion, cross-attention adaptation) incur high computational costs and face an inherent style-content trade-off, while training-free self-attention swapping methods still fail to fully eliminate leaked content and often degrade when given real images as style references.

## Input / Output

For the T2I setting, the method takes a text prompt and a reference image (visual style prompt) and produces a new image whose content follows the text while its style matches the reference. For the I2I setting, a content image is additionally provided and fed through ControlNet to preserve spatial structure. Real reference images are consumed via stochastic encoding (adding Gaussian noise at each timestep) rather than DDIM inversion, avoiding the need to store intermediate latents or accumulate numerical error. The method is entirely training-free and operates on a pretrained latent diffusion model (SDXL).

## Method

StyleKeeper extends classifier-free guidance by incorporating swapping self-attention: the key and value features of self-attention layers are borrowed from a parallel denoising process conditioned on the visual style prompt, while the query retains the original text-conditioned content. The key novelty is Negative Visual Query Guidance (NVQG), which subtracts a second guidance term computed by swapping queries (using Q_visual with KV_text) to explicitly negate the content component of the reference, derived from a Bayes-rule factorization of the KV features into content and style subspaces. Swapping is restricted to late upblock layers only, since bottleneck blocks encode content identity and downblock feature maps lack reliable spatial layout. For real-image references, stochastic encoding replaces DDIM inversion, and an AdaIN-based color calibration on the predicted x0 at intermediate timesteps aligns the output's color statistics with the reference, reducing cumulative sampling error.

## Results

The paper's main quantitative comparison (Figure 9) evaluates style similarity, text alignment, and content leakage across multiple baselines. StyleKeeper achieves the best style similarity while simultaneously maintaining strong text alignment and minimal content leakage, a combination no other method achieves. IP-Adapter attains higher style similarity but significantly compromises text prompt faithfulness. The ablation study (Figure 12) confirms that each component—swapping self-attention, NVQG, stochastic encoding, and color calibration—contributes to improvements in both style similarity and text alignment, with stochastic encoding consistently outperforming DDIM inversion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against StyleAligned [16], StyleKeeper clearly wins on content leakage: StyleAligned transfers poses, layouts, and object counts from the reference (e.g., a horse's pose appears in a dinosaur prompt), while StyleKeeper faithfully follows the text prompt. IP-Adapter [59] achieves higher style similarity scores but at the cost of significantly neglecting text prompts, making it unsuitable when content control matters. Dreambooth-LoRA [39,40], StyleDrop [42], DEADiff [35], CSGO [55], and InstantStyle [50,51] all exhibit varying degrees of content leakage (unwanted layouts, structures, or compositions from the reference) and reduced diversity within a text prompt, whereas StyleKeeper preserves both text alignment and generation diversity. In the I2I setting (Figure 10), CrossAttn [1] produces rough, blocky outputs that fail to capture fine style details, and StyleID [6] suffers from poor style reflection and color deficiency, while StyleKeeper (with ControlNet) effectively transfers detailed style without leaking content-image colors. Overall, StyleKeeper is the only method that simultaneously achieves top style similarity, strong text alignment, and near-zero content leakage across all tested styles and prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the swapping self-attention mechanism introduced by StyleAligned [16] and CrossAttn [1], which inject key-value features from a reference denoising process into the original process to transfer style. The key departures are: (1) restricting swapping to late upblocks only (empirically determined), (2) wrapping the process in classifier-free guidance [17] for improved image quality and text alignment, and (3) introducing Negative Visual Query Guidance (NVQG), which repurposes the concept-negation framework from compositional diffusion [24] to actively suppress content leakage by negating a query-swapped score. For real-image references, stochastic encoding replaces the DDIM inversion used by prior methods [6, 16], and AdaIN-based color calibration [43] aligns output color statistics to the reference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Content leakage is a persistent and practically limiting problem in visual style prompting: it reduces diversity, breaks text alignment, and makes the output look like a copy of the reference rather than a new image in the reference's style. StyleKeeper's training-free approach is immediately deployable on existing pretrained diffusion models (SDXL, etc.) without fine-tuning, and its compatibility with ControlNet broadens applicability to I2I settings. However, the method remains bounded by the pretrained model's generative capacity (cannot produce out-of-distribution concepts), and when visual and textual styles conflict, the visual style dominates—a limitation that suggests the content-style disentanglement is still imperfect. Extending the approach to video generation and resolving style-content conflicts are natural open directions.
