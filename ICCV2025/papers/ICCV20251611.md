## Problem

Adapting pre-trained image models to video for few-shot action recognition is hampered by two issues: full fine-tuning overfits with limited data, and existing adapter-based methods either duplicate entire attention modules (incurring heavy computational overhead) or jointly learn spatio-temporal features via 3D convolution, which is inferior in low-data regimes and suffers from limited local receptive fields. The paper seeks a lightweight adapter that disentangles spatial and temporal feature adaptation while maintaining global receptive fields.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251611/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 8 uniformly sampled frames (T=8) from each video clip, processed through a frozen ResNet-50 or CLIP-ViT-B/16 backbone with one D²ST-Adapter inserted per stage (4 or 12 stages respectively). Classification follows an episodic N-way K-shot protocol where a support set of K labeled samples per class guides the assignment of unlabeled query clips to action classes via metric-based matching (Bi-MHM, OTAM, or TRX). In the multimodal variant, text descriptions of actions are additionally incorporated through a CLIP-style text encoder.

## Method

D²ST-Adapter employs a dual-pathway bottleneck architecture: input features are downsampled, then processed concurrently by a spatial pathway (aDSTA-S) and a temporal pathway (aDSTA-T), whose outputs are fused by element-wise addition and upsampled. The pivotal operation is anisotropic Deformable Spatio-Temporal Attention (aDSTA), which extends deformable attention from 2D to 3D spatio-temporal space by sampling reference points with anisotropic densities—denser along the temporal axis for the temporal pathway (n_t > n_s) and denser along spatial axes for the spatial pathway (n_s > n_t)—then learning 3D offsets via a small convolutional network and performing sparse self-attention over the shifted points. Dynamic Position Embedding (a 3D depth-wise convolution) injects semantic-conditioned positional information before attention. Only the adapter parameters (~7.3M for ViT) are trained end-to-end with cross-entropy loss while the backbone remains frozen, yielding a parameter-efficient framework that outperforms both full fine-tuning and prior adapter methods, particularly on temporally challenging benchmarks like SSv2.

## Results

On SSv2-Full (5-way 1-shot), D²ST-Adapter with CLIP-ViT-B/16 achieves 66.7% accuracy, outperforming the strongest adapter baselines AIM (63.7%) and DUALPATH (64.5%) by 3.0 and 2.2 points respectively. With INet-RN50, it reaches 57.0% on SSv2-Full 1-shot, surpassing the full fine-tuning method MoLo (56.6%) and the prior PEFT method ST-Adapter (52.2%) by 0.4 and 4.8 points. The multimodal extension D²ST-Adapter-MM achieves 74.1% on SSv2-Full 1-shot, beating the best prior multimodal method Task-Adapter (71.3%) by 2.8 points. On spatially-dominant benchmarks (Kinetics, HMDB51, UCF101), gains over adapter baselines are smaller (0.5–3.0 points) but remain consistent across all five datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

With CLIP-ViT-B/16, D²ST-Adapter outperforms all three prior adapter-based methods (AIM, DUALPATH, ST-Adapter) on every dataset, with the largest margins on SSv2-Full (66.7 vs. 64.5 for DUALPATH, +2.2 pts) and SSv2-Small (55.0 vs. 53.5 for DUALPATH, +1.5 pts), confirming the benefit of disentangled spatio-temporal encoding. With INet-RN50, the gap over ST-Adapter widens to +4.8 pts on SSv2-Full 1-shot (57.0 vs. 52.2) and +5.2 pts on SSv2-Small 1-shot (45.8 vs. 41.9), while also surpassing the strongest full fine-tuning method MoLo (56.6) by 0.4 pts. On spatially-dominant benchmarks (Kinetics, HMDB51, UCF101), improvements over adapter baselines are more modest (+0.5 to +3.0 pts), consistent with the paper's observation that disentangled temporal encoding yields limited gain when actions are recognizable from spatial cues alone. In the multimodal setting, D²ST-Adapter-MM beats Task-Adapter by +2.8 pts on SSv2-Full 1-shot (74.1 vs. 71.3) and +3.4 pts on SSv2-Small 1-shot (63.6 vs. 60.2), while also leading on Kinetics (96.2 vs. 95.0) and UCF101 (98.3 vs. 98.0). The method uses only 7.3M tunable parameters versus 14.3M for AIM/DUALPATH/ST-Adapter, achieving the best accuracy with roughly half the adapter parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adapter-tuning paradigm for image-to-video transfer (ST-Adapter, NeurIPS 2022) by replacing its joint 3D-convolutional design with a dual-pathway architecture that disentangles spatial and temporal feature adaptation. The core mechanism, anisotropic Deformable Spatio-Temporal Attention (aDSTA), adapts deformable attention from 2D image space (Deformable DETR) to 3D spatio-temporal space, borrowing Dynamic Position Embedding from Uniformer. Unlike AIM and DUALPATH, which duplicate entire multi-head self-attention modules to handle spatial and temporal features separately, D²ST-Adapter achieves disentangled encoding within a single-stream backbone using only one lightweight adapter per block, reducing tunable parameters from 14.3M to 7.3M.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that disentangled spatial-temporal encoding via anisotropic deformable attention is a principled and efficient way to adapt frozen image backbones to video, particularly in few-shot regimes where overfitting is a major concern. The consistent gains on temporal-heavy benchmarks (SSv2) while maintaining competitiveness on spatial-heavy ones (Kinetics, UCF101) validate the design choice. However, the higher inference latency (18.2ms vs. 11.6–14.1ms for competitors) despite fewer parameters suggests the deformable attention mechanism carries a computational cost that may limit real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the anisotropic sampling strategy generalizes to longer video sequences or other video understanding tasks beyond action recognition.
