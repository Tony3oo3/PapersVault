## Problem

Existing virtual try-on methods are either supervised in-shop approaches that achieve high fidelity but fail to generalize across domains, or unsupervised in-the-wild methods that improve adaptability but remain constrained by data distribution biases. No unified, training-free framework exists that simultaneously preserves fine-grained garment texture and ensures pose consistency across all try-on scenarios (Shop-to-Model, Shop-to-Street, Model-to-Model, etc.) without requiring dedicated training for each setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a garment image (either a standalone product photo or a person wearing the garment) and a target person image, producing a single try-on result at 512×384 (in-shop) or 512×320 (in-the-wild) resolution. It requires no paired training data and operates entirely through off-the-shelf diffusion models, OpenPose skeleton estimation, and TAPPS part segmentation. Notably, it extends to multi-human try-on by concatenating multiple garments spatially to generate pseudo-person images simultaneously for different individuals in one scene.

## Method

OmniVTON is a training-free pipeline built on three mechanisms. Structured Garment Morphing (SGM) first generates a pseudo-person image via cross-attention modulation between the garment and the target person's latent features, then establishes multi-part semantic correspondence using OpenPose keypoints and TAPPS segmentation to define region-specific bounding boxes, and finally applies piecewise homography transformations (optimized via Levenberg-Marquardt) to deform the garment into an anatomically aligned prior. Spectral Pose Injection (SPI) addresses pose alignment by applying DDIM inversion to the target person, decomposing the resulting latent noise via FFT, retaining low-frequency structural components while replacing high-frequency texture residuals with random noise through a Gaussian low-pass mask, thereby decoupling pose from garment texture. Continuous Boundary Stitching (CBS) refines the inpainting stage through bidirectional self-attention modulation between the original garment image and the garment-infused image, suppressing background interference and smoothing the discontinuities introduced by multi-region stitching.

## Results

On VITON-HD, OmniVTON achieves FID_u=9.621, FID_p=7.758, SSIM_p=0.832, and LPIPS_p=0.145, outperforming the strongest baseline IDM-VTON (FID_u=23.035, SSIM_p=0.812) by 58% in FID_u and 0.020 in SSIM. On DressCode (multi-category), it further reduces FID_u to 6.450 and LPIPS_p to 0.119, surpassing IDM-VTON by 33.4% in FID_u. On the StreetTryOn in-the-wild benchmark, OmniVTON achieves the lowest FID across all four cross-scenario settings (Shop-to-Street: 33.9, Model-to-Model: 9.0, Model-to-Street: 33.5, Street-to-Street: 23.5), beating the in-domain-trained StreetTryOn by up to 10.6 FID points on Street-to-Street.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniVTON outperforms all 11 baselines on every metric across all three datasets. On VITON-HD, the largest gap is against IDM-VTON (the strongest VTON baseline), where OmniVTON reduces FID_u from 23.035 to 9.621 (−58%) while improving SSIM_p from 0.812 to 0.832; AnyDoor, the best exemplar-based editor, trails by 5.2 FID_u and 0.036 SSIM. On DressCode, OmniVTON extends its lead, achieving FID_u=6.450 vs. IDM-VTON's 9.685 and LPIPS_p=0.119 vs. 0.138, confirming cross-category generalization. On the StreetTryOn in-the-wild benchmark, OmniVTON beats the in-domain-trained StreetTryOn on all four scenarios (e.g., Model-to-Model: 8.98 vs. 12.19 FID; Street-to-Street: 23.47 vs. 33.04 FID), and substantially outperforms scenario-specific methods like PWS and PastaGAN++ in cross-domain settings where they degrade severely (e.g., PWS reaches 84.99 FID on Street-to-Street vs. OmniVTON's 23.47). TIGIC and Cross-Image, which lack task-specific designs, produce the worst results (FID_u > 60 on VITON-HD), highlighting the necessity of structured garment priors for realistic try-on synthesis.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniVTON departs from the dominant paradigm of training scenario-specific diffusion models (IDM-VTON, StableVITON, D4-VTON, CAT-DM) by repurposing an off-the-shelf diffusion model through a training-free pipeline that combines skeletal-guided piecewise homography (replacing learned optical-flow warping), FFT-based spectral decomposition of DDIM inversion noise (separating pose from texture in latent space), and cross-image attention modulation (borrowed from exemplar-based inpainting methods like AnyDoor and PBE). It is most directly comparable to StreetTryOn, the only prior method targeting cross-domain in-the-wild try-on, but differs fundamentally in being training-free and in using structured garment priors rather than dense garment DensePose warping, which StreetTryOn relies on and which fails for lower-body garments and dresses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for scenario-specific training, OmniVTON addresses a practical bottleneck in VTON deployment: constructing large paired datasets across every garment category, pose, and domain is infeasible, and retraining for each new scenario is costly. The multi-human extension opens group-centric applications (family outfits, uniform design) that no prior method supports. However, the authors acknowledge failure modes in high-density crowds and minimal target body regions, and the reliance on OpenPose and TAPPS for skeletal/parsing cues means the pipeline inherits their error rates. A key open question is whether the spectral pose-injection principle generalizes beyond the specific diffusion architecture used, and whether the multi-region homography approach scales gracefully to garments with highly complex topology (e.g., multi-layered outfits).
