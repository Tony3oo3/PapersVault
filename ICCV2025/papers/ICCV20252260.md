## Problem

Existing dataset distillation methods that leverage diffusion models distill information solely from image features, neglecting the semantic context inherent in the data. This omission causes generated surrogate images to lack logical coherence (e.g., animals with incorrect limb counts), omit target objects entirely, and exhibit co-occurrence bias where frequently paired features dominate over accurate individual representation. No prior work has integrated language information into visual dataset distillation for classification tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes real images with class labels from standard benchmarks (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, ImageNet subsets at 256×256 or 224×224) and auto-generates descriptive text via LLaVA since no existing DD benchmark provides textual annotations. It produces a compact surrogate dataset of IPC synthetic images per class, synthesized at the target resolution by a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion V1-5 model. The number of clusters per class is set equal to IPC, so the output dataset size is directly controlled by this single hyperparameter.

## Method

The framework constructs paired image and text prototypes per cluster to jointly condition a latent diffusion model. Image prototypes are obtained by K-means clustering on VAE-encoded latent features (after LOF-based outlier removal), with cluster count matching IPC. For text prototypes, LLaVA-generated descriptions are tokenized, nonrepresentative words (appearing in >β fraction of the class) are filtered out, and the top-k most frequent remaining words per cluster serve as feature keywords; the sentence with the highest cumulative keyword-match score is selected as the cluster's text prototype. A Stable Diffusion model is first fine-tuned on the full image-text pairs, then at synthesis time each cluster's noised image prototype is concatenated with the CLIP-encoded text prototype to condition the U-Net denoising process, and the VAE decoder produces the final image. This cross-modal conditioning ensures generated images contain the target object with cluster-specific semantic details (posture, color, background) rather than generic class-level features.

## Results

On ImageNette (ResNetAP-10), the proposed method achieves 64.8%, 71.4%, and 81.2% top-1 accuracy at IPC 10, 20, and 50 respectively, outperforming the best baseline (D4M) by 3.9%, 4.9%, and 3.5%. On ImageIDC, it reaches 57.0%, 63.3%, and 71.9% under the same IPC settings, surpassing the strongest competitor by 2.9%, 4.2%, and 2.5%. On ImageWoof with ResNet-18, the method achieves 39.9% at IPC 10 and 68.3% at IPC 100, consistently leading all baselines across ConvNet-6, ResNetAP-10, and ResNet-18 architectures. On CIFAR-100 at IPC 10, it gains 8.0% over RDED (50.6% vs. 42.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all ten baselines across every dataset and IPC setting tested. Among generative DD methods, D4M (Su et al., CVPR 2024) is the closest competitor on ImageNette (60.9% vs. 64.8% at IPC 10), while Minimax (Gu et al., CVPR 2024) leads on ImageIDC at IPC 50 (69.4% vs. 71.9%). On ImageWoof, the method surpasses D4M by up to 4.5% (68.3% vs. 63.8% at IPC 100, ResNet-18) and Minimax by up to 4.6% (60.5% vs. 55.9% at IPC 50, ResNet-18). Traditional selection-based baselines (K-Center, Herding) and early matching-based methods (DM, IDC-1) are substantially outperformed, with gaps exceeding 10–20% at low IPC. On large-scale ImageNet-1K, the method beats RDED (Sun et al., CVPR 2024) by 4.7% at IPC 10 (46.7% vs. 42.0%) and 1.9% at IPC 50 (60.5% vs. 58.6%), and outperforms SRe2L (Yin et al., NeurIPS 2023) by 25.4% at IPC 10. GLaD (Cazenavette et al., CVPR 2023) and DiT (Peebles et al., CVPR 2023) are consistently behind by 3–8% across settings, confirming that integrating text prototypes into the diffusion pipeline yields a clear and consistent advantage over image-only distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-model-based dataset distillation line initiated by Minimax (Gu et al., CVPR 2024) and D4M (Su et al., CVPR 2024), which synthesize surrogate images by conditioning a latent diffusion model on K-means cluster centers of VAE-encoded features. The key departure is the introduction of text prototypes: descriptive sentences generated by LLaVA and selected per-cluster via a word-frequency scoring scheme, which are fed through a CLIP text encoder to jointly condition the U-Net alongside the image prototype. This cross-modal conditioning is the first instance of integrating language information into visual dataset distillation for classification, moving beyond the purely image-feature-based prototypes used in all prior generative DD methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a concrete failure mode of image-only DD—illogical generations (extra limbs, missing target objects, co-occurrence bias)—by grounding synthesis in semantic descriptions, and demonstrates that this fix yields consistent 3–5% accuracy gains across ImageNet subsets and CIFAR benchmarks. The reliance on an open-source VLM to auto-generate descriptions makes the pipeline applicable to any image dataset without manual annotations, broadening the practical reach of DD. However, the method is currently limited to classification, the text-prototype selection is a heuristic (top-k word frequency) that may not capture richer linguistic structure, and the quality of generated descriptions is bounded by the VLM's own biases. Extending the framework to detection, segmentation, or other dense prediction tasks, and exploring learned (rather than frequency-based) text prototype selection, are natural next steps.
