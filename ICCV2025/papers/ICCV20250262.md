## Problem

Existing image protection methods (AdvDM, Photoguard, Glaze) disrupt diffusion-based editing by attacking the image encoder or a single diffusion loss, but their objectives are misaligned with the iterative DDIM inversion trajectory that editing methods actually depend on. No prior work directly targets the deterministic inversion process itself, leaving inversion-based editing methods largely unprotected.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250262/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and produces a pixel-space adversarial perturbation (L-infinity norm ≤ 0.05) optimized via 20 PGD iterations. The perturbed image is then encoded into the latent space of Stable Diffusion v1.4, where the DDIM inversion trajectory is computed over 10 steps. The output is a visually near-identical image that, when subjected to any DDIM inversion-based editing pipeline, yields a corrupted latent code that prevents faithful reconstruction or editing.

## Method

DIA reformulates the DDIM inversion to decompose the inverted latent x_T into a bias term (decayed x_0) and a Process Trajectory (PT) capturing accumulated model predictions. Two attack objectives are defined: DIA-PT maximizes the L2 distance between the inversion trajectory and the VAE-encoded image, while DIA-R maximizes the reconstruction error after a full deterministic forward-to-x_T and reverse-to-x_0 DDIM cycle. To make the multi-step trajectory differentiable without prohibitive memory cost, the method applies FlowGrad's decomposed backpropagation, computing timestep-wise Vector-Jacobian Products instead of the full Jacobian. This trajectory-level targeting, as opposed to single-timestep or encoder-level attacks, yields stronger and more transferable disruption across diverse inversion-editing combinations (DDIM, Null-text, Negative-Prompt, Direct Inversion paired with P2P, MasaCtrl, Proximal-Guidance).

## Results

On PIE-Bench (700 images, 9 inversion-edit combinations), DIA-PT and DIA-R achieve the lowest CLIP similarity (strongest disruption) across most pairings. For example, on Negative-Prompt-to-Proximal-Guidance, DIA-R drops CLIP similarity to 14.87 vs. 25.21 for natural editing and 18.80 for AdvDM. On DDIM-to-MasaCtrl, DIA-PT achieves 18.31 vs. 24.95 (natural) and 22.32 (AdvDM). In background preservation (Table 2), DIA-R shows the largest structural distance (0.1252) and lowest PSNR (16.31 dB), confirming strong disruption while maintaining visual stealthiness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIA-PT and DIA-R outperform all five baselines on 7 out of 9 inversion-edit combinations, with the largest margins on MasaCtrl (up to 6.6 CLIP points below AdvDM) and Proximal-Guidance (up to 10.3 points below natural editing). AdvDM and SDS, which attack the diffusion process without considering the full trajectory, show inconsistent disruption—effective on some pairings (e.g., AdvDM on NegPrompt→P2P at 18.99) but nearly ineffective on others (e.g., AdvDM on DDIM→P2P at 26.15, worse than natural editing). PID is only competitive on DDIM-to-DDIM (21.21) and underperforms DIA on all other combinations. Photoguard and Glaze, which target the VAE encoder, show the weakest overall disruption, with CLIP scores often within 1–2 points of natural editing. The two DIA variants complement each other: DIA-PT excels on MasaCtrl and Proximal-Guidance, while DIA-R achieves the lowest scores on PnP and Negative-Prompt pairings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the image-protection-against-diffusion-models line of work (AdvDM, Photoguard, Glaze, PID, SDS) by identifying a critical misalignment: prior methods attack either the VAE encoder or a single/partial diffusion step, whereas inversion-based editing methods (Null-text Inversion, PnP, P2P, MasaCtrl) depend on the full deterministic DDIM trajectory. DIA departs from this by formulating the adversarial objective directly on the chained inversion trajectory, leveraging FlowGrad's Vector-Jacobian Product decomposition to make the full trajectory differentiable within practical VRAM budgets. The two variants (DIA-PT targeting the process trajectory, DIA-R targeting round-trip reconstruction) are natural reformulations of the DDIM inversion equation rather than new architectural ideas.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DIA is the first method specifically designed to disrupt the dominant inversion-based editing paradigm, filling a gap that prior encoder- or partial-step-attacking methods left open. Practically, it offers a deployable defense against unauthorized deepfake editing, NSFW/CSEM generation, and evidence manipulation before images are shared online. Key limitations acknowledged by the authors include cross-model transferability (the attack is tuned to a specific Stable Diffusion version) and vulnerability to purification (JPEG, crop/resize, AdverseCleaner), which remain open challenges for all adversarial image-protection methods. Whether trajectory-based objectives generalize to non-DDIM samplers (e.g., DPM-Solver, flow-matching) is an open question.
