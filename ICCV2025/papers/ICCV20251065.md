## Problem

Existing motion restoration benchmarks (GoPro, Vimeo90K, KITTI, Sintel) are constrained by fixed frame rates, limited motion variability, and an inability to capture the interplay between motion blur and defocus blur under real-world conditions. No prior dataset offers explicit, continuous control over motion amplitude from minimal to extreme, nor does it simultaneously support deblurring, video frame interpolation, and optical flow estimation within a single unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251065/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For deblurring, a single blurry image (1920×1080, generated by averaging 3–35 frames in MIORe or 1–249 frames in VAR-MIORe) is the input, with the sharp middle frame as ground truth. For VFI and optical flow, the left-most and right-most sharp frames bracketing the blur are the inputs, producing an intermediate frame or a flow map respectively. Unusually, all input frames are guaranteed completely sharp—no blur leaks into reference frames—forcing algorithms to rely solely on subtle spatial cues for motion estimation.

## Method

The core contribution is an adaptive frame-averaging construction pipeline built on 1000 FPS acquisition (CHRONOS 2.1-HD, FHD). For each sequence, mean and maximum optical flow are computed to determine the optimal number of consecutive frames to average: scenes with high per-frame flow (~10 px) are averaged over as few as 3 frames, while sub-pixel motion scenes are averaged over up to 35 frames (MIORe) or 249 frames (VAR-MIORe), targeting a consistent ~30-pixel blur magnitude in MIORe or a continuous 1–249 ms exposure sweep in VAR-MIORe. Four professional lenses (Tamron 15-30mm, Canon 24mm, Sigma 85mm, Laowa 100mm) are used at wide-open apertures to introduce diverse optical degradations (vignetting, chromatic aberration, defocus bokeh). The resulting 333 sequences (1,147,507 raw frames) yield 52,218 MIORe and 83,250 VAR-MIORe samples, each with a sharp middle frame for deblurring/VFI ground truth and a pseudo optical-flow map for OF evaluation.

## Results

On MIORe deblurring, UFPNet achieves the highest overall PSNR of 31.78 dB, narrowly ahead of AdaRevD (31.70 dB) and LoFormer (31.61 dB), while FFTformer (27.48 dB) and NAFNet (28.56 dB) lag by 3–4 dB. On VAR-MIORe, all five deblurring methods degrade monotonically as the number of averaged frames increases from 1 to 249, with PSNR dropping from ~35–41 dB (near-sharp) to ~20–21 dB (extreme blur), where they approach the no-restore baseline (21.52 dB). For VFI on MIORe, VFIMamba leads with 34.41 dB PSNR, followed closely by EMA-VFI (34.35 dB) and SGM-VFI (34.24 dB), while PerVFI trails at 30.88 dB. For optical flow on MIORe, MemFlow achieves the lowest EPE (2.99 px), substantially outperforming FlowDiffuser (6.01 px), Ef-RAFT (7.52 px), FlowFormer (14.65 px), and VideoFlow (19.38 px).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the five deblurring baselines on MIORe, UFPNet [8] edges out AdaRevD [17] and LoFormer [18] by 0.08 and 0.17 dB respectively in total PSNR, while FFTformer [14] and NAFNet [4] trail by over 3 dB; notably, FFTformer suffers a pronounced drop in the Mild-Medium split (26.29 dB) due to its frequency-domain prior overfitting to fast motion, and NAFNet is weakest on Extreme (26.73 dB). On VAR-MIORe at the extreme end (249 averaged frames), LoFormer [18] (21.42 dB) and AdaRevD [17] (21.39 dB) marginally outperform UFPNet [8] (21.15 dB) and FFTformer [14] (20.91 dB), with NAFNet [4] last at 20.03 dB—yet all remain within ~1 dB of the no-restore baseline (21.52 dB), indicating near-total failure at extreme blur. For VFI, VFIMamba [32] (34.41 dB) and EMA-VFI [31] (34.35 dB) are nearly tied on MIORe, both beating SGM-VFI [15] (34.24 dB), BiFormer [34] (32.60 dB), and PerVFI [28] (30.88 dB); on VAR-MIORe at 249 frames, all five VFI methods converge to 18.6–18.9 dB, barely above the simple average baseline (18.22 dB). For optical flow, MemFlow [5] dominates with 2.99 px EPE, roughly 2× better than FlowDiffuser [16] (6.01 px) and Ef-RAFT [7] (7.52 px), while FlowFormer [11] (14.65 px) and VideoFlow [23] (19.38 px) are substantially worse; FlowFormer exhibits an anomalous 33.81 px EPE on the Extreme split, suggesting complete breakdown under high motion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in motion restoration benchmarking by combining 1000 FPS high-speed acquisition (CHRONOS 2.1-HD) with an adaptive frame-averaging strategy driven by computed optical flow metrics, departing from the fixed-frame-averaging paradigm of GoPro and the motion-filtered curation of Vimeo90K. Rather than building on a specific algorithmic lineage, it repurposes the frame-averaging concept into a controllable, flow-regularized blur generator and introduces variable motion amplitude as an explicit benchmark axis for the first time. The multi-task design (deblurring + VFI + OF from a single acquisition) unifies what prior datasets addressed in isolation, while the inclusion of defocus blur as an intrinsic component and professional-lens optical degradations (vignetting, chromatic aberration) extends beyond the consumer-grade or vehicle-centric setups of GoPro, KITTI, and RealBlur.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VAR-MIORe's explicit control over motion magnitude (1–249 ms exposure offsets, Max OF up to 1932) provides the first systematic way to probe the 'breaking point' of restoration algorithms across a continuous motion spectrum, a dimension no prior benchmark offers. The finding that VFI methods perform well under extreme motion yet deteriorate on moderate motion, and that frequency-based deblurring (FFTformer) hallucinates in slow scenes, highlights a mismatch between learned priors and actual blur characteristics that motivates more principled modeling. Limitations include the relatively small sequence count (333), FHD-only resolution, and explicitly preliminary optical flow ground truth (pseudo-labels), leaving open whether conclusions generalize to 4K content or to refined OF supervision. The dataset's multi-task structure also raises the question of whether joint training across deblurring, VFI, and OF yields meaningful shared representations.
