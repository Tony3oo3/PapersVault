## Problem

Affordance learning for robotic manipulation is hindered by three interconnected gaps: the absence of large-scale datasets with precise part-level segmentation (existing work produces only coarse Gaussian heatmaps focused solely on graspable regions, ignoring functional parts), poor cross-domain generalization due to limited training diversity, and a near-total lack of real-world robot deployment. This paper tackles all three simultaneously by building an end-to-end system from automated data collection through model learning to physical robot execution.

## Input / Output

The GAT model takes a cropped RGB object image (often under 100 pixels in one dimension, extracted from egocentric video frames) and a pseudo depth map generated by Depth-Anything, producing a pixel-wise affordance segmentation mask over L categories via cosine similarity between upsampled features and learnable (or CLIP text) embeddings. In the Aff-Grasp deployment pipeline, the system additionally receives a natural-language task instruction (verb + target, e.g., 'cut cake'), which drives open-vocabulary object detection (GroundingDINO) and affordance-specific motion primitive selection, ultimately outputting a 6-DoF grasp pose from Contact-GraspNet constrained to the predicted graspable region.

## Method

The core model, GAT, builds on a frozen DINOv2 backbone fine-tuned via LoRA (low-rank adaptation matrices injected into attention weights) to handle domain shift while preventing overfitting. A novel Depth Feature Injector (DFI) module injects geometric priors by performing cross-attention at the start of each of four transformer blocks, where image features serve as queries and pseudo-depth features serve as keys and values, with a learnable gate β initialized to zero to stabilize training; DFI can be discarded at inference for a 37.6% speedup while retaining most gains. The classifier avoids an explicit background head (which overfits under domain gap) and instead uses cosine similarity against learnable or CLIP text embeddings, with background assigned implicitly via a threshold. Training data are collected automatically from egocentric videos (Epic-Kitchens, Ego4D) by localizing graspable contact points from hand-object interactions and functional points from tool-object interactions, projecting both to occlusion-free pre-contact frames via homography or foundation-model point correspondence, and prompting SAM to generate precise binary masks. The full Aff-Grasp framework couples GAT's segmentation with Contact-GraspNet for dense grasp proposal generation within the predicted mask and affordance-specific sequential motion primitives for tool-object interaction and robot-to-human handover.

## Results

On the proposed Affordance Evaluation Dataset (AED), GAT achieves 68.62 mIoU, 81.09 F1, and 83.51 accuracy, surpassing the previous best (OOAL) by 13.8% in mIoU. In real-world robot experiments across 72 trials, Aff-Grasp achieves 97.2% correct affordance prediction, 80.6% successful grasping, and 65.3% successful tool-object interaction. For generalization on unseen objects (35 trials), Aff-Grasp reaches 91.4% correct affordance and 80.0% successful grasp with an inference time of only 6.3 ms, compared to 12.92 s for Robo-ABC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAT outperforms all nine baselines across both vision and robot evaluations. Among ImageNet-pretrained segmentation models, GAT's 68.62 mIoU exceeds SegFormer (23.72), PSPNet (16.90), and DeepLabV3+ (13.46) by 44.9, 51.7, and 55.2 points respectively, confirming the large domain gap between egocentric training data and diverse evaluation images. Among foundation-model-based methods, GAT surpasses OOAL (54.82 mIoU) by 13.8 points, ViT-Adapter (50.86) by 17.8 points, and raw DINOv2 (46.16) by 22.5 points, with ZegCLIP (18.33) performing poorly due to its CLIP-based design. In robot experiments, Aff-Grasp achieves 97.2% correct affordance prediction versus 86.1% for Robo-ABC and 58.3% for LOCATE, and 80.6% successful grasping versus 61.1% and 45.8% respectively. Notably, Aff-Grasp is the only method that can also perform tool-object interaction (65.3% success), as LOCATE's overlapping predictions and Robo-ABC's inability to infer functional areas make them inapplicable to that task. Aff-Grasp also runs 2000× faster than Robo-ABC at inference (6.3 ms vs. 12.92 s) while being more accurate on unseen objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of affordance learning from egocentric human videos (VRB, Robo-ABC, LOCATE) by shifting from coarse Gaussian heatmaps of graspable regions to precise segmentation masks that jointly capture both graspable and functional affordances. Architecturally, it builds on the ViT-Adapter cross-attention design [5] for the Depth Feature Injector and on DINOv2 with LoRA fine-tuning for domain-robust feature extraction, while leveraging SAM and GroundedSAM for automated data annotation. The key conceptual departure is treating geometric (pseudo-depth) information as a first-class signal for part-level segmentation rather than relying solely on appearance, and coupling the segmentation model directly to a grasp-generation pipeline (Contact-GraspNet) for closed-loop manipulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the long-standing data-scarcity bottleneck in affordance research by demonstrating that precise, dual-affordance (graspable + functional) annotations can be extracted automatically from existing egocentric video datasets, removing the need for expensive manual labeling. The 13.8% mIoU gain and 77.1% real-robot grasping success rate suggest that geometric priors and foundation-model backbones are critical for fine-grained part segmentation under domain shift. Open questions include whether the approach scales to open-vocabulary affordance sets beyond the 8 classes evaluated, whether the DFI's depth-based regularization transfers to 3D point-cloud or multi-view settings, and whether the pipeline generalizes to dexterous or bimanual manipulation tasks.
