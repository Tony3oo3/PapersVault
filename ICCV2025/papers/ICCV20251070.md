## Problem

While Image Quality Assessment (IQA) measures perceived quality at a fixed resolution, the systematic relationship between an image's spatial scale and its perceived quality has remained unquantified. Downscaling can reduce visible degradations (noise, blur) but also loses high-frequency details, creating a non-trivial trade-off that no existing metric captures. This paper introduces the Image Intrinsic Scale (IIS) — the largest scale at which an image achieves its highest perceived quality — and the corresponding IISA task to subjectively measure and automatically predict this value.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image at its original resolution (all images in IISA-DB exceed 2048 pixels in width) and outputs a scalar intrinsic scale value in the range [0.05, 1], where 1 means the image is best at full resolution and 0.05 is the lower evaluation bound. The scale is inherently non-linear (equal slider distances correspond to equal ratios, not equal absolute differences), so ground-truth labels are aggregated via geometric mean (MOIS) rather than arithmetic mean. The predictive task is strictly no-reference: a single image is fed to the model without any reference or multi-scale input.

## Method

The core technical contribution is WIISA (Weak-labeling for Image Intrinsic Scale Assessment), a model-agnostic data augmentation strategy that generates multiple weakly labeled image-IIS pairs from a single ground-truth pair. Given an image I with ground-truth IIS Ω(I), WIISA randomly samples n_wl scales in [max(Ω(I), δ), 1] (δ=0.65), downscales I via Lanczos interpolation, and computes the weak IIS label for each downscaled version using a piecewise function: the label is 1 if the scale is at or below Ω(I), and Ω(I)/s otherwise. This leverages the assumption that quality is concave-down in scale, so the IIS of a downscaled version can be analytically extrapolated. WIISA is applied online during training, augmenting each batch of size B with B·n_wl additional weakly labeled samples, and is independent of the specific model architecture or training strategy. The authors validate this approach across six diverse NR-IQA backbones (DBCNN, TOPIQ, CONTRIQUE, ARNIQA, CLIP-IQA+, QualiCLIP+), showing consistent SRCC gains of up to 5% over ground-truth-only training.

## Results

On the IISA-DB dataset (785 image-IIS pairs, 70/10/20 split, 10-fold cross-validation), WIISA consistently improves all six NR-IQA baselines, with relative SRCC gains up to 5%. The best overall result is TOPIQ + WIISA, achieving SRCC 0.808, PLCC 0.805, RMSE 0.088, and MAE 0.069. Zero-shot transfer of pre-trained NR-IQA models (TOPIQ trained on SPAQ or UHD-IQA) performs poorly on IISA (SRCC 0.475–0.587), confirming the need for IISA-specific training. The assumption that quality follows a concave-down function of scale is validated on KonX, where 90% of image triplets (378/420) show consistent MOS trends across resolutions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WIISA improves every baseline without exception, confirming its model-agnostic utility. TOPIQ (Chen et al., IEEE TIP 2024) benefits the most, jumping from SRCC 0.764 to 0.808 (+5.8% relative), and is the strongest method overall. DBCNN (Zhang et al., IEEE TCSVT 2018) is the second-best, reaching SRCC 0.776 with WIISA. Among self-supervised methods, ARNIQA (Agnolucci et al., WACV 2024) outperforms CONTRIQUE (Madhusudana et al., IEEE TIP 2022) by 5.6 SRCC points (0.687 vs. 0.631 with WIISA), while both remain well behind supervised approaches. The VLM-based methods CLIP-IQA+ (Wang et al., AAAI 2023) and QualiCLIP+ (Agnolucci et al., arXiv 2024) show the smallest absolute gains from WIISA (SRCC 0.666 and 0.670 respectively) and trail the top methods by over 13 SRCC points, suggesting that prompt-learning VLMs are less suited to the fine-grained scale-prediction task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by defining the Image Intrinsic Scale (IIS) and the IISA task, which had no prior formal formulation. It draws conceptual inspiration from viewing-distance studies showing concave-down quality curves [8, 11, 24] and the KonX dataset's observation that quality varies with resolution [33], but departs from camera-level metrics (P-MP, MTF) by conditioning on individual images rather than idealized laboratory conditions. The WIISA weak-labeling strategy repurposes the mathematical structure of the IIS definition (a piecewise function relating scale to quality) to generate training data, a technique not previously applied in IQA. Experimentally, it repositions existing NR-IQA architectures (TOPIQ, DBCNN, CONTRIQUE, ARNIQA, CLIP-IQA+, QualiCLIP+) for a fundamentally different prediction target, demonstrating that zero-shot transfer fails and task-specific training is necessary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IISA-DB is the first dataset for scale-dependent quality assessment, and the IIS concept offers a direct, actionable prescription for maximizing perceived quality (downscale to IIS) that traditional NR-IQA cannot provide. The WIISA technique is model-agnostic and could be adopted by future IISA or cross-resolution IQA methods, though its gains are modest (≤5% relative SRCC) and the dataset is small (785 images), limiting statistical confidence. Open questions include whether the concave-down quality-scale assumption (verified at 90% on KonX) holds universally, whether IISA generalizes to video or multi-image sequences, and whether larger-scale annotation studies would shift the optimal IIS estimates. The paper also raises the practical question of how IISA interacts with display-specific factors (PPI, viewing distance) that are held constant in the current formulation.
