## Problem

Few-step diffusion models (1–8 NFE) enable fast text-guided image editing, but standard DDIM inversion becomes inaccurate at low step counts and existing few-step editing methods (e.g., DDPM-noise inversion) lack sufficient editability to reliably follow target prompts. The paper addresses both the inversion accuracy gap and the editability–consistency tradeoff in the few-step regime.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251778/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a real RGB image, a source text prompt (optionally auto-generated via BLIP captioning), a target text prompt describing the desired edit, and Canny edges extracted from the input image. It outputs a single edited image in as few as 8 NFE (4 sampling steps) using a PeRFlow backbone distilled from Stable Diffusion 1.5, with the ControlNet conditioning scale and DPG scale serving as user-tunable knobs for the consistency–editability tradeoff.

## Method

InstantEdit builds on PeRFlow's piecewise-linear flow trajectories to perform accurate inversion with few steps via PerRFI, which simply reverses the velocity-field ODE step-by-step. During regeneration, Inversion Latent Injection (ILI) stores all intermediate inverted latents and anchors each denoising step back to the corresponding stored latent, preventing error accumulation that plagues naive few-step regeneration. Disentangled Prompt Guidance (DPG) replaces TurboEdit's Pseudo-Guidance by scaling only the component of the target-prompt velocity field that is orthogonal to the source-prompt velocity field, filtering out inaccurate guidance from the source prompt; an optional attention-masking mechanism further restricts guidance to regions relevant to the editing word. A Canny-conditioned ControlNet is plugged in as a structural prior during both inversion and regeneration to suppress unwanted layout changes with minimal overhead.

## Results

On the PIE Bench few-step setting (4 sampling steps), InstantEdit achieves the best consistency metrics among all few-step methods: PSNR 27.96, LPIPS 44.39×10⁻³, MSE 34.94×10⁻⁴, and SSIM 86.44×10⁻², while also attaining the highest prompt-image alignment scores (Whole: 26.28, Edited: 22.82). In the multi-step setting (12 steps), InstantEdit further improves to PSNR 29.63, LPIPS 35.27×10⁻³, and SSIM 87.40×10⁻², surpassing all multi-step baselines on consistency and alignment. The method completes editing in 8 NFE (4 steps) with a wall-clock time of 1.37 s per image, compared to 16 NFE for ReNoise and 4 NFE for TurboEdit/InfEdit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the few-step (4-step) setting, InstantEdit outperforms ReNoise on every quality metric (e.g., LPIPS 44.39 vs. 83.26, PSNR 27.96 vs. 24.89) while using half the NFE (8 vs. 16). Against TurboEdit, InstantEdit achieves substantially better consistency (LPIPS 44.39 vs. 77.53, MSE 34.94 vs. 58.48) and higher alignment (Edited 22.82 vs. 22.30), at the cost of 2× more NFE (8 vs. 4) and slightly higher latency (1.37 s vs. 0.96 s). Versus InfEdit (4 steps), InstantEdit wins on PSNR, LPIPS, MSE, SSIM, and both alignment scores, but trails on Structure Distance (17.14 vs. 16.19) and is slower (1.37 s vs. 0.80 s). In the multi-step (12-step) setting, InstantEdit surpasses all multi-step baselines (EF, ProxG, P2P, DI) on PSNR, LPIPS, MSE, SSIM, and alignment, while using far fewer NFE (24 vs. 70–100) and completing in 3.67 s versus 18–92 s. The only consistent trade-off is that InstantEdit requires more NFE and wall-clock time than the inversion-free methods (InfEdit, TurboEdit) due to its explicit PerRFI inversion step.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of few-step diffusion generation and text-guided image editing, building directly on RectifiedFlow's straight-trajectory formulation (Liu et al., ICLR 2023) and its piecewise variant PeRFlow (Yan et al., 2024) as the backbone. It departs from the dominant DDIM-inversion paradigm by exploiting the fact that linear sampling trajectories reduce inversion error even at low step counts, and it modifies TurboEdit's Pseudo-Guidance into an orthogonal-component Disentangled Prompt Guidance. The regeneration pipeline (Inversion Latent Injection) is a novel calibration strategy that has no direct counterpart in prior few-step editing work, which typically relies on either no intermediate latents or scheduled DDPM noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InstantEdit demonstrates that 8-NFE text-guided editing can match or exceed multi-step (50-step) methods on the PIE benchmark, making near-real-time interactive editing practically viable without fine-tuning. The DPG orthogonal decomposition offers a clean, interpretable mechanism for balancing editability against background preservation that could transfer to other flow-based editing pipelines. However, the authors acknowledge that large structural edits (e.g., pose changes) remain challenging, and the method still incurs more wall-clock time than inversion-free approaches like InfEdit due to the explicit inversion step. Open questions include whether the PerRFI + ILI + DPG recipe generalizes to other flow backbones beyond PeRFlow and whether the consistency-editability tradeoff can be further relaxed for more aggressive edits.
