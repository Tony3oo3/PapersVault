## Problem

Existing LoRA-based continual learning methods (e.g., O-LoRA, InfLoRA) rely on passive orthogonality constraints to isolate task updates, merely preventing shared subspaces without actively identifying directions that are inherently robust to future parameter changes. This reactive stance limits the potential for more strategic adaptation, as it does not anticipate worst-case interference scenarios during training or leverage perturbation sensitivity to guide subspace allocation for subsequent tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 224×224 for ImageNet-R and DomainNet) and produces class labels in a class-incremental setting where tasks are sequential subsets of the full class set. LoRA modules are injected into the key and value projections of the ViT-B/16 attention layers, and at inference the model applies all accumulated task-specific LoRA adapters simultaneously to produce a single class prediction over all previously seen classes.

## Method

PLAN allocates each task a dedicated LoRA adapter (B_t A_t) where A_t is constructed by selecting r_t rows from a predefined standard orthogonal basis, ensuring strict subspace isolation across tasks without additional training. The key innovation is a min-max optimization objective for B_t that applies worst-case ℓ_p-norm-bounded perturbations along the unselected basis vectors (reserved for future tasks), making the current task's parameters robust against anticipated future interference. A frequency-based selection mechanism then identifies which remaining basis vectors consistently exhibit minimal perturbation sensitivity over a sliding window of training steps, and assigns those to the next task's A_{t+1}. This forward-looking loop—perturb to robustify current parameters, then select the most stable directions for the next task—shifts subspace allocation from passive avoidance to proactive planning, requiring only a negligible storage overhead of basis indices compared to storing full gradient spaces or prior LoRA matrices.

## Results

On ImageNet-R (N=5), PLAN achieves 77.79% Average Accuracy and 81.93% Average Anytime Accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline InfLoRA (77.09% / 81.96%). On CIFAR-100 (10 tasks), PLAN reaches 87.54% Acc and 92.21% AAA, surpassing InfLoRA (86.50% / 91.23%) and CODA-Prompt (86.95% / 91.39%). On DomainNet (5 tasks), PLAN attains 72.12% Acc and 77.52% AAA, leading all baselines on accuracy while trailing InfLoRA slightly on AAA (78.29%). Across all three benchmarks and multiple task-sequence lengths, PLAN consistently ranks first or near-first, with the largest margins over prompt-based methods (e.g., +13.2% Acc over L2P on ImageNet-R N=20).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PLAN outperforms all five baselines on the majority of metrics across ImageNet-R, CIFAR-100, and DomainNet. Against the closest competitor InfLoRA [17], PLAN gains +0.70% Acc and +1.10% AAA on ImageNet-R (N=20), +1.04% Acc and +0.98% AAA on CIFAR-100, but trails by 0.77% on DomainNet AAA (77.52 vs. 78.29). Over CODA-Prompt [27], the strongest prompt-based method, PLAN leads by +3.27% Acc on ImageNet-R (N=5) and +0.59% Acc on CIFAR-100. O-LoRA [35] and the prompt methods L2P [37] and DualPrompt [36] are substantially outperformed, with gaps of 4–13 percentage points in accuracy depending on the dataset and task count. The Inc-LoRA baseline (naive per-task LoRA without interference mitigation) shows the largest degradation at N=20 (52.12% Acc), highlighting the importance of PLAN's proactive subspace allocation.

## Relation to Prior Work

PLAN extends the LoRA-based continual learning line initiated by O-LoRA and InfLoRA, which enforce orthogonality between task-specific subspaces to prevent interference. The key departure is conceptual: rather than passively constraining new updates to be orthogonal to old ones, PLAN proactively optimizes the current task's parameters against worst-case perturbations in the unallocated subspace, and uses the resulting sensitivity profile to select the most stable basis vectors for the next task. This connects to min-max optimization in CL (e.g., FS-DGPM's use of SAM to flatten past-task loss landscapes), but inverts the direction of the perturbation—targeting future rather than past parameter changes. The method also positions itself against prompt-based CL approaches (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt) by offering a more scalable, storage-efficient alternative that avoids growing prompt pools.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The proactive-vs-passive framing offers a cleaner conceptual lens for LoRA-based CL and the storage advantage (zero stored features vs. 67 MB for InfLoRA) is practically meaningful for long task sequences. However, the strict orthogonality constraint, while effective at preventing forgetting, explicitly does not promote positive backward transfer, and the method has only been validated on ViT architectures with supervised or self-supervised pre-training. Open questions include whether adaptive or task-dependent basis generation could outperform the standard basis for heterogeneous task sequences, whether relaxing orthogonality selectively could recover some backward transfer, and whether the approach extends to non-ViT backbones or other modalities.
