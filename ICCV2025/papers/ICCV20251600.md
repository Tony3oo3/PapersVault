## Problem

Semi-supervised breast ultrasound (BUS) segmentation relies on pseudo-labels whose quality is severely degraded by low contrast, speckle noise, and imaging artifacts. Existing approaches refine pseudo-labels through entropy filtering, global correlation matching, or contrastive supervision, but they overlook the potential of exploiting intrinsic pixel relations—both within local neighborhoods and across feature layers—to make segmentation robust to these domain-specific challenges.

## Input / Output

The method takes 512×512 breast ultrasound images as input, operating under a teacher-student framework where a limited set of labeled images (1/2, 1/4, or 1/8 of the dataset) is combined with unlabeled images whose pseudo-labels are generated by an EMA-updated teacher network. The output is a pixel-level binary segmentation mask delineating the breast lesion from background, evaluated with Dice, IoU, and Hausdorff Distance on the BUSI and UDIAT datasets.

## Method

RA-BUSSeg builds on a ResNet-50/DeepLabv3+ backbone within a standard teacher-student pseudo-labeling framework and introduces two relation-aware modules. The Adjacent Relation Propagation (ARP) module computes a local relation map via dot-product similarity between a center pixel and its 3×3 neighborhood, then propagates these relations into the student's logit outputs through a Hadamard-weighted sum, enforcing mutual reinforcement of predictions for semantically similar neighboring pixels. The Cross-layer Relation Alignment (CRA) module selects 64 representative proxies per layer via a greedy minimum-correlation strategy, models each pixel's relation to these proxies at multiple encoder depths, and minimizes KL divergence between shallow and deep proxy-level relation distributions, using high-level semantics to correct noise-induced false correlations in shallow features. The total loss combines supervised cross-entropy, pseudo-label cross-entropy, the ARP consistency loss, and the CRA alignment loss with equal weights.

## Results

On the BUSI dataset under the 1/4 partition protocol, RA-BUSSeg achieves a Dice of 77.95% and IoU of 69.30%, outperforming the strongest medical-specific baseline ABD (76.30% Dice) by 1.65%. On UDIAT under the same protocol, it reaches 82.55% Dice and 74.21% IoU, surpassing PH-Net (81.80% Dice) and ABD (81.69% Dice). Under the most challenging 1/8 protocol on BUSI, RA-BUSSeg still leads with 75.06% Dice, exceeding ABD (73.71%) by 1.35%. The method also matches or exceeds the fully supervised BCT-Net (72.52% Dice on BUSI 1/4) while using only a fraction of labeled data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RA-BUSSeg outperforms all ten baselines across every partition protocol on both datasets. Against the strongest general semi-supervised method RankMatch [20], it gains +1.78% Dice on UDIAT 1/4 (82.55 vs 80.77) and +2.19% on BUSI 1/4 (77.95 vs 75.76). Against the SOTA medical-specific method ABD [7], the gains are +0.86% (UDIAT 1/4) and +1.65% (BUSI 1/4) in Dice, with the advantage widening under the 1/8 protocol (+1.82% UDIAT, +1.35% BUSI). Compared to the BUS-specific PH-Net [14], RA-BUSSeg leads by +0.75% Dice on UDIAT 1/4 and +1.14% on BUSI 1/4. Notably, RA-BUSSeg also surpasses the fully supervised BCT-Net [36] by +6.13% Dice on BUSI 1/4, demonstrating that the semi-supervised relation-aware framework extracts more value from unlabeled data than full supervision on this task. The only baseline it does not clearly dominate is PH-Net on UDIAT 1/2 (84.77 vs 84.06, a narrow +0.71% margin), where the larger labeled set reduces the relative benefit of pseudo-label refinement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the proxy-based pixel relation modeling introduced by RankMatch (Mai et al., CVPR 2024) by lifting it from a single feature layer to a multi-layer setting via cross-layer KL-divergence alignment, and by adding a local adjacent-relation propagation mechanism that has no direct counterpart in prior correlation-based methods (CorrMatch, RankMatch). It departs from the broader pseudo-labeling and contrastive-learning SSL literature by arguing that the root cause of poor pseudo-label quality in BUS images is the failure to exploit intrinsic pixel relations, rather than merely filtering low-confidence labels or introducing auxiliary contrastive supervision. The teacher-student EMA framework and ResNet-50/DeepLabv3+ backbone follow standard practice in semi-supervised segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The relation-aware framing offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc pseudo-label refinement for noisy, low-contrast medical imaging, and the two modules (local propagation + cross-layer alignment) are modular enough to be transplanted to other semi-supervised segmentation tasks with similar noise profiles. However, the paper is limited to two BUS datasets and acknowledges failure modes on regular artifacts and mixed-intensity lesion boundaries, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to other modalities (CT, MRI) or to 3D volumes. A key open question is whether the cross-layer alignment benefit persists when the backbone is changed (e.g., to a Transformer), since the method is tightly coupled to the hierarchical CNN feature pyramid.
