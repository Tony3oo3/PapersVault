## Problem

State-of-the-art VLM-based open-vocabulary PSG models (e.g., those built on CLIP, BLIP, GLIP) systematically underperform on spatial relation prediction, especially when object pairs are positioned far apart in the image. This stems from VLMs being trained on image-caption pairs that lack detailed spatial descriptions, leaving them unable to reliably distinguish relative object positions. The paper asks whether spatial knowledge can be injected into a VLM backbone without degrading its open-world recognition capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250707/cropped_page7_idx5.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a full panoptic scene graph: pixel-level instance segmentation masks (via a Mask2Former decoder with Swin-B backbone), open-vocabulary object category labels, and subject-predicate-object relation triples. At inference, no text prompt is required; the CLIP image encoder serves as an implicit text encoder, and open-vocabulary classification is performed by matching decoded features against CLIP text embeddings of arbitrary category names. For the VG dataset, mask heads are replaced with box prediction heads to match the SGG evaluation protocol.

## Method

SPADE is a two-stage framework. In stage 1 (inversion-guided calibration), a pretrained teacher diffusion model (BELM) performs DDIM deterministic inversion on training images to extract cross-attention maps that encode spatial structure; a student UNet is then fine-tuned via LoRA (updating only low-rank matrices in cross-attention layers) to reproduce these maps, using an L1 alignment loss. This preserves the pretrained UNet's general knowledge while injecting PSG-specific spatial priors. In stage 2 (spatial-aware context reasoning), a Relation Graph Transformer (RGT) operates on a spatial-semantic graph built from predicted masks: it alternates between long-range self-attention over both neighbors and non-neighbors (capturing global context) and a GCN layer (capturing local pairwise correlations) across 8 blocks, producing refined relation queries. Open-vocabulary prediction fuses diffusion-model features with CLIP-pooled mask features via a geometric mean, enabling recognition of unseen objects and predicates.

## Results

On the PSG dataset, SPADE achieves 45.1/41.2 R/mR@50 and 54.3/51.7 R/mR@100 in the closed-set setting, and 26.7/23.3 R/mR@50 and 31.8/25.8 R/mR@100 in the open-set (OvR) setting, outperforming the strongest baseline OpenPSG by ~2.3 points (closed) and ~5.5 points (open) in R@50. On Visual Genome, SPADE reaches 37.2/17.3 R/mR@50 (closed) and 24.1/11.2 R/mR@50 (open), surpassing OpenPSG by ~4.5 and ~3.7 points respectively. In the more challenging OvD+R scenario (both objects and predicates unseen), SPADE attains 22.7 R@50 on PSG and 19.4 R@50 on VG, exceeding OpenPSG by 5.5 and 9.9 points in R@50.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPADE outperforms all 18 baselines across every reported metric on both PSG and VG datasets. The closest competitor is OpenPSG [69], which SPADE surpasses by 2.3 R@50 points in closed-set PSG (45.1 vs 42.8) and by 5.5 R@50 points in open-set PSG (26.7 vs 21.2); on VG, the margins are 4.5 (closed) and 3.7 (open) R@50 points. Among open-vocabulary methods, OvSGTR [4] and PGSG [31] trail SPADE by 7.4 and 11.2 R@50 points respectively in the open-set PSG setting, indicating that SPADE's inversion-guided calibration and relation graph transformer provide substantially stronger spatial reasoning. In the OvD+R scenario (both objects and predicates novel), SPADE leads OpenPSG by 5.5 R@50 on PSG (22.7 vs 11.4) and by 9.9 R@50 on VG (19.4 vs 9.5), a particularly large gap that highlights the benefit of the dual diffusion+CLIP scoring fusion. For spatial relation prediction specifically (Table 4), SPADE narrows the distant-vs-non-distant performance gap to 4.2 R@50 points (42.3 vs 46.5), whereas OpenPSG shows a 6.6-point gap (37.1 vs 43.7), confirming that the spatial-aware denoising network directly addresses the VLM spatial-reasoning weakness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the open-vocabulary panoptic scene graph generation line of work (OvSGTR, PGSG, OpenPSG) by addressing a specific weakness of VLM-based approaches: poor spatial relation reasoning, particularly for distant object pairs. Rather than relying on CLIP or similar VLMs for spatial understanding, SPADE repurposes the DDIM inversion process to extract cross-attention maps as explicit spatial priors, then calibrates a pretrained diffusion UNet via LoRA fine-tuning. The Relation Graph Transformer with separate neighbor and non-neighbor attention heads builds on graph transformer architectures for scene understanding but introduces a dual-context mechanism that explicitly models both connected and disconnected object pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a useful empirical quantification of the spatial reasoning gap in VLM-based PSG models (Table 4), showing that performance drops significantly for distant object pairs, which validates the motivation for injecting spatial knowledge. The diffusion-inversion-as-spatial-prior approach is a creative repurposing, but its practical adoption is limited by the added complexity of a two-stage calibration pipeline and dependence on a specific teacher model (BELM). Open questions include whether simpler geometric features (bounding box distances, IoU) could achieve similar spatial reasoning gains without the diffusion machinery, and whether the approach generalizes beyond PSG to other spatially-reasoning tasks like human-object interaction detection, which the authors mention as future work.
