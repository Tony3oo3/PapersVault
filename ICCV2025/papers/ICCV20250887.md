## Problem

SAM's reliance on manual sparse prompts (bounding boxes, points) makes it highly sensitive to prompt placement and size, causing it to fail on camouflaged object detection where foreground and background are nearly indistinguishable. No prior method had combined both vision and language embeddings from foundation models to automatically generate prompts for SAM in this task, eliminating the need for any user-provided input.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 1024×1024) as input and produces a pixel-level segmentation mask as output, requiring no bounding boxes, points, scribbles, or masks from the user. All prompts are generated automatically: BLIP produces a text caption and a vision embedding from the image, and Mamba encodes the caption into a text embedding. The model is trained with batch size 1 for 20 epochs on a composite CAMO+COD10K dataset using focal loss and dice loss.

## Method

The framework (MM-SAM) generates multi-modal prompts by using BLIP to caption the image and extract a vision embedding, then encoding the caption with Mamba (a selective state-space model) to obtain a text embedding; these are concatenated as the sparse prompt for SAM's mask decoder. Two architectural modifications to SAM are introduced: (1) a multi-level adapter that injects BLIP's vision embedding into multiple transformer blocks of SAM's image encoder via shared-weight adapter layers, bridging the semantic gap between VLM features and SAM's pixel-level features; and (2) replacing SAM's dense embedding—which is randomly initialized when no mask prompt is provided—with a GELU-activated linear projection of SAM's own image encoder output, yielding a semantically meaningful dense prompt. The full model is fine-tuned end-to-end on COD data, and the same pipeline can be applied in a training-free mode (without the adapter) for zero-shot transfer to medical image segmentation, saliency detection, and blur detection.

## Results

MM-SAM achieves state-of-the-art performance on 11 out of 12 metrics across three COD benchmarks (CHAMELEON, CAMO, COD10K) in the supervised setting. On CHAMELEON, it reaches Sα=0.923, Eφ=0.946, Fβw=0.853, MAE=0.027; on CAMO, Sα=0.863, Eφ=0.901, Fβw=0.782, MAE=0.059; and on COD10K, Sα=0.896, Eφ=0.907, Fβw=0.808, MAE=0.023. The only metric where a supervised baseline (SAM-Adapter) retains the edge is COD10K E-measure (0.918 vs. 0.907). In the training-free medical image segmentation setting, MM-SAM achieves Sα=0.565 and Fβw=0.220 on CVC-ColonDB, outperforming all prior training-free methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest supervised baseline SAM-Adapter [2], MM-SAM improves Sα by up to +2.7 (CHAMELEON: 0.923 vs. 0.896), Eφ by up to +2.8 (CHAMELEON: 0.946 vs. 0.919), Fβw by up to +2.9 (CHAMELEON: 0.853 vs. 0.824), and reduces MAE by up to 24% (CHAMELEON: 0.027 vs. 0.033); the sole exception is COD10K Eφ where SAM-Adapter retains a narrow lead (0.918 vs. 0.907). Compared to the task-generic prompt method ProMaC [11], MM-SAM wins on 11 of 12 metrics, with ProMaC achieving a higher CHAMELEON Fβw (0.899 vs. 0.853) likely due to its hallucination-based prompting strategy. Against GenSAM [10], MM-SAM dominates across all three datasets and all four metrics (e.g., COD10K: Sα 0.896 vs. 0.775, MAE 0.023 vs. 0.067). In the training-free medical image segmentation setting (Table 3), MM-SAM outperforms ProMaC [11] on CVC-ColonDB Sα (0.565 vs. 0.530) and Kvasir Sα (0.740 vs. 0.573), while in zero-shot saliency/blur detection (Table 4) it achieves the best DUTS Eφ (0.877) and CUHK mIoU (0.698), surpassing AlignSAM [13] and SAMed [48].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SAM-Adapter [2], adopting its ViT-H image encoder and mask decoder as the base architecture, but departs from it in two key ways: (1) it replaces the single-level adapter with a multi-level adapter that injects BLIP vision embeddings at every transformer block, and (2) it eliminates SAM's reliance on manual sparse prompts entirely by auto-generating both text (via BLIP captioning + Mamba encoding) and vision (via BLIP image encoder) prompts. Compared to GenSAM [10], which uses only a generic text prompt, this work is the first to combine image-specific vision and language embeddings as complementary prompts for SAM in COD, and it additionally identifies and fixes the random dense embedding issue in SAM's mask decoder by substituting the image encoder's own embedding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: the framework removes the need for any human-provided bounding boxes, points, or masks, making SAM genuinely autonomous for COD and related segmentation tasks. The dense-embedding fix (replacing a randomly initialized mask embedding with the image encoder output) is a simple architectural insight that could benefit any SAM-based pipeline operating without mask prompts. However, the method still depends on BLIP caption quality and requires fine-tuning (20 epochs on a single GPU) for best results, and the training-free transfer to medical imaging, while competitive, does not consistently outperform dedicated methods. Open questions include whether the multi-level adapter's gains persist with smaller SAM variants (ViT-B/L) and whether the approach generalizes to video or 3D segmentation without further adaptation.
