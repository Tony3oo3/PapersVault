## Problem

Existing self-supervised image copy detection methods rely on view-level contrastive learning and neglect fine-grained patch-level correspondences, limiting robustness against sophisticated editing transformations. Heuristic approaches for establishing local correspondences (feature-based or location-based nearest-neighbor matching) introduce false matches and partial matches, injecting conflicting gradient signals that degrade model convergence and detection performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252107/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images (query and reference) at 224×224 or 336×336 resolution. The matcher produces a binary copy/non-copy classification score via cross-attention fusion between encoded tokens, while the descriptor produces a global feature embedding for cosine-similarity-based retrieval. Both models additionally yield patch-level affinity heatmaps (16×16 pixel patches in ViT) that localize copy regions, enabling interpretability beyond a single scalar score.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-part pipeline: PixTrace, a coordinate-table tracking module that sequentially applies inverse transform functions for each edit operation (affine, perspective, matting, color jitter, etc.) to maintain exact pixel-level spatial mappings between original and edited images; and CopyNCE, a geometrically-guided contrastive loss that replaces the binary positive/negative structure of InfoNCE with a soft prior distribution q(R_j^r | R_i^q) computed from the overlap ratio of each query patch with reference patches via PixTrace. This prior is sharpened by a confidence parameter γ and the loss reduces to a KL divergence between the geometric prior and the model's predicted patch affinity, applied symmetrically in both query→reference and reference→query directions. CopyNCE is added as an auxiliary loss (weighted by w_NCE) on top of standard baselines (BCE for matcher, InfoNCE+KoLeo for descriptor) at a specific ViT layer, providing clean pixel-level supervision that eliminates the false-match and partial-match noise inherent in heuristic correspondence methods.

## Results

On DISC21, CopyNCE achieves state-of-the-art performance of 88.7% μAP / 83.9% RP90 for the matcher (ViT-S, 336×336, with local ensembling) and 72.6% μAP / 68.4% RP90 for the descriptor (ViT-S, 224×224, with score normalization). On the more challenging NDEC benchmark, CopyNCE (ViT-B+ViT-S) reaches 72.5% μAP, surpassing the strongest multi-model baseline by 8.4% μAP. Without any post-processing or ensembling, CopyNCE already attains 83.5% μAP / 75.4% RP90 (matcher) and 70.5% μAP / 63.6% RP90 (descriptor), exceeding the previous best single-model results by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For the matcher, CopyNCE (ViT-S, 336×336, local ensembling) surpasses D²LV [48]—which ensembles 33 models each with ≥ViT-S parameters—by 0.1% μAP and 3.8% RP90, while using a single ViT-S. It also outperforms the Separate ViT-L baseline [24] by 2.5% μAP / 1.7% RP90 under comparable local-ensembling settings. For the descriptor, CopyNCE with score normalization (72.6% μAP / 68.4% RP90) beats SSCD [35] (72.5% / 63.1%) by 5.3% RP90 and BoT [49] (71.5% / 62.9%) by 1.1% μAP / 5.5% RP90, all with a smaller ViT-S backbone versus R50. On NDEC, CopyNCE (72.5% μAP) exceeds Strong ASL [50] (64.1%) by 8.4% μAP and D²LV ASL (61.3%) by 11.2% μAP, demonstrating that the matcher's cross-attention fusion handles hard negatives far better than descriptor-based ensembles. Against the heuristic local-SSL baselines (FeatNN, LocNN) used in ablations, PixTrace-based CopyNCE consistently outperforms by 2–14% μAP, confirming that geometrically-verified pixel tracking is essential over nearest-neighbor matching for copy detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the InfoNCE contrastive framework (from CPC) into a geometrically-guided patch-level loss (CopyNCE) for image copy detection, departing from prior local SSL methods (FeatNN, LocNN) that rely on heuristic nearest-neighbor matching to establish patch correspondences. The key conceptual departure is PixTrace, which exploits the fact that SSL augmentations are deterministic transforms with known inverse functions, enabling exact pixel-level coordinate tracking rather than approximate matching. The work also positions itself against local feature matching methods (LoFTR, LightGlue, OmniGlue) that handle homographic transforms, arguing that ICD requires handling complex non-homographic edits (matting, perspective, color jitter) that those methods cannot formulate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete limitation in ICD: view-level contrastive methods lack fine-grained correspondence learning, making them vulnerable to sophisticated edits. The insight that SSL transforms are fully traceable provides a clean, noise-free supervision signal that eliminates the false-match and partial-match problems plaguing heuristic approaches. However, the method is inherently limited to the SSL training regime where transform functions are known; it does not directly address the inference-time challenge of detecting copies under unknown real-world edits. Open questions include whether the coordinate-tracking principle can be adapted to semi-supervised or weakly-supervised settings, and whether the approach extends to video copy detection where temporal transforms add complexity.
