## Problem

Existing logit distillation methods ignore the impact of incorrect teacher predictions, which exacerbates the divergence between the distillation loss and the cross-entropy loss and undermines the student's learning objectives. Prior correction-based approaches (swap or augment operations) fix teacher errors by directly modifying logits with label information, but in doing so they disrupt the inter-class correlations that constitute the 'dark knowledge' essential for effective distillation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 on CIFAR-100, 224×224 on ImageNet) along with their ground-truth class labels, and produces a class probability distribution from the student network. Unusually, the ground-truth label is not only used for the standard cross-entropy term but is also consumed during distillation loss computation to dynamically construct a class mask from the teacher's logits, meaning the label shapes which subset of classes participates in the correlation alignment.

## Method

RLD decomposes logit distillation into two complementary losses. Sample Confidence Distillation (SCD) aligns a binary distribution: the teacher's {max predicted probability, 1 − max} with the student's {true-class probability, 1 − true-class probability} via KL divergence, guiding the student to assign appropriate confidence to the true class without constraining the full distribution. Masked Correlation Distillation (MCD) dynamically masks all classes whose teacher logit is greater than or equal to the true-class logit, then aligns the re-normalized probability distributions over the remaining (unmasked) classes via KL divergence; when the teacher is wrong, more classes are masked, reducing misleading signal while preserving the correlations among the surviving classes. The final loss is L_CE + α·L_SCD + β·L_MCD. A key design choice is using 'greater-or-equal' (M_ge) rather than 'strictly-greater' (M_g) for the mask, which keeps the true class exclusively in SCD and avoids a loss conflict where MCD would simultaneously demand the true class probability match the teacher while SCD enforces a different target.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (homogeneous pairs, Table 1), RLD achieves top-1 accuracies of 76.64% (ResNet32×4→ResNet8×4), 74.93% (VGG13→VGG8), 74.88% (WRN-40-2→WRN-40-1), 72.00% (ResNet56→ResNet20), 74.02% (ResNet110→ResNet32), and 71.67% (ResNet110→ResNet20), ranking as the best or second-best logit distillation method in all six settings. On ImageNet (Table 3), RLD achieves 71.91% top-1 / 90.59% top-5 (Res34→Res18) and 72.75% top-1 / 91.18% top-5 (Res50→MN-V1), outperforming all feature and logit distillation baselines. The improvement over the closest logit competitor DKD is +0.32% on CIFAR-100 (ResNet32×4→ResNet8×4) and +0.21% on ImageNet (Res34→Res18), with larger gaps when teacher accuracy is lower.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RLD outperforms the strongest logit distillation baseline DKD across all six CIFAR-100 homogeneous pairs (e.g., 76.64% vs. 76.32% for ResNet32×4→ResNet8×4) and both ImageNet pairs (71.91% vs. 71.70% top-1 for Res34→Res18; 72.75% vs. 72.05% for Res50→MN-V1). Among correction-based logit methods, RLD beats LA, RC, and LR in 5 out of 6 CIFAR-100 homogeneous settings and in all 6 heterogeneous settings, with the largest margin over LR on ResNet56→ResNet20 (72.00% vs. 70.74%). On CIFAR-100, two feature distillation methods (SimKD at 78.08% and CAT-KD at 76.91% for ResNet32×4→ResNet8×4) still exceed RLD, but RLD surpasses all feature methods on ImageNet (71.91% vs. ReviewKD's 71.61% top-1). In the heterogeneous CIFAR-100 setting, RLD leads all logit methods by a clear margin (e.g., 77.56% vs. DKD's 77.07% for ResNet32×4→SHN-V2) and approaches or matches the best feature methods (SimKD 78.39%, CAT-KD 78.41%). The gap between RLD and DKD widens when teacher accuracy is lower, as shown in the reversed distillation experiment (Table 4) where RLD gains +0.43 to +0.69% over DKD with weaker teachers.

## Relation to Prior Work

RLD builds directly on the classical logit distillation framework of Hinton et al. and the decoupled loss decomposition of DKD, which separated target-class and non-target-class knowledge transfer. It departs from correction-based approaches (LA, RC, LR) that modify teacher logits via swap or augment operations, arguing these disrupt inter-class correlations that constitute 'dark knowledge.' The key conceptual departure is the masked correlation mechanism: rather than correcting the teacher's full distribution, RLD dynamically masks classes ranked at or above the true class, letting the student learn from a subset of the teacher's distribution whose size adapts to prediction correctness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and previously underappreciated failure mode in logit distillation—incorrect teacher predictions exacerbating the divergence between distillation and label losses—and provides a principled fix that preserves class correlations. The observation that RLD's advantage over DKD grows with teacher imperfection (ImageNet over CIFAR-100, reversed KD) suggests the method is most valuable in realistic deployment settings where teachers are imperfect. Open questions include whether the per-pair hyperparameter sensitivity (α, β) can be resolved via dynamic or meta-learned scheduling, and whether the masking principle extends to feature-level distillation or non-classification tasks.
