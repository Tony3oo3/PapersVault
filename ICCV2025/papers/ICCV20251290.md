## Problem

Video background replacement with foreground relighting requires simultaneously preserving pixel-level foreground details, achieving temporally coherent lighting, and generating a new background — yet existing approaches either demand task-specific training on scarce paired video data or, when applied zero-shot, suffer from foreground identity drift and temporal jitter. The core difficulty is that video diffusion models operate in a highly compact 3D latent space where standard inversion and latent-manipulation techniques degrade, making precise pixel-level control infeasible without training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a foreground video (resized to 480×720, trimmed to 49 frames) and either a text prompt or a user-provided background image, and produces a background-replaced video with harmonized foreground relighting. The entire pipeline runs zero-shot on a single 24 GB GPU (NVIDIA 4090) in approximately 12 minutes per video, with no task-specific training or inference-time optimization required.

## Method

ANYPORTAL is a three-stage training-free pipeline: (1) Background Generation uses Diffusion-As-Shader with CogVideoX to synthesize a camera-motion-aligned background video from an IC-Light-generated first frame; (2) Light Harmonization applies a two-step IC-Light pipeline (image-guided δ_I followed by text-guided δ_p via SDEdit) with cross-frame attention to balance illumination intensity and temporal style consistency; (3) Consistency Enhancement refines the coarse video using SDEdit with the video diffusion model plus edge-based ControlNet. The key technical contribution is the Refinement Projection Algorithm (RPA), which transfers high-frequency details from the input video to the foreground region during denoising by decoding to pixel space, compositing LF/HF components, and re-encoding with a deterministic projection direction ε̂ computed to guarantee zero-error reconstruction of unmodified regions — preventing the background from blurring that would result from the stochastic VAE sampling.

## Results

On a 30-sample test set, ANYPORTAL achieves the best temporal consistency (Tem-Con 0.993), foreground identity preservation (ID-Psrv 0.313, lower is better), and motion preservation (Mtn-Psrv 0.987) among all methods. It attains a Fram-Acc of 0.973, slightly below IC-Light's 0.983 (which is specifically trained for background replacement without temporal constraints). In the user study with 24 participants, ANYPORTAL is preferred 68.06% of the time for prompt alignment, 65.28% for temporal consistency, 61.67% for foreground/motion preservation, and 47.22% for relighting quality — substantially outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ANYPORTAL outperforms all three baselines on temporal consistency (0.993 vs. DAS 0.986, TokenFlow 0.981, IC-Light 0.945), foreground identity preservation (0.313 vs. DAS 0.364, IC-Light 0.578, TokenFlow 0.632), and motion preservation (0.987 vs. TokenFlow 0.985, DAS 0.878, IC-Light 0.844). The only metric where ANYPORTAL trails is Fram-Acc (0.973 vs. IC-Light 0.983), which is expected since IC-Light is purpose-trained for single-frame background replacement without temporal constraints. In the user study, ANYPORTAL dominates every criterion by wide margins — e.g., 68.06% vs. 29.72% (DAS) for prompt alignment and 65.28% vs. 28.61% for temporal consistency. TokenFlow is the weakest baseline overall, scoring only 0.541 Fram-Acc and 1.11% user preference for prompt alignment, indicating limited editing capability. The concurrent work Light-A-Video produces comparable visual quality but can only relight existing backgrounds rather than generating new background content.

## Relation to Prior Work

ANYPORTAL sits at the intersection of image relighting (IC-Light) and video diffusion (CogVideoX), combining their complementary priors in a zero-shot pipeline without any task-specific training. It departs from prior video editing methods (TokenFlow, DAS) that offer only coarse structural control by introducing the Refinement Projection Algorithm, which achieves pixel-level foreground consistency in the compact 3D latent space where standard DDIM inversion and inference-time optimization fail. The two-step IC-Light harmonization (image-guided then text-guided with SDEdit) and cross-frame attention injection extend the single-frame relighting paradigm to video, while the DAS-based background generation borrows motion transfer from 3D-aware video diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's practical accessibility—operating on a single 24GB GPU with no training—democratizes video background replacement that previously required professional VFX pipelines. Its modular design means each component can be swapped for newer pretrained models as they emerge, making it a durable recipe rather than a one-time result. Key limitations remain: low-resolution or blurry inputs degrade high-frequency transfer, unclear foreground boundaries cause inpainting artifacts, and rapid motion still challenges the diffusion backbone. The authors suggest extending the RPA and temporal-prior framework to other video editing tasks (recolorization, stylization, inpainting), which would be a natural and impactful next step.
