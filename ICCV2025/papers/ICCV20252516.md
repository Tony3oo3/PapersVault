## Problem

Inversion-based generative image watermarking relies on DDIM inversion to recover the watermarked latent, but prior work has focused almost exclusively on designing robust embedding mechanisms while treating inversion as a fixed, error-free step. In practice, DDIM inversion accumulates latent-domain errors from its noise-prediction approximation across steps and is further corrupted by pixel-domain distortions (e.g., JPEG compression), both of which significantly degrade extraction fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252516/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At extraction time, the method takes a 512×512×3 distorted watermarked image (produced by a latent diffusion model such as SD-v1.4 or SD-v2.1) and passes it through the restoration network, VAE encoder, DDIM inversion, and the optimization loop to recover the embedded watermark. The output is either a 1-bit detection decision (for Tree-Ring/RingID, evaluated as TPR at FPR=10⁻¹⁰) or a 256-bit binary sequence (for Gaussian Shading, evaluated as bit accuracy). Notably, the original text prompt and guidance scale used during generation are not required at extraction; inversion and optimization proceed under a null condition with unit guidance.

## Method

ROAR introduces two complementary components to reduce inversion errors. First, a Mixture-of-Experts distortion-adaptive restoration network (AR), built on a U-Net backbone with channel attention and bilinear-interpolation upsampling, is trained with an L2 loss on a diverse set of entropy-increasing (Gaussian/salt-and-pepper noise) and entropy-decreasing (median filtering, JPEG) distortions to realign distorted images with the clean watermarked distribution before inversion. Second, a Regeneration-based Optimization (RO) module takes the DDIM-inverted latent as initialization and iteratively (K=20 steps, Adam with cosine-annealed LR from 5×10⁻³ to 1×10⁻⁶) minimizes the L2 distance between the image regenerated under the null condition and the restored watermarked image, thereby pulling the optimized latent toward the true starting point along the unconditional trajectory and ensuring that noise predictions at each step closely match those of the original watermarked latent. Only AR is trained (supervised); RO is a gradient-based optimization performed at inference time, making ROAR a plug-and-play module compatible with any existing inversion-based embedding scheme.

## Results

ROAR achieves a detection TPR of 1.000 (SD-v1.4/v2.1) under all 9 tested distortions when integrated with Tree-Ring, RingID, and Gaussian Shading, compared to 0.838–0.924 for the respective baselines. For multi-bit traceability (GS-ROAR), bit accuracy under distortion reaches 0.988/0.990, up from 0.885/0.894 for vanilla GS. Image quality is fully preserved: FID and CLIP-Score remain identical to the unmodified base methods (e.g., FID 25.20±0.22, CLIP-Score 0.3597±0.0009 for GS-ROAR). The paper reports an average extraction accuracy of 99.71%, outperforming the best prior method by 10.66%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ROAR is a plug-and-play enhancement that outperforms all seven baselines on distorted-image robustness while preserving generation quality (FID and CLIP-Score unchanged). Against the three inversion-based methods it directly augments, ROAR raises TPR(Dis) from 0.838/0.878 (Tree-Ring), 0.894/0.899 (RingID), and 0.912/0.924 (GS) to a perfect 1.000/1.000, and improves GS bit accuracy from 0.885/0.894 to 0.988/0.990. Against fine-tuning-based methods (Stable Signature, LaWa) and image-watermarking methods (DwtDct, DwtDctSvd, RivaGAN), the gap is even larger: ROAR's TPR(Dis) of 1.000 exceeds their 0.111–0.367 range by 60–90 percentage points. Under severe individual distortions (e.g., Gaussian noise σ=0.2), ROAR boosts Tree-Ring TPR by 82% and GS bit accuracy by 28% over the unmodified baselines. No baseline matches ROAR on any distortion-robustness metric; the only trade-off is the additional optimization cost at extraction time (20 gradient steps).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DDIM inversion framework (Song et al., ICLR 2021) that underpins all existing inversion-based generative watermarking methods (Tree-Ring, RingID, Gaussian Shading). Prior work in this line has focused almost exclusively on designing robust embedding mechanisms into the initial latent, while treating DDIM inversion as a fixed, error-free recovery step. ROAR departs from this by explicitly targeting the two sources of inversion error—latent-domain estimation error from the noise-prediction approximation and pixel-domain distortion—through a regeneration-based optimization loop and a MoE-based restoration network, respectively, while remaining fully plug-and-play compatible with existing embedding schemes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying and addressing inversion errors that prior inversion-based watermarking work had overlooked, ROAR provides a practical, drop-in improvement that raises extraction accuracy to near-perfect levels (99.71% average) across a wide range of distortions and generation conditions. The plug-and-play design means existing watermarking pipelines can adopt it without retraining their embedding components. Open questions include the computational cost of the 20-iteration optimization at extraction time, the generalization of the MoE restoration network to truly novel distortion types beyond those in training, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D generative watermarking where inversion errors may compound differently.
