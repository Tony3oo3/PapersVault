## Problem

Existing deep pan-sharpening methods suffer from three key limitations: intermediate features remain unconstrained and uninterpretable (black-box), deeper networks do not uniformly benefit all image patches (some overfit at later stages, leaving fusion diversity across stages unexploited), and there is no semantic-level benchmark dataset to validate pan-sharpening quality in practical downstream tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250769/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution multispectral (MS) image and a paired high-resolution panchromatic (PAN) image at a 4× spatial scaling factor, producing a high-resolution multispectral image. The adaptive stage-exiting mechanism additionally outputs patch-wise binary decisions selecting which of the K=4 candidate stages yields the optimal result for each spatial patch. For the hyperspectral reconstruction extension, the input is a degraded hyperspectral image at 8× scaling, and the output is the reconstructed high-resolution hyperspectral cube.

## Method

The paper formulates pan-sharpening as a linear inverse problem regularized by spatial morphology and spectral correlation priors, then solves it via half-quadratic splitting into three sub-problems (updating z, r, x) that are unrolled into a K-stage network with XNet, RNet, and ZNet sub-networks per stage. The spatial prior incorporates phase-oriented constraints to enforce modal-invariant morphology consistency between PAN and MS, while a physics-driven spectral filtration attention (SFA) module simulates the energy-distribution operator φ by modeling spectral correlations through multi-head self-attention over shuffled patch features. An adaptive stage-exiting (ASE) module, trained separately with binary cross-entropy, compares PSNR of adjacent-stage outputs per patch and retains the best, preventing overfitting in deeper stages. Training uses a composite loss combining L1 reference loss, a morphological loss (L_morph) with smoothness, spatial equality, phase consistency, and reconstruction terms, and the ASE is fine-tuned post-training on the frozen backbone.

## Results

On reduced-scale evaluation across QuickBird, GaoFen-2, and WorldView-II, DAPNet achieves the best values on all five reference metrics, e.g., ERGAS of 1.0177 / 1.1118 / 4.6692 and SSIM of 0.9666 / 0.9330 / 0.7117 respectively, outperforming the nearest competitor DISPNet (ERGAS 1.1164 / 1.2515 / 4.9051). On full-scale non-reference metrics, DAPNet attains the highest QNR on QuickBird (0.9500) and GaoFen-2 (0.9443) and the lowest D_s on both (0.0315, 0.0272), though ZeroSharpen leads on WorldView-II QNR (0.9366 vs. 0.8996). On the CAVE hyperspectral reconstruction benchmark (×8), DAPNet achieves the best PSNR (50.62 dB), ERGAS (0.7840), and SSIM (0.9969) with 7.0 M parameters, surpassing MoG-DCN (50.01 dB) and DHIF-Net (48.52 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAPNet outperforms all 12 pan-sharpening baselines on every reference metric (ERGAS, RMSE, QAVE, SAM, SSIM) across QuickBird, GaoFen-2, and WorldView-II, with the closest competitors being DISPNet and HFIN on reference metrics (e.g., DISPNet ERGAS 1.1164 vs. DAPNet 1.0177 on QuickBird). On full-scale non-reference metrics, DAPNet leads on QuickBird (QNR 0.9500, D_s 0.0315) and GaoFen-2 (QNR 0.9443, D_s 0.0272), but ZeroSharpen achieves a higher QNR on WorldView-II (0.9366 vs. 0.8996) and a lower D_λ (0.0221 vs. 0.0304), indicating DAPNet's advantage is most pronounced on four-band sensors. Traditional methods (AWLP, BT-H, MTF_GLP, MF) are consistently 15–40% worse on ERGAS and 2–3× worse on RMSE compared to deep methods. On the CAVE hyperspectral reconstruction task (×8), DAPNet achieves the best PSNR (50.62 dB) and ERGAS (0.7840), edging out MoG-DCN (50.01 dB, 0.8959) while using fewer FLOPs (1628 G vs. 4390 G); however, MoG-DCN retains a marginal SAM advantage (2.0068° vs. 2.0193°). The segmentation evaluation on the newly introduced SegGF dataset confirms that DAPNet's fusion quality translates to the best mIoU (41.23 reduced-scale, 49.25 full-scale), particularly in road and water categories, validating the semantic-level benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep unfolding paradigm for inverse problems (Zhang et al. 2020; Song et al. 2023) to pan-sharpening by formulating it as a linear inverse problem regularized with spatial and spectral priors, using half-quadratic splitting (Sun et al. 2020; Zhao et al. 2024) to decompose the optimization into unrolled sub-problems. It departs from prior supervised pan-sharpening methods (DISPNet, HFIN) by replacing pseudo-GT constraints with physics-driven phase-oriented spatial morphology stripping and a spectral filtration attention mechanism, and from prior unfolding networks by introducing an adaptive stage-exiting mechanism that selectively retains patches at their optimal convergence stage rather than forcing all through the full depth. The construction of SegGF as the first semantic-level benchmark also distinguishes it from prior work that relied solely on pixel-level or non-reference metrics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The adaptive stage-exiting mechanism addresses a practical and previously underexplored issue: a non-trivial fraction (~27–33%) of image patches actually degrade in quality at deeper stages, meaning that uniformly deeper networks are not always better. The SegGF dataset fills a genuine gap by enabling semantic-level validation of pan-sharpening quality, which is more aligned with downstream applications than pixel-level metrics. However, the performance gains over strong baselines like DISPNet and HFIN are modest, the ASE is trained separately with a fixed criterion (PSNR), and the physics-driven priors (phase constraints, spectral filtration) have not been ablated against purely learned alternatives of comparable capacity, leaving open whether the interpretability gains come at a hidden cost. Broader questions include whether the unfolding framework with adaptive exiting generalizes to other multi-modal fusion tasks beyond pan-sharpening.
