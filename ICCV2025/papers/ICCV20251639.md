## Problem

Existing few-shot font generation methods suffer from broken character structures and inconsistent font styles, particularly for complex glyphs. Deformation-based approaches lack explicit supervision over the transformation process, and latent-space interpolation (e.g., MSD-Font) cannot capture accurate font deformations because the VAE latent space does not define meaningful intermediate shapes. The paper asks whether modeling font generation as a video with explicit per-frame supervision and inter-frame continuity can resolve these structural and stylistic failures.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 128×128 source font image and 8 reference font images (8-shot) as input, and produces a 33-frame font video where the first frame is the source font and the last frame is the generated target font image. Training font videos are synthesized via FILM frame interpolation between source and target font images. At inference, a test-time noise inversion step (3 updating steps at rate 10⁻³) refines the last frame by optimizing the intermediate noise map to minimize content and style feature mismatches, so the final output is a single 128×128 font image extracted from the last frame of the generated video.

## Method

FontAnimate reformulates few-shot font generation as an image-to-video diffusion task: a PixArt-α-based DiT denoising network is extended with temporal self-attention layers and temporal feed-forward layers in each Transformer block, plus frame-index conditioning injected through adaLN-single layers, so that inter-frame continuity preserves global character structure. Training is two-stage—first an I2I font generation model (style encoder + content encoder + DiT denoiser) is trained, then the I2V model is fine-tuned on FILM-interpolated font videos with the temporal modules. At test time, a noise inversion mechanism iteratively optimizes the sampled noise map ε_{t+1}^{vid} by backpropagating a combined content-feature and style-feature L2 loss on the predicted last frame through the DDIM sampling chain, aligning the generated font with the provided reference conditions without retraining any network parameters. Full (non-causal) temporal self-attention is used rather than causal attention, since the I2I pre-training already provides strong generative priors for the target font appearance.

## Results

On the UFUC (Unseen Fonts, Unseen Contents) setting, FontAnimate achieves the best scores across all four metrics: RMSE 0.2470, PSNR 12.47, SSIM 0.7217, and LPIPS 0.1322. On the UFSC (Unseen Fonts, Seen Contents) setting, FontAnimate attains the best LPIPS (0.1211) and SSIM (0.7136), while MSD-Font edges it slightly on RMSE (0.2439 vs. 0.2450) with identical PSNR (12.61). The most notable margin is in perceptual quality (LPIPS), where FontAnimate outperforms the second-best method by 0.0137 on UFSC and 0.0205 on UFUC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FontAnimate dominates on the harder UFUC setting, winning all four metrics against all six baselines. The closest competitor is MSD-Font (also a diffusion-based font-transfer method), which FontAnimate beats by 0.0205 in LPIPS (0.1322 vs. 0.1527) and 0.0028 in SSIM (0.7217 vs. 0.7189) on UFUC. On the easier UFSC setting, MSD-Font slightly edges FontAnimate on RMSE (0.2439 vs. 0.2450) while both share the top PSNR (12.61), but FontAnimate still leads on SSIM and LPIPS. The GAN-based baselines (MX-Font, VQ-Font) and deformation-based methods (NTF, CF-Font) trail by a wider margin, with MX-Font showing the weakest LPIPS (0.1651 UFSC / 0.1635 UFUC) and VQ-Font exhibiting broken structures and uneven curves in qualitative comparisons. FontDiffuser, the other diffusion-based baseline, is outperformed by FontAnimate on every metric in both settings, with the gap most visible in LPIPS (0.1423 vs. 0.1211 on UFSC).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the content-style disentanglement paradigm for few-shot font generation (FFG) by reframing the generation task as a video generation problem, where the font transfer from source to target is modeled as a sequence of interpolated frames. It builds on the deformation-based FFG line (DG-Font, NTF, MSD-Font) that treats font generation as a transformation process, but replaces their implicit or latent-space interpolation with explicit frame-interpolation-based video supervision. The architecture is grounded in PixArt-α (DiT-based latent diffusion) extended with temporal self-attention and frame-index conditioning, following the AnimateDiff/VideoCrafter recipe for image-to-video diffusion, while the test-time noise inversion mechanism for condition alignment is adapted from the text-to-image personalization literature (Null-text Inversion, GSN, INITNO).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The video-generation reframing provides a principled way to enforce structural consistency through inter-frame continuity, addressing a persistent failure mode (broken character structures) in FFG that prior deformation-based methods could not fully resolve. The noise inversion mechanism offers a training-free, test-time correction that improves both style fidelity and structural accuracy without retraining. However, the authors acknowledge that the diffusion-based video pipeline substantially increases inference cost compared to GAN-based FFG methods, and the approach is validated only on 128×128 Chinese characters with 8-shot references, leaving open questions about scalability to higher resolutions, other scripts, and fewer reference samples.
