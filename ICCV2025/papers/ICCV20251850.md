## Problem

Creating photo-realistic, animatable 3D head avatars has traditionally required studio-level multi-view capture and expensive per-subject optimization, limiting use to the VFX industry. Existing feed-forward methods either sacrifice 3D consistency for frontal image quality or cannot generalize to unseen expressions. No prior work combines sparse 3D reconstruction, expression generalization, and robustness to inconsistent inputs in a single feed-forward model.

## Input / Output

The model takes 4 RGB head crops (512×512) with known camera parameters and a FLAME expression code as input. Notably, the input images need not be spatially consistent multi-view captures; they can be frames from a monocular video at different timesteps with different expressions. The output is a per-pixel set of 3D Gaussian primitives (position, scale, rotation, color, opacity) whose count is adaptively determined by DUSt3R confidence maps, yielding a complete animatable head renderable from arbitrary novel viewpoints.

## Method

Avat3r adopts a Vision Transformer backbone (following GRM) that patchifies input images enriched with DUSt3R position maps and Sapiens semantic feature maps, then performs dense self-attention across all view tokens to infer 3D structure. The key architectural insight is that simple cross-attention from intermediate feature tokens to a 4-token expression code sequence (projected from a single FLAME code via MLP) is sufficient to model complex 3D facial animation without any explicit deformation field. DUSt3R position maps are injected via skip connections to the predicted Gaussian positions, and its per-pixel confidence maps act as an adaptive foreground mask that controls how many Gaussians are spawned. The model is trained on Ava256 (244 subjects) with photometric losses (L1, SSIM, LPIPS) on novel-view renderings, deliberately using input images from different timesteps to build robustness against real-world capture inconsistencies.

## Results

On the Ava256 hold-out set, Avat3r achieves 20.7 PSNR, 0.71 SSIM, 0.33 LPIPS, 4.8 px AKD, and 0.59 CSIM, outperforming the strongest baseline GPAvatar (19.4 PSNR, 0.69 SSIM, 0.34 LPIPS, 5.3 px AKD, 0.31 CSIM) across all five metrics. On the out-of-domain NeRSemble dataset, Avat3r reaches 20.5 PSNR, 0.75 SSIM, 0.33 LPIPS, 3.7 px AKD, and 0.50 CSIM, again leading all baselines. The largest relative gains are in identity preservation (CSIM: 0.59 vs. 0.31 for GPAvatar on Ava256) and keypoint alignment (AKD: 3.7 vs. 5.7 px for GPAvatar on NeRSemble), indicating more faithful and geometrically accurate avatars.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Avat3r wins on all five metrics against all three baselines on both Ava256 and NeRSemble. Against the strongest baseline GPAvatar, Avat3r gains +1.3 dB PSNR and +0.02 SSIM on Ava256, and a larger +2.9 dB PSNR and +0.08 SSIM on NeRSemble, where GPAvatar was retrained on Ava256 and thus faces a domain shift. The most striking advantage is in identity preservation: Avat3r's CSIM of 0.59 on Ava256 nearly doubles GPAvatar's 0.31, and on NeRSemble Avat3r (0.50) far exceeds GPAvatar (0.07), suggesting GPAvatar struggles to maintain identity under distribution shift. InvertAvatar, a feed-forward GAN-inversion approach, is competitive on PSNR (17.2 on NeRSemble) but lags significantly in SSIM (0.44) and AKD (5.6 px), indicating poor structural alignment. HeadNeRF, an optimization-based NeRF autodecoder, is consistently the weakest method (9.1–9.8 PSNR), confirming that per-subject fitting from only four images is insufficient for high-fidelity reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

Avat3r sits at the intersection of Large Reconstruction Models (LRM, GRM, GS-LRM) and 3D head avatar creation, making the former animatable for the first time. It departs from optimization-based avatar pipelines (GaussianAvatars, URAvatar) that require hours of multi-view capture and test-time fitting, and from 3DMM-anchored approaches that constrain geometry to a fixed template mesh. The architecture inherits its feed-forward per-pixel Gaussian prediction from GRM, enriches inputs with DUSt3R position maps and Sapiens semantic features via skip connections, and introduces cross-attention to FLAME expression codes as the sole animation mechanism. Training on temporally inconsistent input views (enabled by DUSt3R's robustness to misalignment) is a departure from prior LRM work that assumes multi-view consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing avatar creation to four images and a few minutes on a consumer GPU, Avat3r makes high-fidelity 3D head avatars accessible outside studio settings, with demonstrated generalization to out-of-domain inputs like antique busts and AI-generated portraits. Key open questions include the reliance on accurate camera poses at inference, the baked-in lighting that prevents relighting, and the error accumulation in the single-image path that depends on a 3D GAN for lifting. The authors suggest the architecture could serve as a 3D lifting/denoising module within diffusion frameworks, potentially enabling unconditional 3D head generation trained on large-scale monocular video data.
