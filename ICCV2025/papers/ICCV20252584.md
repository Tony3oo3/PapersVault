## Problem

Existing audio-driven talking head methods either operate in 2D (limited to frontal views), use parametric meshes without appearance modeling, or generate 3DGS/NeRF sequences frame-by-frame, suffering from temporal jitter and missing fine-scale details like dynamic wrinkles. No prior work combines a lightweight 3DGS avatar with a transformer sequence model to produce temporally coherent, free-viewpoint, photorealistic animations that capture expression-dependent skin creasing and mouth interiors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252584/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw speech waveform (16 kHz) and a pre-trained personalized 3DGS avatar (built from 16-camera multi-view captures at 3208×2200, 30 fps, with FLAME mesh tracking) as input. It outputs a temporally coherent sequence of animated 3D Gaussian splats with expression-dependent colors, renderable from arbitrary viewpoints in real time. The pipeline is strictly personalized: the avatar must be optimized on the target identity's multi-view data before audio-driven animation can be performed.

## Method

GaussianSpeech operates in two stages. First, it initializes a compact 3DGS avatar (~32k Gaussians) using volume-based top-k pruning, a two-layer color MLP conditioned on FLAME expression codes and view direction for expression-dependent color, ArcFace-based global/patch perceptual losses, a wrinkle-detector feature loss, and four-way subdivision of teeth-region triangles. Second, it trains a transformer-based sequence model that encodes audio via Wav2Vec 2.0, extracts personalized lip and wrinkle features through dedicated transformer encoders, predicts FLAME expressions, and feeds combined lip-expression features into a multi-head transformer decoder (with a Kronecker-delta alignment mask and look-ahead causal mask) to predict per-vertex offsets over the full sequence. Training alternates between optimizing vertex offsets (L2 reconstruction) and refining the color MLP and per-Gaussian latents via differentiable re-rendering with photometric and perceptual losses, yielding temporally smooth, multi-view-consistent animations with dynamic wrinkles.

## Results

On the GaussianSpeech dataset (frontal camera, averaged over all subjects), GaussianSpeech achieves LSE-D of 11.25, PSNR of 24.73 dB, SSIM of 0.9362, and LPIPS of 0.1286 for audio-driven animation, outperforming all baselines on every metric. For avatar reconstruction, the full method reaches PSNR 29.90 dB, SSIM 0.9495, and LPIPS 0.1104 using only ~32k Gaussians, compared to 98k Gaussians for GaussianAvatars at PSNR 26.53 dB. The method also achieves the best lip synchronization (lowest LSE-D) among all compared approaches, including both monocular and multi-view methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaussianSpeech outperforms all nine baselines on every reported metric (LSE-D, PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS). Against the strongest NeRF-based method (SyncTalk, PSNR 18.24), the gap is substantial at +6.49 dB PSNR and −1.25 LSE-D, reflecting the advantage of explicit 3D representation over monocular radiance fields. Against the best 3DGS-based methods (GaussianTalker, PSNR 20.32; TalkingGaussian, PSNR 20.29), GaussianSpeech gains +4.4 dB PSNR and −0.94 LSE-D, attributed to its transformer-based sequence model producing temporally coherent multi-view-consistent animations rather than frame-by-frame synthesis. Against the strongest FLAME-based baseline (Imitator + GaussianAvatars, PSNR 22.83, LSE-D 11.61), the improvement is +1.9 dB PSNR and −0.36 LSE-D, showing that the expression-dependent color MLP and wrinkle regularization add meaningful photorealism beyond what mesh-based animation with a generic 3DGS renderer can achieve. The largest relative gains appear in LPIPS (0.1286 vs. 0.1519 for the best FLAME baseline), indicating perceptually sharper textures and mouth interiors.

## Relation to Prior Work

GaussianSpeech builds directly on GaussianAvatars [39] for its 3DGS-based avatar representation and on 3D Gaussian Splatting [27] for real-time differentiable rendering, but departs from both by introducing an expression-dependent color MLP, volume-based pruning, and wrinkle/perceptual regularizations to handle dynamic skin creasing and mouth interiors. It positions itself against three prior lines: NeRF-based monocular methods (RAD-NeRF, ER-NeRF, SyncTalk), frame-by-frame 3DGS methods (TalkingGaussian, GaussianTalker), and FLAME-mesh animation methods (FaceFormer, CodeTalker, Imitator). The key conceptual departure is the first transformer-based sequence model that animates a 3DGS avatar coherently over time conditioned on audio, rather than generating each frame independently, and the shift from monocular to multi-view consistent synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap for immersive telepresence and AR/VR where free-viewpoint photorealistic talking heads are needed, and the new 16-camera, 415-sequence-per-subject dataset (an order of magnitude larger than prior audio-visual talking-face datasets) will likely become a standard benchmark. The sequence-modeling approach directly tackles the temporal jitter and scaling artifacts that plague frame-by-frame 3DGS methods, and the compact ~32k Gaussian representation enables real-time rendering. Open questions include whether the personalized (per-identity) formulation can be generalized to zero-shot or few-shot settings, whether the 3D face tracking dependency limits robustness in the wild, and whether the approach extends beyond heads to full-body avatars.
