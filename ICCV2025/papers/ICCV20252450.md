## Problem

Existing lifelong person re-identification (LReID) methods assume fully labeled data streams, but real-world annotation is expensive, leaving most data unlabeled. When semi-supervised strategies are naively combined with LReID, noisy pseudo-labels propagate errors that degrade both new-domain adaptation and long-term knowledge accumulation. This paper pioneers the Semi-Supervised LReID (Semi-LReID) setting and introduces a dedicated benchmark to address this gap.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequential stream of T person-image datasets, each containing a labeled subset and an unlabeled subset with disjoint identity labels across steps; label rates of 10%, 20%, and 50% are evaluated. During training, the model produces pseudo-label assignments for unlabeled samples via prototype-based scoring and dual-model clustering consistency. At inference, a single ResNet-50 feature vector per image is extracted for standard person retrieval (mAP, Rank-1).

## Method

SPRED establishes a self-reinforcing cycle between two modules: (1) Self-Reinforcing Prototype Evolution (SPE) maintains learnable identity prototypes that are updated jointly with the model via a prototype-oriented softmax loss plus triplet loss, and generates pseudo-labels using a top-2 nearest-prototype scoring scheme that discards ambiguous predictions; (2) Dual-Knowledge Cooperation-driven Pseudo-label Purification (DKCP) clusters unlabeled samples with both the current model (new knowledge) and the previous model after distribution alignment via a DANet (old knowledge), then retains only pseudo-labels whose pseudo-label-set and cluster-set overlap exceeds a threshold, taking the union of both purified sets. A prototype-structure KL-divergence loss distills the old model's affinity distribution over prototypes into the new model to prevent catastrophic forgetting. The purified pseudo-labels feed back into prototype learning in the next epoch, iteratively improving label quality and prototype geometry over the long-term stream.

## Results

Under the default 10% label rate, SPRED achieves 43.2/54.4 Seen-Avg mAP/R@1 and 51.7/44.6 UnSeen-Avg mAP/R@1, outperforming the best LReID baseline LSTKC (27.7/37.1) by 15.5/17.3 points and the best SSL+LReID baseline HDC (27.5/37.3) by 15.7/17.1 points on Seen-Avg. With the ShrinkM augmentation strategy (SPRED†), performance further improves to 44.8/58.4 Seen-Avg and 55.9/48.7 UnSeen-Avg. At 50% label rate, SPRED† reaches 51.3/63.8 Seen-Avg and 60.7/53.9 UnSeen-Avg, surpassing all baselines by at least 3.8/2.6 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPRED consistently outperforms all three LReID baselines (PatchKD, DKP, LSTKC) across all label rates, with the largest gap at 10% where it surpasses LSTKC by 15.5/17.3 Seen-Avg mAP/R@1. Against the five SSL+LReID baselines, SPRED wins on Seen-Avg at 20% and 10% label rates by at least 8.5/5.7 and 10.9/9.0 points respectively; at 50% it trails HDC by 0.4 R@1 (61.2 vs. 60.8) but leads in mAP (50.1 vs. 47.5). With the ShrinkM augmentation (SPRED‡), the method dominates all baselines at every label rate, achieving 12.5/13.0 Seen-Avg mAP/R@1 gains over HDC at 10%. On UnSeen-Avg, SPRED‡ leads HDC by 7.9/7.5 at 10% and 2.5/3.3 at 50%, confirming superior generalization. The advantage grows as label scarcity increases, indicating the dual-knowledge cooperation and prototype evolution mechanisms are most impactful when labeled data is limited.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of lifelong person re-identification (LReID) and semi-supervised learning, building directly on LSTKC's prototype-based knowledge consolidation framework as its base model and on Gu et al.'s distribution alignment network for bridging domain shifts between training steps. It departs from existing SSLL methods that rely on replay buffers (impractical for privacy-sensitive human images) and from SSL+LReID approaches that suffer from noisy pseudo-label propagation, by introducing a self-reinforcing cycle between learnable identity prototypes and dual-knowledge (new + old model) clustering-based pseudo-label purification. The key conceptual departure is treating pseudo-label quality as a co-evolving quantity with prototype learning rather than a one-shot prediction, and showing that new and old model clustering results are complementary (union outperforms intersection).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: real-world ReID systems face limited annotation budgets, yet existing lifelong ReID methods degrade severely under low label rates. The introduced 12-dataset Semi-LReID benchmark provides the community with a standardized evaluation protocol for this underexplored setting. Open questions include whether the prototype-based pseudo-label purification generalizes beyond ReID to other identity-level discrimination tasks (e.g., vehicle re-identification), how the method scales as the number of training domains grows (memory for maintaining old model + DANet), and whether the self-reinforcing cycle can be accelerated to reduce the number of epochs needed for convergence.
