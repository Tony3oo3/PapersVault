## Problem

Continual fine-tuning of LLMs on sequential domain-specific tasks suffers from catastrophic forgetting, where previously learned knowledge and general capabilities degrade. Existing remedies either replay historical data (incurring high computational cost and privacy risks) or share a single parameter-efficient module across all tasks, causing interference that limits new knowledge absorption. The paper asks whether one can achieve replay-free, interference-free continual learning of LLMs with a theoretical non-forgetting guarantee.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text sequence x of length t, which first passes through the frozen lower Lf transformer layers to produce a hidden-state matrix h_{≤Lf}(x) ∈ R^{t×d}. This is mean-pooled over the sequence dimension and projected via a frozen Gaussian-initialized linear layer with ReLU into an expanded space of dimension E (E > d, typically 10k). The expanded feature is fed to the analytic router to yield a task-affiliation probability vector, and the selected LoRA adapter in the upper layers produces the next-token prediction y_{t+1}; generation iterates until an EOS token is emitted.

## Method

Any-SSR partitions the LLM into a frozen lower segment (general feature extractor) and an upper segment where each task gets its own independent LoRA adapter, eliminating parameter-level interference between tasks. A ridge-regression router maps expanded low-layer features to task IDs, and its weights are obtained in closed form (Eq. 11) rather than by gradient descent. When a new task arrives, the router is updated recursively via the Woodbury matrix identity using only the stored AutoCor matrix R_k and CrossCor Q_k plus the new task's features and labels, making the update algebraically equivalent to re-solving the joint ridge problem over all tasks seen so far. This recursive least-squares update provides a provable non-forgetting property: the router's solution after k+1 tasks is identical to what joint training on all k+1 tasks would produce, without ever re-accessing historical data.

## Results

On the TRACE benchmark with LLaMA-2-7B-Chat, Any-SSR achieves an Overall Performance (OP) of 55.69% with a Backward Transfer (BWT) of 0.00 across both task orders, making it the only method to exhibit zero forgetting. This surpasses the best exemplar-free baseline (LoRAMoE, 48.54% OP) by 7.15 points and the best replay-based method (SEEKR 1%, 54.99% OP) by 0.70 points, while remaining within 3.69 points of the multi-task learning upper bound (59.38%). Notably, Any-SSR produces identical results regardless of task order, demonstrating order invariance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Any-SSR decisively outperforms all ten exemplar-free baselines on both OP and BWT. Against the strongest exemplar-free competitor LoRAMoE (48.54% OP, -4.27 BWT), Any-SSR gains 7.15 points in OP and eliminates all negative transfer (0.00 vs. -4.27 BWT). Compared to O-LoRA (44.64%, -4.20) and EWC (48.20%, -9.48), the margins are even larger, with Any-SSR achieving 11+ points higher OP. Against replay-based methods, Any-SSR surpasses the state-of-the-art SEEKR (54.99%, -2.61) by 0.70 points in OP while achieving perfectly zero BWT versus SEEKR's -2.61, and outperforms DER++ (49.22%, -8.32) and Replay (48.47%, -9.69) by 6+ points in OP. The only method exceeding Any-SSR is the multi-task learning upper bound (59.38%), which requires simultaneous access to all task data. A notable limitation is that Any-SSR's general ability (GA) after CL drops by 1.26 points (46.51 vs. 47.77 baseline), slightly worse than SEEKR's -1.05 drop but far better than SeqFT's -4.27.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the analytic learning paradigm (ACIL, GKEAL, DS-AL, F-OAL) from class-incremental classifier learning to LLM continual fine-tuning, repurposing the recursive least-squares closed-form solution as a task-routing mechanism rather than a classifier. It combines this with per-task LoRA adapters in a MoE-style architecture, departing from prior MoE-routing work (LoRAMoE) that trains routers via gradient descent and thus suffers catastrophic forgetting. The hierarchical disentanglement—frozen lower layers for shared semantics, task-specific LoRA in upper layers—builds on the parameter-efficient tuning literature but introduces the key structural guarantee that task subspaces are fully disjoint, eliminating interference by construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero backward transfer (BWT = 0.00) and order invariance (identical OP regardless of task sequence) are strong structural properties that no gradient-based baseline achieves, offering a theoretically grounded, replay-free recipe for LLM continual learning. However, the absolute performance gap over the best replay-based baseline (SEEKR) is modest (~0.7% OP), and the paper acknowledges routing ambiguity between semantically similar tasks (e.g., NumGLUE-cm vs. NumGLUE-ds) that could be problematic in safety-critical domains. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 8 tasks, how the frozen feature-expansion layer interacts with domain shift, and whether the 1% router-training data requirement can be further reduced.
