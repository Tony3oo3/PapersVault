## Problem

Existing large-scene 3D Gaussian Splatting methods partition scenes using occlusion-agnostic strategies (grid-based or camera-position-based), producing regions with severe internal occlusions where training cameras have weak correlations and low average contribution to reconstruction. This is particularly problematic for ground-level captures in indoor or urban environments where walls and buildings create frequent occlusions, leading to poor reconstruction quality and inefficient use of training resources.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250019/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes posed training images with SfM-derived camera poses and matched feature point correspondences between camera pairs. The SfM results directly provide the edge weights (number of matched features) for the view graph construction. The output is a merged 3D Gaussian splatting model of the complete scene plus pre-computed per-region visibility masks that enable accelerated rendering at 1080p. Training uses 90,000 iterations with densification between iterations 1,500 and 45,000, and incorporates VastGaussian's appearance modeling to handle appearance variations across captures.

## Method

OccluGaussian introduces an occlusion-aware scene division strategy that constructs an attributed view graph where nodes are cameras (with positional encoding features) and edges encode co-visibility weighted by SfM matched feature counts. An attributed graph clustering algorithm (graph convolution followed by spectral clustering) partitions cameras into regions that align with physical scene layout, with an adaptive procedure to determine the optimal number of clusters. Each region is reconstructed individually using three camera sets (base, extended, and border) to balance supervision and boundary constraints, then regions are merged by clipping Gaussians to their region boundaries. For rendering acceleration, the method pre-computes per-region visibility masks by rasterizing all training views and recording which Gaussians contribute above a threshold, then at render time only processes Gaussians visible from the region containing the viewpoint, further subdividing regions into interior and border sub-regions for tighter culling.

## Results

On the OccluScene3D dataset (three campus scenes with heavy occlusions), OccluGaussian achieves PSNR of 25.81/25.25/25.33 dB and LPIPS of 0.094/0.100/0.083 on GALLERY/CANTEEN/CLASSBUILDING, outperforming the strongest baseline VastGaussian by 0.7–1.3 dB in PSNR while also rendering 30–40% faster (289–340 FPS vs. 211–270 FPS). On the Zip-NeRF dataset, OccluGaussian reaches 28.63 dB PSNR and 0.281 LPIPS, surpassing the best prior method (Zip-NeRF at 27.37 dB). On occlusion-free aerial scenes (Mill-19, UrbanScene3D), OccluGaussian still leads with 25.97/25.75 dB PSNR, confirming generality beyond occluded environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OccluGaussian outperforms all 3DGS-based large-scene baselines on reconstruction quality: it beats VastGaussian [28] by 0.7–1.3 dB PSNR (e.g., 25.81 vs. 25.09 on GALLERY) while also rendering ~30% faster (289 vs. 215 FPS), and dominates CityGaussian [32] by 3.8–4.9 dB PSNR with 2–5× higher FPS. Against Hierarchical-GS [22], OccluGaussian gains 3.0–2.5 dB PSNR with comparable FPS. The only baseline faster in rendering is vanilla 3DGS [21] (345–526 FPS), but it trails OccluGaussian by 4.4–5.9 dB PSNR due to its inability to handle large scenes without partitioning. On Zip-NeRF, OccluGaussian (28.63 dB) surpasses all NeRF-based methods including Zip-NeRF [4] (27.37 dB), SMERF [10] (27.28 dB), RadSplat [38] (26.17 dB), and MERF [41] (23.49 dB). On occlusion-free aerial datasets, OccluGaussian leads VastGaussian [28] by 0.76 dB on Mill-19 and 0.06 dB on UrbanScene3D, and outperforms DOGS [6] by 1.71 dB on Mill-19. In the clustering ablation (Table 4), the proposed attributed graph clustering beats METIS [20] (25.46 vs. 25.02 PSNR) and DBSCAN [11] (25.46 vs. 25.00 PSNR), confirming that combining position features with co-visibility edges is critical for occlusion-aware partitioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) framework for large scene reconstruction by replacing the occlusion-agnostic scene division strategies used in VastGaussian, CityGaussian, and Hierarchical-GS with an occlusion-aware attributed graph clustering approach. It builds on the graph convolution and spectral clustering machinery from Zhang et al. (KDE 2023), applying it to a view graph whose edges encode SfM co-visibility and whose node features encode camera positions. The region-based rendering technique departs from the level-of-detail (LoD) pruning used by prior 3DGS acceleration methods (Octree-GS, CityGaussian, Hierarchical-3DGS) by instead pre-computing per-region visibility masks to cull occluded Gaussians, a strategy enabled by the occlusion-aware partitioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: most large-scene 3DGS methods are validated on aerial or open-space captures where occlusions are rare, but ground-level indoor and urban scenes with walls and buildings remain poorly served. The introduced OccluScene3D dataset fills this benchmark gap, and the region-based rendering offers a simple, quality-preserving speedup that could be adopted by other 3DGS pipelines. A key open question is scalability: the fixed initial clustering number K and the per-region visibility mask computation may not generalize to scenes with hundreds of regions, a limitation the authors acknowledge but leave for future work.
