## Problem

Diffusion models scale quadratically with resolution, making 4K image generation take over 100 seconds, yet the second stage of the two-stage LDM pipeline—decoding fixed-size latents to pixels—has been largely neglected. The paper argues that enhancing this decoder is a more effective route to efficient arbitrary-resolution generation than modifying the diffusion model itself, since the decoder requires only a single forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251507/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

InfGen takes a fixed-size continuous latent (e.g., 4×64×64 produced by a frozen VAE encoder at 8× downsampling) and a target resolution (h, w) as input, and outputs a single RGB image at that resolution in one forward pass. The target resolution is unconstrained at inference time; for ultra-high resolutions beyond the training range (up to 4K+), a training-free iterative extrapolation loop re-encodes each generated image back into a latent and feeds it through the generator again with an additional scaling factor.

## Method

InfGen replaces the VAE decoder with a transformer-based generator that treats the fixed latent as keys/values and a resolution-shaped mask token grid (⌈h/8⌉ × ⌈w/8⌉) as queries in multi-head cross-attention, enabling one-step decoding to any size. A key architectural innovation is the Implicit Neural Positional Embedding (INPE), which standardizes 2D token coordinates onto a unit sphere, applies random Fourier features, and passes them through a small implicit neural network to produce dynamic positional encodings that work for arbitrary token counts without fixed-size lookup tables. Training uses a composite loss of L1 reconstruction, LPIPS perceptual, and PatchGAN adversarial terms on 10M high-resolution LAION images, with the VAE encoder kept frozen. For resolutions beyond the training range, a training-free iterative extrapolation scheme repeatedly encodes the output image back into a latent and regenerates at a higher scale factor, achieving 4K output in under 10 seconds.

## Results

On the primary text-to-image benchmark (LAION captions, A100 GPU), InfGen+SDXL-B-1 achieves the best FID_P of 35.14 at 1024×1024 and 96.41 at 2048×2048, while generating a 2048×2048 image in ~7.3 seconds—roughly 4× faster than UltraPixel (20 s) and 35× faster than Inf-DiT (255 s). When plugged into existing diffusion models, InfGen delivers average FID improvements of 8–42% across resolutions, with the largest gains at 3072×3072 (41% on DiT-XL/2, 44% on SD1.5). For reconstruction, InfGen matches or exceeds SD-VAE and SDXL-VAE on PSNR/SSIM at same-resolution settings and outperforms them when the output resolution exceeds the input (e.g., 256→512).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three dedicated high-resolution generation methods in Table 4, InfGen+SDXL-B-1 wins on FID_P at both 1024² (35.14 vs. 48.37 UltraPixel, 48.48 Inf-DiT, 55.36 ScaleCrafter) and 2048² (96.41 vs. 127.26, 142.05, 144.61), while also achieving the lowest latency (7.3 s vs. 20 s, 255 s, 97 s at 2048²). On reconstruction (Table 2), InfGen is slightly behind SDXL-VAE (rFID 1.07 vs. 0.68) and SD-VAE (1.07 vs. 0.74) at same-resolution 256→256, but surpasses both when the output resolution exceeds the input (256→512: rFID 1.15 vs. 1.43 for SD-VAE) and clearly outperforms VQGAN (rFID 1.07 vs. 1.19). As a plug-in decoder, InfGen improves every tested base model: DiT-XL/2 FID drops 41% at 3072², SiT-XL/2 drops 36% at 1024², MDTv2 drops 33% at 1024², FiTv2-XL/2 drops 42% at 3072², and SD1.5 drops 44% at 3072². UltraPixel remains competitive on Precision at 2048² (0.61 vs. 0.59) but is outperformed on FID_P and latency; ScaleCrafter and Inf-DiT are dominated on both quality and speed across all tested resolutions.

## Relation to Prior Work

InfGen reframes arbitrary-resolution image generation by shifting the burden from the diffusion model to the decoder: rather than modifying the diffusion process to handle variable-sized latents (as ScaleCrafter, Inf-DiT, and UltraPixel do), it treats the diffusion model as a fixed-size content generator and replaces the VAE decoder with a one-step transformer-based generator that decodes the compact latent into any target resolution. This builds directly on the VAE/LDM two-stage paradigm (Kingma & Welling; Rombach et al.) and borrows the implicit neural representation idea (Sitzmann et al.) to design a resolution-agnostic positional encoding (INPE) that enables cross-attention between fixed-size latent tokens and dynamically-sized mask tokens. The key conceptual departure is that the decoder is no longer a mere reconstructor but a generative model that must synthesize missing high-frequency detail, a distinction the paper draws explicitly against standard VAE decoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of InfGen—requiring no retraining of the diffusion model and working across SD1.5, SDXL, DiT, SiT, and FiT—makes it a low-friction upgrade path for the entire latent-diffusion ecosystem, with a 10× latency reduction over the prior fastest method (UltraPixel) for 4K generation. Open questions include how far the iterative extrapolation scheme can be pushed beyond 4K without quality degradation, whether the approach transfers to other latent spaces (e.g., video or 3D), and whether the one-step generator can match the fidelity of multi-step denoising at extreme resolutions. The method's dependence on 10M+ high-resolution training images and 15 days of A100 training is a non-trivial barrier, though the resulting model is small and fast at inference.
