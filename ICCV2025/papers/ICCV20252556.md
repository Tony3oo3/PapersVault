## Problem

Open-vocabulary object detection (OvOD) for X-ray security imaging remains largely unexplored: off-the-shelf RGB OvOD detectors suffer severe performance degradation on X-ray scans because the modality gap disrupts the text-to-visual feature alignment that these models rely on, and retraining is impractical given the scarcity of large-scale annotated X-ray datasets. Prior work has managed to generalize to at most four unseen classes, highlighting the difficulty of the task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252556/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, the system receives a single X-ray image and a user-specified vocabulary of arbitrary object categories (e.g., 'Power bank', 'violin'), and outputs bounding boxes with class labels for all detected instances. No X-ray detection annotations or in-domain training data are required; the only offline prerequisite is a small hold-out set of in-house X-ray images (disjoint from the test split) used to build material appearance statistics, plus web-retrieved RGB images for classes absent from the in-house vocabulary. The method is fully plug-and-play, replacing only the text-based classifier head of any existing RGB OvOD detector while leaving the backbone, RPN, and box regressor untouched.

## Method

RAXO is a training-free adaptation framework built on the insight that intra-modal (visual-to-visual) feature distances remain reliable across the RGB-to-X-ray modality shift, even when inter-modal (text-to-visual) alignment breaks down. It constructs per-class visual descriptors via a dual-source retrieval pipeline: in-house X-ray images are matched by class name, while missing categories are filled by web-retrieved RGB images that are converted to X-ray style through a material-transfer mechanism (an LLM links each class to a material, and the material's average X-ray appearance—computed from in-house segmented samples—is composited onto the RGB object mask). Each descriptor is an ensemble of a class-level average positive prototype plus individual per-sample positive prototypes (foreground-only, via SAM 2 segmentation and DINOv2 ViT-B/14 features), alongside a global background descriptor built from negative prototypes. At inference, the text-based classifier is replaced by a visual classifier that scores proposals via cosine similarity against all prototypes, and a Descriptor Consistency Criterion (DCC) suppresses proposals whose margin over other classes falls below a threshold σ=0.15.

## Results

Under the Cross-Modality Transfer Evaluation (CMTE) protocol, RAXO achieves an average AP improvement of up to 17.0 points over the strongest baseline (GroundingDINO: 10.2 → 27.2 AP) when using fully in-domain X-ray samples (100/0 gallery setting). Even without any in-domain data (0/100 setting, all samples web-retrieved and material-transferred), RAXO still yields a consistent average gain of +1.6 to +2.6 AP across all four baseline detectors. On the DET-COMPASS benchmark specifically, RAXO+G-DINO reaches 47.9 AP (100/0), a 34.5-point improvement over the RGB-only baseline. Against the only prior supervised X-ray OvOD method (OVXD), RAXO+G-DINO achieves 43.5 AP50 versus OVXD's 21.0 AP50 on four unseen categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAXO consistently outperforms all four RGB-trained OvOD baselines (GroundingDINO, VLDet, Detic, CoDet) across every dataset and gallery composition, with the largest gains (↑17.0, ↑14.4, ↑12.9, ↑14.3 avg AP respectively) in the 100/0 in-domain setting. The improvement scales with baseline strength: G-DINO benefits most (↑9.4 avg across all settings) while CoDet, the weakest baseline, gains ↑8.4 avg. Against OVXD [16], the only prior supervised X-ray OvOD method, RAXO+G-DINO achieves 43.5 AP50 versus OVXD's 21.0 AP50 on four unseen categories (Scissors, Wrench, Battery, Pliers) on PIXray — more than doubling performance without any training. Even in the most constrained 0/100 setting (no in-domain X-ray data, relying solely on web-retrieved RGB images with material transfer), RAXO still improves every baseline by 1.6–2.6 AP, confirming robustness to data scarcity. Notably, RAXO+BARON (22.0 AP50) also surpasses OVXD (21.0 AP50) using the same underlying detector, isolating the gain to the training-free adaptation rather than detector choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes off-the-shelf RGB open-vocabulary detectors (GroundingDINO, Detic, VLDet, CoDet) for X-ray imaging by replacing their text-based classifiers with visual prototype classifiers, sidestepping the text-visual alignment that breaks across the RGB-to-X-ray modality gap. Conceptually, it draws on prototype-based classification and the observation that intra-modal visual distances remain reliable even when inter-modal (text-to-visual) alignment fails. The only prior X-ray OvOD work (OVXD) required supervised fine-tuning and generalized to just four unseen classes; RAXO departs by being entirely training-free and leveraging web-retrieved RGB images through a novel LLM-guided material-transfer mechanism to synthesize X-ray-style samples. The DET-COMPASS benchmark extends existing X-ray detection datasets (which top out at 20 classes) by an order of magnitude to 370 classes with pixel-aligned RGB counterparts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work opens a practically critical but previously unexplored task—open-vocabulary detection in security X-ray screening—where data scarcity and modality gaps have blocked direct transfer of RGB OvOD advances. The training-free nature of RAXO is particularly impactful in a domain where large-scale annotated X-ray data is prohibitively expensive to obtain, and the DET-COMPASS benchmark (370 classes, multimodal, pixel-aligned) fills a significant evaluation gap. Open questions include how well the material-transfer mechanism generalizes to materials absent from the in-house database, whether the approach scales to more complex multi-object scenes, and whether the reliance on SAM 2 segmentation and web retrieval quality introduces deployment fragility. The CMTE protocol also sets a useful template for evaluating cross-modality transfer more broadly.
