## Problem

In autonomous driving perception, detection and forecasting are typically treated as separate sequential tasks, with motion forecasts used only for planning and then discarded. Existing joint methods rely on explicit tracking to bridge detection and forecasting, introducing error propagation from incorrect trajectory association and bottlenecking learning. This paper asks whether bidirectional information flow—feeding forecasts back into detection as well as feeding detections into forecasting—can improve both tasks simultaneously without tracking.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252454/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 6 surround-view camera images per timestep (256×704, 512×1408, or 900×1600 depending on backbone) and an optional HD map encoded as a directed lane graph. It produces 428 3D bounding boxes (3D position, size, heading, class, confidence) and 30,816 multi-modal forecast waypoints (428 detections × 6 trajectory modes × 12 future frames of 2D positions). A streaming memory queue of shape K×T_h×T_f×D (128×4×12×256) persists across timesteps, holding the top-K detection and forecast queries so that past forecasts serve as priors for future frames.

## Method

ForeSight introduces a joint streaming memory queue that stores both detection and forecast queries across a 4-frame history and 12-frame future horizon, enabling bidirectional propagation: forecast queries are fed back into the detection transformer as temporal queries, while detection outputs initialize forecast queries. The forecast-aware detection transformer concatenates N=300 anchor-initialized detection queries with K=128 temporal queries, then applies self-attention, cross-attention to all historical temporal queries, and cross-attention to image feature tokens. The joint streaming forecast transformer takes detection anchors combined with k-means-clustered forecast anchors (plus a temporal anchor from the previous highest-confidence forecast), self-attends, cross-attends to past forecast queries, optionally cross-attends to 16 nearest map nodes, and cross-attends to detection queries before decoding multi-modal waypoints. The entire pipeline is tracking-free—no explicit object association is needed—and is trained end-to-end with a multi-task loss combining focal/L1 detection losses, ADE/FDE/classification forecasting losses, and an auxiliary 2D ROI loss.

## Results

On the NuScenes validation set, ForeSight achieves state-of-the-art end-to-end forecasting performance with an EPA of 54.9% (ResNet-101), surpassing the previous best joint method UniAD by 9.3 percentage points. For detection, ForeSight attains the highest mAP of 0.502 and NDS of 0.589 (ResNet-101), exceeding the best sparse-query baseline StreamPETR by 2.1% in mAP. In forecasting, ForeSight achieves a minADE of 0.689 m (ResNet-101), substantially outperforming StreamPETRcv (1.64 m) and matching or beating UniAD (0.708 m) while simultaneously delivering superior detection accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ForeSight outperforms all 15 compared baselines on the joint detection-forecasting benchmark. Against the strongest end-to-end competitor UniAD (R101), ForeSight improves EPA from 45.6% to 54.9% (+9.3 pp) while also achieving higher mAP (0.502 vs. 0.382) and lower minADE (0.689 vs. 0.708 m), demonstrating that tracking-free bidirectional propagation avoids the information bottleneck of UniAD's tracking-based query assignment. Compared to StreamPETRcv (constant-velocity forecast), ForeSight nearly halves minADE (0.689 vs. 1.64 m at R101) and gains 9.6 pp in EPA, confirming that learned multi-modal forecasting vastly outperforms simple kinematic extrapolation. Against VAD under the restricted 4-class/3s-horizon setting, ForeSight achieves EPA 60.6% vs. 51.6% and mAP 0.713 vs. 0.354, showing the advantage persists even when the task is simplified. For pure detection (Table 1), ForeSight (R101) leads StreamPETR by 1.6% mAP (0.502 vs. 0.486) and SOLOFusion by 1.9% mAP (0.502 vs. 0.483), while maintaining competitive FPS (5.1 vs. 6.4). The ablation further shows that removing the tracking-free design (switching to tracking-based forecasting) degrades minADE by 1.78 m, and removing bidirectional propagation (using only forward detection-query propagation) costs 2.3% mAP, validating both architectural choices.

## Relation to Prior Work

ForeSight extends the sparse-query temporal detection lineage (PETR, StreamPETR) by appending a forecasting transformer that shares the same query memory, and adapts the PGP lane-graph encoder from an agent-level to a scene-level component. Its key conceptual departure from prior joint detection-forecasting methods (UniAD, ViP3D, PnPNet) is the elimination of explicit tracking: instead of relying on ground-truth track assignments to bridge detection and forecasting, ForeSight uses a streaming FIFO memory queue that holds both past detections and multi-hypothesis forecasts, enabling bidirectional query propagation where forecast outputs feed back as priors for subsequent detection. This contrasts with the conventional autonomy stack where forecasting is a downstream consumer of detection and tracking, and with earlier works (FaF, FrameFusion) that used forecast-to-present averaging without joint training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tracking-free design addresses a practical bottleneck: prior end-to-end methods that enforce tracking via ground-truth query assignments introduce information bottlenecks and degrade detection accuracy, while the ablation here shows detection-based forecasting outperforms tracking-based forecasting by 1.78 m minADE under real perception errors. The bidirectional propagation (forecasts informing detection) is a simple but effective architectural choice that yields a 2.3% mAP gain over unidirectional propagation, suggesting that motion priors are underexploited in current temporal detectors. Open questions include whether the streaming memory approach scales to longer horizons or denser traffic, whether the EPA advantage over UniAD holds under strictly matched configurations (the paper acknowledges differing perception ranges, horizons, and class sets), and how the method generalizes beyond the single NuScenes benchmark.
