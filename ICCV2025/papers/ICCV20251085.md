## Problem

Stereo matching faces a fundamental architectural trade-off: global matching methods model long-range correspondences well but are computationally prohibitive at high resolutions, while cost-volume-based iterative refinement methods are efficient yet inherently local, struggling with repetitive textures and large discontinuities. No prior work has demonstrated that a global matching architecture can scale in both input resolution and model capacity while achieving state-of-the-art accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251085/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a pair of left and right RGB images at arbitrary resolution (evaluated up to 2448×2048) and jointly produces a disparity map, an occlusion map, and a confidence map. Matching is performed at 1/4 resolution via an all-pairs correlation volume, but unlike cost-volume methods, there is no hard disparity range cap—the Sinkhorn transport plan naturally handles the full epipolar line. The confidence map is not a separate regression target but is derived directly from the probability mass within a local window around the peak of the transport plan, enabling principled uncertainty filtering.

## Method

S²M² replaces the standard single-resolution Transformer with a Multi-Resolution Transformer (MRT) that processes an FPN pyramid in parallel, applying 1D horizontal attention at higher resolutions and full 2D self-attention only at the coarsest level (F32) to balance epipolar alignment with global context. An Adaptive Gated Fusion Layer (AGFL) provides learned cross-scale gating within each MRT block, enabling stable training of deep multi-resolution stacks. Global matching is formulated as entropy-regularized optimal transport (Sinkhorn) over the all-pairs correlation volume, yielding a soft, bidirectionally consistent transport plan from which disparity, occlusion, and confidence are all derived. A novel Probabilistic Mode Concentration (PMC) loss regularizes the transport plan to concentrate probability mass within valid disparity candidate neighborhoods, improving feature discriminability without additional parameters. A hybrid refinement stage combines confidence-aware global disparity propagation with only three RAFT-style local iterations, leveraging the strong initial estimate from the global matching to avoid the many iterations typical of prior iterative methods.

## Results

On Middlebury v3, S²M²-XL(+2Mtr) achieves a new SOTA with Non-Occlusion EPE of 0.69, Bad-1.0 of 3.57, and Bad-2.0 of 1.15, outperforming FoundationStereo (EPE 0.78, Bad-1.0 4.39, Bad-2.0 1.84) by 11–26% on the most stringent metrics. On ETH3D, S²M²-XL reaches Non-Occlusion Bad-0.5 of 0.93 and Bad-2.0 of 0.06, surpassing FoundationStereo (1.26 and 0.08) and all prior methods. On the authors' synthetic benchmark (2448×2048, large disparity), S²M²-XL achieves Non-Occlusion EPE of 2.30, more than 2× better than FoundationStereo (4.60) and 3× better than IGEV (7.03), while using only 381M parameters vs. 375M for FoundationStereo at 56.94 TFLOPs vs. 124.65 TFLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S²M²-XL(+2Mtr) establishes SOTA on both Middlebury v3 and ETH3D, beating the strongest prior method FoundationStereo [40] by 11% on Middlebury Bad-1.0 (3.57 vs 4.39) and 26% on Bad-2.0 (1.15 vs 1.84), while also winning on ETH3D Bad-0.5 (0.93 vs 1.26) and Bad-2.0 (0.06 vs 0.08). Against cost-volume iterative methods, the margins are larger: IGEV [42] scores 9.41 on Middlebury Bad-1.0 vs S²M²-XL's 3.57, and CREStereo [18] achieves 8.25 vs 3.57. On the authors' synthetic large-disparity benchmark, S²M²-XL (EPE 2.30) more than doubles FoundationStereo (4.60) and triples IGEV (7.03), while using roughly half the FLOPs of FoundationStereo (56.94 vs 124.65 TFLOPs). On KITTI 2015, S²M²-XL without fine-tuning (D1-all 3.12) is nearly identical to FoundationStereo without fine-tuning (3.08), but both are far worse than fine-tuned Sel-IGEV (1.44); the authors argue this gap reflects ground-truth noise rather than true generalization differences. The smaller S²M²-L(+1Mtr) variant already outperforms all cost-volume baselines (IGEV, CREStereo, RAFT-Stereo) on Middlebury v3, demonstrating that the architecture's gains are not solely from scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

S²M² departs from the dominant cost-volume iterative refinement paradigm (RAFT-Stereo, CREStereo, IGEV, Sel-IGEV) by reviving the global matching approach that was previously rendered infeasible by quadratic attention costs and prohibitive memory. It builds on entropy-regularized optimal transport (Sinkhorn) as the core matching mechanism and on the authors' own prior confidence-aware aggregation strategy, while introducing a multi-resolution Transformer with hybrid 1D/2D attention and an Adaptive Gated Fusion Layer to make global matching scalable in both input resolution and model capacity. The Probabilistic Mode Concentration loss is a novel training signal that has no direct predecessor in the stereo matching literature, drawing instead on the idea of regularizing probability distributions toward valid candidate regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to conclusively demonstrate that a global matching architecture can scale to high resolutions and large disparity ranges while matching or exceeding the accuracy of iterative refinement methods, challenging the field's default assumption that cost volumes are the only practical path. The critical re-evaluation of KITTI 2015—showing that top leaderboard scores are heavily driven by dataset-specific fine-tuning to noisy LiDAR annotations rather than genuine generalization—has broad implications for how the community interprets benchmark results. Open questions include whether the optimal-transport matching step can be further accelerated for real-time deployment, and whether the PMC loss generalizes to other dense prediction tasks beyond stereo.
