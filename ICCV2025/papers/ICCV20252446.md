## Problem

SAM's zero-shot segmentation degrades significantly on camouflaged object detection (COD) because camouflaged objects exhibit low contrast and texture blending that fall outside SAM's training distribution. Existing SAM adaptation methods (e.g., SAM-Adapter, DSAM) handle only single-modality inputs or lack mechanisms to exploit the complementary structural information in depth images, leaving a gap in how to efficiently fine-tune SAM for RGB-D COD while preserving its generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252446/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired RGB images and source-free (estimated) depth images, both resized to 1024×1024, together with a perturbed ground-truth box prompt. The depth images are not from depth sensors but are predicted by a pre-trained depth estimator, so they may contain noise. The output is a binary segmentation mask obtained by averaging the RGB-stream and depth-stream predictions and thresholding at 0.5; in RGB-only mode, only the RGB prediction is used.

## Method

SAM-DSA embeds two lightweight adapters (one per modality) in parallel into the frozen SAM ViT image encoder's attention blocks. Each adapter performs a down-projection → ReLU → up-projection residual path augmented with Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT, db2 filter) to extract high-frequency details specific to each modality—semantic texture from RGB and structural geometry from depth. Bidirectional knowledge distillation addresses the lack of direct interaction between the two streams: a model distiller transfers PVTv2 features into the RGB adapter via KL divergence, and a modal distiller guides the noisy depth adapter using the RGB adapter as teacher. A mixed prompt embedding module concatenates PVT embeddings with the summed RGB+Depth adapter embeddings and applies depthwise convolution to refine the dense box prompt before feeding it to two parallel mask decoders. Training jointly optimizes DiceCELoss on both streams and the bidirectional KD loss (λ=0.9), with only the adapters, mask decoders, and a small bias-correction module being trainable.

## Results

On the four COD benchmarks, SAM-DSA (RGB-D) achieves M=0.044, Fx=0.884, Fω=0.849, Sm=0.875, Ex=0.953, αE=0.952 on CAMO; M=0.029, Fx=0.887, Fω=0.850, Sm=0.893, Ex=0.969, αE=0.961 on CHAMELEON; M=0.022, Fx=0.873, Fω=0.827, Sm=0.887, Ex=0.972, αE=0.948 on COD10K; and M=0.029, Fx=0.898, Fω=0.866, Sm=0.896, Ex=0.972, αE=0.959 on NC4K. In the RGB-only setting (SAM-DSAA), it achieves M=0.047 on CAMO and M=0.023 on COD10K, outperforming all prior RGB-based methods by 2–6% on average. Compared to vanilla SAM (M=0.132 on CAMO), the improvement is over 3× in absolute error reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAM-DSA (RGB-D) outperforms all 8 RGB-D baselines (CDINet, DCF, CMINet, SPNet, DCMF, SPSN, PopNet, DSAM) across all four datasets and all six metrics. The closest RGB-D competitor, DSAM [65], is surpassed by a margin of 0.017 in M and 0.050 in Fx on CAMO (0.061→0.044, 0.834→0.884), and by 0.011 in M on COD10K (0.033→0.022). In the RGB-only setting, SAM-DSAA beats the strongest SAM-based baseline COMPrompter [70] by 0.007 in M on CAMO (0.054→0.047) and 0.003 on COD10K (0.026→0.023), while improving αE by 0.027 (0.919→0.946). Against vanilla SAM [26], the improvement is dramatic: M drops from 0.132 to 0.044 on CAMO and from 0.049 to 0.022 on COD10K. Among non-SAM RGB methods, SAM-DSAA surpasses PRNet [18] (M=0.050 on CAMO) and ZoomNeXt-R [43] (M=0.069) while matching or exceeding their Fω scores, and it outperforms the best RGB-D method PopNet [62] on COD10K (M: 0.031→0.022) and NC4K (M: 0.043→0.029) despite PopNet's advantage on CHAMELEON (M: 0.022 vs 0.029).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAM-Adapter line of work by introducing dual-stream adapters that operate on both RGB and depth inputs, departing from the single-modality adapter design in SAM-Adapter [7]. It adopts the teacher-student dual-stream philosophy from DSAM [65] but replaces the full dual-network architecture with lightweight adapters embedded in SAM's frozen ViT encoder, adding Discrete Wavelet Transform for high-frequency feature extraction. The bidirectional knowledge distillation (PVTv2→RGB adapter, RGB adapter→depth adapter) and mixed prompt embedding are novel components not present in prior SAM-for-COD methods, positioning the work at the intersection of parameter-efficient fine-tuning, multi-modal fusion, and knowledge distillation for foundation models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a parameter-efficient adapter strategy can close the large performance gap between generic SAM and domain-specific COD models, achieving SOTA across four benchmarks while keeping SAM's architecture intact. This validates the broader hypothesis that foundation models can be adapted to challenging, low-contrast segmentation tasks with minimal parameter modification. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond COD to other low-contrast or texture-blending scenarios, whether the source-free depth dependency limits real-world deployment, and whether the bidirectional KD ordering (PVT→RGB→Depth) is optimal or task-specific. The authors note future directions toward thermal/spectral modalities and lightweight real-time variants.
