## Problem

Existing facial performance capture methods either omit teeth entirely, producing an uncanny-valley effect, or rely on multi-camera rigs and invasive intra-oral setups that are inaccessible for everyday use. No prior method enables both personalized dental geometry reconstruction and dynamic teeth-inclusive performance capture from a single phone camera.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a monocular 1080p RGB video captured in two phases (a ~20 s static toothy-expression pass with an S-shaped camera trajectory, followed by a frontal dynamic-expression pass) along with semi-automatic per-tooth semantic masks extracted via SAM2 (user clicks 4–5 key frames, ~3 min total), facial landmarks, and face-parsing silhouettes. It outputs a personalized 3D teeth mesh (extracted from the optimized DMM latent code), per-frame FLAME expression and pose parameters, and a drivable face-teeth rig where upper teeth are skinned to the skull joint and lower teeth to the jaw joint. Full optimization of a ~500-frame video takes approximately 8 hours on an NVIDIA RTX 3090.

## Method

The core contribution is a representation-adaptation technique that maintains dual teeth representations: a neural SDF-based Dental Morphable Model (DMM) for shape priors and contact detection, and a mesh extracted via Marching Cubes for efficient differentiable rasterization alongside the FLAME face, with gradients propagated back to the SDF through NIE. Teeth are rigidly assembled onto FLAME's kinematic tree (upper→skull, lower→jaw) so they articulate naturally with jaw motion while remaining independent of lip deformations. For optimization, the paper introduces a SemanticXY loss that concatenates per-tooth semantic labels with screen-space XY coordinates and applies optimal-transport-based matching to establish long-range pixel correspondences, overcoming convergence failures caused by large initialization gaps. Two occlusion-aware strategies exploit the structural prior that teeth can only be hidden by lips and surrounding facial regions: a face-vanishing strategy removes occluding geometry to recover gradient flow when rendered teeth are hidden but target teeth are visible, and a teeth-hiding strategy constrains visible rendered teeth toward the correct occluding region when the target is fully occluded. A contact loss evaluated in DMM canonical space prevents teeth-lip interpenetration throughout optimization.

## Results

On the synthetic MetaHuman dataset, the method achieves a Chamfer Distance (CD) of 2.8830×10⁻⁵ for teeth performance capture, substantially outperforming VHAP (9.1174×10⁻⁵) and MonoNPHM (5.9791×10⁻⁵). For teeth shape reconstruction, the method achieves CD of 1.5674×10⁻⁵, improving over TensoSDF (2.0312×10⁻⁵) by approximately 23%. The ablation study further shows that removing the SemanticXY loss degrades CD from 2.8830 to 8.7228 and mIoU from 0.7926 to 0.5872, highlighting the critical role of optimal-transport-based matching for convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For performance capture, the method achieves a 3.2× reduction in Chamfer Distance over VHAP (2.8830 vs. 9.1174×10⁻⁵) and a 2.1× reduction over MonoNPHM (2.8830 vs. 5.9791×10⁻⁵), indicating that neither the mesh-based VHAP nor the SDF-based MonoNPHM can accurately track teeth geometry during dynamic expressions. For shape reconstruction, the method outperforms TensoSDF by 23% in CD (1.5674 vs. 2.0312×10⁻⁵), attributed to the use of the neural SDF-based Dental Morphable Model prior and the semantic mask rendering loss for precise teeth alignment. Qualitatively, VHAP's mesh-based mouth interior fails to align with target teeth, while MonoNPHM struggles to produce geometry that accurately represents individual teeth. The method's advantage is most pronounced in performance capture, where the occlusion-aware optimization strategies and contact loss are specifically designed to handle the teeth-lip interaction that generic face tracking methods do not model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FLAME facial parametric model by integrating a neural SDF-based Dental Morphable Model (DMM) as a teeth prior, and builds its tracking pipeline on VHAP/GaussianAvatars. It departs from prior teeth reconstruction work (Wu et al., which is static-only) and inner-mouth tracking (Rasras et al., which requires multi-camera rigs) by unifying both reconstruction and dynamic performance capture under a single phone camera via analysis-by-synthesis. The key methodological departure is the dual mesh+SDF representation for teeth, enabling both efficient differentiable rasterization (mesh) and contact constraint enforcement (SDF), combined with optimal-transport-based semantic mask losses to handle the alignment ambiguity of visually similar dental components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a visible gap in facial avatar quality: the uncanny valley effect caused by missing or inaccurate teeth in existing monocular tracking methods. The occlusion-aware optimization strategies (face-vanishing and teeth-hiding) offer a generalizable principle for handling visibility transitions in differentiable rendering of occluded components. However, the method requires semi-automatic teeth segmentation (SAM2 with manual clicks), takes ~8 hours per 500-frame video, and is limited to 8 incisors in dynamic phases, leaving full automation and faster optimization as important open questions. Broader impact depends on advances in teeth segmentation and faster per-instance optimization to make this practical for consumer applications.
