## Problem

Drawing-based 3D animation systems suffer from a 'stylization pose gap': the stylization network is fine-tuned on a single occlusion-free rest pose (the only available ground-truth drawing), yet at inference it must handle diverse dynamic poses with overlapping body parts. This mismatch causes contour flickering and stroke blurring in occluded regions, significantly degrading both temporal consistency and perceptual fidelity of the animated output.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252304/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a single hand-drawn character image and a target 3D motion sequence (65-joint Mixamo skeleton poses) as input. An image-to-3D diffusion model (Wonder3D) with automatic rigging generates a 3D structure per pose, which is rendered as a 2D projection; the stylization network then takes this projection plus a computed occlusion-robust edge map and outputs the final animated drawing frame. Training uses only one drawing per character (no multi-pose ground truth), and evaluation covers 120 characters from Amateur Drawings with 20 non-occluded and 20 occluded motions each.

## Method

OSF introduces Flow-Depth Edge Detection (FDED) to produce occlusion-robust edge guidance: depth-based edges (via Gaussian adaptive thresholding on the depth map) capture unoccluded contours, while flow-based edges (propagating previous-frame edge points via RAFT optical flow, filtering to interior regions, and dilating) recover missing contours in occluded areas. The stylization uses a single-stage U-Net (USNet) that replaces DrawingSpinUp's two-stage texture-then-contour pipeline, achieving 2.4× faster inference and 2.1× less memory. Training combines patch-wise L2 reconstruction loss with an edge-guided contrastive ranking loss that enforces the output patch's cosine similarity to its ground-truth contour patch to exceed similarity to randomly sampled other contour patches, yielding sharper and more temporally stable contours.

## Results

On the Amateur Drawings dataset (120 characters, 4800 samples split into non-occlusion and occlusion sets), OSF (USNet+FDED) achieves the best scores across all metrics: texture CLIP 0.974/0.963, contour CLIP 0.982/0.977, contour FID 188/195, and LPIPS 0.586/0.622 (non-occlusion/occlusion). The key advantage emerges under occlusion: while DSU's texture CLIP drops 3.1% (0.958→0.927) and contour CLIP drops 2.2% (0.941→0.919) when occlusion is introduced, OSF's drops are only 1.1% and 0.5% respectively. OSF also wins human preference (0.61 vs. 0.35 for DSU) and runs 2.4× faster with 2.1× less memory than the two-stage DSU pipeline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OSF outperforms all three baselines on every metric in both non-occlusion and occlusion settings. Against the non-stylization baselines (Wonder3D and DreamGaussian), OSF leads by large margins—e.g., contour FID of 188/195 vs. 432/476 (Wonder3D) and 441/513 (DreamGaussian)—confirming that stylization is essential for preserving drawing style. Against the main stylization baseline DSU [68], OSF improves texture CLIP from 0.958/0.927 to 0.974/0.963 and contour CLIP from 0.941/0.919 to 0.982/0.977, with the gap widening substantially under occlusion (contour CLIP gap: 2.2 pts non-occlusion → 5.8 pts occlusion). The human preference score also nearly doubles (0.61 vs. 0.35). Additionally, OSF's single-stage USNet reduces inference time from 0.276 s/frame (DSU) to 0.115 s/frame and memory from 11.62 GB to 5.05 GB, a 2.4× speedup and 2.1× memory saving, while FDED adds only ~0.007 s/frame overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DrawingSpinUp (DSU) [68], the most direct prior work on drawing-based 3D animation with stylization, by identifying the 'stylization pose gap'—the mismatch between occlusion-free training poses and occluded inference poses—as the root cause of contour flickering and stroke blurring. It departs from DSU's two-stage stylization (texture then contour) by proposing a single-stage unified U-Net with edge-guided contrastive learning, and introduces Flow-Depth Edge Detection (FDED) that combines Gaussian adaptive depth thresholding with optical-flow-based edge recovery to provide occlusion-robust guidance. The underlying 3D animation pipeline (Wonder3D + Mixamo rigging) is shared with prior systems, so the contribution is squarely in the stylization stage rather than 3D reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is notable: a 2.4× inference speedup and 2.1× memory reduction make drawing-based 3D animation more accessible, while the FDED module is plug-and-play and improves any existing stylization baseline. However, quality still degrades above ~40% occlusion rate, the method depends on temporal continuity (optical flow between consecutive frames), and evaluation is limited to the Amateur Drawings dataset with a single-drawing-per-character training assumption. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more topologically complex characters, whether the single-pose training constraint can be relaxed, and whether patch-wise quantization (mentioned as future work) can push the occlusion robustness boundary further.
