## Problem

Existing diffusion model personalization methods assume a fixed set of concepts, requiring storage of all LoRA weights and repeated fusion at inference time, which becomes impractical as users incrementally add new concepts. Moreover, naively finetuning for new concepts causes catastrophic forgetting of previously learned ones and degrades the model's zero-shot text-to-image generation ability. The paper addresses how to perform continual, fusion-free personalization that preserves both prior concepts and pretrained capabilities with minimal parameter updates.

## Input / Output

For each new concept, the method takes a small set of few-shot reference images (N images per concept) and a text prompt containing a special concept token (e.g., <new1>). The concept images are captioned by a pretrained model to produce text embeddings used for neuron scoring. The output is a generated image at standard Stable Diffusion 1.5 resolution that composes any subset of previously learned concepts specified in the prompt, with no additional test-time optimization or weight fusion required at inference.

## Method

CNS identifies concept-specific neurons in the cross-attention layers of a diffusion model through a three-step selection process: (1) base neuron selection computes importance scores as the element-wise product of key/value weight magnitudes and the L2 norm of concept-image text embeddings, then aggregates binary masks across all concept images via logical AND; (2) general neuron selection applies the same scoring to K=20 diverse calibration prompts to isolate neurons responsible for general image generation; (3) concept neurons are obtained by subtracting general neurons from base neurons. During continual finetuning, only these sparse concept neurons are updated, with a dual regularization loss that penalizes deviation from both the previous concept's weights (preventing catastrophic forgetting) and the original pretrained weights (preserving zero-shot ability). This eliminates the need to store per-concept LoRAs or perform any test-time fusion, requiring only the concept neuron masks to be retained across learning stages.

## Results

On the 20-concept personalization dataset, CNS achieves the best multi-concept CLIP-I (67.21) and CLIP-T (79.22) scores, outperforming all baselines by a large margin on text alignment (e.g., +8.6 over Mix-of-Show, +18.9 over Continual Diffusion). For single-concept personalization, CNS attains CLIP-I 74.88 and CLIP-T 76.95, slightly trailing Mix-of-Show on image alignment (75.86) but leading on text alignment. Critically, CNS requires zero additional GPU/CPU memory and zero extra inference time for multi-concept generation, whereas Mix-of-Show demands 62,852 MB and 727 s, and Orthogonal Adaptation requires 5,663/3,167 MB and 42 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CNS outperforms all five baselines on multi-concept text alignment (CLIP-T), with the largest gains over Continual Diffusion (+18.9) and Mix-of-Show (+8.6), indicating that prior fusion-based methods suffer severe concept confusion or vanishing when composing multiple concepts. On multi-concept image alignment (CLIP-I), CNS leads by +1.95 over Mix-of-Show and +0.84 over Orthogonal Adaptation. For single-concept image alignment, CNS (74.88) is marginally behind Mix-of-Show (75.86) by 0.98 points but surpasses all other methods; on single-concept text alignment CNS leads all baselines. In terms of computational cost, CNS is the only method requiring zero additional memory and zero extra inference time for multi-concept generation, while Mix-of-Show consumes 62,852 MB and 727 s, Orthogonal Adaptation 5,663/3,167 MB and 42 s, and Continual Diffusion 2,461/4,747 MB and 10 s. A human study with 10 raters further confirms CNS's superiority, with 59.8% of users selecting CNS images as best for image alignment and 55.1% for text alignment, far exceeding the next-best Mix-of-Show (20.2% / 22.3%).

## Relation to Prior Work

CNS builds on the neuron-selection paradigm established in LLM research (where task-specific neurons in feed-forward layers can be identified and selectively updated) and adapts it to diffusion model cross-attention layers, following the initial application by CONES [22] and ConceptPrune [3]. It departs from the dominant LoRA-fusion paradigm (Mix-of-Show, Orthogonal Adaptation) and prior continual personalization methods (Continual Diffusion/C-LoRA) by eliminating the need to store and merge per-concept adapter weights entirely. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of 'general neurons'—a fixed subset (~53%) shared across all prompts—that are identified and excluded from concept-specific updates, leaving a sparse set of truly concept-relevant parameters to fine-tune incrementally.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CNS offers a practically attractive fusion-free alternative for continual personalization: no additional LoRA weights to store, no test-time optimization, and negligible memory overhead, which is significant for real-world deployment where users incrementally add concepts. The notable multi-concept CLIP-T gap (79.22 vs. 70.62 for the next best method) suggests that targeted neuron updates preserve text-alignment far better than weight-fusion approaches. Open questions include scalability beyond ~20 concepts, whether the 53% general-neuron fraction is architecture-specific (only SD 1.5 is tested), and whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation. The authors also note potential applicability to knowledge editing and unlearning tasks.
