## Problem

Existing 3DGS editing methods rely entirely on text prompts, which are inherently ambiguous and produce inconsistent editing features across views—especially problematic for non-rigid deformations like facial expressions, where varying smile intensity or tooth visibility across views leads to blurred artifacts and distorted geometry upon 3DGS refinement. Additionally, the text-only interface makes editing a 'one-shot deal,' preventing users from flexibly controlling the editing degree or anchoring the result to a specific desired appearance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251697/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3DGS scene model, a text editing prompt (with an optional original text for computing the editing direction), and a user-selected key view (one of the initially edited rendered images chosen interactively). The key view acts as a semantic anchor: its CLIP embedding difference from the original image defines the target editing direction against which all other views are scored. The output is a refined 3DGS scene optimized over 20 views for 800–1200 iterations, yielding multi-view consistent edited images and a geometry-coherent 3D model.

## Method

InterGSEdit introduces two key modules. First, CLIP-based Semantic Consistency Selection (CSCS) computes an alignment score for each view by measuring cosine distance between its CLIP image-embedding difference (edited minus original) and the text-embedding difference, then selects reference views whose scores deviate minimally from the user-chosen key view's score, assigning exponential-decay weights to avoid hard-threshold information loss. Second, these weighted cross-attention maps are unprojected onto 3D Gaussians via inverse rasterization to build a 3D Geometry-Consistent Attention Prior (GAP^3D), which is projected back to 2D as a 3D-constrained attention map. An Attention Fusion Network (AFN) then adaptively blends this 3D-constrained attention with the native 2D cross-attention at each U-Net layer using a learnable sigmoid gate biased by a linearly decaying term—prioritizing 3D geometric consistency in early denoising steps and shifting toward 2D detail recovery in later steps—regularized by a KL divergence loss between the two attention distributions.

## Results

On the IN2N dataset, InterGSEdit achieves the best scores across all three evaluation metrics: CLIP Similarity of 0.2285, CTIDS of 0.1531, and CDC of 0.8347. The method outperforms the strongest baseline (VcEdit) by 0.009 in CLIP Similarity, 0.034 in CTIDS, and 0.030 in CDC. The largest relative gain is in CTIDS, where InterGSEdit surpasses DGE (0.1097) by nearly 40%, indicating substantially better alignment between the editing direction and the textual prompt.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InterGSEdit outperforms all four baselines on every metric. Against GSEditor [6] (the closest competitor in CLIP Similarity at 0.2169), InterGSEdit gains 0.0116 in CLIP Similarity and 0.0159 in CTIDS, while also improving CDC from 0.8099 to 0.8347. DGE [4] achieves the second-best CDC (0.8118) but trails InterGSEdit by 0.023 in that metric and by a large margin in CTIDS (0.1097 vs. 0.1531), suggesting its epipolar-constraint approach struggles with non-rigid deformations. VcEdit [33] leads in CLIP Similarity among baselines (0.2195) but has the lowest CDC (0.8043), indicating its cross-attention consistency module improves text alignment at the cost of multi-view geometric coherence. IGS2GS [30] is competitive with GSEditor on CLIP Similarity but lags in CTIDS, reflecting weaker directional editing fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3DGS editing pipeline established by GSEditor and InfEdit by addressing multi-view inconsistency through a user-interactive mechanism rather than purely geometric constraints. Prior methods (DGE's epipolar constraints, VcEdit's attention unification, GaussCtrl's parallel editing) enforce cross-view consistency via hard 3D priors that struggle with non-rigid deformations; InterGSEdit instead constructs a soft 3D Geometry-Consistent Attention Prior from CLIP-selected reference views and fuses it adaptively with 2D cross-attention via a learnable gating network. The framework is built on ThreeStudio and the latent consistency model, positioning it as a plug-in attention module within existing diffusion-based 3D editing pipelines rather than a new end-to-end architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The interactive key-view selection directly addresses the 'one-shot deal' limitation of text-only 3D editing, giving users a practical lever to control editing degree and resolve ambiguity that text prompts inherently carry. The adaptive 3D-early/2D-late attention fusion is a simple but effective design principle that could transfer to other diffusion-based 3D generation or editing tasks. However, evaluation is limited to the IN2N dataset with a single GPU, and the user-interaction step introduces a manual bottleneck that limits fully automated deployment; whether the CLIP-based selection generalizes to more complex or larger scenes remains an open question.
