## Problem

Unified medical foundation models trained via joint pre-training on diverse anatomical domains suffer from domain shifts that degrade performance below domain-specific models. Existing continual learning mitigations either rely on rehearsal (requiring access to prior domain data, which is infeasible in medical settings due to privacy constraints) or use direct distillation that accumulates noise from noisy pseudo-labels across domains, leading to catastrophic forgetting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251703/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pre-training, a single medical image I from the current domain is augmented into 2 global views (X) and 6 local views (Y), which are processed by a dual-stream ViT-B encoder: the current network f_Dt encodes all 8 views while a frozen past network f_D(t-1) encodes only the 2 global views to extract token-wise historical embeddings Z_D(t-1). During fine-tuning, only the pre-trained ViT-B encoder is used, producing a [cls, {p_l}] token sequence that feeds standard classification heads (multi-class, binary, multi-label) or segmentation decoders (MMseg-based).

## Method

CoSMIC sequentially pre-trains a ViT-B across anatomical domains using two complementary losses: a reconstruction loss (L_Recon, MAE-style) for domain-specific knowledge, and a novel conditional contrastive loss (L_CoCst) that maximizes the conditional mutual information I(X_Dt; Y_Dt | Z_D(t-1)) to preserve historical knowledge without rehearsal. The key insight is treating past-domain representations as a conditional variable rather than a pseudo-label: Anatomy-Guided Calibration (AGC) computes a token-wise cosine-similarity weight map between current and past embeddings to denoise Z_D(t-1), then the calibrated joint representation [XZ] = AvgPool(X_Dt) + AvgPool(w·Z_D(t-1)) is contrasted against Y_Dt via InfoNCE, alongside the standard (X_Dt, Y_Dt) contrastive pair. Hierarchical alignment extends L_CoCst across multiple ViT layers (using skip connections between layers n and N−n) to retain multi-scale historical patterns, with the final layer using cross-entropy through a 65,536-dim projection head and intermediate layers using MSE similarity.

## Results

CoSMIC achieves state-of-the-art performance across 15 downstream tasks in 4 medical domains. On Radiography (D2), it reaches 85.11% AUC on NIH, 89.78% on CheXpert, 99.81% on ZhangCXR, and 93.10% on RSNA (all at 100% annotation), outperforming the best domain-specific SSL (cxrMAE) by 0.71–1.89 points. On Retinoscopy (D1), CoSMIC attains 95.39% AUC on APTOS and 94.38% on Glaucoma; on Ophthalmoscope (D3), 99.31% AUC on OCTID; and on Dermoscope (D4), 97.61% AUC on ISIC2017 and 88.24% Dice on ISIC2018 segmentation. On the unseen Histopathology domain (D5), CoSMIC shows only a 0.71-point drop versus the ImageNet-pretrained baseline, demonstrating strong zero-shot transferability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 29 domain-specific SSL models, CoSMIC wins on the majority of datasets: it surpasses cxrMAE (the strongest radiography baseline) by 0.71–1.89 AUC points across NIH, CheXpert, ZhangCXR, and RSNA, and outperforms RETFound on APTOS (95.39 vs. 94.48 AUC) and OCTID (99.31 vs. 98.34 AUC). However, CoSMIC slightly trails RETFound and KeepFIT on Glaucoma (94.38 vs. 94.34/93.05 AUC), PAPILA (80.10 vs. 84.51/88.22 AUC), and Retina (87.28 vs. 85.24/88.80 ACC), where those models benefit from 1M+ pre-training images versus CoSMIC's 0.19M. Against unified medical foundation models (LVM-Med, RadImageNet, UniMed-CLIP, Unimiss+), CoSMIC achieves statistically significant improvements (p<0.05) across all 15 downstream tasks, with the largest gaps on radiography and dermatology. Versus continual SSL baselines, CoSMIC achieves higher Knowledge Forward (KF) than CaSSLe (distillation-based) and comparable Knowledge Backward (KB) to MedCoss (rehearsal-based), while avoiding MedCoss's severe degradation when prior-domain data is unavailable (KB_D1 = 0.21 vs. 7.33). On the unseen Histopathology domain, CoSMIC retains 94.01% average performance (only 0.71 below ImageNet baseline), substantially outperforming both CaSSLe and MedCoss in zero-shot transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoSMIC sits at the intersection of self-supervised learning (building on DINO's dual-stream ViT and MAE's reconstruction pretext), contrastive learning (extending InfoNCE to a trivariate space), and continual learning (departing from CaSSLe's direct self-distillation and MedCoss's rehearsal-based sequential SSL). Its key conceptual departure is treating historical domain knowledge not as a pseudo-label to be matched but as a *conditional variable* that constrains mutual information maximization between current multi-view representations, formalized through Anatomy-Guided Calibration to denoise cross-state token alignments. This positions CoSMIC as a rehearsal-free alternative to joint multi-domain pre-training (LVM-Med, RadImageNet, UniMed-CLIP) and to rehearsal-based continual SSL (MedCoss), addressing domain shift without requiring access to prior domain data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in medical foundation models: joint pre-training across heterogeneous anatomical domains suffers domain shift, while sequential approaches typically require data replay that is infeasible under medical privacy constraints. CoSMIC's rehearsal-free continual SSL paradigm, validated across 15 downstream tasks in 5 domains including an unseen histopathology domain, offers a viable path toward scalable multi-domain medical pre-training. Limitations include evaluation at ViT-B scale on only 4 pre-training domains, and the unseen-domain transfer (0.71 drop) suggests the approach may not fully generalize to arbitrarily distant anatomical spaces. Open questions include whether the conditional MI formulation scales to larger backbones, more domains, or 3D volumetric data, and whether the domain ordering sensitivity (minimal here) grows with domain count.
