## Problem

Autoregressive image generation models (e.g., VAR) have not been adapted for unsupervised image-to-image translation, primarily because the discrete argmax operation in vector quantization severs gradient flow between the VAE decoder and the causal Transformer, blocking end-to-end adversarial training in image space. Additionally, the sequential next-token decoding paradigm introduces long inference times that limit practical usability for translation tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251766/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single source-domain RGB image (256×256 for horse↔zebra, 512×512 for day↔night) and produces a translated RGB image in the target domain. The source image is first tokenized by a frozen VAE+VQ tokenizer into K multi-scale residual maps, which are upsampled and summed to form context features F_k at each scale. These multi-scale features are injected as contextual prompts into the causal VAR Transformer, analogous to prefix conditioning in language models, and the output is decoded back to pixel space by the VAE decoder.

## Method

CycleVAR introduces Softmax Relaxed Quantization (SRQ), which replaces the non-differentiable argmax codebook lookup with a temperature-controlled softmax-weighted average over all codebook vectors, preserving gradient propagation for adversarial training in image space. The framework reformulates unpaired translation as image-conditional visual autoregressive generation: multi-scale source image tokens are prefilled as contextual keys, and the pre-trained causal VAR Transformer (VAR-310M or Infinity-2B) predicts target-domain residual maps. Two generation modes are explored—serial multi-step (K sequential forward passes with iterative refinement) and parallel one-step (all scales processed concurrently in a single forward pass)—with the parallel mode yielding superior FID and faster inference. Training follows CycleGAN-Turbo's loss formulation (cycle consistency, adversarial loss with a CLIP-feature-based discriminator, and identity regularization) while keeping the visual tokenizer frozen.

## Results

On the 256×256 Horse↔Zebra and 512×512 Day↔Night benchmarks, CycleVAR w/ Infinity achieves FID scores of 40.3 (Horse→Zebra), 119 (Zebra→Horse), 30.6 (Day→Night), and 43.6 (Night→Day), with DINO Structure scores of 1.9, 1.8, 3.0, and 3.2 respectively. The smaller CycleVAR w/ VAR-310M variant (256×256 only) records FID 47.8 and 124.1 with DINO Structure 1.8 and 1.9 on the horse/zebra tasks. On the Anime Scene Dataset user study, CycleVAR w/ Infinity attains the highest preference scores across all three criteria: Style 0.650, Content 0.411, and Overall 0.609, substantially outperforming the next-best method Scenimefy (0.157/0.165/0.171).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CycleVAR w/ Infinity outperforms the closest competitor CycleGAN-Turbo on FID across all four tasks (e.g., 40.3 vs 41.0 on Horse→Zebra, 43.6 vs 45.2 on Night→Day) and matches or beats it on DINO Structure (1.9 vs 2.1 on Horse→Zebra, 3.2 vs 3.8 on Night→Day). Against traditional GAN baselines (CycleGAN, CUT), CycleVAR achieves substantially lower FID (40.3 vs 74.9/43.9 on Horse→Zebra) and much better structural preservation (DINO 1.9 vs 3.2/6.6). CycleVAR also dominates diffusion-based zero-shot methods (SDEdit, Plug&Play, Pix2Pix-Zero, DDIB) on both FID and DINO Structure in most settings, though it concedes the Day→Night DINO Structure metric to InstructPix2Pix (3.0 vs 2.1) and Plug&Play (3.0 vs 2.9). The one notable FID loss is to Cycle-Diffusion on Horse→Zebra (40.3 vs 38.6), but CycleVAR recovers decisively on DINO Structure (1.9 vs 6.0) and on all other tasks. On the Anime Scene Dataset, CycleVAR w/ Infinity achieves the top user-preference score in Style (0.650), Content (0.411), and Overall (0.609), far exceeding Scenimefy (0.157/0.165/0.171) and other anime-translation baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

CycleVAR repurposes the pre-trained VAR autoregressive image generation model [44] (and its text-conditional extension Infinity [8]) for unsupervised image-to-image translation, a task previously dominated by GAN-based (CycleGAN, CUT) and diffusion-based (CycleGAN-Turbo, DDIB) methods. The training framework directly follows CycleGAN-Turbo [30], adopting its cycle consistency, adversarial, and identity loss formulation for unpaired training. The key conceptual departure from prior autoregressive translation work (ControlAR [21], ControlVAR [19]) is the elimination of paired supervision: source image multi-scale tokens are injected as contextual prompts analogous to prefix conditioning in LLMs, and a novel Softmax Relaxed Quantization (SRQ) mechanism replaces the non-differentiable argmax in vector quantization to enable end-to-end adversarial training in image space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that pre-trained visual autoregressive models can perform competitive unsupervised image translation, opening a path toward unifying image generation and translation within a single autoregressive framework that aligns naturally with LLM architectures. The SRQ technique is a general, lightweight solution to the gradient-truncation problem inherent in all VQ-based generative models, potentially applicable beyond translation. However, quantitative gains over CycleGAN-Turbo are modest (e.g., FID 40.3 vs 41.0 on horse→zebra), and experiments are limited to 256×256 and 512×512 resolutions; whether the approach scales to higher resolutions or more complex domain shifts remains open. The finding that parallel one-step generation outperforms serial multi-step in unsupervised settings (due to SRQ error accumulation) suggests that iterative refinement strategies may need rethinking in differentiable-quantization pipelines.
