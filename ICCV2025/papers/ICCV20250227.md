## Problem

Conventional object localization losses (L2, IoU-based) exhibit gradient instability for small objects because the Hessian of the loss scales inversely with anchor size squared, producing sharper curvature and oscillatory updates that prevent convergence. This paper identifies this gradient instability as an underexplored root cause of the small-object detection gap and shows that existing remedies (feature enhancement, label reassignment, data augmentation) do not address the optimization landscape itself.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (tested at 1333×800 for two-stage/anchor-free detectors and 640×640 or 1536×1536 for YOLO variants) and outputs bounding box coordinates plus class labels. The key structural difference is that localization is parameterized as a probability distribution over n+1 discrete grid points per coordinate axis rather than four continuous regression values; the final box is recovered as the expectation (weighted sum) of grid positions. This plug-and-play reformulation is compatible with anchor-based (Faster R-CNN, RetinaNet), anchor-free (FCOS, GFL), and DETR-based (DINO) architectures without modifying the backbone or classification head.

## Method

UGS reformulates bounding-box regression as a multi-class classification problem over interval non-uniform (IN) grids, where exponential spacing (controlled by β) concentrates grid points near zero to counteract the label imbalance that small objects induce under uniform quantization. The cross-entropy objective yields gradients bounded in [0,1] that are proportional to the confidence gap between prediction and target, producing scale-invariant, confidence-driven updates. Two uncertainty-aware mechanisms further stabilize training: an entropy-based Uncertainty Minimization (UM) loss that suppresses flat, high-variance prediction distributions, and an Uncertainty-guided Refinement (UR) module that injects closed-form L2 adversarial perturbations (derived from the UM loss gradient) into FPN feature maps to amplify learning in high-uncertainty regions while preserving stable updates elsewhere. The overall localization loss combines the cross-entropy term, the UM loss (weighted by λ), and the layer-wise UR loss (weighted by γ), reducing gradient variance by up to 2.9× for small objects compared to Smooth-L1.

## Results

On the VisDrone validation set, UGS consistently improves all tested detectors by approximately 2–3 AP. The headline result is DINO-5scale + UGS achieving 38.1 AP (24.2 APs), surpassing the prior state-of-the-art DQ-DETR (37.0 AP) by 1.1 AP. On the SODA-A aerial rotated detection benchmark, UGS boosts Rotated RetinaNet by 4.5 AP (22.3→26.8) and Rotated FCOS by 2.6 AP (32.6→35.2). On COCO, Faster R-CNN + UGS with ResNet-101 reaches 39.4 AP and 22.4 APs, demonstrating consistent gains on small objects across general detection benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UGS outperforms all prior small-object detectors on VisDrone: DINO-5scale + UGS (38.1 AP) surpasses DQ-DETR (37.0 AP) by 1.1 AP, CZDet (33.2 AP) by 4.9 AP, and DetectoRS (29.4 AP) by 8.7 AP. On the GFL V1 backbone, UGS (31.2 AP) exceeds the prior SOTA CEASC (28.7 AP) by 2.5 AP. For YOLO-based detection, UGS improves TPH-YOLOv5 by 2.5 AP at both 640² (26.5→29.0) and 1536² (39.2→41.7) resolutions. On SODA-A, UGS beats Rotated RetinaNet by 4.5 AP, Rotated FCOS by 2.6 AP, and Oriented RCNN by 1.6 AP. In ablation comparisons, UGS (24.2 AP) substantially outperforms gradient clipping (21.6 AP), IoU-loss (21.8 AP), and Bayesian YOLO (22.0 AP), confirming that the classification-based objective with uncertainty-aware mechanisms provides stronger stabilization than ad-hoc gradient correction techniques.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the classification-based localization paradigm introduced by GFL V1 (Li et al., NeurIPS 2020) and offset bin classification (Qiu et al., CVPR 2020), extending it with interval non-uniform (IN) grid quantization to address label imbalance specific to small objects. It departs from the dominant small object detection literature—which focuses on feature enhancement, data augmentation, and super-resolution—by reframing the core challenge as a gradient stability problem rooted in sharper loss curvature for small objects. The uncertainty-guided refinement module borrows the closed-form L2 perturbation derivation from Sharpness-Aware Minimization (Foret et al., ICLR 2021), repurposing it not for generalization but for identifying and refining high-uncertainty localization regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The gradient instability diagnosis provides a new, mechanistic explanation for the small object detection gap that is orthogonal to feature-level approaches, suggesting that optimization dynamics—not just representation quality—limit performance. The plug-and-play nature of UGS (applicable to anchor-based, anchor-free, DETR-based, and YOLO architectures with ~2–3 AP gains) makes it a practical drop-in improvement for existing pipelines. Open questions include whether the gradient curvature analysis generalizes to other challenging detection regimes (extreme occlusion, multi-modal sensing) and whether the 15% training overhead of the UR module can be reduced. The authors note future work on video and multi-modal small object detection, indicating the framework's potential to extend beyond static image detection.
