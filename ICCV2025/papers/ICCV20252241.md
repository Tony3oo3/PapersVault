## Problem

Existing open-world model attribution methods rely on handcrafted region partitioning or feature-space designs that are confounded by spurious source-content biases (e.g., identity) rather than true model-specific generation artifacts. This causes learned correlations to break down when confronting previously unseen generative models, leading to poor generalization on novel attacks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252241/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CDAL is a plug-and-play module that consumes intermediate feature maps (X) from an existing baseline attribution network and produces two sets of spatial attention maps (factual F and counterfactual C, each of size H×W) that are used to weight features for final attribution prediction. The overall system takes a single RGB image (deepfake face or GAN-generated image) and outputs either a known source model label or an 'unknown' flag indicating a novel generative model. The module adds at most 0.35M parameters and 0.002 GFLOPs to the baseline.

## Method

CDAL explicitly models the causal relationship between visual forgery traces and source models via a structural causal model (X → A → Y with confounding X → Y). It extracts factual attention (model-specific artifacts) and counterfactual attention (source bias) using Causal Expert (CE) Convolution, which dynamically constructs kernels by weighting N_exp expert kernels based on input-dependent causal relevance scores, rather than using fixed random or uniform interventions. A Causal Attention Augmentation step then expands spatial coverage of the attention maps while preserving causal consistency in factual regions. Training maximizes the causal effect (difference between factual and counterfactual predictions) via a cross-entropy loss, combined with a decorrelation loss that pushes counterfactual predictions toward uniformity and an attention diversification loss that encourages exploration of complementary forgery regions.

## Results

On OW-DFA Protocol 1 (forged images only), CPL+CDAL achieves 76.98% ARI on novel attacks, a +11.27% gain over CPL and +7.72% over the previous state-of-the-art MPSL. On Protocol 2 (real & fake, simulating real-world conditions), CPL+CDAL reaches 93.01% overall ARI, surpassing MPSL by 0.64%. On the OSMA GAN attribution benchmark, POSE+CDAL attains 82.85% OSCR on the unseen-all setting, outperforming POSE (80.50%) and RepMix (69.43%), with only 0.25M additional parameters and 0.002 GFLOPs overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDAL is a plug-and-play module that consistently improves every baseline it is integrated into. Against the primary deepfake attribution baselines on OW-DFA Protocol 1, CPL+CDAL outperforms CPL by +11.27% ARI on novel attacks and +5.31% overall ARI, and surpasses the previous SOTA MPSL by +7.72% novel ARI. When plugged into general open-world recognition methods (ORCA, NACH), CDAL still yields +4.27% and +9.17% novel ARI respectively, confirming its task-agnostic benefit. On the OSMA GAN attribution benchmark, POSE+CDAL improves OSCR on unseen-all by +2.35% over POSE and +2.29% over RepMix, with the largest gains on unseen-seed (+4.48% OSCR) indicating sensitivity to subtle random-initiation differences. On GAN discovery (Protocol 2), POSE+CDAL raises unseen-all purity from 41.04% to 48.93% (+7.89%) and ARI from 26.39% to 29.65% (+3.26%). The method also generalizes to modern diffusion/flow-based models from DF40, where CPL+CDAL gains +8.21% novel ARI in Setting 1 and +6.19% in Setting 2 (completely unseen DDPM), whereas CPL alone struggles with out-of-distribution attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

CDAL repurposes the counterfactual intervention framework from causal reasoning in re-identification and fine-grained classification [33, 45] into the open-world model attribution setting, where prior methods (CPL, MPSL, POSE) rely on handcrafted region partitioning or feature-space spanning that remain confounded by source-content biases. Rather than introducing a new backbone, it is a plug-and-play module built around a Causal Expert Convolution that learns factual and counterfactual attention maps, explicitly decoupling model-specific artifacts from identity-level spurious correlations. The causal-effect maximization objective (difference between factual and counterfactual predictions) is borrowed from the do-operator literature but applied here as a training signal for attention quality in attribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design with negligible overhead (~0.35M params, 0.002 GFLOPs) makes CDAL immediately applicable to any existing attribution pipeline, and the consistent gains across OW-DFA, OSMA, and modern diffusion/flow-based models suggest broad practical utility for forensic attribution in real-world settings. However, the structural causal model is relatively simple (a single confounding path X→Y), and the method has only been validated on face-centric benchmarks; extending to video, multi-modal, or non-face content remains open. The reliance on a shared attribution classifier for both factual and counterfactual branches also raises questions about whether the causal effect signal remains informative when the base model is already well-calibrated.
