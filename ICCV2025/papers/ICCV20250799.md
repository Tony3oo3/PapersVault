## Problem

Existing CIR benchmarks (CIRR, CIRCO, FashionIQ, GeneCIS) do not adequately assess whether models genuinely understand compositional semantics or merely rely on object/concept presence. They contain very few negation or replacement cases (≤10%), offer limited semantic diversity, and lack fine-grained hard negatives that would reveal whether a model retrieves the correct image based on the intended modification or simply matches surface-level object information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250799/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consists of ~1900 CIR triplets, each with a reference image, a long descriptive conditioning text (explicitly naming which object/attribute to modify, e.g., 'remove the left cup'), and a target image. Evaluation uses two protocols: a full candidate set with 5–10 hard negatives per query, and a controlled binary-choice setting with only two candidate images. Triplets span seven semantic categories (left-right, top-bottom, spatial reasoning, size, action, color, object reasoning) crossed with three arithmetic types (addition, negation, replacement), with hard negatives constructed to differ from the target in only the specific modified region.

## Method

The paper contributes both a benchmark and a training remedy. For the benchmark, each triplet is built via a localized manipulation pipeline: objects are removed from reference images using inpainting, then new objects are inserted via text-to-image generation or copy-and-paste with harmonization, producing 5–10 controlled variants per reference that serve as targets and hard negatives for different arithmetic types. For the remedy, the authors adapt the RTD text-triplet training scheme to the E5-V MLLM embedding model (LLaVA-NeXT-8B with LLaMA-3 backbone). They construct BLIP-style text triplets (T_r, T_c, T_t) where subjects and attributes are explicitly separated, use an LLM to generate three (T_c, T_t) pairs per reference covering replacement, negation, and addition, then train E5-V's LLM with a contrastive loss where the concatenated reference+conditioning text is the query, the target text is the positive, and the original reference (or an alternative target) acts as a hard negative. This yields +16% on MA-CIR, +9% on CIRCO, and +6% on CIRR over the unmodified E5-V baseline.

## Results

On MA-CIR (full candidate set with hard negatives), the proposed method achieves 48.5% average R@1, a +16.1% absolute gain over the best prior method E5-V (32.4%). On existing benchmarks, it improves CIRCO mAP@5 from 17.6 to 26.5 (+9.1%) and CIRR R@1 from 30.9 to 36.8 (+5.9%) over E5-V. In the binary-choice evaluation (Table 2), the method reaches 62.2% average accuracy versus 56.2% for E5-V, with particularly large gains on negation (e.g., 73.3% vs 64.1% on Left-Right negation) and object reasoning (73.0% vs 47.4% on negation).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (E5-V + RTD with MA-CIR text triplets and hard negatives) outperforms all 14 baselines on MA-CIR average R@1 (48.5%), with the largest margins over supervised methods like Combiner (11.5%) and SPRC (27.1%), and over the strongest prior ZS-CIR method E5-V (32.4%, +16.1%). On CIRR R@1, the method (36.8%) trails SPRC (54.2%) and Bi-BLIP4CIR (40.2%), which are trained on CIRR triplets, but surpasses all ZS-CIR and synthetic-data baselines. On CIRCO mAP@5, MagicLens (29.6%) edges out the proposed method (26.5%), while CIReVL (26.8%) is nearly matched; the method still leads all other baselines. Among ZS-CIR methods, E5-V is the closest competitor on MA-CIR (32.4%) and CIRR (30.9%), with the gap widening substantially on complex categories like Spatial Reasoning (41.6% vs 21.3%) and Color (56.9% vs 35.3%). In the binary-choice setting, the method's advantage over E5-V is most pronounced on negation and object reasoning, confirming that the RTD adaptation with hard negatives specifically targets the compositional weaknesses identified in the benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two complementary threads: the E5-V multimodal LLM embedding model (Jiang et al., 2024), which adapts LLaVA-NeXT-8B for contrastive sentence retrieval, and the RTD text-triplet training strategy (Byun et al., 2024), originally designed for CLIP-based projection methods in zero-shot CIR. The key methodological departure is transplanting RTD's triplet-based contrastive training from a dual-encoder CLIP setting into a single-backbone MLLM embedding framework, eliminating the need for a separate frozen target encoder. On the benchmark side, MA-CIR extends the CIR evaluation paradigm beyond existing datasets (CIRR, CIRCO, FashionIQ, GeneCIS) by introducing controlled arithmetic types (negation, replacement, addition) across seven semantic categories with explicitly constructed hard negatives, addressing what the authors identify as a critical gap in compositional reasoning assessment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that state-of-the-art CIR models, including supervised and large-scale synthetic-data approaches, perform near random chance on negation and replacement in controlled binary-choice settings, suggesting that gains on existing benchmarks may reflect object-presence matching rather than genuine compositional understanding. The finding that scaling vision backbones (up to ViT/G-14) or using compositionality-tuned CLIP variants yields negligible improvement on arithmetic types, while LLM-based text encoders show robustness, points toward text-encoder quality as the critical bottleneck. Open questions include whether the synthetic, manipulation-based construction of MA-CIR (inpainting + copy-and-paste) limits ecological validity, whether the ~1900-triplet scale is sufficient for reliable conclusions, and whether the RTD-to-E5-V adaptation generalizes to other MLLM architectures or more complex multi-step compositions.
