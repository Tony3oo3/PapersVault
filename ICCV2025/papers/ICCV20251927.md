## Problem

Iterative stereo-matching methods (e.g., RAFT-Stereo, IGEV-Stereo) rely on fixed-receptive-field CNN features and pixel-wise cost volumes, which cannot effectively distinguish similar local patterns in ill-posed regions such as occlusions, textureless surfaces, and repetitive textures. The core gap is the absence of global context and geometric information to guide iterative refinement in these ambiguous areas.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251927/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a rectified RGB stereo image pair and extracts multi-scale features (at 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, 1/32 resolution) via MobileNetV2. It produces a dense disparity map refined over N ConvGRU iterations (typically 22–32), with the initial disparity regressed from a 4D concatenated cost volume of shape 2C₄ × D × H/4 × W/4. The framework is designed as a plug-in module set that can be inserted into existing iterative backbones without altering their recurrent update structure.

## Method

GREAT introduces three attention modules that collectively inject global context into the cost volume. Spatial Attention (SA) applies Outlook Attention (local) followed by Swin-Transformer Window/Shifted-Window Self-Attention (global) on multi-scale left features to encode spatial geometry. Matching Attention (MA) performs Bi-Directional Epipolar Cross-Attention (BECA) — a 1D cross-attention along the width dimension — on fused left/right features (with Selective-Stereo's contextual module for rectification robustness) to aggregate global context along epipolar lines. Volume Attention (VA) uses Per-pixel Disparity Searching Space Cross-Attention (PDCA), where spatial feature vectors attend over the disparity dimension at each pixel to selectively excite global context in specific cost-volume positions, replacing the Feature Attention of IGEV-Stereo. The cost volume is built by channel-wise concatenation of matching features (rather than correlation) to preserve distributional consistency with the spatial features used in PDCA. The framework is validated as a universal plug-in across RAFT-Stereo, IGEV-Stereo, and Selective-IGEV, trained with Smooth L1 on the initial disparity and exponentially weighted L1 on iterative updates.

## Results

On the Scene Flow test set, GREAT-IGEV achieves a state-of-the-art overall EPE of 0.413 px, non-occluded EPE of 0.135 px, and occluded EPE of 1.514 px, outperforming IGEV-Stereo (0.479/0.194/1.649) by 13.8%, 30.4%, and 8.2% respectively. On real-world benchmarks, GREAT-IGEV ranks first on the KITTI 2015 leaderboard (Noc-D1-all 1.37) and ETH3D leaderboard (Bad 0.5: 2.18), and second on Middlebury (Bad 2.0: 2.81). The framework also demonstrates strong zero-shot generalization, matching or exceeding baselines when trained only on synthetic Scene Flow and tested on real KITTI 2015, Middlebury, and ETH3D without fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GREAT-IGEV outperforms its direct baseline IGEV-Stereo [43] across all three Scene Flow metrics: EPE-All improves from 0.48 to 0.41 (13.8% reduction), EPE-Non-Occ from 0.19 to 0.14 (30.4% reduction), and EPE-Occ from 1.65 to 1.51 (8.2% reduction). Against Selective-IGEV [37], GREAT-IGEV wins on all metrics (0.41 vs 0.45 EPE-All, 0.14 vs 0.17 EPE-Non-Occ, 1.51 vs 1.57 EPE-Occ). On occlusion handling, GREAT-IGEV (1.51) beats GOAT-Stereo [23] (1.53) without requiring an additional occlusion mask prediction. On KITTI 2015, GREAT-IGEV surpasses IGEV-Stereo by 8.1% on Noc-D1-all (1.37 vs 1.49) and ranks first among all published methods; GREAT-Selective further improves to 1.40. On ETH3D, GREAT-IGEV achieves a 38.1% reduction in Bad 0.5 over IGEV-Stereo (2.18 vs 3.52), and on Middlebury a 41.8% reduction in Bad 2.0 (2.81 vs 4.83), though it ranks second behind Selective-IGEV (2.51) on Middlebury Bad 2.0. The framework also transfers to RAFT-Stereo [18], where GREAT-RAFT reduces EPE-All from 0.551 to 0.488 and EPE-Occ from 1.938 to 1.747, confirming cross-model universality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends iterative stereo matching methods (RAFT-Stereo, IGEV-Stereo, Selective-IGEV) by injecting global context through three attention modules: Spatial Attention (borrowing Outlook Attention from Volo and Window/Shifted-Window Self-Attention from Swin Transformer), Matching Attention (a novel Bi-Directional Epipolar Cross-Attention along epipolar lines), and Volume Attention (a Per-pixel Disparity Searching Space Cross-Attention that excites cost-volume entries using spatial features). Conceptually, it departs from the local-receptive-field paradigm of existing iterative methods by arguing that ill-posed regions (occlusions, textureless, repetitive textures) require global geometric context that CNN-based feature extractors and pixel-wise cost volumes cannot provide. The framework is positioned as a universal plug-in rather than a standalone architecture, integrating into multiple iterative backbones with minimal modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's universality—demonstrated across three distinct iterative architectures—makes it a practical tool for improving any existing iterative stereo matcher without redesigning the full pipeline. The finding that GREAT-IGEV matches IGEV-Stereo's 32-iteration performance with only 4 iterations suggests that a globally-informed cost volume can substitute for many recurrent refinement steps, which has efficiency implications. Open questions remain: the Matching Attention module incurs significant computational cost on long epipolar lines (high-resolution images), and reflective/specular surfaces represent a different failure mode (lighting-driven rather than geometry-driven) that the current global-context approach does not address.
