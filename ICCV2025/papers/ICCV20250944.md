## Problem

Despite VLMs claiming to handle high-resolution images exceeding 1k resolution, there is no comprehensive, unified benchmark that evaluates HRI understanding across diverse real-world scenes and provides fine-grained diagnostic insight into how models actually utilize different regions of an HRI. Existing benchmarks either target narrow scenarios (e.g., only long-range or only 8k), have average resolutions below 1k, or focus on text-based Needle-in-the-Haystack rather than spatial image regions.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes RGB images with resolutions from 1024×1024 up to 35,503×26,627 pixels paired with a text question, spanning 25 real-world scenes (pathology, remote sensing, street monitoring, scanned documents, artworks, etc.) and 2 synthetic diagnostic grids. Outputs include per-task accuracy scores, a Regional Divergence metric (gap between best-region and mean-region performance), a Size drop metric (performance degradation as grid size grows from 1×1 to 10×10), and Manhattan-distance performance curves revealing spatial utilization patterns.

## Method

HRScene constructs a unified taxonomy of 8 HRI categories covering 25 real-world datasets (7,068 samples total, 2,008 re-annotated by 10 graduate-level annotators) plus two synthetic diagnostic datasets inspired by Needle-in-the-Haystack. The WhiteBackground NIAH places a VQAv2 needle image at varying positions on an NxN white grid to measure Regional Divergence, while ComplexGrid NIAH composites the needle with visually similar distractor images in a grid and asks the model to locate it, revealing a U-shaped 'lost-in-the-middle-manhattan' effect based on Manhattan distance from the upper-left corner. The paper benchmarks 28 VLMs (6 proprietary, 22 open-source) and finds average accuracy of only ~50% on real-world tasks, with Qwen2-VL 72B achieving the best 61.85% largely due to native-resolution input support, and identifies that Medical and Research categories are hardest while Art and Sub-Image are easiest.

## Results

Across 25 real-world HRI tasks, the best model (Qwen2-VL 72B) achieves only 61.85% average accuracy, while the overall mean across 28 VLMs is 49.68%—far below the 64.72% human performance. Medical (36.64%) and Research (36.08%) categories are the hardest, while Art (61.54%) and Sub-Img (60.60%) are the easiest. On the diagnostic WhiteBackground NIAH, most models show significant Regional Divergence (e.g., Gemini-2.0-Flash drops 39.85% from its best region on 10×10 grids), and the ComplexGrid NIAH reveals a U-shaped 'lost-in-the-middle-Manhattan' pattern where performance degrades for needle images in the middle of the grid.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Qwen2-VL 72B (61.85%) is the only model exceeding 60% average accuracy, outperforming Gemini-2.0-Flash (59.82%) by ~2 points and GPT-4o (52.91%) by nearly 9 points; the authors attribute this to Qwen's native-resolution input support versus GPT's 5 MB file limit. Among proprietary models, Gemini-2.0-Flash leads (59.82%), followed by Claude-3.5-Sonnet (54.37%) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (54.21%), while Claude-3-Haiku (41.34%) and GPT-4o-mini (46.12%) fall well below the 49.68% model average. Among open-source models, Qwen2-VL 7B (56.65%) is the best small model, InternVL2 40B (58.45%) leads the medium group, and LLaVA-OneVision 72B (57.45%) and MolMo 72B (55.12%) trail Qwen2-VL 72B in the large group. Notably, GPT-4o scores only 22.63% on Medical (VQA-RAD), the lowest among all major models, while Gemini-2.0-Flash achieves the best Medical score (51.94%). On the diagnostic WhiteBackground NIAH, Qwen2-VL 72B shows the smallest Regional Divergence (~5–10% across grid sizes), whereas DeepSeek-VL2 27B suffers catastrophic degradation (−48% performance drop from 1×1 to 10×10) and GPT-4o-mini drops 35.7% on 10×10 grids.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying the fragmented landscape of high-resolution image (HRI) benchmarks—MME-RealWorld, HR-Bench, MileBench, MuirBench, and others—into a single comprehensive evaluation suite spanning 25 real-world scenes. It repurposes the Needle-in-the-Haystack diagnostic paradigm from long-context LLM evaluation and adapts it to the visual domain, introducing two synthetic diagnostic datasets (WhiteBackground NIAH and ComplexGrid NIAH) that probe *where* in an HRI a model fails, rather than just *whether* it answers correctly. Conceptually, it departs from prior HRI work by treating regional utilization as a first-class evaluation axis, moving beyond aggregate accuracy to expose spatially localized weaknesses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that even the strongest VLMs (Qwen2-VL 72B, Gemini 2.0 Flash) plateau around 60% on real-world HRI tasks, with Medical and Research categories dropping below 40%, reveals a substantial and underappreciated gap in current vision-language capabilities. The diagnostic discoveries—Regional Divergence (performance varying by up to 40% depending on where the answer sits in the image) and the U-shaped 'lost-in-the-middle-manhattan' pattern—are actionable failure signatures that can guide architectural improvements in how VLMs allocate attention across spatial regions. Open questions include whether native-resolution input (as in Qwen2-VL) is the primary differentiator versus model scale, and whether the U-shape pattern persists as models scale further or with improved positional encoding for 2D grids.
