## Problem

Existing On-the-fly Category Discovery (OCD) methods train exclusively on labeled data from known categories, but in fine-grained recognition the annotated data is often too scarce to provide sufficient transferable knowledge for discovering unknown categories. This limitation is particularly acute in streaming settings where the model must assign instance-level labels online without access to a pre-collected query set, and where the known-category feature space alone cannot span the diversity of unseen fine-grained classes.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes a support set of labeled images from known categories (e.g., 10 per class on CUB-200) along with a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model and CLIP encoders to synthesize virtual-category images via latent interpolation. At test time, a single RGB image arrives as a stream; the model outputs a category label (known or newly created) for each instance in real time, maintaining a dynamic leader memory that grows as unknown categories are encountered. The synthesized images are an intermediate training artifact, not a test-time output.

## Method

DiffGRE is a three-stage plug-and-play framework built on top of existing OCD backbones (SMILE, PHE). First, Attribute Composition Generation (ACG) performs spherical interpolation simultaneously in the Stable Diffusion latent space, CLIP visual space, and CLIP textual space between images from two different known categories, then denoises the interpolated latent with a UNet to produce a virtual-category image whose attributes blend those of both parents. Second, Diversity-Driven Refinement (DDR) computes the mean cosine similarity of each generated image to all known-category centers and discards images exceeding a threshold γ, retaining only those that carry genuinely new category information. Third, Semi-supervised Leader Encoding (SLE) clusters the combined labeled-and-generated set with the MAP equation, aligns cluster assignments to ground-truth labels via the Hungarian algorithm, computes per-category leader features, and trains the backbone with a leader-based contrastive loss plus a cross-entropy classifier. At inference, SLE's leader features seed a dynamic leader memory that enables Online Cluster Inference (OCI): each streaming image is compared against current leaders, assigned to the nearest known category, or appended as a new leader if it exceeds an adaptive distance threshold, replacing the brittle binary hash-code matching used by prior OCD methods.

## Results

Under the Online Clustering Inference (OCI) strategy, SMILE + DiffGRE achieves the best average ACC-ALL of 46.7% and ACC-NEW of 43.1% across six fine-grained datasets, while PHE + DiffGRE reaches 46.6% ACC-ALL and 62.9% ACC-OLD. Under hash-like inference, SMILE + DiffGRE improves over SMILE alone by 4.0% in ACC-ALL (38.2% vs. 34.2%) and 3.0% in ACC-NEW (30.2% vs. 27.2%). The SLE-based OCI inference alone (without DiffGRE) already outperforms the SLC baseline by 10.8% in ACC-ALL and 16.5% in ACC-NEW on average, demonstrating the value of high-dimensional leader features over hash descriptors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SMILE [5] (the primary OCD baseline), DiffGRE yields average gains of 4.0% ACC-ALL and 3.0% ACC-NEW under hash-like inference, and 1.5% ACC-ALL and 2.2% ACC-NEW under OCI. Against PHE [55] (the strongest hash-based competitor), DiffGRE improves ACC-ALL by 1.0% (hash) and 2.8% (OCI), with the largest gains on ACC-NEW (+1.9% hash, +3.6% OCI). The SLC inference strategy [12] is substantially outperformed: SLE-based OCI exceeds it by 10.8% ACC-ALL and 16.5% ACC-NEW on average, confirming that high-dimensional leader features are far more informative than binary hash codes. In the synthesis-method comparison (Table 4, CUB), DiffGRE (35.4% ACC-ALL) outperforms Da-Fusion [38] (34.4%), Diff-Mix [44] with known categories (32.9%) and even with unknown categories (35.1%), CutMix [50] (32.8%), and MixUp [51] (33.3%), validating that latent-space attribute composition is more effective than pixel-level mixing or text-prompted generation for discovering unknown categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the On-the-fly Category Discovery (OCD) task introduced by SMILE (Du et al., CVPR 2023) and PHE (Zheng et al., NeurIPS 2025), which rely solely on labeled data and hash-based inference. It departs from existing diffusion-based augmentation methods (Diff-Mix, Da-Fusion) that preserve known-category faithfulness, instead proposing cross-category latent interpolation to synthesize *virtual* categories that do not correspond to any real class. The method also replaces the binary hash-descriptor inference paradigm with an online clustering strategy built on high-dimensional leader features, drawing on semi-supervised contrastive learning and the Hungarian assignment algorithm for pseudo-label alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play DiffGRE framework offers a practical recipe for improving fine-grained OCD when annotated data is scarce, a common real-world constraint. The finding that interpolating between two known categories in diffusion latent space can yield images nearest to *unknown* categories challenges the assumption that latent interpolation stays within the convex hull of seen classes. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large category spaces, how sensitive it is to the quality of the pre-trained diffusion model, and whether the dataset-specific DDR threshold (γ) can be made adaptive. The reliance on a fixed ViT-B-16 backbone and the moderate (3–6%) absolute gains limit the broader impact beyond fine-grained benchmarks.
