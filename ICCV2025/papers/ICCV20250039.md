## Problem

Multimodal models suffer from modality imbalance, where dominant modalities suppress weaker ones during joint optimization, leading to suboptimal fusion performance. Existing gradient modulation methods adjust per-modality gradients but do not simultaneously optimize unimodal and multimodal objectives, and prior knowledge distillation approaches (e.g., UMT) require empirical tuning to combine teacher signals. The paper asks whether unimodal teacher confidence can be leveraged to dynamically schedule which modality leads optimization, thereby preventing any single modality from overshadowing the rest.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes k-modality inputs (audio+video, audio spectrogram+image, audio+visual+text, or image+tabular meteorological data) through modality-specific encoders that feed a late-fusion module, producing a class label (cross-entropy) or a continuous regression value (MSE with sigmoid). A critical requirement is pre-trained unimodal teacher models for each modality, whose batch-wise softmax confidence on the ground-truth class drives the training schedule. The output is a single fused prediction, but the training process also exposes per-modality encoder outputs for evaluation of individual modality performance within the multimodal model.

## Method

G²D introduces a composite loss L_G²D = L_S + α·Σ L_feat^m + β·Σ L_logit^m that fuses the multimodal student's supervised loss with per-modality L2 feature alignment to unimodal teachers and KL-divergence logit distillation, ensuring the student retains modality-specific representations while matching teacher decision boundaries. On top of this loss, Sequential Modality Prioritization (SMP) ranks modalities by their unimodal teacher confidence scores each epoch and assigns a binary modulation coefficient κ_q^m that completely zeroes gradients for all non-prioritized modalities during a dedicated epoch window τ_j, starting from the least confident modality and ending with a joint phase where all modalities train together. This complete (rather than partial) suppression lets each weaker modality train to convergence without interference from the dominant modality before the final joint phase. The approach is modality-count agnostic and adapts to any fusion strategy, with late fusion and cross-attention performing best in experiments.

## Results

On the audio-visual classification benchmarks, G²D achieves 85.89% accuracy on CREMA-D, 73.03% on AV-MNIST, and 53.82% on VGGSound, outperforming the strongest baselines (ReconBoost at 83.62%, MM-Pareto at 72.63%, and DLMG at 53.78%) respectively. On the three-modality UR-Funny dataset (A-V-TXT), G²D reaches 65.49% accuracy versus 63.68% for OGM-GE and 62.58% for joint training. On the soil moisture regression task (MIS-ME), G²D achieves MAPE of 7.01 and R² of 0.82, surpassing the MIS-ME baseline (MAPE 7.52, R² 0.80).

## Baselines & Comparisons

G²D outperforms all 11 baselines on every dataset evaluated. On CREMA-D, the largest margin is over ReconBoost (85.89% vs. 83.62%, +2.27 pp) and MM-Pareto (79.82%, +6.07 pp); the weakest baselines (MSES 60.99%, PMR 59.13%) lag by over 25 pp. On AV-MNIST, G²D leads MM-Pareto (72.63%) by 0.40 pp and MLA (65.32%) by 7.71 pp, with the gap over joint training at 3.26 pp. On VGGSound, the margin is tighter: G²D edges out DLMG and UMT (both 53.78%) by 0.04 pp and ReconBoost (52.74%) by 1.08 pp, while PMR collapses to 33.07%. On UR-Funny (A-V-TXT), G²D achieves 65.49% versus OGM-GE (63.68%) and UMT (63.38%), a 1.8–2.1 pp advantage. On the MIS-ME regression task, G²D improves MAPE from 7.52 to 7.01 and R² from 0.80 to 0.82 over the dedicated MIS-ME baseline. Notably, G²D also substantially recovers the suppressed weak modality: on CREMA-D the video encoder jumps from 27.42% (joint training) to 72.72%, approaching the unimodal teacher's 76.48%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unimodal-teacher knowledge distillation line (UMT/UME [8]) by adding both feature-level and logit-level distillation losses alongside the multimodal student loss, creating a unified G²D objective. It departs from the broader gradient-modulation family (AGM, OGM-GE, MSLR, PMR, MLA) by replacing continuous partial modulation with a discrete, sequential complete-suppression schedule (SMP) driven by unimodal teacher confidence scores. The key conceptual departure is that SMP zeros out gradients of non-prioritized modalities entirely during dedicated epochs rather than merely scaling them, and the prioritization order is determined dynamically from teacher confidence rather than fixed heuristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SMP scheduling strategy is notably plug-and-play: the ablation (Table 4) shows it boosts even vanilla joint-training by over 13 points on CREMA-D, suggesting it could be adopted as a lightweight add-on to existing multimodal pipelines without architectural changes. The paper also extends the modality-imbalance analysis to a regression setting with tabular data (MIS-ME), a setting previously unexplored. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more than three modalities or to early/mid fusion architectures, how sensitive performance is to the τ_j schedule hyperparameters, and whether the requirement for separately trained unimodal teachers limits practicality in settings where unimodal baselines are unavailable.
