## Problem

Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) exhibit opaque internal representations that are both high-dimensional and polysemantic, making it difficult to understand what individual neurons or features encode. Unlike traditional vision models with a few hundred monosemantic concepts, LMMs contain hundreds of thousands of open-domain concepts, rendering exhaustive human labeling infeasible. This paper addresses the gap by proposing an automated, zero-shot pipeline that uses a larger LMM to interpret the features of a smaller one.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251155/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes RGB images (processed via the Anyres strategy yielding 576 base image tokens) and text prompts as input to LLaVA-NeXT-8B with a TopK SAE (2^17 features, k=256) hooked at layer 25. For interpretation, the top-5 most activated images per feature are selected from a cache of ~46,684 images, and their most-activated patches are masked and fed to LLaVA-OV-72B for zero-shot explanation. Outputs include textual feature descriptions, IoU-based reference scores (computed via GroundingDINO-SAM segmentation), steered model responses (by clamping feature activations to target values), and per-token/per-feature attribution maps that localize the causes of specific model decisions.

## Method

The method has three stages. First, a TopK sparse autoencoder (following OpenAI's architecture) is trained on the LLaVA-NeXT SFT dataset (~779k samples) to disentangle polysemantic hidden representations at layer 25 into monosemantic features, with the near-orthogonality of the overcomplete dictionary ensuring each coordinate captures a distinct concept. Second, an automatic interpretation pipeline identifies the top-5 images and patches that maximally activate each feature, applies transparent/black masks to highlight activation regions, and queries LLaVA-OV-72B to detect common visual patterns and generate concise explanations; a smaller LLM refines these into short phrases, and GroundingDINO-SAM produces segmentation masks for quantitative IoU scoring. Third, a steering operation clamps specific SAE feature activations to predetermined values before reconstruction, enabling behavioral intervention, while an attribution-patching procedure (with a linear approximation for efficiency) computes per-token, per-feature influence scores to localize which visual patches and text tokens drive a given model decision.

## Results

The proposed V-Interp pipeline achieves a mean IoU of 0.20 and CLIP Score of 23.6 across all visual concepts (scene, object, part, material, texture, colour), substantially outperforming the random baseline (IoU 0.005, CS 18.2). The highest IoU is observed for material features (0.39), while scene features achieve the highest CLIP Score (24.4). GPT-4o judges the explanations as consistent with the activated regions 89% of the time, compared to 75% for human raters, confirming the pipeline's reliability. Cross-layer ablations show consistent gains over random at layers 8, 25, and 32, with IoU increasing from 0.30 to 0.40 as depth increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an interpretability paper rather than a benchmark comparison, so the primary baseline is random image selection. The V-Interp pipeline outperforms random by roughly 40× on IoU (0.20 vs. 0.005) and by ~5.4 points on CLIP Score (23.6 vs. 18.2) across all six visual concept categories. No competing SAE-based or LMM-based interpretation method is directly benchmarked; the paper positions itself as the first automatic pipeline for open-semantic feature interpretation in the multimodal domain. Cross-layer ablations (Table 3) confirm the method generalizes across LLaVA layers 8, 25, and 32, with all significantly above the random baseline (IoU 0.30–0.40 vs. 0.005). Architecture ablation on InstructBLIP-7B (Table 4) shows a CLIP Score of 28.01 vs. 17.71 for random, validating cross-model generalizability. GPT-4o consistency scoring (89%) exceeds human inter-annotator agreement (75%), suggesting the automated explanations are at least as reliable as human judgments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAE-based monosemanticity framework established for LLMs (Gao et al. [14], Bricken et al. [6], Templeton et al. [37]) to the multimodal domain, and adapts the 'larger model explains smaller model' paradigm demonstrated by Bills et al. [5] for GPT-2 to the LMM setting. The key methodological departure is the integration of visual patch-level activation analysis with GroundingDINO-SAM segmentation to ground feature explanations in spatial image regions, and the use of attribution patching (Nanda [29]) to localize causes of hallucination in LMMs. Unlike prior SAE work that relied on human labeling or fixed concept taxonomies, this paper leverages a 72B LMM for zero-shot, open-semantic feature interpretation, addressing the infeasibility of manual annotation at the scale of LMM feature spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic pipeline for automatically interpreting and steering features in LMMs, offering a concrete path toward understanding why these models hallucinate and how to mitigate it with minimal intervention. However, the quantitative evidence remains thin: only 11 of 100 object neurons showed measurable steering effects, IOU scores are modest (~0.20), and the analysis is confined to a single 8B model with 5000 features. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger feature spaces and more model architectures, whether steering can be made quantitative and reliable, and whether the discovered emotional and low-level perception features generalize across LMM families. The hallucination case study, while illustrative, is a single example and does not yet establish a generalizable mitigation strategy.
