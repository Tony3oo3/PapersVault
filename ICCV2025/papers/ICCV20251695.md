## Problem

Existing text-to-motion synthesis methods treat solo (semantic) and multi-person (social) motions as separate tasks, preventing the generation of the dynamic interleaving of individual and interactive behaviors observed in real-world settings. Additionally, these methods fail to capture the mutual cues through which individuals continuously adapt their movements in response to subtle signals from others, resulting in socially desynchronized interactions.

## Input / Output

The method takes separate text prompts for solo and interaction motions (concatenated into a combined description w_u) along with interaction time indices t_i and t_s that specify where each segment begins, enabling user control over sequence composition. Motion is represented as 263-dimensional per-frame feature vectors in HumanML3D format, and the output is a 2×T×K×C tensor encoding interleaved solo and interactive 3D joint trajectories for multiple characters. The framework supports number-free generation, extending from two-person to multi-agent scenarios at inference time via the coordinator's spatial modeling.

## Method

InterSyn comprises two stages: the Interleaved Interaction Synthesis (INS) module uses a Conditional Motion Diffusion Model (a Transformer-based denoiser adapted from MDM) to jointly generate solo and interactive motions within a unified sequence, where a U(·) function fuses motions from different datasets via forward-kinematics skeleton alignment and the model is conditioned on time indices and text embeddings. The Relative Coordination Refinement (REC) module then applies a Transformer-based coordinator that asymmetrically refines one character's motion using the other as a spatial reference, enforcing social synchronization through a relative coordination loss that constrains the reference motion to remain nearly unchanged. Training proceeds in two stages—INS is trained first with an alternating strategy mixing interleaved and single-person data, then REC is trained with INS frozen—using a combined loss of reconstruction, boundary smoothness, relative coordination, and masked joint distance map terms. The key insight is that interleaving solo and interactive segments in a single diffusion sequence (rather than processing them through separate pathways) eliminates modality bias at transitions, while the REC module's mutual-dynamics modeling ensures physically plausible interpersonal spacing and synchronized coordination.

## Results

On the InterHuman test set, InterSyn achieves a FID of 6.332, Top-1 R-Precision of 0.335, and MModality of 1.601, outperforming all baselines on FID and R-Precision. On the interleaved motion benchmark (unifying HumanML3D and InterHuman), InterSyn reduces FID by 42.1% and improves Top-1 R-Precision by 6.4% over FreeMotion. The method also achieves near-real diversity (7.763 vs. 7.748 ground truth) while maintaining the highest MModality among multi-person methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InterSyn outperforms FreeMotion [6] (the closest competitor) on FID (6.332 vs. 6.740), Top-3 R-Precision (0.584 vs. 0.544), and MModality (1.601 vs. 1.226, a 31% gain), while MM Dist and Diversity are essentially tied. Against InterGen [20], InterSyn achieves a 25.3% improvement in Top-1 R-Precision and a 50.6% reduction in FID (6.332 vs. 13.404). Compared to single-person methods MDM [37] and ComMDM [34], InterSyn reduces MM Dist by 37.0% and 26.2% respectively, confirming superior multi-person spatial coherence. InterSyn leads all baselines on FID and all three R-Precision tiers, but MDM retains a higher MModality (2.355 vs. 1.601), reflecting MDM's broader single-person motion variety. On the interleaved benchmark (Table 2), InterSyn further narrows the gap to FreeMotion, achieving 42.1% lower FID (0.417 vs. 0.721) and 6.4% higher Top-1 R-Precision (0.298 vs. 0.280).

## Relation to Prior Work

InterSyn builds directly on the MDM diffusion framework [36] for single-person motion generation and InterGen's [20] cooperative transformer denoiser for two-person interactions, but departs from both by unifying solo and multi-person motions into a single interleaved training sequence rather than treating them as separate tasks. The paper positions itself against FreeMotion [6], which also targets number-free generation but processes individual and group motions through separate pathways; InterSyn instead encodes both in a shared latent space with explicit temporal conditioning (t_i, t_s) to control where interactions begin. The forward-kinematics-based skeleton alignment (Eq. 5) is a practical bridge between the HumanML3D and InterHuman datasets that prior multi-person methods have not needed to address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The interleaved learning paradigm addresses a genuine gap: real human behavior fluidly transitions between solo actions and social interactions, yet prior methods force a hard split between single-person and multi-person generation. The 40% FID reduction over FreeMotion and the ablation showing that the coordinator module is critical (57.6% MM Dist degradation without it) validate the two-stage design. However, the approach is currently limited to two-person interactions, the optimal interleaving pattern is simply 's-i-s' (more complex patterns degrade performance), and the code is not yet publicly available, limiting reproducibility. Open questions include whether the framework scales to more than two agents, longer sequences, or diverse interaction types beyond the InterHuman dataset's scope.
