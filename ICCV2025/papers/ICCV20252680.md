## Problem

Learning-based sparse-view camera pose estimation models rely heavily on background textures that dominate the image, yet their vulnerability to adversarial background patterns has not been studied. This paper asks whether specific texture priors—particularly multi-fold radial symmetry—can be exploited to consistently disrupt pose estimation across viewpoints in object-centric scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252680/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single segment image (w×h×3, where w and h are derived from the disc radius ρ and segment angle θ=2π/N) and constructs a full kaleidoscopic disc of size 2ρ×2ρ×3 by perspective-projecting and summing N rotated copies. In the physical world, the disc is printed at 15–20 cm radius and imaged by five industrial cameras at 20–50 cm distance; in the digital world, it is rendered at 1 m radius with PyTorch3D. The optimized output is the segment image I_s, which when deployed causes pose estimation models to output near-identical rotation matrices and overlapping translation vectors across all views.

## Method

The approach constructs an N-fold radially symmetric disc (N=12) from a single texture segment, ensuring high background similarity across viewpoints by design. To optimize the segment, the authors propose a projected orientation consistency loss (L_poc) that operates on DUSt3R's pointmap outputs: for each of the three camera-coordinate channels, it computes the average coordinate flow direction within the disc region using three bisection lines at 30° intervals, then maximizes the cosine similarity of these flow directions between two views. This loss is shown to approximate the cosine similarity of projected camera orientation vectors, effectively degrading a multi-view system into a single-view one. Optimization proceeds via PGD (sign-gradient updates with step size 1/255) over differentiable renders with random object/background selection, and periodic CMYK color clipping ensures physical-world printability.

## Results

In the physical world, KBA_opt reduces RRA@15 from 1.00 to 0.00 and mAA(30) from 0.95 to 0.00 on the white-box DUSt3R model, while achieving RRS of 0.94 (near-perfect pose orientation collapse). Transferability to black-box models is strong: KBA_opt drops RRA@15 to 0.06 on MASt3R, 0.06 on Ray Diffusion, 0.10 on Ray Regression, 0.14 on PoseDiffusion, and 0.20 on RelPose++, compared to their respective Nature baselines of 0.99, 0.79, 0.79, 0.64, and 0.57. The optimized KBA_opt consistently outperforms the natural-texture KBA_nat across all models and metrics, with notably smaller standard deviations indicating more stable attack effectiveness across object categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper attacks six sparse-view pose estimation models using a kaleidoscopic background optimized against DUSt3R (white-box) and evaluates transferability to five black-box models. KBA_opt achieves near-complete pose collapse on DUSt3R (RRA@15 = 0.00, mAA(30) = 0.00) and transfers effectively to MASt3R (RRA@15 drops from 0.99 to 0.06), Ray Diffusion (0.79→0.06), and Ray Regression (0.79→0.10). The attack is somewhat less effective on RelPose++ (RRA@15: 0.57→0.20) and PoseDiffusion (0.64→0.14), but still represents a 65–78% reduction in pose accuracy. Across all models, KBA_opt consistently outperforms KBA_nat by 2–5× in RRA@15 reduction, and the optimized version shows markedly lower standard deviations (e.g., 0.02 vs 0.19 on DUSt3R RRA@15), indicating more stable attacks across diverse object categories. The RRS metric confirms that KBA_opt drives predicted relative rotations toward near-identical orientations (RRS ≈ 0.86–0.94) without explicitly constraining camera positions, effectively collapsing a multi-view system into a pseudo-single-view one.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the adversarial patch/texture attack literature (Brown et al., Kurakin et al., Hu et al.) for a new target: sparse-view camera pose estimation. Rather than optimizing a standalone patch or a single texture image, it exploits the geometric property of multi-fold radial symmetry to construct a background disc that naturally maintains high visual similarity across viewpoints, making it physically realizable without per-view optimization. The projected orientation consistency loss is a novel formulation that operates directly on pointmap outputs (as produced by DUSt3R/MASt3R), circumventing the intractability of backpropagating through iterative global alignment. The authors explicitly position this as the first work to use background textures as adversarial examples against pose estimation models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals a concrete, physically deployable vulnerability in a broad family of learning-based pose estimation models, suggesting that reliance on background context—while beneficial for sparse-view performance—creates an exploitable attack surface. The strong transferability across six architecturally diverse models (DUSt3R, MASt3R, Ray Diffusion, Ray Regression, PoseDiffusion, RelPose++) in the physical world indicates the vulnerability is not model-specific but stems from a shared inductive bias. Open questions include whether defenses (e.g., background-agnostic training, inpainting-based robustness) can mitigate this without sacrificing accuracy, and whether the vulnerability extends to scene-centric or dynamic settings beyond the object-centric disc configuration tested here.
