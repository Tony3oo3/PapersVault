## Problem

Existing medical image synthesis methods either generate images without segmentation masks, generate uncontrolled image-mask pairs, or require pre-existing mask annotations as static conditions—making them either useless for downstream segmentation or infeasible at scale due to annotation costs. No prior framework can produce both a medical image and its corresponding segmentation mask from a simple text description alone, leaving a gap in on-demand, controllable paired data generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252537/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The sole input is a concise text prompt specifying the target label, imaging modality (CT, MRI, ultrasound, colonoscopy), anatomical region, and pathological condition—no mask or reference image is required. The model outputs a 256×256 medical image and its spatially aligned segmentation mask, generated via a 50-step denoising process with classifier-free guidance (scale 7.5). The paired outputs are guaranteed to be semantically consistent by construction, since both are produced simultaneously through a shared denoising trajectory.

## Method

MedSegFactory employs a dual-stream diffusion architecture built on Stable Diffusion, where one U-Net stream denoises the medical image latent and the other denoises the segmentation mask latent in parallel. The key innovation is the Joint Cross-Attention (JCA) mechanism, which inserts two cross-attention layers at each denoising step: the image stream attends to the mask stream's intermediate features (mask-to-image) and the mask stream attends to the image stream's features (image-to-mask), enabling dynamic bidirectional conditioning rather than relying on a static mask input. Both streams are also conditioned on their respective text prompt embeddings via standard cross-attention, and the overall training objective is a joint L1 noise-prediction loss over both streams. This collaborative denoising ensures that anatomical structures in the image and boundaries in the mask are mutually refined at every timestep, producing tightly aligned pairs without any pre-existing annotation.

## Results

On downstream 3D CT segmentation (nnUNet augmented with synthetic data), MedSegFactory achieves the best DSC on LiTS17 (62.37%) and KiTS19 (58.03%), outperforming the real-data-only nnUNet baseline by +2.60 and +1.38 DSC points respectively. On 2D tasks, MedSegFactory leads on ACDC (88.02% DSC) and BUSI (83.38% DSC), with a +4.61 DSC gain over nnUNet on BUSI. For image generation quality, MedSegFactory achieves the best Inception Score on AMOS (3.62) and competitive FID scores, though DiffBoost attains lower FID on KiTS19 (84.66 vs. 95.87).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MedSegFactory outperforms all baselines on LiTS17 (62.37% DSC vs. 60.64% ControlNet, 59.92% DiffBoost, 58.47% MAISI) and KiTS19 (58.03% DSC vs. 56.96% DiffBoost, 56.88% ControlNet), with gains of 1.7–3.9 DSC points over the strongest competitor. On AMOS, ControlNet narrowly edges MedSegFactory (77.16% vs. 76.76% DSC), while on CVC-ClinicDB ControlNet also leads slightly (91.11% vs. 90.85% DSC). For image generation fidelity, DiffBoost achieves the best FID on KiTS19 (84.66) and AMOS (73.61), surpassing MedSegFactory (95.87 and 97.94), though MedSegFactory attains the highest Inception Score on AMOS (3.62). MAISI, designed for 3D-only generation, consistently underperforms on both generation quality (FID >158) and downstream segmentation, confirming the advantage of the dual-stream 2D approach. Overall, MedSegFactory's text-prompt-driven paired generation yields the most consistent improvements across heterogeneous modalities without requiring pre-existing mask annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

MedSegFactory builds directly on the Stable Diffusion (SDM) latent diffusion architecture, repurposing its U-Net backbone and VAE encoder-decoder for a dual-stream medical image-mask generation setting. It departs from the three established categories of medical synthesis—unconditional image generation, unconditional paired generation, and mask-conditioned image generation—by introducing a fourth: text-prompt-conditioned paired image-mask generation. The key architectural departure is the Joint Cross-Attention (JCA) mechanism, which replaces the static mask conditioning used by ControlNet and similar methods with dynamic bidirectional cross-conditioning between the image and mask denoising streams, allowing both to refine each other iteratively rather than treating the mask as a fixed input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for pre-existing segmentation masks as conditioning inputs, MedSegFactory significantly lowers the barrier to creating customized synthetic medical datasets, addressing a core bottleneck in computer-aided detection where annotation costs and privacy regulations limit data availability. However, the current 2D-only architecture limits applicability to volumetric CT/MRI tasks, and the paper acknowledges blurred boundaries when no segmentation target is specified in the prompt. The FID scores (90–280 range) indicate that generated images still deviate substantially from real data distributions, suggesting room for quality improvement. Extending the framework to 3D volumetric generation and refining prompt-conditioning strategies for target-free scenarios are the most pressing open questions.
