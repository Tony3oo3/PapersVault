## Problem

In source-free domain adaptive object detection (SF-DAOD), existing student-teacher frameworks suffer from severe training instability because noisy pseudo-labels—caused by domain bias and large domain shifts—propagate through EMA updates and collapse the teacher model. Without access to labeled source data to anchor training, these cumulative errors are particularly destructive, and prior mitigations (larger EMA steps, dynamic teachers) have shown limited effectiveness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252272/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled target-domain images (natural RGB street scenes at 1024×2048 or mammography images) together with a frozen source-pretrained FocalNet-DINO detector, requiring no source data at any stage. Each target image undergoes M stochastic forward passes with dropout enabled to estimate a per-image detection variance (product of bounding-box variance and class-score variance), which partitions the target set into source-similar (easy) and source-dissimilar (hard) subsets. The final output is a set of bounding boxes with class labels, evaluated via mAP on natural benchmarks and FROC curves (recall at fixed false positives per image) on medical benchmarks.

## Method

TITAN introduces a variance-based target domain division: Monte Carlo dropout estimates per-image detection variance, and a rank-normalized threshold σ splits targets into easy (high variance, source-similar) and hard (low variance, source-dissimilar) subsets. Within a FocalNet-DINO student-teacher framework, two complementary adversarial alignment mechanisms are applied at every encoder and decoder layer: (1) a learnable domain query token is prepended to the encoder token sequence and to the decoder object-query sequence, then fed to a domain discriminator to align global scene-layout and inter-object features; (2) each individual token embedding is independently classified by a separate discriminator to align local texture and style. These losses are cascaded across all L_enc and L_dec layers with a balancing hyperparameter (λ_q = 0.1), and the overall min-max objective jointly optimizes the detector G against the discriminators D while the student is supervised by pseudo-labels from the teacher. A covering-number bound on the 3-layer MLP discriminators provides a theoretical generalization guarantee for the adversarial transformation.

## Results

On the C2F benchmark (Cityscapes → Foggy Cityscapes), TITAN achieves 52.2 mAP, surpassing the best source-free method LPLD (40.9) by +11.3 and even exceeding the best UDA method MRT (51.2) that has access to source data. On C2B (Cityscapes → BDD100k), TITAN reaches 38.3 mAP vs. AASFOD's 31.6 (+6.7). On the single-class car detection tasks, TITAN achieves 59.8 AP on S2C (Sim10k → Cityscapes) and 53.2 AP on K2C (KITTI → Cityscapes), outperforming PETS (57.8) and LPLD (51.3) respectively. On medical imaging, TITAN achieves R@0.3 = 0.78 on R2In and 0.51 on D2In, dramatically outperforming the previous best LPLD (0.25 and 0.15).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TITAN outperforms all 35 compared baselines across all six benchmarks. On C2F, it surpasses the strongest source-free method LPLD (ECCV'24) by +11.3 mAP (52.2 vs. 40.9) and also beats the best UDA method MRT (ICCV'23, 51.2 mAP) despite not having access to source data. On C2B, TITAN leads AASFOD (AAAI'23) by +6.7 mAP (38.3 vs. 31.6) and PETS (ICCV'23) by +7.0 mAP. On the single-class car detection tasks, TITAN achieves 59.8 AP on S2C (beating PETS by +2.0) and 53.2 AP on K2C (beating LPLD by +1.9). The most dramatic gains appear in medical imaging: on R2In, TITAN achieves R@0.3 = 0.78 vs. LPLD's 0.25 (+0.53), and on D2In, R@0.3 = 0.51 vs. LPLD's 0.15 (+0.36), with AUC improvements from 0.548 to 0.892 (R2In) and 0.548 to 0.825 (D2In). Notably, TITAN's per-class gains on C2F are most pronounced for Truck (+13.6 over LPLD) and Bus (+19.2 over LPLD), suggesting the variance-based target division is particularly effective for rare or domain-sensitive categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

TITAN builds on the FocalNet-DINO transformer detector as its backbone and extends the student-teacher pseudo-label framework that dominates SF-DAOD (AASFOD, PETS, IRG, LODS). Its key departure is a variance-based target domain division using Monte Carlo dropout (following Gal & Ghahramani) to separate source-similar from source-dissimilar samples, combined with a dual-level adversarial alignment strategy—query-token for global scene layout and token-wise for local texture—that prior SF-DAOD methods did not jointly exploit. The paper explicitly positions against the training-instability problem caused by noisy pseudo-labels and EMA drift, which prior remedies (larger EMA steps, dynamic teachers) addressed only partially.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The variance-based division offers a simple, architecture-agnostic criterion for identifying which target samples can be trusted for self-training, addressing a core practical bottleneck in SF-DAOD. The medical imaging results (3× recall improvement on mammography benchmarks) highlight a setting where source-free adaptation is not just convenient but necessary due to privacy constraints and single-center bias. Open questions include whether the predefined threshold σ generalizes across domain pairs without tuning, whether the approach extends beyond FocalNet-DINO to other detector families, and whether the MC-dropout variance proxy remains reliable under extreme domain shifts where the source model's uncertainty calibration degrades.
