## Problem

Existing CT reconstruction methods face a fundamental trade-off: deep learning-based approaches require large paired training datasets and generalize poorly across scanner geometries, while instance-reconstruction methods (NeRF, 3DGS) are misaligned with tomographic objectives—NeRF is too slow for clinical use, and 3DGS suffers from integration bias in the discretized volume domain. No prior method directly models the 3D volume as discretized Gaussians with an efficient, differentiable aggregation pipeline tailored to CT.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250743/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw X-ray projection data (sinograms) along with CT scan geometry parameters (source/detector positions, number of views, detector elements) and requires no additional training data. It outputs a discretized 3D CT volume (e.g., 256×256×256 voxels) whose intensity at each voxel is the summed contribution of all Gaussians. The general projection transformation T is geometry-agnostic, supporting cone-beam, fan-beam, sparse-view, and limited-angle configurations without architectural changes.

## Method

DGR represents the 3D volume as a set of isotropic Gaussians (150K initial) whose contributions are confined to a local 17×17×17 box around each voxel center. A 'Discretize and Align' technique computes each Gaussian's contribution at floored integer grid positions while preserving differentiability through the continuous center via a residual offset Δμ. Fast Volume Reconstruction decomposes the squared Mahalanobis distance into four smaller Einstein sums, reducing per-iteration reconstruction time from 1.05 s to 0.09 s for a 256³ volume. All Gaussians are jointly optimized in projection space (not per-view or per-block as in 3DGS) using a combined L1 + SSIM + TV loss, converging in under 1K iterations with standard adaptive density control (clone/split/prune).

## Results

On the FIPS dataset (Cone-Beam Sparse-View CT), DGR with 1000 iterations achieves PSNR 41.28 / SSIM 0.952 at 75 views (real-world), surpassing the closest 3DGS-based competitor R²-Gaussian (39.40 / 0.875 at 30k iterations) by ~1.9 dB PSNR while using 30× fewer iterations. On AAPM-Mayo LDCT (Fan-Beam Sparse-View CT), DGR reaches PSNR 46.13 / SSIM 0.997 at 180 views without any training data, outperforming the best DLR method SWORD (45.08 / 0.994) which requires 4839 training images. For Limited-Angle CT (90°), DGR achieves PSNR 38.22 (axial) versus 34.92 for DiffusionMBIR, a 3.3 dB improvement. Reconstruction time is approximately 3 minutes for 300 iterations on FIPS, compared to 13+ hours for NeRF-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGR outperforms all 16 baselines across every evaluated task. Against NeRF-based instance reconstruction methods, DGR (1000 iter) leads SAX-NeRF [2] by 6.35 dB PSNR (41.28 vs 34.93) and NAF [40] by 2.70 dB at 75-view FIPS real-world, while also being 4–13× faster. Among 3DGS-based methods, DGR surpasses R²-Gaussian [41] (30k iter) by 1.88 dB PSNR and 0.077 SSIM at 75 views, and X-Gaussian [3] by 3.01 dB, all while using far fewer iterations (1k vs 30k). In the DLR comparison on AAPM-Mayo LDCT, DGR requires zero training data yet beats SWORD [37] (the strongest DLR baseline) by 1.05 dB PSNR at 180 views and 1.76 dB at 60 views, and outperforms FBPConvNet [16], PLANet [38], GMSD [10], and U-Net [23] by 3–8 dB. For Limited-Angle CT (90°), DGR exceeds DiffusionMBIR [6] by 3.30 dB PSNR (axial) and Lahiri et al. [22] by 10.14 dB, with all DLR baselines requiring 3142–3839 training images. The traditional FBP [1] and FDK [7] baselines are consistently the weakest, trailing DGR by 10–23 dB across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

DGR builds directly on the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework [19] but departs from it in a fundamental way: rather than adapting 3DGS's view-oriented differentiable rendering pipeline to CT (as R²-Gaussian [41] and X-Gaussian [3] attempt), DGR recognizes that CT volumes are inherently discretized voxel grids and designs the Gaussian representation to be discretized from the outset. This sidesteps the integration bias problem that motivated R²-Gaussian's rectified rasterizer/voxelizer, and avoids the hours-long optimization of NeRF-based methods (NAF, IntraTomo, SAX-NeRF). The 'Discretize and Align' technique for maintaining differentiability through the continuous-to-discrete transition is the paper's core conceptual contribution, distinguishing it from both the DLR line (FBPConvNet, SWORD, DiffusionMBIR) and prior 3DGS-for-CT adaptations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DGR's ability to match or exceed diffusion-based DLR methods (SWORD, DiffusionMBIR) while requiring zero training data addresses a critical practical barrier: DLR models trained on one scanner geometry or body region often degrade on others, whereas DGR is geometry-agnostic by construction. The 0.09s per-iteration volume reconstruction from 150K Gaussians, enabling convergence in under 1K iterations versus 30K for R²-Gaussian, makes per-instance CT reconstruction clinically feasible for the first time. Open questions include whether the isotropic Gaussian assumption holds for highly anisotropic structures (e.g., cortical bone, air-tissue interfaces), how the method scales to larger clinical volumes (512³ or beyond), and whether the adaptive density control (cloning/splitting/pruning) remains stable at higher iteration counts.
