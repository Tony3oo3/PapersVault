## Problem

Monocular camera calibration is inherently ill-posed, and existing methods either rely on handcrafted geometric assumptions (Manhattan World, gravity direction) that fail to generalize across diverse real-world scenes, or use learning-based regression constrained by limited public datasets, leading to poor zero-shot performance. While stable diffusion models trained on billion-scale data implicitly encode focal-length-dependent imaging characteristics, no prior work has effectively leveraged these priors for high-precision intrinsic estimation without requiring auxiliary geometric supervision.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image of arbitrary resolution as input and produces a dense 3-channel Camera Image (azimuth, elevation, grayscale) from which the four intrinsic parameters (f_x, f_y, c_x, c_y) are recovered via RANSAC line fitting. Unlike direct regression approaches that output scalar values, the output is structured as a spatially dense image whose per-pixel values encode the per-ray direction, enabling robust extraction through consensus over all pixels. Downstream, the calibrated intrinsics feed into a one-step deterministic depth network producing metric depth maps, and into DUST3R for sparse-view 3D reconstruction and pose estimation.

## Method

The key contribution is the Camera Image representation, which encodes camera intrinsics as a 3-channel image of per-pixel azimuth θ, elevation φ, and input grayscale g, preserving high-frequency scene details to minimize the domain gap with real images that diffusion models were trained on. A frozen VAE encodes both the input image and its ground-truth Camera Image into latent space; multi-resolution noise is added to the camera latent, which is concatenated with the image latent and fed into a U-Net with doubled input channels fine-tuned from Stable Diffusion v2.1. Training minimizes a v-prediction noise-matching loss, and at inference the denoised camera latent is decoded by the VAE to yield the Camera Image, from which RANSAC recovers intrinsics by fitting the linear relationships between pixel coordinates and ray angles. For metric depth estimation, the multi-step stochastic diffusion is reformulated as a one-step deterministic forward pass with a trainable VAE decoder, allowing unbounded depth value prediction beyond the decoder's default range.

## Results

On monocular camera calibration (the primary task), DM-Calib achieves a mean focal-length relative error (e_f) of 0.078 and optical-center error (e_b) of 0.017 averaged across five zero-shot datasets, outperforming the strongest diffusion-based baseline DiffCalib-D (e_f = 0.095, e_b = 0.041) by ~18% on e_f and ~59% on e_b. On zero-shot metric depth estimation, the method achieves δ₁ = 96.0 / SIlog = 6.35 on NYUv2 and δ₁ = 94.4 / SIlog = 7.15 on IBims-1, matching or exceeding UniDepth (δ₁ = 97.2 / SIlog = 6.41 on NYUv2) while surpassing Metric3D (δ₁ = 92.6 / SIlog = 9.13) by a wide margin. For sparse-view 3D reconstruction, incorporating DM-Calib intrinsics as geometry cues reduces the mean relative distance error by ~20% across four in-the-wild scenes compared to DUST3R without intrinsic cues.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DM-Calib outperforms all camera-calibration baselines on every zero-shot dataset. Against the closest diffusion-based competitor DiffCalib-D [23] (which jointly trains with depth), DM-Calib reduces mean e_f from 0.095 to 0.078 (−18%) and e_b from 0.041 to 0.017 (−59%), with the largest gains on RGBD (e_f 0.084→0.041) and MVS (e_f 0.108→0.087, e_b 0.036→0.008). Non-diffusion methods trail further: WildCame [96] averages e_f = 0.155, GeoCalib [72] 0.215, and Perspective [30] 0.239, all roughly 2× worse than DM-Calib. UniDepth [46] is competitive on RGBD (e_f = 0.055) but degrades severely on unconstrained outdoor data (MVS e_f = 0.482, Scenes11 e_f = 0.510), likely due to unbalanced multi-task training. For metric depth, DM-Calib matches UniDepth [46] on NYUv2 (δ₁ 96.0 vs 97.2) and IBims-1 (δ₁ 94.4 vs 79.4, SIlog 7.15 vs 8.88), surpasses Metric3D [85] on all six depth benchmarks (e.g., NuScenes δ₁ 85.7 vs 72.3), and clearly outperforms iDisc [45] and ZoeDepth [4] which were designed for affine-invariant rather than metric depth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based camera calibration line initiated by DiffCalib (He et al., 2024), which first formulated monocular intrinsic estimation as dense incident map generation. The key departure is the proposed Camera Image representation—a 3-channel encoding of azimuth, elevation, and input grayscale—that replaces incident maps to close the domain gap with the image distribution diffusion models were trained on. The broader methodological foundation is Stable Diffusion v2.1 (Rombach et al., 2022), fine-tuned via a U-Net with doubled input channels, while the downstream depth estimation component builds on the one-step deterministic repurposing of diffusion generators explored by GeoWizard (Fu et al., 2024) and Ke et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that pre-trained diffusion models implicitly encode focal-length-dependent imaging characteristics, this work opens a path toward calibration methods that generalize to in-the-wild images without handcrafted geometric assumptions or large labeled calibration datasets. The practical impact is broad: the estimated intrinsics directly improve metric depth, sparse-view reconstruction (DUST3R), and pose estimation, making the method a potential drop-in component for 3D vision pipelines. However, performance degrades on ultra-wide-angle images due to training data scarcity, and the multi-step denoising pipeline limits inference speed; the authors note that few-step consistency models could address this. An open question is whether the Camera Image representation generalizes to non-pinhole or fisheye camera models.
