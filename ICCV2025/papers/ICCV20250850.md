## Problem

Most co-speech gesture synthesis methods focus on holistic body movements and neglect hand gestures with explicit, verifiable semantics (e.g., numbers, directions) that are critical in real-world communication. Existing datasets contain very few instructional hand gestures, and the inherent many-to-many mapping between speech audio and gestures makes reliable activation of specific semantic gestures a persistent challenge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250850/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes speech audio (encoded via wav2vec 2.0), a short seed gesture clip of S frames in SMPL-X parameter space, and a motion style reference sequence. In RAG mode, it additionally accepts a single annotated key-frame from a per-identity semantic gesture repository. The output is a full 3D SMPL-X body gesture sequence synchronized to the audio, with the hybrid-modality design also supporting partial gesture inputs for inpainting, interpolation, and replacement operations.

## Method

GestureHYDRA builds a Hybrid-Modality Diffusion Transformer (HM-DiT) on top of MDM, trained with four masking strategies (Start-Only, Start-End, Random-Frame, Random-Seg) that unify co-speech generation and motion inpainting into a single model. A motion-style injective transformer layer replaces one-hot identity embeddings by attending over both a dynamic style embedding (from a style encoder on an external reference) and a static learnable memory bank, improving generalization to unseen identities. At inference, a cascaded-synchronized RAG pipeline first performs standard co-speech generation, then retrieves a semantic gesture key frame from a per-identity repository and injects it at an adaptively adjusted timestamp found via binary search over a beat-consistency score, ensuring the injected gesture is rhythmically synchronized with the driving audio.

## Results

On the Streamer dataset (seen identity), GestureHYDRA achieves an FGD of 3.24, a ΔBC of 0.003, a Semantic Activation Rate (SAR) of 84.82%, SMD-L1 of 0.107, and SMD-DTW of 20.70, outperforming all baselines on every metric. On the unseen identity test set, it maintains strong generalization with FGD 15.43, SAR 81.36%, SMD-L1 0.143, and SMD-DTW 27.73. On the SHOW dataset, it achieves the best FGD (3.68) and ΔBC (0.006) among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GestureHYDRA outperforms all three baselines (TalkSHOW, Probtalk, and DSG) on every metric in both seen and unseen identity settings. The most dramatic gap is in FGD: on the seen set, GestureHYDRA scores 3.24 versus 50.33–54.59 for the baselines, and on the unseen set, 15.43 versus 61.94–75.35. For semantic gesture quality, the SAR advantage is particularly large on the unseen set (81.36% vs. 31.81% for TalkSHOW, 66.08% for Probtalk, 68.77% for DSG), indicating that the hybrid-modality training and motion-style injection substantially improve generalization to new identities. On the SHOW dataset, GestureHYDRA also leads in FGD (3.68 vs. 5.46–6.33) and ΔBC (0.006 vs. 0.022–0.046), confirming superiority in both feature distribution and beat synchronization. The VQ-VAE-based baselines (TalkSHOW, Probtalk) suffer the most degradation on unseen identities due to limited codebook capacity, while the diffusion-based DSG is closer but still trails GestureHYDRA by a wide margin on all semantic metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Human Motion Diffusion Model (MDM) as its diffusion transformer backbone and adopts the gesture-audio fusion module and velocity loss from DiffSheg. It departs from prior co-speech gesture generation (TalkSHOW, ProbTalk, DSG) in three key ways: (1) a hybrid-modality input design that jointly accepts audio and partial gesture signals, enabling inpainting and editing beyond pure generation; (2) a motion-style injective transformer layer that replaces sparse one-hot identity embeddings with dynamic+static style representations for better generalization; and (3) a cascaded-synchronized RAG strategy that injects a single annotated key frame with adaptive timestamp adjustment, rather than merging or inserting entire retrieved gesture segments as in Semantic Gesticulator or SIGesture. The newly introduced Streamer dataset targets explicit instructional hand gestures (numbers, directions) in a livestreaming context, filling a gap left by prior semantic gesture datasets that focus on coarser, more ambiguous semantic associations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The emphasis on reliable activation of explicit semantic hand gestures addresses a practical bottleneck in digital human and livestreaming applications where specific gestures (e.g., showing a price with fingers) must be produced on cue. The hybrid-modality masking framework is a clean and generalizable design that unifies generation, inpainting, and editing under one model, which could be adopted in other motion synthesis settings. However, the Streamer dataset is self-built and relatively small (281 actors, ~21k clips), the RAG pipeline requires a per-identity pre-annotated gesture repository, and the evaluation is limited to two datasets with a small number of baselines. Open questions include whether the adaptive timestamp adjustment scales to longer sequences or multiple simultaneous gesture activations, and whether the approach generalizes to non-Chinese or multi-speaker scenarios.
