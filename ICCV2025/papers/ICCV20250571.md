## Problem

State-of-the-art video diffusion models can synthesize realistic single-shot clips up to a minute long, but real-world narrative videos consist of multiple shots that require visual consistency (character identity, lighting, color tone) and temporal consistency (action continuity, camera movement) across shot boundaries. Existing scene-level approaches—appearance-conditioned generation and keyframe-based I2V pipelines—struggle to maintain abstract visual elements and fail to guarantee temporal coherence, since each shot is synthesized independently.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a two-tier text prompt (a global prompt describing shared characters, environment, and story, plus per-shot prompts detailing individual events) and optionally accepts reference images or video clips as visual conditions by setting their diffusion timestep low. Training uses scene-level videos at 480×480 area resolution with native aspect ratios, with a maximum context window of nine shots. The output is a coherent multi-shot video sequence (or a single-shot video), supporting joint denoising, visual-conditioned generation, and auto-regressive shot-by-shot generation modes.

## Method

Long Context Tuning (LCT) adapts a pre-trained 3B-parameter single-shot MMDiT video diffusion model to scene-level generation without adding parameters. Three key mechanisms enable this: (1) the full self-attention is expanded from individual shots to all text and video tokens across the entire scene (Long-context MMDiT); (2) an interleaved 3D RoPE assigns each shot a unique absolute position along the space diagonal while preserving the relative text-video token positions inherited from the single-shot model, so each shot retains its text-visual alignment; (3) an asynchronous noise strategy samples independent diffusion timesteps per shot, unifying visual conditioning and generation—shots with low noise naturally serve as appearance conditions for noisier shots. After LCT, the bidirectional model is further fine-tuned (9K steps) to a context-causal attention variant (bidirectional within each shot, causal across shots), enabling efficient auto-regressive generation with KV-cache that eliminates redundant recomputation of history tokens.

## Results

On scene-level video generation, LCT achieves the highest text alignment score (30.14) and user study ranking (3.79/5) among all compared methods, indicating superior semantic coherence and cross-shot consistency. However, it trails baselines on visual quality metrics: aesthetic score (60.79 vs. 61.68 for VideoStudio) and quality score (67.44 vs. 74.04 for StoryDiffusion+Kling). Temporal consistency scores are competitive across all methods (95.65 for LCT vs. 95.25–96.57 for baselines), with no single method dominating this dimension.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LCT clearly outperforms all three baselines on text alignment (30.14 vs. 28.00 for VideoStudio, 27.33 for StoryDiffusion+Kling, 27.90 for IC-LoRA+Kling) and user study ranking (3.79 vs. 2.14, 2.50, 1.57), confirming superior scene-level semantic coherence and cross-shot consistency. On visual quality, LCT lags behind: VideoStudio achieves 73.13 quality and 61.68 aesthetic, while StoryDiffusion+Kling reaches 74.04 quality, compared to LCT's 67.44 quality and 60.79 aesthetic. Temporal consistency is roughly on par across all methods (95.25–96.57 range), with StoryDiffusion+Kling slightly ahead at 96.57. The paper attributes the quality gap to the absence of explicit appearance conditioning, while the semantic advantage stems from direct joint modeling of cross-shot relationships rather than relying on sparse keyframes or predefined identity embeddings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DiT/MMDiT architecture (Peebles & Xie; Esser et al.) by expanding the full-attention context window from a single shot to an entire multi-shot scene, using an interleaved 3D RoPE (building on Su et al.'s RoPE and conceptually related to M-RoPE from Qwen2-VL) to disambiguate shot boundaries. It departs from prior scene-level generation approaches—appearance-conditioned methods (VideoStudio, VideoBooth) and keyframe-then-I2V pipelines (StoryDiffusion, IC-LoRA + Kling)—by treating cross-shot consistency as a long-context modeling problem solvable within a single diffusion forward pass, rather than as an external conditioning or two-stage generation problem. The asynchronous per-shot noise strategy unifies visual conditioning and joint denoising within one training objective, eliminating the need for auxiliary control networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LCT offers a parameter-free, architecturally simple path to scene-level video generation that also unlocks emergent capabilities (compositional generation from separate identity/environment images, interactive shot extension) without task-specific training, suggesting that long-context modeling in diffusion transformers may generalize beyond its training distribution. However, the approach trades visual quality for semantic consistency (lower aesthetic/quality scores than baselines in Table 1), and autoregressive generation still suffers from error accumulation that requires careful timestep tuning. Open questions include whether the context-causal KV-cache approach scales beyond nine shots, whether MLLM-based scene planning can replace the current human-in-the-loop history selection, and whether token-level dynamic routing can reduce the redundancy of attending to all preceding shots.
