## Problem

Existing 3DGS stylization methods rely on per-scene, per-style gradient-based optimization, making rapid style switching impractical and causing GPU memory to scale linearly with scene size. No feed-forward, zero-shot approach existed that could transfer an arbitrary image style to an arbitrary 3DGS scene in seconds without any optimization step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251332/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D style image (features extracted via VGG) and a 3DGS scene represented as N Gaussian primitives with fixed geometry, opacity, and spatial coordinates; only the per-primitive color attribute is modified. The output is a stylized 3DGS scene with the same geometric structure but recolored primitives. The feed-forward design imposes no theoretical limit on the number of primitives—scenes exceeding 15 million Gaussians are processed in batches of local clusters.

## Method

A³GS uses a 3D-GCN-based autoencoder with learnable deformable convolutional kernels (from Lin et al., CVPR 2020) to encode local clusters of Gaussian primitive colors into a downsampled latent space, then decode back to full-resolution colors. In the latent bottleneck, an AdaIN module injects style statistics from VGG-extracted image features, with two MLP mappings (φ and ψ) bridging the 3D feature space and the image feature space to avoid misalignment. Training is two-stage: first the autoencoder is trained with an RGB reconstruction loss to learn faithful color encoding/decoding, then the autoencoder is frozen and the AdaIN stylizer is trained with a weighted content-plus-style loss on randomly rendered novel views. A background masking strategy prevents the empty background of generated objects from corrupting the style loss. The entire pipeline is trained on 40k 3DGS objects (generated from Objaverse via TriplaneGaussian) and 90k WikiArt style images, enabling zero-shot transfer to unseen scenes and styles.

## Results

On multi-view consistency (lower is better), A³GS achieves the best short-range RMSE (0.029) and long-range RMSE (0.057), while tying StyleSplat on short-range LPIPS (0.033) and trailing slightly on long-range LPIPS (0.061 vs. 0.052). In the user study, A³GS scores the highest on Style Consistency (4.1/5) and Visual Naturalness (4.5/5), and ties StyleSplat on Content Preservation (4.5/5). In terms of speed, A³GS completes style transfer in ~10 seconds per scene, an order-of-magnitude speedup over StyleSplat (~300 s) and eliminating the hours-long per-scene training required by StyleGaussian.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against StyleRF (NeRF-based, zero-shot), A³GS dominates on every metric: short-range LPIPS drops from 0.092 to 0.033, long-range RMSE from 0.186 to 0.057, and user-study Style Consistency jumps from 2.1 to 4.1. Against StyleSplat (optimization-based 3DGS), A³GS wins on short-range RMSE (0.029 vs 0.045), long-range RMSE (0.057 vs 0.062), Style Consistency (4.1 vs 3.9), and Visual Naturalness (4.5 vs 4.3), while StyleSplat retains a slight edge on long-range LPIPS (0.052 vs 0.061); both tie on short-range LPIPS (0.033) and Content Preservation (4.5). Against StyleGaussian (feed-forward 3DGS), A³GS wins on short-range RMSE (0.029 vs 0.033), long-range RMSE (0.057 vs 0.060), and all three user-study metrics (4.1/4.5/4.5 vs 3.5/3.3/3.6), but trails on long-range LPIPS (0.061 vs 0.057). The most striking practical advantage is speed: A³GS runs in ~10 s with no per-scene training, whereas StyleSplat needs ~300 s of optimization and StyleGaussian requires hours of training per scene before achieving real-time inference, and StyleGaussian cannot handle scenes beyond ~0.3 M Gaussians due to memory limits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the 3D-GCN deformable kernel architecture (Lin et al., CVPR 2020) to construct a GCN-based autoencoder that aggregates spatially structured features from 3D Gaussian primitives, and on AdaIN (Huang & Belongie, ICCV 2017) for style injection in latent space. It departs from the dominant paradigm of per-scene, per-style gradient-based optimization (StylizedGS, G-Style, StyleSplat, ReGS) by adopting a fully feed-forward, zero-shot design, and from the closest feed-forward competitor StyleGaussian by operating on a GCN latent space rather than per-Gaussian features, enabling batch processing of arbitrarily large scenes. The two-stage training strategy—first learning a 3DGS autoencoder, then training the AdaIN stylizer on generated data from Objaverse and WikiArt—distinguishes it from prior work that required per-scene fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10-second zero-shot inference time and lack of theoretical limit on primitive count make 3DGS stylization practical for large-scale scenes (e.g., city-scale or VR environments) where optimization-based methods hit memory walls, opening the door to real-time artistic control in metaverse and gaming pipelines. However, the method only modifies color while fixing geometry and opacity, limiting its expressiveness for styles that require structural changes; it also struggles with rare art styles outside the training distribution and can lose fine-grained artistic nuances during latent-space transformation. Open questions include whether the GCN autoencoder can be extended to jointly stylize geometry, whether the approach generalizes to dynamic or animated 3DGS scenes, and whether the VGG-based style features can be replaced by more expressive encoders to capture subtler artistic details.
