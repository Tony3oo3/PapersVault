## Problem

Reconstructing continuous 4D deformation from arbitrary-length sequences of independently sampled, noisy, and partial 3D point clouds remains challenging: 4D implicit methods oversmooth geometric details and lack explicit point tracking, while flow-based methods produce topological artifacts when the canonical shape and velocity field are jointly optimized in an ill-posed manner. The core difficulty is that the implicit canonical shape converges faster than the flow, causing a 'chimera-like' shape that incorrectly merges features across temporally distant frames.

## Input / Output

The method takes an arbitrary-length sequence (no fixed frame count) of uncorrelated 3D point clouds, each containing sampled points, surface normals, and a timestamp in [0,1], with no prior cross-frame correspondences required. It produces a continuous 4D mesh at any target time t∈[0,1] by extracting a canonical mesh via marching cubes and deforming its vertices through the learned diffeomorphic flow, enabling reliable tracking of individual surface points. Unlike dataset-learning baselines constrained to 17 frames, CanFields handles sequences of 10–25 frames and supports continuous temporal interpolation rather than discrete keyframe prediction.

## Method

CanFields decomposes the 4D implicit function into a SIREN-encoded canonical shape g_c and a diffeomorphic flow φ parameterized by a smooth velocity field v, integrated via a differentiable Dormand-Prince ODE solver. The key novelty is a Dynamic Consolidator Module Υ that, before ODE integration, applies a learnable spatial deviation δ to homogenize input points and assigns per-point confidence scores p to down-weight outliers; this acts as a curriculum-learning mechanism where regularization weights on δ and p are gradually increased to transition from smooth fitting to faithful reconstruction. The velocity field uses Random Fourier Features processed through a two-layer low-pass MLP filter to suppress jitters, and is regularized with Killing energy (pointwise isometry), Dirichlet energy (spatial smoothness), consistent-speed loss (temporal smoothness), and an articulated rigidity term computed via a motion-segmentation network operating in the consolidated canonical space. This joint fitting with adaptive confidence weighting resolves the premature-convergence mismatch between shape and flow that plagues prior methods, enabling stable SIREN-based detail preservation without the artifacts seen when SIREN is naively inserted into existing flow pipelines.

## Results

On DeformingThings4D, CanFields achieves 82.84% mean IoU (74.12% min), 4.606×10⁻⁴ mean CD, and 78.21% mean NC, outperforming all baselines across every metric. On CAPE (real clothed humans), it reaches 84.17% mean IoU (80.94% min), 6.554×10⁻⁴ mean CD, and 84.98% mean NC. The method also demonstrates the fastest interpolation speed (1.30 s mesh extraction + 15.6 s deformation) among flow-based methods, with only 285K velocity parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CanFields outperforms all four quantitative baselines on every metric (IoU, CD, NC) across both datasets. Against the closest flow-based competitor OFlow [56], it gains +4.37% mean IoU on DeformingThings4D (82.84 vs. 78.47) and +7.02% on CAPE (84.17 vs. 77.15), while reducing mean CD by 42% and 77% respectively. The 4D implicit method DSR [73] achieves competitive CD on DeformingThings4D (6.762 vs. 4.606) but lags in IoU by 7.58 points and oversmooths geometric details. NDF [74] and NVFi [42] trail by 8+ points in mean IoU on DeformingThings4D and suffer from topological artifacts and poor temporal alignment. For raw scans (DFAUST), the dataset-learned Motion2VecSet [12] generalizes poorly to unseen sequences and can only produce a fixed set of discrete frames, whereas CanFields handles arbitrary-length inputs with continuous interpolation and fewer artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the flow-based 4D reconstruction paradigm established by OFlow [56], NDF [74], and NVFi [42], which decompose dynamic shapes into a canonical implicit surface plus a diffeomorphic flow parameterized by a velocity field integrated via Neural ODEs [14]. The key departure is the dynamic consolidator module, a curriculum-learning-inspired mechanism that adaptively reweights and displaces input points to resolve the ill-posed joint fitting of canonical geometry and motion — a problem prior methods addressed only through manual learning-rate tuning or canonical-time emphasis. The paper also departs from prior work by demonstrating that SIREN, while more expressive than DeepSDF, paradoxically destabilizes existing flow-based pipelines, and by proposing a minimal Random Fourier Feature velocity architecture that accelerates ODE integration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dynamic consolidator offers a general-purpose mechanism for balancing competing optimization objectives in joint geometry-and-motion fitting, with potential applicability beyond 4D reconstruction to any setting where a fast-fitting component (e.g., shape) races ahead of a slow-fitting one (e.g., flow). The method's unsupervised, per-sequence fitting with no fixed frame count makes it practical for real-world motion capture and medical imaging where sequences are variable-length and noisy. Key limitations remain: the diffeomorphic flow assumption precludes topological changes (e.g., hole formation), and excessively rapid inter-frame deformations still challenge the interpolation. Open questions include whether the consolidator's curriculum scheduling generalizes to higher-dimensional or multi-object settings, and whether texture or video inputs can be integrated without destabilizing the flow fitting.
