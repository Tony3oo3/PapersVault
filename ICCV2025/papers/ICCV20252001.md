## Problem

Open-vocabulary HOI detection relies on CLIP-based VLMs, but CLIP's holistic image-level pre-training misaligns with the fine-grained region-level reasoning required to localize specific interaction regions. Additionally, existing methods fail to capture subtle intra- and inter-modal relationships, causing confusion between visually similar but semantically distinct actions (e.g., 'hurling' vs. 'pitching').
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252001/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces a set of HOI predictions, each consisting of a human bounding box, an object bounding box, and an interaction class label (action–object pair). The architecture is DETR-style with N learnable HOI queries decoded via a transformer, and the final detection score is the product of the interaction classification score and the bounding box confidence raised to a suppression exponent γ. The visual encoder is CLIP ViT-B/16, and interaction recognition is performed by computing cosine similarity between decoded features and CLIP text embeddings of HOI triplets.

## Method

INP-CC introduces two key mechanisms on top of a DETR-style CLIP-based HOI detector. First, an interaction-aware prompt generator constructs a compact set of prompts (a shared common prompt plus M interaction-specific prompts parameterized as rank-one low-rank matrices via Hadamard product), and a selection mechanism dynamically picks the top-k most relevant prompts per image based on cosine similarity between the image fingerprint and learnable prompt keys, enabling selective sharing among semantically related interactions. Second, a concept calibration module uses GPT-3.5 to generate fine-grained visual descriptions for each HOI category, embeds them with a T5-based Instructor Embedding model, and clusters the resulting embeddings into J groups to model intra-modal relationships; during training, hard negative categories are sampled from clusters that overlap with the current batch's ground-truth categories, forcing the model to distinguish visually similar but semantically distinct actions. The model is trained end-to-end with box regression, IoU, and classification losses on SWIG-HOI and HICO-DET.

## Results

On SWIG-HOI, INP-CC achieves 16.74 mAP on the full split, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art CMD-SE (15.26) by a relative gain of 9.70%. On HICO-DET under the simulated zero-shot setting, INP-CC reaches 23.12 mAP on the full split, surpassing CMD-SE (22.35) by 0.77%. The method also shows consistent gains on fine-grained interaction subsets, e.g., +1.48% on 'admiring', +1.35% on 'browsing', and +1.59% on 'reading' over CMD-SE on SWIG-HOI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

INP-CC outperforms all compared baselines on both datasets. Among open-vocabulary methods (the most relevant comparison group), it beats CMD-SE [35] by 1.48 mAP on SWIG-HOI full split (16.74 vs. 15.26) and by 0.77 mAP on HICO-DET full split (23.12 vs. 22.35). It also surpasses THID [74] by 3.48 mAP on SWIG-HOI full split (16.74 vs. 13.26) and by 0.74 mAP on HICO-DET full split (23.12 vs. 22.38). On SWIG-HOI, it improves over MP-HOI [87] by 4.13 mAP and over QPIC [61] by 5.62 mAP on the full split. The zero-shot methods on HICO-DET (GEN-VLKT [47], HOICLIP [55], CLIP4HOI [51], HOIGen [18]) report higher absolute mAP (30–34) but rely on COCO-pretrained detectors, which the authors note is not a fair comparison since HICO-DET shares object labels with COCO; INP-CC avoids this dependency entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLM-based open-vocabulary HOI detection line initiated by THID and CMD-SE, which transfer CLIP knowledge to recognize novel interaction triplets. It departs from these methods by explicitly addressing two identified gaps: the distribution mismatch between CLIP's image-level pre-training and region-level interaction detection, and the weak fine-grained text-visual alignment for visually similar actions. The interaction-aware prompt generator with low-rank parameterization and selective sharing among semantically related interactions is a novel architectural contribution not present in prior HOI detectors, while the concept calibration pipeline (GPT-3.5 descriptions → Instructor Embedding clustering → hard negative sampling) repurposes discriminative language models in a way that prior CLIP-centric approaches did not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The selective prompt-sharing mechanism offers a practical solution to the open-vocabulary scalability problem—assigning unique prompts to thousands of interaction classes is infeasible, and this paper shows that a compact set of shared prompts with input-adaptive selection can effectively bridge the image-to-region perception gap. The finding that a T5-based discriminative embedding (Instructor Embedding) outperforms CLIP's text encoder for clustering HOI concepts challenges the assumption that CLIP's joint space is sufficient for fine-grained semantic structuring. Limitations include reliance on GPT-3.5 for description generation (introducing a quality bottleneck and potential bias), evaluation on only two datasets, and still-modest absolute mAP scores (~16.7 on SWIG-HOI), leaving open whether the approach scales to even larger interaction taxonomies or benefits from stronger base encoders.
