## Problem

Existing OT-based unsupervised action segmentation methods, particularly ASOT, lack segment-level supervision, limiting the feedback between frame representations and action clusters. This absence of explicit frame-to-segment interaction hinders fine-grained boundary detection, especially for short-duration actions, and prevents the model from ensuring that learned clusters align with true segment boundaries at the video level.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250669/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes precomputed frame-level embeddings X ∈ R^{N×D} (256 frames per video sampled at uniform intervals) extracted from video using dataset-specific features (e.g., Fisher vectors from IDT, HOF+VGG16). It produces three levels of output: initial frame-to-action soft assignments T, segment-level embeddings S with their own pseudo-labels T_S, and refined frame embeddings F_R with refined pseudo-labels T_R. The number of action clusters K is set to the ground-truth action count per dataset, and all OT solutions serve as soft pseudo-labels for self-supervised training.

## Method

CLOT introduces a three-level cyclic architecture that closes the loop between frame and segment representations. An MLP encoder with a learnable feature-dispatching mechanism (using a sigmoid-gated similarity function to dynamically update frame embeddings based on cluster assignments) solves the first unbalanced OT problem (KOT + GWOT) to produce frame embeddings F and pseudo-labels T. A parallel query-based decoder (adapted from action anticipation) then decodes F into segment embeddings S and solves a second OT problem for segment-to-cluster assignments. A cross-attention module (F_R = F + softmax(FS^T/(τ√d))S) integrates segment-level structure back into frame embeddings, and a third projection-based OT problem using Sliced Wasserstein Distance in the cost matrix yields refined pseudo-labels T_R. Training minimizes the sum of three cross-entropy losses between predicted assignment probabilities and their corresponding OT-derived pseudo-labels, all learned end-to-end in an unsupervised manner.

## Results

At the activity level (Table 1), CLOT achieves the best MoF on Breakfast (60.1), 50Salads-Mid (50.6), and 50Salads-Eval (59.4), and the best F1 on all five evaluation settings, with the largest gains on 50Salads-Eval (F1 63.2 vs. 53.6 for ASOT) and 50Salads-Mid (F1 46.6 vs. 37.4). On average, CLOT improves over the previous best method by +2.54% MoF, +5.08% F1, and +3.2% mIoU. At the video level (Table 2), CLOT outperforms ASOT on Breakfast (MoF 66.3 vs. 63.3), 50Salads-Mid (MoF 69.4 vs. 64.3), 50Salads-Eval (F1 69.7 vs. 58.9), and Desktop Assembly (F1 75.2 vs. 68.0), while trailing ASOT on YouTube Instructions (MoF 69.3 vs. 71.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLOT's primary competitor is ASOT [50], the previous state-of-the-art OT-based method. CLOT surpasses ASOT on MoF for Breakfast (+4.0), 50Salads-Mid (+4.4), and 50Salads-Eval (+0.1), and on F1 for all five settings, with the largest F1 gains on 50Salads-Eval (+9.6) and 50Salads-Mid (+9.2). CLOT slightly trails ASOT on mIoU for Breakfast (18.5 vs. 18.6) and YouTube Instructions (23.4 vs. 24.7), which the authors attribute to rare actions disproportionately affecting mIoU. Against HVQ [41], CLOT wins on both MoF and F1 for Breakfast and YouTube Instructions. At the video level, CLOT outperforms ASOT on four of five settings but loses on YouTube Instructions (MoF 69.3 vs. 71.2, F1 60.8 vs. 63.3). CLOT also beats all earlier OT-based methods (TOT, TOT+, UFSA) and classical approaches (CTE, VTE, UDE, ASAL) across every reported metric, with the most substantial margins over UFSA on 50Salads-Eval (F1 63.2 vs. 50.3) and DA (F1 72.6 vs. 63.0).

## Relation to Prior Work

CLOT is a direct architectural extension of ASOT (Xu & Gould, CVPR 2024), which introduced unbalanced Gromov-Wasserstein optimal transport for unsupervised action segmentation without action-order assumptions. The key departure is the introduction of a three-level cyclic pipeline that adds segment-level supervision: a parallel decoder (adapted from action anticipation work by Gong et al. and Nawhal et al.) produces segment embeddings, and a cross-attention refinement stage closes the loop between frame and segment representations. Additionally, CLOT replaces part of ASOT's cosine-based cost matrix with a Sliced Wasserstein Distance term, drawing on the broader SW literature in structured prediction. The paper positions itself against HVQ (Spurio et al., AAAI 2024), which improved short-action detection via hierarchical vector quantization but sacrificed the representation-clustering feedback that OT formulations provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cyclic frame-segment-frame architecture addresses a genuine gap in OT-based segmentation: the absence of segment-level supervision that could align learned clusters with true temporal boundaries. The consistent gains across four diverse datasets (cooking, assembly, instructional video) suggest the approach generalizes beyond a single domain. However, the paper acknowledges that action mislabeling remains the primary open challenge, and the method still relies on precomputed features and a known number of action clusters, limiting its applicability to fully open-world settings. The moderate mIoU regressions on Breakfast and YTI relative to ASOT hint that the cyclic refinement may overfit to dominant actions at the expense of rare ones, an issue that future work on pseudo-label confidence weighting could address.
