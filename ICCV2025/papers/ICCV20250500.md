## Problem

Reinforcement fine-tuning with verifiable rewards (as in DeepSeek-R1) has proven effective for language reasoning tasks like math and code, but its extension to visual perception tasks remains unexplored. Supervised fine-tuning of LVLMs requires large curated datasets and often degrades performance with very few samples, whereas RFT could enable data-efficient adaptation if suitable verifiable reward functions existed for visual tasks such as detection, classification, and grounding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250500/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image paired with a text prompt (e.g., a detection instruction specifying a category, or a classification question asking to identify a species). The output is a structured sequence: a free-form reasoning trace enclosed in thinking tags, followed by a final answer in a task-specific format — for detection, a list of bounding boxes with integer coordinates in [0, 1000] and confidence scores; for classification, a single species/category name. A format reward enforces strict adherence to this tag structure during training.

## Method

Visual-RFT applies GRPO-based policy optimization to LVLMs (Qwen2-VL-2B/7B) using task-specific, rule-based verifiable rewards that eliminate the need for a learned reward model. For object detection, the reward combines three components: the mean IoU between predicted and ground-truth boxes (with a threshold τ for valid matches), a confidence reward that rewards high confidence on matched boxes and penalizes it on unmatched ones, and a format reward. For classification, the reward is a binary accuracy signal plus the format reward. During training, the policy generates G responses per input, rewards are normalized within the group to compute relative advantages, and the policy is updated with a KL-divergence penalty against a reference model. This design enables effective fine-tuning from as few as 1–10 samples per category, substantially outperforming SFT in both accuracy and cross-domain generalization.

## Results

On fine-grained image classification, Visual-RFT with Qwen2-VL-2B achieves 80.3% average accuracy in the 1-shot setting, a +24.3% improvement over the 56.0% baseline, while SFT actually drops to 51.7% (−4.3%). In few-shot COCO detection (8 categories), Visual-RFT reaches 40.6 mAP at 4-shot versus 19.6 for the baseline (+21.0), and on LVIS rare categories it improves mAP from 4.0 to 19.4 (+15.4) in a 10-shot setting. For reasoning grounding on LISA, Visual-RFT achieves 37.6 mIoU (test) with the 2B model, surpassing specialized systems like GroundedSAM (26.2) and X-Decoder (28.5). In open-vocabulary COCO detection, the 2B model's nAP jumps from 9.6 to 27.9 (+18.3) on 17 novel categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against prompt-learning classification baselines (CoOp 62.7, MaPLe 67.7, CoCoOp 68.9, PromptSRC 72.3 average), Visual-RFT with only 1-shot data (80.3) surpasses all of them, and at 8-shot (85.1) it leads by 12.8 points over the strongest prompt method. Compared to SFT on the same Qwen2-VL-2B backbone, Visual-RFT wins decisively across every task: +24.3 avg accuracy in 1-shot classification (80.3 vs 51.7), +21.0 mAP in 4-shot COCO detection (40.6 vs 19.6), +15.4 mAP on LVIS rare classes (19.4 vs 10.0), and +10.7 mIoU on LISA grounding (37.6 vs 28.3). On LISA reasoning grounding, Visual-RFT (37.6 mIoU) outperforms specialized detection/segmentation systems including OV-Seg (28.4), X-Decoder (28.5), and GroundedSAM (26.2), and even the 7B Visual-RFT model (43.9 mIoU) exceeds the 7B SFT model (39.1). In open-vocabulary COCO detection, the 7B Visual-RFT model (32.3 nAP) outperforms the specialized few-shot detector DE-ViT under the same 10-shot protocol, while the 2B model's 27.9 nAP also surpasses CLIP-Self and Zareian et al. baselines shown in Figure 6. The consistent pattern is that SFT degrades or barely improves with minimal data, whereas Visual-RFT's verifiable-reward RL yields large, monotonic gains as shot count increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) paradigm introduced by DeepSeek-R1 from language-only domains (math, code) to visual perception tasks in Large Vision-Language Models. It adopts GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) as the policy optimization backbone and contributes task-specific verifiable reward functions—most notably an IoU-and-confidence-based reward for detection and an accuracy-based reward for classification—that replace the need for a learned reward model. The key conceptual departure from prior SFT-based LVLM fine-tuning is shifting from imitation of ground-truth answers to reward-driven exploration, where the model generates multiple reasoning trajectories and is optimized based on verifiable correctness signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that RL fine-tuning with rule-based rewards can substantially outperform SFT in data-scarce visual settings (1–10 shot), which is practically significant for practitioners who cannot curate large annotated datasets. The consistent gap across four distinct visual tasks (classification, detection, grounding, open-vocabulary detection) and two model scales (2B, 7B) strengthens the case that this is a generalizable training paradigm rather than a task-specific trick. Open questions include whether the approach extends to tasks lacking clean verifiable rewards (e.g., open-ended VQA, video understanding), how it scales to larger LVLMs, and whether the reasoning tokens genuinely improve perception or merely serve as a structured output scaffold.
