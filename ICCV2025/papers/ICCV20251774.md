## Problem

Class-Incremental Semantic Segmentation (CISS) requires models to learn new classes while retaining old ones, but existing visual-only methods suffer from catastrophic semantic entanglement: prototype-feature entanglement (semantic misalignment as new prototypes are added without access to old data) and background-increment entanglement (background semantics shift as foreground classes expand). These issues arise because purely visual supervision lacks the cues needed to disentangle overlapping class representations, leading to cumulative errors across incremental steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251774/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images resized to 512×512 and class text descriptions (with learnable CoOp-style prompts of length 8) as input, producing pixel-level segmentation masks for all classes seen so far. The background is not a single prototype but a manifold of n=4 mutually orthogonal learnable prompts; the final background mask is computed by taking the maximum activation across all background prototypes at each pixel. During incremental steps, only new-class labels are provided in supervision, while old-class pseudo-labels are generated by the previous model.

## Method

The LBD framework builds on CLIP ViT-B/16 (with ClearCLIP modifications removing residual connections and FFN in the final ViT layer) plus a 2-layer transformer decoder that fuses class text embeddings with visual patch embeddings. Language-guided prototypical disentanglement constrains new class prototypes using hand-crafted text templates: a stability loss (Huber loss on pairwise Euclidean distances and angles) preserves macroscopic topology, a top-k orthogonality loss enforces local plasticity only on the most similar pairs, and a temperature-scaled KL divergence aligns patch-level score maps with template score maps. Manifold mutual background disentanglement represents the background with multiple learnable prompts, selectively initializes new-class prompts from the most similar background embedding, and applies mask-pooling-based contrastive learning to push new-class features away from background features while pulling them toward the reference background (old classes minus the new class). Together, these mechanisms bootstrap the model's ability to decouple entangled features using CLIP's prior class semantics rather than relying solely on visual supervision.

## Results

On ADE20k (overlapped setting), the method achieves 47.1% All MIoU in the 100-50 (2-step) setting and 41.8% in the 100-5 (11-step) setting, outperforming the previous SOTA (MBS) by 1.8 and 1.2 points respectively. On Pascal VOC, the method reaches 78.1% All MIoU in the 15-1 (6-step) overlapped setting, surpassing MBS by 0.5 points overall and by 1.7 points on new classes (66.6% vs. 64.9%). The advantage grows with the number of incremental steps, with the largest gap appearing in the 11-step ADE20k scenario where the harmonic mean reaches 38.0 vs. 36.3 for MBS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 12 baselines across both datasets and all incremental step configurations. The closest competitor is MBS [32] (ECCV 2024), the previous SOTA: on ADE20k 100-50, Ours achieves 47.1% All MIoU vs. MBS's 45.3% (+1.8), and on Pascal VOC 15-1 (6-step) overlapped, Ours reaches 78.1% vs. 77.6% (+0.5) with a larger 1.7-point gain on new classes. Against INC [41] (CVPR 2023), the gap is wider in multi-step settings: on ADE20k 100-5 (11 steps), Ours scores 41.8% All vs. INC's 41.7%, but the harmonic mean is 38.0 vs. 37.5, indicating better balance between old and new class retention. On Pascal VOC 15-1 disjoint, Ours achieves 76.3% All MIoU, beating MBS (74.9%) by 1.4 points and INC (76.3%) by matching, while the new-class MIoU is 62.3% vs. 60.9% for MBS. Earlier baselines such as SDR [31], PLOP [11], RCIL [58], and SPPA [23] trail by 3–10+ points in All MIoU on ADE20k, with the largest gaps in the 11-step setting (e.g., SDR: 21.7% vs. Ours 44.1%). The paper also shows that simply swapping the backbone to CLIP without the proposed disentanglement losses (Ours(Fix)) degrades performance to 72.1% All on Pascal VOC 15-1, confirming that the gains come from the language-guided and manifold background disentanglement mechanisms rather than the CLIP backbone alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-to-segmentation pipeline established by DenseCLIP and ClearCLIP into the class-incremental setting, incorporating CoOp-style learnable text prompts for class embeddings. It departs fundamentally from prior CISS methods (MiB, SDR, PLOP, MBS, INC) that rely solely on visual knowledge distillation from previous models, instead leveraging CLIP's pre-trained text-prototype topology as a static structural prior to guide disentanglement. The manifold background representation with multiple orthogonal learnable prototypes and selective weight transfer based on template similarity is a novel departure from the single-prototype background modeling used in all prior CISS work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that CLIP's multimodal topological knowledge, when properly constrained through language-guided distillation, can mitigate the cumulative background shift and prototype confusion that plague multi-step CISS, particularly in long-horizon scenarios (100-5, 15-1) where purely visual distillation degrades. A notable open question is whether the hand-crafted text templates generalize to truly open-vocabulary or unseen-class settings, and whether the approach extends beyond the two evaluated datasets. The method's dependence on CLIP's text encoder quality and the need for careful learning-rate scheduling across modules may limit straightforward adoption in other incremental tasks.
