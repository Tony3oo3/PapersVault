## Problem

Training-free video understanding with image-trained MLLMs faces a fundamental tension: uniform frame sampling to a fixed length inevitably drops critical events, while aggressive token pooling (average/max) erases spatial detail needed for nuanced action recognition. Existing methods like IG-VLM and SlowFast-LLaVA use rigid sampling strategies and simple pooling that cannot adapt to varying temporal complexity across videos, resulting in lost context or excessive token counts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251753/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video uniformly sampled to N=100 frames and a text question as input. Each frame is encoded by a visual encoder into L tokens of dimension D (plus a [CLS] token), and the full N×L×D token sequence is compressed to K×(L−Z/K−1)×D, where K is the number of discovered event clusters and Z is a fixed budget (3680 for 7B models, 7200 for 34B models). The output is either a multiple-choice selection (structured VQA) or a free-form generated answer (open-ended VQA), produced by the underlying MLLM without any video-specific fine-tuning.

## Method

DYTO is a two-stage, training-free token compression pipeline. First, it performs coarse-grained hierarchical clustering: [CLS] tokens from all 100 frames are used to compute temporally-weighted pairwise distances, a 1-NN graph is built and symmetrized, connected components form initial clusters that are recursively merged by feature-average similarity, and the second-largest segmentation is selected to yield K event clusters from which one representative frame is uniformly sampled per cluster. Second, it applies fine-grained dynamic bipartite merging (inspired by ToMe) to each selected frame: tokens are iteratively split into sets P and Q, multi-head cosine similarities are computed between all P–Q pairs, and the top-r_i most similar pairs are merged by pooling, where r_i = R_0 − Z/K is dynamically determined by the number of clusters K. This means videos with more distinct events produce more keyframes but fewer tokens per frame, while videos with fewer events produce fewer keyframes but retain more tokens each, adaptively allocating the fixed token budget Z to match the video's temporal complexity.

## Results

On structured VQA benchmarks, DYTO with InternVL2-8B achieves 81.4% on NExTQA, 67.6% on EgoSchema, 83.0% on IntentQA, 59.3% on VideoMME, and 66.2% on MVBench, setting new state-of-the-art among training-free methods and surpassing most video-fine-tuned 7B models. With LLaVA-NeXT-34B, DYTO reaches 72.9% NExTQA, 56.8% EgoSchema, 67.5% IntentQA, 53.4% VideoMME, and 52.9% MVBench. On open-ended VQA, DYTO (7B, LLaVA-NeXT) scores 77.6% MSVD-QA, 64.1% MSRVTT-QA, 78.0% TGIF-QA, 54.3% ANet-QA, and 3.16 average VCG, outperforming all fine-tuned baselines on most metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among training-free baselines, DYTO consistently outperforms both IG-VLM and SlowFast-LLaVA across every model size and benchmark: e.g., with InternVL2-8B, DYTO gains +1.5% over IG-VLM and +4.2% over SlowFast on NExTQA, and +11.3% over SlowFast on VideoMME. Against fine-tuned 7B models, DYTO (LLaVA-NeXT-7B) beats Video-LLaVA, LLaMA-VID, VideoChat2, and InternVideo2 on most metrics, but trails VideoLLaMA2-7B (51.7% vs. 48.6% EgoSchema) and LLaVA-OneVision-7B (79.4% vs. 65.7% NExTQA). However, when paired with the stronger InternVL2-8B backbone, DYTO surpasses LLaVA-OneVision-7B on all five benchmarks (81.4 vs. 79.4 NExTQA, 67.6 vs. 60.1 EgoSchema, 59.3 vs. 58.2 VideoMME, 66.2 vs. 56.7 MVBench). On open-ended VQA, DYTO-7B outperforms Video-ChatGPT, MovieChat, Vista-LLaMA, and PLLaVA on MSVD-QA, MSRVTT-QA, and TGIF-QA, though it slightly trails VideoGPT+ on VCG average (3.16 vs. 3.28) and PLLaVA on ANet-QA (54.3 vs. 56.3). At 34B scale, DYTO still lags the larger fine-tuned LLaVA-NeXT-Video-32B on NExTQA (72.9 vs. 77.3) and VideoMME (53.4 vs. 60.2), indicating that fine-tuning with abundant video data retains an edge at the highest model scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

DYTO repurposes two established components—bipartite token merging from ToMe (Bolya et al., ICLR 2023) and 1-NN graph-based hierarchical clustering (Sarfraz et al., CVPR 2019)—and combines them into a unified, training-free pipeline for video token compression. It departs from prior training-free video LLMs (IG-VLM, SlowFast-LLaVA, FreeVA) that rely on uniform frame sampling and fixed pooling strategies, instead making both frame selection and per-frame token budget adaptive to the video's content. The method is explicitly positioned as a plug-in module orthogonal to the base MLLM, rather than a new architecture requiring co-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single training-free preprocessing module can be attached to any image-trained MLLM (LLaVA-NeXT, InternVL2, Qwen-VL) to achieve competitive or superior video VQA performance without video-specific fine-tuning, reducing data and compute costs. Open questions include whether the approach extends to streaming/real-time video understanding (the authors note this as future work), how it behaves on very long videos beyond 100 frames, and whether the clustering step introduces latency that limits deployment. A limitation is that the method still requires encoding all N=100 frames before compression, so the visual encoder cost is not reduced—only the LLM-side token count is.
