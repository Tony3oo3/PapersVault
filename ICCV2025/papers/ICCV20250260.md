## Problem

Vision-Language Models are increasingly deployed in safety-critical applications like autonomous driving and surveillance, yet their robustness to real-world illumination variations remains largely unexplored. Existing illumination attacks target only CNN-based models, employ simplistic single-source or binary-mask lighting modifications, and fail to capture the diverse spectrum of natural lighting conditions, leaving a critical gap in systematic VLM illumination robustness evaluation.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a semantic label set (COCO-80 categories) as input, and produces an illumination-aware adversarial image along with the optimized illumination configuration Λ* consisting of N=3 point light sources, each parameterized by spatial position (x,y), intensity I ∈ [0.5, 1.0], and radius r ∈ [10, 50]. The relighting is performed via IC-Light's VAE-diffusion pipeline, and the adversarial image is evaluated against the target VLM's image and text encoders. Optimization runs for 200 iterations with a population of 20 candidates per step on 8× RTX 4090 GPUs.

## Method

ITA decomposes global illumination into N parameterized point light sources modeled as Gaussian functions F_i(z) = I_i·exp(−‖z−(x_i,y_i)‖²/2r_i²), whose sum defines the total scene illumination, enabling fine-grained control over diverse lighting conditions that binary-mask or single-spot methods cannot capture. The relighting is executed through IC-Light's VAE+diffusion architecture, which preserves physical lighting priors by operating in latent space rather than manipulating pixels directly. Because IC-Light is non-differentiable, the illumination configuration is optimized via CMA-ES (a gradient-free evolutionary strategy) with learning-rate adaptation and early stopping. The total loss combines an adversarial objective (negative log-likelihood of the ground-truth class in CLIP's cosine-similarity embedding space), an LPIPS perceptual constraint to prevent over/under-exposure, and a pairwise distance regularization to prevent light sources from collapsing to a single point.

## Results

On zero-shot classification (COCO), ITA(best) reduces top-1 accuracy by 51% on OpenCLIP ViT-B/16 (97%→46%), 34% on Meta-CLIP ViT-L/14 (98%→64%), 14% on EVA-CLIP ViT-G/14 (98%→84%), and 42% on OpenAI CLIP ViT-L/14 (93%→51%). On image captioning, ITA(best) degrades GPT-4 consistency scores by up to 16.27% (OpenFlamingo: 70.20→53.93), and on VQA, by up to 26% (OpenFlamingo: 45.00→19.00). GPT-4o naturalness scores for ITA(best) remain in the 2.11–2.17 range (out of 4), comparable to Shadow Attack and slightly below Natural Light Attack.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ITA(best) substantially outperforms both baselines in accuracy degradation across all four CLIP variants. On OpenCLIP ViT-B/16, ITA drops accuracy by 51 percentage points versus only 13 for Shadow Attack and 3 for Natural Light Attack; on OpenAI CLIP ViT-L/14 the gap is 42 vs. 14 vs. 0. Even on the largest model (EVA-CLIP ViT-G/14), ITA achieves a 14-point drop while Shadow Attack manages only 3 and Natural Light Attack only 1. In terms of GPT-4o naturalness, ITA(best) scores (2.11–2.17) are on par with Shadow Attack (1.78–2.11) and slightly below Natural Light Attack (2.07–2.32), indicating that the larger accuracy drop comes at a modest naturalness cost. On the secondary tasks (captioning and VQA), ITA(best) again yields the largest degradation—e.g., 16.27% consistency drop on OpenFlamingo captioning and 26% correctness drop on OpenFlamingo VQA—compared to 2.40% and 1.00% respectively for Shadow Attack, confirming consistent superiority over both baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on IC-Light, a physics-based relighting model combining a VAE with a diffusion process, repurposing it as the rendering engine for adversarial illumination generation. It departs from prior illumination attacks (laser beam, shadow, natural light) in two key ways: (1) replacing localized, single-source or binary-mask lighting disturbances with a global multi-point-light-source model parameterized by Gaussian spatial distributions, and (2) shifting the target from CNN-based classifiers on narrow tasks (traffic signs, faces) to modern VLMs evaluated on classification, captioning, and VQA. Because IC-Light's diffusion pipeline is non-differentiable, the authors introduce CMA-ES as a gradient-free optimizer over the latent illumination parameters, a departure from the gradient-based optimization used in most prior adversarial illumination work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic evidence that state-of-the-art VLMs (LLaVA, BLIP-2, InstructBLIP, OpenFlamingo) are substantially vulnerable to physically plausible illumination shifts, with accuracy drops up to 51% on CLIP variants and 26% on VQA for smaller models. This opens a concrete robustness gap for safety-critical deployments (autonomous driving, surveillance) where lighting conditions vary naturally. Key limitations include evaluation on only 300 COCO images across 30 categories, transfer attacks for captioning/VQA rather than direct optimization, and naturalness scores that, while competitive, remain noticeably below clean images (2.15 vs. 3.65 GPT-4o). Open questions include whether the vulnerability persists on larger, more diverse datasets, whether illumination-aware training can close the gap, and whether the multi-light-source formulation generalizes to more complex scene lighting (area lights, HDR).
