## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary camouflaged object segmentation methods, particularly the state-of-the-art OVCoser, suffer from 'semantic confusion' where coarse text prompts and inadequate text-visual alignment cause incomplete segmentation (missing object parts that blend with background) and class shift (parts of the same entity being assigned to visually similar but incorrect categories). The root causes are the semantic vagueness of text prompts for camouflaged classes and the failure to properly align the text and visual feature spaces when objects and backgrounds share highly similar visual characteristics.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 384×384) and a text class description as input, producing a pixel-level binary segmentation mask and a predicted class label. The class label is obtained by applying CLIP's zero-shot classification (Eq. 3) to the generated mask, rather than being directly regressed. Training is performed on 14 base classes while testing generalizes to 61 novel classes, with all CLIP parameters kept frozen and only the proposed modules (CAP, CAFS, TQD) being optimized.

## Method

SuCLIP comprises three components built on a frozen CLIP-ConvNeXt-L backbone. The Context-Aware Prompt (CAP) applies density peak clustering to CLIP visual encoder tokens to extract K fine-grained visual tokens, then uses a gated mechanism to blend these with learnable text prompt vectors, injecting local visual attributes into the text representation. The Class-Aware Feature Selection (CAFS) module performs multi-head cross-attention between aggregated visual features and text embeddings to dynamically adjust both, and a semantic consistency loss aligns the inter-class cosine similarity structures in the text and visual spaces to prevent class shift. The Text Query Decoder (TQD) consists of four cascaded blocks, each combining MHSA on text queries, MHCA to retrieve aligned visual features, and a depth-wise separable FFN with upsampling, with outputs aggregated element-wise to produce the final mask supervised by a segmentation loss.

## Results

On the OVCamo dataset (train on OVCamo setting), SuCLIP achieves cSm 0.667, cFβω 0.594, cMAE 0.242, cFβ 0.633, cEm 0.722, and cIoU 0.540. This represents improvements of 8.8%, 10.4%, 9.4%, 11.3%, 10.6%, and 9.7% respectively over the prior SOTA method OVCoser (e Sm 0.579, E Fβω 0.490, E MAE 0.336, E Fβ 0.520, E m 0.616, E IoU 0.443). All six metrics are improved simultaneously, with the largest relative gain on cFβ (11.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SuCLIP outperforms all seven baselines on every metric in the train-on-OVCAMO setting. The closest competitor is OVCoser, the dedicated OVCOS method, which SuCLIP beats by 8.8% on E Sm, 10.4% on E Fβω, 9.4% on E MAE, 11.3% on E Fβ, 10.6% on E m, and 9.7% on E IoU. Among general OVSS methods, SAN is the strongest (E Sm 0.275, E IoU 0.189), yet SuCLIP still leads SAN by 142% on E Sm and 186% on E IoU. The remaining baselines (SimSeg, OVSeg, ODISE, CAT-Seg, FC-CLIP) all score below 0.28 on E Sm and below 0.19 on E IoU, indicating that generic open-vocabulary segmentation methods struggle severely in camouflaged scenarios. Notably, fine-tuning OVSS models on OVCAMO (rather than training from scratch) degrades their performance further, suggesting a knowledge-forgetting problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the OVCOS task first formalized by OVCoser (Pang et al., ECCV 2024), which itself bridges open-vocabulary semantic segmentation (OVSS) and camouflaged object segmentation (COS). It builds directly on CLIP's frozen ConvNeXt-L visual encoder and text encoder, departing from standard OVSS methods that rely on handcrafted or simple learnable text prompts by instead injecting fine-grained visual attributes—extracted via density peak clustering on CLIP's internal features—into the text prompt. The key conceptual departure from OVCoser is the diagnosis of 'semantic confusion' (incomplete segmentation and class shift) as a misalignment problem between text and visual semantic spaces, addressed through a class-aware feature selection module and a semantic consistency loss rather than through multi-source visual cue extraction as in OVCoser.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OVCOS is a nascent task with only one prior benchmark (OVCoser), so establishing a stronger baseline with clear ablations is valuable for the community. The finding that fine-tuning standard OVSS methods on OVCamo drastically degrades performance (e.g., SAN drops from cSm 0.414 to 0.321) highlights a significant domain gap between general scenes and camouflaged scenes that remains largely unaddressed. However, evaluation is confined to a single dataset (OVCamo) with only one direct OVCOS comparison, leaving open questions about generalization to other camouflaged datasets, scalability to more complex scenes, and whether the clustering-based prompt enrichment transfers to other open-vocabulary tasks beyond camouflage.
