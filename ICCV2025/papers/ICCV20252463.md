## Problem

Event-based keypoint detection and matching remains severely limited because existing data-driven methods train with homographic adaptation, which creates static frame pairs that contradict the motion-dependent nature of event generation. This results in unstable descriptor matching across different timestamps, as corresponding keypoints vary in visual appearance due to camera motion. The paper addresses this by training on real event recordings with pseudo-labels that capture the true temporal dependence of event generation.

## Input / Output

The network takes a 10-channel Multi-Channel Time Surface (MCTS) tensor as input, encoding events by polarity (positive/negative) and 5 logarithmically distributed time windows (0.001 s to 0.1 s), and is fully convolutional so it handles arbitrary resolutions without retraining. It outputs a keypoint score heatmap (thresholded via local-maxima NMS with a 2-pixel neighborhood) and 256-dimensional L2-normalized descriptors per detected keypoint. During inference, only the event stream is required; the grayscale frames used for pseudo-label generation are not needed.

## Method

SuperEvent is trained self-supervised by generating temporally sparse pseudo-labels with SuperPoint+SuperGlue on pixel-synchronized grayscale frames, then feeding the corresponding real event data (as MCTS) to the network. Unlike prior homographic-adaptation approaches, multiple pseudo-labels per frame are created at increasing time intervals (with random step sizes), exposing the model to genuinely different event appearances rather than warped copies of the same events. The architecture combines a 3-layer MaxViT transformer backbone with a Feature Pyramid Network and grid-based VGG detector/descriptor heads. The loss couples cross-entropy detection with a margin-based descriptor loss that enforces similarity for correspondences and dissimilarity for non-correspondences. At inference, a parallelizable local-maxima NMS replaces classical box-based NMS, and the model integrates directly into OKVIS2 stereo VI-SLAM by replacing the frame-based detector and descriptor.

## Results

On the Event Camera Dataset (ECD), SuperEvent achieves 22.7% AUC@5°, 35.8% AUC@10°, and 46.7% AUC@20° for pose estimation, outperforming the best baseline RATE (3.3%, 8.4%, 18.0%) by 7–13×. On the more challenging EDS dataset, SuperEvent reaches 15.2% AUC@5° and 40.1% AUC@20°, still 5–7× better than all baselines. When integrated into OKVIS2 for stereo event-visual-inertial SLAM on TUM-VIE, SuperEvent achieves 0.55 cm average ATE, halving the error of the next-best method DEIO (1.00 cm with scale alignment). On VECtor large-scale sequences, SuperEvent achieves 33–133 cm ATE while DEIO fails on most sequences or reports errors of 325–492 cm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SuperEvent outperforms all 10 compared baselines across every dataset and metric. In keypoint-based pose estimation (Tables 2–3), the margin is largest against LLAK (0.5–0.7% AUC@5°) and EventPoint (1.6% AUC@5°), which rely on nearest-neighbor matching or static homographic training that contradicts the motion-dependent nature of events; RATE is the strongest baseline but still trails SuperEvent by 7× on ECD@5° (3.3% vs 22.7%). In the TUM-VIE SLAM comparison (Table 4), SuperEvent (0.55 cm avg ATE) halves the error of DEIO with scale alignment (1.00 cm) and is 8.7× better than ESVO2 (4.78 cm); notably, DEVO and DEIO require scale alignment to be competitive, whereas SuperEvent estimates absolute scale directly. On VECtor (Table 5), DEIO fails on 4 of 6 sequences and ESVO reports errors of 983–1371 cm, while SuperEvent completes all sequences with 33–133 cm ATE. The loop-closure experiment (Table 6) further shows that SuperEvent's descriptors enable reliable place recognition over minutes of separation, reducing ATE from ~120–160 cm (without loop closure) to ~5–9 cm (with loop closure), a capability none of the event-only baselines demonstrate.

## Relation to Prior Work

SuperEvent extends the SuperPoint/SuperGlue architecture paradigm (shared backbone with detector and descriptor heads) to event streams, but makes a fundamental departure in training: instead of homographic adaptation (warping a single event representation), it generates pseudo-labels from frame-based detectors on synchronized gray-scale frames and trains on real, temporally separated event recordings. The MCTS representation generalizes the single-channel time surface of HOTS [38] to a multi-channel tensor encoding both polarities and multiple logarithmically-spaced time windows, addressing the motion-speed sensitivity of prior encodings. The transformer backbone (MaxViT + FPN) replaces the VGG-based CNN used in SuperPoint and EventPoint, drawing on recent findings that attention improves feature extraction in event-based detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling event cameras to plug directly into decades of highly optimized frame-based SLAM infrastructure (demonstrated via OKVIS2 integration), rather than requiring purpose-built event-only pipelines. A key open limitation is that consecutive training samples are biased toward similar motion directions, causing descriptor matchability to degrade under strong motion changes—an inherent property of real event recordings that the authors acknowledge but do not fully resolve. Future directions include combining event and frame detectors for complementary robustness, and extending the pseudo-label approach to other event-based tasks like line detection.
