## Problem

Existing scientific paper understanding benchmarks either rely on model-generated annotations, focus on single-source comprehension (e.g., only charts or only figures), or lack cross-page reasoning entirely. No prior benchmark systematically evaluates whether VLMs can integrate heterogeneous information sources—figures, tables, text, formulas, and pseudocode—across multi-page scientific documents to answer domain-specific questions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251958/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each question is paired with a full scientific paper (averaging 19 pages, up to 35) rendered as high-resolution JPEG images at 144 DPI (~1573×1210 px per page), fed end-to-end to the VLM without OCR. Models that do not natively support multi-image input receive all pages concatenated into a single image. The model must select one of five answer options, and the circular evaluation protocol rotates the option order five times, requiring correct answers in all five rotations to count as solved.

## Method

MMCR is constructed through a three-stage pipeline: (1) expert annotators from 7 AI sub-fields select papers from Arxiv and OpenReview and generate at least 8 cross-source multiple-choice questions per paper across 10 predefined categories (e.g., Figure-Table, Multi-Table, Figure-Formula comprehension); (2) a two-round quality control filters questions that LLMs (Llama-3.3-70B, Qwen2.5-72B) can answer from internal knowledge alone, followed by parallel cross-checking and a voting mechanism to remove near-duplicate items; (3) a circular evaluation protocol (inspired by MMBench) rotates the five answer options five times, counting a question as solved only if the model answers correctly in all five trials. The benchmark comprises 276 questions over 31 papers, and 18 VLMs (15 open-source, 3 proprietary) are evaluated end-to-end on rendered images, with additional analyses on DPI sensitivity, CoT prompt effects by model scale, and a manual error taxonomy over 109 GPT-4o failures.

## Results

On the MMCR benchmark (276 cross-source reasoning questions across 31 scientific papers, avg. 19 pages), GPT-4o achieves the highest overall accuracy at 48.55%, followed by Qwen2.5-VL-72B at 39.86% and GeminiFlash2.0 at 34.42%. Performance drops sharply on multi-table comprehension, where GPT-4o reaches only 20% and Qwen2.5-VL-72B scores 0%. The majority of open-source models (13 out of 15) achieve less than 15% overall accuracy, with several (Molmo-7B-O, DeepSeek-VL2, Llama3.2-Vision) scoring below 2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o leads all 18 evaluated models with 48.55% overall accuracy, outperforming the best open-source model Qwen2.5-VL-72B (39.86%) by 8.69 percentage points and GeminiFlash2.0 (34.42%) by 14.13 points. GPT-4o matches or exceeds Qwen2.5-VL-72B in 9 of 10 categories, with the largest gap in Figure-Formula comprehension (84.61% vs. 38.46%) and Figure Comprehension (44.19% vs. 30.23%). On multi-table comprehension, GPT-4o achieves 20% while Qwen2.5-VL-72B and InternVL2.5-78B both score 0%, making it the single hardest category. Among smaller open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL-7B (21.74%) substantially outperforms peers like mPLUG-Owl3 (7.61%), Idefics3 (9.78%), and InternVL2.5-8B (12.32%), while Molmo-7B-O, DeepSeek-VL2, and Llama3.2-Vision all score below 2%. GPT-4o mini (32.61%) trails the full GPT-4o by 15.94 points, and GeminiFlash2.0 (34.42%) is the only proprietary model to beat GPT-4o mini, particularly on multi-table (30% vs. 10%) and pseudocode (60% vs. 40%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of scientific paper understanding and document VQA. Prior scientific paper benchmarks (ArxivQA, Charxiv, MMSCI, SCI-CQA) rely on model-generated annotations and evaluate single-source comprehension, while document understanding benchmarks (DUDE, MP-DocVQA, MMLongBench-Doc) address multi-page reasoning but neglect cross-source integration and domain-specific density. MMCR departs from both lines by requiring human expert annotation and, critically, by formulating questions whose answers are only derivable by integrating evidence across heterogeneous sources (figures, tables, text, formulas, pseudocode) within multi-page scientific papers—a capability no prior benchmark explicitly targets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even GPT-4o achieves only 48.55% overall accuracy and a mere 20% on multi-table comprehension, exposing a substantial gap in VLMs' ability to synthesize cross-source evidence in scientifically dense documents. The finding that Chain-of-Thought prompting actively degrades performance for small models while improving it for large ones challenges the common assumption that CoT is a universal reasoning aid and points to perceptual and evidence-retrieval bottlenecks as the primary failure modes. Open questions include whether the 276-question scale is sufficient for robust cross-model comparison, whether the 7-subject coverage generalizes beyond AI/ML papers, and whether the end-to-end visual-input protocol (no OCR) is the most informative evaluation setting for scientific paper comprehension.
