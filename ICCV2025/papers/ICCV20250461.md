## Problem

End-to-end multivariable spatiotemporal prediction suffers from representation inconsistency when highly divergent meteorological variables are forced into a shared feature space, often degrading rather than improving accuracy. Adapting two-stage training from generative models (e.g., latent diffusion) introduces a second problem—task inconformity—where the first stage learns reconstruction while the second must perform prediction, misaligning the learning objectives and yielding suboptimal results.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250461/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a spatiotemporal tensor of shape T×N×C×H×W (e.g., 12 past frames of 4 variables at 32×64 or 64×128 resolution) and produces T' predicted frames per variable. Each variable is assigned its own dedicated encoder-decoder pair, so the wind variable (with U and V components) is treated as a single modality with C=2 channels. At inference, only the trained Encoder, Translator, and Decoder are used, keeping parameter count and computational cost equivalent to a standard end-to-end model.

## Method

Met²Net introduces an implicit two-stage training paradigm within a single continuous training cycle: in Stage I, the Translator's gradients are frozen while per-variable Encoders and Decoders learn a shared latent space under a prediction loss; in Stage II, the Encoders and Decoders are frozen and the Translator is trained to model inter-variable interactions, also under a prediction loss in latent space. Momentum updates (α=0.999) propagate parameter changes to the frozen components at the end of each stage, ensuring smooth adaptation without explicit stage separation. The Translator employs a Variable Attention mechanism—self-attention computed over the variable axis via 2D-CNN-extracted Q, K, V—followed by a TAU Block for spatiotemporal feature extraction and prediction. This design keeps both stages aligned on a forecasting objective (unlike generative two-stage methods that shift from reconstruction to denoising), while dedicated encoders/decoders preserve variable-specific structure and the attention module captures cross-variable coupling.

## Results

On the low-resolution Weather_L dataset (12→12 frames), Met²Net achieves SOTA MSE across all four meteorological variables: 1.5055 (UV10), 0.8271 (T2M), 0.0422 (TCC), and 24.3854 (R). Compared to the strongest baseline TAU, Met²Net reduces MSE by 5.46%, 28.82%, 10.59%, and 23.39% for UV10, T2M, TCC, and R respectively, while using fewer parameters (8.65M vs. 12.22M) and a single unified model (N=1) instead of four separate models. On the ERA5 hurricane trajectory task, Met²Net achieves the lowest MSE (9645.28 for MSL) and highest R² (0.9949 for T2M) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Met²Net outperforms all ten baselines on every variable where a comparison is available. Against the strongest single-variable baseline TAU (CVPR'23), Met²Net reduces T2M MSE from 1.1620 to 0.8271 (−28.82%) and R MSE from 31.8310 to 24.3854 (−23.39%), while also improving UV10 (−5.46%) and TCC (−10.59%). Earlier recurrent baselines (ConvLSTM, PredRNN, PredRNN++) and non-recurrent baselines (SimVP, MAU, ConvNeXt, HorNet) are all surpassed by a wide margin on T2M and R, with T2M MSE gaps ranging from 0.34 to 1.09. Wast (AAAI'24) reports only T2M and R results; Met²Net still leads on both (0.8271 vs. 1.0980 for T2M). On the ERA5 hurricane task, Met²Net beats MogaNet (ICLR'24) by 48% in MSL MSE (9645 vs. 18618) and achieves the highest R² across all four variables. Notably, Met²Net uses a single unified model (N=1, 8.65M params) where all baselines require four separate models (N=4), making it both more accurate and more parameter-efficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TAU (Tan et al., CVPR 2023) as its spatiotemporal backbone, using the TAU Block as the ST(·) feature extractor, and borrows the momentum-update mechanism from Momentum Contrast (He et al., CVPR 2020) to handle frozen parameters during training. Conceptually, it adapts the two-stage encoder-decoder paradigm from multimodal generative models (Latent Diffusion, MAGVIT, VideoPoet) to the prediction domain, but departs from those works by replacing the reconstruction-then-denoising pipeline with an implicit two-stage scheme that uses a prediction loss in latent space to maintain task consistency. The key departure from prior spatiotemporal prediction work (ConvLSTM, PredRNN, SimVP, TAU) is the treatment of each meteorological variable as an independent modality with dedicated encoders/decoders, rather than channel-stacking into a single network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: existing SOTA spatiotemporal predictors are single-variable, yet real weather systems require multivariable integration, and the paper demonstrates (via CKA analysis and ablations) that naive channel-stacking actively degrades performance due to representation inconsistency. The implicit two-stage training with momentum updates is a lightweight, architecture-agnostic training strategy that could transfer to other multivariable prediction settings beyond meteorology. However, the evidence base is somewhat narrow—experiments use a single backbone (TAU Block), relatively small spatial resolutions (32×64 to 128×128), and a synthetic multivariable benchmark (MvMmfnist)—leaving open whether the approach scales to the large-scale, high-resolution settings of foundation weather models (Pangu, FuXi) or benefits from deeper/more diverse ST(·) blocks.
