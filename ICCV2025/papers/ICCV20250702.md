## Problem

State-of-the-art optical flow models require multiple high-end GPUs and large training data to push accuracy, making research inaccessible to smaller groups and often yielding poor cross-dataset generalization with missing fine details. The paper argues that brute-force scaling of hardware and data is a shortcut that precludes methodological progress, and that pre-trained vision foundation models can be repurposed to bootstrap flow estimation at a fraction of the training cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250702/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images (I_0, I_1) and produces a dense 2D optical flow field at full resolution. Internally, the depth foundation model (Depth Anything v2) processes each image independently to yield inverse depth maps and decoder features, while the flow backbone operates at 1/8 input resolution with iterative refinement and convex upsampling to recover full-resolution flow. The model is trained and evaluated at standard resolutions (e.g., 436×1024 for Sintel, 370×1242 for KITTI), with down-sampling by 2× or 8× for Spring and LayeredFlow evaluations respectively.

## Method

FlowSeek builds on the SEA-RAFT iterative refinement backbone and injects two types of priors from a frozen depth foundation model (Depth Anything v2): (i) decoder features Φ are downsampled via a shallow BottNeck and concatenated with the backbone's ResNet features to enrich the 4D correlation volume, and (ii) inverse depth maps D are fed to the ContextNet alongside the images to strengthen contextual and hidden-state features. A third component, a BasesNet, processes eight analytically computed motion basis vectors (derived from the six degrees of freedom of rigid camera motion combined with the predicted inverse depth, with focal length eliminated via rescaling) to produce dense geometric prior features that are concatenated with the context and hidden states. The entire system is trained end-to-end with a Laplace mixture log-likelihood loss over iterative flow updates, requiring only a single consumer-grade RTX 3090 GPU, yet achieving 10–15% relative improvement over SEA-RAFT on Sintel Final and KITTI in zero-shot cross-dataset generalization.

## Results

On Sintel Final (Tartan pre-training), FlowSeek(L) achieves 2.18 EPE, outperforming SEA-RAFT(L) (2.73) by ~20% and all other methods. On KITTI 2015, FlowSeek(L) reaches 3.31 Fl-EPE and 11.2 Fl-all, beating SEA-RAFT(L) (4.21 / 13.5) by ~21% and ~17% respectively. On Spring, FlowSeek(L) achieves 3.838 1px and 0.402 EPE, surpassing all SEA-RAFT variants. On LayeredFlow, FlowSeek(L) attains 8.30 EPE on All pixels, improving over SEA-RAFT(L) (10.99) by more than 2 pixels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowSeek(L) with Tartan pre-training achieves the best Sintel Final (2.18) and KITTI 2015 Fl-EPE (3.31) among all methods trained under the SEA-RAFT protocol, beating SEA-RAFT(L) by 0.59 EPE on Sintel Final and 0.42 on KITTI Fl-EPE. Against earlier architectures, FlowSeek(L) outperforms FlowFormer (2.40 Sintel Final, 4.09 KITTI Fl-EPE), CRAFT (2.79, 4.88), RAFT (2.71, 5.04), and PWC-Net (3.93, 10.4) by substantial margins. The only method that surpasses FlowSeek(L) on Sintel Final is DDVM with the full AF+T+KU+Tartan training set (2.00), but DDVM requires significantly more synthetic data (Kubric + TartanAir) than FlowSeek's Tartan-only pre-training. On Spring, FlowSeek(L) (3.838 1px, 0.402 EPE) beats all SEA-RAFT variants and FlowFormer++ (4.482, 0.447), with only MS-RAFT+ (3.577, 0.397) performing slightly better but using 2× A100 GPUs and VIPER data. On LayeredFlow, FlowSeek(L) (8.30 EPE) outperforms all listed baselines including SEA-RAFT(L) (10.99), GMFlow (9.09), and RAFT (9.38), with FlowSeek(M) matching its EPE at 8.30.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowSeek sits at the intersection of three research threads: the iterative-refinement flow backbone lineage (RAFT → SEA-RAFT), the rapidly growing family of single-image depth foundation models (Depth Anything v2, DPT), and the classical 6-DOF rigid-motion basis formulation from Heeger & Jepson (1992). The key conceptual departure is treating the depth foundation model not as a separate pipeline stage but as a feature and prior source embedded directly inside the flow architecture—enriching correlation-volume features with foundation-model decoder outputs and feeding depth-derived motion bases through a dedicated BasesNet. The paper also demonstrates that the BaseNet component is backbone-agnostic, improving CRAFT and FlowFormer when plugged in, positioning the contribution as a general design pattern rather than a single architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: FlowSeek achieves SOTA zero-shot generalization on Sintel Final and KITTI (10–15% relative improvement over SEA-RAFT) while training on a single RTX 3090, roughly 8× less hardware than competing methods. This lowers the barrier for academic groups without multi-GPU clusters and validates the broader strategy of repurposing large pre-trained vision foundation models for downstream geometric tasks. The main limitation is that the foundation model itself was trained at web scale with substantial compute, so the savings are at the flow-training stage rather than end-to-end. Training data remains an open bottleneck, and the authors note that future work should explore synthetic data generation strategies analogous to those in depth estimation.
