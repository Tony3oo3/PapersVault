## Problem

Existing methods for learning fine-grained representations from coarse labels either focus solely on intra-class alignment while neglecting inter-class separability, or enforce separability by predefining the number of fine-grained classes via clustering—making them impractical when that number is unknown or dynamically changing. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a bottom-up learning paradigm that infers fine-grained class structure without any prior on the number of classes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250928/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB images with only coarse labels (e.g., 'Cat' rather than species-level labels) and produces fine-grained class predictions at test time. During training, it additionally constructs a multi-level hierarchical dendrogram over the dataset, where each node represents a group of semantically similar samples and serves as a pseudo fine-grained label. A MoCo-style momentum queue of size 65,536 stores candidate representations for contrastive pair selection, and K-means (with K set to 5–10× the number of fine-grained classes) initializes the dendrogram to reduce computational cost.

## Method

BuCSFR alternates two modules grounded in an EM framework: (1) a Bottom-up Construction (BuC) module that incrementally merges similar clusters into a dendrogram using a minimal information-loss criterion derived from the Information Bottleneck principle, approximated via cluster entropy and inter-cluster mutual information; and (2) a Separable Fine-grained Representation (SFR) module that treats dendrogram nodes as prototypes to select high-quality positives (samples close to the nearest prototype) and high-confidence negatives (samples far from the prototype or from other coarse classes) for a weighted contrastive loss. The merging criterion compares a product of entropy and inverse mutual information against a threshold T, and merging occurs once per epoch per coarse class to ensure stability. This bottom-up construction eliminates the need to predefine the number of fine-grained classes, and the alternating optimization between BuC and SFR iteratively refines both the dendrogram structure and the representation quality.

## Results

BuCSFR achieves state-of-the-art results across all five benchmark datasets. On CIFAR100, it reaches 73.83% R@1 and 78.84% kNN@5, improving over the second-best method (LCR) by 2.59% and 3.37% respectively. On ImageNet-1K, BuCSFR attains 23.38% R@1 and 33.37% kNN@5, surpassing LCR by 1.19% and 2.36%. On the multi-level iNaturalist-2019 dataset, BuCSFR consistently outperforms MaskCon across all seven taxonomic levels, with the largest gap at the species level (76.84% R@5 vs. 69.33% for MaskCon).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BuCSFR outperforms all four baselines (CoIns, Grafit, MaskCon, LCR) on every metric across CIFAR100 and ImageNet-1K, with the largest gains in kNN@5 (+3.37% over LCR on CIFAR100; +2.36% over LCR on ImageNet-1K), confirming superior inter-class separability. On CIFAR10toy, where fine-grained classes are more visually distinct, improvements are marginal (e.g., +0.42% R@1 over MaskCon), and BuCSFR is 0.02% behind LCR on R@10—the only loss in the entire comparison. On the harder FGVC-Aircraft dataset, BuCSFR achieves 57.97% R@1, surpassing the second-best LCR (51.79%) by 6.18%, demonstrating its advantage grows with task difficulty. On iNaturalist-2019, BuCSFR consistently beats MaskCon (the second-best) at all seven taxonomic levels, with the gap widening from ~0.5% at the kingdom level to 7.5% R@5 at the species level, highlighting its robustness to large class-count increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MoCo contrastive learning framework and the Information Bottleneck principle, but departs fundamentally from the dominant top-down paradigm in coarse-label FGVR (e.g., PE-HCM, CoIns, Grafit, MaskCon) that requires predefining the number of fine-grained classes per coarse class. Instead, it introduces a bottom-up dendrogram construction paradigm where fine-grained semantics are inferred incrementally from instance-level data via a minimal information-loss merging criterion, eliminating the need for a fixed cluster count. The SFR module then repurposes dendrogram nodes as pseudo-labels for contrastive learning, creating an EM-style alternating optimization between structure discovery and representation learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical gap in biodiversity recognition, medical imaging, and other domains where the number of fine-grained classes is unknown or dynamically changing (e.g., new species emergence). The EM-theoretic grounding and the ability to discover latent finer-grained classes without supervision are meaningful advances over prior coarse-label methods. However, the authors acknowledge that the constructed dendrogram may not always align with human semantic cognition due to the visual-semantic similarity mismatch, and the reliance on K-means initialization (despite claimed insensitivity) remains a practical consideration. Open questions include whether the bottom-up paradigm scales efficiently to very large datasets and how to incorporate human cognitive priors into the merging criterion.
