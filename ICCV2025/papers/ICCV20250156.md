## Problem

Existing in-generation watermarking methods for text-to-image diffusion models can only embed watermarks across the entire image and typically require architectural modifications (secret encoders, distortion layers, adapters) or direct latent-space access, limiting their plug-and-play applicability. There is no mechanism to selectively watermark specific objects within a generated image without relying on external segmentation masks or post-processing, leaving a gap for fine-grained, object-level provenance protection in T2I pipelines.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt in which the user places a learned pseudo-token W* adjacent to the target object token (e.g., 'A photo of a [cat W*]' watermarks only the cat, while '[A photo of a cat W*]' watermarks the full image). No segmentation masks or auxiliary spatial information are required; object localization is derived entirely from cross-attention maps during generation. The output is a watermarked RGB image carrying a 48-bit key, detectable by a blind neural detector that requires only the watermarked image as input.

## Method

The core contribution is a single pseudo-token W* whose 768-dimensional embedding in the text encoder is fine-tuned (a 10^5× parameter reduction over baselines) to act as a watermark trigger. Training uses an img2img pipeline: the input image is encoded to latent z_0, forward-diffused to an optimal timestep τ*=8, then iteratively denoised with classifier-free guidance while the W* embedding is optimized via a combined loss of BCE watermark-detection loss and a latent-matching loss that preserves image fidelity. For object-level control, the cross-attention map of W* is overlaid onto the attention map of the target object token P_i at each denoising step, with a timestep-dependent overlay strength π(t) that concentrates watermarking near the VAE decoder where attention maps are most reliable. Because only the text encoder embedding changes, the method is plug-and-play across Stable Diffusion variants, personalized UNets, and Textual Inversion tokens without modifying the UNet or VAE.

## Results

On MS-COCO (48-bit watermarking), the proposed method (Ours+SD) achieves 99% bit accuracy on clean images, 96% under JPEG compression, and 89% under the DiffPure adversarial attack, while maintaining a PSNR of 40.92 dB and SSIM of 0.97. On WikiArt, it reaches 99% clean bit accuracy, 95% under JPEG, and 88% under DiffPure with a PSNR of 39.92 dB. The method requires only 768 trainable parameters, a 10^5× reduction compared to all baselines which require 10^5+ parameters. Object-level watermarking maintains 90–99% bit accuracy for up to three non-overlapping objects under various attacks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (Ours+SD) outperforms all six baselines on both imperceptibility and robustness. Compared to AquaLoRA [6] (which shares the same watermark detector), the method achieves over 20% higher bit accuracy on common attacks and over 7 dB higher PSNR (40.92 vs. 31.46). Against Stable Signature [7], the method improves JPEG robustness from 59% to 97% and DiffPure robustness from 57% to 89%, while also achieving a 9 dB PSNR gain. Versus TrustMark [2], which achieves the highest PSNR (40.98), the proposed method is nearly matched on imperceptibility but substantially outperforms on adversarial robustness (89% vs. 74% on DiffPure) and is the only in-generation method. Compared to WAM [29], the only other method supporting object-level watermarking, the proposed method achieves higher bit accuracy across all attacks (e.g., 97% vs. 77% on JPEG) without requiring segmentation masks during inference. The method also uniquely supports plug-and-play integration with personalized pipelines (Textual Inversion, styled UNets) while maintaining watermark fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Textual Inversion paradigm (learnable pseudo-token embeddings) for watermarking, and adapts Prompt-to-Prompt's cross-attention control mechanism to localize watermarks on specific objects. It departs sharply from prior in-generation watermarking methods (Stable Signature, LaWa, AquaLoRA, WAM) that require architectural modifications—LoRA adapters, distortion layers, or segmentation masks—by training only a single 768-dimensional token embedding in the text encoder, leaving the UNet and VAE entirely frozen. The 'prêt-à-porter' positioning is the key conceptual departure: the watermark controller is a plug-in token rather than a structural change to the diffusion pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10^5× parameter reduction (768 trainable parameters vs. 10^5+ in baselines) makes watermarking practically deployable across arbitrary Stable Diffusion variants without retraining the generative model, which is significant for industry adoption where models are proprietary or expensive to fine-tune. Object-level watermarking without segmentation masks opens a new control axis for copyright protection of specific assets within a composite image. However, the method's reliance on cross-attention map accuracy limits precision in complex scenes, and the significant accuracy drop under ≥40% object overlap (0.79 vs. 0.99 for non-overlapping) remains an open challenge for multi-object watermarking. The authors explicitly call for future work toward training-free watermarking and more robust multi-object extraction.
