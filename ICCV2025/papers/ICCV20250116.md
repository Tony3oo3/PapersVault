## Problem

Under-display ToF imaging suffers from severe degradations (signal attenuation, multi-path interference, temporal noise) introduced by transparent OLED panels, and existing iterative diffusion-based restoration methods rely on integer-order differential equations that only depend on the current state, lacking the long-term memory needed to capture historical dependencies. Additionally, continuous convolution approaches based on coordinate-based neural fields incur high computational costs and complex hyperparameter tuning, limiting their practicality for depth restoration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250116/cropped_page0_idx5.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes corrupted ToF raw depth measurements (180×240, cropped to 176×240 patches) or low-resolution depth maps as input and produces a restored high-quality depth map. A Deep Initial State Builder (DISB), which can be any existing restoration network (UD-ToFnet, PE-ToF, NAFNet, Restormer), first generates an initial depth estimate along with auxiliary amplitudes, features, and offsets that feed into the iterative refinement. The DISB acts as a denoiser for UD-ToF, an identity mapping for denoising, or an upsampler for super-resolution, making the framework task-agnostic.

## Method

LFRD² embeds a time-fractional reaction-diffusion PDE (using the Caputo derivative discretized via L1 approximation) into a neural network, where the fractional order α is dynamically generated by a small network rather than fixed, enabling adaptive long-term memory across iterations. The diffusion term implements Perona-Malik anisotropic diffusion with a neural-network-predicted diffusivity g(·), while a reaction term λ(u₀−uₙ) anchors the solution toward the initial estimate. For spatial propagation, the paper replaces coordinate-based MLPs with an efficient continuous convolution operator that predicts two scalar coefficients (A, B) via three lightweight convolution layers and applies repeated differentiation, yielding (u*K)(x,y) = [(Au+B)*K̂^(2)](x,y) with far fewer FLOPs than Neural Field Convolution. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with Adam (lr 1e-4, batch 16) for 250–1000 epochs depending on the dataset, and the fractional order, diffusivity, and convolution coefficients are all learned jointly.

## Results

On the primary UD-ToF task, LFRD² achieves MAE 8.41 mm and RMSE 10.99 mm on SUD-ToF, and MAE 16.73 mm and RMSE 30.94 mm on RUD-ToF, outperforming the closest competitor UD-ToFnet (8.88/11.50 and 17.29/31.11 respectively). On the FLAT denoising benchmark, LFRD² reaches MAE 4.13 mm and RMSE 7.35 mm, surpassing all 9 baselines. For depth super-resolution on NYUv2, LFRD² attains the best MSE/MAE at all three upsampling factors (4×: 2.85/0.47, 8×: 12.8/1.16, 16×: 52.3/2.58), beating the prior best DSR-EI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LFRD² consistently outperforms all 10 baselines on MAE and RMSE across both SUD-ToF and RUD-ToF, with the largest margins over traditional methods CDNLM (MAE 33.23 vs 8.41 on SUD-ToF) and JGDR (MAE 9.14 vs 8.41). Against the strongest learning-based competitor UD-ToFnet, LFRD² improves MAE by 0.47 mm (SUD-ToF) and 0.56 mm (RUD-ToF), and RMSE by 0.51 mm and 0.17 mm respectively; however, UD-ToFnet edges out LFRD² on ρ₁.₀₂ for RUD-ToF (70.13% vs 69.97%) and ties on ρ₁.₁₀ for SUD-ToF (99.94%). On FLAT denoising, LFRD² (MAE 4.13, RMSE 7.35) beats UD-ToFnet (4.41, 8.23) and SHARPnet (4.62, 10.26), with a particularly large gap over ToFnet (54.33, 74.99). For NYUv2 depth super-resolution, LFRD² surpasses DSR-EI at every scale (e.g., 16×: MSE 52.3 vs 57.0, MAE 2.58 vs 2.70) and also outperforms SGNet, DADA, and LGR. In the ablation against RNN variants (GRU, LSTM, NLSPN, dilated convolution), LFRD² achieves the best MAE (16.73) with comparable FLOPs to GRU (+7.69G vs +7.62G) but lower than LSTM (+10.4G), while NFC continuous convolution requires 20.5G FLOPs versus LFRD²'s 7.69G.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the trainable nonlinear reaction-diffusion (TNRD) framework of Chen and Pock by replacing the integer-order PDE with a time-fractional (Caputo) reaction-diffusion equation, introducing long-term memory into the iterative refinement loop. The continuous convolution operator is inspired by Neural Field Convolutions (NFC) via repeated differentiation, but departs substantially: instead of predefining a Gaussian kernel and estimating Dirac deltas on fixed control points, LFRD² directly generates the kernel through a lightweight coefficient-prediction network (Au + B), eliminating the need for coordinate-based MLPs. The deep initial state builder is instantiated with UD-ToFnet, the authors' own prior work on under-display ToF restoration, positioning LFRD² as a physics-informed refinement stage atop an existing learned backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a rare combination of interpretability (each iteration corresponds to a physically meaningful diffusion/reaction step with a learnable fractional order) and efficiency (the continuous convolution costs ~7.7 GFLOPs vs. ~20.5 GFLOPs for NFC), making it attractive for depth-restoration pipelines where both explainability and speed matter. The demonstration on FLAT (ToF denoising) and NYUv2 (depth super-resolution) suggests the fractional reaction-diffusion + continuous convolution module is a general-purpose refinement operator, not locked to UD-ToF. However, the authors acknowledge training instability (NaN values) and the need for careful strategy design, and the performance gains over UD-ToFnet, while consistent, are modest (e.g., 17.29→16.73 MAE on RUD-ToF). Open questions include whether implicit numerical schemes can improve stability and whether the learnable fractional order generalizes to more complex degradation models.
