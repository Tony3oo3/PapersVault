## Problem

Existing multi-view dynamic scene reconstruction methods based on NeRF or 3D Gaussian Splatting are limited to modeling fine-scale motion and suffer from flickering, blurring, and high training/storage costs when applied to large-scale, complex real-world motion (e.g., basketball games). No prior method has successfully unified fine-scale and large-scale dynamic scene reconstruction in a single framework with compact representation and fast training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251447/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view synchronized video sequences (e.g., 21 cameras at 2704×2028 for N3DV, 13 cameras at 1280×720 for MeetRoom, 34 cameras at 1920×1080 for VRU basketball) along with a fused SfM point cloud sampled from N frames across the time domain to initialize seed positions. It produces a set of time-varying Temporal Gaussians (k=10 per seed) that are rasterized via α-blending to render novel-view images at arbitrary viewpoints, with a deactivation mechanism that prunes low-opacity Gaussians at each query time to reduce computational load.

## Method

LocalDyGS decomposes the global 3D space into independent local spaces, each anchored by a seed carrying a position, a 64-dim static feature shared across all time steps, and a scale parameter defining its spatial influence range. A global dynamic residual field built from multi-resolution 4D hash encoding (INSP-style) plus a shallow cross-fusion MLP provides time-specific residual features, while a separate weight field adaptively balances the static and dynamic contributions via a weighted linear sum. The combined weighted feature is decoded by dedicated MLPs into Temporal Gaussian parameters (mean, rotation, scale, opacity, color), with a volume regularization loss encouraging small, locally-responsible Gaussians. An Adaptive Seed Growing strategy monitors 2D projection gradients during training and inserts new seeds where reconstruction error exceeds a threshold, compensating for incomplete SfM coverage. This decoupled static/dynamic design reduces the burden on the dynamic field (whose values tend toward zero) and enables the method to handle both fine-scale and large-scale motion with ~100 MB storage and 0.58 h training on N3DV.

## Results

On the N3DV dataset, LocalDyGS achieves the best PSNR of 32.28 dB and LPIPS of 0.043, surpassing the previous SOTA SpaceTimeGS (32.05 dB, 0.044 LPIPS) while training in 0.58 h (vs. >5 h) and using only 100 MB storage (vs. 200 MB). On MeetRoom, it reaches 32.45 dB PSNR with 90 MB storage, outperforming 3DGStream (30.79 dB, 1230 MB) by 1.66 dB. On the large-scale VRU basketball court dataset, LocalDyGS achieves 30.58 dB PSNR and 0.944 SSIM, substantially outperforming dynamic baselines 4DGS (28.32 dB) and SpaceTimeGS (27.42 dB), and matching the static 3DGS baseline (30.50 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest dynamic baseline SpaceTimeGS [26], LocalDyGS wins on PSNR (+0.23 dB), LPIPS (0.043 vs 0.044), training time (0.58 h vs >5 h, ~9× faster), and model size (100 MB vs 200 MB), but trails on DSSIM₁ (0.028 vs 0.026) and rendering speed (105 vs 140 FPS). Against the streaming method 3DGStream [39], LocalDyGS achieves higher PSNR on both N3DV (32.28 vs 31.67) and MeetRoom (32.45 vs 30.79) with dramatically smaller storage (100 MB vs 1230 MB), though 3DGStream renders faster (215 FPS). On the large-scale VRU basketball court, LocalDyGS outperforms 4DGS [47] by 2.26 dB PSNR and SpaceTimeGS by 3.16 dB, confirming its advantage in complex motion scenes where prior methods suffer from blurring and flickering. Against static baselines (3DGS, 2DGS, GOF) on VRU, LocalDyGS matches or slightly exceeds 3DGS (30.58 vs 30.50 PSNR) while handling full temporal dynamics, whereas 4DGS and SpaceTimeGS degrade significantly. On MeetRoom, LocalDyGS also surpasses the static 3DGS baseline (32.45 vs 31.31 dB) and I-NGP (28.10 dB) by a wide margin, demonstrating that the local-space decomposition generalizes well to sparse-view, textureless scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

LocalDyGS builds directly on the ScaffoldGS anchor-based 3DGS framework (seeds with offsets and MLP-decoded parameters) and the INGP multi-resolution hash encoding for the dynamic residual field, while adopting the standard 3DGS rendering pipeline. Its key conceptual departure from prior dynamic 3DGS methods (4DGS, SpaceTimeGS, 3DGStream) is the rejection of explicit long-term per-Gaussian motion modeling in favor of decomposing the scene into independent local spaces, each capturing only short-range temporal dynamics. The static-dynamic feature decoupling draws inspiration from deformation-based NeRF/3DGS methods (Deformable 3DGS, Swift4D) but operates at the feature level rather than the geometric level, and the Adaptive Seed Growing strategy is a novel contribution to mitigate SfM coverage gaps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to demonstrate competitive-quality dynamic reconstruction on a large-scale real-world basketball court dataset, where prior dynamic 3DGS methods (SpaceTimeGS, 4DGS) degrade by 2–3 dB in PSNR while LocalDyGS matches static baselines. The local-space decomposition paradigm offers a practical path toward scaling dynamic reconstruction to arbitrarily large scenes without the storage and training costs of global motion fields. Open questions include whether the approach handles topological changes (objects entering/leaving the scene), how sensitive it is to SfM quality in textureless environments, and whether the seed-based local spaces can be made fully adaptive to arbitrary camera trajectories at inference time.
