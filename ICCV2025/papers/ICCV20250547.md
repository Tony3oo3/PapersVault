## Problem

Existing unsupervised deblurring methods (prior-based, reblurring augmentation, CycleGAN-based) struggle with the spatial diversity of real-world blur patterns, often overfitting to a single blur template or relying on pre-trained supervised models. While diffusion models have shown promise in supervised deblurring, they have not been adapted to unpaired settings, and prior DM-based approaches either generate sharp images directly (introducing unpredictable artifacts) or use spatially fixed priors that cannot be learned without paired data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250547/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single blurry RGB image (256×256 patches during training) and produces a deblurred RGB image of the same resolution. Training requires only unpaired collections of blurry and sharp images with no pixel-level correspondence. During inference, the denoising network generates a spatially varying texture prior in latent space (H×W×C) from pure Gaussian noise conditioned on the blurry input's extracted features, which then guides the deblurring network through 8 denoising iterations.

## Method

TP-Diff uses a diffusion model to generate a spatially varying texture prior in latent space (rather than generating sharp images directly), which is then exploited by a deblurring network to remove blur. The Texture Prior Encoder (TPE) employs a learnable memory bank that is enhanced by texture-rich tokens from unpaired sharp images, then transfers the most relevant memory entries to represent texture templates in the blurry input. The deblurring network incorporates TTformer layers at multiple scales, where the Filter-Modulated Multi-head Self-Attention (FM-MSA) predicts spatially adaptive filter offsets and weights from the texture prior to perform region-specific blur removal. Training proceeds in two stages: first, TPE and the CycleGAN structure are jointly trained with adversarial, cycle-consistency, and a novel wavelet-based adversarial loss; second, TPE is frozen and the diffusion model is trained to generate the prior from noise, adding a diffusion reconstruction loss. This design requires only 8 denoising iterations at inference and uses 11.89M parameters, substantially fewer than competing methods.

## Results

On the GoPro test set, TP-Diff achieves 28.13 dB PSNR and 0.903 SSIM, outperforming the previous best unpaired method UCL by 3.07 dB PSNR. On RealBlur-R, TP-Diff reaches 34.95 dB PSNR (0.933 SSIM), a 4.42 dB improvement over UCL. With the self-enhancement variant (TP-Diff-se), the method achieves 30.16 dB on GoPro and 35.32 dB on RealBlur-R, surpassing SEMGUD (29.06 / 35.51 dB) while using only 11.89M parameters versus SEMGUD's 67.9M. On the generalization benchmarks RB2V_Street and RSBlur, TP-Diff leads all unpaired baselines by 0.66 dB and 0.23 dB respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among unpaired methods, TP-Diff decisively outperforms all CycleGAN-based baselines: it gains 3.07 dB over UCL (25.06→28.13) and 3.54 dB over FCL-GAN (24.59→28.13) on GoPro, with similar margins on HIDE (+2.85 dB over UCL) and RealBlur-R (+4.42 dB over UCL). Against the strongest unpaired competitor SEMGUD [3], the base TP-Diff trails by 0.93 dB on GoPro (28.13 vs 29.06), but the self-enhancement variant TP-Diff-se reverses this to a 1.10 dB lead (30.16 vs 29.06) while using only 11.89M parameters versus SEMGUD's 67.9M. On RealBlur-J, TP-Diff and SEMGUD tie at 28.01 dB PSNR. On the generalization datasets (Table 2), TP-Diff leads the best unpaired baseline FCL-GAN by 0.66 dB on RB2V_Street (22.89 vs 21.77) and 0.23 dB on RSBlur (28.40 vs 28.17). All supervised methods (UFPNet, NAFNet, HI-Diff) remain substantially ahead, with UFPNet reaching 34.06 dB on GoPro versus TP-Diff's 28.13 dB, highlighting the inherent gap between paired and unpaired training.

## Relation to Prior Work

TP-Diff sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) CycleGAN-based unsupervised deblurring (FCL-GAN, SEMGUD), from which it inherits the blurry-sharp cycle structure and adversarial/cycle-consistency losses; (2) diffusion-model-based deblurring (HI-Diff, DiffIR, icDPM), from which it adopts the forward/reverse diffusion framework but repurposes it to generate spatially varying texture priors in latent space rather than full sharp images; and (3) prior-guided restoration (UFPNet, Restormer), which motivates the TPE's memory-bank design and the TTformer's adaptive filtering. The key departure is that prior diffusion-based deblurring methods either synthesize images directly (risking artifacts) or estimate a fixed latent prior from paired data, whereas TP-Diff learns a *spatially varying* prior from *unpaired* data via a two-stage TPE-then-DM pipeline, making it the first work to integrate DMs into unpaired restoration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that diffusion models can serve as texture-prior generators (requiring only 8 denoising iterations) rather than full image synthesizers in an unpaired setting, TP-Diff opens a path for applying DMs to other unpaired restoration tasks (denoising, dehazing, super-resolution) where pixel-level supervision is unavailable. The wavelet-based adversarial loss and the FM-MSA adaptive filtering are modular components that could be adopted independently. Limitations include the reliance on cycle consistency (no ground-truth pixel supervision), the added inference latency from the denoising network, and the fact that the largest gains over the strongest unpaired baseline (SEMGUD) appear only when a self-enhancement strategy is added, partially undermining the 'fully unpaired' claim. An open question is whether the memory-bank TPE scales to higher-resolution or video deblurring without prohibitive memory cost.
