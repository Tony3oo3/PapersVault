## Problem

Existing domain generalized semantic segmentation methods rely on feature normalization, whitening, or projection into a source-style space to align features across domains, but they neglect the intrinsic latent domain priors that encode domain-specific variations. This omission leads to suboptimal generalization because the models lack a structured, probabilistic mechanism to explicitly model and compensate for domain shifts on unseen target domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251304/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, PDAF takes a single RGB image from an unseen target domain and produces a pixel-wise semantic segmentation map over 19 common urban scene categories. The method is plug-and-play: it wraps around a frozen pre-trained segmentation backbone (DeepLabV3Plus+ResNet-50 or Mask2Former+Swin-T/L), adding only a 4-channel latent domain prior (LDP) of spatial dimensions matching the feature map. During training, paired source and photometrically-augmented pseudo-target images are used to supervise the LDP estimation, but at test time only the target image is required.

## Method

PDAF formulates DGSS as a variational inference problem where a Latent Domain Prior (LDP) is a latent variable capturing domain-specific shifts. Three modules instantiate the ELBO: (1) the Latent Prior Extractor (LPE) acts as the variational posterior, concatenating source and pseudo-target features through residual blocks to predict mean and variance of the LDP via reparameterization; (2) the Domain Compensation Module (DCM) projects the LDP into affine scale-and-shift parameters (inspired by Spatial Feature Transformation) that modulate backbone features to compensate domain discrepancies; (3) the Diffusion Prior Estimator (DPE) implements the prior as a 4-step denoising diffusion process conditioned on target features, enabling LDP estimation without any source-target pairing at inference. Training jointly optimizes a task-specific segmentation loss, a semantic consistency loss between source and pseudo-target predictions, and a prior constraint loss aligning LPE and DPE outputs, all under a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU.

## Results

On the primary Cityscapes-source benchmark (Table 1), PDAF achieves 49.41 average mIoU with DeepLabV3Plus/ResNet-50, surpassing the best prior method BlindNet (47.13) by 2.28 points. With Mask2Former/Swin-L, PDAF reaches 61.08 average mIoU, outperforming CMFormer (59.98) by 1.10 points. On the GTAV-source setting (Table 2), PDAF attains 42.85 average mIoU versus BlindNet's 41.38. On the ACDC corruption benchmark (Table 3), PDAF with Swin-L achieves 70.10 average mIoU across fog, night, rain, and snow, exceeding HGFormer (65.80) by 4.30 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDAF consistently outperforms all 18 compared baselines on average mIoU across every backbone and source-domain setting. With ResNet-50 on Cityscapes source, PDAF (49.41) beats the strongest prior method BlindNet (47.13) by 2.28 mIoU, with the largest per-domain gains on BDD100K (+1.66) and SYNTHIA (+2.17). With Swin-L on Cityscapes source, PDAF (61.08) surpasses CMFormer (59.98) by 1.10, with the most notable improvement on GTA5 (+2.50). On the GTAV-to-real transfer (Table 2), PDAF (42.85) edges out BlindNet (41.38) by 1.47, gaining the most on Mapillary (+2.37) and BDD100K (+2.24). On the ACDC corruption benchmark (Table 3), PDAF with Swin-L (70.10) leads HGFormer (65.80) by 4.30, with the largest margin under foggy conditions (+10.82). The only per-domain loss is on Mapillary with Swin-T, where HGFormer scores 66.90 vs. PDAF's 66.40, though PDAF still wins on the overall average.

## Relation to Prior Work

PDAF carves new ground at the intersection of variational inference and diffusion modeling for domain generalization, departing from the dominant paradigm of feature alignment via normalization/whitening (IBN, SW, ISW, DPCL, BlindNet) and data augmentation (DRPC, GTR-LTR). Rather than treating domain shift as something to be removed from features, PDAF models it as a latent variable (LDP) estimated through a variational posterior and refined via a short diffusion denoising process, then uses it as a conditioning signal for Spatial Feature Transformation–style compensation. The diffusion component is repurposed from generative modeling (LDM, Wurstchen) and prior diffusion-for-DGSS work (DatasetDM, DGInStyle, DIFF) into a lightweight estimator of a compact latent prior, rather than a data synthesis tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The probabilistic framing offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc feature alignment, and the plug-and-play design (compatible with both CNN and Transformer backbones) makes it practically accessible. However, the domain shift is simulated solely through photometric augmentation, which limits the diversity of shifts the LDP actually captures; whether the 4-channel latent prior encodes meaningful domain structure or merely compensates for brightness/contrast changes remains an open question. The very small diffusion budget (T=4 steps) and LDP dimensionality (c'=4) suggest the learned prior is extremely low-capacity, raising questions about scalability to more complex or compositional domain shifts (e.g., novel sensor modalities, multi-domain mixtures).
