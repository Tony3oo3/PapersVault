## Problem

Existing depth completion methods overfit to a single dataset and sensor type, failing catastrophically on unseen sparse depth patterns or new domains. This forces downstream users (e.g., view synthesis, 3D reconstruction) to train custom DC models per domain, which is laborious and often infeasible when RGB-D data is scarce. The paper targets the most challenging setting: zero-shot generalization across diverse sparsity levels, sensor types, and scene categories with a single model.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an RGB image (3×H×W), a sparse depth observation map (H×W), and a binary valid mask (H×W) indicating which pixels carry reliable depth. The sparse depth may originate from LiDAR (4–128 lines), SfM/VIO keypoints, or synthetic patterns with up to 10% noise. The output is a dense depth map at the same spatial resolution as the input, produced by a DDI operating at 1/4 resolution followed by convex upsampling and a DySPN refinement stage. The model guarantees scale equivariance: scaling the input sparse depth by any positive factor β scales the output by the same factor.

## Method

OMNI-DC builds on OGNI-DC's Differentiable Depth Integrator (DDI) but introduces a Multi-resolution DDI that jointly solves a linear least-squares problem over depth gradient constraints at R=3 resolutions (each halved), reducing the integration error accumulation from O(n) to O(n/2^(R-1)) for pixels n steps from the nearest known depth. A probability-based Laplacian loss (predicting per-pixel mean and scale) is combined with L1 and gradient-matching losses to self-adaptively model uncertainty in ambiguous regions. To handle the vast depth-range variation across indoor (<1 m) and urban (>100 m) scenes, the model operates in log-depth space with median-based normalization of the sparse input, preserving scale equivariance through the DDI. Training uses 573K synthetic images from five datasets with diverse sparse patterns (SIFT/ORB keypoints, simulated LiDAR, outlier and boundary noise) rather than random sampling, and the CompletionFormer backbone with DySPN replaces OGNI-DC's iterative ConvGRU updates.

## Results

On ETH3D-SfM outdoor (real COLMAP points), OMNI-DC achieves RMSE=1.069, a 43% reduction over the second-best zero-shot method Marigold (1.883). On KITTI with 8-line LiDAR, it achieves zero-shot MAE=0.597, outperforming all methods trained on KITTI (best trained: 0.634 for Depth Prompting). On KITTI-64Line, it reduces RMSE to 1.191 vs. 1.612 for G2-MonoDepth (26% reduction). On NYUv2-500pt, it reaches RMSE=0.111, approaching the performance of OGNI-DC trained on NYU (0.089) while being fully zero-shot.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OMNI-DC outperforms all zero-shot baselines across every benchmark. Against the strongest zero-shot DC method G2-MonoDepth, it reduces RMSE by 57% on ETH3D-SfM-Out (1.069 vs 2.453), 26% on KITTI-64Line (1.191 vs 1.612), and 26% on KITTI-8Line (2.058 vs 2.769). Against the best generalizable monocular depth models (Marigold, DAv2, Depth Pro), the advantage is even larger: on ETH3D-SfM-Out, Marigold achieves 1.883 RMSE while OMNI-DC reaches 1.069, and DAv2's 2.663 is far worse. On KITTI-8Line, all monocular depth models (Depth Pro 4.893, DAv2 4.689, Marigold 3.498) are dramatically worse than OMNI-DC (2.058), confirming that sparse depth input is critical for outdoor scenes. Among DC methods trained on the target domain, OMNI-DC zero-shot on KITTI-8Line (MAE 0.597) beats the best trained method Depth Prompting (MAE 0.634), and on NYUv2-500pt it is within 25% of the best trained model OGNI-DC (RMSE 0.111 vs 0.089). On the VOID dataset with real VIO sparse depth, OMNI-DC also leads all baselines at 1500 and 500 points (RMSE 0.555/0.551) and is competitive at 150 points (0.650 vs DAv2's 0.644).

## Relation to Prior Work

OMNI-DC builds directly on OGNI-DC's Differentiable Depth Integrator (DDI), extending it from a single-resolution to a multi-resolution formulation to address error accumulation in long-range depth integration. It departs from the dominant paradigm of training DC models on a single dataset with a single sensor type (CompletionFormer, DFU, BP-Net) by targeting zero-shot generalization across datasets and sensor patterns, and positions itself against both DC-specific baselines and generalizable monocular depth models (Depth Anything v2, Marigold, Depth Pro) that cannot effectively exploit sparse depth inputs. The Laplacian loss and scale normalization technique are novel contributions tailored specifically to the DC setting, distinguishing the work from prior probabilistic losses in other vision tasks and from scale-invariant losses in monocular depth estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A single plug-and-play DC model that generalizes zero-shot across indoor, outdoor, and urban scenes with LiDAR, SfM, and noisy inputs removes a significant practical bottleneck for downstream tasks like 3D reconstruction, autonomous driving, and novel view synthesis, where users previously had to train custom DC models per domain. The demonstration that purely synthetic training data (573K images) yields competitive real-world performance is practically valuable given the scarcity of high-quality RGB-D ground truth. Open questions include whether the approach extends to even more exotic sensor modalities, whether mixing in real-world data (which the paper notes produces blurry results) can be resolved, and whether the multi-resolution DDI principle transfers to other integration-based architectures.
