## Problem

Existing score distillation methods for text-guided image editing (e.g., DDS, CDS) struggle with object insertion and require input-specific hyperparameter tuning because gradient magnitudes and spatial distributions vary drastically across noise samples, timesteps, and inputs. This makes it difficult to reliably generate new objects from scratch while preserving the background, as weak or scattered gradients can counteract progress or cause the image to revert to the input.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250872/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image, a source global text description (auto-generated via BLIP or ChatGPT if not provided), and a target global text description, and produces a single edited RGB image. Optimization is performed entirely in the VAE latent space of Stable Diffusion (300 SGD steps at learning rate 2000, timesteps sampled uniformly from [50, 950], CFG scale 0), after which the optimized latent is decoded back to pixel space. A single fixed hyperparameter configuration is used for all inputs, eliminating the need for per-image tuning.

## Method

LUSD builds on the SBP loss (which computes the gradient difference between target- and source-conditioned noise predictions on the same noisy latent) and adds two mechanisms to stabilize optimization. First, an attention-based spatial regularization computes an editing mask via self-attention exponentiation (multiplying self-attention maps by cross-attention maps for edit-relevant noun tokens), takes a moving average across steps, and uses it to modulate the SBP gradient so that updates concentrate on the edit region while an L2 term (λ=0.02) anchors the background to the source. Second, a gradient filtering-and-normalization step discards 'bad' gradients whose pixel-value standard deviation falls below a decaying threshold (resampling noise until a good gradient is found) and then normalizes the combined gradient by its own standard deviation, scaled by an annealing coefficient γ following a reverse-sigmoid schedule from 0.15 to 0.01. Together, these components reduce the sensitivity to noise-seed and input variation that plagues prior score distillation approaches.

## Results

On the MagicBrush benchmark (1,053 examples), LUSD achieves the best prompt fidelity (CLIP-T = 0.287) and the best background-preservation trade-off (CLIP-AUC = 0.074), while also attaining the lowest L1* (0.015, lower is better). In a 1-on-1 human study with 200 samples, users prefer LUSD over the strongest competitor (CDS) by 57.7% overall, 61.7% on background preservation, and 59.8% on prompt fidelity. On object-insertion subsets specifically, LUSD's CLIP-AUC advantage over DDS widens from 35% (all edits) to 47% (add-only edits), and over CDS from 54% to 64%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LUSD outperforms all six baselines on CLIP-T (prompt fidelity) and CLIP-AUC (success-rate trade-off), with the largest margins over the score-distillation baselines DDS (CLIP-AUC +54%) and CDS (+118%). Against the closest score-distillation competitor SBP [26], LUSD gains only 0.002 in CLIP-T but 0.006 in CLIP-AUC, indicating the spatial regularization and gradient filtering primarily improve edit success rather than raw text alignment. LUSD is the only method to simultaneously achieve the best CLIP-T, CLIP-AUC, and L1* (background preservation), whereas CDS and DDS, despite strong CLIP-I* scores (0.197 and 0.195 vs. LUSD's 0.192), suffer large drops on object-insertion subsets (CLIP-AUC −47% and −64% respectively). In the human study, LUSD is preferred over CDS by 57.7% overall and over IP2P by 64%, confirming that the quantitative gains translate to perceptual quality. The main trade-off is runtime: LUSD takes 1.79 min per edit (due to gradient filtering and 300 optimization steps) versus 0.06–0.62 min for the baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

LUSD builds directly on the SBP loss formulation from McAllister et al. (2024), which itself refines the original SDS framework (Poole et al., 2022) for 2D image editing. It departs from the DDS/CDS line of work by diagnosing gradient magnitude and spatial distribution variation as the core bottleneck for object insertion, then addressing it with two targeted modifications: an attention-based spatial mask (borrowed from self-attention exponentiation in Nguyen et al., 2023) to regularize where updates occur, and a gradient filtering-normalization step to discard counterproductive updates. Unlike inversion-based methods (LEDITS++, DiffEdit) or supervised instruction-following models (InstructPix2Pix, HIVE), LUSD remains in the optimization-based score distillation family, making it complementary rather than a replacement for those paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete, previously under-appreciated failure mode in score distillation for editing—gradient scatter and magnitude inconsistency—and shows that two lightweight, plug-and-play modifications (spatial masking and gradient filtering) can substantially improve object insertion without per-image hyperparameter tuning. This is practically important because prior SDS-based editors (DDS, CDS) required careful tuning per input, limiting their usability. Limitations include slower runtime due to gradient rejection, dependence on the diffusion model's language understanding, and a tendency to favor regions with existing visual cues. Open questions include whether these techniques transfer to rectified-flow or DiT-based architectures and whether the approach scales to more complex multi-object or compositional edits.
