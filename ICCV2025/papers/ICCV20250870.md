## Problem

RGB imaging captures high-fidelity surface appearance but no internal structure, while X-ray imaging reveals subsurface density but lacks surface texture. Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting methods handle these modalities in isolation, and no unified 3D representation simultaneously encodes both external appearance and internal structure. The fundamental challenges are the geometric domain gap between SfM-based RGB point clouds and scanner-parameter-based X-ray point clouds, the absence of paired RGB-X-ray datasets, and the need to align fundamentally different information types within a single model.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired RGB images (90–644 per object, 3024×1848 to 5712×4284 px) and cone-beam CT X-ray projections (360–2160 views, 2048 detector elements, 185–330 kV). Cross-sectional X-ray slices at 15 mm intervals along axial and coronal planes serve as pseudo-ground-truth for internal supervision. The output is a single unified 3D Gaussian splat set where each splat carries both RGB color and radiative feature attributes, optionally convertible to a 3D mesh with visible internal layers via Marching Cubes.

## Method

InsideOut trains RGB Gaussian splats (standard 3DGS) and radiative Gaussian splats (X-Gaussian) independently, then aligns them through a three-stage hierarchical fitting: global ICP on aggregate mean/covariance, local ICP within K-means clusters at progressively finer resolutions (N/10, N/2, N), and per-splat fine alignment. Internal rendering sharpens multi-layer boundaries using cross-sectional X-ray slices as pseudo-GT: Canny edges guide RGB splat surface placement, while a novel X-ray reference loss (weighted L1 + D-SSIM + zero-one opacity regularization) enforces thin planar layer structures in the radiative splats. Finally, visual alignment transfers color via k-NN matching and clones high-frequency RGB splats (bottom 95% by largest covariance eigenvalue) into the radiative set, yielding a unified model with both surface detail and internal structure.

## Results

On the InsideOut RGB-X-ray dataset (5 objects), InsideOut achieves the lowest PIQE (perceptual quality) and highest PSNR/SSIM across all objects in cross-sectional internal rendering. On average, InsideOut improves PSNR by 1.67 dB over X-Gaussian and 3.33 dB over 3DGS, while SSIM increases by 0.018 and 0.042 respectively. The largest gains appear on the Skull (PSNR 20.33 vs. 19.42 for X-Gaussian) and Toy Gun (PSNR 20.87 vs. 19.53), where internal layer structure is most complex. In mesh reconstruction, InsideOut achieves the highest spherical occupancy at 42 bins (0.443 mean) and captures internal layer normals that neither R²-Gaussian nor GS2Mesh can represent.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InsideOut outperforms all eight RGB/X-ray rendering baselines (3DGS, 3DGS-MCMC, DoF-Gaussian, MaskGaussian, Analytic-Splatting, RS-NeRF, NAF, X-Gaussian) on every metric (PIQE, PSNR, SSIM) across all five objects. The closest competitor is X-Gaussian [5], the X-ray-only radiative splatting method; InsideOut improves PSNR by 1.67 dB on average (e.g., 20.33 vs. 19.42 on Skull) and reduces PIQE by ~4–5 points, indicating substantially sharper internal layer boundaries. Among RGB-based 3DGS variants, NAF [58] is the strongest baseline (PSNR 17.03–18.64), yet InsideOut still leads by 1.8–2.2 dB, confirming that cross-modal fusion provides gains beyond any single-modality approach. In mesh reconstruction, InsideOut surpasses R²-Gaussian [59] in spherical occupancy at 42 bins (0.443 vs. 0.431 mean) and shows a more diverse normal orientation distribution, while GS2Mesh [50] slightly edges ahead at 242 bins (0.476 vs. 0.471 mean) but cannot capture internal layer normals that InsideOut represents. The ablation (Table 3) confirms that hierarchical fitting reduces chamfer distance by ~70–90% over the unaligned baseline, and internal rendering provides a further 20–30% reduction, validating both modules' contributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and its X-ray variant (X-Gaussian) by unifying both modalities into a single 3D representation, whereas prior work treated RGB and X-ray 3DGS as entirely separate pipelines. The hierarchical fitting strategy (global ICP → multi-resolution K-means clustering → fine alignment) is a novel geometric alignment procedure not present in either 3DGS or X-Gaussian, and the X-ray reference loss with a zero-one opacity constraint introduces a new training signal for internal layer sharpening. The paper also departs from prior multi-modal fusion literature, which focused on 2D image-level alignment (wavelet transforms, camera calibration), by performing full 3D geometric and visual integration within a splat-based representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InsideOut is the first framework to produce a single 3D model that simultaneously preserves high-fidelity RGB surface texture and X-ray internal structure, enabling cross-sectional analysis, physical simulation, and non-destructive testing in medical, cultural heritage, and manufacturing domains. The released paired RGB–X-ray dataset (five objects with cone-beam CT) addresses a critical data gap, though its small scale limits generalizability claims. Key limitations include lower surface texture quality than dedicated RGB mesh methods (GS2Mesh) due to voxelization, the absence of ground-truth for joint internal-external evaluation (relying on no-reference PIQE), and evaluation on only five objects. Open questions include scalability to larger or more complex objects, real-time rendering of the unified model, and extension to additional modalities (e.g., MRI, ultrasound).
