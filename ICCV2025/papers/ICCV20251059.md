## Problem

Existing optimization-free stylized image generation methods empirically select which attention features to modify without understanding their relationship to style, leading to suboptimal results or content leakage. The paper addresses two open questions: which features in attention blocks are actually sensitive to style, and how to transform those features to match a reference style without introducing content from the style image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251059/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference style image (any RGB image) and a content-only text prompt (no style description required) as input, and produces a single stylized image that matches the reference style while following the prompt. It operates entirely within the latent space of a pretrained diffusion model (SDXL or SD3) during the DDIM denoising loop with 50 sampling steps, requiring no additional training or test-time optimization. The only tunable hyperparameter is a channel-selection threshold τ (default 0.2) that controls the trade-off between style fidelity and content preservation.

## Method

WSDT first uses the 2-Wasserstein distance between feature distributions conditioned on different styles versus different content categories to systematically identify style-sensitive features, finding that input (F), value (V), and output (O) features in self-attention blocks are significantly more style-sensitive than any features in cross-attention blocks. During denoising, it extracts these features from both the style image's forward pass and the target generation, then applies a channel-adaptive distribution transform: for each channel, it computes a per-channel Wasserstein distance and only transforms channels below a threshold τ, replacing their mean and variance with those of the style features (a generalization of AdaIN with selective channel application). Channels above the threshold are left unchanged to prevent content leakage from the style image. The entire procedure is training-free and optimization-free, requiring only a single forward pass per timestep for the style image alongside the standard denoising step.

## Results

On the combined StyleDrop and InstaStyle evaluation datasets, WSDT achieves the best score across all four metrics: Content Score 0.285, Style Score 0.546, GM Metric 5.52 (lower is better), and User Preference 0.494. The most substantial margin is on the GM Metric, where WSDT (5.52) nearly halves the next-best StyleAligned (10.53), and on User Preference, where WSDT (0.494) more than doubles the runner-up VSP (0.246). The method also demonstrates strong generalizability, producing high-quality stylized images when integrated into the transformer-based SD3 architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WSDT outperforms all six baselines on every metric. Against the closest competitors, StyleAligned [13] (Style Score 0.538, GM 10.53) and VSP [17] (Content 0.282, Style 0.525, GM 10.87), WSDT leads by a clear margin on GM Metric (5.52 vs. 10.53/10.87) and User Preference (0.494 vs. 0.080/0.246), while matching or slightly exceeding their content and style scores. Optimization-based methods StyleDrop [37], DEADiff [26], and CSGO [45] trail significantly on all metrics, with GM Metrics of 19.63, 22.08, and 12.03 respectively, and user preference scores below 0.05. InstaStyle [5] achieves competitive style scores (0.529) but suffers from content leakage, reflected in its higher GM Metric (11.73) and lower user preference (0.088). On the SD3 transformer-based model, WSDT also produces superior image quality compared to StyleAligned and VSP, which exhibit artifacts from direct feature swapping.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the line of optimization-free stylized image generation methods (VSP, StyleAligned, StyleID, Z*) that empirically replace or re-weight features in self- or cross-attention blocks of diffusion models. It departs from this empirical tradition by providing a principled, Wasserstein-distance-based analysis that identifies which specific features (input F, value V, output O in self-attention) are style-sensitive, rather than relying on trial-and-error feature selection. The proposed channel-adaptive distribution transform connects to AdaIN's mean-and-variance alignment when the covariance is diagonal, but extends it with per-channel Wasserstein distance to selectively transform only style-relevant channels, thereby mitigating content leakage that plagues prior full-replacement approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic feature-sensitivity analysis offers a reusable diagnostic framework for understanding where style information resides in attention-based diffusion models, potentially guiding future stylization and editing methods. The method's simplicity (no training, no optimization at test time, plug-and-play into SDXL/SD3) makes it practically attractive for real-world deployment. However, the fixed threshold τ is a notable limitation—different styles may require different thresholds, and content leakage is only partially mitigated. Open questions include whether adaptive, style-aware thresholding can close this gap, and whether the self-attention-dominance finding generalizes to next-generation architectures beyond UNet-based and early transformer-based diffusion models.
