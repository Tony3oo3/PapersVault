## Problem

Skeleton-based action recognition in real-world settings faces simultaneous class imbalance and partial labeling, yet existing methods address these issues in isolation without understanding their interplay. The paper reveals two previously unexplored theoretical connections: class imbalance systematically shifts the eigenvalue spectrum of normalized affinity matrices (impairing label propagation convergence and accuracy), and conventional softmax-based reliability metrics mistakenly discard boundary samples that are critical for learning under imbalanced conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252465/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D skeleton sequences (for SAR tasks) or raw time-series signals (for TSC tasks like EEG), processing each through parallel time-domain and frequency-domain (Fourier-transformed) branches. It operates under semi-supervised conditions with only 10–50% labeled samples and class-imbalance ratios up to 100:1, producing a single action class label from C classes. At inference, predictions are averaged across both domain branches in an inductive manner, while training uses transductive pseudo-labels generated iteratively.

## Method

SpeLER introduces two coupled mechanisms within an iterative semi-supervised loop. First, spectral-balanced label propagation (SpeL) reweights affinity matrix edges by (p_c(x_i)·p_c(x_j))^(−1/2), where p_c is the class proportion, theoretically shown via eigenvalue derivative analysis to amplify minority-class influence on the spectrum while suppressing majority-class dominance, thereby counteracting imbalance-induced spectral shifts. Second, a propagation energy-based tightened reliability measure E_p(x_i) = −log Σ_j exp(M_ij/p_j) evaluates holistic graph connectivity rather than pairwise class margins, preserving boundary samples that softmax confidence would discard as low-confidence noise. The backbone is a cross-domain time-frequency network (CTR-GCN for skeletons, FCN for TSC) with multi-level attention fusion (shallow, middle, and cross-domain), trained with cross-entropy plus intra- and cross-domain contrastive losses, where pseudo-labeled samples are weighted by their normalized propagation energy.

## Results

On NTU-60 X-sub with 25% labeled data and imbalance ratio π=60, SpeLER achieves 65.02% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the best baseline SCD-Net (64.32%) by 0.70 points. On the more challenging NTU-120 X-sub under the same conditions, SpeLER reaches 62.98% versus SCD-Net's 61.05% (+1.93). On Kinetics-Skeleton (π=60), SpeLER attains 30.01% versus 28.29% for SCD-Net. On classical time-series benchmarks with only 10% labeled data, SpeLER-FCN achieves 96.56% on HAR, 84.31% on SleepEDF, and 98.80% on Epilepsy, all setting new state-of-the-art results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpeLER outperforms all 15 baselines across every dataset, protocol, and imbalance ratio tested. The closest competitor is SCD-Net (self-supervised spatiotemporal disentanglement), which SpeLER beats by 0.70–1.93 points depending on the setting, with the largest gap on NTU-120 X-sub (62.98 vs. 61.05 at π=60). Among label-propagation-based methods, protoLP (prototype-based LP) is the strongest baseline, yet SpeLER still leads by 0.88–1.64 points, confirming that spectral balancing and energy-based reliability provide gains beyond prototype matching. FreqMixFormer, despite incorporating time-frequency modeling, degrades noticeably under high imbalance (e.g., 53.85% vs. SpeLER's 62.98% on NTU-120 X-sub, π=60), indicating that frequency features alone are insufficient without explicit imbalance correction. On the time-series benchmarks (Table 3, γ=10%), SpeLER-FCN surpasses TS-CoT by 2.29 points on HAR (96.56 vs. 94.27) and SimMTM by 1.15 points on Epilepsy (98.80 vs. 97.65), demonstrating that the spectral-balanced energy propagation framework generalizes beyond skeleton data to classical 1-D signal classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Label Propagation framework (Iscen et al., CVPR 2019) by introducing a spectral-balanced affinity matrix that explicitly counteracts eigenvalue distortions caused by class imbalance, grounded in Bauer-Fike spectral perturbation theory. It departs from prior class-imbalanced SAR methods (BRL, Shap-Mix, protoLP) that address imbalance at the data or loss level, and from partial-label learning methods (FixMatch, MAC) that rely on softmax-based confidence, by unifying both concerns through a single propagation mechanism. The cross-domain time-frequency architecture builds on FreqMixFormer's frequency modeling but replaces simple concatenation with multi-level attention-based fusion, and the energy-based reliability draws from energy-based OOD detection (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2020) adapted to graph propagation contexts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical demonstration that class imbalance systematically shifts eigenvalue spectra of affinity matrices—and that this directly degrades both LP convergence and solution accuracy—provides a principled explanation for why naive label propagation fails under imbalance, a gap the authors identify as 'theoretically underexplored.' The propagation energy metric offers a concrete alternative to softmax confidence that preserves boundary samples critical for minority classes, addressing a negative feedback loop in iterative pseudo-labeling. Limitations include the assumption of known class proportions for spectral balancing, the added complexity of the iterative semi-supervised training loop, and the fact that gains (1–3% over strongest baselines) are moderate; the authors themselves note that spectral properties may behave differently in fully-labeled regimes, leaving the method's applicability to abundant-label settings open.
