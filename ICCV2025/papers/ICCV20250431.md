## Problem

Diffusion Transformers (DiT) suffer from Dynamic Feature Instability: the absence of long-range feature preservation mechanisms causes uncontrolled spectral norm growth in their Jacobians, leading to error amplification during cached inference, slow training convergence, and high sensitivity to perturbations. While U-Net-based diffusion models leverage Long-Skip-Connections (LSCs) for both training stability and reliable feature caching, DiT architectures lack this component, and prior caching methods for DiT require adaptive per-sample scheduling and still produce visible artifacts at larger cache intervals.

## Input / Output

The model accepts class labels (ImageNet 256×256), text prompts (MS-COCO 1024×1024, VBench video prompts), or pure Gaussian noise as conditioning, and iteratively denoises over T timesteps (1000 for video, 50 DDIM steps for UCF101) to produce 16-frame videos at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution, or single images. During cached inference, only the shallow DiT blocks are computed at each step while deep block features are reused from a static cache, with the caching schedule determined by a pre-computed error record and a dynamically identified phase boundary (e.g., timesteps 700→50 out of 1000).

## Method

Skip-DiT inserts Long-Skip-Connections between the i-th and (L+1)−i-th DiT blocks, where each skip branch concatenates the shallow block output with the current deep block input, applies LayerNorm, and projects via a fully-connected layer before feeding into the next block. A spectral norm analysis proves that this architecture bounds the per-layer Jacobian spectral norm strictly below that of vanilla DiT (σ_max(J_skip) < σ_max(J_vanilla)), yielding tighter perturbation bounds, faster convergence (4.4× training acceleration), and provably larger allowable feature-reuse intervals. For inference, a static caching mechanism reuses deep block features across timesteps while recomputing only shallow blocks, guided by a pre-computed error record and a dynamically identified phase boundary that separates rapidly-changing early timesteps from stable later ones. The architecture is compatible with pre-trained DiT models via a two-stage continual training strategy: first training only the LSC parameters (1 day on 8×H100), then unfreezing all parameters for full fine-tuning.

## Results

Skip-DiT achieves up to 4.4× training acceleration (reaching FID-50K of 2.29 at 1.6M steps vs. DiT-XL's 7M steps) and 1.5–2.2× inference speedup with minimal quality degradation. On text-to-video (Latte backbone), Skip-DiT with 75% steps cached at n=4 achieves 2.20× speedup while maintaining VBench 74.43 (vs. 76.14 original), PSNR 22.08, LPIPS 0.22, and SSIM 0.73. On text-to-image (Hunyuan-DiT), the caching mechanism reaches 2.09× speedup with FID 27.21 (improving over the 32.64 baseline) and CLIP 30.71. On class-to-image (DiT-XL), Skip-DiT at n=4 achieves 1.93× speedup with FID 2.54 (vs. 2.30 original).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Skip-DiT consistently outperforms all DiT-specific caching baselines (T-GATE, FORA, Δ-DiT, PAB, AdaCache, TeaCache) on reconstruction fidelity metrics (PSNR, LPIPS, SSIM) at equal or higher speedup. For example, at ~1.7× speedup, Skip-DiT (n=3, 65% cached) achieves PSNR 27.46 / LPIPS 0.09 / SSIM 0.85, versus TeaCache's 23.05 / 0.17 / 0.80 and AdaCache's 22.23 / 0.19 / 0.78. The PAB variants suffer the largest quality degradation (VBench drops of 2.4–5.9%) and are clearly dominated by Skip-DiT on all metrics. On VBench (overall generation quality), Skip-DiT incurs a small drop (0.09–1.17%) relative to its own uncached baseline, comparable to the drops of FORA (0.08%) and Δ-DiT (0.08%), while delivering 1.5–2.2× speedup versus their 1.19–1.27×. Against the original Latte baseline, Skip-DiT matches VBench within 0.54% after only 6 days of continual training, confirming architectural compatibility. On class-to-video (Table 1), Skip-DiT with n=6 achieves 2.43× speedup with only +2.37 average FVD and +0.27 FID loss, outperforming all baselines which show larger degradations at lower speedups.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two established lines of work: U-Net diffusion models that leverage Long-Skip-Connections (LSCs) for training stability and efficient caching (DeepCache, Faster Diffusion), and Diffusion Transformers (DiT, Latte, Hunyuan-DiT) that dropped these connections in favor of pure transformer stacks. While Hunyuan-DiT and U-DiT have already incorporated LSCs into DiT architectures, this paper provides the first systematic theoretical analysis (via spectral norm bounds) explaining *why* LSCs stabilize DiT, formalizing the problem as 'Dynamic Feature Instability.' It also departs from prior DiT caching methods (FORA, PAB, AdaCache, TeaCache, Δ-DiT) that require adaptive per-sample scheduling by showing that LSC-stabilized features enable a simpler, fully static caching strategy with comparable or better fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 4.4× training speedup and 1.5–2× inference speedup with minimal quality loss across image and video generation tasks, achieved through a minimal architectural modification (adding skip connections) that can be adopted via short continual training on existing pretrained DiT models. The spectral norm analysis, while relying on idealized assumptions (identical blocks, uniform γ < 1), provides a useful theoretical lens for understanding why vanilla DiT converges slowly and why caching is fragile without LSCs. Open questions include whether the spectral guarantees hold for non-uniform or heterogeneous block designs, how the approach scales to much deeper DiT variants (e.g., 100+ layers), and whether the static caching strategy remains optimal as model scale and task complexity grow.
