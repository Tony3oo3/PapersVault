## Problem

AI-Generated Talking Heads (AGTHs) produced by speech-driven 'talker' methods suffer from diverse quality issues, yet no comprehensive dataset or effective objective quality assessment metric exists for this domain. The prior THQA dataset contained only 800 samples generated exclusively with StyleGAN, and no reliable no-reference quality score was available to benchmark the 14+ talkers now in use.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250004/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single AGTH video with its accompanying audio track. The video is decomposed into four channels: the first frame (spatial quality), a Y-T slice projected through the mouth centroid (temporal dynamics), and two SyncNet-derived lip-sync maps (LSE-C confidence and LSE-D distance) expanded to match the first frame's spatial dimensions. Input resolutions vary from 256×256 to 1024×1024 depending on the talker, with the Y-T slice resized to the first frame resolution before fusion. The output is a scalar predicted MOS score regressed via MSE against human ratings.

## Method

FSCD constructs a four-channel composite image from the first frame, a Y-T slice, and two tone-lip consistency maps, then feeds it into a Swin-T backbone for quality regression. The Y-T slice is generated by projecting the video along the vertical axis at the mouth centroid (located via landmark detection on the T2I reference image and scaled to the video resolution), capturing temporal mouth dynamics in a single 2D representation. Rather than concatenating LSE-C and LSE-D as scalar features into a regression head, the paper expands each into a spatial tensor matching the first frame size, allowing the transformer to learn their relative importance end-to-end. Training uses MSE loss against MOS labels with five-fold cross-validation, and ablation shows the first frame contributes most, followed by the Y-T slice, LSE-D, and LSE-C.

## Results

FSCD achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three datasets: on THQA-10K it reaches SRCC 0.8066 / PLCC 0.8322 / KRCC 0.6228 / RMSE 0.6333, on THQA it reaches SRCC 0.7812 / PLCC 0.8409 / KRCC 0.5951 / RMSE 0.5055, and on THQA-3D it reaches SRCC 0.8235 / PLCC 0.8505 / KRCC 0.6463 / RMSE 0.4577. In every case FSCD surpasses the next-best method by at least +2% in SRCC. The ablation confirms that the first frame contributes the most, followed by the Y-T slice, LSE-D, and LSE-C, with the full FSCD outperforming any single-component variant by 1.4–15.6% in SRCC depending on the dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FSCD outperforms all 15 baselines on every dataset and every metric. On THQA-10K, the closest competitor is SimpVQA (SRCC 0.7775), which FSCD beats by +2.9% in SRCC and by 0.05 in RMSE (0.6333 vs 0.6832); VSFA and FAST-VQA trail further behind at 0.7537 and 0.7351 SRCC respectively. On THQA, VSFA is the strongest baseline (SRCC 0.7601), with FSCD ahead by +2.1% SRCC and a notably lower RMSE (0.5055 vs 0.5966). On THQA-3D, BVQA leads the baselines (SRCC 0.7871), and FSCD extends its lead to +3.6% SRCC while reducing RMSE from 0.5983 to 0.4577. The traditional IQA methods (BRISQUE, NIQE, CPBD, IL-NIQE) and standalone lip-sync metrics (LSE-C, LSE-D) perform very poorly as quality predictors, with SRCC values mostly below 0.15, confirming that neither spatial-only image quality nor audio-visual sync alone captures AGTH quality. Among VQA baselines, the deep-learning methods (SimpVQA, VSFA, FAST-VQA, BVQA) are clearly stronger than hand-crafted ones (VIIDEO, TLVQM, VIDEVAL, V-BLIINDS, RAPIQUE), but none match FSCD's multimodal fusion of first-frame spatial features, Y-T temporal slice, and tone-lip consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the THQA dataset [72] (800 AGTHs, StyleGAN-only portraits) into THQA-10K by incorporating 12 diverse T2I models and 14 talkers, addressing the prior dataset's narrow generative coverage. Methodologically, FSCD repurposes the Y-T slice concept from action recognition [38] for temporal feature extraction in talking-head videos and integrates SyncNet [8] outputs (LSE-C, LSE-D) as spatially-expanded channels rather than scalar features, allowing the Swin-T backbone to learn their relative importance. The paper departs from prior digital-human QA work (DHH-QA, DDHQA, THQA-3D) which focused on 3D mesh distortions, by targeting the 2D video+audio modality of AI-generated talking heads where no reference exists.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

THQA-10K is the largest AGTH quality assessment benchmark to date and will likely become the standard reference for evaluating new talkers, filling a gap where PSNR/SSIM were inappropriately applied due to the absence of ground-truth references. The finding that T2I model choice and prompt diversity have minimal impact on AGTH quality—while talker selection dominates—redirects optimization efforts toward the animation stage rather than portrait generation. Open questions include whether the 10-distortion taxonomy generalizes to future talker architectures, whether FSCD transfers to higher-resolution or longer-duration AGTHs, and whether the limited 14-prompt/70-speech driving set captures the full diversity of real-world deployment scenarios.
