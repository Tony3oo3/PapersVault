## Problem

Audio-Visual Speech Recognition (AVSR) has been largely confined to English or at most nine languages because obtaining labeled audio-visual data with transcriptions for diverse languages remains extremely challenging. This paper addresses the gap of zero-shot language expansion: enabling AVSR in target languages for which no audio-visual speech training data is available at all.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250828/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes 16 kHz audio (converted to log-filterbank features) and 25 fps video of the speaker's mouth (cropped to 96×96 via ReinaFace landmarks, with random 88×88 cropping and horizontal flipping during training) as joint input. The AV-Romanizer first produces a language-agnostic Roman text transcription via CTC decoding; this Roman text is then converted into the target language's native graphemes by an LLM. In the unified Zero-AVSR variant, the audio-visual features bypass the explicit Roman text stage and are fed directly into the LLM through a length compressor and adapter, with the LLM outputting the final grapheme transcription.

## Method

The core architecture is an AV-Romanizer built on the AV-HuBERT backbone (ResNet-18 visual encoder, linear audio encoder, 24-layer transformer with 1024-dim model size) trained with CTC loss to predict Roman text, establishing a fixed, language-agnostic character set for pronunciation. Two zero-shot frameworks are explored: (1) Cascaded Zero-AVSR, where the AV-Romanizer's Roman output is de-romanized by a frozen LLM (e.g., GPT-4o-mini) via a text instruction, requiring no fine-tuning; and (2) Zero-AVSR, which integrates the AV-Romanizer's penultimate-layer features into Llama3.2-3B through a 1D-convolution length compressor (halving sequence length) and a linear adapter, then fine-tunes the LLM with QLoRA under a two-task scheme. Task 1 aligns audio-visual representations to the LLM's text embedding space using seen-language paired data; Task 2 trains the LLM on text-only Roman-to-grapheme conversion across both seen and unseen languages to preserve multilingual de-romanization ability. The approach is trained on the newly introduced MARC corpus (2,916 hours, 82 languages) and leverages the LLM's pre-existing knowledge of Roman-to-native-script mappings as the key mechanism enabling zero-shot generalization to unseen languages.

## Results

On MuAViC, Zero-AVSR achieves an average WER of 25.2% (excluding English) across 9 target languages, with state-of-the-art results in English (1.5%) and German (27.8%). Cascaded Zero-AVSR achieves 30.2% average WER without any LLM fine-tuning. Both methods support 82+ languages, compared to only 9 for existing multilingual AVSR models. The zero-shot evaluation (Table 3) shows that even when a language is entirely excluded from training, Zero-AVSR achieves CERs as low as 19.7% (Spanish) and 6.8–8.6% on seen languages.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Zero-AVSR (25.2% avg WER) outperforms the only other zero-shot approach, MMS Zero-shot [46] (38.9%), by 13.7 points across all 9 languages, with particularly large gains in French (14.3% vs 33.6%) and Portuguese (15.4% vs 35.5%). Against the best 9-language non-zero-shot model, XLAVS-R 2B [28] (23.6%), Zero-AVSR is slightly worse on average (25.2%) but achieves state-of-the-art English WER (1.5% vs 1.7%) and substantially better German (27.8% vs 44.4%), while supporting 82+ languages instead of 9. Zero-AVSR also beats XLAVS-R 300M (26.3%) and both AV-HuBERT/u-HuBERT (35.0%) on average. The key trade-off is that Zero-AVSR lags XLAVS-R 2B on Greek (38.4% vs 19.0%) and Russian (32.6% vs 25.0%), likely because XLAVS-R 2B was explicitly trained on those languages with 436K hours of audio-only pre-training data, whereas Zero-AVSR relies on the LLM's multilingual knowledge for de-romanization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the AV-HuBERT architecture for audio-visual speech encoding and extends the zero-shot ASR paradigm of MMS (which uses romanized text as a language-agnostic intermediate) to the audio-visual setting. The key departure from prior multilingual AVSR work (XLAVS-R, AV-HuBERT) is the elimination of the requirement for target-language training data, and the departure from prior zero-shot ASR is the use of pre-trained LLMs as de-romanizers rather than language-specific language models, plus the integration of visual speech cues. The cascaded design (AV-Romanizer + off-the-shelf LLM) is a novel combination that leverages LLMs' existing multilingual Roman-to-grapheme knowledge without any fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to demonstrate zero-shot AVSR, opening a path toward language expansion without the prohibitive cost of collecting labeled audio-visual data for each new language. The MARC dataset (82 languages, 2,916 hours) is a substantial resource that addresses the data bottleneck limiting prior multilingual AVSR to nine languages. However, zero-shot CERs on unseen languages remain high (40–76%), and the approach's effectiveness is heavily dependent on LLM quality for de-romanization; open questions include whether the gap between seen and unseen languages can be closed with stronger LLMs, and how the framework performs on truly low-resource or non-Romance languages beyond the eight evaluated.
