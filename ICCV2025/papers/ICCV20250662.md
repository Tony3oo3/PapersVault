## Problem

Existing multi-view video generation methods rely on attention mechanisms or single-level noise decomposition to enforce consistency, but they neglect global spatiotemporal information and fail to model the distinct motion properties of foreground objects versus background environments across views. This results in poor cross-view and cross-frame consistency, limited controllability, and reduced diversity in generated videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250662/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method conditions on 3D object bounding boxes, camera poses, and road BEV maps (inherited from the MagicDrive framework) to produce synchronized 6-view, 16-frame driving videos at 224×400 resolution (extendable to 512×1024). The 3D object boxes are projected into per-view binary masks that partition each frame into background and foreground regions, which are then used throughout the noise decomposition and joint denoising pipeline to apply spatially selective operations.

## Method

NoiseController introduces a three-stage noise control framework. First, Multi-Level Noise Decomposition splits each initial noise into scene-level background and foreground components (via element-wise masking with binary masks derived from 3D boxes), then further decomposes each into individual-level shared and residual components with distinct variance parameters (η for background, λ for foreground) to balance consistency and diversity. Second, Multi-Frame Noise Collaboration uses a learned inter-view spatiotemporal collaboration matrix S (2×6×6 per frame) over a sliding window of K preceding frames to capture global cross-view cross-frame effects, combined with an intra-view impact collaboration matrix I (2×2×6) to model local background-foreground interactions within each view. Third, Joint Denoising employs two parallel U-Nets (U-NetB and U-NetF) that separately predict background and foreground noise, with predictions masked by the scene-level masks before summation. The entire framework is trained end-to-end with a composite L1+L2 loss on the collaboration coefficients and denoised noise residuals, fine-tuning only newly added parameters on top of MagicDrive's pre-trained weights.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set at 224×400 resolution, NoiseController achieves FVD 122.9 and FID 14.65, outperforming the MagicDrive(video) baseline (FVD 177.3, FID 20.92) by 30.7% and 30.0% respectively. At higher resolution (512×1024), NoiseController* further improves to FVD 87.23 and FID 13.72, surpassing all compared methods including Panacea+ (FVD 103, FID 15.5) and MagicDriveDiT (FVD 94.84). For downstream tasks, generated data from NoiseController yields mAP 36.07 and NDS 43.54 for 3D detection (BEVFusion), and Vehicle IoU 40.97 / Road IoU 80.13 for BEV segmentation (CVT), setting new state-of-the-art among data augmentation approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At the same 224×400 resolution, NoiseController (FVD 122.9, FID 14.65) clearly outperforms MagicDrive(video) (FVD 177.3, FID 20.92), Panacea (FVD 139.0, FID 16.96), SubjectDrive (FVD 124, FID 15.96), DriveDreamer (FVD 340.8, FID 14.90), and WoVoGen (FVD 417.7, FID 27.60) on both metrics. At 512×1024 resolution, NoiseController* (FVD 87.23, FID 13.72) beats Panacea+ (FVD 103, FID 15.5) by 15.3% in FVD and 11.3% in FID, and also surpasses MagicDriveDiT (FVD 94.84, no FID reported) on FVD despite using a lower resolution (512×1024 vs. 848×1600). For downstream data augmentation, NoiseController-generated data achieves the best NDS (43.54) and mAP (36.07) for detection, and the best Vehicle IoU (40.97) and Road IoU (80.13) for segmentation, outperforming DriveDreamer (NDS 39.50), DrivingDiffusion (NDS 43.40), MagicDrive(img), and BEVGen. The method also integrates effectively onto TrackDiffusion, reducing FVD from 605.0 to 590.2 and FID from 41.81 to 36.27 on YoutubeVIS, confirming generality beyond the MagicDrive backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the noise-decomposition line of work (VideoFusion, Ge et al., Zhang et al.) from single-level shared/residual splits to a two-level hierarchy: scene-level background/foreground separation followed by individual-level shared/residual decomposition. It builds directly on MagicDrive's pre-trained latent diffusion backbone and departs from the dominant attention-based consistency paradigm (Panacea's 4D attention, cross-view/cross-frame attention) by instead controlling consistency at the noise level through learnable inter-view and intra-view collaboration matrices. The joint denoising with two parallel U-Nets for background and foreground is a novel architectural choice that, per the authors, remains compatible with standard diffusion training objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The noise-control framing offers a complementary (and potentially more interpretable) alternative to attention-based consistency mechanisms for multi-view video generation, and the generated data demonstrably improves downstream perception tasks (detection mAP +3.6, segmentation IoU +0.6 over no augmentation). However, evaluation is confined to the nuScenes driving domain with a fixed 6-view, 16-frame setup, leaving open whether the collaboration matrices and multi-level decomposition generalize to arbitrary camera rigs, longer sequences, or non-driving scenes. A key open question is whether the learnable collaboration matrices can be replaced by geometric priors (camera poses, depth) to reduce the number of learned parameters and improve extrapolation.
