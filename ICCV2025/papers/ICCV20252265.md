## Problem

Existing OCR benchmarks for Large Multimodal Models are fragmented across narrow scenarios and single-task evaluations, failing to provide a unified measure of full literacy capabilities spanning multi-scene reading, multilingual recognition, structured document parsing, and key information extraction. No prior benchmark simultaneously covers 10 languages, four distinct output formats, and the fine-grained visual challenges (orientation, grounding, artistic text) that real-world deployment demands.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252265/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark operates at full-image granularity across 7,058 images in 10 languages, sourced from documents, natural scenes, web/UGC, and handwritten captures. Outputs are task-dependent and structurally diverse: plain text with word-level bounding boxes for OCR tracks, LaTeX for document and formula parsing, HTML for table structures, JSON key-value pairs for KIE, and SMILES notation for molecular formulas. This multi-format output requirement distinguishes CC-OCR from prior benchmarks that evaluate only text transcription accuracy.

## Method

CC-OCR is organized into four OCR-centric tracks (multi-scene OCR, multilingual OCR, document parsing, KIE) comprising 39 subsets, with 41% of data newly collected from real-world applications. The annotation pipeline employs model-based pre-annotation, cross-validation across multiple models, and manual correction to ensure accuracy. Evaluation uses task-specific metrics: Eval-Trans/EvalPos for text recognition and grounding, Normalized Edit Distance for document/formula parsing, Tree Edit Distance-based Similarity (TEDS) for tables, and field-level F1 for KIE. The benchmark deliberately incorporates fine-grained visual challenges—multi-orientation text, artistic text, natural noise, and structured I/O—to stress-test model robustness, and evaluates 14 LMMs plus 2 OCR APIs to reveal systematic weaknesses in text grounding, orientation invariance, and repetition hallucination.

## Results

On the CC-OCR benchmark (7,058 images, 39 subsets, 4 tracks), Qwen2.5-VL-72B achieves the highest overall average score of 79.8, ranking first in all four tracks: multi-scene OCR (86.40), multilingual OCR (79.68), document parsing (63.44), and key information extraction (89.85%). Gemini-1.5-Pro ranks second overall (73.0), while InternVL3-78B leads document parsing (63.66) and is second in KIE (77.53%). Specialist models (KOSMOS2.5, Florence, TextMonkey, GOT) all score below 62 on multi-scene OCR, substantially trailing generalist LMMs. Fine-grained text grounding remains a major weakness, with the best model (Gemini-1.5-Pro) achieving only 60.98% and most models below 50%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Qwen2.5-VL-72B outperforms all 13 baselines across every track: it leads multi-scene OCR by 3.15 points over Gemini-1.5-Pro (86.40 vs. 83.25), multilingual OCR by 0.71 over Gemini-1.5-Pro (79.68 vs. 78.97), and KIE by 12.32 over InternVL3-78B (89.85 vs. 77.53). In document parsing, InternVL3-78B narrowly edges out Qwen2.5-VL-72B (63.66 vs. 63.44), with Gemini-1.5-Pro third at 62.37. All four specialist models (KOSMOS2.5: 47.55, Florence: 49.24, TextMonkey: 56.88, GOT: 61.00) are substantially outperformed by every generalist LMM on multi-scene OCR, with the gap exceeding 15 points for the top generalist. GoogleOCR API (80.03) is competitive with mid-tier generalists like Claude-3.5-Sonnet (72.87) and GPT-4o (76.40) on multi-scene OCR but lacks multilingual, parsing, and KIE capabilities. Multi-orientation robustness further differentiates models: Gemini-1.5-Pro degrades only 3.80% on rotated images versus 34.86% for GOT and 38.92% for InternVL2-76B.

## Relation to Prior Work

CC-OCR carves new ground by unifying the fragmented landscape of OCR evaluation for LMMs into a single multi-track benchmark. Prior work like OCRBench focuses on line-level recognition, KOSMOS2.5-Eval covers OCR and parsing but only in English, and DocLocal4K/FOX handle full-image OCR in limited scenarios. This paper repurposes and re-annotates existing datasets (TotalText, IC15, FUNSD, CORD, SROIE, POIE, SIBR, HUST-CELL) while introducing substantial new data (Chinese scenes, 10-language documents, in-house document parsing), extending the evaluation scope from 1–2 languages to 10 and from OCR-only to OCR + document parsing + key information extraction in a single framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CC-OCR fills a critical gap by providing the first comprehensive, multi-scene, multilingual, multi-format benchmark for evaluating LMM literacy, making it likely to become a standard reference in the field. Key open questions it surfaces include: why fine-grained text grounding remains a universal weakness (best model at ~61%), how to close the 15% natural-scene-to-document performance gap, and how to improve robustness to multi-orientation text and handwritten formulas. The finding that even top models score below 70 on document parsing sub-tasks and that multilingual performance lags significantly behind English/Chinese suggests substantial headroom for future LMM development in structured and diverse-language OCR.
