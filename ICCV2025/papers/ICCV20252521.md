## Problem

Existing zero-shot and few-shot anomaly detection methods for industrial inspection perform only binary classification (normal vs. abnormal) without identifying the specific defect type, limiting their utility for automated defect treatment in production lines. No prior approach performs multi-type anomaly segmentation—simultaneously localizing and classifying distinct defect types (e.g., bent, cut, scratch) at the pixel level in a zero-shot setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252521/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB product image and a set of defect-aware text prompts (one normal-state set plus K defect-type sets, each containing multiple prompt variations averaged into a single embedding). In few-shot mode, reference normal images from the target domain are additionally encoded into a memory bank. Outputs include a global anomaly score, a binary anomaly segmentation mask (complement of the normal-channel similarity map), and a (K+1)-channel multi-type defect segmentation map where each channel corresponds to a specific defect superclass, enabling per-pixel defect type assignment.

## Method

MultiADS builds on CLIP (ViT-L-14-336 from OpenCLIP) and introduces a Knowledge Base for Anomalies (KBA) that groups dataset-specific defect labels into abstract superclasses (e.g., bent, cut, scratch) to construct defect-aware text prompts, replacing the generic normal/abnormal prompts used by prior CLIP-based methods. During training, multi-stage ViT patch embeddings (4 stages from layers 6, 12, 18, 24) are projected through a single linear adapter and compared via cosine similarity against K+1 averaged text embeddings, producing m similarity maps optimized with a combined multi-class focal loss and binary dice loss. At inference, the up-sampled similarity maps are averaged to yield the multi-type segmentation map, while the complement of the normal channel produces the binary anomaly map; a global anomaly score is derived from the CLIP global image embedding. A filtering variant (MultiADS-F) excludes text prompt sets for defect types irrelevant to a given product, reducing noise in the similarity computation.

## Results

On the zero-shot anomaly detection and segmentation (ZSAD) task, MultiADS achieves the best or near-best performance across all five datasets. On the most challenging recent benchmarks, MultiADS attains 89.7 pixel-level AUROC and 78.3 image-level AUROC on MAD-real, and 96.6 pixel-level AUROC and 78.7 image-level AUROC on Real-IAD, outperforming the strongest baseline AnomalyCLIP by 1.4 and 0.3 points respectively on image-level AUROC. On VisA, MultiADS reaches 95.0 pixel AUROC / 89.7 AUPRO and 83.6 image AUROC / 86.9 AP, surpassing AnomalyCLIP (95.5 / 87.0 / 82.1 / 85.4). The filtering variant MultiADS-F further improves image-level AUROC on MPDD (79.7 vs. 78.3) and MAD-real (78.5 vs. 78.3) by excluding product-irrelevant defect types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MultiADS outperforms all 12 baselines on the majority of metrics across the five ZSAD datasets. The closest competitor is AnomalyCLIP, which leads on pixel-level AUROC for VisA (95.5 vs. 95.0) and MPDD (96.5 vs. 95.8), but MultiADS surpasses it on image-level AUROC for VisA (83.6 vs. 82.1), MAD-real (78.3 vs. 66.8), and Real-IAD (78.7 vs. 78.4), as well as on pixel AUPRO for VisA (89.7 vs. 87.0) and MAD-real (74.0 vs. 65.1). April-GAN is competitive on pixel-level metrics (e.g., 94.2 AUROC on VisA) but trails MultiADS on image-level AUROC by 5.6 points on VisA and 15.4 points on MAD-real. WinCLIP and AdaCLIP are consistently outperformed by 5–20 points on pixel-level AUROC across datasets. CLIP, CLIP-AC, CoOp, and CoCoOp are substantially weaker, with CoOp achieving only 24.2 pixel AUROC on VisA. In the few-shot setting (Figure 5), MultiADS achieves the highest image-level AUROC across all k-shot settings (k=1,2,4,8) on both VisA and MVTec-AD, outperforming April-GAN, PromptAD, AnomalyGPT, and PatchCore.

## Relation to Prior Work

MultiADS extends the CLIP-based zero-shot anomaly detection line (WinCLIP, April-GAN, AnomalyCLIP, AdaCLIP) by replacing the binary normal/abnormal text prompt scheme with defect-specific prompts for K distinct defect types, enabling multi-type segmentation rather than mere binary localization. It reuses the multi-stage patch embedding strategy from April-GAN [5] and AnomalyCLIP [42], along with memory-bank-based few-shot reference matching from [5], but adds a linear adapter layer and a Knowledge Base for Anomalies (KBA) to construct richer, defect-aware prompts. The key conceptual departure is that prior methods collapse all anomalies into a single 'abnormal' embedding, whereas MultiADS preserves per-defect-type semantic structure in the joint vision-language space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: in industrial optical inspection, knowing the specific defect type (bent vs. scratch vs. hole) enables automated corrective actions, not just rejection. However, the multi-type segmentation F1-scores remain low (22–52%), indicating the task is still in its infancy and far from deployment-ready. Open questions include whether learned (rather than hand-constructed) prompt embeddings could close this gap, how the approach scales to open-ended or previously unseen defect taxonomies, and whether the KBA can be automatically populated for new product lines without manual curation.
