## Problem

Existing person ReID datasets and methods are confined to at most two modalities (RGB and infrared/thermal) captured by static ground cameras, leaving a critical gap for real-world urban surveillance that simultaneously involves multiple sensor modalities and diverse capture platforms (ground vs. UAV). No prior benchmark or method jointly addresses the compounded challenges of modality discrepancy (RGB, infrared, thermal) and platform disparity (viewpoint, resolution, indoor/outdoor) in a unified ReID setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250551/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes person bounding-box crops from four heterogeneous sources: ground RGB (1920×1080), ground infrared (1920×1080), UAV RGB (3840×2160), and UAV thermal (640×512), with the thermal stream at substantially lower resolution. At test time, a single query image from one camera/modality is matched against a gallery of all images from a different camera/modality, and the output is a ranked similarity list evaluated by Rank-1, Rank-5, and mAP. The text prompt structure explicitly encodes which modality and platform produced each image, so the same CLIP backbone handles all four sources without architecture changes.

## Method

Uni-Prompt ReID builds on CLIP and decomposes the learnable text prompt into three sequential segments: a Specific Re-ID Prompt (individual-discriminative tokens), a Modality-aware Prompt, and a Platform-aware Prompt. A lightweight visual-enhanced network (VE-Net) maps image features to context vectors that are added to each prompt segment, allowing the text prompt to adapt to the visual content of the specific pedestrian image. Training proceeds in two stages: first, the Specific Re-ID Prompt is learned via CLIP-ReID image-to-text and text-to-image losses while the other segments are frozen; second, the modality- and platform-aware prompts are optimized with dedicated contrastive losses (i2t and t2i) that pull same-identity samples of the same modality/platform together. This staged, segment-specific prompt design lets the model disentangle identity, modality, and platform factors within a single unified text representation, achieving 7.87% higher average Rank-1 and 15.42% higher mAP over the best baseline on the MP-ReID benchmark.

## Results

On the MP-ReID benchmark, Uni-Prompt ReID achieves an average Rank-1 of 64.73%, Rank-5 of 77.99%, and mAP of 58.45% across all 12 experimental settings, outperforming the strongest baseline (OTLA-ReID) by 7.84% in Rank-1 and 15.42% in mAP. In the most challenging cross-modality & cross-platform setting, Uni-Prompt ReID reaches 43.16% Rank-1 and 40.26% mAP, while the best baseline (OTLA-ReID) drops to 29.31% Rank-1 and 20.66% mAP. The gap widens substantially in combined cross-modality & platform scenarios, where all baselines suffer severe performance degradation (Rank-1 below 30%) while Uni-Prompt ReID maintains over 43%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Uni-Prompt ReID outperforms all seven baselines across every experimental category. Against the strongest baseline OTLA-ReID (56.89% avg R1, 43.03% avg mAP), Uni-Prompt ReID gains +7.84% R1 and +15.42% mAP on average. The advantage is most pronounced in the cross-modality & cross-platform setting, where Uni-Prompt ReID (43.16% R1, 40.26% mAP) exceeds OTLA-ReID (29.31% R1, 20.66% mAP) by 13.85% R1 and 19.60% mAP, reflecting the baselines' inability to handle simultaneous modality and platform shifts. In cross-platform-only tasks, OTLA-ReID is the closest competitor (73.24% R1 vs. 78.77%), while in cross-modality-only tasks DEEN leads among baselines (69.59% R1) but still trails Uni-Prompt ReID by 2.67%. CSDN, the only other CLIP-based method, performs worst overall (28.39% avg R1), suggesting that simply integrating text features without structured prompt design is insufficient for multi-modal, multi-platform ReID.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt-learning paradigm for ReID (CLIP-ReID, DAPrompt, CoCoOp) by decomposing the learnable text prompt into three specialized components: a specific ReID prompt, a modality-aware prompt, and a platform-aware prompt, each enhanced by a visual-conditioned meta-network. Prior cross-modality ReID work (CAJ, DEEN, OTLA-ReID, SAAI) addresses only the RGB–infrared or RGB–thermal gap, while UAV-based datasets (AG-ReID, PRAI-1581) address only the ground–aerial viewpoint gap; this paper is the first to jointly formulate and tackle both gaps within a single framework. The visual-enhanced context mechanism draws directly from CoCoOp's conditional prompt idea, repurposing image features to bias prompt tokens toward the correct modality/platform representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MP-ReID benchmark fills a clear gap: no prior dataset simultaneously covers three modalities (RGB, infrared, thermal) and two platforms (ground, UAV) across indoor and outdoor scenes, making it a necessary resource for evaluating real-world multi-sensor ReID systems. The dramatic performance collapse of existing SOTA methods in the combined cross-modality-and-platform setting (e.g., OTLA-ReID drops from ~68% to ~29% Rank-1) reveals that the field has substantially underestimated the difficulty of multi-sensor deployment. Limitations include the modest scale (1,930 IDs, 14 cameras) constrained by collection cost, the absence of wearable or event-camera modalities, and the reliance on a single VLM backbone (CLIP), leaving open whether the prompt decomposition generalizes to other foundation models or scales to larger identity sets.
