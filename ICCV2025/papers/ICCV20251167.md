## Problem

State-of-the-art human-centric vision models require billions of parameters, extremely large real datasets with noisy annotations, and complex multi-stage training regimes (e.g., Sapiens needs 300M real images for pretraining plus 500K synthetic images for fine-tuning on 1,024 A100 GPUs for 18 days). This paper asks whether a single high-fidelity synthetic dataset, paired with a compact architecture, can match or exceed that accuracy at a fraction of the compute cost, while also providing perfect ground-truth labels and full control over data provenance and diversity.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image of a person (face, upper body, or full body) at 384×512 resolution (3:4 aspect ratio), which is side-padded to 512×512 for the ViT encoder. It produces three dense prediction outputs at the original input resolution: a 1-channel relative depth map, a 3-channel surface normal map (per-pixel xyz), and a 1-channel soft foreground alpha mask. A lightweight fully convolutional resizer processes the original-resolution image in parallel with the fixed-size ViT encoder, enabling the model to handle arbitrary input resolutions without increasing the quadratic self-attention cost.

## Method

The architecture adapts DPT with a ViT encoder (fixed 384×384 input), a four-level fully convolutional resizer that extracts multi-scale features at the original image resolution, and a modified decoder that fuses encoder tokens, resizer features, and the previous decoder output via residual convolutions and bilinear upsampling. Training is purely supervised on 300K procedurally generated SynthHuman images (384×512) with pixel-perfect depth, normal, and alpha annotations, using 100 epochs with AdamW and cosine annealing on 4×A100 GPUs. The key design choice is a single unified architecture where only the convolutional head (output channels) and loss function change per task: shift-and-scale-invariant MSE plus gradient supervision for depth, cosine similarity for normals, and BCE+L1+dice+Laplacian-pyramid loss for matting. This eliminates the need for multi-stage training, data mixing, task-specific refiners, or focal-length prediction modules used by competing methods, and the same dataset and architecture support a multi-task variant with three heads in a single 0.35B-parameter model.

## Results

On the primary depth estimation task (averaged over Goliath and Hi4D), Ours-Large (0.34B params, 663 GFLOPS) achieves RMSE 0.191 and AbsRel 0.012, outperforming Sapiens-0.3B (RMSE 0.312, AbsRel 0.021) by ~39% in RMSE while using the same parameter count but only 53% of the compute. Ours-Large also beats Depth-Pro (RMSE 0.350) and DepthAnythingV2-L (RMSE 0.433) despite being 6.6× and 2.8× faster respectively. For surface normal estimation, Ours-Large achieves 13.96° mean angular error on Goliath-UpperBody, surpassing Sapiens-0.3B (12.54°) and approaching Sapiens-2B (10.65°). For soft foreground segmentation, Ours achieves SAD 5.85 on PhotoMatte85 and SAD 78.17 on PPM-100, substantially outperforming MODNet (SAD 13.94 / 104.35) and P3M-Net (SAD 20.05 / 142.74).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On depth estimation, Ours-Large (0.34B) decisively beats Sapiens-0.3B (same size) by 39% in averaged RMSE (0.191 vs 0.312) and 43% in AbsRel (0.012 vs 0.021), while running ~1.9× faster (663 vs 1242 GFLOPS). It also outperforms Depth-Pro (0.50B, 4370 GFLOPS) by 45% in RMSE and DepthAnythingV2-L (0.34B, 1827 GFLOPS) by 56% in RMSE, despite being 6.6× and 2.8× faster respectively. Against the much larger Sapiens-2B (2.16B, 8709 GFLOPS), Ours-Large is slightly worse in RMSE (0.191 vs 0.170) but better in AbsRel (0.012 vs 0.015), while running ~13× faster. For surface normals, Ours-Large surpasses Sapiens-0.3B on Goliath-Face (17.15° vs 18.86° mean angular error) and Goliath-FullBody (14.60° vs 15.72°), but trails Sapiens-2B by 2–4° across subsets. On soft foreground segmentation, Ours dominates all baselines: SAD of 5.85 on PhotoMatte85 vs 13.94 (MODNet) and 20.05 (P3M-Net), and SAD of 78.17 on PPM-100 vs 104.35 (MODNet) and 142.74 (P3M-Net), with the largest gap over BGMv2 (SAD 159.44 on PPM-100).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DPT architecture (Ranftl et al., CVPR 2021) and the procedural synthetic data pipeline of Hewitt et al. (ACM TOG 2024), extending both to dense prediction tasks. It departs sharply from the dominant paradigm in human-centric vision—exemplified by Sapiens (300M real images + 500K synthetic, 18 days on 1024 A100s), DepthPro (two-stage real+synthetic curriculum), and DepthAnything-v2 (teacher-student with 62M real images)—by demonstrating that a single 300K-image high-fidelity synthetic dataset with a compact DPT model is sufficient for state-of-the-art accuracy. Prior synthetic data efforts (BEDLAM, RenderPeople, THuman) were limited by scan-based mesh fidelity; this work leverages artist-created assets and game-quality rendering to achieve pixel-perfect annotations for hair, clothing, and accessories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the field's assumption that human-centric vision requires massive real datasets and multi-billion-parameter models, showing instead that data fidelity and diversity (not sheer scale) drive accuracy for dense prediction on humans. This has direct implications for privacy, copyright, and consent: training exclusively on synthetic data sidesteps the ethical complications of large real-image collections. Open questions remain around generalization to extreme real-world conditions (tattoos, unusual scale variations, extreme lighting) and whether the approach extends beyond the three tasks demonstrated. The observation that ground-truth annotations in standard benchmarks (Goliath, Hi4D) are coarser than model predictions suggests current evaluation metrics may underestimate true model capability.
