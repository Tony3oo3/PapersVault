## Problem

Applying vision-language pretraining to ophthalmic surgery is hindered by three compounding challenges: severely limited video-text data (existing surgical VLP datasets are an order of magnitude smaller than general-purpose ones), the need for hierarchical understanding spanning fine-grained surgical actions to global procedural reasoning, and the underutilization of large-scale silent surgical videos that lack narrations. No prior dataset or framework simultaneously addresses all three, leaving a gap in building a generalist foundation model for ophthalmic surgical workflows.

## Input / Output

The model consumes surgical video frames extracted at 0.5 FPS from clips averaging 72 seconds (native resolution 1500×912, with >65% at ≥1280×720) paired with two levels of text: clip-level narrations describing specific surgical observations and video-level title summaries capturing overall procedural context. For zero-shot evaluation, class labels are formatted as sentence prompts; phase recognition outputs a single class label, while multi-instrument recognition applies sigmoid activation to per-class cosine similarities to produce independent probabilities for each of up to 35 instrument classes.

## Method

OphCLIP performs two-stage hierarchical contrastive pretraining on a CLIP-like architecture (ViT-B/16 or RN50 visual encoder + text encoder). At the clip level, it aligns short video clips with narrations via InfoNCE and adds SimSiam self-supervision over augmented views; at the video level, it aligns aggregated clip-level visual features with high-level title summaries. The key differentiator is a retrieval-augmented knowledge base: narrative video titles are encoded by PubMedBERT as queries, and a maximum inner product search retrieves top-K semantically similar silent videos from a dynamically updated memory bank storing both visual and textual embeddings. Retrieved silent video embeddings serve as additional positive samples in a contrastive loss, transferring implicit visual knowledge across narrative and silent procedures. An alternating training strategy cycles between clip-level and video-level objectives to prevent catastrophic forgetting while jointly optimizing short- and long-term representations.

## Results

On zero-shot surgical phase recognition, OphCLIP (RN50, OphVL) achieves 41.4% Acc / 28.8% F1 on Cat-21, 62.8% Acc / 48.5% F1 on Cataract-1K, and 59.3% Acc / 41.0% F1 on LensID, substantially outperforming vanilla CLIP (13.3/2.7, 6.9/2.0, 22.9/16.3 respectively). On zero-shot multi-instrument recognition, OphCLIP reduces the False Positive Rate from 100% (CLIP, SLIP, LaCLIP) to 45.1% on Cataract-1K and 51.1% on CatInstSeg, while improving mAP from 13.9 to 21.2 and from 20.2 to 28.3. In few/full-shot linear probing with 100% data, OphCLIP reaches 72.1% Acc / 57.9% F1 on Cat-21 and 23.1% F1 / 24.6% mAP on Cataract-1K, surpassing CLIP* (59.4/40.5 and 13.0/18.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OphCLIP decisively outperforms all three general-purpose VLP baselines (CLIP, SLIP, LaCLIP) across every phase recognition dataset, with the largest gap on Cataract-1K where OphCLIP reaches 62.8% Acc versus 5.4–10.0% for the baselines. On instrument recognition, all three baselines produce 100% False Positive Rate (i.e., flagging every frame as containing an instrument), while OphCLIP reduces FPR to 45.1% (Cataract-1K) and 51.1% (CatInstSeg) with higher mAP. Against CLIP* (CLIP fine-tuned on the same OphVL dataset without the hierarchical retrieval-augmented training), OphCLIP gains 12.6 Acc points on Cat-21 (41.4 vs 28.8), 42.0 Acc points on Cataract-1K (62.8 vs 20.8), and reduces instrument FPR from 80.8% to 45.1%, confirming that the retrieval-based silent-video knowledge base and hierarchical clip/video-level pretraining provide substantial benefits beyond simply scaling the pretraining corpus.

## Relation to Prior Work

OphCLIP extends the CLIP-style contrastive vision-language pretraining paradigm to ophthalmic surgical video, building directly on the CLIP architecture (Radford et al., 2021) and incorporating SimSiam self-supervision (Chen & He, 2021) for visual augmentation. It departs from prior surgical VLP efforts (SurgVLP, GenSurg+, HECVL) in two key ways: (1) a hierarchical two-stage pretraining that jointly learns fine-grained clip-narration and coarse-grained video-title representations, and (2) a retrieval-augmented mechanism that uses a dynamically updated memory bank of silent surgical videos as auxiliary supervisory signals, drawing on retrieval-augmented ideas from NLP and visual recognition. The OphVL dataset itself represents a substantial scale-up (15×) over existing surgical VLP corpora, leveraging LLM-based text refinement and Whisper ASR to produce cleaner, concept-rich annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OphVL dataset (375K clip-text pairs, 7.5K hours) is the first large-scale, hierarchically structured VLP resource for ophthalmic surgery and will serve as a foundational benchmark for the subfield. The retrieval-augmented silent-video knowledge base is a creative way to exploit the large volume of unlabeled surgical footage, though its marginal contribution in ablation (≈1–2 F1 points) suggests the primary gains come from data scale and hierarchical alignment rather than the retrieval mechanism itself. Absolute zero-shot performance remains modest (e.g., 41.4% Acc on Cat-21, 62.8% on Cataract-1K), indicating that ophthalmic surgical understanding is still far from solved. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond ophthalmic surgery to other specialties, whether the retrieval component scales meaningfully with larger silent-video pools, and whether the hierarchical pretraining strategy transfers to other long-form procedural video domains.
