## Problem

Personalized diffusion models can generate faces in dozens of stylistic domains (anime, cyberpunk, painting, etc.), but existing face recognition models—whether CLIP-based or trained on real faces—fail to reliably identify the same identity across these diverse styles. No large-scale dataset dedicated to stylized face recognition existed, leaving the field without a proper training resource or evaluation protocol for this emerging task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250847/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The recognition model takes a 112×112 RGB stylized face image (cropped via RetinaFace with five-landmark alignment) and produces a fixed-dimensional identity embedding for pairwise verification. The dataset provides 4.6 million such images across 62,446 real identities and 80 style prompts, with each identity having multiple stylized variants generated by different diffusion methods. Evaluation uses TAR@FAR at 1e-2, 1e-3, and 1e-4 thresholds on open-set verification pairs.

## Method

The dataset is constructed by applying four ID-preserving diffusion methods (InstantID as primary at 69%, plus PhotoMaker, Flashface, and IP-Adapter) across three pre-trained diffusion backbones (SDXL, YamerMIX, Anything-XL) and 80 curated style prompts, generating over 10 million candidate images from 62k real identities in CASIA-WebFace and Glint360k. A semi-automatic cleaning pipeline alternates between model-based filtering (discarding images below TAR@FAR 1e-2 similarity to their original, or below TAR@FAR 1e-3 similarity to other stylized images of the same ID) and manual annotation of the ambiguous middle band, ensuring ID preservation at scale. For evaluation, three benchmarks are established: standard (same distribution), cross-prompt (60 train / 20 unseen test prompts), and cross-method (train on InstantID, test on Flashface/IP-Adapter/PhotoMaker). Recognition models use IResNet-50/101 backbones trained with angular margin losses (CosFace, ArcFace, CurricularFace) under supervised learning, with the key finding that training on Stylized-Face yields a 15.9% TAR@FAR 1e-4 improvement over real-face-trained models.

## Results

On the Standard Stylized Face Recognition benchmark (IResNet-50, CosFace), models trained on Stylized-Face achieve TAR@FAR of 97.9% (1e-2), 95.5% (1e-3), and 90.6% (1e-4), substantially outperforming models trained on Glint360k (95.0/87.0/74.7) and MS1MV3 (85.1/68.0/49.7). This represents a 15.9 percentage-point improvement in TAR@FAR 1e-4 over the strongest real-face baseline. In cross-method generalization (testing on Flashface, IP-Adapter, and PhotoMaker outputs), Stylized-Face-trained models reach 71.9% TAR@FAR 1e-3 versus 58.6% for Glint360k and 31.8% for MS1MV3, a 13.3-point gain over the best real-face baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is dataset-driven: models trained on Stylized-Face consistently outperform those trained on real-face datasets (Glint360k via Partial FC [6], MS1MV3 via ArcFace [13]) across all three evaluation protocols. On Standard Stylized Face Recognition, Stylized-Face beats Glint360k by 8.5 points at TAR@FAR 1e-4 (90.6 vs. 74.7) and MS1MV3 by 40.9 points. In cross-method generalization, the gap narrows but persists: Stylized-Face achieves 71.9% vs. 58.6% (Glint360k) at TAR@FAR 1e-3 on the combined Flashface/IP-Adapter/PhotoMaker test set. On real-face benchmarks (LFW, CFP_FP, AgeDB), Stylized-Face-trained models (98.7% avg.) are slightly below Glint360k (99.1%) and MS1MV3 (98.9%), confirming a modest trade-off between stylized and real face performance. Among training losses (Table 9), CosFace [56], CurricularFace [23], and ArcFace [13] yield nearly identical results (90.5–90.6% at TAR@FAR 1e-4), indicating the dataset, not the loss function, drives the improvement. CASIA-WebFace [65] and MS-Celeb-1M [17] serve as smaller real-face baselines in Table 8, both scoring below Stylized-Face on real-face accuracy (96.5% and 98.7% avg. respectively vs. 98.7% for Stylized-Face).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of face recognition and personalized diffusion, arguing that the bottleneck for stylized face recognition is not model architecture but the absence of large-scale training data with verified identity preservation. It departs from prior synthetic face datasets (SynFace, DiGiFace-1M) that used virtual IDs and realistic styles, and from cartoon face benchmarks (WebCaricature, IIIT-CFW) that offered single styles at limited scale. The dataset construction repurposes existing ID-preserving diffusion methods (InstantID, PhotoMaker, Flashface, IP-Adapter) as data generators rather than as the recognition model itself, while the recognition side remains standard angular-margin CNN training (CosFace/ArcFace/CurricularFace on IResNet). The semi-automatic cleaning pipeline—alternating model-based filtering with human verification—is a practical contribution that makes million-scale ID-verified stylized data feasible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As generative AI makes it trivial to produce infinite stylized variants of a person's face, the ability to verify identity across these variants becomes a concrete governance need for portrait-rights enforcement. The three-tier benchmark (within-distribution, cross-prompt, cross-method) exposes that cross-method generalization remains a significant open problem: even with the new dataset, TAR@FAR=1e-3 drops from 95.5% (within-distribution) to 71.9% (cross-method), suggesting that style-invariant feature learning across heterogeneous generative pipelines is far from solved. The paper also notes a trade-off between real and stylized recognition performance, and flags long-tail style distributions in real-world deployment as an underexplored challenge.
