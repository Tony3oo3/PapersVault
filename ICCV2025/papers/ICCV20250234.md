## Problem

Existing isolated sign language recognition (ISLR) methods are trained exclusively on front-view data and suffer severe accuracy drops when test cameras are positioned at arbitrary viewpoints. Multi-view training data is scarce in current benchmarks, and directly training on naively synthesized multi-view samples fails because viewpoint-specific and semantics-specific features remain entangled in the learned representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250234/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 64-frame 2D skeleton sequences (upper body: 13 keypoints, hands: 42 keypoints) extracted from front-view sign videos via SMPLer-X 3D keypoint estimation followed by virtual-camera projection. During training, these skeletons are augmented with synthetic viewpoints through random yaw/pitch rotations and temporal perturbations (random clipping, speed variation). The model outputs two predictions: a sign semantic label from 3,215 Auslan glosses and a viewpoint label, with the sign label being the primary recognition target.

## Method

The framework has two stages. First, View Synthesis extracts 3D whole-body keypoints from front-view training videos using SMPLer-X, then applies virtual camera rotations across seven predefined yaw/pitch viewpoints (plus random perturbations during training) to project 2D skeleton sequences, enriching view diversity without multi-camera hardware. Second, the Contrastive Multi-task View-Semantics Recognition (CMVSR) module processes each skeleton through a Part-Aware ST-GCN encoder that separately models upper-body and hand dynamics, then feeds the concatenated features into a Cross-Attention Disentangler where a single learnable view token and 64 learnable semantic tokens attend to the skeleton representation to separate viewpoint cues from sign semantics. Training employs triplet sampling (anchor, same-sign-different-view, same-view-different-sign) with dual contrastive losses that pull same-semantic/same-view embeddings together and push mismatched ones apart, combined with cross-entropy classification losses for both sign and viewpoint, jointly enforcing view-invariant sign representations and view-discriminative viewpoint features.

## Results

On the MM-WLAuslan benchmark, CMVSR achieves 78.16% average Top-1 accuracy on front-view (F) skeleton-based ISLR, outperforming the best prior skeleton method KVNet-K (76.82%) and approaching the multi-modal NLA-SLR (81.90%). The headline result is in the cross-view (MV) setting, where CMVSR reaches 40.72% average Top-1 accuracy, surpassing the strongest skeleton baseline DSTA-SLR (25.42%) by 15.3 percentage points and the multi-modal NLA-SLR (29.58%) by 11.1 points. On the newly introduced MTV-Test set (which includes upward/downward viewpoints and consumer-grade devices), CMVSR achieves 31.14% Top-1 vs. 12.46% for DSTA-SLR and 15.02% for NLA-SLR, demonstrating substantial gains under the most challenging in-the-wild conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three skeleton-based baselines (STC-SLR, DSTA-SLR, KVNet-K), CMVSR wins on both front-view and cross-view settings. On front-view, CMVSR leads by 1.3–4.8 points in average Top-1 over the skeleton baselines (78.16% vs. 73.35–76.82%). The gap widens dramatically in the cross-view (MV) setting: CMVSR achieves 40.72% average Top-1, beating DSTA-SLR (25.42%) by 15.3 points, KVNet-K (24.69%) by 16.0 points, and STC-SLR (22.53%) by 18.2 points. Against the multi-modal NLA-SLR (Pixel+2D Pose), CMVSR trails by 3.7 points on front-view (78.16% vs. 81.90%) but surpasses it by 11.1 points on cross-view (40.72% vs. 29.58%), indicating that the disentanglement strategy is particularly effective when the test viewpoint deviates from the training view. On the new MTV-Test set, CMVSR's advantage is most pronounced: 31.14% Top-1 vs. 12.46% (DSTA-SLR) and 15.02% (NLA-SLR), a 2.5× improvement over the best prior method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends skeleton-based ISLR (built on ST-GCN and its variants) to the cross-view setting by introducing a two-stage pipeline: virtual 3D-to-2D skeleton synthesis via SMPLer-X keypoint extraction and camera rotation, followed by a contrastive multi-task disentanglement module. It draws on cross-view action recognition literature (e.g., DVANet, DA-Net) for the view-semantics separation idea but is the first to apply cross-attention-based token disentanglement with dual triplet contrastive losses specifically within a sign language recognition framework. The work also extends the MM-WLAuslan benchmark with a new in-the-wild MTV-Test set, departing from prior CV-ISLR evaluations that were limited to ±30° yaw angles and controlled RGB-D sensor environments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cross-view ISLR is a critical practical gap: real-world deployment cameras are rarely perfectly frontal, yet all prior skeleton-based ISLR methods were trained and tested on front-view data only. The view synthesis strategy offers a cost-effective alternative to multi-camera data collection, and the MTV-Test set provides a more realistic evaluation protocol with consumer-grade devices, diverse backgrounds, and vertical viewpoints. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to continuous (sentence-level) sign language, whether the 7 predefined viewpoints suffice for truly arbitrary camera placements, and how sensitive the pipeline is to errors in the upstream 3D keypoint estimation (SMPLer-X).
