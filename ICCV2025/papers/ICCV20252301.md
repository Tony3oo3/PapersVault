## Problem

Existing video diffusion models cannot generate text animations because they lack training data for letter-shaped content, producing outputs unrelated to the input glyph. Prior text animation methods apply only simple, fixed motion patterns (shaking, zooming) without semantic understanding of the prompt. The paper addresses the gap of automatically generating semantically meaningful, prompt-driven letter deformations and animations while preserving legibility throughout the sequence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252301/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single letter extracted from a font file via FreeType and converted into a closed curve of cubic Bezier segments (iteratively subdivided to a user-defined control-point count, default 75), along with a free-form text prompt describing the desired animation. The output is a vector animation of k frames (24 in experiments), each defined by 2D displacements applied to the N Bezier control points, producing resolution-independent SVG-compatible geometry rather than pixel frames. A differentiable rasterizer converts the vector output to pixels only for loss computation during optimization.

## Method

The core architecture decomposes the animation into two coordinate-based MLPs: a canonical field that learns a shared base shape for all frames, and a deformation field operating in 3D (x, y, t) space that predicts per-frame displacements decomposed into a global affine transform and per-point local offsets. Training uses Score Distillation Sampling from a frozen text-to-video diffusion model (ModelScope, AnimateDiff, or ZeroScope) to align the rendered animation with the prompt, requiring no text-animation dataset. Two regularization terms enforce quality: an LPIPS loss between the canonical shape and the original letter preserves legibility, and a Delaunay-triangulation-based angle-variation loss between consecutive frames prevents Bezier self-intersections and flickering by maintaining conformal mesh structure. The canonical shape is also triangulation-constrained to the input letter, propagating structural integrity across all frames.

## Results

On the 33-sample Dynamic Typography evaluation set, the proposed method achieves the best Perceptual Input Conformity (PIC) of 0.5302 and the best Text-to-Video Alignment score of 21.4391, outperforming all baselines on both legibility and semantic alignment metrics. CLIP Temporal Consistency is competitive at 0.9963, slightly below DynamiCrafter's 0.9979 but well above FlipSketch (0.9888) and LiveSketch (0.9930). In a user study with 101 participants (808 responses), 89% of selections favor the proposed method over all baselines combined, with p-values below 1e-19 in every pairwise comparison.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DynamiCrafter (image-to-video), the proposed method wins on PIC (0.5302 vs 0.5151) and T2V Alignment (21.44 vs 17.81) but trails slightly on CLIP Temporal Consistency (0.9963 vs 0.9979), reflecting DynamiCrafter's more rigid frame-to-frame stability at the cost of minimal or unrealistic motion. Against the vector-based baselines LiveSketch and FlipSketch, the proposed method dominates on all three metrics, with the largest gap on PIC (0.5302 vs 0.4841/0.5173), indicating that these sketch-animation methods sacrifice legibility more severely. Gen-2 (text-to-video) is excluded from the quantitative table because it produces videos completely unrelated to the input letter shape; in the user study it receives only 79 selections versus 395 for the proposed method. Overall, the proposed method is the only baseline that simultaneously achieves the best legibility and semantic alignment while maintaining near-best temporal consistency, a balance none of the other methods attain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper composes several established techniques into a new pipeline for semantic text animation: the canonical-field/deformation-field decomposition from CoDeF [37], the vectorized Bezier-curve letter representation and FreeType extraction from Word-as-Image [19], Score Distillation Sampling from DreamFusion [40], and the global/local motion split from LiveSketch [15]. It departs from prior kinetic-typography and dynamic-style-transfer work, which relied on fixed motion templates or reference videos, by using only a free-text prompt as input. The key methodological departure is the Delaunay-triangulation-based structure-preservation loss, which addresses a failure mode (Bezier self-intersection causing flickering) not encountered in the sketch-animation or 3D-reconstruction settings where the other components were originally developed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that high-quality, semantically meaningful per-letter animation can be produced without any task-specific training data, purely by distilling priors from off-the-shelf video diffusion models, making the technique immediately upgradable as video models improve. The main limitation is that motion quality is bounded by the underlying video model's understanding of specific motions, and the legibility–semantics trade-off degrades when the prompt is far from the letter's natural shape. Open questions include extending the approach to multi-letter or full-word animation, handling longer sequences, and integrating with future video models that natively understand typography.
