## Problem

Disentangling video into static content and dynamic motion is an under-determined problem that prior work addresses by introducing strong assumptions (e.g., motion-content independence, single-frame content sufficiency) or task-specific priors (optical flow, 3D face models, keypoints). These constraints limit expressiveness on real-world video and restrict applicability to narrow domains. The paper seeks a general, self-supervised framework with minimal inductive bias that produces meaningful disentangled representations on in-the-wild video.

## Input / Output

The model consumes video clips (50 frames at 256×256 for LRS3 talking heads; 8 frames at 64×64 for LPC Sprites) tokenized by a pretrained Stable Diffusion 2.0 image VAE into per-frame latent tokens. It outputs a fixed-length clip-wise content vector (K learnable query tokens) and a per-frame discrete motion sequence (quantized via 64-group VQ at a target of 4 kbps for talking heads). For motion transfer, a reference video supplies the content feature while a driving video supplies the motion sequence; for autoregressive generation, a single-frame prompt seeds a GPT-2 model trained on the learned motion space.

## Method

BCD (Bitrate Controlled Diffusion) uses a T5 transformer encoder with learnable prefix queries to jointly extract a global content feature and per-frame motion features from video latents, then applies a group vector-quantization module with an explicit bitrate constraint (MSE between achieved entropy and a target bitrate) as an information bottleneck on the motion branch. The key insight is that the low-bitrate budget acts as a general disentanglement prior: content leaking into motion would exceed the bitrate cap, while a nonzero target prevents excessive information loss. A DiT-based latent diffusion decoder, augmented with temporal attention layers, reconstructs the original latents conditioned on both features, and the full pipeline is trained end-to-end with a rate-distortion objective (diffusion denoising loss + bitrate loss). A cross-driven training strategy—using content from the first temporal half and motion from the second half to reconstruct the second half—further prevents collapse into entangled representations.

## Results

On the LRS3 talking-head dataset, BCD achieves the best FID (86.0), identity error (0.41×10⁻¹), motion error (3.13×10⁻²), and cross-driven error (3.67×10⁻²) among all compared methods, while matching LivePortrait on CSIM (0.69) but trailing FOMM and MCNET (0.76). In a user study with 18 participants, BCD also scores highest on identity preservation (4.10/5), motion consistency (4.30/5), and visual quality (4.00/5). On the LPC Sprites dataset, BCD demonstrates superior attribute accuracy in motion transfer, confirming generalizability beyond talking heads.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BCD outperforms all five baselines on FID, identity error, motion error, and cross-driven error. Against keypoint-based methods (FOMM, MCNET, LivePortrait), BCD reduces motion error from ~24×10⁻² to 3.13×10⁻² and cross error from ~24×10⁻² to 3.67×10⁻², while sacrificing CSIM (0.69 vs. 0.76 for FOMM/MCNET). Against the 3D-prior method HyperReenact, BCD wins on every metric including FID (86.0 vs. 106.8) and CSIM (0.69 vs. 0.58), avoiding the 3DMM-rendering artifact that degrades HyperReenact's perceptual quality. Against LIA, which uses a linear orthogonal motion basis, BCD dramatically reduces motion error (3.13 vs. 36.1×10⁻²) and cross error (3.67 vs. 34.1×10⁻²), showing that LIA's constrained motion space fails to transfer motion faithfully. The only metric where BCD does not lead is CSIM, where FOMM and MCNET's explicit keypoint-based identity preservation gives them a slight edge (0.76 vs. 0.69), though the user study confirms BCD is perceived as best overall on identity preservation (4.10 vs. 3.66 for LIA, the next-best).

## Relation to Prior Work

BCD sits at the intersection of three research threads: disentangled sequential VAEs (D3VAE, S3VAE, SKD), neural video codecs (DCVC and successors), and latent diffusion models (Stable Diffusion, DiT). It departs from prior disentanglement work by replacing explicit structural assumptions (independence constraints, low-dimensional dynamics, single-frame content) and task-specific priors (optical flow, 3DMM, keypoints) with a purely implicit transformer representation regulated by a low-bitrate group-VQ information bottleneck. The direct conceptual inspiration for using bitrate-controlled VQ as a disentanglement mechanism comes from recent disentangled neural speech codecs, which the authors adapt to the video domain with a diffusion-based generative decoder rather than a deterministic one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a general-purpose information bottleneck (bitrate control) can substitute for domain-specific inductive biases in video motion-content disentanglement, offering a more flexible and assumption-light framework applicable across video domains (talking heads, 2D sprites). Key open questions include: whether the optimal bitrate can be derived theoretically rather than tuned per dataset, how the method scales to longer and more diverse video sequences, and whether the learned motion space can be made interactive or controllable post-hoc. The authors acknowledge that substantial training data is required and that slight temporal flickering persists, limiting immediate deployment in production pipelines.
