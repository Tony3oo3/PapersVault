## Problem

Existing bilateral grid-based enhancement methods (e.g., HDRNet) are restricted to linear affine transformations, limiting their ability to capture complex non-linear color relationships, while MLP-based methods apply globally shared parameters that cannot adapt to localized spatial variations. Additionally, prior bilateral grid approaches fuse RGB channels into a single-channel guidance map, underutilizing multi-channel color information during the slicing operation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250960/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts 8-bit sRGB or 16-bit CIE XYZ images at varying resolutions and produces enhanced 8-bit sRGB output. The bilateral grid spatial size adapts to input resolution (1/4 for 360p, 1/32 for full-resolution FiveK, 1/8 otherwise) with a fixed depth of 8 intensity bins, and the slicing operation is implemented as a CUDA extension for real-time inference exceeding 30 FPS on 4K images.

## Method

BPAM replaces the affine coefficients in the bilateral grid with parameters of a per-pixel 3-8-3 MLP, where each pixel retrieves its unique weight matrices and biases via trilinear interpolation over the grid using its spatial coordinates and intensity value. A key innovation is the grid decomposition strategy: the first grid is partitioned into four subgrids (one per input color channel's weights plus a shared bias) and the second grid into nine subgrids (one per hidden channel's weights plus a shared bias), each extracted by a dedicated channel of a multi-channel guidance map. Two sequential slicing operations are performed—the first retrieves hidden-layer parameters guided by the original image, and the second retrieves output-layer parameters guided by the computed hidden-layer vector. A three-layer U-Net-style NAFNet backbone generates the grids from a downsampled input, and the entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a composite loss of MSE, SSIM, and VGG19 perceptual terms.

## Results

On the FiveK tone mapping task at full resolution, BPAM achieves PSNR 25.12 dB, SSIM 0.934, and ΔE 7.73, outperforming the closest competitor LutBGrid (24.57 dB, 0.931, 8.03) by 0.55 dB in PSNR. On the LCDP exposure correction task, BPAM reaches PSNR 24.22 dB, SSIM 0.872, and LPIPS 0.097, surpassing the best baseline CoTF (23.89 dB, 0.858, 0.104). At 4K resolution (3840×2160), the method processes images at 36.0 FPS (27.8 ms per frame), meeting real-time requirements while 3D LUT-based methods like 3DLUT run at 862 FPS but sacrifice spatial adaptability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BPAM outperforms all 21 baselines across all three tasks (tone mapping, photo retouching, exposure correction) on every reported metric. The closest competitor is LutBGrid [24], which also uses bilateral grids but with 3D LUTs; BPAM leads it by 0.24 dB PSNR and 0.009 SSIM at 480p on FiveK tone mapping, and by 0.55 dB at full resolution. Among 3D LUT-based methods, 3DLUT [58] and AdaInt [56] are the strongest, yet BPAM still exceeds them by 0.76 dB and 0.24 dB PSNR respectively on FiveK 480p tone mapping. On the PPR10K portrait retouching task, BPAM achieves the best PSNR (26.58 dB) and ΔE (6.40) on the 'a' split, edging out LutBGrid (26.45 dB, 6.51). On the challenging LCDP exposure correction task, BPAM surpasses the dedicated exposure-correction method CoTF [28] by 0.33 dB PSNR and 0.007 LPIPS, while also outperforming general-purpose enhancers like SID [2] (21.89 dB) and LCDPNet [46] (23.24 dB) by over 2 dB. In terms of speed, 3D LUT methods (3DLUT at 1124 FPS, LutBGrid at 602 FPS at 1080p) are significantly faster than BPAM (98.3 FPS), but BPAM maintains 36 FPS at 4K resolution where 3D LUT methods degrade due to their fixed-size LUT design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: bilateral-grid-based image enhancement (HDRNet, which uses the grid to store per-pixel affine coefficients) and MLP-based color mapping (CSRNet, which applies a single global MLP across the entire image). BPAM departs from both by storing MLP parameters in the bilateral grid so that each pixel retrieves its own tiny 3-8-3 network via trilinear slicing, and by introducing a grid decomposition strategy that splits MLP weights into category-specific subgrids guided by multi-channel guidance maps—addressing the single-channel intensity bottleneck inherited from grayscale-oriented bilateral grids. The backbone (NAFNet U-Net) and the two-stage slicing pipeline follow HDRNet's architecture, while the non-linear per-pixel transformation is the key conceptual departure from prior bilateral-grid methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that spatially adaptive, non-linear per-pixel color mappings can match or exceed the quality of the fastest 3D LUT methods while retaining genuine spatial awareness, achieving 30+ FPS on 4K images—a practical sweet spot for real-time enhancement pipelines. A notable limitation is that inference speed still lags behind pure 3D LUT approaches (e.g., 3DLUT at 862 FPS vs. BPAM at 36 FPS on 4K), so the method trades raw speed for quality and spatial adaptability. Open questions include whether the grid decomposition strategy generalizes to deeper MLPs or higher-dimensional parameter spaces, and whether the approach can be extended to video or multi-frame enhancement where temporal consistency matters.
