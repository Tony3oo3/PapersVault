## Problem

Deterministic surgical workflow models collapse to dominant population-level patterns, failing to adapt to patient-specific anatomical and procedural variations. While stochastic models like DDPM can capture such variability, directly applying them to surgical tasks produces clinically implausible outputs and cannot meet real-time inference requirements. The paper addresses how to harness the uncertainty-aware benefits of diffusion models for surgical anticipation and recognition without sacrificing clinical consistency or speed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250048/cropped_page0_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes surgical video frames resampled to 1 FPS in an online manner, using only historical and current information (64-frame clips during training). For anticipation, it outputs the remaining time (in minutes) until the next instrument usage or phase transition, clamped to a predefined horizon h ∈ {2, 3, 5} min, plus a three-stage presence classification. For recognition, it outputs a discrete surgical phase label. At inference, only the deterministic branch is retained, yielding 91 FPS throughput with no iterative denoising.

## Method

CoStoDet-DDPM employs a dual-branch collaborative training framework sharing a conditional feature c_t ∈ R^512 extracted by a ConvNeXt-T spatial encoder followed by an LSTM temporal module. The deterministic Task Branch (T) maps c_t through a linear predictor supervised by task loss (SmoothL1 + auxiliary cross-entropy for anticipation; cross-entropy for recognition). The stochastic DDPM Branch (D) uses a U-Net denoiser conditioned on c_t via FiLM to recover labels from Gaussian noise over K=100 timesteps, with the label sequence extended backward by 32 frames to capture longer temporal context. The joint loss L = L_Task + L_DDPM creates mutual refinement: D's denoising constraint compacts the feature distribution, mitigates dominant-pattern collapse, and embeds navigation information into c_t, while T's task loss enforces clinical-semantic alignment that guides D's denoising trajectory. At inference, D is entirely discarded, so only T produces predictions in real time.

## Results

On the Cholec80 anticipation task, CoStoDet-DDPM achieves eMAE of 1.77 min (tool) and 0.88 min (phase) at h=5 min, representing 16.1% and 11.1% relative improvements over the prior SOTA BNPitfalls (2.11 and 0.99 min, respectively). For phase recognition, the method attains 81.4% Jaccard on Cholec80 (+1.0% over DACAT) and 67.9% Jaccard on AutoLaparo (+1.5% over DACAT), while also achieving the highest video-level accuracy (94.5% and 87.6%). Inference runs at 91 FPS since the DDPM branch is discarded at test time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the anticipation task (Cholec80), CoStoDet-DDPM outperforms all seven baselines across every horizon and both tool/phase sub-tasks. The closest competitor, BNPitfalls [48], is surpassed by 16.1% in tool eMAE and 11.1% in phase eMAE at h=5 min, with the gap widening at shorter horizons (e.g., 12.3% tool eMAE at h=2 min). ADANet [69] and IIA-Net [66] are the next-strongest baselines but trail by 8–15% in eMAE. On the recognition task, the method achieves the best Jaccard on both Cholec80 (81.4% vs. DACAT's 80.6%) and AutoLaparo (67.9% vs. DACAT's 66.9%), while also leading in recall (90.2% and 76.4%) and video-level accuracy (94.5% and 87.6%). The only metric where it does not lead is precision on Cholec80 (88.4% vs. DACAT's 89.2%), a 0.8-point trade-off for substantially higher recall. On AutoLaparo, the precision margin over DACAT is +6.0 points (84.5% vs. 78.5%), indicating the stochastic branch particularly benefits the more heterogeneous hysterectomy dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in surgical workflow analysis, where all prior methods (Trans-SVNet, Surgformer, BNPitfalls, DACAT, etc.) rely on deterministic encoder-decoder pipelines (ConvNeXt/Transformer + LSTM/TCN) that establish fixed input-to-label mappings. It draws conceptual inspiration from Diffusion Policy (Chi et al.) and DiffAct/DiffAct++ (Liu et al.), which apply DDPM to robot action generation and natural-domain action segmentation, but repurposes DDPM in a fundamentally different role: not as a generative model at inference, but as a training-time feature regularizer whose stochastic denoising objective reshapes the shared representation space. The deterministic task branch builds directly on the BNPitfalls architecture (no-batch-norm CNN + LSTM), while the stochastic branch introduces a FiLM-conditioned U-Net denoiser operating on label space rather than pixel space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core practical contribution is a training-only stochastic component that improves deterministic inference without adding any latency (91 FPS), making it deployable in real-time robotic-assisted surgery. The 'collapse to dominant patterns' diagnosis and the PCA-based evidence for feature agglomeration provide a mechanistic explanation for why long-tail surgical variations are underrepresented in deterministic models. However, the evaluation is limited to two datasets (Cholec80, AutoLaparo) with small test splits (20 and 7 videos), and the paper acknowledges failure on rare instrument-usage patterns (irrigator), suggesting the approach still struggles with truly absent events. Open questions include whether the DDPM-as-feature-enhancer paradigm transfers to other video understanding tasks, whether the 100-step diffusion schedule is optimal, and how the method scales to larger, more diverse surgical corpora.
