## Problem

Single-dataset ultrasound lesion detectors fail catastrophically on unseen data distributions due to domain shifts from heterogeneous imaging devices, acoustic shadows, and speckle noise. Existing multi-dataset approaches impose architectural constraints—separate encoders, task-specific heads, test-time domain knowledge, or fine-tuning—that contradict the goal of source-agnostic generalization. The paper tackles the open problem of how to mitigate inter-dataset interference while preserving dataset-specific discriminative features within a single universal model.

## Input / Output

The model accepts ultrasound images of arbitrary spatial resolution (H×W×3) from eight heterogeneous datasets spanning breast and thyroid organs, and outputs bounding box coordinates, lesion category labels, and confidence scores via the DINO detection head. During training, a structured text prompt (e.g., 'Diagnosis for Breast Ultrasound Image: Benign') is paired with each image to drive the Semantic Shared Expert, but at inference only the generic prompt 'Diagnosis for Ultrasound Image' is used, making the system deployable without per-dataset text annotations. The framework is evaluated under three training paradigms—single-dataset, intra-organ consolidation, and inter-organ integration—each with an 8:2 train/test split.

## Method

COME replaces the standard FFN layers in the DINO-ViT decoder with a collaborative Mixture-of-Experts module comprising three components: a frozen Structure Shared Expert (STE) built on USFM's spatial-frequency masked image modeling for dataset-agnostic structural priors, a Semantic Shared Expert (SEE) fine-tuned from MedCLIP-SAMv2 for cross-modal semantic alignment, and a Source-Specific Expert bank (S²E) of M′ expert networks with a linear gating router. Before routing, tokens pass through either a Fine2Coarse hierarchical K-means clustering (16 fine → 8 coarse centers) or a Multi-Step iterative clustering that uses prior-step results as priors, ensuring semantically cohesive tokens from the same dataset are routed to dedicated experts. The final feature is the sum of STE, SEE, and S²E outputs (Eq. 1), so shared experts provide a universal latent space while S²E captures source-specific discriminative patterns. Training uses a dual-constrained loss combining a traceability loss (cross-entropy enforcing intra-source routing consistency) and a load-balancing loss (coefficient-of-variation penalties on expert importance and activation load) to prevent expert collapse and ensure balanced utilization.

## Results

Under the inter-organ integration paradigm (training on all 8 heterogeneous US datasets), COME with Fine2Coarse clustering achieves a mean AP of 0.6587, outperforming the strongest baseline CerberusDet (0.6268) by 3.19% and DINO (0.5792) by 7.95%. Under the intra-organ paradigm, COME (Multi-Step) reaches 0.6474 mean AP, a 5.14% improvement over DAMEX (0.5941) and 5.33% over DINO (0.5732). In the domain generalization experiment (train on BUSI only, test on 3 unseen breast datasets), COME achieves a +32.8% aggregate mean AP gain over DINO across the three held-out datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COME consistently outperforms all baselines in both multi-dataset paradigms. In the inter-organ setting, COME (Fine2Coarse) achieves 0.6587 mean AP, beating CerberusDet (0.6268, +3.19%), DAMEX (0.6167, +4.20%), DINO-MoE (0.6077, +5.10%), and DINO (0.5792, +7.95%). In the intra-organ setting, COME (Multi-Step) reaches 0.6474, surpassing CerberusDet (0.5960, +5.14%) and DAMEX (0.5941, +5.33%). The single-dataset baselines (DINO at 0.6125, YOLOv10 at 0.5532, Open-GroundingDINO at 0.4822) are not directly comparable to COME's multi-dataset training but serve as architectural references; notably, COME's inter-organ mean AP exceeds even the best single-dataset DINO by 4.62%. In the domain generalization test (train on BUSI, zero-shot on BUV/BUSBRA/BUSC), COME achieves 0.297 AP on BUSC versus DINO's 0.043 and DAMEX's 0.115, demonstrating substantially stronger cross-dataset transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

COME extends the DINO-ViT detection backbone by replacing standard FFN layers in the decoder with a Mixture-of-Experts module, building on the MoE paradigm popularized by DeepSeekMoE and DAMEX. It departs from prior multi-dataset medical imaging approaches (SLDG, MedUniSeg, MVFA, Datasetg, MapSeg, MiDSS) that require separate encoders, task-specific heads, test-source knowledge, or fine-tuning, by introducing dual frozen shared experts (USFM for structural priors, MedCLIP-SAMv2 for semantic alignment) that provide a universal feature space without any of those constraints. The clustering-based token routing (Fine2Coarse and Multi-Step K-means) and the traceability loss for enforcing expert-source correspondence are novel architectural choices not present in prior MoE-for-detection work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical clinical gap: single-dataset ultrasound detectors fail catastrophically on unseen data (e.g., DINO drops below 0.2 AP on most datasets), and the proposed universal framework offers a source-agnostic alternative without requiring test-time adaptation. However, the evaluation is confined to breast and thyroid ultrasound (8 datasets), and the reliance on two large frozen pre-trained models (USFM, MedCLIP-SAMv2) introduces significant dependency and limits interpretability of where gains originate. Open questions include whether the dual shared expert + clustering routing design generalizes to other modalities (CT, MRI), whether the ad hoc clustering hyperparameters (16→8 centers, 5-step iteration) transfer across scales, and whether the approach scales to truly heterogeneous multi-organ settings beyond the two organs tested.
