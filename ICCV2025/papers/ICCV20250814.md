## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation methods for vision-language models construct prototypes exclusively from textual class names, which suffer from semantic ambiguities (e.g., 'sword lily' vs. 'blackberry lily' share 0.67 cosine similarity in CLIP space). This textual-only representation is insufficient to capture distinguishing visual concepts, limiting classification accuracy especially on fine-grained and semantically overlapping categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250814/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a streaming sequence of unlabeled test images, each augmented into N=50 views (random crop, flip, resize), alongside C class names expanded into M=50 LLM-generated descriptions via GPT-3.5. It outputs a per-class prediction probability computed as a softmax over entropic OT distances between the image distribution and each class's multimodal prototype distribution, from which the argmax class label is derived. The visual cache within each prototype is initialized from the mean of textual descriptions before any test image arrives, then updated online as the stream progresses.

## Method

ProtoMM models both the test image and each class prototype as discrete distributions in the shared CLIP embedding space, with importance weights assigned via entropy-based attention that down-weights uninformative augmentations. The key mechanism is a two-stage process: (1) Distributed Feature Construction expands each prototype from M textual descriptions to M+S points by appending S visual particles, and (2) Multimodal Prototype Learning solves an entropic OT problem (via Sinkhorn) between the image distribution and each prototype distribution to obtain a transport plan. This transport plan serves dual purposes: it computes the prediction score (Eq. 9) and identifies high-quality augmentations (scored by Θ_t = T_tc · w_{y_t}) that are used to update the visual particles via a weighted averaging rule (Eq. 10), gated by a confidence threshold τ=0.8. Unlike AWT, which uses OT purely for prediction-time alignment, ProtoMM leverages the transport plan to dynamically accumulate visual knowledge into textual prototypes across the test stream, progressively reducing distribution discrepancy (KL divergence drops from 18.7 to 9.5 over 10,000 samples on ImageNet).

## Results

On the 11-dataset cross-domain benchmark (Table 1), ProtoMM achieves 63.10% average accuracy with RN50 and 70.70% with ViT-B/16, outperforming the closest training-free baseline AWT by 0.93% and 0.42% respectively. On the 5-dataset OOD benchmark (Table 2), ProtoMM reaches 48.21% (RN50) and 65.69% (ViT-B/16) average accuracy, surpassing DPE by 1.03% and DOTA by 1.17% on the ViT-B/16 OOD average. The largest single-dataset gain is on Flowers102 with ViT-B/16, where ProtoMM scores 77.40% versus AWT's 75.07% (+2.33%). Inference time is 21 minutes on ImageNet (RN50, single RTX 3090), comparable to TDA (17 min) but far faster than TPT (14h 30min) or DiffTPT (37h 25min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProtoMM outperforms all 11 baselines on the 11-dataset average for both backbones. Against the closest training-free competitor AWT [65], ProtoMM gains +0.93% (RN50) and +0.42% (ViT-B/16) on the cross-domain average, with the largest margin on Flowers102 (+1.74% RN50, +2.33% ViT-B/16). Versus the other training-free methods TDA [21] and DOTA [14], ProtoMM improves by +2.04% and +0.94% (RN50) respectively. Against the training-based DPE [55], which requires per-sample adapter tuning, ProtoMM still leads by +1.08% (ViT-B/16) while being ~8× faster (21 min vs 2h 40min on ImageNet). Prompt-tuning methods (TPT, DiffTPT) and prompt-learning methods (CoOp, CoCoOp, CuPL, VisDesc) are all surpassed by 2–6% on average. The only dataset where ProtoMM does not rank first is Pets with RN50 (87.92% vs AWT's 88.22%), a marginal 0.3% gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProtoMM extends the test-time adaptation (TTA) line of work for VLMs (TPT, TDA, DOTA, DPE) by shifting from purely textual prototype learning to multimodal prototype construction. It builds most directly on AWT (Zhu et al., NeurIPS 2024), which also formulates image-text matching as an optimal transport problem, but departs from AWT by introducing dynamically updated visual particles that accumulate discriminative information from the test stream. The use of Sinkhorn-regularized OT for computing transport plans between augmented image views and multimodal prototypes follows the broader OT-for-VLMs trend (PLOT, AWT), while the LLM-generated textual descriptions draw on MetaPrompting and VisDescs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated failure mode—class-name ambiguity in CLIP's embedding space—by showing that visual evidence from the test stream can progressively disambiguate similar classes without any gradient updates. The training-free, streaming nature makes it practical for deployment scenarios where retraining is infeasible. However, the method depends on GPT-3.5 for textual augmentation, assumes a streaming test protocol, and the gains over the closest competitor (AWT) are modest (~1% on ImageNet), leaving open whether the dynamic visual cache provides substantial benefit over simpler caching strategies at scale. The authors note future directions toward video understanding and more efficient prototype learning.
