## Problem

Latent diffusion models are trained in two separate stages: first a VAE tokenizer is trained for reconstruction, then the diffusion model is trained with the VAE frozen. This two-stage pipeline raises the question of whether the VAE and diffusion model can be jointly optimized end-to-end to improve final generation performance. The paper shows that naively backpropagating the diffusion loss to the VAE is ineffective—it causes the latent space to collapse into a simpler structure that is easier to denoise but produces worse images—yet end-to-end training can be unlocked by using the representation-alignment (REPA) loss as the gradient signal to the VAE instead.

## Input / Output

The model takes random Gaussian noise and an ImageNet class label as input and generates 256×256 RGB images. During training, images are center-cropped and resized to 256×256 following the ADM protocol. A key architectural detail is a batch-norm layer inserted between the VAE encoder and the diffusion transformer, which adaptively normalizes VAE latents using running statistics (EMA mean/variance) rather than fixed precomputed dataset-level statistics, enabling the VAE to be updated without expensive recomputation at each step.

## Method

REPA-E replaces the diffusion loss as the end-to-end gradient signal to the VAE with the REPA representation-alignment loss, which aligns diffusion transformer hidden states with frozen DINOv2 features via patch-wise cosine similarity. The diffusion loss is retained but applied with a stop-gradient so it only updates the diffusion model parameters, preventing the latent-space collapse observed when diffusion gradients reach the VAE. A batch-norm layer between VAE and LDM provides differentiable, adaptive latent normalization, and VAE regularization losses (MSE, LPIPS, GAN, KL divergence) preserve reconstruction quality during joint tuning. The total training objective is L_DIFF(θ) + λ·L_REPA(θ,φ,ω) + η·L_REG(φ), where θ, φ, ω are diffusion model, VAE, and REPA projection parameters respectively. This simple recipe accelerates training by over 17× and 45× over REPA and vanilla baselines while also improving the VAE's latent-space structure in a model-agnostic manner.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 (without classifier-free guidance), REPA-E with SiT-XL achieves gFID 4.07 at 80 epochs (400K steps), surpassing vanilla REPA at 800 epochs (gFID 5.90) by a 17× training speedup and beating FasterDiT at 400 epochs (gFID 7.91) by a 5× speedup. With classifier-free guidance, the method reaches a state-of-the-art gFID of 1.26 (and 1.83 without guidance). The end-to-end tuned VAE also improves downstream generation: E2E-VAE achieves gFID-50K of 3.46 with SiT-XL+REPA, outperforming VA-VAE (4.88) and SD-VAE (7.90) at the same 80-epoch budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REPA-E at 80 epochs (gFID 4.07) decisively outperforms vanilla REPA at 800 epochs (gFID 5.90), achieving a 17× training speedup while improving gFID by 1.83 points. It also surpasses FasterDiT at 400 epochs (gFID 7.91) by 3.84 gFID points with only 1/5 the training, and beats SiT (gFID 8.61) and DiT (gFID 9.62) at 1400 epochs by 4.54 and 5.55 gFID points respectively. Against MaskDiT at 1600 epochs (gFID 5.69), REPA-E at 80 epochs is still 1.62 gFID points better with 20× fewer epochs. In the VAE downstream comparison (Table 8), the E2E-tuned VA-VAE (E2E-VAE) outperforms both VA-VAE and SD-VAE across all LDM architectures: with SiT-XL+REPA, E2E-VAE reaches gFID-50K 3.46 vs. 4.88 (VA-VAE) and 7.90 (SD-VAE); with DiT-XL+REPA, it reaches 4.20 vs. 4.71 (VA-VAE) and 12.29 (SD-VAE). The gains are consistent across model scales (SiT-B/L/XL), VAE architectures (SD-VAE, IN-VAE, VA-VAE), and representation encoders (DINOv2-B/L, CLIP-L, I-JEPA-H), with percentage gFID improvements over REPA actually increasing with model size (29.6% → 32.3% → 34.0%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on REPA (Yu et al., 2024), which introduced representation-alignment loss to accelerate diffusion transformer training, by making that loss end-to-end: backpropagating it through the VAE rather than treating the tokenizer as frozen. It departs from the standard two-stage LDM paradigm (train VAE, then train diffusion model) and from LSGM's joint-training approach, which used a variational lower bound with an entropy term to prevent latent collapse. The key conceptual departure is showing that the diffusion loss itself is the wrong signal for updating the VAE—it encourages a degenerate, low-variance latent space—while the REPA alignment score serves as a viable proxy that preserves generation quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 17× and 45× training speedups over REPA and vanilla recipes, respectively, are practically significant for the LDM community, and the finding that the end-to-end-tuned VAE serves as a drop-in replacement with improved downstream generation broadens the impact beyond the specific training recipe. The adaptive improvement of latent-space structure (reducing high-frequency noise in SD-VAE, adding detail to over-smoothed VA-VAE) suggests a general principle for tokenizer-diffusion co-optimization. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to text-to-image, video, or higher-resolution settings, and whether the specific hyperparameters (λ_REPA_g=0.5, λ_REPA_v=1.5) generalize across domains. All experiments are confined to ImageNet 256×256 with SiT/DiT architectures, limiting confidence in broader applicability.
