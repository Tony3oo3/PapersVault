## Problem

It remains unclear whether explicit, structured object representations (e.g., bounding boxes with trajectories) are still necessary in multimodal LLMs, given that pre-trained visual encoders may implicitly capture object information and text captions already achieve strong video understanding performance. The paper investigates how and whether object-centric spatial information can be integrated into MLLMs in a data-efficient manner to improve spatiotemporal reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252235/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes video clips (10 uniformly sampled frames for CLIP embeddings, 6 for captions), a text question, and object bounding boxes extracted by YOLO-World and tracked by SAM 2. Bounding boxes are downsampled per video to keep the language-based representation under 1,000 tokens, with coordinates quantized to integers in [0,100]. The output is a free-form or multiple-choice text answer to the video question.

## Method

ObjectMLLM integrates object bounding boxes into an LLM backbone via two adapters: a language-based (symbolic) adapter that renders quantized box coordinates as plain text tokens, and an embedding projector that maps 4D normalized coordinates through a linear layer into the LLM's input space. The framework also accepts video frame captions (from LLaVA-1.5-13B) and visual embeddings (from CLIP ViT-L/14) as additional modalities, with a modality-by-modality fine-tuning strategy (captions → boxes → visual embeddings) using LLaMA-Adapter or LoRA. The key finding is that the symbolic representation consistently outperforms the embedding projector across all data budgets, particularly on motion-related questions, because it reuses the LLM's existing vocabulary and spatial language priors rather than requiring the model to learn a new embedding space from scratch.

## Results

On CLEVRER-MC, ObjectMLLM (VideoLLaMA2 backbone) achieves 77.6% accuracy, substantially outperforming VideoLLaMA2 (45.6%) and LLaVA-Next-Video-DPO (38.4%). On Perception Test, ObjectMLLM reaches 66.6% (VideoLLaMA2) and 65.7% (LLaMA3), surpassing SeViLA (62.0%) and Vamos (62.3%). On NExT-QA and IntentQA, ObjectMLLM (VideoLLaMA2) scores 78.5% and 75.5% respectively, edging out Vamos (77.3%, 74.2%) and VideoLLaMA2 (74.1%, 73.8%). The LLaMA3-based variant achieves 75.5% on CLEVRER-MC and 65.7% on Perception Test without any pre-trained visual adapter.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ObjectMLLM (VideoLLaMA2) achieves the largest margin on CLEVRER-MC, scoring 77.6% versus 45.6% for VideoLLaMA2 and 38.4% for LLaVA-Next-Video-DPO, a gap of over 30 points that highlights the weakness of distributed visual embeddings in capturing object motion and collision. On Perception Test, ObjectMLLM (66.6%) outperforms SeViLA (62.0%) by 4.6 points and Vamos (62.3%) by 4.3 points, with the gains concentrated on motion-related question types. On STAR, ObjectMLLM (67.2%) narrowly beats ViLA (67.1%) and clearly surpasses SeViLA (64.9%) and Vamos (63.7%). On NExT-QA, ObjectMLLM (VideoLLaMA2) leads with 78.5%, ahead of Vamos (77.3%) and ViLA (75.6%), while the LLaMA3 variant (76.6%) trails Vamos (77.3%) by 0.7 points. On IntentQA, ObjectMLLM (VideoLLaMA2) achieves 75.5% versus 74.2% for Vamos and 73.8% for VideoLLaMA2, a modest but consistent improvement. Overall, ObjectMLLM wins or ties on every benchmark where a direct comparison is available, with the most dramatic gains on spatiotemporal reasoning tasks (CLEVRER-MC, Perception Test) and smaller margins on causal/action-reasoning tasks (NExT-QA, IntentQA).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of caption-enhanced video MLLMs (Vamos, Socratic Models) and object-aware vision-language models (OSCAR, VinVL, CoVLM), but departs from both by asking how structured object-centric representations (bounding boxes from off-the-shelf detectors and trackers) can be integrated into MLLMs in a data-efficient way. It builds directly on Vamos's caption-based pipeline and VideoLLaMA2's pre-trained visual adapter, adding a third input modality: object bounding boxes. The key conceptual departure is from the standard MLLM modality-adaptation paradigm (LLaVA, BLIP-2), which assumes continuous embeddings projected via learned layers are the natural interface; ObjectMLLM instead tests whether rendering coordinates as plain text tokens is a viable and superior alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a trivial text-rendering of bounding box coordinates outperforms a learned embedding projector across all data budgets challenges the prevailing assumption in MLLM design that continuous distributed representations are inherently more expressive and useful. Practically, this means new computer vision modules (detectors, pose estimators, segmentation) can be plugged into LLMs with minimal fine-tuning, lowering the barrier to multi-module video understanding. Limitations include the dependence on a separate detection/tracking pipeline, modest gains on causal-reasoning benchmarks (NExT-QA, IntentQA), and the fact that the approach still requires some fine-tuning to be effective. Open questions include whether zero-shot integration is possible with stronger base models, and how far the symbolic-representation advantage extends to richer structures like 3D point clouds or panoptic segmentation.
