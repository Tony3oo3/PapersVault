## Problem

Existing federated unsupervised learning methods achieve good intra-client (local) uniformity but fail to maintain inter-client (global) uniformity after model aggregation, because non-IID data distributions cause divergent client updates and the server cannot directly impose cross-client uniformity constraints without access to raw data. This paper formalizes the inter-client uniformity gap and asks how to improve it without additional communication overhead or privacy leakage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251323/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives unlabeled RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet-200), applies two independent augmentations per sample, and produces a d-dimensional representation vector from a ResNet-18 encoder. The server only exchanges model parameters and lightweight per-client dimension-scaling vectors (no raw data or embeddings are shared), preserving privacy. Downstream evaluation uses the frozen encoder representations for linear probing or fine-tuning with 1% or 10% labeled data.

## Method

SSD introduces two complementary losses on top of standard alignment and uniformity objectives. First, Dimension-Scaled Regularization (DSR) assigns each client a unique scaling vector that multiplies a disjoint subset of embedding dimensions by a factor α (default 10); a stop-gradient L2 loss then pulls each client's embeddings toward their scaled versions, softly steering different clients' representations toward distinct angular regions of the unit hypersphere without hard subspace partitioning. Second, Projector Distillation (PD) minimizes the KL divergence between softmax distributions of encoder representations and projector embeddings, ensuring that the uniformity gains achieved at the embedding level are internalized by the encoder rather than absorbed by the projector. The full per-client loss is L_align + β·L_uniform + γ·L_DSR + δ·L_distill, trained with standard federated averaging (weighted by local dataset size) across cross-silo (K=10) and cross-device (K=50, 20% participation) settings.

## Results

SSD achieves the highest linear probing accuracy across all four federated settings: 81.32% on CIFAR-10 Cross-Silo, 71.83% on CIFAR-10 Cross-Device, 57.38% on CIFAR-100 Cross-Silo, and 45.21% on CIFAR-100 Cross-Device. It also leads in fine-tuning with 1% labels (e.g., 70.74% on CIFAR-10 Cross-Silo vs. 69.62% for the next-best FedU2) and 10% labels (e.g., 81.67% on CIFAR-10 Cross-Silo). In representation quality, SSD attains the best uniformity (−L_uniform = 3.84) and effective rank among all federated methods, confirming that inter-client uniformity improvements translate to downstream gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSD outperforms all five baselines on the majority of metrics across all four federated settings. The closest competitor is FedU2 (CVPR 2024), which addresses both divergence control and local dimensional collapse; SSD edges it out by +0.31 LP and +1.12 FT1% on CIFAR-10 Cross-Silo, and by +2.31 LP and +1.62 FT1% on CIFAR-100 Cross-Device, though FedU2 narrowly leads on CIFAR-100 Cross-Silo LP (57.40 vs. 57.38). FedDecorr (ICLR 2023), which mitigates local dimensional collapse via decorrelation, is competitive on CIFAR-100 Cross-Device LP (44.74) but still trails SSD by 0.47 points. FedX (ECCV 2022) and L-DAWA (ICCV 2023), which focus on global model consistency via cross-client distillation and layer-wise divergence-aware aggregation respectively, perform the worst overall—L-DAWA drops to 67.65% LP on CIFAR-10 Cross-Device, a 4.18-point gap behind SSD. The consistent pattern across settings confirms that explicitly targeting inter-client uniformity (as SSD does) yields greater gains than either local uniformity alone or global consistency constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Federated Unsupervised Learning (FUL) by identifying a gap that prior methods leave unaddressed: while FedProx-style approaches (FedU, FedEMA, FedX, L-DAWA) constrain local updates to reduce divergence, and dimensional-collapse methods (FedDecorr, FedU2) improve intra-client uniformity, neither line explicitly optimizes inter-client uniformity after aggregation. The proposed Dimension-Scaled Regularization draws on the alignment-uniformity framework of Wang & Isola and the projector's role in BYOL-style learning, but repurposes them for a federated setting by assigning non-overlapping scaled dimensions per client. The Projector Distillation module borrows the KL-divergence distillation mechanism from knowledge distillation literature to bridge the well-known gap between embedding-space optimization and encoder-level representation quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formal decomposition of the uniformity loss into intra-client and inter-client components provides a clear diagnostic for why existing FUL methods underperform globally under non-IID data, and the soft-separation solution is lightweight (no extra communication rounds, no raw data sharing). However, the empirical gains are modest (≈0.3–1% on linear probing) and evaluated only on CIFAR-10/100 and TinyImageNet-200 with ResNet-18, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger backbones, more diverse datasets, or non-contrastive objectives. A key open question is whether the dimension-scaled regularization generalizes beyond the specific α and dimension-assignment scheme tested, and whether the projector distillation is necessary for other self-supervised paradigms (e.g., masked autoencoding) where the projector plays a different role.
