## Problem

Existing incremental unlearning (IU) methods achieve only superficial forgetting by suppressing parameters or confusing predictions without explicit constraints on feature representations and gradient updates, leaving residual class-specific information that is partially recoverable via feature reconstruction or head retraining. This incomplete forgetting risks security breaches and causes degraded features to entangle with remaining-class features, destabilizing decision boundaries and amplifying error propagation across sequential deletion requests.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252622/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained vision model (ViT-B/16 for classification on ImageNet-100/CUB-200/Omnibenchmark, or Face Transformer for face recognition on CASIA-Face100/MS-Celeb-100) and a sequence of unlearning tasks, each specifying a subset of classes to forget (e.g., 20 classes per task over 4 sequential tasks). It produces an unlearned model with only 1.28% tunable parameters (LoRA modules) that irreversibly forgets target classes while preserving remaining-class accuracy, evaluated via H-Mean, per-class accuracy, and recovery-resistance metrics (DIP reconstruction SSIM/PSNR, head-retraining recovery).

## Method

FG-OrIU enforces dual orthogonal constraints at both feature and gradient levels through three stages. First, SVD decomposes each layer's feature matrix into a forgetting subspace S_f (dominant singular vectors of forgetting-class features) and a remaining subspace S_r. Second, during training with frozen backbone and LoRA adapters, a feature orthogonal projection loss minimizes the projection of forgetting-class features onto S_f (erasing discriminative patterns) while a complementary loss minimizes the projection residual of remaining-class features from S_r (preserving fidelity); simultaneously, a gradient orthogonal projection removes gradient components orthogonal to S_f (amplifying updates in forgetting-critical directions) and removes components along S_r (preventing contamination of retained features). Third, for sequential tasks, dynamic subspace adaptation expands S_f by projecting out already-present bases before SVD to add only new orthogonal directions, while contracting S_r to remove overlapping features, ensuring cumulative irreversible forgetting without degrading retained performance.

## Results

On the incremental unlearning (IU) task over 4 sequential tasks (20 classes removed per task) on CASIA-Face100, FG-OrIU achieves H-Mean of 77.71% and remaining-class accuracy of 80.51% by Task 4, while maintaining 0.00% accuracy on all forgetting and old-forgetting classes. On ImageNet100 (same IU protocol), FG-OrIU reaches H-Mean of 89.15% and Acc_r of 92.10% at Task 4. In the static MU setting on CASIA-Face100 with 90 classes removed, FG-OrIU attains H-Mean of 75.49% and Acc_r of 77.15%, outperforming the strongest baseline GS-LoRA++ (72.85% H, 71.86% Acc_r) by 2.64% and 5.29% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FG-OrIU outperforms all 10 baselines across both IU benchmarks. Against the closest competitors GS-LoRA and GS-LoRA++ (designed specifically for IU), FG-OrIU gains 2.48% and 2.48% in H-Mean at Task 4 on CASIA-Face100 (77.71 vs. 73.76 and 75.23), with a 5.15% improvement in Acc_r over GS-LoRA (80.51 vs. 75.36). On ImageNet100, the advantage over GS-LoRA++ narrows to 2.15% in H (89.15 vs. 87.00) but FG-OrIU still leads in Acc_r by 4.6% (92.10 vs. 87.50). Continual learning baselines (EWC, MAS, LwF, DER, FDR) achieve lower H-Mean (67–73 range) and, critically, DER retains 10.40% Acc_f at Task 1 on ImageNet100, indicating incomplete forgetting. MU-specific baselines SCRUB-S and LIRF show degrading forgetting over sequential tasks: SCRUB-S's Acc_o rises to 6.47% by Task 4 on CASIA-Face100, while LIRF maintains 45.34% Acc_f and 27.96% Acc_o, confirming superficial forgetting. BAD-T achieves moderate H (72.71 at Task 4) but lags FG-OrIU by 5% in H and 10% in Acc_r.

## Relation to Prior Work

FG-OrIU repurposes orthogonal projection ideas from two adjacent domains—concept erasure in diffusion models (orthogonalizing sensitive token embeddings) and gradient projection in continual/multi-task learning (PCGrad, GPM)—and unifies them for the first time in the machine unlearning setting. It departs from prior IU methods (GS-LoRA, SCRUB, LIRF) that constrain only final outputs or apply parameter suppression, by explicitly decomposing feature spaces via SVD and enforcing dual orthogonal constraints at both the forward (feature) and backward (gradient) levels. The use of LoRA for parameter-efficient updates follows the GS-LoRA line, but the SVD-based subspace decomposition and dynamic adaptation across sequential tasks are novel contributions not present in prior unlearning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central empirical finding—that widely-used unlearning methods achieve only 'superficial forgetting' where features remain reconstructable via DIP and recoverable by retraining a classification head—challenges the field's implicit assumption that output-level suppression constitutes genuine erasure. This reframes how unlearning effectiveness should be evaluated and motivates feature-level guarantees. Limitations include the requirement of access to forgetting-class data for SVD decomposition, restriction to class-level (not sample-level) unlearning, and evaluation limited to vision transformers on moderate-scale benchmarks. Open questions include whether the orthogonal constraint framework extends to LLMs or generative models, and whether the SVD-based subspace identification remains stable under distribution shift.
