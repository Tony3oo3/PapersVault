## Problem

Existing referring expression comprehension models and benchmarks assume a one-to-one mapping between a description and a single object, which fails to reflect real-world scenarios where a natural language description can match multiple individuals. State-of-the-art MLLMs that score well on RefCOCO/+/g exhibit severe recall degradation on multi-instance referring and hallucinate bounding boxes when the target is absent, making them impractical for applications like human-robot interaction and industrial automation.

## Input / Output

The model takes a high-resolution RGB image (minimum 1000×1000 pixels, typically ~1432×1074) and a free-form natural language referring expression as input. DINO-X first detects all person bounding boxes in the image, and the LLM then outputs a sequence of object index tokens (e.g., `<objm>...<objn>`) selecting which detected boxes match the description, supporting zero, one, or multiple matches. The output format is structured as `<g>referring</g><o><objm>...<objn></o>`, and for rejection cases the model should produce no box indices rather than hallucinating a detection.

## Method

RexSeek formulates referring as a retrieval task over pre-detected person boxes rather than regressing coordinates: a dual vision encoder (CLIP for low-resolution global features + ConvNeXt for high-resolution local features) produces vision tokens, while DINO-X generates person bounding boxes whose RoI features (extracted via multi-scale RoI Align) are augmented with sinusoidal positional embeddings to form object tokens. These three token streams (vision, object, text) are projected through separate MLPs and fed into Qwen2.5, which decodes the indices of matching persons. Training proceeds in four stages—modality alignment via image captioning, perception training on detection/grounding data, general multimodal understanding, and final fine-tuning on HumanRef—progressively building detection and language comprehension before specializing to referring. The accompanying HumanRef dataset (103K expressions, avg. 2.2 instances per expression) is annotated via a property-listing → property-assignment → LLM-rewriting pipeline that explicitly supports multi-instance referring, multi-instance discrimination, and rejection of non-existent targets.

## Results

On the HumanRef benchmark, RexSeek-7B achieves an average DensityF1 of 82.3 (R=85.9, P=85.8), substantially outperforming the next-best model Molmo-7B-D* (DF1=72.6, using the less strict point-in-mask metric) and all other MLLM-based methods. RexSeek also attains a rejection score of 54.1, far exceeding most competitors (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B: 7.1, DeepSeek-VL2-small: 3.1, ChatRex-7B: 0.0), demonstrating its ability to refuse hallucinated detections. On zero-shot RefCOCOg, RexSeek achieves 84.0/84.4 (val/test), competitive with but slightly below ChatRex-7B (88.8/88.6) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B (87.2/87.2), despite being trained exclusively on human referring data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RexSeek-7B outperforms all compared MLLM-based methods on the HumanRef benchmark by a wide margin: it surpasses Molmo-7B-D* (DF1 72.6, though evaluated with the looser point-in-mask criterion) by ~10 points in DF1, and beats DeepSeek-VL2-small (59.5), Qwen2.5-VL-7B (56.2), and ChatRex-7B (55.6) by 23–48 points. The gap is most pronounced in recall: RexSeek achieves 85.9% average recall versus 59.3% for DeepSeek-VL2-small and 50.4% for ChatRex-7B, confirming that existing models fail to detect all matching instances in multi-instance referring. On the rejection subset, RexSeek (54.1) dramatically outperforms most baselines (0–7.1), though Molmo-7B-D* (68.6) and InternVL-2.5-B8 (54.9) are exceptions. On zero-shot RefCOCOg, RexSeek (84.4 test) trails ChatRex-7B (88.6) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B (87.2) by 4–5 points, but this is notable given RexSeek was never trained on general object referring data. DINO-X, used as the person detector backbone, achieves high recall (75.2) but very low precision (33.1) and DF1 (23.3) on HumanRef, illustrating that detection alone without language comprehension is insufficient for referring.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ChatRex's retrieval-based formulation of referring expression comprehension, where a detector proposes bounding boxes and an LLM selects relevant indices, but departs from the dominant one-to-one REC paradigm (RefCOCO/+/g) by explicitly formulating multi-instance referring, multi-instance discrimination, and rejection of non-existence as first-class requirements. The annotation methodology also breaks from the ReferItGame two-player protocol, instead using structured property dictionaries composed by an LLM into natural language expressions. By integrating DINO-X as a dedicated person detector with Qwen2.5 as the language backbone, RexSeek occupies a hybrid position between open-vocabulary detection and MLLM-based grounding, inheriting strengths of both while addressing their respective weaknesses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a significant practical gap: models achieving 87–88% on RefCOCO/+/g drop to 30–70% DensityF1 on HumanRef, primarily because they were never trained to output multiple boxes per expression or to reject non-existent targets. The DensityF1 metric and the rejection subset address two failure modes (over-detection and hallucination) that are critical for real-world deployment in robotics, surveillance, and healthcare. Open questions include whether the multi-stage training recipe generalizes to other entity categories beyond humans, whether the retrieval-based formulation scales to very crowded scenes (hundreds of persons), and whether the rejection capability transfers to video or streaming settings where the target may appear and disappear.
