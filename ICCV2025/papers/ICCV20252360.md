## Problem

Personalizing text-to-image diffusion models introduces an inherent trade-off between subject fidelity (alignment with the target concept) and text editability (preserving the original model's broad knowledge). Existing sampling guidance methods fail to balance these: Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) restricts adaptation to the target distribution, while AutoGuidance (AG) enhances subject fidelity but compromises text alignment. No prior guidance technique can steer outputs toward a well-balanced latent space that simultaneously optimizes both objectives.

## Input / Output

At inference time, the method consumes a text prompt along with the weights of both the pre-trained and fine-tuned diffusion models, performing weight interpolation between them to construct a weak model for guidance. It produces personalized RGB images at 512×512 (SD 1.5, SD 2.1) or 1024×1024 (SANA) resolution, with 20–50 DDIM sampling steps. Crucially, the guidance introduces no additional forward passes beyond standard CFG, so inference cost remains unchanged.

## Method

Personalization Guidance modifies CFG by replacing the fine-tuned model's unconditional noise prediction ε_θ'(x_t|∅) with the pre-trained model's ε_θ(x_t|∅), so the guidance term ε_θ'(x_t|c) − ε_θ(x_t|∅) explicitly encodes the knowledge gained during fine-tuning. To dynamically control the degree of 'unlearning' in this weak model, the authors introduce weight interpolation θ_ω = ωθ' + (1−ω)θ (ω ∈ [0,1]), where ω=1 recovers standard CFG and ω=0 uses the fully pre-trained model. This parameter-space interpolation steers the guidance direction in a way that is distinct from output-space combinations of CFG and AG, because neural networks are non-linear in their parameters. The scalar ω acts as a single inference-time knob to trade off subject fidelity against text fidelity without retraining, and the approach integrates seamlessly with any fine-tuning strategy (DB-LoRA, ClassDiffusion, Textual Inversion, Diffusion-DPO, PairCustom, InstructPix2Pix).

## Results

On the ViCo dataset with DB-LoRA, Personalization Guidance (PG) with ω=0 achieves DINO scores of 0.4985 (SD 1.5), 0.5248 (SD 2.1), and 0.5366 (SANA), outperforming CFG (0.4701, 0.4976, 0.4819) and AG (0.4831, 0.5072, 0.4792) on subject fidelity across all three base models. Text fidelity (CLIP-T) is largely preserved: PG(ω=0.6) scores 0.3288, 0.3277, and 0.3357 on SD 1.5, SD 2.1, and SANA respectively, within ~0.005 of CFG. The largest gain is on SANA, where PG(ω=0) improves DINO by 0.055 over CFG (0.5366 vs. 0.4819) while matching its CLIP-T score (0.3357 vs. 0.3363).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CFG [10], PG wins on both subject-fidelity metrics (DINO and CLIP-I) across all three base models, with the largest DINO gain on SANA (+0.055) and SD 2.1 (+0.027); CFG retains a slight edge on CLIP-T (e.g., 0.3345 vs. 0.3288 on SD 1.5), but PG(ω=0.6) narrows this gap to <0.006. Against AG [18], PG dominates on DINO and CLIP-I for every model (e.g., 0.5366 vs. 0.4792 on SANA) while also matching or exceeding AG on CLIP-T, confirming that AG's lack of text conditioning is a genuine weakness. Against SAG [4] (evaluated in Table 2 for methods that fine-tune text embeddings), PG achieves substantially higher DINO scores—0.5700 vs. 0.5172 for ClassDiffusion on SD 1.5 and 0.6166 vs. 0.5783 on SD 2.1—while keeping CLIP-T within 0.001–0.005 of SAG. In the user study (Table 3), PG is preferred for subject fidelity 55.2% of the time (SD 2.1 + ClassDiffusion) and 68.3% (SANA + DB-LoRA), far exceeding CFG (10.5% / 2.5%) and CFG+AG (12.6% / 2.2%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established guidance paradigms for diffusion models: Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG), which uses the model's own unconditional prediction as a weak model, and AutoGuidance (AG), which uses a structurally weaker model conditioned on the same prompt. The key departure is recognizing that both formulations are suboptimal for personalized models: CFG's weak model (the fine-tuned model with null text) has already absorbed target-distribution knowledge, limiting further subject fidelity gains, while AG's weak model (the pre-trained model with the same text) ignores the text prompt entirely. The proposed Personalization Guidance simply swaps in the pre-trained model's null-text prediction as the weak model and introduces weight interpolation between pre-trained and fine-tuned parameters to dial the degree of 'unlearning,' a mechanism borrowed from weight-space ensembling literature (WISE-FT, Model Soups) but repurposed for inference-time steering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The subject-fidelity vs. text-editability trade-off in personalized diffusion models has been addressed primarily at training time (regularization, fewer steps, parameter reduction), leaving inference-time control largely unexplored. This work shows that a zero-cost, inference-time weight interpolation can meaningfully rebalance the two objectives without retraining, and that the technique generalizes beyond personalization to preference optimization (Diffusion-DPO), style transfer (PairCustom), and instruction-based editing (InstructPix2Pix). Open questions include whether the optimal interpolation weight ω is stable across concepts and prompt types, whether the approach extends to video diffusion or multi-concept personalization, and whether the linear weight-space interpolation is truly optimal compared to more sophisticated ensembling strategies in the non-linear parameter space.
