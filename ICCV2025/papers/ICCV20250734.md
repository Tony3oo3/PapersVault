## Problem

Existing object navigation methods—both modular and end-to-end—deploy fixed models at test time, leaving them unable to adapt to unfamiliar layouts and novel object appearances in unseen environments. While meta-learning has been explored for visual recognition and detection, there is no systematic meta-learning framework for the more complex, real-time task of object navigation that enables the agent to learn dynamically during deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250734/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each time step the agent receives RGB-D sensor input, odometer readings, and a goal category label (e.g., 'bed'), and outputs one of four discrete actions: MOVE_FORWARD, TURN_LEFT, TURN_RIGHT, or STOP. The episode terminates when the agent reaches within 1.0 m of the target and issues STOP, or after a 500-step budget. Crucially, the agent's own accumulated trajectory τ_t = (s_0, …, s_t) is consumed as an additional input to the meta-learning decoder during on-the-go adaptation, making the method self-supervised with respect to interaction data.

## Method

LOG learns a central target-conditioned trajectory distribution p_θ(τ|o) as a prior via meta-learning, on the assumption that environment-specific trajectory distributions are all close to this central distribution. Exact inference is intractable, so the authors reformulate the KL-alignment objective with an ELBO, decomposing the prior into a probabilistic decoder p_θ(o|τ) (a Gaussian centered on a deterministic encoding g_θ(τ)) and a trajectory prior p_θ(τ) induced by a meta-initialized navigation model f_θ. The inner loop adapts environment-specific parameters f_θ_i by maximizing log p_θ(o|τ) (expanded as a telescoping series over trajectory steps), while the outer loop refines f_θ and g_θ using a navigation loss that combines the base method's objective (A3C reward for RL, prediction-map consistency for modular) with a decoder alignment term. The mechanism is pluggable: for end-to-end RL (DAT) it defines a trajectory-aware reward, and for modular methods (PEANUT) it penalizes prediction-network drift over sliding windows of long-term goal updates, enabling real-time adaptation with only a few gradient steps per environment.

## Results

On iTHOR cross-environment evaluation, DAT+LOG achieves 84.02% success rate and 46.95 SPL (ALL), outperforming the DAT baseline (82.02% / 46.54) and the prior meta-learning approach DAT+SAVN (79.67% / 45.66). On HM3D and MP3D, PEANUT+LOG reaches 64.3% Succ / 34.2 SPL and 40.3% Succ / 15.4 SPL respectively, surpassing PEANUT (62.6 / 32.5 and 39.4 / 15.0). In the cross-simulator setting (MP3D→Gibson), PEANUT+LOG achieves 63.9% Succ / 37.1 SPL versus PEANUT's 48.7% / 30.2, a 15.2-point success improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAT+LOG outperforms all iTHOR baselines: it beats DAT by +2.0 Succ and +0.41 SPL, and substantially outperforms DAT+SAVN (the closest meta-learning competitor) by +4.35 Succ and +1.29 SPL, demonstrating that trajectory-distribution alignment is more effective than self-supervised loss meta-learning. Against modular baselines on iTHOR (PONI, SSCNAV, HOZ), DAT+LOG leads by 5.4–15.5 Succ points. On HM3D/MP3D, PEANUT+LOG edges out PEANUT by +1.7 Succ (HM3D) and +0.9 Succ (MP3D), and dominates all other modular baselines (DD-PXPO, PROCTHOR, HABITAT-WEB, OVRL, 3D-AWARE, SGM) by 6–38 Succ points. The cross-simulator result (MP3D→Gibson) shows the largest relative gain: PEANUT+LOG improves over PEANUT by +15.2 Succ and +6.9 SPL, indicating the meta-learning prior is particularly valuable under large domain shift. In the single-environment adaptivity test, DAT+LOG further improves over DAT by +2.84 Succ and +1.78 SPL, confirming that the learned prior enables effective within-environment adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the meta-learning-for-navigation line initiated by SAVN (Wortsman et al., 2019), which meta-learns a self-supervised loss function, by instead meta-learning a central object-conditioned trajectory distribution via variational inference and KL-divergence alignment. It is built directly on two strong navigation baselines—DAT (end-to-end RL) and PEANUT (modular semantic-map prediction)—and adopts the inner-outer loop optimization structure of MAML, but reframes the adaptation objective through an ELBO decomposition that separates a trajectory-conditioned decoder from a trajectory prior. The theoretical grounding draws on the domain-alignment generalization framework of Ben-David et al. (2010), connecting the learned central distribution to tighter generalization bounds for unknown environments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pluggable design—where the same meta-learning mechanism can be grafted onto either RL or modular navigation pipelines—makes the contribution broadly applicable to the object-navigation community without requiring a full re-architecture. The cross-simulator result (15.2% absolute success-rate gain from MP3D→Gibson) suggests the learned central distribution captures environment-invariant navigation logic, though this is demonstrated on a single simulator pair. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly open-world settings with unbounded object categories, how sensitive performance is to the number of adaptation steps in resource-constrained deployment, and whether the meta-training cost (multiple rollouts per task) is prohibitive at scale.
