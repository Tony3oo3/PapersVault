## Problem

Prior-based 3D Gaussian head avatar methods (e.g., HeadGAP, GPHM) rely on identity-shared representations with a fixed number of Gaussians, making it extremely difficult to capture fine-grained geometric and texture details of specific individuals. This leads to degenerate rendering quality, particularly in texture-rich regions like hair, eyebrows, and skin. The paper addresses this by reformulating the problem as a joint reconstruction and registration task that decouples the prior's animation capability from the reconstruction's detail fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252289/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of static multi-view images (e.g., 15 photos) captured around a person's head with a smartphone, with camera poses estimated via COLMAP and FLAME parameters tracked via VHAP. It produces a complete animatable 3D Gaussian head avatar that supports arbitrary facial expressions, poses, and viewpoints, including previously invisible regions (teeth, inner lips) filled from the prior model. Experiments are conducted at both 802×550 and 3208×2202 resolutions, with the method showing particular advantage at the higher resolution where prior-based methods degrade most.

## Method

The core idea is to simultaneously optimize two sets of 3D Gaussians: a prior-based set decoded from a HeadGAP latent code (providing complete animation rigging via FLAME mesh binding) and a prior-free set initialized from FLAME mesh vertices and optimized as vanilla 3DGS (capturing fine details without representational constraints). A non-rigid ICP algorithm is alternately applied to establish correspondences between the two sets, which serves a dual purpose: it transfers binding information from prior-based to prior-free Gaussians (making them animatable via the GaussianAvatars local-to-global transformation), and it constructs registration-aware regularization losses (position alignment and scale penalty) that prevent the prior-free reconstruction from overfitting to the single static expression. After joint optimization, occluded regions (teeth, inner lips) are merged from the prior-based set, and a teeth enhancement strategy distills the HeadGAP CNN output at a mouth-open expression to match the rendering quality of visible facial regions.

## Results

On the NeRSemble dataset (6 IDs), the proposed method achieves PSNR 25.77 / SSIM 0.889 / LPIPS 0.111 at low resolution (802×550) and PSNR 25.22 / SSIM 0.896 / LPIPS 0.172 at high resolution (3208×2202) using only 15 static images as input. These results surpass the strongest image-based baseline HeadGAP by ~1.3 dB PSNR (low-res) and ~1.7 dB PSNR (high-res), and outperform GaussianAvatars trained on multi-view images by ~6.9 dB PSNR at low resolution. Notably, the method's LPIPS (0.111) at low resolution is even better than GaussianAvatars trained on 15 full video sequences (LPIPS 0.135), indicating superior perceptual quality despite using far less data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is compared against four baselines: DiffusionRig, GPAvatar, HeadGAP, and GaussianAvatars (in both image and video configurations). Against HeadGAP, the closest prior-based competitor, the proposed method wins on all three metrics at both resolutions, with the largest gap in LPIPS (0.111 vs 0.137 at low-res; 0.172 vs 0.199 at high-res), confirming that the joint registration-and-reconstruction approach captures finer texture detail than the identity-shared prior. Against GaussianAvatars trained on multi-view images, the improvement is substantial (~6.9 dB PSNR at low-res), attributed to correct binding information from non-rigid ICP registration and the prior-free reconstruction's ability to fit fine geometry. Against GaussianAvatars trained on 15 full video sequences, the proposed method achieves comparable SSIM and slightly lower PSNR (25.77 vs 26.63 at low-res) but notably better LPIPS (0.111 vs 0.135), suggesting superior perceptual fidelity from static captures alone. DiffusionRig and GPAvatar are clearly outperformed across all metrics, with GPAvatar showing the weakest PSNR (15.67) and particularly poor non-frontal view quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the GaussianAvatars paradigm of binding 3D Gaussian ellipsoids to a FLAME mesh for animation, and on HeadGAP's data-driven prior model that decodes latent codes into Gaussian primitives. It departs from prior work by formulating head avatar creation as a joint reconstruction-and-registration problem: rather than relying solely on a prior model (which underfits due to identity-shared representations) or solely on unconstrained 3DGS (which overfits to static captures), it simultaneously optimizes a prior-based and a prior-free Gaussian set while solving a non-rigid ICP registration between them. The registration-aware regularization (position and scale penalties) is a new loss design that leverages the cross-set correspondences to constrain the prior-free reconstruction, and the teeth enhancement via distilling a CNN's output in pseudo-viewpoints is a practical post-processing addition not seen in prior Gaussian avatar methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: the method reduces data acquisition from calibrated multi-camera video rigs to a short smartphone photo session while achieving 4K-resolution modeling quality that matches or exceeds video-trained GaussianAvatars on perceptual metrics (LPIPS). The registration-aware regularization strategy is a transferable technique that could benefit other few-shot 3D reconstruction tasks where a prior provides structure but a data-driven component provides detail. Open questions remain: the method's sensitivity to extremely sparse viewpoints (a known 3DGS limitation) is acknowledged but not resolved, and expression-dependent geometric details like wrinkles cannot be captured from a single static expression, limiting the avatar's expressiveness range.
