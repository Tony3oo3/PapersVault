## Problem

Image conditional diffusion models (e.g., for inpainting and image variation) are vulnerable to backdoor and adversarial attacks that modify the condition image, yet existing defenses target either unconditional diffusion models or text-to-image models, leaving a gap for image conditional settings. The paper asks how perturbations injected into the condition propagate through the reverse diffusion process and whether this propagation can be detected before completing the full generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251478/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single image condition (clean or attacked) and performs k independent reverse samplings from different random initial noises x_T ~ N(0,I) to an early step t_e (e.g., 800 or 960 in a 1000-step schedule), requiring only 2 DDIM steps. From these partial trajectories it computes a scalar Diffusion Anomaly Value (DAV) that is compared against an adaptive threshold derived from 500 clean samples, yielding a binary clean/adversarial-or-backdoor label. No full reverse process or generated image is needed, keeping detection cost low.

## Method

DADet exploits two phenomena arising from the amplification-and-accumulation of condition perturbations during the reverse process: (1) divergence, where the predicted x_0 at an early step deviates substantially from the condition image for attacked samples, and (2) homogeneity, where multiple reverse samplings from different initial noises converge to nearly identical predicted x_0 for attacked samples because the adversarial component dominates over the random noise. Concretely, the divergence metric D is the mean squared Euclidean distance between each predicted x_0 and the flattened condition image, and the homogeneity metric H is the sum of pairwise cosine similarities among the k predicted x_0 vectors; their product DAV is thresholded for classification. The theoretical analysis shows that at early steps (t > 800), the factor 1/sqrt(ᾱ_t) amplifies both Δx_t and Δε_t, while the adversarial discrepancies maintain consistent directionality across steps (unlike random noise), preventing cancellation and producing the observable divergence and homogeneity signatures.

## Results

For backdoor detection on MS COCO, DADet achieves F1 scores of 98–100% across three attack methods (Invisible Backdoor, BadDiffusion, VillanDiffusion) at both ℓ₂-norm budgets of 8/255 and 16/255. For adversarial detection on the image variation task (MS COCO), F1 scores exceed 97% across three attacks (MFA, Photo Guard, LDM-Robustness) at both norm levels. On the more challenging image inpainting task (Places365), F1 scores range from 84% to 97% depending on the attack, model (Latent vs. Stable Diffusion), and norm constraint, with the lowest being 84% for Photo Guard and LDM-R on Stable Diffusion at 8/255.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The only direct baseline comparison is with Elijah [1] on unconditional diffusion models (CIFAR-10, Table 2), where DADet is adapted by using only the homogeneity metric. On BadDiffusion, both methods achieve perfect 1.00 Precision and Recall (tie). On VillanDiffusion, DADet achieves 0.99/0.99 (P/R) versus Elijah's 1.00/0.96, giving DADet a 3-point recall advantage at the cost of 1 point in precision. On TrojDiff, DADet reaches 0.99/1.00 versus Elijah's 0.98/1.00, a 1-point precision gain. Overall, DADet matches or marginally outperforms Elijah on unconditional models, though the paper's primary contribution targets image conditional diffusion models where no prior baseline exists. The paper notes that existing defenses (DisDet, T2IShield, safety filters) are inapplicable to image conditional settings, making DADet the first method to simultaneously detect both backdoor and adversarial attacks in this domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of diffusion model security and image conditional generation. Prior backdoor defenses (DisDet, Elijah) target unconditional models by analyzing noise input distributions, while T2IShield addresses text-to-image models via cross-attention anomalies; adversarial defenses (safety filters, concept erasing, prompt red-teaming) are similarly designed for text-to-image settings. The paper departs from all of these by identifying that image conditional diffusion models—where attacks modify the condition image rather than noise or text—exhibit a distinct failure mode it terms 'diffusion anomaly,' and by providing a theoretical explanation (amplification and directional accumulation of perturbations) that reconciles with and extends Kwon et al.'s observation that single-step noise shifts are neutralized. The detection method itself is a novel construction combining divergence and homogeneity metrics computed from only two DDIM reverse steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Image conditional diffusion models underpin widely deployed applications (inpainting, editing, video synthesis) yet had no unified defense against both backdoor and adversarial attacks before this work; DADet fills that gap with a lightweight, training-free detector requiring only two reverse steps. The conceptual finding that adversarial perturbations maintain consistent directionality across reverse steps (unlike random noise) provides a principled explanation for why these attacks succeed where Kwon et al. predicted they should not, potentially informing future robustness analyses. Limitations include detection-only capability (no mitigation), dependence on a clean-sample threshold calibration, and notably weaker performance on the inpainting task (F1 ~84–85% vs. 97%+ on variation). Open questions include generalization to video/3D conditional models, robustness against adaptive attackers who jointly optimize for both attack success and detection evasion, and whether the divergence/homogeneity signals persist under stronger or multi-step perturbations.
