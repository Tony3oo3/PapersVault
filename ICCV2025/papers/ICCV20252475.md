## Problem

Existing hypergraph clustering methods assume node attributes are fully observed, yet real-world hypergraphs frequently suffer from missing attributes due to privacy constraints, data corruption, or incomplete records. While attribute-missing graph methods exist, they are not designed for hypergraphs, which encode higher-order multi-node relationships that introduce additional challenges for imputation and structure-aware clustering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252475/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a hypergraph defined by a node attribute matrix X ∈ R^{n×d} (where entries are either fully observed or completely missing, indicated by a binary mask M) and an incidence matrix H ∈ {0,1}^{n×m}, and produces node cluster assignments via k-means on fused node representations Z ∈ R^{n×d}. The imputation operates in a binary setting (each attribute is either present or absent), and the method is fully unsupervised, requiring no ground-truth labels. For fair comparison with graph-based baselines, hypergraphs are converted to graphs via clique expansion.

## Method

HCN-PAI comprises three components: (1) Higher-order neighborhood propagation constructs a node affinity matrix A = H D_𝓔^{-1} H^T and iteratively imputes missing attributes by minimizing Dirichlet energy via the Euler scheme, preserving observed values while propagating known information through hypergraph structure; (2) Hypergraph smoothing preprocessing replaces iterative hypergraph convolution with a single propagation matrix P that captures multi-hop structural information, decoupling structure learning from training and reducing computational cost; (3) A dual-level contrastive mechanism uses two independent unshared MLPs to produce two augmented views, then enforces consistency at the node level (aligning cross-view similarity with the affinity matrix) and at the hyperedge level (projecting similarity into hyperedge space via H^T S H and matching diagonal entries to hyperedge node counts). The two views are averaged and clustered with k-means.

## Results

On four benchmark hypergraph datasets (CORA, CITESEER, NTU2012, ModelNet40) with 30%, 60%, and 90% attribute missing rates, HCN-PAI achieves the best clustering accuracy across nearly all settings. At 60% missing rate, HCN-PAI reaches 68.03% ACC on CORA, 62.16% on CITESEER, 67.48% on NTU2012, and 85.47% on ModelNet40, outperforming the strongest baseline MMACL by 24.5, 21.5, 14.2, and 0.0 percentage points respectively. Performance degrades gracefully as missing rate increases from 0.3 to 0.9 (e.g., CORA drops only 6.46% in ACC), while baselines like TriCL and HyperGCL collapse to near-chance levels at 90% missing. Training time is competitive at 44.83 s average, far below MMACL (which times out on ModelNet40) and comparable to lightweight graph methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HCN-PAI is compared against seven baselines: three graph-based imputation methods (FP, GCNMF, PCFI) and four hypergraph contrastive methods (TriCL, HyperGCL, CHGNN, MMACL). On CORA, CITESEER, and NTU2012, HCN-PAI decisively outperforms all baselines at every missing rate; at 60% missing, it beats the strongest hypergraph baseline MMACL by 24.5 pp (CORA), 21.5 pp (CITESEER), and 14.2 pp (NTU2012) in ACC. The graph-based methods (FP, GCNMF, PCFI) are consistently the weakest, with ACC below 35% on CORA and CITESEER, confirming that clique-expanded graph representations lose critical higher-order structure. Among hypergraph baselines, MMACL is the strongest competitor, while TriCL and HyperGCL collapse to near-chance accuracy (≈5–8%) at 90% missing rate, whereas HCN-PAI retains 66.05% ACC on CORA. On ModelNet40, MMACL is competitive at low missing rates (88.46% vs. 85.47% ACC at 30%), but HCN-PAI's advantage widens as missingness increases, reflecting the robustness of its Dirichlet-energy-based imputation and dual-level contrastive objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: hypergraph contrastive clustering (HyperGCL, TriCL, CHGNN, MMACL), which assumes fully observed node attributes, and attribute-missing graph learning (FP, GCNMF, PCFI), which handles incomplete features but only on pairwise graphs. The key conceptual move is transplanting the Dirichlet-energy-based feature propagation of Rossi et al. [21] into the hypergraph setting via a node affinity matrix derived from the incidence structure, and pairing it with a dual-level (node + hyperedge) contrastive objective that has no direct analogue in the graph literature. The hypergraph smoothing preprocessing from Tang et al. [25] is adopted wholesale to replace iterative hypergraph convolution, positioning the method as a lightweight, training-free structural encoder rather than a learned HGNN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: real-world hypergraphs (co-authorship, recommendation, biological networks) frequently have missing node attributes, yet all existing hypergraph clustering methods assume complete features. The demonstrated stability at 90% missing rates (where baselines collapse to near-random) suggests the hypergraph structure itself carries enough signal for imputation, which is a useful empirical finding. Limitations include the binary missingness assumption (an attribute is either fully present or fully absent, not partially corrupted), the restriction to unsupervised clustering (no classification or link-prediction evaluation), and the reliance on k-means for the final partition, which may underperform on non-spherical cluster geometries. Open questions include whether the approach extends to missing hyperedges, to supervised or semi-supervised settings, and to hypergraphs with heterogeneous edge sizes where the uniform normalization in the affinity matrix may be suboptimal.
