## Problem

Existing normal integration methods are limited to orthographic or ideal pinhole cameras and handle depth discontinuities only implicitly through robust estimators or fixed weights. No prior formulation simultaneously models discontinuities explicitly and supports the broader class of generic central cameras (e.g., with lens distortion), while also providing a more accurate geometric relation between depth and surface normals than PDE-derived approximations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251726/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-view per-pixel surface normal map (e.g., from photometric stereo, shape-from-shading, or learned normal estimation) together with known camera intrinsics for an arbitrary central camera model, including Brown-Conrady lens distortion parameters. It produces a per-pixel depth map via iterative optimization in log-depth space, using 4-connectivity pixel neighborhoods. The formulation naturally accommodates non-ideal pinhole cameras by operating on ray direction vectors rather than assuming affine image-to-ray mappings.

## Method

The core contribution is a local planarity formulation that, for each pair of neighboring pixels a and b, introduces a subpixel m and assumes the surface is piecewise-planar with an explicit depth discontinuity ε_{b→a} at m. This yields a closed-form linear relation z_a = ω_{εa}·ε_{b→a} + ω_{b→a}·z_b whose coefficients are dot products of surface normals with ray direction vectors (n^T τ), making the formulation directly applicable to any central camera. Optimization proceeds via iterative conjugate gradient on log-depth values, using the bilateral weighting scheme of BiNI to identify and preserve discontinuities. Discontinuity magnitudes α_{b→a} are iteratively updated through a gating term β_{b→a} that progressively activates them as the bilateral weights indicate a discontinuity, preventing premature convergence to a suboptimal smooth solution. The formulation achieves 1–2 orders of magnitude lower residual error compared to BiNI's PDE-based constraint on the DiLiGenT benchmark.

## Results

On the DiLiGenT benchmark, the proposed method (1200 iterations) achieves the lowest Mean Absolute Depth Error (MADE) on 7 out of 9 objects, with headline values of 0.03 mm (bear), 0.24 mm (buddha), 0.73 mm (harvest), and 4.72 mm (goblet). In formulation accuracy (Table 1), the method's absolute residual on the ground-truth log-depth map is lower by one to two orders of magnitude compared to BiNI on all but one object (e.g., 0.82×10⁻² vs 3.72×10⁻¹ for bear). The method also demonstrates the first direct handling of generic central cameras with Brown-Conrady lens distortion, producing undistorted reconstructions where BiNI shows noticeable artifacts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BiNI [7] (the primary state-of-the-art baseline), the proposed method achieves lower MADE on 7 of 9 DiLiGenT objects, with the largest gains on harvest (0.73 vs 2.73 mm, ~3.7× improvement) and goblet (4.72 vs 8.53 mm, ~1.8× improvement). The two objects where BiNI retains an edge are cow (0.06 vs 0.08 mm) and reading (0.12 vs 0.17 mm), both small margins. Against Kim et al. [24], the proposed method wins on 7 of 9 objects as well, with particularly large advantages on harvest (0.73 vs 2.44 mm) and goblet (4.72 vs 9.02 mm). In formulation accuracy (Table 1), the proposed method's residual is lower by one to two orders of magnitude on 8 of 9 objects compared to BiNI (e.g., 0.82×10⁻² vs 3.72×10⁻¹ for bear), confirming that the local-planarity formulation more faithfully captures the depth–normal relationship. The paper also notes that even without iterative discontinuity computation (α=0), the method already outperforms both baselines on most objects, attributing the gain to the more accurate formulation rather than solely to the discontinuity handling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant PDE-based lineage of normal integration (Horn & Brooks → Zhu & Smith → BiNI) by replacing differential constraints with a local planarity assumption expressed through ray-direction and surface-normal dot products. It directly builds on BiNI's bilateral weighting scheme for discontinuity handling, adapting the semi-smooth assumption to the new formulation and extending the iterative optimization to jointly estimate depth and discontinuity magnitudes. The key conceptual departure is the use of ray directions rather than image-coordinate gradients, which naturally generalizes from ideal pinhole to arbitrary central cameras — a setting no prior method has addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to handle generic central cameras (e.g., with Brown-Conrady lens distortion) is practically significant for photometric stereo and shape-from-shading pipelines that use real, non-ideal optics, removing a previously necessary undistortion step. The 1–2 order-of-magnitude reduction in formulation residual over PDE-based methods suggests the local planarity assumption is a fundamentally tighter model of the depth–normal relationship, though the slower convergence (1200 vs. 150 iterations) and CPU-only runtime (50–120 s) limit immediate deployment. Open questions include whether the local planarity assumption degrades on highly curved surfaces, how the method scales to multi-view or video settings, and whether the discontinuity activation hyperparameters (q, ρ) can be made adaptive rather than fixed.
