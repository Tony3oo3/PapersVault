## Problem

Despite strong video content recognition, current multimodal LLMs exhibit a notable deficiency in temporal grounding—they cannot accurately perceive frame timestamps or localize event boundaries—and fail to produce a temporal-grounded reasoning process. Existing agent-based video QA methods rely on text and captions as intermediaries, limiting their ability to directly capture the visuotemporal dynamics inherent in videos, especially for long-form content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251599/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw video (uniformly sampled to 32 frames by default, longer side resized to 480 px) and a free-form text question. The MLLM outputs a structured chain-of-thought trace (THOUGHT / ACTION / TERMINATE per step) alongside either start-end timestamps for temporal grounding or a natural-language answer. The video memory is dynamically updated at each reasoning step with annotated frames (progress bar overlaid at the bottom), so the model re-processes the modified video rather than a static frame set.

## Method

VTimeCoT is a training-free, tool-augmented framework that inserts a visual progress bar as an intermediate reasoning medium between the MLLM and the video. Two plug-and-play tools are exposed as Python functions the MLLM can invoke: (1) a frame-sync progress bar integration tool that uses OpenCV to draw an elongated rectangle with per-frame second-level timestamps at the bottom of each frame, letting the model read temporal progression via its native OCR and shape-understanding abilities; and (2) a moment-highlighting tool that leverages VideoCLIP-XL to compute cosine similarity between the query embedding and 8-frame video-clip embeddings, selects the top-k most relevant clips, and overlays colored highlight masks on the corresponding progress-bar intervals. The overall reasoning follows a Visuotemporal Chain-of-Thought loop: at each step the MLLM produces a THOUGHT (analysis of current video memory), an ACTION (executable Python script calling one or more tools), and optionally a TERMINATE signal; the framework builds and runs the code, updates the video memory with the annotated frames, and feeds the augmented context back for the next step, up to a maximum of 3 iterations. The entire pipeline is built on AutoGen and requires no fine-tuning of the underlying MLLM (Qwen2VL-7B or GPT-4o).

## Results

On Charades-STA, VTimeCoT+GPT4o achieves 46.78 mIoU, improving over GPT4o alone (40.20) by +6.58 and over the fine-tuned VTimeLLM-13B (34.60) by +12.18. On QVHighlights (discontinuous segments), VTimeCoT+GPT4o reaches 54.49 mIoU vs. GPT4o's 37.66 (+16.83). On Vript-RR, VTimeCoT+GPT4o attains 83.55% multi-choice accuracy (vs. GPT4o's 70.39%) and 68.42% open-ended accuracy (vs. 61.18%). On VideoMME, VTimeCoT+GPT4o achieves 73.6% accuracy with subtitles using only 32 frames, surpassing GPT4o (65.1%) and matching Gemini-1.5-Pro (81.3% with 1 fps) while using far fewer frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VTimeCoT consistently outperforms all baselines across all four benchmarks. On Charades-STA, VTimeCoT+GPT4o (46.78 mIoU) surpasses the fine-tuned VTimeLLM-13B (34.60) by 12.18 points without any training, and improves over GPT4o alone by 6.58 mIoU. On QVHighlights, the gap widens dramatically: VTimeCoT+GPT4o (54.49 mIoU) beats GPT4o (37.66) by 16.83 points, indicating the progress bar is especially critical for discontinuous multi-segment grounding. On Vript-RR, VTimeCoT+GPT4o achieves 83.55% multi-choice accuracy, exceeding GPT4o (70.39%) by 13.16 points and the previous best Qwen2VL-7B (59.87%) by 23.68 points. On VideoMME, VTimeCoT+GPT4o (73.6% with subtitles, 32 frames) outperforms GPT4o (65.1%, 32 frames) by 8.5 points and surpasses all open-source 7B models (best: VideoLLaMA3-7B at 70.3%), approaching Gemini-1.5-Pro (81.3%) which uses 1 fps sampling. The ablation confirms both the progress bar (+7.55 mIoU over CoT-only) and the highlight tool (+5.09 mIoU over progress-bar-only) contribute independently to the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

VTimeCoT sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) visual programming and tool-use for MLLMs (VisProg, ViperGPT, MM-REACT), which prompt models to generate code invoking external functions; (2) visual chain-of-thought methods like Visual Sketchpad [13], which overlay spatial prompts (circles, keypoints) on images during reasoning; and (3) agent-based video QA frameworks (VideoAgent, MoreVQA, Traveler) that use text-based step-by-step reasoning with external tools. The key departure is replacing the text-only or spatial-visual reasoning medium with a *temporal* visual prompt — a frame-synchronized progress bar — combined with a VideoCLIP-XL-based moment-retrieval highlight, all orchestrated in a training-free iterative loop. Unlike fine-tuned temporal grounding models (VTimeLLM, LITA), VTimeCoT requires no additional training data or parameter updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a deceptively simple visual annotation (drawing a progress bar with OpenCV) can close a large gap in MLLM temporal grounding without any fine-tuning, outperforming models specifically trained on temporal grounding data. This suggests that the bottleneck for temporal reasoning in current MLLMs may be perceptual (lacking explicit timestamp cues) rather than architectural, pointing toward lightweight prompting solutions. Limitations include dependence on an external VideoCLIP-XL model for moment retrieval, a fixed 3-step reasoning budget, and evaluation on relatively short-to-medium videos (up to ~1 hour on VideoMME). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-hour videos, whether the progress bar concept can be extended to hierarchical or non-linear temporal structures, and whether the VideoCLIP-XL dependency can be replaced by the MLLM's own representations.
