## Problem

Existing affordance segmentation datasets are constrained by limited scale, narrow domain coverage (typically a single embodiment type), and template-based language prompts that always name the target object. This prevents models from generalizing to unseen categories, unseen image domains, and human-like instructions that describe objects only by function (e.g., 'something to slice bread' instead of 'knife'), which is critical for open-world robotic grasping.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251328/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes an RGB image paired with a natural-language instruction (ranging from explicit category names to purely functional descriptions without any object name) and a depth image. It outputs a pixel-wise affordance segmentation mask identifying the graspable region, which is then combined with the depth map to produce a 3D grasper pose. The reasoning-based instructions come in two difficulty levels: 'easy' (includes the object name plus a functional clause) and 'hard' (functional description only, no category name), mimicking real human-robot interaction.

## Method

AffordanceNet comprises two components: AffordanceVLM and a Pose Generator. AffordanceVLM is built on LISA (ViT-CLIP encoder + Vicuna-7B LLM + SAM decoder) with two task-specific modifications: a specialized system prompt ('You are an embodied robot') and a novel <AFF> token that enriches the mask embedding beyond the generic <SEG> token, directing the decoder toward affordance-specific language expressions. The model is trained on RAGNet (273k images, 180 categories, 26k reasoning instructions spanning wild, robot, ego-centric, and simulation domains) alongside generic segmentation data. The Pose Generator back-projects the 2D affordance mask using camera intrinsics/extrinsics and depth to obtain a 3D point cloud, then applies GraspNet-style grasp proposal generation to produce the final 3D grasper pose for a UR5 robot arm.

## Results

On affordance segmentation, AffordanceNet achieves 60.3 gIoU / 60.8 cIoU on HANDAL, 63.3 / 64.0 on GraspNet seen, 45.6 / 33.2 on GraspNet novel (zero-shot categories), and 37.4 / 37.4 on 3DOI (zero-shot domains), outperforming the best foundation model (VLPart+SAM2, 40.9 gIoU on HANDAL) and the best generalist MLLM (LISA, 16.2 gIoU on HANDAL) by large margins. On reasoning-based affordance segmentation, AffordanceNet reaches 58.2 gIoU on hard instructions (no category name) versus 12.3 for LISA. On real-robot grasping with a UR5 arm, AffordanceNet achieves a 70% average success rate across 10 tasks, nearly doubling GraspNet's 32%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AffordanceNet dominates all baselines across every evaluated dataset. Against foundation models without LLMs (VLPart+SAM2, Grounding DINO+SAM2, Florence2+SAM2), AffordanceNet gains roughly 20 gIoU points on HANDAL (60.3 vs. 40.9 for VLPart) and is the only method evaluated on zero-shot benchmarks. Among generalist MLLMs, LISA achieves 16.2 gIoU on HANDAL and 25.2 on GraspNet novel, while AffordanceNet reaches 60.3 and 45.6 respectively — a gap of ~44 and ~20 gIoU. GLaMM performs slightly better than LISA on HANDAL (24.9 gIoU) but still trails AffordanceNet by over 35 points. On reasoning-based segmentation (Table 6), AffordanceNet scores 58.2 gIoU on hard instructions versus 12.3 for LISA and 5.0 for GLaMM, confirming a decisive advantage in functional-description grounding. For real-robot grasping, AffordanceNet (70% avg.) more than doubles GraspNet (32% avg.), with the largest gains on handle-requiring objects like screwdriver (60% vs. 10%) and hammer (80% vs. 20%). On RLBench simulation, AffordanceNet (54.7% avg.) is slightly below LLARVA (62% avg.), which is fine-tuned for that specific environment, but the gap narrows on close-jar (44% vs. 28%), reflecting AffordanceNet's stronger generalization focus.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LISA (a vision-language segmentation model) as the backbone for its AffordanceVLM, adding a task-specific <AFF> token and a specialized system prompt to steer mask decoding toward affordance regions. For the grasping pipeline, it adopts GraspNet's 3D point-cloud-to-pose formulation. Conceptually, it departs from prior affordance benchmarks (UMD, AGD20k, HANDAL, ManipVQA) in two key ways: (1) unifying wild, robot, ego-centric, and simulation data into a single 273k-image corpus, and (2) introducing 'hard' reasoning instructions that omit the target category name in favor of functional descriptions (e.g., 'something to slice bread' instead of 'knife'), a formulation absent from all prior affordance datasets. Unlike ManipVQA and AffordanceLLM, which use fixed prompt templates at limited scale, RAGNet leverages GPT-4 to generate 26k diverse reasoning instructions and explicitly targets open-world zero-shot generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RAGNet is the first affordance segmentation benchmark to span four embodied domains simultaneously and to include category-name-free reasoning instructions, making it a natural reference for evaluating whether VLMs can truly generalize to unseen objects and scenes in a grasping context. The near-zero performance drop from easy to hard reasoning instructions (58.3 → 58.2 gIoU on HANDAL) suggests that functional-description prompts are a viable, more human-like interface for robot control. However, the simulation results (54.7% vs. LLARVA's 62%) and the limited real-robot evaluation (10 objects, single UR5 arm) leave open whether the approach transfers to multi-step manipulation or more complex dexterous tasks. The pose generator remains a relatively simple 2D-to-3D projection, and the paper does not explore failure modes or safety constraints for deployed grasping.
