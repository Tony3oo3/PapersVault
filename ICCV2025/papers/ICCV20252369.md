## Problem

Existing talking-head generation systems are limited in three key ways: they assume a pre-existing speech signal (requiring cascaded TTS-to-vision pipelines that accumulate latency and error), they only model monadic speaking without authentic listening behavior, and they operate on 2D video or untextured 3D meshes. No prior method jointly synthesizes speech and photorealistic 4D avatar motion from text alone while also supporting dyadic interaction where the avatar actively listens and reacts to a user.

## Input / Output

During training, input tokens are 29-dim Wav2Vec2 character-level logits at 86 fps; at inference, a small text-to-tokens module maps raw text to these logits. In dyadic mode, the user's monocular video is processed by SMIRK to extract per-frame FLAME expression (50-dim), jaw pose (3-dim), and head rotation (3-dim) features, plus user ASR tokens, all concatenated with the actor's input tokens. The model outputs an 80-band mel-spectrogram (decoded to 22050 Hz speech via a frozen BigVGAN vocoder) and per-frame 8-dim head-pose latents plus 256-dim facial-expression latents in Codec Avatar space, rendered to a photorealistic 4D avatar.

## Method

AV-Flow employs two parallel DiT transformers—one generating mel-spectrograms and one generating head/facial dynamics—connected by intermediate highway layers that perform a learned linear fusion of the concatenated [audio; visual] features at every block, enabling tight cross-modal synchronization (e.g., speech intonation with eyebrow motion). The entire system is trained end-to-end with optimal-transport flow matching (straight-line probability paths) using an L1 loss, which yields faster inference than standard diffusion. A frozen BigVGAN vocoder decodes the spectrogram to audio, and a Codec Avatar decoder renders the predicted latents to the 4D avatar. For dyadic conversations, SMIRK-derived user features and user ASR tokens are simply concatenated to the input tokens, allowing the avatar to produce back-channeling expressions, gaze shifts, and head nods in response to the user. Only 8 Euler solver steps are needed, achieving ~200 ms offline synthesis of 20 seconds of audio-visual content on a single A100 GPU.

## Results

AV-Flow achieves the best lip synchronization (F1 lips = 0.964), best audio-visual alignment (BC_h = 0.258, BC_e = 0.229), best audio quality (MCD = 0.900, WER = 0.157), and best expression diversity (Div_e = 0.680) across all evaluated metrics. It also attains the best realism score (FD_e = 0.861) and the fastest inference time (0.398 s for 20 s of content on a single A100 GPU), matching TTSF* (0.400 s) while substantially outperforming diffusion-based VASA-1* (0.965 s) and Audio2Photoreal (1.578 s). The only metric where a baseline slightly edges out AV-Flow is head-pose diversity (Div_h: VASA-1* 0.033 vs. AV-Flow 0.029), which the authors attribute to noisier, less controlled head motion in VASA-1*.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AV-Flow outperforms all four baselines on every metric where they are directly comparable. Against TTSF* (the only other method that jointly generates speech and video from text), AV-Flow improves lip-sync F1 from 0.929 to 0.964, reduces MCD from 1.229 to 0.900, and halves WER from 0.285 to 0.157, while matching its inference speed (0.398 s vs. 0.400 s). Against the audio-driven VASA-1*, AV-Flow gains +0.118 in lip-sync F1 (0.964 vs. 0.846) and +0.048 in BC_h (0.258 vs. 0.210), and is 2.4× faster (0.398 s vs. 0.965 s); VASA-1* marginally leads on Div_h (0.032 vs. 0.029) but the authors note this reflects noisier, less controlled head motion. Against Audio2Photoreal, AV-Flow improves lip-sync F1 by +0.044 (0.964 vs. 0.920) and BC_h by +0.060 (0.258 vs. 0.198), while running 4× faster. When baselines are converted to text-driven mode by attaching a TTS module (VASA-1* w/TTS, Audio2Photoreal w/TTS), their performance degrades substantially (e.g., FD_e jumps to 2.665 and 3.289 respectively), highlighting the advantage of AV-Flow's end-to-end joint generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

AV-Flow builds on the DiT architecture for its dual-transformer backbone, the Codec Avatar latent space as its 3D representation target, and flow matching as its training objective. It departs from the dominant cascaded paradigm (text→speech→visual) by proposing a fully-parallel architecture with intermediate highway connections that jointly generate audio and visual signals from text tokens, avoiding error accumulation and enabling natural cross-modal synchronization. It also extends the monadic talking-head setting to dyadic interaction by conditioning on a user's audio-visual input, a capability absent from prior audio-driven or text-driven methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to produce photorealistic 4D talking avatars from raw text alone while jointly synthesizing speech, facial expression, and head motion, with fast inference (~200 ms for 20 s of content via 8-step Euler flow matching). The dyadic listening capability (back-channeling smiles, head nods) opens a path toward always-on conversational avatars for human-AI interaction. Key limitations include the lack of semantic understanding (the avatar cannot recognize a joke without the user laughing) and identity-specificity, leaving open the question of whether generalizable, semantically-aware 4D avatar generation is achievable.
