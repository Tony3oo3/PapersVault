## Problem

Unified 3D inpainting pipelines face three key challenges: single-reference methods degrade when views are far from the reference, independently inpainting multi-view images with 2D diffusion priors produces appearance inconsistencies, and existing geometry supervision (e.g., monocular depth maps) is inconsistent across views, leading to multi-facet artifacts when inpainting regions require significant geometric changes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251771/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained 3D Gaussian Splatting scene (built on Scaffold-GS), a positive text prompt describing the desired content, a negative prompt describing what to remove, and a mask prompt that drives Lang SAM to segment the inpainting region. Camera poses are estimated via COLMAP, and K-means clustering on camera centers selects reference views. The output is a fully inpainted 3D Gaussian scene that renders consistently from all novel viewpoints, supporting object removal, re-texturing, and replacement in both feed-forward and 360-degree scenes.

## Method

DiGA3D employs a coarse-to-fine strategy over 3D Gaussians. In the coarse stage, DDIM inversion produces deterministic latents from rendered views, and an Attention Feature Propagation (AFP) module injects cross-attention features from K-means-selected reference views into other views' inpainting process within the 2D inpainter (PowerPaint-v1), implicitly enforcing multi-view appearance consistency. In the fine stage, a Texture-Geometry Score Distillation Sampling (TG-SDS) loss warps Canny edge maps and monocular depth maps from reference views to target views via DIBR, then feeds them as multi-control conditions into ControlNet to explicitly and controllably propagate geometric and textural information. The 3D Gaussians are optimized with a combined photometric loss (L1 + D-SSIM), a depth loss aligned via scale-shift, and the TG-SDS gradient, with backpropagation restricted to masked pixels.

## Results

On the SPIn-NeRF object removal benchmark, DiGA3D achieves the best overall PSNR of 20.71 and the highest Masked PSNR of 17.22, outperforming all four baselines. For object re-texturing, DiGA3D scores 0.1751 CLIP_dir (vs. 0.0742 for the next-best GaussCtrl) and wins 83.33% of user-study votes. For object replacement, DiGA3D reaches 0.2247 CLIP_dir, more than doubling the score of GaussCtrl (0.1097).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On object removal (Table 1), DiGA3D outperforms SPIn-NeRF, NeRFiller, and MVIP-NeRF on every metric, with the largest gap in Masked PSNR (17.22 vs. 15.45, 15.48, and 10.00 respectively). Against the closest competitor GScream, DiGA3D wins on PSNR (20.71 vs. 20.49) and Masked PSNR (17.22 vs. 15.84) while tying on SSIM (0.58) and LPIPS (0.28), though GScream edges ahead slightly on Masked LPIPS (0.54 vs. 0.56). For object re-texturing and replacement (Table 2), DiGA3D dominates all three baselines: it achieves 0.1751 CLIP_dir for re-texturing versus 0.0742 (GaussCtrl), 0.0702 (GaussianEditor), and 0.0572 (IN2N), and 0.2247 for replacement versus 0.1097 (GaussCtrl), 0.0908 (GaussianEditor), and 0.0354 (IN2N). In the user study, DiGA3D receives 83.33% of votes compared to 12.97% for GaussCtrl and 1.85% each for GaussianEditor and IN2N, indicating a substantial perceptual quality gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) + diffusion prior paradigm for 3D scene editing by introducing a coarse-to-fine pipeline that addresses two key weaknesses of prior diffusion-guided inpainting methods: single-reference-view fragility and multi-view appearance inconsistency. It builds on Score Distillation Sampling (DreamFusion) by augmenting it with texture and geometry conditions via ControlNet (TG-SDS), and on PowerPaint's 2D inpainting framework by injecting an Attention Feature Propagation (AFP) mechanism that implicitly transfers reference-view attention into other views' latent spaces. Unlike prior methods that independently inpaint each view or rely on a single reference image, DiGA3D uses K-means clustering to select multiple reference views and explicitly warps their texture and depth into other views as ControlNet conditions, bridging implicit (attention) and explicit (warped geometry) consistency propagation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified pipeline for object removal, re-texturing, and replacement in a single framework is practically valuable for 3D content creation workflows, and the coarse-to-fine strategy (implicit attention propagation followed by explicit geometry-regularized SDS) offers a reusable recipe for multi-view-consistent 3D editing beyond inpainting. Limitations include dependence on COLMAP for camera poses, monocular depth estimation that is not metric, and the requirement that the scene be reconstructable as 3DGS. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large or dynamic scenes, whether the K-means reference selection generalizes to non-uniform view distributions, and whether the TG-SDS formulation can be extended to other 3D representations beyond Gaussians.
