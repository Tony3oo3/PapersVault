## Problem

Prior work on human-object interaction synthesis has been limited to either static objects without hand manipulation, dynamic objects without realistic finger movements, or small objects only. No complete system existed that could synthesize physically plausible, long-horizon interactions with synchronized full-body and detailed finger motions from abstract human-level language instructions in contextual 3D environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251949/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form human-level text instruction (e.g., 'I want to do yoga in front of the TV') and a 3D scene description listing objects and their initial layout as input. It produces a scene map specifying target 6D poses (position + orientation) for each object to be moved, a sequential execution plan of textual actions, and finally synchronized kinematic sequences of full-body joint positions with 6D rotations, per-finger 6D rotations, and object position/rotation matrices, all rendered as physically simulated motion with collision-free contact.

## Method

The system decomposes the problem into three stages: (1) a high-level LLM planner that uses spatial relationship primitives (on, adjacent, facing) as an intermediate representation to avoid directly predicting 3D coordinates, then resolves them into precise object poses via a scene-graph topological-sort algorithm; (2) a multi-stage low-level motion generator that avoids requiring a single dataset with all modalities by sequentially running CoarseNet (a CHOIS-based diffusion model for full-body + object motion), a modified DexGraspNet optimization for grasp poses, RefineNet (a conditioned diffusion model that re-generates motion aligned to the grasp pose using a wrist-object relative pose loss), and FingerNet (a diffusion model for approach/release finger transitions), plus a navigation module for locomotion between objects; (3) a PPO-based physics tracker with an importance sampling strategy that imitates the kinematic reference in a physics simulator to eliminate penetration and sliding artifacts while preserving long-horizon multi-object interactions.

## Results

On the FullBodyManipulation dataset, the full system (C+R+FNet) achieves the best interaction quality across all key metrics: contact recall of 0.95 (vs. 0.64 for the CNet+GRIP baseline), contact F1 of 0.92 (vs. 0.70), contact coverage of 5.53% (vs. 3.99%), and intersection volume of 19.06 cm³ (vs. 49.00 cm³), indicating substantially more precise finger-object contact with far less penetration. Waypoint following error (T_xy) improves from 3.58 cm to 2.81 cm, and foot sliding drops from 0.45 to 0.33. The physics tracker achieves tracking errors of 5.45 cm for human joints and 4.67 cm for objects across 20 sequences averaging 30 seconds each.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline combines CHOIS (CNet) for full-body and object motion with GRIP for finger refinement. The full system (C+R+FNet) decisively outperforms this baseline on all interaction-quality metrics: contact recall jumps from 0.64 to 0.95, contact F1 from 0.70 to 0.92, and intersection volume drops from 49.00 to 19.06 cm³ (a 61% reduction in hand-object penetration). Contact coverage also improves from 3.99% to 5.53%, reflecting more fingers in meaningful contact with the object surface. The baseline retains a slight edge on foot height (2.50 vs. 2.89 cm) and MPJPE (14.33 vs. 15.96 cm), but these are secondary to the interaction fidelity that the paper targets. In human perception studies, the full system is preferred over the CNet+GRIP baseline by a large margin across 60 evaluated pairs, and is even preferred over ground-truth data from FullBodyManipulation because it includes detailed finger motions absent in the original dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper assembles a complete pipeline by combining three previously separate research threads: LLM-based scene planning (building on UniHSI and InterDreamer), diffusion-based motion generation (building directly on CHOIS for full-body/object motion and GRAB-trained models for finger motion), and physics-based RL tracking (building on DeepMimic and PhysHOI). The key architectural departure is the three-stage decomposition (CoarseNet → Grasp Optimization → RefineNet → FingerNet), which sidesteps the absence of any single dataset containing synchronized full-body, finger, and object motion by training separate models on different datasets and aligning them through intermediate conditions. Unlike prior HOI synthesis work that either omits finger detail or is restricted to small objects, this system explicitly targets large-object manipulation with dexterous finger coordination in contextual scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end system that produces physically plausible, long-horizon human-object interactions with synchronized full-body and finger motion from natural language, filling a gap that no single prior method addressed. The practical implication is significant for robotics, embodied AI, and computer graphics, where realistic multi-object manipulation sequences are needed. Key limitations include reliance on text/image scene descriptions rather than structured 3D representations, and the multi-stage pipeline introduces cumulative error and computational overhead. The authors identify egocentric vision perception and voxel-grid scene representations as natural next steps toward more autonomous, perception-driven agents.
