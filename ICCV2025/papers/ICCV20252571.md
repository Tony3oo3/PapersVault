## Problem

Existing equivariant self-supervised learning methods rely on dedicated predictor networks (hypernetworks, conditional projectors) to enforce equivariance, adding architectural complexity while operating on CNN backbones that lack natural pose-encoding mechanisms. Additionally, prior equivariant benchmarks evaluate only rotation (SO(3)), leaving open whether these methods generalise to combined geometric transformations such as translation (SE(3)).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252571/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two transformed views of the same RGB image (rendered from ShapeNetCoreV2 3D objects via BlenderProc at 50 views per instance). The CNN encoder preserves 2D spatial structure by omitting global average pooling, feeding feature maps into the CapsNet projector, which outputs per-capsule activation probability vectors (invariant, R^N) and 4×4 pose matrices (equivariant, R^{N×4×4}). Downstream evaluation uses the encoder's globally pooled output for classification, rotation regression, and translation regression, while the projector's structured pose matrices enable direct geometric manipulation for equivariance verification.

## Method

EquiCaps is a Siamese architecture pairing a shared ResNet-18 CNN encoder with a self-routing CapsNet projector (non-iterative routing via subordinate single-layer perceptrons) that eliminates the predictor module entirely. Instead of a predictor network mapping one embedding to another, equivariance is enforced by directly multiplying the pose matrix by the known relative transformation matrix ρ_Y(g_rel) and minimising MSE against the other view's pose matrix, both normalised to the unit hypersphere via Frobenius norm. Invariance is enforced through cross-entropy on activation vectors plus mean-entropy-maximisation regularisation, while VICReg-style variance-covariance regularisation is applied to the transformation-aligned (rather than raw) concatenated embeddings. The key architectural insight is that capsules' routing-by-agreement mechanism inherently encodes pose, making the 4×4 pose matrices directly steerable by affine transformations without any learned predictor.

## Results

On 3DIEBench, EquiCaps achieves a rotation prediction R² of 0.78, surpassing the closest equivariant competitor SIE (0.73) by 0.05 and CapsIE (0.74) by 0.04, while approaching the supervised ResNet-18 upper bound of 0.77. On the more challenging 3DIEBench-T (which adds translations), EquiCaps reaches R² = 0.73 for rotation and R² = 0.60 for translation, outperforming SIE by +0.28 and +0.40 R² respectively. For classification (invariance), EquiCaps scores 83.24% top-1 on 3DIEBench, slightly below invariant baselines like SimCLR (86.73%) and SimCLR+AugSelf (87.44%) but above SIE (82.94%) and CapsIE (79.14%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EquiCaps decisively outperforms all equivariant baselines on geometric prediction tasks: on 3DIEBench-T it beats SIE by +0.28 R² (rotation) and +0.40 R² (translation), CapsIE by +0.13 and +0.14 R² respectively, and SEN/EquiMod by +0.28/+0.30 R² (rotation) and +0.34/+0.36 R² (translation). Against the strongest non-equivariant baseline SimCLR+AugSelf, EquiCaps still leads by +0.04 R² on rotation and +0.10 R² on translation on 3DIEBench-T, confirming that explicit equivariant enforcement via capsule pose matrices surpasses implicit parameter prediction. On classification (invariance), EquiCaps trails SimCLR+AugSelf (87.44%) and SimCLR (86.73%) by ~4–4.2 points on 3DIEBench, but this gap narrows to ~4 points on 3DIEBench-T where the added geometric complexity penalises non-capsule methods more severely. Notably, SIE's rotation advantage on 3DIEBench (0.73 vs. 0.78) collapses to a 0.28 R² deficit on 3DIEBench-T, and its translation R² (0.20) is the worst among all equivariant methods, indicating poor generalisation to multi-geometric settings. CapsIE, the only other capsule-based method, generalises better than non-capsule equivariant methods but still underperforms EquiCaps, supporting the paper's claim that removing the predictor and directly leveraging capsule pose matrices is more effective than routing equivariance through a separate prediction network.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant equivariant SSL paradigm (SEN, EquiMod, SIE, CapsIE) that relies on a dedicated predictor network to map one embedding to another under a known transformation. Instead, EquiCaps exploits the intrinsic pose-awareness of capsule networks—specifically their 4×4 pose matrices produced via self-routing [29]—to enforce equivariance through direct affine transformation in the latent space, eliminating the predictor entirely. Architecturally, it builds on the self-routing CapsNet [29] as a non-iterative projector and the VICReg-style variance-covariance regularisation [3] for collapse prevention. The paper also extends the 3DIEBench benchmark [24] from rotation-only SO(3) to the full SE(3) group by adding translations, creating 3DIEBench-T.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that equivariant SSL may not require the architectural overhead of hypernetworks, conditional projectors, or split representations when the backbone is already pose-aware, simplifying the pipeline and improving interpretability through structured pose matrices. The 3DIEBench-T benchmark provides a more realistic multi-geometric evaluation setting that exposes generalisation failures in non-capsule equivariant methods. However, the method assumes known group elements (as do all prior equivariant SSL methods), is evaluated only on rendered 3D objects rather than natural images, and the invariance-equivariance trade-off remains unresolved for task-specific priorities. Open questions include whether the predictor-free design extends to scaling, shearing, or real-world uncontrolled transformations, and whether capsule architectures can handle object detection or other dense prediction tasks.
