## Problem

Existing point trackers achieve high accuracy but only for sparse sets of query points, while optical flow methods produce dense correspondence but only between adjacent frames. No prior method delivers both all-pixel (dense) tracking and long-range temporal tracking at high resolution, leaving a gap between the spatial coverage of flow methods and the temporal reach of sparse trackers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250302/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a video of shape T×H×W×3 and a query frame index t, processing it in sliding windows of 16 frames with stride 8. It outputs a tensor of shape T×H×W×4: two channels encode the long-range optical flow offset from every pixel in the query frame to its correspondence in each other frame, and two channels estimate per-pixel visibility and confidence. Internally all computation occurs at 1/8 spatial resolution, with a pixel-shuffle layer recovering full resolution at the end, enabling 768×1024 tracking (786,432 simultaneous tracks) on a single 40G A100 GPU.

## Method

AllTracker reframes point tracking as multi-frame long-range optical flow: instead of chaining frame-to-frame flows or tracking sparse points, it simultaneously estimates the displacement from a query frame to every other frame in a 16-frame window. The architecture uses a pre-trained ConvNeXt-Tiny encoder to produce 1/8-resolution feature maps, builds multi-scale correlation volumes (5 scales, radius-4 patches), then iterates a recurrent refinement module four times. Each refinement step interleaves 2D ConvNeXt blocks for spatial message passing with pixel-aligned transformer blocks for temporal propagation (attention only along the time axis, quadratic in window length S=16). The key efficiency insight is that spatial information flows through cheap 2D convolutions on the low-resolution grid rather than expensive spatial attention, while temporal priors are learned via the pixel-aligned transformer. The model is jointly trained on both optical flow datasets (FlyingChairs, KITTI, TartanAir, etc.) and point tracking datasets (Kubric, PointOdyssey, DynamicReplica), which the authors show is crucial for top performance; it uses L1 trajectory loss with visibility-weighted terms and BCE losses for visibility/confidence maps.

## Results

AllTracker achieves a mean δ_avg of 66.1 across nine point-tracking benchmarks at 384×512 resolution, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art CoTracker3 (65.0) with fewer parameters (16.48M vs. 25.39M) and no bootstrapping. At high resolution (768×1024), AllTracker reaches 69.5 δ_avg versus CoTracker3's 66.9, and even the 6.29M-parameter AllTracker-Tiny (67.5) surpasses CoTracker3 at this resolution. The model also achieves superior Average Jaccard (68.9 vs. 63.1) and occlusion accuracy (91.5 vs. 89.3) on TAP-Vid datasets, while running at optical-flow-level throughput on a 40G GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AllTracker (66.1 avg δ_avg) outperforms the bootstrapped CoTracker3 (65.0) by 1.1 points on average, winning on 6 of 9 benchmarks; CoTracker3 retains the edge on CroHD (44.5 vs. 44.0), DAVIS (77.1 vs. 76.3), and DriveTrack (69.8 vs. 65.8). The most striking margin is on RGB-Stacking, where AllTracker scores 90.0 versus CoTracker3's 84.2, suggesting superior spatial context integration in textureless regions. Against the other dense tracker DELTA (59.7), AllTracker leads by 6.4 points on average, and DELTA requires a 96G GPU while AllTracker fits in 40G. At 768×1024 resolution, AllTracker (69.5) beats CoTracker3 (66.9) by 2.6 points, and even the 6.29M AllTracker-Tiny (67.5) exceeds CoTracker3, highlighting the memory and parameter efficiency of the low-resolution-grid-plus-upsampling design. On optical flow benchmarks (Sintel), AllTracker (1.673/3.244 FLE) trails SEA-RAFT (1.309/2.601) but is comparable to GMFlow, confirming that the model is underfitting on short-range flow despite its strong long-range tracking performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

AllTracker sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: iterative optical flow estimation (RAFT, SEA-RAFT) and multi-frame point tracking (PIPs, CoTracker, TAPIR). It borrows the low-resolution iterative refinement and pixel-shuffle upsampling from the flow side, and the per-pixel temporal attention for occlusion handling from the tracking side, but departs from both by framing the problem as simultaneous multi-frame long-range flow rather than pairwise flow or sparse point trajectories. A key architectural departure from concurrent dense trackers (DELTA, DTF) and from CoTracker3 is the use of plain 2D convolutions for spatial message passing on a low-resolution grid, rather than transformer-based attention over sparse anchor tokens or virtual points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By delivering dense all-pixel tracks at optical-flow speed and at 768×1024 resolution on a 40G GPU, AllTracker makes sparse point trackers of comparable accuracy redundant and opens the door to applications requiring full correspondence fields (e.g., dense motion visualization, 3D reconstruction). The finding that joint training on optical flow and point tracking datasets is crucial for top performance suggests a broader data-sharing opportunity across these communities. Open limitations include underfitting on short-range flow (Sintel scores below SEA-RAFT), a fixed 16-frame temporal window that constrains occlusion handling, and the absence of 3D or physical priors on motion.
