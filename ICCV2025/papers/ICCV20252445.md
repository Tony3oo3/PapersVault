## Problem

Existing DiT-based text-to-image models (SD3, Flux) can render text but lack fine-grained word-level typographic control (e.g., applying bold or italic to a specific word), suffer from font inconsistency across generated images, and exhibit style inconsistency and content leakage in artistic text rendering. No prior method has achieved word-level control, and existing approaches operate only at the paragraph or font level.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252445/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt in which specific words are enclosed by ETC-tokens (e.g., `<b*>word</b*>` for bold, `<i*>word</i*>` for italic, `<u*>word</u*>` for underline) along with a font specification, and optionally a style reference image for artistic rendering. It produces a 512×512 RGB image where the enclosed typographic attribute is applied only to the targeted word while the rest of the text uses the base font, and the overall image matches the style of the reference image when provided. The ETC-tokens are added to the T5 text encoder vocabulary, while the CLIP encoder receives a fixed prompt since its pooled embedding provides only coarse-grained conditioning.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages on Flux.1-dev. First, Typography Control Fine-tuning (TC-FT) fine-tunes only the joint text attention (Txt-Attn) in MM-DiT blocks (~5% of parameters) alongside new ETC-tokens, leveraging the observation that Txt-Attn undergoes roughly double the weight change of other components during full fine-tuning, making it the most parameter-efficient target for learning localized typographic effects. The ETC-tokens use enclosing start/end pairs so the model learns both the attribute and its precise word location. Second, a Style Control Adapter (SCA) injects style via a Decoupled Joint Attention (DJA) mechanism that adds a separate cross-attention branch for CLIP-encoded image features (only W_k′ and W_v′ trainable per layer), decoupling style from text content to prevent leakage. SCA training is two-phase: pretraining on ~580k general image-text pairs (SC-general) followed by fine-tuning on ~20k artistic text images (SC-artext) to adapt to the ATR domain. The TC-Dataset is synthesized via an HTML rendering pipeline that produces images with controlled fonts and word-level attributes, constituting the first word-level typography control dataset.

## Results

On BTR-bench, FonTS achieves 96.98 FontCLIP-I, 63.64 Font-Con (user study), 55.00 Word-Acc (GPT-4o verified), and 82.85 OCR-Acc, outperforming all baselines on font consistency and word-level control while trailing only Glyph-ByT5 on OCR accuracy (82.85 vs. 96.36). On ATR-bench, FonTS reaches 64.27 CLIP-I, 31.82/34.09 Style-Con, and 61.78/59.66 OCR-Acc, surpassing Flux-IPA+sc (23.35/25.76 Style-Con, 13.02/30.15 OCR-Acc) by a wide margin. On MARIO-bench for scene text rendering, FonTS improves OCR-Acc from 24.17 (Flux) to 53.57 while maintaining CLIP-I (31.66 vs. 29.93), confirming no degradation in STR capability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Glyph-ByT5 [30], FonTS wins on FontCLIP-I (96.98 vs. 93.68), Font-Con (63.64 vs. 32.73), and uniquely achieves Word-Acc (55.00 vs. not supported), but loses on OCR-Acc (82.85 vs. 96.36), which the authors attribute to Glyph-ByT5 being trained on millions of text images versus FonTS's 50k. Against TextDiffuser-2 [10], FonTS dominates across all four BTR metrics, with Font-Con improving from 1.81 to 63.64 and OCR-Acc from 42.86 to 82.85. Against SD3 [16] and Flux [1], FonTS achieves substantially higher font consistency (Font-Con 63.64 vs. 0.91 for both) and is the only method supporting word-level typographic control; OCR-Acc is lower than Flux (82.85 vs. 66.49) but higher than SD3 (82.85 vs. 48.05). In artistic text rendering, FonTS outperforms Flux-IPA+sc [73] on Style-Con (31.82/34.09 vs. 23.35/25.76) and OCR-Acc (61.78/59.66 vs. 13.02/30.15), demonstrating that the decoupled joint attention prevents the severe content leakage observed in IP-Adapter-based approaches. In scene text rendering on MARIO-bench, FonTS more than doubles Flux's OCR-Acc (53.57 vs. 24.17), confirming that the TC-FT and SCA stages do not degrade the base model's STR capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DiT-based T2I models (SD3, Flux.1-dev) and extends the text rendering line of work (Glyph-ByT5, TextDiffuser-2, AnyText) that has focused on content accuracy and paragraph-level font control. It departs from prior controllable generation methods (IP-Adapter, Textual Inversion, T2I-Adapter) by introducing enclosing typography control tokens (ETC-tokens) for word-level localization and a decoupled joint attention mechanism for style injection, both adapted to the multi-encoder (CLIP + T5) DiT architecture. The key conceptual departure is that previous methods achieved at most paragraph-level typographic control, while this work targets precise word-level attribute application through a parameter-efficient fine-tuning strategy confined to the Txt-Attn component (5% of backbone parameters).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Word-level typography control addresses a practical gap in design workflows (advertising, branding, logo creation) where specific words need distinct typographic treatment within a single image. The parameter-efficient approach (fine-tuning only 5% of parameters) makes the technique accessible without full model retraining, and the SCA's use of CLIP's text-agnostic embeddings offers a principled solution to the content leakage problem in artistic text stylization. Limitations include language drift at higher training steps, reliance on a single base model (Flux.1-dev), and English-only evaluation; the authors note multilingual extension as future work. Open questions include whether the ETC-token approach generalizes to other DiT architectures and whether the 5% parameter finding holds at different scales.
