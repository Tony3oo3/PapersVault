## Problem

Previous 3D morphing methods require explicit point-to-point correspondences and smooth deformation trajectories, restricting them to shape-only morphing on untextured, topologically aligned datasets (e.g., FAUST, Shrec'20). This necessitates labor-intensive domain-specific preprocessing and limits generalization to diverse cross-category object pairs. The paper asks whether explicit correspondence is truly necessary and whether a generic 3D diffusion prior can enable textured 3D morphing without any alignment data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source and target textured 3D object (each represented as a 3D Gaussian with point-cloud geometry and texture features) plus a text prompt describing each object. It produces a sequence of 20 interpolated textured 3D Gaussian representations, each fully renderable as multi-view images. Interpolation weights are sampled from a Beta(5,5) distribution over [0,1], and no category-specific alignment data, explicit correspondence, or topological matching is required at any stage.

## Method

The method uses Gaussian Anything (a two-stage DiT-based 3D diffusion model operating in point-cloud latent space) as a regenerative prior, interpolating source and target information at three levels: initial noises (via slerp after diffusion inversion), LoRA fine-tuned model parameters (linear interpolation), and CLIP text condition features (linear interpolation). Each interpolated frame is then fully regenerated by the diffusion model rather than geometrically blended. Three strategies improve quality: (1) Attention Fusion, which concatenates and blends Q/K/V from source, target, and interpolated attention paths to smooth transitions; (2) Token Reordering, which matches semantically similar tokens (by minimizing pairwise token distance) before attention computation in intermediate denoising steps to guide implicit correspondences; and (3) Low-Frequency Enhancement, which amplifies low-frequency FFT components of tokens at key time steps to counteract surface degradation caused by excessive attention fusion.

## Results

The proposed method achieves a FID of 6.36 and KID of 0.001 on textured 3D morphing, dramatically outperforming the best baseline (Luma, FID 95.49, KID 0.088) by over 15× on FID. On GPT-evaluated plausibility metrics, the method scores a perfect 1.00 on both Structural Plausibility (STP-G) and Semantic Plausibility (SEP-G), compared to 0.83/0.77 for the next-best baseline. In user studies, the method scores 0.915 (STP-U) and 0.950 (SEP-U), far exceeding MorphFlow's 0.555/0.505. On smoothness metrics, PPL is 3.02 (vs. 3.10 for MorphFlow) and PDV is 0.0001 (tied with MorphFlow).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five baselines spanning 2D diffusion (DiffMorpher, AID), multi-view diffusion (MV-Adapter), video generation (Luma), and 3D volumetric morphing (MorphFlow). The proposed method wins on every metric against all five baselines, with the most dramatic margins on FID (6.36 vs. 95.49 for Luma, the best non-3D baseline) and KID (0.001 vs. 0.088). Against MorphFlow, the closest task-matched baseline using optimal transport on volumetric representations, the method improves FID by 23× (6.36 vs. 147.70) and KID by 150×, while also achieving perfect GPT-evaluated plausibility scores (1.00 vs. 0.87/0.90). On smoothness (PPL), the method edges out MorphFlow (3.02 vs. 3.10) and ties on PDV (0.0001). The 2D-based methods (DiffMorpher, AID) suffer from mode collapse and lack of 3D consistency, while MV-Adapter is limited by pixel-aligned morphing across large spatial distances, and Luma produces incomplete or out-of-frame content with suboptimal structural consistency in intermediate frames.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the attention-fusion morphing strategies from 2D diffusion (DiffMorpher, AID) into a native 3D setting by leveraging Gaussian Anything's point-cloud latent diffusion with a DiT backbone. It departs from the dominant correspondence-and-deformation paradigm of prior 3D morphing (NeuroMorph, MorphFlow) by eliminating explicit point-to-point matching entirely, instead relying on the diffusion model's implicit semantic alignment. The closest prior work, MorphFlow, performs textured 3D morphing via optimal transport on volumetric representations but lacks a generative prior to enforce plausibility at intermediate stages, leading to structural artifacts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By framing textured 3D morphing as a regenerative task driven by a 3D diffusion prior, this work removes the labor-intensive preprocessing (registration, topological alignment) that has constrained prior methods to narrow in-domain datasets, enabling cross-category morphing with a single set of hyperparameters. The empirical finding that position-identical tokens do not capture semantic correspondence in 3D diffusion (contradicting the 2D intuition) motivates the Token Reordering strategy, which could inform future work on 3D diffusion model interpretability. Key limitations include dependence on the quality of the underlying 3D diffusion model (Gaussian Anything), and the authors acknowledge that complex morphing remains challenging; future directions include scaling to stronger 3D generators, few-shot motion interpolation, and 4D transitions.
