## Problem

Existing video-based person ReID methods aggregate temporal and spatial features using RNNs, 3D convolutions, or attention but fail to dynamically identify and prioritize the most discriminative biometric cues—static body shape (long-term), appearance (short-term), and gait (temporal)—for a given query-gallery pair. They tend to over-rely on the most stable overlapping features (typically long-term shape), neglecting other features that become critical when clothing changes or when individuals are in motion, leading to degraded matching in diverse real-world scenarios.

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB video sequences (T=16 frames) that are Pad-and-Resized to 224×224 while preserving body aspect ratio, processed frame-by-frame through a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 encoder. In dual-input mode, a query video and a gallery video are processed in parallel and their multi-layer features (4 ViT layers × 257 tokens × d dims, concatenated to 4d) are jointly fed to the gating networks. The output is a single identity feature vector f ∈ R^d per video (or a composite vector per pair in dual-input mode), compared via cosine similarity for retrieval.

## Method

HAMoBE employs a two-level hierarchical Mixture of Biometric Experts on top of frozen CLIP multi-layer features. The first level comprises 8 MLP experts that distill low-level characteristics (motion patterns, shape outlines, appearance cues) from the concatenated 4-layer CLIP representations, with a gating network producing per-token, per-expert weights that route features to the second level. The second level contains three specialized experts: a Long-Term expert (MLP + average pooling over frames for static body shape), a Short-Term expert (MLP + average pooling for per-video appearance), and a Temporal expert (MLP followed by a 4-block Transformer decoder with temporal modulation for gait). A novel dual-input decision gating network, applied at both levels, concatenates gallery and query features along the spatial dimension, applies multi-head self-attention to extract cross-video relevance, and produces adaptive weights that dynamically adjust each expert's contribution based on the specific paired-video context. Training combines cross-entropy on the fused feature with three consistency losses (long-term, short-term, temporal) and a contrastive loss in dual-input mode, using Adam with α=0.5, β=1, and margin m=4.

## Results

On MEVID (the most challenging benchmark), HAMoBE achieves 54.3% mAP and 74.0% top-1 under Same Clothes, and 15.1% mAP and 20.1% top-1 under Different Clothes, surpassing the prior best CLIP3DReID by +14.7% mAP (SC) and +5.5% mAP (DC). On MARS, HAMoBE reaches 91.1% mAP and 94.6% top-1, outperforming TF-CLIP by +1.7% mAP; on LS-VID it achieves 85.2% mAP and 92.1% top-1 (+1.4% mAP over TF-CLIP). On CCVID, HAMoBE attains 87.2% mAP / 88.4% top-1 (General) and 85.8% mAP / 87.6% top-1 (Different Clothes), exceeding CLIP3DReID by +3.3% and +4.2% mAP respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HAMoBE outperforms all 25 baselines across all four datasets and both clothing protocols. On MARS and LS-VID (same-clothing only), the closest competitor is TF-CLIP [99] (AAAI'24), which HAMoBE surpasses by +1.7%/+1.4% mAP and +1.6%/+1.7% top-1 respectively; other strong baselines like DCCT [64] (87.5 mAP on MARS) and LSTRL [65] (82.4 mAP on LS-VID) trail by 3.6% and 2.8% mAP. On CCVID, CLIP3DReID [60] (CVPR'24) is the nearest baseline at 83.9/83.2 mAP (General/DC), with HAMoBE leading by +3.3%/+4.2% mAP; gait-specific methods GaitSet [6] and GaitNet [106] lag substantially (81.9/62.6 mAP General), confirming that gait alone is insufficient without adaptive fusion. On MEVID, the gap widens dramatically: HAMoBE leads CLIP3DReID [60] by +14.7% mAP (SC) and +5.5% mAP (DC), and CAL [26] (CVPR'22) by +15.3% mAP (SC) and +10.8% mAP (DC), while older methods like PSTA [88], AP3D [25], and TCLNet [30] fall 15–45 mAP points behind. The consistent margin across all settings—particularly the large DC gains on MEVID—indicates that the hierarchical MoE and dual-input gating provide robustness that no single-architecture baseline matches under clothing variation and multi-view conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the frozen-CLIP video feature extraction paradigm introduced by Lin et al. (ECCV 2022), adopting their multi-layer ViT feature concatenation and Transformer decoder formulation for temporal modeling. It departs from the dominant video ReID lineage (RNN/LSTM, 3D convolution, attention-based aggregation) by reframing feature fusion as a hierarchical Mixture of Experts problem, where specialized biometric experts (long-term body shape, short-term appearance, temporal gait) are dynamically weighted by a dual-input gating network. The MoE concept is borrowed from NLP and biometric fusion literature, but its application to disentangling and adaptively routing video person ReID features is novel. The dual-input decision mechanism, which conditions gating weights on the joint query-gallery context rather than a single tracklet, represents a further departure from prior single-input ReID pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The adaptive expert-routing mechanism addresses a genuine limitation in prior video ReID methods: fixed feature aggregation cannot prioritize the most discriminative biometric cue (gait vs. appearance vs. body shape) when scene conditions shift. The large margin on MEVID (+13% Rank1 over prior best) suggests that unconstrained, multi-view, clothing-varying scenarios are where adaptive feature selection provides the most value, though the absolute numbers remain modest (74% top-1 SC). Open questions include whether the frozen-CLIP dependency limits ceiling performance, whether the dual-input mode's ~10-minute overhead is acceptable for real-time surveillance deployment, and whether the approach generalizes to longer videos or higher-resolution inputs beyond the 16-frame, 224×224 setting tested here.
