## Problem

Existing instructional video editing methods are not end-to-end (requiring intermediate masks, layouts, or captions), rely on multiple training objectives beyond diffusion loss, and fail to handle diverse editing tasks (addition, removal, stylization, grounding) within a single model. The paper addresses the lack of a unified, versatile framework that can interpret complex user instructions—including those requiring multimodal reasoning—and execute pixel-level video edits, grounding, and segmentation in one pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251565/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a video (sequence of n frames) and a free-form text instruction or reasoning question as input; optionally, a reference image can be supplied for zero-shot multimodal instruction following (e.g., style transfer from an image). The MLLM processes the video frames and instruction to produce per-frame continuous grounded task query embeddings, which condition the video diffusion model to render the target video in pixel space. Outputs span edited videos (addition, removal, object/environment changing, stylization), grounded videos with color-filled regions for object localization, and reasoning segmentation videos—all generated from the same video-to-video formulation.

## Method

VEGGIE couples an MLLM with a video diffusion model via continuous learnable grounded task query embeddings (one per frame) and a single-layer MLP alignment network, enabling end-to-end training with only a diffusion loss—no auxiliary language or mask losses. The MLLM interprets the instruction and video context to emit frame-wise task queries that implicitly encode the target manipulation (object attributes, spatial relations, style parameters), which are fed into the diffusion UNet's cross-attention alongside the original video. Training follows a two-stage curriculum: Stage 1 freezes the MLLM and aligns the diffusion model with language-space queries on ~3.4M image editing pairs; Stage 2 unfreezes the full pipeline and fine-tunes end-to-end on ~136K curated multitask video samples with temporal attention layers inflated from the 2D UNet. A novel data synthesis pipeline lifts high-quality image editing datasets into the video domain by animating source images with an I2V model and generating paired edited videos via first-frame-conditioned editing, followed by automatic quality filtering.

## Results

On VEG-Bench (132 video-instruction pairs spanning 8 editing skills), VEGGIE achieves the best average ranking (1.78) among all 7 evaluated models. VEGGIE dominates on Object Grounding (SSIM 70.90, Jaccard 37.74, F-measure 21.83) and Reasoning Segmentation (SSIM 68.41, Jaccard 22.53, F-measure 15.97), where non-instructional baselines are entirely inapplicable. For Concept Addition, VEGGIE scores the highest MLLM-Judge (7.44) and Detection (57.96) among all methods. On editing tasks like Stylization and Visual Feature Changing, VEGGIE is competitive (MLLM-Judge 8.26 and 7.33) but slightly trails specialized non-instructional models like Flatten (8.31) or InsV2V (7.53).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VEGGIE outperforms all six baselines on Object Grounding and Reasoning Segmentation, where non-instructional models (VidToMe, TokenFlow, Flatten) are entirely inapplicable and instructional baselines (LGVI, InsV2V, InstructDiff) score far lower (e.g., VEGGIE Jaccard 37.74 vs. LGVI 1.52 on grounding; VEGGIE F-measure 15.97 vs. InsV2V 10.45 on reasoning segmentation). Among instructional models, VEGGIE leads on Concept Addition (MLLM-Judge 7.44 vs. InstructDiff 7.26) and is competitive on Stylization (8.26 vs. InsV2V 8.07) and Visual Feature Changing (7.33 vs. InsV2V 7.53). However, VEGGIE trails LGVI on Concept Removal (MLLM-Judge 5.07 vs. 6.59; Detection 70.22 vs. 78.40) and Flatten on Object Changing (6.63 vs. 7.37) and Environment/Background Changing (7.18 vs. 7.37). Non-instructional models like TokenFlow and Flatten achieve higher quality/smoothness scores on editing tasks but cannot handle grounding or reasoning tasks at all, whereas VEGGIE serves as a unified all-in-one model with the best overall average ranking (1.78) among models capable of all 8 skills.

## Relation to Prior Work

VEGGIE sits at the intersection of instructional video editing (InstructV2V, LGVI, VPLM/Raccoon), MLLM-guided image editing (SmartEdit, InstructPix2Pix), and video grounding/segmentation (RVoS, MeViS, LISA). It departs from prior MLLM-to-diffusion pipelines that rely on discrete text tokens or intermediate masks by introducing continuous learnable task query embeddings, enabling end-to-end training with a single diffusion loss. The curriculum learning strategy (image alignment → video fine-tuning) and the I2V-based data synthesis pipeline are novel contributions that address the data scarcity problem for multitask video editing. Unlike tool-use or multi-model approaches, VEGGIE unifies editing, grounding, and reasoning segmentation in one model without auxiliary losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VEGGIE provides the first unified, end-to-end framework that handles 8 distinct video editing/grounding skills under a single diffusion loss, and VEG-Bench offers the first fine-grained benchmark for instructional video editing with per-skill metrics. The emergent zero-shot multimodal instruction following and few-shot in-context editing suggest the MLLM-diffusion coupling generalizes beyond training distribution, opening avenues for more flexible video manipulation. Limitations include reliance on SD1.5 (constraining video quality and length), a small VEG-Bench (132 pairs), and the data synthesis pipeline's dependence on external I2V and video editing models. Open questions include whether the curriculum strategy and single-loss formulation scale to larger video diffusion backbones and longer sequences.
