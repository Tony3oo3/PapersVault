## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting and related methods render primitives using view-independent opacity and per-frame center-based sorting, which violates the assumptions of non-overlap and correct ordering in practice. This causes 'popping' artifacts where rendered colors abruptly change as the camera moves, and prevents physically accurate blending of overlapping primitives. The paper asks whether a primitive-based representation can achieve exact, 3D-consistent volumetric rendering at real-time framerates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251851/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of posed RGB images and a sparse SfM point cloud as input, and produces a scene represented as a collection of constant-density ellipsoids (each with position, rotation quaternion, scale vector, density, and view-dependent color via 2nd-order spherical harmonics). Rendering is performed via ray tracing through an OptiX BVH, supporting arbitrary camera models including fisheye distortion and defocus blur, at approximately 30 FPS at 720p on a single RTX4090. Training takes 1–2 hours on the same GPU.

## Method

EVER represents the scene as constant-density volumetric ellipsoids and renders each ray by intersecting every primitive twice (front and back surfaces), accumulating density and premultiplied-color deltas along the ray to obtain piecewise-constant density and color per interval. Because density and color are constant within each interval, the volume rendering integral has a closed-form solution (identical to Max's quadrature rule), enabling exact alpha compositing with zero approximation error regardless of how many primitives overlap. Density is parameterized through a log-transformed opacity variable to avoid gradient vanishing as primitives become opaque, and an additional splitting criterion triggers when opacity approaches 1 along the major axis. The optimization reuses 3DGS's Adaptive Density Control with modified thresholds for the smaller spatial gradients of density-based primitives, and an anisotropy regularizer keeps bounding boxes compact for efficient BVH traversal.

## Results

On the Zip-NeRF dataset, EVER achieves SSIM 0.845 and LPIPS 0.308, surpassing the offline state-of-the-art Zip-NeRF (SSIM 0.836, LPIPS 0.305) on sharpness metrics while remaining real-time (24 FPS vs 0.5 FPS). On Mip-NeRF 360, EVER achieves the best SSIM (0.825) and LPIPS (0.194) among all real-time methods, though it trails SMERF (PSNR 27.99) and Zip-NeRF (PSNR 28.54) on PSNR. On Tanks & Temples + DeepBlending, EVER achieves the best SSIM (0.889) and LPIPS (0.234) across all compared methods. Training takes 1–2 hours on a single RTX 4090, with rendering at ~30 FPS at 720p on Zip-NeRF scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EVER outperforms all 3DGS-based baselines (3DGS, StopThePop, 3DGRT) on SSIM and LPIPS across every dataset, with the largest gains on the challenging Zip-NeRF scenes (SSIM 0.845 vs 0.817–0.819; LPIPS 0.308 vs 0.352–0.358). Against SMERF, EVER wins on SSIM and LPIPS for Zip-NeRF (0.845/0.308 vs 0.829/0.339) but trails on PSNR (26.58 vs 27.28), and on Mip-NeRF 360 SMERF leads on PSNR (27.99 vs 27.51) while EVER leads on SSIM and LPIPS. Against the offline Zip-NeRF, EVER achieves higher SSIM on Zip-NeRF (0.845 vs 0.836) but lower PSNR (26.58 vs 27.37) and marginally higher LPIPS (0.308 vs 0.305), while running at 24 FPS versus 0.5 FPS. On Tanks & Temples + DeepBlending, EVER achieves the best SSIM (0.889) and LPIPS (0.234) among all methods, including 3DGRT (0.865/0.254). The popping metric (Table 2) confirms EVER eliminates view-inconsistency artifacts, achieving 0.0000 error at step=7 across all four dataset groups, compared to 0.0055–0.0114 for StopThePop and 0.0122–0.0315 for 3DGS.

## Relation to Prior Work

EVER extends 3D Gaussian Splatting by replacing its opacity-based alpha compositing of 2D billboards with a density-based volumetric rendering of 3D ellipsoids, enabling exact integration of the volume rendering equation via a double-intersection (front/back surface) formulation. The ray-tracing infrastructure is borrowed from 3DGRT, and the optimization pipeline (adaptive density control, splitting/cloning heuristics) is inherited from 3DGS with modifications for density parameters. Conceptually, the paper positions itself between the slow-but-accurate NeRF/Zip-NeRF family (which uses quadrature over neural or grid-based fields) and the fast-but-approximate 3DGS family, arguing that explicit constant-density primitives with exact closed-form integration can achieve both real-time speed and physical accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EVER eliminates the popping artifacts that have been a persistent limitation of 3DGS and its variants, with a clean theoretical guarantee (the volume rendering integral is continuous in ray direction) and empirical confirmation via the StopThePop popping metric. The ability to match or exceed offline Zip-NeRF on perceptual quality (SSIM, LPIPS) while rendering in real-time is a meaningful step toward practical large-scale scene exploration. However, the method is significantly slower than 3DGS (36 vs. 224 FPS) and uses more memory, and the paper acknowledges that improved 3DGS densification heuristics could further close the gap. Open questions include whether the ray-tracing overhead can be reduced, whether the approach scales to scenes with tens of millions of primitives, and whether the density-based formulation enables new regularizers (e.g., distortion loss) that were previously inapplicable to 3DGS.
