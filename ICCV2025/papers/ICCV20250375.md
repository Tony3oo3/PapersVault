## Problem

Current concept erasure methods for text-to-image diffusion models struggle to reliably remove malicious concepts that are implicitly embedded in prompts through metaphorical expressions or adversarial prompts, without significantly degrading the model's ability to generate unrelated content. The core tension is that stronger erasure typically requires more aggressive parameter modification, which causes knowledge forgetting on benign concepts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250375/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

TRCE modifies the parameters of a pre-trained latent diffusion model (SD 1.4) so that at inference time, any text prompt (including adversarial ones) is fed through the standard CLIP text encoder and DDIM sampling pipeline (30 steps, 512×512 output) to produce a generated image that avoids the erased concept. The erasure itself is applied offline: Stage 1 requires 300 augmented prompts (generated via GPT-4o) to compute a closed-form matrix update, and Stage 2 requires 100–300 cached sampling trajectories for contrastive fine-tuning over 3 epochs (~300 s on one RTX 4090).

## Method

TRCE employs a two-stage erasure strategy. In Stage 1 (Textual Semantic Erasure), it identifies the [EoT] (End-of-Text) embedding as the critical mapping objective—rather than keyword embeddings—because [EoT] carries the overall prompt semantics while preserving contextual structure; a closed-form solution then refines the cross-attention Key and Value matrices to map malicious prompt embeddings to contextually similar safe-concept embeddings. In Stage 2 (Denoising Trajectory Steering), it fine-tunes only the visual layers (self-attention and cross-attention query matrices) of the Stage-1 model using a triplet margin contrastive loss that steers early denoising predictions (first 50% of timesteps) toward a safe reference prediction and away from an unsafe one, leveraging the deterministic ODE trajectory property so that a small early-stage perturbation shifts the entire sampling path without altering final image content. A regularization term aligns unconditional predictions with the original model to preserve general generation quality.

## Results

On the sexual content erasure task (Table 1), TRCE(T+V) achieves the lowest Attack Success Rate (ASR) across most adversarial prompt benchmarks: 1.29% on I2P, 1.27% on Ring-A-Bell, 0.70% on UnlearnDiff, and 1.99% on P4D, compared to 34.69% for the unmodified SD1.4 baseline. In terms of knowledge preservation, TRCE(T+V) attains FIDgen of 12.08, FIDreal of 26.89, and CLIP-Score of 30.71, closely matching the original model's FIDreal (27.18) and CLIP-S (30.97). On the multi-concept erasure task (Table 2), TRCE(T+V) achieves an overall inappropriate rate of 2.0%, substantially outperforming the next-best method MACE (5.6%) and the unmodified model (35.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRCE(T+V) outperforms all ten baselines on the majority of adversarial prompt benchmarks. Against the strongest prior method AdvUnlearn, TRCE achieves lower ASR on I2P (1.29% vs 1.71%), Ring-A-Bell (1.27% vs 6.33%), and UnlearnDiff (0.70% vs 3.52%), while tying on P4D (1.99%) and losing only on MMA (1.40% vs 0.30%). Compared to MACE, the next-best method on adversarial robustness, TRCE reduces ASR by roughly 4–5× on I2P (1.29% vs 7.09%) and Ring (1.27% vs 10.13%), while also achieving better knowledge preservation (FIDgen 12.08 vs 17.44, CLIP-S 30.71 vs 28.84). On the multi-concept erasure task, TRCE(T+V) achieves an overall inappropriate rate of 2.0%, beating MACE (5.6%), Safree (8.8%), and RECE (8.5%) by large margins, while maintaining FIDreal (27.23) and CLIP-S (30.48) close to the unmodified model. The ablation in Table 3 confirms that the two stages are complementary: TRCE(T) alone is sensitive to the preservation rate η, while adding TRCE(V) consistently improves adversarial robustness with minimal knowledge loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRCE builds directly on UCE's closed-form cross-attention editing [12], repurposing its mathematical framework but shifting the mapping objective from keyword embeddings to [EoT] (End-of-Text) embeddings, which the paper argues carry the holistic prompt semantics that drive salient region generation. The second stage (Denoising Trajectory Steering) departs from prior concept erasure work by introducing a contrastive fine-tuning objective on early denoising predictions, leveraging the deterministic ODE trajectory property of DDIM sampling — a mechanism not exploited by prior erasure methods like RECE [13] or MACE [23]. Conceptually, TRCE positions itself as addressing the implicit-semantics gap: prior methods target explicit keywords, while TRCE targets the distributed semantic influence that persists even when no malicious keyword is present.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and increasingly urgent safety gap: existing concept erasure methods remain vulnerable to adversarial and metaphorical prompts that avoid explicit keywords, leaving T2I models exploitable for generating NSFW content. The [EoT] insight provides a principled, low-cost (closed-form, seconds per concept) intervention point that practitioners can adopt without retraining. However, evaluation is limited to SD 1.4 with a single base architecture, and the approach requires re-running the full two-stage pipeline for each new concept to erase, raising scalability questions for multi-concept or continuously evolving threat landscapes. Whether the [EoT] mechanism generalizes to newer architectures (SDXL, FLUX) with different text encoders remains an open question.
