## Problem

Reconstructing usable 4D mesh animations from a single monocular video remains underexplored. Existing 4D reconstruction methods rely on multi-view or video diffusion priors and output implicit representations (NeRF, Gaussian Splatting) that are difficult to convert into meshes with consistent topology and texture. No prior work directly leverages native 3D mesh generation models to produce a ready-to-use 4D mesh animation asset from a fixed-viewpoint video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252490/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a background-removed monocular video with a fixed camera pose (processed via SAM 2) and produces a GLTF 4D animation file containing a single static mesh, a globally shared texture map, and a per-frame vertex deformation tensor. In practice, the method reconstructs every i-th frame and linearly interpolates vertex positions between keyframes to match the original video's temporal resolution, keeping the output directly loadable in mainstream graphics and game engines without further processing.

## Method

V2M4 extends the TRELLIS 3D mesh generation model to the 4D task through a five-stage pipeline that avoids score distillation sampling entirely. First, a camera search combines particle swarm optimization, DUSt3R point-cloud alignment, and gradient descent to recover per-frame object motion by inverting estimated camera poses. Second, the negative condition embedding of TRELLIS's sparse flow transformer is optimized to refine mesh appearance against the reference frame. Third, iterative pairwise registration with ARAP constraints and differentiable rendering enforces identical vertex/face topology across all frames by deforming each mesh onto the first-frame rest pose. Fourth, a single global texture map is optimized via weighted multi-view Gaussian Splatting renderings to ensure visual continuity. Finally, keyframed vertex offsets are interpolated and packaged into a GLTF animation file.

## Results

On the combined benchmark (40 videos from Consistent4D, Mixamo, and Sketchfab), V2M4 achieves CLIP 0.9073, LPIPS 0.0817, FVD 576.73, and DreamSim 0.1082, outperforming both Naïve TRELLIS (FVD 1014.45) and DreamMesh4D (FVD 855.29) across all four metrics. On the more challenging complex-motion subset, V2M4 reduces FVD by 45% over DreamMesh4D (666.04 vs. 1079.02) and by 45% over Naïve TRELLIS (666.04 vs. 1216.19). In terms of speed, V2M4 processes each frame in ~60 s on average, compared to 40 s for Naïve TRELLIS and 3 min for DreamMesh4D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Naïve TRELLIS (per-frame independent 3D mesh generation), V2M4 wins on every metric: CLIP improves from 0.8891 to 0.9073, LPIPS drops from 0.1352 to 0.0817, FVD drops from 1014.45 to 576.73, and DreamSim drops from 0.1438 to 0.1082, confirming that the reposing, appearance refinement, topology registration, and texture optimization pipeline substantially reduces inter-frame inconsistencies. Against DreamMesh4D (the only prior method that also outputs meshes), V2M4 achieves a 0.0704 CLIP gain (0.9073 vs. 0.8369), a 32.5% FVD reduction (576.73 vs. 855.29), and a 32.8% DreamSim reduction (0.1082 vs. 0.1613), while also being 3× faster per frame (60 s vs. 3 min). The advantage is most pronounced on the complex-motion subset, where DreamMesh4D's FVD degrades to 1079.02 while V2M4 holds at 666.04. A qualitative comparison with L4GM (a trained multi-view Gaussian Splatting 4D model) shows V2M4 preserving more accurate fine-grained geometry, though no quantitative metrics are reported for this comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

V2M4 departs from the dominant 4D reconstruction paradigm that relies on multi-view image or video diffusion models as priors (Consistent4D, STAG4D, DreamMesh4D, L4GM) combined with NeRF or Gaussian Splatting representations. Instead, it builds on TRELLIS, a native 3D mesh generation model using structured latent representations (SLAT) and rectified flow, and extends it to the 4D setting through a structured five-step workflow. The camera search component repurposes DUSt3R's dense stereo predictions and particle swarm optimization in a novel way to recover object motion from canonicalized mesh outputs, while the topology-consistency step adapts differentiable rendering and ARAP constraints from inverse rendering literature to inter-frame mesh registration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that V2M4 outputs directly usable GLTF 4D mesh animation assets compatible with mainstream game engines and graphics software, eliminating the difficult implicit-to-mesh conversion step that plagues NeRF/GS-based 4D methods. Key limitations include dependence on TRELLIS's per-frame 3D quality, artifacts under topology changes (since the first-frame mesh is enforced as rest pose), and a fixed sampling rate that is inefficient for variable motion dynamics. Open questions include whether adaptive temporal sampling, topology-change-aware registration, and integration with more advanced 3D generators (Hunyuan3D-2.0, TripoSG) can further close the gap to ground-truth 4D assets.
