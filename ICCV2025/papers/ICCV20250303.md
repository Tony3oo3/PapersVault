## Problem

Existing world models for autonomous driving (e.g., OccWorld, OccLLaMA) rely on precomputed 3D occupancy as input rather than raw sensor data, suffer from degraded accuracy in long-term predictions, and lack principled handling of sensor uncertainties such as LiDAR noise and image occlusions. This limits their ability to produce reliable future scene forecasts and safe trajectories in complex, rapidly changing driving environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250303/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

GWM consumes raw multi-modal sensor data over a 2-second historical window: multi-view camera images, LiDAR point clouds, and SAM-predicted semantic maps. It outputs future 3D semantic occupancy grids (forecasting 3 seconds ahead) and ego vehicle trajectories. Uniquely, each voxel in the occupancy representation is augmented with a probabilistic uncertainty value derived from the underlying Gaussian distributions, enabling uncertainty-aware downstream planning rather than a single deterministic occupancy estimate.

## Method

GWM is a two-stage sensor-to-planning framework. In Stage 1, a Gaussian representation learner initializes 3D Gaussians from LiDAR priors (each parameterized by mean, scale, rotation, RGB, and 6-channel semantics) and refines them via self-supervised differentiable rendering back to 2D RGB and semantic images, learning physically constrained cross-modal embeddings. In Stage 2, an uncertainty-aware simulator (a spatiotemporal transformer with masked temporal attention) takes past 4D occupancy uncertainty and predicts future scene evolution, while an uncertainty-aware planner generates trajectories by minimizing a cost volume derived from BEV-projected occupancy uncertainty plus safety and collision penalties. The planner's reliability is further enforced through a conformal-prediction-based uncertainty loss computed over reconstruction inconsistency scores. The key architectural distinction from prior voxel-based world models is the replacement of fixed-grid occupancy with sparse 3D semantic Gaussians that natively encode per-point variance, providing a probabilistic foundation for uncertainty propagation through both simulation and planning.

## Results

On the Occ3D 4D occupancy forecasting task, GWM achieves 10.12% Avg. mIoU and 24.60% Avg. IoU over 1–3 s future frames, outperforming OccLLaMA-F (8.66% mIoU, 22.99% IoU) and OccWorld-F (6.16% mIoU, 18.99% IoU). On the nuScenes motion planning task, GWM attains 1.13 m Avg. L2 error and 0.59% Avg. collision rate, beating OccLLaMA-F (1.20 m, 0.70%) and OccWorld-F (1.34 m, 0.73%). GWM also matches or approaches the performance of ground-truth-occupancy baselines (e.g., OccLLaMA-O: 1.14 m L2, 0.49% collision) while using only predicted sensor inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GWM outperforms all camera-based baselines on both tasks. In 4D occupancy forecasting, GWM improves over OccLLaMA-F by +1.46% Avg. mIoU and +1.61% Avg. IoU, and over OccWorld-F by +3.96% mIoU and +5.61% IoU. In motion planning, GWM reduces Avg. L2 error by 0.07 m over OccLLaMA-F (1.13 m vs 1.20 m) and 0.21 m over OccWorld-F (1.13 m vs 1.34 m), while lowering Avg. collision rate to 0.59% (vs 0.70% and 0.73%). Notably, GWM achieves these gains without language or action inputs, relying solely on Camera + LiDAR, whereas OccLLaMA-F additionally uses language and action modalities. On the challenging occlusion/noise subset (Table 3), GWM further widens its lead over OccWorld-F, reaching 9.14% mIoU vs 5.72% and 1.18 m L2 vs 1.42 m, confirming robustness under sensor degradation. GWM's performance is competitive with ground-truth-occupancy baselines (OccLLaMA-O: 1.14 m L2, 0.49% collision), suggesting the Gaussian representation effectively compensates for the absence of oracle occupancy inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

GWM extends the world-model line of work initiated by OccWorld and OccLLaMA, which predict future 3D occupancy and ego trajectories from precomputed occupancy maps, by replacing the fixed voxel grid with a 3D Gaussian Splatting representation initialized from LiDAR priors and refined via differentiable rendering. The 1st-stage self-supervised pre-training draws on the authors' prior point-cloud representation learning work, while the 2nd-stage spatiotemporal transformer and cost-volume planner follow the architectural patterns established by OccWorld and ST-P3. The key departure is a sensor-to-planning pipeline that ingests raw camera and LiDAR inputs directly, avoiding the information bottleneck of intermediate occupancy estimation, and the probabilistic Gaussian formulation provides an explicit uncertainty channel absent in prior voxel-based world models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a Gaussian-based representation can match or exceed ground-truth-occupancy baselines (OccWorld-O, OccLLaMA-O) using only predicted inputs, GWM suggests that the occupancy-estimation bottleneck in current world-model pipelines may be less critical than previously assumed, provided the representation carries sufficient geometric and semantic fidelity. However, the absolute gains over the strongest predicted-input baselines remain modest (~1.5% mIoU, ~0.07 m L2), and the ablation reveals that the semantic map contributes only marginally, leaving open whether the Gaussian prior's advantage is primarily geometric or whether richer semantic integration could widen the gap. Practical deployment questions—real-time inference cost of differentiable Gaussian rendering, scalability to longer prediction horizons, and robustness beyond the curated challenging subset—remain unaddressed.
