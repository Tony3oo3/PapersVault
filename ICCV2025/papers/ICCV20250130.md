## Problem

Existing driving scene synthesis methods face a fundamental trade-off: image-focused approaches produce high-quality frames but fail at temporal consistency, while video-capable methods achieve frame coherence at the cost of geometric accuracy and physically implausible motion. The core gap is the absence of explicit, physics-aligned motion modeling that bridges static map-based control signals with dynamic multi-frame video generation, particularly for complex driving scenes with occlusions, appearing/disappearing objects, and multi-object interactions.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes sequential BEV semantic maps (interpolated from 2 Hz LiDAR to 12 Hz camera rate) as the primary geometric control signal, along with text prompts for content guidance. Stage 1 produces temporally consistent 3D semantic occupancy grids in a VQ-VAE latent space (32× compression ratio); Stage 2 renders these occupancies into per-camera 2D semantic and depth maps that condition a pretrained SVDXL diffusion U-Net to output multi-view driving videos. The semantic flow estimation operates on consecutive BEV map pairs, producing dense flow vectors, per-pixel uncertainty, and categorical flow classification that are consumed at every transformer block during denoising.

## Method

ConsistentCity introduces SF-DiT, a two-stage diffusion framework whose key novelty is a Semantic Flow-Modulated Cross-Attention (SFMCA) module that injects explicit motion priors into DiT blocks. A Semantic Flow Estimation module extracts three complementary signals from consecutive BEV maps—dense flow vectors, flow uncertainty, and flow classification (static/moving/appearing/disappearing)—which together form a physics-aligned motion representation. Within each DiT block, SFMCA performs three steps: (1) warping previous-frame features using estimated flow vectors, (2) learning a residual flow offset via unsupervised 2D convolutions to correct misalignments, and (3) modulating cross-attention keys with a flow-derived key channel whose contribution is dynamically weighted by flow uncertainty and classification. In Stage 2, the same SFMCA mechanism is transplanted into a ControlNet wrapping SVDXL, where rendered occupancy-derived semantic and depth maps plus estimated flow jointly condition the diffusion U-Net for photorealistic video synthesis.

## Results

On nuScenes, ConsistentCity achieves FID 8.3 and FVD 73.6, setting new state-of-the-art on both image quality and temporal consistency simultaneously. The FID of 8.3 represents a 44% improvement over the best image-only method (SyntheOcc, FID 14.8) and a 23.5% FVD improvement over the best prior video method (MagicDriveDiT, FVD 96.3). On temporal occupancy generation (OpenOccupancy), SF-DiT achieves mIoU of 33.7/26.3/23.4 at 1s/2s/3s, substantially outperforming OccWorld (25.8/15.1/10.5) and OccLLaMA (25.1/19.5/15.3), with the largest gaps at longer horizons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConsistentCity outperforms all 12 baselines on every reported metric. Against image-only methods, it reduces FID by 6.5–17.2 points (vs. SyntheOcc 14.8, MagicDrive 16.2, BEVControl 24.9, BEVGen 25.5). Among video-capable methods, the closest competitor MagicDriveDiT (FID 20.9, FVD 96.3) is surpassed by 12.6 FID and 22.7 FVD, while earlier methods show much larger gaps: Delphi (FID 15.1, FVD 115.9), Drive-WM (FID 15.8, FVD 122.7), Panacea (FID 17.0, FVD 139.0), WoVoGen (FID 27.6, FVD 417.7), and DriveDreamer (FID 52.6, FVD 452.0). On temporal occupancy prediction, SF-DiT beats OccWorld by 7.9/11.2/12.9 mIoU at 1s/2s/3s and OccLLaMA by 8.6/6.8/8.1 mIoU, with the advantage widening at longer prediction horizons where error accumulation is most severe.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConsistentCity builds on the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023) and ControlNet (Zhang et al., ICCV 2023) but departs from both direct BEV-to-image synthesis (BEVGen, MagicDrive, DriveDreamer) and prior occupancy-centric methods (UrbanDiff, SyntheOcc, WoVoGen) by introducing explicit semantic flow estimation from sequential BEV maps as a structured motion prior. Where prior video-capable methods rely on learned temporal attention or feature propagation for consistency, SF-DiT instead injects physics-aligned flow vectors, uncertainty, and classification directly into cross-attention via the SFMCA module, operating in a pretrained occupancy VQ-VAE latent space for geometric grounding. This positions the work at the intersection of diffusion transformers, occupancy world models (OccWorld, OccLLaMA), and optical-flow-guided video synthesis, but with the novelty of treating semantic flow as a first-class conditioning signal rather than a byproduct of learned features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in autonomous driving data generation: the quality-consistency trade-off where high-fidelity single frames (SyntheOcc, FID 14.8) cannot produce coherent video, and video-capable methods (MagicDriveDiT, FID 20.9) sacrifice image quality. The two-stage occupancy-then-video pipeline offers a principled path to geometrically grounded, temporally coherent synthetic data for closed-loop validation. Limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (nuScenes), short 8-frame sequences, reliance on a pretrained (not jointly trained) occupancy VQ-VAE, and 2D BEV flow rather than true 3D motion. Open questions include scalability to longer horizons, multi-camera consistency beyond single-view rendering, and whether the semantic flow formulation generalizes to sensor modalities beyond cameras.
