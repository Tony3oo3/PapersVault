## Problem

Existing anomaly detection and segmentation benchmarks produce 2D anomaly maps from single viewpoints, which is insufficient for industrial applications requiring precise 3D defect localisation across an entire object surface. Additionally, prior benchmarks assume multiple nominal training instances per class, ignoring the costly and impractical data collection this demands in manufacturing, where a single nominal sample or even a CAD prototype should suffice. No prior benchmark simultaneously addresses multiview 3D output, single-instance training, and synthetic-to-real generalisation.

## Input / Output

Methods receive 12–36 grayscale images (4096×3000, 12 Mpx) from concentric-hemisphere viewpoints, paired with pixel-aligned point clouds (~5–7M points) or rendered depth maps, plus an integrated 3D mesh per instance. The required output is a voxel-based Anomaly Volume at 2 mm resolution (e.g., 330×326×317 voxels for a Plastic Stool), where each voxel carries a continuous anomaly score, alongside a single global score per instance for detection. Training data consists of exactly one instance per object type—either a real scan or a synthetic render from the CAD model—making this a single-instance, unsupervised setting.

## Method

SiM3D is a benchmark rather than a single method; its core technical contribution is a 3D aggregation pipeline that extends existing single-view ADS methods to the multiview 3D task. For each view, a standard method (PatchCore, EfficientAD, AST, BTF, CFM, M3DM) produces a 2D anomaly map, which is projected onto the integrated mesh using known camera intrinsics, point-cloud-to-camera transforms, and view poses, then discretised into voxel indices. Scores from all views projecting onto the same voxel are combined via max pooling to form the final Anomaly Volume, and the global detection score is the maximum voxel score. The benchmark introduces a voxel-level AUPRO (V-AUPRO@1%) metric adapted from 2D AUPRO to evaluate 3D segmentation, and defines two evaluation setups—real2real (one real nominal instance for training) and synth2real (CAD-rendered data for training, real data for testing)—to probe domain-shift robustness.

## Results

On the SiM3D benchmark, the best detection performance (I-AUROC) in the real2real setup is 0.754 (PatchCore with WRN-101), while in the synth2real setup it drops to 0.544 (AST). For segmentation (V-AUPRO@1%), AST with RGB+Depth achieves the best scores in both setups (0.699 real2real, 0.695 synth2real). The domain gap between real2real and synth2real is stark for detection (0.754 vs. 0.544) but minimal for segmentation (0.699 vs. 0.695), suggesting that localising defects is more transferable than flagging them. All scores are far from saturated, confirming the benchmark's difficulty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Six single-view ADS methods (PatchCore, EfficientAD, AST, BTF, CFM, M3DM) are adapted to the multiview 3D task via 2D-to-3D projection and voxel-wise max-pooling. RGB-only methods consistently outperform multimodal RGB+point-cloud counterparts by 10-30 points in I-AUROC, because the latter were tuned for roughly 8K-point clouds whereas SiM3D uses approximately 2.3M points after downsampling. Memory-bank methods (PatchCore, M3DM) edge out training-based methods (EfficientAD, CFM) by roughly 5-15 points, likely because the single-instance setup provides too few views for the latter to optimise. AST with depth maps is the strongest segmenter (0.699/0.695 V-AUPRO@1%), while PatchCore with WRN-101 is the strongest detector in real2real (0.754 I-AUROC). Replacing point clouds with depth maps in multimodal methods yields large gains (e.g., BTF: 0.478 to 0.707 I-AUROC), yet no method saturates the benchmark, leaving substantial room for improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

SiM3D extends the ADS benchmark lineage (MVTec AD → MVTec 3D-AD → Eyecandies → PAD → Real-IAD → Real3D-AD) by simultaneously addressing four dimensions that no prior benchmark has combined: 3D voxel-based output (Anomaly Volume) rather than 2D Anomaly Maps, multiview integration at test time, single-instance training, and synthetic-to-real generalisation. Rather than proposing a new detection method, the paper repurposes established singleview approaches (PatchCore, EfficientAD, AST, BTF, M3DM, CFM) through a straightforward 3D projection-and-aggregation strategy to establish reference baselines for the new task. The dataset itself is novel, acquired with a ZEISS Atos Q industrial scanner and supplemented with Blender-rendered synthetic scans aligned to the same viewpoints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SiM3D is the first benchmark to formalise comprehensive 3D anomaly detection as a multiview, multimodal, single-instance problem with a synth2real training regime, directly targeting the cost and practicality constraints of real manufacturing pipelines. The baseline results reveal that current multimodal ADS methods do not scale to the ~5–7M-point resolution of industrial scans, and that a significant detection gap (0.754 vs. 0.540 I-AUROC) persists between real2real and synth2real setups, while segmentation is surprisingly robust to the domain shift (0.699 vs. 0.695 V-AUPRO). Open questions include the need for depth-map foundation models, native multiview 3D integration architectures, and training strategies that generalise from a single synthetic prototype to real objects.
