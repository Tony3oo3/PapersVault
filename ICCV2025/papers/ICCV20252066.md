## Problem

Deploying vision-language models for open-vocabulary object detection on satellites faces two critical gaps: existing VLMs are pre-trained on natural images and fail to exploit the distinctive prior of satellite imagery (tiny objects in cluttered yet monotonous backgrounds), and their complex backbones (e.g., Swin) are too computationally expensive for the 10–20 W embedded GPUs available in orbit. No prior method simultaneously addresses domain-specific feature alignment and the aggressive sparsity needed for real-time in-orbit inference.

## Input / Output

VISO takes a 1024×1024 satellite image and a text prompt (category name or referring expression) as input. It outputs oriented bounding boxes with a 5-dimensional regression (x, y, w, h, rotation angle) plus object-text similarity scores via a text-contrastive head. At deployment, the CLIP text encoder is omitted (offline vocabulary), and the learned multi-scale masks drive sparse computation in the neck and heads, so the effective output also includes per-scale binary sparsity patterns that determine which spatial locations are processed.

## Method

VISO builds on YOLO-World (CSPDarkNet backbone + CLIP text encoder + VL-PAN + text-contrastive head) and inserts a language-guided attention module immediately after the backbone. This module projects text embeddings to each feature scale and applies max-sigmoid cross-modal attention to produce multi-scale spatial masks that highlight object-relevant regions while suppressing irrelevant background. The model is pre-trained on 3.4M satellite region-text pairs using a combined loss: quality focal contrastive loss for region-text alignment plus binary cross-entropy deep supervision on the masks at three scales. At inference, the real-valued masks are hard-thresholded to binary, non-sparse locations are aggregated across scales via cross-scale projection, and dense features are converted to sparse tensors processed by SPConv sparse convolutions in the VL-PAN and heads. A level-wise granularity knob (e.g., sparse P₃ but dense P₄, P₅) allows flexible accuracy–speed trade-offs tailored to the target hardware.

## Results

In zero-shot open-vocabulary detection, VISO-L achieves 90.0% AP on MAR20, 70.7% AP on HRSC2016, and 50.2% AP on VEDAI, outperforming the best VLM baseline (GLIP-T) by up to 34.1% AP while using 27× fewer FLOPs (229G vs. 488G). In supervised detection, VISO-L reaches 63.4% AP on DOTAv2 and 52.3% AP on FAIR1Mv2, surpassing the best specialist (LSKNet-S) by 2.3% and 0.9% AP respectively. On the real-world NVIDIA Jetson Xavier NX satellite payload, VISO-S achieves 11.1 FPS (2.8× faster than YOLOv8-S at 4.0 FPS) with 48.3% AP, and sparse inference reduces FLOPs by up to 8.5× at comparable accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VLM baselines (GLIP-T, GroundingDINO-T, MM-GroundingDINO-T), VISO dominates on both accuracy and efficiency: VISO-M achieves 90.2% AP on MAR20 vs. GLIP-T's 89.4% at 4.2× fewer FLOPs (113G vs. 488G), and the gap widens dramatically on HRSC2016 (75.6% vs. 48.4%) and VEDAI (46.6% vs. 1.1%), where natural-image-pretrained VLMs fail almost entirely. Compared to its base model YOLO-World, VISO gains 33–49% AP across all three benchmarks, confirming the value of satellite-specific pre-training and the language-guided attention module. In supervised detection, VISO-L outperforms the best specialist LSKNet-S by 2.3% AP on DOTAv2 (63.4% vs. 61.1%) and 0.9% on FAIR1Mv2 (52.3% vs. 49.7%), while also achieving lower FLOPs after sparsification (128G vs. 161G). For object referring on DIOR-RSVG, VISO-L surpasses the best grounding model VLTVG by 0.4% Pr@0.5 (77.3% vs. 76.9%) and 10.6% Pr@0.7 (72.9% vs. 62.3%), with 8.5× lower FLOPs under sparsity. On the real-world Jetson Xavier NX, VISO-S achieves 11.1 FPS vs. YOLOv8-S's 4.0 FPS (2.8× speedup) and QueryDet's 2.3 FPS (4.8× speedup) while maintaining higher AP (48.3% vs. 43.6% and 34.8% respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

VISO builds directly on YOLO-World (CVPR 2024), adopting its lightweight YOLOv8 backbone and text-contrastive detection head, but departs from the broader VLM-for-detection line (GLIP, GroundingDINO) by replacing heavy Swin transformer backbones with a CNN architecture suitable for resource-constrained hardware. Conceptually, it extends prior remote sensing detection work (LSKNet, RTMDet, QueryDet) that addressed small-object detection through large kernels or high-resolution features, by instead exploiting the domain prior that satellite scenes contain few relevant objects in vast irrelevant backgrounds, enabling language-guided mask learning to produce sparse features. The method also extends region-text contrastive pre-training (as in YOLO-World) with an additional fine-grained mask supervision loss, coupling accuracy and efficiency in a single training objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: deploying open-vocabulary detection on satellites with 10–20 W GPUs, where existing VLMs are simply too expensive. The 2.8–4.8× FPS speedup validated on an actual in-orbit payload (Chaohu-1 SAR satellite's Jetson Xavier NX) makes this one of the few papers with genuine hardware-level validation for space deployment. Limitations include the CNN-only scope (sparse transformers remain unexplored), the single-object detection scenario in real-world tests, and reliance on a specific sparse inference engine (SPConv) whose performance varies across hardware. An open question is whether the language-guided mask sparsity principle generalizes to other domains with similarly sparse foreground, and whether the approach extends to video or multi-sensor satellite streams.
