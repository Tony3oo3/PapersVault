## Problem

Existing multimodal learning methods attribute under-optimized performance to modality imbalance and attempt to balance gradient contributions across modalities. This paper challenges that assumption by showing, via bias-variance decomposition, that balanced learning is suboptimal: the optimal modality dependence ratio should be inversely proportional to the modality variance ratio, not equal to 1. The paper provides both a theoretical proof and an empirical demonstration (e.g., increasing the dominant modality's gradient by 5× on CREMA-D improves rather than hurts performance).

## Input / Output

The method accepts heterogeneous multimodal inputs depending on the task: audio spectrograms (257×299 or 257×1004) paired with single or multi-frame video (224×224), RGB + optical flow sequences, or audio + visual + text triplets. All modalities are encoded by shared-parameter encoders (ResNet-18 or Swin-Base) and fused via concatenation or intermediate fusion modules. The output is a single class label per sample, with the total loss combining the multimodal cross-entropy with per-modality unimodal cross-entropy terms weighted by a hyperparameter γ.

## Method

ARL (Asymmetric Representation Learning) is a parameter-free, architecture-agnostic strategy with three components. First, auxiliary regularizers (sharing all parameters with the main model, computing unimodal logits by zeroing other modality representations) estimate each modality's prediction variance via the inverse of self-information entropy of its softmax output. Second, asymmetric learning computes modulation coefficients through a softmax over the target variance ratio and the current optimization dependence ratio (scaled by a temperature T), then re-weights each modality's backpropagated gradient as g_s + g_s * a^m, preserving a residual path so no modality's update vanishes. Third, unimodal bias regularization adds per-modality cross-entropy losses to the total objective, since the bias-variance analysis shows no positive weight combination can minimize fusion bias—only reducing each unimodal bias independently helps. The theoretical core proves that minimizing Var(f(x)) under w_0 + w_1 = 1 yields w_0/w_1 = Var(s^m_1)/Var(s^m_0), establishing the inverse-variance principle.

## Results

ARL achieves the best performance across all five datasets. On CREMA-D, it reaches 76.61% accuracy (77.14% macro F1), outperforming the second-best D&R (73.52%) by 3.09 points. On Kinetics-Sounds, ARL attains 74.28% accuracy vs. MLA's 69.62% (+4.66 points), and on AVE it scores 72.89% vs. MLA's 70.92% (+1.97 points). On the three-modality MOSI dataset, ARL achieves 79.94% accuracy, and on the 101-class UCF-101 it reaches 83.22% accuracy, both leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARL outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset and metric. The closest competitors are D&R [36] and MLA [40], which lead the balance-based and unimodal-assistance groups respectively; ARL surpasses D&R by 3.09 points on CREMA-D, 4.18 on KS, 3.27 on AVE, 0.98 on MOSI, and 1.11 on UCF-101. Notably, the gradient-balancing methods OGM-GE [21], AGM [18], and PMR [10] actually degrade performance on AVE (65.62%, 64.50%, 63.62% vs. 66.15% for plain concatenation), confirming the paper's argument that forcing balanced optimization harms the dominant modality. In contrast, ARL improves AVE by 5.75% over concatenation by allowing the audio modality to retain a larger optimization share. Grad-Blending [28] and MMPareto [34] provide moderate gains over concatenation but remain 6–8 points below ARL on CREMA-D. The results on MOSI (three modalities) and UCF-101 (101 classes) further confirm that ARL's advantage generalizes beyond the two-modality, small-scale settings where most baselines were originally designed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly challenges the dominant framing in multimodal learning that the under-optimized problem stems from inter-modality imbalance requiring gradient balancing (as pursued by OGM, AGM, PMR, and related methods). Instead, it repurposes the bias-variance decomposition from ensemble learning to prove that the optimal modality contribution ratio is inversely proportional to each modality's prediction variance, not equal. The proposed ARL strategy is a gradient modulation technique in the same family as OGM/AGM/PMR but targets a fundamentally different objective (inverse variance ratio rather than unity), and additionally introduces unimodal bias regularization to address the irreducible fusion bias that no weighting can eliminate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reframing the goal from 'balance the modalities' to 'weight them by inverse variance,' this paper provides a principled, parameter-free plug-in that consistently improves multimodal models across CNN and Transformer backbones, 2- and 3-modality settings, and multiple datasets. The key open question the authors identify is the quality of the uncertainty estimate: the current self-information entropy approximation is computationally cheap but leverages only a single forward pass, and more accurate (but costlier) uncertainty estimation could yield better modulation coefficients. Broader implications include whether the inverse-variance principle generalizes to settings with missing or noisy modalities, where the variance landscape is more complex.
