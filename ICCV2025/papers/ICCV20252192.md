## Problem

Existing streaming video understanding methods either perform frame-level action classification or generate captions at fixed decoding points, but none combine strict online temporal action localization with open-ended, hierarchical description generation (goal → step → substep). Additionally, datasets with fine-grained temporal annotations that also encode hierarchical relationships between actions are extremely scarce, limiting the training of models that can perceive multi-level action structure in real time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252192/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes untrimmed streaming video frames sequentially (processed at 16 fps in experiments) and must emit an action instance—comprising start/end timestamps, a hierarchy level (substep, step, or goal), and a free-form text description—immediately upon detecting action termination, with no retrospective modification allowed. The goal-level description covers the entire video and can be generated before the video concludes, while substep and step instances are emitted individually as they complete. The VLM is invoked only at detected boundaries rather than per-frame, reducing inference cost by roughly 16× compared to per-frame VLM calls.

## Method

OpenHOUSE decouples the task into a lightweight RNN-based streaming module and a frozen VLM (InternVL2-40B). The streaming module employs a hybrid action boundary detection strategy: action starts are identified via conventional actionness transitions (background-to-action), while action ends are detected by monitoring sudden drops in a learned progression score, which is critical for procedural videos where adjacent actions have no background frames between them. When a boundary is detected, the module queries a context memory for hierarchically-aware frame samples and previous text predictions, then passes them to the frozen VLM with hierarchy-specific prompts to generate the description. For training data, an LLM-based pipeline clusters atomic substep annotations into step-level groups and generates goal descriptions, producing pseudo-labels that yield competitive performance compared to ground-truth hierarchical annotations. The streaming module uses a shared RNN backbone with three heads (state-emitting, step progression, substep progression), all trained with cross-entropy loss via histogram binning of the regression targets.

## Results

On Ego4D-GoalStep (EgoGS), OpenHOUSE achieves F1@0.5 (loc.) of 39.70 (Step) and 43.71 (Substep), substantially outperforming the best OAD-based baseline MiniROAD (21.90 Substep) and SDVC (10.43 Step). On the joint localization+description metric F1@0.5 (loc.+desc.), OpenHOUSE reaches 12.67 (Step) and 16.11 (Substep), compared to 7.05 (MiniROAD Substep) and 1.32 (SDVC Step). Goal-level accuracy reaches 47.76% for OpenHOUSE versus 37.31–40.30% for baselines. Cross-dataset evaluation on Epic-Kitchens 100 shows OpenHOUSE achieving F1@0.3 (loc.) of 55.32 (Step) and 65.98 (Substep) when trained on EK100, with comparable results (51.64/53.67) when trained on the merged EgoGS+EgEx dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OpenHOUSE dominates all four baseline families on EgoGS by a wide margin. Against the OAD-based baselines (TeSTra, MiniROAD, MAT), OpenHOUSE nearly doubles F1@0.5 (loc.) for Step-level detection (39.70 vs. 12.34–12.51) and more than doubles it for Substep (43.71 vs. 15.85–21.90), with the gap driven primarily by the hybrid action boundary detection that handles strictly adjacent actions. Against SDVC, the streaming dense captioning baseline, OpenHOUSE achieves roughly 4× higher F1@0.5 (loc.) for Step (39.70 vs. 10.43) and 5.6× for Substep (43.71 vs. 7.79), while also producing substantially better descriptions (GPT Score 2.96 vs. 2.12 for Step). The SDVC* variant, which reuses OpenHOUSE's frozen-VLM description pipeline, improves over SDVC on F1(loc.+desc.) (3.25 vs. 1.32 for Step), confirming that a powerful pretrained VLM outperforms fine-tuning on small datasets. Goal-level accuracy further separates OpenHOUSE (47.76%) from all baselines (37.31–40.30%), indicating that accurate lower-hierarchy predictions are essential for capturing high-level semantics.

## Relation to Prior Work

OpenHOUSE sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) Online Action Detection / Online Temporal Action Localization (OAD/OnTAL), which provides the strict per-frame streaming constraint; (2) Streaming Dense Video Captioning (SDVC), which introduced fixed decoding points for open-ended generation but lacks real-time responsiveness; and (3) hierarchical video understanding, which has been explored only in offline settings. The paper departs from prior class-agnostic OnTAL methods (e.g., ActionSwitch, CAG-QIL) by replacing pure actionness-based boundary detection with a hybrid actionness+progression strategy, and from streaming VLM approaches (e.g., VideoLLM-Online) by decoupling the lightweight perception module from a frozen VLM that is invoked only at action-termination events rather than every frame.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to unify hierarchical temporal localization with open-ended description generation in a strictly online setting, opening a practical path toward real-time procedural video assistants. The validated LLM-based dataset pipeline addresses a genuine bottleneck (scarcity of hierarchical temporal annotations) and could be applied to other datasets beyond the three evaluated. Open questions include whether the three-level hierarchy generalizes to deeper or shallower structures, how the system handles incomplete or overlapping actions at inference time, and whether the frozen-VLM assumption holds as streaming-specific fine-tuning becomes more practical. The 16× efficiency gain over per-frame VLM inference is practically significant for deployment but depends on the action-termination signal being reliable.
