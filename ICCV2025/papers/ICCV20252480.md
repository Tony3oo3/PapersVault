## Problem

Existing long-tailed classification methods that transfer knowledge from head to tail classes often ignore class-specific differences, and the fixed WordNet-based label hierarchy misses implicit semantic similarities between categories in different subtrees (e.g., 'turtle' and 'snail' share traits despite belonging to different coarse-grained groups). This paper addresses the gap of capturing multi-granularity semantic relationships beyond the rigid hierarchy to enable more targeted and effective knowledge transfer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252480/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100-LT, 224×224 for ImageNet-LT) and produces a single class label prediction. Unusually, it also consumes LLM-generated textual descriptions (constrained to ≤300 words via predefined templates) for each class as auxiliary semantic input, which are processed offline by a sentence-transformer to build a fixed semantic similarity matrix used during training. The final prediction comes from a linear classifier on the learned feature representation.

## Method

The core contribution is a semantic knowledge-driven contrastive learning (SKCL) framework that constructs multi-granularity prototypes by applying an MLP to the weights of linear classifiers at each level of the label hierarchy. LLMs (GPT-4.0-turbo) generate enriched class descriptions using standardized templates, which are embedded via all-MiniLM-L6-v2 and compared with cosine similarity to build a semantic knowledge graph; for each class, only the Top-K most similar classes are retained to avoid misleading signals. The SKCL loss (Eq. 4) extends supervised contrastive learning by adding a prototype-attraction term that pulls each sample's representation toward the prototypes of its own class and its Top-K semantically similar classes, enabling both horizontal (same-level) and vertical (cross-level) knowledge transfer. Training jointly optimizes a weighted multi-level cross-entropy loss and the SKCL contrastive loss, with the contrastive branch and classification branch operating in a two-stage framework.

## Results

On CIFAR-100-LT with ResNet-32, the proposed SKCL achieves 54.02% top-1 accuracy at imbalance factor 100, outperforming the strongest baseline ConCutMix (53.16%) by 0.86 points. On CIFAR-10-LT, SKCL reaches 87.50% at imbalance 100 and 92.37% at imbalance 10. On ImageNet-LT with ResNet-50, SKCL achieves 60.4% overall accuracy, improving over ConCutMix (59.7%) by 0.7% and over BCL (56.7%) by 3.7%, with the largest gain of 5.0% on few-shot classes (41.5% vs. 36.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SKCL outperforms all 25 compared baselines across every dataset and imbalance setting. Against the strongest baseline ConCutMix [30], SKCL gains 0.86–1.33 points on CIFAR-100-LT and 0.16–0.95 points on CIFAR-10-LT, with the largest margin (1.33 points) at the most extreme imbalance (β=100) on CIFAR-100-LT. Compared to the contrastive-learning baseline BCL [55], SKCL improves overall accuracy by 2.01 points on CIFAR-100-LT (β=100) and by 3.7 points on ImageNet-LT, with the few-shot class accuracy jumping from 32.9% to 37.2% on CIFAR-100-LT and from 36.5% to 41.5% on ImageNet-LT. Against the multi-granularity knowledge transfer method MGKT-MFF [52], SKCL achieves 7.66 points higher accuracy on CIFAR-100-LT (β=100), demonstrating that LLM-enriched semantic relationships surpass WordNet-only hierarchical transfer. SKCL also substantially outperforms the hierarchical knowledge complementation method HCKC [50] by 15.02 points on CIFAR-100-LT (β=100), confirming that capturing cross-subtree semantic similarities (e.g., turtle–snail) is critical. Notably, SKCL improves head-class (many-shot) accuracy simultaneously with tail-class gains, unlike methods such as τ-norm [19] or DRO-LT [37] that trade head performance for tail performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the supervised contrastive learning framework (SCL) and the class-prototype-based contrastive learning of BCL by introducing multi-granularity prototypes constructed via MLP transformation of multi-level classifier weights. It departs from prior hierarchical knowledge transfer methods (HCKC, MGKT-MFF) that rely on the fixed WordNet label hierarchy, instead using LLMs to generate semantic descriptions that reveal cross-hierarchy similarities (e.g., turtle–snail) invisible to the static hierarchy. The approach also positions itself against LLM-driven data augmentation methods (LLM-AutoDA, LTGC) by using LLMs only as a one-time semantic enrichment tool rather than a generative component during training, keeping the visual model lightweight.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a single LLM call per class label, combined with a sentence-transformer, can meaningfully improve long-tailed classification without altering the training pipeline or adding per-sample inference cost. The ablation showing that both vertical (coarse-to-fine) and horizontal (same-level) knowledge transfer are necessary—neither alone suffices—provides useful guidance for designing multi-granularity transfer. Open questions include how the method behaves in open-world or open-set settings where the label space is not fixed, whether the Top-K selection and temperature hyperparameters transfer across domains without re-tuning, and whether the LLM-generated descriptions remain reliable for very fine-grained or domain-specific taxonomies.
