## Problem

Existing 3D car datasets are either synthetic (SRN-Car, Objaverse-Car) or provide only sparse, unposed views of real vehicles (MVMC with ~10 views per car), leaving a critical gap for photorealistic hazard simulation in autonomous driving and high-fidelity 3D reconstruction. No prior dataset offers dense multi-view real-world captures with controlled lighting diversity and component-level annotations at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251680/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each car instance provides ~200 posed RGB-D views at 1920×1440 resolution (range 50–400), real-world scaled point clouds with background removed, and 13-class semantic parsing masks, all aligned to a unified x-axis coordinate system. For reconstruction benchmarks, the input is the full multi-view RGB-D sequence with Colmap-estimated poses; for generation tasks, a single rendered image or text prompt suffices. Outputs span 3D car models (Gaussian splatting or NeRF representations), novel view renderings, per-component parsing masks, and detection bounding boxes for corner-case scenes.

## Method

Data is captured using iPhone 14 with ARKit, orbiting each stationary vehicle three times at varying heights to achieve dense 360° coverage under three controlled lighting states (standard, reflective, dark). Preprocessing pipelines through Grounding DINO + SAM for background segmentation, Colmap for accurate pose estimation (replacing unreliable smartphone poses), PCA-based orientation rectification to align all cars along the x-axis, and bounding-box-driven rescaling to restore real-world dimensions. The benchmarking evaluates six SOTA reconstruction methods (Instant-NGP, 3DGS, GaussianShader, 2DGS, Pixel-GS, 3DGS-MCMC) across lighting conditions, revealing significant performance drops under reflective and dark settings. Downstream experiments demonstrate that fine-tuning Zero123-XL and Dreamcraft3D on rendered views from the dataset improves novel view synthesis and single-image-to-3D generation, while simulated corner-case scenes from reconstructed cars boost YOLO-series detector mAP on the CODA benchmark by up to 28%.

## Results

On the 3D reconstruction benchmark (Table 4), under standard lighting, all six neural field methods achieve PSNR above 27 dB, with Pixel-GS leading at 27.67 dB and 3DGS-MCMC achieving the best LPIPS of 0.0986. Under reflective lighting, performance drops substantially (best PSNR: 25.41 by GaussianShader), and under dark lighting it drops further (best PSNR: 23.63 by 3DGS-MCMC), revealing a 3–5 dB gap that highlights the difficulty of glossy and low-light surfaces. For 2D car parsing, InternImage achieves the best mIOU of 0.671 and mAcc of 0.786. For corner-case detection, increasing simulated data from 1,000 to 5,000 samples improves CO-DETR's mAP@0.5 on CODA from 0.465 to 0.582.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under standard lighting, Pixel-GS and 3DGS-MCMC lead in PSNR (27.67 and 27.63 dB) while 3DGS-MCMC achieves the best LPIPS (0.0986); all six methods cluster within ~0.4 dB, indicating the standard subset is well-suited for high-quality reconstruction. Under reflective lighting, GaussianShader clearly outperforms all others (25.41 PSNR vs. 24.92 for 3DGS-MCMC), consistent with its explicit shading-function design for specular surfaces, while 2DGS degrades most severely (23.19 PSNR, LPIPS 0.2041). Under dark lighting, 3DGS-MCMC edges out the field (23.63 PSNR, 0.9198 SSIM), but all methods lose 3–5 dB relative to standard conditions, confirming that low illumination remains an open challenge. For 2D car parsing, InternImage dominates (mIOU 0.671, mAcc 0.786) over VMamba (0.610/0.734), DDRNet (0.606/0.732), and SegFormer (0.541/0.652). In corner-case detection, CO-DETR consistently outperforms YOLO variants across all simulated-data scales, reaching 0.582 mAP@0.5 with 5,000 samples versus 0.435 for YOLOv8s. For single-image-to-3D generation, fine-tuning Dreamcraft3D on 3DRealCar improves CLIP-I from 0.812 to 0.847 and reduces Chamfer Distance from 0.587 to 0.371, demonstrating the dataset's value as a car-specific prior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the 3D car dataset space rather than extending a specific method lineage. It departs from synthetic car repositories (SRN-Car, Objaverse-Car) that lack photorealistic geometry and materials, and from sparse real-car collections (MVMC with ~10 unposed views) that are insufficient for high-fidelity reconstruction. The data pipeline repurposes existing tools—ARKit for capture, Colmap for pose estimation, Grounding DINO + SAM for background removal, and PCA for orientation alignment—into a systematic collection protocol, while the benchmarking section positions the dataset against the full spectrum of current 3D reconstruction (NeRF, 3DGS, 2DGS, GaussianShader) and 3D generation (Zero123-XL, Dreamcraft3D) methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

3DRealCar fills a critical gap by providing the first large-scale, real-world, densely-sampled 3D car dataset with controlled multi-lighting conditions, enabling both reconstruction benchmarking and downstream task training (corner-case detection, car parsing, novel view synthesis, 3D generation). The quantified performance drop under reflective and dark lighting (PSNR falling from ~27.5 to ~23–25 dB) exposes a concrete, underexplored challenge for current neural field and Gaussian splatting methods that handle specular highlights and low-light scenes. Limitations include exterior-only captures, smartphone-based acquisition (iPhone 14/ARKit), and a single geographic/cultural context, while future work points toward interior scans and full-vehicle digitization for safety-critical simulation.
