## Problem

Existing PDE-based diffusion corruption methods (Inverse Heat Dissipation, Blurring Diffusion) are purely isotropic, modeling only local averaging or Gaussian noise injection without any directional transport. This limits the forward corruption to uniform blurring and prevents the representation of anisotropic texture shifts, swirling motions, or coherent multi-scale mixing that are natural in physical transport phenomena.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252188/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a sample from a precomputed blurry prior (the terminal state of the advection-diffusion corruption chain) and iteratively denoises it over K steps to produce a clean RGB image. Experiments use 128×128 FFHQ faces and 28×28 MNIST digits; the corruption chain is precomputed and stored as a training tensor so the U-Net only performs regression on stored (u_k, u_{k-1}) pairs. A small constant Gaussian perturbation (σ_T = 0.01) is added at each step to break numerical degeneracy, but the process remains overwhelmingly conservative.

## Method

The forward corruption is governed by an advection-diffusion-reaction PDE where a stochastic turbulent velocity field (generated via Random Fourier Modes with a κ^{-2} energy spectrum) advects pixel intensities while a time-varying diffusion coefficient blurs them. The PDE is solved numerically with a GPU-accelerated D2Q9 Lattice Boltzmann solver (implemented in CUDA via Taichi), and the process is parameterized by two dimensionless numbers: the Fourier number (Fo) controlling the diffusion budget per step and the Peclet number (Pe) controlling the advection-to-diffusion ratio. A U-Net with attention layers is trained via MSE regression to predict the finite-difference between consecutive corruption states, effectively learning the inverse advection-diffusion operator. When Pe = 0 the method reduces exactly to Inverse Heat Dissipation, making the framework a strict generalization of prior PDE-based corruption approaches.

## Results

On FFHQ-128 with 200 sampling steps, the advection-diffusion model substantially outperforms the IHD baseline (Pe=0) in FID: at σ=16, the best Pe=0.14 achieves FID 38.10 versus 52.10 for IHD (a 27% reduction), while at σ=20, Pe=0.06 reaches FID 36.64 versus 55.87 for IHD (a 34% reduction). Diversity metrics also improve markedly: Recall rises from 0.118 to 0.958 (σ=16) and from 0.109 to 1.043 (σ=20), and Coverage increases from 0.507 to 0.627 and from 0.482 to 0.665 respectively. The optimal Peclet number depends on the final blur level, with lower Pe values (0.06–0.08) being optimal at σ=20 and higher values (0.12–0.14) at σ=16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole quantitative baseline is Inverse Heat Dissipation (IHD, Rissanen et al. [27]), which corresponds to Pe=0 (pure isotropic blurring with no advection). The advection-diffusion method decisively outperforms IHD on FID across both blur settings: at σ=16, the best advection setting (Pe=0.14) reduces FID from 52.10 to 38.10, and at σ=20, Pe=0.06 reduces FID from 55.87 to 36.64. Diversity metrics show even larger relative gains: Recall improves from 0.118 to 0.958 (σ=16) and from 0.109 to 1.043 (σ=20), while Coverage rises from 0.507 to 0.627 and 0.482 to 0.665 respectively. Precision remains roughly comparable (within ±0.03), indicating that the FID and diversity gains are not achieved by sacrificing sample fidelity. The paper notes that the optimal Peclet number shifts with the final blur level—higher Pe values help at lower σ, while moderate Pe values are optimal at higher σ—suggesting an interaction between advection strength and the total corruption budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PDE-based corruption line initiated by Inverse Heat Dissipation (IHD) [27] and Blurring Diffusion [12], which model the forward pass as isotropic heat-equation blur with minor additive noise. The key departure is the introduction of a velocity-driven advection term, solved via a GPU-accelerated Lattice Boltzmann Method, so that the corruption operator becomes anisotropic and can produce coherent directional texture shifts. A Spectral Turbulence Generator (inspired by Random Fourier Modes) supplies the stochastic velocity field, and a dimensionless formulation using Fourier and Peclet numbers allows the advection-diffusion budget to be matched across resolutions and compared fairly against the IHD baseline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that physically grounded, anisotropic corruption (turbulent advection + diffusion) can improve generative fidelity (FID drops of ~10–20 points over IHD) while preserving the global color palette, offering a new design axis for diffusion-model forward processes. However, the authors note that no simple closed-form stationary distribution exists for the advected prior, and the step-by-step PDE solve adds ~10% training overhead; the optimal Peclet number is sensitive to the final blur level, limiting straightforward transfer. Open directions include exploring different turbulence spectra, combining advection with the growing-noise schedule of Hoogeboom & Salimans, and evaluating under flow-matching objectives where the final state is decoupled from the corruption trajectory.
