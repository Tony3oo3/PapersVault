## Problem

Unsupervised domain adaptation on 3D point clouds is hindered by the entanglement of domain-invariant semantic features with domain-specific geometric variations, exacerbated by point clouds' local compositional inconsistency and global redundancy. Existing explicit alignment methods fail to capture domain-invariant representations due to geometric ambiguity, while self-supervised approaches remain susceptible to structural distortions. The paper identifies a latent geometry-style confounder that jointly influences both class-relevant and class-irrelevant features, making direct distribution alignment insufficient.

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled source-domain point clouds (T×3 coordinates per object) and unlabeled target-domain point clouds, partitioning each into n point patches of k nearest neighbors arranged in Morton order to preserve spatial locality. A pre-trained 3D foundation model (PointGPT-S or PointGPT-B) serves as the backbone extractor, producing D-dimensional patch embeddings that are decomposed into a class-prototype component z_c and a residual component z_s. The final output is a per-object class label, with pseudo-labels for the target domain refined through counterfactual realignment before self-training.

## Method

CounterPC formulates point cloud UDA within a causal latent space where a geometry-style confounder v jointly influences domain-invariant content features z_c and domain-specific residual features z_s. The first module, Joint Distribution Alignment, fine-tunes a 3D foundation model (PointGPT) via a self-supervised autoregressive masked-patch reconstruction task (Chamfer Distance loss) to align the joint distribution P(z_c, z_s) across domains without explicit disentanglement. The second module, Counterfactual Feature Generation, applies Optimal Transport to realign target residual features toward source class prototypes, using an estimated target-domain class prior β_j to correct source-domain class-imbalance bias in pseudo-labeling. A denoising filter retains only pseudo-labels whose confidence-based prediction agrees with the counterfactual OT assignment, and the overall training alternates between supervised CE loss on source, self-supervised CD loss on both domains, and pseudo-label CE loss on the target domain with weights (w_s^e, w_s^d, w_t^e, w_t^d) = (2, 3, 1, 5).

## Results

On PointDA-10, CounterPC_S and CounterPC_B achieve average classification accuracy of 77.5% and 77.9% respectively across six transfer settings, outperforming the previous SOTA (MLGR, 75.3%) by 2.2 and 2.6 points. On GraspNetPC-10, CounterPC_S and CounterPC_B reach 88.4% and 88.9% average accuracy, surpassing the best prior method (PC-Adapter, 85.3%) by 3.1 and 3.6 points. The largest gains appear in the most challenging real-to-real settings (M→S*: 64.3% vs. 61.2% for MLGR; S*→S: 78.6% vs. 77.3%), indicating the counterfactual realignment is most effective under severe domain shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CounterPC outperforms all 11 baselines on both benchmarks. On PointDA-10, it beats the strongest UDA method MLGR [39] (75.3% avg) by 2.2–2.6 points and the best 3D-FM extension PointGPT-S+UDA [5] (73.9%) by 3.6–4.0 points; the gap widens to 3.1 points over COT [18] and 7.2 points over PC-Adapter [23]. On GraspNetPC-10, CounterPC_B (88.9%) surpasses PC-Adapter [23] (85.3%) by 3.6 points and ImplicitPCDA [30] (84.4%) by 4.5 points, with the largest margin (5.7 points) over PointGPT-B [5] (83.2%). Notably, CounterPC wins on every individual transfer setting on GraspNetPC-10, including the hardest synthetic-to-real directions (S→K: 98.2%, S→R: 82.1%), whereas PC-Adapter leads only on S→K (97.5%) but trails on all other settings. The ablation (Table 2) confirms each module contributes incrementally: SSG adds +0.5%, CFG adds +1.6%, and ETD adds +2.0% over the 3D-FM baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on PointGPT as a 3D foundation model backbone but departs fundamentally from prior point cloud UDA methods (PointDAN, GAST, PC-Adapter, MLGR) that rely on explicit feature alignment or self-supervised geometric prediction. The key conceptual shift is framing domain adaptation through a causal lens with a latent geometry-style confounder, then using counterfactual interventions and Optimal Transport to realign features without explicit disentanglement—extending causal inference techniques previously applied to image domain adaptation into the geometrically ambiguous point cloud setting. The self-supervised autoregressive generation task bridges the 3D-FM's generalization with domain-specific transferability, a combination not explored in prior UDA work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The causal formulation provides a principled explanation for why explicit feature alignment struggles on point clouds (intra-class geometric variation acts as a confounder), and the counterfactual approach offers a practical workaround that avoids the brittle explicit disentanglement step. However, the method's reliance on a large pre-trained 3D foundation model (PointGPT) as an implicit prior for joint distribution alignment is a strong assumption that may not hold under extreme domain shifts or for unseen categories. Open questions include scalability beyond 10-class benchmarks, sensitivity to the quality of the foundation model, and whether the counterfactual OT formulation generalizes to denser or more heterogeneous point cloud distributions.
