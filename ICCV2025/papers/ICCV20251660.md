## Problem

Existing 3DGS- and NeRF-based photo-realistic SLAM methods are limited to indoor scenes with RGB-D sensors and cannot handle the sparse, unevenly distributed point clouds produced by outdoor LiDAR. In large-scale unbounded outdoor environments, unidirectional sensor motion introduces directional bias in Gaussian optimization, and offline methods over-fit to supervised viewpoints, degrading novel-view synthesis. No prior framework achieves real-time photo-realistic mapping for LiDAR-Inertial-Visual fusion in such settings.

## Input / Output

The system consumes synchronized LiDAR point clouds (Livox Avia at 10 Hz, Ouster-16, or Velodyne VLP-16), IMU data at 200 Hz, and monocular RGB images (resolutions ranging from 480×300 to 752×480 depending on the dataset). The tracking thread produces odometry at IMU frequency via ESIKF, while the mapping thread outputs a dense voxel-based 3D Gaussian map where each Gaussian is parameterized by position, 3×3 covariance, opacity, and zero-degree spherical harmonics per color channel. Rendered novel-view images are produced in real time, with the full mapping pipeline completing within the dataset duration on an 8 GB GPU.

## Method

GS-LIVM introduces Voxel-GPR, which applies Gaussian Process Regression at the voxel level to convert sparse, uneven LiDAR observations into uniformly sampled mesh-grid points with per-point covariance estimates, using PCA to determine value and parameter axes per voxel. The GPR covariance drives a covariance-centered initialization of 3D Gaussians: weighted sphere fitting over neighboring predicted points yields the initial position, scale (diagonal of the weighted covariance), and rotation, enabling rapid convergence without many optimization iterations. An iterative mapping framework manages four voxel states (unexplored, pending, active, converged) and refines the map by stacking new LiDAR observations into active voxels until variance converges. Two novel losses accelerate and regularize optimization: a delta depth similarity loss between consecutive rendered depth/silhouette pairs, and a structure similarity loss measuring mean nearest-Gaussian distance to the latest LiDAR frame. All voxel-level GPR solves and Gaussian rasterization are accelerated with custom CUDA kernels, keeping per-frame processing below the real-time budget even during large-scale map expansion.

## Results

GS-LIVM achieves the best or near-best rendering quality across all four outdoor sequences. On PSNR, it reaches 22.43 (hku_campus_seq_00), 21.81 (Visual_Challenge), 20.72 (1005_00 Ouster-16), and 21.12 (1005_00 Livox Avia), outperforming the offline 3DGS baseline in 3 of 4 sequences and all NeRF/3DGS-SLAM baselines in all sequences. On LPIPS (lower is better), GS-LIVM achieves the lowest values in every sequence (0.247, 0.289, 0.319, 0.258). Crucially, all sequences are processed in real time (mapping time ≈ dataset duration, e.g., 202 s for a 202 s sequence) on an 8 GB GPU, whereas the offline 3DGS baseline requires extended optimization and cannot complete full outdoor sequences within the same time budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GS-LIVM is compared against three baselines: NeRF-SLAM (NeRF-based, IROS 2023), MonoGS (3DGS-based monocular SLAM, CVPR 2024), and the original offline 3DGS implementation (ACM TOG 2023). GS-LIVM decisively outperforms both NeRF-SLAM and MonoGS on all metrics across all four sequences, with PSNR gains of roughly 8–13 dB over NeRF-SLAM and 4–8 dB over MonoGS. Against the offline 3DGS baseline, GS-LIVM wins on SSIM and LPIPS in all four sequences and on PSNR in three of four (the sole PSNR loss is 21.12 vs. 22.17 on 1005_00 Livox Avia, a gap of ~1 dB). The critical practical advantage is that GS-LIVM processes every sequence in real time (mapping time ≈ recording duration) on a single 8 GB GPU, while the offline 3DGS baseline requires extended optimization and cannot complete full outdoor sequences within the same time constraint, and NeRF-SLAM/MonoGS are limited to ≤100-frame sub-sequences due to memory and tracking failures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) tightly-coupled LiDAR-Inertial-Visual odometry (R3LIVE, SR-LIVO) for state estimation, (2) 3D Gaussian Splatting as an explicit scene representation for real-time rendering, and (3) Gaussian Process Regression for LiDAR map reconstruction (GP-SLAM+). It departs from prior 3DGS-based SLAM work (SplaTAM, MonoGS, GS-SLAM) which targets indoor RGB-D or monocular settings, and from offline outdoor 3DGS methods (DrivingGaussian, StreetGaussian, LIV-GaussMap) that sacrifice real-time performance for extended optimization. The key conceptual departure is the covariance-centered design: GPR's estimated variance simultaneously drives 3D Gaussian initialization (scale/rotation) and iterative GPR parameter updates, creating a feedback loop absent in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to achieve real-time photo-realistic mapping specifically for LiDAR-Inertial-Visual fusion in unbounded outdoor scenes, a setting where prior 3DGS SLAM methods either cannot run (GPU memory overflow) or require offline optimization. The Voxel-GPR approach of converting sparse, uneven LiDAR returns into uniform mesh-grid points before Gaussian initialization is a practical solution to a problem specific to spinning and non-repetitive LiDAR. Limitations include evaluation on sequences of only ~200 m, reliance on a single 8 GB GPU, and baselines constrained to 100 frames / 5 minutes, which makes the performance gap harder to interpret. Open questions include scalability to kilometer-scale urban environments, handling of dynamic objects, and whether the covariance-centered initialization generalizes to non-LiDAR sparse inputs.
