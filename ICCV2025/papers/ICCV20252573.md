## Problem

Diffusion and flow matching models achieve state-of-the-art pansharpening quality but require 100–1000 sampling steps, making them impractical for real-time remote sensing applications. Existing acceleration strategies (knowledge distillation, ReFlow, consistency training) either require separate teacher-student training pipelines or offline generation of large (noise, image) pairs, and all incur measurable quality degradation. The paper asks whether one-step generation can be achieved directly within a single training process, without post-hoc distillation or complex samplers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252573/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-channel high-resolution panchromatic image (PAN) and a multi-band low-resolution multispectral image (LRMS) as joint conditions, and produces a multi-band high-resolution multispectral image (HRMS) in a single forward pass. Notably, the flow matching initial state y₀ is set to the LRMS itself (rather than standard Gaussian noise), which eases the UOT cost learning and provides a physically meaningful starting point. The PAN and LRMS conditions are injected at every layer via AdaLN-zero modulation, and the entire pipeline requires only one network evaluation (NFE = 1) at inference time.

## Method

The core contribution is deriving the dual formulation of Unbalanced Optimal Transport (UOT) and embedding it as a min-max training objective within the flow matching framework. A mapping network T_θ (U-Net with neighborhood attention and FFN blocks) approximates the UOT c-transform, while a potential network v_φ (t-conditioned patch GAN discriminator) parameterizes the dual potential; the two are trained adversarially via a min-max game. A pansharpening-specific regularization—spatial degradation (blurring + decimation) and spectral degradation (band weighting + MTF filtering) of the predicted HRMS—is added directly to the UOT cost function, enforcing physical consistency without separate loss terms. The flow matching velocity network is algebraically converted into a one-step generator (T_{θ,t} = s_{θ,t} + y₀), and the standard flow matching loss is retained to constrain the interpolation path to near-straight trajectories, which stabilizes the UOT training and enables simulation-free optimization. This yields a single training loop that produces a model capable of one-step HRMS generation matching or exceeding 25-step diffusion baselines.

## Results

On the WV3 reduced-resolution dataset, OTFM achieves SAM 2.76, ERGAS 2.03, Q2n 0.919, and SCC 0.988 with only 1 NFE, matching the 25-step DDIF (SAM 2.74, ERGAS 2.01) while being 25× faster. On the full-resolution WV3 dataset, OTFM attains the best HQNR of 0.954 and lowest D_λ of 0.018, outperforming DDIF (HQNR 0.952, D_λ 0.026). Inference latency is 0.029 s per 256×256 image, compared to 2.602 s for DDIF and 261.410 s for PanDiff (1000 steps). On GF2 and QB datasets, OTFM similarly achieves SOTA or near-SOTA metrics with a single sampling step.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OTFM matches the 25-step DDIF on reduced-resolution metrics (SAM 2.76 vs 2.74, ERGAS 2.03 vs 2.01) while requiring only 1 NFE instead of 25, and surpasses DDIF on full-resolution D_λ (0.018 vs 0.026) and HQNR (0.954 vs 0.952), though DDIF retains a slight edge on D_s (0.023 vs 0.029). Against the 1000-step PanDiff, OTFM improves SAM by 0.54, ERGAS by 0.44, and HQNR by 0.034, demonstrating that the multi-step diffusion process is unnecessary for pansharpening quality. All six regression-based baselines (DiCNN, FusionNet, LAGConv, DCFNet, MMNet, HFIN) are outperformed on reduced-resolution SAM and ERGAS, with OTFM achieving the best or near-best values across all four metrics. Traditional methods (MTF-GLP-FS, BT-H) are substantially outperformed, with OTFM reducing SAM by roughly 2.5–5.5 points. In ablation comparisons, OTFM also outperforms Reflow (SAM 3.18), Consistency Training (SAM 2.88), NOT+FM (SAM 2.89), and GAN+FM (SAM 2.85), confirming that the UOT formulation is the key enabler of one-step quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022) and unbalanced optimal transport (Chizat et al., 2018), deriving the UOT dual formulation and embedding it into the FM training loop to replace multi-step ODE sampling with a single forward pass. It departs from prior acceleration strategies (Reflow, Consistency Models, knowledge distillation) that require separate teacher-student training or offline pair generation, instead achieving one-step generation natively within a single training process. The pansharpening-regularized UOT cost, which folds spatial and spectral degradation constraints directly into the transport cost, is a new formulation not present in prior OT-for-image-translation work (e.g., NOT, LightSB), and the choice to initialize the flow from LRMS rather than a Gaussian prior is a practical departure from standard FM practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 9000× inference speedup over PanDiff (0.029 s vs. 261 s) while matching or slightly exceeding the best multi-step diffusion baseline (DDIF) makes high-quality pansharpening practically deployable on edge or real-time satellite processing pipelines. The conceptual insight—that UOT's relaxed marginal constraints naturally yield a well-posed one-step mapping without distillation—could inform other conditional generation tasks where multi-step sampling is a bottleneck. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the Pancollection benchmark to larger-scale or multi-sensor settings, whether the small performance margins over DDIF hold under distribution shift, and whether the UOT relaxation can be tightened to further close the remaining gap with 1000-step methods.
