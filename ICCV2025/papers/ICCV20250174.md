## Problem

Existing Video Temporal Grounding methods rely solely on final-layer features of frozen backbones, limiting adaptability under distribution shift. While side-tuning offers a parameter- and memory-efficient alternative, prior ST approaches like R²-Tuning treat the problem from a frame-level refinement perspective, overlooking the inherently sparse nature of Moment Retrieval. Additionally, the deformable attention mechanism used in anchor-free architectures suffers from a contextual limitation where offset predictions collapse near their initialization values, preventing the model from attending beyond estimated moment boundaries.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video clip (T×H×W×3) and a text query (L×F_e tokens), extracting K intermediate visual-textual representations from a frozen InternVideo2-1B backbone. The spatio-temporal token dimension of each intermediate layer is pooled via a reused frozen AdaptivePool module rather than CLS or average pooling. Outputs are frame-level saliency scores (R^T) for Highlight Detection and M center-width action moment boundaries (R^{M×2}) for Moment Retrieval, with M learnable recurrent decoder queries refined across K shared-weight levels.

## Method

SDST is a dual-stream side-tuner with a dense stream that refines frame-level embeddings via multi-modal cross-attention and temporal self-attention (serving HD), and a sparse stream that refines recurrent decoder queries—center-width references plus latent embeddings—conditioned on the dense signal (serving MR). The key architectural innovation is Reference-based Deformable Self-Attention (RDSA), which reformulates the standard deformable cross-attention into a deformable self-attention by deriving query embeddings from the dense frame representations (left-most, center, and right-most action embeddings) rather than from the learnable queries themselves, thereby restoring the implicit Q-K context that CNN-based offset predictors require. The first successful integration of InternVideo2 into an ST framework is achieved by reusing the backbone's frozen AdaptivePool module across all K intermediate layers, avoiding the 15× memory increase that loading full spatio-temporal tokens would incur. All SDST layers are weight-shared across the K refinement levels, keeping the total trainable parameter count at only 4.1M (73% fewer than SG-DETR).

## Results

On QVHighlights val (the standard evaluation split), SDST achieves 73.68% R1@0.5, 60.90% R1@0.7, and 55.60% average mAP for Moment Retrieval, and 44.00% mAP / 72.00% HIT@1 for Highlight Detection, using only 4.1M trainable parameters. On Charades-STA, SDST attains SOTA with 52.6% R@0.7 and 61.2% mIoU, and on TACoS it reaches 32.3% R@0.7 and 42.2% mIoU, both surpassing the previous SOTA (SG-DETR). The method reduces the parameter count by up to 73% compared to SG-DETR (15.0M params) while matching or exceeding its performance on most metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the previous side-tuning method R²-Tuning, SDST improves MR by +3.82% average mAP and HD by +2.21% mAP on QVHighlights val, and by +15.6% R@0.7 on Charades-STA, confirming the advantage of an anchor-free (DETR-style) architecture over the anchor-based R²-Tuning. Against the current SOTA SG-DETR (also using InternVideo2), SDST matches or slightly exceeds performance on QVHighlights val (55.60% vs 55.64% avg mAP for MR; 44.00% vs 43.91% mAP for HD) while using only 4.1M vs 15.0M parameters (73% reduction). On Charades-STA, SDST clearly surpasses SG-DETR by +2.71% R@0.7 (52.6 vs 49.5) and +2.1% mIoU (61.2 vs 59.1). On TACoS, SDST improves over SG-DETR by +2.4% R@0.7 (32.3 vs 29.9) and +1.3% mIoU (42.2 vs 40.9), while being slightly behind on R@0.5 (44.5 vs 44.7). Against Flash-VTG (also InternVideo2-based), SDST leads by +3.6% R1@0.7 and +2.8% avg mAP on QVHighlights val, and by +2.7% R@0.7 on Charades-STA. Among non-InternVideo2 methods, SDST's val mAP of 55.60% exceeds all prior DETR-based methods (CG-DETR: 44.93%, QD-DETR: 41.22%) and LLM-based methods (LLaVA-MR: 56.32% mAP@0.75 only, no full mAP reported).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: side-tuning (ST) for parameter- and memory-efficient fine-tuning, initiated by LST [34] and first applied to VTG by R2-Tuning [21], and anchor-free DETR-based architectures for moment retrieval (Moment-DETR, SG-DETR, etc.). SDST departs from R2-Tuning by replacing its anchor-based MR formulation with a proposal-free recurrent decoder-query design inspired by DAB-DETR [20] and DN-DETR [16], while retaining the ST paradigm of refining frozen backbone intermediates. The key conceptual departure is the identification of a context limitation in deformable cross-attention (inherited from Deformable DETR [45]) that motivates RDSA, a reformulation as deformable self-attention using reference-based queries. The paper also extends the ST framework beyond CLIP to InternVideo2 [36], a spatio-temporal video backbone, which had not been previously integrated into any ST pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 73% parameter reduction relative to SG-DETR while matching or exceeding SOTA on QVHighlights, TACoS, and Charades-STA makes this a practically important option for resource-constrained VTG deployment. The finding that deformable cross-attention suffers from offset collapse due to lack of key-space context is a useful diagnostic for the broader DETR community, though its generality beyond VTG remains untested. The depth-pooling trade-off (shallower layers provide complementary information but break the frozen AdaptivePool) highlights a non-trivial constraint when extending ST to deeper or more advanced backbones. The authors explicitly leave cross-domain generalization as future work, an open question that limits the current claims about robustness beyond the training distribution.
