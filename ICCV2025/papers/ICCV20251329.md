## Problem

Existing roadside perception datasets for autonomous driving predominantly emphasize vehicles over Vulnerable Road Users (VRUs) and lack thermal imaging, which is critical for pedestrian detection in low-light and overexposed conditions. No prior dataset integrates all three modalities—LiDAR, RGB, and thermal—from a roadside intersection perspective with a strong VRU focus, leaving a gap for comprehensive multi-modal VRU safety research.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251329/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 10,000 LiDAR point cloud frames at 5 FPS and 2,400 temporally and spatially aligned RGB (1280×720) and thermal (640×512) image pairs at 1.25 FPS, captured from a 4.5 m elevated mobile sensor station at three German intersections across day and night. The thermal image is projected onto the RGB frame via a calibrated homography (mean reprojection error 0.038 px) and padded to match dimensions. Annotations consist of 53,319 2D bounding boxes (8 classes: person, car, bicycle, motorcycle, truck, bus, tramway, e-scooter) for RGB-T and 241,659 3D bounding boxes with tracking IDs (7 classes, e-scooter excluded due to insufficient point density) for LiDAR, with objects annotated up to 50 m in the LiDAR domain.

## Method

The core contribution is the dataset construction pipeline: a dual-LiDAR setup (Ouster OS1 64-beam + RoboSense Bpearl 32-beam, hardware-triggered at 10 Hz) combined with an RGB camera (5 Hz) and thermal camera (60 Hz), all GPS-synchronized via ROS ApproximateTime with ≤40 ms tolerance. RGB-LiDAR calibration uses an aluminum checkerboard, while thermal-RGB alignment uses a circular target to estimate a homography. A key design choice is independent labeling of RGB-T and LiDAR data (rather than projecting LiDAR boxes onto images) to preserve annotation quality given the cameras' deeper FoV. For benchmarking, the authors fine-tune SOTA detectors—PointPillars, PointRCNN, PV-RCNN (LiDAR 3D detection via OpenPCDet/KITTI protocol) and Faster R-CNN, YOLOv8-M, RT-DETR-L (RGB-T 2D detection via COCO protocol, pre-trained on COCO)—and evaluate three 3D MOT methods (AB3DMOT, SimpleTrack, Mahalanobis MOT) using PointPillars detections as input. RGB-T fusion uses simple late fusion with NMS at IoU 0.8, and all experiments are averaged over three random seeds with a fixed 80/20 sequence-level train-test split.

## Results

On the LiDAR 3D detection benchmark, PointPillars achieves the best Car AP75 of 46.73 and Cyclist AP50 of 30.11, while PointRCNN underperforms dramatically (Car AP75 = 28.26, Pedestrian AP50 = 4.62) due to the dataset's low average point count (~110 points per box). For LiDAR tracking, AB3DMOT leads with Car sAMOTA of 78.45, though VRU tracking remains weak (Pedestrian sAMOTA = 30.26). In the RGB-T 2D detection benchmark, YOLOv8 achieves the highest overall AP (44.87 day, 32.27 night for RGB), and adding thermal via late fusion improves night AP to 33.95 while consistently boosting person recall (AR100per rises from 37.76 to 45.34 for YOLOv8 at night).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among LiDAR detectors, PointPillars [18] and PV-RCNN [26] perform comparably on cars (AP75 ≈ 46.7) but both struggle on VRUs (Pedestrian AP50 ≈ 20–23), while PointRCNN [25] collapses on sparse-point objects (Pedestrian AP50 = 4.62), highlighting the dataset's challenge for point-based methods. For tracking, AB3DMOT [30] dominates all three categories (Car sAMOTA 78.45 vs. SimpleTrack [20] 64.40 and Mahalanobis [7] 61.53), but all trackers show a large VRU gap (Pedestrian sAMOTA 19–30 vs. Car 62–78). In RGB-T detection, YOLOv8 [17] outperforms Faster R-CNN [24] and RT-DETR [38] on day AP (44.87 vs. 35.06 and 37.50), while RT-DETR edges ahead at night (32.67 vs. 32.27 for RGB alone). Critically, thermal-only detection is consistently weaker than RGB (e.g., YOLOv8 night AP 26.55 vs. 32.27), but the simple late-fusion RGB+Thermal setup recovers and surpasses RGB-only performance at night (33.95 vs. 32.27) and substantially improves person recall across all models, validating the dataset's motivation for VRU-focused multi-modal perception.

## Relation to Prior Work

R-LiViT carves new ground at the intersection of two established dataset lineages: roadside perception benchmarks (DAIR-V2X-I, IPS300+, LUMPI, TUMTraf, RCooper) that pair LiDAR with RGB but largely ignore thermal and underrepresent VRUs, and RGB-T detection datasets (KAIST, FLIR-aligned, LLVIP, M³FD, SMOD) that provide aligned thermal-visual pairs but from vehicle or surveillance perspectives rather than infrastructure. No prior dataset integrates all three modalities (LiDAR, RGB, thermal) from a roadside vantage point with explicit VRU annotation density, so the paper repurposes standard detection and tracking architectures (PointPillars, PV-RCNN, Faster R-CNN, YOLOv8, RT-DETR, AB3DMOT) as baselines rather than proposing new methods. The calibration pipeline (LiDAR-to-RGB via checkerboard, thermal-to-RGB via homography) follows established multi-sensor registration practice, and the GDPR anonymization protocol mirrors approaches in A2D2 and Zenseact.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset addresses a concrete gap: roadside VRU safety requires thermal complementarity in low-light and glare conditions, yet no publicly available roadside dataset previously offered aligned RGB-T alongside LiDAR with high pedestrian/cyclist density (9.61 pedestrians per frame). Key limitations temper its immediate impact—software-based GPS synchronization introduces occasional RGB-T/LiDAR temporal misalignment, no cross-modal tracking IDs exist, and a single RGB-thermal camera pair covers only a fraction of the LiDAR FoV. Open questions include whether multi-camera roadside RGB-T setups can close the remaining VRU detection gap, how the sparse-point-cloud regime (~110 points per 3D box) affects the transferability of benchmarks to denser LiDAR deployments, and whether the simple NMS-based late fusion demonstrated here is sufficient or whether learned fusion strategies yield meaningful gains in this high-density, small-object setting.
