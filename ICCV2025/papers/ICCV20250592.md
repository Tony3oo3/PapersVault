## Problem

Few-shot industrial anomaly detection methods treat all reference samples with equal confidence, so intra-class variation (lighting, placement, background noise) introduces misleading entries into the memory bank and degrades localization. Additionally, existing vision-language approaches fuse textual and visual priors only at the image level, leaving the encoder unable to focus on subtle, localized anomaly regions at the patch or region level.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250592/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query image resized to 518×518, K normal reference images (K ∈ {1, 2, 4}), and a class-name text prompt. References are encoded by a frozen ViT-L14-336 backbone to build a patch-level memory bank (L = K·H·W vectors) and K class tokens. The output is a pixel-level anomaly segmentation map obtained by averaging and upsampling memory-guided and language-guided maps across P = 4 selected ViT layers, plus an image-level classification score that averages the CLIP-based normal/abnormal probability with the maximum pixel anomaly value.

## Method

ReMP-AD introduces two modules. Intra-Class Token Retrieval (ICTR) first computes per-reference confidence weights via Class Token Balancing (CTB), which scores each reference by its cosine similarity to the query class token and penalizes references that are themselves dissimilar to other references; these weights are then multiplied element-wise with patch-level cosine-similarity weights from Global Prototype Retrieval (GPR), a sparsity threshold λ is applied, and the surviving weights produce a weighted prototype from the memory bank for anomaly scoring. Vision-Language Prior Fusion (VLPF) computes a per-patch Vision Prior Knowledge score from top-k self-similarity, converts it to a weighted binary mask, and injects the masked patch features into the 12th, 18th, and 24th attention layers of the frozen ViT as visual prompts; a parallel Language Prior Knowledge score from CLIP text features is intersected with the VPK mask, and connected-area analysis groups surviving patches into coherent regions whose mean features are blended with patch features for final segmentation. Only a small MLP projecting patch features into the CLIP text space is trained (3–15 epochs, Adam, lr 1e-3); the CLIP backbone, VLPF encoder, and all retrieval logic remain frozen at inference.

## Results

On the VisA dataset under the 4-shot setting, ReMP-AD achieves 97.8% P-AUC and 94.1% I-AUC for anomaly segmentation and classification, respectively, outperforming all baselines. On MVTec-AD (4-shot), it reaches 96.6% P-AUC and 96.8% I-AUC. The method also demonstrates strong generalization on the real-world PCB-Bank dataset, achieving 95.4% P-AUC and 86.9% I-AUC in the 4-shot setting, surpassing both WinCLIP+ and APRIL-GAN by substantial margins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReMP-AD consistently outperforms all five baselines across both MVTec-AD and VisA in every shot setting. Against the strongest language-prior baseline PromptAD (4-shot), ReMP-AD gains +5.0% I-AUC on VisA (94.1 vs. 89.1) and +0.2% P-AUC (97.8 vs. 97.4), while on MVTec-AD it leads by +0.2% I-AUC (96.8 vs. 96.6) and +0.1% P-AUC (96.6 vs. 96.5). Compared to APRIL-GAN (4-shot VisA), ReMP-AD improves I-AUC by +1.5% (94.1 vs. 92.6) and P-F1 by +8.2% (48.2 vs. 40.0). The vision-prior baselines PaDiM and PatchCore lag substantially, with PaDiM achieving only 72.8% I-AUC on VisA (4-shot) versus ReMP-AD's 94.1%. On the real-world PCB-Bank dataset, ReMP-AD surpasses APRIL-GAN by +3.4% I-AUC (86.9 vs. 83.5) and +2.7% P-AUC (95.4 vs. 92.7) in the 4-shot setting. When integrated into the many-shot MuSc framework, the VLPF module alone adds +0.8% P-F1 on MVTec-AD, and combining it with the language-prior branch yields an additional +0.9% P-F1, confirming the complementary benefit of fusing vision and language priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReMP-AD sits at the intersection of two established lines in industrial anomaly detection: memory-bank retrieval methods (PatchCore, PaDiM) that compare query features against stored normal prototypes, and CLIP-based language-prior methods (WinCLIP+, APRIL-GAN, PromptAD) that exploit the semantic gap between 'normal' and 'abnormal' text prompts. The paper departs from prior few-shot approaches in two specific ways: (1) it introduces token-level retrieval with class-token balancing to handle intra-class variation among reference images, rather than treating all references uniformly, and (2) it feeds vision-prior masks back into the ViT encoder as attention prompts, extending the language-prior paradigm from image-level to region-level feature analysis. The modular design is validated by plugging ICTR into PromptAD and VLPF into MuSc, showing the components are not tightly coupled to the overall architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is clear for industrial settings where only a handful of normal reference images are available and subtle defects must be localized precisely. The ICTR module's demonstrated portability (consistent gains when added to PromptAD) makes it a potentially reusable component for other few-shot AD pipelines. However, the method introduces numerous hyperparameters (λ, m_k, m_λ, w_1, w_2, m_α, layer selection p̂) that require per-dataset tuning, and the gains over the strongest baselines (PromptAD, APRIL-GAN) are modest—typically 0.3–1.5% in P-AUC. The authors note future extension to 3D industrial scenarios, but the current framework is limited to 2D images and specific industrial product categories.
