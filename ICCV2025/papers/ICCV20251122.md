## Problem

Coordinate-based surface reconstruction methods (SDF, OF, NeRF) require dense 3D sampling and network evaluations, making them computationally expensive, while existing ray-based methods (RayDF, Pointersect) are limited to per-scene training and object-level shapes. No prior method achieves both efficient explicit surface regression and strong cross-dataset generalizability in a single forward pass on unseen scenes.

## Input / Output

The method accepts either a sparse point cloud (10k points uniformly sampled from ground-truth meshes) or pre-estimated 3D Gaussians (from vanilla 3DGS) as scene representation, along with query rays parameterized by camera center and unit direction vector. For point clouds, raylets are sampled via virtual balls (radius = distance to nearest neighbor); for Gaussians, raylets are placed at ray-Gaussian intersection points weighted by alpha-blending contributions. The network outputs a signed ray-surface distance per ray, which is converted to depth maps and subsequently to 3D meshes via TSDF or Marching Tetrahedra.

## Method

RayletDF introduces the concept of a 'raylet'—a unit ray segment whose starting point is placed near the surface—with a signed raylet distance measuring the offset from that start point to the true surface hit. The pipeline has three modules: (1) a Raylet Feature Extractor using a SparseConv backbone to produce per-point embeddings, then gathering K=5 KNN features (position, relative direction, distance, learned features) around each raylet start; (2) an 8-layer MLP (256 hidden units) that takes the raylet's start point, direction, and local feature vector to predict both a signed distance and a confidence score; (3) a Multi-raylet Blender that samples T=5 raylets along each query ray and aggregates their predictions via softmax-weighted averaging of the confidence scores. The key insight is that raylet distances encode local surface geometry patterns that are transferable across diverse scenes, enabling generalizable reconstruction without per-scene optimization. Training is fully supervised with L1 loss against ground-truth depth-derived distances, and the model generalizes to unseen datasets in a single forward pass.

## Results

On the in-domain ARKitScenes test set (trained on ARKitScenes), RayletDF achieves an ADE of 0.115 m and δ₁.₂₅ of 92.8%, outperforming the next-best ray-based method RayDF (ADE 0.183 m, δ 84.6%) by a large margin. The most striking result is cross-dataset generalization: when trained on ARKitScenes and tested on the unseen MultiScan dataset, RayletDF achieves ADE 0.216 m versus 0.326 m (RayDF), 0.266 m (Pointersect), and 0.536 m (PFGS). On the in-domain ScanNet/ScanNet++ test set (trained on ScanNet/++), RayletDF reaches ADE 0.145 m and δ 92.2%, again surpassing all baselines including per-scene methods like PGSR (ADE 0.202 m) and depth estimators like DepthAnythingV2 (ADE 0.168 m).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RayletDF outperforms all nine baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the closest ray-based competitor RayDF [36], RayletDF reduces ADE from 0.183 m to 0.115 m in-domain (ARKitScenes) and from 0.326 m to 0.216 m on the unseen MultiScan dataset, a gap that widens dramatically under cross-dataset evaluation. The per-scene Gaussian methods (3DGS, GOF, PGSR) achieve competitive in-domain results (e.g., PGSR ADE 0.202 m on ScanNet/++) but cannot generalize to new datasets since they require per-scene optimization. The feed-forward generalizable method MVSGaussian [35] performs poorly across all settings (ADE > 0.54 m), indicating that standard Gaussian splatting representations struggle with surface accuracy. Image-based depth estimators (DepthAnythingV2, Depth-Pro) achieve reasonable in-domain numbers (ADE 0.168–0.206 m) but still trail RayletDF by 0.03–0.09 m and lack geometric consistency. The point cloud rendering method PFGS [59] and ray-based Pointersect [8] are consistently outperformed, with PFGS showing particularly poor generalization (ADE 0.536 m on MultiScan vs. 0.216 m for RayletDF). The key differentiator is that RayletDF's raylet-based local surface representation generalizes far better than global ray or coordinate-based representations, as evidenced by the 0.10+ m ADE gap on unseen datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

RayletDF carves new ground by introducing the 'raylet' concept—a short ray segment anchored near a surface—as a local geometric primitive, departing from both full-ray representations (RayDF, Pointersect, PRIF) that are limited to per-scene object-level reconstruction, and coordinate-based implicit fields (SDF, UDF, NeRF) that require dense 3D sampling. The feature extraction borrows SparseConv from 3D semantic segmentation, while the multi-raylet blender with confidence-weighted aggregation is a novel design not present in prior ray-based or point-based methods. The paper positions itself as unifying the efficiency of ray-based methods with the generalizability of feed-forward architectures, something neither family had achieved before.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is that RayletDF achieves strong cross-dataset generalization (e.g., 0.216 m ADE on MultiScan when trained only on ARKitScenes) in a single forward pass, whereas all ray-based and Gaussian-splatting baselines degrade by 0.1–0.3 m in the same setting. This suggests that local surface geometric patterns encoded by raylets are more transferable than global ray or coordinate representations, a finding that could inform future generalizable 3D reconstruction pipelines. Open questions include whether the method extends to outdoor or large-scale scenes, whether the virtual-ball sampling strategy generalizes beyond indoor point clouds, and whether the pipeline can operate end-to-end from raw RGB without pre-estimated Gaussians or point clouds.
