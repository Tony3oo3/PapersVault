## Problem

Existing in-the-wild facial appearance capture methods either assume a single dominant point or directional light source (e.g., SunStage, CoRA) or ignore self-occlusion entirely (e.g., FLARE), leading to poor diffuse/specular separation and baked-in shading in the recovered albedo. This creates a large quality gap between lightweight capture and production-ready studio-based multiview pipelines, particularly in unconstrained environments with complex, unknown lighting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250354/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a short monocular video of a simple head rotation captured in any environment (indoors or outdoors, sunny or cloudy), along with per-frame rigid head poses and neck rotations estimated via landmark-based tracking and photometric alignment. The subject's expression is assumed constant throughout the sequence. The output is a high-quality 3D facial mesh with three 2D texture maps (diffuse albedo, specular intensity, specular roughness) compatible with modern skin shaders, plus the recovered environment lighting, all of which can be directly relit in novel environments.

## Method

The core contribution is a visibility-modulated split-sum approximation for the specular component: when roughness is small, the visibility-weighted environment integral is approximated by evaluating a view-dependent visibility term at the reflected direction, computed via Monte Carlo sampling from the BRDF normal distribution. For the diffuse component, ray tracing with multiple importance sampling (via OptiX) handles self-occlusion exactly. Geometry and textures are optimized simultaneously using a preconditioning framework that applies (I + λL)² to gradient steps, enabling large learning rates while maintaining mesh smoothness. The loss combines L1 photometric error, MODNet mask loss, Laplacian shape regularization, white-light regularization on the environment map, total-variation smoothing on roughness, and a weak L2 penalty on the diffuse render to prevent specular signal from being baked into the albedo.

## Results

On the in-the-wild facial appearance capture benchmark (skin-region reconstruction errors averaged over all subjects), the proposed method achieves PSNR 38.09, MAE 1.18, SSIM 0.97, and LPIPS 0.10, outperforming all three baselines on every metric. The closest competitor, FLARE, scores PSNR 30.40 / MAE 2.01 / SSIM 0.94 / LPIPS 0.15, yielding a 7.7 dB PSNR gap and a 41% reduction in MAE for the proposed method. An ablation removing the visibility term (Ours w/o vis) drops PSNR to 34.55 and raises MAE to 1.79, confirming the contribution of the occlusion-aware shading model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three in-the-wild facial appearance methods: FLARE, SunStage, and NextFace. Against FLARE (the strongest baseline), the proposed method wins on all four metrics, with the largest gap in PSNR (+7.69 dB) and MAE (−0.83); qualitatively, FLARE bakes specular signal into the diffuse albedo, produces noisy normals with self-intersections, and degrades significantly under relighting. Against SunStage, the advantage is even larger (PSNR +8.62 dB, MAE −4.10), as SunStage's single-point-light assumption causes incorrect shadows, missing specular components, and orange artifacts on the forehead. Against NextFace, the gap is the widest (PSNR +12.79 dB, MAE −9.45), since NextFace's statistical shape prior yields blurry, identity-losing reconstructions. The ablation (Ours w/o vis) further shows that the visibility-modulated split-sum term alone contributes +3.54 dB PSNR and −0.61 MAE over the unmodified split-sum baseline, validating the core novelty of the occlusion-aware shading model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the inverse-rendering line of work initiated by Munkberg et al. [48] and FLARE [4], which jointly optimize geometry, material, and lighting from images using a differentiable split-sum approximation. The key departure is a visibility-modulated split-sum that explicitly accounts for self-occlusion via Monte Carlo integration over the BRDF normal distribution, combined with OptiX ray tracing for the diffuse term—addressing the well-known issue that standard split-sum bakes shadows into albedo. For geometry optimization, it adopts the preconditioning framework of Nicolet et al. [50] to update vertex positions and textures simultaneously, avoiding the two-stage approach used by FLARE. Unlike SunStage [62] and CoRA [28], it makes no assumption about a dominant point light source, and unlike NextFace [15], it does not rely on statistical shape or appearance priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method narrows the quality gap between in-the-wild monocular capture and studio-based multiview appearance reconstruction, making production-grade facial assets accessible from a simple head-rotation video on any camera—directly applicable to film-set VFX pipelines. The visibility-modulated split-sum is a clean, generalizable technique that could benefit other inverse-rendering pipelines beyond faces. Open questions remain: the method requires accurate head pose estimates (errors substantially degrade results), cannot recover appearance in regions under extreme shadow across all frames, does not model eyes, and the illumination-appearance ambiguity means correct skin tone is not guaranteed. Extending the approach to dynamic expressions, multi-subject scenes, or integrating learned priors for pose robustness would be natural next steps.
