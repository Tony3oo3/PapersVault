## Problem

Existing Video-LLMs compress high-dimensional video data through downsampling, spatial-temporal pooling, or resamplers like Q-Former, which inevitably lose fine-grained object dynamics and produce semantically entangled visual tokens. This entanglement creates a fundamental mismatch with the well-defined semantics of text tokens, limiting Video-LLMs to general overviews rather than detailed understanding of object-level interactions and temporal variations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251806/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes video clips with 8 or 16 frames for the Video-Centric Branch (resized to 336×336 for CLIP or 384×384 for SigLIP) and 64 or 128 frames for the Object-Centric Branch, alongside a text instruction. For video-based referring, a bounding box prompt in the first frame specifies the target object, which is converted to a mask via SAM and encoded as a single object token inserted into the prompt. The output is a free-form text answer; the object tokens are not directly exposed but are implicitly referenced through the LLM's attention over the token sequence.

## Method

VideoOrion employs a two-branch architecture: a Video-Centric Branch (CLIP/SigLIP encoder + STC Connector) produces context tokens encoding general scene information, while an Object-Centric Branch generates disentangled object tokens via a detect-segment-track pipeline. The pipeline uses GroundingDINO for open-vocabulary detection, SAM for precise mask extraction, RAM++ with NLTK-based noun filtering to adaptively segment the video into clips at object transitions (thresholds θ_a=3, θ_b=2), and XMem for cross-frame multi-object tracking. An Object Projector then applies mask pooling per frame, temporal pooling across frames, and an MLP to compress each object's full trajectory into a single d-dimensional token. Training proceeds in three stages—Video-Centric pretraining (reusing VideoLLaMA2's STC-Connector), Object-Centric pretraining on 700K filtered video-text pairs, and multi-modal instruction tuning on 4M samples—using auto-regressive cross-entropy loss throughout.

## Results

VideoOrion (8 frames, Mistral-7B) achieves 63.5% on MVBench, 65.1% on EgoSchema, 65.2% on Perception-Test, 54.6/55.3% on VideoMME (w/o/with subtitles), and 57.7% accuracy / 3.7 score on ActivityNet-QA. The stronger VideoOrion+ variant (16 frames, SigLIP + Qwen2-7B) further improves to 67.4% MVBench, 65.0% EgoSchema, 65.9% Perception-Test, 58.9/61.5% VideoMME, and 60.3% / 3.7 ActivityNet-QA. On the video-referring benchmark VideoRef45K, VideoOrion-Ref-FT+ achieves CIDEr 90.6 and SPICE 31.4, substantially surpassing the prior best (Artemis: CIDEr 53.2, SPICE 25.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoOrion outperforms all 13 baselines on every reported metric. The most informative comparison is against VideoLLaMA2 (same Video-Centric Branch, no object tokens): VideoOrion gains +10.1 pts on MVBench (63.5 vs 53.4), +14.6 on EgoSchema (65.1 vs 50.5), +15.6 on Perception-Test (65.2 vs 49.6), +8.7 on VideoMME (54.6 vs 45.1), and +7.8 on ActivityNet-QA (57.7 vs 49.9), isolating the contribution of the Object-Centric Branch. VideoOrion+ similarly dominates VideoLLaMA2.1 (same Qwen2-7B backbone) by +10.1 on MVBench, +11.9 on EgoSchema, +11.0 on Perception-Test, and +7.3 on ActivityNet-QA. Against the strongest Qwen2-based competitor LLaVA-OneVision, VideoOrion+ leads by +10.7 on MVBench, +4.9 on EgoSchema, +8.8 on Perception-Test, and +3.7 on ActivityNet-QA, while tying on VideoMME with subtitles (61.5 vs 61.5). On video referring (VideoRef45K), VideoOrion-Ref-FT+ surpasses Artemis by +37.4 CIDEr (90.6 vs 53.2) and +6.0 SPICE (31.4 vs 25.4), and Merlin by +80.1 CIDEr and +11.3 SPICE, demonstrating that object tokens enable precise target identification that prior referring-specific models lack.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoOrion extends the VideoLLaMA2 architecture by adding an Object-Centric Branch alongside the existing Video-Centric Branch (which reuses VideoLLaMA2's STC Connector). Conceptually, it departs from the dominant paradigm in Video-LLMs—where visual tokens are produced by pooling or resampling image patches and left for the LLM to interpret—by instead leveraging a pipeline of expert vision models (GroundingDINO, SAM, XMem, RAM++) to explicitly extract and track objects, producing semantically disentangled object tokens. The closest prior work is Slot-VLM, which uses slot attention to cluster tokens into object-centric representations, but VideoOrion replaces learned clustering with deterministic detection and tracking, and adds temporal fusion across frames. The approach also draws on the broader vision foundation model ecosystem (open-vocabulary detection, segment-anything, multi-object tracking) to ground visual tokens in explicit object semantics rather than implicit patch-level features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit object-centric tokenization can substantially improve video understanding (10–15% over same-branch baselines) while simultaneously enabling video-based referring as a natural byproduct, unifying two capabilities that prior Video-LLMs treat separately. Key limitations include the computational overhead of running multiple vision models in the detect-segment-track pipeline, potential mask extraction errors on low-quality videos, and continued reliance on the Video-Centric Branch for contextual information. An open question the authors flag is how to better align the two branches, and whether future advances in vision models will reduce the pipeline's cost. The finding that a simple linear layer suffices for the Object Projector (matching or exceeding attention/LSTM) suggests the temporal dynamics are already well-captured by the tracking pipeline, reducing the need for additional temporal modeling in the projector.
