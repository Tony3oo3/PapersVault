## Problem

End-to-end autonomous driving models trained in open-loop environments predict trajectory waypoints that fail in closed-loop deployment because they cannot quickly react to reactive agents and may violate kinematic constraints, leading to compounding errors and unsafe behaviors. Existing open-loop planners lack direct control signal prediction for rapid responses, and neglect kinematic feasibility of predicted trajectories when the ego vehicle deviates from ground-truth paths.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251938/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes front-view and back-view RGB images (resized to 800×450) processed by a ResNet-50 backbone into environment tokens, along with ego longitudinal velocity and a one-hot navigation command. It produces a 3-second trajectory at 2 Hz from a discrete vocabulary of 4096 trajectories, discrete control tuples (brake, throttle, steer) at 2 Hz, and refined high-frequency control sequences at 10 Hz from the diffusion policy. The final output is an ensembled control signal that is kinematically feasible via a bicycle model rollout.

## Method

Hydra-NeXt unifies three planning branches: (1) a Trajectory Decoder (π_traj) inherited from Hydra-MDP that selects from a discrete trajectory vocabulary via a transformer decoder attending to environment tokens, trained with imitation learning plus knowledge distillation from rule-based metrics (collision, soft lane keeping, ego progress); (2) a Control Decoder (π_ctrl) that classifies discrete control tuples per timestep using cross-entropy loss, enabling fast multi-modal reactions to reactive agents without relying on PID/MPC path-following; and (3) a Trajectory Refinement module (π_dp) built on a Diffusion Policy that generates N smooth high-frequency control sequences, from which a Nearest Neighbor Matching algorithm selects kinematically feasible proposals via a bicycle model and ensembles them with the trajectory and control predictions. The key insight is that waypoints handle general navigation, direct control prediction handles short-term reactive decisions, and diffusion-based refinement ensures kinematic feasibility—collectively bridging the open-loop-to-closed-loop gap without requiring RL or external expert features at inference.

## Results

On the Bench2Drive benchmark with the CARLA v2 evaluation protocol, Hydra-NeXt achieves 65.89 Driving Score (DS) and 48.20% Success Rate (SR), surpassing the previous state-of-the-art DriveAdapter by +22.98 DS and +17.49% SR. Under the Bench2Drive protocol, it reaches 73.86 DS and 50.00% SR. On the real-world NAVSIM benchmark, Hydra-NeXt attains 88.6 PDMS, a new state-of-the-art that improves over Hydra-MDP (86.5) and DiffusionDrive (88.1). Notably, Hydra-NeXt uses only front and back cameras (vs. 6 for most baselines) and requires no expert feature distillation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hydra-NeXt outperforms all eight baselines on both Driving Score and Success Rate under the CARLA v2 protocol. The largest margin is against DriveAdapter (42.91 DS, 30.71% SR), the previous SOTA that relies on expert feature embeddings from Think2Drive, with Hydra-NeXt gaining +22.98 DS and +17.49% SR while using only 2 cameras and no expert features. Against Hydra-MDP (52.80 DS, 30.73% SR), its direct predecessor, Hydra-NeXt improves by +13.09 DS and +17.47% SR, demonstrating the value of the control decoder and trajectory refinement. On multi-ability metrics, Hydra-NeXt excels in interactive scenarios (overtaking +38.06%, merging +11.18%, emergency braking +12.91% over DriveAdapter) but lags on traffic sign compliance (50.00% vs. 57.21%). On NAVSIM, Hydra-NeXt (88.6 PDMS) edges out DiffusionDrive (88.1) and Hydra-MDP (86.5), confirming generalization to real-world data. The only metric where Hydra-NeXt trails is Comfort (20.68 vs. TCP's 47.80), attributed to frequent braking in interactive situations.

## Relation to Prior Work

Hydra-NeXt extends Hydra-MDP, the state-of-the-art open-loop E2E planner on NAVSIM, by adding two new components: a classification-based control decoder for short-term reactive actions and a Diffusion Policy-based Trajectory Refinement module that enforces kinematic feasibility. The control decoder draws on the ensembling idea from TCP and the discretization practice from Think2Drive, while the refinement module repurposes Diffusion Policy (originally for robot manipulation) to generate smooth high-frequency control sequences. Conceptually, the paper positions itself in the 'open-loop to closed-loop' direction, arguing that leveraging massive real-world data is more practical than the reverse (closed-loop to open-loop), which faces sim-to-real domain gaps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 22.98 DS improvement over DriveAdapter without relying on expert feature distillation at inference is a meaningful step toward practical E2E driving, as it removes a key deployment barrier. However, the method trades comfort for safety (frequent braking in interactive scenarios), lags behind DriveAdapter on traffic sign compliance, and the diffusion policy adds substantial latency (~528 ms with Flash-attention vs. ~245 ms for trajectory-only). Open questions include whether the kinematic refinement generalizes beyond CARLA's physics, how to reduce the comfort penalty, and whether the approach scales to longer-horizon planning without compounding errors.
