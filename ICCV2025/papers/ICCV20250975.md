## Problem

Existing image quality assessment databases and metrics are designed for natural images or general AI-generated content, but AI-generated faces (AIGFs) exhibit unique distortions—unrealistic skin textures, identity shifts, and text-image misalignments—that are not adequately captured by these general-purpose evaluators. No comprehensive database or benchmark existed that simultaneously covers face generation, customization, and restoration with multi-dimensional human preference annotations aligned to how humans actually perceive face quality.

## Input / Output

F-Eval accepts a generated face image (at original resolution), a text prompt describing the desired content, and an optional reference identity image (required for customization and restoration tasks). It produces four scalar quality scores on a 0–100 Z-scored scale: Quality (overall perceptual quality), Authenticity (photorealism of skin and details), ID Fidelity (identity preservation relative to the reference), and Correspondence (text-image alignment). The model handles both single-image and paired-image inputs within a unified framework, with the reference image being optional depending on the task.

## Method

F-Eval builds on Qwen-VL-2.5-7B as a frozen LLM backbone and introduces a dual-stream vision encoder: a Qwen-VL-2.5-ViT for global visual features and a frozen ArcFace for identity/structure features, both mapped into a shared language space via two-layer MLP projectors. The key architectural innovation is Mixture of LoRA Experts (MoLE), where four separate LoRA adapters (one per evaluation dimension) are trained on the vision encoder and LLM, and a trainable router classifies the input prompt to activate the appropriate expert, preventing cross-dimension interference that a single LoRA would introduce. Training uses instruction tuning with diverse prompt templates for each dimension, fine-tuning only the projectors and LoRA parameters (ranks 16–32) while keeping the backbone frozen, enabling a single model to evaluate all four dimensions across all three face tasks in a one-for-all manner.

## Results

F-Eval achieves the best SRCC across all tasks and dimensions on FaceQ. On Face Generation, it reaches SRCC 0.8486 (Quality), 0.8312 (Authenticity), and 0.8471 (Correspondence), outperforming the strongest baseline MINTIQA* (0.8312/0.8177/0.7908). On Face Customization, F-Eval achieves SRCC 0.9462 (Quality), 0.9188 (ID Fidelity), and 0.9460 (Correspondence), surpassing PromptIQA* (0.9071/0.8693/0.8348) by a substantial margin. On Face Restoration (synthetic), F-Eval attains SRCC 0.8692 (Quality) and 0.8507 (ID Fidelity), edging out TReS* (0.8656) and PromptIQA* (0.8220) respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

F-Eval outperforms all 24 baselines across every task and dimension. Against the strongest AIGC-specific baseline MINTIQA* (fine-tuned), F-Eval gains +0.017 SRCC on Gen Quality, +0.013 on Gen Authenticity, +0.056 on Gen Correspondence, and a large +0.056 on Cus Quality and +0.067 on Cus ID Fidelity. Against the best classical deep-learning IQA methods (MANIQA*, TReS*, PromptIQA*), F-Eval leads by 0.017–0.056 SRCC on generation and 0.039–0.111 on customization. Traditional NR-IQA methods (NIQE, HOSA, BPRI) and face-recognition-based metrics (ArcFace, SER-FIQ) show near-random or negative correlations, confirming they are fundamentally misaligned with AIGF quality. AIGC alignment metrics (CLIPScore, BLIPScore, ImageReward) perform well on Correspondence (0.52–0.77 SRCC) but poorly on Quality and Authenticity, highlighting their focus on high-level semantics rather than low-level perceptual quality. The largest relative gains for F-Eval appear in Face Customization, where existing methods rarely target human preference, and in ID Fidelity, where the dual-stream face encoder provides a clear advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of three previously separate research threads: AIGC image quality assessment (AGIQA-3K, AIGCIQA, RichHF-18K), natural face quality assessment (PIQ23, DSL-FIQA, SER-FIQ), and LMM-based preference evaluation (MINTIQA). None of these prior works specifically target AI-generated faces across the full spectrum of generation, customization, and restoration tasks with multi-dimensional human preference annotations. Methodologically, F-Eval repurposes the Qwen-VL-2.5 LMM backbone and ArcFace identity encoder within a novel Mixture of LoRA Experts (MoLE) framework, extending the instruction-tuning paradigm from MINTIQA to a face-specific, multi-dimensional setting where a trainable router activates dimension-specific LoRA experts to avoid cross-dimensional interference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FaceQ (12K images, 491K ratings) and F-Bench (29 models, 26 QA methods) fill a critical gap: prior AIGC databases contained only limited face content, and prior face IQA databases used only natural images, making neither suitable for evaluating the unique distortions of AI-generated faces. The benchmark reveals that even strong fine-tuned IQA models (MANIQA, TReS) drop substantially on AIGFs compared to natural images, and that face customization is the most under-served task where existing methods show the largest gaps. Open questions include whether the LMM-based evaluator generalizes to unseen generative architectures, whether the MoLE routing strategy scales beyond four dimensions, and whether the findings transfer to video face generation or 3D face reconstruction.
