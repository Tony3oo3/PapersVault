## Problem

Existing self-training frameworks for Day-to-Night Domain Adaptive Object Detection suffer from three systematic biases: distribution bias from the day-night domain gap, training bias that causes the model to favor source-domain features and overrepresented classes, and confirmation bias where the model reinforces its own incorrect pseudo labels. Prior approaches address these issues in isolation (e.g., image transfer, adversarial domain invariance, or fixed-threshold pseudo-labeling) but lack a unified framework that simultaneously mitigates all three.

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled daytime RGB images (source domain) and unlabeled nighttime RGB images (target domain), both resized to 600 pixels on the shorter side, and produces bounding boxes with class labels for the nighttime target domain. Daytime images are additionally transformed into night-like images via physical priors (darkness, noise, lens flare) to serve as an intermediate domain for mixing and confidence calibration. The detector backbone is Faster R-CNN with ResNet-50, and the teacher is updated via EMA from the student without gradient accumulation.

## Method

The Debiased Teacher (DeT) comprises three modules operating within a teacher-student self-training loop. (1) DNDT transforms daytime images into night-like images by modeling illumination (truncated Gaussian darkness coefficient), sensor noise (Poisson shot noise + Gaussian read noise in RAW space), and lens flare (random affine-transformed samples from Flare7K with random brightness), explicitly modeling the day-night distribution bias. (2) CDRC performs bidirectional object-level mixing (day-to-night and night-to-day) between target and night-like images, combined with an Inverse Class Frequency Balancing submodule that up-samples underrepresented classes using an inverse-distribution sampling function with a tunable balance factor α. (3) ConCal generates pseudo labels in two steps: first, it suppresses low-confidence proposals using dynamically updated class-specific thresholds (base threshold + softmax-weighted average confidence from night-like images with ground truth); second, it recovers potential true positives among discarded proposals by requiring IoU matching, minimum confidence, and class consistency between teacher and student predictions, then averaging their coordinates.

## Results

On BDD100k (day→night), DeT achieves 50.9% mAP, surpassing the previous best self-training method CoS (49.4%) by 1.5 points and ISP-Teacher (49.2%) by 1.7 points. On SHIFT, DeT reaches 52.8% mAP, edging out ISP-Teacher (52.4%) and SOCCER (51.5%). On TDND (severe weather), DeT achieves 46.7% mAP, outperforming the runner-up CoS (42.2%) by a substantial 4.5 points. The method also shows the most balanced per-class performance, with a standard deviation of 9.41% on BDD100k versus 9.97% (CoS) and 14.30% (SOCCER).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeT outperforms all 11 compared baselines on all three benchmarks. On BDD100k, it beats the strongest self-training baselines CoS (49.4%) and ISP-Teacher (49.2%) by 1.5 and 1.7 mAP points respectively, and surpasses 2PCNet (47.6%) by 3.3 points. On SHIFT, the margin over ISP-Teacher (52.4%) is narrow (0.4 mAP), but DeT leads SOCCER (51.5%) and CoS (51.0%) by 1.3 and 1.8 points. On TDND, the advantage is most pronounced: DeT (46.7%) exceeds CoS (42.2%) by 4.5 mAP and ISP-Teacher (37.2%) by 9.5 mAP, suggesting ConCal's pseudo-label calibration is particularly effective under severe weather. Notably, DeT achieves the best per-class scores on 7 of 9 BDD100k classes (person, rider, truck, bus, mcycle, bicycle, t-sign), with the largest gains on underrepresented classes like mcycle (+12.0 over ISP-Teacher) and bicycle (+5.9 over ISP-Teacher), validating the ICFB module's class-balancing effect.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-training (teacher-student) framework for day-to-night domain adaptive object detection, building directly on methods like 2PCNet, ISP-Teacher, and CoS that improve pseudo-label quality and cross-domain feature consistency. Its key conceptual departure is the explicit diagnosis of three distinct failure modes—distribution bias, training bias, and confirmation bias—and the design of a dedicated module for each (DNDT, CDRC, ConCal). The DNDT module repurposes physical imaging priors (Poisson shot noise, Gaussian read noise, lens flare from Flare7K) as a parameter-free day-to-night transformation, contrasting with prior learned image-transfer or sensor-specific ISP pipelines. The CDRC's bidirectional object-level mixing with inverse class frequency balancing and ConCal's teacher-student joint distillation of discarded proposals are novel combinations not present in prior DAOD work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The three-bias taxonomy provides a structured diagnostic lens that could generalize beyond day-to-night to other domain gaps (e.g., synthetic-to-real, weather changes), and the parameter-free physical-prior transformation (DNDT) is attractive for practitioners who lack sensor-specific ISP parameters. However, the method is validated only with Faster R-CNN + ResNet-50 on three benchmarks, and the per-benchmark gains over the strongest baselines are moderate (1.5–4.5 mAP), so it remains an open question whether the framework scales to transformer-based detectors or larger-scale datasets. The reliance on a curated flare bank (Flare7K) and hand-tuned hyperparameters (α, β, τ, δ bounds) also limits plug-and-play applicability.
