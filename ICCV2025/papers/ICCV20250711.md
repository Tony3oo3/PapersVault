## Problem

Evaluating the quality of multi-modal pre-training in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) typically requires costly supervised fine-tuning followed by benchmark evaluation, since standard LLM pre-training metrics (loss, perplexity, in-context evaluation) are neither stable nor reliable for measuring cross-modal alignment. This lack of a proper pre-training metric hinders research on training data selection, efficient module design, and training strategy optimization for LVLMs.

## Input / Output

MIR takes N image-text pairs (default N=100) and extracts vision and text tokens from every layer of the language model decoder, computing a single scalar score via log-accumulated Fréchet distances across layers. The computation applies a text-centric normalization factor α_k per layer to neutralize cross-layer magnitude differences, a 3σ outlier removal function to handle abnormally high-norm tokens, and a Newton-Schulz iteration to approximate the matrix square root term efficiently. MoCa, the companion module, takes vision tokens at each LLM layer output and produces calibrated tokens via element-wise scaling (learnable vector u, init all-ones) and shifting (learnable vector v, init all-zeros) before passing to the next layer.

## Method

MIR measures cross-modal alignment by computing an improved Fréchet Distance between vision and text token distributions at each language model layer, then log-accumulating across layers to capture how quickly the base LLM narrows the modality gap. Four targeted modifications make Fréchet Distance suitable for the cross-modality setting: a text-centric normalization factor α_k that preserves the absolute value disparity between modalities while enabling fair cross-layer comparison, a modality-specific 3σ outlier removal to prevent distribution skew, logarithmic accumulation to reflect integration speed, and Newton-Schulz iteration for efficient matrix square root computation. Complementing MIR, MoCa inserts a lightweight learnable calibration (scaling + shifting) exclusively on vision tokens at each LLM layer output, counteracting the text-biased normalization inherited from the base LLM and actively pulling vision token distributions toward the text distribution. Experiments on LLaVA-v1.5, ShareGPT4V, and Mini-Gemini show MIR correlates strongly (PCR up to 0.985) with post-SFT benchmark performance across data scaling, caption detailedness, training recipes, unlocking strategies, and projector architecture choices, while MoCa yields +1.5% and +0.9% average gains on LLaVA-v1.5 and Mini-Gemini respectively.

## Results

MoCa, the proposed lightweight calibration module, reduces MIR from 3.374 to 3.162 on LLaVA-v1.5 7B and from 2.667 to 2.514 on Mini-Gemini 7B, yielding +1.5% and +0.9% average gains across 10 post-SFT benchmarks respectively. MIR demonstrates strong correlation with post-SFT performance (Pearson r = 0.985 for data detailedness, 0.953 for projector selection, 0.712 for unlocking strategies), substantially outperforming loss (r = 0.873), PPL (r = 0.815), and in-context evaluation (r = 0.068) as pre-training quality indicators. In projector design, the 2-layer MLP achieves the lowest MIR (3.182) and highest average score (63.9), outperforming Q-Former (MIR 3.673, avg 53.7) by over 10 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary architectural comparison is between Q-Former (from BLIP-2 [17]) and MLP projectors as vision-language projectors. The 2-layer MLP decisively outperforms Q-Former: MIR drops from 3.673 to 3.182, and average post-SFT score jumps from 53.7 to 63.9 (+10.2 points), with particularly large gains on MMStar (26.3→33.8), SEED-Img (51.3→66.5), and GQA (51.3→62.9). The 1-layer and 4-layer MLP variants perform similarly to the 2-layer version (avg 63.6 and 63.7), confirming 2-layer as the sweet spot. For the MoCa module, the comparison is against the vanilla (no-calibration) versions of LLaVA-v1.5 and Mini-Gemini: MoCa consistently improves MIR and average benchmark scores by +1.5% and +0.9% respectively, with the largest single-benchmark gain on MMStar (+6.2 on LLaVA-v1.5, +1.8 on Mini-Gemini). MIR itself is compared against loss, PPL, and in-context evaluation as pre-training metrics; MIR achieves Pearson correlations of 0.712–0.985 with post-SFT performance across different experimental settings, while in-context evaluation shows near-zero correlation (r = 0.068) and loss/PPL show moderate but inconsistent correlations (r = 0.546–0.873).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Fréchet Distance (originally developed for GAN evaluation) into a cross-modal alignment metric for LVLMs, adding text-centric normalization, outlier removal, and logarithmic layer-wise accumulation to handle the specific challenges of multi-modal token distributions. It departs from the standard practice of borrowing LLM pre-training metrics (loss, perplexity, in-context evaluation) by demonstrating their inadequacy for measuring modality integration, and positions MIR as a distribution-distance-based alternative that directly targets the modality gap observed in t-SNE visualizations of LVLM embeddings. The MoCa module draws on the lightweight calibration idea (learnable scale-and-shift vectors) but applies it specifically to vision tokens at each LLM layer to counteract the text-biased normalization inherited from the base LLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MIR offers a cheap, SFT-free diagnostic for LVLM pre-training that correlates strongly with downstream performance, potentially saving significant compute by enabling early stopping and data-scale decisions without full fine-tuning cycles. The robustness to irrelevant image-text pairs and diverse content types makes it practically deployable as a training monitor. Open questions include whether MIR generalizes beyond 7B-scale models and the LLaVA/Mini-Gemini architecture family, whether it extends to other modality pairs (audio, video), and whether the simple MoCa module's gains persist at larger scales or with more complex projector designs. The paper's experiments are limited to 7B variants and a fixed set of benchmarks, leaving the metric's behavior under extreme scale or novel architectures untested.
