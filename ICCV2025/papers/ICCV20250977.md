## Problem

Standard voxel-wise losses (cross-entropy, Dice, Lovász) in 3D occupancy prediction treat each voxel independently, failing to enforce spatial consistency between neighboring voxels. This produces physically implausible artifacts—floating voxels, disjoint surfaces, misaligned boundaries—that maintain high 3D IoU yet degrade downstream tasks like free-space estimation and motion planning. The missing mechanism is a projective consistency constraint that penalizes 3D configurations producing incoherent 2D projections.

## Input / Output

GaussRender is a plug-and-play training module that consumes the voxel grid output of any existing 3D occupancy architecture (e.g., TPVFormer's tri-plane, SurroundOcc's octree, Symphonies' query-voxel) and adds a differentiable rendering loss; it requires no architectural changes and no inference-time modifications. The 3D occupancy grid resolution varies by dataset: 50 cm voxels over [−50,50]×[−50,50]×[−5,3] m (SurroundOcc-nuScenes), 40 cm over [−40,40]×[−40,40]×[−1,5.4] m (Occ3D-nuScenes), and 20 cm over [0,51.2]×[−25.6,25.6]×[−2,4.4] m (SSCBench-KITTI360). Each voxel is converted into a spherical Gaussian with fixed center and scale, semantic logits as color, and a learned opacity, enabling efficient differentiable projection into 2D.

## Method

GaussRender enforces 3D–2D projective consistency by rendering both predicted and ground-truth voxel grids into 2D camera views via differentiable Gaussian splatting, then applying L1 losses on the resulting semantic and depth images. Each voxel becomes a simple spherical Gaussian (fixed center at voxel center, fixed isotropic scale, identity rotation, semantic logits as color, learned opacity), and the 3D covariance is projected to 2D via the camera Jacobian. Two virtual cameras are used per training step: a fixed orthographic bird's-eye-view camera for global ground-plane consistency, and a dynamically positioned camera elevated along z and randomly translated in xy to reduce occlusions and expose previously hidden surfaces. The total training loss is L = L_3D + λ·L_2D, where L_2D sums per-camera depth and semantic L1 terms, making the module compatible with any architecture that outputs a voxel grid while adding minimal computational overhead compared to NeRF-based alternatives.

## Results

On SurroundOcc-nuScenes, TPVFormer with GaussRender achieves 32.05 IoU / 20.85 mIoU (+3.75 mIoU over the original), and SurroundOcc with GaussRender reaches 32.61 IoU / 20.82 mIoU, both surpassing GaussianFormerV2 (20.02 mIoU). On Occ3D-nuScenes, TPVFormer with GaussRender attains 30.48 mIoU, the best result, outperforming GSRender (29.56) and RenderOcc (26.11) without requiring LiDAR. On SSCBench-KITTI360, Symphonies with GaussRender reaches 44.08 IoU / 18.11 mIoU. On the surface-sensitive RayIoU metric (Occ3D-nuScenes), GaussRender yields the largest gains: +2.0 for SurroundOcc (37.5) and +1.1 for TPVFormer (38.3), both achieving state-of-the-art with a single frame.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaussRender consistently improves every architecture it is applied to: TPVFormer gains +3.75 mIoU on SurroundOcc-nuScenes and +2.65 mIoU on Occ3D-nuScenes, while SurroundOcc gains +0.52 and +1.17 mIoU respectively, and Symphonies gains +0.29 mIoU on KITTI360. On Occ3D-nuScenes, TPVFormer with GaussRender (30.48 mIoU) outperforms all prior rendering-based methods—GSRender (29.56), RenderOcc (26.11), and CTF-Occ (28.53)—without requiring LiDAR inputs. On the surface-sensitive RayIoU metric, SurroundOcc with GaussRender (37.5) surpasses SparseOcc-16f (36.1) and FB-Occ (33.5) using only a single frame, whereas prior SOTA required 16 temporal frames. Under 2D-only supervision (Table 2), GaussRender achieves 25.3 mIoU, beating the temporal version of RenderOcc (23.9) by +1.4 mIoU and the best self-supervised method GaussianOcc (9.9) by a wide margin. On KITTI360, absolute gains are smaller (+0.26 mIoU) due to the finer 20 cm voxel resolution, but visible-voxel mIoU improves by +1.23 and image IoU by +3.83, confirming that GaussRender primarily eliminates visibility artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

GaussRender builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) as an efficient differentiable rendering backend, but repurposes it not as a scene representation (as in GaussianOcc, GSRender, or GaussTR) but as a plug-and-play supervision signal applied to any voxel-based occupancy model. It departs from NeRF-based rendering losses (RenderOcc, SelfOcc) by eliminating the need for ray-casting, temporal frames, or lidar reprojections, and from prior Gaussian-occupancy methods by decoupling the Gaussian primitive from the model architecture entirely. The key conceptual departure is that GaussRender renders both predictions and ground truth from arbitrary virtual cameras, removing the constraint that supervision must come from recorded sensor viewpoints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of GaussRender means any existing 3D occupancy architecture (tri-plane, octree, query-voxel) can benefit from projective consistency supervision without architectural changes, making it a broadly applicable training-time module. The notably larger gains on surface-sensitive metrics (RayIoU +1.1 to +2.0) versus standard IoU confirm that the method addresses a real failure mode—floating voxels and discontinuous surfaces—that voxel-wise losses structurally cannot penalize. Open questions include whether the approach extends to temporal/dynamic occupancy, whether it generalizes to open-vocabulary settings, and whether the arbitrary-camera strategy can be made adaptive per scene complexity rather than relying on fixed elevation/translation heuristics.
