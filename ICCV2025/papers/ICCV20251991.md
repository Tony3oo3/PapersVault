## Problem

Open-world 3D semantic occupancy prediction requires recognizing both known and unknown objects from sensor inputs, but existing approaches face two fundamental limitations: VLM-derived pseudo-labels with traditional supervision are confined to a predefined label space and cannot generalize to novel categories, while direct alignment with pretrained image embeddings suffers from significant modality gaps between image and text representations in VLMs, leading to unreliable predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251991/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes N_cam surrounding camera images (six views from nuScenes) and text prompts (category labels decomposed into finer-grained subcategory phrases, plus randomly sampled noise phrases from a general dictionary). It produces a 200×200×16 voxel grid covering [-40m,40m]×[-40m,40m]×[-1m,5.4m] at 0.4m resolution with per-voxel semantic class assignments. LiDAR point clouds are used only during training to establish geometric correspondence for pseudo-label generation and visibility masking, not at inference time.

## Method

AGO decouples known and unknown category learning through two parallel pathways over the same TPVFormer-based 3D encoder. For known categories, a grounding training paradigm computes dot-product similarity between 3D voxel features and frozen BERT text embeddings (augmented with random noise prompts as negative samples), producing generalized logits supervised by VLM-generated 3D pseudo-labels with cross-entropy and Lovász-softmax losses. For unknown categories, a lightweight 2-layer MLP modality adapter projects the 3D embeddings into a separate space aligned with MaskCLIP+ image embeddings via cosine similarity on visible non-empty voxels, avoiding the modality conflicts that arise when both text and image alignment losses are applied to the same embedding. At inference, an open-world identifier selects per-voxel between the grounding-based and alignment-based probability distributions using minimum information entropy as the discrimination criterion, enabling adaptive handling of both known and novel objects without retraining.

## Results

On the closed-world self-supervised Occ3D-nuScenes benchmark, AGO achieves 19.23 mIoU (21.39 mIoU* excluding 'other' and 'other flat'), surpassing the previous best method VEON (15.14 mIoU) by 4.09 mIoU. AGO also leads in most individual categories, including driveable surface (71.48 IoU), terrain (41.37 IoU), sidewalk (34.48 IoU), and car (22.82 IoU). In the open-world setting, AGO achieves 12.86 mIoU during pretraining, 8.96 mIoU zero-shot, and 14.43 mIoU after few-shot fine-tuning, consistently outperforming alignment-based baselines (GaussTR, POP-3D) and pseudo-label-based baselines (SelfOcc) across all three stages.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGO outperforms all eight baselines on the closed-world self-supervised benchmark, with the largest margin over VEON (19.23 vs. 15.14 mIoU, +4.09) and GaussTR (19.23 vs. 11.70 mIoU, +7.53). Among alignment-based methods (POP-3D, GaussTR, LangOcc), AGO gains 10.92, 7.53, and 7.39 mIoU respectively, attributed to its grounding training with noise prompts and the modality adapter that avoids direct modality conflicts. Among pseudo-label-based methods (SelfOcc, OccNeRF, GaussianOcc), AGO improves by 9.93, 9.70, and 9.29 mIoU, benefiting from multi-frame aggregation and ray casting in pseudo-label generation. In the open-world few-shot setting, AGO achieves 14.43 mIoU versus GaussTR's 9.66 and SelfOcc's 8.06, with particularly strong gains on unknown categories (e.g., 29.63 IoU on 'terrain' vs. 12.74 for GaussTR). Notably, AGO achieves these results with only 62.5M parameters, compared to VEON's 678.1M, making it substantially more efficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

AGO builds on TPVFormer's tri-perspective 3D encoder and departs from two dominant paradigms in open-vocabulary occupancy prediction: (1) pseudo-label-based supervision (SelfOcc, OccNeRF, GaussianOcc), which is limited to a fixed label space, and (2) direct VLM image-embedding alignment (POP-3D, GaussTR), which suffers from modality gaps between text and image representations. The key conceptual departure is 'adaptive grounding,' which decouples closed-world training (text-embedding grounding with noise-prompt negatives) from open-world knowledge transfer (image-embedding alignment through a learned modality adapter), avoiding the modality conflict that arises when both objectives are applied to the same 3D features. The open-world identifier using minimum information entropy at inference is a new mechanism not present in prior occupancy methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe for open-world 3D occupancy that does not require retraining when new categories appear, which is critical for real-world autonomous driving where the object taxonomy is never fixed. The structured 3-stage open-world evaluation protocol (pretraining → zero-shot → few-shot) is a useful benchmarking framework that prior methods lacked. However, evaluation is limited to a single dataset (Occ3D-nuScenes), temporal information is not exploited, and the modality gap is mitigated rather than eliminated—richer text prompts and multi-frame temporal reasoning are identified as open directions that could further close the gap to supervised performance.
