## Problem

Existing lighting estimation methods from regular photographs either extend dynamic range for tonemapping rather than recovering true light source intensities, or rely on representations (parametric lights, spherical harmonics, volumetric fields, environment maps) that cannot simultaneously support near-field effects, spatially-varying illumination, and high-frequency reflections. No prior method can use ordinary LDR images as functional light sources in a 3D renderer with spatially-varying HDR lighting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252358/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of regular LDR photographs (sRGB, γ=2.2) of a scene as input—no multi-exposure bracketing or specialized hardware is required. It produces per-image unsaturated HDR images and a 3D Gaussian Splat scene in linear color space, where each Gaussian carries an HDR intensity that can be queried for incoming radiance from any direction. The resulting 3DGS is directly importable into renderers like Blender/Cycles, with Gaussians above intensity 1 acting as light emitters and the rest as diffuse surfaces.

## Method

GASLIGHT is a two-stage pipeline. First, a diffusion model (IC-Light fine-tuned on 12,611 HDR panoramas) is trained to predict two re-exposed versions (±2 EV) of an input LDR image using text conditioning ('dark'/'bright'); at inference, the network is applied recursively (~5 iterations) until no saturated pixels remain, and the resulting exposure stack is merged via Debevec HDR fusion with a custom weighting scheme that preserves unsaturated pixels from the original image. Second, the per-image HDR outputs are used to train a 3D Gaussian Splatting scene via InstantSplat, optimizing with L1 loss plus SSIM on LDR pixels. The key insight is that diffusion model priors enable plausible extrapolation of bright light source intensities beyond the LDR range, while the 3DGS representation natively supports spatially-varying, high-frequency lighting with efficient renderer integration—something environment maps, spherical harmonics, and volumetric representations each lack.

## Results

On the filtered Si-HDR dataset, GASLIGHT achieves the best MSE (0.0131) and PSNR (25.39 dB) among all compared LDR-to-HDR methods, outperforming the next-best SingleHDR (MSE 0.0178, PSNR 24.07) and ExpandNet (MSE 0.0187, PSNR 24.72). On BtP-HDR, it attains the lowest MSE (0.0041) but a slightly lower PSNR (28.70 dB) than HDRCNN (30.67 dB), which the authors attribute to out-of-distribution color from fixed white balance. For 3D lighting representation, GASLIGHT substantially outperforms PanoHDR-NeRF on both test scenes, e.g., achieving PSNR 30.50 vs. 18.97 on the Chess room and 23.20 vs. 21.68 on the Small class.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GASLIGHT clearly outperforms all five LDR-to-HDR baselines (HDRCNN, MaskHDR, SingleHDR, ExpandNet, Exposure Diffusion) on the Si-HDR dataset in terms of relighting MSE and PSNR, with a margin of 0.0047 MSE and 0.67 dB PSNR over the closest competitor (ExpandNet). On BtP-HDR, it achieves the best MSE (0.0041) but trails HDRCNN (30.67 dB) and MaskHDR (30.95 dB) on PSNR, which the authors attribute to color shifts from fixed white balance in that dataset. Against GlowGAN on a 23-image landscape subset, GASLIGHT wins on PSNR (25.05 vs. 23.82) and P-PIQE (37.56 vs. 56.82) but is slightly behind on MSE (0.013 vs. 0.012) and H-VDP3 (8.49 vs. 5.67), though GlowGAN is category-specific while GASLIGHT is not. For the 3D lighting representation comparison, GASLIGHT dominates PanoHDR-NeRF by a wide margin on both scenes: PSNR improves from 18.97 to 30.50 (Chess room) and from 21.68 to 23.20 (Small class), with MSE dropping by roughly 5× on the Chess room. The ablation replacing GASLIGHT's diffusion-based HDR estimator with LaNet [67] shows a consistent quality drop (PSNR 23.00 vs. 30.50 on Chess room), confirming the contribution of the diffusion-based intensity estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-studied lines: diffusion-based LDR-to-HDR estimation (building on IC-Light, Exposure Diffusion, and LeDiff) and 3D scene representations for lighting (building on 3D Gaussian Splatting and InstantSplat). The key departure from prior HDR estimation work is that it does not require multi-exposure inputs (unlike HDR-GS, HDR-NeRF, or CineGR) and targets actual light-source intensity extrapolation rather than tonemapping/visualization. The key departure from prior lighting representations is that 3DGS simultaneously provides spatially-varying, high-frequency, near-field-capable lighting—a combination that parametric lights, spherical harmonics, volumetric fields, and environment maps each achieve only partially (Tab. 1).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GASLIGHT is the first framework to use regular LDR photographs directly as spatially-varying HDR light sources in a 3D renderer, which has immediate practical value for virtual object insertion, AR, and multimedia production. However, several limitations remain open: the per-image HDR estimation lacks multi-view consistency (mitigated only by SH regularization in 3DGS), the method cannot model interreflections or scattering, it only recovers lighting visible in the input images (no view extrapolation), and the InstantSplat-based pipeline does not scale to large scenes. A natural next step is enforcing multi-view consistency in the diffusion model (e.g., via score distillation) and combining with view extrapolation to recover unseen lighting.
