## Problem

Generating synchronized multi-view videos from a single real-world monocular video remains largely unsolved. Existing multi-view video diffusion models require expensive training on large-scale 4D datasets, are limited to synthetic or object-level scenes, and most generate from image/text inputs rather than from user-provided videos. No prior method can take an arbitrary video and produce temporally consistent videos from multiple camera viewpoints without a dedicated multi-view generative prior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251409/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular video (49 frames at 480×720) and a text prompt specifying the desired camera movement as input. It produces a set of synchronized videos (up to 12 for static view transport, 6 for dynamic camera control) rendered from different 6-DOF camera trajectories of the same scene. In static view transport mode, each output video shows the scene from a fixed target viewpoint; in dynamic camera control mode, the camera gradually transitions from the original pose to the target pose over the video duration.

## Method

Reangle-A-Video decomposes 4D scene generation into view-specific appearance (a starting image) and view-invariant motion (image-to-video synthesis), addressing each separately. Stage I augments training data by estimating per-frame depth (Depth Anything V2), lifting pixels into 3D point clouds, and reprojecting them onto M target camera poses to produce warped videos with visibility masks. Stage II fine-tunes a pre-trained CogVideoX MM-DiT with LoRA (rank 128, ~2% of parameters, 400 steps) using a masked diffusion loss that excludes invisible regions, enabling the model to learn view-invariant scene motion in a self-supervised few-shot manner. Stage III generates multi-view consistent starting images by warping the first frame to target viewpoints and inpainting occluded regions with FLUX + ControlNet, where cross-view consistency is enforced at inference time via stochastic control guidance that scores S=25 denoising paths using DUSt3R-projected DINO feature similarity against previously inpainted views.

## Results

On static view transport, Reangle-A-Video achieves the best MEt3R (0.0412), FID (53.45), and FVD (2690.9), outperforming Generative Camera Dolly (MEt3R 0.1237, FID 155.15, FVD 5264.7) and Vanilla CogVideoX (MEt3R 0.0539, FID 79.62, FVD 3664.2). On dynamic camera control, it leads with MEt3R 0.0648, FID 74.19, and FVD 3019.7, beating NVS-Solver (MEt3R 0.1090, FID 95.82, FVD 3516.5) and Trajectory Attention (MEt3R 0.0965, FID 109.20, FVD 3624.9). VBench metrics confirm superiority in Subject Consistency, Motion Smoothness, and Aesthetic Quality across both settings, with only minor losses in Background Consistency and Imaging Quality to Vanilla CogVideoX in the static setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Generative Camera Dolly [69] in static view transport, Reangle-A-Video wins decisively on every metric, with FID dropping from 155.15 to 53.45 and FVD from 5264.7 to 2690.9, reflecting that GCD (trained on synthetic Kubric data) struggles with real-world video dynamics. Compared to Vanilla CogVideoX [84]—which uses the same input first frame without fine-tuning—Reangle-A-Video improves MEt3R from 0.0539 to 0.0412 and FVD from 3664.2 to 2690.9, while also boosting Subject Consistency (0.9448→0.9516) and Motion Smoothness (0.9859→0.9907), though it trails slightly on Background Consistency (0.9327 vs 0.9398) and Imaging Quality (0.6414 vs 0.6475). In dynamic camera control, Reangle-A-Video outperforms NVS-Solver [87] on MEt3R (0.0648 vs 0.1090), FID (74.19 vs 95.82), and FVD (3019.7 vs 3516.5), and beats Trajectory Attention [78] on the same three metrics (0.0648 vs 0.0965, 74.19 vs 109.20, 3019.7 vs 3624.9). The only near-tie is Dynamic Degree in the dynamic setting (0.8884 vs 0.8889 for Trajectory Attention), and a small gap in Imaging Quality (0.6271 vs 0.6411 for NVS-Solver). Human evaluation with 36 participants further confirms Reangle-A-Video's superiority in viewpoint accuracy, motion preservation, and camera-movement fidelity across both tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on CogVideoX (a pre-trained MM-DiT image-to-video diffusion model) and FLUX (image diffusion with inpainting ControlNet), but conceptually departs from the dominant paradigm of training dedicated multi-view video diffusion models on large-scale 4D datasets (e.g., CAT4D, SyncMaster, 4Real-Video). Instead, it reframes multi-view video generation as a video-to-video translation problem, decomposing the 4D scene into view-specific appearance (handled by warp-and-inpaint) and view-invariant motion (distilled via lightweight LoRA fine-tuning with point-based warping augmentation). It also extends camera-controllable video generation methods (CameraCtrl, MotionCtrl, Trajectory Attention) by requiring the output to faithfully replicate the dynamics of a user-provided input video rather than generating from text or image prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that synchronized multi-view video generation from a single monocular video is achievable with only ~2% parameter fine-tuning (~1 hour on a single A100), avoiding the need for expensive multi-view generative priors or large 4D training datasets. However, the requirement for scene-specific model tuning limits its practical deployment, and the warping stage remains vulnerable to depth estimation errors. A key open question is whether the warped-video augmentation strategy can be scaled to train a general-purpose 4D foundation model without per-scene adaptation, which the authors identify as a promising future direction.
