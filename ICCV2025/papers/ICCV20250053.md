## Problem

Fine-tuning vision foundation models for adverse scene segmentation (fog, rain, snow, night) is challenging because adverse samples are scarce and the appearance gap between clear and adverse scenes causes severe distribution shift in VFM features. While domain generalization methods exist, none specifically tailor a VFM adapter to bridge the clear-to-adverse gap using only clear source data, and prior frequency-based approaches (FFT, wavelet) lack the energy compaction needed to cleanly separate weather-invariant scene content from weather-variant degradation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250053/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 512×512 RGB driving images and produces per-pixel segmentation masks over 19 CityScapes semantic categories. The frozen DINOv2 ViT-L backbone is never updated; instead, the AdaDCP adapter is inserted between every pair of consecutive frozen VFM layers, operating on the patch-token features (n×c) at each layer. Training is supervised solely on the 2,975 clear CityScapes images, and the model is evaluated zero-shot on eight unseen adverse datasets without any target-domain data.

## Method

AdaDCP applies a 2-D discrete cosine transform to each frozen VFM layer's feature map, decomposing it into four frequency components: cA (low-frequency, weather-invariant) and cH, cV, cD (higher-frequency, weather-variant). A LoRA-style side adapter (WIP) fine-tunes cA to preserve scene content, while an identical adapter (WVP) adapts the three weather-variant components. The key alignment step (WPA) performs cross-attention where the weather-invariant cA feature serves as the query and each weather-variant component serves as key/value, implicitly forcing the high-frequency components to retain content-relevant details (edges, small objects) while suppressing weather-specific noise. The four adapted components are then projected back to the spatial feature space and fed into the next frozen VFM layer, with a Mask2Former decoder producing the final segmentation.

## Results

AdaDCP achieves state-of-the-art mIoU on 7 out of 8 unseen adverse scene segmentation benchmarks, with headline results of 83.5% on ACDC-Fog, 85.0% on ACDC-Night, 87.4% on ACDC-Rain, and 88.4% on ACDC-Snow. It outperforms the closest VFM-based competitor REIN by up to 15.2% mIoU on Dark Zurich (79.5 vs. 64.3) and 9.2% on ACDC-Rain (87.4 vs. 78.2). The only dataset where AdaDCP trails is Nighttime Driving (59.6 vs. 60.3 for NightAdapter), a narrow 0.7% gap attributed to coarsely-annotated ground truth. On the per-category evaluation across all 19 CityScapes classes on ACDC, AdaDCP achieves 87.8% mIoU, surpassing REIN by 10.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaDCP outperforms all 36 compared baselines on 7 of 8 benchmarks. Against the strongest VFM-based method REIN [70], it gains +7.1% (fog), +14.4% (night), +9.2% (rain), and +8.9% (snow) mIoU on ACDC, and +15.2% on Dark Zurich. Among unified UDA methods, it surpasses CoDA [24] by 11.7% on ACDC-Fog and 12.1% on ACDC-Rain, and MIC [30] by 16.5% on ACDC-Fog. The sole exception is Nighttime Driving, where the night-specialized NightAdapter [7] edges out AdaDCP by 0.7% (60.3 vs. 59.6), which the authors attribute to coarsely-annotated ground truth in that dataset. In the PEFT comparison (Table 3), AdaDCP also dominates frequency-based methods like SET [75] (+3.4% fog, +27.7% night) and FADA [3] (+3.3% fog, +27.6% night), confirming that DCT-based decomposition is more effective than FFT or wavelet alternatives for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaDCP builds directly on REIN [70], which introduced LoRA-based side adapters for fine-tuning frozen VFMs in domain generalized semantic segmentation. It departs from prior frequency-based domain generalization methods that rely on FFT (phase/amplitude decomposition) or wavelet transforms by arguing that DCT's superior energy compaction yields a cleaner separation of weather-invariant (cA) from weather-variant (cH, cV, cD) components. The paper explicitly positions itself as the first to tailor a VFM adapter specifically for the clear-to-adverse domain generalization setting, where the source domain is exclusively clear-weather scenes and all target domains are unseen adverse conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: adverse scene annotations are expensive (foggy scenes take ~2× longer to annotate) and datasets like Nighttime Driving have only 50 images, making direct training infeasible. The DCT-based decomposition provides a principled, interpretable mechanism for separating scene content from weather artifacts, and the WPA cross-attention module offers a simple yet effective way to preserve high-frequency content details while suppressing weather-specific patterns. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that the method assumes weather-variant components differ significantly across domains; when source and target share similar weather characteristics (e.g., both clear), the alignment step can discard useful information and degrade performance. Open questions include whether the four-component DCT decomposition generalizes to other domain shift types beyond weather, and whether the approach extends to video or multi-modal settings.
