## Problem

Learning semantically and functionally meaningful 3D motion priors from real-world videos at scale remains largely unexplored. Existing approaches either reduce motion to 2D video generation, rely on synthetic data with physically meaningless motion, or handle only very short 3D trajectories in constrained, small-scale robotics setups. No prior work learns dense, camera-disentangled 3D trajectory generation from large-scale real videos.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single first-frame RGB image, an instance segmentation map (encoded as random RGB colors per patch), an XYZ depth map from a monocular depth model, and optionally a text prompt or VLM-generated motion DSL. It produces a MoMap of shape H×W×T×3 (T≈50 frames at 3 FPS), where each pixel's three channels encode the XYZ 3D position in the fixed reference camera frame at each future timestep. This MoMap can be directly rendered via 3D Gaussian Splatting into partial 2D RGB and semantic video frames, which a second lightweight diffusion model then completes.

## Method

The key insight is representing 3D scene motion as pixel-aligned 'Motion Maps' (MoMaps) — images where each pixel stores the XYZ trajectory of the corresponding 3D point over time, anchored to a fixed reference camera frame to disentangle camera motion from object motion. Because MoMaps are image-like and real-world motion is smooth and low-rank, they are compressed via a modified Stable Diffusion VAE from H×W×50×3 into a compact H/8×W/8×32 latent. A finetuned Stable Diffusion U-Net (initialized from pre-trained SD weights) then generates this latent conditioned on the first-frame RGB, segmentation, and XYZ maps, producing 50 frames of 3D motion in a single diffusion sampling step. The training data is a 50K+ video MoMap database built from HOI4D and BRIDGE using a pipeline combining DepthCrafter, SpaTracker, MoSca geometric optimization, and DEVA segmentation.

## Results

On the BRIDGE robotics dataset, MoMap Diffusion achieves the best performance on 7 out of 8 metrics, including ATE-DTW (0.0689 vs. 0.0747 for GFlow-Original), D_sig (0.0463 vs. 0.0536), local distance difference (0.0058 vs. 0.0124), and quantized direction accuracy at ΔT=1 (0.7212 vs. 0.6758). On the more challenging HOI4D egocentric dataset, the advantage is dramatic: ATE-DTW drops from 1.6119 (GFlow-Original) to 0.1112, and D_sig from 1.3888 to 0.0886, while fg_mask_iou reaches 0.4492 versus 0.0667 for GFlow-Original. The only metric where MoMap Diffusion is not best on BRIDGE is fg_mask_iou, where the MoMap-VAE variant slightly edges ahead (0.8271 vs. 0.8128).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three variants of GeneralFlow (GFlow-Original, GFlow-Rollout, GFlow-Extend) and a MoMap-VAE ablation. On BRIDGE, MoMap Diffusion outperforms all GeneralFlow variants on every metric except fg_mask_iou, where MoMap-VAE is marginally better (0.8271 vs. 0.8128); the largest gains are in local_dist_diff (0.0058 vs. 0.0124 for GFlow-Original, a 53% reduction) and quantize_acc_4 (0.6554 vs. 0.5890). On HOI4D, the gap widens substantially: GFlow-Original's ATE-DTW explodes to 1.6119 (vs. 0.1112 for Ours) and D_sig to 1.3888 (vs. 0.0886), indicating that the point-cloud-based GeneralFlow architecture fails to generalize to egocentric, dynamic-camera scenes. GFlow-Rollout performs worst overall due to compounding errors from iterative 4-frame rollouts. The authors note that while GeneralFlow produces lower per-point reconstruction noise, it exhibits inconsistent noise across adjacent points of the same object, causing visible 'object breakdowns,' whereas MoMap Diffusion generates more semantically coherent aggregate motion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Stable Diffusion architecture (Rombach et al., 2022) for a fundamentally different output space—3D trajectory maps rather than RGB images—following the precedent set by Marigold for depth estimation. It departs from prior 3D motion generation work (GeneralFlow, Track2Act, TAPIR) that relies on point-cloud or set-based architectures over sparse trajectories, instead encoding dense per-pixel 3D motion as pixel-aligned 'trajectory images' (MoMaps) that fit naturally into 2D diffusion pipelines. The data pipeline builds on MoSca (Lei et al., 2024) for 4D reconstruction and integrates DepthCrafter, SpaTracker, and DEVA to construct a large-scale real-video MoMap database, moving beyond the synthetic-data or short-trajectory regimes of prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to demonstrate dense, long-term (T=50), camera-disentangled 3D motion generation from real-world videos at scale, opening a new axis for video synthesis that explicitly embeds 3D geometric consistency rather than learning motion implicitly in pixel space. The MoMap representation is a clean, practical abstraction that inherits the full ecosystem of 2D diffusion techniques (control, conditioning, VLM integration), making it a potentially reusable building block. Key open questions include multi-view MoMap joint generation for complete scene coverage, scaling beyond domain-specific datasets (robotics, egocentric hand-object), and whether the VLM-DSL control mechanism can generalize to open-world motion prompts without ground-truth exemplars.
