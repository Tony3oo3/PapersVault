## Problem

Sign language representation learning is hampered by the scarcity of labeled datasets (typically under 2000 unique signs) and the complex spatio-temporal nature of signs. Existing approaches either rely on general pretrained vision models that lack sign-specific features or require complex multimodal, multi-branch architectures combining RGB, depth, and skeleton inputs. There is no scalable, self-supervised framework that learns robust sign representations from unlabeled video without depending on skeletal keypoints at inference.

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB video clips as input (16 consecutive frames for pretraining, extended to 64 frames for recognition) and produces a single sign representation vector z^avg of dimensionality D. During pretraining, the encoder's output is decoded to predict per-frame sign priors (3D hand/body keypoints, joint angles, distance matrices, and hand activity flags); during downstream tasks, only the encoder is retained and the decoder is discarded, so no keypoint extraction is needed at inference. For retrieval, a sliding window over longer videos yields segment representations that are activity-weighted and averaged into a single query vector for cosine-similarity matching against a dictionary.

## Method

SignRep adapts the Hiera-B video transformer by replacing the standard MAE pixel-reconstruction decoder with a lightweight sign decoder that predicts six categories of sign priors (hand keypoints, body keypoints, joint angles, fingertip distances, hand-interaction distances, and signer activity) from a temporally upsampled, pooled encoder representation. Pretraining on YouTube-SL25 uses masked autoencoding where a subset of spatiotemporal patches is masked and the model must reconstruct the corresponding sign priors via smooth L1 loss, rather than reconstructing pixels. Two additional regularization objectives are added: a variance-covariance loss (inspired by VICReg/Barlow Twins) that spreads representations across the feature space and decorrelates dimensions, and an adversarial style-agnostic loss where a discriminator trained on gram-matrix style features is fooled by the encoder, pushing representations to discard background and person-specific appearance. A class probability distribution loss computed from inter-class cosine similarities in the training set is optionally added as a KL-divergence regularizer during recognition fine-tuning to capture visual proximity between sign classes.

## Results

On WLASL2000, SignRep achieves 61.05% instance top-1 and 58.89% class top-1 accuracy, matching the best multi-modal ensemble (NLA-SLR 3ξ: 61.26%) while using only a single RGB modality. On NMFs-CSL, SignRep reaches 84.1% top-1 and 98.8% top-5 accuracy, surpassing all prior methods including multi-modal ensembles. On ASL-Citizen, SignRep improves top-1 recall from 63.10% (I3D) to 81.37% and DCG from 79.13 to 90.84. As a frozen feature extractor for translation, SignRep outperforms DinoV2+LoRA on CSL-Daily (BLEU-4: 16.33 vs 12.96) while reducing GPU memory by 80%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SignRep decisively outperforms all skeleton-based methods (ST-GCN, SignBERT, BEST, SignBERT+) by 12–27 points in instance top-1 on WLASL2000, confirming that RGB self-supervised pretraining captures sign features that keypoints alone cannot. Against the strongest multi-modal ensembles (NLA-SLR 3ξ, StepNet R+F), SignRep is within 0.2–0.3 points on WLASL2000 instance top-1 while using a single modality and no multi-crop inference, and it clearly wins on NMFs-CSL (84.1% vs 83.7% top-1). Among single-modality RGB baselines, SignRep surpasses StepNet by 4.2 points (61.05% vs 56.89%) and I3D(BSL1K) by 14.2 points on WLASL2000. For sign translation, SignRep as a frozen extractor beats DinoV2+LoRA on CSL-Daily (BLEU-4: 16.33 vs 12.96) and ties on Phoenix14T (20.38 vs 19.42), while also outperforming supervised I3D features on How2Sign (BLEU: 8.66 vs 8.03) despite being trained entirely self-supervised.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Hiera hierarchical vision transformer (Ryali et al., ICML 2023) by replacing the standard MAE pixel-reconstruction decoder with a lightweight sign-prior decoder that regresses hand keypoints, joint angles, and distance matrices. It departs from the skeleton-based self-supervised line (SignBERT, BEST, SignBERT+) by using RGB video as the sole input modality while still leveraging skeletal information as training-time supervision signals, and from multimodal ensemble approaches (SAM, NLA-SLR, StepNet) by showing that a single-branch RGB model with sign-specific pretraining can match their performance. The adversarial style-agnostic loss draws on gram-matrix style representations from image style transfer, and the variance-covariance regularization follows the VICReg formulation, both repurposed for sign representation learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single RGB model without architectural modifications matches or exceeds complex multimodal ensembles on three recognition benchmarks, and replacing learnable DinoV2 backbones with frozen SignRep features cuts GPU memory by 80% for sign translation, making large-scale training accessible on standard hardware. The key open question is whether the sign-prior inductive biases generalize across sign languages and signer populations, since pretraining was done on YouTube-SL25 (primarily ASL) and evaluation spans ASL and CSL. The paper does not explore whether the adversarial style loss or the class probability distribution loss transfer to other domains, and the modest SOTA margins (e.g., 61.05 vs. 61.26 on WLASL instance top-1) leave room for whether the gains are robust across seeds and evaluation splits.
