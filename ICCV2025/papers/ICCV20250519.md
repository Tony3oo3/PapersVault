## Problem

State-of-the-art stereo matching relies on 3D convolutions for cost volume aggregation, which are computationally prohibitive on mobile devices. Replacing them with 2D convolutions causes edge blurring, detail loss, and mismatches in textureless regions, while workarounds like deformable convolutions and iterative warping remain too complex for mobile deployment. The paper asks how to design a stereo network that uses only mobile-friendly 2D operations while preserving sharp edges and accurate matching in both detailed and textureless regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250519/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes a pair of left and right RGB images and produces a full-resolution disparity map. The correlation volume is constructed at 1/4 image resolution with a disparity range of 0 to D_max/4−1 (D_max=192), yielding an initial disparity map at H/4 × W/4 that is upsampled to full resolution via superpixel-weighted local averaging. Mobile latency is measured at 512×512 input (45 ms on Snapdragon 8 Gen 3), while benchmark evaluations use 960×540 (Scene Flow) and 1242×375 (KITTI) resolutions.

## Method

BANet's core mechanism is bilateral aggregation: a scale-aware spatial attention module fuses multi-scale left-image features (1/4, 1/8, 1/16) through concatenation and a sigmoid-activated convolution to produce a spatial attention map A that highlights high-frequency detail regions. The full correlation volume is split via Hadamard product into a detailed volume (A ⊙ C_cor) and a smooth volume ((1−A) ⊙ C_cor), each aggregated by a separate branch of inverted residual blocks (2D convolutions with expansion factor 4 at 1/4, 1/8, and 1/16 resolutions) with non-shared weights. The two aggregated volumes are recombined using the same attention map, and softmax over the disparity axis yields the disparity estimate. The backbone is a pretrained MobileNetV2, and the entire pipeline uses only 2D convolutions in the mobile-friendly BANet-2D variant; a 3D-convolution extension (BANet-3D) is also provided for higher accuracy on GPUs.

## Results

On the Scene Flow test set, BANet-2D achieves 0.57 px EPE with only 39G MACs, outperforming all other lightweight methods (next best: LightStereo-L at 0.59 px with 92G MACs). On KITTI 2015, BANet-2D achieves 1.83 D1-all, surpassing MobileStereoNet-2D (2.83) by 35.3%, while BANet-3D achieves 1.77 D1-all, outperforming Fast-ACVNet+ (2.01) by 11.9%. On KITTI 2012, BANet-2D records 1.38 3-noc and 1.01 4-noc, the best among all compared methods. On the Qualcomm Snapdragon 8 Gen 3, BANet-2D runs in 45 ms for 512×512 input, less than one-third of MobileStereoNet-2D's 157 ms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BANet-2D decisively outperforms MobileStereoNet-2D, the closest mobile-friendly baseline, by 35.3% on D1-all (1.83 vs 2.83) on KITTI 2015 and by 3.4× in latency (45 ms vs 157 ms on Snapdragon 8 Gen 3). Against GPU-based real-time methods, BANet-3D surpasses Fast-ACVNet+ by 11.9% on D1-all (1.77 vs 2.01) and HITNet by 10.1% (1.77 vs 1.98), while BANet-2D beats CoEx (1.83 vs 2.13), BGNet+ (1.83 vs 2.19), and AANet+ (1.83 vs 2.03) on D1-all. On Scene Flow, BANet-2D achieves 0.57 px EPE with only 39G MACs, beating LightStereo-L (0.59 px, 92G MACs) and Fast-ACVNet+ (0.59 px, 93G MACs) while using roughly half the compute. BANet-2D also achieves the lowest MACs (36G on KITTI resolution) among all compared methods, confirming its efficiency advantage. The only method that partially matches BANet-3D is Fast-ACVNet+ on KITTI 2012 4-noc (1.06 vs 0.95), where BANet-3D still leads; no baseline matches BANet-2D on any KITTI metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground within the mobile stereo matching niche by proposing a 'divide-and-conquer' cost aggregation strategy: rather than applying a single 2D convolutional branch to the entire cost volume (as in MobileStereoNet-2D) or resorting to non-mobile-friendly operations like deformable convolutions (AANet) or iterative warping (HITNet), it splits the cost volume into high-frequency detailed and low-frequency smooth components via a scale-aware spatial attention map, then aggregates each with a dedicated branch. The backbone and aggregation blocks follow the MobileNetV2 inverted residual design, and the overall pipeline (feature extraction → correlation volume → aggregation → softmax disparity) is standard. The bilateral aggregation concept is presented as a plug-and-play module, demonstrated to improve PSMNet, GwcNet, and Fast-ACVNet+ when integrated, positioning it as a general enhancement rather than a monolithic new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for edge deployment: BANet-2D runs in 45 ms on a Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 (vs. 157 ms for MobileStereoNet-2D) while achieving 35.3% lower D1-all error on KITTI 2015, making high-quality stereo matching viable on smartphones and drones without custom operators. The generalizability of bilateral aggregation (Table 5) suggests it could be adopted as a lightweight add-on to existing stereo pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach extends to higher-resolution inputs, whether the MobileNetV2 backbone is optimal, and whether the bilateral split transfers to related tasks like multi-view stereo or optical flow, which the authors mention as future work.
