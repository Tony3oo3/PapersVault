## Problem

While the affine Grassmannian provides a theoretically exact metric for measuring proximity between lines and planes, no optimizable cost function parameterized by rigid body transformation (R, t) has existed on this manifold. Existing registration methods either reduce features to point-based approximations or parameterize them in Euclidean space (e.g., Plücker coordinates, plane coefficients), where sign ambiguity of vector representations induces inconsistent metrics and suboptimal solutions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired affine primitives in 3D—each represented by an orthonormal basis matrix and a unique displacement vector orthogonal to the subspace—and produces a rigid transformation (R ∈ SO(3), t ∈ R³). A key structural property is that the cost decomposes into rotation-only terms (basis alignment) and rotation-plus-translation terms (embedded displacement alignment), enabling a two-stage solve. In the correspondence-free localization variant, the BnB solver simultaneously searches over both the transformation and the correspondence assignment between a 3D line map and an RGB-D frame's extracted lines.

## Method

The paper derives the SE(n) group action on the affine Grassmannian (Theorem 1), explicitly showing how the orthonormal basis and displacement vector of a subspace transform under (R, t). The central insight (Theorem 2) proves that zero Grassmann distance is equivalent to every basis vector of the smaller subspace being spanned by the larger one, allowing the non-differentiable SVD-based Grassmann distance to be replaced by a projection-based cost: the squared distance between each source basis vector and its projection onto the transformed target subspace, plus an analogous term for the embedded displacement. This cost is agnostic to basis direction (sign ambiguity) and is explicitly differentiable in R and t. For robustness to outliers, the rotation-only terms are solved via inlier-set maximization with a Branch-and-Bound solver using exact bounds derived from the cost, followed by least-squares translation refinement via Levenberg-Marquardt. For plane-to-plane registration, Grassmann duality reduces the two-basis alignment to a single normal-vector alignment, yielding a compact two-term cost.

## Results

On the Replica RGB-D odometry benchmark, the proposed Refine method achieves the lowest translational APE across 7 of 8 sequences (0.04–0.09 m), while BnB achieves the best rotational APE in 5 of 8 sequences (2.25°–7.35°), both substantially outperforming the best baseline LinEq (PlückerNet) which reports 0.16–0.91 m translational and 5.98°–108.59° rotational APE. On the chessboard PnL task, BnB achieves the best rotation error (0.25°) and Refine the best translation error (1.13 mm), compared to RoPnL (0.36°, 1.43 mm) and ASP3L (0.30°, 1.91 mm). Under 80% outlier ratio in synthetic PnL, all baselines fail while the proposed method maintains low error, demonstrating robustness to extreme noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 8 baselines spanning point-based (Olsson, Park), parameter-based (LinEq/PlückerNet, Forstner), and PnL-specific (MinPnL, CvxPnL, ASPnL/ASP3L, RoPnL) methods. On RGB-D odometry, both Refine and BnB outperform all baselines on translational APE by 2–10× (e.g., 0.05 m vs. 0.16 m for LinEq on Off0), and on rotational APE by 2–4× (e.g., 3.91° vs. 8.83° for LinEq on Off0); Olsson and Park fail entirely on Room1. On the chessboard PnL task, BnB achieves the best rotation (0.25° vs. 0.30° for ASP3L and 0.36° for RoPnL) while Refine achieves the best translation (1.13 mm vs. 1.43 mm for RoPnL and 1.91 mm for ASP3L). The closest competitor is RoPnL, which also uses BnB for rotation but relies on point-to-plane linear fitting for translation, resulting in consistently higher translational error. Under 80% outliers in synthetic PnL, all baselines degrade severely while the proposed method maintains low error, and on the Space Station object registration task, Refine reduces LinEq's translation error by over 50% through geodesic-based refinement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the affine Grassmannian framework of Lim et al. [20], which provides the orthonormal matrix representation of affine subspaces, and extends it by deriving the first optimizable cost function parameterized by rigid body transformation (R, t) on this manifold. It departs from two dominant paradigms in line/plane registration: point-based methods (Olsson, Park) that reduce features to endpoints, and parameter-based methods (PlückerNet, Forstner) that minimize Euclidean distance between coordinate vectors, both of which introduce noise sensitivity or sign-ambiguity inconsistencies. The BnB inlier-set maximization solver follows the framework of Campbell et al. [8], while the closest prior work, Lusk and How [24], used Grassmannian distance only for constructing consistency graphs rather than as a differentiable registration objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a principled, ambiguity-free cost function on the affine Grassmannian, this work offers a unified formulation for line-to-line, line-to-plane, and plane-to-plane registration that can replace ad-hoc parameterizations across the field. The correspondence-free localization extension (Sec. 5.4) demonstrates that the BnB solver can search over both transformation and correspondences simultaneously, opening a path toward line/plane-based SLAM frontends that were previously limited to point clouds. Open questions include scalability to large-scale scenes with thousands of features, extension to higher-dimensional affine subspaces, and whether the geodesic cost can be integrated into learned registration pipelines (e.g., as a differentiable loss in PlückerNet-style architectures).
