## Problem

Existing depth completion methods rely on task-specific training and fail catastrophically when transferred to unseen domains, irregular sampling patterns, or varying sparsity levels. Meanwhile, monocular depth estimation has achieved remarkable zero-shot generalization by leveraging foundation-model priors (e.g., Stable Diffusion, DINOv2), yet depth completion has not benefited from this progress. The paper asks why depth completion cannot generalize at least as well as monocular depth and proposes reframing the task as image-conditional depth generation anchored by sparse measurements.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a sparse metric depth map (with valid values at an arbitrary subset of pixels, ranging from ~150 to 1500+ points across evaluation datasets) and produces a dense metric depth map at the original image resolution. The base Marigold model is affine-invariant, outputting depth in [0,1], so the method dynamically learns scale and shift parameters to map predictions into metric units. A final ensembling step over 10 independent inferences yields the output depth map plus an uncertainty map computed as the pixel-wise median absolute deviation.

## Method

Marigold-DC repurposes the pretrained Marigold latent diffusion model (a Stable Diffusion U-Net conditioned on an image latent) for depth completion via test-time optimization, requiring no architectural changes or retraining. At each of 50 DDIM denoising steps, the method computes a 'preview' of the final denoised depth latent using Tweedie's formula, decodes it to pixel space, applies learned scale and shift to obtain a metric prediction, and computes an L1+L2 loss against the sparse depth at valid pixels. Gradients are backpropagated through the decoder, Tweedie formula, and U-Net to simultaneously update the depth latent, the scale parameter (α²·(c_max−c_min)), and the shift parameter (β²·c_min), with the squared parameterization ensuring positivity and preventing saturation. The scale/shift initialization uses min-max of the sparse conditioning, and the depth latent gradient is scaled to match the predicted score gradient norm for stable optimization. This guidance loop runs in tandem with the standard DDIM scheduler, so the image prior from Stable Diffusion acts as a strong regularizer that prevents overfitting to noisy or sparse depth measurements.

## Results

Marigold-DC (with 10-sample ensemble) achieves the best MAE on 5 of 8 benchmark splits: ScanNet (0.017), iBims-1 (0.045), VOID-150 (0.194), VOID-500 (0.158), and NYU-Depth V2 (0.048), and the best RMSE on 6 of 8 splits. On NYU-Depth V2 it improves MAE from 0.077 (VPP4DC, the next best) to 0.048, a 38% relative reduction. On the outdoor KITTI DC benchmark it achieves RMSE 1.465 (best) though VPP4DC edges it on MAE (0.413 vs. 0.434). All results are zero-shot: the base Marigold model was trained only on synthetic Hypersim and Virtual KITTI data and requires no task-specific fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Marigold-DC (with ensemble) achieves the best MAE and RMSE on ScanNet (0.017/0.057), iBims-1 (0.045/0.166), VOID-150 (0.194/0.622), VOID-500 (0.158/0.535), and NYU-Depth V2 (0.048/0.124), outperforming all 8 baselines on these splits. On NYU-Depth V2 the margin is particularly large: 0.048 MAE vs. 0.077 for VPP4DC and 0.110 for Prompt Depth Anything. On KITTI DC, VPP4DC edges out Marigold-DC on MAE (0.413 vs. 0.434) but Marigold-DC wins on RMSE (1.465 vs. 1.609). On DDAD, VPP4DC achieves a substantially lower MAE (1.344 vs. 2.364), though Marigold-DC still wins on RMSE (6.449 vs. 6.781). On VOID-1500 (the densest guidance), VPP4DC leads on both metrics (0.148/0.543 vs. 0.152/0.551). Notably, all baseline methods require task-specific training on indoor or outdoor datasets, whereas Marigold-DC is fully zero-shot with a frozen model trained only on synthetic data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Marigold, a pretrained latent diffusion model for monocular depth estimation, as a frozen prior for depth completion by injecting sparse depth observations via test-time optimization during the DDIM denoising loop. It departs from the dominant depth completion paradigm (spatial propagation networks, multi-modal fusion CNNs, and transformer-based inpainting) that requires task-specific training on real depth data, and instead draws on the diffusion guidance literature (DPS, classifier guidance) to anchor affine-invariant predictions to metric depth at sparse points. The key conceptual departure is reframing depth completion not as inpainting sparse depth guided by an image, but as monocular depth estimation guided by sparse depth, thereby inheriting the rich visual world knowledge of Stable Diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot generalization across six diverse real-world benchmarks (indoor and outdoor, varying sensor types and sparsity levels) without any task-specific training is practically significant for robotics and autonomous driving where sensor configurations are heterogeneous. The main limitation is computational overhead from 50 DDIM steps × 10 ensemble members, which the authors acknowledge as a barrier to real-time deployment. Open questions include reducing inference cost (e.g., via TinyVAE or fewer steps), handling degenerate sparse point distributions where min-max initialization fails, and extending the framework to alternative guidance cues beyond depth.
