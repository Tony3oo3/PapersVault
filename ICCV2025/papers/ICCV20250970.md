## Problem

Existing chain-of-thought reasoning for multimodal LLMs operates at the text level, preventing models from dynamically focusing on spatial regions of an image. The only prior visual CoT method (Visual-CoT) relies on supervised fine-tuning with large-scale human-annotated bounding boxes, making it costly, hard to scale, and limited in generalization to unseen or ambiguous scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250970/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text question as input. During inference, the model first generates bounding box coordinates to identify the most informative region, then processes both the original image and the cropped region (doubling the visual token count) to produce a text answer. The bounding boxes are model-generated rather than annotated, and the model is prompted with a CoT template to guide region identification before answer generation.

## Method

UV-CoT consists of an automatic preference data generation pipeline and a novel Score-DPO (sDPO) loss. In data generation, the target model (LLaVA-1.5-7B) stochastically generates multiple seed bounding boxes, answers the question using each bounded region, and an evaluator MLLM (OmniLMM-12B) scores responses using a cumulative formulation that incorporates the impact on the next response in the chain (weighted by γ). Preference pairs are constructed by ranking these scored responses. The sDPO loss extends standard DPO by incorporating preference scores into the optimization margin via a monotonically increasing function g(·), derived through Gumbel-distributed random variables to capture not just the order but the magnitude of preference differences. An iterative learning scheme regenerates preference data at each round using the current model, mitigating distributional mismatch between training data and the evolving policy.

## Results

On six in-domain benchmarks (DocVQA, TextVQA, InfographicsVQA, Flickr30k, GQA, VSR), UV-CoT with 0% labeled data achieves an average GPT-4o score of 0.473, surpassing all non-CoT baselines (LLaVA-1.5-7B: 0.393, OmniLMM-12B: 0.443) and approaching Visual-CoT-7B (0.482) while using 249K unlabeled pairs vs. 376K labeled pairs. With 10% labeled data, UV-CoT reaches 0.494 average, exceeding Visual-CoT-7B by 1.2%. In zero-shot evaluation on DUDE, SROIE, and Visual7W, UV-CoT achieves 0.286 average (vs. 0.261 for Visual-CoT-7B), and on V*Bench high-resolution reasoning it scores 0.402 (vs. 0.347 for Visual-CoT-7B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UV-CoT (0% label) outperforms all non-CoT baselines by a wide margin: +8.0% over LLaVA-1.5-7B, +4.6% over LLaVA-1.5-13B, +4.5% over MiniCPM-o-8B, and +3.0% over OmniLMM-12B on average. Against the closest competitor Visual-CoT-7B (which uses 376K human-labeled bounding boxes), UV-CoT (0%) trails by 0.9% on average but wins on TextVQA (+1.3%) and VSR (+1.6%); with just 10% labeled data, UV-CoT surpasses Visual-CoT-7B by 1.2% overall, with notable gains on TextVQA (+2.5%), GQA (+2.2%), and VSR (+2.1%). In zero-shot settings, UV-CoT beats Visual-CoT-7B by 2.5% on DUDE/SROIE/Visual7W and by 5.5% on V*Bench, demonstrating stronger generalization. The paper also shows UV-CoT outperforms its own evaluator (OmniLMM-12B) by 5.1% on average, indicating the method goes beyond simple distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to the multimodal setting by introducing Score-DPO (sDPO), which augments the standard binary preference ranking with a continuous score margin derived from an evaluator MLLM. It departs from the closely related Visual-CoT framework, which trains image-level CoT via supervised fine-tuning on human-annotated bounding boxes, by replacing SFT with unsupervised preference optimization over model-generated bounding boxes. The iterative data-generation-and-training loop draws on RLAIF-style self-improvement, while the Gumbel-based derivation of the sDPO loss connects to the offset-DPO line of work. Conceptually, the paper reframes visual CoT from a supervised localization-and-reasoning task into a contrastive ranking problem over candidate regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that UV-CoT eliminates the need for costly bounding-box annotations while matching or exceeding the fully supervised Visual-CoT, suggesting preference optimization is a viable scalable alternative to SFT for teaching MLLMs spatial reasoning. A notable limitation is that bounding-box quality remains weak on document and infographic tasks (DocVQA, InfographicsVQA), indicating that precise localization is still a bottleneck; the paper acknowledges this as future work. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-step visual search (beyond a single crop), whether the evaluator can be replaced by a lighter model without significant degradation, and whether the iterative loop converges to a stable policy at scale. The finding that the 7B student outperforms its 12B evaluator by 5.1% hints that preference optimization extracts signal beyond simple distillation, but this has not been stress-tested across architectures.
