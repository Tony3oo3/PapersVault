## Problem

Existing multimodal instruction-following (IF) training data is scarce, current benchmarks rely on simple atomic instructions with weak visual correlation, and evaluation strategies (primarily LLM-as-a-judge) are imprecise for constraints demanding exact output specifications like word counts or format requirements. This combination of limited data, saturated benchmarks (nearly all models exceed 80%), and imprecise evaluation severely restricts progress in MLLM instruction following.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes diverse RGB images (natural scenes, UI screenshots, diagrams, charts, mathematical expressions) paired with text instructions containing 3–12 constraints drawn from 32 subcategories across 6 major types (text length, mathematical, language/formatting, rhetoric/logic, action, keyword). For SFT, it produces image-instruction-response triples where responses must satisfy ≥80% of constraints; for DPO, it produces preference pairs where rejected samples are generated by removing 33%, 66%, or 100% of constraints (or omitting the image entirely). The MM-IFEval benchmark outputs 400 human-annotated questions split into 300 compose-level (textual constraints on output) and 100 perception-level (visual reasoning with ground truth) problems.

## Method

MM-IFEngine is a three-stage generation pipeline: (1) image filtering using IC9600/RAM semantic richness metrics, (2) task generation where GPT-4o selects from a pool of 16 task descriptions or refines existing QA annotations (filtering out few-shot examples and multiple-choice options via regex), and (3) constraint integration where an LLM generates concrete constraint content from 32 categories and a separate validation LLM checks compatibility between constraints and the task instruction. Training data is constructed by prompting InternVL2.5-78B-MPO for SFT responses (retaining only those meeting an 80% constraint-accuracy threshold) and Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct for DPO rejected samples (generated by removing constraints or omitting the image). Evaluation employs a hybrid strategy combining rule-based verification functions (for objectively checkable constraints like word counts), LLM direct judgment (for soft constraints like keyword inclusion), and LLM comparative judgment (for tone/style constraints, where a control output without the constraint is compared against the constrained output).

## Results

Fine-tuning Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct with MM-IFDPO-23k (DPO) yields average gains of +10.1% across three IF benchmarks: MM-IFEval 52.2 (+10.2), MIA-Bench 88.1 (+7.6), and IFEval 59.7 (+12.3). For LLaVA-NeXT-Llama3-8B, DPO produces even larger gains of +11.6% average (IFEval +18.4). On the MM-IFEval leaderboard, the DPO-fine-tuned Qwen2-VL-7B (52.2) surpasses the 10× larger Qwen2-VL-72B (50.8), though proprietary models GPT-4o (64.6) and Claude-3.5V-Sonnet (61.7) remain ahead. VQA benchmarks (MMMU, MMBench, OCRBench, etc.) show comparable or slightly improved scores post-fine-tuning, confirming no capability degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The DPO-fine-tuned Qwen2-VL-7B (MM-IFEval 52.2) surpasses the 10× larger Qwen2-VL-72B-Instruct (50.8) and all 8B open-source baselines including InternVL2.5-8B (46.2), InternVL2-8B (41.9), and LLaVA-OneVision (34.0). On IFEval, DPO with LLaVA-NeXT-Llama3-8B achieves 69.1 instruction-level accuracy, far exceeding InternVL2.5-8B (62.4) and the original LLaVA-NeXT (50.7). DPO consistently outperforms SFT across all three IF benchmarks for both model families, with the gap most pronounced on IFEval (+12.3 vs +5.2 for Qwen2-VL). However, proprietary models GPT-4o (64.6) and Claude-3.5V-Sonnet (61.7) still lead on MM-IFEval, indicating substantial room for improvement. On VQA benchmarks (Table 2), fine-tuned models maintain or slightly improve scores (e.g., Qwen2-VL-7B + DPO: MMMU 54.0 vs 53.9, OCRBench 86.8 vs 86.7), confirming that IF training does not degrade general visual capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the instruction-following evaluation lineage from text-only LLMs (IFEval, CFBench, InFoBench) to the multimodal setting, departing from prior multimodal IF benchmarks (MIA-Bench, VisIT-Bench) that rely on simple atomic instructions and pure LLM-as-judge evaluation. The MM-IFEngine data synthesis pipeline repurposes the GPT-4-driven synthetic data generation paradigm (as in ShareGPT4V, ALLaVA) but reorients it toward constraint-rich instruction construction with a structured 32-constraint taxonomy. The DPO training component builds directly on the preference optimization framework of Rafailov et al., applying it to multimodal constraint compliance rather than general preference alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: the scarcity of high-quality multimodal IF training data and the saturation of existing benchmarks (nearly all models >80% on MIA-Bench). The hybrid evaluation strategy (rule-based verification + comparative LLM judgment) is a practical improvement over pure GPT-4o scoring for verifiable constraints like word counts and format requirements. Open questions include whether the 80% accuracy threshold for SFT data introduces systematic bias, whether the 400-question benchmark will saturate quickly as models improve, and whether the constraint taxonomy generalizes to truly novel or adversarial constraint combinations not seen during training.
