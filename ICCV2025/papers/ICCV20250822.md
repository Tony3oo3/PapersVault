## Problem

Existing interactive matting methods, particularly SAM-based approaches, freeze the segmentation backbone during training, causing errors in coarse masks to be amplified by downstream refinement modules and resulting in inaccurate edge details. While diffusion models offer strong priors and fine-detail preservation, prior work fine-tunes them with empty text embeddings, discarding their robust text-driven interaction capability that could be repurposed for visual prompt conditioning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250822/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a user-provided visual prompt (one or more points, a bounding box, or a binary mask) as input, and produces a single-channel alpha matte at the same spatial resolution as the input. During training, a mixed prompt strategy randomly assigns point, box, or mask prompts per sample, and a foreground duplication strategy (50% probability) places an unprompted copy of the foreground alongside the prompted one to sharpen prompt sensitivity. Inference is single-step and deterministic, with no iterative denoising, yielding a direct alpha matte prediction in pixel space.

## Method

SDMatte builds on Stable Diffusion v2's VAE and U-Net, adopting a one-step deterministic paradigm that removes noise addition and denoising. The input image and visual prompt are both encoded to latent space via the VAE encoder and concatenated as U-Net input (with duplicated first-layer convolution weights). Three key mechanisms graft interactivity onto the diffusion backbone: (1) a visual prompt-driven cross-attention mechanism that replaces the text embedding with a zero-convolution-projected visual prompt embedding in the U-Net's middle block, inheriting pre-trained text-driven interaction weights; (2) sinusoidal coordinate embeddings of prompt geometry and opacity embeddings of the target object that substitute the time embedding, enhancing spatial and transparency sensitivity; and (3) a masked self-attention mechanism that applies hard binary masks (for box/mask prompts) or soft Gaussian masks (for point prompts) to modulate attention maps, forcing the model to focus on prompt-indicated regions. A lightweight variant, LiteSDMatte, is distilled from the full model using a Tiny AutoEncoder and BK-U-Net to reduce FLOPs from 11,203G to 2,010G.

## Results

SDMatte achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four benchmarks (AIM-500, AM-2K, P3M-500-NP, RefMatte-RW-100) and all three prompt types (point, box, mask). With box prompts, SDMatte attains MSE of 0.0049 on AIM-500, 0.0029 on AM-2K, 0.0020 on P3M-500-NP, and 0.0047 on RefMatte-RW-100, outperforming the strongest baseline SEMat by 31%, 25%, 29%, and 15% respectively. With mask prompts, SDMatte reaches MSE of 0.0007 on P3M-500-NP and 0.0008 on RefMatte-RW-100, representing 77% and 85% relative improvement over MGMatting. The lightweight variant LiteSDMatte (593M params, 366 ms latency) retains most of the performance while reducing FLOPs from 11,203G to 2,010G.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDMatte outperforms all five baselines across every benchmark and prompt type. Against MAM [25] and MatAny [48] (both SAM-based), SDMatte achieves substantially lower MSE (e.g., 0.0049 vs. 0.0116 and 0.0545 on AIM-500 box), confirming that freezing SAM and stacking refinement modules amplifies errors. Against SmartMatting [50] (DINOv2-based), SDMatte improves MSE by 36% on AIM-500, 24% on AM-2K, 46% on P3M, and 73% on RefMatte-RW-100 with box prompts, demonstrating the advantage of diffusion priors over ViT features for fine-grained edge prediction. Against SEMat [42] (SAM2-based, the strongest box-prompt competitor), SDMatte wins on all four benchmarks, with the largest gap on P3M-500-NP (MSE 0.0020 vs. 0.0028) and RefMatte-RW-100 (MSE 0.0047 vs. 0.0055). Against MGMatting [51] (mask-prompt baseline), SDMatte achieves 82% lower MSE on AIM-500 (0.0027 vs. 0.0155) and 97% lower MSE on P3M (0.0007 vs. 0.0100), validating the effectiveness of the visual prompt-driven cross-attention mechanism for mask-guided matting.

## Relation to Prior Work

SDMatte builds directly on Stable Diffusion v2's VAE and U-Net architecture and adopts the one-step deterministic paradigm from GenPercept, which eliminates the iterative denoising process in favor of direct pixel-space supervision. It departs from the dominant SAM-based matting line (MAM, MatAny, SEMat) that freezes SAM and stacks refinement modules, arguing that frozen-backbone errors propagate and amplify. The key conceptual departure from prior diffusion-for-perception work (Marigold, DAS, DiffDIS) is the explicit repurposing of the text-driven cross-attention pathway for visual prompt conditioning, rather than discarding it with empty text embeddings as most prior methods do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that diffusion priors, when their interactive text-conditioning pathway is preserved and redirected to visual prompts, yield substantially better edge-detail matting than SAM-based or DINOv2-based alternatives across diverse benchmarks. However, the 1014 ms inference latency and 957 M parameter count (vs. 190 ms / 27 M for SmartMatting) represent a significant practical barrier, and the LiteSDMatte distillation variant, while 3× faster, still lags the full model. Open questions include whether the visual-prompt cross-attention transfer generalizes to other diffusion backbones (e.g., SDXL, DiT-based models) and whether the one-step deterministic constraint can be relaxed to recover some of the generative flexibility that might help with highly ambiguous transparency.
