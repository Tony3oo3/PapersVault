## Problem

Existing visual localization methods for textureless 3D models (e.g., MeshLoc) rely on pre-trained networks trained on real images, which perform poorly when applied to synthetic rendered images from models with low geometric detail. The core gap is the absence of a training scheme that bridges the domain gap between real query images and the synthetic representations of textureless 3D models, particularly when those models lack rich surface geometry.

## Input / Output

The method takes a real RGB query image (resized to 640 px on the longer side), a textureless 3D model in any representation (CAD, mesh, LiDAR point cloud, SfM/MVS depth maps), and known camera intrinsics. The 3D model is converted to a point cloud, surface normals are estimated via k-NN (k=64 at inference), and rendered normal images are produced as a 3-channel (n_x, n_y, n_z) database where normals are rotated into the camera frame and sign-flipped to face the camera. The output is a 6-DoF camera pose (position and orientation) expressed in the 3D model's world coordinate frame, obtained via PnP with LO-RANSAC over aggregated 2D-3D correspondences.

## Method

NormalLoc introduces rendered normal images as a unified synthetic database representation that can be generated from virtually any 3D model type, enabling a single trained network to handle both real and synthetic inputs. The network uses a dual-branch FPN backbone (no weight sharing) feeding into self-attention layers for 2048-d global descriptors and a LoFTR module (with rotary embeddings replacing absolute positional encoding) for detector-free 2D-2D matching. Training on MegaDepth pairs each real image with a rendered normal derived from its dense depth map (k-NN normals at k∈{8,64,512}), optimizing a combined loss of triplet loss for global descriptors plus LoFTR's confidence and correspondence losses. The offline step renders normals from the target 3D model and precomputes global descriptors; the online step retrieves top-K rendered normals by L2 distance, matches the query to each, lifts 2D-2D matches to 2D-3D via stored depth maps, and solves for pose with PnP+LO-RANSAC.

## Results

On the Aachen Day-Night v1.1 textureless scenario, NormalLoc achieves success ratios of 47.6/65.4/78.0 (AC15) and 36.1/57.1/76.4 (AC13-C, least geometric detail) under (0.25 m, 2°)/(0.5 m, 5°)/(5 m, 10°) thresholds, substantially outperforming the pre-trained baselines (Baseline-AO: 3.7/10.5/24.6; Baseline-tricolor: 8.4/20.9/37.2 on AC15). On CADLoc, NormalLoc dramatically improves on low-detail models: Notre Dame (F) jumps from 5.3% (MeshLoc-LoFTR) to 79.9% at the 10% DCRE threshold. On the InLoc indoor dataset, NormalLoc achieves 6.1/12.6/16.7 at 10° pose error, well below HLoc+LoFTR (47.5/72.2/84.8), highlighting a key limitation in close-range indoor scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NormalLoc decisively outperforms MeshLoc (Panek et al., ECCV 2022) in the textureless scenario on Aachen Day-Night v1.1: on AC13-C (least geometric detail), NormalLoc reaches 36.1/57.1/76.4 versus MeshLoc-SuperGlue's 7.3/23.0/53.9 in the hybrid scenario, a gap of roughly 29 points at the strictest threshold. On CADLoc, the advantage is even more dramatic for low-detail models—Notre Dame (F) improves from 5.3% to 79.9% (10% DCRE threshold) over MeshLoc-LoFTR. Against the pre-trained baselines (AP-GeM + LoFTR applied directly to rendered normals), NormalLoc gains 39–36 points at the (5 m, 10°) threshold on AC15 and AC13-C respectively. However, on the InLoc indoor dataset, NormalLoc (6.1/12.6/16.7) is far outperformed by both HLoc+LoFTR (Sarlin et al., CVPR 2019; 47.5/72.2/84.8) and KAPTURE+R2D2 (Humenberger et al., 2020; 41.4/60.1/73.7), revealing that the method struggles with close-range, geometrically simple indoor scenes where normal maps lack discriminative power. LoFTR (Sun et al., CVPR 2021) serves as the architectural backbone for NormalLoc's matching module rather than a direct competitor, though pre-trained LoFTR is used as a baseline matcher in the textureless setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

NormalLoc extends the MeshLoc pipeline (offline render → online retrieve → match → PnP) by replacing rendered color/depth images with rendered surface normals as the database representation, and by training the LoFTR-based matching network jointly with a global descriptor on a cross-domain setup (real images paired with rendered normals derived from MegaDepth depth maps). This departs from prior textureless localization work that directly applies pre-trained real-image networks to synthetic renders, which the authors show degrades sharply on low-geometric-detail models. The key conceptual shift is treating the normal map as a learnable intermediate representation that bridges the real-image training domain and the textureless 3D model inference domain, rather than relying on the pre-trained network to generalize across that gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical gap: textureless CAD models and standalone LiDAR point clouds are far more readily available than textured meshes or colored point clouds, yet prior methods lose accuracy when geometric detail is limited. The versatility of normals (computable from CAD, meshes, point clouds, 3DGS, NeRF, and SfM depth) positions NormalLoc as a unified framework across 3D representations. However, the significant performance drop on indoor scenes (InLoc ~15% success vs. 75–80% outdoor) and the persistent gap versus textured baselines on Aachen Day-Night highlight that the approach is not yet a general replacement for color-based localization. Open questions include whether wider FoV queries, line-based correspondences, or multi-scale normal representations could close the indoor gap, and whether the method extends naturally to dynamic or partially observed scenes.
