## Problem

Uncalibrated photometric stereo (UPS) cannot be directly transferred to event cameras because events encode brightness ratios rather than absolute intensities (preventing dark-frame ambient subtraction), occur asynchronously (preventing the pixel correspondences needed by matrix-factorization UPS), and are inherently albedo-invariant (invalidating albedo-prior ambiguity resolution). No prior method solves UPS from event data, leaving a gap between the high-bandwidth demands of frame-based UPS and the bandwidth-efficient, high-temporal-resolution sensing paradigm of neuromorphic cameras.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sparse, asynchronous event stream (pixel, timestamp, polarity) from a Prophesee EVK4 HD camera (IMX636 sensor, ~580 µs refractory period) together with the known local 3D positions of LEDs in a portable fixture (dual-ring or trefoil-curve configuration) whose global 6-DoF pose is unknown. It outputs dense per-pixel unit surface normals and a continuous B-spline lighting trajectory over the capture cycle, requiring only ~5% of the data bandwidth of equivalent frame-based UPS (roughly two frames' worth of data).

## Method

Each event is recast as a linear constraint z_x^(k)ᵀ n_x + r_x(1−θ̃) = 0 on an augmented normal vector [n_x, r_x] ∈ ℝ⁴, where r_x = b_x/a_x absorbs ambient illumination, making the formulation albedo-invariant and eliminating the need for a dark frame. The time-varying lighting s(t) is parameterized as a cubic B-spline with M control points, so each asynchronous event's null-space vector becomes a linear combination of only l consecutive control points via time-dependent coefficients C̃_{j,l}(t_x^(k)), bridging per-pixel events to a globally synchronized lighting estimate. Ambiguity is resolved by a lighting fixture with ≥7 known local LED positions in general position: the GBR transformation must map these positions to the estimated light vectors, reducing the residual ambiguity to the classic convex/concave reflection without albedo or reflectance priors. The joint loss (data fidelity + integrability) is minimized by alternating per-pixel SVD-based normal updates (eigenvector of Z_x Z_xᵀ) with a global linear least-squares update of the lighting control points.

## Results

On the DiLiGenT-Ev dataset, EventUPS achieves an average MAE of 14.14° using only 1.6% of the data rate of the best deep-learning baseline (CW20 with 56 frames, 9.22° MAE at 100% data rate). This outperforms the top non-learning frame-based method PF14 (17.16° MAE at 33% data rate) by over 3° while consuming less than 5% of its bandwidth. On real captured data with sensor noise and non-ideal lighting, EventUPS achieves an average MAE of 16.58° across five objects, with the estimated normal map remaining invariant to spatially-varying albedo.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EventUPS (14.14° MAE) outperforms the best non-learning frame-based method PF14 (17.16°) by 3.02° on average, with particularly large gains on complex objects like HARVEST (23.32° vs. 30.65°) and READER (16.71° vs. 20.82°), while using only 1.6% of PF14's data rate (vs. 33%). Against the deep-learning baseline CW20, EventUPS is less accurate in absolute terms (14.14° vs. 9.22° for 56 frames) but achieves this at 1.6% of the data rate, making it far more bandwidth-efficient. Most notably, EventUPS matches the fully calibrated event-based method EventPS (14.17°) in average MAE despite requiring no lighting calibration, and even surpasses it on HARVEST (23.32° vs. 29.80°) and BUDDHA (12.21° vs. 13.22°). The authors attribute this to the explicit residual term that accounts for ambient illumination and non-ideal effects, which the calibrated EventPS does not model.

## Relation to Prior Work

EventUPS extends the event-based photometric stereo line initiated by EventPS [45], which required calibrated lighting and darkroom conditions, by removing both requirements through an augmented null-space formulation that absorbs ambient illumination into a per-pixel residual term. It also draws on the classical UPS ambiguity-resolution framework (GBR ambiguity from Belhumeur et al. [2], Yuille et al. [46]) but replaces albedo-based priors—unavailable in the event domain—with geometric constraints from a known lighting fixture topology. The B-spline parameterization of time-varying lighting is a novel bridge that connects asynchronous per-pixel events to a globally synchronized illumination estimate, addressing a correspondence problem that has no direct analogue in frame-based UPS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to make uncalibrated photometric stereo practical with event cameras, opening a path toward low-bandwidth, calibration-free 3D scanning in uncontrolled environments (robotics, industrial inspection, motion capture) where maintaining calibrated lighting and negligible ambient light is infeasible. The 5% data bandwidth relative to frame-based UPS is a substantial practical advantage for resource-constrained platforms. Key limitations include the Lambertian surface assumption, orthographic projection, and the need for a specific (though portable) lighting fixture with known relative geometry; extending to non-Lambertian materials, perspective projection, and fully dynamic scenes remains open.
