## Problem

Existing multimodal industrial anomaly detection methods fuse unimodal anomaly scores (computed as isotropic Euclidean distances to nearest memory prototypes) using simplistic strategies like addition, max selection, or OCSVM. These unimodal scores suffer from insufficient discriminative capacity because they ignore local directional structure in feature space, leading to elevated scores even in normal regions and compromising fusion quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250524/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of aligned 3D point cloud and RGB image per sample. Features are extracted by frozen DINO (768-dim) for images and Point-MAE (1152-dim) for point clouds, yielding a 1920-dim joint feature space. Output is a pixel-wise anomaly score map (bilinearly upsampled and smoothed with a size-4 Gaussian kernel to match input resolution) and a sample-level score obtained by taking the maximum across all spatial locations.

## Method

G²SF reinterprets memory bank-based anomaly scores as isotropic Euclidean distances in local feature spaces and progressively evolves them into a learned anisotropic metric. A Local Scale Prediction Network (LSPN), a simple MLP with two parallel branches encoding prototypes and directions, predicts direction-aware scaling factors w_{i,j}^m ∈ [e^{-1}, e^{1}] via an exp(tanh(·)) activation, which are combined with original Euclidean distances and trainable global modality weights σ^m to form the fused metric l_{i,j} = Σ_m w_{i,j}^m · s_{i,j}^m · σ^m. Training uses synthetic anomaly injection (CutPaste-style) and a composite loss: discrimination losses (separation + margin) ensure normal/anomaly separation, while geometry-preserved losses (proximal/distal consistency, asymmetric scaling, and crossmodal alignment via index permutation) enforce metric regularity in the high-dimensional space. At inference, the final score s_i is the minimum over k+1 nearest local spaces, reformulating anomaly scoring as distance to the normal data manifold through the anisotropic metric.

## Results

On MVTec-3D AD, G²SF achieves the best P-AUROC (0.997), AUPRO@30% (0.979), and AUPRO@1% (0.468), while tying LSFA on I-AUROC (0.971). On Eyecandies, G²SF leads all four metrics: I-AUROC 0.902, P-AUROC 0.982, AUPRO@30% 0.898, and AUPRO@1% 0.357. The method processes 6.04 samples per second, comparable to other feature-based approaches. The AUPRO@1% metric, which emphasizes low false-positive rates critical for industrial deployment, shows the largest margins over prior work.

## Baselines & Comparisons

G²SF ties LSFA on I-AUROC (0.971) but surpasses it on P-AUROC (0.997 vs. 0.993) and AUPRO@30% (0.979 vs. 0.968) on MVTec-3D AD. Against Shape-guided, the closest competitor on segmentation, G²SF gains +2.4% I-AUROC (0.971 vs. 0.947) and +1.2% AUPRO@1% (0.468 vs. 0.456), confirming that learning an anisotropic metric outperforms SDF-guided memory banks with max-score fusion. CFM, the strongest crossmodal feature-mapping baseline, is outperformed on all four MVTec-3D AD metrics, with the largest gap on AUPRO@1% (+1.3%). On Eyecandies, G²SF leads CFM by +2.2% AUPRO@1% (0.357 vs. 0.335) and +1.5% I-AUROC (0.902 vs. 0.881). The per-category results (Table 1) show G²SF is particularly strong on Foam (I-AUROC 0.991) and Peach (0.994), where texture-rich or geometry-subtle anomalies challenge unimodal methods, while it trails LSFA slightly on Cable Gland (0.923 vs. 0.939) and Rope (0.966 vs. 0.989).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the PatchCore memory bank paradigm for unimodal anomaly detection, reinterpreting the standard nearest-prototype Euclidean distance as an isotropic local metric that ignores directional structure in feature space. It departs from existing multimodal fusion strategies (score addition in BTF, max selection in Shape-guided, OCSVM in M3DM, feature adaptation in LSFA) by proposing to learn a unified anisotropic local distance metric via a Local Scale Prediction Network (LSPN) that predicts direction-aware scaling factors. The geometric encoding operator and the progressive evolution from Euclidean to anisotropic metric represent a conceptual reframing of how unimodal scores should be combined, rather than a new fusion architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical pain point in industrial inspection where unimodal anomaly scores exhibit unreliable false positives in normal regions, and the anisotropic metric learning approach offers a principled way to suppress these while amplifying true anomalies. The notable AUPRO@1% gains (+1.2% over Shape-guided on MVTec-3D AD, +2.2% over CFM on Eyecandies) are particularly relevant for deployment scenarios demanding low false-positive rates. Limitations include reliance on synthetic anomaly injection (CutPaste-style) for training supervision, evaluation on only two datasets, and the LSPN being a simple MLP whose capacity may constrain performance on more complex defect types. Open questions include whether the geometric priors (proximal consistency, distal separation) generalize to more than two modalities or to domains where feature spaces lack clear local manifold structure.
