## Problem

Existing evaluations of adversarial patch defenses on object detectors suffer from inconsistent parameters, attack methods, and patch placement strategies, making fair comparisons impossible. Moreover, prior work relies on patch detection accuracy as the primary metric, which the authors show is poorly correlated with actual defense effectiveness, and existing patch datasets (Apricot: 1,011 images; GAP: 9,266 images) are too small and lack categorization by detector type to rigorously assess robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251707/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes RGB images at a fixed 416×416 resolution (padded or resized) with a single universal adversarial patch applied via geometric transformations (rotation, scaling). The 11 object detectors (YOLOv2–v7, SSD, CenterNet, RetinaNet, MRCNN, FRCNN, DDETR) produce detection boxes scored by person AP@0.5, while the 11 defenses additionally output binary patch masks evaluated by SmIoU and NmIoU. The APDE dataset comprises 94,000 images (56,400 train / 37,600 test) spanning 94 patch types generated by 13 attack methods across 11 detectors on INRIA-Person and MS COCO.

## Method

The paper constructs the first unified patch defense benchmark by standardizing evaluation around pedestrian detection with 2 attack goals (hiding/appearing), 13 patch attacks, 11 detectors, and 4 metrics (AP, ASR, mIoU, inference time). Adversarial patches are generated under white-box conditions using universal patch optimization (Eq. 1) with total-variation regularization, creating a worst-case threat model. Key empirical findings include: (1) naturalistic patches evade defenses due to data-distribution shift (measured via FID) rather than high-frequency content; (2) the AP of the attacked object, not patch detection mIoU, is the metric most consistent with true defense performance; (3) adaptive attacks bypass most defenses, but methods leveraging complex/stochastic models (PAD with SAM, DIFFender with diffusion) or universal patch properties (Zmask, Jedi) remain robust. Retraining defenses on the diverse APDE dataset yields an average 15.09% AP@0.5 improvement and better generalization to out-of-domain patches.

## Results

Across 11 object detectors and 94 adversarial patch types, NutNet achieves the best overall defense performance with a mean person AP@0.5 of 76.53 and minimum of 55.79, compared to an undefended baseline of 30.74. PAD attains a comparable mean AP (76.12) but incurs a prohibitive inference cost of ~32,100 ms per image, whereas SAC is the fastest at 44 ms but yields only 60.88 mean AP. The paper further shows that patch detection accuracy (mIoU) is a misleading metric: NAPGuard achieves the highest SmIoU (77.18 on T-SEA) yet NutNet delivers the lowest attack success rate, demonstrating that strong patch localization does not guarantee superior defense.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NutNet [26] outperforms all other defenses on both mean (76.53) and minimum (55.79) AP@0.5, and also achieves the lowest attack success rate, making it the strongest overall defense while remaining fast (71 ms). PAD [16] matches NutNet on mean AP (76.12) but its minimum drops to 40.99 and its 32,100 ms inference time renders it impractical; in the physical world, PAD is further surpassed by LGS and NAPGuard because it misclassifies humans as patches. NAPGuard [44] achieves the highest patch detection accuracy (SmIoU up to 77.18) yet its defense AP (75.94 mean, 46.93 min) trails NutNet, confirming that detection accuracy is a poor proxy for defense quality. SAC [27] is the fastest (44 ms) but its mean AP (60.88) and especially its minimum (20.62 on DDETR) are substantially lower than the top methods. LGS [31] sits in the middle (71.58 mean) but is easily bypassed by adaptive attacks that incorporate patch smoothness into the loss. Certified defenses (DetectorGuard, ObjectSeeker) achieve ~68–71 AP under constrained threat models but are outperformed by empirical defenses like PAD and NutNet (>80 AP) and scale poorly with patch count.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not propose a new defense method but instead carves new ground by unifying the evaluation of 11 existing adversarial patch defenses (SAC, PAD, NAPGuard, NutNet, DIFFender, LGS, Zmask, Jedi, Adyolo, DetectorGuard, ObjectSeeker) under a single standardized protocol. It departs from prior evaluations that used inconsistent parameters, limited attack coverage, and the misleading metric of patch detection accuracy, arguing instead that average precision of the attacked object and attack success rate are the appropriate measures. The APDE dataset (94 patch types, 94,000 images) is an order of magnitude larger than prior resources like Apricot (60 types, 1,011 images) and GAP (25 types, 9,266 images), and is constructed under white-box conditions to represent worst-case threats.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that patch detection accuracy (mIoU) is poorly correlated with actual defense effectiveness challenges a metric widely used in the literature, potentially invalidating conclusions in prior defense papers. The demonstration that data distribution (measured via FID), rather than high-frequency content, explains why naturalistic patches evade defenses redirects the design rationale for future defenses. The 15.09% AP@0.5 improvement from retraining on APDE shows the dataset has practical utility beyond evaluation. Open questions include whether certified defenses can be extended beyond their strict threat-model assumptions, how defenses hold against physically diverse attack surfaces (clothes, vehicles), and whether the white-box worst-case setting generalizes to realistic black-box threat models.
