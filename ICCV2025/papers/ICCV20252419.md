## Problem

Merging subject and style LoRAs for personalized image generation currently requires per-pair gradient optimization (e.g., ZipLoRA's 100+ steps taking minutes), making real-time merging on resource-constrained devices infeasible. Additionally, standard evaluation metrics (CLIP-I, DINO, CLIP-T) fail to reliably assess joint subject-style fidelity, as they each favor one aspect over the other.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252419/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The hypernetwork receives two LoRA weight update matrices (ΔW_c and ΔW_s, each of shape m×n) and outputs 2n column-wise merging coefficients (m_c, m_s ∈ R^n) per layer in a single forward pass. Different LoRA layers in the diffusion model have different matrix dimensions, so the hypernetwork uses separate input layers per unique size, all mapping to a shared hidden dimension. The merged LoRA is then applied to a base SDXL model with a text prompt to produce the final personalized image; the hypernetwork itself adds negligible memory overhead (~0 GB) at test time.

## Method

LoRA.rar pre-trains a lightweight 0.5M-parameter hypernetwork that predicts adaptive column-wise merging coefficients for arbitrary subject-style LoRA pairs via a single feed-forward pass, eliminating test-time optimization. The key insight is repurposing ZipLoRA's merging loss—originally designed to optimize coefficients per pair at test time—as a training objective for the hypernetwork's weights, enabling generalization to unseen pairs. The hypernetwork processes each LoRA column independently (treating columns as a minibatch), concatenating content and style columns as input features, with separate input layers per matrix size and a shared output layer. Training uses 360 subject-style LoRA combinations (30 subjects × 26 styles) with a loss combining content fidelity, style fidelity, and an orthogonality regularization term between m_c and m_s. The hypernetwork-guided merging is applied to query and output LoRAs, while key and value LoRAs use simple averaging, a configuration that empirically outperformed alternatives.

## Results

On the test set of unseen subject-style LoRA pairs, LoRA.rar achieves a MARS² score of 0.71 (average) and 1.00 (best-of-10), outperforming ZipLoRA (0.58 / 1.00) and all other merging strategies. The method generates merging coefficients in 0.037 s via a single forward pass of a 0.49 M-parameter hypernetwork, a >4000× speedup over ZipLoRA's 158 s per pair. On the lighter KOALA 700m backbone, LoRA.rar also leads with 0.56 average and 0.92 best MARS² versus ZipLoRA's 0.51 and 0.84.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRA.rar outperforms all six baselines on the average MARS² score, with the largest margin over DARE-TIES (+0.41) and DARE (+0.37), and a clear lead over the strongest competitor ZipLoRA (+0.13, i.e. 0.71 vs 0.58). On the best-of-10 metric, LoRA.rar ties ZipLoRA at a perfect 1.00, while all other methods fall between 0.60 and 0.84. In human evaluation, more than 75% of respondents rated LoRA.rar as comparable or better than ZipLoRA. The MARS² metric itself correlates with human preference (r = 0.76), whereas CLIP-I (r = 0.08) and DINO (r = −0.01) show essentially no alignment, validating the need for the proposed MLLM-based evaluation. On the KOALA 700m model, LoRA.rar maintains its advantage over ZipLoRA (0.56 vs 0.51 average; 0.92 vs 0.84 best), confirming generalizability across diffusion backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly builds on ZipLoRA (ECCV 2024), which introduced the task of merging pre-trained subject and style LoRAs via per-pair gradient optimization. LoRA.rar repurposes ZipLoRA's merging loss not for test-time coefficient optimization but as a training objective for a lightweight hypernetwork that predicts coefficients in a single forward pass. The hypernetwork architecture draws on the broader hypernetwork literature (Ha et al. 2017, HyperDreamBooth 2024) but applies it to a new target: predicting column-wise merging coefficients rather than generating LoRA weights. The evaluation protocol departs from standard CLIP-I/DINO metrics by introducing MARS², an MLLM-based judge (LLaVA-Critic) specifically designed for joint subject-style fidelity assessment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4000× speedup (158 s → 0.037 s) and zero extra test-time memory make real-time subject-style personalization feasible on resource-constrained devices like smartphones, a practical barrier that per-pair optimization methods cannot overcome. The MARS² metric exposes that widely-used single-concept metrics (CLIP-I, DINO) are essentially uncorrelated with human preference for joint subject-style generation, a finding that should influence how the community benchmarks personalized image generation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond two-LoRA merging to multi-concept compositions, whether it transfers to non-SDXL backbones without retraining, and how the small 0.5 M-parameter hypernetwork scales as LoRA rank or model depth increases.
