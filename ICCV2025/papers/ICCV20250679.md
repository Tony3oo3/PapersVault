## Problem

Pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP encode rich semantic concepts, but the semantic concept space and the forgery trace space are misaligned, causing fake images sharing similar concepts with real ones to be misclassified. Existing approaches either freeze the VLM (missing fine-grained forgery details) or rely on coarse text prompts (introducing semantic bias and limited description granularity), leaving no mechanism to align the two spaces at a fine-grained visual level.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (H×W×3) and produces a binary real/fake prediction via a linear classifier on a concatenated feature vector (LoRA-CLIP CLS token + enhanced low-level features). Internally, it also generates a reconstruction difference map (element-wise absolute difference between reconstructed and original features) that drives multi-scale forgery feature refinement. Real image patch tokens sampled from the training set serve as additional visual-clue inputs to the reconstruction encoder, and the system is trained on ProGAN real/fake pairs or Stable Diffusion v1 fakes with LAION real images.

## Method

SDD aligns the CLIP semantic concept space with the forgery space through three modules. (1) Semantic Tokens Sampling selects a subset of real-image patch tokens via Jensen-Shannon divergence to create distributionally representative visual clues that bridge real and forged domains without text-prompt bias. (2) Concept-level Forgery Discrepancy Learning fine-tunes CLIP-ViT-L/14 with LoRA, then uses a transformer encoder-decoder to reconstruct features; reconstruction loss is applied only to real samples, so fake images accumulate larger reconstruction errors that expose concept-guided forgery discrepancies. (3) Low-level Forgery Feature Enhancement applies a multi-scale CNN to the reconstruction difference map with an adaptive exponential-inverse weighting scheme that up-weights features strongly tied to forgery but weakly tied to semantics, preventing over-reliance on concept alignment. The total training objective jointly minimizes binary cross-entropy, triplet loss on the enhancer's anchor features, and the reconstruction loss.

## Results

On the UnivFD benchmark, SDD achieves a mean AP (mAP) of 98.51% and mean accuracy of 93.61%, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art FatFormer by 0.34% mAP and 2.75% mean accuracy. On the SynRIS benchmark, which features high-fidelity images from cutting-edge text-to-image models, SDD attains an average AUROC of 95.1%, outperforming the best baseline (NPR at 90.3%) by 4.8%. The method also substantially outperforms older forgery-detection approaches (e.g., CNN-Spot, PatchFor, Freq-spec) by large margins across all generative model categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDD outperforms all nine baselines on both evaluation benchmarks. On UnivFD, it surpasses the closest competitor FatFormer (CVPR 2024) by 0.34% mAP (98.51 vs. 98.18) and 2.75% mean accuracy (93.61 vs. 90.86), while UnivFD (CVPR 2023) trails by 5.13% mAP. On SynRIS, SDD achieves 95.1% average AUROC, beating NPR (90.3%) by 4.8% and FatFormer (78.0%) by 17.1%; notably, UnivFD drops to 62.3% AUROC on this dataset, confirming that frozen VLM features alone struggle with high-fidelity diffusion outputs. Older methods relying on monotonous forgery cues (CNN-Spot, PatchFor, Freq-spec) score below 70% average AUROC on SynRIS, highlighting their poor generalization to unseen generative models. The ablation (Table 4) further confirms that all three proposed modules (STS, CFDL, feature enhancement) contribute additively, with the full pipeline reaching 98.52% mAP versus 97.37% without STS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLM-based forgery detection paradigm established by UnivFD (frozen CLIP features) and FatFormer (text-prompt-based forgery-adaptive transformer) by replacing text prompts with purely visual semantic tokens sampled from real images via Jensen-Shannon divergence. It departs from FatFormer's soft-prompt approach, which the authors argue has limited semantic granularity, and from reconstruction-based detectors like DIRE and FakeInversion, which reconstruct both real and fake images, by reconstructing only real images in the fine-tuned CLIP space to define an authenticity boundary. The use of LoRA to fine-tune CLIP's image encoder while preserving its semantic concept space, combined with a transformer encoder-decoder for discrepancy amplification, positions the work as a vision-only extension of the VLM forgery detection lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a purely visual (text-prompt-free) approach within the VLM paradigm can match or exceed text-prompt-based methods, which is practically significant for deployment scenarios where text descriptions are unavailable or unreliable. The 4.8% AUROC gap over the previous best on SynRIS (high-fidelity text-to-image models) suggests that aligning semantic concept space with forgery space via reconstruction is a promising direction as generative models produce increasingly realistic outputs. However, the UnivFD improvements over FatFormer are modest (0.34% AP), and the authors acknowledge that continued advances in generative models will require further adaptation, leaving open how well the reconstruction-based alignment strategy scales to next-generation architectures.
