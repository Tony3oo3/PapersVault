## Problem

With over 100,000 LoRA adapters available on platforms like CivitAI, users lack an efficient way to select a subset that is both relevant to their prompt and diverse in style and concept. Existing retrieval methods relying on top-K cosine similarity surface redundant, near-duplicate adapters, while LLM-based selection introduces prompt bias that further limits output variation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250301/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt as input and produces a diverse set of generated images (15 per prompt in experiments) along with the selected LoRA adapter subset. The prompt is first decomposed into discrete concept chunks; for each concept, top-200 adapters are retrieved and top-8 are selected via submodular optimization. Images are generated using Stable Diffusion checkpoints (Realistic-Vision-v6, Counterfeit-v3) with 35 denoising steps and the DPM Solver++ scheduler, with a gpt-4o-based safety filter applied to exclude inappropriate adapters.

## Method

LoRAverse is a two-stage framework: a Concept Extractor (a single-step LLM call) decomposes the prompt into non-overlapping concept chunks, and a Submodular Retriever selects diverse adapters per concept. For each concept, the retriever first fetches top-200 adapters by cosine similarity, clusters them semantically using HDBSCAN via BERTopic, then maximizes a combined objective F(P) = λ₁·F_relevance(P) + λ₂·F_diversity(P) with a greedy submodular algorithm (λ₁=7.0, λ₂=1.0). The relevance term sums cosine similarities between adapter and prompt embeddings, while the diversity term uses a log-based cluster-coverage reward so that selecting from an unexplored cluster yields higher marginal gain than adding another adapter from an already-covered one. This guarantees a (1−1/e) approximation to the optimal diverse-relevant subset. Finally, images are produced by randomly sampling and linearly combining the selected LoRAs per concept, ensuring each generated image reflects a different adapter combination.

## Results

On 15,000 images (5,000 per method, 20 per prompt) from MS-COCO and PartiPrompts, LoRAverse achieves a 16.5% improvement in Truncated CLIP Entropy (TCE: 22.63 vs. 19.43 for SD v1.5) and a 5.1% gain in Truncated Inception Entropy (TIE: 40.06 vs. 38.12), while maintaining a competitive CLIP score of 25.07 (only 3.1% below SD v1.5's 25.88). In the user study, LoRAverse receives a 47.55% preference rate (vs. 29.90% for Stylus and 22.55% for SD v1.5) and a 39.5% higher diversity rating (US-D: 3.92 vs. 2.81). The VLM-as-a-Judge evaluation (gpt-4o) confirms LoRAverse's clear advantage in diversity while matching or surpassing baselines on visual quality and textual alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRAverse is compared against two baselines: Stylus (a top-K cosine-similarity retrieval + LLM composer pipeline) and Stable Diffusion v1.5 (no adapter retrieval). Against Stylus, LoRAverse wins on every diversity metric—TCE by 2.33 points (22.63 vs. 20.30), TIE by 1.53 points (40.06 vs. 38.53), and I2I by 0.041 lower (0.784 vs. 0.825, meaning more dissimilar images)—while also achieving a higher user-study diversity rating (3.92 vs. 2.82) and preference rate (47.55% vs. 29.90%). Against SD v1.5, the gains are even larger: TCE improves by 3.20 points and US-D by 1.11 points. The only metric where LoRAverse trails both baselines is CLIP text-image alignment (25.07 vs. 25.41 for Stylus and 25.88 for SD v1.5), a 3.1% drop the authors attribute to the natural broadening of the output distribution when diversity is prioritized. In the VLM-as-a-Judge evaluation, LoRAverse achieves the highest preference rate in diversity while remaining competitive on quality and alignment, confirming that the submodular selection strategy outperforms both the LLM-based composer in Stylus and the no-retrieval baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the submodular optimization framework from document summarization (Lin & Bilmes, 2011) to the problem of diverse LoRA adapter retrieval for diffusion models. It departs from Stylus [26], which relies on top-K cosine-similarity ranking followed by LLM-based pruning, by formulating adapter selection as a monotone submodular maximization with an explicit diversity term over HDBSCAN/BERTopic clusters. The concept-extraction stage (LLM-based prompt decomposition) is a lightweight addition that feeds per-concept retrieval, distinguishing it from methods that retrieve against the full prompt embedding. Clustering-based diversity regularization draws on GAN editing literature [6, 30, 37], but the combination with submodular greedy selection for adapter retrieval is the paper's core methodological contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With over 100K LoRA adapters on platforms like CivitAI, the practical need for structured, diversity-aware adapter selection is significant, and this paper provides the first framework with a theoretical approximation guarantee (1−1/e) for that task. The ~26-second inference overhead from per-concept clustering is a practical limitation that may constrain real-time use, and the slight CLIP score drop (3.1%) raises the question of how to better preserve prompt fidelity while maximizing diversity. Open questions include whether the approach scales to even larger adapter pools, whether the submodular formulation generalizes to video or 3D generation adapters, and whether the per-concept clustering step can be amortized across prompts.
