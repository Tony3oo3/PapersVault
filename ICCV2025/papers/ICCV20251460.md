## Problem

Fitting a parametric body model to 3D clothed human point clouds remains challenging: optimization-based pipelines are sensitive to pose initialization and rely on fragile multi-stage cues, while learning-based methods struggle to generalize across diverse poses, garment types, and non-rigid dynamics. Existing tightness-aware approaches model the cloth-to-body relationship as scalars or double-layer occupancy, losing the directional information that consistently points from clothing toward the underlying body regardless of pose or garment deformation.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unordered 3D point cloud randomly sampled from a clothed human surface (no camera views, no 2D keypoints, no skin segmentation required) and operates in posed space rather than canonical space, making it robust to out-of-distribution poses. It outputs 86 sparse body markers on the SMPL surface, from which SMPL pose (24 joints), shape (10 blendshape coefficients), and translation parameters are recovered via Levenberg-Marquardt optimization. The intermediate tightness vectors, marker labels, and per-point confidences are also produced as byproducts of the network.

## Method

ETCH encodes cloth-to-body tightness as displacement vectors (direction + magnitude) from each outer cloth point to the corresponding inner body point, exploiting the observation that these vectors are approximately locally SE(3)-equivariant under clothing deformation. Direction is predicted from EPN equivariant features via self-attention over the 60-element icosahedral rotation group, yielding a per-point rotation matrix; magnitude, marker label (86 classes), and confidence are predicted from invariant (mean-pooled) features using Point Transformer for contextual information. At inference, outer points are displaced inward along predicted tightness vectors to approximate inner body points, which are then aggregated into 86 sparse markers via confidence-weighted voting (top-m points per label with exponent α). This reformulates clothed human fitting into a tightness-aware marker-based fitting problem, where the final SMPL optimization uses only the 86 markers as targets without additional pose priors or geometric regularizers.

## Results

On CAPE, ETCH achieves V2V=1.647 cm, MPJPE=0.922 cm, and CD=1.019 cm, outperforming all baselines. On 4D-Dress, ETCH reaches V2V=1.939 cm, MPJPE=1.116 cm, and CD=1.065 cm, reducing V2V error by 16.7%–59.2% and MPJPE by 32.6%–69.5% over competitors. In one-shot (≈1% data) settings, equivariant tightness vectors reduce directional angular errors by 67.2%–89.8% compared to non-equivariant alternatives, demonstrating strong out-of-distribution generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ETCH outperforms all four baselines across every metric on both datasets. Against the tightness-agnostic NICP, ETCH reduces CAPE V2V by 4.6% (1.647 vs. 1.726) and MPJPE by 31.3% (0.922 vs. 1.343), while on 4D-Dress the gains are far larger: V2V drops 59.2% (1.939 vs. 4.754) and MPJPE drops 69.5% (1.116 vs. 3.654). Against ArtEq, ETCH improves 4D-Dress MPJPE by 32.6% (1.116 vs. 1.657) and V2V by 16.7% (1.939 vs. 2.328). Among tightness-aware methods, ETCH beats PTF by 19.1% in CAPE V2V (1.647 vs. 2.036) and by 30.7% in 4D-Dress V2V (1.939 vs. 2.796), and beats IPNet by 36.5% in CAPE V2V (1.647 vs. 2.593) and 49.5% in 4D-Dress V2V (1.939 vs. 3.826). Notably, ETCH achieves these gains without pose priors, shape regularizers, or chamfer-based post-refinement, relying solely on 86 predicted sparse markers for SMPL optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

ETCH sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: articulated SE(3) equivariant fitting (ArtEq, built on EPN's equivariant point network backbone) and tightness-aware body fitting (IPNet's double-layer occupancy, PTF's piecewise transformation fields, TightCap's scalar tightness). The key departure is encoding tightness as SE(3)-equivariant displacement vectors rather than scalars or occupancy fields, which allows the cloth-to-body mapping to remain approximately equivariant under clothing deformation while consistently pointing inward. The final fitting step reuses the well-established sparse-marker-to-SMPL optimization from MoSh, replacing the dense correspondence regression used by PTF with a confidence-weighted marker aggregation that the authors argue is more robust to outliers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-shot OOD generalization result (67–90% directional error reduction with ~1% of training data) is the most practically significant finding, suggesting that equivariant tightness vectors encode a geometric prior that transfers with very few examples — a property that could reduce data requirements for new garment types or body models. However, the method remains tied to SMPL and paired clothed/body training data, and the full-fitting improvements over strong baselines (PTF, ArtEq) are consistent but moderate (16–69% on 4D-Dress, 4–36% on CAPE). Open questions include whether the equivariant tightness formulation extends to non-parametric or generative body models, whether it handles extreme occlusions (e.g., heavy outerwear), and whether the 86-marker aggregation can be made adaptive to clothing type.
