## Problem

Open-set fine-grained recognition suffers from semantic drift: as visual features are progressively refined from coarse to fine-grained levels, small biases in unknown samples accumulate and weaken discriminative cues, causing misclassification into known classes. Existing hierarchical OSFGR methods neglect consistency constraints across representation levels and rely solely on visual information, which hits a bottleneck when known and unknown categories share high visual similarity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252437/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 224×224 RGB image and a set of pre-computed CLIP text embeddings (one per known category, generated from human-readable descriptions before training) as input. It produces a class label via argmax over final logits and a known/unknown decision by comparing the maximum logit against a fixed threshold τ; if below τ the sample is rejected as unknown, otherwise the argmax class is returned.

## Method

MPBR introduces a bidirectional reasoning loop over a Swin Transformer backbone: the forward pass progressively extracts multi-scale visual features X_g, while the backward pass employs a conditional VAE at each layer to reconstruct the original image from shallow features X_g conditioned on the next deeper feature X_{g+1} and a category-specific CLIP text embedding T_y, enforcing hierarchical consistency in a category-aware latent space. The CVAE uses a layer-dependent isotropic Gaussian prior N(0, a^{-g}I) whose variance shrinks with depth, compressing semantic representations while preserving detail at shallow layers. A lightweight per-layer classifier selects the top-1 predicted text embedding to condition the backward reconstruction, and a Multi-Granularity Aggregation module fuses the sequence of latent variables and visual features via DG-Mamba (Vision Mamba) blocks into a single vector for final classification. Training minimizes a combined loss of per-layer ELBO (L2 reconstruction + KL divergence) and cross-entropy at both intermediate and final classifiers.

## Results

On the iNat2021-OSR-Aves fine-grained benchmark (1-hop), MPBR achieves 80.43% ACC, 77.17% AUROC, and 68.43% OSCR, surpassing the second-best HAN-OSFGR by +1.14% ACC, +2.48% AUROC, and +2.45% OSCR. On iNat2021-OSR-Insecta (1-hop), MPBR reaches 89.10% ACC, 80.69% AUROC, and 75.88% OSCR. In the coarse-grained regime (5–7 hop), MPBR maintains its lead, e.g., 80.32% ACC and 98.03% AUROC at 5-hop on Aves, and 89.38% ACC and 97.95% AUROC at 5-hop on Insecta.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPBR outperforms all five baselines across every hop distance and both datasets on ACC, AUROC, and OSCR. The closest competitor is HAN-OSFGR [25], which uses a hierarchical attention network with LSTM aggregation; MPBR leads it by +1.14% ACC and +2.48% AUROC at 1-hop on Aves, and by +0.89% ACC and +1.04% AUROC at 1-hop on Insecta. MoEP-AE-OSR [24], which models class-conditional distributions via exponential power mixtures, is the second-closest baseline but trails MPBR by 1.88% AUROC at 1-hop on Aves. HAL [12] (hierarchical adversarial learning) reports only ACC and AUROC (no OSCR); MPBR exceeds it by 8.75% ACC at 1-hop on Aves, reflecting HAL's weaker closed-set performance. ARPL [4] and Cross-Entropy+ [30] are substantially weaker, with AUROC gaps of 3–4% at 1-hop, indicating that adversarial reciprocal-point and closed-set classifier strategies struggle more with fine-grained semantic drift. In the coarse-grained regime (5–7 hop), MPBR's advantage narrows slightly (e.g., −0.04% AUROC vs. HAN-OSFGR at 7-hop on Aves) but it still leads on ACC and OSCR across all splits.

## Relation to Prior Work

MPBR extends the open-set fine-grained recognition (OSFGR) line initiated by Lang et al. (HAL) and Sun et al. (HAN-OSFGR), which model hierarchical taxonomic structure but rely solely on unidirectional visual feature refinement. The key departure is the introduction of a backward variational inference branch (CVAE) conditioned on CLIP text embeddings, which imposes cross-hierarchical consistency constraints absent in prior OSFGR methods, and the use of Vision Mamba for multi-granularity aggregation. Conceptually, the paper repurposes the CVAE literature (originally for image generation) as a feature-alignment mechanism and borrows the multimodal grounding idea from CLIP-based recognition, combining them in a bidirectional architecture not previously explored for open-set tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bidirectional reasoning + multimodal constraint framework offers a principled way to combat semantic drift in fine-grained open-set settings, and the SDI/Silhouette/DBI analysis provides a useful quantitative lens for evaluating feature-space compactness beyond AUROC. However, the paper itself notes that text embeddings are tailored to single-hop semantics and gains diminish at coarser granularities, leaving open the question of how to generate hierarchical or multi-hop textual priors. Generalization beyond the iNat2021-OSR benchmark (plants/animals) and to other fine-grained domains (e.g., aircraft, car models) remains untested, as does the computational overhead of running CVAE reconstruction at every backbone layer.
