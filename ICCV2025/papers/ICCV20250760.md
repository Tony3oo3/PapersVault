## Problem

Hyperparameter optimization in deep reinforcement learning is consistently challenging because deep RL operates in non-stationary environments where optimal HP configurations shift across training stages, and the high computational cost of RL training makes multi-run methods like PBT or Bayesian optimization impractical. Existing single-run approaches (e.g., HOOF) either greedily optimize short-term improvements without exploration or rely on importance sampling that introduces high variance in dynamic settings. The paper addresses this gap by seeking a lightweight, single-run HPO method that optimizes long-term returns with minimal overhead.

## Input / Output

ULTHO consumes only the mean episode return estimated by the value network (computed via GAE over the episode) and a user-defined set of HP clusters (e.g., learning rate, batch size, value loss coefficient, entropy loss coefficient, number of update epochs), each containing a fixed number of discrete candidate values. It produces a single selected HP value per episode to feed into the RL algorithm's policy update, operating as a plug-and-play module that requires no access to gradients or internal algorithm state. A sliding window of length W (default configurable) stores past returns for utility estimation, and the exploration coefficient c controls the UCB trade-off.

## Method

ULTHO formulates HPO as a Multi-Armed Bandit with Clustered Arms (MABC) and solves it via a two-tier UCB strategy: at each episode, it first selects an HP cluster (inter-cluster) and then a specific value within that cluster (intra-cluster), both using UCB with a sliding-window average of value-network-estimated returns as the reward signal. This hierarchical design reduces the effective search space and ensures only one HP changes at a time, stabilizing learning. An extended variant, Relay-ULTHO, identifies the most-selected cluster (COI) and least-selected cluster (NOI) after an initial phase and restricts subsequent optimization to these two clusters for incremental refinement. The entire framework requires no additional learning process, no gradient access, and operates within a single training run, making it compatible with any RL algorithm that exposes a value network.

## Results

On the Procgen benchmark (16 procedurally-generated environments), ULTHO achieves the highest aggregated mean score of 56.0% and interquartile mean (IQM) of 56.41%, outperforming the PPO baseline (40.42% mean, 38.41% IQM) by approximately 15.6 percentage points. On ALE-5, ULTHO outperforms the PPO baseline in all five environments and achieves the best score in two of them, particularly in Q*Bert. On PyBullet continuous control tasks (Ant, HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2D), ULTHO produces significant performance gains over vanilla PPO across all four environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ULTHO outperforms all six HPO baselines on the Procgen benchmark: it beats TMIHF (51.16% mean) by ~4.8 points, PB2 (44.63%) by ~11.4 points, PPO (40.42%) by ~15.6 points, and PBT (33.55%) by ~22.5 points in aggregated mean score. On ALE-5, ULTHO surpasses SMAC and SMAC+HB (which rank second and third) in average performance across all five games, while PBT excels in BattleZone but underperforms PPO in Q*Bert. Notably, ULTHO achieves these gains with a single training run and no population of agents, whereas PBT, PB2, and TMIHF all require multiple parallel agents (marked with ‡), making ULTHO substantially more computationally efficient. The Relay-ULTHO variant underperforms the standard ULTHO on Procgen (48.04% vs. 56.0%), suggesting that the restricted two-cluster optimization is less effective in highly non-stationary environments, though it does help on the more stationary MiniGrid and PyBullet benchmarks. On MiniGrid sparse-reward tasks, ULTHO does not outperform PPO in final score, but Relay-ULTHO reaches the goal in fewer environment steps, indicating faster convergence rather than higher asymptotic performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

ULTHO builds on the multi-armed bandit with clustered arms (MABC) formulation from Carlsson et al. and the hierarchical two-tier HPO idea from Barsce et al. and Jaderberg et al., but departs sharply from population-based methods (PBT, PB2, TMIHF) by operating within a single training run with no population of agents. It also contrasts with gradient-based meta-learning approaches (e.g., Zahavy et al.'s self-tuning actor-critic) by requiring no access to internal gradients, and with HOOF by optimizing long-term estimated returns via UCB rather than one-step policy improvement via importance sampling. The result is a plug-and-play, gradient-free module that sits atop any RL algorithm and selects hyperparameters using only value-network estimates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ULTHO addresses a practical bottleneck—RL-specific HPO is underexplored despite the high sensitivity of deep RL to hyperparameter choices—and does so with minimal overhead (no extra learning, no population, no gradient access), making it broadly applicable across RL algorithms and environments. The finding that the more elaborate Relay-ULTHO variant underperforms the simpler ULTHO on highly dynamic Procgen environments is a useful negative result, suggesting that comprehensive single-pass exploration may be preferable to staged refinement in non-stationary settings. Open questions include extending to continuous hyperparameter spaces, exploring alternative bandit strategies (Thompson sampling, Exp3), and validating the approach on larger-scale or model-based RL settings.
