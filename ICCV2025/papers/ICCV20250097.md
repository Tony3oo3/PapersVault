## Problem

Learning-based stereo matching models degrade severely under adverse weather (rain, fog, snow) due to two compounding issues: a scarcity of high-quality training data for degraded conditions, and encoders that produce noisy, unreliable features from low-visibility, high-noise images. This makes zero-shot generalization to unseen weather conditions particularly challenging, as models trained on clean data face a large domain gap with no fine-tuning opportunity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250097/cropped_page3_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The stereo matching network takes a pair of left and right RGB images (H×W×3) captured under adverse weather and outputs a left-view disparity map (H×W). The data generation pipeline additionally consumes monocular depth maps (predicted by DepthAnythingV2) and LLM-generated text prompts as conditioning signals for the diffusion model. The Disparity Fusion Module operates on patch-level feature tensors of shape 2×N×C (two views, N patches, C feature dimensions) to enforce inter-view consistency during synthesis.

## Method

RobuSTereo addresses both data and model limitations. For data generation, it uses Stable Diffusion 1.5 with a Depth2Image ControlNet conditioned on predicted depth maps and LLM-generated weather prompts to transform clean stereo pairs into adverse-weather versions while preserving geometric structure; a Disparity Fusion Method (DFM) consistency module then matches patches between left and right views based on disparity and image similarity, fuses the top-n matched pairs through self-attention, and un-fuses them to enforce stereo correspondence. For the matching network, a robust feature encoder combines VGG19 (extracting multi-scale local features at 1/4, 1/8, 1/16 resolutions) with a Denoising Vision Transformer (DVT) that produces a high-level semantic feature map at 1/32 resolution, reducing feature-level noise from degraded inputs. The disparity refinement stage follows StereoBase's iterative architecture, and the full model is trained with L1 loss on the synthetic RST-Dataset alongside SceneFlow.

## Results

On the DrivingStereo benchmark, RobuSTereo achieves an overall EPE of 0.836 and D1 of 1.598, outperforming the strongest prior method StereoAnything (EPE 1.042, D1 3.865) by roughly 20% in EPE and 59% in D1. The advantage is most pronounced in rainy conditions, where RobuSTereo records EPE 0.973 and D1 1.939 versus StereoAnything's 1.144 and 5.395. On the more extreme SeeingThroughFog dataset, RobuSTereo (EPE 3.359, D1 20.881) again leads StereoAnything (EPE 4.204, D1 26.526) and LightStereo (EPE 3.853, D1 28.431) across all four fog/snow/rain splits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RobuSTereo outperforms all 13 baselines on every weather split of DrivingStereo in both EPE and D1. Against the strongest recent methods—StereoAnything (EPE 1.042, D1 3.865), LightStereo (1.088, 4.107), and MonSter (1.081, 4.325)—the gains are 19–23% in EPE and 58–63% in D1 overall, with the largest relative improvement in rainy conditions (D1 drops from ~5.3 to 1.94). On SeeingThroughFog, RobuSTereo (EPE 3.359, D1 20.881) beats StereoAnything (4.204, 26.526) by 20% in EPE and 21% in D1, and LightStereo (3.853, 28.431) by 13% and 27% respectively. The dataset ablation (Table 3) shows that simply training StereoBase on RST-Dataset already yields EPE 0.875 / D1 2.050, surpassing the same model trained on SceneFlow (1.302 / 5.974) or vKITTI (1.159 / 5.157), confirming that the synthetic data itself is the primary driver of the improvement. Older architectures (PSMNet, GwcNet, CasStereo) remain far behind, with D1 scores above 20 in most conditions, underscoring that both the data and the robust encoder are necessary for competitive zero-shot performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: learning-based stereo matching (building directly on StereoBase's iterative refinement architecture and IGEV-style cost volume design) and diffusion-based image synthesis (repurposing Stable Diffusion with ControlNet for domain-adaptive data generation). It departs from prior adverse-weather stereo work, which relied on CG simulation (vKITTI) or labor-intensive real data collection (DrivingStereo), by introducing a diffusion pipeline that transfers normal-weather stereo pairs into adverse conditions while preserving geometric structure via a Disparity Fusion Method. On the model side, it extends the standard CNN/ViT encoder paradigm by integrating DVT's denoising transformer with VGG19's multi-scale features, a combination not previously explored in stereo matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for autonomous driving and robotics, where zero-shot stereo matching in rain, fog, and snow remains a critical unsolved reliability gap. The diffusion-based data generation pipeline offers a scalable, low-cost alternative to real-world adverse-weather data collection, and the finding that training on RST-Dataset alone (without architectural changes) already yields substantial gains suggests the data bottleneck is the primary limiter. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to truly unseen degradation types (night, motion blur, sensor failure), how the domain gap between synthetic and real adverse weather evolves with diffusion model quality, and whether the consistency module's patch-matching strategy scales to higher resolutions or multi-view settings.
