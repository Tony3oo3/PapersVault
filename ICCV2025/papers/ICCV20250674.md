## Problem

Quantizing large DNN-based imitation learning policies (e.g., 7B-parameter VLA models) to low-bit precision causes mission failures in robotic control, yet the failure mechanism differs from typical quantization: errors are minor across most timesteps but produce large action deviations at a small number of mission-critical states. Existing PTQ and QAT methods, well-studied in CV and NLP, do not specifically address this sparse critical-state failure pattern in IL, where environment feedback for error correction is unavailable.

## Input / Output

The method operates on an already-trained full-precision policy and its expert demonstration dataset, requiring no new data collection. For OpenVLA, inputs are RGB images plus a natural-language instruction, producing 7-DoF robot actions (x, y, z, roll, pitch, yaw, grasp) auto-regressively; for CILRS, inputs are camera images plus a measurement vector, producing acceleration and steering; for D4RL, inputs are state vectors producing continuous control actions. The quantized model is initialized from FP weights and fine-tuned with only a small parameter subset (e.g., 110M via QLoRA r=32), while the frozen FP policy serves as an additional teacher signal during training.

## Method

SQIL combines standard quantization-aware training (QAT) with a novel Quantization-Robust Action Distillation (QRD) loss that selectively amplifies the discrepancy between the quantized and FP policy action distributions at mission-critical states. Critical states are identified unsupervised via a Saliency-based State-Importance Score (SIS), which measures how much the FP policy's output changes under Gaussian-blur perturbations at each spatial position of the input image; states with high average saliency are flagged as critical. During training, QRD applies an extra weight β (>1) to the L2 divergence between quantized and FP action distributions only for states whose SIS exceeds a threshold T (top 20%), while all other states receive unit weight. The total loss L^SQIL = L^QAT + L^QRD is optimized with the same expert dataset and hyperparameters as standard QAT, making SQIL a drop-in replacement that requires no environment interaction or additional data.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark with OpenVLA (INT4, AWQ), SQIL achieves 83.9%/83.5%/76.3%/49.2% success rate across Spatial/Object/Goal/Long, nearly matching the FP baseline (84.0%/83.9%/76.6%/50.7%), while PTQ drops to 80.1%/81.3%/74.3%/47.2% and QAT to 80.9%/82.4%/75.7%/47.3%. On NoCrash-dense with CILRS (W4A4), SQIL maintains 80/72/72/68% success rate versus FP's 82/74/80/68%, whereas PTQ collapses to 34/43/36/29%. On D4RL MuJoCo tasks, SQIL recovers near-FP returns (e.g., Cartpole 635 vs. 652 FP) while LPPD drops to 424. On a Jetson AGX Orin, the INT4 OpenVLA model delivers 2.5× speedup and 2.5× energy savings over BF16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the prior quantized-policy method LPPD [36] on D4RL MuJoCo tasks, SQIL consistently outperforms by large margins: e.g., Cartpole Balance 635 vs. 424, Walker Stand 688 vs. 442, and Humanoid Walk 535 vs. 205, while remaining within 1–3% of the FP32 IL baseline. Against standard PTQ (AWQ) on LIBERO, SQIL recovers 2.3–3.8 percentage points of success rate (e.g., 83.9% vs. 80.1% on Spatial), and against QAT it gains 3.0–1.9 points (83.9% vs. 80.9% on Spatial). On NoCrash-dense (W4A4), SQIL achieves 80/72/72/68% success rate versus PTQ's 34/43/36/29% and QAT's 62/58/58/48%, a gap of 18–46 points over PTQ. The advantage is consistent across quantizers (AWQ and QuaRot) and domains (manipulation, driving, physics), confirming that the saliency-weighted distillation is the critical differentiator rather than the specific quantization kernel.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends quantization-aware training (QAT) from its well-studied domains (computer vision, NLP) to imitation learning (IL) policies, where the absence of environment interaction makes standard error-correction strategies inapplicable. It builds on the perturbation-based saliency analysis of Greydanus et al. [17] to define a State-Importance Score (SIS) that identifies mission-critical states without supervision, and combines this with a selective knowledge-distillation loss (QRD) that aligns the quantized policy's action distribution with the full-precision teacher only at those salient states. The work departs from prior RL-oriented quantization methods (e.g., LPPD, QuARL) that rely on environment feedback, and from OpenVLA's own QAT+LoRA experiments that showed mixed results, by introducing a targeted, saliency-driven weighting mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a 4-bit quantized VLA model (7.6B parameters) can recover full-precision success rates while delivering 2.5× speedup and 2.5× energy savings on an edge GPU, making large IL-based policies practically deployable on battery-powered robots. The method's generality—validated across robot manipulation (OpenVLA, π₀), autonomous driving (CILRS), and physics simulation (D4RL/MuJoCo)—suggests it could become a standard component in the quantization toolkit for policy models. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 2-bit or mixed-precision regimes, whether the saliency computation cost is prohibitive for very long-horizon tasks, and whether the fixed 20% threshold for critical states adapts well to tasks with varying criticality distributions.
