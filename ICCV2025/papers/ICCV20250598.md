## Problem

Remote physiological measurement via visible light cameras degrades under challenging conditions such as low light or diverse skin tones, and while complementary modalities (near-infrared, radar) capture distinct physiological information, existing methods lack a unified framework to holistically fuse these physically heterogeneous sensing modalities. Prior fusion work (e.g., EquiPleth) addresses only specific modality pairs with ad-hoc strategies, without exploiting commonalities across physiologically relevant modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250598/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework accepts visible light video (C₁×T×H×W), near-infrared video (same format), or radar signals (C₂×T_rf) as input, standardizing all to a D×T×K tensor where K is the total modality count (including sub-modalities from spatial decomposition). Radar requires temporal downsampling to match camera frame rate, and test sequences are segmented into 896-frame clips (~30 s at 30 fps). The output is a continuous BVP waveform from which heart rate is derived, evaluated via MAE, RMSE, Pearson R, and fairness metrics (T-Test gap between dark- and light-skin groups).

## Method

FusionPhys models cross-modal fusion as an adaptive modulation process inspired by the McGurk effect: each modality's features are refined by a temporally varying modulation factor learned from all modalities jointly. A sub-modality decomposition step applies strided convolutions (stride 1,2,2) to split the video's spatial dimension into N sub-modalities, treating physiologically distinct facial regions as separate channels. The core Physiological Interaction Block (PIB) applies multi-level Haar wavelet transforms along the temporal axis to extract multi-scale features, passes them through shared-weight FC layers with interleaved duplication (rpt_intlv) to reconstruct full temporal resolution, and produces a modulation matrix Λ that is applied via Hadamard product to the base features. Four stacked PIB blocks iteratively refine cross-modal interactions, after which features are averaged across the modality dimension and a point-wise convolution predictor generates the BVP signal. This design operates identically for multi-modal fusion (visible+NIR, visible+radar) and single-video scenarios, requiring no architectural modifications.

## Results

On single visible-light modality, FusionPhys achieves state-of-the-art MAE of 0.15 bpm on UBFC-rPPG, 0.42 bpm on PURE, and 0.75 bpm on MMSE-HR, outperforming the previous best (Contrast-Phys+) by 29%, 13%, and 33% respectively. On the OBF dataset, FusionPhys attains the lowest MAE (0.24 bpm) though Contrast-Phys+ retains a slight edge in RMSE (0.75 vs. 1.11 bpm). For visible-light + radar fusion on EquiPleth, FusionPhys-Fusion achieves MAE 1.07 bpm with a fairness gap of only 0.08% between dark- and light-skin groups, compared to 3.1% for the prior best fusion method (EquiPleth-Fusion). Cross-dataset generalization (UBFC-rPPG → MMSE-HR) yields MAE 1.03 bpm and R 0.98, substantially surpassing the runner-up Contrast-Phys+ (MAE 1.76, R 0.92).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FusionPhys outperforms the strongest single-modality baseline, Contrast-Phys+ [37], on three of four datasets: UBFC-rPPG (MAE 0.15 vs. 0.21), PURE (0.42 vs. 0.48), and MMSE-HR (0.75 vs. 1.11), while on OBF it achieves a lower MAE (0.24 vs. 0.34) but a higher RMSE (1.11 vs. 0.75). Against earlier deep-learning methods such as PhysNet [48] and Dual-GAN [20], FusionPhys consistently delivers 2–10× lower MAE across all benchmarks. In the multi-modal fusion setting on EquiPleth, FusionPhys-Fusion beats the prior fusion baseline EquiPleth-Fusion [41] on MAE (1.07 vs. 1.12 bpm), MAPE (1.44% vs. 1.52%), and correlation (R 0.96 vs. 0.95), while dramatically reducing the dark/light-skin fairness gap from 3.1% to 0.08%. On the OBF dark-skin subset, FusionPhys-Fusion achieves the best MAE (0.281 bpm) and RMSE (1.140 bpm), outperforming Contrast-Phys+ (0.341 / 2.113) and PhysFormer [51] (0.492 / 2.413). In cross-dataset transfer (UBFC-rPPG → MMSE-HR), FusionPhys leads all baselines with MAE 1.03 bpm and R 0.98, compared to Contrast-Phys+ (1.76 / 0.92) and PhysFormer (2.68 / 0.86), indicating substantially better generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the remote physiological measurement (rPPG) literature, which has been dominated by single-modality visible-light approaches (PhysNet, Contrast-Phys+, PhysFormer), by introducing a unified multi-modal fusion framework. It departs from the only prior multi-modal rPPG work (EquiPleth, Vilesov et al. 2022), which performed post-hoc frequency-domain fusion of independently extracted BVP signals, by instead operating at the feature level with a learned modulation mechanism inspired by the McGurk effect. The sub-modality decomposition is a conceptual repurposing: spatial regions of a single video are treated as 'sub-modalities' so that the same Physiological Interaction Block (PIB) architecture handles both genuine multi-modal fusion and single-modality video, unifying two previously separate problem settings under one framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant result is the dramatic reduction in the fairness gap between dark-skin and light-skin groups (T-Test gap from 3.1% to 0.08% on EquiPleth), which addresses a critical barrier to clinical deployment of camera-based vital-sign monitoring. The unified framework also lowers the barrier to adopting multi-modal sensing by requiring no architectural changes when switching modality pairs. Open questions include whether the approach scales to three or more simultaneous modalities, whether the wavelet-based PIB generalizes to other physiological signals (respiration, SpO2), and whether the 64-sub-modality granularity transfers across different face crops and resolutions. The reliance on paired multi-modal data for fusion training also limits real-world applicability where only one modality may be available at inference time.
