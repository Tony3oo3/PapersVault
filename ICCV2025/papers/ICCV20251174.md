## Problem

Existing methods for improving diffusion model outputs rely on image-level quality scores or pairwise comparisons, which overlook the fine-grained spatial information of where artifacts actually occur. No prior work uses pixel-level artifact feedback to directly tune diffusion models, leaving a gap between identifying artifact locations and leveraging that localization to reduce future artifact generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251174/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The artifact detector takes a synthesized RGB image and outputs a per-pixel confidence map (values 0–1) indicating artifact likelihood, implemented as a SegFormer-b5 with a sigmoid head. The treating stage consumes only text prompts (no reference images) to synthesize images, obtain artifact maps, and update the diffusion model via LoRA. The final output is a tuned diffusion model that generates fewer artifacts on unseen prompts while preserving image quality and layout.

## Method

DiffDoctor is a two-stage diagnose-then-treat pipeline. In the diagnosis stage, a SegFormer-b5 artifact detector is trained on 1M+ samples using a class-balance strategy that combines real photos as negative samples, human-labeled hard cases selected via MLLM-based category analysis, and semi-supervised pseudo-labeling with a dynamic augmentation that shrinks low-confidence images to expose small complex regions. In the treating stage, the frozen detector's confidence map is back-propagated through the diffusion model's denoising chain (with gradient truncation in the last 25% of steps) to minimize a pixel-level loss over a threshold-selected mask of high-confidence artifact pixels. An optional rectified-flow regularization term (γ=0.25) is added to the loss to prevent model collapse during extended training, and only LoRA layers (rank 16) are updated to keep the procedure lightweight.

## Results

On FLUX.1, DiffDoctor reduces the mean artifact frequency from 82.66% to 22.00% while maintaining ImageReward (1.179→1.183) and improving CLIP-T (35.463→35.611). On SDXL, artifact frequency drops from 55.33% to 27.50% with ImageReward improving from 0.974 to 1.008. On Kolors, artifact frequency decreases from 65.31% to 29.33% with CLIP-T improving from 34.251 to 34.424. User studies with 24 annotators confirm the gains: artifact winning rates jump from 36.36% (FLUX.1), 31.82% (SDXL), and 39.02% (Kolors) to 63.64%, 68.18%, and 60.98% respectively, while general quality winning rates remain near 50%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffDoctor dramatically outperforms HPSv2 (global preference optimization) on artifact reduction: on FLUX.1, HPSv2 barely moves artifact frequency (80.67% vs. 82.66% baseline) while DiffDoctor cuts it to 22.00%; on SDXL, HPSv2 actually worsens artifacts (81.19% vs. 55.33%) whereas DiffDoctor achieves 27.50%. HPSv2 also degrades CLIP-T on all three backbones (e.g., 35.037 vs. 35.463 on FLUX.1), indicating a text-image consistency trade-off. HandsXL, a hand-specific LoRA, reduces FLUX.1 artifact frequency to 60.54% but at the cost of dropping ImageReward from 1.179 to 0.961 and CLIP-T from 35.463 to 33.528, and it only addresses one artifact category. For the artifact detector component, DiffDoctor's SegFormer-based detector (MSE 0.337, KL 1.004, KL(1-) 2.231) substantially outperforms both PAL4VST (MSE 0.480, KL 5.053) and the RichHF reimplementation (MSE 1.601, KL(1-) 7.044), with the largest gap on false-positive rate (KL(1-)), confirming the importance of the class-balancing strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the direct-reward-fine-tuning paradigm (DRaFT, AlignProp) from image-level scalar rewards to pixel-level dense supervision, back-propagating gradients from a differentiable artifact segmentation network (SegFormer) through the full denoising chain. It departs from prior artifact-mitigation work (PAL4VST, RichHF, SynArtifact) that either used pixel annotations only for inpainting/ranking or relied on global artifact scores for RL, by actually using per-pixel confidence as a training signal for the diffusion model. The data construction builds on active and semi-supervised learning ideas, introducing a class-balance strategy and human-in-the-loop pseudo-labeling to address severe category imbalance in existing artifact datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiffDoctor establishes a new post-training paradigm—diagnose-then-treat with pixel-level feedback—that is orthogonal to and composable with existing reward-based alignment methods, and it generalizes to downstream tasks like DreamBooth. The 1M+ balanced artifact dataset and the finding that existing annotations are dangerously imbalanced (hiding high false-positive rates) are practically important for anyone building artifact-aware pipelines. A key open question is whether this approach can handle semantically complex artifacts requiring physical or commonsense reasoning, which the authors explicitly leave for future work; the current detector is limited to shape distortions, unreasonable content, and watermarks.
