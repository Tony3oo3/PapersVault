## Problem

Previous group detection methods assume a fixed set of groups throughout a video (static detection), but people frequently join and leave groups, making this assumption invalid. Dividing a video into short clips where groups are stable is a chicken-and-egg problem because the change points are unknown a priori. Additionally, prior methods extract features from individual person bounding boxes, missing the spatial scene context (background objects, shared focus) that is critical for identifying indirect interactions between group members.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 336×336 pixel bounding-box crops that enclose a target pair of people plus surrounding background, with red circles drawn around each person as a visual prompt to direct CLIP's attention. Trajectory features (bounding box locations, face directions) are computed over a window of T frames, and inter-frame person tracking IDs (or multi-object tracking probabilities) provide temporal edge weights. The output is a set of dynamic group assignments per frame (which people share a group at each timestep), pairwise groupness probabilities, and a derived static group assignment obtained by merging the dynamic results.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-part pipeline: (1) a groupness-augmented CLIP (GA-CLIP) image encoder, fine-tuned with three-class labels ('a group of people,' 'individual people,' 'occlusion') on circled-pair bounding boxes, which captures spatial scene context unavailable to per-person feature extractors; and (2) a temporal groupness graph that connects framewise pairwise groupness probabilities across consecutive frames via tracking IDs, then clusters the entire graph with the Louvain modularity-maximization algorithm. Trajectory features (framewise encoder + Transformer temporal encoder) are concatenated with GA-CLIP features and passed through a fully connected softmax layer to produce per-pair groupness probabilities. Unlike prior static methods that merge or average all frames into a single graph, preserving the temporal structure in the graph allows the Louvain algorithm to cut temporal edges where group membership changes, yielding dynamic group assignments without requiring pre-specified change points.

## Results

On the primary dynamic group detection task, the method (using Louvain clustering) achieves F1 of 0.769 on JRDB and 0.776 on Café, outperforming the best baselines (P-HAR+Louvain at 0.626 on JRDB and S3R2+CNM at 0.631 on Café) by margins of 0.143 and 0.145 respectively. On the secondary static group detection task, the method achieves F1 of 0.790 on JRDB and 0.819 on Café, surpassing the strongest baselines (P-HAR at 0.684 and S3R2 at 0.647) by 0.106 and 0.172. The method also achieves the highest precision (0.724 JRDB, 0.681 Café) and recall (0.820 JRDB, 0.904 Café) among all methods in dynamic detection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all six baselines across every metric on both datasets and both tasks (static and dynamic). Against the strongest baseline P-HAR [17], the method improves F1 by 0.106 on static JRDB (0.790 vs. 0.684) and by 0.143 on dynamic JRDB (0.769 vs. 0.626 with Louvain). Against S3R2 [22], the gains are 0.221 F1 on static JRDB (0.790 vs. 0.569), 0.172 on static Café (0.819 vs. 0.647), and 0.185 on dynamic JRDB (0.769 vs. 0.584 with LP). GroupTransformer [50] is the weakest recent baseline, with F1 dropping to 0.283 on dynamic Café (Louvain) and 0.366 on dynamic JRDB, where the method achieves 0.776 and 0.769 respectively. The older baselines JRDB-Group [14] (F1 0.384) and Dis.Mat+ [48] (F1 0.334) on static JRDB, as well as ARG [44] (F1 0.352), are all surpassed by large margins exceeding 0.4 F1. Notably, the method's advantage is most pronounced in dynamic detection, where baselines that assume static group structure degrade significantly when forced into a temporal graph framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the group detection line of work (S3R2, GroupTransformer, P-HAR) in two key directions: (1) replacing per-person bounding-box features with VLM-augmented pair-and-background features (GA-CLIP), building on CLIP's image encoder and the visual prompt engineering idea of circling regions of interest, and (2) formulating dynamic group detection via a temporal groupness graph clustered with the Louvain algorithm, departing from the static-group assumption that all prior video group detection methods rely on. The trajectory feature extraction follows GroupTransformer's encoder-decoder pipeline, while the graph-based clustering framework is shared with S3R2 and GroupTransformer, but the temporal connectivity of framewise graphs is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical limitation of all prior video group detection methods: the assumption that group membership is fixed throughout a video, which fails when people join or leave groups. The GA-CLIP approach demonstrates that VLM features capture higher-level spatial scene context (e.g., shared background objects, indirect interactions) that pure visual encoders like DINOv2 or ResNet miss, suggesting VLMs may be broadly useful for relational perception tasks. However, the authors acknowledge that their method still relies more on trajectory features than image features, and failure cases show persistent false positives from prolonged proximity, indicating that robust group detection in highly dynamic or crowded scenes remains open.
