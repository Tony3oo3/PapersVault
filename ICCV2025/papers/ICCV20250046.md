## Problem

Existing few-shot CLIP classification methods are benchmarked on standard datasets (StanfordCars, CUB, etc.) that CLIP has almost certainly seen during its large-scale pretraining, making the evaluation partially transductive rather than truly inductive. This inflates reported performance because the model retains class-specific knowledge from pretraining, masking the methods' actual ability to generalize from few-shot examples to genuinely unseen classes. The paper identifies that under a proper inductive evaluation, baseline methods drop by ~55% on average, revealing a significant gap in current few-shot generalization.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a pretrained CLIP model, a target dataset to unlearn, and a retain set of other datasets to preserve general knowledge. After unlearning, the few-shot methods receive k labeled support images per class (1–16 shots) and class-name text prompts (e.g., 'A photo of a {class}') to adapt the model, then produce classification logits over the m novel classes for each query image. The unlearned CLIP model has near-zero zero-shot accuracy on the target dataset while retaining >97% accuracy on held-out validation datasets, confirming that only the target class knowledge is removed.

## Method

The paper proposes two contributions: (1) an inductive benchmark pipeline that adapts Selective Synaptic Dampening (SSD) for CLIP, computing Fisher Information Matrices over forget and retain sets to selectively dampen parameters important for the target dataset while preserving general knowledge, and (2) SEPRES, a few-shot method that simultaneously modifies CLIP weights internally (via SEP's Token-Fusion Module integrating frozen tokens into learnable prompts) and at the output (via residual learnable parameters added to final textual embeddings, inspired by TaskRes). The residual design allows the model to leverage CLIP's pretrained semantic knowledge (e.g., understanding of animals) while learning genuinely new class-specific representations from few-shot examples. The pipeline is validated against oracle baselines where CLIP is trained from scratch on ImageNet excluding target subsets, confirming that unlearning approximates true inductive conditions. Across 5880 experiments spanning 7 datasets, 5 shot settings, 3 seeds, and 4 unlearning intensities, SEPRES consistently outperforms 13 baselines in both inductive and partially transductive settings.

## Results

In the proposed inductive (unlearned) setting, SEPRES achieves 56.43% average accuracy across 7 datasets and 5 shot settings (1–16 shots), substantially outperforming all 13 baselines. The next-best methods are SEP (21.49%), PromptSRC (20.10%), and CLIPLora (19.20%), while the weakest baselines (CLIPAdapter, CoCoOp, KgCoOp) score below 7%. Compared to the partially transductive setting, baselines drop by ~55% on average (e.g., CoOp: 71.4%→15.3%), whereas SEPRES drops only 19.4% (75.4%→56.4%), demonstrating robustness to unlearned class knowledge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEPRES dominates all 13 baselines in the inductive setting by a wide margin: it outperforms the second-best method SEP by 34.9 points (56.43% vs. 21.49%) and PromptSRC by 36.3 points (56.43% vs. 20.10%). Among the prompt-tuning baselines, CoOp (15.36%) and IVLP (15.85%) are the strongest, but both remain far below SEPRES. Adapter-based methods (CLIPAdapter at 4.43%, CoCoOp at 6.76%) and knowledge-guided methods (KgCoOp at 6.80%, MaPLe at 7.33%) perform worst, suggesting they rely heavily on pre-existing CLIP knowledge that is removed by unlearning. In the oracle setting (CLIP trained from scratch on ImageNet excluding a subset), SEPRES again leads all baselines (e.g., 16.95% on Dogs-unlearned vs. 6.91% for CLIPLora), confirming the inductive results are not an artifact of the unlearning procedure. Notably, method rankings flip between transductive and inductive settings (e.g., PromptSRC beats CLIPLora inductively but loses transductively), underscoring the paper's central claim that standard benchmarks mask true generalization ability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the extensive line of CLIP-based few-shot prompt-tuning and adapter methods (CoOp, CoCoOp, MaPLe, TaskRes, LoRA, SEP, etc.) but departs from them by questioning the evaluation protocol rather than proposing yet another prompt architecture. It repurposes Selective Synaptic Dampening (SSD), a machine unlearning technique originally designed for standard classifiers, and adapts it to CLIP's contrastive architecture via Fisher Information Matrix computation over image-text logits. The conceptual lineage connects to the inductive vs. transductive distinction in few-shot learning, but the key departure is recognizing that foundation models' opaque pretraining corpora make standard benchmarks implicitly transductive, and that unlearning is the practical mechanism to restore inductive validity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 55% average performance drop across 13 methods (5880 experiments, 7 datasets, 5 shot settings, 3 seeds) fundamentally reframes how the community should interpret existing few-shot CLIP results, and the ranking flips between settings mean method selection depends on whether the target classes are truly novel. The pipeline is general—any model with a reliable unlearning method can be converted to an inductive benchmark—making it a reusable evaluation tool beyond CLIP. Open questions include whether unlearning perfectly approximates 'never seen' (oracle validation is on ImageNet-trained CLIP, not the actual 400M-pair pretraining corpus), whether the approach extends to larger or architecturally different vision-language models, and whether the modest knowledge loss (≤2.5%) during unlearning could still introduce subtle biases that affect conclusions.
