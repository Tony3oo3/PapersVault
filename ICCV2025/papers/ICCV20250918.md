## Problem

Semi-supervised instance segmentation suffers from unreliable pseudo-labels in teacher-student frameworks because limited labeled data causes the teacher to under-segment, grouping multiple instances into a single mask. While SAM offers strong fine-grained localization, its class-agnostic design and tendency toward over-segmentation make direct integration into instance segmentation non-trivial, requiring a careful balance between under- and over-segmentation.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes RGB images (1024×2048 for Cityscapes, standard COCO resolution) and produces per-instance binary masks with class labels. During training, the teacher processes weakly augmented views to generate pseudo-labels (retaining predictions above a class confidence threshold of 0.7 and instance size threshold of 5), while the student learns from strongly augmented views. SAM 2 with a Hiera backbone is used as a frozen meta-teacher, prompted with stochastically sampled points to produce refined masks and structural features for distillation.

## Method

S⁴M integrates SAM into a Mask2Former-based teacher-student framework through three components. First, structural distillation (SD) transfers SAM's spatial understanding to the teacher by computing self-similarity matrices from SAM's decoder features (prompted with P randomly sampled points) and minimizing a Huber loss against the teacher's corresponding self-similarity matrix, improving localization without inheriting SAM's over-segmentation. Second, pseudo-label refinement (PR) stochastically samples K=3 points from the teacher's per-pixel mask probability distribution as prompts to SAM, producing higher-quality binary masks that reduce error propagation. Third, augmentation with refined pseudo-labels (ARP) bidirectionally pastes detected instances between paired images using the refined masks, introducing diverse spatial variations and potential occlusions for robustness. The SD loss is applied only during teacher pre-training, as applying it to both teacher and student stages degrades performance after the burn-in period.

## Results

On Cityscapes, S4M achieves mask-AP of 30.1, 33.3, 37.3, and 37.8 at 5%, 10%, 20%, and 30% label ratios, outperforming the previous best (Depth-Guided) by 6.9, 2.4, 3.2, and 1.1 AP points respectively. On COCO, S4M reaches 24.2, 28.1, and 32.1 AP at 1%, 2%, and 5% label ratios, setting new state-of-the-art results. The gains are most pronounced in the most label-scarce settings (5% Cityscapes, 1% COCO), where S4M improves over the prior best by 6.9 and 1.9 AP points, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S4M outperforms all five baselines on Cityscapes across every label ratio, with the largest margin over Depth-Guided (the prior SOTA) of +6.9 AP at 5% and +1.1 AP at 30%. On COCO, S4M achieves the best AP at 1% (24.2 vs. 22.3 for Depth-Guided), 2% (28.1 vs. 26.3), and 5% (32.1 vs. 31.5), but shows a slight 0.4–0.5 AP drop at 10% (34.6 vs. 35.0/35.1), which the authors attribute to stochasticity in pseudo-label refinement when labels are already high-quality. Compared to Guided Distillation [3], the primary architectural baseline, S4M gains 7.1 AP at 5% Cityscapes and 2.7 AP at 1% COCO. The older baselines (DataDistillation, NoisyBoundaries, PAIS) are consistently surpassed by 5–17 AP points, confirming that the SAM-integrated distillation and refinement pipeline provides substantial gains over prior semi-supervised instance segmentation approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Guided Distillation teacher-student framework for semi-supervised instance segmentation by introducing SAM as a meta-teacher through three complementary mechanisms: structural distillation of self-similarity matrices, pseudo-label refinement via point prompts, and copy-paste-style augmentation with refined masks. The structural distillation objective draws on relational knowledge distillation (RKD) literature but applies it to a frozen foundation model rather than a trained teacher, and the choice to distill decoder features (rather than encoder features) is motivated by SAM's prompt-conditioned architecture. The pseudo-label refinement and ARP augmentation build on FixMatch-style consistency and copy-paste augmentation, respectively, but adapt them to the instance-level granularity where naive center-point or bounding-box prompting to SAM fails.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6.9 AP gain at 5% Cityscapes and consistent SOTA across both benchmarks demonstrate that foundation-model knowledge can substantially alleviate the pseudo-label quality bottleneck that has limited semi-supervised instance segmentation. The careful balancing of SAM's over-segmentation tendency against the teacher's under-segmentation is a practical design insight likely to inform future work integrating class-agnostic foundation models into class-conditional tasks. Limitations include a slight performance drop at 10% COCO (attributed to refinement stochasticity when pseudo-labels are already high-quality) and the unresolved issue that applying structural distillation to both teacher and student stages degrades performance after burn-in, suggesting the interaction between distillation and consistency regularization is not yet fully understood.
