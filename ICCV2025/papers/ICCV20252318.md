## Problem

State-of-the-art SSL methods, particularly Transformer-based ones like Semi-ViT, assume balanced class distributions and suffer from training collapse or severe performance degradation on long-tailed imbalanced datasets with limited labeled data. Semi-ViT-Small, for instance, fails entirely at 1% labeled data, and even the 632M-parameter Semi-ViT-Huge underperforms on ImageNet-LT. The paper addresses both the architectural inefficiency of ViT backbones for SSL and the lack of stability-aware mechanisms to prevent pseudo-label-driven training collapse under label imbalance.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 224×224 RGB images, which are partitioned into patches and projected into a high-dimensional feature space before being processed by the bidirectional SSM. During training, each unlabeled sample receives both a weakly augmented view (processed by the teacher to produce a pseudo-label and per-class confidence score) and a strongly augmented view (used for the student's unsupervised loss). The final output is a class label prediction with associated confidence scores, where only pseudo-labels exceeding a threshold τ=0.7 are retained for student training.

## Method

Semi-ViM replaces the ViT backbone with Vision Mamba's bidirectional state space model, achieving subquadratic computational complexity and linear memory scaling while maintaining comparable representational power. Two key mechanisms address training instability and generalization: LyapEMA dynamically adjusts the EMA teacher decay factor via a sigmoid of the student-teacher parameter discrepancy (a Lyapunov candidate function), and adds a Lyapunov regularization term to the student update to guarantee monotonic decrease of the student-teacher gap; SSMixup extends mixup beyond the input level by interpolating the forward and backward hidden states of the bidirectional SSM using confidence-driven weights, injecting the mixed hidden state as a residual into the output. Training follows a three-stage pipeline: autoregressive Mamba (ARM) self-supervised pre-training, supervised fine-tuning on labeled data, and SSL with LyapEMA and SSMixup, where the total loss combines cross-entropy on labeled samples with thresholded pseudo-label cross-entropy on strongly augmented unlabeled samples.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, Semi-ViM-Base (146M params) achieves 85.40% top-1 accuracy with only 10% of labeled data, surpassing Semi-ViT-Huge (632M params) at 84.27% and REACT (651M) at 84.74%. On the long-tailed ImageNet-LT, Semi-ViM-Base reaches 77.40% top-1 accuracy at 10% labeled data and 66.30% at 1% labeled data, outperforming Semi-ViT-Huge by 3.9% and 7.4% respectively. With only 1% of labeled data on ImageNet-LT, Semi-ViM-Base achieves 83.07% top-5 accuracy, exceeding Semi-ViT-Huge (76.90%) by 6.17% and CNN-based methods like FixMatch and PAWS by over 15%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Semi-ViM-Base (146M) outperforms all Transformer-based baselines despite having roughly 4× fewer parameters than Semi-ViT-Huge (632M) and REACT (651M): it leads by 1.13% on ImageNet-1K at 10% labels (85.40 vs 84.27) and by 3.9% on ImageNet-LT at 10% labels (77.40 vs 73.50). The gap widens dramatically on the long-tailed ImageNet-LT at 1% labels, where Semi-ViM-Base achieves 66.30% versus 58.90% for Semi-ViT-Huge and 59.36% for REACT, a margin of over 7%. Against CNN-based methods, the advantage is even larger: Semi-ViM-Base exceeds SimCLRv2 (ResNet-152, 28M) by 5.5% on ImageNet-LT at 10% labels and by 17.75% on top-5 accuracy at 1% labels. Even the smallest variant, Semi-ViM-Small (27M), surpasses Semi-ViT-Base (86M) on ImageNet-LT at 1% labels (63.24 vs 53.20), suggesting the bidirectional SSM backbone is more sample-efficient than ViT under extreme label scarcity. On ImageNet-1K at 5% labels, Semi-ViM-Base (83.25%) slightly trails MCT (85.20%) and REACT (83.23%), but leads all other methods; the co-training baselines (Co-Training, MCT) remain competitive on ImageNet-1K but fall behind on ImageNet-LT, where Semi-ViM-Base leads MCT by 2.1% at 10% labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

Semi-ViM transplants the FixMatch-style teacher-student SSL paradigm (weak/strong augmentation, confidence-thresholded pseudo-labeling) from the Transformer backbone (Semi-ViT) to a bidirectional State Space Model backbone (Vision Mamba), and replaces the standard EMA teacher update with LyapEMA, an adaptive decay mechanism motivated by Lyapunov stability theory. The SSMixup component extends mixup from the input level to the hidden-state level of the bidirectional SSM, a design choice specific to the sequential recurrence structure of Mamba. For pre-training, the paper substitutes MAE and DINO-V2 with Autoregressive Mamba (ARM), arguing that autoregressive token prediction aligns more naturally with SSM's sequential state transitions than masked reconstruction or contrastive alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a 146M-parameter SSM-based SSL model can match or exceed a 632M-parameter ViT-based SSL model (Semi-ViT-Huge) on both balanced and long-tailed ImageNet, suggesting SSMs are a credible, more parameter-efficient backbone for SSL. The LyapEMA mechanism addresses a real and under-studied failure mode—training collapse with very few labeled samples—that plagues EMA-based SSL, and the adaptive sigmoid-of-distance decay is a simple, drop-in fix. Open questions include whether the Lyapunov framing provides genuine theoretical guarantees beyond 'adaptive EMA,' whether SSMixup's hidden-state mixing generalizes to other SSM variants (e.g., selective SSMs like S4D), and whether the ARM pre-training advantage persists at larger scales or on non-ImageNet benchmarks.
