## Problem

Streaming video understanding requires frame-by-frame processing with causal access to history, but existing video backbones rely on bidirectional attention over fixed clips, making them unsuitable for real-time inference. Recent streaming approaches that pipe frame features into LLMs lose fine-grained spatial and temporal details during coarse aggregation. The paper asks how to build a single streaming video backbone that preserves both spatial precision and temporal dynamics while operating under strict causality constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252046/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a streaming video as a sequence of RGB frames (224×224, patch size 16, 16 frames sampled by default) processed sequentially with causal access, alongside a text prompt or free-form query encoded by SigLIP's text encoder. It produces three granularities of visual features in a shared visual-text space: a global video vector (last-frame feature), per-frame temporal vectors (attention-pooled over spatial patches), and full spatiotemporal patch embeddings. Downstream, these features feed task-specific heads to yield per-frame action scores, pixel-level segmentation masks, or free-form text answers via an LLM.

## Method

StreamFormer converts a pre-trained SigLIP image ViT into a streaming video model by inserting causal temporal attention (a divided space-time attention block where the temporal dimension uses a causal mask so each frame attends only to past frames) and applying LoRA (rank 32) to the spatial attention layers to preserve image representation quality while enabling video-specific adaptation. A learnable tanh gate initialized to zero on the temporal attention ensures the model starts as a pure image encoder and gradually learns temporal dependencies. Training unifies six sub-tasks—action recognition, video-text retrieval, temporal action localization, temporal video grounding, video object segmentation, and referring video object segmentation—into a single visual-language alignment objective using sigmoid contrastive loss at global, temporal, and spatial granularities respectively. This multitask formulation lets the backbone simultaneously capture global semantics, per-frame temporal dynamics, and pixel-level spatial structure from only ~1M annotated video-text pairs, avoiding the need for massive video-text contrastive pre-training.

## Results

On Online Action Detection (THUMOS-14), StreamFormer achieves 73.9 mAP with optical flow and 68.4 mAP without flow, improving over the SigLIP ViT baseline (MAT) by +2.8 and +9.5 mAP respectively. On Online Video Instance Segmentation (YouTubeVIS-2019), StreamFormer reaches 45.1 AP and 43.6 AR1, outperforming the SigLIP ViT backbone (40.9 AP, 41.8 AR1) by +4.2 AP under identical training settings. On VideoQA, StreamFormer (224² resolution) scores 45.0 on VideoMME and 48.4 on MLVU, surpassing all other 224²-resolution methods and approaching the 336²-resolution VideoLLaMA2 (47.9/48.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Online Action Detection, StreamFormer (73.9 mAP w/ flow) outperforms all prior OAD methods including GateHUB+TimeSformer (72.5), TesTra (71.2), MAT+ResNet50 (71.6), and LSTR+TimeSformer (69.6); the most notable gain is the +9.5 mAP improvement over the SigLIP ViT backbone without optical flow (68.4 vs. 58.9), demonstrating that causal temporal attention captures motion cues that a static image encoder misses. On Online VIS, StreamFormer (45.1 AP) beats the SigLIP ViT backbone (40.9 AP) and ResNet50 without COCO pre-training (43.6 AP) under the same CTVIS protocol, but remains below methods using higher resolution (480p DVIS++: 55.5) or COCO-initialized mask heads (IDOL: 49.5, MinVIS: 47.4). On VideoQA, StreamFormer at 224² resolution (45.0 VideoMME, 48.4 MLVU) surpasses LLaMA-VID (25.9/18.1), Video-LLaVA (39.9/18.8), Video-Chat2 (42.3/44.5), and LLaVA-NeXT-Video (41.0/36.9), but trails the higher-resolution VideoLLaMA2 (47.9/48.4) on VideoMME while tying on MLVU. Overall, StreamFormer consistently wins against the SigLIP ViT backbone across all three tasks, confirming that the causal temporal attention and multitask training add meaningful spatiotemporal understanding beyond the image pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

StreamFormer builds directly on the divided space-time attention architecture of TimeSformer (Bertasius et al., ICML 2021) and the pre-trained SigLIP image encoder (Zhai et al., ICCV 2023), introducing two key modifications: causal masking on the temporal attention axis and LoRA-based spatial adaptation. It departs from the dominant video-language pre-training paradigm (e.g., InternVideo, VideoPrism) that relies on 50M+ video-text pairs for global contrastive learning, instead leveraging ~1M densely annotated video data across multiple granularities in a unified multitask visual-language alignment framework. It also contrasts with recent streaming approaches that delegate temporal aggregation to LLMs (e.g., Flash-VStream, VideoLLM-Online), arguing that modeling temporal dependencies at the backbone level preserves fine-grained spatial-temporal details that coarse frame-level features lose.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The causal temporal attention with KV-Cache support enables near-constant per-frame inference latency regardless of video length, which is a critical practical property for real-time applications in robotics and autonomous driving. The data efficiency finding—that 0.1M annotated pairs outperform 1M WebVid contrastive pairs—suggests a viable path to training strong video backbones without prohibitively expensive large-scale video-text corpora. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much longer videos (beyond the 4096-frame evaluation), whether the multitask framework generalizes to additional task types (e.g., video generation, dense prediction), and whether the modest gap between causal and bi-directional attention (62.5 vs. 62.8 mAP) widens or narrows at larger model scales.
