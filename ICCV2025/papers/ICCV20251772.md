## Problem

Existing text-to-motion generation methods are restricted to a single standardized skeleton, forcing practitioners to rely on a technically demanding and often unreliable retargeting pipeline to adapt motions to characters with different body proportions. This creates a significant gap between research capabilities and real-world production needs where diverse skeletal configurations are routine.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251772/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language text description and a target skeleton specification (joint topology graph plus static bone-length properties, easily obtained from any character rig) as input, and produces a temporally coherent 3D motion sequence (20 FPS, up to 10 seconds) whose joint features include relative positions, 6D rotations, velocities, and root displacement. A key output guarantee is zero bone length error: the generated motion is physically consistent with the target skeleton's proportions, unlike position-based restoration which introduces bone stretching or rotation-based restoration which introduces jitter.

## Method

OmniSkel employs an asymmetric Skeleton-aware Residual VQ-VAE (SR-VAE) in which a Kinematic Graph Cross Attention (K-GCA) module fuses motion and skeleton features via cross-modal attention masked by the skeleton adjacency matrix, enabling the encoder to extract skeleton-invariant motion tokens while the decoder reconstructs motion for an arbitrary target skeleton. The token hierarchy is generated by a two-stage transformer pipeline: a bidirectional Masked Transformer predicts base-layer tokens from text, and a Residual Transformer sequentially refines higher-layer tokens conditioned on text and all prior layers. At inference, a training-free Motion Restoration Optimizer (MRO) post-processes the decoded motion by extracting per-bone unit direction vectors and re-projecting them onto the exact target bone lengths, simultaneously guaranteeing zero skeletal error and preserving trajectory smoothness. The entire system is trained on the newly constructed SkeleMotion-3D dataset (43,848 text-motion-skeleton triplets across 29,233 skeletal configurations), which provides the diversity needed for robust cross-skeleton generalization.

## Results

On the HumanML3D test set (standard skeleton), OmniSkel achieves FID 0.084 and R-Precision 0.507/0.699/0.797 (Top-1/2/3), outperforming all baselines except MoMask (FID 0.045, R-Precision 0.521/0.713/0.807). The SR-VAE reconstruction component alone achieves FID 0.027 with zero skeleton error after MRO post-processing. In the ablation study (Table 1), removing K-GCA increases jitter error from 1.824 to 9.594, and replacing MRO with rotation-based restoration inflates jitter from 2.084 to 30.724 while both achieve zero skeleton error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniSkel (FID 0.084) outperforms all baselines except MoMask on FID, beating ReMoDiffuse (0.103), AttT2M (0.112), T2M-GPT (0.141), MLD (0.473), MDM (0.544), MotionDiffuse (0.630), T2M (1.087), and TM2T (1.501). On R-Precision, MoMask retains a slight edge (Top-1: 0.521 vs 0.507; Top-2: 0.713 vs 0.699), while OmniSkel matches or exceeds ReMoDiffuse (0.510/0.698/0.795) and all earlier methods. The critical distinction is that OmniSkel is the only method capable of generating motions for arbitrary skeletal configurations without retargeting; all nine baselines are architecturally restricted to a single fixed skeleton and cannot be trained or evaluated on the SkeleMotion-3D dataset. In the ablation, MRO achieves zero skeleton error with jitter 2.084, dramatically outperforming rotation-based restoration (jitter 30.724) while matching its skeleton accuracy. Computationally, OmniSkel uses fewer parameters (38.73M vs 44.85M for MoMask) with comparable inference time (0.099s vs 0.075s) and slightly lower GPU memory (1402M vs 1434M).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-to-motion generation lineage (T2M, MDM, T2M-GPT, MoMask) by introducing skeleton-awareness as a first-class input, departing from the assumption that all methods target a single standardized skeleton. Architecturally, it builds directly on MoMask's masked-transformer + residual-quantization pipeline and T2M-GPT's residual block design, while replacing standard self-attention with a cross-modal Kinematic Graph Cross Attention (K-GCA) that extends GAT to fuse motion and skeletal features. Conceptually, it subsumes the separate motion retargeting literature (Gleicher, SAME, SMT) by making retargeting unnecessary—skeleton adaptation is handled natively within the VAE decoder rather than as a post-hoc correction step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of the retargeting stage addresses a genuine production bottleneck in animation and game pipelines, and the SkeleMotion-3D dataset (43,848 text-motion-skeleton triplets) provides a much-needed resource for skeleton-agnostic research. However, the evaluation is limited to human skeletons with varying proportions; generalization to stylized, non-humanoid, or dramatically different topologies remains untested. Open questions include whether the K-GCA mechanism scales to skeletons with different joint counts or hierarchical structures, and whether the training-free MRO can handle extreme proportion differences without quality degradation. The modest FID gap (0.084 vs. MoMask's 0.045) on the standard skeleton suggests a small quality cost for the added flexibility, which is a reasonable trade-off but worth monitoring as the field matures.
