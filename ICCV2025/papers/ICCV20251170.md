## Problem

Existing remote sensing instance segmentation models are constrained to close-vocabulary prediction, limiting their ability to recognize novel categories or generalize across datasets. While open-vocabulary segmentation methods have advanced for natural images, their direct application to aerial imagery fails due to diverse landscapes, seasonal variations, small or ambiguous objects, and the lack of domain-specific adaptability in frozen CLIP-based classifiers. This paper formalizes open-vocabulary remote sensing instance segmentation as a new task and addresses the gap between general-domain vision-language models and the unique challenges of aerial imagery.

## Input / Output

The method takes a remote sensing RGB image (resized to 512×512) and a set of category names formatted into prompt templates (e.g., 'satellite imagery of .', 'aerial imagery of .') as input. It outputs a set of pixel-wise instance masks paired with aerial category labels. A key constraint is the open-vocabulary setting: the model is trained on a predefined category set C_train and evaluated on a disjoint set C_test, where only the category names of C_test are available at inference time, requiring cross-dataset generalization without retraining.

## Method

SCORE integrates a three-branch architecture: a context branch (frozen RemoteCLIP ViT-L/14) extracts multi-granularity scene context via [CLS] tokens and patch embeddings; a semantic branch (frozen CLIP text encoder) encodes category names into text embeddings; and an instance branch (frozen ConvNeXt-Large CLIP backbone + Mask2Former proposal generator) produces class embeddings and mask proposals. The key innovation is Region-Aware Integration, which adaptively dilates predicted masks using a learnable kernel size, pools RemoteCLIP patch features within the expanded regions to obtain regional context, and injects this into class embeddings through sequential Transformer layers, exploiting the correlation between aerial objects and their surroundings. Complementary to this, Global Context Adaptation injects the RemoteCLIP [CLS] token into text embeddings via multi-head cross-attention (global context as query, text embeddings as key and value), enriching the frozen classifier with domain-specific visual priors while preserving its open-vocabulary generalization. At inference, final predictions combine in-vocabulary scores (region-aware class embeddings matched against domain-adapted text embeddings) with out-vocabulary scores from the general CLIP backbone.

## Results

SCORE achieves an average mAP of 38.68 when trained on iSAID and evaluated cross-dataset on NWPU-VHR-10, SOTA, FAST, and SIOR, and 32.59 when trained on SIOR and evaluated on NWPU-VHR-10, SOTA, FAST, and iSAID. This represents an average improvement of 5.53% and 4.32% over the strongest baseline (ZoRI) in the two training settings, respectively. The largest single-dataset gain reaches 12.55% mAP (e.g., 42.57 vs. 30.02 on SOTA when trained on iSAID). All experiments use mean Average Precision (mAP) as the evaluation metric for open-vocabulary instance segmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCORE outperforms all four baselines across every test dataset in both training settings. Against the strongest competitor ZoRI (AAAI'25), SCORE gains +5.93 mAP on average when trained on iSAID (38.68 vs. 32.75) and +4.32 mAP when trained on SIOR (32.59 vs. 28.27). FC-CLIP (NeurIPS'23) is the second-strongest baseline, with SCORE improving by +5.44 mAP (iSAID training) and +4.73 mAP (SIOR training) on average. ODISE (CVPR'22) and MAFT+ (ECCV'24) are substantially weaker, trailing SCORE by over 20 mAP in most settings, indicating that general-purpose OV segmentation methods struggle significantly with remote sensing imagery. The largest individual gains appear on SOTA (+12.55 mAP over ZoRI when trained on iSAID) and NWPU-VHR-10 (+7.51 mAP over ZoRI when trained on iSAID), suggesting that scene-context integration is particularly beneficial for datasets with diverse object scales and ambiguous categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the open-vocabulary segmentation lineage (MaskCLIP, ODISE, FC-CLIP, MAFT+) from natural images to remote sensing, but departs from prior OV remote sensing work (OVRS, GSNet) which was limited to semantic segmentation. The instance branch directly adopts Mask2Former's query-based proposal generator, while the context branch repurposes remote sensing-specific CLIPs (RemoteCLIP, SkyCLIP, GeoRSCLIP) as frozen feature extractors for multi-granularity scene context. The key conceptual departure is the explicit integration of regional and global scene context into both visual (class embeddings) and textual (classifier) representations, motivated by the strong object-environment correlations unique to aerial imagery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formally define and benchmark open-vocabulary remote sensing instance segmentation, establishing cross-dataset evaluation protocols across four aerial datasets. The finding that domain-specific RS CLIPs consistently underperform general CLIP for OV classification—despite superior scene context extraction—highlights a fundamental tension between domain specialization and open-vocabulary generalization that the field must resolve. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-resolution or multi-spectral imagery, whether the frozen CLIP text encoder bottleneck can be overcome with larger or RS-specific language models, and whether the scene-context paradigm transfers to other geospatial tasks like change detection or 3D reconstruction.
