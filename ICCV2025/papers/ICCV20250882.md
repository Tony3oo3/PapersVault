## Problem

Semi-supervised semantic segmentation (SSSS) relies on self-training to exploit unlabeled data, but the pseudo-labels generated by the model itself are noisy in early training. Foundational segmentation models like SEEM (a SAM variant with text prompts) could serve as external annotators, yet directly using their predictions as supervision actually degrades performance due to the domain gap between pre-training and target data. The paper asks how to reliably extract useful supervision from a foundation model in a target domain with only limited labeled data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250882/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images in two roles: a small labeled set (e.g., 92–732 images on PASCAL VOC) used both for supervised training and as the conformal calibration set, and a large unlabeled set for which SEEM generates K per-class probability maps via text prompts (one class name per channel). The CP calibration produces a spatially varying threshold map q̂_α ∈ R^{H×W}, and the final output is a trained SegFormer-B5 segmentation model evaluated by mIoU. Pseudo-labels are assigned only to pixels whose SEEM prediction falls within the conformal prediction set; all other pixels are left unlabelled (NaN), so the supervised loss is computed over a variable subset of pixels per image.

## Method

ConformalSAM is a two-stage framework. In Stage I, the pre-trained SEEM model is calibrated on the labeled set: for each pixel location, non-conformity scores (1 − SEEM probability of the ground-truth class) are collected across all labeled images, and the (1−α)-quantile (α=0.05) yields a per-pixel threshold. Unlabeled images are then passed through SEEM, and a pixel receives a pseudo-label only if its predicted class score exceeds the threshold; a class-conditional rule further prefers object classes over the dominant background class to avoid background saturation. In Stage II, SEEM-generated masks are discarded entirely and the model switches to standard self-training using its own pseudo-labels, with a dynamically decaying weight λ(t) on the unsupervised loss to prevent overfitting to residual noise. The framework is modular: Stage I's calibrated pseudo-labels can replace the model-generated pseudo-labels in any existing SSSS pipeline (e.g., AllSpark), and the CP calibration is black-box, requiring no gradient access to the foundation model.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC 2012 (original), ConformalSAM achieves 81.21 mIoU with 1/16 (92) labeled images and 83.85 mIoU with full labels, outperforming the strongest baseline AllSpark by 5.14 mIoU at the 1/16 split and 1.73 mIoU at full labels. On ADE20K, ConformalSAM reaches 26.21 mIoU at 1/128 (158) labeled images and 36.25 mIoU at 1/8 (2526), surpassing AllSpark by up to 10.04 mIoU at the most label-scarce setting. Across all three standard SSSS benchmarks, ConformalSAM outperforms AllSpark by an average of 3.37 mIoU, and when integrated as a plug-in (ConformalSAM(AllSpark)), it further boosts AllSpark's average performance by 2.07 mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConformalSAM consistently outperforms all 18 compared baselines across PASCAL VOC 2012 (original and augmented) and ADE20K. Against the strongest recent baseline AllSpark [34], ConformalSAM gains +5.14 mIoU at 1/16 labels, +3.81 at 1/8, +2.07 at 1/4, +2.77 at 1/2, and +1.73 at full labels on VOC original; on ADE20K the gap widens to +10.04 mIoU at 1/128 and +4.15 at 1/8. Compared to other CVPR/NeurIPS 2024 methods (DDFP, RankMatch, CorrMatch), ConformalSAM leads by 2–6 mIoU depending on the label budget, with the largest margins at the most label-scarce splits. The plug-in variant ConformalSAM(AllSpark) also surpasses standalone AllSpark by an average of 2.46 mIoU on VOC original, confirming that the CP-calibrated SEEM pseudo-labels provide complementary supervision even when combined with a strong self-training framework. On the augmented VOC set, ConformalSAM achieves 80.09–81.43 mIoU, beating AllSpark (78.32–81.14) by 0.91 mIoU on average, with the largest gain (+1.92) at 1/16 labels. The advantage is most pronounced under extreme label scarcity (1/16 and 1/128 splits), where the foundation model's generalization knowledge is most valuable, and narrows as labeled data increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two research threads: semi-supervised semantic segmentation (SSSS) via self-training (CPS, UniMatch, AllSpark) and conformal prediction (CP) for uncertainty quantification in segmentation (Kandinsky CP). It departs from prior SAM-based works (S2C, WS-SAM, CPC-SAM, SemiSAM) that focus on engineering better prompts for the foundation model, instead using SEEM's raw textual-prompt outputs directly as pseudo-labels. The key conceptual departure is treating the foundation model as a black-box annotator whose outputs must be calibrated via CP before being trusted as supervision, combined with a two-stage curriculum (foundation-assisted → self-reliance) that has no direct analogue in prior SSSS literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that foundation models can serve as effective annotators in SSSS only when their outputs are uncertainty-calibrated, and that naive use of foundation-model masks actively degrades performance—a cautionary finding for the broader trend of leveraging pre-trained models in low-data regimes. The plug-in design (ConformalSAM(AllSpark)) makes the approach broadly applicable to existing SSSS pipelines. A key limitation is that effectiveness depends on overlap between the foundation model's pre-training distribution and the target domain; for datasets with many novel categories (ADE20K, Cityscapes), gains shrink. Open questions include whether the CP calibration strategy transfers to other foundation model families (e.g., LLMs for structured prediction) and whether the two-stage transition point can be learned rather than hand-tuned.
