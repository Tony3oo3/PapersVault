## Problem

Existing SVG generation methods either produce oversimplified single-layer outputs or optimization-induced shape redundancies, and none capture the designer's cognitive process—logical sequencing, spatial reasoning, and element grouping—during layer construction. This results in AI-generated SVGs that resemble fragmented collages rather than intentionally editable professional designs with clear hierarchical structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250964/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-SVG, the model takes a natural language prompt and generates a 2×2 or 3×3 grid (1024×1024 or 1056×1056) of sequential raster frames representing progressive design steps, which are then vectorized into a layered SVG with non-overlapping paths and independent editable layers. For image vectorization, a reference raster image is VAE-encoded into latent tokens and appended to the denoising sequence, guiding the model to reverse-engineer plausible layer construction steps. The final SVG output preserves global structure while isolating per-layer changes as separate vector paths.

## Method

LayerTracer fine-tunes a FLUX.1 DiT via LoRA (rank 256) on a novel 20,000+ sample dataset of designer creation sequences, using a serpentine grid layout that places temporally adjacent frames spatially adjacent to exploit the DiT's local-attention bias for sequence coherence. Training uses conditional flow matching loss. For image-conditioned vectorization, the reference image's VAE latent is concatenated to the denoising tokens and processed through multi-modal attention, enabling the model to predict earlier construction frames from the final image. The generated raster sequences are converted to SVG via differential analysis between adjacent grid cells (grayscale conversion, absolute difference, binary thresholding, morphological refinement) followed by vtracer Bézier optimization to eliminate redundant paths.

## Results

On the text-to-SVG generation task, LayerTracer achieves a CLIP-Score of 33.76, surpassing the best baseline SVGDreamer (32.68) by over 1 point, while reducing inference time from 3,374–5,715 s to just 27 s and path count from 128–512 to 35.39. On the layer-wise vectorization task, LayerTracer attains the lowest MSE (1.96×10⁻⁴) versus 2.01–5.21×10⁻⁴ for baselines, with the fewest paths (29.98 vs. 46–256) and fastest runtime (34 s vs. 393–3,147 s). All baselines except LIVE and O&R fail to produce layered outputs at all.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LayerTracer outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric in both tasks. In text-to-SVG generation, it beats VecFusion [17] (CLIP 31.10, 128 paths, 4668 s), SVGDreamer [56] (CLIP 32.68, 512 paths, 5715 s), and DiffSketcher [55] (CLIP 31.47, 512 paths, 3374 s) on CLIP-Score, path count, and runtime simultaneously, with a ~125× speedup over the fastest baseline. In layer-wise vectorization, it edges out O&R [13] (MSE 2.01×10⁻⁴, 64 paths, 612 s) and DiffVG [19] (MSE 2.02×10⁻⁴, 256 paths, 393 s) on reconstruction fidelity while using roughly 2× fewer paths and 10–18× less time. Against LIVE [23], the only other layer-wise baseline, LayerTracer improves MSE by 62% (1.96 vs. 5.21×10⁻⁴) and cuts runtime from 3,147 s to 34 s. Notably, VecFusion, SVGDreamer, DiffSketcher, and DiffVG do not produce layered outputs at all, a structural limitation LayerTracer addresses natively.

## Relation to Prior Work

LayerTracer builds on the DiT/FLUX.1 architecture and LoRA fine-tuning paradigm, but departs fundamentally from prior SVG generation methods (VecFusion, SVGDreamer, DiffSketcher) that rely on iterative SDS optimization over differentiable rasterizers. Conceptually, it extends ProcessPainter's idea of learning creative processes from sequence data, repurposing DiT's in-context learning to generate the *construction sequence* of an SVG rather than a final image. For vectorization, it reframes the task (previously handled by optimization-based methods like LIVE and O&R) as a conditional denoising problem where a reference image guides the model to 'reverse-engineer' layer assembly steps, eliminating the need for per-sample gradient optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: AI-generated SVGs have lacked the hierarchical, layer-organized structure that professional designers expect, making outputs difficult to edit. The released LayerSVG dataset (20K+ designer process traces) fills a critical data scarcity that has constrained the field. However, the method still depends on Vtracer for final path extraction (inheriting its hyperparameter sensitivity and OOD fragility), and the evaluation baselines are primarily slow optimization-based methods rather than other neural generators, leaving open whether the cognitive-alignment advantage persists against faster neural alternatives. The serpentine layout finding—that spatial arrangement of temporally adjacent frames in a grid significantly affects sequence coherence—may generalize to other multi-step visual generation tasks.
