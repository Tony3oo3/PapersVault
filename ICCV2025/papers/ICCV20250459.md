## Problem

Closely-spaced infrared small targets (CSIST) in dense clusters produce overlapping blob-like spots that obscure target count, sub-pixel positions, and radiation intensities, yet deep learning has never been applied to this unmixing task. The gap stems from the difficulty of separating superimposed point-source signals and the complete absence of benchmark datasets, task-specific metrics, and open-source implementations for this specialized problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250459/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an 11×11-pixel infrared image containing 1–5 overlapping point-source targets (each modeled by a 2D Gaussian PSF with σ_PSF = 0.5 px, intensities 220–250 units, separated by ≥0.52 Rayleigh units) along with a precomputed steering matrix G(Ω) that maps L = UVn² sub-pixel locations to pixel measurements. The output is a high-resolution unmixed grid at c×c sub-pixel division (c = 3, 5, or 7), where non-zero entries directly encode target count, sub-pixel coordinates, and radiation intensities; post-processing applies an intensity threshold of 50 and projects coordinates back to the original 11×11 space for CSO-mAP evaluation.

## Method

DISTA-Net reformulates the ℓ₁-regularized sparse reconstruction (solved by ISTA) into a 6-stage deep unfolding network with two key dynamic mechanisms. First, a dual-branch Dynamic Transform module F_d generates an adaptive convolutional kernel from the previous sparse estimate via a fully connected network, applies it to the gradient-step output r^(k), and blends this with a standard Conv-ReLU-Conv branch through a learned coefficient α. Second, a Dynamic Thresholding Generator (DTG) produces spatially-varying soft-thresholding parameters θ_d by extracting multi-scale features through parallel 3×3 convolutions, computing average and max pooling for spatial attention masks, and combining them via a final convolution. Each stage follows the sequence: gradient descent step → dynamic transform → dynamic soft-thresholding → left-inverse transform (F̃∘F_d = I without structural symmetry). Training is supervised with an MSE reconstruction loss plus a multi-stage identity constraint (γ = 0.01) enforcing F̃^(k)∘F_d^(k) ≈ I at every stage.

## Results

On the CSIST-100K benchmark, DISTA-Net achieves a CSO-mAP of 46.74%, with AP-20 of 86.18% and AP-25 of 97.14%, outperforming all 17 baselines across traditional optimization, image super-resolution, and deep unfolding categories. It also attains the best reconstruction quality with PSNR of 37.87 dB and SSIM of 99.79, while maintaining moderate computational cost (2.179M parameters, 35.10G FLOPs). The ablation confirms both the Dynamic Transform and Dynamic Soft-Thresholding modules contribute, with removing the latter causing the largest mAP drop (46.74 → 46.17).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DISTA-Net outperforms all 17 baselines on the primary CSO-mAP metric (46.74%), with the closest competitor being ISTA-Net+ (46.06%) and SRFBN (46.05%). On the stricter AP-20 threshold, DISTA-Net leads by a clear margin (86.18% vs. 85.71% for EGASR, the best SR method, and 84.46% for ISTA-Net+), and on AP-25 it reaches 97.14% versus 96.57% (SAN) and 96.43% (RDN). Against traditional optimization methods (ISTA, LISTA, LAMP, LIHT, TiLISTA), the improvement is dramatic—mAP jumps from 7–30% to 46.74%—confirming that deep unfolding with dynamic adaptation is essential for this task. In terms of reconstruction quality, DISTA-Net achieves the highest PSNR (37.87 dB) and SSIM (99.79%), surpassing the best SR method (SAN: 36.50 dB / 99.74%) and the best deep unfolding method (ISTA-Net+: 36.09 dB / 99.72%). Notably, DISTA-Net achieves these gains with only 2.179M parameters and 35.10G FLOPs, far lighter than HAN (64.342M / 495G) and RDN (22.306M / 173G), while being slightly heavier than ISTA-Net+ (0.337M / 24.33G) due to the dynamic transform and thresholding modules.

## Relation to Prior Work

DISTA-Net extends the deep unfolding lineage from ISTA and ISTA-Net by replacing their static proximal-mapping parameters (fixed convolution weights and a single learnable threshold) with input-adaptive, dynamically generated counterparts. Conceptually, it departs from the broader image super-resolution literature by reframing the task as sparse reconstruction over a discretized sub-pixel grid rather than pixel-space upscaling, and from traditional optimization-based CSIST methods (e.g., second-order cone programming with ℓ₁ regularization) by learning the entire iterative process end-to-end. The paper also carves out a new application niche: while deep unfolding has been applied to MRI, deblurring, and ultrasound, its use for infrared small-target unmixing had not been explored, and the authors position this as the first such attempt.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primary practical contribution is the establishment of the first open-source ecosystem for CSIST unmixing—CSIST-100K dataset, CSO-mAP metric, and GrokCSO toolkit—which removes the infrastructure barrier that previously prevented deep learning research in this domain. However, the methodological gains over strong baselines (ISTA-Net, SRFBN) are modest (≈1.5 CSO-mAP points), and the evaluation is confined to 11×11-pixel simulated scenes with 1–5 targets under a single Gaussian PSF, leaving open whether the dynamic adaptation advantage persists on real sensor data, larger focal planes, or more complex atmospheric PSF models. A key open question is whether the sub-pixel unmixing formulation generalizes to multi-spectral or temporal infrared sequences where target motion and background clutter further complicate the steering matrix.
