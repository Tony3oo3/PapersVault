## Problem

Existing speech-driven 3D facial animation methods cannot capture individual speaking styles (e.g., mouth opening amplitude, pouting extent) without requiring identity class labels or additional 3D facial mesh sequences at inference time. One-hot encoding approaches fail entirely on unseen speakers, while reference-mesh methods are impractical for real-world deployment. The paper asks whether speaking style can be inferred from audio alone to produce personalized 3D facial animation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251653/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw audio signal as its sole input at both training and inference, with no identity labels, reference meshes, or other priors required. It produces a per-frame 3D vertex displacement sequence over a neutral-face mesh template (FLAME topology with 5,023 vertices on VOCASET at 60 fps; 23,370 vertices on BIWI at 25 fps). Internally, the audio is consumed twice: once through a frozen HuBERT encoder to extract frame-aligned text representations, and once as a mel-spectrogram to extract a global speaking-style feature vector.

## Method

MemoryTalker uses a two-stage training strategy built around a key-value motion memory with n slots. In stage 1 (Memorizing), facial motion features are stored into the memory and a KL-divergence alignment loss forces HuBERT-derived text representations to address the same slots as the motion features, so that a single phoneme maps to consistent general motion across speakers. In stage 2 (Animating), a style encoder trained with a triplet loss on mel-spectrograms produces a speaker-specific style vector that modulates each memory slot via sigmoid-gated weights, yielding a stylized motion memory that amplifies or suppresses particular motion patterns per speaker. A Transformer decoder then synthesizes the final 3D motion from the concatenation of the text representation and the recalled (stylized) motion feature. The entire pipeline requires only audio at inference, achieving ~120 fps on a single A6000 GPU with 94M parameters.

## Results

On VOCASET, MemoryTalker achieves the best scores across all five metrics: FVE 0.506×10⁻⁶, LVE 0.293×10⁻⁵, FID 3.045×10⁻¹, LDTW 0.418×10⁻⁵, and Lip-max 0.331×10⁻⁴, outperforming the strongest baseline UniTalker by 11.2% on FVE and 23.3% on LVE. On BIWI, it similarly leads on every metric (FVE 0.901×10⁻⁴, LVE 0.187×10⁻⁴, FID 7.202×10⁻¹, LDTW 0.107×10⁻⁴, Lip-max 0.398×10⁻³), with the largest margin on Lip-max (13.7% over UniTalker). In terms of efficiency, MemoryTalker runs at 7.8 ms per second of audio with only 94 M parameters, making it the fastest and most compact model among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MemoryTalker outperforms all six baselines on every metric across both VOCASET and BIWI. The closest competitor is UniTalker [11], which is surpassed by 11.2% on FVE, 23.3% on LVE, and 18.7% on Lip-max on VOCASET, and by 2.0% on FVE and 13.7% on Lip-max on BIWI. Among one-hot-encoding methods (FaceFormer, CodeTalker, Imitator), MemoryTalker achieves substantially lower errors—e.g., 20.3% lower FVE than FaceFormer and 29.8% lower than CodeTalker on VOCASET—while requiring no identity label at inference. Reference-mesh methods (Imitator, ScanTalk) also trail MemoryTalker on all shared metrics, with Imitator showing the worst FID (3.918) and LDTW (0.554) on VOCASET. In the user study, MemoryTalker is preferred over all five competitors on lip-sync, realism, and speaking-style similarity, with the largest margins against ScanTalk (94.4% lip-sync, 92.8% speaking style). In terms of efficiency, MemoryTalker (7.8 ms, 94 M params) is roughly 5× faster than CodeTalker (297.6 ms, 315 M) and uses fewer parameters than SelfTalk (450 M) and UniTalker (313 M).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two established lines of speech-driven 3D facial animation: (1) one-hot identity encoding methods (FaceFormer, CodeTalker, MeshTalk) that cannot generalize to unseen speakers, and (2) reference-based personalization methods (Imitator, Mimic, Wu et al., Yang et al.) that require additional 3D mesh sequences or 2D video at inference. It repurposes key-value memory network architectures—previously applied in object tracking, few-shot learning, and multimodal alignment—to bridge the audio-to-3D-motion modality gap, combined with a pre-trained HuBERT encoder for text-level motion retrieval. The two-stage memorize-then-stylize training strategy is novel in this domain, though each component (memory slots, triplet loss, transformer decoder) is drawn from existing literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for identity labels or reference 3D meshes at inference, MemoryTalker makes personalized speech-driven facial animation practically deployable in VR telepresence, gaming, and metaverse applications where only audio is available. However, evaluation is limited to two small English-only datasets (VOCASET: 12 subjects, BIWI: 14 subjects), and the 'personalization' is demonstrated by averaging over all training speakers rather than truly zero-shot generalization to unseen individuals. Open questions include whether the memory-based stylization scales to larger, more diverse speaker populations, whether it captures emotional or paralinguistic variation beyond basic speaking style, and how it performs on multilingual speech.
