## Problem

Transformer-based 3D vision-language models suffer from quadratic attention complexity and simplistic positional embeddings that limit long-range spatial dependency modeling. While State Space Models (Mamba) offer linear-complexity alternatives with selective scanning, straightforward adoption to 3D-VL tasks fails because (1) converting sparse, unordered 3D point clouds to 1D sequences destroys spatial relationships, and (2) vanilla Mamba lacks native cross-modal interaction mechanisms to align semantics with 3D geometries. This paper presents the first mamba-based framework specifically designed to overcome both obstacles.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251811/cropped_page0_idx9.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3D point clouds (encoded by PointNet++), multi-view RGB images (encoded by OpenSeg with per-pixel embeddings back-projected to 3D), and multi-scale voxels (encoded by a sparse convolutional U-Net over Mask3D discretization), all grouped into S segments and aligned in a shared 3D coordinate space. Task-specific prompts—natural language descriptions or bounding boxes—condition the decoding. Three universal output heads produce instance masks (dice loss), grounding scores (cross-entropy), and scene-grounded text (cross-entropy); for embodied tasks, the generation head is swapped to Vicuna-7B to emit navigation or manipulation actions.

## Method

The Mamba Mixer performs two sequential SSM passes per decoder layer: a spatial SSM that reorders instance queries via a relation-prioritized scanning policy combining Nearest Instance Scanning (local spatial relations like 'adjacent') and Furthest Instance Scanning (long-range dependencies like 'the farthest'), followed by a channel SSM that scans a hybrid feature chain formed by channel-wise concatenation of 3D queries and prompt embeddings, enabling task-conditioned inter-modal fusion at every spatial location. The Instance-aware Dynamic Position Adapter (IDPA) supplements this with fine-grained, instance-specific positional embeddings: EdgeConv layers encode relative orientation and distance from KNN-based object proxies as geometric priors, while the Language-modulated Instance Adapter (LISA) applies a geometric-semantic warping that dynamically modulates these embeddings via prompt-dependent learnable factors α(T) and β(T). Training follows a two-stage protocol—800-epoch ScanNet200 instance segmentation pretraining with a classification head, then 80-epoch multi-task fine-tuning across all benchmarks—with only 1% of LLM parameters updated for embodied AI generalization.

## Results

On the primary 3D visual grounding benchmarks, Mamba-3VL achieves 53.5% average accuracy on ScanRefer (79.9% Unique, 48.9% Multiple), 69.7% on Nr3D, 81.7% on Sr3D, and 53.2% on Multi3DRefer, surpassing the strongest prior method PQ3D by 2.3%, 3.0%, 2.0%, and 3.1% respectively. On SQA3D question answering, Mamba-3VL reaches 52.4% overall accuracy (vs. PQ3D's 47.1%), and on Scan2Cap dense captioning it attains 91.2 CIDEr@25 and 84.0 CIDEr@50 (vs. PQ3D's 87.1 and 80.3). For instance segmentation on ScanNet200, Mamba-3VL records 27.9 AP in closed-vocabulary and 22.4 AP in open-vocabulary settings, outperforming PQ3D by 0.9 and 2.2 AP respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mamba-3VL outperforms all 30 compared baselines across every benchmark. Against the strongest unified baseline PQ3D [74], it gains +2.3% on ScanRefer average, +3.0% on Nr3D, +2.0% on Sr3D, and +3.1% on Multi3DRefer, with the largest margin on the challenging Multi3DRefer ZT subset (+11.8%). Among grounding-specific methods, it surpasses LIBA [60] (49.0% ScanRefer avg) by 4.5% and G3-LQ [59] (45.6%) by 7.9%. On SQA3D, Mamba-3VL (52.4%) beats 3D-VisTA [73] (48.5%) by 3.9% and PQ3D (47.1%) by 5.3%. On Scan2Cap, it leads PQ3D by +4.1 CIDEr@25 and +3.7 CIDEr@50, and 3D-VisTA by +20.2 and +17.1 respectively. For embodied tasks, Mamba-3VL matches LEO [21] on seen CLIPort manipulation (98.8%) but exceeds it by +6.3% on unseen tasks (81.5% vs. 75.2%), and outperforms PQ3D on HD3M navigation (53.4% vs. 50.0% success). Against linear-complexity alternatives (Linformer, SOFT, Focused-Linear), Mamba-3VL achieves consistent 1–3% gains, validating the synergy of relation-prioritized scanning and channel twisting over generic linear attention.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant transformer-based paradigm in 3D vision-language learning (3D-VisTA, SceneVerse, UniT3D, PQ3D) by replacing full self-attention with a State Space Model (Mamba) backbone, motivated by the quadratic complexity and attention entropy limitations of transformers on long 3D sequences. Architecturally, it adopts PQ3D's query-based decoding framework as its skeleton but introduces two novel modules: a Mamba Mixer that performs relation-prioritized spatial scanning (combining nearest and furthest instance ordering) and channel twisting for cross-modal fusion, and an Instance-aware Dynamic Position Adapter (IDPA) that provides instance-specific positional embeddings via EdgeConv and language-modulated warping. The work also positions itself against prior linear-attention alternatives (Linformer, SOFT, Focused-Linear) and earlier mamba-for-3D efforts (PointMamba, VoxelMamba, PointCloudMamba), which applied mamba to single-modality 3D tasks without the multi-modal interaction mechanisms needed for 3D-VL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Mamba-3VL establishes that SSMs can serve as a viable, linear-complexity backbone for multi-task 3D-VL understanding, achieving SOTA on seven benchmarks while maintaining efficiency advantages over transformer-based competitors. The generalization to embodied AI tasks (robotic manipulation, object navigation) with only 1% LLM parameter fine-tuning suggests the learned 3D-VL representations transfer well to action-oriented settings. Open questions remain: whether the hand-crafted scanning policies (NIS/FIS) scale to very large scenes with hundreds of objects, whether the performance gap over PQ3D widens or narrows at larger model scale, and whether mamba's inductive biases genuinely outperform well-tuned transformers when compute budgets are equalized. The paper does not report wall-clock inference times or memory comparisons that would concretely validate the efficiency claims.
