## Problem

Existing spatial reasoning benchmarks for LMMs focus on 2D viewer-relative relationships (e.g., left/right in the image plane) or rely on synthetic environments with significant domain gaps to natural images. There is no comprehensive benchmark that evaluates true 3D spatial reasoning—height, location, orientation, and multi-object relationships in world space—on diverse, open-vocabulary entities from real-world images, nor one that tests robustness to uncommon camera 6D viewpoints relevant to robotics and embodied AI.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251953/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each question pairs an RGB image (MS-COCO natural photo or HSSD synthetic render) with a multiple-choice text question (2 or 4 options) about 3D spatial relationships. Evaluation uses CircularEval, repeating each question N times with permuted choice orderings and requiring all passes to be correct, plus FlipEval which horizontally flips images to generate complementary questions that remove left/right answer biases. The benchmark comprises three splits: 3DSRBench-real (5,250 augmented questions on MS-COCO), and 3DSRBench-synthetic-common/uncommon (1,692 questions total on paired multi-view HSSD renders), with the synthetic splits enabling direct measurement of performance degradation under uncommon camera poses.

## Method

3DSRBench is a manually annotated benchmark of 2,772 VQA pairs spanning 12 subtypes across four question categories (height, location, orientation, multi-object reasoning), covering open-vocabulary entities including rigid objects, humans, animals, and implicit concepts like logos or arrows. Key design choices include balanced answer distributions, complementary image pairs yielding opposite answers for the same question, and explicit avoidance of shortcut or trivial-answer questions. The novel FlipEval strategy horizontally flips images to generate paired questions, eliminating left/right priors (e.g., drivers sitting on the left) and enriching the test distribution. For robustness evaluation, the synthetic split renders the same HSSD 3D scenes from both common (eye-level, zero roll, small pitch) and uncommon (large roll/pitch, ground-level, or elevated) 6D camera poses, enabling direct quantification of viewpoint sensitivity. The paper benchmarks 15+ open-sourced and proprietary LMMs, ablates visual encoder choices (CLIP, DINOv2, MAE, SAM, mixed encoders with SVA connector), and studies language model scaling from 0.5B to 72B parameters.

## Results

On 3DSRBench-real (5,250 questions), human evaluators achieve 95.7% overall accuracy, while the best LMM, SpatialReasoner, reaches only 60.3%—a gap of over 35 points. Among proprietary models, QWenVLMax leads at 52.0% overall, followed by Gemini-2.0-Flash-think (51.1%) and GPT-4o (44.2%). On the synthetic splits, all models degrade substantially on uncommon camera viewpoints, with relative drops ranging from 12.0% (Qwen-VL-Max) to 32.2% (Gemini-1.5-Pro), confirming that current LMMs lack robustness to 6D camera pose variation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpatialReasoner (60.3% overall) is the clear winner among all 15 compared LMMs, outperforming the best proprietary model QWenVLMax (52.0%) by 8.3 points and GPT-4o (44.2%) by 16.1 points, with particularly large margins on orientation (55.2% vs. 21.6% for GPT-4o) and multi-object reasoning (51.8% vs. 39.0%). Among proprietary models, QWenVLMax leads on location (70.7%) and multi-object (44.8%), while Gemini-2.0-Flash-think edges ahead on overall (51.1%) and height (53.0%). GPT-4o underperforms expectations, scoring only 21.6% on orientation—near the Random++ baseline of 41.7%—despite strong performance on height (53.2%). On the synthetic-uncommon split, all models suffer significant degradation: Gemini-1.5-Pro drops 32.2% relative (59.9%→49.5%), LLaVA-NeXT-8B drops 19.1% (45.5%→36.8%), and GPT-4o drops 13.5% (51.2%→44.3%), indicating that no current LMM generalizes well to uncommon 6D camera viewpoints. The paper further shows that scaling language model size (0.5B→72B) monotonically improves 3D spatial reasoning but remains highly inefficient, with even the 72B InternVL2.5 falling over 40 points below human performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior spatial reasoning benchmarks that focused on 2D viewer-relative relationships (derivable from bounding boxes) or on synthetic environments with rule-based question generation from 3D annotations (e.g., SpatialRGPT, Cambrian-1 on Omni3D). It builds on MMBench's CircularEval protocol for robust multiple-choice evaluation and extends the left-right bias analysis from [49] into a novel FlipEval strategy. The key conceptual departure is shifting from rigid-object, domain-specific 3D annotations to manually annotated, open-vocabulary 3D spatial reasoning on natural images, and introducing a 6D camera viewpoint robustness axis via paired synthetic multi-view images from HSSD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even frontier LMMs (GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5-Pro) achieve only 44–60% accuracy on 3D spatial reasoning versus 95.7% for humans, and suffer 13–32% relative accuracy drops when camera viewpoints shift from common to uncommon poses—posing a direct threat to deployment in robotics and embodied AI where cameras are rarely at eye level. The finding that standard scaling of language model size yields diminishing returns for 3D spatial reasoning, and that the core limitation is the absence of explicit 3D representations (e.g., metric depth), points toward a need for architecturally 3D-aware models rather than simply larger ones. Open questions include whether explicit 3D scene representations can be effectively integrated into LMM pipelines and whether the viewpoint robustness gap can be closed with targeted 3D-aware pretraining data.
