## Problem

Existing fusion-based multi-illuminant white-balance methods (MixedWB, StyleWB) linearly blend sRGB WB presets, but ground-truth pixels frequently fall outside the convex hull of preset values in sRGB space, making linear combination fundamentally insufficient. Additionally, prior methods are trained on single-illuminant sRGB datasets (RenderedWB, Cube+) and evaluated on a small synthetic multi-illuminant set, leaving a gap in both the fusion mechanism and the training data for real multi-illuminant scenes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a RAW sensor image and renders it into five sRGB images using factory-calibrated WB presets (tungsten, fluorescent, daylight, cloudy, shade), which are concatenated into a single H×W×15 tensor. The output is a single white-balance corrected sRGB image of the same spatial resolution. During training, images are resized to 614×920 (Nikon) or 500×700 (Sony), and color charts are masked to prevent trivial solutions.

## Method

The core architecture is a lightweight transformer block that fuses the five WB presets non-linearly. After concatenation, a 3×3 convolution projects the 15-channel input to C=15 feature channels, which then feed a Multi-Head Transposed Attention module (from Restormer) where the attention map is computed as A = Softmax(KᵀQ) — swapping query and key so that attention operates across the preset channels rather than spatially. A Feed-Forward Network and a final 3×3 convolution decode the features back to a 3-channel sRGB image. This design eliminates the need for explicit per-pixel blending weight maps, achieving end-to-end fusion with only 7.9K trainable parameters. Training uses Adam with a cosine learning-rate schedule (1e-3 to 1e-5), L2 loss, and model selection by validation ΔE2000.

## Results

On the combined Sony+Nikon multi-illuminant split of the new LSMI-sRGB dataset, the proposed transformer-based fusion method achieves a mean ΔE2000 of 4.55, MSE of 75.60, and MAE of 3.61, substantially outperforming the best baseline DeepWB (ΔE2000 6.83, MSE 139.32, MAE 5.17) by approximately 33% in ΔE2000 and 46% in MSE. The method also demonstrates strong cross-camera generalization (ΔE2000 5.80 Sony→Nikon, 6.41 Nikon→Sony) and cross-dataset transfer to the Synthetic Multi-Illuminant test set (ΔE2000 7.53 vs. 9.90 for the best prior method, StyleWB). With only 7.9K trainable parameters and 179 ms inference time, the method is roughly 5× faster and an order of magnitude smaller than competing approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five baselines across every metric and every dataset split. Among standard sRGB WB methods, DeepWB [2] is the strongest baseline (ΔE2000 6.83 on the combined split), yet the transformer-based fusion still improves by 2.28 ΔE2000 points. Notably, the linear fusion-based methods MixedWB [6] and StyleWB [32] actually underperform the standard sRGB methods (e.g., MixedWB ΔE2000 9.31 vs. DeepWB 6.83), confirming the paper's central argument that linear blending in sRGB is suboptimal for multi-illuminant scenes. On the cross-camera generalization test (Table 2), the gap widens: Ours achieves ΔE2000 5.80 (Sony→Nikon) and 6.41 (Nikon→Sony) versus 8.91 and 9.44 for DeepWB. On the Synthetic Multi-Illuminant test set [6] (Table 3), Ours reaches ΔE2000 7.53 versus 9.90 for StyleWB, the best prior method, despite a domain shift from real to rendered images. On the single-illuminant Cube+ benchmark (Table 4), Ours also leads with ΔE 4.19 and only 7.9K parameters, compared to 4.28 for WBFlow/SWBNet (7.8M/2.1M params) and 5.03 for MixedWB (1.3M params).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the fusion-based multi-illuminant WB formulation introduced by MixedWB [6], which renders five sRGB WB presets and learns per-pixel linear blending weights. The key departure is showing that linear blending is fundamentally suboptimal in sRGB space (99.76% of ground-truth pixels fall outside the convex hull of presets) and replacing it with a nonlinear transformer-based fusion using the transposed attention mechanism from Restormer [53]. The paper also repurposes the LSMI RAW multi-illuminant dataset [29] to create the first large-scale sRGB multi-illuminant WB benchmark, filling a gap left by prior work that relied on single-illuminant datasets (RenderedWB, Cube+) or a small 30-scene synthetic set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The new 16,284-image sRGB multi-illuminant dataset is a practical resource that the field has lacked, enabling proper training and evaluation of fusion-based WB methods on real multi-illuminant scenes. The extreme efficiency (7.9K parameters, ~5× faster than DeepWB) makes the approach deployable in resource-constrained settings, and the cross-camera generalization results suggest the sRGB-domain formulation transfers across sensors without retraining. Open questions include whether the brightness-adjustment step in ground-truth generation (assuming Lambertian reflectance) introduces systematic bias, and whether the five-preset formulation is optimal or whether additional presets or learned presets could further improve accuracy.
