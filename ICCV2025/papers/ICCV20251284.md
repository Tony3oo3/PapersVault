## Problem

Existing Plug-and-Play (PnP) inverse solvers based on latent diffusion models require hundreds to thousands of neural function evaluations per sample and cannot efficiently leverage text-prompt conditioning for zero-shot image restoration. Moreover, specifying a suitable prompt a priori is difficult, and no prior method combines fast latent consistency models (LCMs) with prompt conditioning in a gradient-free PnP framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251284/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a linearly degraded measurement y = Ax + n (e.g., blurred or downsampled image with additive Gaussian noise) and a free-text prompt c (encoded via CLIP) as input, and produces a single restored image x at up to 1024×1024 resolution. LATINO requires only 8 NFEs and ~13 GB GPU memory (no backpropagation through the VAE), while LATINO-PRO uses 68 NFEs to additionally self-calibrate the prompt. The prompt is split into a frozen descriptive prefix and an optimizable suffix, updated in latent space without gradient flow through the encoder or decoder.

## Method

LATINO replaces the intractable prior step of a Langevin SDE discretization with a Stochastic Auto-Encoder (SAE) built from a Latent Consistency Model (DMD2/SDXL): the stochastic encoder adds VP-noise to the VAE-encoded latent, and the decoder applies the CM consistency function followed by the VAE decoder, yielding a contractive Markov kernel with p(x|c) as fixed point. The likelihood is enforced via an explicit proximal operator step, making the full iteration gradient-free and numerically stable for any step size. LATINO-PRO wraps this in a stochastic approximation proximal gradient (SAPG) loop that maximizes the marginal likelihood p(y|c) using Fisher's identity, computing prompt gradients in latent space over LATINO-generated samples without backpropagating through the VAE, thereby simultaneously calibrating the prompt and producing the final restored image.

## Results

On FFHQ-512, LATINO-PRO (68 NFE) achieves PSNR 29.11 dB and LPIPS 0.292 on Gaussian deblurring (σ=3.0), outperforming P2L (2000 NFE) by 2.14 dB in PSNR while using 30× fewer NFEs. On AFHQ-512, LATINO-PRO achieves FID 18.37 and PSNR 26.82 on Gaussian deblurring (σ=5.0), dramatically surpassing P2L (FID 85.80, PSNR 20.96) and TReg (FID 35.47, PSNR 21.13). The base LATINO solver with only 8 NFEs already matches or exceeds methods requiring 1000–2000 NFEs on most metrics, and requires only ~13 GB GPU memory due to the absence of gradient computation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LATINO-PRO (68 NFE) decisively outperforms P2L (2000 NFE) on AFHQ-512 across all metrics (e.g., FID 18.37 vs 85.80 on deblurring; PSNR 26.82 vs 20.96), and on FFHQ-512 it wins on PSNR and LPIPS for both deblurring tasks (PSNR 29.11 vs 26.97 for Gaussian; 27.14 vs 27.23 for motion) while using 30× fewer NFEs. However, P2L retains an advantage on FFHQ-512 super-resolution (FID 31.23 vs 40.95; PSNR 28.55 vs 26.58; LPIPS 0.290 vs 0.355), suggesting the prompt-optimized prior is less effective for extreme upscaling. Against TReg (200 NFE) on AFHQ, LATINO-PRO wins by a wide margin (FID 18.37 vs 35.47; PSNR 26.82 vs 21.13). LATINO-PRO also surpasses LDPS, PSLD, and LDIR (all 1000 NFE) on PSNR and LPIPS for deblurring on FFHQ, though P2L and LDPS achieve slightly lower FID on SR×8. The base LATINO solver (8 NFE) is remarkably competitive, matching or exceeding 1000-NFE methods on PSNR for deblurring, demonstrating that the LCM-based PnP paradigm is inherently efficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

LATINO departs from the dominant LDM-based inverse solver paradigm (P2L, TReg, PSLD, LDPS) in two fundamental ways: it replaces the multi-step diffusion sampling with a few-step Latent Consistency Model (DMD2), and it replaces the denoiser-based PnP operator with a Stochastic Auto-Encoder (SAE) whose contractive kernel is derived from the CM's consistency function. The Langevin splitting framework connects to the PnP-ULA line of work (Laumont et al.), while the prompt optimization via stochastic approximation proximal gradient follows De Bortoli et al.'s SAPG scheme, here applied for the first time to CM-based priors. The key conceptual departure is treating the CM not as a denoiser but as a pushforward map that defines a Markov kernel with the target distribution as its fixed point, enabling a gradient-free, low-NFE sampling loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8-NFE requirement (vs. 200–2000 for prior SOTA) and ~13 GB GPU footprint make generative-prior inverse solvers practical for high-resolution (≥1024²) imaging in settings where compute is constrained, and the zero-shot, prompt-conditioned design removes the need for task-specific fine-tuning. Open questions include: (i) formal non-asymptotic convergence guarantees for the SAE-based Langevin scheme under realistic CM assumptions, (ii) automatic calibration of the annealing schedule (t_k, δ_k) rather than manual tuning, and (iii) decoding the optimized latent prompt back into interpretable text. The quality comparison is somewhat confounded by using a stronger base model (SDXL/DMD2) than the SD1.5-based competitors, though the authors provide ablations to partially address this.
