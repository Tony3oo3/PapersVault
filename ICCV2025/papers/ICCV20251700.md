## Problem

GAN-based SAR image generation suffers from severe instability and mode collapse under extreme data scarcity (few dozen samples per class), and standard stabilization techniques designed for natural images (rotation augmentation, gradient penalties, latent optimization) fail because SAR targets exhibit electromagnetic scattering that changes dramatically with azimuth angle, making geometric augmentations physically meaningless. No prior method incorporates the underlying electromagnetic scattering physics of SAR into GAN training to enforce physical consistency and prevent discriminator overfitting to speckle noise.

## Input / Output

The generator receives a latent noise vector z concatenated with a one-hot target class label c and a 10-dimensional azimuth angle embedding r(θ) = [sin θ, cos θ, …, sin 5θ, cos 5θ] (Cyclic High-frequency information-based Embedding), and produces a single-channel SAR image. The physics module additionally outputs per-target PSC parameters (intensity A_i and 2D position (x_i, y_i) for N scatterers) and a reconstructed EM scattering image s, which are used by both the generator and discriminator losses. The framework is plug-and-play, demonstrated on ACGAN and StyleGAN/StyleGAN2 backbones across 10-class MSTAR, 2-class OpenSARShip, and 2-class SAR-Airplane datasets.

## Method

Φ-GAN embeds the ideal Point Scattering Center (PSC) model into GAN training via a physics-inspired neural module F_est that unrolls the Half-Quadratic Splitting (HQS) sparse-reconstruction algorithm into a 2-stage network with only 6 learnable parameters (t, ρ, μ per stage), enabling efficient end-to-end estimation of PSC parameters from SAR images. A frozen reconstruction step F_phy then maps these parameters back to an EM scattering image. Two physics-based losses regularize training: L_phy^G constrains the generator by matching PSC reconstruction outputs (image-level MSE) and aligning latent features between the image discriminator D_img and a physics-informed regularizer R_phy (feature-level L2); L_phy^D distills R_phy's hierarchical features into D_img to force the discriminator to base decisions on electromagnetic scattering cues rather than speckle noise patterns. The final discriminator score is a weighted combination α·D_img(u) + (1−α)·R_phy(s), and the entire pipeline is trained with standard alternating GAN updates plus the two physical losses.

## Results

On 10% MSTAR, Φ-GAN achieves FID 87.27 and KID 0.0414, reducing FID by 70% over the ACGAN baseline (290.05) and outperforming the best competing regularization (DIG, FID 373.07) by a wide margin. On 5% MSTAR, FID drops to 130.23 (KID 0.0861) versus 340.24 for ACGAN. On 1% OpenSARShip, Φ-GAN reaches FID 14.80 and KID 0.0046, and on 14% SAR-Airplane it achieves FID 19.15 and KID 0.0046, both substantially below the ACGAN baseline (FID 18.29/25.22). Full-reference IQA metrics (SSIM, VIF, FSIM, GMSD) also improve consistently across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Φ-GAN decisively outperforms all natural-image-oriented regularization baselines on MSTAR: DA [64] catastrophically degrades FID to 1089 (10%) and 976 (5%), ADA [27] and RLC [53] offer only marginal gains over ACGAN, and DIG [9] actually worsens FID (373 vs. 290 on 10%). On StyleGAN-based models, Φ-GAN reduces FID from 148.36 (StyleGAN2) to 115.97 on 10% MSTAR and from 168.40 to 164.97 on 5%, outperforming NoisyTwins [45] and AdaptiveMix [33]. Against SAR-specific generation methods, ACGAN+Φ-GAN achieves FID 14.80 on 1% OpenSARShip versus 41.24 for CAE [14] and 23.95 for CVAE-GAN [18], and FID 87.27 on 10% MSTAR versus 134.86 for CAE. The method also improves CVAE-GAN when applied to 10% MSTAR (FID 495.97 → 104.90), demonstrating plug-and-play compatibility. Across all three datasets and both ACGAN and StyleGAN backbones, Φ-GAN consistently achieves the lowest FID and KID, with the largest relative gains in the most data-scarce settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: GAN stabilization under limited data (via augmentation, regularization, and latent-space optimization) and SAR image generation with conditional GANs. It departs from both by arguing that neither natural-image stabilization techniques nor existing SAR-specific GAN architectures adequately capture the electromagnetic scattering physics of SAR targets. The core algorithmic building block is the Half-Quadratic Splitting (HQS) algorithm for sparse reconstruction, which the authors unroll into a six-parameter neural module, drawing on the deep-unfolding literature. The PSC model itself is a classical electromagnetic scattering approximation; the novelty lies in making it differentiable and embedding it end-to-end within a GAN training loop as a physics-informed regularizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a minimal (6-parameter) physics-inspired module can close a large gap in SAR generation quality under extreme data scarcity, and that standard natural-image techniques like differentiable augmentation can actively harm SAR generation—a useful negative result for practitioners. The approach is architecture-agnostic (validated on ACGAN, StyleGAN, and CVAE-GAN), suggesting the physics-regularization principle could transfer to other SAR tasks. However, the method is currently limited to GANs; the authors explicitly note that extending the PSC-based regularization to diffusion models or NeRF-based SAR generation remains open. The narrow scope to SAR and the reliance on a specific scattering model (ideal PSC) limit immediate generalizability to other remote-sensing modalities.
