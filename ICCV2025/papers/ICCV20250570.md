## Problem

Generating high-quality 4D assets from a single monocular video remains challenging because prior novel-view video synthesis (NVVS) methods produce blurry details and poor spatio-temporal consistency, particularly under large motion and self-occlusion. Existing approaches are further limited to low resolution, small object motion, or short video lengths, and methods like SV4D depend on a separate multi-view diffusion model (SV3D) for reference views, introducing fragility to occlusion in the anchor frame and reducing practical flexibility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250570/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video of F frames and a user-specified camera trajectory (elevation and azimuth angles relative to the input view, not world-space poses) as input, and produces V novel-view videos of F frames each that are consistent across both the view and temporal axes. These synthesized multi-view videos then serve as photogrammetry pseudo-ground-truths to optimize a dynamic NeRF 4D asset. Longer videos are generated by chaining inference passes, where the last generated multi-view frame conditions the next segment, a flexibility enabled by the random masking strategy during training.

## Method

SV4D 2.0 replaces SV4D's view attention with 3D attention that attends over merged view-and-spatial dimensions, enabling correspondence across sparse novel views, and introduces separate learnable α-blending weights for 3D and frame attention layers to independently preserve multi-view and video diffusion priors while learning joint spatio-temporal consistency. A random masking strategy on reference multi-view latents removes the SV3D dependency, making inference robust to self-occlusion and enabling flexible sampling. Training follows a progressive 3D-to-4D schedule: first on static Objaverse with frame attention bypassed (α_f=0), then unfreezing frame attention to finetune on the quality-enhanced ObjaverseDy++ dataset. For 4D optimization, a 2-stage refinement noises and denoises stage-1 dynamic NeRF renders to produce more 3D-consistent photogrammetry targets, combined with visibility-weighted MSE losses (using surface-normal dot products as per-pixel confidence) and progressive frame sampling with orthogonal view selection to stabilize training under large motion.

## Results

On the ObjaverseDy 4D generation benchmark, SV4D 2.0 achieves LPIPS 0.107, CLIP-S 0.916, PSNR 19.37, and FV4D 532.55, outperforming the closest baseline SV4D (LPIPS 0.122, FV4D 699.04) by 12% in LPIPS and 24% in FV4D. For novel-view video synthesis on the same dataset, SV4D 2.0 reaches LPIPS 0.105 and FV4D 256.84, a 14% and 44% improvement over SV4D respectively. On the Consistent4D dataset, SV4D 2.0 attains LPIPS 0.117 and FVD-F 692.68 for 4D outputs, matching L4GM (LPIPS 0.120) while surpassing SV4D (LPIPS 0.118, FVD-F 732.40). A user study on 20 real-world DAVIS videos shows SV4D 2.0 is preferred 85.2% of the time over Diffusion² (1.8%), STAG4D (5.1%), and SV4D (7.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SV4D 2.0 achieves state-of-the-art results across all evaluated metrics on both ObjaverseDy and Consistent4D, winning against all 11 baselines. The closest competitor is SV4D [69] (the direct predecessor), which SV4D 2.0 surpasses by 12% LPIPS and 24% FV4D on 4D generation, and by 14% LPIPS and 44% FV4D on NVVS. L4GM [44] is the second-closest baseline on 4D generation (LPIPS 0.146 vs. 0.107) but struggles with non-zero elevation angles in ObjaverseDy and lacks a video prior for real-world generalization. Among SDS-based methods, DG4D [43] and STAG4D [76] produce the worst results (LPIPS 0.156 and 0.155 respectively), suffering from oversaturated textures, temporal flickering, and geometric inaccuracies. On NVVS, per-frame SV3D [61] preserves geometry but fails temporally (FVD-F 799.73), while Diffusion² [70] over-smooths results (FVD-F 987.19). On the Consistent4D 4D benchmark, SV4D 2.0 (LPIPS 0.117, FVD-F 692.68) edges out L4GM (LPIPS 0.120, FVD-F 691.87) and SV4D (LPIPS 0.118, FVD-F 732.40), with the gap narrowing compared to ObjaverseDy.

## Relation to Prior Work

SV4D 2.0 is a direct architectural and training-strategy upgrade to SV4D [69], which itself repurposes Stable Video Diffusion [3] and SV3D [61] for multi-view video synthesis. The key conceptual departures are: (1) replacing view attention with 3D attention to handle sparse novel views, (2) eliminating the dependency on a separate multi-view diffusion model (SV3D) for reference views via random masking during training, and (3) adopting progressive 3D-to-4D training rather than direct finetuning on 4D data. On the 4D optimization side, the paper moves beyond the single-stage photogrammetry approach of SV4D by introducing a 2-stage refinement with visibility-weighted losses, positioning itself within the broader trend of replacing SDS-based optimization with reconstruction-from-synthesized-videos pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses practical bottlenecks in 4D generation—blurry details under large motion, poor spatio-temporal consistency, and the impractical dependency on a separate multi-view model during inference—that have limited the adoption of NVVS-based 4D pipelines. The progressive 3D-to-4D training and 2-stage optimization strategies are broadly applicable techniques that other 4D methods could adopt. However, the model remains trained exclusively on synthetic 3D/4D object datasets, and generalization to complex real-world scenes (multiple objects, non-rigid deformations, unbounded environments) remains an open challenge. The lack of large-scale real 4D datasets continues to be a fundamental bottleneck for the field.
