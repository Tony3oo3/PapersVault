## Problem

Mamba-based state space models have shown strong performance in single-label image classification, but their potential for multi-label image classification remains unexplored. Existing multi-label methods either rely on iterative propagation (RNNs, graph networks) or suffer from quadratic complexity in multi-head attention, and existing Mamba architectures are limited to processing a single sequence of image patches, making them unable to handle the multi-sequence, variable-length setting required for label-feature cross-correlation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250767/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes RGB images resized to 448×448 (or 576×576) as input, extracts feature maps via a ResNet-101 backbone, and produces a binary confidence score per label (80 for MS-COCO, 20 for Pascal VOC 2007, 81 for NUS-WIDE) through a sigmoid of a linear projection on each label embedding. Label embeddings are N randomly initialized D-dimensional vectors that serve as learnable query tokens, and the entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with an asymmetric loss (γ⁺=0, γ⁻=1) over 80 epochs.

## Method

MambaML consists of a bi-directional Mamba module that injects global context into convolutional feature maps, followed by a Mamba-based decoder whose each layer combines a Mamba-based cross-attention module and a bi-directional Mamba module. The cross-attention leverages the reformulation of Mamba's recurrent scan as an attention-like operation (Q·H·K), where label embeddings act as queries and image features as keys, enabling cross-correlation between sequences of different lengths. Two key strategies address the length mismatch: state space sharing replicates hidden states across all N labels so they share a single scan, and label-wise max-pooling aggregates the spatial dimension to yield one representation per label. The bi-directional Mamba within each decoder layer then models semantic dependencies among label embeddings. The entire architecture operates with linear computational complexity, requiring 85M parameters and 36.9–59.6 GFLOPs depending on resolution.

## Results

On MS-COCO (448×448), MambaML achieves 85.7% mAP with 85.0M parameters and 36.9G FLOPs, outperforming C-TMS (85.1%, 217M params) and DATran (84.9%, 133M params). At the larger 576×576 resolution, MambaML reaches 86.7% mAP, surpassing Q2L-R101 (86.5%) and DATran (86.3%). On NUS-WIDE, MambaML attains 65.9% mAP (vs. Q2L's 65.0%), and on Pascal VOC 2007 it achieves 95.0% mAP, the best among all compared methods. Notably, MambaML uses roughly 2–3× fewer parameters than competing Transformer-based methods while matching or exceeding their accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MS-COCO at 448×448, MambaML (85.7% mAP) outperforms C-TMS (85.1%) by 0.6%, DATran (84.9%) by 0.8%, Q2L-R101 (84.9%) by 0.8%, and ASL (85.0%) by 0.7%, while using only 85M parameters versus 217M (C-TMS), 143M (Q2L), and 133M (DATran). At 576×576, MambaML (86.7%) leads Q2L-R101 (86.5%) by 0.2%, DATran (86.3%) by 0.4%, and C-TMS (86.2%) by 0.5%. On NUS-WIDE, MambaML achieves 65.9% mAP, surpassing Q2L (65.0%) by 0.9% and ASL (63.9%) by 2.0%. On Pascal VOC 2007, MambaML reaches 95.0% mAP, edging out C-TMS (94.8%) by 0.2%, CSRA (94.7%) by 0.3%, and DATran (94.6%) by 0.4%. Across all three benchmarks, MambaML consistently achieves the highest mAP while requiring fewer parameters and comparable or lower FLOPs than Transformer-based competitors, demonstrating a favorable accuracy–efficiency trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

MambaML is the first work to apply Mamba's selective state space model to multi-label image classification, extending the label-as-query paradigm from Query2Label (Liu et al., 2021) by replacing the Transformer decoder with a Mamba-based cross-attention mechanism. The cross-attention design is directly motivated by the reformulation of Mamba's recurrent process as an implicit attention matrix (Ali et al., 2024), which reveals that Mamba can model cross-correlations between sequences of different lengths. Unlike prior Mamba-based visual architectures that process only a single sequence of image patches, MambaML introduces state space sharing and label-wise pooling to handle the heterogeneous sequence lengths of label embeddings and patch features within a unified linear-complexity framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving SOTA mAP on MS-COCO (85.7%) and Pascal VOC 2007 (95.0%) with substantially fewer parameters (85M vs. 217M for C-TMS) and lower FLOPs, MambaML demonstrates that linear-complexity state space models can replace quadratic-complexity multi-head attention in multi-label classification without sacrificing accuracy. This opens a path toward more efficient multi-label classifiers, particularly for deployment-constrained settings. However, the visualization reveals persistent difficulty with small or occluded objects, and the modest 0.6–0.9% mAP gains over strong Transformer baselines suggest that the efficiency advantage, rather than raw accuracy, is the primary practical benefit. Open questions include whether richer scanning topologies or Mamba2's structured state space duality could further close the gap with attention-based methods.
