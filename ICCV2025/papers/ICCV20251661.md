## Problem

Personal videos uploaded to social media are routinely harvested to train deep visual object tracking (VOT) models without consent, yet the VOT community has largely overlooked this data-privacy risk. Existing Unlearnable Example (UE) methods were designed for image classification and, when naively extended to video, suffer from prohibitive per-frame optimization cost, inability to handle scale-varying temporal matching, and poor transferability across trackers, datasets, and matching tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251661/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The TUE generator (a lightweight DiT-S/8 with 124 MB parameters) takes a cropped target patch and its normalized bounding box (top-left, width, height) as conditioning inputs and produces a single feed-forward perturbation of the same spatial size, which is bilinearly interpolated and pasted onto the target region in every frame of the video. The final output is a full unlearnable video dataset where each frame carries an imperceptible, L∞-bounded noise on the tracked object; no per-video or per-frame iterative optimization is required at inference time, enabling zero-shot generation on unseen datasets.

## Method

The core contribution is a generative framework that replaces the iterative EM min-max loop with a single DiT-based generator G_w that maps (target patch, normalized bbox) → perturbation in one forward pass, reducing training to 7 hours / 124 MB versus 33 hours / 3.4 GB for the EM baseline on GOT-10k. Training follows a min-max schedule (Algorithm 1): the inner step back-propagates the tracking cross-correlation loss through G_w to shape the perturbation, while the outer step updates a frozen-architecture SiamFC surrogate tracker. A Temporal Contrastive Loss (TCL) is added to the inner objective, treating the TUE-perturbed template as the anchor and the TUE-perturbed search-region crop from the same video as the positive, pushing the tracker to match on the injected noise rather than the underlying appearance. Because the generator is conditioned on the normalized target state, it adapts to scale and position changes across frames, and because it is a learned function rather than per-sample parameters, it transfers zero-shot to unseen datasets and to dense temporal-matching tasks such as video object segmentation.

## Results

On GOT-10k (AO metric, lower = stronger privacy), TUEs trained with SiamFC in ~7 hours on a single GPU degrade SOTA trackers dramatically: OSTrack-256 drops from 71.0 to 18.0 (53.0↓), SeqTrack-256 from 74.7 to 2.1 (72.6↓), MixFormer-CvT from 70.7 to 1.9 (68.8↓), and STARK-S50 from 67.2 to 2.6 (64.6↓). On OTB-100 (AUC), the same TUEs reduce OSTrack from 67.4 to 30.5 and SeqTrack from 68.1 to 7.0. The method also transfers zero-shot to unseen datasets (LaSOT) and to the dense VOS task (DAVIS-17, YTVOS-19), where XMEM's J&F drops from 84.5 to 64.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TUEs consistently outperform all four baselines across nearly every tracker and dataset. Against EM [31] (the strongest baseline), TUE achieves substantially larger drops: on GOT-10k AO, TUE degrades SeqTrack by 72.6 points vs. EM's 66.6, MixFormer by 68.8 vs. EM's 63.7, and OSTrack by 53.0 vs. EM's 44.7. TAP [22] and LSP [85] are intermediate, with TAP performing best on some ViT-based trackers (e.g., TAP drops OSTrack AO to 17.0 vs. TUE's 18.0) but failing on others (TAP only drops DropTrack AO to 51.7 vs. TUE's 17.1). AR [56] is the weakest baseline, barely degrading several trackers (e.g., OSTrack AO drops only 4.0 points, DropTrack only 9.0 points), confirming that class-wise perturbations are ineffective for temporal matching. The one notable exception is HIPTrack, where TUE (AO 43.2) degrades less than EM (AO 63.0) would suggest, but TUE still achieves a 34.2-point drop vs. EM's 14.4-point drop, so TUE remains superior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Unlearnable Examples (UEs) paradigm from 2D image classification (EM, TAP, LSP, AR) to the fundamentally different setting of temporal matching in video, where perturbations must support scale-invariant cross-frame correspondence rather than static feature disruption. The key architectural departure is replacing per-sample iterative PGD optimization with a lightweight DiT-based generative framework conditioned on normalized target states, enabling single-step inference and zero-shot transfer to unseen videos. A temporal contrastive loss is introduced to exploit the Siamese matching structure of VOT, encouraging trackers to overfit to the generated noise rather than the underlying object appearance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of video data privacy in VOT, addressing a real gap: large-scale tracking benchmarks (GOT-10k, LaSOT) are built from user-uploaded social media videos used without consent. The strong transferability results—a 124MB generator trained with SiamFC degrading SOTA trackers (SeqTrack, MixFormer, STARK) on unseen datasets and even VOS—suggest a practical, scalable defense for content creators. Open questions include robustness of TUEs against denoising or compression-based removal, applicability to multi-object tracking and online adaptation, and whether the approach generalizes to other video understanding tasks beyond temporal matching.
