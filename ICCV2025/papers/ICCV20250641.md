## Problem

Multiparameter persistence (MP) offers richer topological representations than single-parameter persistence by tracking multiple filtrations simultaneously, but its adoption in image analysis is hindered by the lack of practical vectorization algorithms for cubical complexes (the native structure of images) and the difficulty of integrating non-differentiable multifiltration computations into deep learning pipelines. Existing MP vectorizations target point clouds and graphs, not the grid-based cubical complexes that images naturally form.

## Input / Output

The method consumes grayscale or color medical images (resized to 224×224) and RGB images, processing them through a Swin Transformer V2 backbone with learnable cubical multipersistence modules inserted after each transformer block and on the input. For each module, a multifiltration decoder produces an 8×16 MP grid at 112×112 (or 224×224 for the input-level module), yielding per-row persistence pairs that are vectorized into H-dimensional latent features and concatenated into a single topological vector. The final output is a class label (7-way for ISIC, binary for others) or a pixel-wise segmentation mask (PASCAL VOC 2012).

## Method

CuMPerLay circumvents the partial-ordering problem of MP by slicing the bifiltration grid horizontally into M single-parameter filtrations, then applying a level-dependent PersLay vectorization (triangle point transformation with learnable sample locations and power weighting with learnable exponents) to each slice, aggregating the resulting vectors via a learned MLP. The bifiltration functions themselves are learned end-to-end: a convolutional multifiltration decoder outputs continuous per-pixel values, which are discretized via a straight-through staircase function to enforce row-wise filtration structure, while a soft regularization loss enforces column-wise monotonicity. A CUDA GPU implementation of cubical persistence (union-find for H₀, duality for H₁) propagates straight-through gradients to pixel locations producing birth/death pairs, enabling joint optimization of filtrations and vectorizations. The topological vector is fused back into the network via a residual gated linear unit, and a topological classification head with cross-entropy loss guides the multifiltration decoder. The paper proves stability of the induced MP vectorization under generalized Wasserstein metrics, showing the bound scales with the L∞ distance between bifiltration functions.

## Results

On the three medical classification benchmarks, TopoSwin-MP achieves 84.63% accuracy and 92.87 AUC on Glaucoma, 68.32% accuracy and 76.32 AUC on CBIS-DDSM, and 83.47% accuracy and 96.50 AUC on ISIC 2018, consistently outperforming the Swin V2 baseline (82.73/92.24, 66.76/74.17, 84.39/95.54 respectively). For segmentation on PASCAL VOC 2012, Topo-MP improves mIoU from 23.96 to 30.42 on U-Net and from 38.73 to 40.36 on FCN-ResNet50. The GPU cubical multipersistence implementation runs 73× faster than the CPU GUDHI baseline (0.57 s vs. 41.76 s per batch of 16×32 grids on 224×224 images).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TopoSwin-MP outperforms the Swin V2 baseline on Glaucoma (+1.90 Acc, +0.63 AUC) and CBIS-DDSM (+1.56 Acc, +2.15 AUC), though it trails slightly on ISIC accuracy (83.47 vs. 84.39) while leading on AUC (96.50 vs. 95.54). Against PHG-Net, the improvement is substantial across all datasets: +3.37 Acc and +2.55 AUC on Glaucoma, +10.08 Acc and +15.25 AUC on DDSM, and +10.78 Acc and +6.88 AUC on ISIC, demonstrating the benefit of learnable multiparameter filtrations over fixed single-parameter PointNet-based features. For segmentation, Topo-MP improves U-Net mIoU by 6.46 points (23.96→30.42) and FCN mIoU by 1.63 points (38.73→40.36), with the U-Net gain being particularly notable. The standalone MP vectors (without deep learning) already surpass SP vectors on all three medical datasets, confirming that multiparameter persistence captures richer structural information. The GPU implementation is 73× faster than single-process GUDHI and 9× faster than 24-process parallelized GUDHI, making end-to-end training with cubical multipersistence practical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PersLay vectorization framework [13] from single-parameter to multiparameter persistence by decomposing a cubical bifiltration into row-wise single-parameter filtrations and applying level-dependent PersLay to each. It builds on PHG-Net [52], which integrated single-parameter cubical persistence into Swin Transformers, by replacing fixed SP filtrations with end-to-end learnable bifiltration functions and a differentiable MP vectorization layer. The GPU cubical persistence algorithm is influenced by CubicalRipser [38], but the paper provides the first CUDA implementation of cubical *multiparameter* persistence, which prior CPU-only tools (e.g., multipers) did not support for cubical complexes. Conceptually, the paper departs from the broader MP literature (which has focused on point clouds and graphs) by targeting cubical complexes—the native topological structure of images—and by making the bifiltration itself a learnable component rather than a fixed preprocessing choice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CuMPerLay offers a practical pathway for integrating global topological structure into state-of-the-art vision architectures, with the learnable bifiltration removing the need for hand-crafted filtration design. The demonstrated benefit in limited-data scenarios suggests topological features can serve as robust inductive priors when labeled data is scarce, a practically important setting in medical imaging. Open questions remain around learning truly optimal bifiltrations (the current approach uses a simple staircase thresholding with soft regularization), extending the framework to 3D volumetric data, and whether the stability guarantees translate into meaningful robustness under adversarial or distributional shifts. The publicly available GPU implementation also lowers the barrier for the broader TDA community to experiment with multiparameter persistence on image data at scale.
