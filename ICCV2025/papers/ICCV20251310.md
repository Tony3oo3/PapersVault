## Problem

Conventional LiDAR point cloud pipelines discard all uncertainty information from raw SPAD timing histograms when constructing point clouds, causing severe 3D object detection failures under challenging conditions such as low signal-to-background ratio, distant or low-albedo objects, and strong ambient illumination. No prior work propagates sensor-level measurement confidence into point cloud representations for downstream 3D inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251310/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw SPAD timing histograms (e.g., 1024 bins at 97 ps width in simulation, 4096 bins at 41.1 ps for the FLIMera sensor, 672 bins at 297 ps for the Adaps ADS6311) and converts them into 3D point clouds where each point carries an additional scalar probability attribute computed as the ratio of peak-bin photon count to total histogram photon count. The output is a Probabilistic Point Cloud (PPC) that feeds into standard 3D detection backbones (VoteNet, PV-RCNN, etc.) to produce 3D object bounding boxes, with the probability attribute used for noise-aware filtering and keypoint sampling rather than as a direct network input in the primary pipeline.

## Method

The core contribution is a lightweight, physics-based confidence measure per point (peak-bin count divided by total histogram count) that requires only simple arithmetic on the raw histogram, making it suitable for on-chip computation. Two drop-in inference modules exploit this attribute: (1) NPD Filtering computes a Neighbor Probability Density score for each point by averaging the probabilities of up to L=64 neighbors within a radius r=0.2 m, penalizing spatially sparse noise points while preserving dense ground-truth surfaces; and (2) Farthest Probable Point Sampling (FPPS) restricts the standard FPS keypoint selection to a candidate set of points exceeding a probability threshold β=0.01, preventing noise-dominant keypoints from degrading feature extraction. These modules are architecture-agnostic and plug into existing PointNet++- or voxel-CNN-based detectors without modifying training procedures. The paper further explores three deeper integration strategies (probability as input attribute, probability-weighted point features, probability-weighted objectness scores) that yield an additional ~2% AP gain on top of the baseline PPC pipeline.

## Results

On SUN RGB-D with VoteNet, PPC achieves AP@25 of 58.61 (clean) and 29.42 at SBR 0.01, substantially outperforming the best baseline (Thresholding at 16.47 AP@25) by 13 points under the most challenging noise level. On KITTI with PV-RCNN, PPC reaches mAP of 55.39 for pedestrians and 47.76 for cyclists at SBR 0.01, versus 49.23 and 44.96 for the best baseline. Inference runtime is only 95 ms per scene, comparable to simple filtering (87–89 ms) and 8–14× faster than learned denoising networks (755–1345 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPC outperforms all four baselines across every SBR level on both SUN RGB-D and KITTI. Against Matched Filtering [51], PPC gains 18.1 AP@25 points at SBR 0.01 (29.42 vs 11.34), as matched filtering cannot suppress the spatially scattered outlier points inherent to low-SBR noise. Against PointCleanNet [44], PPC leads by 16.6 AP@25 at SBR 0.01 (29.42 vs 12.78); PointCleanNet's isotropic Gaussian noise assumption fails to remove the anisotropic, far-from-surface outliers produced by background photons. Against Score-based Denoising [31], PPC gains 14.9 AP@25 at SBR 0.01 (29.42 vs 14.55); score denoising corrects points near surfaces but leaves distant outliers intact. Against PathNet [54], PPC leads by 15.6 AP@25 at SBR 0.01 (29.42 vs 13.87). Crucially, PPC achieves these gains at 95 ms runtime—comparable to simple thresholding—while the three learned denoisers require 755–1345 ms, making PPC the only approach that is both more accurate and computationally lightweight.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard LiDAR signal-processing pipeline that discards all information beyond the peak location of SPAD timing histograms, instead retaining a lightweight per-point probability derived from the raw histogram. It contrasts with learned point-cloud denoising methods (PointCleanNet, Score-based Denoising, PathNet) that add a separate, computationally expensive denoising stage, and with histogram-denoising approaches for 3D reconstruction that require full histogram readout. Conceptually, it extends the idea of depth uncertainty (previously explored in monocular depth estimation) to the LiDAR sensing domain, where raw sensor data is typically inaccessible, and repurposes existing 3D detection backbones (PointNet++, sparse voxel CNNs) without architectural modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is high: PPC adds a single scalar per point and two lightweight modules (NPD filtering, FPPS) that are drop-in compatible with a wide range of 3D detectors, with negligible runtime overhead (~95 ms vs. 755–1345 ms for learned denoisers), making it suitable for on-chip or edge deployment. A key open question is whether richer, task-aware confidence measures (e.g., Cramér-Rao bounds, learned uncertainty) could further close the gap at extreme low-SBR regimes where even PPC degrades. The paper also leaves open the extension to other 3D sensing modalities (stereo, structured light, indirect ToF) and to tasks beyond object detection, suggesting a broader 'uncertainty-aware 3D inference' research direction.
