## Problem

Vision Transformers suffer from quadratic self-attention complexity, and existing token-grouping remedies either rely on spatial windows that ignore semantic relationships or use iterative k-means clustering that produces uneven cluster sizes. No prior method simultaneously captures global semantic relevance, completes clustering in a single pass, and guarantees equal token counts per cluster for efficient GPU parallelism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252051/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the vision backbone, SECViT consumes RGB images (224×224 for classification, 512×2048 for segmentation) and outputs class labels, bounding boxes, instance masks, or pixel-wise segmentation maps depending on the downstream head. For the MLLM application, SEC takes the 576 vision tokens from a ViT encoder plus a text prompt and produces a text response, while reducing the vision token count to 288, 256, 192, or 144 tokens via attentive pooling within each cluster. The SEC module itself operates on C×H×W feature tensors, producing equally-sized token groups that are processed in parallel.

## Method

SEC computes a single global cluster center by average-pooling all key tokens, then measures each token's cosine similarity to this center and sorts tokens by similarity score. For the vision backbone, consecutive tokens in the sorted sequence are partitioned into equal-sized clusters (Eq. 3), and standard self-attention is applied independently within each cluster (Eq. 4), requiring only one forward pass with no iterative refinement. For the MLLM connector, tokens are instead interleaved across groups (Eq. 5) and each group is merged into a single token via attentive pooling (Eq. 6), reducing the sequence length fed to the LLM. SECViT stacks four stages (downsampling 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, 1/32) where each block comprises a Convolutional Positional Encoding, SEC-based self-attention, and an FFN, enabling feature-pyramid construction for dense prediction tasks.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification, SECViT-S achieves 84.3% Top-1 accuracy with only 27M parameters and 4.6G FLOPs, outperforming all prior small-scale models. SECViT-B reaches 85.2% and SECViT-L reaches 85.7% at the base and large scales respectively. On COCO object detection (Mask R-CNN 1×), SECViT-S attains 49.9 AP^b and SECViT-L attains 52.0 AP^b. When applied as a vision-language connector in LLaVA-1.5, SEC reduces vision tokens from 576 to 288 while achieving 1.5× speedup and improving TextVQA from 58.2 to 60.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SECViT consistently outperforms all compared backbones across every model scale on ImageNet-1K: SECViT-S (84.3%) surpasses BiFormer-S (83.8%), SMT-S (83.7%), and MaxViT-T (83.6%) by 0.5–0.7 points at the small scale, while SECViT-L (85.7%) leads RMT-L (85.5%) and GC-ViT-B (85.0%) at the large scale. In the strict comparison (Table 1), simply replacing the attention mechanism with SEC yields +2.5 points over Swin-T (81.3→83.8) and +0.7 over DeiT-S (79.8→80.5) without adding any extra modules. On COCO detection (Mask R-CNN 1×), SECViT-S achieves 49.9 AP^b, beating RMT-S (49.0) and STViT-B (49.7), and SECViT-L reaches 52.0 AP^b, exceeding MLLA-B (50.5) and InternImage-B (48.8). For semantic segmentation on ADE20K, SECViT-L attains 52.2 mIoU with Semantic FPN, outperforming RMT-L (51.4) and SGFormer-B (50.6). In the MLLM setting, SEC as a connector in LLaVA-1.5 outperforms Resampler, EViT, and simple Pooling at every token budget (e.g., at 288 tokens: SEC scores 60.1 on TextVQA vs. 54.6 for EViT and 52.1 for Resampler) while achieving 1.5×–2.1× inference speedup over the original MLP connector.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two lines of work on reducing ViT's quadratic attention cost: spatial grouping (Swin Transformer's windows, CSWin's cross-shaped windows, MaxViT's multi-axis attention) and semantic grouping (DGT's k-means clustering of queries). SEC departs from spatial methods by incorporating token semantics, and from k-means-based methods by replacing iterative multi-center clustering with a single-pass, single-center scheme (average-pooled global token + cosine similarity + sorting). It also distinguishes itself from similarity-based pruning methods like EViT, which discard low-similarity tokens, by instead partitioning all tokens into equal-sized clusters for intra-cluster attention. The method is applied as a drop-in attention replacement in Swin/DeiT backbones and as a vision-language connector in LLaVA-1.5.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SEC's single-pass, equitable-partition design eliminates the need for custom CUDA kernels to handle uneven cluster sizes, making it a practical drop-in for any existing ViT backbone without architectural redesign. The dual applicability—as both a vision backbone component and an MLLM connector—broadens its relevance across the CV and multimodal communities. An open question is whether the single-center (average-pooled) assumption generalizes to architectures where global pooling is not a natural semantic summary, and whether the approach scales to very long sequences in video or high-resolution settings. The paper does not explore adaptive cluster counts or learned center tokens, which could be natural extensions.
