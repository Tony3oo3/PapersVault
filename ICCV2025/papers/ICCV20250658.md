## Problem

Existing model merging methods suffer from task interference that degrades performance, yet they only address this interference in the spatial domain. The paper reveals that task interference is severe in the frequency domain of model parameters—specifically in the low-frequency region—and that spatial-domain operations (e.g., Ties-Merging, DARE, PCB-Merging) reduce frequency-domain variance by only 1–5%, leaving a critical gap unaddressed. Additionally, cost-free merging inevitably loses task-specific information, and existing expert-based methods require excessive storage and computation for practical edge deployment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes task vectors (differences between fine-tuned and pre-trained weights) from K models sharing a common pre-trained base, along with the pre-trained weights themselves. It outputs a single merged backbone plus K lightweight task-specific experts (each ~1% of total parameters) and a small router. At inference time, the router selects which expert to add to the backbone based on the input (image, text, or image+text), producing task-specific predictions such as class labels, generated text, or retrieval scores without any additional training.

## Method

FREE-Merging operates in two stages. First, FR-Merging applies a high-pass Fourier filter to each task vector before summation, removing low-frequency components that encode task-specific structure and cause interference; the merged backbone is then θ_m = θ_pre + Σ λ_i G(v_i), where G is the inverse Fourier transform of the filtered spectrum and λ_i are normalization coefficients ensuring output consistency. Second, a lightweight expert for each task is extracted by selecting the top-K most-magnitude-changed parameters and rescaling them by a factor μ_i = −E(M(v_i,d))·log(d) / (λ_i·E(v_i)) to preserve output magnitude. At inference, a router (e.g., a small MLP) dynamically activates the appropriate expert, adding it to the backbone in a Mixture-of-Experts fashion, so the model handles multiple tasks with only ~1% extra parameters per task and negligible additional latency.

## Results

On 8 image classification tasks with ViT-B/32, FREE-Merging achieves 89.7% average accuracy (vs. 90.5% for individual models), outperforming the best expert-based baseline EMR-Merging (87.7%) by 2.0% and Twin-Merging (87.8%) by 1.9%. For ViT-L/14, FREE-Merging reaches 93.7% average accuracy, surpassing EMR-Merging (92.8%) and Twin-Merging (92.7%). On LLaMa2-13B merging, FR-Merging alone achieves 61.5% average (AlpacaEval/GSM8K/MBPP), exceeding TIES-Merging (57.2%) and Twin-Merging (58.4%), while FREE-Merging reaches 61.8%. On multi-modal BEiT3, FREE-Merging attains 0.8093 COCO-Retrieval accuracy and 0.8168 NLVR2 accuracy, outperforming Twin-Merging (0.7937/0.7762) and EMR-Merging (0.7946/0.7475).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among cost-free methods, FR-Merging consistently outperforms all spatial-domain baselines: it improves over PCB-Merging (the strongest cost-free baseline) by 2.3% on ViT-B/32 and 1.4% on ViT-L/14 average accuracy, and over Task Arithmetic by 8.0% and 3.8% respectively. Against expert-based methods, FREE-Merging surpasses EMR-Merging by 2.0% (ViT-B/32) and 0.9% (ViT-L/14), and Twin-Merging by 1.9% and 1.0%, while requiring only 1% additional parameters per model versus 3% for EMR-Merging. On LLaMa2-13B, FR-Merging alone (61.5% avg) beats TIES-Merging (57.2%), DARE (56.5%), and even Twin-Merging (58.4%) and EMR-Merging (58.7%), demonstrating that frequency-domain filtering generalizes to large language models. On multi-modal BEiT3, FREE-Merging leads on COCO-Retrieval (0.8093 vs. 0.7937 Twin-Merging) and NLVR2 (0.8168 vs. 0.7762 Twin-Merging), though it trails slightly on VQAv2 (0.7297 vs. 0.7232 Twin-Merging). For language models (RoBERTa, T0-3B, Qwen-14B), FREE-Merging achieves the best average (73.87%) over Twin-Merging (72.22%) and EMR-Merging (70.76%), with the largest margin on RoBERTa full fine-tuning (80.16% vs. 78.29%). The transferability analysis (Table 8) further shows FR-Merging adds 4.7–5.5% on top of Ties-Merging and Breadcrumbs, and 0.9–0.6% on TSVM, confirming it is composable with existing methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the task-vector formulation from Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2022) and the magnitude-based parameter selection from Ties-Merging (Yadav et al., 2024), but departs fundamentally by shifting the analysis from the spatial (weight) domain to the frequency domain. All prior model merging methods—Fisher Merging, Ties-Merging, DARE, PCB-Merging—address task interference through spatial operations (sign conflicts, magnitude thresholds, Fisher matrices), yet the paper demonstrates these reduce frequency-domain variance by only 1–5% versus 20–24% for the proposed high-pass filter. The lightweight expert module extends the MoE routing paradigm (Shazeer et al., 2016) and the top-K extraction idea from Ties-Merging by adding a theoretically motivated rescaling factor, positioning the work as the first to apply Fourier filtering to neural network parameters in the merging context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of frequency-domain interference as the dominant source of merging performance loss provides a new diagnostic lens that could inform future merging methods beyond this specific pipeline. The 'no free lunch' theorem (Theorem 5.1) formally justifies why cost-free merging cannot fully recover individual model performance, grounding the need for task-specific experts. Practical limitations include the requirement of a shared pretrained base model, the dependence on router quality (though robustness to random routing is demonstrated), and the fact that gains over the strongest baselines (Twin-Merging, EMR-Merging) remain modest at 1–2%. Open questions include whether the frequency-domain interference phenomenon holds for non-transformer architectures, whether adaptive (per-layer or per-head) cutoff frequencies could further improve results, and how the method scales to merging models with different pretrained bases.
