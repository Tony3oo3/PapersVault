## Problem

Existing recovery-based deep incomplete multi-view clustering methods assume all views share a similar feature space, overlooking the heterogeneity in distributions across views, and treat per-sample recovery independently without leveraging intra-view structural or cross-view clustering consistency. This leads to large distribution discrepancies between recovered and true data, degrading downstream clustering quality especially at high missing rates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251159/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N samples across V views, where each view v has its own dimensionality d_v, along with binary indicators w_i^v marking which views are missing per sample. It outputs recovered latent representations for missing views (in the common d-dimensional subspace) and final cluster assignments obtained by concatenating recovered and complete representations and applying k-means. The flow models operate in a standard Gaussian latent space, so the recovered representations are distributionally aligned with the true view-specific encoder outputs rather than being pixel-level reconstructions.

## Method

BURG comprises three jointly optimized modules: (1) view-specific encoder-decoder pairs project heterogeneous views into a common subspace with dual reconstruction (view-specific and common representations); (2) view-specific normalizing flows with affine coupling layers map encoder outputs to a standard Gaussian, and the consistent Gaussian embedding from all available views is inverse-transformed through the missing view's flow to perform cross-view distribution transfer; (3) a dual-consistency strategy guides recovery—neighbor-aware consistency transfers cross-view nearest-neighbor topology to the missing view, while prototypical consistency aligns both complete and recovered representations toward k-means prototypes derived solely from complete views, with a Shannon entropy regularizer preventing cluster collapse. Training proceeds in three stages: pre-training encoders/decoders and flows separately, then joint DTL optimization, then full optimization with both consistency losses (weighted by α and β).

## Results

BURG achieves the best clustering performance across all six datasets (CUB, HandWritten, CiteSeer, Animal, Reuters, YouTubeFace10) at all four missing rates (0.1–0.7), evaluated by ACC, NMI, and ARI. At the standard MR=0.5 setting, BURG leads the second-best method by 18.80% ACC on HandWritten (94.30% vs. 75.50%), 7.21% on CiteSeer (47.43% vs. 40.22%), and 2.19% on Animal (48.61% vs. 46.42%). On the largest dataset YouTubeFace10 (38,654 samples), BURG maintains ACC=80.69% and NMI=82.47% at MR=0.3, demonstrating strong scalability. Ablation confirms that the dual-consistency strategy (NAC+PC) is critical: removing both drops HandWritten ACC from 94.30% to 52.65% at MR=0.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BURG outperforms all nine baselines on every dataset and missing rate. The largest margins appear on HandWritten (18.80% ACC over ICMVC at MR=0.5) and CiteSeer (7.21% ACC over ICMVC), where the graph-based ICMVC is the strongest competitor but still trails BURG despite BURG not using adjacency graphs. Among GAN-based methods, GP-MVC is the closest competitor on CUB and YouTubeFace10 but falls 1.00–1.45% behind BURG in ACC at MR=0.5. Recovery-free methods (DIMVC, APADC) are consistently weaker than recovery-based approaches, with APADC particularly struggling on CiteSeer (30.10% ACC) and YouTubeFace10 (39.71% ACC). On datasets with inherently strong representations (Animal with deep features, Reuters with multilingual text), the gap narrows to ~1–2% ACC, suggesting BURG's distribution-transfer advantage is most pronounced when views are more heterogeneous.

## Relation to Prior Work

BURG departs from the dominant recovery-based DIMVC paradigm (GAN-based, graph-structured, and information-entropy-based methods) by reframing missing-view imputation as a cross-view distribution transfer problem rather than per-sample point-wise reconstruction. It repurposes Normalizing Flow architectures (affine coupling layers à la NICE/GLOW) in a novel way: each view's encoder output is mapped to a shared Gaussian latent space, and the missing view's representation is recovered by inverting the view-specific flow from the fused latent distribution. The dual-consistency mechanism (neighbor-aware + prototypical) is a new loss design that injects local topological and global clustering structure into the recovery process, addressing a gap the authors identify in prior methods that treat recovery as structurally agnostic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important limitation—inter-view heterogeneity in recovery—that prior DIMVC methods largely ignore, and the flow-based formulation provides an explicit, differentiable measure of distributional discrepancy that GAN-based or entropy-based alternatives lack. However, the method still relies on k-means for final clustering, the flow model introduces non-trivial computational overhead (three-stage training, per-view flow networks), and performance degrades noticeably at the highest missing rate (0.7). Open questions include whether the Gaussian latent assumption holds for highly heterogeneous view pairs, how the approach scales to very high-dimensional or continuous-view settings, and whether the dual-consistency guidance can be made adaptive rather than fixed-weight.
