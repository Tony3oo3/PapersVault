## Problem

Single-frame 3D hand pose estimation produces temporally jittery results due to self-occlusion and depth ambiguity, while existing sequential refinement methods designed for human body pose fail to capture the high degrees of freedom, rapid local finger movements, and coupled short-term/long-term motion patterns unique to hands. The paper addresses how to jointly model subtle short-term joint variations and stable global hand trends while leveraging consistent hand shape priors to disambiguate monocular estimation errors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250498/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D hand pose sequence of shape T×J×3 (default T=27 frames) produced by any single-frame estimator (e.g., HAMER, InterWild, DIR) as plug-and-play input, requiring no access to the original images. It outputs refined 3D joint positions (T×3J), frame-wise MANO pose parameters θ (T×45), a single frame-shared shape parameter β (10-dim), and corresponding MANO mesh vertices (T×3V). Evaluation uses root-aligned, bone-length-scaled MPJPE/MPVPE in millimeters plus an acceleration error metric for temporal smoothness.

## Method

Built on MotionBERT's dual-branch spatio-temporal transformer, the framework introduces two key mechanisms. First, a dynamic temporal convolution module (1D deformable convolution) is decoupled into joint-level and hand-level branches: the joint-level variant generates per-joint offsets and modulation weights to adaptively track rapid local motion, while the hand-level variant operates on spatially pooled global features to capture stable forearm/wrist trends; both are placed after temporal MHSA to leverage long-range context for reliable kernel prediction. Second, a hand prior memory consisting of M learnable tokens (M=8 optimal) is updated via multi-head cross-attention from global temporal features and injected as additional tokens into both spatial and temporal self-attention, providing shape disambiguation cues that mitigate depth and scale ambiguity. Training uses supervised smooth L1 loss on joints, vertices, pose, and shape parameters, plus an acceleration loss to enforce temporal smoothness.

## Results

On InterHand2.6M, the proposed method reduces MPJPE from 10.32 mm (InterWild) to 7.21 mm (30.1% reduction) and acceleration error from 7.18 to 4.54 mm/frame² (36.8% reduction). On Re:InterHand, improvements are even more pronounced: MPJPE drops from 7.55 mm (InterWild) to 4.86 mm (35.6% reduction) and Accel from 6.14 to 3.84 mm/frame² (44.2% reduction). On HanCo, the method improves HAMER's MPJPE from 10.1 to 7.3 mm, and on DexYCB it reduces MPJPE from 10.18 (HAMER) to 8.42 mm while cutting Accel from 12.97 to 7.51 mm/frame².

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 12 compared baselines across all four datasets. Against the two strongest single-frame two-hand methods, InterWild (Moon, CVPR 2023) and DIR (Ren, ICCV 2023), the proposed refinement reduces MPJPE by 30.1% and 29.6% respectively on InterHand2.6M, and by 35.6% and 31.0% on Re:InterHand. As a temporal refinement, it substantially surpasses MotionBERT (Zhu, ICCV 2023) which only achieves 8.13 MPJPE vs. 7.21 for the proposed method on InterHand2.6M, and also outperforms classical smoothing approaches (Savitzky-Golay, Gaussian1d, SmoothNet) that provide minimal gains. On HanCo, the method beats single-frame baselines EpipolarPose (10.5), MobRecon (9.9), HaMuCo (11.1), and HAMER (10.1) with 7.3 MPJPE. On DexYCB, it outperforms all temporal methods including VIBE (16.95), TCMR (16.03), S2HAND(V) (19.67), Deformer (13.64), and HAMER (10.18), achieving 8.42 MPJPE and 7.51 Accel. Notably, the method is plug-and-play: it improves any single-frame estimator (InterWild, DIR, HAMER) without retraining the front-end, demonstrating strong generalization across different pose estimation backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MotionBERT's dual-branch spatio-temporal transformer architecture, repurposing it from human body motion modeling to the fundamentally different domain of hand pose refinement. It adapts the deformable convolution mechanism (originally a 2D spatial tool) into a 1D temporal operator that dynamically adjusts receptive fields per joint, and introduces a learnable memory token pool to encode sequence-level hand shape priors. The work positions itself within the plug-and-play temporal refinement lineage (SmoothNet, SynSP) but explicitly departs from body-oriented methods, arguing that hand motion's higher degrees of freedom, faster local variations, and coupling of global/short-term dynamics require decoupled joint-level and hand-level temporal modeling rather than the uniform temporal convolutions or attention used for full-body pose.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a notable gap: while temporal refinement methods for 3D human body pose are well-developed, sequential 3D hand pose estimation has received comparatively little attention despite being critical for VR/AR and HCI applications. The plug-and-play design (accepting outputs from any single-frame estimator like HAMER or InterWild) makes the approach broadly applicable, and the 30–35% MPJPE reductions across four diverse datasets demonstrate practical impact. Open questions include whether the hand prior memory generalizes to longer sequences or multi-person settings, whether the decoupled joint/hand-level modeling transfers to other fine-grained articulations (face, feet), and whether the MANO-dependent supervision limits applicability to non-MANO hand models.
