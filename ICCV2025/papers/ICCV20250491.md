## Problem

Early visual token pruning in VLMs (e.g., FastV) appears to maintain strong performance across most benchmarks, but the paper reveals this masks a critical flaw: the attention-based pruning criteria, due to RoPE's long-term decay property, disproportionately retains bottom image tokens while discarding top ones, causing catastrophic failure on vision-centric tasks like localization. Furthermore, the paper shows that many standard vision-language benchmarks do not actually require fine-grained visual grounding, making them poor indicators of whether a pruning strategy preserves visual capability.

## Input / Output

The method operates on an adapter-style VLM where an RGB image is encoded by SigLIP ViT-SO400M into n patch features, projected via a one-layer MLP to Llama-2 7B's embedding dimension, and concatenated with text prompt embeddings. After the first pruning stage at layer 8, a fraction (1-R)% of visual tokens are retained; after the second stage at layer 16, only (1-R)²% of the original tokens remain (as few as 3.3% in the paper's configuration). The output is standard autoregressive text generation, with the pruned tokens simply removed from the sequence while their positional indices are preserved.

## Method

FEATHER is a two-stage visual token pruning strategy that addresses the positional bias in attention-based token selection. The key modification is computing attention scores from the last text token without applying RoPE (φ_{-R}), which eliminates the long-term decay effect that causes bottom tokens to receive artificially higher scores in early layers. In the first stage (layer 8), FEATHER ensembles this RoPE-free attention criterion with uniform spatial sampling (stride 3) to ensure broad image coverage while the attention signal is still noisy. In the second stage (layer 16), it applies only the RoPE-free attention criterion more aggressively, retaining (1-R)²% of the original tokens, since by this depth the attention scores reliably identify task-relevant regions. This 'feathering the throttle' analogy reflects gradually increasing pruning aggressiveness as the criteria becomes more discriminative.

## Results

FEATHER achieves a 64% FLOPS reduction while retaining only 3.3% of visual tokens after layer 16, yet suffers only a 26% average localization performance drop compared to the unpruned baseline. On localization benchmarks (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg, OCID-Ref), FEATHER's ensemble criteria (φ_{-R}+φ_{uniform}) at K=8 yields an average score of 35.6, compared to 23.3 for the original FastV criteria at the same layer. Across non-localization tasks (TextVQA, GQA, VQAv2, VizWiz, POPE, TallyQA, VSR, AI2D), the ensemble achieves an average of 62.7 at K=3 and 63.6 at K=8, with minimal degradation relative to the unpruned model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FastV (K=3, φ_original, 68% FLOPS reduction), FEATHER (64% FLOPS reduction) achieves more than 5× average improvement on localization tasks (e.g., RefCOCO: 27.7 vs 6.7 at K=3; 38.8 vs 26.3 at K=8) and a 7.8% improvement on non-localization tasks. Against PyramidDrop (K=[8,16,24], φ_original, 65% FLOPS reduction), FEATHER achieves a 36% average improvement on localization and a 1.5% improvement on non-localization tasks. The key differentiator is the RoPE-free attention criterion (φ_{-R}), which eliminates the positional bias that causes FastV to disproportionately prune top-image tokens; at K=3, removing RoPE alone yields a 183% average localization improvement over φ_original. The ensemble with uniform sampling (φ_{-R}+φ_uniform) further boosts localization by 30% over φ_{-R} alone at K=8, while incurring only a 1% additional FLOPS cost. Notably, even with only 3.3% of tokens retained after layer 16, FEATHER's localization performance drops just 26% from the unpruned baseline, demonstrating that the pruning strategy—not the token count—is the critical factor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on FastV, which prunes visual tokens after shallow LLM layers using attention scores from the last text token, and extends the multi-stage pruning idea from PyramidDrop. The key conceptual departure is diagnosing that FastV's attention-based criteria inherits a positional bias from RoPE's long-term decay property, causing disproportionate retention of bottom-image tokens. The proposed FEATHER method addresses this with a minimal architectural change (removing RoPE from the pruning attention computation) combined with uniform sampling and a two-stage pruning schedule, positioning itself as a targeted fix within the within-LLM visual token compression family rather than a new compression paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Beyond the practical 5× localization improvement, the paper's most impactful finding is meta-level: many widely-used VLM benchmarks (GQA, TallyQA, AI2D, POPE) do not genuinely assess fine-grained visual grounding, as performance remains strong even when all visual information is removed before the LLM. This challenges the field's reliance on these benchmarks for evaluating both VLM capabilities and acceleration methods. Open questions include whether the RoPE-induced bias generalizes to other positional encoding schemes and architectures, whether removing RoPE for pruning introduces subtle attention distortions, and how to construct benchmarks that truly stress-test visual perception in VLMs.
