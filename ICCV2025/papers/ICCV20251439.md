## Problem

Robot manipulation learning is bottlenecked by the high cost of action-labeled data, while abundant interaction-rich video data remains underutilized. Prior video pre-training for robotics focused on static frame-level details or pixel-level prediction, ignoring the motion dynamics that humans naturally attend to when learning skills. The paper asks whether an autoregressive pre-training approach analogous to LLMs can be applied to video data by identifying a motion-centric representation that is hardware-agnostic and transferable to real robot actions.

## Input / Output

The Latent Motion Tokenizer consumes pairs of consecutive RGB frames (encoded via a frozen ViT into patch features) and outputs 8 discrete tokens per frame pair from a 128-entry codebook. Moto-GPT takes the initial frame's visual features, a language instruction's text features (via frozen T5), and a sequence of motion token chunks as input, autoregressively producing the next motion token and, during fine-tuning, N robot actions (Δx, Δθ, Δgrip) per time step via dedicated action query tokens. The 50% random masking of attention from action queries to motion tokens enables direct action prediction at inference without generating motion tokens first.

## Method

Moto introduces a three-stage pipeline centered on Latent Motion Tokens as a bridging 'language.' First, a VQ-VAE-based tokenizer (M-Former encoder + ViT decoder) is trained unsupervised to compress inter-frame dynamics into 8 discrete tokens, with the decoder reconstructing frame t from frame t−1 and the tokens, forcing the tokens to encode motion. Second, a GPT-style transformer (Moto-GPT, 98M params) is pre-trained via next-motion-token prediction on video-instruction pairs, learning motion priors without any action labels. Third, co-fine-tuning inserts learnable action query tokens after each motion token chunk, jointly optimizing the original motion prediction loss and a Smooth-L1/BCE action loss, while randomly masking 50% of attention from action queries to motion tokens to reduce overfitting to ground-truth conditions and enable efficient inference. This design preserves learned motion priors during fine-tuning and enables cross-embodiment transfer, including from human hand videos (SSV2) to robot actions.

## Results

On SIMPLER, Moto achieves an overall success rate of 61.4%, surpassing RT-2-X (60.7%), RT-1-X (53.4%), OpenVLA fine-tuned (34.9%), OpenVLA (24.8%), and Octo-Base (16.9%), while using only 98M parameters and no action labels during pre-training. On CALVIN (ABC→D), Moto achieves an average task chain length of 3.10, outperforming GR-1 (3.06), SuSIE (2.69), RoboFlamingo (2.47), and MT-R3M (0.93), using only static RGB input. In real-world evaluation, Moto improves average success rate from 23.33% (without motion tokens) to 60%, with 20–30% gains under novel-object and visual-distractor conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On SIMPLER, Moto (61.4% overall) narrowly beats RT-2-X (60.7%) and clearly outperforms RT-1-X (53.4%), OpenVLA fine-tuned (34.9%), OpenVLA (24.8%), and Octo-Base (16.9%), despite having only 98M parameters versus RT-2-X's 55B backbone. On CALVIN, Moto (avg. length 3.10) edges out GR-1 (3.06) while using only static RGB input (GR-1 additionally uses gripper RGB and proprioceptive state), and substantially outperforms SuSIE (2.69), RoboFlamingo (2.47), and MT-R3M (0.93). The ablation variant Moto w/o Motion Token (48.0% on SIMPLER, 2.14 on CALVIN) confirms that the motion-token pre-training contributes 13.4 and 0.96 points respectively over training from scratch. Notably, Moto matches or exceeds RT-2-X on Pick Coke Can (74.0% vs. 78.7%—slightly behind) and Move Near (60.4% vs. 77.9%—behind), but leads on Open/Close Drawer (43.1% vs. 25.0%), suggesting the motion prior is particularly beneficial for dexterous, multi-step actions.

## Relation to Prior Work

Moto builds on the VLA model lineage (RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA, Octo) and the emerging trend of video pre-training for robotics (GR-1, GR-2, MT-R3M), but departs from these by replacing pixel-level or patch-level video tokens with discrete latent motion tokens that capture inter-frame dynamics via a VQ-VAE-based tokenizer. It also draws on latent action learning from Genie, LAPO, and DynaMo, but differs by using these tokens as an autoregressive pre-training objective for an end-to-end policy rather than for RL reward shaping or representation learning. The co-fine-tuning strategy—retaining next-motion-token prediction while inserting action query tokens with partially masked attention—is a novel architectural choice not seen in prior VLA fine-tuning approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core bottleneck in robot learning: the high cost of action-labeled data, by showing that motion-focused video pre-training can substitute for large amounts of supervised trajectories (52.5% success with 1% labeled data vs. 0% without motion priors). The finding that a static-RGB-only model outperforms GR-1 (which uses gripper RGB and proprioceptive states) challenges the assumption that richer sensory inputs are necessary, suggesting motion dynamics are the key signal. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more complex multi-step manipulation, whether the 128-token codebook is sufficient for diverse embodiments, and how to incorporate more diverse and longer human videos for truly generalist policies.
