## Problem

Sparse IMU sensor placement introduces inherent uncertainty in unseen joint rotations that compounds over time, yet existing methods only mitigate this indirectly through physical constraints (contact probability, ground reaction forces, joint torques) rather than explicitly modeling the uncertainty in the data. No prior approach directly represents the distribution of plausible poses from limited sensor signals, leaving a gap between deterministic single-pose prediction and the true ambiguity of the reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250110/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes global (unnormalized) acceleration and orientation signals from up to six IMUs placed on the pelvis, wrists, lower legs, and head, deliberately avoiding pelvis-centered normalization so that the pelvis sensor can be dropped entirely. It outputs a matrix Fisher distribution over SO(3) for each of the 24 SMPL joints—encoding both the most likely rotation and its uncertainty—along with a predicted body translation velocity. During training, five rejection-sampled poses are propagated through layers; at inference, only the distribution mode is forwarded for efficiency.

## Method

ProbIP replaces deterministic pose regression with a probabilistic model that predicts matrix Fisher distribution parameters over SO(3) for every joint. The core architectural unit is the RU-Mamba block, which decouples rotation prediction (R) from a scalar uncertainty value (u ∈ [0,1]) and reconstructs the distribution parameter as F = U·diag(exp(s_α · u)·S)·Vᵀ via SVD of R, where s_α is a per-layer scaling factor. Progressive Distribution Narrowing assigns ascending scaling factors (2→8) across successive layers so early layers maintain broad, diverse motion distributions while later layers progressively concentrate on the most probable trajectory. Training optimizes a weighted negative log-likelihood with gradient-stabilization coefficients tied to each layer's scaling factor, supplemented by angular-rotation losses on both the mode and five sampled poses, plus a velocity regression loss. The Mamba-based state-space backbone enables parallel long-sequence training and iterative real-time inference with minimal per-frame overhead.

## Results

On the DIP-IMU test set, ProbIP with 6 sensors achieves 13.65° SIP Error, 6.57° Angular Error, 4.77 cm Position Error, and 5.61 cm Mesh Error, outperforming the previous SOTA DynaIP* (13.78°, 7.07°, 4.98 cm, 5.99 cm). Notably, ProbIP with only 5 sensors (13.63° SIP, 7.01° Ang) surpasses DynaIP* trained with 6 sensors on both SIP and Angular Error. Across five public datasets (DIP-IMU, CIP, Natural Motion, Andy, UNIPD), ProbIP consistently achieves the lowest Angular Error, with the largest margin on Andy (2.88° vs. 3.45° for DynaIP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProbIP outperforms all five baselines (DIP, TransPose, TIP, PIP, DynaIP) on every metric in the 6-sensor setting, with the most significant gains over the closest competitor DynaIP*: 0.50° lower Angular Error (6.57° vs. 7.07°) and 0.21 cm lower Mesh Error (5.61 vs. 5.99 cm). The advantage widens dramatically under sparser configurations: with 4 sensors, ProbIP achieves 8.18° Angular Error versus DynaIP's 9.98° and TransPose's 13.40°; with 2 sensors, ProbIP reaches 12.49° versus DynaIP's 18.72°. A key finding is that ProbIP with 5 sensors (no pelvis IMU) beats DynaIP* with 6 sensors on both SIP Error (13.63° vs. 13.78°) and Angular Error (7.01° vs. 7.07°), demonstrating that the probabilistic formulation compensates for the missing pelvis sensor. On the Natural Motion dataset, ProbIP shows a relative weakness in SIP Error (21.62° vs. DynaIP's 15.78°) due to misinterpreting subtle movements during long sitting sequences as standing, a limitation the authors attribute to the absence of explicit physical constraints. Overall, ProbIP's non-deterministic, distribution-based approach provides the largest performance gap over deterministic baselines as sensor count decreases, with the margin growing from ~0.5° at 6 sensors to ~6° at 2 sensors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the IMU-based human pose estimation lineage (DIP → TransPose → TIP → PIP → DynaIP) by replacing the deterministic, physics-constrained paradigm with a probabilistic one. It builds directly on the Mamba state-space architecture for temporal modeling and on the matrix Fisher distribution over SO(3) previously applied to single-frame pose estimation (Sengupta et al., ICCV 2021; Mohlin et al., NeurIPS 2020), but is the first to use it for temporal, multi-frame motion prediction with progressive uncertainty narrowing. The RU-Mamba block and Progressive Distribution Narrowing strategy are novel architectural contributions that decouple rotation prediction from uncertainty scaling, addressing a training instability specific to matrix Fisher parameterization that prior single-frame methods did not encounter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that wearable motion capture can operate with fewer sensors (as few as 2–4) while maintaining accuracy comparable to 6-sensor SOTA, reducing hardware cost and user burden for applications in healthcare monitoring, VR/AR, and human-computer interaction. The finding that a purely probabilistic, end-to-end model can match or exceed methods relying on physical constraints (contact probability, ground reaction forces, physics simulators) suggests a simpler, more generalizable training pipeline. Limitations include degraded performance on long-duration static motions (e.g., sitting in the Natural Motion dataset) where the model occasionally misinterprets micro-movements, and the approach has only been validated on upper-body-centric sensor placements. Open questions include whether the probabilistic framework extends to other sensor modalities or to longer-horizon prediction where distribution narrowing may become overly restrictive.
