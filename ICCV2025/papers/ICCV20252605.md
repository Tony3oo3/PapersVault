## Problem

Masked Image Modeling (MIM) representations consistently underperform Joint-Embedding Architectures (JEAs) in out-of-the-box high-level perception tasks, yet the underlying reason has remained unclear. This paper identifies that MIM's [cls] token spreads attention almost uniformly across all patches rather than selectively attending to semantically relevant ones, resulting in ineffective aggregation of the rich information actually stored in patch tokens.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252605/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on the N patch tokens (each of dimension D) from the final encoder block of a pre-trained ViT, without modifying the backbone. It produces a single D-dimensional global image representation via a weighted sum of patch tokens, serving as a drop-in replacement for the [cls] token or naive average pooling. The only trainable component is a single linear regressor (D+1 parameters) trained jointly with a standard linear classification head, adding negligible overhead relative to the classifier's (D+1)×K parameters.

## Method

Selective Aggregation replaces the standard [cls] token or uniform patch averaging with an Attention-based Multiple Instance Learning Pooling (AbMILP) mechanism. A lightweight linear model t: R^D → R is applied independently to each patch token, and the resulting scalar scores are normalized via softmax to yield importance weights in [0,1]. The global representation is then computed as the weighted sum of all patch tokens (optionally including the [cls] token), effectively letting the model dynamically select which patches carry the most relevant semantic information. The empirical analysis motivating this design shows that MIM's [cls] token exhibits near-maximal entropy in its attention to patches (approaching the theoretical bound of 5.27 for 196 patches), while JEA models and fine-tuned MAEs show significantly lower, more selective attention patterns. This approach requires no fine-tuning of the pre-trained backbone and consistently improves linear probing accuracy across MAE, SimMIM, MaskFeat, BEIT-v2, I-JEPA, and CAPI models.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k linear probing, Selective Aggregation consistently improves MIM models over both [cls] and average pooling: MAE ViT-B goes from 67.8% ([cls]) to 71.6% (Selective), MAE ViT-L from 75.8% to 77.4%, and SimMIM ViT-B from 51.5% to 62.8%. On more challenging tasks (Table 2), gains are even larger: CAPI ViT-L on CUB-200 jumps from 25.9% to 79.7%, and MAE ViT-B on ImageNet-1% improves from 39.1% to 48.3%. For JEA models (DINO-v2, iBOT, MoCo-v3), Selective Aggregation matches or slightly exceeds [cls] performance, confirming that JEAs already aggregate information effectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Selective Aggregation outperforms both [cls] and average pooling across all six MIM models tested (MAE, SimMIM, MaskFeat, BEIT-v2, I-JEPA, CAPI), with gains ranging from +2.0% (BEIT-v2 ViT-B: 78.9→80.9) to +11.3% (SimMIM ViT-B: 51.5→62.8) on ImageNet-1k. Against JEA baselines, MIMs with Selective Aggregation close much of the gap: MAE ViT-B (71.6%) approaches DINO (76.2%) and MoCo-v3 (75.2%), while CAPI ViT-L (82.4%) surpasses all JEA models except DINO-v2 (83.5%). For JEA models themselves, Selective Aggregation provides negligible improvement over [cls] (e.g., DINO-v2: 83.2→83.5; MoCo-v3: 75.1→75.2), confirming that JEAs already perform selective attention naturally. On fine-grained and low-shot tasks, the advantage is most dramatic: CAPI ViT-L on CUB-200 improves by +53.8 points (25.9→79.7), and MAE ViT-B on Stanford Cars gains +27.0 points (31.8→58.8), far exceeding the ImageNet-1k gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on prior analyses of MIM vs. JEA representation differences (Park et al. 2023, Balestriero & LeCun 2024, Xie et al. 2023) that noted MIMs focus on local structure and produce weaker global descriptors, but provides a more precise mechanistic diagnosis: the [cls] token in MIMs recycles its own representation and spreads remaining attention nearly uniformly across patches. It departs from recent work proposing complex probing with additional attention layers [12, 22] by showing that a lightweight AbMILP-style linear regressor suffices to recover the high-level information already present in MIM patch tokens. The Selective Aggregation mechanism repurposes the Multiple-Instance Learning pooling paradigm [43] as a drop-in replacement for the [cls] token or naive average pooling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that MIM [cls] tokens are essentially non-functional as global descriptors (high self-attention + near-maximal entropy over patches) provides a clear, actionable diagnosis for why MIMs underperform out-of-the-box, and the proposed fix is trivially cheap (one extra linear layer) and broadly applicable across MIM variants and scales. This is particularly important for practitioners who cannot afford fine-tuning due to data, compute, or expertise constraints. Limitations include reliance on single pretraining configurations from original authors and the fact that the analysis is restricted to ImageNet-pretrained ViTs, leaving open whether the uniform-attention pathology persists in domain-specific or multi-modal MIM pretraining. A natural follow-up question is whether the MIM pretraining objective itself could be modified to encourage selective [cls] aggregation, potentially eliminating the need for post-hoc correction.
