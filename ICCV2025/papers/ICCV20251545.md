## Problem

CLIP and LVLMs are mutually dependent—CLIP provides the visual backbone for LVLMs, yet LVLMs have not been systematically leveraged to improve CLIP training data. Existing augmentation methods either operate in single-modality (ignoring cross-modal correlations) or rely on cascaded captioning+LLM pipelines that introduce error propagation, and all produce only long-form descriptions, missing the multi-grained and negative supervision signals that could sharpen CLIP's discriminative ability.

## Input / Output

The LVLM refinement pipeline consumes each web-crawled image together with its raw alt-text and produces four structured outputs: a long positive description, a long negative description (plausible but semantically deviant), a set of short positive tags, and a set of short negative tags. Because CLIP's text encoder is limited to 77 tokens, long descriptions are segmented by LVLM-generated punctuation and a random segment is sampled per training iteration. The final HQ-CLIP model takes an image and a text query and outputs a similarity score usable for zero-shot classification, cross-modal retrieval, or as a vision tower for downstream LVLMs like LLaVA-1.5.

## Method

The pipeline first uses GPT-4o to generate 10K high-quality recaption exemplars, then SFTs a compact Qwen2-VL-7B on those pairs to achieve near-72B captioning quality at 9× lower compute, and deploys it to process 150M DFN-Large pairs into VLM-150M. For training, HQ-CLIP extends standard bidirectional contrastive loss with two additional objectives: (1) a Hard Negative Identification (HNI) loss that pulls image embeddings away from LVLM-generated negative descriptions/tags, gated by a curriculum parameter k_i that suspends the loss whenever the model misclassifies standard in-batch negatives; and (2) a Short-Tag Classification (STC) loss using multi-label binary cross-entropy over a top-K tag vocabulary to preserve categorical semantics that long descriptions can obscure. Training mixes original and LVLM-refined captions at a 25:75 ratio to avoid the distributional homogeneity that degrades generalization when using only synthetic text.

## Results

On the DataComp benchmark (Large scale, 147M pairs), HQ-CLIP achieves 70.6% ImageNet zero-shot accuracy, 60.6% average IN-shifts, 57.6% VTAB, and 60.9% average retrieval, yielding a 58.6% average across 38 datasets — the best among all methods at comparable or larger data scales. In cross-modal retrieval, HQ-CLIP reaches 77.9% on COCO and 60.9% average, surpassing DFN-2B (trained on 2B pairs) which achieves 77.3% on COCO. When deployed as the vision encoder in LLaVA-1.5, HQ-CLIP (ViT-B) scores 1574.0 on MME and 29.7 on MMStar, outperforming LAION-400M (1402.9, 29.1) and DFN† (1452.1, 28.3) at similar pre-training scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At the same 147M data scale, HQ-CLIP outperforms the reproduced DFN† baseline by +1.9 on ImageNet, +5.6 on IN-shifts, +2.8 on VTAB, and +6.4 on retrieval average. Against DataComp (140M), the gains are larger: +7.5 on ImageNet and +11.1 on retrieval. Notably, HQ-CLIP (147M) surpasses LaCLIP (400M) by +1.2 on ImageNet and +4.0 on retrieval, and beats OpenAI CLIP (400M) by +2.3 on ImageNet and +8.2 on retrieval, despite using roughly 2.7× less training data. In retrieval specifically, HQ-CLIP exceeds DFN-2B (2B pairs) on COCO (52.5% vs. 51.9%) and Flickr30K (77.9% vs. 77.3%). For LVLM integration, HQ-CLIP as LLaVA-1.5's vision tower leads on MME (1574.0) and MMStar (29.7) over LAION-400M and DFN†, though LAION-400M retains a slight edge on MMBench (54.6 vs. 52.8). The ablation (Table 6) confirms that the HNI loss contributes +0.8 average and STC adds a further +0.4 over mixed training alone, with the largest gains on retrieval (+1.1 combined) and fine-grained attribution tasks (+1.3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP training paradigm (Radford et al., 2021) and the data filtering approach of DFN (Fang et al., 2023) by introducing an LVLM-driven data refinement pipeline that generates multi-grained bidirectional descriptions (positive/negative, long/short) from a single unified model, departing from prior single-modality augmentation (LaCLIP, WhatIf) and cascade architectures (CapFusion, VeCLIP). The training framework further extends standard contrastive learning with two novel loss components—Hard Negative Identification and Short-Tag Classification—that exploit the generated negative descriptions and categorical tags as additional supervision signals. Conceptually, the paper positions itself as the first step toward a self-reinforcing cycle where LVLMs (which depend on CLIP as their visual backbone) reciprocally improve CLIP's training data quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a 150M-sample refined dataset can match or exceed a 2B-sample dataset on retrieval benchmarks (COCO +0.6%, Flickr +0.6%) suggests that data quality via LVLM enrichment can partially substitute for raw scale, an important consideration given the computational costs of billion-scale training. However, the margins are narrow and the advantage does not extend to classification metrics, where the 150M model still trails the 2B model. Open questions include whether this quality-over-quantity effect persists at even larger scales, whether the multi-grained bidirectional description framework can be further exploited (e.g., for LVLM training itself), and whether the approach generalizes beyond the CommonPool/DFN data source. The publicly released VLM-150M dataset and HQ-CLIP models provide a practical resource for the community.
