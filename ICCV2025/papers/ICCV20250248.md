## Problem

Optimization-based 3DGS methods struggle with sparse unposed views due to insufficient geometric priors, while feed-forward Gaussian models like NoPoSplat are limited to a fixed small number of input images (typically 2). This leaves a gap for reconstructing scenes from arbitrary numbers of unposed sparse views: can locally generated Gaussian models be incrementally registered into a single globally consistent 3D representation?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250248/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N unposed RGB images (tested at 2, 8, 16, and 32 views) with no camera poses or intrinsics, processed through a pre-trained NoPoSplat backbone that generates local 3D Gaussians per image pair. It outputs a merged global 3D Gaussian scene, per-frame Sim(3) camera poses (rotation, translation, scale), and rendered novel views. The pipeline is incremental: two initial images seed the main map, and each subsequent image's sub-Gaussians are registered and merged before the next frame is processed, followed by a final global refinement with adaptive pruning.

## Method

RegGS formulates 3DGS registration as aligning two Gaussian Mixture Models in Sim(3) space, using the Mixture 2-Wasserstein (MW2) distance as a differentiable structural similarity metric. The MW2 is computed via an entropy-regularized Sinkhorn algorithm that reduces the infinite-dimensional W2 optimization to a discrete linear assignment over Gaussian component pairs, with log-space accumulation and Cholesky decomposition for numerical stability on GPU. A joint registration module simultaneously optimizes three losses — MW2 (coarse global distribution alignment), photometric L1 (pixel-level rendering consistency via the 3DGS differentiable renderer), and depth geometry (rendered depth map agreement) — using quaternion-parameterized Sim(3) with projected gradient updates. This coarse-to-fine multi-loss strategy avoids local minima that any single term would produce, and the entire pipeline is end-to-end differentiable with gradients flowing through the Sinkhorn transport plan and the 3DGS rendering equations.

## Results

On RE10K, RegGS achieves 24.27 PSNR / 0.853 SSIM with only 2 input views, surpassing NoPoSplat (23.25 PSNR) and CF-3DGS (19.33 PSNR) by a wide margin. At 16 views, RegGS reaches 28.66 PSNR / 0.913 SSIM, outperforming MASt3R+3DGS (27.02 PSNR) and CF-3DGS (23.03 PSNR). For pose estimation, RegGS attains an ATE of 0.023 at 8 views on RE10K, roughly 10× more accurate than CF-3DGS (0.237) and VideoLifter (0.335). On the ACID drone dataset, RegGS similarly leads with 24.29 PSNR at 2 views and 27.75 PSNR at 16 views.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RegGS outperforms all unposed baselines on PSNR and SSIM across every view count on both RE10K and ACID. Against NoPoSplat (the closest feed-forward competitor at 2 views), RegGS gains +1.02 PSNR and +0.021 SSIM while also supporting 8/16/32 views that NoPoSplat cannot handle. CF-3DGS, the strongest optimization-based baseline, trails RegGS by 4.95 PSNR at 2 views and 5.63 PSNR at 16 views on RE10K. MASt3R+3DGS (a posed SfM pipeline) is the only method that approaches RegGS at 32 views (28.31 vs 28.33 PSNR), but requires known poses and still lags by 1.64 PSNR at 16 views. NoPeNeRF and VideoLifter are consistently far behind (≤16.7 PSNR), with NoPeNeRF failing to converge on ACID (6.84 PSNR at 16 views). For pose estimation (ATE), RegGS achieves 0.023 at 8 views on RE10K, an order of magnitude better than CF-3DGS (0.237) and VideoLifter (0.335), confirming that the MW2-based registration yields substantially more accurate camera poses.

## Relation to Prior Work

RegGS builds directly on NoPoSplat's feed-forward 3D Gaussian prediction as its local generation backbone, but departs from the feed-forward paradigm's fundamental limitation of handling only a fixed small number of input views. The key conceptual contribution is repurposing optimal transport theory—specifically the Mixture 2-Wasserstein (MW2) distance between Gaussian Mixture Models—into a differentiable registration metric for aligning 3D Gaussian sets in Sim(3) space, a use case not previously explored in the 3DGS literature. This positions the work at the intersection of feed-forward 3D reconstruction (NoPoSplat, Splatt3R) and optimization-based pose-free methods (CF-3DGS, NoPe-NeRF, VideoLifter), bridging the gap by incrementally registering locally-generated Gaussians rather than optimizing a global representation from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck: feed-forward 3DGS models are constrained to 2 input views, severely limiting coverage in real-world sparse-view scenarios. The MW2-based registration framework offers a principled, differentiable way to merge local Gaussian reconstructions, and the joint loss design (MW2 + photometric + depth) provides a reusable recipe for 3DGS alignment. However, the method inherits the quality ceiling of the feed-forward backbone, training cost scales with view count due to MW2 computation, and registration can fail under large inter-frame motion—open questions that limit immediate deployment. Whether the optimal-transport formulation generalizes beyond Sim(3) (e.g., to non-rigid or dynamic scenes) remains unexplored.
