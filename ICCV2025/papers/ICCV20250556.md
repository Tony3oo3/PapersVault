## Problem

Existing fMRI-to-video reconstruction methods (e.g., NeuroClips, MinD-Video) rely on implicitly aligning brain activity with CLIP's hidden feature space, which is fragile because CLIP encodes highly semantic information while fMRI voxels carry multi-granularity signals. Moreover, video reconstruction demands capturing spatiotemporal dynamics—object motion, scene transitions, and temporal coherence—that prior approaches struggle to represent holistically, leading to poor video consistency and semantic accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250556/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes fMRI signals at 2-second temporal resolution (matching the fMRI acquisition rate) corresponding to 6-frame video clips, processed through a MindEyeV2 backbone and a motion projection into spatiotemporal embeddings. At inference, four decoupled outputs—binary key-object segmentation masks, 51-class concept labels, a GPT-2-generated scene caption, and a blurry video in Stable Diffusion VAE latent space—jointly condition an AnimateDiff T2V diffusion model to produce the final reconstructed video. The segmentation masks are rescaled to [0.5, 1] and multiplied onto both the control image and blurry video to emphasize the key object.

## Method

NEURONS decouples fMRI-to-video reconstruction into four progressive sub-tasks that emulate the hierarchical specialization of the human visual cortex: (1) key object segmentation via a text-driven VAE video decoder with cross-attention between video and text embeddings, (2) concept recognition via a multi-label classifier over 51 WordNet-derived categories, (3) scene description via a GPT-2 decoder trained with prefix language modeling, and (4) blurry video reconstruction via the same VAE decoder with a reconstruction head trained with MAE loss in the Stable Diffusion VAE latent space. A sinusoidal weight scheduling strategy (w = 1 + 9|sin(C/T·π)|) progressively emphasizes each loss over staggered training periods, mirroring the cortex's bottom-up processing hierarchy. At inference, all four task outputs serve as explicit, interpretable conditioning signals for a pre-trained AnimateDiff T2V diffusion model, replacing the brittle implicit CLIP-space alignment used by prior work.

## Results

On the cc2017 fMRI-to-video dataset, NEURONS achieves 0.863 2-way accuracy, 0.262 50-way accuracy, and 0.934 CLIP-pcc (spatiotemporal consistency) averaged across three subjects. The headline improvements over the strongest baseline NeuroClips are a 26.6% relative gain in CLIP-pcc (0.934 vs. 0.738) and a 19.1% relative gain in 50-way accuracy (0.262 vs. 0.220). Per-subject results are consistent, with subject 3 reaching the highest 50-way score of 0.278 and CLIP-pcc of 0.937.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NEURONS outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest competitor NeuroClips [10], it improves CLIP-pcc from 0.738 to 0.934 (+26.6% relative), 50-way accuracy from 0.220 to 0.262 (+19.1%), and 2-way accuracy from 0.834 to 0.863 (+3.5%). Against MinD-Video [4], the gains are even larger on CLIP-pcc (0.934 vs. 0.408, +129%) and 50-way (0.262 vs. 0.197, +33%). The older baselines—Wen [45], Wang [42], and Kupershmidt [19]—trail by a wide margin on all metrics, with CLIP-pcc values below 0.41. MindAnimator [23] reports only 2-way (0.830) and CLIP-pcc (0.428), both substantially below NEURONS. The paper also notes that NEURONS exhibits lower standard deviations across subjects, indicating more consistent performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

NEURONS extends the fMRI-to-video reconstruction paradigm established by NeuroClips and MinD-Video, which both rely on implicitly aligning brain model outputs with CLIP's hidden feature space. The key conceptual departure is replacing this monolithic alignment with a four-task decomposition (key object segmentation, concept recognition, scene description, blurry video reconstruction) that explicitly mirrors the hierarchical functional specialization of the human visual cortex. The brain model backbone is MindEyeV2, and the inference pipeline reuses AnimateDiff's T2V diffusion model, but the decoupled sub-task outputs serve as structured conditioning signals rather than a single CLIP embedding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 26.6% improvement in spatiotemporal consistency (CLIP-pcc) over NeuroClips is the most practically significant result, addressing a core weakness of prior fMRI-to-video methods where generated videos lacked temporal coherence. The interpretability contribution—mapping each sub-task's projected embeddings to specific visual cortex regions (V1/V2, V4/LOC, PPA/FFA)—provides a biologically grounded validation that goes beyond pure performance metrics, which is valuable for brain-computer interface applications. However, the evaluation is limited to three subjects on a single dataset (cc2017), the segmentation Dice score is modest (35.63%), and the pipeline depends heavily on off-the-shelf models (Qwen2.5-VL, Grounded-SAM, GPT-2), raising questions about generalizability and robustness to out-of-distribution content.
