## Problem

Existing single-view 3D generation methods are trained on geometrically symmetric objects (airplanes, chairs) where texture-to-form correspondence is rigid, making them poorly suited for building facades where window placement is a free design variable. In the AEC design workflow, architects need to specify and control window-to-wall ratios (WWR) for energy compliance, yet current methods cannot enforce this constraint, produce inconsistent window details across views, or support interactive local editing of window regions during generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252324/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single architectural image (one of three types: line drawing, shading diagram, or rendering) at standard resolution plus a scalar WWR value as a conditioning signal, and produces six geometrically consistent multi-view images, a textured 3D mesh, and an interactively editable 3D building model. The WWR is defined as the pixel-level ratio of window area to wall area averaged across all n facades, computed via segmentation masks, and serves as a supervisory constraint during diffusion. The ArchiSet dataset provides 13,728 SketchUp-derived 3D models (as point clouds, voxels, and meshes) with 1,482,624 paired images and window masks, split 7:2:1 for training/testing/validation.

## Method

BuildingMesh is a three-stage pipeline: (1) a fine-tuned Zero123++ diffusion model generates six target views from one input image, with WWR injected as an additional conditioning input to the denoising network and a window-consistency regularization loss that computes the mean pixel value across all K views within window mask regions and penalizes per-view deviations (L_consistency), ensuring geometrically aligned window textures; (2) an EditNeRF-based editing module allows local modifications on any single view and synchronizes changes across the remaining five views using a hybrid color/shape branch update with depth, normal, and mask losses; (3) a sparse-view Large Reconstruction Model (InstantMesh/LRM) trained on ArchiSet converts the multi-view images into a final 3D mesh. The key differentiator is the joint use of WWR as a hard generation constraint and the pixel-level window consistency loss, which together enforce both global ratio compliance and local window detail alignment across views—something no prior architectural 3D generation method provides.

## Results

On the ArchiSet dataset, BuildingMesh achieves the best Chamfer Distance (CD) of 0.0832/0.0768/0.0816 and F-Score of 0.6835/0.7396/0.7259 across line drawings (WL), shading diagrams (WS), and renderings (WR), respectively. Compared to the strongest baseline PC2, the method reduces CD by 3.03% and increases F-Score by 4.35%; against Shap-E, Point-E, and Wonder3D, CD reductions reach 44.4%, 45.4%, and 38.1% with F-Score gains of 31.5%, 112.2%, and 34.1%. The method also demonstrates WWR controllability with relative errors of 3.24% (WS), 8.89% (WR), and 9.32% (WL), and maintains consistent performance across all three image types where baselines show type-specific degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BuildingMesh outperforms all five baselines (Shap-E, Point-E, Wonder3D, One-2-3-45, and PC2) on every metric (CD, F-Score, FPD) across all three image types on ArchiSet. The largest margins are against Shap-E (44.4% CD reduction, 31.5% F-Score gain) and Point-E (45.4% CD reduction, 112.2% F-Score gain), while the closest competitor is PC2, which BuildingMesh edges out by 3.03% in CD and 4.35% in F-Score. One-2-3-45 is competitive on CD (0.0879 vs. 0.0832 on WL) but lags more noticeably on F-Score (0.5827 vs. 0.6835). A notable finding is that baselines exhibit image-type sensitivity—Shap-E and One-2-3-45 degrade on line drawings, while Point-E and Wonder3D underperform on renderings—whereas BuildingMesh maintains consistent performance across WL, WS, and WR. On the external BuildingNet and RealCity3D datasets (Table 3), BuildingMesh again achieves the best CD and FPD, confirming generalizability beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Zero123++ multi-view diffusion framework, fine-tuning it with a WWR-conditioned consistency loss to generate architecturally coherent views, then chains EditNeRF for local editing and a sparse-view LRM (InstantMesh-style) for final mesh reconstruction. It departs from prior single-view 3D generation work (Shap-E, Point-E, One-2-3-45, Wonder3D, PC2) by introducing window-to-wall ratio as an explicit generation condition and a pixel-level window consistency regularizer, neither of which appear in general-purpose 3D generators. It also differentiates from existing building datasets (BuildingNet, BuildingGAN, RealCity3D, BuildingGenerator) by sourcing real design-phase SketchUp models from architecture students with paired WWR annotations, rather than synthetic or scanned geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ArchiSet dataset fills a genuine gap: no prior public dataset provides real architectural design forms with quantitative WWR labels and multi-stage design images (line drawings, shading, renderings) suitable for training and evaluating 3D generation in AEC workflows. The WWR control mechanism (3–9% relative error) offers a practical handle for early-stage energy compliance, though the paper does not yet connect generated geometry to actual energy simulation. Open questions include generalization beyond office buildings, integration into production BIM tools, and whether the three-stage pipeline (diffusion → NeRF editing → LRM meshing) can be unified or made faster for interactive use.
