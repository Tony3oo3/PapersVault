## Problem

Existing 3D occupancy reconstruction for autonomous driving relies on costly LiDAR-based annotations, preventing scalable auto-labeling from the vast crowdsourced RGB data available on consumer vehicles. Vision-only reconstruction is further hampered by sparse viewpoints (~1000 frames from only 5 cameras per scene), severe occlusions, dynamic objects, and long-horizon motion, while prior mesh-based approaches produce incomplete geometry requiring extensive post-processing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251318/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-camera RGB images (5 views, ~1000 frames per Waymo scene) along with camera poses estimated via detector-free SfM and 3D bounding boxes from a vision-based tracker for dynamic vehicles. It produces vision-only binary 3D occupancy voxel grids that distinguish observed from unobserved voxels via ray-casting, alongside the underlying dense point cloud and the optimized octree-based Gaussian surfel scene representation. The final labels are structured to match the perception range and point density of a single LiDAR sweep for direct compatibility with downstream occupancy models.

## Method

GS-Occ3D decomposes the driving scene into three components—static background, ground, and dynamic objects—each modeled with tailored strategies within a unified octree-based Gaussian Surfel representation that provides hierarchical multi-scale fidelity via cumulative Level-of-Detail levels. Ground is explicitly reconstructed by projecting camera poses onto the xy-plane with height-offset z adjustment and planar regularization to enforce flatness, addressing the weakly-textured road problem. Dynamic vehicles are initialized from tracked 3D bounding boxes with learnable pose corrections (ΔR, Δt) refined during optimization. Training minimizes a composite loss combining RGB (L1 + D-SSIM), geometry (surfel flattening, depth distortion, depth-normal consistency), object opacity entropy, road smoothness, and sky binary cross-entropy. A label curation pipeline then applies frame-wise division to define per-frame perception ranges, multi-frame aggregation to densify dynamic object points in box coordinates, and ray-casting to resolve occlusions before voxelization into occupancy grids.

## Results

On the Waymo Static-32 split, GS-Occ3D achieves the best Chamfer Distance of 0.56 (vs. 0.82 for GVKF, 1.23 for 2DGS, and 0.76 for LiDAR-assisted NeuS*), while maintaining competitive rendering quality (PSNR 26.89) with the lowest GPU memory (10 GB) and fastest training time (0.8 h) among all methods. For downstream occupancy prediction with CVT-Occ, models trained on GS-Occ3D vision-only labels achieve IoU 44.7 on Occ3D-Val(Waymo) (vs. 46.8 for LiDAR-based Occ3D labels) and demonstrate superior zero-shot generalization on Occ3D-nuScenes, matching or exceeding LiDAR-based baselines despite using only camera inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GS-Occ3D outperforms all six geometry reconstruction baselines on Chamfer Distance, achieving 0.56 vs. 0.82 (GVKF), 1.23 (2DGS), 2.90 (PGSR), 1.02 (StreetSurf vision-only), 0.90 (StreetSurf† with LiDAR), and 0.76 (NeuS* with LiDAR). Notably, it surpasses even LiDAR-assisted methods (NeuS*, StreetSurf†) while using only RGB cameras, and does so with 2.4× less GPU memory than GVKF and 2.5× faster training. On rendering, GS-Occ3D (PSNR 26.89) is competitive with StreetSurf (27.12) and StreetSurf† (26.85) while being far more efficient. For downstream occupancy prediction, models trained on GS-Occ3D labels achieve IoU 44.7 on Occ3D-Val(Waymo), slightly below the 46.8 from LiDAR-based Occ3D labels, but the paper reports superior zero-shot generalization on Occ3D-nuScenes where camera coverage differs from LiDAR's 360° sensing, suggesting the vision-only labels capture more transferable geometric structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Octree-GS [62] for its hierarchical LOD-structured Gaussian surfel representation and on 3DGS [33] as the base rendering framework, while adapting geometry constraints from 2DGS [27] and Gaussian Opacity Fields [96] to enforce surface fidelity. For dynamic scene handling, it follows Street Gaussians [87] with learnable pose corrections, and for the label curation pipeline (frame-wise division, multi-frame aggregation, ray-casting), it adapts the Occ3D [71] protocol. The key departure from prior geometry reconstruction work (StreetSurf, PGSR, GVKF) is the explicit decomposition into ground, static background, and dynamic objects with tailored modeling strategies, and the use of an octree-based surfel representation instead of meshes, which eliminates the post-processing bottleneck that limits scalability of prior GS-based street scene methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that full-dataset vision-only occupancy reconstruction is feasible at Waymo scale, opening a path to scalable auto-labeling that eliminates the costly LiDAR annotation pipeline. The superior zero-shot generalization on Occ3D-nuScenes compared to LiDAR-based Occ3D labels is a notable practical result, suggesting camera-only labels can match or exceed LiDAR labels for downstream occupancy models. Key limitations remain: no rear-view coverage, degradation at night, failure on ego-static scenes, and geometry-only output without semantic labels. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other sensor configurations, whether semantic reconstruction can be integrated, and whether the 66-category RGB-derived semantics can replace labor-intensive LiDAR annotation at scale.
