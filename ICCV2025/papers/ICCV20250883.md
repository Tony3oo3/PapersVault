## Problem

Vision-Language Models degrade sharply on long-context multimodal inputs (long videos, high-resolution images, lengthy documents) because directly applying the LLM's uniform positional encoding to visual tokens causes position indices to exceed the trained context window. The paper identifies that this positional encoding mismatch is the primary bottleneck, and that standard LLM position-encoding extension methods (interpolation, NTK-aware scaling) provide only marginal improvements for VLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250883/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes interleaved image-text sequences where each image contributes a block of visual tokens and text contributes standard tokens; sequences range from short VQA prompts to 1M-token multimodal documents. At inference, the positional increment δ for visual tokens is selected per image based on total sequence length (e.g., δ=1/2 for short contexts, δ=1/256 for 256K–1M token sequences), while text tokens always increment by 1. Outputs are free-form text answers for VQA/reasoning tasks or the located target (image or text segment) for retrieval tasks.

## Method

V2PE replaces the uniform +1 position increment with a modality-specific recursive rule: text tokens advance the position index by 1, while visual tokens advance it by a fractional δ < 1, reflecting the higher redundancy and spatial continuity of adjacent pixels. During training, δ is randomly sampled per image from {1, 1/2, 1/4, …, 1/256}, forcing the model to generalize across arbitrary visual position spacings; during inference, δ is chosen to keep all position indices within the trained RoPE range. The approach is applied to InternVL2-2B in a two-stage fine-tuning: (1) mixed long-context data (Long-VQA + Long-MR-32K + short SFT) up to 32K tokens, then (2) Long-MR-256K up to 256K tokens using Ring Attention for multi-GPU parallelism. This enables zero-shot extrapolation to 1M-token sequences without additional fine-tuning, outperforming both token compression and RoPE interpolation/extrapolation baselines.

## Results

On the MM-NIAH long-context benchmark, InternVL2-V2PE-32K (δ=1/256) achieves 81.8% average (78.1% image, 85.5% text), substantially outperforming GPT-4o (63.7%) and Gemini-1.5 (55.2%). On the extended MM-NIAH_1M image-retrieval task, InternVL2-V2PE-256K (δ=1/256) reaches 64.5% at 1M tokens, 95.1% at 512K, and 99.1% at 128K, whereas GPT-4o drops to 58.6% at 512K and is not evaluated at 1M. On standard VQA benchmarks, the model maintains a competitive 72.9% average (δ=1/2), within 0.2 points of Qwen2-VL-2B (73.1%) and 0.5 points above the base InternVL2-2B (72.4%). On MileBench, V2PE-32K scores 72.4% average, slightly below GPT-4o (73.2%) but above Gemini-1.5 (68.8%) and LongVILA (70.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base InternVL2-2B, V2PE-32K improves MM-NIAH average from 21.0% to 81.8% and MileBench from 49.9% to 72.4%, confirming that the gains come from the method and data rather than the backbone. Compared to GPT-4o, V2PE-32K wins decisively on MM-NIAH (81.8% vs 63.7%) but trails on VideoMME (52.3% vs 64.7%) and MileBench (72.4% vs 73.2%), suggesting GPT-4o retains an edge on video-centric long-context tasks. Against Gemini-1.5, V2PE-32K leads on MM-NIAH (81.8% vs 55.2%) and MileBench (72.4% vs 68.8%) but falls behind on VideoMME (52.3% vs 69.6%). Among dedicated long-context VLMs, V2PE-32K outperforms LongVILA (81.8% vs 33.9% MM-NIAH), LongLLaVA-13B (81.8% vs 41.7%), and VILA1.5 (81.8% vs 8.2%) by large margins on MM-NIAH, though LongVILA edges ahead on VideoMME (60.1% vs 52.3%). On standard short-context benchmarks (Table 4), V2PE-32K (72.9% avg) is competitive with same-size models like Qwen2-VL-2B (73.1%) and InternVL2-2B (72.4%), but well below the much larger Qwen2-VL-72B (85.0%). Finally, V2PE consistently outperforms position-encoding extension methods: on MM-NIAH at 256K tokens, V2PE-32K (δ=1/256) scores 56.9% versus 21.9% for linear interpolation and 16.8% for NTK-aware scaling applied to InternVL2-FT-32K.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard practice in VLMs of applying uniform positional increments (δ=1) to all tokens regardless of modality, identifying this as a key bottleneck for long-context multimodal understanding. It builds on the RoPE-based position encoding framework used in LLMs and VLMs like InternVL2, but introduces a modality-specific modification (V2PE) that assigns smaller, variable increments to visual tokens. The work also extends the long-context LLM literature (Position Interpolation, NTK-Aware Scaled RoPE) by showing these methods provide only marginal gains when applied to VLMs, and positions itself against long-context VLM efforts (LongVA, LongVILA, LongLLaVA) that are limited to specific scenarios like video or a small number of images. The augmented datasets (Long-VQA, Long-MR) and the extended MM-NIAH₁M benchmark fill a gap in systematic long-context multimodal evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that visual tokens require fundamentally different positional encoding than text tokens challenges a default architectural assumption in VLM design and offers a simple, training-free-at-inference mechanism (selecting δ) to extend context length without additional fine-tuning. The practical impact is significant: a 2B-parameter model handling 1M-token multimodal sequences opens the door to affordable long-context multimodal applications. However, the results are demonstrated primarily on InternVL2-2B, leaving open whether the insight generalizes to other VLM architectures (e.g., those using different vision encoders or position encoding schemes). An open question is the optimal δ selection strategy for diverse real-world workloads, and whether V2PE can be integrated into pre-training rather than post-hoc fine-tuning to avoid the small performance drop on short-context tasks observed here.
