## Problem

Existing referring audio-visual segmentation (RAVS) datasets like Ref-AVS rely on superficial audio cues (sound occurrence, intensity) and support only text-based referring expressions, failing to capture the deeper reasoning about audio content (e.g., inferring illness from coughing) and the flexible multimodal interaction (text, speech, sound, image) that real-world scenarios demand.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250926/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes audio-visual videos of varying durations (3–15 FPS, 11–251 frames) paired with one of 8 omnimodal expression types that flexibly combine text/speech with optional sound clips and reference images. It outputs per-frame binary segmentation masks for an arbitrary number of target objects (0 to 7) along with a natural-language explanation for reasoning-based expressions. During inference, 32 frames are sampled with 4 designated as dense frames for temporal context.

## Method

OISA builds on InternVL2-1B (InternViT-300M vision encoder + Qwen2-0.5B LLM) with a Whisper-large-v3 audio encoder and a Mask2Former-based mask head. Two key mechanisms distinguish it: (1) Audio-Visual Interleaving segments audio tokens into per-frame clips and alternates them with vision tokens ({v₁, a₁, v₂, a₂, …}) to achieve temporal alignment without extra parameters, outperforming sequential concatenation and cross-attention fusion; (2) Query Propagation updates the [SEG] object embedding frame-by-frame rather than reusing a single token for all frames, enabling the model to track dynamic motion and avoid ID switches. Multi-scale features are extracted via a ViT-Adapter in parallel with vision tokens and fed to the mask decoder. Training uses LoRA on the LLM with full mask-head training across a multi-dataset curriculum (semantic, referring image/video, and audio-visual segmentation), preceded by an audio-text alignment stage on GigaSpeech and Auto-ACD.

## Results

On the proposed OmniAVS benchmark, OISA-1B achieves 41.1% J&F (average across 8 modality splits), outperforming the strongest prior method LISA-13B (36.1%) by 5.0 points and the best traditional method MUTR (32.3%) by 8.8 points. The model also generates reasoning explanations with a METEOR score of 21.7%, surpassing LISA-13B (16.5%). On the Ref-AVS benchmark, OISA-1B reaches 54.5% J&F (Mix split), improving over EEMC (41.9%) by 12.6 points, and on ReVOS it attains 47.3% J&F with a 19.3% robustness score, setting new state-of-the-art.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the primary OmniAVS benchmark, OISA-1B (41.1% J&F) decisively outperforms all baselines: LISA-13B (36.1%, +5.0), MUTR (32.3%, +8.8), EEMC (29.6%, +11.5), and LMPM (25.8%, +15.3), with the largest gains on multimodal splits VII/VIII (speech+sound+image) where it reaches 52.6–53.0% versus 45.7–46.7% for LISA-13B. On Ref-AVS, OISA-1B improves J by 17.5% (Seen) and 8.8% (Unseen) over EEMC, though it scores 9.8% on the Null split versus EEMC's 0.7%, which the authors attribute to MLLM hallucination. On ReVOS, OISA-1B achieves 47.3% J&F and 19.3% robustness, narrowly beating VISA-7B (47.1%, 15.3%) and substantially outperforming VideoLISA-3.8B and LISA-7B. On refCOCO val, OISA-1B (74.2% cIoU) marginally surpasses LISA-7B (74.1%) and VideoLISA-3.8B (73.8%), but on ReasonSeg it trails VideoLISA-3.8B (67.1% vs. 61.2%) due to the smaller 0.5B LLM. On standard video segmentation benchmarks (MeViS, R-YTVOS, R-DAVIS), OISA-1B is competitive but slightly below VISA-7B (43.2 vs. 44.5, 62.1 vs. 63.0, 65.2 vs. 70.4), indicating that its advantage is most pronounced in audio-visual and reasoning-heavy settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the referring audio-visual segmentation (RAVS) line initiated by Ref-AVS [68] by introducing omnimodal expressions (text, speech, sound, image) and reasoning-based audio understanding, moving beyond surface-level acoustic properties. Methodologically, OISA builds directly on LISA's [33] [SEG] token mechanism and Mask2Former's [14] mask decoder, but departs from VideoLISA's [3] One-Token-Seg-ALL strategy in favor of frame-by-frame query propagation, and replaces VideoLLaMA's [80] sequential audio-visual concatenation with a parameter-free Audio-Visual Interleaving scheme for temporal alignment. The MLLM backbone (InternVL2-1B) and audio encoder (Whisper-large-v3) are standard components, with the novelty lying in their integration for joint audio-visual reasoning and segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OmniAVS fills a clear gap: prior RAVS datasets treat audio as a binary presence signal, while real-world applications (robotics, video conferencing) require understanding *what* a sound means and reasoning about its implications. The 8 expression types and 34,841 reasoning explanations make the benchmark directly applicable to developing omnimodal AI systems. Key open questions remain: disentangling overlapping sound sources before reasoning, scaling to longer videos with variable FPS, and whether the 1B-parameter baseline's advantage over 13B LISA reflects architectural choices (query propagation, AV interleaving) or simply dataset-specific tuning. The 17-point gap between OISA's Ref-AVS score (58%) and OmniAVS score (41%) confirms the dataset is substantially harder, but also suggests the field is far from saturated.
