## Problem

Machine unlearning methods typically minimize a weighted sum of forgetting and preservation losses, but the gradients for these two subgoals frequently conflict in direction and differ substantially in magnitude, causing one objective to dominate the joint update and preventing the model from reaching an optimal balance. This paper identifies and empirically demonstrates this gradient conflict and dominance issue across the unlearning process, then addresses it by reframing MU as a cooperative bargaining game between a forgetting player and a preservation player.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained model (ResNet, CLIP ViT-B/32, or Stable Diffusion v1.4), a forgetting set D_f, and a remaining set D_r, and iteratively computes mini-batch gradients from both sets to produce updated model parameters. For classification tasks, inputs are 32×32 (CIFAR-10, SVHN) or 224×224 (Celeb-HQ-307) RGB images; for generation tasks, the forgetting signal is derived from text prompts (e.g., 4703 I2P NSFW prompts or class-specific prompts on Imagenette) that condition the diffusion model. The output is the scrubbed model's parameter vector after T iterations, which can be directly deployed for classification or plugged into downstream pipelines such as replacing CLIP's text encoder in Stable Diffusion.

## Method

MUNBa formulates unlearning as a two-player Nash bargaining game where the forgetting and preservation players each define a utility as the dot product of their gradient with the joint update direction, and the objective is to maximize the log-product of both utilities within a bounded ball. This yields a closed-form solution for the mixing coefficients α_r and α_f expressed in terms of the angle φ between the two gradients and their norms (Eq. 8), which automatically scales each gradient so that both contribute proportionally to the update. The key mechanism is that the Nash bargaining solution simultaneously resolves gradient conflict (by finding a direction with positive alignment to both objectives) and gradient dominance (by equalizing the effective gradient norm contributions), steering the model toward the Pareto stationary point. Theoretical analysis proves boundedness of the coefficients, monotonic loss decrease for both players under Lipschitz-smooth losses, and convergence to a stationary point. The algorithm requires only a 2×2 Gram matrix solve per iteration, making it a lightweight drop-in replacement for the standard weighted-sum gradient in any MU pipeline.

## Results

On Celeb-HQ-307 (forgetting 10% identities), MUNBa achieves 0.00% accuracy on forgetting data, 87.24% test accuracy, and an average gap of 0.10 to the retrained model, outperforming SalUn (0.60) and SHs (0.39). On CIFAR-10 (forgetting 10% random samples), MUNBa reaches 93.12% test accuracy with an average gap of 0.97, the lowest among all baselines. For nudity erasure in Stable Diffusion, MUNBa achieves the lowest attack success rate (3.52%) against UnlearnDiffAtk while maintaining a competitive FID of 15.92, compared to SalUn (11.27% ASR, FID 25.06) and ESD (73.24% ASR, FID 15.76).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MUNBa achieves the lowest average gap to the retrained model on both Celeb-HQ-307 (0.10 vs. 0.39 for SHs and 0.60 for SalUn) and CIFAR-10 (0.97 vs. 1.24 for SalUn and 1.62 for SHs), indicating the closest match to gold-standard retraining. On Celeb-HQ-307, MUNBa matches the retrained model's forgetting accuracy (0.00%) while surpassing SalUn (0.94%) and SHs (0.06%), and achieves higher test accuracy (87.24%) than both. On CIFAR-10, MUNBa attains the best test accuracy (93.12%) among all unlearning methods, exceeding SHs (92.92%) and SalUn (92.10%). For nudity erasure in Stable Diffusion, MUNBa dramatically outperforms all baselines on adversarial robustness: its attack success rate of 3.52% is far below SalUn (11.27%), SHs (35.92%), SA (48.59%), and ESD (73.24%), while maintaining a near-best FID of 15.92 (vs. ESD's 15.76). FT, GA, IU, BE, BS, and ℓ₁-sparse all fail to achieve effective forgetting on sample-wise tasks, with average gaps ranging from 2.19 to 49.06, confirming that class-wise or boundary-based methods are unsuitable for identity/sample-level unlearning.

## Relation to Prior Work

MUNBa adapts the Nash bargaining framework from Navon et al. (ICML 2022), which formulated multi-task learning as a cooperative game with gradient-based utility functions, to the machine unlearning setting where the two 'players' are the forgetting and preservation objectives. It departs from the standard weighted-sum formulation (min α_r L_r + α_f L_f) used by most MU methods by identifying gradient dominance—where one objective's gradient magnitude overwhelms the other—as a distinct problem from gradient conflict, and addressing both simultaneously through a closed-form 2×2 Gram matrix solution. The paper positions itself against conflict-aversion methods (CAGrad, FAMO, GDR-GMA) that mitigate directional conflicts but leave magnitude imbalance unaddressed, and against MU-specific methods (SalUn, SHs, ESD) that do not explicitly model the interplay between forgetting and preservation gradients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded optimization rule (with Pareto stationarity and convergence guarantees) that is simple to implement—solving a 2×2 system per step—making it a drop-in replacement for the weighted-sum update in existing MU pipelines. The notably large robustness gap (3.52% vs. 11.27% attack success rate against UnlearnDiffAtk) suggests that balanced gradient contributions may inherently produce more robust unlearning, though this is demonstrated on a single concept and attack. Open questions include computational efficiency (MUNBa is slower than some baselines), applicability when training data for the forgetting set is unavailable, and whether the Nash bargaining framing extends to more than two objectives (e.g., multi-concept erasure). The authors also acknowledge that unlearning can be weaponized for malicious purposes, an underexplored risk.
