## Problem

Novel view synthesis methods for urban driving scenes are predominantly evaluated on interpolated poses where training and test viewpoints are highly correlated, leaving extrapolated poses—where test views substantially deviate from training data—largely unexplored. This gap is critical because real-world autonomous driving involves significant viewpoint shifts (lane changes, camera direction switches, intersection traversals) that exceed the training distribution. The absence of ground truth data for extrapolated views has prevented rigorous quantitative evaluation of generalization in urban NVS.

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes multi-view RGB images from multiple traversals, agents, or cameras (90,810 frames across 345 videos and 104 cases) along with COLMAP-estimated camera poses and sparse point clouds for initialization, with dynamic objects masked via Grounded-SAM-2 during both training and evaluation. It produces novel view renderings and depth maps at extrapolated test poses across three settings: translation-only (lane changes), rotation-only (camera direction switches), and combined translation+rotation (intersection traversals). Evaluation employs PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, DINOv2 feature cosine similarity, depth metrics (RMSE, δ1.25), and FID for assessing generative inpainting quality.

## Method

The paper constructs the first Extrapolated Urban View Synthesis (EUVS) benchmark by integrating multi-traversal, multi-agent, and multi-camera data from nuPlan, Argoverse 2, and MARS, requiring over 300 hours of manual traversal selection and 800+ hours of COLMAP processing. It defines a systematic evaluation framework crossing three pose-change settings (translation, rotation, translation+rotation) with three data configurations, enabling controlled assessment of generalization difficulty. The benchmark evaluates nine SOTA methods spanning 3DGS variants (3DGS, 3DGM, GSPro, VEGS, PGSR, 2DGS, Feature 3DGS), NeRF-based approaches (Zip-NeRF, Instant-NGP), and dynamic/lighting-handling methods (OmniRe, GS-W). Key findings reveal a 28% PSNR, 22% SSIM, and 50% LPIPS drop from interpolation to extrapolation, with no single method dominating all settings. Incorporating diffusion priors (VEGS) or geometry refinement (GSPro, 2DGS) yields only marginal improvements, indicating that current approaches fundamentally overfit to training views and require more robust representations or large-scale training.

## Results

Across all three evaluation settings, every baseline exhibits a significant performance drop from interpolated to extrapolated views. In Setting 1 (translation only), average PSNR falls from 20.67 to 15.59 (−24.6%); in Setting 2 (rotation only), from 25.75 to 19.15 (−25.6%); and in Setting 3 (translation + rotation), from 20.40 to 14.15 (−30.6%). VEGS is the standout in Setting 2 with only a 4.9% PSNR drop (24.54→23.33), while in Setting 3 no method exceeds 15 PSNR on extrapolated views. LPIPS drops are even more severe, reaching 70–172% in Settings 2 and 3, indicating substantial perceptual degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a benchmark paper evaluating 11 existing NVS methods rather than proposing a new one, so the key finding is the relative ranking under extrapolation. In Setting 1 (translation only), GSPro achieves the best extrapolated PSNR (16.39) and lowest LPIPS (0.2450), while 3DGM leads in SSIM (0.7248); differences among top methods are marginal (<0.1 PSNR). In Setting 2 (rotation only), VEGS dramatically outperforms all others with only a 4.9% PSNR drop (23.33 vs. 17–19 for competitors), attributed to its diffusion-prior-based inpainting of unseen regions. In Setting 3 (translation + rotation), Feature 3DGS achieves the best LPIPS (0.3816) and 3DGM the best SSIM (0.7233), but all methods fall below 15 PSNR, indicating no current approach handles the largest view changes well. NeRF-based methods (Zip-NeRF, Instant-NGP) consistently underperform 3DGS-based methods across all settings, with Zip-NeRF showing the worst extrapolated PSNR in Settings 1 and 2. The overarching conclusion is that no method—whether incorporating diffusion priors (VEGS), geometry refinement (GSPro, 2DGS, PGSR), or feature enhancement (Feature 3DGS)—fundamentally resolves the extrapolation gap, with average PSNR drops of 25–31% across settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of 3D Gaussian Splatting-based NVS and autonomous driving simulation, building on the 3DGS family (3DGS, 3DGM, GSPro, VEGS, PGSR, 2DGS, Feature 3DGS) and NeRF-based methods (Zip-NeRF, Instant-NGP). While prior work on extrapolated view synthesis (RapNeRF, GGS, AutoSplat, FreeSim, VEGS, SGD) has addressed specific sub-problems like lane changes or camera switching, none provided a unified quantitative benchmark with real-world ground truth across multiple extrapolation settings. The paper carves new ground by integrating multi-traversal, multi-agent, and multi-camera data from nuPlan, Argoverse 2, and MARS into a single evaluation framework with three systematically defined settings (translation, rotation, and combined), rather than treating extrapolation as an ad-hoc qualitative demo.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that all current NVS methods suffer severe and consistent performance degradation under extrapolation (average PSNR drops of 24.6%, 25.6%, and 30.6% across the three settings), and that neither diffusion priors nor geometry refinement fundamentally resolves the gap—pointing to a need for more robust representations or large-scale training. This is practically critical because photorealistic simulators for AVs must handle arbitrary viewpoint shifts, not just near-training poses. Key limitations include the absence of foreground/dynamic object evaluation in Settings 1 and 3, reliance on manually curated lighting-consistent traversals, and the fact that test viewpoints are still 'well covered' by training trajectories, leaving truly unseen regions unexplored.
