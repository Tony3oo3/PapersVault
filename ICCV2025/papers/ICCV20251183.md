## Problem

Adapting vision-language models to new domains with only a handful of labeled samples per class is severely hampered by overfitting, yet state-of-the-art low-rank reparameterization methods (LoRA, ReLoRA, DoRA, etc.) constrain weight updates to fixed-rank subspaces that reduce expressiveness and still overfit rapidly, requiring careful tuning of rank and training iterations that is impractical without a validation set. This paper argues that sparsity, rather than low-rank projection, is the more effective mechanism for controlling overfitting in few-shot VLM adaptation.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pretrained CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone, taking RGB images and a simple text prompt template ('a photo of a [class name]') as input and producing a class label via cross-entropy over class-specific text prototypes. In the few-shot setting, only K images per class (K ∈ {1, 2, 4}) are available for adaptation, and fine-tuning is applied to all 12 vision and text Transformer blocks. The sparse optimizer updates only κ = 0.05% of parameters per iteration, refreshing the sparsity support every T = 10 iterations, which drastically reduces memory overhead compared to dense or low-rank alternatives.

## Method

SO is a sparse optimizer built on Adam that enforces two paradigms. First, local sparsity and global density: at each iteration only M = ⌊κd⌋ gradient entries are retained, and the sparsity support is dynamically reselected every T iterations, so different parameters contribute to learning over time while local learning capacity stays minimal. Second, local randomness and global importance: the sparse gradient is selected uniformly at random (Random-M) to avoid bias toward high-magnitude local updates, while the first moment is pruned by magnitude (Top-M) to retain parameters with long-term significance, and the second moment is aligned to the same indices. Concretely, the temporary first and second moments can hold up to 2M values (accounting for non-overlapping supports), are reduced to M via Top-M on the first moment, bias-corrected, and used to update only the corresponding parameter entries. This combination of stochastic gradient selection and importance-based moment pruning consistently outperforms both dense Adam and all low-rank baselines across 11 datasets at 1, 2, and 4 shots.

## Results

On 11 diverse few-shot classification datasets with a ViT-B/16 CLIP backbone, SO achieves the highest average top-1 accuracy across all shot settings: 73.8% (1-shot), 76.6% (2-shot), and 78.9% (4-shot). The closest competitor, ReLoRA, reaches 72.5%, 75.3%, and 77.1% respectively, yielding a consistent 1.3–1.8 point gap. Full fine-tuning with Adam collapses to 3.6% average at 1-shot, confirming severe overfitting, while zero-shot CLIP achieves 65.1% without any adaptation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SO outperforms all seven low-rank and full-fine-tuning baselines across every shot setting. Against the strongest low-rank competitor ReLoRA, SO gains 1.3 points (1-shot), 1.3 points (2-shot), and 1.8 points (4-shot) in average accuracy, with the largest per-dataset margins on Aircraft (+2.7 to +1.0) and EuroSAT (+4.4 to +3.0). Against LoRA, DoRA, and VeRA, the average gaps are 3.6–4.1 points at 1-shot, narrowing to 2.9–3.1 points at 4-shot. GaLoRE and PiSSA, which project gradients into low-rank subspaces rather than constraining weight updates, trail SO by 6.7–7.4 points at 1-shot. Full fine-tuning with Adam catastrophically overfits, achieving only 3.6% average at 1-shot versus SO's 73.8%, while zero-shot CLIP (65.1%) is surpassed by SO by 8.7 points without any adaptation. Notably, SO requires no rank selection or early-stopping tuning, whereas all low-rank baselines depend on carefully chosen rank and iteration counts that vary per dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant low-rank reparameterization paradigm (LoRA, ReLoRA, DoRA, VeRA, PiSSA, GaLoRE) for parameter-efficient few-shot VLM adaptation, arguing that constraining updates to a fixed low-rank subspace causes severe overfitting when labeled data is scarce. It builds on the Adam optimizer and the CLIP-LoRA experimental framework but replaces low-rank projection with a sparse optimization strategy that dynamically updates a minimal subset of parameters per iteration. The approach also diverges from prior sparsity-based pruning methods by combining random gradient selection with importance-based moment pruning and a dynamically evolving sparsity support, rather than relying on static masks or importance-based gradient pruning alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, memory-efficient alternative to low-rank methods for few-shot VLM adaptation, with the added benefit of reduced sensitivity to hyperparameters like rank and early-stopping iterations. However, the evaluation is limited to a single backbone (ViT-B/16) and 11 image classification datasets, and the authors themselves note that evaluating on large language models—where memory efficiency is even more critical—remains future work. Open questions include whether the sparsity ratio κ and update interval T can be set without any validation data, and whether the approach generalizes to other architectures or modalities beyond vision-language models.
