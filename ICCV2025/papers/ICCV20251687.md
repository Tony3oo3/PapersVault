## Problem

Large-scale datasets used to train conditional diffusion models contain mislabeled or ambiguous samples, causing generated images to misalign with their conditioning labels. Existing remedies—transition-matrix estimation, data cleaning, or retraining with modified objectives—are computationally prohibitive for large pre-trained models. This paper asks whether the generation trajectory of an already-trained noisy conditional diffusion model can be corrected at inference time without any retraining of the score network.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251687/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At training time the method consumes a noisy labeled dataset (e.g., CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, Tiny-ImageNet and Clothing-1M at 64×64) to train a small time-dependent discriminator via a noise detector (CORES or Confident Learning). At inference time, the input is a class label (potentially noisy) fed to the frozen pre-trained EDM score network; the discriminator's gradient is injected only during a gated middle interval of the 18-step Heun sampling schedule. The output is a single generated image whose class alignment is corrected relative to the unguided baseline, with negligible added wall-clock cost (~4 s per 50k images on CIFAR-10).

## Method

SBDC trains a lightweight time-dependent discriminator D_φ^t(x_t, y) with a binary cross-entropy objective that separates pseudo-clean samples (r=1) from pseudo-corrupt samples (r=0), where the partition is obtained from an off-the-shelf noise detector. Theorem 1 proves that, under a Lipschitz condition, the optimally trained discriminator's logit gradient approximates ∇_{x_t} log p(x_t|y)/p(x_t|ỹ), i.e., the exact correction needed to recover the clean conditional score from the noisy one. During inference the corrected score is s_θ(x_t, ỹ) + γ(t)·log[D(x_t,ỹ)/(1−D(x_t,ỹ))], where the gate γ(t) activates the discriminator only in the conditional (middle) phase of sampling—identified empirically via confidence C(t) and instability I(t) curves—thereby avoiding interference in the high-noise and fine-grained phases. Two data augmentations improve discriminator training: Pseudo-clean Shuffle swaps labels of a fraction of clean samples to balance class-conditional corrupt sets, and SiMix performs similarity-based MixUp in CLIP ViT-B/32 feature space to preserve structural coherence and prevent gradient explosion. The entire pipeline is plug-and-play: the score network is never retrained, the discriminator trains in 50–200 epochs (vs. 4000 for TDSM's classifier), and the method can be stacked on top of existing noisy-label-robust training such as TDSM.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with 50% symmetric noise, SBDC achieves CW-FID of 15.6 (vs. TDSM 18.2 and EDM 38.6) and CW-Density of 98.1 (vs. TDSM 95.8 and EDM 66.8), while remaining competitive on FID (2.43 vs. TDSM 2.43). At 80% symmetric noise, SBDC reduces CW-FID to 48.2 compared to TDSM's 59.8 and EDM's 71.7. On CIFAR-100 with 40% symmetric noise, SBDC achieves FID 3.64 versus TDSM's 6.84 (a 47% reduction) and CW-FID 77.13 versus TDSM's 91.13. On real-world datasets, SBDC outperforms TDSM on FOOD101 (FID 4.41 vs. 5.21) and Clothing-1M (FID 4.17 vs. 6.75).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SBDC is compared against two baselines: the original EDM model trained on noisy labels [Karras et al., NeurIPS 2022] and TDSM (Transition-aware Weighted Denoising Score Matching) [Na et al., ICLR 2024], the state-of-the-art noisy-label diffusion method. SBDC consistently outperforms TDSM on all class-wise metrics (CW-FID, CW-Density, CW-Coverage) across every noise setting tested. The advantage is most dramatic at high noise: on CIFAR-100 with 40% symmetric noise, SBDC achieves FID 3.64 versus TDSM's 6.84, and on CIFAR-10 with 80% symmetric noise, SBDC reduces CW-FID from 59.8 (TDSM) to 48.2. Against the uncorrected EDM baseline, SBDC dramatically improves class-wise fidelity (e.g., CW-FID drops from 38.6 to 15.6 at 50% symmetric noise on CIFAR-10) while remaining competitive on global FID/IS. On real-world datasets (FOOD101, Clothing-1M), SBDC also beats TDSM on FID, with the gap widening on the multi-class FOOD101 dataset where TDSM's transition-matrix approximation degrades.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the discriminator guidance framework of Kim et al. (ICML 2023), which originally distinguished real from synthetic samples, to instead distinguish clean-labeled from corrupt-labeled samples in a conditional diffusion model. It also adopts the limited-interval guidance scheduling idea from Kynkäänniemi et al. (2024), restricting the discriminator's influence to the conditional phase of sampling where class instability peaks. The key departure from prior noisy-label diffusion work (e.g., TDSM) is that SBDC operates entirely at inference time on a frozen pre-trained model, avoiding the expensive multi-stage retraining and transition-matrix estimation that those methods require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, no-retraining nature of SBDC makes it practically attractive for large-scale pre-trained diffusion models where retraining is prohibitive, and the ~4-second overhead per 50k images confirms its efficiency. However, the method still depends on an external noise detector (CORES or Confident Learning) whose imperfections propagate into the discriminator, and experiments are limited to small-scale datasets (CIFAR-10/100, Tiny-ImageNet) and class-conditional generation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to text-conditioned generation (e.g., CLIP-based), whether it scales to much larger models, and whether the limited-interval scheduling generalizes across different noise schedules and architectures.
