## Problem

Existing video motion transfer methods face a fundamental trade-off: dense-visual-condition approaches (e.g., depth, optical flow) impose rigid structural constraints that limit cross-scene generalization, while optimization-based methods (e.g., MotionClone, DMT) achieve better motion fidelity but require sample-specific optimization per reference video, incurring 100–500 seconds of additional computation. No prior work achieves both flexible, fine-grained motion transfer and fast end-to-end inference without test-time optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference video (any real-world clip exhibiting a target motion pattern) and a free-form text prompt describing the desired subject, action, and background. The reference video is encoded through the T2V backbone at later denoising timesteps with a null-text prompt to isolate motion dynamics. At inference, a single forward pass through the adapted T2V model produces the output video in approximately 15–21 seconds, with no per-sample optimization or fine-tuning required.

## Method

EfficientMT repurposes the pretrained T2V backbone (AnimateDiff or VideoCrafter2) as a frozen feature extractor, pulling inputs from the temporal attention layers of the upsampling stage as reference motion features. A learned scaler module predicts a fine-grained spatial-temporal scale map by conditioning on both reference and generation-branch features, adaptively suppressing motion-irrelevant information (static textures, subject identity) while preserving dynamic motion signals. A temporal integration mechanism then concatenates the scaled reference features with generation features along the temporal axis, so that the query is projected from the generation branch while keys and values are drawn from the integrated sequence—effectively treating reference frames as additional context tokens for temporal attention. Training fine-tunes only the projection matrices of all upsampling temporal attention layers on ~150 high-quality synthetic paired samples (constructed via MotionClone/MotionInversion with automated motion-fidelity and temporal-consistency filtering), enabling zero-shot motion transfer at inference.

## Results

On the AnimateDiff backbone, EfficientMT achieves the best Text Alignment (0.2712) and near-best Motion Fidelity (0.8470) among all compared methods, while completing generation in just 16 seconds per sample — roughly 10–30× faster than optimization-based baselines (127–473 s). On the VideoCrafter2 backbone, it attains the highest Temporal Consistency (0.9456) in 21 s. The method also outperforms all dense-visual-condition baselines on every quality metric while matching or beating their inference speed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three dense-visual-condition methods (VideoComposer, Control-A-Video, ControlVideo), EfficientMT (AnimateDiff) wins on all four metrics: it improves Motion Fidelity by 0.21–0.30, Text Alignment by 0.01–0.03, and Temporal Consistency by 0.01–0.01, while matching or beating their inference speed (16 s vs. 18–80 s). Among optimization-based methods, EfficientMT (AnimateDiff) achieves Motion Fidelity of 0.8470, narrowly trailing MotionClone (0.8569) by 0.01 but doing so in 16 s versus 190 s (≈12× speedup); it also surpasses MOFT (0.7698), DMT (0.6974), and MotionInversion (0.7373) on Motion Fidelity while being 8–26× faster. On Text Alignment, EfficientMT leads all methods (0.2712), indicating more flexible subject/background editing. On Temporal Consistency, MotionInversion (0.9329) and MotionDirector (0.9327) slightly edge out EfficientMT-AnimateDiff (0.9291), but EfficientMT-VideoCrafter2 achieves the best score (0.9456). In the user study, EfficientMT is preferred over VideoComposer, MotionDirector, MotionClone, and MotionInversion on all three subjective criteria (Temporal Consistency, Text Alignment, Motion Fidelity), with win rates of 75–81%.

## Relation to Prior Work

EfficientMT bridges two previously separate lines of video motion transfer: dense-visual-condition methods (VideoComposer, Control-A-Video, ControlVideo) that are fast but impose rigid structural constraints, and optimization-based methods (MotionClone, MotionInversion, DMT, MOFT, MotionDirector) that achieve higher motion fidelity but require per-sample optimization. The key architectural departure is repurposing the pretrained T2V backbone itself as a frozen feature extractor for reference motion, then injecting those features via a temporal concatenation mechanism into the generation branch's attention layers—effectively converting the implicit motion representations that prior work distilled through optimization into a single forward pass. The scaler module, inspired by SmartControl's adaptive conditioning, addresses the domain gap between reference and generation features without requiring the large paired datasets that dense-condition methods rely on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10–30× speedup over optimization-based methods (16 s vs. 190–473 s per sample) makes motion transfer practical for interactive and real-time applications, while the requirement of only ~150 synthetic training samples lowers the barrier to adapting new T2V backbones. The demonstrated generalizability across two distinct architectures (AnimateDiff and VideoCrafter2) suggests the temporal-integration-plus-scaler recipe could be a reusable module for other control modalities in video diffusion. Open questions include whether the approach degrades gracefully under violent or discontinuous motion (acknowledged limitation), whether the synthetic-data pipeline can be automated at scale, and whether the method extends to longer videos or multi-subject motion transfer.
