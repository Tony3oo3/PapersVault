## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting in in-the-wild scenes produces severe artifacts when transient objects are present, because the Gaussian densification process (splitting/cloning) grows additional primitives that overfit to moving objects. Existing robust methods that learn transient masks via photometric residuals or feature similarity fail in early training, when the under-optimized 3DGS representation yields unreliable supervision signals that misclassify static regions as dynamic.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251147/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes casually captured multi-view posed RGB images containing transient objects along with SfM-derived camera poses and sparse point initialization. It outputs a clean 3D Gaussian splatting representation for distractor-free novel-view rendering, plus a per-image transient mask predicted by a two-layer MLP. DINOv2 ViT-S/14 features are extracted at two spatial resolutions (224×224 and 504×504) with a downsampling factor of 8 (4 for specific scenes), and the mask MLP is trained self-supervisedly without ground-truth annotations.

## Method

RobustSplat introduces two coupled designs. First, a delayed Gaussian growth strategy postpones the default 3DGS densification (splitting/cloning) from iteration 500 to iteration 10K, ensuring the static scene structure is optimized before new Gaussians are introduced that could latch onto transient objects; a decaying regularization term (L_reg = exp(-i/β_reg)·‖1−M_t‖) further encourages the mask MLP to initially treat all regions as static. Second, a scale-cascaded mask bootstrapping approach supervises the mask MLP with low-resolution (224×224) image residuals and DINOv2 cosine similarity before densification begins—exploiting the stronger global consistency and noise robustness of low-res features to tolerate under-reconstructed static regions—then switches to high-resolution (504×504) supervision once densification starts for finer transient/static discrimination. The mask MLP is jointly optimized with a combined loss of image residual supervision, feature cosine similarity supervision, and the early-stage regularization, all applied to the DINOv2 feature space.

## Results

On the NeRF On-the-go dataset (5 scenes), RobustSplat achieves a mean PSNR of 23.22, SSIM of 0.818, and LPIPS of 0.149, outperforming the strongest baseline T-3DGS (22.87 / 0.803 / 0.167) by +0.35 dB in PSNR and −0.018 in LPIPS. On the RobustNeRF dataset (4 scenes), RobustSplat reaches a mean PSNR of 29.36, SSIM of 0.895, and LPIPS of 0.135, surpassing T-3DGS (28.25 / 0.888 / 0.149) by +1.11 dB in PSNR. Compared to vanilla 3DGS, the gains are +4.13 dB (NeRF On-the-go) and +3.15 dB (RobustNeRF) in PSNR, with LPIPS reduced from 0.248 to 0.149 and 0.168 to 0.135 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RobustSplat outperforms all five baselines on both datasets across all three metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS). The closest competitor is T-3DGS, which RobustSplat beats by +0.35 dB PSNR on NeRF On-the-go and +1.11 dB on RobustNeRF; the gap widens on LPIPS (−0.018 and −0.014 respectively). Against SpotLessSplats, the improvement is larger: +1.05 dB PSNR on NeRF On-the-go and +0.78 dB on RobustNeRF, with a notable LPIPS reduction from 0.220 to 0.149. WildGaussians and Robust3DGaussians trail by roughly 0.77–0.80 dB PSNR on NeRF On-the-go and 1.9–2.3 dB on RobustNeRF. The only scene where RobustSplat does not lead in PSNR is Android (RobustNeRF), where T-3DGS scores 24.81 vs. 24.62, though RobustSplat still matches it on LPIPS (0.125) and leads on SSIM (0.831 vs. 0.839 is actually T-3DGS winning SSIM there too). Overall, the delayed Gaussian growth and scale-cascaded mask bootstrapping consistently yield the best transient-free reconstruction quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the robust 3DGS line of work (SpotLessSplats, WildGaussians, Robust3DGaussians) by identifying a previously overlooked mechanism: Gaussian densification itself is a primary driver of transient artifacts, as newly spawned Gaussians overfit to moving objects. Rather than proposing a new architecture, it introduces two targeted modifications to the standard 3DGS pipeline—delaying the densification schedule and cascading mask supervision from low to high resolution—building directly on the mask-MLP formulation from SpotLessSplats and the DINOv2 feature-based supervision from WildGaussians. The conceptual departure from prior work is the explicit decoupling of the densification timeline from the mask-learning timeline, which prior robust 3DGS methods did not consider.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that densification is the key artifact mechanism provides a practical, low-cost lever (simply delaying the start iteration) that other 3DGS-based methods could adopt without architectural changes. However, the method explicitly does not handle illumination variation, limiting its applicability to fully uncontrolled in-the-wild settings, and the authors acknowledge this as a key open direction. The scale-cascaded mask bootstrapping idea—using low-resolution features for early supervision to avoid misclassifying under-reconstructed static regions—could generalize to other iterative reconstruction settings where early-stage signals are noisy. A broader open question is whether the delayed-growth principle interacts with other 3DGS improvements (e.g., alternative initialization, opacity regularization) in ways not yet explored.
