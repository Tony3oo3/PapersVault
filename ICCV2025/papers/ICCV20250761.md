## Problem

Existing sign language video generation methods rely on sparse skeleton keypoints or 2D heatmaps that lack volumetric geometric information, producing anatomically implausible hands and faces with poor temporal consistency. Although SMPL-X has been adopted for the Text2Sign stage of sign language production, its fine-grained geometric cues (surface normals, edge gradients) have not been effectively exploited for the Sign2Video stage, leaving a gap in generating structurally accurate and temporally coherent sign videos from 3D pose sequences.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single source RGB image (256×256) providing identity and appearance, paired with SMPL-X-rendered XYZ surface normal maps for both the source and target poses as geometric control signals. It produces a sequence of 256×256 sign language video frames. Internally, the pipeline derives an optical flow field and occlusion mask from the normal map pair, crops K key regions (face, left hand, right hand) from the target normal map, and computes per-region gradient maps weighted by spatial Gaussians centered on each region's midpoint.

## Method

GReg is a GAN-based framework that uses SMPL-X normal maps as volumetric geometric conditions. Two novel modules address key-region detail: (1) Regional Prior Generation trains dedicated UNet+StyleGAN2-modulated-convolution experts per region (face, left hand, right hand) that produce structural priors constrained by target regional gradients, which are painted into a full-resolution prior map; (2) Gradient-enhanced Refinement encodes spatially-weighted gradient maps into features that serve as cross-attention queries over the global generator's features, sharpening edges in key regions. The source image is warped to the target pose via estimated optical flow, then concatenated with the target normal map and region prior map before feeding the global UNet generator. Training combines perceptual loss (VGG-19), a regional perceptual loss on cropped face/hand patches, an L1 prior-structure loss aligning RE outputs to target gradients, and region-specific PatchGAN adversarial losses applied to both cropped regions and their gradient maps.

## Results

On HamNoSys, GReg achieves the best scores across all six metrics: L1 0.01907, SSIM 0.8836, LPIPS 0.03591, AKD 0.02429, Region L1 0.009441, and FVD 391.3. On How2Sign, GReg similarly leads on every metric: L1 0.02452, SSIM 0.8557, LPIPS 0.05157, AKD 0.01726, Region L1 0.04831, and FVD 453.2. The most notable gains are in FVD (14–20% lower than the best baseline) and Region L1 (12–5% lower), confirming superior structural accuracy in key regions (face, hands) and temporal coherence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GReg outperforms all three baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against NTED (a pose-transfer method also using SMPL-X), GReg reduces L1 by ~25% on HamNoSys (0.01907 vs 0.02543) and FVD by ~30% (391.3 vs 554.8), showing that SMPL-X alone is insufficient without the proposed region-refinement modules. Against SignGAN (skeleton-based with keypoint-guided adversarial training), GReg achieves substantially lower Region L1 (0.009441 vs 0.019519 on HamNoSys), indicating far better structural fidelity in face and hand regions. Against PGMM (heatmap + optical-flow based), GReg reduces FVD by 14% on HamNoSys (391.3 vs 454.2) and 20% on How2Sign (453.2 vs 564.0), confirming superior temporal consistency. The consistent margin across image-level, region-level, and video-level metrics suggests the gains come from both geometric conditioning (SMPL-X normal maps) and the proposed RPG/GR refinement pipeline rather than from any single component.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Sign Language Production (SLP) pipeline by addressing the Sign2Video stage, which prior work has handled with skeleton-based (SignGAN) or heatmap-based (PGMM) representations that lack explicit geometric structure. It departs from these by repurposing SMPL-X normal maps—previously used only as motion representations in Text2Sign—as volumetric control signals for video generation, and introduces region-specific refinement (Regional Prior Generation and Gradient-enhanced Refinement) that has no direct analogue in prior SLVG methods. The architectural choices draw on StyleGAN2's modulated convolutions for the Regional Experts and PatchGAN for region-level adversarial training, but the combination with geometric gradient maps as both generation priors and adversarial constraints is novel within this domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GReg closes a practical gap in SLP pipelines by showing that SMPL-X's rich geometric information can be directly exploited for realistic video synthesis, making it a drop-in replacement for the Sign2Video stage in existing Text2Sign systems. The region-specific refinement strategy (face, hands) addresses a well-known failure mode of whole-image generation where fine-grained anatomical details are lost. Limitations include the GAN-based formulation at 256×256 resolution, reliance on a single GPU, and the fact that the How2Sign evaluation uses a re-split training set rather than the official held-out test set. The authors themselves identify integration with diffusion models as the natural next step, suggesting the current framework is a stepping stone rather than a final solution.
