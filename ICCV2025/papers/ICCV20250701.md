## Problem

Despite strong general visual understanding, current MLLMs exhibit systematic and severe shortcomings in visual correspondence—the ability to determine whether an object in one image is the same instance as an object in another. No prior benchmark or dataset has systematically evaluated this capability, and the paper shows that even GPT-4o achieves only 42.65% overall accuracy on the newly proposed MMVM benchmark, with no open-source model exceeding 50%.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250701/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives image pairs (or multi-image sets) where query and candidate objects are marked with colored highlight contours and white ID tags overlaid on the images, along with a text question asking which candidate matches the query. The output is a two-turn dialogue: first a multiple-choice selection from 2–37 candidate options (average 10), then a chain-of-thought reasoning explanation for the chosen match. The MMVM SFT dataset contains 220K such multi-turn samples generated automatically from video segmentation datasets.

## Method

CoLVA introduces two key designs on top of InternVL2-4B. First, an Object-Level Contrastive Learning (OCL) strategy trains a RADIO fine-grained vision expert (distilled from SAM, DINOv2, and CLIP) via a contrastive loss between the MLLM's CLIP visual encoder and RADIO, simultaneously learning instance-discriminative features and aligning RADIO's feature space with the MLLM's semantic space; only the RADIO adapter is trained during this pre-training stage. Second, an Instruction Augmentation (IA) strategy randomly alternates between two instruction formats during SFT: one using edited images with contour overlays and ID tags, and another that directly embeds object-level representations (obtained via masked average pooling) into the instruction text, enabling gradients to backpropagate directly through object features to image features. The RADIO visual tokens are concatenated with the MLLM's original visual tokens and fed jointly into the LLM for answer generation.

## Results

On the MMVM benchmark (1,510 multi-image matching examples), CoLVA-InternVL2-4B achieves 49.80% overall accuracy, surpassing GPT-4o (42.65%) by 7.15% and the best open-source MLLM Qwen2-VL-72B (38.08%) by 11.72%. No evaluated MLLM exceeds 50% overall accuracy under the full candidate setting, confirming that visual correspondence remains a fundamental weakness. On general VQA benchmarks (MMBench, MME, POPE, BLINK, NaturalBench, VideoRefer-Bench), CoLVA causes minimal degradation and even improves several metrics (e.g., MME Perception 1536→1553, POPE 84.5→86.1, BLINK 45.8→47.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoLVA-InternVL2-4B (49.80% OA) outperforms all 36 evaluated MLLMs on the MMVM benchmark. It surpasses GPT-4o (42.65%) by 7.15% and Qwen2-VL-72B (38.08%) by 11.72%, despite being a 4B model versus 72B+ competitors. Among open-source models, it beats InternVL2.5-78B (36.42%) by 13.38% and LLaVA-OneVision-72B (29.34%) by 20.46%. The base InternVL2-4B without CoLVA achieves only 17.62%, so the full pipeline (MMVM data + OCL + RADIO + IA) yields a +32.18% absolute gain. In the generalization study (Table 7), CoLVA also improves Qwen2VL-2B from 15.69% to 47.48% and LLaVA1.5-7B from 14.64% to 36.56%, confirming the method transfers across architectures. On standard VQA benchmarks, CoLVA does not degrade general capabilities (MMBench 77.40→77.32, MMMU 47.56→44.11) while improving MME, POPE, BLINK, and multi-image benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on InternVL2 as its base MLLM and draws conceptual inspiration from contrastive learning in visual tracking and video segmentation (MoCo, DVIS, TubeLink), repurposing object-level contrastive objectives to align a fine-grained vision expert (RADIO) with the MLLM's CLIP-based feature space. It departs from the standard MLLM paradigm where a single CLIP encoder handles all visual understanding, instead introducing a dual-encoder architecture with a dedicated discriminative expert. The instruction augmentation strategy connects to visual prompt learning work (VIP-LLaVA, Draw-and-Understand) but extends it by directly injecting object-level representations into the instruction stream for gradient backpropagation. The authors explicitly position this as the first work addressing fine-grained visual correspondence in MLLMs, carving new ground in a direction unexplored by prior MLLM research.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MMVM benchmark and 220K SFT dataset fill a critical gap in MLLM evaluation, providing the first systematic measurement of visual correspondence ability across 36+ models. The finding that even GPT-4o achieves only 42.65% overall accuracy on matching challenges the assumption that strong general VQA performance implies strong fine-grained discriminative visual understanding, with implications for any application relying on object identity across views (tracking, re-ID, multi-view reconstruction). Open questions include whether the OCL+RADIO recipe generalizes to video correspondence, whether the gap between open-source and proprietary models will narrow with scale alone, and whether the 8 matching cue categories capture the full diversity of real-world correspondence challenges.
