## Problem

Existing 3D human geometry estimation methods are primarily designed for single images and produce temporally inconsistent, flickering predictions when applied to videos, missing fine dynamic details like clothing wrinkles and hair movement. This is compounded by the scarcity of high-quality 4D human training data and the fundamental tension in depth representation: affine-invariant depth loses human scale information while metric depth sacrifices spatial detail due to its unbounded numeric range.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252152/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB video of a single moving human (evaluated at 512×512 resolution) and produces temporally consistent depth maps and normal maps for all F frames. Depth is output in a root-relative representation (distance from the pelvis joint, range [-h/2, h/2]) rather than absolute metric or normalized values, and can be converted to absolute metric depth by adding the root depth estimated from a 3D pose model like VIMO. The system handles both moving-subject/static-camera and moving-camera/static-subject scenarios, as well as multi-person and long-video settings.

## Method

GeoMan decomposes video geometry estimation into two stages to exploit pre-trained diffusion priors with minimal 4D data: an Image-to-Geometry (I2G) model (a Marigold-style latent diffusion model) estimates depth or normal for the first frame, and a Video-to-Geometry (V2G) model (a ControlNet-based image-to-video diffusion model) generates geometry for all subsequent frames conditioned on the first frame's prediction as a reference image. The V2G model unifies depth and normal estimation in a single set of weights by simply switching which modality's first-frame map serves as the reference, enabling joint multi-modal training. A key representation choice is the root-relative depth (D_metric − d_root), which preserves human scale information while constraining the value range to [-h/2, h/2], making it easier for the network to capture fine geometric detail than unbounded metric depth and more temporally stable than per-frame affine-invariant normalization. Training requires only 20 subjects in 4D (XHumans) and ~500 subjects in 3D (THuman-2.0), with the video model leveraging temporal priors from large-scale pre-trained video diffusion models rather than requiring hundreds of thousands of 4D sequences.

## Results

On the ActorsHQ benchmark, GeoMan achieves 16.19° mean angular error and 45.2% of pixels within 11.25° for zero-shot normal estimation, with 96.76% temporal consistency (TC-11.25°), outperforming the strongest baseline Sapiens (16.28° mean, 41.25% at 11.25°, 82.80% TC-11.25°). For metric depth estimation, GeoMan reaches 0.972 δ₁ and 2.116 SI-log10 on moving-subject videos, surpassing Metric3Dv2 by ~100% in δ₁ (0.491) and ~65% in SI-log10 (6.213). For affine-invariant depth, GeoMan achieves 0.012 AbsRel with shift-only alignment, beating the best video baseline DepthCrafter (0.016) and all image-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoMan outperforms Sapiens [29]—the strongest human-centric baseline trained on proprietary data—on all normal estimation metrics: 16.19° vs 16.28° mean angular error, 45.2% vs 41.2% pixels within 11.25°, and a large temporal consistency gap (96.8% vs 82.8% TC-11.25°). When Sapiens is fine-tuned on GeoMan's smaller public dataset (Sapiens*), the gap widens substantially (18.34° vs 16.19° mean), confirming GeoMan's data efficiency. For metric depth, GeoMan dominates Metric3Dv2 [22] by ~100% in δ₁ (0.972 vs 0.491) and ~65% in SI-log10 (2.116 vs 6.213), while also achieving better temporal consistency (OPW 0.038 vs 0.085). Among affine-invariant depth baselines, GeoMan beats the best video method DepthCrafter [23] on accuracy (0.012 vs 0.016 AbsRel with shift-only) and matches its temporal consistency (TC-δ₁ 0.915 vs 0.914), while substantially outperforming image-based methods like Sapiens (0.040 AbsRel) and Marigold (0.021 AbsRel). GeoMan also surpasses generic-scene diffusion methods GeoWizard [14] and Lotus [17] by 6–7° in mean angular error for normals, demonstrating the benefit of the human-specific root-relative depth formulation and image-to-video decomposition.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoMan sits at the intersection of diffusion-based image geometry estimation (Marigold, GeoWizard) and controllable video generation (Ctrl-Adapter, Stable Video Diffusion), but departs from both by reframing video geometry estimation as an image-to-video synthesis problem rather than a direct video prediction task. The I2G+V2G decomposition offloads geometric reasoning to a single-frame model and reduces the video model's role to temporal propagation, a strategy that contrasts with prior video depth methods (DepthCrafter, Depth Any Video) that train end-to-end on large-scale video-depth pairs. The root-relative depth representation is a novel middle ground between affine-invariant and metric depth, specifically designed for human-centric scale preservation, and the multi-modality sharing of a single V2G model across depth and normal tasks via reference-image switching is a practical architectural choice not seen in prior human geometry work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decomposition strategy offers a general recipe for extending image-based geometry models to video without requiring massive 4D datasets, which is practically important given the difficulty of collecting high-quality 4D human scans. The root-relative depth representation is a simple but effective formulation that could be adopted in other human-centric 3D tasks beyond this specific pipeline. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-person scenes and longer sequences (the paper mentions these in supplementary but the main evaluation is single-person), whether the reliance on external pose estimation (VIMO) for absolute depth introduces error propagation in the wild, and whether the I2G model's quality bottleneck (shown in Tab. 5d) can be closed to further improve end-to-end performance.
