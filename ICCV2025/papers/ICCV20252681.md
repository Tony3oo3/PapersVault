## Problem

Existing open-set recognition (OSR) methods rely on the familiarity hypothesis, detecting novelty only by the absence of familiar features. This paper identifies that classification-layer weights attenuate features via the Hadamard product, discarding information that is critical for distinguishing known from unknown inputs. The authors propose an attenuation hypothesis showing that both pre-attenuation deep features and post-attenuation Hadamard-product features are necessary for robust OSR, and that prior methods exploit at most one side of this attenuation.

## Input / Output

COSTARR operates as a post-processing step on any pretrained network with a linear classification head. It consumes the penultimate-layer feature vector F(x) and the classification weight vector W_j for the predicted class, requiring no retraining or architectural modification. The output is a pair: the predicted class label (from the maximum globally-normalized logit) and a scalar COSTARR score S(x) ∈ [0,1] that approximates the joint probability the input is known and correctly classified, enabling a single threshold for known/unknown rejection.

## Method

COSTARR constructs a consolidated per-class vector C_j(x) = Concat(F(x), F(x) ⊙ W_j), combining pre-attenuation features with the post-attenuation Hadamard product for the predicted class. It computes cosine similarity between C_j(x) and a precomputed per-class mean of C_j over correctly classified training samples, rescaled to [0,1]. This similarity is then multiplied by the globally min-max-normalized maximum logit (GNL), yielding the final score S(x) = λ_m(x) · C_m(x). The probabilistic interpretation shows S(x) ≈ P(x ∈ K ∩ (Ω(x) = m)), where the logit factor cancels the conditional-probability denominator, making the score robust to per-class calibration errors. The method is training-free, architecture-agnostic, and adds only constant test-time overhead beyond a single dot product per class.

## Results

COSTARR achieves the best Operational Open-Set Accuracy (OOSA) across all five architectures (ResNet-50, ConvNeXt-L, ConvNeXtV2-H, ViT-H/MAE-H, Hiera-H) and all four unknown datasets (iNaturalist, NINCO, OpenImage-O, Textures). On the strongest architecture Hiera-H, COSTARR reaches 0.879 OOSA on iNaturalist, 0.788 on NINCO, 0.857 on OpenImage-O, and 0.826 on Textures. Improvements over the prior state-of-the-art PostMax are statistically significant (p < 10⁻⁵ per architecture, p < 10⁻⁶ pooled), with the largest gains on the contamination-free NINCO dataset (e.g., +3.0 points on Hiera-H). The secondary AUOSCR metric confirms the same ordering, with COSTARR leading on every architecture–dataset combination.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COSTARR outperforms the prior state-of-the-art PostMax [7] on every architecture and every unknown dataset, with the largest margins on the contamination-free NINCO set (e.g., 0.788 vs. 0.758 on Hiera-H, 0.780 vs. 0.749 on ViT-H/MAE-H, +3.0 and +3.1 points respectively). Against the simpler logit/softmax baselines, COSTARR leads MSP [15] by 4–8 OOSA points and MaxLogit [16] by 7–11 points across architectures. The OOD-specific post-processors SCALE [44] and NNGuide [29] trail COSTARR by 8–25 OOSA points, with NNGuide particularly weak on NINCO (0.644 vs. 0.788 on Hiera-H). COMBOOD [30] is compared only in the supplemental material using its published OOD scores, as it was not yet integrated into the OpenOOD benchmark at evaluation time. All improvements over PostMax are confirmed statistically significant via Wilcoxon signed-rank test with Bonferroni correction (p < 10⁻⁵ per architecture, p < 10⁻⁶ pooled across architectures).

## Relation to Prior Work

COSTARR departs from the prevailing 'familiarity hypothesis' (Dietterich & Guyer, 2022), which frames OSR as detecting the absence of familiar features, by proposing an 'attenuation hypothesis' that explains why small classification weights serve a dual role: differentiating known classes while discarding information useful for known/unknown separation. Prior post-processors each exploit only one side of this attenuation—OpenMax and EVM use raw deep features F, logit/softmax baselines use only the post-attenuated Hadamard product H, and PostMax (the prior SOTA) weakly incorporates F through feature-magnitude normalization without a clear theoretical justification. COSTARR is the first to explicitly formalize the need for both pre- and post-attenuation features and to provide a probabilistic interpretation of the resulting score as an estimate of P(input is known AND correctly classified).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The attenuation hypothesis offers a unifying explanation for why different OSR methods succeed or fail, which should guide future method design beyond the specific COSTARR formulation. The method is architecturally agnostic (validated on ResNet, ConvNeXt, ViT, Hiera) and adds only constant test-time overhead, making it a practical drop-in for deployed systems. Key limitations include reliance on pretrained networks with a linear classification layer, the approximate (not exact) nature of the probabilistic interpretation, and the fact that the specific cosine-similarity-on-concatenated-features combination is not claimed to be optimal. Open questions include whether the hypothesis and its exploitation transfer to fine-tuned or non-pretrained settings, and whether per-class adaptive weighting of F vs. H could yield further gains.
