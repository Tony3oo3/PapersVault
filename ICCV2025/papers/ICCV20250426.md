## Problem

Video diffusion models produce visually realistic videos but consistently fail to generate physically plausible motion because they rely on memorization and case-based imitation rather than understanding physical laws. Existing remedies—prompt refinement (PhyT2V) or graphics-engine simulation—are either limited to narrow physical phenomena or incur high computational costs, leaving no generalizable framework that injects physics as a condition into the diffusion process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250426/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a single RGB image (720×480) as the first frame and a free-text description of the physical event to be generated. The VLM predicts bounding-box trajectories for 12 future frames, which are linearly interpolated to 49 frames to match the VDM's temporal resolution. The final output is a 49-frame video at 720×480 that adheres to the physical dynamics implied by the input description.

## Method

VLIPP is a two-stage pipeline that decouples coarse physical planning from fine motion synthesis. In stage one, GPT-4o acts as a motion planner: it identifies relevant objects via Grounded-SAM2, determines the governing physical law (gravity, momentum, optics, thermodynamics, magnetism, fluid mechanics), and uses chain-of-thought reasoning with in-context physical descriptions to predict per-object bounding-box trajectories over 12 frames. In stage two, these coarse trajectories animate a draft video (objects are translated and resized on an inpainted background), from which RAFT extracts optical flow that is reformulated as structured noise. This structured noise conditions Go-with-the-Flow (a fine-tuned CogVideoX) for video synthesis, with Gaussian noise injected at γ=0.4 (even frames) and γ=0.6 (odd frames) to give the VDM freedom to synthesize fine-level dynamics such as speed, acceleration, and vibration that the coarse planner does not specify.

## Results

On PhyGenBench, VLIPP achieves an average physical realism score of 0.60, outperforming the best T2V baseline (PhyT2V, 0.52) by 15.3% and the best I2V baseline (SG-I2V, 0.54) by 11.1%. The largest gains appear in Thermal (0.60 vs. 0.51) and Material (0.53 vs. 0.39) domains, where bounding-box trajectory reasoning is most informative. On Physics-IQ, VLIPP scores 42.3 in Solid Mechanics and 34.1 in Fluid Dynamics, improving over the second-best model (SG-I2V) by 22.2% and 9.2% respectively, with an overall average of 34.6 vs. 29.7.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLIPP outperforms all six baselines on both benchmarks. Against CogVideoX (T2V-5B and I2V-5B), VLIPP gains 0.15 and 0.08 in average PhyGenBench score respectively, with the I2V variant being the closest competitor in Optics (0.69 vs. 0.71). LTX-Video (both T2V and I2V) trails by 0.21 and 0.10 in average score, showing the weakest performance overall. PhyT2V, which refines text prompts for physics, is the strongest T2V baseline (0.52 average) but still lags VLIPP by 0.08, particularly in Thermal (0.49 vs. 0.60) and Material (0.47 vs. 0.53). LLM-Grounded Video Diffusion performs worst among all baselines (0.31 average), confirming that text-only LLM guidance is insufficient for physical plausibility. SVD-XT matches CogVideoX-I2V at 0.52 average but is outperformed by VLIPP across all four domains. SG-I2V, the motion-controllable I2V baseline, is the closest competitor (0.54 average on PhyGenBench, 29.7 on Physics-IQ), yet VLIPP still leads by 0.06 and 4.9 points respectively, with the largest gap in Solid Mechanics (42.3 vs. 34.6 on Physics-IQ).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Go-with-the-Flow, a motion-controllable video diffusion model that accepts structured optical-flow noise as conditioning, and repurposes it as a fine-level motion synthesizer. It departs from prior physically plausible video generation approaches in two key ways: rather than relying on explicit physics engines (PhysGen, MotionCraft, PhysDreamer) or iterative prompt refinement (PhyT2V), it uses a VLM (GPT-4o) as a coarse-level motion planner that predicts bounding box trajectories via chain-of-thought and physics-aware reasoning. The conceptual lineage draws from LLM-guided video planning (VideoDirectorGPT, Pandora) but extends it to physical phenomena by grounding the VLM's reasoning in identified physical laws and in-context physics context, then bridging to the VDM through optical-flow-based structured noise with deliberate noise injection to preserve fine-grained dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that a two-stage VLM-planner + VDM-synthesizer pipeline can substantially close the physical plausibility gap in video generation without requiring explicit physics simulation, offering a scalable and generalizable alternative to solver-based methods. The 35.8% improvement in the Material domain and 22.2% in Solid Mechanics over the best I2V baselines suggest that coarse semantic motion cues are sufficient to steer a powerful generative model toward physically correct behavior. Key limitations remain: the framework lacks 3D spatial perception (failing on depth-dependent interactions like domino chains), cannot model intrinsic state changes well (e.g., gas solidification), and is sensitive to optical-flow noise on small objects. Open questions include whether 3D-aware planning or multi-object interaction modeling could address these gaps, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the six physical categories currently supported.
