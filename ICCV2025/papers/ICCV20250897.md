## Problem

Existing point cloud completion methods are trained and evaluated on synthetic datasets (PCN, ShapeNet, MVP) that exhibit uniform point distributions, minimal noise, and lack rich topological structure. The paper demonstrates via Persistent Homology analysis that real-world point clouds possess numerous 0- and 1-dimensional topological features, higher non-uniformity, and significantly more noise, causing state-of-the-art completion methods to fail in realistic settings. No real-world paired dataset existed to benchmark or train on such challenging data.

## Input / Output

The method takes incomplete or sparse point clouds of industrial structures (21 categories, ~40,000 pairs) captured by multiple sensors in railway environments, where inputs can be uniformly sparse, non-uniformly sparse, or have missing regions due to occlusion. The output is a complete point cloud matching the ground truth. Internally, TopODGNet operates on sparse seed point clouds of 256–1024 points extracted at intermediate decoder layers for efficient topological computation, while BOSHNet samples proxy backbones directly from the ground truth surface at multiple sparsity levels as additional training inputs.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary approaches leveraging 0-dimensional Persistent Homology. TopODGNet integrates a topological loss into ODGNet's decoder by computing the Vietoris-Rips complex over sparse multi-level seeds and minimizing the sum of persistence values of 0-dim features (excluding the first k components), which enforces a single connected skeleton that guides point generation along a topologically consistent structure. BOSHNet circumvents the expensive homology computation entirely by directly sampling multiple proxy backbones from the ground truth surface at varying sparsity levels, using them as additional seeds in the training loss so the network has access to precise shape skeletons from the very first training step rather than waiting for learned seeds to sharpen. The key insight is that 0-dim PH features encode the global connected-component topology of a shape, and enforcing this as a prior constrains the completion to follow the correct structural skeleton.

## Results

On the RealPC dataset, BOSHNet achieves a CD-L2 of 5.4 (×10⁻³), dramatically outperforming the best baseline SnowflakeNet (72) and ODGNet (111). On CD-L1, BOSHNet scores 69, comparable to SnowflakeNet (60) and significantly better than ODGNet (119). TopODGNet, which integrates 0-dim persistent homology priors into ODGNet, reduces CD-L1 from 119 to 103 and CD-L2 from 111 to 80, confirming the benefit of topological regularization. Across all 21 RealPC classes, existing completion methods (ODGNet, PointTr, FoldingNet, PCN, TopNet, SnowflakeNet, GRNet, AnchorFormer, SeedFormer, SVDFormer) exhibit CD-L1 scores ranging from 21 to 1103 (×10⁻³), demonstrating widespread failure on real-world point clouds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BOSHNet decisively outperforms all baselines on CD-L2 (5.4 vs. 72 for SnowflakeNet and 111 for ODGNet), a 13× improvement over the best baseline, while remaining competitive on CD-L1 (69 vs. 60 for SnowflakeNet). TopODGNet improves over its ODGNet backbone by 16 points on CD-L1 (103 vs. 119) and 31 points on CD-L2 (80 vs. 111), validating the utility of 0-dim PH topological priors. Among the 11 baselines benchmarked on RealPC (Table 4), SnowflakeNet achieves the lowest mean CD-L1 (60) and F-Score (7), while TopNet performs worst (mean CD-L1 of 1103), and methods like SeedFormer (21) and SVDFormer (84) show inconsistent per-class performance. The paper's central finding is that all existing methods, regardless of architecture (transformer-based like PointTr/AnchorFormer, voxel-based like GRNet, or point-based like PCN/FoldingNet), fail to generalize from synthetic datasets (PCN, ShapeNet) to the noisy, non-uniform, topologically rich RealPC data. BOSHNet's advantage stems from providing the network with multiple 0-dim PH proxy backbones from the start of training, unlike TopODGNet which only obtains accurate backbones in later training phases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on ODGNet (Cai et al., AAAI 2024) as the architectural backbone, extending it with 0-dimensional Persistent Homology priors (TopODGNet) and further replacing expensive PH computation with a lightweight Homology Sampler (BOSHNet). Conceptually, it departs from the dominant paradigm of evaluating point cloud completion on synthetic, noise-free, uniformly-sampled datasets (PCN, ShapeNet, MVP) by introducing RealPC, a real-world industrial dataset, and by importing tools from Topological Data Analysis—specifically Persistent Homology—into the completion pipeline, a direction the authors note has not been explored for PC completion. The broader completion literature (PointTr, SnowflakeNet, AnchorFormer, SeedFormer, etc.) is treated as the baseline landscape that the paper benchmarks against to expose a real-world performance gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RealPC dataset (~40,000 paired samples, 21 industrial categories) is the first real-world, multi-sensor, paired object-level PC completion benchmark, and the paper's central empirical finding—that state-of-the-art completion methods degrade dramatically on noisy, non-uniform, topologically rich real-world scans—challenges the field's reliance on synthetic benchmarks. The PH-based topological prior offers a principled direction for encoding global shape structure, but the demonstrated gains are modest (TopODGNet: 119→103 CD-L1; BOSHNet matches the best baseline on CD-L1 while improving CD-L2 substantially), leaving open whether 0-dim PH priors generalize to higher-dimensional homology, to other domains beyond railway infrastructure, or to larger-scale scene completion. The dataset's domain specificity (railway industrial structures) and the modest method improvements limit immediate broad adoption, but the benchmark itself is likely to become a standard stress-test for future completion methods.
