## Problem

Existing 3D few-shot class-incremental learning methods that bridge 3D point clouds with 2D foundation models (e.g., CLIP) rely on single-step 3D-to-2D projections that extract only CLIP's texture-biased final-layer representations, discarding intermediate spatial priors that naturally correspond to 3D geometric hierarchies. This causes semantic blurring, geometric myopia, and unstable decision prototypes under extreme data scarcity, leading to catastrophic forgetting during incremental updates.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw 3D point clouds (processed by a Recon-based transformer encoder) and text class descriptions (encoded by CLIP's text encoder into prototypes). Point clouds are simultaneously rendered as multi-view depth maps for the 2D branch, which are enhanced with learned background colors before being fed to a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32. The output is a single class label selected by argmax over task-specific logits that combine geometric feature-prototype similarity with CLIP's image-text alignment score.

## Method

CMGR repurposes CLIP's intermediate transformer layers (selected set L_r = {0, 4, 8}) as hierarchical '2D lenses' for geometric rectification rather than relying solely on the final layer. The Structure-Aware Geometric Rectification (SAGR) module performs layer-adaptive cross-attention between 3D point features and 2D depth features at these intermediate layers, augmented with self-masking attention (mask ratio 0.9) to suppress geometrically irrelevant attention patterns and a contrastive regularization loss between masked and unmasked features. The Texture Amplification Module (TAM) synthesizes minimal learnable RGB background colors conditioned on point cloud features, filling ambiguous projection regions in depth maps to align them with CLIP's natural-image input space without modifying CLIP's architecture. The Base-Novel Discriminator (BND) is a lightweight binary classifier trained independently before each incremental task that routes samples to a frozen base network or the evolving novel-class network, isolating geometric variations and preventing catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

On the primary cross-domain ShapeNet→CO3D setting (39 base / 50 novel classes), CMGR achieves 78.6% average accuracy (AA) with a forgetting rate of ΔA=3.2%, outperforming the strongest prior method FILP-3D (70.8% AA, ΔA=4.9%) by 7.8 points in AA. On ModelNet→ScanObjectNN, CMGR reaches 84.6% AA (ΔA=7.0%) versus FILP-3D's 82.7% (ΔA=7.5%), and on ShapeNet→ScanObjectNN it attains 88.4% AA (ΔA=3.1%) versus FILP-3D's 86.5% (ΔA=3.7%). In within-domain settings, CMGR leads on ShapeNet→ShapeNet with 87.6% AA and ΔA=1.2%, and on ModelNet→ModelNet with 79.8% AA, though its ΔA (8.7%) is slightly higher than C3PR's (6.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMGR outperforms all nine baselines on AA across all three cross-domain settings. Against the strongest 3D FSCIL competitor FILP-3D [37], CMGR gains +7.8 AA on S2C (78.6 vs 70.8), +1.9 on M2O (84.6 vs 82.7), and +1.9 on S2O (88.4 vs 86.5), while also reducing ΔA on S2C (3.2 vs 4.9) and S2O (3.1 vs 3.7). Against C3PR [8], the margin is larger: +6.6 AA on S2C, +9.0 on M2O, and +11.0 on S2O, with lower ΔA on S2C (3.2 vs 2.8 is actually slightly worse for CMGR) but substantially lower on S2O (3.1 vs 5.2). Among 2D FSCIL methods adapted to 3D, the gap is dramatic: FACT [44] achieves only 65.4% AA on S2C versus CMGR's 78.6%, and LwF [19] collapses to 15.3% AA, confirming that texture-biased 2D features are inadequate for 3D geometric hierarchies. In the within-domain ModelNet→ModelNet setting, CMGR's AA (79.8%) marginally beats MicroShape [11] (79.6%) and C3PR (78.6%), though its ΔA (8.7%) is higher than C3PR's (6.2%), suggesting slightly more forgetting in that specific configuration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-based 3D FSCIL line initiated by FILP-3D and C3PR, which project point clouds to depth maps and leverage CLIP's final-layer representations for cross-modal alignment. The key departure is the observation that CLIP's intermediate transformer layers encode part-level spatial relationships (adjacency, symmetry) that naturally correspond to 3D geometric hierarchies, whereas prior methods discard these by using only the texture-biased final layer. The proposed CMGR framework introduces hierarchical cross-attention with selected intermediate CLIP layers (SAGR), a lightweight learnable background-color mechanism to bridge the depth-to-RGB domain gap (TAM), and a binary base-novel discriminator to stabilize incremental prototypes (BND), collectively repositioning CLIP from a texture projector to a geometry-refining lens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practically important gap: deploying 3D recognition systems in open-world settings where new object categories emerge with only a handful of point cloud samples, a scenario critical for autonomous navigation and industrial robotics. The insight that intermediate CLIP layers carry geometrically meaningful spatial priors—rather than just the final layer's texture-dominated semantics—could inform broader cross-modal 3D representation learning beyond FSCIL. However, the authors acknowledge the assumption that CLIP's 2D spatial priors adequately capture 3D structure may fail for highly deformable or heavily occluded objects, leaving open questions about adaptive geometric priors and uncertainty modeling for complex real-world 3D scenarios.
