## Problem

Existing video-text foundation models rely on large ViT-L or larger backbones trained on tens of A100 GPUs for thousands of hours, making them impractical for mobile deployment. While efficient image-text models like MobileCLIP exist for mobile devices, they lack temporal modeling and cannot handle video-text understanding tasks. This paper bridges the gap by adapting an efficient image-text architecture into a video-text model that runs on mobile hardware with strong zero-shot retrieval and classification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251007/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 8 frames sampled from a video (resized to 256×256) and a corresponding text description. The video encoder produces frame-level embeddings of shape T×D, which are average-pooled (a parameter-free temporal pooling operation) into a single video-level vector of dimension D for contrastive matching against the text embedding. For zero-shot action recognition, the model constructs template sentences (e.g., 'a video of [action]') and performs video-to-text retrieval to assign a class label.

## Method

MobileViCLIP starts from MobileCLIP (a hybrid CNN-Transformer image-text model based on FastViT) and introduces two minimal architectural modifications to add temporal modeling: (1) Spatiotemporal RepMixer, which prepends a 1D depthwise convolution for temporal mixing before the existing 2D depthwise convolution, and (2) Spatiotemporal Attention, which adds learnable temporal positional encodings (TPE) before the existing conditional positional encodings (CPE). Both modules exploit structural reparameterization at inference, collapsing skip connections into single depthwise convolutions to minimize latency. Training uses video-text contrastive learning (InfoNCE) on InternVid-10M-FLT with the text branch frozen and the video branch fully fine-tuned, requiring only 8 RTX 3090 GPUs for 2 days. The paper also provides an in-depth latency decomposition showing that attention layers dominate mobile inference cost and scale super-linearly with stacked depth, unlike convolutional layers.

## Results

On zero-shot text-to-video retrieval, MobileViCLIP-Small (99.4M total params) achieves 42.5/43.5 R@1 (T2V/V2T) on MSR-VTT, 40.7/41.1 on DiDeMo, and 35.3/31.9 on ActivityNet, matching or exceeding InternVideo2-L14 (644M params) on MSR-VTT while being 55.4× faster on mobile (41.8ms vs 2319.2ms). On zero-shot action recognition, MobileViCLIP-Small reaches 63.1% on Kinetics-400, 81.0% on UCF-101, and 53.7% on HMDB-51, outperforming InternVideo2-S14 by +1.0/+2.0/+1.2 points respectively. The Tiny variant (53.9M params) achieves 38.7/38.1 R@1 on MSR-VTT with only 15ms mobile latency, surpassing the image-only MobileCLIP-S0 baseline by +1.5/+1.2 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MobileViCLIP-Small achieves zero-shot MSR-VTT T2V R@1 of 42.5, matching InternVideo2-L14 (42.1) and ViCLIP (42.4) while using only 99.4M vs. 644M/433M parameters and running 55.4×/55.4× faster on mobile (41.8ms vs. 2319.2ms). Against the same-size InternVideo2-S14 (133M params), MobileViCLIP-Small gains +6.9 T2V and +7.6 V2T on MSR-VTT while being 6.7× faster on mobile (41.8ms vs. 282.2ms). On DiDeMo, MobileViCLIP-Small (40.7/41.1) outperforms InternVideo2-B14 (40.3/39.1) and approaches InternVideo2-L14 (42.8/43.2). On zero-shot action recognition, MobileViCLIP-Small beats InternVideo2-S14 on Kinetics-400 (63.1 vs. 62.1), UCF-101 (81.0 vs. 79.0), and HMDB-51 (53.7 vs. 49.2), but trails on SSV2-MC (46.1 vs. 46.4). For fine-tuned retrieval (Table 3), MobileViCLIP-Small reaches 49.3/50.3 on MSR-VTT, competitive with UMT-B (51.0/49.0) and CLIP4CLIP (45.6/45.9) at a fraction of the latency. On temporal grounding (QVHighlights), MobileViCLIP-Small features outperform CLIP+SlowFast (68.9 vs. 67.4 R1@0.5), and on zero-shot temporal action detection (THUMOS14), it slightly exceeds Coca ViT-L/14 (5.3 vs. 4.8 avg mAP).

## Relation to Prior Work

MobileViCLIP extends the efficient image-text model MobileCLIP (Vasu et al., CVPR 2024) by introducing minimal temporal modeling—specifically a 1D depthwise convolution in the RepMixer block and learnable temporal positional encodings in the attention block—converting an image encoder into a video encoder. Rather than training a video-text foundation model from scratch with large ViT backbones (as in InternVideo2, ViCLIP, or UMT), it adopts a parameter-efficient transfer learning paradigm: fine-tuning the pre-trained MobileCLIP on InternVid-10M-FLT with the text branch frozen. The architectural lineage traces through FastViT's structural reparameterization and hybrid CNN-Transformer design, which the paper preserves to maintain low mobile latency while adding spatiotemporal capacity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first video-text model explicitly designed for on-device deployment, demonstrating that a 36M-parameter video encoder can match a 309M-parameter ViT-L model on zero-shot retrieval while running 55× faster on mobile hardware. The detailed latency decomposition (Section 6) revealing that attention layers scale super-linearly on mobile while depthwise convolutions remain nearly constant provides actionable guidance for future efficient video architecture design. Limitations include the fixed 8-frame/256×256 input, a performance gap on long-form video (ActivityNet), and the relatively simple temporal modeling that may not capture complex long-range dependencies. Open questions include whether this reparameterization-based approach can extend to longer temporal contexts and whether the efficiency-performance tradeoff holds for more demanding tasks like video generation or dense temporal reasoning.
