## Problem

Relative pose estimation for rolling shutter cameras has traditionally relied on parametric motion models (SLERP, linearized rotation, etc.) that may not match real-world trajectories, and the general problem without any motion model remained unsolved. This paper asks whether relative pose can be estimated from the intersections of line projections with a single scanline per image, eliminating the need for explicit motion modelling entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251392/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only 1D intersection points (x-coordinates where detected lines cross a chosen scanline) from m images, plus optional gravity/vertical directions and known intrinsics (focal length, no distortion). It outputs per-scanline camera poses (rotation R_i(y_i) and center C_i(y_i)), with the caveat that camera centers are only determined up to a shift along the common line direction. In the parallel-lines setting without gravity, poses are additionally determined only up to a projective transformation H ∈ R^{3×3}.

## Method

The core geometric constraint is that the backprojected ray from a scanline intersection must intersect the 3D line, yielding a scalar triple product equation (Eq. 3) per line-camera pair. For parallel lines, the problem algebraically reduces to estimating 2D structure from uncalibrated 1D cameras: each camera is encoded by a 2×3 matrix A_i with no internal constraints (verified via Gröbner basis), and the trifocal tensor T ∈ R^{2×2×2} is estimated linearly from 7 correspondences via SVD. With a gravity prior and vertical lines, the problem becomes calibrated 1D camera pose estimation, solvable with existing 3-view (5 lines) and 4-view (4 lines) solvers. The (D,3,7) solver (parallel lines + gravity) requires homotopy continuation due to 48 solutions. Minimal problems are enumerated via DoF balancing and Jacobian rank tests, and all solvers are wrapped in RANSAC for robustness on real data.

## Results

On the Fastec dataset (19 sequences), the (D,3,7) solver achieves the best multi-view performance, producing at least one pose with error below 10° in 10/19 sequences and below 30° in 17/19 sequences. In single-view mode, (D,3,7) achieves 4/19 sequences below 10° and 9/19 below 30°. In terms of overall pose accuracy (Table 3), the (D,3,7) multi-view solver yields 5.6% of poses below 10° and 13.8% below 20°, while the (E,4,4) multi-view solver reaches 22.4% below 30°. The (B,3,7) solver achieves a median tensor error of 0.85 (multi-view) and 0.89 (single-view), with minimum errors of 0.44 and 0.62 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper introduces the first scanline-based relative pose estimation method for rolling shutter cameras without explicit motion modeling, so no direct comparison to prior RS relative pose methods is provided. The comparisons are between the four proposed solver variants: (B,3,7), (D,3,7), (E,3,5), and (E,4,4). The (D,3,7) solver (parallel lines + gravity prior) consistently outperforms the others in multi-view mode, achieving the highest number of sequences with at least one pose below 10° (10/19) and 30° (17/19). In single-view mode, (E,4,4) slightly edges out (D,3,7) at the 30° threshold (13 vs. 9 sequences). The (E,3,5) solver, while fastest at 9.16 µs, shows the weakest performance overall. The paper notes that incorporating a motion model would likely improve stability, but the model-agnostic design is the key contribution enabling independent per-scanline pose estimation for SfM initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in rolling shutter (RS) relative pose estimation, which relies on parametric motion models (SLERP, Cayley, linearized rotation, etc.) to relate scanline poses. Instead, it formulates the problem per-scanline using line projections, eliminating any motion model entirely. The key theoretical insight is a clean equivalence: the parallel-lines setting reduces to uncalibrated 1D camera pose estimation (Quan & Kanade, 1997), while the vertical-lines-plus-gravity setting reduces to calibrated 1D camera pose (Åström & Oskarsson, 2000). The enumeration of minimal problems follows the PLMP framework (Duff et al., 2024), and the canonical-form matrix decomposition for the (B,3,7) solver mirrors the approach in Hruby et al. (2023) for four-view geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formulation enabling per-scanline relative pose without a motion model, positioning it as a foundational building block for motion-model-free RS SfM where each scanline's pose is computed independently. However, the practical impact is currently limited: success rates on Fastec are modest (only 1–6 of 19 sequences achieve <5° error in multi-view), the method requires known gravity direction and specific scene structure (parallel or vertical lines), and it estimates pose only up to a shift along the line direction. Open questions include whether robust curve detectors can handle RS line bending, whether the approach can be integrated into a full SfM pipeline, and whether motion-model-free estimation can ever match the stability of parametric approaches.
