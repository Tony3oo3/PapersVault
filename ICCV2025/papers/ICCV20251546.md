## Problem

Existing AI-generated image detection benchmarks (GenImage, Fake2M, WildFake, Chameleon) rely almost exclusively on everyday-life images and evaluate detectors only on pristine digital files, ignoring two critical real-world degradations: repeated social-media transmission and re-digitization (scanning, re-photographing). This inflates reported accuracy and leaves open whether detectors can generalize to high-stakes scenarios such as war, disasters, and public health events where misinformation is most damaging.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251546/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample is an RGB image that exists in three variants: the original file, a version that has passed through 2–6 rounds of compression across platforms (Telegram, WeChat, Facebook, QQ, WhatsApp, X, Instagram, Tinder), and a version altered by one of four re-digitization methods (scanning a color printout, photographing a printout, photographing a screen, photographing a projection). Detectors and VLMs output a binary label (AI-generated or Real); VLMs additionally return a JSON-structured prediction. The dataset spans multiple resolutions, including lower-resolution samples from StyleGAN and ProGAN to mirror the quality distribution of real-world imagery.

## Method

RRDataset is constructed by generating 10,000 AI images (via SD 3.5 Large, Flux.1, DALL-E 3, SDv1.4/1.5, Midjourney, StyleGAN, ProGAN) and collecting 10,000 real images (from Reuters, AP, BBC, UN Photo, ICRC, COCO, CC3M-val, Unsplash) across seven scenarios, then applying systematic internet transmission and re-digitization transformations to every image. RRBench evaluates 17 supervised detectors (pretrained on GenImage-SD v1.4, fine-tuned on an RRDataset subset) and 10 VLMs (zero-shot) under all three conditions, revealing that 14–16 of 17 detectors suffer large accuracy drops under transmission or re-digitization. A 192-participant human benchmark with 240 test images and a 2-shot learning phase shows that humans also degrade under these conditions but recover substantially after minimal exposure. Finally, a human-inspired robustness-oriented in-context learning prompt is designed for VLMs, explicitly instructing them to focus on core content while disregarding transmission/re-digitization artifacts, which lifts GPT-4o's overall accuracy from 84.09% to 87.47%.

## Results

On RRDataset, the best detector DRCT-ConvB achieves 89.59% overall accuracy (93.52% fake / 95.52% real on original images), while the best VLM GPT-4o-latest reaches 84.09% overall (96.30% fake / 92.68% real on original). Under internet transmission, 14 of 17 detectors show reduced fake accuracy, with Freq-Net, Fusing, and SAFE dropping by 71.61%, 79.86%, and 97.41% respectively. Under re-digitization, 16 of 17 detectors suffer major drops—DIRE collapses from 89.72% to 1.42% fake accuracy and DNF from 90.62% to 0.05%—while AIDE remains remarkably stable with only a 2.91% fake-accuracy reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRCT-ConvB [8] achieves the highest overall accuracy (89.59%) among all 17 detectors, benefiting from its diffusion-based reconstruction and contrastive training strategy; it remains relatively stable under transmission (−0.70% fake) but drops 29.18% under re-digitization. GPT-4o [1] as a zero-shot VLM surpasses 16 of the 17 specialized detectors on original images (96.30% fake accuracy vs. DRCT-ConvB's 93.52%), yet degrades more uniformly across conditions (−27.07% fake under re-digitization). Diffusion-noise-based methods DIRE [54] and DNF [60] are nearly immune to transmission (±0.6% change) but catastrophically fail under re-digitization (fake accuracy drops to 1.42% and 0.05% respectively), revealing that their learned noise signatures are destroyed by physical re-capture. AIDE [56] demonstrates the best re-digitization robustness among all detectors (only −2.91% fake accuracy), attributed to its reliance on CLIP-extracted semantic features that survive physical transformations. Frequency-domain methods Freq-Net [50], Fusing [20], and SAFE [29] are the most fragile to transmission, with fake-accuracy drops of 71.61%, 79.86%, and 97.41% respectively, indicating that compression artifacts they exploit are quickly erased by social-media re-encoding. The human-inspired in-context learning approach applied to GPT-4o further narrows the gap to the best detector, reaching 87.47% overall accuracy (vs. DRCT-ConvB's 89.59%) with a 5.50% improvement in re-digitization robustness over zero-shot GPT-4o.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by combining three previously disconnected evaluation dimensions—scenario diversity, internet transmission robustness, and re-digitization robustness—into a single unified benchmark (RRDataset/RRBench). Prior detection datasets (GenImage, WildFake, Chameleon) focus on everyday-life content and ideal conditions, while earlier robustness studies (SEMI-TRUTHS, WildRF, FOSID) examine only limited augmentations or a narrow set of platforms. The human evaluation component extends HPBench (50 participants, 100 images) and FakeBench (34 participants) to 192 participants and 240 images, and introduces a few-shot learning phase absent from prior human benchmarks. The paper does not propose a new detector but systematically evaluates 17 existing detectors and 10 VLMs under these combined stressors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that state-of-the-art detectors, particularly diffusion-based methods (DIRE, DNF), suffer catastrophic accuracy collapse (dropping to near 0%) under re-digitization—a degradation mode largely ignored in prior evaluations—while hybrid CLIP-based approaches (AIDE) remain surprisingly stable. The human study's finding that just two examples boost detection accuracy by ~14% suggests a rapid adaptation mechanism in human perception that current algorithms lack, pointing toward a concrete design direction for future detectors. The broader societal implication is a 'trust crisis': 89% of participants in sensitive scenarios default to labeling uncertain images as AI-generated, which could undermine legitimate news and public information. Open questions include whether in-context learning strategies for VLMs can close the gap to specialized detectors at scale, and how to design detectors that are simultaneously robust to both transmission and re-digitization without sacrificing accuracy on clean images.
