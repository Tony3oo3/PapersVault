## Problem

Existing occupancy prediction and forecasting research suffers from three critical gaps: (1) widely used driving datasets (nuScenes, Waymo) lack official 3D occupancy labels, forcing reliance on suboptimal pseudo labels that capture only LiDAR-reflective surfaces, and standard IoU metrics cannot expose this quality degradation; (2) methods are siloed to single datasets with incompatible sensor configurations, preventing cross-domain training and out-of-distribution evaluation; (3) no dataset provides per-voxel motion flow or supports cooperative multi-agent occupancy forecasting.

## Input / Output

The unified format represents scenes as a 3D voxel grid G ∈ {0,…,C}^{L×W×H} (200×200×16 at 0.4 m resolution, ego-centered) alongside K_cam RGB images, binary FOV masks, SE(3) camera extrinsics, and per-voxel forward/backward flow tensors F, B ∈ R^{L×W×H×3}. For 2D tasks, the height dimension is collapsed via a class-priority scheme (e.g., Pedestrian > Car > Road) to prevent occlusion of critical classes. Outputs include the predicted occupancy grid, optional future flow, and a suite of both label-dependent (IoU_geo, mIoU_geo) and ground-truth-free metrics (GMM-based dimension probability, temporal shape consistency, background consistency).

## Method

UniOcc is primarily a unified benchmark and toolkit rather than a single model. Its key technical contributions are: (1) a per-voxel flow annotation pipeline that computes individual 3D displacement vectors for each voxel (capturing full rotation and translation, unlike prior per-object velocity assignment), provided in both forward and backward conventions; (2) ground-truth-free evaluation metrics—Key Object Dimension Probability via per-category GMM likelihood, Temporal Foreground Shape Consistency via PCA-aligned voxel IoU across frames, and Temporal Background Consistency via ego-motion-projected binary IoU—that assess geometric plausibility without relying on flawed pseudo labels; (3) a voxel-space processing toolkit using 6-connected-component labeling, rotating-calipers bounding-box fitting, flow-guided step prediction with Hungarian bipartite matching for tracking, and PCA-based rotation alignment. For empirical validation, the authors augment OccWorld's Spatial-Temporal Generative Transformer with a flow encoder, cross-attention fusion with scene tokens, and a flow decoder supervised by L2 loss, demonstrating that explicit voxel-level flow improves mIoU and that multi-source training (nuScenes + Waymo + CARLA) enhances cross-domain generalization and long-horizon accuracy.

## Results

On the occupancy prediction task (nuScenes), CVTOcc achieves mIoUgeo of 31.57 and IoUgeo of 81.20, substantially outperforming Cam4DOcc (mIoUgeo 13.59, IoUgeo 13.33). For occupancy forecasting on nuScenes, augmenting OccWorld with voxel-level flow improves mIoUgeo from 66.79 (no flow) to 70.64 at 0s and IoUgeo from 60.66 to 62.62, with gains persisting through the 3s horizon. Cross-domain experiments show that training on nuScenes+Waymo+CARLA yields the best CARLA test performance (mIoUgeo 85.26 at 0s) and that multi-source real-world training (nuScenes+Waymo) consistently outperforms single-source training on both in-domain and out-of-domain splits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CVTOcc dominates Cam4DOcc on nearly all metrics for camera-based occupancy prediction on nuScenes: mIoUgeo is more than 2× higher (31.57 vs. 13.59) and IoUgeo is ~6× higher (81.20 vs. 13.33), attributed to CVTOcc's temporal cost-volume fusion mechanism. Cam4DOcc edges out CVTOcc only on background IoU (52.46 vs. 48.93), while car IoU favors CVTOcc by 24.5 points (80.60 vs. 56.13). For cooperative occupancy prediction, CoHFF on OpenCOOD achieves IoUcar of 87.22 and IoUbg of 51.90, demonstrating that multi-agent fusion substantially improves car detection over single-ego baselines. The flow ablation (Table 2) shows that voxel-level flow consistently outperforms both object-level flow and no-flow baselines across all prediction horizons, with the largest gains in mIoUgeo (70.64 vs. 66.79 at 0s) and car IoU (81.50 vs. 78.39). Cross-domain results (Table 3) reveal that no single training source generalizes well: CARLA-only models drop to mIoUgeo ~30 on real-world test sets, while multi-source training (nuScenes+Waymo+CARLA) achieves the best CARLA scores (85.26 mIoUgeo at 0s) but slightly degrades background consistency (IoUbg drops ~7.5% vs. nuScenes-only), highlighting an unresolved sim-real gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a unification effort analogous to UniTraj for trajectory prediction, bringing together occupancy prediction (CVTOcc, Cam4DOcc, Occ3D, SurroundOcc, OpenOccupancy) and occupancy forecasting (OccWorld, DynamicCity, OccSora) under a single data format and evaluation protocol. It departs from prior occupancy datasets by introducing per-voxel 3D flow annotations (as opposed to object-level velocity in CarlaSC or horizontal-only flow in LetOccFlow) and by adding ground-truth-free evaluation metrics (GMM-based dimension plausibility, temporal shape consistency, background IoU) that address the well-known pseudo-label quality problem. The methodological contribution is modest—augmenting OccWorld's Spatial-Temporal Generative Transformer with a flow encoder and cross-attention fusion—while the primary value lies in the unified data infrastructure and toolkit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UniOcc fills a critical gap by providing the first cross-dataset, cross-task benchmark for occupancy work, enabling fair comparison and cross-domain generalization studies that were previously impossible due to fragmented data formats. The finding that CARLA simulation data improves object shape fidelity (P_car) yet degrades background temporal consistency (IoU_bg drops ~7.5%) due to speed-range mismatch highlights a concrete, actionable sim-real gap that the community must address before relying on synthetic data at scale. Open questions include whether the per-voxel flow annotation generalizes to higher-resolution grids, whether the ground-truth-free metrics can replace IoU entirely for multi-modal forecasting, and how cooperative occupancy forecasting scales beyond the two-vehicle OpenCOOD setting.
