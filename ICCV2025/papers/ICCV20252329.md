## Problem

While self-supervised Vision Transformers show promise for in-context scene understanding via nearest-neighbor retrieval, they still fall short of LLMs' in-context learning capabilities. Existing post-training methods that improve dense representations rely on complex self-distillation frameworks with multiple architectural components (differentiable sorting, RoI alignment, Sinkhorn-Knopp optimization), making them hard to interpret, tune, and stabilize. This paper asks whether a simpler, more direct training objective can produce better dense features for in-context scene understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252329/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a query image and a large memory bank of 10,240,000 patch-wise features (randomly sampled from available training images) as input, and produces a dense semantic segmentation mask or depth map via top-30 nearest-neighbor retrieval with cross-attention (τ=0.07). During post-training, the input is an automatically constructed pseudo-task: one query image plus K support images (one positive sharing objects with the query, K−1 random distractors), all with pseudo-segmentation labels generated offline via DiffCut and K-means clustering on DINOv2R features. The fine-tuned ViT encoder outputs patch-wise features in a D′-dimensional space after an MLP projection head with ℓ₂-normalization.

## Method

DIP post-trains a pretrained ViT encoder (e.g., DINOv2R) on automatically generated in-context pseudo-tasks that directly simulate the downstream retrieval-based segmentation scenario, inspired by meta-learning episodes. Each pseudo-task pairs a query image with one positive support image (found via DINOv2R global-feature nearest neighbors sharing a pseudo-class occupying >5% of image area) and K−1 random distractors from the mini-batch. The model predicts the query's segmentation via a cross-attention layer where query patch features serve as queries, support patch features as keys, and patchified pseudo-labels as values, trained with pixel-wise cross-entropy loss. Pseudo-segmentation maps are generated by combining DiffCut (Stable Diffusion-based recursive Normalized Cuts for class-agnostic segments) with K-means clustering on DINOv2R pooled features for pseudo-class assignment. The approach eliminates self-distillation, EMA teachers, and complex architectural components, requiring only a randomly initialized MLP projection head and under 9 hours on a single A100 GPU.

## Results

On the in-context scene understanding benchmark (Table 2), DIP with ViT-B/14 achieves an average mIoU improvement of +2.00 over DINOv2R across six datasets (ADE20K 42.6, PascalVOC 82.1, Pascal-Context 51.5, Cityscapes 59.5, CS→ACDC 52.1, COCO 76.0), outperforming NeCo's +1.50 average delta. In low-shot regimes (Table 1), DIP with ViT-B/14 reaches 70.1 mIoU on PascalVOC at 1/128 training data, compared to 65.0 for NeCo and 60.3 for DINOv2R. For monocular depth prediction on NYUv2, DIP achieves the best RMSE of 0.756 (×10), improving over DINOv2R (0.771) and NeCo (0.769). Post-training is computationally efficient, requiring under 9 hours on a single A100 GPU for ViT-S.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIP consistently outperforms its base model DINOv2R on all six evaluation datasets for both ViT-S and ViT-B, with the gap widening in low-shot settings (e.g., +9.8 mIoU on PascalVOC at 1/128 data for ViT-B). Against NeCo (ICLR'25), the closest competitor, DIP wins on 5 of 6 datasets for ViT-B (ADE20K +1.4, Pascal-Context +0.3, Cityscapes +1.6, CS→ACDC +0.6, COCO +0.6) but trails by 0.3 mIoU on PascalVOC (82.1 vs 82.4). DIP surpasses the supervised RADIOv2.5 (82.1 vs 81.3 on PascalVOC, 42.6 vs 42.1 on ADE20K) despite using no manual annotations, and dramatically outperforms SAM (82.1 vs 32.8 on PascalVOC) and the Stable Diffusion features it leverages for pseudo-labeling (82.1 vs 59.4). When applied to other base models, DIP improves CLIP by +2.0 mIoU on PascalVOC and MAE by +33.4 mIoU, transforming MAE from a weak (13.9) to a competitive (47.3) in-context segmentation model. On depth prediction (NYUv2), DIP achieves the best RMSE (0.756) over both DINOv2R (0.771) and NeCo (0.769), despite its pseudo-tasks being designed for segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIP builds directly on DINOv2R as its base encoder and on DiffCut (which leverages Stable Diffusion features) for generating class-agnostic pseudo-segmentations, combining these in a way not previously explored for dense representation post-training. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant self-distillation paradigm used by NeCo, CrIBO, and Leopart—methods that rely on EMA teachers, differentiable sorting, Sinkhorn-Knopp optimization, or RoI alignment—by replacing these with a meta-learning-inspired objective that trains on automatically constructed in-context retrieval tasks. The in-context dense prediction framing itself follows Balazevic et al. (Hummingbird) and NeCo, but DIP's use of cross-attention over pseudo-labeled support patches as the training signal, rather than contrastive or ranking objectives, is a distinct design choice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DIP offers a practical, computationally cheap (<9 h on one A100) alternative to complex self-distillation pipelines for improving dense features, which is valuable for practitioners who want to specialize pretrained VFMs without engineering elaborate training architectures. The finding that a diffusion model's internal features (via DiffCut) can serve as effective pseudo-segmentation sources for post-training opens a path toward leveraging generative models as unsupervised annotation tools more broadly. Open questions include whether the pseudo-label quality (which the ablation shows is critical—removing DiffCut drops PascalVOC from 81.0 to 59.1) limits ceiling performance, whether the approach extends to video or 3D dense prediction, and whether the modest but consistent gains over DINOv2R compound when combined with other post-training strategies.
