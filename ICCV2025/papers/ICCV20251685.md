## Problem

Diffusion Transformers generate high-fidelity short videos, but scaling to longer sequences (>64 frames) is computationally prohibitive under full quadratic attention, while autoregressive chunk-by-chunk generation suffers from catastrophic forgetting and temporal incoherence. The paper asks whether a parallel inference paradigm can generate long, temporally consistent videos without the sequential bottleneck or the quadratic cost of full attention.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251685/cropped_page0_idx7.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and generates photorealistic videos at 192×320 resolution and 16 FPS, up to 512 frames (80 latent frames via a 3D VAE that compresses 16 pixel frames into 5 latent frames). During the diffusion process, the noisy latent video is consumed by both the VIN encoder (which subsamples keyframes every 1.0 s) and the DiT (which denoises 20-latent-frame chunks in parallel), with 12 overlapping local latent frames shared between adjacent chunks at inference time.

## Method

Video Interface Networks (VINs) augment a DiT with a fixed-size abstraction module inspired by dual-process cognition: a VIN encoder uses cross-attention to compress subsampled keyframes into 512 global tokens (d=4096), and a stack of 4 self-attention processor blocks refines these tokens conditioned on the text prompt. The DiT and VIN are trained end-to-end on a factorized denoising objective where the DiT simultaneously denoises multiple temporal chunks in parallel, each conditioned on the shared global tokens plus stop-gradient local context from the preceding chunk. At inference, all chunks are denoised in parallel at every diffusion step, and overlapping tokens in adjacent chunks are fused via a position-weighted average (early fusion, t>20, is most effective). The key architectural insight is that decoupling the encoding into fixed-size tokens independent of input length allows the VIN to scale to arbitrarily long videos while the DiT only ever processes short local chunks, yielding 25–40% fewer FLOPs than full attention with superior motion smoothness.

## Results

On VBench Long, VINs achieve the best subject and background consistency among all chunk-based methods, meaningfully closing the gap with full-attention generation while maintaining high dynamic degree (motion magnitude) that full attention paradoxically loses at longer lengths. In optical flow analysis, VINs sustain MAWE scores below 2.0 even at 512 frames, with stable optical flow magnitudes of 1.5–2.0 and warping errors of 4.0–5.0, compared to more erratic motion in baselines. Efficiency-wise, VINs use 25–40% fewer FLOPs and achieve 40–75% faster denoising than full attention while requiring only marginally more memory. In a user study, human raters rated VIN-generated videos as comparable or better on overall appearance (loss rate < 30%) and matched full generation on temporal consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Full Attention (Open-Sora base model), VINs maintain high dynamic degree and temporal consistency at 256–512 frames where full attention stagnates and degrades, while using 25–40% fewer FLOPs and 40–75% faster denoising. Against the Autoregressive baseline, VINs substantially improve subject and background consistency (DINOv2/CLIP features) by avoiding the finite-context catastrophic forgetting inherent in sequential generation. Against StreamingT2V (ST2V), the state-of-the-art sequential method with memory modules, VINs further reduce catastrophic forgetting and achieve lower MAWE scores, indicating smoother motion without handpicked anchors. Against FreeNoise (noise rescheduling) and Spectral Blending/FreeLong (band-pass filtering), both training-free parallel methods, VINs achieve significantly lower MAWE and fewer scene cuts at 256 and 512 frames, demonstrating that learned deep semantic global tokens outperform surface-level frequency or noise-based consistency priors. In the user study, VINs are preferred over all sequential and parallel baselines on temporal consistency, matching full generation in human preference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie) as its generative backbone and draws conceptual inspiration from Recurrent Interface Networks (RINs), which offload pixel-level denoising to learned semantic tokens. It departs from the dominant autoregressive chunk-generation paradigm (SVD, StreamingT2V) that requires sequential sampling with handpicked memory modules, and from parallel methods (FreeNoise, FreeLong/Spectral Blending) that enforce consistency via pre-specified surface-level priors like noise rescheduling or band-pass filtering. The key departure is learning deep, input-aware global tokens end-to-end through a cross-attention encoder, framing the problem through a 'fast and slow' cognitive analogy where VINs provide System-1 abstractions that guide DiT System-2 denoising in parallel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VINs offer a practical middle ground between the quadratic cost of full attention and the sequential bottleneck of autoregressive generation, achieving 25–40% fewer FLOPs and 40–75% faster denoising while matching or exceeding temporal consistency. The observation that full attention paradoxically degrades in dynamic degree at longer lengths (motion stagnation) despite higher compute is a useful diagnostic for the field. Limitations include learning only through the generation objective (no auxiliary downstream tasks), modest resolution (192×320), and the open question of whether richer representations (depth, 3D structure) or concurrent multi-task training could further improve the global tokens.
