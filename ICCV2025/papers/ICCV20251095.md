## Problem

Learned image compression models trained offline on entire datasets suffer from an amortization effect: the estimated signal distribution only approximates the general image distribution and fails to capture per-image characteristics. This gap manifests at two levels—global statistical distribution gaps (content/theme differences) and local structural gaps (quantization and sampling randomness)—but existing per-sample optimization methods only address the image-level gap, leaving fine-grained structural details unoptimized.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (evaluated at 768×512 on Kodak and ~2K resolution on CLIC Professional) and produces a compressed bitstream consisting of three transmitted components: a quantized latent ŷ, a sparse index map id_t, and a syntax vector s. At the decoder side, these are combined to reconstruct the image, with the syntax vector generating adaptive decoder parameters and the index map selecting codebook vectors for local detail compensation.

## Method

The paper proposes a Cross-Granularity Online Optimization strategy with two complementary modules. For coarse-grained optimization, a Data-Dependent Transform (DDT) module extracts a multi-scale syntax vector from the image, performs gradient descent with a relaxed (distortion-only) objective to avoid model collapse, then uses binary search over an adaptive quantization parameter to minimize total bitrate cost; the optimized vector generates adaptive convolution kernels for the decoder's final layer. For fine-grained optimization, a Mask-based Selective Compensation (MSC) module encodes the quantization residual through a VQ codebook (K=4096), then applies a binary sparse mask—determined by per-pixel loss-differential testing—to transmit only the most beneficial compensation vectors, with a binary search over λ_c aligning the rate-gain trade-off with the global distortion parameter. Training follows a progressive multi-stage schedule: DDT module first (with relaxed rate constraint), then MSC encoder/decoder (MSE loss), then codebook (with a warm-up phase using random vector selection to maximize codebook utilization).

## Results

On the CLIC Professional Validation dataset, the proposed method achieves a BD-rate of −43.86% (BPG anchor), outperforming the strongest learned compression baseline Li (ICLR-24) at −42.80% and the VVC reference (VTM-12.1) at −34.64%. On the Kodak dataset, the method achieves −35.21% BD-rate. The model has 43.76M parameters (only ~2% more than the Liu CVPR-23 baseline at 42.89M), with an encoding time of 36.34 s and decoding time of 0.25 s for a 768×512 image, comparable to VVC's 31.53 s encoding time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method achieves the best BD-rate among all compared methods on both CLIC (−43.86%) and Kodak (−35.21%). It outperforms its direct baseline Liu (CVPR-23) by 2.21 percentage points in BD-rate with only a 2% increase in parameters, and surpasses Li (ICLR-24) by 1.06 points while using roughly 38% fewer parameters (43.76M vs. 70.97M). Against the VVC reference (VTM-12.1), the method gains 9.22 points in BD-rate on CLIC. In terms of computational cost, the encoding time (36.34 s) is comparable to VVC (31.53 s) and substantially lower than implicit-representation methods like COOL-CHIC (64.97 s) and NIF (147.27 s), while the decoding time (0.25 s) matches the Liu baseline (0.24 s). The method also outperforms earlier learned compression baselines—Cheng (CVPR-20), Xie (ACMMM-21), He (CVPR-22), and Zou (CVPR-22)—by 5–14 BD-rate points, confirming consistent gains across the learned compression literature.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the end-to-end learned image compression framework of Liu et al. (CVPR-23) by adding two per-sample optimization modules: a Data-Dependent Transform (DDT) module for coarse-grained, image-level distribution alignment (building on Yang et al., CVPR-22) and a Mask-based Selective Compensation (MSC) module using a VQ codebook for fine-grained structural detail recovery (training strategy from Esser et al., CVPR-21). The key conceptual departure from prior per-sample optimization work is the explicit cross-granularity framing: existing methods like [38] only optimize at the image level via gradient descent, while this paper argues that local structural distribution gaps require a separate sparse-representation mechanism. The adaptive QP selection strategy to prevent model collapse during gradient descent is a practical refinement over the vanilla variational objective used in prior DDT-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that jointly optimizing global and local distribution alignment can yield meaningful BD-rate gains (43.86% over BPG on CLIC) with only a 2% parameter increase, suggesting that the amortization effect in learned compression is not solely addressable by larger networks but benefits from structured per-sample adaptation. However, the 36-second encoding time (vs. 0.25s for the baseline) limits practical deployment, and the approach has only been validated on 2K-resolution still images. Open questions include whether the cross-granularity strategy extends to video compression, whether the VQ codebook can be made more adaptive per-sample, and whether the encoding cost can be reduced through fewer gradient descent iterations or learned initialization.
