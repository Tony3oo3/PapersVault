## Problem

Existing 3D style transfer methods, whether NeRF-based or 3DGS-based, operate under a fixed-output paradigm that produces a single stylized result with a predetermined content-style balance. This makes it impossible for users to adjust the degree of stylization to match their preferences, as different users desire different levels of style injection relative to content preservation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250846/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained 3D Gaussian Splatting scene (trained on Mip-NeRF 360, Tanks&Temples, LLFF, or Instruct-N2N data), a single reference style image, and a continuous style intensity parameter β ∈ [0%, 100%] as input. It produces a single stylized 3D Gaussian scene whose rendering changes continuously with β, rather than separate scenes per intensity level. The continuous β is quantized into Z=10 discrete levels via a staircase function, and the least important 50% of Gaussian primitives are filtered out before style injection to prevent occlusion-related artifacts.

## Method

The method comprises two components: Intensity-tunable Style Injection (ISI) and Tunable Stylization Guidance (TSG). ISI assigns a learnable Gaussian neuron to each 3D Gaussian primitive that predicts attribute offsets (position, scaling, rotation, opacity, color) conditioned on the style image, and a style tuner maps the quantized intensity β to a learnable embedding that modulates these offsets via element-wise multiplication, enabling continuous intensity control. TSG uses IP-Adapter-SDXL to stylize rendered views, then enforces multi-view consistency through cross-view style alignment: anchor view features are back-projected to 3D and re-projected to each target view for content calibration before mutual self-attention injection into the U-Net up_blocks. Training follows a two-stage strategy: Stage 1 (2000 steps) optimizes the Gaussian neurons with full-style guidance only (L1 + LPIPS against diffusion-stylized views), while Stage 2 (2000 steps) freezes the full-offset parameters and optimizes remaining tuner embeddings using a weighted blend of zero-style guidance (against original renders) and full-style guidance (against stylized views), modulated by the sampled β value.

## Results

On five scenes (face, truck, train, lego, garden) from Mip-NeRF 360, Tanks&Temples, LLFF, and Instruct-N2N, Tune-Your-Style achieves the best multi-view consistency with short-range LPIPS/RMSE of 0.033/0.035 and long-range LPIPS/RMSE of 0.062/0.067, substantially outperforming the closest 3DGS-based baseline G-Style (0.044/0.059 and 0.093/0.096). For style fidelity, the method attains CLIP similarity of 0.2619 and CLIP directional similarity of 0.2881, exceeding all baselines. In the user study (5-point scale), the method scores 3.97±0.13, well above G-Style (3.10±0.40), StyleGaussian (2.79±0.16), and InstantStyleGaussian (2.06±0.22).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Tune-Your-Style outperforms all three 3DGS-based baselines on every reported metric. Against StyleGaussian [30], the method nearly halves the short-range LPIPS (0.033 vs 0.067) and long-range LPIPS (0.062 vs 0.126), while improving CLIP directional similarity by 0.066 (0.2881 vs 0.2223) and user preference by 1.18 points (3.97 vs 2.79). Against G-Style [25], the closest competitor, the gains are more modest but consistent: short-range LPIPS drops from 0.044 to 0.033, long-range LPIPS from 0.093 to 0.062, and CLIP directional similarity rises from 0.2391 to 0.2881. Against InstantStyleGaussian [51], the advantage is the largest in user study (3.97 vs 2.06) and CLIP directional similarity (0.2881 vs 0.2160). NeRF-based methods (StylizedNeRF [19], StyleRF [29], ARF [54]) are compared only qualitatively in the appendix, where the authors report visually superior stylized outcomes due to the diffusion-based guidance. Notably, the method's unique intensity-tunable capability has no direct counterpart in any baseline, which all operate under a fixed-output paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3DGS-based style transfer line (StyleGaussian, G-Style, InstantStyleGaussian) by introducing an explicit intensity-tunable mechanism via Gaussian neurons and a learnable style tuner, departing from the fixed-output paradigm of all prior 3DGS and NeRF-based stylization methods. The diffusion-based guidance leverages IP-Adapter-SDXL for efficient 2D stylization, while the cross-view style alignment borrows mutual self-attention from MASA-CTRL and adds a content-calibration step via depth-based feature warping. The two-stage optimization strategy (full-style then tunable) is a novel training recipe that addresses the instability of jointly learning tunable parameters from scratch, and the 3D Gaussian filter draws on LightGaussian's importance scoring to prune redundant primitives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The intensity-tunable paradigm addresses a genuine practical gap: users of 3D style transfer tools often need to dial style strength up or down, and prior methods force a single fixed output that may over- or under-stylize. The two-stage optimization and cross-view alignment are practical engineering solutions that make the tunable objective tractable, and the ~20-minute per-scene training on a single GPU keeps the method accessible. Limitations include the per-scene optimization (no generalization across scenes), the fixed 10-level quantization of the style tuner, and dependence on a specific diffusion backbone (IP-Adapter-SDXL). Open questions include whether the tunable formulation can be made real-time, whether it extends to text-driven or multi-reference stylization, and whether the Gaussian neuron offsets can be distilled into a feed-forward model.
