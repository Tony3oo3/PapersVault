## Problem

Existing long-tailed recognition methods enlarge tail-class decision regions or sharpen boundaries but leave the robustness of those regions underconsidered. In the pre-trained era, tail-class test samples often differ from their sparse training counterparts, eroding classifier margins and causing misclassification as head classes. This paper addresses the gap by treating the problem from a space exploration perspective, synthesizing diverse exploratory examples that simultaneously balance class distribution, sharpen boundaries, and strengthen decision regions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100-LT, variable resolution for ImageNet-LT and iNaturalist 2018) and outputs a single class label via a linear classifier. Crucially, SEL operates entirely in the learned feature space rather than the input pixel space, so it is backbone-agnostic and can be plugged into any encoder–classifier pipeline without architectural modification. It can be applied either during the main training loop or as a short fine-tuning stage on the classifier alone.

## Method

SEL synthesizes exploratory examples in feature space using an adaptive Optimal Foraging Algorithm operator: z_e = z_s + r(z_s′ − z_s), where z_s is a target sample and z_s′ is a candidate sample. Two modules regulate the selection: Class-biased Complement (CbC) assigns each tail-class sample as a target k_c = ⌈(n_max − n_c)/n_c⌉ times to balance the effective class distribution, while Fitness-weighted Sampling (FwS) probabilistically picks the candidate from a pre-identified neighboring class (determined by inter-class-centroid distance) weighted by the model's predicted confidence, ensuring the exploratory example falls between the target and adjacent class regions with a rational label. The training objective is a weighted sum of the original task loss and a cross-entropy loss on the exploratory examples (λ = 0.9). A rational analysis proves that optimizing these examples forces the classifier to increase tail-class scores relative to head-class scores, directly counteracting the head-class bias.

## Results

On CIFAR-100-LT (ρ=100), ResNet-32+SEL achieves 44.53% top-1 accuracy, a +4.57% improvement over the vanilla ResNet-32 (39.96%) and outperforming IB (40.50%), KPS (41.97%), and H2T+CE (42.27%). On ImageNet-LT, ResNet-50+SEL reaches 47.86% overall accuracy (+3.06% over baseline), with the largest gain in the Few class (31.27% vs. 26.04%). On iNaturalist 2018, ResNet-50+SEL achieves 69.10% (+3.15%), and GLMC+SEL reaches 74.51% (+1.37%). Across 48 experiment groups on CIFAR-LT, SEL improves 44 of them with an average gain of 1.37%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEL is a plug-and-play framework that improves all eight compared baselines, with the largest gains on methods that do not already address decision-region robustness. ResNet-32+SEL (44.53% on CIFAR-100-LT ρ=100) surpasses IB (40.50%), KPS (41.97%), and H2T+CE (42.27%), though it remains below GCL (46.50%) and BCL (51.76%). GLMC+SEL achieves the strongest overall results (56.48% on CIFAR-100-LT ρ=100; 57.24% on ImageNet-LT; 74.51% on iNaturalist 2018), consistently outperforming all other methods. SEL consistently outperforms MixUp across all datasets and imbalance ratios (e.g., +4.57% vs. +1.59% on CIFAR-100-LT ρ=100). Improvements are most pronounced at high imbalance ratios (ρ=100) and for Few-class accuracy on ImageNet-LT and iNaturalist, while gains on BCL and H2T are modest (≤0.83%), likely because BCL already tightens decision regions via contrastive learning and H2T also uses an evolutionary-inspired augmentation strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEL builds directly on the Optimal Foraging Algorithm (OFA) [51], extending its scalar operator to a vector operation in feature space to synthesize exploratory examples. It positions itself as addressing a specific gap left by MixUp and its variants (which smooth decision space but introduce label ambiguity and boundary confusion) and by contrastive learning methods like BCL and TSC (which sharpen boundaries but weaken decision region robustness). The framework is explicitly designed as a plug-and-play complement to existing long-tailed recognition methods—re-balancing, logit adjustment, contrastive learning, and feature compression—rather than a replacement, operating in the feature space to remain architecture-agnostic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SEL offers a simple, parameter-free training framework that practitioners can bolt onto any existing long-tailed recognition pipeline without modifying network architecture, making it immediately adoptable. The theoretical analysis (Sec. 3.4) provides a clear mechanistic explanation for why tail-class accuracy improves, which is valuable for understanding the interplay between decision region size and boundary sharpness. However, the average improvement is modest (~1.3–1.5%), gains are inconsistent across methods (BCL and H2T see minimal benefit), and the added computational cost of synthesizing exploratory examples limits its appeal for very large-scale training. Open questions include whether the OFA-based exploration generalizes to non-CNN backbones (e.g., vision transformers) or to other forms of data imbalance beyond long-tailed classification.
