## Problem

Open-vocabulary semantic segmentation (OVSS) methods universally adopt CLIP's template-averaging strategy, where each class is represented by the mean of 80 hand-crafted prompt embeddings. This paper shows that for every class, individual templates (class-experts) can significantly outperform the averaged classifier, yet identifying and exploiting them without ground-truth labels or training has been entirely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252162/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, a set of K class names in natural language, and an unlabeled image set (used only for expert identification, not training). At inference, the image is processed through a frozen CLIP vision encoder with sliding windows (448×448, stride 224 for CLIP-DINOiser/MaskCLIP; 336×336, stride 112 for NACLIP), and the output is a per-pixel semantic segmentation map over K classes. The only hyperparameter is N=4 experts per class, and the method is fully training-free and label-free.

## Method

FLOSS operates in two stages. First, for each of the 80 CLIP templates and each class k, it computes the average per-pixel softmax entropy over all pixels that template's classifier assigns to class k across the unlabeled image set; the N=4 templates with the lowest entropy are selected as class-experts for that class. Second, K expert classifiers are constructed (each averaging the N expert template embeddings for all K class names), producing K soft-segmentation maps. A fusion rule then assigns each pixel to the class whose expert both predicted its own class of expertise and achieved the highest softmax score among such experts; if no expert predicts its own class at a pixel (~2% of cases), the default W_CLIP classifier is used as fallback. The entire pipeline is plug-and-play, requiring no gradient updates, no labels, and no modification to the base OVSS model.

## Results

FLOSS is a plug-and-play, training-free method that consistently improves three state-of-the-art OVSS models across five benchmarks. On average mIoU, CLIP-DINOiser improves from 38.5 to 39.7 (+1.2), NACLIP from 38.2 to 39.0 (+0.8), and MaskCLIP from 28.7 to 29.3 (+0.6). The largest per-dataset gain is +3.3 mIoU on Cityscapes for CLIP-DINOiser (31.3→34.6), while gains on VOC20 and PC59 are smaller (+0.05 to +1.4), attributed to these datasets being closer to ImageNet where CLIP's 80 templates were originally engineered. In low-data regimes, FLOSS outperforms the base model with as few as 1 unlabeled image on Cityscapes and ~25 images on PASCAL VOC 20.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLOSS is evaluated as an add-on to three training-free OVSS backbones (MaskCLIP, NACLIP, CLIP-DINOiser) and consistently outperforms each base model on all five primary datasets, with the most substantial gains on Cityscapes (+0.8 to +3.3 mIoU) and ADE20K (+0.7 to +1.0 mIoU), where the semantic vocabulary is larger and further from ImageNet. Against the strongest standalone baselines in Table 1, CLIP-DINOiser+FLOSS (39.7 avg) surpasses NACLIP (38.2) and SCLIP (37.1), while NACLIP+FLOSS (39.0) also exceeds SCLIP. On VOC20, gains are minimal (+0.05 to +1.4 mIoU) because the dataset's ontology closely matches the ImageNet templates CLIP was tuned on. In out-of-domain generalization (Table 3), FLOSS identified on Cityscapes transfers to ACDC weather conditions, BDD-100K, and MAPILLARY with consistent improvements of up to +4.9 mIoU (CLIP-DINOiser on ACDC Fog). The method also demonstrates cross-dataset expert transferability: experts identified on COCO-Stuff (171 classes) improve performance on Cityscapes, VOC20, and PC59 for the shared classes (Table 5). Overall, FLOSS never degrades performance relative to its base model, confirming its plug-and-play nature.

## Relation to Prior Work

FLOSS builds directly on CLIP's template-averaging paradigm, which was adopted by default in OVSS methods like MaskCLIP, CLIP-DINOiser, and NACLIP, all of which focus on densifying or modifying the visual encoder while leaving the text-side classifier unchanged. The paper departs from this visual-encoder-centric lineage by shifting the intervention to the text modality: rather than engineering new prompts or learning prompt parameters, it selects a subset of CLIP's existing 80 ImageNet templates per class using an unsupervised entropy criterion. This positions FLOSS as orthogonal and complementary to prior OVSS work, functioning as a plug-and-play wrapper rather than a replacement for the underlying segmentation pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FLOSS offers a practical, zero-cost improvement to OVSS that requires no labels, no training, and no architectural changes, making it immediately deployable on top of any text-based segmentation model. The oracle experiments reveal substantial headroom (e.g., >30 IoU points for the 'sky' class), suggesting that template selection is a largely untapped axis of improvement. Open questions include whether better unsupervised proxies than entropy exist, whether performance gains scale with more or augmented templates beyond the current 80, and how the method behaves when K exceeds 100 classes where the per-class expert computation becomes more expensive.
