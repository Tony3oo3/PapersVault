## Problem

Existing single-view camera calibration methods are either tied to a specific camera model (pinhole, radial, UCM) or require extrinsic cues such as the gravity direction or visible horizon, limiting their applicability to edited, distorted, or unconstrained in-the-wild images. No prior method provides a unified, model-agnostic framework that calibrates arbitrary central camera models from a single image without conditioning on scene-level geometric priors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252273/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized and center-cropped to the nearest training resolution and aspect ratio at inference) and produces a dense 2D FoV field — one tangent-plane vector per pixel encoding the polar angle relative to the optical axis. From this field, intrinsics (focal length, principal point, and distortion coefficients) are recovered in closed-form for any user-selected camera model (pinhole, Brown-Conrady, Kannala-Brandt, UCM, EUCM, Division) at runtime, with a final 5-step Gauss-Newton refinement. Inference runs in ~25 ms on an RTX 4090.

## Method

AnyCalib uses a DINOv2-pretrained ViT-L backbone with a modified DPT CNN decoder (replacing transposed convolutions with convex upsampling) to regress dense FoV fields in the tangent plane at the optical axis, supervised with an L1 loss on 54k synthetic images from an extended OpenPano dataset of 4055 unconstrained panoramas. The key insight is that FoV fields are a minimal Euclidean representation bijective to per-pixel ray directions, and that rays combined with image coordinates yield a closed-form linear solution for the principal point and pixel aspect ratio (Eq. 11), followed by a second linear system for focal length and distortion coefficients under any supported camera model. This model-agnostic closed-form recovery eliminates the need to retrain or architecturally modify the network when switching camera models. A final stage iteratively minimizes the angular distance between predicted rays and those implied by the recovered intrinsics using five Gauss-Newton steps, converting the algebraic optimum into a geometrically meaningful solution.

## Results

On perspective images, AnyCalib_pinhole achieves the best angular error (AE) of 1.13° and reprojection error (RE) of 12.11 px on Stanford2D3D, outperforming GeoCalib (1.45°/15.16 px) and MoGe (1.92°/21.60 px). On LaMAR, AnyCalib_pinhole reaches 0.85° AE and 28.61 px RE, second only to MoGe (0.69°/25.64 px) but with far less training data (54k vs. >3M images). On distorted images, AnyCalib_dist achieves 1.59° AE on ScanNet++ and 1.60° on Mono, substantially outperforming GeoCalib-rad (4.97°/3.94°) and SVA (2.92°/8.29°). On edited (stretched/cropped) images, AnyCalib achieves the best focal-length error (e_f = 0.15–0.22) and principal-point error (e_c = 0.26–0.41) across all three tested datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnyCalib_pinhole achieves the best AE and RE on Stanford2D3D (1.13°/12.11 px), beating GeoCalib by ~0.3° AE and MoGe by ~0.8° AE. On LaMAR, MoGe edges out AnyCalib_pinhole on AE (0.69° vs. 0.85°) and RE (25.64 vs. 28.61 px), but AnyCalib is trained on 54k images versus MoGe's >3M, making the gap notable. On TartanAir and MegaDepth, MoGe and DUSt3R respectively achieve slightly lower AE (0.73° and 0.77°) than AnyCalib_pinhole (1.63° and 1.31°), though AnyCalib remains competitive within ~0.5–0.9°. On distorted images, AnyCalib_dist dominates: it achieves 1.59° AE on ScanNet++ versus 2.92° for SVA (which also crashes on 38% of images) and 4.97° for GeoCalib-rad, and 1.60° on Mono versus 3.94° for GeoCalib-rad. On edited images, AnyCalib consistently outperforms WildCam and DiffCalib on focal-length error (e.g., 0.15 vs. 0.32 on Stanford2D3D) and principal-point error (0.26 vs. 0.47), confirming that the FoV-field representation handles non-centered principal points more accurately than direct ray regression.

## Relation to Prior Work

AnyCalib builds on the hybrid calibration paradigm introduced by GeoCalib [86] and the ray-regression approaches of WildCam [100] and DiffCalib [34], but departs from them by replacing direct ray or perspective-field prediction with FoV fields—a minimal 2D tangent-space representation at the optical axis that is bijective to unit rays. Unlike prior learned methods (Perspective Fields [38], GeoCalib [86]) that condition on extrinsic cues like gravity direction or horizon visibility, AnyCalib relies solely on image content. The architecture follows the DINOv2 ViT-L + DPT decoder pattern, and the training data extends OpenPano [86] by adding unconstrained panoramas, removing the gravity-alignment requirement that limited prior synthetic data generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AnyCalib provides the first single-view calibration method that is genuinely model-agnostic, allowing practitioners to select among pinhole, Brown-Conrady, Kannala-Brandt, UCM, EUCM, or Division models at runtime without retraining—addressing a practical gap where prior methods were locked to one projection model. The data-efficiency result (54k training images outperforming 3D foundation models trained on >3M images) suggests that dense geometric supervision via FoV fields is a more sample-efficient signal than large-scale 3D reconstruction pretraining for calibration. Open questions include whether the ill-posed principal-point estimation for edited images can be resolved without additional cues, and whether the closed-form linear recovery generalizes to non-central or non-pinhole camera models beyond the currently implemented set.
