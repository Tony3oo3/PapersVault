## Problem

Face recognition training datasets are typically collected via web crawling, raising serious privacy and portrait-rights concerns, while existing synthetic face generation methods fail to simultaneously guarantee realistic data distribution, novel identities fully separated from real subjects, and per-identity consistency. Prior SOTA methods such as CemiFace and HSFace exhibit identity leakage from their training data, and others like SynFace can generate only a limited number of unique subjects.

## Input / Output

The diffusion model receives four inputs: a timestep t, the FR prototype vector w_r (a D-dimensional unit vector from the ArcFace embedding space), a five-point facial landmark image y (left eye, right eye, nose, left mouth corner, right mouth corner, extracted via RetinaFace) for pose conditioning, and the noisy image x_t. It outputs a synthetic RGB face image whose FR embedding is constrained to match the input prototype. The resulting VIGFace dataset provides 0.5M–6.0M images across 10K–120K virtual identities, each with 20–50 pose-varied variations, serving as a drop-in replacement for real datasets like CASIA-WebFace.

## Method

VIGFace is a two-stage framework. In Stage 1, an ArcFace backbone is trained on real faces while simultaneously maintaining k virtual prototypes W_V alongside the n real prototypes W_R; since no real images exist for virtual IDs, synthetic virtual embeddings f'_FR(x_j) = w_v^j + N(0,1)·σ (where σ is an EMA-tracked estimate of the real embedding spread) are injected to update virtual prototypes via the ArcFace loss, driving them to be orthogonal to all other prototypes. In Stage 2, a DiT-based diffusion model (predicting velocity v_t rather than noise) is trained to denoise face images conditioned on the FR prototype vector and the five-landmark image, with an additional constraint minimizing the L2 distance between the FR embedding of the generated image and the target prototype. Classifier-free guidance (10% zero-conditioning) and a guidance weight of 4.0 balance identity consistency against visual diversity, while the landmark conditioning enables controlled pose variation across generated images of the same identity.

## Results

On five 1:1 verification benchmarks (LFW, CFP-FP, CPLFW, AgeDB-30, CALFW), VIGFace(B) trained on 1.2M synthetic images (60K virtual IDs) achieves 94.64% average accuracy, surpassing CemiFace (93.22%), Arc2Face (93.14%), and HSFace20K (92.47%) at the same scale. At the 0.5M scale, VIGFace(S) reaches 92.56% average, outperforming all other synthetic methods including CemiFace (92.30%) and HSFace10K (92.00%). VIGFace(L) at 6.0M images achieves 94.91% average, matching the real CASIA-WebFace baseline (94.93%) while using only virtual identities, and outperforming HSFace300K (93.52%) which uses 15M images from WebFace4M. As a data augmentation method, combining VIGFace(B) synthetic images (both real and virtual IDs) with CASIA-WebFace yields 95.92% average, a +0.99% improvement over real data alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIGFace outperforms all synthetic face generation baselines on average verification accuracy at every scale tested. At the 0.5M image scale, VIGFace(S) (92.56%) beats CemiFace (92.30%), HSFace10K (92.00%), Arc2Face (91.73%), ID3 (89.80%), DCFace (89.56%), IDiffFace (88.20%), DigiFace (83.45%), GANDiffFace (75.48%), and SynFace (74.75%). At the 1.2M scale, VIGFace(B) (94.64%) leads CemiFace (93.22%) by 1.42% and HSFace20K (92.47%) by 2.17%, despite using only CASIA-WebFace as its training source while competitors leverage WebFace4M or WebFace42M. Notably, VIGFace(L) at 6.0M images (94.91%) surpasses HSFace300K at 15M images (93.52%) by 1.39%, demonstrating superior data efficiency. The paper also identifies identity leakage in CemiFace and HSFace (Vec2Face), where generated faces resemble real individuals from WebFace4M, whereas VIGFace shows no such leakage in its nearest-neighbor similarity analysis against CASIA-WebFace. As a data augmentation method, VIGFace(B) with both real-ID and virtual-ID synthetic images achieves 95.92% average (+0.99% over real-only), outperforming HSFace20K augmentation (+0.66%) and DigiFace augmentation (+0.37%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the ArcFace training framework by extending its prototype matrix to include virtual identity prototypes that are trained simultaneously with real ones, ensuring orthogonality in the embedding space. For generation, it adopts the DiT diffusion architecture with velocity prediction (following Lin et al.) and conditions on the FR prototype vector plus five-point facial landmarks for pose control. Conceptually, it departs from prior synthetic face methods (SynFace, DigiFace, DCFace, IDiffFace, CemiFace, HSFace) by closing the loop between the FR model and the generator: the FR model's own prototype space serves as both the conditioning signal and the separability guarantee, rather than sampling from pre-existing embeddings or requiring external identity vectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical and growing concern in face recognition research: the reliance on web-crawled datasets that raise portrait rights and privacy issues, particularly for non-celebrity and minor subjects. The identity leakage finding in CemiFace and HSFace is practically important, as it undermines the privacy claims of prior SOTA synthetic methods. Open questions include whether the virtual-prototype approach scales to the 10M+ identity regime (e.g., WebFace42M), whether the method generalizes to other biometric modalities, and whether the computational cost of generating millions of high-quality synthetic images is prohibitive for smaller research groups. The released VIGFace dataset could become a standard privacy-safe alternative for FR training.
