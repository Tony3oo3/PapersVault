## Problem

Spatial-aware 3D LUT methods for image enhancement improve quality by incorporating spatial information, but the additional modules for spatial feature fusion introduce large parameter counts and long inference times that scale poorly with image resolution. Moreover, the 3D LUTs themselves are highly redundant, with under 10% of generated vertices actually referenced during inference, wasting model capacity on unused parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251285/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 8-bit sRGB images (photo retouch) or 16-bit XYZ images (tone mapping) at arbitrary resolution as input. A backbone network processes a resized version of the input to extract image context, while the full-resolution image is used directly for the LUT transform and spatial slicing. The output is an enhanced 8-bit sRGB image, and the architecture is resolution-agnostic at inference, supporting 480p through 4K without retraining.

## Method

The core contribution decomposes each 3D LUT into a weighted linear combination of three 2D LUTs (rg, rb, gb pairs) plus a bias, then applies SVD to each 2D LUT to further reduce parameters by 88% relative to the original 3D LUT. Bilateral grids used for spatial awareness undergo an analogous 3D-to-2D decomposition. A cache-effective structure fuses the slicing and LUT transform into a single operation, eliminating high-resolution intermediate writes that caused runtime to balloon at 4K resolution. The backbone (ResNet-18 or a simpler CNN) predicts SVD components (S, U, V), 2D bilateral grids, and decomposition weights from the resized context, and the entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a combined MSE, CIE94 LAB color-difference, and LPIPS perceptual loss.

## Results

On the FiveK photo retouch task, SVDLUT achieves 25.76 dB PSNR and 0.931 SSIM at 480p, and 25.69 dB PSNR and 0.938 SSIM at 4K, with only 160.5K parameters. At 4K resolution, inference takes just 1.38 ms, compared to 3.64 ms for SABLUT and 4.39 ms for SA-3DLUT, while maintaining the best PSNR among all spatial-aware methods. On PPR10K, the method achieves 26.50/25.51/25.77 dB PSNR across the three ground truths, outperforming SABLUT by 0.05–0.09 dB in each case.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SVDLUT outperforms the closest spatial-aware competitor SABLUT [22] on PSNR (25.76 vs. 25.66 dB at 480p) and SSIM (0.931 vs. 0.930) while using only 1/3 the parameters (160.5K vs. 463.7K) and running 2.6× faster at 4K (1.38 ms vs. 3.64 ms). Against SA-3DLUT [38], the method achieves +0.26 dB PSNR with 1/28 the model size (160.5K vs. 4.5M). Among non-spatial-aware LUT methods, SVDLUT surpasses 3D LUT [46] by +0.47 dB PSNR and AdaInt [43] by +0.27 dB at 480p, though 3D LUT remains slightly faster at 4K (1.04 ms vs. 1.38 ms). On PPR10K, SVDLUT consistently beats SABLUT by 0.05–0.09 dB PSNR across all three expert ground truths while also improving ΔE_ab. The method also outperforms tone-mapping baselines (UPE, DPE, HDRNet, CSRNet) by 0.5–3.9 dB PSNR, confirming that the LUT-based approach with spatial awareness is substantially stronger than general-purpose enhancement networks for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the image-adaptive 3D LUT framework (Zeng et al., TPAMI 2022) and its spatial-aware variants (SA-3DLUT, ICCV 2021; SABLUT, ECCV 2024) by decomposing the 3D LUT into a weighted sum of 2D LUTs, further compressed via SVD, and by restructuring the bilateral-grid spatial fusion to be cache-efficient. The key conceptual departure from SABLUT is the observation that image-adaptive 3D LUTs are >90% redundant in practice, making the full 3D structure unnecessary. The SVD-based compression of 2D LUTs draws on matrix factorization ideas previously applied to LUT compression in super-resolution (Li et al., CVPR 2024) but applies them to the enhancement setting with a different decomposition strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 84% parameter reduction and flat 4K inference time (1.38 ms vs. 3.64 ms for SABLUT) make spatial-aware LUT-based enhancement practical on resource-constrained hardware, addressing a real deployment bottleneck. The finding that SVD is effective for LUTs but not for bilateral grids (due to the latter's spatial complexity) provides useful guidance for future LUT compression work. Limitations include the continued dependence on GPU for real-time performance (CPU inference at 21.7 ms for 480p is usable but not real-time) and the modest absolute performance gains over SABLUT (~0.1 dB PSNR), suggesting the decomposition approach has reached a practical ceiling for this architecture.
