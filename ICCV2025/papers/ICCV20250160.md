## Problem

GAN-based talking head generation methods suffer from two coupled failures in extreme poses and expressions: identity leakage, where the driven image's appearance bleeds into the source identity, and rendering artifacts caused by detail loss. The paper identifies that these issues share a common root—identity information embedded within motion features—and shows that the very leakage causing the first problem can be repurposed to fix the second.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250160/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single source image (identity) and either a driven video (motion) or an audio signal as input, producing a 512×512 talking head video at 25 fps. It supports decoupled, independent control over lip sync, head pose, and emotional expression via the inherited Face Decoupling Module. Inference runs at 27.6 FPS on an A100 with only 3.65 GB GPU memory, exceeding real-time requirements.

## Method

FixTalk builds on EDTalk's GAN encoder-decoder with a Face Decoupling Module and introduces two lightweight, plug-and-play components. The Enhanced Motion Indicator (EMI) replaces direct use of the 4th-layer driven feature with a Q-Former-style cross-attention extractor that uses learnable queries to pull motion-specific information from f_4^d (as K/V), then merges multi-scale features via average pooling and weighted sum; a cosine-similarity disentanglement loss further penalizes identity overlap between source and driven motion features. The Enhanced Detail Indicator (EDI) employs a dual-memory network (driven-identity memory and motion-source memory) that stores compressed driven tokens during self-supervised training; at inference, it queries the memory with the source feature concatenated with the motion difference to retrieve identity-matched detail tokens, which are decompressed and spatially integrated with source features via multi-head cross-attention before generation. A KL-divergence alignment loss ensures both memory address spaces point to the same slots, enabling correct retrieval without access to the driven identity at test time.

## Results

On the HDTF video-driven benchmark, FixTalk achieves the best scores across all six metrics: PSNR 27.16, F-LMD 1.093, FID 12.72, CSIM 0.613, NIQE 13.44, and CPBD 0.282, outperforming the closest GAN-based competitor LivePortrait (PSNR 27.05, NIQE 15.93) and diffusion-based methods X-Portrait (FID 46.55) and FYE (FID 42.68). On the MEAD audio-driven benchmark, FixTalk leads in PSNR (22.38), SSIM (0.743), M-LMD (1.314), F-LMD (1.215), and emotion accuracy (68.25%), with only a marginal loss in SyncNet confidence (8.01 vs. EDTalk's 8.12). In the user study, FixTalk scores highest on motion (4.21/5), identity (4.47/5), and quality (4.19/5). FixTalk also achieves real-time inference at 27.6 FPS with only 3.65 GB GPU memory, far exceeding the 25 FPS real-time threshold.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FixTalk outperforms all six video-driven baselines on HDTF across every metric. Against the closest GAN-based competitor LivePortrait [18], FixTalk gains +0.11 PSNR, −0.026 F-LMD, and a substantial −2.49 NIQE (13.44 vs. 15.93), indicating markedly fewer rendering artifacts. Against diffusion-based X-Portrait [79] and FYE [37], FixTalk achieves dramatically lower FID (12.72 vs. 46.55 and 42.68) and higher CSIM (0.613 vs. 0.505 and 0.544), showing superior identity preservation and overall quality. On the audio-driven MEAD benchmark, FixTalk beats EDTalk [59] (its base model) by +0.75 PSNR, +0.021 SSIM, and −0.223 M-LMD, while trailing only marginally on SyncNet confidence (8.01 vs. 8.12). FixTalk also surpasses FlowVQTalker [54] by +0.81 PSNR and +1.73 emotion accuracy, and dominates SadTalker [91], AniTalker [34], and Hallo [80] by large margins (e.g., +3.34 PSNR over SadTalker). In the system-efficiency comparison (Table 4), FixTalk runs at 27.6 FPS with 3.65 GB memory, compared to 0.057–0.463 FPS and 7.3–37.7 GB for diffusion-based EchoMimic [7], EchoMimicV2 [38], Hallo [80], and Hallo3 [10]. Qualitative comparisons with IP-LAP [97], TalkLip [67], PD-FGC [66], and EAT [14] show FixTalk produces correct head poses, accurate expressions, and fewer extreme-pose artifacts, though these methods lack quantitative scores in the main tables.

## Relation to Prior Work

FixTalk builds directly on EDTalk, a GAN-based talking head model with a Face Decoupling Module for disentangled control of mouth, pose, and expression. Rather than adopting the increasingly popular diffusion-based approaches (X-Portrait, Follow-Your-Emoji) that sacrifice real-time efficiency and fine-grained controllability, the paper stays within the GAN paradigm and addresses its two known weaknesses—identity leakage and rendering artifacts—through a targeted architectural intervention. The EMI module draws on Q-Former-style learnable queries and cross-attention for motion extraction, while the EDI module introduces a memory network (analogous to those in retrieval-augmented generation) to store and retrieve identity-relevant features. The key conceptual departure from prior GAN-based work is the reframing of identity leakage from a purely negative artifact to a dual-edged phenomenon that can be selectively suppressed (EMI) or harnessed (EDI).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners needing real-time, controllable talking head synthesis (virtual humans, interactive avatars, live streaming), FixTalk offers a plug-and-play upgrade to existing GAN-based pipelines without sacrificing the 25+ FPS throughput that diffusion methods cannot match. The memory-network-based EDI raises an open question about whether similar retrieval-augmented strategies could address detail loss in other one-shot generation tasks beyond faces. A limitation is that the approach is inherently tied to GAN architectures; whether the identity-leakage analysis and the taming strategy transfer to diffusion-based or autoregressive models remains unexplored. The paper also does not extensively validate on very long sequences or multi-person scenarios, leaving temporal consistency over extended durations as an open concern.
