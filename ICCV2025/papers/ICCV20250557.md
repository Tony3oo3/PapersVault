## Problem

Existing controllable image synthesis methods for multi-object scenes rely on 2D conditioning signals (text prompts, 2D bounding boxes, depth maps) that inherently discard 3D geometric structure, making them unable to handle nested objects, maintain viewpoint consistency, or account for off-screen context. No prior approach provides a unified, view-independent 3D layout representation that can be plugged into pretrained text-to-image diffusion models while preserving their generative prior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250557/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unordered set of 3D semantic bounding boxes (each with center position, size, rotation matrix, and a free-form open-vocabulary text caption), a camera extrinsic pose, and an optional floor plan point cloud as input, and produces a single photorealistic RGB rendering of the scene from that viewpoint. Unlike prior layout-based methods that use fixed category labels, object semantics here are arbitrary natural-language descriptions, and the 3D layout is defined in world coordinates independent of the rendering camera, enabling the same scene to be rendered from multiple viewpoints by simply changing the camera pose.

## Method

LACONIC introduces a lightweight adapter that conditions a frozen Stable Diffusion v1.5 backbone on 3D scene structure via decoupled cross-attention. A 3D layout encoder embeds each object's spatial attributes (position, size, rotation) through sinusoidal positional encoding and dense layers, its semantic caption through a pretrained CLIP text encoder, and an optional floor plan through PointNet; these per-object tokens are processed by a transformer encoder and projected to match the text embedding dimension. The key architectural insight is a closed-form camera viewpoint transformation that reframes all 3D object positions and rotations into the target camera's coordinate system, enabling free camera control without the network needing to learn the 3D-to-2D mapping. The resulting scene token sequence is injected into the diffusion UNet via a weighted-sum decoupled cross-attention mechanism (following IP-Adapter), where a scalar γ controls the strength of 3D layout conditioning relative to the global text prompt. Training uses supervised denoising with classifier-free guidance (randomly dropping the 3D layout) and empty global text prompts, so the model learns to rely on per-object captions for semantics while retaining the T2I backbone's out-of-distribution generalization for test-time text-driven restyling.

## Results

On HyperSim, LACONIC achieves a FID of 9.50 and KID of 3.44 without text prompting, substantially outperforming SceneCraft (FID 39.36, KID 28.26) and the from-scratch diffusion baseline DM-FS (FID 15.83, KID 7.29). With a global text caption, LACONIC reaches FID 10.12, KID 3.91, and a novel Scene Object CLIP score (SOC) of 18.39, again surpassing SceneCraft (FID 27.69, KID 15.21, SOC 17.40). The paper also introduces SOC as a new object-level alignment metric, where LACONIC scores 18.36 (no text) and 18.39 (with text) versus 17.59 and 17.40 for SceneCraft respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LACONIC is compared against SceneCraft [64], the closest prior method that also synthesizes 2D images from 3D layout controls via an adapter on a pretrained diffusion backbone, and against a from-scratch diffusion model (DM-FS) that shares LACONIC's 3D layout encoder but lacks the pretrained T2I prior. Without text prompting, LACONIC dominates SceneCraft on every metric: FID drops from 39.36 to 9.50 (≈4×), KID from 28.26 to 3.44, and IS rises from 7.72 to 9.74. With text prompting, LACONIC still leads on FID (10.12 vs 27.69), KID (3.91 vs 15.21), and SOC (18.39 vs 17.40), but SceneCraft achieves a higher Inception Score (14.55 vs 10.60), which the authors attribute to LACONIC's tighter adherence to the limited structural variety in HyperSim. CLIP Score is essentially tied (19.74 vs 19.75). Against DM-FS, LACONIC wins on all five metrics (FID 9.50 vs 15.83, KID 3.44 vs 7.29, IS 9.74 vs 8.69, SOC 18.36 vs 18.22), validating the benefit of leveraging a rich pretrained text-to-image prior over training a layout-conditioned model from scratch.

## Relation to Prior Work

LACONIC extends the layout-guided image synthesis line (SceneCraft, Build-A-Scene, ControlRoom3D) by replacing their 2D-projected conditioning (depth maps, semantic maps, panorama renders) with a direct encoding of explicit 3D semantic bounding boxes transformed into the camera coordinate frame. Architecturally, it adapts the decoupled cross-attention mechanism from IP-Adapter to a new input modality—3D geometric and per-object semantic tokens processed by a transformer encoder—rather than image embeddings. The 3D scene representation (unordered object set with position, size, rotation, and free-form caption) draws on prior 3D scene generation work (Debara, ATISS, DiffuScene, Lego-Net), but is here repurposed as a conditioning signal for a frozen text-to-image diffusion backbone rather than as a generative target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By conditioning directly on 3D geometry rather than 2D projections, LACONIC enables object-level manipulation (moving, resizing, re-captioning individual items) and multi-view consistency without requiring depth estimators, panorama data, or pixel-space editing—capabilities that prior 2D-conditioned methods cannot offer. The introduction of the Scene Object CLIP score (SOC) metric addresses a real gap in evaluating spatial-semantic alignment at the object level. Key limitations remain: the model is bounded by its training distribution (bedroom-trained models struggle with kitchens), cross-view geometric consistency is not yet enforced, and the evaluation relies primarily on a single dataset (HyperSim) with limited structural diversity. Future directions include deriving continuous 3D representations (NeRFs) from the generated views and coupling geometric consistency across camera changes.
