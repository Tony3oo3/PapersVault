## Problem

Prior work on long-tailed multi-label image classification assumed the feature extraction backbone was largely unaffected by label imbalance, with degradation confined to the classifier. This paper demonstrates through class activation map analysis that the backbone actually suffers significant attention-weight attenuation for medium and tail categories, while retaining its ability to localize category-specific regions. The gap is a mechanism to selectively restore the backbone's confidence for underrepresented categories without sacrificing head-category performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as visual input and a set of candidate label names (e.g., 20 for LT-VOC, 80 for LT-COCO) as text input, which are encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder into label embeddings. The output is a vector of per-class sigmoid probabilities (one score per category), evaluated via mAP partitioned into head (>100 samples), medium (20–100), and tail (<20) groups. The visual backbone is a pretrained ResNet-50 producing a spatial feature map, and the text encoder is not updated during training.

## Method

CSSFE operates in three stages. First, a 2-D convolutional layer over the ResNet-50 feature map produces per-class activation maps (CAMs) that localize label-relevant regions. Second, a progressive attention enhancement module computes a label co-occurrence attention matrix from CLIP label embeddings via dot-product attention, then multiplies it with a one-way binary mask that permits information flow only from head→medium→tail categories, selectively amplifying the CAMs of underrepresented classes while protecting well-learned head-class maps. Third, the optimized CAMs gate the visual feature map to yield class-specific visual vectors, which are concatenated with the corresponding semantic embeddings and fused through a Transformer block before a linear head produces classification logits trained with asymmetric loss (γ₊=0, γ₋=4). The key design choice is the directional mask: ablation shows that bidirectional semantic adjustment improves tail performance at the cost of head performance, whereas the one-way flow avoids this trade-off.

## Results

CSSFE achieves state-of-the-art mAP of 86.38% (Total) on LT-VOC and 66.93% (Total) on LT-COCO, outperforming the second-best method CPRFL by 0.10% and 0.24% respectively. The gains are most pronounced in tail categories: +0.83% on LT-VOC (87.26% vs. 86.43%) and +1.08% on LT-COCO (62.41% vs. 61.33%). CSSFE also leads in medium categories by 0.65% (LT-VOC) and 0.22% (LT-COCO), while maintaining competitive head-category performance (81.93% and 66.96%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSSFE outperforms all 15 baselines across every metric on both LT-VOC and LT-COCO. The closest competitor is CPRFL [39], which also leverages a text encoder; CSSFE edges it out by 0.10–0.83% on LT-VOC and 0.22–1.08% on LT-COCO, with the largest gap in tail categories. Traditional long-tailed single-label methods (ERM, RW, OLTR, LDAM, CB Focal, BBN, ASL) show a pronounced head-to-tail performance gap—for example, BBN's tail mAP on LT-COCO is only 44.94% versus 49.79% for head—confirming that re-sampling and re-weighting paradigms struggle with multi-label co-occurrence. Multi-label-specific methods (DB Focal, LTML, Bilateral-TFS, PG Loss, COMIC, CAE-Net) narrow this gap substantially but still trail CSSFE by 4–5% in total mAP on LT-COCO. Notably, COMIC [41] achieves strong medium/tail scores but sacrifices head performance (49.21% head on LT-COCO), whereas CSSFE maintains balanced performance across all frequency groups without the typical head-tail trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the prevailing assumption in long-tailed multi-label classification that the feature extraction backbone is largely unaffected by label imbalance, with the primary vulnerability residing in the classifier. By conducting CAM-based comparative analysis between label-balanced and long-tailed training, the authors show the backbone retains localization ability but suffers systematic attention attenuation for tail categories — a finding that reframes the problem from a classifier-overfitting issue to a feature-representation-confidence issue. Methodologically, the progressive attention enhancement module repurposes CLIP text embeddings and dot-product attention (borrowed from vision-language pretraining) to construct a one-way semantic flow from head to tail categories, combined with a binary mask that prevents reverse interference. This sits at the intersection of interpretability-driven feature analysis (CAM literature) and the re-weighting/re-sampling paradigm for long-tailed recognition, but introduces a selective, direction-aware correction mechanism not present in prior multi-label long-tailed methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conceptual reframing — that the backbone's confidence, not just the classifier, degrades under long-tailed multi-label distributions — offers a new diagnostic lens for practitioners debugging imbalanced multi-label systems. The architecture-agnostic nature of the progressive attention enhancement (validated across CNN and Transformer backbones in Table 2) suggests it could be plugged into existing pipelines without redesign. However, the practical gains over the strongest baseline (CPRFL) are modest (≤1.08% mAP), and evaluation is limited to two datasets with relatively small training sets (1,142 and 1,909 images). Open questions include whether the one-way head-to-tail mask generalizes to more extreme imbalance ratios, whether the CLIP-based semantic prior remains effective for domain-specific or novel categories, and whether the approach scales to very large label spaces where the c×c attention matrix becomes prohibitive.
