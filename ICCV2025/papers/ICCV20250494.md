## Problem

Existing few-shot action recognition methods treat action labels as monolithic textual cues, overlooking the fine-grained spatiotemporal structure of actions—specifically how subject, motion, and object interactions evolve across the initiation, progression, and conclusion phases. This coarse representation limits the model's ability to capture discriminative temporal dynamics and generalizes poorly to unseen action categories with only a handful of labeled examples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250494/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 8 uniformly sampled 224×224 video frames per clip and a textual action label as input, processed by a CLIP-initialized ViT-B/16 backbone. The LLM decomposes each label into exactly 3 atomic action descriptions (initiation, progression, conclusion), and the video is correspondingly segmented into 3 atomic phases via iterative cosine-similarity clustering with frame overlap. Output is a classification probability over N action classes, computed as a weighted geometric mean of video-video (AB-MHM distance) and video-text (atomic-description cosine similarity) matching scores.

## Method

LGA anatomizes both modalities at the atomic level: an LLM decomposes each action label into 3 sequential descriptions encoding subject, motion, and object, while a CLUSTER-Segment module iteratively merges adjacent frame clusters by cosine similarity to produce 3 temporal phases with overlap frames. A Fine-grained Multimodal Fusion module then applies multi-head cross-attention where each atomic query is the sum of its visual and textual features, attending over all concatenated visual features, followed by an FFN residual to produce fused atomic prototypes. For classification, a novel Aligned Bidirectional Mean Hausdorff Metric (AB-MHM) enforces ordered temporal alignment of atomic actions in video-video matching, while a complementary video-text branch matches pooled atomic visual features against atomic textual descriptions. The two branches are combined via a weighted geometric mean at inference, and the full pipeline is trained end-to-end with cross-entropy and contrastive losses under episodic meta-learning.

## Results

On the five FSAR benchmarks under 5-way 1-shot and 5-shot settings with a CLIP-ViT-B/16 backbone, LGA achieves 86.8%/89.3% on HMDB51, 95.2%/96.2% on Kinetics, 98.2%/99.1% on UCF101, 58.9%/69.3% on SSv2-Small, and 63.8%/74.4% on SSv2-Full (1-shot/5-shot accuracy). The largest gains over the CLIP-FSAR baseline are +9.7% on HMDB51 1-shot and +7.5% on SSv2-Small 5-shot. The method sets new state-of-the-art results on all five datasets in the 1-shot setting and matches or exceeds all prior methods in the 5-shot setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LGA outperforms the primary baseline CLIP-FSAR [42] on all five datasets in the 1-shot setting, with the most substantial margin on HMDB51 (+9.7%, 86.8% vs. 77.1%) and SSv2-Small (+4.3%, 58.9% vs. 54.6%), and a smaller but consistent gain on Kinetics (+0.4%) and UCF101 (+1.2%). In the 5-shot setting, LGA leads on HMDB51 (+1.6%), SSv2-Small (+7.5%), and SSv2-Full (+2.3%), while tying CLIP-FSAR on UCF101 (99.1%) and Kinetics (96.2% vs. 95.4%). Against the strongest recent competitors, LGA surpasses MVP-shot [29] by +9.8% on HMDB51 1-shot and +3.5% on SSv2-Small 1-shot, and exceeds EMP-Net [47] by +10.0% on HMDB51 1-shot and +1.8% on SSv2-Small 5-shot. On SSv2-Small 5-shot, LGA achieves 69.3%, a clear +3.6% improvement over the next-best CLIP-based method (EMP-Net at 65.7%). The authors attribute the larger gains on HMDB51 and Kinetics to richer action-related elements (subject, motion, object) that align well with the atomic action descriptions, while UCF101 shows limited room for improvement because the backbone's pre-training already covers those action categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-FSAR line of work by replacing the monolithic action label with LLM-generated atomic action descriptions (subject, motion, object) and correspondingly segmenting videos into temporal phases via a CLUSTER-Segment strategy. It also refines the Bi-MHM matching metric from HyRSM [40] into an Aligned Bi-MHM that respects the ordered structure of atomic sub-actions. More broadly, it sits within the metric-based meta-learning paradigm for FSAR (OTAM, TRX, STRM, MoLo) but departs from prior multimodal FSAR methods (CLIP-FSAR, MORN, SAFSAR) by performing fine-grained cross-attention fusion at the atomic level rather than global label-level alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that injecting LLM world knowledge into the textual side of FSAR—without any additional labeled video data—can yield substantial gains, particularly in the most data-scarce 1-shot regime (~10 points on HMDB51). A notable limitation is the dependence on LLM quality: the ablation showing that 4 atomic actions degrades performance due to hallucination risk and temporal overlap suggests the approach may not scale to more complex or longer actions. Open questions include whether the fixed 3-phase decomposition (initiation, progression, conclusion) generalizes to actions with irregular temporal structure, and whether the CLUSTER-Segment heuristic can be replaced by learned segmentation without sacrificing the non-parametric efficiency that makes AB-MHM attractive.
