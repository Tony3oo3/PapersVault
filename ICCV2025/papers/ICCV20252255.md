## Problem

Federated learning suffers from performance degradation under data heterogeneity, partial client participation, and limited communication bandwidth. Existing quantized FL methods experience severe accuracy loss at ultra-low bit-widths (1–2 bits), and prior approaches that use weight standardization (e.g., FedWon) transmit normalized parameters that discard client-specific information. No prior method jointly addresses both the gradient-divergence problem caused by non-i.i.d. data and the communication bottleneck through a distribution-aware quantization strategy.

## Input / Output

Each client receives the global model parameters (ResNet-18 with group normalization) and a per-layer global scaling vector, then trains on local RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet). The client transmits B-bit quantized local model parameter updates (LMPUs) together with its own per-layer standard deviation vector; no additional quantization parameters are sent. The server dequantizes, aggregates, and outputs updated global parameters, while the final model produces class labels (10, 100, or 200 classes) at inference.

## Method

FedWSQ integrates two components: (1) Weight Standardization (WS), which standardizes each conv/linear weight vector before use, implicitly projecting local gradients onto the orthogonal complement of both the weight direction and the all-ones vector—thereby filtering out biased, overfitting-prone gradient components that cause client drift; and (2) Distribution-Aware Non-Uniform Quantization (DANUQ), which maps normalized LMPUs to pre-computed quantization levels derived by minimizing expected squared error under a standard normal prior, using per-layer standard deviation (rather than absmax) as the scaling factor for outlier robustness. A global scaling vector is maintained server-side via momentum averaging across participating clients, ensuring consistent quantization without transmitting extra parameters. Unlike FedWon, FedWSQ transmits pre-standardized parameters (PSPs) rather than weight-standardized ones, preserving local information while still benefiting from WS's gradient filtering. An adaptive mixed-precision strategy (FBA/DBA) further allows each client to select from {1, 2, 4} bits per round, achieving ~2.3 bits/parameter on average with minimal accuracy loss.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 under extreme heterogeneity (α=0.1), FedWSQ with 1-bit quantization achieves 62.05% accuracy, surpassing the best 32-bit baselines FedRCL (58.26%) and FedACG (58.14%) by 3.79% and 3.91% respectively. On Tiny-ImageNet (α=0.1), FedWSQ (1-bit) reaches 45.11%, outperforming FedRCL (37.86%) and FedACG (39.75%) by 7.25% and 5.36%. Even at 4-bit, FedWSQ (86.84% on CIFAR-10, α=0.1) dramatically exceeds quantized baselines FedPAQ (66.81%) and FedHQ+ (66.89%) by over 20 percentage points. With the mixed-precision DBA strategy (~2.3 bits average), FedWSQ maintains near-4-bit accuracy while halving communication cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedWSQ (1-bit) outperforms all 32-bit SOTA baselines on CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet under α=0.1: it beats FedRCL by 3.79% (62.05 vs 58.26) and FedACG by 3.91% (62.05 vs 58.14) on CIFAR-100, and by 7.25% and 5.36% respectively on Tiny-ImageNet. Against quantized baselines, the gap is even more dramatic: FedWSQ (4-bit) achieves 86.84% on CIFAR-10 (α=0.1) versus 66.81% for FedPAQ (4-bit) and 66.89% for FedHQ+ (4-bit), a margin exceeding 20 points. Even FedWSQ at 1-bit (84.79% on CIFAR-10, α=0.1) surpasses the 32-bit FedACG (83.62%) and FedRCL (83.45%). FedWSQ also consistently outperforms FedWon [56] across all datasets and heterogeneity levels, with a smoother loss landscape (Hessian top eigenvalue 135.8 vs 203.4). In the NUQ comparison (Table 3), DANUQ at 1-bit achieves 84.8% on CIFAR-10 (α=0.1) while NF/FP collapses to 24.0%, and at 2-bit DANUQ (85.9%) far exceeds NF (57.8%) and FP (45.0%). The mixed-precision DBA strategy (~2.3 bits) retains within 1% of the 4-bit accuracy while halving communication cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedWSQ repurposes weight standardization (WS) from Qiao et al. [35]—originally a plug-and-play training stabilizer—into a gradient filtering mechanism for federated learning, showing that the projection operations in WS implicitly remove biased gradient components that cause client drift under non-i.i.d. data. For communication compression, the paper introduces DANUQ, a distribution-aware non-uniform quantization scheme that derives optimal quantization levels from a standard normal distribution prior over local model parameter updates, departing from prior NUQ methods (NF, FP) that use different distributional assumptions or learned parameters. The framework is built on FedAvg [31] and explicitly contrasts with FedWon [56], which also uses WS but transmits weight-standardized parameters rather than pre-standardized ones, fundamentally altering what information is preserved across clients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for bandwidth-constrained FL deployments (mobile, IoT), where uplink communication is the dominant bottleneck: FedWSQ achieves near-4-bit accuracy at an average of 2.3 bits via mixed-precision strategies, and even at 1-bit it surpasses full-precision SOTA methods on CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet. A key open question is whether the normal-distribution assumption on LMPUs holds for all architectures and training regimes, since the optimal QLs are precomputed via brute-force search for specific bit-widths and may degrade if the distributional assumption is violated. The approach is also limited to image classification benchmarks with moderate-size CNNs; generalization to larger models, other modalities, or asynchronous FL settings remains unexplored.
