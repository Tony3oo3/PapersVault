## Problem

Existing open-set Chinese text recognition methods treat the task as a single-task problem, relying exclusively on either linguistic decomposition or glyph matching, without integrating both perspectives as humans do. Linguistic methods treat all components as equally important, ignoring the critical distinction between primary and secondary structures, while glyph-based methods remain sensitive to style variations across fonts and writing styles.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251247/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB Chinese text images as input, resized to a height of 128 pixels (preserving aspect ratio) for text-line recognition or 96×96 for isolated character recognition. It outputs a recognized character sequence via a CTC or attention sequence decoder. In open-set settings, the model identifies characters not present in the training vocabulary by matching predicted representations against a pre-built lexicon covering the full GB18030-2000 standard, with non-Chinese characters handled as basic characters with special radical codes.

## Method

MSA² is a multi-task framework that jointly predicts linguistic and glyph representations through two lightweight decoders, fuses them via element-wise summation of similarity scores, and decodes the final sequence. SACE organizes each character's linguistic components in a full binary tree (max depth 7) with a dynamic representation space: structures at parent nodes receive multi-hot codes whose length halves per depth (16-bit at root down to 1-bit), while radicals at leaf nodes use fixed 60-bit codes, ensuring primary structures dominate similarity matching. SAGE builds a robust glyph lexicon via a two-stage glyph-centric contrastive learning pipeline: first, a frozen CLIP encoder extracts embeddings from T diverse glyph styles per character to initialize pseudo-representations; second, the encoder is fine-tuned with a contrastive loss that pulls same-character embeddings toward their pseudo-representation while pushing different-character embeddings apart. The total training loss sums the similarity-based recognition losses on both the linguistic and glyph branches, trained with Adadelta at learning rate 0.1.

## Results

On the BCTR benchmark (closed-set), MSA² achieves 77.1% average line-level accuracy, surpassing the previous SOTA CCR-CLIP (75.8%) by 1.3% and SVTR-L (75.5%) by 1.6%. Under open-set text recognition with limited training data, MSA² yields average accuracy improvements of 4.6%, 8.9%, 6.6%, and 3.7% over HierCode on Scene, Web, Doc, and Handwriting subsets, respectively. For open-set character recognition on ICDAR-char, MSA² reaches 60.17% accuracy at m=2755 training classes (vs. 56.21% for HierCode) and 98.85% in the full-vocabulary setting (vs. 97.68%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSA² outperforms all 17 compared baselines across every BCTR subset in closed-set recognition, with the largest gains over CCR-CLIP (+1.3% avg) and SVTR-L (+1.6% avg). Against the closest open-set baseline HierCode under identical model configurations, MSA² improves by 1.2% average in closed-set and by 4.6–8.9% in open-set text recognition, with the Web subset showing the largest open-set gain (+8.9%). In open-set character recognition (Table 3), MSA² surpasses HierCode by 2.02–5.96% on handwritten characters (ICDAR-char) and by 0.38–0.63% on scene characters (CTW) across all training-set sizes. Glyph-only baselines (SideNet, CUE) and linguistic-only baselines (HDE, DenseRAN) are all outperformed, confirming the benefit of the multi-task fusion. For non-Chinese characters (Latin, digits, symbols), MSA² shows particularly large recall improvements over HierCode (e.g., +2.17% on ICDAR-line), attributed to the glyph branch effectively representing characters that lack rich Chinese linguistic structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on HierCode [48], which introduced a lightweight hierarchical codebook for zero-shot Chinese text recognition using linguistic components, and adopts its similarity-based recognition loss. However, it departs from the single-task paradigm that dominates prior open-set CTR work—where methods are either purely linguistic (strokes, radicals, IDS, hierarchical decomposition) or purely glyph-based (prototype learning, deep matching)—by unifying both perspectives into a multi-task framework. SACE extends the linguistic encoding line by organizing components in a binary tree with a dynamic representation space that allocates more bits to primary structures, while SAGE extends glyph-based methods by introducing a glyph-centric contrastive learning pipeline initialized from CLIP to produce style-robust glyph embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6% average improvement in open-set accuracy on BCTR is practically significant for real-world Chinese text recognition, where new characters continuously emerge and retraining is infeasible. The finding that linguistic knowledge dominates for compound characters while glyphs are more beneficial for basic characters (numbers, Latin letters) provides useful guidance for hybrid system design. Open questions remain: the method relies on available linguistic decompositions (limiting applicability to ancient or non-standard scripts), does not model interactions among same-type structures at different importance levels, and the CLIP-based glyph encoder introduces a dependency on a large pretrained model that may not transfer well to highly stylized or artistic fonts.
