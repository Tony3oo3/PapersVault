## Problem

Existing multimodal knowledge editing methods treat the entire image as a single target entity and rely primarily on textual semantics, making them unable to perform fine-grained edits that target specific entities within complex images containing multiple interacting objects. This paper identifies that when visual information is central to knowledge representation—such as spatial relationships between entities in the same image—text-only editing approaches fail to localize and update the correct knowledge while preserving unrelated content.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an image containing multiple interacting entities paired with a text prompt, along with a stored editing sample triple (image, prompt, expected output). The multimodal scope classifier produces a similarity score in [0,1] that routes the query: scores below 0.5 send the input to the frozen base model for an unmodified response, while scores at or above 0.5 route to the counterfactual model, which generates a corrected text response conditioned on the editing sample's textual components. The test image itself is passed to the counterfactual model to provide full visual context, while the editing sample's image is omitted since the test image already supplies the necessary visual grounding.

## Method

MSCKE extends the SERAC parameter-preserving editing framework by replacing its text-only scope classifier with a multimodal scope classifier that jointly encodes images and text via a fine-tuned CLIP model, projecting both modalities into a shared feature space and fusing them with a parameter-free dot-product attention mechanism. The classifier is trained as a binary classifier with binary cross-entropy loss on paired in-scope and out-of-scope inputs, learning to distinguish fine-grained entity relationships that textual semantics alone cannot capture. At inference, the framework routes each query to either a frozen base model (out-of-scope) or a counterfactual model (in-scope) that is conditioned on the relevant editing sample's text and expected output. The design is modular: the scope classifier is decoupled from the counterfactual model, enabling transfer across different base MLLMs (BLIP-2 OPT, MiniGPT-4) and editing backends (SERAC-style memory, MEND hypernetwork) without retraining.

## Results

On the FGVEdit benchmark, MSCKE achieves a Specificity of 61.60% on BLIP-2 OPT and 57.20% on MiniGPT-4, substantially outperforming the text-only SERAC baseline (31.92% and 37.85% respectively). The enhanced MSCKE-MEND variant further improves Specificity to 68.38% (BLIP-2 OPT) and 71.98% (MiniGPT-4), surpassing MEND alone (65.85% and 67.39%). On common editing metrics, MSCKE achieves 100% Locality on both base models and 99.13% Reliability on BLIP-2 OPT, while maintaining strong Generality (98.56% on BLIP-2 OPT, 93.00% on MiniGPT-4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSCKE dramatically outperforms SERAC on the novel Specificity metric, improving from 31.92% to 61.60% on BLIP-2 OPT (+29.68) and from 37.85% to 57.20% on MiniGPT-4 (+19.35), demonstrating that the multimodal scope classifier is essential for fine-grained visual editing. MSCKE-MEND further improves over MEND by 2.53 points (BLIP-2 OPT) and 4.59 points (MiniGPT-4) on Specificity, showing the classifier's benefit even when paired with a parameter-modifying editor. On common metrics, MSCKE achieves 100% Locality on both models, matching FT-Visual and far exceeding IKE (48.49% on BLIP-2 OPT, 52.45% on MiniGPT-4), while also improving Reliability over SERAC (99.13% vs. 93.08% on BLIP-2 OPT). Notably, MEND alone already achieves competitive Specificity (65.85%/67.39%) because it updates both visual and language modules, but MSCKE-MEND still edges it out, confirming the added value of the multimodal scope classifier. On the secondary MMEdit benchmark, MSCKE achieves 98.08% M-Locality versus SERAC's 2.91%, further validating the classifier's advantage in multimodal settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the memory-based editing paradigm of SERAC (Mitchell et al., ICML 2022) from text-only LLMs to multimodal settings by replacing the text-only scope classifier with a CLIP-based multimodal scope classifier that fuses visual and textual features. It departs from existing multimodal editing benchmarks (MMEdit, KEBench, MIKE, MC-MKE), which treat the entire image as a single coarse-grained target entity, by formulating a fine-grained task where only specific entities within multi-entity images should be modified. The counterfactual model component is modular and can be swapped with parameter-modifying methods like MEND, positioning MSCKE as a wrapper framework rather than a monolithic editing algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FGVEdit benchmark and its specificity metric fill a genuine gap: prior multimodal editing evaluations cannot distinguish whether a method correctly localizes edits to the right entity within a complex image or simply overwrites all image-related knowledge. The finding that text-only editing methods (SERAC, IKE) drop to ~20–37% specificity while the multimodal classifier recovers to ~57–61% underscores that visual grounding is non-negotiable for fine-grained editing. However, the evaluation is limited to two MLLM backbones (BLIP-2 OPT, MiniGPT-4), the benchmark construction relies on GPT-4o-mini for scope classification (introducing potential annotation bias), and MEND's competitive baseline performance (65–67% specificity) suggests that parameter-modifying approaches may already partially address the challenge, leaving open whether the scope-classifier framing is the most scalable path forward.
