## Problem

When hybrid representations (combining implicit anchors with explicit Gaussians) are applied to large-scale scene reconstruction via divide-and-conquer block training, two critical issues arise: reconstruction accuracy degrades because each block's Gaussian decoder sees limited data diversity, and parallel training constrains the number of blocks to the available GPU count. Furthermore, independently trained blocks produce visible inconsistencies—abrupt lighting shifts and structural discontinuities—at block boundaries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250984/cropped_page0_idx10.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a COLMAP sparse point cloud (each voxel initialized from a corresponding 3D point) and RGB training/test images downsampled by 4× (MatrixCity resized to 1600 px width). The scene is partitioned into 8 blocks (vs. 32 in CityGaussian), and the output is a hybrid representation of 32-dimensional anchor features decoded on-the-fly by a two-layer MLP into per-view Gaussians (color, opacity, rotation, scale) that are rendered via tile-based alpha blending, achieving 59.91 FPS with only 4.62 GB memory on the 2.7 km² MatrixCity scene.

## Method

Momentum-GS introduces Scene Momentum Self-Distillation: a teacher Gaussian decoder D_t is updated via exponential moving average (momentum m=0.9) of the shared student decoder D_s, providing a temporally stable global reference that guides all blocks through a consistency loss (MSE between teacher and student predictions on anchor features and viewing directions). This decouples the number of blocks from GPU count by periodically sampling k blocks from n total blocks and distributing them across k GPUs in a sequential-parallel schedule. A second contribution, Reconstruction-guided Block Weighting, maintains momentum-smoothed PSNR and SSIM per block and assigns each block a Gaussian-shaped weight w_i = 2 − exp((δ_p² + λδ_s²)/(−2σ²)) that amplifies the loss contribution of underperforming blocks, preventing the shared decoder from converging on locally easy regions. The total loss combines L1 + λ_SSIM·SSIM reconstruction terms with λ_consistency·consistency, all modulated by the per-block weights.

## Results

Momentum-GS achieves the best PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS on 4 out of 5 scenes in Table 1 (Building, Rubble, Campus, Residence) and on all three metrics for the extremely large MatrixCity scene (PSNR 29.11, SSIM 0.881, LPIPS 0.180). The headline result is an 18.7% LPIPS improvement over CityGaussian while using only 8 blocks versus CityGaussian's 32. On Sci-Art, Switch-NeRF achieves a higher PSNR (26.52 vs. 25.06) due to inherent blurriness in the source images, but Momentum-GS still leads on SSIM (0.860 vs. 0.795) and LPIPS (0.204 vs. 0.360).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Momentum-GS outperforms all six baselines on SSIM and LPIPS across every scene, and on PSNR for 4 of 5 scenes (losing only to Switch-NeRF on Sci-Art, where blurred source images favor NeRF's smoother outputs). Against the strongest Gaussian-based competitor CityGaussian, Momentum-GS improves LPIPS by 18.7% on average while using only 8 blocks versus CityGaussian's 32, and gains 0.95 dB PSNR on MatrixCity (29.11 vs. 28.61). DOGS is the closest competitor on LPIPS for Building (0.204 vs. 0.194) but Momentum-GS still leads by a clear margin on all other scenes. VastGaussian and 3D-GS are consistently outperformed by 1–2 dB in PSNR and 0.05–0.10 in SSIM. The NeRF-based baselines (Mega-NeRF, Switch-NeRF) trail significantly on all Gaussian-favorable metrics, with LPIPS gaps of 0.15–0.30, confirming the rendering-quality advantage of explicit Gaussian representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the hybrid representation paradigm of Scaffold-GS (anchor features decoded by an MLP into view-adaptive Gaussians) and the divide-and-conquer strategy popularized by CityGaussian and VastGaussian for large-scale 3DGS reconstruction. It departs from prior block-based methods by introducing a momentum-updated teacher Gaussian decoder (an EMA-style self-distillation mechanism borrowed from self-supervised learning) that provides stable global guidance across staggered training blocks, thereby decoupling the number of scene blocks from the physical GPU count. The reconstruction-guided block weighting further differentiates the approach by dynamically re-weighting blocks based on smoothed PSNR/SSIM, a mechanism not present in CityGaussian, VastGaussian, or DOGS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical decoupling of block count from GPU availability addresses a real scalability bottleneck in 3DGS-based large scene reconstruction, enabling practitioners to use more blocks (and thus finer spatial partitioning) without proportional hardware. The 18.7% LPIPS improvement over CityGaussian with 8 blocks versus 32 is a meaningful efficiency-quality tradeoff. Open questions include whether the momentum self-distillation strategy extends to dynamic or multi-temporal scenes, how sensitive the method is to the momentum coefficient in practice, and whether the approach generalizes beyond aerial/drone-captured scenes to indoor or street-level captures with different lighting and geometry characteristics.
