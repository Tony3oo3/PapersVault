## Problem

Diffusion-based 3D LiDAR scene completion models achieve high completion quality but require 50+ denoising steps, taking over 30 seconds per frame—far too slow for real-time autonomous driving perception. Existing diffusion acceleration techniques have not been adapted to LiDAR scene completion because the complex geometric structure of 3D point clouds is not explicitly captured by standard distillation objectives, leading to loss of local details.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse 3D LiDAR scan of N points in R³ and produces a dense completed scene of M points (M = K·N, where K is a duplication factor). Following LiDiff's local noise offset formulation, noise is added per-point rather than globally, and the initial noisy scene is constructed by duplicating sparse points K times before applying the noise schedule. The distilled student model can sample in as few as 1–8 steps (1.1–5.4 s) versus 50 steps (~30 s) for the teacher, with an optional refinement network for local detail recovery.

## Method

ScoreLiDAR distills a pre-trained LiDiff teacher into a student model using a bidirectional gradient guidance mechanism: the student generates a completed scene, then both the teacher (ε_θ) and an auxiliary diffusion model (ε_φ, trained on student-generated samples) predict noise scores at random timesteps, and the student is updated along the difference of these two score gradients to minimize KL divergence between the student's and teacher's distributions. To preserve geometric structure lost by distribution-level matching alone, a Structural Loss is added, comprising a scene-wise term (mean squared distance from generated points to ground truth) and a point-wise term (L2 distance between n×n inter-point distance matrices of curvature-selected keypoints in the completed scene vs. ground truth). The auxiliary model and student are initialized from the teacher and optimized alternately, with the total objective combining the KL divergence and structural loss (λ_scene=0.5, λ_point=0.01).

## Results

On SemanticKITTI, ScoreLiDAR (Refined) achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.342 and JSD of 0.399, outperforming the teacher model LiDiff (Refined) (CD 0.375, JSD 0.416) while completing each scene in 5.37 s versus 30.55 s (~5.7× speedup). On KITTI-360, ScoreLiDAR (Refined) reaches CD 0.452 and JSD 0.437, again beating LiDiff (Refined) (CD 0.517, JSD 0.446) at 5.14 s per frame. EMD values are comparable across methods (≈23.0–23.3), with a slight increase after refinement due to point-count mismatch. The method also supports 4-step (3.23 s) and even 1-step (1.10 s) sampling while retaining competitive quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ScoreLiDAR (Refined) outperforms the primary SOTA baseline LiDiff (Refined) on both datasets: on SemanticKITTI it improves CD by ~8.8% (0.342 vs 0.375) and JSD by ~4.1% (0.399 vs 0.416) while being ~5.7× faster (5.37 s vs 30.55 s); on KITTI-360 the CD gain is larger at ~12.6% (0.452 vs 0.517) with a ~2% JSD improvement. Against the non-diffusion baselines (LMSCNet, LODE, MID), ScoreLiDAR achieves substantially better CD (0.342 vs 0.503–1.029) at the cost of higher inference time (5.37 s vs 0.40–6.42 s), though it still beats MID's 6.42 s. PVD is the slowest baseline by a wide margin (262.54 s) and also has the worst CD (1.256). EMD is the one metric where ScoreLiDAR (Refined) is marginally worse than LiDiff (Refined) (23.26 vs 23.16 on SemanticKITTI), which the authors attribute to point-count mismatch after the refinement stage altering the global transport cost. Overall, ScoreLiDAR achieves the best CD and JSD among all compared methods on both datasets while delivering a >5× speedup over the teacher model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the bidirectional gradient guidance mechanism from variational score distillation (ProlificDreamer [35]) to the 3D LiDAR scene completion setting, using LiDiff [23] as the teacher diffusion model. While prior diffusion acceleration methods (progressive distillation, consistency models, DPM solvers) target image or general 3D generation, none address the unique geometric structure of sparse LiDAR point clouds. The key departure is the introduction of a Structural Loss—combining a scene-wise Chamfer-type term and a curvature-based point-wise distance-matrix term—that explicitly constrains the student model to preserve local geometric configuration, something generic score-matching distillation does not capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5× speedup (30.55 s → 5.37 s per frame) makes diffusion-based LiDAR completion viable for real-time autonomous driving perception, a practical bottleneck the authors emphasize throughout. The finding that the distilled student outperforms the teacher in CD and JSD (thanks to the structural loss) suggests that targeted geometric regularization can extract more structure from the data than the teacher's implicit multi-step denoising alone. However, performance remains bounded by the teacher model's quality, and the paper acknowledges that semantic scene completion and more effective training to surpass the teacher are open directions. The method's reliance on a pre-trained teacher and an auxiliary diffusion model (ε_φ) adds training complexity that may limit adoption in resource-constrained settings.
