## Problem

Planar disk primitives (e.g., 2DGS) are first-order surface approximations that require dense sampling to capture high-curvature geometry, and their Euclidean-distance-based density distributions become misaligned with surface geometry when primitives deform. This paper addresses both limitations by introducing second-order quadric surfaces with a geometry-aware density metric that adapts intrinsically to curvature.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250923/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes posed multi-view RGB images and a sparse SfM point cloud, optimizing quadric surfel parameters end-to-end. Outputs include a 3D mesh extracted via TSDF fusion (voxel size 0.004, truncation 0.02) from median-depth maps, along with per-pixel depth, normal, and curvature maps. The curvature map is a unique byproduct of the second-order representation, used both for visualization and as a weighting signal in the training loss. All geometric evaluations are performed at half image resolution.

## Method

QGS replaces planar disks with paraboloid quadrics parameterized by a 4×4 conic matrix, where a signed scale on the z-axis enables differentiable transitions between elliptic, hyperbolic, and planar paraboloids (2DGS is the degenerate case as s₃→0). The key insight is defining the Gaussian density using closed-form geodesic arc length along the paraboloid rather than Euclidean distance, ensuring density weights remain consistent under primitive deformation. Splatting uses ray-quadric intersection with a 3σ validity criterion to select the correct intersection point for multi-view consistency. Training combines photometric loss with depth distortion, a curvature-guided normal consistency loss (where the analytically computed Gaussian curvature modulates the normal supervision to avoid over-smoothing at geometric edges), and multi-view reprojection/NCC regularization. Per-pixel resorting from StopThePop addresses depth pop-out artifacts.

## Results

On the DTU dataset, QGS achieves a mean Chamfer Distance of 0.54 mm, reducing geometric error by 33% over 2DGS (0.80 mm) and 27% over GOF (0.74 mm), while also outperforming PGSR (0.56 mm). On Tanks and Temples, QGS attains a mean F1-score of 0.50, surpassing 2DGS (0.33), GOF (0.46), and PGSR (0.47). For appearance on Mip-NeRF 360, QGS achieves competitive indoor PSNR of 30.45 dB (vs. 2DGS 30.39, GOF 30.80) but slightly trails GOF in outdoor scenes (24.32 vs. 24.76 dB). Training time is 48 min on DTU and 75 min on TNT, comparable to other GS-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QGS outperforms all explicit GS-based baselines on DTU Chamfer Distance: it beats 2DGS by 0.26 mm (33% reduction), GOF by 0.20 mm (27%), and PGSR by 0.02 mm, while also surpassing implicit methods like Neuralangelo (0.61 mm) and NeuS (0.84 mm) by a wide margin. On TNT F1-score, QGS (0.50) leads all methods including PGSR (0.47) and GOF (0.46), with the gap over 2DGS (0.33) being particularly large. Even without multi-view regularization, QGS w/o MV (0.71 mm CD on DTU, 0.45 F1 on TNT) already surpasses 2DGS and GOF, isolating the contribution of quadric primitives. On Mip-NeRF 360 appearance, QGS is competitive with 2DGS and PGSR in indoor scenes (PSNR 30.45 vs. 30.39/30.35) but trails GOF (30.80) in both indoor and outdoor settings, suggesting the higher geometric fidelity comes at a slight appearance cost in texture-sparse regions. The ablation confirms that fixing the quadric to a disk (s₃ fixed) drops TNT F1 from 0.45 to 0.37, and using Euclidean instead of geodesic distance drops it to 0.38, validating both design choices.

## Relation to Prior Work

QGS sits at the intersection of two lines of work: the Gaussian splatting family (3DGS → 2DGS) and the broader push toward surface-aligned primitives for geometric reconstruction. It directly generalizes 2DGS's planar disk to a quadric (paraboloid) surface, inheriting 2DGS's ray-splat intersection and multi-view consistency properties while adding second-order curvature expressiveness. The key conceptual departure from prior surfel-based methods is the use of geodesic distance (rather than Euclidean distance) for the Gaussian density kernel, which is made practical by restricting to paraboloids where closed-form geodesic solvers exist. Per-pixel resorting from StopThePop is adopted to handle depth pop-out artifacts introduced by the non-planar primitives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 33% Chamfer Distance reduction over 2DGS on DTU demonstrates that second-order geometric primitives can capture curvature that planar surfels fundamentally cannot, potentially reducing the number of primitives needed for high-fidelity reconstruction. The geodesic-distance formulation addresses a subtle but important mismatch: Euclidean-based density kernels distort under surface deformation, a problem that becomes visible as uneven splatting artifacts. However, the method is constrained to paraboloids (not general quadrics) to maintain closed-form geodesics, and outdoor scene rendering degrades due to overfitting in sparse-view regions—suggesting that curvature-aware regularization remains an open problem. Whether this paraboloid-restricted framework extends to dynamic scenes or large-scale reconstruction is unexplored.
