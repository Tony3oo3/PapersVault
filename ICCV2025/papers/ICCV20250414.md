## Problem

VideoLLMs face two intertwined challenges: a scarcity of high-quality, diverse synthetic video-text training data (existing video datasets cover narrow task ranges like activity recognition), and a rigid fixed-ratio token compression that either wastes tokens on short videos or degrades quality on long ones. The paper asks how to balance the context window against per-frame detail so that a single model handles videos of vastly different lengths without quality loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250414/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each video is sampled at 1 FPS (up to 256 frames), with every frame independently encoded by CLIP-ViT Large@336p and then compressed to a variable token count of min(N_max/T, 576), where N_max is 12K (7B) or 10K (14B) and T is the frame count. Frames are interleaved with explicit timestamp text ("1s: <image>; 2s: <image>; …") before being fed to the LLM alongside the question. Outputs are free-form text for open-ended QA or a single letter for multiple-choice QA, with a formatting prompt ("Answer with only one letter") used during training to enforce the latter.

## Method

Dynamic-VLM pairs a CLIP-ViT Large@336p encoder with a Qwen 2.5 LLM through a dynamic visual token compressor whose output size adapts to video length. Three compressor candidates are explored—AdaptiveAvgPool2d (H×W grid, H=W∈[4,28]), ToMe-style k-th Bipartite Soft Matching, and Gumbel-Softmax + KeepTopK pruning—and ablation shows simple adaptive pooling performs best, so it is adopted as default. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) pretraining on LLaVA-558K with only the compressor unfrozen, (2) end-to-end fine-tuning on mixed captioning data (ALLaVA, ShareGPT4V, LLaVA-RecapCC3M) with tokens-per-frame randomly sampled from [16, 576], and (3) video instruction tuning on 2M synthetic QA pairs generated by GPT-4V/GPT-4o from WebVid-10M, InternVid-10M, and HDVILA-100M, covering perception, temporal, reasoning, general, and formatting tasks. The key design insight is that short videos retain near-full token budgets per frame for fine detail, while long videos automatically receive higher compression to fit more frames within a fixed 16K context window, achieving an optimal balance around 64–100 tokens per frame.

## Results

On multi-choice VideoQA, Dynamic-VLM-14B achieves 64.6%/68.8% on VideoMME (w.o./w. subtitles), 70.1% on MLVU, 66.2% on TempCompass, 75.2% on EgoSchema, and 72.1% on PerceptionTest, setting new SOTA among open-source models up to 14B parameters. The 7B variant reaches 60.9%/64.0% on VideoMME and 68.6% on EgoSchema, surpassing LLaVA-OneVision-7B by 2.7% (VideoMME w.o. subtitles) and 8.5% (EgoSchema). On the zero-shot multi-image benchmark MuirBench, Dynamic-VLM-14B scores 52.5%, exceeding LLaVA-OneVision-7B (41.8%) by 10.7 points despite never being trained on multi-image data. On open-ended VideoQA, Dynamic-VLM-7B leads on MSVD-QA (76.0 acc.), MSRVTT-QA (64.5 acc.), TGIF-QA (76.6 acc.), and ActivityNet-QA (63.9 acc.).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dynamic-VLM-7B outperforms LLaVA-OneVision-7B on all five multi-choice VideoQA benchmarks, with the largest gains on EgoSchema (+8.5 pp, 68.6% vs. 60.1%) and PerceptionTest (+11.7 pp, 68.8% vs. 57.1%). The 14B variant surpasses the much larger LLaVA-OneVision-72B on MLVU (70.1% vs. 68.0%), EgoSchema (75.2% vs. 62.0%), and PerceptionTest (72.1% vs. 66.9%), while remaining slightly below on VideoMME (64.6% vs. 66.2% w.o. subtitles). Dynamic-VLM-14B matches GPT-4o mini on VideoMME (64.6% vs. 64.8% w.o. subtitles) but still trails GPT-4o (71.9%) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (75.0%). On the zero-shot multi-image MuirBench, Dynamic-VLM-7B (50.7%) exceeds LLaVA-OneVision-7B (41.8%) by 8.9 pp and LLaVA-v1.5-7B (23.5%) by 27.2 pp, despite no multi-image training. On open-ended VideoQA, Dynamic-VLM-7B leads all 7B baselines on MSVD-QA (76.0 vs. 72.4 for VideoGPT+), MSRVTT-QA (64.5 vs. 60.6), TGIF-QA (76.6 vs. 74.6), and ActivityNet-QA (63.9 vs. 58.3 for FreeVA), confirming consistent gains across both evaluation paradigms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the token-compression lineage from ToMe (Bolya et al., 2022) and Elysium (Wang et al., ECCV 2025) by making the compression ratio dynamic per video length rather than fixed, and it follows the synthetic-data paradigm established by LLaVA, ShareGPT4V, and ALLaVA but scales it to video with GPT-4V/GPT-4o-generated QA pairs. It departs from memory-based VideoLLMs (MovieChat, SlowFast-LLaVA) that sacrifice frame-level detail, and from fixed-ratio long-context approaches (LongLLaVA, LongVA) that degrade quality at high token counts, by instead adapting tokens-per-frame inversely with frame count. The architecture itself is deliberately minimal—CLIP-ViT + Qwen 2.5 + a pluggable compressor—positioning the contribution in the compression strategy and data pipeline rather than in the backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2M-sample synthetic video dataset (sourced from WebVid-10M, InternVid-10M, and HDVILA-100M) fills a genuine gap in high-quality, diverse video instruction-tuning data and is likely to be widely adopted. The finding that a trivial AdaptiveAvgPool2d outperforms both ToMe-style merging and Gumbel-Softmax pruning as a dynamic compressor is a useful negative result that simplifies the design space for future VideoLLMs. Open questions include whether the dynamic compression strategy generalizes to even longer (hour-scale) videos without further architectural changes, and whether the zero-shot multi-image transfer advantage persists as models scale beyond 14B parameters.
