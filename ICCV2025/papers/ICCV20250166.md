## Problem

Existing storytelling generation methods, whether training-free or training-based, lack fine-grained guidance and inter-frame interaction, making it difficult to maintain subject consistency across frames. Moreover, the absence of large-scale datasets with detailed layout annotations (subject position, appearance, clothing, expression, posture) prevents precise control over the generated subject, hindering further progress in the field.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250166/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a reference image with its bounding box, a global caption (identity prompt + frame prompt), and optionally togglable layout conditions consisting of per-frame subject captions and bounding boxes. It produces a sequence of 4–6 consistent story images at 720×1280 resolution. The layout conditions are optional at inference time: without them the model falls back to identity and frame prompts only, while with them it gains pixel-level control over subject placement and appearance details.

## Method

Lay2Story extends PixArt-α (a DiT-based T2I model) with a two-branch architecture: a global branch (self-attention + cross-attention guided by global T5 embeddings) handles overall image quality, while a subject branch (masked self-attention, masked cross-attention, and masked 3D self-attention) enforces subject consistency and spatial control. The subject branch receives a 9-channel input formed by concatenating the noise latent, the VAE-encoded reference image, and the reference mask, then reduces it to 4 channels via a convolution. Masked attention operations constrain computation to subject bounding-box regions, and the 3D self-attention reshapes tokens across frames to propagate identity information temporally. ControlNet-style zero-initialized linear skip connections inject subject-branch features back into the global branch every two blocks. During training, 25% of spatial masks are set to fully valid and 25% of subject captions are replaced with global captions to ensure robustness when layout annotations are absent at inference.

## Results

On Lay2Story-Bench, Lay2Story with layout conditions achieves the best scores across all five metrics: DreamSim 0.1324 (lower is better), CLIP-I 0.9299, FID 26.71, Recall@1 0.7012, and Human Preference 0.7561. Even without layout conditions, Lay2Story remains competitive, ranking second in CLIP-I (0.9214) and third in DreamSim, FID, and Recall@1. Inference time is 14.02 s per 4-frame story at 720×1280 resolution, substantially faster than FLUX.1-dev IP-Adapter (61.38 s) and StoryDiffusion (24.36 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Lay2Story with layout conditions outperforms all six baselines on every metric. The closest competitor is FLUX.1-dev IP-Adapter (DiT-based), which Lay2Story surpasses by 2.0 points on DreamSim (0.1324 vs 0.1533), 1.6 points on CLIP-I (0.9299 vs 0.9138), 6.5 points on FID (26.71 vs 33.18), 5.3 points on Recall@1 (0.7012 vs 0.6482), and 5.0 points on Human Preference (0.7561 vs 0.7059), while running 4.4× faster (14.02 s vs 61.38 s). Among U-Net-based methods, 1Prompt1Story is the strongest baseline (DreamSim 0.2429, CLIP-I 0.8461), but Lay2Story still leads by 11.0 points on DreamSim and 8.4 points on CLIP-I. The gap widens dramatically on FID, where Lay2Story (26.71) nearly halves the FID of the best U-Net method (1Prompt1Story at 66.79). Even without layout conditions, Lay2Story matches or exceeds FLUX.1-dev IP-Adapter on CLIP-I (0.9214 vs 0.9138) and Human Preference (0.7123 vs 0.7059), confirming the base model's strength independent of the layout-toggling mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the consistent story generation task (addressed by training-free methods like StoryDiffusion and 1Prompt1Story, and training-based methods like StoryGen and BLIP-Diffusion) by introducing layout conditions—subject bounding boxes and detailed per-frame captions—as togglable control signals. Architecturally, it builds on PixArt-α (a DiT-based T2I model) for the global branch and adopts a ControlNet-style subject branch with masked self-attention, masked cross-attention, and a 3D self-attention mechanism borrowed from video generation to enforce cross-frame subject consistency. The dataset construction pipeline repurposes standard tools (GroundingDINO for detection, CLIP for feature extraction, K-means for clustering, GPT-4o mini for annotation) into a scalable cartoon-video-to-story-sequence pipeline, filling a gap in large-scale layout-annotated storytelling data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Lay2Story-1M (1M+ images at 720p+ with per-subject positions and captions) and Lay2Story-Bench (3,000 prompts with high-quality ground truth) address a real data scarcity bottleneck that has limited fine-grained control in storytelling research. The layout-toggable design is practically useful: users can opt into precise positional and attribute control or fall back to standard identity+frame prompting without retraining. Key limitations include the cartoon-only focus, single-subject-per-frame constraint, and reliance on GPT-4o mini for annotation quality; open questions include whether the approach generalizes to photorealistic or multi-subject scenes and whether the 3D masked attention scales beyond 6-frame sequences.
