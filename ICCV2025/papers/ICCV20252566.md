## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks on cooperative perception (e.g., James' Attack, AdvGPS) indiscriminately perturb transmitted features, producing numerous false positives that are readily detected by consensus-based defenses like ROBOSAC. No prior method strategically exploits the fundamental vulnerability that the ego agent cannot independently verify information received in uncertain perception regions, leaving a gap between attack stealth and defense effectiveness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252566/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The attacker consumes its own local feature map, feature maps received from neighboring agents, and the victim's feature map (used by ARS to partition the perception space into confidence-based subregions). The output is a region-specific adversarial perturbation δ (constrained by ‖δ‖_P ≤ ε) added to the attacker's feature map before transmission, where different spatial regions carry different perturbation objectives. In the real-time variant, historical feature flows over k frames replace the current-frame received features, with a SpatioTemporal Transformer predicting the current-frame feature map to close the communication latency gap.

## Method

PB operates in four stages: fusion of received features, Attack Region Selection (ARS), Multi-target Adversarial Perturbation Generation (MAPG), and transmission. ARS partitions the victim's perception space into four subregions—High Confidence Region (HCR), False Ambiguous Region (FAR), True Ambiguous Region (TAR), and Imperceptible Region (IR)—using two confidence thresholds μ_h and μ_l on the victim's heatmap. MAPG then assigns a distinct adversarial loss to each region: HCR detections are only slightly degraded (with a regularization term preserving score and IoU lower bounds to maintain consensus), TAR detections are removed, FAR detections have their confidence boosted to mimic a benign correction, and IR regions receive injected false objects. This region-differentiated strategy ensures the attacker's feature similarity and heatmap IoU distributions closely match those of benign collaborators, bypassing consensus-based defenses. For real-time execution, a SpatioTemporal Transformer (combining Multi-Head Self-Attention and Shifted-Window MSA) predicts current-frame features from historical feature flows, and the previous frame's perturbation is rigidly transformed as initialization to maintain temporal consistency.

## Results

On OPV2V (PointPillars + Average fusion), PB degrades cooperative perception AP from 0.91/0.91/0.81 (no attack) to 0.52/0.51/0.45 (IoU 0.3/0.5/0.7) while achieving a 100% Attack Success Rate (ASR) in bypassing both ROBOSAC and the proposed FLD defense. On V2XSet, PB similarly reduces AP to 0.51/0.47/0.35 with ASR of 0.98 under ROBOSAC and 0.95 under FLD. In stark contrast, all other attacks (PGD, C&W, James' Attack) are completely neutralized (ASR = 0.0) by both defenses, and AdvGPS only partially bypasses ROBOSAC (57% on OPV2V, 81% on V2XSet) while failing entirely under FLD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PB decisively outperforms all four baseline attacks in stealth (defense bypass) while maintaining competitive attack effectiveness. Under both ROBOSAC and FLD defenses, PGD, C&W, and James' Attack are completely neutralized (ASR = 0.0) on both OPV2V and V2XSet, as their indiscriminate perturbations create obvious divergence from the ego agent's perception. AdvGPS partially bypasses ROBOSAC (ASR 0.57 on OPV2V, 0.81 on V2XSet) by perturbing pose information, but is fully detected by FLD (ASR = 0.0). PB, by contrast, achieves ASR of 1.0 on OPV2V and 0.95–0.98 on V2XSet under both defenses, while degrading AP to 0.51–0.52 (IoU 0.3) — comparable to or better than the no-defense degradation of James' Attack (0.58) and AdvGPS (0.46–0.51). The real-time variant of PB retains >90% ASR even with up to 3-frame prediction delay, confirming practical feasibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial attack line initiated by Tu et al. (ICCV 2021) on multi-agent communication, which applied indiscriminate perturbations to shared features, and by AdvGPS (ICRA 2024), which targeted pose information. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of region-aware attack planning: rather than perturbing all transmitted information uniformly (which generates detectable false positives), PB strategically selects attack regions based on the victim's confidence levels and assigns different objectives per region to maintain partial consensus. The SpatioTemporal Transformer for real-time execution repurposes video prediction architectures (Swin-style shifted-window attention) for adversarial feature forecasting, and the proposed FLD defense refines ROBOSAC's result-level consensus checking into a more efficient feature-level mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a fundamental structural vulnerability in cooperative perception: the ego agent has no independent verification mechanism for information received in uncertain (low-confidence) regions, making it inherently susceptible to targeted manipulation that preserves consensus elsewhere. This finding undermines the assumption that consensus-based defenses are sufficient, as PB achieves near-100% bypass rates across multiple datasets, detectors, and fusion methods while all prior attacks are fully neutralized. The authors explicitly acknowledge they do not provide a defense against PB, leaving open the critical question of how to secure cooperative perception against attacks that exploit the unverifiability of uncertain-region information—a gap that is particularly consequential for safety-critical autonomous driving deployments.
