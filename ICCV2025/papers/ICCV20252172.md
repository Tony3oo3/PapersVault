## Problem

Existing 3D object detection architectures from LiDAR point clouds capture sparsely distributed objects but lack the fine-grained, locally dense spatial features needed for precise human keypoint localization. Simply appending a keypoint head to these detectors yields suboptimal pose estimation, and the height-compressed BEV representations commonly used for efficiency discard the vertical information essential for 3D keypoint placement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252172/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw LiDAR point cloud (N×C with x, y, z, intensity, elongation) and voxelizes it once into a sparse 3D grid at 0.1 m resolution over a 150.4 m × 150.4 m × 6 m range; all subsequent operations remain fully sparse with no intermediate dense conversions. The network outputs 14 3D keypoint locations (nose, shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, ankles, head) per detected human along with per-keypoint visibility scores, regressed directly in global coordinates without any positional encoding.

## Method

VoxelKP is a single-stage, fully sparse network built on VoxelNeXt that introduces two key components. First, each of four encoder stages uses a dual-branch spatial-context block: a spatial branch combining a sparse selective kernel module (two submanifold convolutions at 3×3×3 and 5×5×5 with softmax channel-wise attention) and a sparse box-attention module (self-attention within non-overlapping spatial partitions) to capture local keypoint correlations, alongside a lightweight MLP context branch that preserves per-voxel global details; the two branches are merged by element-wise summation. Second, a spatially aware multi-scale BEV fusion module projects the last three stages' sparse 3D features into 2D BEV maps using height-encoding convolutions (1×1×h) and scale-wise position offsets (x_p·2^r + r) to avoid overlapping multi-scale features, then collapses the stacked scales via proportional scaling rather than additional convolutions. The sparse box-attention is applied only in the last two stages to balance computational cost, and the BEV channel count is expanded from 256 to 384 to compensate for projection information loss.

## Results

On the Waymo v1.4.2 validation set, VoxelKP achieves an MPJPE of 8.87 cm, improving by 27% over HUM3DIL (12.21 cm) trained on the same data and by 12% over GC-KPL (10.10 cm) pre-trained on 200,000 additional real-world samples. The method also outperforms Zheng et al.'s multi-modal approach (10.32 MPJPE with LiDAR + RGB) using LiDAR alone. In the ablation (Table 2), the full VoxelKP architecture improves MPJPE by 36% and PEM by 14% over the VoxelNeXt baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VoxelKP outperforms all four compared methods on MPJPE. Against HUM3DIL [42], the primary same-data competitor, VoxelKP reduces MPJPE from 12.21 cm to 8.87 cm (27% improvement) using only LiDAR point clouds without any extra supervision. Against GC-KPL [37], which pre-trains on 200,000 additional real-world Waymo objects (a 25× larger dataset), VoxelKP still achieves a 12% lower MPJPE (8.87 vs. 10.10 cm). Against Zheng et al. [44], which uses an internal dataset and 2D pseudo-labels, VoxelKP improves by 18% (8.87 vs. 10.80 cm). Notably, VoxelKP also beats Zheng et al.'s multi-modal variant (LiDAR + RGB, 10.32 cm) using LiDAR alone. In the detection-estimation trade-off (Table 6), VoxelKP maintains comparable detection AP (within 1% at NMS 0.1) while improving MPJPE by ~35% over the VoxelNeXt baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

VoxelKP builds directly on the fully sparse voxel architecture of VoxelNeXt (designed for 3D object detection) and adapts it for the fundamentally different requirement of dense local keypoint localization within sparse human regions. It departs from prior 3D HPE methods (HUM3DIL, GC-KPL, Zheng et al.) that relied on multi-stage pipelines, RGB supervision, synthetic pre-training, or frozen backbones from larger datasets, instead proposing a single-staged, end-to-end sparse network trained solely on LiDAR keypoint annotations. The spatially aware BEV fusion module specifically addresses the height-information loss inherent in standard BEV projection used by object detection methods, while the sparse box-attention and selective kernel modules draw from dense-image attention and multi-scale feature selection literature but are reformulated to operate entirely in sparse voxel space without voxelization/devoxelization overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first single-staged, fully sparse network purpose-built for 3D HPE from LiDAR, demonstrating that careful architectural design (local attention, height-preserving BEV, hybrid per-voxel features) can match or exceed methods trained on 25× more data using only 8,125 annotated human examples. The finding that global stratified attention actively degrades keypoint accuracy—contrary to the prevailing assumption that wider context always helps—offers a useful design principle for dense-localization tasks on sparse inputs. Key limitations include reliance on a single publicly available dataset (Waymo), single-frame processing without temporal modeling, and the absence of physical constraints (e.g., inverse kinematics) on predicted poses, all of which the authors identify as directions for future work.
