## Problem

Streaming text-to-motion generation requires incrementally producing human poses as text arrives online while maintaining kinematic continuity with variable-length history. Existing diffusion models are locked to fixed-length, non-incremental generation, and discrete-token autoregressive models (e.g., T2M-GPT) suffer from quantization information loss, error accumulation over long sequences, and non-causal decoding that prevents online frame output.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250420/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequential stream of natural-language prompts (encoded by T5-XXL) together with variable-length historical motion latents, and produces 272-dimensional SMPL-based pose vectors (root XZ velocity, 6D root rotation, and per-joint local positions, velocities, and rotations for 22 joints) at 30 FPS. Each motion latent is decoded into frames immediately upon prediction thanks to the causal decoder, and generation auto-terminates when the predicted latent approaches a learned 'impossible pose' reference, eliminating the need for a pre-defined sequence length.

## Method

MotionStreamer couples a Causal Temporal AutoEncoder (1D causal convolutions with a padding scheme enforcing strict temporal causality, compressing 272-dim motion into 16-dim continuous latents at 4× downsampling, trained with σ-VAE loss plus a root-joint term) with a diffusion-based autoregressive Transformer. The Transformer applies a causal mask over the concatenated sequence [text embedding, historical latents, current latents] and feeds its output to a small MLP diffusion head that predicts the next continuous motion latent, avoiding VQ discretization entirely. A Two-Forward training strategy mitigates exposure bias by replacing a cosine-scheduled subset of ground-truth latents with first-pass predictions in a second forward pass, while Mixed training unifies atomic (text, motion) pairs and contextual (text, history, current) triplets to support compositional generalization. The continuous stopping condition encodes an all-zero 'impossible pose' into latent space, and generation halts when the predicted latent falls within a distance threshold of this reference.

## Results

On HumanML3D, MotionStreamer achieves the best FID of 11.79, R@1 of 0.631, R@3 of 0.859, and MM-D of 16.08, outperforming the closest competitor MoMask (FID 12.23, R@1 0.621, R@3 0.846). On BABEL for long-term streaming generation, it achieves FID 15.74, R@3 0.568, MM-D 15.40, and Peak Jerk 0.04, substantially outperforming FlowMDM (FID 18.74, R@3 0.492, MM-D 20.25, PJ 0.06) and the adapted T2M-GPT* (FID 39.48, R@3 0.364). The method also achieves the lowest first-frame latency among all compared approaches, benefiting from its causal latent decoding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On HumanML3D, MotionStreamer outperforms all seven baselines on FID, R@1, R@2, R@3, and MM-D. The closest competitor is MoMask (FID 12.23 vs. 11.79; R@3 0.846 vs. 0.859), while VQ-based methods T2M-GPT and MotionGPT lag further behind (FID 12.48 and 14.38 respectively), and diffusion-based MDM is the weakest (FID 23.45). On BABEL for long-term streaming generation, MotionStreamer achieves FID 15.74 and R@3 0.568, beating FlowMDM (FID 18.74, R@3 0.492) by a wide margin, and substantially outperforming the adapted T2M-GPT* (FID 39.48) and VQ-LLaMA (FID 24.34). The only metric where MotionStreamer does not lead is AUJ on BABEL (0.90 vs. FlowMDM's 0.51), though it achieves the best Peak Jerk (0.04 vs. 0.06). The paper also demonstrates that replacing the continuous Causal TAE with VQ-VAE or a non-causal VAE degrades both reconstruction and generation quality, confirming the advantage of the continuous causal latent design over discrete tokenization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the autoregressive-without-vector-quantization paradigm from image generation (Li et al., 2024) to human motion, replacing discrete VQ tokens with continuous causal latents predicted by a diffusion head. It departs from the dominant VQ-VAE + GPT pipeline (T2M-GPT, MotionGPT, MoMask) by introducing a Causal Temporal AutoEncoder whose 1D causal convolutions enforce strict temporal causality, enabling frame-by-frame online decoding rather than whole-sequence reconstruction. The σ-VAE calibrated decoder loss (Rybkin et al., 2021) is adopted for the encoder, and the Two-Forward training strategy addresses exposure bias in a manner analogous to scheduled sampling in NLP autoregressive models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making motion generation truly streaming with variable-length history and online response, MotionStreamer unlocks applications (multi-round interactive generation, dynamic motion composition with prefix preservation) that fixed-window or offline methods cannot support. The ablation revealing that a near-perfect-reconstruction AE (MPJPE 1.7 mm) produces the worst generation quality (FID 43.8) while a moderately compressed causal VAE (MPJPE 22.9 mm) yields the best generation (FID 11.8) is a useful reminder that latent-space structure matters more than reconstruction fidelity for downstream generation. Open questions include whether the 'impossible pose' stopping mechanism generalizes to more complex motion boundaries, whether the approach scales to multi-person or scene-interaction settings, and whether the 30 FPS / 272-dim SMPL representation limits expressiveness for high-frequency or contact-rich motions.
