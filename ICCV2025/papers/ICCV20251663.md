## Problem

Current caption-based MLLMs lack visual grounding ability, making them unsuitable for tasks requiring precise spatial understanding. Existing approaches to add grounding—such as PIN's architectural modifications or LoRA's low-rank updates—either cause catastrophic forgetting of pre-trained image understanding or lack the capacity to capture complex multi-object spatial relationships. The paper addresses how to equip MLLMs with robust grounding while preserving their existing vision-language skills without full retraining.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an RGB image (processed through a frozen vision encoder into visual tokens) and a free-form text prompt, then autoregressively generates grounded text where bounding box coordinates are interleaved with natural language (e.g., 'A cat at [x1,y1,x2,y2] sits to the left of a dog at [a1,b1,a2,b2]'). A lightweight 8-bit quantized BERT classifier (~1M parameters) inspects the prompt at inference time to route the computation: image-understanding queries bypass the grounding expert entirely (α=1), while grounding queries activate both experts with the learned α weighting. This dual-mode output means the same model produces plain text answers for VQA and coordinate-embedded captions for grounding tasks.

## Method

TWIST replaces the FFN in each transformer decoder block with two parallel Mixture-of-Experts modules: a frozen MoE (4 experts) pre-trained on image understanding and a learnable MoE (2 experts) initialized from the frozen one for visual grounding, combined via a learnable scalar α that controls knowledge transfer between them. Training employs a stepwise loss inspired by Lightman et al. that decomposes the grounding objective into sequential subtasks (identify object → locate position → describe spatial relationship), each contributing a separate cross-entropy term, which simplifies learning compared to end-to-end box prediction. The SCOUT synthetic dataset is generated in two steps: an LLM (Mixtral) produces spatial 'what' and 'where' questions from Flickr30k captions, then CogVLM answers them with step-by-step grounded reasoning directly from the image, yielding 94.7% human-verified accuracy versus 63.1% for Shikra's caption-only approach. The model is fine-tuned on SCOUT (512k samples), RefCOCO (128k pairs), and a CogVLM-generated grounded caption set (GIC) from COCO2017, with only 1.67B parameters trainable (0.8B active) on four A6000 GPUs for 1.5 days.

## Results

On object localization, TWIST & SCOUT achieves mIoU of 0.68 (PVOC), 0.66 (COCO), and 0.65 (LVIS), substantially outperforming PIN (0.45/0.35/0.26) and LoRA (0.43/0.36/0.24). On visual grounding, it reaches 90.2/80.3 on RefCOCO test-A/test-B and 15.0 AP / 49.3 AP50 on grounded image captioning (GIC), the best GIC score among all compared methods. Critically, image understanding is fully preserved: TWIST & SCOUT matches its MoE-LLaVA base exactly on GQA (61.4), SQA (68.5), VQA v2 (51.4), POPE (86.3), MME (1423.0), LLaVA-Wild (94.1), and MM-Vet (34.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PIN [13], TWIST & SCOUT dominates on every grounding metric: mIoU improves from 0.45 to 0.68 on PVOC (+22%), RefCOCO test-A jumps from 26.4 to 90.2, and PIN scores 0 on GIC while TWIST & SCOUT reaches 15.0 AP. Against LoRA fine-tuning of the same MoE-LLaVA backbone, TWIST & SCOUT improves mIoU from 0.43 to 0.68 on PVOC, demonstrating that low-rank adaptation is insufficient for grounding. On GIC, TWIST & SCOUT (15.0 AP, 49.3 AP50) outperforms both Ferret-7B (13.9/47.1) and Shikra-7B (13.2/46.8) despite being roughly 4× smaller, while Grounding DINO fails entirely (0 AP) due to its lack of free-form language generation. On REC, Grounding DINO (93.2 test-A) and Ferret-7B (91.3) slightly edge out TWIST & SCOUT (90.2), but the gap is within 1–3 points. For image understanding, TWIST & SCOUT exactly matches its MoE-LLaVA base across all seven benchmarks (GQA, SQA, VQA, POPE, MME, LLaVA-Wild, MM-Vet), confirming zero forgetting, whereas PIN reports no image understanding scores at all.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MoE-LLaVA (Lin et al., 2024) as its base architecture, extending its mixture-of-experts decoder with a parallel grounding expert. It departs from PIN (Dorkenwald et al., CVPR 2024), which modifies the vision encoder for localization but suffers catastrophic forgetting, and from LoRA-based fine-tuning, whose low-rank constraints are insufficient for capturing new spatial relationships. The stepwise loss is inspired by Lightman et al.'s process-reward work, and the SCOUT dataset is positioned as a visually-grounded alternative to Shikra's caption-only chain-of-thought generation, which the authors show produces hallucinated spatial narratives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The twin-expert MoE design offers a practical, modular recipe for adding grounding to caption-based MLLMs without full retraining, which is valuable for practitioners who cannot afford massive pre-training budgets. However, the approach is constrained by CogVLM's 3-object-per-image ceiling (causing SCOUT scaling to saturate at ~1M samples), requires a BERT classifier at inference for task routing, and the grounding expert is initialized from the image-understanding expert rather than being truly independent. Open questions include whether the twin-expert paradigm generalizes to more than two task families, whether the 3-object limit can be broken with better data generators, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D grounding.
