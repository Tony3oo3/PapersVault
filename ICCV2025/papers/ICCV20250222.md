## Problem

Existing wide-angle image correction relies on a cascaded two-stage pipeline that independently performs distortion rectification and boundary rectangling. This separation causes error accumulation (rectification errors propagate irreversibly into rectangling) and misaligned optimization, as the two stages cannot benefit from each other's constraints, ultimately degrading both geometric fidelity and downstream vision task performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250222/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single distorted wide-angle RGB image (256×256 in the released dataset) and produces a rectified image with regular rectangular boundaries in one forward pass. Unlike two-stage pipelines that output an intermediate rectified image with irregular boundaries before a separate rectangling step, ConBo-Net directly outputs the final corrected image with both geometric accuracy and rectangular shape, running at 42 ms per image for real-time inference.

## Method

ConBo-Net unifies rectification and rectangling by modeling both as dual Thin-Plate Spline (TPS) deformations and learning a spatially varying interpolation between them. Two parallel encoder branches (inspired by RecRecNet) extract rectification and rectangling features, which are fused via a spatial attention mechanism that assigns higher rectification weight near the principal point and higher rectangling weight at boundaries. The fused features regress a global TPS control-point offset, which is further refined by a local flow offset predicted from ResNet18 features. Training is supervised by four losses: L1 appearance, VGG-19 perceptual, a novel ordinal geometry loss that enforces curvature monotonicity consistent with radial distortion physics, and a B-spline smoothness regularizer on the deformation grid.

## Results

On the ConBo-Net dataset (500 test pairs), ConBo-Net achieves PSNR 18.59, SSIM 0.54, LPIPS 0.13, and MS-SSIM 0.75, outperforming the best two-stage pipeline (DR-GAN+RecRecNet: PSNR 18.09, SSIM 0.51, LPIPS 0.18, MS-SSIM 0.70) across all image quality metrics. For downstream tasks, ConBo-Net reaches mIoU 0.56 (SAM) and mIoU 0.65 / mAP 0.46 (YOLO), surpassing the best two-stage baselines (mIoU 0.47 SAM, mIoU 0.56 YOLO, mAP 0.40 YOLO). Inference time is 0.04 s per image, competitive with the fastest two-stage pipeline (PCN+RecRecNet at 0.02 s) and dramatically faster than He et al.-based pipelines (~19.5 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConBo-Net outperforms all ten two-stage combinations across every image-quality and downstream-task metric. The closest competitor is DR-GAN+RecRecNet (PSNR 18.09, SSIM 0.51, MS-SSIM 0.70), which ConBo-Net surpasses by +0.50 dB PSNR, +0.03 SSIM, and +0.05 MS-SSIM while also improving LPIPS from 0.18 to 0.13. For downstream segmentation (SAM mIoU), ConBo-Net achieves 0.56 versus 0.47 for the best two-stage (ODE+RecRecNet), a relative gain of ~19%. On YOLO detection, ConBo-Net reaches mAP 0.46, exceeding the best two-stage (SimFIR+RecRecNet at 0.40) by 0.06. The He et al. rectangling component is consistently inferior to RecRecNet as a second stage and introduces ~19 s latency, making those pipelines impractical for real-time use. The only metric where ConBo-Net is not the absolute fastest is inference time (0.04 s vs. PCN+RecRecNet at 0.02 s), though both are well within real-time budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the TPS deformation framework (Bookstein, 1989) and the RecRecNet encoder architecture (Liao et al., 2023), repurposing both as the mathematical and architectural backbone for a unified correction pipeline. It departs from the dominant cascaded paradigm—where rectification (e.g., DR-GAN, SimFIR, DeepCalib) and rectangling (e.g., He et al., RecRecNet) are optimized independently—by reframing both as dual TPS transformations that can be interpolated via spatially-varying attention weights. The ordinal geometry loss, which encodes the monotonicity prior of radial distortion from the wide-angle camera model, is a novel constraint not present in prior rectification or rectangling methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the two-stage cascade, ConBo-Net removes a practical bottleneck (42 ms vs. ~19–20 s per image) and avoids irreversible error propagation, yielding measurable gains in downstream segmentation and detection (SAM mIoU 0.56 vs. 0.47; YOLO mAP 0.46 vs. 0.40). The released 5,660-pair dataset addresses a data scarcity gap for the unified task. However, the paper acknowledges residual curvature at extreme boundary regions where geometric fidelity and rectangular constraints conflict, and the evaluation is limited to 256×256 synthetic images with a single test split, leaving open whether the morphing formulation scales to higher resolutions or more diverse real-world distortion regimes.
