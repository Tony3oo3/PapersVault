## Problem

Estimating full-body human dance motion from egocentric video alone is difficult because much of the body falls outside the camera's field of view, while music-only approaches lack visual grounding for head and upper-body alignment. The joint task of fusing egocentric video with music for dance motion estimation remains largely unexplored, and no large-scale dataset combining both modalities existed prior to this work.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251978/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of egocentric frames from a forward-facing head-mounted camera and a corresponding music track of equal duration T, both at 30 fps. Visual features are extracted via a ResNet backbone, while music features are obtained through a pre-trained JukeBox model followed by a transformer encoder. The output is a full-body SMPL parameterized dance motion sequence (24 joints) over the same T frames, with sequences segmented into 5-second clips during training on the 36-hour EgoAIST++ dataset (~3.9M frames).

## Method

The EgoMusic Motion Network (EMM) is a conditional diffusion model whose denoiser is built around a Skeleton Mamba module that explicitly encodes human skeletal structure at three levels: a Group Scan applies multi-directional State Space Duality (SSD) over permuted joint-group tokens to capture inter-group relationships, a Joint Scan applies SSD within each group to model intra-group joint dependencies, and a Temporal Scan applies bidirectional SSD across frames for temporal coherence. Joints are tokenized into overlapping groups reflecting skeletal symmetry (e.g., left arm, right arm), and a Human Tokenizer/Inverse Human Tokenizer maps between the joint-level and group-level representations. Training combines the standard diffusion loss with a kinematic loss (position, velocity, foot-contact) and a symmetric contrastive alignment loss that unifies music and vision embeddings in a shared space. At inference, a Head Guidance Sampling step uses a goal function (positional + rotational head alignment) to steer the denoising trajectory toward egocentric-consistent head poses. The paper provides a theoretical universality guarantee (Theorem 1) showing Skeleton Mamba can approximate any H-equivariant function on compact sets, where H encodes human body symmetries.

## Results

On the EgoAIST++ test set, EMM (ego+music) achieves the best performance across all six metrics: O_head = 0.53 rad, T_head = 342.37 mm, MPJPE = 137.54 mm, Accel = 11.84, FS = 12.79, and MMV = 0.262. Compared to the strongest egocentric baseline EgoEgo, EMM reduces head orientation error by 0.21 rad, head translation by 31.3 mm, MPJPE by 14.5 mm, and foot skating by 9.34, while improving the proposed MMV alignment score by 0.044. In the cross-dataset evaluation on Ego-Exo4D, EMM maintains its lead with MPJPE = 191.55 mm (vs. 234.58 for EgoEgo) and MMV = 0.239 (vs. 0.203 for EgoEgo). On the auxiliary text-to-motion task (HumanML3D), Skeleton Mamba achieves R-Prec = 0.795 and MM-Dist = 2.983, surpassing MDM, MLD, and MotionMamba.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against egocentric-video baselines, EMM dominates: it outperforms EgoEgo [41] (the strongest ego-only method) by 0.21 rad on O_head, 31.3 mm on T_head, 14.5 mm on MPJPE, and 9.34 on FS, while also achieving a substantially higher MMV score (0.262 vs. 0.218). Against music-driven baselines, EMM improves MPJPE by 29.9 mm over EDGE [75] (137.54 vs. 167.43) and by 36.1 mm over FACT [43], with the largest gap on foot skating (12.79 vs. 14.75 for EDGE). The ablation rows confirm that combining both modalities is essential: ego-only EMM has the best head metrics (O_head = 0.61) but the worst MPJPE (186.49), while music-only EMM has the best FS (14.04) but poor head alignment (O_head = 1.43). In the cross-dataset test on Ego-Exo4D, EMM extends its lead, achieving MPJPE = 191.55 mm versus 234.58 for EgoEgo and 213.37 for EDGE, with the largest absolute gap on T_head (322.19 vs. 644.62 for EDGE). On the auxiliary text-to-motion benchmark (HumanML3D), Skeleton Mamba achieves R-Prec = 0.795 and MM-Dist = 2.983, narrowly beating MotionMamba (0.790 / 3.060) and clearly surpassing MDM (0.611 / 5.566) and MLD (0.772 / 3.196). For skeleton-based action recognition, Skeleton Mamba reaches 94.4% Top-1 on NTU RGB+D 60 and 52.4% on Kinetics-400, outperforming PoseConv3D (93.1% / 47.7%), MotionBERT (93.0% / –), MS-G3D (91.5% / 38.0%), and DSTA-Net (91.5% / –).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: egocentric human pose estimation (EgoEgo, Kinpoly, PoseReg) and music-conditioned dance generation (FACT, Bailando, EDGE), which have previously been treated as separate problems. It departs from prior Mamba-based motion models (MotionMamba, Text-Controlled Motion Mamba) that flatten each frame into a single latent vector or ignore the spatial ordering of joints, instead introducing a hierarchical Skeleton Mamba that explicitly scans over grouped joints and individual joints before applying temporal scanning. The generative backbone follows the conditional diffusion paradigm of Ho et al. and the head-guided sampling strategy of EgoEgo, while the core scanning mechanism is built on State Space Duality (SSD) from Dao and Gu.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The EgoAIST++ dataset (36 hours, 3.9M frames) is the first large-scale resource pairing egocentric video with music for dance, filling a gap where prior datasets offered only one modality or very limited coverage (~2 hours in Ego-Exo4D). The theoretical expressiveness guarantee (Theorem 1) under body-symmetry equivariance provides a principled justification for the skeleton-aware scanning design. Key open questions include whether the simple bidirectional temporal scan can be extended to very long sequences, how robust the method is to mismatched ego/music pairings, and whether the Skeleton Mamba inductive bias transfers to other structured sequential domains beyond human motion.
