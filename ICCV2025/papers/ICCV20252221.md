## Problem

Estimating oriented normals from noisy point clouds remains difficult because supervised methods require extensive labeled data and unsupervised implicit-representation methods that overfit individual point clouds tend to over-smooth geometry or suffer gradient degradation (∇f_θ → 0), which collapses the learned surface to an invalid solution. No existing unsupervised approach robustly recovers high-quality oriented normals from noisy observations while preserving local geometric detail.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252221/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single unstructured noisy point cloud P (no ground-truth labels, no clean reference) and overfits a lightweight MLP (~461K parameters, 8 linear layers with skip connection) to it. It produces three outputs: oriented normals per point (via local gradient aggregation over κ=8 nearest neighbors in the combined {P, Q} set), a reconstructed surface mesh extracted by marching cubes on the zero level set, and a denoised point cloud obtained by projecting all raw points onto the learned surface. An auxiliary Gaussian-sampled set Q is constructed around P during training to provide additional spatial coverage near the underlying surface.

## Method

The core mechanism is a local gradient-aware surface filtering pipeline built on a neural signed distance field. A distance measurement operator combines Euclidean distance with two projection distances along the normals at both the noisy point and its projected surface counterpart, guiding global surface fitting without forcing the surface through all noisy points. Points are projected onto the surface via q′ = q − f_θ(q)·n_q, and three gradient-aware constraints are introduced to prevent over-smoothing and gradient degradation: (1) inter-level gradient consistency with adaptive confidence weighting based on predicted signed distance, (2) intra-level gradient orientation using multi-scale neighborhood plane fitting with an additive normal n̄_q = (n_q + n_q′)/‖n_q + n_q′‖ to avoid zero-gradient collapse, and (3) inter-level gradient aggregation at inference time via distance- and angle-weighted averaging of additive normals over local neighbors. The final loss combines signed-distance, projection-distance, gradient-consistency, and gradient-orientation terms, all trained in an unsupervised per-shape overfitting regime.

## Results

On the PCPNet dataset, the method achieves an average oriented normal RMSE of 17.00° (unsupervised), outperforming the best supervised method SHS-Net (19.79°) and the closest unsupervised baseline NeuralGF (18.70°). On FamousShape, the average RMSE is 24.07°, again beating all supervised baselines (best: NGLO at 27.69°) and NeuralGF (26.16°). On sparse point clouds (3K points), the method achieves 24.03° RMSE, surpassing NeuralGF (25.54°) and all other baselines. For surface reconstruction on the SRB dataset, the method attains the best F-Score of 0.9786 and lowest CD_L1 of 0.2518, and for denoising on PointCleanNet (50K, 3% noise), it achieves CD of 2.168×10⁴ and P2M of 0.793×10⁴, competitive with supervised methods using only 14% of their parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method (unsupervised) outperforms all 34 baselines on average across both PCPNet and FamousShape for oriented normal estimation. Against the strongest supervised competitor SHS-Net, it reduces average RMSE from 19.79° to 17.00° on PCPNet (−2.79°) and from 32.79° to 24.07° on FamousShape (−8.72°), with the gap widening under high noise (32.74° vs. 35.51° on PCPNet; 49.25° vs. 52.67° on FamousShape). Against the closest unsupervised baseline NeuralGF [32], it improves PCPNet average from 18.70° to 17.00° and FamousShape from 26.16° to 24.07°, with particularly large gains on clean data (9.71° vs. 10.60°) and high noise. On sparse point clouds (3K/5K points), the method achieves 24.03°/21.80° RMSE, beating NeuralGF (25.54°/24.35°) and all supervised methods (NGLO: 32.65°/28.34°). On real-world SceneNN and ScanNet, it achieves 42.74° and 37.09° average RMSE respectively, outperforming NeuralGF (48.24°/39.10°) and NGLO (46.97°/39.40°). For surface reconstruction on SRB, it marginally beats IF [36] (F-Score 0.9786 vs. 0.9782) and NeuralGF (0.9758), while for denoising on PointCleanNet it matches or exceeds supervised methods like IterativePFN (CD 2.660 vs. 2.621 at 10K/1% noise) using only 461K parameters versus 3.2M.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unsupervised implicit-field line of work initiated by Neural-Pull [40] and NeuralGF [32] (same first author), which learn a signed distance field by overfitting a small MLP to a single point cloud and derive normals from the field gradient. The key departure is a local gradient-aware surface filtering framework that replaces the single-point projection loss with a composite distance operator (Euclidean + two projection distances), inter-level gradient consistency, intra-level gradient orientation via multi-scale neighborhood plane fitting, and inter-level gradient aggregation at inference. Conceptually, the paper reframes the projection step as a filtering problem where noisy points are pulled onto the zero level set while enforcing geometric coherence across neighboring iso-surfaces, rather than treating each point's constraint independently as in prior implicit-field methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a lightweight (~461K parameter) unsupervised method matches or exceeds supervised normal estimators on noisy data, removing the need for expensive labeled training sets and enabling per-shape adaptation without retraining. However, the per-shape overfitting paradigm limits scalability to very large scenes or real-time applications, and the improvements over the direct predecessor NeuralGF are modest (e.g., 18.70→17.00 average RMSE on PCPNet). Open questions include whether the gradient-consistency and multi-scale orientation losses transfer to other implicit-representation tasks (e.g., shape completion, generative modeling) and whether the approach can be made amortized to avoid per-shape optimization.
