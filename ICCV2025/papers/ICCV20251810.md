## Problem

Existing unified image generation models either train from scratch on per-task curated datasets or adapt pre-trained image models with task-specific architectural modifications, both of which violate the task-agnostic data and generation principles that make LLMs successful at unifying text tasks. The paper asks whether pre-trained video diffusion models, which inherently model temporal correlations across frames, can serve as a more natural and general foundation for unifying diverse visual generation tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251810/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a condition image (canny edge map, depth map, or reference subject image) and a text prompt, both resized to 512×512, and produces a single generated image. The condition image is encoded as the first frame of a two-frame video sequence while the target image (with its text description) forms the second frame, so the model performs next-frame prediction rather than standard conditional generation. A replication strategy is applied along the temporal dimension before patchification to prevent the 3D VAE's temporal compression from intermingling condition and target latent tokens.

## Method

RealGeneral reformulates multi-condition image generation as temporal in-context learning within a pre-trained CogVideoX1.5 video diffusion model, treating the condition image as a preceding frame and the target as the frame to predict. Three modules address multi-modal interference: (1) the Unified Conditional Embedding (UCE) module aligns text and image conditions by mapping subject-specific text embeddings into the visual latent space and adding a learnable task-specific bias to the condition frame; (2) the Separated Condition AdaLN (SC-AdaLN) module decouples the original two-branch AdaLN into three independent branches (text, condition frame, target frame) so each modality receives its own scale, shift, and residual gate; (3) the Frame-Condition Decoupling (FCD) module applies a custom attention mask that blocks all text-to-condition and condition-to-text interactions while preserving full condition-to-target visual attention. The entire framework is trained via LoRA (rank 256) injected only into the SC-AdaLN and FCD modules, requiring just 2 A800 GPUs for 2 days and 0.1% of the training data used by comparable from-scratch unified models.

## Results

On the subject-driven customization task (Dream-Bench), RealGeneral achieves CLIP-I 0.849, DINO 0.668, and CLIP-T 0.314, surpassing the second-best method (EMU2) by 0.054 in CLIP-I and 0.085 in DINO. On the canny-to-image task (COCO2017), RealGeneral attains the best FID (17.50) and SSIM (0.44) among all compared methods. On the depth-to-image task, it achieves FID 23.40 and SSIM 0.35, placing second-best on both metrics behind OmniGen (FID 21.62) and OminiControl (SSIM 0.39) respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RealGeneral outperforms all six customization baselines on every metric (CLIP-I, DINO, CLIP-T). The largest margins are against EMU2 (CLIP-I +0.054, DINO +0.085) and IP-Adapter (CLIP-I +0.059, DINO +0.098), indicating that the video-model foundation and the UCE/SC-AdaLN/FCD modules substantially improve subject fidelity over image-model-based approaches. On canny-to-image, RealGeneral beats ControlNet-SD1.5 (FID 17.50 vs 18.79, SSIM 0.44 vs 0.28), OmniGen (FID 17.50 vs 22.51, SSIM 0.44 vs 0.34), and OminiControl (FID 17.50 vs 20.63, SSIM 0.44 vs 0.40). On depth-to-image, RealGeneral is competitive but not dominant: OmniGen achieves a lower FID (21.62 vs 23.40) and OminiControl a higher SSIM (0.39 vs 0.35), though RealGeneral still surpasses ControlNet on both metrics. Overall, the video-model-based approach wins decisively on subject-driven generation and canny conditioning, while depth conditioning shows a more balanced picture where no single method dominates all metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both dominant streams in unified image generation—training-from-scratch (OmniGen, PixWizard) and image-model-adaptation (ControlNet, OminiControl)—by repurposing a pre-trained video diffusion model (CogVideoX1.5) as the foundation. The key conceptual move is reformulating conditional image generation as temporal in-context learning, where condition images serve as 'preceding frames' analogous to context tokens in LLMs, rather than as additional encoder inputs or partial synthesis targets. Architecturally, it introduces three modules (UCE, SC-AdaLN, FCD) specifically designed to handle the modality-conflict problem that arises when video models process condition and target frames jointly, a challenge absent in prior image-model adaptations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that video models' inherent temporal modeling provides a principled, task-agnostic foundation for unified image generation, achieving strong results with dramatically less data and compute (LoRA fine-tuning on 2 A800 GPUs for 2 days, 0.1% of UniReal's training samples). However, the framework still relies on task-specific LoRA adapters rather than a single universal model, and the base video model (CogVideoX1.5) has measurably weaker image quality than SDXL or FLUX, suggesting the approach's ceiling is tied to video model quality. Open questions include whether the formulation generalizes to higher-fidelity video models, whether task-specific LoRA can be eliminated for true unification, and whether the two-frame setting extends naturally to multi-condition or multi-frame generation.
