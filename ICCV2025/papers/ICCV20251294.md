## Problem

Current text-conditioned video diffusion models cannot serve as fine-grained world simulators because text descriptions lack the temporal precision needed for delicate manipulation tasks (e.g., culinary or surgical activities). This paper introduces multisensory interoceptive signals—haptic forces, muscle EMG, hand pose, body pose, and gaze—as conditioning inputs to enable per-frame fine-grained control over generative video simulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251294/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 4 context video frames (64×64 in main experiments) and temporally fine-grained multisensory action trajectories (hand haptic forces, EMG, hand pose, body pose, gaze) spanning 8 future timesteps, optionally augmented with a text phrase or sentence. It outputs 8 predicted future video frames via a diffusion process. Unlike text-conditioned simulators where a single prompt is reused for all frames, the action conditioning is frame-specific, allowing the temporal attention to vary per predicted frame. Experiments use 12-frame sequences from the ActionSense dataset (4 context + 8 prediction).

## Method

Each sensory modality is encoded by a dedicated Mixture-of-Experts head with a self-supervised reconstruction loss to ensure meaningful features. Modality-specific features are aligned to a shared latent structure via channel-wise cross-attention with an anchor derived from context video frames, while preserving each modality's unique information (avoiding contrastive alignment that would erase complementary differences). A permutation-invariant softmax-weighted symmetric fusion aggregates modalities into a single cross-modal action vector. A projective regularization removes the component of the action vector along the context frame direction, and a relaxed hyperplane variant clips only the negative-hemisphere component, making the interaction vector context- and consequence-aware. A norm-matching loss ties the interaction vector magnitude to frame-to-frame visual change. The final interaction feature conditions a modified I2VGen 3D UNet diffusion backbone via cross-attention, trained end-to-end with a weighted sum of diffusion, reconstruction, and norm losses.

## Results

On the ActionSense dataset (64×64, 4 context frames → 8 predicted frames), the proposed multisensory method achieves MSE 0.110, PSNR 16.0 dB, LPIPS 0.276, and FVD 203.5, outperforming the best text-conditioned baseline (UniSim sentence: MSE 0.117, FVD 251.7) by ~6% in MSE and ~19% in FVD. Against the strongest unimodal action baseline (Hand-force only: MSE 0.120, FVD 278.9), the multisensory method improves MSE by 8% and FVD by 27%. The paper reports an overall 36% accuracy improvement and 16% temporal consistency improvement over prior approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed multisensory method outperforms all five baselines across every metric. Against UniSim (text-conditioned video diffusion, Du et al. 2023), even the most detailed sentence-level prompt (MSE 0.117, FVD 251.7) is surpassed by the multisensory method (MSE 0.110, FVD 203.5), with the gap widening for verb-level prompts (FVD 337.9 vs. 203.5). Among multimodal feature extraction baselines (Table 2), Mutex (Shah et al. 2023) performs worst (MSE 0.164, FVD 410.1), followed by LanguageBind (Zhu et al. 2023, MSE 0.143, FVD 332.0) and ImageBind (Girdhar et al. 2023, MSE 0.134, FVD 315.6), all of which rely on contrastive alignment that erases fine-grained temporal action details. Signal-Agnostic learning (Du et al. 2021) is the closest competitor (MSE 0.127, FVD 267.5) but still trails the proposed method by 13% in MSE and 24% in FVD, attributed to its loose coupling between action and video modalities. The paper also shows that adding a text phrase as an auxiliary modality to the multisensory method yields marginal further gains (FVD 200.4 vs. 203.5), suggesting text and sensory signals are complementary rather than redundant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on I2VGen's cascaded diffusion architecture for image-to-video synthesis, repurposing it as a generative simulator conditioned on learned multimodal action features rather than text prompts. It departs from prior multimodal feature learning (ImageBind, LanguageBind, Mutex, Signal-Agnostic) which emphasizes cross-modal alignment for retrieval, arguing instead that generative simulation requires preserving modality-specific complementary information. The projective/hyperplane interaction regularization is a novel geometric constraint not seen in prior action-conditioned generation work, and the overall framing of multisensory interoceptive signals as a conditioning modality for video diffusion is new relative to UniSim's text-based approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work opens a path toward fine-grained, real-time video simulation for embodied AI and robotics, where text descriptions are too coarse for delicate manipulation tasks. The demonstrated robustness to missing modalities at test time is practically important for deployment scenarios where sensors may fail. However, experiments are limited to 64×64 resolution on a single kitchen dataset (ActionSense), and the 2292-dimensional action space is specific to that setup; scaling to higher resolutions, diverse tasks, and real-time inference remain open. The downstream policy optimization application is promising but underexplored, with only a single goal-conditioned task demonstrated.
