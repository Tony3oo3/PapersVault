## Problem

Existing personalized T2I generation methods rely on prompt-level modeling, which is fundamentally limited by the constrained token capacity of text encoders, leading to inaccurate personalization when incorporating multiple user preferences. Additionally, these approaches depend on large-scale LLMs or multimodal models for user profiling, incurring significant computational costs and limiting scalability. The paper addresses how to accurately identify individual preferences, overcome prompt-level token constraints, and precisely integrate user preferences into the generation process without requiring fine-tuning of foundation T2I models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user's historical prompts (ranging from 18 to ~4,700 entries), a target prompt for the current generation request, and optional preference intensities (e.g., ratings) as input. Coreset sampling reduces the historical set to a 10% subset before conditioning. The output is a personalized T2I condition (a latent embedding vector) that replaces the standard text encoder output in the diffusion pipeline, ultimately producing a personalized image. The personalization degree parameter α (set to 0.3 for PIP, 0.1 for ML) controls the balance between target fidelity and historical preference incorporation at the latent level.

## Method

DrUM operates at the condition level (latent space) rather than the prompt level, using a lightweight Personalized Conditioning Adapter (PeCA) composed of only 10 cross-attention layers attached to open-source text encoders (OpenCLIP or Google T5). Historical prompt conditions are concatenated with the target prompt condition, and PeCA is trained via cosine similarity to reconstruct the target condition, learning to reproduce meaningful token-level information while preserving the original encoder's embedding distribution. A key innovation is the conditioning guidance mechanism, which decouples attention score computation across reference and target conditions, applies softmax independently to each, and modulates the overall personalization through a weighting parameter α, enabling fine-grained control over preference intensity. Coreset sampling with CLIP similarity and minimum enclosing ball (iterative greedy approximation) selects representative historical prompts while incorporating preference intensities, reducing computational overhead. The entire adapter is trained on CC3M prompts without images and integrates seamlessly with Stable Diffusion V1/V2/XL/V3 and FLUX without any fine-tuning of the base T2I model.

## Results

On the PIP dataset, DrUM achieves the highest CLIP score across all four Stable Diffusion versions (25.13 on SD V1, 25.82 on SD V2, 26.77 on SD XL, 25.14 on SD V3), with improvement rates of +17.86% to +34.83% over the unpersonalized baseline. On the more complex ML dataset, DrUM shows positive improvement on SD V1 (+0.38%), SD V2 (+1.34%), and SD XL (+4.26%), though it underperforms on SD V3 (−5.60%). For Text alignment, DrUM dramatically outperforms baselines, reaching 96.42% target alignment on OpenCLIP ViT-L (PIP) versus 68.50% for TV, while also improving history alignment by +6.42% over the original model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DrUM consistently outperforms all three baselines on the PIP dataset across every T2I model, with the largest margins on SD V1 (+34.83% vs. +32.04% for FABRIC and +22.40% for TV) and SD V2 (+34.68% vs. +25.61% for FABRIC). On the more challenging ML dataset, DrUM is the only method that achieves positive improvement on SD V2 (+1.34%) and SD XL (+4.26%), whereas FABRIC degrades severely (−16.05% on SD V2) and TV/PMG all show negative improvements. FABRIC is limited to SD V1/V2 and cannot be applied to SD XL or V3; PMG is restricted to the ML domain and does not support PIP. On Text alignment, DrUM dominates by a wide margin (e.g., 96.42% vs. 68.50% target on ViT-L/PIP), indicating that condition-level modeling preserves the original prompt semantics far better than prompt-rewriting approaches. The one notable exception is SD V3 on ML, where TV achieves a higher target CLIP score (35.83 vs. 30.17), which the authors attribute to the complexity of movie product information exceeding what a single image canvas can represent.

## Relation to Prior Work

DrUM departs from the dominant prompt-level personalization paradigm (TV, PMG, ViPer, Pigeon) by shifting the personalization mechanism into the latent embedding space via a lightweight transformer adapter (PeCA) attached to existing text encoders. It repurposes coreset sampling from computational geometry for user profiling and adapts the classifier-free guidance principle into a condition-level weighting scheme, but does not build on any single prior architecture. The key conceptual departure is treating the text encoder's output embeddings as the unit of personalization rather than the prompt string, thereby sidestepping token-length constraints that limit all prior prompt-rewriting or keyword-extraction approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DrUM offers a practical, plug-and-play personalization layer for widely deployed T2I models (SD V1–V3, FLUX) without fine-tuning or LLM API calls, lowering the barrier for individual users. However, performance degrades on complex, information-dense prompts (MovieLens product descriptions), and the small cross-attention depth may not capture all preference patterns. Open questions include whether deeper adapter architectures or token-level refinements can close the gap on complex inputs, and whether the condition-level formulation generalizes beyond text encoders to other conditioning modalities.
