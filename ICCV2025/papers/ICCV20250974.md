## Problem

Existing content-style personalization methods like ZipLoRA treat content and style as independent entities, merging LoRAs via output-dimension masks that assume orthogonality. In reality, content and style are intertwined, and using a fixed rank for both adapters across all UNet layers is suboptimal. The paper asks how to enable adaptive rank flexibility, reduce fine-tuning costs, and better separate content and style distributions during LoRA merging.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250974/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of content reference images (1–5 per subject) and a single style reference image, each used to train a separate rank-64 LoRA on SDXL. At inference, a text prompt with learned tokens (e.g., 'a <V1> object in <S2> style') is fed to SDXL with the merged LoRA adapter, producing a blended image. The merged adapter requires only 0.07M trainable parameters (vs. 1.33M for ZipLoRA) and 0.35 MB of extra storage, with merging training taking ~5–6 minutes on an A6000.

## Method

DuoLoRA introduces three components for content-style LoRA merging. First, ZipRank learns binary masks in the rank dimension (rather than the output dimension as in ZipLoRA), enabling adaptive rank flexibility per layer while reducing learnable parameters by 19×. Second, layer-prior-informed merging exploits the observation that low-resolution SDXL UNet layers (up_block.2, down_block.2, mid_block; resolution < 32) encode content while high-resolution layers (up_block.1, down_block.1; resolution ≥ 32) encode style, applying a nuclear-norm minimization under an L1 sparsity constraint to enforce Rank(m_content) > Rank(m_style) in content-dominant layers and the reverse in style-dominant layers. Third, the Constyle loss enforces cycle-consistency: style is added to and removed from content images (and vice versa) during merging, minimizing reconstruction MSE to ensure the merged adapter preserves both concepts without cross-contamination, inspired by CycleGAN's domain-translation framework.

## Results

On the Dreambooth + StyleDrop benchmark, DuoLoRA achieves DINO 0.56, CLIP-I 0.69, CLIP-T 0.314, and CSD-s 0.48, outperforming all baselines across every metric and dataset. On the single-image Subjectplop benchmark, DuoLoRA reaches DINO 0.78 and CSD-s 0.40, the highest content and style fidelity scores. Critically, DuoLoRA requires only 0.07M learnable parameters (0.35 MB storage) versus ZipLoRA's 1.33M (6.5 MB), a 19× reduction, while training in 6.38 minutes. A user study with 50 AMT raters (1000 questionnaires) confirms 50% preference for DuoLoRA over all baselines combined.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DuoLoRA outperforms ZipLoRA [29] on every metric across all four datasets (e.g., DINO 0.56 vs 0.53, CSD-s 0.48 vs 0.41 on Dreambooth+StyleDrop) while using 19× fewer learnable parameters (0.07M vs 1.33M). Against B-LoRA [10], DuoLoRA achieves substantially higher scores on all metrics, with the largest gap on style fidelity (CSD-s 0.48 vs 0.28 on Dreambooth+StyleDrop, 0.40 vs 0.32 on Subjectplop). Compared to PairCustomization [20] on their paired-image test set, DuoLoRA wins on all three reported metrics: DINO 0.62 vs 0.56, CLIP-I 0.69 vs 0.65, and CSD-s 0.50 vs 0.47. Naive merging, the training-free baseline, is consistently the weakest method, confirming that learned merging is necessary for effective content-style blending. The ablation in Table 3 further shows that even when DuoLoRA is constrained to the same 1.33M parameter budget as ZipLoRA, it still achieves higher scores (DINO 0.57, CLIP-I 0.73, CSD-s 0.50), indicating the gains come from the merging strategy rather than parameter count alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LoRA merging paradigm for content-style personalization, building directly on ZipLoRA's output-dimension masking approach and B-LoRA's layer-specific content/style identification. The key conceptual departure is shifting the mask from the output dimension to the rank dimension (ZipRank), which the authors argue better captures the intertwined (rather than independent) nature of content and style, while also enabling adaptive rank flexibility. The Constyle loss repurposes CycleGAN's domain-translation cycle-consistency principle to regularize the merging process, and the layer-prior rank constraints formalize the SDXL architectural observation that low-resolution layers encode content while high-resolution layers encode style into a nuclear-norm minimization problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 19× reduction in trainable parameters (0.07M vs. 1.33M for ZipLoRA) while matching or exceeding performance across four benchmarks makes this a practically attractive option for parameter-efficient personalization pipelines. The layer-prior observation, though architecturally intuitive, provides a principled initialization and constraint strategy that could inform future LoRA composition methods beyond content-style merging. A notable limitation is that the approach currently handles only two concepts jointly, and the multi-concept extension relies on naive arithmetic averaging rather than learned composition, leaving open the question of how to scale the cycle-consistency framework to more than two concepts. Generalization beyond SDXL is demonstrated on SSD-1B and Segmind-Vega, but the layer-prior thresholds (resolution < 32 vs. ≥ 32) are SDXL-specific and would need re-derivation for other architectures.
