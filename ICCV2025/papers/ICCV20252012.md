## Problem

Existing dense feature matching methods depend on scarce, clean multi-view image collections that are expensive to capture and limit generalization to diverse real-world conditions. Additionally, standard feature encoders (e.g., DINOv2) are pretrained on single-view 2D images and lack 3D geometric awareness, making them struggle with viewpoint changes, occlusions, and geometric distortions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252012/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images at 584×584 resolution and outputs a dense pixel-wise warp field W (predicting the correspondence for every pixel) and a certainty map σ indicating match confidence. The shared encoder (DINOv2-based) extracts per-image features, and the decoder jointly predicts both outputs. During training, input pairs are synthesized by lifting single-view images to 3D via monocular depth estimation, warping, inpainting, and re-rendering with varied lighting—no actual multi-view captures are required.

## Method

L2M is a two-stage framework that lifts single-view 2D images into 3D space to generate training data and inject geometric knowledge. In Stage 1, a 3D-aware encoder is learned by building 3D feature Gaussians (extending the Fit3D formulation) from multi-view images synthesized via depth estimation and warping; the encoder is trained with a pixel-wise L1 loss against features rendered from the Gaussians via differentiable α-blending, embedding multi-view 3D geometry into the feature space. In Stage 2, a robust decoder is trained on large-scale synthetic pairs generated by reconstructing 3D meshes (Poisson Surface Reconstruction) from monocular depth and re-rendering with a physics engine under varied viewpoints and lighting conditions, drawing from 12 diverse real-world datasets (COCO, Cityscapes, KITTI, NuScenes, etc.) totaling over 400K images. This eliminates the need for calibrated multi-view captures while providing the decoder with far greater scene, viewpoint, and lighting diversity than traditional SfM-based datasets.

## Results

On the Zero-shot Evaluation Benchmark (ZEB) comprising 12 datasets, L2M achieves a mean AUC of 51.8% for pose error under 5°, outperforming the previous best dense method GIM (51.2%) and RoMa-outdoor (48.8%). On the in-domain MegaDepth-1500 test set, L2M reaches 63.1% AUC@5°, 77.1% AUC@10°, and 86.6% AUC@20°, surpassing RoMa (62.6/76.7/86.3). For cross-modal RGB-IR matching on METU-VisTIR, L2M achieves 30.13% AUC@5° and 71.80% AUC@20°, substantially exceeding the best prior dense method RoMa (25.61/68.37) and the dedicated cross-modal method XoFTR (18.47/51.50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

L2M achieves the highest mean score (51.8%) on the ZEB benchmark, edging out GIM (51.2%) by 0.6 points and RoMa-outdoor (48.8%) by 3.0 points. Against GIM, L2M wins on 9 of 12 individual datasets (e.g., ETO 83.7 vs 76.7, MUL 61.7 vs 56.6, SCE 38.5 vs 32.2) but trails on GL3 (51.5 vs 63.3), BLE (46.0 vs 53.0), and GTA (60.6 vs 61.6). Compared to RoMa-outdoor, L2M leads on every real-world dataset (e.g., KIT 44.9 vs 39.9, WEA 36.0 vs 34.4) and on all synthetic datasets except GTA. On the in-domain MegaDepth-1500 test, L2M (63.1% AUC@5°) narrowly beats RoMa (62.6%) and GIM (60.7%). In the cross-modal RGB-IR setting (METU-VisTIR), L2M (30.13% AUC@5°) substantially outperforms RoMa (25.61%) and the dedicated cross-modal XoFTR (18.47%), with a 20.3-point gap over XoFTR at the 20° threshold (71.80% vs 51.50%). All sparse (SuperGlue, LightGlue) and semi-dense (LoFTR, ELoFTR) baselines are consistently outperformed by 15–40 points across the board.

## Relation to Prior Work

L2M extends the 3D-aware encoder learning paradigm introduced by Fit3D [60], which fine-tunes 2D feature representations using multi-view 3D Gaussians, but removes the dependency on hard-to-collect multi-view image collections by lifting single 2D images to 3D space via monocular depth estimation. For the decoder, it departs from prior dense matchers (DKM, RoMa, GIM) that rely on curated multi-view datasets like MegaDepth or BlendedMVS, instead generating large-scale training pairs through novel-view rendering and re-lighting of reconstructed meshes. The two-stage design—first injecting 3D geometry into the encoder via 3D feature Gaussians, then training the decoder on diverse synthetic pairs—repurposes the 3D Gaussian Splatting and monocular depth estimation toolkits (Depth Anything V2, Poisson Surface Reconstruction) in a way not previously applied to feature matching training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that single-view images lifted to 3D can replace expensive multi-view collections for training robust feature matchers, L2M offers a practical path to scaling feature matching training data without the logistical burden of multi-camera capture. The strong cross-modal (RGB-IR) generalization suggests the 3D-aware encoder captures geometry-invariant structure rather than surface appearance. However, the pipeline's quality is bounded by monocular depth estimation accuracy and inpainting fidelity, and the modest ~0.6-point gain over GIM on the main ZEB benchmark (51.8 vs 51.2) raises questions about whether the added 3D reconstruction complexity is always justified over simpler data augmentation strategies. Open questions include how the method handles dynamic scenes, whether the 3D-aware encoder transfer is architecture-agnostic, and whether the depth errors from single-view estimation systematically bias the learned correspondences.
