## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary semantic segmentation (OVSS) models can segment objects using arbitrary text descriptions but cannot distinguish a user's specific personal concept (e.g., 'my mug cup') among multiple objects of the same class. Few-shot segmentation methods learn specific visual concepts from a few image-mask pairs but do not support open-vocabulary prediction. This paper introduces the task of personalized OVSS, which requires recognizing a personal visual concept while maintaining the original OVSS capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251176/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, a set of open-vocabulary text prompts, and K (1, 3, or 5) image-mask pairs exemplifying the personal concept. It produces per-pixel segmentation masks at H/16 × W/16 resolution (resized to original image size) that include both standard OVSS class predictions and a dedicated personalized concept label. The plug-in adds one extra text embedding and one extra mask channel to the base OVSS model, so the output vocabulary size increases by one relative to the original model.

## Method

The method is a plug-in for off-the-shelf OVSS models (SAN, ODISE) with three components. First, a learnable text embedding T_per is initialized from the class name and concatenated to the existing text embeddings, trained via standard OVSS losses (dice, BCE, cross-entropy). Second, a negative mask proposal is introduced: a learnable linear combination of existing mask embeddings (Z_neg) is supervised to assign equal probability to all non-personal words, and a learnable convolutional layer on mask proposals (M_neg) is supervised with the complement of the ground-truth personal mask (1 − M_gt), explicitly teaching the model what is NOT the personal concept to reduce false positives. Third, masked visual features extracted from a pretrained CLIP image encoder (averaged over mask pixels) are interpolated with T_per via a scalar α to enrich the textual representation with visual information. The total loss combines the standard OVSS segmentation loss with weighted negative mask supervision terms, and only T_per, W_M, and W_Z are trained for 200 iterations.

## Results

On the three newly established personalized OVSS benchmarks, the proposed plug-in method substantially improves IoU_per while maintaining mIoU. With SAN as the backbone, average IoU_per improves by 12.48 (FSS^per: 41.08→53.56), 8.65 (CUB^per: 68.25→76.90), and 15.79 (ADE^per: 6.88→22.67) points, while mIoU is preserved or slightly improved (e.g., CUB^per mIoU: 77.32→77.83). With ODISE, gains are even larger in relative terms (e.g., CUB^per IoU_per: 1.24→11.24), though absolute values remain lower. The method works with as few as one image-mask pair (K=1), confirming practicality for real-world personalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the OVSS model without personalization (SAN or ODISE using only text prompts like 'black footed albatross'), which the method consistently outperforms on IoU_per across all three datasets while keeping mIoU within ~1–2 points. Against kNN-CLIP [10], a training-free open-vocabulary retrieval method, the proposed approach achieves IoU_per of 76.80 versus 1.27 on CUB^per (K=5), a >60× margin, showing that retrieval-based OVSS cannot capture personal concepts. Compared to state-of-the-art few-shot segmentation methods SEEM [49] (IoU_per 45.54) and SegGPT [36] (IoU_per 47.00) on CUB^per, the proposed method reaches 76.80, a 30+ point advantage. However, SEEM and SegGPT achieve higher IoU_per_recall (90.82 and 93.88 vs. 89.07), indicating they capture the target bird more completely but at the cost of much lower IoU_per_precision (47.64 and 48.56 vs. 84.51), i.e., they misclassify other birds as the personal concept. The ablation (Table 2) confirms that the negative mask proposal is the key component for precision (74.75→80.07), while visual embedding injection alone actually degrades IoU_per (69.70→65.94) without it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of open-vocabulary semantic segmentation (OVSS) and few-shot/personalized segmentation, explicitly positioning itself as distinct from both. It builds directly on SAN and ODISE as plug-in-compatible base OVSS models, extending their text-embedding and mask-proposal pipelines with a learnable personalized text embedding, a negative mask proposal, and visual embedding injection. The authors contrast their task with few-shot segmentation (which lacks open-vocabulary capability and same-class discrimination), class-incremental learning (which lacks open-vocabulary text grounding), and Yo'LLaVA (personalized VQA rather than segmentation), arguing that no prior work combines arbitrary text prompts with recognition of a specific personal concept among same-class objects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper formalizes a practically motivated task—identifying 'my mug cup' among many mug cups—that is essential for deploying segmentation in personalized robot-assistant or smart-home settings, and provides the first benchmarks (FSS^per, CUB^per, ADE^per) for evaluating it. A key limitation is the mIoU evaluation on FSS^per and CUB^per, which uses the pretrained OVSS model's own predictions as ground truth, creating a circular dependency that may overstate or understate true generalization. Open questions include scaling to multiple simultaneous personal concepts, extending to instance-level or panoptic settings, and whether the negative mask proposal generalizes to more complex same-class distinctions (e.g., different colors or patterns of the same object).
