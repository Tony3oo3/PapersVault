## Problem

Fine-grained segmentation of internal animal textures (fur stripes, spots, skin markings) remains largely unsolved in unsupervised settings, as existing shallow methods (clustering, watershed) are sensitive to lighting and lack structural coherence, while deep methods (SAM, DINO, CLIP-Seg) prioritize object-level boundaries over internal pattern detail. Additionally, no public dataset with pixel-accurate pattern annotations exists, making evaluation and progress difficult.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image of an animal as input and produces a binary segmentation mask (pattern vs. background) along with two representative RGB color centroids per local s×s region. Because the framework operates in a zero-shot, per-image optimization setting, no training set is required; the model is independently optimized for each input image over 1000 epochs. The final mask is derived from a confidence map computed via exponential distance to the two local color centroids, thresholded at a user-defined value (default 0.5).

## Method

GloPER couples a U-Net segmentation branch with a local two-color reconstruction branch in a jointly optimized framework. The segmentation branch produces discrete yet differentiable binary masks via Gumbel-Softmax sampling (τ=0.1), while the reconstruction branch predicts two representative colors per local region using adaptive average pooling on encoder features followed by a 1×1 convolution, then reconstructs the image by hard-masked blending of those two colors. The total loss is a per-pixel L2 reconstruction error averaged across two spatial scales (fine k and coarse 2k), which simultaneously preserves texture detail and large-scale structural coherence. After per-image optimization, a confidence-based selection step sorts the two color centroids by intensity (darker = pattern), computes per-pixel exponential distance scores, and thresholds the resulting probability map to yield the final binary mask. This local-reconstruction constraint is the key mechanism that forces segmentation boundaries to align with actual color transitions in the pattern, making the method robust to global illumination shifts and shadows.

## Results

GloPER achieves a mean DICE score of 0.757 across 10 animal species, outperforming the best baseline (K-means, 0.614) by 23% and the average of all five baselines (0.531) by 42.44%. The largest gains appear on Cow (0.964 vs. 0.721 for K-means) and Nyala (0.789 vs. 0.379 for K-means), while the smallest margin is on Cat (0.711 vs. 0.675 for K-means). In the animal ReID experiment, GloPER's binary pattern masks surpass full RGB images on Turtle, confirming that structured segmentation can remove irrelevant background clutter and improve identity discrimination.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GloPER wins on all 10 species in DICE score, with the strongest advantage over SAM (mean 0.457), which collapses to 0.187 on Tiger and 0.185 on Zebra due to its object-centric design. K-means is the closest competitor (mean 0.614) and even edges GloPER on Panda (0.741 vs. 0.788 is still GloPER, but Watershed reaches 0.755 on Panda), yet it remains sensitive to lighting shifts and fails on Nyala (0.379) and Tiger (0.487). CLIP-Seg shows high variance: it reaches 0.772 on Seal but drops to 0.299 on Nyala, reflecting the difficulty of encoding species-specific fur textures in text prompts. Watershed is inconsistent, scoring 0.755 on Panda but only 0.236 on Cow, where smooth color gradients defeat its edge-based mechanism. DINO provides the most stable but weakest performance (range 0.419–0.643), confirming that self-supervised global features alone cannot capture fine-grained local pattern boundaries.

## Relation to Prior Work

GloPER builds on the U-Net encoder-decoder architecture for its segmentation backbone and adopts Gumbel-Softmax for differentiable discrete mask sampling, but its core departure from prior unsupervised segmentation work (SAM, DINO, CLIP-Seg) is the introduction of a local two-color reconstruction loss that constrains each spatial region to a compact color representation. Where prior deep unsupervised methods prioritize global shape consistency and object-level grouping, GloPER explicitly targets fine-grained internal textures by operating on localized patches at multiple scales, preventing global illumination shifts from distorting segmentation. The method also diverges from shallow clustering and edge-detection approaches by learning structured, spatially coherent regions rather than relying on low-level color or gradient cues that fail under lighting variation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in fine-grained pattern segmentation where labeled pixel-accurate annotations are prohibitively expensive, and the curated 10-species dataset (1,000+ images) provides a useful benchmark for this underexplored niche. However, the per-image optimization (1000 epochs per image) and manual kernel-size tuning limit scalability and robustness, and the evaluation relies on a single annotator with acknowledged over-segmentation relative to ground truth. Open questions include whether adaptive kernel selection can eliminate the need for per-species hyperparameter search, whether the two-color assumption generalizes to animals with multi-color or iridescent patterns, and whether the approach extends beyond binary pattern-vs-background to multi-class internal structure segmentation.
