## Problem

Existing video face restoration methods face a fundamental tension: image-based approaches (e.g., CodeFormer) apply discrete codebook lookups independently per frame, causing abrupt latent code changes and perceptual flicker, while general video restoration methods (e.g., BasicVSR++) lack face-specific priors such as codebooks and geometric constraints. This paper addresses the gap by reformulating the discrete VQ codebook representation as a continuous probabilistic distribution, enabling smooth temporal transitions while preserving the rich facial detail prior learned from high-quality portraits.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of degraded 512×512 face video frames (or masked/grayscale variants for inpainting/colorization) and produces temporally coherent restored high-quality frames. At inference, a sliding window of 5 consecutive frames is processed with stride 1, predicting only the center (third) frame per window; boundary frames are replicated for padding. The encoder compresses each frame to a 16×16 spatial grid (H/32 × W/32) with 256-dimensional latent codes, and the codebook contains N ∈ {256, 512, 1024} entries of dimension d=256.

## Method

The core contribution replaces hard VQ quantization with a Dirichlet-distributed continuous relaxation: each spatial location's latent code becomes a convex combination of codebook entries weighted by a Dirichlet-sampled probability vector, parameterized by a spatio-temporal Transformer that alternates spatial and temporal self-attention blocks (H=4, 8 heads) over a 5-frame window. Training maximizes an ELBO objective where the KL term regularizes the predicted Dirichlet parameters toward a learnable prior (α=1.0), and the reconstruction term uses a Laplacian likelihood estimated via Monte Carlo sampling from the Dirichlet, supplemented by LPIPS perceptual loss. The codebook is initialized from CodeFormer pretrained on FFHQ and kept frozen, while the encoder, decoder, and temporal Transformer are trained with progressive unfreezing. This variational formulation allows the model to smoothly interpolate between codebook entries across frames, directly mitigating the flicker artifacts inherent to per-frame discrete quantization.

## Results

On the VFHQ-Test benchmark for blind video face restoration, the proposed method achieves 29.099 dB PSNR, 0.831 SSIM, and 0.246 LPIPS, outperforming all baselines on every metric except FVD. Compared to the strongest video-specific baseline KEEP, the method improves PSNR by 1.29 dB, reduces LPIPS by 8.2%, and lowers the temporal consistency metric TLME from 1.156 to 1.091 (a 5.6% reduction). On the secondary tasks of video face inpainting and colorization, the method also achieves the best LPIPS (0.069/0.141), AKD (1.379/0.907), and FVD (147.7/155.7) among all compared approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is compared against eight baselines spanning three categories: image-based face restoration (GPEN, GFPGAN, CodeFormer), general video restoration (RealBasicVSR, BasicVSR++), and video-specific face restoration (PGTFormer, KEEP). Against the two strongest video face restoration baselines, the method wins on PSNR (+1.27 dB over PGTFormer, +1.29 dB over KEEP), LPIPS (0.246 vs. 0.292/0.268), AKD (2.093 vs. 2.566/2.466), and TLME (1.091 vs. 1.333/1.156), but is marginally worse on FVD (336.015 vs. PGTFormer's 332.340). Against image-based methods applied frame-by-frame, the gains are larger: CodeFormer drops to 26.064 dB PSNR and 1.530 TLME when naively extended to video, while the proposed method reaches 29.099 dB and 1.091 TLME. General video restoration methods (BasicVSR++, RealBasicVSR) suffer the most, with FVD values of 823.9 and 635.2 respectively, reflecting their lack of face-specific priors. On the inpainting and colorization tasks, the method similarly dominates, reducing FVD from 212.1 (GPEN) to 155.7 for colorization and from 224.3 (CodeFormer) to 147.7 for inpainting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VQ-AE-based face restoration paradigm established by CodeFormer (Zhou et al., NeurIPS 2022) and VQ-GAN (Esser et al., CVPR 2021) from single-frame image restoration to multi-frame video restoration. The key conceptual departure is replacing hard discrete codebook lookup with a Dirichlet-distributed continuous relaxation, where each spatial location's latent code becomes a probabilistic convex combination of codebook entries rather than a single nearest-neighbor selection. A spatio-temporal Transformer with alternating spatial and temporal attention blocks is introduced to model inter-frame dependencies and predict the Dirichlet parameters, bridging the gap between the image-pretrained codebook prior and the temporal coherence required for video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides a principled mechanism for transferring powerful image-pretrained facial codebook priors into video restoration while mitigating the flicker artifacts that plague per-frame discrete quantization. The Dirichlet relaxation is a clean, generalizable idea that could apply to other VQ-based video generation or restoration tasks beyond faces. However, the evaluation is limited to the VFHQ benchmark at 512×512, the codebook is frozen during video training, and the FVD metric is not the best among compared methods, suggesting the temporal modeling could be further refined. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer sequences, higher resolutions, or non-facial video restoration, and whether the ELBO formulation is truly necessary compared to simpler temporal smoothing of code assignments.
