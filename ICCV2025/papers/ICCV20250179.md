## Problem

Open-set 3D object retrieval requires generalizing to unseen categories, but existing methods overfit to limited 3D training data and rely on complex multi-modal pipelines (voxels, point clouds, multi-view images) or even test data for training. The paper asks whether a pretrained vision-language model (CLIP) combined with a multi-modal LLM can produce sufficiently generalized 3D representations using only multi-view images, without test data or additional 3D modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250179/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each 3D object is rendered into 24 grayscale multi-view images of 256×256 pixels, which are the sole 3D input (no point clouds or voxels). At inference, an MLLM (InternVL-4B) generates a one-sentence description from those views, which is encoded by the adapted CLIP text encoder. The final output is a single d-dimensional descriptor per object, obtained by fusing the mean-pooled visual embedding with the textual embedding via tanh(g + α·f_t), and retrieval is performed by cosine similarity.

## Method

DAC follows a three-step pipeline: (1) Describe — an MLLM generates category-level descriptions for training (to better align with CLIP's contrastive pretraining objective) and object-level descriptions at inference (as external semantic hints); (2) Adapt — an Additive-Bias LoRA (AB-LoRA) is inserted into CLIP's self-attention weights (W_query, W_key, W_value) in both text and visual encoders, where the added bias term Φ breaks the tight gradient linkage between the low-rank update and seen-category inputs, reducing overfitting; (3) Combine — the adapted CLIP encodes both the multi-view images (mean-pooled) and the MLLM description, and the two embeddings are fused by weighted addition followed by tanh normalization. Training uses cross-entropy loss between the global visual descriptor and the corresponding category text embedding, with only the AB-LoRA parameters (rank r=8) and bias updated while the base CLIP weights remain frozen.

## Results

In the open-set setup, DAC with CLIP ViT-L/14 achieves mAP of 57.80, 65.83, 68.98, and 70.74 on OS-ESB-core, OS-NTU-core, OS-MN40-core, and OS-ABO-core respectively, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art HGM²R by an average of +10.01% mAP across the four benchmarks. The largest gain is on OS-NTU-core (+20.95% mAP over HGM²R), while DAC uses only multi-view images (no point clouds or voxels) and does not require test data for training. In the zero-shot setup, DAC (ViT-L/14) also leads on three of four datasets, reaching 61.33 mAP on OS-NTU-core and 59.04 mAP on OS-ABO-core.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAC (ViT-L/14) decisively outperforms the previous state-of-the-art HGM²R [15] on all four open-set benchmarks by +6.06, +20.95, +4.78, and +7.35 mAP respectively, while using only multi-view images (vs. HGM²R's point clouds + voxels + images) and without incorporating test data for training. Among the zero-shot vision-language baselines (OpenShape [36], ULIP-2 [62], Uni3D [68]), DAC leads on three of four datasets; MV-CLIP [50] achieves higher mAP on OS-MN40-core in zero-shot (63.74 vs. 59.77) but is inapplicable to open-set retrieval because its view-selection step requires ground-truth category labels. All closed-set 3DOR methods extended to the open-set setting (TCL [20], SDML [25], CMCL [28], MMSAE [59], MCWSA [66], PROSER [67], InfoNCE [43]) are substantially outperformed, with the largest gaps on OS-NTU-core where DAC (L/14) reaches 65.83 mAP versus 41.08 for the best among them (CMCL). In the cross-dataset transfer (MN40→ABO), DAC (L/14) achieves 69.86 mAP, exceeding HGM²R by +12.31 mAP, confirming strong domain-shift robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on CLIP's contrastive vision-language pretraining and LoRA's parameter-efficient fine-tuning, but departs from prior open-set 3DOR methods (HGM²R, TCL, CMCL) that rely on multi-modal inputs (voxels, point clouds, multi-view images) and often incorporate test data for training. The key conceptual departure is the first synergistic combination of a discriminative VLM (CLIP) with a generative MLLM (InternVL) for 3D retrieval, where the MLLM serves dual roles: enriching category descriptions during training and providing external textual hints at inference. The introduction of an additive bias into LoRA (AB-LoRA) represents a small but principled modification to the standard LoRA formulation, motivated by the observation that ΔW directly accumulates seen-category information and overfits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple multi-view-image-only pipeline with CLIP + MLLM can surpass complex multi-modal methods by over 10% mAP on average, suggesting that the field may have over-invested in multi-modal 3D representations when strong 2D pretraining plus textual reasoning suffices. The AB-LoRA finding—that a single bias vector meaningfully improves open-set generalization—is a practically useful and broadly applicable insight for parameter-efficient fine-tuning in data-scarce regimes. Limitations include the inference-time cost of MLLM calls and the failure of global-only fusion on hard category pairs (e.g., wardrobe vs. bookshelf), pointing toward future work on fine-grained features and MLLM-free training strategies.
