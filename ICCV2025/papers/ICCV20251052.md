## Problem

Existing gaze redirection methods based on NeRF are computationally expensive and produce results lacking fine facial details, while recent 3DGS-based head avatar methods ignore precise gaze direction control and cannot generalize across different subjects. No prior work has integrated 3D Gaussian Splatting into the gaze redirection task, leaving a gap between fast 3DGS rendering and accurate, generalizable eye manipulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251052/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB face image along with a target gaze direction (normalized to pitch-yaw angles in the head coordinate system), head rotation, and expression latent codes extracted via 3D face tracking. It produces a gaze-redirected head image at the same resolution, with the face and eye regions rendered separately before compositing. The model is trained on 14.4K images (10 frames × 18 camera views per subject) from ETH-XGaze and evaluated on person-specific test sets of 15 subjects with 200 labeled images each.

## Method

GazeGaussian uses a two-stream 3DGS architecture that decouples face and eye regions, initialized from a DMTet SDF neutral mesh partitioned by 3D landmarks. The face branch applies expression and head-pose MLPs with distance-based weighting to 3D landmarks, while the eye branch introduces a novel Gaussian Eye Rotation Representation that explicitly rotates eye Gaussians via a gaze-conditioned MLP (G_μ^e) rather than implicitly warping feature maps as in GazeNeRF. An Expression-Guided Neural Renderer (EGNR), a UNet-like module, injects expression latent codes into its bottleneck via cross-attention to preserve subject-specific identity across unseen subjects. Training is supervised with L1, SSIM, and LPIPS losses on rendered face-only, eye, and head images plus their feature maps, combined with an angular gaze redirection loss computed by a frozen pre-trained gaze estimator.

## Results

On the ETH-XGaze within-dataset benchmark, GazeGaussian achieves a gaze redirection error of 6.62° and head pose error of 2.13°, outperforming the previous SOTA GazeNeRF (6.94° / 3.47°) and substantially surpassing Gaussian Head Avatar (30.96° / 13.56°). Image quality metrics also improve markedly: SSIM 0.823, PSNR 18.73, LPIPS 0.216, and FID 41.97, compared to GazeNeRF's 0.733 / 15.45 / 0.291 / 81.82. Identity preservation (FaceX-Zoo score) reaches 67.75, nearly 1.5× that of GazeNeRF (45.21), and rendering speed is 74 FPS, roughly 1.6× faster than GazeNeRF (46 FPS). Cross-dataset evaluation on ColumbiaGaze, MPIIFaceGaze, and GazeCapture confirms consistent superiority in gaze error, LPIPS, and identity similarity across all three unseen datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest competitor GazeNeRF (the previous SOTA NeRF-based gaze redirection method), GazeGaussian reduces gaze error from 6.94° to 6.62° and head pose error from 3.47° to 2.13°, while nearly halving FID (41.97 vs 81.82) and boosting identity similarity by 50% (67.75 vs 45.21); rendering speed also improves from 46 to 74 FPS. Against Gaussian Head Avatar, the strongest 3DGS-based head synthesis method, GazeGaussian achieves dramatically lower gaze error (6.62° vs 30.96°) and head pose error (2.13° vs 13.56°), reflecting GHA's lack of dedicated gaze control; GHA is faster (91 vs 74 FPS) but produces far less accurate and less identity-preserving results. Against HeadNeRF and STED, GazeGaussian dominates on every metric, with gaze errors of 6.62° versus 12.12° and 16.22° respectively. In cross-dataset evaluation (ColumbiaGaze, MPIIFaceGaze, GazeCapture), GazeGaussian maintains its lead over all four baselines on gaze error, LPIPS, and identity similarity, confirming that the expression-guided neural renderer provides genuine generalization rather than dataset-specific overfitting.

## Relation to Prior Work

GazeGaussian sits at the intersection of two active research threads: 3DGS-based head avatar synthesis (Gaussian Head Avatar, GaussianAvatars) and NeRF-based gaze redirection (GazeNeRF, NeRF-Gaze). It inherits the two-stream face/eye decoupling concept from GazeNeRF and the canonical-mesh initialization and deformation MLP design from Gaussian Head Avatar, but departs from both in critical ways: it replaces GazeNeRF's implicit feature-map rotation with an explicit Gaussian-position rotation for the eye branch, and it extends Gaussian Head Avatar's single-subject scope to cross-subject generalization via an expression-guided neural renderer. The paper is explicitly positioned as the first integration of 3DGS into the gaze redirection task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that 3DGS can match or exceed NeRF-based gaze redirection accuracy at roughly 1.6× the rendering speed (74 vs 46 FPS), this work makes high-fidelity gaze augmentation practical for downstream gaze estimation pipelines, where data volume and generation throughput matter. The expression-guided renderer addresses a real gap in 3DGS head avatars (poor cross-subject identity preservation), and the ablation confirms that all three components (two-stream, explicit eye rotation, expression guidance) contribute meaningfully. Open questions include whether the per-subject training requirement can be eliminated entirely, how the method handles occlusions or extreme expressions beyond the training distribution, and whether the two-stream split is optimal compared to learned soft region boundaries.
