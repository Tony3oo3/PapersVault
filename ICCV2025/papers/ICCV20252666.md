## Problem

Existing quantization-aware training (QAT) methods converge to suboptimal solutions under low-bit quantization because weights rarely cross quantization thresholds (below 0.5% frequency), restricting exploration to a tiny subset of the quantization solution space. Prior work treated the resulting weight oscillation as harmful and suppressed it throughout training, but this paper argues that oscillation is actually a necessary exploration mechanism that, when suppressed too early, prevents the model from escaping poor quantization bin assignments.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained full-precision CNN and Vision Transformer models (ResNet-18/34/50, MobileNet-V2, DeiT-T/S, Swin-T) and produces low-bit quantized versions at 2-bit, 3-bit, or 4-bit weight and activation precision. Inputs are standard RGB images from ImageNet (224×224), and outputs are class labels over 1000 categories. The quantization is applied to all convolutional and fully connected layers except the first and last, with patch embedding and classification layers in ViTs kept at 8-bit.

## Method

AOQ introduces a three-stage training strategy that strategically leverages weight oscillation. In Stage 1 (1/5 of epochs), quantization threshold intervals (s_th) and level intervals (s_le) are manually reduced to 30–35% of their initial values, forcing more weights to oscillate near thresholds and explore diverse quantization bin assignments. In Stage 2 (2/5 of epochs), thresholds are fixed while quantization levels become learnable parameters optimized directly with the task loss via a decoupled quantizer (Eq. 6–7), which also introduces differentiability for clipped weights to avoid the zero-gradient problem of standard STE. In Stage 3 (2/5 of epochs), Oscillation Dampening regularization is added to the loss to suppress residual oscillation and stabilize convergence. The key architectural difference from LSQ is the decoupling of thresholds and levels: a single scaling factor in LSQ jointly determines both, causing inaccurate gradient updates, whereas AOQ independently controls thresholds (manually) and levels (learned), enabling more faithful gradient flow aligned with task loss.

## Results

On ImageNet with 2-bit weight/activation quantization (W2A2), AOQ achieves 70.3% top-1 accuracy on ResNet-18 (FP: 71.8%), 73.9% on ResNet-34 (FP: 74.9%), and 76.4% on ResNet-50 (FP: 77.0%) when combined with N2UQ activation quantization, reducing the gap to full-precision to 1.5%, 1.0%, and 0.6% respectively. On Vision Transformers, LSQ+AOQ reaches 66.5% on DeiT-T (FP: 72.1%), 76.7% on DeiT-S (FP: 79.9%), and 78.9% on Swin-T (FP: 81.2%) at W2A2, outperforming the prior SOTA OFQ by 2.2%, 1.0%, and 0.4% respectively. The method consistently improves over its base quantization framework (LSQ or N2UQ) by 0.4%–2.6% across all tested architectures and bit-widths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AOQ outperforms all 11 baselines across every tested architecture and bit-width. Against the strongest prior QAT methods, N2UQ+AOQ improves over N2UQ [32] by 0.9% (ResNet-18 W2A2: 70.3 vs 69.4), 0.6% (ResNet-34: 73.9 vs 73.3), and 0.6% (ResNet-50: 76.4 vs 75.8), with gains widening at lower bit-widths. On MobileNet-V2, N2UQ+AOQ achieves 69.9% at W3A3, surpassing LSQ+Dampen [36] (67.8%) and LSQ+BR [14] (67.4%) by 2.1% and 2.5% respectively. On Vision Transformers at W2A2, LSQ+AOQ beats OFQ [30] (the prior SOTA) by 2.2% on DeiT-T (66.5 vs 64.3), 1.0% on DeiT-S (76.7 vs 75.7), and 0.4% on Swin-T (78.9 vs 78.5), while at W3A3 the gap narrows to a near-tie (72.3 vs 72.7 on DeiT-T, 79.8 vs 79.6 on DeiT-S, 81.2 vs 81.1 on Swin-T). AOQ also substantially outperforms earlier methods: LSQ [12] is beaten by 12.0% on DeiT-T W2A2 (66.5 vs 54.5), and QViT* [28] by 16.1% (66.5 vs 50.4). The advantage is most pronounced at 2-bit where the expanded solution space provides the greatest benefit, and diminishes at 4-bit where the quantization constraint is less severe.

## Relation to Prior Work

AOQ builds directly on LSQ's learnable scaling factor framework and incorporates Oscillation Dampening (Nagel et al., ICML 2022) as its final training stage, but departs fundamentally from the prevailing view that weight oscillation is purely harmful. While OFQ (Liu et al., ICML 2023) and Nagel et al. treat oscillation as a pathology to suppress throughout training, AOQ reframes it as a beneficial exploration mechanism in early stages, drawing an analogy to branch-and-bound depth-first search in mixed-integer programming. The decoupling of quantization thresholds from levels extends the non-uniform quantization ideas in N2UQ but applies them to a three-stage schedule that first narrows thresholds to induce oscillation, then learns levels, and finally damps oscillation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that existing QAT methods explore less than 0.5% of the quantization solution space provides a concrete diagnostic for why low-bit quantization underperforms, and the three-stage oscillation strategy offers a practical, plug-in improvement that consistently narrows the accuracy gap to full-precision models (e.g., 0.6% gap for 2-bit ResNet-50). The approach is architecture-agnostic (CNNs and ViTs) and composable with existing activation quantization methods (LSQ, N2UQ), making it broadly applicable. Open questions include whether the benefits persist without knowledge distillation, whether the fixed 1/5–2/5–2/5 stage schedule is optimal, and whether the oscillation-expansion principle transfers to other discrete optimization settings beyond quantization.
