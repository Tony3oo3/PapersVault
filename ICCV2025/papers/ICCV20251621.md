## Problem

Existing diffusion-based articulated object generators such as NAP produce objects without conditioning on sensor measurements or enforcing physical constraints, so generated parts can penetrate each other and shapes may not match observed geometry. No prior method combines category-aware generation, partial point cloud alignment, and physical plausibility guidance while remaining agnostic to the articulation graph structure (number of parts and joint connectivity).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251621/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a partial point cloud of 1000 points sampled from a single rendered view (elevation 30°–80°, azimuth 120°–240°) in object coordinates, optionally augmented with a known object category label. It outputs a full articulation graph of up to 8 nodes (parts) with edges encoding joint Plücker coordinates and state ranges, part shapes as 128-dim SDF latent codes decoded into meshes via marching cubes, and 3D translations for each part. The graph topology is not specified as input; the model must infer which parts exist and how they are connected.

## Method

PhysNAP extends the NAP diffusion model (a DDPM over articulation graph attributes processed by an attentional GNN) in two ways: it adds a learnable category embedding to the noise-prediction network, and it applies training-free loss-guided sampling during the last 500 of 1000 reverse diffusion steps. Three differentiable losses guide the denoising: (1) a point cloud alignment loss that computes soft EM-style correspondences between observed points and predicted SDF part surfaces, (2) a penetration loss that measures SDF intersection volume between all part pairs in the zero-articulation state using a fixed-budget 3D grid, and (3) a mobility loss that evaluates SDF penetration between graph-adjacent parts at randomly sampled articulation states. Following DPS, the negative gradient of the combined loss with respect to the current latent x_t is added to the denoising update, making all generated properties (node/edge existence, shape latents, translations, joint parameters) adjustable to satisfy the constraints.

## Results

On PartNet-Mobility, PhysNAP with all guidance losses (pc+pen+mob) and no category conditioning reduces point cloud alignment error E_pc from 0.0564 (NAP baseline) to 0.0024 and D_pc from 0.2063 to 0.0705, while driving penetration error E_pen to 0.0000 and mobility error E_mob to 0.0003. The category-aware variant further improves E_pc to 0.0012 and D_pc to 0.0483. However, all guided variants suffer degraded generative metrics: MMD increases from 0.0915 to 0.1435 (no cat) or 0.1970 (cat), and 1-NNA rises from 0.9440 to 0.9547 or 0.9774, reflecting reduced diversity due to the guidance constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is unmodified NAP [10] (unconditional, unguided diffusion). PhysNAP with all three guidance losses (pc+pen+mob) dramatically outperforms NAP on every guidance metric: E_pc drops by ~23× (0.0564→0.0024), D_pc by ~3× (0.2063→0.0705), E_pen by 35× (0.0035→0.0000), and E_mob by 11× (0.0033→0.0003). However, this comes at a clear cost to generative quality: MMD worsens from 0.0915 to 0.1435 and 1-NNA rises from 0.9440 to 0.9547, indicating reduced diversity. The category-aware variant further improves alignment (E_pc=0.0012, D_pc=0.0483) but degrades generative metrics more substantially (MMD=0.1970, 1-NNA=0.9774), as the category constraint narrows the space of plausible outputs. The paper explicitly frames this as a tradeoff: stronger guidance yields better constraint satisfaction at the expense of generative variety, and the user must choose the appropriate balance depending on whether alignment or diversity is prioritized.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhysNAP extends NAP, an unconditional diffusion model that generates articulation graphs with SDF-encoded part shapes, by adding training-free loss guidance during the reverse diffusion process. The guidance framework follows the DPS/loss-guided diffusion paradigm, but the specific loss terms—SDF-based inter-part penetration, mobility at sampled articulation states, and soft-correspondence point cloud alignment—are novel for the articulated object setting. The approach is most closely related to PhysPart, which also uses SDF penetration and mobility losses, but PhysNAP generalizes by not requiring the articulation graph structure as input and operating on partial point clouds rather than a full base point cloud. Unlike CAGE or MIDGaRD, which condition on category or image features but require the graph topology, PhysNAP generates the graph structure end-to-end while still supporting category conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PhysNAP provides the first diffusion-based framework that simultaneously handles point cloud alignment, physical plausibility, and category conditioning for articulated objects without assuming the articulation graph structure, making it applicable to real-world perception where only partial views and possibly a category label are available. The demonstrated tradeoff between guidance compliance and generative diversity is an important practical consideration for downstream use in robotics or AR, where one must balance fidelity to observations against the richness of the generated object space. Key limitations include the assumption of known sensor pose relative to the object frame, the ~2-minute per-sample generation time, and the quality ceiling imposed by the SDF decoder. Open questions include whether the pose assumption can be relaxed with learned pose estimation, whether faster samplers (DDIM) preserve guidance quality, and whether image-based conditioning can further improve reconstruction fidelity.
