## Problem

Reflective surface reconstruction requires accurate separation of specular and diffuse components, but existing SDF-based methods (NeRO, PANDORA, NeRSP) that handle this well take 8–11 hours per object, while fast 3DGS-based methods produce poor geometry on reflective materials. Polarimetric information provides strong geometric constraints via the pBRDF model, yet no prior work has integrated it into the 3DGS framework, leaving a gap between reconstruction speed and quality for reflective surfaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252135/cropped_page0_idx5.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view polarized images captured in a single shot at four angles (0°, 45°, 90°, 135°), along with per-view object masks and camera poses, and converts them into Stokes vectors (s₀, s₁, s₂) for supervision. It outputs a 3D Gaussian surfel representation from which a mesh is extracted via Poisson surface reconstruction, alongside per-view diffuse and specular Stokes vectors and an estimated environment light cubemap. The full training pipeline completes in approximately 7 minutes on a single RTX 4090, compared to 8–11 hours for SDF-based baselines.

## Method

PolGS builds on Gaussian Surfels (2D Gaussian ellipsoids with explicit surface normals) and adds a Cubemap Encoder (borrowed from 3DGS-DR) to model the specular reflection, while setting spherical harmonic coefficients to zero-order so the rendered diffuse color is view-independent. The key mechanism is a pBRDF-based rendering equation that decomposes the predicted Stokes vector into diffuse and specular terms, each modulated by Fresnel transmission/reflection coefficients and the surface normal's azimuth angle; the s₁ and s₂ channels of this decomposition are supervised against ground-truth polarization, providing a geometric prior that directly constrains surface normals. Training uses a warm-up schedule where the polarimetric loss and deferred specular rendering are activated only after 1000 iterations, combined with mask, opacity, and depth-normal consistency losses. This polarimetric constraint is particularly effective on texture-less reflective surfaces where RGB photometric cues alone leave shape ambiguities unresolved.

## Results

On the combined SMVP3D and RMVP3D benchmarks, PolGS achieves a mean MAE of 14.35° and mean Chamfer Distance of 7.57 mm, completing reconstruction in 0.1 h (7 minutes). This is the best result among all 3DGS-based methods, outperforming 3DGS-DR (MAE 19.76°, CD 13.32 mm) and Gaussian Surfels (MAE 18.56°, CD 10.30 mm) by a wide margin. Compared to the strongest SDF-based baseline NeRO (MAE 8.27°, CD 4.10 mm), PolGS is approximately 80× faster while remaining within a reasonable accuracy gap. The ablation shows that removing the polarimetric loss (L_pol) degrades MAE from 14.35° to 14.89° and CD from 7.57 mm to 7.80 mm, confirming the contribution of polarization cues.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among 3DGS-based methods, PolGS clearly wins on both MAE and CD: it reduces mean MAE by 5.4° over 3DGS-DR (19.76°→14.35°) and by 4.2° over Gaussian Surfels (18.56°→14.35°), while cutting mean CD from 13.32 mm to 7.57 mm. Against SDF-based baselines, PolGS does not match the best accuracy (NeRO achieves 8.27° MAE and 4.10 mm CD) but comes closest to PANDORA (13.54° / 8.17 mm) and NeRSP (12.05° / 5.39 mm) while being 80–110× faster (0.1 h vs. 8–11 h). On texture-less objects like DAVID, PolGS narrows the gap to SDF methods substantially (13.99° MAE vs. NeRO's 7.64°), whereas on texture-rich objects like HEDGEHOG the gap is smaller (10.83° vs. 3.40°). The ablation (PolGS w/o L_pol) confirms that the polarimetric loss contributes a consistent ~0.5° MAE and ~0.2 mm CD improvement, with the largest gains on texture-less surfaces where RGB photometric cues are insufficient. Overall, PolGS establishes the best accuracy–speed trade-off: it is the fastest method in the comparison and the most accurate 3DGS-based approach, approaching SDF-level quality at a fraction of the training time.

## Relation to Prior Work

PolGS sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: polarization-based 3D reconstruction (PANDORA, NeRSP, MVAS), which relies on implicit neural representations and pBRDF constraints, and 3D Gaussian Splatting surface reconstruction (Gaussian Surfels, 3DGS-DR), which offers speed but lacks polarization-aware rendering. The paper's core architectural contribution is the first integration of polarimetric Stokes-vector supervision into a 3DGS pipeline, combining Gaussian Surfels' 2D-surfel geometry with 3DGS-DR's Cubemap Encoder for specular and a pBRDF-based rendering equation that couples diffuse and specular through Fresnel coefficients. It departs from prior 3DGS reflective methods (3DGS-DR, Re-3DGS) by replacing their RGB-only photometric supervision with physics-grounded polarization constraints, and from prior polarization methods by replacing slow MLP-based SDF optimization with explicit Gaussian splatting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 80× speedup over the fastest SDF-based baseline (NeRO, 8 h → 7 min) makes reflective surface reconstruction viable for real-time VR and interactive inverse rendering, a regime previously inaccessible to polarization-guided methods. However, absolute reconstruction accuracy (Chamfer Distance, MAE) still trails the best SDF-based approaches, and the polarimetric constraint's benefit is concentrated on texture-less or sparsely-viewed surfaces; on texture-rich objects with >30 views, RGB photometric cues already suffice, limiting the method's marginal gain. Open questions include whether the zero-order SH restriction (needed for view-independent diffuse color) caps achievable detail, whether the approach extends to unbounded or multi-object scenes, and whether the Cubemap Encoder's lack of explicit roughness estimation becomes a bottleneck for highly glossy or metallic materials.
