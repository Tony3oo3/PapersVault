## Problem

Existing image conditioning methods for diffusion models either introduce substantial parameter overhead through dedicated control modules (e.g., ControlNet adds ~42% parameters) or handle only one class of control task—spatially-aligned (edge, depth) or spatially-unaligned (subject-driven)—but rarely both. Moreover, these approaches are designed for UNet architectures and yield suboptimal results when applied to newer Diffusion Transformer (DiT) models due to fundamental architectural differences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250037/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a condition image (Canny edge map, depth map, binary mask, grayscale color channel, blurred image, or a reference subject photo) at 512×512 resolution along with a text prompt. The condition image is encoded by the base model's own VAE encoder into a 32×32 grid of latent tokens, which are then concatenated with the noisy image tokens and text tokens into a single unified sequence. The output is a 512×512 controlled generated image, with an optional strength factor γ adjustable at inference time to modulate how strongly the image condition influences generation.

## Method

OminiControl achieves minimal overhead (0.1% extra parameters) by reusing the DiT's VAE encoder to project condition images into the same latent space as noisy image tokens, then processing all tokens jointly through the original transformer blocks with only lightweight LoRA fine-tuning. Instead of spatially adding condition features to image features (as in ControlNet), condition tokens are concatenated with noisy image and text tokens in a unified sequence, allowing the multi-modal attention mechanism to discover spatial or semantic relationships without rigid constraints. A dynamic position encoding strategy assigns shared RoPE indices to condition tokens for spatially-aligned tasks (enabling direct spatial correspondence) but shifts them by a fixed offset Δ for non-aligned tasks (preventing spatial overlap that constrains semantic matching). Finally, a structured bias matrix B(γ) is added to the attention logits to enable continuous, inference-time control over conditioning strength without retraining.

## Results

On spatially aligned tasks (Canny, Depth, Mask, Colorization, Deblur), OminiControl achieves the best or near-best results across most quality and controllability metrics, with particularly large gains on Colorization (FID 73 vs. 994 for ControlNet Pro) and Deblurring (FID 62 vs. 338). For subject-driven generation, OminiControl reaches 75.8% modification accuracy and 50.6% identity preservation (averaged over seeds), substantially outperforming IP-Adapter (57.7% and 29.4% respectively). The method requires only 14.5M additional parameters (0.1% of the 12B FLUX.1 base), compared to 3.3B (27.5%) for ControlNet and 918M (7.6%) for IP-Adapter.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ControlNet (SD1.5) and T2I-Adapter (SD1.5), OminiControl consistently outperforms on image quality metrics (SSIM, CLIP-IQA, MUSIQ, PSNR) and controllability, though FID is slightly higher on Canny (0.50 vs. 0.20–0.35), likely due to the stronger FLUX.1 base generating more diverse outputs. Compared to ControlNet Pro (FLUX.1), OminiControl achieves dramatically lower FID on Colorization (73 vs. 994) and Deblurring (62 vs. 338), and superior SSIM, CLIP-IQA, and MUSIQ scores across all tasks. Against FLUX.1 Tools, OminiControl wins on CLIP-IQA, MUSIQ, CLIP Image alignment, and the controllability metric on every task, while FLUX.1 Tools retains a slight FID edge on Canny (0.20 vs. 0.50). For subject-driven generation, OminiControl surpasses IP-Adapter (FLUX) by 18.1 percentage points in modification accuracy (75.8% vs. 57.7%) and IP-Adapter (SD1.5) by 21.2 points in identity preservation (50.6% vs. 29.4%), with best-seed results reaching 90.7% and 82.3% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

OminiControl departs from the dominant paradigm of image conditioning in diffusion models—spatial feature addition (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter) or cross-attention via a separate CLIP encoder (IP-Adapter)—by instead reusing the DiT's own VAE encoder and transformer blocks, concatenating condition tokens directly into the unified multi-modal attention sequence. The dynamic RoPE position strategy (shared indices for aligned tasks, shifted indices for non-aligned tasks) is a novel mechanism not present in prior control methods, which were largely designed for UNet architectures. The Subjects200K dataset extends the data landscape for subject-driven generation by bootstrapping identity-consistent pairs from FLUX.1 itself, moving beyond the identical-pair or small-scale datasets used by IP-Adapter and DreamBooth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 0.1% parameter overhead and single-framework handling of both spatially-aligned and non-aligned tasks make OminiControl a practical, low-cost alternative to the current patchwork of specialized control modules, and the released Subjects200K dataset addresses a real data bottleneck for subject-driven generation. The finding that condition signals must propagate through the full transformer depth (not just early blocks, as in UNet-based ControlNet) is a useful architectural insight for future DiT-based control work. A key open question is the inference cost of the increased token count from sequence concatenation, which the authors acknowledge but do not fully resolve; efficient attention or token pruning strategies for the unified sequence remain to be explored.
