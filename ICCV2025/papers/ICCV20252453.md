## Problem

Existing cross-modal image matching methods are either restricted to specific sensor pairs (e.g., visible-infrared, optical-SAR) or rely on data-driven deep networks that lack generalizability across arbitrary modalities. The paper asks whether a fundamental, theoretically derivable invariant feature exists that is stable across any combination of imaging sensors, enabling universal cross-modal matching without modality-specific training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252453/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a pair of images from any two sensor modalities (RGB, infrared, SAR, depth, map, etc.) with arbitrary rotation (up to 360°) and scale ratio (up to 2×), requiring no modality-specific preprocessing or training data. It outputs RANSAC-filtered matched keypoint pairs, a homography matrix, and per-sample metrics (NCM, DCM, RMSE_CP100, success rate). Because the pipeline is entirely handcrafted, inference runs on CPU without GPU or learned weights, and the same code handles all modality combinations identically.

## Method

The core insight is that local gradient orientations remain invariant across modalities even when intensity, contrast, and texture change dramatically. The Major Orientation Map (MOM) is extracted in three stages: (1) Alienated LogGabor weighting, where odd-LG captures edge responses and a sign-guided even-LG captures contour responses, both projected onto orthogonal directions; (2) multi-scale Averaging Squared Gradient (ASG) pooling to compute robust orientation magnitude and angle per pixel; (3) odd-even coupling that selects the orientation of the stronger response. Built on MOM, the full HOMO pipeline adds DoM (Difference of MOM) for scale-stable keypoint enhancement, a Generalized-Polar (GPolar) descriptor with an Orientation Consistency Discrimination (OCD) module that detects and corrects descriptor reversal caused by the ±π/2 orientation ambiguity, and a Multi-scale Strategy (MsS) that iteratively filters valid matches outward from the finest scale to the coarsest, using each layer's matches as spatial priors for the next.

## Results

HOMO achieves the highest NCM across all cross-modal groups (VIS-IR, VIS-SAR, VIS-Depth, VIS-Map, VIS-Other) among both traditional and deep-learning baselines on the GCZ dataset. Among 10 deep network methods, no single network is effective across every cross-modal group; MINIMA_LG shows the best generalization but still falls short of HOMO overall. Under rotation (30°–180°) and scale (1.2×–2×) perturbations, networks show a substantial performance decline beyond 30° rotation, while HOMO's RMSE_CP100 remains relatively stable in VIS-IR, performing on par with MS-PIIFD. Even after tuning D2-Net and LoFTR directly on the GCZ dataset, neither surpasses HOMO in overall cross-modal matching performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 7 traditional handcrafted methods (SIFT, PSO-SIFT, PIIFD, MS-PIIFD, RIFT/RIFT2, LNIFT, CoFSM), HOMO achieves the highest NCM in every cross-modal group; even the single-scale variant (HOMO-single) outperforms most baselines and surpasses MS-PIIFD in several groups. Against 10 deep network methods (SP+SG/LG, D2-Net, LoFTR, ELoFTR, ASpanFormer, TopicFM, XoFTR, MINIMA_LG, MINIMA_LoFTR, and self-tuned D2-Net/LoFTR), HOMO wins in most groups; XoFTR marginally exceeds HOMO only in low-modal-difference categories (e.g., IRVIS), while MINIMA_LG is the strongest network generalist but still trails HOMO overall. Self-tuned LoFTR on GCZ shows significant improvement and obtains sufficient matches across all groups, yet still does not surpass HOMO. In rotation/scale stress tests, all networks degrade sharply beyond 30° rotation, whereas HOMO maintains stable RMSE_CP100 in VIS-IR comparable to MS-PIIFD and SRIF. The one acknowledged weakness is 3D multi-view capability: in wide-baseline scenes, XoFTR produces more global matches while HOMO's matches concentrate in regions with minimal viewpoint change.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the handcrafted local feature tradition (SIFT, SURF, GLOH) by replacing gradient-based descriptors with a novel Major Orientation Map (MOM) built on LogGabor filtering and Average Squared Gradient weighting, arguing that orientation is a fundamental invariant preserved across arbitrary sensor modalities. It departs from the dominant deep-learning paradigm for cross-modal matching (LoFTR, XoFTR, SuperGlue) by constructing a fully interpretable, non-data-driven pipeline, while also extending prior cross-modal work that was limited to specific sensor pairs (visible-infrared, optical-SAR) toward truly arbitrary modality combinations. The GPolar descriptor inherits its polar structure from GLOH and C-HOG, and the multi-scale strategy follows the SIFT scale-space framework, but the OCD module addressing descriptor reversal under modality-induced orientation boundary crossings is a new contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a purely handcrafted method can outperform ten or more deep network models—including recent transformer-based matchers—on general cross-modal matching challenges the assumption that learned representations are necessary for cross-sensor invariance, and provides a transparent, interpretable alternative for domains where training data is scarce or modalities are unpredictable. The GCZ dataset, the first public benchmark with more than ten modalities of the same scene, fills a critical gap that has constrained progress in this area. Key limitations remain: HOMO's 3D multi-view capability is weaker than transformer-based methods, and extremely sparse textures (e.g., maps) still cause failures in a substantial fraction of samples. An open question is whether MOM-based features could serve as effective inputs or regularizers within deep architectures to combine interpretability with learned multi-view reasoning.
