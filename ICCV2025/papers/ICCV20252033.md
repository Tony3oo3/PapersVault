## Problem

Existing multi-modal tracking methods (prompt-based and double-branch) inherently treat RGB as the dominant modality, underutilizing complementary information from thermal, depth, or event channels in complex scenarios like occlusion and blur. Additionally, freezing backbone weights to avoid overfitting on scarce multi-modal datasets prevents the backbone from learning to extract meaningful features from non-RGB modalities, while full fine-tuning leads to overfitting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252033/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a pair of aligned images: one RGB frame plus one additional modality frame (thermal, depth, or event), processed through a Siamese-style Transformer backbone (OSTrack). It produces a single bounding box prediction per frame via a BoxHead. The architecture internally maintains three parallel data streams (RGB, Modal, and a fused Mix stream) that are only combined at the final prediction stage, rather than being merged during feature extraction.

## Method

SMSTracker introduces a tri-path architecture where a Score Mask Fusion (SMF) module computes multi-head self-attention scores for both RGB and Modal features at each backbone layer, applies MaxPool over concatenated scores to select high-reliability patches, and accumulates them into a third Mix stream—acting as a unidirectional mixer that does not perturb the original feature extraction. At the output of the backbone, a Sigma Interaction (SGI) module uses Cross Mamba Block and Concat Mamba Block (borrowed from the Sigma segmentation network) to perform cross-modal feature sharing and refinement across all three streams before summing them for BoxHead prediction. For training, a Drop Key Fine-tuning (DKF) strategy randomly masks attention matrix entries with a Bernoulli-distributed mask (DKR=0.1), adding −1e12 to masked positions before SoftMax, which regularizes the backbone, prevents overfitting on small multi-modal datasets, and forces the model to learn robust cross-modal representations rather than over-relying on RGB.

## Results

SMSTracker achieves state-of-the-art results on four of five benchmarks: LasHeR (PR 70.3%, SR 56.0%), RGBT234 (MPR 86.9%, MSR 64.5%), DepthTrack (Pr 64.1%, Re 63.1%, F-score 63.6%), and VOT2022RGBD (EAO 74.8%, Accuracy 82.2%, Robustness 89.7%). On VisEvent, it reaches MPR 76.3% and MSR 60.4%, slightly trailing OneTracker (76.7/60.8) and SDSTrack (76.7/59.7). The largest margins over the nearest competitor (OneTracker) appear on LasHeR (+3.1 PR, +2.2 SR) and DepthTrack (+3.7 Pr, +2.4 Re, +2.7 F-score).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMSTracker outperforms all 19 baselines on LasHeR, RGBT234, DepthTrack, and VOT2022RGBD, with the closest competitors being OneTracker and SDSTrack (both CVPR 2024). On LasHeR, SMSTracker leads OneTracker by +3.1 PR and +2.2 SR; on DepthTrack, it leads SDSTrack by +2.2 Pr and +2.2 F-score. On VOT2022RGBD, the Robustness score of 89.7% exceeds the next best (SDSTrack, 88.3%) by 1.4 points. The sole exception is VisEvent, where SMSTracker's MPR of 76.3% trails OneTracker and SDSTrack (both 76.7%) by 0.4 points, and its MSR of 60.4% trails OneTracker (60.8%) by 0.4 points, though it still beats SDSTrack on MSR (60.4 vs. 59.7). Older baselines such as OSTrack, ProTrack, and ViPT are consistently 5–15 points behind SMSTracker across all datasets, confirming that the tri-path fusion and DKF strategy provide substantial gains over prior prompt-based and adapter-based multi-modal tracking approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

SMSTracker departs from the dominant paradigm in multi-modal tracking where RGB serves as the backbone and other modalities are treated as supplementary (via visual prompts in ViPT/ProTrack or adapter-based fusion in BAT/SDSTrack). It builds on two specific prior works: the Drop Key mechanism from Li et al. (CVPR 2023), repurposed as a fine-tuning strategy (DKF) to let the backbone learn from non-RGB data without overfitting, and the Cross/Concat Mamba Blocks from Sigma (Wan et al., 2024), originally designed for multi-modal semantic segmentation, here adapted for feature interaction in a tracking context. The tri-path architecture (RGB, Modal, Mix) represents a conceptual departure from both prompt-based and double-branch fusion, introducing a dedicated fused stream that is generated unidirectionally without interfering with per-modality feature extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine structural limitation in multi-modal tracking—the systematic underutilization of non-RGB modalities—by elevating modal information to an equal status through the tri-path design and DKF training. The DKF strategy is a practical and transferable technique that could benefit other multi-modal learning scenarios facing data imbalance. However, the inference speed of 24.11 FPS limits real-time deployment, and the reliance on Mamba blocks (borrowed from segmentation) raises questions about whether the SGI module's contribution is truly architectural or primarily a capacity addition. The modest gap over SDSTrack and OneTracker on VisEvent (MPR 76.3 vs. 76.7) suggests the advantage is not uniform across all modalities.
