## Problem

Conditional entropy models for video compression face two key limitations: incorporating temporal context increases computational overhead and inference latency, while existing spatial context strategies (autoregressive, checkerboard, minimum-entropy) fail to explicitly model the ordering of spatial dependencies, leaving the most informative context unavailable during progressive decoding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250529/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB video frames (448×256 crops for training on Vimeo-90k; 1792×1024 center-crops from 1080p test sequences) and produces a compressed bitstream plus reconstructed frames. The entropy model operates on quantized latent tokens from a CNN encoder, conditioning on three temporal context sources (previous-frame latents, hyper-priors, temporal-priors) and the already-decoded portion of the current frame as spatial context, with an 8-step progressive decoding schedule based on a sinusoidal function.

## Method

The CGT entropy model comprises two modules built on Swin Transformer blocks. The Temporal Context Resampler (TCR) uses a small set of learnable window queries fused with full-resolution temporal latents via window cross-attention, producing a fixed-length compact token sequence that captures inter-frame dependencies while reducing downstream computation by ~65%. The Dependency-Weighted Spatial Context Assigner (DWSCA) employs a shared-parameter teacher-student Swin Transformer decoder: during training, the teacher processes randomly masked latents to generate an attention map (token importance) and an entropy map (prediction certainty), computes a weighted dependency score S = αH + (1−α)A, and selects positions via differentiable soft top-k; the student then decodes those positions, ensuring train-inference consistency. At inference, only the student network runs, iteratively decoding the highest-dependency tokens at each step to estimate the PMF for entropy coding.

## Results

On three 1080p test datasets (MCL-JCV, UVG, HEVC-B), CGT achieves an average BD-rate of −50.6% (PSNR) and −74.7% (SSIM) relative to the VTM anchor, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art MIMT by 8.9% (PSNR) and 9.4% (SSIM). The temporal context resampler reduces entropy modeling time by approximately 65% (from 1,226 ms to 427 ms on MIMT; from 1,305 ms to 488 ms on CGT) while incurring only a +1.8% BD-rate cost. The dependency-weighted spatial context assigner further yields an 11.2% average BD-rate improvement over the minimal-entropy decoding strategy used in MIMT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CGT consistently outperforms all seven baselines across all three test datasets under both PSNR and SSIM metrics. Against the strongest neural baseline MIMT [42], CGT improves average BD-rate from −41.7% to −50.6% (PSNR) and from −65.3% to −74.7% (SSIM), a gain of roughly 9 percentage points. Compared to DMC [20] (−33.3% PSNR), the improvement is 17.3 points; against DCVC [19] (70.5% PSNR) and FVC [14] (95.9% PSNR), CGT is substantially better, confirming the advantage of conditional entropy modeling over residual coding. C2F [15] (16.6% PSNR) and VTM (0%) are far behind, with C2F actually worse than VTM on HEVC-B. In the generalization experiment (Figure 3), swapping the frame codec from DCVC to DCVC-DC [21] still shows CGT outperforming the DCVC-DC baseline, indicating the entropy model's gains are codec-agnostic. VCT [29] is referenced qualitatively in the RD-curve discussion (Figure 4) as being outperformed, though it does not appear in the quantitative BD-rate tables.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DCVC contextual video compression framework (Li et al., NeurIPS 2021) as its backbone and extends the MIMT masked-image-modeling transformer (Xiang et al., ICLR 2023) in two specific ways. First, it replaces the plain Swin Transformer blocks used for temporal context with a Temporal Context Resampler that compresses temporal latents into fixed-length tokens via learnable window queries and cross-attention, reducing downstream computational cost. Second, it departs from MIMT's minimum-entropy decoding strategy by introducing a teacher-student network that explicitly models spatial position dependencies through a weighted combination of attention (importance) and entropy (certainty) maps, selecting top-k tokens via a differentiable soft top-k operation. The paper positions these as addressing two identified gaps: the computational overhead of temporal context and the lack of explicit spatial dependency ordering in prior conditional entropy models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 65% reduction in entropy modeling time while maintaining competitive BD-rate is practically significant for real-time and near-real-time video compression pipelines, where entropy coding is often the bottleneck. The finding that explicit spatial dependency modeling (teacher-student with weighted scoring) outperforms the random-masking proxy task used in MIMT suggests that the training-inference mismatch in passive masking strategies is a genuine limitation, opening the door to more principled progressive decoding schemes. However, the evaluation is limited to 7-frame sequences from Vimeo-90k and three 1080p test sets, and the generalization experiment (swapping the frame codec to DCVC-DC) is brief, leaving open questions about scalability to longer sequences, higher resolutions, and whether the teacher-student overhead is justified at larger scales.
