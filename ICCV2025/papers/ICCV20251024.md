## Problem

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) such as LLaVA perform poorly on ordinal regression tasks, achieving less than 60% accuracy on most datasets even after fine-tuning. The paper identifies two root causes: a lack of specificity modeling (domain knowledge and category boundary information are overlooked) and a lack of commonality modeling (the intrinsic ordered structure among categories is not explicitly exploited). Unlike traditional ordinal regression methods that require training a separate model per task, MLLMs offer a path to a unified model, but no prompting paradigm existed to elicit their ordinal understanding.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a single ordinal category label from a task-specific ordered set (e.g., 8 age groups for Adience, 5 decades for HCI, 5 DR severity levels, or 5 aesthetic quality ranks). The prediction is not a single-shot classification but the terminal output of a multi-step progressive refinement: at each RO-CoT step the model selects one candidate sub-interval from a balanced partition, and the process terminates when the remaining candidate set contains exactly one category. The number of CoT steps is determined by the total category count and the chosen tree branching factor (binary or trinomial).

## Method

OrderChain is a prompting paradigm built on LLaVA-1.5-7B (fine-tuned with LoRA) that combines three components. (1) A Range Optimization Chain-of-Thought (RO-CoT) decomposes the ordinal prediction into a sequence of smaller-range classification subtasks, iteratively narrowing the candidate interval until a single label remains. (2) A Category Recursive Division (CRD) method automatically plans the narrowing path by structuring the full category set into a balanced k-tree (binary for ≤5 categories, trinomial for larger sets), so each step presents the model with a constrained, balanced subset of candidates. (3) Task-aware prompts inject specificity: Domain Knowledge Prompts (generated by the LLM itself as a preliminary task description) provide contextual priors, while Category Feature Prompts specify the total category range (description) and the current step's candidate subset (instruction). The ablation shows that CRD-generated prompts alone without RO-CoT actually hurt performance, confirming that the progressive CoT structure is what enables the model to exploit the ordinal structure across steps.

## Results

On the Adience facial age estimation dataset, LLaVA-1.5 with OrderChain achieves 93.2% accuracy and 0.12 MAE, surpassing the previous SOTA (L2RCLIP at 66.2% / 0.36 MAE) by 27 percentage points in accuracy and 0.24 in MAE. On the Diabetic Retinopathy dataset, OrderChain reaches 85.7% accuracy and 0.23 MAE, outperforming the best supervised method (Ord2Seq at 84.2% / 0.25). On the HCI historical image dating dataset, OrderChain attains 73.0% accuracy and 0.32 MAE, edging out NumCLIP (69.6% / 0.35). On the Image Aesthetics dataset, OrderChain achieves 73.83% overall accuracy and 0.260 MAE, remaining competitive though slightly below NumCLIP (76.53% / 0.236) due to the subjective nature of human aesthetic ratings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OrderChain decisively outperforms all 13 supervised baselines on the Adience dataset, with the largest gap over L2RCLIP (93.2% vs. 66.2% accuracy, +27 pp) and Ord2Seq (93.2% vs. 63.9%). On the HCI dataset, OrderChain beats NumCLIP (73.0% vs. 69.6%) and L2RCLIP (73.0% vs. 67.2%), establishing a new SOTA. On the DR dataset, OrderChain surpasses Ord2Seq (85.7% vs. 84.2%), CIG (85.7% vs. 83.3%), and MT (85.7% vs. 82.8%), while also dramatically outperforming SORD (85.7% vs. 53.4%) and Poisson (85.7% vs. 77.1%). The one notable exception is the Image Aesthetics dataset, where NumCLIP (76.53%) and L2RCLIP (76.07%) slightly edge out OrderChain (73.83%), which the authors attribute to the highly subjective nature of human aesthetic ratings and MLLMs' positive bias toward people. Across all four main datasets, OrderChain consistently and substantially outperforms the vanilla LLaVA baselines (zero-shot and LoRA fine-tuned), confirming that the gains come from the proposed prompting paradigm rather than from the backbone model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by being the first to apply MLLMs to ordinal regression, a task previously dominated by task-specific deep learning architectures (CNNPOR, POE, Ord2Seq, OrdinalCLIP, L2RCLIP) that each require separate training per domain. Conceptually, it repurposes the Chain-of-Thought prompting paradigm (Wei et al., 2022) and the coarse-to-fine decomposition idea from Ord2Seq, but transplants them into a pure prompt-engineering framework rather than a model architecture. The Category Recursive Division mechanism and task-aware prompts are novel contributions that bridge the gap between MLLM instruction-tuning and the ordinal structure inherent in OR labels, positioning the work at the intersection of CoT reasoning, prompt engineering, and vision-language modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a single MLLM with structured prompting can outperform specialized OR architectures by 27% on Adience suggests that ordinal understanding was already latent in MLLMs but inaccessible without the right reasoning scaffold. This opens a path toward truly unified ordinal regression models that generalize zero-shot to unseen domains (e.g., KonIQ image quality), reducing the need for task-specific training. However, limitations remain: performance on subjective tasks (aesthetics, especially the 'People' category) is still below SOTA due to MLLM biases, and validation is confined to LLaVA-1.5-7B on relatively small datasets. Open questions include whether the RO-CoT paradigm scales to continuous-valued ordinal regression, larger category spaces, or other MLLM backbones, and whether the prompting overhead is acceptable in latency-sensitive deployment.
