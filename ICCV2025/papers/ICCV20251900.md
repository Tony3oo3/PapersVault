## Problem

Existing dataset distillation methods optimize storage (rate) and downstream performance (utility) separately—either fixing a pixel-based storage budget and maximizing accuracy, or learning compact representations without an explicit information-theoretic cost. No prior work formalizes the storage cost of synthetic data using Shannon entropy and jointly optimizes rate and utility in a single end-to-end objective, precluding Pareto-optimal trade-offs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251900/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled RGB image dataset (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, ImageNet subsets at 128×128) and produces a small synthetic dataset represented as multiscale latent codes (at L spatial scales from full resolution down to 1/2^(L-1)), per-sample lightweight decoder network parameters, and hard class labels. The total storage is measured in bits per class (bpc), which accounts for the entropy-coded latents, quantized decoder and entropy-network weights, and label bits—unlike the conventional images-per-class metric that ignores encoding overhead and scales linearly with raw pixel dimensions.

## Method

The core contribution is a joint rate-utility objective min r(S) + λℓ(D;S), where the rate r(S) is decomposed into additive Shannon-entropy terms for quantized multiscale latents (modeled under a spatially autoregressive, context-aware Laplace distribution), quantized decoder parameters, and class labels, while the utility ℓ is any off-the-shelf distillation loss (trajectory matching, gradient matching, or distribution matching). Synthetic samples are generated by decoding the latents through extremely lightweight per-sample networks, and all parameters are initialized via an 'overfitted' neural compression pretraining step on the original data. A tunable Lagrange multiplier λ controls the rate-utility trade-off, and post-training uniform quantization of the entropy and decoder network weights (with grid-searched step sizes) fixes their bit costs as constant offsets, avoiding backpropagation through quantization. The method introduces the bits-per-class (bpc) metric to enable fair, unit-consistent comparisons across methods with different storage representations.

## Results

On six 128×128 ImageNet subsets under a bpc budget of ≤192 kB, TM-RUO achieves 76.5% on Nette, 74.3% on Yellow, and 74.8% on Squawk, outperforming the strongest baseline DDiF by 4.5–7.8 percentage points. On CIFAR-10, the method reaches 77.5% accuracy at 94 kB (a 2.5% absolute gain over the next best method), and on CIFAR-100 it attains 49.2% at 53 kB. Across all evaluated bpc budgets, distillation losses (TM, GM, DM), and backbone architectures (AlexNet, VGG-11, ResNet-18, ViT-B), the method consistently establishes superior rate-utility trade-offs, achieving up to 170× greater compression than vanilla distillation at comparable accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TM-RUO outperforms all 12 baselines across every ImageNet subset. The closest competitor, DDiF (neural field distillation), trails by 4.5 pp on Nette (76.5 vs. 72.0), 3.1 pp on Woof (46.0 vs. 42.9), 2.6 pp on Fruit (50.8 vs. 48.2), 5.3 pp on Yellow (74.3 vs. 69.0), 6.1 pp on Meow (53.5 vs. 47.4), and 7.8 pp on Squawk (74.8 vs. 67.0). Representation-based methods such as FreD (DCT) and SPEED (sparse parameterization) lag further behind by 10–25 pp, while vanilla trajectory matching (TM) without rate optimization achieves only 51.4% on Nette. Under cross-architecture evaluation (Table 2), TM-RUO averages 50.7% across AlexNet, VGG-11, ResNet-18, and ViT-B, surpassing DDiF (45.2%) and FreD (28.5%). The plug-and-play compatibility is confirmed by GM-RUO (49.5% avg) and DM-RUO (59.1% avg) both exceeding their respective vanilla counterparts (28.1% and 25.7%) and all competing methods under the same bpc budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges dataset distillation (DD) and neural lossy compression by importing the rate-distortion framework from the latter into the former. It builds directly on C3's 'overfitted' per-sample compression (context-aware Laplace entropy models, lightweight decoders) and on hybrid latent-plus-decoder parameterizations from HaBa, but departs from all prior DD work by explicitly modeling storage cost as Shannon entropy and jointly optimizing it with a distillation utility loss via a Lagrangian. The introduction of the bits-per-class (bpc) metric also marks a conceptual departure from the standard images-per-class (ipc) convention, which conflates raw pixel count with information-theoretic cost and ignores decoder and label overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The joint rate-utility formulation gives DD a principled, information-theoretic objective that was previously absent, enabling systematic exploration of the storage–accuracy Pareto frontier rather than ad-hoc budget choices. The bpc metric is likely to become a standard for fair cross-method comparison, since ipc silently varies with image resolution and ignores auxiliary storage. Open questions include scalability to full ImageNet-1K (the method still trains per-sample entropy and decoder networks), whether fully joint optimization of soft labels and decoder architectures can tighten the frontier further, and extension to non-image modalities (video, text, graphs) where the current spatial autoregressive entropy model would need substantial redesign.
