## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks on Vision-Language Pre-training (VLP) models are instance-specific, requiring a separate perturbation for every input pair and incurring substantial computational cost. Universal adversarial perturbations (UAP), which use a single perturbation to fool a model across all inputs, have not been effectively explored for VLP models. Naive transplants of image-only UAP methods (UAP, GAP) fail because they ignore the cross-modal alignment that is the core mechanism of VLP models.

## Input / Output

The generator takes a fixed random noise vector as input, conditioned on the embedding of the paired modality (text embedding for image perturbation, image embedding for text perturbation), and outputs a single universal image perturbation δ_v (pixel-level, l∞ norm ≤ 12/255) and a universal text perturbation δ_t (token-level, replacing exactly one word in the sentence). The text perturbation is applied via a token-wise substitution strategy that identifies the single most influential word by measuring the embedding distance between the original sentence and its word-masked version. The same pair of perturbations is applied to all test samples without per-instance optimization.

## Method

C-PGC is a decoder-based perturbation generator (extending GAP's architecture) augmented with cross-attention modules that inject the other modality's embedding as conditioning, enabling the generator to exploit cross-modal interactions during perturbation synthesis. The training objective combines two losses: (1) a malicious InfoNCE contrastive loss that treats the originally matched texts as negatives and farthest-selected texts (those with maximum embedding distance from the clean image) as positives, explicitly training the generator to push matched-pair embeddings apart and pull mismatched-pair embeddings together in the VLP feature space; and (2) a unimodal distance loss that minimizes the negative Euclidean distance between adversarial and clean image embeddings to ensure the perturbation moves samples away from their original semantic region. Set-level guidance is incorporated by augmenting both clean and adversarial samples across five scales with Gaussian noise, and the overall loss is balanced by a hyperparameter λ (optimal at 0.1). The generator is trained once on a source dataset and the resulting fixed noise is mapped to a single UAP pair that generalizes across diverse VLP models and downstream tasks.

## Results

On the Flickr30K image-text retrieval task, C-PGC achieves a white-box ASR of 90.13% (TR) and 88.82% (IR) when ALBEF is both the surrogate and target model, and reaches up to 96.50% (TR) and 94.22% (IR) on CLIP_CNN. In the more challenging black-box transfer setting, C-PGC attains 72.51% ASR for IR when transferring from ALBEF to CLIP_CNN, substantially exceeding both baselines. Across all six target models, C-PGC delivers an average black-box improvement of 17.76% over the concurrent ETU method on Flickr30K. The method also generalizes to visual grounding (e.g., dropping ALBEF's RefCOCO+ TestA accuracy from 65.9% to 39.8%) and image captioning (reducing BLIP's CIDEr from 133.3 to 62.5 when sourced from BLIP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

C-PGC is compared against two baselines: GAP (a generative adversarial perturbation method transplanted to V+L) and ETU (a concurrent non-generative UAP method that only perturbs the image modality). C-PGC consistently outperforms both across all six target VLP models and both retrieval directions (TR and IR). Against GAP, the advantage is substantial in every setting; for example, when ALBEF is the surrogate, C-PGC achieves 90.13% white-box TR ASR versus GAP's 69.78%, and 72.51% black-box IR ASR (ALBEF→CLIP_CNN) versus GAP's 44.29%. Against ETU, C-PGC also wins uniformly, with the gap widening markedly in black-box transfer: the paper reports a 17.76% average ASR improvement over ETU on Flickr30K in the black-box setting, compared to a smaller margin in white-box. ETU's limitation is that it is non-generative and image-only, whereas C-PGC produces both image and text perturbations via a contrastive-learning-trained generator with cross-modal conditioning, which the ablation confirms contributes an additional ~9.78% average ASR gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative UAP paradigm from GAP (Poursaeed et al., CVPR 2018) to the multimodal VLP setting by modifying the decoder-based generator with cross-attention modules conditioned on the opposite modality's embeddings. It departs from instance-specific VLP attacks (Co-Attack, SGA, TMM) by producing a single universal perturbation pair, and from the concurrent ETU work by being truly multimodal (joint image and text perturbations) and generative rather than optimization-based. The central conceptual departure is repurposing contrastive learning—the very mechanism VLP models use to establish cross-modal alignment—as the training objective for the perturbation generator, effectively turning the model's own pre-training paradigm into an attack vector.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Universal adversarial perturbations are practically significant because a single crafted perturbation can compromise an entire deployment, making them a more efficient threat model than instance-specific attacks. The demonstrated black-box transferability (e.g., 72.51% ASR from ALBEF to CLIP_CNN) suggests that defenses tuned to one VLP architecture may not generalize, raising urgent questions about robustness across the rapidly growing ecosystem of VLP models. Limitations include the strict single-word text perturbation budget, evaluation on only six models, and the reliance on a surrogate model for training; open questions include whether the contrastive-attack paradigm extends to newer architectures (e.g., LLaVA-style VLMs) and whether effective defenses against universal multimodal perturbations exist.
