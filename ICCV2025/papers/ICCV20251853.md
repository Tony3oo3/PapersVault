## Problem

Existing generalized category discovery (GCD) methods process images as atomic entities, applying contrastive learning or prototype classifiers to holistic embeddings without decomposing objects into semantic visual primitives. This image-level processing fails to emulate human attribute-level reasoning, where objects are understood by binding shared visual primitives (e.g., stripes, beak shape) to spatial regions and establishing cross-category consensus, leading to confusion between classes with similar global appearances.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251853/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images encoded by a frozen DINOv1 or DINOv2 ViT, extracting patch-level features (N×D_z) from the 10th block for primitive decoupling and modifying the last two transformer blocks with the consensus mechanism. Beyond standard class labels (known/novel), the model produces K spatial primitive masks (each N×1) that bind individual visual attributes to image regions, enabling interpretable attribute-level analysis. The module is plug-and-play, requiring no changes to the backbone or the downstream GCD training objective.

## Method

ConGCD introduces two coupled mechanisms. First, self-deconstruction discovers K visual primitives via iterative slot-attention (T GRU-refined steps) followed by competitive reconstruction: each primitive is decoded to a spatial feature map plus an activation region, and softmax normalization across primitives enforces that each mask focuses on a single attribute, with an L2 reconstruction loss providing the unsupervised training signal. Second, the multiplex consensus framework reformulates the FFN in the last two ViT blocks into K dominant consensus units (retaining top-r_k% high-activation neurons masked by primitive regions to capture class-discriminative patterns) and M contextual consensus units (preserving weak-activation neurons to model distributional invariants shared across categories). A consensus scheduler with an Activation Separation Distributor (ASR) dynamically generates per-sample, per-unit separation ratios, enabling adaptive boundary determination between dominant and contextual neurons. The final output is a weighted sum over all units with softmax-gated routing, trained end-to-end with the standard GCD objective.

## Results

On the fine-grained Semantic Shift Benchmark (CUB-200, FGVC-Aircraft, Stanford-Cars) with DINOv1, ConGCD as a plug-and-play module yields an average improvement of +1.18% on All-class accuracy and +2.83% on Novel-class accuracy across four baselines (SimGCD, LegoGCD, SPTNet, SelEx). On the coarse-grained benchmark (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet-100), the average gains are +0.63% (All) and +0.75% (Novel). The strongest absolute result is SelEx + ConGCD on CUB-200, reaching 81.7% All and 82.4% Novel accuracy (DINOv1), and 86.3% All with DINOv2. ConGCD also improves category number estimation, reducing the error rate to 3% on CIFAR-100 and 1% on ImageNet-100 when combined with CMS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConGCD is designed as a plug-and-play module rather than a standalone method, so it is evaluated by augmenting four existing GCD frameworks (SimGCD, LegoGCD, SPTNet, SelEx) on both DINOv1 and DINOv2 backbones. Across all four fine-grained baselines with DINOv1, ConGCD consistently improves Novel-class accuracy by 1.8–2.8 percentage points (e.g., SelEx Novel: 77.5→82.4 on CUB-200; SPTNet Novel: 65.1→67.8), while All-class accuracy gains range from +0.7 to +3.0 points. On the coarse-grained benchmark, improvements are smaller but still positive (e.g., SimGCD CIFAR-100 Novel: 77.8→78.9; SPTNet ImageNet-100 All: 85.4→85.9). With the stronger DINOv2 backbone, gains narrow (SelEx CUB-200 All: 86.0→86.3), suggesting the module is most beneficial when the base representation is weaker. In the category number estimation task (Table 3), ConGCD combined with CMS achieves the lowest error rate (3% on CIFAR-100, 1% on ImageNet-100), outperforming GPC (0% and 3%) and PIM (5% and 2%) on the fine-grained datasets where estimation is hardest. The ablation (Table 4) confirms that removing either the dominant or contextual consensus units degrades performance, and that a conventional MoE replacement is inferior, validating the multiplex design.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConGCD builds directly on Slot Attention [30] from object-centric learning, repurposing its iterative competitive binding mechanism to discover fine-grained visual primitives (attributes like stripes, beak shape) rather than whole objects. It departs from the dominant GCD paradigm (GCD, SimGCD, CMS, SPTNet, SelEx) that processes images as atomic entities by introducing a self-deconstruction step that decomposes representations into primitive-oriented components before classification. The multiplex consensus mechanism is conceptually related to Mixture-of-Experts but introduces a key distinction: routing is determined by neuron activation magnitude (dominant vs. contextual) rather than learned gating, and the two pathways capture complementary information (class-discriminative vs. distributional invariants) rather than redundant expert specializations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play compatibility with multiple GCD backbones (SimGCD, LegoGCD, SPTNet, SelEx) across both DINOv1 and DINOv2 makes ConGCD a practical drop-in module for the GCD community, and the cognitive-science framing (visual primitives, multiplex consensus) offers interpretability that purely objective-function-driven methods lack. However, the performance gains are modest (≈1–2% on fine-grained, ≈0.5–1% on coarse-grained), and the approach inherits the assumption that a fixed number of primitives (K=8) suffices across all datasets, which may not hold for highly compositional scenes. Open questions include whether the primitive decomposition generalizes to video or multi-modal settings, whether the activation-based routing can be made more principled, and whether the modest gains scale with larger backbones or more challenging open-world distributions.
