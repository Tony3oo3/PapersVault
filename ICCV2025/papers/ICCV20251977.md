## Problem

Single-pass driving clips leave large portions of the road structure unobserved, making expansive reconstruction essential for sensor simulators. While diffusion models can generate plausible novel views, naively integrating their outputs into 3D Gaussian Splatting training introduces cumulative physical inconsistencies and degrades convergence, because generated images contain inherent biases that no amount of fine-tuning fully eliminates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251977/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single trajectory of front-view images (1280×720@10 Hz), a colored LiDAR point cloud, and camera extrinsics/intrinsics. It produces a 3DGS scene representation capable of rendering cross-lane novel views at lateral shifts up to 7 m. Unusually, the training loop also ingests diffusion-generated pseudo-images (from a fine-tuned SD v1.5 + ControlNet) and a per-pixel reward confidence map that gates how strongly each generated pixel supervises the Gaussian optimization.

## Method

RGE-GS introduces two coupled mechanisms. First, a lightweight U-Net reward network predicts pixel-wise confidence for each diffusion-generated image; it is trained adversarially (no ground-truth confidence) using a reprojection error against colored LiDAR point-cloud projections and a binarization regularizer, then frozen after 5,000 joint iterations. The resulting confidence map multiplicatively weights the L1 and SSIM terms in the reconstruction loss, effectively rejecting biased diffusion outputs at the source. Second, a differentiated training strategy partitions Gaussians into three groups—missing (newly initialized from LiDAR geometry or DepthAnything-V2 depth), immature (identified by a positional-gradient threshold of 5×10⁻⁴ and still receiving gradient updates), and mature (frozen to preserve stability)—so that optimization effort is concentrated where it is needed, reducing both training time and artifact propagation.

## Results

On the Para-Lane dataset (one-lane expansion, ~3.5 m lateral shift), RGE on Scaffold-GS achieves the best PSNR of 19.72 dB, SSIM of 0.586, and LPIPS of 0.415, outperforming the strongest baseline FreeSim (19.07 / 0.537 / 0.424) by +0.65 dB PSNR and +0.049 SSIM. On the Waymo Open Dataset, RGE on Scaffold-GS attains the lowest FID at every lateral shift from 1 m to 5 m (52.41 → 129.12), with the margin over FreeSim widening from 4.5 FID at 1 m to 45.0 FID at 5 m, indicating the reward-guided mechanism becomes increasingly critical as rendering degradation grows.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RGE on Scaffold-GS outperforms all six baselines on PSNR and SSIM for both one-lane and two-lane expansion on Para-Lane. Against the strongest diffusion-aided baseline FreeSim (reimplemented), RGE gains +0.65 dB PSNR and +0.049 SSIM on one-lane, and +0.15 dB PSNR and +0.005 SSIM on two-lane; however, FreeSim retains a marginal LPIPS advantage on two-lane (0.466 vs. 0.468). On the Waymo dataset, RGE on Scaffold-GS achieves the lowest FID at all five lateral shifts (1–5 m), beating FreeSim by 4.5 FID at 1 m and by 45.0 FID at 5 m, and beating ReconDreamer by 7.4 FID at 1 m and 38.9 FID at 5 m. Pure 3DGS-based baselines (3DGS, 2DGS, Street Gaussians, Scaffold-GS without diffusion) trail RGE by 2.5–4.7 dB PSNR on one-lane, confirming that the diffusion prior is essential for cross-lane generalization. The consistent gains of RGE when applied to both 3DGS and Scaffold-GS backbones demonstrate that the reward network and differentiated training strategy are backbone-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

RGE-GS extends the diffusion-aided 3DGS reconstruction line (DriveDreamer4D, MagicDrive3D, FreeSim, ReconDreamer) by addressing a gap those methods leave open: how to selectively trust diffusion-generated views during progressive scene expansion. Rather than feeding all generated scans directly into GS optimization, it introduces a lightweight adversarially-trained reward network that scores pixel-wise confidence of diffusion outputs, and a differentiated training strategy that partitions Gaussians into missing/immature/mature groups with tailored optimization. The approach is explicitly modular, demonstrated to plug into both 3DGS and Scaffold-GS backends, and builds on controllable diffusion models (SD v1.5 + ControlNet, SVD) fine-tuned on driving data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For autonomous driving simulators that must synthesize sensor data along trajectories beyond the original scan, RGE-GS offers a practical, drop-in mechanism to reduce artifacts from imperfect diffusion priors without retraining the diffusion model itself. The reward network's plug-in design means existing GS pipelines can adopt it with minimal modification. Limitations include evaluation on only 4 Para-Lane scenes and 10 Waymo scenes, a noticeable quality drop at larger lateral shifts (7 m), and reliance on a frozen reward network after 5,000 joint iterations. Open questions include whether the reward mechanism generalizes to dynamic scenes, whether video-diffusion priors (as the authors note for future work) would further reduce the need for per-pixel confidence filtering, and whether the approach extends to feed-forward or real-time GS reconstruction.
