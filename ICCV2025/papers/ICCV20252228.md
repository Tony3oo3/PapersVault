## Problem

Open-vocabulary 3D object detection has been explored almost exclusively through point cloud-based methods that require expensive 3D sensors (depth cameras, LiDAR) at inference time, while multi-view image-based detectors that avoid this dependency have not been extended to open-vocabulary settings. Additionally, existing OV 3D detectors still rely on human 3D annotations or low-quality per-frame pseudo-boxes, limiting their accuracy and efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252228/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, OpenM3D consumes only multi-view RGB images (480×640, 20 views per scene) with known camera poses and a free-form text prompt, producing axis-aligned 3D bounding boxes with open-vocabulary class labels in 0.3 seconds per scene on a V100 GPU. During training, posed RGB-D images are additionally required to generate 3D pseudo-boxes and align voxel features with CLIP embeddings, but depth is never needed at test time. The OV classification is performed by matching average voxel features within each predicted box against precomputed CLIP text embeddings via cosine similarity.

## Method

OpenM3D is a single-stage anchor-free detector built on ImGeoNet's geometry-aware 3D voxel feature volume, jointly trained with two losses: a class-agnostic 3D localization loss (rotated 3D IoU, centerness, and focal loss) supervised by high-quality 3D pseudo-boxes, and a novel Voxel-Semantic Alignment loss that minimizes cosine distance between 3D voxel features and CLIP image embeddings extracted from 2D segments projected onto those voxels. The pseudo-boxes are generated by a graph embedding pipeline: SAM produces class-agnostic 2D segments, which are lifted to partial 3D segments via depth back-projection; a graph is built where edges connect overlapping partial segments (overlap ratio > threshold), DeepWalk produces node embeddings, and KMeans clustering groups nodes into complete 3D objects, optionally refined by mesh segmentation. This global graph-based clustering considers all viewpoints simultaneously, avoiding the per-frame errors of OV-3DET and the local adjacent-frame merging of SAM3D. At inference, the CLIP image encoder is entirely unnecessary—only precomputed text embeddings are used for classification, making the detector 7× faster than a two-stage CLIP-based baseline.

## Results

On ScanNet200, OpenM3D with its own graph-embedding pseudo boxes achieves 4.23% mAP@25 and 15.12% mAR@25 for open-vocabulary 3D detection, outperforming the same architecture trained with OV-3DET boxes (3.13% mAP@25) and SAM3D boxes (3.92% mAP@25). In the class-agnostic setting, OpenM3D reaches 26.92% AP@25 and 51.19% AR@25, a 37% relative AP improvement over OV-3DET-trained boxes (19.53% AP@25). Against the strong two-stage S2D baseline (which uses CLIP ViT at inference), OpenM3D matches mAP@25 (4.23% vs. 4.17%) while substantially outperforming on mAR@25 (15.12% vs. 10.05%) and running ~6× faster. On ScanNetv2, OpenM3D achieves 19.76% AP@25 using only multi-view images, comparable to point-cloud-based methods like CoDA (19.32%) and OV-3DET (18.02%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OV-3DET [28], OpenM3D wins decisively across all metrics: pseudo-box precision improves from 11.62% to 32.07% (ScanNet200, IoU@25), class-agnostic AP@25 rises from 19.53% to 26.92%, and OV mAP@25 increases from 3.13% to 4.23%. Against SAM3D [55], OpenM3D achieves substantially higher pseudo-box precision (32.07% vs. 14.48%) while matching recall (58.30% vs. 57.70%), and outperforms on class-agnostic AP@25 (26.92% vs. 23.77%) and OV mAP@25 (4.23% vs. 3.92%). On ScanNetv2, OpenM3D (19.76% AP@25) slightly edges out CoDA [3] (19.32%) but falls short of ImOV3D [54] (21.45%), which leverages additional LVIS training data and point-cloud inputs. The S2D two-stage baseline, which applies CLIP ViT at inference, is outperformed by OpenM3D on mAR@25 (15.12% vs. 10.05%) while matching mAP@25, and is ~6× slower due to CLIP image-encoder computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

OpenM3D builds directly on ImGeoNet's geometry-shaping 3D voxel feature volume for multi-view 3D object detection, extending it from fixed-vocabulary to open-vocabulary classification via a novel Voxel-Semantic Alignment loss that aligns voxel features with pre-trained CLIP embeddings during training. It departs from the point-cloud-based OV 3D detection line (OV-3DET, CoDA, ImOV3D) by eliminating the need for 3D sensors at inference, requiring only multi-view RGB images. The graph embedding-based pseudo box generation (using DeepWalk over a scene-wide overlap graph) is a conceptual departure from OV-3DET's per-frame Detic back-projection and SAM3D's sequential adjacent-frame merging, treating all viewpoints simultaneously to mitigate per-frame segmentation errors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first multi-view OV 3D detector that runs in 0.3 seconds per scene without depth or point clouds at inference, making it practical for deployment on standard RGB cameras where 3D sensors are unavailable or too expensive. The 270× speedup over a depth-estimation baseline and the 16× speedup over OV-3DET address a real bottleneck for real-time indoor scene understanding. However, absolute mAP remains low (~4% on ScanNet200), and the method still requires depth maps during training for pseudo box generation and voxel-semantic alignment, leaving open whether fully depth-free training is feasible. Generalization to outdoor scenes, larger-scale environments, and the impact of the relatively small CLIP ViT/L-14 encoder on long-tail classes remain unexplored.
