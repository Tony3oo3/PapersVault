## Problem

Customized instructional image editing—learning a specific artistic editing style from just a few before-and-after examples and applying it to new images—remains largely unexplored. Existing approaches either focus on content generation rather than editing, rely on brittle multi-stage pipelines (DDIM inversion, segmentation, mask prediction) that resist end-to-end fine-tuning on small datasets, or lack a dedicated benchmark for evaluation. The paper asks whether a customized instruction-guided editor can be trained as easily as a customized image generator.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image (resized to 768×512) and a natural-language editing instruction (e.g., 'Add colorful magical effects and flowing color blocks to the girl') as input, and produces a single edited image that applies the learned artistic style while preserving the original content and spatial layout. The source image is consumed as noise-free VAE-encoded condition tokens concatenated with noised latent tokens along the sequence dimension, rather than being noised or inverted, which is unusual compared to SDEdit-style add-noise paradigms. Output images maintain pixel-level spatial correspondence with the source, avoiding ghosting or background drift.

## Method

ArtEditor is a two-stage framework built on the FLUX.1 DiT architecture. In stage one, a high-rank LoRA (rank 256) is trained on 3.5M image-editing pairs from SeedEdit to produce ArtEditor-Base, a general-purpose instruction-following editor. In stage two, a low-rank LoRA (rank 128) is fine-tuned on only 30–50 artist-specific before-and-after pairs to yield ArtEditor-LoRA, capturing a particular editing style. Two key architectural innovations enable this: (1) Position Encoding Cloning, which assigns identical RoPE coordinates to both the source-image condition tokens and the noised target tokens, injecting explicit spatial correspondence and suppressing misalignment; and (2) a Noise-Free Conditioning paradigm, where the source-image tokens remain at noise level zero throughout denoising, preserving high-frequency detail and letting the model selectively copy or generate via multi-modal attention. Training uses a conditional flow-matching loss over the concatenated [noised latent; image condition; text] token sequence.

## Results

On the general image editing benchmark (HQ-Edit), ArtEditor-Base achieves a CLIP Score of 0.261, GPT Score of 51.159, and CLIPimg of 0.871, outperforming all baselines. On the customized editing task (DoodleArt), the full ArtEditor (with PE cloning + noise-free conditioning + LoRA) reaches a CLIP Score of 0.279, GPT Score of 63.207, and CLIPimg of 0.854, surpassing the strongest baseline OmniGen (0.260 / 50.684 / 0.828) by a wide margin on instruction-following metrics. Ablations confirm that removing PE cloning drops GPT Score to 59.748 and removing noise-free conditioning drops it to 38.438, validating both architectural contributions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ArtEditor outperforms all four baselines on every metric in both the general and customized editing settings. Against InstructPix2Pix, ArtEditor gains +0.030 CLIP Score and +26.8 GPT Score on the customized task, reflecting far stronger style adherence. MagicBrush is the closest competitor on CLIPimg (0.885 vs. 0.854) but trails ArtEditor by 24.7 GPT Score points, indicating it preserves content well yet fails to follow the artist's specific style. SDEdit(FLUX) is the weakest baseline, scoring only 21.793 GPT Score, as its add-noise paradigm degrades both instruction following and content preservation. OmniGen is the strongest single competitor, achieving 50.684 GPT Score, but ArtEditor still leads by 12.5 GPT Score points and 0.019 CLIP Score, demonstrating that the PE cloning and noise-free conditioning mechanisms provide a meaningful advantage over a unified generation approach without explicit spatial alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

ArtEditor extends the instruction-guided image editing line (InstructPix2Pix, MagicBrush, OmniGen) by introducing a few-shot style personalization dimension absent from prior work. Architecturally, it repurposes the FLUX.1 DiT backbone and SD3's flow-matching objective, but departs from the multi-stage editing pipelines (DDIM inversion + segmentation + mask prediction) in favor of an end-to-end conditional generation formulation. The two-stage training recipe—high-rank LoRA pre-training on SeedEdit followed by low-rank LoRA fine-tuning on 30–50 artist pairs—adapts the LoRA paradigm from text-to-image customization to the editing domain, while the PE cloning and noise-free conditioning mechanisms are novel architectural modifications to the DiT's multi-modal attention that enforce spatial alignment and source-image fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DoodleArt dataset fills a genuine gap as the first benchmark for customized instructional editing, enabling reproducible comparison in a previously unmeasured setting. The practical recipe (50 pairs, single GPU, 10k steps) lowers the barrier for artists to create personalized editing tools, though generalization beyond the six doodle-art styles and to more complex structural edits remains untested. Open questions include how the approach scales to diverse editing domains (e.g., photorealistic retouching, architectural modification), whether the few-shot requirement can be further reduced, and whether the GPT-4o-based evaluation adequately captures fine-grained stylistic fidelity.
