## Problem

Existing feed-forward 3D reconstruction models operate in a single-scale manner, forcing the network to simultaneously capture global structure and local fine details, which leads to oversmoothed geometry and blurry textures. Triplane-based representations lack explicit 3D spatial structure, while 3D Gaussian-based methods produce noisy meshes with floating artifacts after extraction. The paper asks whether decomposing reconstruction into a progressive coarse-to-fine process—inspired by sculpting—can resolve this fundamental tension.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250485/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The geometry stage takes sparse posed RGB and normal images (typically 4–6 views generated by Zero123++ from a single image, or ground-truth views during training) and outputs a signed distance field from which a watertight mesh is extracted via dual marching cubes at up to 256³ resolution. A separate lightweight texture auto-encoder then propagates multi-view colors to unseen regions, producing a fully textured mesh in under 5 seconds total. For text-to-3D, a text-to-multi-view diffusion model (MVDream/Zero123++) generates the input views from a prompt before feeding them into the same pipeline.

## Method

MS3D represents geometry as a hierarchy of S sparse structured latents at resolutions 2^(s-1)·W, where each level's voxels are spatially contained within the parent level's voxels (octree-like). Progressive refinement at each scale follows three steps: (1) a Transformer block with self- and cross-attention captures global shape coherence, (2) two layers of sparse 3D convolution smooth and aggregate local features, and (3) an occupancy prediction head prunes and subdivides voxels exceeding a threshold δ, adaptively allocating resolution to geometrically important regions. A multi-scale SAMv2 encoder extracts image features at multiple resolutions, which are injected via view-aware cross-attention into the corresponding structural scale—low-frequency semantic features guide coarse shapes while high-frequency features refine fine details. The continuous SDF is defined by interpolating and concatenating features from all scales through an MLP, and training combines hierarchical structure cross-entropy, dense TSDF L1 loss, and multi-scale differentiable rendering (MSE + LPIPS) for joint geometry and appearance supervision.

## Results

On GSO, MS3D achieves the best scores across all metrics: PSNR 20.54, LPIPS 0.154, PSNR-N 25.50, LPIPS-N 0.105, F-Score 0.979, and Chamfer Distance 0.026. On OmniObject3D, it similarly leads with PSNR 21.86, LPIPS 0.155, F-Score 0.963, and CD 0.033. The most notable margins are in geometric accuracy: F-Score improves by 0.018 over the next-best MeshFormer (0.961) on GSO and by 0.030 over TRELLIS (0.933) on OmniObject3D, while Chamfer Distance is reduced by ~16% relative to the closest competitor on both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MS3D outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across both GSO and OmniObject3D. The closest competitor is MeshFormer, which uses a single-scale sparse voxel representation; MS3D improves F-Score from 0.961 to 0.979 (+0.018) and reduces Chamfer Distance from 0.031 to 0.026 (−16%) on GSO, with similar relative gains on OmniObject3D (F-Score 0.921→0.963, CD 0.041→0.033). TRELLIS, a 3D-native diffusion model, is second-closest on OmniObject3D (F-Score 0.933, CD 0.039) but trails MS3D by 0.030 in F-Score; notably, its multi-view variant (TRELLIS*) actually degrades performance, dropping to F-Score 0.888. Among triplane-based feed-forward methods, InstantMesh (F-Score 0.937 on GSO) and TripoSR (0.895) show clear gaps in geometric fidelity, while 3D-Gaussian-based LGM lags significantly (F-Score 0.807, CD 0.069 on GSO), confirming the paper's observation that Gaussian-to-mesh extraction introduces substantial artifacts. The diffusion-based ShapE and CRM baselines are the weakest overall, with F-Scores below 0.88 on GSO.

## Relation to Prior Work

MS3D carves new ground at the intersection of two previously separate ideas: octree-inspired hierarchical 3D structure (used in point-cloud methods like Adaptive O-CNN and GS-Octree) and multi-scale image feature extraction (borrowed from 2D vision via SAMv2). It departs from the dominant triplane-based feed-forward reconstruction paradigm (LRM, InstantMesh, TripoSR) by introducing explicit sparse 3D structure in the latent space, and from single-scale sparse-voxel methods like MeshFormer by operating at multiple resolutions with scale-corresponding feature injection. The progressive coarse-to-fine decomposition is conceptually inspired by sculpting, but the specific combination of hierarchical structured latents with a multi-scale encoder and unified SDF supervision is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sub-5-second inference for high-quality textured mesh generation makes multi-scale feed-forward reconstruction practically viable for interactive applications like gaming and digital content creation, where prior methods either required optimization (SDS) or produced noisy Gaussian-based meshes. The clean ablation showing that single-scale features (even from strong encoders like DINOv2) fundamentally limit geometric fidelity provides a useful design principle for future 3D reconstruction architectures. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to complex multi-object scenes, whether the reliance on Zero123++ for single-view multi-view synthesis remains a bottleneck, and whether the 5-second budget holds as object complexity increases.
