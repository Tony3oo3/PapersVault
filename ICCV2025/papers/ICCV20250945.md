## Problem

When a text prompt contains semantically correlated objects (e.g., 'rusty tracks with an old train'), Stable Diffusion's CLIP-based text encoder produces biased self-attention representations that cause one object to be entirely omitted from the generated image. This 'object omission' problem arises from unidirectional token correlation in the text module and cannot be addressed by existing methods that assume the pre-trained model can already generate all referenced objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250945/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt containing correlated object pairs and produces a 512×512 RGB image via Stable Diffusion V1.5. Fine-tuning operates exclusively on the self-attention maps of the text encoder (specifically the 11th transformer layer), requiring no external reference images or auxiliary models. At inference time, the method uses a dual-model sampling scheme where the fine-tuned text encoder provides conditional guidance while the original pre-trained encoder serves as the negative/unconditional branch.

## Method

The paper fine-tunes the self-attention blocks in SD's text module using a decorrelation loss that reduces the attention probability from the most correlated preceding token to the target (omitted) token, while preserving the minimum attention level to avoid zeroing out the value. Model checkpoint selection balances two criteria: the log-ratio of attention between focused tokens (measuring decorrelation) and the maximum absolute difference in attention maps among irrelevant tokens (measuring preservation of unrelated token interactions), combined via a trade-off parameter τ. At sampling time, a modified classifier-free guidance formula uses the fine-tuned debiased model for the conditional noise prediction and the original pre-trained biased model for the unconditional term, so that the difference between the two models actively pushes the denoising process away from the correlated representation, effectively strengthening decorrelation throughout the generation process.

## Results

On 30 GPT-4-generated prompts with correlated object pairs (50 images each), the proposed decorrelation method achieves the best overall BERTScore of 76.35±2.44 and the lowest FID of 243.11, outperforming the pre-trained Stable Diffusion baseline (75.74 BERTScore, 290.57 FID) and all attention-based baselines. In a user study (3×2×5 comparisons on a 5-point scale), the method scores 3.60±0.56 on text-image alignment and 3.79±0.40 on image fidelity, roughly doubling the alignment scores of all baselines (1.81–1.98). The method also generalizes across prompt variations: for each of three object pairs with three different prompt phrasings (1,500 images total), it consistently achieves the highest BERTScore and lowest FID.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines on both BERTScore and FID across every object pair. Against the pre-trained Stable Diffusion model, it improves overall BERTScore by 0.61 points and reduces FID by 47.46 (290.57→243.11). Compared to PAG (self-attention perturbation in the U-Net), the method gains 0.94 BERTScore and 33.66 FID, confirming that modifying the text encoder's self-attention is more effective than perturbing vision self-attention. Against AaE (cross-attention patch separation), the advantage is 0.38 BERTScore and 33.16 FID. DisenDiff (cross-attention fine-tuning in the U-Net) is the weakest baseline on FID (308.16), and the proposed method surpasses it by 0.42 BERTScore and 65.05 FID. In the user study, the method's alignment score of 3.60 is nearly double that of the best baseline (DisenDiff at 1.98), while also achieving the highest fidelity score (3.79 vs. 2.87 for the pre-trained model), indicating that decorrelation does not sacrifice image quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion framework and classifier-free guidance, but departs from prior alignment-focused methods (PAG, Attend-and-Excite, DisenDiff) that modify the vision module's self- or cross-attention. The key conceptual shift is identifying the CLIP-based text encoder's self-attention as the root cause of object omission, rather than the UNet's denoising process. The sampling guidance (Eq. 6) repurposes the 'guiding with a bad version of itself' idea from Karras et al. (2024), using the biased pre-trained model as negative guidance against the decorrelated fine-tuned model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper diagnoses a previously underappreciated failure mode in T2I generation—unidirectional token correlation in the text encoder silently suppressing object generation—and offers a lightweight fix (fine-tuning one self-attention layer in the text module) that requires no external knowledge. However, the experimental scope is narrow: only three correlated object pairs, 30 prompts, SD 1.5 at 512×512, and no evaluation on newer architectures (SDXL, Flux) or prompts with more than two objects. Open questions include whether the decorrelation strategy generalizes to multi-object scenes, longer prompts, or transformer-based diffusion backbones, and whether the effect persists as text encoders improve.
