## Problem

Existing end-to-end audio-driven human animation models rely on heavily filtered training data (often retaining less than 10% after lip-sync and pose-visibility cleaning), which prevents them from scaling up like general video generation models and limits them to narrow scenarios such as front-facing portraits with static backgrounds. No prior work has demonstrated how to scale training data for human animation while preserving the quality gains that large-scale mixed-condition training provides in other generative tasks.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a single reference image at any aspect ratio or body proportion (face close-up through full-body), a driving audio waveform, and optionally a text prompt and pose skeleton map sequence. It produces a temporally coherent human animation video at 25 fps in a compact latent space (via a causal 3D VAE), with experiments at 480P and 720P. For long-video generation, the last five frames of each segment are concatenated as motion frames to maintain identity and temporal continuity across segments.

## Method

OmniHuman is built on an MMDiT backbone and injects three motion-related conditions of increasing strength—text (via the standard text branch), audio (wav2vec features compressed by an MLP and injected through frame-wise cross-attention), and pose (pixel-aligned features from a pose guider concatenated along the channel dimension)—while preserving identity by reusing the denoising DiT itself to encode the reference image with modified RoPE (zeroed temporal component for reference tokens), adding no extra parameters. The key contribution is the omni-conditions training strategy governed by two principles: (1) stronger-conditioned tasks (audio, pose) are trained alongside weaker-conditioned tasks (text) so that data filtered out for strict audio/pose criteria still contributes to learning, and (2) stronger conditions receive lower training ratios (e.g., audio 50%, pose 25%) to prevent them from dominating gradient updates. Training proceeds in three progressive stages—text+image, then adding audio, then adding pose—on 18.7K hours of data across 400 A100 GPUs, starting from a pretrained general text-to-video model. At inference, classifier-free guidance (scale 6.5) is applied only to audio and text, not pose, and an image captioning model auto-generates the text prompt.

## Results

On CelebV-HQ (portrait animation), OmniHuman achieves the best IQA (3.875), ASE (2.656), Sync-C (5.199), and FID (31.435) among all baselines, with FVD of 46.393. On RAVDESS, it leads on every metric: IQA 4.564, ASE 2.815, Sync-C 5.255, FID 16.970, FVD 15.906. For body animation on CyberHost, OmniHuman dramatically outperforms CyberHost on hand motion richness (HKV 47.56 vs 24.73) while also achieving better lip-sync (Sync-C 7.44 vs 6.63) and lower FVD (27.03 vs 28.00). In pose-driven evaluation (Table 4), OmniHuman achieves the best AKD (2.14) and FVD (7.32), surpassing CyberHost (AKD 3.12, FVD 7.72).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniHuman outperforms all six portrait animation baselines (SadTalker, Hallo, VExpress, EchoMimic, Loopy, Hallo-3) on every metric across both CelebV-HQ and RAVDESS, with the closest competitor being Loopy (FVD 49.15 vs 46.39 on CelebV-HQ; FVD 16.13 vs 15.91 on RAVDESS). For body animation, OmniHuman surpasses CyberHost (the strongest audio-driven body baseline) on all metrics, most notably on hand motion variance (HKV 47.56 vs 24.73, nearly 2× richer gestures) and lip-sync confidence (Sync-C 7.44 vs 6.63). Against pose-driven methods in Table 4, OmniHuman achieves the best driving accuracy (AKD 2.14 vs CyberHost's 3.12) and FVD (7.32 vs 7.72), despite being a unified multi-condition model rather than a pose-specialized one. The largest margins over baselines appear in FVD and Sync-C, indicating that the omni-condition training strategy primarily improves temporal coherence and audio-visual alignment. OmniHuman also uniquely supports arbitrary aspect ratios and body proportions in a single model, a capability none of the compared baselines offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniHuman builds on the MMDiT/DiT video generation backbone (Esser et al., 2024; Peebles & Xie, 2023) and adapts the pose guider from Animate Anyone (Hu, 2024) for skeleton encoding, but its core contribution is a training strategy rather than an architectural novelty. It departs from prior audio-driven human animation methods (Loopy, Hallo, SadTalker, CyberHost) that rely on heavily filtered, single-condition datasets (<2.2K hours) by introducing an omni-conditions training paradigm that mixes text, audio, and pose conditions with principled data allocation. The two training principles—stronger conditions can leverage weaker-condition data, and stronger conditions should receive lower training ratios—are the conceptual departure from the field's standard practice of introducing auxiliary conditions (e.g., pose) alongside audio to stabilize training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that human animation models can benefit from data scaling (18.7K hours vs. <2.2K hours in prior work), suggesting the field has been artificially constrained by filtering rather than by model capacity. The two training principles are generalizable insights that could reshape how multi-condition generative models are trained beyond human animation. However, the paper acknowledges that uncoordinated or overly expressive movements persist due to the weak audio-motion correlation, and a high CFG scale (6.5) is needed for stability, hinting at residual overfitting. Open questions include whether the scaling trend continues at larger scales, whether richer motion conditions (style, intensity, intention) would further close the gap to natural motion, and whether the paradigm transfers to other conditioned generation tasks.
