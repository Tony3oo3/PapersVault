## Problem

Existing implicit neural representation frameworks for unified task modeling either rely on a single global latent vector (Functa), which cannot capture fine-grained spatial details, or use spatially local latents (Spatial Functa) that are computationally expensive and lack global context needed for classification. Additionally, SIREN-based INRs suffer from a convergence-capacity gap where the network's implicit spectral bias toward low frequencies slows learning of high-frequency details. The paper seeks a task- and data-agnostic encoding that captures multiscale information efficiently across diverse modalities.

## Input / Output

The method consumes discrete signals (RGB images at 32×32 through 256×256, 3D voxel grids at 64³, or audio) as coordinate-value pairs over a normalized [0,1]^D domain, which is partitioned into M^D spatial regions. Each region is handled by an independent parallel localized MLP (P-MLP) with SIREN activations, modulated by shift parameters derived from a three-level hierarchical latent (global 1×1×d_g, intermediate P_i×P_i×d_i, local P×P×d_l). The primary output is a compact multiscale latent representation (e.g., 0.9M parameters for CelebA-HQ 64²) that serves as input to downstream task models: DDPM/DDIM for generation, VMamba for classification, or direct coordinate queries for reconstruction and inverse problems.

## Method

LIFT is a two-stage meta-learning framework (inspired by CAVIA) where Stage 1 (Context Adaptation) alternates an inner SGD loop over multiscale latents and an outer Adam loop over shared P-MLP weights, optimizing reconstruction MSE plus a K-nearest-neighbor latent smoothness loss. The Hierarchical Latent Generator (HLG) fuses global, intermediate, and local latents via progressive upsampling and concatenation to produce a compositional latent Z^α, which is linearly mapped to per-layer shift modulations for each P-MLP, ensuring smooth transitions across patch boundaries. Stage 2 (Task-Driven Generalization) applies the fitted inner loop to every training and test sample to build a modulation dataset, then trains a task-specific model (DDPM+UNet for generation, VMamba for classification) on these latents. ReLIFT, a single-task variant, modifies the SIREN activation by inserting a residual connection after the first-layer sine and scaling input frequencies by a factor γ>1, which the Fourier-Bessel analysis shows amplifies higher-order harmonics while preserving fundamental components, thereby closing the convergence-capacity gap without adding parameters.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ 64², LIFT achieves a reconstruction PSNR of 39.4 dB and rFID of 2.6, surpassing the previous best (mNIF-L: 34.5 dB / 5.8 rFID) by 4.9 dB and 55% respectively. For generation, LIFT attains an FID of 10.0 with Recall 0.631 and F1 0.742, outperforming mNIF-L (FID 13.2, Recall 0.544) and DPF (FID 13.2, Recall 0.347). On ShapeNet 64³, LIFT sets a new reconstruction benchmark with MSE 0.00053 and PSNR 35.2 dB (28× lower MSE than the second-best). For CIFAR-10 classification, LIFT reaches 95.47% Top-1 accuracy with 10 augmentations, exceeding Spatial Functa (90.30%) and matching ResNet-50 (95.36%) while using only 4.04M parameters and 0.136 GFLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LIFT decisively outperforms Functa [10] across all CelebA-HQ metrics (PSNR 39.4 vs 26.6, rFID 2.6 vs 28.4, FID 10.0 vs 40.4) while using 158× fewer FLOPs (54.52M vs 8.602G). Against the strongest INR-based competitor mNIF-L [43], LIFT improves reconstruction PSNR by 4.9 dB, rFID by 55%, generation FID by 24%, and Recall by 16%, all while requiring 6.2× fewer FLOPs (54.52M vs 340M) and 37× fewer parameters. On 3D ShapeNet, LIFT achieves a 28× reduction in MSE (0.00053 vs 0.0166 for mNIF-L) and a 13.75 dB PSNR gain, with 47× fewer FLOPs than GEM [9]. For generation, LIFT's FID of 10.0 is competitive with DDMI [29] (9.7) but with substantially better Recall (0.631 vs 0.408) and F1 (0.742 vs 0.524), and at a fraction of the computational cost (54.52M vs 27.99G FLOPs). On CIFAR-10 classification, LIFT (95.47%) surpasses Spatial Functa [1] (90.30%) by 5.17 points using half the FLOPs (0.136 vs 0.271 GFLOPs) and matches ResNet-50 [15] (95.36%) with 5.8× fewer parameters (4.04M vs 23.52M). The only metric where a baseline edges out LIFT is generation FID on CelebA-HQ, where DDMI achieves 9.7 vs LIFT's 10.0, though DDMI requires 272.7M parameters and 27.99G FLOPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

LIFT extends the Functa line of work (global latent vectors with shift modulation) by replacing the single global latent with a three-level hierarchical latent generator (global, intermediate, local) that feeds parallel localized SIREN-based P-MLPs, directly addressing the fine-grained detail limitation of global representations identified in Functa and mNIF. The meta-learning training protocol is adapted from CAVIA's inner/outer loop structure, while the ReLIFT variant modifies SIREN's sinusoidal activations with a frequency scaling factor and a residual connection, grounded in a Fourier-Bessel expansion analysis. Compared to Spatial Functa, which also uses spatially structured latents, LIFT achieves comparable or better accuracy at roughly half the FLOPs by partitioning the domain into parallel sub-MLPs rather than relying on a single deep network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the dramatic efficiency gap: LIFT matches or exceeds mNIF-L's reconstruction quality (39.4 vs 34.5 dB PSNR on CelebA-HQ) while using ~158× fewer FLOPs than Functa and ~60× fewer than GEM, making multiscale INR encoding feasible at scale. The hierarchical latent design offers a principled mechanism for balancing local fidelity and global coherence that could transfer to other modulated-architecture settings. Open questions include whether the meta-learning overhead (inner-loop SGD per sample) remains tractable at ImageNet-scale resolutions, whether the approach generalizes to video or audio as the paper claims but does not deeply evaluate, and whether the 3D generation results (comparable MMD/coverage) can be pushed further given the modest parameter budget.
