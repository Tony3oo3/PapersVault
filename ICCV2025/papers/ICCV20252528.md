## Problem

2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) enables accurate mesh extraction by using flat primitives aligned to surfaces, but its rendering quality degrades noticeably compared to 3D Gaussian Splatting, especially on in-the-wild scenes. The paper addresses this gap by introducing a new primitive representation that achieves 3DGS-level rendering quality while retaining the geometric accuracy of 2D primitives for reliable mesh extraction, and further tackles the storage overhead of textured primitives through sparsity-aware compression.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252528/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of calibrated images and an SfM point cloud (from COLMAP) as input, optionally augmented with 10K Fibonacci-distributed points on a circumscribing sphere to model sky and distant objects. It produces a scene representation of textured billboard primitives (each with a 16×16 RGB texture and 16×16 alpha map, quantized to 8 bits) that can be rendered for novel views, fused via TSDF into a 3D mesh, or directly rasterized in a graphics engine (e.g., Blender) to enable ray-tracing effects without explicit mesh extraction.

## Method

BBSplat replaces the Gaussian-distributed transparency of 2DGS with two learnable per-primitive textures: an RGB texture for pixel-wise color and an alpha texture for arbitrary silhouette shape, while inheriting the 2DGS plane parametrization (position, 2D scale, quaternion rotation, spherical harmonics). During rendering, a ray-splat intersection yields (u,v) plane coordinates that are bilinearly sampled from the textures, and the final color is computed as the sum of view-dependent SH color and the sampled RGB offset, disentangling lighting from albedo. A visibility-weighted regularization term pushes low-impact billboards toward zero RGB and Gaussian-shaped alpha, encouraging sparse textures that enable 8-bit quantization plus dictionary-based (ZIP) compression for up to 17× storage reduction over 3DGS. The densification strategy adapts MCMC sampling by adjusting only the alpha-map (rather than the covariance matrix) when cloning primitives, preserving rendering state without violating the arbitrary-shape constraint.

## Results

On DTU at Full HD resolution, BBSplat achieves a state-of-the-art PSNR of 29.72 with only 60K primitives and 32 MB storage, outperforming 3DGS (28.39 PSNR, 121 MB) and 3DGS-MCMC (29.68 PSNR, 121 MB). Under fixed storage constraints (Table 1), BBSplat leads on all three datasets in PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS while using 2–5× fewer primitives than competitors (e.g., 15K vs. 30K on DTU, 30K vs. 80K on Tanks&Temples). On Mip-NeRF-360, BBSplat achieves the best LPIPS (0.2336) at fixed storage but trails 3DGS-MCMC in PSNR on highly detailed outdoor scenes, which the authors identify as a limitation of texture-based representation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3DGS, BBSplat wins on DTU (29.72 vs. 28.39 PSNR) and Tanks&Temples (25.12 vs. 24.31) while using 3.8× less storage on DTU (32 MB vs. 121 MB); it slightly trails on Mip-NeRF-360 (28.33 vs. 28.97 PSNR) where highly detailed outdoor textures are challenging. Against 3DGS-MCMC, BBSplat edges out on DTU (29.72 vs. 29.68 PSNR) with 8.5× fewer primitives (60K vs. 512K), but loses on Tanks&Temples (25.12 vs. 25.56) and Mip-NeRF-360 (28.33 vs. 29.52). Against 2DGS, BBSplat dominates across all datasets in both PSNR and LPIPS (e.g., 29.72 vs. 28.04 on DTU) while also reducing storage (32 MB vs. 59 MB). Against Mip-Splatting and Mini-Splatting, BBSplat achieves superior PSNR on DTU and Tanks&Temples with substantially fewer primitives. In the compression comparison (Table 3), BBSplat at 7 MB storage outperforms Reduced-3DGS (30 MB, 28.31 PSNR) and Compact-3DGS (10 MB, 28.39 PSNR) in both quality and size, achieving a ×17 reduction over 3DGS. For mesh extraction, BBSplat reaches 0.91 Chamfer Distance on DTU—close to 2DGS (0.80) but far better than 3DGS (1.96) and SuGaR (1.33)—while simultaneously delivering the best NVS PSNR and lowest storage.

## Relation to Prior Work

BBSplat extends 2D Gaussian Splatting by replacing the Gaussian-distributed transparency with learnable per-primitive RGB textures and alpha maps, drawing on the classic billboard-clouds idea from real-time graphics. It inherits the ray-splat intersection and plane parametrization from 2DGS, adopts the MCMC densification strategy from 3DGS-MCMC (modified to adjust alpha maps instead of covariance scales), and reuses the spherical-harmonics-plus-texture-offset color formulation from Texture-GS. The key conceptual departure is treating each flat primitive as a small textured quad rather than a Gaussian blob, which shifts the representational burden from implicit spatial distribution to explicit pixel-level appearance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By closing the rendering-quality gap between 2D and 3D Gaussian Splatting while retaining 2DGS's accurate mesh extraction, BBSplat offers a single representation that serves both photorealistic NVS and downstream geometry tasks, with up to ×17 storage reduction over 3DGS. The explicit planar formulation also unlocks classical rasterization effects (ray tracing, per-polygon editing) without a separate mesh-extraction step. A noted limitation is degraded performance on highly detailed outdoor scenes (Mip-NeRF-360), suggesting the fixed 16×16 texture resolution and planar assumption may not capture all high-frequency geometric detail; scaling texture size or allowing non-planar primitives are natural open directions.
