## Problem

Deploying personalized OOD detection on resource-constrained edge devices is infeasible with existing approaches because both conventional fine-tuning and federated learning require gradient computation and backpropagation, which exceed the computational capacity of most edge hardware. This paper addresses the gap by eliminating all on-device training while still adapting the model to user-specific data distributions and preserving data privacy during cloud-device communication.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252688/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes video clips captured on an edge device as input. The device runs a fixed vision backbone to extract feature tensors (C×T×W×H), then applies Shapley-value-based channel importance scoring to select the top 50% most informative channels, which are encrypted via homomorphic encryption before upload. The cloud returns personalized classifier parameters (not raw predictions), which the device decrypts and applies to its local model to produce a binary OOD/ID decision. The unusual I/O structure is that the device sends encrypted features up and receives encrypted parameters back, rather than sending raw data or receiving final predictions.

## Method

SecDOOD uses a HyperNetwork trained in the cloud with a meta-learning objective to map device-extracted features directly to personalized classifier parameters, completely bypassing on-device backpropagation. The HyperNetwork is optimized so that the generated parameters minimize the OOD detection loss on device-specific real-time samples, effectively learning how to adapt a fixed backbone's features to heterogeneous device distributions. A dynamic feature sampling module computes Shapley values over feature channels to identify the most informative ones, encrypting only the top 50% via homomorphic encryption to reduce on-device computational overhead while preserving the HyperNetwork's ability to generate effective parameters. The visual backbone remains frozen throughout; only the lightweight classifier head is personalized per device, reducing on-device FLOPs by approximately 3× compared to full on-device training.

## Results

On the Near-OOD task (Table 3), SecDOOD achieves FPR95 of 7.48 and AUROC of 98.39 on UCF101, reducing FPR95 by 38.4% compared to the best on-device-training method (VIM, FPR95=12.52). On the Far-OOD task with Kinetics-600 as ID (Table 1), SecDOOD attains an average AUROC of 82.15 and FPR95 of 57.14, surpassing all 'Without On-Device Training' baselines by over 25 points in AUROC. On EPIC-Kitchens (Far-OOD, Kinetics as ID), SecDOOD achieves AUROC 89.01 and FPR95 31.37, outperforming the best fully-trained method (ASH) by +0.96 AUROC and −3.01 FPR95. In FLOPs, SecDOOD reduces on-device computation by 3× compared to traditional on-device training, with only a 16% increase over pure inference due to encryption/decryption.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SecDOOD is compared against nine OOD detection baselines (MSP, Energy, MaxLogit, ReAct, ASH, GEN, KNN, VIM, Mahalanobis) that all require on-device training or fine-tuning, plus two 'no-training' ablations (randomly initialized classifier and hypernetwork). On the Near-OOD task, SecDOOD outperforms VIM (the strongest on-device-training baseline) on 3 of 4 datasets, with the largest margin on UCF101 (FPR95 7.48 vs. 12.52, a 38.4% reduction) and EPIC-Kitchens (AUROC 74.93 vs. 63.41). Mahalanobis achieves a lower FPR95 on UCF101 (12.14) but collapses on EPIC-Kitchens (FPR95 98.69, AUROC 42.99), while SecDOOD maintains consistent performance across all four datasets. On the Far-OOD task (Kinetics-600 as ID), SecDOOD achieves the best average AUROC (82.15) among all methods, beating GEN (80.30) and LogitNorm (80.30), though GEN and LogitNorm have slightly lower average FPR95 (54.90, 55.88 vs. 57.14). Against the no-training ablations, SecDOOD improves AUROC by over 20 points (e.g., 82.15 vs. 62.55 for Ini-Classifier on Far-OOD), confirming that the HyperNetwork-based personalization is essential rather than a trivial gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

SecDOOD sits at the intersection of OOD detection (building on post-hoc scorers like MSP, Energy, ReAct, ASH, GEN, KNN, VIM) and cloud-device collaborative learning (federated learning, split learning, knowledge distillation). Its key departure from federated learning and split learning is the complete elimination of on-device backpropagation: instead of gradient-based local updates, a cloud-trained HyperNetwork generates personalized classifier parameters from device-extracted features, drawing on the meta-learning framing of HyperNetworks (Ha et al., 2022). The dynamic feature sampling module repurposes Shapley-value-based channel importance estimation (typically used for interpretability) as a mechanism to reduce encryption overhead, and homomorphic encryption is applied selectively rather than to the full feature tensor—a combination not seen in prior privacy-preserving inference work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper addresses a concrete deployment gap: OOD detection on resource-constrained edge devices (autonomous vehicles, medical equipment) where backpropagation is infeasible, and it demonstrates that a backprop-free cloud-device pipeline can match or exceed fully fine-tuned on-device methods across five video datasets and both near- and far-OOD settings. The 3× FLOPs reduction on-device is practically significant for edge hardware. However, the framework assumes stable cloud connectivity and incurs a 16% FLOPs overhead from encryption/decryption, and the authors acknowledge that reducing communication costs and adapting to ultra-low-power devices remain open. A broader open question is whether the HyperNetwork-based parameter generation generalizes beyond video to other modalities or to settings where the cloud model itself must be updated over time.
