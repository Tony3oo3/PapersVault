## Problem

Existing language instruction-guided online 3D reconstruction systems rely on explicit instructions or pre-built queryable maps, making them unable to handle implicit and complex instructions that require multi-step reasoning (e.g., 'Fixtures used for hygiene. Find a smaller one suitable for a child'). This paper introduces the reasoning reconstruction task, which demands online 3D reconstruction of only instruction-relevant instances from implicit instructions, and notes that no established benchmark exists for evaluating such capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250613/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes posed RGB-D keyframe sequences (9 keyframes per Fragment Bounding Volume, 4 cm voxel resolution) and a single implicit language instruction, producing an incrementally updated 3D geometric map (TSDF + Marching Cubes mesh) with only instruction-relevant instances segmented and fused. The 2D reasoning segmentation stage outputs 0, 1, or n candidate instances per view, each with structured text attributes (color, class) and a binary mask, supporting multi-class, multi-target, single-target, and zero-target output configurations. The process is fully online and can halt at any keyframe to identify targets from the current partial map.

## Method

LIRA operates in three stages: (I) a fine-tuned LLaVA-7B VLM (via LoRA) reasons over the implicit instruction and RGB images to identify instruction-relevant 2D candidate instances, predicting structured attributes and a [SEG] token whose embedding prompts SAM's mask decoder; (II) the proposed Text-enhanced Instance Fusion (TIFF) module back-projects candidate masks into the TSDF volume and fuses them using L transformer blocks with masked cross-attention over voxel features (averaged from N views), text features (from [SEG] embeddings), and 3D bounding box features, simultaneously fusing all keyframes within an FBV rather than frame-by-frame, followed by DBSCAN clustering on cosine similarity; (III) a separate LLM (ChatGPT-4o-mini) performs global reasoning over fused instance attributes to identify the final target. The key differentiator is the integration of text features into the learning-based fusion process and the multi-keyframe simultaneous fusion within an FBV, which yields richer discriminative representations than prior frame-by-frame approaches.

## Results

On the ReasonRecon benchmark for reasoning reconstruction, LIRA achieves AP=11.57, AP50=34.39, and AP25=66.24, outperforming all 12 baselines across every metric. The most dramatic margin is on AP25, where LIRA (66.24) nearly doubles the best baseline BBQ (35.86), indicating a strong ability to locate target instances even at low IoU thresholds. With a stronger MLLM backbone (LLaVA-13B + ChatGPT-4o), LIRA* further improves to AP=12.49, AP50=35.32, AP25=67.34. The LIRA-Fast variant achieves real-time inference at 5.63 KFPS while retaining AP25=61.75, substantially ahead of OVIR-3D (18.99) and Open-Fusion (19.50) at comparable or lower speeds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LIRA outperforms all 12 baselines on every metric (AP, AP50, AP25) in the reasoning reconstruction task. The closest competitor is BBQ (AP=11.52, AP50=22.17, AP25=35.86), which LIRA surpasses by a narrow margin on AP (+0.05) but by a large margin on AP50 (+12.22) and AP25 (+30.38), demonstrating LIRA's superior ability to precisely localize target instances. Among offline point-cloud segmentation methods, 3D-STMN is the strongest (AP=10.93, AP25=25.67), yet LIRA still leads by +0.64 AP and +40.57 AP25. Among online queryable-map methods, ConceptGraphs (AP=11.13, AP25=30.55) and Open-Fusion (AP=9.51, AP25=19.50) are the most competitive, but both trail LIRA by 10+ points on AP25. The large AP25 gap across all baselines suggests that existing methods struggle to even roughly locate instruction-relevant instances under implicit reasoning, whereas LIRA's MLLM-based reasoning and text-enhanced fusion (TIFF) substantially improve instance detection recall.

## Relation to Prior Work

LIRA departs from the dominant paradigm of language-guided 3D reconstruction (OVIR-3D, Open-Fusion, ConceptGraphs, BBQ) that builds queryable maps containing all scene objects and then matches instructions against them. Instead, it uses a multimodal LLM (LLaVA) to actively reason about implicit instructions and detect only instruction-relevant 2D candidate instances, drawing on the 2D reasoning segmentation lineage (LISA, LISA++, GSVA) but extending it to support multi-target and zero-target outputs. The instance fusion stage (TIFF) builds on EmbodiedSAM's learning-based fusion but introduces text features and multi-keyframe simultaneous fusion within a Fragment Bounding Volume, a departure from the frame-by-frame strategy used by prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of ReasonRecon (12,500 scene-instruction pairs with implicit reasoning) fills a critical gap: no prior benchmark systematically evaluates 3D reconstruction under implicit, multi-step reasoning instructions, which is the natural way humans communicate with embodied agents. The stark AP25/AP25-vs-AP gap (66.24 vs 11.57) reveals that current methods excel at identifying the correct instance but remain weak at precise geometric reconstruction, pointing to an open problem in 3D-space optimization. The real-time capability (5.63 KFPS with LIRA-Fast) makes the approach practically deployable for embodied agents, though the multi-model pipeline (VLM + SAM + LLM + fusion network) raises questions about robustness and generalization beyond ScanNetV2-style indoor scenes.
