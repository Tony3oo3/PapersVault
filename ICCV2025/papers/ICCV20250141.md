## Problem

Existing data-driven neuronal morphology generators (MorphVAE, MorphGrower) either decompose neurons into sequential branches with exponential computational cost as complexity grows, or require real reference morphologies during inference, limiting them to conditional generation. No unconditional model can efficiently synthesize high-fidelity morphologies of complex long-range projection neurons while maintaining stable computational cost across varying structural complexity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250141/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard Gaussian noise as input to the DDPM and produces a full 3D neuronal morphology represented as a tree T=(V,E) with skeleton points and directed edges. During training, the auxiliary smoothing CNN consumes real branches corrupted with random noise blocks (K blocks of amplitude a over intervals [s_k, e_k)) and outputs the original undistorted branch. All point clouds are normalized to fixed-size sets via farthest point sampling, and evaluation uses 128-point downsampled representations. No real reference data is needed at inference time, making the model fully unconditional.

## Method

MorphoGen is a three-stage global-to-local pipeline: (1) a DDPM with a DiT-3D backbone learns the distribution of neuronal point clouds and generates coarse global geometry via iterative denoising; (2) L1-medial skeletonization extracts skeleton points, which are then linked into a tree via a growth-guided MST algorithm that penalizes edges deviating from the principal morphological direction (computed via PCA every 10 connected points) and enforces binary bifurcation constraints; (3) a ResNet-18 1D-CNN smooths individual branches, trained with an inverse strategy where real branches are distorted with random noise blocks and the network learns to recover the originals via MSE loss. The key architectural insight is decoupling global structure prediction (point cloud diffusion, cost-independent of branching depth) from local topology reconstruction and refinement, avoiding the exponential branch-count growth that plagues layer-by-layer seq2seq approaches.

## Results

On three long-range projection neuron datasets (IT, CT, PT), MorphoGen achieves 1-NNA of 66.5%/70.7% (IT), 74.8%/80.9% (CT), and 66.3%/71.8% (PT) under CD/EMD, outperforming MorphGrower by approximately 3.0%, 11.8%, and 22.0% respectively. MorphoGen also leads on MMD (e.g., 0.01624/0.04699 for CT vs. 0.02160/0.04945 for MorphGrower) and JSD for CT and PT neurons, while MorphGrower retains a slight edge on COV for IT and CT. Training time is approximately 33 hours for MorphoGen versus 37–195 hours for MorphGrower depending on neuronal complexity, and MorphoGen requires no real reference data during inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MorphoGen is compared against two learning-based baselines: MorphVAE (ICML 2021) and MorphGrower (2024). MorphVAE performs poorly across all metrics, with 1-NNA at 100% (indicating generated neurons are trivially distinguishable from real data) and JSD above 0.91, confirming it produces simplistic, homogeneous morphologies unsuitable for complex neurons. Against MorphGrower, MorphoGen wins decisively on 1-NNA (by 3.0% on IT, 11.8% on CT, and 22.0% on PT) and on MMD for all three datasets, while also achieving lower JSD on CT (0.161 vs. 0.191) and PT (0.124 vs. 0.232). MorphGrower retains a modest advantage on COV for IT (~2.6%) and CT (~1.4%), and on JSD for IT (0.133 vs. 0.159). Critically, MorphoGen operates unconditionally (no real reference needed at inference), whereas MorphGrower requires a real morphology as a condition, and MorphoGen's training time (~33 h) is stable across datasets versus MorphGrower's 37–195 h that scales with branching complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant branch-sequential paradigm in neuronal morphology generation (MorphVAE's 3D-walk VAE and MorphGrower's layer-by-layer pairwise branch growth) by reframing the problem as global point cloud generation followed by local structural refinement. It builds on DDPM-based point cloud generation (using DiT-3D as backbone) and L1-medial skeleton extraction, combining them with a novel growth-guided linking algorithm and an auxiliary ResNet-18 smoothing network. The key conceptual shift is treating the neuron as a holistic 3D shape rather than a sequence of dependent branches, which eliminates the exponential cost growth with branching depth that plagues prior seq2seq approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work is the first to evaluate unconditional neuronal morphology generation on real, high-resolution long-range projection neurons (mouse prefrontal cortex, fMOST-imaged), moving beyond the synthetic or simple morphologies used in prior benchmarks. The practical efficiency gain (~55% less training time, stable inference cost regardless of complexity) makes large-scale connectome modeling more feasible. Key limitations include the non-end-to-end pipeline (separate CNN for smoothing), evaluation restricted to one brain region and two baselines, and the fact that COV (coverage) still favors MorphGrower on two of three datasets, suggesting the global-to-local trade-off is not uniformly favorable. Open questions include whether the smoothing stage can be integrated into a unified generative objective and whether the approach generalizes to other neuron types or brain regions.
