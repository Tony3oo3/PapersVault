## Problem

Traditional structure-from-motion pipelines (COLMAP, GLOMAP) frequently fail on outward-facing spherical camera motion sequences, producing incomplete or inside-out reconstructions. While calibrated spherical SFM was previously solved, the uncalibrated case with a single unknown focal length remained open: two-view spherical motion is always a critical case for self-calibration, and it was unclear whether self-calibration is possible from multiple views or how to exploit the spherical constraint in a global pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of perspective RGB images captured with (near-)spherical camera motion and pre-extracted feature matches between image pairs, assuming a single unknown focal length with known principal point and zero skew (K(f) = diag(f,f,1)). It outputs camera extrinsics (rotation and translation per view), the recovered focal length, and a triangulated 3D point cloud. The pipeline is designed to tolerate deviations from perfect spherical motion by relaxing the spherical constraint in the final bundle adjustment stage.

## Method

The core insight is that any spherical fundamental matrix is equivalently a spherical essential matrix at a different focal length (Propositions 1–2), allowing the calibrated three-point minimal solver to be reused directly for uncalibrated pairwise estimation via LO-RANSAC. The global pipeline then (1) estimates all pairwise spherical fundamental matrices, (2) performs a one-dimensional search over focal length f, evaluating a cost function that measures agreement between absolute rotations (initialized by temporal chaining or hybrid rotation averaging) and the f-dependent relative rotations R^{ij}(f) derived from the analytic relationship θ'_xy = atan2(2f·sin θ_xy, (1+f²)cos θ_xy + (1−f²)), (3) refines f and all rotations via non-linear optimization with a robust loss, and (4) triangulates points and runs spherical-constrained bundle adjustment followed by general bundle adjustment to absorb residual deviations from the spherical assumption.

## Results

On the PhoneSweep dataset (combined iPhone 13 and Nexus 5X), the proposed method achieves perfect RRA (100% at all thresholds) and AUC@30 of 90.87, compared to 67.04 for COLMAP, 55.79 for GLOMAP, and 60.41 for RadialSFM. The Absolute Focal Error (AFE) is 0.58%, versus 2.95% for COLMAP and 4.59% for GLOMAP. On the Deep View Video dataset with metric ground truth, the method (without general BA) achieves 93.12% recall (cameras within 10 cm) and AUC@30 of 91.99, substantially outperforming COLMAP (58.15% recall, AUC@30=36.44) and GLOMAP (15.80% recall, AUC@30=21.03). Even COLMAP with ground-truth focal length (AUC@30=83.16 on PhoneSweep) is outperformed by the proposed uncalibrated method (90.87).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all three baselines on both datasets across every metric. Against COLMAP (Schönberger & Frahm, CVPR 2016), the gap is most dramatic in translation accuracy: RTA@5 improves from 56.18% to 84.83% on PhoneSweep and from 18.76% to 93.63% on Deep View Video, while AUC@30 jumps from 67.04 to 90.87 (PhoneSweep) and 36.44 to 91.99 (Deep View Video). Against GLOMAP (Pan et al., ECCV 2024), the advantage is even larger, with GLOMAP frequently failing to reconstruct the correct outward-facing motion (e.g., only 15.80% recall on Deep View Video). Against RadialSFM (Hruby et al. / Larsson et al., CVPR 2023 / ECCV 2020), the proposed method wins on all metrics while also being faster (3.97 s/image vs. 11.53 s/image on PhoneSweep). Notably, even COLMAP given the ground-truth focal length (AUC@30=83.16) is outperformed by the fully uncalibrated proposed method (90.87), indicating that the spherical-motion-aware pipeline provides a structural advantage beyond correct calibration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Ventura's calibrated spherical SFM [37] to the uncalibrated case with a single unknown focal length, building on the three-point minimal solver for the spherical essential matrix. The key conceptual departure from Sweeney et al. [36], who proposed uncalibrated spherical SFM using a rotation-only solver limited to distant points, is the non-trivial observation that spherical fundamental matrices are themselves spherical essential matrices (Propositions 1–2), enabling direct reuse of the calibrated solver. The global pipeline also departs from the incremental radial-camera-model approaches of Larsson et al. [21] and Hruby et al. [14], instead using rotation averaging and a one-dimensional focal-length search over pairwise relative rotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for casual 3D capture: a user can obtain a dense reconstruction by simply spinning with a phone, a workflow that standard SFM pipelines (COLMAP, GLOMAP) handle poorly or fail on entirely. The PhoneSweep dataset with pseudo-ground truth fills a gap in benchmarking for spherical-motion SFM. Open questions include extending the method to handle radial distortion, varying focal lengths, and integrating learning-based approaches; the current assumption of a single unknown focal length with known principal point limits generality. The finding that even COLMAP with ground-truth intrinsics underperforms the proposed method suggests the core difficulty is geometric (critical motion structure) rather than purely calibration-related.
