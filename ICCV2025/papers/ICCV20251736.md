## Problem

Graduated Non-Convexity (GNC) methods for robust estimation rely on a fixed annealing factor and a fixed final shape parameter, both of which are data-dependent and cannot be universally tuned. This leads to suboptimal accuracy or wasted iterations, and the continuous monotonic decrease of σ prevents recovery from a poorly chosen early annealing step. The paper asks whether a search-based, adaptive strategy can replace these fixed hyperparameters while remaining problem-agnostic.

## Input / Output

For 3D registration, the method consumes N potentially outlier-contaminated 3D point correspondences (p_i^A, p_i^B) between two point clouds and outputs a rotation R ∈ SO(3) and translation t ∈ R³, solved via Umeyama's closed-form solution at each GNC iteration. For pose graph optimization, it takes relative SE(2) edge measurements along a trajectory (with a fraction of loops randomly perturbed as outliers) and outputs all global camera poses, solved via GTSAM's Levenberg-Marquardt optimizer. The method is solver-agnostic: it wraps around any GNC-based inner solver and only controls the shape parameter σ schedule, scoring, and termination.

## Method

SAC-GNC replaces the fixed annealing factor with a sample-and-consensus loop: at each GNC iteration it draws T candidate annealing factors uniformly from [γ_GNC·α⁻, γ_GNC·α⁺], solves the robust cost for each, and scores the resulting models using MSAC (truncated squared residuals). A breadth-first priority queue (sorted by tree depth, then score) selects which hypothesis to expand next, allowing the algorithm to backtrack from a poor σ choice rather than being locked into a monotonic decrease. Two additional contributions round out the framework: a data-driven σ₀ initialization derived analytically from the maximum initial residual (setting the Geman-McClure weight to 0.95), and convergence-based stopping criteria that replace the fixed σ_end threshold. The result is the first GNC variant that integrates discrete sample-consensus scoring into the continuous annealing schedule, with two operating modes (SAC-GNC for speed, SAC-GNC++ for accuracy) controlled by the number of trials T and queue parameters.

## Results

On 3D registration, SAC-GNC achieves the best accuracy-efficiency trade-off across all four datasets. On ModelNet (≈50.8% inliers), SAC-GNC reaches mAA (e_R, 5°) = 0.708 and (e_t, 0.3 m) = 0.976 in 1.02 ms, outperforming GNCp (0.627 / 0.967 in 0.96 ms) and TEASER++ (0.613 / 0.963 in 6.53 ms). On the challenging 3DMatch low-inlier set (≈11.6%), SAC-GNC scores 0.421 / 0.544 in 7.05 ms versus GNCp's 0.348 / 0.509 in 25.7 ms and TEASER++'s 0.287 / 0.429 in 125 ms. For pose graph optimization on INTEL and CSAIL, SAC-GNC is substantially faster than GTSAM-GNC and surpasses it in accuracy beyond 50% outlier loops.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAC-GNC outperforms all four 3D registration baselines (RANSAC, FGR, TEASER++, GNCp) on rotation error mAA (e_R, 5°) across every dataset, with the largest margins on low-inlier data: on 3DMatch ↓ (≈11.6% inliers) it scores 0.421 vs. GNCp's 0.348 (+7.3 pts) and TEASER++'s 0.287 (+13.4 pts). On translation error (e_t, 0.3 m), SAC-GNC leads on ModelNet (0.976 vs. GNCp 0.967) and 3DMatch ↓ (0.544 vs. GNCp 0.509), though RANSAC edges it out on KITTI (0.463 vs. 0.363). In terms of efficiency, SAC-GNC is 3.6× faster than GNCp on 3DMatch ↓ (7.05 ms vs. 25.7 ms) and 17.7× faster than TEASER++ (7.05 ms vs. 125 ms); TEASER++ fails entirely on 3DMatch ↑ (>30 min per instance). For pose graph optimization, SAC-GNC is substantially faster than GTSAM-GNC at all outlier levels and surpasses it in trajectory accuracy beyond 50% perturbed loops. The adaptive SAC-GNC++ variant achieves the highest accuracy (e.g., 0.435 on 3DMatch ↓) at roughly 8× the runtime of SAC-GNC, confirming the accuracy–efficiency trade-off between the two configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAC-GNC extends the Graduated Non-Convexity (GNC) framework, which has been widely adopted in 3D registration (FGR, TEASER++) and pose graph optimization, by replacing the fixed annealing factor with an adaptive, sample-consensus-driven search over shape parameters. It builds most directly on GNCp (Sidhartha et al., 2023), which introduced adaptive annealing via the Hessian of the cost function, but departs from it by being problem-agnostic and using a tree-search strategy with model scoring rather than problem-specific curvature information. The paper also borrows scoring mechanisms (MSAC, RANSAC inlier counting) from the sample-consensus literature, explicitly framing its contribution as a bridge between what the field has treated as contrasting paradigms: continuous (GNC) and discrete (RANSAC-like) robust estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GNC-based methods are the de facto standard for robust estimation in SLAM, SfM, and 3D registration pipelines, so improvements in both accuracy and efficiency have broad practical impact. The paper demonstrates that the previously assumed dichotomy between continuous and discrete robust estimators is not a strict one, opening the door to further hybrid strategies. However, the method introduces additional hyperparameters (T, Q_add, Q_size, α±, inlier threshold for scoring) that require tuning per problem, and the computational overhead of T parallel trials per iteration may limit applicability to very large-scale or real-time settings. An open question is whether the tree-search strategy generalizes cleanly to problems where model scoring is expensive or where the hypothesis space is continuous and high-dimensional.
