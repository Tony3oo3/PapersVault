## Problem

Automatically localizing artifacts in novel views of 3D scene reconstructions is difficult because no-reference metrics produce only global scores without spatial maps, full-reference metrics require aligned ground truth that is unavailable for synthesized views, and the sole prior cross-reference metric (CrossScore) is trained to mimic SSIM, which is known to be poorly aligned with human perception. Additionally, no benchmark dataset exists to evaluate cross-reference metrics in the 3D reconstruction setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251777/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single novel view image and a set of unaligned training view images from the same scene (e.g., the 3DGS training set). It produces a per-pixel artifact similarity map at the original image resolution, where low values indicate regions poorly supported by the training distribution. No spatial alignment or camera poses between views are required. For the inpainting application, the similarity map is further thresholded into a binary mask that drives iterative LaMa inpainting.

## Method

Puzzle Similarity embeds both the test image and all reference images through a frozen pre-trained SqueezeNet backbone, then for each spatial location in the test embedding computes the maximum cosine similarity against every feature vector from all reference images at all spatial positions, effectively discarding spatial correspondence to be robust to camera motion. This best-match similarity is computed independently at three convolutional layers (ℓ ∈ {2, 3, 4}) with empirically chosen weights (0.67, 0.2, 0.13), and the resulting maps are bilinearly upscaled and combined via a weighted affine sum. The computation is expressed as a row-max over an outer product of flattened, L2-normalized embeddings, enabling efficient blockwise GPU implementation. No training or fine-tuning is required; the method is purely inference-time patch matching in a learned perceptual feature space, making it trivially adaptable to other domains by swapping the backbone.

## Results

On the novel human-labeled artifact dataset (36 renderings across 12 scenes from Mip-NeRF360, Tanks and Temples, and Deep Blending), PuzzleSim achieves a mean Pearson correlation of 0.615±0.120 and Spearman correlation of 0.474±0.137 with human-perceived artifacts, outperforming all no-reference, cross-reference, and full-reference baselines. The method also exhibits the lowest standard deviation across scenes (0.120 vs. 0.204 for CrossScore), indicating more consistent artifact localization across diverse artifact types (holes, blurry textures, color distortions). Notably, PuzzleSim surpasses even the best full-reference VDPs, despite requiring no aligned reference image.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PuzzleSim outperforms all five baselines on both Pearson and Spearman correlation. Against the no-reference metrics, the margin is substantial: PAL4VST (0.078) and CNNIQA (0.144) are near-random, PIQE reaches 0.292, and PaQ-2-PiQ reaches 0.402, all well below PuzzleSim's 0.615. Against the main cross-reference baseline CrossScore (0.510±0.204), PuzzleSim gains +0.105 in mean Pearson while also halving the standard deviation (0.120 vs. 0.204), indicating more robust performance across scenes with different artifact types. The paper attributes CrossScore's limitation to its reliance on SSIM as the training target (known to misalign with human perception) and its 14×14 patch resolution from the DINOv2 encoder. Additionally, PuzzleSim is reported to outperform all tested full-reference VDPs (e.g., FovVideoVDP), a striking result given that FR metrics have access to a pixel-aligned ground-truth reference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces Puzzle Similarity as a new cross-reference metric, a category recently established by CrossScore [47] for evaluating novel views using unaligned reference captures. Conceptually, it repurposes the well-known idea of measuring perceptual similarity in pre-trained CNN feature space (as in LPIPS [62]) but reframes it as a patch-level max-cosine-similarity search against a scene-specific reference pool, rather than a pooled global distance. Unlike CrossScore, which requires domain-specific pretraining of a cross-attention module on SSIM targets, Puzzle Similarity is training-free and backbone-agnostic, leveraging off-the-shelf CNNs (SqueezeNet, VGG) with empirically chosen layer weights. The paper also carves new ground by collecting the first human-labeled artifact-map dataset specifically for benchmarking cross-reference metrics in 3D reconstruction, a validation gap the authors explicitly identify.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The human-labeled artifact dataset (36 renderings, 22 annotators) fills a critical benchmarking void for cross-reference metrics in 3D reconstruction, enabling future work to be evaluated against human perception rather than proxy signals like SSIM. The finding that a simple, training-free patch-matching metric outperforms both learned cross-reference methods and even full-reference VDPs suggests that scene-specific context in feature space may be more informative than complex HVS models for this particular task. Open questions include scalability to high-resolution or large-reference-set settings, whether the empirically calibrated layer weights generalize across domains, and whether the max operation can be replaced with differentiable alternatives (e.g., softmax) to enable gradient-based optimization of reconstructions.
