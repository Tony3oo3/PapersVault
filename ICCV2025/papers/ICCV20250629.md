## Problem

Constructing a physically realistic digital twin of a deformable object requires jointly recovering its complete geometry, physical parameters, and appearance, yet existing methods either capture appearance without underlying physics (dynamic NeRFs, dynamic 3D Gaussians), require dense multi-view coverage, or are restricted to specific object types and limited motion ranges. No prior approach can build a fully simulatable, interactive replica from just a handful of sparse RGB-D video views of an object under active human interaction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250629/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three synchronized RGB-D video streams (RealSense-D455, 1–10 s) of a deformable object being manipulated by one or both hands, split 7:3 into training and test segments. It produces a complete spring-mass system (mass nodes, spring topology, per-spring stiffness, collision and damping parameters) coupled with a set of 3D Gaussian splats for photorealistic rendering, together forming a real-time simulatable digital twin. Control points for interaction are extracted from hand segmentation and tracking, and the output model supports arbitrary future control inputs beyond those seen in the training video.

## Method

PhysTwin formulates twin construction as a two-stage optimization over physics, geometry, topology, and appearance parameters. In the first stage, a generative shape prior (TRELLIS) initialized from a single super-resolved RGB frame provides full geometry, which is aligned to observations via a SuperGlue + PnP + ARAP registration pipeline; a hierarchical sparse-to-dense strategy then optimizes non-differentiable topology and homogeneous stiffness with zero-order CMA-ES, followed by first-order gradient descent on a custom differentiable spring-mass simulator to refine dense per-spring stiffness and collision parameters, supervised by 3D-lifted CoTracker3 tracking and Chamfer geometry losses. In the second stage, static 3D Gaussian splats are optimized at the first frame via L1 + D-SSIM rendering loss and deformed at subsequent timesteps through Linear Blend Skinning tied to the spring-mass state. The resulting model runs in real time on GPU (Warp), enabling interactive simulation and model-based robot planning.

## Results

On the Reconstruction & Resimulation task, PhysTwin achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.005, tracking error of 0.009, IoU of 84.4%, and PSNR of 28.21 dB, substantially outperforming GS-Dynamics (CD 0.014, IoU 72.1%, PSNR 26.26) and Spring-Gaus (CD 0.041, IoU 57.6%, PSNR 23.45). On Future Prediction, PhysTwin reaches CD 0.012, IoU 72.5%, and PSNR 25.62 dB, compared to GS-Dynamics (CD 0.041, IoU 49.8%) and Spring-Gaus (CD 0.062, IoU 46.4%). On Generalization to Unseen Interactions, PhysTwin achieves CD 0.013 and IoU 72.18%, versus GS-Dynamics' CD 0.029 and IoU 63.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhysTwin outperforms both baselines across all metrics and all three tasks. Against Spring-Gaus [72] (a physics-based spring-mass + Gaussian splatting method), PhysTwin reduces Chamfer Distance by 88% in reconstruction (0.005 vs. 0.041) and 81% in future prediction (0.012 vs. 0.062), while improving IoU by 26.8 and 26.1 percentage points respectively; the authors note Spring-Gaus is unstable and metrics are reported only for its successful runs. Against GS-Dynamics [69] (a GNN-based neural dynamics model), PhysTwin achieves a 64% lower CD in reconstruction (0.005 vs. 0.014) and a 71% lower CD in future prediction (0.012 vs. 0.041), with IoU gains of 12.3 and 22.7 points. In the generalization-to-unseen-interactions setting, PhysTwin halves the CD of GS-Dynamics (0.013 vs. 0.029) and improves IoU by 8.8 points, indicating that the physics-based representation generalizes more robustly to novel hand configurations and interaction types than the learned neural dynamics model.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhysTwin sits at the intersection of three research threads: dynamic scene reconstruction (NeRF, 3D Gaussian Splatting), physics-based deformable object simulation (Spring-Gaus, PhysGaussian, PIE-NeRF), and learning-based dynamics modeling (GS-Dynamics, graph neural networks). It most directly extends Spring-Gaus by replacing dense-viewpoint requirements with a generative shape prior (TRELLIS) for complete geometry initialization, adding proper Newtonian physics (momentum conservation, gravity), and introducing control-point springs for interactive manipulation. The hierarchical sparse-to-dense optimization strategy—zero-order CMA-ES for non-differentiable topology followed by gradient-based refinement of dense spring stiffness via a custom differentiable simulator—departs from prior works that either manually specify physics parameters or rely on dense observations to mitigate ill-posedness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PhysTwin demonstrates that a single interaction trial from three sparse RGBD cameras suffices to build a simulatable digital twin that generalizes to unseen interactions, which is a practical step toward real-world robotic manipulation without extensive data collection. The real-time simulation capability (via Warp) and model-based planning applications position it as a bridge between perception and control for deformable objects. Limitations include dependence on three synchronized RGBD cameras, restriction to single-object deformable scenarios, and evaluation on a small custom dataset (22 scenarios) with only two augmented baselines, leaving open questions about scalability to multi-object interactions, more complex topologies, and deployment in uncontrolled environments.
