## Problem

Existing RGB-IR multi-modal object detection methods focus on designing complex fusion modules but neglect the mono-modality insufficient learning problem, where each backbone's feature extraction capability degrades during multi-modal joint training. This leads to a prevalent 'Fusion Degradation' phenomenon in which objects detectable by a mono-modal detector are missed by the corresponding multi-modal detector, a problem the paper identifies for the first time via linear probing evaluation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251564/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of spatially aligned RGB and IR images as input and produces oriented bounding boxes with class labels (e.g., car, truck, bus, van, freight car on DroneVehicle; person, car, bicycle on FLIR/LLVIP). The LIF module additionally consumes the RGB image to predict a brightness map supervised by the LAB color-space L-channel, but at inference only the dual-branch backbone and LIF module are active, adding just 0.06M parameters and 6.5 GFLOPs over the CSPDarknet53 baseline.

## Method

M²D-LIF combines two components: (1) Mono-Modality Distillation (M²D), which introduces frozen mono-modal teacher backbones pretrained on single-modality data to distill the multi-modal student backbones via an inner-modality loss (L_IM) aligning same-modality features and a cross-modality loss (L_CM) that uses parameter-free SimAM attention maps as object-location priors to guide object-relevant feature transfer across modalities; (2) Local Illumination-aware Fusion (LIF), a lightweight convolutional module that predicts a spatial brightness map from the RGB image (supervised by the LAB L-channel) and converts it into per-pixel weight maps W_V and W_I bounded by hyperparameters α and β, enabling element-wise weighted fusion that dynamically emphasizes the higher-quality modality in each illumination region. The overall training loss combines the detection loss with M²D and LIF supervision terms, while inference uses only the student backbone plus LIF.

## Results

On the FLIR dataset, M²D-LIF achieves 46.1% mAP, surpassing the previous best multi-modal method UniRGB-IR (44.3%) by 1.8 points while using only 36.5M parameters versus 147.0M. On LLVIP, it reaches 70.8% mAP, outperforming ICAFusion (64.3%) by 6.5 points. On DroneVehicle (OBB detection), the method attains 81.4% mAP₅₀ and 68.1% mAP on the test set, and 84.5% mAP₅₀ on validation, with only 37.1M parameters—substantially fewer than TSFADet (104.7M) or CAGTDet (140.3M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

M²D-LIF outperforms all 22 compared baselines across all three datasets. On FLIR, it beats the strongest multi-modal competitor UniRGB-IR [36] by 1.8 mAP points (46.1 vs. 44.3) and RSDet [46] by 2.3 points, while using roughly 4× fewer parameters than UniRGB-IR and 10× fewer than RSDet. On LLVIP, the margin is larger: 70.8% mAP versus ICAFusion [25] at 64.3% (+6.5) and CFT [23] at 63.9% (+6.9). On DroneVehicle, the method achieves 81.4% mAP₅₀ (test), exceeding OAFA [3] (79.4%) by 2.0 points and CALNet [7] (76.4% val mAP₅₀) by a wide margin, while using only 37.1M parameters compared to 104.7M for TSFADet [35] and 140.3M for CAGTDet [37]. All mono-modal baselines (RetinaNet, Faster R-CNN, S²A Net, YOLOv8m, DDQ-DETR) are substantially outperformed, confirming the benefit of multi-modal fusion. The fusion-method ablation (Table 1) further shows that LIF with M²D training (46.1/70.6/70.8) surpasses attention-based CMX [41] (44.1/68.9/69.5) and weighted IWM [9] (44.9/70.4/69.3) on FLIR/DroneVehicle/LLVIP with only +0.06M parameters and +6.5 GFLOPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in RGB-IR object detection, where prior work (CMX, ICAFusion, CSSA, CALNet, TSFADet) focuses on designing increasingly complex attention-based or cross-modal fusion modules. Instead, it repurposes knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015) in an inverted direction: rather than compressing a large teacher into a small student, it uses pre-trained mono-modal encoders as teachers to prevent the multi-modal student backbones from under-learning during joint training. The linear probing evaluation, borrowed from representation learning, is introduced as a diagnostic tool for multi-modal detectors, and the LIF module deliberately returns to a lightweight weighted fusion strategy that prior work had largely abandoned in favor of attention mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of Fusion Degradation—a phenomenon where multi-modal detectors miss objects that mono-modal detectors catch—provides a concrete diagnostic that the field had not formally articulated, and the finding that a 0.1M-parameter weighted fusion module can outperform 22M+ parameter attention-based fusion (when paired with proper mono-modal learning) challenges the assumption that fusion complexity is necessary. Limitations include reliance on pre-trained mono-modal teachers (adding training infrastructure), evaluation on a single backbone (CSPDarknet53), and the method being specific to RGB-IR pairing; whether the mono-modality distillation principle generalizes to other modality combinations (e.g., LiDAR-camera, radar-camera) remains an open question.
