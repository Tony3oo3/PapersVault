## Problem

Existing gaze target prediction methods rely on N-way classification over a fixed set of target categories, making them unable to generalize to novel object categories that lack gaze annotations. This severely limits their utility in real-world AR/VR and interactive systems where users may search for any object. The paper tackles zero-shot gaze target prediction: identifying the object a user is attending to during categorical visual search for categories never seen during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250561/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a gaze scanpath (up to 20 fixations, each with x/y location, duration binned into 50 ms bins, and sequence index), an RGB image processed by a frozen ResNet-50 into 20×32 patch embeddings, and a dynamically constructed vocabulary of category labels at test time. It outputs a predicted target category via argmax cosine similarity between the gaze embedding and per-category language embeddings, plus a binary target-presence probability from a separate MLP head. The vocabulary size is unconstrained at inference, enabling prediction over arbitrary novel categories.

## Method

GLAM is a dual-encoder vision-language model: a Gaze Encoder (stacked cross-attention/self-attention/FFN submodules) processes fixation encodings (foveal spatial, temporal, and visual patch information) alongside learnable peripheral queries that attend to masked image patches within a small window (w=1) to capture spatial context, plus Target and Presence queries; a Language Encoder (MP-Net + attentional pooling + linear projections) encodes LLM-generated visual search descriptions (appearance and contextual cues). The key training strategy, GLAD, decomposes alignment into two phases: (1) pre-training the attentional pooling and projection layers on ~2,500 LLM-generated search descriptions aligned to frozen CLIP text embeddings via continuously weighted contrastive loss (CWCL), and (2) aligning gaze and language embeddings on the 18-category COCO-Search18 dataset using CWCL plus a binary cross-entropy presence loss. This two-stage design lets the language encoder learn category-discriminative search-description features for thousands of categories before ever seeing gaze data, enabling zero-shot generalization.

## Results

On COCO-Search18, GLAM + GLAD + LLM-Generated Prompts achieves 58.22% target prediction accuracy in the Fully Supervised setting and 30.17% in the Zero-Shot setting, both state-of-the-art. Notably, its Zero-Shot accuracy (30.17%) exceeds the Fully Supervised accuracy of the prior best classification-based method BoVW (27.38%). For target presence prediction, the GLAM + CWCL + Category Label variant achieves the highest accuracy at 85.07%, while the GLAM + GLAD variant reaches 83.48%. The GLAM Gaze Encoder with a classification head also reaches 58.19% Fully Supervised target prediction, confirming the encoder's strength independent of the alignment strategy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLAM + GLAD + LLM-Generated Prompts outperforms all three prior baselines by substantial margins in the Fully Supervised setting: 58.22% vs. 46.45% (GST, +11.77 pp), 38.77% (GazeGNN, +19.45 pp), and 27.38% (BoVW, +30.84 pp). In the Zero-Shot setting, where prior methods cannot operate at all (they are restricted to N-way classification over training categories), GLAM + GLAD achieves 30.17%, surpassing even BoVW's Fully Supervised accuracy of 27.38%. Among GLAM variants, GLAD consistently outperforms naive CWCL contrastive learning: with LLM-generated prompts, GLAD yields 30.17% Zero-Shot vs. 23.82% for CWCL (+6.35 pp), and with category labels, GLAD yields 58.22% Fully Supervised vs. 57.95% for CWCL. The GLAM Gaze Encoder alone (with a classification head) already reaches 58.19% Fully Supervised, indicating that the foveal-peripheral encoding architecture is a major contributor to performance gains over prior scanpath encoders.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the classification-based paradigm of prior gaze target prediction methods (Barz et al. 2020, Nishiyasu et al. 2024, GazeGNN 2024), which were constrained to a fixed set of categories seen during training. It adapts the dual-encoder contrastive alignment framework popularized by CLIP/ALIGN to the gaze domain, using CWCL (Srinivasa et al., NeurIPS 2023) as the core loss to handle non-unique prompts within batches. The key conceptual departure is the GLAD two-stage strategy: pre-training the language encoder on LLM-generated visual search process descriptions for ~2,500 categories (most lacking gaze data) before aligning with gaze scanpaths, drawing on the idea of LLM-generated prompts for zero-shot classification (Menon & Vondrick 2023, Pratt et al. 2023) but repurposing them specifically to encode search-behavior heuristics rather than object appearance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GLAM is the first method to enable zero-shot gaze target prediction, which is essential for real-world AR/VR systems where the set of searchable objects is unbounded and gaze annotation for every category is infeasible. The finding that the GLAD pre-training stage (aligning search descriptions with category labels before gaze alignment) yields roughly a one-third improvement over naive contrastive learning highlights that the language encoder must learn category-discriminative search representations before it can be effectively aligned with gaze. A key limitation is the single-target assumption per scanpath, and evaluation is confined to COCO-Search18's 18 categories, leaving open whether the approach scales to truly open-vocabulary settings with hundreds of novel categories or multi-target searches.
