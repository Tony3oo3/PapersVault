## Problem

Backpropagation-based alignment of diffusion models with reward functions requires end-to-end gradient flow through a ~50-step denoising chain, incurring prohibitive memory costs and risking gradient explosion. Existing work either truncates the chain (losing early-stage supervision) or selectively disables gradients (introducing bias and instability), both yielding suboptimal alignment. The paper asks whether a fundamentally shorter denoising chain can enable complete, stable gradient backpropagation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251416/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a 512×512 RGB image via the fine-tuned diffusion model. During training, a differentiable reward function (HPS v2, PickScore, or Symmetry) scores the generated image against the prompt, and the reward gradient is backpropagated through the shortcut-based denoising chain to update LoRA parameters. At inference, the denoising shortcut is bypassed and the full 50-step DDIM chain of the original SD 1.5 (or SD 3) is used, so no additional inference cost is incurred.

## Method

SHORTFT leverages a trajectory-preserving few-step diffusion model (4-step Hyper-SD distilled from SD 1.5) as a denoising shortcut that skips multiple timesteps in the chain, reducing its length from ~50 to a handful of steps and enabling full gradient backpropagation without explosion. On top of this shortened chain, the method introduces timestep-aware LoRA: the denoising chain is divided into k=4 segments, each with its own LoRA branch stacked progressively, reflecting the distinct temporal dynamics of early (text-driven) vs. late (texture-driven) denoising. A progressive training strategy further eliminates the training-inference gap by initially training with the full chain and gradually introducing shortcuts in later stages, so that LoRA parameters learned under shortcut conditions are refined under the original chain before deployment. The approach is architecture-agnostic (validated on both UNet-based SD 1.5 and Transformer-based SD 3) and requires no gradient checkpointing.

## Results

Under the same computational budget (6 hours on 2 A800 GPUs), SHORTFT achieves HPS v2 = 33.88, PickScore = 24.16, and Symmetry = 0.138 on the HPDv2 test set, outperforming DRaFT-LV (33.13 / 23.35 / 0.418) and DRTune (32.79 / 23.22 / 0.207) on all three metrics. With full 10k-step training, SHORTFT reaches an HPS v2 score of 35.97, surpassing the previously reported DRaFT-LV score. In a human preference study, SHORTFT is preferred over DRTune 79.1% of the time and over DRaFT-LV 64.6% of the time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHORTFT outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against DRaFT-LV (Clark et al., ICLR 2024), which truncates backpropagation to the latter half of the denoising chain, SHORTFT gains +0.75 HPS v2, +0.81 PickScore, and reduces Symmetry from 0.418 to 0.138 — a 2.3× improvement on the symmetry task where DRaFT-LV struggles due to its lack of early-stage supervision. Against DRTune (Wu et al., ECCV 2024), which uses gradient checkpointing to partially propagate gradients but introduces gradient bias, SHORTFT achieves +1.09 HPS v2, +0.94 PickScore, and further reduces Symmetry from 0.207 to 0.138. The unaligned SD 1.5 baseline lags substantially (HPS v2 = 26.91), confirming that the alignment gains are meaningful. In the user study, SHORTFT is preferred over DRTune 79.1% of the time by human evaluators and 85% by GPT-4V, and over DRaFT-LV 64.6% and 70% respectively, indicating consistent perceptual superiority beyond the automated metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

SHORTFT sits at the intersection of two research threads: backpropagation-based reward alignment for diffusion models (DRaFT, ReFL, AlignProp, DRTune) and trajectory-preserving diffusion distillation (Hyper-SD, consistency models, progressive distillation). Prior alignment methods either truncate the backpropagation chain to the latter half (DRaFT, ReFL) or partially disable gradients with checkpointing (AlignProp, DRTune), both introducing supervision gaps or gradient bias. SHORTFT departs from this lineage by repurposing a trajectory-preserving few-step model as a denoising shortcut, shortening the chain so that full gradient backpropagation becomes feasible without truncation or checkpointing. The timestep-aware LoRA design draws on EDiffI's observation of temporal dynamics in denoising, and the progressive training strategy addresses the training-inference gap inherent to using an approximate shortcut during optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SHORTFT offers a practically efficient path to reward-aligned diffusion models: no gradient checkpointing, shorter backpropagation chains, and faster convergence than the current most efficient baseline (DRTune). The denoising-shortcut idea is broadly applicable to any trajectory-preserving distillation output, making it a reusable training accelerator for alignment tasks. However, the method's effectiveness is bounded by the fidelity of the few-step model (residual training-inference gap), and the demonstrated gains over strong baselines are modest (e.g., 33.88 vs. 32.79 HPSv2). Open questions include scalability to larger foundation models, robustness across diverse reward functions beyond the tested set, and whether the progressive training strategy generalizes when the shortcut quality degrades (e.g., with more aggressive distillation).
