## Problem

Existing continual learning methods for generative VLMs update a single shared visual projector to adapt to new tasks, which causes the model to overfit on repetitive instruction templates and neglect diverse text instructions during response generation. This leads to catastrophic forgetting of previously learned visual-linguistic knowledge and degraded instruction-following ability, particularly when task instruction types vary (e.g., classification vs. captioning vs. question answering).

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a free-form text instruction as input. The image is encoded by a frozen ViT-g/14 (optionally with Q-Former) into visual features, while the instruction is tokenized by the LLM's text embedding function. The output is an auto-regressively generated text response (a class name, a caption sentence, or a short answer) produced by a frozen LLM (Vicuna-7B or LLaMA-2-7B). During inference, the model adaptively calibrates how much weight to give the learned mixture-of-experts projection versus the original pre-trained projector based on the semantic relevance of the input to previously seen tasks.

## Method

The proposed Mixture of Visual Projectors (MVP) replaces the single visual projector with N_E=20 expert projectors, each initialized from the pre-trained projector, and a router that selects K=2 experts conditioned on both the image embedding and the instruction token embedding via Top-K softmax. An expert recommendation loss (contrastive distribution over activation patterns) encourages the router to reuse experts associated with semantically similar previous tasks, while an activation bias reduction loss suppresses over-concentration on a few experts. After training each task, expert pruning identifies and reinitializes redundantly activated experts to free capacity for future tasks and break cycles of negative transfer. At inference, an adaptive knowledge aggregation step linearly combines the pre-trained projector output with the MoE-translated output, weighting the MoE branch by the maximum semantic relevance score between the input and stored task embeddings, thereby preserving zero-shot generalization on unseen data.

## Results

On the 18-task continual learning sequence (classification → captioning → QA) using Vicuna-7B, MVP achieves 85.22% average classification accuracy, 77.76% RefCLIP-Score for captioning, and 76.34% QA accuracy under the Last metric, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline MoEAdapter (66.89%, 75.78%, 47.33%). Under the Avg metric, MVP reaches 82.81% classification, 76.95% captioning, and 57.68% QA, while the Transfer metric (zero-shot on unseen tasks) shows 77.94%, 76.63%, and 55.05% respectively. The paper reports classification gaps of 12.80–75.71% over EProj and MoEAdapter, and QA gaps exceeding 28% over MoEAdapter in the Last setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVP dominates all five baselines across every metric and task type. Against the strongest competitor MoEAdapter [50], MVP gains +18.33% in classification (Last), +1.98% in captioning, and +29.01% in QA, with Transfer advantages of +8.38%, +1.15%, and +4.38% respectively. The single-projector VLM methods (GMM [4] and EProj [16]) suffer severe catastrophic forgetting in classification (1.30% and 11.04% Last accuracy vs. MVP's 85.22%) because their shared projector overwrites prior knowledge when trained on subsequent QA tasks. Classical regularization methods (LwF [30], EWC [25]) also collapse in classification (5.00% and 6.08%) while showing inflated QA scores that mask their inability to retain earlier tasks. Notably, MVP is the only method that exceeds the Zero-Shot baseline on all three task types simultaneously under the Last metric, confirming that instruction-grounded expert routing preserves both learned and pre-trained capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual-projector-update paradigm for continual learning of VLMs introduced by GMM (Cao et al., 2024) and EProj (He et al., 2023), which modify a single shared projector to adapt to new tasks. The key departure is replacing that single projector with a Mixture of Visual Projectors (MoE), where each expert specializes in translating visual features for a particular instruction context, and the router conditions on both image and text embeddings. The expert recommendation and pruning mechanisms draw on MoE-based continual learning (Expert Gate, MoEAdapter) but invert the logic: rather than freezing old experts or expanding the router, the paper recommends semantically relevant experts and prunes redundantly activated ones to preserve learning capacity for future tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete failure mode in VLM continual learning—neglect of diverse text instructions when a shared projector overfits to repetitive task templates—which is likely to become increasingly relevant as VLMs are deployed across heterogeneous instruction distributions. The adaptive knowledge aggregation mechanism that calibrates MoE contributions at inference time based on semantic relevance is a practical design choice that preserves zero-shot generalization without requiring replay. Open questions include whether the fixed expert count (20) and top-K routing (K=2) scale gracefully to longer task sequences or more diverse modalities, and whether the approach transfers to VLMs without Q-Former-style cross-attention encoders.
