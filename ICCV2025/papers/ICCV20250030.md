## Problem

Few-shot keypoint detection has been studied primarily within a single image domain, leaving the cross-modal scenario—where the support modality (sketches) differs fundamentally from the query modality (photos)—unexplored. Existing methods cannot address the sketch-photo domain gap, the lack of content-level pairing between support and query, or the user-specific style variability inherent in sketches, making source-free few-shot keypoint localization on unseen classes an open problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250030/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes K annotated sketches (or synthetic edgemaps from PiDiNet, HED, and Canny) as the support set and M query photos, all resized to 384×384 RGB images, and produces 2D keypoint coordinates (normalized to [-1,1]) with visibility for N keypoints per query. During training, three edgemap versions per photo are generated to simulate style diversity, while at inference the model accepts real free-hand sketches as support. The setup is formulated as N-way K-shot learning (K=1, M=5 in experiments), with 11 base keypoints for training and 6–7 novel keypoints for evaluation.

## Method

The architecture uses a ResNet-50 encoder to produce 2048-channel feature maps, from which Gaussian pooling extracts per-keypoint embeddings. A de-stylization network Z (multi-scale channel attention) disentangles style from these embeddings by enforcing consistency across three edgemap versions of the same photo, and support prototypes are computed as means of the de-stylized embeddings. Prototypes are correlated with query features via element-wise multiplication, refined by a descriptor network, and localized by a multi-scale Grid-Based Locator (grid classifier with cross-entropy + grid regressor with L1 loss at scales 8, 12, 16). A supervised prototypical domain adaptation loss pulls support keypoint prototypes toward query keypoint embeddings at the keypoint level (not feature level), and auxiliary keypoints interpolated between visible pairs augment all three loss objectives (localization, domain adaptation, de-stylization) to improve robustness.

## Results

On the Animal Pose dataset (K=1 shot), the proposed method achieves 39.00% PCK@0.1 for novel keypoints on unseen classes, outperforming FSKD (33.92%) by ~5 PCK and the vanilla baseline (17.39%) by ~22 PCK. On the larger Animal Kingdom dataset (850 species), the method reaches 14.42% PCK@0.1 for novel keypoints on unseen classes versus 10.06% for FSKD. For seen classes with base keypoints, the proposed method scores 55.10% (Animal Pose) and 29.68% (Animal Kingdom), consistently leading all baselines across all four evaluation settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against FSKD [67] (Lu et al., CVPR 2022), the state-of-the-art few-shot keypoint detector repurposed for the cross-modal sketch-to-photo setting, and a vanilla baseline (B-Vanilla) that removes domain adaptation and de-stylization. The proposed method outperforms FSKD by approximately 5 PCK on Animal Pose and 4 PCK on Animal Kingdom for the hardest setting (novel keypoints, unseen classes), and by 4–8 PCK across all other settings. FSKD itself substantially outperforms B-Vanilla (e.g., 33.92% vs. 17.39% on Animal Pose unseen-novel), confirming the value of uncertainty-aware localization. Notably, when the proposed method is jointly trained with both sketches and photos, it reaches 46.54% PCK@0.1 on Animal Pose unseen-novel, surpassing FSKD's photo-only result of 44.75% by 1.79 PCK, demonstrating that cross-modal training yields a more generalized keypoint representation than either modality alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the few-shot keypoint detection framework of Lu et al. (FSKD) by replacing its photo-to-photo same-domain setup with a cross-modal sketch-to-photo paradigm, where support examples are annotated sketches (or synthetic edgemaps) rather than photos. It repurposes prototypical networks for keypoint-level prototypes and adapts prototypical domain adaptation from an unsupervised classification setting to a supervised keypoint-level transport loss. A novel de-stylization network with multi-scale channel attention is introduced to handle user-specific sketch style variance, and the GBL localization module is simplified from FSKD's uncertainty-based regression to cross-entropy plus L1 offset prediction, justified by the sparsity of sketch data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is among the first works to formalize cross-modal few-shot keypoint detection, offering a source-free pipeline where only a few annotated sketches are needed to localize novel keypoints on unseen photo classes—practically relevant when photos are unavailable due to rarity, hazard, or privacy constraints. The finding that a model trained entirely on synthetic edgemaps (PiDiNet, HED, Canny) generalizes to real free-hand sketches with only ~0.5–1.8 PCK degradation is encouraging but the evaluation is limited to 30 sketches on animal datasets. Open questions include whether the approach scales to human pose or medical landmark detection, whether real sketch annotations (rather than edgemaps) would further close the gap, and how the method performs with more than one shot or in truly zero-shot class scenarios.
