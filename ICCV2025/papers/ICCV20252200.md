## Problem

CLIP achieves strong image-level vision-language alignment but fails to perceive fine-grained actions (states and relationships between objects), leading to incorrect image-text matching when the distinguishing factor is an action. Existing prompt-learning approaches for CLIP target object-level or attribute-level understanding but leave action perception unaddressed, and no prior method systematically injects action-specific external knowledge into the visual encoding process.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and its paired text caption as input. The caption is first processed offline by GPT-3.5 (via in-context learning) to produce structured action triplets (subject, action, object) and free-form action state descriptions, which serve as prompts during training. At inference, the pipeline operates in two stages: CLIP pre-selects the top-20 candidates, and the proposed model re-ranks them by computing cosine similarity between action-enhanced image embeddings and LLM-enriched text embeddings, yielding R@1/R@5/R@10 retrieval scores.

## Method

The core contribution is an LLM-enhanced action-aware multi-modal prompt-tuning framework with two complementary prompt types: (1) action triplet prompts, where CLIP word embeddings of (subject, action, object) tuples are processed through multi-transformer layers to capture compositional semantics, and (2) action state prompts, where GPT-3.5-generated causal state descriptions are encoded by a frozen text encoder. Both prompt types pass through a bottleneck MLP adapter (d→t→d) to produce visual-space prompts. An Action-aware Adaptive Interaction Module (AIM) then performs cross-attention between these prompts and image patch embeddings, followed by self-attention and a λ-weighted re-weighting (λ=0.7) to suppress noisy action cues. Training is two-stage: Stage 1 optimizes prompts, the triplet encoder, and AIM under a bidirectional contrastive loss with CLIP frozen; Stage 2 fine-tunes only AIM under a triplet loss to sharpen intra/inter-class separation.

## Results

On the COCO 5K test set with ViT-B/16, the proposed method achieves Rsum of 421.1 (I2T R@1: 58.4, T2I R@1: 43.2), outperforming the strongest baseline EVA-02-CLIP (413.1) by 8.0 points. On Flickr30K with the same backbone, it reaches Rsum 548.9 (I2T R@1: 88.1, T2I R@1: 74.7), surpassing EVA-02-CLIP (538.2) by 10.7 points. With the larger ViT-L/14-336 backbone, the method attains the best Flickr30K Rsum of 556.7, edging out MetaCLIP (555.3), though MetaCLIP leads on COCO Rsum (443.4 vs. 438.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ViT-B/16 (the most thoroughly compared backbone), the proposed method outperforms all baselines on both datasets: COCO Rsum 421.1 vs. EVA-02-CLIP 413.1, MGCA 406.7, FineCLIP 401.7, and CLIP 374.9; Flickr30K Rsum 548.9 vs. EVA-02-CLIP 538.2, FineCLIP 528.6, MGCA 525.8, and CLIP 515.8. On ViT-B/32, it similarly leads all baselines, achieving COCO Rsum 404.7 (vs. MGCA 395.3) and Flickr30K Rsum 535.6 (vs. MGCA 515.1). With the larger ViT-L/14-336 backbone, the method achieves the best Flickr30K Rsum (556.7) over MetaCLIP (555.3) and FILIP (553.5), but trails MetaCLIP on COCO Rsum (438.5 vs. 443.4), where MetaCLIP's diffusion-based feedback provides a slight edge in image-to-text retrieval (I2T R@1: 65.5 vs. 62.5). Notably, the gap between the proposed method and CLIP widens with smaller backbones (e.g., +46.2 Rsum on ViT-B/32 COCO), suggesting the action-aware prompts are most beneficial when the base model has limited capacity for fine-grained perception.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP prompt-learning line (CoOp, CoCoOp, MAPLE) by shifting the focus from object-level or attribute-level alignment to action-level understanding, a gap the authors identify through failure-case analysis on COCO and Flickr30K. It departs from prior LLM-enhanced prompting work (KgCoOp, Kan et al., Wang et al.) that injects general entity or attribute knowledge, instead targeting action-specific compositional (triplet) and causal (state) knowledge. It also differentiates from the closest action-aware retrieval method AME, which relies on fixed action-similar texts from a memory bank, by using LLM in-context learning to generate richer, per-sample action descriptions that are then fed as visual prompts into CLIP's image encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated weakness of CLIP—its inability to discriminate actions and inter-object relationships—which is a recurring bottleneck in fine-grained retrieval and downstream VQA. The finding that small open-source LLMs (Llama-2-7B) perform comparably to GPT-3.5 for action-knowledge generation lowers the practical barrier to adoption. However, the method's reliance on LLM-generated text at inference time introduces a dependency on LLM quality (as shown by the 'backhand ball' failure case), and the evaluation is limited to two static-image datasets; whether the approach transfers to video-text matching or action recognition, as the authors suggest, remains an open question.
