## Problem

Existing audio-visual semantic segmentation (AVSS) methods either lose critical cross-modal information through suboptimal fusion or rely on static textual prompts that lack spatial and dynamic context. In particular, no prior work has exploited optical flow as an auxiliary mask prompt to guide segmentation, and stationary sound-emitting objects (e.g., an alarm clock) remain poorly handled because they produce no motion signal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251712/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a T-frame video clip, an audio spectrogram (encoded by VGGish to 256-d features), optical flow computed by Perceiver IO between consecutive frames, and two hierarchical textual prompts generated by the MiniCPM-o-2.6 MLLM (a scene description A₁ and a list of potential sound-producing objects A₂ derived from A₁). The output is a per-pixel segmentation mask with semantic class labels. During training, the ground-truth mask M_GT is fused with the optical-flow mask to form a three-valued pre-mask (1 = confirmed, 0.5 = uncertain, 0 = background) that modulates visual features; at inference, M_GT is removed and the model must rely on optical flow and textual prompts alone.

## Method

SSP introduces a four-component pipeline built on the AAVS baseline (Swin-Base visual encoder, VGGish audio encoder, Mask2Former decoder with FPN). First, a pre-mask technique converts Perceiver IO optical flow into a binary mask M_O, intersects it with M_GT to form a three-valued M_Pre, and multiplies M_Pre onto the raw video before encoding, giving the decoder a motion-informed starting point. Second, two textual prompts from MiniCPM-o-2.6 (scene description and sound-source identification) compensate for stationary emitters that optical flow misses. Third, a Visual-Textual Alignment (VTA) module uses BERT as a shared backbone—encoding visuals with CLIP and text with BLIP tokenizers, then performing two sequential BERT passes with a unified attention mask—to produce aligned cross-modal features that are added to the decoder's final hidden state. Fourth, a post-mask training objective introduces an additional mask prediction loss L'_mask (λ'=10) whose target is the coarser intersection M_O ∩ M_GT, forcing the model to internalize motion-sound correlations so it generalizes when M_GT is unavailable at test time.

## Results

SSP achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three datasets: 85.4 mIoU / 93.3 F-score on S4, 72.3 mIoU / 84.6 F-score on MS3, and 50.1 mIoU / 54.5 F-score on AVSS. Compared to the direct baseline AAVS, SSP improves by 2.2% mIoU and 1.9% F-score on S4, 5.0% mIoU and 7.0% F-score on MS3, and 1.6% mIoU and 1.3% F-score on AVSS. On the most challenging AVSS dataset (semantic segmentation), SSP surpasses the next-best method (AAVS) by 1.6% mIoU, while on MS3 it leads the runner-up (AVS-Mamba) by 3.7% mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSP outperforms all 14 baselines on every dataset where results are available. Against the strongest fusion-based method Save(1024), SSP gains +0.3% mIoU on S4 and a substantial +5.3% mIoU on MS3 (72.3 vs. 67.0). Among prompt-based methods, SSP surpasses AVS-Mamba (the best on MS3) by 3.7% mIoU and 5.8% F-score, and exceeds AAVS (the direct baseline) by 5.0% mIoU on MS3. On the AVSS semantic segmentation benchmark, SSP leads AAVS by 1.6% mIoU and 1.3% F-score, and dominates COMBO by 8.0% mIoU. Notably, SSP is the only method evaluated on all three datasets, and it achieves the best mIoU and F-score simultaneously on each, confirming consistent gains from the optical-flow pre-mask, textual prompts, and VTA alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

SSP extends the AAVS (Stepping Stones) framework [28], which introduced a two-stage progressive training strategy (AVS then semantic segmentation) with GT-mask-assisted pre-masking. The key departure is the introduction of optical flow as a spatial prompt for the pre-mask stage—first in the AVS literature—combined with MLLM-generated textual prompts to handle stationary sound sources that optical flow cannot capture. The VTA module departs from prior prompt-based AVS methods that encode text via CLIP and fuse it with visual features through cross-attention, instead using BERT to jointly process both modalities in a unified latent space, addressing the modality-misalignment problem identified in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-prompt design (dynamic optical flow + static textual context) addresses a genuine gap in prompt-based AVS: motion cues miss stationary emitters while text lacks spatial grounding. The finding that textual prompts without proper cross-modal alignment can degrade performance (Table 2, 'w/o VTA' row) underscores that simply adding MLLM outputs is insufficient and that alignment architecture matters. Limitations include dependence on an external MLLM (MiniCPM-o-2.6) for prompt generation, sensitivity to optical flow quality in high-motion scenes, and evaluation confined to three AVSBench-derived datasets. Open questions include whether learned (rather than MLLM-generated) prompts could reduce inference cost, and whether the approach generalizes to longer videos or more diverse sound categories.
