## Problem

While vision foundation models like DINOv2 provide highly discriminative features for OOD detection, their performance remains unsatisfactory on in-domain data with large semantic spaces (e.g., ImageNet-1K), where fine-tuning actually degrades performance due to increasingly complex decision boundaries. Additionally, prior work has largely focused on CLIP-based models, leaving other foundation models (e.g., DINOv2, MAE) underexplored for this task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250391/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images as input, processed through a ViT backbone (DINOv2 ViT-B-14 or CLIP ViT-B-16) into patch and [CLS] token embeddings. The MoFE module routes each sample to a single expert via the [CLS] token, and the final class embedding from the activated expert is used to compute an OOD score (KNN for DINOv2-based, CLIPN-style scoring for CLIP-based). The output is a binary ID/OOD decision via a threshold on the score, alongside the predicted ID class label.

## Method

The core contribution is the Mixture of Feature Experts (MoFE) module, which replaces the final transformer block of a pre-trained foundation model with K expert transformer blocks, each specializing in a semantic subspace. Subspaces are determined by first partitioning categories via WordNet superclasses, then refining with K-Means clustering on class feature prototypes; a linear router using the [CLS] token assigns each sample to exactly one expert (top-1 routing), supervised by the clustering labels. Training combines a routing cross-entropy loss with an expert loss that treats same-cluster categories as positives and outside-cluster categories as a single negative class, with 1:1 positive-to-negative sample balancing. A complementary Dynamic-β Mixup strategy modulates the Mixup interpolation weight by sampling from a Beta(σ,σ) distribution where σ = 1 − w·s (s being per-category validation accuracy), so well-represented categories receive larger λ (similar samples) while poorly represented ones receive smaller λ (dissimilar samples), plus a regularization term on the final class embedding to prevent feature norm growth during fine-tuning.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K as ID, the proposed method (DINOv2-based) achieves 17.01% average FPR95 and 95.89% AUROC across four OOD test sets (iNaturalist18, Places, SUN, Textures), with 86.40% ID accuracy. On the CLIP-based variant, it reaches 20.02% FPR95 and 94.89% AUROC. On the smaller ImageNet-100 ID dataset, the DINOv2-based method achieves 8.10% FPR95 and 97.75% AUROC, while the CLIP-based variant reaches 7.40% FPR95 and 98.10% AUROC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet-1K (DINOv2-based), the proposed method achieves 17.01% average FPR95, outperforming MOS (26.40%) by 9.39% and the raw DINOv2+KNN baseline (28.34%) by 11.33%, while also improving ID accuracy from 86.01% to 86.40%. In the CLIP-based setting, the method reaches 20.02% FPR95, beating NegPrompt (23.01%) by 2.99% and LSN (30.22%) by 10.20%, though it trails NegPrompt on iNaturalist18 specifically (5.19% vs 6.32% is actually a win; NegPrompt leads on iNat18 at 6.32% vs Ours 5.19%—wait, Ours is better). On ImageNet-100, the DINOv2-based method achieves 8.10% FPR95 vs KNN's 13.42% (5.32% gain), and the CLIP-based variant reaches 7.40% FPR95, surpassing LSN (8.56%) by 1.16%. The paper also reports that DINOv2+KNN without fine-tuning already outperforms NegPrompt on challenging test sets (ImageNet-O, NINCO) by 49.94%, establishing that the foundation model's raw feature space is a strong starting point. Overall, the method wins on all four standard OOD test sets in both paradigms, with the largest gains on Places and SUN where complex decision boundaries are most challenging.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of foundation-model-based OOD detection and Mixture-of-Experts architectures. It builds directly on DINOv2's self-supervised feature space as the backbone, adapts the sparsely-gated MoE routing mechanism (Shazeer et al., 2017) into a feature-space partitioning module (MoFE), and modifies the standard Mixup augmentation with a category-dependent Beta distribution. Conceptually, it departs from MOS (Huang & Li, 2021), which decouples complex distributions at the loss level, by instead assigning dedicated expert transformer blocks to each semantic subspace at the model level. The empirical study also extends prior foundation-model OOD work (which focused almost exclusively on CLIP) to systematically compare DINOv2, MAE, and multiple CLIP variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway that DINOv2+KNN without any fine-tuning already rivals complex fine-tuned methods (e.g., NegPrompt) gives practitioners a simple, strong baseline and shifts the research question from 'how to build a good OOD score' to 'how to refine already-discriminative features.' The counterintuitive finding that naive fine-tuning of DINOv2 on ImageNet-1K degrades OOD performance highlights an open tension between in-domain classification accuracy and OOD generalization in foundation models. Open questions include whether the WordNet+K-Means partitioning generalizes beyond ImageNet's taxonomy, whether MoFE benefits persist with even larger semantic spaces (e.g., 10K+ classes), and whether the approach transfers to other foundation models or modalities.
