## Problem

Existing unsupervised instance segmentation methods such as CutLER separate instances using only 2D semantic similarity, which fails when same-class instances overlap or are connected in image space. The paper argues that 3D geometric information about scene structure is necessary to cut along true instance boundaries, and that the quality of resulting pseudo-masks can be assessed spatially to produce a cleaner learning signal for the detector.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251586/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 480×480) and a zero-shot monocular depth map (from ZoeDepth) as input, from which it constructs an orthographic point cloud. It produces per-instance segmentation masks and bounding boxes via a Cascade Mask R-CNN. The depth map is the only additional input beyond the RGB image; no human annotations are used at any stage, preserving the unsupervised setting.

## Method

CutS3D extends CutLER's pseudo-mask-then-CAD pipeline with three 3D-informed components. First, LocalCut replaces 2D connected-component filtering with a MinCut on a k-NN graph built from the depth-unprojected point cloud, where the source and sink nodes are set by the NCut eigenvalues from the semantic affinity graph, enabling cuts along actual 3D object boundaries. Second, Spatial Importance Sharpening computes a depth-gradient map (Gaussian-blur subtraction) and uses it to re-weight the semantic affinity matrix via element-wise exponentiation, making NCut more likely to cut at 3D boundaries. Third, Spatial Confidence is obtained by performing LocalCut at multiple τ_knn scales and averaging the binary masks; this per-pixel confidence map is then used to (a) select only high-confidence masks for copy-paste augmentation, (b) alpha-blend uncertain regions during pasting, and (c) re-weight the BCE mask loss per pixel (Spatial Confidence Soft Target Loss). The full pipeline uses a DiffNCuts ViT-S/8 encoder, a Cascade Mask R-CNN detector, and three rounds of self-training on ImageNet pseudo-masks.

## Results

On zero-shot unsupervised instance segmentation, CutS3D achieves 10.7 AP^mask and 20.8 AP_50^mask on COCO val2017, and 10.9 AP^mask and 21.3 AP_50^mask on COCO20K, outperforming the previous SOTA (CuVLER) by +0.9 AP^mask and +1.5 AP_50^mask. For zero-shot object detection, CutS3D reaches an average of 23.9 AP_50^box and 12.5 AP^box across six benchmarks (COCO, VOC, Objects365, KITTI, LVIS), improving over the best baseline by +2.3 AP_50^box on average. In the in-domain self-training setting on COCO, CutS3D further extends its lead over CuVLER by +1.0 AP^mask on val2017 and +0.7 AP^mask on LVIS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CutS3D outperforms all five baselines across every reported metric. Against the closest competitor CuVLER [1] (which uses an ensemble of 6 DINO ViTs for pseudo-mask extraction), CutS3D gains +0.9 AP^mask on COCO val2017 and +0.9 AP^mask on COCO20K using only a single DiffNCuts feature extractor plus 3D depth. Over CutLER [47], the direct architectural baseline, CutS3D improves by +1.0 AP^mask (10.7 vs. 9.7) on COCO val2017 and +0.9 AP^mask on COCO20K, with even larger margins on AP_50^mask (+1.9 and +1.3 respectively). The earlier methods FreeSOLO [46] (4.3 AP^mask), UnSAM [48] (3.6 AP^mask), and ProMerge+ [26] (8.9 AP^mask) are all substantially outperformed by 2–6 AP^mask points. In the in-domain self-training setting (Table 3), CutS3D also beats CuVLER by +1.0 AP^mask on COCO val2017 and +0.7 AP^mask on LVIS, confirming that the 3D-based pseudo-masks transfer well to domain-specific refinement. Notably, CutS3D achieves these gains with only +4% computational overhead over MaskCut, and its detector inference cost is identical to CutLER's.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CutLER (Wang et al., CVPR 2023), which extracts pseudo-masks via Normalized Cuts on DINO self-supervised features and trains a class-agnostic detector with iterative self-training. The key departure is the introduction of 3D geometric information—obtained from zero-shot monocular depth estimation—into the pseudo-mask extraction and detector training pipeline, addressing the fundamental limitation that 2D semantic similarity alone cannot separate same-class instances that overlap in image space. The method also draws on prior work showing spatial information aids unsupervised semantic segmentation (Sick et al., CVPR 2024) and on classical graph-cut and point-cloud segmentation techniques (MinCut, k-NN graphs), repurposing them for the 2D instance segmentation setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CutS3D establishes that 3D scene geometry, readily available from modern monocular depth estimators without breaking the unsupervised setting, provides a principled mechanism for separating instances that 2D semantics alone cannot distinguish. The approach is notably robust to depth quality (performing comparably across ZoeDepth, Marigold, Kick Back & Relax, and even lower-quality MiDaS Small), suggesting it will benefit from future depth estimator improvements. Open questions remain: the method still struggles when adjacent same-class instances lack discernible 3D boundaries, small-object segmentation remains challenging, and the pipeline is inapplicable to domains where 3D information is unavailable (e.g., medical imaging). The authors also identify in-domain pseudo-mask extraction without relying on ImageNet as a promising future direction.
