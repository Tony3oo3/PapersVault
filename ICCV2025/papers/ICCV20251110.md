## Problem

Existing person Re-ID methods are designed for homogeneous camera settings (ground-to-ground or aerial-to-aerial) and underperform on cross-platform Aerial-Ground Re-Identification (AG-ReID) due to extreme viewpoint discrepancies between top-down drone views and frontal/rear CCTV views. Prior AG-ReID approaches rely on auxiliary information such as view labels or attribute annotations to decouple view-specific features, which is costly and impractical for real-world deployment. This paper tackles the gap of learning view-invariant representations without any auxiliary supervision.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB person images from both aerial (drone, 15–75 m altitude) and ground (CCTV, ≥5 m height) cameras, resized to 256×128 pixels, and processes them through a ViT-base backbone with 16×16 patches to produce D-dimensional feature embeddings. Identity matching is performed via cosine similarity in the embedding space, evaluated with CMC Rank-1, mAP, and mINP under multiple protocols (ALL, G↔G, A↔A, A↔G, A→G, G→A). No auxiliary inputs such as view labels, pose, or attribute annotations are required at inference time.

## Method

VIF-AGReID introduces two synergistic components on top of an ImageNet-pretrained ViT-base backbone. First, Patch-Level RotateMix (PLRM) augmentation divides each training image into n×n non-overlapping patches and applies a Multi-Patch-Level Probability mechanism that assigns higher transformation probability to central (identity-relevant) patches; selected patches are randomly rotated by angles sampled from U(−45°, 45°) and blended with their originals via a mixing factor α=0.5, introducing fine-grained rotational diversity while preserving structural information. Second, the View-Invariant Angular Loss (VIAL) computes cosine similarity between feature embeddings and applies an exponential penalty term (1 + λe^(−β·cos θ)) to positive pairs, which dynamically scales the loss to exponentially penalize large angular deviations caused by viewpoint shifts; a Hard Negative Mining term additionally penalizes cross-identity pairs whose cosine similarity exceeds a margin M. The total objective combines Identity (cross-entropy) loss, soft Triplet loss, and VIAL-HNM with learned balancing weights, trained for 120 epochs with SGD and cosine LR decay.

## Results

On the CARGO dataset under the cross-platform A↔G protocol, VIF-AGReID achieves 51.25% Rank-1, 44.55% mAP, and 31.20% mINP, outperforming the previous SOTA VDT by 3.13%/1.79%/1.25% respectively. Under the ALL protocol on CARGO, VIF reaches 65.71% Rank-1 and 57.46% mAP. On AG-ReIDv1, VIF attains 87.32% Rank-1 and 79.19% mAP under the G→A protocol, surpassing VDT by 0.73%/0.62%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIF-AGReID outperforms all 11 baselines across both CARGO and AG-ReIDv1 under every evaluation protocol. Against the strongest AG-ReID-specific baseline VDT [42], VIF gains +3.13% Rank-1 and +1.79% mAP on CARGO A↔G, and +0.73% Rank-1 on AG-ReIDv1 G→A, while requiring no auxiliary view labels or attribute annotations that VDT depends on. Compared to the general Re-ID ViT baseline, VIF improves A↔G Rank-1 by 8.12% (51.25 vs. 43.13) on CARGO, demonstrating that standard transformers fail to handle cross-platform viewpoint shifts. Against the attribute-guided Explain method [28] on AG-ReIDv1 G→A, VIF achieves +4.47% Rank-1 and +5.80% mAP, showing that explicit attribute supervision is not necessary for robust cross-view matching. General Re-ID methods (SBS, PCB, BoT, MGN) show the largest degradation on A↔G (Rank-1 drops to 31–36%), confirming that homogeneous-camera training is insufficient for aerial-ground matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends patch-based augmentation strategies (Hong et al. [12], Kang et al. [16]) from generic object detection/classification settings to the specific challenge of aerial-ground person Re-ID, introducing rotational diversity at the patch level rather than pixel shuffling or random erasing. It departs conceptually from the two dominant AG-ReID approaches: VDT [42], which requires auxiliary view labels to decouple view-dependent features, and Explain [28], which relies on attribute annotations to guide cross-view matching. By combining a targeted augmentation (PLRM) with an angular-constrained loss (VIAL) on a standard ViT-Base backbone, the paper positions itself as a simpler, annotation-free alternative that achieves view invariance through training dynamics rather than architectural disentanglement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that cross-platform person Re-ID can be improved without the costly auxiliary annotations (view labels, attribute labels) that prior AG-ReID methods require, making the approach more deployable in real-world surveillance networks where such metadata is unavailable. The paper acknowledges that altitude differences between aerial and ground cameras remain an unaddressed scalability challenge, and the evaluation is limited to three datasets with a single ViT-Base backbone, leaving open whether the view-invariant learning strategy transfers to other cross-platform or cross-modality Re-ID settings. The relatively narrow scope (aerial-ground only, RGB only) and the modest absolute performance gap over VDT suggest the approach is a solid incremental step rather than a paradigm shift in cross-view Re-ID.
