## Problem

Existing training-free open-vocabulary semantic segmentation (TF-OVSS) methods leverage Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA) to generate self-correlation maps for improving CLIP's local spatial representation, but they treat all attention heads uniformly, ignoring the diversity and redundancy among them. The paper reveals that individual heads contribute unequally to segmentation quality—some heads actively degrade performance—yet no prior work has exploited this head-level heterogeneity to improve TF-OVSS.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251890/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 336×336 and scanned with a 224×224 sliding window at 112-pixel stride; Cityscapes images at 560×560) and a set of text prompts built from standard ImageNet templates with class names. It produces a pixel-level segmentation mask by computing cosine similarity between refined visual patch features and CLIP text class prototypes. No training, fine-tuning, or post-processing (e.g., PAMR, DenseCRF) is applied; all modifications operate at inference time on a frozen ViT-B/16 CLIP model.

## Method

DIH-CLIP introduces Selective Head Attention (SHA) to replace the standard MHSA in CLIP's ViT encoder. SHA has two components: (1) a Selection step that computes the information entropy of each head's key-key self-correlation map and retains only the top-K heads with the lowest entropy (i.e., most spatially focused), discarding redundant or diffuse heads; (2) a Transfer step that applies each selected head's attention weights to the full-dimensional global value vector (rather than the per-head sub-value), iteratively aggregating local spatial cues into the global visual feature. SHA is embedded not only in the final layer but also in middle layers of the ViT, where it generates additional detail-rich features that are summed with the final-layer output before matching against text prototypes. The entire pipeline is training-free and plug-and-play, demonstrated to improve SCLIP, ProxyCLIP, and NACLIP when SHA is simply swapped in.

## Results

DIH-CLIP achieves a mean mIoU of 43.6% across eight benchmark datasets, setting a new state-of-the-art among all training-free open-vocabulary semantic segmentation methods. On individual datasets, it reaches 64.2% on VOC21, 36.0% on Context60, 84.9% on VOC20, 26.7% on COCO Stuff, 40.2% on Cityscapes, and 19.6% on ADE20K. In terms of pixel accuracy (pAcc), DIH-CLIP scores 65.2% average, surpassing the previous best (ProxyCLIP at 63.7%) by 1.5 points. All results are obtained with ViT-B/16, no training, and no post-processing (e.g., no PAMR or DenseCRF).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIH-CLIP outperforms the strongest training-free method without an external VFM (SCLIP, 38.2% avg) by 5.4 points in average mIoU, with gains of 5.1 on VOC21, 5.6 on Context60, 4.3 on Stuff, and 8.0 on Cityscapes. Against the best VFM-augmented training-free method (ProxyCLIP with SAM, 40.8% avg), DIH-CLIP still leads by 2.8 points on average, winning on 7 of 8 datasets (e.g., 64.2 vs 59.3 on VOC21, 40.2 vs 37.0 on City). It also surpasses the strongest weakly supervised baseline (CLIP-DINOiser with DINO, 40.3% avg) by 3.3 points, despite requiring no training data or auxiliary model. The only dataset where DIH-CLIP trails another method is VOC21, where OVDiff (using Stable Diffusion) scores 66.3 vs DIH-CLIP's 64.2; however, DIH-CLIP dominates OVDiff on all other seven datasets. The plug-and-play ablation (Table 4) further shows that adding SHA to SCLIP, ProxyCLIP, or NACLIP consistently improves their scores by 2–5 points, confirming SHA's generality across different TF-OVSS frameworks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free OVSS lineage initiated by MaskCLIP (which removed FFN and residual connections from CLIP's ViT) and advanced by SCLIP (which replaced query-key attention with key-key self-correlation). While prior TF-OVSS methods (SCLIP, ClearCLIP, NACLIP, ProxyCLIP) all apply the full multi-head self-attention mechanism uniformly to generate self-correlation maps, DIH-CLIP departs by treating individual attention heads as heterogeneous: it uses information entropy to identify and discard redundant heads, then transfers the local spatial cues of selected heads into the global feature embedding. The middle-layer feature fusion component draws on standard dense-prediction practice (FPN, DenseNet) but applies it within the training-free CLIP pipeline for the first time in this context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play property of SHA—demonstrated by consistent gains when bolted onto SCLIP, ProxyCLIP, and NACLIP—makes it a broadly applicable component for the TF-OVSS community rather than a monolithic pipeline. The empirical finding that certain attention heads actively degrade segmentation (and that a single well-chosen head can outperform the full MHSA) challenges the implicit assumption in prior work that all heads contribute positively to self-correlation. Open questions include whether the entropy-based selection criterion generalizes to larger ViT variants (ViT-L, ViT-H) or other VLMs beyond CLIP, and whether the fixed 5–9 head range transfers across architectures or requires per-model tuning.
