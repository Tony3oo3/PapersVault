## Problem

Existing text-guided image-to-video generation models struggle to achieve fine-grained, simultaneous control over both object movement and camera motion using only natural language. Text-encoder-based approaches lack precision for individual elements, while specialized control modules (e.g., point trajectories, camera extrinsics) require expert knowledge and typically handle only one motion type at a time, creating a usability barrier for non-professional users.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (fed to the agent at 2560×1600) and a free-form text prompt describing object movement and/or camera motion. The agent internally decomposes the prompt, identifies objects via Grounded-SAM, and plots trajectories on a 20×10 grid. The output is a 512×512 video of 24 frames (sampled with stride 4) generated by a frozen SVD backbone conditioned on computed optical flow. An optional rethinking step allows the agent to inspect the generated video and iteratively refine trajectories and camera parameters.

## Method

A GPT-4o-based motion field agent parses the text prompt into two explicit representations: object trajectories (via Grounded-SAM-assisted identification and grid-based plotting) and camera extrinsics (translation constrained to (−1,1)). A novel analytical optical flow composition module then lifts the input image to 3D using Metric3D depth, computes 3D displacement offsets from object flow (via CMP), applies camera reprojection, and projects back to produce a unified 2D optical flow map encoding both motion types simultaneously. The MOFA-Video optical flow adapter is fine-tuned on synthetic unified flow maps (constructed from Unimatch flow + DROID-SLAM camera poses) to close the domain gap between geometrically composed and real optical flow. The frozen SVD diffusion model generates the final video conditioned on this unified flow, and an optional rethinking loop lets the agent correct its prior actions by analyzing the generated output.

## Results

On the controllable I2V generation benchmark (Table 2), MotionAgent achieves 45.69% on Object Movement Q&A and 77.76% on Complex Camera Motion, yielding a total score of 65.10%, substantially outperforming the best competitor CogVideoX (22.93% total). With the optional rethinking step, scores improve to 49.58% and 89.04% respectively (73.45% total). On the general VBench evaluation (Table 1), MotionAgent scores 81.91% on Video-Text Camera Motion—more than double the next-best DynamiCrafter (35.81%)—while maintaining a competitive 97.51% overall I2V Score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MotionAgent decisively outperforms all ten baselines on the controllable I2V generation benchmark. The closest competitor, CogVideoX (DIT-based), scores 22.93% total versus MotionAgent's 65.10%, a gap of over 42 points, with the largest margin on Complex Camera Motion (20.62% vs. 77.76%). Among U-Net-based models, DynamiCrafter (16.58%) and Motion-I2V (15.65%) are the strongest, but both trail MotionAgent by roughly 48–49 points in total score. On the general VBench evaluation, MotionAgent's 81.91% Video-Text Camera Motion score dwarfs DynamiCrafter's 35.81% and I2VGen-XL's 13.00%, while its overall I2V Score of 97.51% is competitive with AnimateAnything (98.31%) and DynamiCrafter (97.98%), confirming that the motion control mechanism does not degrade general video quality. The base model SVD, which does not support text input, is not directly comparable on camera motion but serves as the frozen backbone that MotionAgent enhances. The rethinking step further widens the gap, pushing Complex Camera Motion to 89.04% and total score to 73.45%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) as a frozen I2V backbone and MOFA-Video's optical flow adapter as the motion control module, but departs from both by introducing an LLM-based motion field agent (GPT-4o) that automatically parses text prompts into explicit object trajectories and camera extrinsics. It positions itself between two existing paradigms: text-encoder-based I2V models (VideoCrafter, DynamiCrafter) that achieve only coarse control, and specialized control modules (DragNUWA, CameraCtrl, MotionCtrl) that require expert-specified trajectories or camera parameters. The analytical optical flow composition module, which geometrically unifies object and camera motion in 3D before projecting to a single flow map, is the key technical departure from prior methods that either handle one motion type at a time or simply add flow fields.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that natural-language motion descriptions can be reliably converted into precise geometric motion representations without requiring users to specify trajectories or camera parameters, lowering the barrier for controllable video generation. However, the evaluation methodology raises concerns: GPT-4o serves as both the motion-parsing agent and the Q&A judge for object movement alignment, creating a circularity that may inflate scores. The benchmark is relatively small (432 object-movement and 662 camera-motion prompts), and the method's dependence on a proprietary LLM limits reproducibility and scalability. Open questions include whether the agent approach generalizes to longer videos, more complex multi-object interactions, and whether the analytical composition strategy extends to other base architectures beyond SVD and Motion-I2V.
