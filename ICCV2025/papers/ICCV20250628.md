## Problem

No existing benchmark can uniformly evaluate world generation across the diverse methodological landscape of video generation, 3D scene generation, and 4D generation models. Current video benchmarks (e.g., VBench) assess only single-scene quality without spatial layout control, while 3D/4D scene generation methods require camera trajectories and reference images that video benchmarks do not provide, making cross-paradigm comparison impossible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250628/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance is a triplet (current scene, next-scene prompt, layout), where the current scene is an RGB image paired with a text prompt, and the layout consists of a sequence of camera matrices plus a natural-language camera movement description (e.g., 'move right'). All 20 evaluated models—regardless of whether they are T2V, I2V, 3D, or 4D—must produce a rendered or generated video as output, enabling direct comparison. The 3,000-example dataset includes 2,000 static world tasks (10 scene categories, varying sequence lengths of 1 or 3 new scenes) and 1,000 dynamic world tasks (5 motion types with fixed camera), each with a photorealistic and a stylized counterpart.

## Method

WorldScore decomposes world generation into a sequence of next-scene generation tasks, each governed by an explicit camera trajectory, enabling a single evaluation protocol across 3D, 4D, I2V, and T2V models. The metric aggregates 10 sub-metrics into three aspects: controllability (camera error via scale-invariant rotation/translation against ground-truth trajectory, object detection success rate via Grounding DINO, and CLIPScore content alignment), quality (3D geometric consistency via DROID-SLAM reprojection error, photometric consistency via optical-flow AEPE, style consistency via Gram-matrix F-norm, and subjective quality via an ensemble of CLIP IQA and CLIP Aesthetic scores validated by a 400-participant human study), and dynamics (motion accuracy via region-specific optical flow, motion magnitude, and motion smoothness via frame-interpolation comparison). The dataset is curated by sourcing images from open-source scene datasets and Unsplash, generating captions with GPT-4o, producing next-scene prompts autoregressively via an LLM, and assigning one of 8 movie-industry camera movements per task.

## Results

On the WorldScore benchmark (3,000 test examples), 3D scene generation models dominate static world generation: WonderWorld achieves the highest WorldScore-Static (72.69) followed by LucidDreamer (70.40), both substantially outperforming the best video model CogVideoX-I2V (62.15). For dynamic world generation, CogVideoX-I2V leads with WorldScore-Dynamic 59.12, surpassing closed-source Gen-3 (57.58) and Hailuo (56.36). 3D models achieve near-perfect camera controllability (84–94) but score 0 on all dynamics metrics, while video models struggle with camera control (best: CogVideoX-T2V at 40.22). The 4D model 4D-fy performs poorly overall (WorldScore-Static 27.98, WorldScore-Dynamic 32.10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

3D scene generation models (WonderWorld 72.69, LucidDreamer 70.40) substantially outperform all video models on WorldScore-Static, driven by near-perfect camera controllability (84–94 vs. 21–40 for video models) and superior 3D/photometric consistency. However, all 3D models score 0 on dynamics metrics, making them unable to generate dynamic worlds. Among video models, CogVideoX-I2V (62.15 static, 59.12 dynamic) outperforms both closed-source leaders Gen-3 (60.71/57.58) and Hailuo (57.55/56.36) on overall WorldScore, though it trails them in object controllability (40.07 vs. 62.92/69.56) and content alignment (36.73 vs. 50.49/73.53). T2V models generally achieve higher camera controllability and motion magnitude than I2V models, while I2V models score higher on quality metrics, revealing a controllability–quality trade-off. The single 4D model evaluated (4D-fy) performs poorly across all metrics (27.98 static, 32.10 dynamic), with particularly low photometric consistency (1.59) and subjective quality (0.89), highlighting the current difficulty of 4D scene generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by reframing the evaluation of world generation as a unified multi-scene, camera-trajectory-controlled task, departing from existing video generation benchmarks (VBench, EvalCrafter, FETV) that assess only single-scene quality without spatial layout control. It repurposes established evaluation tools—DROID-SLAM for 3D consistency, Grounding DINO for object controllability, CLIPScore for content alignment, and optical flow for photometric/motion metrics—into a coherent framework that can simultaneously score 3D scene generation, 4D generation, and video generation models. The key conceptual departure is the decomposition of world generation into sequential next-scene tasks with explicit (current scene, next scene, layout) triplets, a formulation absent from all prior benchmarks listed in Table 1.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As world generation moves from individual scene synthesis toward large-scale, multi-scene environments for embodied AI, simulation, and entertainment, this benchmark provides the first common yardstick across 3D, 4D, I2V, and T2V paradigms. Key open questions it surfaces include: how to bridge the 3D-to-4D gap (4D-fy scores 27.98 vs. 72.69 for WonderWorld in static), how to give video models reliable camera controllability (best video model scores 40.22 vs. 94.01 for Text2Room), and how to handle long-sequence and outdoor scene generation where video models degrade significantly. The benchmark's 3,000-example dataset and public leaderboard position it as a likely reference standard for the field.
