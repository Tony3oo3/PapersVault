## Problem

Video foundation models (ViFMs) pretrained on large-scale general video data have shown promise, but their ability to transfer across diverse scientific disciplines—medical imaging, animal behavior, weather forecasting—remains untested. Existing ViFMs are typically evaluated only within a single domain, leaving it unclear whether a single pretrained backbone can be competitive with domain-specific state-of-the-art methods across multiple scientific applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251046/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each SCIVID task consumes 3-channel spatio-temporal clips of varying resolution and length: 16-frame grayscale clips (144×144 or 285×512) for animal behavior, RGB video (1024×1280) with query points for tissue tracking, 16 weather variable frames at 181×360 for medium-range forecasting, and 12 infrared satellite frames (128×128) for cyclone pressure prediction. Outputs range from discrete class labels and 2D point coordinates to dense multi-channel future frame predictions and scalar pressure sequences, all produced by lightweight readout modules operating on frozen backbone features.

## Method

The evaluation framework appends task-specific trainable readout modules to frozen pretrained ViFM backbones (4DS, VideoMAE, V-JEPA, VideoPrism, DINOv2), training only the readouts with strong supervision on each downstream dataset. Readout architectures are task-matched: cross-attention modules for classification, tracking, and pressure regression; a DPT-style convolutional upsampler for dense weather frame prediction. The key finding is that this simple frozen-backbone-plus-readout protocol achieves state-of-the-art results on three of five tasks (CalMS21, FlyVsFly, Digital Typhoon) without any domain-specific architectural components, while also revealing that no single backbone dominates across all tasks and that weather forecasting remains a significant gap. The paper further ablates backbone scaling, readout architecture choices, and the effect of temporal ordering to identify factors governing cross-domain transfer performance.

## Results

Across five scientific video tasks, the best frozen ViFM backbones achieve state-of-the-art results on three of five benchmarks: V-JEPA-H reaches 92.4 mAP on CalMS21 (beating the previous SOTA of 91.5), 4DS-L achieves 3.84/5.23 RMSE on Digital Typhoon (vs. 11.71 for the prior method), and VideoPrism-g with extended training reaches 92.5 mAP on FlyVsFly. On STIR tracking, 4DS-e with finetuning attains 61.2% val / 69.2% test δ_avg accuracy, approaching but not matching the SOTA MFT tracker (68.5%/77.6%). On WeatherBench 2, even the strongest backbones (4DS-e, VideoMAEv2-g) show only modest wRMSE improvements over persistence, trailing significantly behind GraphCast and GenCast.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CalMS21, the paper's framework with frozen V-JEPA-H (92.4 mAP) and VideoPrism-g (92.0 mAP) both outperform the previous VideoPrism-g SOTA of 91.5 mAP [70]. On FlyVsFly, VideoPrism-g with 400k training steps reaches 92.5 mAP, marginally beating the prior 92.0 mAP [70], while V-JEPA-H achieves the best single-backbone score of 90.1 mAP. On STIR tracking, MFT [41] remains the clear SOTA at 68.5%/77.6% (val/test), ahead of the best ViFM result (4DS-e finetuned at 61.2%/69.2%); however, 4DS-e frozen (51.3%/57.8%) already surpasses CSRT [35] (52.6%/58.6% val) and RAFT [59] (47.0%/42.4%). On Digital Typhoon, 4DS-L (5.23 test RMSE) and V-JEPA-H (5.53) dramatically outperform Kitamoto et al. [29] (11.71 test RMSE), more than doubling accuracy. On WeatherBench 2, all ViFMs trail significantly behind GraphCast [32] and GenCast [45], with 4DS-e and VideoMAEv2-g showing only modest gains over the persistence baseline, indicating that current video backbones are not yet competitive for high-accuracy numerical weather prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the frozen-backbone-plus-trainable-readout evaluation paradigm established by 4DS [11] to a cross-domain scientific setting, applying it uniformly to six ViFMs (VideoMAE, V-JEPA, 4DS, VideoPrism, DINOv2) across five tasks. Prior ViFM evaluations have been confined to single domains—animal behavior (VideoPrism [70], Sun et al. [57]), medical imaging (Endo-FM [64], EchoCLIP [15]), or weather (ClimaX [43], Aurora [9])—and the closest concurrent effort, VideoEval [33], covers a narrower task scope. SCIVID departs from this by constructing a unified benchmark that deliberately spans distribution shifts (grayscale, RGB, geophysical fields) and task types (classification, tracking, dense forecasting, scalar regression) to test whether general-purpose spatiotemporal representations transfer across scientific disciplines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SCIVID provides the first systematic, open-source testbed for evaluating ViFMs on scientific video tasks, lowering the barrier for the CV community to engage with high-impact applications without deep domain expertise. The finding that simple cross-attention readouts on frozen ViFMs reach SOTA in animal behavior and pressure forecasting validates a lightweight adaptation recipe, while the persistent gap on WeatherBench 2 (even after finetuning) and the absence of a single dominant backbone highlight that truly versatile scientific backbones remain an open problem. Key limitations include the focus on short-clip settings, limited data-efficiency analysis, and incomplete domain coverage (no microscopy, underwater, or satellite time-series tasks), all of which point to concrete directions for future benchmark expansion and adaptation research.
