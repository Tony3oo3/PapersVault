## Problem

Existing 3D shape VAEs encode all shapes into fixed-size latent representations, allocating identical token budgets regardless of geometric complexity. This wastes capacity on simple objects (e.g., a cube) while under-allocating to intricate regions, leading to inefficient compression and degraded downstream generation quality. The paper asks whether latent representation size can be adaptively matched to local shape complexity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251552/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The VAE encoder consumes 1M surface points with normals sampled from a mesh normalized to a unit cube, producing a variable-length sequence of octree tokens (average 439 vs. 512 for fixed-size baselines), where each token is a pair of a quantized latent index and an 8-bit structural code indicating which of eight child octants exist. The autoregressive generator takes a 77-token CLIP text embedding as conditioning and outputs the same variable-length token sequence, which is decoded through a Perceiver-based decoder into an occupancy field and finally a mesh via marching cubes.

## Method

OAT constructs a sparse octree by subdividing cells only when a quadric-error-based complexity metric exceeds a threshold, rather than subdividing all occupied cells to maximum depth as in prior octree methods. A Perceiver-based VAE encodes the shape into per-leaf-cell latents using global cross-attention, and a multi-scale residual quantization scheme (Algorithm 1) quantizes each node's latent as the residual relative to its accumulated parent, enabling coarse-to-fine token ordering. OctreeGPT is a decoder-only transformer that autoregressively generates the BFS-ordered token sequence with dual prediction heads (one for latent indices, one for structural codes) and a tree-structured positional encoding that captures both 3D spatial coordinates and octree depth. The model dynamically determines sequence length during generation by processing structural codes on the fly, analogous to end-of-sentence tokens in text generation.

## Results

On the text-to-3D generation task (Table 3), OctreeGPT achieves FID 56.88, KID 5.79×10⁻³, CLIP-score 0.34, and 11.3 s runtime, outperforming all baselines on every metric. For shape reconstruction with discrete latents (Table 1), OAT at 439 tokens achieves IoU 88.6 and CD 1.78×10⁻³, surpassing Craftsman-VQ at 512 tokens (IoU 83.8, CD 1.94×10⁻³) with ~15% fewer tokens. With continuous latents (Table 2), OAT-KL at 439 tokens reaches IoU 91.6 and CD 1.29×10⁻³, beating Craftsman at 512 tokens (IoU 91.0, CD 1.83×10⁻³) and OctFusion at 4096 tokens (IoU 88.9, CD 1.87×10⁻³) by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OctreeGPT decisively outperforms Craftsman [29] (the strongest 3D-native baseline) on all four generation metrics: FID 56.88 vs. 65.18, KID 5.79 vs. 6.42, CLIP-score 0.34 vs. 0.27, and runtime 11.3 s vs. 54.8 s (~5× faster). Against XCube [55], the gap is even larger (FID 56.88 vs. 132.56), though XCube uses a pre-trained model on different data; in the reconstruction comparison (Table 2), XCube at 4096 tokens achieves a marginally lower CD (1.26 vs. 1.29) than OAT-KL at 439 tokens, but OAT-KL uses ~9× fewer tokens. InstantMesh [83] (an image-to-3D method) is beaten on FID (56.88 vs. 67.93), KID (5.79 vs. 7.23), and runtime (11.3 vs. 21.5 s), though it edges out on CLIP-score (0.31 vs. 0.34 — actually Ours wins here too). OctFusion [82] is only compared in the continuous-latent reconstruction setting (Table 2), where OAT-KL at 439 tokens achieves IoU 91.6 vs. OctFusion's 88.9 at 4096 tokens, a 2.7-point IoU gain with ~9× fewer tokens. The ablation without adaptive subdivision (OAT w/o A.S.) confirms that the quadric-error criterion is essential: at comparable token budgets, removing it drops IoU by 1–2 points (e.g., 88.3 vs. 88.6 at ~600 tokens in Table 1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Perceiver-based VAE paradigm for 3D shape encoding (3DShape2VecSet, Craftsman, CLAY) by replacing fixed-size latent vectors with variable-length latents allocated via an adaptive octree. The key conceptual departure from prior octree-based 3D representations (XCube, OctFusion, Trellis) is the subdivision criterion: rather than splitting cells based on volumetric occupancy, OAT uses a quadric-error metric borrowed from classical mesh simplification (Garland & Heckbert, 1997) to measure local geometric complexity and terminate subdivision early in planar regions. The autoregressive generation component (OctreeGPT) builds on decoder-only transformer architectures (GPT-2) and residual quantization (VQGAN), but is the first to co-design encoding, decoding, and generation around a variable-depth octree structure with structural tokens for tree topology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50% average token reduction (439 vs. 512) while matching or exceeding reconstruction quality has direct practical implications for autoregressive 3D generation, where sequence length governs inference cost and context-window constraints. The repurposing of quadric error—a 25-year-old mesh simplification tool—as a tokenization guide is an elegant cross-pollination that could inform other hierarchical 3D representations. However, the framework is limited to geometry-only (no texture), and the evaluation relies partly on off-the-shelf baselines (XCube, OctFusion) not retrained on the same data, leaving open whether the advantage persists under fully matched conditions. Whether this adaptive tokenization strategy transfers to texture generation, scene-level content, or diffusion-based backbones remains an open question.
