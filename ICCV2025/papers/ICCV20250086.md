## Problem

SAM's Automatic Mask Generation (AMG) produces multi-granularity masks (object, part, sub-part) per point prompt, but naive NMS filtering leads to both over-segmentation (retaining redundant overlapping masks) and under-segmentation (discarding fine details). Existing entity segmentation methods require large annotated datasets and expensive training, limiting scalability and open-world generalization. The paper asks how to achieve high-quality entity segmentation of all perceptually distinct entities in an image without any additional training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250086/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a complete entity-level segmentation mask map covering all perceptually distinct entities without predefined class labels. Internally, SAM's frozen image encoder, prompt encoder, and mask decoder are used with uniform point prompts (32 per side for initial generation, 64 per side for refinement), and the output is a set of non-overlapping, non-redundant binary masks at entity granularity. The entire pipeline is training-free, operating purely as post-processing on SAM's AMG outputs.

## Method

E-SAM is a training-free post-processing pipeline with three sequential modules applied to SAM's AMG outputs. Multi-level Mask Generation (MMG) stratifies masks into object/part/subpart levels by area, applies high-threshold NMS plus IoU-based filtering against best-confidence masks for the object level, and uses superpixel density maps with Adaptive NMS to retain finer-grained masks in dense regions. Entity-level Mask Refinement (EMR) employs a split-then-merge strategy: it separates overlapping object-level masks using a mask gallery built from 64-point sampling as guidance, then merges highly similar adjacent masks by constructing a superpixel-centroid cosine similarity matrix and checking the gallery for encompassing masks. Under-Segmentation Refinement (USR) identifies uncovered superpixel regions, uses their centroids (or part/subpart mask centroids when available) as additional SAM prompts to generate supplementary masks, and fuses them into the entity map via IoU thresholding with a greedy selection to avoid excessive part-level fragments.

## Results

On the EntitySeg benchmark (high-resolution), E-SAM with ViT-H achieves 50.2 AP^e, 66.8 AP_50^e, and 49.9 AP_75^e, outperforming all prior methods. On the low-resolution subset (EntitySeg-LR), E-SAM (ViT-H) reaches 48.9 AP_L^e, more than doubling SAM's object-level map (23.7 AP_L^e). The framework is entirely training-free, leveraging frozen SAM backbones (ViT-B/L/H) with post-processing modules only.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E-SAM (ViT-H) surpasses the strongest panoptic baseline Mask2Former (Swin-L) by +4.0 AP^e (50.2 vs. 46.2) and the best ES-specific method CropFormer (Swin-L) by +2.2 AP^e (50.2 vs. 48.0), all without any task-specific training. Against SAM-based baselines, the gains are dramatic: E-SAM (ViT-H) improves over SAM AMG (ViT-H) by +30.1 AP^e (50.2 vs. 20.1) and over Semantic-SAM (Swin-L) by +33.2 AP^e (50.2 vs. 17.0). On the low-resolution EntitySeg-LR subset, E-SAM (ViT-H) achieves 48.9 AP_L^e versus 23.7 for SAM's object-level map (ViT-H), a +25.2 improvement. The only comparison where E-SAM trails is against CropFormer (Swin-T) at the ViT-B scale (40.1 vs. 42.7 AP^e), but E-SAM recovers and exceeds it at ViT-L and ViT-H. Qualitative results on unseen datasets (SA1B, T-LESS, Urban100, open-world images) confirm strong generalization beyond the EntitySeg benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

E-SAM builds directly on SAM's Automatic Mask Generation (AMG) mode, which was designed for full-image instance segmentation but suffers from over- and under-segmentation due to its uniform point-prompt sampling and naive NMS. The paper departs from prior entity segmentation methods (EntityFramework, CropFormer) that require large annotated datasets and dedicated training, and from Semantic-SAM which couples SAM with a semantic recognition head. Instead, E-SAM introduces a purely training-free post-processing pipeline—Multi-level Mask Generation, Entity-level Mask Refinement, and Under-Segmentation Refinement—that leverages superpixel clustering, adaptive NMS, and a mask gallery to restructure AMG's multi-granularity outputs into coherent entity-level masks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of E-SAM eliminates annotation and training costs entirely, making entity segmentation accessible in open-world settings where new entities appear frequently and labeled data is unavailable. The +30 AP^e gain over SAM's AMG (and near-parity with trained methods like CropFormer at ViT-H) demonstrates that careful post-processing of a frozen foundation model can close most of the gap to supervised approaches. However, the 9.84-second inference time with ViT-H remains a practical barrier for real-time deployment, and the paper acknowledges that parallelization and model distillation are needed. Open questions include whether the pipeline's hand-tuned thresholds (θ_O, γ_O, δ, τ, ρ) transfer robustly to substantially different domains or image resolutions beyond those tested.
