## Problem

Ab initio cryo-EM reconstruction from hundreds of thousands of unordered, unposed particle images still relies on per-scene iterative optimization (EM, SGD, or hybrid neural-iterative pipelines), which is slow, prone to local minima, and requires precomputed CTF parameters. While geometric foundation models like DUSt3R have enabled feed-forward 3D reconstruction in macroscopic settings, they remain unexplored in cryo-EM, where extremely low SNR and CTF distortions make direct pose estimation particularly challenging.

## Input / Output

The method takes N unordered 128×128 grayscale cryo-EM particle images (128 views at inference) as input, with no requirement for precomputed CTF parameters or any pose/shift annotations. It outputs dense Fourier planar maps (H×W×3) encoding per-pixel 3D displacements in Fourier space for each view relative to a reference view, along with confidence maps. These planar maps are then regressed via a weighted Kabsch algorithm into explicit 5D pose parameters (rotation + 2D in-plane translation), which feed into standard Fourier-space back-projection to produce the final 3D density volume.

## Method

CryoFastAR uses a shared ViT-Large encoder with 2D RoPE and learnable view embeddings to extract patch-level features, followed by a decoder of stacked View Integration and Refinement blocks that use cross-attention (linear in the number of views, avoiding quadratic self-attention cost) to progressively integrate multi-view information into a reference view. Two separate prediction heads decode the refined features into Fourier planar maps and confidence maps, a novel representation that encodes where each 2D Fourier slice lies in 3D Fourier space rather than regressing explicit pose parameters. Training follows a three-stage progressive curriculum: pre-training on clean 2-view projections, then full-scale training on 113,600 PDB-derived structures with increasing views (2→32), decreasing SNR (10→0.1), and CTF distortions, followed by fine-tuning on real cryo-EM images. At inference, a reference view is selected by maximum average confidence among 64 candidates, and the predicted planar maps are converted to 5D poses via confidence-weighted least-squares solved by SVD (weighted Kabsch algorithm), enabling fast Fourier back-projection without iterative refinement.

## Results

On simulated datasets (Spliceosome, Spike, FA), CryoFastAR achieves the best in-plane translation error across all three benchmarks (0.596/0.547/0.764 px) and, after a brief CryoSPARC refinement step, the best rotation F-norm error (0.0148/0.0151/0.0169) and reconstruction resolution (8.41/4.26/5.33 Å). Inference takes only ~1 min 21 s per dataset, an order-of-magnitude speedup over CryoSPARC (4–11 min) and ~40× faster than CryoDRGN2 (~55 min). On real EMPIAR datasets, CryoFastAR achieves competitive pose accuracy (e.g., 0.956 rot error on Spliceosome vs. 2.40 for CryoSPARC) while running 3.3× faster than CryoSPARC and 33× faster than CryoDRGN2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CryoSPIN, CryoFastAR dominates on every reported metric: rotation error drops from 1.703 to 0.0416 (Spike), translation error is best-in-class (0.547 px vs. unreported), and inference is ~15× faster (1:21 vs. 21:30). Against CryoDRGN2, CryoFastAR achieves 6× lower translation error (0.596 vs. 3.531 px on Spliceosome) and 40× faster inference (1:22 vs. 56:23); after refinement, it also surpasses CryoDRGN2 on rotation (0.0148 vs. 0.0456) and resolution (8.41 vs. 9.50 Å). Against CryoSPARC, CryoFastAR wins on translation error (0.596 vs. 1.004 px) and speed (1:22 vs. 11:29), and after refinement matches or slightly beats CryoSPARC on rotation (0.0148 vs. 0.0161) and resolution (8.41 vs. 8.41 Å) while remaining ~4× faster. On real EMPIAR data, CryoFastAR clearly outperforms CryoSPARC on the Spliceosome dataset (rot 0.956 vs. 2.400) and RAG (trans 5.11 vs. 7.08 px), but underperforms on the 50S ribosome (rot 1.152 vs. 0.211), which the authors attribute to membrane-protein flexibility not well captured by their simulation-trained model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the DUSt3R paradigm of end-to-end geometric foundation models from macroscopic 3D reconstruction into the cryo-EM domain, replacing per-scene iterative pose optimization (RELION, CryoSPARC) and amortized-inference neural methods (CryoAI, CryoSPIN, CryoDRGN2) with a single feed-forward pass. The key architectural departure is the Fourier planar map representation, which encodes each view's pose as a dense per-pixel 3D displacement in Fourier space rather than regressing conventional 5D parameters, and a linear-complexity cross-attention decoder that avoids the quadratic cost of full multi-view self-attention. The progressive training strategy (clean→noisy, few→many views) addresses the extreme domain gap between typical CV data and cryo-EM images at SNR 0.1.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 10× speedup in ab initio reconstruction with comparable or superior pose accuracy could substantially accelerate structural biology workflows, particularly for large-scale screening of conformational states. The elimination of precomputed CTF parameters simplifies the pipeline and improves generalization. However, the method currently processes only 128 views per forward pass (limiting accuracy on large datasets), struggles with structurally heterogeneous or membrane-associated complexes (50S ribosome), and relies heavily on simulated training data, leaving open questions about scalability to full experimental datasets and robustness to unmodeled experimental artifacts.
