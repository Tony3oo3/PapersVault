## Problem

Existing bias mitigation methods for diffusion-based text-to-image models suffer from attribute entanglement: adjusting target bias attributes (e.g., race, gender) unintentionally alters non-target attributes (e.g., background architecture, socioeconomic context), introducing new biases and degrading output fidelity. No prior method explicitly decouples target from non-target attributes during debiasing, leaving a gap between fair attribute distribution and preservation of the original generation context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250635/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt (e.g., 'photo of a nurse in a hospital') and produces a debiased RGB image from a pretrained Stable Diffusion v1.5 model. At inference, no segmentation masks or external references are required; a target attribute is randomly sampled with equal probability from a predefined set (e.g., {female, male} or {White, Black, Asian, Indian}) and the corresponding EFA module is applied to the U-Net's 16×16-resolution upblock layers. Training, however, relies on human segmentation masks extracted via Grounded SAM2 to constrain where attribute enhancement occurs.

## Method

EFA introduces a lightweight Attention Predictor (three convolutional layers with SiLU activations) that predicts additional attention scores and value vectors for a target attribute, which are concatenated with the original cross-attention scores before the softmax, allowing the target attribute to be injected spatially selectively. Training operates in two scenarios: when intermediate features already encode the target attribute, an L1 regularization drives the AP output toward zero to avoid unnecessary modification; when features encode a counterfactual attribute, a masked reconstruction loss (constrained to human regions via segmentation masks) plus an L1 penalty outside human regions forces the AP to enhance the target attribute only where the counterfactual is semantically active. All AP modules for a given bias share a convolutional backbone with distinct output channels per attribute, enabling joint training. At inference, the randomly sampled attribute's EFA module is applied without any masks, achieving fair attribute distribution while preserving non-target regions.

## Results

On the WinoBias evaluation (T_basic prompts), EFA achieves a deviation ratio (DR) of 0.06 for gender bias, 0.04 for race bias, and 0.03 for combined gender×race bias, substantially outperforming the next-best method (Interpret Diffusion at 0.26, 0.16, and 0.16 respectively). Simultaneously, EFA preserves non-target attributes far better than all baselines, achieving PSNR of 32.52 (gender), 30.93 (race), and 25.58 (gender×race) versus 17.18, 16.87, and 16.91 for Interpret Diffusion. On the COCO-no-person model preservation benchmark, EFA achieves FID scores of 0.23 (gender), 0.13 (race), and 0.45 (gender×race) against the original SD, with CLIP-T scores (26.03–26.21) matching the original model's 26.17, confirming minimal degradation of generative capacity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EFA dominates all four baselines on every metric across all three bias settings. Against Interpret Diffusion [15] (the strongest prior method in bias mitigation), EFA reduces DR by 4–5× (e.g., 0.06 vs. 0.26 for gender) while improving PSNR by ~15 dB (32.52 vs. 17.18) and DINO by ~0.30 (0.916 vs. 0.616), indicating that Interpret Diffusion's uniform h-space manipulation severely distorts non-target attributes. UCE [11] and Finetuning Diffusion [26] both modify model parameters and achieve moderate DR (0.27–0.48) but sacrifice non-target preservation (PSNR 18.96–22.62, DINO 0.709–0.814), confirming the paper's hypothesis that parameter-level edits shift the overall output distribution. Concept Algebra [28] performs worst in bias mitigation (DR 0.51–0.64, sometimes worse than the original SD) while offering only marginal preservation gains over UCE. On the COCO-no-person model preservation benchmark, EFA's FID (0.13–0.45) is an order of magnitude lower than all baselines (1.92–41.65), and its CLIP-T scores (26.03–26.21) match the original SD (26.17), whereas Interpret Diffusion degrades CLIP-T to 22.45–24.80.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work on debiasing diffusion-based T2I models: parameter-modifying finetuning (UCE, Finetuning Diffusion) and inference-time steering (Interpret Diffusion, Concept Algebra, Fair Diffusion). It builds directly on Stable Diffusion v1.5's cross-attention architecture, introducing a lightweight Attention Value Predictor (AP) module that augments the standard cross-attention with attribute-specific value vectors. The key conceptual departure from prior inference-time methods is the explicit identification and treatment of 'attribute entanglement'—the observation that even parameter-preserving steering inadvertently shifts non-target attributes (e.g., background architecture, socioeconomic cues)—addressed through counterfactual-prompt training and spatially constrained regularization via human segmentation masks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that debiasing T2I models without introducing new, potentially more harmful biases (e.g., associating certain racial groups with impoverished housing) is a critical requirement for real-world deployment, yet prior methods fail at this. The method's practicality—no masks needed at inference, equal-probability attribute sampling for user-controllable fairness, and application to only a few upblock layers—makes it relatively easy to adopt. Limitations include the focus on human-centric biases with predefined attribute sets, reliance on SD v1.5's U-Net architecture (unclear transferability to DiT-based models), and the need for a lightweight training phase. Open questions include whether the entanglement problem and EFA's solution generalize to non-human biases (e.g., object-level stereotypes), to newer diffusion architectures, and to settings where the target attribute set is not known a priori.
