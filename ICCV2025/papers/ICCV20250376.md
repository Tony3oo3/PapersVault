## Problem

Existing regression losses for heatmap-based facial landmark detection (MSE, Wing, Adaptive Wing, STAR) are insensitive to small errors caused by peak mismatches and cannot adaptively focus on hard-to-detect landmarks. Additionally, the extreme pixel-level imbalance between sparse landmark regions and dominant background pixels causes standard losses to produce blurry, dilated heatmaps with reduced foreground intensity, leading to inaccurate landmark localization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250376/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB face images and produces per-landmark heatmaps (probability distributions in [0,1]) via a four-stacked hourglass network, with landmark counts varying by dataset (29 for COFW, 68 for 300W, 98 for WFLW). The loss is computed pixel-wise over the heatmap, where each pixel's ground truth value p_y and predicted value p_x are both in [0,1], and the final landmark position is extracted as the argmax of the predicted heatmap.

## Method

PossLoss is defined as -p_y · |(p_y - p_x)/d|^r · log(p_x), combining three terms: a self-weighting term (p_y) that uses the ground truth heatmap value as a position-aware weight so pixels nearer the landmark receive greater influence, a distribution-modulating term |(p_y - p_x)/d|^r that captures the absolute distribution error to guide global convergence and distinguish easy from hard landmarks, and a standard entropy term log(p_x). The temperature hyperparameter d (set to 0.1) scales the distribution error magnitude, while the weighting parameter r (set to 2) controls the emphasis on distribution error; when r=0 the loss reduces to cross-entropy. The key insight is that the self-weighting term replaces fixed class-frequency weights with a continuous, position-dependent weighting derived from the Gaussian ground truth, and the distribution-modulating term provides sensitivity to peak mismatches that cross-entropy and focal loss cannot capture, since they still assign non-zero loss even when predictions perfectly match ground truth.

## Results

On WFLW, PossLoss achieves the best FR₁₀% of 2.12 (vs. STAR's 2.32) and a competitive NME of 4.07 (vs. STAR's 4.02), with AUC₁₀% of 0.600. On COFW, PossLoss attains the lowest NME of 4.60, surpassing STAR (4.62) and ADNet (4.68). On 300W, PossLoss leads on all three splits: Common (2.84 vs. STAR 2.87), Challenging (2.51 vs. STAR 2.52), and overall (4.21 vs. STAR 4.32), with the Challenging subset showing the largest margin (+0.11 NME over the second-best).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PossLoss is compared against 15 state-of-the-art methods using the same four-stacked Hourglass backbone (13.37 M params). The closest competitor is STAR loss [40], which achieves the best WFLW NME (4.02 vs. 4.07) and AUC₁₀% (0.605 vs. 0.600), but PossLoss wins decisively on WFLW FR₁₀% (2.12 vs. 2.32, a 0.20 absolute improvement) and on all three 300W splits (Comm 2.84 vs. 2.87, Chall 2.51 vs. 2.52, All 4.21 vs. 4.32). On COFW, PossLoss edges out STAR by 0.02 NME (4.60 vs. 4.62). Against earlier heatmap losses, PossLoss improves over Adaptive Wing [31] by 0.29 NME and 0.72 FR₁₀% on WFLW, and over Wing loss [9] by 0.92 NME and 3.88 FR₁₀%. Among coordinate-regression methods (SLPT [35], DTLD [19], RePFormer [20]), PossLoss achieves lower NME on COFW (4.60 vs. 4.79/–/–) and 300W Comm (2.84 vs. 3.17/2.96/3.01). The largest single margin is on 300W Challenging, where PossLoss improves 0.11 NME over the second-best (STAR 2.52), highlighting its sensitivity to hard-to-detect landmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the loss-function design space for heatmap-based facial landmark detection rather than building on a single prior method. It identifies specific limitations across the existing loss landscape—MSE's insensitivity to small peak errors, Wing loss's gradient accumulation on background pixels, Adaptive Wing loss's inability to focus on hard landmarks, and STAR loss's focus on semantic ambiguity rather than distribution reliability—and proposes a unified solution combining a position-aware self-weighting term with a distribution-modulating term. Conceptually, the self-weighting mechanism repurposes the ground-truth heatmap intensity as an adaptive pixel weight (analogous to focal loss's confidence-based reweighting but tied to spatial position), while the distribution-modulating term introduces an explicit distribution-distance penalty absent from prior heatmap regression losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The loss function is model-agnostic and could be dropped into any heatmap-based detector, making it a practical tool for practitioners working on facial or anatomical landmark detection. However, the evaluation is limited to a single backbone (four-stacked Hourglass) and three facial landmark datasets, leaving open whether the gains transfer to other architectures (e.g., Transformers, U-Nets) or to non-facial anatomical landmark tasks. The improvements over the strongest baseline (STAR loss) are modest and selective—PossLoss achieves the best FR₁₀% on WFLW but slightly worse NME (4.07 vs. 4.02)—suggesting the distribution-modulating term trades some overall accuracy for better hard-landmark coverage. A key open question is whether the hyperparameter sensitivity (r and d must be carefully tuned) limits real-world adoption, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the specific Gaussian-heatmap formulation used here.
