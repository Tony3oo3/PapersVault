## Problem

Existing VLM benchmarks (SEED-Bench, MMMU) evaluate general vision-language capabilities but do not address geospatial-specific challenges such as temporal change detection, tiny object counting, and multi-scale remote sensing interpretation. Prior geospatial benchmarks like VLEO lack multi-temporal analysis, non-optical imagery, and segmentation tasks, and do not compare geospatial-specific models against generic VLMs. This leaves no comprehensive, manually verified benchmark to objectively assess VLM performance across the full spectrum of earth-observation tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250987/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes satellite/aerial imagery in optical, SAR, and multi-spectral modalities, along with temporal image pairs and text-based queries (MCQ prompts or referring expressions). For MCQ tasks, each question presents five options: one correct answer, one semantically similar 'closest' distractor, and three plausible alternatives; counting questions use controlled deviations of ±20% and ±40% from ground truth. Outputs are structured as MCQ selections, bounding boxes (evaluated at IoU 0.25 and 0.50), binary segmentation masks (mIOU), free-text captions (BERTScore), and object counts, spanning 31 fine-grained tasks across 8 categories with over 10,000 manually verified instructions.

## Method

GEOBench-VLM is constructed via a hybrid pipeline that samples images from multiple open-source remote sensing datasets (DOTA, DIOR, FAIR1M, MAR20, FireRisk, RarePlanes, etc.) and generates structured MCQs using GPT-4o, followed by manual verification and refinement. Spatial relationship annotations are manually labeled from detection dataset coordinates with cross-verification, while caption generation combines GPT-4o outputs with object attributes and spatial interactions, then manually edited for precision. The benchmark evaluates 13 VLMs (7 generic open-source, 1 closed-source GPT-4o, and 5 geospatial-specific) using task-specific metrics: accuracy for MCQs, precision at multiple IoU thresholds for referring expression detection, mIOU for segmentation, and BERTScore for captioning. A key design choice is the MCQ format with a 'closest' distractor to reduce hallucination and enable automated, bias-resistant scoring. The evaluation further includes ablations on object density, prompt sensitivity, single vs. multi-temporal inputs, and object size to characterize model failure modes.

## Results

On GEOBench-VLM (10,000+ manually verified MCQs across 31 geospatial tasks), the best-performing model LLaVA-OneVision achieves 41.7% overall accuracy, slightly ahead of GPT-4o (41.1%) and Qwen2-VL (40.2%), all roughly double the 20% random-guess baseline. At the category level, GPT-4o leads in object classification (58.6%) and scene understanding (71.1%), EarthDial dominates scene understanding (77.1%) and event detection (54.2%), LLaVA-OneVision tops counting (43.8%), and Sphinx achieves the highest BERTScore for captioning (0.6451). For referring expression detection, Sphinx attains the best precision (0.341 at IoU 0.5) while GPT-4o is worst (0.009), and for non-optical earthquake magnitude estimation, Qwen2-VL leads (0.273).

## Baselines & Comparisons

No single VLM dominates all geospatial categories. LLaVA-OneVision [18] achieves the highest overall MCQ accuracy (41.7%) and leads in counting (43.8%), outperforming GPT-4o (39.7%) and Qwen2-VL (40.2%) on that task. GPT-4o [34] is strongest in object classification (58.6%) and disaster-type classification (63.0%), but performs worst in referring expression detection (Prec@0.5 = 0.009) and non-optical earthquake estimation (0.083). EarthDial [40], a geospatial-specific model, excels in scene understanding (77.1%) and event detection (54.2%), surpassing all generic VLMs on those categories. Qwen2-VL [46] is the top performer on non-optical earthquake magnitude estimation (0.273) and competitive in disaster classification (59.9%). Sphinx [22] and Ferret [54] trail in overall accuracy (20.3% and 17.7%) yet Sphinx leads in captioning BERTScore (0.645) and referring expression detection precision (0.341 at IoU 0.5). Geospatial-specific models (GeoChat, RS-LLaVA, SkySenseGPT, LHRS-Bot-Nova) cluster between 27.9%–31.5% overall, generally underperforming the top generic VLMs on counting and object classification but remaining competitive on scene understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying the fragmented landscape of geospatial VLM evaluation into a single comprehensive suite. Prior geospatial benchmarks (VLEO-Bench, EarthVQA, GeoChat-Bench, RSIEval) each cover narrow subsets of tasks, while generic VLM benchmarks (SEED-Bench, MMMU, MMBench) ignore geospatial-specific challenges like scale variation, temporal change, and non-optical modalities. GEOBench-VLM repurposes existing remote sensing datasets (DOTA, DIOR, FAIR1M, MAR20) and combines them with GPT-4o-assisted question generation and manual verification to create a 10,000+ instruction benchmark spanning 31 fine-grained tasks across 8 categories, deliberately filling gaps in temporal analysis, non-optical imagery, and segmentation that no prior geospatial benchmark addressed simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even the best-performing VLM (LLaVA-OneVision) achieves only 41.7% MCQ accuracy—barely double random guess—exposing a substantial gap between current VLM capabilities and the demands of practical geospatial applications like disaster response and environmental monitoring. Temporal reasoning and non-optical data interpretation remain particularly weak across all 13 evaluated models, and no single model dominates all task categories, suggesting that geospatial VLM development requires task-specific rather than one-size-fits-all approaches. Open questions include how to effectively leverage multi-temporal information (which sometimes degrades performance), how to improve counting in high-density scenes, and whether domain-specific pretraining can close the gap with generic models on spatial reasoning tasks.
