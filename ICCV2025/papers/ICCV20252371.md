## Problem

Dense 4D reconstruction of egocentric videos requires per-pixel 3D geometry across all frames in a global coordinate system, but existing supervised feed-forward methods (DUSt3R, MonSt3R, CUT3R) are constrained by the severe scarcity of labeled egocentric video data, while traditional SfM/SLAM pipelines struggle with dynamic hand-object interactions and accumulate errors across modules. No prior work has demonstrated a self-supervised, generalizable approach to dense pointclouds sequence reconstruction for the label-scarce egocentric domain.

## Input / Output

The model takes a monocular egocentric video as input, resized to 288×384, and processes it in 4-frame windows with a sliding-window inference strategy (1-frame overlap) to handle arbitrarily long sequences. It simultaneously predicts per-frame depth (H×W), a shared camera intrinsic matrix (3×3), per-frame camera-to-world poses (4×4), and confidence masks; these are combined to produce the final dense pointclouds sequence (H×W×3 per frame in global coordinates). Camera poses are not directly regressed but derived in closed form via weighted Procrustes alignment (SVD) between unprojected pointclouds from adjacent frames, making the pose estimation differentiable and integrated into the end-to-end training loop.

## Method

EgoMono4D extends the pretrained UniDepth single-frame depth-and-intrinsic estimator to a video model by inserting temporal adaptor blocks (a Transformer over global tokens and a Unet3D over DINO patch features) and a new 3-layer MLP confidence-mask decoder. Camera poses are recovered by solving a weighted Procrustes alignment between unprojected per-frame pointclouds, where the weights come from predicted confidence masks that exclude dynamic regions, occlusions, and flow-inaccurate areas. Training is fully self-supervised on 11.2M unlabeled egocentric frames using a composite loss: a 3D photometric loss that minimizes reprojection error between flow/track-induced correspondences in high-confidence regions (scale-normalized by the first principal component of the pointcloud), a shape regularization loss aligning per-frame geometry to UniDepth predictions under a scaled SE(3) transform, a BCE mask regularization against EgoHOS segmentation priors, and a camera-intrinsic consistency loss across overlapping sub-clips. The encoder is frozen and only the adaptors, decoders, and confidence-mask head are trained with Adam at lr 5e-5 for 350k iterations on 8 A800 GPUs.

## Results

On the in-domain HOI4D dataset, EgoMono4D achieves the best Chamfer Distance (5.9 mm) and F-scores (F1=27.9%, F2.5=59.6%, F5=83.1%), outperforming the strongest baseline DS+UniDepth (CD=6.7, F1=23.2%). On the zero-shot POV-Surgery dataset, EgoMono4D shows a particularly large margin, achieving F1=13.5% vs. 7.7% for DS+UniDepth and F5=53.9% vs. 38.7%, a 10–20% improvement in F-score over all baselines. On ARCTIC-HOI (zero-shot), EgoMono4D attains the lowest CD (2.8 mm) and highest F1 (24.1%) and F5 (86.2%), while on H2O it leads in F1 (54.2%) and F2.5 (83.9%) despite a slightly higher CD than the modularized baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EgoMono4D outperforms all six baselines on the majority of metrics across all four datasets. Against the strongest modularized baseline DS+UniDepth (DROID-SLAM + UniDepth), EgoMono4D wins on HOI4D (CD 5.9 vs 6.7, F1 27.9 vs 23.2), POV-Surgery zero-shot (F1 13.5 vs 7.7, F5 53.9 vs 38.7), and ARCTIC-HOI zero-shot (CD 2.8 vs 2.9, F1 24.1 vs 22.2), though DS+UniDepth edges ahead on ARCTIC-HOI F2.5 (60.2 vs 57.5). The supervised feed-forward methods (DUSt3R, MonSt3R, Align3R, CUT3R) all underperform EgoMono4D on egocentric data, with MonSt3R showing the largest degradation on H2O (CD 14.7, F1 15.1) due to domain gap from fine-tuning on small synthetic datasets. The modularized no-training version of EgoMono4D (MapFreeVR + UniDepth) performs significantly worse than the end-to-end model, confirming that self-supervised 4D alignment via backpropagation is critical. On the zero-shot POV-Surgery benchmark, EgoMono4D achieves a 10–20% F-score advantage over all baselines, highlighting the benefit of self-supervised training on large-scale in-the-wild egocentric videos over supervised methods trained on limited lab-based data.

## Relation to Prior Work

EgoMono4D extends the pretrained single-frame UniDepth model to video via adaptor blocks (Transformer for global tokens, Unet3D for DINO patch features) and reframes camera pose estimation as a confidence-masked Procrustes alignment problem following FlowMap/FlowCam. It departs from the supervised feed-forward 4D reconstruction line (DUSt3R, MonSt3R, Align3R, CUT3R) by replacing ground-truth labels with self-supervised 3D flow/track alignment losses and regularization from off-the-shelf models (UniDepth for shape, EgoHOS for masks). The approach also builds on self-supervised monocular depth estimation (Kick Back & Relax, etc.) but elevates the objective from 2D photometric consistency to explicit 3D geometric alignment, and targets the label-scarce egocentric domain specifically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that self-supervised training on large-scale unlabeled egocentric videos can match or exceed supervised methods for dense 4D reconstruction in a label-scarce domain, offering a practical path toward internet-scale egocentric scene understanding without expensive annotation. The >30× speedup over test-time-optimization methods (e.g., EgoGaussian) makes large-scale reconstruction feasible. Limitations include reliance on multiple pretrained components (UniDepth, EgoHOS, GMFlow, CoTracker), evaluation restricted to small lab-based datasets, and performance degradation at very low frame rates. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly in-the-wild egocentric data (e.g., Ego4D) and whether the self-supervised paradigm can be extended to handle more complex dynamic interactions.
