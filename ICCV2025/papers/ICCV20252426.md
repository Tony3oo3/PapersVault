## Problem

Video object segmentation in low-light environments suffers from severe texture loss and noise, making image-based feature alignment and mask propagation unreliable. Existing event-assisted methods simply concatenate or fuse image and event features without exploiting event data for spatial alignment or iterative memory refinement, leaving a gap in how to effectively leverage high-temporal-resolution motion cues under degraded illumination.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252426/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each time step, EVOLVE consumes a current low-light RGB frame, its corresponding event stream, and N=3 reference frames (the first annotated frame plus two randomly sampled from the five nearest), each with their predicted masks and events. Inputs are cropped to 256×256 (LLE-VOS) or 480×480 (LLE-DAVIS) and encoded via ResNet-50 backbones. The output is a per-pixel binary segmentation mask produced by a Mask2Former-style pixel decoder, with auxiliary masks also predicted at each DMOT iteration for memory updates.

## Method

EVOLVE's key insight is deriving deformable convolution offsets from event features rather than image features, since events remain robust to low-light degradation and provide fine-grained motion cues for spatial alignment. The Event-Guided Deformable Feature Transfer (EDFT) module applies these event-driven offsets to both current and reference image/mask features, then performs dual cross-attention (image-based and event-based) to transfer reference mask information to the current frame. The Dual-Memory Object Transformer (DMOT) iteratively refines the resulting object features through self-attention, cross-attention with separate image-based and event-based memory banks, and FFN layers over K=3 iterations. A Memory Refinement Module (MRM) updates each memory by extracting object-relevant features via predicted spatial masks and weighting tokens by their IoU with the auxiliary segmentation, ensuring memories retain only object-relevant information across iterations.

## Results

On LLE-VOS Indoor, EVOLVE achieves J&F of 0.832 (J=0.847, F=0.817), surpassing the previous best event-based method LLE-VOS [12] (J&F=0.749) by +8.3 points. On LLE-VOS Outdoor, EVOLVE reaches J&F of 0.714, improving over LLE-VOS [12] by +11.8 points. On LLE-DAVIS, EVOLVE attains J&F of 0.699, outperforming LLE-VOS [12] by +7.1 points. EVOLVE also achieves the best efficiency among all compared methods with 45.3M parameters, 113.86G FLOPs, and 39.3 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EVOLVE outperforms all seven baselines across all three benchmarks. Against the closest event-based competitor LLE-VOS [12], EVOLVE gains +8.3 J&F on LLE-VOS Indoor (0.832 vs. 0.749), +11.8 on LLE-VOS Outdoor (0.714 vs. 0.596), and +7.1 on LLE-DAVIS (0.699 vs. 0.628). Among image+event baselines, OneVOS [14] (J&F=0.724/0.622/0.631) and Cutie [9] (J&F=0.733/0.612/0.623) are the strongest, yet EVOLVE still leads by 9.8–10.8 J&F points on LLE-VOS Indoor. The image-only baselines (STCN, XMem, AOT, DeAOT) show substantially lower performance, with DeAOT [40] reaching at most J&F=0.712 on LLE-VOS Indoor, confirming that event information is critical in low-light settings. In terms of efficiency, EVOLVE uses fewer parameters (45.3M) and FLOPs (113.86G) than all baselines while achieving the highest FPS (39.3), compared to OneVOS (96.3M, 344.28G, 13.8 FPS) and Cutie (46.4M, 121.6G, 36.2 FPS).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of memory-based VOS (AOT, DeAOT, Cutie, OneVOS) and event-camera-based low-light segmentation (LLE-VOS [12]), repurposing deformable convolutions in a novel way: rather than deriving spatial offsets from image features (which are unreliable under low illumination), it computes them from event features that retain fine-grained motion cues. The dual-memory architecture extends the single-memory paradigm of prior VOS methods by maintaining separate image-based and event-based memory streams, while the memory refinement module with IoU-based token weighting departs from simpler mask-pooling strategies used in Cutie. Conceptually, the paper carves new ground by treating event data not merely as an additional input channel but as a structural guide for spatial adaptation and memory curation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Low-light VOS is critical for real-world applications (autonomous driving, surveillance, robotics) where illumination is uncontrolled, and the demonstrated efficiency gains (lowest FLOPs and highest FPS among all compared methods) make the approach practical for deployment. The finding that event-driven deformable offsets substantially outperform image-driven ones in low-light settings offers a transferable insight for other vision tasks under degraded sensing. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to scenarios without synchronized event cameras, how it scales to longer sequences with more reference frames, and whether the dual-memory overhead becomes prohibitive in resource-constrained edge settings.
