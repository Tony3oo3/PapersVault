## Problem

Video-based robotic arm action recognition (RVAR) lacks a large-scale, diverse benchmark: existing robot learning datasets are small, task-specific, and exclude anomalous executions. Moreover, robotic arm actions are composed of multiple atomic sub-behaviors whose inter-relationships are difficult to capture with abstract category labels alone, making fine-grained recognition particularly challenging.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251019/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a video clip (16 frames at 224×224, sampled via primary sparse sampling) and produces an action class label from 8 categories (RobAVA-S) or 100 categories (RobAVA-R), where each category is further split into normal and anomalous variants. Textual prompts—category labels and LLM-generated atomic attribute descriptions (4 per category, ≤12 words each)—are fed through a frozen CLIP text encoder to form the semantic conditioning signals. At inference, the final prediction is the argmax cosine similarity between the video-conditioned attribute central embedding and the stored category prototypes.

## Method

AGPT-Net redefines robotic arm action recognition as alignment between video sequences and fine-grained atomic attributes (temporally decoupled sub-action descriptions generated by ChatGPT-4) rather than coarse category labels. A video-conditioned attribute encoder first applies DPC-KNN density-peaks clustering for spatial saliency merging (reducing tokens by a factor of s²=16), then performs multi-scale temporal downsampling (factors {1,2}) and multi-headed cross-attention between attribute prompts and video tokens to produce video-specific attribute embeddings. A parallel video-conditioned category encoder processes category prompts identically. A Joint Semantic Space Constraint (JSC) ranking loss pulls each sample's attribute embedding toward its normal-class prototype while repelling it from the anomalous prototype by a margin α=0.2, explicitly amplifying the separation between normal and anomalous executions. The total objective combines cross-entropy classification with the JSC loss (λ=0.2), and the backbone is a CLIP-pretrained ViT-L/14 from UniFormerV2.

## Results

On RobAVA-S, AGPT-Net achieves 94.8% Mean and 96.5% Top-1 accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline UniFormerV2 ViT-L/14 (92.6% Mean, 94.1% Top-1) by 2.2 and 2.4 percentage points respectively. On the more challenging RobAVA-R (100 fine-grained categories), AGPT-Net reaches 53.8% Mean and 77.6% Top-1, surpassing UniFormerV2 (50.2% Mean, 76.6% Top-1) by 3.6 and 1.0 points. On the fine-grained FineGym benchmark, AGPT-Net also leads with 89.8% Mean / 94.0% Top-1 on FineGym99 and 54.7% Mean / 89.7% Top-1 on FineGym288, confirming generalizability beyond robotic arm actions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGPT-Net outperforms all 13 baselines on both RobAVA subsets. The closest competitors are UniFormerV2 ViT-L/14 (92.6% Mean on RobAVA-S, 50.2% on RobAVA-R) and Wu et al. ViT-L/14 (92.3% / 49.3%), which AGPT-Net surpasses by 2.2–3.6 and 2.5–4.5 percentage points respectively. Among CLIP-pretrained VLM baselines, X-CLIP (91.0% / 48.8%) and ActionCLIP (91.4% / 44.8%) trail AGPT-Net by 3.8–9.0 points, highlighting the benefit of atomic-attribute-guided prompt tuning over direct category-label alignment. Traditional CNN/Transformer backbones (I3D, TSM, TEA, TAM, ViViT, TimeSformer, VideoSwin, MViT) are consistently 2–7 points behind on RobAVA-R, where fine-grained distinctions and anomalous instances pose the greatest challenge. Notably, AGPT-Net achieves these gains with only 16 input frames and 16.9 GFLOPs, matching or exceeding methods that use 32–128 frames or substantially higher FLOPs (e.g., VideoSwin-L at 105.4 GFLOPs).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of video action recognition and robotics by introducing the first large-scale dataset (RobAVA) purpose-built for robotic arm action understanding, as opposed to existing robot learning datasets (GraspNet, RoboNet, Open X-Embodiment) that target manipulation or grasping rather than action classification. Methodologically, AGPT-Net extends the vision-language prompt tuning paradigm (CLIP, X-CLIP, ActionCLIP) by replacing abstract category labels with LLM-generated atomic attribute descriptions, and introduces a Joint Semantic Space Constraint to separate normal from anomalous executions. The video encoder builds on UniFormerV2's spatial-temporal transformer architecture, while the spatial saliency merging step repurposes DPC-KNN density-peak clustering for token compression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RobAVA fills a critical gap by providing a standardized benchmark with both normal and anomalous instances, enabling research on robust robotic arm action recognition that mirrors real-world operational variability. The atomic-attribute framing offers a principled way to decompose complex multi-step actions into learnable sub-behaviors, a strategy that could transfer to other fine-grained video understanding domains. However, the paper acknowledges that detecting subtle anomalous actions remains challenging, and the dataset's scale (~40K videos) is modest compared to human action recognition benchmarks like Kinetics, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to more complex multi-robot or long-horizon scenarios.
