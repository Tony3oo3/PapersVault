## Problem

Current video training methods rely on fixed spatiotemporal sampling grids that extract a predetermined number of tokens, making models rigid and unable to adapt to diverse computational budgets. This inflexibility means high-performance models trained in resource-rich settings cannot be efficiently deployed on constrained devices, and simply reducing frame counts or resolution at inference yields sub-optimal accuracy-computation trade-offs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250851/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes video clips sampled at variable frame counts (4–24 frames) and spatial resolutions (168–252 px) during training, with a token pool threshold of 2048–4096 tokens. At inference, it accepts a fixed token budget (e.g., 3072, 2048, 1024, or 512 tokens) and dynamically selects which spatiotemporal positions to fill from a more densely sampled source, producing class labels, video-text similarity scores, or free-form text responses depending on the downstream task. The same trained weights serve all token counts without architectural modification.

## Method

Flux introduces a flexible training augmentation that combines (1) Flexi-Sampling, which randomly varies frame count and spatial resolution per video within a batch, and (2) a Group-Dynamic Selector that partitions the tokenized frame sequence into sparse temporal groups and selects the top-K tokens per group by L2 temporal change, ensuring full-video coverage while prioritizing informative regions. This double-mask strategy (dynamic selection for the teacher, attention-based masking for the student) is integrated into the Unmasked Teacher pre-training framework at zero additional forward cost. Two plug-in modules—Global-Local Positional Embedding (a depth-wise conv over sine-cosine global positions plus a value-dependent linear projection in attention) and Dual Patch Normalization (an extra LayerNorm before and after patch embedding)—stabilize training under variable token counts and resolutions. The model is co-trained with multiple token counts (e.g., 2048/1024/512) per batch using self-distillation alignment, enabling seamless deployment at any budget via a heuristic Token Optimization search over sampling configurations.

## Results

On Kinetics-400, FluxViT-S achieves 88.0% top-1 accuracy (vs. 85.8% for InternVideo2-S) and FluxViT-B achieves 90.0% (vs. 88.4% for InternVideo2-B) under standard computation. On motion-intensive SSv2, FluxViT-S reaches 73.8% and FluxViT-B reaches 75.6% top-1, both setting new state-of-the-art. With Token Optimization using only 1/4 of the tokens (49×12 GFLOPs for S, 49×12 for B), FluxViT-S still achieves 84.7% on K400 and FluxViT-B achieves 87.4%, matching or exceeding prior SOTA models at full computation with ~90% computational savings. On zero-shot retrieval, FluxViT-B attains 49.9% R@1 on MSRVTT, 53.5% on DiDeMo, and 56.7% on ActivityNet, surpassing much larger models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FluxViT consistently outperforms the InternVideo2 series (its direct predecessor built on the same UMT framework) across all benchmarks: +2.2% on K400 (88.0 vs 85.8 for S), +2.3% on SSv2 (73.8 vs 71.5 for S), +2.1% on COIN (92.1 vs 90.0 for S), and +4.7% R@1 on MSRVTT (45.0 vs 40.3 for B-scale). Against UMT-B/L, FluxViT-B gains +2.6% on K400 (90.0 vs 87.4) and +4.8% on SSv2 (75.6 vs 70.8), while FluxViT-S surpasses UMT-L on retrieval (45.0 vs 40.7 R@1 on MSRVTT). On SSv2, FluxViT-B (75.6%) exceeds all prior methods including VideoMAE-L (74.0%) and UniFormerV2-L (73.0%), and notably maintains 75.1% at only 25% of the standard computation cost, whereas FFN [88] shows substantial degradation at 2 frames. In zero-shot retrieval, FluxViT-B (49.9% MSRVTT, 53.5% DiDeMo, 56.7% ANet) surpasses LanguageBind-H (44.8/39.9/41.0) and VAST-G (49.3/55.5/–) despite using far less training data (27M vs. billions). On chat-centric benchmarks, FluxViT-L (49.0 MVbench, 29.5 Dream1k-F1) outperforms SigLIP336-L (46.7/29.2), CLIP-L (45.6/28.4), and UMT-L (45.0/24.6) in the linear-probe setting, confirming the ViT's superior representation quality under frozen-LLM evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Unmasked Teacher (UMT) framework by introducing flexible spatiotemporal sampling and a Group-Dynamic token selector on the teacher side, harvesting more diverse representations without additional cost. It departs from prior flexible-training approaches (Resformer, FFN) that handle spatial or temporal resolution variation separately, arguing that simply reducing frames or resolution under budget constraints is suboptimal compared to optimizing which tokens are selected from a richer sampling pool. The Token Optimization paradigm also contrasts with post-hoc token reduction methods (MAR, MCM, Vid-TLDR), which the authors show perform poorly at high reduction rates because rigidly-trained models cannot generalize to sparse token inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: FluxViT achieves 70–95% computation savings while matching or exceeding prior SOTA across classification, retrieval, and chat-centric benchmarks, making high-quality video understanding feasible on resource-constrained devices. The Flux augmentation tool is plug-and-play with mainstream training frameworks (UMT, supervised, contrastive, chat-centric), lowering the barrier to adoption. Open questions include whether more advanced token selectors (e.g., Vid-TLDR) can further improve results without the associated cost and instability, and whether the group-based selection adequately handles videos with consistent camera motion. The approach has not yet been validated at the largest model scales or with fully end-to-end chat model training.
