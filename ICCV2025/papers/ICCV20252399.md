## Problem

Existing affordance detection models are limited to single-object settings, closed semantic vocabularies, and heavy reliance on high-quality manual annotations, making them ineffective for robots operating in cluttered real-world scenes with complex, ambiguous natural language instructions. OVA-Fields addresses this gap by enabling open-vocabulary, part-level affordance detection directly in multi-object 3D scenes under a weakly supervised paradigm that learns affordance correlations from scene observations rather than per-instance labels.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of RGB-D frames (captured with consumer-grade LiDAR devices), camera intrinsics and pose data for 3D reconstruction, and a free-form natural language query. Small-object bounding boxes below 128×128 pixels are upscaled via BSRGAN before affordance extraction. The output is a set of 3D coordinates (x, y, z) marking actionable regions, selected by thresholding per-point cosine similarity scores against the query embedding, where the threshold is dynamically set to the last prominent peak in the similarity histogram rather than a fixed value.

## Method

OVA-Fields comprises three modules: (1) Multi-Modal Affordance Perception (MAP), which extracts 2D affordance heatmaps via LOCATE, encodes them into semantic vectors with SentenceBERT, computes CLIP visual embeddings per bounding box, and fuses them with a sigmoid-gated dynamic weight that adaptively balances geometric and semantic contributions; (2) Spatial Feature Extraction, which maps each 3D point through a Multiresolution Hash Encoder over a 0.1 m spherical neighborhood, concatenates the result with an affordance embedding, and refines it via multi-head attention and an MLP that splits the output into separate image and affordance sub-embeddings; (3) Query Mapping, which encodes the user instruction with SentenceBERT and retrieves actionable points by cosine similarity. Training is weakly supervised: an InfoNCE-style affordance contrastive loss aligns 3D point features with LOCATE-derived affordance descriptions, while a visual contrastive loss aligns them with CLIP embeddings from corresponding RGB regions, eliminating the need for per-instance manual affordance labels.

## Results

OVA-Fields achieves 52.4% mIoU on the Lab dataset and 54.2% mIoU on SceneFun3D, representing a 3.4× improvement over the best baseline (15.4% mIoU for 3D AffordanceNet on Lab). On the Home dataset, it reaches 46.2% mIoU with 73.3% mPrec, substantially outperforming all baselines. On ScanNet, OVA-Fields attains 13.2% mIoU, slightly below CLIP-FO3D's 15.3%, but still leads in precision (27.6% vs. 21.2%). In real-robot manipulation, the framework achieves a 90% success rate on refrigerator door-opening tasks with 4.7 s average latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OVA-Fields decisively outperforms all seven baselines on three of four datasets. On Lab, it achieves 52.4% mIoU versus the best baseline OpenAD at 19.9% (2.6× gap) and closed-set 3D AffordanceNet at 15.4% (3.4× gap). On SceneFun3D, the 54.2% mIoU nearly doubles the next-best CLIP-FO3D (24.5%) and CLIP-Fields (23.2%). On Home, OVA-Fields leads with 46.2% mIoU against OpenAD's 25.2%. The sole exception is ScanNet, where CLIP-FO3D edges out OVA-Fields (15.3% vs. 13.2% mIoU), though OVA-Fields still achieves higher precision (27.6% vs. 21.2%). Among open-vocabulary baselines, CLIP-FO3D and CLIP-Fields are the strongest competitors, while closed-set methods (3D AffordanceNet, Mask3D) and OpenMask3D perform poorly in scene-level settings, confirming the advantage of the proposed multilevel geometric encoding and contrastive affordance fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) single-object 3D affordance detection (3D AffordanceNet, AffordanceNet), (2) open-vocabulary 3D scene understanding via CLIP-based neural fields (CLIP-Fields, OpenScene, CLIP-FO3D), and (3) open-vocabulary affordance detection on point clouds (OpenAD, OpenMask3D). It departs from prior work by unifying part-level affordance localization with scene-level open-vocabulary querying in a single weakly supervised pipeline, using Multiresolution Hash Encoding for spatial representation and SentenceBERT for mapping complex natural language to actionable 3D regions. The key architectural departure is the dynamic sigmoid-gated affordance weighting that adaptively balances geometric and semantic cues, rather than the fixed or absent fusion in prior open-vocabulary scene models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that weakly supervised, open-vocabulary affordance detection can bridge the gap between natural language understanding and precise robotic manipulation in cluttered real-world scenes, achieving 90% success on refrigerator-opening tasks with consumer-grade RGB-D sensing. However, the paper itself highlights a critical open problem: the detection-to-manipulation gap, where 90% spatial accuracy on cup affordances translates to only 20% grasping success due to unmodeled approach angles and force directions. Future work on torque-aware manipulation and spatial-temporal trajectory prediction for articulated objects remains necessary before this class of methods can handle the full range of household interactions.
