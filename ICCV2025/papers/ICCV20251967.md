## Problem

Scaling diffusion transformers to 2K video resolution is computationally prohibitive due to quadratic attention cost growth with token count, and existing efficient methods (high-compression VAEs, token compression) sacrifice generative quality and structural coherence. No prior framework achieves both high-quality 2K video synthesis and practical inference speed simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251967/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt as input and produces a 5-second, 24fps, 2K-resolution video (49 or 121 frames). Internally, the pipeline operates in a highly compressed latent space (32×32×8 spatial-temporal compression via LTX-Video's VAE, yielding 8× fewer tokens than standard 8×8×4 VAEs), and the two-stage design means the model first synthesizes a low-resolution video whose intermediate DiT features are extracted to condition the final high-resolution generation—no explicit pixel-space LR-to-HR conditioning is used.

## Method

Turbo2K combines two key mechanisms. First, heterogeneous model distillation transfers generative capability from a 13B HunyuanVideo teacher to a 2B LTX-Video student despite their different VAEs and architectures, by aligning intermediate DiT feature representations via a projector network with a cosine-similarity loss; critically, synchronizing teacher and student timesteps (t_tea = t_stu) during distillation outperforms fixed-timestep alignment, as it captures the teacher's progressive denoising trajectory rather than just its clean-data endpoint. Second, a hierarchical two-stage synthesis framework generates a low-resolution video, extracts multi-level intermediate features from selected DiT blocks at a late diffusion step (t=0.1), and injects them into the corresponding HR DiT blocks via fusion blocks (upsampled guidance added to normalized HR features, then modulated by time-embedding parameters), eliminating the decode-reencode overhead of conventional super-resolution pipelines while providing stable structural guidance for fine-grained detail generation.

## Results

On VBench-Long, Turbo2K (4B) achieves a Total Score of 82.78, ranking second only to HunyuanVideo (13B, 83.24) while being roughly 3× smaller and ~26× faster per denoising step (5.08 s vs 132.70 s at 49 frames @ 2K). Turbo2K surpasses CogVideoX-5B (81.61) by 1.17 points in Total Score and leads all baselines on Dynamic Degree (74.65) and Aesthetic Quality (61.78). In the VSR comparison (Table 3), Turbo2K outperforms all four video super-resolution baselines on every metric, including MUSIQ (52.45 vs 51.48 for RealBasicVSR), CLIPIQA (0.47 vs 0.44), and DOVER Aesthetic (13.70 vs 12.58 for STAR). Notably, Turbo2K is the only method that can generate 121-frame 2K videos without OOM, completing each step in 22.80 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Turbo2K (4B) trails only HunyuanVideo (13B) on VBench-Long Total Score (82.78 vs 83.24), a gap of just 0.46 points, while running ~26× faster per step (5.08 s vs 132.70 s) and using roughly one-third the parameters. Against CogVideoX-5B, Turbo2K gains +1.17 Total Score and is ~43× faster (5.08 s vs 220.12 s per step); versus CogVideoX-2B it gains +1.21 Total Score and is ~19× faster. Turbo2K also outperforms Vchitect/VEhancer (+0.54), CogVideoX-1.5 (+0.61), Mochi-1 (+2.65), LTX-Video (+2.78), OpenSora-1.2 (+2.78), and OpenSoraPlan-V1.1 (+4.78) on Total Score. In the VSR comparison (Table 3), Turbo2K beats all four baselines (RealBasicVSR, Upscale-a-Video, VEhancer, STAR) on every reported metric—MUSIQ, NIQE, CLIPIQA, DOVER Technical, DOVER Aesthetic, and Semantic consistency—indicating that its two-stage feature-guided synthesis produces more detail-rich and semantically coherent 2K outputs than patch-based or latent-conditioned super-resolution approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

Turbo2K builds directly on LTX-Video's high-compression VAE (32×32×8) as its student backbone, inheriting the 8× token reduction that makes 2K generation computationally feasible. It departs from standard video super-resolution pipelines (RealBasicVSR, Upscale-a-Video, VEhancer, STAR) that condition HR generation on decoded/re-encoded LR latents, instead proposing feature-level guidance from intermediate DiT blocks. The heterogeneous distillation strategy is novel in transferring knowledge across fundamentally different VAE latent spaces (HunyuanVideo's 8×8×4 to LTX-Video's 32×32×8), a setting not addressed by prior distillation work in video diffusion. The two-stage synthesis framework also distinguishes itself from tuning-free upscaling methods (ScaleCrafter, DeMoFusion) by explicitly training a dedicated HR model guided by LR semantic features rather than modifying denoising schedules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20× inference speedup for 2K video generation (5.08s vs ~100s per step) makes high-resolution video synthesis practically deployable, and the ability to generate 121-frame 2K videos where all baselines OOM is a concrete scalability win. The finding that DiTs exhibit structurally similar internal representations across different VAE spaces, enabling effective cross-architecture distillation, opens a path for transferring capabilities from large proprietary models to small efficient ones without requiring architectural alignment. Limitations include evaluation on a single benchmark (VBench-Long), a single teacher-student pair, and the 4B model still trailing the 13B teacher on semantic scores; open questions include whether the approach generalizes to 4K resolution, longer durations, or more diverse teacher-student pairs, and whether the two-stage framework can be extended to conditional generation tasks.
