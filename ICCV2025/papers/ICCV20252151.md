## Problem

Identity-preserved image generation with modern Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) like FLUX remains underexplored: existing DiT-based attempts (PuLID-FLUX, FLUX.1-dev IP-Adapters) suffer from insufficient identity similarity, poor text-image alignment, visible face copy-paste artifacts, and substantially degraded generation quality compared to the base model. Prior work was predominantly built on U-Net-based models (SDXL) whose limited generative capacity caps output quality, and no systematic framework existed for robustly leveraging rectified-flow DiTs for this task.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference face image (encoded by a frozen face identity encoder into 8 projected tokens), a free-form text prompt (encoded by frozen T5-XXL and CLIP), and an optional five-facial-keypoint control image for subject positioning (a pure black image is used when no spatial control is needed). It produces a high-resolution identity-preserved image in the latent space of FLUX's pretrained autoencoder, generated via multi-step denoising with logit-normal sampling under the rectified-flow formulation.

## Method

The core contribution is InfuseNet, a ControlNet-style branch with N DiT blocks (where the frozen FLUX base model has M = N·i blocks, i=4) that injects identity features purely via residual connections between DiT blocks, deliberately avoiding IP-Adapter's modification of attention layers to prevent text-identity entanglement and preserve the base model's generative capability. Identity embeddings are projected and fed into InfuseNet through its own attention layers, while text conditioning enters the base model through its attention layers, cleanly separating the two signal paths. Training uses a multi-stage strategy: Stage 1 pretrains on 43M real single-person-single-sample (SPSS) portraits with Conditional Flow Matching loss, then Stage 2 performs supervised fine-tuning on 2M synthetic single-person-multiple-sample (SPMS) pairs generated by the Stage-1 model augmented with aesthetic LoRAs, enhancement modules, and face-swap tools, which improves text-image alignment, quality, and aesthetics while alleviating face copy-paste. Only InfuseNet and the projection network are trainable; the FLUX backbone, text encoders, and face encoder remain frozen throughout.

## Results

InfU achieves the best performance across all three evaluation metrics: ID Loss of 0.209 (lower is better), CLIPScore of 0.318, and PickScore of 0.221. The CLIPScore improvement is particularly notable, reducing the gap to the FLUX.1-dev upper bound (0.334) by 66.7% compared to PuLID-FLUX. In a user study with 16 participants evaluating 70 sample sets, InfU was preferred 72.8% of the time over PuLID-FLUX (27.2%) in overall performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfU outperforms both baselines on all three metrics. Against PuLID-FLUX (the most competitive baseline), InfU reduces ID Loss from 0.225 to 0.209 (7.1% relative improvement), increases CLIPScore from 0.286 to 0.318 (11.2% relative improvement), and raises PickScore from 0.212 to 0.221 (4.2% relative improvement). Against FLUX.1-dev IP-Adapter, the gains are much larger: ID Loss drops from 0.772 to 0.209 (73% reduction), CLIPScore rises from 0.243 to 0.318 (31% increase), and PickScore increases from 0.204 to 0.221 (8.3% increase). The paper notes that FLUX.1-dev IPA is not tailored for human faces, explaining its substantially worse identity similarity. Qualitatively, PuLID-FLUX exhibits poor text-image alignment, degraded image quality (e.g., bad hands), and a visible face copy-paste issue, while InfU avoids these artifacts thanks to its residual-based identity injection that preserves the base model's generative capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as one of the first robust frameworks for identity-preserved image generation built on Diffusion Transformers (specifically FLUX's MMDiT architecture), departing from the U-Net-based lineage of IP-Adapter, InstantID, PuLID, and PhotoMaker. Its key architectural departure is replacing the standard IP-Adapter mechanism (which injects identity by modifying attention layers) with InfuseNet, a ControlNet generalization that injects identity features purely through residual connections between DiT blocks, thereby disentangling text and identity conditioning. The multi-stage training strategy with synthetic SPMS data generation extends the pretraining-then-finetuning paradigm from LLMs into the diffusion model training pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As one of the earliest open-source DiT-based identity preservation frameworks with a plug-and-play design, InfU provides a practical foundation for the community to build on FLUX for personalized generation, and its compatibility with LoRAs, ControlNets, and OminiControl broadens its utility. The ablation showing that IP-Adapter actively degrades text-image alignment and aesthetics when used for identity injection (not just stylization) challenges a widely adopted assumption in the field. Open questions include whether the residual-injection paradigm generalizes to other conditioning modalities beyond faces, how the approach scales to multi-person or non-human subjects, and whether the SPMS synthetic data pipeline can be made less computationally expensive.
