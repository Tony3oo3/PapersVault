## Problem

Existing training-free text-driven image translation methods rely on coarse feature-level guidance (convolution and attention features) that struggles to faithfully preserve style (visual patterns) and structure (edges) of the source image. Prior frequency-based approaches like FBSDiff substitute high-frequency components of diffusion latents, but this is suboptimal because the denoising process inherently removes high-frequency noise, creating a fundamental contradiction that leads to noised and distorted generations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251672/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image (resized to 512×512) and a target text prompt as input. The source image is first encoded by a VAE and inverted through the frozen Stable Diffusion v2.1 denoising network to obtain a trajectory of intermediate latents. The output is a single translated image decoded from the final sampled latent, where either the global style (low-frequency) or the structural layout and edges (high-frequency) of the source is preserved while the content follows the target prompt.

## Method

FGD tailors two distinct frequency-domain guidance mechanisms to a frozen T2I diffusion model. For style-guided translation, low-frequency components of the sampling latent are replaced with those from the inversion trajectory at each timestep, followed by AdaIN normalization to correct color distribution shift caused by the composited spectrum. For structure-guided translation, two complementary modules operate: (1) high-frequency alignment adjusts the predicted noise via gradient of a guidance function that penalizes the L2 difference in grayscale high-frequency image regions (identified by a binary mask from FFT high-pass filtering) between the predicted clean data and the source; and (2) high-frequency injection composites the spectrum of decoder convolution features by replacing only the high-frequency band of the sampling features with those from inversion, preserving layout without interfering with the denoising process. This feature-level, high-frequency-only injection contrasts with prior methods that replace both low- and high-frequency components of all features.

## Results

On style-guided translation (Aesthetics), FGD achieves CSD 0.649, CLIP 0.260, and HPS 27.34, leading in CLIP and HPS while P2P+NT leads in CSD (0.669). On structure-guided translation (Aesthetics), FGD achieves DINO 0.035, Edge 0.931, CLIP 0.271, and HPS 27.56, leading in CLIP and HPS while LEDITS++ leads in DINO (0.024) and Edge (0.947). On PIE-Bench, FGD achieves the best CLIP score (0.273 style / 0.303 structure) and competitive HPS (27.54 / 27.87) across both tasks. The method achieves the best balance between source preservation and target-prompt adherence without sacrificing image quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FGD outperforms the closest frequency-based baseline FBSDiff [12] on all metrics in both tasks: on structure-guided translation (Aesthetics), FGD improves DINO from 0.058 to 0.035, Edge from 0.908 to 0.931, CLIP from 0.253 to 0.271, and HPS from 26.58 to 27.56. Against attention/feature-based methods, FGD beats FreeControl [30] on DINO (0.035 vs 0.045) and Edge (0.931 vs 0.913) on Aesthetics, and beats PnP [43] on DINO (0.035 vs 0.044) and HPS (27.56 vs 26.99). However, FGD does not match LEDITS++ [3] or P2P+NT [16,31] on pure structure preservation (DINO/Edge), as these editing methods sacrifice prompt adherence (CLIP 0.227/0.252 vs FGD's 0.271). On style-guided translation, FGD leads in CLIP (0.260) and HPS (27.34) but trails P2P+NT in CSD (0.649 vs 0.669), reflecting a trade-off between style fidelity and prompt following. The training-based FCDDiff [13] is outperformed by FGD on CLIP (0.271 vs 0.231) and HPS (27.56 vs 26.36) for structure-guided translation, confirming that the training-free frequency guidance is more effective than the ControlNet-based frequency injection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the frequency-domain approach to diffusion-based image translation initiated by FBSDiff, which substitutes low- or high-frequency components of diffusion latents between sampling and inversion. FGD departs from FBSDiff by recognizing that high-frequency latent substitution is fundamentally at odds with the denoising process, and instead replaces it with a frequency-domain guidance function (high-frequency alignment) plus selective injection of high-frequency convolution features (adapted from PnP's full feature replacement). The low-frequency guidance retains FBSDiff's substitution mechanism but adds AdaIN-based color normalization to correct feature-statistics drift. Conceptually, the paper repositions guidance from the deep feature space (as in FreeControl, PnP, Ctrl-X) to the frequency domain, arguing that low- and high-frequency bands naturally encode style and structure respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free frequency-domain formulation offers a more interpretable and modular way to separate style preservation from structure preservation in text-driven translation, with each component (substitution, alignment, injection) having a clear physical meaning. The finding that high-frequency latent substitution is suboptimal due to its contradiction with denoising provides a useful diagnostic for the broader class of latent-manipulation methods. Limitations include reliance on empirical frequency thresholds, evaluation only at 512×512 with a single backbone (SDv2.1), and the fact that the frequency decomposition of style vs. structure, while intuitive, is not formally grounded. Open questions include whether this frequency-domain guidance generalizes to video diffusion, 3D generation, or architectures without a VAE latent space.
