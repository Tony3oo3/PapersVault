## Problem

Reconstruction-based anomaly detection models trained per-class (one-for-one) degrade significantly when naively extended to a single multi-class model (one-for-all), due to inter-class confusion where the model incorrectly reconstructs samples from one class as another. Existing one-for-all methods either adopt complex architectures or disregard the readily available object category information, leaving a performance gap relative to one-for-one baselines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251588/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images standardized to 256×256 pixels from industrial and multi-view datasets (MVTec, VISA, BTAD, Real-IAD, MANTA, COCOAD, MVTec 3D) and produces both an image-level anomaly score and a pixel-level anomaly map. During inference, anomaly scores are computed via cosine similarity between multi-scale encoder features, followed by Gaussian smoothing (σ=4), following the RD protocol. The model is trained exclusively on normal samples across all C classes simultaneously, with each sample carrying its raw category label as a supervised signal.

## Method

CCL adds two contrastive losses to a standard reconstruction-based encoder-neck-decoder architecture (e.g., RD), requiring only a lightweight 4-conv-block projector after the encoder. Local CL operates on multiscale spatial features: for each anchor position, it searches a k×k window in same-class samples for the most similar feature vector as a positive pair, applying a supervised contrastive loss to compact class-specific spatial patterns. Global CL operates on the neck's compressed image-level features, treating all same-class samples (not just augmented versions) as positives to tighten intra-class boundaries and separate inter-class representations. The total loss combines the original knowledge-distillation reconstruction loss with weighted local and global contrastive terms (λ₁·L_lcl + λ₂·L_gcl), and each training batch is constructed to include samples from different classes to preserve discriminative signal. This simple modification consistently recovers one-for-one-level performance under one-for-all training across multiple backbones (RD, DRAEM, DMAD, DeSTSeg) and even 3D models, with pseudo-class labels from K-means clustering achieving comparable results.

## Results

On the domain-in-all setting (Table 1), CCL(+RD) achieves the best I-AUROC/P-AUROC/PRO across nearly all five datasets, e.g., 99.3/98.2/93.6 on MVTec AD (C=15) and 95.9/98.5/92.6 on VISA (C=12). In the all-in-all setting combining four datasets (C=60), CCL reaches 90.6/97.9/90.6, outperforming the strongest baseline MambaAD (89.2/97.7/90.3). On the larger COCOAD benchmark (C=81), CCL attains 65.2/71.5 I-/P-AUROC and 43.5 PRO, surpassing MambaAD (63.9/69.3, 40.5) and the original RD backbone (57.6/66.5, 39.8) by substantial margins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCL(+RD) outperforms all six one-for-all baselines (UniAD, CRAD, OneNIP, DiAD, RLR, MambaAD) on the all-in-all setting (C=60), achieving 90.6/97.9/90.6 vs. MambaAD's 89.2/97.7/90.3 and CRAD's 89.5/97.7/86.5. On individual datasets, CCL leads on MVTec AD (99.3 I-AUROC, tying CRAD but surpassing it by 0.4 in P-AUROC and 1.9 in PRO), VISA (95.9 vs. MambaAD's 94.3 I-AUROC), BTAD (95.7 vs. MambaAD's 95.0), Real-IAD (87.6 vs. MambaAD's 86.3), and MANTA (84.3 vs. RLR's 83.1). On COCOAD (C=81), CCL improves over the raw RD backbone by +7.6 I-AUROC and +3.7 PRO, and beats MambaAD by +1.3 I-AUROC and +3.0 PRO. The gains are consistent across all metrics (I-AUROC, P-AUROC, PRO) and scale with the number of classes, from C=3 (BTAD) to C=81 (COCOAD), indicating that the class-aware contrastive losses effectively mitigate inter-class confusion as category diversity increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends one-for-one reconstruction-based anomaly detection methods (primarily RD) to the one-for-all multi-class setting by identifying inter-class confusion as the root cause of performance degradation and addressing it with class-aware contrastive learning. It departs from prior one-for-all approaches (UniAD, CRAD, MambaAD, etc.) that either employ complex Transformer/Mamba architectures or disregard raw object category information, instead showing that a lightweight projector plus local and global supervised contrastive losses suffice. The method draws on supervised contrastive learning (SupCon) for pair construction and on the observation that same-class normal samples naturally form positive pairs, a property previously underexploited in multi-class AD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for industrial deployment: a minimal modification (projector + two losses, ~2-3 GFLOPs overhead) to existing one-for-one models recovers near-one-for-one performance in multi-class settings, avoiding the need for complex architectures. The finding that pseudo-class labels from K-means clustering achieve comparable performance to raw class labels suggests class structure is a broadly available 'free lunch' even when true labels are unavailable. Open questions include why CCL yields only marginal gains for synthesis-based and embedding-based methods (which already contain discriminative classifiers), and the acknowledged weakness in generalizing to truly unseen data.
