## Problem

Conventional Retinex-based multi-exposure image fusion decomposes images into illumination and reflectance via pixel-wise multiplication, but this formulation inadequately models the glare effect induced by overexposure, which corrupts the estimated reflectance and causes color shifts and detail loss. Existing unsupervised MEF methods still suffer from image degradation under extreme exposures because they lack an explicit mechanism to separate glare from true scene reflectance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252355/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-exposure image sequence and processes it as an underexposed–overexposed pair {I_u, I_o} plus an auxiliary image I_aux randomly sampled from the full sequence during training, which strengthens holistic scene understanding. At inference, only the pair is needed; the output is a single fused RGB image whose global exposure can be adjusted via a scalar parameter k ∈ [0,1] in a monotonic, symmetric fusion function, allowing the user to brighten (k > 0.5) or darken (k < 0.5) the result while preserving contrast.

## Method

The core contribution extends the classic Retinex model to I = L·(R̂ + G), where G ≥ 0 explicitly captures glare from overexposure. Three modules handle the decomposition: a 5-layer convolutional network with Sigmoid estimates single-channel illumination L; a two-layer Restormer block extracts the shared three-channel reflectance R̂ from the image pair; and a glare estimation module (Restormer with illumination-modulated features) predicts G to reconstruct the input. Training is fully unsupervised, driven by a bidirectional constraint on R̂: a suppression loss (max(0, L·R̂ − I)) caps R̂ from above, while a consistency loss (‖R̂ − R‖₁) pushes it upward, synergistically converging R̂ to the true glare-free reflectance. At inference, a parameterized fusion function f(x;k) = kx/(kx + (1−k)(1−x)) applied to the averaged illumination enables controllable, contrast-preserving exposure adjustment.

## Results

On the MEFB dataset, Retinex-MEF achieves the best scores across all six metrics: Qcb 0.465, NMI 0.855, Qncie 0.823, SSIM 0.923, PSNR 11.856, and CC 0.910. On the SICE dataset, it similarly leads on every metric with Qcb 0.448, NMI 0.671, Qncie 0.814, SSIM 0.893, PSNR 9.508, and CC 0.841. The method is trained unsupervised on 300 SICE sequences and evaluated on 100 SICE test pairs plus 30 MEFB pairs, requiring no ground-truth fused images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Retinex-MEF outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across both datasets. On MEFB, the closest competitor is MEFLUT [18] on Qcb (0.460 vs. 0.465) and Qncie (tied at 0.823), while CRMEF [40] is nearest on SSIM (0.921 vs. 0.923) and DDMEF [53] on PSNR (11.843 vs. 11.856). On SICE, the margins are tighter: MEFLUT [18] leads on Qncie (0.815 vs. 0.814) by a negligible 0.001, and CRMEF [40] is close on SSIM (0.890 vs. 0.893). The largest gaps appear in NMI, where Retinex-MEF leads by 0.020 over FILM [76] on MEFB (0.855 vs. 0.835) and by 0.003 over MEFLUT [18] on SICE (0.671 vs. 0.668). Notably, CUNet [9] and DeFusion [33] lag substantially on NMI (0.467 and 0.777 on MEFB; 0.402 and 0.573 on SICE), indicating weaker information preservation, while FILM [76] suffers the lowest PSNR on SICE (7.999), reflecting greater pixel-level distortion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Retinex-based image decomposition framework (I = L·R) to multi-exposure fusion by introducing an explicit glare term, formulating I = L·(R̂ + G). It builds most directly on PairLIE [12], which pioneered unsupervised shared-reflectance learning from paired low-light images, but adapts that concept to the MEF setting where overexposure—not just underexposure—corrupts reflectance estimation. The bidirectional loss (suppression + consistency) and the parameterized exposure fusion function f(x;k) are novel training and inference mechanisms not present in prior Retinex-MEF or unsupervised MEF methods, which typically rely on pixel-level similarity constraints or fixed exposure targets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By explicitly modeling glare as an additive distortion to reflectance, the paper addresses a concrete failure mode of prior Retinex-based MEF that produces color shifts and detail loss in overexposed regions, without requiring ground-truth supervision. The controllable exposure function offers a practical advantage over fixed-target fusion, letting users dial brightness while preserving contrast. Limitations include evaluation on only two relatively small datasets (SICE, MEFB), a glare model that is a single additive term (G ≥ 0) rather than a spatially varying or physically grounded model, and the method's reliance on a Restormer backbone whose computational cost may limit real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the glare formulation generalizes to dynamic scenes or to more than a handful of exposure levels without the auxiliary-image sampling strategy.
