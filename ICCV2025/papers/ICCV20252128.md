## Problem

Diffusion-based video super-resolution methods achieve superior detail generation over traditional approaches but are computationally prohibitive, requiring tens of minutes to upscale a mere 2-second 1080p video. This inefficiency stems from two compounding factors: high-resolution inputs produce extremely long token sequences, and diffusion sampling requires many iterative denoising steps. The paper asks how to make diffusion-based VSR practical by reducing both the sequence length and the number of sampling steps without sacrificing perceptual quality.

## Input / Output

The model takes a low-resolution video or image (degraded via RealESRGAN) along with an auto-generated text caption (produced by Res-Captioner from the LR input) and outputs a super-resolved video or image. Training uses fixed 1024×1024, 49-frame clips, but inference supports arbitrary resolutions and lengths through tile-wise multi-diffusion (spatial) and segment fusion (temporal), enabling outputs up to 4K (3840×2160) for images. The same architecture handles both modalities by treating a single image as a one-frame video.

## Method

TURBOVSR builds on LTX-DiT (a video generation DiT) with three efficiency-oriented modifications. First, it replaces the standard SD-VAE with LTX-VAE, which compresses 32× spatially and 8× temporally (latent dim 128), reducing token sequences by 32×; this is viable for VSR because the degraded input provides strong conditioning, so the autoencoder need only preserve enough information for detail synthesis. Second, it introduces factorized conditioning: the model is multi-task trained to (a) super-resolve the first frame as a standalone image SR task, and (b) super-resolve the full video conditioned on the SR first frame plus LR remaining frames, which decomposes the problem and accelerates convergence by letting the video branch focus on temporal correspondence rather than full detail generation. Third, it converts the flow-matching objective to a non-uniform shortcut model, where timestep and step-size sampling are shifted toward high-noise regions (following SD3's shift principle) and multiple base step sizes are used with nearest-neighbor selection, enabling 4-step inference (vs. 10) with negligible quality loss. At inference, a tile-wise multi-diffusion strategy with Gaussian-weighted fusion and a fixed optimal initial noise handles arbitrary spatial resolutions, while a segment-fusion approach (discarding the first-frame latent during overlap merging) handles arbitrary video lengths.

## Results

On the SPMCS benchmark, TURBOVSR (10-step) achieves the best DOVER score of 79.29 and competitive NIQE of 4.120, while the 4-step variant maintains DOVER 79.15 at only 140 ms per 1080p frame — roughly 100× faster than other diffusion-based VSR methods (e.g., Upscale-A-Video at 24,032 ms, MGLDVSR at 37,822 ms). On UDM10, TURBOVSR (10-step) leads in DOVER (80.59) and NIQE (3.991). On the DRealSR image SR benchmark, TURBOVSR (4-step) achieves PSNR 24.51, SSIM 0.704, and NIQE 6.042, comparable to StableSR (PSNR 24.69, NIQE 6.282).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against non-diffusion baselines RealBasic-VSR and RealVIFormer, TURBOVSR trades some fidelity (PSNR/SSIM) for substantially better perceptual quality (DOVER, NIQE, MUSIQ), while matching their ~100 ms/frame latency with the 4-step variant (140 ms). Among diffusion-based methods, TURBOVSR outperforms Upscale-A-Video on DOVER (79.29 vs 75.82 on SPMCS) and NIQE (4.120 vs 3.814), and beats MGLDVSR on DOVER (79.29 vs 67.75) and NIQE (4.120 vs 4.204) on SPMCS, while being 270× faster. STAR, despite being the most recent diffusion VSR method, is notably weaker in perceptual quality (DOVER 48.75, NIQE 6.114 on SPMCS) and 86× slower. VEnhancer is outperformed by TURBOVSR on all three SPMCS perceptual metrics. On the DRealSR image SR benchmark, TURBOVSR (4-step) is competitive with StableSR (PSNR 24.51 vs 24.69, NIQE 6.042 vs 6.282) and ResShift (PSNR 24.51 vs 23.94, NIQE 6.042 vs 7.371), confirming the high-compression autoencoder generalizes to image SR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LTX-Video ecosystem (LTX-VAE + LTX-DiT) as its base architecture, adopting the ControlNeXt framework for condition injection and the flow-matching training paradigm. It departs from prior diffusion-based VSR methods (Upscale-A-Video, STAR, MGLDVSR) primarily in its efficiency strategy: rather than using standard 16× spatial-compression VAEs, it argues that the 32×32×8 LTX-VAE is well-suited for VSR because the degraded input provides strong conditional signal, making the autoencoder's generative quality loss less critical. The non-uniform shortcut model extends the vanilla shortcut framework (Frans et al.) with resolution-aware timestep and step-size sampling, and the factorized conditioning decomposes VSR into image-SR-then-video-SR to bridge the token-length gap between T2V pretraining and VSR fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100× speedup (140 ms/frame vs. 10–38 s/frame for prior diffusion VSR) is a transformative practical improvement that makes diffusion-based VSR deployable in near-real-time settings, potentially reshaping how the field approaches the efficiency-quality tradeoff. The insight that high-compression autoencoders—typically detrimental for open-ended generation—are well-matched to conditional restoration tasks could generalize to other image/video enhancement problems. Limitations include reliance on in-house training data (5M images, 0.5M videos), the absence of 4K video SR (deferred to future work), and the fact that reference metrics (PSNR/SSIM) remain lower than non-diffusion baselines, reflecting the inherent generative nature of the approach.
