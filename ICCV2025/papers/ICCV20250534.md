## Problem

Existing data-driven HOI motion synthesis methods struggle with the compositional structure of complex multi-object manipulation tasks, where spatial coordination (managing multiple objects across body parts) and temporal sequencing (chaining sub-tasks) must be jointly reasoned about. The exponential growth of possible interaction scenarios makes collecting comprehensive training data infeasible, and end-to-end models fail to generalize to unseen task compositions. The paper asks how to represent prior HOI knowledge as reusable, composable primitives that enable zero-shot transfer to novel multi-object tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form natural language task description (e.g., 'pick up two boxes and open the door'), an initial 3D object layout specifying mesh poses for all objects, and an initial SMPLX human pose. The LLM translates the text into a PDDL problem instance, and the object meshes provide vertex-level geometry for contact point sampling. The output is a temporally coherent sequence of SMPLX human poses paired with per-frame object trajectories, structured as K segments each bridging consecutive key poses, with a post-optimization pass enforcing contact constraints, collision avoidance, and temporal smoothness.

## Method

PrimHOI introduces four interaction primitives (grasp, clamp, support, dual-support) defined by relative geometry between body parts and object surfaces, serving as reusable building blocks for complex manipulation. The pipeline operates in three stages: (1) high-level planning via PDDL-HOI, an extension of PDDLStream where interaction primitives become predicates and actions encode state transitions, with an LLM translating task descriptions into PDDL problems and PDDLStream's streams mechanism sampling diverse body-part assignments; (2) key pose generation, where a diffusion-based contact model samples object contact points per primitive, object poses are drawn from a Mixture-of-Gaussians placement prior, and human poses are optimized with DPoser regularization; (3) intermediate motion generation, where A* planning with SDF collision checking produces object trajectories, and a retrained spatial-guided diffusion model (LocalControl, adapted from OmniControl) generates human motion conditioned on inferred contact point sequences. A final post-optimization step minimizes a six-term objective (contact closeness, normal alignment, collision, self-penetration, temporal smoothness, pose prior) to refine the generated motion.

## Results

On the low-level motion generation benchmark (Table 2), LocalControl achieves the highest Success score (7.3/10) and Naturality (8.3/10) on Task 1, outperforming ProgMoGen (6.7, 7.2) and IK (6.2, 6.5), while also reducing constraint error from 0.170 to 0.079. On the most complex Task 4 (two-step motions), LocalControl×2 reaches Success 7.4 and C.Err. 0.129 versus ProgMoGen's 5.1 and 0.241. For distribution-based metrics (Table 3), LocalControl achieves FID 0.151 on dual-hand contact guidance, substantially below OmniControl's 0.464 on the equivalent dual-wrist task. At the planning level (Table 1), GPT-4o + PDDL-HOI achieves 10/10 success rate on the complex Task 3, compared to 5.6/10 for GPT-4o alone and 1.8/10 for GPT-4o + Primitives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ProgMoGen [30], LocalControl wins on Success, Constraint Error, and Naturality across all four low-level tasks, but loses on Maximum Joint Acceleration (e.g., 0.147 vs. 0.020 on Task 1), indicating ProgMoGen produces smoother but less accurate and less natural motions. The gap is most pronounced on Task 2 (start/end positioning without trajectory), where ProgMoGen without trajectory guidance drops to Success 2.6 and Naturality 4.4, while LocalControl with trajectory planning maintains 7.3 and 8.3. Against the original OmniControl [55] backbone, LocalControl (retrained on 'No-Walk' HumanML3D with contact-point guidance) reduces FID from 0.464 to 0.151 on dual-hand guidance, a 3.4× improvement, demonstrating that focusing on local manipulation operations yields better distributional fidelity than global joint-level control. At the planning level, GPT-4o + PDDL-HOI dominates GPT-4o alone on Task 3 (10/10 vs. 5.6/10 success) and GPT-4o + Primitives (10/10 vs. 1.8/10), showing that formal symbolic constraints are critical for complex multi-object planning where informal primitive descriptions lead to frequent rule violations.

## Relation to Prior Work

PrimHOI sits at the intersection of symbolic planning and data-driven motion synthesis, adapting PDDLStream's optimistic adaptive planning into a domain-specific language (PDDL-HOI) for HOI task decomposition, while leveraging OmniControl's spatial-guided diffusion (retrained as LocalControl) and OMOMO's contact prediction model for low-level generation. It departs from prior compositional motion work (SINC, CoMo, TEACH, Multi-Act) by addressing both spatial and temporal composition simultaneously for multi-object interactions, and from prior HOI methods (InterDreamer, UniUSI) by replacing whole-body representations with local interaction primitives defined by relative geometry. The key conceptual departure is treating HOI as a planning problem over a small vocabulary of reusable contact patterns rather than an end-to-end generation task requiring task-specific training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a small set of four interaction primitives (support, grasp, clamp, dual support) combined with symbolic planning can achieve zero-shot transfer to unseen multi-object HOI tasks, offering a path toward systematic generalization that data-driven methods struggle with as the interaction space grows combinatorially. However, the evaluation relies on small sample sizes (5–10 trials per task) and limited baselines (GPT-4o prompting, ProgMoGen, IK), leaving open questions about robustness at scale. The authors acknowledge that the modular decomposition can introduce failures when interdependent variables are separated, and that individual submodules (contact prediction, pose optimization, motion generation) each carry quality limitations that compound over long sequences. Future work on handling interdependent variables more gracefully and expanding the primitive vocabulary would strengthen the approach.
