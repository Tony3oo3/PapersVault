## Problem

Existing dataset ownership verification (DOV) methods are tailored to supervised models or contrastive pre-trained models and cannot be directly applied to the increasingly prevalent masked models (MAE, BEiT, iBOT, etc.). Because masked modeling and contrastive learning use fundamentally different proxy tasks, the representations learned by masked models are harder to distinguish, rendering contrastive-gap-based DOV techniques ineffective for this model family.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250076/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The defender operates in a black-box Encoder-as-a-Service (EaaS) setting, querying only the suspicious model's API for feature vectors without knowledge of its architecture or loss function. The defender supplies their public dataset (split into a 20,000-sample training subset and a validation subset), a private dataset never publicly released, and applies random masking at 75% (images) or 20% (text) to generate masked inputs. The output is a p-value from a one-tailed paired t-test on relative embedding reconstruction difficulties, yielding a binary illegal/legal verdict at the 0.05 significance level.

## Method

DOV4MM exploits the observation that in the embedding space of a pre-trained masked model, seen samples from pre-training exhibit lower reconstruction difficulty for masked information than unseen samples. The defender trains a small Transformer decoder (512-dim, 8 layers, 16 heads) on the training subset using the suspicious model's embeddings to reconstruct masked positions, then computes per-sample reconstruction difficulty as the L2 error at masked positions normalized by the number of masked elements. The key metric is the relative embedding reconstruction difficulty ΔR, defined as the difference between reconstruction difficulty on a validation set and the decoder's training set, which amplifies the subtle seen-vs-unseen gap. A one-tailed paired t-test compares ΔR between the validation set and a private set: if the p-value falls below 0.05, the null hypothesis is rejected, indicating the suspicious model was pre-trained on the defender's public dataset.

## Results

On ImageNet-50 and ImageNet-100, DOV4MM achieves perfect sensitivity (1.00), specificity (1.00), and AUROC (1.00), correctly classifying all illegal and legal scenarios. On the full ImageNet-1K with ten masked image modeling methods, DOV4MM yields p-values as low as 10⁻⁶ for illegal models (e.g., MAE-ViT-L/16) while maintaining p-values above 0.72 for all legal models, using only 3% of the training data. For masked language modeling on WikiText-103 subsets, DOV4MM similarly rejects the null hypothesis for illegal models and fails to reject it for legal ones across BERT, RoBERTa, ALBERT, and XLM-R.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DOV4MM decisively outperforms all three baselines on both ImageNet-50 and ImageNet-100, achieving a perfect AUROC of 1.00 versus 0.50 for DI4SSL and CTRL and 0.36–0.39 for PartCrop. DI4SSL (dataset inference for self-supervised models) achieves 0.00 sensitivity, meaning it never correctly identifies an illegal model, while CTRL (backdoor watermarking) achieves 0.00 specificity, flagging every legal model as illegal due to the fixed-pattern trigger producing spurious similarity. PartCrop (membership inference via part-aware capability) performs worse than random (AUROC < 0.5), likely because it relies on high-dimensional representations containing redundant information. The paper attributes DOV4MM's advantage to extracting the most discriminative signal—relative embedding reconstruction difficulty—rather than validating raw high-dimensional embeddings or relying on fragile watermark patterns.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dataset ownership verification (DOV) line of work, which has previously targeted supervised models via backdoor watermarking and contrastive pre-trained models via contrastive relationship gaps in embedding space. The authors identify a key gap: masked modeling's proxy task produces representations that are harder to distinguish than those from contrastive learning, rendering the contrastive-gap approach [52] ineffective. DOV4MM carves new ground by introducing 'relative embedding reconstruction difficulty'—a metric that exploits the reconstruction behavior inherent to masked models—rather than repurposing contrastive or backdoor-based signals. It also draws conceptual inspiration from dataset inference [14] and membership inference [59] for self-supervised encoders, but avoids their need to process entire datasets or rely on full high-dimensional representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Masked models (MAE, BEiT, iBOT, etc.) are now the dominant self-supervised paradigm in both vision and NLP, yet no prior DOV method could protect the unlabeled datasets they are pre-trained on; this paper fills that gap with a black-box, watermark-free approach that requires only 3% of the dataset. The method's generality across 10 MIM methods, 4 architectures, and both image and text modalities makes it broadly applicable. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other self-supervised paradigms (e.g., generative models), whether it remains robust against more sophisticated obfuscation by suspects, and how it scales to even larger or multimodal datasets.
