## Problem

GUI agents built on vision-language models frequently misground actions because high-resolution screenshots contain many irrelevant elements (menu bars, ads, extraneous buttons) that overwhelm single-inference visual grounding. Existing test-time scaling methods for AI agents rely on textual reasoning or reflection but do not exploit the visual modality to dynamically narrow attention, leaving no feedback loop to correct early grounding errors before they compound.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251934/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a high-resolution GUI screenshot (often >3k×2k pixels) and a natural-language task instruction, optionally augmented by environment feedback (e.g., whether a click caused a page change). It produces a single refined action—typically a click or scroll with (x, y) coordinates—by first generating multiple candidate actions from zoomed sub-regions and then aggregating them. The image-as-map annotation layer (numbered pink-star landmarks) is overlaid only on the RegionFocus sub-pipeline; the base agent always receives unaltered screenshots.

## Method

RegionFocus is an inference-time plug-in that activates when the agent's predicted action is flagged as erroneous (via environment feedback on interactive pages or VLM self-judgment on static screenshots). Upon triggering, the VLM proposes a focal point near the intended target; fixed-ratio bounding boxes (e.g., 0.5×0.5 of the image) are then centered on that point, and the agent independently predicts an action for each cropped, upscaled sub-region. An image-as-map mechanism annotates the screenshot with numbered pink-star landmarks to record previously examined focal points (temporal history) and to visually mark candidate action coordinates (spatial disambiguation), which the model uses during aggregation to select one final action. The key design choice is deriving bounding boxes from VLM-predicted focal points rather than asking the model to predict boxes directly, since VLMs localize points reliably but produce inaccurate box coordinates. Empirically, the image-as-map representation consistently outperforms text-based coordinate lists, particularly for distinguishing elements separated by only a few pixels.

## Results

On ScreenSpot-Pro, RegionFocus achieves a new state-of-the-art grounding accuracy of 61.6% when applied to Qwen2.5-VL-72B (up from 47.8%, a +28.9% absolute gain), and 50.2% on UI-TARS-72B (up from 38.1%, a +31.7% gain). Notably, the smaller UI-TARS-7B + RegionFocus (41.2%) surpasses the much larger UI-TARS-72B baseline (38.1%). On WebVoyager, RegionFocus yields 24%+ absolute improvements over both base models, with Qwen2.5-VL-72B + RegionFocus reaching approximately 53.5% task success rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RegionFocus is evaluated as a plug-in on two state-of-the-art open VLM agents: UI-TARS and Qwen2.5-VL. On ScreenSpot-Pro, UI-TARS-72B + RegionFocus (50.2%) outperforms the base UI-TARS-72B (38.1%) by 12.1 points, and Qwen2.5-VL-72B + RegionFocus (61.6%) surpasses the base Qwen2.5-VL-72B (47.8%) by 13.8 points, setting a new SOTA. The 7B variant with RegionFocus (41.2%) even exceeds the 72B UI-TARS baseline (38.1%), demonstrating that visual test-time scaling can compensate for model scale. Compared to other grounding methods listed in the table—SeeClick (1.1% avg), CogAgent-18B (7.7%), UGround-V1-7B (31.1%), and Claude Computer Use (17.1%)—RegionFocus-enhanced models dominate by wide margins. On WebVoyager, RegionFocus consistently improves task success across all 15 websites tested, with the largest gains on complex sites like ArXiv and Booking, though absolute numbers are affected by real-world constraints such as bot detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

RegionFocus positions itself as a visual test-time scaling framework that extends the LLM test-time scaling paradigm (self-consistency, tree-of-thoughts, reflection) into the visual/GUI domain, where prior agent-level test-time scaling relied on textual cues, intermediate action histories, or external information retrieval. It builds directly on two state-of-the-art VLM GUI agents—UI-TARS and Qwen2.5-VL—as plug-in hosts, requiring no retraining or architectural changes, and draws on visual attention and region-proposal ideas (SAM, recurrent visual attention) but repurposes them for iterative, error-triggered zooming during inference. The image-as-map mechanism departs from the standard practice of encoding agent history as textual coordinates, instead annotating the screenshot itself with visual landmarks to convey both temporal (previously visited) and spatial (candidate action) information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in design means any existing VLM-based GUI agent can adopt RegionFocus without modification, making the 28–31% grounding gains broadly applicable across the rapidly growing GUI-agent ecosystem. The finding that a simple fixed-ratio bounding box around a VLM-predicted focal point suffices (without SAM or learned segmentation) lowers the adoption barrier considerably, though the authors note that direct VLM-generated attention regions remain an open direction. Key limitations include reliance on VLM self-judgment for triggering in static-screenshot settings (which is imperfect), sensitivity to bot-blocking in live web evaluation, and the fact that the approach cannot help when the target element is simply not visible on the current page. Open questions include whether the image-as-map representation generalizes to longer-horizon tasks with many accumulated landmarks, and whether the generation-verification gap exploited here can be formalized more rigorously.
