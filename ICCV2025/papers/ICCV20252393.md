## Problem

Despite near gold-standard performance on individual saliency benchmarks, models suffer a ~40% information gain drop when transferred to unseen datasets, and training on more diverse data fails to close this gap (58% of the inter-dataset gap persists). The paper identifies that this residual generalization gap is driven by a small number of interpretable dataset-specific biases—center bias, multiscale structure, priority scaling, and fixation spread—rather than fundamental differences in visual content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252393/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image and internally rescales it into multiple resolutions defined both in absolute visual angle (pixels per degree of visual angle, capturing how large objects appeared to observers) and in relative total pixel size (capturing object size relative to the image frame). The output is a 2D fixation probability distribution over pixels, produced by decoding deep features into a spatial priority map and then postprocessing with a Gaussian blur, a priority scaling factor, and a center bias log-density map before applying a softmax.

## Method

The architecture is a multiscale encoder-decoder that extracts deep activations from CLIP (global scene understanding) and DINOv2 (precise local features) backbones at multiple resolutions, averages them across scales, and decodes via a compact 5-layer 1×1 convolution readout network (26,460 shared parameters). The key novelty is that fewer than 20 additional parameters are made dataset-specific and govern interpretable mechanisms: 10 multiscale averaging weights (disentangling absolute vs. relative visual size effects), a priority scaling factor, a Gaussian blur size in degrees of visual angle, a center bias spatial prior (2–5 parameters), and a center bias downweighting factor. In the generalization setting, these bias parameters are averaged across training datasets; in the adaptation setting, they are finetuned on as few as 50 target-dataset samples, closing ~76% of the generalization gap. The model is pretrained on SALICON and then trained jointly across five datasets (MIT1003, CAT2000, COCO-Freeview, DAEMONS, FIGRIM) using log-likelihood loss, with the readout network shared and only the bias parameters varying per dataset.

## Results

The model sets a new state-of-the-art on all three MIT/Tuebingen Saliency Benchmark datasets (MIT300, CAT2000, COCO-Freeview) in generalization, adaptation, and full-training settings, improving AUC by at least 1.1%–1.5% over the previous SOTA (DeepGaze IIE). With full joint training across five datasets, the model achieves average AUC of 0.891 and average information gain of 1.034 bits/fixation, surpassing the inter-observer consistency gold standard (0.885 AUC, 0.995 IG) on average. A key finding is that naively generalizing from one dataset to another incurs a ~40% information gain penalty (inter-dataset gap), and training on four additional datasets still leaves ~58% of that gap unresolved; adapting fewer than 20 interpretable dataset-bias parameters to just 50 target samples closes 76% of the remaining generalization gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The model outperforms all four prior deep saliency baselines (DeepGaze IIE, UNISAL, SalFBNet, EML-NET) on every dataset in both full-training and generalization settings. Against the previous SOTA DeepGaze IIE, the improvement is most pronounced on DAEMONS (0.848 vs. 0.822 AUC, +2.6%) and CAT2000 (0.891 vs. 0.878 AUC, +1.3%), with smaller but consistent gains on MIT1003 (+1.1%), COCO Freeview (+1.4%), and FIGRIM (+1.6%). UNISAL, which also uses domain-specific parameters, trails by 2.6% average AUC and shows particularly weak generalization (0.865 average vs. 0.874 for the paper's generalization setting). SalFBNet and EML-NET are further behind, with EML-NET lagging by over 8% average AUC, largely due to its weaker performance on CAT2000 (0.766). Notably, the model's full-training AUC (0.891 average) exceeds the inter-observer consistency gold standard (0.885 average), suggesting that standard KDE-based gold standard estimates may underestimate achievable performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DeepGaze architecture lineage (DeepGaze I/II/IIE) by replacing older CNN backbones with CLIP and DINOv2, introducing a multiscale feature extractor that disentangles absolute (px/deg) and relative (pixel) image scales, and—most distinctively—parameterizing fewer than 20 interpretable dataset-specific biases (center bias, multiscale weights, priority scaling, blur). It departs from prior multi-dataset approaches like UNISAL (which uses non-interpretable batch-norm and linear adapters) and DeepGaze IIE (which relies on ensembling) by showing that a small set of mechanistically meaningful parameters, adapted on as few as 50 samples, closes most of the generalization gap. The empirical framing—systematically quantifying the inter-dataset gap across five large fixation datasets and attributing it to specific biases—is novel relative to prior work that either trained per-dataset or used opaque domain-adaptation modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the perceived plateau on MIT300 masks a substantial cross-dataset generalization gap that more training data cannot fix, reframing the field's progress narrative. The practical takeaway—that 20 interpretable parameters adapted on ~50 samples recover 75% of the gap—offers a lightweight, data-efficient path to deploying saliency models in new experimental settings without full retraining. Open questions include whether additional biases (semantic preferences, subject-cohort effects) further explain residual gaps, whether the multiscale structure has genuine image-level (vs. dataset-level) dependencies, and how to establish a new gold standard given that the model now outperforms standard inter-observer consistency estimates on several datasets.
