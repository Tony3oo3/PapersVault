## Problem

Existing multi-modal large language models are trained exclusively on visible images and produce severe hallucinations when interpreting real-world infrared imagery, where thermal saliency and sparse semantics differ fundamentally from RGB. Prior attempts to bridge this gap (e.g., InfraredLLaVA) rely on synthetic infrared images generated via style transfer, introducing a modality gap and neglecting the authenticity of real infrared data. Additionally, no large-scale dataset of authentic infrared-text pairs existed to support training an infrared-specific MLLM.

## Input / Output

The model accepts real-world infrared images spanning near-infrared, short-wavelength infrared, and thermal infrared spectral bands, paired with free-form text prompts. Depending on the task, outputs range from free-text responses (scene description, recognition) to structured predictions including bounding boxes (grounding), pixel coordinates for all instances of a category (location), integer counts (aerial/pedestrian counting), binary spatial-relationship judgments, and re-identification indices from a 2×4 grid. The benchmark comprises 9 tasks with 37K+ test samples, evaluated via accuracy, mAP@0.5, and MAE as appropriate.

## Method

IRGPT is built on InternVL2-8B and trained in two stages: incremental pre-training of the vision encoder and projector on the IR-TD dataset (260K+ real infrared-text pairs from 63 public sources, annotated via LLM-adapted visible-image descriptions and rule-based generation), followed by supervised instruction fine-tuning with LoRA on the LLM. The key innovation is a bi-cross-modal curriculum transfer learning strategy that orders pre-training samples by difficulty along two axes: Lesson 1 (IR-VIS) computes a per-sample domain distance by projecting intra-domain feature distances onto the inter-domain direction estimated via MMD in a Gaussian-kernel RKHS, while Lesson 2 (IR-T) uses the loss variation rate of a pre-warmed CLIP to quantify infrared-text alignment difficulty. These dual scores are integrated into an ascending-stratified random sampling schedule, and the loss variation rate additionally serves as an adaptive per-sample weight that suppresses misaligned samples while amplifying hard but valid ones.

## Results

In zero-shot evaluation, IRGPT with bi-cross-modal curriculum (CL) achieves a positive sum (psum) of 328.65 and negative sum (nsum) of 93.87, outperforming the InternVL2-8B baseline by +76.35 psum and −31.56 nsum. After instruction fine-tuning, IRGPT(CL) reaches psum 485.79 and nsum 4.39, surpassing even the larger InternVL2-26B (psum 473.78, nsum 19.57) by +12.01 psum and −15.18 nsum. The curriculum strategy (CL) consistently outperforms both random ordering (+14.47 psum zero-shot) and anti-curriculum ordering, with the most dramatic gains on ReID (33.55 vs. 7.33 zero-shot) and Pedestrian Counting (0.82 vs. 6.64 fine-tuned MAE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IRGPT(CL) dominates all four baseline families in both zero-shot and fine-tuned settings. Against the direct baseline InternVL2-8B, IRGPT gains +76.35 psum and −31.56 nsum zero-shot, and +21.30 psum / −22.46 nsum after fine-tuning. Against the larger InternVL2-26B (3× parameters), IRGPT still wins by +12.01 psum and −15.18 nsum in fine-tuned evaluation, with particularly large margins on Pedestrian Counting (0.82 vs. 5.68 MAE) and Location (3.32 vs. 12.50 MAE). PandaGPT-7B, the prior infrared-specific MLLM, trails IRGPT by 50.65 psum zero-shot and 10.65 psum fine-tuned, while also showing substantially worse nsum (143.02 vs. 93.87 zero-shot). LLaVA1.5-7B and Qwen2-VL-7B, general-purpose VLMs, perform worst on infrared-specific tasks (e.g., LLaVA1.5 scores only 2.61 on ReID zero-shot), confirming the modality gap that motivates IRGPT's dedicated infrared training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM-for-infrared line initiated by PandaGPT and InfraredLLaVA, which relied on synthetic infrared images generated via style transfer from visible counterparts. IRGPT departs from this by constructing its 260K+ IR-TD dataset from authentic infrared imagery, using visible images only as a semantic bridge for LLM-generated captions rather than as the source of pixel data. The training strategy builds on curriculum learning and domain adaptation (MMD-based distance scoring) but introduces a bi-cross-modal formulation that jointly ranks samples by IR-VIS geometric dissimilarity and IR-Text CLIP alignment difficulty, a combination not present in prior multi-modal curriculum work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The open-source IR-TD dataset and 9-task benchmark fill a critical gap for the infrared imaging community, which previously lacked large-scale, authentic image-text resources. The finding that a curriculum-trained 8B model outperforms a 26B generalist (InternVL2-26B) after fine-tuning suggests that domain-specific data quality and training order can compensate for model scale in specialized modalities. Limitations include continued dependence on visible images for text generation (introducing potential semantic drift), the modest nsum improvement indicating the curriculum aids recognition more than precise localization/counting, and the curriculum being applied only during pre-training rather than fine-tuning. Open questions include whether the bi-cross-modal curriculum generalizes to other non-visible modalities (e.g., millimeter-wave, hyperspectral) and whether the difficulty scoring transfers across different infrared spectral bands.
