## Problem

Existing Data-Free Knowledge Transfer methods for Federated Class-Incremental Learning require training and communicating the entire model, incurring prohibitive training and transmission overhead, while prompt-pool-based alternatives store all task-specific prompts, creating privacy leakage risks and failing to truly inherit knowledge across tasks. The paper addresses both issues by learning task-agnostic and task-specific knowledge within a single set of prompts while preventing catastrophic forgetting without retaining separate per-task parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251566/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) as input, which are patch-embedded and processed through a frozen pre-trained ViT with learnable visual prompts (r tokens of dimension d) prepended at each of U transformer layers. The output is a class label predicted by a linear head on the final [CLS] token. In the federated setting, raw images never leave the client; only the small prompt vectors (0.1M parameters total, ~1% of the full 11.5M model) are transmitted to the server for FedAvg aggregation, and the server additionally generates synthetic data from prior tasks to supply clients for knowledge distillation.

## Method

TPPR builds on the VPT-Deep architecture with a frozen ViT backbone and learnable visual prompts at every layer, combined with LANDER's data-free knowledge transfer for generating synthetic data of previous tasks. The key anti-forgetting mechanism, Task-Aware Prompt Gradient Projection (TAPGP), decomposes the combined matrix of virtual-data patch embeddings and previous-task aggregated prompts via SVD, then projects deep-layer prompt gradients onto the null-space (near-zero singular vectors) of this matrix during backpropagation, ensuring new-task updates are orthogonal to old-task knowledge while shallow layers remain free to learn shared features. A second mechanism, Dual-Level Prompt Replay (DLPR), applies exponential moving averages at two granularities: intra-task replay smooths prompt aggregation across communication rounds to counter client data heterogeneity, and inter-task replay blends the current task's prompts with the previous task's server prompts (via weighted mixing) so that optimization is always conditioned on inherited knowledge. Together these two modules reduce trainable parameters to 0.1M while achieving >5% accuracy gains over the same-backbone SOTA across all evaluated settings.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, TPPR achieves 77.44% average accuracy with a forgetting measure of 11.61 under IID T=5, and 61.42% accuracy with F=19.19 under the most challenging NIID(0.5) T=10 setting. On Tiny-ImageNet, the method reaches 74.63% accuracy (F=13.04) under IID T=5 and 63.23% (F=19.33) under NIID(0.5) T=10. The method trains only 0.1M parameters (1% of the 11.5M required by the LANDER baseline), reducing communication overhead by 99% while improving accuracy by over 23% on CIFAR-100 and over 43% on Tiny-ImageNet compared to the strongest full-model SOTA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TPPR outperforms all 8 baselines across every setting on both CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet. Against the strongest full-model SOTA (DDDR, ECCV'24), TPPR gains +26.4% accuracy on CIFAR-100 IID T=5 (77.44 vs 51.04) and +21.2% on Tiny-ImageNet IID T=5 (74.63 vs 25.47). Compared to the same-backbone baseline LANDER+VPT-Deep, TPPR improves accuracy by +8.8% on CIFAR-100 IID T=5 (77.44 vs 68.61) and by +11.3% on Tiny-ImageNet IID T=5 (74.63 vs 63.31), while simultaneously reducing the forgetting measure from 27.74 to 11.61 (CIFAR-100) and from 31.7 to 13.04 (Tiny-ImageNet). The advantage is most pronounced under high data heterogeneity (NIID β=0.5) and more tasks (T=10), where LANDER+VPT-Deep suffers severe forgetting (F=33.93 on CIFAR-100) while TPPR maintains F=13.11. Older baselines such as TARGET, FedLwF, and FedEWC are substantially outperformed, with gaps exceeding 40% in accuracy on CIFAR-100.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) data-free knowledge transfer for federated class-incremental learning (LANDER, TARGET, DDDR), which it inherits as the DFKT backbone; (2) prompt-based continual learning (L2P, DualPrompt, CODAPrompt, PGP), from which it borrows the gradient-projection mechanism but deliberately departs from the prompt-pool paradigm to avoid storage overhead and privacy leakage; and (3) visual prompt tuning (VPT-Deep), which provides the parameter-efficient architecture. The key conceptual departure is adapting centralized gradient projection (PGP/GPM) to the federated setting by replacing unavailable real data with synthetic data from the DFKT generator, and combining it with a dual-level EMA-based prompt replay that has no direct analogue in prior FCIL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 99% reduction in trainable parameters (11.5M → 0.1M) makes FCIL viable on resource-constrained edge devices where full-model training and transmission are prohibitive, and the unified-prompt design eliminates the privacy risk of storing task-specific prompt pools. However, validation is limited to two small-scale datasets (CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet) with a single 12-layer ViT backbone, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger models (e.g., ViT-L/16) or more diverse datasets. An interesting open question is whether the SVD-based projection matrix, recomputed per task, remains tractable as the number of tasks or the embedding dimension grows, and whether the method extends to other modalities or non-visual federated settings.
