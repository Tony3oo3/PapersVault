## Problem

Transfer-based black-box attacks on 3D point clouds suffer from poor transferability because existing methods indiscriminately destroy all features, including those unique to the source model, causing adversarial perturbations to overfit to the source architecture. The paper identifies that critical features (key points driving classification) are consistent across diverse DNN architectures, and leverages this consistency to guide adversarial search toward shared vulnerable regions rather than source-specific ones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252127/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean 3D point cloud (1024 uniformly sampled surface points) and its true class label, along with the unit normal vector of the benign point cloud used to constrain perturbation direction. It outputs an adversarial point cloud whose perturbation is bounded by an l∞ norm constraint (ε ∈ {0.18, 0.45}) and further regularized by Chamfer distance to ensure imperceptibility on unordered point sets. The perturbation is initialized from N(0, 0.001) and iteratively refined over 200 steps.

## Method

CFG (Critical Feature Guidance) introduces a regularization term L_CFG = G ⊙ A_k^F_φ(x^adv) that computes gradient-based feature importance at a middle layer (Conv2) of the source model and assigns high weights to critical features shared across architectures, thereby directing the adversarial search to destroy these shared features rather than source-specific ones. The full objective combines a classification loss (to fool the source model), the CFG regularization (to enhance transferability), and a Chamfer distance term (to ensure imperceptibility), optimized via 200-step Adam iterations with l∞ clipping and normal-vector projection. The key design choice is targeting a middle feature layer rather than the softmax output, since middle-layer features better capture the geometric structure common to all architectures while avoiding overfitting to source-model-specific high-level semantics.

## Results

On ModelNet40, CFG achieves a transferability score of 53.1% at ε=0.18 and 63.6% at ε=0.45, outperforming the strongest baseline PF-Attack (40.2% and 46.8%) by 12.9 and 16.8 percentage points respectively. With PointNet as source, CFG reaches 72.6% ASR on PointNet++(MSG) and 80.6% on PointNet++(SSG) at ε=0.18, versus 49.6% and 61.5% for PF-Attack. Against state-of-the-art target models (Table 3), CFG achieves 68.1% ASR on PointConv and 65.6% on Point Transformer at ε=0.18, exceeding PF-Attack by 17.8 and 15.7 points. On the real-world ScanObjectNN dataset, CFG maintains its lead with 66.5–70.3% ASR on classical targets at ε=0.45.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CFG outperforms all five baselines by large margins across every evaluation setting. The three white-box methods (3D-Adv, KNN, GeoA3) achieve only 9–13% transferability score, confirming that direct gradient-based attacks on a single model produce adversarial examples that are highly overfit and fail to transfer. Among transfer-based methods, AdvPC (30.8% at ε=0.18) improves over white-box attacks by jointly perturbing original and autoencoder-reconstructed point clouds, but still lags CFG by 22.3 points. PF-Attack, the strongest baseline, uses random perturbation factorization to regularize the search and reaches 40.2% (ε=0.18) / 46.8% (ε=0.45), yet CFG surpasses it by 12.9 and 16.8 points respectively. Against potential defenses (SRS, SOR, CCN, AT), CFG maintains 50.6–87.9% ASR versus 30.0–61.1% for PF-Attack, an average improvement of ~20 percentage points. In terms of efficiency, CFG's generation time (0.07 min/sample) is faster than PF-Attack (0.10 min) while achieving substantially higher ASR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of transfer-based black-box attacks on 3D point clouds initiated by AdvPC (autoencoder-based joint attack) and PF-Attack (sub-perturbation regularization), both of which optimize against the softmax output without considering internal feature structure. The key departure is borrowing the feature-disruptive attack paradigm from 2D (FDA, feature importance-aware attacks) and adapting it to point clouds by identifying architecture-invariant 'critical features' via gradient saliency in a middle convolutional layer, then weighting their destruction in the loss. The method also adds a Chamfer distance term for imperceptibility, building on the l-infinity norm constraint standard in prior 3D attacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The observation that critical features for point cloud classification are consistent across diverse architectures (PointNet, DGCNN, PointConv, PCT, etc.) provides a principled direction for improving transferability, and the 12–17 percentage-point gain over the strongest baseline (PF-Attack) is practically meaningful for security evaluation of 3D perception systems. However, the attack still degrades substantially against adversarial training (ASR drops to ~23–31%), and the method is limited to classification; the authors note that extending to segmentation would require spatial-consistency constraints. An open question is whether the critical-feature consistency observation holds for more heterogeneous architectures (e.g., vision-language models for 3D) or for tasks beyond classification.
