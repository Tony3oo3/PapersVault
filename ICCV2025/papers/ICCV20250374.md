## Problem

Adversarial Training (AT) has been extensively studied for l∞-bounded attacks on classifiers, but AT against physically realizable attacks on object detectors remains largely unexplored. Existing defenses are either designed exclusively for adversarial patches (the simplest physical attack), vulnerable to adaptive attacks, or rely on l∞-AT whose global perturbation model is fundamentally mismatched to localized physical threats. Defenses against more pervasive physically realizable attacks such as adversarial textures, which cover entire object surfaces, are virtually absent.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (MS-COCO 2017, 80 categories, 118K training images) and outputs bounding boxes with class labels via standard object detection heads. During training, each bounding box region may receive a localized adversarial patch whose size is scaled by λ relative to the box diagonal, while the entire image simultaneously receives an imperceptible global perturbation bounded by ‖δg‖∞ ≤ 4/255. Evaluation targets the security-critical person detection subtask on Inria and a synthetic 3D-rendered dataset, using AP50 as the primary metric.

## Method

PBCAT formulates patch-based AT by replacing the global l∞ constraint with a binary mask M that restricts high-intensity perturbations (β = 64/255) to small regions within bounding boxes, while simultaneously adding a low-intensity global l∞-bounded perturbation (ε = 4/255) across the full image. To efficiently identify effective patch locations without expensive trial-and-error, PBCAT partitions each randomly placed patch into n×n sub-patches, computes average gradient norms via a single backward pass, and retains only the top 50% highest-gradient sub-patches via a binary mask. The composite perturbation (masked patch + global noise) prevents training collapse from large-area unrestricted patches while ensuring full-image adversarial coverage, enabling generalization to unseen large-area attacks like adversarial textures. Training is accelerated using FreeAT's gradient-recycling mechanism with an adversarially pre-trained backbone, reducing the cost to near standard training.

## Results

On the security-critical person detection task, PBCAT achieves 77.6% AP₅₀ against AdvPatch, 60.2% AP₅₀ against AdvTexture, and 56.4% AP₅₀ against AdvCaT, while maintaining 95.4% clean accuracy on Inria. The headline result is a 29.7% AP₅₀ improvement over the SOTA l∞-AT method (AdvOD, 30.5%) against the AdvTexture attack. PBCAT also outperforms AdvOD by 21.5% on AdvPatch (77.6% vs 56.1%) and by 16.8% on AdvCaT (56.4% vs 39.6%). On the general object detection task (MS-COCO), PBCAT achieves 37.8% AP₅₀ under PGDPatch, surpassing AdvOD by 6.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PBCAT outperforms all 12 baselines across every attack setting. Among the 10 non-AT defenses (LGS, SAC, EPGF, Jedi, FNC, APE, UDF, PatchZero, NAPGuard, AD-YOLO), the best performer is Jedi at 64.4% AP₅₀ on AdvPatch, but all non-AT methods collapse to near-zero (≤6.0%) against the larger-area AdvTexture and AdvCaT attacks, confirming their vulnerability to adaptive attacks. The two l∞-AT baselines (MTD and AdvOD) substantially outperform non-AT methods, with AdvOD reaching 56.1% on AdvPatch, 30.5% on AdvTexture, and 39.6% on AdvCaT. PBCAT improves over AdvOD by 21.5% on AdvPatch (77.6% vs 56.1%), 29.7% on AdvTexture (60.2% vs 30.5%), and 16.8% on AdvCaT (56.4% vs 39.6%), demonstrating that the composite patch-plus-global-noise training strategy generalizes to unseen large-area physically realizable attacks. PBCAT also slightly sacrifices clean accuracy (95.4% vs 95.9% for AdvOD on Inria), a common trade-off in adversarial training.

## Relation to Prior Work

PBCAT extends the l∞-AT formulation (Madry et al., ICLR 2018) by replacing the global perturbation constraint with a masked, local patch constraint, and incorporates FreeAT's gradient-recycling mechanism (Shafahi et al., NeurIPS 2019) to keep training cost comparable to standard training. It departs from earlier patch-based AT works (Rao et al., Wu et al., Metzen et al.), which were designed for single-object classification and relied on expensive trial-and-error patch placement, by introducing gradient-guided sub-patch selection that requires only one forward/backward pass. Conceptually, the key departure is the composite perturbation design—combining small-area high-intensity patches with global l∞-bounded noise—which bridges the gap between the two distinct threat models of adversarial patches and adversarial textures that prior defenses treated separately.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first unified adversarial training method that defends object detectors against both adversarial patches and the newer, more pervasive adversarial texture attacks, which prior defenses had largely ignored. The finding that all non-AT defenses (designed specifically for physical attacks) are completely broken by adaptive texture attacks, while a simple l∞-AT baseline already outperforms them, shifts the field's focus toward AT-based approaches for physical-world robustness. An open question the authors acknowledge is whether the slight clean-accuracy degradation reflects an inherent trade-off between physical-attack robustness and standard accuracy. The method's practical relevance is high for security-critical deployments (autonomous driving, surveillance), though its generalization to other attack families beyond patches and textures remains untested.
