## Problem

Existing dense point tracking methods are limited by the fixed frame rate of shutter cameras, making it difficult to model fine-grained temporally continuous motion, while event cameras—despite their microsecond temporal resolution—produce spatially sparse data that cannot alone capture dense motion. No prior work has combined both modalities to recover spatially dense and temporally continuous trajectories for every pixel at arbitrary times.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251791/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video of N_v frames at fixed frame rate together with a corresponding asynchronous event sequence (each event carrying pixel position, microsecond timestamp, and polarity), where events are first converted to dense grid representations for network processing. The output is, for every pixel, a B-spline curve trajectory with N_c control points per inter-frame interval that is accumulated across all t processed frames into a global curve with t×N_c control points, along with per-pixel visibility maps and a cached global motion representation at the feature level.

## Method

The framework operates as a streaming pipeline with two stages: (1) a two-frame basis model encodes consecutive images and inter-frame event grids, builds local correlation via matrix multiplication, and uses a GMA-style transformer fusion module plus a RAFT-style FPN decoder to estimate N_c B-spline control points and a visibility map; (2) a global aggregation module recurrently fuses the new local motion representation with the cached global representation using cross-attention and a GRU update gate at the feature level. Trajectories are accumulated by warping the new local curve into the global coordinate frame (with learned sub-pixel offsets to mitigate sampling error) and concatenating control points, while occluded pixels are handled by a learnable Fusion module that regresses coarse motion from the global representation. Training combines supervised L1 loss on discrete ground-truth tracks with two self-supervised consistency losses: an event-consistency loss that warps chunked events along predicted trajectories and penalizes mismatch, and an image-consistency loss that enforces photometric agreement at sampled discrete times.

## Results

On the CVO dense tracking benchmark, EDCPT achieves 1.23 EPE_all and 82.1% OA on the Clean set, outperforming the previous best dense method DOT (1.32 EPE_all, 80.4% OA) by 0.09 EPE and 1.7 OA. On the TAP-Vid-DAVIS sparse tracking benchmark, EDCPT reaches 63.8 AJ and 90.6% OA (First) and 67.5 AJ and 91.1% OA (Strided), surpassing DOT by 2.2 AJ and 1.1 OA. On the real-event DSEC optical flow benchmark, EDCPT achieves 0.63 EPE and 2.17 AE, improving over BFlow with image+event input (0.69 EPE, 2.42 AE) by 0.06 EPE and 0.25 AE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDCPT outperforms all dense tracking baselines on CVO: it beats DOT (the closest competitor) by 0.09 EPE_all and 1.7 OA on Clean, 0.07 EPE and 1.7 OA on Final, and 0.19 EPE and 0.9 OA on the Extended set. It also surpasses all query-based methods (PIPs++, TAPIR, CoTracker) by large margins on dense evaluation, with CoTracker being the strongest query method at 1.51 EPE vs. EDCPT's 1.23. On TAP-Vid-DAVIS, EDCPT leads DOT by 2.2 AJ (63.8 vs. 61.6) and 1.1 OA (90.6 vs. 89.5) on the First split, and by 0.8 AJ and 0.7 OA on Strided, while also outperforming FlowTrack (63.2 AJ) and DINO-Tracker (62.3 AJ) on Strided. On the real-event DSEC benchmark, EDCPT achieves the best EPE (0.63) and AE (2.17), beating BFlow with image+event input (0.69 EPE, 2.42 AE) and all event-only methods (E-RAFT 0.79, TMA 0.74, IDNet 0.72, ECDDP 0.70). The ablation in Table 7 confirms that removing event data degrades performance from 2.23 to 2.50 EPE on CVO-third, validating the contribution of event modality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the B-spline curve trajectory representation from CPFlow [31] (limited to 4 frames) into a streaming multi-frame accumulation framework, and adapts the transformer-based global motion aggregation from GMA [22] as the local motion extractor. It departs from the dominant point-tracking paradigm (TAP, TAPIR, CoTracker) that treats trajectories as discrete per-frame positions, and from event-based motion estimation (BFlow, MPCMax) that is confined to fixed two-frame intervals. The key conceptual departure is introducing event camera data as an auxiliary modality for point tracking for the first time, establishing a self-supervised link between event triggering and continuous curve trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work opens a new direction for leveraging event cameras in long-term tracking, potentially enabling motion estimation at temporal resolutions far beyond the 30–60 fps of conventional video. However, practical impact is currently limited by the absence of real-event point-tracking benchmarks (the authors rely on simulated events via vid2e for most evaluations) and by computational cost (12.6 s for 48 frames vs. 2.1 s for two-frame GMA). Open questions include whether the event consistency loss generalizes to real noisy event streams at scale, and whether the streaming GRU aggregation can be made more efficient for very long sequences.
