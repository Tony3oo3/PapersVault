## Problem

Existing robust NAS methods require repeated adversarial training to evaluate candidate architectures, making search 30× slower than standard NAS. Training-free proxies that avoid this cost (e.g., CRoZe, ZCPRob) suffer from inaccurate robustness ranking—CRoZe fails on ~21% of architectures due to gradient orthogonality, and neither achieves strong Spearman consistency with true adversarial robustness. Additionally, prior methods handle the multi-objective trade-off between robustness, clean accuracy, and model size through fixed or adaptively tuned aggregation coefficients, introducing bias.

## Input / Output

The search operates over the DARTS cell-based architecture space (~10^20 candidates), where each of 14 edges per cell selects from 8 candidate operations. The R-Score proxy consumes only randomly initialized weights and a small set of input samples (no trained parameters), computing activation patterns and FGSM-perturbed feature/parameter cosine similarities. The output is a population of ranked architectures balancing robustness (R-Score) and parameter count, from which the best architecture is extracted and subsequently adversarially trained (7-step PGD, 120 epochs) before evaluation on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet under FGSM, PGD, and AutoAttack.

## Method

TRNAS proposes a zero-cost robustness proxy called R-Score that integrates two complementary signals: (1) linear activation capability (expressivity), which counts distinct ReLU activation patterns across layers to measure how finely the network partitions input space, and (2) feature consistency, which computes layer-wise cosine similarity of both feature vectors and parameter vectors before and after a single FGSM perturbation, then aggregates via product-summation. The final R-Score is the geometric mean of expressivity and consistency (Ψ^{1/2} × Φ). For population management, TRNAS introduces a Multi-Objective Selection (MOS) strategy that first applies a MOEA/D-style decomposition to rank architectures by weighted deviation from an ideal (low R-Score, low params) vector, then uses k-means clustering on the lower-ranked subset to preserve architectural diversity. The overall search is an evolutionary loop of multi-point crossover, mutation, R-Score evaluation, and MOS selection, completing in 0.02 GPU days on the DARTS space.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, TRNAS achieves 85.66% clean accuracy and 51.76% AutoAttack robust accuracy with only 4.3M parameters, completing the search in 0.02 GPU days. On CIFAR-100, it reaches 61.32% clean and 27.32% AutoAttack accuracy, and on ImageNet it attains 55.10% clean and 9.08% AutoAttack accuracy. TRNAS outperforms the closest training-free robust NAS baseline (ZCPRob) by 1.79% under AutoAttack on CIFAR-10 and by 1.73% on CIFAR-100, while matching ZCPRob's search cost of 0.02 GPU days.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRNAS outperforms all seven robust NAS baselines (RACL, RobNet, AdvRush, DSRNA, LRNAS, CRoZe, ZCPRob) on every adversarial metric across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet. Against the closest competitor ZCPRob, TRNAS gains +1.79% AutoAttack accuracy on CIFAR-10 (51.76 vs 49.97), +1.73% on CIFAR-100 (27.32 vs 25.59), and +0.83% on ImageNet (9.08 vs 8.25), while matching its 0.02 GPU-day search cost. TRNAS also surpasses standard training-free NAS methods (SWAP, RoBoT) on all adversarial metrics, e.g., 51.76% vs 50.68% AutoAttack over SWAP on CIFAR-10. The only baseline that edges out TRNAS is DenseNet-121 on clean accuracy (85.95% vs 85.66%), but TRNAS leads it by 4.30% under AutoAttack. In black-box transfer experiments, TRNAS as a source model produces the most transferable adversarial examples (lowest target accuracy in every column of Tables 4–5), and as a target it achieves the highest robustness against all other source models.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRNAS sits at the intersection of two research threads: training-free NAS proxies (which use zero-cost metrics like linear activation capability from Chen et al. and feature consistency from CRoZe to evaluate architectures without training) and robust NAS (which seeks architectures resistant to adversarial perturbations). It departs from prior robust NAS methods (RACL, RobNet, DSRNA, AdvRush) that all require repeated adversarial training of a supernet, and from the closest training-free competitors CRoZe and ZCPRob by replacing their single-theory proxies with a combined expressivity-plus-consistency score (R-Score) and adding a multi-objective selection strategy borrowed from MOEA/D decomposition. The evolutionary search framework itself is standard, but the specific combination of linear activation capability theory with FGSM-perturbed feature/parameter consistency is new to the robustness setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a 10× speedup over CRoZe and a 100% evaluation effective rate (vs. 79.3% for CRoZe, which assigns zero scores to ~21% of architectures due to gradient orthogonality), making robust NAS accessible at 0.02 GPU days. The architectural insight that topological density in early layers drives robustness more than later-layer complexity offers a design principle for future robust architectures. Open questions include whether R-Score generalizes beyond the DARTS cell-based search space to more diverse topologies (e.g., transformer-based or multi-branch architectures), whether the MOS strategy's k-means diversity preservation remains beneficial at much larger population sizes, and whether the modest 1–2% gains over ZCPRob persist under stronger attack budgets or larger-scale training.
