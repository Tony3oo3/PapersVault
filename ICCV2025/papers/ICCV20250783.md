## Problem

Existing federated prompt-tuning methods are restricted to unimodal data, while multimodal federated learning approaches either rely on custom architectures that cannot exploit pre-trained foundation models or assume all clients share the same set of modalities. In realistic deployments, clients collect data from different sensors, producing local datasets with both intra-heterogeneity (varying missing features within a client) and inter-heterogeneity (different missing-pattern distributions across clients), which causes naive prompt aggregation to collapse into uninformative instructions.

## Input / Output

Each client provides a private dataset of multimodal samples (text and image) where individual samples may have one or both modalities missing, and the distribution of missing patterns differs across clients. The model consumes tokenized sequences of whichever modalities are present, prepended with a dynamically retrieved subset of learned prompts, and outputs a single class label (e.g., food category or movie genre). The backbone is the frozen ViLT transformer, with only the prediction head and prompt parameters being updated.

## Method

FED-PRIME decomposes the learnable prompt set into two subsets: inter-client prompts that encode prototypical distributional patterns of missing data (aligned and clustered across clients) and intra-client prompts that encode input-agnostic compensation for specific missing modalities (aggregated via FedAvg). At each client, an input-adaptive retrieval mechanism learns key and query functions to select the κ most relevant prompts from each subset per sample, with a distance-based regularizer preventing diverse fine-tuning knowledge from being overloaded onto a single prompt. On the server, inter-client prompt alignment is formulated as a discrete clustering optimization with a learnable cost function and a popularity regularizer that prioritizes more generic prompts; the resulting assignment problem is solved per-client via the Hungarian algorithm in polynomial time. This two-level design ensures that distributional heterogeneity across clients is handled through learned alignment rather than destructive averaging, while shared input-agnostic knowledge is still consolidated efficiently.

## Results

On UPMC Food-101 (accuracy), FED-PRIME achieves 92.12–93.64% under Full Modal test across all three training scenarios (Miss Text, Miss Image, Miss Both), and 78.88–90.55% under the matching ~Train test. On MM-IMDB (F1-macro), FED-PRIME reaches 37.67–38.68% under Full Modal test and 31.92–36.08% under ~Train test. Relative improvements over the second-best baseline range from 1.73% to 107.83% on UPMC Food-101 and from 4.41% to 69.65% on MM-IMDB across all 15 train/test combinations per dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FED-PRIME is compared against two external baselines adapted to the federated multimodal missing-modality setting: FEDAVG-P (the centralized missing-modality prompt-tuning method of Lee et al. [24] combined with standard FedAvg) and FEDMSPLIT-P (the same prompt-tuning method combined with the FedMSplit aggregation of Chen et al. [8]). FED-PRIME outperforms FEDAVG-P in every single train/test combination on both datasets, with the largest gaps appearing under Miss Text training on Food-101 (e.g., 78.88% vs. 15.71% accuracy on ~Train test, a 107.83% relative improvement). Against FEDMSPLIT-P, FED-PRIME also wins in all settings, though the margin is smaller in a few cases (e.g., Miss Image on Food-101 ~Train: 90.55% vs. 74.16%). Two internal ablation variants—FED-INTER (inter-client prompts only) and FED-INTRA (intra-client prompts only)—confirm that both prompt sets are necessary: FED-PRIME beats each variant by 10–30+ points in most configurations, demonstrating that handling both inter-client distributional heterogeneity and intra-client modality-missing patterns is essential for strong performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of federated learning (FedAvg, FedMSplit), parameter-efficient prompt tuning (Lester et al.), and multimodal prompt-tuning with missing modalities (Lee et al., CVPR 2023). It departs from prior multimodal FL work (FedMSplit, FedMAC, FedInMM) that relies on custom architectures or assumes uniform modalities across clients, and from centralized missing-modality prompt-tuning that cannot handle inter-client heterogeneity. The key architectural departure is the dual prompt decomposition (inter-client prompts for distributional missing patterns, intra-client prompts for input-agnostic missing modalities) combined with a server-side clustering-based alignment via the Hungarian algorithm, which has no direct precedent in either the FL or prompt-tuning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FED-PRIME addresses a practically important gap: real-world IoT and sensor data is inherently multimodal, distributed across private devices, and exhibits heterogeneous missing patterns—yet no prior method combined pre-trained multimodal fine-tuning with federated prompt-tuning under such conditions. The approach's reliance on a single backbone (ViLT), two modalities (image+text), and small-scale datasets (8 classes) limits immediate generalizability, and the baselines are adapted rather than purpose-built, which inflates apparent margins. Open questions include scalability to more modalities, larger client populations, alternative backbones (e.g., CLIP), and whether the clustering-based alignment generalizes beyond the Hungarian-algorithm formulation to more complex assignment structures.
