## Problem

Subject-consistent video generation (S2V) requires simultaneously aligning text instructions with reference image content, but existing approaches either chain subject-to-image with image-to-video (causing ID information loss) or train on in-pair image-video data (leading to copy-paste behavior and poor text responsiveness). Additionally, no unified framework exists that handles both single- and multi-subject references while avoiding confusion between similar subjects.

## Input / Output

The model accepts 1 to 4 reference images (encoded via both a 3D VAE and CLIP) plus a text prompt, which is first processed by a rephraser to embed detailed per-subject appearance descriptions and disambiguate similar subjects. At inference, classifier-free guidance separates image and text conditions with distinct weights (ω₁=3, ω₂=7.5) over 50 Euler steps. Training data consists of cross-video text-image-video triplets (~1M pairs) where reference images come from different videos than the target, ensuring non-rigid deformations and color variations between reference and output.

## Method

Phantom builds on the MMDiT dual-stream DiT architecture and redesigns the joint text-image injection mechanism: reference images are encoded by both a VAE (low-level detail) and CLIP (high-level semantics), with VAE latents concatenated into the visual branch and CLIP features into the text branch. A dynamic window-attention strategy appends reference Q/K/V tokens to the end of each spatial window while shifting in-situ tokens forward, preserving window structure and position encoding while supporting 1–4 subjects without changing window size. Training uses rectified flow with a two-phase schedule (256p/480p for 50k steps, then mixed 720p for 20k steps) on cross-video paired data to prevent copy-paste, with a 0.7 dropout rate on VAE features to prevent them from overshadowing CLIP semantics. The data pipeline constructs cross-video multi-subject pairs scored by an improved CLIP image embedder, filtering out pairs that are too similar (copy-paste risk) or too dissimilar (different subjects).

## Results

On the single-subject S2V benchmark (Table 2), Phantom-IP achieves the best prompt-following score (ViCLIP-T 23.41) and the best segmented subject consistency (CLIP-I-Seg 0.731), while Kling 1.6 leads in raw CLIP-I (0.732) and DINO-I (0.554). On the facial ID-consistency benchmark (Table 1), Phantom-ID outperforms both Hailuo-ID and ConsisID across all three face-similarity metrics (FaceSim-Arc 0.581, FaceSim-Cur 0.529, FaceSim-glink 0.590) and prompt following (ViCLIP-T 24.12). In the multi-subject user study, Phantom's performance is rated comparable to commercial solutions with a slight edge in subject consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Kling 1.6, Phantom-IP trades a small gap in raw subject similarity (CLIP-I 0.714 vs 0.732, DINO-I 0.523 vs 0.554) for substantially better prompt following (ViCLIP-T 23.41 vs 21.62) and segmented subject consistency (CLIP-I-Seg 0.731 vs 0.715), suggesting Kling's higher raw scores partly stem from I2V-like copy-paste behavior that reduces text responsiveness. Against Vidu 2.0 and Pika 2.1, Phantom-IP leads in prompt following and segmented consistency while remaining competitive on overall subject similarity. On the facial ID subtask, Phantom-ID clearly wins over both Hailuo-ID (FaceSim-Arc 0.581 vs 0.542) and the open-source ConsisID (0.581 vs 0.538) across all three face-recognition backbones, with the largest margin over ConsisID on FaceSim-Cur (0.529 vs 0.417). The paper notes that Kling exhibits a notable I2V artifact where the first generated frame nearly matches the reference image, hurting virtual try-on success rates, while Pika shows the weakest subject consistency overall.

## Relation to Prior Work

Phantom builds directly on the MMDiT dual-stream DiT architecture (Esser et al., 2024) and the window-attention variant from SeedVR (Wang et al., 2025), fine-tuning a pre-trained video foundation model (Lin et al., 2025) rather than training from scratch. Conceptually, it departs from the adapter-based S2I paradigm (IP-Adapter, DreamBooth-style) by jointly training cross-modal alignment on text-image-video triplets, and from the 'S2I + I2V' pipeline by arguing that the intermediate image step causes irreversible ID information loss. The data pipeline's use of cross-video subject pairs (rather than in-pair keyframes as in VideoAlchemist or ConceptMaster) is a deliberate departure to prevent the model from learning copy-paste shortcuts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Phantom provides the first open, unified framework that handles both single- and multi-subject reference video generation in one model, narrowing the gap between academic research and proprietary commercial tools (Vidu, Kling, Pika). The cross-video pairing strategy and the dual VAE+CLIP injection offer practical recipes for anyone building S2V systems, and the constructed evaluation protocol (portrait ID, single-subject, multi-subject) is a useful starting point for a field lacking standard benchmarks. Key limitations include instability beyond 4 reference subjects, reliance on a rephraser for inference-time prompt alignment, and evaluation against closed-source baselines that limits reproducibility. Open questions include whether the approach scales to long-form multi-scene narratives and whether the window-attention constraint becomes a bottleneck at higher resolutions or longer durations.
