## Problem

Adapting one-step diffusion generators to new controls (structural constraints, user preferences) typically requires modifying the base diffusion model and performing full redistillation, which is computationally expensive. Naively combining a pre-trained ControlNet with a distilled one-step generator degrades both image quality and fine-grained control, while existing score distillation methods assume the teacher already understands the target condition. The paper tackles how to train a one-step student that handles controls entirely unknown to the teacher diffusion model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252517/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt together with one or more additional control signals (Canny edges, HED edges, depth maps, or low-resolution images for 8× super-resolution) and produces a single high-quality image in exactly one network function evaluation. Control signals are injected through a ControlNet module attached to the student UNet, while the text condition is handled via the standard cross-attention mechanism inherited from the teacher. The framework also supports a single-condition mode where a reward score (e.g., ImageReward) serves as the sole conditioning signal for human-preference alignment.

## Method

JDM minimizes the reverse KL divergence between the student's image-condition joint distribution p_θ(x_t, c) and the target joint p(c|x_t)·p_φ(x_t), then derives a tractable upper bound whose gradient decomposes into a condition-learning term ∇_{x_t} log p(c|x_t) and a fidelity-learning term ∇_{x_t} log p_φ(x_t)/p_θ(x_t). This decoupling is the key asymmetry: the teacher only needs to model the marginal p_φ(x_t), while the student acquires conditional capability through the discriminative model p(c|x_t), which is approximated via a consistency model trained with LoRA on the fake score. The fake score s_ψ is learned by standard denoising on student-generated samples, and the consistency model maps noisy x_t back to clean x to evaluate the condition likelihood. A two-phase warm-up strategy first jointly trains the generator and ControlNet on a primary condition, then freezes the generator and trains only the ControlNet for additional conditions, enabling a shared one-step UNet across multiple control types without mode collapse.

## Results

In one-step controllable generation, JDM achieves an average FID of 14.58 and average consistency of 0.071 across Canny, HED, Depth, and 8× Super Resolution tasks, outperforming the 50-NFE ControlNet baseline (FID 15.21, consistency 0.093) with 50× fewer function evaluations. In one-step text-to-image generation, JDM with decoupled CFG achieves a CLIP score of 33.97 and average HPS of 29.75, surpassing the 25-step SD-v1.5 base model (CLIP 33.03, HPS 25.50) and the best one-step baseline Hyper-SD (CLIP 30.87, HPS 28.01). The HFL variant achieves HPS 29.46 and CLIP 33.75, also setting new SOTA among one-step methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JDM decisively outperforms the 50-NFE ControlNet baseline in controllable generation, improving average FID from 15.21 to 14.58 and average consistency from 0.093 to 0.071 while reducing NFEs by 50×. Against the naive DI+ControlNet integration (1 NFE), JDM improves FID from 22.21 to 14.58 and consistency from 0.120 to 0.071, confirming that joint training is essential rather than simple composition. In text-to-image generation, JDM w/ better CFG surpasses all one-step baselines: it beats Hyper-SD (the strongest HFL-based competitor) by 1.74 HPS and 3.10 CLIP, Diff-Instruct by 3.40 HPS and 1.89 CLIP, and DMD2 by 6.22 HPS and 3.05 CLIP. The only metric where a baseline edges out JDM is Aesthetic Score, where LCM-dreamshaper (5.94) marginally exceeds JDM (5.90), though LCM requires 4 steps versus JDM's 1. JDM also surpasses the 25-step SD-v1.5 base model on HPS (29.75 vs 25.50) and Aes (5.90 vs 5.49) while matching or exceeding its CLIP score (33.97 vs 33.03), demonstrating that one-step generation can now rival or beat multi-step quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the score distillation lineage (Diff-Instruct, VSD) by replacing the marginal KL objective with a joint-distribution KL divergence between p(x_t, c) and p(c|x_t)·p_φ(x_t), which algebraically decouples condition learning from fidelity learning. This decoupling is the key departure: prior one-step distillation methods require the teacher to already understand the conditioning signal, whereas JDM's asymmetric formulation lets the student acquire controls (e.g., a new ControlNet branch) that the teacher has never seen. The consistency-model-based approximation of p(x|x_t) for noisy condition alignment borrows from the consistency-model literature but applies it in a novel role as an implicit generator for the conditional density.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployment: a single one-step generator can be extended to new control modalities without re-distilling the entire pipeline, and the decoupled CFG mechanism allows leveraging a stronger teacher (e.g., SD 2.1) purely for text-image alignment while keeping a lighter model for fidelity. Open questions include whether the joint-KL formulation generalizes to continuous or multi-condition settings beyond the four discrete controls tested, whether the two-phase warm-up strategy scales to many control types without degrading the shared generator, and whether the internal-dataset evaluation transfers to public benchmarks. The finding that a better-CFG variant outperforms the HFL variant also raises the question of how much of the HFL benefit is genuine preference alignment versus reward hacking.
