## Problem

Pretrained representation uncertainties enable zero-shot uncertainty estimation in natural image CV, but it remains unclear whether they generalize to Earth Observation, where data exhibits unique semantic characteristics—multiple spatial scales, no clear background, geographic and spectral diversity, and varying ground sampling distances. This paper systematically investigates how these EO-specific factors affect the transfer of pretrained uncertainties to unseen EO domains, tasks, and resolutions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250380/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes EO images (satellite or aerial) in RGB bands at resolutions ranging from 120×120 pixels (10 m GSD, e.g., BigEarthNet) to 512×512 pixels (20 cm GSD, e.g., Flair), with the framework extensible to multispectral and SAR modalities. The frozen ViT encoder produces a token sequence, from which the uncertainty module outputs a single scalar aleatoric uncertainty value per image; by feeding each patch token independently into the module, a per-patch spatial uncertainty map (16×16 pixel patches) is also obtained without additional training.

## Method

Building on Kirchhof et al.'s pretrained visual uncertainties, the paper trains ViT-Tiny/Small/Base/Large as supervised classifiers on large EO datasets (BigEarthNet, Flair), freezes the encoder, and trains an MLP uncertainty module on top to predict the ranking-based upstream training loss as a proxy for aleatoric uncertainty. The key technical contribution is a systematic evaluation framework: four Semantic Factors (GSD, domain of interest, target granularity, spatial arrangement) are varied across inference datasets, and novel metrics—Label Agreement@1 (LA@1) in multiple variants and its CPA-based generalization (LA-CPA)—extend uncertainty evaluation beyond single-label Recall@1 to multi-label classification and semantic segmentation. Downstream utility is assessed via the Discard Test, which iteratively removes the most uncertain samples and checks whether task-specific loss decreases, and spatial uncertainty is extracted by passing individual ViT patch tokens through the uncertainty module.

## Results

The paper evaluates pretrained representation uncertainties across six EO inference datasets (BigEarthNet, MLRSNet, Woody, Waititu, Flair, MARIDA) using four ViT variants (Tiny, Small, Base, Large) and multiple LA-CPA metrics. EO-pretrained uncertainties (on BigEarthNet, Flair, or BigEarthNet-5) consistently outperform both the random baseline (R-CPA = 0.5) and ImageNet-pretrained uncertainties across nearly all metrics and model sizes. The only exception is One-LA-CPA, where LA@1 is close to 1, making CPA less interpretable. In the Discard Test, EO-pretrained models show a clear monotonic decrease in downstream loss as uncertain samples are removed, confirming alignment with task-specific uncertainties, while ImageNet-pretrained models fail to exhibit this trend.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the ImageNet-pretrained uncertainty module from Kirchhof et al. [31], which uses publicly available ViT weights trained on 21,841 natural image classes. EO-pretrained uncertainties (BigEarthNet, Flair, BigEarthNet-5) consistently outperform ImageNet-pretrained models across all LA-CPA metrics (One-LA-CPA, All-LA-CPA, %-LA-CPA, PD-LA-CPA, Patches_p-LA-CPA, Patches_p-PD-LA-CPA) and all ViT variants, as shown in Figure 2. Critically, ImageNet-pretrained models underperform even the random baseline (R-CPA = 0.5) on most metrics, indicating that the domain gap between natural images and EO imagery fundamentally breaks uncertainty transfer. The paper isolates this failure to the uncertainty module specifically: ImageNet encoders produce representations with comparable LA@1 accuracy to EO-pretrained encoders, so the degradation is not due to poor representations but to the uncertainty module being trained on an irrelevant upstream loss distribution. In the Discard Test (Figure 6), EO-pretrained ViT-Large models show decreasing downstream loss as the most uncertain samples are iteratively removed across all datasets except Flair (where mean pixel loss is misleading); with max pixel loss, EO models exhibit near-ideal monotonic behavior while ImageNet models fail to align. For GSD sensitivity (Figures 4–5), aligning pretraining and inference resolution yields the best performance, with extreme downscaling (16×16) degrading all metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the pretrained visual uncertainty framework of Kirchhof et al. [31], which trains a lightweight MLP on top of frozen ViT representations to predict upstream loss as a proxy for aleatoric uncertainty, from natural images to the Earth Observation domain. It builds on the broader EO representation learning literature (MAE-based pretraining, foundation models like FoMo-Net and DOFA) but shifts focus from learning representations to learning uncertainties on those representations. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of Semantic Factors (GSD, domain, target granularity, spatial arrangement) as a structured lens for analyzing uncertainty generalization, along with new evaluation metrics (LA@1, LA-CPA) that extend the single-label R-AUROC to multi-label classification and semantic segmentation settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of representation uncertainty generalization in EO, providing actionable guidance (align GSD between pretraining and inference; EO-domain pretraining is essential for the uncertainty module) that EO practitioners can immediately apply. The finding that the domain gap degrades uncertainty estimates while leaving representations intact isolates a previously unexamined failure mode in transfer learning. Key limitations include that per-patch spatial uncertainties are extracted post-hoc from image-level pretraining rather than learned on dense prediction tasks, and the evaluation is limited to RGB bands. The authors envision a universal EO foundation uncertainty model, but learning uncertainty directly on segmentation tasks and extending to multi-spectral/multi-modal inputs remain open.
