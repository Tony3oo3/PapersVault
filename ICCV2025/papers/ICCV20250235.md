## Problem

Existing visual-language models struggle to perform deep, structured reasoning over visual content, often failing on complex multimodal tasks due to incomplete image interpretation or shallow reasoning. Additionally, no comprehensive benchmark exists to evaluate multimodal reasoning across diverse domains and educational difficulty levels, making it hard to assess where models truly stand in their reasoning capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250235/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image paired with a text question and produces a structured output consisting of a step-by-step reasoning chain enclosed in <think>/ tags followed by a final answer in <answer>/</answer> tags. The reasoning chain begins with a formal textual description of the image content (e.g., SPICE for circuits, CSV/JSON for tables, bounding-box annotations for scenes) before proceeding to deductive steps, effectively converting the visual modality into a language-reasonable format.

## Method

The core contribution is a cross-modal reasoning pipeline that converts images into formal textual representations using GPT-4o, Grounding DINO, and EasyOCR, then leverages DeepSeek-R1 with a role-playing strategy to generate step-by-step multimodal chain-of-thought annotations, producing the 155k-sample R1-Onevision dataset. Training follows a two-stage post-training strategy on Qwen2.5-VL: supervised fine-tuning on the dataset to establish coherent reasoning patterns and output structure, followed by rule-based reinforcement learning using GRPO with two reward signals (format compliance and answer accuracy via regex/IoU matching). The formal description step is critical—ablations show it nearly doubles MathVision accuracy over plain GPT-4o captions, and the role-playing iteration further improves fidelity by emulating iterative human visual re-examination.

## Results

R1-Onevision-7B achieves 29.9% on MathVision, 46.4% on MathVerse (ALL), 40.0% on MathVerse (Vision Only), 64.1% on MathVista, and 61.8% on WeMath. On the R1-Onevision-Bench, it scores 36.2% average accuracy, outperforming all other open-source models in the 4B–10B parameter range. On general multimodal benchmarks, R1-Onevision-7B reaches 66.18 on Hallusion, 59.76 on MMStar, and 68.58 on MMVet, surpassing both LLaVA-CoT-11B and LlamaV-o1-11B across all seven evaluated benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B, R1-Onevision-7B improves by +4.5 on MathVision, +2.8 on MathVerse ALL, and +1.8 on MathVerse (Vision Only), confirming the benefit of the cross-modal formalization pipeline and two-stage post-training. Compared to GPT-4o, R1-Onevision-7B wins by +5.2 on MathVerse ALL, +5.5 on MathVerse (Vision Only), and +4.1 on MathVista, but trails by 0.7 on MathVision and 7.2 on WeMath. Against other open-source models, the gains are substantial: +12.8 over InternVL2.5-8B on MathVision, +16.2 over Qwen2-VL-7B on MathVerse ALL, and +17.2 over LLaVA-CoT-11B on MathVerse (Vision Only). On the R1-Onevision-Bench (Table 5), R1-Onevision-7B achieves 36.2% average accuracy, ranking first among open-source models under 10B parameters and trailing only Qwen2.5-VL-72B (52.0%) and closed-source Gemini-2.0-Flash (59.1%). On general benchmarks (Table 3), R1-Onevision-7B outperforms LLaVA-CoT-11B by +18.4 on Hallusion and +8.3 on MMVet, and beats LlamaV-o1-11B by +2.7 on Hallusion and +3.2 on MMVet.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DeepSeek-R1 reinforcement learning paradigm (GRPO with rule-based rewards) from text-only reasoning to the multimodal setting, but its core departure is the cross-modal formalization pipeline: rather than feeding raw images to a VLM and hoping for structured reasoning, it converts visual content into formal textual representations (SPICE, PlantUML, CSV, bounding boxes, OCR text) before invoking a language reasoning model. This contrasts with prior multimodal CoT approaches like LLaVA-CoT and LlamaV-o1, which impose predefined thinking templates, and with MAmmoTH-VL, which relies on direct imitation of curated answers. The paper also positions itself against existing math-centric benchmarks (MathVision, MathVista, WeMath) by introducing a grade-structured, multi-discipline benchmark that mirrors human educational progression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cross-modal formalization pipeline offers a concrete, reproducible recipe for bridging the perception-reasoning gap in VLMs, and the 155k-sample dataset plus grade-level benchmark provide useful resources for the community. However, several open questions remain: the pipeline's heavy reliance on GPT-4o for both image formalization and quality assurance raises circularity concerns; the 7B model still trails the 72B Qwen2.5-VL by a wide margin (36.2 vs. 52.0 on R1-Onevision-Bench), suggesting the approach may not fully close the scale gap; and the benchmark's 942 questions, while well-structured, are limited in scale for robust statistical conclusions. Whether formal descriptions generalize to novel or highly ambiguous visual domains beyond the curated training distribution remains to be tested.
