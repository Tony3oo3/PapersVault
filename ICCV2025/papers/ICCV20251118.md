## Problem

Existing VLM hallucination benchmarks (POPE, AMBER) rely on small curated datasets like MSCOCO with limited object classes, making them unable to capture open-world hallucinations or reveal whether errors are random or systematic. There is no scalable, fully automated method to identify the specific types of real-world images that consistently trigger object hallucinations in a given VLM.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251118/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a single object class label and a target VLM as input, requiring no human annotations. It produces clusters of semantically similar real-world images (sourced from ReLAION-5B) where the VLM answers 'yes' to 'Can you see a OBJ in this image?' while an open-world detector (OWLv2) reports low confidence for that object. The final output is a set of agglomeratively clustered image groups (averaging ~40–57 images per cluster), plus a curated 2,642-image benchmark (DASH-B) with balanced positive/negative splits for 70 objects.

## Method

DASH is a fully automated, source-data-free pipeline with two complementary query generators: DASH-LLM uses Llama 3.1-70B to produce 50 text prompts per object targeting spurious-feature associations, while DASH-OPT optimizes the conditioning of a single-step diffusion model (Hyper-SD) to generate images that simultaneously maximize the VLM's 'yes' token probability and minimize the OWLv2 detector's confidence for the target object (minimizing L_vlm + L_det). These 50 text or image queries are then used for CLIP-based kNN retrieval over ReLAION-5B in an exploration phase (1,000 images per object), followed by an exploitation phase that retrieves 50 neighbors per successful exploration image, deduplicates via DreamSim (threshold 0.9), and applies agglomerative clustering with average linkage on DreamSim distances to form final hallucination clusters. DASH-OPT discovers substantially more diverse and unexpected hallucination patterns than DASH-LLM because the diffusion optimization steers generated queries away from the initial text prompts into regions of the image manifold that the VLM is specifically vulnerable to.

## Results

Across 380 object classes, DASH identifies over 19k hallucination clusters comprising 950k+ images for PaliGemma, LLaVA-NeXT Vicuna, and LLaVA-NeXT Mistral. DASH-OPT consistently outperforms DASH-LLM in both cluster count (e.g., 3895 vs. 1892 for PaliGemma) and image count (221.7K vs. 99.3K), revealing more diverse and unexpected hallucination patterns. The new DASH-B benchmark (1341 negative + 1341 positive images) shows substantially lower true negative rates (48.6% ± 10.1%) compared to POPE (96.0% ± 1.2%), confirming it is not saturated. Fine-tuning PaliGemma on DASH data improves DASH-B accuracy by +11.6% (56.4% → 68.0%) and AMBER Existence by +2.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark a single method against competing hallucination-detection methods; instead it compares its two pipeline variants and its new benchmark against existing ones. DASH-OPT (image-based retrieval via diffusion optimization) finds roughly 2× more clusters and images than DASH-LLM (text-based retrieval) across all three VLMs, and uncovers hallucination patterns far from the initial LLM prompts (e.g., beads → leopard, comic frogs → dam). On the benchmark side, DASH-B exposes a clear gap: models that score 95–97% TNR on POPE drop to 41–77% TNR on DASH-B, demonstrating that POPE is saturated while DASH-B remains discriminative. For mitigation, fine-tuning PaliGemma on DASH images improves DASH-B accuracy by +11.6% and AMBER Existence by +2.2%, at a small cost to POPE (−0.8%) and captioning/VQA (−1.8%/−0.9%). Transfer experiments show that hallucination images found for one VLM transfer to others at 10–67% rates, with the LLM backbone (Vicuna > Mistral > Llama) and vision encoder (CLIP > SigLIP) having large effects while LLM scale (7B vs. 72B Qwen2) has minimal impact.

## Relation to Prior Work

DASH builds directly on DiG-IN [1], which demonstrated that optimizing the latent inputs of a diffusion process yields meaningful images on the natural image manifold rather than adversarial pixel perturbations; DASH-OPT repurposes this optimization with a novel dual objective (maximizing VLM 'yes' probability while minimizing open-world detector confidence). Conceptually, the paper extends the spurious-correlation literature from image classifiers (Spurious ImageNet [34], Salient ImageNet [47]) to VLMs, but departs sharply from existing hallucination benchmarks (POPE, AMBER, CHAIR, THORNE) by replacing small curated datasets with web-scale retrieval over ReLAION-5B and by identifying *clusters* of semantically similar hallucination-triggering images rather than isolated error instances. The LLM-based query generation (DASH-LLM) is a lightweight but effective addition that leverages textual co-occurrence priors without any VLM-specific knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical finding is that object hallucinations persist extensively in current top VLMs (Qwen2-VL-72B, Llama 3.2-VL-11B) when evaluated in open-world settings, and that the DASH-B benchmark (TNR ≈ 48.6%) is far from saturated compared to POPE (TNR ≈ 96%), providing a meaningful yardstick for future progress. The observation that model scale (7B vs 72B Qwen2) has negligible effect on hallucination vulnerability while vision-encoder choice (CLIP vs SigLIP: 60% vs 43% transfer) and LLM backbone have large effects redirects mitigation efforts toward architectural design rather than brute-force scaling. Open questions include whether the conservative detector threshold underestimates hallucinations in the most advanced models, whether the fine-tuning gains generalize beyond the specific object set, and whether the identified clusters capture the full diversity of systematic failure modes in truly open-world deployment.
