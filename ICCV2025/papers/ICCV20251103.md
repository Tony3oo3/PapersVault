## Problem

Existing zero-shot composed image retrieval methods either require training a pseudo-token module or cascade a caption model with an LLM, introducing component incompatibility, visual information loss, and insufficient reasoning. Moreover, prior approaches perform reasoning only at the global image scale, neglecting fine-grained object-level details that are critical for precise retrieval.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251103/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference image and a short modification text as a composed query, and retrieves the top-k most relevant target images from a candidate set. Beyond the final ranking, the LVLM produces three intermediate textual outputs: a full target image caption, a list of 'existent objects' that should appear in the target, and a list of 'nonexistent objects' that should be absent. These multi-grained outputs are each encoded by CLIP's text encoder and scored against candidate images separately before being combined.

## Method

CoTMR replaces the caption-model-plus-LLM pipeline with a single Large Vision-Language Model (Qwen2-VL-72B) that directly perceives the reference image and reasons over the modification text. A novel Chain-of-Thought strategy called CIRCoT pre-divides the reasoning into four fixed subtasks (image observation, modification text analysis, modification execution, and target caption generation), guiding step-by-step inference and adding few-shot examples for stability. Multi-scale reasoning extends this to an object level, where the LVLM explicitly enumerates which objects must be present and which must be absent in the target image. A Multi-Grained Scoring mechanism then computes three CLIP similarity scores—base (caption vs. image), positive (concatenated existent objects vs. image), and negative (averaged individual nonexistent objects vs. image)—and combines them via S = S_base + λ·S_pos − μ·S_neg to reward relevant content while penalizing distracting or conflicting elements.

## Results

On Fashion-IQ (ViT-L/14), CoTMR achieves R_mean=46.50, outperforming the best training-free baseline LDRE (39.52) by 6.98 points. On CIRCO (ViT-L/14), CoTMR reaches mAP@10=28.22, surpassing LDRE (24.03) and CIReVL (19.01). On CIRR (ViT-L/14), CoTMR attains Recall@10=76.18 and Recall_sub@1=69.39 (Avg=67.07), exceeding LDRE (58.00) and CIReVL (55.92). On GeneCIS (ViT-L/14), CoTMR achieves R@1=17.6, R@2=29.6, R@3=39.8, leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoTMR outperforms all six baselines across all four benchmarks and all three CLIP backbones. Against the most direct training-free baseline CIReVL [17], CoTMR gains +7.27 mAP@5 on CIRCO (ViT-B/32) and +11.21 mAP@10 on CIRCO (ViT-L/14), while improving CIRR Recall@10 by +11.26 points (ViT-L/14). Against LDRE [47], the other training-free method, CoTMR leads by +4.27 mAP@5 on CIRCO and +6.08 Recall_sub@1 on CIRR (ViT-B/32). Among training-based methods, CoTMR surpasses SEARLE [3] by +12.84 mAP@10 on CIRCO (ViT-L/14) and Pic2Word [36] by +18.71 mAP@10 on the same setting. The one notable exception is LinCIR [13] on Fashion-IQ with ViT-G/14, where LinCIR (R_mean=53.91) edges out CoTMR (49.78), though the authors note that combining CoTMR with LinCIR yields even better results. PALAVRA [6] and SEARLE [3] are consistently the weakest baselines, confirming that training-free LVLM-based reasoning substantially outperforms pseudo-token textual inversion approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoTMR departs from both major ZS-CIR paradigms: textual inversion methods (Pic2Word, SEARLE, LinCIR) that learn pseudo-tokens from reference images, and cascaded LLM-based methods (CIReVL, LDRE) that chain a caption model with an LLM for text editing. Instead of this pipeline, CoTMR uses a single LVLM (Qwen2-VL-72B) to jointly perceive the reference image and modification text, eliminating the modality gap and information loss inherent in caption-then-edit cascades. The method also repurposes chain-of-thought prompting (building on DDCoT and Wei et al.) but replaces model-driven task decomposition with four CIR-specific predefined subtasks (CIRCoT), and introduces a multi-scale reasoning + reward-penalty scoring mechanism (MGS) that has no direct analogue in prior ZS-CIR work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, interpretable design makes CoTMR practically attractive: users can inspect and intervene in the step-by-step reasoning, and no task-specific training data is needed. The multi-grained scoring (rewarding existent objects, penalizing nonexistent ones) offers a principled way to handle the 'preserve vs. modify' tension central to CIR, and the ablation showing that object-level scores alone underperform the global caption highlights the importance of compositional context. Limitations include dependence on a 72B LVLM for best results (7B drops substantially), per-dataset hyperparameter tuning for λ and μ, and the fact that a trained method (LinCIR) still outperforms CoTMR on Fashion-IQ with ViT-G/14. Open questions include whether the predefined-subtask CoT strategy transfers to other compositional tasks and whether smaller or open-weight LVLMs can close the gap.
