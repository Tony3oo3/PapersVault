## Problem

Generating photorealistic driving videos that remain 3D-consistent over long horizons (20+ seconds) is a fundamental challenge: existing video diffusion models suffer from progressive geometric drift and quality degradation during autoregressive generation, while keyframe-based approaches lack explicit geometry awareness, producing inconsistent depth across frames. The core bottleneck is maintaining both appearance and geometric coherence across hundreds of frames spanning significant viewpoint changes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252157/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a single input RGB image and a vehicle trajectory (with HD maps, object bounding boxes, and text prompts as optional controls), AutoScape produces a 20-second, 250-frame driving video. Keyframe viewpoints are selected along the trajectory at intervals exceeding 10 m distance or 20° view-angle change, and generation proceeds autoregressively in reverse (from trajectory end to start). The intermediate outputs include RGB-D keyframes and back-projected 3D point clouds that anchor the scene geometry before dense video interpolation.

## Method

AutoScape decomposes long-horizon generation into two stages: (1) iterative RGB-D keyframe generation using a novel latent diffusion model that jointly models color and depth in a shared VAE latent space, and (2) dense video interpolation via a point-cloud-conditioned video diffusion model (ViewCrafter). The RGB-D diffusion model incorporates three key mechanisms: explicit geometry conditioning by concatenating rendered point-cloud projections and visibility masks as additional input channels to the U-Net, a two-stage training pipeline (large-scale RGB-D inpainting pre-training on 13M images from nuScenes, Argoverse2, and SA1B, followed by rendering-conditioned fine-tuning on 280k driving samples), and Warp Consistent Guidance (WCG), a classifier-guidance-style mechanism that adds the gradient of a masked MSE loss between the generated frame and rendered keyframe points to the score function at each denoising step, steering sampling toward geometrically consistent regions.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set (150 videos, 20-second generation), AutoScape achieves an overall FID of 35.1 and FVD of 359.0, reducing Vista's scores of 68.3 and 629.8 by 48.6% and 43.0% respectively. The advantage grows with horizon: at the 15–20 s split, AutoScape reaches FID 56.8 / FVD 657.4 versus Vista's 157.9 / 1614.5 (64% and 59.3% improvements). On Argoverse2 without fine-tuning, AutoScape scores [FID 49.2, FVD 317.9] compared to Vista's [80.4, 614.2], demonstrating strong cross-dataset generalizability from RGB-D pre-training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoScape outperforms both baselines across every time split and both metrics. Against Vista [19] (the prior state-of-the-art driving video generator), AutoScape reduces overall FID from 68.3 to 35.1 (−48.6%) and FVD from 629.8 to 359.0 (−43.0%); the gap widens dramatically at 15–20 s where Vista's FID degrades to 157.9 while AutoScape holds at 56.8. Against WonderJourney [82] (a general-scene keyframe method), even the driving-adapted variant (WonderJourney†) trails AutoScape by 28.6 FID and 580.6 FVD overall, and both WonderJourney variants show severe long-horizon degradation (FVD > 1500 after 10 s) whereas AutoScape remains stable. A user study (22 valid responses) confirms the quantitative advantage: participants preferred AutoScape for 3D consistency in 88.39% of comparisons against Vista and both WonderJourney variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

AutoScape builds on the latent diffusion model framework (Stable Diffusion/SD-Inpainting) and extends it with a joint RGB-D VAE and U-Net, while leveraging ControlNet for HD-map and bounding-box conditioning and ViewCrafter's point-cloud-conditioned video diffusion for the interpolation stage. It departs from prior keyframe-based scene generation (WonderJourney, WonderWorld) that relies on pretrained image inpainting models, instead proposing a purpose-built RGB-D diffusion model with explicit geometry conditioning via rendered point clouds and a novel warp-consistent guidance mechanism. The hierarchical decomposition—sparse geometrically-anchored keyframes followed by dense video interpolation—contrasts with methods like Vista that generate long driving videos purely through temporal attention in a single video diffusion model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 48.6% FID and 43.0% FVD improvements over Vista, especially the 64% FID gain in the 15–20 s window, demonstrate that explicit geometry anchoring is a more robust strategy for long-horizon consistency than relying solely on temporal modules. The finding that joint RGB-D generation (vs. separate monocular depth prediction) substantially reduces FVD (359 → 511) highlights a practical lesson: depth must be co-generated with appearance to maintain 3D coherence. Open questions include whether the 13 M-image RGB-D pre-training stage is strictly necessary or whether smaller curated driving datasets suffice, whether the approach extends beyond 20 s without further architectural changes, and whether the off-the-shelf ViewCrafter interpolation can be replaced by a more tightly coupled model for even longer sequences.
