## Problem

Image-goal navigation requires an agent to reach the exact location and orientation where a goal image was captured, but existing modular methods rely on topological graphs or 2D BEV maps that discard the 3D geometric structure needed to match fine-grained visual features. RNR-Map addresses this with a renderable NeRF representation but must project it into a 2D BEV map, forcing the goal image to be captured at a horizontal angle and limiting real-world applicability. The paper tackles the more general free-view setting where the goal image is taken by an arbitrary camera at any 6-DoF pose, with no shared intrinsics or height constraints with the agent's camera.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes a posed RGB-D video stream (I_t, D_t, T_t) at each timestep and a single goal image I_g captured by a potentially different camera A with unknown intrinsics and extrinsics; if depth or intrinsics of I_g are unavailable, they are estimated via UniDepth. The system outputs discrete navigation actions (move_forward, turn_left, turn_right, stop) at each step, while internally maintaining an incrementally growing 3DGS scene representation, a coarse target pose in a 5D sphere-based space (x, y, z, θ, φ), and a refined 6-DoF SE(3) target pose. The sphere-based pose representation exploits the observation that the camera's top frame is nearly parallel to the ground, reducing the rotation to two angles (θ, φ) rather than a full 3D rotation.

## Method

IGL-Nav builds a feed-forward monocular RGB-D-to-3DGS model (UNet encoder + Gaussian head) that predicts pixel-aligned Gaussian parameters (position, opacity, covariance, spherical harmonics) in real time, incrementally accumulating them into a scene representation without offline optimization. For coarse localization, the target image's 3D embedding is rotated according to N discretized orientations on a γ-level icosahedron subdivision, forming a 3D convolutional kernel that is convolved with the voxelized scene embedding to produce an activation map over the 5D pose space—reducing the search to a single GPU-accelerated 3D convolution. For fine localization, once the agent is near the target, a rendering-based stopper (LoFTR matching between the goal image and a 3DGS-rendered view) triggers iterative pose optimization that minimizes 3D geometric discrepancy only over confidently matched point pairs, avoiding degradation from imperfect incremental 3DGS quality. Navigation alternates between frontier-based exploration guided by the coarse activation map and FMM path planning toward the fine-estimated target pose.

## Results

On the standard NRNS image-goal navigation benchmark (Gibson dataset), IGL-Nav achieves 76.8% SR / 64.1% SPL on Straight paths and 73.5% SR / 62.4% SPL on Curved paths, outperforming the previous best (RNR-Map: 68.2/43.9 and 65.7/40.8) by 8.6 and 21.5 points in SPL respectively. On the more challenging free-view image-goal setting, IGL-Nav (supervised) reaches 57.0% SR / 48.2% SPL (Narrow FOV) and 63.3% SR / 55.0% SPL (Wide FOV), while even its zero-shot transfer (43.1/35.9 and 47.4/39.4) surpasses all baselines trained on the free-view data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IGL-Nav outperforms all seven baselines on every difficulty level and both path types in the standard image-goal setting. The largest margin is over RNR-Map (previous SOTA), with +8.6 SR and +21.5 SPL on Straight Overall, and +7.8 SR and +21.6 SPL on Curved Overall. FeudalNav is the closest competitor on SPL (55.5 vs 64.1 Straight), but IGL-Nav still leads by 8.6 SPL points. On the free-view benchmark, the gap widens dramatically: IGL-Nav's zero-shot transfer (43.1 SR Narrow) already exceeds the best supervised baseline NRNS+SLING (32.4 SR), and with free-view training IGL-Nav reaches 57.0 SR vs 32.4 for NRNS+SLING — a 24.6-point advantage. RL-based methods (DDPPO, OVRL) and modular methods (NRNS, ZSEL) all degrade severely under the free-view setting, confirming that 3D-aware representation is critical when camera constraints are removed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representation for scene modeling and the feed-forward 3DGS paradigm (PixelSplat, LARA, MVSplat) that eliminates iterative optimization, adapting both to the online, incremental setting of embodied navigation. It departs from prior image-goal navigation methods (NRNS, OVRL, RNR-Map) that rely on topological graphs or 2D BEV-projected NeRF representations, which inherently discard 3D geometric structure and constrain goal-image acquisition to horizontal viewpoints. The key conceptual departure is reformulating the 6-DoF goal-pose search as a 5-dimensional matching problem solvable via 3D convolution over voxelized scene embeddings, combined with differentiable 3DGS rendering for fine-grained pose refinement via LoFTR matching-constrained optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IGL-Nav is the first system to combine incremental feed-forward 3DGS construction with a coarse-to-fine localization pipeline for image-goal navigation, enabling real-time operation without offline scene optimization. The free-view image-goal setting (arbitrary goal camera intrinsics/extrinsics) is a practically important generalization that prior methods cannot handle, and the real-world deployment with a cellphone-captured goal demonstrates genuine sim-to-real transfer. A remaining limitation is the dependence on depth and camera intrinsics of the goal image (mitigated but not eliminated by UniDepth), and the approach has only been validated in indoor residential environments, leaving open questions about scalability to large-scale outdoor or highly dynamic scenes.
