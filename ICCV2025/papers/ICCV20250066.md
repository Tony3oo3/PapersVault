## Problem

Deep stereo matching methods require synchronized left-right camera setups with planar rectification, which breaks down for monocular sequences where the epipole falls inside the image (forward translation). Polar and cylindrical rectification methods can handle arbitrary camera motion but have not been integrated into deep networks due to unbounded image sizes and severe distortion. This paper bridges that gap by introducing a differentiable spherical epipolar rectification that is compatible with self-supervised deep stereo matching.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250066/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two RGB frames from a monocular sequence (e.g., 480×640 on TartanAir, 375×1242 on KITTI) along with their known camera poses (rotation and translation) and intrinsics. Rather than processing full images, it operates on small crops (e.g., 120×160) that are rectified into spherical patches (e.g., 256×256), and the output is an absolute metric depth map per frame recovered via triangulation from the estimated disparity. The method naturally extends to multi-frame inference (3 or 5 frames) without retraining, and depth is defined per image rather than per image pair.

## Method

The core contribution is a differentiable spherical epipolar rectification that maps rectilinear pixels to a spherical coordinate system (α, β) where the z-axis is aligned with the inter-camera translation, making epipolar lines correspond to rows in the rectified image. A novel spherical crop operation restricts rectification to small angular regions, dramatically reducing distortion compared to full-image polar rectification and enabling stable neural network training. The rectified crops are fed into Permutation Stereo (or a spherical SGM variant) to estimate disparity, which is then converted to absolute metric depth via closed-form triangulation using the known camera geometry. Training supports both supervised (L1 depth loss) and self-supervised (photometric consistency with SSIM on derectified reconstructions) paradigms, with the self-supervised variant achieving competitive absolute depth accuracy using only 0.73M parameters.

## Results

On the TartanAir carwelding test set, Spherical Stereo (supervised) achieves the best absolute depth estimation among all compared methods: Abs Rel 0.10, RMSE 0.98, RMSE log 0.12, and δ<1.25 of 0.92, using only 0.73M parameters. In the self-supervised setting (no ground-truth depth), it reaches Abs Rel 0.23, RMSE 2.39, and δ<1.25 of 0.75, remaining competitive with fully supervised baselines. On the real-world KITTI Eigen split, filtering to baselines >0.3 m yields Abs Rel 0.37 and δ<1.25 of 0.47 for Spherical Stereo, confirming proper stereo behavior in forward-motion driving scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Spherical Stereo (supervised) outperforms all five baselines on the primary error metrics: it achieves Abs Rel 0.10 vs. 0.15 for DeepSfm Revisited and MonST3R, 0.23 for Lite-Mono, 0.24 for GMDepth, and 0.33 for Depth Anything V2. On RMSE, it reaches 0.98, beating MonST3R (1.30), DeepSfm Revisited (1.42), Lite-Mono (1.41), GMDepth (2.55), and Depth Anything V2 (2.63). Notably, it does so with 0.73M parameters, roughly 10× fewer than GMDepth (7.32M) and ~800× fewer than MonST3R (577.81M). On the accuracy metric δ<1.25, Spherical Stereo leads at 0.92, ahead of DeepSfm Revisited (0.80), MonST3R (0.79), Lite-Mono (0.70), GMDepth (0.60), and Depth Anything V2 (0.38). In the self-supervised setting, Spherical Stereo (Abs Rel 0.23, δ<1.25 0.75) matches or exceeds GMDepth and Lite-Mono, which require ground-truth depth for training, demonstrating that the spherical epipolar rectification provides sufficient geometric constraint for self-supervised learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends cylindrical epipolar rectification (Roy et al., 1997) and polar coordinate rectification (Pollefeys et al., 1999; Darmon & Monasse, 2021) into a fully differentiable spherical formulation that can be embedded in a deep network. It departs from prior polar rectification work by introducing a spherical crop operation that bounds distortion to a manageable level, solving the long-standing obstacle of constantly varying rectified image dimensions that prevented integration with deep stereo matching. The stereo matching backbone is Permutation Stereo (Brousseau & Roy, 2022), and the overall framework positions monocular-sequence stereo as a natural generalization of synchronized stereo-camera matching to arbitrary camera motion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is enabling absolute metric depth from a single camera with known pose (GPS/IMU), which is directly relevant to mobile robots and AR devices that cannot afford a second camera. The spherical crop is a broadly useful technique for making any polar-coordinate rectification compatible with deep learning. Key limitations remain: the static-scene assumption breaks with independently moving objects, the focus-of-expansion point is degenerate, and forward translation (the dominant motion in driving) is the hardest geometry. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic scenes and whether the known-pose advantage can be relaxed to handle pose uncertainty more gracefully.
