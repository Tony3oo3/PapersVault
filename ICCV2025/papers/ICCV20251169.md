## Problem

Large vision-language models deliver strong perception and reasoning for self-driving but incur hundreds of milliseconds of inference latency, making them impractical for real-time closed-loop control. Existing dual-system architectures either query the large model only at low frequency or retrieve cached results from memory, leaving most online frames without timely large-model contributions. The paper asks whether a large model can be made to inform every single frame while still meeting real-time latency constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251169/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes two RGB frames separated by Δ = 0.5 s (the current frame I_t and the previous frame I_{t−Δ}), the ego vehicle's current speed, and target route waypoints. Following LLaVA's anyres strategy, each image is split into two patches and passed through a vision encoder with 2×2 spatial pooling to reduce token count by 4×. The output is a driving action structured as a path of equidistant points plus varying-distance waypoints, predicted as residuals that are summed at test time to reconstruct absolute positions.

## Method

ETA (Efficiency through Thinking Ahead) shifts the expensive large-model computation from the current frame to a previous time step and uses asynchronous batch inference to process multiple frames in parallel, trading space for time. A large encoder (CLIP-ViT-L-336px) extracts features from the previous frame I_{t−Δ}; a forecasting module then predicts the current frame's large-model features conditioned on the prior predicted action and conditioning inputs, supervised during training only by the ground-truth large-model features at time t. In parallel, a small encoder (first 8 layers of CLIP-ViT-L-336px) processes the current frame to capture unpredictable changes such as sudden braking or traffic-light transitions. The forecasted large-model features and small-model features are concatenated and fed to a 12-layer transformer action decoder, which also receives an attention-based action mask that aligns predicted waypoints with relevant image regions (supervised by projecting expert actions onto the image). This dual pathway achieves 50 ms latency—half that of the base large-model-only variant—while retaining most of its driving performance on Bench2Drive.

## Results

On the Bench2Drive benchmark, the proposed Async Model achieves a Driving Score (DS) of 69.53±1.62, a Success Rate (SR) of 38.64%, Efficiency of 184.51, and Comfort of 28.43, at a latency of 50 ms. This represents an 8.2% improvement in DS over the previous best method (DriveAdapter, 64.22) while reducing latency from 931 ms to 50 ms. The Base Model (without asynchronous inference) achieves the highest DS of 74.33±0.99 and SR of 48.33% but at 102 ms latency, establishing the performance upper bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Async Model outperforms all seven baselines on Driving Score, Success Rate, Efficiency, and Comfort simultaneously. Against the closest competitor DriveAdapter (DS 64.22, 931 ms), the Async Model gains +5.31 DS while reducing latency by 18.6×. Compared to DriveTransformer-Large (DS 63.46, 211.7 ms), the Async Model improves DS by +6.07 with 4.2× lower latency. The gap widens substantially against earlier methods: +7.09 DS over ThinkTwice (762 ms), +9.63 over TCP-traj (83 ms), and +23.72 over UniAD-Base (663.4 ms). The only baseline with lower latency is AD-MLP (3 ms), but it achieves a negligible DS of 18.05 and 0% success rate, serving only as a sanity check. The Base Model further extends the lead to +10.11 DS over DriveAdapter, confirming that the dual framework's 50 ms latency comes at a modest 4.8 DS cost relative to the 102 ms base.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dual-system (System-1/System-2) paradigm previously applied to driving by DriveVLM-Dual, ADH, and LeapAD, but departs from their parallel architecture where the large model runs at lower frequency or is retrieved from memory. Instead, ETA introduces a 'thinking ahead' paradigm that shifts the large model's computation to a prior time step and uses batched asynchronous inference, combined with a forecasting module (analogous to a latent world model) to propagate features forward and a small model to capture unpredictable changes. The base model follows the CarLLaVA/LLM4AD recipe (CLIP-ViT encoder + transformer action decoder), while the action mask mechanism is a novel alignment loss not present in prior dual-system driving work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ETA demonstrates that large-model-driven end-to-end driving can operate at 50 ms latency (20 Hz control) while achieving competitive driving scores, addressing a critical bottleneck that has kept prior SOTA methods (DriveTransformer, DriveAdapter) at 200–900+ ms. The ablation revealing that a simple attention-based action mask accounts for a ~27-point driving score drop, and that ground-truth features at test time paradoxically underperform the model's own forecasts due to distribution mismatch, point to open questions about feature-space alignment and forecasting quality. Limitations include a persistent gap to the base model in traffic sign handling and merging, and evaluation is confined to the CARLA/Bench2Drive setting, leaving open whether the thinking-ahead paradigm generalizes to real-world deployment or other embodied tasks.
