## Problem

Existing image alignment methods fail under feature-sparse textures, extreme scale and field-of-view differences, and large deformations: feature-based approaches cannot detect sufficient keypoints, intensity-based methods have limited effective range, and iterative refinement pipelines are sensitive to poor initialization. The paper addresses the need for a single framework that robustly handles all these failure modes simultaneously without relying on tentative one-to-one correspondences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252237/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source image I_s and destination image I_d of identical spatial resolution (768×768 for high-resolution, 192×192 for low-resolution settings) and outputs dense transform field parameters D_M and D_A, each of shape (H, W, 2), encoding per-pixel multiplicative scaling and additive translation. Point coordinates are normalized to [-1, 1] before processing. The transform field is initialized at a fixed 12×12 spatial grid and progressively refined to full image resolution over K autoregressive steps (K=6 for HR, K=4 for LR), after which it can warp the source image via linear or quadratic point-based warping.

## Method

ART performs coarse-to-fine autoregressive refinement of the transform field: at each step k, the spatial resolution of D_M and D_A doubles, and a Cross-Attention Layer (CAL) computes an attentive feature map by treating source multi-scale features as queries and destination features as keys/values, identifying regions that need refinement. This attentive map, concatenated with the upsampled previous-step parameters, is passed through convolutional layers to produce the next-step parameters. A multi-scale feature extractor (adapted from [1, 66]) provides features at all pyramid levels with progressive integration. Training is end-to-end with a stochastic pixel matching loss (L2 between warped source points and ground-truth destination points at each scale) plus a regularization loss (L1 between a differentiable-RANSAC-estimated homography and the ground-truth homography), with 100 randomly sampled points per step to avoid dependence on fixed grids or keypoint detectors.

## Results

On the challenging KBSMC retinal dataset (SFI-UWFI pairs with ×1–×4 scale differences), ART achieves a 64.71% Acceptable rate and 40.1 mAUC, substantially outperforming the next-best method GeoFormer (36.10%, 24.1). On in-domain retinal datasets FIRE and FLORI21, ART reaches 99.25% and 100% Acceptable rates with mAUCs of 78.5 and 92.5. On scene datasets, ART achieves the best mAUC on HPatches (78.6) and the lowest ACE on GoogleEarth (0.17) and GoogleMap (0.19), while remaining competitive with RoMa on 3D scene benchmarks (MegaDepth-1500: 74.9 vs. 75.2; ScanNet-1500: 51.1 vs. 52.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ART's most dramatic win is on KBSMC, where it achieves a 64.71% Acceptable rate versus 36.10% for GeoFormer [34] and 34.76% for SuperRetina [35]—a gap of roughly 28–30 percentage points over the strongest baselines. On in-domain retinal tasks (FIRE, FLORI21), ART ties RetinaRegNet [50] at 99.25% and 100% Acceptable but edges it out on mAUC (78.5 vs. 77.9; 92.5 vs. 86.8). On the planar HPatches benchmark, ART (78.6 mAUC) narrowly surpasses RoMa [18] (78.4) and LightGlue [33] (77.5), while on 3D scene datasets (MegaDepth-1500, ScanNet-1500) ART trails RoMa by 0.3 and 0.9 mAUC points respectively. On low-resolution scene datasets, ART dominates: it reduces ACE on GoogleEarth from 0.60 (MCNet [66]) to 0.17 and on GoogleMap from 0.23 to 0.19, while tying MCNet on MSCOCO (0.03). Feature-based methods (SuperPoint, SuperGlue, LoFTR) and iterative refinement methods (DLKFM, IHN, MCNet) are consistently outperformed by 10–50+ points on Acceptable rate across all retinal datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

ART builds on the multi-scale feature extraction architecture from Bai et al. [1] and MCNet [66], repurposing their pyramid design for iterative transform refinement rather than single-shot homography prediction. Conceptually, it departs from the three dominant families in image alignment—feature-based matching (SuperGlue, LoFTR, GeoFormer), intensity-based optimization (ISTN, REMPE), and iterative deep homography estimation (DLKFM, IHN, MCNet)—by replacing global homography or dense flow prediction with a locally linear point-wise transform field (multiplicative + additive) refined auto-regressively from coarse to fine. The cross-attention layer that conditions each refinement step on global appearance cues is the key architectural departure from prior iterative methods, which typically rely on fixed or heuristic sampling without explicit attention-based region selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ART's auto-regressive design offers a practical efficiency advantage (0.16 s, 261 MB vs. ~1.1 s for LoFTR/GeoFormer) while achieving the largest reported gap on the challenging KBSMC retinal dataset (64.71% vs. 36.10% Acceptable rate), suggesting the approach is particularly well-suited to feature-sparse, large-scale-difference registration in medical imaging. The ablation showing that the network converges in fewer steps than the full K iterations implies the method can be adapted to real-time or resource-constrained settings. However, the fixed input resolutions (768×768 / 192×192) and the modest margins on 3D scene benchmarks (MegaDepth, ScanNet) relative to RoMa leave open whether the approach scales to arbitrarily large images or truly outperforms dense-matching transformers in general-purpose 3D registration. Future work on adaptive sampling depth and resolution-agnostic inference would address the most immediate limitation.
