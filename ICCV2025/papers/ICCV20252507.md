## Problem

Consumer-grade dual-fisheye 360° cameras (e.g., Insta360, Ricoh360) produce panoramas with visible stitching artifacts due to physical lens separation and angular distortions, yet existing omnidirectional 3DGS methods assume perfectly calibrated spherical inputs. Prior calibration approaches for 360° rendering address only lens distortions while neglecting the inter-camera gap, leaving a critical gap in achieving truly seamless novel view synthesis from real-world captures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252507/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes equirectangular (ERP) 360° panoramic images as input, operating directly on the standard ERP format without requiring raw fisheye pairs. During training, it internally generates synthetic imperfect panoramas by applying learned dual-fisheye distortions to the ideal Gaussian scene and compares them against the actual (artifact-laden) captures. At inference, the distortion module is deactivated, so the output is a clean, distortion-free 360° rendering from the ideally calibrated Gaussian representation, along with the learned calibration parameters (inter-camera gap and angular distortion fields).

## Method

The core contribution is a joint calibration framework embedded within the 3DGS pipeline that simultaneously optimizes Gaussian scene parameters and two types of calibration variables. First, learnable translation vectors (ΔT_F, ΔT_B) shift each Gaussian splat to align with the actual optical centers of the front and back fisheye cameras, compensating for the physical inter-camera gap. Second, learnable rotation embedding grids (ΔR_F, ΔR_B of shape M×N×3 over spherical coordinates) are bilinearly sampled to apply ray-dependent angular distortions that emulate stitching-induced misalignment. During training, these distortions are applied to the ideal Gaussians to synthesize realistic imperfect 360° images, which are then photometrically matched against the actual captures via the standard 360° Gaussian rasterizer (from OmniGS). At inference, the distortion module is simply switched off, yielding seamless renderings at no additional cost while the underlying Gaussians remain in an ideally calibrated space. A total variation loss enforces smoothness on the angular distortion grids to prevent spurious rotation variations.

## Results

On the synthetic OmniBlender dataset, Seam360GS achieves 34.99 dB PSNR, 0.947 SSIM, and 0.048 LPIPS with only 308K Gaussians, outperforming the best baseline (OmniGS) by 2.57 dB in PSNR. On the real-world Ricoh360 (EgoNeRF) dataset, it reaches 26.96 dB PSNR and 0.852 SSIM, and on 360Roam (Insta360) it achieves 26.10 dB PSNR and 0.829 SSIM, consistently leading all baselines. In the synthetic calibration experiment, the method recovers inter-camera gaps with a mean absolute error of just 0.115 cm, compared to 3.30 cm for OmniNeRF. At inference, the method renders at 136 FPS (Office scene) versus 96.5 FPS for OmniGS, a 30% speedup, while using fewer Gaussians (265K vs. 600K).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Seam360GS outperforms all five baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against the strongest 3DGS competitor OmniGS, it gains +0.90 dB PSNR on Ricoh360 (26.96 vs. 26.06) and +1.02 dB on 360Roam (26.10 vs. 25.08), while also reducing LPIPS from 0.125 to 0.105 and 0.145 to 0.144 respectively. ODGS and OP43DGS trail by 2–4 dB in PSNR on real-world scenes and require substantially more Gaussians (971K–1276K vs. 447K–621K for Seam360GS). The NeRF-based EgoNeRF baseline is the weakest, achieving only 23.02 dB PSNR on 360Roam versus 26.10 for the proposed method. In the dedicated calibration experiment (Table 1), Seam360GS recovers inter-camera gaps with 0.115 cm MAE, a 28× improvement over OmniNeRF's 3.30 cm, despite training for less than half the time (90 min vs. >180 min). The ablation (Table 3) confirms that removing the angular distortion component costs ~0.65 dB PSNR on average, while removing the gap translation costs ~0.18 dB, indicating lens distortion correction is the more critical factor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting paradigm (Kerbl et al., 2023) and its omnidirectional adaptations (OmniGS, 360GS, ODGS, OP43DGS) by explicitly modeling the dual-fisheye stitching imperfections—inter-camera gap and angular distortion—that prior 360° GS methods implicitly assume away. It draws on the camera-calibration-in-neural-rendering line (NeRF--, SCNeRF, COLMAP-Free 3DGS) but departs from SC-OmniGS, which corrects lens distortion in the 2D image domain, by instead translating and rotating 3D Gaussian primitives in scene space to emulate both the physical lens separation and the ray-dependent angular errors introduced during panoramic stitching. The result is a unified framework where calibration variables are jointly optimized with Gaussian parameters during training and simply deactivated at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Because virtually all consumer 360° content (YouTube, VR platforms, robotics datasets) originates from dual-fisheye systems with non-negligible stitching artifacts, a method that absorbs these imperfections during training and produces clean renderings at inference has broad practical relevance for VR, autonomous navigation, and 3D reconstruction pipelines. The paper's stated limitation—degraded performance in textureless regions such as uniform walls and sky—remains an open challenge, as does extending the approach to real-time or streaming settings. The 30× calibration accuracy improvement over OmniNeRF on synthetic data (0.11 cm vs. 3.3 cm MAE) is encouraging, but the real-world PSNR gains over the strongest baseline (OmniGS) are modest (~1 dB), suggesting the practical ceiling may be constrained by the quality of the input captures themselves.
