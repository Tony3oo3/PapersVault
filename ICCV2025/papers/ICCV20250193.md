## Problem

Long-form video understanding requires handling thousands of interrelated frames while capturing both sequential episodic events and overarching semantic themes, yet existing approaches largely extend short-video techniques (pooling, 3D convolutions) that fail to account for the unique temporal and narrative structure of long videos. The paper identifies a gap in efficiently compressing temporal redundancy while preserving both micro-level event continuity and macro-level conceptual context within tight memory and latency budgets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250193/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a long video of arbitrary length (demonstrated on 14k-frame clips) and free-form text instructions. The video is consumed in non-overlapping windows encoded by a frozen ViT-G/14, and the output is a natural language response generated by a frozen Vicuna-7B LLM (for VQA) or a class label (for classification). Notably, HERMES processes only ~0.7% of frames in qualitative evaluations while outperforming baselines that use 20× more frames.

## Method

HERMES introduces two plug-in modules inspired by cognitive memory: (1) ECO, an online episodic compressor that maintains a fixed-size buffer of E episodes and, when full, iteratively merges the most cosine-similar pair of frame features by averaging, reducing N frames to K≪N while preserving temporal continuity; an Episodic Q-Former applies the same merging logic to learned queries. (2) SeTR, a semantics retriever that partitions frames into two groups (every k-th frame vs. the rest), computes cross-group dot-product similarities, and merges each frame with its most similar counterpart, reducing N to N/k frames; a hierarchical frame-to-sequence Q-Former then consolidates these into video-level semantic queries. The episodic and semantic query vectors are concatenated, linearly projected into the LLM's embedding space, and fed to a frozen Vicuna-7B. Both modules are architecture-agnostic and can replace or augment the memory mechanisms of existing models (MA-LMM, LongVA, LLaVA-OneVision), reducing inference latency by up to 43% and GPU memory by 46% while improving accuracy.

## Results

On MovieChat-1k zero-shot VQA, HERMES achieves 78.6% accuracy and 4.23 score, surpassing the previous best (MovieChat at 63.7%) by 14.9%. On long-form video classification, HERMES reaches 70.3% average accuracy on LVU (vs. 63.0% for MA-LMM, a 7.3% gain), 95.2% on Breakfast, and 93.5% on COIN. As a plug-in module, ECO reduces inference latency by up to 43% and GPU memory by up to 46% while maintaining or improving accuracy across MA-LMM, LongVA, and LLaVA-OneVision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HERMES outperforms all 14 baselines across every evaluated benchmark. On MovieChat-1k zero-shot VQA, it beats MovieChat [32] by 14.9% (78.6% vs. 63.7%), VideoChat [20] by 18.4%, Video-ChatGPT [22] by 19.9%, and Video-LLaMA [49] by 22.3%. On LVU classification, HERMES surpasses the previous best MA-LMM [14] by 7.3% (70.3% vs. 63.0%), and leads state-space models S5 [41] (59.2%), TranS4mer [16] (54.5%), and VIS4mer [15] (53.7%) by 11.1–16.6%. On Breakfast, HERMES improves over MA-LMM by 2.2% (95.2% vs. 93.0%) and over S5 by 4.5%. In plug-in experiments, ECO integrated into MA-LMM [14] yields +3.4% accuracy with 43% less latency, and SeTR adds a further +3.8% with only 1.5% extra time; on LongVA [51] and LLaVA-OneVision [19], ECO reduces latency by 30–35% while matching or slightly exceeding baseline accuracy. Against ToMe [3] as a compression baseline, HERMES achieves 13.8% higher accuracy at comparable or faster inference speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

HERMES builds on the Q-Former architecture from InstructBLIP for its episodic query aggregation and draws on the episodic/semantic memory distinction from cognitive science (Tulving) to motivate a dual-module design. It departs from prior long-video approaches that simply extend short-video techniques (pooling, 3D convolutions) by explicitly separating temporal episode compression (ECO) from high-level semantic retrieval (SeTR), and positions itself against token-compression methods like ToMe and memory-augmented models like MA-LMM. The framework uses a frozen ViT-G/14 encoder and Vicuna-7B LLM, treating the video understanding pipeline as a compression-and-retrieval problem rather than a sequence-modeling one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design—where ECO and SeTR can be dropped into existing SOTA models (MA-LMM, LongVA, LLaVA-OneVision) to improve accuracy while cutting latency by up to 43% and memory by 46%—makes this practically relevant for deployment of long-video understanding systems. However, the heuristic nature of both modules (greedy cosine-similarity merging, fixed stride-based frame grouping) limits their ability to capture subtle temporal nuances, and the independent operation of ECO and SeTR may introduce redundancy. Open questions include whether these heuristics can be learned end-to-end, whether the approach scales to much larger LLMs or video lengths, and whether the 20-episode / 20%-keep-ratio sweet spots generalize beyond the tested datasets.
