## Problem

Existing geometric constraints for 3D point cloud registration suffer from three key limitations: pairwise constraints are ambiguous in associating multiple correspondences, static compatibility thresholds lack flexibility across varying input conditions, and explicit graph construction is highly sensitive to noise from matching and measurement errors. No prior method dynamically learns high-order geometric constraints from hypergraphs to robustly guide hypothesis generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252332/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes source and target point clouds, extracts local features (FCGF or FPFH), and establishes an initial correspondence set via nearest-neighbor search. Notably, the network input is the 3D coordinates of correspondences rather than learned features, making HyperGCT descriptor-agnostic and applicable to any feature extractor. The output is a 6-DoF rigid transformation (rotation + translation), per-correspondence inlier confidence scores, and a dynamically optimized hypergraph incidence matrix that encodes high-order consistency among inliers.

## Method

HyperGCT constructs an initial hypergraph where each hyperedge groups vertices connected to a central correspondence in a second-order rigid-distance compatibility graph, using a dynamically computed threshold to handle varying inlier ratios. The core learning module stacks hypergraph convolution blocks (vertex-to-hyperedge aggregation followed by hyperedge-to-vertex aggregation with a NonLocal enhancement) interleaved with hypergraph update blocks that refine the incidence matrix via top-K vertex-hyperedge similarity, progressively narrowing the neighborhood from broad information exchange to focused inlier interactions. A joint loss combining binary cross-entropy classification, spectral matching, and graph structure supervision trains the network end-to-end. For hypothesis generation, a Graph-Filtering NMS selects reliable seeds, and guided iterative sampling from each seed's hyperedge produces diverse candidate transformations evaluated by MAE-based scoring.

## Results

On 3DMatch, HyperGCT achieves the highest Registration Recall (RR) of 85.89% with FPFH and 94.45% with FCGF, outperforming all baselines. On the challenging low-overlap 3DLoMatch benchmark, HyperGCT reaches 63.73% RR (FCGF), surpassing the next-best MAC (60.08%) by 3.65 points. On KITTI-LC, where outlier ratios increase with translation distance, HyperGCT leads on all three subsets (97.05%, 72.46%, 25.95% for 0-10m, 10-20m, 20-30m), with the largest margin over MAC at 20-30m (+2.22%). Generalization from 3DMatch to ETH yields 45.72% average RR (FPFH) and 66.62% (FCGF), both the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyperGCT outperforms all seven registration baselines (SC²-PCR, MAC, PointDSC, PG-Net, VBReg, Hunter, 3DPCP) on the primary RR metric across every dataset. The most notable margins are on 3DLoMatch (63.73% vs. 60.08% for MAC, +3.65%) and KITTI-LC 20-30m (25.95% vs. 23.73% for MAC, +2.22%), where high outlier ratios stress geometric constraints. On 3DMatch with FCGF, 3DPCP achieves higher inlier precision (89.65% vs. 80.72%) and F1 (91.25% vs. 83.75%), but HyperGCT still leads in overall registration recall (94.45% vs. 92.54%), indicating its hypothesis generation is more effective despite slightly weaker outlier filtering. In the hypothesis correctness comparison (Table 12), HyperGCT dramatically outperforms RANSAC (87.62% vs. 10.17% on 3DMatch/FCGF) and SAC-COT (87.62% vs. 9.35%), validating that dynamic hypergraph-guided sampling produces far more valid pose hypotheses than random or triplet sampling. For generalization to ETH, HyperGCT trained on 3DMatch achieves 45.72% avg RR (FPFH), beating 3DPCP (41.65%) and Hunter (37.31%), while deep-learned methods like PointDSC (14.45%) and PG-Net (29.31%) degrade severely, confirming HyperGCT's descriptor-agnostic and modality-robust design.

## Relation to Prior Work

HyperGCT extends the hypergraph neural network framework (Feng et al., AAAI 2019) to 3D registration, departing from both traditional geometric-constraint methods (RANSAC, SAC-COT's triplet sampling, MAC's maximal cliques) and deep-learned inlier classifiers (PointDSC, 3DPCP-Net). The closest prior work is Hunter (Yao et al., TPAMI 2023), which also employs a hypergraph for high-order consistency but uses a static topology; HyperGCT's key conceptual departure is the dynamic, continuously updated hypergraph structure via HyperGNN-style vertex-hyperedge feature aggregation and top-K re-selection. The initial hypergraph construction borrows MAC's second-order rigid-distance compatibility measure, and the training objective reuses PointDSC's NonLocal module and spectral matching loss, positioning the contribution as a new architectural treatment of an existing problem rather than a new formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dynamic hypergraph formulation offers a principled way to handle the long-standing tension between explicit graph construction (sensitive to matching/measurement noise) and purely learned inlier scoring (weak on high outlier ratios), and the demonstrated cross-dataset generalization (3DMatch-trained model performing near-identically on KITTI-10m) is practically valuable for deployment where per-dataset retraining is costly. The authors acknowledge that the GF-NMS seeding module may not retrieve enough correct matches, leaving open the question of whether a learned or adaptive seeding strategy could further close the gap on low-overlap benchmarks like 3DLoMatch. More broadly, the implicit, parameter-insensitive hypergraph update mechanism could transfer to other correspondence-based tasks (e.g., multi-view stereo, 4D registration) where high-order consistency matters.
