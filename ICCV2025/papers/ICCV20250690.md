## Problem

While camera control has been extensively studied for text-to-video and image-to-video generation, re-shooting a given video with novel camera trajectories remains under-explored due to the additional constraints of maintaining multi-frame appearance consistency and dynamic synchronization. Existing video-to-video approaches suffer from domain gaps (GCD), per-video optimization overhead (ReCapture), or reliance on inaccurate 3D point tracking for 4D reconstruction, limiting their practical utility on in-the-wild videos.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video (f frames at 384×672 resolution), a target camera trajectory specified as per-frame rotation and translation matrices (cam_t ∈ R^{f×3×4}), and a text prompt, and produces a re-rendered video at the novel camera trajectory while preserving the source video's dynamic scene. Notably, the source video's own camera parameters are not required as input—the model infers them implicitly—making the method practical for real-world footage where accurate SfM is unreliable. As a byproduct of the training strategy, the model also supports camera-controlled T2V and I2V generation by dropping source frames during training.

## Method

ReCamMaster fine-tunes a pre-trained text-to-video latent diffusion model (DiT with 3D VAE under Rectified Flow) by introducing a frame-dimension conditioning mechanism: source and target video latents are each patchified into tokens and concatenated along the frame dimension, doubling the token sequence so that standard 3D self-attention naturally mediates spatio-temporal interaction between conditional and target tokens without any additional cross-view attention layers. Camera control is injected via a lightweight learnable encoder (a single fully-connected layer mapping 12-dim extrinsics to the feature dimension) whose output is added to spatial-attention features in every transformer block. Training freezes all base-model parameters except the camera encoder and 3D-attention layers, applies moderate noise (200–500 steps) to the conditional latent to bridge the synthetic-to-real domain gap, and randomly drops source frames (20% T2V, 20% I2V) to preserve generative capability. The model is trained on a 136K-video multi-camera synchronized dataset rendered in Unreal Engine 5 across 40 environments and 122K diverse camera trajectories, carefully curated to mimic real-world filming characteristics.

## Results

On the WebVid evaluation set (1000 videos, 10 camera trajectories), ReCamMaster achieves FID 57.10, FVD 122.74, and CLIP-T 34.53 for visual quality, substantially outperforming the best baseline DaS (FID 63.25, FVD 159.60). For camera accuracy, ReCamMaster attains RotErr 1.22° and TransErr 4.85, the lowest among all methods. For view synchronization, ReCamMaster reaches 906.03K matched pixels and FVD-V 90.38, nearly doubling the matched-pixel count of baselines (619–639K) and reducing FVD-V by 41–75%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReCamMaster outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against GCD [45], the largest gap appears in FVD (122.74 vs. 367.32, a 67% reduction) and FVD-V (90.38 vs. 365.75, a 75% reduction), reflecting GCD's limited generalization from its Kubric-simulator training domain. Against Trajectory-Attention [54], ReCamMaster improves FID by 12.11 points and matched pixels by 46% (906K vs. 619K), while also achieving lower rotation error (1.22° vs. 2.18°). Against DaS [15], the closest competitor, ReCamMaster still leads by 6.15 FID, 36.86 FVD, and 272.5K matched pixels, with the advantage most pronounced in view synchronization (FVD-V 90.38 vs. 154.25). On VBench metrics (Table 2), ReCamMaster also leads in Aesthetic Quality (42.70 vs. 38.21–39.86), Imaging Quality (53.97 vs. 41.56–51.55), and Subject Consistency (92.05 vs. 88.94–90.60), though DaS edges it slightly on Temporal Flickering (97.44 vs. 97.36).

## Relation to Prior Work

ReCamMaster builds on the Rectified Flow training framework (Esser et al., ICML 2024) and the dedicated camera encoder design from CameraCtrl (He et al., 2024), adapting both to a video-to-video re-rendering setting. It departs from prior camera-controlled video-to-video methods—GCD (channel-dimension conditioning on Kubric-simulated data), ReCapture (per-video LoRA optimization), and DaS/TrajectoryCrafter (4D point-tracking + post-optimization)—by proposing a frame-dimension token concatenation mechanism that lets the base DiT's 3D self-attention naturally mediate source-target interaction without extra modules. The paper also positions against its own prior work SyncamMaster (view-dimension conditioning for multi-view generation), showing that the simpler frame-axis strategy is strictly superior for the re-shooting task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frame-dimension conditioning is a minimal, architecture-agnostic trick (doubling sequence length) that could be adopted across DiT-based video generators for any conditional task, and the 136K-video Multi-Cam dataset with 122K trajectories is a substantial public resource for camera-controlled generation and 4D reconstruction research. Practical applications in stabilization, super-resolution, and outpainting demonstrate the method's versatility beyond the core re-shooting task. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer videos or higher resolutions without prohibitive compute cost (the token doubling is a real overhead), whether the UE5-to-wild domain gap can be further closed, and whether the conditioning mechanism transfers to non-DiT architectures.
