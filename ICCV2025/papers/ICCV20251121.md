## Problem

Large vision-language models face a dual bottleneck: quadratic attention computation during prefill and KV cache memory pressure during decoding, which is especially severe for high-resolution images and long videos producing tens of thousands of visual tokens. Existing sparse attention and KV cache compression methods typically address only one of these bottlenecks, rely on fixed token retention ratios that ignore layer-specific and task-specific attention patterns, and lack a unified framework that jointly optimizes both phases.

## Input / Output

ZipVL operates as a plug-and-play inference framework on existing LVLMs (LLaVA, LLaVA-Next, LongVA) without retraining. It accepts standard image or video inputs (up to 128K tokens for 128-frame videos) concatenated with a text prompt, and produces a generated text response. The only additional hyperparameter is a threshold τ (e.g., 0.96 or 0.975) controlling the fraction of attention scores to preserve, which implicitly determines the per-layer token retention ratio.

## Method

ZipVL introduces a layer-wise adaptive ratio assignment scheme: for each attention layer, it computes accumulated attention scores per token, sorts them in descending order, and selects the minimum number of tokens p whose cumulative score exceeds τ × n, where n is the total token count. This makes the retention ratio dynamic across layers and tasks—simpler tasks yield fewer important tokens while complex tasks retain more. Important tokens are then identified via normalized attention scores (column sums divided by non-zero counts) and top-k selection. In the prefill phase, attention is computed exclusively over the p important tokens using FlashAttention, reducing complexity from O(n²) to O(p²) without custom kernels. In the decoding phase, the KV cache is compressed by discarding entries for less important tokens, reducing memory fetch overhead. Full attention scores are approximated using a small fixed subset of probe tokens to avoid the cost of computing the complete attention matrix.

## Results

On LLaVA-Next-13B, ZipVL (τ=0.975) retains only 36.8% of tokens while achieving 80.4 on VQAv2 (vs. 80.9 full), 58.5 on ChartQA (vs. 66.2 full), and 1574 on MME (vs. 1570 full), with a minimal 0.5% drop on VQAv2. On the VideoMME benchmark with 128-frame videos, ZipVL achieves 82.3% attention FLOPs reduction and 57.9% KV cache reduction while matching the full model's overall score of 52.6. In deployment, ZipVL delivers a 2.3× reduction in prefill-phase latency at 128K sequence length and a 2.8× improvement in decoding throughput at 16K sequence length compared to FlashAttention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZipVL consistently outperforms FastV [4] across all five image benchmarks and three model architectures, with the gap most pronounced on ChartQA (58.5 vs. 51.6 on LLaVA-Next-13B) where FastV's fixed 50% token dropping causes severe degradation. Against HiRED [2], ZipVL wins on VQAv2, ChartQA, and GQA while HiRED matches or slightly edges ZipVL on MME for LLaVA-Next-13B (1570 vs. 1574), though HiRED uses a higher token ratio (40% vs. 36.8%). On the VideoMME benchmark, ZipVL achieves the highest overall score (52.6) while delivering 82.3% attention FLOPs reduction and 57.9% KV cache reduction, surpassing MInference [18] (77.1% FLOPs, 0% KV reduction) and QK-sparse [30] (46.9% FLOPs, 0% KV reduction) on both efficiency metrics. For KV cache compression on LLaMA3-8B/GSM8k, ZipVL achieves a 6.18× compression ratio with 54.06% accuracy, outperforming ZipCache [15] (4.69× at 53.75%). In deployment, ZipVL matches FlashAttention [7] latency at short sequences (8K) but achieves 2.3× lower TTFT at 128K and 2.8× higher decoding throughput at 16K, while FastV and MInference either OOM or are significantly slower at longer sequence lengths.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZipVL sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: structured sparse attention for the prefill phase (FastV, HiRED) and KV cache compression for the decoding phase (ZipCache, H2O, KIVI). Its key conceptual departure is replacing the fixed, layer-uniform token retention ratio used by all prior methods with a layer-wise adaptive ratio derived from the empirical distribution of attention scores, so that each layer retains only as many tokens as needed to preserve a threshold fraction (τ) of total attention mass. By using the same normalized-attention-score criterion for both token selection in sparse attention and KV cache eviction, ZipVL unifies what were previously treated as separate optimization problems into a single inference pipeline that plugs directly into FlashAttention without custom kernels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployment: 2.3× prefill latency reduction and 2.8× decoding throughput improvement with under 1% accuracy loss on VQAv2 make long-context LVLM inference substantially more tractable on a single GPU, and the method's compatibility with FlashAttention lowers the adoption barrier. The single hyperparameter τ transferring across model sizes (7B, 13B) and task types (VQAv2, ChartQA, VideoMME) without per-model tuning is a useful robustness property. However, the paper explicitly notes that only the attention module is sparsified while MLP layers remain dense, leaving a clear avenue for further speedup; it also remains to be seen whether the adaptive-ratio idea composes cleanly with other compression axes (e.g., quantization of the retained tokens) at scale, and whether the approximation via a fixed probe-token subset degrades gracefully on highly non-stationary inputs.
