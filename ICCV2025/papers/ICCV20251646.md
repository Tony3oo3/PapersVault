## Problem

Existing video inpainting methods require manually annotated binary masks to specify corrupted regions, a process that is labor-intensive, expensive, and often infeasible when boundaries between corrupted and valid regions are blurred. This paper formulates the first blind video inpainting task, which learns a direct mapping from corrupted video to inpainted results without any corrupted-region annotation, addressing both 'where to inpaint' and 'how to inpaint' simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251646/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a corrupted video sequence of T frames as the sole input, with no mask or any other auxiliary annotation provided. It produces a spatially and temporally consistent inpainted video sequence, while internally generating per-frame binary masks that identify corrupted regions. The corruption is assumed to arise from external factors (e.g., bullets, watermarks, scratches) or internal undesired objects, and the model must infer both the location and content of the missing regions purely from visual and temporal context.

## Method

BVINet is an end-to-end framework comprising a Mask Prediction Network (MPNet) and a Video Completion Network (VCNet) trained jointly. MPNet uses a short-term encoder-decoder with Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) downsampling to detect semantic-discontinuous regions per frame, followed by a long-term sequence-to-sequence transformer that refines masks across T frames using spatial-temporal affinity computed within local response windows. VCNet is a wavelet sparse transformer that decomposes query/key/value features into low- and high-frequency components via DWT, then applies a two-branch self-attention mechanism combining dense self-attention (DSA) and ReLU-gated sparse self-attention (SSA) to selectively aggregate relevant context while suppressing noise and irrelevant features. A consistency loss enforces that the binarized difference between the completed frame and the corrupted frame matches both the predicted mask and the ground-truth mask, creating mutual constraint between the two sub-networks. The authors also construct a dedicated dataset of 2,400 synthesized clips (free-form strokes filled with real image patches and boundary-smoothed via iterative Gaussian filtering) plus 1,250 real-world bullet-removal clips to avoid the shape, content, and border priors present in existing inpainting datasets.

## Results

Under the blind video inpainting setting, BVINet achieves PSNR 30.528 / SSIM 0.9088 / LPIPS 0.6556 on YouTube-VOS and PSNR 30.961 / SSIM 0.9107 / LPIPS 0.7338 on DAVIS, outperforming the strongest blind baseline (MPNet+WaveFormer) by 1.34 dB PSNR and 0.061 LPIPS on YouTube-VOS. Under the non-blind setting, the VCNet component alone reaches PSNR 34.107 / SSIM 0.9521 / LPIPS 0.2145 on YouTube-VOS and PSNR 34.936 / SSIM 0.9561 / LPIPS 0.2706 on DAVIS, surpassing the best non-blind baseline WaveFormer by 0.84 dB PSNR and 0.079 LPIPS on YouTube-VOS. The blind model also achieves comparable performance to the suboptimal non-blind baseline E2FGVI, validating the feasibility of annotation-free video inpainting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the blind setting, BVINet outperforms all six MPNet+X baselines across every metric on both datasets; the largest gap is against MPNet+WaveFormer (the strongest blind baseline), with +1.34 dB PSNR and −0.060 LPIPS on YouTube-VOS, and +1.17 dB PSNR and −0.033 LPIPS on DAVIS. The weaker blind baselines (MPNet+CPVINet, MPNet+STTN, MPNet+FuseFormer) lag by 5+ dB PSNR, indicating that the video completion backbone is critical. In the non-blind setting, VCNet surpasses all eleven non-blind baselines, with the closest competitor being WaveFormer [81] (33.264 vs. 34.107 PSNR on YouTube-VOS) and DiffuEraser [25] (31.625 PSNR). Notably, BVINet under the blind setting (30.528 PSNR on YouTube-VOS) approaches the performance of the non-blind E2FGVI [41] (30.064 PSNR), demonstrating that the annotation-free pipeline incurs only a modest quality penalty. For the real-world bullet removal task, BVINet produces cleaner results than both OGNet [90] and RAVUNet [91] without requiring any mask input.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by transplanting the blind image inpainting paradigm (where no corruption mask is provided) into the video domain, a setting that had no prior formulation. It departs from all existing video inpainting methods—which assume a binary mask indicating corrupted regions—and from one-shot variants that still require annotating a single frame. Technically, it repurposes DWT-based downsampling from noise-robust CNNs [75,76] for mask prediction, adapts adaptive sparse self-attention [80] into a wavelet-sparse transformer for video completion, and introduces a consistency loss that couples the two sub-networks in a mutually constraining fashion. The customized dataset, using free-form strokes filled with natural image patches and iterative Gaussian smoothing, deliberately removes the shape, content, and border priors that plague existing inpainting benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for per-frame mask annotation, this work addresses a major practical bottleneck in video inpainting deployment, particularly for real-world scenarios like bullet removal where boundaries are blurred and annotation is infeasible. The consistency loss providing a principled coupling between mask prediction and content completion is a reusable design pattern. However, the paper focuses only on externally-introduced corruption (scratches, watermarks, bullets) and does not address object removal or occlusion, which remain the more common real-world use cases. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer sequences, higher resolutions, and more diverse corruption types, and whether the remaining ~3 dB PSNR gap to non-blind SOTA can be closed.
