## Problem

Existing driving scene reconstruction methods that incorporate generative priors (e.g., ReconDreamer, DriveDreamer4D) mix synthesized novel-view data with real sensor observations to train shared Gaussian parameters, but overlook the inherent domain gap between these two sources. This causes significant quality degradation on the original trajectory and exacerbates poor reconstruction of structured elements like the ground surface, where the camera's optical axis is parallel to the road, making traditional 3DGS modeling particularly fragile.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-frame RGB images, multi-frame fused LiDAR point clouds (used to initialize the ground model with fixed positions and to provide depth supervision at novel viewpoints), camera poses for both original and novel trajectories, and dynamic object masks for filtering depth. The output is a decomposed 3D Gaussian scene representation (ground, non-ground static background, and per-object dynamic Gaussians) that renders images for both the original trajectory (directly from base Gaussian parameters) and novel trajectories (after NTDNet deformation), evaluated via PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS on original views and NTA-IoU/NTL-IoU/FID on shifted trajectories.

## Method

ReconDreamer++ decomposes the driving scene into three independently modeled components: a ground model with fixed LiDAR-derived positions and fixed Gaussian count (optimizing only appearance and covariance), a fully optimized non-ground background, and per-object dynamic Gaussians transformed via ego-vehicle pose. The key architectural contribution is the Novel Trajectory Deformable Network (NTDNet), which computes a normalized delta pose between original and novel trajectories, feeds it through a pose feature MLP, and combines it with a temporal field MLP (conditioned on timestep and Gaussian parameters via positional encoding) to predict per-Gaussian parameter deltas applied exclusively to novel-trajectory rendering. Depth supervision is introduced for novel views by projecting multi-frame fused static point clouds into the novel camera and masking out dynamic objects, providing geometric constraints that mitigate the domain gap. The ReconDreamer two-stage pipeline (DriveRestorer for video restoration) is retained, with restored novel-trajectory videos used to further refine the reconstruction model under a combined RGB + SSIM + depth loss.

## Results

On the Waymo dataset, ReconDreamer++ achieves 36.29 PSNR / 0.9573 SSIM / 0.1076 LPIPS on the original trajectory, matching Street Gaussians (36.50 / 0.9570 / 0.1149) while improving LPIPS by 6.4%. On novel trajectories with a 3 m lane shift, it reaches 0.572 NTA-IoU, 57.06 NTL-IoU, and 72.02 FID, outperforming ReconDreamer by 6.1% (NTA-IoU), 4.5% (NTL-IoU), and 23.0% (FID). At a 6 m shift, gains over ReconDreamer grow to 4.7% NTA-IoU, 7.6% NTL-IoU, and 25.0% FID. On PandaSet, ReconDreamer++ achieves the lowest FID across all shift conditions (e.g., 71.7 at Lane 3 m vs. 97.5 for UniSim and 93.9 for NeuRAD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Street Gaussians, ReconDreamer++ matches PSNR/SSIM on the original trajectory (36.29 vs 36.50; 0.9573 vs 0.9570) and improves LPIPS by 6.4% (0.1076 vs 0.1149), while dominating on all novel-trajectory metrics (e.g., 0.572 vs 0.498 NTA-IoU at 3 m). Versus its direct predecessor ReconDreamer, ReconDreamer++ wins on every metric: original-trajectory PSNR improves from 34.31 to 36.29, and novel-trajectory FID drops from 93.56 to 72.02 at 3 m (23.0% gain). FreeVS suffers severe original-trajectory degradation (26.59 PSNR) due to its LiDAR-dependent generation, and ReconDreamer++ surpasses it on both original and novel trajectories. DriveDreamer4D with PVG is outperformed across all settings, with particularly large FID gaps at 6 m (111.92 vs 261.81). On PandaSet, ReconDreamer++ achieves the best FID in every shift condition, beating UniSim by 25.8 points at Lane 3 m (71.7 vs 97.5) and NeuRAD by 22.2 points (71.7 vs 93.9). On EUVS, ReconDreamer++ improves over ReconDreamer by 1.9% PSNR, 1.7% SSIM, and 7.0% LPIPS, and substantially over Street Gaussians (20.52 vs 15.08 PSNR).

## Relation to Prior Work

ReconDreamer++ extends the ReconDreamer [29] framework, which itself builds on Street Gaussians [46] for 3D Gaussian Splatting scene decomposition and introduces a DriveRestorer video restoration model to enhance novel trajectory rendering. The key departure is twofold: (1) the Novel Trajectory Deformable Network (NTDNet) explicitly models and corrects the spatial domain gap between generated and recorded observations, rather than treating them as interchangeable training signals; and (2) the ground surface is independently modeled with fixed LiDAR-derived positions, separating geometric structure from appearance optimization. This positions the work as a targeted fix for two specific failure modes identified in prior generative+reconstructive pipelines: original-trajectory quality degradation and poor ground surface fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in closed-loop autonomous driving simulation: the domain gap between diffusion-generated views and real sensor data, which prior methods implicitly ignored by co-training on both. The fixed-position ground Gaussian model is a simple but effective insight that reduces the optimization search space for the most geometrically challenging scene component. Limitations include reliance on LiDAR for ground initialization (limiting applicability to LiDAR-free settings) and the fact that NTDNet is a per-scene learned network rather than a generalizable module. Open questions include whether the domain-gap correction generalizes to more extreme trajectory deviations or to multi-vehicle interactions, and whether the approach transfers to other structured surfaces beyond the road.
