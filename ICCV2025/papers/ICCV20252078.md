## Problem

LoRA's low-rank constraint limits the effective rank of weight updates, degrading performance on complex tasks and out-of-distribution generalization. While full-rank PEFT alternatives (KronA, SinLoRA, RandLoRA) exist, the paper shows they often produce approximations with rapidly decaying singular values, meaning their theoretical full rank does not translate to high effective rank in practice. The paper identifies effective rank as the critical factor differentiating successful full-rank PEFT methods and seeks a construction that provably achieves it.

## Input / Output

For vision-language tasks, the method fine-tunes CLIP ViT attention layers (query projections) on image+text pairs to produce class labels across 11 classification datasets and VTAB1k. For language tasks, it fine-tunes key and value projection matrices in Llama3.1-8B and Qwen2.5-7B attention layers under 4-bit quantization-aware training, producing multiple-choice or binary answers on commonsense reasoning benchmarks. The adapter is applied only to attention layers while all other pre-trained weights remain frozen, and the output is the standard model prediction with the adapter's contribution added to the frozen weight matrix.

## Method

KRAdapter replaces LoRA's low-rank product BA with a Khatri-Rao product U⊙V, where U ∈ R^{k1×din} and V ∈ R^{k2×din} with k1·k2 = dout, yielding a full-rank update ΔW = α(U⊙V) added to the frozen pretrained weight W0. The Khatri-Rao product is a column-wise Kronecker product that, by Theorem 3.1, is provably full column rank almost surely when U and V are randomly initialized, unlike LoRA which is inherently rank-deficient. The parameter count N = din(k1+k2) is minimized at k1 = k2 = √dout, matching the budget of a rank-16–32 LoRA. U is initialized to zeros and V with Kaiming uniform (negative slope √(1/k1)), and the key empirical finding is that the resulting update matrices exhibit significantly higher tail singular values (higher effective rank) than other full-rank methods, producing smaller update norms and superior OOD generalization.

## Results

On 4-bit quantized Llama3.1-8B commonsense reasoning, KRAdapter achieves 72.43% average accuracy, outperforming LoRA (68.34%), DoRA (70.14%), SinLoRA (71.46%), RandLoRA (71.23%), and Krona (71.31%). On Qwen2.5-7B, KRAdapter reaches 79.28% average, surpassing LoRA (69.58%) by nearly 10 points and RandLoRA (77.93%) by 1.35 points. For out-of-distribution generalization on vision-language models, KRAdapter achieves the highest r_gen ratio (0.45 on ViT-B/32, 0.49 on ViT-L/14) compared to LoRA (0.27, 0.39) and full fine-tuning (0.26, 0.27), while also producing the smallest update norms (nuclear norm 7.3 vs. 19.6 for LoRA on ViT-B/32). On VTAB1k, KRAdapter attains 71.1% average accuracy on ViT-B/32, exceeding LoRA (66.1%) and full fine-tuning (70.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KRAdapter consistently outperforms LoRA across all evaluated settings: on Llama3.1-8B commonsense reasoning it gains +4.09 points in average accuracy (72.43% vs. 68.34%) and +9.04 points on OOD tasks (64.66% vs. 55.62%); on VTAB1k it improves by +5.0 points over LoRA on ViT-B/32 (71.1% vs. 66.1%). Against DoRA, KRAdapter leads by +2.29 points on Llama3.1-8B and +2.33 on Qwen2.5-7B, with the gap widening on OOD tasks (64.66% vs. 57.80% on Llama). Compared to the full-rank baselines SinLoRA, RandLoRA, and Krona, KRAdapter achieves the best OOD accuracy on both LLMs (64.66% vs. 58.43%, 60.83%, 61.37% on Llama; 70.78% vs. 57.12%, 68.04%, 68.23% on Qwen), while remaining competitive on in-distribution tasks. On vision-language OOD generalization (Table 2), KRAdapter achieves the highest r_gen ratio (0.45–0.49) and the smallest update norms (nuclear 7.3–32.8 vs. 19.6–508.3 for other methods), indicating it stays closest to pre-trained weights while still improving performance. The one setting where KRAdapter is not the clear winner is the structured subset of VTAB1k on ViT-H/14, where RandLoRA edges ahead (59.5% vs. 58.1%), likely due to implicit regularization in RandLoRA's random matrix construction.

## Relation to Prior Work

KRAdapter directly extends LoRA's frozen-weight-plus-update framework by replacing the low-rank BA product with a Khatri-Rao (column-wise Kronecker) product U⊙V, thereby removing the rank constraint while preserving parameter efficiency comparable to rank-16–32 LoRA. It is positioned against a family of recent full-rank PEFT methods—KronA (Kronecker product), SinLoRA (sine-activated low-rank), and RandLoRA (random basis combinations)—and departs from them by identifying *effective rank* (tail singular value distribution) rather than theoretical full rank as the critical spectral property. The Khatri-Rao product itself is borrowed from signal processing and tensor decomposition, repurposed here as a natural mechanism for constructing high-effective-rank updates with a provable full-column-rank guarantee under random initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, drop-in PEFT alternative to LoRA that consistently improves OOD generalization (measured via r_gen) across both vision-language (CLIP/ViT up to 1B) and language (Llama3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B) models while matching LoRA's memory and compute footprint. The effective-rank lens reframes the full-rank PEFT literature: not all theoretically full-rank constructions are equivalent, and tail singular value mass matters for robustness. Open questions include whether low-rank factorizations of U and V can reduce the minimum parameter count without sacrificing the full-rank guarantee, and whether the advantage persists in extreme few-shot or continual-learning regimes where strict subspace constraints may favor LoRA.
