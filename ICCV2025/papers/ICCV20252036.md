## Problem

Existing visual encapsulation methods in video MLLMs—both linear projectors and resamplers—suffer from semantic indistinctness (multiple tokens redundantly encode the same visual instance while ignoring others) and temporal incoherence (tokens fail to consistently track their corresponding instances across all frames). While resamplers' cross-attention mechanism offers a promising path to address both issues, no prior work explicitly guides the token-to-concept assignment or enforces frame-level temporal consistency.

## Input / Output

DisCo operates as a drop-in replacement for the resampler in existing video MLLM pipelines. It consumes T video frames encoded by a frozen ViT into patch-level features (n×c per frame) and a text instruction, then produces a fixed set of N visual tokens (e.g., 96 for InternVideo2, 32 for ST-LLM) via cross-attention with learnable queries. These tokens are fed to an LLM (Mistral-7B or Vicuna-7B) which generates a natural language answer. Notably, DisCo can reduce the token count by 75% (e.g., 16 tokens vs. 64) while maintaining or exceeding baseline performance, thanks to reduced semantic redundancy.

## Method

DisCo introduces two modules on top of a standard Q-Former-style resampler. The Visual Concept Discriminator (VCD) uses GPT-4 to extract discrete semantic concepts from video captions, then performs bipartite matching (Hungarian algorithm) between groups of visual tokens and text concept embeddings, enforcing one-to-one alignment via a contrastive VSC loss and a matching VSM loss; global tokens are added to capture information not covered by matched concepts. The Temporal Focus Calibrator (TFC) extracts frame-level attention features from the resampler's cross-attention maps and applies a Frame-level Focus Alignment (FFA) loss that pulls each token's per-frame attention toward its own temporal centroid while pushing it away from other tokens' centroids, ensuring consistent tracking of the assigned instance across all frames. Training proceeds in two stages: vision-text alignment (with LLM loss plus VSC, VSM, and FFA losses) followed by instruction tuning. The design is plug-and-play, validated on both ST-LLM and InternVideo2 frameworks.

## Results

On the strongest backbone (InternVideo2-HD, 7B), DisCo achieves 68.2 on MVBench, 77.7 on STAR, 67.4 on PerceptionTest, 72.2 on EgoSchema, 49.5 on MLVU, and 47.4/57.9 (w/o & w/ subtitles) on VideoMME overall, setting new state-of-the-art across all benchmarks. DisCo also improves the lighter InternVideo2 backbone by +3.0 on MVBench (63.3 vs. 60.3) and +8.2 on STAR (72.7 vs. 64.5). On the VideoChatGPT-Bench conversation evaluation, InternVideo2-HD+DisCo reaches an average GPT-scored rating of 3.24, surpassing the previous best (Chat-UniVi at 2.99) by 0.25 points. Notably, DisCo maintains performance with 75% fewer visual tokens (16 vs. 64), demonstrating significant token efficiency gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DisCo, applied as a plug-and-play module, consistently outperforms all 14 baselines on every benchmark where scores are reported. On MVBench, InternVideo2-HD+DisCo (68.2) surpasses the previous best VideoChat2-HD (62.3) by 5.9 points and InternVideo2-HD (66.3) by 1.9 points. On EgoSchema, DisCo achieves 72.2, beating VideoChat2-HD (65.6) by 6.6 points and InternVideo2-HD (67.0) by 5.2 points, indicating strong gains on long-video temporal reasoning. On PerceptionTest, DisCo leads with 67.4, outperforming VideoChat2-HD (54.3) by 13.1 points, a particularly large margin on fine-grained visual detail tasks. On MLVU, DisCo (49.5) edges out VideoLLaMA2 (48.5) and InternVideo2-HD (47.1). On VideoMME overall, DisCo achieves 47.4/57.9, surpassing VideoLLaMA2 (47.9/50.3) on the w/o-subtitle setting and VideoChat2-HD (45.3/55.7) on both settings. On the VideoChatGPT-Bench conversation evaluation, InternVideo2-HD+DisCo (avg 3.24) outperforms Chat-UniVi (2.99), LLaVA-Mini (2.94), and all other baselines, with the largest gains in Correctness of Information (+0.22) and Detail Orientation (+0.29) over the previous best.

## Relation to Prior Work

DisCo builds directly on the Q-Former resampler architecture from BLIP-2, which uses cross-attention to compress video patches into a fixed set of visual tokens for LLM consumption. It departs from both linear projectors (which preserve local detail but introduce semantic redundancy across frames and cannot model temporal coherence) and existing resamplers (which reduce redundancy in form but still produce multiple tokens attending to the same semantic instance and exhibit inconsistent frame-level focus). The key conceptual departure is the explicit, structured guidance of the cross-attention process: VCD uses bipartite matching to assign distinct semantic concepts to token groups, while TFC introduces a frame-level contrastive loss to enforce temporal consistency—neither of which appears in prior encapsulation designs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of DisCo means it can be dropped into any resampler-based video MLLM without architectural redesign, making it broadly applicable across the rapidly growing ecosystem of video MLLMs. The finding that 16 DisCo tokens outperform 64 standard resampler tokens on MVBench suggests that semantic distinctness is a more important axis of token quality than raw token count, which could reshape how practitioners allocate visual token budgets. Open questions include whether the GPT-4 dependency for concept extraction can be eliminated or replaced with a learned module, whether the approach generalizes to non-resampler connectors (e.g., linear projectors with downsampling), and whether the benefits persist for very long videos where the number of frames T grows substantially.
