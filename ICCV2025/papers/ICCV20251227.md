## Problem

Existing dynamic urban scene rendering methods (e.g., OmniRe, StreetGS, 4DGF) rely on 3D-specific annotations such as object tracklets, 3D bounding boxes, and SMPL body templates, and often require LiDAR data. This paper asks whether off-the-shelf 2D priors—monocular depth and point tracking—combined with a dual SDF + 3D Gaussian Splatting representation can achieve comparable or superior rendering quality without any 3D motion annotations or LiDAR.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251227/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes posed RGB frames with per-object masks (generated by SAM2 prompted with CoTracker point tracks), metric depth maps from UniDepth, 2D point tracks from CoTracker, and camera intrinsics/extrinsics; LiDAR point clouds are optional. It produces a per-object 4D representation (a deforming SDF network plus a set of motion-parameterized 3D Gaussians) that supports novel view synthesis, scene decomposition, composition, and object removal. Training uses every 8th frame as test and the remainder as training, and the method handles one dynamic object at a time in a canonical coordinate frame.

## Method

UGSDF jointly learns a multi-resolution hash-grid SDF network (with a deformation MLP and a topology-aware HyperNeRF-style mapping) and a set of 3D Gaussian primitives whose positions and orientations evolve via a linear combination of learnable basis trajectories with a sparsity penalty. The two representations guide each other: SDF values queried at cubic grid centers drive Gaussian densification (adding Gaussians near the zero-level set in sparse regions) and pruning (removing Gaussians far from the surface over a temporal window), while the depth map rendered from the Gaussians narrows the ray-sampling interval for the SDF network. Canonicalization warps noisy depth-back-projected point clouds across time and cameras using 2D point tracks to initialize the scaffold. Training alternates between Gaussian and SDF updates, supervised by RGB, depth, mask, free-space, eikonal, and smoothness losses, plus 2D-track and rigidity constraints on motion.

## Results

On the NOTR (Waymo) benchmark, UGSDF achieves 33.98 PSNR / 0.944 SSIM / 0.104 LPIPS for scene reconstruction and 30.63 PSNR / 0.871 SSIM / 0.129 LPIPS for novel view synthesis, outperforming all baselines. Even without LiDAR, UGSDF retains 33.88 PSNR (reconstruction) and 30.32 PSNR (NVS), still surpassing OmniRe (33.79 / 29.35 PSNR). On KITTI MOT, UGSDF reaches 29.55 PSNR / 0.934 SSIM (reconstruction) and 28.63 PSNR / 0.926 SSIM (NVS), improving over the next-best OmniRe by 1.6 dB PSNR in reconstruction and 2.0 dB in NVS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UGSDF outperforms all seven baselines on the NOTR full-image metrics, with the largest margin over StreetGS (+4.87 dB PSNR in NVS) and S3Gaussians (+3.81 dB PSNR in NVS). Against the strongest competitor OmniRe, UGSDF gains +1.28 dB PSNR in NVS (30.63 vs 29.35) and +0.091 LPIPS improvement, despite not using 3D tracklets, SMPL templates, or LiDAR. On KITTI, UGSDF leads OmniRe by +1.60 dB PSNR in reconstruction and +2.04 dB in NVS. In the per-category breakdown (Table 3), UGSDF wins on vehicles across all metrics (e.g., 30.34 vs 28.91 PSNR reconstruction) but OmniRe retains a slight edge on human reconstruction (28.15 vs 27.89 PSNR), while UGSDF still surpasses OmniRe on human NVS (25.48 vs 24.36 PSNR). Against template-free methods (4DGF, StreetGS, S3Gaussians, PVG, DeformGS, EmerNeRF), UGSDF dominates on both human and vehicle categories in NVS, confirming that 2D priors (point tracks + depth) combined with the dual SDF-3DGS representation are sufficient to match or exceed methods that rely on 3D motion annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

UGSDF builds on the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework [33] for high-fidelity rendering and NeuS-style SDF networks [83] for geometric precision, but is the first to jointly optimize both representations for individual dynamic objects in urban scenes. It departs from scene-graph methods like OmniRe [13] and StreetGS [94] that require 3D tracklets, SMPL templates, or LiDAR by instead deriving motion and geometry from 2D priors (CoTracker point tracks and UniDepth metric depth). The SDF-guided densification and pruning of Gaussians extends static-scene hybrids like GaussianRoom [92] and GSD [102] into the dynamic, non-planar object setting, while the Gaussian-guided ray sampling for SDF training inverts the typical one-way dependency seen in prior surface-Gaussian alignment work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for 3D tracklets, SMPL templates, and optionally LiDAR, UGSDF lowers the data barrier for dynamic urban scene reconstruction, making it applicable to more casually captured or sensor-limited settings. The method also enables downstream editing tasks (object removal, scene composition) that are difficult with monolithic dynamic fields. Key limitations remain: sensitivity to 2D track quality, reduced controllability compared to SMPL for human geometry, and degraded novel views far from training trajectories. Open directions include incorporating video generative model priors and jointly inferring motion segmentation alongside rendering.
