## Problem

Supervised contrastive learning in parametric GCD methods (e.g., SimGCD) operates exclusively on labeled data, which contains only known classes. The paper demonstrates empirically that this restriction yields negligible or even negative gains in novel-class accuracy, because novel samples receive no direct contrastive supervision. The core question is how to incorporate the abundant unlabeled data—which contains both known and novel samples—into contrastive learning to improve novel category discovery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252099/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled set of images with known-class labels (50% of seen categories) and an unlabeled set containing both known and novel class images, processed through a DINO-pretrained ViT-B/16 encoder with batch size 128. It produces per-image class predictions over K total categories (assumed known) via a parametric prototype classifier, and additionally maintains a dictionary of voted pseudo-labels across training iterations. The total class count K is either given (main experiments) or estimated via an off-the-shelf clustering method (secondary experiments).

## Method

AllGCD extends SimGCD with two contrastive components that jointly exploit all unlabeled data. Intra-CL identifies potential known samples in unlabeled data using a confidence-and-agreement criterion (both views exceed threshold δ and predict the same known class), stores their pseudo-labels in a dictionary Q across iterations, and votes for the most frequent label to form a reliable pseudo-known set; this set is then added to the supervised contrastive loss to tighten intra-class compactness of known categories. Inter-CU, activated after a warm-up epoch e, applies the Hungarian algorithm to the classifier's predictions over the entire unlabeled set to obtain a global class assignment, filters by a confidence threshold γ, and applies a second supervised contrastive loss on this assignment to enforce inter-class separation between known and novel categories. The total loss combines the unsupervised contrastive term, Intra-CL, Inter-CU, and the parametric classifier loss, with λ balancing the supervised contrastive components.

## Results

AllGCD achieves state-of-the-art or near-SOTA performance across six GCD benchmarks under the standard setting (K known). Headline improvements over the SimGCD baseline include +7.4% on CUB New (65.1 vs. 57.7), +7.5% on Stanford Cars New (52.5 vs. 45.0), and +4.4% on ImageNet-100 New (82.3 vs. 77.9). On the 'All' metric, AllGCD reaches 82.3 (CIFAR-100), 86.5 (ImageNet-100), 68.4 (CUB), 60.5 (Stanford Cars), 57.4 (FGVC-Aircraft), and 45.2 (Herbarium-19). The method also outperforms SPTNet on CUB All by 2.6% and Stanford Cars All by 1.5% while using only 200 training epochs versus SPTNet's 1000.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AllGCD most directly extends SimGCD [45], its baseline, and achieves substantial gains in novel-class accuracy: +7.4% on CUB (65.1 vs. 57.7), +7.5% on Stanford Cars (52.5 vs. 45.0), and +4.4% on ImageNet-100 (82.3 vs. 77.9), while also improving 'All' accuracy on every dataset. Against SPTNet [44], AllGCD surpasses it by 2.6% on CUB All (68.4 vs. 65.8) and 1.5% on Stanford Cars All (60.5 vs. 59.0) using only 200 epochs versus SPTNet's 1000, though SPTNet edges ahead on FGVC-Aircraft New (58.1 vs. 54.9). Compared to CMS [5], AllGCD trails by 2.7% on CIFAR-100 Old (82.3 vs. 85.7) but outperforms it by 6.7% on CIFAR-100 New (82.2 vs. 75.5) and by 6.1% on FGVC-Aircraft New (54.9 vs. 47.6), confirming its strength in novel-class discovery. Against LegoGCD [3], AllGCD leads on CUB New (65.1 vs. 59.8), Stanford Cars New (52.5 vs. 48.4), and Herbarium-19 New (38.0 vs. 38.5, roughly tied). On the estimated-K setting (Table 1b), AllGCD also provides more accurate K estimation than SimGCD and CMS on most datasets (e.g., 6.5% error on CUB vs. 15.5% for SimGCD) and achieves the best 'New' accuracy on Stanford Cars (+7.2%) and Herbarium-19 (+9.1%) over the second-best method.

## Relation to Prior Work

AllGCD extends the parametric GCD framework established by SimGCD (Wen et al., ICCV 2023), which replaced k-means clustering with a prototype-based classifier trained via supervised contrastive learning (S-CL) on labeled data. The key departure is that prior parametric methods (SimGCD, SPTNet, LegoGCD) restrict S-CL to the labeled set, which contains only known classes; AllGCD extends S-CL to all unlabeled data through two mechanisms: Intra-CL (voting-based pseudo-labeling of known samples to strengthen intra-class compactness) and Inter-CU (Hungarian-algorithm-based global assignment to enforce inter-class separation between known and novel categories). The problem formulation and two-view training pipeline follow Vaze et al. (CVPR 2022), while the pseudo-labeling strategy draws on the entropy-regularization idea from Cao et al. (CVPR 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and previously underappreciated limitation: S-CL restricted to labeled data provides no direct contrastive signal for novel classes and can even slightly degrade novel-class accuracy, which explains why prior parametric GCD methods plateau on fine-grained benchmarks. The practical takeaway—that incorporating unlabeled data into S-CL via pseudo-labeling and global assignment yields substantial gains (7.4% on CUB, 7.5% on Stanford Cars) with only 200 training epochs—offers a simple, drop-in improvement for the GCD community. Open questions include scalability of the Hungarian assignment to very large unlabeled sets, sensitivity to the confidence thresholds δ and γ across domains, and whether the approach transfers to non-ViT backbones or video/multimodal GCD settings.
