## Problem

Existing EO foundation models are limited in three dimensions: pretraining datasets focus on high-resolution surface sensors (S1/S2, Landsat) while excluding temporally rich atmospheric and coarse-scale missions (S3, S5P); model architectures are rigid and cannot natively process non-spectral inputs or integrate metadata; and evaluation benchmarks are surface-centric, overlooking atmospheric tasks. This paper addresses all three gaps simultaneously by unifying all major Copernicus Sentinel missions into a single pretraining pipeline, model, and benchmark.

## Input / Output

The model consumes one image per modality from a shared 0.25°×0.25° grid cell, yielding 8 inputs with highly heterogeneous spatial resolutions (10 m S1/S2 at 264×264, 300 m S3 at 96×96, 1 km S5P at 28×28, 30 m DEM at 960×960) and channel counts (2, 13, 21, 1, 1). Metadata (central coordinates, patch area in km², temporal offset in days) is optionally provided and Fourier-encoded into the token sequence. The frozen encoder produces a unified embedding usable for k-NN/linear-probe classification, UPerNet segmentation, dense regression on atmospheric variables, or as a semantically rich grid representation for climate parameter prediction.

## Method

Copernicus-FM is a ViT-Base whose patch-embedding layer is replaced by two sensor-aware hypernetworks: a spectral hypernetwork that Fourier-encodes each channel's central wavelength and bandwidth to generate convolution kernels, and a variable hypernetwork that uses a frozen Llama 3.2 encoder to embed non-spectral variable names (e.g., 'NO2', 'elevation') into the same kernel-generation pipeline. A FlexiViT-style dynamic reshape adapts the kernel to modality-specific patch sizes (16×16, 8×8, 4×4, 64×64) to avoid memory explosion from resolution gaps. Metadata (location, area, time) is Fourier-encoded, MLP-projected, and added to positional encodings, with learnable tokens substituting for missing values (70% random drop during training). Pretraining combines MAE-style masked image modeling (70% masking, reconstruction on flattened patch tokens) with auxiliary continual distillation from frozen DINOv2 (RGB, weight 0.1) and SoftCon (S1/S2, weight 0.2) to anchor the latent space and accelerate convergence.

## Results

On the 15-task Copernicus-Bench, Copernicus-FM (ViT-B/16) achieves the best or near-best frozen-encoder performance on 11/15 tasks, outperforming supervised training in most cases. The most dramatic gains appear on atmospheric and coarse-resolution tasks: AQ-O3-S5P RMSE drops from 1755.6 (DOFA) to 789.4, LC100Seg-S3 mIoU improves from 16.5 to 24.1, and LC100Cls-S3 mAP rises from 89.5 to 93.3. On high-resolution S1/S2 tasks, Copernicus-FM matches or slightly trails the specialized SoftCon model (e.g., BigEarthNet-S2 mAP 79.0 vs. 83.6) but leads on EuroSAT-S1 OA (87.2 vs. 83.6). In the climate prediction experiment (Table 5), combining Copernicus-FM grid embeddings with coordinates yields the lowest RMSE across all 12 climate parameters, e.g., 1.98 °C for 10-year mean temperature vs. 3.67 for SatCLIP location encodings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DOFA (the closest prior multi-spectral model), Copernicus-FM wins on all 15 tasks where DOFA reports results, with the largest margins on S3 and S5P: LC100Seg-S3 mIoU +7.6 (24.1 vs. 16.5), AQ-O3-S5P RMSE −55% (789.4 vs. 1755.6), and BigEarthNet-S1 mAP +7.4 (77.9 vs. 70.5). Against SoftCon (a strong S1/S2-specialized model), Copernicus-FM leads on EuroSAT-S1 OA (+3.6), EuroSAT-S2 OA (+1.2), and LCZ-S2 OA (+0.8), but trails on BigEarthNet-S2 mAP (79.0 vs. 83.6) and BigEarthNet-S1 mAP (77.9 vs. 78.7), indicating that the unified model still lags a dedicated S1/S2 model on some fine-grained optical classification. Against CROMA (dual S1+S2), Copernicus-FM wins on 7 of 9 shared tasks, with notable gains on BigEarthNet-S1 mAP (+7.1) and EuroSAT-S1 OA (+3.3), but loses on DFC2020-S2 mIoU (64.5 vs. 66.5). In the climate regression experiment, Copernicus-FM grid embeddings combined with coordinates outperform SatCLIP location encodings on all 12 climate parameters (e.g., temperature mean RMSE 1.98 vs. 3.67 °C), demonstrating that EO-derived semantic features add value beyond pure geographic position.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends DOFA's wavelength-conditioned dynamic patch embedding by adding a variable hypernetwork (using frozen LLM-encoded variable names) for non-spectral modalities and a FlexiViT-inspired mechanism for resolution-adaptive patch sizes, while retaining DOFA's MIM + continual distillation training recipe. The dataset generalizes prior EO pretraining corpora (SSL4EO-S12, SatlasPretrain, MMEarth) from surface-only, high-resolution sensors to all operational Sentinel missions including coarse-scale atmospheric products (S3, S5P) and DEM, aligned on a 0.25° grid consistent with ERA5. The benchmark extends GEO-Bench, PANGAEA, and FoMo-Bench by adding hierarchical atmospheric and S3/S5P tasks that prior benchmarks entirely omitted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ablation showing that metadata encoding (geolocation, area, time) yields the single largest performance gain (+22.4 OA on EuroSAT-S1) challenges the prevailing assumption that image content alone drives remote sensing representation quality, suggesting that contextual signals are underexploited in current EO foundation models. The climate-prediction experiment demonstrates that EO grid embeddings substantially outperform coordinate-only and even SatCLIP location encodings for predicting 10-year climate statistics, opening a concrete pathway toward unified EO–climate foundation models. Key limitations include restriction to the Sentinel family, a ~1-year temporal window, and lack of native time-series or cross-modal fusion in the architecture, leaving open whether the approach scales to additional constellations and longer temporal horizons.
