## Problem

Existing VLM unlearning methods adapt LLM techniques that require extensive annotated forget sets and perform coarse-grained weight updates, often causing the model to refuse to describe any image containing the target concept rather than selectively suppressing just that concept. This leads to excessive forgetting and degraded model utility. Fine-grained, selective concept unlearning in VLMs—where only the target concept is removed while all other image details are preserved—remains largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250646/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text prompt (e.g., 'Please describe this image') as input and produces a text description in which the target concept is suppressed or replaced with alternative terms, while all other objects, attributes, and scene details remain intact. The intervention occurs entirely at inference time on the vision encoder's residual stream, requiring no weight updates to the VLM itself. The SAE operates on the last image token from the penultimate vision encoder layer, capturing global image-level features rather than per-patch representations.

## Method

SAUCE trains a single-layer ReLU sparse autoencoder on the residual stream of the penultimate vision encoder layer (using ImageNet-1k with the VLM frozen), expanding the feature dimension by 64× to learn high-dimensional monosemantic representations. It then identifies the top-k SAE features most relevant to a target concept by comparing mean activations on positive samples (containing the concept) versus negative samples (not containing it) using a normalized scoring function. At inference, the identified features are scaled by a negative factor (γ = −0.5) before being decoded back through the SAE decoder and passed to the next transformer layer, effectively suppressing the target concept while leaving all other features untouched. This design enables fine-grained, annotation-light unlearning at the feature level within the vision module, distinguishing it from prior SAE-based unlearning work that operates on the language module of LLMs.

## Results

SAUCE achieves unlearning quality (UAg) of 86.3% on concrete concepts and 83.1% on abstract concepts with LLaVA-v1.5-7B, and 93.8%/86.3% with Llama-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct, outperforming the best baseline (PO) by an average of 18.04% in unlearning quality. Model utility is well preserved, with in-domain retain accuracy (IRA) of 90.6% (concrete) and 82.7% (abstract) on LLaVA, and MME scores of 90.1% and 85.2% respectively. On the larger Llama model, SAUCE reaches 92.7% IRA and 91.0% MME on concrete unlearning, demonstrating that the method scales with vision encoder quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAUCE dominates all four baselines (GA, GD, KL, PO) across every metric on both models and both task types. Against the strongest baseline PO, SAUCE improves UAg by 17.95% and UAd by 18.13% on average, while also achieving higher IRA (87.55% vs. 83.15% average), indicating better preservation of non-target concepts. KL is the closest competitor on model utility (CRA and MME), reaching 90.2% MME on LLaVA concrete unlearning, but its unlearning quality (UAg=70.5%) lags SAUCE by 15.8 points. GA and GD are substantially weaker on both unlearning quality and utility, with GA scoring only 59.1% UAg and 53.6% IRA on LLaVA concrete unlearning. A notable qualitative difference is that baselines like PO often refuse to generate any description when the target concept is present, whereas SAUCE replaces the target concept with an alternative term while preserving all other image details.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SAE-based unlearning from LLMs (Farrell et al., O'Brien et al.) and diffusion models (Cywiński and Deja) to the vision module of VLMs, shifting the intervention point from text generation to visual feature representations. It departs fundamentally from prior VLM unlearning methods (Gradient Ascent, Gradient Difference, KL Minimization, Preference Optimization) that require weight updates and large annotated forget sets, instead performing inference-time feature suppression on monosemantic SAE features. The correlation-based feature selection scoring function is directly adapted from Cywiński and Deja's diffusion model work, but the integration into a ViT residual stream and the multi-concept, multi-domain evaluation framework are novel to this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The inference-time, annotation-light approach addresses a critical practical gap: existing VLM unlearning methods are data-intensive and produce coarse-grained forgetting that degrades model utility on unrelated concepts. The finding that SAEs transfer across models sharing the same vision encoder but fail across different encoders provides a clear practical guideline for deployment. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other layers of the vision encoder, how it scales to very large unlearning requests, and whether the storage cost of SAE training limits applicability to the largest VLMs. The persistent gap between concrete and abstract concept unlearning suggests that contextual, distributed representations remain harder to isolate with sparse features.
