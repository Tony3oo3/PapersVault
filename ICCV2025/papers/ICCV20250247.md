## Problem

Large-scale indoor 3D mapping with omnidirectional cameras remains unsolved: existing SfM pipelines (COLMAP, OpenMVG) fail to register nearly half of images in sparsely scanned, textureless indoor scenes, and no complete SfM pipeline operates natively on spherical camera models. Additionally, neural rendering methods (NeRF, 3DGS) overfit to training views and produce severe artifacts in sparse-view indoor environments, while classical texture mapping suffers from visible seams and lacks view-dependent appearance.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes equirectangular projection (ERP) images as input, resized to 640×320 for EDM dense matching and 1024×512 for alternative matchers, along with monocular depth and normal estimates from the pretrained OmniData model. Matching pairs are determined via room-segmentation labels (manual annotation), with one highest-overlap pair linking adjacent rooms. The method outputs camera poses, a triangle mesh extracted via Marching Cubes from a learned SDF, a diffuse texture map, a specular feature map, and a small 2-layer MLP (32 hidden dims) encoding view-dependent specular color, all rendered at 512×512 resolution.

## Method

IM360 is a three-stage pipeline. First, a spherical SfM performs dense feature matching (EDM) directly on ERP images, estimates two-view geometry via the essential matrix on unit bearing vectors (u₁ᵀEu₂=0) with DLT normalization, and runs bundle adjustment with spherical projection Π:ℝ³→S² — the first complete SfM where every step operates on the sphere manifold. Second, ERP images are projected to cubemaps and a neural SDF (DebSDF, 8-layer MLP with 256 hidden dims) is trained with monocular geometric priors from OmniData using volumetric rendering, then a mesh is extracted via Marching Cubes. Third, classical TexRecon initializes the diffuse texture, which is then jointly optimized with a specular feature map and a small fragment-shader MLP via differentiable rendering (Nvdiffrast), minimizing a combined L1+SSIM photometric loss (α=0.2) over 7,000 iterations to capture view-dependent appearance.

## Results

On Matterport3D, IM360 achieves 100% image registration across all six evaluated scenes (e.g., 37/37, 59/59, 53/53), substantially outperforming all SfM baselines that register only 5–51 images. In terms of pose accuracy, IM360 reaches AUC@3° scores of 49.16–73.95 on Matterport3D and 19.52–53.44 on Stanford2D3D, compared to 0.27–45.11 and 0.23–15.27 for the best competing methods. For textured mesh rendering, IM360 achieves a mean PSNR of 19.4 dB, a 3.5 dB improvement over the best classical baseline (TexRecon, 15.9 dB) and 5.1 dB over the best neural rendering method (SparseGS, 14.3 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

For SfM registration, IM360 achieves 100% image registration on all six Matterport3D scenes and 96–100% on Stanford2D3D, while OpenMVG registers only 2–25 images per scene and DKM COLMAP (the strongest SfM baseline) reaches at most 51/59 on Matterport3D and 79/258 on Stanford2D3D. In pose accuracy (AUC@3°), IM360 leads by 24–52 points over the best competing method across scenes, e.g., 49.16 vs. 25.14 (DKM) on scene 2t7WUuJeko7 and 53.44 vs. 41.56 on RPmz2sHmrrY. For rendering, IM360 (19.4 mean PSNR) outperforms TexRecon (15.9) by 3.5 dB, SparseGS (14.3) by 5.1 dB, ZipNeRF (13.9) by 5.5 dB, and 3DGS (13.4) by 6.0 dB across all six scenes. The ablation IM360* (diffuse-only texture optimization, no specular MLP) already gains 2.7 dB over TexRecon, confirming that the differentiable rendering refinement is the primary source of improvement, with the specular component contributing an additional 0.8 dB. Neural rendering methods (NeRF, 3DGS, SparseGS) consistently underperform classical texture mapping in these sparsely scanned indoor scenes, with a large gap between training and novel-view PSNR indicating overfitting to the limited input views.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper assembles a complete spherical SfM pipeline by integrating EDM (dense matching for equirectangular images), Solarte et al.'s normalized 8-point algorithm for spherical two-view geometry, and spherical bundle adjustment into a unified framework built on SphereSfM and COLMAP. For geometry, it repurposes DebSDF (a perspective-image neural SDF method) via cubemap projection rather than using ERP-native approaches like OmniSDF, which the authors found fail to converge on large-scale scenes. For texturing, it extends classical TexRecon with a differentiable-rendering-based optimization that jointly refines diffuse and specular textures plus a small MLP for view-dependent effects, departing from both pure classical mapping and pure neural rendering (NeRF/3DGS) approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that sparse omnidirectional scanning (0.23 images/m², ~300× less dense than ScanNet) is viable for high-quality indoor 3D mapping, which has direct practical implications for AR/VR content creation and robotics where acquisition time is critical. A notable finding is that mesh-based rendering with optimized textures outperforms NeRF and 3D Gaussian Splatting in sparse-view indoor settings, challenging the prevailing assumption that neural rendering is universally superior. Key limitations include reliance on human-annotated matching pairs (no automated retrieval) and degradation of the cubemap-based geometry pipeline at very large scales, leaving open the question of fully automated, arbitrarily large-scale 360° mapping.
