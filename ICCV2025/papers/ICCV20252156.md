## Problem

LiDAR-based 3D occupancy prediction models are typically trained and tested within a single dataset, causing severe performance degradation when deployed on platforms with different LiDAR sensors due to geometric disparities (beam count, angular resolution, point range) and semantic inconsistencies (disparate label taxonomies). No prior pipeline existed to simultaneously train a single occupancy model across multiple LiDAR datasets while bridging these domain gaps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252156/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method ingests raw LiDAR point cloud frames from heterogeneous sensors (32-beam nuScenes or 64-beam SemanticKITTI), which are first range-aligned to the minimum common annotation volume (L=[0,51.2]m, W=[−25.6,25.6]m, H=[−3.4,3.0]m) and then cylindrical-voxelized. Dataset-specific heads produce occupancy grids at each dataset's native resolution—[512, 512, 40] for nuScenes and [256, 256, 32] for SemanticKITTI—followed by a coarse-to-fine upsampling stage via geometric queries to refine the output.

## Method

MergeOcc introduces a multi-dataset training (MDT) framework with a shared 3D backbone (ResNet3D or Swin Transformer) and dataset-specific occupancy heads, jointly trained with cumulative per-dataset losses under identical hyperparameters. Two modules bridge the cross-LiDAR gap: (1) a geometric realignment module that aligns point ranges to the minimum common volume and applies dataset-specific normalization (per-dataset mean/variance in BatchNorm with shared γ, β) to encapsulate each LiDAR's unique statistical characteristics; and (2) a semantic label mapping (SLM) module that formulates the construction of a unified label space as a combinatorial integer linear program over boolean column selections, minimizing the round-trip mapping loss (dataset-specific → unified → dataset-specific) while penalizing label-space size. The SLM automatically harmonizes disparate taxonomies without manual annotation, and the joint prediction is computed by averaging mapped outputs across datasets before re-projecting to each dataset's label space.

## Results

On OpenOccupancy-nuScenes, MergeOcc-P (projection-based) achieves 42.7% geometric IoU and 26.1% semantic mIoU, while MergeOcc-V (voxel-based) achieves 40.2% IoU and 22.9% mIoU. On SemanticKITTI, MergeOcc-P reaches 72.7% IoU and 20.1% mIoU, and MergeOcc-V reaches 70.1% IoU and 14.5% mIoU. Compared to the Direct Merging (D.M.) baseline, MergeOcc-V improves IoU by +14.8% and mIoU by +17.2% on nuScenes, and by +33.4% IoU and +12.5% mIoU on SemanticKITTI. Notably, despite using identical architectures and hyper-parameters, MergeOcc outperforms its single-dataset baselines (L-CoNet, PointOcc) by up to +14.5% IoU and +11.9% mIoU, demonstrating the benefit of multi-dataset training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MergeOcc-P outperforms the SOTA multimodality model M-CONet [21] by 13.2% IoU and 6.0% mIoU on nuScenes, and surpasses the SOTA camera-based model SurroundOcc [22] by 38.0% IoU and 8.2% mIoU on SemanticKITTI. Using the weaker voxel-based L-CoNet backbone, MergeOcc-V still outperforms the LiDAR-based SOTA PointOcc [30] by 6.1% IoU on nuScenes. Against its own single-dataset baselines, MergeOcc-V improves over L-CONet by +9.3% IoU / +7.1% mIoU (nuScenes) and +14.5% IoU / +9.8% mIoU (SemanticKITTI), while MergeOcc-P improves over PointOcc by +9.4% IoU / +7.4% mIoU (nuScenes) and +4.8% IoU / +11.9% mIoU (SemanticKITTI). The Direct Merging baseline (simply concatenating datasets without any alignment) suffers severe degradation (e.g., 25.4% IoU vs. 40.2% for MergeOcc-V on nuScenes), confirming that geometric realignment and semantic label mapping are essential. Pre-training/fine-tuning (P.T.) also fails to transfer knowledge across domains, with the model forgetting source-domain performance (e.g., -3.8% IoU on nuScenes after fine-tuning on SemanticKITTI).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-dataset 3D occupancy prediction paradigm (L-CoNet, PointOcc, and related methods) into a multi-dataset training (MDT) setting, drawing on Uni3D's geometric realignment for 3D object detection but adapting it for dense-prediction tasks (notably choosing minimum rather than maximum point-cloud range). The semantic label mapping module is inspired by Zhou et al.'s combinatorial optimization for multi-dataset detection, but replaces the intersection-based label strategy with a union-based approach to preserve broader semantic coverage. Conceptually, the work bridges two previously separate lines of research—LiDAR occupancy prediction and multi-dataset generalization—into a unified pipeline that had not been attempted for 3D occupancy grids.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that an identical architecture trained on two datasets simultaneously outperforms the same architecture trained on either dataset alone (by up to +14.5% IoU) challenges the assumption that current 3D occupancy datasets are sufficiently large and diverse, suggesting a broader MDT paradigm could be a key direction for the field. However, the evaluation is limited to two datasets (32-beam nuScenes and 64-beam SemanticKITTI) and two backbone architectures, and the method does not yet generalize to truly unseen LiDAR configurations. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more than two datasets (the combinatorial optimization becomes NP-hard), whether it handles LiDARs with fundamentally different scanning patterns, and whether the gains persist as individual datasets grow larger.
