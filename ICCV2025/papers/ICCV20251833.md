## Problem

Existing 4D generation methods produce only a single motion per object per expensive inference pass, requiring repeated diffusion and reconstruction runs to obtain diverse motions, which loses identity and costs extensive compute. Meanwhile, category-specific motion models like SMPL rely on labor-intensive motion capture and cannot generalize to arbitrary objects. The paper asks: how can we build an SMPL-like model for any dynamic object that encodes a diverse motion space sampleable instantly at inference?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251833/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-view RGB image of any general object (humans, robots, quadrupeds, birds, plants, fluid, deformable objects) and optionally a natural language text prompt. It produces a 32-dimensional motion latent space from which diverse 3D motions are sampled in a single forward pass, yielding dynamic 3D Gaussians (~80k primitives) rendered at 512×512 at 250 FPS. Each motion is represented as 512 neural key points with 6DoF SE(3) trajectories over 21 frames, and the system also supports latent-space interpolation and test-motion reconstruction by optimizing a latent code in ~300 steps.

## Method

DIMO distills diverse motion priors from a video diffusion model (CogVideoX-5B-I2V) by generating 50+ motion-rich videos of the target object using GPT-4o-generated prompts, then embeds each motion into a shared 32-dim VAE latent space. The key representation factorizes each motion into sparse neural key point trajectories (512 points with RBF-based influence weights) that serve as a low-rank motion basis, with a shared 8-layer MLP decoder predicting per-key-point 6DoF SE(3) transformations conditioned on the latent code, sinusoidal time embeddings, and canonical positions. Canonical 3D Gaussians are deformed via Linear Blend Skinning driven by these key points and rendered with differentiable splatting. Training follows a coarse-to-fine two-stage schedule: first pre-training the motion basis and latent space (2.8k steps), then jointly optimizing geometry and motion with photometric losses (RGB, mask, LPIPS, depth, normal), ARAP rigidity regularization, and KL divergence, at progressively increasing resolution from 128×128 to 512×512.

## Results

On the Image-to-4D benchmark (Animate124 + Objaverse), DIMO achieves CLIP-I 0.9505, CLIP-F 0.9912, and 59.4% motion diversity in a user study, while matching the fastest training time of 10 minutes. On the Video-to-4D benchmark (Consistent4D), DIMO attains the best LPIPS (0.112) and FVD (625.30), surpassing SV4D (0.118 / 732.40). In the Text-to-4D setting, DIMO generates motions with 4.319 average optical flow amplitude (vs. 0.428 for Animate124) and 81.3% text alignment in a user study, completing generation in seconds versus hours for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIMO outperforms all seven baselines across every evaluation setting. On Image-to-4D, it leads SV4D (the strongest prior method) by +0.0133 CLIP-I and +0.0008 CLIP-F, while achieving 59.4% motion diversity versus SV4D's 8.1%—a 7× improvement—without increasing training time (10 min vs. 15 min). On Video-to-4D (Consistent4D), DIMO beats SV4D on both LPIPS (0.112 vs. 0.118) and FVD (625.30 vs. 732.40), and matches its CLIP score of 0.92. In the 3D Motion Generation user study, DIMO dominates with 80% preference for motion diversity versus DG4D's 11.4% and 4DGen's 8.6%. For Text-to-4D, DIMO's motion amplitude (4.319) is roughly 10× that of Animate124 (0.428) and 17× that of 4D-fy (0.254), with text alignment at 81.3% versus 6.3% and 12.5% respectively. The key differentiator is that DIMO jointly models all motions in a single generative model, whereas baselines optimize each motion independently, leading to identity drift and limited diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIMO builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting for geometric representation and on motion factorization literature (sparse key points with RBF-based skinning) for compact motion encoding, but its key conceptual departure is using pre-trained video diffusion models (CogVideoX) as a substitute for the expensive motion capture that underpins category-specific models like SMPL. Prior 4D generation methods (DG4D, 4DGen, Animate124, SV4D) produce a single motion per object per inference pass via score distillation or photogrammetry; DIMO instead distills diverse motion priors from video models into a shared VAE-like latent space with a joint motion decoder, enabling a single generative model to sample an entire motion space. The language-guided component repurposes BERT embeddings projected through a lightweight MLP into the motion latent code, extending the text-to-4D line of work (4D-fy, Animate124) from per-instance optimization to feed-forward generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a viable path toward an 'SMPL for any object' by showing that video models encode sufficient motion prior knowledge to replace labor-intensive mocap, which could reshape how dynamic 3D content is created for arbitrary categories. However, the current formulation requires training a separate generative model per object (roughly 3.5 hours on one A100), limiting scalability, and the language-to-motion mapping is learned in a separate two-stage process rather than jointly. Open questions include whether the latent space generalizes across objects, whether joint language-motion training closes the remaining gap, and how performance scales as underlying video models improve. The reliance on video model quality as a ceiling on motion diversity is an acknowledged limitation that ties DIMO's future to the broader video generation landscape.
