## Problem

Previous 3D edge reconstruction methods first recover a 3D edge point set from multi-view 2D edge images and then fit parametric edges to that point set. This two-step pipeline is sensitive to noise (causing fragmented, gapped edges) and the fitted edges may not align with the input 2D edge images because the fitting step ignores image supervision. The paper asks whether parametric 3D edges can be optimized directly under multi-view image supervision to achieve simultaneously accurate, complete, and compact reconstructions.

## Input / Output

The method takes calibrated multi-view posed RGB images as input and additionally derives per-view depth maps (via 2DGS reconstruction) and normal maps (via a diffusion-based estimator) to produce its own 2D edge supervision signal. The output is a set of 3D parametric sketches—lines defined by two 3D control points and third-order Bézier curves defined by four 3D control points—each carrying an optimizable opacity and a 3D local scale that control rendered intensity and thickness. All scenes are normalized to fit within a 1 m³ bounding box, and distance-based topology thresholds are set as 1% of scene size.

## Method

SketchSplat represents 3D edges as parametric sketches (lines and Bézier curves with control points, per-sketch scale, and opacity), initialized from EdgeGS point-based reconstruction. At each gradient step, points are sampled along each sketch at a fixed 5 mm interval, assigned the sketch's opacity, scale, and local tangent direction, and rasterized as 3D Gaussians onto a chosen camera view following the 3DGS volume-rendering formulation. The L1 loss between the rendered edge image and the ground-truth edge image is back-propagated to update all sketch parameters, directly aligning 3D edges with 2D image evidence. During training, adaptive topological operations—endpoint merging, overlapping-sketch merging, co-linear line merging, and multi-view visibility filtering—are applied iteratively to reduce redundancy and enforce connectivity. The authors also contribute a 2D edge detector (2DGS-SN) that combines a foreground mask with Sobel gradients of depth and normal maps, which they show substantially improves both their method and existing baselines by reducing detector bias.

## Results

On the ABC-NEF dataset, SketchSplat with the proposed 2DGS-SN detector achieves the first simultaneous sub-7 mm accuracy (A=6.8 mm) and completeness (C=5.8 mm), while exceeding 90% on R5 (90.8%), P5 (92.9%), and F5 (91.3%). At the 10 mm threshold it reaches F10=95.4%, and at 20 mm F20=96.5%. The method also produces far more compact reconstructions (44.3 edges on average vs. 140.8 for EdgeGS) in roughly 10 minutes per scene, comparable to EdgeGS and much faster than neural-field methods (NEF: 1 h 26 min, EMAP: 2 h 30 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SketchSplat outperforms all baselines on the primary F5 metric: 91.3% vs. 80.3% (EdgeGS), 66.3% (EMAP), 72.9% (LIMAP), and 13.0% (NEF), all using the same 2DGS-SN detector where applicable. Against the closest competitor EdgeGS (same detector), SketchSplat improves R5 by 15.1 points (90.8 vs. 75.7) and reduces both accuracy and completeness by roughly 1 mm (6.8/5.8 vs. 7.4/7.2 mm). LIMAP achieves the best raw accuracy (5.9 mm) but suffers from very poor completeness (29.6 mm) and recall (64.2% at 5 mm) because it cannot handle curves. The proposed 2DGS-SN detector itself provides a large boost to every method: e.g., EdgeGS jumps from F5=45.2 (DexiNed) to 80.3 (2DGS-SN), and EMAP from 59.1 to 66.3, confirming that 2D edge-image quality is a major bottleneck. On compactness, SketchSplat uses 44.3 edges on average versus 140.8 for EdgeGS and 45.8 for EMAP, while maintaining the highest accuracy and completeness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm in 3D edge reconstruction (reconstruct 3D edge points, then fit parametric edges to them) used by NEF, EMAP, and EdgeGS. Instead, it directly optimizes parametric sketch parameters (control points, scales, opacity) via differentiable multi-view rasterization, borrowing the Gaussian sampling and splatting machinery from 3DGS and the parametric line/Bezier representation from EdgeGS. The topological operations (endpoint merging, co-linear merging, overlap merging) are applied during training rather than as post-processing, and the multi-view visibility filter is adapted from EMAP. The proposed 2DGS-SN edge detector repurposes 2DGS depth reconstruction and a normal estimator with Sobel gradients as a lightweight alternative to learned detectors like PiDiNet and DexiNed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SketchSplat is the first method to simultaneously achieve sub-7 mm accuracy and completeness while exceeding 90% F-score at 5 mm on ABC-NEF, and it reduces edge count by ~3× compared to EdgeGS through adaptive topology control, making outputs more directly usable for CAD modeling. The 2DGS-SN detector, despite its simplicity, substantially improves all compared methods, suggesting that geometric cues (depth, normals) are underexploited in edge detection for structured objects. Open questions include scalability to larger or more cluttered scenes, handling of severe occlusions, and whether the 1000-epoch optimization can be accelerated for real-time or online applications such as SLAM or autonomous driving.
