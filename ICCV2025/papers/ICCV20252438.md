## Problem

Existing domain generalization methods for 3D point cloud segmentation rely on data augmentation to simulate domain shifts, but they learn only global geometric patterns while ignoring the fine-grained, category-level geometric distribution within each class. Additionally, the alignment of geometric features before and after perturbation is neglected, causing the model to miss domain-invariant geometric structure that could improve robustness to unseen environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252438/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D LiDAR point cloud of N points, each with (x, y, z) coordinates and a reflection intensity value, and produces a per-point semantic label across 19 categories. The point cloud is processed by a MinkUNet voxel-based backbone to extract N×D features, which are then projected into a category-level geometric space of dimension N×C×M before final classification. The framework is trained in a fully supervised manner on source-domain data only, with no access to target-domain samples.

## Method

The proposed framework introduces two coupled modules. First, Category-level Geometry Embedding (CGE) constructs per-class geometric properties by solving an optimal transport problem in Wasserstein space via Sinkhorn divergence, yielding a class-specific embedding matrix A that maps features into a geometric representation; a correlation parameter Q then couples this geometric embedding to semantic prediction through a Geometry Property Learning (GPL) loss, with A stabilized by momentum updates over training iterations. Second, Geometric Consistent Learning (GCL) applies a physics-inspired adverse geometry simulation—randomly perturbing point heights and intensities to mimic matter accumulation (snow/rain) and masking low-intensity points to simulate fog-induced signal loss—and then enforces consistency by aligning the geometric embeddings of the original and simulated features through a GCL loss, pushing the model to rely on domain-invariant geometric structure rather than domain-specific appearance cues.

## Results

On SemanticKITTI→SemanticSTF (real-to-adverse-weather), the proposed method achieves a mean mIoU of 37.5%, outperforming the strongest baseline LiDARWeather (36.1%) by 1.4% and DGUIL (34.8%) by 2.7%. On SynLiDAR→SemanticSTF (synthetic-to-adverse-weather), it reaches 26.3% mean mIoU, surpassing UniMix (23.6%) by 2.7%. On SynLiDAR→SemanticKITTI, the method achieves 31.3% mIoU versus DGUIL's 29.1%, and on the MinkUNet34 backbone it reaches 39.4% mIoU versus DGUIL's 37.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 8 baselines on both primary benchmarks. On SemanticKITTI→SemanticSTF, it beats LiDARWeather (36.1%) by 1.4% and DGUIL (34.8%) by 2.7% in mean mIoU, with the largest margin in dense fog (39.9% vs. 39.5% for LiDARWeather). On SynLiDAR→SemanticSTF, it surpasses UniMix (23.6%) by 2.7% and DGUIL (22.5%) by 3.8%, indicating stronger cross-domain (synthetic-to-real) generalization. On SynLiDAR→SemanticKITTI (Table 2), the method achieves 31.3% mIoU, exceeding DGUIL (29.1%) by 2.2% and PointDR (24.9%) by 6.4%. On the MinkUNet34 backbone (Table 3), it reaches 39.4% mIoU versus DGUIL's 37.6%, confirming backbone-agnostic gains. The method is particularly strong in dense fog and rain conditions, where geometric invariance provides the largest advantage over purely augmentation-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the domain generalization line for 3D point cloud segmentation (PointDR, DGUIL, UniMix, LiDARWeather), which has primarily relied on data augmentation to simulate domain shift. It departs from this augmentation-centric paradigm by introducing a geometric invariance perspective: category-level features are embedded in Wasserstein space via Sinkhorn divergence (building on Frogner et al., ICLR 2019), and a physics-inspired simulation (matter accumulation, fuzzy recognition) is used to align these embeddings across simulated domain shifts. The MinkUNet backbone and standard training pipeline are shared with prior work, so the contribution is orthogonal to the backbone choice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete gap—prior DG methods treat geometric perturbations globally while ignoring per-category geometric structure—and shows that explicitly modeling category-level geometric invariance yields consistent gains across multiple source-to-target settings. However, the approach is tied to LiDAR point clouds with a fixed 19-class vocabulary, and the Wasserstein-space embedding plus momentum-updated geometry matrix adds non-trivial implementation complexity. Open questions include whether the category-level geometric framing transfers to open-vocabulary or few-shot 3D segmentation, and whether the physics-inspired simulation generalizes beyond the specific adverse-weather conditions tested.
