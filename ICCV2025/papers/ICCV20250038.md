## Problem

Zeroth-order (ZO) optimization enables memory-efficient LLM fine-tuning without backpropagation, but the variance of its gradient estimates scales linearly with the model's parameter dimension, causing severe performance degradation for large LLMs. Existing random subspace ZO methods reduce this dimensionality dependence but require storing a full d×q projection matrix that is impractical at LLM scale, leaving a gap between theoretical subspace benefits and practical deployability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained LLM (OPT-1.3B/13B, LLaMA2-7B, Mistral-7B, RoBERTa-large), a task-specific training dataset with prompt templates, and hyperparameters (perturbation scale ε, subspace rank r, update frequency F). It produces updated parameter matrices layer-by-layer using only two forward passes per step—no backpropagation or gradient storage—making it compatible with non-differentiable objectives like accuracy or F1. For highly non-square parameter matrices (e.g., LoRA's A_i ∈ R^{m_i×k} with k=8), a reshaping step converts them to near-square form before perturbation.

## Method

SubZero replaces MeZO's full-dimensional Gaussian perturbation with a layer-wise low-rank perturbation: for each layer's parameter matrix W_i ∈ R^{m_i×n_i}, it constructs Z̃_i = U_i Z_i V_i^T where U_i ∈ R^{m_i×r} and V_i ∈ R^{n_i×r} are column-orthogonal matrices obtained via QR decomposition of random Gaussian matrices, and Z_i ∈ R^{r×r} is a small random matrix with r ≪ min{m_i, n_i}. The gradient is estimated from the loss difference over two forward passes with ±εZ̃_i applied, yielding per-layer updates W_i ← W_i − ηρU_iZ_iV_i^T. A lazy update strategy regenerates U_i and V_i only every F steps rather than every iteration, balancing subspace adaptability against computational overhead. The block-diagonal structure of the implicit projection matrix P = bdiag(V_1⊗U_1, …, V_l⊗U_l) avoids storing a model-scale matrix, keeping memory comparable to MeZO while reducing gradient variance from O(d) to O(lr²). Theoretical analysis proves the expected estimate equals the BP gradient projected onto the subspace, the variance scales with subspace dimension q = lr² rather than d, and convergence is guaranteed under SGD with T = O(d/ε) steps.

## Results

On SuperGLUE with OPT-13B, SubZero(FT) achieves a 1.89% average relative improvement over MeZO(FT) across 11 tasks, and SubZero(LoRA) achieves 1.57% over MeZO(LoRA). On LLaMA2-7B full-parameter tuning (CB task), SubZero reaches 71.4% accuracy versus MeZO's 64.3% (a 7.1-point gain), and on OPT-1.3B prompt tuning (SST-2), SubZero achieves 89.1% versus MeZO's 85.9%. Memory usage is nearly identical to MeZO (2.690 GB vs. 2.683 GB on RoBERTa-large/SST-2), while the competing random-subspace method S-RGF requires 23.845 GB—roughly 8.8× more.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SubZero consistently outperforms MeZO [40], its primary competitor, across all tasks and fine-tuning schemes: +1.89% average in FT and +1.57% in LoRA on OPT-13B SuperGLUE, and up to +7.1 points on LLaMA2-7B FT (CB). Among other ZO methods, SubZero beats ZO-AdaMU [28] (+1.89% vs. +0.46% in FT), LOZO [9] (+1.89% vs. +0.10%), S-MeZO [39] (+1.89% vs. −0.10%), and HiZOO [68] (+1.89% vs. −2.15%) in the FT setting; in LoRA, the gap widens further (SubZero +1.57% vs. S-MeZO −5.79%, ZO-AdaMU −1.78%, HiZOO −1.16%). Against inference-only baselines, SubZero substantially exceeds ICL [7] and linear probing [32] on nearly all SuperGLUE tasks (e.g., 92.1 vs. 87.0 on SST-2, 84.5 vs. 75.9 on SQuAD). Compared to the random-subspace method S-RGF [43], SubZero achieves comparable or better accuracy while using 8.8× less GPU memory (2.690 GB vs. 23.845 GB). SubZero approaches but does not match full backpropagation SGD [3] (e.g., 92.1 vs. 94.9 on SST-2 FT), which is expected given the inherent variance of ZO gradient estimates, though the gap is notably smaller than MeZO's gap to SGD. On non-differentiable objectives (accuracy/F1), SubZero also outperforms MeZO by 0.3–0.9 points across SST-2, SST-5, SNLI, MNLI, and SQuAD [40].

## Relation to Prior Work

SubZero extends the zeroth-order fine-tuning line initiated by MeZO [40], which introduced SPSA-style gradient estimation to LLM training, by incorporating the random subspace idea from [43, 46] to reduce gradient variance. The key departure from prior random subspace methods is the replacement of a single large model-scale projection matrix P ∈ R^{d×q} with layer-wise low-rank perturbations (U_i Z_i V_i^T) built from column-orthogonal matrices via QR decomposition, eliminating the prohibitive memory cost of storing P. The method also builds on the empirical observation that BP gradients during LLM fine-tuning concentrate in low-dimensional subspaces, and integrates with standard PEFT schemes (LoRA, prefix/prompt tuning) through a reshaping strategy for highly non-square parameter matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SubZero offers a practical drop-in replacement for MeZO that matches its memory footprint while delivering consistent 3–7% accuracy gains across models (OPT, LLaMA, Mistral, RoBERTa) and fine-tuning schemes, making memory-efficient LLM fine-tuning more competitive with first-order methods. The theoretical guarantees—bounded distance to the BP gradient independent of parameter dimension, variance scaling with subspace dimension q rather than model dimension d, and convergence under SGD—provide a clearer understanding of why subspace ZO methods work. Open questions include the interaction with adaptive optimizers (Adam), second-order ZO variants, and the lack of a theoretical analysis for the reshaping strategy used with non-square matrices in LoRA.
