## Problem

While diffusion models excel at capturing fine-grained visual details through pixel-level generation, their potential for interaction-level relationship modeling in HOI detection remains largely unexplored. Existing VLM-based approaches (e.g., CLIP) struggle with fine-grained spatial and relational details due to their contrastive learning paradigm, and directly applying text-conditioned diffusion to HOI detection introduces computational overhead from synthetic image generation and risks weakening visual feature refinement through strong textual priors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251010/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and operates in a two-stage pipeline: an off-the-shelf DETR detector first localizes all human and object instances, then the interaction recognition stage processes candidate human-object pairs. During training, the frozen Stable Diffusion (SD-2-1-base) model refines visual relation features via denoising feedback; at inference, the diffusion branch is entirely removed and interactions are classified directly from the refined visual relation features, producing <human, verb, object> triplets with bounding boxes and interaction category scores across 600 HOI classes.

## Method

VRDiff introduces a purely visual conditioning mechanism for a diffusion model to refine HOI interaction features without text inputs or synthetic image generation. The interaction-aware condition representation concatenates human and object features (extracted via ROIAlign from a CLIP encoder with learnable semantic adapters) with spatial interaction embeddings (relative position, scale ratio, IoU from bounding boxes processed by an MLP), then applies multi-head self-attention across all candidate pairs to enrich each pair with cross-pair contextual semantics. This visual relation embedding conditions the denoising network via cross-attention at each timestep, and the diffusion model's generative feedback dynamically refines the relation representations through a reconstruction loss. The overall training objective combines the diffusion denoising loss with a focal classification loss, and the entire framework is trained end-to-end with only the adapter layers and VRDiff components updated while the instance detector, CLIP encoder, and diffusion model remain frozen.

## Results

On HICO-DET under standard settings, VRDiff with a ResNet-50+ViT-L backbone achieves 41.40 mAP (Full), 41.69 mAP (Rare), and 41.31 mAP (Non-Rare) in the Default setting, and 44.25/44.65/44.13 mAP in the Known Object setting, setting new state-of-the-art results. With the smaller ViT-B backbone, VRDiff reaches 36.77 mAP (Full) while narrowing the Rare vs. Non-Rare gap to just 0.45 mAP. In zero-shot evaluation, VRDiff with the larger backbone leads across all four protocols (RF-UC, NF-UC, UO, UV), achieving up to 41.52 mAP on Unseen Object categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

With the ViT-L backbone, VRDiff (41.40 mAP Full) outperforms the previous best standard-setting methods BCOM (39.34), DIFFUSIONHOI (38.12), and ADA-CM (38.40) by 2.06, 3.28, and 3.00 mAP respectively. On the Rare category, VRDiff surpasses DIFFUSIONHOI by 2.76 mAP (41.69 vs. 38.93) and BCOM by 1.79 mAP, while reducing the Rare–Non-Rare gap to only 0.38 mAP. With the smaller ViT-B backbone, VRDiff (36.77) trails ViPLO (37.22) by 0.45 mAP on Full but still outperforms all ResNet-only baselines. In zero-shot settings, VRDiff with the larger backbone achieves the best scores across all four protocols: RF-UC (40.45 Full vs. CMMP 37.13), NF-UC (39.09 Full vs. EZ-HOI 34.84), UO (39.97 Full vs. CMMP 36.74), and UV (39.43 Full vs. EZ-HOI 36.84), with the largest margin on NF-UC Unseen (39.33 vs. 36.33). The ablation on condition design confirms that purely visual conditioning (EmptyText+Visual, 39.43) outperforms text-conditioned alternatives (RealText, 38.19; RealText+Visual, 38.81), validating the paper's core design choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

VRDiff builds on the two-stage HOI detection paradigm (DETR for instance detection followed by interaction classification) and the CLIP-based visual encoder with adapter layers introduced by ADA-CM [20]. It departs from prior diffusion-based HOI methods (DIFFUSIONHOI [23], CycleHOI [38]) that condition the diffusion process on text descriptions or generate synthetic images, instead introducing purely visual relation conditions—combining spatial geometry from bounding boxes with cross-pair contextual features via multi-head self-attention—to guide the denoising network. The key conceptual shift is repurposing the diffusion model not as an image generator but as a feature-refinement engine whose feedback dynamically updates interaction representations through a reconstruction loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that fine-grained perceptual capabilities of diffusion models can be transferred to relational understanding tasks without the computational cost of synthetic image generation, and that visual conditions outperform text conditions for interaction-level refinement—a finding that challenges the default assumption in diffusion-based knowledge transfer. However, evaluation is limited to a single benchmark (HICO-DET), and the diffusion branch is entirely removed at inference, raising questions about whether the learned refinement generalizes to more complex relational tasks or larger-scale datasets. An open question is whether the visual relation conditioning strategy can be extended to other structured prediction problems beyond HOI, such as scene graph generation or video interaction understanding.
