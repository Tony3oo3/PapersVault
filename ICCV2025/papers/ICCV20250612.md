## Problem

Existing 3DGS-based street scene reconstruction methods suffer from rapidly escalating per-viewpoint reconstruction costs as scene scale grows, due to three pipeline redundancies: unnecessary local-to-global coordinate transformations, excessive 3D-to-2D projections of invisible Gaussians, and uniform α-blending of all visible content regardless of distance. Additionally, most methods depend on ground-truth 3D bounding boxes for dynamic/static decomposition, which are expensive to annotate and limit real-world applicability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images with calibrated camera intrinsics/extrinsics, LiDAR point clouds (voxel-downsampled at 0.15 m), tracked 2D bounding boxes (rather than 3D boxes), and 2D semantic segmentation maps for BEV augmentation. It outputs an explicit 3D Gaussian scene representation (with per-Gaussian position, covariance, opacity, temporal visibility, and instance ID) that can be rasterized into novel-view images via α-blending, with colors queried from distance-aware neural fields (NeurF) rather than stored per-Gaussian SH coefficients.

## Method

S3R-GS streamlines the reconstruction pipeline through three key mechanisms: (1) instance-specific projection matrices that compose the camera extrinsic with each object's local-to-global transform, eliminating explicit coordinate changes; (2) temporal visibility intervals that pre-filter Gaussians before projection, reducing redundant 3D-to-2D mappings; and (3) an adaptive LOD strategy that culls Gaussians whose projected 2D scale falls below a threshold, applies depth-weighted Bernoulli dropout to remaining distant Gaussians, adds noisy positional offsets, and queries their colors from a depth-conditioned neural field. For scene decomposition, a NeuralODE model learns continuous 3D object trajectories from coarse positions estimated via 2D-box-masked LiDAR projection, with per-instance embeddings to distinguish objects of different categories and speeds. A BEV-semantic initialization augmentation re-samples points along the z-axis in grids occupied by 'tree' or 'building' semantic labels to cover tall structures missed by LiDAR's limited vertical FOV.

## Results

On the Argoverse 2 large-scale benchmark (7 cameras), S3R-GS achieves a mean PSNR of 25.68 dB, SSIM of 0.780, and LPIPS of 0.435, surpassing the previous best 4DGF (24.97 dB, 0.772, 0.447) while cutting reconstruction time from 54.39 h to 26.71 h (a 50.9% speedup). On KITTI large-scale scenes, S3R-GS reaches 23.86 dB PSNR on sequence 0009 and 24.96 dB on 0020, outperforming 4DGF by 1.22 and 1.88 dB respectively, with reconstruction times of 3.02 h and 3.37 h versus 15.69 h and 18.04 h for 4DGF (≈5× speedup). On nuScenes, S3R-GS attains 29.21 dB PSNR and 0.139 LPIPS, beating DrivingGaussian (28.74 dB, 0.237 LPIPS) and all NeRF-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S3R-GS outperforms all ten baselines on every reported metric across all three datasets. Against the closest 3DGS competitor 4DGF [11], S3R-GS gains +0.71 dB PSNR and −0.012 LPIPS on Argoverse 2 while halving reconstruction time (26.71 h vs 54.39 h); on KITTI large-scale scenes the PSNR margin widens to +1.22 dB (0009) and +1.88 dB (0020) with a 5–5.3× speedup. StreetGaussian [53] trails by 1.69 dB PSNR on KITTI 0009 and 1.88 dB on 0020, and also requires roughly 2× more reconstruction time. On the smaller KITTI NVS benchmark (Table 3), S3R-GS leads 4DGF by 0.41–0.67 dB PSNR across 25/50/75% training fractions. On nuScenes, S3R-GS beats DrivingGaussian [59] by 0.47 dB PSNR and 0.098 LPIPS, and surpasses the best NeRF-based method EmerNeRF [54] by 4.98 dB PSNR. The NeRF baselines (Mip-NeRF360 [3], UC-NeRF [7], SUDS [44], ML-NSG [12], NSG [34], MARS [50]) are consistently 3–9 dB below S3R-GS in PSNR, confirming the advantage of explicit Gaussian representations for large-scale street scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

S3R-GS builds directly on the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation (Kerbl et al., 2023) and the dynamic urban 3DGS pipeline established by 4DGF (Fischer et al., 2024), which introduced neural fields for color and the local-to-global transformation paradigm. The paper's core departure is a systematic identification of three computational redundancies in the conventional pipeline—unnecessary local-to-global transforms, redundant 3D-to-2D projections, and uniform α-blending of distant content—and their targeted elimination via instance-specific projection, temporal visibility filtering, and adaptive LOD culling. Additionally, it replaces the widely-used 3D bounding box decomposition with a 2D box + NeuralODE formulation, carving out a more accessible annotation pathway that prior street-scene 3DGS methods (DrivingGaussian, StreetGaussian, HUGS) did not explore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-constant per-viewpoint reconstruction cost as scene scale grows (Fig. 4) addresses a fundamental scalability bottleneck that has limited the practical deployment of 3DGS-based street reconstruction, making large-scale autonomous driving simulation and corner-case synthesis more tractable. The 2D box + NeuralODE decomposition lowers the annotation barrier substantially, opening the door to in-the-wild scenarios where 3D boxes are impractical. Open questions include whether the adaptive LOD threshold and temporal visibility mechanism generalize to highly dynamic or occluded scenes, whether the NeuralODE trajectory model degrades over very long sequences, and whether the BEV-semantic augmentation can be replaced by learned priors to remove the dependency on 2D segmentation models.
