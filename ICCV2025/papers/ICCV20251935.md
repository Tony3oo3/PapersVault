## Problem

Existing specular highlight removal methods rely on multi-stage pipelines or multi-label datasets, making training complex and limiting generalization to real-world images with challenging conditions like achromatic surfaces and complex lighting. No prior approach efficiently leverages the generative capabilities of large-scale pretrained diffusion models in a single inference step for this task.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image containing specular highlights and produces the corresponding diffuse (highlight-free) image in one forward pass. The input is encoded by a VAE encoder into a latent representation, to which Gaussian noise is added; a fixed text prompt ('remove specular highlight from image') serves as the conditional state throughout the diffusion process. Zero-convolution modules inject the original VAE encoder features into the decoder via skip connections to preserve high-frequency structural details in the output.

## Method

The approach fine-tunes Stable Diffusion Turbo (a one-step distilled diffusion model) using a novel parameter-efficient adapter called ProbLoRA, which modifies standard LoRA by applying a sigmoid-weighted importance score to each row of the low-rank matrix A, computed via a max-mean measurement inspired by sparse attention mechanisms. This allows the model to selectively emphasize task-relevant adaptation parameters while preserving pretrained knowledge, with the temperature parameter (τ=0.7) controlling the sharpness of the selection. ProbLoRA is applied only to the linear layers within Transformer blocks (not convolutional layers), and the first U-Net layer is also trained, while all other pretrained weights remain frozen. Training uses a combined multi-scale L1 loss (at full, half, and quarter resolution) plus a VGG-based perceptual loss, and the entire model is trained for 85K iterations with rank r=4 on the SHIQ and SSHR datasets.

## Results

The proposed one-step diffusion model with ProbLoRA achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three benchmark datasets. On SHIQ, it reaches 36.19 PSNR and 0.971 SSIM; on SSHR, 35.76 PSNR and 0.965 SSIM; and on PSD, 29.99 PSNR and 0.957 SSIM. These results surpass the strongest prior method (TSHRNet) by 10.61 dB in PSNR on SHIQ and 6.86 dB on SSHR, while also outperforming the LoRA baseline by 2.25 dB on SHIQ and 1.64 dB on SSHR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines on every dataset and metric. Against TSHRNet [3], the strongest prior specular highlight removal method, the gap is substantial: 10.61 dB higher PSNR on SHIQ (36.19 vs. 25.58) and 6.86 dB on SSHR (35.76 vs. 28.90), with SSIM improvements of 0.038 and 0.025 respectively. Compared to the multi-task network of Fu [2], the advantage is even larger on SSHR (35.76 vs. 26.98 PSNR) and PSD (29.99 vs. 21.52 PSNR). Against Wu's GAN-based approach [32], the method leads by 12.77 dB on SHIQ and 8.19 dB on PSD. The closest competitor is Pan [22] (34.34 PSNR, 0.966 SSIM on SHIQ only), which the proposed method surpasses by 1.85 dB in PSNR and 0.005 in SSIM. Internally, ProbLoRA also beats the standard LoRA baseline by 2.25 dB on SHIQ and 1.64 dB on SSHR, confirming the benefit of the probabilistic row-wise adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of one-step diffusion-based image editing (building on SD Turbo and the img2imgturbo framework of Parmar et al.) and parameter-efficient fine-tuning via LoRA. It departs from prior specular highlight removal methods—which typically employ multi-stage pipelines (TSHRNet) or multi-task prediction (Fu et al.)—by reframing the problem as a single forward pass through a distilled diffusion model. The key methodological departure is ProbLoRA, which reinterprets the LoRA low-rank factorization through the lens of sparse attention (Informer) to introduce probabilistic row-wise gating, selectively modulating which adaptation parameters contribute to the output. Zero-convolution skip connections from ControlNet provide structural conditioning, completing the architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-step inference capability is a practical advantage over iterative diffusion methods (e.g., PHR-DIFF) and multi-stage pipelines, making specular highlight removal more deployable in real-time or resource-constrained settings. ProbLoRA's demonstrated generalization to the Fill50K inpainting task (FID 6.788 vs. 10.384 for standard LoRA) suggests the probabilistic row-wise gating mechanism could transfer to other vision tasks, though this is validated on a single prompt. Open questions include whether the approach extends to more challenging real-world conditions (achromatic surfaces, complex multi-lighting scenes) and whether the fixed text prompt can be replaced with more flexible conditioning. The reliance on a single GPU and 85K iterations keeps the training cost modest, but the method inherits the representational limits of the SD Turbo backbone.
