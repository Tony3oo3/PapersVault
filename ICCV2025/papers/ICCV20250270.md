## Problem

Existing camera pose estimation methods (SfM, SLAM, VO) assume the camera is stationary during exposure and fail under fast motion where blur degrades feature matching. Conventional workarounds either discard blurred frames or rely on IMUs, which introduce synchronization and drift issues. No prior method exploits motion blur itself as a signal to estimate camera motion from a single image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250270/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single motion-blurred RGB image at its native resolution, along with the known exposure time τ and (optionally) adjacent video frames for direction disambiguation. It outputs a 6-DoF instantaneous camera velocity (3 translational in m/s, 3 rotational in rad/s) analogous to an IMU reading, plus intermediate dense flow field and metric depth map. The flow field encodes pixel displacement between the first and last 'virtual frames' within the exposure window, not between consecutive video frames.

## Method

A SegNeXt backbone with a shared encoder and separate decoders predicts a pixel-wise flow field and monocular depth map from the single blurred image. These are fed into a differentiable linear least-squares solver built on the motion field equations of Trucco and Verri, which recovers the 6-DoF relative pose (and hence velocity) in closed form. Because the solver is fully differentiable, the entire pipeline trains end-to-end with direct pose supervision. Training proceeds in three stages: flow/depth pretraining on ~120k synthetic samples (ScanNet++v2 frames blurred via RIFE interpolation), joint pose+flow+depth training, and fine-tuning on 10k real motion-blurred images. A photometric-error heuristic using adjacent frames resolves the inherent 180° directional ambiguity of blur, yielding a drift-free velocity estimate at 30 FPS.

## Results

On four real-world motion-blurred video sequences, the proposed method achieves average rotational velocity RMSE of 1.22/0.91/1.76 rad/s (x/y/z) and translational velocity RMSE of 1.11/1.03/0.92 m/s, representing a 31% reduction in rotational RMSE and 24% reduction in translational RMSE over the second-best baseline. COLMAP fails entirely on all four sequences, while the method runs at 30.17 FPS compared to 0.95–4.34 FPS for baselines. The method also outperforms a real IMU's preintegrated velocity estimates, which drift after ~20 seconds, while remaining drift-free throughout the sequence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COLMAP (with both SIFT+AdaLAM and DISK+LightGlue) fails to reconstruct poses on all four test sequences due to severe motion blur, making it non-competitive. MASt3R, the strongest multi-image baseline, achieves average rotational RMSE of 4.04/2.62/3.90 rad/s and translational RMSE of 1.60/1.54/2.17 m/s, while the proposed method achieves 1.22/0.91/1.76 rad/s and 1.11/1.03/0.92 m/s respectively — a 31% and 24% improvement. DROID-SLAM performs slightly worse than MASt3R on rotations (4.41/3.83/4.10 rad/s) but better on translations (2.23/1.63/1.51 m/s), yet is still substantially outperformed by the proposed method on both metrics. The zero-velocity baseline (assuming a stationary camera) actually performs worse than MASt3R on rotations, confirming that the motion-blurred sequences contain significant camera motion. Notably, the proposed method operates on a single image per frame, whereas all baselines require at least two images, making the comparison particularly favorable to the single-image approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in camera pose estimation (SfM/SLAM/VO) that treats motion blur as a nuisance to be discarded or deblurred, instead repurposing blur traces as direct motion cues. It builds on the classical motion-field equations of Trucco and Verri for the linear velocity solver, the SegNeXt backbone from GeoCalib for the network architecture, and RIFE for synthesizing training blur, but the key conceptual departure is formulating single-image 6DoF velocity estimation as an end-to-end differentiable pipeline (flow + depth prediction → least-squares solve). Prior single-image motion-from-blur work (Klein & Drummond, Boracchi) recovered only rotation or translation, and the closest neural approach [61] required a clean depth map as input, whereas this method estimates both flow and metric depth jointly from the blurred image alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for robotics and VR/AR where fast camera motion causes existing SfM/SLAM pipelines to fail entirely, and the 30 FPS real-time operation (vs. <5 FPS for DROID-SLAM) makes it viable for closed-loop control. The demonstration that a single blurred frame can outperform multi-frame methods on velocity estimation challenges the assumption that blur is purely detrimental. Key limitations include the indoor-only training scope, the rigid-scene assumption, the heuristic (rather than learned) direction disambiguation, and the absence of rolling-shutter modeling. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to outdoor scenes, non-rigid motion, and whether it can be integrated as a front-end into existing SLAM systems to replace or augment IMU preintegration.
