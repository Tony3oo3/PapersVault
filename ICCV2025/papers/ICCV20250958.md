## Problem

Existing multimodal LLMs either rely on modality-specific continuous encoders that create a representation gap between visual and textual tokens, or use discrete visual tokens with simple frequency-based BPE that ignores spatial structure. The practical translation of BPE visual tokenization from theory (Being-VL-0) to high-performing models remains unresolved: how to construct vocabories that capture spatial consistency, compose training data to match the hierarchical nature of BPE tokens, and design training procedures that fully exploit these encoded visual tokens.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an RGB image and a text prompt. The image is first patchified and quantized by a VQ-GAN codebook (8K entries) into a 2D grid of discrete indices, then further compressed by the BPE vocabulary (8K or 16K entries) into a 1D token sequence. These visual tokens are bracketed by [BOI]/[EOI] special tokens and concatenated with text tokens to form a single unified sequence fed to the transformer. The model outputs a text response generated autoregressively over the shared vocabulary of text and visual tokens.

## Method

The framework replaces modality-specific visual encoders with a two-stage discrete pipeline: VQ-GAN quantization followed by BPE visual tokenization, where the BPE vocabulary is built via a priority-guided encoding that scores candidate token pairs by P(a,b) = F(a,b) + α·S(a,b), combining normalized co-occurrence frequency with a spatial consistency term (Gaussian-weighted distance between relative positions across images). The pre-trained LLM's embedding layer is expanded to accommodate the new visual tokens (initialized with He initialization), and training proceeds in three stages with progressive parameter unfreezing: (1) train only the new visual embeddings with frozen LM weights, (2) unfreeze the first 25% of transformer layers, (3) full fine-tuning. A curriculum-based data composition shifts the mixture from basic image-caption pairs (Foundation) through perception, reasoning, and finally instruction data across stages, matching the hierarchical complexity of BPE tokens from simple to complex visual patterns.

## Results

Being-VL-0.5+ (8B, 16K BPE vocabulary) achieves 80.6 on VQAv2, 72.1 on MMBench, 1536.3 on MME-P, 86.0 on POPE, and 57.8 on VizWiz, matching or exceeding the best continuous-embedding baselines (e.g., VILA-1.5: 80.9/72.3) while using a fully discrete token representation. The 8K-vocabulary variant Being-VL-0.5 scores 80.2/71.8/1525.8/84.3/57.4, already surpassing the prior discrete-token SOTA Being-VL-0 (60.6/44.0/1316.2/81.3/48.2) by 20+ points on VQAv2 and MMBench. Removing BPE entirely (Being-VL-0.5 w/o BPE) causes a catastrophic drop to 54.3/38.2, confirming that the priority-guided BPE vocabulary is the core driver of performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest discrete-token predecessor Being-VL-0, Being-VL-0.5+ gains +20.0 on VQAv2 (80.6 vs 60.6), +28.1 on MMBench (72.1 vs 44.0), and +220.1 on MME-P (1536.3 vs 1316.2), attributable to priority-guided vocabulary construction and multi-stage training. Versus the strongest continuous-embedding model VILA-1.5, Being-VL-0.5+ is within 0.3 on VQAv2 (80.6 vs 80.9) and 0.2 on MMBench (72.1 vs 72.3), effectively closing the gap between discrete and continuous paradigms. On MMBench, Being-VL-0.5+ (72.1) surpasses LLaVA-Next (67.4), ShareGPT4V (68.8), and LLaVA-1.5 (64.3) by 4.7–7.7 points. The one clear loss is on SciQA-IMG, where Unified-IO-2 scores 86.2 versus 69.0 for Being-VL-0.5+; the authors note their model is evaluated zero-shot on this benchmark without task-specific fine-tuning. On hallucination (POPE), Being-VL-0.5+ (86.0) matches LLaVA-Next (86.5) and exceeds Being-VL-0 (81.3) and Chameleon (78.2), indicating that BPE tokenization does not introduce the hallucination issues typical of continuous-encoder MLLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct practical extension of Being-VL-0 (Zhang et al., ICLR 2025), which established the theoretical case for applying byte-pair encoding to VQ-quantized visual tokens but relied on frequency-only merging. The authors address three gaps left by that work: (1) a priority-guided encoding that incorporates spatial consistency alongside co-occurrence frequency, (2) a curriculum-based data composition aligned with the hierarchical nature of BPE tokens, and (3) a three-stage progressive parameter-unfreezing schedule. Conceptually, the paper sits at the intersection of NLP subword tokenization (BPE) and discrete visual representation learning (VQ-GAN), positioning itself against both the dominant continuous-embedding MLLM paradigm (LLaVA, InstructBLIP, VILA) and other discrete-token approaches (Chameleon, Unified-IO-2) that use VQ without BPE-style merging.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant result is that a discrete-token MLLM can now match continuous-embedding models on most standard benchmarks (e.g., 80.2 vs. 80.9 on VQAv2, 71.8 vs. 72.3 on MMBench), suggesting that the modality gap can be closed through better tokenization rather than relying on CLIP-style encoders. The ablation showing a ~26-point VQAv2 drop when BPE is removed underscores that hierarchical visual token merging is not a minor refinement but a load-bearing component. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 8B parameters, whether the 16K vocabulary's underutilized tokens can be activated with more data, and whether the unified token space naturally extends to multimodal generation as the authors speculate.
