## Problem

Existing parametric CAD generation methods (DeepCAD, SkexGen, HNC-CAD) rely on Transformer self-attention with quadratic complexity, limiting them to short sequences (<60 commands) and making them unable to model industrial-grade CAD designs containing hundreds of commands. The core gap is the lack of an efficient architecture that can capture long-range geometric and topological dependencies in CAD command sequences while maintaining computational tractability for sequences of length 60–256.

## Input / Output

For generation, the model takes Gaussian noise in a latent space of shape N×256×64 and iteratively denoises it over T diffusion steps to produce a latent vector, which is then decoded into a fixed-length sequence of 256 parametric CAD commands (6 command types, each with 16 parameters quantized to 256 discrete levels, unused entries padded with −1). For autoencoding, the input is a raw parametric command sequence (padded to 256 with ⟨EOS⟩ tokens) and the output is the reconstructed sequence. The introduced ABC-256 dataset contains 13,705 models with sequence lengths strictly between 60 and 256, extracted via the Onshape FeatureScript API from CSG representations.

## Method

MamTiff-CAD is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 trains an autoencoder whose encoder stacks four Mamba+ blocks—each a dual-branch SSM module where a forget gate (G_f = 1 − σ(G_b2)) modulates the SSM output to retain historical information while extracting new features—followed by a compression block that maps the sequence to a compact latent Z; a non-autoregressive Transformer decoder (4 blocks) reconstructs the full command sequence from Z and learnable positional embeddings, trained with cross-entropy loss (β=2 weighting for parameters). Stage 2 freezes the encoder and trains a Multi-Scale Transformer diffusion denoiser (6 encoder blocks + MST core) on the latent distribution; each MST layer runs three parallel windowed-attention branches (sizes 64, 128, 256) capturing local geometry, medium-range topology, and global semantics, fused via a learnable sigmoid gating MLP, with time-step conditioning through adaptive scale/shift/residual parameters. The key architectural insight is pairing linear-complexity SSM encoding (avoiding quadratic attention over 256-length sequences) with multi-scale attention-based denoising in latent space, enabling coherent long-sequence generation from pure noise.

## Results

On the ABC-256 dataset (sequence lengths 60–256), MamTiff-CAD achieves state-of-the-art unconditional generation with MMD 1.32, JSD 3.19, Coverage 65.31%, and Step Ratio 85.38%, outperforming all baselines on every metric. For autoencoding reconstruction, the method reaches 99.99% command accuracy, 99.93% parameter accuracy, and a median Chamfer Distance of 0.75×10⁻³, with only 8.50% invalid sequences. On the unseen Fusion 360 Gallery dataset, MamTiff-CAD generalizes well, maintaining 99.99% command accuracy and 95.16% Step Ratio.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MamTiff-CAD outperforms all three baselines on every generation metric. Against DeepCAD, the gains are most dramatic: JSD drops from 6.49 to 3.19 (51% reduction) and Step Ratio jumps from 23.96% to 85.38%, reflecting DeepCAD's inability to handle sequences beyond 60 commands. Compared to SkexGen, MamTiff-CAD improves MMD from 2.31 to 1.32 and Step Ratio from 75.26% to 85.38%, while also achieving near-perfect uniqueness (99.6 vs. 80.5). Against the strongest baseline HNC-CAD, MamTiff-CAD still leads on all metrics—MMD 1.32 vs. 1.63, JSD 3.19 vs. 4.25, and Step Ratio 85.38% vs. 80.86%—with the largest relative gap in JSD (25% lower), indicating better distributional fidelity. In the autoencoding task (Table 2), MamTiff-CAD also dominates: 99.99% command accuracy vs. 92.24% (DeepCAD), 89.72% (MT-CAD), and 86.09% (MLSTM-CAD), with MCD of 0.75 vs. 41.02–121.35 for baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parametric CAD generation line initiated by DeepCAD (autoregressive Transformer over command sequences) and its successors (SkexGen, HNC-CAD), all of which are constrained to sequences under 60 commands due to Transformer's quadratic attention cost. The key architectural departure is replacing the autoregressive Transformer encoder with a Mamba+ (selective SSM augmented with a forget gate) for linear-complexity long-sequence encoding, and replacing autoregressive decoding with a non-autoregressive latent diffusion model (multi-scale Transformer denoiser) trained in the autoencoder's latent space. The paper also introduces the ABC-256 dataset, which pushes sequence lengths to 256 commands via Onshape FeatureScript extraction, filling a gap left by DeepCAD (max 60) and Fusion 360 Gallery (short sketch-extrude sequences).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to apply latent diffusion to parametric CAD command sequence generation, and the ABC-256 dataset (13,705 models, 60–256 commands) provides a much-needed benchmark for long-sequence CAD modeling that prior datasets could not support. The practical impact is significant for industrial design workflows where models routinely exceed 100 commands, yet the authors acknowledge that the dataset does not cover all industrial CAD command types and that the framework does not directly exploit B-rep geometric features, leaving open the question of how to unify CSG and B-rep representations for more generalizable generation. Future work on conditional generation (text/image-guided) and integration with interactive design loops would extend the framework's applicability.
