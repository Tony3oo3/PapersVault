## Problem

Pre-trained diffusion models are limited to their training resolution (e.g., 1024² for SDXL), and existing tuning-free upscaling methods each suffer from distinct failure modes: ScaleCrafter produces local repetition, DemoFusion causes small object duplication, and FouriScale introduces unnatural colors and textures. The root cause is the inevitable increase in high-frequency information when generating beyond training resolution, which accumulates into repetitive patterns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251520/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a frozen pre-trained U-Net diffusion model (SDXL for images, VideoCrafter2 for videos) with no fine-tuning or additional data. It progressively scales resolution from the training size up to 8192² (16×) for images and 640×1024 for videos, all on a single A800 GPU. A spatially-varying cosine decay parameter allows per-region control over how much new detail is introduced at each upscaling stage, enabling local semantic editing via cross-attention injection.

## Method

FreeScale is a three-component tuning-free inference pipeline. First, Tailored Self-Cascade Upscaling progressively denoises at training resolution, upscales (in RGB space for images to suppress over-frequency, in latent space for videos), re-noises to an intermediate timestep, and continues denoising at the higher resolution. Second, Restrained Dilated Convolution enlarges the receptive field but is applied only in down-blocks and mid-blocks (not up-blocks) and only in early timesteps to avoid messy textures. Third, and most critically, Scale Fusion replaces the standard self-attention with a dual-path computation: global self-attention captures full-image semantics while local patch-based self-attention (via shifted crop sampling) enhances local focus; the two are fused by extracting high-frequency components from the global output and low-frequency components from the local output using a Gaussian blur low-pass filter, thereby eliminating both local repetition and small object duplication simultaneously.

## Results

On text-to-image generation with SDXL, FreeScale achieves FID of 44.72 at 2048² and 49.80 at 4096², substantially outperforming all tuning-free baselines (next best: DemoFusion at 72.38 FID at 4096²). FreeScale also attains the best KID (0.001 at 2048², 0.004 at 4096²) and IS (12.57 at 4096²) among all methods. For text-to-video generation with VideoCrafter2 at 640×1024, FreeScale achieves the best FVD (484.71), Dynamic Degree (0.383), and Aesthetic Quality (0.621) while being the fastest method (3.79 min). The paper demonstrates that FreeScale can extend SDXL to 8192² resolution without any fine-tuning on a single A800 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeScale decisively outperforms ScaleCrafter on all quality metrics at both resolutions (e.g., FID 49.80 vs. 100.42 at 4096²), while also being ~33% faster (6.24 vs. 9.26 min). Against DemoFusion, FreeScale wins on FID (49.80 vs. 72.38), KID (0.004 vs. 0.020), and IS (12.57 vs. 12.45) at 4096², and is nearly 2× faster (6.24 vs. 11.38 min); DemoFusion retains a slight IS edge at 2048² (13.28 vs. 12.75). FreeScale dominates FouriScale across all metrics at both resolutions (FID 49.80 vs. 93.08 at 4096²) and is ~26% faster. SDXL-DI (direct inference) paradoxically achieves the best FIDc and KIDc because its duplicated objects make local crops closer to the reference, but it suffers the worst global FID (134.08) and IS (7.04) at 4096², confirming severe structural degradation. For video generation, FreeScale beats VC2-DI on FVD (484.71 vs. 611.09), Dynamic Degree (0.383 vs. 0.191), and Aesthetic Quality (0.621 vs. 0.580) while also being faster (3.79 vs. 4.08 min).

## Relation to Prior Work

FreeScale sits at the intersection of several tuning-free higher-resolution generation lines: self-cascade upscaling (from Make-A-Cheap-Scaling and DemoFusion), dilated convolution for receptive-field expansion (ScaleCrafter), and frequency-domain signal manipulation (FouriScale). Rather than adopting any single strategy, it composes them and introduces a new 'Scale Fusion' module that splits self-attention into a global pass (retaining high-frequency detail) and a local patch pass (retaining low-frequency semantics), recombining them via a Gaussian-blur frequency split. This departs from FouriScale's aggressive high-frequency removal and DemoFusion's patch-averaging by treating the frequency separation as a soft, learnable-free operation embedded directly in the self-attention layer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FreeScale demonstrates that 8k-resolution text-to-image generation is achievable on a single A800 GPU without any fine-tuning, which is practically significant for practitioners who cannot afford retraining at higher resolutions. The tuning-free nature and minimal time overhead make it immediately applicable to existing SDXL and VideoCrafter2 deployments. However, the method is tied to U-Net architectures (the authors explicitly note DiT models are too memory-hungry for single-GPU high-res inference), and the 8k result relies on a specific cascade schedule; whether the same frequency-fusion principle transfers to transformer-based diffusion backbones or to even higher resolutions remains open. The local semantic editing capability (injecting different text prompts into masked regions) is an interesting side effect that could open a new direction for spatially-conditional generation without additional training.
