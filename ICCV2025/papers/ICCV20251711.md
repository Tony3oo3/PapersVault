## Problem

Despite MLLMs demonstrating strong 2D image and video understanding, no publicly standardized benchmark exists to evaluate their ability to understand 4D objects (3D objects with temporal evolution). This is critical because 4D object understanding requires multi-view spatial-temporal reasoning—integrating appearance across viewpoints and tracking motion over time—that neither existing 2D video benchmarks nor static 3D scene benchmarks capture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251711/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For 4D object QA, the model receives K=3 views × N=6 frames (18 total frames) sampled from rendered multi-view videos of a single 4D object, along with a question and four answer choices; it outputs the selected choice. For 4D object captioning, the same multi-view frame input is provided without a question, and the model generates a free-form text description covering both appearance and actions. The 4D objects are synthetic assets rendered from Objaverse-XL, deliberately including counterfactual objects (e.g., a six-legged spider) to test whether models rely on prior knowledge rather than visual evidence.

## Method

The paper introduces 4D-Bench, a benchmark constructed through a multi-stage pipeline: (1) rendering tens of thousands of dynamic 3D objects from Objaverse-XL into multi-view videos, (2) filtering via pixel-change motion detection and a fine-tuned CLIP-based visual quality classifier, (3) generating 751 QA pairs across five subtasks (Appearance, Action, Object Counting, Spatial Relationship, Temporal Relationship) using a hybrid approach where GPT-4o and Qwen2-VL generate questions via chain-of-thought prompting, followed by automated validation with Qwen2-VL 7B, blind text-only filtering with Qwen2.5 and Llama 3.1, and manual review, and (4) collecting five human-written captions per object for 580 selected 4D objects. Evaluation employs both traditional n-gram/embedding metrics (BLEU, ROUGE, CIDEr, METEOR, BERTScore, SBERT) and GPT-4o-based LLM scoring (GPT-Appearance, GPT-Action, GPT-Eval on a 0–5 scale) for captioning, with accuracy for QA. The benchmark serves as an out-of-distribution evaluation since synthetic 4D assets with counterfactual properties are absent from real-world training data.

## Results

On the 4D object QA task, GPT-4o achieves the highest overall accuracy at 62.98%, followed by LLaVA-Video 72B (62.32%) and LLaVA-OneVision 72B (61.38%), while the human baseline reaches 91.08%. The weakest subtask across all models is Object Counting (37.29% average accuracy), while Appearance is the strongest (63.92% average). On 4D object captioning, GPT-4o attains the best GPT-Eval score of 3.382/5 versus a human score of 3.826/5, with all models scoring higher on GPT-Appearance than GPT-Action, indicating weaker temporal/action understanding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o leads all evaluated MLLMs with 62.98% overall QA accuracy, narrowly outperforming LLaVA-Video 72B (62.32%) and LLaVA-OneVision 72B (61.38%), while Gemini 1.5 Pro trails at 59.52%. Among 7B open-source models, Qwen2-VL 7B (56.99%) and LLaVA-Video 7B (56.86%) are the strongest, substantially ahead of MiniGPT4-Video (23.17%) and VideoChat2 (32.36%). A striking finding is that all models collapse on Object Counting (37.29% average) despite achieving 63.92% on Appearance, revealing a severe multi-view fusion weakness. On captioning, closed-source models (GPT-4o: 3.382, Gemini 1.5 Pro: 3.147, GPT-4o mini: 3.221 GPT-Eval) consistently outperform open-source alternatives, with only Qwen2-VL 72B (3.057) crossing the 3.0 threshold. Notably, open-source models like LLaVA-Video 7B (3.235) and Qwen2-VL 72B (3.324) match or exceed Gemini 1.5 Pro (3.311) on GPT-Appearance, but fall behind on GPT-Action, highlighting a temporal understanding gap between open and closed-source models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first benchmark for 4D object understanding by MLLMs, a task that sits at the intersection of 3D-language understanding (e.g., ScanQA, SceneVerse) and 2D video understanding (e.g., MVBench, Video-MME) but requires neither to be sufficient alone. Rather than building on a specific prior architecture, it repurposes existing MLLMs by representing 4D objects as multi-view video sequences, leveraging the rich world knowledge these models have already acquired from 2D data. The annotation pipeline draws on recent MLLM-assisted data generation approaches (GPT-4o, Qwen2-VL for QA synthesis) combined with human validation, and the data curation uses a CLIP-based quality filter trained on manually annotated samples from Objaverse-XL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

4D-Bench fills a clear gap: no prior public benchmark evaluated MLLMs on the joint multi-view spatial-temporal understanding that 4D objects demand, and the synthetic/counterfactual nature of the data provides a strong OOD generalization test. The findings that even GPT-4o reaches only 63% accuracy (vs. 91% human) on simple single-object 4D QA, and that object counting averages just 37%, highlight substantial limitations that will guide future research. Open questions include how to develop view-selection and dynamic frame-sampling strategies (targeted view selection improved performance by 3.6%), whether vision-based chain-of-thought can overcome the failure of text-based CoT (which actually degraded accuracy by 9.72%), and how to close the temporal-understanding gap that persists even as open-source models approach closed-source performance on appearance tasks.
