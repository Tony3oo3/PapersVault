## Problem

Existing unsupervised visible-infrared person re-identification (USL-VI-ReID) methods assume precisely paired cross-modality data during training, which contradicts the unsupervised paradigm and is inconsistent with real-world surveillance where unpaired settings are prevalent (e.g., a person captured by daytime visible cameras may never appear in nighttime infrared footage). This paper introduces the first public visible-infrared pedestrian benchmarks under unpaired settings and tackles the challenge of establishing cross-modality associations without any paired identity supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251322/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled visible and infrared person images resized to 288×144 pixels, where a hyper-parameter α (0.25–1.0) controls the proportion of identities that have no cross-modality counterpart. It produces modality-invariant feature embeddings via a TransReID backbone, which are used for cross-modality retrieval ranking evaluated by Rank-1, mAP, and mINP. The unpaired training data is constructed by replacing a portion of visible identities with external images from Market-1501, MSMT17, and LLCM while preserving overall dataset size.

## Method

The MCL framework comprises two components. First, a Cross-modality Feature Mapping (CFM) module estimates modality-specific mean and variance of feature distributions and applies a learnable affine transformation to project features into the target modality's space, synthesizing fake cross-modality positive pairs that preserve identity. Second, a Static-Dynamic Collaborative (SDC) learning strategy performs dual-level alignment: static learning uses four memory banks (actual and fake, per modality) updated via momentum to compute multi-ClusterNCE losses at the cluster level, while dynamic learning applies a Cross-modality Label Preserving (CLP) contrastive loss at the instance level to pull each fake feature toward its corresponding actual feature. The overall loss combines both with a balancing weight η, and the full pipeline is trained for 50 epochs with CFM and SDC activated in the final 20 epochs after an initial pre-training phase.

## Results

Under unpaired settings (α=0.5), MCL achieves 54.29% Rank-1 and 53.98% mAP on SYSU-MM01 (All Search), surpassing the best baseline N-ULC (49.36% r1, 45.12% mAP) by ~5% in both metrics. On RegDB (Visible→Infrared), MCL reaches 60.78% r1 and 59.69% mAP versus N-ULC's 50.24% r1 and 45.96% mAP. Under paired settings, MCL attains 62.95% r1 and 62.71% mAP on SYSU-MM01 (All Search), outperforming all unsupervised baselines and approaching supervised methods like DFLN-ViT (59.84% r1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCL outperforms all five unsupervised baselines (ADCA, OTPA, MMM, PCAL, N-ULC) across every unpaired ratio α from 0.25 to 1.0 on both datasets. The advantage is most pronounced at α=1.0 (fully unpaired), where MCL achieves 45.98% r1 on SYSU-MM01 versus N-ULC's 34.40% and ADCA's 23.48%, a gap of over 11 and 22 percentage points respectively. Under paired settings, MCL (62.95% r1, 62.71% mAP on SYSU-MM01 All Search) surpasses the strongest unsupervised competitors N-ULC (61.81% r1), MMM (61.60%), and GUR (60.95%), while remaining competitive with the supervised DFLN-ViT (59.84% r1) but trailing top supervised methods such as PartMix (77.78%) and MUN (76.24%). On RegDB paired, MCL reaches 89.83% r1 (Visible→Infrared) and 88.64% r1 (Infrared→Visible), edging out N-ULC (88.75% / 88.17%) and MMM (89.70% / 85.80%). The consistent margin across all α values confirms that the CFM + SDC design specifically addresses the cross-modality identity mismatch that degrades existing methods as unpairedness increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ADCA [32], a dual-path contrastive learning framework with modality-specific memory banks for unsupervised VI-ReID, extending it to handle the absence of cross-modality identity pairs. It departs from the entire line of USL-VI-ReID methods (H2H, OTLA, PGM, DOTLA, GUR, MMM, PCAL, N-ULC) that implicitly or explicitly require paired cross-modality data for pseudo-label generation. The Cross-modality Feature Mapping module repurposes the classical moment-matching idea from domain adaptation (affine transformation to align mean and variance) into a cross-modality feature space, while the Static-Dynamic Collaborative strategy extends single-memory contrastive learning to multi-memory banks operating in both original and pseudo-identity spaces, drawing on ISE-style label-preserving losses for instance-level alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing and benchmarking the unpaired setting, this work addresses a critical gap between the assumptions of existing USL-VI-ReID methods and real-world surveillance where visible and infrared cameras rarely capture the same individuals simultaneously. The constructed unpaired benchmarks (using external ReID datasets to replace identities) provide a reproducible evaluation protocol, though the artificial nature of the unpaired data limits direct transferability to truly organic unpaired scenarios. Open questions include whether the simple affine CFM can capture complex non-linear modality discrepancies at scale, how the method behaves under extreme unpaired ratios (α=1.0 shows notable degradation), and whether the approach generalizes beyond the two-modality visible-infrared setting to broader multi-spectral ReID.
