## Problem

Existing human-centric interaction datasets cover only specialist interaction categories (e.g., handover, single-object manipulation) and ignore the egocentric first-person perspective that physical AI assistants actually rely on to perceive and react. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing InterVLA, the first large-scale dataset that integrates diverse human-human, human-object, and human-scene interactions within a vision-language-action framework with stable egocentric perception.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252191/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sequence provides two egocentric GoPro videos (head-mounted and chest-mounted, 5312×2988 at 30 fps), five exocentric RGB videos (1920×1080 at 30 fps), GPT-generated verbal commands, and OptiTrack-captured motions. Human motion is parameterized as SMPL (23×6 body pose, 6D global orientation, 3D root translation, 10D shape), while objects are represented as rigid bodies with 6D rotation and 3D translation. Downstream tasks consume subsets of these modalities: motion estimation uses only the head-mounted egocentric video; synthesis takes initial poses, object meshes, and text; prediction tasks use past motion frames or historical egocentric video plus language.

## Method

The core contribution is a hybrid RGB-MoCap capturing system combining two egocentric GoPro cameras, five exocentric cameras, and a 20-camera OptiTrack infrared system in an 8.5×5.4 m volume. Reflective markers (12.5 mm) are glued directly to skin and clothing rather than using MoCap suits, preserving RGB fidelity while enabling precise tracking; objects are scanned with a KSCAN Magic Scanner and tracked as rigid bodies with ≥4 markers. SMPL parameters are fitted via a weighted optimization balancing joint-position accuracy, inter-frame smoothness, and regularization. The dataset is organized around 100 GPT-generated scripts (averaging 2–3 furniture items, 5 objects, and 8 atomic commands each), yielding 3,906 sequences across 27 instructor-assistant pairs. Four benchmark tasks are established—egocentric motion estimation, text-driven interaction synthesis, motion-based prediction, and vision-language prediction—each evaluated with re-implemented state-of-the-art baselines (WHAM, TRACE, GLAMR, TRAM; MDM, priorMDM, HIMO; CAHMP, InterDiff; FHOI, OCT, USST) under a unified 80/15/5 train/test/validation split.

## Results

On the egocentric human motion estimation task, WHAM achieves the best MPJPE (333.6 mm), PVE (359.7 mm), and Accel (8.7), while TRACE leads on PA-MPJPE (91.2 mm); all methods show large errors (MPJPE > 330 mm) indicating the difficulty of world-grounded estimation from egocentric video. For interaction synthesis, HIMO attains the best FID (0.6805) and R Precision (0.5707), yet all methods remain far from the real-data FID of 0.0203. In motion-based interaction prediction, CAHMP achieves the lowest joint error (172.5 mm), translation error (115.6 mm), and rotation error (9.5°). For vision-language interaction prediction, USST achieves the best average displacement error (0.24 m) and final displacement error (0.32 m).

## Baselines & Comparisons

For egocentric human motion estimation, WHAM dominates on MPJPE (333.6 mm vs. 589.9–720.1 mm for others), PVE (359.7 mm), and temporal smoothness (Accel 8.7), while TRACE achieves the best PA-MPJPE (91.2 mm vs. 102.7–134.8 mm), suggesting TRACE better preserves relative pose but struggles with global alignment. In interaction synthesis, HIMO outperforms MDM and priorMDM on R Precision (0.5707 vs. 0.4897/0.5250), FID (0.6805 vs. 2.8039/6.2766), and MM Dist (4.9609 vs. 5.4879/5.5129), though priorMDM leads on MModality (2.1227 vs. 1.9888/1.1478). For motion-based interaction prediction, CAHMP achieves the best joint error (172.5 mm), object translation error (115.6 mm), and rotation error (9.5°), with InterDiff marginally ahead of MDM on translation (138.7 vs. 140.2 mm) and rotation (10.8° vs. 11.0°). In vision-language interaction prediction, USST beats OCT and FHOI on both average displacement error (0.24 vs. 0.28/0.29 m) and final displacement error (0.32 vs. 0.36/0.38 m). Overall, no method achieves satisfactory performance on any task, confirming InterVLA as a challenging benchmark for egocentric interaction understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Vision-Language-Action (VLA) paradigm from instruction-following robotics (RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA) to structure human-human interaction data collection, where the 'assistant' is a human wearing egocentric cameras rather than a robot. It carves new ground by unifying three previously siloed dataset families—human-human interactions (InterHuman, Inter-X, You2Me), human-object interactions (HIMO, HOH, HOI-M³), and human-scene interactions—into a single egocentric multi-object framework with precise optical MoCap ground truth for both humans and objects. Unlike Ego-Exo4D or EPIC-Kitchens which provide egocentric video without 4D motion ground truth, or CORE4D which offers egocentric HOH data but only for one interaction type, InterVLA combines all modalities across diverse interaction categories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InterVLA is the first dataset to simultaneously provide egocentric and exocentric RGB, verbal commands, and high-precision 4D human/object motions in multi-object human-object-human scenarios, making it a natural testbed for physical AI assistants and robot learning from human demonstrations. The benchmarks reveal that even the strongest SOTA methods (WHAM at 333.6 mm MPJPE, HIMO at FID 0.68) remain far from satisfactory, quantifying the substantial gap between current capabilities and the demands of real-world egocentric assistance. Open questions include whether these methods can close the gap with more data or architectural changes, and how the VLA formulation transfers to actual robot deployment. The dataset's scale (11.4 hours, 1.2M frames) is modest compared to video-only datasets, and the 27 unique pairs limit demographic diversity.
