## Problem

Existing human image completion methods—whether non-reference or reference-based—fail to faithfully reconstruct unique, fine-grained details such as specific clothing patterns, tattoos, and distinctive accessories. Non-reference approaches lack the source information entirely, while current reference-based inpainting methods struggle to establish accurate spatial correspondences between the occluded input and reference images, especially when poses differ significantly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250403/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a masked human image (occluded via random grid masking of 1–30 iterations or human body shape masks), one or more reference images each depicting a distinct body part (upper body, lower body, shoes, hair/headwear, face), corresponding binary reference masks, and an optional text prompt generated by LLaVA. It outputs a fully completed RGB human image. The architecture is flexible at inference: it operates with as few as a single reference image and can optionally incorporate textual guidance, making it practical for real-world editing workflows.

## Method

CompleteMe employs a dual U-Net architecture: a Reference U-Net (initialized from Stable Diffusion 1.5, operating at timestep zero without diffusion noise) encodes multiple reference images into spatially-detailed latent features, while a Complete U-Net (initialized from SD 1.5 inpainting) processes the masked source image. The key mechanism is the Region-focused Attention (RFA) Block, which explicitly masks reference features using per-part reference masks, concatenates them with the input latent, and applies attention (Q from input, K/V from concatenated features) to establish precise fine-grained spatial correspondences. A decoupled cross-attention mechanism (borrowed from IP-Adapter) then fuses the refined local features with global CLIP semantic features via two separate cross-attention operations whose outputs are summed. Training uses MSE loss with a random-drop strategy (0.2 probability) on both reference features and individual reference conditions to ensure robustness to partial or missing reference information.

## Results

On the proposed 417-image benchmark, CompleteMe achieves the best scores on all four perceptual/semantic metrics: CLIP-I (97.18), DINO (96.29), DreamSim (0.0419, lower is better), and LPIPS (0.0588, lower is better). It trails LeftRefill slightly on pixel-level metrics (PSNR 28.70 vs 28.87, SSIM 0.9239 vs 0.9283) and BrushNet on CLIP-T (29.83 vs 30.69). In the user study (2,895 judgments from 15 annotators), CompleteMe is preferred over all four reference-based baselines by 85–99% on both Quality and Identity criteria.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CompleteMe outperforms all six baselines on the four perceptual/semantic metrics (CLIP-I, DINO, DreamSim, LPIPS), with the largest margins over AnyDoor (CLIP-I +7.53, DreamSim −0.1035) and LOHC (DINO +13.77, DreamSim −0.0313). Among reference-based methods, CompleteMe leads MimicBrush by +0.20 CLIP-I and −0.0232 DreamSim, and LeftRefill by +0.85 CLIP-I and −0.0155 DreamSim. However, LeftRefill retains a small edge on pixel-fidelity metrics (PSNR +0.16, SSIM +0.0044), and BrushNet scores higher on CLIP-T (+0.86), suggesting CompleteMe's completions are slightly less text-aligned. In the user study, CompleteMe is preferred over LeftRefill (the closest competitor) by 85.03% on Quality and 95.42% on Identity, and over AnyDoor by 99.45% on Quality, confirming that the quantitative gaps translate into clear human-perceived advantages in detail preservation and identity consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

CompleteMe extends the reference-based inpainting line (Paint-by-Example, AnyDoor, LeftRefill, MimicBrush) from generic object-level insertion to the specific challenge of preserving fine-grained human identity details (tattoos, unique clothing patterns, accessories) during occluded-region completion. Architecturally, it builds on Stable Diffusion 1.5's latent U-Net as the backbone for both its Reference and Complete U-Nets, and adopts IP-Adapter's decoupled cross-attention to fuse local spatial features with global CLIP semantics. The key departure from prior reference-based methods is the Region-focused Attention (RFA) Block, which uses explicit reference masks to constrain attention to semantically relevant body-part regions, rather than relying on global feature matching as CLIP or DINOv2-based encoders do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in human image editing: without reference images, even state-of-the-art inpainting methods cannot reproduce a person's unique visual identity, and existing reference-based methods struggle with fine-grained correspondence under pose changes. The introduced 417-group benchmark with significant pose differences and unique-pattern requirements provides the community a missing evaluation standard for this sub-task. Limitations include training on a fashion-centric dataset (DeepFashion-MultiModal), a relatively small benchmark, and evaluation limited to a single in-house dataset, leaving open questions about generalization to diverse demographics, real-time inference, and scalability to multi-person or video settings.
