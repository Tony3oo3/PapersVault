## Problem

Mamba-based video models like VideoMamba achieve markedly lower accuracy than transformer-based counterparts (e.g., 82.4% vs. 85.2% top-1 on Kinetics-400), yet the underlying reasons for this gap were not formally understood. This paper identifies two fundamental limitations in Mamba's token processing—historical decay, where earlier tokens lose influence due to the lower-triangular structure of its similarity matrix, and element contradiction, where interleaved token structures in vision create conflicting requirements on the evolution matrix—that are particularly detrimental for video understanding where relevant tokens are sparse and unordered.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a video clip of shape 3×T×H×W, which is converted into L = t×h×w non-overlapping patch tokens via a 3D convolution with a 1×16×16 kernel, then augmented with learnable spatial and temporal position embeddings and a [CLS] token. The output is a single action class label obtained from the [CLS] token after normalization and linear projection. Experiments use 16–64 frames at 224² or 336² spatial resolution depending on the benchmark, with multi-crop evaluation (e.g., 4×3 temporal×spatial crops) for test-time augmentation.

## Method

VideoMambaPro (VMP) extends VideoMamba's bi-directional Mamba backbone with two targeted modifications. First, to address historical decay, it applies masked backward computation: the diagonal elements of the backward similarity matrix M_b are set to zero before combining with the forward result, eliminating the duplicated self-similarity on the diagonal without the side effect of uniformly scaling down all off-diagonal weights (as a simple 0.5 coefficient would). Second, to address element contradiction, it introduces a residual SSM where the recurrence becomes m_ij = Ā_i·m_{i-1,j} + Ā_i, distributing the propagation requirement across multiple terms so that a single Ā_i no longer must simultaneously satisfy conflicting constraints imposed by interleaved irrelevant tokens. The theoretical analysis formalizes Mamba's output as Y = C̄(MX), showing M is a lower-triangular matrix analogous to self-attention's similarity matrix S, and derives the recurrence m_ij = Ā_i·m_{i-1,j} that causes the contextual dependency problem. Both modifications are orthogonal to existing Vision Mamba advances and are validated through ablations, a patch-replacement robustness test, and a progressive masking experiment on ImageNet-1K.

## Results

On Kinetics-400, VideoMambaPro-M (72M params, 4.7T FLOPs) achieves 84.0% top-1 accuracy with 224² inputs and 85.0% with 336² inputs, surpassing VideoMamba-M by 1.6% and 1.7% respectively. On Something-Something V2, VideoMambaPro-M reaches 69.4% top-1, a 1.1% gain over VideoMamba-M. On UCF-101 and HMDB51, VideoMambaPro-M scores 91.6% and 63.2%, outperforming VideoMamba-M by 3.4% and 1.8%. On AVA V2.2, VideoMambaPro-M achieves 31.9 mAP with only 183G FLOPs and 72M parameters, compared to VideoMAE V2's 42.6 mAP at 4220G FLOPs and 1050M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoMambaPro consistently outperforms its direct baseline VideoMamba across all model sizes and datasets: +1.6–3.0% top-1 on K400, +1.1–1.9% on SSv2, +3.4% on UCF-101, and +1.8% on HMDB51. On K400, VideoMambaPro-M (84.0%) surpasses Uniformer-B (82.0%), MViTv2-S (81.0%), and ViViT-L (81.3%), but remains below VideoMAE-H (86.6%) and X-CLIP-L/14 (87.7%), which benefit from IN-21K or CLIP-400M pre-training. On SSv2, VideoMambaPro-M (69.4%) trails MViTv2-B (70.5%) and VideoMAE-L (75.4%) but exceeds MViTv1-B (67.1%) and TimeSformer-L (62.5%). On UCF-101, VideoMambaPro-M (91.6%) edges out VideoMAE (91.3%) and substantially beats VideoMamba-M (88.2%), though it falls short of GDT (95.2%) and VideoMAE V2 (99.6%). The key efficiency advantage is stark: VideoMambaPro-M uses 72M parameters and 4.7T FLOPs versus 633M/17.9T for VideoMAE-H and 1300M/86.2T for InternVideo-1B, while being pre-trained only on ImageNet-1K rather than multi-dataset corpora.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends VideoMamba [22] and the broader Mamba [11] selective state-space model lineage by providing the first systematic theoretical analysis of why Mamba underperforms transformers in vision and video tasks. While prior Vision Mamba works (Vision Mamba [61], VMamba [27]) introduced bi-directional processing to mitigate token-ordering issues, this paper shows that approach still suffers from diagonal similarity duplication and a deeper 'element contradiction' problem where the multiplicative SSM chain creates conflicting requirements on evolution matrices when relevant tokens are interleaved with irrelevant ones. The two proposed fixes—masked backward computation and residual SSM—are positioned as orthogonal to existing Vision Mamba advances, meaning they can be layered onto future architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of historical decay and element contradiction as principled, mathematically-grounded limitations of SSMs in vision provides a clear roadmap for future Mamba-based video models, moving the field beyond trial-and-error architectural modifications. The efficiency gains are substantial in practice: VideoMambaPro-M achieves 84.0% top-1 on K400 with only 72M parameters and 4.7T FLOPs, compared to 1000M+ parameters for competitive transformers. However, the remaining gap to heavily pre-trained models like InternVideo2 (7.6% on K400) is large, and the paper itself acknowledges that extensive pre-training on diverse video corpora is likely needed to close it. An open question is whether the residual SSM and masking ideas transfer cleanly to 2D/3D Mamba variants (e.g., MambaND) or to other SSM-based architectures beyond the 1D sequence formulation.
