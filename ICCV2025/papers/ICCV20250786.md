## Problem

Multi-view diffusion models for 3D content creation suffer from a significant gap in image quality and prompt-following ability compared to 2D diffusion models, primarily due to the scarcity of high-quality 3D training data with detailed captions. Existing approaches rely on small subsets of Objaverse, leading to motion blur, distortion, and catastrophic forgetting of 2D priors, while improving view consistency often comes at the cost of aesthetic quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250786/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes free-form text prompts as input and produces 4-view images arranged in a 2×2 grid, which can be directly fed into sparse view reconstruction models (GRM, InstantMesh) to yield textured 3D meshes or Gaussian splatting representations. The MV-LLaVA component separately takes 4 multi-view images as input and outputs a human-aligned quality score plus a dense descriptive caption. Training data consists of 200K Objaverse-rendered 4-view pairs, 1M synthetic 4-view pairs from SV3D/Zero123++, and 35K high-quality single-view images from ShareGPT4V.

## Method

Bootstrap3D introduces a synthetic data generation pipeline that chains GPT-4 prompt construction, PixArt-α single-view synthesis, SV3D novel-view generation, and a fine-tuned MV-LLaVA (based on LLaVA with CLIP-L/14 vision encoder) for quality filtering and dense caption rewriting, producing 1M+ high-quality multi-view image-text pairs. The key training innovation is Training Timestep Reschedule (TTR), which restricts synthetic multi-view data to large denoising time steps (t ∈ [200, 1000]) so they only contribute low-frequency structural information, while Objaverse-rendered images sample more frequently in [50, 200] and high-quality single-view data is limited to t ∈ [0, 50] for texture details. This leverages the frequency decomposition inherent in the diffusion denoising process to prevent residual motion blur in synthetic data from degrading high-frequency texture while still learning multi-view consistency from the structural components. The fine-tuned PixArt-α (DiT-XL/2 backbone) is trained with batch size 1024 at learning rate 8e-5 for 20K steps on 32 A100 GPUs, with text features and VAE features pre-extracted.

## Results

On the primary multi-view image generation benchmark (Table 2), PixArt+Bootstrap achieves the best CLIP-R Score of 88.8 (CLIP-L/14) and 92.5 (CLIP-bigG), CLIP Score of 25.8/40.1, and FID of 42.4 (PG2.5) / 41.6 (FLUX), substantially outperforming the next-best T2MV method MVDream (FID 60.2) and Instant3D (FID 83.2). For 3D object generation via sparse-view reconstruction (Table 3), Bootstrap3D+GRM achieves CLIP-R 86.3/91.6 and FID 51.2/55.4, while Bootstrap3D+InstantMesh reaches CLIP-R 87.1/92.0 and FID 61.2/59.6, both leading all baselines. On view consistency (MEt3R, lower is better), SDv2.1+Bootstrap achieves the best score of 0.2359, with PixArt+Bootstrap at 0.2391, both competitive with or better than all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Bootstrap3D (PixArt+Bootstrap) achieves the best CLIP-R Score (88.8 vs. 84.8 for MVDream, 83.6 for Instant3D) and the lowest FID (42.4 vs. 60.2 for MVDream, 83.2 for Instant3D, 55.7 for SV3D) among all multi-view diffusion baselines, indicating substantially better prompt-following and image quality. Against image-to-multi-view methods (SyncDreamer, HarmonyView, SV3D, CRM), Bootstrap3D leads by 8–11 points on CLIP-R Score and by 13–41 points on FID (PG2.5). On view consistency (MEt3R, lower is better), Bootstrap3D variants achieve the best scores (0.2359–0.2413), narrowly beating CRM (0.2382) and clearly outperforming MVDream (0.3021) and Instant3D (0.3179). For 3D object generation (Table 3), Bootstrap3D+GRM outperforms Instant3D+GRM by 4.6 CLIP-R points and 34.2 FID points, and Bootstrap3D+InstantMesh surpasses Zero123+++InstantMesh by 13.9 CLIP-R points and 21.1 FID points. The ablation (Table 4) confirms that removing TTR causes severe degradation (FID jumps from 42.4 to 133.5), while replacing Cap3D captions with MV-LLaVA dense captions adds ~4 CLIP-R points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three active threads: (1) fine-tuning 2D diffusion models for multi-view generation (Instant3D, MVDream, Zero123++), which are bottlenecked by the small, noisy Objaverse dataset; (2) video diffusion for novel view synthesis (SV3D), previously used as an inference-time tool rather than a data generator; and (3) MLLMs for quality assessment and captioning (GPT-4V, LLaVA, Cap3D). The key departure is repurposing video diffusion as a *training-data synthesizer* and coupling it with a fine-tuned MLLM (MV-LLaVA) for filtering and dense recaptioning, then introducing the Training Timestep Reschedule (TTR) to prevent motion-blur artifacts in synthetic frames from corrupting the diffusion prior. The TTR idea draws on timestep curriculum strategies explored in SDS-based 3D optimization (DreamTime, Hifa) but applies them at training time for mixed-data fine-tuning rather than at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the central data-scarcity bottleneck in text-to-3D generation and demonstrates that a synthetic-data bootstrap pipeline (video diffusion + MLLM filtering + TTR) can close much of the gap between multi-view diffusion models and 2D T2I models in both prompt-following and image quality. A notable limitation is that the pipeline currently depends on SV3D as the sole novel-view synthesizer, constraining data diversity, and the MEt3R view-consistency scores for Bootstrap3D (≈0.24) remain below Instant3D (≈0.32) and MVDream (≈0.30) on the multi-view image benchmark, suggesting the consistency gains are realized primarily after 3D reconstruction rather than in the raw views. Open questions include whether stronger open-source video diffusion models will unlock substantially more diverse synthetic data, and whether the TTR threshold (t=200) generalizes across architectures and data sources.
