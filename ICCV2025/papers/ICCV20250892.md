## Problem

Tracking individual neurons in 3D fluorescence microscopy recordings is extremely challenging because neurons are densely packed, nearly indistinguishable in appearance, and subject to complex non-rigid brain deformations. Existing deep learning methods for this task require extensive manual track annotations (identity labels across many frames), which are costly and limit scalability. ASCENT addresses this by learning discriminative neuron embeddings in a fully self-supervised manner, eliminating the need for any track-level labeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250892/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 4D fluorescence microscopy volumes (T×C×D×H×W) and per-frame neuron detection coordinates (x, y, z) from off-the-shelf detectors (StarDist or Cellpose) as input. The 3D volume depth must be a power of two to satisfy the iterative volume compression constraint, and only the stable red channel is used. The output is a 256-dimensional discriminative embedding vector per detected neuron, which is then used for track linking via serial bipartite matching across consecutive frames, producing a set of tracked neuron trajectories.

## Method

The core architecture, VCE-Net, compresses each 3D volume into a multi-scale 2D representation by iteratively performing Maximum Intensity Projection along the z-axis and halving the depth at each step (log₂D steps total), concatenating all projections into a single multi-channel 2D image. Two additional channels encode relative (x,y) positional offsets from each pixel to the neuron center (inspired by CoordConv), and a separate local patch pathway extracts fine-grained features from a small sub-volume around the neuron. These global and local features are processed by a ResNet-18 backbone and mapped through a projection head to a compact embedding. Training uses a SimCLR-style contrastive objective (NT-Xent loss) where a batch is formed from all detections in a single frame (ensuring all pairs are different neurons), two independently augmented views are generated via rotation, elastic deformation, resized cropping, position jitter, and intensity variations, and the network is trained to pull together embeddings of the same neuron under different augmentations while pushing apart embeddings of different neurons. At inference, volume projections are precomputed once per frame and shared across all detections, reducing embedding computation to under 0.1 s per volume.

## Results

On the in-house dataset, ASCENT achieves 98.85% Modified MOTA with 1.56 s/vol total inference (0.07 s/vol for tracking only), and in tracking-only mode reaches 99.98% accuracy. On the NeRVE benchmark, ASCENT attains 95.39% Modified MOTA (97.66% in tracking-only mode), surpassing the best prior method ZephIR (94.43%) by 0.96% while requiring zero manual labeling time versus ZephIR's estimated 53 minutes. The embedding and track-linking modules complete in under 0.1 s per volume, with the majority of inference time consumed by the upstream StarDist detector.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASCENT outperforms ZephIR [56] by 0.96% Modified MOTA on NeRVE (95.39% vs. 94.43%) in full-pipeline mode, and by 3.23% in tracking-only mode (97.66% vs. 94.43%), while eliminating the 53 minutes of manual labeling that ZephIR requires. Against CeNDeR [75] (86.51%) and fDNC [77] (79.1%), ASCENT leads by 8.9 and 16.3 percentage points respectively on NeRVE, and both of those methods also demand extensive track annotations (130 frames / 342 min for CeNDeR). The NeRVE method [49] achieves only 82.9% accuracy with >40 s/vol inference, far behind ASCENT's 95.39% at 2.42 s/vol. 3DeeCellTracker [72] reports 99.8% accuracy but under a non-standard protocol (visual inspection of a 90-track subset over 500/1532 volumes) and at 86 s/vol inference, making direct comparison unreliable. Overall, ASCENT is the only method combining state-of-the-art accuracy, zero annotation cost, and sub-second tracking-only inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

ASCENT adapts the SimCLR contrastive learning paradigm (NT-Xent loss, paired augmented views) to the 3D neuron tracking domain, where prior methods either required manual track annotations (ZephIR, CeNDeR) or relied primarily on positional features without rich image context (fDNC, 3DeeCellTracker). The core architectural novelty is the Volume Compression Embedding Network (VCE-Net), which iteratively projects 3D volumes into multi-scale 2D representations along the z-axis, enabling the use of standard 2D backbones (ResNet) at a fraction of 3D convolutional cost. The relative positional encoding channels draw directly from CoordConv, and the overall pipeline (detect → embed → bipartite match) follows the tracking-by-detection structure common in multi-object tracking, but the self-supervised training objective is the key departure from annotation-dependent baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for track-level annotations, ASCENT removes a major practical bottleneck in 3D neuron tracking that has limited adoption of deep learning methods in this domain. The volume compression module's dramatic advantage over both 3D convolutions (95% vs. 58%) and single MIP (95% vs. 83%) suggests a general principle for efficiently encoding volumetric context in 2D networks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to organisms with far more neurons (zebrafish, Drosophila, mammalian cortex), whether the learned embeddings can support inter-individual neuron identity matching, and whether the pipeline can be made real-time by improving the upstream detection stage. The current evaluation is limited to C. elegans and two datasets, so broader generalizability remains untested.
