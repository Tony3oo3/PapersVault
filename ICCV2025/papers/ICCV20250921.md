## Problem

Previous multi-view indoor 3D object detectors lift 2D features to 3D voxels via single-point sampling at fixed locations, restricting each voxel's receptive field and amplifying sensitivity to geometric errors. Additionally, dense 3D volume construction wastes computation on the large free-space regions inherent to indoor scenes, and many methods require ground-truth scene geometry for supervision, limiting practical applicability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250921/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes N posed RGB images (40 for training, 100 for testing) at 320×240 resolution along with camera intrinsics and extrinsics, producing 3D bounding boxes (axis-aligned for ScanNet/ScanNet200, oriented for ARKitScenes). The 2D feature maps are 80×60, and the 3D volume is 40×40×16 (voxel size 0.2m×0.2m×0.16m) or 80×80×32 for the larger SGCDet-L variant. Notably, the entire network is supervised solely with 3D bounding box annotations, requiring no ground-truth scene geometry or depth maps during training.

## Method

SGCDet introduces two key mechanisms for adaptive 3D volume construction. First, a geometry and context aware aggregation module lifts 2D features into a 3D pixel space via outer product with depth distributions, then applies 3D deformable attention to sample features from adaptive neighborhoods around each voxel's projected location, followed by a multi-view attention that dynamically reweights contributions across views. Second, a sparse volume construction strategy builds the 3D volume in a coarse-to-fine manner over L refinement stages, where a lightweight occupancy head selects only the top-25% voxels by predicted occupancy probability for feature refinement at each stage, avoiding redundant computation in free space. Occupancy supervision is achieved through pseudo-labels generated directly from 3D bounding boxes, eliminating the need for ground-truth geometry. A DepthNet fuses multi-view plane-sweeping cost volumes with monocular depth features to provide the depth distributions used for 2D-to-3D projection.

## Results

On ScanNet, SGCDet achieves mAP@0.25 of 61.2 and mAP@0.50 of 35.2, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art MVSDet (56.2 / 31.3) by +5.0 and +3.9 respectively, while reducing training memory by 42.9%, training time by 47.2%, inference memory by 50%, and inference time by 40.8%. On ARKitScenes, SGCDet reaches 62.3 / 44.7 (mAP@0.25 / mAP@0.50), and the larger SGCDet-L variant achieves 70.4 / 57.0, outperforming CN-RMA (67.6 / 56.5) which uses a higher voxel resolution (192×192×80) and ground-truth geometry supervision. On ScanNet200, SGCDet-L attains 28.9 mAP@0.25 overall, with 14.9 on the tail (smallest) object group, substantially ahead of ImGeoNet (22.3 / 9.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MVSDet [40], the previous state-of-the-art without ground-truth geometry, SGCDet improves mAP@0.25 by 5.0 (61.2 vs 56.2) and mAP@0.50 by 3.9 (35.2 vs 31.3) on ScanNet, while cutting training memory from 35 GB to 20 GB and inference memory from 28 GB to 14 GB. Compared to NeRF-Det [38] and ImVoxelNet [29], SGCDet gains 7.7 and 14.5 in mAP@0.25 respectively, confirming that adaptive aggregation and sparse construction substantially outperform single-point sampling and dense volume construction. Against CN-RMA [30], which relies on ground-truth geometry and a multi-stage training pipeline, SGCDet achieves a higher mAP@0.25 (61.2 vs 58.6) at a comparable mAP@0.50 (35.2 vs 36.8), while using far less training time (19 h vs 242 h) and no geometry supervision. On ARKitScenes, SGCDet-L (70.4 / 57.0) surpasses CN-RMA (67.6 / 56.5) despite using a lower voxel resolution (80×80×32 vs 192×192×80) and no ground-truth geometry. On ScanNet200, SGCDet-L (28.9 mAP@0.25) outperforms ImGeoNet [31] (22.3) by 6.6 points, with the largest gap on small objects (tail group: 14.9 vs 9.7).

## Relation to Prior Work

SGCDet extends the multi-view indoor 3D object detection line initiated by ImVoxelNet, which lifts 2D features along rays, and subsequent geometry-aware methods (ImGeoNet, NeRF-Det, CN-RMA, MVSDet) that incorporate opacity or explicit depth to suppress free-space features. Its key conceptual departure is twofold: (1) replacing single-point sampling with a 3D deformable attention in pixel space (adapted from DFA3D's autonomous-driving formulation to use view-specific queries rather than view-agnostic ones) plus a multi-view attention for inter-view fusion, and (2) introducing a coarse-to-fine sparse volume construction guided by occupancy prediction, where pseudo-labels are derived from 3D bounding boxes rather than ground-truth scene geometry. This positions SGCDet as a method that simultaneously addresses the receptive-field limitation and the computational waste of dense volume construction without requiring expensive geometric supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the dependency on ground-truth scene geometry for supervision, SGCDet removes a significant practical barrier for deploying multi-view 3D detectors in settings where only 3D bounding box annotations are available (e.g., ARKitScenes-style mobile captures). The 50% reduction in inference memory and 40.8% reduction in inference time relative to MVSDet, while improving mAP@0.50 by 3.9 points, makes the approach substantially more deployable in real-time or resource-constrained indoor perception pipelines. A clear open question highlighted by the authors is the depth estimation bottleneck: using ground-truth depth yields 64.3 mAP@0.25 versus 61.2 with the learned DepthNet, indicating that more accurate multi-view depth (or learned geometry) remains the primary lever for further gains. The robustness to noisy bounding-box annotations (Table 7) also suggests the pseudo-labeling strategy could extend to datasets with imperfect or partial annotations.
