## Problem

Learning-based stereo matching networks lack robustness across diverse real-world domains due to scene differences and imbalanced disparity distributions. While Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) offer strong general-purpose features, they produce limited zero-shot performance for dense cross-view matching, and existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods (fixed-rank LoRA, uniform CNN decoders) apply rigid one-size-fits-all refinement that cannot adapt to the varying complexity of in-the-wild stereo scenes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a rectified RGB stereo image pair and outputs a dense disparity map via iterative GRU-based cost aggregation over a correlation volume pyramid. During training, image pairs are randomly cropped to 320×832 pixels with 16 recurrent iterations; inference uses 24 iterations. The model is first pre-trained on the synthetic SceneFlow dataset (35K samples) and then fine-tuned on mixed real-world datasets (KITTI 2012/2015, Middlebury, ETH3D) for joint generalization evaluation.

## Method

SMoEStereo integrates two heterogeneous MoE modules into each ViT block of a frozen VFM backbone (DAMV2, SAM, or DINOv2) within a RAFT-Stereo architecture. The MoE-LoRA layer contains experts with different matrix ranks (4, 8, 16, 32), and a learned router dynamically selects the optimal rank per input to adapt long-range feature refinement; the MoE-Adapter layer contains CNN experts with different kernel sizes (3, 5, 7, 9) to inject local geometric inductive bias into the transformer. A lightweight decision network, trained with Gumbel-softmax, predicts binary activation masks for each MoE layer, selectively skipping redundant modules for simple inputs while retaining them for complex ones. A usage loss regularizes the fraction of activated layers toward a target budget γ, and a balance loss encourages uniform expert utilization. The total training objective combines L1 disparity supervision with these two auxiliary losses, requiring only ~6.8M trainable parameters versus 97.5M for full fine-tuning.

## Results

On cross-domain generalization (zero-shot from SceneFlow), SMoEStereo with DAMV2 (ViT-Base) achieves 4.22 / 4.86 / 7.05 / 2.10 on KITTI 2012 / KITTI 2015 / Middlebury / ETH3D (Bad 3.0/3.0/2.0/1.0), outperforming the strongest VFM-based baseline Former-RAFT (ViT-Large) on three of four datasets while using a smaller backbone. In the Robust Vision Challenge (joint generalization with a single fixed model), SMoEStereo_RVC attains 1st overall rank, recording 9.74% bad-2.0 on Middlebury, 1.73% D1-All on KITTI 2015, and 1.13% bad-1.0 on ETH3D. On zero-shot DrivingStereo under adverse weather, SMoEStereo achieves 4.3 average D1, beating the best prior method (Former-CFNet, 5.0) by 0.7 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest VFM-based baseline Former-RAFT (ViT-Large, DAM) [79], SMoEStereo (ViT-Base, DAMV2) wins on KITTI 2015 (4.86 vs 5.1), Middlebury (7.05 vs 8.1), and ETH3D (2.10 vs 3.3) while using roughly half the activated parameters (2.86M vs 6.9M) and 2.6× faster inference (0.18s vs 0.47s); it trails only on KITTI 2012 (4.22 vs 3.9). In the RVC joint-generalization setting (Table 6), SMoEStereo_RVC achieves 1st overall rank, edging out LoS_RVC (2nd; 9.30 vs 9.74 Middlebury bad-2.0, 1.83 vs 1.73 KITTI D1-All, 1.47 vs 1.13 ETH3D bad-1.0) and CREStereo++RVC (3rd). On zero-shot DrivingStereo, SMoEStereo (4.3 avg D1) surpasses Former-CFNet (5.0) and IGEVStereo (10.3), with the largest margin under rainy conditions (6.0 vs 8.3). Among PEFT methods on the same DAMV2 backbone (Table 4), SMoE outperforms LoRA (4.47/5.03/7.67/2.83), AdapterTuning (4.40/5.09/8.03/2.61), and VPT (4.65/5.18/8.44/2.71) on all four cross-domain benchmarks, while also reducing inference-time parameters from 4.72M to 2.86M.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RAFT-Stereo iterative stereo matching backbone by replacing its CNN feature extractor with Vision Foundation Models (DAMV2, SAM, DINOv2) and introducing a Selective Mixture-of-Experts (SMoE) module for parameter-efficient adaptation. It departs from prior PEFT methods (VPT, Adapter, fixed-rank LoRA) by making the adaptation heterogeneous and input-conditioned: LoRA experts with varying matrix ranks and CNN adapter experts with varying kernel sizes are dynamically routed per input, rather than applying a uniform low-rank subspace or fixed decoder. The selective activation of entire MoE layers via a Gumbel-softmax decision network is a novel efficiency mechanism not present in prior MoE-for-PEFT work, which typically activates all experts at every layer. Compared to the closest prior work (Zhang et al., ECCV 2025), which attaches a fixed CNN decoder to VFMs for stereo matching, SMoEStereo replaces the rigid decoder with scene-adaptive experts and adds a learned policy to skip redundant layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployment: SMoEStereo achieves SOTA cross-domain and joint generalization with only ~3–4M activated parameters (vs. 97.5M for full fine-tuning) and sub-0.2s inference on a single GPU, making VFM-based robust stereo matching feasible on resource-constrained platforms. The plug-and-play design across multiple VFM backbones (DAMV2, SAM, DINOv2) suggests the framework could transfer to other dense prediction tasks requiring robust cross-domain generalization. Open questions include whether the learned decision policies generalize to truly unseen domains without retraining, whether the approach benefits from even larger VFMs (ViT-Huge), and whether the heterogeneous expert design is necessary or whether a simpler adaptive mechanism could achieve comparable gains. The paper does not explore self-supervised or unsupervised adaptation, leaving the reliance on SceneFlow pre-training as a potential bottleneck for novel sensor modalities.
