## Problem

Fluorescence Diffraction Tomography (FDT) can reconstruct 3D label-free structures of semi-transparent biological samples without transmission access, but requires hundreds of 2D images, making it too slow for dynamic intravital imaging. Existing neural field approaches (explicit, implicit, triplane) either demand many views for volumetric reconstruction or introduce grid artifacts from low-rank decomposition, and none incorporate the diffraction physics essential at microscale where object dimensions are comparable to the wavelength of light.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250123/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 6–7 sparse-view fluorescence diffraction images (512×512 pixels) captured from a single fixed camera position, where the 'views' arise from different internal point-source fluorescence positions (excited by a focused laser) rather than external camera angles. Fluorescence source positions are self-calibrated via Gaussian fitting and jointly optimized during training. The output is a full 3D refractive index volume (e.g., 512×512×12 voxels at 0.3×0.3×2 µm voxel size) with micron-scale resolution and optical sectioning across axial planes.

## Method

NVP is a hybrid neural representation that stores a learnable F-dimensional feature vector at each voxel of a sparse 3D grid W_xyz and maps it through a 3-layer MLP F_nvp to produce the RI value, combining the sparsity prior of explicit grids with the spatial-correlation modeling of implicit fields. The rendering equation uses a multi-slice Born approximation that propagates the light field through N_z thin slices, applying a transmission function derived from the local RI and a propagation operator at each step to model diffraction, then computing intensity as |E|² at the camera. Training minimizes a composite loss (L1, L2, SSIM on coherence-masked images plus total-variation regularization on the RI volume) using Adam on a single GPU, converging in ~20 minutes. A coherent-alignment mask bridges the gap between the coherent diffraction model and the partially coherent fluorescence measurements, while self-calibration jointly refines source positions with network parameters to eliminate the need for external calibration.

## Results

On the synthetic cells dataset, NVP with only 6 fluorescence images achieves a RI PSNR of 30.73, SSIM of 0.8910, and LPIPS of 0.1034, outperforming both the explicit method (PSNR 28.62) and triplane method (PSNR 28.73) under the same sparse-view condition. Scaling to 20 images yields only marginal gains (PSNR 31.38, SSIM 0.8959), confirming robustness to view count. On real MDCK cells (19 images), NVP reaches SSIM 0.9977 and PSNR 40.70 for predicted images, while converging in ~20 minutes versus 60 minutes for the explicit baseline. Overall, NVP reduces the required number of 2D images by ~50-fold and processing time by 3-fold compared to prior work.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NVP is compared against two baselines: the explicit neural field method (He et al., 2024) and the triplane representation (Fridovich-Keil et al., 2023). On synthetic cells with 6 illuminations, NVP outperforms the explicit method by +2.11 PSNR (30.73 vs. 28.62) and +0.037 SSIM (0.8910 vs. 0.8540), and outperforms triplane by +2.00 PSNR and a substantial +0.178 SSIM (0.8910 vs. 0.7128), indicating triplane's low-rank decomposition introduces significant grid artifacts in volumetric settings. Notably, NVP with 6 images matches or exceeds the explicit method's performance with 20 images (PSNR 30.73 vs. 28.88), demonstrating the ~3× view reduction. On real MDCK cells, NVP achieves PSNR 40.70 vs. 36.65 (explicit) and 32.69 (triplane), with the triplane gap being particularly large (−8.01 PSNR) due to severe grid artifacts in high-dimensional volumetric reconstruction. NVP wins on all reported metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) across all three datasets and both RI and predicted-image evaluations, with no comparison where a baseline outperforms NVP.

## Relation to Prior Work

NVP extends the explicit neural field approach for fluorescence diffraction tomography (He et al., 2024) by adding a lightweight MLP on top of the non-parametric grid, creating a hybrid representation that captures spatial correlations the pure grid cannot. The rendering equation is grounded in the multi-layer Born approximation for diffraction optics (Chen et al., 2020) and the FDT imaging geometry (Xue et al., 2022), departing from the ray-optics assumptions that underpin most sparse-view 3D reconstruction methods in computer vision. The paper explicitly contrasts with triplane/K-planes representations, arguing that their low-rank decomposition introduces grid artifacts unsuitable for volumetric (as opposed to surface) reconstruction at the microscale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing the required number of 2D images by ~50× (from hundreds to 6–7) and cutting processing time by 3×, NVP opens the door to real-time 3D imaging of dynamic biological processes such as cardiomyocyte contraction and embryo development, which were previously infeasible with FDT. However, validation is limited to cultured MDCK cells and two synthetic datasets; performance on thicker, more heterogeneous in vivo specimens remains untested. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other imaging modalities, whether integrating 3D convolutions or generative priors (diffusion models, masked auto-encoders) can further reduce the view count, and how the method scales to larger volumes or faster dynamics.
