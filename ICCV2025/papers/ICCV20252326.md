## Problem

Low-light image enhancement and low-light visual understanding have been treated as separate problems: enhancement methods rely on physical or geometric priors that limit generalization and are evaluated primarily on pixel-level quality, while understanding methods use task-specific domain adaptation that lacks scalability. No existing framework simultaneously achieves zero-shot generalization across multiple downstream tasks (classification, detection, segmentation) while producing semantically faithful enhanced images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252326/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a low-light RGB image, a short text prompt (e.g., 'normal light photo'), and an illumination map derived from the V channel of the image's HSV representation (both the original and its reverse) as the illumination-aware image prompt. The output is a single enhanced normal-light image produced in one forward pass through the SD-Turbo-based generator (Encoder–UNet–Decoder), with no iterative denoising steps. Training uses 256×256 crops with batch size 1 on ~1k unpaired low-light/normal-light images from EnlightenGAN.

## Method

The framework fine-tunes SD-Turbo via LoRA in a CycleGAN-style cycle generation (lightening then darkening) to learn an unsupervised enhancer. Three key components address semantic degradation: (1) a Cycle-Attention Adapter that uses two sequential cross-attention layers to query illumination-aware image prompt features against multi-scale UNet latents and feed the response back into the prompt, unlike IP-Adapter which treats text and image prompts symmetrically; (2) Caption Consistency, which copies the lightening-stage encoder, feeds a BLIP-generated caption into the initial UNet, and enforces cosine-similarity between its output and the darkening-stage UNet output to preserve high-level semantics; (3) Reflectance Consistency, which adds a reflectance decoder predicting Retinex-based reflectance maps from latent features and enforces both inter-stage consistency and reconstruction against a pre-trained RetinexNet reference to preserve spatial semantics. Training combines cycle L1 loss, GAN loss with CLIP-based discriminators, identity regularization, and the two consistency losses, all optimized with AdamW at lr 1e-5 for 25k iterations on a single GPU.

## Results

On the GEFU benchmark (Table 2), the proposed method achieves 60.92% Top-1 on CODaN classification, 16.9% mAP on DARK FACE detection, and 20.1% mIoU on BDD100k-night semantic segmentation, setting new state-of-the-art on all three tasks among unsupervised enhancement methods. Against zero-shot day-night domain adaptation methods (Table 3), it further reaches 29.4% mAP on DARK FACE and 20.1% mIoU on BDD100k-night, outperforming DAI-Net (28.0% mAP, 18.2% mIoU) and Sim-MinMax (25.7% mAP, 18.6% mIoU). On traditional image quality (Table 4), the method scores 18.41 PSNR / 0.2974 LPIPS on LSRW and 21.32 PSNR / 0.1928 LPIPS on LOL, competitive with the best unsupervised baselines while using only ~1k training images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 12 unsupervised enhancement baselines on all three GEFU tasks. On CODaN classification, it leads Zero-DCE++ (59.88%) by 1.04 points and QuadPrior (59.48%) by 1.44 points; on DARK FACE detection, it surpasses PairLIE (16.0%) by 0.9 mAP and LightenDiffusion* (16.4%) by 0.5 mAP; on BDD100k-night segmentation, it exceeds NeRCo (18.1%) by 2.0 mIoU and SCI (18.0%) by 2.1 mIoU. Against supervised methods, it beats CIDNet (58.32% Top-1, 14.5% mAP, 17.4% mIoU) on all three tasks despite requiring no paired training data. In the domain adaptation comparison (Table 3), the method achieves 29.4% mAP and 20.1% mIoU, surpassing DAI-Net (28.0%, 18.2%) and Sim-MinMax (25.7%, 18.6%) while remaining task-agnostic. On traditional image quality, LightenDiffusion (trained on 180k images) retains a PSNR advantage on LOL (22.79 vs. 21.32), but when retrained on the same ~1k-image dataset, it drops to 18.27 PSNR, well below the proposed method's 21.32.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CycleGAN-Turbo one-step translation architecture and SD-Turbo (adversarial diffusion distillation) as its generative backbone, extending the IP-Adapter's decoupled cross-attention into a Cycle-Attention Adapter that feeds image-prompt features back to themselves. It departs from prior low-light enhancement work (Retinex-based, GAN-based, or physics-prior methods) by leveraging a pretrained diffusion model's semantic prior for zero-shot generalization, and from domain adaptation methods by being task-agnostic rather than task-specific. The key conceptual departure is the GEFU paradigm: rather than optimizing for pixel-level image quality or jointly training with a single downstream task, the paper frames enhancement as a bridge to multiple pretrained normal-light models evaluated directly without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GEFU benchmark provides a practical evaluation protocol that directly measures whether an enhancer improves downstream understanding, addressing a gap where prior methods optimized PSNR/SSIM without verifying task-level gains. The finding that visual quality metrics are not strictly correlated with downstream performance (e.g., NeRCo and LightenDiffusion excel in PSNR but underperform on classification/detection/segmentation) challenges a common assumption in the field and motivates semantic-fidelity-oriented enhancement. Limitations include moderate inference speed compared to lightweight methods, reliance on ~1k training images, and the need for pretrained normal-light models at test time; open questions include whether the visual-quality/semantic-quality gap can be formally characterized and whether knowledge distillation can yield real-time variants.
