## Problem

Cross-modal identity annotations for visible-infrared person ReID are prohibitively expensive because infrared and visible cameras operate asynchronously and the two modalities differ drastically in appearance, making manual pairing labor-intensive. Existing semi-supervised and unsupervised methods that avoid full cross-modal labels still suffer from noisy pseudo-labels that limit performance. This paper introduces a weakly supervised paradigm that requires only intra-modal identity labels, eliminating the need for any cross-modal correspondence annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250274/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes visible and infrared person images (resized to 288×144) with identity labels available only within each modality—no cross-modal identity pairs are provided. It produces two types of output: (1) cross-modal identity correspondence matrices (consistent M_c, unique M_s, and conflicting M_w) that implicitly link identities across modalities, and (2) a shared feature encoder whose embeddings are used for ranked retrieval, evaluated via CMC, mAP, and mINP. The ResNet-50 backbone uses modality-specific first layers with shared subsequent convolutional layers to balance modality-specific and modality-invariant feature extraction.

## Method

The framework has three stages: (1) Heterogeneous Expert Learning (HEL) trains separate identity classifiers W^v and W^r per modality using cross-entropy plus weighted regularization triplet loss, yielding two 'heterogeneous experts' that specialize in different identity cues; (2) Cross-Modal Relationship Establishment (CRE) uses Count Priority Selection to build expert decision matrices, then partitions all cross-modal identity pairs into consistent, unique, and conflicting matches via Hadamard products and zero-indicator functions, exploiting the complementarity between the two experts (e.g., the visible expert struggles with IR due to missing color, while the IR expert does not); (3) Collaborative Consistency Learning (CCL) applies a strong cross-modal cross-entropy loss on M_c/M_s samples, a relaxed negative-class loss on M_w samples to tolerate label noise, and an entropy-weighted prototype-based consistency loss that dynamically adjusts constraint strength based on expert prediction confidence, so that reliable predictions are less constrained while uncertain ones are more tightly aligned.

## Results

On SYSU-MM01 (all-search), the proposed weakly supervised method achieves 70.4% Rank-1 and 66.6% mAP, outperforming all semi-supervised (e.g., DPIS: 58.4% Rank-1, 55.6% mAP) and unsupervised (e.g., GUR: 63.5% Rank-1, 61.6% mAP) baselines while approaching top fully supervised methods (CSDN: 76.7% Rank-1, 73.0% mAP). On LLCM (VIS→IR), it reaches 55.3% Rank-1 and 58.7% mAP, surpassing the best unsupervised method PGM (44.9% Rank-1, 49.0% mAP) by 10.4 and 9.7 percentage points respectively. In indoor-search mode on SYSU-MM01, the method attains 76.5% Rank-1 and 80.2% mAP, competitive with fully supervised CAJ (76.3% Rank-1, 80.4% mAP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method substantially outperforms all semi-supervised baselines: on SYSU-MM01 (all-search) it improves Rank-1 by 12.0% and mAP by 11.0% over DPIS (ICCV'23), the strongest SSVIReID method, and by 22.2% Rank-1 over OTLA (ECCV'22). On LLCM (VIS→IR), it surpasses OTLA by 11.1% Rank-1 and 10.5% mAP. Against unsupervised methods, the gains are equally pronounced: it exceeds GUR (ICCV'23) by 6.9% Rank-1 and 5.0% mAP on SYSU-MM01, and PGM (CVPR'23) by 10.4% Rank-1 and 9.7% mAP on LLCM. However, the method still trails the top fully supervised approaches—CSDN (TMM'25) leads by 6.3% Rank-1 and 6.4% mAP on SYSU-MM01 all-search, and by 8.4% Rank-1 on LLCM VIS→IR—indicating that the absence of explicit cross-modal labels imposes a residual performance gap of roughly 5–8 percentage points relative to the best SVIReID methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new 'weakly supervised' setting for VIReID that sits between semi-supervised (one modality entirely unlabeled) and unsupervised (no labels at all) paradigms: both modalities carry intra-modal identity labels, but cross-modal correspondences are unknown. Methodologically, it builds on the Count Priority Selection technique from ADCA [39] for constructing cross-modal decision matrices and the weighted regularization triplet loss from AGW [48], but repurposes them within a novel heterogeneous expert framework where modality-specific classifiers act as cross-modal predictors. The collaborative consistency learning mechanism, which uses entropy-weighted prototype-based alignment to harmonize expert predictions, departs from prior pseudo-label refinement strategies (e.g., DPIS, MUCG) by treating the two experts as complementary rather than redundant sources of supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The weakly supervised formulation addresses a practical deployment gap: asynchronous camera operation makes cross-modal annotation infeasible in real surveillance systems, yet both modalities can be labeled independently. Achieving performance within a few points of fully supervised methods (e.g., 70.4% vs. 74.7% Rank-1 on SYSU-MM01) without any cross-modal labels suggests a viable path toward reducing annotation costs in cross-modal ReID. Open questions include whether the expert collaboration mechanism scales to more than two modalities, how sensitive the method is to the quality of intra-modal labels (e.g., noisy or partial annotations), and whether the ResNet-50 backbone with shared layers is optimal or whether transformer-based encoders would benefit more from the consistency constraints.
