## Problem

Existing logit-based knowledge distillation methods treat categorical probabilities as singularly deterministic values, implicitly assuming each sample's class membership is a fixed point on the simplex. This deterministic modeling ignores the inherent uncertainty in network predictions (e.g., different weight initializations yielding different predictions), creating a bottleneck that limits the effective transfer of diverse and uncertain knowledge from teacher to student.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250198/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 224×224 for ImageNet) as input to both teacher and student networks. Rather than producing a single softmax probability vector, each network outputs logits that are passed through an exponential activation to form non-negative evidence vectors, which are combined with a prior weight λ to yield K-dimensional Dirichlet parameters. The final classification is obtained from the expectation (centroid) of the Dirichlet distribution, while the full second-order distribution encodes the network's uncertainty over all possible categorical distributions on the (K−1)-simplex.

## Method

EKD reformulates both teacher and student predictions as second-order Dirichlet distributions (evidential deep learning) and distills knowledge at two complementary levels. At the macro level, a first-order loss (KL divergence between the expectations/centroids of the two Dirichlet distributions) aligns the proportional relationships across classes, generalizing vanilla KD as a special case when σ=exp and λ=0. At the micro level, a second-order loss (KL divergence directly between the two Dirichlet distributions) aligns the magnitudes of Dirichlet parameters within each class, providing fine-grained supervision across the entire simplex. The combined objective L_EKD = L_1st + γ·L_2nd is shown via PAC-Bayesian theory to constitute an upper bound on the student's expected risk when treating teacher predictions as ground truth, providing a principled justification for both loss terms. Both teacher and student are trained with the EDL cross-entropy loss (Eq. 1) rather than standard cross-entropy throughout all training stages.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with same-architecture teacher-student pairs, EKD achieves top-1 accuracy improvements of 0.66%–3.61% over vanilla KD, with the largest gains (4.44%, 4.04%, 2.29%) in groups with wider teacher-student performance gaps. On ImageNet, EKD attains 72.54% top-1 / 91.20% top-5 (ResNet50→MNV1) and 71.73% top-1 / 90.69% top-5 (ResNet34→ResNet18), outperforming all logit-based baselines. For ViT students on CIFAR-100, EKD delivers an average 3.75% improvement over KD and 0.25% over AutoKD across four transformer variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EKD consistently outperforms vanilla KD [30] by 0.66–3.61% across all seven CIFAR-100 same-architecture pairs, with the largest margin (4.44%) when the teacher-student gap is widest (ResNet32×4→ResNet8×4). Against the strongest logit-based competitor DKD [88], EKD wins in Groups 1, 2, and 7 (e.g., 77.21 vs. 76.32 in Group 1) but is comparable in Groups 3–6 where the teacher-student gap is smaller (~3.3%). EKD surpasses CTKD [45], Logit_Stand [69], SDD [48], and TeKAP [31] in the majority of CIFAR-100 groups, with the clearest advantage over CTKD (e.g., 77.21 vs. 73.39 in Group 1). On ImageNet, EKD achieves the best top-1 accuracy among all logit-based methods in both ResNet34→ResNet18 (71.73%) and ResNet50→MNV1 (72.54%) settings, edging out DKD [88] by 0.03% and 0.49% respectively. For ViT distillation, EKD improves over AutoKD [42] by an average of 0.25% across DeiT-Ti, PiT-Ti, PVT-Ti, and PVTv2, while surpassing KD [30] by 3.75% on average. Feature-based methods (CAT-KD [23], CRD [70], SimKD [3]) are included as references; EKD is complementary to them, as combining EKD with RKD or CAT-KD yields further gains (Table 5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reformulates logit-based knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015) by replacing the deterministic softmax-to-categorical pipeline with a second-order Dirichlet distribution over categorical probabilities, drawing directly on the evidential deep learning framework of Sensoy et al. (2018) and its roots in Subjective Logic and Dempster-Shafer Theory. It departs from Bayesian distillation methods, which approximate teacher uncertainty via finite ensembles, by using a continuous parametric distribution that requires no sampling. The PAC-Bayesian analysis extends prior single-network PAC-Bayesian bounds for Dirichlet posteriors (Haussmann et al., 2019) to the cross-network setting, establishing a theoretical link between the distillation objective and the student's expected risk.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EKD offers a principled, hyperparameter-light (γ=1 default) drop-in replacement for vanilla KD that captures both inter-class proportions and intra-class magnitudes, with a PAC-Bayesian guarantee that the training objective upper-bounds the student's expected risk. The observation that gains scale with the teacher-student performance gap (5.51% gap → up to 3.61% improvement vs. 3.31% gap → ~2.5%) suggests the method is most valuable when the student has substantial room to grow, while capacity-limited students (e.g., MobileNet) see diminishing returns. Open questions include whether the Dirichlet second-order modeling generalizes to other distribution families (e.g., Gaussian for regression), whether the micro-level alignment transfers to feature-based or hybrid distillation settings beyond the modest compatibility experiments shown, and whether the framework extends naturally to open-set or multi-task distillation.
