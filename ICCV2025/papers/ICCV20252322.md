## Problem

Reconstructing HDR images from modulo camera measurements is a challenging non-linear inverse problem because the periodic wrapping disrupts smooth intensity variations. Existing deep learning methods either treat rollover estimation as a rigid classification task or operate entirely in the image domain without exploiting the physical structure of the modulo operation, limiting their robustness and generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252322/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 8-bit modulo camera image (pixel values wrapped at a=256) and produces a 12-bit HDR image (values up to 4095). Training uses 256×256 crops from 400 images with over-exposure rates between 5% and 30%, while evaluation is performed on full 512×512 images. The modulo image is the sole input; no additional exposures or auxiliary sensors are required.

## Method

The key insight is reformulating modulo HDR reconstruction in the image gradient domain via Itoh's continuity condition, which guarantees that the modulo operation is directly invertible on gradients bounded by a/2. Pixels violating this bound (large-gradient outliers) are handled by an auxiliary variable V with a learned sparsity prior. The optimization is unfolded into a 3-phase deep unfolding network: each phase updates Y via Nesterov accelerated gradient descent followed by a UNet proximal step, and updates V via a spiking-neuron (LIF) module that accumulates residual evidence across phases to produce a binary outlier mask, combined with a convolutional branch for value prediction. Training uses a composite loss of gradient-domain L1 and LogCosh pixel-wise terms applied at every unfolding phase.

## Results

On the synthetic 12-bit HDR reconstruction from 8-bit modulo images, the proposed DUN achieves 42.56 dB PSNR, 0.99 SSIM, and 5.56% NRMSE, outperforming all baselines across all four metrics. The model uses only 4.15M parameters and 62.73G FLOPs, making it the second-lightest method tested (after ExpandNet at 0.46M params) while delivering the best reconstruction quality. On the real-RGB dataset (no ground truth), visual evaluation confirms that DUN produces the most coherent results, avoiding the modulo-pattern artifacts and illumination errors seen in competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed DUN outperforms the strongest modulo-specific baseline, UnModNet [56], by 2.14 dB in PSNR (42.56 vs. 40.42) and 1.08% in NRMSE, while using only ~10% of its parameters (4.15M vs. 40.83M) and ~20% of its FLOPs. Against the general-purpose transformer UFormer [48], DUN gains 0.22 dB PSNR with only 20% of the parameters (4.15M vs. 20.60M) and 38% of the FLOPs. The conventional MRF [53] and PnP-UA [4] baselines are far behind, achieving only 25.08 and 29.38 dB PSNR respectively, confirming the limitations of hand-crafted priors for this problem. Single-image HDR methods (ExpandNet [31], Santos et al. [41]) perform poorly on modulo inputs even after retraining (23.57 and 39.09 dB), highlighting the fundamental mismatch between LDR-to-HDR and modulo-to-HDR tasks. On the real-RGB dataset, DUN produces visually coherent results without the modulo-pattern artifacts visible in UnModNet and PnP-UA outputs, while UFormer achieves comparable but slightly less accurate illumination recovery.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior modulo HDR methods (MRF, UnModNet, PnP-UA) that operate directly in the image domain by reformulating the reconstruction problem in the gradient domain, leveraging Itoh's continuity condition originally developed for phase unwrapping in optics. The deep unfolding network framework follows the broader DUN literature for inverse problems (deconvolution, CT, hyperspectral imaging), but the specific combination of gradient-domain modulo inversion with a spiking-neuron-based sparsity module for outlier handling is novel. Unlike UnModNet's end-to-end classification of rollover counts, this approach explicitly encodes the physical structure of the modulo operation into the network architecture, and unlike PnP-UA's reliance on a pre-trained denoiser, it learns the sparsity prior end-to-end via LIF neurons.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Modulo cameras offer a practical, low-cost path to unbounded dynamic range imaging, and this work provides the first DUN specifically designed for this modality, achieving state-of-the-art quality with only 4.15M parameters. The gradient-domain reformulation via Itoh's condition is a clean theoretical contribution that could transfer to other modulo-based inverse problems (e.g., phase unwrapping, compressed sensing with wrapping). Open questions include whether the spiking-neuron outlier module generalizes beyond the 12-bit/8-bit setting studied here, how the method behaves with more complex noise or sensor imperfections, and whether the approach extends to video or multi-frame modulo capture.
