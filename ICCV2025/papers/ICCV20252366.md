## Problem

Existing visual localization methods face a trade-off between accuracy and privacy: explicit map-based and Scene Coordinate Regression (SCR) methods leak 3D scene geometry, while Absolute Pose Regression (APR) suffers from the compactness of 6-DoF pose parameters making it noise-sensitive. Camera rays, an overparameterized and privacy-preserving pose representation, have not been explored for VL, and the inherent ambiguity in regressing patch-level rays (both global ambiguity across repetitive patches and local ambiguity from varying viewing angles within the same patch) remains unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB query image, uniformly divided into s×s patches, and regresses 6-dimensional Plücker coordinates (3D direction + 3D moment) for each patch center in the world coordinate frame. The final camera pose is recovered by solving two decoupled linear problems: rotation from ray directions and translation from ray moments, optionally refined by a ray-level RANSAC. Because the output consists solely of camera rays rather than 3D scene points, the 3D structure of the scene cannot be inverted, providing inherent privacy protection.

## Method

DIMM employs a scene-agnostic DINOv2 encoder (fine-tuned on the last two blocks) that outputs patch tokens, a CLS token for global perception, and Fourier-encoded XY positional tokens, concatenated per patch to form the input feature. A scene-specific mapper head projects each patch feature into separate geometric and semantic embeddings; the geometric embedding is fed through M parallel MLP sub-mappers, and their ray embeddings are softly fused via a multi-layer cross-attention module where the replicated semantic embedding acts as the query, enabling adaptive, semantics-driven ensemble weighting. Training uses patch-level Gradient Decorrelation Training with a simple L2 ray loss plus a geometric center loss (enforcing that all rays pass through a common camera center), requiring no 3D supervision. At inference, RC-RANSAC first estimates rotation from ray directions via R-RANSAC, corrects all rays with the recovered rotation, then estimates translation via C-RANSAC on the corrected rays.

## Results

On the outdoor MapFree dataset, DIMM-R achieves an average recall@5°/5cm of 86.6% with median errors of 0.7° rotation and 2.4 cm translation, substantially outperforming the best SCR baseline ACE (67.7%) and the best APR baseline marepoS (72.7%). On the indoor Indoor-6 dataset, DIMM-R reaches 63.2% average recall@5°/5cm, matching the strongest SCR baseline D2S (63.1%) while requiring no 3D supervision and only 16.2 MB of model storage. The RC-RANSAC post-processing yields consistent gains, e.g., improving recall from 44.3% to 64.8% on MapFree scene s00006.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SCR baselines, DIMM-R clearly outperforms ACE (86.6% vs. 67.7% recall on MapFree) and DSAC* (86.6% vs. 62.1%), and matches D2S on Indoor-6 (63.2% vs. 63.1%) while avoiding 3D coordinate supervision. Against APR baselines, DIMM-R achieves a 13.9-point recall advantage over marepoS on MapFree (86.6% vs. 72.7%), confirming that the overparameterized ray representation is more robust than direct 6-DoF pose regression. Against FM-based methods (Hloc+SLD), DIMM-R trails by ~5.7 points on Indoor-6 (63.2% vs. 68.9%), but FM methods require explicit 3D maps (~2 GB) and raise privacy concerns, whereas DIMM uses only a 16.2 MB implicit model. The RC-RANSAC variant consistently improves over the raw DIMM output across all scenes (e.g., 66.7%→77.4% on Indoor-6 scene2a; 44.3%→64.8% on MapFree s00006), validating the decoupled rotation-then-translation RANSAC strategy. Notably, DIMM's lightweight architecture achieves the highest inference throughput (57.7 fps) among all compared methods, surpassing even the APR baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces Camera Ray Regression (CRR) as a new implicit-mapping formulation for Visual Localization, positioned between Scene Coordinate Regression (SCR) and Absolute Pose Regression (APR). It directly builds on ACE's scene-centric architecture (scene-agnostic encoder + scene-specific head with Gradient Decorrelation Training) and adopts the ray-to-pose linear solver from 'Cameras as Rays' (Zhang et al., ICLR 2024). The key conceptual departure is replacing unbounded 3D coordinate regression (SCR) or compact pose vectors (APR) with Plücker-coordinate camera rays as the intermediate representation, motivated by bounded ray error for stable optimization and the inability to invert rays back to 3D scene geometry for privacy. The semantic attention multi-mapper head further departs from ACE's single-MLP or hard spatial-partitioning strategies by softly ensembling sub-mappers guided by DINOv2's semantic features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CRR offers a principled middle ground for VL that simultaneously addresses two long-standing tensions: the accuracy-privacy tradeoff (rays cannot be inverted to recover 3D scene structure, unlike SCR's 3D coordinates or FM-based explicit maps) and the optimization-stability tradeoff (bounded ray error avoids the unbounded 3D point error that necessitates outlier rejection in SCR). However, practical limitations remain: mapping time (~1 hour) is substantially longer than ACE (5 minutes), rotation accuracy still lags behind SCR baselines, and the privacy claim is argued theoretically but not tested under adversarial inversion. Open questions include whether CRR scales to very large outdoor scenes without the multi-mapper overhead, whether the semantic attention mechanism generalizes beyond DINOv2's pre-trained semantics, and whether the ~1-hour mapping cost can be reduced through distillation or architectural simplification.
