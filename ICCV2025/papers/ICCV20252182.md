## Problem

Existing 3DGS-based scene understanding methods either embed high-dimensional CLIP features into Gaussian primitives (incurring heavy training overhead and semantic ambiguity from dimensionality reduction) or rely on contrastive learning for instance differentiation (adding significant computational cost to the rendering pipeline). Moreover, 3DGS point clouds concentrate on instance surfaces with sparse coverage near centroids, making direct spatial clustering ambiguous at instance boundaries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252182/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images with known camera poses, SAM-generated hierarchical segmentation masks (filtered to exclude masks extending beyond the field of view), and optionally a text query or 2D pixel click for interactive selection. It produces per-Gaussian 3D spatial votes (offset vectors pointing to instance centroids), HDBSCAN-clustered instance IDs assigned to each Gaussian primitive, and per-instance CLIP features extracted by rendering instance ID maps and sampling corresponding pixel regions. The output supports downstream tasks including open-vocabulary 3D object rendering, click-based object removal, and hierarchical part-level segmentation.

## Method

VoteSplat embeds a learnable 3D offset vector Δp_i into each Gaussian primitive so that the 3D vote V_i^3d = p_i + Δp_i points toward the instance centroid. Training uses a 3D-2D Votes Association scheme: SAM mask centroids provide 2D ground-truth votes, and a custom voting transmittance (uniform averaging over depth-ordered points rather than standard α-weighting) prevents depth bias and excludes occluded background points from voting. A depth distortion regularization term L_d* = Σ|z_i − z_j| enforces tight depth alignment of votes around the centroid, compensating for depth information lost during 2D projection. After training, HDBSCAN clustering on the 3D votes assigns instance IDs, which are rendered as ID maps to extract per-instance CLIP features—enabling open-vocabulary queries without embedding high-dimensional features into the Gaussians themselves.

## Results

On the LERF dataset, VoteSplat achieves a mean mIoU of 50.10 and mean mAcc@0.25 of 67.38 for open-vocabulary 3D instance localization, outperforming OpenGaussian (44.90 mIoU, 58.99 mAcc) and LangSplat (9.66 mIoU, 12.41 mAcc). VoteSplat also achieves the shortest training time (~43–57 min over 60k iterations on an RTX 3090), compared to OpenGaussian (~55–117 min) and Gaussian Grouping (~90–150 min). Qualitatively, VoteSplat produces cleaner instance boundaries, better handles occluded objects (e.g., reconstructing a bear's lower body behind a table), and clusters fine-grained details such as a rope on a camera.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VoteSplat outperforms all three baselines on both mIoU and mAcc across every LERF scene. Against LangSplat, the gains are dramatic (+40.4 mIoU, +55.0 mAcc), reflecting LangSplat's reliance on low-dimensional CLIP features that introduce semantic ambiguity at the point level. Against OpenGaussian, VoteSplat improves mIoU by 5.2 points (50.10 vs. 44.90) and mAcc by 8.4 points (67.38 vs. 58.99), with the largest per-scene margin on ramen (+8.2 mIoU) and teatime mAcc (+8.5). In terms of training efficiency, VoteSplat is roughly 2× faster than OpenGaussian (which uses intra-/inter-class contrastive learning) and faster than Gaussian Grouping (which relies on KNN-based feature consistency), completing training in ~43–57 minutes versus ~55–117 and ~90–150 minutes respectively. Gaussian Grouping is excluded from the semantic-query comparison in Table 1 because it does not natively support text-based queries on Gaussian primitives.

## Relation to Prior Work

VoteSplat transplants the Hough voting mechanism from VoteNet (3D point cloud detection) into the 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering pipeline, where each Gaussian primitive learns a spatial offset vector pointing toward its instance centroid rather than a high-dimensional semantic feature. This departs from the dominant 3DGS-semantic paradigm (LangSplat, OpenGaussian, Gaussian Grouping), which embeds CLIP-derived or contrastively-learned feature vectors directly into Gaussians and relies on pixel-level α-blending or KNN consistency for instance separation. By replacing per-Gaussian semantic features with a lightweight 3D vote + HDBSCAN clustering + instance-ID-to-CLIP mapping, VoteSplat sidesteps both the dimensionality-reduction ambiguity of compressed CLIP features and the computational cost of intra-/inter-class contrastive learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a 2–3× reduction in training time (≈43–57 min vs. ≈114–150 min for competitors) while achieving the best mIoU and mAcc on the LERF and 3D-OVS benchmarks, making open-vocabulary 3D scene understanding more accessible on consumer GPUs. The instance-ID-based semantic association also cleanly supports downstream tasks like click-based object selection, scene editing, and hierarchical (part-level) segmentation without retraining. Open limitations include degraded performance when an instance's 2D projection exceeds the field of view (making 2D vote centroids unreliable) and difficulty separating instances enclosed in concave containers where spatial proximity defeats voting-based clustering; scaling to very large or unbounded scenes remains untested.
