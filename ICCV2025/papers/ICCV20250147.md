## Problem

Training robust MIL models for whole-slide image analysis requires data augmentation, but patch-level augmentation is infeasible for gigapixel WSIs (tens to hundreds of thousands of patches per slide), and existing feature-level augmentation methods (diffusion-based AugDiff, GANs) lack explicit control over which transformations are applied and incur prohibitive compute and memory costs. No efficient method exists that can simulate specific, parameterized image augmentations directly in the latent space of a frozen pathology foundation model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250147/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The generator takes a frozen foundation model's patch embedding (z ∈ R^d, e.g., 1024-dim from UNI or 512-dim from CONCH) and a sequence of K named transformations with continuous or discrete parameters (e.g., hue shift of 0.3, horizontal flip, erosion radius). The embedding is split into C chunked tokens, and the transformation parameters are encoded via per-transformation linear projections. The output is a single augmented embedding of the same dimensionality that approximates what the frozen encoder would produce on the actually-transformed image patch. The generator is trained at 10× magnification but generalizes to 20× without retraining, and can process up to 1M patches in a single forward pass in under 10 seconds on a V100 GPU.

## Method

HistAug is a lightweight conditional transformer that learns to simulate image-space augmentations in latent space. The patch embedding is chunked into C tokens (queries), while each transformation's parameter is projected into a token via a dedicated linear layer, with learnable positional embeddings encoding transformation order. L transformer blocks perform cross-attention from the chunked feature tokens to the transformation tokens, followed by residual connections, layer normalization, and feed-forward MLPs; an MLP head then concatenates and maps the updated chunks to the final augmented embedding. Training uses a two-term loss: a reconstruction term matching the frozen encoder's output on the truly transformed image, and an identity term ensuring the generator recovers the input embedding when all transformation parameters are set to their identity values. During MIL training, the frozen generator can augment entire WSI bags in a single forward pass, either instance-wise (independent transforms per patch) or WSI-wise (uniform transform across all patches to preserve global slide consistency).

## Results

At 10% training data, WSI-wise HistAug consistently outperforms all baselines across five TCGA cancer types and two foundation models (UNI, CONCH). For survival prediction (C-index), UCEC improves from 59.3→63.2 (UNI) and 58.6→64.9 (CONCH); for classification (AUC), NSCLC improves from 92.8→94.6 (CONCH). At 100% training, gains narrow but remain positive for survival tasks (e.g., BLCA C-index 54.5→59.5 with UNI instance-wise). In terms of efficiency, HistAug processes 1M patches in <10 s and is ~300× faster than AugDiff for realistic MIL bag sizes (50k–100k patches), while using far less GPU memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AugDiff [6], HistAug (WSI-wise) wins at 10% training on nearly all dataset–encoder combinations: e.g., KIRC C-index 69.6 vs 65.9 (CONCH), UCEC C-index 64.9 vs 61.9 (CONCH), and NSCLC AUC 94.6 vs 93.8 (CONCH). AugDiff is not evaluated at 100% training due to prohibitive cost (>60 GPU-hours per fold for BLCA alone), whereas HistAug is ~300× faster and uses far less GPU memory (handles 200k patches vs AugDiff's 1k before saturating a 32 GB V100). Against SSRDL [26] on TCGA-EGFR, HistAug with UNI achieves 87.9 AUC (TransMIL) and 89.4 AUC (CLAM), substantially outperforming SSRDL's 79.7 and 83.1, though the UNI baseline without augmentation already reaches 86.5, indicating that the foundation model itself provides most of the gain over SSRDL's dedicated encoder. HistAug also matches or slightly exceeds offline patch augmentation (PAug) at 10% training while offering on-the-fly diversity without additional storage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the existing family of feature-level augmentation methods for MIL in digital pathology, which includes MixUp-based interpolation, GAN-based generation, diffusion-based approaches (notably AugDiff), and online representation sampling (SSRDL). It departs from these by introducing a lightweight transformer with cross-attention that explicitly conditions on named transformation parameters (hue, erosion, HED, etc.), providing direct controllability absent in prior generative methods. The key conceptual departure is leveraging the known non-invariance of foundation model embeddings (UNI, CONCH) to image transforms as a training signal, rather than treating the latent space as a fixed target. Unlike SSRDL, HistAug requires no dedicated patch encoder and operates entirely on frozen foundation model features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for the digital pathology community: a 300× speedup over AugDiff and the ability to process 1M patches in under 10 seconds makes latent augmentation feasible at every MIL training step, removing the primary barrier to using augmentation in WSI pipelines. Cross-magnification generalization (trained at 10×, effective at 20×) and WSI-wise consistency further reduce the engineering burden. Open questions include extending the transformation vocabulary beyond the current set, whether controllability can be improved for more complex or compound augmentations, and whether the approach transfers to non-H&E staining protocols or 3D volumetric data. The modest but consistent gains (1–5 points) suggest the method is most impactful in low-data regimes, which is precisely where many clinical pathology datasets reside.
