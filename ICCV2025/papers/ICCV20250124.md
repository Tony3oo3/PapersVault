## Problem

Traditional proposal-based visual grounding methods depend on pre-trained detectors, making them slow and suboptimal, while recent direct-referring methods optimize solely for the referred target and lose general foreground perception. Additionally, existing generalized visual grounding approaches handle object existence discrimination only at a single granularity, failing to robustly determine whether a target is present in complex zero- or multi-target scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250124/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a natural language referring expression as input, processed jointly by a BEiT3 vision-language encoder. It produces four outputs simultaneously: foreground bounding box proposals with confidence scores (from a DETR-style decoder), a global pixel-level segmentation mask, a per-proposal referring score computed via contrastive similarity to the text, and a scalar target existence score for generalized scenarios. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end without any external detector, and the multi-scale features are split along the channel dimension so that detection and segmentation branches operate on dedicated feature subsets.

## Method

PropVG integrates a DETR-type multi-scale deformable decoder for foreground proposal generation directly within the visual grounding pipeline, eliminating the need for a pre-trained detector. A Contrastive-based Refer Scoring (CRS) module then evaluates each proposal's relevance to the expression by computing cosine similarities at both the sentence level (global pooled text features) and word level (per-token features), combined via a learnable sigmoid-gated weight. For generalized scenarios, a Multi-granularity Target Discrimination (MTD) module uses Score Prior Cross Attention to inject the referring scores and segmentation mask as attention priors into a learnable existence query, and further multiplies the max referring score, a TopK-average of the segmentation mask, and the query-derived existence probability. The framework is trained jointly with detection (L1+CE+GIoU), referring (BCE), segmentation (BCE+Dice), and existence (BCE) losses, with Hungarian matching assigning queries to ground-truth foreground objects.

## Results

On RefCOCO/+/g (REC), PropVG (BEiT3-ViT-B, pretrained) achieves 92.70/95.07/89.58 (val/testA/testB) on RefCOCO, 87.27/90.87/81.26 on RefCOCO+, and 88.15/88.30 on RefCOCOg, surpassing the prior SOTA OneRef by 0.5–1.2% while running at 76 ms (4× faster than MAttNet's 320 ms). On gRefCOCO (GRES), PropVG reaches 73.29 gIoU (val), outperforming HDC by +5.0%. On R-RefCOCO/+/g, PropVG improves rIoU by 9.5–11.2% over HDC, and on Ref-ZOM it achieves 98.11% accuracy (+4.8% over HDC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest same-backbone competitor OneRef (BEiT3-ViT-B, direct referring), PropVG gains +0.5% on RefCOCO, +1.2% on RefCOCO+, and +0.9% on RefCOCOg (REC), while also delivering strong RES results (+2.1% mIoU on RefCOCO val). Compared to the classic proposal-based MAttNet, PropVG improves average REC accuracy by +14% and runs 4× faster (76 ms vs. 320 ms), demonstrating that end-to-end proposal generation eliminates the detector bottleneck. On generalized tasks, PropVG outperforms HDC (the prior SOTA for GRES) by +5.0% gIoU on gRefCOCO val and by 9.5–11.2% rIoU on R-RefCOCO/+/g. On Ref-ZOM, PropVG achieves 98.11% accuracy, surpassing HDC (93.34%) by +4.8% and MLLM-based GSVA (94.59%) by +3.5%. For GREC detection, PropVG beats SimVG (same BEiT3 backbone) by +10.1% F1 on val, confirming the benefit of the proposal-driven architecture for multi-target scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

PropVG revives the proposal-based visual grounding paradigm that was largely abandoned in favor of direct referring methods (OneRef, SimVG, SeqTR) due to the inefficiency and suboptimal performance of traditional two-stage pipelines relying on pre-trained detectors like Faster R-CNN. The key architectural departure is integrating a DETR-type multi-scale deformable decoder for end-to-end proposal generation, eliminating the external detector dependency while preserving foreground perception that direct referring methods sacrifice. The contrastive-based refer scoring module draws on contrastive learning principles (sentence- and word-level similarity) to replace the simpler scoring mechanisms in prior proposal-based work, and the multi-granularity target discrimination module extends the generalized VG setting (GRES/GREC) by fusing object-level and pixel-level priors through a novel Score Prior Cross Attention mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a detector-free, end-to-end proposal-based framework can match or exceed direct referring SOTA while being 4× faster than legacy proposal methods, PropVG offers practitioners a viable alternative that retains explicit foreground perception—a capability direct referring methods inherently lack. The multi-granularity discrimination for generalized scenarios (zero or multiple targets) addresses a practical gap in robust deployment where referred objects may be absent. Open questions include whether the contrastive scoring generalizes to longer or more compositional expressions, whether the framework extends naturally to video grounding, and whether the modest gains over the strongest direct referring baselines (0.5–1.2% on REC) will widen with stronger backbones or larger-scale pretraining.
