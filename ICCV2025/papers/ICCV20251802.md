## Problem

Existing LiDAR-4D RADAR fusion methods for 3D object detection either neglect Doppler entirely, encode it as a sub-dimension alongside RADAR power, or rely on Range-Doppler map representations that are misaligned with the 3D coordinate systems of other sensors. This prevents Doppler's unique motion-related cues from being effectively leveraged in multi-modal feature fusion, limiting weather-robust detection performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251802/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three separate sparse point clouds: raw LiDAR (x, y, z, intensity), CFAR-filtered 4D RADAR power (x, y, z, log-scaled power, top 10% points retained), and CFAR-filtered 4D RADAR Doppler (x, y, z, ego-compensated Doppler via bin-mean subtraction with Δd=0.2). All inputs are voxelized at 0.4×0.4×0.4 m within a 72×12.8×8 m driving corridor. The network outputs a BEV classification map (focal loss) and a BEV regression map (smooth L1 loss) for 3D bounding box detection, evaluated as AP_BEV and AP_3D at IoU thresholds of 0.3 and 0.5.

## Method

DLRFusion encodes LiDAR, RADAR power, and Doppler as three independent sparse tensors through separate 3D sparse convolution encoders, then fuses them via Multi-path Iterative Interaction (MPII) blocks repeated over 3 stages. Each MPII block performs three structured interactions: (1) Doppler refines RADAR power via a kernelized attention combining a Gaussian motion-emphasis kernel and a feature-separation kernel, shifting power toward dynamic objects; (2) the Doppler-refined power updates LiDAR through KNN-based voxel attention over neighboring RADAR voxels; (3) Doppler independently updates LiDAR via the same voxel attention with separate weights. The refined features (Doppler-updated LiDAR, Doppler-refined power, and Doppler) feed into the next MPII block for progressive multi-scale refinement, while parallel BEV encoders at each stage concatenate into the final detection head. The key design choice—treating power and Doppler as separate tensors rather than a combined 5D feature—is empirically validated to outperform joint encoding by preserving each modality's distinct characteristics.

## Results

On the K-RADAR dataset, DLR-Fusion achieves a total AP_BEV/AP_3D of 73.2/45.7 at IoU=0.5, outperforming the strongest baseline LOD_PDR (71.3/40.4) by 1.9 points in AP_BEV and 5.3 points in AP_3D. At the stricter IoU=0.3 threshold, DLR-Fusion reaches 82.9/74.8, again leading LOD_PDR (82.1/73.2). The method shows consistent gains across all seven weather conditions, with particularly large improvements in rain (AP_3D: 46.1 vs. 31.6 for LOD_PDR) and heavy snow (AP_3D: 30.6 vs. 24.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DLR-Fusion outperforms all six baselines across every weather condition and both IoU thresholds. Against the closest multi-modal competitor LOD_PDR [5], DLR-Fusion gains +1.9 AP_BEV and +5.3 AP_3D at IoU=0.5 (73.2/45.7 vs. 71.3/40.4), with the largest gap in rain (+14.5 AP_3D) and heavy snow (+5.7 AP_3D). Compared to BEVFusion† [35], the improvement is even more pronounced in AP_3D (+16.7 at IoU=0.5), highlighting the benefit of Doppler-aware sparse interaction over BEV-space fusion. Against LiDAR-only baselines, DLR-Fusion surpasses VoxelNext [9] by +2.7 AP_BEV and +13.0 AP_3D, and RTNH† [44] by +7.4 AP_BEV and +8.5 AP_3D, confirming that RADAR fusion adds substantial value. The 4D RADAR-only RTNH [44] and pillar-based InterFusion [62] are far behind, with AP_3D gaps of over 30 and 23 points respectively, underscoring the necessity of LiDAR for precise 3D localization. PointPillars [28] is the weakest LiDAR baseline, trailing DLR-Fusion by 25.4 AP_BEV and 24.2 AP_3D.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own prior work on LiDAR-4D RADAR fusion (LOD_PDR [5] and the voxel-attention-based approach [6]) by explicitly incorporating Doppler as a first-class modality rather than ignoring it or folding it into RADAR power. It departs from prior fusion methods (InterFusion, BEVFusion, Bi-LRFusion) that treat RADAR as a single feature stream, instead encoding power and Doppler as separate sparse tensors and introducing a Multi-path Iterative Interaction (MPII) module with three distinct interaction paths (Doppler→Power, Power→LiDAR, Doppler→LiDAR). The kernelized attention mechanism for the Doppler-Power interaction, combining a motion-emphasis kernel with a feature-separation kernel, is a novel design not seen in prior Doppler-based perception work, which typically relies on RD/RAD map representations incompatible with multi-sensor 3D coordinate systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that properly structuring Doppler information—through separate encoding, simple binning-based ego-velocity compensation, and hierarchical multi-path interaction—yields substantial gains in 3D detection robustness across weather conditions, particularly in AP_3D where the 13% improvement over the next-best method is notable. However, the evaluation is limited to a single dataset (K-RADAR) and a single object class (Sedan), and the inability to estimate absolute velocity due to limited RADAR sensor metadata constrains the method's practical applicability. The authors identify multi-frame detection and tracking as natural extensions once absolute velocity becomes available, and the approach's generalizability to other 4D RADAR datasets and object categories remains untested.
