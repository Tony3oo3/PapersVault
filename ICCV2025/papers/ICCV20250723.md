## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models struggle with complex scenes, fine-grained details, and compositional accuracy, yet current inference-time scaling methods for diffusion models are limited to parallelized noise-space search. Unlike LLMs, which have demonstrated strong self-reflection capabilities at inference time, no existing diffusion model can interpret textual reflections and iteratively correct its own generated images, leaving the sequential self-refinement paradigm largely unexplored for visual generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250723/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a user text prompt and random noise as initial inputs, then in each refinement iteration additionally consumes the previously generated flawed image (downsampled from 1024×1024 to 512×512 for conditioning) and a textual reflection prompt describing specific shortcomings and improvement directions. The output is a refined 1024×1024 image, with the overall process parameterized by search width N (parallel noise chains) and reflection depth M (iterative refinement rounds), allowing flexible trade-offs between compute budget and quality.

## Method

ReflectionFlow formulates self-refinement as a generalized image editing problem and trains a corrector on FLUX.1-dev by concatenating the original prompt, reflection prompt, flawed image tokens, and target image tokens into a single unified sequence for joint multimodal attention within the MMDiT architecture, requiring no additional modules like ControlNet or IP-Adapter. Training uses LoRA (rank 32) with a flow-matching loss on the target image, a random-dropping strategy to prevent distributional drift, and a task warm-up schedule that initially emphasizes editing data before gradually incorporating other domains. The training data, GenRef, comprises 1 million triplets (flawed image, high-quality image, textual reflection) constructed from four sources—rule-based verification with Grounded SAM, ensemble reward-model scoring, long-short prompt pairs, and OmniEdit editing data—plus 227K chain-of-thought annotations from GPT-4o and Gemini 2.0. At inference time, an MLLM verifier evaluates and ranks candidate images, generates actionable textual reflections, and refines prompts, which jointly condition the corrector for the next refinement round across three complementary scaling axes: noise-level, reflection-level, and prompt-level.

## Results

On the GenEval benchmark (553 prompts, 1024×1024 resolution, 30 sampling steps), ReflectionFlow with 32 samples achieves an overall score of 0.91, substantially outperforming the FLUX.1-dev baseline (0.67 with 1 sample) and noise-level scaling alone (0.85 with 32 samples). The framework also surpasses the strongest standalone T2I model, Janus-Pro-7B (0.80), by 0.11 points, and the concurrent reflection-based method Reflect-DiT (0.81 with 20 samples) by 0.10 points. The oracle upper bound for the GenEval benchmark is 0.98, indicating remaining headroom with better verifiers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReflectionFlow (0.91 overall, 32 samples) outperforms all standalone T2I baselines: SDXL (0.55), DALLE 3 (0.67), SANA-1.5 4.8B (0.72), Lumina-Image 2.0 (0.73), SD3 (0.74), Playground v3 (0.76), and Janus-Pro-7B (0.80), with the largest margin over SDXL (+0.36) and the smallest over Janus-Pro-7B (+0.11). Against inference-time scaling baselines on the same FLUX.1-dev backbone, ReflectionFlow beats noise-level scaling alone (0.85) by +0.06 and noise+prompt scaling (0.87) by +0.04, confirming that explicit reflection provides substantial gains beyond parallel noise search. Compared to the concurrent Reflect-DiT method (0.81 with 20 samples), ReflectionFlow achieves +0.10 overall with 32 samples, and the paper notes that even at 16 samples ReflectionFlow already surpasses Reflect-DiT's 20-sample result. On individual GenEval sub-metrics, ReflectionFlow leads on Position (0.93 vs. 0.80 for noise+prompt scaling) and Colors (0.96 vs. 0.91), while Counting (0.90) is slightly below noise-only scaling (0.91), suggesting reflection is most effective for spatial and attribute reasoning rather than pure counting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the inference-time scaling paradigm for diffusion models, which prior work (SANA-1.5, Ma et al.) restricted to parallel noise-space search, by introducing a sequential self-refinement axis drawn from LLM self-correction literature (Self-Refine, Reflexion). It reframes the self-correction step as a generalized image editing problem, building on the MMDiT unified-attention architecture and efficient fine-tuning strategies from OminiControl and OmniEdit, thereby avoiding additional modules like ControlNet or IP-Adapter. The concurrent Reflect-DiT work explores in-context reflection for DiTs but without a dedicated large-scale training dataset or the multi-axis scaling framework proposed here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GenRef dataset (1M triplets + 227K CoT annotations) fills a critical gap in reflection-guided T2I training data and can serve broader applications like preference tuning and reward modeling. The oracle gap (0.98 vs. 0.91 achieved) and the verifier-dependent scaling curve suggest substantial untapped headroom, making the quality of the verifier a key open question. Limitations include evaluation on a single benchmark (GenEval) and a single base model (FLUX.1-dev), and the reliance on external MLLM verifiers adds non-trivial inference overhead. Whether the reflection paradigm transfers to video generation, 3D synthesis, or other generative modalities remains unexplored.
