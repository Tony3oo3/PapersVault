## Problem

Existing camouflaged object detection methods produce fragmented predictions because they process edge and texture features in isolation, rely on fixed geometric priors in standard convolutions that cannot adapt to irregular boundaries, and lack hierarchical mechanisms to enforce multi-scale edge-texture consistency. The paper addresses this by establishing a self-reinforcing loop where edge perception and texture discrimination mutually enhance each other rather than being treated as independent signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250068/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes a single RGB image resized to 416×416 pixels and produces two outputs: a multi-scale camouflaged object segmentation mask (five levels from coarse to fine) and a binary edge prediction map. The encoder is PVT-v2-b4 with ASPP aggregation, and all predictions are bilinearly upsampled to original resolution before loss computation. Training uses 1,000 CAMO and 3,040 COD10K images with a hybrid loss combining edge Dice loss and multi-scale weighted BCE+IoU losses with progressive scale emphasis (γ = [1.3, 1.1, 0.9, 0.7, 0.5]).

## Method

ESCNet couples three modules in a hierarchical feedback loop. The Adaptive Edge-Texture Perceptor (AETP) generates edge predictions via texture-conditioned deformable convolutions and cross-scale multi-head self-attention, so boundary localization and texture discrimination mutually reinforce. The Dual-Stream Feature Augmentor (DSFA) uses PatchRef to crop spatially-aligned high-resolution patches from the original image, then applies edge-conditioned deformable convolutions whose offsets are guided by the AETP edge map, followed by a cascaded residual block with expanding kernels (3×3, 5×5, 7×7) to progressively enrich features at fractal boundaries. The Multi-Feature Modulation Module (MFMM) performs hierarchical prediction refinement through Structure-Constrained Self-Attention conditioned on three complementary priors—upper-level mask, edge map, and Laplacian—aggregated via CBAM, with each MFMM level receiving input from the corresponding DSFA output plus the previous MFMM output to form a coarse-to-fine refinement chain.

## Results

ESCNet achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three COD benchmarks. On NC4K, it reaches F_β^w = 0.859, E_φ = 0.941, S_m = 0.892, and MAE = 0.028; on COD10K, F_β^w = 0.804, E_φ = 0.939, S_m = 0.873, MAE = 0.021; and on CAMO, F_β^w = 0.843, E_φ = 0.934, S_m = 0.871, MAE = 0.044. The method outperforms the strongest Transformer-based competitor CamoFormer by 1.2% in F_β^w and 0.3% in E_φ on NC4K, and by 1.2% in F_β^w on CAMO. On the secondary SOD task (DUTS-TE), ESCNet achieves F_β^m = 0.912 and E_φ = 0.950, competitive with but slightly below the best SOD-specific methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESCNet outperforms the strongest Transformer-based baseline CamoFormer [53] on all four metrics for NC4K (F_β^w: 0.859 vs 0.847; E_φ: 0.941 vs 0.938; MAE: 0.028 vs 0.030) and on three of four for CAMO (F_β^w: 0.843 vs 0.831; E_φ: 0.934 vs 0.929; MAE: 0.044 vs 0.046), though CamoFormer edges ahead on CAMO S_m (0.872 vs 0.871). Against HitNet [22], the best COD10K F_β^w scorer (0.806), ESCNet trails by a narrow 0.002 but leads on E_φ (0.939 vs 0.935), S_m (0.873 vs 0.871), and MAE (0.021 vs 0.023). Compared to CNN-based methods, ESCNet's advantage is more pronounced: over ZoomNet [39] it gains 7.5% in F_β^w on NC4K and 5.5% on COD10K, while over FEDER [19] it improves F_β^w by 6.5% on NC4K and 8.8% on COD10K. On the secondary SOD task, ESCNet (F_β^m = 0.912 on DUTS-TE) is competitive but trails the best SOD-specific method SENet [18] (0.925) by 1.3%, suggesting the edge-texture coupling design is most beneficial for the inherently harder COD setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

ESCNet positions itself within the Transformer-based COD lineage (FSPNet, CamoFormer, UGTR) by adopting PVT-v2 as its encoder, but departs from prior edge-aware COD methods (EGNet, FEDER, BGNet) that treat edge detection as a simple auxiliary branch or concatenate edge features with segmentation features. Its conceptual departure is the 'dynamically coupled edge-texture perception loop,' where edge and texture representations mutually reinforce each other through three coordinated modules (AETP, DSFA, MFMM) rather than operating in isolation. The deformable convolution component draws on boundary-aware segmentation literature, while the Structure-Constrained Attention in MFMM extends masked-attention ideas from Mask2Former to incorporate edge and Laplacian priors as conditioning signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The edge-texture coupling addresses a core ambiguity in COD that has resisted resolution through single-modality approaches, and the demonstrated generalization to salient object detection (competitive but not SOTA on DUTS-TE/ECSSD) suggests the framework's principles may transfer to other ambiguous-boundary segmentation tasks. However, the SOD results trail the best methods by a noticeable margin (0.912 vs. 0.925 F_β on DUTS-TE), and the paper does not report computational cost or inference latency, leaving open whether the multi-module pipeline is practical for real-time applications. A key open question is whether the self-reinforcing loop generalizes beyond the three standard COD benchmarks to more diverse or video-based camouflage scenarios.
