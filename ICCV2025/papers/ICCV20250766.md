## Problem

Existing MLLMs deployed on smartphones suffer significant pure-language performance degradation (often >10% on subjective tasks) compared to their base LLMs, while the MoE architectures commonly used to preserve language capabilities during multimodal training are unsupported by mainstream smartphone NPU platforms (MediaTek Dimensity 9400, Qualcomm Snapdragon 8 Elite). The paper addresses both gaps simultaneously: maintaining full language capability without MoE, within the memory and storage constraints of mobile devices.

## Input / Output

The model accepts either a pure text prompt or an RGB image paired with a text prompt, and generates a text response in both cases. For multimodal inference, a single-patch ViT (SigLIP) encodes the image, and the LLM operates with W4A16 quantization while the ViT, projector, and LoRA modules use W8A16 quantization on the Qualcomm Snapdragon 8 Elite NPU. The non-shared base deployment means the same stored model serves both modes: pure-text queries route through the original frozen LLM weights, while multimodal queries swap in the trained duplicated blocks and LoRA adapters at inference time.

## Method

GenieBlue freezes all original LLM parameters during multimodal training and acquires vision-language capabilities by (1) duplicating transformer blocks at every quarter interval of the LLM's layers for full fine-tuning, and (2) inserting LoRA modules (rank 8) into the attention and feed-forward layers of all remaining blocks. The key architectural insight is the non-shared base deployment strategy: for pure-text inference the original frozen LLM is used unchanged, while for multimodal inference the trained duplicated blocks replace the originals and LoRA weights are applied to the rest, effectively decoupling the two inference paths without any MoE routing. This design is motivated by systematic ablations showing that adding pure-text data to multimodal training yields only marginal gains on subjective NLP tasks, and that CogVLM-style visual experts at every-quarter intervals outperform first-quarter or last-quarter placements. Trained with SigLIP as ViT and BlueLM-3B as the LLM using 2.5M pre-training and 645M fine-tuning samples, GenieBlue-Skip retains 100% of the base LLM's language scores while achieving ~97% of full fine-tuning MLLM performance with only ~1B trainable parameters.

## Results

On MLLM benchmarks (OpenCompass suite), GenieBlue (3.2B + 0.55B additional) achieves an average score of 64.2, retaining 97% of BlueLM-V-3B's performance (66.1) while outperforming the larger InternVL2-8B (64.1) on average. On pure language benchmarks, GenieBlue retains 100% of BlueLM-3B's performance across all seven LLM benchmarks (DROP, GPQA, GSM8K, MATH, MMLU, AlignBench, MT-Bench), in contrast to Qwen2.5-VL-3B which drops to 92.98% retention. On the Qualcomm Snapdragon 8 Elite NPU, GenieBlue achieves 31 token/s output speed with 1.92 GB storage and 2.10 GB memory usage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenieBlue (64.2 AVG) slightly trails BlueLM-V-3B (66.1) and Ovis2-2B (65.2) on MLLM benchmarks, but outperforms Qwen2.5-VL-3B (64.5) by a narrow margin and clearly beats SAIL-VL-2B (61.0) and InternVL2.5-2B-MPO (60.9). Notably, GenieBlue edges out the 2.5× larger InternVL2-8B (64.1) on average while using fewer parameters. The critical differentiator is LLM retention: GenieBlue preserves 100% of BlueLM-3B's language performance (60.16 AVG across DROP, GPQA, GSM8K, MATH, MMLU, AlignBench, MT-Bench), whereas Qwen2.5-VL-3B drops to 56.05 (92.98% retention) and InternVL2.5-4B drops by 10–15% on subjective benchmarks. Among the structural ablations, GenieBlue-Skip (98.99% MLLM retention vs. full fine-tuning) outperforms CogVLM-Skip (96.60%) and full-LoRA (91.82%), confirming that duplicated blocks at quarter intervals with LoRA on remaining blocks is the optimal configuration.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenieBlue directly extends CogVLM's visual-expert architecture (duplicating transformer blocks alongside frozen LLM layers) and LoRA-based parameter-efficient adaptation, but restructures them to eliminate the MoE routing mechanism that makes CogVLM and Wings incompatible with current smartphone NPU platforms. The non-shared base deployment strategy—using the original frozen LLM for text inference and swapping in trained blocks plus LoRA weights for multimodal inference—is a practical departure from prior MoE-based designs that require loading all experts simultaneously. The empirical analysis of pure-text data augmentation builds on and extends observations from InternVL2.5 and Qwen2.5-VL, providing a more systematic quantification of why data-side solutions remain insufficient for subjective NLP tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper addresses a concrete deployment bottleneck: mainstream smartphone NPU platforms (MediaTek Dimensity 9400, Qualcomm Snapdragon 8 Elite) do not support MoE architectures, which are the standard solution for preserving language capabilities in MLLMs. The finding that adding 2M pure-text samples partially recovers objective NLP performance but leaves subjective tasks (AlignBench, MT-Bench) essentially unchanged is a useful negative result that redirects attention toward architectural solutions. Limitations include evaluation on a single device, single-patch ViT inference, and a modest 0.55B parameter overhead; open questions include whether the approach generalizes to larger models, multi-patch inference, and whether the ~1-2% MLLM performance gap versus full fine-tuning can be closed with more training data or architectural refinements.
