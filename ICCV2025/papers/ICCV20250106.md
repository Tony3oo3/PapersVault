## Problem

Existing text-to-motion retrieval models operate in an empty space, ignoring the scene in which the motion occurs, while Human-Scene Interaction evaluation methods rely on fragmented geometric metrics (interpenetration, distance) rather than a global coherence measure. No prior work provides a unified framework to assess the alignment among skeletal motion, textual intention, and surrounding scene context simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250106/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes three modalities: a 768-dim DistilBERT text vector, a colored point cloud scene (N×6: xyz coordinates + RGB), and a 3D skeleton motion sequence (T×3×22: frames × xyz × 22 joints). The unified latent space supports flexible retrieval in all directions—single-to-single (e.g., t2m), double-to-single (e.g., st2m), and single-to-double (e.g., m2st)—by computing cosine similarity between the appropriate unimodal or cross-modal embeddings. Downstream, the same embeddings yield FID/Recall scores for HSI evaluation, zero-shot object placement coordinates, and motion captions via a frozen GPT-2 decoder.

## Method

MonSTeR is inspired by topological decomposition of a triangle into its edges and vertices: each unimodal term (t, m, s) is encoded by a transformer-based variational autoencoder, while three cross-modal encoders (ST, MT, MS) process pairwise concatenations of the residual tokens from the unimodal encoders to produce joint latent vectors. Training uses InfoNCE contrastive loss over six alignment pairs—(t,s), (m,t), (m,s) for unimodal-to-unimodal, and (st,m), (mt,s), (ms,t) for unimodal-to-cross-modal—deliberately excluding terms like (st,t) to prevent the cross-modal encoders from collapsing to identity functions. This higher-order alignment, rather than simple all-to-all unimodal matching, is what enables the model to capture the many-to-many interdependence among modalities where a single text may correspond to different motions depending on scene context.

## Results

On HUMANISE+ (All protocol), MonSTeR achieves a mean mRecall of 4.80 across 12 retrieval tasks, outperforming TMR+S (2.72) and MoPa+S (1.88). The headline st2m (scene+text → motion) score is 13.91, a 209% relative improvement over the best baseline (TMR+S at 4.10). Under the easier Small Batches protocol (32 candidates), MonSTeR reaches 60.00 average mRecall vs. 47.44 (TMR+S) and 38.32 (MoPa+S). On TRUMANS+, MonSTeR leads with 7.42 average mRecall (All) and 47.05 (Small Batches), though TMR retains an edge on the t2m/m2t sub-tasks due to the dataset's weaker scene-text coupling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TMR [27] (extended with a scene encoder as TMR+S), MonSTeR dominates on HUMANISE+ across nearly all trimodal tasks: st2m improves from 4.10 to 13.91 (+239%), m2st from 3.30 to 13.14 (+298%), and the overall average mRecall from 2.72 to 4.80 (+76%). On TRUMANS+, TMR+S retains a narrow edge on t2m (5.65 vs. 5.67) and m2t (12.03 vs. 5.90), which the authors attribute to TRUMANS+'s weaker scene-text coupling and TMR's reliance on Guo features that ignore spatial grounding. Against MoPa [39] (MoPa+S), MonSTeR wins on every trimodal task on both datasets, with the largest gaps on st2m (13.91 vs. 2.10 on HUMANISE+) and m2st (13.14 vs. 2.45). For the downstream Motion Captioning task, MonSTeR+GPT2 surpasses MotionGPT [16] on HUMANISE+ in BLEU-4 (23.59 vs. 17.47), ROUGE-L (50.85 vs. 40.23), CIDER (13.70 vs. 11.13), and BERT F1 (35.57 vs. 22.16), though MotionGPT edges ahead on BLEU-1 (42.16 vs. 42.93 is actually MonSTeR winning). On TRUMANS+, MonSTeR+GPT2 leads in BLEU-1, BLEU-4, ROUGE-L, and BERT F1 but trails MotionGPT on CIDER (12.24 vs. 15.08).

## Relation to Prior Work

MonSTeR extends the text-to-motion retrieval line (TMR, MoPa) by introducing scene as a third modality and, crucially, by explicitly modeling higher-order tri-modal interactions rather than relying solely on unimodal alignment. The architectural design is motivated by topological deep learning: the tri-modal relation is decomposed into vertices (unimodal encoders) and edges (cross-modal encoders), and both levels are aligned via contrastive learning. This departs from prior multimodal alignment strategies that either average unimodal embeddings to form cross-modal representations (ImageBind-style) or use a single shared encoder for all modalities, neither of which supports flexible retrieval from partial modality combinations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MonSTeR provides the first unified latent space where text, motion, and scene can be jointly retrieved, and it doubles as a practical evaluation tool for text-conditioned Human-Scene Interaction generative models—filling a gap where existing metrics (FID, interpenetration distance) fail to capture global coherence. The ablation results confirm that both unimodal and cross-modal terms are necessary, validating the topological decomposition. Key limitations include static scenes only, cross-modal encoders trained exclusively on aligned pairs, and the observation that scene cues add limited value on datasets with high motion diversity (TRUMANS+), suggesting the approach's benefit is most pronounced when scene context is genuinely discriminative. Open questions include extending to dynamic scenes and whether the higher-order formulation generalizes beyond three modalities.
