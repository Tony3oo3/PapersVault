## Problem

Standard 3D Gaussian Splatting requires global depth-sorting of Gaussians before rasterization, which is computationally expensive, introduces view-dependent popping artifacts, and provides no mechanism to trade off rendering cost against visual fidelity. While stochastic transparency is a well-established technique in real-time graphics for order-independent compositing, it had not been adapted to 3DGS for both rendering and differentiable training, and existing pop-free or fast 3DGS variants typically sacrifice portability or quality.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained 3DGS scene (anisotropic Gaussians with position, covariance, opacity, and spherical-harmonic color), a camera pose, and a user-specified number of Monte Carlo samples per pixel (SPP). It outputs a novel-view RGB image whose quality scales predictably with SPP, and during training it additionally produces photometric loss gradients with respect to all Gaussian parameters. The SPP knob enables a direct, predictable latency–quality trade-off: 1 SPP renders 2–4× faster than sorted 3DGS, while 256–1024 SPP approaches alpha-blending PSNR. The implementation is provided in both portable OpenGL and CUDA.

## Method

The core contribution replaces sorted alpha blending with an unbiased Monte Carlo estimator of the volume rendering equation via stochastic transparency: each pixel probabilistically selects a single opaque Gaussian sample (weighted by opacity and depth) using standard Z-buffering, eliminating the need for any global or per-pixel sort. For differentiable training, the authors derive a detached gradient estimator following path replay backpropagation, where the loss gradient and the rendering use decorrelated random seeds to avoid biased products of noisy terms; gradients flow only to the sampled Gaussian's color and to the opacities of Gaussians in front of it. To remove popping without expensive per-fragment depth updates, billboards are re-oriented to linearly approximate each Gaussian's maximum-density surface, enabling hardware-interpolated depth. For full volumetric intermixing, the fixed per-Gaussian depth is replaced by a sampled free-flight distance (Beer–Lambert law), with decomposition tracking resolving overlaps of arbitrary Gaussians without sorting or ray tracing.

## Results

On MipNerf360, StochasticSplats at 256 SPP achieves PSNR 28.17 / SSIM 0.840 / LPIPS 0.187, approaching the alpha-blended 3DGS baseline (PSNR 28.99 / SSIM 0.869 / LPIPS 0.185). At just 1 SPP, the OpenGL implementation renders in 1.85 ms on an RTX 4090 versus 5.60 ms for 3DGS-CUDA (3× faster) and 20.70 ms for 3DGS-OpenGL (11× faster). On the LERF open-vocabulary localization task, 1-SPP rendering retains 82.0% mean accuracy versus 84.3% for alpha blending, while delivering 4× faster rendering speed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3DGS-CUDA (the original sorted alpha-blending implementation), StochasticSplats at 1 SPP is 2–4× faster across all tested GPUs (e.g., 1.85 ms vs. 5.60 ms on RTX 4090) while sacrificing PSNR (17.94 vs. 28.99); at 256 SPP the quality gap narrows to 0.82 dB PSNR at the cost of 8.00 ms render time. Against StopThePop, StochasticSplats matches or slightly exceeds PSNR at 256+ SPP (28.17 vs. 28.79) and is significantly faster in the OpenGL setting, whereas StopThePop cannot be easily ported to standard graphics frameworks due to its per-pixel sorting. Against Splatapult (3DGS-OpenGL), StochasticSplats at 1 SPP is roughly 11× faster (1.85 ms vs. 20.70 ms on RTX 4090) and eliminates popping artifacts that the OpenGL alpha-blending pipeline still exhibits. On the LERF localization task, 1-SPP StochasticSplats matches alpha blending on three of four scenes (Teatime, Ramen, Figurines) but drops 9.1 points on Waldo-Kitchen, yielding a 2.3-point mean accuracy reduction while rendering 4× faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants stochastic transparency—a Monte Carlo rendering technique well-established in real-time graphics [8]—into the 3D Gaussian Splatting pipeline, replacing the mandatory depth-sorted alpha blending with an unbiased, sorting-free estimator. It draws on path replay backpropagation [46] to derive a differentiable gradient estimator for training, adapts StopThePop's [37] per-fragment depth idea into a cheaper linear surface approximation, and extends the stochastic framework to full volumetric intermixing via free-flight sampling [5, 33]. The key conceptual departure from prior 3DGS work is that the rendering cost becomes a tunable parameter (samples per pixel) rather than a fixed function of scene complexity, and the implementation targets portable OpenGL rather than vendor-specific CUDA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the global sort, the method removes a major source of popping artifacts and a significant computational bottleneck, while simultaneously introducing a quality–speed dial (SPP) that 3DGS previously lacked—a property that is especially valuable for latency-critical applications like robotics and VR. The counterintuitive finding that lowering render resolution does not speed up sorted 3DGS (because more Gaussians per pixel increase per-pixel cost) underscores a structural limitation of the sorted paradigm that stochastic rendering sidesteps. Open questions include variance reduction (stratified sampling, control variates, neural denoising) for low-SPP rendering, and whether the gradient estimator's bias under non-L2 losses limits training quality on more complex scenes.
