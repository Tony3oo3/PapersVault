## Problem

Video LLMs require large numbers of spatio-temporal tokens, causing quadratic attention scaling and high pre-filling latency. Existing training-free token reduction methods are query-aware (requiring recomputation per question, blocking KV cache reuse) and treat tokens as a 1D sequence, ignoring the local spatial and temporal redundancy inherent in video data.

## Input / Output

STTM operates as a plug-and-play module at an early LLM layer (e.g., before layer 3 for 7B models), consuming the video token tensor Z_V ∈ R^{T×H×W×C} from the previous layer's output and producing a reduced set of spatio-temporally merged tokens Z_ST ∈ R^{N_ST×C} with N_ST ≪ T×H×W. Because the merging is entirely query-agnostic, the resulting KV cache can be reused across different questions for the same video. Video frames are sampled at 1 FPS (up to 128 frames for most benchmarks, 180 for VNBench), and the method is applied to LLaVA-Video-7B/72B, LLaVA-OneVision-7B, and Qwen2VL-7B.

## Method

STTM performs decomposed spatio-temporal merging in two sequential stages. First, spatial merging builds a quadtree per frame and performs a coarse-to-fine search: a parent node is retained if all four child nodes exceed a cosine-similarity threshold τ_S, otherwise the region is subdivided, yielding multi-granular spatial tokens that preserve fine detail where needed. Second, temporal merging compares spatially overlapping tokens across consecutive frames; pairs exceeding threshold τ_T are linked into directed graphs (edges point toward earlier frames), and a vectorized union-find algorithm identifies connected components to merge each tracklet into its earliest occurrence. The decomposed design (spatial-then-temporal) is preferred over a joint octree-based approach, which fails on dynamic scenes with rapid spatial changes. After merging, tokens are reordered via Z-shaped spatial scan with temporal priority, and positional embeddings are handled by retaining the surviving tokens' original RoPE values.

## Results

Using LLaVA-Video-7B, STTM achieves 99.5% relative accuracy with 45.5% token retention under a 50% budget (only 0.5% accuracy drop), and 97.8% relative accuracy with 27.6% token retention under a 30% budget (2.2% drop). On the challenging VNBench (needle-in-a-haystack) at 30% budget, STTM retains 98.0% accuracy while query-agnostic baselines like ToMe drop to 82.7% and DyCoke-stage1 to 82.3%. STTM also generalizes to LLaVA-OneVision-7B and Qwen2VL-7B, where it actually *improves* accuracy by 1.1% and 0.5% respectively while using fewer tokens, and scales to a 72B model with a 1.3% accuracy gain at 44.2% token retention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STTM outperforms all four baselines on average accuracy at both 50% and 30% token budgets. At 50% budget, STTM achieves 99.5% relative accuracy vs. FrameFusion (98.6%), ToMe (98.1%), DyCoke-stage1 (98.0%), DyCoke (97.5%), and FastV (96.8%), while also using the fewest tokens (45.5% vs. 48–50% for others). At 30% budget, the gap widens: STTM reaches 97.8% vs. FrameFusion (96.2%), DyCoke-stage1 (94.6%), ToMe (93.6%), DyCoke (92.8%), and FastV (92.5%). The advantage is most pronounced on VNBench (fine-grained needle-in-a-haystack), where at 30% budget STTM retains 98.0% accuracy while ToMe and DyCoke-stage1 drop to ~82–83% and FastV to 79.3%. Among query-agnostic methods (ToMe, DyCoke-stage1), STTM consistently leads by 1.4–4.2% in average accuracy, confirming that exploiting spatio-temporal structure via quadtree-based multi-granular merging is more effective than uniform or single-granularity token reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of token merging (ToMe), temporal redundancy exploitation (FrameFusion), and video compression principles (quadtree partitioning, H.264 skip mode), but departs from all prior training-free token reduction methods in two key ways: it is query-agnostic (enabling KV cache reuse) and it explicitly exploits 2D spatial structure via a multi-granular quadtree rather than treating tokens as a 1D sequence. Unlike FrameFusion, which compares single-granularity tokens across frames, STTM handles varying spatial granularities across frames through directed pairwise merging restricted to spatially overlapping regions, and unlike joint spatio-temporal approaches (e.g., octree-based partitioning), it decomposes the problem into sequential spatial-then-temporal merging.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The query-agnostic property is practically significant for deployment: it enables KV cache reuse across multiple questions on the same video, a scenario that existing query-aware methods (FastV, FrameFusion) cannot support. The finding that a simple top-left approximation for merge direction matches optimal matching in accuracy while enabling vectorized union-find is a useful engineering insight. The main limitation is the reliance on manually tuned thresholds (τ_S, τ_T) to hit a target token budget, and the authors identify adaptive threshold selection as a natural next step. Broader open questions include whether the decomposed strategy generalizes to 3D video understanding tasks beyond QA, and whether the multi-granular representation interacts favorably with emerging long-context architectures like Ring Attention at million-token scales.
