## Problem

Autoregressive policies for robotic manipulation, which predict actions via next-token or next-chunk generation, struggle to capture long-term bidirectional dependencies within action sequences, resulting in suboptimal performance compared to holistic generative policies like Diffusion Policy. The paper asks whether the full potential of autoregressive modeling has been realized for action generation, and proposes that a bidirectional, coarse-to-fine expansion strategy—inspired by how humans plan keyframes before refining execution—can close this gap.

## Input / Output

The model takes visual observations (2D RGB images encoded by ResNet18 with GroupNorm, or 3D point clouds encoded by Minkowski sparse convolutional networks) and optional proprioceptive end-effector pose (encoded via MLP and randomly masked during training to prevent position memorization). It outputs a dense sequence of T continuous TCP pose actions in raw action space—no discrete tokenization or codebook—constructed hierarchically from a single initial frame through log₂(T) bidirectional expansion levels, with the final level projected to the action space via a linear layer.

## Method

Dense Policy decomposes the joint action distribution P(A|O) into a product of conditional distributions over progressively finer-grained action sequences A¹, A², …, A^{log₂T}. Starting from a zero vector A⁰, each level linearly upsamples the previous level's keyframes (doubling sequence length via midpoint interpolation) and then refines them through 4 layers of BERT-style cross-attention with observation features, yielding the next level's actions. This 'Dense Process' is repeated log₂(T) times, giving logarithmic inference complexity versus linear for next-token/chunk methods. The entire action head is an encoder-only architecture (no decoder), trained end-to-end with L2 loss against ground-truth actions, and is modular—swappable onto any visual backbone without modification.

## Results

Across 11 simulation tasks (Adroit, DexArt, MetaWorld), 3D Dense Policy achieves a 72±4% average success rate, outperforming 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) at 53±7% by 19 percentage points and 2D Diffusion Policy (DP) at 25±5% by 27 percentage points. In 2D settings, Dense Policy reaches 52±5% average versus DP's 25±5%. In real-world experiments (4 tasks), 3D Dense Policy achieves 85% on Put Bread, 45% on Open Drawer, 7.30/10 balls poured on Pour Balls, and 70% success on Flower Arrangement, consistently surpassing RISE, Diffusion Policy, and ACT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3), 3D Dense Policy wins on 9 of 11 simulation tasks, with the largest margins on Shelf Place (77% vs 17%), Bin Picking (47% vs 34%), and Pen (61% vs 43%). Against 2D Diffusion Policy (DP), Dense Policy dominates across all 11 tasks, with particularly large gaps on Pen (61% vs 13%), Hammer (100% vs 15%), and Shelf Place (77% vs 11%). In real-world 3D settings, 3D Dense Policy outperforms RISE on Put Bread (85% vs 75%), Pour Balls (7.30 vs 6.85 balls), and Flower Arrangement (70% vs 50% success, 1.0 vs 0.6 flowers per trial), though RISE edges ahead on Pour Balls' at-least-one-ball metric (95% vs 85%). In 2D real-world settings, 2D Dense Policy beats both Diffusion Policy and ACT on all three evaluated tasks (Put Bread: 55% vs 40%/35%; Open Drawer: 20% vs 20%/10%; Pour Balls: 3.30 vs 2.35/2.75 balls). The paper also shows Dense Policy achieves comparable inference speed to ACT with fewer than half the parameters, and is ~10× faster than DP despite having 9.19M more parameters in the action head.

## Relation to Prior Work

Dense Policy carves new ground at the intersection of two established lines: holistic generative policies (Diffusion Policy, ACT, 3D Diffusion Policy, RISE) that model the joint distribution of action sequences, and autoregressive policies (ICRT, ARP, CARP) that predict actions token-by-token or chunk-by-chunk. Conceptually, it repurposes BERT's bidirectional cross-attention and VAR's multi-scale coarse-to-fine prediction for the action modality, replacing unidirectional next-token prediction with a bidirectional expansion process (linear upsampling followed by encoder refinement). The key architectural departure is the use of an encoder-only (rather than decoder-only or diffusion-based) action head that iteratively doubles sequence length, achieving logarithmic inference complexity versus the linear complexity of next-token/chunk approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the prevailing assumption in manipulation that holistic generative models (diffusion, VAE) are inherently superior to autoregressive approaches for continuous action prediction, showing that a well-designed bidirectional autoregressive policy can match or exceed them with fewer parameters and faster inference. Open questions remain: whether Dense Policy scales to vision-language-action models and foundation-model-scale architectures, whether its advantages persist under high-frequency action variations or contact-rich dynamics, and whether 2D variants can ever match 3D spatial reasoning for complex multi-object tasks. The authors explicitly flag the unexplored potential of Dense Policy as a VLA backbone as the most important next step.
