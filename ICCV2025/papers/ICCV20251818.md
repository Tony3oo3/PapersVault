## Problem

Vision-Language Models like CLIP are vulnerable to adversarial perturbations in zero-shot inference, and existing adversarial fine-tuning methods (e.g., TeCoA, PMG-FT, FARE) perform sample-wise feature alignment between each adversarial sample and a static text prompt. This neglects the second-order statistics of groups of related samples (e.g., augmented variants of an image), causing augmented test samples to degrade faster under attack than clean ones and limiting generalizable robustness to unseen adversaries.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image together with n=20 DADA-generated augmentations, and a text class prompt expanded into q=20 synonymous and 20 antonymous phrases (ordered by ChatGPT-4o). During fine-tuning, a single Universal Adversarial Set Perturbation (UASP) is shared across all augmentations in the image set and all prompts in the text set, rather than generating per-instance perturbations. At inference, the fine-tuned CLIP image encoder produces class labels for zero-shot classification; when extended to BLIP, it also yields image captions and retrieval rankings.

## Method

The core contribution replaces sample-wise adversarial fine-tuning with subspace-level alignment: embeddings of an image and its augmentations span a subspace (via a covariance matrix), which is aligned with a subspace built from the class text prompt and its synonymous prompts, while being pushed away from antonymous-prompt subspaces and other-class subspaces. Grassmann feature maps are approximated using MaxExp (fast spectral expectation of max-pooling) instead of SVD, yielding lower computational cost and numerical stability for non-simple singular values. Joint image-text adversaries are generated iteratively via PGD with a shared UASP, and intermediate adversarial subspaces (from steps 1 through m-1) are aligned toward the clean subspace through a weighted regularization term Ω whose weights reflect the magnitude of classification perturbation at each step. Theorem 2 formalizes that this weighting enforces localized κ-Lipschitz smoothness of the decision boundary, providing a theoretical guarantee for improved generalizable robustness.

## Results

On the primary zero-shot robustness evaluation (PGD-20, ε=1/255) across 15 datasets, AoS achieves 43.88% average robust accuracy, outperforming the best competitor PMG-FT (39.72%) by 4.2% and FARE (37.93%) by 6.0%, while vanilla CLIP collapses to 7.21%. On clean accuracy, AoS reaches 61.70% average, approaching vanilla CLIP (64.90%) while surpassing all adversarial fine-tuning baselines (TeCoA 52.62%, PMG-FT 57.36%, FARE 59.67%). Under stronger attacks (CW, Auto-Attack) and larger perturbation radii (up to 4/255), AoS consistently maintains the highest robust accuracy across all three vision backbones (ViT-B, ViT-L, ResNet-50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AoS outperforms all three adversarial fine-tuning baselines (TeCoA, PMG-FT, FARE) on both clean and robust accuracy across all 15 datasets. On robust accuracy (the primary metric), AoS leads PMG-FT by 4.2% (43.88% vs 39.72%), TeCoA by 5.0% (43.88% vs 38.91%), and FARE by 6.0% (43.88% vs 37.93%). On clean accuracy, AoS (61.70%) beats FARE (59.67%) by 2.0%, PMG-FT (57.36%) by 4.3%, and TeCoA (52.62%) by 9.1%, while remaining only 3.2% below vanilla CLIP (64.90%)—indicating minimal natural-performance degradation. The gap widens under stronger attacks: at ε=4/255, AoS retains 9.51% average robust accuracy versus 8.38% (TeCoA), 6.58% (PMG-FT), and 7.74% (FARE). AoS also extends its advantage to text-level and bi-level attacks (Table 6), where it leads by ~4–5% over all baselines on BERT-Attack, GBDA, Co-Attack, and SGA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial fine-tuning paradigm for CLIP established by TeCoA (Mao et al., ICLR 2023), PMG-FT (Wang et al., CVPR 2024), and FARE (Schlarmann et al., 2024), which all perform sample-wise feature alignment between individual adversarial images and static text prompt embeddings. The key departure is replacing this point-wise alignment with a distributional (second-order) alignment: embeddings of an image and its augmentations span a subspace that is aligned with a subspace formed by a class text prompt and its synonymous/antonymous prompts, using Grassmann projection distance. The computational backbone is the MaxExp approximation (Koniusz et al., CVPR 2016) to Grassmann feature maps, which avoids costly SVD and its gradient instability at non-simple singular values, while the geometric foundation draws on Grassmann discriminant analysis (Hamm & Lee, ICML 2008).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete failure mode of prior adversarial fine-tuning methods—augmented test samples degrade faster than clean ones under attack—attributable to ignoring group-level statistics, and shows that subspace alignment closes this gap consistently across 15 datasets, three architectures, and four attack types. The practical contribution of UASP (one shared perturbation per image set rather than per augmentation) achieving near-instance-wise performance at 2.5× lower cost is immediately useful for practitioners. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger VLMs (e.g., ViT-H or LLaVA-class models), whether the number of augmentations/synonyms needed grows with model capacity, and whether the subspace formulation can be extended to multi-modal generation tasks beyond classification and retrieval.
