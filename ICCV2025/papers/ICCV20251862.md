## Problem

Existing 3D language-embedded scene methods (e.g., LERF, LangSplat, LangSurf) require calibrated dense views (typically 20+) and per-scene optimization, producing severe artifacts and implausible semantics when only sparse views are available. They also depend on scene-specific autoencoders to compress 512-dim CLIP features, which is time-consuming, prone to domain overfit, and prevents cross-scene generalization. The paper addresses how to build generalizable, language-embedded 3D scenes from as few as two uncalibrated images without per-scene retraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes as few as two sparse RGB images (no camera calibration required) and a free-form text prompt as input. It first generates a 49-frame video at 720×480 resolution containing three synchronized streams: RGB images, surface normal maps, and multi-hierarchy semantic segmentation masks. These dense frames are then used to reconstruct a 3D Gaussian Splatting scene whose primitives carry 3-channel discrete language latents (compressed from 512-dim CLIP features via the LQC), enabling real-time open-vocabulary text queries from any novel viewpoint.

## Method

The core is a TriMap video diffusion model built on CogVideoX's DiT architecture, modified for key-frame interpolation, and trained through a four-stage progressive multi-task scheme: (1) general key-frame interpolation on large-scale web data, (2) 3D-consistency fine-tuning on ~10K 3D-consistent videos, (3) normal-map generation using StableNormal-annotated clips, and (4) semantic-mask generation using SAM2-annotated clips, all under a shared denoising objective. A Language Quantized Compressor (LQC) then maps dense CLIP features into 3-channel discrete indices via a 2048-entry codebook with vector quantization, trained on COCO with reconstruction, embedding, and pseudo-mask alignment losses to preserve language properties at extreme compression ratios. Finally, 3D Gaussians are initialized from DUSt3R point clouds and trained with progressive normal regularization (switching from raw to filtered normals after a threshold step) plus 2D and 3D clustering losses derived from generated segmentation masks, anchoring language features tightly to object surfaces.

## Results

On the LERF-OVS dataset, LangScene-X achieves 80.85% mAcc and 50.52% mIoU for open-vocabulary 2D segmentation, outperforming the best 3D language field baseline (LSM) by 31.18% in mAcc and 27.63% in mIoU. On ScanNet, it reaches 94.14% mAcc and 66.54% mIoU, surpassing the best existing method by 14.92% in mIoU. The method operates from as few as two sparse input views, whereas per-scene baselines (LangSplat, LangSurf) require calibrated dense views (typically >20 images).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LangScene-X decisively outperforms all four baselines on both datasets. Against the per-scene optimized methods LangSplat (35.72% mAcc, 15.37% mIoU) and LangSurf (40.26% mAcc, 19.78% mIoU) on LERF-OVS, LangScene-X leads by over 40% in mAcc and ~30% in mIoU, while requiring only 2 input views versus >20 calibrated views. Against the generalizable LSM method (49.67% mAcc, 22.89% mIoU), the gap is 31.18% in mAcc and 27.63% in mIoU on LERF-OVS, and 20.32% in mAcc and 18.23% in mIoU on ScanNet. Even against the 2D open-vocabulary baseline LSeg (64.17% mAcc, 39.94% mIoU on LERF-OVS; 79.49% mAcc, 56.08% mIoU on ScanNet), LangScene-X achieves superior performance, demonstrating that its 3D-consistent generative pipeline produces more accurate language-embedded representations than purely 2D approaches. The ablation (Table 3) further confirms that both the progressive TriMap training and the LQC contribute meaningfully, with the full model reaching 51.68% mIoU on ScanNet vs. 44.25% without progressive training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on CogVideoX's DiT-based video diffusion architecture but repurposes it as a multi-modal 3D-consistent generator (RGB, normals, semantics) rather than a single-modality novel-view synthesizer as in ReconX or ViewCrafter. It departs from the per-scene optimization paradigm of LERF, LangSplat, and LangSurf—which require 20+ calibrated dense views and scene-specific autoencoders—by replacing per-scene feature compression with a generalizable Language Quantized Compressor (LQC) trained once on COCO. The progressive multi-task training strategy (RGB → normals → semantics) is a novel training recipe that bridges domain gaps between modalities within a single diffusion model, distinguishing it from prior work that handles each modality separately.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LangScene-X demonstrates that a generative video diffusion model can simultaneously produce the appearance, geometry, and semantics needed for 3D language-embedded scene reconstruction from as few as two images, eliminating the dense calibrated-view requirement that has limited prior methods to controlled capture setups. The LQC's ability to compress 512-dim CLIP features into 3-channel discrete latents without per-scene retraining is a practical efficiency win for scaling to large-scale deployment. However, the evaluation is limited to 2D segmentation metrics (mAcc, mIoU) on two datasets, and the per-scene Gaussian training step remains, so full end-to-end generalizability and 3D reconstruction quality (e.g., geometric accuracy, novel-view PSNR) are not directly validated. Open questions include whether the progressive training strategy scales to additional modalities (depth, optical flow) and whether the LQC's discrete codebook generalizes to open-world scenes beyond COCO's distribution.
