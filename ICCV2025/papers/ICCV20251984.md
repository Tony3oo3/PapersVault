## Problem

Existing visual grounding research focuses almost exclusively on ground-level images, leaving aerial/UAV visual grounding unexplored. Aerial scenes introduce two compounding challenges: high-resolution imagery with broad fields of view makes objects small and hard to localize, and crowded urban environments contain many visually similar objects (e.g., multiple red cars) that cannot be distinguished by appearance alone, requiring explicit spatial-relation reasoning. No dataset or model had been designed to address these challenges jointly.

## Input / Output

The model consumes high-resolution aerial RGB images (predominantly >1000×500 pixels, sourced from VisDrone2019) paired with natural language descriptions that explicitly encode positional relations using an 8-direction vocabulary (above, below, left, right, top-left, top-right, bottom-left, bottom-right) and reference auxiliary objects for disambiguation. Beyond the standard bounding box output, the model produces an m×m relation score matrix capturing pairwise spatial compatibility between all detected objects, from which the final target is selected by combining per-object relation scores with class-head logits.

## Method

Building on GroundingDINO (Swin-L backbone + BERT text encoder), the paper introduces two modules. First, Hierarchical Cross-Attention preserves the layered structure of Swin-Transformer features rather than flattening them; it propagates attention from higher to lower layers via bilinear interpolation (weighted by α) and from the first to the last layer via convolution (weighted by β), ensuring small objects in high-resolution aerial images receive adequate focus across all scales. Second, the Relation-Aware Grounding module, placed after the decoder, concatenates every pair of m object features to form m² pairwise representations, applies self-attention within each column to capture inter-object spatial context, then cross-attends with text features to align visual relations with linguistic descriptions; a softmax over the resulting m×m matrix yields a relation score per object pair, and the row-maximum is combined with class-head scores for final localization. Training is two-stage: the base model is first trained on standard VG datasets (RefCOCO series) without the relation module, then all parameters are frozen while the Relation-Aware Grounding module is fine-tuned exclusively on the AerialVG dataset. The loss combines L1+GIoU regression, contrastive classification (GLIP-style), and a cross-entropy relation loss computed over the relation matrix using Hungarian matching to identify target–auxiliary object pairs.

## Results

On the AerialVG dataset (fine-tuning setting), the proposed model achieves 50.01% Top-1 accuracy and 87.00% Top-5 accuracy, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline GroundingDINO (29.36% Top-1, 78.87% Top-5). In the zero-shot setting, all existing VG models struggle, with Top-1 accuracy below 12%, highlighting the difficulty of aerial visual grounding. On the standard RefCOCO series benchmarks, AerialVG (Swin-L) achieves 90.58/93.23/88.56 on RefCOCO val/testA/testB, 82.83/89.02/76.03 on RefCOCO+, and 86.52/88.04 on RefCOCOg, matching or slightly exceeding GroundingDINO with the same backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the primary AerialVG benchmark (fine-tuning), the proposed model wins decisively over all four baselines: it surpasses GroundingDINO by +20.65 points in Top-1 (50.01 vs. 29.36) and +8.13 in Top-5 (87.00 vs. 78.87), and leads SimVG by +29.07 in Top-1 and +55.68 in Top-5. The gap over TranVG and Dynamic-MDETR is even larger, with Top-1 improvements of +38.48 and +30.14 respectively. On the RefCOCO series, AerialVG (Swin-L) narrowly edges out GroundingDINO (same backbone) on RefCOCO testB (+0.32) and RefCOCOg test (+1.02), but trails SimVG (ViT-L) on RefCOCO+ val (82.83 vs. 85.36) and testB (76.03 vs. 79.74), indicating that the aerial-specific modules do not compromise general grounding but do not universally dominate on ground-level benchmarks. Overall, the paper's method clearly wins on its target aerial task while remaining competitive on standard VG benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual grounding task from ground-level scenes to aerial/UAV perspectives, building directly on GroundingDINO's transformer encoder-decoder architecture and query-selection mechanism. It departs from prior VG methods (TransVG, Dynamic-MDETR, SimVG) by introducing two aerial-specific components: Hierarchical Cross-Attention that exploits Swin-Transformer's multi-scale features to counteract the small-object-in-large-scene problem, and a Relation-Aware Grounding module that explicitly models pairwise spatial relations among objects—something prior VG methods handle only implicitly through appearance matching. The dataset is sourced from VisDrone2019 but re-annotated with spatial-relation descriptions, positioning it as a bridge between the object-detection literature (VisDrone) and the referring-expression literature (RefCOCO series).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a clear gap: no prior dataset or benchmark addressed multi-object spatial-relation grounding in high-resolution aerial imagery, a setting critical for UAV navigation, emergency response, and ecological monitoring. The ablation cleanly separates the contributions—Hierarchical Cross-Attention improves candidate retrieval (Top-5) while Relation-Aware Grounding drives precise disambiguation (Top-1)—giving practitioners a clear recipe for handling visually homogeneous object sets. A stated limitation is the inability to leverage unstructured background cues (roads, terrain), which constrains holistic scene understanding; the authors flag promoting spatial intelligence in perception models as key future work. The benchmark's narrow domain (UAV, 5K images) limits immediate broad adoption, but the spatial-relation annotation paradigm could transfer to other dense-scene grounding tasks.
