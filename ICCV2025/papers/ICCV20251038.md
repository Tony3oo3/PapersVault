## Problem

Recent pose-guided text-to-image generation has shifted toward dense signals (depth, DensePose, SMPL) because sparse signals like OpenPose were perceived as insufficient for precise pose control. However, dense signals introduce inflexibility in condition creation/editing and risk contradicting textual prompts by over-constraining object shapes. This paper asks whether sparse signals can be made sufficiently expressive to achieve pose alignment comparable to dense signals while retaining their simplicity, shape-agnosticism, and cross-species generalizability.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse OpenPose skeleton (N predefined keypoints with 2D coordinates and visibility flags, plus skeleton edges) and a natural-language text prompt. The OpenPose is not fed as a standard 3-channel RGB image; instead, it is rendered into a multi-channel (C′-channel) skeleton image using learned per-keypoint embeddings, and the text prompt is augmented with N new keypoint-concept tokens (one per visible keypoint) before encoding. The output is a single RGB image whose pose strictly aligns with the input skeleton, generated via 50-step UniPC sampling with CFG scale 7.5.

## Method

SP-Ctrl builds on a frozen Stable Diffusion v1.5 with a from-scratch ControlNet adapter and introduces two key mechanisms. First, a Spatial-Pose Representation (SPR) module replaces OpenPose's fixed RGB colors with learnable keypoint embeddings produced by an MLP G(·) applied to randomly initialized vectors, yielding a multi-channel skeleton image where semantically similar keypoints are pulled together in embedding space; skeleton edges use a simple all-ones embedding. Second, a Keypoint Concept Learning (KCL) strategy appends new keypoint tokens to the text prompt (initialized via textual inversion from keypoint descriptions) and imposes a heatmap loss on the cross-attention maps of those tokens from the 3rd transformer block during diffusion time steps 250–500, forcing each keypoint token to attend to its corresponding spatial location. The model is trained jointly on the standard ε-prediction denoising loss plus the heatmap alignment loss (weighted by η=0.1), with gradients from the heatmap loss detached from the noisy-image query to prevent information leakage.

## Results

On AP-10K, SP-Ctrl achieves a Pose mAP of 55.63, outperforming the ControlNet baseline (44.25) by 11.38% and T2I-Adapter (48.16) by 7.47%, while also attaining the best FID (18.52) and Detection AP.75 (25.10). On Human-Art, SP-Ctrl reaches a Pose mAP of 51.11, matching the SOTA GRPose (50.93) without requiring pretrained pose estimators during training, and achieves the best Detection AP.75 (9.11). CLIP-Score remains comparable to baselines (24.51 vs. 24.77 on AP-10K; 26.30 vs. 27.84 on Human-Art), indicating that the pose-alignment gains come at only a minor cost to text-image alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline ControlNet (with OpenPose), SP-Ctrl wins decisively on Pose mAP on both datasets (+11.38% on AP-10K: 55.63 vs. 44.25; +5.85% on Human-Art: 51.11 vs. 45.26) and on FID for AP-10K (18.52 vs. 19.40), while CLIP-Score drops only marginally (24.51 vs. 24.77). Compared to T2I-Adapter, SP-Ctrl dominates on every metric across both datasets, with the largest gap in Pose mAP (55.63 vs. 48.16 on AP-10K; 51.11 vs. 38.37 on Human-Art). On Human-Art, SP-Ctrl slightly surpasses GRPose (50.93) in Pose mAP and substantially outperforms it in Detection AP.75 (9.11 vs. 6.51), while avoiding the need for pretrained pose estimators during training; the trade-off is a lower CLIP-Score (26.30 vs. 27.95) and a marginally higher FID (29.30 vs. 28.85). Against HumanSD, SP-Ctrl achieves higher Pose mAP (51.11 vs. 49.92) and a significantly better FID (29.30 vs. 35.18), again without external pose-estimation losses. Overall, SP-Ctrl trades a small CLIP-Score reduction for substantially improved pose alignment and detection fidelity, matching or exceeding dense-signal methods while retaining the flexibility of sparse pose inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the ControlNet architecture (Zhang et al., ICCV 2023) atop frozen Stable Diffusion (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022), but repositions the problem by arguing that the field's shift toward dense signals (DensePose, depth, SMPL) has underexploited the potential of sparse OpenPose representations. It extends OpenPose from a visualization format to a learnable spatial-pose representation with discriminative keypoint embeddings, and borrows the textual-inversion mechanism (Gal et al., 2022) to introduce a novel Keypoint Concept Learning strategy that constrains cross-attention maps via heatmaps. Unlike prior sparse-signal enhancement methods (HumanSD, GRPose, Stable-Pose) that rely on pretrained pose estimators or graph/ViT features, SP-Ctrl achieves competitive alignment without such external dependencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that sparse signals can match dense signals in pose alignment while retaining practical advantages—ease of editing, shape-agnosticism, and cross-species generalization—this work challenges the implicit assumption that dense representations are necessary for precise spatial control. The finding that OpenPose's default RGB embeddings actively degrade pose guidance (performing worse than random initialization) is a useful diagnostic for practitioners. Open questions include whether the keypoint concept learning strategy generalizes to multi-person or multi-object scenes, whether it transfers to 3D parametric models (SMPL), and whether the approach scales to video generation where temporal consistency adds further constraints.
