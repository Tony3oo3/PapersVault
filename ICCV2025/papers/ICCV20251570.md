## Problem

Text-to-video generation models are trained on detailed, carefully crafted descriptions, but real-world user inputs are often concise, vague, or poorly structured, creating a critical train-inference gap. Existing prompt optimization methods that rely on LLM in-context learning can distort user intent, introduce safety risks, and fail to account for how the refined prompt actually affects generated video quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251570/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single user text query (ranging from brief phrases like 'A person is hugging' to potentially unsafe or ambiguous descriptions) and produces a detailed, well-structured optimized prompt designed to be harmless, accurate to the original intent, and sufficiently descriptive for the target video generation model. The optimized prompt is then consumed by a diffusion-based video model (CogVideoX-2B/5B or Open-Sora 1.2) to generate the final video, making the prompt optimizer a text-to-text intermediary that does not modify the video model itself.

## Method

VPO employs a two-stage training pipeline. In the first stage (Principle-Based SFT), an SFT dataset of ~20k query-prompt pairs is constructed from VidProM using GPT-4o in-context learning, then refined via LLM-as-a-judge critiques against three principles (harmlessness, accuracy, helpfulness); a model is fine-tuned on the resulting pairs. In the second stage (Multi-Feedback Preference Optimization), K prompts are sampled per query from the SFT model, and DPO training pairs are constructed from two feedback sources: text-level (LLM-as-a-judge identifies principle violations and produces refined corrections) and video-level (VisionReward scores videos generated from each prompt, yielding preference pairs based on quality). The final model is trained with DPO on the union of both preference datasets, explicitly optimizing for safer, more accurate, and higher-quality video outputs simultaneously.

## Results

On CogVideoX-5B, VPO achieves a MonetBench Overall score of 4.15 and VBench Human Action of 99.60%, outperforming GPT-4o Few-Shot (4.03 / 99.20%) and the official GLM-4 Few-Shot method (3.98 / 98.40%). In pairwise human evaluation, VPO improves the win rate by 37.5% over original user queries and 14% over the official prompt optimization method. On the text-level alignment benchmark, VPO reaches 94.8% aligned rate with only 0.4% unsafe outputs, compared to 86.4% aligned and 2.4% unsafe for GPT-4o Few-Shot.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VPO outperforms all four prompt-optimization baselines on both MonetBench and VBench for CogVideoX-5B. Against GPT-4o Few-Shot, VPO gains +0.12 on MonetBench Overall (4.15 vs 4.03) and +0.40 on VBench Human Action (99.60 vs 99.20). Against the official GLM-4 Few-Shot method, VPO improves MonetBench Overall by +0.17 (4.15 vs 3.98) and VBench Multiple Objects by +3.35 (75.73 vs 72.38). Prompt-A-Video, which incorporates video reward models but lacks text-level alignment, trails VPO by 0.10 on MonetBench Overall and 7.80 on VBench Human Action. Promptist, an image-domain prompt optimizer, performs worst among all baselines (3.42 MonetBench Overall), confirming that image-specific optimization does not transfer well to video. In the RLHF comparison (Section 4.5), VPO outperforms Diffusion DPO in VisionReward pairwise evaluation and can be combined with Diffusion DPO for additional orthogonal gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the HHH (Harmless, Honest, Helpful) alignment framework from LLMs and the SFT-then-DPO training recipe into the domain of text-to-video prompt optimization, a task previously handled by ad-hoc LLM in-context rewriting (CogVideoX's GLM-4 few-shot, GPT-4o rewriting) or by reward-guided search (Prompt-A-Video). It departs from prior prompt-optimization work by explicitly incorporating safety as a first-class objective and by grounding the 'helpfulness' criterion in actual video-level reward signals (VisionReward) rather than purely textual richness. The multi-feedback DPO stage also positions prompt optimization as an orthogonal alignment lever to model-level methods like Diffusion DPO, rather than a mere preprocessing step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for anyone deploying text-to-video models: a small, trainable prompt optimizer can close the training-inference gap, improve safety, and boost video quality without modifying the video model itself. The demonstrated cross-model generalization (CogVideoX-trained optimizer improving Open-Sora) suggests a single general prompt optimizer could serve multiple video backbones, reducing per-model engineering. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer or more complex multi-shot prompts, how it interacts with emerging autoregressive video models, and whether the reliance on a specific reward model (VisionReward) limits robustness. The paper does not deeply explore failure modes of the DPO stage or the cost of the iterative video-generation-and-scoring loop.
