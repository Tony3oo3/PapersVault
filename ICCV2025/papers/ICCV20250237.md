## Problem

Post-hoc logit adjustment (LA) is widely used in class-imbalanced semi-supervised learning (CISSL) to debias classifier predictions, but it relies on fixed label frequencies from the labeled set. When the unlabeled set has a different (and typically unknown) class distribution, LA applies an inappropriate degree of adjustment, potentially degrading performance. The paper asks whether the degree of logit adjustment can be learned adaptively to account for both the labeled and unlabeled class distributions simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a class-imbalanced labeled set (with known per-class counts) and an unlabeled set whose class distribution may differ from the labeled set and is generally unknown. Inputs are standard RGB images at resolutions ranging from 32×32 (CIFAR-10/100) to 96×96 (STL-10) and 64×64 (Small-ImageNet-127). The output is a per-class probability vector adjusted by learned per-class logit shifts, from which a single class label is predicted via argmax.

## Method

LLA introduces a per-class adjustment parameter λ_k and learns it by minimizing a class-averaged cross-entropy loss (a differentiable surrogate for balanced error rate) computed over both labeled and unlabeled minibatches, where unlabeled samples use refined pseudo-labels generated by adjusting the base SSL algorithm's biased predictions with the current λ. This allows λ to implicitly encode both the labeled and unlabeled class distributions without explicit distribution estimation. The learned λ is then used to refine pseudo-labels during training and to shift logits at test time. In parallel, Extended Feature Cluster Compression (EFCC) replaces the fixed scaling factors of FCC with λ-derived factors to densify minority-class feature clusters, improving separability. Gradients from the class-averaged loss are not backpropagated into the CNN backbone to avoid degrading feature quality, and the total training loss is simply L_base + L_c.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-LT with matched distributions (γ=γ_l=γ_u), ReMixMatch+LLA achieves 89.0% bACC / 88.8% GM at γ=50, 85.8% / 85.6% at γ=100, and 83.2% / 82.9% at γ=150, outperforming the strongest baseline (ReMixMatch+CDMAD) by 0.7–1.0 bACC points. Under the paper's key mismatch setting (γ_l=100, γ_u=1), ReMixMatch+LLA reaches 90.4% bACC / 90.2% GM, surpassing CDMAD (89.9/89.6) and DARP+cRT (88.7/88.5). On CIFAR-100-LT, LLA leads by 1.2–1.6 bACC over the best baseline across γ=20/50/100, and on Small-ImageNet-127 it gains 5.3 bACC (32×32) and 6.0 bACC (64×64) over the next-best method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLA outperforms all 14 baselines across every dataset and imbalance setting. Against the closest competitor CDMAD [39], LLA gains 0.7 bACC on CIFAR-10-LT (γ=50, ReMixMatch) and 0.5 bACC under the mismatch setting (γ_l=100, γ_u=1), with the gap widening to 1.2 bACC on CIFAR-100-LT (γ=20). Compared to DARP+cRT [27,28], LLA improves by 1.7 bACC on CIFAR-10-LT (γ=50) and 1.7 bACC under mismatch (γ_u=1). Against SAW+cRT [27,35], the advantage is 1.4 bACC (γ=50) and 1.6 bACC (γ_u=1). On Small-ImageNet-127, LLA leads ABC [40] by 5.3 bACC (32×32) and 6.0 bACC (64×64), and CReST+LA [47,66] by 11.3 bACC (32×32). The ablation (Table 5) confirms that replacing LLA with the original fixed LA [47] causes a catastrophic drop to 62.3 bACC under mismatch, validating that the learnable adjustment is essential when labeled and unlabeled distributions differ.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends post-hoc logit adjustment (LA) [Menon et al., ICLR 2020] from a fixed, label-frequency-based correction to a learnable per-class adjustment parameter, motivated by the observation that LA degrades performance when labeled and unlabeled class distributions mismatch. It also extends Feature Cluster Compression (FCC) [Li et al., CVPR 2023] to the semi-supervised setting by reusing the learned adjustment parameters to compute scaling factors, addressing FCC's inability to account for the unlabeled set's distribution. The work positions itself against a family of CISSL algorithms (SAW, CReST, UDAL, DARP, ACR, TCBC, CDMAD) that either assume matched distributions or require explicit estimation of the unlabeled class prior, arguing that minimizing a class-averaged loss on both sets implicitly captures the relevant distributional information without such estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: real-world unlabeled sets (e.g., STL-10) often have unknown and mismatched class distributions, a setting where standard LA-based CISSL methods can actively harm performance. The Fisher consistency proposition (LLA is Fisher consistent for BER when φ(λ)=π) provides a clean theoretical justification, and the class-averaged loss as a BER surrogate offers a principled training objective. Limitations include evaluation only on image classification with CNN backbones (ResNet/WideResNet), and the authors note future extension to tabular and NLP datasets. An open question is whether the learned λ generalizes well under extreme label scarcity or when the unlabeled set is very small, and whether the approach transfers to other semi-supervised paradigms beyond FixMatch/ReMixMatch.
