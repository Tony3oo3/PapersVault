## Problem

Existing diffusion-based video super-resolution methods consistently fail on severely degraded real-world videos, producing over-smoothed oil-painting results or leaving complex artifacts intact. The paper identifies that the primary bottleneck is not architectural design but the overwhelming learning burden of simultaneously modeling complex degradation distributions, content representations, and temporal relationships with limited high-quality training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250764/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 4x low-resolution video (processed in 8-frame subsequences with 4-frame overlaps) and an optional text prompt as input, producing a high-resolution video in the latent space of a pretrained Stable Diffusion x4 Upscaler. Training uses 320×320 patches from 8-frame segments with temporal sampling strides of 1–6 to capture diverse motion patterns, and the model performs 4× super-resolution at inference.

## Method

The core contribution is a Progressive Learning Strategy (PLS) that decomposes the learning burden across three stages: (1) fine-tuning only temporal layers with simple degradations (Gaussian blur, bicubic downsampling) on large-scale data, (2) introducing complex real-world degradations (noise, compression artifacts) while maintaining dataset scale, and (3) fully unfreezing all parameters and refining on high-quality video data. An Interweaved Latent Transition (ILT) technique ensures temporal consistency across video segments via position-based latent interpolation in overlapping regions and strategic noise rescheduling, requiring no additional training. Supporting architectural components include a Multi-Scale Temporal Attention module fusing temporal information at two resolutions and a Temporal-Enhanced 3D VAE with 3D residual blocks and temporal attention, trained with L1, perceptual, and adversarial losses. Ablations show PLS yields substantially larger gains than architectural improvements, particularly on severely degraded inputs.

## Results

On complex-degradation benchmarks, DiffVSR achieves the best perceptual quality across all evaluated datasets: MUSIQ 66.431 and DOVER 0.826 on UDM10 (complex), MUSIQ 67.679 and DOVER 0.710 on YouHQ40 (complex), and MUSIQ 66.829 and DOVER 0.710 on MVSR4x. Under simple degradations, DiffVSR remains competitive (e.g., MUSIQ 67.731 on UDM10, DOVER 0.878 on YouHQ40) while RealBasicVSR retains a PSNR advantage (28.526 vs. 26.966 on UDM10). The performance gap over competing methods widens substantially as degradation complexity increases, with DiffVSR leading by 5.8 MUSIQ points and 0.107 DOVER over the next-best method on UDM10 (complex). On the real-world RealVideo10 dataset, DiffVSR achieves the best CLIP-IQA (0.856), MUSIQ (59.566), and DOVER (0.749).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffVSR dominates all perceptual quality metrics (MUSIQ, DOVER, CLIP-IQA) over every baseline under complex degradations, with the largest margins against diffusion-based competitors: on UDM10 (complex), it leads UAV by 9.3 MUSIQ points (66.431 vs. 57.117) and MGLD by 7.9 points, while also surpassing the non-diffusion RealBasicVSR by 5.8 points. Under simple degradations, the gap narrows but DiffVSR still leads on MUSIQ and DOVER across all four synthetic benchmarks. RealBasicVSR retains a consistent PSNR advantage (e.g., 28.526 vs. 26.966 on UDM10) and the best warping error on UDM10 (0.499 vs. 0.604), reflecting its fidelity-oriented design, but DiffVSR surpasses it on every perceptual metric. Among diffusion-based video methods, UAV and MGLD show the most severe degradation under complex conditions (e.g., UAV drops to DOVER 0.606 on YouHQ40 complex vs. DiffVSR's 0.710), confirming the paper's thesis that architectural complexity alone is insufficient. On real-world MVSR4x, DiffVSR achieves the best scores on all four metrics (NRQM 6.432, CLIP-IQA 0.491, MUSIQ 66.829, DOVER 0.710), while on RealVideo10 it trails RealBasicVSR slightly on MUSIQ (59.566 vs. 61.121) and DOVER (0.749 vs. 0.773) but leads on CLIP-IQA (0.856 vs. 0.836).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Stable Diffusion x4 Upscaler backbone and the diffusion-prior-for-VSR framework established by Upscale-A-Video (UAV), while also drawing on noise-rescheduling ideas from FreeNoise and Tune-A-Video for its Interweaved Latent Transition component. Its key conceptual departure from prior diffusion-based VSR work (UAV, MGLD) is the argument that the primary bottleneck is not architectural sophistication but the overwhelming learning burden of simultaneously modeling degradations, content, and temporal relationships. The Progressive Learning Strategy reframes the problem as a curriculum design question rather than an architecture design question, positioning the paper as a methodological counterpoint to the field's prevailing focus on structural innovations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central claim—that staged training decomposes the learning burden more effectively than architectural complexity for severely degraded video—offers a practical recipe that could transfer to other diffusion-based restoration tasks (image restoration, video inpainting, video generation). However, the evidence is confined to a single backbone (Stable Diffusion x4 Upscaler) and a specific three-stage decomposition, leaving open whether PLS generalizes across architectures or whether the stage boundaries are optimal. The multi-stage training also introduces practical overhead (multiple training runs on different datasets) that the paper does not fully quantify against single-stage alternatives. Broader implications for video generation and editing are noted but not explored.
