## Problem

Existing interactive image editing methods built on text-to-image diffusion models lack real-world dynamic priors, requiring massive training samples (hundreds of thousands) and additional reference encoders (e.g., Reference U-Net, IP-Adapter) to achieve plausible edits and visual consistency. This paper asks whether reformulating image editing as an image-to-video generation task can inherit powerful video diffusion priors to drastically reduce training costs while improving edit quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252576/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a source image (576×1024) and a visual editing signal—sketch edges, point pairs, dragging regions, or a coarsely warped image—and produces a two-frame video where the first frame reconstructs the source and the second frame is the edited target. The editing signal is encoded by a lightweight sparse control encoder and injected exclusively into target-image features to avoid disturbing source reconstruction. At inference, euler discrete sampling with 25 steps generates the final edited image from the second frame.

## Method

FramePainter is initialized with Stable Video Diffusion and reformulates editing as generating a two-frame video (source → target), so the model inherits temporal consistency and physical dynamics from pre-trained video priors. A sparse control encoder (inspired by ControlNext) injects the editing signal only into target-image tokens. The key architectural contribution is matching attention: an auxiliary branch of spatial attention that uses target tokens as queries and source tokens as keys/values, extending the receptive field along the temporal axis to enforce dense token-to-token correspondence. During training, matching attention weights are supervised by CoTracker-v3 correspondence matrices and visibility masks via an L2 matching loss (λ=1.0), while the overall objective combines standard diffusion loss (including source-image reconstruction) with this matching loss. The model is fine-tuned on only ~20k image pairs sampled from static-camera videos, achieving state-of-the-art results with 1–10% of the training data used by prior methods.

## Results

FramePainter achieves the best scores across all three editing signals (sketch, coarse edit, and points) on CLIP-FID, LPIPS, and SSIM, using only 20k training samples. For sketch editing, it reduces CLIP-FID from 17.933 (MasaCtrl+ControlNet) to 7.783 and improves SSIM from 0.655 to 0.859. For coarse editing, it outperforms Magic Fixup (trained on 2,500k samples) with CLIP-FID 7.573 vs. 8.757 and SSIM 0.888 vs. 0.855, using only 1% of the training data. For point-based editing, it surpasses both DragDiffusion (training-free) and LightningDrag (220k samples) on all metrics, achieving CLIP-FID 8.513, LPIPS 0.166, and SSIM 0.825.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FramePainter decisively outperforms all four baselines across every editing signal and metric. Against MasaCtrl+ControlNet (training-free, sketch editing), it nearly halves CLIP-FID (7.783 vs. 17.933) and more than doubles SSIM (0.859 vs. 0.655), demonstrating that video diffusion priors provide far stronger dynamic understanding than tuning-free attention control. Against Magic Fixup (coarse editing, 2,500k samples), FramePainter wins on all three metrics while using only 0.8% of the training data, with the largest gap in SSIM (0.888 vs. 0.855). Against DragDiffusion (training-free, point editing), FramePainter improves CLIP-FID by 0.68 and SSIM by 0.014, and unlike DragDiffusion it does not require user-provided masks. Against LightningDrag (220k samples, point editing), FramePainter achieves lower CLIP-FID (8.513 vs. 9.894), lower LPIPS (0.166 vs. 0.214), and higher SSIM (0.825 vs. 0.794) with roughly 9% of the training data. In the user study, raters prefer FramePainter over each baseline in 69.5%–86.4% of pairwise comparisons across visual consistency, edit accuracy, and image quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of building interactive image editing on text-to-image diffusion models (as in Magic Fixup, LightningDrag, DragDiffusion) by reformulating the task as image-to-video generation initialized with Stable Video Diffusion. It inherits the sparse control encoder design from ControlNext and the video-frame-pair supervision strategy from Magic Fixup and LightningDrag, but eliminates the need for reference encoders (Reference U-Net, IP-Adapter) by leveraging SVD's first-frame conditioning for identity preservation. The matching attention module is a novel contribution that extends cross-frame attention with CoTracker-v3-guided supervision to enforce dense token-level correspondence, going beyond the inflated spatial attention used in prior video diffusion work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10–100× reduction in training data (20k vs. 220k–2500k samples) while outperforming prior methods is practically significant, suggesting that video diffusion priors encode sufficient real-world dynamics to substitute for large-scale image-pair training. However, the evaluation is limited to a small custom benchmark of 200 samples, and the method is constrained to two-frame generation, which limits its applicability to more complex multi-step editing workflows. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer editing sequences, whether the CoTracker supervision can be replaced by self-supervised alternatives at inference time, and how the method scales to higher-resolution or more diverse editing signals beyond the three tested.
