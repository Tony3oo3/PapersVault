## Problem

Standard LoRA and its SVD-based variants (e.g., PiSSA) treat all weight components uniformly, ignoring the highly non-uniform activation distributions in large foundation models where a small number of outlier activations disproportionately drive performance. This leads to suboptimal low-rank initialization, uniform rank allocation that misallocates adaptation capacity across layers, and undifferentiated gradient updates that waste capacity on inactive features. The paper asks whether activation outlier statistics can be systematically integrated into initialization, rank assignment, and training to close the gap between PEFT and full fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

Before fine-tuning, AIRA requires a representative calibration batch of inputs to compute per-layer activation statistics (centered covariance for OwSVD and outlier ratios for rank assignment). During training, it consumes the same modalities as the base model—RGB images paired with text prompts for vision-language tasks, text-only prompts for mathematical reasoning, and image-plus-prompt pairs for subject-driven generation—and produces text answers, class labels, or generated images. The method operates on frozen pre-trained backbones (CLIP ViT-B/16, LLaVA-1.5-7B, Mistral-7B, diffusion U-Net) with only the low-rank adapters and a scalar α per layer being updated.

## Method

AIRA integrates activation statistics into three stages of LoRA fine-tuning. First, Outlier-weighted SVD (OwSVD) performs Cholesky decomposition on the centered activation covariance matrix to obtain an invertible transformation S_k, reweights the pre-trained weight matrix as W_k^S = W_k S_k, then applies truncated SVD and inverts the transformation (W' = U·Trunc(Σ)·V·S_k^{-1}) so that initialized adapters emphasize weight components tied to high-variance, outlier-prone activations. Second, Outlier-Driven Rank Assignment formulates per-layer rank allocation as a constrained optimization problem—maximizing Σ|O_l|·f(r_l) subject to a total parameter budget—solved via SLSQP, where f(·) is a logarithmic function capturing diminishing returns and |O_l| is the count of activations exceeding τ·μ in magnitude. Third, Activation-Informed Training applies Input-Weighted Normalization by Hadamard-multiplying the input with a batch-normalized activation-magnitude vector s_in before the low-rank projection, which naturally scales gradients to prioritize updates on high-magnitude input dimensions while suppressing noise from inactive features.

## Results

On few-shot visual classification (ViT-B/16 backbone), AIRA achieves 80.2% average top-1 accuracy in the 4-shot setting and 84.6% in the 16-shot setting, outperforming the strongest baselines (PiSSA, MoSLoRA, CLIP-LoRA) by 0.9–1.0 points. On vision-language instruction tuning (LLaVA-1.5-7B), AIRA reaches 73.75% average accuracy across VQAv2, SQA, TextVQA, and POPE, surpassing PiSSA (73.37%) and DoRA (73.18%). On mathematical reasoning (Mistral-7B), AIRA attains 45.23% average on GSM8K and MATH, exceeding PiSSA (44.35%) and LoRA (43.69%) by 0.9 and 1.5 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AIRA consistently outperforms all nine baselines across every evaluated task. Against the closest LoRA-family competitors, AIRA beats PiSSA by 0.9 points (80.2 vs 79.3) on 4-shot few-shot, 0.4 points (73.75 vs 73.37) on vision-language, and 0.9 points (45.23 vs 44.35) on math reasoning. MoSLoRA and CLIP-LoRA trail AIRA by 0.9 and 1.0 points respectively on 4-shot few-shot. Among prompt-learning baselines, PLOT++ (77.8%) and MaPLe (77.2%) are the strongest, yet both fall 2.4 and 3.0 points behind AIRA on 4-shot. On the 16-shot setting, AIRA (84.6%) edges out PLOT++ (84.4%) and CLIP-LoRA (84.3%) by narrow margins. Notably, AIRA uses fewer trainable parameters (166–182M) than DoRA (172–186M) while achieving higher accuracy, and its outlier-driven rank allocation eliminates the need for AdaLoRA-style sparse mask training.

## Relation to Prior Work

AIRA extends the LoRA framework (Hu et al., 2021) by integrating activation outlier statistics into three previously independent stages: initialization, rank allocation, and training dynamics. Its initialization component (OwSVD) builds directly on PiSSA's SVD-based approach (Meng et al., 2024) but adds a Cholesky-decomposition-based reweighting step that encodes activation covariance before truncation. For rank allocation, AIRA departs from AdaLoRA's learned sparse-mask strategy by deriving layer-wise ranks offline from a principled outlier-ratio metric solved via SLSQP, avoiding the additional optimization overhead. Unlike DoRA and SARA, which modify the adapter architecture itself, AIRA preserves the original LoRA structure and gains its improvements purely from activation-informed priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AIRA provides the first systematic demonstration that activation outliers should inform the entire PEFT pipeline rather than being addressed only in compression contexts (quantization, pruning). The practical benefit—faster convergence with fewer effective parameters and zero inference overhead—makes it attractive for resource-constrained fine-tuning of vision-language and reasoning models. However, the method requires a representative input batch for the offline activation statistics, which limits applicability in truly data-free or streaming settings. Open questions include whether the activation-aware initialization and rank assignment transfer to other PEFT paradigms (adapters, prompt tuning) and whether the outlier-driven allocation generalizes to architectures without the characteristic activation outlier structure observed in transformers.
