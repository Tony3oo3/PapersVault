## Problem

WSI-based survival analysis models trained on data from one hospital or laboratory fail to transfer to another due to significant distribution shifts in both feature and category (risk-level) distributions. This paper is the first to formally address this domain shift problem in the WSI survival analysis setting, formulating it as a graph domain adaptation (GDA) task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250949/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each WSI is cropped into 512×512 non-overlapping patches, with 1024-dimensional features extracted per patch via CLAM; a graph is then constructed using KNN over Euclidean distances between patch features, with nodes as patch embeddings and edges encoding spatial neighborhood. The model outputs a discrete-time hazard function over K risk levels (derived from binned survival times), from which the survival function S(y|x) is computed as a product of complementary hazards. In the GDA setting, the source domain provides labeled graphs with risk levels while the target domain supplies unlabeled graphs for which risk levels must be predicted.

## Method

DETA employs a dual-branch graph encoder where the MP branch implicitly captures topological semantics through standard GCN-style neighborhood aggregation, while the SP branch explicitly extracts high-order structural semantics via shortest-path kernels with sinusoidal position encodings over path lengths. For category-level alignment, the two branches are coupled by deriving dual ELBOs in which each branch alternately serves as the target distribution p and the approximation q, with a confidence threshold ζ filtering low-quality pseudo labels for the target domain. For feature-level alignment, learnable perturbations δ^MP and δ^SP are adversarially optimized against a domain classifier to augment source-domain graph features, driving the feature distributions of both domains closer. Training proceeds in two stages: pre-training the encoder and hazard predictor on the source domain with the survival loss, followed by a GDA fine-tuning stage that jointly minimizes the survival loss, the two coupled ELBO losses, and the adversarial perturbation objective.

## Results

DETA achieves the best C-index across all 12 source-to-target adaptation directions on four TCGA cancer datasets (BLCA, LGG, LUAD, UCEC). The strongest result is BLCA→UCEC with a C-index of 0.7026±0.0015, while the weakest is LUAD→BLCA at 0.6127±0.0108. DETA outperforms the best baseline (CTL or ECB, both 2024) by 1.4–5.0 C-index points depending on the adaptation direction, with the largest margin on BLCA→UCEC (+5.0 over ECB) and BLCA→LGG (+3.5 over CTL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DETA outperforms all 12 baselines across every one of the 12 adaptation directions. The closest competitors are the 2024 domain-adaptation methods CTL and ECB [43], which themselves lead most non-adaptation baselines; DETA surpasses them by 1.4–5.0 C-index points (e.g., 0.7026 vs. 0.6526 on BLCA→UCEC, 0.6566 vs. 0.6215 on BLCA→LGG). Among non-adaptation survival methods, STRAP [47] (style-transfer augmentation) and SurvPath [18] (multimodal pathway modeling) are the strongest, but DETA still leads by 2–5 points. Graph-based WiKG [21] and attention-based CLAM [24] show the weakest cross-domain performance, with C-index values often below 0.56, highlighting the severity of domain shift without explicit adaptation. The consistent margin over both domain-adaptation and non-adaptation baselines across all directions suggests that the dual-branch encoder combined with two-level alignment provides a robust advantage regardless of the specific source-target pair.

## Relation to Prior Work

DETA sits at the intersection of three established lines of work: graph-based WSI survival analysis (using GCNs and MIL formulations), graph domain adaptation (transferring knowledge across source/target graphs via GNNs), and shortest-path kernel methods for explicit high-order graph semantics (Borgwardt & Kriegel, ICDM 2005). The paper departs from prior GDA methods, which typically rely solely on neighborhood aggregation (MP) for cross-domain transfer, by introducing a complementary SP branch that captures explicit global structural information. The two-level alignment strategy—coupling ELBOs for category distribution alignment and adversarial perturbation for feature distribution alignment—repurposes variational inference and GAN-style training in a novel dual-branch context, going beyond the single-level alignment used in most prior UDA or GDA work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to explicitly address domain shift in WSI-based survival analysis, a practically critical gap since WSIs from different hospitals or laboratories exhibit significant distribution differences that degrade model transfer. The consistent C-index improvements across all 12 adaptation directions on four TCGA datasets, along with the thorough ablation isolating each component's contribution, provide credible evidence for the framework's utility. However, the evaluation is limited to four TCGA cancer types with a single metric (C-index), and the method depends on CLAM-extracted patch features and KNN graph construction, raising open questions about scalability to more diverse clinical settings, semi-supervised target domains, and alternative graph construction strategies.
