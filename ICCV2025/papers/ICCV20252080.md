## Problem

Existing text-to-motion generation methods (MDM, MotionGPT, MoMask) suffer from poor zero-shot generalization because they are trained on small, limited datasets (HumanML3D, MotionX) and lack sufficient model capacity. Even recent scaling efforts (ScaMo at 250 hours, OMG at 1B parameters) fail to handle complex compositional motions or out-of-domain prompts, and no comprehensive benchmark exists to evaluate zero-shot motion generation capability.

## Input / Output

The model takes free-form natural language text prompts (including long, multi-step compositional descriptions) as input, processed via T5XL word-level encoding, and produces 3D human motion sequences as SMPL parameters (root translation, body pose, root orientation, shape) at 30 fps. The motion representation is reformulated to avoid inverse-kinematics errors present in the HumanML3D format, preserving redundant information such as velocity. Generated motions are discretized into FSQ tokens during training and decoded back to continuous SMPL space at inference.

## Method

The architecture has two stages: (1) an FSQ-based motion tokenizer that discretizes continuous motion into a structured grid of codes, with a wavelet transform applied before encoding and an inverse wavelet transform after decoding to suppress high-frequency jitter caused by discretization information loss at million-scale data; (2) a LLAMA-style autoregressive transformer (scaled from 1B to 7B parameters) that uses a mixed attention strategy—bidirectional among text tokens, causal among motion tokens—within Hybrid Attention Blocks containing RMS-Norm, mixed attention, and FFN layers. The model is trained with cross-entropy loss on motion token prediction conditioned on text, and the key insight is that wavelet-domain preprocessing decouples low-frequency motion structure from high-frequency detail, allowing FSQ to encode the former stably while the inverse transform restores the latter without amplifying quantization artifacts.

## Results

On the MotionMillion-Eval benchmark (126 prompts, 7 categories), the 7B model achieves the best Text Alignment score of 261, substantially outperforming ScaMo-3B (226.6), T2M-GPT (207), MDM (195.5), and MotionGPT (170). For Physical Plausibility (495.5) and Motion Smoothness (501), the 7B model is comparable to MDM and MotionGPT while significantly surpassing ScaMo-3B (477.5 and 494 respectively). On the quantitative MotionMillion evaluation, the 7B model achieves FID 10.3 and R@1 0.79, dramatically outperforming ScaMo (FID 89.0, R@1 0.67).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The 7B model decisively wins on Text Alignment, scoring 261 versus ScaMo-3B's 226.6 (+34.4), T2M-GPT's 207 (+54), MDM's 195.5 (+65.5), and MotionGPT's 170 (+91), demonstrating that the 2M-sequence MotionMillion dataset and 7B scale unlock substantially stronger text-motion alignment. On Physical Plausibility, the 7B model (495.5) ties T2M-GPT (495.5) and is within 1.5 points of MotionGPT (497), while clearly beating ScaMo-3B (477.5) and MDM (478.5) by ~18 points. On Motion Smoothness, the 7B model (501) is essentially tied with MotionGPT (501.5) and T2M-GPT (500), and beats ScaMo-3B (494) by 7 points, though it trails MDM (416.5) by a wide margin. The authors note that Physical Plausibility and Motion Smoothness are largely determined by dataset quality rather than model scale, explaining why scaling from 3B to 7B yields minimal gains on these two metrics. In the detailed win/tie/lose comparison (Table 6), Ours-7B beats ScaMo-3B in 5 of 7 categories (Combat, Communication, Daily Life, Sports, Work) but ties in Art/Dance and Non-human behavior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends ScaMo's FSQ-based autoregressive motion generation framework, which first demonstrated a scaling law for text-to-motion models. The key departures are threefold: (1) scaling training data from ~250 hours (MotionUnion) to over 2,000 hours (MotionMillion) via a novel web-video annotation pipeline using GVHMR, GPT-4o, and LLaMA 3.1-8B; (2) introducing wavelet pre/post-processing around FSQ to suppress reconstruction jitter that emerges at larger data scales; and (3) scaling the LLAMA-style transformer from 3B to 7B parameters with a hybrid bidirectional-causal attention design. The paper also departs from prior CLIP-aligned approaches (MotionCLIP, AvatarCLIP) by arguing that modality mismatch in image embeddings fundamentally limits compositional reasoning, and from diffusion-based methods (MDM, MotionDiffuse) by adopting a discrete autoregressive paradigm for scalability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MotionMillion (2M+ sequences, 2000+ hours) is an order of magnitude larger than any prior text-motion dataset and is likely to become the default training resource for the field, while MotionMillion-Eval (126 prompts, 7 categories, human-verified) fills a critical gap in evaluating zero-shot generalization that standard in-domain metrics (FID, R-precision) cannot capture. A notable open question is whether the zero-shot gains observed on 126 prompts truly generalize to arbitrary novel compositions, and whether the web-video-derived motion quality (despite low jerk) is sufficient for downstream physics-based or interactive applications. The finding that physical plausibility and smoothness plateau with model scale—being data-quality-limited rather than capacity-limited—suggests that further scaling alone may not close the gap to mocap-level fidelity without additional data curation or physics-aware objectives.
