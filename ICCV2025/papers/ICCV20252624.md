## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models rely on CLIP text encoders with causal attention masks, which cause 'relation leakage' where attributes or relations from one entity incorrectly transfer to another in complex multi-object scenes (e.g., 'playing guitar' leaking from a man to a woman). This sequential attention structure fails to preserve distinct subject-relation-object bindings, and directly replacing the causal mask at inference causes out-of-distribution failures. The paper addresses this by introducing a lightweight adapter that rectifies text embeddings using structured scene graph information without retraining the base model.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt and automatically extracts a scene graph (a set of subject-relation-object triplets) via an NLP parser or GPT-4, along with a token-to-triplet mapping matrix that links each text token to its corresponding triplet. The adapter is plugged into Stable Diffusion's text conditioning pipeline, and the output is a standard 512×512 RGB image. The scene graph is derived entirely from the input text at inference time, requiring no separate user-provided structural annotations.

## Method

The SG-Adapter is a single cross-attention transformer module inserted after the CLIP text encoder. It constructs composite triplet embeddings by concatenating and projecting the CLIP embeddings of each triplet's subject, relation, and object, then uses these as keys and values while the original CLIP token embeddings serve as queries. A novel Token-Triplet attention mask (SG Mask) restricts each token to attend only to its own triplet embedding, preventing cross-triplet information leakage that the causal mask permits. Training uses the standard diffusion noise-prediction loss with only the adapter parameters updated, preserving the pre-trained Stable Diffusion weights. The approach is validated on a newly curated 309-image multi-relational dataset (MultiRels) and evaluated with three GPT-4V-derived metrics (SG-IoU, Entity-IoU, Relation-IoU) that measure relation correspondence beyond what FID or CLIP-Score can capture.

## Results

On the MultiRels test set, SG-Adapter achieves SG-IoU of 0.623, Entity-IoU of 0.812, and Relation-IoU of 0.753, substantially outperforming all baselines (SG-IoU range: 0.141–0.198). In human evaluation, SG-Adapter reaches 77.6% Relation-Accuracy and 77.1% Entity-Accuracy, compared to only 5.38–5.96% for all baselines. Image quality is well preserved with FID 26.2, close to the Stable Diffusion baseline (25.0) and far better than fine-tuning CLIP (58.2). In the SG-to-Image comparison on Flickr30k, SG-Adapter achieves FID 25.1 and Inception Score 57.8, surpassing the best prior method R3CD (FID 32.9, IS 19.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SG-Adapter dominates all four text-to-image baselines on every metric. Against Stable Diffusion (the base model), SG-Adapter improves SG-IoU from 0.157 to 0.623 (4×) and Relation-Accuracy from 5.38% to 77.6%, while keeping FID nearly identical (26.2 vs. 25.0). Finetuning CLIP directly yields the worst FID (58.2) and lowest Entity-IoU (0.499), confirming that small-scale fine-tuning degrades generation quality without fixing relation correspondence. GLIGEN Adapter and LoRA Adapter both integrate scene graph or low-rank information but without the proposed SG Mask; they achieve SG-IoU of only 0.141 and 0.145 respectively, showing that merely adding scene graph tokens or low-rank updates is insufficient—structured token-to-triplet attention is critical. In the SG-to-Image comparison (Table 3), SG-Adapter outperforms all six prior methods (SG2IM, PasteGAN, SGDiff, SceneGenie, R3CD, ISGC) on FID (25.1 vs. 32.9–99.1), Inception Score (57.8 vs. 8.2–30.2), and SG-IoU (0.413 vs. 0.085–0.122), demonstrating that the adapter strategy transfers effectively to the dedicated SG-to-Image setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Stable Diffusion and its CLIP text encoder, identifying the causal attention mask in CLIP as the root cause of 'relation leakage' in multi-entity scenes. It departs from prior adapter-based control methods (ControlNet, GLIGEN) by targeting the text embedding space rather than the U-Net, and from scene graph-to-image pipelines (SG2IM, SGDiff, SceneGenie) by leveraging the generative quality of pre-trained T2I models rather than training from scratch on small SG-image pairs. The work also explicitly distinguishes relation generation from layout-to-image generation, arguing that spatial placement alone cannot capture complex relational semantics like 'back to back' versus 'hug.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SG-Adapter offers a lightweight, plug-and-play solution to a persistent and visually obvious failure mode in text-to-image generation, and the GPT-4V-derived metrics (SG-IoU, Relation-IoU, Entity-IoU) fill a gap in evaluation where FID and CLIP-Score are blind to relational correspondence. However, the MultiRels dataset is very small (309 images, 20 test scenarios), limiting confidence in generalization beyond the tested relation types, and the face-anonymization artifacts noted by the authors may affect real-world deployment. Open questions include whether the triplet-token attention mask generalizes to longer, more complex scene graphs and whether the approach transfers to non-CLIP text encoders or video generation.
