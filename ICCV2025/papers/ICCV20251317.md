## Problem

Existing object reassembly methods (both part and fractured assembly) lack a data-driven, category-agnostic complete shape prior when working with fragmentary inputs, instead relying on category-specific templates or assuming most fragments are available. This paper addresses the gap by learning a generative model that can imagine the complete object shape from a partially assembled point cloud, providing an orthogonal layer of information that improves any existing assembly algorithm.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251317/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a partially assembled point cloud with an arbitrary number of points, produced by an off-the-shelf assembly algorithm (e.g., Jigsaw, SE(3), DGL), with no quantification of which pieces are correctly positioned or how accurate the assembly is. It outputs a complete object point cloud that captures the outer shape of the underlying object and may contain geometries absent from the input. The bidirectional coordinate-to-color mapping between point clouds and RGB images handles variable point densities and bridges the domain gap to image-based 3D reconstruction models.

## Method

Jigsaw++ operates in two stages. First, it learns a rectified flow generative model (U-ViT backbone) that jointly generates a global embedding and a reconstruction latent, where encoding is performed via LEAP's DINOv2-based image-to-3D pipeline after mapping point clouds to RGB images through a bidirectional coordinate-to-color scheme that leverages large-scale 2D pretraining. Second, the 'retargeting' phase performs reverse ODE sampling from the partially assembled input to obtain a latent, applies Langevin dynamics (x₀ = αx̂₀ + √(1−α²)ξ) to move it toward higher likelihood under N(0,I), and then fine-tunes the velocity field on (x₀, x₁) pairs where x₁ is the ground truth complete object. The rectified flow formulation is critical because its straight trajectories reduce reverse sampling to 1/25 of the original steps, dramatically lowering the fine-tuning cost while preserving faithful latent representations. The approach is orthogonal to existing assembly methods and can be plugged in after any assembly algorithm to provide a complete shape prior.

## Results

On the Breaking Bad fracture assembly dataset, Jigsaw++ reduces Chamfer Distance from 10.5 to 4.5 (×10⁻³) when applied on top of Jigsaw, while improving Precision from 45.6% to 48.7% and Recall from 42.7% to 49.5%. On PartNet part assembly, Jigsaw++ applied to DGL reduces CD by 6.8–22.5 (×10⁻³) across Chair, Table, and Lamp categories, with Precision gains of +23.7 to +37.0 percentage points. The method also demonstrates robustness to 20% missing pieces, maintaining CD of 2.0×10⁻³ and ~59.4% precision/recall on the Bottle category.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Jigsaw++ is designed to be orthogonal to existing assembly methods, so it is evaluated as an add-on layer on top of three baselines. On the Breaking Bad fracture dataset, Jigsaw++ applied to SE(3) [60] yields the largest absolute gains: CD drops by 8.1×10⁻³, Precision jumps by +17.6 pp, and Recall by +14.1 pp, reflecting that SE(3) produces very poor initial assemblies. When applied to the stronger Jigsaw [33] baseline, improvements are more modest but still consistent: CD decreases from 10.5 to 4.5 (×10⁻³), Precision improves by +3.1 pp, and Recall by +6.8 pp. On PartNet part assembly, Jigsaw++ applied to DGL [67] produces the most dramatic relative improvements, with Precision increasing by +30.5 pp (Chair), +37.0 pp (Table), and +23.7 pp (Lamp), and CD reductions of 6.8, 11.0, and 22.5 (×10⁻³) respectively. In all three comparisons, Jigsaw++ wins on every reported metric (CD, Precision, Recall) without any degradation, confirming its role as a complementary shape-prior module rather than a competing assembly algorithm.

## Relation to Prior Work

Jigsaw++ positions itself as orthogonal to existing reassembly algorithms (Jigsaw, SE(3), DGL), adding a learned complete-shape prior layer rather than replacing the assembly step itself. It departs from template-based complete-shape methods (TAssemble, skull assembly, fragment reassembly) by learning a generative model of complete objects via rectified flow, avoiding category-specific templates. The key technical repurposing is using LEAP's image-to-3D pipeline (built on DINOv2) as a 3D shape encoder, bridged to point clouds through a novel coordinate-to-color mapping, thereby inheriting the generalization of large-scale 2D pretraining. The 'retargeting' fine-tuning phase, which adapts the generative model to handle biased and incomplete assembly inputs, is the paper's core methodological departure from standard inversion-then-generate pipelines used in image editing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The orthogonal design means Jigsaw++ can immediately improve any existing assembly pipeline without retraining it, and the demonstrated 50% error reduction in Jigsaw's global alignment when given the generated shape prior (even with 20% noise) suggests a practical path forward. However, the paper explicitly acknowledges it has not yet devised a principled way for reassembly algorithms to fully exploit the generated shape prior, leaving a significant open question. Limitations in size (color-mapping constraints), topological fidelity (e.g., mug handles), and generalization to unseen categories constrain near-term applicability, and the authors note that larger datasets and models are needed. The retargeting-with-rectified-flow strategy, which reduces reverse sampling to 1/25 of standard diffusion steps, may have broader utility for any generative task requiring adaptation to noisy or incomplete conditioning.
