## Problem

Iterative PnP solvers such as LHM rely on weak-perspective initialization (e.g., Horn's least-squares quaternion), which assumes nearly uniform depth and degrades significantly when 3D scene points are well distributed. This paper addresses the gap by providing a closed-form orthographic-projection initialization that yields a substantially more accurate SO(3) starting point, enabling faster convergence and higher accuracy in the subsequent iterative refinement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251162/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes N ≥ 5 calibrated 2D-3D point correspondences and a known intrinsic matrix K; translation is eliminated in the initialization stage via data centering (subtracting centers of mass). It outputs a full SE(3) pose — a 3×3 rotation matrix (recovered from the adjugate quaternion via Bar-Itzhack correction) and a 3D translation vector. A quasi-planarity check (eigenvalue ratio of the self-covariance matrix below ~2×10⁻² or orthogonality residual > 10⁻²) triggers a fallback to the standard LSQ initialization, making the pipeline adaptive to degenerate configurations.

## Method

DRaM-LHM is a two-stage pipeline. In the initialization stage, rotation is parameterized by the adjugate quaternion matrix (all quadratic terms qᵢqⱼ), which reduces the orthographic reprojection objective from quartic to quadratic degree. The resulting least-squares problem admits a closed-form solution expressible as ratios of 3×3 subdeterminants of a 5×5 cross-covariance matrix (the Determinant Ratio Matrix), requiring only O(n) covariance computation. The recovered rotation is then projected back to SO(3) via the Bar-Itzhack quaternion extraction. In the refinement stage, the LHM Orthogonal Iteration alternates between a closed-form translation solve (given fixed rotation) and an SVD-based rotation update on centered point clouds, minimizing the object-space collinearity error until convergence — typically within 10 iterations.

## Results

On real-world PnP benchmarks (TUM RGB-D fr1/teddy, fr2/360, and EuRoC MAV MH01), DRaM-LHM achieves median rotation errors of 0.0031, 0.0024, and 0.0194 rad, and translation errors of 0.0045, 0.0091, and 0.0361, respectively—matching or slightly exceeding the state-of-the-art SQPnP (0.0030, 0.0023, 0.0206 rad rotation). In terms of AUC, DRaM reaches 0.9353, 0.8826, and 0.5708 on the three sequences, closely trailing SQPnP (0.9558, 0.9069, 0.5755) while clearly surpassing all other baselines. In synthetic experiments with 8 points and σ=5 noise, DRaM-LHM converges in fewer than 10 orthogonal iteration steps to accuracy comparable to SQPnP, while running faster than Gröbner-basis and null-space methods for N ≤ 9.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRaM-LHM matches or slightly beats SQPnP (the current SOTA) on median rotation error for the challenging MH01 sequence (0.0194 vs. 0.0206 rad) and ties on translation error (0.0361), while being marginally behind on the easier fr1/teddy and fr2/360 sequences (by ~0.0001 rad). Against EPnP, DLS, and UPnP, DRaM consistently achieves lower rotation and translation errors across all three sequences—for example, on MH01, DRaM's 0.0194 rad rotation error is ~0.002 rad better than EPnP (0.0218) and DLS (0.0212). Compared to REPPnP, DRaM is substantially more accurate (e.g., 0.0024 vs. 0.0037 rad on fr2/360), consistent with the authors' note that REPPnP is unreliable for N < 10. Against the original LHM pipeline (using Horn's LSQ initialization), DRaM-LHM improves rotation error by 0.0006–0.0015 rad and translation error by 0.0015–0.0065 across sequences, demonstrating the benefit of the DRaM orthographic initialization over weak-perspective LSQ. On AUC, DRaM (0.9353/0.8826/0.5708) trails SQPnP (0.9558/0.9069/0.5755) by 1–2% but leads all other methods, including LHM which drops sharply on fr2/360 (0.6572 AUC).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two previously separate threads in PnP estimation: Hanson's DRaM closed-form solution for orthographic pose via the quaternion adjugate matrix [8], and the Lu-Hager-Mjolsness (LHM) iterative pipeline that refines pose by minimizing object-space collinearity error [15]. The key departure from LHM is replacing its weak-perspective Horn's least-squares quaternion initialization with DRaM's exact orthographic solution, augmented by Bar-Itzhack's quaternion-from-matrix correction [2] to enforce SO(3) constraints. Conceptually, the paper repositions DRaM from a standalone orthographic solver into a robust initialization stage for a general perspective PnP problem, bridging the gap between algebraic closed-form methods and iterative refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in providing a more noise-robust initialization for iterative PnP solvers, which is critical in SLAM and relocalization pipelines where pose estimation runs at every keyframe. The method's known limitation in quasi-planar configurations (requiring a fallback to LSQ initialization based on eigenvalue-ratio and orthogonality checks) is honestly characterized, but the authors note this triggers only in extreme cases. Open questions include handling truly degenerate planar targets (analogous to Schweighofer's pose ambiguity resolution) and exploring DRaM's applicability in near-orthographic domains such as cryo-electron microscopy and aerial imagery, where the orthographic assumption is naturally closer to valid.
