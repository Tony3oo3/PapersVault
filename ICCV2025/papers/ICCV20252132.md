## Problem

Patch-based adversarial attacks insert localized perturbations into images that cause DNNs to misclassify, misdetect, or misestimate, yet standard defenses (JPEG compression, quantization, feature distillation) degrade clean accuracy because they uniformly affect the entire image. The core gap is the lack of a defense that can simultaneously localize the adversarial patch within the image and neutralize it without disturbing the surrounding clean regions.

## Input / Output

ODDR takes a standard RGB image (potentially containing an adversarial patch) and outputs a defended image of the same dimensions where the detected patch region has been replaced by its truncated-SVD projection. The image is consumed as a collection of overlapping 20×20 pixel fragments (stride 10), and the output preserves all non-patch pixels exactly while only the square mask (size d, default 50 px) surrounding the outlier cluster is modified. The method is model-agnostic and operates as a preprocessing step before any downstream DNN (CNN or Transformer).

## Method

ODDR is a three-stage, model-agnostic pipeline: (1) Fragmentation splits the image into overlapping square fragments via a moving window; (2) Segregation applies an Isolation Forest (500 trees, 30% subsample) to score each flattened fragment as an anomaly, then selects the top (1−c)·n fragments as outliers and groups them into spatial clusters of at least minPts fragments; (3) Neutralization places a square mask centered on the highest-scoring fragment in each cluster and applies Truncated SVD, retaining a tunable fraction (inf, 70–85%) of singular values to project the mask into a lower-dimensional space. The key insight is that adversarial patches—whether synthetic or naturalistic—exhibit a statistical distribution distinct from the surrounding image content, making them detectable as outliers, while SVD-based dimension reduction strips the adversarial signal while preserving enough structural information for the downstream task. This combination yields up to 13% improvement over dimension reduction alone and outperforms LGS, Jujutsu, and Jedi across classification, detection, and depth estimation benchmarks.

## Results

On ImageNet with ResNet-152, ODDR achieves 79.1% robust accuracy against GoogleAP (vs. 39.3% without defense) and 74.1% against LAVAN, while preserving 81.2% clean accuracy. On ViT-B/16, robust accuracy reaches 83.1% against GoogleAP. For object detection, ODDR achieves 93.54% robust average precision on CASIA and 83.21% on INRIA against AdvYOLO. For monocular depth estimation on KITTI, ODDR reduces mean depth estimation error from 0.59 to 0.11 and affected region ratio from 99% to 21% against SSAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ODDR outperforms all six compared defenses across every task and dataset. Against the strongest empirical baselines on ImageNet (GAP/ResNet-152), ODDR achieves 79.1% robust accuracy versus LGS (53.9%), Jedi (64.4%), DefensiveDR (66.2%), and Jujutsu (60%), a margin of 13–25 percentage points. The gap narrows somewhat on Swin-v2-B where LGS reaches 65.2% and Jedi 70.6%, but ODDR still leads by 11–17 points. For object detection against AdvYOLO, ODDR achieves 93.54% robust AP on CASIA versus LGS (84%) and Jedi (80%), and 83.21% on INRIA versus LGS (21%) and Jedi (28.03%). Against certified defenses, ODDR's empirical approach yields substantially higher accuracy: 79.1% vs. De-randomized Smoothing (35.02%) and PatchGuard (30.96%), though the certified methods provide provable robustness guarantees that ODDR does not. Against Naturalistic Patches on CASIA, ODDR reaches 87.5% robust AP and 90.27% recovery rate, surpassing Jedi (67%/76%) and LGS (50%/48%).

## Relation to Prior Work

ODDR combines two previously independent ideas into a unified defense pipeline: the Isolation Forest algorithm (Liu et al., 2008) repurposed for localizing adversarial patches as statistical outliers in fragmented feature space, and Truncated SVD-based dimension reduction (building directly on the authors' own DefensiveDR, 2023) for neutralizing the detected region. This departs from the dominant gradient- or entropy-based localization strategies used by LGS, Jujutsu, and Jedi, and from certified defenses (De-randomized Smoothing, PatchGuard) that trade accuracy for provable guarantees. The key conceptual move is framing patch detection as an unsupervised anomaly-detection problem rather than a differentiable optimization problem, which also confers inherent resistance to gradient-based adaptive attacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that ODDR achieves a large accuracy gap over prior empirical defenses (79.1% vs. 64.34% for Jedi on ImageNet/GAP) while remaining model-agnostic, non-differentiable, and cheap enough for CPU-only edge deployment (~0.034 s/sample). However, the adaptive-attack analysis reveals that a distribution-mapping attack can still degrade clean accuracy from 78% to 73%, and the lower-dimensional-patch finding (1% SVD loss suffices to kill the patch) cuts both ways: it validates the defense but also suggests the attack surface is narrower than it appears. Open questions include robustness to multi-patch or video-stream attacks, the sensitivity of the many hyperparameters (c, inf, k, str, minPts, d) to unseen domains, and whether the outlier-detection assumption (patches are distributional anomalies) holds for increasingly naturalistic or semantically coherent patches.
