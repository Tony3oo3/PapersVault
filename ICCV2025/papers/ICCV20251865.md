## Problem

Generating photorealistic 360° indoor panoramas from 2D top-down views or floorplans remains unexplored: text-to-panorama methods ignore geometric constraints, layout-guided 3D scene generation produces meshes of insufficient quality for high-fidelity rendering, and cross-view panorama synthesis has focused exclusively on outdoor satellite-to-ground settings. The core difficulty is inferring occluded 3D structure and fine texture from a single 2D projection that lacks explicit depth, semantics, or vertical information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251865/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 2D top-down RGB image (rendered orthographically to resemble a floorplan), a specified camera position, and an optional text prompt for stylization. The top-down view is first segmented by SAM to extract room boundaries and furniture shapes, which are fed alongside the original image into the occupancy estimator. The output is a single equirectangular 360° panorama; the model also generalizes to schematic and hand-drawn floorplans, and supports LoRA-based image-guided stylization with fewer than five reference images per style.

## Method

Top2Pano is a three-stage pipeline. First, an OccRecon module—a 2D diffusion-based encoder-decoder (avoiding costly 3D diffusion)—estimates a 3D volumetric occupancy map from the top-down view and its SAM segmentation, with structural reinforcement that solidifies wall voxels and constrains floor geometry. Second, volumetric rendering along equirectangular camera rays produces a coarse depth panorama, while direct ray-projection of the top-down image yields a coarse color panorama (with halved ray length for color to reduce banding artifacts). Third, a PanoGen module built on ControlNet-conditioned Stable Diffusion refines both coarse inputs into a photorealistic panorama, treating color and depth as separate conditioning branches. Training uses a combined loss of denoising MSE, depth alignment MSE, and per-channel histogram L1 color loss, with the full 3.3B-parameter model trained end-to-end on Matterport3D and Gibson.

## Results

On Matterport3D, Top2Pano achieves PSNR 11.72, SSIM 0.4409, FID 30.84, and LPIPS 0.6029, outperforming all baselines on every metric. On Gibson, it reaches PSNR 11.58, SSIM 0.4851, FID 28.68, and LPIPS 0.6282. The most striking gain is in FID, where Top2Pano scores roughly 2.5–3× lower than the best baseline (30.84 vs. 85.74 on Matterport3D; 28.68 vs. 79.53 on Gibson), indicating substantially better perceptual realism. PSNR and SSIM improvements over the strongest baseline (PanFusion) are more modest, at approximately 0.2–0.3 dB and 0.01–0.02 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Top2Pano is compared against two families of baselines: (1) Sat2Density adapted with either LDM or ControlNet for indoor panorama synthesis, and (2) PanFusion, a text-to-panorama framework with layout-conditioned ControlNet. Top2Pano wins on all four metrics (PSNR, SSIM, FID, LPIPS) against every baseline on both Matterport3D and Gibson. The largest margin is in FID: on Matterport3D, Top2Pano's 30.84 is roughly 55 points lower than PanFusion's 85.74 and nearly 70 points lower than Sat2Density+LDM's 100.06; on Gibson, the gap is similarly dramatic (28.68 vs. 79.53 for PanFusion). Against the Sat2Density variants, Top2Pano gains about 0.3 dB PSNR and 0.02 SSIM on Matterport3D, and about 0.6 dB PSNR and 0.03 SSIM on Gibson. Against PanFusion specifically, the PSNR advantage is smaller (0.27 dB on Matterport3D, 0.22 dB on Gibson), but the FID advantage remains dominant, suggesting that the volumetric-occupancy-guided coarse rendering provides a stronger geometric prior than PanFusion's layout conditioning alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

Top2Pano sits at the intersection of two research threads: cross-view panorama synthesis (notably satellite-to-ground methods like Sat2Density, whose ray-projection technique is adapted here for indoor color rendering) and diffusion-based conditional generation (ControlNet, whose conditioning mechanism is modified to accept separate coarse color and depth inputs). The paper departs from layout-guided 3D scene generation (Plan2Scene, SceneCraft, Ctrl-Room) by requiring only a top-down image rather than explicit semantic layouts, and from text-to-panorama methods (PanFusion, DiffPano) by grounding generation in geometric structure rather than language alone. SAM is leveraged as a semantic-free segmentation front-end to bridge the gap between the lack of object labels in top-down views and the 3D occupancy estimation that follows.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to generate indoor 360° panoramas from top-down views or floorplans, opening a direct pipeline for real-estate virtual tours, interior design exploration, and robotic spatial understanding from lightweight 2D inputs. The demonstrated generalization to hand-drawn floorplans and the LoRA-based stylization capability make it practically useful beyond benchmark evaluation. Key open questions include handling full vertical FoV (current training data limits ceiling/upper-wall fidelity), resolving inherent 2D-to-3D ambiguity for thin or vertically-oriented objects, and whether the volumetric occupancy stage can be replaced by lighter alternatives without sacrificing geometric consistency.
