## Problem

CLIP is pre-trained for image-level classification, so its patch embeddings lack the spatial coherence required for pixel-level segmentation. Existing training-free methods (MaskCLIP, SCLIP, ClearCLIP, ProxyCLIP) only modify the self-attention map at the last layer, leaving spatial information in early layers and fine-grained local details from other models largely untapped.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252203/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image resized to a shorter side of 336 pixels (aspect ratio preserved) and a set of text category prompts in the style of SCLIP/ProxyCLIP. It produces a pixel-level segmentation map at the input resolution. The pipeline requires both a CLIP ViT encoder (ViT-B/16 or ViT-L/14) and a frozen Stable Diffusion V2.1 model; an optional sliding-window strategy can be applied for higher-resolution inference, and the entire pipeline is training-free.

## Method

CLIPer hierarchically improves CLIP's spatial representation via two modules. The Early-Layer Fusion (ELF) module averages the self-attention maps across all transformer blocks (layers 1 through N−1) and substitutes this averaged map for the last layer's self-attention, while also feeding early-layer patch embeddings into the final attention computation; the last block omits its FFN and residual connection (following ClearCLIP) to better align with text embeddings. The resulting patch embeddings are matched against CLIP text-encoder embeddings via cosine similarity to yield a coarse segmentation map. The Fine-Grained Compensation (FGC) module then feeds the image with an empty prompt into Stable Diffusion, extracts multi-head self-attention maps at the highest spatial resolution (timestep 45 of 50), fuses all heads by matrix chain multiplication, and multiplies the fused attention map with the upscaled coarse map to refine local contours. The key insight is that early-layer embeddings share a similar embedding space with the last layer (verified via t-SNE and cosine similarity), enabling training-free fusion, while diffusion-model attention maps capture local detail that CLIP's broader semantic attention misses.

## Results

With ViT-L/14 and sliding-window inference, CLIPer achieves an average mIoU of 48.6% across eight datasets, with headline scores of 72.2% on PASCAL VOC, 44.7% on COCO Object, and 25.0% on ADE20K. Without sliding-window inference, the method still reaches 47.3% average mIoU (69.8% on VOC, 43.3% on Object). With the smaller ViT-B/16 backbone, CLIPer achieves 45.3% average mIoU with sliding-window inference. In inference time, the lightweight variant CLIPer* (without fine-grained compensation) runs in 47 ms per image with ViT-L, faster than ProxyCLIP (105 ms) and ClearCLIP (68 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLIPer outperforms all training-free baselines on average mIoU across all eight datasets. With ViT-L/14 and sliding-window inference, it surpasses ProxyCLIP (the strongest prior training-free method) by 5.7% in average mIoU (48.6% vs. 42.9%), with particularly large gains on VOC (+11.6%) and Object (+5.5%). The advantage over SCLIP and ClearCLIP is even more pronounced, with improvements of 18.3% and 12.9% in average mIoU respectively, indicating that modifying only the last-layer self-attention is substantially less effective than the hierarchical early-layer fusion strategy. Against the training-based CLIP-DINOiser (ViT-B/16), CLIPer achieves a 4.1% average mIoU improvement (44.4% vs. 40.3%) without any training. On the weakly-supervised pseudo-mask generation task (Table 3), CLIPer also beats the best training-free competitor iSeg by 1.7% on VOC (76.9% vs. 75.2%) and 1.8% on COCO (47.3% vs. 45.5%), and outperforms all training-based weakly-supervised methods. The only method with partially overlapping results is CaR, which reports 67.6% on VOC (no sliding-window) but does not report scores on all datasets, making a full comparison incomplete.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of training-free CLIP adaptation for open-vocabulary segmentation (MaskCLIP, SCLIP, ClearCLIP, ProxyCLIP), which all focus on modifying the self-attention map at the last transformer layer. CLIPer departs from this single-level strategy by operating hierarchically: it fuses embeddings and attention maps from early CLIP layers (motivated by the observation that early layers share embedding space with the last layer) to improve spatial coherence, and then applies Stable Diffusion's multi-head self-attention maps as a post-processing step to refine local details. The ClearCLIP design of omitting FFN and residual connections in the last block is adopted, and the diffusion model is repurposed not for image generation but as a spatial detail extractor via null-prompt attention maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 11.6% mIoU gain over ProxyCLIP on VOC (72.2% vs. 60.6%) with a fully training-free pipeline is practically significant, as it removes the need for any fine-tuning while approaching or exceeding weakly-supervised methods. The hierarchical decomposition (coarse spatial coherence from early layers + fine local detail from diffusion) offers a clear modular recipe that other CLIP-based dense prediction methods could adopt. However, the authors acknowledge that tiny objects remain poorly segmented, and the reliance on a frozen Stable Diffusion model (extracting attention at a fixed timestep) introduces a non-trivial computational overhead (~158 ms vs. 14 ms without FGC). An open question is whether better image-text pre-training strategies for the diffusion model, or high-resolution input adaptation, could close the remaining gap on fine-grained structures.
