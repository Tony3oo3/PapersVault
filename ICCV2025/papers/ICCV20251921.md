## Problem

Existing Video-to-Audio (V2A) and Audio-to-Video (A2V) methods rely on separate pretrained feature extractors (CLIP, CLAP, ImageBind, CAVP) that capture semantic content but lack precise temporal alignment, and require distinct models for each generation direction. This prevents a unified framework capable of handling both cross-modal tasks alongside text-to-audio and text-to-video generation, and limits the quality of audio-video synchronization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251921/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For V2A, the method takes a 5.16-second video clip (base 64×36 at 6 FPS, upscaled to 512×288 at 24 FPS via a MAGVIT-v2 latent upsampler) and an optional text prompt, producing a 16 kHz audio waveform via a HiFi-GAN vocoder. For A2V, it takes a variable-length audio clip (encoded as a 1D Mel-spectrogram latent sequence) and an optional text prompt, producing an RGB video. The conditioning modality is partially noised at a fixed flow timestep (t=0.96 for V2A, t=0.8 for A2V) before being fed through the frozen generator to extract conditioning features.

## Method

AV-Link connects two frozen 576M-parameter Flow Matching DiT generators (one for audio, one for video) through 8 lightweight Fusion Blocks (186M total new parameters) that perform multi-head self-attention over concatenated audio and video token sequences. Temporal alignment is achieved via a temporally-aligned 1D RoPE that rotates corresponding audio and video tokens by the same angle proportional to their real-time position, using modality-specific token-per-second rates. A symmetric feature reinjection mechanism passes the cross-modal-aware activations back into subsequent DiT blocks of both frozen backbones, enabling bidirectional information flow that dynamically refines conditioning features throughout the generation process. Time-aware conditioning is injected via adaLN-Zero layers that receive both the audio and video flow timesteps, and the entire Fusion Block is trained with the rectified flow objective on paired audio-video data while keeping both generators frozen.

## Results

On the V2A task (VGGSounds, with text prompt), AV-Link achieves FAD 1.33, FD 11.99, IS 12.40, and Onset ACC 0.540, outperforming all baselines on every metric except IB-AI/IB-AV (where S&H is higher by design). The headline temporal alignment metric (Onset ACC) improves by up to 76.4% over the best baseline (Frieren at 0.301 without prompt; FoleyCrafter at 0.307 with prompt). On the A2V task, AV-Link achieves FID 37.62 and FVD 158.71 (with prompt), substantially outperforming TempoTokens (FID 77.87, FVD 1275.50). User studies show AV-Link is preferred over Movie Gen Audio (13B params) 63.6% of the time for temporal alignment, and over TempoTokens >73.9% across all A2V criteria.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AV-Link outperforms all V2A baselines on FAD, FD, IS, and Onset ACC. Against the strongest temporal-alignment baselines (Frieren at 0.301 and FoleyCrafter at 0.307 Onset ACC without/with prompt), AV-Link achieves 0.531/0.540, a 76% relative improvement. S&H reports higher IB-AI/IB-AV scores (0.300/0.318) but this is expected since it explicitly optimizes ImageBind similarity; its Onset ACC (0.080) and FAD (10.72) are far worse. In user studies, AV-Link is preferred over Movie Gen Audio (13B params) 63.6% of the time for temporal alignment, despite Movie Gen's superior raw audio quality. For A2V, AV-Link dominates TempoTokens on all metrics: FID 37.62 vs 77.87, FVD 158.71 vs 1275.50, and IB-AV 0.201 vs 0.173 (with prompt), with user preference exceeding 73.9% across all criteria. The method also outperforms its own VGGSounds-only variant, confirming that training on the larger AudioSet + internal dataset provides consistent gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Flow Matching generative framework and DiT transformer architecture, using a frozen audio DiT (from the authors' prior audio generation work) and a frozen video DiT as base generators. It departs fundamentally from prior V2A and A2V methods (Diff-Foley, FoleyCrafter, Movie Gen, TempoTokens, Seeing-and-Hearing) that condition cross-modal generation on external pretrained feature extractors (CLIP, MetaCLIP, CAVP, ImageBind, BEATs). Instead, AV-Link directly leverages the internal activations of the frozen flow models themselves as conditioning signals, connected through a novel Fusion Block with temporally-aligned RoPE and symmetric feature reinjection, unifying both V2A and A2V in a single 186M-parameter module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework eliminates the need for separate V2A and A2V models and their associated external feature extractors, simplifying deployment for applications requiring comprehensive T2V, T2A, V2A, and A2V capabilities. The substantial temporal alignment improvement (76.4% Onset ACC gain) addresses what the authors identify as the core unsolved challenge in cross-modal generation. Limitations include the relatively small base models (576M parameters), low base video resolution (64×36 at 6 FPS, upscaled to 512×288 at 24 FPS), and the finding that joint audio-video generation underperforms factorized approaches, suggesting the factorization strategy may constrain scalability. Open questions include whether the activation-based conditioning approach transfers to much larger generators (e.g., 13B+ models) and whether the symmetric reinjection mechanism can be adapted for longer-form or higher-fidelity generation.
