## Problem

Co-speech gesture understanding in the wild remains largely unexplored because gesture-speech associations are sparse, ambiguous, and highly variable across speakers, with no reliable word-level annotations available. Prior approaches either target specific gesture classes in controlled settings, learn only low-level audio-visual synchronization (e.g., GestSync), or focus on sign language where the mapping to text is fundamentally different. This paper asks whether a single tri-modal representation can capture both the global semantics and fine-grained word-level correspondences of gestures from weakly-supervised in-the-wild video.

## Input / Output

The model consumes gesture video clips (270×480, 2–10 s at 25 FPS, face region masked), 16 kHz speech waveforms converted to mel-spectrograms, and word-aligned text transcripts tokenized into wordpieces. During training, one of the speech or text modalities is randomly zeroed 50% of the time (modality dropout), enabling any subset of modalities at inference. Outputs are frame-level gesture embeddings (T×d), word-level speech-text embeddings (W×d), and their average-pooled phrase-level counterparts, which support retrieval ranking, per-frame word-localization heatmaps (threshold δ=0.5), and active-speaker argmax over P candidate clips.

## Method

JEGAL encodes the three modalities with a frozen 3D-CNN+Transformer gesture backbone (initialized from GestSync), a frozen XLM-RoBERTa text encoder, and a 2D-CNN audio encoder, then fuses word-level speech and text features by concatenation. Training uses two complementary contrastive objectives: a global phrase-level InfoNCE loss over average-pooled embeddings, and a novel local gesture-word coupling loss that scores each phrase pair by the mean over words of the maximum cosine similarity to any gesture frame within a padded temporal window, encouraging matching pairs to exhibit more strong word-level couplings than non-matching pairs. An attention-based alignment module pools gesture frames over a speech-word window expanded by 10 frames on each side, allowing the model to locate gestures that extend beyond the spoken word boundary. The final loss is a weighted sum (β·L_seq + (1−β)·L_couple), and ablations confirm each loss is necessary for its respective task granularity.

## Results

On the AVS-Ret cross-modal retrieval benchmark, JEGAL with fused speech+text achieves R@5=18.80 and Median Rank=31.0 for speech-text→gesture retrieval, and R@5=18.20 and MR=24.5 for gesture→speech-text retrieval, substantially outperforming the best fine-tuned baseline (GestSync: R@5=10.00, MR=70.5). On the AVS-Spot word spotting benchmark, JEGAL (T+A) reaches 63.60% accuracy versus 21.04% for the best baseline (fine-tuned GestSync). On active speaker detection (AVS-Asd), JEGAL (T+A) achieves 76.8/57.8/48.0% accuracy for 2/4/6 speakers, slightly trailing fine-tuned GestSync (81.2/64.8/54.4%) which is specifically trained for frame-level synchronization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JEGAL decisively outperforms all four baselines on cross-modal retrieval: the best fine-tuned baseline (GestSync) achieves R@5=10.00 and MR=70.5, while JEGAL (T+A) reaches R@5=18.80 and MR=31.0, roughly doubling recall and halving median rank. On gesture word spotting, the gap is even more dramatic—JEGAL (T+A) scores 63.60% accuracy versus 21.04% for fine-tuned GestSync and 19.50% for GestureDiffuCLIP's semantic encoder, a ~3× improvement attributable to the local gesture-word coupling loss. On active speaker detection, JEGAL (T+A) at 76.8/57.8/48.0% (2/4/6 speakers) is slightly behind fine-tuned GestSync (81.2/64.8/54.4%), which is expected since GestSync is explicitly trained for frame-level audio-visual synchronization. Zero-shot vision-language models (Clip4Clip, LanguageBind) perform only marginally above chance on all three tasks, confirming that generic video-text representations do not capture gesture-specific semantics. The paper also shows that fusing speech and text consistently outperforms either modality alone across all tasks, validating the tri-modal design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GestSync (same first author), reusing its frozen 3D-CNN+Transformer gesture backbone, but departs fundamentally from GestSync's audio-visual synchronization objective by introducing semantic text supervision and a novel word-level coupling loss. It positions itself against large vision-language models (CLIP4Clip, LanguageBind) that capture non-gesture attributes like identity and scene, and against GestureDiffuCLIP which learns gesture-text embeddings but lacks word-level correspondences. The key conceptual departure is the tri-modal (video + speech + text) contrastive framework with a dual loss design—global phrase-level InfoNCE for holistic semantics and a local gesture-word coupling loss for fine-grained temporal alignment—trained entirely with weak phrase-level supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to learn gesture representations that simultaneously capture semantics, utterance style, and word-level associations, filling a gap in a relatively under-explored area of non-verbal communication. The finding that speech and text encode complementary gesture signals (prosody/stress vs. semantics) and that their fusion consistently outperforms either modality alone provides a clear design principle for future multimodal gesture work. The three manually curated benchmarks (AVS-Ret, AVS-Spot, AVS-Asd) offer the community a standardized evaluation for gesture understanding that was previously absent. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 3D keypoint inputs for efficiency, whether the weak phrase-level supervision can be replaced with richer (e.g., word-level) annotations, and how well the representations generalize across cultures and gesture styles.
