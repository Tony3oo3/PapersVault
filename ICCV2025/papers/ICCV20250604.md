## Problem

Large diffusion transformers like FLUX.1 (12B parameters) incur substantial inference overhead, and existing compression methods such as pruning reduce total parameters but inherently limit model capacity, causing severe performance degradation at high compression rates. The paper asks whether one can reduce the number of *activated* parameters per inference step while preserving the full model capacity, by dynamically routing different inputs through different parameter subsets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250604/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt and a random noise tensor as input and produces a 1024×1024 RGB image through iterative denoising (28 NFEs by default, or 8 NFEs when combined with HyperFLUX step distillation). A key operational detail is that routing decisions are input-dependent: the MoE gating network selects top-k experts per token based on the token's feature, while the MoB block router selects which transformer blocks to traverse based on a joint function of the current image features, text features, and the global AdaLN condition embedding (which encodes timestep and text).

## Method

Dense2MoE applies two complementary sparsification strategies to a dense DiT: (1) replacing each FFN with a MoE layer containing one shared expert and multiple smaller normal experts (top-k routed), reducing activated FFN parameters by 62.5% while keeping total capacity unchanged; and (2) introducing Mixture of Blocks (MoB), which groups consecutive transformer blocks and uses a feature-level router to dynamically activate only a subset of blocks per sample, reducing effective depth. A three-stage distillation pipeline trains the sparse model from the dense teacher: first, Taylor-metric-based importance scoring partitions FFN weights into shared and normal experts, followed by knowledge distillation to strengthen the shared expert; second, the full MoE is distilled with a load-balancing loss to prevent expert collapse; third, a group feature loss aligns MoB group outputs with the corresponding teacher block outputs, with isolated blocks frozen. Applied to FLUX.1 [dev], the pipeline compresses activated parameters from 12B to 5.2B (Level-L) or as low as 2.6B (Level-XS) while matching or exceeding pruning-based baselines of comparable size.

## Results

FLUX.1-MoE-L (5.15B activated params) achieves CLIP 31.39, GenEval 0.5702, and DPG 81.63, reducing activated parameters by 57% from the 11.90B FLUX.1 [dev] baseline while maintaining competitive performance. At the same ~3.2B scale, FLUX.1-MoE-S (3.19B) substantially outperforms FLUX-Mini (3.18B) across all metrics, e.g., GenEval 0.4441 vs. 0.3209 and DPG 75.61 vs. 69.34. The most aggressive variant, FLUX.1-MoE-XS (2.64B), still achieves GenEval 0.4036 and DPG 73.66, surpassing FLUX-Mini despite having fewer activated parameters. Combined with 8-NFE step distillation (HyperFLUX-MoE-XS), latency drops to 2.50s on H20 GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FLUX.1-Lite (8.16B, depth pruning), FLUX.1-MoE-L (5.15B) wins on GenEval (0.5702 vs. 0.5229) and DPG (81.63 vs. 79.00) with 3B fewer activated parameters and 20% fewer FLOPs, though it trails slightly on CLIP (31.39 vs. 31.79) and ImageReward (0.8011 vs. 0.8380). At the ~3.2B scale, FLUX.1-MoE-S dominates FLUX-Mini (3.18B) on every metric: GenEval 0.4441 vs. 0.3209, DPG 75.61 vs. 69.34, and ImageReward 0.5942 vs. 0.2151, demonstrating that dynamic MoE routing preserves far more capacity than static depth pruning. The 2.64B FLUX.1-MoE-XS also beats FLUX-Mini on all metrics despite having fewer activated parameters. In the controlled MLP-pruning comparison (Table 2), MoE at 62.5% compression (r_a=1.5) outperforms Diff-Pruning at the same ratio (GenEval 0.5728 vs. 0.4113) and even beats Diff-Pruning at a milder 50% compression. For depth pruning, MoB grouping consistently outperforms both FLUX.1-Lite and BK-SDM under identical activated-block counts (e.g., GenEval 0.5396 vs. 0.4919 vs. 0.4789 at D=9, S=38), with the gap widening dramatically at higher compression (S=26: MoB 0.4956 vs. Lite 0.0926). Against the full FLUX.1 [dev] baseline, MoE-L retains ~86% of GenEval and ~98% of DPG while activating only 43% of the parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the dense-to-MoE paradigm well established in LLMs (DeepSeek-V3, Mixtral, GShard) to diffusion transformers, departing from the dominant pruning-based compression approaches (Diff-Pruning, FLUX.1-Lite, BK-SDM) that permanently reduce model capacity. The key conceptual shift is from selecting a fixed subset of parameters to dynamically routing inputs through different parameter subsets, preserving total capacity while reducing activated parameters. The Mixture of Blocks (MoB) mechanism, which routes at the transformer-block/depth level rather than the token level, is a novel contribution not seen in prior MoE or dynamic-depth work (e.g., Mixture-of-Depths), specifically motivated by the observation that block importance in diffusion models varies across timesteps and prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes a practical pathway to deploy large diffusion models (12B FLUX.1) with 60% fewer activated parameters while matching original quality, which is directly relevant to practitioners facing memory and latency constraints. The MoB concept opens an interesting direction for input-adaptive depth in generative models, and the expert specialization analysis (spatial structure, timestep-dependent routing) provides interpretability insights into how sparse diffusion models organize their computation. Limitations include validation only on FLUX.1, a complex multi-stage distillation pipeline requiring 32 A100s, and the question of whether these gains transfer to other DiT architectures or to video generation. An open question is whether the multi-stage distillation (Taylor init → shared expert KD → full MoE KD → MoB KD) can be simplified or made more scalable.
