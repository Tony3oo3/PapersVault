## Problem

Prior continual visual instruction tuning (CVIT) work follows traditional continual learning paradigms and overlooks a dual form of catastrophic forgetting specific to MLLMs: models not only lose previously acquired visual understanding but also progressively degrade in their ability to follow instructions as new tasks are learned. This dual forgetting is neither captured nor addressed by existing CVIT benchmarks or mitigation strategies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251224/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each training and inference instance is a triple (textual instruction, RGB image, target text response) standardized into a unified text-to-text format. The visual understanding router consumes the average-pooled hidden features across the sequence dimension of the current linear layer input, while the instruction following router consumes a SentenceBERT embedding of the instruction text. SMoLoRA is applied specifically to the FFN layers of the LLM and the vision-to-language adapter, with the vision encoder kept frozen throughout.

## Method

SMoLoRA extends MoLoRA by replacing its single shared router with two separable routing modules: a visual understanding (VU) module that routes based on instance-level average features over a set of M LoRA blocks, and an instruction following (IF) module that routes based on SentenceBERT instruction embeddings over a separate set of LoRA blocks. Each module uses a top-k softmax gating over its own router matrix to select the most relevant LoRA block(s) for the current input. An adaptive fusion module then combines the two module outputs using trainable importance matrices that produce per-position weights via a softmax over concatenated importance scores, allowing the model to dynamically balance visual and instruction contributions. This dual-domain separation prevents dissimilar tasks from overwriting each other's LoRA parameters, directly targeting both forms of forgetting simultaneously.

## Results

On the upstream CVIT benchmark (single-type instructions, after tuning on all 6 tasks), SMoLoRA achieves 83.44% AP, 84.85% MAP, −3.23% BWT, and 97.79% MIF, substantially outperforming the next-best method Eproj (65.29% AP, 73.53% MAP, −14.02% BWT, 89.81% MIF). Compared to the closest MoE-based baseline MoeLoRA, SMoLoRA improves AP by +38.17%, MAP by +28.69%, and MIF by +17.82%, while reducing forgetting (BWT) from −48.05% to −3.23%. On downstream zero-shot generalization, SMoLoRA reaches 34.35% AP and 90.94% MIF, surpassing the best competitor DoRA (25.21% AP, 61.98% MIF) by 9.14% and 28.96% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMoLoRA decisively outperforms all seven baselines across every metric in both single-type and multi-type instruction settings. Against the closest CVIT-specific method Eproj [12], SMoLoRA gains +18.15% AP, +11.32% MAP, and +7.97% MIF while reducing forgetting from −14.02% to −3.23% BWT. The MoE-based MoeLoRA [3] (CoIN) and the sequential SeqLoRA [14] both suffer severe forgetting (BWT ≈ −48%), whereas SMoLoRA keeps BWT near zero (−3.23%), indicating minimal interference between tasks. Classic regularization methods EWC [19] and Replay [2] perform poorly on AP (46.26% and 49.70% respectively) and show large forgetting (−49.71% and −22.71% BWT). The enhanced LoRA variants DoRA [24] and C-LoRA [35] also fail to prevent forgetting (BWT −31.12% and −19.58%) and trail SMoLoRA by 39.37% and 37.66% in AP. On downstream zero-shot transfer, SMoLoRA leads all baselines by 9.14% AP over DoRA [24] and 11.47% AP over Replay [2], with a particularly large 28.96% MIF advantage over DoRA, confirming superior instruction-following retention after continual training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MoLoRA framework (which routes inputs to task-specific LoRA blocks for multitask learning) into the continual learning setting by introducing separable routing across two distinct domains: visual understanding and instruction following. It departs from prior CVIT methods (CoIN, Eproj, EWC+TIR) that treat forgetting as a single unified phenomenon, instead identifying and architecturally addressing two independent forgetting channels. The adaptive fusion mechanism and the use of SentenceBERT embeddings for instruction-based routing are novel design choices not present in earlier MoE-for-MLLM work like MoELLAVA or MoCLE, which rely on token-level or cluster-based routing without explicit domain separation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of instruction-following degradation as a distinct and often more severe form of forgetting than visual understanding loss provides a useful diagnostic lens for the CVIT community, and the MIF metric offers a practical way to track it. The new benchmark's inclusion of instruction diversity and unseen-task generalization addresses real gaps in prior CVIT evaluations. However, the method is validated only on 7B-scale models (LLaVA-v1.5, MiniGPT-4) with a single-epoch training protocol and a relatively small set of six upstream tasks, leaving open questions about scalability to larger models, longer task sequences, and whether the separable routing design generalizes beyond the FFN layers where it is applied.
