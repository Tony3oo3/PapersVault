## Problem

Existing unsupervised domain adaptation methods (both feature adaptation and self-training) suffer from negative transfer in chest X-ray classification, where the entanglement of class imbalance and distribution shift causes them to underperform a source-only baseline. The paper identifies two root causes: confirmation bias in self-training (unreliable pseudo-labels reinforcing majority-class predictions) and representation bias (feature spaces of majority classes and source domains dominating minority classes and target domains).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250919/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a labeled source chest X-ray set and an unlabeled target chest X-ray set, both processed through a ResNet-50 backbone fine-tuned from ImageNet. It outputs a scalar confidence score per image indicating the probability of a specific disease (binary classification for 6 diseases: Atelectasis, Pneumonia, Cardiomegaly, Consolidation, Pleural Effusion, Edema), evaluated via AUROC across 72 transfer tasks (4 domains × 6 diseases × 3 directions). The mixup mechanism means target images are never directly backpropagated; they only contribute through convex combinations with source images.

## Method

DCA replaces direct pseudo-label self-training with Curriculum Adaptation: source and target samples are mixed via Mixup with a mixing coefficient λ drawn from a Beta(α_p, β_p) distribution whose parameters shift over training progress (α_p increases, β_p decreases), so early training is source-dominated and later training becomes target-dominated, forming an easy-to-hard curriculum that avoids confirmation bias. Spectral Debiasing regularizes the learned feature space using SVD: Class-wise Spectral Debiasing (CSD) penalizes the squared spectral norms of both positive and negative feature matrices to counteract minority collapse, while Domain-wise Spectral Debiasing (DSD) penalizes the squared spectral norm of the source feature matrix to prevent source-distribution dominance. The total loss is L_s + L_st + γ(L_csd + L_dsd), where L_s is source BCE, L_st is BCE on mixed samples, and γ trades off the debiasing strength.

## Results

Across 72 transfer tasks (6 diseases × 12 domain pairs), DCA achieves an average AUROC improvement of +3.196% over the Source-only baseline, while all seven compared UDA methods show negative or near-zero average improvements (ranging from −3.854% for CST to +0.438% for MDD). For the three diseases reported in detail, DCA attains average AUROC of 75.994% (Atelectasis), 74.238% (Pneumonia), and 81.471% (Cardiomegaly), outperforming the best baseline (MDD) by 0.97, 2.62, and 2.35 percentage points respectively. On the most challenging tasks where Open-i (the most imbalanced dataset) is the source domain, DCA achieves an average improvement of 8.623%, compared to at most 1.361% for any other method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCA is the only method that achieves positive average improvement across all six diseases, with a +3.196% mean gain over Source-only. Among feature adaptation baselines, MDD is the closest competitor (+0.438% average) but still underperforms DCA by roughly 2.8 percentage points; DANN, CDAN, MCD, and DALN all exhibit net negative transfer. Among self-training baselines, vanilla ST (−0.228%) and Cycle Self-Training CST (−3.854%) both suffer from confirmation bias, with CST showing the most severe degradation (e.g., −6.268% on Edema). DCA wins on 9/12 Atelectasis tasks, 10/12 Pneumonia tasks, and 8/12 Cardiomegaly tasks, and is the only method that improves on every single task without any negative transfer. The advantage is most pronounced when Open-i (the most class-imbalanced dataset) serves as the source domain, where DCA gains 8.623% on average versus at most 1.361% for any baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant feature adaptation paradigm in UDA (DANN, CDAN, MCD, MDD, DALN) by explicitly citing impossibility theories that limit invariant representation learning under label shift, and instead builds on the self-training/pseudo-labeling paradigm. It repurposes Mixup (Zhang et al., ICLR 2018) not as a data augmentation trick but as a curriculum mechanism that generates intermediate distributions between source and target domains via a dynamically scheduled Beta distribution. The Spectral Debiasing component introduces SVD-based regularization of feature matrices as a novel loss term, drawing on the minority collapse phenomenon from neural collapse theory to address class-imbalance-induced representation bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a useful empirical diagnosis of why standard UDA methods systematically fail (negative transfer) in chest X-ray classification, attributing it to the entanglement of class imbalance and distribution shift — a finding that should caution practitioners applying off-the-shelf UDA to imbalanced medical datasets. The spectral debiasing regularization is a lightweight, architecture-agnostic technique that could potentially transfer to other imbalanced domain adaptation settings beyond chest X-rays. However, the evaluation is limited to binary classification on four datasets with a single backbone (ResNet-50), and the average improvement of ~3.2% AUROC over Source-only, while consistent, is moderate; whether the approach generalizes to multi-class settings, other medical modalities, or deeper architectures remains open.
