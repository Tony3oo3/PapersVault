## Problem

Existing prompt-based continual learning methods integrate task-specific prompts either through fixed learned representations that remain unchanged during new task learning, or through prompts generated in an entangled task-shared space prone to interference and dominance. Both strategies limit the representational diversity of the unified prompt, which the authors show empirically correlates with lower accuracy and higher forgetting in sequential task learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (224×224) or video clips (three equal temporal segments with one frame randomly sampled from each) and outputs a class label over the cumulative set of seen classes. It operates under a rehearsal-free, class-incremental protocol where task identity is unknown at test time and no previous-task data is stored. At inference, only the stored RainbowPrompts and the frozen ViT-B/16 are needed; the prompt-evolving components (76.5% of trainable parameters) are discarded.

## Method

RainbowPrompt introduces a prompt-evolving mechanism that progressively transforms and aligns all accumulated base prompts (from every previously learned task plus the current one) into a single unified prompt per layer. The transformation has two stages: an attention-based transformation that computes task-level and feature-level affinity matrices to dynamically reweight each prompt's contribution based on its relevance to the new task, and a task-guided alignment that applies a non-linear bottleneck (two linear layers with ReLU in a reduced dimension) to refine representations toward the new task while preserving each prompt's intrinsic properties. A learnable task embedding conditions the prompts before transformation, and a Gumbel-Softmax probabilistic gate adaptively selects which ViT layers receive the RainbowPrompt at each task, replacing fixed manual layer selection. The evolution is performed only during training; at test time the resulting RainbowPrompts are directly appended to the selected layers of the frozen ViT.

## Results

On ImageNet-R (10-task), RainbowPrompt achieves 79.09% average accuracy and 3.90% forgetting, surpassing the strongest baseline ConvPrompt (77.86% / 4.33%) by 1.23% accuracy and 0.43% forgetting. On CIFAR-100 (20-task), it reaches 90.15% accuracy with 3.75% forgetting, outperforming ConvPrompt (87.37% / 5.16%) by 2.78% accuracy and 1.41% forgetting. On video action recognition (UCF-101, 10-task), RainbowPrompt attains 89.03% accuracy and 4.91% forgetting, beating ConvPrompt (85.58% / 5.34%) by 3.45% accuracy. Across all five datasets and both 10-task and 20-task settings, RainbowPrompt achieves an overall average margin of 8.23% over competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RainbowPrompt outperforms all six baselines on every dataset and task setting. The largest gaps are against L2P (15.60% accuracy on ImageNet-R 10-task) and DualPrompt (10.59%), which use simpler prompt retrieval or fixed prompt pools. Against the two strongest competitors—CODA-Prompt and ConvPrompt—the margins are 4.85% and 1.23% on ImageNet-R 10-task, respectively, but widen to 7.50% and 3.26% in the 20-task setting, indicating that RainbowPrompt's diversity-enhancing evolution becomes more advantageous as the number of accumulated tasks grows. On CIFAR-100 20-task, RainbowPrompt is the only method that does not degrade relative to its 10-task performance (89.86% → 90.15%), while ConvPrompt drops by 1.50% and L2P by 4.81%. On video action recognition (UCF-101, 20-task), RainbowPrompt leads ConvPrompt by 5.22% accuracy and 0.44% forgetting, and on ActivityNet 20-task it gains 10.54% accuracy over ConvPrompt. Forgetting is consistently lowest for RainbowPrompt across all settings, with the largest reduction (1.41%) observed on CIFAR-100 20-task versus ConvPrompt.

## Relation to Prior Work

RainbowPrompt extends the prompt-based continual learning (PCL) lineage initiated by L2P and DualPrompt, which store task-specific prompts and retrieve them at test time. It most directly builds on CODA-Prompt's decomposed attention mechanism for integrating multiple prompts, but departs by replacing input-conditioned cosine-similarity weighting with a two-level (task-level and feature-level) attention-based transformation that dynamically reweights all accumulated base prompts at each new task. Unlike ConvPrompt, which generates new prompts from an entangled task-shared space, RainbowPrompt evolves the representations of all previously learned base prompts through progressive alignment, preserving their individuality while adapting them to the current task. The task-guided alignment component and the Gumbel-Softmax probabilistic gate for adaptive layer selection are novel additions not present in prior PCL methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides empirical evidence (Fig. 2) that representational diversity of integrated prompts is a key bottleneck in prompt-based CL, linking nuclear-norm diversity directly to both accuracy and forgetting. This reframes the prompt-integration problem from a retrieval or generation task to a diversity-preservation problem, which could inform future work beyond prompt-based methods. However, the approach still requires storing all base prompts (memory grows linearly with task count), and the evolution mechanism adds non-trivial training overhead (8.2M parameters during training). Open questions include scalability to very long task sequences (beyond 20 tasks), applicability to non-class-incremental settings (domain or task-incremental), and whether the diversity gains persist when the frozen backbone is replaced by a smaller or differently-architected model.
