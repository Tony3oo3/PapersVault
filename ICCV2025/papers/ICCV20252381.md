## Problem

Discrete motion generation methods (e.g., VQ-VAE token predictors) achieve high faithfulness to conditioning signals but produce unnatural artifacts—frame-wise noise and under-reconstruction—when tokens are decoded by a simple feedforward network. Continuous methods generate smooth motion but struggle with faithfulness due to high-dimensional cross-modal mapping ambiguity, especially in data-constrained settings. DisCoRD targets this gap by decoding discrete tokens in continuous space to recover both faithfulness and naturalness.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of discrete motion tokens (from any pretrained VQ-VAE or RQ-VAE codebook) as conditioning and produces a full 3D human motion sequence in raw joint-position space. Each token spans q consecutive frames (the downsampling factor), and the Condition Projection module expands each token into q distinct frame-wise features that independently condition each motion frame. The decoder is framework-agnostic: it can be plugged into any discrete token predictor (autoregressive, masked, discrete diffusion) regardless of the upstream conditioning signal (text, speech, or music), and inference requires only 16 Euler ODE steps by default.

## Method

DisCoRD replaces the standard feedforward VQ-VAE decoder with a conditional rectified flow model that transports Gaussian noise to motion in the raw motion space, guided by frame-wise conditioning features extracted from discrete tokens. The Condition Projection module repeats each token q times, stacks the copies into a single vector, applies a linear projection, and unstacks to yield q distinct per-frame features, preserving temporal correspondence between tokens and frames. The rectified flow decoder is a U-Net that predicts a vector field v_θ(X_t, t, C), where C is concatenated along the channel dimension so each frame is conditioned independently; training minimizes the flow-matching least-squares objective over sliding windows rather than full sequences, and omits attention layers (which were found to degrade performance). At inference, the ODE is solved with the Euler method over 16 steps, yielding smooth, dynamic motion while preserving alignment with the original conditioning signal.

## Results

On HumanML3D text-to-motion generation, MoMask+DisCoRD achieves the best FID of 0.032 (vs. 0.045 for MoMask alone, 0.055 for BAMM, and 0.116 for T2M-GPT), while maintaining R-Precision Top-1 of 0.524. On KIT-ML, MoMask+DisCoRD reaches FID 0.169 (vs. 0.204 for MoMask and 0.718 for T2M-GPT). In motion reconstruction (Table 1), DisCoRD reduces FID by 40–79% and sJPE by 7–25% across all discrete baselines, e.g., T2M-GPT FID drops from 0.089 to 0.031 and MMM FID from 0.097 to 0.020 on HumanML3D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DisCoRD consistently improves FID over every discrete baseline it is applied to: on HumanML3D, MoMask FID drops from 0.045→0.032 (+29%), BAMM from 0.055→0.041 (+25%), and T2M-GPT from 0.116→0.095 (+18%); on KIT-ML, MoMask drops from 0.204→0.169 (+17%) and T2M-GPT from 0.718→0.541 (+25%). Faithfulness (R-Precision) is preserved or slightly improved when paired with RQ-VAE-based tokenizers (MoMask, BAMM), but drops modestly with T2M-GPT's vanilla VQ-VAE (e.g., RP@1 0.491→0.476 on HumanML3D), which the authors attribute to a single token mapping to multiple motions. Against continuous baselines (MDM, MLD, MotionDiffuse, ReMoDiffuse), MoMask+DisCoRD achieves the best FID on both datasets (0.032 and 0.169) while matching or exceeding their R-Precision. On co-speech gesture generation (SHOW), TalkSHOW+DisCoRD reduces sJPE from 0.284 to 0.077 and FGD from 74.88 to 43.58; ProbTalk+DisCoRD improves sJPE from 0.406 to 0.349 and FGD from 5.21 to 4.83. On music-to-dance (AIST++), TM2D+DisCoRD improves sJPE from 0.275 to 0.261, though Dist_k and Dist_g show mixed results (Dist_k worsens slightly from 8.851 to 9.830 while Dist_g worsens from 4.225 to 8.519), suggesting the gains are more pronounced in naturalness than in distributional spread for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

DisCoRD sits at the intersection of two well-established lines of work: discrete motion generation (VQ-VAE/RQ-VAE tokenization followed by autoregressive or masked token prediction, as in T2M-GPT, MoMask, BAMM) and rectified flow / flow matching for generative modeling (Liu et al. 2022; Lipman et al. 2022). Rather than proposing a new tokenization or token-prediction scheme, the paper repurposes rectified flow as a *decoder*: pretrained discrete tokens are projected into frame-wise conditioning features that guide a conditional flow model to synthesize motion from Gaussian noise in the raw motion space. This departs from prior discrete methods, which decode tokens via a single feedforward VQ-VAE decoder, and from prior continuous methods (MLD, MDM), which generate motion from scratch without discrete conditioning. The paper also introduces sJPE, a sample-wise jerk-based metric, to fill an evaluation gap left by FID and MPJPE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DisCoRD offers a practical, framework-agnostic upgrade path for any existing discrete motion generation pipeline, closing the faithfulness–naturalness gap that has limited discrete methods in data-constrained settings. The sJPE metric addresses a real blind spot: FID is nearly insensitive to the frame-wise noise that most visibly degrades perceived motion quality, and sJPE makes this visible. Limitations include a consistent MPJPE regression (positional accuracy trades off for smoothness), evaluation restricted to human motion, and the added (though modest) inference cost of iterative ODE solving. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to other discrete generative domains (talking faces, hand motion) and whether the sliding-window training strategy generalizes to longer or more complex motion sequences.
