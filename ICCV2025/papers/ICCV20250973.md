## Problem

Template-based 6D object pose estimation typically relies on retrieving a single best-matching template before estimating pose, so a retrieval failure cascades into an incorrect prediction. Additionally, diffusion-based pose estimation methods designed for camera poses (e.g., RayDiffusion) use world-coordinate Plücker rays that entangle intrinsics, rotation, and translation, making them poorly suited to the compact, object-centric scale of 6D object pose estimation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB query image of an unseen object (cropped via CNOS detection/segmentation) and a set of N synthetically rendered posed template images with their known 6D poses. Internally, the model denoises two structured 2D maps of shape (p×p×3): a rotation ray map encoding unit direction vectors from the object center, and a translation offset map encoding per-pixel offsets to the object centroid plus normalized depth. The final output is the absolute 6D pose (R ∈ SO(3), t ∈ R³) recovered by composing the predicted relative pose with the known template poses.

## Method

RayPose reformulates template-based pose estimation as a multiview ray-bundling problem: instead of selecting one best template, it simultaneously aligns object-centered rays from multiple posed templates with the query. The key representation decouples rotation (a 2D grid of unit ray directions from the object center, recovered via SVD alignment to a canonical frame) from translation (a dense offset map extending SITE), avoiding the entanglement of camera-centric Plücker coordinates. The architecture uses a frozen DINOv2 image encoder plus Fourier-encoded view embeddings for both query and templates, a DiT-based multiview fuser for cross-template attention, and a DiT diffusion transformer decoder with cross-attention to denoise the pose maps conditioned on query and template embeddings. A coarse-to-fine strategy reuses the same network trained with different template sampling distributions (random for coarse, ±30°/±5 cm around the query pose for fine), enabling progressive refinement without architectural changes.

## Results

On the BOP benchmark, RayPose achieves an average AR of 61.2 in the full setting (refinement + multi-hypothesis), outperforming the previous best FoundPose (60.9) and GigaPose (58.9). In the single-prediction setting without refinement, RayPose reaches 39.1 average AR, improving over the prior best FoundPose (37.8) by 1.3 points and GigaPose (27.9) by 11.2 points, with the largest gains on LM-O (+2.5) and T-LESS (+3.1). With refinement enabled, RayPose scores 56.2 average AR, edging out FoundPose (55.9) and GigaPose (55.1), with particularly strong results on TUD-L (66.5 vs. 63.3 for FoundPose).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RayPose outperforms all six baselines in every evaluation setting. Against the strongest competitor FoundPose [56], RayPose gains +1.3 AR in the no-refinement setting (39.1 vs. 37.8), +0.3 with refinement (56.2 vs. 55.9), and +0.3 with multi-hypothesis refinement (61.2 vs. 60.9). Against GigaPose [40], the margins are larger: +11.2 AR without refinement (39.1 vs. 27.9) and +2.3 with multi-hypothesis refinement (61.2 vs. 58.9). On T-LESS specifically, RayPose achieves 59.1 AR in the full setting, surpassing FoundPose (57.0) by 2.1 points and GigaPose (57.0) by 2.1 points. The largest single-dataset gain is on TUD-L in the full setting, where RayPose reaches 70.2 AR versus 69.4 for FoundPose and 68.4 for GenFlow/MegaPose. RayPose is the only method to exceed 60 AR on both LM-O and T-LESS simultaneously in the multi-hypothesis setting, though it trails FoundPose on IC-BIN (45.5 vs. 47.9) and YCB-V (68.9 vs. 69.0) in that configuration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the camera-centric ray diffusion framework of RayDiffusion (Cameras as Rays, ICLR 2024) to the object pose domain by reparameterizing Plücker coordinates into object-centered ray bundles on a structured 2D grid, decoupling rotation from translation and camera intrinsics. It extends the Scale-Invariant Translation Estimation (SITE) formulation from CDPN to a dense per-pixel translation offset map, and builds on the DiT-based diffusion transformer architecture for the denoising decoder. The template-based pose estimation lineage (MegaPose, GigaPose, FoundationPose) provides the posed-template conditioning paradigm, but RayPose departs from the typical retrieve-then-align pipeline by treating all template-query pairs jointly through a multiview diffusion process rather than selecting a single best template.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method achieves competitive SOTA across five standard BOP benchmarks for unseen object pose estimation, with the coarse-to-fine strategy improving accuracy without any architectural change—a practical design choice that lowers the barrier to adoption. However, the approach still requires a textured CAD model for template rendering and accurate object detection/segmentation, limiting its applicability to settings where these priors are unavailable. Open questions include whether the reliance on ground-truth template pose embeddings (whose removal causes a large performance drop) can be relaxed, and whether the diffusion-based probabilistic formulation can be extended to handle symmetric objects or multi-object scenes without per-object template sets.
