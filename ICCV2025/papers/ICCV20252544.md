## Problem

Vision-language models such as CLIP exhibit 'bag-of-words' behavior, failing to distinguish fine-grained compositional relationships (attributes, spatial positions, object interactions) in complex scenes. Prior data augmentation strategies like ARO and VL-Checklist only perturb text (reordering or swapping words) without generating matching images, so the text encoder can achieve near-perfect accuracy without relying on visual information, and gains do not transfer to complex relational benchmarks like Winoground or sDCI.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252544/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes real COCO image-text pairs (200k+ images with 5+ captions each), which are first expanded into dense spatially-aware descriptions by GPT-4o. It then produces counterfactual sets: groups of 5–20 image-text pairs derived from one original image, where each variant differs by adding, removing, or modifying one entity (attribute, position, or relationship change). The final output is a LoRA-fine-tuned CLIP ViT/B-32 model (r=α=32) that scores image-text alignment with improved compositional discrimination.

## Method

The core mechanism is a block-based diffusion strategy that decomposes image generation into semantic 'puzzle pieces.' An LLM extracts per-entity local descriptions, spatial coordinates, and a global scene description from each dense caption; each entity is rendered as a reference image via a separate text-to-image diffusion call. During the main denoising process, cross-attention layers receive both global text guidance and per-block local guidance (concatenated text and image key-value embeddings) restricted by spatial masks, with a dynamic weighting schedule that favors local blocks in early timesteps and shifts to global coherence in later timesteps. A novel paired loss (L_sets) replaces standard contrastive loss by computing intra-set sigmoid losses over all positive-negative pairs within each counterfactual set and inter-set losses using only one representative pair per set, reducing negative-sample requirements and cutting training time by ~14–16% versus contrastive or sigmoid baselines.

## Results

On the ARO benchmark, GCS-generate-300k achieves 85.7% (VGR), 73.4% (VGA), 94.6% (COCO), and 95.6% (Flickr) accuracy, surpassing all baselines including DACLLM-3m (81.3/73.9/94.4/95.4) and NegCLIP (81.1/70.9/85.5/90.9). On the complex-reasoning sDCI benchmark, GCS-300k reaches 47.9% SCM@1 and 90.2% Neg@1, outperforming sDCIp1 (45.2/88.2) and DACSAM-3m (37.9/84.7). On Winoground, GCS-300k scores 32.8% (Text), 19.5% (Image), and 10.0% (Group), exceeding the original CLIP (31.3/11.3/9.0) and all fine-tuned baselines. On VL-Checklist, GCS-300k attains 89.1% (Object), 81.4% (Attribute), and 85.6% (Relation), though DACSAM-3m slightly leads on Relation (89.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GCS-generate-300k outperforms NegCLIP (ARO: 81.1/70.9/85.5/90.9) and SVLC (80.6/73.0/84.7/91.7) by 4–10 points across all ARO sub-datasets, and by 9–22 points on VL-Checklist Attribute and Relation. Against the strongest text-perturbation baseline DACLLM-3m (81.3/73.9/94.4/95.4), GCS-300k leads on ARO-VGR (+4.4) and VL-Checklist Attribute (+4.2) but trails slightly on ARO-VGA (−0.5) and VL-Checklist Relation (−0.8). On the spatial-reasoning sDCI benchmark, GCS-300k (47.9/90.2) surpasses sDCIp1 (45.2/88.2) and DACSAM-3m (37.9/84.7), confirming that generated counterfactual images provide genuine spatial grounding rather than textual shortcuts. On Winoground, GCS-300k (32.8/19.5/10.0) beats every fine-tuned baseline, including sDCIp1NL0 (30.5/10.8/8.3) and DACSAM-3m (28.3/14.5/8.5), with the largest margin on the Image metric (+5 to +9 points over CLIP). The method also generalizes to BLIP-2 and MiniGPT-4, where GCS-300k improves ARO-R from 41.2→74.9 (BLIP-2) and 46.9→60.2 (MiniGPT-4), demonstrating architecture-agnostic gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of compositional-reasoning data augmentation (ARO, VL-Checklist, DAC, sDCI), which modifies only the text side—reordering words, swapping attributes, or generating dense captions—while leaving the image unchanged. By contrast, GCS actually synthesizes new images via a block-based diffusion pipeline guided by LLM-parsed entity coordinates and reference images, closing the image-text alignment gap that prior text-only perturbations leave open. The training objective also moves beyond standard contrastive or sigmoid losses to a paired intra-set/inter-set formulation that exploits the structured counterfactual-set geometry, and the fine-tuning is applied via LoRA to CLIP, BLIP-2, and MiniGPT-4, positioning the method as a model-agnostic data-and-loss recipe rather than a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically relevant finding is the data-efficiency gap: 10k generated counterfactual sets approach or exceed the performance of models trained on 3M LLM-augmented real captions (DACLLM-3m) on ARO and Winoground, suggesting that targeted compositional variation matters more than raw volume. However, the absolute margins over the strongest baselines (sDCIp1, DACSAM-3m) remain modest (1–4 points), and the pipeline's quality is bottlenecked by LLM entity parsing and diffusion-model fidelity, which may limit scalability to scenes with many interacting objects. Open questions include whether the block-based diffusion strategy generalizes to video or 3D compositional reasoning, whether the paired loss transfers to non-CLIP architectures without re-tuning, and whether the 99.6% attribute-modification accuracy holds under adversarial or out-of-distribution entity combinations.
