## Problem

Existing multi-layer image generation methods either ignore spatial relationships between layers (causing unintended overlaps) or restrict layers to non-overlapping compositions, failing to model essential inter-layer effects like occlusion and shadowing. Additionally, the field lacks a large-scale, high-quality multi-layer dataset, with prior work relying on randomly stacked segmentation data or limited samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250656/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-layer generation, the method takes a user text prompt that is decomposed by GPT-4 into a background prompt and multiple foreground prompts (up to 3 foregrounds plus background, yielding 4 layers total). For image-to-layer, it accepts an RGB image plus text prompts, using null-text inversion to initialize the denoising latent. The output is a set of transparent layers (each a 3-channel RGB image plus a 1-channel alpha mask) at resolutions of 896–1152 pixels, plus a global composite layer that represents the fully blended result.

## Method

DreamLayer introduces three components operating within a Stable Diffusion v1.5 U-Net trained via Custom Diffusion (fine-tuning K&V attention layers). Context-Aware Cross-Attention (CACA) extracts spatial layout maps from the global layer's cross-attention and aligns foreground layers to them via a layout loss, ensuring consistent object placement. Layer-Shared Self-Attention (LSSA) concatenates all layer latents along the sequence dimension and computes attention with per-layer queries against joint keys/values, enabling direct inter-layer information exchange. Information Retained Harmonization (IRH) retains noisy latents during a mid-denoising window, performs alpha-weighted latent-level layer fusion, then re-denoises to synthesize shadows and texture details that simple alpha compositing cannot produce. The framework is trained on a 400k-sample multi-layer dataset constructed by generating images with Flux/SDXL, decomposing them via GroundingDINO detection, DepthAnything depth ordering, matting, and inpainting.

## Results

On the DreamLayer test set (3k multi-layer images), DreamLayer achieves the best composite image quality across all layer counts: for four-layer generation, it scores 6.422 AES, 35.723 CLIP, and 53.598 FID, outperforming LayerDiffusion (5.975 AES, 29.158 CLIP, 79.997 FID) by approximately 0.45 in aesthetic score, 6.6 in CLIP alignment, and 26.4 in FID. For two-layer generation, DreamLayer reaches 7.013 AES and 50.761 FID, surpassing both SD v1.5 (6.930 AES, 53.950 FID) and LayerDiffusion (6.522 AES, 63.481 FID). In a user study with 20 subjects on 200 samples, DreamLayer receives a 71.34% preference rate for overall multi-layer quality and 83.03% for background quality over LayerDiffusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamLayer consistently outperforms LayerDiffusion [42] across all three metrics (AES, CLIP, FID) for two-, three-, and four-layer generation, with the gap widening as layer count increases (e.g., FID drops from 63.5 to 53.6 for four layers vs. 80.0 for LayerDiffusion). Against SD v1.5 [24] (which generates a single composite image from the global prompt), DreamLayer achieves higher CLIP scores and lower FID for three- and four-layer settings, while SD v1.5 retains a marginal AES advantage only in the two-layer case (6.930 vs. 7.013). In the user study, DreamLayer is preferred over LayerDiffusion 71.34% of the time for overall multi-layer coherence and 83.03% for background quality, though the foreground preference is more modest at 47.84%. For the layer editing task, DreamLayer is qualitatively compared to DesignEdit [13], where DreamLayer additionally handles amodal completion of objects moved beyond image boundaries, a capability DesignEdit lacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamLayer extends the line of layered diffusion generation initiated by Text2Layer and LayerDiffusion (both limited to two-layer foreground/background composites) and LayerDiff (multi-layer but non-overlapping). It departs from these by introducing an explicit global layer that provides a coherent layout reference, and by modeling inter-layer interactions—occlusion, spatial alignment, and shadowing—through dedicated attention mechanisms (CACA, LSSA) rather than relying on simple alpha-channel stacking. The dataset construction pipeline repurposes off-the-shelf tools (GPT-4 for prompt decomposition, GroundingDINO for detection, DepthAnything for depth ordering, inpainting for hole-filling) in a novel compositional workflow, and the IRH harmonization step borrows the re-denoising idea from inversion-based editing but applies it to latent-level layer fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 400k multi-layer dataset is a significant practical resource that addresses a clear bottleneck in the field, and the IRH mechanism—generating physically plausible shadows by re-denoising a fused latent—offers a reusable technique for any layered generation or editing pipeline. However, the approach is capped at four layers, depends on GPT-4 for prompt decomposition, and is evaluated only on SD v1.5, leaving open whether the inter-layer attention design transfers to DiT-based backbones or scales to more complex scenes. A key open question is whether the global-layer-guided paradigm can be extended to video layers or to user-interactive multi-object editing without retraining.
