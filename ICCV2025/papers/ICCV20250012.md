## Problem

Subject-driven image generation faces a data scalability bottleneck: curating high-quality multi-subject paired datasets is impractical, and most existing methods are limited to single-subject conditioning, making it difficult to scale visual subject control without sacrificing text controllability. The paper identifies that existing models are designed around their available data, creating a circular dependency that limits scalability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250012/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

UNO accepts one or two noise-free reference images (encoded to latents via a VAE) concatenated with a text prompt, and generates a single subject-consistent image at high resolutions (1024×1024, 1024×768, or 768×1024). The reference images are injected as additional token sequences alongside the noisy target latent and text tokens within the MM-DiT's multi-modal attention, rather than through a separate encoder or cross-attention pathway. The synthetic data pipeline produces paired images at these three resolutions, significantly exceeding the 512×512 limit of prior synthetic-data approaches.

## Method

UNO transforms a pretrained FLUX.1 MM-DiT into a subject-to-image model via progressive cross-modal alignment: Stage I fine-tunes on single-subject in-context generated pairs to introduce one reference image token sequence, then Stage II extends to two reference images, avoiding the training instability of directly exposing the model to multiple noise-free image conditions. A key architectural addition is UnoPE, which assigns diagonal position-index offsets to reference image tokens (shifting by cumulative width/height of preceding references) so the model learns semantic subject identity rather than copying spatial layout from references. The training data itself is synthesized through a model-data co-evolution pipeline: a less-controllable S2I model generates multi-subject consistent pairs via in-context generation, which are then filtered by DINOv2 similarity and VLM-based multi-dimensional scoring to ensure high quality, enabling iterative improvement of both data and model.

## Results

On the single-subject DreamBench benchmark, UNO achieves the highest DINO score (0.760) and CLIP-I score (0.835) among all compared methods, with a competitive CLIP-T of 0.304. On the multi-subject driven generation benchmark, UNO attains the best DINO (0.542) and CLIP-I (0.733) scores, while OmniGen edges ahead on CLIP-T (0.331 vs. 0.322). UNO's DINO advantage over the next-best single-subject method (RealCustom++, 0.702) is approximately 8.3%, and over the next-best multi-subject method (MS-Diffusion, 0.525) is about 3.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNO decisively outperforms all 12 baselines on DINO (subject similarity) in both single-subject (0.760 vs. next-best RealCustom++ 0.702) and multi-subject (0.542 vs. next-best MS-Diffusion 0.525) settings. On CLIP-I (image-image consistency), UNO also leads in both settings (0.835 single-subject vs. SSR-Encoder 0.821; 0.733 multi-subject vs. MIP-Adapter/MS-Diffusion 0.726). However, UNO trails on CLIP-T (text controllability): in the single-subject setting, RealCustom++ (0.318), OmniGen (0.315), OminiControl (0.312), and BootPIG (0.311) all exceed UNO's 0.304; in the multi-subject setting, OmniGen (0.331) surpasses UNO's 0.322. Fine-tuning-based methods (DreamBooth, Textual Inversion) are consistently outperformed by tuning-free methods on DINO, with UNO extending this gap substantially. Among tuning-free baselines, SSR-Encoder and FLUX.1 IP-Adapter achieve strong CLIP-I but sacrifice subject similarity (DINO 0.612 and 0.582 respectively), whereas UNO maintains the best balance of both metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

UNO builds directly on the DiT/FLUX.1 MM-DiT architecture, extending its multi-modal attention mechanism to accept multiple noise-free reference image tokens alongside text and noisy latent tokens. Conceptually, the paper departs from the prevailing data-driven model design paradigm (where models are shaped by whatever paired data is available) by borrowing the LLM self-improvement idea of model-data co-evolution: a less-controllable model synthesizes training data for a more-controllable successor. The in-context generation capability of DiT, previously explored in IC-LoRA and OminiControl for single-subject conditioning at 512×512, is here systematized into a progressive single-to-multi-subject synthesis pipeline at 1024×1024, while the UnoPE modification of RoPE addresses a position-encoding gap that prior multi-reference methods (MS-Diffusion, MIP-Adapter) handled through more complex architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The model-data co-evolution paradigm offers a general recipe for breaking the data bottleneck in subject-driven generation: if a model can generate consistent pairs in-context, it can bootstrap its own training data at higher resolution and with more subjects than any real dataset provides. The near-oracle DINO score (0.760 vs. 0.774) and the dramatic UnoPE ablation (DINO 0.542→0.386) suggest that position encoding, not architectural complexity, is the key lever for multi-subject disambiguation in DiT-based models. Open questions include whether the paradigm scales beyond two reference subjects, whether the synthetic-data quality ceiling eventually limits further gains, and whether the co-evolution loop can be automated end-to-end without human-in-the-loop VLM filtering.
