## Problem

Existing diffusion-based inverse rendering methods rely on an image-conditioned noise-to-intrinsic paradigm that operates on noisy latents with degraded structure and appearance, requires many denoising steps for slow inference, and lacks explicit physical constraints tying predicted intrinsics back to the source image. This paper addresses all three limitations by reformulating the task as a deterministic image-to-intrinsic mapping that directly exploits the full visual information in the input.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252013/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB indoor image (encoded to latent space via a pre-trained VAE) and per-property text prompts (e.g., "albedo", "normal") as input, and produces five intrinsic maps: albedo, metallic, roughness, normal, and depth. Single-channel properties (metallic, roughness, depth) are replicated across three channels during training to match the VAE's RGB latent format, then averaged back at inference. Inference is deterministic and requires only 10 flow-matching steps (achieving near-optimal quality even in 1 step), yielding a 0.1-second runtime per image.

## Method

The core idea is to replace the standard noise-to-intrinsic diffusion process with a deterministic image-to-intrinsic flow: the VAE-encoded latent of the input image serves as the starting point of a straight ODE trajectory toward the target latent intrinsic, with a pre-trained DiT (from Stable Diffusion 3) fine-tuned via LoRA (~19M parameters) to predict the flow velocity. Text prompts act as lightweight triggers that steer the single shared DiT along different intrinsic trajectories, enabling one-to-many prediction without architectural changes. A generative renderer built on ControlNet + Stable Diffusion provides a reconstruction loss (implemented as SDS-style score distillation) that explicitly constrains the predicted intrinsics to be physically consistent with the source image, effectively integrating over unknown lighting during training. This combination of deterministic flow matching, minimal LoRA adaptation, and physics-based reconstruction supervision yields state-of-the-art results with the fewest trainable parameters and fastest inference among compared methods.

## Results

On the synthetic InteriorVerse dataset, DNF-Intrinsic achieves 21.95 PSNR on albedo (vs. 18.84 for the best baseline RGBX), 17.64 PSNR on metallic, 16.72 PSNR on roughness, 12.23° angular error on normals, and 0.08 AMRE on depth, outperforming all five baselines on every intrinsic property. On real-world datasets, the method achieves 28.40° AE and 0.07 AMRE on NYUv2, and 28.29° AE and 0.11 AMRE on DIODE for geometry, substantially beating the best baselines (IndoorIR: 34.38° AE / 0.23 AMRE on NYUv2; 36.03° AE / 0.19 AMRE on DIODE). The only metric where the method trails is IIW albedo WHDR (20.90 vs. 21.80 for RGBX), though it still outperforms InverseIndoor and IntrinsicDiff.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DNF-Intrinsic outperforms all five baselines on every available metric in the synthetic InteriorVerse evaluation (Table 1), with the largest margins on albedo PSNR (+3.11 over RGBX, +4.53 over IntrinsicDiff) and normal angular error (−3.18° over IndoorIR, −6.34° over RGBX). On real-world geometry (Table 2), the method dominates: it achieves 28.40° AE on NYUv2 versus 34.38° for IndoorIR and 35.24° for RGBX, and 28.29° AE on DIODE versus 36.03° for IndoorIR and 42.41° for RGBX. The sole exception is IIW albedo WHDR, where DNF-Intrinsic (20.90) is slightly worse than RGBX (21.80), InverseIndoor (21.99), IntrinsicDiff (22.02), and IndoorIR (22.90), though it still beats IntrinsicAnything (27.75) is not applicable since IntrinsicAnything reports a higher (worse) WHDR. Among the diffusion-based baselines, RGBX is the strongest on albedo PSNR (18.84) but weakest on metallic (11.92) and roughness (11.67), while IntrinsicDiff leads on roughness PSNR (13.33) among non-IndoorIR methods; DNF-Intrinsic surpasses all of them across the board on the synthetic benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant diffusion-based inverse rendering paradigm (IntrinsicAnything, IntrinsicDiff, RGBX) that formulates the task as image-conditioned noise-to-intrinsic denoising, instead proposing a deterministic image-to-intrinsic flow matching formulation where the source image latent serves as the trajectory起点 rather than Gaussian noise. It builds on rectified flow / flow matching (Lipman et al., Liu et al.) for the ODE-based trajectory, the DiT architecture from Stable Diffusion 3 as the flow estimator, and LoRA for parameter-efficient fine-tuning. The generative renderer with SDS reconstruction loss borrows the DreamFusion-style score distillation idea but applies it as a training-time physical consistency constraint rather than an optimization objective. Conceptually, the paper aligns with the 'cold diffusion' line of work showing that stochastic denoising is not always necessary when the task is inherently deterministic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a 10-step, 0.1-second inference pipeline with only ~19M trainable parameters that outperforms multi-step diffusion baselines by large margins (e.g., 21.95 vs. 18.84 PSNR on albedo), making high-quality indoor inverse rendering accessible in near-real-time settings. The finding that replacing noise input with the source image yields a ~3.7 PSNR gain (Table 3) validates the intuition that structure and appearance are critical for inverse rendering, and suggests the noise-to-intrinsic paradigm may be suboptimal for other deterministic image-to-image tasks. Limitations include restriction to indoor scenes, reliance on synthetic training data (InteriorVerse, 45K images), and the absence of explicit lighting estimation (requiring a separate optimization step). Open questions include whether the deterministic image-to-intrinsic formulation generalizes to outdoor scenes, multi-view settings, or tasks where genuine ambiguity (e.g., material-lighting decomposition) makes stochastic sampling beneficial.
