## Problem

Real-world autonomous driving data is severely imbalanced, with safety-critical long-tail events (near-collisions, jaywalking pedestrians) and hard-to-drive conditions (night, rain, sharp turns) being extremely scarce. Simply mixing simulated data with real data in traditional architectures like VAD yields only marginal gains (~1% L2 reduction) due to the persistent Sim2Real domain gap in both visual appearance and geometric sensor configuration. The paper asks how multimodal large language models can effectively transfer driving knowledge from synthetic hard cases to real-world scenarios.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 5 consecutive frames from 6 cameras (900×1600, 360° coverage) alongside a text driving instruction, a structured prompt encoding the city name and data-source label (simulation vs. real-world), and per-view camera intrinsic/extrinsic parameters fed through the I2E Encoder. Ego pose is optional and ablated separately. The model autoregressively generates predicted trajectory waypoints and corresponding vehicle speeds at 1s, 2s, and 3s horizons, evaluated via L2 distance, collision rate, and boundary violation rate on nuScenes.

## Method

RoboTron-Sim builds on LLaVA-OneVision and introduces three key components to bridge the Sim2Real gap. First, the HASS dataset (47,553 samples) is generated in CARLA using Think2Drive as a privileged-information teacher, covering 13 high-risk edge-case categories with deliberately balanced environmental conditions (41% night, 52% rainy, 54% turns) to counteract real-world data imbalance. Second, Scenario-aware Prompt Engineering (SPE) prepends each input with a structured descriptor ('You are driving in [City] under [Simulation/Real-World] scenario') that activates the LLM's commonsense knowledge about regional traffic rules and sensor characteristics, reducing boundary violations by 38.2%. Third, the Image-to-Ego Encoder is a two-layer MLP that maps per-view camera-to-ego transformation matrices into the LLM's embedding space, explicitly disentangling dataset-specific sensor geometry from driving policy learning and reducing L2 distance by 34.9% over the baseline without it. Training is supervised on 28,130 nuScenes samples plus HASS for 3 epochs on 16 A100 GPUs (~20 hours).

## Results

On nuScenes open-loop planning without ego pose, RoboTron-Sim achieves the best average L2 distance of 0.56 m, collision rate of 0.58%, and boundary violation rate of 3.02%, outperforming the next-best method BEV-Planner (0.57 m, 0.59%, 4.26%). With ego pose as input, RoboTron-Sim further reduces L2 to 0.23 m and collision rate to 0.26%, setting new SOTA across all three metrics. In hard-to-drive (H2D) scenarios specifically, the HASS-augmented model achieves 42.1% L2 reduction in night, 51.5% in turns, and 51.3% in rainy conditions compared to its own nuScenes-only baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoboTron-Sim outperforms all nine baselines on average L2 distance and collision rate in both evaluation settings. Without ego pose, it edges out BEV-Planner (0.56 m vs. 0.57 m L2; 0.58% vs. 0.59% collision) and substantially beats OmniDrive (0.56 m vs. 0.84 m L2; 0.58% vs. 0.94% collision), while also improving boundary violation over OmniDrive (3.02% vs. 4.29%). The MLLM baselines (LLaVA, Vicuna, Merlin) are far behind, with L2 distances of 1.71–1.80 m, roughly 3× worse than RoboTron-Sim. With ego pose, RoboTron-Sim achieves 0.23 m L2, beating EMMA (0.32 m), OmniDrive (0.33 m), and VAD-Base (0.37 m) by 28–38%. Notably, UniAD achieves the lowest boundary violation without ego pose (1.93%) but is outperformed on L2 (1.03 m) and collision (0.77%), indicating a trade-off between lane-keeping and overall trajectory accuracy. The scenario-specific ablation (Table 4) further shows that while VAD gains only 1.1–1.2% L2 improvement from HASS in H2D scenarios, RoboTron-Sim achieves 42–51% improvements, highlighting that the SPE and I2E Encoder components are critical for effective Sim2Real transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on LLaVA-OneVision as its MLLM backbone but departs fundamentally from prior end-to-end AD systems (UniAD, VAD, BEV-Planner) that rely on supervised learning with fine-grained 3D annotations. While prior work has explored simulation for AD data augmentation (CARLA, AirSim, OASIS SIM), and MLLMs have been applied to driving (Senna, DriveVLM, OmniDrive), this is the first to systematically investigate and address the Sim2Real domain gap specifically within MLLMs for planning. The key conceptual departure is from naively mixing real and simulated data (as VAD does) to explicitly encoding data provenance via Scenario-aware Prompt Engineering and disentangling sensor geometry via the I2E Encoder, enabling the LLM's commonsense reasoning to bridge the domain gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that traditional architectures (VAD) extract almost no benefit from simulated data while a properly designed MLLM framework achieves ~50% improvement in hard scenarios reframes how the community should think about simulation-to-reality transfer in AD. The practical implication that ~20% real data combined with HASS matches 100% real-data performance could significantly reduce data collection costs for safety-critical scenarios. However, evaluation is limited to open-loop planning on a single dataset (nuScenes) and one simulator (CARLA), leaving open whether these gains transfer to closed-loop evaluation, other sensor suites, or real-world deployment. The approach also assumes access to a capable teacher model (Think2Drive) for generating simulation trajectories, which may limit reproducibility.
