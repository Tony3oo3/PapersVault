## Problem

Open-Vocabulary Video Instance Segmentation requires simultaneously aligning features across spatial-temporal domains (tracking instances across frames) and text-visual domains (matching category text to visual features), but existing methods address these alignments separately and inadequately. The inherent data heterogeneity across these four domains leads to significant feature distribution differences that degrade both temporal consistency and cross-modal matching accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252054/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video as a sequence of T RGB frames (3×H×W) along with a set of category name text prompts, and produces per-frame segmentation masks, per-instance category labels, and consistent tracking IDs across frames. The CLIP ViT-B/32 image and text encoders remain frozen throughout training, while only the pixel decoder, Transformer decoder, and mask head are updated, meaning no category or identity annotations are required. The framework operates in an online fashion, processing frames sequentially without a memory bank, which keeps inference lightweight on a single GPU.

## Method

SWbridge uses the Sliced Wasserstein Distance (SWD) as a unified metric-learning tool to bridge four domains without adding network parameters. A Spatio-Temporal Bridge computes SWD between Transformer query embeddings of adjacent frames by constructing random projection directions from pairwise sample differences, resampling perturbed directions for robustness, and applying an exponential importance weighting that amplifies the contribution of instances with larger inter-frame changes. A Text-Visual Bridge similarly computes SWD between mask-pooled visual embeddings and CLIP text embeddings, but uses a full-path full-projection strategy (rather than random sampling) since the embedding sets are small. An additional contrastive loss (L_VT) aligns visual and text embeddings in a shared space, and the total loss is a weighted sum of the temporal SWD, modal SWD, contrastive, and standard segmentation (BCE + Dice) losses. The overall complexity is O(N·D·L + N log N), dominated by matrix multiplication over L projection directions, enabling efficient online processing of large-scale video.

## Results

SWBridge achieves the highest AP and APn across 19 out of 22 evaluation metrics on five open-vocabulary video instance segmentation benchmarks. With a Swin-B backbone, it reaches 33.0 AP / 40.5 APn on LV-VIS val, 43.0 AP / 28.0 APn on YT-VIS 2019, and 19.7 AP / 8.3 APn on OVIS. With ResNet-50, it attains 20.7 AP / 24.9 APn on LV-VIS val and 33.4 AP / 24.0 APn on YT-VIS 2019, consistently outperforming the strongest prior method CLIP-VIS by 1.2–1.5 AP points. The method is trained solely on LVIS image data without any video-specific fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIP-VIS (the strongest direct competitor), SWBridge wins on every reported metric for both backbones: with R50 it gains +1.2 AP on LV-VIS val (20.7 vs 19.5) and +1.2 AP on YT-VIS 2019 (33.4 vs 32.2); with SwinB it gains +0.8 AP on LV-VIS val (33.0 vs 32.2) and +0.9 AP on YT-VIS 2019 (43.0 vs 42.1), while matching CLIP-VIS exactly on OVIS APn (8.3). Against OVFormer, SWBridge (R50) loses on YT-VIS 2019 AP (33.4 vs 34.8) but substantially outperforms on APn (24.0 vs 16.5), indicating stronger novel-category generalization; with SwinB, SWBridge again trails on YT-VIS 2019 AP (43.0 vs 44.3) and OVIS AP (19.7 vs 21.3) but leads on APn for both YT-VIS splits (28.0 vs 21.5; 22.5 vs 18.3). Against OV2Seg and the Detic/DetPro pipeline baselines, SWBridge achieves large margins (e.g., +11.5 AP on LV-VIS val over OV2Seg-R50, +20.2 AP over Detic-SORT-R50), confirming that the Sliced Wasserstein bridging strategy provides a clear advantage over memory-based tracking and simple detection-plus-tracking pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the standard OV-VIS pipeline (Mask2Former-VIS for class-agnostic features, CLIP for text embeddings) but departs from prior alignment strategies: where OV2Seg and OpenVIS rely on memory-based cross-frame matching, BriVIS uses contrastive learning with Brownian bridges, and OV-Former employs attention-based embedding alignment, SWbridge instead unifies both spatio-temporal and text-visual alignment under a single metric-learning objective based on the Sliced Wasserstein distance. The conceptual lineage traces to optimal transport (Bonneel et al., 2015) and metric learning for sequence alignment (Garreau et al., 2014), repurposing SWD as a distributional similarity measure rather than a transport cost. The importance weighting mechanism and Monte Carlo projection sampling are adapted from the SWD literature but applied here for the first time to video instance tracking and cross-modal matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a parameter-free, computationally efficient alternative to memory modules and additional CLIP image encoders for OV-VIS, which could reduce training cost and simplify deployment for large-scale video processing. However, the performance gains over strong baselines (CLIP-VIS, OV-Former) are modest (1–2 AP points), and the method still inherits CLIP's zero-shot limitations for truly novel categories. Open questions include whether the Monte Carlo approximation of SWD (with fixed L) is sufficient for very long videos or highly dynamic scenes, whether the exponential importance weighting generalizes beyond the tested datasets, and whether the approach can be extended to multi-camera or 3D video settings where the projection geometry changes fundamentally.
