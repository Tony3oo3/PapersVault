## Problem

Current deep unfolding networks for pansharpening require laborious approximation of four degradation matrices (spatial B, spectral R, and their transposes), with performance uncertain in complex remote sensing scenes. Additionally, the prior-extraction step relies on black-box CNN/Transformer modules, sacrificing the interpretability that unfolding is meant to provide. The paper asks whether the spatial degradation can be entirely eliminated and whether explicit low-rank priors can be integrated into data-driven modules without heavy computation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252364/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a low-resolution multispectral (LRMS) image and a high-resolution panchromatic (PAN) image of the same scene. The LRMS is first upsampled to PAN resolution and serves as the network's initial input, while the PAN constrains spectral fidelity through the small spectral degradation matrix R (1×S). The output is a high-resolution multispectral (HRMS) image with PAN spatial dimensions and LRMS band count, trained end-to-end with an ℓ₁ loss against ground-truth HRMS.

## Method

The core insight is that the spatial offset component (SOC = upsampled LRMS − HRMS) is significantly sparse, so the spatial degradation matrix B and its transpose are replaced by a simple ℓ₁ sparsity penalty, reducing the problem to a Robust PCA formulation solved via ADMM unfolding. For the low-rank regularizer, a Nonlinear transformation-based Tensor Nuclear Norm (NTNN) is designed: two 1-D convolutions with ReLU form a dual nonlinear spectral transform, and a depthwise 3×3 convolution plus SoftShrink substitutes the traditional SVD-based singular value thresholding, cutting complexity from O(n⁴) to O(9n³). NTNN is then embedded plug-and-play into both a Low-rank Transformer (where it generates the query for cross-attention with Swin-Transformer keys/values) and a Low-rank CNN (where it modulates multi-scale depthwise convolution outputs via element-wise multiplication). The full RPCA model is unrolled into K=4 ADMM blocks with a U-shaped encoder-decoder prior extractor, trained end-to-end in a supervised fashion.

## Results

RUN achieves the best PSNR across all three satellite datasets: 32.61 dB on WorldView-3, 42.75 dB on WorldView-2, and 46.91 dB on GaoFen-2, outperforming the second-best methods (DISPNet, LGTEUN, and MSAN respectively) by 0.05 dB, 0.13 dB, and 0.90 dB. It also leads on SSIM (0.9575/0.9791/0.9872), spectral fidelity (Q8/Q4: 0.9530/0.8423/0.9122), and angular distortion (SAM: 3.44°/1.19°/1.27°). The method uses only ~0.5M parameters and 3.6 GFLOPs, placing it in the above-average efficiency range while delivering top accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RUN outperforms all 11 baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against the three classical model-based methods (GSA, SFIM, Wavelet), the gains are dramatic—over 10 dB PSNR on WV3—confirming the superiority of learned approaches. Among deep unfolding networks, RUN beats the closest competitor DISPNet by 0.05 dB PSNR on WV3 and 0.29 dB on WV2, while surpassing LGTEUN by 0.39 dB on WV2 and 1.08 dB on GF2. Against the strongest DL-based baselines, WINet and MSAN, RUN leads by 0.10 dB on WV3, 0.29 dB on WV2, and a substantial 0.90 dB on GF2. The largest margin appears on GaoFen-2 (0.90 dB over MSAN), suggesting the RPCA paradigm's sparsity-based spatial modeling is particularly effective for the higher spatial resolution and more complex textures in that sensor. On spectral fidelity (Q4/Q8) and angular distortion (SAM), RUN also consistently achieves the best values, indicating that eliminating the spatial degradation matrix does not compromise spectral preservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep unfolding network (DUN) framework for pansharpening by reframing the problem through a Robust Principal Component Analysis (RPCA) lens, building directly on the self-supervised nonlinear transform-based tensor nuclear norm (STNN) of Luo et al. [16,17]. The key departure is conceptual: rather than learning four separate networks to approximate spatial and spectral degradation matrices (and their transposes), the authors exploit the sparsity of the spatial offset component (SOC) to eliminate the spatial degradation entirely, reducing the problem to a single spectral matrix. The NTNN module further extends STNN's single nonlinear transform to a dual-transform formulation and replaces the computationally expensive SVD-based singular value thresholding with a depthwise convolution plus SoftShrink surrogate (NTSVT), which is then embedded plug-and-play into both Swin-Transformer and multi-scale CNN backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RPCA paradigm offers a principled simplification that reduces architectural design effort from four degradation networks to one, which is practically significant for practitioners working with large-scale satellite imagery where spatial degradation matrices are enormous. The NTNN/NTSVT module, with its plug-and-play compatibility with both Transformer and CNN architectures, could find utility beyond pansharpening in other low-rank tensor recovery problems. However, the sparsity assumption on the SOC is validated on only three satellite datasets with the Wald protocol, and its robustness to more complex degradation scenarios (e.g., atmospheric effects, mixed land-cover types) remains untested. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) that NTSVT preserves or reduces rank is an approximation argument rather than an exact equivalence, leaving open whether the depthwise-convolution surrogate can faithfully capture global spectral structure in all regimes.
