## Problem

Vision-language object detectors (e.g., YOLO-World, Grounding DINO) suffer severe performance degradation when applied to non-RGB modalities such as infrared, depth, event-based, and LiDAR, yet no prior work has adapted these models to new modalities while preserving their zero-shot capability. Traditional fine-tuning causes catastrophic forgetting, and existing visual prompt strategies (fixed patches, random padding, weight maps) apply the same static transformation to every image, making them ineffective under large modality shifts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251334/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single non-RGB image (IR at 640×512, depth at 640×480, event-based, or LiDAR) and a text prompt listing target object categories. The encoder-decoder produces a 3-channel pseudo-RGB image (values in [0,1]) that is element-wise added to the original input before feeding the frozen detector. The output is standard COCO-format bounding boxes with category labels; the MPDR module adds no inference overhead since it operates on precomputed text embeddings and can be zero-masked to recover exact zero-shot behavior.

## Method

ModPrompt replaces static visual prompts with an input-conditioned encoder-decoder (UNet-inspired, with MobileNet or ResNet backbones) that generates a per-image pseudo-RGB translation, trained end-to-end with the detection loss rather than a reconstruction loss. This is combined with MPDR (Modality Prompt Decoupled Residual), which adds learnable residual tokens to precomputed text embeddings so that the original embedding knowledge is preserved and can be fully recovered by zero-masking the residuals at inference. The two components are jointly optimized: the visual prompt adapts the image to a modality-appropriate representation while MPDR adapts the language side, and together they achieve near full-fine-tuning AP on LLVIP-IR (92.80 AP50 vs. 97.43) while retaining 51.90 AP50 on COCO (identical to zero-shot). The approach is backbone-agnostic and validated on both CNN-based (YOLO-World) and transformer-based (Grounding DINO) detectors across four modalities.

## Results

On LLVIP-IR, ModPrompt achieves AP50 of 92.80 (YOLO-World) and 93.13 (Grounding DINO), approaching full fine-tuning (97.43 and 97.17) while preserving zero-shot COCO performance (51.90 AP50 unchanged). On the much harder NYUv2-Depth dataset, ModPrompt lifts YOLO-World from 4.80 to 37.17 AP50 and Grounding DINO from 8.30 to 21.70 AP50, a 7–8× improvement over zero-shot. The method uses only 3.08M trainable parameters (vs. 76.81M for full fine-tuning) and outperforms all fixed visual prompt baselines (best: WeightMap at 82.00 AP50 on LLVIP-IR) by 10+ points. On broader modalities (PEDRo event-based, STCrowd LiDAR), ModPrompt reaches 92.9 and 85.7 AP50 respectively, matching or exceeding full fine-tuning while retaining zero-shot capability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HalluciDet [Medeiros et al., WACV 2024], ModPrompt outperforms on all AP metrics (AP50, AP75, AP) for the LLVIP infrared dataset (Figure 4), while also preserving zero-shot COCO knowledge that HalluciDet loses during its two-step RGB fine-tuning + translation pipeline. Against ModTr [Medeiros et al., ECCV 2024], ModPrompt again achieves superior AP across all metrics on LLVIP, and additionally extends to depth, event-based, and LiDAR modalities that ModTr does not address. Internally, ModPrompt surpasses the strongest visual prompt baseline (WeightMap) by 10.8 AP50 points on LLVIP-IR (92.80 vs. 82.00 for YOLO-World) and by 26.5 AP50 points on NYUv2-Depth (37.17 vs. 10.67), while using fewer trainable parameters (3.08M vs. 3.93M). Critically, unlike head fine-tuning (COCO AP50 drops to 0.66) and full fine-tuning (COCO AP50 drops to 0.10), ModPrompt retains the original zero-shot COCO AP50 of 51.90, yielding a 73.77 average across LLVIP and COCO versus 47.12 for HFT and 48.77 for FT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends visual prompt tuning (originally developed for VLM classification, e.g., Bahng et al. and Jia et al.) to the object detection setting, and further generalizes modality-translation approaches for detection (HalluciDet, ModTr) from traditional fixed-vocabulary detectors to vision-language open-vocabulary detectors (YOLO-World, Grounding DINO). The key conceptual departure is replacing static, input-independent visual prompts (fixed patches, random padding, weight maps) with an input-conditioned encoder-decoder (UNet-inspired) that generates a per-image pseudo-RGB translation guided by detection loss. The MPDR text-embedding mechanism adapts the task-residual idea from Yu et al. to the detection context, decoupling learnable modality-specific residuals from frozen pre-trained embeddings to preserve zero-shot language knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to adapt state-of-the-art VLM-based open-vocabulary detectors to non-RGB modalities (IR, depth, event-based, LiDAR) while preserving zero-shot capability, filling a gap between modality-adaptation methods for traditional detectors and the growing ecosystem of VLM detectors. The practical impact is significant for surveillance, autonomous driving, and robotics where IR/depth sensors are standard but annotated data is scarce relative to RGB. A notable limitation is the persistent gap to full fine-tuning on refined localization metrics (AP75, AP), particularly for small objects, and the substantially weaker results on depth (NYUv2) compared to IR suggest the input-space translation paradigm has inherent limits for very large modality shifts. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-modal fusion (e.g., RGB+IR jointly) and whether the encoder-decoder prompt can be made more efficient for real-time deployment on edge devices.
