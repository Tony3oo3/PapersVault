## Problem

Existing automated red-teaming frameworks for text-to-image models either rely on multi-model coordination without iterative feedback (ART) or use simple binary safety signals that lack visual understanding (FLIRT), leaving no efficient approach that combines structured multimodal feedback with iterative prompt optimization while maintaining prompt-level stealthiness. This paper addresses the open challenge of building a single-agent, feedback-driven red-teaming system that can systematically and adaptively probe T2I model vulnerabilities.

## Input / Output

The red-teaming VLM receives a seed prompt (or generates one autonomously in seed-free mode), the corresponding 512×512 (or 1024×1024 for DeepFloyd IF) generated image, and compliance verdicts from a suite of five image detectors and three text detectors, all conditioned on a predefined attack category. The output is a concise, tag-style adversarial prompt that passes text safety filters while inducing unsafe image generation; the process iterates up to 10 rounds, with each round producing a refined prompt fed back into the target T2I model and judge models.

## Method

FGPI employs a single fine-tuned VLM (LLaVA-1.6-Mistral-7B with LoRA) as the red-teaming agent, structured around a three-stage Chain-of-Thought cycle: Feedback (analyzing the prompt-image pair against detection results), Guide (formulating targeted revision instructions referencing five attack strategies—visual similarity, cultural references, unsafe combination, word play, and cultural deficits), and Rewrite (producing a compliant yet adversarial prompt). The VLM is fine-tuned on a novel multi-turn conversational VQA dataset of over 6,000 instances, constructed by using Meta-Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct to simulate the feedback-guide-rewrite paradigm over effective and ineffective prompt-image pairs sourced from Adversarial-Nibbler, ARTMeta-Dataset, and Unsafe-Diffusion. Ten prompt templates per stage are randomly combined to generate diverse training conversations, enabling the VLM to internalize iterative prompt optimization in a single inference pass (single-turn) while retaining multi-turn capability for harder cases.

## Results

On the 560-prompt seed dataset for Stable Diffusion 2.1, FGPI (Multi-Turn) achieves a 75.71% attack success rate (ASR) with 97.50% of generated prompts remaining text-safe, substantially outperforming FLIRT (70.00% ASR but only 54.64% safe prompts) and ART (56.43% ASR, 92.68% safe prompts). On the NSFW adversarial prompt dataset, FGPI (Multi-Turn) reaches 78.35% ASR with 97.40% prompt safety, beating SneakyPrompt (62.33% ASR, 61.04% safe) and Groot (55.41% ASR, 95.67% safe). Across five T2I models (SD 1.5, SD 2.1, SDXL, FLUX.1-dev, DeepFloyd IF), FGPI exceeds 90% ASR within five attack rounds and approaches 100% by ten rounds. In seed-free generation, FGPI reaches 88.57% ASR at five rounds on SD 2.1, and on DALL-E 3 it achieves 85.71% ASR at three rounds while reducing request rejections from 12.14% to 6.43%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FGPI outperforms all four baselines on the combined metric of attack success rate and prompt safety. Against FLIRT, FGPI gains ~7 percentage points in ASR (75.71% vs. 70.00%) while nearly doubling the safe-prompt rate (97.50% vs. 54.64%), indicating FLIRT's prompts frequently trigger text safety filters. Against ART, FGPI improves ASR by ~19 points (75.71% vs. 56.43%) while maintaining comparable prompt safety (97.50% vs. 92.68%), showing that ART's multi-model pipeline without iterative feedback is less effective. On the NSFW dataset, FGPI (Multi-Turn) beats SneakyPrompt by 16 ASR points (78.35% vs. 62.33%) and 36 points in safe-prompt rate (97.40% vs. 61.04%), and outperforms Groot by 23 ASR points (78.35% vs. 55.41%) with a marginal safety advantage. Single-turn FGPI performs nearly on par with multi-turn in most settings, suggesting the fine-tuned VLM internalizes the iterative optimization process, making single-turn inference more cost-efficient without significant quality loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

FGPI consolidates the multi-model red-teaming pipelines of ART (which coordinates LLMs, VLMs, and detectors) and the simple binary-feedback loop of FLIRT into a single fine-tuned VLM operating under a structured feedback-guide-rewrite paradigm inspired by Chain-of-Thought reasoning. It departs from token-perturbation methods (SneakyPrompt) and tree-based transformations (Groot) by generating natural-language, interpretable prompts through iterative visual-textual analysis rather than surface-level modifications. The construction of a multi-turn conversational VQA dataset (6,000+ instances) to fine-tune the red-teaming agent is a new contribution not present in prior red-teaming work, which relied on in-context learning or multi-model orchestration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FGPI provides a practical, scalable tool for systematically stress-testing T2I safety mechanisms, with the seed-free capability removing dependence on curated adversarial prompt collections. The >90% attack success rate within five iterations and >80% cross-model transferability suggest that current safety filters in open-source diffusion models remain substantially vulnerable to structured, iterative prompt optimization. Open questions include whether the fine-tuned VLM's strategies generalize to video generation or multimodal models, whether the detector-based evaluation of 'prompt safety' captures all meaningful compliance dimensions, and whether the high ASR reflects genuine model vulnerabilities or artifacts of the specific detector suite used.
