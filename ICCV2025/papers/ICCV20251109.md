## Problem

Remote photoplethysmography (rPPG) signal extraction is severely degraded by motion and illumination interference in real-world settings, yet existing methods lack 4D geometric understanding to explicitly separate physiological signals from motion and specular noise at the source. Two specific barriers prevent applying Generalizable Gaussian Models (GGM) to rPPG: rPPG datasets contain no camera intrinsics or extrinsics, making 4D Gaussian rendering infeasible, and no prior work has explored unsupervised explicit decoupling of chroma components in 4D Gaussian space.

## Input / Output

The method consumes facial video converted into a Spatial-Temporal Map (STMap), where face patches (detected via FAN 2D landmarks) are flattened into 3xN vectors per frame and stacked over T frames (window 256, step 10). Beyond the standard BVP, HR, and HRV outputs, the model produces a full 4D Gaussian Map (22xNxT) with per-point depth, scale, rotation, opacity, and three disentangled chroma channels (specular, physiological, motion noise), which can be re-rendered into video via the virtual camera for qualitative verification.

## Method

RhythmGaussian repurposes a Generalizable Gaussian Model by introducing a 4D virtual camera with fixed intrinsic parameters (face center as focal point, half-face-size focal length) and identity extrinsics, eliminating the need for real camera parameters while encoding depth through Gaussian scale and in-plane shifts through the v-coordinate. A Gaussian Adapter predicts per-point motion flow [dh, dw, ds] (Explicit Motion Modeling), which warps the 4D point set to track inter-frame displacement and is constrained by L1 reconstruction on both original and motion-corrected renderings. Explicit Chroma Modeling then decouples the color channel into specular (vs), physiological (vd), and motion-noise (vn) components using three unsupervised constraints: spatio-temporal consistency loss enforcing periodicity and cross-region similarity on vd, a negative Pearson correlation loss tying vn magnitude to the predicted optical flow, and pixel-wise reconstruction loss anchoring vs. The full pipeline is trained end-to-end with an encoder shared between a physiological decoder (BVP/HR head) and the GS Adapter, under a combined loss with weights lambda_rec=0.5, lambda_st=0.4, lambda_m=0.1, where the first three terms are fully unsupervised.

## Results

On the challenging VIPL-HR dataset, RhythmGaussian achieves MAE 7.05 bpm, RMSE 10.36 bpm, and Pearson r = 0.68, outperforming the previous best (HiBa*+) by 0.29 MAE and 0.07 correlation. On V4V, it reaches MAE 9.06, RMSE 11.07, r = 0.55, improving over HiBa*+ (MAE 9.68, r = 0.42) by 0.62 MAE and 0.13 correlation. In the intra-dataset VIPL-HR evaluation (Table 3), the full model achieves MAE 4.22, RMSE 7.12, r = 0.92, surpassing NEST (MAE 4.76, r = 0.84) and Dual-GAN (MAE 4.93, r = 0.81). In the self-supervised linear-probing setting, the method attains MAE 7.24 (VIPL pretrain) and 6.43 (10-dataset pretrain), beating rPPG-MAE (MAE 7.83) without any label supervision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RhythmGaussian outperforms all 18 baselines across every evaluated dataset and metric. Traditional signal-processing methods (GREEN, CHROM, POS) are substantially outperformed, with MAE gaps of 4–8 bpm on VIPL-HR, confirming that hand-crafted subspace assumptions fail under motion. Among deep learning baselines, the closest competitor is HiBa*+ (MAE 7.34 on VIPL-HR, 9.68 on V4V); RhythmGaussian improves by 0.29 and 0.62 MAE respectively, with a notably larger correlation gain on V4V (0.55 vs. 0.42). NEST*+ and Dual-GAN*+ trail by 0.81 and 1.83 MAE on VIPL-HR, indicating that the 4D Gaussian geometric modeling provides a meaningful advantage over prior STMap-based CNNs. In the self-supervised linear-probing protocol (Table 4), the method beats all contrastive baselines (MoCo, SIMSIAM, BYOL, SIMCLR) by 1.2–2.0 MAE and outperforms the rPPG-specific rPPG-MAE by 0.59 MAE, demonstrating that the Gaussian reconstruction pretraining yields superior physiological representations without any labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Generalizable Gaussian Models (GGM), originally developed for 3D/4D scene reconstruction (LGM, LRM), into the rPPG domain for the first time, introducing a '4D virtual camera' to circumvent the absence of camera parameters in rPPG datasets. It departs from the dominant rPPG paradigm—where methods like NEST, Dual-GAN, and BVPNet operate purely in signal or feature space—by explicitly modeling 4D facial geometry and decomposing chroma into specular, physiological, and motion-noise components. The spatiotemporal consistency loss is adapted from Contrast-Phys, and the experimental protocol follows NEST's multi-source domain generalization setup, but the core architectural contribution (4D Gaussian adapter + EMM + ECM) is new to the field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Bringing explicit 4D geometric understanding to rPPG addresses a root cause of motion noise that prior methods only mitigate at the signal level, offering a more principled path to robustness in unconstrained settings. The '4D virtual camera' is a practical and elegant workaround for the missing-camera-parameter problem, making Gaussian splatting applicable to existing rPPG datasets without re-annotation. Open questions include whether the geometric modeling provides genuine inductive bias beyond a more expressive encoder, how the method scales to longer or multi-subject videos, and whether the STMap input (rather than raw video) limits the geometric fidelity. The expanded 10-dataset benchmark also provides a useful stress test for future rPPG methods under diverse motion and illumination conditions.
