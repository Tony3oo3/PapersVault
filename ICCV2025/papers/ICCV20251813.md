## Problem

3D Gaussian splatting achieves photorealistic novel view synthesis only with dense image captures, which are expensive and laborious to obtain. Existing generative approaches that augment sparse captures with synthetic views rely on noise-to-data diffusion or flow matching conditioned on reference images, leading to hallucinated details, multi-view inconsistencies, and reconstruction artifacts. The paper asks whether a generative model can instead directly refine incorrect renderings from an initial sparse reconstruction into ground-truth-quality images, avoiding the hallucination problem entirely.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a set of posed RGB images (ranging from 12 sparse to thousands of dense views) with camera intrinsics and extrinsics, plus a set of target camera poses for novel views. The flow matching model operates in a 16-channel VAE latent space, encoding each image to an h×w×16 tensor, and processes up to 45 views at 540×960 resolution (91K tokens) in a single forward pass on one H100 GPU. Camera poses are encoded as Plücker coordinate ray maps in a shared reference frame. The output is an improved 3D Gaussian splatting scene trained on both the original source images and the generated novel-view images, which are decoded back to pixel space from the latent representation.

## Method

The key insight is replacing the standard Gaussian source distribution p₀(z) in flow matching with a conditional source distribution p₀(z|y) where z₀ are renderings from an initial sparse 3DGS reconstruction, so the model learns a velocity field that directly corrects imperfect renderings toward ground-truth images rather than generating from noise. The velocity field is implemented as a 1.75B-parameter multi-view diffusion transformer initialized from a 2.7B text-to-image model (with text layers removed), using 2×2 patch tokens, 1D sinusoidal view-index encodings for long-context inference, and a modified DiT block with two self-attention layers (image-level and full multi-view) connected by zero-initialized linear projections to preserve pre-trained weights. The robust initial reconstruction pipeline combines MASt3R dense matching on a co-visibility graph, COLMAP triangulation, monocular depth alignment for metric scale, and short 5k-step 3DGS training with adaptive density control. The model is trained on 3.6M rendered/ground-truth image pairs from 10.3k scenes across DL3DV-10K and ScanNet++, and at inference generates N consistent novel views simultaneously that are incorporated into a refined 3DGS fit using a separate L2+SSIM+LPIPS loss for generated views to tolerate VAE compression artifacts.

## Results

On the DL3DV140 sparse-view benchmark, FlowR achieves 22.43 PSNR / 0.766 SSIM / 0.280 LPIPS (12-view) and 25.13 PSNR / 0.836 SSIM / 0.212 LPIPS (24-view), outperforming InstantSplat (20.47/0.698/0.297 at 12-view) and ViewCrafter (19.19/0.638/0.375 at 12-view) by a significant margin. On the dense-view ScanNet++ benchmark, FlowR reaches 24.11 PSNR / 0.870 SSIM / 0.303 LPIPS, surpassing GANeRF (23.95/0.856/0.306). On Nerfbusters, FlowR attains 18.31 PSNR / 0.607 SSIM with 0.932 coverage, beating Nerfbusters (17.99/0.606/0.250) in PSNR and SSIM while maintaining higher coverage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowR consistently outperforms all baselines across all three benchmarks. On DL3DV140 (12-view), FlowR beats InstantSplat by +1.96 PSNR and −0.017 LPIPS, and ViewCrafter by +3.24 PSNR and −0.095 LPIPS; notably, InstantSplat's performance actually degrades from 12 to 24 views (20.47→19.57 PSNR), while FlowR improves (22.43→25.13). On ScanNet++ (dense-view), FlowR surpasses GANeRF by +0.16 PSNR and −0.003 LPIPS, and with post-hoc refinement (FlowR++) the gap widens to +0.89 PSNR and −0.041 LPIPS over GANeRF w/ GAN. On Nerfbusters, FlowR outperforms the original Nerfbusters method in PSNR (+0.32) and SSIM (+0.001) while maintaining 0.932 coverage versus Nerfbusters' 0.630; with opacity thresholding (FlowR*), the PSNR gap grows to +0.95 and LPIPS drops to 0.181 vs. 0.250. The Splatfacto baseline (vanilla 3DGS) is substantially outperformed in all settings, confirming that the generative densification step provides meaningful gains beyond standard reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowR builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting as the scene representation and rectified flow matching as the generative framework, but departs from prior generative-view approaches (ViewCrafter, InstantSplat, CAT3D) that use a standard Gaussian noise-to-data process conditioned on reference views. The key conceptual shift is conditioning the source distribution itself on the reference views—using actual renderings from a sparse 3DGS reconstruction as p₀(z|y) rather than N(0,I)—so the learned velocity field directly corrects reconstruction errors instead of hallucinating content from noise. The reconstruction pipeline additionally integrates MASt3R for dense 3D point initialization, COLMAP for triangulation, and monocular metric depth for scale alignment, combining recent SfM advances into a scalable pipeline that handles arbitrary view counts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck for VR/AR applications: dense multi-view captures are expensive and laborious, yet 3DGS quality degrades sharply with sparse inputs. By generating consistent, view-aligned augmentations that are used as auxiliary training data, FlowR narrows the sparse-to-dense quality gap without requiring additional captures. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to dynamic scenes or very large-scale environments, whether the 1.75B-parameter model can be distilled for on-device use, and whether the conditional-source formulation transfers to other scene representations beyond 3DGS. The 3.6M-pair dataset and the robust reconstruction pipeline are also independently useful resources for the community.
