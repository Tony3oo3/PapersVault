## Problem

Existing class-agnostic motion prediction methods on BEV cell grids perform per-pixel regression, neglecting structured motion field priors such as rigidity constraints, temporal consistency, and inter-agent interactions. This leads to degraded performance in sparse and distant regions where point cloud data is inherently limited, and simple convolutional decoders cannot capture the physical regularities embedded in the motion field ground truth.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251004/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 5 synchronized LiDAR point cloud frames cropped to a 64m×64m×5m range and voxelized at 0.25m×0.25m×0.4m resolution. It produces a 20-frame future BEV motion field (per-cell 2D displacement), per-cell classification labels across N_C categories, and a static probability map. During training only, the dense Motion Field Ground Truth (MFGT) is additionally fed into the RVpE module to extract raster and vector priors; at inference, no ground-truth motion is required since structural priors are already encoded in the learned latent space.

## Method

PriorMotion reformulates motion prediction as a generative process by integrating a Raster-Vector prior Encoder (RVpE) and a Dynamic-Static prior Generator (DSpG) into a standard BEV encoder-decoder pipeline. The RVpE distills prior knowledge from the MFGT via two parallel branches: a raster branch (1×1 convolutions for local features plus temporal/spatial self-attention for global context, fused by a learnable factor ρ) and a vector branch (per-instance trajectory sampling with sinusoidal embeddings, LSTM temporal encoding, and a 6-layer Transformer for inter-instance interaction), combined through Prior Cross-Attention with the BEV features. The DSpG then projects the prior-enhanced BEV features into a Gaussian latent space via a dual-branch VAE that disentangles dynamic and static components, followed by a Spatial GRU that autoregressively evolves the latent state over the 20-frame prediction horizon and a Squeeze-and-Excitation Feature Selection Decoder that decodes each frame's motion field. Training is supervised with a composite loss including motion, classification, state, and a KL-divergence prior loss that enforces the predicted latent distribution to match the ground-truth-conditioned distribution, ensuring the network internalizes structured motion priors even when the RVpE is absent at test time.

## Results

On nuScenes, PriorMotion with the STPN backbone reduces mean speed error by 8.92% for slow-moving objects (0.2247 vs. 0.2467 m) and 15.73% for fast-moving objects (0.8324 vs. 0.9878 m) compared to MotionNet. With the STI backbone, the reductions are 4.67% (slow) and 14.75% (fast) over BE-STI. On the newly proposed metrics, PriorMotion achieves a generalization index of 86.4% (vs. 81.6% for MotionNet), reduces motion stability variance from 0.0267 to 0.0088, and cuts distant-region (>20 m) fast-object error by 31.52% (0.8549 vs. 1.2990 m).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PriorMotion consistently outperforms all class-agnostic baselines on fast-moving object prediction: it beats MotionNet (0.8324 vs. 0.9878 m) and BE-STI (0.7739 vs. 0.9078 m) by 15.73% and 14.75% respectively, and also surpasses the augmented MotionNet‡ (0.8324 vs. 0.8427 m). For slow-moving objects, it edges out MotionNet (0.2247 vs. 0.2467 m, −8.92%) and BE-STI (0.2264 vs. 0.2375 m, −4.67%), though it trails the MGDA-enhanced BE-STI† (0.2115 m) on this sub-metric. On the novel generalization metric, PriorMotion achieves 86.4% GI with STPN (vs. 81.6% for MotionNet) and 85.4% with STI (vs. 84.3% for BE-STI), confirming that the learned latent priors transfer to unseen categories. In distant regions (>20 m), PriorMotion reduces fast-object error by 31.52% over MotionNet (0.8549 vs. 1.2990 m) and 29.0% over STI (0.7962 vs. 1.1200 m), while also cutting motion stability variance by ~67% (0.0088 vs. 0.0267). The method is compatible with both STPN and STI backbones, demonstrating that the generative prior framework is backbone-agnostic and adds only ~50 ms inference overhead (69 ms total vs. 19 ms for MotionNet alone).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the class-agnostic BEV motion prediction line (MotionNet, BE-STI, SelfMotion) by replacing the standard regression decoder with a generative VAE-based framework that models motion as a distribution in a structured latent space. It draws conceptual inspiration from prior-enhanced mapping frameworks (P-MapNet, MapPrior) that inject structured priors into perception, but repurposes this idea for motion prediction by encoding the Motion Field Ground Truth (MFGT) through a novel raster-vector dual representation. The dynamic-static decomposition and Spatial GRU for autoregressive latent evolution are new architectural choices not present in prior grid-centric motion predictors, which rely on convolutional or attention-based decoders with hand-crafted consistency losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine weakness of grid-based motion prediction—poor performance in sparse, distant regions and lack of instance-level consistency—by explicitly encoding physical priors (rigidity, temporal continuity, inter-agent interaction) into the learning process rather than hoping the encoder discovers them. The proposed evaluation metrics (generalization index, motion stability, distance-stratified error) fill a gap in how the community assesses class-agnostic predictors beyond mean speed error. Limitations include evaluation on a single public dataset (nuScenes) plus one private FMCW benchmark, a 69 ms inference time that is tight for real-time deployment, and reliance on a pretrained perception model for dynamic/static masks that may not transfer to unseen sensor configurations. Open questions include whether the generative latent space scales to longer prediction horizons or more complex multi-agent interactions, and whether the prior encoder's training-time-only role limits its utility in online or streaming settings.
