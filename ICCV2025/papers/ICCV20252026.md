## Problem

Zero-shot HOI detection must generalize to unseen action and object classes, but existing VLM-based methods face a dual failure: frozen VLM text features cluster actions involving the same object together (since VLMs are trained for object recognition, not action discrimination), while prompt-learning adaptations overfit to seen-class supervision and fail to transfer to unseen classes. No prior approach simultaneously addresses both the generalization gap and the action-distinction gap.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image, from which a pretrained DETR (ResNet-50 backbone) extracts human and object bounding boxes and their features. The language branch ingests LLM-generated (LLaMA-3) textual descriptions of HOI, action, and object classes, encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-B text encoder. The final output is a per human-object pair interaction score computed as (s_h · s_o)^τ · σ(s_a), where s_a is a weighted combination of cosine similarities between adapted visual features and adapted text features across two parallel prediction paths.

## Method

HOLa decomposes VLM text features via low-rank factorization into a shared basis matrix B (d×m, m=71) and per-class weight vectors W (N×m), with L1 sparsity on W and orthogonality constraints on B to enforce disentangled, class-shared representations. A text adapter (self-attention → cross-attention with image features → residual) refines W for action distinction while keeping B frozen, and LLM-derived action regularization constrains the adapted weights via KL divergence against independently decomposed action features, injecting generalizable action knowledge beyond seen-class supervision. In the vision branch, human-object tokens (averaged human+object features plus spatial features from paired bounding boxes) are fed alongside image patches into the VLM visual encoder to enrich interaction representations. A text fusion module concatenates reconstructed action features with object text features through self-attention to capture action-object context (e.g., riding a bus vs. riding an elephant). Training combines focal-loss action classification, a semantic loss, and the decomposition losses (reconstruction, sparsity, orthogonality, and KL regularization) into a single objective.

## Results

On the HICO-DET unseen-verb setting, HOLa achieves a new state-of-the-art unseen-class mAP of 27.91 and HM of 31.09, surpassing the previous best (CMMP) by +1.68 mAP on unseen classes and +1.96 on HM. In the unseen-object setting, HOLa reaches 36.45 unseen mAP (+2.69 over EZ-HOI), and in the non-rare first unseen-composition setting it achieves 35.25 unseen mAP (+1.59 over EZ-HOI). HOLa also leads on seen-class mAP (35.09) and full mAP (34.09) in the unseen-verb setting, indicating balanced seen-unseen performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HOLa outperforms all eight baselines across every metric in all four zero-shot HOI settings. In the unseen-verb setting, the closest competitor is CMMP (26.23 unseen mAP), which HOLa surpasses by 1.68 mAP; EZ-HOI (25.10) and CLIP4HOI (26.02) trail by 2.81 and 1.89 mAP respectively. In the unseen-object setting, ADA-CM and CMMP (both ~33.3–33.8 unseen mAP) are the strongest baselines, but HOLa leads by 2.69 mAP over EZ-HOI (33.28) and 2.69 over CMMP (33.76). In the non-rare first unseen-composition setting, EZ-HOI is the nearest baseline at 33.66 unseen mAP, with HOLa ahead by 1.59 mAP. Notably, HOLa also achieves the highest seen-class mAP (35.09) in the unseen-verb setting, whereas CLIP4HOI and LogicHOI, which rely on frozen VLM features, score lower on both seen and unseen classes, confirming that the low-rank decomposition and LLM-derived regularization improve generalization without sacrificing seen-class accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

HOLa carves new ground at the intersection of two existing lines of zero-shot HOI detection: frozen-VLM alignment methods (HOICLIP, CLIP4HOI, GEN-VLKT) that struggle with action distinction, and VLM prompt-adaptation methods (CMMP, EZ-HOI, ADA-CM) that fail to generalize to unseen classes. Rather than building directly on either, the paper repurposes low-rank factorization—a concept well-established in parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA)—to decompose VLM text features into class-shared basis and per-class adaptable weights, then introduces LLM-generated action descriptions as a regularization signal via KL divergence. This combination of low-rank decomposition with LLM-derived regularization and human-object tokens is architecturally novel for the HOI setting, though each individual component draws from established ideas in PEFT and vision-language adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HOLa provides a principled mechanism for balancing generalization (shared basis features preserving unseen-class information) against specificity (adaptable weights refined by action supervision), addressing a core tension in zero-shot HOI detection that prior methods handled ad hoc. The LLM-derived action regularization offers a template for incorporating external linguistic knowledge without requiring training annotations for unseen classes, a strategy potentially transferable to other zero-shot recognition tasks. Limitations include evaluation primarily on HICO-DET, dependence on LLM-generated descriptions whose quality may bottleneck at scale, and the method's complexity (multiple loss terms, two decomposition stages, text fusion, and human-object tokens) which may hinder adoption. Open questions include whether the low-rank decomposition approach generalizes to larger class taxonomies or other interaction detection benchmarks beyond HICO-DET.
