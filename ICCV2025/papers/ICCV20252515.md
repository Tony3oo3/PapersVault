## Problem

Existing agricultural VLM datasets (Agri-LLaVA, AgroInstruct, CDDM) rely on GPT-4-generated synthetic QA pairs for training, leaving no reliable, expert-validated benchmark to assess whether VLMs truly possess agricultural knowledge across diverse disease, pest, weed, and crop categories. AgroBench addresses this gap by providing a comprehensive evaluation benchmark with human agronomist annotations covering a state-of-the-art range of 682 disease, 134 pest, 108 weed, and 203 crop categories.

## Input / Output

Each QA pair pairs an agricultural image (sourced from real farm settings under Creative Commons licenses) with a five-option multiple-choice question; for Weed Identification, a bounding box is overlaid to disambiguate the target species among co-occurring weeds. The model outputs a single letter (A–E), and correctness is determined by exact matching against the option letter or full answer sentence. The overall score is the unweighted mean across the seven tasks, preventing categories with more QA pairs from dominating the aggregate metric.

## Method

AgroBench comprises 4,342 expert-annotated QA pairs across seven tasks (Disease Identification, Pest Identification, Weed Identification, Crop Management, Disease Management, Machine Usage QA, Traditional Management), all manually created by a Ph.D.-level agronomist using textbooks and academic journals, with GPT restricted to sentence rephrasing only. The evaluation protocol uses exact matching on five-option multiple-choice answers, and the paper benchmarks 12 VLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-4o mini, Gemini 1.5 Pro/Flash, LLaVA-Next 8B/72B, QwenVLM 7B/72B, CogVLM-19B, EMU2Chat, LLaVA 7B/13B) under zero-shot, text-only, and Chain-of-Thought settings. A manual error analysis of GPT-4o failures categorizes mistakes into Lack of Knowledge (51.92%), Perceptual Error (32.69%), Reasoning Error (7.6%), and Other (7.79%), revealing that domain-specific agricultural knowledge is the primary bottleneck for current VLMs.

## Results

On AgroBench's seven agricultural VLM tasks, GPT-4o achieves the highest overall accuracy of 73.45% (79.26% on the human-validated subset), followed by Gemini 1.5-Pro at 72.24% and QwenVLM-72B (best open-source) at 70.66%. Weed Identification (WID) is the most challenging task, where most open-source VLMs score near the random baseline of 17.90% (e.g., LLaVA-Next-8B: 30.05%, CogVLM-19B: 25.45%), while the best model (Gemini 1.5-Pro) reaches only 55.17%. Disease Identification (DID) consistently scores lower than Disease Management (DMN) across all models, indicating a perceptual gap in fine-grained visual recognition versus contextual reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o leads all 12 evaluated models with 73.45% overall accuracy, outperforming the second-best Gemini 1.5-Pro (72.24%) by 1.2 points and the best open-source model QwenVLM-72B (70.66%) by 2.8 points. All four closed-source VLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-4o mini, Gemini 1.5-Pro, Gemini 1.5-Flash) surpass every open-source VLM on overall accuracy, with the gap ranging from ~11 points (GPT-4o vs. QwenVLM-72B) to ~33 points (GPT-4o vs. EMU2Chat). On Weed Identification, Gemini 1.5-Pro (55.17%) and Gemini 1.5-Flash (50.90%) substantially outperform GPT-4o (44.17%) and all open-source models (≤34.48%), suggesting that open-source VLMs lack sufficient weed-specific knowledge. For Disease Identification, GPT-4o (64.18%) leads, but the gap to Gemini 1.5-Pro (62.92%) is narrow, while open-source models trail by 5–35 points. Notably, all models score well above the human baseline (36.79% on the subset) and the random baseline (19.03%), yet even the best model achieves only ~55% on WID, confirming that fine-grained agricultural identification remains an open challenge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two lines of work: general-purpose VLM benchmarking (MMMU, MMStar) and agricultural computer vision (PlantDoc, Plant Village, IP102). It departs from prior agricultural VLM datasets (Agri-LLaVA, AgroInstruct, CDDM) that relied on GPT-4-generated synthetic QA pairs for training, instead introducing an expert-annotated evaluation benchmark. The key conceptual departure is shifting the focus from training data generation to rigorous, human-validated evaluation across a much broader category space (682 diseases, 203 crops, 108 weeds, 134 pests) and seven distinct task types, including management and traditional practices that prior agricultural VLM work largely ignored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AgroBench fills a critical gap by providing the first expert-validated, multi-task agricultural VLM benchmark, enabling systematic diagnosis of where current models fail. The finding that open-source VLMs perform near-random on weed identification, and that over half of all errors stem from knowledge gaps rather than perceptual failures, points toward domain-specific fine-tuning as the most promising path forward. The text-only ablation revealing that management tasks can be answered above chance without visual input raises questions about whether the benchmark truly tests visual understanding or merely general agricultural common sense. Open questions include whether targeted agricultural pre-training or retrieval-augmented approaches can close the identified knowledge gaps, and whether the benchmark's multiple-choice format adequately captures the open-ended nature of real farming decisions.
