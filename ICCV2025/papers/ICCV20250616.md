## Problem

CLIP-based VLMs struggle to adapt to new domains without expensive fine-tuning, and existing test-time adaptation methods either require gradient-based prompt tuning or rely on limited-capacity caches that cannot capture the full test distribution. A critical underexplored challenge is dynamically estimating test-domain statistics from streaming data arriving one sample at a time, compounded by CLIP's inter-modal contrastive objective that leaves vision-space similarity poorly optimized, resulting in large intra-modal variance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250616/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes test images sequentially (batch size 1, no backpropagation) along with hand-crafted text prompts for K class names, producing a single class label per image. The streaming constraint means the model must maintain and update a compact database of K Gaussian centers (means, covariances, and pseudo-labels) online, with no access to batch statistics or the full test set. The final output is a K-dimensional logit vector combining CLIP's original prediction with retrieval-augmented scores from both vision and multimodal spaces, weighted by a scalar α.

## Method

TT-RAA introduces two complementary modules. First, a Streaming Mixture of Gaussian Database (SMGD) maintains one Gaussian component per class, updating its mean and covariance via exponential moving average only when an incoming sample's CLIP prediction entropy is lower than the current center's entropy, thereby retaining only high-confidence statistics with minimal storage. Second, a Multimodal Retrieval Augmentation (MRA) module addresses CLIP's intra-modal misalignment by projecting Gaussian centers into a shared text-image space via sigmoid(Z^T G), then computing KL divergence between the test sample's projected distribution and each class center's projected distribution. The vision-space retrieval combines cosine-similarity-based lookup with Gaussian discriminant analysis scores, while the multimodal-space retrieval uses negated KL divergences; both are summed and added to CLIP's original logits with a weighting factor α to produce the final prediction.

## Results

TT-RAA achieves 68.76% average top-1 accuracy on the 10-dataset cross-domain benchmark and 63.96% on the 4-dataset out-of-distribution (ImageNet variants) benchmark, both evaluated with CLIP ViT-B/16 under a single-image (batch size 1) streaming test-time setting. This represents a +4.17% improvement over the CLIP zero-shot baseline (64.59% CD, 59.42% OOD) and outperforms the strongest training-free baseline (ZERO at 67.72% CD, TDA at 63.89% OOD). The method is entirely training-free, requiring no gradient updates or backpropagation during inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against training-based methods (CoOp, CoCoOp, TPT, DiffTPT), TT-RAA achieves a consistent 3–4% average advantage on the cross-domain benchmark (68.76% vs. 63.88–65.47%), despite requiring no gradient updates or task-specific training. Among training-free baselines, TT-RAA outperforms the strongest competitor ZERO (67.72%) by 1.04% on the cross-domain average and TDA (63.89%) by 0.07% on the OOD average. The most dramatic individual win is on EuroSAT (remote sensing), where TT-RAA reaches 66.12% versus TDA's 58.00% (+8.12%), attributed to SMGD's ability to capture satellite imagery distribution. On fine-grained tasks, TT-RAA leads Flower102 (72.68% vs. CoCoOp's 70.85%) and UCF101 action recognition (71.29% vs. TDA's 70.66%). TT-RAA does not dominate every single dataset (e.g., Caltech101: 94.08% vs. TDA's 94.24%; Food101: 86.09% vs. DiffTPT's 87.23%), but its consistent gains across the full benchmark yield the best overall average.

## Relation to Prior Work

TT-RAA builds directly on CLIP's contrastive vision-language pre-training and the Tip-Adapter similarity function, positioning itself within the training-free test-time adaptation (TTA) lineage for VLMs. It departs from cache-based methods (TDA, DMN) that store individual samples with limited capacity, and from distribution-based methods (MTA, DN) that use simpler statistics, by introducing a streaming mixture of Gaussians that dynamically tracks per-class distributional structure with minimal storage. The multimodal retrieval augmentation module is a conceptual departure from prior TTA work, explicitly addressing CLIP's known intra-modal similarity gap (citing the modality gap analysis of Liang et al.) by projecting retrieval into a shared vision-text space rather than operating purely in vision space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, streaming (batch-size-1) design makes TT-RAA practical for real-world deployment where backpropagation is infeasible and data arrives sequentially, addressing a gap that training-based TTA methods (TPT, DiffTPT) cannot fill for large VLMs. The MRA module's insight—that CLIP's contrastive objective leaves vision-space clustering suboptimal and that multimodal-space retrieval partially compensates—offers a reusable diagnostic for any CLIP-based pipeline. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger multimodal LLMs (GPT-4V, LLaVA), whether it extends beyond classification to detection or segmentation, and how sensitive the streaming GMM is to adversarial or highly non-Gaussian test distributions.
