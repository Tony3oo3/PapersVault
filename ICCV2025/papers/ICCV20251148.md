## Problem

Existing multi-view clustering methods assume that corresponding samples across views are fully aligned, but real-world data often exhibits only partial alignment, causing significant performance degradation. Prior approaches to partially aligned data (e.g., PVC, SURE, MVC-UM) lack a unified causal framework that explicitly models the shift from fully to partially aligned data as a data distribution change, leaving the generalization gap unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251148/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view raw features (e.g., text descriptions paired with video frames, or multiple feature vectors per sample) that may be fully or partially aligned across views, along with pre-computed clustering labels r' from a DealMVC pretraining model that condition the variational encoder. It outputs soft cluster assignments via a multinomial distribution over the latent representations, with the number of clusters matching the dataset's ground-truth partition. The partially aligned scenario is simulated by randomly splitting each view's data into two 50% partitions and shuffling one partition's ordering.

## Method

CauMVC formulates multi-view clustering as a structural causal model where variant features x_va (raw inputs) and invariant features x_in (latent, drawn from a standard Gaussian prior) jointly determine variant and invariant representations e_va and e_in, which in turn produce cluster assignments r. A variational autoencoder with amortized inference estimates the invariant features from the pretraining clustering results and variant features, while a decoder network performs post-intervention inference p(r | do(x_va = x_va'), x_in) to handle partially aligned inputs. Monte Carlo sampling with a Jensen-gap approximation (averaging sampled representations before the final MLP) makes the intractable integration over e_va and e_in computationally feasible. A contrastive regularizer constrains the cosine similarity matrix between e_va and e_in to approximate the identity, pulling same-sample representations together and pushing different-sample representations apart. The overall training objective combines a reconstruction loss, an ELBO loss (with KL annealing), and the contrastive loss, optimized end-to-end with Adam.

## Results

On partially aligned data (50% alignment), CauMVC achieves the best ACC across all eight datasets, with particularly large margins: 80.99% on UCI-digit (vs. 77.30% for PVC), 70.90% on Caltech101-7 (vs. 41.52% for MVC-UM), and 56.64% on BBCSport (vs. 35.85% for MVC-UM). On fully aligned data, CauMVC also leads on all eight datasets, e.g., 91.85% ACC on UCI-digit (vs. 87.00% for MFLVC), 90.43% on Caltech101-7 (vs. 88.71% for DealMVC), and 83.09% on BBCSport (vs. 80.70% for DealMVC). The method demonstrates strong generalization, maintaining competitive performance under the data shift from fully to partially aligned settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CauMVC outperforms all 13 baselines on every dataset under both fully and partially aligned settings. Under partially aligned data (the paper's primary focus), the margins are substantial: on Caltech101-7, CauMVC achieves 70.90% ACC versus 41.52% for MVC-UM (KDD 2021) and 46.00% for PVC (NeurIPS 2020), a gap of over 25 percentage points. On UCI-digit, CauMVC reaches 80.99% ACC, surpassing PVC (77.30%) by 3.69% and MVC-UM (61.09%) by nearly 20%. On BBCSport, CauMVC (56.64%) exceeds the next-best MVC-UM (35.85%) by 20.79%. Under fully aligned data, CauMVC leads DealMVC (ACM MM 2023) by 2.39% on BBCSport, 1.72% on Caltech101-7, and 5.65% on UCI-digit, while also surpassing TGM-MVC (ACM MM 2024) and MFLVC (CVPR 2022) across the board. The consistent superiority across both alignment regimes confirms the causal framework's generalization advantage over methods that assume strict cross-view correspondence.

## Relation to Prior Work

CauMVC sits at the intersection of two research threads: partially aligned multi-view clustering (PVC, MVC-UM, SURE, MvGLN, RMCNC) and causal representation learning (CausalVAE, causal OOD recommendation). Prior partially-aligned MVC methods address misalignment through robustness mechanisms—differentiable assignment (PVC), NMF-based mapping recovery (MVC-UM), or noise-robust contrastive losses (SURE, MvGLN)—but treat the problem as a robustness challenge without a unifying generative model. CauMVC departs by reframing the fully-to-partially aligned transition as a causal intervention (do-operator) on variant features, then performing post-intervention inference via a VAE-based structural causal model. It also builds directly on DealMVC as a pretraining backbone to supply the initial clustering labels r′ that condition the variational encoder for invariant features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The causal framing provides a principled explanation for *why* MVC performance degrades under partial alignment (variant features shift while invariant features should remain stable) and a structured recipe for recovering clustering under that shift, which prior robustness-based methods lack. However, the approach depends on a pretraining model (DealMVC) to bootstrap the invariant-feature encoder, introducing a circularity that limits standalone applicability, and the 'invariant features' are estimated from the very clustering output they are supposed to stabilize. The authors themselves flag incomplete and noisy data as open extensions, and the evaluation is confined to eight relatively small tabular/image datasets, leaving scalability to large-scale or high-dimensional settings untested.
