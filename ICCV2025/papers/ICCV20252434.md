## Problem

Early-exiting networks require early stages to simultaneously provide low-level fundamental features to deep stages and high-level discriminative features to early-stage predictors, creating an inherent conflict. Prior work such as Dyn-Adapter fails to fully decouple these two abilities, resulting in suboptimal accuracy when inference cost is reduced to 30% FLOPs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252434/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images and produces a class label, but inference can terminate at any of S=4 stages (each with L=3 ViT blocks) depending on a FLOPs budget or a confidence threshold. At each stage, a predictor outputs a Softmax probability vector over classes; the final prediction comes from whichever stage the early-exit criterion is first satisfied, so the effective output is a class label with variable computational cost per sample.

## Method

DMPO decouples representative and discriminative abilities in early stages through two complementary mechanisms. Architecturally, lightweight LoRA-based bypass modules create a residual path where original features X_i are preserved for propagation to deep stages while BYP_i(X_i)+X_i feeds a high-order statistics-based predictor (multi-head convolutional cross-covariance) that extracts richer second-order information for classification. For training, a two-phase decoupled optimization (R2D) first assigns smaller loss weights to early stages (α_early < α_deep) to let deep stages acquire discriminative ability while early stages retain representativeness, then reverses the weighting (α_early > α_deep) to drive discriminative ability toward earlier stages. An adaptive inter-stage control factor σ(ℓ_{i-1})·ℓ_i further modulates deep-stage updates based on early-stage loss, preventing degradation of deep stages once early stages become sufficiently discriminative.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 and five FGVC datasets (CUB-200-2011, NABirds, Oxford Flowers, Stanford Cars, Stanford Dogs), DMPO achieves an average top-1 accuracy of 88.08% at only 5.19 GFLOPs (≈30% of full ViT-B/16) and 88.57% at 11.62 GFLOPs (≈70%). The key finding is that DMPO at 30% FLOPs suffers only a 0.49% accuracy drop relative to its 70% FLOPs variant, whereas Dyn-Adapter drops 5.13% and DyT drops 4.32% under comparable FLOPs reductions. On the VTAB-1K benchmark (19 tasks), DMPO at 30% FLOPs reaches 76.0% average accuracy, surpassing Dyn-Adapter at 70% FLOPs (75.0%) and DyT at 40% FLOPs (72.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary early-exiting PEFT baseline Dyn-Adapter [47], DMPO at 30% FLOPs (88.08% avg) outperforms Dyn-Adapter at 70% FLOPs (86.80%) by 1.28 points, and beats Dyn-Adapter at 30% FLOPs (81.67%) by 6.41 points. Against the token-pruning method DyT [49], DMPO at 30% FLOPs (88.08%) surpasses DyT at its minimum 40% FLOPs (82.60%) by 5.48 points, and also exceeds DyT at 70% FLOPs (86.92%) by 1.16 points. Against DeeBERT [41], DMPO dominates overwhelmingly: at 30% FLOPs, DMPO achieves 88.08% while DeeBERT collapses to 33.52% (a 43.68-point drop from its 70% FLOPs result). Against standard PEFT baselines Adapter [15] (86.93%) and LoRA [16] (90.78%), DMPO at 30% FLOPs (88.08%) exceeds Adapter by 1.15 points while using only ~30% of the inference FLOPs, though LoRA at full FLOPs retains a 2.7-point edge. Across all five baselines, DMPO's defining advantage is its near-zero accuracy degradation (0.49%) when reducing FLOPs from 70% to 30%, compared to 4–44 point drops in competitors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Dyn-Adapter (ECCV 2024), which first combined early exiting with parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA) for vision transformers, by identifying and resolving the core limitation that Dyn-Adapter uses identical features simultaneously for deep-stage propagation and early-stage prediction. Architecturally, it repurposes LoRA's bottleneck structure as a lightweight bypass module and adapts the multi-head convolutional cross-covariance predictor from moment probing (ICCV 2023) to early stages, while introducing a two-phase loss weight schedule (R2D) that departs from the fixed or monotonically-increasing weight strategies used by DeeBERT, MuE, CALM, and Dyn-Adapter. The method also contrasts with token-pruning approaches like DyT, which reduce representation size rather than inference depth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving ~70% FLOPs reduction with only ~0.5% accuracy drop on VTAB-1K and FGVC benchmarks, making early-exiting PEFT viable for latency-sensitive deployment where token pruning degrades out-of-distribution robustness. The decoupling principle—separating representative and discriminative roles in early stages via both architecture and optimization—offers a general design pattern that could transfer to other early-exiting or multi-branch architectures. Limitations include the additional trainable parameters (1.73M vs. 0.95M for Dyn-Adapter), evaluation restricted to ViT-family backbones and image classification, and the two-phase schedule requiring a fixed transition point. The authors note extension to NLP and multi-modal settings as future work, leaving open whether the decoupling challenge and its solution generalize beyond vision transformers.
