## Problem

Existing Active Geo-Localization (AGL) methods rely on distance-based extrinsic rewards that become unreliable when the goal location is unknown at inference time, limiting robustness and generalization to unseen targets and environments. Additionally, prior approaches only predict action sequences without explicitly modeling how actions change the observed state, preventing the agent from anticipating transitions and adapting to unfamiliar search areas.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251497/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a starting aerial image patch and a goal specified in one of three modalities (aerial image, ground-level image, or text description), and must navigate a 5×5 grid of 25 non-overlapping patches within a search budget of 10 steps. The goal's spatial location is hidden during inference, and the agent outputs a sequence of discrete actions (UP, DOWN, LEFT, RIGHT) forming a trajectory. All modalities are encoded into a shared embedding space via pre-aligned contrastive encoders (CLIP for ground images/text, Sat2Cap for aerial images).

## Method

GeoExplorer uses a three-stage pipeline: (1) modality-specific encoders map goals and environment patches into a joint embedding space; (2) a causal Transformer (Falcon-7B) is supervised to jointly predict both the optimal goal-directed action and the next state representation, trained with binary cross-entropy on actions plus MSE on state predictions; (3) a PPO actor-critic head, operating on the frozen Transformer's outputs, is trained with a combined reward that sums a distance-based extrinsic reward and a goal-agnostic curiosity-driven intrinsic reward (MSE or negative cosine similarity between predicted and observed next-state features). The key insight is that explicit state modeling enables a dense, content-aware intrinsic reward that guides exploration independently of goal location, balancing direct goal-reaching with adaptive environment discovery.

## Results

On the Masa validation set, GeoExplorer achieves a success ratio (SR) of 0.9497 at C=8, outperforming the strongest baseline GOMAA-Geo (0.7854) by 0.1643. The advantage grows with search distance: at C=4 the gap is 0.0234, while at C=6 it reaches 0.0988. On the unseen-target SwissViewMonuments dataset, GeoExplorer improves SR by 0.1433 (aerial goal) and 0.1366 (ground-level goal) over GOMAA-Geo at C=6, demonstrating strong generalization to novel targets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoExplorer consistently outperforms all four baselines across every distance setting on the Masa validation set. Against the closest competitor GOMAA-Geo [25], the improvement ranges from +0.0234 (C=4) to +0.1643 (C=8), with the largest gains at longer search paths where distance estimation is most unreliable. The gap over Decision Transformer [7] is substantial (e.g., 0.9497 vs. 0.4559 at C=8), confirming that joint action-state modeling plus curiosity-driven exploration far exceeds action-only sequence modeling. AiRLoc [21] and the PPO policy [28] remain far behind, with SR below 0.26 even at C=8, highlighting the importance of dense reward shaping. In cross-modal generalization (MM-GAG), GeoExplorer matches or exceeds GOMAA-Geo on text goals (0.4431 vs. 0.4000 at C=4) and ground-level image goals at longer distances (0.8246 vs. 0.7489 at C=7), while showing a marginal 0.0075 drop at C=4 for ground-level images. On the unseen-target SwissViewMonuments benchmark, GeoExplorer's advantage over GOMAA-Geo widens to +0.1433 (aerial) and +0.1366 (ground-level) at C=6, underscoring the robustness of curiosity-driven exploration for novel targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoExplorer extends the GOMAA-Geo framework (Sarkar et al., NeurIPS 2024), which already combined a causal Transformer for action sequence modeling with an actor-critic RL stage guided by distance-based extrinsic rewards. The key departure is twofold: (1) the causal Transformer is repurposed to jointly predict both actions and next-state representations (action-state dynamics modeling), going beyond the action-only prediction of prior AGL methods; and (2) a curiosity-driven intrinsic reward—measuring prediction error between the Transformer's state forecast and the actual observation—is added to the extrinsic distance reward, drawing directly on the self-supervised prediction literature (Pathak et al., 2017). The sequence-modeling backbone follows the Decision Transformer paradigm, while the feature encoders (CLIP, Sat2Cap) are inherited unchanged from GOMAA-Geo.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical weakness of distance-based AGL: the reward signal becomes unreliable or unavailable at inference when the goal location is unknown, which is the realistic search-and-rescue scenario. By showing that goal-agnostic curiosity rewards improve generalization to unseen targets (SwissViewMonuments) and cross-domain transfer, the work provides evidence that intrinsic exploration complements extrinsic guidance in spatial navigation tasks. Limitations include the small 5×5 search grid (25 patches), reliance on a 7B-parameter causal Transformer (Falcon-7B) that may be impractical for on-board UAV deployment, and moderate absolute gains over the strong GOMAA-Geo baseline. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger, continuous search spaces and whether the state-modeling component can be replaced by lighter-weight world models.
