## Problem

Deep learning methods for CMB signal cleaning have been proposed by multiple groups, but each uses a different dataset with different generation assumptions, lacks rigorous evaluation against astrophysics baselines, and has not been made publicly available. This inconsistency makes it impossible to compare models or to attract computer vision researchers to the problem. CMB-ML addresses this by providing a unified, reproducible pipeline for simulation, modeling, and benchmarking.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250745/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Input consists of 9 frequency-channel sky maps (30–857 GHz) pixelized on the HEALPix sphere at N_side=512 (≈3.15M pixels per map), where each channel carries a different Gaussian beam window function (different blur) and anisotropic, spatially correlated noise derived from Planck variance maps. The output is a single cleaned CMB temperature map of shape (1, N_pix). All foreground components are fully superimposed with the CMB, and the task is evaluated both in pixel space (at three beam-smoothing levels) and in harmonic space via power spectra up to ℓ_max=1024.

## Method

CMB-ML is a three-phase pipeline (simulation → modeling → analysis) that separates configuration parameters from operational code so new models can be added in parallel. The simulation phase generates realistic sky maps by combining a CAMB-produced CMB realization (from WMAP9 ΛCDM+MNU chains) with PySM3 foreground templates, applying channel-specific Gaussian beams, and adding anisotropic noise correlated via Planck noise-simulation statistics. Two baselines are packaged: CNILC (a needlet-domain internal linear combination from PyILC, operating on all detectors except 30 GHz) and cmbNNCS (a UNet8 convolutional network trained on 4 frequency channels with 120 epochs, batch size 12, and exponential LR decay from 0.1 to 1e-6). The analysis phase applies a standard post-processing procedure (beam deconvolution, masking, monopole/dipole removal) and reports pixel-space metrics (MAE, RMSE, NRMSE, PSNR) at three beam conditions alongside harmonic-space power-spectrum comparisons using ANAFAST, enabling both CV-style and cosmology-style interpretation.

## Results

On the CMB-ML-512-1450 test set (200 simulations), the ML baseline cmbNNCS achieves a MAE of 3.314±0.017 μK_CMB and NRMSE of 0.04920±0.0009 at 20.6′ beam smoothing, substantially outperforming the astrophysics baseline CNILC (MAE 6.391±0.420, NRMSE 0.1009±0.0062). At 60′ beam, cmbNNCS reaches a PSNR of 56.81±0.48 dB versus CNILC's 39.43±1.12 dB. In the deconvolved (unsmoothed) case, the gap narrows and CNILC actually achieves a slightly higher PSNR (33.29 vs. 32.72 dB), though it still lags on MAE (59.83 vs. 18.50 μK_CMB) and NRMSE (0.7492 vs. 0.2280).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks two methods: cmbNNCS [Wang et al., ApJS 2022], a UNet-based CNN trained on four frequency channels (100, 143, 217, 353 GHz) using a 3×4 rectangular rearrangement of HEALPix pixels, and CNILC [McCarthy & Hill, PRD 2024], a needlet-domain internal linear combination method that operates on all Planck detectors except 30 GHz. cmbNNCS clearly outperforms CNILC on pixel-space metrics (MAE, RMSE, NRMSE) at both 20.6′ and 60′ beam smoothing, with roughly a 2× advantage in NRMSE at 20.6′ (0.049 vs. 0.101) and a ~7× advantage at 60′ (0.013 vs. 0.094). However, in the deconvolved case CNILC achieves a marginally higher PSNR (33.29 vs. 32.72 dB), and the paper notes this comparison is not entirely fair: CNILC is a 'blind' method that does not use foreground templates, whereas cmbNNCS was explicitly trained on foreground information. In the power-spectrum domain, CNILC exhibits overshoot at ℓ > 500 due to residual point sources, while cmbNNCS shows undershoot likely from an averaging effect. The authors also highlight practical differences: CNILC is linear (enabling rigorous error propagation for cosmological parameter estimation) and operates per-map without training, while cmbNNCS is a black box requiring a full 1,000-simulation training set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not introduce a new cleaning algorithm but rather unifies the fragmented landscape of CMB signal extraction into a single, reproducible benchmark. Prior simulation tools (PSM, PySM3) and cleaning methods (NILC, ILC/PyILC, UNet-based networks like cmbNNCS, DeepSphere) each operated on different datasets with different generation assumptions and evaluation protocols, making cross-method comparison impossible. CMB-ML carves new ground by providing a standardized pipeline that decouples simulation configuration from operational code, enabling both astrophysics baselines (CNILC) and deep learning baselines (cmbNNCS) to be evaluated under identical conditions for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset addresses a critical reproducibility gap: no public, standardized CMB cleaning dataset existed, and the unique combination of spherical HEALPix pixelization, fully superimposed components, spectral correlation, and channel-varying beam effects has no direct analogue in standard CV benchmarks. A key open question is whether ML-based methods can support the error propagation needed for actual cosmological parameter estimation, since the UNet baseline is a black box while the linear CNILC method permits bias calculation. Future extensions to polarization modes, additional baselines, and ablation studies are planned but not yet realized, leaving the practical impact on cosmological inference still to be demonstrated.
