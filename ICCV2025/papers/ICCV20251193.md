## Problem

Existing MLLMs excel at generating humorous captions for memes with inherent funny content but fail to produce appealing descriptions for ordinary images that lack superficial engaging features. Two key gaps are identified: insufficient integration between visual and language clues to capture latent interest in ordinary scenes, and the absence of supervised training dedicated to distinguishing appealing from non-appealing descriptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251193/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single ordinary RGB image (sourced from GQA, COCO, or ImageNet) and produces a text description employing one of three appeal strategies—wordplay, phonetic similarity, or reversal—at one of three perspectives (single-object, multi-object, or global). Descriptions are constrained to be factually accurate (score ≥4 on informativeness, factual accuracy, appeal, and fluency) while being creative, and the framework also supports downstream tasks like visual storytelling (image sequences → narrative) and situational dialogue generation (image + dialogue history → response).

## Method

CharmNet combines three components: (1) a two-stage instruction tuning pipeline that first trains a QFormer on frozen Qwen-VL visual features, then unfreezes the encoder and fine-tunes with LoRA under multi-task learning across discrimination, selection, ranking, and generation objectives; (2) a referee model built on LLaVA-1.6 trained with a pairwise ranking loss plus a human-alignment MSE loss to output scalar appeal scores for any image-description pair; and (3) a heuristic active learning loop that iteratively identifies the hardest samples (referee score below threshold μ=0.2), retrains on them, and replaces the hard set if no validation improvement is observed, continuing until five consecutive evaluations show consistent gains. This targeted retraining on weak examples—rather than uniform sampling or reinforcement learning—yields an 11.4% improvement over the strongest baseline in global-perspective generation.

## Results

On AppealImage, CharmNet achieves 77.5% discrimination accuracy, 59.5% selection accuracy, and 83.9% ranking position consistency, surpassing GPT-4o (76.4%, 57.7%, 83.7%) on the first two tasks. In the generation task, CharmNet attains a human-rated appeal score of 4.4/5 and a referee score of 0.38, outperforming the strongest open-source baseline XMeCap (3.7/5, 0.33) by 11.4% in the DSRG global-perspective composite. CharmNet also outperforms human literary experts on both quality (4.5 vs. 4.3 human score) and speed (3.2 s vs. 30.5 s per description).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CharmNet outperforms all 11 open-source MLLM baselines across every task. Against the strongest open-source competitor XMeCap (meme captioning), CharmNet gains +10.0 points in discrimination accuracy (77.5 vs. 67.5), +16.8 in selection (59.5 vs. 42.7), and +8.9 in ranking (83.9 vs. 75.0), while also leading in all generation sub-metrics (e.g., BLEU 47.2 vs. 43.5, human appeal 4.4 vs. 3.7). Against GPT-4o, CharmNet edges ahead in discrimination (77.5 vs. 76.4) and selection (59.5 vs. 57.7) but trails slightly in ranking (83.9 vs. 83.7); GPT-4o is excluded from generation to avoid stylistic self-bias. On the creative transfer tasks, CharmNet beats Qwen-VL-7B+CLoT by +3.67 R@1 and +5.65 MRR on DialogCC, and reduces the overall distance metric d_HM from 0.069 to 0.045 (−53.3%) on VIST visual storytelling. The weakest baselines (InstructBLIP-7B, Otter-I, MiniGPT-4-7B) score below 45% in discrimination and below 3.0 in human-rated generation appeal, highlighting the difficulty of producing appealing descriptions without dedicated training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a distinct niche between two well-studied lines of work: accurate image captioning (Rotstein et al., Dwibedi et al., Li et al.) and multi-modal humor generation for inherently funny content like memes (Zhong et al., Chen et al., Wang and Lee). Rather than building directly on any single prior method, it repurposes standard instruction-tuning pipelines (QFormer + LoRA, following Li et al.) and active learning into a newly formulated problem: generating *appealing* (not merely accurate or humorous) descriptions for *ordinary* images. The three appeal strategies (wordplay, phonetic similarity, reversal) draw inspiration from meme theory but are explicitly decoupled from the need for inherent visual humor, distinguishing this work from XMeCap, MemeCraft, and Leap-of-Thought approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap—making ordinary images engaging for social media and brand marketing—by providing both a large-scale dataset (74,942 images) and a four-task benchmark with quantitative metrics, which the field previously lacked. The finding that a relatively lightweight framework (instruction tuning + heuristic active learning + a referee model) can outperform GPT-4o on discrimination/selection and beat human literary experts on generation quality and speed is encouraging for cost-effective deployment. However, open questions remain: the ground-truth descriptions are generated by GPT-4o, introducing potential stylistic bias; the evaluation of 'appeal' is inherently subjective and the referee's reliability, while validated (Pearson r > 0.8), may not generalize beyond the three defined strategies; and personalization of appeal to different audiences is left as future work.
