## Problem

Existing multi-view diffusion methods for image-to-3D generation (e.g., Zero123, Zero123++) synthesize all target views simultaneously with equal priority, producing geometric and textural inconsistencies—especially when camera poses deviate significantly from the input view. The paper identifies that views closer to the input inherently exhibit higher generation fidelity, yet prior work underutilizes this sequential relationship between views of varying distance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252144/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (white background, resized to 128–512 px) and produces six target views arranged in a 3×2 grid (320×640 px) with fixed camera poses: interleaving elevations of 20° and −10° paired with azimuths at 30°, 90°, 150°, 210°, 270°, and 330° relative to the input. These six views are then fed to a pre-trained InstantMesh large reconstruction model to yield a textured 3D mesh. Training renders use 7 images per object (1 input + 6 targets) from ~210K Objaverse objects.

## Method

AR-1-to-3 reformulates multi-view generation as a three-step autoregressive next-view prediction over the rows of the 3×2 grid, generating views from near to far so that earlier (closer) views condition later (farther) ones. Two conditioning strategies encode the partial view sequence: Stacked-LE runs each reference view through the denoising UNet, records key/value matrices at every self-attention layer, and concatenates them along the spatial dimension to augment the current step's K/V matrices for pixel-wise spatial guidance; LSTM-GE extracts CLIP image features, splits them into two elevation-based subsequences, encodes each with a separate LSTM, and projects the concatenated hidden states through an MLP and trainable weights to form the cross-attention global condition. The model is fine-tuned from Zero123++ (Stable Diffusion with v-prediction and linear noise schedule) using AdamW with cosine-annealing warm restarts over 150k steps at batch size 32 on 8 A100 GPUs, with the autoregressive step k randomly sampled from {1,2,3} during training.

## Results

On the GSO dataset, AR-1-to-3 achieves PSNR 13.18, SSIM 0.709, LPIPS 0.232, Chamfer Distance 0.063, and F-Score 0.258, substantially outperforming the next-best method (SyncDreamer at PSNR 10.82, F-Score 0.125) by a wide margin. On the Omni3D dataset, AR-1-to-3 leads all baselines with PSNR 10.25, SSIM 0.629, LPIPS 0.388, CD 0.148, and F-Score 0.097, though the gaps are narrower than on GSO. The method wins on every reported metric across both out-of-domain benchmarks, with the largest relative gains in 3D geometric accuracy (F-Score more than doubles the runner-up on GSO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AR-1-to-3 outperforms all four quantitative baselines (Michelangelo, SyncDreamer, LGM, InstantMesh) on every metric across both GSO and Omni3D, with the most dramatic gains on GSO where PSNR improves by 2.36 dB over SyncDreamer and F-Score more than doubles (0.258 vs. 0.135 for InstantMesh). In qualitative novel-view synthesis (Fig. 5), AR-1-to-3 produces more consistent views than Zero123-XL, Zero123++, SyncDreamer, and One-2-3-45, which all exhibit geometric or textural inconsistencies at large camera pose differences. In image-to-3D comparisons (Fig. 7), AR-1-to-3 generates meshes with more plausible geometry and consistent appearance than InstantMesh, One2345++, TripoSR, and Unique3D; for instance, InstantMesh and One2345++ hallucinate an extra storage cabinet on a desk, while SyncDreamer produces significant geometric deformations due to inconsistent multi-view inputs. The ablation on sequence order (Table 3) further confirms that the near-to-far autoregressive strategy is critical: random ordering degrades PSNR from 20.28 to 17.36 and CLIP-Score from 0.887 to 0.774, while reverse ordering performs nearly as well as the normal order due to the circular camera trajectory.

## Relation to Prior Work

AR-1-to-3 builds directly on Zero123++'s 3×2 grid multi-view diffusion framework and Stable Diffusion backbone, but departs from the simultaneous-generation paradigm by introducing an autoregressive next-view prediction strategy that generates views sequentially from near to far. The autoregressive concept is borrowed from image generation (PixelRNN, VQ-GAN, Parti) and 3D mesh generation (MeshGPT, SAR3D), but its application to multi-view synthesis for 3D reconstruction is novel. The closest prior work is CascadeZero123, which uses a two-stage cascade, but AR-1-to-3 is more systematic in exploiting the sequential structure of the grid layout and in encoding both local (Stacked-LE) and global (LSTM-GE) context from previously generated views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the simple principle of generating reliable near views first and using them as context for harder far views yields substantial consistency improvements over simultaneous generation, offering a practical recipe for improving multi-view diffusion pipelines. However, the approach is still tied to the fixed Zero123++ 3×2 grid and relies on a separate InstantMesh model for 3D reconstruction, limiting its generality. Open questions include whether the autoregressive paradigm extends to arbitrary view counts or non-grid camera arrangements, and whether the near-to-far ordering assumption holds for all object categories and camera configurations.
