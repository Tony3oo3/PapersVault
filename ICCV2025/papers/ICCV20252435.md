## Problem

Existing sampling-based methods for 6D pose confidence region estimation suffer from two critical limitations: inference speed degrades as the number of samples increases, and the resulting confidence regions are excessively large due to low-quality sampled poses inflating the convex hull. This paper addresses both issues by replacing stochastic sampling with a deterministic propagation mechanism that yields compact, statistically guaranteed confidence regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252435/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (H×W×3) and produces a 6D pose estimate (Euler angles + translation) along with two types of confidence regions: 2D keypoint confidence regions as ball regions centered at each predicted keypoint with radius scaled by the covariance determinant and a conformal threshold, and 6D pose confidence regions as ellipsoids in rotation (Euler angle) and translation space. All regions are parameterized by a user-specified error rate ε, with the guarantee that the ground truth is covered with probability at least 1−ε.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages: (1) a CNN regresses N Gaussian distributions over 2D semantic keypoints, trained with a negative log-likelihood loss that incorporates aleatoric uncertainty via the covariance term; (2) inductive conformal prediction (ICP) calibrates these Gaussians into 2D keypoint confidence regions using a nonconformity score φ = ‖x̃−x‖/det(σ), producing ball regions whose radius scales with both the predicted uncertainty and the calibration quantile; (3) a single-shot PnP solver yields the 6D pose, and the implicit function theorem is applied to the PnP optimality conditions to analytically compute the Jacobian ∂g/∂x, enabling direct covariance propagation (Σ_y = J Σ_x Jᵀ) from keypoint to pose space. This eliminates the need for repeated PnP evaluations on sampled keypoints, producing ellipsoidal pose confidence regions that are up to 99.9% smaller in volume than sampling-based counterparts while maintaining comparable coverage rates.

## Results

On the LineMOD Occlusion (LMO) dataset, the proposed deterministic method achieves 74.45% keypoint reprojection accuracy (vs. 67.33% for Yang et al. [9] at ε=0.1) and 90.65% keypoint coverage, while reducing mean translational confidence region volume from 109.9×10⁻³ m³ to 8.6×10⁻³ m³ (92.2% reduction) and rotational volume from 252.2×10³ deg³ to 91.3×10³ deg³ (63.8% reduction). On the occlusion-free SPEED dataset, the gains are dramatic: translational volume drops from 287.8×10⁻³ m³ to 0.7×10⁻³ m³ (99.8% reduction) and rotational volume from 210.6×10³ deg³ to 0.2×10³ deg³ (99.9% reduction), while accuracy reaches 97.09% (vs. 57.80% for [9]). Inference time is reduced by 50% for keypoint detection and ~33% for pose confidence region estimation compared to the heatmap-based baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Yang et al. [9] (CVPR 2023), which uses ICP on heatmaps for keypoint confidence regions and a sampling-based PnP to propagate to 6D pose. The proposed method outperforms [9] on all key metrics: higher keypoint accuracy (74.45% vs. 67.33% on LMO; 97.09% vs. 57.80% on SPEED), comparable keypoint coverage (90.65% vs. 91.16% on LMO), and dramatically smaller confidence region volumes (up to 99.9% reduction in rotation and 99.8% in translation on SPEED). The sampling-based [9] method frequently exceeds the volume threshold (1070 out of 1214 LMO images for rotation; 920 out of 12000 SPEED images for translation), effectively failing to produce useful compact regions. A secondary baseline, PVNet [11], is compared only on keypoint accuracy and is outperformed on both datasets (61.06% on LMO, 57.46% on SPEED). An ablation variant ([9]+Det.) that applies the same IFT propagation to [9]'s heatmap-based keypoint regions confirms that the volume reduction comes primarily from the deterministic propagation step rather than the keypoint regression change alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the ICP-based 6D pose confidence region framework of Yang et al. (CVPR 2023), which first introduced conformal keypoint detection with sampling-based propagation to pose-level confidence regions. The key departure is twofold: (1) replacing heatmap-based keypoint detection with deterministic regression of Gaussian keypoint distributions (following Wang et al.), and (2) replacing the computationally expensive sampling-and-convex-hull propagation with a closed-form Jacobian derived via the implicit function theorem (building on the differentiable PnP formulation of Chen et al., CVPR 2020). The result is a fully deterministic pipeline that produces ellipsoidal pose confidence regions rather than the irregular convex hulls of the sampling approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For safety-critical applications (robotic grasping, satellite pose estimation), the practical utility of a confidence region depends on both coverage and compactness; this paper demonstrates that the sampling-based baseline produces rotation regions so large they almost universally exceed a 90°×90°×90° threshold, rendering them nearly useless for decision-making. The 99.9% volume reduction with comparable coverage is a significant practical improvement. However, the authors acknowledge that the Gaussian assumption on the 6D pose may not hold under the non-linear 2D-to-6D mapping, which could undermine the finite-sample coverage guarantees that ICP provides at the keypoint level. An open question is whether the deterministic propagation preserves the conformal guarantee end-to-end or whether the linearization error in the IFT step introduces systematic under-coverage in high-occlusion or high-ambiguity scenarios.
