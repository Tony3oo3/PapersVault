limitations: The authors note that DEDA's performance declines as the number of distilled samples per class increases (e.g., IPC=100), indicating a trade-off between class-specific information retention and diversity enhancement that remains unresolved. surprise_factor: The 12–14% absolute improvement over SRe2L on CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet is a large gap for a method that adds no extra model training, but it is a natural consequence of replacing a lossy statistic (BN mean/variance) with a richer one (full covariance), so the magnitude is impressive rather than conceptually surprising. ## Problem

Existing large-scale dataset distillation methods (SRe2L, LPLD) align only the mean and variance of batch normalization layers, ignoring the covariance matrix that encodes inter-feature correlations, which limits the diversity of distilled data. Furthermore, the last layer of a well-converged pre-trained model exhibits highly compact intra-class features (cosine distance < 0.1), so aligning distilled data to those features produces low-diversity samples that cause gradient starvation during downstream training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251748/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an original large-scale RGB image dataset (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64, ImageNet-1K at 224×224) and a pre-trained ResNet-18 model as input. The pre-trained model is loaded only once to extract per-class Gaussian statistics (mean vectors and covariance matrices) at each intermediate layer via spatially average-pooled features, which are stored in a memory bank. The output is a compact distilled dataset of IPC synthetic images per class (IPC=10/50/100), accompanied by soft labels generated by the pre-trained model for downstream training.

## Method

DEDA operates in two stages: (1) Offline Gaussian Distribution Estimation, where per-class mean and covariance matrices are computed from average-pooled features of the pre-trained model at each layer and cached in a memory bank; (2) Distilled Data Optimization, where synthetic samples are iteratively updated to match the stored Gaussian distributions. The core loss L_GDA aligns both means and covariances across layers 1 through L−1 (weighted by hyperparameter γ), going beyond BN statistics to capture inter-feature relationships. To counteract the compactness of the last layer, a Diversity-Enriched Regularizer is applied: L_Var uses a hinge loss to maximize diagonal covariance elements (feature variance), and L_Cov minimizes squared off-diagonal elements (feature decorrelation). The total objective is L_CE + L_GDA + λ₁·L_Var + λ₂·L_Cov, optimized class-by-class without requiring the original data during the distillation loop.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with IPC=50 (ResNet-18), DEDA achieves 65.1% top-1 accuracy, outperforming SRe2L (52.2%) by 12.9% and LPLD (not reported for this setting). On Tiny-ImageNet IPC=50, DEDA reaches 55.2% versus SRe2L's 41.1% (+14.1%). On ImageNet-1K with ResNet-18 IPC=50, DEDA attains 56.3%, surpassing SRe2L (46.8%) by 9.5% and LPLD (55.4%) by 0.9%. Cross-architecture evaluation on CIFAR-100 IPC=50 shows DEDA generalizes to ResNet-50 (63.7%), MobileNetV2 (55.6%), and VGG-16 (52.3%), consistently outperforming SRe2L by 10–19 points across all unseen architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEDA decisively outperforms SRe2L across all datasets and IPC settings: +11.0% on CIFAR-10 IPC=10, +12.7% on CIFAR-10 IPC=50, +12.9% on CIFAR-100 IPC=50, and +14.1% on Tiny-ImageNet IPC=50. Against LPLD (the strongest large-scale baseline), DEDA wins on Tiny-ImageNet IPC=50 (55.2 vs 48.8) and IPC=100 (59.3 vs 53.6), and on ImageNet-1K ResNet-18 IPC=10 (36.4 vs 34.6) and IPC=50 (56.3 vs 55.4), though LPLD retains a narrow edge at IPC=100 on ResNet-18 (59.4 vs 58.4) and ResNet-50 (65.7 vs 65.4). Among ConvNet-128-based classical methods, MTT achieves 71.6% on CIFAR-10 IPC=50, exceeding DEDA's 60.2%, but DEDA surpasses all ConvNet methods on CIFAR-100 IPC=50 (65.1 vs MTT's 47.7) and Tiny-ImageNet IPC=50 (55.2 vs MTT's 28.0), demonstrating superior scalability. On ImageNet-1K with ResNet-101, DEDA achieves 64.9% at IPC=100, outperforming SRe2L (62.8%) by 2.1 points, while LPLD is not evaluated at this architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the decoupled large-scale dataset distillation paradigm introduced by SRe2L (Yin et al., NeurIPS 2023), which aligns distilled data to pre-trained model BN statistics, and LPLD (Xiao & He, NeurIPS 2024), which refines this to intra-class BN alignment. The key conceptual departure is the argument that mean-and-variance (BN) statistics are insufficient to capture feature distributions, motivating full Gaussian alignment (mean + covariance) across multiple intermediate layers. Additionally, the paper introduces a covariance regularizer targeting the last layer specifically to counteract the well-known feature-space compactness (neural collapse) in well-converged models, a problem previously identified in self-supervised learning literature (ViCReg) but not addressed in the dataset distillation context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in providing a computationally efficient (no extra model training) method that substantially improves large-scale distillation quality, with strong cross-architecture generalization that reduces the need for multi-architecture optimization. The covariance regularizer offers a simple, interpretable remedy for gradient starvation in downstream training. However, the authors note performance degradation at IPC=100, suggesting the class-specific alignment strategy may not scale well to larger distilled sets, and the method's reliance on a single pre-trained teacher model limits its applicability when no such model is available. Open questions include whether the Gaussian assumption holds for all layer types (e.g., attention layers in transformers) and whether the approach transfers to non-visual modalities.
