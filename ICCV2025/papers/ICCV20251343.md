## Problem

Existing VLM-based walking assistance for visually impaired people relies on small, non-public Q&A datasets without a standardized benchmark, and struggles with real-time streaming video due to excessive response redundancy and high inference cost. No unified framework exists that proactively generates concise, context-aware walking reminders from continuous video rather than passively answering explicit queries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251343/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

WalkVLM consumes a streaming video at 2 FPS (source 1080p–4K, 60 fps) with a sliding window of N=3 historical frames, augmented by open-vocabulary object detection results (category + pixel position) from a generic detector, and an optional free-form text question from the user. The model outputs a hierarchical chain-of-thought sequence (perception attributes → scene summary → final reminder or QA answer) and a binary trigger state that gates whether the full VLM inference is invoked at the current frame, effectively suppressing redundant reminders.

## Method

WalkVLM is built on MiniCPM-V2.6 (8B, Qwen2-7B backbone) fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 64) on the newly introduced Walking Awareness Dataset (12k video-annotation pairs). The core architectural contribution is a CoT-based Hierarchical Planning (CHP) pipeline that decomposes reminder generation into three semantic levels—perception (static scene attributes plus Priori-Object Location Module filtering key obstacles by size and confidence), comprehension (global scene summary), and decision (final concise reminder or QA response)—so the model reasons step-by-step before emitting output. A second key module, Temporal-Aware Adaptive Prediction (TAP), uses a lightweight ConvNext3D encoder over the last N frames concatenated with embedded historical trigger states to predict a three-level danger probability, deciding whether to invoke the full VLM and thereby cutting temporal redundancy. This dual mechanism—hierarchical CoT for content quality and TAP for temporal efficiency—enables real-time streaming guidance with significantly fewer redundant prompts than baseline VLMs.

## Results

On the Walking Awareness Dataset (WAD), WalkVLM achieves the best performance across nearly all metrics on both the Reminder and QA tasks. For the Reminder task, WalkVLM scores 0.166 TF-IDF, 0.191 ROUGE-1, and 1.103 GPT Score, outperforming the strongest fine-tuned baseline (*Qwen2-VL at 0.147 TF-IDF, 0.163 ROUGE-1, 1.018 GPT Score). For the QA task, WalkVLM leads with 0.202 ROUGE-1 and 4.168 GPT Score, surpassing *Qwen2-VL (0.196 ROUGE-1, 3.246 GPT Score). On temporal redundancy (TRF), WalkVLM achieves 0.505, the highest among all tested models, indicating the most efficient reminder triggering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WalkVLM outperforms all six baselines on the Reminder task across TF-IDF, ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, ROUGE-L, and GPT Score, with the closest competitor being the fine-tuned *MiniCPM-V2.6 (8B) at 0.152 TF-IDF vs. WalkVLM's 0.166. On the QA task, WalkVLM leads in ROUGE-1 (0.202), ROUGE-2 (0.051), ROUGE-L (0.174), and GPT Score (4.168), but trails *Qwen2-VL (7B) on TF-IDF (0.189 vs. 0.246), which the authors attribute to WalkVLM's optimized conciseness potentially omitting detail. GPT-4o, the strongest zero-shot baseline, is substantially outperformed by WalkVLM on both tasks (e.g., GPT Score 1.103 vs. unavailable for GPT-4o on Reminder; 4.168 vs. unavailable on QA), and also on temporal redundancy (TRF 0.505 vs. 0.430). Among open-source baselines, the fine-tuned *Qwen2-VL (7B) is the strongest competitor on QA TF-IDF, while WalkVLM's advantage is most pronounced on GPT Score (4.168 vs. 3.246), reflecting superior overall output quality as judged by an LLM evaluator.

## Relation to Prior Work

WalkVLM extends the line of VLM-based walking assistance (VizWiz, Zain et al., Yang et al.) by shifting from a reactive Q&A paradigm to proactive streaming-video reminder generation. It builds directly on MiniCPM-V2.6 with LoRA fine-tuning, but departs from prior work in two architectural ways: a CoT-based hierarchical planning pipeline (perception → comprehension → decision) that enforces conciseness, and a Temporal-Aware Adaptive Prediction module using ConvNext3D that gates VLM inference to reduce temporal redundancy. The 12k-pair WAD dataset is the first large-scale, publicly available, standardized benchmark for this task, filling a gap where prior datasets were small, self-constructed, and non-public.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The WAD benchmark and WalkVLM's TAP module address the two practical bottlenecks that have kept VLM walking assistance out of real deployment: lack of a unified evaluation and per-frame inference cost in streaming video. Limitations include a conciseness-accuracy tradeoff in QA (TF-IDF lags GPT-4o), a closed-loop study with only three VIPs, and 12% of surveyed VIPs flagging redundancy or inaccuracy risk. Open questions include whether the six reminder categories and three QA types fully capture real-world VIP needs, and whether TAP's three trigger levels generalize to higher-speed or indoor navigation.
