## Problem

While LLM internal states have been shown to encode overall truthfulness of generated responses, it remains unclear whether LVLM hidden states can serve as reliable per-token hallucination indicators, whether different LVLMs share universal hallucination patterns in common latent subspaces, and whether such signals can be exploited for practical, transferable object hallucination mitigation at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251928/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a free-form text prompt (e.g., 'Please describe this image in detail') and produces a text caption with reduced object hallucination. Unusually, the intervention operates entirely at inference time by intercepting the LVLM's decoding loop: it reads the middle-layer (layer 16) hidden state of the token immediately preceding each candidate object token, scores it with a lightweight 3-layer MLP, and conditionally rewrites the generation trajectory by backtracking to low-confidence trigger words and substituting alternative tokens.

## Method

TruthPrInt is a two-stage inference-time framework. First, it learns a 'truthful direction' by training a 3-layer MLP detector on per-token hidden states collected from the token preceding each object token, labeled as hallucinated or truthful based on reference captions; the key finding is that these states achieve near-20 Likelihood Ratio for Positive Results (LR+) at FPR=0.01, making them high-specificity hallucination indicators despite limited overall accuracy. Second, during decoding, when the detector flags a potential hallucinated object, TruthPrInt backtracks within the current sentence to locate the preceding token with the lowest top-1 confidence (the hypothesized trigger word) and replaces it with the model's second-best candidate, repeating up to N_B=5 iterations. To enable cross-model and cross-dataset transfer, CommHallu performs unsupervised subspace alignment: it computes PCA eigenvectors (top d'=64) separately for source and target domain hidden states, constructs an alignment matrix M = K_S^T K_T, and projects both domains into a shared subspace so that a detector trained on one LVLM generalizes to others without retraining.

## Results

On the COCO CHAIR benchmark, TruthPrInt achieves CHAIR_S of 16.87 (MiniGPT-4), 10.33 (Llava-v1.5), and 11.13 (mPLUG-Owl2), outperforming the strongest baseline HALC by 12–14% in CHAIR_S and over 2% in CHAIR_I across all three LVLMs. TruthPrInt simultaneously improves caption quality, reaching BLEU scores of 17.21, 19.79, and 18.82 respectively—nearly 2 points above all baselines. On the offline POPE benchmark, TruthPrInt achieves the best Precision (93.10 on MiniGPT-4, 92.66 on Llava-v1.5, 92.62 on mPLUG-Owl2) and F_β scores, confirming the high-specificity design. On non-Llama backbones, TruthPrInt reduces CHAIR_S from 12.0 to 6.2 (QWen2-VL) and from 13.2 to 3.2 (InternVL-2.5) compared to greedy decoding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TruthPrInt outperforms all seven baselines across every LVLM and every metric in the CHAIR evaluation. Against the strongest baseline HALC, TruthPrInt reduces CHAIR_S by 12–14% (e.g., 16.87 vs. 24.47 on MiniGPT-4, 10.33 vs. 18.27 on Llava-v1.5) and CHAIR_I by 2–2.5 points, while also improving BLEU by ~1.4–2.2 points. The closest method in spirit, Nullu (which also operates in latent space), is outperformed by 4.5–9.9 points in CHAIR_S and 1.1–1.5 points in CHAIR_I, with TruthPrInt additionally preserving caption quality (BLEU 17.21 vs. 14.81 on MiniGPT-4). Contrastive decoding methods (VCD, OPERA, DoLa) and post-processing methods (Woodpecker, LURE) all show larger CHAIR_S values and lower BLEU, confirming that their reliance on specific biases or heavy auxiliary models limits their effectiveness. On non-Llama backbones (Table 3), TruthPrInt reduces CHAIR_S from 12.0 to 6.2 on QWen2-VL and from 13.2 to 3.2 on InternVL-2.5, substantially outperforming HALC (9.2 and 10.1 respectively) and demonstrating strong cross-architecture transferability via CommHallu.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work showing that LLM internal states (hidden states) encode truthfulness information, but shifts the focus from response-level uncertainty quantification to per-token hallucination detection in LVLMs. It departs from prior LVLM-specific approaches that rely on statistical proxies (attention patterns, RoPE decay) by explicitly training a detector on labeled internal states, and from Nullu's approach of editing the model away from a hallucination subspace by instead performing inference-time pre-intervention that preserves benign generation quality. The CommHallu subspace alignment mechanism draws on the observation that different LVLMs share common latent semantic structures, applying a PCA-like projection to enable cross-model and cross-dataset transfer of the hallucination detector.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that LVLM hidden states serve as high-specificity per-token hallucination indicators (LR+ ≈ 20 at FPR=0.01) despite limited overall discriminative accuracy opens a practical path for targeted, low-false-alarm intervention during decoding. The cross-model transferability via CommHallu reduces the need to retrain detectors for each new LVLM, making the approach more deployable. However, the method is validated primarily on image captioning; whether the truthful direction generalizes to other hallucination types (attribute, event) or to longer-form generation remains open. The heuristic nature of the backtracking mechanism (selecting second-best candidates) also limits theoretical guarantees on when intervention will succeed.
