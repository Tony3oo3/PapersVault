## Problem

Existing proactive perturbation methods against Deepfake suffer from visual degradation due to direct pixel-level addition, remain largely ineffective against face swapping specifically, and depend on white- or grey-box access to generative models during training. The paper further argues that prior work protects the wrong image: in face swapping, the source identity (the face being swapped in) is the true victim, not the target image being edited.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251630/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 256×256 RGB source facial image and produces a visually indistinguishable perturbed image in which the identity embeddings extracted by face recognition tools are substantially shifted. Unlike prior approaches that add a fixed perturbation tensor element-wise to pixels, NullSwap embeds the perturbation at the feature level through a reconstruction pipeline, preserving spatial structure while altering identity-relevant representations. The perturbed image is then vulnerable to no specific face swapping model; it simply causes any downstream identity extractor to retrieve an incorrect embedding.

## Method

NullSwap comprises four modules: an Identity Extraction block (SE-ResNet bottleneck layers) that produces identity features in matrix form, a Perturbation Block that generates identity-guided perturbations with adaptive random noise (Eq. 1), a shallow Feature Block for local image features, and a Cloaking Block that fuses the perturbation with image features via concatenation and multi-scale deconvolutional reconstruction to embed the signal invisibly. The key training innovation is Dynamic Loss Weighting (DLW), which adaptively balances cosine-similarity identity losses from multiple face recognition tools (ArcFace, FaceNet) by combining per-loss variance over a sliding window with relative progress between iterations, avoiding the instability of fixed or averaged weighting. Training is entirely black-box: no face swapping generative model participates; only face recognition tools provide gradient signals for the identity loss, combined with MSE, LPIPS, and a from-scratch discriminator loss for visual fidelity.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ, NullSwap reduces average top-1 face recognition accuracy from 0.885 (clean) to 0.565, outperforming all baselines (best baseline Initiative: 0.867). On the face swapping nullification task, NullSwap achieves average identity cosine similarity of 0.334 (ArcFace) and 0.330 (VGGFace) across five swapping models, compared to 0.769/0.731 for the strongest baseline DF-RAP and ~0.88/0.87 for others. Visual quality is also superior: PSNR 41.31, SSIM 0.9864, LPIPS 0.0049, versus PSNR ≤39.38, SSIM ≤0.9544, LPIPS ≥0.0200 for all baselines. Cross-dataset validation on LFW confirms generalizability with average top-1 accuracy of 0.278.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NullSwap decisively outperforms all four baselines on identity cloaking. Against Initiative (AAAI 2021, grey-box), NullSwap reduces average top-1 accuracy from 0.867 to 0.565 (a 34% relative drop), while Initiative barely moves the needle (0.885→0.867). Anti-Forgery (IJCAI 2022, grey-box) achieves 0.857 average top-1, still far above NullSwap's 0.565. CMUA (AAAI 2022, white-box) performs worst among baselines at 0.856, showing its universal watermark is ineffective against face recognition. DF-RAP (IEEE TIFS 2024, grey-box) is the closest competitor at 0.855 average top-1, but on the face swapping task (Table 3) it only nullifies SimSwap (0.468/0.431) while failing on other models (≥0.85), whereas NullSwap consistently achieves ≤0.40 similarity across all five swapping models. Notably, several baselines (Initiative, CMUA) even show *higher* accuracy than clean images on some tools, indicating their perturbations can inadvertently aid recognition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of proactive perturbation defenses (Initiative, Anti-Forgery, CMUA, DF-RAP) on two conceptual axes: it targets the source identity rather than the target image, and it operates entirely in a black-box training regime without any generative model. Architecturally, it repurposes SE-ResNet bottleneck blocks and a GAN discriminator from the broader adversarial-perturbation and image-reconstruction literature, but reorganizes them into an identity-guided pipeline (ID Extraction → Perturbation Block → Feature Block → Cloaking Block) that embeds perturbations at the feature level rather than via direct pixel-wise addition. The Dynamic Loss Weighting mechanism is a novel training strategy with no direct predecessor in the Deepfake defense literature, drawing loosely on multi-task learning weight-scheduling ideas.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reframing of the protection target from the edited (target) image to the identity-providing (source) image aligns the defense with the actual victim in real-world Deepfake scandals, and the black-box training removes the impractical requirement of access to specific generative models. However, the evaluation is limited to 256×256 still images on two datasets, five face swapping models, and two training-time face recognizers; robustness to video, social-network compression, and future face swapping architectures remains untested. An open question is whether the identity-cloaking signal survives the additional degradations (JPEG, video encoding, resolution changes) that real-world distribution introduces, and whether the approach generalizes to face swapping pipelines that do not rely on explicit identity embedding extraction.
