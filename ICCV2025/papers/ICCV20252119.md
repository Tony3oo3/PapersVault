## Problem

Existing multimodal cancer survival prediction methods primarily fuse histology whole slide images with genomic data, leaving pathology reports—an essential, routinely generated clinical resource containing expert-driven diagnostic insights such as tumor grading, staging, and biomarker status—largely untapped. A further challenge is severe modality imbalance: WSIs contain billions of pixels, pathology reports are short variable-length texts, and gene expression is a flat vector of scalars, causing data-rich modalities to dominate fusion and degrade predictive performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three inputs per patient: (1) H&E-stained WSIs (gigapixel, tiled into non-overlapping patches and encoded by a frozen PLIP vision-language model), (2) free-text pathology reports (variable length, tokenized and encoded by a frozen text encoder), and (3) RSEM-normalized, log2-transformed RNA-Seq expression vectors (~4000 genes) mapped onto 50 MSigDB Hallmark pathway masks. The output is a single continuous survival risk score per patient, trained under a Cox proportional hazards framework and evaluated via C-Index on disease-specific survival across six TCGA cancer cohorts.

## Method

PS3 converts each modality into a fixed-size set of prototype tokens to equalize token counts before fusion: pathology reports are compressed via a Transformer self-attention mechanism that scores and selects the top-N_T most diagnostically relevant text segments; WSIs are summarized by fitting a Gaussian Mixture Model (soft assignment, EM-optimized) over patch embeddings, yielding C prototypes whose (π, μ, Σ) parameters form the slide representation; and gene expression is projected into 50 pathway prototypes via element-wise multiplication with binary Hallmark pathway masks, each passed through a dedicated self-normalizing network. All prototypes are projected into a shared embedding space, concatenated into one multimodal sequence, and processed by a Transformer that computes nine attention sub-blocks—three intra-modal (self-attention) and six inter-modal (cross-attention)—with a single row-wise softmax over all key tokens per query modality. Post-fusion, each modality's tokens are layer-normalized, averaged, concatenated, and passed through a linear head to produce the final risk score. This prototyping strategy mitigates modality dominance while preserving biologically and clinically meaningful structure, enabling balanced cross-modal interaction that outperforms both MIL-based and non-prototype multimodal baselines.

## Results

PS3 achieves the highest average C-Index of 0.699 across six TCGA cancer cohorts (BLCA, LUAD, KIRC, STAD, CRC, HNSC), outperforming the next-best multimodal baseline MMP_OT (0.655) by 6.72% and the best unimodal baseline PS3_p (0.652) by 7.21%. PS3 attains the top C-Index in 5 of 6 cancer types, with particularly strong results on CRC (0.826) and KIRC (0.776). All methods surpass the clinical-only baseline (0.561) by at least 1.4%, confirming the added value of histology, transcriptomics, and pathology reports over standard clinical variables.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PS3 outperforms all 11 baselines on the average C-Index across six TCGA cohorts. Among multimodal baselines, PS3 (0.699) surpasses the closest competitors MMP_OT (0.655) and MMP_Trans (0.653) by 4.4 and 6.7 percentage points respectively, and leads FSM (0.639), CMTA (0.627), MOTCat (0.625), SurvPath (0.621), MCAT (0.620), and SurvivMIL (0.569) by even wider margins. Among unimodal baselines, PS3 exceeds the best gene-only method PS3_p (0.652) by 4.7 points, TransMIL (0.623) by 7.6 points, and CLAM (0.600) by 9.9 points. Notably, PS3 achieves the highest C-Index in 5 of 6 individual cancer types (BLCA, LUAD, KIRC, CRC, HNSC), with the largest single-cohort gain on CRC (0.826 vs. 0.689 for MMP_OT/MMP_Trans). The ablation further confirms that the three-modal configuration (h, p, t) outperforms any two-modality subset, with the histology+text combination (0.644) lagging PS3 by 8.54% and pathways+histology (0.653) by 7.04%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multimodal prototyping framework introduced by Song et al. (ICML 2024, MMP) and Song et al. (CVPR 2024), which established GMM-based histological prototypes and pathway prototypes for WSI–genomics survival prediction. The key departure is the addition of pathology reports as a third modality, represented through novel 'diagnostic prototypes' constructed via self-attention over text segments to standardize variable-length reports into fixed-size embeddings. The PS3 transformer generalizes the two-modal cross-attention fusion of MMP into a nine-attention-mechanism architecture (three intra-modal + six cross-modal) that explicitly models all pairwise interactions among the three prototype sets, addressing the modality imbalance that arises when gigapixel-scale WSIs are fused with concise textual summaries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Pathology reports are a routinely generated, clinically mandated resource that has been largely ignored in computational survival prediction; demonstrating that they contribute meaningfully (the p,h→p,t ablation drops only 1.72% while p,h→p,h drops 6.58%) validates their inclusion and opens a practical pathway for integrating existing clinical text into prognostic pipelines without requiring new data collection. The prototype-based balancing strategy is a reusable technique for any heterogeneous multimodal setting where token counts differ by orders of magnitude. Open questions include generalizability beyond TCGA (single-institution, retrospective cohorts), whether the diagnostic prototype mechanism transfers to other clinical text types, and whether the approach holds under prospective, multi-center validation with diverse reporting styles.
