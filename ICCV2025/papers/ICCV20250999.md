## Problem

Existing Novel Class Discovery (NCD) methods suffer from two critical limitations: they operate exclusively on single-view data, ignoring the increasingly prevalent multi-view settings (e.g., multi-omics in disease diagnosis), and they rely on pseudo-labels to supervise novel class clustering, which is highly sensitive to data noise and feature dimensionality. This paper presents the first framework to address NCD in a multi-view setting while eliminating the need for pseudo-labels entirely.

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled dataset D_l (n_l samples, k_l known classes) and an unlabeled dataset D_u (n_u samples, k_u novel classes), where both datasets share V views with feature dimensions d_v per view, and the class sets are disjoint. The number of novel clusters k_u is assumed known a priori. The output is a binary cluster assignment matrix Y_u ∈ {0,1}^{k×n_u} where each column is a one-hot vector indicating the predicted cluster for each novel sample. Supervision comes solely from the true labels of known classes, not from any pseudo-label generation step.

## Method

The core mechanism operates at two levels. At the intra-view level, each view's concatenated feature matrix [X_v^l, X_v^u] is decomposed via constrained matrix factorization into a shared orthonormal basis matrix W_v ∈ R^{d_v×k} (k = k_l + k_u) and a factor matrix Z_v, where the shared basis captures distributional consistency between known and novel sets. At the inter-view level, the factor matrices are further decomposed into view-specific centroid matrices A_v and one-hot predicted labels Y, with learnable view weights α_v that are dynamically adjusted by the supervised loss between predicted labels Y_l and ground-truth labels G_l of known classes. A negative disjointness term in the objective (−λ₂ Σ‖g^i − y^j‖²) explicitly prevents novel samples from collapsing into known class labels. The four-variable optimization (W_v, A_v, Y, α) is solved by alternating closed-form updates: SVD for W_v, linear-system solve for A_v, per-sample argmin for Y, and Cauchy-Schwarz normalization for α, with a proven monotonically decreasing objective guaranteeing convergence.

## Results

IICMVNCD achieves the best ACC on all eight datasets, with particularly large margins on Cora (76.36% vs. 63.20% for the best MVC baseline AEVC) and Wiki (65.42% vs. 64.31% for LMVSC). On the multi-omics BRCA dataset, it reaches 98.79% ACC and 90.45% NMI, surpassing the strongest MVC method LMVSC (98.18% ACC, 87.81% NMI). The NCD baselines (IIC, CKD) are consistently outperformed by both MVC methods and IICMVNCD, with CKD trailing by 14.4 points on Cora ACC (35.23% vs. 76.36%) and 29.4 points on Wiki NMI (35.10% vs. 66.44%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IICMVNCD outperforms all eight baselines on every dataset across ACC, NMI, and Purity. Among the six MVC baselines, the strongest competitors are LMVSC (best on BRCA ACC at 98.18%), AEVC (best on KIPAN ACC at 91.67%), and OPMC (best on STL10 ACC at 98.95%), but IICMVNCD surpasses each by 0.6–13.2 points depending on the dataset. The two NCD baselines (IIC and CKD), originally designed for single-view image data, are substantially weaker: CKD trails IICMVNCD by 14.4 ACC points on Cora (35.23% vs. 76.36%) and 29.4 NMI points on Wiki (35.10% vs. 66.44%), while IIC is even further behind on most datasets. Notably, the NCD methods are often outperformed by the MVC baselines themselves, underscoring that simply adapting single-view NCD pipelines to multi-view data (via concatenated MLP features) is insufficient. The largest absolute gains over the best MVC baseline occur on Cora (+13.16 ACC) and Wiki (+1.11 ACC, +16.46 NMI), where the inter-view weight guidance and shared basis matrix provide the most benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the matrix factorization-based multi-view clustering framework (Liu et al., 2013) by introducing a shared view-specific basis matrix that jointly decomposes both known and novel class features, leveraging the distributional similarity assumption central to NCD. It departs from existing one-stage NCD methods (e.g., IIC, CKD) that rely on pseudo-labels for novel class supervision, instead using true labels of known classes to directly guide view-weight learning and label prediction. The closed-form SVD solution for the basis matrix update is borrowed from Wang et al. (2019), and the overall alternating optimization structure follows the MFMVC lineage, but the inter-view weight adaptation via supervised loss on known classes is a novel coupling not present in prior multi-view clustering or NCD work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first multi-view NCD framework, this paper opens a practically important direction for domains like multi-omics disease diagnosis where single-view data is insufficient and pseudo-label-based NCD is unreliable. The theoretical convergence guarantee distinguishes it from most NCD methods, and the avoidance of pseudo-labels addresses a well-known instability issue. However, the evaluation is limited to 8 datasets, the NCD baselines are adapted from single-view methods (replacing backbones with per-view MLPs) rather than being native multi-view NCD approaches, and the assumption that k_u is known a priori restricts real-world applicability. Open questions include whether the distributional similarity assumption holds in truly heterogeneous multi-view settings and how to automatically select hyperparameters from known-class performance.
