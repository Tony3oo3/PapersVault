## Problem

Existing face swapping methods struggle to simultaneously preserve source identity and target motion without inadvertently transferring identity information from the target face. 3DMM-based approaches face a fundamental trade-off between identity loss and reconstruction loss, while diffusion-based methods lack precise, disentangled motion guidance and cannot easily extend to temporally consistent video swapping.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252290/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single source face image and a sequence of target face video frames (or a single target image), all resized to 512×512. 3DDFA-V3 extracts 3D facial parameters (identity shape, pose, expression, albedo) from both inputs, which are then recombined to render four spatially aligned 2D conditions. For video, 16-frame segments are processed with FusionTVO handling longer sequences via overlapping segment fusion, producing a face-swapped video where source identity is transferred while target expression, pose, hair, background, and lighting are preserved.

## Method

DynamicFace builds on Stable Diffusion's latent UNet with AnimateDiff temporal attention layers, introducing four disentangled face conditions derived from 3D facial priors: a background inpainting mask, a shape-aware normal map (source identity shape + target pose/expression), expression landmark segmentation (eyebrows, eyes, eyeballs, mouth), and a blurred UV texture map for illumination-only guidance. A Mixture-of-Guiders architecture (lightweight convolutions + self-attention + fusion net with zero-convolution) injects these conditions into the denoising UNet. Identity is preserved through two complementary modules: Face Former (ArcFace embedding + querying transformer for high-level cross-attention injection) and ReferenceNet (trainable UNet replica with spatial attention for fine-grained texture transfer). For video consistency, FusionTVO is a plug-and-play inference module that applies position-weighted latent fusion at segment boundaries and temporal Total Variation regularization via Half-Quadratic Splitting, eliminating flicker without retraining. Training proceeds in two stages: 260K steps adapting the spatial modules to the face domain, followed by 40K steps fine-tuning temporal attention on 16-frame sequences.

## Results

On the FaceForensics++ (FF++) benchmark, DynamicFace achieves the best identity retrieval score (99.20%), the lowest mouth position error (1.69), and the lowest eye motion error (0.16) among all compared methods. Its pose error (1.73) is slightly higher than SimSwap (1.05), which the authors attribute to DynamicFace altering face shape and affecting the shape-sensitive head pose estimator. Expression error (3.08) is competitive with SimSwap (2.85) and DiffSwap (3.05), while substantially outperforming Face Adapter (4.15) and Deepfakes (5.02).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynamicFace outperforms all six baselines on identity retrieval (99.20% vs. 98.69% for Face Adapter, 98.50% for SimSwap, and 91.40% for Deepfakes), mouth position error (1.69 vs. 2.39 for SimSwap and 4.08 for Face Adapter), and eye motion error (0.16 vs. 0.22 for SimSwap and 0.23 for Face Adapter). The only metric where DynamicFace trails is pose error (1.73 vs. 1.05 for SimSwap), which the authors note is due to their method altering face shape in a way that confuses the shape-sensitive pose estimator. DiffSwap, the other diffusion-based baseline, shows a dramatically lower ID retrieval score (17.21%), indicating severe identity leakage. Among GAN-based methods, SimSwap is the strongest competitor, leading on pose and expression, while DynamicFace dominates on identity fidelity and fine-grained motion (mouth, eyes). Face Adapter, the most recent diffusion-based method, is competitive on identity (98.69%) but lags significantly on motion fidelity (mouth 4.08, expression 4.15).

## Relation to Prior Work

DynamicFace builds on the latent diffusion framework of Stable Diffusion and the temporal attention mechanism of AnimateDiff, combining them with 3D facial priors from 3DDFA-V3 to create a unified image-and-video face swapping pipeline. It departs from prior GAN-based swappers (Deepfakes, SimSwap, FaceShifter) that relied on pixel-level warping and blending, and from earlier diffusion-based swappers (DiffSwap, DiffFace, Face Adapter) that lacked video capability or sufficient motion control. The key conceptual departure is the explicit decomposition of the target face into four spatially-aligned, mutually disentangled conditions (background, shape-aware normal map, expression landmarks, identity-erased UV texture), each routed through a dedicated guider in a Mixture-of-Guiders architecture, rather than the single-condition or entangled-condition designs used in prior 3DMM-guided methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving state-of-the-art identity retrieval (99.20%) and the best motion-fidelity scores on FF++ while extending seamlessly to video, DynamicFace offers a practical, high-quality face swapping tool that addresses the long-standing identity-vs-motion trade-off. The disentangled-condition design and the plug-and-play FusionTVO module are reusable components that could inform other conditional diffusion tasks. Limitations include a slightly elevated pose error (attributed to face-shape alteration confusing the pose estimator), reliance on a fine-tuned 3D reconstruction model whose accuracy bounds the quality of all four conditions, and evaluation limited to 512×512 resolution and 16-frame video segments, leaving open questions about scalability to longer sequences, higher resolutions, and more diverse identity distributions.
