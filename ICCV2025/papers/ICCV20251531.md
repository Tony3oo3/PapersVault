## Problem

Supervised fine-tuning on chain-of-thought data trains MLLMs to imitate only successful reasoning paths without understanding flawed ones, and directly applying outcome-level RL (e.g., GRPO from DeepSeek-R1) to MLLMs suffers from severe sparse reward issues—most generated reasoning paths from smaller models fail to reach correct answers, yielding very few positive reward signals and unstable training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251531/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a multimodal question (an RGB image paired with a textual task instruction) and produces a structured step-by-step reasoning trajectory followed by a final answer. The expected reasoning structure comprises three components: a background analysis (image description and rationale), a progressive step-by-step solution, and a final answer that must appear only after reasoning is complete. During RL training, the model generates M=4 diverse reasoning paths per question at temperature 1.2 with a maximum sequence length of 1024 tokens.

## Method

StepGRPO is a two-phase online RL framework: a policy warm-up phase (SFT on Mulberry-260k CoT data) followed by step-wise online policy optimization. The key contribution is two rule-based step-wise rewards that provide dense supervision without a process reward model: StepRAR uses soft key-step matching (with format augmentation) to reward paths containing necessary intermediate reasoning steps even when the final answer is wrong, and StepRVR rewards paths that satisfy both reasoning completeness (background analysis + steps + answer) and logical ordering. The overall reward combines both step-wise rewards with the outcome-level reward, and the advantage is estimated via group-relative normalization (mean group reward as baseline), optimizing the policy with KL regularization against a frozen reference model—extending GRPO from DeepSeek-R1 with fine-grained per-step supervision to mitigate the sparse reward problem specific to MLLMs.

## Results

R1-VL-7B (built on Qwen2-VL-7B) achieves an average score of 52.1 across 9 benchmarks, improving over its baseline by 3.4 points. The strongest variant, R1-VL-7B* (built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B), reaches 58.4 average, surpassing GPT-4o (56.2) and Claude-3.5 Sonnet. On the reasoning-intensive MathVista benchmark, R1-VL-7B* scores 74.3, outperforming R1-ShareVL-7B (75.4) by a narrow margin and Vision-R1-7B (73.5) by 0.8 points. The 2B variant (R1-VL-2B) achieves 41.6 average, a 4.1-point gain over Qwen2-VL-2B (37.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

R1-VL-7B* (58.4 AVG) outperforms GPT-4o (56.2 AVG) by 2.2 points and surpasses all open-source reasoning models including R1-ShareVL-7B and Vision-R1-7B on most benchmarks, though R1-ShareVL-7B edges it out on MathVista (75.4 vs. 74.3). R1-VL-7B (52.1 AVG) improves over its Qwen2-VL-7B baseline (48.7) by 3.4 points and beats Mulberry-7B on MathVista (63.5 vs. 63.1) and LlamaV-o1-11B by 9.1 points (63.5 vs. 54.4). The 2B variant R1-VL-2B (41.6 AVG) gains 4.1 points over Qwen2-VL-2B (37.5) and outperforms LLaVA-Reasoner-8B on MathVista (52.1 vs. 50.6), though it trails LLaVA-CoT-11B (54.8) on that benchmark. Against closed-source models, R1-VL-7B* matches or exceeds GPT-4o on MathVista (74.3 vs. 63.8), MMStar (66.2 vs. 63.9), and HallusionBench (57.2 vs. 55.0), while Claude-3.5 Sonnet retains an edge on MathVista (67.7) and ChartQA (90.8). Overall, StepGRPO consistently improves the base model across all 9 benchmarks, with the largest gains on Math-Vision (+8.4 for 7B) and MathVerse (+7.5 for 7B), confirming the benefit of dense step-wise rewards over outcome-only signals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) from DeepSeekMath and the online RL paradigm of DeepSeek-R1 to the multimodal setting, but identifies a critical gap: outcome-level rewards that work for LLMs become too sparse for MLLMs, especially smaller ones, where most generated reasoning paths yield incorrect final answers. The key departure is the introduction of two rule-based step-wise rewards (StepRAR via soft key-step matching and StepRVR via completeness/logic evaluation) that provide dense supervision along the reasoning trajectory without requiring a process reward model. Conceptually, the paper also positions itself against the dominant SFT-on-CoT paradigm (Mulberry, LLaVA-CoT, etc.), arguing that passive imitation of positive paths is insufficient and that self-exploration with step-wise feedback is necessary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The step-wise reward design offers a practical, low-overhead solution to the sparse reward problem that plagues RL training of smaller MLLMs, potentially making reasoning-capable multimodal models accessible at 2B scale. However, the approach relies on GPT-4-extracted key steps and rule-based structural checks, raising open questions about generalization to open-ended or non-mathematical reasoning tasks where 'key steps' are harder to define. Whether the dense step-wise signal continues to provide gains as base models improve in long-chain reasoning accuracy (reducing sparsity naturally) remains to be seen, and the rule-based rewards may not transfer cleanly to domains without clear intermediate verifiable steps.
