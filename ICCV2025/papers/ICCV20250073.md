## Problem

Existing vision-language foundation models for the Tree of Life (BioCLIP, BioTroveCLIP, TaxaBind) treat taxonomic labels as a flat set, failing to encode the transitive partial order that defines the hierarchy. While Alper et al.'s radial embeddings relax fixed cone structures, they only enforce local entailment (immediate parent–child) and provide no guarantee that fine-grained concepts are progressively projected farther from coarse-grained ancestors across the full hierarchy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250073/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images (ViT-B/16, 224×224) paired with the full taxonomic path of a species (e.g., Eukarya → Animalia → Chordata → Mammalia → Carnivora → … → species name), using 'Eukarya' as the entailment root. It produces two sets of embeddings—textual and visual—organized radially so that distance from the root encodes semantic granularity. Downstream, these embeddings support zero-shot classification at any taxonomic rank, hierarchical image-to-text retrieval, and intra-modal image-to-image retrieval, all without requiring a fixed label database.

## Method

RCME introduces a global entailment loss (L_GE) that enforces the transitivity constraint S(T_{j-1}, T_{j+1}) ≥ S(T_j, T_{j+1})·S(T_{j-1}, T_j) via a margin-based hinge on exterior angles, complementing the local entailment loss (L_LE) that minimizes the angle between a concept and its immediate ancestor. A cross-modal alignment loss (L_CMA) jointly fine-tunes both the ViT-B/16 vision encoder and the text encoder, replacing Alper et al.'s frozen-vision prior-preservation term. Hard negative mining recursively samples negatives by matching all ancestor ranks and selecting a sibling's child, forcing the model to learn fine-grained distinctions within shared ancestry. The combined objective (L_GLE + β·L_CMA) is trained on TreeofLife-10M, and the framework generalizes to non-biological hierarchies as demonstrated on HierarCaps.

## Results

On iNaturalist-2021 zero-shot classification, RCME achieves 65.09% average accuracy across all taxonomic ranks, outperforming the strongest baseline ATMG (61.89%) by +3.20%. On the hierarchical retrieval task, RCME attains a Kendall's τ_d of 0.993 (vs. 0.825 for OpenCLIP, the next best) and 0.508 F1 (vs. 0.193 for MERU), demonstrating near-perfect preservation of taxonomic partial order. On BioCLIP-Rare, RCME reaches 62.64% average accuracy, surpassing ATMG (61.39%) and all other baselines. For image-to-image retrieval, RCME achieves 86.88% average R@1, exceeding ATMG (84.08%) and BioCLIP (81.92%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RCME outperforms all eight baselines on every evaluated task. Against the closest competitor ATMG [31] (61.89% avg on iNaturalist-2021), RCME gains +3.20% overall, with particularly large margins at the species rank (73.52% vs. 39.52%) and genus rank (67.41% vs. 49.59%), while ATMG leads at kingdom (99.12% vs. 88.18%). MERU [11] achieves high accuracy at coarse ranks (95.82% kingdom) but collapses at fine ranks (0.73% species), whereas RCME maintains balanced performance across all ranks. Radial Embeddings [1], the direct predecessor, achieves only 41.50% average and 0.521 Kendall's τ_d, confirming that local entailment alone is insufficient for partial-order preservation. General-purpose models (CLIP [30], OpenCLIP [18]) and domain-specific models (BioCLIP [36], BioTroveCLIP [45], TaxaBind [34]) all fall between 25–40% average accuracy, far below RCME. On the HierarCaps generalization experiment, RCME also surpasses Radial Embeddings (0.991 vs. 0.988 τ_d; 0.241 vs. 0.229 F1), validating the framework's domain-agnostic applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Alper et al.'s Radial Embeddings (ECCV 2024), which introduced radial fine-tuning of vision-language models for hierarchical structure but only optimized local (parent-child) entailment. The key departure is the introduction of a global entailment loss that enforces transitivity across all sub-hierarchies, eliminating the need for explicit cone/aperture-angle definitions used by earlier methods like MERU and Ganea et al.'s hyperbolic entailment cones. Additionally, RCME replaces Alper's text-only fine-tuning with a cross-modal alignment loss that jointly updates both vision and text encoders, and introduces a taxonomically-aware hard negative mining strategy. The framework is demonstrated on the Tree of Life domain (building on BioCLIP, BioTroveCLIP, TaxaBind) and generalized to the HierarCaps captioning hierarchy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Hierarchical representations for the Tree of Life enable classification and retrieval at any taxonomic rank, which is critical given that a large fraction of species remain undescribed and taxonomic labels are subject to revision. The paper reveals that intermediate ranks (class, family, order) are systematically harder for plants than animals, pointing to convergent-evolution challenges in plant taxonomy that flat classification metrics would obscure. Open questions include whether the transitivity constraint generalizes to non-tree hierarchies (e.g., multi-parent ontologies), how the approach scales to the full ~8.7M described species, and whether the learned geometry can support evolutionary inference or anomaly detection within the Tree of Life.
