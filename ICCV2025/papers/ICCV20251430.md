## Problem

Existing autoregressive vision-language models lack the ability to interpret visual in-context prompts for generalizable visual learning, and the prohibitive context length of raw image tokens (1024–4096 per image, with ≥4 images needed for image-to-image tasks) makes direct visual prompting impractical. Additionally, visual prompts implicitly convey task intent without explicit textual explanation, requiring models to reason about differences between example pairs—a capability that prior unified models have not adequately addressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251430/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes in-context example pairs (input–output images), text instructions, and optionally a retrieved similar editing pair, all processed through a two-stage pipeline: stage 1 compresses the example images into 32 fixed-length X-Prompt tokens, and stage 2 uses those tokens (with their hidden states) as task guidance for generating the target output. Outputs span a wide range of visual tasks—generated or edited images, segmentation masks, depth maps, surface normal maps, enhanced/derained/deblurred images, and text descriptions of image differences—all produced as discrete VQ-VAE tokens or text tokens via next-token prediction within a 5120-token context window.

## Method

X-Prompt builds on Chameleon's early-fusion autoregressive architecture and introduces a prompt-token compression mechanism: learnable X-Prompt tokens (S=32) are inserted between In-Context Example Tokens and TODO Tokens, with attention masking that severs direct attention from example tokens to target tokens, forcing all contextual information to flow through the compressed X-Prompt representation. A unified training strategy jointly optimizes next-image-token prediction (VQ-VAE) and next-text-token prediction, where the text task requires the model to articulate differences between input and output images, explicitly encouraging intent inference from visual prompts. Task augmentation via reverse transformations (e.g., deraining ↔ adding rain) doubles task variety, and an optional Retrieval-Augmented Image Editing (RAIE) module retrieves the most similar editing pair from a database using CLIP text-embedding cosine similarity to provide additional in-context guidance at inference time.

## Results

On the unified dense prediction and low-level vision benchmark (Table 1), X-Prompt achieves the best depth estimation RMSE (0.277 on NYU-v2), the best derain PSNR (24.77) and SSIM (0.815), and the best deblur PSNR (21.04) among all discrete-token unified models, while also leading on semantic segmentation mIoU (31.21 on ADE-20K) among discrete methods. On GenEval for text-to-image generation, X-Prompt improves Chameleon's overall score from 0.39 to 0.49, and further to 0.57 with the text-prediction augmentation, approaching Emu3Gen (0.54) but trailing DALL-E 3 (0.67). On completely unseen tasks (image inpainting and colorization), X-Prompt achieves the best MSE (0.45/0.49) and LPIPS (0.34/0.37), outperforming OmniGen, LVM, Painter, and Visual Prompting by large margins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among discrete-token unified models, X-Prompt outperforms LVM [3] on every shared metric (depth RMSE 0.277 vs 0.443, segmentation mIoU 31.21 vs 11.71, derain PSNR 24.77 vs 12.12) and Lumina-mGPT [33] on depth (N/A vs ×), segmentation (31.21 vs 20.87), surface normal (19.17 vs 22.10), deblur (21.04 vs 17.59), and derain (24.77 vs 13.31). Against continuous-token generalists, X-Prompt beats OmniGen [74] decisively on all low-level tasks (e.g., derain PSNR 24.77 vs 9.11) and depth estimation (0.277 vs 0.480), though Painter [67] retains an edge on semantic segmentation (49.90 vs 31.21) and lowlight enhancement (22.40 vs 19.71 PSNR). In text-to-image generation (GenEval), X-Prompt (+text pred.) at 0.57 surpasses Chameleon [59] (0.39) and LLaMA-Gen [53] (0.32) but trails Emu3Gen [69] (0.54), SDXL [42] (0.55), and DALL-E 3 [5] (0.67). For image editing on MagicBrush, X-Prompt+RAIE (CLIPdir 0.097, CLIPimg 0.862) is competitive with MagicBrush [81] (0.106/0.933) and UltraEdit [85] (0.093/0.899), clearly beating InstructPix2Pix [6] (0.081/0.852) and Lumina-mGPT [33] (0.025/0.810). On completely unseen tasks (inpainting, colorization), X-Prompt dominates all baselines including Visual Prompting [4], Painter [67], LVM [3], and OmniGen [74], achieving roughly 2× lower LPIPS than the next-best method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Chameleon, a mixed-modal early-fusion foundation model that autoregressively predicts VQ-VAE image tokens, extending it from text-conditioned generation to generalizable visual learning via in-context prompting. It departs from the diffusion-decoder line of VLMs (Emu, Show-o, OmniGen) by avoiding external diffusion models, and from prior visual prompting methods (Painter, LVM) by unifying generation, editing, perception, and low-level processing in a single autoregressive framework. The key architectural departure is the X-Prompt token compression mechanism, which uses attention masking to force contextual information from multi-image examples into a fixed-length token sequence, addressing the prohibitive context-length problem that limited prior in-context visual learners to at most three images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single autoregressive VLM can be competitive across a remarkably broad spectrum of visual tasks—text-to-image generation, instruction-based editing, semantic segmentation, depth estimation, and low-level processing—while also generalizing to previously unseen tasks (inpainting, colorization, one-shot segmentation) given just one in-context example. The prompt-token compression (reducing thousands of tokens to 32) is a practical contribution that makes multi-image visual prompting feasible within standard context windows. However, the VQ-VAE discretization (16× compression) inherently limits fidelity on low-level tasks, and the model still trails strong diffusion-based baselines on editing quality; the approach's ceiling will likely depend on future advances in discrete visual tokenizers. Open questions include whether this paradigm scales to video, whether the compression mechanism generalizes to longer or more diverse prompt sequences, and whether the unified training strategy remains effective as task diversity grows further.
