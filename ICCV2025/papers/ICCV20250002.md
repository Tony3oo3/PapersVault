## Problem

Pre-trained ViT backbone weight matrices exhibit approximate orthogonality among their row and column vectors, a property linked to tighter generalization error bounds. However, the down/up-projection matrices in standard PEFT methods (LoRA, Adapter) lack this orthogonality, creating a mismatch between the adaptation layers and the frozen backbone. The paper asks whether enforcing approximate orthogonality in these adaptation matrices can further improve the generalization capability of fine-tuned ViTs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250002/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images resized to 224×224 pixels as input and produces a single class label per image across 24 downstream classification benchmarks (FGVC and VTAB-1k). The backbone ViT (B/16, L/16, or H/14) is fully frozen; only the low-rank adaptation matrices are learned, with the number of trainable parameters reduced by more than half compared to standard LoRA or Adapter. A key design property is that the bottleneck dimension can be freely adjusted without increasing the parameter count, since the entire projection matrix is generated from a single learnable vector.

## Method

AOFT replaces the down/up-projection matrices in LoRA and Adapter with submatrices of an approximately orthogonal matrix generated from a single learnable vector via a construction derived from quantum chemistry (Mayer, 2013). The operator AO(q) takes a vector q ∈ R^N, constructs an N×N matrix Q whose columns are pairwise orthogonal under normalization, and returns the first d columns as the bottleneck projection. The theoretical justification uses Rademacher complexity: because the L2-norms of the AOFT-generated projection matrices are significantly smaller than those of standard LoRA/Adapter matrices, the upper bound on generalization error is correspondingly tighter. The strategy is plug-and-play, applicable to LoRA (applied to W_q and W_v), Adapter (applied to MHA and FFN modules), and VPT (replacing prompt tokens with a single approximately orthogonal matrix), and an extended variant AOFT* introduces a learnable scaling vector for additional flexibility.

## Results

On the VTAB-1k benchmark (19 datasets, ViT-B/16 backbone), LoRA+AOFT† achieves a mean accuracy of 74.1% with only 0.08M trainable parameters, outperforming LoRA (72.3%, 0.29M params) by 1.8% while using 3.6× fewer parameters. On the FGVC benchmark (5 fine-grained datasets), Adapter+AOFT* reaches 90.1% mean accuracy with 0.20M params, surpassing full fine-tuning (88.5%, 85.98M params) by 1.6% with 430× fewer parameters. Across all PEFT baselines on VTAB-1k, AOFT variants consistently reduce parameter counts by 50–90% while matching or exceeding baseline accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRA+AOFT† (74.1%) outperforms LoRA (72.3%) by 1.8% and DoRA (72.3%) by 1.8% on VTAB-1k mean accuracy, while using 3.6× and 2.4× fewer parameters respectively. It also beats GOFT (72.5%) by 1.6%, OFT (71.8%) by 2.3%, VeRA (71.4%) by 2.7%, and MoIL (73.0%) by 1.1%, all with substantially lower parameter counts. Adapter+AOFT* (72.7%) surpasses Adapter (71.4%) by 1.3% with 2.7× fewer parameters, and VPT-Deep+AOFT (70.7%) improves over VPT-Deep (69.4%) by 1.3% while reducing parameters from 0.60M to 0.05M (12× reduction). The closest competitor is Adapter+r=1 [27] at 74.0%, which LoRA+AOFT† edges out by 0.1% with comparable parameter count (0.08M vs 0.07M). On the FGVC benchmark, Adapter+AOFT* (90.1%) exceeds full fine-tuning (88.5%) by 1.6% with 430× fewer parameters, and LoRA+AOFT (89.9%) beats LoRA (89.5%) by 0.4% with half the parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the low-rank adaptation lineage (LoRA, Adapter, VPT) by observing that pre-trained ViT backbone weight matrices exhibit approximate orthogonality among their row/column vectors, while the down/up-projection matrices in standard PEFT methods do not. It departs from prior orthogonal fine-tuning work (OFT, GOFT, BOFT) that applies orthogonal transformations to the backbone itself, instead enforcing approximate orthogonality within the adaptation matrices via a single learnable vector that generates the entire projection matrix. The construction draws on a classical result from quantum chemistry (Mayer, 2022) for building orthogonal matrices from a vector, and the theoretical justification via Rademacher complexity connects the L2-norm of the adaptation matrices to generalization error bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AOFT offers a practical, plug-and-play modification to existing PEFT frameworks that halves the parameter count of LoRA/Adapter while delivering consistent 1–2% accuracy gains across 24 downstream classification tasks and multiple backbone scales (ViT-B/L/H, Swin). The theoretical grounding, while correct, is relatively thin (a simple L2-norm bound on Rademacher complexity), and the 'approximate orthogonality' of pre-trained matrices is an empirical observation without a deeper mechanistic explanation for why it emerges. Open questions include whether the orthogonality property holds across different pre-training objectives and scales, whether the single-vector parameterization is truly optimal versus other low-parameter constructions, and whether the approach transfers to NLP or multimodal transformers where the backbone geometry may differ.
