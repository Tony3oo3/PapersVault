## Problem

Over-parametrized deep neural networks incur high computational costs, yet existing depth-reduction methods (Layer Folding, EGP, NEPENTHE, EASIER) primarily remove non-linearities and leave the fusion of consecutive linear layers unresolved, which is often infeasible in architectures with padding or residual connections. Few approaches can remove entire blocks from a network while preserving performance, and those that do require expensive retraining or large search spaces.

## Input / Output

LaCoOT operates on a neural network (ResNet-18, MobileNet-V2, Swin-T, or DiT-XL/2) during its training or fine-tuning phase, consuming standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 256×256 for ImageNet generation). It requires that consecutive blocks share the same feature dimensionality so the Max-Sliced Wasserstein distance can be computed directly; blocks violating this constraint are excluded from the removable set. The output is a shallower network where selected blocks are replaced by identity mappings, yielding reduced critical path length, MACs, and inference time.

## Method

LaCoOT introduces a block-wise regularization term R that averages the Max-Sliced Wasserstein distance (p=2) between the input and output feature distributions of each block, added to the standard task loss with weight λ. During training, this regularizer encourages each block to act as a near-identity mapping on its input distribution, effectively imposing a soft 1-Lipschitz constraint on the block Jacobian. Post-training, the block with the lowest Max-Sliced Wasserstein distance is identified as redundant and replaced with an identity function; this removal is repeated iteratively until validation performance drops below a user-specified threshold δ. The key distinction from prior work is that LaCoOT removes entire blocks (including their non-linearities and linear layers) in a single training pass, rather than iteratively pruning non-linearities and attempting to merge the resulting consecutive linear layers.

## Results

On ResNet-18 trained on CIFAR-10, LaCoOT achieves 90.99% top-1 accuracy while halving MACs from 140.19M to 64.69M and reducing inference time by 40% (4.78 ms vs. 7.90 ms on an NVIDIA A4500). It is the only method among the compared baselines that actually reduces MACs; all others leave MACs unchanged or increase them. For image generation with DiT-XL/2 on ImageNet, removing two DiT blocks with LaCoOT regularization yields a FID-50k of 2.9, compared to 118.6 without the regularization, demonstrating that the method scales to foundation models with only 5k fine-tuning steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaCoOT outperforms all four depth-reduction baselines on ResNet-18/CIFAR-10 in both accuracy and computational efficiency: it achieves 90.99% top-1 vs. 88.76% (Layer Folding), 90.64% (EGP), 89.26% (NEPENTHE), and 90.35% (EASIER), while being the only method to reduce MACs (64.69M vs. 140.19M for all baselines). Training time is also dramatically lower at 40 minutes compared to 160–533 minutes for the iterative baselines. On Swin-T/Tiny-ImageNet-200, LaCoOT outperforms competing methods by up to 10% at the same critical path length. However, on parameter-efficient architectures like MobileNet-V2, EASIER achieves comparable or slightly better accuracy at matched critical path lengths, though it requires 34 separate training runs versus one for LaCoOT. EGP also exhibits a critical failure mode on MobileNet-V2, where it prunes the final layer before the classifier head, completely severing the information flow.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaCoOT departs from the dominant line of depth-reduction work (Layer Folding, EGP, NEPENTHE, EASIER) that targets individual non-linearities and leaves the fusion of consecutive linear layers as an open architectural problem. Instead, it repurposes the Max-Sliced Wasserstein distance—originally introduced for GAN training (Deshpande et al., CVPR 2019)—as an internal block-wise regularizer that trains the network so that entire blocks become near-identity, enabling their complete removal post-training. This contrasts with other OT-in-ML applications (NAS, knowledge distillation) where OT operates between separate models; here it operates within a single network's intermediate feature distributions. The theoretical framing connects the regularization to a soft 1-Lipschitz constraint on block Jacobians, linking it to the Lipschitz-constrained network literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving a 40% inference-time reduction and halved MACs on ResNet-18/CIFAR-10 with a single training run, compared to iterative baselines requiring dozens of retraining cycles, and in demonstrating that the approach extends to foundation-scale generative models (DiT-XL/2) with only 5k fine-tuning steps. Key limitations include performance degradation on already parameter-efficient architectures (MobileNet-V2) and the requirement that input/output dimensions match within a block, leaving the Gromov-Wasserstein extension for mismatched dimensions as an open direction. The broader implication is that over-parametrization in pre-trained models can be exploited for depth reduction without architectural redesign, which is relevant as inference costs of large models continue to grow.
