## Problem

Pretrained depth completion models suffer significant performance degradation when deployed in novel environments due to covariate shift, yet existing test-time adaptation methods either require multiple passes over target data, apply uniform updates across the entire prediction, or lack an explicit mechanism to quantify how well predictions align with the source distribution. This paper addresses the gap by introducing an energy-based criterion that scores local regions of depth predictions for in-distribution likelihood, enabling targeted, single-pass adaptation without ground truth.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image and a synchronized sparse depth map (projected LiDAR/radar/VIO point cloud) and outputs a dense depth map. The energy model operates at a patch or region level, producing a lower-resolution energy map where each element corresponds to the likelihood of a local region in the full-resolution prediction. At test time, only a lightweight adaptation module inserted in the RGB encoder and BatchNorm statistics are updated, while all other depth completion parameters remain frozen.

## Method

ETA trains a region-based energy model in a preparation stage by synthesizing out-of-distribution examples via FGSM adversarial perturbations of the source RGB and sparse depth inputs, then mapping prediction error to bounded energy values through a negative exponential (Gibbs) formulation. At test time, the frozen energy model scores each patch of the predicted depth map conditioned on the sparse depth input, localizing high-energy (erroneous) regions. A lightweight adaptation layer in the RGB encoder is updated by minimizing a composite loss combining energy minimization, sparse depth L1 consistency, and edge-aware local smoothness, effectively pulling predictions back toward the source distribution. The region-based energy formulation provides spatial regularity and interpretability, allowing the model to correct specific erroneous regions rather than applying uniform global adjustments.

## Results

Across four depth completion architectures (MSG-CHN, NLSPN, CostDCNet, BP-Net) and six target datasets, ETA consistently achieves the lowest MAE and RMSE among all test-time adaptation methods. On outdoor targets (VKITTI-FOG, nuScenes, Waymo), ETA improves over the previous state-of-the-art ProxyTTA by an average of 5.36% in MAE and 1.97% in RMSE; on indoor targets (NYUv2, SceneNet, ScanNet), the improvement is 10.13% in MAE and 10.33% in RMSE. Against the closest energy-based baseline TEA, ETA gains 24.9% (MAE) and 19.0% (RMSE) outdoors, and 58.2% (MAE) and 27.13% (RMSE) indoors. For example, on NLSPN adapting from KITTI→VKITTI-FOG, ETA reduces MAE from 1.309 (pre-trained) to 0.545, outperforming ProxyTTA (0.686) and TEA (0.735).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ETA outperforms all four baselines across every model and dataset combination. Against ProxyTTA (the previous state-of-the-art for depth completion TTA), ETA achieves an average 5.36% MAE and 1.97% RMSE improvement outdoors, and 10.13% MAE and 10.33% RMSE improvement indoors; the gap is largest when the pre-trained model suffers severe degradation (e.g., KITTI→VKITTI-FOG on MSG-CHN, where ETA reduces MAE from 2.842 to 0.703 vs. ProxyTTA's 0.728). Against TEA, the closest energy-based method originally designed for classification, ETA gains 24.9% MAE and 19.0% RMSE outdoors and 58.2% MAE and 27.13% RMSE indoors, attributed to ETA's region-based energy formulation and its explicit handling of continuous regression. Against CoTTA, ETA wins on all six datasets for every model; CoTTA's pseudo-label-based strategy is particularly weak on outdoor fog (VKITTI-FOG) where it sometimes degrades below the pre-trained baseline. Against BN Adapt (a TENT variant using sparse-depth and smoothness losses instead of entropy), ETA consistently achieves lower MAE and RMSE, with the largest margins on outdoor targets where entropy minimization is ill-suited for regression.

## Relation to Prior Work

ETA extends the test-time adaptation framework for depth completion established by ProxyTTA [44], which introduced a lightweight adaptation module in the image encoder and a proxy embedding from sparse depth to guide updates. It also draws on TEA [85], which repurposed pretrained classifiers into energy-based models for test-time adaptation in classification, but ETA adapts this energy-based paradigm to continuous regression (depth completion) where log-sum-exp over logits is inapplicable. The key conceptual departure is the use of adversarial perturbations (FGSM) on source data to synthesize out-of-distribution training examples for the energy model, circumventing the need to know the target distribution a priori, combined with a region-based energy map that localizes errors rather than producing a single global score.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ETA addresses a practical deployment bottleneck: depth completion models degrade under covariate shift (illumination, weather, sensor changes) and conventional domain adaptation requires multiple passes and ground truth. The energy-based, single-pass approach with a frozen energy model and lightweight adaptation layer is well-suited to real-time robotic and autonomous driving pipelines. Limitations include the reliance on adversarial perturbations to approximate unknown target shifts (which may miss extreme or semantically novel conditions) and the region-based formulation's potential inadequacy for global scene-level domain gaps. The authors note the principle could extend to other dense regression tasks (optical flow, surface normals, medical segmentation), but this remains untested.
