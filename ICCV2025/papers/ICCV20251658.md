## Problem

Self-supervised contrastive learning with multi-stage alignment (e.g., MoCo v2) performs well on generic categories but paradoxically degrades on fine-grained datasets, where strong data augmentation distorts semantic information at shallow encoder stages and causes misalignment between query and key features. This paper identifies this failure mode and proposes a solution that preserves semantic consistency across stages while also capturing discriminative local parts.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images resized to 224×224 (training) or center-cropped from 256 to 224×224 (testing) and produces learned image representations evaluated via linear probing classification (top-1 accuracy) and nearest-neighbor retrieval (rank-1, rank-5). A ResNet-50 backbone with a MoCo v2-style memory queue of 4096 negatives and momentum 0.999 generates the representations. Additionally, the prototype clustering module yields N=5 part-level feature vectors that can be visualized as spatial heatmaps over the input image.

## Method

PAPN introduces two modules on top of MoCo v2. First, Stage-wise Progressive Augmentation (SPA) replaces the uniform strong augmentation used in multi-stage alignment with progressively increasing intensities {0.45, 0.7, 0.95} across the last three encoder stages, so shallow stages receive weaker augmentation to preserve fine-grained semantics; an MSE loss then aligns query and key features at each stage. Second, Prototype-based Holistic-Partial Contrastive Learning (PHP) introduces five learnable prototypes that cluster spatial feature descriptors from the final stage via cosine similarity, producing part-level features that are concatenated with whole-level features (pooled from the last three stages) to form a holistic-partial representation. An extended InfoNCE loss is applied to this combined representation, and the total training objective is simply L_SPA + L_PHP with unit weights, making the method end-to-end trainable without external supervision or saliency priors.

## Results

On CUB200, PAPN achieves 69.93% top-1 classification accuracy, 45.39% rank-1, and 72.81% rank-5 retrieval accuracy. On Stanford Cars, it reaches 67.48% top-1, 35.98% rank-1, and 59.94% rank-5. On FGVC-Aircraft, it attains 60.13% top-1, 35.13% rank-1, and 58.75% rank-5. On the large-scale iNat2019 pretraining setting, PAPN achieves 48.35% on iNat2019 val, 53.83% on CUB200, 49.78% on Cars, and 40.47% on Aircraft, consistently outperforming MoCo v2 and DINOv2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAPN outperforms the strongest prior SSL baseline LDF [43] on all top-1 classification metrics: +3.76% on CUB200 (69.93 vs 66.17), +1.88% on Cars (67.48 vs 65.60), and +4.85% on Aircraft (60.13 vs 55.28). It also surpasses DINOv2 [28] by 3.91% on CUB200 top-1 and 5.46% on Aircraft top-1. In retrieval, PAPN leads on rank-1 across all three datasets (e.g., 45.39% vs 42.06% on CUB200, 35.13% vs 33.27% on Aircraft) and on rank-5 for CUB200 (72.81% vs 70.28% DINOv2) and Aircraft (58.75% vs 57.64% DINOv2). The sole exception is Cars rank-5 retrieval, where LDF achieves 61.94% versus PAPN's 59.94%. Compared to the MoCo v2 [7] backbone it builds upon, PAPN gains +5.95% on CUB200 top-1 and +9.00% on Aircraft top-1, demonstrating the substantial contribution of the SPA and PHP modules over standard contrastive learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the MoCo v2 momentum contrastive learning framework and the multi-stage alignment strategy introduced by LEWEL and HICO, which aligns features across different encoder stages to improve representation quality. It departs from prior self-supervised fine-grained methods (LCR, LDF, CVSA) that rely on external guidance such as Grad-CAM attention maps or saliency detectors, instead redesigning the data augmentation pipeline itself and introducing end-to-end prototype clustering for part-level features. The key conceptual departure is the diagnosis that uniform augmentation across stages causes semantic misalignment specifically in fine-grained settings, motivating the stage-wise progressive augmentation strategy that applies weaker augmentation to shallower stages and stronger augmentation to deeper stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and diagnoses a concrete failure mode—multi-stage alignment degrading rather than improving SSL on fine-grained data—which provides a useful caution for practitioners applying multi-stage strategies beyond generic categories. The proposed fix (progressive augmentation with manually tuned per-stage intensities) is simple and practical, but the intensities {0.45, 0.7, 0.95} are dataset-specific hyperparameters that may not transfer automatically, and all experiments use only ResNet-50, leaving open whether the findings generalize to ViT backbones or deeper architectures. The authors note future directions toward visual language models and generative applications, but the current scope is limited to four fine-grained classification/retrieval benchmarks.
