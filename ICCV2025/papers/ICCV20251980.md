## Problem

Existing synthetic pedestrian data for person ReID fails to simultaneously satisfy three requirements: generating diverse virtual identities (including hard identities) without exposing real identities, providing scalable cross-camera variations in poses and scenes, and producing camera-realistic styles. Engine-based methods lack realism, while learning-based methods either replicate real identities (raising privacy concerns) or offer limited cross-camera diversity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251980/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes random Gaussian embeddings (for novel identities) or dropout text embeddings derived from CLIP-encoded BLIP descriptions (for hard identities) as identity conditions, plus pose skeletons extracted via OpenPose, inpainted scene backgrounds, and style reference images from LUPerson. It outputs a complete ReID dataset of 5,000 virtual identities comprising 114,738 bounding boxes, where each identity's intra-ID group contains images with varied poses, scenes, and transferred camera-realistic styles, all at 256×128 resolution for downstream ReID training.

## Method

VIPerson is a three-stage generation pipeline built on a latent diffusion model (modified UNet) trained on the 1.5M-image MALS dataset with CLIP text embeddings from BLIP-generated descriptions. The key novelty is the flexible identity generator: new random identities are produced by conditioning the reverse denoising process on Gaussian-sampled embeddings, while hard identities are generated via a proposed dropout text embedding that randomly zeros dimensions of the CLIP embedding to perturb subtle identity features while preserving core semantics. Cross-camera diversification uses IP-Adapter (ControlNet-based) with OpenPose-extracted poses for pose variation and a segment-and-paste strategy (inpainting to remove pedestrians from real scenes, then semantic segmentation to composite generated persons) for scene variation. Finally, style realization applies a training-free neural style transfer method as post-processing to inject identity-agnostic camera-realistic styles (illumination, motion blur, camera bias) from LUPerson reference images, decoupling style from identity to avoid privacy leakage.

## Results

On direct transfer (Table 2), VIPerson achieves 32.5% rank-1 and 10.3% mAP on MSMT17, and 66.5% rank-1 and 38.3% mAP on Market1501, surpassing the strongest prior synthetic baseline UnrealPerson (26.8% / 64.0% rank-1) by 5.7 and 2.5 points respectively. With target-scenario information (VIPerson*), performance further improves to 34.3% rank-1 on MSMT17 and 67.5% on Market1501. In supervised fine-tuning (Table 3), VIPerson* reaches 76.7% rank-1 on MSMT17 and 95.1% on Market1501, and in unsupervised domain adaptation (Table 4), VIPerson attains 70.4% rank-1 on MSMT17 and 94.9% on Market1501, consistently outperforming all synthetic baselines across all three downstream tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIPerson outperforms all five compared synthetic baselines across every downstream task. Against the strongest prior method UnrealPerson [57], VIPerson gains +5.7 rank-1 on MSMT17 (32.5 vs. 26.8) and +2.5 on Market1501 (66.5 vs. 64.0) in direct transfer, +2.0 rank-1 on MSMT17 (76.5 vs. 74.5) in fine-tuning, and +4.5 rank-1 on MSMT17 (70.4 vs. 65.9) in UDA. The gap widens dramatically against older engine-based methods: RandPerson [42] trails by 18.0 rank-1 on MSMT17 direct transfer (14.5 vs. 32.5), PersonX [41] by 24.9 (7.6 vs. 32.5), and SyRI [1] by 24.7 (7.8 vs. 32.5). The learning-based text-retrieval dataset MALS [50] is essentially non-functional for ReID (0.2% rank-1 on MSMT17), confirming that virtual identities alone without cross-camera variation and realistic style are insufficient. Notably, VIPerson's direct-transfer performance on MSMT17 (32.5%) even exceeds the fully-supervised cross-domain result of training on Market1501 real data (9.2%), demonstrating that the synthetic dataset's diversity surpasses what a single real dataset can provide for cross-domain generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

VIPerson sits at the intersection of two prior streams of synthetic ReID data: engine-based methods (SyRI, PersonX, RandPerson, UnrealPerson) that produce virtual identities but suffer from a style gap, and learning-based methods (GCL, Pose-dIVE, MALS) that achieve realism but inadvertently replicate real identities, raising privacy concerns. The pipeline repurposes the latent diffusion model (Rombach et al.) as its generative backbone, IP-Adapter (Ye et al.) for pose-conditioned cross-camera diversification, and a training-free style-injection method (Chung et al.) for identity-agnostic style transfer. The key conceptual departure is the 'dropout text embedding' mechanism, which perturbs CLIP text embeddings to generate hard identities without manual design, and the segment-and-paste strategy that decouples scene variation from identity generation, making cross-camera diversity scalable without additional training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By producing a fully virtual-identity dataset that outperforms all prior synthetic baselines across direct transfer, supervised fine-tuning, and unsupervised domain adaptation, VIPerson offers a practical path to training ReID models without exposing real pedestrian identities—a critical need under GDPR and similar regulations. The ablation isolating three key factors (identity diversity, cross-camera variation, camera-realistic style) provides actionable guidance for future synthetic data design. However, the paper acknowledges a performance bottleneck at larger data scales, and the gap to fully supervised real-data training (76.7% vs. 74.1% on MSMT17 fine-tuning) remains modest, suggesting that synthetic data alone may not fully replace real annotations. Open questions include whether the pipeline generalizes to other ReID sub-tasks (video ReID, cross-domain) and how to break the observed scaling ceiling.
