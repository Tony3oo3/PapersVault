## Problem

Existing multimodal trackers assume all sensor modalities are perfectly synchronized and available at every frame, yet real-world systems suffer from temporal dropouts due to differing frame rates and exposure times. This paper presents the first comprehensive study of tracker performance under temporally incomplete multimodal data, revealing that rigid architectures degrade significantly when modalities are intermittently missing, and asks whether a tracker should dynamically adjust its computational complexity based on data availability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250051/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The tracker consumes video clips (N frames) from RGB plus one auxiliary modality (depth, thermal, or event) along with corresponding search-region crops, where any modality may be absent at individual timesteps. It is trained jointly across three modality pairs (RGB-Thermal on LasHeR, RGB-Event on VisEvent, RGB-Depth on DepthTrack) in a single process to form a unified model. Output is a bounding box (center x, y, height, width) with a classification confidence score, evaluated via precision, AUC, F-score, and VOT metrics.

## Method

FlexTrack introduces a Heterogeneous Mixture-of-Experts (HMoE) fusion module where each expert is a linear layer with a distinct hidden dimension (2^d, d ∈ {2,…,D−1}), and the entire video clip is routed to the top-2 experts via a gating function rather than routing individual tokens. This enables the model to dynamically select lighter or heavier experts depending on the missing-modality rate and scene complexity. A novel video-level masking strategy is applied during training: at each timestep, at least one modality is guaranteed to remain unmasked, preserving both spatial completeness and temporal continuity—unlike random or VideoMAE-style masking that disrupts one or the other. Expert outputs are combined with a linear-attention pathway (two learned weight matrices) and a final softmax-weighted aggregation to produce the fused representation. The training loss includes standard tracking losses (classification, L1, GIoU) plus Switch-Transformer-style load-balancing and importance losses to prevent expert collapse.

## Results

FlexTrack achieves SOTA across all 9 evaluated benchmarks. On complete datasets, it leads by 1.5% P (81.4 vs. 79.9) on VisEvent, 1.7% AUC (62.0 vs. 60.3) on LasHeR, 1.9% F-score (67.0 vs. 65.1) on DepthTrack, and 0.4% MSR (69.9 vs. 69.5) on RGBT234. Under missing-modality conditions, the advantage widens substantially: 6.2% P on VisEvent_miss (72.8 vs. 66.6), 2.4% P on LasHeR_miss (65.1 vs. 61.7 for IPT), and 2.1% MPR on RGBT234_miss (84.1 vs. 82.0 for IPT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlexTrack outperforms all 36 compared baselines across every benchmark. On complete datasets, the closest competitor is STTrack (arXiv 2024), which FlexTrack surpasses by 1.5–1.7% in P/AUC on VisEvent and LasHeR, and by 1.9% F-score on DepthTrack. SUTrack (arXiv 2024) is the second-strongest baseline, trailing FlexTrack by 1.5% P on VisEvent and 1.9% F-score on DepthTrack. The only method explicitly designed for missing modalities, IPT (IJCV 2024), is limited to RGB-Thermal and requires separate prompt parameters and three inference branches; FlexTrack beats it by 2.4% P on LasHeR_miss and 2.1% MPR on RGBT234_miss without any additional parameters. Under missing-modality conditions, the performance gap widens dramatically: FlexTrack leads SUTrack by 6.2% P on VisEvent_miss and by 7.9% F-score on DepthTrack_miss, while prompt-based trackers like ViPT (CVPR 2023) and MCITrack (arXiv 2024) degrade severely (e.g., ViPT drops to 40.1 P on LasHeR_miss vs. 65.1 for FlexTrack). Older single-modal trackers (OSTrack, ATOM, DiMP, etc.) show the largest gaps, confirming that rigid template-matching architectures cannot handle intermittent modality absence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends multimodal video tracking (RGB + depth/thermal/event) by addressing the practically critical but underexplored problem of temporally missing modalities caused by sensor synchronization failures. Architecturally, it builds on the Switch Transformer's MoE routing and load-balancing losses but departs from prior MoE-based fusion methods (FuseMoE, Flex-MoE) by introducing heterogeneous experts of varying hidden dimensions rather than uniform-sized experts, enabling adaptive test-time complexity. The video-level masking strategy is a deliberate adaptation of VideoMAE's tube masking to the tracking setting, where future frames are inaccessible, and contrasts with prior per-scenario prompt-based approaches like IPT that require separate branches for each missing-modality configuration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic benchmark of existing trackers under temporally missing multimodal data, revealing significant performance degradation that motivates the adaptive-complexity design. The finding that a single unified model can match or exceed SOTA on complete data while substantially outperforming under missing modalities (with the gap widening as missing rates increase) is practically important for real-world multisensor systems where perfect synchronization is unattainable. Open questions include whether the heterogeneous expert approach scales to more than two modalities simultaneously, whether generative reconstruction of missing modalities could complement the adaptive routing, and whether the video-level masking strategy transfers to other temporal tasks beyond tracking.
