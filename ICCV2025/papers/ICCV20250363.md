## Problem

Existing counterfactual explanation (CFE) methods for deep image classifiers rely on external encoders and generative models to produce plausible images, thereby neglecting the classifier's own feature space and decision boundaries. This means the generated CFEs do not faithfully reflect how the classifier actually decides. Additionally, prior methods produce only a single discrete CFE image, failing to reveal the gradual transformation process that would help users understand *how* a decision flip occurs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a source image (28×28 grayscale for MNIST/F-MNIST, 128×128 RGB for Blood-MNIST, 224×224 RGB for CelebA-HQ), a target class label, and a frozen pretrained ResNet classifier that serves as the sole feature encoder. It outputs a counterfactual explanation image (k > 0.5), a semi-factual explanation image (k < 0.5), and a full animated transition sequence by sweeping the step factor k from 0 to 1, yielding images at each intermediate confidence level. The classifier is never fine-tuned, preserving faithfulness to its original decision boundary.

## Method

Mirror-CFE treats the pairwise decision boundary between source and target classes (parameterized by W_t − W_s) as a 'mirror' in the classifier's latent space, computing the source feature's projection and reflection on this hyperplane to define CFE/SFE points along the line segment. A GAN-based mapping function G: Z → I is then trained to project these latent points back to pixel space, using classification loss (KLD-based), adversarial loss, reconstruction loss, and a novel triangulation loss that enforces proportional distance relationships between latent-space and image-space counterparts to prevent adversarial-like trivial solutions. The decoder employs U-Net-style skip connections from the frozen encoder, regulated by a Skip Connection Controller (SCC) comprising a Spatial Pattern Editor that lets the KFE latent encoding modulate high-frequency style information and a CAM-guided Spatial Prior module that localizes changes to class-discriminative regions. This design ensures the classifier remains the sole feature extractor and decision maker, making the explanation intrinsically faithful to the model's learned geometry.

## Results

On MNIST, Mirror-CFE (1st CFE) achieves 100% validity with a denoised validity (D.Val) of 0.99, the best LPIPS of 0.17, and FID of 3.25, while maintaining 0% failure rate. On Fashion-MNIST, it achieves the best L1 (0.12), LPIPS (0.10), and FID (2.80) among all methods with 99% validity and 99% D.Val. On Blood-MNIST, Mirror-CFE (1st CFE) achieves 99% validity and 99% D.Val with the best L1 (0.05) and LPIPS (11.81), substantially outperforming C3LT on sparsity (LPIPS 11.81 vs. 30.14). The key differentiator is D.Val: Mirror-CFE retains 99% validity after Gaussian denoising across all datasets, whereas C3LT drops to 79% (MNIST) and 87% (F-MNIST), indicating C3LT's CFEs rely on imperceptible adversarial perturbations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mirror-CFE (1st CFE) outperforms all five baselines on the critical denoised validity (D.Val) metric, achieving 0.99 across all three datasets versus C3LT's 0.79 (MNIST) and 0.87 (F-MNIST), indicating its CFEs are not adversarial perturbations. On sparsity (LPIPS), Mirror-CFE achieves 0.17 on MNIST and 0.10 on F-MNIST, beating C3LT (0.25 and 0.30) and all other methods. On realism (FID), Mirror-CFE achieves 2.80 on F-MNIST (best) and 3.25 on MNIST (second best after REVISE's 1.96, which has near-zero validity). REVISE and CEM, while achieving low L1 distances, fail catastrophically on validity (0.02–0.19) with high failure rates (9.77% and 16.72% on MNIST), making them unreliable. PGD and ExpGAN achieve high validity but at the cost of poor realism (FID 15.62 and 26.22 on MNIST) and moderate D.Val (0.74 and 0.61), confirming their adversarial-like behavior. C3LT is the closest overall competitor, matching Mirror-CFE on validity and D.Val for B-MNIST, but Mirror-CFE wins decisively on LPIPS (11.81 vs. 30.14) and L1 (0.05 vs. 0.16) for B-MNIST, and on D.Val for MNIST and F-MNIST.

## Relation to Prior Work

Mirror-CFE departs from the three dominant CFE paradigms—optimization-based search, region replacement, and generative-model-based synthesis—by operating entirely within the classifier's own feature space and treating pairwise decision boundaries as geometric mirrors. Rather than reverse-engineering a separate encoder or relying on pretrained GANs/diffusion models (as in C3LT, ExpGAN, or REVISE), it uses the classifier's frozen encoder F as the sole feature extractor, ensuring the generated explanations are grounded in the model's actual decision geometry. The triangulation loss, which enforces distance-ratio preservation between latent and pixel spaces, is a novel regularization not seen in prior CFE or GAN literature, and the animated step-wise transition (varying k from 0 to 1) extends the standard single-point CFE formulation to a continuous trajectory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By anchoring CFE generation to the classifier's own decision boundaries, Mirror-CFE addresses a persistent faithfulness gap in visual XAI: prior methods using external encoders or generative models can produce plausible images that do not reflect how the target classifier actually decides. The animated transition capability is practically valuable for clinical and safety-critical settings, where users need to see *how* a decision flips rather than just the endpoint. Key limitations include validation only on ResNet architectures (ViT adaptation is left to future work), the need for a trained discriminator and generator (non-trivial training cost), and the observation that at small k values the method can still produce adversarial-like perturbations. Open questions include whether the mirror formulation generalizes to non-linear decision boundaries in deeper or transformer-based classifiers, and whether the triangulation loss scales to high-dimensional, high-resolution image spaces without additional architectural support.
