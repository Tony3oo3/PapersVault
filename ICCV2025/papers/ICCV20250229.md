## Problem

Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) learns a small set of input-space prompts to adapt a frozen ViT, yet prompt learning is empirically difficult and sensitive to initialization. The paper identifies the root cause: the data that interacts with the prompt inside the self-attention module (W_q W_k^T X^T) exhibits burstiness and non-Gaussian (Laplacian / hyper-Laplacian) distributions, which impede gradient-based optimization of the prompt. No prior work had diagnosed or addressed these distributional pathologies in the attention computation.

## Input / Output

For classification, the method consumes 224×224 RGB images and outputs a class label; for detection and segmentation it uses variable-resolution images (short side 480–800, long side ≤1333) and outputs bounding boxes or pixel-level masks via Mask R-CNN / Cascade Mask R-CNN heads. The learned prompt is not a single matrix but a bilinear product (e.g., P·W^T or A·B^T) that is concatenated with the n image patch tokens before entering the frozen ViT, so at inference the two factors are pre-multiplied into a single effective prompt of shape m×d, keeping test-time cost identical to standard VPT.

## Method

Bilinear Prompt Tuning (BPT) re-parameterizes the VPT prompt as a product of two matrices, motivated by the observation that the attention data W_q W_k^T X^T is bursty and non-Gaussian. BPT-fWhiten first computes a ZCA whitening matrix W = Σ^{-1/2} (via SVD of the covariance of W_q W_k^T X^T over a small random image subset) and fixes it, so the effective prompt becomes P·W^T; this decorrelates and equalizes variance, making the data more Gaussian and dramatically accelerating convergence. BPT-tWhiten additionally tunes W jointly with P. BPT-bilinear replaces the fixed W with a randomly initialized compact matrix B ∈ R^{d×p} (p ≪ d) and learns A ∈ R^{m×p} so that the final prompt is A·B^T, yielding a low-rank prompt with fewer parameters and ~3.5× fewer multiplications than VPT. All three variants share the same bilinear two-matrix-multiplication structure, which the paper shows naturally produces bursty prompts—and this burstiness, counterintuitively, is what enables the large accuracy gains over standard VPT and SPT.

## Results

On the FGVC benchmarks with MAE-pretrained ViT-B, BPT-Shallow achieves 80.39% mean accuracy, outperforming SPT-Shallow (73.95%) by +6.44 points and VPT-Shallow (57.84%) by +22.55 points. BPT-Deep reaches 84.60% mean accuracy, surpassing SPT-Deep (83.26%) and GateVPT (73.39%). On CUB-200 specifically, BPT-fWhiten jumps from VPT's 42.15% to 77.48% (+35.33 points), and BPT-bilinear achieves 77.86% with fewer parameters (6.51×10⁻²M vs. 7.68×10⁻²M). On COCO object detection (Cascade Mask R-CNN), BPT-Deep achieves 44.97 AP^box vs. SPT-Deep's 41.32 and VPT-Deep's 38.70.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BPT-Shallow decisively outperforms VPT-Shallow by +22.55 mean accuracy (MAE) and SPT-Shallow by +6.44, while using fewer parameters (6.51 vs. 7.68×10⁻²M). In the deep setting, BPT-Deep beats SPT-Deep by +1.34 mean accuracy and GateVPT by +11.21, with a comparable parameter budget (18.36 vs. 18.43×10⁻²M). Against backbone fine-tuning methods (LoRA, SSF, SNF, Adapter, Bias), BPT-Deep achieves 91.72% mean FGVC accuracy with only 18.36×10⁻²M parameters, surpassing LoRA (89.85%, 39.46×10⁻²M) and SSF (90.72%, 21.69×10⁻²M) while using fewer parameters. On COCO detection, BPT-Deep outperforms SPT-Deep by +3.65 AP^box and +2.16 AP^mask (Cascade Mask R-CNN), and VPT-Deep by +6.27 AP^box. The only method that surpasses BPT is full fine-tuning (88.54% mean FGVC, 8.60×10⁹ parameters), but BPT-Deep achieves 91.72% with ~470× fewer parameters, and the gap narrows or reverses in limited-data regimes (<30% of ImageNet). BPT also accelerates convergence: within 100 epochs it matches SPT's 400-epoch accuracy on ImageNet (64.63% vs. 64.61%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) by identifying a structural issue in the attention module's data (the product W_q W_k^T X^T) that makes prompt learning difficult: burstiness and non-Gaussian (Laplacian/hyper-Laplacian) distributions. It draws on two classical ideas — ZCA data whitening from exploratory data analysis and bilinear models from fine-grained recognition — and unifies them into a single bilinear prompt parameterization (P·W^T or A·B^T). Unlike SPT, which addresses prompt difficulty through careful initialization via clustering, BPT addresses it through data preprocessing and a different parameterization, and unlike GateVPT which focuses on where to place prompts, BPT focuses on how to structure the prompt itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a simple whitening step plus bilinear parameterization yields >25-point gains on CUB-200 and consistent improvements across FGVC, ImageNet, and COCO detection/segmentation, while reducing both parameter count and computation (3.5× fewer multiplications in BPT-bilinear). The conceptual finding that bursty prompts are beneficial — the opposite of what bilinear feature literature prescribes — challenges a working assumption in the field and opens questions about whether burstiness throughout the Transformer architecture can be exploited more broadly. Key limitations acknowledged by the authors include the simplified analysis (focusing on only one term in the attention computation), the lack of theoretical grounding for why burstiness aids learning, and the absence of evaluation on imbalanced or biased data distributions.
