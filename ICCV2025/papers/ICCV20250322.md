## Problem

Online 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction from unposed image streams remains unsolved because existing generalizable methods require known camera poses and intrinsics, while pose-free methods like CF-3DGS rely on per-frame optimization loops that are too slow for real-time streaming. No prior work achieves feed-forward, pose-free, generalizable 3DGS reconstruction that can process an endless image stream frame-by-frame with minimal latency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250322/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequential stream of unposed monocular RGB frames (resized to 224×224) with no SfM preprocessing, no known intrinsics, and no prior camera poses. At each timestamp it outputs an incrementally updated 3D Gaussian Splatting representation (merged across all frames seen so far), estimated camera intrinsics and extrinsics for the current frame, and can render novel views. The per-frame processing budget is ~0.11 s, enabling up to 9 frames per second on a single A100 GPU.

## Method

StreamGS builds on a frozen DUSt3R backbone to predict initial pixel-wise 3D point maps and coarse camera poses for each adjacent frame pair. A content-adaptive matching head extracts local 3D features to establish reliable reciprocal nearest-neighbor correspondences, which are then used to refine both the 3D point map (via residual transforms) and the camera pose (via PnP-RANSAC), mitigating out-of-domain inaccuracies of the frozen predictor. The key efficiency mechanism is Feed-Forward Adaptive Density Control: Gaussian features from the current frame are warped onto the reference frame using the established pixel correspondences and merged through a two-layer convolutional MergeNet, converting what would be a costly 3D Gaussian aggregation into a lightweight 2D pixel-wise operation that prunes ~37–40% of redundant Gaussians. A separate lightweight decoder predicts per-pixel Gaussian parameters (quaternion, scale, opacity, spherical harmonics) from concatenated 2D image features, refined 3D points, and 3D features. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end on RE10K with a combined L2+LPIPS rendering loss and a reconstruction loss that encourages the merged Gaussians to reproduce the pre-merge rendering.

## Results

On the source domain RE10K, StreamGS achieves 22.42 PSNR, competitive with MVSplat (23.54) and CF-3DGS (23.46). On out-of-domain datasets, StreamGS significantly outperforms pose-dependent generalizable methods: 20.54 PSNR on DL3DV (vs. 19.43 pixelSplat, 17.84 MVSplat) and 25.05 PSNR on MVImgNet (vs. 19.22 pixelSplat, 16.29 MVSplat). StreamGS also surpasses the optimization-based CF-3DGS on ScanNet (28.43 vs. 22.88 PSNR) while being 150× faster in reconstruction speed. The method is the only one that is both pose-free and generalizable (feed-forward).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against pixelSplat [2] and MVSplat [3] (both pose-dependent generalizable methods), StreamGS achieves substantially higher PSNR on all out-of-domain datasets: +1.11 dB on DL3DV and +5.82 dB on MVImgNet over pixelSplat, and +2.70 dB on DL3DV and +8.76 dB on MVImgNet over MVSplat, while also being pose-free. Against the optimization-based CF-3DGS [9] (also pose-free but not generalizable), StreamGS wins decisively on ScanNet (+5.55 dB PSNR) and DL3DV (+0.61 dB), though it trails on MVImgNet (−1.28 dB) and RE10K (−1.04 dB). The critical efficiency advantage is that StreamGS processes frames in a single feed-forward pass (~0.11 s/frame) versus CF-3DGS's thousands of optimization steps per view, yielding a 150× speedup. On the source domain RE10K, StreamGS is slightly behind MVSplat (−1.12 dB) and CF-3DGS (−1.04 dB), but this gap narrows or reverses on all OOD benchmarks, confirming superior domain generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

StreamGS sits at the intersection of two lines of work: feed-forward geometry prediction (DUSt3R/MASt3R) and generalizable 3D Gaussian Splatting (pixelSplat, MVSplat). It departs from generalizable 3DGS methods by eliminating the dependence on known poses and intrinsics, and from pose-free optimization-based methods (CF-3DGS) by replacing iterative per-frame optimization with a single feed-forward pass. The key conceptual departure is using content-adaptive feature matching (rather than DUSt3R's predicted 3D points) to establish correspondences, which enables both pose refinement and a 2D pixel-wise Gaussian merging strategy (feed-forward ADC) that avoids costly 3D-space operations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to achieve online, pose-free, generalizable 3DGS reconstruction from unposed image streams, filling a clear gap between offline optimization-based approaches and sparse-view generalizable splatting. The 150× speedup over CF-3DGS with comparable quality makes real-time interactive 3D reconstruction from monocular video practically feasible. However, the method remains slower than pose-dependent generalizable methods (MVSplat) due to the refinement step, and it inherits DUSt3R's limitations on textureless regions and very long sequences. Open questions include whether the DUSt3R dependency can be replaced by a lighter backbone, how the method scales to hundreds of frames without drift accumulation, and whether the ADC strategy generalizes to dynamic scenes.
