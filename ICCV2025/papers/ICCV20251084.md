## Problem

Existing color-matching methods either require paired source-target images at inference time, rely on global polynomial or MLP approximations that struggle with spatially nonuniform ISP-induced color discrepancies, or are computationally heavy for on-device deployment. This paper addresses the need for a single lightweight framework that performs accurate, spatially adaptive color matching across multiple tasks (raw-to-raw, raw-to-sRGB, sRGB-to-sRGB) in both supervised and unsupervised settings without requiring target images at test time.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a source image in either raw sensor space or sRGB space and outputs a single color-matched image in the target color space. In paired-based optimization mode, a corresponding target image is also provided at inference to fine-tune the model in 10 iterations. The full model has only 76.4K parameters (7.8K for the cmKAN-Light variant), and training uses patches of 256×256 (raw-to-raw), 448×448 (raw-to-sRGB), or 1024×1024 (sRGB-to-sRGB). The generator internally produces a 2D spatial parameter map with 90 KAN coefficients per pixel, which is the key intermediate representation driving the color transformation.

## Method

cmKAN replaces the standard global color-matching transform with a per-pixel KAN layer whose cubic B-spline coefficients (u, v, c parameters for each of 9 channel pairs) are predicted spatially by a lightweight hypernetwork generator. The generator comprises three modules: an Illumination Estimator (dilated depth-wise CNN producing illumination features and maps), a Color Transformer (ViT-inspired block with a novel Multi-Scale Color Attention that shifts attention from spatial to channel dimensions using strided-convolution anchors for efficient cross-channel covariance), and a Color Feature Modulator (memory-color-style modulation with trainable bias projections). Training supports three paradigms: supervised (L1 + SSIM loss), unsupervised (two-stage: 200-epoch pre-training with random color jitter reconstruction, then 500-epoch CycleGAN training with identity and cycle-consistency losses), and paired-based optimization (pre-trained cmKAN-Light fine-tuned on specific image pairs with L1 loss over 10 iterations). The KAN layer's spline-based nonlinearity provides smoother, more accurate approximations of complex color mappings compared to polynomial or MLP alternatives, while the spatially varying parameters enable localized correction of illumination and ISP artifacts.

## Results

On the proposed Huawei P40 Pro dual-camera sRGB dataset, cmKAN achieves PSNR 25.64 / SSIM 0.88 / ΔE 4.86 in the unsupervised setting, PSNR 25.94 / SSIM 0.89 / ΔE 4.51 in the supervised setting, and PSNR 28.06 / SSIM 0.88 / ΔE 3.88 in the paired-based setting (cmKAN-Light), all with only 76.4K (or 7.8K for the light variant) parameters. On the MIT-Adobe FiveK dataset for photographer-style transfer, cmKAN reaches PSNR 31.74 and ΔE 2.83, substantially outperforming SIRLUT (PSNR 27.25, ΔE 6.19). The paper reports an average 37.3% improvement over existing methods across all tasks and training paradigms, while remaining 300–400× lighter than transformer-based competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

cmKAN outperforms all 14 baselines across every scenario and metric. In the unsupervised setting, it beats RawFormer [54] (the strongest prior method) by +1.83 PSNR and −0.92 ΔE while using 342× fewer parameters (76.4K vs 26.1M) and running 6× faster (1.1s vs 6.7s). In the supervised setting, cmKAN surpasses SIRLUT [42] by +1.82 PSNR and −1.08 ΔE, and LYT-Net [10] by +1.75 PSNR, with comparable parameter counts to SIRLUT but 14× fewer than LYT-Net. In the paired-based setting, cmKAN-Light (7.8K params) achieves PSNR 28.06, exceeding Root-poly [25] by +3.53 PSNR and NeuralPreset [35] by +4.71 PSNR, while running 40× faster than NeuralPreset (0.5s vs 20.4s). On the MIT-Adobe FiveK dataset, cmKAN leads SIRLUT [42] by +4.49 PSNR and −3.36 ΔE. The only near-tie is in raw-to-raw PSNR (41.01 vs RawFormer's 40.98 for Samsung-to-iPhone), where cmKAN still wins decisively on ΔE (1.23 vs 2.09).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical color-matching transform (spatially varying linear + non-linear per-channel mapping, as formalized by Gil Rodríguez et al.) by replacing polynomial or learned non-linearities with KAN's trainable cubic B-spline basis functions, arguing that splines are a natural fit for the smooth, non-linear color corrections performed by camera ISPs. The hypernetwork architecture that generates spatially varying KAN parameters draws on the hypernetwork paradigm (Ha et al.) and the MCA module builds directly on RawFormer's channel-query attention, but shifts the attention computation from spatial to channel dimensions for efficiency. For unsupervised training, the paper adopts a CycleGAN-like framework, while the paired-based optimization variant (cmKAN-Light) is a simplified single-block architecture designed for rapid online fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is the extreme parameter efficiency: cmKAN uses 76.4K parameters versus 26–33M for competing methods (RawFormer, UVCGANv2) while achieving lower ΔE across all three task settings, making it genuinely viable for on-device camera ISP deployment. The introduced 2,520-image dual-camera paired dataset (Huawei P40 Pro, Quad-Bayer vs. RYYB sensors) fills a gap in publicly available cross-camera sRGB benchmarks. Open questions include whether the KAN-spline advantage persists for more complex multi-channel or HDR color spaces, how the method generalizes beyond the two-camera pairs tested, and whether the 10-iteration paired fine-tuning remains stable for highly divergent source-target color distributions.
