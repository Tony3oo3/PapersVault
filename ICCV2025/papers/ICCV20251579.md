## Problem

Existing robust 3D reconstruction methods (e.g., GLO, Bilarf, WildGaussians) jointly optimize per-view degradation parameters alongside the 3D scene representation, requiring dense observations to stabilize the optimization. When input views are sparse or highly inconsistent (photometric variation, transient occlusions, motion blur), these coupled approaches produce severe artifacts and floaters. The paper identifies that decoupling restoration from reconstruction is the missing step that makes robust reconstruction feasible under sparse observations.

## Input / Output

The method takes K inconsistent multi-view RGB images (20–50 per scene in experiments) with known camera poses, along with a user-selected style image. Images are sorted into a camera trajectory via ThreadPose, zero frames are inserted between neighboring views to form an f-frame initial video (f=25), and SAM-generated inpainting masks mark occluded regions. The output is K consistent restored images at 576×1024 resolution, from which a standard NeRF (ZipNeRF) or 3DGS reconstruction is performed. The style image controls the appearance of all restored frames, enabling style transfer of the final 3D scene.

## Method

UniVerse decouples robust reconstruction into two stages: (1) a fine-tuned video diffusion model (VDM) restores all inconsistent images to a consistent state, and (2) a standard differentiable renderer reconstructs the 3D scene. The VDM is initialized from ViewCrafter's interpolation model and fine-tuned progressively (320×512 then 576×1024) on DL3DV with synthetic inconsistencies. Key architectural additions include a Multi-input Query Transformer that aggregates CLIP embeddings from all N input images into a single global semantic embedding injected via cross-attention, inpainting masks to explicitly mark occluded pixels for the VDM to fill, and style masks to anchor appearance to a reference frame. A novel consistency loss reweights per-frame MSE so that input frames receive at least λ=0.98 of the total loss weight, forcing the VDM to prioritize making existing images consistent over generating the interpolated zero frames. This general scene prior learned from large-scale data replaces per-view handcrafted degradation modeling, making the approach robust to diverse and unknown inconsistencies.

## Results

On the real-world dataset, UniVerse achieves 19.65 PSNR / 0.6511 SSIM / 0.2532 LPIPS (sRGB) and 22.91 PSNR / 0.6998 SSIM / 0.2132 LPIPS (affine-aligned sRGB), outperforming all baselines by a wide margin. On the synthetic LLFF dataset with simulated inconsistencies, UniVerse reaches 18.09 PSNR / 0.5789 SSIM / 0.3015 LPIPS (sRGB) and 20.12 PSNR / 0.5926 SSIM / 0.2979 LPIPS (affine-aligned sRGB). The method consistently leads on all three metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) under both evaluation settings across both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniVerse outperforms all four baselines on every metric in both the synthetic and real-world evaluations. On the real-world dataset, UniVerse surpasses the strongest baseline (ZipNeRF w/GLO) by 3.51 dB in PSNR (19.65 vs. 16.14) and 2.24 dB in affine-aligned PSNR (22.91 vs. 20.67), while also achieving the best SSIM (0.6511 vs. 0.5668) and LPIPS (0.2532 vs. 0.3095). On the synthetic LLFF dataset, the margin over the best baseline (Bilarf) is 2.03 dB in PSNR (18.09 vs. 16.06) and 1.54 dB in affine-aligned PSNR (20.12 vs. 18.58). WildGaussians, the 3DGS-based method, performs worst overall, suggesting that Gaussian splatting is particularly sensitive to photometric inconsistencies without explicit restoration. The consistent gap across all baselines indicates that the decoupled restoration-then-reconstruction strategy provides a fundamental advantage over methods that jointly optimize degradation modeling and 3D representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the video diffusion model lineage (DynamiCrafter, ViewCrafter) by repurposing a VDM's learned 3D-consistency prior as an image restoration tool, rather than for video generation. It departs fundamentally from prior robust reconstruction methods (NeRF in the Wild/GLO, Bilarf, WildGaussians) that couple per-view degradation modeling with 3D optimization, instead decoupling the problem into a restoration stage (handled by the VDM) followed by standard reconstruction. The Multi-input Query Transformer extends DynamiCrafter's single-input design to aggregate global semantic context from multiple views, and the consistency loss reweights frame-level MSE to prioritize input-image coherence over novel-view generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling framework offers a clean conceptual simplification: by leveraging a VDM's general scene prior learned from large-scale data, the method avoids the instability of jointly optimizing per-view degradation parameters with 3D structure, which prior methods require dense observations to resolve. The ability to control reconstructed scene style via a reference image and to handle extremely sparse inputs (as few as 2 views) opens practical applications in AR/VR and autonomous driving where controlled captures are unavailable. A key limitation is the need to synthesize inconsistent training data for VDM fine-tuning, and whether generative models can realistically simulate all types of in-the-wild inconsistencies (e.g., complex lighting changes) remains an open question.
