## Problem

Pre-trained Gaussian denoisers are known to generalize poorly to real-world noise, and existing methods that avoid requiring specific clean/noisy pairs (e.g., Noise2Void, Neighbor2Neighbor) must train their own networks from scratch on noisy images alone. This paper asks whether a powerful off-the-shelf Gaussian denoiser can be directly repurposed for real-world denoising without any retraining, by learning a lightweight model-free variance-stabilizing transformation (VST) that maps the unknown real noise distribution to approximately Gaussian.

## Input / Output

The method requires only a single noisy image (grayscale, RGB, or raw-RGB Bayer) and a frozen off-the-shelf Gaussian denoiser (DRUNet or FFDNet). For raw-RGB data, the 4-channel Bayer image is split into two overlapping 3-channel images, denoised separately, and recombined by averaging shared channels. Training is performed on randomly cropped 64×64 patches with data augmentation (flips, 90° rotations); the total number of learnable parameters is just 130 (128 spline y-values plus two affine correction terms for the inverse). The output is a denoised image in the original domain.

## Method

Noise2VST parameterizes the VST as an increasing continuous piecewise linear (CPWL) function with 128 uniformly spaced knots, where only the y-values are learned via an exp-based parameterization that guarantees monotonicity. The inverse transform is the algebraic inverse of the CPWL function plus a learned affine correction (αz + β) to mitigate the bias typically introduced by a naive inverse. Training exploits the blind-spot strategy: a blind-spot variant of the frozen Gaussian denoiser (constructed via pixel partitioning à la Noise2Self) is used to compute the self-supervised loss ‖(f_inv ∘ D̄ ∘ f)(z) − z‖², which equals the true risk up to a constant under the spatially independent zero-mean noise assumption. Because only 130 parameters are optimized against a frozen deep network, overfitting is negligible even on a single image. At inference, the blind-spot denoiser is replaced by the full non-blind Gaussian denoiser, recovering the information lost at the blind spot and eliminating checkerboard artifacts.

## Results

On the SIDD benchmark (raw-RGB smartphone images), Noise2VST achieves 51.07 dB PSNR / 0.991 SSIM, surpassing the supervised baseline (50.60/0.991) and the best self-supervised competitor SST-GP (50.87/0.992). On the SIDD validation set, it reaches 51.66/0.992, again outperforming all baselines. On the W2S microscopy dataset, Noise2VST leads by a clear margin (e.g., 36.88 dB on channel 2 avg1 vs. 35.18 dB for DivNoising), and on synthetic Poisson noise it matches the oracle-parameter GAT+DRUNet baseline within 0.06 dB across Kodak, BSD300, and Set14.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Noise2VST outperforms all 18 compared baselines on the SIDD benchmark and validation sets, beating the strongest self-supervised method SST-GP by 0.20 dB (benchmark) and 0.09 dB (validation) in PSNR, and exceeding the supervised N2C baseline by 0.47 dB on the benchmark. On the W2S microscopy dataset, it leads every channel and noise level, surpassing DecoNoising by up to 1.70 dB (ch2 avg1: 36.88 vs. 35.09) and DivNoising by 1.70 dB. On FMD, Noise2VST wins on confocal fish (32.88 vs. 32.79 baseline) and two-photon mice (34.06 vs. 34.03 B2UNB) but trails B2UNB slightly on confocal mice (38.27 vs. 38.44). On synthetic Poisson noise, Noise2VST matches the oracle-parameter GAT+DRUNet baseline within 0.06 dB on all three datasets, while clearly outperforming N2V, S2S, ZS-N2N, and R2R; however, B2UNB and SST-GP edge it out on BSD300 by ~0.1 dB. The fast variant Noise2VST† (using FFDNet instead of DRUNet) achieves nearly identical quality at roughly half the GPU runtime (20 s vs. 50 s for 256×256 images).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the blind-spot self-supervised training paradigm from Noise2Self and Noise2Void, but applies it not to train a denoiser from scratch, but to learn a lightweight model-free variance-stabilizing transformation (a 128-knot piecewise linear spline) that bridges an off-the-shelf Gaussian denoiser to real-world noise. It departs from traditional VST pipelines (Anscombe/GAT) by removing the need for a parametric noise model and its parameter estimation, and from prior zero-shot denoisers (N2V, ZS-N2N, B2UNB) by leveraging a powerful pre-trained Gaussian denoiser with frozen weights rather than training a denoising network on the input image. The 'visible blind spots' inference trick—switching from the blind-spot denoiser used in training to the full denoiser at test time—is borrowed from B2UNB but applied here in the VST context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: practitioners can immediately deploy state-of-the-art Gaussian denoisers (DRUNet, FFDNet) on real-world images without collecting application-specific clean/noisy pairs or retraining, at a cost of only ~50 seconds per image and 130 learnable parameters. A key limitation is the dependence on the off-the-shelf denoiser's robustness to slight distribution shifts; the authors note that transformer-based denoisers (Restormer, X-Former) failed within this framework, suggesting the approach is most effective with CNN-based denoisers. Open questions include whether the model-free VST can be extended to structured or spatially correlated noise (e.g., after demosaicking), whether a single learned VST can generalize across multiple images, and whether the approach transfers to other restoration tasks beyond denoising.
