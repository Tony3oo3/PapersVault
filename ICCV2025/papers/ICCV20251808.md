## Problem

Standard multiple instance learning (MIL) for whole slide image (WSI) analysis enforces permutation invariance, which destroys the spatial correlations between tissue patches that are essential for accurate classification. The paper demonstrates empirically and theoretically that relaxing this constraint and explicitly modeling spatial/semantic correlations between instances yields superior performance, yet no prior method has leveraged instance ordering information in this way.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted patch-level feature embeddings (from ResNet-18, Swin-ViT, or CTransPath backbones) arranged in their original 2D spatial grid, with approximately 11,000–13,000 patches per slide at 20× magnification. For the CNN variant, the 1D sequence of instance embeddings is reshaped into a 2D tensor with padding to exploit convolutional locality. The network outputs a bag-level class prediction (binary or multi-class) via global average pooling and a linear head, or a discrete-time survival risk score optimized with negative log-likelihood loss.

## Method

The core contribution is a shuffling equivalence regularization that frames WSI analysis as an instance jigsaw puzzle: a Siamese network with shared weights processes both the original instance arrangement X and a randomly shuffled version S[X], and a loss penalizes the MSE between f(S[X]) and S[f(X)], forcing the network to learn semantics that are invariant to reordering. This is theoretically justified as the solution to an inverse optimal transport problem, where the known transport plan is the inverse shuffling S⁻¹ and the learned cost function operates on instance embeddings. The relaxation of permutation invariance enables the use of 2D CNNs (stacks of convolutional residual blocks) alongside transformers, which can exploit spatial inductive biases that standard MIL aggregators cannot. The final training objective combines binary cross-entropy (or survival NLL) with the equivalence loss weighted by λ, and the approach consistently outperforms state-of-the-art MIL baselines across classification and survival prediction benchmarks.

## Results

On CAMELYON16 with Swin-ViT features, the proposed method (Transformer variant) achieves 89.53% accuracy, 88.57% F1, and 92.17% AUC, surpassing the strongest MIL baseline (DGR-MIL) by 1.93%, 2.10%, and 3.98% respectively. On TCGA-NSCLC, the same variant reaches 92.32% accuracy, 92.31% F1, and 96.40% AUC. For survival prediction, the CNN variant achieves C-Index of 0.683 on TCGA-LUAD and 0.757 on TCGA-BRCA, outperforming the second-best method by an average of 3.97% across both datasets. The method consistently outperforms all MIL baselines across three feature extractors (Swin-ViT, ResNet-18, CTransPath) and both network architectures (CNN, Transformer).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all eight MIL baselines across every dataset, metric, and feature extractor tested. The strongest competitor, DGR-MIL (ECCV'24), is surpassed by 1.93% accuracy and 3.98% AUC on CAMELYON16 (Swin-ViT), and by 1.44% accuracy and 0.59% AUC on TCGA-NSCLC. TransMIL (NeurIPS'21), which shares the same transformer architecture as the proposed method, is beaten by 4.49% accuracy and 3.91% AUC on CAMELYON16, confirming that the gain comes from the jigsaw puzzle regularization rather than architecture. The DTFD-MIL variants (MaxS, AFS, MaxMinS) show the largest F1 gaps (up to 9.7% for AFS on CAMELYON16), indicating that distillation-based MIL struggles more with imbalanced instance distributions. For survival prediction, the CNN variant outperforms the second-best MHIM-MIL by 3.3% C-Index on TCGA-LUAD (0.683 vs. 0.650) and 2.1% on TCGA-BRCA (0.757 vs. 0.736). Notably, even with the same transformer backbone, the proposed method surpasses TransMIL by an average of 5.61% C-Index on survival tasks, isolating the contribution of the shuffling equivalence loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard MIL paradigm for WSI analysis by rejecting its core permutation-invariance assumption, arguing instead that spatial/semantic correlations between instances are essential. Conceptually, it repurposes the jigsaw puzzle pretext task from self-supervised representation learning (Noroozi & Favaro, 2016) into a supervised regularization for WSI classification, and formally grounds the resulting shuffling equivalence loss in inverse optimal transport theory (Li et al., 2019; Stuart & Wolfram, 2020). Methodologically, it builds on the TransMIL transformer architecture for fair comparison while introducing a Siamese network structure and, notably, a plain CNN variant that had not been explored in the WSI MIL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that permutation invariance is not only unnecessary but actively harmful for WSI analysis, this work opens the architectural space to CNNs and other spatially-aware networks for gigapixel pathology images, which were previously excluded by the MIL constraint. The negative instance collapse phenomenon observed under the equivalence regularization offers a mechanistic explanation for the performance gains and connects WSI analysis to the broader neural collapse literature. Open questions include whether the Siamese overhead can be eliminated, whether the approach extends to end-to-end (non-two-stage) training, and whether the instance jigsaw puzzle formulation generalizes to other bag-level problems beyond histopathology.
