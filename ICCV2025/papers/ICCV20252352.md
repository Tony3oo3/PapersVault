## Problem

Existing CNN-based and Transformer-based multi-organ segmentation networks rely on fixed-size kernels or patches for information aggregation, which inherently conflicts with irregular organ morphologies and introduces cross-organ interference in densely packed anatomical regions. This leads to imprecise shape modeling and ambiguous boundary delineation, particularly where organs are spatially interleaved or adherent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252352/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes 224×224 CT or MRI slices as input (no data augmentation applied) and produces pixel-wise multi-organ segmentation masks. In the encoder, each pixel is embedded as a graph node, and in the decoder, upsampled nodes are reconstructed via category-guided graph connections rather than standard interpolation. Evaluation spans five datasets (CHAOS-T2, ACDC, BTCV, Cervix, CMR) covering both CT and MRI modalities with 3–13 organ classes.

## Method

DGRNet is a U-shaped graph-based segmentation network that replaces fixed-grid aggregation with dynamic graph reconstruction. The encoder (Dynamic Graph Encoding) embeds pixels as nodes, builds a similarity-driven top-K adjacency graph, and propagates features via EdgeConv. A Dynamic Perception module maintains learnable category units that self-update through directed message passing from feature nodes, encoding organ-specific priors. The decoder (Dynamic Graph Reconstruction) guides upsampling by connecting each new node to its Top-1 most compatible category unit and restricting spatial edges to adjacent encoder counterparts, thereby enforcing anatomical continuity and category-specific boundary delineation. Training uses a composite Dice-CE loss plus a category-perception loss that decorrelates category units, with dynamic weighting that prioritizes decorrelation early in training.

## Results

DGRNet achieves state-of-the-art DSC across all five datasets: 0.8692 (CHAOS-T2), 0.9454 (ACDC), 0.8657 (BTCV), 0.6927 (Cervix), and 0.9171 (CMR). The most substantial gains are on BTCV (+8.8 pp DSC over the best baseline UTNet) and Cervix (+11.15 pp DSC over EMCAD), where complex multi-organ boundaries are most challenging. DGRNet uses 10.8G FLOPs and 20.2M parameters, placing it in the mid-range of computational cost among compared methods. It also leads on IoU across all datasets, with the largest IoU margin on Cervix (0.5436 vs. 0.4246 for UTNet).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGRNet outperforms all eight baselines on every dataset and both metrics (DSC and IoU). The largest margins appear on BTCV, where DGRNet (0.8657 DSC) surpasses the best baseline UTNet (0.7777) by 8.8 percentage points, and on Cervix, where it leads EMCAD (0.5812) by 11.15 pp and EGE (0.5703) by 12.24 pp. On the cardiac datasets (ACDC, CMR), the advantage is more modest: DGRNet gains 1.69 pp over UTNet on ACDC and 1.39 pp on CMR, suggesting the graph-based approach is most beneficial for dense abdominal multi-organ scenarios. Among baselines, CNN-based methods (EGE, SelfRegUNet, TinyUNet) and hybrid networks (UTNet, Daeformer, EMCAD) generally outperform pure Transformer approaches (MedT, SwinUNet), with UTNet being the strongest single competitor. In terms of efficiency, DGRNet (10.8G FLOPs, 20.2M params) is heavier than TinyUNet (1.3G, 0.5M) and EMCAD (4.3G, 26.8M) but lighter than EGE (34.8G) and Daeformer (26.1G), while MedT is the most parameter-heavy at 35.5M.

## Relation to Prior Work

DGRNet departs from the dominant information-aggregation paradigm (fixed-size CNN kernels and Transformer patches) by adopting an information-reconstruction paradigm grounded in dynamic graph topology, repurposing EdgeConv from point-cloud processing [25] for 2D medical image segmentation. While prior graph-based methods in vision (e.g., Vision GNN [10]) use graphs for general feature extraction, DGRNet specifically leverages graph subgraph decomposition to model irregular organ morphology and introduces a category-aware guidance mechanism—learnable category units that direct reconstruction—that has no direct analogue in prior multi-organ segmentation work. The U-shaped backbone and training protocol follow standard medical segmentation practice, so the novelty is concentrated in the graph encoding/reconstruction blocks and the category-perception loss rather than in the overall architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a compelling conceptual argument that fixed-grid aggregation is fundamentally mismatched to irregular anatomical shapes, backed by large DSC gains (8.8% on BTCV, 11.15% on Cervix) across five datasets with fair re-training of all baselines. The category-aware guidance mechanism is a practical, modular technique that could be transplanted into other dense multi-class segmentation settings beyond organs. However, the computational overhead of dynamic graph construction (10.8G FLOPs, 20.2M params) limits immediate applicability to 2.5D and 3D volumetric segmentation, and the authors acknowledge that lightweight graph construction remains an open problem. Whether the graph-based advantage persists at 3D scale, and whether the category-aware loss generalizes to tasks with far more classes or open-vocabulary settings, are important follow-up questions.
