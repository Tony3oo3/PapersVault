## Problem

Supervised AI-generated face detectors overfit to artifacts of specific training generators and fail to generalize to novel generative models, while existing self-supervised approaches use pretext tasks designed without explicit regard for the detection objective, leaving them suboptimal. This paper addresses the gap by asking how to dynamically align self-supervised pretraining with AI-generated face detection without ever exposing the model to synthetic faces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250111/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224×3 RGB face images; during pretraining, EXIF metadata (categorical tags like camera make, ordinal tags like ISO/aperture) is encoded as text templates and jointly embedded with images via a CLIP-style architecture (ResNet-50 vision encoder + GPT-2 text encoder). At inference, the frozen encoder produces a 768-d feature vector that feeds either a 10-component GMM (yielding a log-likelihood anomaly score thresholded at the 5th percentile) or a lightweight 768→1536→2 two-layer perceptron (yielding a binary AI-generated probability). No AI-generated faces appear in the self-supervised pretraining pipeline.

## Method

BLADES formulates self-supervised pretraining as a bi-level optimization: the inner loop updates encoder parameters θ by minimizing a linearly weighted sum of four pretext losses (coarse-grained face manipulation detection as a surrogate primary task, categorical EXIF tag classification with focal loss, ordinal EXIF tag ranking via Thurstone's model, and fine-grained region-level manipulation detection), while the outer loop adjusts the task weight vector λ to minimize the surrogate primary loss on a held-out validation batch, thereby automatically prioritizing the most detection-relevant pretext objectives. All tasks are implemented through a shared joint image-text embedding space where similarity scores between image features and task-specific text templates are converted to probabilities via softmax or sigmoid. After pretraining, the text encoder is discarded and the vision encoder is frozen; detection then proceeds either as one-class anomaly detection (GMM fitted to photographic face embeddings) or as binary classification (two-layer MLP trained on a small set of photographic and AI-generated embeddings with pseudo-outlier augmentation). The key distinction from prior self-supervised detectors is that task weights are not fixed or hand-tuned but are learned end-to-end to steer the feature space toward the downstream detection goal.

## Results

On the primary cross-generator evaluation (Table 1), BLADES-BC achieves 91.86% average detection accuracy across nine generative models, outperforming the strongest supervised baseline FatFormer (89.68%) by 2.18 points. The one-class variant BLADES-OC, trained exclusively on photographic faces without any AI-generated examples, reaches 88.01% average accuracy—surpassing all supervised detectors except FatFormer. In the sensitivity/specificity analysis, BLADES-BC attains 94.64% TNR and 88.97% TPR (F-score 0.91), and in feature separability (Table 4), BLADES-OC achieves 95.05% average AUC, the highest among all compared feature extractors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BLADES-BC (91.86% avg accuracy) outperforms all ten competing detectors, with the closest rival being FatFormer [32] at 89.68%—a 2.18-point gap that widens to over 13 points against DIRE [53] (78.66%) and over 19 points against LGrad [47] (72.43%). The one-class variant BLADES-OC (88.01%), which never sees AI-generated faces during training, surpasses every supervised baseline except FatFormer, and also beats the prior self-supervised method Zou25 [66] (86.63%) by 1.38 points. In the feature separability AUC comparison (Table 4), BLADES-OC achieves 95.05% average AUC, exceeding Zou25 (93.43%), EAL [61] (84.12%), CLIP [41] (76.13%), and FaRL [62] (75.12%), while handcrafted corneal-highlight features (Hu21 [22]) perform at chance level (~50%). Under mild JPEG compression (Table 5), BLADES-BC maintains 85.82% average AUC versus FatFormer's 78.33%, confirming superior robustness. In terms of efficiency (Table 6), BLADES-BC requires 8× fewer parameters (26M vs. 202M), 12× fewer FLOPs, and runs 20× faster than FatFormer, making it substantially more deployable without sacrificing accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own prior work on self-supervised EXIF-based anomaly detection for AI-generated faces [66] by introducing a bi-level optimization scheme that dynamically adjusts pretext task weights to align pretraining with the downstream detection objective. The outer-loop task-weighting mechanism is adapted from Auto-Lambda [33], which disentangles dynamic task relationships in multi-task learning, here repurposed to steer self-supervised feature learning toward a surrogate manipulation-detection task. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior self-supervised detectors (Ojha23, LNP, EAL) that use fixed or heuristically chosen pretext tasks by making the task weighting an explicit optimization variable, and from supervised detectors that overfit to generator-specific artifacts by relying exclusively on photographic training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical practical gap: as new generative models emerge rapidly, detectors must generalize without retraining on new synthetic data. The bi-level optimization provides a principled, mathematically grounded alternative to ad-hoc multi-stage training heuristics for aligning self-supervised pretraining with a specific downstream goal. However, performance degrades noticeably under mild JPEG compression (91.86% → 78.87% accuracy), and the method is restricted to face images, leaving open the question of whether the surrogate manipulation-detection task generalizes to broader natural-scene forgery. The authors also identify robustness to benign post-processing and integration with multimodal LLMs for explainable forensics as important future directions.
