## Problem

Vision Transformers achieve strong representations through quadratic self-attention, but this O(L²) cost becomes prohibitive for high-resolution and long-sequence inputs. While linear-time recurrent alternatives like Mamba-based models exist, they have been trained only at modest scales and lack the rich token-wise dependency structure that ViTs develop. The paper asks how to effectively transfer ViT's quadratic knowledge into linear-time recurrent architectures without significant performance degradation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251146/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images patchified into non-overlapping 16×16 tokens (or 14×14 for large models) and produces class labels (ImageNet-1k) or pixel-level segmentation masks (ADE20K at 512×512, Cityscapes at 512×1024). A key asymmetry in the pipeline is that the frozen ViT teacher receives the complete image while the recurrent student receives a masked version with 75% of patch tokens replaced by a learnable [mask] token, so the student must reconstruct the teacher's representations at unseen positions.

## Method

ViT-Linearizer distills a pretrained ViT (e.g., CLIP ViT-Base/16) into a linear-complexity Adventurer model with Mamba-2 token mixers using two complementary objectives. First, activation matching computes pairwise cosine-similarity activation maps at K intermediate stages for both teacher and student, then minimizes the L2 distance between their normalized rows (restricted to visible, unmasked tokens to prevent information leakage), effectively forcing the recurrent student to replicate the teacher's token-wise dependency structure. Second, masked prediction requires the student to predict the teacher's final-layer representations at masked positions using a Smooth L1 loss, following the MAE-style asymmetric masking strategy. The total loss is L_act + λ·L_mask (λ=1 by default), and the quadratic O(L²) cost of the activation matching loss is argued to be a necessary component for faithfully transferring quadratic self-attention knowledge.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k classification at 224×224, ViT-Linearizer (distilling CLIP ViT-B/16 into Adventurer-Base with Mamba-2) achieves 84.3% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the supervised DeiT-III-Base (83.8%) and Vim-Base (81.9%) while matching the CLIP teacher (84.7%) within 0.4%. At 448×448, the distilled model reaches 85.0% with a 2.1× inference speedup over the ViT teacher. On ADE20K semantic segmentation (512×512), it attains 51.3% mIoU with 2.74× throughput, and on Cityscapes (512×1024), it achieves 82.0% mIoU with a 4.21× speedup, both slightly exceeding the CLIP ViT teacher's mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the CLIP ViT-B/16 teacher, ViT-Linearizer sacrifices only 0.4% top-1 accuracy at 224×224 (84.3% vs. 84.7%) and 0.3% at 448×448 (85.0% vs. 85.3%), while delivering a 2.1× inference speedup and reducing memory from >80 GB to 45.2 GB at 448 resolution. Compared to the supervised DeiT-III-Base (83.8%), the distilled model gains 0.5% accuracy with 1.2× higher throughput. Against the Mamba-based Vim-Base (81.9%), ViT-Linearizer improves accuracy by 2.4% and throughput by 4.1× (736 vs. 180 img/s). Versus the supervised Adventurer-Base baseline (82.6%), the distillation adds 1.7% accuracy without any change in model size or inference speed. On ADE20K segmentation, the distilled model (51.3% mIoU) outperforms all baselines including the CLIP teacher (51.0%), DeiT-III (49.3%), and supervised Adventurer (47.8%), while on Cityscapes it achieves 82.0% mIoU versus 81.8% for CLIP and 80.2% for supervised Adventurer, with a 4.21× throughput advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cross-architecture distillation lineage from DeiT (CNN→ViT) and recent NLP Transformer-to-RNN distillation (Kasai et al., Bick et al., Mercat et al.) into the purely visual domain, specifically targeting ViT→Mamba-2 transfer. It builds on the Adventurer architecture (a Mamba-2-based plain vision model) and the masked image modeling paradigm (MAE, BEiT), but introduces two key departures: an activation-matching loss that enforces a quadratic O(L²) constraint on intermediate token-wise dependencies, and an asymmetric masked-prediction objective where the student must reconstruct the teacher's representations for unseen tokens. Unlike prior linear-attention or SSM vision models trained from scratch, this work positions distillation as the mechanism to inject the 'quadratic knowledge' that self-attention encodes at high computational cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for high-resolution and long-context vision tasks: a 4.2× inference speedup on Cityscapes (512×1024) with no accuracy degradation, and a 2.1× speedup on 448×448 ImageNet with only 0.3% loss, makes previously infeasible resolutions tractable on consumer hardware. The finding that a linear-time model can match or slightly exceed its ViT teacher (85.0% vs 85.3% at 448×448) challenges the assumption that quadratic attention is necessary for top-tier visual understanding. Open questions include whether the approach scales to the 50K+ token regimes explored in recent patchification work, whether the O(L²) activation-matching loss during training becomes a bottleneck at extreme sequence lengths, and whether the gains persist for generative or multi-modal tasks beyond classification and segmentation.
