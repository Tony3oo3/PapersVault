## Problem

Existing CZSL methods treat attributes and objects as independent entities during representation disentanglement, overlooking two critical phenomena: semantic constraints (e.g., 'striped' applies to 'zebra' but not 'sky') and contextual dependencies (e.g., 'young' looks different in 'young tree' vs. 'young dog'). This independence assumption causes the model to assign similar scores to multiple attributes without object context, leading to erroneous predictions on unseen compositions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (shorter side 224px at inference) and produces a composition label (attribute-object pair) by additively aggregating three probability scores: composition p(c|x), conditional attribute p(a|x, v^o), and object p(o|x). The visual backbone (ViT-B or CLIP ViT-L/14) extracts both deep-level features (last transformer block) for object learning and shallow-level features (3rd, 6th, 9th blocks) for attribute learning, while frozen GloVe (300-dim) or trainable CLIP text embeddings (1024-dim) provide the semantic anchors for both attributes and objects.

## Method

CPF decomposes the composition likelihood as p(o,a|x) = p(o|x)·p(a|o,x) and jointly optimizes both factors. For object likelihood, a text-enhanced object learning module first constructs a textual descriptor by projecting the deep-level class token into the text space and computing a weighted combination of object embeddings, then uses this descriptor as a query in cross-attention over deep-level patch tokens to highlight semantically relevant regions, yielding an enhanced object feature v^o. For conditional attribute likelihood, an object-guided attribute learning module uses v^o as a query in cross-attention over shallow-level patch tokens, so that attribute representation is explicitly conditioned on the recognized object, capturing both semantic constraints and contextual dependencies. A third composition-matching loss aligns the concatenated [v^a, v^o] visual feature with the concatenated [w^a, w^o] textual feature. Training minimizes L = L_com + 0.6·L_att + 0.4·L_obj with cross-entropy losses (τ=0.05), and inference uses an additive score formulation to avoid the probability-vanishing issue of multiplicative decomposition.

## Results

Under the Closed-World (CW) setting with ViT-B backbone, CPF achieves AUC of 11.2 (MIT-States), 41.4 (UT-Zappos50K), and 8.2 (C-GQA), with corresponding HM scores of 26.8, 55.7, and 23.9. Under the more challenging Open-World (OW) setting, CPF reaches AUC of 4.4, 31.2, and 2.10 on the three datasets respectively, without relying on external knowledge. With a CLIP backbone on C-GQA, CPF attains AUC 15.4 (CW) and 3.6 (OW), outperforming all prior CLIP-based CZSL methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPF outperforms all 25 baselines across all three datasets in both CW and OW settings. In CW, the most notable gains are +17.9% AUC over ADE [9] on UT-Zappos50K (41.4 vs. 35.1), +6.7% over CoT [22] on MIT-States (11.2 vs. 10.5), and +10.8% over CoT on C-GQA (8.2 vs. 7.4). In OW, CPF improves AUC by +175% over the best prior method (ProCC [13], 1.9) on MIT-States, +8.3% over ADE on UT-Zappos50K (31.2 vs. 27.1), and +47.9% over ADE on C-GQA (2.10 vs. 1.42), all without external knowledge. With CLIP backbone on C-GQA, CPF surpasses the strongest prior method LOGiCZSL [59] by +0.1 AUC in CW (15.4 vs. 15.3) and +0.2 AUC in OW (3.6 vs. 3.4). The consistent wins across both evaluation protocols and both backbone types (ViT-B and CLIP) confirm that the conditional probability decomposition generalizes broadly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant CZSL paradigm of treating attributes and objects as independent entities to be disentangled through separate modules or attention mechanisms. While recent works (CoT, CANet) began addressing contextual dependencies, they remained ineffective at modeling semantic constraints between attributes and objects. CPF synthesizes the probabilistic decomposition perspective (p(o,a|x) = p(o|x)·p(a|o,x)) with a concrete architectural mechanism: text-enhanced object features guide attribute learning via cross-attention, making the conditional dependency explicit rather than implicit. The framework is also designed to be modular and pluggable into existing CZSL pipelines or CLIP-based systems, positioning it as a general-purpose component rather than a standalone model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By challenging the long-standing independence assumption in CZSL, this work provides a principled probabilistic justification for why disentanglement-based methods fail on unseen compositions, and offers a clean, minimal mechanism (cross-attention conditioned on text-enhanced object features) to fix it. The large relative gains in the open-world setting (e.g., +175% AUC on MIT-States) suggest that modeling attribute-object interdependence is especially critical when the search space is unconstrained. However, absolute performance remains modest (e.g., 4.4 AUC on MIT-States OW), and the authors acknowledge that semantic ambiguity among class labels and visually confusing targets remain unresolved, pointing toward LLM-generated discriminative descriptions as a future direction.
