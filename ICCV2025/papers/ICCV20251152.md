## Problem

Existing single domain generalization methods apply uniform style perturbations to entire images, failing to simulate complex real-world scenes where different regions exhibit distinct colors and textures. Prior approaches enhance either color diversity (via Gaussian noise) or texture diversity (via random convolutions) but not both simultaneously, leaving a gap in generating augmented data that matches the multi-region stylistic complexity of real-world images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251152/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images from a single source domain and produces class labels via a CNN feature extractor and classifier. During training, each input image is split into an N×N grid of patches that undergo independent style augmentation before being recombined, so the generative module consumes and outputs full-resolution images while internally operating on sub-image patches. The training pipeline is progressive: K style generation modules are trained sequentially in an adversarial max-min game against a shared representation learner.

## Method

SAC first applies Patch-Aware Augmentation (PAA), which encodes the image, divides the latent representation into N×N patches, and applies independent Gaussian-noise-driven style transfer (AdaIN/AdaIN_noise/StyIN) to each patch, yielding distinct local colors. It then applies Adaptive Random Convolutions (ARC), which iteratively (up to 10 times) applies a deformable convolution with random weights multiplied element-wise by a Gaussian filter, followed by affine transformation, to enhance texture complexity while smoothing cross-patch boundaries. An entropy-based adaptive control (Eq. 9) determines per-sample whether further augmentation iterations should proceed, preventing semantic distortion in already-diverse samples. An energy-based distribution discrepancy score (Helmholtz free energy from classifier logits) is maximized to push augmented data further from the original distribution. The overall training uses a progressive adversarial max-min game: the style generator is trained to maximize distributional shift while the representation learner is trained to maintain classification accuracy on both original and augmented data.

## Results

SAC achieves state-of-the-art average accuracy across all four benchmarks: 88.3% on Digits (+3.6% over PSDG), 71.2% on PACS (+0.8% over PSDG), 81.7% on CIFAR-10-C (+1.6% over UDP), and 28.4% on DomainNet (+0.7% over AdvFrequency). The largest gains appear on Digits, where SAC leads every individual target domain (e.g., 91.0% on MNIST-M vs. 86.7% for PSDG) and on CIFAR-10-C's Noise and Digit corruption categories (83.8% and 80.4% vs. 79.9% and 76.1% for UDP). On PACS, SAC achieves the best Sketch accuracy (64.6%) despite using simplistic images without backgrounds as training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAC outperforms all 13 baselines on average across all four datasets. On Digits, SAC leads every target domain, beating the strongest baseline PSDG by 3.6% on average (e.g., 91.0% vs. 86.7% on MNIST-M, 87.9% vs. 83.4% on SYN). On PACS, SAC wins on Art (79.2% vs. 78.2%), Cartoon (79.2% vs. 78.3%), and Sketch (64.6% vs. 63.0%) over PSDG, though it trails AdvST on Photo (61.7% vs. 64.1%). On CIFAR-10-C, SAC dominates the Noise (83.8% vs. 79.9%) and Digit (80.4% vs. 76.1%) categories over UDP but slightly underperforms on Weather (80.1% vs. 80.7%) and Blur (82.6% vs. 83.6%). On DomainNet, SAC achieves the best Sketch (34.9% vs. 31.2% for AdvFrequency) and Clipart (43.3% vs. 42.7%) scores but lags on Infograph (13.9% vs. 15.1%), yielding a modest 0.7% average advantage. Gradient-based methods (ADA, M-ADA, ME-ADA) and frequency-based methods (AdvFrequency) consistently underperform style-based generative approaches, confirming that visually perceptible style augmentation is critical for large domain shifts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the style-based augmentation paradigm for single domain generalization, building directly on the encoder-decoder generative framework of PDEN and the random deformable convolution mechanism of RandConv/Pro-RandConv. The key conceptual departure is that prior methods enhance style diversity along a single axis—either color (via Gaussian noise in UDP, PSDG) or texture (via random convolutions in RandConv, Pro-RandConv)—whereas SAC explicitly combines both by splitting images into patches for independent color perturbation (PAA) and then recombining them with adaptive random convolutions for texture enrichment (ARC). The entropy-based adaptive iteration control and the energy-based distribution discrepancy score are novel additions not present in any of the cited predecessors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated limitation: single-aspect style augmentation produces images that are either color-rich but texture-poor or texture-rich but color-uniform, failing to match the multi-region complexity of real-world domain shifts. The plug-and-play nature of SAC (demonstrated by consistent gains when added to PDEN, L2D, PSDG, and Pro-RandConv) makes it a practical component for the broader SDG community. Open questions include whether the iterative deformable convolution overhead scales to larger datasets or higher-resolution images, whether the entropy threshold (0.3 × log C) generalizes across architectures, and whether the approach extends beyond classification to tasks like segmentation or detection where spatial coherence is more critical.
