## Problem

Exemplar-based semantic image synthesis aims to generate images matching a target segmentation while preserving the local appearance of a reference image. Existing structure-guidance models (e.g., ControlNet) rely solely on text prompts and cannot exploit exemplar images, while tuning-free attention-based methods that inject exemplar keys/values into self-attention are restricted to single-object or foreground-centric transfer and produce category mismatches (e.g., cars matched to trees) in complex, multi-object scenes.

## Input / Output

The method takes an exemplar RGB image and a target segmentation map of the same spatial dimensions (H×W). The exemplar is encoded by a VAE and inverted to the noise level via DDIM inversion, while the target is synthesized from random Gaussian noise conditioned on the segmentation. At inference, an automatic retrieval step selects the best-matching exemplar from a pool based on grayscale structural similarity to a randomly generated image from the target segmentation, eliminating the need for manual exemplar selection. The output is a single RGB image that preserves the local, object-level appearance of the exemplar while strictly following the target segmentation structure.

## Method

AM-Adapter introduces a learnable 4D cost aggregation module that refines the implicit query-key matching cost in augmented self-attention by concatenating it with a binary categorical matching cost derived from pixel-wise correspondences between the exemplar and target segmentation maps. The 4D convolution network captures locally consistent regions across the 4D (h×w×h×w) matching space, and its output is added residually to the original implicit cost for stable training. A dual-branch architecture separates an Appearance Net (which reconstructs the exemplar via DDIM inversion) from a Structure Net (which generates the target from noise via ControlNeXt), with exemplar keys and values injected into the Structure Net's self-attention at each denoising step. Training is stage-wise: ControlNeXt is first trained for structural control, then the diffusion model is fine-tuned with ControlNeXt frozen, and finally AM-Adapter is trained with both the diffusion model and ControlNeXt frozen, disentangling generation from matching. At inference, classifier-free guidance is applied to the refined matching cost to further strengthen exemplar-to-target correspondence.

## Results

On the combined BDD100K and NYUv2 benchmark, AM-Adapter achieves the best scores across all five metrics: Self-Similarity 0.041 (lower is better), I_DINO 0.765, DINO[cls] loss 0.056, I_CLIP 0.819, and FID 75.89. The closest competitor, ControlNet+IP-Adapter, trails by 10.6 FID points (84.82 vs. 75.89) and 0.014 in I_CLIP similarity (0.805 vs. 0.819). Tuning-free methods such as Cross-Image Attention (FID 233.76) and FreeControl (FID 179.00) degrade substantially in image quality, while also failing to preserve structural alignment (Self-Sim 0.177 and 0.062, respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AM-Adapter outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest learned baseline, ControlNet+IP-Adapter, it improves FID from 84.82 to 75.89 (≈10% relative reduction) and I_CLIP from 0.805 to 0.819, while also achieving the best structural consistency (Self-Sim 0.041 vs. 0.049). The structure-guidance-only baselines (ControlNet, ControlNeXt) lag significantly in appearance fidelity (I_CLIP 0.638–0.731) and image quality (FID 94–95), confirming that text prompts alone cannot convey exemplar-specific local details. Among tuning-free attention-control methods, Ctrl-X is the closest in appearance (I_CLIP 0.700) but still trails AM-Adapter by 0.119 in I_CLIP and 31 FID points; Cross-Image Attention and FreeControl suffer severe structural misalignment (Self-Sim 0.177 and 0.062) and poor image quality (FID 233.76 and 179.00), indicating that implicit query-key matching without semantic guidance fails in multi-object scenes. The ablation (Table 2) further shows that simply fine-tuning augmented self-attention (row V) or adding the categorical cost without the 4D aggregation module (row VI) yields only marginal gains over the untrained baseline (row IV), underscoring that the full AM-Adapter architecture is necessary for the observed improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the augmented self-attention paradigm for exemplar-based appearance transfer (MasaCtrl, DreamMatcher, Ctrl-X, Cross-Image Attention) by introducing a learnable 4D convolution adapter that refines implicit query-key matching costs using categorical matching derived from segmentation maps. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) instead of hand-crafted attention manipulation or global CLIP-based projection (IP-Adapter), it learns a lightweight matching adapter in a stage-wise fashion that keeps the diffusion model and ControlNeXt frozen, and (2) it generalizes from single/foreground-object transfer to multi-object scene-level transfer by leveraging semantic correspondence between exemplar and target segmentation maps. The 4D cost aggregation module draws on ideas from Neural Matching Fields and HyperCorrelation Squeeze, repurposing them for cross-image appearance matching within diffusion self-attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AM-Adapter addresses a practical gap: prior exemplar-based methods fail on complex, content-rich scenes with multiple objects, which are the norm in driving, indoor, and AR/VR applications. The stage-wise training strategy (freeze diffusion + ControlNeXt, train only the adapter) makes the approach computationally efficient and avoids the instability of joint matching-generation training, a finding validated by the ablation showing fine-tuning actually degrades performance. However, evaluation is limited to 300 pairs from two datasets (BDD100K, NYUv2) with no established benchmark, and the method's reliance on accurate segmentation maps as input limits its applicability to domains where such annotations are unavailable. Open questions include whether the categorical matching cost generalizes to open-vocabulary or hierarchical scene understanding, and whether the 4D convolution approach scales to video or 3D generation.
