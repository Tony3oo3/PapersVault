## Problem

Despite REC models achieving over 90% accuracy on standard benchmarks like RefCOCO, localizing extremely small objects (occupying ~0.1% of image area) in high-resolution driving scenes remains a significant challenge. No dedicated dataset existed for small-object REC, and the combination of long descriptive expressions and tiny bounding boxes creates a distributional gap that standard fine-tuning struggles to bridge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250753/cropped_page0_idx5.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-resolution driving image (average 3407×2470 pixels) and a natural language referring expression (average 25.5 words, roughly 7× longer than typical RefCOCO expressions) as input. Rather than producing a single bounding box in one forward pass, the model autoregressively outputs a sequence of progressively smaller bounding boxes (zooming steps, averaging 2.11 steps), where each intermediate box defines the crop region fed back into the model for the next step. The final bounding box localizes the target object, which occupies less than 1% of the image area.

## Method

PIZA (Progressive-Iterative Zooming Adapter) models small-object localization as an autoregressive search process over a sequence of bounding boxes from the full image to the target. A lightweight 0.27M-parameter module extracts zooming-step embeddings from the history of bounding boxes using learnable Fourier embeddings, a small transformer encoder, and average pooling, with two auxiliary heads predicting whether to continue searching (EOS) and the fractional progress of the search. These embeddings are injected into existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning frameworks (CoOp, LoRA, or Adapter+) so that the frozen GroundingDINO backbone receives step-aware conditioning at each iteration. Training uses an extended dataset where ground-truth search processes are synthesized by sampling zoom factors from the pre-training bounding-box area-ratio distribution, ensuring the learned zooming schedule aligns with the model's prior. The best configuration, PIZA-Adapter+, uses only 3.5M learnable parameters yet surpasses full fine-tuning (173M parameters) on the SOREC benchmark.

## Results

On the SOREC dataset (Train-S), PIZA-Adapter+ (3.5M trainable params) achieves 36.8 mAcc / 53.5 Acc50 / 41.8 Acc75 on Validation, 43.1 / 59.6 / 50.1 on Test-A, and 30.4 / 45.9 / 34.1 on Test-B. This surpasses full fine-tuning of GroundingDINO (173M params) by 7.3 mAcc on Val and 7.4 mAcc on Test-B while using 49× fewer learnable parameters. With the larger Train-L split, PIZA-Adapter+ further improves to 59.5 mAcc on Validation, demonstrating strong data scaling. The zero-shot GroundingDINO baseline achieves near-zero performance (0.2 mAcc), highlighting the difficulty of small-object REC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PIZA-Adapter+ (3.5M params) outperforms full fine-tuning of GroundingDINO (173M params) by 7.3 mAcc on Val (36.8 vs. 29.5), 7.2 on Test-A (43.1 vs. 35.9), and 7.4 on Test-B (30.4 vs. 23.0), while using only 2% of the trainable parameters. Against the three PEFT baselines, PIZA consistently yields large gains: PIZA-Adapter+ improves over Adapter+ by 10.8 mAcc on Val (36.8 vs. 26.0), PIZA-LoRA over LoRA by 9.3 mAcc (30.9 vs. 21.6), and PIZA-CoOp over CoOp by 6.1 mAcc (26.3 vs. 20.2). The zero-shot GroundingDINO baseline is essentially non-functional on SOREC (0.2 mAcc), confirming that the extreme small-object scale (bounding boxes ~0.05% of image area) and long expressions (~25.5 words) are far outside the pre-training distribution. Among PEFT methods, Adapter+ and LoRA-based approaches outperform prompt-based CoOp, suggesting that the large distribution gap requires more capacity than prompt tuning alone can provide. The paper also shows PIZA-Adapter+ is 7.3× faster and uses 49.4× fewer parameters than the best sliding-window baseline, while achieving higher accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) literature for vision-language models by introducing PIZA, a zooming-step embedding module inspired by time-step embeddings in diffusion models, applied on top of GroundingDINO. Rather than treating REC as a single-shot prediction, PIZA reframes small-object localization as an autoregressive search process over progressively cropped image regions, integrating with existing PEFT methods (CoOp, LoRA, Adapter+) by injecting the zooming-step embedding into each. The dataset contribution (SOREC) fills a gap identified in the related work: while small object detection datasets exist (SODA, VisDrone, DOTA), no prior REC dataset targeted extremely small objects (~0.1% of image area) in driving scenarios, and existing REC benchmarks (RefCOCO family) feature normal-sized objects with short expressions (~3.5 words vs. ~25.5 words here).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SOREC provides the first large-scale benchmark for REC on extremely small objects in driving, a setting critical for autonomous driving safety but previously unaddressed in the REC literature. PIZA's ability to match or exceed full fine-tuning with 50× fewer parameters (3.5M vs. 173M) makes progressive zooming a practical option for adapting large vision-language models to scale-shifted domains. Limitations include the driving-scenario focus, the semi-automated dataset construction relying on GPT-4o (with 18.45% of test expressions containing minor errors), and evaluation on a single base model (GroundingDINO with Swin-T). Extending to video, diverse environments, and other backbone architectures remain open.
