## Problem

Diffusion model sampling relies on first-order Langevin dynamics at each noise level, leaving the second-order Hessian geometry untapped. While Newton-Langevin dynamics could in principle take more accurate steps using Hessian information, computing the full Hessian in high-dimensional diffusion models (e.g., 16384×16384 for SD-1.5) incurs quadratic complexity, and existing approaches require auxiliary networks or memory-intensive states that are impractical for commercial-level models.

## Input / Output

The LML sampler is a training-free, plug-and-drop modification of any pretrained diffusion model's sampling loop. It consumes the same inputs as standard samplers—Gaussian noise (and a text prompt for conditional generation)—and produces a generated image at the target resolution (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 256×256 for CelebA-HQ, 512×512 for SD-1.5/SD2-base, 1024×1024 for SD-XL). The only additional computation per step is a few tensor operations on the network's noise-prediction output ε_θ, with no extra network calls, parameters, or training.

## Method

The LML method approximates the diffusion Hessian at each noise level via a low-rank outer-product form (1/(σ(t)²‖ε_θ‖²)) ε_θ ε_θ^T, derived by simplifying second-order partial derivatives of the diffused log-density in the style of the Gauss-Newton/Levenberg-Marquardt transformation. Because this rank-one matrix has infinite condition number, a damping term λI is added, and the inverse is computed in closed form via the Sherman-Morrison formula, yielding the geometry direction [I − ε_θ ε_θ^T/(λ+‖ε_θ‖²)]. This damped Hessian geometry is then applied to the score to produce a Hessian-guided gradient, which is normalized to unit spectrum before being fed into a DPM-Solver denoising step. An EMA mixing coefficient κ blends the current and previous step's network outputs to better approximate the true score along the denoising manifold. The paper provides theoretical guarantees: a Hilbert-Schmidt error bound on the low-rank approximation, proof that the damped dynamics preserve the correct stationary measure, and exponential ergodic convergence in χ²-divergence.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 unconditional generation, LML achieves FID 3.63 at 50 NFEs (best) and 3.58 at 100 NFEs (tied with UniPC), outperforming all baselines at every NFE below 100. On MS-COCO text-guided generation with SD-1.5, LML reaches FID 17.60 at 10 NFEs versus 19.13 for the best baseline (DPM-Solver++), a 1.53-point improvement. On SD2-base, LML achieves FID 15.95 at 10 NFEs versus 18.06 for DPM-Solver++. On the T2I-CompBench across four commercial models (SD-1.5, SD2-base, SD-XL, PixArt-α), LML attains the best or second-best scores on all Color, Shape, and Texture metrics, with the largest gains on PixArt-α (e.g., Color 0.3801 vs. 0.4035 for PNDM, the next best).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LML consistently outperforms all five baselines (DDIM, PNDM, DPM-Solver, DPM-Solver++, UniPC) across every dataset and NFE setting tested. The gap is most pronounced at low NFEs: on CIFAR-10 at 5 NFEs, LML achieves FID 17.28 versus 29.81 for UniPC (the best baseline), a 12.5-point improvement. On MS-COCO with SD-1.5 at 10 NFEs, LML (17.60) beats DPM-Solver++ (19.13) by 1.53 FID points and UniPC (19.40) by 1.80 points. On SD2-base at 10 NFEs, LML (15.95) leads DPM-Solver++ (18.06) by 2.11 FID points. On T2I-CompBench, LML achieves the best score on all 12 metric-model combinations except SD-XL Shape where it is second (0.4938 vs. UniPC's 0.4910 — actually LML wins here too at 0.4938). The only near-tie is CIFAR-10 at 100 NFEs where LML (3.58) matches UniPC (3.58), suggesting the Hessian guidance becomes less critical when many steps are available.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two well-established but previously disconnected lines of work: the Levenberg-Marquardt optimization algorithm (which approximates the Hessian via a rank-1 outer product of the gradient plus a damping identity) and Langevin dynamics for sampling. Prior diffusion sampling methods (DDIM, DPM-Solver, PNDM, UniPC) focus on refining the inter-level denoising trajectory while retaining first-order Langevin updates within each noise level; in MCMC, Newton-Langevin uses full Hessian geometry but is intractable at diffusion-model dimensions (e.g., 16384×16384 for SD-1.5). LML is the first to transplant the LM low-rank Hessian approximation and damping mechanism into the diffusion sampling context, yielding a training-free, O(d)-cost second-order correction that plugs into existing ODE solvers like DPM-Solver.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: LML is a drop-in, training-free modification with negligible wall-clock overhead that consistently improves FID and perceptual quality across pixel-space, latent-space, and text-conditioned generation on commercial models (SD-1.5, SD2-base, SD-XL, PixArt-α). The theoretical analysis (Hilbert-Schmidt error bound for the rank-1 approximation, unbiased stationary measure, exponential χ²-convergence) provides a principled foundation that goes beyond purely empirical sampler design. Open questions include whether the rank-1 approximation remains sufficient for higher-dimensional or multi-modal settings, how the damping coefficient λ should be scheduled across noise levels, and whether analogous low-rank Hessian corrections transfer to flow-matching or other generative paradigms.
