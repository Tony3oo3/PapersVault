## Problem

Volumetric 3D scene reconstruction from a single image typically requires expensive 3D ground truth from accumulated LiDAR scans or multi-view supervision from specialized camera rigs, both of which are costly to obtain and fragile to dynamic objects. This paper asks whether pre-trained 2D generative models (diffusion) and depth predictors can substitute for such supervision, enabling high-quality scene geometry generation and distillation from single images alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252351/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (384×1280 on KITTI-360, 320×480 on Waymo) and produces a discretized 3D occupancy field of resolution 64×H×W with inverse spacing between 3 m and 50 m, along with a self-calibrating uncertainty field of the same shape. During the synthesis stage, the pipeline also generates multiple synthetic novel views (RGB + depth) at predefined camera poses, which are used both to refine the occupancy field and to supervise the distilled model. The depth predictor (Metric3D) operates off-the-shelf, providing per-pixel metric depth without additional training.

## Method

The approach has three stages. First, a View Completion Model (VCM) implemented as a ControlNet on StableDiffusion-2.1-unCLIP is trained via forward-backward warping from a single image, receiving warped RGB, incomplete depth, an inpainting mask, and a CLIP embedding to inpaint occluded regions and remove rendering artifacts. Second, the render-refine-repeat loop iteratively renders novel views from a synthetic occupancy field (a binary field where a point is occupied iff it lies behind the predicted depth in every active view), uses the VCM to complete them, predicts depth, and fuses the new views back into the occupancy field; occlusions are detected via Sobel gradients on the inverse depth map. Third, a ResNet-50 U-Net scene reconstruction model is distilled from the synthesized geometries using two losses: an uncertainty-weighted occupancy loss and a Gaussian NLL depth loss computed by volume-rendering the predicted occupancy from the same synthetic camera poses, providing surface-level supervision that is particularly informative for invisible and empty regions.

## Results

On KITTI-360, the full Dream-to-Recon pipeline achieves the best overall occupancy accuracy (O_acc = 0.93), invisible/empty region accuracy (IE_acc = 0.72), and recall (IE_rec = 0.75), outperforming multi-view-supervised baselines BTS (0.92/0.69/0.64) and KYN (0.92/0.70/0.72). On Waymo, it reaches O_acc = 0.97, IE_acc = 0.73, and IE_rec = 0.96, matching or exceeding BTS (0.95/0.63/0.94) and the depth-supervised BTS variant (0.95/0.61/0.96). The distilled feed-forward model (Ours Distilled) achieves 0.90/0.71/0.71 on KITTI-360 and 0.96/0.72/0.93 on Waymo, remaining competitive while being significantly faster at inference. Notably, the method handles dynamic objects that cause BTS to fail, and requires only single-image training data rather than multi-view sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BTS [60], the strongest multi-view volumetric baseline, Dream-to-Recon wins on all three metrics on both datasets: O_acc improves from 0.92→0.93 (KITTI-360) and 0.95→0.97 (Waymo), while IE_acc jumps from 0.69→0.72 and 0.63→0.73 respectively. Adding depth supervision to BTS (BTS+depth) yields only marginal gains (IE_acc 0.70, IE_rec 0.66 on KITTI-360) and still trails the proposed method, which the authors attribute to inconsistent depth scales harming multi-view training. Compared to KYN [27], which reports 0.92/0.70/0.72 on KITTI-360, the full method improves O_acc by 0.01 and IE_rec by 0.03, while the distilled variant (0.90/0.71/0.71) is roughly on par. Against the depth-only baselines UniDepth (0.89) and Metric3D-ViT-Large (0.91) on KITTI-360, the proposed method achieves higher O_acc and additionally provides occlusion-aware IE metrics that depth methods cannot report. A key qualitative advantage is that BTS fails to reconstruct dynamic objects due to multi-timestep inconsistency, whereas Dream-to-Recon handles them naturally since it operates on a single image.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the render-refine-repeat framework—previously applied to novel view synthesis (Infinite Nature, Text2Room, LucidDreamer)—specifically for high-quality geometry generation, then distills the results into a feed-forward model. It draws on the forward-backward warping training strategy from DiffDreamer to train a ControlNet-based view completion model using only a single image per scene, and leverages off-the-shelf metric depth prediction (Metric3D) as a geometric prior. Conceptually, it departs from multi-view supervised volumetric reconstruction methods (BTS, KYN) by eliminating the need for multi-camera data or accumulated LiDAR ground truth, instead synthesizing training geometry from 2D generative priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is that volumetric scene reconstruction can now be trained without expensive 3D annotations or multi-view sensor rigs, and it handles dynamic objects naturally since each scene is reconstructed from a single static image. Limitations include reliance on the quality of the off-the-shelf depth predictor (errors propagate into geometry), occasional artifacts in synthesized scenes from imperfect view completion, and the compute-intensive generation phase that necessitates the distillation step. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond driving scenes to indoor or unstructured environments, whether the synthetic occupancy field formulation can be made differentiable for end-to-end optimization, and whether the distilled model's slight performance gap versus direct synthesis can be closed with larger training sets.
