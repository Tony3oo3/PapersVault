## Problem

Existing multi-person motion prediction methods (MRT, TBIFormer, JRT, T2P, SoMoFormer) rely on Transformer-based architectures to model spatial and temporal interactions, incurring large parameter counts (3.4M–12.9M) and high computational costs. This paper asks whether a lightweight, non-Transformer architecture can match or exceed their accuracy while using only 1–10% of the parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252281/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes historical 3D joint sequences of P persons over T frames (X ∈ R^{3J×P×T}) and predicts future joint positions for T′ frames. To handle the order ambiguity of individuals, a Permutation-Invariant Person Sorting (PIPS) step reorders persons by descending pairwise hip distances before processing, and an inverse PIPS restores the original ordering at output. Temporal dynamics are compressed via DCT before feature learning and recovered via IDCT at prediction, so the network operates in a low-frequency spectral domain rather than raw frame space.

## Method

EMPMP replaces all attention mechanisms with lightweight linear layers and layer normalization. The core ESTFL stage contains two blocks: (1) a Multi-level Estimation (ME) block with a dual-stream design — a local stream applies N=16 iterations of shared temporal linear transforms per person, while a global stream applies M=1 iteration of a person-temporal (PT) linear transform over the merged C×PT dimension; and (2) a Cross-level Interaction (CI) block that performs bidirectional refinement via learned affine transformations (scale + translation for local, translation for global) and explicitly injects an inter-person hip-distance embedding to capture spatial proximity. The full pipeline uses K=4 ESTFL stages, a two-dimensional linear prediction head, and is trained with a combined joint-position and velocity L2 loss. This design achieves SOTA MPJPE/VIM/APE on 3DPW, CMU-Syn, and MuPoTS-3D with only 0.04M–0.65M parameters.

## Results

On 3DPW-Ori (1030ms/900ms), EMPMP achieves MPJPE 131.8, VIM 53.3, and APE 98.6, outperforming the best prior method T2P (137.5 / 56.3 / 115.1) by 5.7, 3.0, and 16.5 points respectively. On CMU-Syn (2s/2s), it reaches MPJPE 128.0, VIM 50.2, APE 83.2, again beating T2P (136.4 / 54.6 / 94.5). Across all eight evaluation settings, EMPMP attains the best or near-best scores on all three metrics while using only 0.04M parameters (vs. 3.39M–12.91M for baselines), a reduction of over 98%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMPMP outperforms all five Transformer-based baselines (MRT, SoMoFormer, TBIFormer, JRT, T2P) on every metric in the standard 3DPW-Ori, 3DPW-RC, and CMU-Syn settings, with the largest margins on APE (e.g., 98.6 vs. 115.1 for T2P on 3DPW-Ori, a 16.5-point gap). The closest competitor is T2P [20], which is the strongest baseline on most settings; EMPMP still leads T2P by 5.7 MPJPE on 3DPW-Ori and 8.4 MPJPE on CMU-Syn 2s/2s. In the cross-dataset CMU-Syn/MuPoTS 1s/1s setting, SoMoFormer [41] edges out EMPMP on MPJPE (89.1 vs. 92.4) and VIM (39.3 vs. 40.6), though EMPMP wins on APE (76.2 vs. 80.6). In the pre-trained AMASS/3DPW settings, EMPMP achieves the best scores in four of six comparisons and remains competitive in the other two. The most striking advantage is efficiency: EMPMP uses 0.04M parameters (0.65M with pre-training) versus 3.39M–12.91M for the baselines, a 98–99% reduction, while also requiring substantially fewer FLOPs (1.67G vs. 39.83G for an attention-based variant).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant Transformer-based paradigm in multi-person motion prediction (MRT, SoMoFormer, TBIFormer, JRT, T2P) by replacing self-attention and cross-attention with lightweight linear layers and layer normalization, following the MetaFormer/MLP-Mixer insight that attention is not the essential component of deep architectures. It extends the single-person MLP-based pose forecasting line (Motion Mixer, SiMLP) to the multi-person setting by introducing a dual-stream Multi-level Estimation block (local per-person and global scene-level temporal updates) and a Cross-level Interaction block that fuses these streams via learned affine transformations. The explicit inter-person distance embedding and Permutation-Invariant Person Sorting are novel design choices that address spatial interaction modeling and order ambiguity without resorting to attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1–10% parameter reduction (0.04M vs. 3.4M–12.9M) while achieving consistent SOTA across six evaluation settings on three datasets makes multi-person motion prediction substantially more deployable in resource-constrained settings such as real-time VR/AR or edge devices. The result reinforces the broader MetaFormer thesis that attention is not a prerequisite for strong sequential modeling, but its specific application to multi-agent spatial-temporal interaction is the first demonstration in this domain. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 2–3 persons, whether stochastic or multi-modal future prediction is feasible within this lightweight framework, and whether the DCT-based temporal compression generalizes to longer prediction horizons.
