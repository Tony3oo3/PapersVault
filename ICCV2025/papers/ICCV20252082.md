## Problem

Existing affordance learning focuses on static human-object interaction patterns (contact regions, spatial relationships), while dynamic HOI patterns—object-specific movement over time during interaction—remain largely unexplored. Compounding this, 4D HOI datasets are scarce and typically captured in lab-controlled settings, limiting the ability to learn diverse dynamic affordance patterns across a broad range of object categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252082/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D object mesh (from sources like ShapeNet, SAPIEN, SketchFab) and a text prompt (object category name plus optional hand-index annotation) as input, and produces a temporally aligned 4D HOI motion sequence: per-frame SMPL-X human parameters (21×3 body pose, 10 shape, root rotation/translation) and per-frame object pose (6D rotation + 3D translation). The human motion is generated first by the LoRA-adapted MDM, and the object pose sequence is then synthesized conditioned on the generated human motion, so the two outputs are jointly consistent rather than independently sampled.

## Method

DAViD comprises two components: (1) a pre-trained Motion Diffusion Model (MDM) fine-tuned per object category via a LoRA module inserted into the transformer encoder's multi-head attention layers, trained with a joint-recovery loss on human motion extracted from synthesized 4D HOI samples; and (2) a score-based diffusion model (following GenPose architecture) that predicts the conditional object pose distribution given SMPL-X body pose, trained with a denoising score-matching objective. At inference, a novel Temporal Guidance Sampling (TGS) technique augments the Probability Flow ODE with gradient-based smoothness and Chamfer-distance contact guidance terms, encouraging temporally consistent and physically plausible object trajectories rather than independent per-frame sampling. The training data pipeline synthesizes 4D HOI samples by rendering the 3D object from multiple viewpoints, generating HOI images with FLUX + ControlNet, animating them into 2D videos with Kling AI, then uplifting to 4D via PnP (object) and GVHMR/TRAM (human) with a depth-scale optimization step. A key advantage of the LoRA design is that separately trained HOI motion concepts can be merged via weighted summation and combined with the pre-trained MDM's existing motion vocabulary (e.g., walking, jumping) to produce novel multi-object HOI motions in a zero-shot manner.

## Results

On the FullBodyManipulation Dataset, DAViD achieves a contact F1 score of 0.634 (vs. CHOIS at 0.330) and 0.616 (vs. OMOMO at 0.552), with contact recall of 0.517 and 0.488 respectively. For human motion quality, DAViD reports lower foot sliding (FS = 0.234–0.247) and foot height (2.30–2.35 cm) than both CHOIS (FS = 0.472, Hfeet = 3.47 cm) and OMOMO (FS = 0.255, Hfeet = 2.58 cm). The ablation in Table 2 further shows that Temporal Guidance Sampling reduces object jitter from 30.9×10³ to 2.95×10³ (close to the real-data level of 10.7), and LoRA preserves pre-trained MDM knowledge (FID 1.30 vs. 2.93 without LoRA on HumanML3D).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAViD outperforms both baselines on all reported metrics. Against CHOIS [52], DAViD improves contact F1 by 0.304 (0.634 vs. 0.330) and contact recall by 0.296 (0.517 vs. 0.221), while also reducing foot sliding from 0.472 to 0.234 and foot height from 3.47 to 2.30 cm. Against OMOMO [51], DAViD achieves a higher contact F1 (0.616 vs. 0.552) and recall (0.488 vs. 0.434), with marginally lower FS (0.247 vs. 0.255) and foot height (2.35 vs. 2.58 cm). MDM [89] is included only as a human-motion quality reference (no HOI contact metrics), and DAViD matches or slightly improves upon its FS and Hfeet values. The comparison is not perfectly symmetric: CHOIS and OMOMO condition on object motion/waypoints, while DAViD conditions on human motion, so the paper restricts comparison to HOI contact quality for fairness.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAViD extends the affordance learning literature from static patterns (contact regions, spatial distributions) to dynamic, object-specific 4D interaction patterns, building on prior work that used 2D diffusion models to synthesize static HOI images for affordance learning. For the generative model, it adapts the Motion Diffusion Model (MDM) with LoRA—a technique borrowed from image/LLM concept customization—applied here for the first time to the human motion space, and pairs it with a score-based diffusion model for object pose estimation following GenPose. The data synthesis pipeline repurposes pre-trained video diffusion models (Kling AI) and 3D lifting tools (GVHMR, PnP) to produce 4D HOI training samples, extending the 2D-image-based synthesis approaches of prior static affordance work into the temporal domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical data bottleneck in 4D HOI learning by showing that video diffusion models already encode plausible dynamic interaction patterns, enabling training without expensive multi-view capture. The LoRA-based concept combination—merging multiple object-specific HOI concepts with pre-trained human motions (walking, jumping) to produce novel interactions—opens a path toward scalable, composable HOI generation. Limitations include reliance on the quality of video diffusion outputs, the depth-scale ambiguity resolution being heuristic, and evaluation limited to a single dataset (FullBodyManipulation) with hand-centric interactions. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to full-body manipulation, physically plausible force modeling, and whether the synthesized 4D samples faithfully capture the full distribution of real-world interactions.
