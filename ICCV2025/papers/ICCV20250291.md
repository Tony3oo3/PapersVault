## Problem

Naive MLLM-based text-video retrieval relies on candidate likelihood P(t|v), which is distorted by candidate prior bias: candidates with inherently higher prior probabilities (e.g., long, repetitive text or static videos) are favored over those truly relevant to the query. This paper is the first to formally identify and characterize this bias in MLLM-based retrieval, showing that a single high-prior text can be retrieved for 37% of irrelevant videos.

## Input / Output

A video input is segmented into 4 clips of 4 frames each (16 frames total), encoded by UMT into visual tokens, and concatenated with text tokens before being fed to the LLM. At inference, a two-stage pipeline first retrieves top-16 candidates per query using InternVideo2 1B, then BLiM reranks them by jointly scoring both candidate and query likelihoods. The output is a ranked list of candidates with calibrated relevance scores; CPN decoding can also be applied as a training-free post-hoc calibration to any MLLM's text generation output.

## Method

BLiM is built on VideoChat-Flash 7B (UMT encoder + linear projection + Qwen2 LLM) and is trained with a bidirectional likelihood objective: video-grounded text generation P(t|v) via standard autoregressive cross-entropy, and text-grounded video feature generation P(v|t) via a softmax over candidate clip features conditioned on the text. During inference, both likelihoods are summed to rank candidates, so that query likelihood counteracts the prior bias embedded in candidate likelihood. Additionally, Candidate Prior Normalization (CPN) is a training-free calibration that divides the candidate likelihood by P(candidate)^α, directly subtracting the prior's contribution from the score. Only the linear projection layer is updated with LoRA, keeping the backbone frozen for parameter efficiency.

## Results

On four Text-Video Retrieval benchmarks (DiDeMo, ActivityNet, LSMDC, MSRVTT), fine-tuned BLiM achieves R@1 of 86.4, 81.0, 55.7, and 64.7 for text-to-video and 82.8, 74.4, 49.1, and 62.2 for video-to-text retrieval, outperforming the previous best (InternVideo2 6B) by an average of 6.4 R@1. In the zero-shot setting, BLiM⁻ (CPN only, no query likelihood) already surpasses InternVideo2 6B by 4.9 R@1 on average. On Text-Image Retrieval (Flickr30K, COCO), BLiM achieves R@1 of 92.1 (T2I) and 97.9 (I2T) on Flickr30K, beating BLIP-2 by 2.4 and 0.3 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BLiM (fine-tuned) achieves the best R@1 across all four datasets in both retrieval directions, beating InternVideo2 6B by 12.2 R@1 on DiDeMo T2V, 6.9 on ActivityNet T2V, 9.3 on LSMDC T2V, and 1.9 on MSRVTT T2V (average +6.4). In the zero-shot setting, BLiM⁻ (CPN applied to candidate likelihood only) already outperforms InternVideo2 6B by 4.9 R@1 on average, demonstrating that CPN alone provides substantial gains without requiring the bidirectional training objective. Against MLLM-based reranking methods (MM-Embed, RagVL, LamRA), BLiM leads by 3.0–5.0 R@1 on all four datasets (Table 3), confirming that direct bidirectional likelihood estimation outperforms logit-based or retrieval-augmented reranking strategies. On Text-Image Retrieval, BLiM surpasses BLIP-2 on 3 of 4 settings (Flickr30K T2I +2.4, Flickr30K I2T +0.3, COCO T2I +1.4) but trails slightly on COCO I2T (84.2 vs. 85.4). For CPN decoding on understanding benchmarks, applying CPN to VideoChat2, LLaVA-Onevision, and InternVL2 yields average improvements of +11.8, +4.4, and +4.1 points respectively across seven benchmarks, with the largest gains on MME perception/cognition scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the VideoChat-Flash 7B video MLLM (UMT encoder + Qwen2 LLM) and departs from the standard MLLM-based retrieval paradigm (MM-Embed, RagVL, LamRA) that relies solely on candidate likelihood P(t|v) for scoring. The key conceptual departure is identifying candidate prior bias as a systematic failure mode of autoregressive MLLMs in retrieval, and addressing it through bidirectional likelihood estimation (jointly training P(t|v) and P(v|t)) plus a training-free score calibration (CPN). The two-stage retrieval pipeline (InternVideo2 1B retrieval + BLiM reranking) follows established practice in efficient MLLM-based retrieval, while the CPN decoding extension to VQA and captioning connects to the broader literature on language-prior mitigation in multi-modal generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of candidate prior bias as a fundamental, quantifiable issue in MLLM-based retrieval (with a single high-prior text retrieved for 37% of irrelevant videos) provides a clear diagnostic for practitioners building MLLM retrievers. CPN's training-free, model-agnostic nature makes it a practical plug-and-play module applicable beyond retrieval to VQA and captioning, where it reduces hallucination by shifting reliance from textual to visual evidence. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to larger candidate pools without the two-stage bottleneck, whether the CPN hyperparameter α requires per-task tuning, and whether the bidirectional training objective transfers to other MLLM backbones beyond VideoChat-Flash.
