## Problem

Existing 3D scene editing and synthesis methods operate on simple synthetic scenes with 5–20 objects, while real-world 3D scans contain 69–306 objects that overwhelm the context windows and spatial reasoning of LLMs/VLMs. No prior approach can perform instruction-driven, functional rearrangement of such complex, cluttered real-world scans while maintaining physical plausibility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251964/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D scan mesh reconstructed from an RGB-D sequence, a semantic instance decomposition into N objects, and a natural language editing instruction. The RGB-D sequence is rasterized into 2D object crops to feed a VLM for attribute annotation (color, material, description). The output is a rearranged scene where each object receives a new 4×4 transformation (x, y translation and z-axis rotation), preserving original object identities while satisfying the instruction and physical constraints (no collisions, no floating, within scene bounds).

## Method

ScanEdit constructs a hierarchical scene graph with VLM-annotated node attributes (class, color, material, front normal, point cloud, support surfaces) and three spatial edge types (on-top-of, facing, against-wall) that LLMs struggle to infer. An LLM agent filters the full graph to a relevant subgraph for the instruction, then a planner LLM decomposes the high-level prompt into per-object localized instructions in each parent's reference frame, using multiple-hypothesis generation for robustness. A hierarchical placement LLM then assigns relative (x,y,z) poses and orientations to child objects. Finally, a convex differentiable optimization resolves LLM-generated constraints via four custom losses: a signed-distance on-top-of loss against support surface contours, an OBB-based against-wall loss, a point-cloud collision loss with a stop condition, and a group loss preserving relative inter-object vectors — all optimized with SGD and cosine annealing over 3 DOF per object.

## Results

On 15 real-world scenes (ScanNet++ and Replica) with 126 evaluation samples, ScanEdit achieves 88.87% NoFloat and 99.22% InBound, substantially outperforming LayoutGPT (51.34% / 62.12%) and LayoutVLM (66.47% / 85.01%). ScanEdit also attains the lowest collision volume (ColVol 1.3774 m³) and best PIoU (0.498), while achieving the highest CLIP score (22.2) for instruction adherence. The ablation (Table 2) confirms that the full pipeline—hierarchical planning, placement, and joint optimization—contributes incrementally, with the final optimization step adding ~12 points to NoFloat and ~10 points to InBound over placement alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ScanEdit outperforms both baselines on all five metrics. Against LayoutGPT [11], which relies on in-context retrieval without explicit optimization, ScanEdit improves NoFloat by 37.5 points (88.87% vs. 51.34%) and InBound by 37.1 points (99.22% vs. 62.12%), reflecting LayoutGPT's inability to handle spatial constraints in cluttered scenes. Against LayoutVLM [44], which uses VLM-based initialization followed by differentiable optimization, ScanEdit still leads by 22.4 points on NoFloat (88.87% vs. 66.47%) and 14.2 points on InBound (99.22% vs. 85.01%), while also reducing ColVol from 1.4813 to 1.3774 m³. The CLIP score gap is smaller (22.2 vs. 21.9 and 21.6), suggesting both baselines capture semantic intent reasonably well but fail primarily on geometric plausibility. The perceptual study (36 human raters) further confirms that users strongly prefer ScanEdit's outputs in both instruction adherence and layout quality over both baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM/VLM-driven 3D scene synthesis line (LayoutGPT, LayoutVLM, Holodeck) from generating synthetic scenes with ~5–20 objects to editing complex real-world 3D scans containing 69–306 objects. It departs from prior scene editing work (Instruct-Nerf2Nerf, Text2Room, RoomDreamer) by preserving original object identities and rearranging them rather than regenerating geometry. The key conceptual contribution is a hierarchical scene graph decomposition that makes LLM-based planning tractable at scale, combined with convex differentiable losses (support-surface, against-wall, collision, group) that enforce physical plausibility — a hybrid of LLM semantic reasoning and explicit 3D geometric constraints not seen in prior retrieval-based or diffusion-based scene synthesis methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to handle instruction-driven editing of cluttered real-world 3D scans at the scale of hundreds of objects, addressing a practical gap between synthetic scene generation and the complexity of captured environments. The hierarchical decomposition strategy (subgraph identification → localized planning → hierarchical placement → joint optimization) offers a reusable template for scaling LLM-based spatial reasoning beyond context-window limits. Key open questions remain: the method cannot reason about aesthetic or common-sense placement quality (e.g., which spot on a shelf is most natural for a vase), struggles with small/long-tail objects under machine-generated masks, and the partial nature of real scans leaves visible holes after rearrangement that require post-hoc filling.
