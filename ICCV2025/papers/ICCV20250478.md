## Problem

Existing video identity customization methods rely on self-reconstruction of static reference images, which causes two compounding failures: identity consistency degrades as video length increases (due to the domain shift between single-frame references and multi-frame sequences), and motion dynamics progressively diminish as training progresses (due to distribution shift from reconstructing static content). No prior work has applied preference optimization to directly address both issues simultaneously in personalized video generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250478/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of static reference images (as few as one) and a free-form text prompt describing the desired scene and action. It produces a 61-frame personalized video at the resolution supported by the underlying HunyuanVideo DiT model. The reference images are consumed in two ways: first to fine-tune a LoRA adapter (1000 steps), and second to inflate static 'videos' that serve as identity anchors in the preference data repository. The text prompt conditions both the initial generation and the VLM-based semantic reward scoring.

## Method

MagicID replaces self-reconstruction training with a two-stage hybrid preference optimization (HPO) built on top of a LoRA-finetuned HunyuanVideo DiT. A base video repository is assembled from three sources: LoRA-generated videos, original-model videos, and static videos inflated from reference images. Each video is scored on three customized rewards (ArcFace identity similarity, RAFT optical-flow dynamic degree, and Qwen-VL prompt alignment, all on a 1–10 scale). A hybrid pair-selection strategy first constructs identity-preferred pairs (large ID-score gaps, tolerant on dynamics) from static and original-model videos, then constructs dynamic-preferred pairs via Pareto-frontier sampling over the full repository. The final HPO loss adapts the DPO Bradley-Terry objective to video diffusion by expressing the log-likelihood ratio as a difference of noise-prediction MSEs between the policy and reference models, yielding a practical training signal that simultaneously pushes the model toward higher identity fidelity and richer motion.

## Results

On the evaluation set of 40 characters with 40 action-specific prompts (61-frame videos), MagicID achieves a face similarity of 0.600, dynamic degree of 14.42, CLIP-I of 78.83, and FVD of 1228.33, outperforming all baselines on these metrics. It also leads in CLIP-T (26.28) and temporal consistency (0.9933), trailing only ID-Animator by 0.0005 on temporal consistency. The user study with 25 evaluators across 40 video sets confirms MagicID's superiority in ID consistency, dynamic degree, text alignment, and overall quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MagicID substantially outperforms DreamBooth (face sim. 0.600 vs. 0.276, dynamic degree 14.42 vs. 5.690) and MagicMe (0.600 vs. 0.322, 14.42 vs. 5.332), both of which rely on self-reconstruction of reference images and suffer from identity degradation and dynamic reduction. Against the encoder-based methods, MagicID surpasses ID-Animator (face sim. 0.600 vs. 0.433, dynamic degree 14.42 vs. 10.33, FVD 1228 vs. 1558) and ConsisID (0.600 vs. 0.482, 14.42 vs. 9.26, FVD 1228 vs. 1633), while also achieving better CLIP-I (78.83 vs. 49.33 and 63.88). The only metric where MagicID does not lead is temporal consistency, where ID-Animator edges ahead by a negligible margin (0.9938 vs. 0.9933). In the user study, MagicID is preferred over all four baselines across all four evaluation criteria (ID consistency, dynamic degree, text alignment, overall quality).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) from LLM alignment and recent video diffusion alignment work (HuViDPO, VideoDPO) to the identity-specific video customization setting, where prior methods (DreamBooth, MagicMe) rely on self-reconstruction of static reference images. The key departure is replacing self-reconstruction with a hybrid preference-optimization objective that explicitly rewards identity consistency and dynamic quality, using a two-stage sampling strategy (identity-preferred then dynamic-preferred pairs) to handle the absence of naturally co-occurring high-identity and high-dynamics training data. The base model is HunyuanVideo with LoRA fine-tuning, and the reward signals draw on ArcFace embeddings, RAFT optical flow, and Qwen-VL semantic scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first application of preference optimization to personalized video generation, offering a principled alternative to self-reconstruction that directly targets the two failure modes (identity drift over frame count, dynamic collapse over training steps) that plague existing tuning-based methods. The hybrid sampling strategy is a practical recipe for constructing preference pairs when only a few static reference images are available, a constraint inherent to real-world customization. A clear limitation is the single-person scope; extending the reward mechanism to multi-identity scenes remains open, as does validating the approach on longer video durations and non-facial identity attributes.
