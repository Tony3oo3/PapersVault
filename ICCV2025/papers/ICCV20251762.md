## Problem

LLM-based language-grounded autonomous driving suffers from two critical bottlenecks: visual comprehension deficiencies (collisions and obstructions) are the dominant cause of driving failures, and the 7B-parameter LLMs impose prohibitive deployment costs. The paper demonstrates that driving performance does not scale linearly with LLM size, motivating a paradigm shift toward lightweight language models augmented with stronger visual representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251762/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of T frames, each containing multi-view camera images and LiDAR data encoded into 106 visual tokens, alongside natural-language navigation instructions. After CCDP sparsification (retaining 30% of tokens) and MEFA aggregation, the lite language model (1.3B params) processes the compressed visual tokens and instruction embeddings to predict a future trajectory, which PID controllers convert into lateral steering and longitudinal acceleration commands. A memory bank of Z=10 adjacent frames provides temporal context for each current frame.

## Method

VLDrive replaces the 7B LLM with a 1.3B model and compensates through three vision-centric mechanisms. CCDP uses Gumbel-Softmax binary masks to dynamically select critical visual tokens per frame, then enforces a training-only cycle-consistency loss that reconstructs pruned tokens from the retained set, compelling the model to preserve global information in fewer tokens. MEFA injects temporal context by averaging a memory bank of 10 adjacent frames into a temporal embedding added to current visual features, then aggregates via a modified Q-Former that uses the retained tokens themselves as queries rather than learnable ones. DDIA decouples cross-modal (visual-to-instruction) attention from RoPE position encoding while preserving intra-modal positional information, preventing instruction attention dilution as the visual context window grows. The full training objective combines trajectory L1 loss with a pruning-ratio constraint and a reconstruction loss on pruned tokens.

## Results

VLDrive-TinyLLaMA (1.3B parameters) achieves driving scores (DS) of 43.8%, 67.4%, and 81.9% on LangAuto (>500m), LangAuto-Short (150–500m), and LangAuto-Tiny (<150m) respectively, setting new state-of-the-art across all three benchmarks. This represents improvements of 7.6%, 16.8%, and 15.4% over the best LMDrive baseline (LLaVA-v1.5, 7B) while using 81% fewer parameters (1.3B vs. 7B). Route completion (RC) also improves substantially, reaching 54.5%, 78.1%, and 85.5% respectively, with infraction scores (IS) of 0.84, 0.85, and 0.94 indicating strong driving safety.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline family is LMDrive [28], evaluated with seven LLM backbones ranging from 1.3B (TinyLLaMA) to 7B (LLaMA, LLaMA2, Vicuna, Vicuna-v1.5, LLaVA-v1.5) and 3B (Phi-2). VLDrive-TinyLLaMA (1.3B) outperforms the strongest LMDrive variant (LLaVA-v1.5, 7B) by 7.6% DS on LangAuto (43.8% vs. 36.2%), 16.8% on LangAuto-Short (67.4% vs. 50.6%), and 15.4% on LangAuto-Tiny (81.9% vs. 66.5%), while using 81% fewer parameters. Even VLDrive-LLaMA* (also 1.3B) surpasses all 7B LMDrive configurations on every benchmark. Notably, the lightweight LMDrive-TinyLLaMA baseline (1.3B) scores only 25.2% DS on LangAuto, confirming that the gains come from VLDrive's vision-augmented architecture (CCDP, MEFA, DDIA) rather than simply swapping the language model. The infraction score (IS) of VLDrive-TinyLLaMA (0.84–0.94) also exceeds or matches the best LMDrive variants, indicating that the performance gains are not achieved at the cost of driving safety.

## Relation to Prior Work

VLDrive extends the LMDrive framework for language-grounded closed-loop driving by replacing the 7B LLM backbone with a 1.3B lightweight model, compensating through three vision-centric innovations: cycle-consistent dynamic visual pruning (CCDP, building on DynamicViT's token sparsification and adding a reconstruction loss), memory-enhanced feature aggregation (MEFA, a modified Q-Former from BLIP-2 that injects temporal context), and distance-decoupled instruction attention (DDIA, inspired by Vista-LLaMA's equal-distance principle). The key conceptual departure from prior LLM-driven driving work is the argument that visual comprehension, not language-model scale, is the primary bottleneck, shifting the design emphasis from larger LLMs to richer visual representations. The token reduction strategy also draws from the broader vision-language efficiency literature (SPViT, LLaVA-PruMerge) but uniquely couples pruning with a cycle-consistency reconstruction objective to preserve information integrity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 1.3B model with enhanced vision outperforms 7B LLM-based drivers by 15–17% in driving score while using 81% fewer parameters provides a concrete, deployable pathway for language-grounded autonomous driving on resource-constrained hardware. The failure analysis identifying visual misunderstanding (collisions, obstructions) rather than instruction misunderstanding as the dominant failure mode offers a useful diagnostic lens for the field. However, all evaluation is confined to the CARLA simulator on a single dataset, leaving open whether the visual enhancements and DDIA transfer to real-world sensor noise, diverse urban environments, or longer-horizon planning. The moderate absolute driving scores (43.8% DS on LangAuto) also suggest substantial room for improvement before practical deployment.
