## Problem

State-of-the-art learned video codecs that use temporal context mining (e.g., DCVC-FM) must store and simultaneously access 48+ full-resolution feature maps per frame, imposing heavy memory bandwidth demands that become prohibitive for high-resolution, high-frame-rate content. While traditional codecs manage this through explicit decoded frame buffer management with selective reference access, this strategy has not been explored for learned video codecs, leaving a gap between coding efficiency and practical hardware feasibility.

## Input / Output

The system consumes RGB video sequences (converted from YUV420 via BT.601) at arbitrary resolution, processing frames as 3×W×H tensors. It outputs a compressed bitstream and reconstructed frames, with the decoded frame buffer storing multiple reference frames and their accumulated flow maps. The prediction structure uses a 4-frame mini-GOP with a nested quality hierarchy (Q1–Q3), and the long-term key frame selection is signaled in the bitstream. Training uses 256×256 patches from 5-frame subsequences of Vimeo 90k.

## Method

MH-LVC introduces a multi-hypothesis temporal prediction scheme in a conditional residual coding framework that strictly limits temporal prediction to two reference frames at a time: one short-term (most recently decoded) and one long-term key frame selected from a managed buffer. A spatially adaptive gating mechanism (γ at three scales) fuses the warped long-term key frame features with short-term features at the sub-frame level, down-weighting regions where motion estimates are unreliable (e.g., dis-occlusions). The decoded frame buffer employs a 4-frame mini-GOP with a nested quality structure, periodically marking high-quality key frames to mitigate temporal cascading errors, and accumulates flow maps via backward warping to track motion over long prediction distances. The encoder can adaptively select the best long-term key frame online via exhaustive rate-distortion search, a flexibility borrowed from traditional codecs but unexplored in learned codecs. The backbone is a CNN-based conditional residual autoencoder (adapted from DCVC-FM) within the MaskCRT framework, trained with a weighted rate-distortion loss that enforces the quality hierarchy.

## Results

MH-LVC outperforms VTM-17.0 (low-delay B) in PSNR-RGB across UVG, HEVC-B, and MCL-JCV datasets under intra-period 32, and performs comparably to DCVC-FM on most test sets while using a decoded frame buffer of only 14.25 (MH-LVC-1) or 19.25 (MH-LVC-2) full-resolution feature maps, versus 51.75 for DCVC-FM. Decoding time is similar to DCVC-FM (0.54–0.56 s vs. 0.47 s on HEVC-B), while encoding time is slightly higher for MH-LVC-1 (0.73 s vs. 0.60 s) and substantially higher for MH-LVC-2 (1.27 s) due to exhaustive long-term key-frame search. Under an infinite intra-period, MH-LVC-2 significantly outperforms MH-LVC-1 and remains competitive with DCVC-FM on UVG and HEVC-B, despite being trained on only 5-frame GOPs (vs. 32-frame GOPs for DCVC-FM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the traditional codec VTM-17.0 (low-delay B), MH-LVC achieves better PSNR-RGB across all tested datasets (UVG, HEVC-B, MCL-JCV) under intra-period 32, confirming that the learned multi-hypothesis approach surpasses the current VVC reference. Compared to DCVC-FM (the strongest learned baseline), MH-LVC-1 and MH-LVC-2 perform comparably on most datasets but with a dramatically smaller decoded frame buffer (14.25/19.25 vs. 51.75 full-resolution feature maps) and similar decoding time; MH-LVC-2 is slightly worse than DCVC-FM on MCL-JCV under infinite intra-period. Both MH-LVC variants clearly surpass B-CANF (the state-of-the-art B-frame learned codec) despite using only uni-directional prediction, and outperform MaskCRT and DCVC-TCM considerably in BD-rate. In a controlled comparison within the same conditional residual coding framework (Table 5), MH-LVC-1 achieves 7.8%, 3.2%, and 8.9% BD-rate savings over DCVC-TCM on UVG, HEVC-B, and MCL-JCV respectively, while using a buffer 4× smaller (14.25 vs. 55 full-res maps). The main trade-off is encoding complexity: MH-LVC-2's exhaustive key-frame search inflates encoding kMAC/pixel to 2611 (vs. 1137 for DCVC-FM) and encoding time to 1.27 s, an issue the authors flag for future fast-selection algorithms.

## Relation to Prior Work

MH-LVC builds on the masked conditional residual coding framework of MaskCRT [7] and the CNN backbone of DCVC-FM [17], but departs fundamentally from the dominant temporal context mining paradigm (DCVC-TCM, DCVC-HEM, DCVC-FM) that learns and propagates large latent feature buffers. Instead, it revives the explicit decoded-frame-buffer management strategy long standard in traditional codecs (VTM, HEVC), limiting temporal prediction to two hypotheses—one short-term and one long-term key frame—fused via a spatially adaptive gating mechanism. This contrasts with earlier multi-reference learned codecs like M-LVC [18], which fuse all buffered frames simultaneously without reliability weighting, and with the single-hypothesis P-frame codecs (DCVC, MaskCRT) that reference only the immediately preceding frame.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a much smaller explicit buffer (14–19 full-resolution feature maps vs. 52 for DCVC-FM) can achieve comparable BD-rate performance, directly addressing the memory-bandwidth bottleneck that hinders ASIC deployment of learned codecs at 4K/60fps. It also opens the door to content-adaptive prediction structures during inference—a flexibility common in traditional codecs but previously unexplored for learned ones. Key limitations include a substantial encoding complexity penalty for MH-LVC-2 (exhaustive key-frame search, 2611 vs. 1137 kMAC/pixel) and training on only 5-frame GOPs versus 32 for DCVC-FM, which likely caps performance at infinite intra-period. A fast frame-selection algorithm and longer training sequences are identified as necessary next steps before the approach can be considered a practical drop-in replacement for implicit buffering.
