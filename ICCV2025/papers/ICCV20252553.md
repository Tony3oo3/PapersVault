## Problem

State-of-the-art VLN agents spend ~99.5% of their computation in visual encoders that process all 36 panoramic views at every navigation step, creating a bottleneck for deployment on resource-constrained robots. Existing input-adaptive inference methods (e.g., MuE early-exit) fail in VLN because they treat each input independently, ignoring the spatio-temporal dependencies inherent to sequential navigation, resulting in inaccurate decisions that actually increase total computation. No prior work provides a tunable efficiency-accuracy trade-off for VLN agents.

## Input / Output

At each navigation step, the method receives a 36-view panorama (224×224 for standard VLN, 480×640 for continuous), a natural language instruction encoded once via BERT, and the agent's navigation history. Only a subset of views (navigable views plus their k-extensions) are forwarded through the visual encoder; all other views are replaced with zero-vectors, so the output is a set of 36 visual embeddings where most entries are zeros. For continuous VLN, a scan-only U-Net SGM predicts navigable views from 2D laser occupancy scans before any image encoding occurs, decoupling view selection from visual processing.

## Method

The method combines three input-adaptive mechanisms at different levels. (1) Spatial locality via k-extension: only navigable views and their k nearest neighbors (k=4–6) are processed by the ViT, reducing per-step computation by ~2×. (2) Importance-based adaptive thresholding: within the k-extended views, MuE early-exit thresholds are set via an exponential decay T_{i,j} = T_0 · exp(−A·R_{i,j}) where R is the rank (distance from the nearest navigable view), so views farther from navigable directions exit earlier; budgeted-batch inference allows each view in the 36-view batch to stop at a different layer. (3) Temporal locality via SimHash LSH: views are mapped to 10-bit binary keys via random hyperplane projections, and if a view's key matches a cached entry with cosine similarity ≥ 0.85 (standard) or 0.95 (continuous), its embedding is reused without re-encoding. For continuous VLN, a scan-only SGM (U-Net trained with Sinkhorn divergence on laser scans) predicts navigable views prior to image encoding, eliminating the need for k-extensions entirely.

## Results

On REVERIE (standard VLN), the full method (k-extension + LSH + adaptive thresholds) reduces GFLOPs by 62% for HAMT (5434.71→2073.69) and 49% for DUET (6185.15→3145.92), with SR drops of 7.2% and 7.7% respectively. Across all seven benchmarks, the average computation reduction is 56% with an 11.7% average SR drop. In the continuous setting (R2R-CE), the scan-only SGM achieves an 87% GFLOPs reduction (18074→2396.54) with only an 8% SR decrease, and adding LSH pushes savings to ~90%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is MuE (Tang et al. [51]), the only prior input-adaptive method compatible with encoder models. MuE fails catastrophically in VLN: on REVERIE it drops HAMT's SR from 31.81% to 13.83% (−56.5%) and DUET's from 47.09% to 27.35% (−41.9%), while total trajectory GFLOPs actually *increase* (4763→4410 for HAMT on R2R) because the agent takes more erroneous steps. The proposed method, by contrast, achieves 49–62% GFLOPs savings on REVERIE with only 7–22% SR loss, and 56% average savings with 11.7% average SR drop across all six standard benchmarks. Against the HAMT base agent (Chen et al. [10]), the full method preserves 77–83% of SR while halving computation; against DUET (Chen et al. [11]), it preserves 83–84% of SR with ~49% savings. In continuous VLN, the scan-only SGM (adapted from Krantz et al. [35]) reduces VLN-CE BERT's GFLOPs by 87% (18074→2396) at an 8% SR cost, far outperforming what MuE could achieve (which is inapplicable to ResNet-152). The LSH component adds 2–4% further savings with negligible or zero SR loss in the standard setting, and the adaptive thresholding contributes an additional 7.5–12.8% GFLOPs reduction at ~1–6% SR cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends input-adaptive inference (early-exit architectures, MuE, AdNNs) from classification and encoder tasks to the sequential, spatio-temporal setting of vision-and-language navigation. It departs from the one-size-fits-all philosophy of prior adaptive methods by exploiting two localities unique to VLN: spatial locality (navigable views and their neighbors dominate decision-making) and temporal locality (consecutive steps share overlapping views). The base agents (HAMT, DUET, VLN-CE BERT) are used as-is; the contribution is a plug-and-play inference-time strategy rather than a new architecture, and the scan-only subgoal generation module adapts the U-Net SGM from Krantz et al. to enable the method in continuous environments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2×+ reduction in visual-encoder computation directly addresses the deployment bottleneck for VLN agents on resource-constrained robots, and the method is tunable (k, aggressiveness A, LSH threshold) so practitioners can trade accuracy for speed per application. However, the 11.7% average SR loss in standard VLN and the 7–10% additional degradation under visual corruptions (where the agent overshoots targets rather than failing to navigate) indicate that efficiency gains come at a real robustness cost. Open questions include whether denoising or corruption-robust pretraining can close the robustness gap, whether the k-extension strategy transfers to unseen environments without recalibration, and how the method interacts with model compression (quantization, pruning) that the authors note is orthogonal.
