## Problem

In data-free black-box adversarial attacks, existing generator-based methods rely on disagreement loss to guide sample synthesis, which cannot fully explore the input space for producing high-value hard samples. Additionally, substitute model training typically uses only a discrimination loss to mimic the victim model, neglecting the generalization of the substitute ensemble and thereby limiting the transferability of generated adversarial examples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252421/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only random Gaussian noise (Z ~ N(0,1)) fed into a generator and hard-label (one-hot) predictions from the black-box victim model—no soft probabilities, model architecture, or real training data are required. The generator produces synthetic RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100/SVHN, 64×64 for Tiny ImageNet) that are iteratively perturbed into hard samples before being used to train a VGG-13 + Inception-v3 ensemble. The final output is a set of adversarial examples crafted via FGSM, PGD, or BIM on the trained ensemble, evaluated by attack success rate against the victim model under a fixed query budget.

## Method

KOEnsAttack introduces two key modules over a standard generator + ensemble-substitute pipeline. First, the Sample Hardness Enhancement (SHE) replaces the disagreement loss on the generator with a direct iterative transformation: starting from generator outputs, it repeatedly adds the sign of the gradient of a cosine-similarity discrepancy loss between the two substitute networks (T steps with step size α), pushing each sample into the controversial region where the dual networks disagree. Second, the Knowledge Orthogonalization Module (KOM) augments the standard cross-entropy mimicry loss with an orthogonal loss on the non-target class logit vectors of the two networks, forcing them to learn complementary rather than redundant knowledge from the same black-box feedback. The ensemble output is an average soft-vote, and an experience replay buffer stores all SHE-transformed samples to mitigate catastrophic forgetting during substitute training. Adversarial examples are then generated by applying white-box attacks with aggregated gradients from both networks.

## Results

KOEnsAttack achieves 97.85% untargeted ASR on CIFAR-100 (ResNet-34) and 78.23% on Tiny ImageNet (ResNet-50) using only 4M black-box queries, surpassing the SOTA DisGUIDE (95.98% and 39.35% respectively) while reducing the query budget from 10M to 4M. In targeted attacks, the method reaches 97.82% ASR on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-34) and 15.22% on Tiny ImageNet, outperforming DisGUIDE by 2.5% and 14.2% respectively. Notably, KOEnsAttack also outperforms data-driven baselines (DFMS, STDatav2) that rely on external proxy data, achieving 99.89% untargeted ASR on CIFAR-10 (VGG-19) without any real data prior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KOEnsAttack outperforms all six baselines across nearly every dataset-model combination in both untargeted and targeted settings. Against the closest data-free competitor DisGUIDE, the gains are most dramatic on Tiny ImageNet (78.23% vs. 39.35% untargeted, a 38.9-point margin) and CIFAR-100/ViT (85.43% vs. 74.55%), while on CIFAR-10/ResNet-34 the margin narrows to 0.18 points (99.78% vs. 99.60%). In targeted attacks, KOEnsAttack leads DisGUIDE by 16.9 points on CIFAR-10/VGG-19 (95.44% vs. 69.46%) and by 14.2 points on Tiny ImageNet (15.22% vs. 1.03%). The method also surpasses the two data-driven baselines (DFMS and STDatav2) that use external proxy data, e.g., 99.89% vs. 99.66% on CIFAR-10/VGG-19 untargeted and 97.82% vs. 91.48% on CIFAR-10/ResNet-34 targeted, all without any real data. The only near-tie is CIFAR-10/ResNet-34 untargeted where DisGUIDE achieves 99.60% vs. 99.78%, a negligible 0.18-point gap. Overall, KOEnsAttack achieves these gains while using fewer queries (4M vs. 10M on CIFAR-100 and Tiny ImageNet), demonstrating both higher accuracy and greater query efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DisGUIDE framework [23], which introduced dual-network ensemble substitute models trained with a disagreement loss on the generator, by replacing that loss-based sample selection with a direct gradient-based Sample Hardness Enhancement (SHE) that iteratively pushes synthetic samples into the controversial regions between the two networks. It further departs from prior data-free attack pipelines (DASt [40], DFME [30], IDEAL [38]) by introducing a Knowledge Orthogonalization Module (KOM) that constrains the non-target class logits of the dual networks to be orthogonal, encouraging complementary knowledge acquisition from the victim model. The experience replay mechanism is borrowed from MAZE [12] to mitigate catastrophic forgetting during substitute training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The query-efficiency gain (4M vs. 10M queries on CIFAR-100) is practically significant for pay-per-query black-box settings, and the dramatic improvement on Tiny ImageNet targeted attacks (15.22% vs. 1.025% for DisGUIDE) suggests the method is particularly effective when the task is harder. However, the approach remains limited to image classification with hard-label feedback, and the orthogonalization is applied only at the logit level, leaving open whether deeper feature-level complementarity would yield further gains. Whether the ensemble-orthogonalization principle generalizes to larger ensembles, other modalities, or soft-label settings remains unexplored.
