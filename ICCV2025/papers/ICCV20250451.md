## Problem

Existing automated cel-animation colorization methods rely on a single reference keyframe, which fails when segments appear or disappear between keyframes and degrades sharply under large motion or deformation. No prior approach effectively combines temporal information from multiple keyframes with structural alignment to achieve robust segment-level colorization in industrial inbetweening workflows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250451/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a binary line-drawing target frame (an inbetweening result) and two or more colored keyframe images (the first and last keyframes of the sequence, each with flat segment-level colors from a production color chart) as input. It outputs a per-segment color label assignment from a global color set of M unique colors, producing a flat-colored line drawing where every enclosed region receives exactly one consistent RGB value. The pipeline supports variable sequence lengths (tested at S=3, 5, 10) and can extend to more than two reference keyframes at inference time.

## Method

The framework introduces two key modules on top of a multiplex-transformer matching backbone. First, a Gated Score Fusion (GSF) module encodes relative temporal distance between keyframe and target via sign-and-magnitude MLP embeddings, computes per-keyframe similarity matrices through the multiplex transformer, and fuses them with segment-wise data-dependent gates (derived from intermediate affinity scores) to handle segments that appear in one keyframe but not the other. Second, a Multi-Scale Feature Matching (MS-MA) module, inspired by video object segmentation memory matching, uses two U-Net-like encoders to extract features at three resolutions (d=4, 8, 16), performs cross-attention between target line-art features (queries) and keyframe line-art features (keys) with colored keyframe features as values, and adds the resulting multi-scale matched features to the lineart-and-color feature extraction network to correct optical-flow warping failures under large deformation. Training is supervised with cross-entropy loss on a subset-matching formulation where target segments are matched to K unique segment subsets (merged by identical label index) across all keyframes, rather than to individual segments in a single frame.

## Results

On the most challenging setting (sequence length S=10), the proposed method achieves 71.7% segment accuracy (Acc) and 76.5% thresholded accuracy (Acc-Thres) on the Industrial Animation Dataset, outperforming the previous SOTA BasicPBC (62.8% / 68.1%) by ~9 percentage points. On the Paint Bucket Colorization Dataset at S=10, the method reaches 68.7% Acc vs. BasicPBC's 53.3%, a gap of ~15 points. At the easier S=3 setting, the method still leads with 89.1% Acc (Paint Bucket) and 82.0% Acc (Industrial), demonstrating consistent superiority across all sequence lengths and both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against six baselines: three pixel-level methods (Tooncrafter, Manganinja, LVCD), one optical-flow method (RAFT), one segment-matching method (Animation Transformer/Ant), and the prior SOTA segment-based method (BasicPBC). The proposed method wins on all segment-level metrics (Acc, Acc-Thres) across every sequence length and both datasets. Against BasicPBC, the gap widens with sequence length: from ~6 Acc points at S=3 (89.1% vs 82.6% on Paint Bucket) to ~15 points at S=10 (68.7% vs 53.3%), confirming the multi-reference GSF module's value under large deformation. Against RAFT, the advantage is even more pronounced at S=10 (71.7% vs 52.2% Acc on Industrial), as optical flow degrades sharply with motion. Pixel-level methods (Tooncrafter, Manganinja, LVCD) trail by 40–60 Acc points, confirming their incompatibility with the flat-color requirement of cel animation. Ant is only evaluated on the Paint Bucket dataset (S=3) where it scores 66.4% Acc, still well below the proposed method's 89.1%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the paint bucket colorization pipeline established by BasicPBC [6] and the multiplex transformer matching architecture from Animation Transformer [3], moving from a single-reference-frame paradigm to a multi-keyframe temporal-structural framework. The key conceptual departure is the 'subset matching' formulation, where target segments are aligned to merged segment subsets across multiple keyframes rather than individual segments in one frame, enabling the Gated Score Fusion module to weight contributions from different temporal references. The Multi-Scale Feature Matching module draws directly from video object segmentation literature (memory matching, multi-level feature alignment), repurposing cross-attention-based feature warping to correct optical-flow failures under large deformation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a concrete industrial bottleneck in cel-animation production where manual colorization of 200+ segments per frame is extremely time-consuming, and the multi-reference formulation better mirrors how animators actually reference multiple keyframes. However, the Industrial Animation Dataset is small (200 images, 15 sequences), limiting confidence in generalization, and the method is evaluated with only 2 keyframes despite claiming support for more. Open questions include scalability to longer sequences with many keyframes, robustness to style transfer across different animation studios, and whether the gated fusion mechanism generalizes beyond the binary appear/disappear scenarios it was designed for.
