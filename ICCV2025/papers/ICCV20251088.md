## Problem

Monocular 3D face reconstruction is highly ill-posed, and existing methods either lack full 360-degree view coverage or suffer from view inconsistency due to insufficient multi-view supervision. Methods that train on synthetic multi-view data to overcome this gap (e.g., RodinHD) exhibit significant identity loss and poor generalization to real-world images, leaving a gap between synthetic training and real inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251088/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single near-frontal RGB facial image as input and produces a complete 3D Gaussian splats head representation with pixel-aligned Gaussians (one Gaussian per pixel per view). Internally, the pipeline generates six sparse views at 512×512 resolution covering azimuths {α, α±45°, α±90°, α+180°} at zero elevation. For real-world inference, MTCNN estimates face size and center, and the image is cropped/resized to match training statistics with a fixed 50° FOV to compensate for unknown intrinsics.

## Method

FaceLift is a two-stage feed-forward pipeline. Stage 1 is a multi-view latent diffusion model that generates six consistent views from a single input, using a multi-view attention mechanism that reshapes the feature tensor from B×V×H×W×C to B×VHW×C so self-attention operates across all views simultaneously, and an input-view reconstruction objective that forces the first generated view to match the input camera, which the authors show is critical for preventing overfitting to synthetic identities. Stage 2 is a transformer-based feed-forward reconstructor (following GS-LRM) that patchifies the six views concatenated with Plücker ray coordinates, processes them through transformer blocks, and decodes pixel-aligned 3D Gaussians via a linear layer. The reconstructor is trained in two stages: pretraining on Objaverse general objects to acquire broad geometric priors, then fine-tuning on a synthetic head dataset (200 identities × 50 appearances rendered in Blender/Cycles with PBR textures) to capture head-specific structure. This combination of multi-view consistent supervision, input-view reconstruction, and two-stage reconstructor training enables high-fidelity 3D head reconstruction from real images despite training exclusively on synthetic data.

## Results

On the Cafca synthetic benchmark, FaceLift achieves PSNR 16.61, SSIM 0.7968, LPIPS 0.2694, DreamSim 0.1096, and ArcFace distance 0.1573, outperforming the strongest baseline LGM (PSNR 16.52, DreamSim 0.1552, ArcFace 0.2557) on every metric. On the real-captured Ava-256 dataset, FaceLift reaches PSNR 16.52, SSIM 0.8271, LPIPS 0.2277, DreamSim 0.1065, and ArcFace 0.1871, again leading all baselines. The most notable margin is in identity preservation (ArcFace), where FaceLift reduces the distance by ~38% over LGM on Cafca (0.1573 vs. 0.2557) and ~17% on Ava-256 (0.1871 vs. 0.2264).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FaceLift outperforms all five baselines on every metric in both Cafca and Ava-256. Against the closest competitor LGM [56], FaceLift gains +0.09 PSNR, −0.036 LPIPS, −0.046 DreamSim, and −0.098 ArcFace on Cafca, with the largest relative advantage in identity preservation (~38% lower ArcFace distance). GAN-inversion methods (GGHead, PanoHead, Dual Encoder) trail by 5–6 dB in PSNR and suffer from limited view coverage or identity drift; PanoHead in particular fails on challenging hairstyles and back-of-head rendering. General-object methods Era3D [31] and LGM [56] produce plausible geometry but introduce artifacts (Era3D on the back of the head, LGM on nose/jaw shape), confirming the value of head-specific synthetic training data. The ablation variant 'Our MV+LGM' shows that plugging FaceLift's multi-view diffusion outputs into LGM improves LGM's ArcFace from 0.2557 to 0.1767, yet the dedicated transformer reconstructor still yields superior texture fidelity (DreamSim 0.1096 vs. 0.1282).

## Relation to Prior Work

FaceLift builds directly on the large reconstruction model (LRM) lineage, specifically adopting the GS-LRM transformer architecture for its feed-forward 3D Gaussian reconstructor, and on multi-view diffusion approaches (Era3D, Zero123++) for sparse view generation. It departs from prior 3D face reconstruction methods (GGHead, PanoHead, Dual Encoder) that rely on GAN inversion over parametric or triplane representations, and from RodinHD which also trains on synthetic data but outputs triplane NeRFs rather than explicit Gaussians. The key conceptual departure is the two-stage pipeline—diffusion-based view generation followed by a pre-trained-then-fine-tuned transformer reconstructor—combined with the synthetic data pipeline inspired by 'Fake it till you make it' [65], positioning the work at the intersection of generative view synthesis and feed-forward 3D reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a model trained exclusively on Blender-rendered synthetic heads generalizes to real-world portraits with no perceptible domain gap is practically significant, as it removes the need for expensive multi-view studio captures during training. The two benchmarks (Cafca and Ava-256) provide the community with standardized evaluation for full-head 3D reconstruction, a gap in prior literature. Limitations include the ~8-second inference time, restriction to head-only reconstruction (no body or clothing dynamics), and reliance on a fixed 50° FOV assumption for real images. Open questions include whether the synthetic-to-real transfer extends to full-body avatars, whether the multi-view diffusion stage can be made faster, and whether the approach handles extreme poses or occlusions beyond the training distribution.
