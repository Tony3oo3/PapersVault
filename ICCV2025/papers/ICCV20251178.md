## Problem

Existing co-speech motion generation methods rely predominantly on rhythm-related audio features, which overshadow the sparse yet critical semantic gestures that convey meaning and emphasis. Because semantically relevant motions occur in only a small fraction of frames, rhythm-dominant models dilute or overlook these key gestures, and methods that do incorporate semantic cues (e.g., LivelySpeaker) provide only global control rather than frame-level emphasis. The paper addresses how to simultaneously maintain a coherent rhythm-aligned base motion while selectively amplifying expressive gestures at semantically significant frames.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-modal inputs: rhythmic audio features (beat onsets and HuBERT embeddings), frame-level text embeddings, sentence-level CLIP features, emotion2vec emotion features, a speaker identity embedding, and a 4-frame seed pose. The body is segmented into four regions (face, hands, upper body, lower body), each encoded by a dedicated 6-layer RVQ-VAE (codebook size 256, 4× temporal downsampling). The output is a sequence of discrete motion codes per body part, decoded back to 3D mesh vertices at 30 fps; additionally, a per-frame scalar semantic score ψ is produced to gate which frames receive semantic emphasis.

## Method

SemTalk decomposes co-speech motion into a rhythm-related base motion and a semantic-aware sparse motion, then adaptively fuses them via a learned semantic score. The base motion is generated by a hierarchical Coarse2Fine Cross-Attention module that cascades rhythmic conditioning from face → hands → upper body → lower body, combined with a local-global rhythmic consistency loss (InfoNCE applied at both frame and sequence levels on latent face and hand features). The sparse motion is produced by a Semantic Emphasis Learning stage where a sem-gate computes a frame-level semantic score ψ from multi-modal semantic features, applying feature weighting (W_f) to filter semantic inputs and loss weighting (W_l) to supervise key-frame classification. Fusion is threshold-based: at frames where ψ exceeds β, the base motion code is replaced by the sparse semantic code, while the RVQ-VAE decoder's convolutional structure ensures smooth transitions between replaced and non-replaced frames.

## Results

On BEAT2, SemTalk achieves the best FGD (4.278 vs. EMAGE's 5.512), BC (7.770), MSE (6.153 vs. EMAGE's 7.680), and LVD (6.938 vs. EMAGE's 7.556) among all baselines. On SHOW, SemTalk similarly leads with FGD 20.18 (vs. EMAGE 22.12), BC 8.304, MSE 134.1 (vs. EMAGE 136.1), and LVD 39.15 (vs. EMAGE 42.44). The only metric where SemTalk trails is DIV (diversity): TalkSHOW reaches 13.47 on BEAT2 and EMAGE reaches 12.46 on SHOW, versus SemTalk's 12.91 and 11.36 respectively. The semantic gate achieves 82.76% classification accuracy on BEAT2 semantic labels, substantially above the 50.07% of random scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SemTalk outperforms all nine baselines on FGD, BC, MSE, and LVD across both BEAT2 and SHOW. The closest competitor is EMAGE [31], which leads on DIV (13.06 on BEAT2, 12.46 on SHOW) but trails SemTalk by 1.234 in FGD on BEAT2 (5.512 vs. 4.278) and by 1.94 in FGD on SHOW (22.12 vs. 20.18). TalkSHOW [48] achieves the highest DIV on BEAT2 (13.47) but has substantially worse FGD (6.209) and LVD (7.771) than SemTalk. DiffSHEG [9] and LivelySpeaker [53] show the weakest overall performance, with FGD values of 8.986 and 11.80 on BEAT2 respectively, and 24.87 and 32.17 on SHOW. The Habibie et al. [17] baseline, which does not model full-body motion, is consistently outperformed on all available metrics. Notably, SemTalk's advantage is most pronounced on MSE and LVD (facial metrics), where it reduces error by roughly 20% over EMAGE on BEAT2, indicating that the rhythmic consistency learning and coarse2fine cross-attention specifically improve facial animation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

SemTalk adapts the motion decomposition paradigm from TM2D (which splits dance motion into music-related components) to the co-speech setting, separating rhythm-related base motion from semantic-aware sparse motion. Its architectural backbone draws on RVQ-VAE (from AudioLM/SoundStream), body-part segmentation from EMAGE, cascading face-to-body guidance from DiffSHEG, and InfoNCE-based consistency losses from CoG. The key departure from prior work like LivelySpeaker and DisCo is the explicit frame-level semantic gate that selectively activates sparse gestures within a unified latent space, rather than fusing separate rhythm and semantic generators in distinct representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: most co-speech gesture methods prioritize rhythmic alignment and dilute the rare but expressive semantic gestures that make interactions feel natural. The frame-level semantic emphasis mechanism offers a practical recipe for balancing these two competing objectives, and the ablations (especially the sem-gate accuracy jumping from 50% random to 83% with learned weighting) provide clear evidence the mechanism works. Open questions include whether the empirical threshold β generalizes across domains, how the approach performs on longer sequences or real-time streaming settings, and whether the reliance on frame-level semantic labels (available only in BEAT2) limits broader applicability.
