## Problem

Existing roadside collaborative perception methods focus on model architecture design but overlook critical data issues: calibration drift in fixed-view cameras, sparse vehicle density in collected frames, and multi-view annotation inconsistency. No simulation framework existed that could generate large-scale, multi-view consistent training data from the fixed, sparse viewpoints inherent to roadside deployments, leaving a gap between model capability and achievable performance on real datasets.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a single real roadside image per camera, a 3D point cloud for depth calibration, a library of 3D vehicle assets, and (initially inaccurate) camera extrinsic parameters. It outputs photorealistic simulated roadside images with corresponding 3D bounding box annotations, where edits made in 3D space automatically propagate to all camera viewpoints, guaranteeing multi-view geometric consistency. A post-processing stage can additionally apply style transfer (e.g., rain, night) to the full scene while preserving foreground-background relationships.

## Method

RoCo-Sim renders 3D vehicle assets onto real 2D backgrounds via a 3D-to-2D projection pipeline rather than reconstructing the full scene, making it feasible under fixed sparse viewpoints where NeRF/3DGS methods fail. Four components work together: (1) a Camera Extrinsic Optimizer uses BFGS to minimize projection error between 3D box corners and user-adjusted 2D keypoints, correcting calibration drift; (2) a Multi-View Occlusion-Aware Sampler (MOAS) scores 3D grid cells by multi-view visibility uniformity and inter-point distance, then applies a rule-based checker to reject placements that cause 3D overlap or are invisible from all cameras; (3) DepthSAM segments foreground objects, predicts relative depth with DepthAnything, and calibrates to absolute depth via least-squares fitting against projected point-cloud depth, ensuring correct front-to-back occlusion during rendering; (4) a Scalable Post-Processing Toolkit applies style transfer and scene enhancements. The key insight is that performing edits in 3D space and projecting through calibrated cameras automatically yields multi-view consistent results without requiring 3D scene reconstruction or 3D layout inputs.

## Results

RoCo-Sim achieves an 83.12% relative improvement in AP70 (3D@0.7) on TUMTraf-V2X (BEVHeight: 13.19 → 24.16) and substantial gains on RCooper-Intersection (e.g., 3D@0.7 on scenario 136: 7.25 → 16.70, a 130% relative gain). On RCooper-117, BEVHeight with RoCo-Sim reaches 42.02 AP70 versus 31.87 without, while BEVSpread improves from 19.85 to 23.06 AP70 on TUM. The paper highlights that BEVHeight trained with RoCo-Sim simulation data outperforms the newer BEVSpread trained only on original data, demonstrating that simulation data surpasses algorithmic improvements in impact.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two perception backbones—BEVHeight and BEVSpread—each evaluated with and without RoCo-Sim simulation data. BEVHeight with RoCo-Sim outperforms BEVSpread without RoCo-Sim on RCooper-117 (42.02 vs. 34.53 AP70) and on TUMTraf-V2X (24.16 vs. 19.85 AP70), demonstrating that simulation data yields larger gains than the architectural improvements in BEVSpread. On RCooper-136, BEVHeight+RoCo-Sim (16.70 AP70) also surpasses BEVSpread without simulation (13.23 AP70). Both backbones benefit from RoCo-Sim, but BEVHeight shows larger relative improvements (e.g., +31.9% AP70 on RCooper-117, +83.1% on TUM) compared to BEVSpread's more modest gains (+17.7% on RCooper-117, +16.2% on TUM), suggesting BEVHeight's simpler architecture is more amenable to data-driven enhancement.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoCo-Sim departs from two dominant lines of driving simulation: NeRF/3DGS-based scene reconstruction (S-NeRF, OmniRe, ChatSim), which requires multi-view camera coverage incompatible with fixed sparse roadside viewpoints, and 2D generative methods (BEVGen, DriveDreamer, MagicDrive), which cannot guarantee multi-view geometric consistency or produce aligned 3D bounding box annotations. Instead, it repurposes the classical 3D-to-2D rendering pipeline—leveraging a 3D asset library and Blender for compositing—onto real single-view roadside images, using DepthAnything for relative depth that is then calibrated to absolute depth via point cloud projection. The camera extrinsic optimizer (BFGS-based) and the Multi-View Occlusion-Aware Sampler are novel components with no direct prior in the roadside perception literature, positioning RoCo-Sim as the first simulation framework purpose-built for this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that data-quality issues (calibration drift, sparse traffic, multi-view annotation inconsistency) are the primary bottleneck in roadside collaborative perception, not model architecture—simulation data yields larger gains than the algorithmic leap from BEVHeight to BEVSpread. This reframing is practically important: it suggests that investing in simulation infrastructure can unlock performance that better architectures alone cannot. Key limitations remain: the 3D asset library is manually constructed and limited in variety, no trajectory generator models real traffic flow, and detection performance still drops significantly on unseen intersections, leaving generalization as an open challenge for the field.
