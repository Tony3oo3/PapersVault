## Problem

Consistency distillation applied to video diffusion models suffers from a conflicting learning dynamic: optimization gradients and loss contributions differ substantially between high-noise (semantic layout/motion) and low-noise (fine detail) timesteps, causing a single student model to underfit both aspects simultaneously. This leads to distorted layouts, unnatural motion, and degraded appearance details in few-step video generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces a generated video, operating on 129-frame sequences at 1280×720 (HunyuanVideo, 13B params) or 29-frame sequences at 720×480 (CogVideoX, 2B params). Inference requires only 4 or 8 sampling steps (versus 50 for the teacher), with the model dynamically switching between a semantic expert and a detail expert based on the current noise level along the ODE trajectory.

## Method

DCM splits the 50-step ODE trajectory at timestep κ=37 into a high-noise semantic phase and a low-noise detail phase, training two specialized denoisers on each sub-trajectory. For parameter efficiency, the detail expert is initialized from the trained semantic expert with all base weights frozen, adding only new timestep-dependent embedding layers and LoRA adapters on attention linear layers (identified via weight-difference analysis as the primary divergence points). Expert-specific losses are introduced: a Temporal Coherence Loss penalizes frame-to-frame motion inconsistency in the semantic expert's output, while a GAN loss combined with a Feature Matching term (using the frozen teacher as feature extractor) stabilizes adversarial training for the detail expert. This decoupled, parameter-efficient design achieves VBench scores comparable to the 50-step teacher with only 4 inference steps.

## Results

On HunyuanVideo (129 frames, 1280×720), DCM at 4 steps achieves a VBench total score of 83.83, nearly matching the 50-step original model (83.87) while LCM (80.33) and PCM (80.93) degrade by 3.5 and 3.0 points respectively. At 8 steps, DCM reaches 83.86, again essentially matching the 50-step baseline, whereas LCM and PCM score 81.49 and 81.63. On CogVideoX (29 frames, 720×480), DCM at 4 steps scores 79.99 VBench total versus 78.88 (LCM) and 79.09 (PCM), with the 50-step reference at 80.59. Inference latency is comparable across all few-step methods (~121 s for HunyuanVideo at 4 steps, ~3.3 s for CogVideoX at 4 steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCM consistently outperforms both LCM and PCM across all settings. On HunyuanVideo at 4 steps, DCM's VBench total (83.83) exceeds LCM (80.33) by 3.5 points and PCM (80.93) by 2.9 points, and nearly matches the 50-step original model (83.87). The gap is even more pronounced on the Quality sub-metric, where DCM scores 85.12 versus 80.83 (LCM) and 81.94 (PCM). On CogVideoX at 4 steps, DCM leads LCM by 1.11 points and PCM by 0.90 points on VBench total, closing most of the gap to the 50-step reference (80.59). A user study further confirms the advantage: raters prefer DCM videos over LCM 82.67% of the time on HunyuanVideo and 75.33% on CogVideoX, and over PCM 77.33% and 72.67% respectively. Inference latency is essentially identical across all few-step methods, so DCM's quality gains come at no additional speed cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the consistency distillation framework (Song et al., 2023; LCM; PCM) to video generation by identifying a previously underexplored optimization conflict: the gradients and loss contributions differ substantially between high-noise (semantic layout/motion) and low-noise (detail refinement) timesteps, causing a single student model to underfit both regimes. While PCM already partitions the ODE trajectory into sub-trajectories, DCM goes further by training architecturally distinct expert denoisers with expert-specific losses (Temporal Coherence for semantics, GAN + Feature Matching for details) and then collapsing them into a single parameter-efficient model via LoRA on a frozen semantic backbone. The approach also draws on distribution-matching distillation techniques (GAN loss, feature matching) typically used in image-domain methods like DMD, repurposing them for the detail-refinement phase of video synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Video diffusion distillation remains significantly underexplored compared to image distillation, and this paper provides the first systematic diagnosis of why naive consistency distillation degrades in video (temporal inconsistency, detail loss) along with a practical, parameter-efficient remedy. The finding that a LoRA adapter on a frozen semantic expert can approximate a fully separate detail expert at minimal cost is directly actionable for practitioners working with large video DiT models. However, the method still struggles below 4 steps (e.g., 2-step generation), and the reliance on a frozen teacher for GAN feature extraction limits applicability when the teacher is unavailable or mismatched. Open questions include whether the dual-expert decomposition generalizes to longer videos, higher resolutions, or autoregressive generation settings.
