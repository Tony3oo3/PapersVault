## Problem

While MLLMs have demonstrated strong single-image visual grounding, their ability to localize instances across multiple images using open-ended text prompts remains largely unexplored. Existing multi-image MLLM benchmarks focus on image-level tasks such as VQA and perception, leaving a critical gap in evaluating cross-image contextual reasoning at the instance level with flexible, real-world textual expressions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250190/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample consists of a pair of images (horizontally concatenated for models without native sequence support) and an open-ended text prompt of 2–24 words (average 7.2) in one of three styles: referring, comparison, or reasoning. The model must output bounding boxes for all referenced instances with group assignments (1–17 instances across 1–7 groups per positive sample) and must reject negative samples where no instance matches the prompt. The 2,000 samples span 20 practical skills and diverse domains including natural images, charts, document photos, artworks, and scientific diagrams.

## Method

MC-Bench is built through a multi-stage annotation pipeline: 3,345 images from 10+ sources are grouped by theme, annotators write open-ended prompts linking image pairs, and a separate team draws bounding boxes with a cyclic review process for quality control. Evaluation employs AP50 with Hungarian algorithm for group-to-group matching at the instance level, alongside image-level accuracy for identifying which images contain targets. The paper benchmarks over 20 models and introduces two baselines: an agentic approach that uses GPT-4o for cross-image reasoning and discriminative phrase generation followed by G-DINO for localization, and a fine-tuned Qwen2-VL-7B trained with LoRA on 50K synthesized multi-context instruction tuning samples. Key findings reveal that specialist grounding models underperform generalist MLLMs in multi-context settings, the agentic baseline outperforms all end-to-end MLLMs by a clear margin, and a significant gap persists between the best models and human performance (14.4% in Acc, 5.1% in AP50).

## Results

On MC-Bench, the agentic baseline (GPT-4o + G-DINO) achieves the best overall performance among all evaluated approaches with 77.9% image-level accuracy (Acc) and 36.2% instance-level AP50, surpassing all end-to-end MLLMs by a clear margin. Among end-to-end MLLMs, Qwen2-VL-72B with image sequence inputs leads with 71.4% Acc and 30.7% AP50, while Gemini-1.5 Pro achieves 62.5% Acc and 28.2% AP50. GPT-4o shows strong image-level comprehension (78.3% Acc) but extremely weak fine-grained localization (2.8% AP50). Human evaluators establish an upper bound of 92.3% Acc and 41.3% AP50, revealing a significant 14.4% Acc and 5.1% AP50 gap over the best model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The agentic baseline (GPT-4o + G-DINO) outperforms all 20+ end-to-end MLLMs and foundation models by a substantial margin, achieving 36.2% AP50 versus the best end-to-end model Qwen2-VL-72B (30.7%) and Gemini-1.5 Pro (28.2%), and 77.9% Acc versus 71.4% for Qwen2-VL-72B. Among proprietary MLLMs, GPT-4o achieves the highest image-level accuracy (78.3%) but fails catastrophically at instance-level localization (2.8% AP50), while Gemini-1.5 Pro provides a more balanced profile (62.5% Acc, 28.2% AP50). Specialist MLLMs trained for single-image grounding (Shikra, Groma, GroundingGPT, Ferret, CogVLM-Grounding) consistently underperform generalist MLLMs of similar or smaller scale, with the 17B CogVLM-Grounding (17.5% AP50) trailing the 7B Qwen2-VL (19.1% AP50). Foundation models without LLMs (G-DINO, APE, ONE-PEACE) achieve moderate AP50 (13–19%) but suffer from poor image-level accuracy (19–41%) due to redundant low-confidence detections. The finetuned Qwen2-VL-7B baseline gains 2.8% Acc and 3.5% AP50 over its unfinetuned counterpart, confirming the benefit of multi-context instruction tuning. Even the best model (agentic baseline) trails humans by 14.4% in Acc and 5.1% in AP50, indicating substantial room for improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two well-studied but previously separate threads: single-image visual grounding (REC, DOD, open-vocabulary detection) and multi-image MLLM understanding (MMBench, MMMU, BLINK). Prior grounding benchmarks (RefCOCO, RIO, OmniLabel) are restricted to single images with relatively constrained text, while multi-image MLLM benchmarks (MMBench, SEED-Bench, Mantis-Eval) evaluate image-level VQA rather than instance-level localization. The paper identifies that no existing dataset or task formulation combines multi-image input, instance-level bounding-box annotation, and open-ended text prompts, and constructs MC-Bench to fill this gap. Methodologically, it repurposes the divide-and-conquer agentic paradigm (GPT-4o for reasoning + G-DINO for localization) and applies LoRA-based multi-context instruction tuning to Qwen2-VL, but the core contribution is the task definition and benchmark rather than a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a substantial and actionable performance gap: the best end-to-end MLLM (Qwen2-VL-72B) achieves only 30.7% AP50 versus a 41.3% human upper bound, and specialist grounding MLLMs trained exclusively on single-image data underperform generalist models in multi-context settings. The finding that a simple GPT-4o + G-DINO agentic pipeline (36.2% AP50) outperforms all end-to-end MLLMs suggests that current architectures have not yet learned to integrate cross-image reasoning with precise localization in a unified way. Open questions include whether scaling to more than two images, improving group-assignment accuracy (most models predict only one group), and reducing false positives on negative samples can close the remaining gap. The benchmark is likely to become a standard evaluation for any future MLLM claiming multi-image instance-level capabilities.
