## Problem

Existing monocular 3D HPE methods discard camera intrinsics when cropping to the human bounding box, making it impossible to accurately estimate the relative depths of joints from the cropped image alone. Moreover, because the subject can appear anywhere in the original frame, the principal point of the cropped intrinsics varies widely across samples, introducing large perspective distortions that complicate model fitting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251347/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image together with its camera intrinsics (focal length and principal point) as input. The original image is first perspective-rotated to center the subject, then center-cropped to 256×256; the corresponding cropped intrinsics K_crop are analytically computed and encoded as a 2D perspective-encoding map. The network outputs 2D joint coordinates, per-joint relative depths, and a global scale factor (mm/pixel), from which 3D XYZ joint positions are recovered by back-projection with K_crop^{-1} followed by an inverse rotation. Optionally, two additional linear heads predict SMPL shape and twist parameters for full mesh recovery.

## Method

PersPose introduces two complementary modules. Perspective Encoding (PE) projects every pixel of the cropped image onto the z=1 plane via K_crop^{-1}, producing a 2D map whose spatial pattern geometrically encodes the view frustum; this map is processed by a separate convolutional branch and element-wise added to the image features before the HRNet-W48 backbone. Perspective Rotation (PR) computes a 3D rotation (via Rodrigues' formula) that swings the bounding-box center onto the optical axis, warping the original image so the cropped principal point is fixed at the image center for every sample, thereby reducing the mapping to f_θ(I_crop, f_crop) → P_XYZ and eliminating the high-variance principal-point term. The decoder predicts a 2D heatmap (soft-argmax for pixel coordinates) and, from a global feature vector, relative depths and a scale factor; 3D positions are obtained by d_i^abs · K_crop^{-1} · [u_i, v_i, 1]^T, then rotated back by R^T. Training is fully supervised with L1 losses on UVD, scale, shape, and twist, using AdamW over 70 epochs on a single 3090 GPU.

## Results

PersPose achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three evaluation datasets. On the in-the-wild 3DPW dataset, it reaches an MPJPE of 60.1 mm, a 7.54% (4.9 mm) improvement over the previous best (Zolly, 65.0 mm), and a PA-MPJPE of 39.1 mm. On Human3.6M, PersPose attains 28.3 mm PA-MPJPE and 43.0 mm MPJPE, outperforming ProPose (29.1 / 45.7). On MPI-INF-3DHP, it achieves 94.0% PCK and 55.2 AUC, surpassing PLIKS by 2.2 and 2.9 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PersPose outperforms all 16 compared baselines on every reported metric across all three datasets. On 3DPW, the closest competitor is Zolly (ICCV'23) at 65.0 mm MPJPE; PersPose improves by 4.9 mm (7.54%), while GLNet-W48 (ECCV'24) is nearest on PA-MPJPE (39.5 vs. 39.1). On Human3.6M, ProPose (CVPR'23) is the strongest baseline at 29.1 PA-MPJPE / 45.7 MPJPE, with PersPose improving by 0.8 and 2.7 mm respectively; VirtualMarker (CVPR'23) leads on PVE (58.0) but PersPose reduces it to 52.7. On MPI-INF-3DHP, PLIKS (CVPR'23) is the nearest rival at 91.8% PCK / 52.3 AUC / 72.3 MPJPE, with PersPose gaining 2.2 PCK, 2.9 AUC, and 0.2 MPJPE. Notably, PersPose is the only method reporting results on all three datasets simultaneously, and its advantage is most pronounced on the in-the-wild 3DPW benchmark, suggesting the perspective encoding and rotation modules are particularly effective under diverse real-world shooting conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the UVD (2D coordinates + relative depth) formulation for monocular 3D HPE used by methods like HybrIK, VirtualMarker, and PLIKS, but departs from them by explicitly incorporating camera intrinsics as model input. While CLIFF [18] demonstrated that bounding box information is needed for global rotation estimation, this paper identifies a previously overlooked limitation: cropped images alone cannot determine relative joint depths without the corresponding cropped intrinsics. The Perspective Encoding (PE) module repurposes the standard pinhole projection geometry to create a 2D encoding of the view frustum, and the Perspective Rotation (PR) module introduces a geometric recentering step that has no direct analogue in prior 3D HPE pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conceptual demonstration that relative depth is fundamentally ambiguous without intrinsics (Figure 2) reframes how the community thinks about what information a cropped image actually contains, and the PE/PR modules offer a practical, lightweight fix that could be adopted by other UVD-based or even direct-regression 3D HPE methods. The 7.54% MPJPE improvement on 3DPW and consistent gains across three datasets with thorough ablations make this a credible SOTA contribution. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video-based 3D HPE (where intrinsics may be estimated rather than given), and whether the benefits persist when intrinsics are noisy or only approximately known, as is common in in-the-wild settings.
