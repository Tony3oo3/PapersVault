## Problem

Existing V2X cooperative perception datasets (DAIR-V2X, V2X-Seq, V2X-Real) lack systematic coverage of challenging multi-condition scenarios such as heavy rain at night, congestion, and occlusion over extended road sections. This scarcity of real-world data under adverse conditions makes it difficult to validate whether and how much cooperative perception actually improves safety in the high-risk driving situations that matter most.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251883/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides synchronized multi-sensor data from 35 sensors (13 cameras, 11 LiDARs of three beam types, and 2 4D millimeter-wave radars on the roadside; 6 cameras and 3 LiDARs on the vehicle) across a 600 m corridor with 7 roadside positions, all time-aligned via PTP to <10 ms. Outputs are 332,596 seven-dimensional 3D bounding boxes (center, height, width, length, heading) across 8 object classes plus a dedicated autonomous-vehicle category, each carrying a unique tracking ID that persists across the full road section and sequential frames. Sequences span 8–51 seconds and are tagged with human-written scene-descriptive labels (e.g., 'Driving to the intersection on a heavy rainy Night').

## Method

V2XScenes is a dataset and benchmark contribution rather than a new perception architecture. The collection pipeline deploys heterogeneous LiDARs (128-beam mechanical, 125-beam solid-state, 32-beam blind-repair) and 4D radars in close proximity at each roadside position to enable direct sensor-comparison under identical conditions, while the vehicle carries a full surround-view camera and LiDAR suite. Calibration unifies all sensors into a global ENU coordinate frame via a two-stage process: roadside LiDAR–camera extrinsics are solved with a planar target and optimized by EPnP/Kabsch, and vehicle-to-infrastructure alignment is refined with point-to-point ICP against a dense pre-built map. For the detection benchmark, all roadside data is transformed into ego-vehicle coordinates and fused with vehicle-side data using a 100 m communication threshold; state-of-the-art intermediate-fusion methods (V2X-ViT, CoBEVT, CoAlign, Where2Comm, F-Cooper, AttuFuse) are evaluated on a PointPillars backbone trained for 30 epochs. The tracking benchmark pairs the fused detection pipeline with AB3DMOT and compares specialized roadside trackers (ISN, SAIKF, SpaRTA, PCGDA, CMSMC, InfraDet3D) using MOTA, MOTP, MT, and ML metrics, with results stratified by weather condition and transmission latency.

## Results

On the V2XScenes cooperative 3D object detection benchmark, intermediate fusion methods substantially outperform naive fusion: CoAlign achieves the best AP3D@0.3 (58.41%) and AP3D@0.7 (28.20%), while V2X-ViT leads at AP3D@0.5 (51.11%), compared to Early Fusion (35.59/23.75/10.31) and Late Fusion (39.97/36.21/23.72). Under heavy rain, CoBEVT delivers the largest improvement over no-fusion, gaining +89.4% at IoU=0.3 and +133.0% at IoU=0.7 for car detection. For large-range roadside tracking, ISN achieves the best MOTP (72.54%), MT (60.32%), and lowest ML (14.51%), while SAIKF attains the highest MOTA (55.47%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the six intermediate fusion methods evaluated on the detection benchmark, CoAlign [28] and V2X-ViT [49] form the top tier, with CoAlign leading at IoU=0.3 (58.41 vs. 58.10) and IoU=0.7 (28.20 vs. 26.48), while V2X-ViT edges ahead at IoU=0.5 (51.11 vs. 50.57). AttuFuse [52] (57.11/46.69/20.88) and CoBEVT [48] (54.50/43.48/21.92) trail by 1–4 points at IoU=0.3 but remain well above the naive baselines. F-Cooper [7] (53.48/40.45/17.27) and Where2Comm [13] (52.43/42.90/17.79) are competitive at moderate IoU but degrade sharply at IoU=0.7. Early and Late Fusion serve as lower bounds, with Early Fusion particularly weak (10.31 at IoU=0.7), confirming that raw data merging is insufficient for cooperative perception. On the tracking benchmark, ISN [32] achieves the best MOTP (72.54%), MT (60.32%), and ML (14.51%), outperforming SAIKF [16] (67.61/53.53/22.94) and SpaRTA [5] (63.42/42.84/23.46); however, SAIKF records a slightly higher MOTA (55.47 vs. 53.39). PCGDA [45], CMSMC [21], and InfraDet3D [61] all fall below 50% MOTA, indicating that generic tracking pipelines struggle with the large-range, multi-occlusion conditions in V2XScenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the V2X cooperative perception dataset lineage (DAIR-V2X, V2X-Seq, V2X-Real, V2V4Real) by introducing the first dataset explicitly organized around challenging multi-condition scenarios (heavy rain, night, congestion, occlusion) over a 600 m successive road corridor with seven roadside positions. Unlike prior V2X datasets that treat all sequences uniformly, V2XScenes assigns scene-descriptive labels to each sequence and provides unique tracking IDs across the entire road section, enabling condition-specific evaluation. The benchmark itself reuses existing cooperative perception methods (V2X-ViT, CoBEVT, CoAlign, Where2Comm, F-Cooper, AttuFuse) and tracking approaches (ISN, SpaRTA, SAIKF) as baselines, positioning the contribution squarely in the dataset-and-benchmark space rather than proposing a new fusion architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset fills a clear gap: prior real-world V2X datasets lack systematic coverage of adverse-weather and congestion scenarios, making it difficult to quantify how much cooperative perception actually helps when single-vehicle sensing degrades. The finding that intermediate-fusion methods yield dramatically larger gains under rain (e.g., CoBEVT +133 % at IoU=0.7) compared to sunny conditions provides concrete evidence for deploying roadside sensors in safety-critical weather. Open questions include whether the 35 k-frame scale is sufficient for training large models, how results transfer to other geographic regions and sensor configurations, and whether the 100 m communication-distance threshold generalizes to sparser roadside deployments. The multi-sensor comparison (three LiDAR beam types plus 4D radar) also raises practical questions about cost-effective sensor selection for roadside infrastructure.
