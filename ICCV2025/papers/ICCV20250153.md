## Problem

Cooperative 3D multi-object tracking remains underexplored compared to single-frame cooperative perception. Existing cooperative tracking methods either follow a decoupled tracking-by-cooperative-detection pipeline that fails to unify tracking with cooperation, or (as in UniV2X) use rule-based query association with a fusion-before-decoding pipeline that yields suboptimal performance. No prior work provides a fully instance-level end-to-end framework with learnable cross-agent association for cooperative sequential perception.

## Input / Output

The method consumes time-series RGB images from both the ego vehicle and a roadside infrastructure unit, along with their inter-agent relative poses (rotation matrix and translation vector) at each timestamp. The vehicle perception range is [-51.2, 51.2, -51.2, 51.2] m and the infrastructure range is [0, 102.4, -51.2, 51.2] m, both with height [-5, 3] m. Output consists of per-frame 3D bounding boxes parameterized as [x, y, z, w, l, h, θ, v_x, v_y] with consistent track IDs across frames and class labels, decoded from separate motion and semantic feature streams via two FFNs.

## Method

CoopTrack introduces a fusion-after-decoding pipeline: each agent first decodes its own instance-level features, then cross-agent association and fusion occur before final box prediction. The Multi-Dimensional Feature Extraction (MDFE) module decouples semantic features (MLP on query features) from motion features (PointNet on relative 3D box corner coordinates), then refines both through a temporal transformer block with a sliding-window history of 4 frames. Cross-Agent Alignment bridges the vehicle-infrastructure domain gap by predicting a latent rotation matrix and translation vector from the explicit pose via a segmented mapping approach, transforming received features into the ego's latent space. Graph-Based Association constructs a fully connected bipartite graph over multi-dimensional features and 3D reference points, applying graph attention (node dot-product plus edge features from pairwise distances) to produce a learned affinity matrix, followed by Hungarian matching for feature aggregation. Training is two-stage: first each agent's tracker is trained independently, then the cooperative module is trained end-to-end with association supervision treated as binary classification (Focal Loss, α=0.5, γ=1.0) using labels generated by matching first-stage predictions to ground truth.

## Results

On the V2X-Seq dataset (2Hz, ResNet101), CoopTrack achieves 39.0% mAP and 32.8% AMOTA with a transmission cost of 5.64×10⁴, setting a new state-of-the-art for cooperative 3D MOT. This represents a 9.5% mAP and 8.9% AMOTA improvement over the previous end-to-end method UniV2X (29.5% mAP, 23.9% AMOTA) at a comparable transmission cost. On the Griffin aerial-ground dataset, CoopTrack reaches 47.9% mAP and 48.8% AMOTA, outperforming UniV2X by 6.0% mAP and 3.2% AMOTA. On the 10Hz V2X-Seq variant, CoopTrack (ResNet101) further improves to 44.2% mAP and 43.5% AMOTA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoopTrack outperforms all nine baselines on the primary V2X-Seq benchmark. Against the strongest tracking-by-cooperative-detection method V2X-ViT (26.8% mAP, 28.7% AMOTA), CoopTrack gains 12.2% mAP and 4.1% AMOTA while transmitting only 2.2% of the data volume (5.64×10⁴ vs. 2.56×10⁶). Compared to the closest end-to-end competitor UniV2X (29.5% mAP, 23.9% AMOTA), CoopTrack improves mAP by 9.5% and AMOTA by 8.9% at a slightly lower transmission cost. Against the No Fusion BEVFormer baseline (16.5% mAP), the cooperative gain is 22.5% mAP and 16.5% AMOTA. On Griffin, CoopTrack (47.9% mAP, 48.8% AMOTA) beats UniV2X (41.9% mAP, 45.6% AMOTA) by 6.0% mAP and 3.2% AMOTA, though Early Fusion still leads in raw accuracy (60.7% mAP) at a transmission cost 2,658× higher. On the 10Hz V2X-Seq variant, CoopTrack (ResNet101: 44.2% mAP, 43.5% AMOTA) surpasses BFF+AB3DMOT (43.0% mAP, 39.0% AMOTA) and UniV2X-Track (39.4% mAP, 41.8% AMOTA) while using roughly 1/1400th the transmission of BFF.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoopTrack extends the end-to-end query-based 3D MOT paradigm (MOTR, MUTR3D, PF-Track, ADA-Track, OneTrack) into the multi-agent cooperative setting, where prior work either followed a tracking-by-cooperative-detection pipeline or, in the case of UniV2X, used rule-based query association with a fusion-before-decoding architecture. The key departures are (1) replacing UniV2X's interpretable but rigid association with a learnable graph-attention-based module, and (2) inverting the pipeline to fuse instance-level features *after* decoding rather than merging queries *before* decoding. The multi-dimensional feature extraction (decoupling semantic and motion features via PointNet and temporal transformers) draws on PF-Track's temporal modeling and STARTrack's latent-space alignment, but repurposes them for cross-agent instance matching rather than single-agent tracking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first fully instance-level end-to-end cooperative tracking framework, and the 9.5% mAP / 4.1% AMOTA gap over the closest end-to-end competitor (UniV2X) at comparable or lower transmission cost is meaningful for the cooperative perception community, which has largely been stuck in the TBCD paradigm. The fusion-after-decoding insight and the graph-based association module are reusable architectural components that could transfer to other multi-agent perception tasks. Limitations include the two-stage training procedure (which the authors acknowledge as complex), evaluation on only two cooperative scenarios (vehicle-infrastructure and aerial-ground), and the fact that on Griffin the transmission cost is actually higher than UniV2X, weakening the efficiency claim in that setting. Open questions include scalability to more than two agents, real-time deployment on embedded hardware, and whether the graph-based association generalizes to heterogeneous sensor modalities beyond cameras.
