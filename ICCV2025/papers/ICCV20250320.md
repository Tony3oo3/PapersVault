## Problem

Existing single-view human reconstruction methods rely on 2D multi-view diffusion to hallucinate unseen views, but the resulting images are sparse and 3D-inconsistent, producing blurry appearance and artifacts in self-occluded regions. Parametric body priors like SMPL provide geometry but lack an appearance prior and cannot handle clothing or hair that deviate from the minimally-clothed body topology. The paper addresses the need for a 3D generative avatar prior that captures both realistic appearance and geometry to regularize reconstruction from these imperfect synthetic views.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single in-the-wild RGB image as input and produces a 3D Gaussian avatar represented as 2D Gaussians parameterized on a 256×256 UV map anchored to the SMPL-X template mesh. The avatar is inherently animatable via SMPL-X joint parameters without any post-processing, and supports real-time novel view synthesis through 2D Gaussian splatting. During fitting, the method consumes 6 synthetic views hallucinated by a pre-trained multi-view diffusion model (PSHuman) along with estimated SMPL-X pose and camera parameters.

## Method

MoGA introduces a generative 3D avatar prior consisting of 2D Gaussians on a UV map (with geometry residuals from SMPL-X), a shared CNN auto-decoder mapping a 64×64×32 latent code to the UV map, and a latent diffusion model, all trained jointly in a single-stage pipeline. At test time, the method performs model inversion: it initializes the latent code via image-guided sampling from the learned diffusion model, then optimizes the latent code against rendering losses (L2, VGG perceptual, normal consistency, depth distortion) on the 6 synthetic views while simultaneously applying the frozen diffusion model as a score-distillation regularizer in latent space. The generative prior contributes three distinct roles—meaningful initialization to avoid local minima, 3D appearance/geometry regularization to enforce view consistency, and photometric pose optimization to correct inaccurate camera and body poses estimated from inconsistent synthetic views. The latent code and pose parameters are optimized in an alternating manner, and the deformed Gaussians are rendered via 2D Gaussian splatting with a linear blend skinning deformation module.

## Results

On THuman2.1, MoGA achieves 24.09 PSNR, 0.9455 SSIM, and 0.0732 LPIPS, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline PSHuman (19.96 PSNR, 0.9350 SSIM, 0.0778 LPIPS) by over 4 dB in PSNR. On CustomHuman, MoGA reaches 23.44 PSNR and 0.9351 SSIM versus PSHuman's 18.67 PSNR and 0.9223 SSIM. For geometry, MoGA attains the best Chamfer Distance (1.36 mm on THuman2.1, 1.81 mm on CustomHuman) and Point-to-Surface distance (1.22 mm, 1.48 mm) across all baselines, with the largest gains in appearance metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoGA outperforms all four baselines on every metric across both datasets. The largest margin is in PSNR: MoGA leads PSHuman (the strongest baseline) by 4.13 dB on THuman2.1 and 4.77 dB on CustomHuman, and leads SIFU by 6.56 dB and 7.42 dB respectively. In geometry (CD and P2S), MoGA also wins but the gap over PSHuman is narrower (e.g., 1.36 vs. 1.41 mm CD on THuman2.1), suggesting PSHuman's SMPL-based regularization already provides reasonable shape constraints. SiTH and SIFU lag significantly on both appearance and geometry, with SIFU showing the worst PSNR (17.53 / 16.02) and the largest Chamfer distances. Human3Diffusion is excluded from quantitative comparison due to its low-resolution rendering and is only compared qualitatively in Fig. 3, where it also underperforms MoGA.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoGA builds directly on the single-stage joint training pipeline of SSDNeRF for co-optimizing a decoder and latent diffusion model, and on AG3D's deformation module with voxel-grid skinning weights, while adopting 2D Gaussian Splatting as the canonical-space representation. It departs from prior single-view human reconstruction methods (SIFU, SiTH, PSHuman) that use SMPL as a purely geometric template and rely on 2D multi-view diffusion for hallucination, by introducing a learned 3D generative avatar prior that encodes both appearance and geometry. The key conceptual shift is repurposing a 3D generative model—previously used only for unconditional or few-shot generation—as a regularizer and initializer during per-identity model inversion, bridging the generation and reconstruction literatures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core weakness of diffusion-based multi-view hallucination (3D inconsistency) by showing that a learned 3D generative prior can enforce cross-view coherence during reconstruction, yielding substantially sharper and more geometrically faithful avatars. The threefold role of the prior (initialization, regularization, pose refinement) provides a reusable framework that could extend to other articulated or deformable object categories. Limitations include dependence on 3D human training data for the generative prior, continued reliance on a 2D multi-view diffusion model for view hallucination, and qualitative-only evaluation on in-the-wild images; open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-human subjects or can be made fully data-free.
