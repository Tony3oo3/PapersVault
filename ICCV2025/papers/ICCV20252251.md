## Problem

Foundational monocular depth estimators (FMDEs) trained on tens of millions of perspective images produce erroneous depth estimates on fisheye images due to covariate shift from differing camera calibration and distortion parameters. Existing remedies—re-projection to a canonical frame or finetuning the full model—introduce spatial artifacts, require error-prone calibration, or risk parameter drift that degrades generalizability across 3D scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252251/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image (fisheye or perspective) at 518×518 for training and up to 700×700 at test time, and outputs a dense monocular depth map. No camera intrinsics or distortion parameters are required at inference; the model simply appends or omits Calibration Tokens to switch between fisheye and perspective modes. The approach is entirely lossless in pixel space—no warping, cropping, or re-projection is applied to the input or output.

## Method

The core mechanism inserts a small set of trainable Calibration Tokens (8 tokens per layer, 8 layers total) into the encoder of a frozen ViT-based FMDE, where they modulate patch embeddings via self-attention to align fisheye latent representations toward the perspective-image distribution. Training is self-supervised: perspective images are synthetically distorted into fisheye images using the Kannala–Brandt polynomial model, and the loss (LogL1) is computed by undistorting the fisheye depth prediction back into the perspective frame, thereby preserving the high-fidelity supervision signal without re-projection artifacts. Only the Calibration Tokens are optimized (~0.8 MB for ViT-L backbones); the entire FMDE backbone and decoder remain frozen, keeping the original perspective-image performance intact and adding less than 1 ms of inference latency.

## Results

On ScanNet++ (indoor), UniDepth with Calibration Tokens achieves the best RMSE of 0.244 and δ₁ of 0.766, outperforming the best baseline (DepthAnyCamera, RMSE 0.275) by 11%. On KITTI-360 (outdoor), UniDepth with Calibration Tokens achieves RMSE 2.040 and δ₁ 0.664, beating DepthAnyCamera (RMSE 2.067) and FoVA-Depth (RMSE 3.096). Calibration Tokens consistently improve all three FMDEs: MiDaS gains 12% (indoor) and 29% (outdoor) RMSE reduction, DepthAnything gains 17% (indoor) and 8% (outdoor), and UniDepth gains 13% (indoor) and 2% (outdoor). Notably, a single set of tokens trained on a joint 200K-sample dataset handles both indoor and outdoor settings without domain-specific retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two state-of-the-art fisheye depth estimation methods: DepthAnyCamera [24] and FoVA-Depth [36], both of which rely on equirectangular projection and require camera intrinsics at test time. On ScanNet++ (indoor), UniDepth + Calibration Tokens (RMSE 0.244, δ₁ 0.766) outperforms DepthAnyCamera (RMSE 0.275, δ₁ 0.761) by 11% in RMSE and matches it on δ₁, while beating FoVA-Depth (RMSE 0.285, δ₁ 0.548) by 14% in RMSE and 22% in δ₁. On KITTI-360 (outdoor), UniDepth + Calibration Tokens (RMSE 2.040) narrowly beats DepthAnyCamera (RMSE 2.067) by 1.3% in RMSE, though DepthAnyCamera achieves a higher δ₁ (0.852 vs. 0.664); however, it substantially outperforms FoVA-Depth (RMSE 3.096) by 34% in RMSE. A key practical advantage is that Calibration Tokens require no camera intrinsics at inference and use a single token set across both domains, whereas DepthAnyCamera and FoVA-Depth were trained on separate indoor/outdoor datasets and depend on per-image calibration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of foundational monocular depth estimators (MiDaS, DepthAnything, UniDepth) that are trained exclusively on perspective images, by introducing lightweight Calibration Tokens appended to ViT encoder layers to modulate latent embeddings toward the perspective distribution. The training strategy is directly inspired by AugUndo's use of invertible transformations to preserve supervision signals, applied here to synthesize fisheye images from perspective inputs and undo the distortion in output space. It departs from prior fisheye depth methods (DepthAnyCamera, FoVA-Depth) that rely on equirectangular projection and camera intrinsics at test time, and from approaches that train entirely separate networks for fisheye cameras, by instead reusing a frozen FMDE backbone with only a tiny set of trainable tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for multi-camera systems in autonomous driving, robotics, and XR, where a single depth model must handle both perspective and fisheye inputs without retraining or per-camera calibration. The backward-compatibility (simply omit tokens for perspective images) and the negligible overhead (0.05% parameter increase, <1 ms latency) make this an attractive drop-in adaptation. A key open question is whether the approach generalizes beyond the Kannala-Brandt distortion model to other wide-angle or non-standard camera geometries, and whether the method's success is bounded by the representational ceiling of the underlying FMDE, as the authors acknowledge.
