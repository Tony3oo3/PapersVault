## Problem

One-step diffusion models, which enable real-time image synthesis by collapsing multi-step denoising into a single forward pass, lack support for negative prompting. Classifier-free guidance (CFG), the standard mechanism for suppressing unwanted features, relies on iterative refinement across dozens of denoising steps and produces severe blending artifacts when applied directly to one-step generation. This speed-versus-control gap is the primary barrier to adopting one-step generators in professional creative workflows.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a positive text prompt, a negative text prompt (e.g., 'worst quality, blurry, bad anatomy'), and a random latent noise vector, producing a single generated image in one forward pass (or a short video in the CausVid extension). The negative prompt is encoded by the same CLIP text encoder as the positive prompt and injected through the existing cross-attention layers, so no additional model parameters are introduced. A scalar scale factor α (typically 0.1–1.0) controls the strength of negative feature suppression, and the method is drop-in compatible with existing one-step/few-step backbones such as LCM, DMD2, and FLUX.1-schnell.

## Method

NASA (Negative-Away Steer Attention) relocates the guidance subtraction from the output (noise-prediction) space to the intermediate cross-attention representation space. At each cross-attention layer, the same query Q is attended against both positive (K⁺, V⁺) and negative (K⁻, V⁻) key-value pairs derived from the respective text embeddings, yielding separate attention outputs Z⁺ and Z⁻; the final output is Z⁺ − α·Z⁻, which attenuates attention responses associated with unwanted semantics before the image is synthesized. This representation-space operation avoids the blending artifacts of output-space CFG while incurring only a 1.89% FLOPs increase (versus CFG's 100% doubling). Two deployment modes are provided: NASA-I, a training-free inference-time modification applicable to any pretrained one-step/few-step model, and NASA-T, which integrates negative-prompt conditioning into the SwiftBrush variational score distillation training loop so the student model learns to suppress undesirable features during distillation.

## Results

On the PixArt-α backbone, NASA-T+NASA-I (α=1) achieves a state-of-the-art HPSv2 average of 31.21, surpassing the SBv2* baseline (30.34) by +0.87 and the DMD baseline (28.71) by +2.50. On SDv1.5 and SDv2.1 backbones, NASA-T+NASA-I reaches 27.46 and 27.93 HPSv2 respectively, improving over SBv2* by +0.44 and +0.58. On the NegOpt dataset, NASA-I reduces CLIP⁻ (negative-prompt alignment) from 0.09 to 0.05 while maintaining CLIP⁺ at 0.35, confirming effective suppression of unwanted features. Notably, direct CFG application degrades one-step outputs (DMD+CFG=2.5 drops from 28.71 to 25.10), whereas NASA-I consistently improves quality across all tested models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NASA-T+NASA-I outperforms all compared one-step distillation methods across all three backbones. Against the closest baseline SBv2* (Dao et al., ECCV 2024), the combined method gains +0.87 HPSv2 on PixArt-α (31.21 vs 30.34), +0.44 on SDv1.5 (27.46 vs 27.02), and +0.58 on SDv2.1 (27.93 vs 27.35). Against DMD (Yin et al., CVPR 2024), the improvement is +2.50 on PixArt-α (31.21 vs 28.71), and against DMD2 (Yin et al., NeurIPS 2024) it is +1.21 on SDv1.5 (27.46 vs 26.25). YOSO (Luo et al., 2024) and InstaFlow (Liu et al., 2023) trail by +2.73 and +1.31 respectively on their respective backbones. Critically, the paper demonstrates that naive CFG application to one-step models is harmful (DMD+CFG=2.5 drops 3.61 points), while NASA-I provides consistent gains of +0.01 to +0.87 depending on the backbone and model, establishing it as the first viable negative-prompt mechanism for single-step generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classifier-free guidance (CFG) paradigm by relocating the positive-minus-negative subtraction from the output (noise-prediction) space to the intermediate cross-attention representation space, enabling negative prompting in one-step and few-step diffusion models where iterative CFG is inapplicable. It builds directly on the SwiftBrush v2 (SBv2) image-free distillation framework, modifying its VSD training loop to incorporate negative-prompt conditioning in both the teacher and student. The approach also draws on the cross-attention control literature (e.g., Prompt-to-Prompt) for the insight that attention maps encode image-text semantic alignment, repurposing them as a guidance surface rather than an editing interface.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NASA fills a practical gap that blocks professional adoption of one-step generators: without negative prompting, users cannot suppress artifacts or unwanted attributes in real-time generation. The 1.89% FLOP overhead (vs. CFG's 2×) makes this viable for latency-sensitive applications, and the method's architecture-agnostic design (validated on U-Net, DiT, and MMDiT backbones) suggests broad adoptability. Open questions include whether the fixed per-architecture α scaling generalizes to unseen models, whether the technique composes with other guidance strategies (e.g., attention guidance, prompt-to-prompt editing), and whether the distillation-time integration (NASA-T) can be extended to multi-step student models or other modalities beyond the demonstrated text-to-video case.
