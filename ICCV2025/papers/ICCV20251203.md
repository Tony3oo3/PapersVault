## Problem

Spatiotemporal video grounding has been extensively studied on exocentric videos, but the egocentric setting remains underexplored despite its importance for AR and robotics. Existing egocentric datasets (EgoTracks, RefEgo) provide only bounding box annotations without pixel-level masks, and no benchmark exists for fine-grained spatiotemporal grounding across varying video durations in the egocentric domain. The paper also identifies a systematic gap: egocentric objects have shorter durations (21.56% vs. >75%), sparser trajectories (1.33% vs. >65%), smaller sizes (1.20% vs. >5% frame area), and larger positional shifts (14.96% vs. >50% adjacent-frame IoU).

## Input / Output

The model receives egocentric video frames sampled at 1 FPS (videos range from 5 seconds to 16 minutes) along with a natural-language referring expression averaging 15 words that may encode visual attributes, spatial relations, or world knowledge about the target object. The output is a sequence of pixel-level segmentation masks across the video, forming a temporal tube that localizes the referred entity frame-by-frame. The benchmark is stratified into short (<1 min, 400 queries), medium (1–3 min, 200 queries), and long (>3 min, 100 queries) subsets to test robustness across durations.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is an automatic annotation pipeline that constructs both a benchmark (EgoMask: 700 queries over 315 videos) and a large-scale training set (EgoMask-Train: 2,624 videos, 9,592 objects, 47,968 expressions). Mask generation uses SAM2 prompted with bounding boxes from EgoTracks, with post-processing that clips masks to the annotated box region to suppress hallucination. Referring expressions are generated by GPT-4o via two complementary strategies: (1) direct short/long description generation from three representative frames, and (2) metadata extraction (caption, visual attributes, world knowledge) followed by template-based composition. For evaluation, the paper introduces IoU_gold and IoU_gold_pred metrics that penalize or ignore background-frame predictions, addressing the fact that egocentric videos contain far more background frames than exocentric ones. Experiments fine-tune Sa2VA-4B and VideoLISA-3.8B on EgoMask-Train (mixed with exocentric segmentation data), showing 41.30% average relative improvement on EgoMask while preserving exocentric performance.

## Results

On the proposed EgoMask benchmark, all SOTA spatiotemporal grounding models perform poorly: the best IoU_gold_pred score is under 50% even on the easiest subset (EgoMask-Short), and drops below 30% on Medium and Long subsets. Fine-tuning VideoLISA-3.8B on EgoMask-Train yields an average relative improvement of 41.30% (absolute +3.67%) in IoU_gold_pred across all three subsets, while Sa2VA-4B gains a relative 5.74% (absolute +1.20%). Crucially, fine-tuned models retain or improve performance on exocentric benchmarks (e.g., VideoLISA-3.8B(+FT) gains +1.77% on ReasonVOS; Sa2VA-4B(+FT) surpasses its baseline on all three exocentric benchmarks).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates three baselines: Grounded-SAM2 (pipeline: GroundingDino + SAM2), Sa2VA (end-to-end VideoLLM linking LLaVA with SAM2, in 4B and 26B variants), and VideoLISA (end-to-end VideoLLM using [SEG] token with SAM). Grounded-SAM2 achieves the highest IoU_gold_pred on EgoMask-Short (49.95) but degrades sharply on longer videos (25.73 Medium, 24.80 Long), and scores 0.00 on ReasonVOS due to error propagation from its detection stage. Sa2VA-26B outperforms Sa2VA-4B across all subsets (e.g., 37.30 vs. 29.00 on Short), yet both struggle on Long videos (12.96 and 8.11 respectively). VideoLISA-3.8B is the weakest baseline overall (17.85/6.48/5.15), confirming that independent per-frame segmentation is inferior to SAM2-based video propagation. Fine-tuning on EgoMask-Train produces the largest gains for VideoLISA-3.8B (+5.51 on Short, +3.50 on Medium, +2.01 on Long in IoU_gold_pred), while Sa2VA-4B gains are more modest (+1.97/+1.50/+0.13). On exocentric benchmarks, fine-tuned models match or exceed their zero-shot counterparts, confirming the training data is complementary rather than domain-specific.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established task of spatiotemporal video grounding (referring video object segmentation) from exocentric to egocentric videos, where prior benchmarks (Ref-DAVIS, MeViS, ReasonVOS) have exclusively featured third-person footage. It builds directly on EgoTracks for bounding box annotations and RefEgo for short-video text queries, but departs from both by introducing pixel-level mask annotations via SAM2 and diverse referring expressions via GPT-4o, filling the gap that neither dataset supports. The automatic annotation pipeline repurposes SAM2 as a segmentation annotator and GPT-4o as a language generator, a strategy analogous to how prior RVOS datasets were constructed but adapted to the challenges of egocentric footage (small objects, sparse trajectories, large camera motion).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EgoMask is the first pixel-level benchmark for egocentric spatiotemporal grounding, and its accompanying training set (EgoMask-Train) is shown to be complementary to exocentric data—fine-tuned models improve substantially on egocentric videos without degrading on exocentric benchmarks—making it a practical resource for building egocentric video foundation models for AR and robotics. A key open question the paper surfaces is the initialization problem: SAM2-based models collapse to near-zero performance when the first few frames lack the target object, which is common in long egocentric videos with sparse trajectories, and no current architecture adequately addresses this. The finding that even Grounded-SAM2, which uses the same SAM2 backbone as the annotation pipeline, fails on the benchmark underscores that the difficulty is not merely a segmentation quality issue but a fundamental challenge of grounding under rapid camera motion and small object sizes.
