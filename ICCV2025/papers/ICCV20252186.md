## Problem

Existing face retouching methods struggle to simultaneously preserve local fine details (pores, texture) and maintain global consistency in skin colour and tone, partly because large-scale paired training data with diverse blemish types (acne, moles, freckles) is scarce. Most available benchmarks contain fewer than 20% images with actual blemishes, limiting both training signal and evaluation robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252186/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 1024×1024 RGB face image with blemishes and produces a blemish-free retouched portrait at the same resolution. Internally, the input is decomposed into a 3-level Laplacian pyramid (l=2 downsampling steps), and a U-Net-based soft mask branch simultaneously predicts a low-resolution mask highlighting defect regions to guide targeted repair. The final output is reconstructed by progressively fusing multi-scale retouched results back to full resolution.

## Method

The framework combines three components: (1) a Soft Mask Generation Branch (U-Net) that predicts defect-region masks to focus repair; (2) Frequency Selection and Restoration (FSR), which applies channel-wise DFT to feature maps, uses learnable quantization matrices (inspired by JPEG compression) to selectively weight magnitude and phase spectra, and refines features via spatial-domain self-attention with GEGLU activations; (3) Multi-Resolution Fusion (MRF), which fuses Laplacian pyramid levels using CLC (Conv-LeakyReLU-Conv) modules and injects high-frequency components from the original image to compensate for downsampling loss. The benchmark HGFR (25,000 pairs at 1024×1024) is generated by three fine-tuned Stable Diffusion models for clean faces and LoRA-based blemish synthesis on segmented facial regions, yielding 83% blemished images versus <20% in prior benchmarks.

## Results

On the combined FFHQR+HGFR training setup, the proposed method achieves PSNR 49.15 dB, SSIM 0.994, and LPIPS 0.0094, outperforming all baselines across all three training configurations (FFHQR, HGFR, and FFHQR+HGFR). On HGFR alone, the method reaches PSNR 48.02 dB and SSIM 0.991, while on FFHQR alone it achieves PSNR 45.48 dB and SSIM 0.993. The method consistently attains the lowest LPIPS (best perceptual quality) in every setting, with the largest margin over AutoRetouch (LPIPS 0.0082 vs. 0.0744 on FFHQR+HGFR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five baselines on every metric across all three training setups. The closest competitors are RetouchFormer and BPFRe: on FFHQR+HGFR, the proposed method gains +0.37 dB PSNR over RetouchFormer (49.15 vs. 48.78) and +0.63 dB over BPFRe (49.15 vs. 48.52), while also achieving the lowest LPIPS (0.0094 vs. 0.0105 and 0.0128 respectively). ABPN, which uses a pyramid architecture, trails by 1.5 dB PSNR on FFHQR+HGFR and shows notably higher LPIPS (0.0178), indicating weaker perceptual quality. pix2pixHD, a general image-to-image translation model, lags by 6.5 dB PSNR, and AutoRetouch is far behind at 31.06 dB PSNR, confirming that task-specific designs are critical. Qualitatively, the paper notes that AutoRetouch introduces global noise, pix2pixHD suffers color shifts, and ABPN/RetouchFormer/BPFRe fall short in fine detail preservation, whereas the proposed FSR+MRF framework balances local blemish repair with global facial consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the deep-learning face retouching lineage (AutoRetouch, ABPN, BPFRe, RetouchFormer) but departs in two key ways. First, it repurposes Stable Diffusion with LoRA fine-tuning as a data-generation engine to synthesize paired blemished/blemish-free faces, rather than relying on manually collected or GAN-generated pairs as prior benchmarks (FFHQR, PPR10K) do. Second, the retouching architecture introduces frequency-domain operations—DFT-based dynamic aggregation with learnable quantization matrices inspired by JPEG compression—combined with Laplacian pyramid multi-resolution fusion, a combination not previously explored in face retouching pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HGFR benchmark (25K pairs, 83% blemish coverage vs. <20% in existing datasets) addresses a genuine data-scarcity bottleneck that has limited face retouching research, and its synthetic nature sidesteps privacy concerns. However, the method's quantitative gains over strong baselines like RetouchFormer and BPFRe are modest (≈0.2–0.4 dB PSNR), and the evaluation is limited to three blemish types (moles, freckles, acne) on 1024×1024 images. Open questions include whether the frequency-domain quantization generalizes to more diverse or severe blemishes, whether synthetic-to-real transfer holds for real-world retouching, and whether the multi-resolution fusion overhead is justified at higher resolutions.
