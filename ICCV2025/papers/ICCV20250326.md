## Problem

Existing crowd evacuation simulation methods rely on simplified 2D representations or lack the ability to integrate 3D proxemics-aware decision-making with personalized, physically realistic motion in real time. No prior method can simultaneously support online path planning, personalized gait for diverse agent attributes (age, disability), collision avoidance in dense 3D crowds, and uneven terrain within a single physics-based framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250326/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a scene mesh, an exit location, initial agent positions, and per-agent attribute labels (categorized into five groups: young, middle-aged, elderly, patients, disabled) as input. It produces a real-time 3D evacuation animation in a physics engine (Isaac Gym) where each agent's trajectory, speed, and gait are individually tailored, along with a part-level force visualization that maps contact forces across 24 body parts using a color gradient. The simulation runs online in an iterative SDM loop, with each agent perceiving its local neighborhood and updating its state every timestep.

## Method

RESCUE follows a neuroscience-inspired Sensory-Decision-Motor (SDM) loop where each agent perceives self-state, partial other-state, and environment state, then computes a desired speed via a 3D-adaptive Social Force Model, and finally executes motion through a physics-based controller. The Decision module extends the classic SFM with a novel evasive force (computed over a 45° front sector to trigger lateral detours when obstacles block the path) and personalized force coefficients optimized empirically so that simulated speeds match real-world escape speeds per agent group. The Motor module uses Pacer for trajectory tracking and a diffusion-based Personalized Gait Converter (CAMDM backbone) that transforms non-personalized action frames into attribute-specific gaits by matching frames at four gait events (Initial Contact, Mid Stance, Opposite Initial Contact, Feet Adjacent) from the 100STYLE dataset; only upper-body actions are replaced with personalized versions while lower-body motion remains physics-driven for terrain adaptation. A Part-level Force Visualization layer records contact forces at 24 body parts per timestep and renders them as a color gradient to support evacuation safety analysis.

## Results

Across four evacuation scenes (50 agents, 1000 frames, 10 runs per scene), RESCUE achieves an average escape success rate of 0.84, substantially outperforming OmniControl (0.48) and MaskedMimic (0.60). In terms of physical stability, RESCUE produces an average of 12.26 falls per scene, compared to 18.55 for MaskedMimic; OmniControl does not produce falls as it lacks physical simulation. The ablation study further confirms that removing the 3D-adaptive SFM leads to collisions and crowd accumulation, while removing the Personalized Gait Controller results in homogeneous, non-personalized gaits across agents.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RESCUE outperforms both baselines on both metrics. Against OmniControl [43] (a diffusion-based motion generation method adapted for evacuation), RESCUE improves the average success rate from 0.48 to 0.84; OmniControl's agents deviate from trajectories and stop prematurely due to insufficient controllability for long-distance movements, and it lacks physical realism so no falls are recorded. Against MaskedMimic [37] (a physics-based character control method), RESCUE raises the success rate from 0.60 to 0.84 and reduces the average fallen count from 18.55 to 12.26; MaskedMimic lacks a collision-avoidance mechanism, causing agents to collide during linear movement and accumulate in crowds, and all agents exhibit similar gaits without personalization. The Social Force Model [14,15] is included only as a qualitative 2D baseline (Figure 5) since it cannot represent 3D human motion or physical interactions; it assumes agents always reach their desired positions, producing distorted speed outputs. Overall, RESCUE is the only method that simultaneously achieves online path planning, 3D human models, personalized gait, collision visualization, and uneven-terrain compatibility (Table 1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads—Social Force Model–based pedestrian decision-making (Helbing & Molnar, 1995) and physics-based character control (Pacer, Rempe et al., CVPR 2023)—into a single online 3D evacuation pipeline structured around a neuroscience-inspired Sensory-Decision-Motor loop. The 3D-adaptive SFM extends the classical 2D force model with an evasive force and per-agent coefficient optimization, while the Motor module repurposes Pacer's trajectory-tracking policy and wraps it with a diffusion-based gait converter (built on CAMDM) trained on the 100STYLE dataset to inject personalized locomotion. The key conceptual departure from prior crowd-simulation work is the explicit integration of a physics engine with per-agent personalized motion, rather than treating agents as point masses or using generic motion patterns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first online, physically grounded 3D evacuation simulator that simultaneously handles personalized gait, collision avoidance, and uneven terrain, enabling quantitative analysis of stampede risk as a function of crowd density, corridor width, and surface friction. Part-level force visualization offers a new diagnostic tool for identifying where and how contact forces concentrate during congestion. Open questions include scalability beyond a few hundred agents, long-duration simulations with dynamic scene changes (e.g., fire spread, door closures), and whether the diffusion-based gait converter can be replaced by lighter-weight alternatives to further reduce per-agent compute cost. The authors note generalizability to autonomous-driving and other multi-agent settings but do not validate this claim experimentally.
