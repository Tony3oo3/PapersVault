## Problem

Existing continual VQA methods assume balanced data distributions, yet real-world VQA answers follow long-tailed distributions where a few classes dominate. This paper identifies that the combination of continual learning and long-tailed imbalance produces two distinct failure modes—inner-task prototype drift (classifier prototypes collapse toward majority classes) and inter-task feature drift (features deviate from prototypes across tasks)—and that no prior method addresses both simultaneously in the multimodal VQA setting.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images (36 object regions extracted by Faster R-CNN, encoded with region features, bounding box coordinates, and learned region IDs) and question text (WordPiece-tokenized, max 20 tokens) as input, and produces a single answer label from K classes via argmax over inner products of the fused multimodal feature with classifier prototypes. The encoder-decoder transformer uses 12 blocks with 768-dimensional embeddings and 12 attention heads, and a 5000-sample memory buffer provides replay from prior tasks during training.

## Method

The proposed dual-balance approach comprises two modules built on the Neural Collapse principle. The Balanced Classifier Prototype (BCP) module replaces random classifier initialization with an Equiangular Tight Frame (ETF) structure and introduces a learnable orthogonal projection matrix U* that is optimized via cross-entropy loss, preserving maximal equiangular separation among K class prototypes while adapting their directions to the data. The Multi-modal Feature Alignment (MFA) module computes an entropic optimal transport distance between the empirical distribution of updated features and the distribution of balanced prototypes, penalizing feature drift during continual learning. The total training loss combines the CE loss with a weighted OT alignment term (λ=5), and the model is trained sequentially over tasks organized by question type, with a replay buffer mitigating forgetting from prior tasks.

## Results

On VQA v2 (ordered scenario), the proposed method achieves 40.69% Average Performance (AP) and 2.11% Average Forgetting (AF), outperforming the strongest replay-based baseline ER (34.30% AP, 2.90% AF) by 6.39% in AP and the VQA-specific continual learning method VQACL (37.98% AP, 2.47% AF) by 2.71% in AP. On TDIUC (ordered scenario), the method reaches 54.69% AP and 6.06% AF, surpassing VQACL (52.27% AP, 6.70% AF) and ER (48.64% AP, 7.25% AF). The Joint upper bound (all tasks trained simultaneously) is 46.3% on VQA v2 and 63.78% on TDIUC, indicating the method closes a substantial portion of the gap between continual and offline training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all eight baselines across both datasets and both task-ordering scenarios. Against the strongest replay-based baseline ER [9], it gains +6.39% AP on VQA v2 (40.69 vs 34.30) and +6.05% AP on TDIUC (54.69 vs 48.64) in the ordered setting, while also reducing AF. Compared to the VQA-specific continual learning method VQACL [56], which does not address long-tailed imbalance, the method improves AP by +2.71% on VQA v2 and +2.42% on TDIUC (ordered), confirming the benefit of the balanced prototype design. The regularization-based methods EWC [24] and MAS [1] lag far behind (AP 20.74–29.69% on TDIUC), as their parameter constraints limit adaptation to new tasks. Long-tailed-only methods LDAM [6] and GCL [25] achieve only ~16% AP on VQA v2 without any anti-forgetting mechanism, highlighting that addressing class imbalance alone is insufficient in a continual setting. The method also shows greater robustness to task ordering: while EWC and MAS exhibit large AP swings between ordered and random scenarios on TDIUC (e.g., 20.74% vs 28.04% for EWC), the proposed method maintains consistent performance (54.69% vs 54.66% AP).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads—neural-collapse-inspired balanced classification (Papyan et al., NAS 2020; Yang et al., NeurIPS 2022) and continual learning for VQA (VQACL, CVPR 2023; TRIPLET, ICCV 2023)—into a single framework. It departs from standard continual VQA methods that assume balanced class distributions by introducing the long-tailed constraint, and from long-tailed learning methods (LDAM, GCL) that ignore sequential forgetting, by proposing a dual-balance mechanism (ETF-based prototype learning + OT-based feature alignment) that addresses both simultaneously. The use of optimal transport as the alignment metric, rather than simpler L2 or cosine distances, is a deliberate choice motivated by geometric preservation of the prototype structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CLT-VQA setting is practically relevant: real-world VQA systems (AI assistants, robotics) face both evolving question types and inherently imbalanced answer distributions, yet prior work treated these challenges in isolation. The paper's finding that long-tailed handling alone (LDAM, GCL) yields larger gains than anti-forgetting regularization (EWC, MAS) in this setting suggests the community may be over-investing in forgetting mitigation at the expense of distributional balance. Open questions include whether the approach scales to autoregressive or open-ended VQA, whether the ETF constraint remains beneficial when the number of answer classes far exceeds the feature dimension, and whether the OT alignment generalizes beyond the two relatively small datasets (VQA v2, TDIUC) used here.
