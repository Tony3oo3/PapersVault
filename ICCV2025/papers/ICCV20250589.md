## Problem

Existing data poisoning backdoor attacks on self-supervised contrastive learning (DPCLs) achieve limited attack success rates because they only exploit the implicit co-occurrence between backdoor triggers and target objects, without explicitly suppressing the discriminative features of the noisy background in poisoned images. This paper identifies that the missing noise compression component—formally derived from the oracle attack's noisy alignment loss—is the key factor limiting DPCL efficacy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250589/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The adversary collects a small reference set of target-class images, a shadow set of noisy images, and a fixed-size trigger (50×50 for ImageNet-100, 8×8 for CIFAR-10), then crafts composite images by placing the trigger-embedded shadow image and a reference image on a 2D canvas with theoretically optimal positions and canvas dimensions. The output is a poisoned dataset (~650 images for ImageNet-100, ~2500 for CIFAR-10, i.e., 0.5% poisoning ratio) injected into the victim's pre-training data, requiring no access to the training loop, model architecture, or optimizer settings.

## Method

Noisy Alignment (NA) decomposes the oracle attack's noisy alignment loss into an alignment term (maximizing projection of poisoned features onto the reference direction) and a compression term (minimizing the orthogonal residual), showing that the compression term forces dimensional collapse in the subspace orthogonal to the target. To simulate this under data poisoning constraints, NA crafts composite images where random cropping augmentation produces positive CL pairs that simultaneously retain the trigger (from the shadow region) and match reference features, while enforcing spatial disjointness between the two views to prevent shortcut learning. The poison crafting is formulated as a 2D layout optimization problem over reference position, shadow position, trigger position, and canvas size, with Theorems 1 and 2 analytically deriving optimal parameters for each of four layout directions (left-right, right-left, up-down, down-up). At poisoning time, one of the four layouts is randomly selected per image, and the trigger is placed at the center of the shadow image region.

## Results

On ImageNet-100, Noisy Alignment (NA) achieves ASR of 84.8% (MoCo v2), 71.4% (BYOL), 97.1% (SimSiam), and 64.8% (SimCLR), substantially outperforming all data-poisoning baselines while maintaining clean accuracy within ~1% of uninfected models. On CIFAR-10, NA reaches 75.9–85.6% ASR across the four CL frameworks, surpassing the oracle BadEncoder upper bound on MoCo v2 (75.9% vs. 69.3%), SimSiam (79.9% vs. 78.9%), and SimCLR (85.6% vs. 79.5%). The paper reports ASR improvements of 1.2% to 45.9% over the best existing DPCL on ImageNet-100, with the largest gap on SimSiam (97.1% vs. 51.2% for SSLBKD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NA dominates all data-poisoning baselines on ImageNet-100: it outperforms SSLBKD by 33.9–45.9% ASR, CorruptEncoder by 29.7–71.0%, and BLTO by 19.7–65.5% across the four CL frameworks. CTRL is essentially ineffective on ImageNet-100 (≤4.7% ASR) due to its frequency-domain trigger being destroyed by CL augmentations, making it a trivial comparison. On CIFAR-10, BLTO reports ~99% ASR, but the authors note this is inflated because BLTO's generated poisons already contain strong target-class semantics (80–90% ASR without any backdoor training); after normalization (BLTO-N), NA outperforms by 62–70% ASR. NA surpasses the oracle BadEncoder upper bound on CIFAR-10 for MoCo v2, SimSiam, and SimCLR, while on ImageNet-100 it narrows the gap to within 12.5–32.9% ASR. For detection defenses, NA evades DECREE and Beatrix on ImageNet-100 (AUC ≈ 0.52, near random), while SSL-Cleanse and DeDe fail to flag it at all; on CIFAR-10, DECREE and Beatrix still detect NA (AUC 0.96, 0.91) but with reduced precision compared to their detection of BadEncoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines: data-poisoning backdoor attacks to contrastive learning (SSLBKD, CTRL, CorruptEncoder, BLTO) and training-controllable oracle attacks (BadEncoder) that define the performance upper bound. The key conceptual departure is a geometric decomposition of the oracle attack's noisy alignment loss into an alignment component (which existing DPCLs implicitly target) and a noise-compression component (which they ignore), revealing why prior methods underperform. The paper then bridges the oracle-to-poisoning gap by formulating the compression objective as a 2D image layout optimization problem solvable offline, rather than requiring real-time training access.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The noise-compression insight provides a principled explanation for why existing DPCLs plateau well below oracle performance, and the layout optimization offers a concrete, theoretically grounded recipe for closing that gap. However, the attack's dependence on random cropping makes it vulnerable to a simple adaptive defense (removing or restricting crops), and detection methods still achieve reasonable recall on CIFAR-10. Open questions include whether the compression mechanism generalizes to CL variants without random cropping (e.g., frequency-domain or semantic augmentations) and whether the layout optimization extends to non-square or variable-size triggers in realistic web-scale pretraining pipelines.
