## Problem

Traditional bundle adjustment, the core of SLAM and SfM, assumes a static scene and relies on the epipolar constraint, which is violated by moving objects in casual videos. Existing work either masks out dynamic regions (yielding incomplete reconstructions) or models object motion independently (producing inconsistent estimates). The paper asks whether bundle adjustment can be made to operate directly on all scene elements—static and dynamic—by isolating the camera-induced component of each point's observed motion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251499/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of RGB frames with known camera intrinsics and per-frame monocular depth maps (from ZoeDepth or UniDepth) that initialize 3D query points. The 3D tracker processes temporal windows of S=12 frames with N=512 query points, while BA operates over a 15-frame window. Outputs include per-frame SE(3) camera poses, sparse 3D trajectories for both static and dynamic points, and refined dense depth maps produced by a per-frame 2D scale grid at coarser resolution (H_g × W_g) that multiplicatively adjusts the initial depth estimates.

## Method

BA-Track has three stages. First, a motion-decoupled 3D tracker (two Co-Tracker-style transformers sharing a CNN feature encoder) predicts the total observed 3D trajectory and, via a shallower second network, the object-induced dynamic component; the static (camera-induced) component is computed as X_static = X_total − m·X_dyn, where m is a learned static-dynamic gate. Second, classical RGB-D bundle adjustment with Huber loss and Schur decomposition optimizes camera poses and sparse depths using only the static component, so dynamic points are treated as pseudo-static in their local reference frame and the epipolar constraint holds for all points. Third, a lightweight global refinement introduces a per-frame 2D scale grid and per-point local scale variables, optimized with a depth-consistency loss (aligning dense depth to sparse BA tracks) and a scene-rigidity loss (preserving inter-point distances among static points across frames), yielding temporally coherent, scale-consistent dense depth. The tracker is trained end-to-end on TAP-Vid-Kubric synthetic data with per-iteration 3D trajectory supervision and binary cross-entropy for visibility and dynamic labels.

## Results

On camera pose estimation (Table 1), BA-Track achieves ATE of 0.034 on MPI Sintel, 0.028 on AirDOS Shibuya, and 0.385 on Epic Fields, outperforming all learning-based VO/SLAM baselines by a wide margin (e.g., 2.6× lower ATE than LEAP-VO on Sintel). On depth estimation (Table 2), BA-Track with ZoeDepth achieves Abs Rel of 0.408 (Sintel), 0.299 (Shibuya), and 0.084 (Bonn), improving over the raw ZoeDepth prior by 12–48% while remaining competitive with test-time optimization methods like MonST3R. The method also demonstrates high memory efficiency, processing full sequences where MonST3R is limited to 90 frames on a 48 GB GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BA-Track decisively outperforms all learning-based VO/SLAM baselines on camera pose ATE: it achieves 0.034 vs. LEAP-VO's 0.089 (2.6× improvement) and DPVO's 0.115 (3.4×) on MPI Sintel, and 0.028 vs. LEAP-VO's 0.031 on Shibuya. On the challenging Epic Fields dataset with rapid egocentric motion, BA-Track (0.385 ATE) edges out DPVO (0.394) and substantially beats DytanVO (1.608) and DROID-SLAM (1.424). Among test-time optimization methods, BA-Track's ATE of 0.034 on Sintel is 3.2× better than MonST3R (0.108) and 4.1× better than CasualSAM (0.141), while also being far more memory-efficient. On depth estimation, BA-Track with ZoeDepth (Abs Rel 0.408 on Sintel, 0.084 on Bonn) improves over the raw ZoeDepth prior (0.467, 0.087) but remains slightly behind MonST3R (0.335, 0.063) and CasualSAM (0.387, 0.169) on Sintel and Bonn respectively, suggesting the scale-map refinement is effective but not yet matching dedicated test-time optimization. The ablation in Table 3 confirms that motion decoupling (Total−Dynamic) combined with dynamic-point masking yields the best VO accuracy (0.034 ATE), outperforming both a single total-motion tracker with masking (0.047) and a direct static-motion tracker (0.072).

## Relation to Prior Work

BA-Track builds on Co-Tracker's transformer architecture for 3D point tracking and classical bundle adjustment (Triggs et al.) for camera pose and depth estimation, but introduces a novel motion-decoupling front-end that separates camera-induced from object-induced motion using a dual-network design. This departs from prior dynamic SLAM/VO methods (DynaSLAM, ParticleSfM, LEAP-VO) that discard dynamic points, and from test-time optimization approaches (Robust-CVD, MonST3R, CasualSAM) that rely on monocular depth priors with additional alignment. The key conceptual departure is treating dynamic points as pseudo-static in their local reference frame, thereby making the epipolar constraint applicable to all points and enabling seamless BA without special handling of dynamic regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a lightweight scale-map refinement (few parameters, simple bilinear scaling) can effectively align dense monocular depth with sparse BA tracks, offering a practical alternative to heavier neural deformation models. Limitations include dependence on off-the-shelf depth priors (ZoeDepth/UniDepth) whose quality bounds the final reconstruction, and the scale grid's inability to capture complex non-rigid deformations. Open questions include whether more expressive deformation models (e.g., neural networks) would further improve dynamic reconstruction quality, and how the approach scales to very long sequences or scenes with extreme parallax. The work reinforces the broader trend that classical optimization augmented with learned priors can match or exceed purely end-to-end learning approaches for geometric tasks.
