## Problem

Existing compositional single-view scene reconstruction methods (e.g., DeepPriorAssembly, Gen3DSR) use monocular depth only for post-hoc pose alignment, failing to exploit its rich geometric priors during shape generation. Additionally, they rely on object reconstruction models trained on complete, unoccluded images, leading to inconsistencies when objects are partially visible in real scenes. DepR addresses both gaps by integrating depth as a first-class conditioning signal throughout training and inference while incorporating global scene context to handle occlusions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and internally derives instance segmentation masks (via Grounded-SAM), a depth map (via Depth Pro), and image features (via DINOv2). It outputs per-object 3D shapes represented as SDFs decoded from latent tri-planes (32×32 resolution, 2 channels) in canonical coordinates, along with per-object poses (3D translation, 1D vertical-axis rotation, 1D scale) and a shared global rotation, which together constitute the full scene reconstruction. The pipeline focuses on object instances (furniture) and excludes background geometry such as walls.

## Method

DepR follows a compositional pipeline: segment objects, reconstruct each via a latent tri-plane diffusion model, then optimize layout. The key architectural contribution is depth-guided conditioning: instance features are back-projected using estimated depth into a 3D voxel volume, processed by a 3D CNN, and projected onto the three tri-plane axes to form spatially aligned conditioning features for a 3D-Aware UNet denoiser. A local-global cross-attention mechanism between instance-level and scene-level DINOv2 features injects broader visual context to handle occluded regions. At inference, depth-guided DDIM sampling renders the predicted SDF into a depth map, computes a scale-invariant depth loss, and uses its gradient to steer the denoising trajectory toward geometry consistent with observed depth. A two-stage sampling strategy first generates an initial shape without guidance, estimates pose via Chamfer-distance-based layout optimization, then performs a second guided pass in camera space for refinement.

## Results

On the 3D-FRONT test set, DepR achieves a scene-level Chamfer distance of 153.2×10⁻³ and F-score of 25.00%, outperforming all baselines. At the object level, DepR reaches a CD of 2.57×10⁻³ and F-score of 89.66%, substantially surpassing the next-best method Gen3DSR (CD 9.74, F-score 31.42%). Inference time is 1.2 minutes per scene on a single A100 GPU, roughly 13× faster than Gen3DSR (15.5 min) and 5.6× faster than DeepPriorAssembly (6.7 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DepR outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest generative compositional baseline DeepPriorAssembly [67], DepR reduces scene-level CD by 20% (153.2 vs. 191.8) and improves scene F-score by 8.3 points (25.00 vs. 16.72), while cutting inference time from 6.7 min to 1.2 min. Against Gen3DSR [9], the object-level gains are especially dramatic: CD drops from 9.74 to 2.57 (×3.8) and F-score jumps from 31.42% to 89.66% (+58.2 points), reflecting the benefit of depth-guided conditioning and local-global attention for handling occlusions. Feed-forward holistic methods (InstPIFu, Uni-3D, BUOL) are far behind on object-level metrics (F-scores 12.6–13.7% vs. 89.66%) and also lose on scene-level CD and F-score, though they run in seconds rather than minutes. The paper also shows qualitative results on real-world images from Pix3D and self-collected samples, where DepR generalizes well despite being trained only on synthetic 3D-FRONT data.

## Relation to Prior Work

DepR builds on the latent tri-plane representation and 3D-Aware UNet denoiser from BlockFusion and Frankenstein, and adopts the shape-registration formulation for scene composition from DeepPriorAssembly. Its key departure from prior compositional methods (DeepPriorAssembly, Gen3DSR) is the integration of depth as a conditioning signal throughout training and inference, rather than using it solely for post-hoc pose alignment. The local-global attention mechanism and depth-guided DDIM sampling are novel additions that address occlusion and geometric consistency, respectively, moving beyond the feed-forward back-projection paradigm of holistic methods like Uni-3D and InstPIFu.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DepR demonstrates that depth priors, when properly integrated into a generative reconstruction pipeline, can close a large gap between synthetic-trained and real-world performance in single-view scene reconstruction. However, the ablation in Table 4 reveals a significant dependency on upstream quality: ground-truth layout alone lifts scene-level F-score from ~25 to ~67, indicating that error propagation from segmentation and depth estimation remains the dominant bottleneck. Open questions include whether the pipeline can be made end-to-end differentiable to mitigate upstream errors, and whether the depth-guided sampling strategy generalizes beyond indoor scenes to outdoor or unstructured environments.
