## Problem

Unified image fusion methods treat all fusion tasks identically, ignoring task-specific characteristics that limit performance, while general image fusion methods require explicit task identification at inference, preventing generalization to unseen tasks. Additionally, joint training across heterogeneous fusion tasks introduces gradient conflicts that degrade per-task optimization. The paper addresses how to build a task-agnostic unified framework that simultaneously exploits shared complementary-information integration and adapts to task-specific properties without any task label at test time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251053/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The network accepts two aligned source images of arbitrary channel counts (e.g., 1-channel infrared + 3-channel visible, or 3-channel multi-exposure pairs) and produces a single fused image. After patch tokenization, inputs become token sequences of shape N×D processed by a Swin-transformer backbone. Crucially, at inference the model requires no task identifier or task-specific prompt; the OAF module infers appropriate fusion operations purely from the input features, enabling zero-shot application to unseen fusion scenarios such as medical image fusion and pan-sharpening.

## Method

TITA is built on the SwinFusion backbone and introduces three complementary mechanisms. First, the Interaction-enhanced Pixel Attention (IPA) module modifies Pixel Attention by removing noise injection from value projections and redesigning key projections so that the relation discriminator's irrelevance score asymmetrically amplifies cross-attention weights, explicitly encouraging complementary information extraction; all intra-domain fusion in SwinFusion is replaced with inter-domain fusion (ISF) to increase cross-attention operations. Second, the Operation-based Adaptive Fusion (OAF) module runs three parallel branches—spatially-variant high-pass filtering (HPF), residual addition (ADD), and element-wise multiplication (MUL)—whose outputs are combined with weights predicted by a lightweight hypernetwork from the input features, eliminating the need for explicit task identification. Third, the Fast Adaptive Multitask Optimization (FAMO) strategy dynamically reweights per-task gradients during joint training to mitigate gradient conflicts and ensure balanced loss reduction across all fusion tasks.

## Results

On the IVF task (LLVIP), TITA achieves the best MI (4.176), FMI (0.896), Qp (0.473), and Qw (0.877) among all compared methods, with VIF of 0.926 (second only to Text-IF's 0.932). On MEF (MEFB), TITA leads in MI (6.207), SD (10.73), EI (55.07), and VIF (1.534), while SwinFusion edges ahead on FMI (0.901 vs 0.900) and Qw (0.918 vs 0.891). On MFF (Lytro/MFFW/MFIWHU), TITA attains the highest FMI (0.885), Qabf (0.697), and SSIM (0.993), with TC-MoA slightly ahead on MI (6.695 vs 6.546) and VIF (1.655 vs 1.637). TITA also generalizes to unseen tasks (medical image fusion and pan-sharpening) where several unified baselines (FusionDN, CCF) fail to produce valid outputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TITA outperforms all six unified baselines (IFCNN, PMGI, U2Fusion, FusionDN, DeFusion, CCF) across every metric on all three tasks, with the largest margins on MI and Qw (e.g., MI 4.176 vs CCF's 2.789 on IVF; Qw 0.877 vs 0.726). Against the two general methods, TITA beats SwinFusion on MI, FMI, Qp, and Qw for IVF (4.176 vs 3.873; 0.896 vs 0.889) and on MI, SD, EI, and VIF for MEF, while SwinFusion retains a narrow lead on FMI (0.901 vs 0.900) and Qw (0.918 vs 0.891) for MEF. TC-MoA is the closest competitor, slightly exceeding TITA on MI (6.695 vs 6.546) and VIF (1.655 vs 1.637) for MFF, but TITA surpasses it on FMI, Qabf, and SSIM for that task and on all metrics for IVF and MEF. Among specialized methods, TITA matches or exceeds Text-IF on IVF for all information-theoretic and structural metrics (MI 4.176 vs 3.322; Qw 0.877 vs 0.859) but trails by a small margin on Qabf (0.679 vs 0.684) and VIF (0.926 vs 0.932), which the authors attribute to Text-IF's pretrained LLM advantage. For MEF, TITA surpasses both MEF-GAN and HoLoCo on MI (6.207 vs 4.844/4.644), EI (55.07 vs 45.96/49.85), and VIF (1.534 vs 1.371/1.463). For MFF, TITA outperforms MFF-GAN on all six metrics and is competitive with IFCNN (which was originally trained in a supervised manner for MFF), leading on FMI (0.885 vs 0.882), Qabf (0.697 vs 0.658), and SSIM (0.993 vs 0.991) while trailing slightly on MI (6.546 vs 6.495) and Qw (0.907 vs 0.912).

## Relation to Prior Work

TITA is built directly on SwinFusion's Swin Transformer backbone, modifying its intra-domain fusion blocks into inter-domain (cross-attention) blocks to strengthen multi-source interaction. The Interaction-enhanced Pixel Attention (IPA) module is a targeted refinement of the Pixel Attention mechanism from GeminiFusion, removing noise injection to values and asymmetrically amplifying cross-attention weights to prioritize complementary information. The paper positions itself in the gap between unified fusion methods (which ignore task-specific physics) and general fusion methods like TC-MoA (which require explicit task identification at inference), proposing a task-agnostic framework that adapts via learned operation weights rather than task labels. FAMO is adopted as a lightweight multi-objective optimizer to resolve gradient conflicts observed during joint training across heterogeneous fusion tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The task-agnostic design removes the inference-time dependency on task labels that plagues general fusion methods, making the framework more deployable in settings where the fusion scenario is unknown or varies. The demonstrated generalization to unseen tasks (medical image fusion, pan-sharpening) where other unified methods collapse is a practically important property. However, the OAF module's three operation branches (HPF, ADD, MUL) remain hand-designed, and the framework is limited to two-source fusion; extending to more modalities or arbitrary source counts is left as future work. The finding that task-specific adaptation itself introduces gradient conflicts that must be resolved by multi-objective optimization is a useful diagnostic for multi-task fusion training.
