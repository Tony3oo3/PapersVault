## Problem

CNN-based VSR methods are limited by local receptive fields, while Transformer-based methods suffer from quadratic attention complexity that becomes impractical for long video sequences. Although Mamba offers linear complexity with large receptive fields and data-dependent selectivity, no prior work has adapted it for video super-resolution. Additionally, existing alignment modules rely on fixed reference windows that fail under varying inter-frame motion, and pixel-domain losses cannot capture frequency-domain gaps between reconstructed and ground-truth frames.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251619/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

VSRM takes a consecutive window of 2N+1 low-resolution video frames (T×H×W×C) and produces the corresponding super-resolution frames at 4× upscaling (T×sH×sW×C). Low-resolution inputs are generated via bicubic downsampling, and the model is trained on REDS and Vimeo-90K (which cover large and small motion regimes respectively) before being evaluated on REDS4, Vimeo-90K-T, and Vid4 benchmarks using PSNR and SSIM.

## Method

VSRM's core is the Dual Aggregation Mamba Block (DAMB), which stacks N Spatial-to-Temporal Mamba blocks (bidirectional scan: spatial first, then temporal) followed by a Temporal-to-Spatial Mamba block (forward-only scan: temporal first, then spatial) to capture long-range spatio-temporal correlations with linear complexity. For inter-frame alignment, the Deformable Cross-Mamba Alignment (DCA) module estimates optical flow via pre-trained SpyNet, learns a deformable offset to adapt the reference window to local motion, and then performs implicit warping through a cross-mamba module that selectively fuses reference and target point values. A Temporal Gated Feed-forward Network (TGFN) with 3D depthwise convolutions and a chunking-based gating mechanism replaces standard FFNs to model spatial-temporal neighborhood relationships. Training combines Charbonnier loss in the spatial domain with a proposed Frequency Charbonnier-like Loss (FCL) computed on the Real and Imaginary parts of the FFT, which treats low- and high-frequency components equally and avoids the discontinuities of magnitude/phase-based frequency losses.

## Results

VSRM achieves state-of-the-art PSNR and SSIM across all three benchmarks (REDS4, Vimeo-90K-T, Vid4) at ×4 upscaling. In the 16-frame setting, VSRM reaches 33.11 dB PSNR / 0.9162 SSIM on REDS4, 38.33 dB / 0.9543 on Vimeo-90K-T, and 28.44 dB / 0.8552 on Vid4, outperforming the previous best (IART) by 0.21 dB, 0.19 dB, and 0.18 dB respectively. In the 6-frame setting on REDS4, VSRM scores 32.43 dB / 0.9059 SSIM, surpassing IART by 0.28 dB. The model uses 17.1M parameters and 217.4 GFLOPs, with a runtime of 223 ms per frame on an RTX A6000.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VSRM outperforms all compared baselines on every benchmark and both frame settings. Against the strongest Transformer-based competitor IART [54], VSRM gains 0.28 dB PSNR (6-frame, REDS4) and 0.21 dB (16-frame, REDS4), 0.19 dB (Vimeo-90K-T), and 0.18 dB (Vid4), while using only 17.1M vs. 13.4M parameters. Compared to PSRT-recurrent [46], the margins are 0.4 dB (6-frame) and 0.39 dB (16-frame) on REDS4. Against the CNN-based EDVR [52], VSRM leads by over 1 dB PSNR in the 6-frame setting and 0.72 dB in the 16-frame setting. VRT [33] is surpassed by up to 0.83 dB (6-frame) and 0.92 dB (16-frame) on REDS4, and RVRT [32] by 0.36 dB (16-frame, REDS4). In the frequency-loss ablation (Table 7), VSRM's FCL also beats FFL [23] by 0.08 dB, FSL [14] by 0.10 dB, and WHFL [27] by 0.19 dB, confirming the loss design advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

VSRM is the first framework to adapt Mamba's selective state space model to video super-resolution, departing from the dominant CNN (BasicVSR, EDVR) and window-based Transformer (VRT, PSRT, IART) backbones that have defined the field. Architecturally, it retains the BasicVSR/BasicVSR++ pipeline of feature extraction, propagation, and upsampling, but replaces the propagation backbone with Dual Aggregation Mamba Blocks (S2TMB + T2SMB) to exploit Mamba's linear complexity and global receptive field. The Deformable Cross-Mamba Alignment module extends the deformable alignment lineage (TDAN, BasicVSR++, RVRT) by replacing fixed-window implicit attention with a deformable cross-mamba mechanism, while the Frequency Charbonnier-like Loss builds on prior frequency-domain losses (FFL, FSL, WHFL) but computes loss directly on Real/Imaginary Fourier components rather than magnitude/phase or reweighted spectra.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a linear-complexity state space model can match or exceed full-attention Transformers on VSR benchmarks (31.09 vs 31.06 dB PSNR at ~6× lower FLOPs in ablation), VSRM opens a practical path toward processing longer video sequences without the quadratic cost that has constrained Transformer-based VSR. The DCA module addresses a concrete limitation of fixed-window alignment under varying motion, and FCL offers a simple drop-in loss that improves high-frequency recovery. However, the method still relies on a frozen SpyNet for flow estimation, incurs higher runtime than window-attention baselines (223 ms vs 173–180 ms), and the authors note that Mamba hardware acceleration is still maturing, so real-time deployment remains an open question. Broader applicability to other low-level video tasks (deblurring, denoising, colorization) is suggested but not yet demonstrated.
