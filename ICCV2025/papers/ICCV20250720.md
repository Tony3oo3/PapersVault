## Problem

Existing data-driven human avatar frameworks (3DGS, NeRF, mesh-based) treat hair as a static or rigidly-attached component of the head, failing to capture hair's non-rigid, momentum-dependent dynamics. The fundamental challenge is the incompatible paradigms governing body and hair: body avatars are determined by instantaneous pose, while realistic hair motion requires modeling inertial effects that cannot be inferred from the current pose alone.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view multi-scale 4K RGB videos (3500–4000 frames from 69 cameras: 51 short-focus for full-body and 18 long-focus for half-body captures), along with foreground masks, SMPL-X registrations (10 shape + 50 expression coefficients), and SAM 2 segmentation maps. It produces a real-time-renderable 3D Gaussian avatar where 10,000 hair strands (1,000 independently driven, the rest k-NN interpolated) are anchored to the scalp and deform dynamically under pose sequences, outputting rendered RGB images at both focal lengths.

## Method

HADES introduces a heterogeneous representation: the body is modeled via StyleUNet-based front/back Gaussian maps conditioned on LBS-deformed SMPL-X pose maps, while hair is represented as 3D polylines with Gaussians anchored at segment midpoints (fixed physical radius r=5×10⁻⁴). Hair deformation is predicted by an MLP that takes the canonical hair point position, deformed positions at t−1 and t−2, the point's index along the strand, and scalp poses β at t, t−1, t−2, enabling the network to learn inertial and velocity-aware dynamics from data. A temporal fusion module ensures coherence by conditioning on two preceding frames of motion state. For multi-scale data fusion, a 2D Mip filter prevents aliasing during zoom-out, and a lightweight MLP generates a per-frame 3×3 color correction matrix to align long-focus and short-focus camera tones. Training uses L1 + VGG perceptual + mask separation + hair direction losses under a fully supervised, data-driven paradigm.

## Results

On the self-reenactment task across 5 dynamic hair datasets, HADES achieves 23.79 PSNR, 0.883 SSIM, 0.162 LPIPS (long-focus), 0.156 LPIPS (short-focus), and 29.84 FID, outperforming both baselines on every metric. On the novel-view-synthesis (3D consistency) task, HADES reaches 23.58 PSNR, 0.882 SSIM, and 0.139 LPIPS, again leading all comparisons. The method also demonstrates qualitative advantages in capturing realistic hair inertia and high-frequency facial details that baselines fail to reproduce.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HADES is compared against two baselines: Animatable Gaussians (Li et al., CVPR 2024) and Gaussian Haircut (Zakharov et al., 2024). On self-reenactment, HADES surpasses Animatable Gaussians (best variant, 'all') by +1.60 dB PSNR, +0.046 SSIM, −0.012 LPIPS(l), and −3.69 FID, and outperforms Gaussian Haircut by +2.65 dB PSNR, +0.034 SSIM, −0.035 LPIPS(l), and −13.57 FID. On novel-view synthesis, HADES leads Animatable Gaussians (all) by +0.91 dB PSNR and +0.071 SSIM, and Gaussian Haircut by +1.93 dB PSNR and +0.097 SSIM. Qualitatively, Animatable Gaussians treats hair as part of the head (no independent dynamics), producing blurry hair that merely follows head motion, while Gaussian Haircut relies on a physics engine for driving and cannot re-render via splatting, leading to trajectories inconsistent with real motion. HADES's strand-based representation with inertial MLP-based deformation captures momentum-dependent hair swing that neither baseline reproduces.

## Relation to Prior Work

HADES builds on the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework (Kerbl et al., 2023) and the pose-dependent Gaussian maps approach from Animatable Gaussians (Li et al., 2024) for body modeling, but introduces a heterogeneous representation that departs from the uniform treatment of all avatar components. While prior avatar systems treat hair as a static geometric component of the head, and hair-specific methods (Gaussian Haircut, GaussianHair) achieve photorealistic reconstruction without full-body integration, HADES is the first to bind hair as optimizable 3D polylines anchored to the scalp and drive them with a velocity-aware MLP that models inertial dynamics. This bridges the gap between data-driven avatar animation and physically plausible hair motion, which prior work addressed only through rigid-body approximations or offline physics engines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing a data-driven pipeline where hair dynamics emerge from learned inertial conditioning rather than explicit simulation, HADES opens a path toward real-time, physically plausible hair in production avatar systems without the computational cost of per-strand physics. Key limitations remain: the method is purely data-driven (generalization to motions far from training sequences is untested), strand-strand collisions are not modeled, and SMPL-X fitting can fail at extreme head velocities. Open questions include whether the inertial MLP formulation scales to longer temporal dependencies, whether the k-NN interpolation strategy holds for very sparse strand counts, and how the approach extends to stylized or non-photorealistic hair rendering.
