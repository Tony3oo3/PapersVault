## Problem

Existing post-training quantization methods for diffusion models suffer severe quality degradation at extremely low bit-widths (1–2 bits), limiting compression to at most 4× from FP16. While vector quantization (VQ) offers better low-bit performance, its fixed-length shared codebook lacks the capacity to accurately represent high-dimensional weight vectors, and the iterative denoising process of diffusion models amplifies per-step reconstruction errors into significant generative quality loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251321/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained diffusion model (DiT-XL/2, LDM-4, PixArt-α, FLUX.1-dev) or LLM (LLaMA-2 7B) as input and produces a compressed model consisting of INT8 weight assignments plus a compressed codebook, reducing size by up to 13× (C2I) or 8× (T2I). The compressed model accepts the same inputs as the original (class labels at 256×256 or 512×512 for C2I, text prompts at 1024×1024 for T2I) and produces generated images or text sequences. Notably, the compressed model remains trainable for further fine-tuning, unlike standard PTQ outputs.

## Method

The paper extends product quantization (PQ) to diffusion models by decomposing each weight vector into N sub-vectors, each quantized independently against its own codebook of k centroids, yielding an effective k^N codeword capacity at O(kN) storage cost. To keep the codebook memory-efficient, a codebook compression step merges similar centroids (below an L2 threshold τ_c) ordered by usage frequency, capping the codebook at 1/4 of the assignment storage. Calibration follows an EM procedure: the E-step reassigns each sub-vector to the centroid that minimizes output-feature reconstruction error (Wx − W'x)² rather than raw weight distance, and the M-step updates centroids by backpropagating the DDPM loss. A codebook re-initialization mechanism periodically replaces dead centroids (unused for τ_dead steps) via k-means++ on a reservoir of recent vectors, preventing codebook collapse and ensuring uniform utilization.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation with DiT-XL/2 (250 steps), the proposed method achieves FID 6.84 at 2-bit compression, compared to FP16 FID 5.33, while GPTQ and Q-DiT collapse to FID 280.11 and 313.63 respectively. At 1-bit, the method still achieves FID 14.03 versus VQ's 91.71. For text-to-image generation, the method achieves GenEval overall scores of 0.46 (PixArt-α) and 0.57 (FLUX.1-dev) at 2-bit, versus 0.48 and 0.66 for FP16, while SVDQuant drops to 0.17 and 0.32. The method achieves up to 13× compression for C2I and 8× for T2I models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all PTQ baselines at 2-bit: GPTQ (FID 280.11) and Q-DiT (FID 313.63) produce nearly meaningless images on DiT-XL/2, while the proposed method achieves FID 6.84, close to the FP16 baseline of 5.33. For U-Net-based LDM-4, Q-Diffusion (FID 43.96) and PTQD (FID 39.37) also degrade severely, whereas the proposed method reaches FID 7.98 versus FP16's 11.69. Against VQ4DiT (FID 11.87), the proposed method improves FID by 5.03 points at 2-bit. On text-to-image generation, SVDQuant collapses to GenEval overall 0.17 (PixArt-α) and 0.32 (FLUX.1-dev), while the proposed method maintains 0.46 and 0.57 respectively—nearly lossless relative to FP16 (0.48 and 0.66). For LLM compression, the method outperforms Quip# on all three benchmarks (Winograd 64.87 vs 62.43, PiQA 73.96 vs 71.38, HellaSwag 48.12 vs 42.94). For 3DGS, the method slightly outperforms Compact3D on PSNR (23.57 vs 23.39) but is marginally worse on SSIM (0.838 vs 0.836) and LPIPS (0.192 vs 0.200).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes product quantization (PQ), originally developed for approximate nearest-neighbor search in information retrieval, as a model compression technique for diffusion models. It departs from the vector quantization (VQ) line of work (VQ4DiT, Quip#, Compact3D) by identifying that VQ's fixed-length shared codebook lacks the capacity to represent high-dimensional weight vectors at extreme bit-widths, a problem amplified by diffusion's iterative error accumulation. The key methodological departure is extending PQ's multi-codebook decomposition to diffusion models, combined with a novel codebook compression scheme and an EM-style calibration that jointly optimizes assignments (via output reconstruction) and centroids (via DDPM loss with re-initialization).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 13× compression (1-bit) of DiT-XL/2 with FID 14.03 (vs. 5.41 FP) and 8× compression of 12B-parameter FLUX.1-dev with near-lossless GenEval scores opens the door to deploying and fine-tuning state-of-the-art generative models on memory-constrained hardware, a barrier that PTQ methods (capped at ~4×) cannot overcome. The compressed model remains trainable, unlike standard PTQ, which preserves research and adaptation workflows. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that the method reduces memory footprint but does not accelerate inference, leaving the joint goal of compactness and speed open. The broad validation across C2I, T2I, LLM, and 3DGS tasks suggests the PQ-based compression framework is a general-purpose tool rather than a diffusion-specific trick.
