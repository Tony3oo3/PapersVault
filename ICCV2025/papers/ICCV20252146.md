## Problem

Existing diffusion-based super-resolution methods are constrained to fixed scaling factors and rely on simple upsampling (e.g., bicubic interpolation) as conditioning priors, which limits their expressive power and prevents recovery of high-frequency details. This paper addresses both limitations by introducing a neural operator that learns continuous, scale-adaptive mappings in function space, providing richer priors to guide the diffusion denoising process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252146/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB remote sensing image and a continuous scaling factor s (sampled uniformly from U(1,8] during training, evaluated up to ×10 out-of-distribution) and produces a high-resolution RGB remote sensing image at a fixed target resolution (e.g., 256×256). Unlike fixed-scale SR methods, the same model handles arbitrary magnification factors without retraining, with the scaling factor directly modulating the neural operator's output resolution.

## Method

NeurOp-Diff integrates a Super-Resolution Neural Operator (SRNO) as the conditional prior for an SR3-style denoising diffusion model. The neural operator encodes the LR image through an EDSR-based encoder, lifting functions, and a multilayer kernel integral using Galerkin-type attention, producing a continuous latent representation that captures high-frequency information at arbitrary scales. This representation replaces the conventional bicubic-upsampled LR image as the conditioning signal fed into a U-Net denoiser during the reverse diffusion process (T=2000 forward steps, 50 inference steps). Training proceeds in two stages: the neural operator is pre-trained for 500 epochs, then frozen while the full model is trained for ~1M iterations with L1 loss and a uniform scaling factor sampled per iteration, enabling the model to generalize across the continuous range of magnification factors.

## Results

On the generative SR benchmark (Table 1), NeurOp-Diff achieves the best PSNR and SSIM across all three datasets at both 4× and 8× scales, e.g., 28.14 dB / 0.7761 SSIM on UCMerced 4× (vs. 27.15 / 0.7587 for SR3) and 24.51 dB / 0.6502 SSIM on UCMerced 8× (vs. 23.12 / 0.5846 for SR3). For continuous SR on UCMerced (Table 3), NeurOp-Diff attains the best SSIM and LPIPS at every tested magnification from 2× to 10×, with the advantage widening at higher factors (e.g., 0.650 SSIM / 0.195 LPIPS at 8× vs. 0.623 / 0.215 for SRNO). Against regression-based continuous methods (Table 4), NeurOp-Diff trades a small PSNR deficit (28.14 vs. 29.21 for SRNO) for substantially better structural and perceptual quality (0.78 SSIM / 0.136 LPIPS vs. 0.72 / 0.164).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three generative diffusion baselines (SR3, IDM, TCDM), NeurOp-Diff consistently wins on both PSNR and SSIM across all three datasets at both 4× and 8×, with the largest margin over SR3 (up to +1.02 dB PSNR and +0.066 SSIM on UCMerced 8×). Against SRNO (the regression-based continuous SR baseline), the picture is mixed: SRNO retains a slight PSNR edge at low magnifications (34.32 vs. 33.93 at 2×), but NeurOp-Diff surpasses it on SSIM at every factor (e.g., 0.824 vs. 0.786 at 2×; 0.650 vs. 0.623 at 8×) and on LPIPS (0.098 vs. 0.118 at 2×; 0.195 vs. 0.215 at 8×), with the gap widening at out-of-distribution 9×–10× factors. Against IDM (implicit diffusion for continuous SR), NeurOp-Diff wins on SSIM and LPIPS at all magnifications while trailing slightly on PSNR at 2× (33.93 vs. 33.54) but pulling ahead at 8× (24.51 vs. 23.68). Against LIIF (implicit image function), NeurOp-Diff sacrifices ~0.8 dB PSNR (28.14 vs. 28.96) but gains +0.09 SSIM and −0.046 LPIPS, indicating markedly better perceptual and structural fidelity. Overall, NeurOp-Diff is the only method that simultaneously leads in SSIM and LPIPS across all continuous-SR magnifications tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeurOp-Diff sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: the conditional denoising diffusion framework for super-resolution (SR3, which provides the forward/reverse diffusion process and U-Net denoiser) and the super-resolution neural operator (SRNO, which provides the Galerkin-type attention mechanism for resolution-agnostic mapping). The key architectural departure is replacing the standard bicubic-upsampled LR prior used in SR3 with the neural operator's latent continuous representation, thereby inheriting SRNO's ability to handle arbitrary scaling factors while retaining the diffusion model's capacity to synthesize high-frequency detail. This combination is presented as the first integration of neural operators with diffusion models for remote sensing SR, addressing the fixed-scale limitation of prior diffusion-based SR methods and the over-smoothing of prior continuous SR methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a neural operator's function-space prior can meaningfully improve diffusion-based SR quality (notably SSIM and LPIPS) while enabling continuous, scale-adaptive reconstruction in a single model, which is practically valuable for remote sensing workflows where required magnification varies by application. However, the PSNR gains over strong baselines (TCDM, SRNO) are modest (≤0.3 dB), and evaluation is limited to three small-scale remote sensing datasets (UCMerced, AID, RSSCN7) with 256×256 outputs, leaving open questions about scalability to larger scenes, more diverse degradation models, and non-remote-sensing domains. The two-stage training (pre-train NO, then freeze and train diffusion) also raises questions about whether end-to-end joint training could yield further gains.
