## Problem

Text-to-image systems trained on web-scraped data are heavily Amero- and Euro-centric, producing hallucinated or culturally inaccurate images for artifacts in the long tail of global cultures. Existing proxy scorers (perceptual similarity to ground truth, CLIP-based image-text alignment, Vendi diversity) empirically fail to correlate with human judgments of cultural representativeness, and no scalable benchmark exists to measure these cultural gaps across T2I systems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250879/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The CuRe framework takes a cultural artifact described by a hierarchical attribute set (name n, category c, supercategory s, region r) and generates images via a T2I system using prompts that specify different subsets of these attributes (e.g., just n, then {n,r}, then {n,c,r}). The scorer suite then produces three types of scores—perceptual similarity, image-text alignment, and diversity—by measuring how the T2I system's output changes as attribute informativeness increases, rather than comparing to a fixed reference. The benchmark dataset contains 300 artifacts across 6 cultural axes, 32 categories, and 64 countries, sourced from the Wikimedia knowledge graph.

## Method

The core contribution is Marginal Information Attribution (MIA) scoring: instead of comparing a generated image I(n) to ground-truth images or a single text prompt, CuRe measures the divergence in T2I system behavior as cultural attributes are incrementally added to the prompt (n → {n,c} → {n,c,r}). For perceptual similarity, φ_PS computes cosine distance between I(n) and I(c) (generated category-level images) rather than I(n) and ground truth, and the divergence Δφ_PS captures how much adding category/region information shifts the output. For image-text alignment, φ_ITA averages sim(I(n),P(n)) with sim(I(n),P(a)) for varying attribute subsets, capturing both visual correctness and cultural linkage. For diversity, φ_DIV computes LPIPS over the set {I(n), I({n,c}), I({n,r}), I({n,c,r})}, where low divergence indicates the artifact is well-learned. This approach requires no ground-truth images, avoids generative entanglement with T2I pretraining data, and correlates more strongly with human judgments than baselines across FLUX.1, Stable Diffusion 3.5 Large, and SD 1.5.

## Results

On the CuRe benchmark (300 cultural artifacts across 64 countries), the proposed φ_ITA({c,r}) scorer achieves Spearman ρ = 0.27/0.38/0.42 against human judgments of cultural representativeness, ground-truth likelihood, and perceptual similarity on FLUX.1 [dev], outperforming all baselines including Gemini 2.0 Flash (0.23/0.41/–) and PickScore (0.20/0.29/0.34). For diversity scoring, φ_DIV achieves ρ = −0.20/−0.23/−0.30 on FLUX.1 [dev], roughly doubling the correlation of LPIPS(n) (−0.11/−0.06/−0.16) and substantially outperforming Vendi Score variants (ρ ≈ 0). No quantitative scorer surpasses ρ = 0.51, indicating that dense-embedding-based metrics still fall short of fully capturing human cultural judgments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed φ_ITA({c,r}) scorer outperforms all cultural-specific baselines (Khanuja et al. [31], Ventura et al. [65], o3-mini [42]) by a wide margin on FLUX.1 [dev], achieving ρ = 0.27 vs. 0.13/0.19/0.17 for cultural representativeness, and also surpasses the simple sim(I(n), P(r)) baseline (0.20). Against human-preference reward models, φ_ITA({c,r}) wins on FLUX.1 [dev] (0.27/0.38/0.42 vs. PickScore 0.20/0.29/0.34) but is slightly outperformed by PickScore and Gemini 2.0 Flash on SD 3.5 Large for φ_GT* and φ_PS* (0.37/0.40 vs. 0.34/0.39). For diversity scoring, φ_DIV (ρ = −0.20 to −0.30) roughly doubles the correlation of LPIPS(n) (ρ = −0.02 to −0.16) and substantially outperforms Vendi Score and quality-weighted Vendi Score, which are nearly uncorrelated with human judgments (ρ ≈ 0). The perceptual similarity divergence scorer Δφ_PS matches or outperforms the ground-truth-based φ_GT on FLUX.1 [dev] and SD 3.5 Large with AIMV2 and DINOv2 encoders while requiring no ground-truth images. Overall, the MIA-based scorers consistently outperform or match all baselines on the strongest T2I systems (FLUX.1 [dev], SD 3.5 Large) but show weaker differentiation on the lower-quality SD 1.5.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of cultural bias measurement in T2I systems (Kannen et al. 2024, Khanuja et al. 2024, Ventura et al. 2023, Zhang et al. 2024) by introducing a novel scoring lens—marginal information attribution (MIA)—that evaluates how a T2I system's output changes as more cultural attributes are specified in the prompt, rather than comparing static outputs to ground-truth images or fixed text queries. The CuRe dataset departs from prior cultural benchmarks by constructing a coarse-to-fine categorical hierarchy (axis → category → artifact, region) directly from the Wikimedia knowledge graph, enabling fine-grained inter- and intra-cultural comparisons. The paper also identifies and formalizes 'generative entanglement,' a circularity problem where scorers (e.g., CLIP trained on LAION) share pretraining data with the T2I systems they evaluate, a concern not explicitly addressed in prior proxy-scorer work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CuRe provides a scalable, ground-truth-free benchmarking suite that democratizes cultural representativeness evaluation, which previously required expensive crowdsourcing or domain experts. However, the sobering finding that no quantitative scorer exceeds Spearman ρ ≈ 0.51 with human judgments underscores that automated cultural assessment remains fundamentally limited, and the MIA approach, while competitive, does not close this gap. Open questions include extending beyond geography-as-culture-proxy to religion and language, resolving ambiguous artifact names, and improving MLLM-based scorers (e.g., Gemini 2.0 Flash) that still miss culture-specific details. The generative entanglement finding has broader implications for any evaluation pipeline where scorer and system share training data.
