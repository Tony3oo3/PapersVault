## Problem

Existing domain generalized semantic segmentation (DGSS) methods built on Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) rely exclusively on RGB visual cues, which degrade severely under extreme conditions such as night, snow, fog, and rain. The underlying geometric structure of a scene, however, remains stable across domains, yet no prior DGSS framework has exploited a dedicated depth VFM to complement visual features and forge geometric consistency within the segmentation pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252123/cropped_page0_idx10.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB driving-scene image as input (resolutions range from 1280×720 for BDD100K to 2048×1024 for Cityscapes) and internally generates a depth map via a frozen Depth Anything V2—no external depth sensor or depth annotation is required. The output is a pixel-wise semantic segmentation mask over the standard 19-class driving vocabulary. Training uses only source-domain data (Cityscapes or GTA5), and evaluation is performed on unseen target domains without any target-domain access.

## Method

DepthForge is a parameter-efficient fine-tuning strategy (~3M trainable parameters) that runs two frozen VFMs in parallel: DINOv2 or EVA02 for visual features and Depth Anything V2 for depth features. At each transformer layer, depth-aware learnable tokens are updated via an attention mechanism that separately attends to visual and depth features, then fuses them with a learnable scale factor λ to produce a unified visual-spatial attention map. An Attention Optimization module then applies token-learned alignment weights and biases to the attention map, followed by residual connections, yielding enhanced per-layer features. A Depth Refinement Decoder fuses multi-scale enhanced features from all layers through MLP projections and multi-head Transformer blocks to produce the final segmentation. The key design choice is that gradients flow only through the tokens (not the frozen backbones), and each modality contributes independently, preventing one from dominating optimization and enabling robust generalization under extreme visual conditions.

## Results

On the Cityscapes→ACDC extreme-condition setting, DepthForge (DINOv2 backbone) achieves 75.4% mIoU on Snow, 60.4% on Night, 81.2% on Fog, and 75.4% on Rain, outperforming the strongest VFM baseline FADA by +1.9%, +3.0%, +1.0%, and +0.4% respectively. On the GTA5→Cityscapes+BDD100K+Mapillary setting, DepthForge reaches 69.04%, 62.82%, and 69.22% mIoU, surpassing FADA by +0.81%, +0.88%, and +1.13%. On the Cityscapes→BDD100K+Mapillary+GTA5 setting, it achieves 66.19%, 75.93%, and 67.24% mIoU, beating FADA by +1.07%, +0.07%, and +3.46%. The largest gains appear in Night and Snow scenes where visual cues are most degraded, with improvements of up to +4.8% over REIN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DepthForge consistently outperforms all 22 baselines across all three evaluation settings. Against the two strongest VFM-based competitors, it beats REIN (CVPR 2024) by 1.7–4.8% mIoU and FADA (NeurIPS 2024) by 0.07–3.46% mIoU, with the largest margins in Night (+3.0% over FADA) and Snow (+1.9% over FADA) on ACDC. Compared to Transformer-based methods (HGFormer, CMFormer), DepthForge gains 6–27% mIoU depending on the scene, and over ResNet-based methods (IBN, RobustNet, WildNet, etc.) the margin exceeds 20% in most conditions. In the GTA5→real-world setting (Table 3), DepthForge surpasses FADA by ~1% on all three targets and REIN by 2.4–2.6%. Among parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods tested in Table 7 (LoRA, AdaptFormer, VPT, PASTA, GTR-LTR, AdvStyle), DepthForge achieves the highest average mIoU (67.0% with DINOv2) while using only 2.99M trainable parameters, matching REIN's parameter budget but exceeding it by 2.7% average mIoU.

## Relation to Prior Work

DepthForge directly extends the REIN framework (Wei et al., CVPR 2024), which introduced learnable tokens for parameter-efficient fine-tuning of frozen VFMs in DGSS, by augmenting those tokens with depth cues from a frozen Depth Anything V2 model. It also builds on the broader VFM-based DGSS line (FADA, NeurIPS 2024) that treats frozen foundation model features as a backbone for domain-generalized segmentation. The key conceptual departure is the first explicit integration of a depth VFM into the DGSS pipeline, motivated by the observation that geometric structure is more stable than visual appearance across domains. The paper positions itself as the first depth-aware framework specifically designed for DGSS, carving out a niche between RGB-only VFM fine-tuning and traditional RGB-D segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in autonomous driving: RGB-only DGSS methods degrade sharply under extreme conditions (night, snow, fog, rain), and the ~4–5% mIoU gains in those scenes with only 2.99M trainable parameters (vs. 11.65M for FADA) are practically meaningful for safety-critical deployment. However, the approach relies on monocular depth estimation (Depth Anything V2) rather than true depth sensors, raising open questions about whether the learned depth features capture genuine geometric structure or merely learned correlations that may not transfer to novel sensor modalities. The evaluation is confined to driving-scene datasets, so generalizability to other domains (indoor, medical, aerial) remains untested. A key open question is whether the depth-aware token mechanism can be extended to multi-modal settings beyond RGB+monocular depth, or whether it would benefit from explicit 3D geometric supervision.
