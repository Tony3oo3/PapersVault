## Problem

Diffusion-model-based real-world SR achieves high quality but requires 50–200 sampling steps, making it impractical for real-time use. Existing single-step or few-step alternatives either start from a noise-perturbed LR image (losing structural information) or bypass iterative generative modeling entirely, and naive consistency distillation does not guarantee that the distilled output aligns with the ground-truth HR target.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250182/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-resolution RGB image with unknown real-world degradations (blur, noise, compression) and produces a corresponding high-resolution RGB image in one forward pass. The LR image is bilinearly upscaled to match the HR spatial dimensions before being fed into the model. Internally, the network operates in the latent space of SD 2.1-base (VAE-encoded), but all perceptual and fidelity losses are computed in pixel space after VAE decoding. Training uses 512×512 patches with a batch size of 16.

## Method

FlowSR reformulates SR as a rectified flow defining a straight ODE trajectory directly from LR (t=1) to HR (t=0), avoiding the information loss of noise-perturbed starting points used by ResShift or DDPM-based methods. To enable single-step inference, it distills a multi-step teacher SR flow via consistency learning, but augments the standard consistency distillation loss with an HR regularization term that explicitly forces the student's predicted origin to match the ground-truth HR image, mitigating teacher approximation errors that would otherwise drift the distillation target. A fast-slow time scheduling strategy samples adjacent timesteps from two distinct schedulers (fast: ~4 steps for large jumps; slow: ~1000 steps for fine-grained velocity estimation), introducing controlled perturbations that improve robustness. The full training pipeline first pre-trains the SR flow with a flow-matching loss (L2 + LPIPS in image space), then fine-tunes with the combined HR-regularized consistency loss, a GAN adversarial loss using the pre-trained diffusion model as a feature extractor, and a CLIP-based image quality alignment loss that pushes outputs toward positive quality captions and away from negative ones. The backbone is SD 2.1-base with LoRA (rank 32) fine-tuning of the U-Net only.

## Results

On RealSR, FlowSR (1 step) achieves the best FID (112.60), LPIPS (0.2728), DISTS (0.2013), MUSIQ (69.22), MANIQA (0.6486), and CLIPIQA (0.6701) among all compared methods, while on DRealSR it attains the best FID (130.30), DISTS (0.2115), MUSIQ (65.46), MANIQA (0.6172), and CLIPIQA (0.7074). FlowSR matches or exceeds multi-step methods (50–200 steps) on no-reference quality metrics while reducing inference to a single step. On PSNR, FlowSR is competitive but slightly trails SinSR on RealSR (25.54 vs. 26.28) and DoSSR on DRealSR (28.50 vs. 28.55), indicating a minor fidelity trade-off for substantially better perceptual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest one-step competitor OSEDiff, FlowSR wins on FID (112.60 vs. 123.49 on RealSR; 130.30 vs. 135.30 on DRealSR), MUSIQ (69.22 vs. 69.09; 65.46 vs. 64.65), MANIQA (0.6486 vs. 0.6326; 0.6172 vs. 0.5899), and CLIPIQA (0.6701 vs. 0.6693; 0.7074 vs. 0.6963), while OSEDiff edges out FlowSR on LPIPS on DRealSR (0.2968 vs. 0.2975). Compared to SinSR, FlowSR trades a small PSNR gap (25.54 vs. 26.28 on RealSR) for substantially better perceptual quality (MUSIQ 69.22 vs. 60.80, CLIPIQA 0.6701 vs. 0.6122) and FID (112.60 vs. 135.93). Against DoSSR, FlowSR achieves comparable PSNR on DRealSR (28.50 vs. 28.55) but dominates on all no-reference metrics (MUSIQ 65.46 vs. 56.72, CLIPIQA 0.7074 vs. 0.5739, FID 130.30 vs. 166.19). Versus multi-step methods (SeeSR, PASD, StableSR, DiffBIR), FlowSR matches or exceeds their no-reference scores (e.g., MUSIQ 69.22 vs. SeeSR's 69.77 on RealSR) while reducing inference from 50–200 steps to 1. Against ResShift (15 steps), FlowSR achieves better FID (112.60 vs. 141.71) and dramatically better no-reference quality (MUSIQ 69.22 vs. 58.43) in a single step.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowSR sits at the intersection of rectified flow [21] and consistency models [38], applying both to the SR domain for the first time in a unified framework. It departs from the dominant DDPM-based SR paradigm (StableSR, ResShift, DoSSR) by replacing curved diffusion trajectories with a straight LR-to-HR flow, and from prior one-step SR methods (SinSR, OSEDiff) by explicitly modeling the generative trajectory rather than learning a direct mapping. The HR-regularized consistency loss and fast-slow scheduling are novel modifications to the standard consistency distillation recipe, addressing the specific challenge that SR demands both fidelity to ground truth and high perceptual quality simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Single-step SR is a critical efficiency bottleneck for deploying diffusion-based restoration in real-world pipelines, and FlowSR demonstrates that a straight-flow formulation combined with consistency distillation can match or exceed 50-step methods (SeeSR, PASD) in a single forward pass. The HR regularization insight—that vanilla consistency distillation can drift from the true HR manifold due to teacher approximation errors—is a practical concern that likely affects other distillation-based SR approaches. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to higher magnification factors (beyond 4×), whether the image-space loss requirement (vs. latent-space) is fundamental or an artifact of the SD 2.1-base backbone, and whether the fast-slow scheduling strategy transfers to other flow-based restoration tasks.
