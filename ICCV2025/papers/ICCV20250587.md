## Problem

Existing overlapping text segmentation methods are confined to document-specific scenarios (financial forms, legal documents, historical archives) with homogeneous backgrounds and similar overlap patterns, limiting generalization to diverse real-world settings such as artistic designs, street signage, and bills. The paper identifies two compounding challenges: the absence of a multi-scenario overlapping text benchmark, and the increased difficulty posed by varied fonts, colors, perspectives, and irregular occlusions across heterogeneous scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250587/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image of overlapping text as user input; a pre-trained monocular depth estimation network (Depth Anything) is applied internally to produce a corresponding depth map, so no explicit depth capture is required. The output is a per-pixel semantic segmentation mask over three classes—Occlusion (foreground text), Occluded (background text), and Overlap (intersection region)—effectively separating intertwined text instances into ordered foreground-to-background layers. The MOT dataset additionally provides character-level bounding boxes and text-level labels for downstream recognition.

## Method

Built on Mask2Former, the method introduces a depth-guided decoder that fuses RGB and depth modalities. A shared ResNet-like encoder (without weight sharing) extracts multi-scale features from both the image and its estimated depth map; image features pass through a standard pixel decoder while depth features go through a lightweight FPN-like convolutional block. The core Depth-guided Cross-Attention module uses normalized depth features as queries and concatenated multi-scale image+depth features as shared keys/values, so multi-head attention dynamically re-weights spatial regions according to depth-informed occlusion cues; residual connections and an FFN then refine the fused representations. For training, a Hierarchical Synthetic Overlapping Text (HSOT) strategy generates 280k pre-training samples via two scenario-specific pipelines—SynthTiger extension for document-style overlaps and a ControlNet-based text-to-image model for scene-style overlaps—followed by supervised fine-tuning on the real MOT dataset with a combined cross-entropy, dice, and focal loss that up-weights the rare overlap class.

## Results

On the MOT dataset, the proposed method (with HSOT pre-training) achieves 75.53% mIoU_Text, outperforming the strongest baseline WASNet (70.76%) by 4.77% and the Mask2Former backbone (70.28%) by 5.25%. The largest gain is on the overlap class (IoU_Ov), where the pre-trained model reaches 60.66% versus 53.12% for WASNet (+7.54%). On SignaTR6K, the method achieves 90.46% PT IoU, surpassing MFM (73.05%) by 17.41%. In a downstream recognition experiment, the segment-then-recognize pipeline boosts accuracy from 4.04% (SVTRv2 direct) to 78.15%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 10 baselines on the MOT dataset across every IoU metric. The closest competitor is WASNet (70.76% mIoU_Text), which the pre-trained model surpasses by 4.77%; the Mask2Former backbone (70.28%) is beaten by 5.25%. General-purpose segmentation models (U-Net 62.21%, DeepLab v3+ 67.87%, OCRNet 65.75%, SegFormer 68.99%, MaskFormer 68.38%) trail by 7–10% in mIoU_Text, indicating that task-specific text segmentation architectures are substantially stronger. On SignaTR6K, the method achieves 90.46% PT IoU versus MFM's 73.05%, a 17.41% margin that highlights the advantage of depth-guided occlusion reasoning over MFM's post-processing pixel assignment. In the downstream recognition experiment (Table 6), the segment-then-recognize pipeline (78.15% accuracy, 86.95 edit distance) dramatically outperforms one-stage recognizers SVTRv2 (4.04% acc) and MAERec (8.72% acc), confirming that explicit overlap separation is critical before recognition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of overlapping text segmentation work (RecycleNet, WGM-MOD, MFM) that was confined to document-specific scenarios (legal documents, historical archives, financial forms) into a genuinely multi-scenario setting encompassing street signs, artistic designs, bills, and house numbers. Architecturally, it builds on Mask2Former as the meta-architecture and introduces a depth-guided decoder that fuses monocular depth features (from Depth Anything) with RGB features via a cross-attention mechanism — a departure from prior purely RGB-based approaches. For data augmentation, it repurposes SynthTiger for document-style synthesis and ControlNet for scene-style synthesis in a hierarchical pipeline (HSOT), combining non-deep-learning and diffusion-based generation in a single strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MOT dataset (1,250 images, 2,620 text instances) fills a clear gap by providing the first multi-scenario overlapping text benchmark with pixel-level, character-level, and text-level annotations including occlusion-layer ordering. The depth-guided decoder offers a principled way to exploit 3D spatial priors for occlusion reasoning, and the downstream recognition experiment (78.15% accuracy vs. 4.04% for direct recognition) underscores the practical value of accurate overlapping text separation for OCR pipelines. However, the authors acknowledge a key limitation: when overlapping texts share nearly identical visual features (same font, color, size), depth cues become insufficient, leaving high-IoU 'text-in-text' cases unresolved. Open questions include whether the depth-guided approach generalizes to 3D scene understanding or video, and whether the HSOT strategy can be extended to additional scenarios beyond documents and outdoor scenes.
