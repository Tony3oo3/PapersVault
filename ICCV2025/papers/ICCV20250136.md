## Problem

State-of-the-art large-scale dataset distillation methods (RDED, SRe2L, G_VBSM) generate and store a distinct soft label for every augmented synthetic sample across all downstream training epochs, causing storage costs that dwarf the distilled images themselves (e.g., 572 MB of labels vs. 15 MB of images at 1 IPC on ImageNet-1K, and 110 GB at 200 IPC). No prior work has addressed this 'heavy label' storage bottleneck, which undermines the practical efficiency gains of dataset distillation for large-scale datasets.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed, open-source CLIP (ResNet-50) model plus a small set of synthetic images (224×224, initialized via RDED's patch-selection strategy) and class-name text descriptions. During downstream training, augmented synthetic images are passed through the projector to produce soft labels on-the-fly, so no labels are ever written to disk. The only persisted artifacts are the LoRA low-rank matrices (~3.3 MB for ImageNet-1K) and the synthetic images, replacing what would otherwise be gigabytes of pre-computed soft labels.

## Method

HeLlO replaces stored soft labels with an online image-to-label projector built on CLIP's image encoder followed by a linear transformation. The linear layer is initialized with normalized text embeddings of class descriptions (proven equivalent to zero-shot CLIP in Proposition 1), eliminating the need to store initial weights. A LoRA-like fine-tuning step decomposes weight updates into low-rank matrices (rank 8 for the image encoder, rank 64–128 for the linear head) and trains them against multi-weak-teacher soft labels using a combined MSE + cross-entropy objective, preserving pre-trained knowledge while adapting to the target distribution. An optional image-optimization step (downsample-then-upsample, following LIC) further reduces information loss on the surrogate projector. The net effect is compressing label storage from thousands of MB to ~3.3 MB (0.001%) while matching or exceeding SOTA distillation accuracy.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with IPC 10, HeLlO achieves 43.7% top-1 accuracy with ResNet-18, surpassing the prior state-of-the-art RDED (42.0%) by 1.7% while requiring only 3.3 MB of extra storage versus 44.7 MB for RDED and ~5,800 MB for SRe²L/G_VBSM. At IPC 1 on ImageNet-1K, HeLlO reaches 12.9% accuracy, more than doubling RDED's 6.6%. On ImageNet-100 at IPC 10, the gap widens to +12.9% over RDED (48.9% vs. 36.0%). Across all three datasets, HeLlO reduces label storage to approximately 0.001% of the full soft-label storage while matching or exceeding prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HeLlO is compared against three state-of-the-art large-scale dataset distillation methods: SRe²L, G_VBSM, and RDED. Against RDED (the strongest baseline), HeLlO wins decisively at low IPCs—by +12.9% on ImageNet-100 IPC 10, +6.3% on ImageNet-1K IPC 1, and +4.2% on Places365 IPC 10—while at IPC 50 on Places365 and ImageNet-1K it trails RDED by 2.8% and 4.3% respectively, suggesting the advantage diminishes as more synthetic images are available. Against G_VBSM and SRe²L, HeLlO dominates across all settings by margins of 10–40+ percentage points. Critically, HeLlO achieves these results with 3.3 MB of extra storage (low-rank matrices) versus 44.7 MB for RDED's teacher model and ~5,800 MB for SRe²L/G_VBSM's stored soft labels, a reduction of over 99.9%. Cross-architecture evaluation (Table 2) further shows HeLlO outperforming RDED by 3–12% across ShuffleNet-V2, MobileNet-V2, EfficientNet-B0, Swin-V2-Tiny, and VGG-11, with the largest gain (+11.7%) on the transformer-based Swin-V2-Tiny.

## Relation to Prior Work

HeLlO sits at the intersection of large-scale dataset distillation (RDED, SRe2L, G_VBSM) and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA). While prior large-scale distillation methods rely on storing teacher-generated soft labels for every augmented sample—creating a storage bottleneck that scales linearly with IPC and number of classes—HeLlO replaces this with an online image-to-label projector built on CLIP's frozen image encoder plus a LoRA-adapted linear head. The synthetic dataset initialization follows RDED's patch-selection strategy, and the image refinement step borrows from LIC, but the core conceptual departure is treating label generation as a learnable, compressible function rather than a fixed lookup table.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: reducing soft-label storage from ~110 GB (ImageNet-1K, 200 IPC) to ~3 MB makes large-scale dataset distillation feasible in settings where storing per-sample labels was previously prohibitive. The finding that a lightweight CLIP+LoRA projector outperforms stronger pre-trained models (ResNet-50, ViT-B-16) as label generators challenges the assumption that more capacity in the teacher always helps downstream transfer. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-image modalities, whether the dependency on an open-source foundation model (CLIP) limits applicability to domains where no suitable pre-trained encoder exists, and whether the small performance gap at high IPC (50 IPC on ImageNet-1K) can be closed without increasing projector rank.
