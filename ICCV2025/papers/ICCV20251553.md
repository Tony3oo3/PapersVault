## Problem

Layout-to-image (L2I) generation and object detection (OD) are natural inverse tasks, yet this duality has been largely overlooked in prior work. Existing approaches either use a pre-trained reward model to fine-tune the L2I generator or use synthetic images to retrain the detector, but none jointly optimize both tasks end-to-end to enable mutual enhancement. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a unified cycle-consistent framework that simultaneously improves generation controllability and detection accuracy.

## Input / Output

The L2I generator consumes a structured layout (N bounding boxes of the form [x1, y1, x2, y2] plus class labels, encoded as an N×5 tensor) together with a text prompt and Gaussian noise, producing a synthesized RGB image at 256×256 or 512×512 (COCO) or 64×64 (NuImages). The object detector takes an RGB image and outputs a predicted layout of N' bounding boxes with class labels, filtered by a score threshold of 0.5. During training, the perturbative single-step sampling strategy perturbs the original annotated image with small noise (t ≤ t_thre) rather than starting from pure Gaussian noise, making the cycle computation tractable in a single denoising step.

## Method

GDCC jointly optimizes a diffusion-based L2I generator G and an object detector D through two complementary cycle losses. The layout translation cycle loss (L_layoutTC) generates an image from the ground-truth layout via G, runs D on the synthesized image to recover a predicted layout, and penalizes the discrepancy (smooth L1 + cross-entropy) between the original and predicted layouts, thereby improving generation fidelity. The image translation cycle loss (L_imageTC) feeds the detector's predicted layout back into G and penalizes the pixel difference between the two generated images, which simplifies to the L2 distance between two U-Net noise predictions at the same timestep, thereby improving detection accuracy. A perturbative single-step sampling strategy replaces the full T-step denoising with a single denoising step on a lightly perturbed image (t ≤ t_thre), and a priority timestep re-sampling strategy re-weights the timestep distribution to increase the frequency of cycle-reward steps, accelerating convergence without sacrificing the original inference pipeline.

## Results

On COCO 2017 (256×256), GDCC fine-tuned on GeoDiffusion achieves FID 18.02 and YOLO mAP 31.4, improving over the GeoDiffusion baseline (FID 20.16, mAP 29.1) by 2.14 FID and 2.3 mAP points, and surpassing DetDiffusion (FID 19.28) despite using only bounding-box labels rather than mask annotations. On 512×512, GDCC on GeoDiffusion reaches FID 17.15 and mAP 32.6. For object detection, GDCC fine-tuning Faster R-CNN yields a 1.2% mAP gain (38.5 vs. 37.3), and with 150k extra auto-synthesized samples the detector mAP further improves to 39.2 while generator FID drops to 17.16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GDCC consistently outperforms all 14 baselines on both generation fidelity (FID, YOLO score) and detection metrics. On COCO 256², GDCC on GeoDiffusion beats the strongest prior method DetDiffusion [70] by 1.26 FID (18.02 vs. 19.28) and 1.6 mAP (31.4 vs. 29.8), despite DetDiffusion using additional mask annotations. On 512², GDCC on GeoDiffusion surpasses GLIGEN [35] by 4.66 FID (17.15 vs. 21.04) and 10.2 mAP (32.6 vs. 22.4), and improves ControlNet [79] by 1.76 FID and 1.8 mAP. For detection fine-tuning, GDCC yields +1.2% mAP over the Faster R-CNN baseline and +1.0% over plain fine-tuning; for generation trainability, GDCC on GeoDiffusion reaches 39.0 mAP vs. 38.4 for the unmodified GeoDiffusion and 36.9 for ControlNet. On NuImages, GDCC achieves FID 7.97 and mAP 33.6 (512²), outperforming all baselines including ControlNet (FID 23.26) and GLIGEN (FID 16.68). The key distinction is that GDCC is a training framework that enhances any existing L2I or OD model without altering its architecture, whereas prior works like DetDiffusion [70] and ControlNet++ [33] rely on fixed pre-trained reward or perceptual models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the perturbative single-step denoising strategy from ControlNet++ [33] and applies it within a novel cycle-consistent training loop that jointly optimizes a diffusion-based L2I generator (GeoDiffusion [6] or ControlNet [79]) and an object detector (Faster R-CNN). Prior work has used one task to improve the other in a one-directional manner—ControlNet++ uses a pre-trained reward model to fine-tune generators, while GeoDiffusion and DetDiffusion use synthetic images to augment detector training—but none have exploited the inherent inverse-task duality between L2I and OD for mutual, end-to-end enhancement. The cycle-consistent learning paradigm itself has been applied to other task pairs (VQA/VQG, captioning/grounding, tracking), but this is the first application to the L2I–OD pair, where the symmetrical input-output structure makes the cycle particularly natural.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GDCC offers a practical, architecture-preserving training recipe that can be plugged into any existing L2I generator and detector pair with only 2–3 additional epochs, making it broadly applicable without changing inference pipelines. The ability to leverage annotation-free synthetic layouts (via VisorGPT) for further gains opens a path toward reducing reliance on expensive manual annotation. Open questions include whether the cycle-consistency signal degrades when the detector and generator are both weak (cold-start), whether the approach extends to open-vocabulary or video settings, and whether the perturbative single-step approximation introduces a systematic bias that limits ultimate quality. The moderate but consistent improvements across multiple generators, detectors, and datasets suggest the framework is robust, though the gains are incremental rather than transformative.
