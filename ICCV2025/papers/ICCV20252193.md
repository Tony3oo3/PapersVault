## Problem

Concept Bottleneck Models and VLMs are typically evaluated only on training classes, making it impossible to distinguish whether they genuinely ground concept predictions in image content or merely memorize class-associated concept vectors. This paper demonstrates that CBMs and VLMs fail to generalize to even single-attribute substitutions from known concepts, revealing that their concept predictions are not reliably grounded in the visible image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252193/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For generation, TDG consumes two text prompts (a reference bird class with the target attribute, and a guidance bird that naturally exhibits the target attribute) plus shared Gaussian noise, producing a photorealistic synthetic bird image with exactly one attribute substituted and a discarded guidance image. For evaluation, the SUB benchmark feeds 38,400 synthetic images (33 CUB bird classes × 768 unique bird-attribute pairings, 50 images each) into CBMs and VLMs, which output binary attribute predictions or cosine-similarity-based attribute selections. The evaluation isolates single-attribute changes so models cannot shortcut via other visual cues, and the dataset is verified by both automated VQA filtering (InternVL-2.5-8B) and human annotation achieving 94.0% target-attribute accuracy.

## Method

Tied Diffusion Guidance (TDG) is a test-time modification to latent diffusion models that enables precise single-attribute substitution by running two parallel denoising processes from shared initial noise. At each denoising step, the model predicts noise for both the reference image (target bird + substituted attribute) and a guidance image (a bird where the target attribute is in-distribution); the predictions are compared element-wise, and where the difference falls below an η-th percentile threshold the two predictions are averaged, otherwise the reference prediction is retained. An η schedule (Eq. 4) starts at η=1 (fully tied) and decays to η=0 (fully independent) over a configurable window, so early steps enforce strong structural alignment while later steps allow each image to specialize to its prompt. This mechanism overcomes the LDM's failure at zero-shot composition—naive prompting for 'a Blue Jay with a yellow crown' simply produces a standard Blue Jay—by using the guidance image to anchor the substituted attribute while preserving the reference bird's identity. The resulting SUB dataset is further refined through VQA-based filtering (retaining only the top 10% of generated images per pairing by target-attribute confidence) and human validation, yielding 98.90% attribute-label accuracy versus 57.50% for CUB's class-level annotations.

## Results

On the SUB benchmark, all tested CBMs fail to detect the substituted target attribute S⁺ above random chance (50%): the best CBM, CEM, achieves only 45.7% S⁺ accuracy, while standard CBMs range from 5.8% to 40.8%. In stark contrast, these same models achieve 95.9–96.9% accuracy on the CUB test set (T), revealing a severe generalization gap. Among VLMs, EVA-CLIP achieves the highest S⁺ accuracy at 46.8% (vs. 9.3% random chance in the multiclass setting), while humans achieve 94.0% on S⁺ and 96.8% on S⁻, confirming the benchmark is solvable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new CBM or VLM; instead, it benchmarks existing methods on SUB to expose their failure to ground concept predictions in the image. Standard CBMs (Koh et al.) in both independent and joint training configurations score 34.3–40.8% on S⁺, below the 50% random-chance baseline, despite achieving 95.9–96.9% on CUB. The per-concept CBM (Heidemann et al.) performs worst at 0.39% S⁺ with 100% S⁻ removal, indicating it simply never predicts any attribute. CEM (Espinosa Zarlenga et al.) is the best-performing CBM at 45.7% S⁺ but still falls below chance. Among VLMs, EVA-CLIP (Sun et al.) leads at 46.8% S⁺, followed closely by CLIP ViT-L/14 (45.5%) and SigLIP 400m/16 (45.7%), all well below the 92.4% human baseline. SigLIP2 (Tschannen et al.) underperforms its predecessor SigLIP at 40.0% S⁺. A notable pattern across VLMs is a 2–3× over-selection of the original (removed) attribute S⁻ relative to random chance, suggesting hallucination of absent features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CBM evaluation literature (which has focused on information leakage, concept correlation, and robustness) by introducing a fundamentally harder test: detecting concepts in novel combinations of known attributes that never co-occurred in training. For image generation, TDG departs from training-time composition methods like CoInD by operating purely at test time, and differs from Composed GLIDE's noise-injection approach by using element-wise noise tying with a scheduled relaxation. For synthetic evaluation datasets, SUB moves beyond cartoon-style benchmarks like FunnyBirds by generating photorealistic images grounded in the real CUB distribution, enabling direct evaluation of models already trained on CUB without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all tested CBMs perform at or below random chance on detecting substituted attributes—despite 95–97% accuracy on CUB test classes—challenges the core assumption that CBM concept predictions are grounded in visual evidence rather than memorized class-level vectors. This has direct implications for high-stakes deployments (e.g., medical imaging) where CBMs are proposed as interpretable alternatives to black-box models. Open questions include whether the failure is specific to the CUB domain, whether larger or differently-architected CBMs can overcome it, and whether the TDG pipeline can be fully automated to scale beyond birds. The SUB dataset itself becomes a useful stress-test benchmark for future interpretable model development.
