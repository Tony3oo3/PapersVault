## Problem

Existing Generic Event Boundary Detection (GEBD) methods require access to all frames (past and future) to determine boundaries, which fundamentally differs from how humans perceive events in real-time using only past and present information. Moreover, prior online video understanding methods are designed for predefined action classes and cannot handle the taxonomy-free, diverse granularity of generic events. This paper introduces Online GEBD (On-GEBD) as a new task and proposes a dedicated framework to detect subtle, taxonomy-free event transitions in streaming video without future context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251592/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes streaming video frames sequentially, extracting per-frame features (D=2048) via a pre-trained ResNet-50 encoder at 24 FPS (Kinetics-GEBD) or 6 FPS (TAPOS). At each timestep t, the model receives only the L most recent frame features plus a learnable token, with strict causal masking preventing access to future frames. The output is a binary boundary decision per frame, produced by comparing the current prediction error against a dynamically computed threshold derived from a FIFO queue of past errors.

## Method

ESTimator is inspired by Event Segmentation Theory, which posits that humans detect event boundaries when actual visual input diverges from their prediction of the ongoing event. The Consistent Event Anticipator (CEA) is a 3-layer transformer decoder that takes L past frame features concatenated with a learnable token and predicts the next frame feature under causal masking; it is trained with two losses: an EST loss (binary cross-entropy on cosine-distance error, pushing errors high at boundaries and low elsewhere) and a REST loss (region-level soft supervision averaging errors over K frames to handle smooth intra-event transitions). The Online Boundary Discriminator (OBD) stores past errors in a fixed-size FIFO queue and computes a z-score of the current error against the queue's mean and standard deviation, flagging a boundary when the z-score exceeds a threshold τ=1.5, thereby adapting sensitivity to local context without requiring future frames or a fixed global threshold.

## Results

On the Kinetics-GEBD dataset, ESTimator achieves an average F1 of 0.748 under the online setting, outperforming the strongest online baseline MiniROAD-BC (0.681) by 6.7 points and the next-best Sim-On-BC (0.618) by 13.0 points. On TAPOS, ESTimator reaches 0.547 Avg F1, surpassing MiniROAD-BC (0.528) and OadTR-BC (0.416). Compared to offline GEBD methods, ESTimator achieves performance on par with TCN (0.685) and TransParser (0.474) on Kinetics-GEBD and TAPOS respectively, though it trails the strongest offline methods PC (0.817 / 0.642) and CoSeg (0.782) by 3–10 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESTimator outperforms all four online baselines on both datasets. On Kinetics-GEBD, the margin over the strongest baseline MiniROAD-BC is +6.7 Avg F1 (0.748 vs 0.681), while over Sim-On-BC, OadTR-BC, and TeSTra-BC the gaps are +13.0, +19.0, and +19.1 points respectively. On TAPOS, ESTimator leads MiniROAD-BC by +1.9 (0.547 vs 0.528) and OadTR-BC by +13.1 (0.547 vs 0.416). Against offline GEBD methods (Table 2), ESTimator in the online setting surpasses TCN (0.685) and TransParser (0.474) on Kinetics-GEBD and TAPOS, and edges out the unsupervised PA baseline on TAPOS (0.547 vs 0.543), but remains below the strongest offline supervised method PC (0.817 on Kinetics-GEBD, 0.642 on TAPOS) and the unsupervised CoSeg (0.782 on Kinetics-GEBD). The paper also shows that ESTimator maintains its advantage when switching from ResNet-50 to TSN backbones (Table 6), confirming robustness across feature extractors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends offline Generic Event Boundary Detection (GEBD) to a streaming, online setting, drawing its architectural and conceptual foundation from Event Segmentation Theory (EST) in cognitive science. It departs from prior offline GEBD methods (UBoCo, DDM-Net, CoSeg) that rely on full-sequence access by instead using a transformer decoder with causal masking to predict the next frame from past context alone, and replaces static or peak-based thresholds with a FIFO-queue statistical test. The online video understanding literature (TeSTra, OadTR, MiniROAD, Sim-On) provides baselines but is shown to be insufficient for taxonomy-free boundary detection, motivating the dedicated CEA+OBD design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

On-GEBD is practically relevant for real-time video processing (surveillance, live streaming, AR/VR) where future frames are unavailable, and the EST-inspired framework offers a cognitively grounded, interpretable mechanism for boundary detection that avoids hand-tuned thresholds. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very long videos or multi-modal inputs, whether the empirically fixed OBD threshold (τ=1.5) can be learned, and how performance degrades under domain shift. The finding that retaining boundary-error outliers in the OBD queue is essential (rather than harmful) suggests the dynamic threshold mechanism is more robust than a naive 'clean statistics' approach would predict.
