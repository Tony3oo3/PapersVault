## Problem

Open-vocabulary 3D object detection requires aligning 3D LiDAR features with 2D image embeddings from vision-language models, but existing pipelines suffer from two noise sources that degrade this alignment: inaccurate 3D bounding boxes produced by naive projection of 2D proposals onto sparse point clouds, and corrupted 2D features arising from partial occlusion or insufficient pixel resolution at long range. Prior methods either use inefficient exhaustive greedy search for box fitting or apply only multi-view IoU filtering, which fails to catch occlusion- and resolution-induced semantic inconsistencies.

## Input / Output

At inference, OV-SCAN consumes a single LiDAR point cloud frame and produces 3D bounding boxes (center, dimensions, yaw) with fine-grained class labels (e.g., 'SUV' rather than 'car') via prompt-based classification against CLIP text embeddings. During the offline annotation phase, it additionally requires K multi-view camera images per frame, from which Grounding DINO generates 2D proposals and SAM produces instance masks; CLIP ViT-H/14 (frozen) extracts 1024-dim 2D alignment embeddings from the cropped regions. The 3D object embeddings from TransFusion-L are upsampled to match CLIP's 8H-dimensional space for alignment, and no human-annotated 3D labels are used for training.

## Method

OV-SCAN builds on TransFusion-L and introduces two key components. The SC-NOD module first reformulates 3D box fitting as a continuous nonlinear optimization problem solved via particle swarm optimization, with a composite cost combining point density, L-shape edge alignment, ego-relative surface bias, and 2D projection IoU; it then applies selective alignment filtering using class-specific occlusion thresholds (fraction of object pixels in the crop) and a minimum-resolution threshold to exclude semantically inconsistent pairs from the alignment loss while retaining all boxes for regression loss. The H2SA head performs hierarchical two-stage alignment: TransFusion-L first predicts a high-level novel class, whose text prompt then guides a multi-scale (H, 2H, 4H) mapping of 3D object embeddings toward the target 2D CLIP embedding, enabling coarse-to-fine cross-modal alignment without compromising real-time inference.

## Results

On nuScenes, OV-SCAN achieves 31.1 mAP and 32.8 NDS, surpassing the previous best method OpenSight by 8.2 mAP and 9.5 NDS without using any human-annotated 3D labels. The method exceeds 60 AP on both car (61.6) and pedestrian (60.1) categories. On KITTI, OV-SCAN reaches 34.17 mAP₃D@50 (medium difficulty), outperforming ImOV3D (31.01) and OV-3DET (25.52). Adding camera input (OV-SCAN-Fusion) further improves nuScenes performance to 33.8 mAP and 34.4 NDS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OV-SCAN outperforms all six compared OV-3D baselines on both datasets. On nuScenes, it leads OpenSight [49] by 8.2 mAP (31.1 vs 22.9) and 9.5 NDS (32.8 vs 23.3), and Find n' Propagate [10] by 14.4 mAP and 10.4 NDS. The gap widens against earlier methods: UP-VL [30] trails by 19.8 mAP, CoDA [4] by 20.8 mAP, and OV-3DET [25] by 25.4 mAP. On KITTI, OV-SCAN achieves 34.17 mAP₃D@50, beating ImOV3D [42] by 3.16 points and OV-3DET [25] by 8.65 points. Notably, OV-SCAN surpasses OpenSight on 8 of 10 individual nuScenes classes, with the largest gains on truck (+18.7 AP), bus (+16.9 AP), and traffic cone (+14.0 AP), while OpenSight retains an edge on barrier (42.2 vs 18.8) and construction vehicle (8.7 vs 6.8). The supervised upper bound (ground-truth boxes) still leads by 18.7 mAP, indicating remaining headroom in the pseudo-labeling pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

OV-SCAN builds directly on the offline novel-object-discovery pipeline established by Find n' Propagate and OpenSight, where 2D proposals from Grounding DINO are projected into 3D to generate pseudo-labels for a LiDAR detector. It departs from this lineage in two key ways: (1) it reformulates 3D box fitting as a continuous nonlinear optimization (via particle swarm) with an extended cost function that includes surface alignment, replacing the exhaustive greedy search of prior work; and (2) it introduces selective cross-modal alignment, filtering out occlusion- and resolution-corrupted pairs before they contribute to the alignment loss—a noise source that prior methods largely overlooked. The H2SA head further extends the alignment mechanism by adding a hierarchical coarse-to-fine text-guided stage on top of the TransFusion-L backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central practical insight—that roughly 46% of auto-generated 3D annotations are too noisy for reliable cross-modal alignment (39% occluded, 7% low-resolution)—suggests that the OV-3D community has been training alignment heads on substantial amounts of corrupted supervision, and that filtering is as important as architectural design. This selective-alignment principle is broadly applicable to any pipeline that pairs 3D geometry with 2D VLM features. Key limitations remain: the method still depends on 2D proposals (objects visible only in LiDAR go unlabeled), class-based anchors struggle with shape-variable objects like trailers and construction vehicles, and far-range performance degrades significantly. Open questions include anchor-free box parameterization and discovery strategies that do not require multi-view image visibility.
