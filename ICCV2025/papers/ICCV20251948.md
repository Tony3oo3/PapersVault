## Problem

Existing text-to-point-cloud localization frameworks assume each query text corresponds precisely to the center of one submap, a one-to-one constraint that fails in real-world settings where query positions can fall anywhere within overlapping submaps. This partially matching scenario introduces cross-modal ambiguity that degrades both coarse retrieval (contrastive learning is undermined by intermediate-similarity samples) and fine-grained regression (treating partial matches as full matches causes overfitting).

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language query text describing spatial relationships to scene instances and a city-scale 3D point cloud map partitioned into 30 m × 30 m semantic submaps. Coarse stage outputs top-k retrieved submaps; fine stage outputs a 2D position within the submap parameterized as a 2D Gaussian (mean μ and 2×2 covariance σ). Partially matching submaps (containing only 2–4 of the referenced instances) are injected as training augmentation only; validation and test evaluation uses exclusively fully matching samples for fair comparison.

## Method

The core contribution is an uncertainty modeling and propagation strategy that handles partially matching submaps across both stages of a coarse-to-fine pipeline. In fine-grained localization, the predicted position is modeled as a 2D Gaussian whose covariance σ is learned via an MLP (decomposed through a lower-triangular Cholesky factor with ELU-activated diagonals); training uses a Gaussian negative log-likelihood loss so that high-uncertainty partially matching samples automatically receive attenuated gradients. In coarse place recognition, the uncertainty σ estimated in the fine stage is propagated into an IOU-based assignment loss through an uncertainty-aware similarity metric S(C,σ) = 1 − (1−C)·φ(σ), where φ(σ) is a decaying exponential; this modulates the gradient so that challenging partially matching samples contribute less to contrastive updates while fully matching samples retain full supervision. The net effect is that partially matching submaps act as informative regularizers during training without corrupting the embedding geometry or regression targets.

## Results

On KITTI360Pose, the method achieves top-1 fine-grained localization recall of 0.42 (val) and 0.39 (test) at ε<5m, outperforming the previous SOTA Text2Loc (0.37/0.33) by 14% and 18% respectively. For coarse place recognition, top-1 submap retrieval recall reaches 0.37 (val) and 0.34 (test), surpassing Text2Loc (0.32/0.28) by 16% and 21%. On the CityRefer dataset, the method maintains a 16% and 26% top-1 recall advantage over Text2Loc on validation and test sets, demonstrating robustness to linguistic variability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all five baselines across both coarse and fine stages on KITTI360Pose. Against the strongest baseline Text2Loc (CVPR'24), it gains 14–18% in top-1 fine-grained recall and 16–21% in top-1 retrieval recall, while also reducing normalized Euclidean distance error by 7–10%. IFRP-T2P (ACM MM'24) trails by a wider margin (19–23% in top-1 fine recall), partly because it forgoes ground-truth semantic submaps. RET (AAAI'23) and Text2Pose (CVPR'22) are substantially weaker, with top-1 fine recall below 0.20. On CityRefer, where linguistic variability is higher, the gap over Text2Loc widens to 16–26% in top-1 recall, confirming that the uncertainty-aware framework generalizes beyond the fixed-template queries of KITTI360Pose. The retrieval-only method GOTLoc (arXiv 2025) is outperformed on coarse place recognition by 7–14% in top-1/3/5 recall, validating the benefit of integrating uncertainty propagation into the full coarse-to-fine pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-to-point-cloud localization line initiated by Text2Pose and advanced by RET, Text2Loc, and IFRP-T2P, all of which assume a strict one-to-one correspondence between a query text and the center of a single submap. The key departure is relaxing this assumption to allow partially matching submaps, where only a subset of referenced instances overlap with the query. Methodologically, it combines probabilistic regression (2D Gaussian with log-likelihood loss, building on UGGLI-style uncertainty estimation) with a novel uncertainty-aware similarity metric that propagates fine-grained uncertainty scores into the coarse contrastive retrieval stage, a cross-stage coupling not present in prior coarse-to-fine text-to-point-cloud pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making the one-to-one submap assumption explicit and showing it is violated in practice, the paper identifies a practical gap that limits real-world deployment of text-based city-scale localization. The uncertainty propagation mechanism—using fine-grained regression uncertainty to modulate coarse retrieval gradients—is a reusable pattern that could transfer to other cross-modal retrieval tasks with ambiguous correspondences. Limitations include evaluation on only two datasets with specific submap geometries (30 m squares, 15 m reference radius), and the partially matching augmentation strategy is tightly coupled to the KITTI360Pose submap overlap structure, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to irregular or non-overlapping map partitions. The authors note future work on more efficient similarity metrics with modeled uncertainty.
