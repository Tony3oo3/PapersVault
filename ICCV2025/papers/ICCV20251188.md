## Problem

Facial expression recognition datasets remain orders of magnitude smaller than general image datasets (e.g., RAF-DB has ~30K images vs. ImageNet's 1.4M), and the largest available dataset (AffectNet) suffers from low-quality images and noisy labels. No existing method can simultaneously achieve fine-grained control over facial expression generation and produce reliable categorical FER labels for synthetic images, leaving a gap between diffusion-based image synthesis and the abstract, subjective semantics of emotional expressions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251188/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a coarse human portrait description (enriched by ChatGPT with appearance details, muscle movements, and contextual cues), discrete facial action unit labels mapped to high-dimensional embeddings via an MLP, and a random face image from FEText for layout initialization. It produces high-fidelity synthetic facial expression images paired with validated FER labels, where the final label is determined by ensemble voting between the diffusion-based FERAnno calibrator and existing FER classifiers, replacing the predefined label when discrepancies arise.

## Method

SynFER fine-tunes Stable Diffusion on FEText (400K image-text pairs curated from FFHQ, CelebA-HQ, AffectNet, and SFEW with ShareGPT-4V captions), then adds two control mechanisms: (1) an AU adapter using decoupled cross-attention (inspired by IP-Adapter) to inject FAU embeddings as explicit muscle-level control signals, and (2) a semantic guidance technique that, during later denoising steps, computes a classification loss on the one-step image prediction via an external FER classifier and updates the text embedding via gradient to steer generation toward the target expression class. For label reliability, FERAnno inverts each generated image back to a partially denoised state (t=1), extracts multi-scale U-Net features and cross-attention maps, and fuses them through a dual-branch encoder with bidirectional cross-attention to predict the FER class, providing a diffusion-native pseudo-label that is cross-validated against existing FER models.

## Results

SynFER achieves a FID of 16.32 and FER accuracy of 55.14% on generated images, substantially outperforming FineFace (FID 74.61, FER Acc. 38.05%) and raw Stable Diffusion (FID 88.40, FER Acc. 20.06%). In self-supervised learning, MoCo v3 trained solely on 1M synthetic images reaches 55.56% on AffectNet (+4.53% over real AffectNet data alone), and combining real + synthetic data pushes it to 57.84% (+6.81%). In supervised learning, POSTER++ improves from 67.49% to 69.04% on AffectNet (+1.55%) with synthetic data augmentation, and training exclusively on synthetic data at AffectNet scale yields 67.23%, rising to 69.84% at 5× scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In generation quality (Table 1), SynFER dominates all baselines: it reduces FID from 88.40 (Stable Diffusion), 145.23 (PixelArt), 81.76 (Playground v2.5), and 74.61 (FineFace) to 16.32, while raising FER accuracy from 20.06–38.05% to 55.14% and FAU accuracy to 93.31%. In self-supervised learning (Table 2), SynFER-trained MoCo v3 (81.68% RAF-DB, 57.84% AffectNet) surpasses SOTA SSL methods pre-trained on much larger face datasets—MCF on 20M Laion-Face (65.22% RAF-DB), FRA on 3.3M VGGFace2 (73.89%), and PCL on 1.8M VoxCeleb (74.47%)—by 6–16 points, demonstrating that expression-specific synthetic data outperforms generic face data at scale. In supervised learning (Table 3), adding synthetic data consistently improves every SOTA FER model: POSTER++ gains +1.55% on AffectNet (67.49→69.04) and +0.36% on RAF-DB, APViT gains +0.32% on AffectNet, and Ada-DF gains +1.48% on AffectNet. In few-shot learning (Table 4), synthetic data augmentation improves all four baselines (InfoPatch, LR+DC, STARTUP, CDNet) by 0.5–1.7 points across CFEE_C, EmotionNet_C, and RAF_C in both 1-shot and 5-shot settings. The label calibrator FERAnno itself achieves 92.56% on RAF-DB and 70.38% on AffectNet, exceeding the second-best supervised model (POSTER++ at 91.59% and 67.49%) by +0.97% and +2.89% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Stable Diffusion as its generative backbone, adapts the IP-Adapter decoupled cross-attention mechanism to inject Facial Action Unit embeddings as explicit control signals, and leverages the DDPM one-step prediction formula to implement a novel semantic guidance loop that steers text embeddings using an external FER classifier during denoising. It departs from prior synthetic-data-for-CV work (segmentation, depth, face recognition) by confronting the unique challenge that facial expressions encode abstract, subjective emotions rather than tangible pixel-level attributes, making reliable label generation fundamentally harder. Unlike FineFace and EmojiDiff, which prioritize perceptual quality of individual generated faces, SynFER is explicitly designed as a training-data factory, introducing FERAnno—a diffusion-feature-based label calibrator—as the first mechanism to automatically validate and correct FER annotations on synthetic images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a carefully engineered diffusion pipeline can produce FER training data that is genuinely useful across supervised, self-supervised, zero-shot, and few-shot paradigms, directly addressing the data-scarcity bottleneck that has stalled FER foundation-model development. The finding that MoCo v3 pre-trained solely on 1M synthetic images outperforms pre-training on 0.2M real AffectNet images (55.56% vs 51.03%) is encouraging, though partly confounded by the 5× scale difference. A key open question is distribution alignment: supervised gains remain modest (+1.55% for POSTER++), and the authors themselves note that Real-Fake distribution matching is needed to close the gap, suggesting synthetic data cannot yet fully replace real data in supervised settings. The FEText dataset and the semantic-guidance + FERAnno recipe are reusable components that could extend to other affective or subjective-label generation tasks.
