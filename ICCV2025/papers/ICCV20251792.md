## Problem

Data-driven 3D Sim2Real methods have stagnated due to the scarcity of synthetic-real paired datasets and the training instability and mode collapse inherent to GAN-based approaches. While physics-based simulation can model sensor noise, it cannot capture the full spatially-variant complexity of real-captured patterns. This paper revisits the data-driven path by leveraging large-scale paired depth data (LASA) and the stable training dynamics of diffusion foundation models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a synthetic CAD depth map as conditioning input and produces a realistic depth map that mimics real-captured sensor noise. Rather than generating the full real depth directly, the model generates the residual (difference) between real and synthetic depth, which is then added back to the clean CAD depth. The resulting depth maps are fused via a standard depth-to-3D pipeline to yield simulated 3D point clouds, mirroring how real 3D data is acquired from 2D depth captures.

## Method

Stable-Sim2Real is a two-stage depth diffusion framework built on finetuned Stable Diffusion V2.1 with a ControlNet-style conditioning encoder. Stage I learns to generate the depth residual (real minus CAD) rather than the full real depth, which is theoretically equivalent under a Gaussian assumption but empirically yields more stable, view-consistent outputs with better geometry preservation. A PointNet-based 3D binary discriminator then identifies local patches where Stage I output deviates from real-captured distributions. Stage II diffusion is conditioned on both the CAD depth and the Stage I output, with its denoising loss re-weighted (λ=1.5 vs. ω=0.5) to prioritize the identified unsatisfactory regions, effectively specializing the second stage on correcting spatially-variant noise patterns that Stage I's uniform noise bias fails to capture. The 3D discriminator is used only during training; at inference, both stages run sequentially with a DDIM sampler.

## Results

On few-shot 3D instance reconstruction (Table 1), Stable-Sim2Real achieves the best mIoU across all six categories, reaching 63.2 on Cabinet and 62.9 on Sofa, with the lowest Chamfer L2 (e.g., 4.09 on Sofa vs. 5.93 for vanilla Stable Diffusion). On 3D object classification and scene segmentation (Table 3), the method attains 77.5% accuracy and 51.2% mIoU, outperforming the next-best baseline (Stable Diffusion) by 2.9 and 2.3 points respectively. Direct comparison of simulated data against real captures on LASA validation (Table 4) shows the closest match: 62.9 mIoU / 2.57 Chamfer L2 / 28.2 F-score, versus 62.3 / 2.83 / 27.5 for vanilla SD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Stable-Sim2Real consistently outperforms all four baselines across every evaluated task and category. Against the closest competitor, vanilla Stable Diffusion (single-stage finetuning without residual generation or the 3D discriminator), the two-stage approach gains 1.9 mIoU on Chair (29.2 vs. 27.3), 3.5 mIoU on Sofa (62.9 vs. 59.4), and 1.6 mIoU on Cabinet (63.2 vs. 61.6), while also reducing Chamfer L2 by roughly 15–30% per category. Giga-GAN, the strongest GAN-based baseline, trails by 2.0–3.7 mIoU on reconstruction and by 2.9% accuracy / 2.3% mIoU on object classification and scene segmentation. Cycle-GAN and Pix2Pix perform at or below the no-simulation baseline on several categories (e.g., Cycle-GAN scores 55.2 mIoU on Sofa vs. 55.6 without simulation), indicating that their low-fidelity simulated data can actually harm downstream pretraining. The gains are most pronounced under zero-shot evaluation (Table 2), where the model relies entirely on simulated data for pretraining, confirming that the improvement stems from the quality of the simulated 3D data rather than from fine-tuning on real examples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDPM/Stable Diffusion framework and the ControlNet conditioning mechanism, repurposing a 2D text-to-image foundation model for 3D depth simulation. It departs from two prior lines of 3D Sim2Real work: physics-based sensor simulation (which relies on explicit physical priors and is confined to simulators) and earlier data-driven GAN-based approaches (Cycle-GAN, Pix2Pix) that suffered from training instability and mode collapse. The key conceptual departure is framing the task as learning a depth residual distribution via diffusion rather than direct image translation, and introducing a 3D-aware local discriminator to guide a second refinement stage — a design not present in prior depth diffusion or image translation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work revives a stagnant research direction (data-driven 3D Sim2Real) by showing that large-scale synthetic-real paired data (LASA) combined with diffusion foundation models can effectively simulate real-captured noise, and it provides the first comprehensive benchmark scheme for evaluating 3D data simulation methods. A notable practical finding is that low-quality simulated data (from Cycle-GAN or Pix2Pix) can actually degrade downstream performance compared to using no simulation at all, highlighting that simulation quality is a critical prerequisite. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the LASA dataset and object-centric scenes to more complex multi-object or outdoor 3D environments, and whether the two-stage design can be replaced by a single end-to-end model with learned attention on unsatisfactory regions.
