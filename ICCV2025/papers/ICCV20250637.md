## Problem

LoRA's fixed, uniform low-rank structure is insufficient for few-shot transfer tasks with large domain gaps (e.g., medical or satellite imaging from ImageNet pretraining), where higher ranks are needed to capture domain-specific complexity. Existing adaptive rank-allocation methods (AdaLoRA, DyLoRA, Pissa) rely on iterative pruning, rank searches, or additional regularization, introducing computational overhead and deployment complexity. The paper asks whether the pretrained model's own weight structure can serve as a free, search-free prior for per-layer rank allocation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (medical X-rays, pathology slides, colonoscopy frames, satellite and remote-sensing imagery) along with a pretrained vision transformer backbone (ViT-B16, ViT-L, or Swin variants initialized from ImageNet-21K, DINO, or MAE). From the frozen pretrained weight matrices (W_q, W_v, W_o in each multi-head attention layer), it computes stable ranks to determine per-layer LoRA dimensions. The output is a class label (multi-label or single-label depending on the task) produced by a trainable classifier head, together with the adapted LoRA A/B matrices whose ranks vary across layers.

## Method

SR-LoRA assigns each LoRA module's rank to the stable rank (Frobenius-norm-squared over spectral-norm-squared) of the corresponding pretrained weight matrix, providing a layer-wise, search-free rank allocation that reflects the intrinsic dimensionality of each layer's parameter space. This avoids both the under-capacity of a uniformly small rank and the over-parameterization of a uniformly large rank. To keep the number of trainable parameters manageable when stable ranks exceed typical LoRA hyperparameters, SR-LoRA introduces Stochastic Partial Updating (SPU): at each training iteration, a random subset size r_s ∈ [0, r] is sampled, and only the first r_s columns of A and rows of B participate in the forward pass and receive gradient updates, progressively covering the full low-rank space over many steps. The approach requires no SVD-based initialization, no orthogonality regularization, no iterative pruning, and no hyperparameter search beyond the standard LoRA training recipe.

## Results

On the VTAB Specialized 1-shot benchmark (large domain gap), SR-LoRA achieves 56.38% mean accuracy, outperforming Full Fine-Tuning (46.27%), LoRA-r256 (54.35%), and Adapter (50.27%). On the MedFM 1-shot medical imaging benchmark, SR-LoRA reaches 72.47% mean AUC versus 67.31% for Full-FT and 64.09% for standard LoRA-r8. The largest single-task gain is on Retinopathy (VTAB), where SR-LoRA scores 73.60% accuracy compared to 43.88% for Full-FT and 67.42% for LoRA-r256, demonstrating that stable-rank-guided allocation is particularly effective for fine-grained, high-complexity tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SR-LoRA wins against all 14 baselines on the VTAB Specialized 1-shot mean accuracy (56.38%), with the largest margins over GLoRA (+11.07%), LoRA-r8 (+13.39%), and Full-FT (+10.11%). Among LoRA variants specifically, SR-LoRA outperforms the strongest competitor LoRA-r256 by 2.03 points on mean accuracy and by 6.18 points on Retinopathy, while using only 4.52% trainable parameters versus 16.50% for LoRA-r256. On MedFM 1-shot, SR-LoRA (72.47% mean AUC) beats DyLoRA (70.40%), MeLoRA (68.49%), CPB (67.38%), Pissa (65.96%), and LoRA-r256 (65.67%), confirming that stable-rank-guided per-layer allocation surpasses both uniform high-rank and stochastic partial updating strategies. SR-LoRA also outperforms non-LoRA PEFT methods: Adapter (68.65% AUC on MedFM, 50.27% on VTAB), SSF (68.54%, 44.00%), and BitFit (64.05%, 48.38%). The only baseline that comes close on individual VTAB tasks is Adapter on Camelyon (62.97 vs. 64.22) and MoRA on EuroSAT (50.82 vs. 49.70), but neither matches SR-LoRA's overall consistency across all four specialized domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LoRA (Hu et al., ICLR 2022) by replacing its fixed, task-agnostic rank hyperparameter with a layer-wise rank derived from the stable rank of the corresponding pretrained weight matrix. It departs from two existing lines of adaptive LoRA work: (1) rank-increasing methods (ReLoRA, MeLoRA, MoRA, CPB) that expand capacity through ensembling or masking, and (2) rank-allocation methods (AdaLoRA, Pissa, DyLoRA, GLoRA) that rely on SVD-based scoring, iterative pruning, or random sampling. SR-LoRA's key conceptual departure is treating the stable rank—a quantity computable in closed form from the frozen pretrained weights—as a search-free model prior, eliminating the need for task-specific optimization or regularization to determine per-layer ranks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SR-LoRA offers a practically attractive solution for the common scenario of adapting vision foundation models to few-shot, large-domain-gap tasks (medical imaging, remote sensing) where fixed-rank LoRA underperforms and full fine-tuning overfits. The stable-rank prior is appealing because it requires no additional training, search, or hyperparameter tuning beyond computing a norm ratio once per layer. However, the method is currently validated only on ViT and Swin architectures with Q/V/O projection targets, and its extension to LLMs, other PEFT paradigms (adapters, prompts), or non-attention layers remains unexplored. An open question is whether the stable-rank signal remains informative when the pretrained model is trained with self-supervised objectives (DINO, MAE) at scale, where weight geometry may differ substantially from supervised pretraining.
