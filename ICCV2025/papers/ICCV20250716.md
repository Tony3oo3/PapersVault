## Problem

Current HD map construction methods (e.g., MapTRv2) perform poorly on high-quality predictions—those with both high classification and localization scores—due to two inherent defects: one-to-many matching assigns identical binary labels to all candidate samples regardless of their localization quality, and task-shared deformable attention features create conflicts between classification and localization branches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250716/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 6 (NuScenes) or 7 (Argoverse2) surrounding RGB camera images per frame as input, processes them through a shared CNN backbone and BEV view-transform module, and outputs vectorized map element instances (road boundaries, lane dividers, pedestrian crossings, optionally centerlines) as ordered point sets. Evaluation uses Chamfer Distance with hard ({0.2, 0.5, 1.0} m) and easy ({0.5, 1.0, 1.5} m) threshold sets over a 30 m × 60 m prediction range.

## Method

DAMap introduces three complementary components on top of a MapTRv2-style cascade decoder. (1) Distance-aware Focal Loss (DAFL) converts per-instance localization loss into a continuous confidence score via likelihood estimation (P = e^{−λL_dist}) and uses it as the classification label in Focal Loss, so that poorly localized candidates receive lower supervision targets. (2) Hybrid Loss Scheme (HLS) applies standard Focal Loss in early decoder layers (where random query initialization yields poor localization) and DAFL in later layers (where cascade refinement improves localization), avoiding unstable early-training gradients. (3) Task Modulated Deformable Attention (TMDA) doubles the query channel dimension, splits the self-attended query into task-specific sub-queries, and computes task-specific attention weights while sharing sampling offsets—reducing the optimization burden of unbounded offset learning while still extracting discriminative features for each task.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set (24 epochs, ResNet-50), DAMap applied to MapTRv2 achieves 62.8 mAP (easy) and 39.0 mAP (hard), improving over the reproduced MapTRv2 baseline by +2.4 mAP on both settings. When applied to the stronger MapQR baseline, DAMap reaches 68.8 mAP (easy) and 46.0 mAP (hard), a +2.4/+2.6 mAP gain. On Argoverse2 (6 epochs), the method improves MapTRv2 by +2.6 mAP (easy) and +2.8 mAP (hard), and with a Swin-B backbone on nuScenes it still yields +1.9 mAP (easy) over MapQR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAMap is applied as a plug-in module on top of three strong baselines (MapTRv2, MapQR, Mask2Map) and consistently improves them: +2.4 mAP (easy) over MapTRv2, +2.4 mAP over MapQR, and +1.0 mAP over Mask2Map on nuScenes (24 epochs, ResNet-50). Against other SOTA methods reported in the same table, MapQR+Ours (68.8 mAP easy) outperforms HIMap (66.7), SQD-MapNet (65.0), StreamMapNet (63.4), and MapTRv2+MGMap (64.8), while Mask2Map+Ours (72.6) surpasses all of them. On Argoverse2, the gains are larger under the shorter 6-epoch schedule (+2.6 mAP easy over MapTRv2, +2.0 over MapQR) and remain positive at 24 epochs (+0.9/+1.5). With a Swin-B backbone, MapQR+Ours still improves over the reproduced MapQR by +1.9 mAP (easy) and +1.6 mAP (hard), confirming generalizability across backbones. The method also extends to a Centerline category, gaining +5.1 mAP on nuScenes and +4.0 mAP on Argoverse2 over the reproduced MapTRv2 baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAMap extends the MapTRv2 pipeline (cascade DETR decoder with one-to-many matching and deformable attention in BEV space) by targeting two specific weaknesses: label misalignment in one-to-many matching and task-shared feature extraction. The Distance-aware Focal Loss draws on the high-quality detection literature (GFL, VFNet, Align-DETR) but adapts the idea of incorporating localization quality into classification supervision to the vectorized map setting without requiring an explicit IoU head. The Task Modulated Deformable Attention modifies the standard Deformable DETR cross-attention by splitting attention weights into task-specific branches while keeping sampling offsets shared, a design choice distinct from prior decoupled-attention approaches (DESTR, Decoupled DETR) that use fully separate cross-attention modules. The paper positions its contributions as complementary to recent SOTA methods (MapQR, Mask2Map, StreamMapNet), demonstrating plug-and-play gains on top of each.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical safety concern in autonomous driving: predictions that are confidently classified but poorly localized are dangerous, and the analysis showing only 35% recall at 0.5 m threshold even with 0.6 classification confidence highlights a real gap. The plug-and-play nature (applicable to MapTRv2, MapQR, Mask2Map, StreamMapNet) makes the components broadly adoptable for the HD map construction community. However, the gains are modest (1–2.8 mAP) and the conceptual contributions—incorporating localization quality into focal loss and splitting attention weights by task—are natural extensions of ideas already established in object detection. Open questions include whether the task-specific feature separation generalizes to other multi-task transformer decoders beyond map construction, and whether the hybrid loss schedule (FL early, DAFL late) is optimal or merely a heuristic workaround for early-training instability.
