## Problem

Multi-view 3D object detection methods rely on fully data-driven, implicit 2D-to-3D feature transformations that limit the network's 3D geometry estimation capability. While radiance fields (NeRF, 3DGS) have shown promise in occupancy prediction and BEV segmentation, directly applying scene-level radiance field rendering as an auxiliary task to a detector actually degrades performance due to strong background responses. The paper identifies that detection focuses on foreground objects of interest rather than the entire scene, making scene-centric rendering fundamentally misaligned with the detection task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252262/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images from 6 surround cameras (256×704 with ResNet-50+FPN on validation, 640×1600 with ViT-B on test) and optionally integrates temporal information from up to 8 consecutive frames. It outputs 3D bounding boxes with category, translation, scale, orientation, velocity, and attribute predictions in a 128×128 (validation) or 256×256 (test) BEV grid. The radiance field rendering branch is used only during training as an auxiliary head and is removed at inference to avoid additional computational cost.

## Method

OcRFDet builds on the DualBEV baseline and introduces two geometric enhancement modules. First, Object-Centric Radiance Fields (OcRF) decode 3D voxel features into hybrid NeRF and 3DGS representations via separate MLPs, then render a randomly selected viewpoint using degenerate depth-guided splatting (3DGS) and single-point volume rendering (NeRF); critically, the rendering loss is computed only over foreground regions defined by 2D masks projected from 3D ground-truth boxes, with a learnable linear fusion of the two rendered results and a scene-level warm-up phase for the first 2 epochs. Second, Height-Aware Opacity-Based Attention (HOA) fuses the opacity fields from both radiance representations via cross-attention (the higher-weighted field acts as query), then applies multi-scale height slice attention that divides the fused opacity into k=4 height groups processed in parallel through self-attention, MaxPooling, and 1×1 convolutions to produce height-specific attention maps, integrated across a three-layer opacity pyramid and applied to BEV features to emphasize foreground while suppressing background noise.

## Results

On the nuScenes test set (8-frame input, V2-99 backbone), OcRFDet achieves 57.2% mAP and 64.8% NDS, setting a new state-of-the-art. On the nuScenes validation set with a single frame (ResNet-50 + FPN), it reaches 36.8% mAP and 43.4% NDS, and with two frames, 40.0% mAP and 50.9% NDS. The method outperforms the previous best (DualBEV) by 2.0 pp in mAP and 1.1 pp in NDS on the test set, and by 1.6 pp in mAP on the single-frame validation setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OcRFDet outperforms all 18 compared baselines on both the nuScenes validation and test sets. On the test set, it surpasses the closest competitor OPEN (56.7% mAP, 64.4% NDS) by 0.5 pp in mAP and 0.4 pp in NDS, and beats DualBEV (55.2%, 63.4%) by 2.0 pp in mAP and 1.1 pp in NDS. On the single-frame validation setting, it leads DualBEV (35.2%, 42.5%) by 1.6 pp in mAP and 0.9 pp in NDS, and BEVDepth (34.2%, 40.7%) by 2.6 pp in mAP. With two frames on validation, it exceeds BEVStereo (37.2%, 50.0%) by 2.8 pp in mAP and SA-BEV (37.8%, 49.9%) by 2.2 pp in mAP. The method also generalizes to query-based detectors: when applied to BEVFormer-tiny, it improves mAP from 25.2% to 27.4% (+2.2 pp) and NDS from 35.5% to 36.9% (+1.4 pp). Notably, OcRFDet achieves the best mAP while having slightly higher mASE (0.253) and mAOE (0.331) than some competitors, indicating its gains come primarily from improved recall and localization rather than scale/orientation accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the depth-based multi-view 3D detection pipeline of DualBEV and BEVDepth, and repurposes radiance field representations (NeRF and 3DGS) that have been applied to occupancy prediction (RenderOcc, GaussianFormer) and BEV segmentation (GaussianBEV). The key conceptual departure is identifying that the scene-level rendering paradigm successful in those tasks actually degrades detection performance due to background noise, and proposing an object-centric rendering objective that restricts supervision to foreground regions. The hybrid generalizable radiance field design follows NeRF-Det and Fast Generalizable Gaussian Splatting in decoding voxel features via MLPs rather than per-scene optimization, while the opacity-based attention mechanism is a novel use of a rendering byproduct for feature refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to integrate radiance fields into multi-view 3D object detection, establishing that geometric rendering can serve as a useful auxiliary training signal for detection beyond its traditional role in reconstruction and occupancy. The finding that scene-level rendering is counterproductive for detection (unlike occupancy/segmentation) is a useful negative result that clarifies when radiance field supervision helps versus hurts. Open questions include whether the object-centric formulation extends naturally to dynamic scenes with moving objects, whether the approach generalizes to other sensor configurations (LiDAR, radar), and whether the modest 1–2 pp gains scale with larger backbones or longer temporal sequences. The rendering branch is removed at inference, so the practical cost is limited to training, but the added latency of ~19 ms during training may matter for large-scale pretraining scenarios.
