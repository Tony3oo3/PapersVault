## Problem

Existing visual counting methods assume objects are visible, but in many practical settings (pallets, crates, bins) objects are stacked so that most are occluded by those above them. This paper tackles the previously unaddressed problem of counting identical stacked 3D objects from multi-view images, a task shown to be difficult even for humans (33 participants averaged 77% sMAPE error).

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of multi-view RGB images of a container with stacked objects, a real-world reference measurement for scale calibration, and optionally a single image of a template object for unit-volume estimation. SAM2 propagates object and container segmentations across all frames to automatically identify the key view (largest visible object area). The pipeline outputs a total object count N, the occupancy ratio γ, and the reconstructed stack volume V, with the count computed as N = γV/v.

## Method

The core insight is decomposing counting into two independent subproblems: estimating the 3D volume of the stack and estimating the occupancy ratio γ (fraction of volume actually filled by objects). For γ, a DINOv2 encoder plus a CNN decoder regresses a scalar from a monocular depth map (produced by Depth Anything V2) of the key view, trained on 400K images from 14K physics-simulated scenes with analytically computed ground-truth ratios. For volume, COLMAP recovers camera poses, 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstructs the scene from segmented images, and a voxel-carving algorithm computes the enclosed volume; a separate dilated-convolution decoder Ψ predicts container thickness to erode the reconstruction and isolate contents. The approach is shape-agnostic at test time because the occupancy network learns depth-to-ratio relationships independent of object geometry, and training on estimated (rather than ground-truth) depth maps reduces the synthetic-to-real domain gap.

## Results

On the real-world dataset (45 scenes, 2381 images), 3DC achieves NAE 0.36, SRE 0.06, MAE 382.59, and sMAPE 53.31, substantially outperforming all baselines including human annotators (NAE 0.79, sMAPE 76.85) and the best prior method BMNet+ (NAE 0.93, sMAPE 131.44). On the synthetic benchmark (100 unseen scenes), the method achieves NAE 0.22, SRE 0.09, MAE 79.48, and sMAPE 27.65. For occupancy ratio estimation alone, 3DC reaches R²=0.79 and sMAPE 29.18, compared to 0.28 and 34.80 for the best depth-based baseline (DepthCorrected).

## Baselines & Comparisons

3DC outperforms all baselines on every metric for both synthetic and real-world evaluation. Against BMNet+ (density-map counting), the proposed method reduces NAE from 0.93 to 0.36 and sMAPE from 131.44 to 53.31 on real scenes, a roughly 2.5× improvement in relative error. The SAM+CLIP pipeline (segmentation + text-prompt identification) performs comparably to BMNet+ (NAE 0.94 vs. 0.93), confirming that neither density estimation nor instance-level segmentation can handle heavy occlusion in stacks. Direct end-to-end prediction (CNN or ViT+H) fails catastrophically on real data (NAE ≈ 0.94–0.95), motivating the decomposition into volume and occupancy subproblems. Notably, 3DC also beats the Human-Vote baseline (average of 1485 guesses from 33 participants) by a wide margin (NAE 0.36 vs. 0.60, sMAPE 53.31 vs. 57.91), and LlamaV3.2 11B is essentially useless for this task (NAE 1.00, sMAPE 190.48). On the synthetic set, the gap is even larger: 3DC achieves NAE 0.22 versus 0.66–0.91 for all other methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of counting research (single-view density maps, multi-view fusion) by targeting a fundamentally different regime: objects stacked in containers where the vast majority are occluded. Rather than extending prior counting architectures, it repurposes 3D Gaussian Splatting for volume reconstruction and monocular depth estimation (Depth Anything V2) for occupancy prediction, combining them through a novel decomposition into volume and occupancy-ratio subproblems. The use of DINOv2 as a frozen encoder and SAM2 for cross-frame segmentation propagation positions the method within the current foundation-model toolchain, while the physics-simulated synthetic training data (built on ABC CAD models) is a new contribution not seen in prior counting work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that counting stacked objects is a genuinely hard problem—humans performing a structured contest achieve NAE 0.60 even with voting, and direct end-to-end prediction (ViT+H, CNN) collapses to near-random performance on real scenes (NAE ~0.94)—making the decomposition strategy a necessary rather than optional design choice. The 400K-image synthetic dataset and 2381-image real-world benchmark, together with the human baseline, provide the first infrastructure for evaluating this task. Open questions include handling non-uniform or heterogeneous stacks, localizing individual visible instances for interpretability, and closing the remaining synthetic-to-real gap (NAE 0.22 synthetic vs. 0.36 real), particularly for scenes with thousands of objects.
