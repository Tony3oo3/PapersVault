## Problem

While DiT-based diffusion models like SD3 have surpassed UNet-based architectures in image generation quality, it remains unclear how to effectively adapt them for Real-ISR. Existing approaches simply graft ControlNet onto DiT, which forces a one-directional information flow from the LR stream to the noise stream and fails to exploit DiT's core strength of bidirectional cross-stream interaction. This paper asks whether a control mechanism designed natively for DiT can unlock superior restoration performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded LR image (128×128 or 256×256 pixels) and a text caption describing the scene as input, and produces a 512×512 HR image via a ×4 super-resolution factor. The LR image is encoded into latent space by a pre-trained VAE encoder, while the caption is processed by three frozen text encoders (CLIP-L, CLIP-G, T5-XXL) whose pooled representations also modulate internal features via the diffusion timestep. The diffusion process runs for T denoising steps in the latent space before VAE decoding yields the final HR output.

## Method

DiT4SR modifies each MM-DiT block of SD3 into an MM-DiT-Control block by introducing a third LR Stream alongside the existing Noise and Text Streams. The LR token is concatenated with noise and text tokens in the joint attention computation (LR Integration in Attention), enabling bidirectional information exchange so the LR guidance evolves alongside the diffusion process rather than being a static condition. An LR Residual shortcut directly connects the attention input to output within the LR Stream to prevent guidance degradation in deeper blocks. Additionally, a 3×3 depth-wise convolution layer (LR Injection between MLP) transfers intermediate MLP features from the LR Stream into the Noise Stream, compensating for DiT's limited local information capture that pure global attention cannot provide. All new LR projections and the convolution layer are zero-initialized so the pre-trained SD3 weights remain undisturbed at the start of training.

## Results

On the two benchmarks without ground truth (RealLR200 and RealLQ250), DiT4SR achieves the best performance across all four non-reference metrics: MUSIQ 70.469 and 71.832, MANIQA 0.645 and 0.632, ClipIQA 0.588 and 0.578, and LIQE 4.331 and 4.356, respectively. On DrealSR and RealSR (with GT), DiT4SR is competitive, achieving MUSIQ 64.950/68.073 and MANIQA 0.627/0.661, closely matching or exceeding SeeSR and DiffBIR. In the user study with 80 participants, DiT4SR wins 72.7–83.6% on realism and 64.5–79.5% on fidelity against SeeSR, DiffBIR, SUPIR, and DreamClear.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiT4SR clearly outperforms the GAN-based baselines (Real-ESRGAN, SwinIR) and the UNet-based diffusion methods (ResShift, StableSR, SUPIR) on all non-reference metrics across all four benchmarks, with margins of 5–15 points on MUSIQ and 0.05–0.10 on MANIQA. Against the strongest UNet-based competitors SeeSR and DiffBIR, DiT4SR is competitive on DrealSR and RealSR (e.g., MUSIQ 64.950 vs. SeeSR 65.077 and DiffBIR 65.665 on DrealSR) but achieves decisive wins on RealLR200 and RealLQ250, where it leads by 1.0–1.9 MUSIQ points and 0.016–0.026 MANIQA over both. DiT4SR also surpasses OSEDiff on the no-GT benchmarks (e.g., LIQE 4.331 vs. 4.069 on RealLR200) while being slightly behind on DrealSR LIQE (3.964 vs. 3.942). Against the DiT-based DreamClear, DiT4SR wins on every metric of every dataset, with the largest gap on LIQE (4.331 vs. 3.775 on RealLR200; 4.356 vs. 3.688 on RealLQ250). The user study confirms these quantitative trends: DiT4SR wins 72.7–83.6% on realism and 64.5–79.5% on fidelity against SeeSR, DiffBIR, SUPIR, and DreamClear.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SD3's MM-DiT architecture (Esser et al., ICML 2024) and the broader DiT lineage (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023), but departs sharply from the dominant ControlNet paradigm used by prior diffusion-based Real-ISR methods (StableSR, DiffBIR, SeeSR, DreamClear, DPIR). Rather than duplicating DiT blocks as a separate trainable branch that injects LR features unidirectionally, DiT4SR integrates the LR stream into the original joint attention computation, enabling bidirectional information flow between the LR and noise streams. This positions the work as an architectural rethinking of how conditioning should interact with transformer-based diffusion models, rather than a straightforward application of existing control mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiT4SR demonstrates that the bidirectional attention structure of DiT can be natively exploited for conditional generation tasks, offering a design principle that may generalize beyond SR to other restoration and editing problems. The finding that a simple 3×3 depth-wise convolution is necessary to compensate for DiT's weak local feature capture is a practical insight for anyone adapting transformer-based generators to pixel-level tasks. Open questions include whether this approach scales to even larger DiT models (e.g., Flux), whether the multi-step diffusion process can be distilled to one or few steps while preserving the bidirectional interaction benefit, and whether the architecture generalizes to video or 3D restoration.
