## Problem

Multi-step diffusion models offer strong restoration quality for JPEG artifact removal but incur prohibitive computational costs (50+ denoising steps), while existing one-step and CNN/Transformer-based methods still struggle with severe compression (e.g., QF=5) because they lack explicit JPEG compression priors. Without such priors, models cannot reliably distinguish compression-induced distortions (blockiness, color banding) from natural image features, leading to inharmonious restorations.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single JPEG-compressed RGB image (tested at 1024×1024) as input and produces a restored high-quality RGB image in one forward pass. It operates in a blind setting: the quality factor is unknown at inference time and must be inferred from the image itself via the learned compression embeddings. Training pairs are synthesized by sampling QF uniformly from 8–95, and the model is evaluated across QF levels from 1 to 100, including unseen levels like QF=1 and QF=5.

## Method

CODiff operates in two stages. Stage 1 trains a Compression-aware Visual Embedder (CaVE), a UNet encoder paired with a lightweight QF-predictor head, using a dual learning objective: explicit learning (L1 loss on predicted vs. ground-truth QF) forces the embeddings to encode compression level, while implicit learning (L1 reconstruction loss on HQ images) captures the full compression-to-reconstruction mapping, improving generalization to unseen QFs. Stage 2 fine-tunes a StableDiffusion-based one-step diffusion generator (VAE encoder + UNet via LoRA rank 16) that feeds the LQ image latent directly into the UNet at a fixed timestep T_L, conditioned on CaVE's compression prior embeddings, and performs a single denoising step to recover the HQ latent. The generator is trained with a combined perceptual loss (L2 + DISTS) and a GAN loss using a discriminator built on the pre-trained UNet encoder, avoiding the distillation bottleneck of prior one-step methods.

## Results

On LIVE-1 at QF=5 (most severe compression), CODiff achieves LPIPS 0.2062, DISTS 0.1121, MUSIQ 73.16, MANIQA 0.5321, and CLIPIQA 0.7212, outperforming all baselines across both full-reference and no-reference metrics. At QF=10, CODiff further improves to LPIPS 0.1428 and DISTS 0.0867, and at QF=20 reaches LPIPS 0.1101 and DISTS 0.0692. The method achieves these results with a single denoising step (vs. 50 for multi-step methods) and 9.46T MACs, compared to 188.24T for DiffBIR and 464.29T for SUPIR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CODiff consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every metric and QF level on all three datasets. Against the closest one-step competitor OSEDiff*, CODiff improves LPIPS from 0.2675 to 0.2062 (−23%) and DISTS from 0.1653 to 0.1121 (−32%) at QF=5 on LIVE-1, while also surpassing it on all no-reference metrics (e.g., MUSIQ 73.16 vs. 65.51, CLIPIQA 0.7212 vs. 0.5623). Against multi-step methods, CODiff beats DiffBIR* (50 steps) by 0.1447 in LPIPS and 0.0914 in DISTS at QF=5, and outperforms the much larger SUPIR (50 steps, 4.49B params) by 0.2794 in LPIPS at QF=5, despite using only 1.00B parameters and 1 step. Among CNN/Transformer baselines, CODiff surpasses FBCNN by 0.1674 in LPIPS and JDEC by 0.2051 in LPIPS at QF=5, confirming that diffusion-based restoration with compression priors substantially outperforms traditional architectures under severe compression. The advantage is most pronounced at QF=5, where information loss is greatest, and narrows (but persists) at QF=20.

## Relation to Prior Work

CODiff extends the one-step diffusion (OSD) paradigm introduced by OSEDiff and SinSR by injecting task-specific compression priors into the denoising process, rather than relying solely on generic text or learnable prompt embeddings. It departs from prior diffusion-based restoration methods (DiffBIR, SUPIR) that treat the LQ image as a generic conditioning signal, instead explicitly modeling the JPEG compression process through a dedicated embedder (CaVE) trained with a dual objective—QF prediction plus reconstruction. The LoRA-based fine-tuning of StableDiffusion follows the efficient adaptation trend, while the GAN-based training (replacing distillation) aligns with recent work showing that adversarial objectives can surpass teacher-student limits in one-step generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving 50× speedup over multi-step diffusion while matching or exceeding their quality, CODiff makes diffusion-based JPEG artifact removal practically deployable for real-time or large-scale applications. The compression-aware embedding with dual learning offers a template for incorporating degradation-specific priors into other one-step restoration tasks (e.g., denoising, deblurring), though the paper does not test this generalization. An open question is whether the approach extends to other compression formats (WebP, HEIC) or to combined degradations, and whether the reliance on StableDiffusion's generative prior introduces hallucination risks in highly compressed regions where true content is unrecoverable.
