## Problem

Existing face-swapping methods replace only localized facial regions, neglecting holistic head morphology such as head shape and hairstyle, while head-swapping approaches struggle with hairstyle diversity, complex backgrounds, and background artifacts. Critically, no prior method allows users to modify or edit the transplanted head's expressions after swapping, and expression transfer accuracy degrades when source and target subjects have significantly different facial feature proportions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250996/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single static reference portrait image and a driving video sequence as input, producing a head-swapped video at 512×512 resolution where the reference person's entire head (including hair and head shape) replaces the target video's head while preserving the target's body and background. The driving video's 3D landmarks (extracted via Mediapipe) and an inpainted background (generated by LAMA after human parsing segmentation) serve as spatial conditions, while the reference image is preprocessed to retain only the head region and randomly scaled/rotated to prevent identity leakage.

## Method

The framework reformulates head swapping as a conditional inpainting task within a Latent Diffusion Model, extending Stable Diffusion with an AppearanceNet (identity injection via self-attention), a PoseGuider (spatial alignment of 3D landmark conditions), a MotionModule (temporal coherence), and an Image Encoder (global reference features via cross-attention). Three key strategies address identity leakage: a Shape-Agnostic Mask that replaces smooth segmentation boundaries with block-wise binary masks to prevent invisible shape priors, a Hair Enhancement strategy that erodes the cloth mask with random long-hair masks and shoulder-keypoint rectangles to break hair-body associations, and an Expression-Aware Landmark Retargeting module that uses a 3DMM to neutralize the reference image's expression before applying scale-aware retargeting that adjusts eye and mouth expression magnitudes according to inter-subject facial feature size ratios. Training combines the standard LDM denoising loss with an ID loss that enforces both pixel-level head-region fidelity and ArcFace embedding similarity between the denoised output and the reference.

## Results

On the VFHQ evaluation set (200 videos × 48 frames, 200 reference images), the proposed method achieves an ID Similarity of 0.895, Expression error of 0.014, and Pose error of 9.83. It substantially outperforms face-swapping baselines (SimSwap: 0.592 ID, DiffSwap: 0.397 ID) and the head-swapping baseline HeSer (0.319 ID) on identity preservation, while achieving the best expression error (0.014) among all methods that fully regenerate the head. Face-swapping methods report lower pose and expression errors (e.g., SimSwap: 0.702 pose, 0.005 expression) but at the cost of poor identity transfer, since they only modify localized facial regions rather than regenerating the entire head.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against face-swapping baselines SimSwap and DiffSwap, the proposed method achieves dramatically higher ID Similarity (0.895 vs. 0.592 and 0.397), confirming that full-head regeneration preserves identity far better than localized face replacement; however, face-swapping methods report lower pose (0.702, 1.144 vs. 9.83) and expression (0.005, 0.008 vs. 0.014) errors because they make only subtle modifications to the target image. Against the head-swapping baseline HeSer, the method wins on both ID Similarity (0.895 vs. 0.319) and Expression error (0.014 vs. 0.022), while HeSer has a slightly lower pose error (6.840 vs. 9.83). Among portrait-animation methods, the proposed approach matches AniPortrait on ID Similarity (0.895 vs. 0.892) and substantially outperforms both AniPortrait (0.025) and Follow-Your-Emoji (0.026) on Expression error, though it trails AniPortrait on Pose error (9.83 vs. 6.55). Overall, the method is the only one that simultaneously achieves high identity fidelity, low expression error, and full head regeneration with diverse hairstyle support.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Latent Diffusion Model framework (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) and extends it with multi-condition inputs (background, 3D landmarks, reference identity) to reformulate video head swapping as a conditional inpainting problem. It departs from face-swapping methods (SimSwap, DiffSwap) that only replace localized facial regions, and from prior head-swapping approaches (HeSer, HS-Diffusion) that struggle with hairstyle diversity and background fidelity. The expression control mechanism specifically builds on Follow-your-emoji's use of Mediapipe 3D landmarks but augments it with 3DMM-based expression neutralization and scale-aware retargeting to address cross-identity proportion mismatches that prior landmark-based methods ignore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in video production pipelines: the ability to transplant a full head (including hairstyle and head shape) into a video while simultaneously controlling and editing the transplanted head's expressions. The Shape-Agnostic Mask finding—that invisible inpainting boundaries leak head shape information to the diffusion model—is a useful insight for anyone using inpainting-based training data augmentation. Open questions include scalability to longer video sequences, real-time inference feasibility given the multi-component pipeline, and whether the expression editing capability generalizes to extreme cross-identity scenarios beyond the VFHQ evaluation.
