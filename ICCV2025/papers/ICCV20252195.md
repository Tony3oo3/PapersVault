## Problem

Likelihood-based video anomaly detection methods are blind to anomalies residing in local modes near the learned normal distribution, because the Stein score (gradient of log density) tends to be small at such points. The paper identifies three specific gaps between likelihood estimation and video anomaly characteristics: a scene gap (scene-dependent anomalies), a motion gap (motion consistency), and an appearance gap (visual details), all of which limit the discriminative power of a pure score-based indicator.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252195/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 8-frame video clips of 160×160 pixels centered on objects detected by a pretrained ByteTrack tracker, along with a scene class label (camera ID). The input sequence is tokenized into N=800 non-overlapping patches of size 16×16×c. At inference, L=20 noise levels are used, and the final per-frame anomaly score is normalized to [0,1] by dividing the score norm by the PSNR between the denoised and original data, then min-max normalizing across all frames in the video.

## Method

The core architecture is a Noise-Conditioned Score Transformer (NCST) built on the Latte latent diffusion transformer, trained with a patch-wise denoising score matching objective that estimates the score function for Gaussian-perturbed video patches across multiple noise levels. Two conditioning strategies are integrated: scene-dependent score matching via S-AdaLN that embeds the scene class label alongside the diffusion timestep, and key-frame motion weighting that assigns patch-level weights proportional to the absolute difference between the first and last frames of the input sequence. The key novelty is an autoregressive inference mechanism: instead of adding noise to the original data at each step, intensifying Gaussian noise is injected into the previously denoised output, causing abnormal context to accumulate across iterations. The final anomaly score at each noise level is the L2-norm of the estimated score divided by the PSNR between the denoised and original data, combining likelihood and appearance information to suppress local-mode blind spots.

## Results

ADSM achieves state-of-the-art AUC-ROC on all three VAD benchmarks: 91.6% (micro) / 94.2% (macro) on Avenue, 84.5% / 93.2% on ShanghaiTech, and 76.9% / 78.1% on NWPU Campus. The macro score improvement is most pronounced on ShanghaiTech (+3.9% over the previous best SSMTL at 89.3%) and NWPU Campus (+1.2% over SSAE at 76.9%). The method also outperforms the closest likelihood-based baselines (MULDE, MSMA) by 2.8% and 5.7% macro AUC on ShanghaiTech, respectively, confirming that the autoregressive denoising mechanism and visual information integration are critical for handling local-mode anomalies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADSM outperforms all 21 baselines on the macro AUC metric across all three datasets. On Avenue, it surpasses the strongest competitor HF2-VAD (93.5%) by 0.7% and Two-stream (93.0%) by 1.2%. On ShanghaiTech, the margin is largest: ADSM (93.2%) beats SSMTL (89.3%) by 3.9% and Two-stream (90.8%) by 2.4%, demonstrating the benefit of the scene-conditioned and motion-aware score function in multi-scene settings. On NWPU Campus, ADSM (78.1% macro) edges out SSAE (76.9%) by 1.2%, the only prior method reporting results on this dataset. Against the two closest likelihood-based baselines (MULDE at 90.4% and MSMA at 87.5% macro on ShanghaiTech), ADSM gains 2.8% and 5.7% respectively, validating that the autoregressive mechanism and PSNR-based visual aggregation effectively address the local-mode blindness inherent in pure score-norm indicators. Notably, ADSM uses only raw video clips as input, while several competitors (FFP, AMMC-Net, HF2-VAD, MTP, STG-NF, MoCoDAD) rely on additional modalities such as optical flow or skeleton data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the denoising score matching framework (Song & Ermon, 2019) into a transformer-based architecture for video anomaly detection, building directly on the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, 2023) and its video variant Latte (Ma et al., 2024) for the noise-conditioned score transformer. It departs from prior likelihood-based VAD methods (MULDE, MSMA, SSAE) that relied solely on score norms by identifying a fundamental blindness to local modes and addressing it through an autoregressive mechanism that injects noise into denoised data rather than original data. The scene-conditioned and motion-weighted score function further differentiates the approach from standard score matching by incorporating video-specific priors about scene-dependent anomalies and temporal consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine limitation of likelihood-based anomaly detection—the inability to distinguish anomalies residing in local modes of the learned distribution—through a practical autoregressive mechanism that accumulates abnormal context across noise levels. The finding that operating in original data space substantially outperforms latent space (Table 2) challenges the common assumption that latent representations are always preferable for VAD, suggesting that distributional fidelity matters more than computational efficiency for this task. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer video sequences, whether the reliance on an external object detector (ByteTrack) limits deployment flexibility, and whether the autoregressive mechanism generalizes to other score-based detection tasks beyond video.
