## Problem

Video-radar remote physiological measurement suffers from two intertwined challenges: multimodal datasets are too small to train robust models from scratch (leading to severe overfitting), and the fundamental measurement principles of video (facial skin color changes) and radar (chest mechanical displacements) create a large modality gap that impedes effective cross-modal feature fusion. Existing multimodal RPM methods either ignore the data scarcity issue or apply crude modality alignment that hinders learning of modality-specific features.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB face video (cropped to 96×96 pixels, sampled into 160-frame clips with 80-frame stride) and a mmWave radar intermediate-frequency signal (processed via range-FFT, coarse range gating, 2D beamforming over azimuth/elevation angles, and a second-order phase differentiator to produce a 1×S×T₂ tensor). The output is a 1D PPG signal from which heart rate is estimated via band-pass filtering, detrending, and peak detection in the frequency domain, evaluated over 300-sample windows with 128-sample stride.

## Method

The paper proposes the SATM (Spatial Alignment and Temporal Matching) adapter for parameter-efficient multimodal fine-tuning of frozen UniFormer backbones pre-trained on large-scale unimodal datasets. Spatial Alignment projects 2D video and 1D radar spatial tokens into a unified 1D time-series space by compressing channel dimensions via linear projections, then applies cross-attention to align their spatial distributions (face vs. chest ROIs). Temporal Matching refines the spatially aligned features using a matched-filter operation in the frequency domain (conjugate FFT of the video feature multiplied by FFT of the radar feature, followed by inverse FFT, depthwise convolution, and batch normalization) to mitigate waveform discrepancies between color-change and displacement signals. SATM adapters are inserted in parallel with MHRA and FFN modules only in the last two UniFormer stages, while vanilla adapters handle the first two stages to balance unimodal and cross-modal feature learning. The entire adapter stack (≈10.78M trainable parameters) is trained end-to-end on the small multimodal dataset while backbones remain frozen.

## Results

On the EquiPleth dataset, SATM achieves an RMSE of 1.242 bpm, MAE of 0.540 bpm, and Pearson correlation R of 0.994, outperforming the best adapter baseline LAVISH (RMSE 1.498 bpm) by 17% in RMSE. On the more challenging MMRPM dataset (with low-light and head-motion conditions), SATM achieves RMSE 5.074 bpm and MAE 1.292 bpm, again leading all baselines. The method uses only 10.78M trainable parameters, comparable to other adapter-based methods, while the full fine-tuning baselines (e.g., Fusion-Vital*) require up to 194.61M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SATM outperforms all seven baselines on both EquiPleth and MMRPM across every metric. Against the three video-radar RPM methods (EquiPleth*, Fusion-Vital*, CardiacMamba*), SATM reduces RMSE by 13–47% (1.242 vs. 1.436, 2.157, 2.324 bpm on EquiPleth), demonstrating that adapter-based fine-tuning with unimodal pre-training substantially outperforms full fine-tuning on small-scale multimodal data. Among the four cross-domain adapter baselines, LAVISH (audio-visual) is the closest competitor at RMSE 1.498 bpm, but SATM still improves by 0.256 bpm in RMSE and 0.017 in R. UniAdapter and MMA, which use parameter-sharing adapters, underperform SATM by 0.414 and 0.600 bpm in RMSE respectively, confirming that naive parameter sharing hinders modality-specific feature learning in RPM. BAT, despite using 85.35M trainable parameters (nearly 8× more than SATM), achieves RMSE 1.684 bpm, showing that simply increasing adapter capacity without addressing the video-radar modality gap is insufficient. On the harder MMRPM dataset, the gap widens: SATM achieves RMSE 5.074 bpm versus 5.312 bpm for LAVISH and 5.510 bpm for UniAdapter, highlighting better robustness under low-light and head-motion conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video-radar RPM line initiated by EquiPleth, Fusion-Vital, and CardiacMamba, all of which train end-to-end fusion models from scratch on small-scale multimodal datasets. It departs from this paradigm by introducing unimodal pre-training followed by adapter-based fine-tuning, drawing on the PEFT/adapter literature (UniAdapter, MMA, BAT, LAVISH) but rejecting their parameter-sharing or direct cross-attention strategies as ill-suited to RPM's need to preserve modality-specific features. The SATM adapter's two components—Spatial Alignment via cross-attention in a unified time-series space and Temporal Matching via a matched filter (FFT-based convolution)—are novel architectural contributions specifically designed to bridge the ROI and waveform gaps between facial color changes and chest mechanical displacements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in multimodal RPM: the scarcity of synchronized video-radar-PPG data makes from-scratch training prone to overfitting, and the demonstrated superiority of pre-train-then-adapter over full fine-tuning provides a clear recipe for future multimodal RPM work. The MMRPM dataset, covering low-light and head-motion scenarios absent from EquiPleth, is a useful addition for stress-testing robustness. However, the method still relies on vanilla adapters for unimodal modeling and the cross-dataset generalization gap (RMSE 7.66 vs 5.07 intra-dataset) suggests the approach has not yet fully solved the domain-shift problem. Open questions include whether a unified framework can harmonize unimodal and cross-modal learning, and whether the matched-filter design generalizes to other modality pairs with different waveform characteristics.
