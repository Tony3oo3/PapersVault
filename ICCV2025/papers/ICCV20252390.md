## Problem

Existing zero-shot tissue segmentation models require substantial labeled data on seen classes to achieve good performance, while traditional active learning strategies are not designed for the zero-shot setting and ignore the need to generalize to unseen tissue types. This paper addresses the unexplored question of how to design an active query strategy that selects the most valuable seen-class samples for annotation while simultaneously improving segmentation on both seen and unseen categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252390/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 pathology image patches and GPT-3-generated text descriptions of tissue categories as input. The image is split into 7×7 patches processed by a ViT-B/32 encoder, and the resulting patch features are upsampled to full-resolution pixel-level features (64-dim) via a patch-to-pixel decoder. The output is a pixel-wise segmentation mask where each pixel is assigned to one of N_c tissue classes by argmax over prototype-convolved score maps. In the active learning loop, the system additionally consumes an unlabeled pool of seen-class images and outputs a ranked list of the most informative samples for expert annotation.

## Method

AcZeroTS is built on ProZS, a prototype-guided zero-shot segmentation model that uses the pre-trained PLIP pathology VLM: a vision pathway extracts multi-layer patch features decoded to pixel-level representations, while a text pathway encodes GPT-3-generated tissue descriptions into category prototypes via a three-layer projector. Segmentation is performed by treating prototypes as convolutional kernels over pixel features with a focal loss, augmented by a category-wise pixel-level contrastive loss that aligns pixel features with their class centroids. The active learning strategy combines three criteria: (1) Evidence-based Uncertainty (EUN) using Dirichlet-distribution belief masses from ReLU-activated score maps, (2) Contrast Among Labeled and Unlabeled Data (COT) measuring KL divergence between pixel feature distributions of unlabeled samples and their K nearest labeled neighbors, and (3) Complete Summarization Ability of Prototypes (CSA) selecting samples farthest from all category prototypes to improve prototype coverage. The final query score is the sum of min-max-normalized US, CS, and PS scores, and top-N samples are selected per cycle for annotation and model retraining.

## Results

On TNBC (the primary pathology dataset), AcZeroTS achieves 54.1% hIoU at cycle 6 with 1 unseen class, surpassing the best baseline RIPU (49.3%) by 4.8 points; with 3 unseen classes it reaches 38.9% vs. S4AL's 36.7%. On HPBC, AcZeroTS attains 42.3% hIoU at C6, outperforming the next-best S4AL (39.7%) by 2.6 points. On Pascal VOC 2012, AcZeroTS reaches 86.9% hIoU at C6, exceeding S4AL (84.7%) by 2.2 points. The underlying ProZS model also outperforms the best VLM-based ZSS method TagCLIP by 3.9% hIoU on TNBC (1 unseen) and 2.6% on HPBC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AcZeroTS consistently outperforms all eight active learning baselines (Random, Entropy, Coreset, DEAL, MADA, RIPU, S4AL, PBAL) across all three datasets and all unseen-class settings. On TNBC with 1 unseen class at C6, AcZeroTS (54.1%) leads RIPU (49.3%) by 4.8 points and MADA (48.5%) by 5.6 points; the gap narrows slightly with 3 unseen classes where AcZeroTS (38.9%) still beats S4AL (36.7%) by 2.2 points. On HPBC, AcZeroTS (42.3% at C6) surpasses S4AL (39.7%) and Coreset (39.5%) by roughly 2.6–2.8 points, with the largest margin appearing in early cycles (C1: 33.2% vs. 23.6% for S4AL). On Pascal VOC 2012, AcZeroTS (86.9% at C6) edges out S4AL (84.7%) and Coreset (83.4%) by 2.2 and 3.5 points respectively. For the underlying ProZS model (Table 4), it beats the strongest VLM-based ZSS baselines TagCLIP (51.7% hIoU) and ZegCLIP (49.7%) on TNBC 1-unseen by 3.9 and 5.9 points, and outperforms USE (50.5%) and SaLIP (42.2%) on the same setting, confirming that the prototype-guided architecture itself contributes to the overall gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: VLM-based zero-shot segmentation (ZegCLIP, TagCLIP, ZegFormer, SaLIP) and active learning for semantic segmentation (Entropy, Coreset, DEAL, MADA, RIPU, S4AL, PBAL). It departs from prior ZSS methods by introducing a prototype-guided architecture (ProZS) that uses a text-to-prototype projector to map PLIP text embeddings into a shared vision-semantic space, and from prior active learning methods by designing a query strategy (EUN + COT + CSA) that explicitly accounts for unseen-class generalization rather than optimizing only seen-class performance. The evidence-based uncertainty criterion borrows from subjective logic and Dirichlet distribution formulations, while the contrast criterion leverages KL divergence between pixel feature neighborhoods—both repurposed from classification and domain adaptation contexts into the pixel-level ZSS setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: pathology annotation is expensive and the tumor microenvironment contains many tissue types that are difficult to label exhaustively, making a data-efficient zero-shot approach clinically relevant. The CSA criterion—selecting samples far from all category prototypes to improve their summarization ability—is a conceptually clean mechanism for linking sample selection to unseen-class performance, and its consistent contribution across datasets suggests it captures a real signal. However, the evaluation is limited to three datasets with at most 3 unseen classes, and the method's dependence on PLIP/CLIP text encoders and GPT-3-generated descriptions raises open questions about robustness to domain shift, very large class spaces, and settings where text descriptions are unreliable. Whether the three-criteria combination generalizes beyond the tested pathology and natural-image domains remains to be seen.
