## Problem

Acquiring 3D articulated object data is prohibitively expensive (CAD modeling costs >$100 and 2+ hours per model, or requires costly scanning), and comprehensive annotations for diverse tasks (affordance, part pose, semantics) are extremely labor-intensive to produce manually. Existing datasets like PartNet-Mobility offer only ~50 objects per category, and conventional data augmentation methods cannot assign new labels to augmented samples, limiting them to specific tasks. This data scarcity fundamentally constrains the performance of deep learning models across articulated object understanding tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an existing articulated object's point cloud (uniformly sampled from visible surfaces), its hand-annotated structure program (parameterized geometric primitives with connectivity constraints), and the point-wise displacement field mapping structure surface points to the actual object surface. It produces new point clouds (2048 or 16384 points) with analytically aligned annotations: per-point part segmentation labels, 7-DoF part poses (scale, rotation, translation), affordance regions defined as mathematical functions on primitive parameters, and geometric detail displacements stored relative to local point normals for coordinate-invariant transfer.

## Method

Arti-PG represents each object as a program of parameterized geometric primitives (elementary templates like cuboids and cylinders, plus advanced composite templates capturing hierarchical regularities) connected by binary DoF-reducing constraints, with micro geometric details encoded as point-wise displacements inspired by Basis Point Sets. New objects are synthesized by applying three procedural manipulation rules in sequence—Advanced Primitive Alteration (replacing composite templates), Discrete Parameter Alteration (changing repetition counts), and Continuous Parameter Alteration (perturbing sizes and positions)—with an exception handler that detects and resolves primitive collisions. Geometric details are migrated to the new structure by analytically mapping points between old and new primitives (using symmetry-based completion for newly visible surfaces and map-projection-inspired techniques for cross-template correspondences), with displacements stored relative to point normals to handle orientation changes. Annotations are defined as closed-form mathematical functions on primitive parameters, so they automatically and exactly propagate to any synthesized variant without additional labeling effort.

## Results

On part segmentation, Arti-PG-enhanced PointTransformer improves mAcc from 89.5% to 91.3% (+1.8) and mIoU from 74.5% to 79.4% (+4.9) over the baseline. For part pose estimation, the 3D mIoU rises from 44.1% to 48.3% (+4.2) and translation error drops from 0.043 cm to 0.039 cm. Point cloud completion CD decreases from 11.3×10⁻⁴ cm to 10.4×10⁻⁴ cm. In manipulation, the largest gain is Pull-Where2Explore, whose success rate jumps from 9.3% to 11.9% (+2.6), while Push-Where2Explore improves from 25.9% to 32.8% (+6.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Arti-PG is a data synthesis toolbox rather than a competing model, so all comparisons measure the gain from augmenting training with Arti-PG-generated objects and annotations. Against the data augmentation baseline PointWOLF [11], Arti-PG achieves substantially larger segmentation gains (mIoU 79.4% vs. 75.8%) and, critically, extends to part pose estimation and completion where PointWOLF is not applicable. For manipulation, Where2Act [19] improves by +5.0% push and +1.6% pull success rate; Where2Explore [20] gains +6.9% push and +2.6% pull; and GAPartNet [5] gains +6.9% push and +3.6% pull. The largest relative improvement appears in Pull-Where2Explore (9.3%→11.9%, a 28% relative increase), which the authors attribute to the high-quality affordance labels that Arti-PG provides analytically, avoiding the noisy simulation-based affordance estimation used by the original Where2Explore pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

Arti-PG repurposes procedural generation (originally from game content creation) for 3D articulated object synthesis, combining it with BPS-style point correspondence for geometric detail transfer and a novel 'structure program' representation inspired by recognition-by-components theory in visual cognition. It departs from data augmentation methods (PointMixup, PointWOLF) that can increase shape diversity but cannot assign new labels, by enabling analytic label alignment through mathematical descriptions defined on the structure program. The approach also contrasts with manual CAD design or real-world scanning for articulated object acquisition, offering an automated alternative that simultaneously produces objects and their annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Arti-PG addresses a genuine bottleneck in articulated object research—data scarcity compounded by the difficulty of multi-task annotation—by providing a single-line-of-code toolbox that generates objects with unlimited shape variations and automatically aligned labels across vision and manipulation tasks. The 28% improvement in Pull-Where2Explore success rate and consistent gains across four diverse tasks demonstrate practical utility. Limitations include coverage of only 26 categories, dependence on initial structure programs being manually annotated, and the assumption that geometric details can be faithfully migrated between structurally similar primitives. Open questions include whether the approach scales to open-vocabulary categories, how well synthesized objects transfer to real-world (non-simulated) manipulation, and whether the structure program abstraction generalizes beyond the hand-designed primitive templates.
