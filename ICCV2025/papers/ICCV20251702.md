## Problem

Parameter-efficient LoRA variants like VeRA and LoRA-XS reduce trainable parameters to under 1M but still suffer 1–5% accuracy drops relative to standard LoRA. The paper identifies two root causes: (1) initialization methods ignore activation outliers in large foundation models, leading to large SVD decomposition errors, and (2) the design space of trainable structures (serial vs. parallel, placement, nested vs. adapter) has never been systematically explored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251702/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

NoRA is applied to frozen pre-trained backbones (CLIP ViT-B/16, LLaVA-1.5-7B, Mistral-7B, SDXL) and only trains 0.92M–3.92M parameters. For few-shot classification it consumes 4 or 16 RGB images per class and outputs a class label; for instruction tuning it takes an image plus a text question (or a pure text math problem) and produces a free-form text answer; for subject-driven generation it takes a reference image and a text prompt and outputs a 1024×1024 generated image via 50 diffusion steps. The method is modality-agnostic at the adapter level—the same nested LoRA module is inserted into projection layers regardless of whether the backbone processes vision, language, or both.

## Method

NoRA replaces the single trainable LoRA pair with a nested serial structure: an outer LoRA (A, B) initialized by Activation-aware SVD (AwSVD) and frozen, plus an inner LoRA (A′, B′) initialized from a Gaussian and trained in serial, giving the forward pass h = Wx + BB′A′Ax. AwSVD first scales the pre-trained weight matrix W by the diagonal of activation standard deviations (S = √diag(XᵀX)), performs truncated SVD on the scaled matrix, then compensates by multiplying A with S⁻¹, which reduces decomposition error by aligning the low-rank approximation with the directions that actually carry signal. Empirical ablations establish three design guidelines—serial beats parallel, the w_a position beats w_b, and nested LoRA beats a single adapter—and theoretical results provide a spectral-norm error bound (σ_{r+1}·κ(S)), a gradient-norm amplification analysis, and a magnitude/direction decomposition showing how the inner matrices modulate the frozen outer SVD components. With only r·r′ + r′·r trainable parameters per layer, NoRA matches or exceeds full LoRA accuracy while training ~40% faster and using less peak GPU memory.

## Results

On visual few-shot classification (ViT-B/16, 5 datasets), NoRA achieves 80.9% average top-1 accuracy at 4-shot and 86.1% at 16-shot, outperforming the strongest baselines DyLoRA (80.5%/84.9%) and AdaLoRA (80.5%/85.0%). On visual instruction tuning (LLaVA-1.5-7B), NoRA reaches 73.88 average across VQAv2, SQA, TextVQA, and POPE with only 3.92M trainable parameters, surpassing LoRA-XS (69.94) by 3.94 points. On mathematical reasoning (Mistral-7B), NoRA with 0.92M parameters achieves 44.27 average on GSM8K/MATH, exceeding LoRA (43.69) and LoRA-XS (42.94) while using ~183× fewer parameters than full fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NoRA outperforms all 17 baselines on the 4-shot few-shot average (80.9%), with the closest competitors being DyLoRA and AdaLoRA at 80.5% (a 0.4-point margin) and LoRA-FA at 80.3%. Among prompt-based methods (CoOp, CoCoOp, PLOT++, KgCoOp, MaPLe, ProGrad), NoRA leads by 2.7–5.0 points on average, with the largest gap over CLIP-Adapter (72.3%). Among LoRA variants, NoRA surpasses VeRA (80.1%) by 0.8 points and CLIP-LoRA (79.2%) by 1.7 points, while using far fewer trainable parameters (0.92M vs. 168M for standard LoRA). On instruction tuning (Table 3), NoRA (73.88) beats LoRA-XS (69.94) by 3.94 points and DoRA (73.18) by 0.7 points at the same 3.92M parameter budget. On mathematical reasoning (Table 4), NoRA with 0.92M parameters (44.27 avg) exceeds LoRA (43.69, 168M params) by 0.58 points and VeRA (40.73, 0.98M params) by 3.54 points, demonstrating that the nested structure recovers the 1–5% performance gap typically observed in parameter-efficient LoRA variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

NoRA extends the parameter-efficient LoRA lineage initiated by VeRA (frozen random matrices + trainable vectors) and LoRA-XS (frozen SVD-decomposed matrices + adapter), replacing their minimal trainable components with a nested serial LoRA structure. It departs from SVD-based initialization methods like AdaLoRA and PiSSA by introducing AwSVD, which incorporates activation statistics to mitigate decomposition errors caused by activation outliers in large models. The paper's unified design space framework systematically ablates initialization strategies, trainable structure types (adapter vs. serial vs. parallel LoRA), and placement choices (w_a vs. w_b), providing empirical guidelines that prior parameter-efficient LoRA variants had not explicitly addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NoRA demonstrates that ~0.92M trainable parameters can match or exceed full LoRA (168M) across diverse tasks (few-shot vision, instruction tuning, math reasoning, image generation), offering a practical ~180× parameter reduction for resource-constrained fine-tuning. The AwSVD initialization and the finding that serial nested LoRA consistently outperforms parallel and adapter alternatives provide actionable design principles for the broader PEFT community. However, the authors acknowledge that optimal hyperparameter configurations (r, r′, number of nested layers) may be task- and model-dependent, and the improvements over VeRA/LoRA-XS, while consistent, remain in the 1–3% range on most benchmarks. Open directions include integration with AutoML for automatic rank allocation, distillation pipelines, and extension to MoE architectures.
