## Problem

Radiology report generation suffers from two intertwined challenges: disease class imbalance causes models to overlook rare (tail) findings, and weak image-report-level supervision prevents fine-grained region-sentence alignment. Existing retrieval-based approaches rely on generic CLIP features without medical domain knowledge, yielding imprecise reference reports and introducing irrelevant or incorrect textual knowledge into the generation process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251706/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 chest X-ray image and a reference report database as input. The image is encoded by a ResNet-101 backbone, while MedKLIP produces entity-specific logits over 75 predefined anatomical entities and CheXbert-derived 72-dimensional status vectors (18 diseases × 4 statuses) used for retrieval supervision. The output is a free-form radiology report generated autoregressively by a transformer decoder conditioned on fused visual-text features and auxiliary disease-classification prompts.

## Method

REVTAF combines two modules. The Learnable Retrieval Enhancer (LRE) projects MedKLIP entity logits into a Poincaré-ball hyperbolic space via a Hyperbolic Neural Network and trains a ranking-based metric (cross-entropy over nearest-neighbor indices) supervised by Hamming distances between CheXbert status vectors, enabling adaptive retrieval of the most relevant reference report as Global Reference Prompts (GRPs) that particularly benefit tail-class inputs. The Fine-grained Visual-Text Alignment and Fusion (FVTAF) module fuses visual features with both GRPs and entity-specific Local Reference Prompts (LRPs) using MultiPrompts Sinkhorn Attention (MPSA), an optimal-transport-based cross-attention that reweights query-key interactions with global context to filter irrelevant text. A novel Fine-grained Cross-modal Consistency (FCC) loss penalizes discrepancies between the cosine semantic similarity of GRP sentences and LRP entities and the IoU of their corresponding cross-attention maps, enforcing spatial alignment under weak supervision. The total objective combines the standard cross-entropy generation loss with the ranking loss (α=2) and FCC loss (β=0.5), trained end-to-end for 6 epochs with AdamW.

## Results

On MIMIC-CXR, REVTAF achieves an average score of 0.397 across all NLG and CE metrics, outperforming the second-best method EKAGen (0.323) by 7.4% on average. Key individual metrics include BLEU-1 of 0.465, METEOR of 0.199, and CheXbert F1 of 0.592, surpassing EKAGen by 4.6%, 4.2%, and 9.3% respectively. On IU X-Ray, the method achieves an average of 0.251, improving over the runner-up PromptMRG (0.222) by 2.9%, with the largest gap in CE F1 (0.273 vs. 0.211, a 6.3% gain). Inference time is 3.36 s per report, substantially faster than GPT-4 (15.02 s) while achieving higher scores on every metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REVTAF outperforms all 14 compared baselines on every reported metric for MIMIC-CXR. The closest competitor is EKAGen (CVPR 2024), which REVTAF surpasses by 4.6% in BLEU-1 (0.465 vs. 0.419), 4.2% in METEOR (0.199 vs. 0.157), and 9.3% in CheXbert F1 (0.592 vs. 0.499). Among earlier methods, PromptMRG (AAAI 2024) and RGRG (CVPR 2023) trail by 6.4% and 6.3% in average score respectively, with the gap widening substantially on CE metrics (F1: 0.592 vs. 0.476 and 0.447). On IU X-Ray, REVTAF achieves the best CE F1 (0.273) and ROUGE (0.309), beating PromptMRG by 6.3% in CE F1 and 1.2% in mean NLG, though it is marginally behind PromptMRG on BLEU-3 (0.159 vs. 0.160). Against GPT-4-series multimodal LLMs on a 16-sample subset, REVTAF leads GPT-4 by 15.5% in average score (0.347 vs. 0.192) while running 4.5× faster (3.36 s vs. 15.02 s per report).

## Relation to Prior Work

REVTAF builds directly on PromptMRG [13], adopting its MedKLIP backbone and auxiliary long-tailed classification branch, but replaces the fixed CLIP-similarity retrieval with a learnable hyperbolic-space ranking metric (LRE) supervised by CheXbert label hashing distances. The fine-grained fusion module borrows the MultiPrompts Sinkhorn Attention (MPSA) from OTSeg [40] (a zero-shot segmentation method) and repurposes it for cross-modal report generation, while the Fine-grained Cross-modal Consistency (FCC) loss is a novel alignment constraint not present in prior radiology report generation work. Conceptually, the paper sits at the intersection of hyperbolic representation learning (previously applied to social networks and knowledge graphs) and optimal transport (previously applied to segmentation and domain adaptation), applying both to the medical imaging report generation setting for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses two persistent and practically important problems in radiology report generation—tail-class disease underrepresentation and the absence of region-sentence level annotations—without requiring new annotation effort, making it deployable with existing datasets. The 7.4% average improvement on MIMIC-CXR over the strongest prior method (EKAGen) is a meaningful margin for a task where gains have been incremental. However, the comparison with GPT-series multimodal LLMs rests on only 16 randomly sampled test images, which is too thin to draw firm conclusions about the method's advantage over increasingly capable general-purpose models. Open questions include whether the hyperbolic retrieval generalizes beyond chest X-rays to other modalities (CT, MRI), whether the FCC constraint remains effective when the number of entity classes grows substantially, and whether the added complexity (hyperbolic HNN + MPSA + FCC) is justified in resource-constrained clinical deployment settings.
