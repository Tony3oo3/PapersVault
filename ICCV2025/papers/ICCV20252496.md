## Problem

It remains uncertain whether 3D reassembly models trained on physics-simulated synthetic fractures (e.g., Breaking Bad) can generalize to real-world fractures with more complex, irregular breakage patterns. Existing SOTA methods like PuzzleFusion++ degrade significantly on unseen object shapes and artifact subsets, and no prior dataset captures the diversity of real fracture types (random breakage, incomplete ossification, conchoidal flintknapping) alongside common real-world complications such as missing or extraneous fragments.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-fragment 3D point clouds (sampled via Poisson disk sampling weighted by surface area), along with per-point normals and scale information, which are encoded as pose-invariant shape priors. The network outputs a 6-DoF rigid pose (rotation in SO(3) parameterized via exponential/logarithmic maps, and translation in R³) for each fragment, iteratively refined through flow matching steps. At inference, the two-session scheme first produces a coarse one-step pose initialization before a standard 20-step refinement, with only a ~5% increase in computational cost over single-session inference.

## Method

GARF decouples local fracture understanding from global alignment: a PTv3 backbone pretrained on 1.9M fragments (14× more than prior work) learns fracture-point segmentation via Dice loss, producing fragment-level features that generalize to unseen shapes and handle missing/extraneous pieces. A transformer denoiser with FlashAttention-based variable-length self-attention (weighted toward large fragments) and global cross-fragment attention predicts the SE(3) vector field, with rotation and translation flows decoupled onto SO(3) geodesics and R³ linear paths respectively. A multi-anchor training strategy randomly fixes k fragments to identity poses and enforces zero vector fields on them, broadening the probability-path distribution beyond the single-anchor approach of prior work. At inference, two-session flow matching performs a one-step coarse pose initialization followed by multi-step refinement, and LoRA adapters (rank 128) on the final Transformer block enable domain-specific fine-tuning with as few as 5–10 objects.

## Results

On the Breaking Bad Everyday subset, GARF achieves 6.10° RMSE(R), 1.22×10⁻² RMSE(T), 95.33% part accuracy (PA), and 0.22×10⁻³ chamfer distance (CD), approaching the theoretical maximum PA of 96.49%. On the unseen Artifact subset, GARF maintains 5.82° RMSE(R) and 95.04% PA, while the previous SOTA (PF++) degrades to 47.03° RMSE(R) and 57.97% PA. On the FRACTURA synthetic fracture subset with unseen domain-specific objects, GARF achieves 19.63° RMSE(R) and 83.41% PA, outperforming all baselines. The two-session flow matching and fracture-aware pretraining contribute incremental gains of ~1.2° RMSE(R) and ~1.2% PA over the single-session baseline in ablation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GARF decisively outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest prior method PF++ [50], GARF reduces rotation error by 82.87% (35.61° → 6.10°) and translation error by 79.83% (6.05 → 1.22×10⁻²) on Breaking Bad Everyday, while raising PA from 76.17% to 95.33%. The gap widens dramatically on the Artifact subset, where PF++ degrades to 47.03° RMSE(R) and 57.97% PA while GARF holds at 5.82° and 95.04%, confirming that PF++'s reliance on global geometry fails on unseen shapes. Jigsaw [30] and PMTR [21] are intermediate baselines (42.19° and 31.57° RMSE(R) on Everyday), both far behind GARF. The earliest baselines—Global [22] and DGL [59]—achieve only ~28–32% PA with >80° rotation error, rendering them impractical. Notably, even GARF-mini (trained only on the Everyday subset) generalizes to the Artifact subset and FRACTURA synthetic fractures without fine-tuning, whereas PF++ and Jigsaw suffer severe degradation on unseen object shapes.

## Relation to Prior Work

GARF departs from the dominant diffusion-based paradigm in 3D fracture assembly (PuzzleFusion++, DiffAssemble) by introducing flow matching on the SE(3) manifold, leveraging SO(3) geodesics for rotation estimation. It also shifts the feature-learning philosophy: rather than jointly learning global and local geometry (Jigsaw, PF++), GARF decouples local fracture-feature extraction via large-scale fracture-aware pretraining from global pose alignment, drawing on the large-scale pretraining paradigm from NLP and vision. The multi-anchor training strategy and two-session inference at test time are novel design choices not present in prior reassembly work, and the LoRA-based fine-tuning for domain adaptation is the first such application in this domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic evidence that models trained on synthetic fracture data can generalize to real-world fractures across diverse scientific domains (ceramics, bones, eggshells, lithics), addressing a fundamental open question in the field. FRACTURA fills a critical gap as the first multi-domain, multi-fracture-type benchmark with real-world scans, enabling controlled study of how object shape, fracture type, and missing/extraneous fragments affect reassembly. Key limitations remain: high-ambiguity fractures (lithic refitting, eroded fresco surfaces) still defeat current methods, and the dataset is relatively small (53K fragments). Future directions include multimodal (texture + geometry) reassembly, test-time policy optimization with expert feedback, and scaling FRACTURA.
