## Problem

State-of-the-art diffusion models (e.g., FLUX, SDXL) frequently overlook or misinterpret parts of text prompts, producing images that fail to match user intent. Existing alignment solutions either require large-scale preference finetuning data that is difficult to scale, or rely on local editing operations that degrade overall image quality. No prior method can improve prompt-image alignment without additional training data or explicit editing while preserving generation quality.

## Input / Output

IMG takes a single generated image (at the native resolution of the base diffusion model, e.g., 1024×1024 for SDXL/FLUX) and its corresponding text prompt as input. It produces a re-generated image of the same resolution with improved prompt adherence. The pipeline is plug-and-play: the Implicit Aligner is trained once per base model (using the model's IP-Adapter encoder) and can be applied at inference time without modifying the diffusion model weights, and it generalizes across different MLLMs (LLaVA, Qwen-VL) as the guidance source.

## Method

IMG is a re-generation-based alignment framework with three components. First, a finetuned MLLM (LLaVA 1.5-13b, trained on Instruct-Pix2Pix triplets) analyzes the generated image against the prompt and outputs hidden states encoding detected misalignments. Second, an Implicit Aligner—a stack of cross-attention layers—takes these MLLM guidance features and IP-Adapter-encoded image features of the initial image, producing refined conditioning features that steer the diffusion model toward better alignment. Third, the Implicit Aligner is trained from scratch using an Iteratively Updated Preference Objective that combines a basic L2 fitting loss with a DPO+SPIN preference loss, where the reference network is not frozen but updated at runtime whenever the current model outperforms it for k consecutive iterations. Unlike editing-based methods that modify local regions, IMG re-runs the full diffusion sampling process with the refined features, preserving global image quality while correcting misalignments.

## Results

On the Human Preference Datasets (HPD) benchmark, IMG improves average HPS scores across all three base models: SDXL from 28.03 to 28.56 (87.2% win rate), SDXL-DPO from 28.39 to 28.79 (82.6% win rate), and FLUX from 29.21 to 29.48 (65.7% win rate). On Parti-Prompts, gains are consistent (e.g., FLUX 29.23→29.61). On T2I-CompBench, SDXL-DPO+IMG achieves the best Complex score (0.3874) and FLUX+IMG leads all categories (e.g., Color 0.8060, Complex 0.3983). In user studies with 33 evaluators, approximately 70% of participants prefer IMG's re-aligned images over the originals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base diffusion models (SDXL, SDXL-DPO, FLUX), IMG consistently improves HPS scores with win rates of 87.2%, 82.6%, and 65.7% respectively, demonstrating it functions as a general plug-and-play adapter. Against the finetuning-based method SDXL-DPO [62], IMG not only outperforms it when applied to SDXL (28.56 vs 28.39 avg HPS) but also stacks on top of SDXL-DPO for further gains (28.79), showing complementarity. Against the editing-based method SLD [65], IMG wins decisively: SLD actually degrades HPS scores (e.g., FLUX drops from 29.21 to 28.14), while IMG improves them (29.21→29.48), confirming that local editing compromises overall quality. On T2I-CompBench, SDXL-DPO+IMG surpasses both ELLA [24] (which lacks a Complex score) and CoMat [31] (0.3680 Complex) with 0.3874, and FLUX+IMG leads all compositional categories. InstructPix2Pix [6] is shown qualitatively to produce incorrect edits (e.g., wrong object placement), whereas IMG's re-generation approach avoids such failures. Overall, IMG outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

IMG departs from two dominant paradigms for diffusion alignment: finetuning-based methods (Diffusion-DPO, DDPO) that require large preference datasets to update model weights, and editing-based methods (SLD, InstructPix2Pix) that locally modify generated images. Instead, it introduces a re-generation approach where an MLLM identifies misalignments and a lightweight cross-attention adapter (the Implicit Aligner) refines IP-Adapter conditioning features, leveraging the native diffusion sampling loop. The training objective is a novel fusion of DPO and SPIN into an 'Iteratively Updated Preference Objective' where the reference network is updated at runtime rather than frozen, distinguishing it from prior preference-learning formulations for diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IMG's plug-and-play design—trained once on SDXL's IP-Adapter and directly transferred to FLUX without retraining—makes it a practical drop-in for any IP-Adapter-compatible diffusion model, and it complements rather than replaces finetuning-based alignment (SDXL-DPO+IMG outperforms either alone). A notable finding is that editing-based alignment (SLD) actually degrades overall HPS scores (e.g., 29.21→28.14 on FLUX), challenging the assumption that targeted local edits preserve global quality. Open questions include whether the 76.3% MLLM detection accuracy is a bottleneck for very complex prompts, the computational cost of multi-round re-generation, and whether the approach generalizes to video or 3D generation.
