## Problem

DUSt3R and similar Transformer-based 3D reconstruction models assume static scenes and degrade significantly when dynamic objects violate epipolar geometry constraints. Existing dynamic extensions (MonST3R, CUT3R, DAS3R) require costly fine-tuning on large-scale 4D datasets or rely on auxiliary geometric priors like optical flow and depth estimators, limiting generalization. This paper asks whether the attention mechanisms already learned by DUSt3R can be directly exploited to disentangle camera and object motion without any additional training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a casually captured RGB video sequence and processes it with a sliding temporal window of size n, inferring DUSt3R (or MonST3R) pairwise within each window. It produces three outputs: per-frame binary dynamic object segmentation masks, a globally aligned camera pose sequence (extrinsics and intrinsics), and a 4D dense point cloud consisting of a temporally consistent static scene plus per-frame dynamic point clouds. The approach is plug-and-play on pre-trained DUSt3R/MonST3R weights, requires no fine-tuning, and optionally incorporates optical flow for segmentation-aware global alignment.

## Method

The core insight is that DUSt3R's cross-attention layers implicitly assign low attention to tokens violating rigid-body epipolar constraints, thereby encoding information about dynamic objects, textureless regions, and under-observed areas. The method aggregates cross-attention maps spatially (averaging across layers and query dimensions) and temporally (computing mean and standard deviation across all pairs in the sliding window where a frame serves as source or reference). Dynamic object segmentation is extracted via an element-wise product formula combining the inverted mean source attention, source attention standard deviation, mean reference attention, and inverted reference attention standard deviation, which isolates object motion from camera motion and textureless artifacts. A second inference pass then applies attention re-weighting by zeroing cross-attention values for dynamic regions in the reference-view decoder only, preventing dynamic tokens from corrupting static reconstruction while preserving the static reference frame. Finally, globally aligned point maps are optimized with an optional flow-consistency loss restricted to static regions, yielding robust 4D reconstruction at minimal additional computational cost.

## Results

On the DyCheck 4D reconstruction benchmark, Easi3R_dust3r (with flow) achieves the best Distance (0.301 mean / 0.186 median) and Completeness (1.474 mean / 0.586 median), outperforming CUT3R (0.326 / 0.229 distance) and MonST3R (0.353 / 0.254 distance). For camera pose estimation, Easi3R_dust3r attains the lowest ATE on DyCheck (0.021) and TUM-dynamics (0.070), and the lowest RTE on DyCheck (0.014) and ADT (0.015). On dynamic object segmentation (DAVIS-16, w/o SAM2), Easi3R_monst3r reaches 57.7 JM / 71.6 JR, substantially surpassing DAS3R (41.6 / 39.0) and MonST3R (40.9 / 42.2) without any flow input or segmentation training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Easi3R_dust3r (with flow) achieves the best overall 4D reconstruction on DyCheck, beating CUT3R by 7.4% in mean distance (0.301 vs. 0.326) and 26.4% in median distance (0.186 vs. 0.229), while also improving completeness by 9.8% over CUT3R. Against MonST3R (with flow), Easi3R_dust3r reduces mean distance by 14.7% (0.301 vs. 0.353) and median distance by 26.4% (0.186 vs. 0.254). For camera pose, Easi3R_dust3r outperforms CUT3R on DyCheck ATE (0.021 vs. 0.029) and TUM-dynamics ATE (0.070 vs. 0.079), though CUT3R retains an edge on ADT RRE (0.490 vs. 0.655). On dynamic segmentation, Easi3R_monst3r surpasses DAS3R by 16.1 JM on DAVIS-16 (57.7 vs. 41.6) and by 13.1 JM on DAVIS-17 (56.5 vs. 43.5), all without optical flow or segmentation-specific training. Notably, Easi3R requires no fine-tuning on dynamic datasets, whereas CUT3R, MonST3R, and DAS3R all rely on extensive dynamic training data or flow-based supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

Easi3R builds directly on DUSt3R's Transformer-based pose-free reconstruction architecture and its dynamic extension MonST3R, but takes a fundamentally different path from concurrent work (CUT3R, DAS3R, MonST3R) that fine-tunes these models on large-scale dynamic datasets with optical flow supervision. Instead of learning from 4D data, the paper repurposes the cross-attention maps already present in a pre-trained 3D model, showing that spatial and temporal aggregation of these maps (mean, standard deviation, element-wise product) yields dynamic object segmentation without any additional training. This positions Easi3R as a training-free, plug-and-play inference-time adaptation rather than a new model architecture, drawing on the attention re-weighting idea from prompt-to-prompt editing [17] applied to geometric reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that a model trained exclusively on static 3D scenes implicitly encodes motion information in its attention layers, extractable with simple arithmetic—opens a broader question about what geometric and dynamic knowledge is latent in pre-trained reconstruction models. Practically, this means any future DUSt3R-family improvement automatically benefits from Easi3R's dynamic handling without retraining, which is valuable given the scarcity of 4D training data. Limitations include the need for a second inference pass (doubling compute), dependence on a hand-tuned threshold α for segmentation, and evaluation limited to relatively short sequences (~40 frames) with moderate dynamic content; generalization to highly non-rigid or articulated motion remains untested.
