## Problem

Existing wide-baseline panorama reconstruction methods (PanoGRF, Splatter-360, PanSplat) depend on accurate camera poses, which are costly to obtain and highly noise-sensitive in real-world capture. Additionally, the domain gap between perspective-pretrained foundational models and equirectangular panoramic imagery creates a barrier to transferring the strong generalization learned from millions of perspective training samples to the panoramic setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252685/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two equirectangular panorama images at 512×1024 resolution with no pose information as input. It outputs a unified 3D Gaussian Splatting representation (using 2DGS) parameterized per-pixel by center location, opacity, rotation, scaling, and spherical-harmonic color, from which photorealistic novel views and depth maps can be rendered at arbitrary viewpoints. A post-hoc PnP-based uniform scaling resolves the inherent scale ambiguity of unposed two-view geometry.

## Method

PanoSplatt3R builds on the Dust3R/MASt3R ViT encoder–cross-attention decoder architecture and initializes from MASt3R's perspective-pretrained weights, transferring generalization to the panoramic domain. The key architectural contribution is RoPE rolling: for each of M attention heads, the x-coordinate in rotary positional embeddings is shifted by Wm/M (mod W), so that different heads perceive different horizontal offsets, collectively modeling the 360° periodicity of panoramas while preserving RoPE's relative-position mechanism for seamless weight transfer. A DPT head predicts 2D Gaussian parameters per view, and the two views' Gaussians are fused by concatenation in a shared coordinate frame. Training is progressive: Stage 1 (50k steps) supervises only Gaussian centers with L1+L2 loss against depth-projected ground truth, then Stage 2 (100k steps) optimizes all parameters using cubemap-projected photometric (MSE + LPIPS) and depth (L2) losses at a novel viewpoint.

## Results

On HM3D, PanoSplatt3R achieves 28.94 PSNR, 0.869 SSIM, and 0.147 LPIPS for novel view synthesis, and 0.044 AbsRel, 0.147 RMSE, and 97.65% δ₁ for depth accuracy—all without any camera pose input. On Replica, it reaches 31.52 PSNR, 0.935 SSIM, 0.082 LPIPS, 0.034 AbsRel, 0.126 RMSE, and 98.12% δ₁. These results surpass the best pose-dependent 360° method (Splatter-360: 28.31/29.89 PSNR) and all posed perspective baselines by substantial margins, demonstrating that unposed panorama reconstruction can exceed pose-dependent approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PanoSplatt3R outperforms the strongest posed 360° baseline, Splatter-360 [10], by +0.63 dB PSNR on HM3D and +1.63 dB on Replica, while also improving depth accuracy (AbsRel 0.044 vs 0.078 on HM3D; 0.034 vs 0.063 on Replica) and δ₁ by ~4 percentage points—all without requiring camera poses. Against PanoGRF [8] and MVSplat† [7], the gains are even larger (e.g., +3.08 dB PSNR over PanoGRF on HM3D). All posed perspective methods (HiSplat, MVSplat, DepthSplat) are far behind, with DepthSplat [64] the closest at only 18.49 PSNR on HM3D versus PanoSplatt3R's 28.94. In the unposed pipeline comparison (Table 3), feeding SIFT+8PA-estimated poses into posed methods causes severe degradation (e.g., Splatter-360 drops to 14.77 PSNR), while PanoSplatt3R remains unaffected at 28.94. Against fixed-baseline methods (Table 4), PanoSplatt3R is competitive with PanSplat [72] on narrow baselines (~0.3 m) and outperforms Splatter-360 trained on the same data by a large margin, though it trails PanSplat on the 1.0 m Matterport3D setting (27.47 vs 30.01 WS-PSNR) where the aligned-axis assumption simplifies the task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unposed two-view reconstruction paradigm of Dust3R/MASt3R from the perspective domain to the panoramic domain, where existing methods (PanoGRF, Splatter-360, PanSplat) all rely on accurate camera poses and domain-specific cost volumes. The key conceptual departure is a transfer-learning strategy that reuses perspective-domain pretraining weights rather than training from scratch on limited panoramic data, combined with a minimal RoPE rolling modification to encode the horizontal periodicity of equirectangular images. For rendering, it adopts 2D Gaussian Splatting with a DPT head, following the broader trend of explicit Gaussian-based NVS methods (pixelSplat, MVSplat, Splatter-360) but applied in an unposed panoramic setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the pose requirement, PanoSplatt3R makes wide-baseline panorama reconstruction practical in real-world settings where precise relative poses are noisy or unavailable, and the large performance gap over pose-dependent methods fed with estimated poses (Table 3) underscores how fragile existing pipelines are. The RoPE rolling technique is a lightweight, generalizable modification that could benefit other transformer-based panoramic models. Open questions include whether the approach extends beyond two views, how it performs on real (non-synthetic) panoramic captures, and whether the scale-ambiguity workaround via PnP remains robust in degenerate or texture-poor scenes.
