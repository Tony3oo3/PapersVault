## Problem

Existing multimodal event-based action recognition methods rely on paired RGB-Event data, introducing additional sensor costs and undermining the inherent privacy-preserving property of event cameras. Moreover, event streams are spatially sparse and edge-dominant, lacking the texture information critical for action semantics, while independent encoders for each modality fail to exploit inter-modal correlations and introduce redundant feature interference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251983/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw asynchronous event stream E = {(u_i, p_i, t_i)} as sole input, with no paired RGB required. It internally generates two complementary representations: edge-dominant stacked frames (temporal aggregation) and texture-enriched reconstructed frames (via a lightweight FireNet with only 38k parameters, ~1ms latency at 240×180). The output is a single action class label obtained by contrasting the encoded event feature against CLIP text features derived from hand-crafted and learnable prompts over C action classes.

## Method

The EMP framework hierarchically exploits multimodal cues from raw event streams through two synergistic modules. First, the Cross-modal Frequency Enhancer (CFE) applies 3D FFT to both stacked and reconstructed frames, then uses a sigmoid-activated frequency filter derived from the stacked frames' spectrum to selectively amplify high-frequency edge dynamics in the reconstructed frames before inverse FFT, bridging the spectral gap between edge-dominant and texture-rich representations. Second, the High-frequency Guided Selector (HGS) deploys cross-attention between stacked and reconstructed tokens within CLIP's shared vision encoder, then computes cosine similarity between the stacked [cls] token and reconstructed patch tokens to adaptively select top-K semantically consistent tokens (K = μ·N, optimal μ=0.6 at layer 8), suppressing modality-specific redundancy. Training combines a modality consistency MSE loss between stacked and reconstructed features with a contrastive event-text recognition loss, all within a CLIP-based vision-language framework.

## Results

On the PAF dataset, EMP achieves 99.80% Top-1 accuracy, surpassing the previous best (EventCLIP+Recon at 96.55%) by 3.31 points. On SeAct, EMP reaches 75.00% Top-1, outperforming EventCrab (72.41%) by 2.59 points. On DVS128 Gesture, EMP attains 98.86% Top-1, matching ExACT and trailing SpikMamba (99.01%) by 0.15 points. On the large-scale HARDVS dataset (300 classes), EMP achieves 97.68% Top-1 with 182.1M parameters, dramatically outperforming the next-best event-only method ExACT (90.10%) and all RGB+Event fusion baselines (best: SSTFormer at 52.97%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMP outperforms all 11 baselines on PAF (99.80% vs. 96.55% for EventCLIP+Recon) and SeAct (75.00% vs. 72.41% for EventCrab), with margins of 3.31 and 2.59 points respectively. On DVS128 Gesture, EMP ties ExACT at 98.86% and falls 0.15 points short of SpikMamba (99.01%), the only dataset where it does not achieve the top score. On HARDVS, EMP's 97.68% Top-1 represents a 7.58-point gain over the strongest event-only baseline ExACT (90.10%) and a 44.71-point margin over the best RGB+Event fusion method SSTFormer (52.97%), demonstrating that the reconstructed-frame paradigm eliminates the need for paired RGB data while substantially exceeding multi-sensor fusion approaches. Point-based methods (Motion SNN, Slayer, SpikePoint) consistently underperform frame-based approaches on PAF and DVS128 Gesture, confirming that visual encoder architectures better exploit spatiotemporal structure. The closest competitor EventCrab [7] combines frame and point representations but still trails EMP by 3.31 points on PAF, suggesting that frequency-domain cross-modal enhancement and guided token selection provide more effective multi-modal exploitation than simple frame+point concatenation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CLIP-based event recognition paradigm established by EventBind [56] and ExACT [57], adopting their text-prompt formulation and vision encoder as the backbone. It departs from conventional RGB-Event multimodal methods (SAFE, SSTFormer) that require paired RGB acquisition, instead repurposing event-to-image reconstruction (FireNet) to generate a texture-enriched surrogate modality from the event stream alone. The frequency-domain cross-modal enhancement (CFE) and high-frequency-guided token selection (HGS) represent a novel architectural contribution that bridges the gap between edge-dominant stacked frames and texture-rich reconstructed frames within a unified CLIP encoder, rather than the independent-encoder-plus-late-fusion paradigm used by prior multimodal approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The privacy-preserving framing—eliminating RGB dependency while retaining multimodal benefits—addresses a practical deployment concern for event cameras in sensitive environments, and the 97.68% Top-1 on HARDVS (300 classes) demonstrates that a single event stream can rival or exceed RGB-Event fusion methods. However, the approach inherits limitations from the reconstruction network: FireNet's 38k parameters may not capture fine texture in complex scenes, and the method's reliance on a pre-trained CLIP encoder constrains adaptability to domain-specific action vocabularies. Open questions include whether the frequency-domain enhancement generalizes to higher-resolution or longer-duration event streams, and whether the token selection strategy remains effective when the modality gap widens (e.g., under occlusion or extreme lighting).
