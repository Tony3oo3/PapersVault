## Problem

Event cameras produce sparse asynchronous data when scenes are stationary or motion is minimal, making dense disparity estimation an ill-posed problem. Existing event-intensity fusion methods require both modalities at inference, sacrificing the low-latency advantage of events and doubling computational cost. This paper asks whether intensity image knowledge can be distilled into an event-only network during training so that inference requires only events while still producing dense, accurate disparities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250830/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the network consumes four inputs: left and right event streams encoded as voxel grids of shape B×H×W (discretized time bins × spatial dimensions) and corresponding left and right intensity images (256×256 random crops, max disparity 192). At inference, only the two event voxel grids are needed, and the network outputs a single dense disparity map of shape 1×H×W. The intensity branch is entirely discarded after training, so no additional inference cost is incurred.

## Method

The framework (IED²S) contains two identical stereo-matching branches—an event branch and a pre-trained intensity branch—connected by a three-level distillation pipeline. Shallow distillation uses GCBlock to align long-range relational features and spatial/channel attention maps to align focal texture details, avoiding the modality contamination that simple L2 feature alignment would cause. Deep distillation applies MSE on high-level task-related features, and logit-level distillation uses KL divergence to match the disparity probability distributions. An intensity-event joint left-right consistency (IE-LRC) module warps the left intensity image using the event branch's predicted disparity and enforces cross-view agreement via SSIM and edge-aware smoothness losses. The total training loss combines stereo matching (smooth L1), all distillation terms, and the LRC term; at inference only the event branch remains, preserving event-camera low latency.

## Results

On the MVSEC dataset (indoor flying), IED²S achieves a mean disparity error of 0.32 px (Split 1) and 0.37 px (Split 3), with one-pixel accuracy of 94.8% and 93.4%, and mean depth error of 11.2 cm and 13.2 cm, respectively. On the DSEC dataset (outdoor driving), it attains MAE of 0.493, 1PE of 8.823, 2PE of 2.134, and RMSE of 1.069. The method outperforms the strongest event-only baseline (TES) by 13.85% and 12.00% in mean depth error on MVSEC Split 1 and Split 3, and by 5.37% in MAE on DSEC, while requiring only event input at inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IED²S outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric across both datasets. Against the strongest event-only method TES [13], it reduces mean depth error by 13.85% (Split 1) and 12.00% (Split 3) on MVSEC, and lowers mean disparity error from 0.46/0.49 px to 0.32/0.37 px. On DSEC, it beats Se-CFF [38] by 5.37% in MAE (0.493 vs. 0.521) and 7.46% in 1PE (8.823 vs. 9.586). Compared to DTC-PDS [62], the improvement is 42.9% in mean disparity error on MVSEC Split 1 (0.32 vs. 0.56 px). EIS-E [37] and DDES [49] are substantially weaker, with EIS-E reporting only 80.6% one-pixel accuracy on Split 1. Notably, IED²S achieves these gains using only events at inference, whereas fusion-based methods like EIS-E require both events and intensity images simultaneously, sacrificing the low-latency advantage of event cameras.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: event-based stereo matching (DDES, DTC, TES) and cross-sensor knowledge distillation (BEVDistill, MonoDistill, ProtoTransfer). Prior event-based stereo methods either rely solely on sparse events (ill-posed for dense disparity) or fuse events with intensity images at inference, sacrificing the low-latency advantage of events. The key departure here is treating the intensity branch purely as a training-time teacher, distilling multi-level features (long-range via GCBlock, focal via spatial/channel attention, deep via MSE, logit via KL) into the event branch so that inference requires only events. The left-right consistency module adapts Godard et al.'s SSIM-based warping loss to a cross-modal setting where disparity comes from the event branch but warping operates on intensity images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: dense, accurate disparity from events alone at inference preserves the microsecond-level latency that makes event cameras attractive for high-speed and extreme-lighting scenarios, while avoiding the compute overhead of dual-modality fusion. The cross-domain generalization results (training on MVSEC, testing on DSEC and vice versa) suggest the distilled knowledge captures sensor-agnostic structural priors rather than dataset-specific patterns, which is encouraging for real-world deployment. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other sensor pairs (e.g., LiDAR-to-camera for depth), how sensitive performance is to the quality of the pre-trained intensity teacher, and whether the multi-level distillation can be simplified without significant performance loss for edge deployment.
