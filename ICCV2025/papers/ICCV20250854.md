## Problem

DiT-based video generation models are computationally expensive due to iterative denoising and quadratic attention over all latent tokens, yet they apply a uniform temporal resolution regardless of content. Real-world videos exhibit strong temporal non-uniformity—static segments contain highly redundant frames while high-motion segments demand fine-grained detail—making uniform compression wasteful and limiting practical deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250854/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces a 540P, 97-frame video by modifying the denoising pipeline of the pre-trained HunyuanVideo DiT model. Because different temporal segments are compressed at different latent frame rates, the decoded output has non-uniform temporal spacing that is restored to full frame rate via RIFE frame interpolation (taking <1 s). The entire pipeline is training-free and requires no architectural changes to the base model.

## Method

DLFR-Gen introduces a five-step compression module inserted mid-denoising: (1) a one-step denoise from a noisy latent at timestep k yields a preliminary clean latent; (2) a lightweight compression decoder (VAE decoder with spatial upsampling removed, >16× faster) projects it to a low-resolution video; (3) a dynamic frame rate scheduler compares inter-frame SSIM against a threshold θ to identify segments where all pairwise similarities exceed θ, merging those frames by averaging; (4) a compression encoder maps the temporally compressed video back to latent space; (5) a renoise step linearly interpolates between the original noise and the compressed clean latent to reconstruct the noisy state at timestep k for continued denoising. To handle the altered temporal token spacing, the paper proposes DyRoPE, which applies Global-RoPE (removing merged-frame position encodings) in layers sensitive to redundant information and Local-RoPE (retaining a continuous position span) in layers sensitive to distribution shift, with the specific layer assignment determined empirically.

## Results

On the VBench benchmark (540P, 97-frame videos), DLFR-Gen achieves up to a 2.92× speedup over the original HunyuanVideo (714.90 s → 245.12 s) with the most aggressive setting (k=5, θ=0.5), while maintaining CLIPSIM at 0.1861 vs. 0.1871 and VQA at 97.85 vs. 98.63. The speedup ranges from 1.24× to 2.92× across the tested parameter grid (k ∈ {5,10,15}, θ ∈ {0.5–0.9}), with higher θ preserving more frames and yielding better PSNR (up to 20.6 dB) and SSIM (up to 0.723) at the cost of reduced speedup. Temporal flicker scores actually improve slightly (0.9919 vs. 0.9793) under the fastest configuration, suggesting the compression does not introduce temporal artifacts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the original HunyuanVideo DiT model (no acceleration). DLFR-Gen consistently outperforms the baseline on inference speed, achieving 1.24×–2.92× speedup depending on the (k, θ) configuration, while the quality gap remains small: CLIPSIM drops by at most 0.0028 (0.1871 → 0.1843) and VQA by at most 2.06 points (98.63 → 96.57). On the temporal flicker metric, DLFR-Gen actually exceeds the original (e.g., 0.9919 vs. 0.9793 at k=5, θ=0.5), indicating no temporal degradation. The trade-off is visible in pixel-level fidelity: PSNR ranges from 15.4 dB (most aggressive) to 20.6 dB (most conservative), and LPIPS from 0.398 to 0.210, reflecting the inherent information loss from the encode–decode–re-noise pipeline. The authors note that semantic content is preserved (e.g., scene layout, motion direction) even when minor details like speaker gender shift, and that the method is entirely training-free, requiring no fine-tuning of the base DiT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on HunyuanVideo's DiT-based architecture and flow-matching denoising framework, inserting a training-free compression module into the iterative denoising loop. It departs from prior token-merging approaches (ToMe, video ToMe variants) that reduce QKV tokens within attention by instead merging entire latent frames before subsequent denoising steps, operating at the sequence-length level rather than the attention-computation level. The dynamic frame rate scheduling draws on classical video compression ideas of variable frame rates but is the first to apply them adaptively within a latent diffusion generation pipeline, and the DyRoPE layer-wise positional encoding strategy is a novel contribution addressing the distribution shift introduced by non-uniform temporal token spacing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 3× speedup for DiT-based video generation without any fine-tuning is practically significant given that current models require tens of minutes per short clip, and the training-free design means the approach could in principle be ported to other DiT video models. However, the evaluation is limited to a single architecture (HunyuanVideo) at 540P/97 frames, and the encode-decode cycle through the compression VAE introduces irreducible noise discrepancies that prevent bit-exact reproduction of the original output. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer videos (where temporal non-uniformity is more pronounced), higher resolutions, and other DiT variants, and whether the layer-wise RoPE sensitivity pattern generalizes across architectures.
