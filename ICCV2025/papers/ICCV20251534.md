## Problem

Image-based diffusion models for video frame interpolation cannot extract temporal information and are less efficient than non-diffusion methods, while video-based diffusion models that do capture temporal dynamics require over ten million training videos, billions of parameters, and extremely long inference times. The paper addresses how to extract rich temporal information from video inputs while keeping training scale, model size, and inference cost comparable to image-based approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251534/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3-frame video clip V=[I_0, I_n, I_1] during training and Ṽ=[I_0, 0, I_1] (intermediate frame zeroed) during inference, operating on RGB frames at resolutions from 480p to 4K. The output is not a raw pixel prediction but a structured decomposition: a soft mask M and residual Δ that are combined with optically-flow-warped versions of I_0 and I_1 via Î_n = M⊙warp(I_0) + (1−M)⊙warp(I_1) + Δ, making the model a refinement on top of flow-guided blending rather than a from-scratch generator.

## Method

TLB-VFI introduces a temporal-aware autoencoder that splits temporal extraction into two complementary stages: latent-space temporal blocks (3D convolutions + spatiotemporal attention) that aggregate multi-frame features while preserving per-frame multi-level encoder features for decoder cross-attention, and pixel-space 3D wavelet feature gating that encodes high-frequency temporal changes via a sigmoid-gated skip connection. A Brownian Bridge Diffusion model then bridges the distribution gap between E(V) and E(Ṽ) in latent space, with the key design choice of zeroing I_n to create a large mean shift (MAPE 40–50%) that prevents the bridge from degenerating into an identity mapping—a failure mode the paper formalizes in Proposition 1. The denoising U-Net uses 3D convolutions and spatiotemporal self-attention to predict x_t − x_0, and the entire pipeline is trained on only 51K Vimeo 90K triplets with optical flow guidance, yielding 46.7M parameters and 0.69 s/frame inference.

## Results

TLB-VFI achieves the best LPIPS, FloLPIPS, and FID across all evaluated datasets (Xiph-4K, Xiph-2K, DAVIS, and all four SNU-FILM subsets) with 10 diffusion sampling steps. On the most challenging benchmarks, it achieves FID 19.114 on Xiph-4K and FID 29.868 on SNU-FILM-extreme, representing approximately 20% FID improvement over the prior SOTA (Consec. BB: 24.424 and 36.631 respectively). Inference runtime is 0.69 s/frame on an A5000 GPU, yielding 2.3× speedup over Consec. BB and 4.3× over LDMVFI. The model uses only 46.7M parameters, over 3× fewer than image-based diffusion baselines and over 20× fewer than video-based diffusion models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TLB-VFI outperforms all 12 baselines on every metric across all seven dataset splits. Against the closest diffusion baseline Consec. BB [26], it improves FID by ~22% on Xiph-4K (19.114 vs 24.424) and ~18% on SNU-FILM-extreme (29.868 vs 36.631), while also being 2.3× faster (0.69 s vs 1.62 s). Against LDMVFI [9], it achieves lower FID on all overlapping datasets (e.g., 8.299 vs 12.554 on DAVIS) and is 4.3× faster. Against the strongest non-diffusion SOTA PerVFI [45] (excluded from ranking due to its larger training scale with high-resolution flow data), TLB-VFI wins on 6 of 7 dataset splits but is marginally worse on Xiph-4K FID (19.114 vs 18.852) while being 2.2× faster. Compared to video-based diffusion models (VIDIM [17], DreamMover [37], ViBiDSampler [48]), TLB-VFI uses over 20× fewer parameters (46.7M vs >1B/943M) and 9000× less training data (51K triplets vs >10M–244M videos), while achieving competitive or superior interpolation quality. Among traditional flow-based methods (IFRNet, AMT, UPR-Net, EMA-VFI, VFIformer), TLB-VFI consistently achieves the best FID and LPIPS, with the largest margins on high-motion and high-resolution settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Consecutive Brownian Bridge (Consec. BB) framework for VFI by identifying and fixing a fundamental flaw: the Brownian Bridge between adjacent-frame latent features degenerates into an approximate identity mapping because those features are nearly identical. It builds on LDMVFI's autoencoder design (multi-level encoder features guiding the decoder, flow-based output formulation) and the Latent Diffusion Model paradigm, but departs from image-based diffusion methods by introducing temporal information extraction in both latent space (3D convolutions + spatiotemporal attention) and pixel space (3D wavelet feature gating). Unlike video-based diffusion models (VIDIM, DreamMover, ViBiDSampler) that generate raw pixels from scratch, TLB-VFI uses optical flow warping as a pixel-level prior, dramatically reducing the generative burden and enabling training on standard VFI-scale datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: achieving SOTA VFI quality with 3× fewer parameters and 2.3× faster inference than the prior image-based diffusion SOTA, while requiring 9000× less training data than video-based diffusion models, makes diffusion-based VFI genuinely deployable. The theoretical constraint (Proposition 1) on when Brownian Bridge Diffusion is effective—requiring a statistically significant mean shift between endpoint distributions—provides a useful diagnostic tool beyond VFI. Open questions include whether the approach scales gracefully to interpolating more than 3 frames (the authors note flexibility but only demonstrate triplets), whether the 3D wavelet gating generalizes to other video tasks, and whether the flow-guided formulation can be extended to handle occlusions or non-rigid deformations more robustly.
