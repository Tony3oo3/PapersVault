## Problem

Existing skeleton-based contrastive learning methods treat each trimmed sequence as an isolated instance, learning sequence-wise representations that ignore inter-sequence temporal dependencies. This leads to inconsistent feature representations when the learned encoder is applied to untrimmed videos containing multiple actions in varying contexts, resulting in suboptimal transfer to action segmentation tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252096/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

Pretraining consumes trimmed single-action skeleton sequences of shape T×V×C (frames × joints × spatial dims), which are augmented online into multi-action permutations of variable length. At evaluation, the pretrained feature extractor processes untrimmed skeleton videos and produces per-frame hidden representations that feed a lightweight frame-wise classifier (1×1 conv + SoftMax for multi-class, or Sigmoid for multi-label composite segmentation), yielding a label at every temporal frame rather than a single sequence-level prediction.

## Method

DuoCLR introduces a 'Shuffle and Warp' augmentation that randomly permutes multiple trimmed sequences and aligns them via rotation, translation, and scaling before concatenation, generating dynamic multi-action permutations with cross-sequence context. Two surrogate tasks operate on paired permutations: Cross Permutation Contrasting (CPC) uses InfoNCE with class-specific and permutation-level memory banks to enforce permutation-invariant intra-class similarity, while Relative Order Reasoning (ROR) predicts the relative mapping between two permutations via a classification head over pairwise feature-difference positional encodings. The feature extractor is a cascaded ST-GCN (spatial) + MS-TCN (temporal) architecture that provides a large temporal receptive field without sliding windows. The combined loss L = L_CPC + α·L_ROR is optimized end-to-end, and the resulting encoder transfers to action segmentation via either linear probing or joint fine-tuning with a frame-wise classifier.

## Results

On the multi-class action segmentation task (NTU→PKU1, Linear evaluation), DuoCLR achieves 85.2% mAP₀.₁ and 74.3% mAP₀.₅ (cross-subject), substantially outperforming the supervised Baseline-I (56.0%/50.3%) and LAC (61.8%). Under fine-tuning on PKU1, DuoCLR reaches 94.4% mAP₀.₁ (CS) and 96.8% mAP₀.₁ (CV), surpassing the end-to-end Baseline-II (89.6%/92.1%) and LAC (92.6%/94.6%). On PKU2 (fine-tune), DuoCLR attains 66.1% mIoU and 82.6% Top-1 accuracy (CS), exceeding Baseline-II (50.9%/69.3%) and LaSA (73.4% Acc). In composite segmentation (TS→TSU, fine-tune), DuoCLR scores 35.1% mAP (CS) and 26.3% (CV), and on K4→Charades it reaches 33.9% mAP, outperforming LAC (28.0%) and Baseline-II (21.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DuoCLR decisively outperforms the supervised ST-GCN baseline (Baseline-I) by ~29 points in mAP₀.₁ on PKU1 Linear evaluation (85.2 vs. 56.0), demonstrating the value of contrastive pretraining over vanilla supervised learning. Against the end-to-end segmentation model Baseline-II [16], DuoCLR gains +4.8 mAP₀.₁ (CS) on PKU1 fine-tune and +15.2 mIoU on PKU2 fine-tune, confirming that pre-trained features transfer better than training from scratch. Compared to LAC [48], DuoCLR wins on PKU1 (94.4 vs. 92.6 mAP₀.₁) and PKU2 (66.1 vs. not reported for F-T), and on composite tasks it leads on CV (26.3 vs. 23.1 mAP on TSU) and Charades (33.9 vs. 28.0 mAP), though LAC edges ahead by 1.7 points on TSU CS mAP (36.8 vs. 35.1). Against prior contrastive methods, DuoCLR surpasses USDRL (90.1 vs. 75.7 mAP₀.₅), Hi-TRS (90.1 vs. 63.5), AimCLR (50.9 vs. 15.7 mIoU on PKU2 CV), ActCLR (50.9 vs. 21.4 mIoU), and LaSA (79.2 vs. 69.4 Acc on PKU2 CV) by wide margins, indicating that the dual-surrogate design (CPC + ROR) captures inter-sequence context that single-action contrastive paradigms miss. On the composite task, DuoCLR also beats the RGB-based MS-TCT [10] (35.1 vs. 33.7 mAP on TSU CS), showing skeleton modality with proper pretraining can match or exceed RGB approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the skeleton-based contrastive learning line (AimCLR, ActCLR, CrossSCLR) by shifting the pretraining objective from sequence-wise action recognition to frame-wise action segmentation. It departs from prior single-action augmentations (Shear, Crop, Spatial Flip) by introducing 'Shuffle and Warp,' a multi-action permutation strategy that creates cross-sequence contextual variations, and replaces standard instance discrimination with two new surrogate tasks: Cross Permutation Contrasting (CPC) for permutation-invariant intra-class similarity and Relative Order Reasoning (ROR) for inter-class temporal mapping. Architecturally, it moves from single-encoder designs to a cascaded GCN+TCN structure that provides a large temporal receptive field without sliding windows, building on the MS-TCN and ST-GCN components used in prior segmentation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in action segmentation: the need for expensive frame-level annotations. By demonstrating that contrastive pretraining on trimmed sequences with multi-action permutations can save 42–63% of labeled data while matching fully supervised performance, it offers a viable path toward reducing annotation costs in real-world deployment. Open questions include the factorial computational complexity of ROR (limiting action granularity to ~4 in practice), whether the approach generalizes beyond skeleton modalities, and whether the permutation-based augmentation scales to the hundreds of action classes in large-scale datasets. The method's reliance on a fixed number of actions per permutation also raises questions about applicability to highly variable-length untrimmed sequences.
