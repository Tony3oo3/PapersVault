## Problem

IMUs in sparse inertial motion capture systems rely on magnetometer readings to estimate global orientation, but magnetic disturbances in real-world environments (near electronics, indoor spaces) cause erroneous yaw estimates that severely degrade MoCap accuracy. This critical issue has been largely overlooked in prior sparse inertial MoCap work, confining these systems to magnetically clean environments. MagShield is the first method specifically designed to make sparse inertial MoCap robust to magnetic interference.

## Input / Output

The method takes raw sensor-local measurements (acceleration, angular velocity, magnetic field vector) from 6 body-worn IMUs (forearms, lower legs, head, root) at 100 Hz, along with a previously estimated human pose used to determine spatial neighbor relationships between IMUs. It outputs corrected global IMU readings (rotation matrices, accelerations, angular velocities) that serve as a drop-in replacement for standard ESKF outputs, feeding directly into existing sparse inertial posers such as PNP or DynaIP. The module runs at 1000 FPS for the filter stage and 400 FPS for the neural corrector, maintaining real-time compatibility.

## Method

MagShield employs a two-stage detect-then-correct strategy. In the detection stage, a pose-aware multi-IMU filter (built on ESKF) performs a spatial consistency check: for each IMU, it verifies that the normalized magnetic field magnitudes of its k=3 nearest neighbors (determined from the previously estimated pose) are all within ε=0.15 of 1, rejecting the magnetic vector observation if any neighbor deviates. In the correction stage, an LSTM-based YawCorrector estimates relative yaw errors of the 5 leaf IMUs (assuming the root is correct) from root-frame orientations, accelerations, and the gravity vector, trained via supervised learning on a novel synthetic dataset where magnetic disturbances are simulated by placing virtual magnets in a virtual room (via MagPyLib) and propagating the resulting field through ESKF to generate erroneous orientations. The correction is modulated by a confidence weight that incrementally increases when any IMU detects disturbance and decreases when all fields appear clean, preventing over-correction in benign environments.

## Results

On the MagIMU dataset under severe magnetic interference, MagShield reduces Angular Error by ~18% for PNP (24.65° → 20.23°) and ~23% for DynaIP (28.79° → 22.12°) compared to the standard ESKF baseline. Positional Error improves from 8.99 cm to 8.17 cm (PNP) and 9.10 cm to 8.05 cm (DynaIP), while Mesh Error drops from 10.86 cm to 9.74 cm (PNP) and 11.17 cm to 9.84 cm (DynaIP). The method also reduces cumulative translation error when integrated with PNP. On the clean-field TotalCapture dataset, MagShield achieves performance comparable to the unmodified PNP, confirming it introduces no adverse effects in undisturbed environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the standard Error State Kalman Filter (ESKF) with per-IMU magnetic disturbance detection (k=1), which the authors note performs comparably to the commercial Noitom system. MagShield consistently outperforms this baseline across all four local-pose metrics and both inertial posers (PNP and DynaIP). The largest gains appear in Angular Error: 24.65°→20.23° (−18%) for PNP and 28.79°→22.12° (−23%) for DynaIP, reflecting the method's ability to suppress yaw errors caused by magnetic interference. SIP Error (torso pose) shows the smallest improvement (~9–10%), which the authors attribute to yaw errors at leaf joints partially canceling out at the torso level. The method also reduces cumulative translation error in the PNP pipeline (Fig. 5), confirming that improved local pose accuracy propagates to global trajectory estimation. On the clean-field TotalCapture dataset, MagShield matches the unmodified PNP, demonstrating it can operate as an always-on module without degrading performance in undisturbed environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the ESKF framework (Sola 2017) for per-IMU sensor fusion and on PNP's (Yi et al. 2024) data synthesis pipeline for generating training data, but departs from both prior magnetic disturbance detection methods (threshold-based and model-based, which treat each IMU independently) and prior sparse MoCap noise-handling approaches (which train on general noisy data) by introducing a spatial consistency check across multiple body-worn IMUs and a dedicated yaw-error correction network. The key conceptual departure is leveraging the known relative geometry of body-mounted sensors to validate magnetic field readings collectively, rather than relying on single-sensor magnitude thresholds or probabilistic disturbance models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to specifically target magnetic interference in sparse inertial MoCap, a practical bottleneck that confines current systems to magnetically clean environments and limits real-world deployment (indoor spaces, near electronics). The plug-and-play design—integrating with existing posers like PNP and DynaIP without retraining them—makes it broadly applicable. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-standard IMU placements or body types, whether the synthetic magnetic field distribution adequately captures real-world disturbance statistics, and how the method behaves under prolonged disturbance where global heading drift accumulates.
