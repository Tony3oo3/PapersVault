## Problem

Grounded video question answering requires both generating correct answers and localizing the supporting temporal evidence, but existing approaches either demand expensive temporal annotations, rely on closed-set multiple-choice evaluation, or generate answers and groundings independently. TOGA addresses all three limitations simultaneously by performing open-ended grounded video QA under weak supervision, where no temporal grounding labels are available during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250594/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a video (uniformly sampled into 16 dense-scale and 4 sparse-scale frames) and a free-form text question as input, and produces a single generated sequence containing both an open-ended natural-language answer and a temporal grounding in the format 'Answer [start, end]' where start and end are normalized integers in [0, 100]. Videos average ~40 seconds in length and contain multiple overlapping events, so the grounding must disambiguate among concurrent actions. At inference, the same system prompt is used, with the output format specified in the user prompt to control whether the model generates answer-only, grounding-only, or joint answer-with-grounding responses.

## Method

TOGA combines a frozen CLIP-ViT-Large vision encoder, a frozen Mistral-7B text encoder, a trainable multi-scale vision-language connector (MS-VLC) built from RegNet stages with 3D convolutions operating at two temporal resolutions (16-frame dense for short events, 4-frame sparse for long events), and a trainable Mistral-7B Instruct decoder that jointly generates the answer and grounding tokens via cross-attention. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) vision-text alignment on diverse video-text pairs, (2) instruction tuning with pseudo temporal labels generated by cropping segments and using the stage-1 model to describe them, and (3) a consistency-constraint stage that enforces agreement between a grounding question (e.g., 'What is the boy doing?') and a paired referring question (e.g., 'What is happening in [25,73]?'), ensuring both yield the same answer. This consistency mechanism is the key to reliable weakly supervised grounding, improving mIoU from 12.1 to 24.4 on NExT-GQA, and the joint generation of answer and grounding in a single sequence allows the model to adjust temporal duration based on answer content.

## Results

On NExT-GQA (grounded video QA), TOGA achieves mIoU 24.4, mIoP 40.5, IoU@0.5 21.1, IoP@0.5 40.6, and Acc@GQA 24.6, outperforming the strongest prior method LLoVi (mIoU 20, mIoP 37.3) by 4.4 mIoU and 3.2 mIoP. On open-ended QA, TOGA reaches 73.8% accuracy (score 3.9) on MSVD-QA and 52.0% accuracy (score 3.4) on ActivityNet-QA, surpassing Video-LLaMA2 (70.9% / 50.2%). On the ReXTime zero-shot grounding benchmark, TOGA achieves mIoU 25.53 and R@1(IoU=0.5) 19.79, leading all generative LLM-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TOGA outperforms all seven grounded-QA baselines on NExT-GQA across every grounding metric (mIoU, mIoP, IoU@0.5, IoP@0.5), with the largest gains over LLoVi (+4.4 mIoU, +3.2 mIoP) and Grounded-VideoLLM (+3.3 mIoU, +6.0 mIoP). Notably, TOGA is the only method generating open-ended answers without seeing answer options, making its comparison more challenging than the closed-set baselines. On Acc@GQA, Grounded-VideoLLM (26.7) slightly edges TOGA (24.6), but Grounded-VideoLLM relies on external ground-truth temporal labels from ActivityNet-Captions and GPT-4-generated annotations, whereas TOGA uses no explicit grounding labels. On open-ended QA (Table 3), TOGA beats Video-LLaMA2 by 2.9 points on MSVD-QA accuracy and 1.8 points on ActivityNet-QA accuracy, and surpasses Video-LLaVA, Chat-UniVi, and Video-ChatGPT by 3–8 points. On ReXTime zero-shot grounding (Table 2), TOGA leads all generative LLM-based methods (LITA, TimeChat, VTimeLLM) by 4+ mIoU, though non-generative models UniVTG and CG-DETR still score higher on mIoU (28.17, 23.87) since they are not constrained to produce open-ended text answers.

## Relation to Prior Work

TOGA sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: open-ended video QA (Video-ChatGPT, Video-LLaVA, Video-LLaMA2) and grounded video QA (SeViLA, LLoVi, Grounded-VideoLLM). It departs from prior grounded QA by replacing the multiple-choice setup with free-form answer generation and by jointly producing the answer and its temporal span in a single decoding pass, rather than using separate modules or post-hoc localization. The multi-scale vision-language connector draws on SlowFast-style dual-rate processing, while the multi-stage instruction-tuning pipeline follows the LLaVA/Video-ChatGPT paradigm. The key conceptual departure is the consistency constraint for pseudo-label generation, which replaces expensive temporal annotations with a self-supervised agreement check between grounding and referring questions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for temporal annotations while still producing grounded answers, TOGA makes grounded video QA accessible in settings where such labels are unavailable, broadening the practical applicability of the task. The consistency constraint is a lightweight, generalizable technique that could transfer to other weakly supervised temporal tasks. However, the paper acknowledges that it handles only a single grounding interval per answer and treats questions independently, leaving open the challenge of distributed evidence and inter-question temporal dependencies (e.g., before/after reasoning). Whether the joint-generation advantage persists at longer video durations or with more complex multi-actor scenes remains to be tested.
