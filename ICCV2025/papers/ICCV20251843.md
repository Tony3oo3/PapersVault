## Problem

Existing controlled image synthesis methods fail to simultaneously preserve multiple target objects with high fidelity, maintain semantic alignment with text prompts, and provide explicit user control over background layout and lighting. Approaches relying on Poisson blending lose high-frequency details like text and patterns, while subject-driven fine-tuning methods (DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion) are computationally expensive and introduce artifacts when compositing multiple objects. Additionally, training datasets for CIS lack comprehensive annotations for lighting direction, camera parameters, and aesthetic quality.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 6-channel conditioning input: the target object rendered on a white canvas (3 RGB channels), a binary segmentation mask (1 channel), an optional HED edge map encoding background layout (1 channel), and an optional lighting gradient map derived from spherical-coordinate azimuth/elevation angles (1 channel), alongside a free-form text prompt. The binary mask can be auto-extracted via BiRefNet if not user-provided, and the background layout can be predicted from a reference image by the Background Guidance Module. Output is a synthesized scene at 512×512 (SD v1.5) or 1024×1024 (SDXL) resolution with the object preserved in color, detail, and structural integrity.

## Method

Preserve Anything extends ControlNet to an N-channel (N=6) architecture where a lightweight CNN encoder fuses all conditioning channels into a single vector injected into the diffusion latent space, replacing the single Canny-edge input of standard ControlNet. Three specialized modules handle distinct concerns: an Object Preservation Module that uses the RGB+mask channels to enforce color and structural fidelity without auxiliary losses, a Background Guidance Module that derives HED edge maps from reference images to control scene composition, and a High-Frequency Overlay Module that post-processes the generated image by adding high-frequency details (text, fine patterns) from the original object onto the low-frequency base of the synthesis, eliminating the need for Poisson blending. Training is two-stage: pre-training on 18K Blender Cycles renders (with albedo, shadow, normal, and depth maps) to learn lighting and shadow consistency, followed by fine-tuning on 240K aesthetically-filtered real images for object preservation. The model is initialized from a Canny-ControlNet checkpoint, uses classifier-free guidance with 0.05 prompt/condition dropout, and randomly prunes the background and lighting channels during training to ensure robustness when those cues are absent.

## Results

Preserve Anything achieves the best FID of 15.26 (vs. AnyScene 16.09 and Inpaint Anything 17.17), the highest CLIP-IQA of 0.6869, and the best NRQM of 8.23 on the MS-COCO evaluation set. In the user study on Dreambooth images, it outperforms Inpaint Anything by ~25% in prompt alignment, ~19% in photorealism, ~13% in AI artifact reduction, and ~14% in natural aesthetics (overall ~18% average improvement). The method also supports SDXL backbone, further reducing FID to 14.54 and raising CLIP-S to 33.49.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AnyScene (CVPR 2024), Preserve Anything wins on FID (15.26 vs. 16.09) and LAION-Aesthetic (6.06 vs. 5.94), while AnyScene's CLIP-S (15.18) is anomalously low, likely due to a different evaluation protocol. Against Inpaint Anything, the method achieves a lower FID (15.26 vs. 17.17), higher CLIP-IQA (0.6869 vs. 0.6688), and substantially better NRQM (8.23 vs. 7.64), but trails slightly on CLIP-S (32.85 vs. 33.30) and LAION-Aesthetic (6.06 vs. 6.20). Compared to the SD v1.5 and SDXL baselines, Preserve Anything dramatically improves FID (15.26 vs. 31.00/32.84) and NRQM (8.23 vs. 7.29/6.41), confirming that the N-channel ControlNet with object preservation guidance is far more effective than unconditioned generation. The Canny Edge ControlNet baseline performs worst on FID (48.59), showing that structural edge guidance alone is insufficient for object preservation. In the user study, Preserve Anything beats Inpaint Anything across all four subjective criteria (prompt alignment 3.58 vs. 2.85, photorealism 3.50 vs. 2.93, AI artifacts 2.52 vs. 2.91, natural aesthetics 3.51 vs. 3.07).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends ControlNet from a single-condition architecture (e.g., Canny edges) to an N-channel design (N=6) that simultaneously conditions on the target object RGB, its binary mask, a background layout edge map, and a lighting direction map. It departs from prior object-preservation methods like AnyScene and Inpaint Anything, which rely on Poisson blending or multi-step pipelines for compositing, by replacing those with a lightweight frequency-decomposition overlay and by providing explicit geometric control over lighting and background structure. The PA-240K dataset, combining aesthetically-filtered real images with 3D-rendered scenes carrying explicit lighting and camera metadata, addresses annotation gaps that prior CIS benchmarks (e.g., AnyScene's BLIP2-captioned random samples) leave unaddressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The N-channel ControlNet formulation and the 3D-rendered lighting subset of PA-240K are reusable components that could benefit adjacent tasks such as shadow estimation, depth prediction, and surface-normal estimation, as the authors note. However, the evaluation is somewhat narrow: quantitative benchmarks use only 3K MS-COCO images, the user study compares solely against Inpaint Anything (not AnyScene or Replace Anything), and the CLIP-S score (32.85) actually trails the SDXL baseline (34.18), suggesting prompt alignment gains are not uniform. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond SD/SDXL backbones, how it scales to more than two objects with complex occlusion, and whether the modest FID improvement (~5% over AnyScene) translates to meaningful gains in production e-commerce pipelines.
