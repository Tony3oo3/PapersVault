## Problem

Existing video editing methods struggle to disentangle motion from appearance: attention-based approaches (e.g., Tune-A-Video, TokenFlow) inadvertently retain source appearance details, while dense correspondence signals like optical flow or depth maps cause flickering and incoherence. Sparse semantic point methods like VideoSwap can align motion accurately but require labor-intensive manual annotation. The paper asks whether self-supervised DINOv2 features can serve as automatic, sparse, and semantically rich video correspondences that capture motion trajectories without appearance leakage.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video (16 frames at temporal stride 4, cropped to 512×512 or 512×896) and either 3–5 reference images of a target subject or a text prompt specifying the target identity. DINOv2 features are extracted at a reduced resolution (448×448 or 448×768) and foreground-isolated via PCA thresholding before being projected into the diffusion latent space. The output is a 16-frame edited video where the source subject is replaced by the target identity while preserving the original motion trajectory and unedited background, produced via 50-step DDIM inversion followed by 50-step denoising.

## Method

DIVE operates in three stages. In Stage 1 (temporal motion modeling), DINOv2 features from each source frame are foreground-masked via PCA and projected into the Stable Diffusion U-Net encoder's four downsample levels through learnable MLPs, then added element-wise to intermediate features; the MLPs are trained with a masked diffusion loss restricted to high timesteps (T/2 to T) to emphasize motion over low-level detail. In Stage 2 (identity registration), DINOv2 features from reference images are similarly projected via separate MLPs and fused with diffusion features while training LoRAs on the T2I model across all timesteps, encoding the target subject's identity in a parameter-efficient manner. In Stage 3 (inference), DDIM inversion recovers latent noise from the source video; the trained motion MLPs are applied during denoising from T to T/2 to guide trajectory, the LoRAs inject target identity, and latent blending with a P2P-derived foreground mask preserves the unedited background.

## Results

On reference-image-guided subject editing, DIVE achieves the best scores across all metrics: Text Alignment 29.43, Image Alignment 84.27, Temporal Consistency 92.33, Overall Video Quality 0.775, and a 65.6% user preference rate. On text-guided subject editing, DIVE similarly leads with Text Alignment 32.29, Temporal Consistency 95.89, Overall Video Quality 0.614, and 52.8% user preference. The largest margin over the next-best method is in Image Alignment (84.27 vs. 78.26 for AnyV2V) and user preference (65.6% vs. 13.2%), indicating substantially better identity fidelity and perceptual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIVE outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric in both reference-image and text-guided settings. Against the attention-based methods (Slicedit and AnyV2V), DIVE gains the most on Image Alignment (+5.01 over AnyV2V, +19.70 over Slicedit in the reference-image setting) and Overall Video Quality (+0.336 over AnyV2V), reflecting that simple attention transfer fails to disentangle motion from source appearance. Against the correspondence-based methods (FLATTEN and RAVE), DIVE leads on Text Alignment (+0.64 over RAVE) and Overall Video Quality (+0.092 over FLATTEN), while also achieving the highest Temporal Consistency (92.33 vs. 92.09 for FLATTEN). The user study gap is particularly striking: DIVE receives 65.6% preference in the reference-image setting versus 13.2% for the next-best AnyV2V, and 52.8% versus 19.9% in the text-guided setting, confirming that the quantitative advantages translate to clearly superior perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIVE sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) diffusion-based video editing methods that inject temporal attention or motion modules (Tune-A-Video, TokenFlow, FateZero, Slicedit, AnyV2V), (2) correspondence-guided editing that uses auxiliary signals like optical flow (FLATTEN), depth (RAVE), or manually defined semantic points (VideoSwap), and (3) subject-driven image generation via parameter-efficient fine-tuning (DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion, LoRA). The key conceptual departure is repurposing DINOv2 self-supervised features—previously validated for sparse image semantic correspondence—as implicit *video* correspondences, thereby avoiding the flickering of dense signals and the annotation burden of manual point selection. The framework unifies motion modeling (Stage 1) and identity registration (Stage 2) in a single pipeline, whereas prior methods typically address only one of these concerns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a frozen, off-the-shelf self-supervised vision model can provide sufficient motion guidance for video editing without any task-specific training, which is a practical and elegant insight for the community. However, the evaluation is limited to 16-frame clips at fixed resolutions (512×512 or 512×896) on a single GPU, leaving open questions about scalability to longer videos, more complex multi-subject scenes, and real-time or interactive editing. The reliance on PCA-based foreground masking and a fixed number of reference images (3–5) also constrains generality. Future work could explore whether DINO features generalize to longer temporal windows, whether the approach extends to multi-subject or scene-level editing, and whether the LoRA-based identity registration can be made more efficient or combined with newer personalization techniques.
