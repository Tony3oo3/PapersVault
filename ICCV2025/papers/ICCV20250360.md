## Problem

Existing post-training quantization methods for SAM suffer from quantization collapse at 4-bit and severe accuracy degradation at 5-bit, limiting practical edge deployment. The paper identifies two root causes overlooked by prior work: the heavy-tailed, skewed distribution of post-GELU activations that defeats both uniform and log2 quantizers, and high inter-channel variation in linear projection activations that renders per-tensor quantization ineffective while per-channel quantization incurs prohibitive hardware overhead.

## Input / Output

The quantized SAM model receives RGB images paired with bounding box prompts generated by external detectors (YOLOX, DINO, Faster R-CNN, or H-Deformable-DETR) and outputs segmentation masks, evaluated via mAP on COCO. Quantization is applied to all layers except the first and last blocks, using 32 calibration images and MinMax initialization. The method targets SAM-B, SAM-L, and SAM-H variants at W4A4, W5A5, and W6A6 bit-widths, with HLUQ applied specifically to Linear-2 (post-GELU) activations and CAG to Query/Key/Value and Linear-1 activations.

## Method

AHCPTQ introduces two complementary techniques. Hybrid Log-Uniform Quantization (HLUQ) partitions the quantization grid at a threshold b̂, applying log2 quantization to densely clustered small post-GELU values and uniform quantization to sparse large values, with scales s1, s2 and threshold b̂ initialized via auxiliary parameters α and β searched to minimize reconstruction error. Channel-Aware Grouping (CAG) progressively clusters channels with similar quantization parameters using K-Means at designated milestones during block-wise reconstruction, reducing N per-channel parameters to K=4 shared groups that fit in 144 on-chip registers (99.7% reduction). Hardware co-optimization employs a dual processing-element architecture where MSB label bits route activations to either a multiplier (uniform branch) or bit-shifter (log2 branch), and counter-based logic switches quantization parameters for grouped channels, achieving 7.89× speedup and 8.64× energy efficiency over FP32 on a Xilinx Artix UltraScale+ FPGA.

## Results

On COCO instance segmentation, AHCPTQ achieves 36.6% mAP under W4A4 quantization on SAM-L with the DINO detector, compared to only 2.3% for PTQ4SAM and 1.0% for QDrop under the same setting. At W5A5 on SAM-H with DINO, AHCPTQ reaches 47.6% mAP, losing only 1.5 points from the FP32 baseline of 49.1%, while PTQ4SAM achieves 43.8%. On the FPGA platform, AHCPTQ INT4 delivers a 7.89× speedup and 8.64× energy efficiency improvement (43.57 vs. 5.04 GOPS/W) over the FP32 accelerator.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AHCPTQ decisively outperforms all three baselines across every detector, SAM variant, and bit-width configuration. Against PTQ4SAM (the closest prior method for SAM), the gap is most dramatic at W4A4: on SAM-L with DINO, AHCPTQ achieves 36.6% mAP versus PTQ4SAM's 2.3%, a 16× improvement; on SAM-H with DINO, 41.2% vs. 8.9%. At W5A5 on SAM-H, AHCPTQ narrows the gap to FP32 to just 1.5 mAP points (47.6 vs. 49.1), while PTQ4SAM still lags by 5.3 points (43.8). BRECQ and QDrop, designed for CNNs or generic ViTs, collapse to near-zero mAP at W4A4 on SAM-B (0.2–2.6%) and remain substantially below AHCPTQ at all bit-widths (e.g., 19.0% vs. 31.8% for BRECQ vs. AHCPTQ at W5A5 on SAM-B YOLOX). At W6A6, where all methods perform reasonably, AHCPTQ still leads by 0.1–1.5 mAP points, confirming consistent gains even in the easier regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on PTQ4SAM, the first PTQ method tailored to SAM, which addressed bimodal Key activations and post-Softmax attention distributions but collapsed at 4-bit. It departs from PTQ4SAM by identifying two previously overlooked challenges—heavy-tailed post-GELU activations and high inter-channel variance in linear projections—and proposing targeted solutions: a hybrid log2-uniform quantizer (HLUQ) that partitions the quantization grid between two hardware-friendly schemes, and a progressive channel-grouping strategy (CAG) that clusters channels with similar statistics to share parameters. The broader PTQ-for-ViT literature (FQ-ViT, RepQ-ViT, ERQ, PTQ4ViT) informs the design, but those methods rely on reparameterization or non-uniform quantizers that are incompatible with SAM's mask decoder or hardware constraints, motivating the hybrid and grouping approaches here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAM is a foundational model underpinning a wide ecosystem of downstream applications, so reducing its deployment cost from FP32 to 4-bit with minimal accuracy loss has broad practical impact for edge and embedded systems. The paper's first demonstration of functional 4-bit PTQ on SAM, combined with FPGA validation showing 7.89× speedup and 8.64× energy efficiency, moves the field beyond purely algorithmic PTQ toward hardware-realizable deployment. Limitations include evaluation restricted to instance segmentation on COCO, hardware validation limited to a single MLP block in the mask decoder, and no demonstration on GPU/CPU platforms. Open questions include whether the progressive grouping and hybrid quantizer strategies generalize to other large foundation models or to tasks beyond segmentation.
