## Problem

Existing visual foundation models (VFMs) adopt result-oriented paradigms that ignore the cross-modal interaction processes encoded in vision-language models (VLMs), creating a representational gap that hinders effective knowledge transfer. This paper asks whether the cognitive interaction patterns—rather than final outputs—can serve as a transferable knowledge carrier between VLMs and VFMs, enabling cross-modal and cross-task generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252256/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images as input; for detection and segmentation, bounding boxes are additionally drawn on the input image as visual prompts to guide the VLM's cross-attention. The VLM (LLaVA-1.5) processes a concatenated sequence of image tokens and task-specific question tokens to extract cross-modal interaction matrices. The VFM produces task-specific outputs—class labels, bounding boxes, or segmentation masks—derived from fused interaction tokens that combine visual and linguistic interaction structures.

## Method

The Interaction Vision Transformer (I-ViT) introduces a dual-query architecture where Original Queries maintain task-specific visual interactions while additional Interaction Queries encode cross-modal patterns distilled from a pre-trained VLM. Vision-language interactions are extracted by prompting LLaVA-1.5 with task-conditioned language queries and computing cross-modal attention, then applying a non-negative contrastive filtering operator to emphasize foreground-background differentiation. A Gated Control Network dynamically fuses the two interaction structures by separating binary logical gates (what to interact) from continuous importance weights (how strongly to interact), producing fused interaction tokens that modulate value transformations. Training combines a task-specific loss with a KL-divergence alignment loss between the VLM-guided interaction matrix and the VFM's intrinsic interaction pattern, requiring only the GCN, interaction queries, and task heads to be updated while freezing the original transformer layers.

## Results

On TinyImageNet, I-ViT-L/16 achieves 91.08% Top-1 accuracy, a +3.3% absolute gain over ViT-L/16 (87.83%), with smaller gains at S (+1.6%) and B (+1.9%) scales. On COCO 2017, integrating vision-language interactions (C_VLM) improves detection mAP from 42.0 to 43.6 (+1.6) and segmentation AP_mask from 31.1 to 33.5 (+2.4), with less than 1% parameter overhead. Zero-shot cross-domain evaluation on PACS and VLCS yields ~2.4% and ~9.3% average improvements respectively, and I-ViT converges approximately 7× faster than the result-oriented ViT baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ViT (Dosovitskiy et al., ICLR 2021), I-ViT consistently wins across all evaluated tasks and scales: +1.6 to +3.3% Top-1 on TinyImageNet, +1.6 mAP on COCO detection, +2.4 AP_mask on COCO segmentation, +2.3 mAP on VOC few-shot detection, and positive gains on 3 of 4 PACS domains and all 4 VLCS domains. The only exception is PACS Sketch, where I-ViT drops slightly (15.18 vs 15.49), attributed to the simplistic content making consistent interactions harder. Against LLaVA-1.5 (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2023) on the VLCS zero-shot benchmark, I-ViT surpasses the VLM on PASCAL (63.98 vs 56.40), CALTECH (68.75 vs 62.89), and SUN (36.61 vs 32.71), but trails on LABEL (49.35 vs 52.58), suggesting the interaction transfer is most effective for natural-scene and object-centric domains. Overall, the paper demonstrates that interaction-based supervision from a VLM enables a smaller VFM to match or exceed the VLM's own zero-shot performance on most cross-domain tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Vision Transformer (ViT) and DETR architectures by introducing Interaction Queries and a Gated Control Network that fuse cross-modal attention patterns extracted from a pre-trained VLM (LLaVA-1.5) into the VFM's internal representations. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant knowledge distillation paradigm (which transfers output distributions or feature maps) by instead transferring the *process* of reasoning—formalized as structured AND/OR interactions between tokens—drawing on symbolic interaction theory from interpretability literature. The work also builds on the Platonic representation hypothesis and cognitive science framing to argue that interaction structures are representation-agnostic carriers of knowledge, positioning itself against result-oriented training that the authors argue neglects underlying cognitive processes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, task-agnostic recipe for injecting VLM knowledge into lightweight VFMs with minimal parameter overhead (<1%) and a claimed 7× convergence speedup, which could be practically useful for practitioners who want to leverage large VLMs without their inference cost. However, the evidence base is limited: a single VLM (LLaVA-1.5), relatively small-scale benchmarks (TinyImageNet, COCO val), and the human-evaluation protocol is only briefly described. Open questions include whether the interaction-transfer paradigm scales to larger VFMs (e.g., ViT-H, SAM), whether it generalizes beyond the three tested task families, and whether the gains persist when the VLM and VFM are from different architectural families. The authors themselves flag bi-directional transfer and video understanding as future directions.
