## Problem

Existing text-to-video retrieval methods face a fundamental trade-off: Single-Tower architectures achieve high accuracy through fine-grained video-text interaction but require per-query computation over all videos at inference time, while Two-Tower architectures enable fast offline pre-computation but lack such interaction. No prior framework simultaneously delivers Single-Tower-level effectiveness and Two-Tower-level serving efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250384/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip (default 12 frames, 32/64 for DiDeMo) and a text query as input. During inference, video features are fully pre-computed offline—including pseudo-query generation and cross-modal fusion—so the online stage reduces to a single dot product between the cached video embedding and the text query embedding, yielding a ranked video list. The video encoder is CLIP-ViT-B/32 (or B/16) with an added frame-level [cls] token, producing multi-grained features at video, frame, and patch levels.

## Method

PIG introduces a Hybrid-Tower framework where a pseudo-query generator reconstructs discriminative text features from multi-grained visual inputs (video-level, frame-level, and top-k informative patch-level tokens selected via an Informativeness Token Selector that exploits ViT attention sparsity). The generator is a causal attention transformer initialized from the CLIP text encoder, fed with [bos], multi-grained visual tokens, and [eos], outputting a pseudo-query at the [eos] position. A pseudo-interaction fusioner (XPool Attention) then uses this pseudo-query as Q and concatenated video+frame features as K&V to produce an enhanced video representation, effectively performing fine-grained cross-modal fusion offline. Training proceeds in two stages: first pre-training the generator with a cosine reconstruction loss against real text features (encoders frozen), then jointly fine-tuning the full network with symmetric InfoNCE contrastive loss plus the reconstruction loss. At inference, all video-side computation is cached, so retrieval is a simple dot product matching Two-Tower efficiency while retaining Single-Tower-level interaction quality.

## Results

PIG achieves a mean R@1 of 48.0 across four datasets (MSRVTT-1k, MSRVTT-3k, MSVD, VATEX) with only 0.5K FLOPs per video-text matching, matching the efficiency of Two-Tower models. On MSRVTT-1k, PIG reaches R@1 of 48.6, surpassing the previous Two-Tower SOTA TeachCLIP (46.8) by 1.8 points and its own backbone CLIP-ViP (46.0) by 2.6 points. Single-Tower methods like ProST (48.2) and UCoFiA (49.1) achieve marginally higher R@1 on MSRVTT-1k but at 4,035× and 19,673× higher inference FLOPs (2,017.5K and 9,836.7K respectively). PIG also achieves the best cross-dataset generalization, scoring 44.9 on MSVD and 49.8 on VATEX when trained only on MSRVTT-1k.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PIG outperforms all Two-Tower baselines across every reported dataset: it beats TeachCLIP (the strongest Two-Tower method) by 1.8 R@1 on MSRVTT-1k, 0.9 on MSRVTT-3k, 0.5 on MSVD, and 0.4 on VATEX, while maintaining identical 0.5K FLOPs inference cost. Against its own backbone CLIP-ViP, PIG gains 2.6 R@1 on MSRVTT-1k, 1.6 on MSRVTT-3k, 2.8 on MSVD, and 1.9 on VATEX. Compared to Single-Tower methods, PIG matches or exceeds ProST (48.6 vs 48.2 on MSRVTT-1k) and UCoFiA (48.6 vs 49.1, a 0.5 gap) while using 4,035× and 19,673× fewer FLOPs respectively; it also surpasses X-CLIP (48.6 vs 45.3) and TS2-Net (48.6 vs 46.7) on MSRVTT-1k with far lower cost. All four feature re-learning baselines (SEA, W2VV++, MMT, LAFF) are substantially outperformed, with PIG exceeding the best of them (LAFF, 45.8) by 2.8 R@1 on MSRVTT-1k. In cross-dataset evaluation (trained on MSRVTT-1k, tested on MSVD/VATEX/DiDeMo), PIG achieves the best mean R@1 of 42.0, edging out X-CLIP (42.4 on MSVD but 48.9 on VATEX) and TeachCLIP (41.5 mean).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on CLIP-ViP as its video backbone and XPool's cross-attention mechanism as its fusioner, but introduces a fundamentally new architectural paradigm: the Hybrid-Tower framework. Unlike Two-Tower methods (e.g., TeachCLIP, PromptSwitch) that rely solely on dot-product matching, or Single-Tower methods (e.g., ProST, UCoFiA) that perform fine-grained text-video interaction at inference time, PIG generates pseudo-queries from video features offline and pre-fuses them with video representations, decoupling fine-grained interaction from the online retrieval step. The Informativeness Token Selection module, which exploits ViT attention sparsity to select top-k patch tokens, is a novel input-selection strategy not seen in prior T2VR work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Hybrid-Tower framework offers a practical resolution to the long-standing effectiveness-efficiency trade-off in T2VR, making fine-grained cross-modal interaction viable for real-time retrieval systems where Single-Tower models are infeasible. The finding that a trained pseudo-query generator outperforms captions from powerful external video captioners (ShareGPT4Video, m-PLUG2) suggests that task-aligned feature-space generation is more effective than natural-language intermediation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to longer videos (DiDeMo performance degrades with CLIP-ViP's proxy attention on 32-frame clips), whether the offline overhead (54.90G vs. 53.65G FLOPs) becomes prohibitive at scale, and whether the framework generalizes beyond CLIP-based backbones.
