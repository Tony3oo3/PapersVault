## Problem

Existing DETR-based semi-supervised object detection methods suffer from three interrelated issues: noisy pseudo-labels produced by one-to-many assignment strategies, a confidence bias that causes detectors to over-predict common categories while filtering out rare ones, and denoising queries that assign identical labels to both foreground and background, making object-background separation difficult. These limitations are amplified when only a small fraction of labeled data is available, as rare-class pseudo-labels fall below confidence thresholds and are discarded entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250458/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (COCO 640×640 or VOC 512×512) along with a small set of labeled bounding boxes (1%, 5%, or 10% of COCO train2017, or VOC2007) during training; unlabeled images receive weak augmentation for the teacher and strong augmentation for the student. The 'text' modality is not natural language but 256-dimensional one-hot encodings of class labels, which are concatenated with bounding box coordinates to form query vectors. The model outputs a set of predicted bounding boxes with class labels and confidence scores, evaluated via AP50:95, AP50, and AP75.

## Method

STEP-DETR builds on DINO (ResNet-50 backbone, 900 object queries) with a two-stage teacher-student training process. The Super Teacher combines a static teacher (trained once on labeled data, then frozen) with a dynamic teacher (updated via EMA of student weights), and their concatenated pseudo-labels are filtered by a confidence threshold to produce high-quality supervision. Pseudo-Label Text Queries convert class labels into 256-d one-hot embeddings, concatenate them with bounding boxes, and feed them as additional decoder queries alongside standard and consistency queries, ensuring rare categories receive explicit query representations. Denoising Text Guided Object Queries assign correct text embeddings to positive (high-IoU) noise boxes and random class embeddings to negative (low-IoU) noise boxes, creating synthetic foreground-background pairs for contrastive learning. A Query Refinement Module then filters denoising queries whose maximum similarity to pseudo-label text embeddings falls below a threshold, reducing redundant queries and cutting training time by ~7.6 minutes per 1k iterations relative to Semi-DETR.

## Results

On the COCO-Partial benchmark, STEP-DETR achieves 45.4 mAP with 10% labeled data, 41.1 mAP with 5%, and 31.7 mAP with 1%, surpassing the strongest DETR-based baseline Semi-DETR (43.5 mAP) by 1.9 mAP and Sparse Semi-DETR (44.3 mAP) by 1.1 mAP. On Pascal VOC, STEP-DETR reaches 65.87 mAP (AP50:95) and 86.85 AP50, outperforming Semi-DETR (65.20/86.10) and Sparse Semi-DETR (65.51/86.30). On COCO-Full (100% labeled + unlabeled), STEP-DETR attains 52.1 mAP, a 2.7 mAP gain over its supervised DINO baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STEP-DETR outperforms all 16 compared baselines on the COCO-Partial benchmark. Against the closest DETR-based competitors, it gains 1.9 mAP over Semi-DETR (45.4 vs. 43.5 at 10%), 1.1 mAP over Sparse Semi-DETR (45.4 vs. 44.3), and 0.8 mAP over MixPL (45.4 vs. 44.6). The gap widens substantially against CNN-based SSOD methods: STEP-DETR leads Dense Teacher by 8.27 mAP and PseCo by 9.34 mAP at 10% labels. On Pascal VOC, STEP-DETR achieves 65.87 mAP, beating Semi-DETR (65.20) by 0.67 and Sparse Semi-DETR (65.51) by 0.36. On COCO-Full, STEP-DETR reaches 52.1 mAP, surpassing Semi-DETR (50.4) by 1.7 mAP and Sparse Semi-DETR (51.3) by 0.8 mAP. For small objects specifically, STEP-DETR gains 2.5 mAP over Semi-DETR and 0.8 mAP over Sparse Semi-DETR at 10% labels, indicating the text-query mechanism particularly benefits rare and small categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

STEP-DETR extends the DETR-based semi-supervised object detection lineage initiated by Semi-DETR (CVPR 2023) and refined by Sparse Semi-DETR (CVPR 2024), using DINO as the underlying detector. It departs from prior single-teacher SSOD frameworks by introducing a dual-teacher 'Super Teacher' (static + dynamic) to improve pseudo-label quality, and introduces pseudo-label text queries—a concept borrowed from open-vocabulary detection (Grounding DINO, CLIP)—but implemented here as simple one-hot class-label embeddings rather than learned language representations. The denoising strategy is a direct modification of DN-DETR's approach, adding text-guided foreground/background separation to address a gap the authors identify in Semi-DETR and Sparse Semi-DETR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and well-recognized weakness in SSOD—confidence bias toward frequent categories that causes rare objects to be filtered out—by injecting class-level text information into the query space. However, the 'text queries' are one-hot encodings of class labels, not learned semantic embeddings, which limits the generality of the approach and its applicability to open-vocabulary or long-tail settings. The authors themselves note that open-vocabulary models (BERT, GPT) could refine pseudo-labels more effectively, leaving the full potential of text-guided queries unrealized. With modest gains (1.9 mAP over Semi-DETR at 10% labels) and evaluation limited to COCO and VOC, the contribution is a solid engineering improvement rather than a paradigm shift.
