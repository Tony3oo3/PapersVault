## Problem

Existing visual imitation learning methods for robot manipulation rely on behavior cloning from supervised trajectories, which limits their ability to capture the 3D spatial structure of environments and 4D spatiotemporal dynamics of interactive tasks. This gap causes policies to fail at complex manipulation requiring accurate spatial reasoning (e.g., dexterous object interaction) and generalization to unseen scenarios, as the learned representations lack explicit physical-world structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250157/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single-view RGB-D observation (RGB image plus depth image) from a camera with known extrinsic and intrinsic parameters, along with the current robot state. The depth image is converted to a point cloud, which is then cropped to a bounding box for local representation and voxelized to a 100³ grid for global representation. The output is a continuous action trajectory generated by a DDIM-based diffusion denoising process (100 training timesteps, 10 inference timesteps), with no Gaussian world model constructed at inference time.

## Method

DP4 introduces a dynamic Gaussian world model that provides structured supervision during training to imbue the policy with 3D spatial and 4D spatiotemporal awareness. A Generalizable Gaussian Regressor parameterizes 3D Gaussian primitives from the multi-level 3D representation (local MLP features from cropped point clouds + global features from a 100³ voxel grid), and is trained to reconstruct current RGB-D via alpha-blend rendering (3D loss). For 4D awareness, a deformable MLP predicts changes in Gaussian parameters conditioned on the current parameters and the robot action, propagating the scene forward in time; the rendered future RGB-D is supervised against ground truth (4D loss). These spatiotemporal losses are jointly optimized with the standard diffusion action-prediction loss (weighted by λ_3D=0.1 and λ_4D=0.01) using a warm-up phase that freezes the deformable mapper for 500 iterations before end-to-end training. At inference, the Gaussian world model is discarded entirely—the learned 3D representation already encodes the spatial and temporal structure, so the diffusion policy simply conditions on it to denoise actions.

## Results

DP4 achieves an overall success rate of 84.7% on Adroit (Hammer 100%, Door 80%, Pen 75%), 82.5% on DexArt (Laptop 92%, Faucet 84%, Bucket 90%, Toilet 64%), and 63.3%/39.9% on the two RLBench task groups. On real-robot tasks, DP4 reaches 54.6% overall success (Grasping 48%, Stacking 72%, Pouring 44%). These correspond to average improvements of +16.4% over DP3 on Adroit, +14.0% on DexArt, +6.45% on RLBench, and +8.6% on real-robot tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DP4 outperforms all seven baselines across every benchmark. Against the closest competitor, 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) [47], DP4 gains +16.4% on Adroit (84.7% vs 68.3%), +14.0% on DexArt (82.5% vs 68.5%), and +8.6% on real-robot tasks (54.6% vs 46.0%). The 2D-based Diffusion Policy (DP) [5] trails significantly at 41.0% on Adroit and 49.0% on DexArt, while IBC [7] and BCRNN [26] are near-zero on Adroit (3.0%) and DexArt (6.0% and 2.3%), confirming that pure behavior cloning without spatial-temporal structure is insufficient for dexterous tasks. On RLBench, DP4 leads ManiGaussian [25] (63.3% vs 57.1%), GNFactor [46] (63.3% vs 47.5%), and PerAct [35] (63.3% vs 30.4%), with the largest gap on the second task group (39.9% vs 33.2%, 16.0%, and 10.4% respectively). Notably, DP4 does not increase inference time relative to DP3, as the Gaussian world model is used only during training for supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

DP4 extends the diffusion-policy line of work (Diffusion Policy, 3D Diffusion Policy) by replacing passive trajectory cloning with active spatial-temporal supervision derived from a dynamic Gaussian world model. It repurposes 3D Gaussian Splatting and 4D Gaussian Splatting—previously used for rendering and scene reconstruction—as training-time supervision signals that shape the multi-level 3D representation feeding the diffusion denoiser. The key architectural departure from DP3 and other 3D-based policies is that the Gaussian world model is never invoked at inference; its role is purely to inject 3D structural and 4D dynamic knowledge into the learned features during training, keeping deployment cost unchanged.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit 3D/4D supervision from a Gaussian world model consistently improves imitation-learning success rates across dexterous, articulated, and real-robot manipulation without adding inference overhead, suggesting that structured world-model signals are a practical lever for closing the sim-to-real gap in visuomotor policies. Open questions include whether the single-view RGB-D constraint and the relatively short prediction horizon (one step ahead) limit scalability to longer-horizon or multi-agent tasks, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the curated benchmark suites to truly open-ended environments. The reliance on offline expert demonstrations also leaves the method within the behavior-cloning paradigm, so combining the Gaussian world-model supervision with online or reinforcement-based learning remains an important next step.
