## Problem

Large multimodal models (LMMs) exhibit a significant performance gap compared to specialist detectors on object detection benchmarks, despite being trained on large-scale detection data. The paper identifies the root cause as an insufficient recall rate: LMMs generate too few bounding boxes due to incomplete ground-truth annotations and the autoregressive next-token prediction paradigm, which causes premature truncation of predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250785/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a high-resolution RGB image (1008×1008, processed by OWLv2-ViT) and a text prompt specifying a single category set (e.g., 'Detect all the objects in the image that belong to the category set {orange}'). It outputs a sequence of normalized bounding box coordinates [x1, y1, x2, y2] each paired with a confidence score, generated as standard token predictions rather than special vocabulary tokens. Inference is class-specific: the model is queried once per category and all predicted boxes across categories are aggregated as final proposals, with NMS applied as post-processing.

## Method

LMM-Det uses a straightforward LMM architecture (OWLv2-ViT encoder + linear projector + Vicuna-1.5-7B LLM) without any extra detection modules, and addresses the recall-rate bottleneck through two key mechanisms. First, Data Distribution Adjustment (DDA) augments incomplete COCO ground-truth annotations with pseudo-labels generated by a pre-trained Salience-DETR, merges them via NMS, and assigns confidence scores (1.0 for GT, detector scores for pseudo-labels) to shift the training distribution toward higher recall. Second, Inference Optimization (INO) restructures both training and inference to a class-specific prediction strategy, where the model predicts all objects of one category per forward pass and this is repeated across all categories, rather than attempting to output all boxes in a single autoregressive sequence. Training proceeds in three stages: projector alignment on 595K image-text pairs, pre-training on Object365, and instruction fine-tuning on re-organized COCO detection data, with an optional fourth stage to preserve captioning and VQA capabilities.

## Results

On the COCO validation set (fine-tuned), LMM-Det achieves AP 47.5, AP50 66.5, AP75 51.1, and AR@100 63.6 without any specialist detection module, outperforming the best LMM baseline without specialist modules (Griffon v2, AP 38.5) by 9.0 AP points. In the zero-shot setting, LMM-Det reaches AP 24.5, more than doubling the next-best LMM (InternVL-2.5, AP 11.8). The versatile variant LMM-Det† (with optional Stage IV) retains strong captioning (CIDEr 99.0) and VQA (74.1%) performance while maintaining AP 47.1. However, specialist detectors like Salience-DETR (AP 57.3) and RT-DETR (AP 55.3) still lead by roughly 8–10 AP points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among LMMs without specialist detection modules, LMM-Det (AP 47.5) substantially outperforms Griffon v2 (AP 38.5), Griffon-13B (AP 24.8), and the retrained LLaVA* (AP 38.7), with the largest margin over Griffon-13B (+22.7 AP). LMM-Det also surpasses Groma with a DINOv2 specialist module (AP 43.6) by 3.9 AP, demonstrating that the proposed data distribution adjustment and inference optimization are more effective than bolting on a region proposal network. Against specialist detectors, LMM-Det trails Salience-DETR (AP 57.3) and RT-DETR (AP 55.3) by 9.8 and 7.8 AP respectively, though it narrows the gap considerably compared to prior LMMs. In the zero-shot setting (Table 2), LMM-Det (AP 24.5) more than doubles InternVL-2.5 (AP 11.8) and Groma (AP 12.8), and is 3.2× better than KOSMOS-2 (AP 7.6). Notably, VisionLLM v2 (AP 56.3) integrates Grounding DINO as a specialist module but still underperforms standalone Grounding DINO (AP 57.2), suggesting that module integration introduces overhead that LMM-Det avoids entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LLaVA architecture (visual encoder + linear projector + LLM) and its instruction-tuning paradigm, but departs from the dominant strategy of bolting specialist detection modules (Grounding DINO, RPNs) onto LMMs. It extends the grounding capabilities demonstrated by Shikra and KOSMOS-2 (which handle single-object referring expressions) to full vanilla object detection, arguing that the missing ingredient is not architectural but data-distributional: LMMs already possess detection capability that is suppressed by incomplete ground-truth annotations causing premature truncation of predictions. The method repurposes a specialist detector (Salience-DETR) solely as a pseudo-label generator during training, rather than as an inference-time component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear diagnostic (low recall rate due to data distribution mismatch) and a practical recipe (pseudo-label augmentation + class-specific inference) that narrows the LMM-to-specialist-detector gap from ~49 AP to ~10 AP without any extra detection module at inference. This is architecturally appealing for unified multimodal systems that must also handle captioning and VQA. However, the ~4-second per-image inference time and the remaining 10 AP gap to SOTA specialist detectors (e.g., Salience-DETR at 57.3) limit near-term practical deployment, and the analysis is confined to a single LLM backbone (Vicuna-7B) and a single benchmark (COCO), leaving open whether the findings generalize to larger models or more diverse detection settings.
