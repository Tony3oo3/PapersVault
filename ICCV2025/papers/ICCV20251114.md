## Problem

Existing prompt-based class-incremental learning methods (L2P, DualPrompt, Coda-Prompt) incur substantial computational overhead from two sources: an extra forward pass through a raw ViT to query a prompt pool, and concatenation of prompt tokens that extends the input sequence by up to 50%. The paper asks whether such complex prompt selection and concatenation-based injection is truly necessary, or whether a simpler additive mechanism can match or exceed their performance at a fraction of the cost.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 224×224 for ImageNet-R, CUB200, and Stanford Cars) processed by a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone and outputs a class label via a task-specific linear head. Critically, at inference time no task identity is provided and no memory buffer is consulted, making the approach fully rehearsal-free. The only additional hyperparameter beyond standard training settings is the PPF fusion coefficient α.

## Method

APT replaces prompt concatenation with additive modification of the CLS token's key and value vectors inside each transformer layer's self-attention: two learnable vectors per layer (p_k^l, p_v^l) are added to the CLS token's projected key and value before the attention computation, while all patch-token keys and values remain untouched. This yields only 24 trainable prompt vectors for ViT-B/16 (12 layers × 2), compared to hundreds or thousands in prompt-pool methods, and preserves the original sequence length so inference GFLOPs match a plain ViT. A single shared prompt set is trained across all tasks (no pool, no query mechanism), and training uses only standard cross-entropy loss. To mitigate forgetting, a Progressive Prompt Fusion (PPF) strategy computes a weighted average P_{t+1}^{PPF} = α·P_t + (1−α)·P_{t+1} between the stored pre-task prompts and the newly trained prompts, applied exclusively at inference.

## Results

On the 10-task rehearsal-free CIL setting, APT achieves the best average accuracy across all four benchmarks: 88.88% on Split CIFAR-100, 79.40% on Split ImageNet-R, 78.50% on Split CUB200, and 71.04% on Split Stanford Cars, while also reporting the lowest forgetting on CIFAR-100 (3.47) and Stanford Cars (7.95). In terms of efficiency, APT requires only 16.80 GFLOPs (identical to a plain ViT-B/16) and 0.02M trainable prompt parameters, compared to 35.73 GFLOPs and 3.07M parameters for Coda-Prompt. On the 20-task setting, APT extends its lead to 86.37% on CIFAR-100 and 76.22% on ImageNet-R, outperforming the second-best by over 5% on both.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APT outperforms all prompt-based baselines on every dataset where they overlap. On Split CIFAR-100, APT (88.88%) beats the closest competitor EvoPrompt (87.97%) by 0.91% and CPrompt (87.82%) by 1.06%. On the more challenging Split ImageNet-R, APT (79.40%) leads CPrompt (77.14%) by 2.26% and EvoPrompt (76.83%) by 2.57%. On fine-grained benchmarks, the margins widen: APT surpasses OVOR-Deep (78.11%) by 0.39% on CUB200 and CPrompt (66.77%) by a substantial 4.27% on Stanford Cars. Against non-prompt methods (LAE, InfLoRA, EASE), APT also leads on both CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-R. Crucially, APT achieves these gains while using 16.80 GFLOPs and 0.02M trainable parameters, compared to 2.0–2.25× the GFLOPs and up to 153.5× the parameters of methods like Coda-Prompt, making it both more accurate and substantially more efficient than every baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct refinement of Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT), replacing the concatenation of learnable prompt tokens to the input sequence with additive modifications applied only to the CLS token's key and value vectors during self-attention. It departs from the dominant prompt-based CIL lineage (L2P → DualPrompt → Coda-Prompt → ESN → EvoPrompt → CPrompt), which relies on maintaining a pool of prompts and a query-based selection mechanism requiring an extra forward pass through a raw ViT. The design draws on the observation that CLS tokens aggregate global visual information, and the Progressive Prompt Fusion inference strategy borrows the weighted-averaging idea from EMA-based continual learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is that APT achieves state-of-the-art CIL accuracy with zero additional inference overhead (16.80 GFLOPs, identical to a plain ViT) and only 24 trainable prompt vectors, compared to 2×+ GFLOPs and hundreds to thousands of prompt parameters in prior methods. This challenges the working assumption that prompt pools and query-based selection are necessary for competitive rehearsal-free CIL, suggesting the field may have over-engineered the prompt selection component. Open questions include whether the additive CLS-token design generalizes to larger ViT variants, non-classification tasks, or other modalities, and whether the simple weighted-average PPF strategy remains effective beyond 20 tasks or under highly non-IID task distributions.
