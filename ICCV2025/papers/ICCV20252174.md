## Problem

Existing anomaly detection methods designed for industrial settings are ill-suited to computational pathology due to computational bottlenecks on gigapixel whole-slide images, high tissue structural diversity, domain shifts across institutions and staining protocols, and a lack of interpretability. No prior method simultaneously achieves lightweight inference, cross-domain robustness, and clinically meaningful explanations for rare pathology anomalies such as lymph node metastasis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252174/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pathology image patches (256×256 px for GastricLN, 512×512 px for Camelyon16) together with two curated term pools of 92 normal and 48 abnormal pathology terms, each formatted as the prompt 'an image showing KEYWORD' for the frozen CONCH text encoder. It produces patch-level anomaly scores, WSI-level scores via max or top-1% aggregation over a 3×3-eroded heatmap, and—uniquely among compared methods—histologically meaningful matching pathology terms that explain the prediction to a pathologist.

## Method

Ano-NAViLa freezes the CONCH vision-language model and trains only a 3-layer MLP (1024→128) via a contrastive loss that pulls intra-pool embeddings (image–normal-term and image–abnormal-term) together while pushing the two pools apart, using exclusively normal training images. For each patch, the image embedding is concatenated with every text embedding in a pool after softmax-weighted scaling by cosine similarity, the MLP maps each 1024-dim pair to a 128-dim text-augmented embedding, and averaging across all terms yields one representative embedding per pool. At inference, anomaly score equals the sum of (1 − cosine similarity) deviations from two centroid embeddings computed on normal validation patches, so an abnormal patch deviates from both the normal-term and abnormal-term clusters simultaneously. This dual-pool design, combined with the frozen VLM backbone, yields only 0.69 M trainable parameters and 3.61 ms latency while providing interpretable text–image associations.

## Results

On GastricLN (WSI-level), Ano-NAViLa achieves 0.9967 AUROC and 0.9971 AUPR (A_max score), with patch-level AUROC of 0.9681. On the external Camelyon16 test set, Ano-NAViLa attains 0.8594 AUROC and 0.8309 AUPR (A_max), and on Camelyon16_macro it reaches 0.9858 AUROC and 0.9547 AUPR, detecting macro-metastases near-perfectly. In terms of efficiency, Ano-NAViLa has only 0.69M trainable parameters and 3.61 ms latency, making it the most parameter-efficient model tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ano-NAViLa outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and metric. On the in-domain GastricLN test set, the gap over the second-best model (STFPM, 0.9779 AUROC) is modest at 0.0188 AUROC, but on the cross-domain Camelyon16 test set the advantage widens dramatically: Ano-NAViLa achieves 0.8594 AUROC versus 0.7158 for STFPM and 0.7003 for CFA, a margin of 0.14–0.16 AUROC. The gap is even more pronounced on Camelyon16_macro, where Ano-NAViLa reaches 0.9858 AUROC compared to 0.7710 (STFPM) and 0.7574 (CFA), a margin of 0.21–0.23 AUROC. Reconstruction-based methods (GANomaly, AnoDDPM) and the VLM-based AnomalyCLIP perform substantially worse, with AnomalyCLIP achieving only 0.3314 patch-level AUROC. Notably, Ano-NAViLa also has the fewest trainable parameters (0.69M) and the lowest latency (3.61 ms) among all compared methods, combining superior accuracy with computational efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of VLM-based anomaly detection (AnomalyCLIP, WinCLIP, InCTRL) and pathology-specific vision-language models (CONCH, PLIP, QuiltNet), but departs from both by introducing expert-curated normal and abnormal pathology term pools as a structured knowledge layer. The text-augmented embedding mechanism is inspired by TQx, but the dual-pool contrastive clustering and centroid-deviation scoring are novel. Unlike prior VLM-based AD methods that treat text prompts as a generic interface, Ano-NAViLa leverages histologically specific terminology validated by pathologists, making the VLM's image-text alignment clinically grounded rather than purely data-driven.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combination of near-SOTA detection accuracy, cross-organ generalization (gastric→breast), and histologically interpretable text explanations addresses a critical gap in clinical AD: most existing methods are black-box and computationally heavy, limiting real-world adoption. The lightweight design (0.69M trainable parameters, frozen VLM) makes deployment feasible. Key open questions include automating term-pool construction (currently manual and pathologist-dependent), validating across diverse organ types beyond lymph nodes, and reducing the VLM's memory footprint (2098 MiB). The narrow evaluation scope—two datasets, both lymph node metastasis—limits confidence in broader generalizability.
