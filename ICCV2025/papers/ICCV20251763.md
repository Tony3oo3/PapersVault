## Problem

Existing human-human interaction methods either reconstruct static interactive poses without temporal dynamics or generate two-person motions from text while struggling to maintain realistic close-contact interactions. No prior work exploits the strong prior embedded in proximal interactive poses—where a pair of close-contact poses intuitively constrains both past and future motion—as an explicit anchor for interaction animation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251763/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework accepts flexible inputs: a two-person image (with interactive poses estimated via BUDDI), a single-person image (pose estimated via SMPLer-X, optionally with a text prompt), or a short text phrase alone. Outputs are 3-second two-person motion sequences at 10 fps represented as SMPLX parameters (joint rotations, global orientation, translation, and shape), plus intermediate interactive pose pairs. The motion is parameterized as residuals relative to the anchor interactive pose, and the interactive time index I is adjustable to control whether the animation predicts future, reconstructs past, or produces symmetric dynamics.

## Method

Ponimator decomposes interaction animation into two conditional diffusion models bridged by interactive poses: a pose animator that generates motion sequences from a given interactive pose (temporal prior) and a pose generator that synthesizes interactive poses from a single pose, text, or both (spatial prior). The animator uses a DiT architecture with alternating spatial and temporal attention layers, where the denoising target is the motion residual relative to the interactive pose, and an imputation strategy zeros out noise at the interactive frame to preserve its spatial structure. Conditions (interactive pose, shape, time index) are injected via AdaIN using SMPLX forward-kinematics joint positions. The generator employs a unified masking scheme with Bernoulli-sampled text and pose masks, CLIP-encoded text, and rest-pose joint positions to handle shape/gender generically. Both models are trained on close-proximity two-person clips from Inter-X and Dual-Human mocap datasets with diffusion, SMPL, interaction, and velocity losses, and the animator is augmented with Gaussian noise on interactive poses for robustness to real-world estimation errors.

## Results

On the Inter-X unconstrained interaction synthesis benchmark, Ponimator achieves a FID of 22.6, substantially outperforming all baselines (InterGen: 56.6, RIG: 65.2, MDM*: 62.6, ComMDM: 88.8). It also attains the highest Contact Frame Ratio (68.1% vs. 44.3–66.4% for baselines) and best Recall (0.72 vs. 0.20–0.65), while maintaining competitive Precision (0.58) and Diversity (10.2, close to GT's 10.1). On the interactive pose animation task (Table 2), Ponimator achieves FID 5.0 on Inter-X and 24.2 on Dual-Human, outperforming the adapted InterGen* (18.9 and 88.8 respectively) and all ablation variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ponimator decisively outperforms all four baselines on FID (22.6 vs. 56.6–88.8) and Contact Frame Ratio (68.1% vs. 44.3–66.4%), confirming that anchoring on interactive poses yields both more realistic motion and more reliable physical contact. Against InterGen [29], the closest competitor, Ponimator improves FID by 2.5× and CR by 17.2 percentage points, while matching Precision (0.58 vs. 0.57). Against ComMDM [46] and RIG [53], which rely on single-person priors fine-tuned with interaction data, Ponimator achieves 3–4× lower FID and roughly 24 percentage points higher contact ratio, indicating that explicit interactive-pose conditioning is far more effective than implicit interaction learning. The only metric where Ponimator trails is Inter-person Penetration (5.0 cm vs. 4.3 cm for RIG and InterGen), a minor trade-off for the substantial gains in motion quality and contact fidelity. MDM* [54], adapted for two-person motion, shows the weakest Recall (0.20) and highest FID among the adapted baselines, suggesting that general-purpose motion diffusion struggles with close-contact interaction dynamics without dedicated interaction modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

Ponimator builds on the DiT transformer-diffusion architecture and standard conditional diffusion training, but its core conceptual contribution is the factorization of interaction motion into a temporal prior (pose-to-dynamics) and a spatial prior (single-pose-to-interactive-pose), bridged by close-proximity interactive poses as anchors. This departs from prior two-person motion generation work (InterGen, RIG, ComMDM) that either generates motions end-to-end from text or fine-tunes single-person priors, and from static interactive pose estimation (BUDDI, RemiPS) that ignores temporal dynamics. The paper positions interactive poses as a natural, interpretable intermediate that encodes both spatial constraints and temporal context, reducing the modeling complexity compared to learning interaction dynamics from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's practical value lies in its versatility: a single trained model supports image-based interaction animation, single-pose reaction generation, and text-to-interaction synthesis, with demonstrated generalization to out-of-domain datasets and even multi-person scenarios without retraining. However, limitations remain significant: the model only generates 3-second clips, ignores scene context, and is sensitive to pose estimation errors that propagate into contact failures or foot sliding. Open questions include whether the interactive pose prior scales to longer sequences, whether scene-aware conditioning can be integrated, and whether the approach extends to more than two persons in a principled way beyond the qualitative multi-person example.
