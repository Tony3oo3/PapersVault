## Problem

Semi-supervised continual learning (SSCL) requires simultaneously managing unlabeled learning, memory stability, and learning plasticity, yet prior methods address only one or two of these aspects in isolation. This paper argues that a holistic, divide-and-conquer approach is needed to synergistically enhance all three dimensions rather than treating them as independent sub-problems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251855/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32x32 for CIFAR, 224x224 for ImageNet-100/CUB) in a sequential task-by-task stream where each task provides a small labeled subset (e.g., 13-150 images per class) and a much larger unlabeled subset, with only a 5120-slot exemplar buffer retaining past data. At test time, the DCP strategy acts as a 'free lunch': high-confidence predictions use the classifier directly, while low-confidence samples are reclassified via nearest-class-mean matching against the full exemplar set, improving accuracy without any additional training cost.

## Method

USP builds on a FixMatch-based SSL loss combined with iCaRL or DER distillation, and adds three coupled components that all operate on L2-normalized features from a shared 512-dim projection head. For plasticity, FSR pre-computes an equiangular tight frame (ETF) over the full label space and applies a contrastive loss pulling labeled and high-confidence unlabeled features toward their corresponding ETF prototype vectors, reserving geometric capacity for future classes. For unlabeled learning, DCP splits unlabeled data by confidence: high-confidence samples receive hard pseudo-labels from the classifier (as in FixMatch), while low-confidence samples are assigned pseudo-labels via nearest-class-mean (NCM) matching against per-class feature means computed from labeled exemplars, recovering data that standard thresholding would discard. For stability, CUD reuses the NCM class-mean vectors as anchors and applies a KL-divergence distillation loss between the current and previous model's similarity distributions over these anchors for all unlabeled samples, explicitly preventing forgetting on the unlabeled portion of the data.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with 80 labels per class (10 tasks), USP with DER&FixMatch achieves 68.67% average and 60.45% last accuracy, outperforming DSGD (65.48% / 55.40%) by 3.19% and 5.05% respectively. On ImageNet-100 with 13 labels per class, USP (iCaRL&Fix) reaches 43.91% average accuracy versus DSGD's 28.35%, a 15.56% gain. On the few-shot CUB benchmark (11 tasks), USP improves average accuracy from 64.33% (UaD-CIE) to 66.43%, with a 10.99% gain in novel-class accuracy. Overall, USP achieves up to 5.94% gain in last accuracy and 4.10% in average accuracy over the strongest baseline (DSGD) across settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

USP outperforms the main competitor DSGD [17] on nearly all settings: with iCaRL&Fix on CIFAR100-80, USP gains +0.67% Avg and +6.39% Last; with DER&Fix on CIFAR100-125, it gains +2.46% Avg and +4.58% Last. On ImageNet-100-13 (iCaRL&Fix), USP achieves 43.91% Avg vs DSGD's 28.35%, a 15.56% advantage, indicating superior unlabeled-data utilization under extreme label scarcity. Against fully-supervised CL baselines iCaRL [38] and DER [52] (adapted to SSCL by discarding unlabeled data), USP gains 33–45% in average accuracy, confirming the value of leveraging unlabeled samples. On CIFAR100 settings, USP surpasses NNCSL [23] by 1.3–3.2% in average accuracy (e.g., 58.79% vs 55.19% on CIFAR100-20 with DER&Fix). On the few-shot CUB benchmark, USP improves over UaD-CIE [12] by 2.10% in average accuracy (66.43% vs 64.33%) and by 10.99% in novel-class accuracy, while also outperforming Semi-CEC [55] (61.32%), Us-KD [11] (62.89%), and Semi-SPRI [62] (49.34%). The only setting where USP does not lead is CIFAR10-150 (iCaRL&Fix) last accuracy (78.21% vs DSGD's 79.69%), suggesting DSGD retains a slight edge when labeled data is abundant.

## Relation to Prior Work

USP builds on the FixMatch pseudo-labeling framework for the SSL component and iCaRL/DER for the CL component, following the baseline SSCL learner established by DSGD [17], which is positioned as the main competitor. Conceptually, it departs from prior SSCL methods (CCIC, ORDisCo, DSGD, NNCSL) that each address one or two of the three core challenges—unlabeled learning, memory stability, and learning plasticity—by proposing a unified divide-and-conquer framework where all three are tackled simultaneously through a shared projection head. The ETF-based feature space reservation draws on neural collapse theory [54, 60], while the divide-and-conquer pseudo-labeling repurposes NCM classification [32] from CL to complement classifier-based pseudo-labeling, and the class-mean-anchored distillation reuses intermediate NCM computations to create a cross-labeled-unlabeled distillation signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, modular framework for SSCL where each component (FSR, DCP, CUD) is independently interpretable and ablatable, making it a useful reference architecture for future SSCL work. The 'free lunch' test-time benefit of DCP (using NCM for low-confidence test samples) is a practical advantage that costs nothing at inference. However, the method adds multiple loss terms and a projection head, increasing implementation complexity, and the evaluated datasets (CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet-100, CUB) are relatively small; scalability to larger-scale or non-visual SSCL settings remains untested. Open questions include whether the ETF-based reservation generalizes to overlapping-class or domain-incremental settings, and whether the synergy between components holds under different backbone architectures or memory buffer constraints.
