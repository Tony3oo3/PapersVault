## Problem

Existing VFM adapters (ViT-Adapter, ViT-CoMer) suffer from two inefficiencies: the CNN-ViT cross-branch interaction forces gradient backpropagation through all early VFM layers, inflating compute and memory costs, and all components must be jointly tuned, adding complexity. Moreover, these adapters modify the VFM's pretrained features during training, underutilizing the rich prior knowledge already embedded in the frozen backbone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251254/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image (512×512 or 896×896 for segmentation; standard COCO resolutions for detection) that is processed by a frozen VFM backbone into h×w vision tokens of D channels per layer. For VQA, an image plus a text question is fed through the same frozen encoder before being projected into an LLM decoder. Outputs are task-specific: a 4×-upsampled segmentation map, four-scale feature pyramids for Mask R-CNN (bounding boxes + instance masks), a depth map, or a text answer generated by the LLM.

## Method

ViT-Split is built on the observation that VFM layers (especially DINOv2) naturally partition into a low-level feature extractor (early layers) and a task-specific adapter (later layers), verified via CKA similarity and feature visualizations. The entire VFM backbone is kept frozen; a task head, initialized by copying the last Kt transformer layers, receives features from layer (L−Kt) and learns task-specific representations without backpropagating into early layers. A prior head (a 1×1 convolution followed by a 3×3 deformable convolution) aggregates multi-scale prior features from Kp uniformly sampled frozen layers into a single D-channel map. A fusion net with the same two-layer CNN architecture concatenates the task and prior feature maps along the channel dimension and produces the final D-channel output for downstream heads. This design eliminates the separate CNN branch entirely, reduces trainable parameters to roughly 1/4–1/5 of prior adapters, and achieves up to 4× faster training while matching or exceeding their accuracy.

## Results

On ADE20K val (512×512), ViT-Split achieves 51.6/55.7/58.2 mIoU (S/B/L) with only 10.2M/40.5M/88.6M trainable parameters and a simple linear head, surpassing ViT-Adapter (46.2/48.8/53.4) and ViT-CoMer (46.5/48.8/54.3) by 5–7 mIoU while using 1/5 to 1/4 the parameters and 1/4 the training iterations. On ADE20K val (896×896), ViT-Split-G reaches 60.2/60.8 mIoU (SS/MS) with 326M params, competitive with ViT-CoMer-L (61.7/62.1, 604M params). On COCO detection, ViT-Split-S achieves 48.5 AP^b, matching ViT-CoMer-S (48.6) with fewer parameters and 42% less training time. Training speed is ~4× faster (small) and ~3× faster (base) compared to ViT-Adapter and ViT-CoMer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViT-Split decisively outperforms the two main VFM adapter baselines (ViT-Adapter [12] and ViT-CoMer [65]) on ADE20K segmentation by 5.4 mIoU (S), 6.9 mIoU (B), and 3.9 mIoU (L), while tuning 1/5 to 1/4 the parameters and training in 1/4 the iterations. Against full fine-tuning of DINOv2 [57] (the backbone), ViT-Split improves mIoU by +2.0 (S), +1.9 (B), and +2.1 (L) over the Linear-head variant, confirming that the prior head adds value beyond simply fine-tuning the last layers. On COCO detection (Table 5), ViT-Split is within 0.1–0.4 AP of ViT-CoMer at all sizes (e.g., 48.5 vs. 48.6 AP^b at S; 53.0 vs. 53.4 at L), a near-tie achieved with fewer parameters and 42% less training time. On ADE20K 896×896 (Table 2), ViT-Split-G (60.2/60.8 SS/MS) slightly trails ViT-CoMer-L (61.7/62.1) but uses half the parameters and no extra pretraining data, while beating ViT-Adapter-G (~/60.2) with half the training cost. For VQA (Table 4), integrating ViT-Split into LLaVA-1.5's CLIP encoder yields consistent gains over the original LLaVA-1.5 (e.g., +5.7 on LLaVA-Wild, +3.6 on SciQA-IMG, +2.1 on MMBench), demonstrating generalization beyond dense prediction tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViT-Split departs from the dominant two-branch CNN+ViT adapter paradigm (ViT-Adapter, ViT-CoMer) by eliminating the CNN branch entirely, based on the observation that VFM early layers already learn low-level features equivalent to what a CNN would provide. It also contrasts with PEFT methods (LoRA, prompt tuning, AdaptFormer) by keeping the entire backbone frozen and instead introducing two lightweight heads—a task head copied from the VFM's last few layers and a prior head that aggregates multi-scale frozen features—rather than inserting learnable parameters into every layer. The method builds directly on DINOv2 as the primary backbone but demonstrates generality across MAE, SAM, and SigLip.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a 4× training speedup and 3–5× fewer trainable parameters while matching or exceeding prior VFM adapters on segmentation, detection, and VQA, making multi-task deployment with a shared frozen backbone significantly cheaper in both compute and memory. The conceptual insight that VFM layers naturally partition into a task-agnostic extractor and a task-specific adapter challenges the assumption that a separate CNN inductive-bias branch is necessary for dense prediction. Open questions include whether the layer-splitting observation holds as strongly for all VFM families (it is clearest for DINOv2), how the method behaves on tasks requiring substantial architectural changes beyond the last few layers, and whether the frozen-backbone constraint becomes a bottleneck for very domain-specific downstream tasks.
