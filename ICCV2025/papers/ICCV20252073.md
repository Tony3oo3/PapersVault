## Problem

Existing video-to-music retrieval (V2MR) methods retrieve an entire music track for a query video, but in practice music tracks (~139 s) are far longer than short videos (~24 s), requiring manual trimming to a suitable segment. No prior task, dataset, or method addresses the problem of automatically localizing the specific music moment that best serves as background music for a given short video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252073/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query video (decoded at 1 FPS, frames resized to 256×256) and a music track (represented as partially overlapped 128-bin Mel-spectrogram segments) as multi-modal input. It produces a three-dimensional output (p_s, p_c, p_w): a relevance score, and the normalized temporal center and width of the detected music moment. In single-music mode the relevance score is discarded; in music-set mode all candidate tracks are ranked by p_s and the moment from the top-ranked track is selected as the final grounding result.

## Method

MaDe is a unified end-to-end Transformer network with three modules: (1) multimodal embedding using frozen CLIP (ViT-B/32) and AST encoders followed by FC compression to 256-d and a self-attention block for temporal enhancement; (2) video-music matching that combines mean-pooled and X-Pool (video-conditioned cross-attention) track embeddings via summed cosine similarity; and (3) music moment detection that adapts Moment-DETR by concatenating visual and audio tokens, fusing them with two SA blocks, then decoding with six cross-attention blocks (no SA in the decoder) using a single query token initialized with the video embedding h(v) rather than zero. A key empirical finding is that removing cross-attention from the multimodal fusion stage (as in original DETR-based grounding models) consistently prevents overfitting and improves performance. Training uses a combined symmetric InfoNCE loss for matching and L1 + GIoU regression loss for detection, optimized end-to-end with Adam and a cosine learning-rate schedule.

## Results

On the MGSV-EC benchmark, MaDe achieves an mIoU of 0.722 on single-music grounding (SmG), surpassing the best video-grounding baseline UVCOM+ (0.661) by 6.1 points. For music-set grounding (MsG), MaDe attains MoR1 of 8.3, MoR10 of 17.6, and MoR100 of 30.7, outperforming the composite MVPt+/UVCOM+ solution (5.4/11.8/23.0) and CONQUER (4.4/9.6/18.4). On the video-to-music retrieval (V2MR) sub-task, MaDe reaches R1 8.8, R5 16.3, and R10 19.8, exceeding MVPt+ (6.7/11.9/14.9). Across three random data splits, MaDe maintains its lead with mIoU 0.743±0.017 and MoR1 8.4±0.3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaDe outperforms all seven baselines across every reported metric. On SmG, it beats the strongest video-grounding baseline UVCOM+ (mIoU 0.661) by 6.1 points, and the original UVCOM (0.652) by 7.0 points; notably, removing cross-attention from QD-DETR and TR-DETR more than doubles their mIoU (0.423→0.634 and 0.393→0.630), revealing that CA-based fusion harms music grounding. On V2MR, MaDe (R1 8.8) surpasses MVPt+ (R1 6.7) by 2.1 points and CONQUER (R1 5.8) by 3.0 points. On the full MsG task, MaDe (MoR1 8.3) exceeds the composite MVPt+/UVCOM+ (MoR1 5.4) by 2.9 points and CONQUER (MoR1 4.4) by 3.9 points, while also using fewer parameters (10.5M vs. 16.5M and 39.4M respectively). A human evaluation on 100 test videos confirms the gap: MaDe is preferred over the composite solution in 38.1% of cases versus 28.6%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the DETR-based Video Grounding paradigm (Moment-DETR, QD-DETR, UVCOM, etc.) for a new modality pair—video-to-music instead of text-to-video—and combines it with Video-to-Music Retrieval (MVPt) in a unified network. It departs from prior Video Grounding models in three key ways: removing cross-attention from multimodal fusion (finding it causes overfitting), using the video embedding as the initial decoder content token rather than zero, and reducing query tokens from 10 to 1. For the matching component, it adapts X-Pool (originally for video-text retrieval) to obtain a video-conditioned music track embedding, addressing the partial-relevance problem inherent in matching short videos to long music tracks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MGSV addresses a practical gap: V2MR retrieves entire tracks but real-world short video creation requires localizing a specific ~24-second segment within a ~140-second track. The MGSV-EC dataset (53k videos, 35k moments, 4k tracks) is the first large-scale benchmark enabling this evaluation, and its e-commerce provenance with user editing logs provides reliable ground truth. The finding that CA-based multimodal fusion consistently degrades performance across multiple architectures is notable but the paper leaves open the deeper question of how video-music interaction fundamentally differs from text-video interaction. Personalization to user preferences and the diversity of acceptable music choices remain unaddressed.
