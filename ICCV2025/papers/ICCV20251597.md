## Problem

Amodal depth estimation—predicting depth for occluded, invisible parts of objects—has been limited to synthetic datasets and metric depth formulations, causing poor zero-shot generalization to real-world scenes. No device can capture ground-truth depth behind occlusions at scale, and prior synthetic-data approaches require manually placing occluders, making them hard to scale and domain-shifted from natural images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251597/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes three inputs: an RGB observation image I_o, a corresponding relative depth map D_o (produced by Depth Anything V2), and a target amodal mask M_a (obtainable via SAM + amodal segmentation or manual annotation). It outputs a relative depth map for the occluded (invisible) portion of the target object; the visible portion's depth is taken directly from D_o. All depth values are relative (scaled to [0,1]) rather than metric, and the amodal mask defines which pixels the model must predict.

## Method

Two complementary frameworks are proposed with minimal modifications to large pre-trained depth models. Amodal-DAV2 adds a zero-initialized Guidance Convolution layer in parallel with the RGB Conv in DAV2's ViT encoder, allowing it to ingest the observation depth map and amodal mask as extra channels while preserving pre-trained weights; it is trained end-to-end with a scale-invariant log loss over the full object. Amodal-DepthFM extends DepthFM's conditional flow-matching UNet by widening the first Conv layer to accept the same guidance channels (zero-initialized for the new channels) and applies scale-and-shift alignment at inference to blend predicted and observed depth over shared visible regions. Both are trained on the newly introduced ADIW dataset (564K images), constructed by compositing objects from SA1B segmentation masks onto natural backgrounds, generating depth with DAV2 (ViT-G), and enforcing consistency via a scale-and-shift alignment between the composite and background depth maps.

## Results

On the ADIW dataset, Amodal-DAV2-L achieves the best overall RMSE of 3.418 and δ accuracy of 94.179%, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art (Jo et al. with alignment, RMSE 4.712) by 27.4% in RMSE. The generative Amodal-DepthFM with scale-and-shift alignment reaches RMSE 4.645 and δ 92.295%, also surpassing all prior methods. Across difficulty levels, Amodal-DAV2-L maintains consistent performance (RMSE 3.324–3.476 from Easy to Hard), while baselines degrade more sharply on harder occlusion levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Amodal-DAV2-L (RMSE 3.418) outperforms the strongest prior amodal depth method, Jo et al. with retraining and alignment (RMSE 4.712), by 27.4% in RMSE and 5.0 points in δ accuracy. Against the inpainting-based Invisible Stitch (RMSE 7.481), the improvement is 54.3% in RMSE, with the paper attributing the gap to cascade errors from inaccurate RGB inpainting. The amodal segmentation approach pix2gestalt (RMSE 5.114) is also surpassed by 33.2% in RMSE, as its uncontrollable shape completion introduces depth estimation errors. Sekkat et al.'s synthetic-driving method (RMSE 5.194 with alignment) lags by 34.2% in RMSE, reflecting the domain gap between synthetic driving scenes and diverse natural images. Notably, even Amodal-DepthFM without alignment (RMSE 5.410) beats all prior methods except Jo et al. with alignment, and with alignment it narrows the gap to Amodal-DAV2-L to just 1.2 RMSE points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the amodal depth estimation task (previously addressed by Jo et al. [17] and Sekkat et al. [38] on synthetic driving/indoor scenes with metric depth) by reframing it as relative depth prediction on real-world images. Methodologically, it builds on two strong pre-trained depth backbones—Depth Anything V2 (a ViT-based deterministic estimator) and DepthFM (a flow-matching generative model)—adding only lightweight guidance channels for the observation depth map and amodal mask. The dataset construction pipeline repurposes the pix2gestalt compositing strategy [28] and SAM [21] for segmentation, then uses DAV2 itself to generate depth labels, creating a self-referential but scalable annotation loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ADIW dataset (564K images) is the first large-scale real-world resource for amodal depth, removing the synthetic-domain bottleneck that limited prior work. The shift from metric to relative depth is the key conceptual contribution: it enables training on natural images and dramatically improves zero-shot generalization, as evidenced by the 50.7% RMSE gap over prior SoTA. However, ground-truth depth for truly occluded regions is still synthesized via compositing rather than measured, and the 2× RMSE degradation on edge regions signals that fine-grained occluded geometry remains an open challenge. The finding that inpainting-based pipelines (Invisible Stitch, pix2gestalt) suffer severe cascade errors from inaccurate RGB completion, while direct depth regression with mask guidance avoids this, provides a useful design principle for future occlusion-aware geometry tasks.
