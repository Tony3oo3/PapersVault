## Problem

Genotype-to-phenotype prediction is fundamentally limited to individual species because phenotypic labeling is expensive and domain-specific, making cross-species generalization intractable with conventional GWAS or QTL mapping approaches. This paper reframes the problem as conditional image generation using morphological images as phenotypic proxies, enabling cross-species prediction by leveraging large-scale multimodal data across diverse taxa.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250131/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a query DNA sequence (tokenized into k-mers), geographic coordinates (longitude and latitude mapped to 3D spherical coordinates), and a set of m homologous sequences retrieved via MMseqs2 from an external database. It produces a 256×256 RGB morphological image of the organism. During sampling, n candidate images are generated per DNA input and the best-scoring one is selected based on CLIBDScore, Success Rate, or PES metrics.

## Method

G2PDiffusion combines three components: (1) an MSA retrieval engine using MMseqs2 to fetch homologous sequences and compute a binary conservation vector; (2) an environment-aware MSA conditioner built on a transformer that applies evolution-aware row attention (gated by the conservation vector via an MLP) and environment-aware column attention (gated by spherical-coordinate-derived weights) to produce a genotype-environment (GxE) representation; and (3) a conditional diffusion model (DDPM backbone) that generates images conditioned on the GxE embedding. A dynamic alignment module trained with a contrastive (InfoNCE-style) loss aligns noisy intermediate images to the DNA embedding at each denoising step, injecting gradient guidance into the reverse diffusion trajectory to improve genotype-phenotype consistency beyond standard classifier-free guidance.

## Results

On BIOSCAN-5M, G2PDiffusion achieves a Top-5 CLIBDScore of 0.302 and Top-5 Success Rate of 65.8%, substantially outperforming Stable Diffusion (0.219 / 36.6%) and ControlNet (0.228 / 39.1%). At Top-100, the Success Rate reaches 94.0% (vs. 74.8% for Stable Diffusion and 77.0% for ControlNet), approaching the ground-truth rate of 96.4%. On the Phenotype Embedding Similarity (PES) metric, G2PDiffusion scores 0.291 at Top-5 and 0.511 at Top-100, roughly 37–40% higher than the best diffusion baseline (ControlNet: 0.212 / 0.403).

## Baselines & Comparisons

G2PDiffusion outperforms all three baselines across every metric and every top-k threshold. Against DF-GAN (a GAN-based text-to-image method), the advantage is the largest: at Top-5 Success Rate, G2PDiffusion reaches 65.8% versus only 18.7% for DF-GAN, and at Top-1 CLIBDScore the gap is 0.182 vs. 0.054. Against the two diffusion-based baselines, the margin narrows but remains substantial: at Top-5 Success Rate, G2PDiffusion (65.8%) beats Stable Diffusion (36.6%) by ~29 points and ControlNet (39.1%) by ~27 points. At Top-100 the gap shrinks to ~20 points over Stable Diffusion (94.0% vs. 74.8%) and ~17 points over ControlNet (94.0% vs. 77.0%), suggesting that with more samples the baselines can occasionally hit the correct phenotype. On the unseen-species (open-world) split, G2PDiffusion still leads (Top-100 Success Rate 80.3% vs. 53.1% for Stable Diffusion and 56.3% for ControlNet), though absolute scores drop noticeably, indicating that cross-species generalization remains challenging.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the conditional diffusion model paradigm (Stable Diffusion, ControlNet) from text-to-image generation to the genotype-to-phenotype domain, where prior work has relied on statistical methods (GWAS, QTL mapping) or single-species deep learning regressors/classifiers. It departs from the DNABERT-style DNA encoding by introducing an MSA retrieval engine (via MMseqs2) that injects evolutionary conservation signals, and from standard cross-attention conditioning by designing row/column attention mechanisms that separately encode sequence-level evolutionary gating and geographic environment. The CLIBD contrastive model provides the evaluation backbone, extending CLIP-style alignment from text-image to DNA-image pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reframing G2P prediction as cross-species conditional image generation, this work sidesteps the expensive, species-specific phenotype labeling bottleneck that has constrained prior approaches, and the BIOSCAN-5M dataset (5M+ insect specimens) provides a rare large-scale multimodal resource. However, several open questions remain: whether the generated images carry genuine biological signal beyond visual similarity (the CLIBDScore metric is trained on closely related data, risking circularity), whether the approach generalizes beyond insects to vertebrates or plants, and whether the modest unseen-species performance (8.8% Top-1 success rate) is sufficient for practical conservation or breeding applications. The optimal MSA depth finding (m=1–2) also raises questions about how much evolutionary context is actually informative versus noisy.
