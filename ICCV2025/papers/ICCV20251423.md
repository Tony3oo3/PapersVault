## Problem

Diffusion-based object removal and insertion are typically treated as isolated tasks with separate implementations, creating conflicts when deployed together and requiring large paired datasets that are scarce in the real world. Additionally, existing methods struggle to handle complex physical effects (shadows, reflections, occlusions) during editing, and no robust reference-free metric exists to detect hallucinated objects introduced by generative models during removal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251423/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

For removal, the model takes an RGB image with a binary mask (masked pixels zeroed out) and produces a background-only image with the object and its physical effects eliminated. For insertion, it additionally takes a reference object image (preprocessed with CarveKit for background removal and resized to match the scene), producing a composited image with the object seamlessly integrated. Task switching at inference is controlled by selecting one of two learnable embedding vectors rather than text prompts, and the model operates in FLUX's latent space via its VAE encoder and 2×2 patchify layer at 512×512 resolution.

## Method

OmniPaint builds on the frozen FLUX-1.dev MM-DiT backbone with separate LoRA parameter sets for removal (θ) and insertion (φ), using prompt-free learnable task embeddings to switch between modes. Training follows a three-phase pipeline: (1) inpainting pretext on LAION to establish basic completion ability, (2) paired warmup on 3,300 real-world triplets to learn effect-aware removal and identity-preserving insertion, and (3) CycleFlow unpaired post-training on COCO-Stuff/HQSeg, where the trained removal model (φ) preprocesses unpaired latents to strip physical effects before insertion training, and a cycle-consistency loss (remove→insert≈original) enforces that reinserting a removed object restores its latent representation. The key insight is that removal and insertion are mathematical inverses, so the well-trained removal pathway can bootstrap insertion training on unpaired data without requiring effect annotations. A novel no-reference CFD metric combines SAM-based hallucination detection (comparing nested vs. overlapping mask features via DINOv2) with a context coherence term to evaluate removal quality.

## Results

On the 300-sample object removal test set, OmniPaint achieves a FID of 51.66, CMMD of 0.0473, and CFD of 0.2619, substantially outperforming the best baseline FreeCompose (FID 88.77, CMMD 0.1790, CFD 0.3743). On the 1000-sample RORD benchmark, OmniPaint further reduces FID to 19.17 and LPIPS to 0.0424 while attaining the highest ReMOVE score of 0.9053. For object insertion, OmniPaint achieves the best identity preservation across all four metrics (CLIP-I 92.27, DINOv2 84.37, CUTE 90.29, DreamSim 0.1557) and the highest overall image quality (MUSIQ 70.59, MANIQA 0.5209), surpassing the strongest baseline IMPRINT by 1.64 on CLIP-I and 7.48 on DINOv2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniPaint dominates all 12 baselines on the object removal task, achieving the lowest FID (51.66 vs. 88.77 for FreeCompose, the next best), lowest CMMD (0.0473 vs. 0.1790), lowest CFD (0.2619 vs. 0.3743), and lowest LPIPS (0.0738 vs. 0.1182). The only near-tie is ReMOVE, where FreeCompose edges out OmniPaint by 0.004 (0.8654 vs. 0.8610), while LaMa marginally leads on SSIM (0.8278 vs. 0.8135). Among diffusion-based removal methods, OmniPaint outperforms FLUX-Inpainting by 42% in FID and 43% in CMMD, and surpasses the dedicated ObjectRemovalFLUX LoRA by 49% in FID. For object insertion, OmniPaint beats all five baselines (PbE, ObjectStitch, FreeCompose, AnyDoor, IMPRINT) on every identity and quality metric; the closest competitor IMPRINT trails by 7.48 points on DINOv2 (76.89 vs. 84.37) and 4.14 on CUTE (86.15 vs. 90.29), while AnyDoor is closest on DreamSim (0.2208 vs. 0.1557). The gap over early methods like PbE is particularly large, with OmniPaint improving DINOv2 by 34.37 points and CUTE by 25.19 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniPaint builds on the FLUX diffusion backbone (MM-DiT architecture) and adapts OmniControl's image-conditioning mechanism for object-aware editing, but departs from prior work by treating object removal and insertion as mathematically complementary inverse problems rather than independent subtasks. The CycleFlow mechanism draws on cycle-consistency principles from GAN literature (CycleGAN) but transplants them into the flow-matching/rectified-flow setting, enabling unpaired post-training for insertion. The prompt-free adaptive control via learnable task embeddings replaces text conditioning, and the CFD metric introduces a SAM+DINOv2-based reference-free evaluation that addresses a gap left by existing metrics like ReMOVE, which the authors show cannot distinguish hallucination-prone outputs from clean removals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The interdependent framing and CycleFlow mechanism offer a practical path to reducing reliance on large-scale paired datasets (only 3K paired samples needed), which is a significant data-efficiency gain for object editing. The CFD metric fills a real evaluation gap by detecting hallucinated objects that standard metrics miss, potentially becoming a standard for object removal assessment. Limitations include single-subject insertion, 2D-only scope, and dependence on a frozen FLUX backbone; open questions include whether the interdependent formulation extends to video editing, multi-object scenes, or whether the cycle-consistency approach generalizes beyond the specific flow-matching formulation used here.
