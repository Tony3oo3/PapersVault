## Problem

Existing promptable segmentation foundation models (SAM, MedSAM, SAM-Med3D) are designed for structural modalities like CT and MRI with clear anatomical boundaries, and exhibit poor generalization on molecular PET images due to their inherently low resolution, partial volume effects, and diffuse organ boundaries. Compounding this, publicly available PET datasets are small and suffer from heterogeneous annotation quality, making it difficult to train a general-purpose PET segmentation model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250567/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SegAnyPET takes a 3D whole-body PET volume and a small number of point prompts (1, 3, or 5 points, simulated from ground truth with random perturbations) as input, and directly outputs a full 3D segmentation mask for the prompted organ or lesion. Unlike 2D foundation models (SAM, MedSAM) that require per-slice prompting across all N slices containing the target, SegAnyPET operates on the volumetric input in a single forward pass, leveraging inter-slice context. The model is trained on PETS-5k (5,731 3D whole-body PET volumes, >1.3M 2D slices) and evaluated on both seen organs (liver, kidneys, heart, spleen) and training-invisible organs (aorta, lung lobes, prostate).

## Method

SegAnyPET reformulates SAM's architecture into a holistic 3D structure with an image encoder, prompt encoder, and mask decoder that process volumetric PET data directly. The key training innovation is the Cross Prompting Confident Learning (CPCL) strategy, which addresses mixed annotation quality without modifying the network. CPCL has two components: (1) a cross-prompting consistency loss that enforces invariance of segmentation outputs across different prompt configurations on low-quality (LQ) data, acting as unsupervised regularization; and (2) an uncertainty-guided self-rectification process that estimates per-voxel predictive entropy from multi-prompt predictions and flips labels in high-uncertainty regions to correct noisy annotations before supervised training. The total loss combines supervised loss on expert-verified HQ data, the consistency regularization, and rectified supervised loss on LQ data, with a ramp-up coefficient λ to prevent early-stage domination by noisy targets.

## Results

On the PETS-5k internal test set, SegAnyPET achieves a mean DSC of 90.49% with a single point prompt, 90.90% with 3 points, and 91.05% with 5 points for seen organ segmentation. For training-invisible organs (Table 2), SegAnyPET maintains a mean DSC of 89.04% with 1 point and 89.98% with 5 points, demonstrating strong zero-shot generalization. The best competing foundation model (SAM-Med3D-turbo) reaches only 72.09% (1 point) on seen organs and 36.20% (1 point) on unseen organs, while the best task-specific model (nnUNet trained on HQ data) achieves 89.83% without any prompt requirement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SegAnyPET decisively outperforms all promptable foundation model baselines. SAM [14] and MedSAM [20] (both 2D) fail catastrophically on PET, achieving only 13.15% and 0.46% mean DSC respectively with N-point prompts, due to the lack of volumetric context and the distribution gap between natural/structural images and molecular PET. Among 3D medical SAM variants, SAM-Med3D [32] reaches 35.46% (1 pt), SAM-Med3D-organ reaches 60.79%, and SAM-Med3D-turbo reaches 72.09%—all substantially below SegAnyPET's 90.49% (1 pt), a gap of 18.40% over the best foundation model. Against task-specific fully supervised models trained on HQ data, SegAnyPET (90.49%, 1 pt) edges out nnUNet (89.83%), STUNet (89.61%), SwinUNET (88.57%), and SegResNet (87.11%) while requiring only a single point prompt rather than full supervision. On training-invisible organs (Table 2), the advantage widens dramatically: SegAnyPET achieves 89.04% (1 pt) versus 42.87% for the best foundation model (SAM-Med3D-organ), confirming that the CPCL training strategy and 3D architecture confer genuine generalization rather than task-specific memorization.

## Relation to Prior Work

SegAnyPET directly extends the SAM architecture (image encoder, prompt encoder, mask decoder) from 2D natural images to a 3D volumetric formulation tailored for PET, departing from prior medical SAM adaptations (MedSAM, SAM-Med3D) that target structural modalities like CT and MRI. The training strategy draws on uncertainty-guided consistency learning [40] but repurposes the prompting mechanism itself as the source of uncertainty estimates, yielding the novel CPCL strategy for noisy-label-robust training. The PETS-5k dataset fills a gap left by existing PET benchmarks (AutoPET, HECKTOR), which cover limited targets and lack the scale needed for foundation-model pre-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first foundation model purpose-built for molecular PET imaging, SegAnyPET opens a path toward universal organ and lesion segmentation in a modality that has been largely neglected by the medical foundation-model community. The CPCL strategy offers a practical recipe for leveraging the large volumes of imperfectly annotated PET data that are inevitable in clinical settings, and the strong zero-shot generalization to unseen organs (~89% DSC) suggests the learned representations transfer well across anatomy. Key limitations remain: the model still requires manual point prompts (limiting clinical throughput), is validated only on FDG-PET, and has not yet been extended to tumor lesion segmentation or multi-tracer scenarios—areas the authors flag as future work.
