## Problem

Existing Mamba-based point cloud methods suffer from two fundamental limitations: (1) serializing 3D points into 1D sequences destroys spatial adjacency, preventing the SSM from modeling fine-grained local structure, and (2) the selective state update mechanism is insensitive to input length, so models pre-trained on short masked sequences fail to retain long-sequence memory when applied to full point clouds in downstream tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw 3D point cloud of N₀=1024 points, which is partitioned into 64 patches of 32 points each via FPS and KNN clustering. During pre-training, 60% of patches are masked and the model reconstructs the missing geometry; during downstream fine-tuning, the full point cloud (1024 or 2048 points) is fed in. The encoder outputs per-point token embeddings and M=16 spatial state features that serve as structural summaries, which are then passed to task-specific heads for class labels, per-point part labels, or few-shot predictions.

## Method

StruMamba3D introduces a Structural SSM Block that assigns 3D positional attributes to the SSM's latent states (initialized as FPS+KNN centroids), turning them into 'spatial states' that act as structural proxies. A state-wise update mechanism computes relative offsets between each input point and each spatial state, feeding these offsets through an MLP into the SSM's B and C parameters so that states selectively aggregate features from spatially nearby points. A lightweight graph-style convolution over k-nearest spatial states enables inter-state interaction, and bidirectional scanning captures both forward and backward dependencies. To address the length-sensitivity problem, a Sequence Length-Adaptive Strategy normalizes the per-token sampling interval Δ by the total sequence length (via a learnable scalar τ) so the total sampling time remains constant regardless of input length, and a spatial state consistency loss (SmoothL1 between student states from masked input and EMA-teacher states from full input) is added to the MPM reconstruction loss during pre-training.

## Results

On the most challenging ScanObjectNN PB-T50-RS split, StruMamba3D achieves 92.75% accuracy without voting, surpassing the previous best Mamba-based method Mamba3D (92.05%) and the best Transformer-based method PointGPT-S (89.17%). On ModelNet40, it reaches 95.1% (w/o voting) and 95.4% (w/ voting), setting new SOTA among all single-modal pre-training methods. For part segmentation on ShapeNetPart, StruMamba3D achieves 85.0% mIoU_c and 86.7% mIoU_i, outperforming all single-scale baselines including PointM2AE (84.9/86.5). In few-shot classification, it leads with 97.5% (5-way-10shot) and 93.5% (10-way-10shot) accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StruMamba3D outperforms all 21 baselines across every evaluated metric. Against the closest Mamba-based competitors, it beats PointMamba [21] by +0.86%, +1.03%, and +3.44% on the three ScanObjectNN splits, +1.5% on ModelNet40 (w/o voting), and +0.6%/+0.5% in mIoU_c/mIoU_i on ShapeNetPart. Against Mamba3D [15], the margins are +2.06%, +1.55%, +0.70% on ScanObjectNN, +0.4% on ModelNet40, and +1.4%/+1.2% on ShapeNetPart. Versus the strongest Transformer-based pre-training method PointGPT-S [3], StruMamba3D gains +3.58% on ScanObjectNN PB-T50-RS and +1.8% on ModelNet40. It also surpasses the cross-modal method ReCon [29] by +2.12% on PB-T50-RS and +0.6% on ModelNet40, while using fewer parameters (15.8M vs. 43.6M). In few-shot settings, it leads PointMamba by +0.6% (5-way-10shot) and +0.5% (10-way-10shot), and Mamba3D by +1.1% and +0.9% respectively, confirming robustness under limited fine-tuning data.

## Relation to Prior Work

StruMamba3D extends the Mamba-based point cloud line (PointMamba, PCM, Mamba3D) by replacing their serialization-dependent design with spatial states that carry positional attributes, effectively using SSM hidden states as structural proxies rather than relying on 1D ordering to preserve 3D adjacency. It also departs from the standard Mamba pre-training recipe (inherited from PointMAE's masked point modeling) by introducing a sequence length-adaptive strategy that adjusts the SSM sampling interval Δ to maintain consistent total sampling time across input lengths, addressing a gap none of the prior Mamba-based methods considered. The lightweight convolution module for inter-state interaction draws on graph convolution ideas (DGCNN) but applies them to the latent state space rather than the point space, a conceptual shift from prior point-based structural modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses two concrete, previously unacknowledged limitations of applying selective SSMs to 3D point clouds—spatial adjacency distortion and length-dependent memory degradation—making it a useful diagnostic for the broader effort to replace quadratic-complexity Transformers in 3D. The spatial state mechanism offers a serialization-free alternative that could generalize to other 3D tasks (detection, registration) where ordering heuristics are particularly fragile. However, the method remains single-scale and is validated only on standard classification/segmentation benchmarks; whether the spatial state concept scales to large-scale scenes (e.g., autonomous driving point clouds with 100k+ points) or benefits from multi-scale hierarchies remains open. The sequence length-adaptive strategy, while effective, introduces a learnable hyperparameter τ whose interaction with model depth and state count is not fully explored.
