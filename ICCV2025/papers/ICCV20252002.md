## Problem

Existing depth super-resolution methods rely on reconstruction losses that minimize global pixel-level differences, leading to blurred or oversmoothed depth predictions that fail to capture fine-grained local geometric details. These methods also exhibit poor robustness to input noise and limited generalization across diverse resolutions and degradation patterns, and none have leveraged depth foundation models as prompts to improve generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252002/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution depth map (upsampled to target resolution via bicubic interpolation) and a corresponding RGB image as input. The RGB image is processed by a frozen depth foundation model (Depth Anything v2) to extract four multi-scale intermediate feature maps and a sharp-edged relative depth prediction, which serve as prompts. The output is a single-channel high-resolution depth map, evaluated at integer scales (×2, ×4, ×8, ×16), arbitrary non-integer scales (e.g., ×1.5, ×2.7, ×11.6), and real-world degradation patterns including noise and compression artifacts.

## Method

DuCos reformulates DSR as a constrained optimization problem under Lagrangian duality theory, where the L1 reconstruction loss is the objective and two constraint terms—geometric consistency (alignment loss in CF) and edge preservation (gradient loss in GR)—are enforced via Lagrangian multipliers λ and μ updated iteratively with decaying step sizes and KKT non-negativity clamping. The prompt learning component has two parts: Correlative Fusion (CF) iteratively fuses RGB foundation-model features with depth-branch features using a sigmoid-mapped Pearson Correlation Coefficient as an adaptive weighting, repeated over three iterations per scale across four stages; Gradient Regulation (GR) computes normalized first-order gradients of both the predicted depth and the foundation model's sharp-edged relative depth, then minimizes their L1 difference to transfer edge structure. The depth branch is a CNN with residual groups, while the foundation model (DA v2-S by default) is frozen and provides both intermediate features and the relative depth output. This design allows flexible integration of multiple constraints within a principled optimization framework, yielding exact solutions for convex losses and approximate solutions for non-convex ones.

## Results

On synthetic DSR benchmarks (Table 1), DuCos achieves the best RMSE, MAE, and δ₁ across all five datasets (Middlebury, Lu, NYU v2, RGB-D-D, TOFDSR) at all four scales (×2, ×4, ×8, ×16). At ×2, DuCos outperforms the second-best method by an average of 29.1% in RMSE and 29.6% in MAE; at ×4, the margins are 14.9% in RMSE and 24.1% in MAE. On real-world DSR (Table 2), DuCos surpasses the strongest baseline SFG by 4.2 mm in MAE on RGB-D-D and 5.7 mm on TOFDSR, and under noisy conditions it outperforms suboptimal methods by 17.1% in RMSE and 35.6% in MAE on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DuCos outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and scale tested. Among the guided-filtering methods, DKN/FDKN [12] (Kim, IJCV 2021) and CUNet [4] (Deng, TPAMI 2020) are the strongest synthetic DSR competitors, yet DuCos still leads by 14–29% in RMSE at ×4. The diffusion-based DADA [24] (Metzger, CVPR 2023) and DCTNet [57] (Zhao, CVPR 2022) are competitive at lower scales but degrade more sharply at ×8 and ×16, where DuCos maintains a clear advantage. On real-world DSR, the structure-flow-guided SFGNet [55] (Yuan, AAAI 2023) is the closest competitor, but DuCos surpasses it by 4.2 mm MAE on RGB-D-D and 5.7 mm on TOFDSR, with the gap widening under noise (35.6% MAE improvement over the next-best method). The older joint-filtering baselines DJF [15] and DJFR [16] (Li, ECCV 2016 / TPAMI 2019) are consistently the weakest, confirming that the Lagrangian duality formulation with foundation-model prompts provides a substantial leap over prior guided-filtering paradigms.

## Relation to Prior Work

DuCos sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: guided depth super-resolution (DJF, DKN, FDSR, DCTNet, DADA) and foundation-model prompting for depth perception (DepthPrompting, UniDC, Marigold-DC, PromptDA). It departs from prior DSR methods by reformulating the problem as a constrained optimization under Lagrangian duality theory rather than simply summing auxiliary losses, and it is the first to repurpose a monocular depth foundation model (Depth Anything v2) as a prompt specifically for the DSR task. The Correlative Fusion module's use of the Pearson Correlation Coefficient for adaptive feature blending is a creative repurposing of a classical statistical measure within a CNN pipeline, while Gradient Regulation exploits the observation that foundation-model depth maps, despite large absolute errors, retain remarkably sharp edge structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Lagrangian duality framing provides a principled, extensible mechanism for incorporating multiple geometric constraints into DSR, which could transfer to other image restoration tasks where global reconstruction and local structural fidelity must be balanced. The foundation-model prompting strategy opens a new direction for DSR that leverages the strong priors of large pretrained models without requiring them to be fine-tuned. Limitations include reliance on a frozen foundation model (DA v2) whose relative-depth output must be normalized, the added optimization complexity of the dual update loop, and the fact that all experiments use a single foundation model family. Open questions include whether the LD framework scales to multi-frame or video DSR, whether alternative correlation measures or attention mechanisms in CF would yield further gains, and how the approach behaves when the foundation model's edge quality degrades (e.g., on out-of-distribution scenes).
