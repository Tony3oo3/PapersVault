## Problem

Existing adapter-based VLM continual learning methods treat visual and language branches symmetrically, despite the visual modality exhibiting significantly broader parameter distribution shifts and higher forgetting rates during class increments. Additionally, prior null space projection methods suffer from non-zero residual terms due to SVD approximation and rely on static hyperparameters to control projection magnitude, limiting the stability-plasticity balance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252456/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images (64×64 for TinyImageNet, 32×32 for CIFAR-100, 224×224 for ImageNet-R) paired with text class labels, using a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone with trainable bottleneck adapters (down-projection, ReLU, up-projection) inserted into every transformer block of both branches. In the class-incremental setting, classes are partitioned into sequential subsets (e.g., 100 classes split into 5/10/20 subsets), and the model outputs a class prediction from the growing label space. Only 7.8M adapter parameters are trained per task, with the visual branch receiving DMNSP-constrained updates while the language branch follows standard AdamW optimization.

## Method

DMNSP applies an asymmetric optimization strategy: the visual adapter gradients are projected onto the common null subspace of all L adapter layers (sequentially multiplying projection matrices U_j(U_j)^T), while the language adapter is updated with the original optimizer. A dynamic projection coefficient λ_t = ζ·exp(−s_t) modulates the projection magnitude at each task, where s_t is the mean of the top-q% cosine similarities between the current task's main feature space eigenvectors and the accumulated null space basis vectors—large similarity weakens projection (preserving plasticity) while small similarity strengthens it (preserving stability). The null space is updated by simple concatenation of per-task null spaces across all layers rather than the elimination-based approach in prior work, ensuring the most comprehensive constraint. This multi-layer sequential projection drives residual terms toward zero more effectively than single-layer projection, as confirmed by empirical residual analysis.

## Results

On TinyImageNet (100 base classes), Adapter-based CLIP+DMNSP achieves 83.28% average and 79.14% last accuracy with 5 subsets, outperforming the prior SOTA MoE4Adapters (81.12%/76.81%) by +2.16/+2.33 points. On CIFAR-100, the method reaches 87.59%/79.94% (10 subsets) and 83.66%/74.58% (50 subsets), surpassing MoE4Adapters by +2.38/+2.42 and +0.06/−0.66 respectively. On ImageNet-R (cross-domain), DMNSP attains 87.49%/81.94% (10 subsets) and 86.81%/82.72% (20 subsets), exceeding the strongest baseline RAPF (85.85%/79.62%) by +1.64/+2.32 points. The method also reduces average forgetting rate from 3.34% to 2.15% (TinyImageNet, 5 subsets) and from 7.14% to 4.36% (CIFAR-100, 10 subsets) compared to the unprojected adapter baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the prior SOTA MoE4Adapters [40], DMNSP wins on all three TinyImageNet subset settings in both average and last accuracy (e.g., +2.16/+2.33 on 5-subsets), with the gap widening as task count increases. Compared to the single-layer projection baseline Adapter CLIP+GPM [29], DMNSP improves last accuracy by +0.90 (5-sub), +1.40 (10-sub), and +1.52 (20-sub) on TinyImageNet, confirming that multi-layer projection better suppresses residual propagation. Against Adapter CLIP+TRGP [22], DMNSP leads in average accuracy across all settings (+1.37 to +0.47) and matches or exceeds it in last accuracy. On CIFAR-100, DMNSP outperforms MoE4Adapters by +2.38% average accuracy (10-sub) and +1.57% (20-sub), while on the 50-subset setting the average accuracy is nearly tied (83.66 vs. 83.60) but last accuracy is slightly lower (74.58 vs. 75.24). On ImageNet-R, DMNSP surpasses all transformer-based baselines including RAPF [16] (+1.64/+2.32 on 10-sub) and VPT-NSP² [24] (+2.65/+3.06 on 10-sub), demonstrating robustness under cross-domain distribution shift. In terms of forgetting, DMNSP reduces average forgetting rate by ~35% relative to the unprojected adapter baseline on both TinyImageNet and CIFAR-100, whereas GPM yields negligible improvement (<0.1% reduction).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient projection lineage for continual learning (GPM, SVD-based null space estimation, and stability/plasticity decoupling) by moving from single-layer to sequential multi-layer null space projection, thereby constraining gradients to the intersection of all layers' null spaces. It departs from prior projection-based CL methods in two key ways: introducing a dynamic projection coefficient driven by cosine similarity between the current feature space and the null space (replacing static hyperparameters), and applying the projection asymmetrically—only to the visual branch of an adapter-based CLIP—based on the empirical observation that visual adapter parameters exhibit substantially broader distributional shifts than language adapter parameters during class increments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The asymmetric modality treatment offers a practical and interpretable principle for VLM-based continual learning: rather than uniformly constraining all parameters, one should target the modality most vulnerable to forgetting. The multi-layer projection with dynamic coefficient provides a more principled mechanism for balancing stability and plasticity than fixed projection ratios. However, the accuracy gains over the strongest baseline (MoE4Adapters) are modest (1–2 points), and the method is validated only on CLIP ViT-B/16 across three datasets, leaving open whether the asymmetric insight generalizes to other VLM architectures (e.g., LLaVA, Flamingo) or to non-adapter PEFT strategies like LoRA. The growing storage cost of accumulating null spaces across all tasks and layers also remains an unresolved scalability concern.
