## Problem

CLIP's 77-token text limit prevents it from understanding the detailed, multi-sentence descriptions that images actually require, severely restricting its use in MLLMs and text-to-image generation. Prior long-text CLIP methods either degrade short-text generalization or must train from scratch on expensive synthetic datasets, and their contrastive objective emphasizes only global [CLS] alignment while neglecting the local, fine-grained correspondences that long captions demand.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251027/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 224×224 RGB images and text sequences truncated or padded to 248 tokens. Images are consumed in two forms: raw patches for the long-text branch and randomly masked patches (75% mask ratio, replaced with learnable tokens) for the short-text branch. Outputs are aligned image-text embeddings in a shared cosine-similarity space for retrieval, zero-shot class scores for classification, and text encoder features that can be directly swapped into Stable Diffusion XL's CLIP-L text encoder for long-prompt text-to-image generation.

## Method

Fix-CLIP performs incremental training on a pre-trained CLIP with three key modules. (1) A dual-branch pipeline aligns short captions with 75%-masked images and long captions with raw images, using position embeddings that freeze the first 20 tokens and interpolate the remaining 57 fourfold to reach 248 tokens. (2) M learnable regional prompts are injected into every Transformer layer of the image encoder, each constrained by a unidirectional attention mask to attend only to its assigned spatial region of patches, enabling fine-grained local feature extraction without corrupting the original patch embeddings. (3) A hierarchical alignment module groups intermediate-layer [CLS] tokens from both encoders, aggregates each group via Gaussian-weighted pooling, projects through a linear layer, and applies InfoNCE contrastive loss from the K-th to the G-th group with increasing weights. Training uses 30M synthetic long-text captions generated by Llama3-LLaVA-NeXT-8B with 20 diverse prompts, cleaned to remove hallucinated or degenerate outputs.

## Results

On long-text retrieval (Table 1), Fix-CLIP with ViT-B/16 trained on 1M ShareGPT4V data achieves an average R@1 of 82.6, surpassing Long-CLIP (76.8) by 5.8 points, with the largest gains on DCI (I2T: 59.7 vs. 51.1, +8.6%) and IIW (T2I: 95.6 vs. 86.9, +8.7%). Scaling to 30M synthetic data pushes the average R@1 to 89.8 (B/16) and 91.2 (L/14), outperforming LoTLIP trained on 100M data (81.9) by 7.9 points. On short-text retrieval (Table 2), Fix-CLIP (1M) achieves 67.9 average R@1 on COCO/Flickr30k, exceeding Long-CLIP (64.6) by 3.3 points, and at 30M data reaches 70.4 (B/16) and 73.2 (L/14). For zero-shot classification (Table 3), Fix-CLIP (L/14) attains 71.4 average top-1 accuracy, outperforming Long-CLIP (68.3) and matching or exceeding original CLIP (66.0) on most benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fix-CLIP consistently outperforms all nine baselines. Against CLIP [44], Fix-CLIP (1M) improves long-text R@1 by 19.8 points (82.6 vs. 62.8 avg) and short-text R@1 by 10.7 points (67.9 vs. 57.2 avg), while also surpassing CLIP on zero-shot classification (64.4 vs. 61.5 avg, B/16). Against Long-CLIP [63], the primary incremental-training baseline, Fix-CLIP gains +5.8% (B/16) and +6.6% (L/14) average R@1 on long-text retrieval at 1M data, and +3.3% on short-text retrieval, confirming that the dual-branch pipeline prevents the short-text degradation that plagues Long-CLIP. Against from-scratch models trained on much larger data—LoTLIP [55] (100M), FLAIR [56] (30M), and SigLIP [62] (12B)—Fix-CLIP trained on only 5M synthetic data surpasses all of them on long-text retrieval (87.8 vs. 81.9, 73.9 avg R@1), demonstrating superior data efficiency. On short-text retrieval, Fix-CLIP (5M) outperforms EVA-CLIP [50] (2B data, 64.5 avg R@1), DreamLIP [66] (30M, 63.3), LoTLIP [55] (30M, 62.5), and FLIP [32] (400M, 67.2) with B/16, and at 30M data reaches 70.4 (B/16) / 73.2 (L/14), exceeding all prior methods. TULIP [38], which uses two-stage distillation + fine-tuning, is outperformed by Fix-CLIP on both COCO (60.9 vs. 56.8 I2T R@1) and Flickr30k (77.4 vs. 73.7 T2I R@1) with the same 1M training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP long-text understanding line initiated by Long-CLIP, which first demonstrated that position embedding interpolation could extend the 77-token limit to 248 tokens but at the cost of degrading short-text performance. Subsequent works (LoTLIP, DreamLIP, FLAIR) abandoned incremental training entirely, instead training from scratch on large synthetic datasets to avoid this degradation. Fix-CLIP departs from both by retaining the pre-trained CLIP weights and introducing a dual-branch pipeline that aligns short text with masked images and long text with raw images, thereby preserving the original short-text feature space. The regional prompts with unidirectional masks and hierarchical intermediate-layer alignment are novel architectural additions not present in any prior long-text CLIP variant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the plug-and-play integration with Stable Diffusion XL, where Fix-CLIP's text encoder directly improves long-text text-to-image generation without retraining the diffusion model. The dual-branch incremental training strategy offers a general recipe for extending pre-trained models to new input regimes without catastrophic forgetting, which is broadly applicable beyond vision-language. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 248 tokens, whether the regional prompts transfer to dense prediction tasks (segmentation, detection), and whether the 30M synthetic data pipeline can be further improved to reduce MLLM hallucination artifacts.
