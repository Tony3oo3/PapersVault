## Problem

CLIP's image-level pretraining leaves its patch embeddings without explicit vision-language alignment, limiting open-vocabulary dense prediction. Existing adaptation methods (e.g., CLIPSelf) improve fine-grained alignment but collapse the semantic coherence among patch features that CLIP naturally preserves, and often require extra modules or region-level supervision. The paper identifies the need to simultaneously enhance both fine-grained alignment and semantic coherence without external supervision.

## Input / Output

ATAS trains only the CLIP image encoder on unlabeled object-centric images from ImageNet (1.2M images), using mosaic augmentation to concatenate multiple single-object images into high-resolution composites. The teacher CLS tokens are extracted from individual single-object images while student patch tokens come from the mosaic composites, decoupling global and local representation sources. The refined encoder is then plugged into downstream frameworks (CAT-Seg for segmentation, F-ViT for detection) that consume RGB images and text prompts to produce pixel-level segmentation masks or object bounding boxes.

## Method

ATAS refines CLIP's ViT encoder via three self-distillation losses computed between a frozen teacher CLIP and a trainable student: (1) Global-to-Local Distillation (GLD) constructs a similarity-weighted aggregation of student patch embeddings and aligns it to the teacher CLS token via a contrastive objective, injecting vision-language grounding into local features; (2) Local-to-Local Distillation (LLD) minimizes MSE on pairwise patch cosine similarities between student and teacher, preserving the relational structure that encodes semantic coherence; (3) Global-to-Global Distillation (GGD) applies a contrastive loss between student and teacher CLS tokens to prevent degradation of high-level semantics. Mosaic augmentation is central to the design: by sourcing teacher CLS tokens from clean single-object images and student patch tokens from multi-object mosaics, the method trains robust local representations while keeping global semantics unambiguous. The total loss is a weighted sum (λ₁=1, λ₂=0.01, λ₃=1, τ=1) trained for 6 epochs with AdamW on unlabeled images only, requiring no region-level labels or additional modules.

## Results

In zero-shot semantic segmentation, ATAS improves average mIoU by 4.2 points over vanilla CLIP (16.8 vs. 12.6) and by 3.8 points over MaskCLIP (31.2 vs. 27.4), while also surpassing CLIPSelf in every setting. In the fine-tuning scenario with CAT-Seg, ATAS achieves 49.1 average mIoU (CAT-Seg v2) versus 48.3 for CLIPSelf. For open-vocabulary object detection, F-ViT+ATAS reaches 37.2 AP₅₀ⁿᵒᵛᵉˡ on OV-COCO and 25.8 mAPᵣ on OV-LVIS, outperforming F-ViT+CLIPSelf by 3.6 and 0.5 points respectively, and exceeding all prior methods on OV-COCO novel AP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ATAS consistently outperforms CLIPSelf across all four CLIP variants in zero-shot segmentation: e.g., with MaskCLIP, ATAS gains +4.8 mIoU over CLIPSelf (31.2 vs. 26.4), and with SCLIP it recovers the 1.6 mIoU drop CLIPSelf causes (35.5 vs. 33.6). Against vanilla CLIP, ATAS yields the largest absolute gain in the MaskCLIP setting (+3.8 mIoU) and the largest relative gain in the Vanilla setting (+34% mIoU). In detection, F-ViT+ATAS surpasses F-ViT+CLIPSelf by 3.6 AP₅₀ⁿᵒᵛᵉˡ on OV-COCO (37.2 vs. 33.6) and 0.5 mAPᵣ on OV-LVIS (25.8 vs. 25.3), and beats all prior OVOD methods (Detic 27.8, VLDet 32.0, BARON-KD 34.0, OADP 35.6) on OV-COCO novel AP. In the fine-tuning scenario, ATAS with CAT-Seg v2 achieves 49.1 average mIoU, exceeding both CAT-Seg v2+CLIPSelf (48.3) and the original CAT-Seg v2 (48.6), while CLIPSelf actually degrades CAT-Seg v2 performance. The one case where ATAS does not lead is ADE20 mIoU under the Vanilla setting (3.4 vs. CLIPSelf's 3.5), though it still wins on every other dataset and metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

ATAS extends the self-distillation line initiated by CLIPSelf (Wu et al., ICLR 2024), which aligned patch tokens to the CLS token to improve open-vocabulary dense prediction. The key departure is the diagnosis that CLIPSelf's single global-to-local objective degrades the semantic coherence of patch representations, and the remedy: a three-level distillation (global-to-local, local-to-local, global-to-global) that simultaneously sharpens fine-grained vision-language alignment while preserving the relational structure among patches. The mosaic augmentation strategy is repurposed from CLIM and MOSAICOS, but here it serves a distinct role—decoupling the source of global (single-object) and local (multi-object) representations to avoid ambiguous class-token semantics. Unlike PACL and RegionCLIP, ATAS requires no region-caption pairs or external projection modules, operating solely on unlabeled ImageNet images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a useful diagnostic framework—quantifying semantic coherence (AUROC on patch-level binary classification) and fine-grained alignment (patch-level classification accuracy) as two separable axes—giving practitioners a principled way to evaluate CLIP adaptations for dense prediction. The practical appeal is strong: ATAS needs only unlabeled images and a frozen teacher CLIP, making it a lightweight drop-in improvement for any downstream dense-prediction pipeline. However, gains in the fine-tuning regime are modest (0.5–1.5 mIoU), and detection performance still trails methods that leverage large-scale region-level training data, suggesting the approach may hit a ceiling without additional supervision. Open questions include whether the multi-level distillation transfers to other VLMs (e.g., SigLIP, DINOv2-based encoders) and whether the hyperparameter sensitivity (especially λ_LLD) limits robustness across architectures.
