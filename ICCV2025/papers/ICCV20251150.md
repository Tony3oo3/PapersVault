## Problem

Federated Meta-Learning (FML) for Neural Fields (NFs) has a fundamental privacy flaw: when a client holds data for only one or a few task instances, the local meta-learner effectively becomes an optimized NF for that private data, and since NFs are compressed representations that inherently encapsulate the signal, a malicious server can reconstruct the client's private data from the shared parameters. This breaks the core FL assumption that sharing trained weights is safe, and no prior work addresses federated learning of NFs on private data.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a small set of images, video frames, or input views of a single personal object (e.g., one cat, one person's golf swings, one car), split into support and query sets for local meta-optimization. The NF backbone is SIREN (6 layers, hidden dim 128) for images/videos or a standard NeRF MLP (6 layers, hidden dim 256) for 3D scenes. At test time, a client provides a few new images of a new object and the global meta-learner enables rapid NF optimization (e.g., reaching high PSNR in ~39 steps vs. 8192 for from-scratch training), producing reconstructed images, videos, or novel views.

## Method

FedMeNF introduces a privacy-preserving loss L_pp = L(φ_K, B_K) − γ·L(w_i, B_K) whose gradient removes the query-set term g_K from the meta-gradient, leaving only the inner-product alignment term I_K that drives fast optimization without memorizing data. Proposition 3 shows that at γ=1 the local meta-learner no longer reduces its loss on the query set (hence PSNR_p stays bounded), learning purely how to align gradients across minibatches for rapid adaptation. The coefficient γ acts as a tunable privacy–performance knob, and an adaptive variant sets γ from a privacy budget ζ analogous to differential privacy's ε, bounding the cumulative loss change over all rounds. The framework is plug-and-play: it composes with any FL aggregation (FedAvg, FedProx, Scaffold, FedNova, FedExP, FedACG) and any meta-learning algorithm, requiring only the modified outer-loop update in Algorithm 1.

## Results

On the Cars NeRF dataset with FedAvg, FedMeNF achieves PSNR 24.05 with PSNR_p 12.15 (Δ = 11.9), substantially outperforming MAML (PSNR 23.08, PSNR_p 19.73, Δ = 3.35) in the privacy-reconstruction trade-off. On PetFace images, FedMeNF reaches PSNR 27.0 with PSNR_p 14.77 (Δ = 12.23) versus MAML's PSNR 27.39 and PSNR_p 16.57 (Δ = 10.82). The privacy advantage is most dramatic on 3D scenes: FedMeNF reduces PSNR_p by ~7 dB over MAML on Cars while simultaneously improving reconstruction PSNR by ~1 dB. Across all six FL algorithms tested (FedAvg, FedProx, Scaffold, FedNova, FedExP, FedACG), FedMeNF consistently achieves the highest Δ metric, confirming robustness to the aggregation strategy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedMeNF dominates MAML and FOMAML on the privacy-reconstruction trade-off (Δ) across all four datasets: on Cars, Δ jumps from 3.35 (MAML) / 3.93 (FOMAML) to 11.90, while PSNR actually improves by ~1 dB. Against Reptile, FedMeNF achieves both lower PSNR_p (12.15 vs 19.96 on Cars) and higher PSNR (24.05 vs 21.98), indicating Reptile's lower privacy leakage comes at a significant reconstruction cost. The DP-based meta-NSGD achieves the lowest PSNR_p (6.85 on Cars) but catastrophically degrades reconstruction (PSNR 10.62 vs 24.05), making it impractical; FedMeNF closes most of the privacy gap (12.15 vs 6.85) while retaining near-MAML-level reconstruction. In the attack-based evaluation (Table 2), FedMeNF reduces Membership Inference Attack accuracy from 95.83% (MAML) to 55.56% and Property Inference Attack accuracy from 48.78% to 29.27%, while maintaining the highest NVS PSNR (24.05). Against HF-MAML and A-MAML, FedMeNF achieves comparable or better PSNR with substantially lower PSNR_p (12.15 vs 19.24/19.64) and lower attack accuracies, confirming that the privacy-preserving loss provides gains beyond what existing federated meta-learning variants offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of federated meta-learning (FML) and coordinate-based neural fields, building directly on MAML/FOMAML/Reptile-style meta-optimization and standard FL aggregation schemes (FedAvg, FedProx, Scaffold, etc.). It departs from prior FML work by identifying a fundamental privacy vulnerability specific to neural fields: because NF parameters are a compressed encoding of the data itself, sharing local meta-learner weights is equivalent to sharing the private signal, breaking the core FL assumption that model parameters are safe to transmit. The privacy-preserving loss (a single γ-weighted regularization term) is conceptually distinct from differential-privacy-based meta-learning (meta-NSGD), which the authors use as a comparison point but find too costly in reconstruction quality. Prior FL-for-NF studies were restricted to public data (landmarks, cityscapes), making this the first to address private-data NFs in a federated setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and quantifies a previously unaddressed privacy failure mode in federated NF training, providing both a theoretical account (Propositions 1–3) and a practical, tunable fix (the γ coefficient) that lets each client trade off privacy against optimization speed. The PSNR_p metric is simple, modality-agnostic, and empirically correlated with standard attacks (MIA, PIA), giving practitioners a lightweight diagnostic beyond full DP accounting. Limitations include reliance on relatively small SIREN/NeRF architectures (impractical for transformer-scale generalizable NFs), vision-only experiments, and a centralized FL topology; extending the approach to larger, generalizable NF models and decentralized or asynchronous settings remains open.
