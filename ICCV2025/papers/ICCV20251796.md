## Problem

Existing camera-controlled video generation methods (e.g., CameraCtrl, MotionCtrl) require fine-tuning a pretrained video diffusion model on large paired video–camera-pose datasets, which is data-intensive, computationally expensive, and risks degrading the model's learned distribution. This paper asks whether precise camera trajectory control can be injected at inference time without any additional training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251796/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, a target camera trajectory (80 poses sampled from RealEstate10K), and a pretrained video diffusion backbone (AnimateDiff, VideoCrafter2, or EasyAnimate) as input, and produces a 16-frame video at 512×384 resolution that follows the specified camera path. The intervention occurs at a fixed denoising step (step 8 of 25 DDIM steps), where the partially denoised latent is decoded to pixel space, reframed via 3D reprojection, and re-encoded before resuming denoising. Inference requires approximately 4–5 minutes per sample on an RTX A5000 GPU.

## Method

Latent-Reframe is a training-free, sampling-stage method with two core operations. First, **latent reframing**: at a chosen mid-denoising step, the model estimates a clean latent z₀, decodes it to pixels, and uses MonST3R to extract per-frame time-aware 3D point clouds and camera poses; a sliding-window connectivity graph (following DUSt3R) globally aligns all point maps into a unified coordinate system, after which each frame is reprojected to the target camera pose and re-encoded to latent space via the VAE. Second, **latent space rehabilitation**: occlusion-induced blank regions are inpainted using a RePaint-style iterative scheme that re-noises masked regions to full noise while keeping unmasked regions at their current denoising level, and a FIFO-Diffusion-inspired harmonization step offsets the known-region noise by 3 steps to guide coherent content generation in unknown regions. The use of time-aware (per-frame) point clouds rather than a single static global cloud is critical for preserving dynamic motion, and the approach is backbone-agnostic, working with both U-Net and DiT architectures.

## Results

On 800 generated videos (10 prompts × 80 camera trajectories from RealEstate10K), Latent-Reframe achieves the best FID (60.18) and lowest translational error (5.52) among all compared methods, without any fine-tuning. It also outperforms CameraCtrl on FVD (509.11 vs. 531.69) and rotational error (2.29 vs. 2.57). Compared to MotionCtrl, Latent-Reframe wins on FID (60.18 vs. 63.96) and TransErr (5.52 vs. 7.44) but trails on FVD (509.11 vs. 468.29) and RotErr (2.29 vs. 1.23). The authors attribute the higher rotational error to the greater depth variation and occlusion complexity inherent in 3D camera rotations versus translations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Latent-Reframe is compared against two training-based camera-control baselines: MotionCtrl and CameraCtrl, both fine-tuned on AnimateDiff. Against CameraCtrl, Latent-Reframe wins on all four metrics—FID (60.18 vs. 92.40), FVD (509.11 vs. 531.69), TransErr (5.52 vs. 8.39), and RotErr (2.29 vs. 2.57)—demonstrating that the training-free approach substantially outperforms this baseline in both video quality and pose accuracy. Against MotionCtrl, the comparison is mixed: Latent-Reframe achieves a lower FID (60.18 vs. 63.96) and lower translational error (5.52 vs. 7.44), indicating better distribution preservation and more accurate camera translation, but MotionCtrl retains an advantage on FVD (468.29 vs. 509.11) and rotational error (1.23 vs. 2.29). The authors note that the rotational gap likely stems from the inherent difficulty of reframing under complex 3D rotations, which produce more severe occlusions and depth discontinuities than translations. Overall, the key takeaway is that a training-free, sampling-stage method can match or exceed fine-tuned baselines on most metrics while preserving the pretrained model's generative distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of camera-controlled video generation, which relies on fine-tuning pretrained diffusion models with paired video–pose datasets (CameraCtrl, MotionCtrl, GEN3C, ReCapture). It instead operates entirely at the sampling stage, borrowing the global-alignment optimization formulation from DUSt3R and the dynamic-scene point-cloud estimation capability of MonST3R to reframe video latents mid-denoising. The latent-space rehabilitation step draws on RePaint-style inpainting and FIFO-Diffusion's asymmetric noise scheduling, repurposing these image-editing ideas for the video latent domain. Unlike the training-free CamTrol, which uses a single static point cloud and is restricted to image-to-video, Latent-Reframe employs per-frame time-aware point clouds, enabling both text-to-video and image-to-video generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for pose-annotated fine-tuning datasets, Latent-Reframe removes a significant practical barrier to camera-controlled video generation and preserves the pretrained model's learned distribution, yielding better FID/FVD than training-based alternatives. The method's architecture-agnostic design (validated on U-Net and DiT backbones) suggests broad applicability as video diffusion models evolve. Key limitations remain: large camera translations produce large occluded regions that degrade coherence, and the approach inherits errors from point-cloud and pose estimation. Open directions include geometry-aware or uncertainty-guided latent rehabilitation, iterative reframing for improved robustness, and scaling to longer videos and higher resolutions.
