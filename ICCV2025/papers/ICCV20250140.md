## Problem

Point-cloud upsampling faces a fundamental efficiency-precision trade-off: point-to-point frameworks achieve sub-voxel geometric accuracy but suffer from irregular memory access and fixed output counts, while voxel-based methods scale well via structured grids but lose fine-grained detail through discretization. No existing approach simultaneously preserves continuous spatial precision and exploits the computational benefits of regular grid operations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250140/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 2048 points sampled from a mesh, converted into a 128³ hybrid voxel grid where each active voxel stores 4 channels (1 binary occupancy + 3 continuous offset vector from voxel center to nearest surface). At inference, the network produces 8-channel outputs per voxel across multiple resolutions (32³ through 256³): occupancy, offset vector, normal vector, and unsigned distance field. The final dense point cloud is recovered by adding predicted offset vectors to occupied voxel centers, yielding resolution-dependent point counts (e.g., ~416K points at 256³) rather than a fixed multiplier, and estimated normals enable downstream Poisson surface reconstruction.

## Method

HVPUNet introduces a Hybrid Voxel representation that pairs discrete voxel occupancy with a continuous 3D offset vector, enabling both efficient sparse 3D convolution (via TorchSparse++) and sub-voxel point localization. The U-Net architecture operates in two stages—Shape Completion fills empty voxels to restore missing geometry, then Super-Resolution progressively upscales spatial resolution to capture finer surface details. A key mechanism, Operational Voxel Expansion, marks a 7-neighborhood of empty voxels around each active voxel as operationally active, allowing sparse convolutions to generate new occupied voxels in previously empty regions. The network is trained with progressive multi-scale supervision (32³→256³) and jointly predicts implicit geometric fields (unsigned distance fields and offset vector fields) as auxiliary targets, which also drive distance-based pruning to limit computation to a thin shell around surfaces. The loss combines Dice (occupancy), L1 (UDF and OVF), and angular-sine + L1 (normals) terms with equal weighting.

## Results

On PU-GAN at the highest resolution (256³), HVPUNet achieves the best occupancy metrics (Dice 0.707, IoU 0.549) and the best maximum point-based scores (P2F Max 26.023×10⁻³, F2P Max 15.723×10⁻³), indicating superior robustness to outliers. On PU1K (128³), it leads in Dice (0.537), IoU (0.376), F2P Avg (1.262×10⁻³), and F2P Max (10.101×10⁻³). Computationally, HVPUNet requires only 2.817 GB VRAM and 1.543 s inference time at 256³, compared to 17–34 GB and 273–576 s for the point-based baselines at 256×, a >100× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Grad-PU [17], HVPUNet wins on Dice (0.707 vs 0.698), IoU (0.549 vs 0.539), P2F Max (26.023 vs 28.409), and F2P Max (15.723 vs 22.027), while Grad-PU retains a slight edge on P2F Avg (2.143 vs 2.736) and F2P Avg (1.127 vs 1.756). Against PUDM [36], HVPUNet dominates on every occupancy and maximum point metric (Dice 0.707 vs 0.639, P2F Max 26.023 vs 30.365, F2P Max 15.723 vs 24.949) and uses ~6× less VRAM. Against RepKPU [39], HVPUNet achieves higher Dice (0.707 vs 0.695) and IoU (0.549 vs 0.539), a substantially lower P2F Max (26.023 vs 46.822), and a lower F2P Max (15.723 vs 16.238), though RepKPU edges ahead on P2F Avg (2.261 vs 2.736) and F2P Avg (0.963 vs 1.756). On PU1K, HVPUNet leads in Dice, IoU, and both F2P metrics, but RepKPU achieves a lower P2F Max (13.872 vs 25.438), suggesting HVPUNet's offset vectors occasionally place points farther from the surface in the worst case. The most striking advantage is computational: HVPUNet runs in 1.5 s with 2.8 GB VRAM versus 273–576 s and 17–34 GB for the baselines, a >100× speedup at comparable or better accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

HVPUNet sits at the intersection of two well-studied paradigms for 3D upsampling: point-to-point networks (PU-Net, PU-GAN, Grad-PU, RepKPU) that achieve precise continuous-space reconstruction but suffer from irregular memory access, and voxel-based methods (MinkowskiUNet, PVCNN) that exploit structured grids for efficiency but lose sub-voxel precision. The paper's core departure is the Hybrid Voxel representation, which augments each voxel with a continuous 3D offset vector to recover sub-voxel surface positions while retaining the computational benefits of sparse 3D convolutions. It also extends MinkowskiUNet's shape-completion pipeline with operational voxel expansion (marking empty neighbors as active) and adopts early dilation from XCube to broaden receptive fields, going beyond what standard generative sparse convolutions can achieve on highly sparse inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic efficiency gap (roughly 4000× faster inference and ~20× less VRAM than Grad-PU at 256× upsampling) makes high-resolution 3D reconstruction feasible on modest hardware, which is practically important for large-scale scenes in robotics, AR/VR, and autonomous driving. However, the accuracy is comparable rather than superior to point-to-point SOTA on point-based metrics, and the different upscaling strategy (resolution-based vs. point-count-based) complicates direct comparison. Open questions include whether the hybrid voxel formulation scales to very large scenes beyond 256³, whether the qualitative normal estimation can be made quantitative, and how the method performs on truly non-watertight or open-surface real-world scans beyond the limited ScanObjectNN/KITTI samples shown.
