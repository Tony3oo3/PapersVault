## Problem

Existing SOD and COD datasets enforce a mutual-exclusivity constraint (each scene contains either salient or camouflaged objects), and current models lack explicit modeling of the relationship between the two aspects. This leads to a counterintuitive failure mode: SOD models misclassify camouflaged objects as salient, and COD models misinterpret salient objects as camouflaged, with false-positive scores far above the expected value of zero.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250018/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image at 352×352 resolution and produces three pixel-wise masks—saliency, camouflage, and background—that are softmax-normalized to form a three-class semantic segmentation. Unlike prior SOD/COD methods that output a single binary foreground mask, USCNet simultaneously predicts all three aspects, enabling correct handling of scenes where both salient and camouflaged objects coexist or where neither is present.

## Method

USCNet builds on SAM2 (hiera-large) with lightweight adapter layers for parameter-efficient fine-tuning (4.04M trainable parameters) and a frozen SAM mask decoder. The core contribution is the Aspect Relation Modeling (ARM) module, which generates three aspect prompts (saliency, camouflage, background) by combining two complementary prompt queries: Inter-SPQ uses fixed learnable embeddings to capture universal discriminative features across samples, while Intra-SPQ is generated per-sample by applying a supervised attention head to encoder features, isolating context-specific aspect information. The combined queries are processed through self-attention and query-to-image cross-attention to produce the final prompts. Training uses a focal loss with class-weighted coefficients (1:4:6 for background:salient:camouflaged) applied to both the prediction maps and the ARM attention maps, supervised on the new USC12K dataset (12,000 images across four scene types with 179 sub-classes).

## Results

On the USC12K benchmark, USCNet achieves 78.03% mIoU, 87.92% mAcc, and 7.49% CSCS across all scenes, outperforming the best unified baseline Spider (74.92% mIoU, 85.79% mAcc, 7.86% CSCS) by +3.11 mIoU and −0.37 CSCS. In the most challenging Scene C (both salient and camouflaged objects coexist), USCNet reaches 75.57% IoU_S and 61.34% IoU_C, surpassing the next-best method (Spider: 71.34% / 56.68%) by over 4 points on both. On generalization to external datasets, USCNet attains F_β^max of 0.784 on DUTS, 0.844 on HKU-IS, 0.794 on NC4K, and 0.743 on COD10K, all leading the compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

USCNet outperforms all 22 baselines on every metric across all four scenes. Among unified methods, it leads Spider [76] by +3.11 mIoU (78.03 vs 74.92), +2.13 mAcc, and −0.37 CSCS, while using only 4.04M trainable parameters versus Spider's 175M. Against the strongest COD baseline SAM2-Adapter [2] (74.98 mIoU, 9.12 CSCS), USCNet gains +3.05 mIoU and reduces CSCS by 1.63 points, indicating substantially less confusion between salient and camouflaged objects. The advantage is most pronounced in Scene C (coexisting objects), where USCNet's IoU_C of 61.34 exceeds the next-best (ICEG: 58.71) by 2.63 points, and its IoU_S of 75.57 surpasses Spider's 71.34 by 4.23 points. On external generalization (Table 5), USCNet leads all methods on DUTS (F_β^max 0.784 vs 0.732 for SINet-V2), NC4K (0.794 vs 0.626 for VSCode), and COD10K (0.743 vs 0.684 for VSCode), confirming that training on the unconstrained USC12K dataset transfers effectively to standard SOD/COD benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unified SOD/COD detection line (VSCode, UJSC, Spider, EVP) by identifying two structural limitations: the mutual-exclusivity annotation constraint in existing datasets and the absence of explicit intra-sample aspect relationship modeling. Architecturally, it builds on SAM2 with parameter-efficient adapters (following SAM-Adapter and SAM2-Adapter) but departs from prior unified methods by introducing an Aspect Relation Modeling module with both inter-sample and intra-sample prompt queries, rather than relying solely on inter-sample loss-based separation (e.g., UJSC's latent space loss or VSCode's discrimination loss). The paper also introduces the first unconstrained dataset (USC12K) that covers all four logical coexistence scenarios, breaking from the single-aspect paradigm of DUTS, COD10K, and similar benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The USC12K benchmark and CSCS metric fill a genuine gap: prior evaluation could not quantify how much models confuse salient with camouflaged objects, and no dataset allowed testing in scenes where both coexist or neither is present. The dramatic reduction in cross-task misinterpretation after training on USC12K (e.g., VSCode dropping from 0.87 to 0.05 on HKU-IS) validates the dataset-constraint hypothesis and suggests that much of the 'confusion' in unified models is an artifact of training data rather than an inherent modeling difficulty. Open questions include whether the ARM module's benefits persist at larger scale, whether the 12K dataset size is sufficient for robust generalization, and whether the unconstrained formulation extends naturally to other opposing visual concepts beyond saliency/camouflage.
