## Problem

Existing masked point cloud modeling methods rely on regression to reconstruct exact coordinates or features of masked regions, which overconstrains the student model to memorize point-wise details rather than learning generalized semantic representations. Additionally, most approaches construct local patches using KNN-based grouping that ignores geometric consistency, causing information leakage between visible and masked patches and degrading the quality of learned features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252201/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud sampled to N=1024 points (via FPS) and partitions it into L=64 non-overlapping, geometrically homogeneous patches before feeding them through a mini-PointNet patch embedding layer. During pre-training, the output is a set of learned patch-level feature representations (dimension D=384) from the student encoder. Downstream, these frozen features are used to produce class labels (ModelNet40, ScanObjectNN), per-point semantic or part segmentation masks (S3DIS, ShapeNetPart), or completed point clouds (ShapeNet55).

## Method

PointGAC replaces regression-based feature reconstruction with a clustering-based alignment paradigm: a teacher encoder processes complete patch features and maintains an online codebook (K=8192 vectors) via EMA-weighted k-means, while a student encoder reconstructs masked patch features from visible patches; both sets of features are then soft-assigned to codebook cluster centers, and a KL divergence loss aligns the two assignment distributions. Patches are constructed using Potts Energy Segmentation for coarse geometric partitioning followed by masked optimal transport (solved via Sinkhorn) to produce exactly L non-overlapping, geometrically consistent patches. A codebook maintenance mechanism applies frequency-dependent perturbations—larger for rarely-updated 'dead' vectors, smaller for frequently-updated ones—to keep all codebook entries active and improve alignment efficiency. The teacher is updated via EMA of the student parameters, and gradients flow only through the student branch during training.

## Results

On ModelNet40, PointGAC achieves 93.5% Linear SVM accuracy, 94.3% without voting, and 94.6% with voting, outperforming all baselines including RIMAE (92.8/93.1/93.9) and PointM2AE (92.9/93.4/94.0). On ScanObjectNN, PointGAC reaches 92.6% (OBJ-BG), 91.4% (OBJ-ONLY), and 89.4% (PB-T50-RS), surpassing the next-best methods by 0.7–4.2 points. On S3DIS semantic segmentation, it attains 70.9 mAcc and 61.8 mIoU; on ShapeNetPart, 85.1 mIoU_C and 86.7 mIoU_I; and on ShapeNet55 completion, 0.95 CD-Avg and 0.49 F1-Score, all setting new state-of-the-art among compared self-supervised methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PointGAC outperforms all 20 compared baselines across every evaluated task. On ModelNet40 classification, it leads the closest competitors RIMAE (93.9% w/ Vote) and PointM2AE (94.0% w/ Vote) by 0.6–0.7 points, while the gap widens to 1.2–1.5 points over PointMAE and Point2Vec. On the more challenging ScanObjectNN (real-world objects with background noise and perturbations), PointGAC's advantage is most pronounced: it exceeds RIMAE by 0.7 points on OBJ-BG, 2.1 points on OBJ-ONLY, and 2.6 points on PB-T50-RS, with the largest margin (4.2 points) over PointMAE on OBJ-ONLY. For few-shot classification (Table 2), PointGAC surpasses PointOTG by 0.2–0.3 points in 5-way settings and 0.5–0.3 points in 10-way settings. On S3DIS segmentation, it edges out PCP-MAE (61.1 mIoU) and Point-LGMask (61.3 mIoU) by 0.5–0.7 mIoU. In point cloud completion (Table 4), PointGAC achieves the lowest Chamfer Distance (0.95 CD-Avg) and highest F1-Score (0.49), beating RIMAE (0.99 CD-Avg, 0.46 F1) and PM-MAE (1.04 CD-Avg, 0.41 F1), with the largest gap over Point-JEPA (1.37 CD-Avg). No baseline matches or exceeds PointGAC on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

PointGAC departs from the dominant regression paradigm in masked point cloud modeling (Point-BERT, PointMAE, Point2Vec, RIMAE), where the student must reconstruct exact teacher features or coordinates, by replacing point-wise feature regression with a clustering-based assignment over an online codebook. Its geometric partitioning strategy builds on Potts Energy Segmentation (Landrieu & Simonovsky, CVPR 2018) for coarse segmentation and on masked optimal transport (Luo & Ren, CVPR 2023) for balanced patch assignment, combining these into a non-overlapping, geometrically homogeneous patching scheme. The codebook update mechanism draws from online k-means (VQ-VAE lineage) and the codebook maintenance idea is inspired by OBoW's queue-based approach, but the paper explicitly rejects the uniform-assignment assumption of SwAV/Sinkhorn-based methods in favor of data-driven cluster centers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from regression to clustering for MPM offers a principled way to avoid over-constraining the student network, and the consistent (if modest) gains across classification, segmentation, few-shot, and completion tasks suggest the learned representations are more transferable. The codebook maintenance mechanism addresses a practical failure mode (dead vectors) that plagues any online k-means-based representation, making it a reusable component for other codebook-based self-supervised methods. However, pre-training is limited to the synthetic ShapeNet55 dataset, and the evaluation, while broad in task type, does not include large-scale benchmarks like ScanNet or large-scale 3D detection, leaving open whether the clustering paradigm scales to more complex, real-world scenes with diverse semantic distributions.
