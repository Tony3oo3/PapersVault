## Problem

Autoregressive visual generation methods that tokenize images into spatial patches (raster-scan) violate the inherent translation invariance and equality among image pixels, contradicting the causal premise of autoregressive modeling. While VAR addresses this with multi-scale tokenization, it requires 680 tokens and uses bidirectional attention within each scale, deviating from the standard unidirectional autoregressive paradigm. The paper seeks a tokenization scheme that simultaneously achieves true sequence causality and high token efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252208/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For class-conditional generation, the model takes a class label and produces a 256×256 RGB image by sequentially generating 64 spectral tokens through a GPT-2-like transformer (one forward pass per token). For reconstruction, an input RGB image is encoded into 64 discrete spectral tokens and decoded back. The method also supports truncated generation (discarding the last k high-frequency tokens) to trade quality for speed, and super-resolution by splitting a low-res image into sub-patches and refining each independently in the spectral domain.

## Method

SpectralAR converts images into 1D causal sequences via Nested Spectral Tokenization (NST): a ViT-B encoder with learnable query tokens produces continuous representations that are vector-quantized into 64 discrete tokens, where each token s_i is trained to reconstruct the inverse-DCT of the image's spectral components up to frequency threshold ω_i. A non-uniform token-frequency mapping (ω_i − ω_{i−1} ≤ ω_{i+1} − ω_i) allocates more tokens to low-frequency bands and fewer to high-frequency bands, exploiting the power-law distribution of spectral energy. Causal masks are applied to the spectral tokens in both encoder and decoder to prevent high-to-low frequency information leakage. The autoregressive generator (GPT-2-like, 310M params for d16) predicts each next spectral token conditioned on all previous ones, progressively adding higher-frequency detail in a coarse-to-fine manner that mirrors human visual perception.

## Results

On the class-conditional ImageNet-1K 256×256 benchmark, SpectralAR-d16 (310M params) achieves a gFID of 3.02 and IS of 282.2 using only 64 tokens and 64 autoregressive steps, outperforming the same-parameter VAR-d16 (gFID 3.30) and the 1D-AR TiTok-B64-d16 (gFID 6.30). Scaling to 1.0B parameters, SpectralAR-d24 reaches gFID 2.13 and IS 307.7, surpassing StyleGAN-XL (gFID 2.30) and DiT-XL/2 (gFID 2.27) while using only 64 tokens versus 256 for DiT or 680 for VAR. The reconstruction quality (rFID 4.03) is slightly worse than TiTok's overall-reconstruction baseline, reflecting the harder nested spectral supervision objective.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At the 310M parameter scale, SpectralAR-d16 (gFID 3.02) decisively outperforms the 1D-AR TiTok-B64-d16 (gFID 6.30) and VAR-d16 (gFID 3.30), demonstrating that spectral causality substantially eases autoregressive learning over both spatial 1D and multi-scale 2D tokenization. At 600M, SpectralAR-d20 (gFID 2.49) edges out VAR-d20 (gFID 2.57), while at 1.0B, SpectralAR-d24 (gFID 2.13) is marginally behind VAR-d24 (gFID 2.09) but still surpasses non-autoregressive baselines including StyleGAN-XL (2.30), DiT-XL/2 (2.27), and LDM-4-G (3.60). A key differentiator is token efficiency: SpectralAR uses only 64 tokens per image versus 680 for VAR and 256–1024 for 2D-scan methods, a 10× reduction over VAR that is critical for multimodal integration under limited context windows. SpectralAR's reconstruction rFID (4.03) is notably worse than TiTok's mask-based reconstruction (rFID 1.70), a trade-off the authors attribute to the harder nested spectral supervision requiring each prefix of tokens to reconstruct a progressively sharper sub-image. Overall, SpectralAR establishes the best gFID among strictly autoregressive (next-token) methods at matched parameter counts while using an order of magnitude fewer tokens than competing AR approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpectralAR builds directly on the 1D tokenizer architecture of TiTok (ViT encoder/decoder with learned query tokens) and the GPT-2-style autoregressive generator of VAR, but departs from both in a fundamental way: instead of spatial patches or multi-scale 2D token maps, it uses DCT to decompose images into a 1D sequence of spectral tokens ordered from low to high frequency. The nested spectral tokenization and non-uniform token-frequency mapping draw on image compression principles (JPEG, H.264) where most perceptual energy resides in low-frequency components. This positions the work as a spectral-domain alternative to the two dominant autoregressive paradigms—raster-scan (VQGAN, RQ-Transformer) and scale-wise (VAR)—arguing that frequency ordering provides a more natural causal structure than spatial or scale ordering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 64-token budget (vs. 680 for VAR) is practically significant for multimodal and long-context settings where token count directly limits sequence length, and the clean ablation isolating the contribution of spectral supervision, causal masking, and non-uniform mapping provides a useful design recipe. However, the reconstruction FID (4.03) is notably worse than TiTok (1.70), and evaluation is limited to class-conditional ImageNet-1K at 256×256 with no text-to-image or open-vocabulary experiments, leaving open whether the spectral ordering advantage persists under richer conditioning or higher resolutions. The truncated-generation and super-resolution applications are promising but underexplored, and it remains unclear whether the spectral causality benefit generalizes to video or 3D generation where temporal and spatial frequencies interact.
