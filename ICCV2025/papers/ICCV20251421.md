## Problem

Reconstruction-based driving view synthesis methods excel at interpolating along recorded trajectories but degrade severely when extrapolating to novel, free-form trajectories due to sparse viewpoints, minimal frame overlap, and large textureless regions in driving videos. Existing generative-prior approaches struggle in this setting because driving datasets contain limited trajectory diversity (primarily forward motion), making it infeasible to train restoration models on realistic off-trajectory degradation patterns or to learn precise explicit pose control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251421/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes surrounding camera videos (front and side views at 10 Hz, downsampled to 640×960), synchronized LiDAR point clouds, and tracked bounding boxes for dynamic traffic participants, along with a user-specified novel camera trajectory (lateral shift of up to 6 m from the recorded path). It produces an optimized 3D Gaussian scene representation that renders novel-view videos in real time (34 FPS on an A6000) without requiring the diffusion model at inference, enabling closed-loop evaluation of driving policies on arbitrary trajectories.

## Method

DriveX progressively distills a video diffusion prior into a 3D Gaussian Splatting representation during its optimization. The key mechanism is an inpainting-based video restoration task: a geometry-aware unreliability mask (computed via SSIM between rendered novel views and depth-warped recorded views) explicitly marks degraded regions, freeing the diffusion model from identifying artifacts and eliminating the need to simulate specific degradation patterns during training. The diffusion model (initialized from ViewCrafter, fine-tuned on Waymo with edge-aware masks) refines the masked renderings using accumulated LiDAR projections as auxiliary geometric guidance, and the resulting pseudo ground truth supervises the Gaussian optimization. An iterative refinement loop updates the pseudo ground truth every K=3000 steps as the Gaussian model improves, creating a co-adaptation cycle that progressively tightens consistency between generated and rendered content while the lateral shift length of the novel trajectory is gradually extended from 2 m to 6 m.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Dataset, DriveX achieves the best novel-trajectory synthesis quality across all lateral shift lengths (±1m, ±2m, ±3m). At the most challenging ±3m shift, DriveX attains IoU 0.2699, AP 0.5712, and FID 79.78, surpassing the strongest reconstruction-only baseline StreetGaussian (IoU 0.1720, AP 0.5697, FID 93.04) by 56.9% in IoU and 14.3% in FID reduction. On recorded-trajectory views, DriveX matches StreetGaussian closely (PSNR 29.74 vs 29.76, SSIM 0.8700 vs 0.8738), confirming that generative prior integration does not degrade in-distribution quality. Inference runs at 34 FPS, identical to StreetGaussian, since only the 3DGS is used at test time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against reconstruction-only baselines (EmerNeRF, S³Gaussian, PVG, StreetGaussian), DriveX dominates on all novel-trajectory metrics at every shift length, with the largest gap over StreetGaussian at ±3m (IoU +56.9%, FID −14.3%). On recorded-trajectory PSNR/SSIM, DriveX is essentially tied with StreetGaussian (within 0.02 dB / 0.004), confirming no in-distribution regression. Against generative-prior methods, DriveX outperforms DriveDreamer4D and ReconDreamer on lane-change NTA-IoU (0.620 vs 0.495/0.554) and lane-shift ±3m NTA-IoU (0.567 vs 0.340/0.539), while also running at 34 FPS versus their much slower generation. Compared to FreeVS (the closest concurrent generative method), DriveX wins on lane-shift ±1m (IoU 0.2880 vs 0.2782, AP 0.5932 vs 0.5777) and AP at ±3m (0.5712 vs 0.5688), though FreeVS edges out DriveX by 0.0024 on IoU at ±3m (0.2723 vs 0.2699); DriveX's key advantage is its 34 FPS real-time rendering versus FreeVS's 0.43 FPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

DriveX builds on the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) scene representation and the StreetGaussian driving-scene reconstruction pipeline, extending them with a video diffusion model (initialized from ViewCrafter) that provides novel-trajectory supervision during optimization. It departs from prior generative-prior-for-reconstruction work (ReconX, 3DGS-Enhancer) by formulating an inpainting-based restoration task rather than direct pose-conditioned generation, specifically to handle the limited trajectory diversity in driving datasets where simulating off-trajectory degradation for training is infeasible. The iterative co-adaptation loop—where the diffusion model refines in-training renderings that in turn improve the Gaussian model—distinguishes it from one-shot pseudo-ground-truth approaches like DriveDreamer4D.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Free-form trajectory synthesis is a prerequisite for closed-loop evaluation of end-to-end driving policies, and this paper demonstrates that a 3DGS model trained with generative-prior regularization can render novel lateral-shift views at 34 FPS with substantially better lane and vehicle fidelity than reconstruction-only baselines. The inpainting formulation is a practical workaround for a real data limitation (single-trajectory driving videos), and the technique may transfer to other sparse-view 3DGS reconstruction settings. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to larger trajectory deviations (e.g., full lane changes or turns), whether the LiDAR dependency can be relaxed, and how the method scales to multi-scene or long-horizon driving sequences.
