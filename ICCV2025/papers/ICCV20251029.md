## Problem

Despite strong performance on clean benchmarks, MLLMs face significant robustness degradation under image corruption, yet no existing benchmark simultaneously evaluates a broad range of corruption types (40) across fine-grained MLLM capabilities (34 low-level tasks). Prior corruption benchmarks cover only one or a few task types (e.g., classification, detection, captioning), while MLLM capability benchmarks largely ignore corruption, leaving a critical gap in understanding which model abilities are most vulnerable to which distortions.

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs a corrupted RGB image (generated by applying one of 40 corruption types at one of 5 severity levels to a clean source image) with a multiple-choice or open-ended text question drawn from MMBench (4,329 QA pairs) and SeedBench (17,023 QA pairs), yielding 4,270k total QA pairs. The model produces a text answer, and accuracy is reported not just as a single scalar but decomposed along two independent three-level hierarchies—capability (perception/reasoning → 5 mid-level → 34 low-level) and corruption (global/regional/pixel → 9 general → 40 specific)—enabling multi-dimensional robustness profiling. A smaller MLLM-IC-mini subset is provided for faster evaluation.

## Method

The core contribution is a dual three-level hierarchical benchmark design. The capability hierarchy organizes 34 low-level tasks (selected and consolidated from MMBench and SeedBench) under 5 mid-level categories (global context sensing, instance & part recognition, spatial perception, knowledge reasoning, logical reasoning) and 2 high-level categories (perception, reasoning). The corruption hierarchy classifies 40 specific types (implemented via the imgaug library) into 9 general categories based on shared formation mechanisms, which are further grouped into 3 operational levels (global: K=1 region; regional: K>1 regions; pixel: K=number of pixels) defined by a formal transformation-function framework. Each corruption type is instantiated at 5 severity levels (200 scenarios total), with parameters set via range discretization for bounded values and human perceptual thresholding for unbounded ones. The corruption hierarchy is validated through t-SNE visualization of WideResNet mean features on CIFAR-10, confirming that corruptions cluster by operational level and general category. Eleven SOTA MLLMs are then evaluated across all dimensions, revealing that spatial perception and knowledge reasoning are most vulnerable, while geometric and color corruptions are least damaging.

## Results

On the MLLM-IC benchmark (40 corruption types × 34 capability dimensions × 5 severity levels), Gemini 2.0 achieves the highest average accuracy of 73.9% on clean images, followed by Qwen2.5-VL at 71.6%. Under the most severe corruption (level 5), GPT-4o exhibits the largest degradation with a 14.3% accuracy drop, while DeepSeek-VL drops by 5.8% on average and 9.8% at maximum severity. Across capability dimensions, MLLMs retain relatively higher performance in global context sensing (avg 68.0%) and logical reasoning (avg 62.0%), but suffer the most in spatial perception (avg 50.4%) and knowledge reasoning (avg 51.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gemini 2.0 consistently outperforms all other models across nearly every capability and corruption dimension, achieving the top score in 4 of 5 mid-level capabilities (all except spatial perception) and in 8 of 9 corruption categories. Qwen2.5-VL is the second-best overall (71.6% clean accuracy) and uniquely leads in spatial perception (64.3%), while GPT-4o, despite strong clean-image performance, suffers the largest corruption-induced degradation (−14.3%) and shows a notable weakness in instance recognition (59.3%) attributed to its conservative 'don't know' fallback behavior. DeepSeek-VL and Transcore-M are competitive mid-tier models (55–70% range) but degrade more sharply under blur and compression corruptions. MiniGPT-4 and Otter remain near-random across all conditions (23–45%), making them effectively uninformative for robustness comparison. Among corruption types, all models handle geometric transformations (avg 62.9%) and color perturbations (62.4%) well, but blur (54.6%) and compression (55.5%) cause the most uniform degradation across the board.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two benchmarking traditions: MLLM capability evaluation (MMBench, SeedBench, MME, LLaVA-Bench) and image corruption robustness evaluation (ImageNet-C, COCO-C, R-Bench, MMC-Bench, MMRobustness). Prior MLLM benchmarks assess capabilities on clean images but ignore robustness, while prior corruption benchmarks evaluate only narrow tasks (classification, detection, segmentation, or captioning) with limited capability granularity. MLLM-IC repurposes QA pairs from MMBench and SeedBench and corruption generation from imgaug, but introduces a novel three-level hierarchical structure for both capability dimensions (34 low-level tasks) and corruption types (40 specific types across 9 general categories and 3 operational levels), a design absent from all prior corruption benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the most comprehensive MLLM corruption robustness benchmark to date (4.27M QA pairs, 34 capabilities, 40 corruptions, 5 severity levels), MLLM-IC provides a practical diagnostic tool for identifying which model capabilities and corruption types are most problematic, directly informing the development of more resilient MLLMs for safety-critical applications like autonomous driving and monitoring systems. A key limitation is that all corruptions are synthetic, and the authors acknowledge a gap to real-world degradation patterns. Open questions include whether the observed vulnerability patterns (spatial perception and knowledge reasoning most affected; blur and compression hardest) persist under real-world corruptions, and whether the hierarchical corruption structure generalizes to novel or compound corruption scenarios not covered by the 40 types.
