## Problem

Existing detector-free semi-dense matchers suffer from three interrelated limitations: (1) context interaction over the entire coarse feature map is computationally redundant due to neighboring token similarity, (2) attention indiscriminately aggregates context from non-covisible regions (occluded, viewpoint-changed areas), degrading feature distinctiveness, and (3) fine-level refinement only achieves subpixel accuracy in the target view while source-view keypoints remain at pixel level, which is detrimental to keypoint location-sensitive downstream tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images depicting the same 3D scene, resized so the longer edge is 832 pixels, and produces semi-dense correspondences where both source and target view keypoints are refined to subpixel accuracy. Coarse features are extracted at 1/8 image resolution via a down-sampling-only CNN backbone (no FPN up-sampling), with fine features at 1/4 and 1/2 resolutions used for progressive fusion. The final output is a set of matched point pairs (i′, j′) with subpixel coordinates in both views, obtained through a two-stage correlation-and-expectation process.

## Method

CoMatch introduces a Dynamic Covisibility-Aware Transformer (DCAT) stacked L=4 times for coarse-level feature transformation. The key mechanism is a Covisibility-Guided Token Condenser (CGTC) that uses a lightweight MLP to predict per-token covisibility scores, then adaptively condenses tokens: the query side applies element-wise multiplication with covisibility scores followed by depth-wise convolution, while the key/value side uses covisibility-weighted average pooling over 4×4 neighborhoods, preserving representational capacity while reducing token count by 16×. A Covisibility-Assisted Attention (CAA) module then applies vanilla attention with a diagonal weight matrix derived from max-pooled covisibility scores, selectively suppressing message flow from non-covisible condensed tokens. For fine-level refinement, a Bilateral Subpixel Refinement (BSR) module performs two-stage correlation: first a dense patch-level MNN to get pixel-level candidates, then a 3×3 soft-argmax that simultaneously regresses residual subpixel offsets for both views, supervised by an epipolar geometry (Sampson distance) loss to enforce geometric consistency. The full model is trained end-to-end on MegaDepth with a joint loss combining coarse NLL, fine NLL, epipolar geometry, and covisibility binary cross-entropy.

## Results

On MegaDepth, CoMatch achieves AUC@5°/10°/20° of 58.0/73.2/84.2, outperforming the best semi-dense baseline ELoFTR (56.4/72.2/83.5) by 1.6 points at 5° while running at 123.8 ms per pair. On ScanNet, CoMatch reaches 21.7/40.2/56.7, surpassing ELoFTR (19.2/37.0/53.6) by 2.5 points at 5°. Compared to the dense matcher RoMa (62.6/76.7/86.3), CoMatch is approximately 6× faster (123.8 ms vs. 760.9 ms) with only a 4.6-point gap at 5°. On HPatches homography estimation, CoMatch attains AUC@3px/5px/10px of 68.4/78.2/86.8, setting new state-of-the-art among all evaluated methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoMatch decisively outperforms all sparse baselines (SP+NN, SP+IMP, SP+SG, SP+LG, SP+DG) by 7.8–26.3 points in AUC@5° on MegaDepth, confirming the advantage of detector-free matching. Among semi-dense competitors, CoMatch surpasses the strongest prior method ELoFTR by 1.6 points (AUC@5°) on MegaDepth and 2.5 points on ScanNet, while also beating ASpanFormer by 2.7 and 2.1 points respectively; it further outperforms LoFTR, QuadTree, MatchFormer, and TopicFM by 3.6–5.2 points on MegaDepth. On HPatches homography estimation, CoMatch (68.4/78.2/86.8) exceeds ASpanFormer (67.4/76.9/85.6) and ELoFTR (66.5/76.4/85.5) across all thresholds, and also surpasses Sparse-NCNet and DRC-Net by 17.5–19.8 points at 3px. The only methods that exceed CoMatch in raw accuracy are the dense matchers DKM and RoMa, but RoMa is 6.1× slower (760.9 ms vs. 123.8 ms) and DKM is 4.8× slower, making CoMatch the best accuracy–efficiency trade-off. On visual localization (Aachen Day-Night, InLoc), CoMatch achieves the best or near-best results, e.g., 59.5/84.7/87.8 on InLoc DUC2 vs. ELoFTR's 58.0/80.9/89.3, demonstrating strong generalizability to real-world scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoMatch extends the detector-free coarse-to-fine matching paradigm established by LoFTR, which itself replaced sparse keypoint detection with dense grid-based Transformer matching. Its most direct predecessor is ELoFTR, which introduced static token reduction (depth-wise convolution + max pooling) to cut attention cost but sacrificed representational capacity; CoMatch replaces this with a dynamic, covisibility-guided token condenser that adaptively weights tokens before aggregation. The bilateral subpixel refinement departs from all prior semi-dense matchers (LoFTR, ASpanFormer, ELoFTR, etc.), which fix source-view keypoints at pixel level and refine only the target view, by symmetrically regressing subpixel offsets in both views via a simple correlation-then-expectation step supervised with an epipolar geometry loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bilateral subpixel accuracy is a practical but underexplored improvement: many downstream applications (SfM, SLAM, visual localization) are sensitive to keypoint location, yet prior semi-dense matchers left half the correspondences at pixel granularity. The covisibility-aware design offers a principled mechanism for handling occlusion and viewpoint change without the quadratic cost of full-resolution attention, and the ablation showing a ~10-point AUC drop when removing the BSR module underscores how much the unilateral refinement was silently limiting performance. Open questions include whether the covisibility prediction degrades under extreme viewpoint changes or severe occlusion beyond what MegaDepth training covers, and whether the approach extends naturally to multi-view or 3D correspondence settings.
