## Problem

Existing stereo image generation methods face a fundamental trade-off: warping-based approaches provide geometric accuracy but produce semantic inconsistencies in occluded regions, while diffusion-based methods (e.g., StereoDiffusion) maintain semantic coherence but lack pixel-level geometric precision due to latent-space warping. This gap limits both practical stereo viewing and the quality of synthetic training data for unsupervised stereo matching, which has been constrained to small-scale static scenes or random background filling.

## Input / Output

The method takes a left-view RGB image (512×512 for SD v1.5, 768×768 for SD v2.1) and a disparity map as input. During training, ground-truth disparity is used; at inference, disparity is predicted by a monocular depth estimation model (e.g., Depth Anything v2) and can be sparse (as in KITTI's LiDAR-derived maps). The output is a right-view stereo image that maintains pixel-level correspondence with the left view, with a scale factor γ (typically 0.05–0.35) controlling the maximum disparity as a fraction of image width. The generated triplet ⟨I_l, D̂_l, Î_r⟩ also serves as training data for unsupervised stereo matching.

## Method

GenStereo employs a two-stream U-Net architecture (reference and denoising) initialized from Stable Diffusion, where the reference U-Net is conditioned on the left image and its Fourier-encoded coordinate embedding, while the denoising U-Net is conditioned on the disparity-warped image and the warped coordinate embedding. A cross-view attention mechanism (q = F_r, k = v = [F_l, F_r]) enables semantic feature alignment between views, and a CLIP image encoder replaces text conditioning. Training uses a dual-space objective combining standard latent-space denoising loss with a pixel-space L2 loss (α=1) to enforce pixel-level accuracy. An adaptive fusion module learns spatially-varying weights via a 3×3 convolution to blend the generated image with the warped image, favoring warped content in high-confidence regions and generated content in occluded areas. Random disparity dropout (applied to 10% of samples) improves robustness to sparse real-world disparity maps, and the model is trained on 684K images from 11 diverse synthetic datasets for open-world generalization.

## Results

On Middlebury 2014, GenStereo (SD v2.1, pseudo disparities) achieves 25.14 dB PSNR, 0.911 SSIM, and 0.060 LPIPS, outperforming the best baseline StereoDiffusion (15.46 dB PSNR, 0.468 SSIM, 0.231 LPIPS) by ~10 dB in PSNR. On KITTI 2015, it reaches 23.49 dB PSNR and 0.849 SSIM versus StereoDiffusion's 15.68 dB and 0.481 SSIM. For zero-shot unsupervised stereo matching (Table 4), GenStereo with RAFT-Stereo achieves 3.22 D1-all and 0.716 EPE on KITTI 2012, surpassing NeRFStereo (3.79 D1-all, 0.855 EPE) and MfS (4.32 D1-all, 1.009 EPE) across all five evaluation benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenStereo substantially outperforms all generation baselines in Table 2: it exceeds StereoDiffusion [55] by ~10 dB PSNR on both Middlebury 2014 (25.14 vs. 15.46) and KITTI 2015 (23.49 vs. 15.68), and surpasses SD-Inpainting [35] by ~9.4 dB on KITTI (23.49 vs. 9.79), where SD-Inpainting degrades severely due to sparse LiDAR disparities. Among traditional methods, 3D Photography [40] and RePaint [26] trail by 10+ dB PSNR, confirming that diffusion-based approaches with explicit geometric conditioning are necessary. For unsupervised stereo matching (Table 4), GenStereo with RAFT-Stereo achieves the best D1-all on KITTI 2012 (3.22), KITTI 2015 (3.95), ETH3D (0.835), Middlebury-Q (4.07), and Middlebury-H (4.62), beating NeRFStereo [50] by 0.57–1.41 D1-all and MfS [60] by 1.1–1.4 D1-all across benchmarks. Mono2Stereo [59] is competitive on KITTI (4.08 D1-all) but is not evaluated on ETH3D or Middlebury, while PSMNet [6] and IGEV++ [62] serve as supervised reference points that GenStereo's unsupervised pipeline approaches or surpasses on several metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenStereo builds directly on GenWarp's two-stream U-Net architecture with cross-view attention, repurposing it from novel-view synthesis to stereo generation by replacing camera-matrix-based warping with disparity-map-based warping and introducing disparity-aware Fourier coordinate embeddings. It departs from StereoDiffusion's latent-space disparity shifting (which sacrifices pixel-level accuracy) and from SD-Inpainting/Mono2Stereo's occlusion-filling approach (which lacks geometric control) by conditioning the denoising network on both a warped reference image and coordinate embeddings, then applying a pixel-space loss and adaptive fusion to recover sub-pixel alignment. For unsupervised stereo matching, it extends the MfS paradigm by replacing random background patch filling with diffusion-generated right views, enabling large-scale training on diverse real-world images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By simultaneously addressing visual quality and geometric accuracy, GenStereo removes the primary bottleneck in unsupervised stereo matching: the need for high-quality synthetic stereo pairs that are both photorealistic and pixel-accurate. The zero-shot generalization across five datasets and three stereo architectures (PSMNet, RAFT-Stereo, IGEV++) demonstrates that the generated training data transfers broadly, a property prior methods like MfS and NeRF-Stereo could not achieve. The authors acknowledge that large disparities remain challenging due to extensive unconditioned regions, and the reliance on synthetic training data (684K images) with a domain gap to real scenes is an open limitation. Future work could explore extending the framework to larger baselines and integrating it with real-world calibration pipelines for XR and autonomous driving applications.
