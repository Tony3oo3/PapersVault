## Problem

Existing video-to-audio (V2A) methods based on diffusion or flow matching model the generation as a noise-to-audio mapping conditioned on video, which entangles cross-modal mappings with stochastic Gaussian priors and results in inefficient training and convoluted transport paths. The paper asks whether directly transforming the video feature distribution into the audio distribution—bypassing noise entirely—yields a more effective and simpler V2A paradigm.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250317/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a silent video clip and produces a temporally aligned audio waveform. Video is encoded by a pretrained visual encoder (CLIP at 10 fps, CAVP at 4 fps, or VidTok at 15 fps) into temporal features of shape T_v × D_v (e.g., 100×768 for a 10-second clip), which must be shape-aligned to the audio latent space (e.g., 215×64) before flow matching can operate. The output latent is decoded by a frozen WaveVAE into a continuous waveform, with the variational sampling of the aligned video latent introducing stochasticity for diverse outputs from the same video.

## Method

VAFlow replaces the Gaussian source distribution in flow matching with video features, establishing a direct video-to-audio ODE transport. A 1D alignment VAE (convolutional encoder-decoder with KL, reconstruction, and multi-granularity contrastive losses) compresses heterogeneous video features into the audio latent shape while preserving spatiotemporal semantics. A DiT-based velocity estimator predicts the transport direction x_a − x_v^AE, with cross-attention layers persistently injecting original video features to counteract signal degradation as t→1. Training proceeds in three stages: pretraining the alignment VAE, training the estimator with the VAE frozen, and joint fine-tuning of both. Classifier-free guidance is applied at inference by interpolating conditioned and unconditioned velocity predictions, and the framework supports multiple visual feature types (semantic, spatiotemporal, reconstruction) as source distributions.

## Results

On the VGGSound test set, VAFlow-CLIP achieves the best audio quality scores among all methods: FD_pas=87.7, FD_mel=3.86, IS_mel=73.6, and KL_pas=1.91, outperforming the strongest diffusion baseline V2A-Mapper (FD_pas=94.9, FD_mel=4.21) and the flow-based Frieren (FD_pas=259.9, FD_mel=6.70). VAFlow-CAVP achieves near-best synchronization accuracy of 96.3%, comparable to Frieren's 97.0%. In semantic relevance (IB-VA), VAFlow-CLIP scores 28.6, second only to TiVA (30.3) among standard V2A models, while text-augmented models like MMAudio-L (33.9) and Seeing&Hearing (36.7) score higher due to external textual supervision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VAFlow-CLIP achieves the best FD_pas (87.7) and FD_mel (3.86) among all methods, beating the strongest diffusion baseline V2A-Mapper (94.9 / 4.21) by ~7.5 and ~0.35 points respectively, and the flow-based Frieren (259.9 / 6.70) by a large margin. On IS_mel, VAFlow-CLIP (73.6) surpasses TiVA (67.0) and V2A-Mapper (64.9), indicating superior audio diversity. For synchronization, VAFlow-CAVP reaches 96.3% accuracy, nearly matching Frieren's 97.0% while achieving far better quality metrics (FD_pas 91.8 vs. 259.9). VAFlow-CLIP trails TiVA (30.3) and MMAudio-L (33.9) on IB-VA semantic relevance, though the latter two rely on auxiliary text data or classifier guidance. In human evaluation against Frieren, VAFlow wins on all three criteria: quality (66.0%), synchronization (60.4%), and semantic consistency (60.9%). Overall, VAFlow dominates on audio quality metrics (FD, IS, KL) while remaining competitive on synchronization, with the main trade-off being slightly lower semantic alignment compared to text-augmented models.

## Relation to Prior Work

VAFlow builds directly on the Flow Matching framework (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023) and rectified flow (Liu et al., ICLR 2023), adopting their linear interpolation path and ODE-based generation, while repurposing the DiT velocity estimator architecture from Stable Audio Open (Evans et al., 2024). The key conceptual departure from prior V2A work (Frieren, MMAudio, TiVA, Diff-Foley, etc.) is replacing the standard Gaussian noise source distribution with video features compressed via an alignment VAE, thereby modeling a direct video-to-audio transport rather than a noise-to-audio denoising conditioned on video. This reframing is enabled by FM's theoretical support for arbitrary source distributions, but no prior V2A system had exploited this freedom.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that the source modality's own features can serve as a superior flow-matching source compared to Gaussian noise, VAFlow offers a potentially generalizable recipe for cross-modal generation tasks beyond V2A (e.g., image-to-audio, video-to-music). The paper also establishes the first consistent scaling property for flow-based V2A models, addressing a gap noted in prior work. Open questions include whether the alignment VAE + joint training recipe transfers to other modality pairs, whether the approach extends to speech and music domains (acknowledged as future work), and whether the reliance on pretrained visual encoders (CLIP, CAVP) limits ceiling performance.
