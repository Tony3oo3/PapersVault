## Problem

Existing scene flow estimation methods rely solely on a single consecutive point cloud pair, capturing only the instantaneous scene state and lacking long-term motion context, which leads to high errors under occlusion, noise, and diverse foreground/background motion. No prior work jointly addresses sequential scene flow estimation (exploiting multi-frame history) and future scene flow prediction (generating motion to unseen frames) within a single unified model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250372/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a point cloud sequence of length N (N=3 in experiments) with 8,192 sampled points per cloud (ground points and points beyond 35 m removed) and produces per-point displacement vectors for each consecutive pair (SF₁…SF_{N−1}) plus M future steps (SF_N…SF_{N+M−1}). Future point clouds are obtained by cumulatively warping the last historical cloud with the predicted flows. The model implicitly switches between estimation and prediction modes depending on whether the next point cloud is present in the input.

## Method

GenFlow3D is a recurrent network that processes each point cloud in a sequence, combining a hierarchical U-Net encoder (set conv / set upconv), a Bidirectional Correlation Block (BDCB) that captures instantaneous inter-frame motion via a bidirectional flow embedding layer, and a Spatiotemporal Iterative Block (STIB) that accumulates long-term motion history through a SetGRU cell with a learnable spatial interpolation step. A Transformer-based Diffusion Decoder then refines scene flow in a coarse-to-fine manner: a conventional estimator produces the coarsest-level flow, and at each finer level a denoising diffusion process (DDIM, T=1000) generates the scene flow residual conditioned on a multi-scale signal comprising encoder features, BDCB correlation features, STIB hidden states, and upsampled coarse flow. A temporal initialization strategy back-warps the previous timestep's full scene flow onto the current frame to provide a reliable starting point. The unified supervised loss (L2 on residuals + L1 on full flow) is summed across all timesteps and decoder levels, enabling a single model to perform both estimation and prediction.

## Results

On nuScenes, GenFlow3D achieves EPE3D of 0.0162 (SF1), 0.0139 (SF2), and 0.0158 (SF3) for scene flow estimation and prediction, with Acc3DS reaching 0.9576, 0.9754, and 0.9611 respectively. On Argoverse 2, the corresponding EPE3D values are 0.0175, 0.0164, and 0.0248. The model improves SF2 over SF1 by 14.2% (nuScenes) and 6.3% (Argoverse 2) in EPE3D, demonstrating the benefit of sequential historical information. For future scene flow prediction (SF3), GenFlow3D achieves 55.6% and 57.2% lower EPE3D than the best prediction baseline (MoNet) on nuScenes and Argoverse 2 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For scene flow estimation, GenFlow3D outperforms all six pair-based baselines on both datasets. Against the strongest estimation baseline DifFlow3D (also diffusion-based), GenFlow3D reduces EPE3D by 27.0% for SF1 and 37.1% for SF2 on nuScenes, and by 7.9% and 13.2% respectively on Argoverse 2. The gap widens for SF2 because pair-based methods (PointPWC-Net, FLOT, PV-RAFT, PT-FlowNet, Bi-PointFlowNet, DifFlow3D) cannot exploit sequential history, while GenFlow3D leverages the Spatiotemporal Iterative Block and temporal initialization. For future scene flow prediction, GenFlow3D dominates both recurrent prediction baselines: it achieves 55.6% lower EPE3D than MoNet on nuScenes (0.0158 vs. 0.0356) and 57.2% lower on Argoverse 2 (0.0248 vs. 0.0580), while also improving Acc3DS from 0.8107 to 0.9611 (nuScenes) and 0.6785 to 0.9234 (Argoverse 2). PointRNN is substantially weaker than MoNet on both datasets, confirming that the diffusion-based decoder design is the key differentiator over purely recurrent architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenFlow3D extends the sequential scene flow estimation line (SPCM-Net [9], MoNet [19]) by being the first to jointly perform sequential estimation and future prediction within a single recurrent model, rather than treating them as separate tasks. Architecturally, it repurposes the bidirectional flow embedding from Bi-PointFlowNet [3] for instantaneous motion capture, the SetGRU from FlowStep3D [13] for temporal state accumulation, and the diffusion-based residual refinement paradigm from DifFlow3D [16] for the decoder. The key conceptual departure is using discriminative condition signals (hidden state vs. correlation feature) to let a single diffusion decoder switch between estimation and prediction modes, inspired by the conditional mode-switching in MCVD [40].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The joint formulation is practically important for autonomous driving, where a system needs both accurate current-frame motion (for tracking, SLAM) and short-horizon future motion (for planning) from the same sensor stream. The paper demonstrates that sequential context (acceleration, trajectory priors) meaningfully reduces estimation error beyond what pair-based methods achieve, and that diffusion-based generation captures general motion patterns that benefit prediction. Limitations include the modest CD improvement (attributed to lack of one-to-one correspondence and high scan rates), the negative effect of increasing prediction length M beyond 1 step due to error accumulation, and the reliance on ground-truth scene flow for supervised training. Open questions include whether the recurrent architecture scales to much longer sequences, whether the diffusion decoder can be made fast enough for real-time deployment, and whether the mode-switching mechanism generalizes to other generative 3D tasks.
