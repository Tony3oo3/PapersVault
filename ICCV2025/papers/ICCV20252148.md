## Problem

Online continual learning (OCL) in streaming data requires handling catastrophic forgetting when each sample is seen only once, yet current state-of-the-art methods rely on storing and replaying exemplars or features from previous tasks, which is impractical under data privacy constraints. Prompt-based methods achieve strong rehearsal-free performance in offline CL, but growing prompt components or generators with each new task violate the throughput requirements of streaming data, causing unprocessed data to be skipped. The paper addresses how to achieve rehearsal-free OCL with a fixed, lightweight parameter budget that maintains high throughput.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images in streaming fashion, where data arrives in small batches (|s_j| ≈ 10) and can be seen only once before being discarded. The input image is processed by a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone (pre-trained on ImageNet-21K) whose patch embeddings are fed to the prompt generator to produce class-conditional prompts prepended to the multi-head self-attention layers. The output is a class label over all previously and currently learned classes, with the task ID available only during training (class-incremental setting).

## Method

PROL generates prompts using a single lightweight 1D-CNN generator (G_K, G_V) with 3 parameters per head per layer (<1K total), trained only on the first task and then frozen for stability. Plasticity is achieved through per-class learnable scalers-and-shifters (16 parameters per class) that scale and shift the generator output, selected at inference via cosine similarity between the input and class-wise learnable keys K^c. A cross-correlation matrix M between PTM-only and PTM+prompt features enforces generalization preservation (adapted from SAFE), while a hard-soft update strategy adaptively switches between high and decayed learning rates based on a cross-entropy threshold. The joint loss combines intra-task and inter-task cross-entropy, input-to-key similarity, current-to-previous class orthogonality on keys, and the generalization-preserving matrix loss, all weighted by tunable coefficients.

## Results

PROL achieves 86.32% FAA and 91.35% CAA on CIFAR-100, 73.50% FAA and 78.13% CAA on ImageNet-R, 47.72% FAA and 58.96% CAA on ImageNet-A, and 72.51% FAA and 79.89% CAA on CUB-200, all in a rehearsal-free online setting. These results outperform the closest rehearsal-free competitor ConvPrompt by 1.5–2.6% FAA across datasets, and surpass the joint (full-replay) versions of RanPAC and RanDumb by 2–20% FAA on three of four datasets. In terms of forgetting, PROL achieves FM values of 6.34 (CIFAR-100), 4.82 (ImageNet-R), 3.29 (ImageNet-A), and 8.92 (CUB-200), comparable to or better than the joint baselines. PROL requires only 0.213M trainable parameters with moderate throughput (35.42 samples/s), far fewer parameters than ConvPrompt (1.434M) or MOS (3.197M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PROL outperforms all rehearsal-free baselines by large margins: it beats RanPAC and RanDumb (which collapse to ~9–10% FAA without replay) by over 70 points, and surpasses L2P, DualPrompt, ConvPrompt, and MOS by 2–33% FAA depending on dataset. Against the joint (full-replay) versions RanPAC-J and RanDumb-J, PROL wins on CIFAR-100, ImageNet-R, and ImageNet-A (e.g., 73.50 vs. 52.63 FAA on ImageNet-R) but loses on CUB-200 (72.51 vs. 85.82), where the fine-grained bird classes benefit from full replay. ConvPrompt is the closest rehearsal-free competitor, trailing PROL by only 1.5–2.6% FAA across datasets, while MOS (adapter-based) is outperformed by 2–21% FAA with much higher forgetting (FM 11–35 vs. PROL's 3–9). In Table 2, PROL with a smaller ViT-Tiny backbone (5.6M params) achieves 64.59% FAA on CIFAR-100, exceeding the best non-PTM method ERSM (47.72% with ResNet-18 and 5000 exemplars) by 16.9 points, demonstrating that PTM-based parameter-efficient tuning with rehearsal-free learning substantially outperforms larger backbones with memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

PROL builds directly on ConvPrompt's 1DCNN prompt generator architecture (distributed across all MSA heads) and adapts SAFE's cross-correlation matrix for preserving pre-trained model generalization, but departs from both by freezing the generator after the first task and introducing per-class scalers-and-shifters as the sole plasticity mechanism. It positions itself against two lines of work: (1) OCL methods (RanPAC, RanDumb, MOS) that rely on storing and replaying exemplars or features, which the paper argues is impractical under data-privacy constraints, and (2) growing-component prompt methods (ConvPrompt, CODA-Prompt) that increase parameter count and reduce throughput as tasks accumulate. The hard-soft update strategy and the orthogonality constraint on class-wise keys are novel mechanisms not present in either lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: rehearsal-free OCL under data-privacy constraints, where previously seen data cannot be stored or replayed. The finding that rehearsal-free RanPAC/RanDumb collapse to ~9.5% FAA (vs. ~80% with joint data) while PROL achieves 86% without any stored data underscores both the severity of the problem and the value of the prompt-based solution. Limitations include evaluation only on the disjoint-task setting (not blurry or iBlurry), a single backbone (ViT-B/16), and lower performance than joint baselines on CUB-200. Open questions include scalability to very long task sequences, applicability to non-ViT backbones, and whether the frozen-generator assumption holds when the first task is unrepresentative of the full class distribution.
