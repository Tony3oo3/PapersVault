## Problem

Mixture of Experts (MoE) has proven effective in large vision-language models, but its potential in real-time open-vocabulary object detectors—compact models trained on large-scale vision-language data—remains unexplored. The central challenge is expanding the subnet search space to handle the diverse visual and contextual patterns in open scenarios without increasing inference cost or memory, particularly when training data is limited.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252460/cropped_page0_idx9.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (processed at 640×640 or 800×1333 resolution) and a free-form text prompt describing target objects, and outputs bounding boxes with corresponding object category labels in a zero-shot setting. After Language-guided Query Selection, only 900 tokens proceed through the 6-layer MoE decoder, and at each MoE layer a router activates exactly k experts per token (e.g., top-2 out of 16), keeping activated parameters equivalent to a single FFN. The model is evaluated on COCO, LVIS (minival and val), and ODinW benchmarks.

## Method

Dynamic-DINO extends the reproduced Grounding DINO 1.5 Edge from a dense model to a dynamic inference framework via MoE-Tuning applied to the decoder. The key mechanism is granularity decomposition: each FFN's intermediate hidden dimension is split into k partitions, and the FFN is replicated N times, yielding k×N fine-grained experts whose total parameter count matches the original FFN. Pre-trained FFN weights are decomposed and allocated to initialize the experts (horizontal split of W₁, vertical split of W₂, divided bias b₂/k) so that the sum of all k expert outputs exactly equals the original FFN output. A router (single linear layer) is initialized by replicating N random centroid vectors k times, guaranteeing that at the start of fine-tuning it always selects the k experts from the same FFN, preventing performance degradation. During inference, the router scores all kN experts via softmax and activates only the top-k, forming a compact input-dependent subnet. Training fine-tunes only the Cross-Attention in the Feature Enhancer, the MoE layers in the decoder, and the Detection Head, with a load-balancing auxiliary loss to ensure uniform expert utilization.

## Results

Trained on only 1.56M open-source samples (Objects365, GoldG, V3Det), Dynamic-DINO (16-Top2) achieves 43.7 AP on COCO-val and 33.6 AP_all on LVIS-minival at 640×640, surpassing the official Grounding DINO 1.5 Edge (pretrained on the private 20M-sample Grounding dataset) which scores 42.9 and 33.5 respectively. At 800×1333, Dynamic-DINO reaches 46.2 COCO AP and 36.2 LVIS-minival AP_all, matching or exceeding the official model (45.0 / 36.2). On ODinW, Dynamic-DINO improves over the reproduced baseline by +1.0 AP on ODinW13 (46.8 vs 45.8) and +0.4 on ODinW35 (20.0 vs 19.6). Inference speed is comparable: 98.0 FPS with TensorRT at 640×640 versus 111.6 FPS for the official Grounding DINO 1.5 Edge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline, Grounding DINO 1.5 Edge (official, pretrained on 20M private samples), Dynamic-DINO trained on only 1.56M open-source data achieves +0.8 COCO AP (43.7 vs 42.9) and +0.1 LVIS-minival AP_all (33.6 vs 33.5) at 640×640, while substantially outperforming on rare-class detection (AP_r: 37.0 vs 28.0) and common-class detection (AP_c: 36.6 vs 34.3). At 800×1333, Dynamic-DINO matches the official model on LVIS-minival AP_all (36.2) but leads on COCO AP (46.2 vs 45.0) and LVIS-val AP_all (29.6 vs 29.3). Compared to other real-time detectors, Dynamic-DINO surpasses YOLO-World v2-L (33.0 LVIS-minival AP_all), OmDet-Turbo-T (30.0), and OVLW-DETR-L (33.5) on LVIS-minival AP_all, and also leads on COCO AP (43.7 vs 42.5 for OmDet-Turbo-T). Against the non-real-time end-to-end methods GLIP (46.3 COCO) and Grounding DINO (48.4 COCO), Dynamic-DINO trails on COCO but is competitive on LVIS rare-class metrics while operating at real-time speed. On ODinW, Dynamic-DINO improves over the reproduced baseline by +1.0 AP (ODinW13) and +0.4 AP (ODinW35), confirming gains on out-of-distribution categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Grounding DINO 1.5 Edge, a real-time open-vocabulary detector, by converting its dense FFN layers into a sparse Mixture-of-Experts structure via a fine-tuning paradigm (MoE-Tuning) rather than full pre-training. The granularity decomposition strategy draws directly from DeepSeekMoE and QwenMoE, which split FFN intermediate dimensions into expert sub-networks, but departs critically by initializing experts from pre-trained FFN weights (ensuring output equivalence at initialization) instead of random initialization. The load-balancing loss follows MoE-LLaVA, and the overall positioning targets the underexplored intersection of MoE and compact multimodal detectors, as opposed to the well-studied LVLM setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that MoE fine-tuning can improve open-vocabulary detection with substantially less data (1.56M open-source vs. 20M private), suggesting that structured sparsity helps mitigate long-tail and gradient-conflict issues in compact models. However, the practical impact is tempered by a modest speed regression (17.1 vs. 20.2 FPS in PyTorch) due to sequential expert evaluation, and the authors acknowledge they could not validate scaling to larger models (e.g., Grounding DINO 1.5 Pro) or with more data. Open questions include whether parallel expert inference can close the speed gap, whether the expert-specialization patterns observed in deep layers generalize across architectures, and whether the data-efficiency advantage persists at larger training scales.
