## Problem

Existing 3D panoptic segmentation methods rely on costly test-time optimization (NeRF or 3DGS fitting), accurate camera poses, and a decoupled pipeline that first runs a 2D panoptic model and then lifts predictions to 3D. This separation sacrifices both efficiency and multi-view consistency, and the standard per-pixel mask merging heuristic is ill-suited for globally consistent multi-view instance assignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250523/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unconstrained collection of unposed, uncalibrated RGB images (no camera parameters or depth maps required) and produces, in a single forward pass, a global 3D point map, a semantic class label, and an instance ID for every pixel in every input image. At test time, 50 keyframes are selected via farthest-point sampling to generate shared instance queries, while remaining views are processed frame-by-frame for mask prediction at W/2 × H/2 resolution. Optionally, panoptic labels are uplifted to a 3DGS scene via LUDVIG to render novel-view segmentations.

## Method

PanSt3R fuses frozen DINOv2 (2D semantic) and MUSt3R (3D geometric) features through an MLP into joint 3D-semantic tokens, then decodes multi-view-consistent instance masks via a Mask2Former-style transformer with a shared set of learnable queries that attend to all views simultaneously, ensuring the same instance ID is assigned across views. Classification uses an open-vocabulary head computing cosine similarity between query embeddings and SigLIP text embeddings of class names. A key contribution is replacing the standard MaskFormer per-pixel voting merge with a QUBO formulation (solved by simulated annealing) that globally optimizes mask selection to minimize overlap and holes across all views. For novel-view synthesis, the method optionally regularizes 3DGS optimization with an instance-color rendering loss and uplifts one-hot instance labels via LUDVIG, yielding state-of-the-art PQ at roughly 100× lower inference cost than NeRF-based baselines.

## Results

On the PanLift benchmark, PanSt3R+LUDVIG achieves state-of-the-art scene-PQ of 66.3 on Hypersim and 67.5 on ScanNet, surpassing the previous best (PLGS at 62.4 and PCF-Lift at 63.5 respectively). The direct forward-pass variant PanSt3R (without LUDVIG) already reaches 65.7 PQ on ScanNet and 62.0 on Replica, outperforming all baselines on two of three datasets. On ScanNet++ (100 classes), PanSt3R+LUDVIG achieves 54.7 PQ versus 29.5 for PanLift and 28.4 for Contrastive Lift. Inference takes approximately 4.5 minutes (or ~40 min with LUDVIG), compared to 420–900 minutes for NeRF-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PanSt3R+LUDVIG sets a new state-of-the-art on Hypersim (66.3 vs. 62.4 for PLGS, the previous best) and ScanNet (67.5 vs. 63.5 for PCF-Lift), with margins of +3.9 and +4.0 PQ respectively. On Replica, the direct PanSt3R forward pass (62.0) outperforms all baselines including Contrastive Lift (59.1), while PanSt3R+LUDVIG (60.6) is slightly lower than the direct variant but still above all baselines. On ScanNet++ with 100 fine-grained classes, PanSt3R+LUDVIG (54.7 PQ) surpasses PanLift (29.5) and Contrastive Lift (28.4) by over 25 PQ points, with particularly large gains on thing instances (PQth: 51.7 vs. 15.6 and 14.8). All competing methods require accurate camera poses and costly test-time NeRF or 3DGS optimization (420–900 min), whereas PanSt3R operates in a single forward pass in ~4.5 min without any camera parameters, representing a 100× speedup over the slowest baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

PanSt3R sits at the intersection of two recent foundation-model lines: MUSt3R (a scalable multi-view 3D reconstruction Transformer) and Mask2Former (a unified 2D panoptic segmentation decoder). Prior 3D panoptic segmentation methods (PanLift, Contrastive Lift, DM-NeRF, PLGS, PCF-Lift) all follow a two-stage pipeline—run an off-the-shelf 2D panoptic model per view, then lift and fuse predictions into 3D via costly NeRF or 3DGS test-time optimization, requiring accurate camera poses. PanSt3R departs from this by jointly regressing 3D geometry and multi-view-consistent panoptic masks in a single forward pass on unposed images, and introduces a QUBO-based mask merging step that replaces the heuristic per-pixel voting of MaskFormer with a globally optimal instance selection across all views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: PanSt3R matches or exceeds state-of-the-art PQ on ScanNet, Hypersim, and ScanNet++ while running in ~4 minutes versus 400–900 minutes for NeRF-based competitors, and it removes the dependency on camera poses entirely. The finding that standard MaskFormer merging is poorly suited to multi-view settings (QUBO yields large PQ gains) is a useful diagnostic for anyone extending 2D segmentation decoders to 3D. Open questions include scalability to large outdoor scenes, real-time inference, and whether the open-vocabulary classification head (SigLIP text embeddings) can be extended to truly open 3D scene understanding without a fixed class set.
