## Problem

Directly applying existing binarization techniques to change detection causes severe accuracy degradation because 1-bit networks exhibit consistently lower mutual information I(X,Z) between inputs and hidden representations, limiting their ability to distinguish meaningful 'interest changes' from irrelevant 'noise changes.' No binary neural network had been specifically designed for the change detection task prior to this work.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired bi-temporal RGB images (t0, t1) at resolutions ranging from 224×224 (PCD, cropped from 224×1024 panoramas) to 512×512 (VL_CMU_CD) and 256×256 (LEVIR-CD), and produces a pixel-level binary change mask. All weights and activations are constrained to 1 bit, with convolution replaced by XNOR-PopCount bit-wise operations; the auxiliary module used during training is entirely removed at inference, so the deployed model incurs no additional computational cost.

## Method

BiCD builds on a 1-bit C-3PO or DR-TANet backbone and introduces a training-only auxiliary objective grounded in the Information Bottleneck principle. The final loss (Eq. 14) combines three components: an L2-norm term approximating I(X,Z(θ)) to compress features, the standard change detection loss approximating I(Z(θ),Y), and a separability objective Ψ that splits auxiliary-module outputs into 'noise' and 'interest' components via element-wise multiplication with the ground-truth mask, then applies L1 reconstruction losses to suppress noise and preserve interest changes. The auxiliary module consists of four parallel MLP branches plus a convolutional output layer that maps 1-bit features to dimension-aligned representations Z(θ,η), enabling mutual information estimation without intractable posterior computation. Ablation studies show the optimal configuration places the reconstruction loss I(X,Z(θ,η)) on the Siamese backbone and the full separability objective on the 1-bit change generator, yielding a 1.8% F1 improvement over the 1-bit baseline.

## Results

On the PCD dataset (TSUNAMI subset), BiCD with the 1-bit C-3PO backbone achieves 86.5% F1-score, outperforming the best prior 1-bit method (ReActNet at 84.0%) by 2.5% and approaching the real-valued C-3PO (88.4%) within 1.9%. On the GSV subset, BiCD (C-3PO) reaches 74.1% F1, surpassing the real-valued DR-TANet (72.3%) while using only 2.1M parameters and 6.6G OPs versus 33.4M/28.5G for DR-TANet. On VL_CMU_CD, BiCD (C-3PO) achieves 71.9% F1, and on LEVIR-CD it reaches 89.9% F1 with a 2.5× latency reduction (158.4 ms vs 392.8 ms) on an ARM Cortex-A76 edge device.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiCD consistently outperforms all three 1-bit baselines (BNN, ReActNet, AdaBin) across every dataset and framework: on PCD-TSUNAMI with C-3PO, BiCD gains +14.8% over BNN (86.5 vs 71.7), +2.5% over ReActNet (86.5 vs 84.0), and +3.7% over AdaBin (86.5 vs 82.8). Against real-valued change detection methods, BiCD (1-bit C-3PO) surpasses DR-TANet on GSV (74.1 vs 72.3) and FC-Siam variants by a wide margin, while remaining within 1.9% of real-valued C-3PO on TSUNAMI (86.5 vs 88.4). On VL_CMU_CD, BiCD (C-3PO) at 71.9% F1 matches the real-valued CSCDNet (71.0%) and exceeds real-valued DR-TANet (73.2%) by a narrow 1.3% gap. On LEVIR-CD, BiCD (89.9%) outperforms the lightweight real-valued BIT (89.3%) and FC-Siam variants (84.4%, 81.8%), and is within 0.7–2.2% of heavier real-valued methods like EGRCNN (89.6%), DARNet (90.6%), and Changeformer (90.4%), while using only 2.1M parameters versus 9.6–107M for those baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: binary neural networks (XNOR-Net, ReActNet, AdaBin) and Information Bottleneck theory (Tishby et al.). Prior BNN research has been almost exclusively evaluated on image classification, with no systematic exploration of dense prediction tasks like change detection. The architectural backbone is C-3PO (Wang et al., 2023), a recent change detection framework that merges temporal and spatial features, and the binary convolution formulation follows XNOR-Net's sign-function + XNOR-PopCount paradigm. The key departure is the IB-guided auxiliary module, which repurposes the local-learning auxiliary decoder concept (BiDet, InfoPro) not for gradient isolation but as a training-time proxy for mutual information terms, specifically targeting the separability between 'interest changes' and 'noise changes' that binarization degrades.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first BNN for change detection, BiCD opens a practical path for deploying change detection on resource-constrained edge devices (2.5× latency reduction, 30× memory savings), which is relevant for autonomous driving, mobile robotics, and on-board satellite processing. The training-only auxiliary module design is a clean pattern that could transfer to other binarized dense-prediction tasks. However, the IB connection is somewhat loose in practice—mutual information terms are replaced by L2/L1 reconstruction losses following standard engineering approximations, making the theoretical framing more suggestive than rigorous. Open questions include whether the IB-guided separability objective generalizes beyond change detection to other binarized segmentation tasks, and whether the modest 1.7–3.3% F1 gains over ReActNet/AdaBin persist at larger scale or with deeper backbones.
