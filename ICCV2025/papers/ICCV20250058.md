## Problem

Training accurate 3D animal pose and shape regressors requires large amounts of precisely labeled data, but capturing such data for wild animals is impractical, monocular pseudo-labeling produces physically implausible fits, and game-engine synthetic data lacks visual realism while demanding substantial manual effort per species. The paper addresses this by proposing a language-controllable synthetic data generation pipeline that produces realistic images with exact ground-truth pose and shape parameters without requiring artist-designed 3D assets for each new species.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250058/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The regression model takes a single RGB image of a quadrupedal animal and regresses SMAL pose parameters (θ), shape parameters (β), and a posed 3D mesh. The data-generation pipeline consumes a species name, sampled shape/pose parameters, and a synthesized text prompt, producing 1024×1024 images paired with exact SMAL+ ground-truth. Control signals (depth map and Canny-edge render) are extracted from a Pyrender render of the posed mesh and fed alongside the prompt to the image generator.

## Method

The pipeline samples a taxon from Laurasiatheria (with 247 dog breeds at 50% probability), then samples shape by fitting a multivariate Gaussian over 128 CLIP text embeddings per species and decoding through the deterministic AWOL flow model, and samples pose from BITE-extracted pseudo-poses collected from online dog images. A posed SMAL+ mesh is rendered in Pyrender, captioned by Molmo-7B for global orientation, combined with sampled camera and scenery descriptors, and synthesized into a coherent prompt by Qwen2.5-7B. FLUX (a rectified-flow text-to-image transformer) is conditioned jointly on the text prompt and a pretrained ControlNet receiving both depth and Canny-edge control signals, with no finetuning of either component. The resulting GenZoo dataset (1M images) is used to train a ViT-Pose backbone with a parameter-estimation head (or ResNet-50 for baseline matching), supervised by 2D joint re-projection and direct pose/shape losses, achieving state-of-the-art S-MPJPE on Animal3D without any real-world training images.

## Results

On the Animal3D benchmark, the GenZoo-trained ViT-Pose model achieves 97.0 PCK@0.5, 160.1 S-MPJPE, and 116.6 PA-MPJPE, outperforming the strongest prior baseline (PARE) by 57% in S-MPJPE (374.9 → 160.1). The ResNet-50 variant achieves 95.1 PCK@0.5 and 201.1 S-MPJPE. On the newly introduced GenZoo-Felidae benchmark (1000 unseen feline samples), the full model reaches 99.6 PCK@0.5, 83.5 S-MPJPE, 62.0 PA-MPJPE, 91.5 S-V2V, and 72.1 PA-V2V, demonstrating strong generalization to unseen species.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines reported in Animal3D [55]: HMR, PARE, and WLDO. The GenZoo-trained model decisively outperforms all three on every metric. Against the strongest baseline PARE, S-MPJPE drops from 374.9 to 160.1 (a 57% reduction), PCK@0.5 rises from 85.6 to 97.0, and PA-MPJPE improves from 127.2 to 116.6. Against HMR and WLDO, the gains in S-MPJPE are even larger (496.2→160.1 and 484.0→160.1 respectively), though PA-MPJPE improvements are more modest (124.8→116.6 and 123.9→116.6). The authors note that PA-MPJPE gains appear comparatively saturated, partly because Animal3D's pseudo-labels themselves contain physically implausible 3D fits, suggesting a ceiling on achievable performance with that ground truth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of synthetic data generation for pose estimation, which relies on explicit 3D scene rendering via game engines (SURREAL, AGORA, BEDLAM for humans; ZooBuilder, SyDog-Video for animals). Instead, it replaces the graphics engine with a conditional diffusion model (FLUX + ControlNet), using rendered depth maps and Canny edges as control signals to guide generation. The pipeline builds directly on AWOL for stochastic shape sampling in CLIP space, BITE for extracting a diverse pseudo-pose distribution from dog images, and ViT-Pose as the regression backbone. Conceptually, it reframes synthetic data generation as a controllable image-synthesis problem rather than a 3D-scene-compositing problem, making species and environment specification a matter of language prompting rather than asset authoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a purely synthetic, generatively-produced dataset (GenZoo, 1M images) can yield state-of-the-art 3D animal pose and shape estimation without any real-world training data, addressing a fundamental data-scarcity bottleneck in the field. The 57% S-MPJPE improvement over prior SOTA on Animal3D is striking, though the authors themselves note that Animal3D's pseudo-labels may impose a performance ceiling, as evidenced by the more modest PA-MPJPE gains and visual inspection of implausible ground-truth fits. Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond quadrupeds (SMAL's fixed topology limits expressivity), whether more expressive models like VAREN would unlock further gains, and whether the diffusion-model-as-renderer paradigm can be extended to other domains (e.g., human pose, object 6D pose) where game-engine rendering is similarly costly to adapt.
