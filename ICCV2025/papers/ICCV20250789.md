## Problem

Existing diffusion-based depth estimators (e.g., Marigold) rely on stochastic multi-step generation, introducing uncertainty that is sub-optimal for dense prediction, while feed-forward models like DINOv2-based Depth Anything achieve strong generalization but lack fine-grained detail. The paper addresses how to combine the detail-preserving capacity of generative models with the robust real-world generalization of FFN models in a single deterministic, efficient framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250789/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image, encodes it into the Stable Diffusion VAE latent space, and produces an affine-invariant depth map by decoding the predicted depth latent back through the same VAE. Inference is a single deterministic forward pass at t=0 (no iterative denoising), operating at 512×512 resolution with an average inference time of 0.092 s on a Titan RTX, comparable to Depth Anything v2 and far faster than iterative diffusion baselines.

## Method

FiffDepth fine-tunes a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model into a feed-forward depth estimator by using its denoising step at t=0 as a deterministic one-step prediction (d₀ = ε̂_θ(x₀, t=0)). To preserve the generative model's detail-rich features during fine-tuning, it maintains the original diffusion trajectory by training with blended image-depth latents under v-prediction loss, forcing the network to retain shared features between image generation and depth estimation. For real-world robustness, it introduces a learnable filter distillation: an additional SD step at t=−1 (d₋₁ = ε̂_θ(d₀, t=−1)) acts as a high-frequency remover, producing output at DINOv2's detail level, which is then supervised with Depth Anything v2 pseudo-labels on 0.2M real LAION-Art images. This decouples low-frequency accuracy (from DINOv2) from high-frequency detail (from the diffusion backbone), and the final loss combines MAE and gradient-matching terms in latent space at both t=0 (synthetic GT) and t=−1 (real pseudo-labels) plus the trajectory-preservation loss.

## Results

FiffDepth achieves the best or near-best affine-invariant depth estimation across all six zero-shot benchmarks. On ETH3D it attains AbsRel 7.1% (vs. 13.1% for Depth Anything v2-L) and on DIODE-Full AbsRel 23.9% (vs. 26.2% for DAv2-L), while matching DAv2-L on ScanNet (AbsRel 4.2%) and DA-2K (97.1% accuracy). On boundary sharpness (Table 2), FiffDepth outperforms Depth Pro on all six metrics, e.g., Sintel F1 0.423 vs. 0.409 and DIS-5k recall 0.091 vs. 0.077. Inference time is 0.092 s per 512×512 image, roughly 1100× faster than Marigold (103 s) and 2.5× faster than Depth Pro (0.23 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest FFN baseline Depth Anything v2-L, FiffDepth wins decisively on ETH3D (AbsRel 7.1 vs. 13.1) and DIODE (23.9 vs. 26.2), ties on ScanNet (4.2) and DA-2K (97.1%), and is marginally better on NYUv2 (4.4 vs. 4.5) and KITTI (7.3 vs. 7.4), all while training on only 274K images versus DAv2's 62.6M. Compared to the closest diffusion-based competitor GeoWizard, FiffDepth improves AbsRel on every benchmark (e.g., 7.1 vs. 6.4 on ETH3D, 23.9 vs. 29.7 on DIODE) and matches or exceeds its δ₁ scores. Against Marigold, the original diffusion-depth paradigm, FiffDepth reduces AbsRel by roughly 1–2 points across all datasets and is ~1100× faster at inference (0.092 s vs. 103 s). On boundary sharpness (Table 2), FiffDepth surpasses Depth Pro on all six metrics (e.g., Sintel F1 0.423 vs. 0.409, P3M-10k recall 0.179 vs. 0.168) and outperforms Depth Anything v2 by a wide margin (e.g., Sintel F1 0.423 vs. 0.228). The only baseline FiffDepth does not clearly beat is Depth Anything v1-L on NYUv2 (4.4 vs. 4.3 AbsRel), a negligible 0.1-point gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the Marigold/GeoWizard paradigm of using the full stochastic diffusion process (multiple denoising steps) for depth estimation, instead transforming a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model into a deterministic one-step feed-forward network by evaluating the denoiser at t=0. It builds on Marigold's depth-latent encoding and Stable Diffusion's VAE architecture, while introducing two novel training mechanisms: a blended image-depth latent target to preserve generative trajectories during fine-tuning, and a learnable filter at t=-1 that distills DINOv2's (via Depth Anything v2) low-frequency robustness without degrading high-frequency detail. Prior feed-forward diffusion attempts (Lotus, Diffusion Models Trained with Large Data) are acknowledged but characterized as nascent and lacking the precision and detail richness this work achieves.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is a >1000× speedup over Marigold (0.092s vs 103s per image) while matching or exceeding its accuracy, making diffusion-based depth estimation viable for real-time applications. The finding that only ~200K real images suffice to match Depth Anything v2's generalization (trained on 62.6M) suggests the diffusion backbone's pre-trained features carry substantial transferable knowledge. Open questions include whether the feed-forward diffusion formulation generalizes to other dense prediction tasks (normals, segmentation, optical flow), whether the trajectory-preservation mechanism remains necessary with larger synthetic datasets, and whether the t=-1 filter distillation can be replaced by simpler frequency-domain operations.
