## Problem

Existing INR-based point cloud compression methods are limited to lossy compression because the decoder network must be encoded into the bitstream, forcing the use of small, weakly-fitting networks. Additionally, the overfitting time required to train the INR on each target point cloud is prohibitively long for practical deployment. This leaves a gap: no INR-based method exists for lossless point cloud geometry compression (PCGLC) with acceptable encoding speed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a point cloud sequence (geometry only, 10-bit voxel coordinates) and groups it into GoPs of 32 frames. The output bitstream contains three components: arithmetic-coded occupancy bits at each spatial scale (from highest to lowest), the lowest-scale point cloud encoded directly as bytes (≤64 points), and the quantized (8-bit) decoder network parameters entropy-coded with a Laplace model. Decoding is fully lossless, recovering exact point coordinates.

## Method

LINR-PCGC overfits a lightweight multiscale SparseConv network per GoP, where all frames in a GoP share the same decoder, amortizing the parameter bitstream cost. The network progressively downsamples via maxpooling to a few dozen points, then reconstructs higher scales using an 8-stage Child Node Prediction (CNP) module that predicts child-node occupancy channel-by-channel with GDFE/LDFE feature extractors and sparse convolutions. A Scale Context Extraction (SCE) module injects scale embeddings and neighbor occupancy to distinguish spatial levels. To accelerate encoding, the previous GoP's overfitted network initializes the next GoP (saving ~65% time), and an L2 regularization term during training makes parameters follow a Laplace distribution for compact entropy coding via the Adaptive Quantization and Model Compression modules.

## Results

On the MVUB dataset, LINR-PCGC achieves 0.806 bpp (fast setting) and 0.725 bpp (extended setting), outperforming G-PCC (0.921 bpp) by 12.5% and SparsePCGC (0.93 bpp) by 13.3% in the fast setting, and by 21.2% and 21.9% respectively in the extended setting. On 8iVFB and Owlii, the method similarly leads all baselines: 0.616/0.564 bpp vs. G-PCC 0.743 and SparsePCGC 0.625 (8iVFB), and 0.465/0.423 bpp vs. G-PCC 0.59 and SparsePCGC 0.477 (Owlii). Decoding time is consistently the fastest across all datasets (0.42–0.55 s/frame), roughly half that of G-PCC or SparsePCGC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LINR-PCGC outperforms all three baselines on every dataset in both fast and extended encoding settings. Against G-PCC v23, the method reduces bpp by 12.5% (fast) to 21.2% (extended) on MVUB, 17.1% to 24.1% on 8iVFB, and 21.4% to 28.4% on Owlii. Against SparsePCGC, the gains are 13.3% (fast) to 21.9% (extended) on MVUB, 1.3% to 9.7% on 8iVFB, and 2.5% to 11.4% on Owlii. Notably, on MVUB SparsePCGC actually performs slightly worse than G-PCC (0.930 vs. 0.921 bpp), illustrating its sensitivity to distribution shift, whereas LINR-PCGC maintains a clear advantage over both. V-PCC is consistently the weakest in compression ratio (1.4–1.5× higher bpp) and has encoding times 50–70× longer. In decoding speed, LINR-PCGC is roughly 2× faster than G-PCC and SparsePCGC across all datasets, thanks to its lightweight multiscale SparseConv decoder shared across a GoP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multiscale sparse tensor framework of SparsePCGC (Wang et al., TPAMI 2023) by replacing its fixed-parameter inference paradigm with an Implicit Neural Representation (INR) approach, where the network is overfitted per point cloud sequence and its parameters are encoded into the bitstream. It departs from prior INR-based compression methods (Hu & Wang 2022, Isik et al. 2022, Pistilli et al. 2022, Ruan et al. 2024), which were limited to lossy compression due to decoder size constraints, by introducing a Group-of-Pictures (GoP) sharing strategy borrowed from video coding that amortizes the network parameter cost across multiple frames. The multiscale SparseConv backbone, Child Node Prediction, and entropy coding pipeline are adapted from SparsePCGC, while the Scale Context Extraction, Adaptive Quantization, and Model Compression modules are novel contributions specific to the INR setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making the compression distribution-agnostic (no reliance on a fixed training set), LINR-PCGC addresses a key practical limitation of learned PCGC methods that suffer performance drops under domain shift, making it more deployable in real-world scenarios with diverse content. The GoP initialization strategy reducing encoding time by ~65% is a practical enabler, though encoding time still exceeds non-INR baselines by an order of magnitude at full convergence. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse point cloud sequences (beyond human bodies), whether inter-frame temporal prediction can be incorporated without breaking the INR paradigm, and whether the lossy compression extension the authors mention will preserve the distribution-agnostic advantage. The method is currently limited to three human-body datasets with 96 frames each, so generalizability to LiDAR, indoor scenes, or longer sequences remains untested.
