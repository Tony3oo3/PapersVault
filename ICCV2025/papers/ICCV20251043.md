## Problem

Existing image-to-3D reconstruction models assume objects are fully visible and fail to recover complete 3D geometry and appearance when objects are partially occluded. Prior two-stage pipelines that first perform 2D amodal completion and then 3D reconstruction suffer from two key limitations: 2D completion lacks explicit 3D geometric reasoning, and independently completing multiple views produces multi-view inconsistencies that confuse downstream 3D reconstructors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251043/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a partially occluded RGB image centered on the target object, along with a binary visibility mask (M_vis) marking the object's visible pixels and a binary occlusion mask (M_occ) marking pixels occupied by foreground occluders. These masks are obtained via Segment Anything with point prompts, and DINOv2 features are extracted from the image as additional conditioning. The output is a complete textured 3D mesh (geometry plus appearance) generated in TRELLIS's structured latent (SLAT) space, supporting both single-view and multi-view inputs where images are prioritized by visibility ratio during the denoising process.

## Method

Amodal3R fine-tunes the pre-trained TRELLIS 3D generator by injecting two lightweight attention mechanisms into each transformer block without requiring aggressive retraining. First, a mask-weighted cross-attention modulates the standard cross-attention scores by multiplying each image-token's contribution by the fraction of visible pixels in its corresponding 14×14 patch (A_ij = c_vis,j · exp(S_ij) / Σ_k c_vis,k · exp(S_ik)), smoothly zeroing out attention to fully occluded patches. Second, an occlusion-aware attention layer is placed immediately after, pooling flattened 1-D tokens of the occlusion mask to explicitly distinguish foreground occluders from background, guiding the model to complete only where occlusion actually occurs. Training uses synthetic occlusions (random 2D shapes and 3D-consistent contact occlusions generated via random-walk on mesh triangles) on 3D-FUTURE, ABO, and HSSD, and the model generalizes to real-world scenes without additional heuristics.

## Results

On the Google Scanned Objects (GSO) benchmark, Amodal3R achieves FID 30.64, KID 0.35%, P-FID 7.69, and MMD 3.62‰ in the single-view setting, substantially outperforming the best baseline (TRELLIS + pix2gestalt: FID 58.82, P-FID 26.43, MMD 4.17‰). In the 4-view setting, Amodal3R further improves to FID 26.27, KID 0.22%, P-FID 5.03, and MMD 3.61‰. On Toys4K, Amodal3R reaches FID 23.45 and P-FID 5.00 (single view), and on the Replica scene dataset it achieves P-FID 61.46 and MMD 10.98‰, roughly halving the error of the next-best baseline (TRELLIS: P-FID 128.52, MMD 23.95‰).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Amodal3R is compared against four state-of-the-art image-to-3D generators (TRELLIS, Real3D, GaussianAnything, LaRa), all of which require a separate 2D amodal completion step (pix2gestalt) before 3D reconstruction. Amodal3R wins decisively on every metric in both single- and multi-view settings: on GSO single-view, it reduces FID from 58.82 (TRELLIS) to 30.64 and P-FID from 26.43 to 7.69, while also achieving the highest CLIP score (0.81 vs. 0.76) and COV (39.61% vs. 31.65%). A notable finding is that multi-view two-stage baselines (4 views + pix2gestalt) perform *worse* than their single-view counterparts (e.g., LaRa FID drops from 172.84 to 97.53 with MV, but TRELLIS FID worsens from 58.82 to 65.69), confirming that inconsistent 2D completions across views degrade 3D reconstruction. In contrast, Amodal3R benefits from additional views, improving FID from 30.64 to 26.27. On the out-of-domain Replica scene dataset, Amodal3R achieves P-FID 61.46 and MMD 10.98‰, roughly 2× better than TRELLIS (128.52 / 23.95‰) and 1.5× better than Real3D (79.55 / 18.28‰), demonstrating strong generalization beyond the synthetic training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

Amodal3R directly extends TRELLIS, a structured 3D latent diffusion model, by injecting mask-conditioning mechanisms (mask-weighted cross-attention and an occlusion-aware attention layer) into its transformer blocks. It departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm—2D amodal completion (e.g., pix2gestalt) followed by standard image-to-3D reconstruction—by performing completion and reconstruction jointly in 3D latent space, thereby avoiding multi-view inconsistency issues inherent to independent 2D completions. The mask-weighted attention design draws inspiration from masked attention in 2D inpainting, while the overall formulation connects to 3D shape completion literature, though Amodal3R is end-to-end and recovers both geometry and appearance rather than geometry alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first dedicated benchmark for occlusion-aware 3D reconstruction and demonstrates that a single-stage 3D-space approach substantially outperforms sequential 2D-completion-then-3D-reconstruction pipelines, with particularly large gains in geometric fidelity (P-FID dropping from ~24 to ~5). A notable finding is that multi-view two-stage baselines actually degrade relative to their single-view counterparts due to inconsistent 2D completions, challenging the assumption that more views always help. Limitations include reliance on explicit visibility/occlusion masks (though obtainable via SAM), training exclusively on synthetic data, and focus on single-object reconstruction; open questions include scalability to dense multi-object scenes and whether the approach can operate without explicit mask inputs.
