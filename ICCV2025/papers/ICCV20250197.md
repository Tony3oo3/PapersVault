## Problem

Existing controllable text-to-motion generation methods, predominantly diffusion-based, face a fundamental trade-off: they achieve either high-precision joint control or high-fidelity motion quality, but not both simultaneously. Moreover, these models cannot support both sparse and dense joint control signals in a unified framework, and they lack the ability to adapt to arbitrary objective functions at inference time. Generative masked motion models, which offer superior quality and speed, have remained uncontrollable, leaving a gap in spatio-temporal precision for this architecture class.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language text prompt (encoded via CLIP) and a joint control signal S = [s_1, ..., s_F] where each s_i ∈ R^{j×3} specifies target 3D coordinates for a subset of joints at frame i (uncontrolled joints are zeroed out), supporting both sparse (e.g., 1–5 keyframes) and dense (up to 100% of frames) control densities. At inference, an arbitrary differentiable objective function over joint positions and frames can additionally be supplied for zero-shot control. The output is a full 3D human motion sequence decoded from discrete VQ-VAE motion tokens, with the model generating in as few as ~10 unmasking steps.

## Method

MaskControl builds on a pretrained text-conditioned masked motion model (MoMask-style) and introduces a ControlNet-inspired Logits Regularizer: a trainable replica of the masked transformer connected via zero-initialized linear layers, which receives both the text embedding and the joint control signal (added via a projection layer) to perturb the token classifier's logits toward spatially consistent distributions. Training combines a Logits Consistency Loss (NLL over all positions, masked and unmasked) with a Motion Consistency Loss that directly penalizes the L2 distance between input control signals and the decoded joint positions from the generated motion. At inference, a Logits Optimization step iteratively applies gradient descent on the predicted logits (or directly on codebook embeddings in the final unmask step) to minimize the motion consistency loss, enabling zero-shot adaptation to arbitrary objective functions. To make both the regularizer and optimization differentiable through the discrete token selection, the paper introduces Differentiable Expectation Sampling (DES), which replaces argmax token lookup with a Gumbel-Softmax-reparameterized expectation over codebook entries, allowing gradients to flow from spatial losses back through the categorical distribution.

## Results

On HumanML3D with pelvis-only control, MaskControl achieves FID of 0.061 (77% lower than TLControl's 0.271) and average joint error of 0.98 cm, while maintaining the highest R-Precision Top-3 of 0.809 and zero trajectory/location errors. When trained on all six joints (pelvis, both feet, head, both wrists), MaskControl further reduces FID to 0.083 and average error to 0.72 cm, outperforming TLControl (FID 0.256, error 1.11 cm) and OmniControl (FID 0.310, error 4.04 cm). In zero-shot objective control tasks (e.g., head height constraint, avoiding barriers, walking inside a square), MaskControl achieves zero constraint error and zero unsuccess rate, surpassing ProgMoGen on all metrics including FID (0.246 vs. 0.556 for head height).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaskControl outperforms all nine baselines across the primary evaluation. Against the closest competitor TLControl (test-time optimization with a feedforward transformer), MaskControl reduces FID from 0.271 to 0.061 (pelvis) and 0.256 to 0.083 (all joints) while also achieving lower average error (0.98 vs. 1.08 cm pelvis; 0.72 vs. 1.11 cm all joints). OmniControl, which extends ControlNet-style conditioning to any joint, is surpassed by a wide margin: FID drops from 0.218 to 0.061 and average error from 3.38 to 0.98 cm in the pelvis setting, and from 4.04 to 0.72 cm in the all-joints setting. GMD and MotionLCM, both diffusion-based, show substantially worse control precision (14.39 and 18.97 cm average error respectively) and higher FID. In the body-part editing task (Table 3), MaskControl (FID 0.074) beats MMM (0.103), MotionLCM (0.311), OmniControl (1.213), and MDM (4.827) without any retraining. In zero-shot objective control (Table 4), MaskControl achieves zero constraint violation on all three human-scene interaction tasks, outperforming ProgMoGen (constraint error 0.012–0.097) and uncontrollable baselines MDM and MoMask (constraint error 0.092–0.464), while also yielding the best FID (0.246 vs. 0.556 for ProgMoGen on head height).

## Relation to Prior Work

MaskControl draws a direct architectural analogy to ControlNet for diffusion models, transplanting the 'trainable replica with zero-initialized cross-connections' principle into the generative masked motion model paradigm established by MoMask and MMM. Prior controllable text-to-motion work (GMD, OmniControl, MotionLCM, TLControl) has been dominated by diffusion-based approaches operating in motion or latent space, which the paper argues suffer from quality-precision trade-offs and computational overhead. The key departure is operating entirely in discrete token space: rather than denoising continuous motion, MaskControl manipulates the categorical logits of a VQ-VAE token classifier, using a novel Differentiable Expectation Sampling (DES) mechanism to make the inherently discrete sampling process differentiable for gradient-based control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that masked token-based generative models can simultaneously outperform diffusion-based methods on both generation quality (FID 0.061 vs. 0.271) and control precision (0.91 vs. 1.08 cm average error), challenging the prevailing assumption that diffusion is necessary for high-fidelity controllable motion synthesis. The zero-shot objective control capability—swapping in arbitrary differentiable loss functions at inference without retraining—opens a path toward flexible, task-agnostic motion generation for human-scene and human-object interaction. However, evaluation is confined to HumanML3D with relatively simple geometric constraints (square walking, head height), leaving open whether the approach scales to complex multi-body interactions, longer sequences, or real-time applications. The reliance on a pretrained VQ-VAE tokenizer also means control precision is bounded by the codebook's representational granularity.
