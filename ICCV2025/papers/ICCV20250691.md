## Problem

Discriminative hand mesh recovery methods learn a deterministic 2D-to-3D mapping, producing a single mesh per image and failing to capture the inherent ambiguities caused by self-occlusions, hand-object interactions, and extreme viewpoints. No prior generative hand mesh method provides explicit, per-hypothesis confidence scores that enable principled selection among multiple plausible reconstructions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250691/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image (processed as 16×16 patches by a ViT-H/16 encoder producing multi-scale feature maps) and 2D hand keypoints from OpenPose (refined through a GCN with a fixed hand-skeleton adjacency matrix). It outputs full MANO parameters—48-dim pose θ, 10-dim shape β, and 3-dim camera π—which are fed into the MANO model to yield a 778-vertex, 21-joint 3D hand mesh. In a secondary text-to-mesh mode, the image encoder is replaced by a CLIP text encoder, producing diverse mesh samples from a text prompt.

## Method

MaskHand is a two-stage generative masked model. Stage 1 trains a VQ-MANO tokenizer (a 1D convolutional VQ-VAE) that discretizes the 48-dim MANO pose into 64 discrete tokens via an upsampling mechanism, enabling per-token probability estimation. Stage 2 trains a Context-Guided Masked Transformer that randomly masks a cosine-scheduled subset of tokens and learns their joint conditional distribution p(y_i | Y_masked, X_2D, X_img) using a decoder that combines Graph-based Anatomical Pose Refinement (GCN + graph transformer + SE block fusing 2D pose and 3D tokens) with a Context-Infused Masked Synthesizer (deformable cross-attention over multi-scale image features). Training uses an expectation-approximated differential relaxation—softmax over token logits multiplied by the codebook—to make the discrete sampling differentiable and enable end-to-end optimization with 3D/2D joint losses. At inference, confidence-guided iterative sampling starts from a fully masked sequence and over T decoding steps predicts tokens, retaining high-confidence ones while re-masking low-confidence ones according to a decaying schedule, progressively refining the reconstruction.

## Results

On the FreiHAND benchmark (supervised), MaskHand achieves 5.5 mm PA-MPJPE and 5.4 mm PA-MPVPE, outperforming the previous best (HaMeR at 6.0/5.7 mm) and attaining the highest F@5 (0.801) and F@15 (0.991). In zero-shot evaluation on HO3Dv3, MaskHand reduces PA-MPJPE to 7.0 mm (vs. 8.7 mm for the best prior method AMVUR), a 19.5% improvement. On the HInt occluded-joint benchmark, MaskHand improves PCK@0.05 by 8.1% (NewDays), 21.2% (VISOR), and 27.8% (Ego4D) over HaMeR, demonstrating superior robustness under severe occlusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaskHand outperforms all 25 compared baselines across every evaluated benchmark. On FreiHAND, it beats the strongest discriminative baselines HaMeR (6.0→5.5 mm PA-MPJPE) and Zhou et al. (5.7→5.5 mm), as well as the only other generative method HHMR (5.8→5.5 mm), while also achieving the best vertex precision (F@5: 0.801 vs. 0.785 for HaMeR). In zero-shot HO3Dv3 evaluation, MaskHand surpasses AMVUR (8.7→7.0 mm), HandGCAT (9.3→7.0 mm), and SPMHand (8.8→7.0 mm) by 1.7–2.3 mm in PA-MPJPE. On DexYCB, it reduces MPJPE from 12.4 mm (Zhou et al.) to 11.7 mm and PA-MPJPE from 5.5 mm to 5.0 mm. On the HInt occluded-joint benchmark, MaskHand's advantage over HaMeR is most pronounced under severe occlusion: with 90% of the hand masked, MaskHand achieves PCK@0.05 of 10.4/9.1/8.0 (NewDays/VISOR/Ego4D) versus HaMeR's 8.7/7.1/7.1, confirming that the confidence-guided sampling strategy specifically benefits ambiguous, heavily occluded scenarios where discriminative methods degrade most.

## Relation to Prior Work

MaskHand departs from the dominant discriminative paradigm in monocular hand mesh recovery (HaMeR, MeshGraphormer, METRO), which learns a deterministic image-to-mesh mapping, by instead learning a probabilistic 2D-to-3D distribution via generative masked modeling. Its VQ-MANO tokenizer is built directly on VQ-VAE [60], and the masked transformer decoder draws conceptual inspiration from MaskGIT [8] and BERT-style masked language modeling, repurposing these generative frameworks for structured 3D pose token prediction. The only prior generative hand mesh method, HHMR [37], uses graph diffusion and cannot assign per-hypothesis confidence scores; MaskHand's masked-token formulation makes explicit uncertainty quantification a natural byproduct of the learned token distribution. Architecturally, it reuses the ViT-H/16 multi-scale encoder from HaMeR [48], OpenPose 2D keypoints, and deformable cross-attention from Deformable DETR [75], but wraps them in a novel two-stage (VQ-MANO + Context-Guided Masked Transformer) pipeline with Graph-based Anatomical Pose Refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing per-token confidence scores, MaskHand offers a principled mechanism for uncertainty-aware hand reconstruction that discriminative regressors and diffusion-based methods lack, which is practically important for downstream applications (AR/VR, robotics) where knowing *when* a reconstruction is unreliable matters as much as the reconstruction itself. The zero-shot generalization on HO3Dv3 and the strong occlusion robustness on HInt (up to 27.8% PCK@0.05 improvement over HaMeR) suggest the probabilistic formulation genuinely helps in ambiguous, real-world settings rather than merely fitting training data. Open questions include whether the approach extends beyond the MANO parametric model to more expressive hand representations, whether the 2D pose auxiliary input can be eliminated for fully end-to-end operation, and whether the iterative confidence-guided decoding (which shows diminishing returns beyond 3 iterations) can be replaced by a single-pass formulation without sacrificing the uncertainty signal.
