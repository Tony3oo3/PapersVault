## Problem

Existing federated learning frameworks for LMMs assume a fixed set of tasks, while real-world applications require models to continuously acquire new instruction-following knowledge while retaining previously learned tasks. No comprehensive benchmark or method exists that organically integrates federated learning and continual learning for large multimodal model instruction tuning, and prior federated continual learning approaches are limited to image classification tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250852/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes image-text instruction pairs (image + natural language instruction) as input and produces autoregressive text responses as output. Training operates in a federated setting with 50 clients (5 selected per round), where local data is partitioned via Dirichlet distribution (β ∈ {0.5, 1.0, 5.0}) to model varying degrees of non-IID heterogeneity. Only LoRA parameters (rank 8, embedded in FFN layers) and the multimodal projector are updated, keeping the base LLaVA-1.5-7B frozen to minimize communication overhead.

## Method

DISCO addresses FCIT through two complementary mechanisms. Dynamic Knowledge Organization (DKO) maintains a dynamic cache at the global server that stores task-specific LoRA subspaces {B_i A_i}; each client computes an identity token as the mean CLIP text-encoder feature of its training instructions, and cosine-similarity matching (threshold τ=0.9) routes uploaded parameters to the correct subspace, preventing inter-task conflicts and catastrophic forgetting. Subspace Selective Activation (SSA) redefines LoRA's intrinsic mixing matrix W as a diagonal activation matrix during inference: it computes cosine similarities between each global identity token and the test instruction's CLIP text feature, applies softmax normalization (temperature ε=0.05) to obtain per-subspace activation factors, and scales each subspace's output accordingly—amplifying task-relevant knowledge while suppressing irrelevant outputs without any additional training.

## Results

Under the Hom-FCIT setting (Task-related, β=1.0), DISCO achieves 56.22 Last and 73.03 Avg, surpassing the best baseline (O-LoRA: 49.87 Last, 70.26 Avg) by 6.35% and 2.77% respectively. Under the Het-FCIT setting (Task-related, β=1.0), DISCO reaches 63.25 Last and 61.99 Avg, outperforming the best baseline (O-LoRA: 59.74 Last, 55.20 Avg) by 3.51% and 6.79%. Across all four FCIT settings and three heterogeneity levels, DISCO consistently achieves the highest Last and Avg scores, with average improvements of 4.84% (Last) and 1.43% (Avg) over the best baseline on Hom-FCIT, and 2.17% (Last) and 3.62% (Avg) on Het-FCIT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DISCO outperforms all six baselines across every FCIT setting and heterogeneity level. The closest competitor is O-LoRA [50], which uses orthogonal subspace learning; DISCO leads it by 6.35% on Last (56.22 vs. 49.87) and 2.77% on Avg (73.03 vs. 70.26) in the Hom-FCIT Task-related β=1.0 setting, and by 3.51% on Last (63.25 vs. 59.74) and 6.79% on Avg (61.99 vs. 55.20) in the Het-FCIT Task-related β=1.0 setting. M-LoRA (PILoRA) [12] and MoELoRA (COIN) [6] are the next strongest baselines, but DISCO still leads them by 4–7% on Last in most settings. Classical regularization-based methods (EWC [24], LwF [29]) and prompt-based methods (L2P [51]) trail DISCO by 6–9% on Last, indicating that parameter-space regularization alone is insufficient for the multi-task, multi-stage challenges of LMM instruction tuning. Notably, DISCO's advantage grows with the number of tasks (8-task Task-related vs. 4-task Capability-related) and with data heterogeneity (β=0.5 vs. β=5.0), suggesting the dynamic knowledge organization and subspace selective activation mechanisms become increasingly valuable as the problem complexity rises.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: federated learning for LMMs (e.g., Open-FedLLM, FederatedGPT) and continual instruction tuning for LMMs (e.g., Continual LLaVA, COIN, ModalPrompt), neither of which had previously been combined. The DISCO framework repurposes LoRA's low-rank decomposition as the substrate for task-specific subspaces, drawing on the intrinsic mixing matrix concept from Mixture-of-Subspaces, while the identity token matching mechanism borrows the prototype-matching idea from federated prototype learning (FedProto). The key departure from prior federated continual learning work (MFCL, PILoRA, AFCL) is the shift from image classification to the far more heterogeneous and open-ended setting of multimodal instruction tuning, where tasks differ in format, length, and reasoning style rather than merely in class labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FCIT benchmark is the first to formalize the combined FL+CL challenge for LMMs, providing a structured evaluation protocol (2 scenarios × 2 dataset settings × 3 heterogeneity levels) that the community can adopt for future methods. However, the evaluation is limited to a single LMM architecture (LLaVA-1.5-7B) and 12 datasets, leaving open whether the identity token mechanism and subspace activation generalize to larger models, different projector designs, or longer task sequences. A practical limitation is that the rehearsal-free setting, while realistic, may understate the benefit of small replay buffers that are often available in deployed systems. The framework's reliance on a frozen CLIP text encoder for identity tokens also raises questions about robustness when instruction styles shift substantially across tasks.
