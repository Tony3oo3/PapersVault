## Problem

Existing sketch-based 3D shape generation methods suffer from inherent semantic ambiguity in 2D sketches—missing parts, unclear component counts, and oversimplified geometry—while text-based methods lack precise geometric control. No prior approach seamlessly integrates both modalities to let text semantics compensate for visual gaps in the sketch, leaving a fundamental trade-off between geometric fidelity and semantic completeness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251104/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single hand-drawn sketch (grayscale line drawing from CLIPasso or non-photo-realistic rendering, normalized to a unit cube) and a text description automatically generated by a VLM (LLaVA) that specifies object composition (e.g., number of legs, presence of armrests). It produces a 3D mesh via marching cubes from occupancy fields predicted by the SPAGHETTI decoder, with the intermediate representation being N Gaussian Mixture Models whose latent vectors are aligned to the pre-trained decoder's latent space. The framework also generalizes to real-world RGB photographs as input.

## Method

PASTA employs a dual-branch encoder (DINOv2 for visual features, LLaVA for text embeddings) whose outputs are fused by a 12-layer Text-Visual Transformer Decoder using N learnable queries that alternately attend to visual and text embeddings. The key architectural contribution is ISG-Net, a two-level graph refinement module: IndivGCN learns an N×N adjacency matrix via an MLP predictor (supervised by inverted GMM center distances) to capture fine-grained inter-query relationships, while PartGCN groups queries into K parts via hierarchical clustering, performs average pooling, and applies a K×K part-level graph convolution to enforce global structural coherence. The final queries are fused with a residual connection and passed through an MLP to produce latent vectors aligned to the SPAGHETTI decoder via L1 loss, with additional MSE losses supervising both adjacency matrices. This design enables part-level editing (adding, deleting, or transforming individual GMM components) while maintaining structural consistency across the full shape.

## Results

On AmateurSketch-3D, PASTA achieves CD=0.090, EMD=0.071, and FID=143.9, outperforming the previous best (DY3D: CD=0.109, EMD=0.091) by ~17% in CD and ~22% in EMD. On ProSketch-3D, PASTA further improves to CD=0.055, EMD=0.049, and FID=112.2, setting new state-of-the-art across all three metrics. On the airplane and lamp categories (Table 2), PASTA also surpasses SENS by 22% in CD and 30% in EMD for airplanes, and by 23% in CD and 24% in EMD for lamps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PASTA outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric across both chair benchmarks. Against the closest competitor DY3D [4], PASTA reduces CD from 0.109 to 0.090 (Amateur) and 0.093 to 0.055 (Pro), while also reporting FID (143.9 / 112.2) where DY3D does not. Compared to SENS [5], PASTA improves CD by 26% (0.121→0.090) and EMD by 26% (0.096→0.071) on AmateurSketch-3D, and by 53% in CD (0.116→0.055) on ProSketch-3D. LAS-D [54] and Sketch2Mesh [16] trail substantially, with Sketch2Mesh showing the weakest performance (CD=0.257 on Amateur). On the airplane and lamp categories, PASTA also beats SENS consistently, confirming generalization beyond the chair category.

## Relation to Prior Work

PASTA builds directly on SPAGHETTI's part-level GMM-based implicit shape decoder, inheriting its latent-space formulation and inversion mechanism for training supervision. It departs from prior sketch-based 3D generation methods (SENS, DY3D, LAS-D, Sketch2Mesh) that condition solely on visual sketch input by introducing a text-aligned prior extracted from a VLM (LLaVA) to compensate for semantic ambiguity in hand-drawn sketches. The dual-GCN refinement module (ISG-Net) is a novel architectural contribution that has no direct precedent in the sketch-to-3D literature, though it draws on standard graph convolution and hierarchical clustering techniques. The paper also extends the input domain from grayscale sketches to real-world RGB images via ControlNet, a practical generalization not explored in prior sketch-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By grounding sketch interpretation in VLM semantic knowledge, PASTA addresses a persistent weakness of sketch-based 3D generation—ambiguity and part omission—without requiring multi-view input or user interaction, making it a practical tool for single-sketch design workflows. The part-level GMM representation enables intuitive local editing (add, delete, transform), which is valuable for interactive design applications. However, the method is constrained by SPAGHETTI's fixed number of Gaussians and limited category coverage (chairs, airplanes, lamps), leaving open the question of whether the text-aligned prior and ISG-Net architecture scale to open-vocabulary or high-complexity shapes. A dedicated implicit decoder that removes the fixed-Gaussian constraint would be a natural next step.
