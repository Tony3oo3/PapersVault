## Problem

Creating high-fidelity 3D meshes with arbitrary topology (open surfaces, complex interiors) at high resolutions remains a significant challenge. Existing implicit field methods require costly watertight preprocessing that degrades fine details, while rendering-based supervision with dense implicit fields suffers from prohibitive memory consumption at high resolutions, limiting achievable fidelity. No prior method enables differentiable mesh reconstruction at resolutions up to 1024³ while naturally handling open surfaces and interior structures using only rendering losses.

## Input / Output

The VAE encoder takes point clouds uniformly sampled from mesh surfaces (with corresponding normals) as input, voxelizes them into a sparse structure, and encodes them into a compact latent vector; no watertight mesh or SDF precomputation is required. For image-to-3D generation, a single RGB image is processed by DINOv2 to extract conditioning features. The output is a SparseFlex representation (sparse voxel set with per-voxel interpolation weights and per-corner SDF values/deformations) that is extracted into a triangle mesh via Dual Marching Cubes at resolutions up to 1024³, preserving open boundaries and interior geometry.

## Method

SparseFlex replaces the dense voxel grid of Flexicubes with a sparse set of surface-adjacent voxels, drastically reducing memory while enabling natural open-surface representation by simply omitting empty-region voxels. The key training innovation is frustum-aware sectional voxel training: at each iteration, only voxels whose centers fall within the camera's viewing frustum (controlled by an adaptive visibility ratio α) are activated for isosurface extraction and rendering, reducing memory by an order of magnitude and enabling 1024³ training. The VAE uses a PointNet-based local feature aggregator feeding a sparse shifted-window transformer encoder, a decoder with two self-pruning convolutional upsampling modules (4× resolution each, pruning voxels not containing input points), and a final linear layer predicting SparseFlex parameters. Training is end-to-end with rendering losses (depth, normal, mask L1 + SSIM + LPIPS), a BCE structure loss for voxel occupancy, KL divergence, and Flexicubes regularization. For generation, a two-stage rectified flow pipeline (structure flow at 1/4 resolution, then structured latent flow) is trained on the VAE latent space with DINOv2 image conditioning via cross-attention.

## Results

On Toys4k, SparseFlex at 1024³ resolution achieves a Chamfer Distance of 1.33 (full set) / 0.60 (watertight subset) and F-score of 25.95 / 35.69 at threshold 0.001, representing a ~82% reduction in CD and ~88% increase in F-score over the best prior method (XCube: CD 4.35, F1 1.61). On the Dora Benchmark, Ours_1024 reaches CD 0.86 / 0.12 and F1(0.001) 25.71 / 39.50. For image-to-3D generation on Toys4k, SparseFlex achieves FID 44.95 and KID 1.05×10⁻³, outperforming TRELLIS (FID 47.66, KID 1.28×10⁻³), Direct3D (FID 50.84), and InstantMesh (FID 68.74).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SparseFlex decisively outperforms all baselines on both reconstruction and generation. In VAE reconstruction (Table 1), Ours₁₀₂₄ reduces CD by ~70% over the closest competitor XCube (1.33 vs 4.35 on Toys4k) and by ~93% over TRELLIS (1.33 vs 12.90), while boosting F1(0.001) from 1.61 (XCube) and 4.05 (TRELLIS) to 25.95. On the Dora Benchmark, the gap is even wider: Ours₁₀₂₄ achieves CD 0.86 vs XCube's 4.74 and TRELLIS's 17.42. For open-surface reconstruction on Deepfashion3D (Table 2), SparseFlex at 1024³ reaches CD 0.04 and F1(0.001) 37.22, surpassing 3PSDF (CD 0.26, F1 8.14) and Surf-D (CD 63.79). In image-to-3D generation (Table 4), SparseFlex achieves the best FID (44.95) and KID (1.05×10⁻³), beating TRELLIS (47.66 / 1.28), Direct3D (50.84 / 2.04), and InstantMesh (68.74 / 9.68) by a clear margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

SparseFlex is a direct sparsification of Flexicubes [61], replacing its dense N³ voxel grid with a sparse set of surface-adjacent voxels while preserving the differentiable Dual Marching Cubes extraction. The VAE architecture and rectified-flow generation pipeline follow TRELLIS [79], but diverge by using point clouds (rather than multiview images) as VAE input and adding a self-pruning upsampling decoder to maintain sparsity at higher resolutions. The frustum-aware sectional voxel training strategy is the paper's most original contribution, borrowing visibility-culling ideas from real-time rendering [1] to activate only frustum-relevant voxels during differentiable rendering, a technique not previously applied to implicit-field mesh training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the watertight-conversion preprocessing step and enabling 1024³ training with rendering-only supervision, SparseFlex addresses two long-standing practical bottlenecks in 3D shape modeling: detail loss from SDF/occupancy conversion and prohibitive memory at high resolution. The ability to reconstruct mesh interiors purely from rendering supervision (by placing cameras inside the object) opens a path toward modeling hollow or partially-enclosed structures that watertight-based methods fundamentally cannot capture. Remaining limitations include minor boundary artifacts on open surfaces at lower resolutions, continued computational cost at 1024³, and the lack of explicit control over interior geometry during generation.
