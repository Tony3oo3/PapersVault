## Problem

Existing single-view 3D reconstruction methods are confined to the input field-of-view and cannot reconstruct occluded regions behind visible surfaces. In particular, 3DGS-based feed-forward methods like Splatter Image predict only one Gaussian per pixel, causing Gaussians to cluster near visible surfaces and leaving holes or ripple artifacts in novel views with significant viewpoint differences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251613/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a 3D Gaussian scene representation consisting of leader Gaussians (one per pixel, initialized by a pre-trained UniDepth monocular depth estimator and a UNet encoder-decoder) plus M=3 follower Gaussians per leader, each parameterized by position, opacity, scale, rotation quaternion, and spherical harmonics coefficients. The scene is rendered via differentiable rasterization to synthesize novel-view images and depth maps. Training uses posed image sequences where one frame serves as input and the remaining frames provide photometric supervision.

## Method

Hi-Gaussian introduces two key mechanisms on top of a feed-forward 3DGS pipeline. First, a Normalized Spherical Projection module maps each pixel's camera-space coordinates to normalized latitude-longitude spherical coordinates after the UNet encoder-decoder, then assigns a 0.25× broader angular range to expand the effective FOV, allowing Gaussians to cover areas beyond the input image with less distortion than planar projection. Second, a Hierarchical Gaussian Sampling strategy uses a variational approach: leader Gaussians are positioned via pre-trained depth and attribute prediction, then follower Gaussians are sampled from a multivariate Gaussian whose mean is the leader's attribute vector and whose covariance is predicted by a shallow MLP, enabling followers to adaptively spread into occluded regions. The model is trained end-to-end in a self-supervised manner with a combined loss of source-view photometric error, reprojection error, LPIPS perceptual loss, and input-view RGB loss.

## Results

On SemanticKITTI (outdoor driving scenes), Hi-Gaussian achieves 16.78 PSNR, 0.518 SSIM, and 0.358 LPIPS, outperforming all five baselines on every metric. On BundleFusion (indoor scenes), it reaches 25.50 PSNR, 0.877 SSIM, and 0.179 LPIPS, again leading all competitors. The largest gains over the closest NeRF-based competitor (SceneRF) are +0.32 PSNR and +0.025 SSIM on SemanticKITTI, and +0.43 PSNR and +0.024 SSIM on BundleFusion. Cross-dataset generalization from BundleFusion to NeRF-LLFF further confirms the method's robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hi-Gaussian outperforms all five baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against the strongest NeRF-based method SceneRF, it gains +0.32 PSNR on SemanticKITTI (16.78 vs 16.46) and +0.43 PSNR on BundleFusion (25.50 vs 25.07), while also improving SSIM by ~0.025 and reducing LPIPS by ~0.12 on BundleFusion. Against the 3DGS-based Splatter Image, the advantage is even more pronounced on BundleFusion LPIPS (0.179 vs 0.205), reflecting better handling of occluded regions. On SemanticKITTI, Hi-Gaussian leads Splatter Image by +0.95 PSNR and +0.061 SSIM, with the gap attributable to the hierarchical Gaussian sampling reconstructing occluded areas that per-pixel Gaussians cannot capture. The NeRF-based methods (PixelNeRF, MINE, VisionNeRF) trail by 0.3–1.0 PSNR on SemanticKITTI and 4–5 PSNR on BundleFusion, consistent with their known limitations in point sampling and volume rendering speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

Hi-Gaussian extends the feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting paradigm established by Splatter Image, which predicts one Gaussian per pixel from a single image, by addressing two specific limitations: the inability to reconstruct beyond the input field-of-view and the failure to model occluded regions. The Normalized Spherical Projection module builds on the spherical-coordinate idea from SceneRF but introduces normalization to reduce spatial compression, while the Hierarchical Gaussian Sampling strategy introduces a variational latent-sampling mechanism (leader Gaussians parameterize a Gaussian distribution from which follower Gaussians are drawn) that has no direct precedent in prior 3DGS-based single-view methods. The use of a pre-trained monocular depth estimator (UniDepth) for 3D position initialization follows the approach of Flash3D and Szymanowicz et al., but is combined here with the hierarchical structure to separate visible-surface fidelity from occluded-region hallucination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By combining FOV expansion with occluded-region reconstruction in a single feed-forward pass, Hi-Gaussian addresses two practical failure modes that limit single-view 3D reconstruction in real-world applications like autonomous driving and AR, where the camera sees only a narrow slice of the scene. The hierarchical leader-follower formulation offers a principled way to decouple what is directly observed from what must be inferred, potentially generalizable to deeper hierarchies or other scene representations. However, the 0.25× FOV expansion is modest, the method still requires posed multi-view sequences for training (not truly single-image inference at test time in the generative sense), and the approach has not been validated on dynamic scenes or very large-scale environments beyond the two benchmark datasets.
