## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary 3D instance segmentation (OV-3DIS) methods employ individual concepts—agglomerative clustering, progressive region growing, graph clustering—that are complementary rather than mutually exclusive, yet no prior work systematically combines and refines them. Key failure modes remain unaddressed: 2D foundation model predictions that span multiple objects, fragile 3D proposal aggregation to wrong 2D detections, CLIP classification contaminated by co-visible objects, and persistent false positives from overlapped or partial 3D proposals.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of RGB images (downsampled by a factor of 5 for ScanNet200), a 3D point cloud represented as superpoints, and free-form open-vocabulary text queries (e.g., functional descriptions like 'drink water' or attribute queries like 'red chair'). It outputs a list of K binary 3D instance masks m ∈ {0,1}^{K×N} over N points, each assigned an open-vocabulary class label. Proposals are generated from both image-based and point-cloud-based pipelines, concatenated, and deduplicated via NMS (IOU threshold 0.95) before classification.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages. For proposal generation, 2D instances are grounded per-frame with Grounded SAM, then sorted by size with overlapping regions removed from larger masks to prevent multi-object proposals. These 2D masks are lifted to 3D superpoints using camera matrices with frame-wise and instance-wise visibility filtering, then aggregated across frames via a tracking scheme that matches new observations to existing tracklets using frame-wise superpoint-level IOU (sIOU) restricted to co-visible superpoints—more robust than tracklet-wise matching because wrong 2D predictions do not pollute the aggregated mask. An iterative merging/removal step then fuses partial proposals (IOU > τ_merge) with multi-view consensus refinement after each merge, and removes proposals contained within larger ones (inclusion rate > τ_incl). For classification, Alpha-CLIP replaces standard CLIP by incorporating SAM-generated object masks as an alpha channel to obtain object-centric visual features, and a Standardized Maximum Similarity (SMS) score—z-normalizing each proposal's max similarity across queries using scene-level statistics—filters out low-confidence predictions to boost precision.

## Results

On ScanNet200 (Top-K, 2D+3D), the method achieves mAP 32.7, mAP50 41.4, mAP25 45.3, and mAPtail 33.1, outperforming the previous SoTA Open3DIS by 9.0% in mAP. On S3DIS (2D+3D), it reaches mAP 31.3 and mAR 48.2, surpassing OpenYOLO3D by 3.2% mAP and 10.4% mAR. On Replica (Top-K, 2D+3D), it achieves mAP 25.7 and mAR 48.8, the highest mAR across all settings. The 2D-only variant alone outperforms all prior image-based methods by 8.8% mAP over SAI3D on ScanNet200 (Top-1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 13 baselines across every dataset and evaluation setting. On ScanNet200 (Top-K, 2D+3D), it beats the previous SoTA Open3DIS by 9.0% mAP (32.7 vs. 23.7) and 11.3% mAPtail (33.1 vs. 21.8), and surpasses OpenYOLO3D (3D-only) by 8.0% mAP. In the 2D-only setting, it exceeds SAI3D by 8.8% mAP (25.4 vs. 12.7 Top-1) and OVIR-3D by 16.1% mAP, demonstrating that the tracking-based aggregation and iterative merging/removal are critical. On S3DIS, the 2D+3D method improves mAR by 10.4% over OpenYOLO3D (48.2 vs. 37.8) and 4.1% over Open3DIS (48.2 vs. 44.1). On Replica, the 2D-only variant achieves mAP50 of 32.6, exceeding the 3D-only OpenYOLO3D (28.6), highlighting strong generalization of the image-based pipeline. The method also surpasses fully-supervised closed-vocabulary baselines (Mask3D: 26.9 mAP, ISBNet: 24.5 mAP) in the open-vocabulary Top-1 2D+3D setting (25.8 mAP), and its class-agnostic AR of 74.0 exceeds Mask3D's fully-supervised AR of 65.3.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a careful recipe that combines and refines previously independent concepts for OV-3DIS: tracking-based aggregation (from OVIR-3D), visibility-ratio filtering (from Open3DIS), multi-view consensus refinement (from MaskClustering), and progressive region growing (from SAI3D). Rather than proposing a new architectural paradigm, the authors argue these concepts are complementary and design specific refinements—frame-wise sIOU matching instead of tracklet-wise, iterative merging/removal after aggregation, Alpha-CLIP for object-centric classification, and SMS-based filtering—to address failure modes each component leaves unaddressed. The two-stage proposal-then-classify scheme and the use of Grounded SAM for 2D grounding follow the established OV-3DIS pipeline, while the point cloud-based proposals reuse Mask3D/ISBNet as off-the-shelf backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a well-engineered combination of existing components can surpass both prior open-vocabulary methods and end-to-end closed-vocabulary baselines, suggesting that careful pipeline design matters as much as individual model innovations for OV-3DIS. However, the method is computationally expensive due to heavy 2D foundation models (Grounded SAM, Alpha-CLIP), and the authors acknowledge it does not improve performance on small objects, limiting its applicability to fine-grained scenes. Open questions include whether the iterative merging/removal strategy can be made more efficient, whether the approach generalizes to outdoor or large-scale 3D scenes, and whether the domain gap between real and synthetic data (observed on Replica) can be better addressed.
