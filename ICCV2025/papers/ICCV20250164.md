## Problem

Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) has been widely regarded as inferior to adapter-based PEFT methods in vision, but the root cause has not been clearly identified. This paper shows that VPT's prompt deployment—concatenating prompt tokens with embedded tokens in self-attention—both inflates computational cost quadratically with prompt count and dilutes the self-attention weights among embedded tokens, fundamentally limiting how many prompts can be used effectively.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250164/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input, processed by a ViT backbone (ViT-B/16 for classification, ViT-L for segmentation) pre-trained on ImageNet-21K. For image classification (VTAB-1K, FGVC), it outputs a class label; for semantic segmentation (ADE20K via SETR), it outputs a per-pixel mask. The number of learnable prompt vectors (dimension d, count 1–200) is the primary hyperparameter, and only these prompts plus the final classification head are trained while the backbone remains frozen.

## Method

CVPT decouples prompts from self-attention by replacing VPT's token concatenation with a cross-attention module: embedded tokens act as queries and prompt tokens act as key-value pairs, and the cross-attention output is added as a residual to the embedded tokens after the self-attention block. This preserves the original self-attention structure intact while introducing only linear (not quadratic) computational overhead in the number of prompts. A weight-sharing mechanism initializes the cross-attention projection matrices from the frozen self-attention weights and keeps them frozen, so the only learnable parameters are the prompt vectors themselves (≈0.09 M for 10 prompts). The cross-attention is inserted after self-attention within each transformer block, and the same design is applied at every layer (VPT-Deep style), enabling the use of up to 200 prompts with minimal additional memory or FLOPs.

## Results

On the VTAB-1K benchmark (19 datasets, ViT-B/16 pre-trained on ImageNet-21K), CVPT achieves 77.2% average top-1 accuracy with only 0.39M trainable parameters, outperforming the best adapter-based method DM-LoRA (77.0%) and the best prior prompt-based method SA2VP (75.8%). On the FGVC fine-grained benchmarks, CVPT reaches 90.5% average accuracy, trailing DM-LoRA by just 0.2%. On ADE20K semantic segmentation (ViT-L/SETR), CVPT with 200 prompts achieves 45.66 mIoU-SS and 47.92 mIoU-Ms, surpassing SPT-LoRA (45.40/47.50) and RepAdapter (44.44/46.71).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CVPT (77.2%) outperforms all 16 baselines on VTAB-1K, beating the strongest adapter-based method DM-LoRA (77.0%) by 0.2% and the best prior prompt-based method SA2VP (75.8%) by 1.4%. Among prompt-based methods, the gap over VPT-Deep (72.0%) is 5.2%, and over E2VPT (73.9%) is 3.3%. On FGVC, CVPT (90.5%) edges out RLRR (90.4%) and SPT-LoRA (90.1%) but trails DM-LoRA (90.7%) by 0.2%, while still surpassing all prompt-based baselines (best prior: SA2VP at 90.1%). On ADE20K, CVPT with 200 prompts (45.66 mIoU-SS) exceeds SPT-LoRA (45.40) and RepAdapter (44.44), and improves over VPT (42.11) by 3.55 points. Notably, CVPT uses fewer parameters than VFPT (0.48M) and SA2VP (0.41M) while achieving higher accuracy, and its efficiency advantage over VPT grows substantially as the number of prompts increases (e.g., at 200 prompts, VPT degrades to 64.0% while CVPT reaches 74.8%).

## Relation to Prior Work

CVPT is a direct architectural modification of Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT, Jia et al., ECCV 2022), which introduced learnable prompt tokens concatenated with embedded tokens in self-attention for visual fine-tuning. The paper's key departure is identifying that this concatenation strategy causes quadratic computational overhead and distorts the softmax-normalized self-attention among embedded tokens, and replacing it with a cross-attention module that decouples prompts from the self-attention computation. A further departure from prior prompt-based variants (E2VPT, EXPRESS, VFPT, SA2VP) is the weight-sharing mechanism, which initializes and freezes cross-attention weights from the pretrained self-attention layer, avoiding the large parameter overhead that would otherwise make cross-attention as costly as full-tuning. The paper positions CVPT against both the prompt-based lineage and adapter-based methods (Adapter, LoRA, DM-LoRA, RepAdapter), arguing that the perceived superiority of adapters over prompts in vision is an artifact of VPT's deployment rather than an inherent limitation of the prompt paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a widely held assumption in the PEFT community—that prompt-based methods are fundamentally inferior to adapter-based methods for visual fine-tuning—by showing the gap is attributable to a specific architectural choice (prompt concatenation in self-attention) rather than to the prompt paradigm itself. The weight-sharing result is practically significant: a frozen cross-attention module with only 0.09M learnable parameters achieves near-parity with a fully learnable 28.4M-parameter version, offering a low-cost path to effective prompt-based fine-tuning. However, the performance margins over the strongest adapter baselines (e.g., 77.2 vs. 77.0 on VTAB-1K) remain modest, and the authors acknowledge they did not develop new prompt initialization strategies, leaving open whether the approach generalizes beyond ViT architectures or to other modalities such as video or multimodal settings.
