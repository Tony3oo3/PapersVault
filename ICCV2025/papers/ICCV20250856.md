## Problem

Existing perceptual similarity metrics operate at pixel/patch level (LPIPS, MSE) or compress image features into low-dimensional vectors (CLIP, DINO), failing to capture mid-level similarities in layout, pose, and semantic content while also losing fine-grained appearance details. This gap is particularly acute for evaluating customized generation outputs, where subtle appearance and style consistency matters. The paper is the first to demonstrate that pretrained diffusion models can serve as effective visual similarity evaluators without any additional fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method takes two RGB images at 512×512 resolution (configurable from 384 to 1024) and produces a scalar similarity score. Before feature extraction, Gaussian noise is added to each image to simulate the forward diffusion process, since the U-Net was trained on noisy inputs. The score is computed as the sum of two directional Aligned Attention Scores (AAS(L_A, L_B) + AAS(L_B, L_A)), and the specific layer index and denoising timestep can be tuned to target style, instance, or low-level similarity. For video consistency, the method measures variance of similarity scores between a reference frame and subsequent frames.

## Method

DiffSim repurposes the self-attention (DiffSim-S) or cross-attention (DiffSim-C) layers of a pretrained Stable Diffusion 1.5 U-Net as feature extractors, motivated by the observation that these layers are precisely where appearance-consistency methods (ReferenceNet, Custom Diffusion, IP-Adapter) inject reference features. The key technical contribution is the Aligned Attention Score (AAS), which computes cosine similarity between attn(Q_A, K_A, V_A) and attn(Q_A, K_B, V_B) (and vice versa), using the attention mechanism to implicitly align spatially misaligned features rather than assuming pixel correspondence. For DiffSim-C, IP-Adapter Plus is used to convert one image into 16 IP tokens that serve as keys/values in the cross-attention layer. The approach requires no fine-tuning or supervision, and the same AAS formulation generalizes to CLIP and DINO v2 Vision Transformer architectures, yielding CLIP AAS and DINO v2 AAS metrics. Shallow layers with high timesteps capture style/low-level similarity, while deeper layers with lower timesteps capture semantic/instance similarity.

## Results

DiffSim achieves the best single-model accuracy on 4 out of 7 benchmarks: NIGHTS (86.52%), IP (91.84%), Sref (97.40%), and InstantStyle (99.05%). On the remaining benchmarks, it is competitive: Dreambench++ (71.50% vs. DINO v2's 72.25%), CUTE (76.17% vs. DINO v2's 77.27%), and TID2013 (94.17% vs. LPIPS's 94.50%). The proposed ensemble of CLIP + DINO v2 + DiffSim (hard vote) reaches 72.15% on Dreambench++ and 77.78% on CUTE, surpassing the fine-tuned DreamSim ensemble (70.01% and 76.44% respectively) without any human-preference training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffSim outperforms CLIP on all seven benchmarks, with the largest margins on Sref (+7.07 pp) and InstantStyle (+16.15 pp), and a narrow lead on IP (+0.14 pp). Against DINO v2, DiffSim wins on NIGHTS (+1.28 pp), IP (+6.49 pp), Sref (+10.20 pp), and InstantStyle (+12.95 pp), but trails slightly on Dreambench++ (−0.75 pp) and CUTE (−1.10 pp). LPIPS remains the strongest low-level metric on TID2013 (94.50% vs. 94.17%), while FFA is consistently the weakest baseline across all tasks. The Gram metric is competitive only on style benchmarks (84.05% Sref, 88.30% InstantStyle) but is far below DiffSim. The proposed CLIP+DINO v2+DiffSim ensemble matches or exceeds the fine-tuned DreamSim ensemble on Dreambench++ (72.15% vs. 70.01%) and CUTE (77.78% vs. 76.44%) without any human-preference supervision, while DreamSim retains an edge on NIGHTS (90.91% vs. 89.43%) and IP (94.92% vs. 94.92%, tied).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the denoising U-Net of pre-trained Stable Diffusion (Rombach et al., 2022) as a feature extractor for visual similarity, drawing on the observation that self-attention layers in diffusion models encode appearance-consistent representations (as exploited by ReferenceNet, Animate Anyone, and related generation methods). The Aligned Attention Score (AAS) mechanism is the key conceptual departure from prior similarity metrics: rather than compressing features into a single vector (as CLIP and DINO v2 do) or comparing pixel-aligned feature maps (as LPIPS does), AAS uses the attention operation itself to implicitly align two images' dense spatial features before computing cosine similarity. The approach is further extended to CLIP and DINO v2 architectures, and the cross-attention variant (DiffSim-C) leverages IP-Adapter Plus (Hu et al., 2023) to inject image tokens into the U-Net's cross-attention layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiffSim offers a training-free, supervision-free similarity metric that outperforms CLIP and DINO v2 across human-aligned, style, and instance-level benchmarks without any fine-tuning, making it a practical drop-in tool for evaluating generative model outputs. The introduction of the Sref and IP benchmarks addresses a gap in style- and instance-level similarity evaluation that prior work (NIGHTS, CUTE, TID2013) did not cover. However, the method requires grid search over layers and timesteps per task, and the finding that the larger SD-XL model performs worse than SD1.5 raises open questions about scalability. The acknowledged limitation of overemphasizing background features over subject details remains a practical concern for small-object scenarios.
