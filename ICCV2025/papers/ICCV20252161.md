## Problem

Conventional test-time training (TTT) methods rely on a single auxiliary self-supervised task to adapt models to domain shifts, but when applied to multi-task architectures, this leads to an unsynchronization problem: the adaptation steps that optimize one task's performance misalign with those needed for other tasks. No prior TTT method explicitly addresses this cross-task misalignment, resulting in suboptimal and inconsistent adaptation across multiple dense prediction tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252161/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes unlabeled RGB images from the target domain during test-time and produces multiple dense prediction outputs simultaneously: semantic segmentation masks, depth maps, surface normal maps, and edge maps. A shared ResNet50 encoder extracts a latent representation, which is projected into task-specific latent vectors via lightweight convolutional layers. During adaptation, the model consumes only target-domain images (no ground truth), and the TBS branch operates on masked versions of the task-specific latents to generate pseudo-labels that guide the main decoders.

## Method

S4T introduces a Task Behavior Synchronizer (TBS), a lightweight ViT that predicts task labels from masked task-specific latent vectors, thereby learning inter-task relations in the source domain. Task-specific projection layers extract distinct latent vectors z_i from the shared encoder output, and a masking strategy (identical spatial patches masked across all tasks) forces the TBS to rely on cross-task information rather than trivially reading from a single task's features. During source-domain training, the TBS and projections are supervised with ground truth; during test-time adaptation, the only loss is a pseudo-label prediction loss that aligns TBS outputs with the main decoder outputs, propagating gradients to synchronize all task decoders simultaneously. A theoretical bound (Proposition 1) shows that minimizing the masked-to-unmasked prediction gap reduces the target-domain task loss under the assumption that inter-task relations are preserved across domain shifts.

## Results

On the Taskonomy→NYUD-v2 domain shift, S4T achieves 59.37 mIoU on semantic segmentation, 1.052 RMSE on depth, 45.33 mErr on normals, and 0.1441 RMSE on edges, yielding an overall ΔTTT improvement of +34.94% over the unadapted baseline. On Taskonomy→PASCAL-Context, S4T reaches 45.42 mIoU, 41.41 mErr, and 0.1183 RMSE with a ΔTTT of +34.20%. S4T outperforms the strongest baseline (TTTFlow at +28.47% on NYUD-v2 and TIPI at +32.41% on PASCAL-Context) by 6.5 and 1.8 percentage points in ΔTTT, respectively, while also leading on every individual task metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S4T outperforms all eight baselines on both domain shifts across every individual task metric and the aggregate ΔTTT score. On Taskonomy→NYUD-v2, S4T leads the closest competitor TTTFlow by 6.6 mIoU on semantic segmentation (59.37 vs. 52.75) and by 6.5 points in ΔTTT (+34.94% vs. +28.47%); TIPI achieves the best depth RMSE (1.029) but lags S4T on all other tasks. On Taskonomy→PASCAL-Context, S4T surpasses TIPI (the strongest baseline at +32.41% ΔTTT) by 2.4 mIoU on segmentation (45.42 vs. 43.01) and 2.4 points in ΔTTT. Class-cluster-based methods (ClusT3, ActMAD, NC-TTT) show limited gains on regression tasks such as depth and normals, with ActMAD even degrading performance (−1.63% ΔTTT on PASCAL-Context), confirming that feature-level clustering strategies struggle to generalize to dense prediction. The paper further shows that S4T maintains stable or improving performance over extended adaptation iterations, whereas TENT, TTT++, and others degrade after a certain number of steps, highlighting S4T's robustness to the unsynchronization problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time training (TTT) paradigm, which traditionally relies on a single self-supervised auxiliary task (e.g., MAE-style image reconstruction) to bridge domain gaps, to the multi-task setting. It repurposes the MAE masking mechanism not for pixel reconstruction but for predicting task labels from masked task-specific latent vectors, thereby encoding inter-task relations as the adaptation signal. The approach also draws on multi-task learning literature on task relations (e.g., Taskonomy, cross-task consistency) but departs from those works by using task relations specifically for cross-domain adaptation rather than intra-domain performance improvement, and by isolating the relation-capturing module (TBS) from the main prediction decoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the 'unsynchronization problem'—where different tasks require different numbers of adaptation steps under domain shift—provides a concrete diagnostic for why naive multi-task TTT underperforms, and the proposed synchronization metrics (step variance, DTW, cosine similarity) give practitioners tools to measure this. The theoretical bound (Proposition 1) grounds the approach, but its reliance on Assumption 1 (task relations preserved across domains) limits applicability to extreme domain shifts where inter-task structure may break down. The 24.1% parameter overhead from the TBS ViT branch and the requirement for task-specific projection layers are practical costs. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to tasks with heterogeneous modalities (e.g., language + vision) or to very large task suites where the joint prediction in TBS becomes computationally prohibitive.
