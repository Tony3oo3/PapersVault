## Problem

Existing human action recognition datasets and methods assume fixed camera positions, short capture ranges, and single modalities, making them inadequate for Natural Human-Robot Interaction (N-HRI) where robots perceive humans at varying distances (3–50 m) while themselves in motion. No large-scale benchmark or method specifically addresses the combined challenges of long-range subtle action perception, kinematic interference from robot platform motion, and multimodal (RGB + LiDAR point cloud) sensing in dynamic interaction scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252293/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the point cloud modality, input is a LiDAR video sequence (HESAI AT128P, 10 Hz, 120° HFOV) segmented into fixed-length clips with 768 points per frame after human detection via TransFusion; for RGB, input is 1280×720 video at 30 fps, split into 8 or 12 segments with one frame per segment resized to 224×224. The output is a 30-class composite action label (e.g., 'Walking forward + Waving') plus attribute labels (gender, clothing). A key distinction from standard action recognition is that the input inherently contains kinematic disturbances from robot self-motion and changing human-robot geometry, which the model must explicitly decouple from the human action signal.

## Method

ACTIVE-PC processes point cloud video through three coupled modules. Multilevel Neighborhood Sampling (MNS) builds hierarchical point sets by constraining each layer's candidates to the local neighborhoods of the previous layer's anchors, yielding three density levels (N/4, N/16, N/32) that preserve both global structure and fine local detail. A PointTube backbone with Elastic Ellipse Query (EEQ) extracts spatiotemporal features using a learnable anisotropic distance metric d(p_n, p_q) = √(α(x_n−x_q)² + β(y_n−y_q)² + γ(z_n−z_q)²), where axis-specific scaling (α=3.56, β=3.68, γ=2.80 on ACTIVE) compresses horizontal kinematic displacements while preserving vertical posture changes. Layered Recognizers then route sparse-layer features (F₂, F₃) through a Kinematic Interpreter (Transformer + Global Average Pooling + MLP) to model platform motion, and dense-layer features (F₁) through a Human Action Recognizer (Transformer + Global Max Pooling + MLP) to capture subtle actions; the two score vectors are averaged to produce the final action prediction.

## Results

On the ACTIVE dataset (point cloud, 12×768 setting), ACTIVE-PC achieves 60.10% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the strongest baseline PST-Transformer (46.76%) by 13.34 percentage points. On NTU RGB+D, ACTIVE-PC reaches 91.7% (cross-subject) and 96.8% (cross-view), outperforming PST-Transformer (91.0% / 96.4%). For the RGB modality on ACTIVE (12×224), ACTIVE-RGB attains 57.56% accuracy, exceeding InternVideo2 (54.88%) by 2.68%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACTIVE-PC outperforms all 14 baselines across every evaluation setting. On the primary ACTIVE benchmark (point cloud, 12×768), it leads 3DinAction (49.19%) by 10.91 points and PST-Transformer (46.76%) by 13.34 points, with the gap widening at lower resolutions (e.g., 58.71% vs. 41.61% for PST-Transformer at 8×512). On NTU RGB+D, ACTIVE-PC edges out PST-Transformer by 0.7% (CS) and 0.4% (CV), confirming generalizability beyond the N-HRI setting. In the RGB modality, ACTIVE-RGB surpasses InternVideo2 (54.88%) by 2.68% and UniFormerV2 (50.74%) by 6.82% on ACTIVE 12×224. The consistent margin over both CNN-based (PSTNet, PSTNet++) and Transformer-based (P4Transformer, PST-Transformer) point cloud methods highlights the benefit of the proposed MNS + Layered Recognizers + EEQ pipeline for handling kinematic disturbances at long range.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first large-scale dataset and benchmark specifically for action recognition from perception-centric robotic views in N-HRI scenarios, a setting that existing datasets (NTU RGB+D, MSRAction3D, UAVHuman) do not address due to their fixed capture distances, static platforms, and single-modality constraints. Methodologically, ACTIVE-PC builds on the point cloud video transformer lineage (PST-Transformer, P4Transformer) but repurposes their neighborhood-query and feature-extraction machinery through three targeted modifications: Multilevel Neighborhood Sampling replaces global FPS with hierarchical local-neighborhood constraints, Elastic Ellipse Query replaces isotropic Ball Query with learnable anisotropic scaling, and Layered Recognizers decouple dense fine-grained features from sparse global features into separate sub-networks. The RGB branch (ACTIVE-RGB) extends ViT-based video understanding (InternVideo2, UniFormer) with the same Layered Recognizers architecture, positioning the work as a cross-modality framework rather than a single-architecture contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ACTIVE dataset fills a genuine gap by providing realistic N-HRI conditions (3–50 m dynamic distances, moving camera platform, day/night, indoor/outdoor, composite actions) that no prior benchmark captures, making it a practical reference for mobile service robot perception. However, the method's 13.34% gain over PST-Transformer is demonstrated primarily on the authors' own dataset, and generalization to other N-HRI settings or larger action taxonomies remains untested; the NTU RGB+D result (91.7% CS) confirms compatibility with standard benchmarks but does not validate the N-HRI-specific components. Open questions include whether the Elastic Ellipse Query's learned anisotropic scaling transfers across sensor configurations, how the framework scales beyond 30 composite action categories, and whether the TransFusion-based human detection front-end introduces systematic biases at extreme ranges. Pose estimation annotations are deferred, limiting the dataset's immediate utility for fine-grained kinematic analysis.
