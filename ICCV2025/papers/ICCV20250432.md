## Problem

Existing point cloud pre-training methods based on masked reconstruction (e.g., Point-MAE, PointBERT) may overlook contextual relationships between masked and unmasked regions, limiting the model's ability to capture global geometric structure. This paper addresses this gap by leveraging the progressive denoising process of diffusion models, which naturally models long-range dependencies, to guide backbone pre-training for point clouds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250432/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud of 1,024 points, groups them into 64 patches of 32 points via KNN, and encodes them through a transformer backbone (384-dim embeddings, 6 heads). During pre-training, the output is a reconstructed clean point cloud generated stepwise from a fully noisy version; during fine-tuning, the frozen backbone features feed task-specific heads that produce class labels (classification) or per-point semantic labels (segmentation). The conditional vector c (768-dim) extracted by CGNet serves as the bridge between feature extraction and the diffusion denoising process.

## Method

PreDifPoint comprises three modules: CGNet extracts and fuses local/global features via a novel DXCA-Attention mechanism (bi-directional covariate attention computing softmax(QK^T/√C) in both local-to-global and global-to-local directions) to produce a density-aware conditional vector; CDNet performs layer-wise denoising using a dynamic gating mechanism that modulates features with the fused condition and sinusoidal time embedding, connected by residual links; CDPNet orchestrates the full diffusion pipeline with point-to-point mapping between adjacent time steps. A density-adaptive sampling strategy divides the time step range into sub-intervals and weights sampling probability by local k-NN point density, allocating more training signal to high-density regions. The density-weighted loss combines standard MSE noise prediction with interval-level density weighting, and the entire framework is pre-trained on ShapeNet (41,952 shapes, 300 epochs) before fine-tuning only task-specific components.

## Results

On ScanObjectNN (PB-T50-RS), PreDifPoint achieves 87.96% Overall Accuracy, surpassing the closest competitor PointDif (87.61%) by 0.35%. On ShapeNetPart segmentation, PreDifPoint reaches 87.5% mIoU, outperforming the best baseline MPCT (86.7%) by 0.8%. On S3DIS Area 5 indoor segmentation, PreDifPoint attains 71.8% mIoU, improving over PointDif (70.0%) by 1.8%, though its mAcc (76.5%) trails PointDif (77.1%) slightly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PreDifPoint outperforms all 11 baselines across the three main evaluation benchmarks. On ScanObjectNN classification, it leads every subset: 92.25% on OBJ-ONLY, 93.42% on OBJ-BG, and 87.96% on PB-T50-RS, edging out PointDif (the closest diffusion-based competitor) by 0.34–0.35% and surpassing non-pretrained methods like DGCNN by roughly 9–10 points. On ShapeNetPart segmentation, PreDifPoint achieves 87.5% mIoU, beating MPCT (86.7%), PCT (86.4%), and PointCNN (86.1%), with per-category gains in most of the 15 object classes. On S3DIS Area 5, PreDifPoint improves mIoU by 1.8% over PointDif (71.8% vs. 70.0%) and by 2.9% over Point-Bert (68.9%), but its mAcc of 76.5% is 0.6% below PointDif's 77.1%, suggesting slightly weaker per-class coverage in a few categories. Among self-supervised pretraining baselines (Point-Bert, Point-MAE, TAP), PreDifPoint consistently leads by 2–5 points on ScanObjectNN, indicating that the diffusion-based pretraining paradigm with conditional guidance yields stronger geometric priors than masked-reconstruction or contrastive approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

PreDifPoint is a direct extension of PointDif [48], which first established conditional diffusion models for point cloud pretraining. The broader lineage includes masked-reconstruction pretraining (PointBERT, Point-MAE, Point-M2AE) and generative pretraining via VAEs and diffusion (DDPM, PVD), but PreDifPoint departs from the masking paradigm by using progressive denoising as the pretraining signal. Its two main architectural departures from PointDif are the bi-directional covariate attention (DXCA-Attention) for fusing local and global features in the conditioning network, and a density-adaptive time-step sampling strategy that weights diffusion steps by local point density.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical recipe for point cloud pretraining that only requires fine-tuning task-specific heads, which is useful in data-limited or compute-constrained settings. However, the gains over PointDif are modest (0.35% OA on ScanObjectNN, 1.8% mIoU on S3DIS), and the mAcc on S3DIS actually lags behind PointDif, suggesting the density-adaptive sampling may not uniformly benefit all categories. Open questions include whether the density-adaptive strategy generalizes to highly non-uniform real-world scans (e.g., LiDAR) and whether the DXCA-Attention mechanism scales to larger backbones or multi-modal conditioning.
