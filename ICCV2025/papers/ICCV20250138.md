## Problem

Existing video instance segmentation methods assume a fixed set of object categories and suffer catastrophic forgetting when required to learn new classes incrementally. Simply combining continual learning with VIS only addresses frame-level forgetting while ignoring the lack of global video context, which significantly hinders retention of previously learned categories at the video level.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250138/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a video as a sequence of Nf RGB frames (Nf × H × W × 3) processed frame-by-frame through a frozen Mask2Former backbone and pixel decoder, augmented by two learnable prompt vectors: a task-specific frame prompt (Lp^f × D) and a task-specific video prompt (Lp^v × D). It outputs per-frame instance segmentation masks (Nf × Lp^v × H × W) and class probabilities for all categories learned so far, with consistent instance tracking across frames. Notably, the model is rehearsal-free—no old-task training data is stored—and the frame prompt length shrinks from 100 (first task) to 10 (subsequent tasks) to limit parameter growth.

## Method

HVPL hierarchically addresses forgetting at two levels. At the frame level, a task-specific frame prompt encodes new-class semantics, while an Orthogonal Gradient Correction (OGC) module projects the prompt's update gradient onto the orthogonal complement of old-class features (constructed via SVD on PCA-reduced prompt features from a small sample of old-task videos), ensuring the Transformer decoder produces unchanged outputs for old inputs. At the video level, a video context decoder first applies Graph-Guided State Space (GSS) layers whose GT-SSM module traverses a minimum spanning tree over frame-prompt features to capture structural inter-class relationships across frames, then applies multi-head self-attention layers to propagate task-specific global video context into a task-specific video prompt. The entire backbone, pixel decoder, and Transformer decoder remain frozen; only the prompts, GSS/MSA layers, and classifier/mask heads are trained, yielding ~0.9M trainable parameters.

## Results

On YouTube-VIS 2021 under the 30-10 setting, HVPL achieves AP=43.24, AP₅₀=60.13, AP₇₅=47.50, and AR₁=40.32 with only 0.92M trainable parameters, substantially outperforming the next-best method ECLIPSE (AP=29.68) by 13.56 AP points. Under the more challenging 20-4 setting (6 tasks), HVPL reaches AP=35.05 and AP₇₅=39.17, surpassing ECLIPSE (AP=30.52) and CoMFormer+NeST (AP=15.37) by large margins. HVPL also achieves the lowest forgetting rates (FAP=15.69, FAR₁=14.83 in 30-10; FAP=20.42, FAR₁=20.15 in 20-4), indicating minimal degradation of previously learned classes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HVPL outperforms all six baselines on AP across both YouTube-VIS 2021 settings. Against the closest prompt-learning competitor ECLIPSE (0.47M params), HVPL gains +13.56 AP in 30-10 (43.24 vs 29.68) and +4.53 AP in 20-4 (35.05 vs 30.52), while also reducing FAP from 40.59 to 15.69 (30-10) and from 22.83 to 20.42 (20-4). Against knowledge-distillation methods, HVPL leads CoMFormer+NeST by +14.54 AP in 30-10 (43.24 vs 28.70) and by a dramatic +19.68 AP in 20-4 (35.05 vs 15.37), with FAP reduced from 46.27 to 15.69. Against BalConpas, HVPL improves AP by +21.12 in 30-10 and +24.18 in 20-4, while cutting FAP from 76.72 to 15.69. HVPL uses only 0.92M trainable parameters versus 42–55M for the distillation-based baselines, achieving superior accuracy with roughly 50× fewer parameters. The only metric where HVPL does not lead is FAP in the 20-4 setting, where ECLIPSE (22.83) is slightly lower than HVPL (20.42) — though the gap is small and HVPL still dominates on all segmentation quality metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Mask2Former as a frozen frame-level detector and introduces a hierarchical prompt-learning framework that addresses catastrophic forgetting at two distinct granularities: frame-level (via an orthogonal gradient correction module that projects prompt updates onto the SVD-derived null space of old-task features) and video-level (via a graph-traversal state space module inspired by Mamba, followed by multi-head self-attention layers that propagate global video context into a task-specific video prompt). It departs from prior continual learning work for segmentation (CoMFormer, PLOP, BalConpas, ECLIPSE), which operates purely at the frame or image level and cannot capture inter-frame structural relationships, and from standard VIS methods that assume a fixed, known class set. The CVIS problem formulation itself is new, combining the rehearsal-free continual learning setting with the temporal tracking and segmentation requirements of video instance segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing CVIS as a rehearsal-free, open-ended problem, the paper addresses a practical gap: real-world video systems must encounter new object categories without retraining on all prior data. The hierarchical decomposition of forgetting into frame-level and video-level components is a useful conceptual lens that could inform future video CL research. However, the evaluation is limited to three datasets with relatively small class budgets (up to 40 classes total), and the baselines are largely adapted from semantic or panoptic segmentation CL rather than purpose-built for video, which somewhat limits the strength of the comparison. The authors note future work on integrating large language models for large-scale continual tasks, suggesting the current approach may not yet scale to open-world settings with hundreds of categories.
