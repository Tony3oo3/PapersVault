## Problem

Of approximately 4,500 discovered Oracle Bone Script characters, only ~1,600 have been deciphered, and existing AI approaches like OBSD rely on end-to-end diffusion models that lack explicit semantic analysis of glyph components, limiting interpretability and providing minimal insight into the cultural and historical context essential for expert decipherment. No prior method simultaneously performs structured component-level semantic reasoning and generates visually enhanced representations that faithfully preserve the original glyph geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251561/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single oracle bone script glyph image as input, which is first vectorized into SVG via a skeleton-based Oracle Glyph Vectorization (OGV) pipeline (Zhang-Suen skeletonization → normal-vector offset → cubic spline fitting). The output is a semantically enriched black-and-white SVG vector pictogram that modifies the original glyph's geometry to depict the scene the character represents, alongside structured textual outputs: identified key components (radicals), a directed acyclic graph of their relative spatial relationships, a fine-grained semantic caption, and per-component bounding box layouts at 16×16 resolution.

## Method

OracleFusion is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 (OBSUG) fine-tunes Qwen-VL with LoRA in a multi-turn dialogue to sequentially identify key structural components, perform Spatial Awareness Reasoning (SAR) to output relative positional relationships as a DAG, generate a fine-grained semantic description, and ground each component with a bounding box. Stage 2 (SOVF) is a training-free optimization over SVG parameters using Stable Diffusion's latent space, combining four losses: LSDS (standard score distillation for semantic generation), GSDS (a novel glyph structural constraint that enforces high cross-attention responses inside and low responses outside per-component binary masks at 16×16 resolution, backpropagating through the UNet to SVG parameters), SKST (a skeleton structure loss using Delaunay triangulation of skeleton and contour points to enforce angular consistency with the original glyph), and a tone loss for color preservation. The key differentiator is the region-based cross-attention constraint (GSDS) that ensures each radical is synthesized in its correct spatial position, combined with the skeleton-triangulation-based structural fidelity loss that prevents the generated shape from deviating from the original glyph outline.

## Results

On the RMOBS test set, OracleFusion achieves a CLIPScore of 28.30, outperforming ClipDraw (27.78) and Word-As-Image (27.28) in semantic relevance. For glyph maintenance, OracleFusion records the lowest Distance to the original glyph (0.86) compared to Word-As-Image (0.92) and ClipDraw (1.05). In a user study with 70 participants rating 28 randomly selected OBS characters, OracleFusion leads on all three dimensions: Semantics Representation (3.97), Visual Appeal (3.90), and Glyph Maintenance (3.95), each on a 1–5 scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OracleFusion is compared against two baselines: ClipDraw [11], which uses CLIP-based text-to-drawing synthesis, and Word-As-Image [20], which combines SDS loss with glyph contour preservation for semantic typography. OracleFusion outperforms both on every reported metric. Against ClipDraw, OracleFusion gains +0.52 CLIPScore and reduces Distance by 0.19, while also leading user-study ratings by roughly 0.7–0.8 points across SR, VA, and GM. Against Word-As-Image, the gains are +1.02 CLIPScore and −0.06 Distance, with user-study advantages of +0.55 (SR), +0.42 (VA), and +0.51 (GM). Qualitatively, Word-As-Image tends to omit or misplace radicals in complex glyphs (e.g., the person in 'Light', footprints in 'Yan'), whereas OracleFusion's GSDS loss enforces region-level cross-attention constraints that correctly localize each component. ClipDraw produces sharp, unsmooth edges that deviate from the original glyph structure, while OracleFusion's SKST loss preserves skeleton-level structural fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

OracleFusion extends the SDS-based vector generation pipeline established by VectorFusion and Word-As-Image by introducing two key departures: (1) a Glyph Structural Constraint (GSDS loss) that manipulates cross-attention maps to enforce spatial placement of radicals, drawing on Attend-and-Excite's attention-editing ideas but applied to SVG parameters rather than raster images; and (2) a skeleton-based SKST loss that replaces Word-As-Image's contour-only preservation with Delaunay-triangulated angular constraints. On the understanding side, the paper departs from OBSD's end-to-end diffusion approach by explicitly decomposing OBS decipherment into MLLM-based semantic reasoning (with a novel Spatial Awareness Reasoning step) followed by constrained generation, and from GenOV's photographic output by targeting structurally faithful SVG vectors. The RMOBS dataset (20K samples, 900 characters) substantially exceeds GenOV's 364-character corpus and adds radical-level bounding box annotations absent in prior OBS resources.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With only ~1,600 of ~4,500 known OBS characters deciphered, any tool that provides interpretable, structure-preserving visual hypotheses for undeciphered glyphs has direct value for paleographers. The two-stage design (MLLM reasoning → constrained vector generation) offers a level of interpretability that end-to-end diffusion methods like OBSD lack, potentially making the system a practical assistant rather than a black-box generator. Key limitations include reliance on a vector font library covering only ~4K of an estimated 50K OBS images, test-time optimization of 800 iterations per sample, and evaluation on just 900 characters. Open questions include whether the SAR and GSDS mechanisms generalize to other ancient scripts (cuneiform, Linear B) and whether the MLLM's semantic reasoning can be validated against expert consensus at scale.
