## Problem

Existing video motion control methods are each limited to a single control type (camera pose, object dragging, or motion brush), and naively combining them in one pipeline produces logical conflicts—for example, a camera tilt invalidates a fixed drag target, or a motion brush's 'keep background static' constraint contradicts camera-induced background motion. No prior framework can orchestrate multiple heterogeneous control signals simultaneously without such conflicts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252063/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (704×448) plus user-specified control signals: binary masks designating brush or drag regions, scalar motion-strength values per region, a 6-DOF SE(3) camera pose sequence, and 6-DOF rigid transformations for drag units. These are internally converted into a dense (T, 5, H, W) tensor comprising projected point trajectories, a motion-strength map, a partition-index map, and a category map, which is fed to the adapter. The output is a 24-frame generated video with the specified multi-region motion behavior.

## Method

I2VControl unifies camera control, object dragging, and motion brush under a single dense point-trajectory representation in the camera coordinate system, where each spatial region (motion unit) is assigned a rigid SE(3) transformation and a scalar motion strength. A spatial partitioning strategy divides the image into borderland, brush-units, and drag-units, each governed by its own formulation of the trajectory function, so that different control types coexist without conflict. A lightweight adapter—comprising convolutional layers that encode the (T,5,H,W) control tensor into tokens, followed by additional trainable cross-attention layers—injects this information into a frozen pre-trained diffusion model (MagicVideo-V2) by concatenating control tokens with diffusion tokens before self-attention and adding the result back. Training data is generated by a perception-based pipeline that applies SAM segmentation, motion segmentation, SpatialTracker 3D point tracking, and camera pose estimation to 970K RGB video clips, producing ground-truth control signals that exactly mirror the inference-time user inputs. The adapter is trained with standard MSE loss while all other base-model settings remain unchanged, and the same architecture transfers to other backbones (Seedance, Wan 2.1) with only attention-layer adjustments.

## Results

On the VIPSeg dragging benchmark, I2VControl achieves an ObjMC of 197.60 (lower is better), outperforming DragAnything (211.01) and MOFA-Video (239.76), with a FID of 127.44 and a user-study score of 3.87/5. On RealEstate10K camera control, I2VControl reduces rotation error to 0.52° (vs. 2.69° for MotionCtrl and 1.19° for CameraCtrl) and translation error to 9.93 (vs. 12.31 and 22.72), while also achieving the best FID (153.81) and user score (4.31). On the motion brush task, I2VControl dramatically outperforms Motion-I2V, achieving an MSC of 64.59 vs. 6.87 on Object Brush and 97.29 vs. 2.57 on Fluid Brush, with IoU scores of 60.34% and 46.37% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

I2VControl outperforms all five baselines across every task and metric. On dragging (VIPSeg/MD), it beats DragAnything by 6.4% ObjMC and MOFA-Video by 17.4% ObjMC on VIPSeg, and by 45.4% and 60.8% respectively on the Manual Dragging set, while also achieving the best FID and user-study scores. On camera control (RealEstate10K/MO), it reduces rotation error by 5.2× over MotionCtrl (0.52° vs. 2.69°) and 2.3× over CameraCtrl (0.52° vs. 1.19°) on RE10K, and achieves the lowest translation error on both datasets. On motion brush, the advantage over Motion-I2V is the most dramatic: MSC improves by ~9.4× on Object Brush (64.59 vs. 6.87) and ~38× on Fluid Brush (97.29 vs. 2.57), with IoU jumping from 15.43% to 60.34% on objects. The paper notes that a fully unified multi-control comparison is infeasible because no prior method supports simultaneous camera + drag + brush, which is itself the key differentiator of I2VControl.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the authors' prior I2VControl-Camera work [8], which introduced the motion-strength formulation and the trajectory-function representation in SE(3). It departs from the broader line of single-control-type methods (DragAnything, MotionCtrl, CameraCtrl, Motion-I2V, MOFA-Video) by reformulating all control signals—camera extrinsics, sparse drag points, and optical-flow-based motion brush—into a unified dense point-trajectory representation, then resolving the logical conflicts that arise when these controls are naively combined through a spatial partitioning mechanism. The adapter architecture follows the ControlNet-style plug-in pattern but is explicitly designed to be agnostic to the underlying diffusion backbone, as demonstrated on MagicVideo-V2, Seedance, and Wan 2.1.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

I2VControl is the first framework to support camera control, object dragging, and motion brush simultaneously without logical conflicts, which is a practical gap that has limited real-world video-creation workflows. The spatial partitioning + unified trajectory representation provides a clean, extensible design that could accommodate additional control modalities. Limitations include reliance on a complex data pipeline (SAM + SpatialTracker + UniDepth) for training signal construction, evaluation across heterogeneous base models that complicates fair comparison, and the current 24-frame / 704×448 resolution constraint. Open questions include whether the partitioning strategy scales to scenes with many interacting objects, how the framework handles long-horizon temporal consistency, and whether text-level semantic control can be integrated alongside the spatial controls.
