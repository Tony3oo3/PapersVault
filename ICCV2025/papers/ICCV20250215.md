## Problem

Existing tuning-free personalized image generation methods are largely confined to single-ID scenarios and suffer from identity blending when multiple subjects are present, because standard cross-attention injection disrupts the base diffusion model's behavior and forces fixed attention mass onto irrelevant regions. Additionally, current approaches entangle identity features (facial structure, skin texture) with motion features (expression, orientation) in the latent representation, making it impossible to flexibly edit one while preserving the other.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method requires only a single reference face image per identity (no multi-ID training pairs) plus a free-form text prompt; optionally, facial expression and head orientation can be specified as lightweight prompts decoded by pre-trained classifiers. The face encoder combines ArcFace (buffalo_1) global embeddings with CLIP-ViT-H local embeddings, projected into a unified latent. Output is a full-resolution personalized image (SD1.5 latent space, 50-step DDIM sampling) where identity is preserved while expression, pose, clothing, background, and scene composition follow the text prompt, with independent per-identity editing in multi-ID compositions.

## Method

DynamicID introduces two core modules. Semantic-Activated Attention (SAA) augments standard cross-attention with a query-level activation gate w = Norm(QKᵀJ) that scales the attention output element-wise, so only queries semantically relevant to the face receive strong activation while background queries are suppressed—this eliminates the fixed-attention-mass bias of softmax, prevents identity blending in multi-ID settings, and enables zero-shot layout control via parametric modulation of w in early denoising steps. The Identity-Motion Reconfigurator (IMR) sits between the face encoder and SAA, using a DisentangleNet to extract identity features from source facial features and motion cues (text-encoded prompts + perturbed landmarks), then an EntangleNet to recombine identity with target motion features, trained with a dual loss of direct feature matching plus latent diffusion consistency. Training is task-decoupled into an anchoring stage (SAA + projection module trained on single-ID images with standard diffusion noise loss) and a reconfiguration stage (IMR trained on VariFace-10k, a curated dataset of 10k identities × 35 varied facial images each), eliminating the need for multi-ID training samples.

## Results

On single-ID personalization (Table 1), DynamicID achieves the best CLIP-T (0.239), Expr (0.456), and Pose (0.878) scores, substantially outperforming all baselines on facial editability metrics while maintaining competitive identity fidelity (FaceSim 0.671). On multi-ID personalization (Table 2), DynamicID again leads in CLIP-T (0.237), Expr (0.431), and Pose (0.867), with the Expr score exceeding the next-best method (OMG-InstantID, 0.343) by 26%. Notably, DynamicID operates on the lighter SD1.5 backbone, whereas several baselines rely on the heavier SDXL or FLUX architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynamicID decisively outperforms all five single-ID baselines on the two facial editability metrics: Expr (0.456 vs. 0.151–0.243) and Pose (0.878 vs. 0.264–0.869), with the largest gap over InstantID (Expr +0.305) and IPA-FaceID-Plus (Expr +0.300). On identity fidelity (FaceSim), DynamicID (0.671) trails InstantID (0.723) and IPA-FaceID-Plus (0.712), which the authors attribute to those methods performing near-verbatim face replication rather than generating varied expressions. In the multi-ID setting, DynamicID dominates on Expr (0.431 vs. 0.144–0.343) and Pose (0.867 vs. 0.256–0.742), again with the closest competitor being OMG-InstantID (0.343 Expr, 0.742 Pose). UniPortrait and StoryMaker achieve higher FaceSim in multi-ID (0.718, 0.678) but at the cost of minimal facial variation, mirroring the single-ID pattern. PhotoMaker-v2 is the only baseline that approaches DynamicID on Pose (0.869 vs. 0.878) but sacrifices identity fidelity (FaceSim 0.592). Overall, DynamicID is the only method that simultaneously achieves top-tier text alignment, strong identity preservation, and fine-grained facial editability across both single- and multi-ID scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tuning-free attention-based personalization lineage (IP-Adapter, InstantID, PhotoMaker) by identifying a fundamental flaw in standard cross-attention injection: the softmax's fixed attention mass simultaneously perturbs the base model's behavior and causes identity blending in multi-ID settings. The proposed Semantic-Activated Attention replaces this with query-level activation gating, a conceptual departure that eliminates the need for the auxiliary masking/routing modules (UniPortrait, InstantFamily, MagicID) that prior multi-ID methods bolt onto single-ID frameworks. The Identity-Motion Reconfigurator further departs from prior work by explicitly disentangling identity from motion features in latent space, a capability absent from all compared baselines, and the task-decoupled training paradigm sidesteps the data requirements that make end-to-end multi-ID training impractical.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot multi-ID generation with independent per-face editability (expression, orientation) addresses a practical gap that has persisted across the entire tuning-free personalization literature, where high-fidelity methods (InstantID, IPA-FaceID-Plus) essentially copy the reference face and editing-capable methods sacrifice fidelity. The VariFace-10k dataset (10k identities × 35 varied images) fills a concrete resource gap for training identity-motion disentanglement. However, the work is limited to SD1.5, struggles with faces near image edges and complex prompts, and the multi-ID evaluation is restricted to two-to-three-person scenes; scaling to larger models (SDXL, FLUX) and video remains open.
