## Problem

Fine-tuning dual-encoder vision-language models introduces a fundamental trade-off between in-domain learning gains and out-of-domain catastrophic forgetting, yet no existing metric can predict both outcomes a priori without actually performing the fine-tuning. Prior transferability measures are either designed for uni-modal encoders, rely on source training data and labels incompatible with contrastively pretrained models, or only provide rank correlations rather than direct performance-change predictions.

## Input / Output

The method requires only a single forward pass of the target dataset's training split through the pre-trained dual-encoder model, producing normalized unit-norm image and text embeddings in a shared d-dimensional space. From these embeddings, it computes a scalar IIMM score bounded in [-1, 1], which can then be plugged into a pre-fitted linear regression (calibrated on a small set of standard benchmarks) to predict both the in-domain accuracy gain over zero-shot error and the average out-of-domain accuracy loss across other tasks.

## Method

The IIMM is derived directly from the InfoNCE contrastive loss decomposition into two geometric terms: S_inter, the average cosine similarity between each image embedding and all incorrect text embeddings (capturing inter-modal misalignment), and S_intra, the average pairwise cosine similarity among image embeddings (capturing intra-modal clustering). The final metric is IIMM = ½(S_inter + S_intra), where high values indicate both substantial re-alignment capacity and elevated forgetting risk. The paper proves a theoretical bound showing that |IIMM(Q) − IIMM(P)| ≤ δ_A/2 + δ_B, where δ_A and δ_B are 1-Wasserstein distances between pre- and post-fine-tuning joint and marginal embedding distributions, guaranteeing that only significant representational shifts produce meaningful IIMM changes. Empirically, the IIMM achieves R² > 0.85 in linear regression to scaled in-domain accuracy gains across four base models (CLIP, EVA-02, SigLIP, CoCa) and five fine-tuning strategies, substantially outperforming six uni-modal transferability baselines.

## Results

The IIMM achieves Kendall's τ of 0.78–0.89 with gain over zero-shot error across four base models (CoCa, EVA-02, CLIP, SigLIP), outperforming the next-best baseline GBC (0.72–0.83) on three of four models. In linear regression to gain over zero-shot error, the IIMM attains R² > 0.89 for all base models (0.92 CoCa, 0.89 EVA-02, 0.92 CLIP, 0.92 SigLIP), substantially exceeding GBC's R² of 0.59–0.72. The IIMM also shows a strong negative linear relationship with average out-of-domain accuracy loss, confirming its dual role in predicting both learning gains and catastrophic forgetting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The IIMM outperforms all six baselines on Kendall's τ for gain over zero-shot error on CoCa (0.89 vs. 0.78 GBC, 0.67 TransRate) and EVA-02 (0.83 vs. 0.72 GBC, 0.61 TransRate), and ties GBC on CLIP (0.78) while trailing it slightly on SigLIP (0.78 vs. 0.83). In the more informative linear-regression fit (Table 3), the IIMM dominates GBC across every model: R² = 0.92/0.89/0.92/0.92 versus GBC's 0.72/0.68/0.59/0.71, a gap of 0.17–0.33 in explained variance. The remaining baselines—TransRate, EMMS, NCTI, SFDA, and H-Score—consistently show weaker or even negative correlations (e.g., NCTI τ = −0.59 on EVA-02; H-Score τ = −0.78 on EVA-02), confirming that uni-modal transferability metrics fail to capture the cross-modal geometry that drives fine-tuning outcomes in dual-encoder vision-language models.

## Relation to Prior Work

The IIMM is derived directly from the InfoNCE contrastive loss [37], decomposing its optimization into inter-modal misalignment (average similarity of image embeddings to incorrect text embeddings) and intra-modal uniformity (average pairwise image-image similarity), the latter drawing on the alignment-uniformity framework of Wang et al. [50]. This positions the metric as a principled, multi-modal extension of uni-modal transferability estimators (TransRate, SFDA, NCTI, EMMS, GBC, H-Score) that operate only on the vision encoder and ignore cross-modal geometry. The paper also builds on the modality-gap literature [26] and the growing body of work on catastrophic forgetting in dual-encoder VLMs [8, 36], but departs from prior transferability work by targeting direct prediction of accuracy gains (not just rank correlation) in the dual-encoder setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IIMM offers a lightweight, label-free, single-forward-pass metric that simultaneously predicts in-domain learning gains and out-of-domain forgetting, with R² > 0.85 across four state-of-the-art VLMs—a level of predictive strength unusual for a scalar transferability score. The Wasserstein-distance bound provides theoretical assurance that the metric is stable under small distributional shifts, making it a robust decision tool for practitioners allocating fine-tuning compute. Key limitations include the need to fit a linear predictor on a small set of prior fine-tuning runs before applying it to novel tasks, and the evaluation is confined to nine standard benchmarks and four models, leaving open whether the relationship holds across more diverse architectures or specialized domains (e.g., medical, satellite). The authors note that additional embedding-geometry features might enable prediction without any prior fine-tuning data, an important direction for future work.
