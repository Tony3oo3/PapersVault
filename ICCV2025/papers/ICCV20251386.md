## Problem

Existing layout-to-image (L2I) diffusion methods achieve strong results in natural domains but suffer from poor generative fidelity and weak layout alignment when applied to degraded scenes (low-light, underwater, adverse weather, blur). The paper identifies the root cause as a 'contextual illusion dilemma': in degraded conditions, high-frequency details of foreground instances are attenuated while low-frequency context dominates the overall frequency spectrum, causing generated objects to blend into or be overwhelmed by their surroundings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of N bounding boxes (each with a semantic class label) and a global text caption describing the degraded scene, and outputs a 512×512 RGB degraded image. Bounding boxes are encoded via Fourier positional embedding plus an MLP, while class labels and the global caption are processed by a CLIP text encoder. Generation is performed in the latent space of a frozen SD v1.5 model, with FICGen modules attached only to the mid-level (8×8) and lowest-resolution (16×16) decoder layers, and inference uses 50 Euler steps with classifier-free guidance scale 7.5.

## Method

FICGen introduces a frequency-inspired contextual disentanglement pipeline on top of a frozen LDM. First, a Frequency Perceiver Resampler (FPR) extracts high-frequency (HF) and low-frequency (LF) prototypes from same-class degraded exemplars via DFT, binary spectral masking, and learnable channel weights, then uses a dual-query mechanism (inspired by the Perceiver architecture) to produce frequency-aware instance and context tokens. Second, a Visual-Frequency Enhanced Attention (VFEA) module injects these tokens into the latent diffusion space, where an instance coherence map (binary mask per bbox plus its complement) enforces spatial disentanglement between individual instances and the surrounding context. Third, an Adaptive Spatial-Frequency Aggregation (ASFA) module recombines the disentangled instance and context features using parallel spatial self-attention and frequency attention, fused by a learnable depthwise convolution. Only FICGen parameters are trained (300 epochs, AdamW, lr=1e-4, 8×A100) with the standard LDM noise-prediction MSE loss, while the pretrained LDM backbone remains frozen.

## Results

On the five degraded benchmarks, FICGen achieves FID scores of 25.10 (RUOD), 42.40 (ExDARK), 31.25 (DIOR-H), 58.02 (blurred VOC 2012), and 68.31 (DAWN), consistently outperforming MIGC and CC-Diff. In terms of alignment, FICGen reaches 27.6 mAP on DIOR-H (82% of the Oracle's 33.4) and 42.5 mAP on ExDARK, which actually exceeds the real-data Oracle (37.2). On DAWN, FICGen improves mAP from 15.7 (CC-Diff) to 22.6, a 44% relative gain over the strongest baseline. The method also demonstrates strong trainability, boosting downstream Faster R-CNN detection by ~2.0 mAP on average and up to ~6.0 AP on specific classes like 'airport' (32.2→38.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FICGen outperforms MIGC [54] on every metric across all five datasets; for example, on DIOR-H it improves mAP from 21.8 to 27.6 (+5.8) and AP75 from 17.5 to 27.6 (+10.1). Against CC-Diff [52], FICGen wins on mAP, AP50, and AP75 across all datasets (e.g., +7.3 mAP on RUOD, +7.4 mAP on ExDARK), though CC-Diff retains a marginal FID advantage on DIOR-H (30.88 vs. 31.25). In the broader DIOR-H comparison (Table 2, YOLOv8), FICGen surpasses AeroGen [36] on FID (31.25 vs. 38.57) and mAP (31.2 vs. 29.8), but AeroGen edges ahead on AP50 (54.2 vs. 49.9). FICGen also clearly dominates the earlier layout-to-image baselines—GLIGEN [15] (mAP 25.8), LayoutDiffusion [53] (20.0), ReCo [46] (21.1), and LostGAN [35] (14.3)—on DIOR-H, confirming that frequency-aware contextual guidance provides a substantial advantage over purely spatial or text-driven conditioning in degraded scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

FICGen extends the layout-to-image (L2I) generation paradigm—established by methods like GLIGEN, MIGC, and LayoutDiffusion for natural scenes—into degraded visual conditions (low-light, underwater, adverse weather, remote sensing, blur). It builds directly on the Latent Diffusion Model (SD v1.5) as its generative backbone and departs from prior L2I methods (MIGC, CC-Diff, AeroGen) that condition solely on spatial layout and text by introducing a frequency-domain perspective: DFT-based frequency prototypes extracted via Perceiver-inspired resamplers are injected into the latent space through a visual-frequency enhanced attention module. The paper also draws on instance-level masking strategies from IF-Adapter and MIGC for spatial disentanglement, but augments them with an instance coherence map and a spatial-frequency aggregation module specifically designed to counter the 'contextual illusion dilemma' unique to degraded scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical data-scarcity bottleneck in degraded-condition perception (e.g., ExDARK has only ~1/20th the images of COCO) by showing that frequency-aware synthetic images can consistently boost downstream object detection by ~2 mAP across five diverse benchmarks, with the best training protocol (synthetic pre-training + real fine-tuning) approaching or exceeding real-data baselines. The plug-and-play design (deployed only on mid/low decoder layers of a frozen LDM) makes the approach broadly applicable to other diffusion-based generators. Open questions include whether the frequency prototype extraction generalizes to unseen degradation types not present in the training exemplars, whether the 512×512 resolution constraint limits real-world deployment, and whether the paradigm extends meaningfully to 3D content generation as the authors suggest.
