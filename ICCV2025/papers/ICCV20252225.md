## Problem

3D motion skeletons vary in joint hierarchies and proportions, preventing data-driven motion synthesis from generalizing across characters. While Temporal Point Clouds (TPCs) provide a skeleton-agnostic motion representation, no method exists to synthesize TPCs directly, leaving motion learning locked to skeleton-specific pipelines. This paper tackles the unexplored challenge of learning motion synthesis natively in the TPC medium, where temporal consistency and point identifiability pose unique architectural difficulties.

## Input / Output

The autoencoder consumes 256-point TPC frames (each point carrying a 3D position and a one-hot body-group label from {body, left arm, right arm, left leg, right leg}) sampled from posed skeletons with Gaussian offsets (σ ∈ [0.025, 0.075]), and produces a 512-dimensional latent vector per frame. The latent motion synthesizer operates on 128-frame latent sequences, predicting masked frames for interpolation, transition, or prediction tasks. For fine-tuning, 2D keypoint sequences or noisy skeletal poses are projected into the latent space via added MLP layers, and outputs are decoded back to 3D joint positions and quaternions.

## Method

PUMPS is a two-stage pipeline: first, a PointTransformer encoder with 4 layers of farthest-first pooling compresses each TPC frame into a 512-d latent vector, followed by a decoder that reconstructs the full TPC using a key innovation—per-point Gaussian noise vectors (z_β ~ N(0,1)) appended to RoPE-attended temporal features to stochastically identify and generate each point's trajectory without any point-wise attention, reducing complexity from O((|P||V|)²) to O(|V|²) + O(|P||V|). Reconstruction is optimized with a linear-assignment (Hungarian) point-pairing loss rather than Chamfer distance, preventing point collapse toward cluster centroids. Second, a frozen encoder feeds a Transformer-based latent motion synthesizer (Φ^LMS) trained via masked data modelling (keyframe interpolation, motion transition, short-term prediction) across AMASS, LaFAN1, Human3.6M, and 100STYLE, yielding a skeleton-agnostic pre-trained model that can be fine-tuned for 2D-to-3D estimation or denoising by adding input/output projection MLPs.

## Results

On keyframe interpolation (121-frame sequences), PUMPS achieves the best L2P of 0.137 m at 5-frame intervals and the best NPSS across all intervals (0.249, 1.080, 1.973 for 5/15/30-frame gaps), outperforming all learned baselines. On motion transition, PUMPS achieves the best L2P at all tested gaps (0.168, 0.443, 0.900 for 15/30/60-frame gaps) and the best L2Q at 15- and 30-frame gaps (0.271, 0.609). When fine-tuned for 2D-to-3D motion estimation, PUMPS reduces MPJPE by ~40% over its scratch-trained variant, reaching 44.15 mm on Aim motions (second only to MHFormer's 40.38 mm). For motion denoising, fine-tuned PUMPS achieves the best MPJPE across most motion categories (e.g., 49.83 mm on Aim vs. 58.68 mm for the Transformer baseline), a ~25% improvement over scratch training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PUMPS outperforms all learned baselines on L2P for both keyframe interpolation and motion transition, with the largest margins over Pose-NDF (e.g., 0.137 vs. 0.206 L2P at 5-frame KF) and BERT (0.137 vs. 0.203). On NPSS (signal similarity), PUMPS leads all methods at every tested interval for keyframe interpolation (0.249 vs. 0.322 for Δ-interp at 5-frame) and for motion transition at 15- and 30-frame gaps (0.241 vs. 0.218 for Δ-interp; 0.946 vs. 1.004 for MAE). CITL is the closest competitor on L2Q for keyframe interpolation (0.688 vs. PUMPS's 0.693 at 15-frame), but PUMPS still wins on L2P and NPSS. For 2D-to-3D motion estimation, fine-tuned PUMPS (44.15 mm MPJPE on Aim) trails only MHFormer (40.38 mm) while outperforming PoseFormer v2 (59.19 mm), MixSTE (61.05 mm), and MotionBERT (57.80 mm). In motion denoising, fine-tuned PUMPS achieves the best MPJPE in 6 of 8 motion categories (e.g., 49.83 mm on Aim vs. 58.68 mm for Transformer, 64.39 mm for U-ViT), though it is slightly worse than scratch-trained PUMPS on acceleration error (10.45 vs. 8.92 on Aim), a trade-off the authors note is acceptable.

## Relation to Prior Work

PUMPS builds directly on the authors' prior TPC framework (PC-MRL, ECCV 2024) for cross-skeleton motion representation and the CITL architecture (CVPR 2023) for Transformer-based motion interpolation. It departs from the skeleton-specific paradigm that dominates motion synthesis (joint positions, quaternions) by operating entirely in the skeleton-agnostic TPC medium, and from conventional point cloud reconstruction (Chamfer distance, point-wise attention) by introducing Gaussian noise vectors as point identity tokens and linear assignment for the reconstruction loss. The work also extends the masked autoencoder pre-training paradigm from image/point-cloud domains into the temporal motion domain, where the decoder must preserve point trajectories across frames rather than reconstructing static geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single pre-trained model in TPC space can perform motion interpolation, transition, prediction, denoising, and 2D-to-3D estimation across arbitrary skeletons, PUMPS points toward a unified motion synthesis pipeline that eliminates the need for per-skeleton retraining — a long-standing practical bottleneck in animation. Key limitations remain: the TPC-to-skeleton conversion relies on PC-MRL, which requires at least one known pose at evaluation time and cannot handle zero-length bones, and the fine-tuning demonstrations are limited to two tasks on a single dataset. Open questions include whether the noise-vector decoder generalises to higher point counts or non-humanoid characters, and whether the approach can be integrated into diffusion-based motion generation, which the authors flag as future work.
