## Problem

Existing pedestrian trajectory prediction methods rely on fixed-frame observation protocols (8-frame traditional or 2-frame momentary), which exclude pedestrians with insufficient or excessive observation history, introduce prediction delays, and prevent transfer across tasks with different temporal information. This paper introduces the Transferable Online Pedestrian Trajectory Prediction (TOTP) task, which requires simultaneously predicting all pedestrians in a video with their naturally variable observation lengths and enabling seamless transfer between online, traditional, and momentary prediction settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250332/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes variable-length observed trajectories (minimum 2 frames, no upper bound) for each pedestrian along with neighbor pedestrian positions and velocities for social interaction encoding. All pedestrians in a video frame are processed simultaneously, so a single batch contains trajectories of heterogeneous lengths corresponding to pedestrians entering, centered in, or leaving the observation area. Output is a future trajectory of up to 12 frames per pedestrian (truncated if the pedestrian exits the scene), evaluated via Best-of-N with 20 sampled trajectories using ADE_o and FDE_o metrics that account for variable prediction horizons.

## Method

TAMLD comprises three modules built on Mamba state-space models. The Social-Implicit Mamba Synthesizer (SIMS) encodes ego-motion and cross-attention social features into a Mamba SSM that incrementally updates motion latent states, while a Temporal-Aware Distillation loss randomly drops initial frames of long observations to reduce temporal sparsity imbalance. The Trend-Conditional Mamba Decomposer (TCMD) is a CVAE where a Trend-Aware LSTM encodes future motion trends as conditional constraints on the latent distribution, and an iterative loop of SSM state updates and velocity decomposition produces coarse trajectories. The Motion-Latent Mamba Diffusion (MLMD) module then denoises the sampled motion latent features via DDPM, using a separate Mamba SSM as the noise predictor conditioned on learned motion states. Training proceeds in two phases: first optimizing decomposition, KL-divergence, and distillation losses jointly, then freezing all prior parameters and training the diffusion denoiser with a noise-prediction objective.

## Results

On the primary TOTP (online trajectory prediction) task, TAMLD achieves ADE_o/FDE_o of 0.21/0.35 m on ETH-UCY (average) and 7.86/11.87 px on SDD, outperforming the previous SOTA MART [19] by 44.7%/43.5% on ETH-UCY and 5.87%/4.51% on SDD. On the traditional 8-12 protocol, TAMLD reaches 0.21/0.32 m average ADE/FDE on ETH-UCY, matching or exceeding all baselines. On the momentary (2-frame) task, TAMLD achieves 0.24/0.38 m, improving 4%/5% over SingularTrajectory [3].

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAMLD achieves the best ADE_o/FDE_o across all five ETH-UCY sub-datasets and SDD, with the largest margin over the previous SOTA MART [19] (44.7% ADE_o reduction on ETH-UCY average). Against RNN-based SocialVAE [55], TAMLD improves by 27.6%/22.2% on ADE_o/FDE_o; against Transformer-based TUTR [40], the gains are 51.2%/53.3%. On the traditional 8-12 protocol (Table 2), TAMLD matches or slightly exceeds MART [19] (0.21/0.32 vs 0.21/0.33 average) and clearly outperforms LB-EBM [30] (0.21/0.38) and EqMotion [54] (0.21/0.35). On the momentary 2-frame task (Table 3), TAMLD (0.24/0.38) edges out SingularTrajectory [3] (0.25/0.40) by 4%/5%, while substantially outperforming AgentFormer [59] (0.62/1.26) and STT+DTO [29] (0.46/0.93). The paper also demonstrates that a model trained solely on the online task (M_o) transfers to traditional and momentary tasks with minimal degradation, a property none of the compared baselines exhibit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Mamba state-space model as a sequence backbone, replacing the RNN and Transformer architectures that dominate prior pedestrian trajectory prediction work. It integrates a CVAE-style latent generation (following the conditional variational framework of Sohn et al.) with a DDPM-based denoising stage (following the noised-latent construction of MID), creating a three-stage pipeline: SSM-based motion state extraction, trend-conditional latent generation, and diffusion-based latent reconstruction. The social feature representation is adapted from Social Ways, and the overall positioning departs from prior work by framing trajectory prediction as a variable-observation, multi-task-transferable problem rather than a fixed-protocol one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TOTP task formulation addresses a genuine practical gap: real-time systems must simultaneously handle pedestrians with sparse, moderate, and ample observation histories, yet prior methods were locked into fixed-frame protocols that either discard short-observation agents or waste computation on long ones. The task transferability results—particularly that a model trained on the online task transfers well to momentary prediction—suggest a path toward unified deployment across prediction settings. However, the evaluation is limited to two datasets (ETH-UCY, SDD), the 'online' evaluation is still conducted offline, and the large performance margins over baselines are partly an artifact of evaluating methods not designed for the variable-observation setting, making it difficult to isolate methodological gains from task-formulation advantages.
