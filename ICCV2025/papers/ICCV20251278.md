## Problem

Monocular 3D object detectors trained on a single ego camera height (e.g., passenger car) degrade severely when deployed at unseen heights (bots, trucks), yet this generalization challenge has been largely understudied. The paper identifies depth estimation as the primary failure mode and mathematically proves that regressed depth and ground-based depth exhibit opposing extrapolation trends under camera height changes, providing a principled basis for mitigation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251278/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image together with camera intrinsics (focal length f, principal point) and extrinsics (ego height H, rotation R, translation T). At inference, the ego height can differ from the training height without any retraining or image warping. The output is a set of 3D bounding boxes whose depth is a simple average of a regressed depth and a closed-form ground-plane depth queried at the projected 3D bottom center of each box.

## Method

CHARM3R augments an existing Mono3D detector (GUP Net or DEVIANT) with a second depth pathway: it predicts a learnable shift coefficient α to compute the projected 3D bottom center from the projected 3D center and 2D height, then queries the ground-plane depth at that pixel in closed form (Lemma 1). The key theoretical result (Theorems 1 and 2) shows that the regressed depth has a negative mean-error trend while the ground depth has a positive mean-error trend as ego height ΔH changes, so their simple average cancels the opposing extrapolation biases. A ReLU is applied to the ground-depth numerator to enforce positivity and training stability, and the product formulation (α × (v_c − v_{c,2D})) for the bottom-center shift outperforms a direct sum. The entire fusion is end-to-end trainable within the detector, requiring no multi-height training data, no image transformation, and no Plücker embeddings.

## Results

On the CARLA Val split, CHARM3R (built on GUP Net) achieves AP₃D₇₀ of 27.33% at +0.76 m ego height change and 19.45% at −0.70 m, compared to 7.23% and 9.46% for the source model, respectively — a >2× improvement at unseen heights. The mean depth error (MDE) drops from −0.63 m (source) to −0.02 m at +0.76 m, nearly matching the oracle's +0.03 m. On DEVIANT, CHARM3R reaches 26.24% AP₃D₇₀ at +0.76 m versus 6.25% for the source. The oracle upper bound at +0.76 m is 62.25% (GUP Net) and 62.56% (DEVIANT), so CHARM3R closes roughly 55–60% of the remaining gap to in-distribution performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CHARM3R decisively outperforms both baselines at unseen ego heights on both GUP Net and DEVIANT. Against Plücker embeddings [77], CHARM3R gains +17.2 AP₃D₇₀ points at +0.76 m on GUP Net (27.33 vs. 10.13) and +16.7 at −0.70 m (19.45 vs. 8.43); Plücker embeddings actually degrade in-distribution AP₃D₇₀ slightly (55.56 vs. 53.82 source) while offering only marginal OOD gains. Against UniDrive [47] and its optimized variant UniDrive++, CHARM3R leads by +21.8 AP₃D₇₀ at +0.76 m on GUP Net (27.33 vs. 5.54 UniDrive, 12.27 UniDrive++) and by +10.3 at −0.70 m (19.45 vs. 10.83 UniDrive++). The MDE gap is equally stark: CHARM3R achieves near-zero depth bias (−0.02 m at +0.76 m) while UniDrive++ still shows −0.48 m and Plücker −0.63 m, confirming that the averaging of regressed and ground-based depths cancels the opposing extrapolation trends identified in Theorems 1 and 2. At the in-distribution height (ΔH = 0), CHARM3R matches or slightly exceeds the source model on GUP Net (55.68 vs. 53.82 AP₃D₇₀) but is marginally lower on DEVIANT (48.74 vs. 50.18), indicating the depth-averaging mechanism does not sacrifice in-domain accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

CHARM3R builds on GUP Net and DEVIANT as base Mono3D detectors and departs from prior camera-height robustness approaches (Plücker embeddings, UniDrive's constant-depth image warping, and multi-height data augmentation) by identifying a geometric duality: regressed depth and ground-plane depth exhibit provably opposing extrapolation trends under ego height shifts. Rather than transforming inputs or augmenting training data, the method introduces a lightweight in-model fusion that averages these two depth estimates, leveraging the ground depth as a closed-form, height-parameterized prior. This positions the work at the intersection of Mono3D detection and OOD generalization, extending the camera-parameter robustness literature from minor intrinsics/rotation variations to significant vertical baseline changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: AVs are increasingly deployed at diverse ego heights (delivery bots, trucks) where retraining per height is infeasible, and the 45%+ improvement over baselines at unseen heights is substantial. However, validation is confined to the simulated CARLA dataset with two base detectors and one backbone variant, leaving open whether the opposing-trend cancellation holds on real-world data with sensor noise, non-flat roads, and diverse object categories. The Oracle bias observed at extreme heights (higher AP at lower camera height) is acknowledged but left unexplained, and the method's reliance on accurate ego height as an input at inference time limits its applicability when height is uncertain.
