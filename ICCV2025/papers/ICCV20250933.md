## Problem

Existing driving video understanding methods rely on shallow probabilistic correlations rather than genuine causal inference, fail to model ego-vehicle-level state transitions, and are vulnerable to spurious correlations between visual and linguistic modalities. This limits their ability to explain *why* a driving action occurs, not just *what* action is performed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250933/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 32-frame driving video clip (uniformly sampled from 3–28 s segments at 1280×720, preprocessed to 224×224×3) and produces two structured text outputs: a narration describing the ego-vehicle's action and a reasoning passage identifying the causal environmental factors that triggered that action. The causal features are computed at a spatial resolution of H/32 × W/32 with 4FC channels, and the text output is tokenized to a fixed length with sparse attention masking to enforce object–sentence alignment.

## Method

MCAM comprises three modules: (1) a Multi-level Feature Extractor (MFE) that jointly encodes local features via 3DResNet and global features via VidSwin, fusing them through 1×1 convolutions and a linear layer; (2) a Causal Analysis Module (CAM) that constructs a Driving State Directed Acyclic Graph (DSDAG) modeling the transition from an initial safe state X_s through an action Y to an end safe state X_e, with the environment Z as a confounding variable, and uses attention-weighted fusion of initial-frame, end-frame, and whole-video features to identify the key causal factors V that most influence the action; (3) a Vision-Language Transformer (VLT) that aligns the causal feature vector with tokenized narration/reasoning text using an MLP projector and a sparse attention mask (regularized by L_sparse) to suppress hallucination. Training is supervised with a combined loss of signal regression, cross-entropy caption prediction, and KL-divergence regularization, optimized for 40 epochs on a single A100 GPU.

## Results

On BDD-X, MCAM achieves a BLEU-4 of 35.7 for narration and 9.1 for reasoning, outperforming the ADAPT baseline (33.4 / 8.2) and all other non-LLM baselines. On CoVLA, MCAM surpasses the baseline across all eight metrics, reaching BLEU-4 of 75.3, CIDEr of 275.4, and METEOR of 50.2. MCAM uses 885.3M parameters and runs at 336 FPS on BDD-X, substantially fewer parameters than the 7B+ LLM-based competitors (DriveGPT4, RAG-Driver) while matching or exceeding them on key metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCAM outperforms the ADAPT baseline on BDD-X narration BLEU-4 (35.7 vs. 33.4) and ROUGE (63.4 vs. 62.6), and on reasoning CIDEr (94.1 vs. 75.5) and ROUGE (32.0 vs. 30.7). Against the LLM-based RAG-Driver (7.08B params), MCAM wins on narration BLEU-4 (35.7 vs. 34.3) but trails on CIDEr (252.0 vs. 260.8) and METEOR (30.5 vs. 30.7); on reasoning, RAG-Driver leads on BLEU-4 (11.1 vs. 9.1), CIDEr (109.1 vs. 94.1), and METEOR (14.8 vs. 14.0). DriveGPT4 (7.85B) is outperformed by MCAM on narration BLEU-4 (35.7 vs. 30.0) and CIDEr (252.0 vs. 214.0), though DriveGPT4 edges ahead on reasoning BLEU-4 (9.4 vs. 9.1). On CoVLA, MCAM beats the ADAPT baseline on every reported metric, including BLEU-4 (75.3 vs. 74.2), CIDEr (275.4 vs. 236.9), and METEOR (50.2 vs. 48.8). The older S2VT, SAA, and WAA baselines are consistently outperformed by MCAM across all available metrics by 3–5 BLEU-4 points on narration and 2–3 points on reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

MCAM sits at the intersection of two research threads: causal representation learning (building on Pearl's structural causal models, front-door/back-door adjustments, and causal attention mechanisms) and ego-vehicle driving video captioning (ADAPT, S2VT, SAA, WAA, DriveGPT4, RAG-Driver). It departs from prior driving video understanding methods that rely on probabilistic correlations or retrieval-augmented matching by introducing a hand-constructed Driving State Directed Acyclic Graph (DSDAG) to explicitly model state transitions, actions, and environmental changes as causal nodes. Architecturally, it combines VidSwin (global) and 3DResNet (local) feature extraction with a causal analysis module and a vision-language transformer, positioning itself as a compact alternative to the 7B+ parameter LLM-based approaches (DriveGPT4, RAG-Driver) while claiming to be the first to embed structured causal analysis into ego-vehicle-level video understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a real gap—spurious correlations in driving video captioning—by proposing a structured causal graph, but the empirical gains are modest (1–2 BLEU-4 points over the ADAPT baseline on BDD-X, similar small deltas on CoVLA) while incurring a ~43% parameter increase and a noticeable FPS drop. The DSDAG is largely hand-specified rather than learned, limiting its generalizability to unseen driving scenarios, and the authors acknowledge dataset labeling errors as a confounding factor. Open questions include whether the causal structure can be discovered end-to-end, whether the framework scales to multi-vehicle or long-horizon reasoning, and whether the compact architecture can close the remaining gap to LLM-based methods without further parameter growth.
