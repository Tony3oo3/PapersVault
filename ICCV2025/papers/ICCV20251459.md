## Problem

Transformer-based VLMs suffer from quadratic sequence-length complexity, making them computationally expensive for training and inference. While Mamba-based VLMs achieve linear scaling, their sequential processing limits long-range dependency capture, causes slow convergence, and requires complex multi-stage training. The paper addresses how to retain transformer expressiveness for complex reasoning while gaining Mamba's efficiency in a single, practical training pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251459/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images at 384×384 resolution (processed by a SigLIP vision encoder) paired with free-form text prompts, and generates text responses autoregressively. A key practical detail is that MaTVLM sustains inference at 32,768 generated tokens where the teacher model (TinyLLaVA) hits out-of-memory, and the inference speedup grows with output length, reaching 3.6× at longer sequences. The hybrid ratio of Mamba-2 layers (12.5%, 25%, 50%) is a deployment knob that trades a small performance delta for additional speed and memory savings.

## Method

MaTVLM replaces a configurable fraction of transformer decoder layers in a pre-trained VLM with Mamba-2 layers (replacing only the attention sublayer, leaving FFN and other components intact), distributed at equal intervals. A key initialization insight derives from the equivalence between softmax-free attention and a linear RNN (Eq. 5–6): Mamba-2's input, B, and C projection weights are directly initialized from the corresponding V, K, Q attention weights, accelerating convergence. Training is a single-stage knowledge distillation where only the Mamba-2 layers and connector are trainable while all transformer layers remain frozen, guided by a combined loss of temperature-scaled KL divergence on output logits and layer-wise L2 alignment between student Mamba outputs and teacher transformer outputs. This avoids the multi-stage training pipelines typical of Mamba-based VLMs while achieving up to 4.3× inference speedup and 27.5% GPU memory reduction over the teacher.

## Results

MaTVLM-3.1B achieves 1484.1 on MME, 61.2 on MMBench, 57.7 on TextVQA, and 86.0 on POPE, while MaTVLM-4B reaches 1619.0 on MME, 82.2 on MMBench, and 91.0 on POPE. Across all three model sizes (2B, 3.1B, 4B), MaTVLM maintains performance within 1–5 points of its respective teacher model on most benchmarks, with notable gains on MME (+17.6 for 3.1B) and POPE (+0.1 for 4B). The method achieves up to 4.3× faster inference and a 27.5% reduction in peak GPU memory compared to the teacher TinyLLaVA, while the 25% Mamba-2 hybridization ratio yields the best average benchmark score (62.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Mamba-based VLMs, MaTVLM-3.1B dominates: it outperforms VL-Mamba by +114.5 on MME, +8.8 on TextVQA, and +5.3 on GQA, and beats ML-Mamba by +5.5 on TextVQA and +3.7 on VQAv2. The only losses to Mamba-based models are on POPE, where Cobra (88.2) and ML-Mamba (88.3) edge out MaTVLM-3.1B (86.0) by 2.2 and 2.3 points respectively. Versus similar-scale transformer VLMs, MaTVLM-3.1B leads LLaVA-Phi by +149 on MME and +9.1 on TextVQA, and surpasses MobileVLM 3B by +195 on MME and +10.2 on TextVQA. Compared to its teacher TinyLLaVA-3.1B, MaTVLM-3.1B gains +17.6 on MME but trails by 2–5 points on MMBench, TextVQA, SQA, and MMMU. MaTVLM-4B slightly exceeds its teacher InternVL2.5-4B on MMBench (+1.1) and POPE (+0.1) but falls short on MME (−51.2), TextVQA (−5.4), and MMMU (−5.9). Overall, MaTVLM trades a small performance gap (typically 1–5 points) on individual benchmarks for a 4.3× inference speedup and 27.5% memory reduction, while consistently outperforming all Mamba-only VLMs on the majority of evaluated benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hybrid Mamba-Transformer line of work from LLMs (MambaInLlama, MOHAWK) and vision backbones (MambaVision) to vision-language models, but departs from prior Mamba-based VLMs (Cobra, VL-Mamba, ML-Mamba) that replace the entire transformer LLM with a Mamba-2 LLM. Instead, MaTVLM performs partial layer replacement (12.5–50%) within a pre-trained VLM, leveraging the state-space duality framework of Mamba-2 to derive a principled initialization that maps attention Q/K/V weights onto Mamba-2's B/C/x projections. The single-stage distillation strategy with combined KL-divergence and layer-wise L2 losses further distinguishes it from multi-stage training pipelines used by prior Mamba-VLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4.3× inference speedup with under 2% average performance degradation and 27.5% memory reduction makes hybrid Mamba-Transformer VLMs practically deployable on resource-constrained hardware, addressing a key bottleneck for real-world multimodal applications. The attention-to-Mamba-2 weight initialization provides a theoretically grounded shortcut that could generalize to other SSM-Transformer hybrids beyond VLMs. However, the paper is limited to models up to 4B parameters, the optimal Mamba-2 ratio (25%) suggests diminishing returns from further replacement, and the authors acknowledge that the initialization may underutilize Mamba-2's implicit state representations—open questions include whether these findings scale to 7B+ models and whether more sophisticated initialization (e.g., gradient matching) could push the efficiency-performance frontier further.
