## Problem

Existing 3D medical image self-supervised learning methods partition volumes into fixed-size patches and treat all patches uniformly, ignoring anatomical structure variations in location, scale, and morphology. This leads to poor intra-structure semantic consistency (patches of the same organ not clustering together) and weak inter-structure discrepancy (patches of different organs not being sufficiently separated), as evidenced by similarity heatmap analyses on CT and MRI.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250900/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D medical image volumes (CT, MRI, PET) cropped into 96×96×96 sub-volumes, which are partitioned into non-overlapping patches processed by a Swin-B Transformer encoder. During pre-training, each sub-volume is paired with a geometrically augmented counterpart (affine transformation), and the model produces patch-level token features. These structure-aware representations are then transferred to downstream heads (Swin-UNETR for segmentation/I2I/reconstruction, Swin-B for classification) that output segmentation masks, class labels, translated images, or reconstructed images.

## Method

S²DC builds on MoCo's teacher-student contrastive framework (InfoNCE loss) and adds two structure-aware losses. First, patch-to-patch discrepancy establishes reliable one-to-one patch correspondences between two augmented views using a dual-softmax operator (inspired by neighbourhood consensus networks) applied to the token similarity matrix, then minimizes cross-entropy against ground-truth geometric correspondences derived from the known affine transformation, pushing semantically distinct patches apart. Second, patch-to-structure consistency computes a simplified Sharpe ratio for each patch's neighborhood similarity distribution; patches with dispersed distributions (indicating multiple semantically similar patches from the same structure) receive higher softmax-normalized weights on the patch-to-patch loss, adaptively enforcing intra-structure consistency. The total objective is L = L_g + L_p2p + L_p2s, and the framework is trained for 100k iterations across CT, MRI, and PET modalities.

## Results

On the BTCV segmentation benchmark (pre-trained on 1k CT), S²DC achieves 84.14% average Dice, surpassing the strongest baseline VoCo (83.85%) by 0.29% and the from-scratch Swin-UNETR (80.53%) by 3.61%. Across all 10 downstream datasets spanning 4 tasks and 3 modalities, S²DC attains an overall average of 81.43%, outperforming VoCo (80.65%) by 0.78% and from-scratch training (77.93%) by 3.5%. On multi-dataset segmentation (MSD-Liver/Lung/Spleen, AUTOPET, BraTs21), S²DC reaches 75.47% total Dice, a 0.81% gain over VoCo. On classification (CC-CCII + ADNI-cls), S²DC achieves 76.03% average accuracy versus 75.46% for VoCo.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S²DC outperforms all 18 compared baselines on the BTCV segmentation task, with the closest competitor being VoCo (83.85% vs. 84.14% avg Dice). The gap over VoCo is most pronounced on small/irregular structures: Esophagus (+2.09%), Pancreas (+3.36%), and Spleen (+0.51%), while large organs like Liver show a smaller margin (96.95% vs. 97.41%, where VoCo actually leads by 0.46%). On multi-dataset segmentation (Table 3), S²DC leads VoCo by 0.81% overall, with the largest gains on AUTOPET (46.47% vs. 45.10%) and MSD-Lung (64.40% vs. 63.34%). For classification, S²DC achieves 95.34% on CC-CCII and 56.72% on ADNI-cls, beating VoCo by 0.74% and 0.40% respectively. On I2I translation (BraTs23), S²DC improves SSIM by 2.41% (T1→T2) and 0.97% (T1→T1ce) over VoCo, and notably is the only method that improves over from-scratch on the T1→T2 task. On PET reconstruction (UDPET), S²DC achieves the best PSNR (58.46) and SSIM (0.99917), marginally ahead of SwinMM (58.40) and VoCo (58.26). Overall, S²DC consistently ranks first or second across all 10 datasets, with the average margin over the runner-up (VoCo) being 0.78%.

## Relation to Prior Work

S²DC builds on the MoCo/InfoNCE contrastive learning framework (He et al., 2020) and repurposes the dual-softmax neighbourhood consensus operator from Rocco et al. (2018) to establish reliable patch-to-patch correspondences under geometric equivariance (Mao et al., 2023). It departs from prior medical SSL methods (VoCo, SwinMM, PCRLv2, GVSL) that partition volumes into fixed-size patches and treat all patches uniformly, by introducing a structure-aware formulation: patches within the same anatomical structure should share semantics (consistency) while patches from different structures should be distinct (discrepancy). The patch-to-structure consistency loss, which uses a simplified Sharpe ratio to measure similarity-distribution dispersion as a soft regularizer, is a novel mechanism not present in prior mSSL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structure-aware perspective addresses a fundamental limitation in mSSL where fixed-size partitioning ignores anatomical variations in location, scale, and morphology, and the authors argue the principles extend to functional and metabolic imaging beyond anatomical CT/MRI. However, the performance gains over the strongest baseline (VoCo) are modest (~0.8% average across tasks), and the method relies on the geometric equivariance assumption that may not hold under large deformations or pathological anatomy. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger pre-training corpora, generalizes to 2D medical imaging, and whether the structure-aware assumption holds uniformly across all organ types and pathologies.
