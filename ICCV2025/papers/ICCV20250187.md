## Problem

Existing discriminative DIS methods rely on per-pixel classification and cannot flexibly handle user-specified targets when multiple foreground objects are present, nor can they adapt to variable patch sizes for local detail refinement. This paper addresses both gaps by recasting DIS as a conditional mask generation problem within a latent diffusion model, enabling seamless integration of language and window-based user controls.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250187/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a full RGB image (uniformly resized to 1024×1024 px) and a natural-language prompt as input for the macro mode, producing an initial binary segmentation mask. For the micro mode, the user selects one or more windows on the initial mask; the corresponding image patches and mask patches are cropped, upsampled to 1024×1024, and fed as diffusion starting points (rather than pure noise) to produce refined local masks that replace the original regions. All inputs pass through a shared VAE encoder into latent space before entering the U-Net.

## Method

LawDIS extends a pre-trained Stable Diffusion v2 model with a one-dimensional mode switcher (ψ_a for macro, ψ_b for micro) added to the time embeddings, enabling a single U-Net to serve both segmentation and refinement. In macro mode, CLIP-encoded language prompts are injected via cross-attention to condition the denoising process on user-specified objects; in micro mode, the latent features of the initial local mask replace Gaussian noise as the diffusion starting point, indirectly transferring global context into local refinement. The U-Net is jointly trained with both modes (sum of diffusion losses), then the VAE decoder is fine-tuned with shortcut connections and a single-channel output layer under weighted BCE + IoU supervision. Trajectory Consistency Distillation (TCD) compresses the 500-step DDPM sampling into a single step, enabling practical inference at 3.09 FPS on a single A100 GPU.

## Results

On the DIS5K benchmark (DIS-TE, 2000 images), LawDIS with both language-controlled segmentation and window-controlled refinement (Ours-R) achieves 0.908 F_β^ω, 0.932 F_β^mx, 0.024 M, 0.926 S_α, and 0.959 E_φ^mn, outperforming the runner-up MVANet (0.862 F_β^ω) by 4.6%. With only the language-controlled strategy (Ours-S), the model still achieves 0.898 F_β^ω, a 3.6% gain over MVANet. On the validation set (DIS-VD, 470 images), Ours-R reaches 0.905 F_β^ω and 0.929 F_β^mx, again leading all 11 compared methods across every metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LawDIS outperforms all 11 compared methods across every metric on both DIS-TE and DIS-VD. The closest competitor, MVANet [45], is surpassed by 4.6% in F_β^ω on DIS-TE with the full model (Ours-R: 0.908 vs. 0.862) and by 3.6% with only the language strategy (Ours-S: 0.898 vs. 0.862). BiRefNet [49] and InSPyReNet [16], the next-strongest baselines, trail Ours-R by 5.0% and 7.0% in F_β^ω on DIS-TE respectively. GenPercept [43], the only other diffusion-based method, lags by 9.2% in F_β^ω, suggesting that the macro-micro dual-mode design and VAE decoder fine-tuning are critical. Earlier discriminative methods (BASNet, U²Net, HRNet, PGNet, IS-Net, FP-DIS, UDUN) are all outperformed by more than 10% in F_β^ω. Notably, the WR strategy also functions as a general post-refinement tool, improving IS-Net by 6.4% and MVANet by 3.2% in F_β^ω on DIS-TE4 when applied to their existing predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

LawDIS departs from the dominant discriminative (per-pixel classification) paradigm used by prior DIS methods (IS-Net, InSPyReNet, BiRefNet, MVANet) by reformulating dichotomous segmentation as an image-conditioned mask generation task within a latent diffusion model, building directly on Stable Diffusion v2's VAE-U-Net architecture. It also draws on GenPercept's demonstration that diffusion models can be repurposed for dense perception, but extends this by introducing a mode switcher that unifies language-controlled segmentation (macro) and window-controlled refinement (micro) in a single network. For efficient inference, the paper adopts Trajectory Consistency Distillation (TCD) to collapse multi-step denoising into a single step, and uses CLIP for encoding user language prompts into the U-Net via cross-attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The controllable dual-mode design addresses a practical gap in DIS: existing methods cannot handle ambiguous multi-object scenes or adapt to variable patch sizes, as demonstrated by the significant performance drops (3.8–3.9%) when MVANet and BiRefNet are fed non-standard local patches. The WR strategy's ability to serve as a model-agnostic post-refinement tool (improving IS-Net by 6.4% in Fβ) suggests a broadly applicable component beyond LawDIS itself. Open questions include whether the generative formulation generalizes to other dense prediction tasks beyond DIS, whether truly user-authored (rather than VLM-generated) prompts maintain the reported gains, and whether the single-dataset evaluation (DIS5K) reflects performance on out-of-domain or medical imaging scenarios.
