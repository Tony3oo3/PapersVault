## Problem

Photoacoustic computed tomography (PACT) images are degraded by wavefront distortions caused by heterogeneous speed of sound (SOS) in tissue, but existing joint reconstruction methods are either computationally prohibitive (APACT requires 23 minutes via exhaustive search) or rely on oversimplified wavefront models that miss higher-order aberrations. Supervised learning is infeasible in this data-starved modality, and direct SOS measurement via ultrasound CT is expensive and impractical to combine with PACT. The paper addresses this gap with a self-supervised, fully differentiable framework that recovers both the SOS map and a clean initial pressure image from PA signals alone.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw PA signals from a ring array of N_t transducers along with a single background SOS value (measured from water temperature) and a user-defined set of M=16 delay parameters that generate a DAS 'focal stack' of aberrated reconstructions. It produces two outputs: a 2D speed-of-sound map (represented as either a pixel grid at image resolution or a SIREN neural field with only 1027 parameters) and a deconvolved initial pressure image assembled from overlapping patches merged with a 1.5 mm FWHM Gaussian window. The SOS representation is restricted to a circular mask matching the sample boundary, and the background SOS is fixed rather than learned.

## Method

The core contribution is a fully differentiable forward model that maps a coordinate-based SOS representation (pixel grid or SIREN neural field) through path-integral wavefront error computation to Fourier-domain PSFs, enabling end-to-end backpropagation of a self-supervised aberration-matching loss. For each image patch, the method computes a stack of PSFs across 16 delay channels and performs multi-channel deconvolution via a pseudo-inverse in the frequency domain, making the problem overdetermined and robust to per-channel artifacts. The SOS parameters are optimized by minimizing a loss that enforces consistency between predicted and observed aberrated images across all patches and delays, with |k| weighting for Fourier-domain fidelity and either TV regularization (pixel grid) or the NF's implicit smoothness (neural field). This test-time training requires no external dataset and converges in 10 epochs (NF) or 30 epochs (PG) on a single GPU, achieving state-of-the-art image and SOS quality in 40 seconds versus 23 minutes for APACT.

## Results

On 5 numerical phantoms, the NF variant achieves the best learned reconstruction quality with IP PSNR 25.08 and IP SSIM 0.519, while also recovering the SOS map with SOS PSNR 22.29 and SOS SSIM 0.931. The NF method completes reconstruction in 40.3 seconds, a 35× speedup over APACT (23 min). The PG variant achieves comparable IP quality (PSNR 25.05, SSIM 0.514) but produces stripe-like SOS artifacts. Providing the ground-truth SOS (oracle) yields only a marginal IP improvement (PSNR 25.61 vs. 25.08), indicating the learned SOS is near-optimal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Dual-SOS DAS [19], the NF method improves IP PSNR from 24.42 to 25.08 and IP SSIM from 0.446 to 0.519, while additionally recovering a full 2D SOS map that Dual-SOS DAS cannot produce. Against APACT [5], the NF method wins on every reported metric: IP PSNR (25.08 vs. 21.49), IP SSIM (0.519 vs. 0.434), SOS PSNR (22.29 vs. 17.74), and SOS SSIM (0.931 vs. 0.908), while running 35× faster (40.3 s vs. 23 min). The PG variant matches NF on IP quality but degrades on SOS SSIM (0.903 vs. 0.931) due to stripe-like artifacts from the path-integral wavefront computation. On real-data experiments (leaf phantom and in vivo mouse liver), the NF method qualitatively outperforms both baselines by resolving fine structures (leaf veins, blood vessels) and producing SOS maps that better match anatomical boundaries, whereas APACT oversmooths the SOS and Dual-SOS DAS cannot capture spatially varying heterogeneity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adaptive PACT (APACT) framework of Cui et al. (2021), which introduced wavefront-error-based aberration correction using isoplanatic patches and a simplified zeroth-plus-second-order Fourier wavefront model solved by exhaustive search. The key departures are: (1) replacing the truncated wavefront model with a fully differentiable path-integral forward model, (2) parameterizing the SOS with a coordinate-based neural field (SIREN) or pixel grid rather than a few Fourier coefficients, and (3) introducing a multi-delay 'focal stack' deconvolution that makes the inverse problem overdetermined and more robust. The NF parameterization draws on the broader computational-imaging literature on implicit neural representations, while the self-supervised test-time training paradigm avoids the data-hungry supervised approaches that struggle in this data-starved modality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 35× speedup over APACT (40 s vs. 23 min) combined with improved image and SOS quality makes aberration-corrected PACT practically viable for time-sensitive applications, and the self-supervised design eliminates the need for large paired training datasets that are unavailable for this modality. The finding that a 1,027-parameter SIREN outperforms a ~200,000-pixel grid for SOS recovery highlights the value of implicit regularization in ill-posed inverse problems. Open questions include scaling to 3D ring or full-sphere geometries, handling non-circular or moving samples, and whether the straight-ray wavefront model remains adequate for strongly scattering media. The PG representation's persistent stripe artifacts and the NF's sensitivity to network size (too small over-smooths, too large under-regularizes) suggest the SOS parameterization problem is not yet fully solved.
