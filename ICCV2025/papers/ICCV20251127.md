## Problem

Generating well-coordinated, music-aligned holistic 3D dance (body + hands + face) remains unsolved because existing datasets omit facial expressions or rely on noisy video-based pose estimation, current architectures lack capacity to model the hierarchical interdependencies among body, hands, and face, and no explicit cross-modal alignment mechanism exists to synchronize generated motion with musical rhythm and emotion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251127/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes raw music audio as input, encoded via a pretrained Jukebox encoder followed by a dedicated music encoder into a latent representation. The output is a full-body 3D dance sequence represented in SMPL-X parameters (body and hands) and FLAME parameters (52 ARKit-derived face blendshapes), spanning 55+52 joints/parameters at 60 FPS, with sequence length determined by the music duration. The motion is internally structured as V=5 layers of discrete tokens (body, hand, and face tokens concatenated per layer) that are decoded back to continuous pose space.

## Method

SoulNet combines three components: (1) Hierarchical Residual Vector Quantization (HRVQ), which extends RVQ with a body→hands→face chain where each component's residual quantization is conditioned on the preceding component's quantized output via a transformation T, enabling progressive refinement across V=5 layers while preserving spatial dependencies; (2) a Music-Aligned Generative Model (MAGM) that uses a transformer layer for masked primary-token prediction and V−1 residual layers for finer tokens, both conditioned on music features; (3) a pretrained Music-Motion Retrieval (MMR) module extending TEMOS with modality-specific encoders, trained via contrastive alignment losses that provide dual-granularity supervision—L_Align-body for local beat-level synchronization and L_Align-whole for global emotional coherence—during MAGM training. The Gumbel-Softmax trick enables gradient flow through the discrete VQ sampling, and the MMR module also constrains the motion space to reduce generation difficulty.

## Results

On the SoulDance dataset, SoulNet achieves a FID of 0.029, MMR-Matching Score of 0.369, and Emotion Alignment Score of 0.594, substantially outperforming all baselines (e.g., FACT: FID 1.008, MMR-MS 0.685, EAS 0.358). On AIST++, SoulNet achieves the best FID (0.081) and MMR-MS (0.580) while also being the fastest method at 0.086s runtime per sequence, compared to 1.782s for FACT. In the user study, SoulNet scores 7.33/10 on Whole Score and 7.78/10 on Alignment Score, exceeding the next-best method FineNet (6.44 and 6.56 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SoulNet decisively outperforms all four baselines on the SoulDance dataset across every metric: FID drops from 1.008 (FACT) to 0.029, MMR-MS improves from 0.685 (FACT) to 0.369, and EAS rises from 0.358 (FACT) to 0.594. On AIST++, SoulNet achieves the best FID (0.081 vs. 0.138 for FACT) and MMR-MS (0.580 vs. 0.702 for FACT), though it shows slightly lower diversity (1.649 vs. 3.836 for FineNet), which the authors attribute to the small size of AIST++. FineNet [32] is the closest competitor on hand-motion quality (FIDh 2.524 vs. SoulNet's 0.375), but SoulNet still dominates on overall FID and alignment metrics. EDGE [52] and Bailando [49] perform poorly on AIST++ FID (21.370 and 11.079 respectively), suggesting they struggle with generalization to body-only data. SoulNet is also the fastest method at 0.086s per sequence, roughly 20× faster than FACT (1.782s) on AIST++.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VQ-VAE/RVQ motion quantization lineage (Bailando, MoMask, T2M-GPT, DanceMeld) by introducing a hierarchical body-hand-face chain within the residual quantization structure, so that each modality's quantization is conditioned on the preceding one. For music-dance alignment, it adapts the contrastive retrieval paradigm from CLIP/TEMOS into a dedicated Music-Motion Retrieval module that serves as a training-time alignment prior rather than a standalone retrieval tool. On the data side, it departs from pose-estimation-based datasets (AIST++, PhantomDance) and prior mocap datasets that omit facial expressions (FineDance, Choreomaster) by capturing synchronized body, hand, and face data via professional optical mocap plus ARKit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SoulDance is the first publicly available holistic 3D dance dataset with synchronized body, hands, and facial expressions paired with music, filling a clear gap that has limited prior work to body-only or body+hand generation. The HRVQ formulation and the MMR alignment prior are practical, modular techniques that other motion-generation pipelines could adopt. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to group dance (the dataset includes group sequences but experiments focus on single-dancer), whether 52 ARKit blendshapes suffice for expressive facial animation beyond Ekman's 7 basic emotions, and whether the strong FID results on SoulDance (0.029) transfer to out-of-distribution music genres or longer sequences.
