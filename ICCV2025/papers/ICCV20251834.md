## Problem

Existing 3D multimodal large language models fail at complex spatial reasoning queries (e.g., 'the chair near the desk with a monitor on it') despite point clouds providing precise geometric cues, because they lack an explicit mechanism to decompose multi-object spatial relationships. Compounding this, current 3D datasets contain oversimplified and ambiguous spatial instructions that inadequately train or evaluate such reasoning capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251834/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 3D point cloud (300k randomly sampled points per scene) and a natural-language question or instruction. It produces a two-stage output: first, segmentation masks of target-relevant objects serving as visual reasoning priors; second, a refined text answer plus the final target segmentation mask triggered by a [SEG] token in the LLM output. The segmentation head operates on super-point features aggregated via mask-pooling, and the final mask is decoded from the hidden state of the [SEG] token against the full point-cloud feature map.

## Method

R²S (Relevant Reasoning Segmentation) decomposes spatial reasoning into two sequential stages that emulate human cognition. In Stage 1 (Reasoning Prior Learning), the model is prompted to segment all question-relevant objects; their super-point features are aggregated via mask-pooling to form visual reasoning priors. In Stage 2 (Prior-guided Refinement), these priors are injected into a revised instruction and fed back through the Q-Former alongside the original latent queries and full point-cloud features, enriching the queries with target-related spatial context before the frozen OPT-1.3B LLM generates the final answer and [SEG]-triggered mask. The architecture builds on LISA's [SEG] token mechanism with a OneFormer3D encoder/decoder and a 32-query Q-Former. The authors also introduce 3D ReasonSeg (25,185 train / 3,966 val samples), a dataset of complex reasoning questions generated by Llama 3.1 from SceneVerse object descriptions and 3D spatial metadata, with rule-based filtering to ensure unambiguous spatial relationships.

## Results

On ScanRefer, R²S achieves 59.5% Acc@25 and 48.7% Acc@50, surpassing the previous SOTA Grounded 3D-LLM (47.9% / 44.1%) by 11.6 and 4.6 points respectively. On ScanQA, R²S attains B-4=13.9, CIDEr=77.3, METEOR=16.4, and Rouge-L=38.1, outperforming LL3DA (13.5/76.8/15.9/37.3) and Chat-3D v2 (7.3/77.1/16.1/40.1). On the newly proposed 3D ReasonSeg benchmark, R²S achieves 33.1 gIoU, a 7.5-point improvement over Grounded 3D-LLM (25.6 gIoU), demonstrating substantially stronger spatial reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R²S outperforms all 14 compared baselines on every metric where both methods report results. On ScanRefer, R²S (59.5/48.7 Acc@25/50) surpasses the closest competitor ScanReason [42] (53.1/41.1) by 6.4 and 7.6 points, and Grounded 3D-LLM [9] (47.9/44.1) by 11.6 and 4.6 points. On ScanQA, R²S leads LL3DA [6] (13.5/76.8/15.9/37.3) across all four metrics, though Chat-3D v2 [17] slightly edges out R²S on Rouge-L (40.1 vs 38.1) while R²S dominates on CIDEr (77.3 vs 77.1) and METEOR (16.4 vs 16.1). On the 3D ReasonSeg benchmark, R²S achieves 33.1 gIoU versus 25.6 for Grounded 3D-LLM [9], a 7.5-point margin that highlights the benefit of explicit reasoning priors for complex spatial queries. The ablation in Table 3 further confirms that the Prior-guided Refinement step is essential: removing it drops gIoU from 33.1 to 27.5, and a text-only variant (without segmentation masks) only reaches 29.8, validating that instance-level visual features from segmentation are critical for disambiguating multiple objects of the same category.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LISA's instruction-tuning paradigm (SEG token + segmentation head) and the 3D MLLM architecture lineage (3D-LLM, Chat-3D, LL3DA, Grounded 3D-LLM), which pair a visual encoder with a Q-Former and LLM for scene-level understanding. The key conceptual departure is a two-stage reasoning mechanism—first segmenting question-relevant objects as visual priors, then refining latent queries conditioned on those priors—rather than the single-pass instruction-to-mask pipeline used by prior 3D MLLMs. The accompanying 3D ReasonSeg dataset is positioned as a remedy for the oversimplified and ambiguous spatial instructions in ScanRefer and ScanQA, which the authors argue inadequately exercise complex multi-object reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a concrete and underexplored gap: despite 3D point clouds encoding precise geometric information, current MLLMs still fail on instructions requiring multi-step spatial reasoning (e.g., 'the chair near the desk with a monitor'). The 3D ReasonSeg benchmark (25K train / 4K val samples with 5.4 average relevant objects vs. ~1.5–1.8 in prior datasets) provides a more demanding evaluation axis that the community can adopt. Limitations include residual noise from LLM-generated annotations, reliance on a single 1.3B LLM backbone, and evaluation restricted to indoor ScanNet scenes; whether the two-stage prior mechanism scales to larger LLMs or outdoor/embodied settings remains open.
