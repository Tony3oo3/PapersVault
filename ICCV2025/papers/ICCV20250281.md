## Problem

No existing video depth estimation method simultaneously achieves temporal consistency, high-resolution sharp boundaries, and real-time streaming processing. Optimization-based and diffusion-based methods are too slow for streaming, while lightweight online methods lack accuracy and cannot scale to 2K resolution without becoming at least 3.5× slower than real-time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250281/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes one RGB frame at a time in a streaming fashion (no batch or future-frame access), processing at 2044×1148 resolution at 24 FPS on a single A100 GPU. It outputs a per-frame depth map temporally aligned to a common scale across the entire sequence. When the input short side is below 518 pixels, the system defaults to running only the larger FlashDepth-L stream directly, since it can already operate in real-time at that resolution.

## Method

FlashDepth builds on Depth Anything v2 (DINOv2 ViT encoder + DPT decoder) and adds two key components. First, a lightweight Mamba recurrent module (1% of total parameters) is inserted between the DPT decoder and the final convolution head to align intermediate features to a consistent scale on-the-fly, with zero-initialized output to preserve the pretrained model at initialization. Second, a hybrid architecture runs a lightweight FlashDepth-S stream at full 2K resolution for sharp boundaries in parallel with a more accurate FlashDepth-L stream at 518px, fusing their intermediate DPT features via cross-attention (queries from S, keys/values from L). Training is two-stage: first, temporal modules are trained from scratch on ~500K synthetic image-depth pairs at 518×518 with L1 loss; then, the cross-attention fusion is trained on ~16K high-resolution 2K pairs while freezing FlashDepth-L and fine-tuning FlashDepth-S.

## Results

FlashDepth (Full) achieves an average δ₁ rank of 3.4 across five datasets, trailing only the concurrent VidDepthAny (rank 1.6), which processes 32 frames at once rather than streaming. On boundary sharpness (F1), FlashDepth (Full) scores 0.109 on Unreal4K and 0.185 on UrbanSyn at 2044×1148 resolution and 24 FPS, substantially outperforming all baselines (best: DAv2 at 0.058/0.118 at 924×518). The hybrid model matches VidDepthAny's 24 FPS throughput while operating at 2.2× higher resolution, and is 11× faster than DepthCrafter (24 vs. 2.1 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VidDepthAny (concurrent, processes 32 frames at once), FlashDepth (Full) trails on raw accuracy (avg δ₁ rank 3.4 vs. 1.6) but wins decisively on boundary sharpness (F1 0.109 vs. 0.049 on Unreal4K; 0.185 vs. 0.097 on UrbanSyn) and operates at 2.2× higher resolution (2044×1148 vs. 924×518) while maintaining the same 24 FPS. Against DepthCrafter (video diffusion, 110 frames at once), FlashDepth wins on accuracy (rank 3.4 vs. 4.6), sharpness (0.109 vs. 0.021 F1), and speed (24 vs. 2.1 FPS). Against CUT3R (streaming pointmap model), FlashDepth wins on accuracy (3.4 vs. 3.6), sharpness (0.109 vs. 0.007 F1), and resolution (2044×1148 vs. 512×288). Against the base DAv2 model, FlashDepth improves accuracy (rank 3.4 vs. 5.4) and sharpness (0.109 vs. 0.058 F1), confirming that the Mamba temporal module and hybrid cross-attention both contribute meaningfully. The ablation (Table 3) further shows that the hybrid model loses only 1–3% accuracy relative to FlashDepth-L, versus a 3–10% drop when simply using the smaller FlashDepth-S backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlashDepth builds directly on Depth Anything v2 (DAv2) as its single-image depth backbone, departing from the dominant video-diffusion-model paradigm (DepthCrafter, ChronoDepth) that finetunes expensive diffusion models for temporal consistency. For streaming consistency, it revives the recurrent-alignment idea from earlier online depth methods (LSTM-based approaches) but replaces them with a modern Mamba state-space module operating on downsampled DPT features. The hybrid small-at-full-resolution / large-at-low-resolution architecture with cross-attention fusion is a new design not present in prior single- or multi-frame depth models, motivated by the observation that accuracy is resolution-insensitive while boundary sharpness is not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FlashDepth is the first method to deliver real-time (24 FPS) streaming depth at 2K resolution on a single A100, unblocking applications in robotics, AR, and video editing that previously required either sacrificing resolution, sacrificing speed, or processing frames in non-streaming batches. The main acknowledged limitation is residual flickering, and the authors suggest a SLAM-like concurrent optimization of already-processed frames as a path forward. The released code, weights, and integration into Netflix's Eyeline VFX pipeline make this a practically adoptable tool, while the finding that a 1%-parameter Mamba module suffices for temporal scale alignment lowers the barrier for future work in streaming dense prediction.
