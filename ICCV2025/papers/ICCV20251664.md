## Problem

Automated model evaluation (AutoEval) for object detection remains underexplored: covariate-shift-based methods designed for classification cannot capture the spatial complexities (scale variation, occlusion, background clutter) that govern detection performance. The only prior detection-specific AutoEval method (BoS) is stochastic at test time, doubles inference cost via an extra dropout forward pass, ignores confidence scores, and relies on an augmentation-based meta-dataset that produces unrealistic images and a narrow mAP range.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251664/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single forward pass of a trained detector (RetinaNet or Faster R-CNN with ResNet-50 or Swin-T backbone) on unlabeled RGB test images, extracting both the post-NMS final predictions and the discarded pre-NMS candidate boxes with their confidence scores. It outputs two per-image scalar scores (consistency and reliability), which are averaged over a meta-dataset of 250 images and fed into a linear regression to produce a single estimated mAP value. The meta-dataset comprises 50 transformed versions of each source dataset, generated by applying 10 ImageNet-C corruptions at 5 severity levels.

## Method

PCR exploits the pre-NMS boxes that conventional detectors generate before NMS, which are normally discarded but retain localization information. The consistency score measures spatial alignment between each post-NMS box and a merged box enclosing all overlapping pre-NMS boxes, using both IoU and a center-closeness term for scale invariance; it is weighted toward low-confidence predictions via a sigmoid gate, capturing repeated mislocalization. The reliability score measures the proportion of overlapping pre-NMS boxes whose confidence exceeds a threshold, weighted toward high-confidence predictions, capturing consensus in both classification and localization. A simple linear regression over the two averaged scores maps them to mAP, trained via leave-one-out cross-validation on the corruption-based meta-dataset. The meta-dataset itself is a contribution: it uses ImageNet-C corruptions (gaussian noise, fog, blur, etc.) at graded severities rather than strong augmentations, yielding a broader and more realistic mAP distribution for benchmarking AutoEval methods.

## Results

On vehicle detection (10 source datasets), PCR achieves the lowest average RMSE of 5.03% and best average rank of 1.13 across four detectors (RetinaNet and Faster R-CNN with ResNet-50 and Swin-T backbones) and both meta-datasets. On pedestrian detection (9 source datasets), PCR achieves an average RMSE of 3.60% and a perfect average rank of 1.00. The advantage is most pronounced on the proposed corruption-based meta-dataset, where PCR's RMSE (e.g., 6.43 for RetinaNet+ResNet-50) is roughly half that of the strongest baseline BoS (13.50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCR outperforms all five baselines on every detector–meta-dataset combination for both vehicle and pedestrian detection. The closest competitor is BoS [45] (bounding-box stability via MC dropout), which achieves an average RMSE of 6.94% on vehicle detection and 6.22% on pedestrian detection, compared to PCR's 5.03% and 3.60% respectively. The classification-derived baselines (PS [20], ES [40], AC [15], ATC [10]) perform substantially worse, with average RMSEs ranging from 7.44% to 9.27% on vehicle detection, and their performance degrades sharply on the corruption-based meta-dataset (RMSEs of 10–15%). PCR's advantage is largest on the corruption-based meta-dataset, where BoS's stochastic dropout-based approach is particularly unreliable (e.g., 13.50% RMSE for RetinaNet+ResNet-50 vs. PCR's 6.43%). Combining PCR with BoS (Table 7) yields a further small improvement (5.04% vs. 5.66% avg. RMSE), confirming that the two methods capture complementary aspects of prediction quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AutoEval paradigm—previously studied almost exclusively for image classification (via confidence scores, entropy, feature distances, and prediction disagreement)—to object detection, following the initial foray by Yang et al. (BoS, ICLR 2024). It departs from BoS in three key ways: it avoids the stochastic MC-dropout forward pass by repurposing pre-NMS candidate boxes that are already generated in a single inference, it explicitly incorporates confidence scores to separate low-confidence (consistency) from high-confidence (reliability) predictions, and it replaces the augmentation-based meta-dataset with ImageNet-C-style corruptions of varying severity. The conceptual link to prior work on confidence calibration and Soft-NMS (which showed discarded boxes retain localization signal) is repurposed here as an evaluation signal rather than a detection refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PCR offers a practical, single-forward-pass AutoEval method for object detection that avoids the inference cost and stochasticity of dropout-based baselines, making it more deployable in real-world monitoring pipelines. The corruption-based meta-dataset is a useful community resource that covers a broader and more realistic mAP range than prior augmentation-based benchmarks. However, the evaluation is limited to vehicle and pedestrian detection with two detector families (RetinaNet, Faster R-CNN); whether the pre-NMS box strategy transfers to NMS-free architectures (e.g., DETR) or to other detection-adjacent tasks (segmentation, keypoint detection) remains open. The linear regression head is also a simple choice, and whether richer models or per-class scores could further improve estimation accuracy is unexplored.
