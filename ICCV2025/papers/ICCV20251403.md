## Problem

Existing VLM-based adversarial attacks on person re-identification rely on global image features or integral textual semantic representations, which overemphasize discriminative local features and produce less comprehensive disruption. Because re-id is a retrieval task where all pedestrian images share the same attribute vocabulary (clothing, hairstyle, shoes, etc.), it is possible to explicitly target fine-grained attribute-specific semantics — a granularity that is impractical to exploit in classification tasks where attributes differ per category. This paper addresses the gap by showing that destroying attribute-level textual embeddings yields significantly more transferable adversarial examples.

## Input / Output

The method takes clean pedestrian images (from DukeMTMC, Market-1501, MSMT17, or CUHK03) and produces adversarial pedestrian images with L∞-bounded perturbations (ε = 8/255 per pixel). A predefined prompt template with five attribute slots ('A photo of a person wearing S₁ on top, S₂ underneath, S₃ hairstyle, S₄ shoes, carrying S₅') is used to structure the textual side, where each Sᵢ is a learned pseudo-token. The adversarial generator is trained in a white-box setting on a surrogate model (IDE on DukeMTMC) and then applied unchanged to generate perturbations for unseen images to attack black-box target models.

## Method

AP-Attack operates in two stages. First, five three-layer fully-connected inversion networks are trained in CLIP-ReID's frozen joint vision-language space using a cycle-consistency contrastive loss, mapping global visual features to attribute-specific pseudo-tokens that are composed into the prompt template. Second, a prompt-driven semantic attack loss is applied per attribute: for each of the five semantic spaces, the adversarial pseudo-token is pushed away from its benign counterpart and pulled toward the least similar negative semantic in the batch, while a parallel triplet-style loss operates in the surrogate model's feature space. The adversarial generator (following the Mis-Ranking architecture) is optimized by the sum of these losses, producing perturbations that disrupt all attribute semantics simultaneously rather than relying on a single global representation. This fine-grained, multi-attribute disruption is the key differentiator from prior prompt-driven attacks like PDCL-Attack, which perturb only integral text embeddings.

## Results

In the most challenging cross-model&dataset attack setting (trained on DukeMTMC/IDE, tested on Market with 8 diverse architectures), AP-Attack achieves 21.2% aAP and 72.4% mDR, outperforming the strongest baseline PDCL-Attack (38.7% aAP, 49.5% mDR) by 17.5% on aAP and 22.9% on mDR. In cross-model attacks (same dataset, different architectures), AP-Attack reaches 6.9% aAP and 89.4% mDR, surpassing the next-best method Mis-Ranking (17.5% aAP, 73.1% mDR) by 10.6% and 16.3% respectively. In cross-dataset attacks (same architecture, different datasets), AP-Attack achieves 5.7% aAP and 89.9% mDR, edging out PDCL-Attack (6.6% aAP, 88.3% mDR). All experiments use ε=8/255 L∞ perturbations with the IDE surrogate model on DukeMTMC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AP-Attack outperforms all 8 baselines across every attack setting (cross-dataset, cross-model, and cross-model&dataset). Against the most recent prompt-driven method PDCL-Attack [54], AP-Attack achieves 17.5% lower aAP (21.2 vs. 38.7) and 22.9% higher mDR (72.4 vs. 49.5) in the cross-model&dataset scenario, with the gap attributed to fine-grained attribute-level disruption versus PDCL-Attack's reliance on global semantic features. Against the strongest re-id-specific attack MetaAttack [53], AP-Attack reduces aAP from 59.6% to 21.2% and increases mDR from 22.3% to 72.4% in cross-model&dataset, while using only a single training dataset (DukeMTMC) versus MetaAttack's multi-dataset meta-learning. Among generative attack methods, AP-Attack dominates GAP [36] (51.6→21.2 aAP), CDA [34] (53.7→21.2), LTP [40] (57.3→21.2), and BIA [63] (59.9→21.2) in cross-model&dataset, with mDR improvements of 39.7, 42.4, 47.1, and 50.5 percentage points respectively. Against the re-id-specific baselines Mis-Ranking [46] and MUAP [8], AP-Attack achieves 21.9% and 18.6% lower aAP with 28.6% and 24.3% higher mDR in cross-model&dataset. In the cross-model setting, AP-Attack's 89.4% mDR exceeds Mis-Ranking (73.1%) and PDCL-Attack (70.8%) by 16.3 and 18.6 percentage points, confirming consistent superiority across all evaluation protocols.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of VLM-guided adversarial attacks (GAMA, PDCL-Attack) that exploit CLIP's image-text alignment to improve transferability, but departs from their reliance on global or integral textual semantics by introducing attribute-aware textual inversion networks that decompose pedestrian appearance into five fine-grained semantic subspaces (top, bottom, hairstyle, shoes, accessories). The textual inversion technique itself originates from text-to-image personalization (Gal et al., 2023) and has been adapted to re-id for identity-level prompts (CLIP-ReID, PromptSG), but this is the first work to use it for attribute-specific semantic extraction to guide adversarial perturbation. The adversarial generator and surrogate-model loss follow the Mis-Ranking formulation, positioning the contribution primarily in the semantic-guidance component rather than the attack optimization framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The attribute-decomposition strategy offers a principled way to achieve more comprehensive feature disruption in retrieval-based tasks where all samples share a coarse category label, a setting where classification-oriented prompt attacks are ill-suited. The clean ablation progression (baseline 40.3% → global visual 63.6% → integral text 66.2% → fine-grained semantic 72.4% mDR) supports the core hypothesis that finer semantic granularity yields better transferability. Open questions include whether the fixed five-attribute template generalizes to other retrieval domains (e.g., vehicle re-id, object retrieval), whether the approach scales to more attributes or hierarchical attribute structures, and whether the reliance on CLIP's attribute understanding introduces a ceiling on disruption quality.
