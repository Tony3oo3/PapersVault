## Problem

Existing lightweight Mamba-based vision backbones (e.g., EfficientVMamba, QuadMamba, VMamba) fail to match the accuracy of Convolution- or Transformer-based models at similar scales, despite Mamba's linear-complexity global context modeling. The paper identifies through spectral analysis that Mamba under a Convolution-Mamba hybrid architecture predominantly captures low-frequency information while suppressing high-frequency details, and that simply modifying the 2D scanning path in the image domain does not fully exploit Mamba's potential for lightweight vision tasks.

## Input / Output

The model accepts standard RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet classification; standard COCO/ADE20K resolutions for detection and segmentation) and produces class labels, bounding boxes with class scores, per-instance segmentation masks, and pixel-wise semantic segmentation maps. Internally, the input is first processed by a two-layer 3×3 convolutional stem (stride 2) to yield H/4 × W/4 × D₁ feature maps, which are then progressively downsampled through four stages to H/32 × W/32 × D₄. The Laplace Mixer splits each stage's feature map along the channel dimension into a low-frequency branch (αD channels) and a high-frequency branch ((1−α)D channels), where α varies per stage.

## Method

TinyViM introduces a Laplace Mixer that decouples features into low- and high-frequency components via an efficient Laplace pyramid (average pooling + nearest-neighbor upsampling + subtraction). Only the downsampled low-frequency component is fed into the SS2D (2D selective scanning) Mamba block for global context modeling, while the high-frequency component is enhanced by a reparameterized 7×7 axial depth-wise convolution. A frequency ramp inception strategy progressively shifts channel allocation from high-frequency to low-frequency branches across stages (α = 0.25, 0.5, 0.5, 0.75), reflecting the observation that shallow layers need more high-frequency detail while deep layers benefit from more global low-frequency information. The overall architecture alternates Local Blocks (reparameterized 3×3 conv + FFN) and TinyViM Blocks (Laplace Mixer + FFN) across four stages, with patch-embedding layers between stages for channel expansion and spatial downsampling. This design reduces Mamba's input token count (since the low-frequency feature is spatially downsampled), yielding 2–3× higher throughput than comparable Mamba backbones while achieving higher accuracy.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, TinyViM-S (5.6M params, 0.9 GMACs) achieves 79.2% top-1 accuracy with 2563 img/s throughput, outperforming SwiftFormer-S (78.5%) and EfficientVMamba-T (76.5%) at similar compute. TinyViM-B (11.0M, 1.5 GMACs) reaches 81.2% top-1, surpassing MobileOne-S4 (79.4%) and MSVMamba-M (79.8%). TinyViM-L (31.7M, 4.7 GMACs) achieves 83.3% top-1, beating VMamba-T (82.6%), FastViT-SA24 (82.6%), and EfficientVMamba-B (81.8%). On MS-COCO detection, TinyViM-L achieves 44.5 AP^box and 40.7 AP^mask; on ADE20K semantic segmentation, it reaches 44.2 mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TinyViM-S (79.2%) outperforms all Mamba-based models at similar scale: EfficientVMamba-T (76.5%, +2.7%), Vim-Ti (76.1%, +3.1%), MSVMamba-N (77.3%, +1.9%), and QuadMamba-Li (74.2%, +5.0%), while also beating the CNN+ViT hybrid SwiftFormer-S (78.5%, +0.7%) with 2× higher throughput (2563 vs. 2626 img/s is comparable, but TinyViM-S has fewer GMACs). At the medium scale, TinyViM-B (81.2%) surpasses MobileOne-S4 (79.4%, +1.8%), Agent-PVT-T (78.4%, +2.8%), and MSVMamba-M (79.8%, +1.4%), with 1.7× the throughput of EfficientVMamba-S. At the large scale, TinyViM-L (83.3%) beats VMamba-T (82.6%, +0.7%), FastViT-SA24 (82.6%, +0.7%), EfficientVMamba-B (81.8%, +1.5%), and MobileMamba-B4 (82.5%, +0.8%), while maintaining 1.5× higher throughput than EfficientVMamba-B. On MS-COCO detection, TinyViM-B achieves 42.3 AP^box vs. SwiftFormer-L1's 41.2 (+1.1) and EfficientVMamba-S's 39.3 (+3.0); on ADE20K segmentation, TinyViM-L reaches 44.2 mIoU vs. SwiftFormer-L3's 43.9 (+0.3) and FastViT-SA24's 41.0 (+3.2). The consistent throughput advantage (2–3× over other Mamba models) is a key differentiator, making TinyViM the strongest performance-efficiency trade-off among lightweight backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Mamba's selective state space model and VMamba's SS2D scanning to construct a lightweight vision backbone, but departs fundamentally from prior vision Mamba work (Vim, VMamba, EfficientVMamba, QuadMamba, MSVMamba) that focuses on modifying scanning paths in the image domain. The key conceptual departure is a frequency-domain analysis showing that Mamba under a Conv-Mamba hybrid primarily captures low-frequency information, motivating a Laplace-pyramid-based decoupling where only low-frequency components are fed to Mamba while high-frequency details are handled by reparameterized depth-wise convolutions. The frequency ramp inception further draws on the well-known observation that shallow layers need more high-frequency detail while deep layers benefit from global (low-frequency) context, but applies it specifically to channel allocation between the two branches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frequency decoupling insight provides a principled explanation for why prior lightweight Mamba backbones underperformed CNN and Transformer counterparts: feeding full-resolution features to Mamba wastes capacity on high-frequency components it suppresses anyway, while also inflating computational cost. The practical impact is significant for real-time deployment, with 2–3× throughput over comparable Mamba models at equal or better accuracy. Open questions include whether the frequency-ramp heuristic generalizes to other SSM variants (e.g., H3, GSS), whether the Laplace-pyramid decoupling transfers to video or multimodal settings (the authors mention SAM as a future direction), and whether the specific 0.25/0.5/0.5/0.75 channel split is robust across architectures or requires per-model tuning.
