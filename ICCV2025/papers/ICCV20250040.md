## Problem

Cross-domain image composition—merging a foreground subject into a stylistically different background—remains under-explored because existing methods rely heavily on detailed text prompts to specify style and content, which fails for styles that are visually perceptible but linguistically inexpressible. Additionally, prevailing dual-branch diffusion pipelines are computationally expensive and introduce artifacts from the stochastic inversion of separate foreground and background latents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250040/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a background image, a foreground image with corresponding binary masks, and an optional (often empty) text prompt. Background images are resized so the longest edge is 1024 pixels to match the SDXL backbone. The output is a single composed image in which the foreground subject is implicitly stylized to harmonize with the background while preserving its identity, produced in only 10 diffusion steps per direction rather than the standard 20.

## Method

AIComposer replaces the conventional dual-branch inversion with a single-branch pipeline: the foreground and background are first blended in latent space via AdaIN tone matching, then jointly inverted and reconstructed over fewer steps (10 vs. 20) to preserve the diffusion prior. A lightweight 3-layer MLP (25.45M parameters) is trained on 30,000 content–style–stylized triplets to integrate CLIP image features, combining foreground content with background style in a residual formulation that leverages the additive property of CLIP embeddings. During denoising, a rectified cross-attention mechanism injects the integrated image features and optional text features only within a dilated foreground mask, while AdaIN is applied in the first 5 steps to fine-tune color tone and unmasked background regions are replaced with their inverted latents to prevent drift. The entire diffusion process is training-free and tuning-free; only the small MLP requires supervised training.

## Results

On the extended cross-domain benchmark, AIComposer achieves LPIPS 0.4195, CSD 0.5283, PSNR 19.48, and CLIP-T 29.29, outperforming all baselines on every metric. The headline improvements are a 30.5% reduction in LPIPS (vs. the best baseline AnyDoor at 0.6036) and an 18.1% increase in CSD (vs. the best baseline BLD at 0.4473). Notably, the no-text-prompt variant (Ours-NP) retains nearly identical performance (LPIPS 0.4221, CSD 0.5294), confirming the method's ability to handle any style and content without textual descriptions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AIComposer outperforms all four baselines on every metric in the extended benchmark. Against TF-ICON [30] (the closest cross-domain competitor), it reduces LPIPS from 0.6707 to 0.4195 (−37.5%), raises CSD from 0.4013 to 0.5283 (+31.6%), and improves PSNR from 13.62 to 19.48 dB. Against AnyDoor [5], which is the strongest baseline on LPIPS (0.6036) and CLIP-T (29.02), AIComposer still leads by 30.5% on LPIPS and 78.3% on CSD (0.5283 vs. 0.2963), while matching CLIP-T within 0.27 points. Paint by Example [63] and Blended Latent Diffusion [2] are same-domain methods that struggle with style consistency, yielding CSD scores of 0.3175 and 0.4473 respectively—far below AIComposer's 0.5283. On the smaller TF-ICON baseline benchmark, AIComposer wins on LPIPS, CSD, and PSNR but trails Paint by Example by 0.73 points on CLIP-T, which the authors attribute to copy-paste-style methods scoring higher on text alignment without genuine style adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SDXL + IP-Adapter ecosystem for diffusion-based image composition, extending the line of training-free cross-domain composition methods (TF-ICON, TALE, PrimeComposer) that rely on dual-branch diffusion and explicit text prompts. The key departure is a single-branch, fewer-step pipeline that inverts a pre-blended latent rather than separate foreground/background latents, combined with a simple 3-layer MLP that integrates CLIP image features from foreground (content) and background (style) to replace the text prompt entirely. The approach is informed by AdaIN for tone harmonization and by CSGO's content-style-stylized triplets for MLP training data, but the central conceptual contribution—demonstrating that style and content are approximately linearly separable in CLIP image feature space via LDA—has no direct precedent in the composition literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the text prompt dependency, this work addresses a practical bottleneck: many real-world styles and objects resist precise verbal description, making prompt-based composition unreliable. The introduced extended benchmark (367 backgrounds × 89 foregrounds across diverse styles) fills a notable gap in evaluation infrastructure for cross-domain composition. Limitations include inheriting biases from the frozen SDXL backbone, non-real-time inference (~13s per image), and stochastic output variability; the linear-separability claim rests on a single LDA experiment with 80 images per class, leaving open whether it generalizes to more nuanced or overlapping style/content boundaries. Future work could explore whether the MLP integration transfers to newer diffusion architectures or whether the separability property holds in other embedding spaces.
