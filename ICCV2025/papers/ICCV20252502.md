## Problem

Existing 3D vision-language models rely on static, pre-computed 3D reconstructions (meshes or point clouds) and cannot actively explore partially observable environments, while RL-based embodied agents can explore but lack explicit spatial representations and semantic grounding. Bridging passive visual grounding with active frontier-based exploration in a single end-to-end framework remains an open challenge for building versatile embodied navigation agents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252502/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes streaming RGB-D observations (360×640 RGB images, depth maps, and SE(3) camera poses) in an online fashion without requiring explicit 3D reconstruction, alongside diverse goal specifications including natural language descriptions, reference images, object categories, task plan sequences, and questions. It outputs a unified decision score over both object queries and frontier points, producing either a grounded 3D bounding box with instance mask or a frontier waypoint to navigate to, which feeds into a trajectory planner for the next perception-action cycle.

## Method

MTU3D extracts per-frame local queries by fusing DINO pixel features pooled over FastSAM segments with sparse convolutional U-Net 3D features, then merges them into a dynamic spatial memory bank via IoU-based matching with exponential moving average fusion. A spatial reasoning transformer jointly scores object queries (from memory) and frontier queries (from an occupancy map) against the goal embedding (CLIP text or image), selecting the highest-scoring candidate to either ground an object or navigate to an unexplored region. Training proceeds in three stages: low-level perception (instance segmentation loss on queries), Vision-Language-Exploration pre-training over 1M+ trajectories from HM3D simulation and ScanNet real-world data using a trajectory mixing strategy that blends expert and noisy exploration, and task-specific fine-tuning. This eliminates the need for pre-computed 3D reconstructions and unifies grounding and exploration under a single differentiable objective.

## Results

On GOAT-Bench (multi-modal lifelong navigation), MTU3D achieves 52.2% SR and 30.5% SPL in Val Seen, 47.2% SR and 27.7% SPL in Val Unseen, outperforming the best baseline (SenseAct-NN Skill Chain) by 23.0% in SR. On HM3D-OVON (open-vocabulary navigation), MTU3D reaches 55.0% SR (Val Seen) and 40.8% SR (Val Unseen), surpassing the previous best (Uni-NaVid at 39.5% Val Unseen SR) by 13.7%. On SG3D (sequential task navigation), MTU3D achieves 23.8% s-SR and 16.5% SPL, nearly doubling the best prior method. On A-EQA, MTU3D-enhanced GPT-4o reaches 51.1% LLM-SR and 42.6% LLM-SPL, a 29.5% improvement in LLM-SPL over baseline GPT-4V.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MTU3D decisively outperforms all modular and RL-based baselines across every benchmark. On GOAT-Bench, it surpasses SenseAct-NN Skill Chain (the strongest prior method) by 23.0% in SR (52.2% vs. 29.2%) and by 17.7% in SPL (30.5% vs. 12.8%), and beats Modular GOAT by 25.9% in SR. On HM3D-OVON, MTU3D leads Uni-NaVid by 13.7% in Val Unseen SR (40.8% vs. 39.5%) and by 14.8% in Val Seen SR (55.0% vs. 41.3%), though Uni-NaVid retains an SPL advantage (19.8% vs. 12.1% Val Unseen) due to its video-based direct navigation on short trajectories. On SG3D, MTU3D nearly doubles the s-SR of Embodied Video Agent (23.8% vs. 14.7%) and SenseAct-NN Monolithic (23.8% vs. 12.1%). On A-EQA, MTU3D as a trajectory generator boosts GPT-4V's LLM-SPL from 7.5% to 37.0% and GPT-4o's to 42.6%, demonstrating that its exploration strategy is far more efficient than exhaustive search. TANGO, a training-free agent, is outperformed by MTU3D on both HM3D-OVON (40.8% vs. 35.5% Val Unseen SR) and GOAT-Bench (47.2% vs. 32.1% Val Unseen SR).

## Relation to Prior Work

MTU3D sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: 3D vision-language grounding models (PQ3D, 3DVLP, LEO) that operate on pre-computed static 3D reconstructions, and RL-based embodied agents (PIRLNav, VER) that explore but lack explicit spatial-semantic representation. The paper departs from both by replacing explicit 3D reconstruction with online query-based representation learned directly from RGB-D streams (building on EmbodiedSAM's streaming segmentation and PQ3D's promptable query decoder), and by unifying object grounding and frontier-based exploration into a single decision objective rather than treating them as separate pipeline stages. The trajectory data collection strategy draws on PIRLNav's imitation+RL hybrid, while the spatial memory bank extends EmbodiedSAM's online tracking into a persistent, goal-conditioned structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit 3D reconstruction is not a prerequisite for strong embodied grounding, which is practically significant for real-world deployment where complete scene geometry is rarely available. The dramatic spatial memory ablation (SR jumping from ~10% to ~52% for object goals on GOAT-Bench) underscores that lifelong memory is the critical bottleneck in multi-modal navigation, not perception alone. Open questions remain: the SPL gap versus video-based methods on short-horizon OVON episodes suggests the exploration overhead may not always pay off, the local trajectory planner is still a classical Habitat-Sim shortest-path module rather than learned, and scalability of the spatial memory bank to very large or multi-episode environments is not thoroughly stress-tested.
