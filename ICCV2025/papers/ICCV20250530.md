## Problem

Editing real images with pre-trained text-to-image flow models typically relies on an inversion step that maps the image to a noise vector and back, but this editing-by-inversion paradigm produces poor structure preservation even when the inversion is exact. Existing remedies—improving inversion accuracy or injecting internal model representations during sampling—are either insufficient or tightly coupled to specific architectures and sampling schemes, limiting transferability to newer flow-based models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250530/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a real RGB image (1024×1024 in experiments), a source text prompt describing the image, and a target text prompt specifying the desired edit, and outputs a single edited image. The source prompt has minimal effect on results and can be omitted entirely. The method is applied to pre-trained, frozen flow models (FLUX.1 dev and SD3 medium) with no fine-tuning, using a small number of discretization steps (28 for FLUX, 50 for SD3) and a single noise draw per timestep (n_avg=1) in practice.

## Method

FlowEdit reinterprets editing-by-inversion as a direct ODE path between the source and target conditional distributions, then constructs a shorter alternative path that avoids traversing through the Gaussian noise distribution. At each timestep t, the method adds fresh random noise to the source image to form a noisy source point, uses a parallelogram relation to derive the corresponding noisy target point, computes the velocity difference V^tar − V^src at those two points, and averages this direction over multiple noise draws to obtain the ODE update. The resulting ODE is solved with a monotonically decreasing timestep schedule and a fixed step size equal to the discretization interval, requiring no optimization, no access to model internals, and no architectural assumptions. A parameter n_max controls edit strength by skipping the first timesteps, analogous to partial inversion in prior methods.

## Results

On a synthetic cat-to-dog editing benchmark (1000 SD3-generated images), FlowEdit achieves a transport cost of 1376 MSE and 0.15 LPIPS, substantially lower than exact ODE inversion (2239 MSE, 0.25 LPIPS), confirming superior structure preservation. FlowEdit also better aligns with the target distribution, achieving FID 51.14 and KID 0.017 versus inversion's 55.88 and 0.023. On the broader DIV2K-based evaluation (70+ real images, 250+ prompt pairs) with both SD3 and FLUX, Figure 7 shows FlowEdit occupies a favorable Pareto region between LPIPS (structure preservation) and CLIP (text adherence), outperforming all five baselines on this balance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowEdit outperforms all five baselines on the joint structure-preservation / text-adherence trade-off shown in Figure 7. Against ODE Inversion [Wu, ICCV 2023], FlowEdit reduces transport cost by ~38% in MSE (1376 vs. 2239) and ~40% in LPIPS (0.15 vs. 0.25) on the cat-to-dog benchmark, while also improving FID and KID. SDEdit [Meng, ICLR 2022] can preserve structure at low edit strengths but produces weak edits that fail to fully adhere to the target prompt; at higher strengths it degrades structure. RF-Inversion [Rout, ICLR 2025] can modify text content (e.g., changing a gas-station sign) but fails to maintain the overall scene structure. iRFDS [Yang, ICLR 2025] partially preserves structure but does not fully realize the target edit (e.g., incomplete sign text change). RF Edit [Wang, arXiv 2024] shows similar limitations to RF-Inversion. FlowEdit is the only method that simultaneously achieves strong text adherence and high structure preservation across both SD3 and FLUX backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from the dominant editing-by-inversion paradigm (DDIM inversion, Null-text inversion, RF-Inversion) and from feature-injection methods (Prompt-to-Prompt, MasaCtrl) that patch up inversion's structural failures. Conceptually, it builds on the rectified flow framework and the ODE formulation of diffusion/flow sampling, but its key move is a reinterpretation: showing that inversion-based editing is equivalent to a direct ODE between source and target distributions, then proposing a shorter direct path that never traverses the Gaussian noise space. It also positions itself against optimization-based flow editing (iRFDS/DDS/PDS), arguing that its velocity-difference update is not an optimization step despite superficial similarity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FlowEdit offers a remarkably simple, training-free, model-agnostic editing recipe (a few lines of code, n_avg=1 in practice) that achieves state-of-the-art structure preservation across both FLUX and SD3 without any intervention in model internals, making it immediately transferable to future flow architectures. The demonstrated transport-cost reduction on synthetic data (with exact inversion as baseline) provides a clean geometric justification for why the method works. A key limitation is that the strong structure preservation becomes a constraint when large-scale modifications (pose changes, background replacement) are desired, and the method remains heuristic rather than a principled optimal-transport solution. Open questions include whether the random-noise averaging can be replaced by a more principled coupling, and how the method extends to video or 3D editing.
