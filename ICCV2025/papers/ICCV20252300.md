## Problem

Existing image-to-point cloud registration methods rely on metric learning (contrastive/triplet losses) to force cross-modal feature alignment, but this ignores the inherent modality gap: 2D backbones struggle to capture 3D geometry from images, while colorless point clouds lack texture cues. Additionally, the standard matching-then-PnP pipeline is non-differentiable (argmax in correspondence retrieval, RANSAC in transformation estimation), preventing end-to-end gradient-based optimization of the registration network.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252300/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (480×640) and a 3D point cloud as input and outputs a rigid transformation [R|t] aligning the point cloud to the camera coordinate system, along with refined point-pixel correspondences. During training, the predicted transformation is used to project the point cloud into a sparse depth map, which is densified via morphological operations to serve as a ControlNet conditioning signal for the diffusion model. At inference, the diffusion model is discarded entirely, and only the distilled ResNet/KPConv backbones and DCT module are used, yielding 0.074 s runtime and 3.1 GB VRAM.

## Method

Diff²I2P constructs a fully differentiable registration pipeline with three key components. First, a Deformable Correspondence Tuning (DCT) module predicts per-correspondence 3D point offsets via an MLP on concatenated image-point features and coordinates, replacing the non-differentiable argmax matching with a differentiable refinement step. Second, a differentiable BPnP solver estimates the rigid transformation from the offset-adjusted correspondences. Third, a Control-Side Score Distillation (CSD) loss projects the transformed point cloud into a depth map, feeds it as ControlNet conditioning alongside the input image into a pretrained depth-conditioned diffusion model, and computes an SDS-style denoising loss whose gradient (with the large UNet Jacobian omitted) backpropagates through the transformation to the feature backbones. This distills 2D texture and 3D geometry knowledge from the diffusion model into the registration network, bridging the modality gap without requiring the diffusion model at test time.

## Results

On 7-Scenes, Diff²I2P achieves a Registration Recall (RR) of 83.0%, surpassing the strongest baseline 2D3D-MATR (75.8%) by 7.2 percentage points, with an Inlier Ratio (IR) of 53.2% and Feature Matching Recall (FMR) of 92.2%. On RGB-D Scenes V2, it reaches 60.5% RR versus 56.9% for 2D3D-MATR, with IR of 36.9% and FMR of 77.1%. At inference, Diff²I2P runs in 0.074 s with 3.1 GB VRAM, compared to FreeReg's 9.634 s and 14.2 GB, while achieving 11.8% higher RR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Diff²I2P outperforms all four training-based baselines (FCGF-2D3D, P2-Net, Predator-2D3D, 2D3D-MATR) on every metric across both 7-Scenes and RGB-D Scenes V2. The most direct comparison is with 2D3D-MATR [25], the prior SOTA: Diff²I2P gains +7.2% RR on 7-Scenes (83.0% vs 75.8%) and +3.6% RR on RGB-D Scenes V2 (60.5% vs 56.9%), with the largest per-scene gain being +21.9% RR on the challenging Heads scene. Against the diffusion-based FreeReg [54], Diff²I2P achieves 11.8% higher RR (83.0% vs 71.2%) while being ~130× faster at inference (0.074 s vs 9.634 s) and using 4.6× less VRAM (3.1 GB vs 14.2 GB), since the diffusion model is only used during training for distillation. The older baselines FCGF-2D3D, P2-Net, and Predator-2D3D trail by 17–35% in RR on 7-Scenes, confirming that the differentiable CSD supervision and DCT module provide a substantial advantage over purely metric-learning-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the correspondence-based I2P registration pipeline of 2D3D-MATR [25] by making the entire registration process differentiable through a Deformable Correspondence Tuning module and the BPnP solver [3], enabling end-to-end gradient flow. Its key conceptual departure from prior metric-learning-based methods (which forcibly align cross-modal features via contrastive/triplet losses) is the use of a depth-conditioned diffusion model (ControlNet [62]) as a training-time teacher, distilling cross-modal knowledge via a novel Control-Side Score Distillation loss derived from SDS [35]. Rather than directly supervising the transformation with ground-truth correspondences, CSD exploits the diffusion model's sensitivity to depth-image misalignment as an implicit registration signal, a repurposing of SDS from 3D generation to cross-modal alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that diffusion priors can effectively bridge the modality gap in I2P registration while keeping inference cost identical to non-diffusion baselines (0.074s, 3.1 GB VRAM), addressing a key practical limitation of prior diffusion-based registration like FreeReg. The 7.2% RR improvement over 2D3D-MATR on 7-Scenes and consistent gains on RGB-D Scenes V2 suggest that the modality gap, long treated as an inherent obstacle, can be substantially mitigated through generative-model distillation. Open questions include generalization to outdoor/dynamic scenes (only briefly addressed via KITTI in supplementary), whether the heavy diffusion teacher can be replaced by lighter surrogates, and whether the differentiable registration framework extends to other cross-modal pairs (e.g., LiDAR-to-image in autonomous driving).
