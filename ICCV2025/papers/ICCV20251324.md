## Problem

Diffusion models struggle to generate morphologically accurate images of specific animal species, particularly when training samples per species are scarce. Existing approaches treat each species as an independent category, ignoring the rich hierarchical visual similarities encoded in biological taxonomy, where closely related species share body structures, layouts, and poses with distinctions often limited to subtle shape, color, or pattern variations.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a taxonomic name as a text prompt at any level of the seven-rank hierarchy (Kingdom through Species), processed by a frozen CLIP text encoder producing up to 77 token embeddings. It outputs a fine-grained animal image generated via 250 denoising steps with a guidance scale of 6. A key design choice is that the model can be conditioned at any single taxonomic level during inference, enabling trait discovery by visualizing shared features at higher ranks.

## Method

TaxaDiffusion progressively trains a Stable Diffusion v1.5 backbone across taxonomic levels from Kingdom down to Species, using LoRA for initial domain adaptation and trainable two-layer transformer modules as level-specific conditioning heads. At each training stage, only the current level's module is active while all previously trained modules are frozen, and their output embeddings are summed to form a cumulative conditioning signal fed into the diffusion model. At inference, the standard Classifier-Free Guidance is modified by replacing the unconditional score estimate with the Kingdom-level conditioning signal, so that guidance is anchored to the broadest taxonomic prior rather than a null condition. This hierarchical cascade ensures the model first learns shared morphological traits before refining species-specific distinctions, enabling effective knowledge transfer to rare species with as few as one training sample.

## Results

On FishNet, TaxaDiffusion achieves FID of 41.92 (Order) and 31.87 (Species), substantially outperforming zero-shot Stable Diffusion (74.98/61.93) and fully fine-tuned SD (50.72/39.41). For taxonomy-image alignment, TaxaDiffusion scores 15.06 BioCLIP at Order and 10.43 at Species, versus 7.48/3.35 for zero-shot SD. Against the state-of-the-art FineDiffusion on iNaturalist, TaxaDiffusion reduces FID from 74.80 to 43.71 and improves BioCLIP from 6.46 to 8.15, despite using a U-Net backbone rather than DiT-XL/2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TaxaDiffusion outperforms all three baselines across every metric and taxonomic level. Against zero-shot Stable Diffusion [37], TaxaDiffusion reduces FID by 33–44 points (e.g., 41.92 vs. 74.98 at Order) and more than doubles BioCLIP scores (15.06 vs. 7.48 at Order; 10.43 vs. 3.35 at Species), showing that vanilla SD cannot capture fine-grained species morphology. Against SD+LoRA [14], TaxaDiffusion improves FID by 14–18 points (e.g., 27.35 vs. 45.41 at Genus) and BioCLIP by 6–11 points, indicating that progressive taxonomy conditioning is far more effective than uniform LoRA fine-tuning even when both receive the same hierarchical labels. Against FineDiffusion [29] on iNaturalist, TaxaDiffusion achieves FID 43.71 vs. 74.80 (a 41% reduction) and BioCLIP 8.15 vs. 6.46, despite FineDiffusion using the more scalable DiT-XL/2 architecture; this gap is attributed to TaxaDiffusion's seven-level progressive hierarchy versus FineDiffusion's two-level (superclass/subclass) conditioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

TaxaDiffusion builds on the Stable Diffusion v1.5 (U-Net) architecture with CLIP text encoders and LoRA-based domain adaptation, but departs from standard fine-grained generation methods (sketch control, attribute manipulation, personalized fine-tuning) by introducing a progressive, taxonomy-level-by-level training schedule with sequential parameter freezing. It is most directly comparable to FineDiffusion [29], which also leverages taxonomic hierarchy but only at two coarse levels (superclass/subclass) on a DiT-XL/2 backbone; TaxaDiffusion extends this to a full seven-level hierarchy and adds a modified classifier-free guidance that replaces the unconditional branch with the Kingdom-level conditioning signal. The progressive training philosophy echoes hierarchical VAEs (VQ-VAE-2, NVAE) and HI-Diff, but applies the idea to the *training schedule* of a diffusion model rather than to its architecture or latent space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that incorporating structured biological domain knowledge (taxonomy) into the training procedure of a diffusion model enables morphologically accurate generation even for species with as few as one training sample, opening a practical path for biodiversity documentation and trait discovery. A key open question is whether the progressive-training benefit generalizes beyond the U-Net/SD-v1.5 setting to scalable DiT architectures, which the authors themselves flag as a limitation. The ablation showing that random-level training achieves a slightly lower FID than progressive training (Table 4) suggests the advantage of the hierarchical schedule may be more about taxonomy-image alignment (BioCLIP) than raw image quality, a nuance worth further investigation.
