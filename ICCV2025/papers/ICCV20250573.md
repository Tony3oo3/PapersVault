## Problem

Existing reflective flare removal methods primarily target in-focus flares (bright, regular spot patterns) and struggle with out-of-focus flares, which are diffuse, semi-transparent, and spread unevenly across large image areas. The absence of a dedicated out-of-focus flare dataset has further hindered progress, as conventional inpainting-based approaches cannot handle the subtle color and brightness shifts characteristic of semi-transparent artifacts that retain significant underlying scene detail.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250573/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single flare-corrupted RGB image (intensity range [-1, 1]) as a conditioning signal to the DDIM sampling process and produces a restored flare-free RGB image of the same resolution. During training, the diffusion model is conditioned on the flare-corrupted image while iteratively denoising from pure Gaussian noise toward the clean ground truth, with the flare image serving as a spatial guide for where color adjustments are needed. The approach is trained exclusively on synthetic paired data (over 50,000 images synthesized from 5,000 unique flare patterns) and evaluated on both synthetic and real-world images without any real training data.

## Method

The paper formulates out-of-focus flare removal as a color distribution alignment problem rather than a reconstruction or inpainting task, leveraging a DDIM-based diffusion model conditioned on the flare-corrupted image. The key technical contribution is a differentiable histogram loss derived from Earth Mover's Distance (EMD), which uses kernel density estimation with a logistic-derived kernel to construct smooth, differentiable approximations of per-channel histograms (256 bins over [-1, 1]). The EMD loss is computed as the squared difference between cumulative distribution functions (CDFs) of the predicted and clean image histograms, enabling smooth gradient flow for perceptually coherent color alignment. The total training objective combines the standard L1 diffusion noise-prediction loss with the EMD loss, weighted by balancing parameters α and β. The authors also construct a large-scale synthetic dataset using Blender's Flared plugin, generating 5,000 unique out-of-focus flare patterns across 20 distinct types with varied capture angles, light-source positions, and lens designs tailored to mimic real mobile camera artifacts.

## Results

On synthetic data, the proposed diffusion model (DM) achieves M-PSNR 36.47, PSNR 40.58, SSIM 0.9842, and LPIPS 0.0136, substantially outperforming all baselines (next best: Zhou et al. at M-PSNR 24.13). On real-image data, DM reaches M-PSNR 30.45 and PSNR 32.98, surpassing the strongest baseline (Flare7K at M-PSNR 28.71). On the ISPRGB benchmark, DM achieves M-PSNR 29.88, PSNR 33.72, and LPIPS 0.0337, beating BracketFlare (M-PSNR 29.01) and Flare7K (M-PSNR 28.07). The ablation confirms the EMD loss contributes an additional ~1 dB M-PSNR and reduces LPIPS from 0.0294 to 0.0136 on synthetic data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed DM outperforms all nine baselines across every metric on all three evaluation sets. Against the strongest SOTA flare-removal methods in Table 3, DM leads by a large margin on synthetic data (M-PSNR 36.47 vs. 24.13 for Zhou et al.) and by a more modest but consistent margin on real data (M-PSNR 30.45 vs. 28.71 for Flare7K) and ISPRGB (M-PSNR 29.88 vs. 29.01 for BracketFlare). BracketFlare [8] is the closest competitor on ISPRGB (M-PSNR 29.01, PSNR 33.05) but is trained exclusively on in-focus nighttime flares, limiting its generalization to out-of-focus artifacts. Flare7K [6] and Flare7K++ [7] handle scattering glare well but degrade on real images with complex out-of-focus flares (PSNR drops to 29.32 and 24.12 respectively). Among the retrained restoration baselines in Table 2 (UNet, UFormer, MPRNet, Difflare), the proposed DM trained on the same synthetic dataset achieves the best M-PSNR (36.47 vs. 33.30 for UFormer) and lowest LPIPS (0.0136 vs. 0.0243 for UFormer), confirming that the diffusion architecture combined with EMD loss is the key differentiator beyond dataset quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-model-based image restoration line (DDIM, DDRM, DiffIR) by reframing out-of-focus reflective flare removal as a color distribution alignment problem rather than the conventional reconstruction/inpainting approach used for in-focus flares (Flare7K, BracketFlare, Wu et al.). The differentiable histogram loss based on Earth Mover's Distance is adapted from Avi-Aharon et al. [2] and integrated into the diffusion training objective, while the synthetic dataset is generated with Blender's Flared plugin to cover 20 flare types absent from prior datasets like Flare7K. Conceptually, the paper departs from the 'fill in missing content' paradigm by arguing that semi-transparent out-of-focus flares primarily require subtle color and brightness adjustments rather than structural reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and under-studied sub-problem in mobile photography where out-of-focus flares are common but lack dedicated datasets and methods. The Blender-generated dataset (5,000 patterns, 50,000+ training images) is a useful community resource, and the color-alignment formulation offers a principled alternative to inpainting for semi-transparent artifacts. However, the approach is validated primarily on synthetic data with limited real-world evaluation (142 + 50 images), and it remains unclear whether color alignment alone suffices for more severe or spatially non-uniform flare corruption. Open questions include whether the method generalizes to multi-source flares, dynamic scenes, or can be combined with reconstruction for mixed in-focus/out-of-focus artifacts.
