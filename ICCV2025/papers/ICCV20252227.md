## Problem

Existing tuning-free regional control methods for text-to-image generation either rely on attention masks that lose control strength as the number of regions grows, or use divide-and-merge strategies that produce visible boundary artifacts and lack cross-region interaction. No prior tuning-free approach decouples the fundamental construction of individual regions from the subsequent detail refinement and boundary harmonization, leading to either coarse attribute rendering or object omission in complex multi-region compositions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252227/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single complex multi-object text prompt plus per-region spatial specifications (offset and scale pairs defining rectangular areas) and produces a compositional RGB image where each region faithfully renders its assigned object with correct attributes and positioning. It is implemented on Flux.1-dev with 20 denoising steps and guidance scale 3.5. A key additional capability is image repainting: given a previously generated image and a modified prompt for one target region, the method regenerates only that region (by re-initializing noise there) while preserving all other regions, without requiring a separate inpainting model.

## Method

RAGD is a tuning-free two-stage pipeline on DiT-based models. In the early denoising steps (first r steps), Regional Hard Binding decomposes the prompt into short fundamental descriptions per region, denoises each region independently with its own text condition, and pastes the resulting regional latents back into the global latent at the specified spatial positions, ensuring accurate entity localization and preventing object omission. In the remaining steps, Regional Soft Refinement uses highly descriptive sub-prompts as K and V projections within the MM-DiT multi-modal attention layers while Q is derived from the global image latent, producing a globally-informed regional latent that is cropped, spliced back, and blended with the base latent via a weighted recombination controlled by a fusion strength parameter δ. This decoupling of hard positional binding from soft attribute refinement eliminates boundary artifacts while preserving spatial accuracy, and the same mechanism enables repainting by re-initializing noise only in the target region while simultaneously denoising the inherited latent for all other regions.

## Results

On GenEval, RAGD achieves the best scores across all five metrics: Counting (0.80), Position (0.82), Color Attribute (0.79), Two-Object (0.95), and VQAScore (0.8072). On T2ICompBench, RAGD leads in all six sub-metrics: Color (0.8039), Shape (0.6016), Texture (0.7085), Spatial (0.5193), Complex (0.4377), and VQAScore (0.8907). The paper reports a 76.6% average improvement over RPG and MultiDiffusion on spatial-relationship prompts, and VQAScore gains of 14.0% (GenEval) and 23.8% (T2ICompBench) over RPG. In a 24-user blind study (2,616 votes), RAGD is preferred 51.9% of the time for aesthetics and 54.0% for text-image alignment, far exceeding Flux.1-dev (18.8%/17.8%) and RPG (16.1%/14.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAGD outperforms all six quantitative baselines on nearly every metric across both GenEval and T2ICompBench. Against the closest tuning-free competitor RPG [45], RAGD gains +0.37 on Counting (0.80 vs 0.43), +0.19 on Position (0.82 vs 0.63), +0.15 on Color Attribute (0.79 vs 0.64), +0.13 on Two-Object (0.95 vs 0.82), and +0.157 on GenEval VQAScore (0.8072 vs 0.6502); on T2ICompBench the Spatial gap is +0.118 (0.5193 vs 0.4017) and VQAScore gap is +0.240 (0.8907 vs 0.6502). Versus the strongest tuning-based method 3DIS [59], RAGD still leads on Counting (+0.14), Position (+0.10), Color Attribute (+0.29), Two-Object (+0.12), and all five T2ICompBench sub-metrics. MultiDiffusion [1] and Ranni [11] are substantially weaker, with MultiDiffusion scoring only 0.06 on Position and 0.2320 on Spatial. InstanceDiffusion [41] and MIGC [58] are intermediate but trail RAGD by 0.10–0.50 on most GenEval metrics. In the user study, RAGD is preferred over Flux.1-dev [2] by a margin of 33.1 percentage points on aesthetics (51.9% vs 18.8%) and 36.2 points on alignment (54.0% vs 17.8%), and over RPG [45] by 35.8 and 39.2 points respectively. For the repainting task, RAGD is qualitatively compared against BrushNet [18], with the paper arguing RAGD produces more region-aware and harmonious edits without requiring a separate inpainting model.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAGD operates within the tuning-free regional control paradigm, building on the DiT/MM-DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023) and leveraging the Chain-of-Thought prompt decomposition template from RPG (Yang et al., ICML 2024). It departs from prior tuning-free methods (MultiDiffusion, RPG, Omost) that rely on simple split-and-merge of latents or attention mask manipulation by introducing a temporally decoupled two-stage process: Regional Hard Binding in early denoising steps for precise entity localization, followed by Regional Soft Refinement in later steps that exploits the multi-modal attention layers to enable cross-region interaction. This contrasts with tuning-based approaches (GLIGEN, InstanceDiffusion, MIGC) that require additional trainable modules and are locked to specific base models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RAGD provides a practical, plug-and-play solution for precise compositional generation on modern DiT-based models (Flux, SD3.5) without any architectural modification or retraining, which is valuable for practitioners who cannot fine-tune large models. The repainting capability—modifying specific regions while preserving others without an inpainting model—opens a new interaction pattern for iterative image editing. However, inference time scales linearly with the number of regions, and the paper acknowledges this as a key limitation; whether the two-stage decoupling generalizes to even more complex scenes (10+ regions) or to video generation remains open.
