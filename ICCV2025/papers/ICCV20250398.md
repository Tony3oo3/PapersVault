## Problem

Graph-based point cloud registration methods that rely on maximal clique enumeration (e.g., 3DMAC) achieve high recall under low inlier ratios but suffer from exponential time complexity and poor parallelizability due to unbounded clique growth and uneven branching. This limits their use in real-time or time-sensitive applications such as SLAM and autonomous driving.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250398/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

TurboReg takes a set of N putative 3D keypoint correspondences (established by external descriptors like FPFH, FCGF, or Predator) between a source and target point cloud as input, and outputs a single rigid SE(3) transformation. The method is entirely learning-free; the only critical hyperparameter is the compatibility threshold τ, which should be set to roughly one-fourth of the point cloud resolution (e.g., 1.2 cm for 3DMatch's 5 cm resolution). The number of hypotheses generated is K₁·K₂ (e.g., 2000 for K₁=1000, K₂=2), far fewer than the millions required by RANSAC.

## Method

TurboReg replaces maximal clique search with a fixed-size 3-clique (TurboClique) defined within a highly-constrained compatibility graph (small τ), trading data-scaling stability for pairwise-compatibility stability while enabling efficient parallel search. To ensure high inlier ratios despite the small clique size, the Pivot-Guided Search (PGS) algorithm selects the top-K₁ SC²-scored edges as pivots and, for each pivot, identifies compatible third nodes to form TurboCliques, retaining the top-K₂ per pivot by aggregated edge weight. A directed variant of the SC² graph (O2Graph) orients edges from lower to higher indexed nodes, guaranteeing each TurboClique is assigned to at most one pivot and eliminating redundant detection. PGS achieves O(N) time complexity (vs. exponential for MCE) and exploits two levels of parallelism—pivot-level and search-level—so that on GPU the workload reduces to near-constant time O(K₁N/R). Each TurboClique's three correspondences are fed to the Kabsch solver, and the transformation with the highest inlier count is selected as the final output.

## Results

On the 3DMatch dataset, TurboReg (2K) achieves the highest registration recall of 84.10% (FPFH) and 93.59% (FCGF), surpassing 3DMAC (83.92%/92.79%) and FastMAC@50 (82.87%/92.67%). On the more challenging 3DLoMatch dataset (10–30% overlap), TurboReg (2K) reaches 40.99% (FPFH) and 59.74% (FCGF) RR, outperforming 3DMAC (40.88%/59.85%). On KITTI, TurboReg (0.25K) achieves 98.56% (FPFH) and 98.38% (FCGF) RR. In terms of speed, TurboReg (1K) runs at 61.25 FPS on GPU and 2.73 FPS on CPU for 3DMatch, representing a 208.22× speedup over 3DMAC on GPU while also achieving higher recall.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TurboReg outperforms 3DMAC [61] on registration recall across all three datasets (e.g., 84.10% vs. 83.92% FPFH on 3DMatch; 98.56% vs. 97.66% FPFH on KITTI) while being 208× faster on GPU, directly addressing 3DMAC's exponential-time maximal clique enumeration. Against FastMAC [62], TurboReg (2K) improves RR by 2.53% (FPFH) and 1.51% (FCGF) on 3DLoMatch while achieving a 10× speedup over FastMAC@50 on GPU. TurboReg also surpasses SC²-PCR [9] in both recall (84.10% vs. 83.73% FPFH on 3DMatch) and speed (61.25 vs. 15.84 GPU FPS). Among deep learning baselines, TurboReg dominates: it exceeds VBReg [25] by 1.35% RR (FPFH, 3DMatch), PointDSC [5] by 11.15% (FPFH), and DGR [12] by 51.26% (FPFH), all without any training. On the low-overlap 3DLoMatch dataset, TurboReg (2K) achieves 40.99% RR (FPFH) vs. 40.88% for 3DMAC and 38.46% for FastMAC@50, with a 161× GPU speedup over 3DMAC, confirming its advantage in the most challenging registration scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

TurboReg builds directly on the SC²-PCR framework (Chen et al., CVPR 2022), adopting its second-order compatibility graph and SC² scores as the foundation for its O2Graph. It departs from 3DMAC (Zhang et al., CVPR 2023) and FastMAC (Zhang et al., CVPR 2024), which rely on maximal clique enumeration with exponential time complexity, by replacing variable-size maximal cliques with fixed-size 3-cliques (TurboCliques) in a tightly constrained graph. The O2Graph's directed ordering strategy is motivated by Wang et al.'s (PACMMOD 2024) edge-oriented branching approach for efficient k-clique listing, adapted here to eliminate redundant TurboClique detection across pivots.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TurboReg's 208× speedup over 3DMAC while matching or exceeding recall makes real-time correspondence-based PCR practical for SLAM and AR applications where exponential-time MCE was previously prohibitive. The linear-time PGS algorithm with GPU-parallelizable design (near-O(1) with sufficient parallelism) opens the door to registration on very large point clouds where MCE memory and runtime explode. However, the method remains learning-free and depends on upstream feature matching quality; its behavior under extreme outlier ratios (>99%) or in degenerate geometric configurations (e.g., planar scenes) is not thoroughly explored, and the fixed 3-clique size may limit stability in very noisy settings despite the tight compatibility threshold.
