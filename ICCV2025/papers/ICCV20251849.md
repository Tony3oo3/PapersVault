## Problem

Existing MLLMs struggle to model long-term temporal context in videos spanning minutes to hours, and current agent-based methods that augment a single MLLM with external tools (CLIP retrieval, memory banks, RAG) still yield only a partial understanding of long video content. Two specific gaps remain: (1) CLIP-based retrieval suffers from a domain gap with long videos and cannot capture long-term temporal information, and (2) no existing agent paradigm enables multiple MLLMs to dynamically collaborate, discuss, and refine answers across rounds for long video question answering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251849/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a long video (durations from ~11 seconds to over 1 hour across benchmarks), a text question, the video's subtitle transcript, and multiple-choice options. Rather than feeding all frames to a single model, the perception stage adaptively samples only 4 frames initially for a rough overview, then retrieves 16 frames from the most relevant of 6 equal video chunks, resulting in an average of only 71.2 frames per query. The output is a multiple-choice answer accompanied by per-agent reasoning explanations that are exchanged and refined across up to three collaboration rounds.

## Method

LVAgent introduces a four-step multi-round dynamical collaboration pipeline among MLLM agents: (1) Selection uses pseudo-label voting on 150 unlabeled videos to pre-select the top-3 agents from a library (Qwen2-VL, InternVL-2.5, LongVU, LLaVA-Video); (2) Perception performs three-stage adaptive retrieval where agents first decide whether to watch the full video, then generate key information for retrieval, and finally use an ASP-CLIP model fine-tuned on the newly constructed LongVR dataset (82K clips) to score 6 equal video chunks and select the most relevant frames (threshold 0.8); (3) Action has agents produce answers and reasons, with an early-stopping consensus check; (4) Reflection scores each agent's reasoning, removes the lowest-scoring agent, and regenerates key information to trigger the next retrieval round. This cycle repeats for up to 3 rounds, enabling dynamic agent composition per query and iterative refinement toward consensus, achieving 80%+ accuracy across four long video benchmarks while using fewer frames and less inference time than single-model baselines.

## Results

LVAgent achieves over 80% accuracy on all four long video understanding benchmarks: 82.9% on EgoSchema, 80.0% on LongVideoBench, 83.9% on MLVU, and 81.7% (w/o subs) / 86.6% (w/ subs) on VideoMME. This makes it the first agent-based method to surpass 80% on every benchmark simultaneously. On LongVideoBench, LVAgent improves accuracy by 13.3% over GPT-4o (66.7%), and on MLVU it surpasses GPT-4o by 19.3% (83.9% vs. 64.6%). In terms of efficiency, LVAgent uses only 71.2 frames and 33.6s inference time on VideoMME, compared to 568 frames and 227.2s for Gemini 1.5 Pro.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LVAgent outperforms all closed-source models across every benchmark: on LongVideoBench it beats GPT-4o by 13.3% (80.0 vs. 66.7) and Gemini 1.5 Pro by 16.0% (80.0 vs. 64.0); on EgoSchema it surpasses GPT-4o by 10.7% (82.9 vs. 72.2). Among open-source MLLMs, LVAgent exceeds the strongest single models—Qwen2-VL-72B (77.9% EgoSchema, 71.2/77.8 VideoMME) and InternVL-2.5-78B (75.7% MLVU, 72.1/74.0 VideoMME)—by 5–19 percentage points depending on the benchmark. Against agent-based baselines, LVAgent dominates: it improves over VideoRAG-72B by 14.6% on LongVideoBench (80.0 vs. 65.4) and 10.1% on MLVU (83.9 vs. 73.8), and over DrVideo by 21.9% on EgoSchema (82.9 vs. 61.0). The largest single-benchmark gain is on LongVideoBench, where the 13.3% improvement over GPT-4o is attributed to the multi-round dynamic collaboration and the ASP-CLIP retrieval pipeline. Notably, LVAgent achieves these gains while using far fewer frames (71.2 vs. 384–568) and less inference time (33.6s vs. 153.6–227.2s) than the top MLLM baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

LVAgent extends the agent-based video understanding line (VideoAgent, VideoTree, VCA, DrVideo, VideoRAG) by replacing the single-MLLM-plus-tools paradigm with a multi-round dynamic collaboration among multiple MLLM agents. The retrieval mechanism in the Perception stage is built directly on ASP-CLIP, fine-tuned on a newly constructed LongVR dataset to close the domain gap that limits vanilla CLIP on long videos. The multi-round collaboration design draws inspiration from dynamic LLM agent networks in NLP (Liu et al., 2024), but adapts it to the video domain by integrating a pseudo-label voting Selection step and a Reflection step that scores and prunes agents per question. Unlike prior agent methods that rely on a fixed single model, LVAgent dynamically reconfigures its agent team across rounds, making the collaboration truly task-adaptive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LVAgent is the first agent-based system to surpass all closed-source MLLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro) on four mainstream long-video QA benchmarks, achieving 80%+ accuracy while using fewer frames (71.2 vs. 384–568) and less inference time (33.6 s vs. 153–227 s). This suggests that structured multi-agent collaboration can compensate for the temporal-context limitations of individual MLLMs without simply scaling up context windows. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to open-ended (non-multiple-choice) video QA, how it scales to hour-plus videos beyond the current benchmarks, and whether the computational overhead of running three large MLLMs in parallel across multiple rounds is practical for real-time or edge deployment.
