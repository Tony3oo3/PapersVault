## Problem

Existing pretraining strategies for fluorescence microscopy images (e.g., MAE, BYOL, WSL) generalize to unseen perturbations but fail on de novo cell lines because models exploit spurious cell-line-specific correlations rather than true perturbation-specific morphological signals. The paper asks how to explicitly disentangle perturbation-specific from cell line-specific representations during pretraining so that the learned features transfer to previously unseen cell lines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250791/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-channel fluorescence microscopy images (6 channels for RxRx1 Cell Painting, 5 channels for RxRx19a lacking MitoTracker) alongside a per-cell-line gene expression vector (k highly variable genes from GSE288929 RNA-seq). The gene expression vector is first compressed by a fine-tuned single-cell foundation model (scGPT or scVI) into a fixed-length embedding, which is then expanded into m transcriptomic tokens prepended to the n image patch tokens of a ViT-S/16. The output is a perturbation class label (genetic or chemical), evaluated via one-shot fine-tuning on U2OS (RxRx1) and few-shot fine-tuning on HRCE and VERO (RxRx19a).

## Method

The core contribution is a two-pronged biological-knowledge integration into any existing ViT pretraining pipeline. First, a perturbation relational graph is constructed where nodes are perturbations and edge weights combine STRING protein-protein interaction scores, Hetionet random-walk probabilities, and image-derived gene similarities; two graph-based losses (Laplacian regularization and node-level contrastive learning) are added to the pretraining objective to push the encoder toward perturbation-specific features and away from cell-line confounds. Second, a single-cell foundation model fine-tuned on cell-type annotation produces a compact transcriptomic embedding per cell line, which is projected into m learnable tokens and prepended to the image token sequence in a vision-language-style fusion, giving the ViT explicit access to cell-line identity. A proposition shows the graph losses implicitly minimize intra-class image distances, and the entire framework is plug-and-play with WSL, SimCLR, BYOL, MoCo v3, and MAE pretraining objectives.

## Results

On the de novo cell line setting, the proposed method consistently improves top-1 accuracy across all five pretraining baselines and three test cell lines. For the strongest baseline (WSL), the method achieves 4.79% top-1 on U2OS (vs. 4.12% baseline), 4.24% on HRCE (vs. 3.57%), and 38.95% on VERO (vs. 34.11%), representing a ~16% relative gain on U2OS and ~14% on VERO. The largest absolute improvements appear on the VERO cell line from RxRx19a, where MoCo v3 gains +4.83 points and SimCLR gains +5.89 points in top-1 accuracy. All results are averaged over five random splits with one-shot (U2OS) or few-shot (HRCE, VERO) fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's method is evaluated as an add-on to five existing pretraining strategies, and it wins on every comparison across all three de novo cell lines. Against WSL (Moshkov et al., 2024), the strongest baseline, the method adds +0.67 points top-1 on U2OS and +4.84 on VERO, confirming that graph regularization and cell-line tokens complement the already task-aligned WSL objective. Against SimCLR and BYOL (Bendidi et al., 2024), gains are smaller on U2OS (+0.37 and +0.19 respectively) but more substantial on VERO (+5.89 and +3.63), suggesting the biological priors are most valuable when the self-supervised objective is less directly aligned with perturbation classification. Against MoCo v3 (Chen et al., 2021), the method yields the largest relative improvement on VERO (+4.83 points, from 20.73% to 25.56%), indicating that momentum-based contrastive pretraining benefits most from explicit perturbation-relationship guidance. Against MAE (Kraus et al., 2024), the weakest baseline, the method still improves all three cell lines, with the largest gain on HRCE (+0.55 points top-1), where the imaging protocol shift between RxRx1 and RxRx19a makes transfer hardest. No baseline is matched or outperformed by the proposed method in any setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends existing microscopy image pretraining strategies (MAE, BYOL, SimCLR, MoCo v3, and WSL) by injecting external biological knowledge into the pretraining objective. It draws on two distinct sources: a perturbation relational graph built from STRING and Hetionet protein-protein interaction data, which guides the vision encoder via graph Laplacian and contrastive regularization losses, and single-cell foundation models (scGPT, scVI) that produce cell line-specific transcriptomic tokens prepended to image tokens in a ViT, inspired by vision-language model architectures. The WSL framework of Moshkov et al. [37] serves as the primary supervised baseline, while the self-supervised methods provide the unsupervised baselines into which the biological priors are plugged as additional loss terms or input tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The de novo cell line setting is a practical bottleneck in phenotype-based drug discovery, where new cell lines are routinely introduced but models trained on existing lines fail to generalize. The modular design—graph regularization losses and scFM-derived tokens that can be attached to any pretraining method—makes the approach broadly applicable. However, the pretraining set contains only three cell lines, the absolute accuracy gains are modest (under 1 percentage point in most settings), and the ablation reveals that cell line-specific tokens can degrade performance (e.g., BYOL), suggesting the approach's benefits are sensitive to dataset scale and pretraining method. Open questions include whether the biological knowledge integration scales to larger, more diverse cell line collections and whether the graph regularization gains persist under different imaging protocols and perturbation types.
