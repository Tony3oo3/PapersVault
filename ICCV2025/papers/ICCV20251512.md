## Problem

In conventional Vector Quantization, all weight subvectors assigned to the same codeword are forced to update in the same direction during fine-tuning, since only the codebook is trainable. This causes gradient dominance, where a few high-magnitude gradients dictate the update for the entire cluster, pushing the majority of weights toward suboptimal values and severely limiting accuracy recovery at extreme compression ratios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251512/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on the weight matrices of a pre-trained neural network, partitioning them into subvectors of dimension d. The compressed representation consists of three components: a codebook of K all-positive codewords, assignment indices mapping each subvector to a codeword, and a 1-bit sign mask. At inference, weights are reconstructed by element-wise multiplying the looked-up codeword with the sign mask, after which the model processes standard inputs (RGB images, text prompts) and produces task-specific outputs (class labels, bounding boxes, segmentation masks, generated images, or text).

## Method

SSVQ decouples each weight into its sign and absolute value, then applies k-means clustering only to the all-positive magnitudes, which requires significantly fewer codewords than clustering signed vectors at the same error. The sign mask is made learnable by introducing a continuous latent variable Ls (initialized as α·W), whose sign function replaces the fixed sign in the reconstruction; gradients for Ls are approximated via the Straight-Through Estimator, incorporating the codeword magnitude to stabilize training. To prevent sign-bit oscillations that destabilize optimization, an enhanced iterative freezing strategy tracks each sign's flip frequency with an exponential moving average and, at periodic intervals, permanently freezes signs whose oscillation exceeds a cosine-decaying threshold, using majority positive/negative voting rather than sign-EMA to determine the frozen value. This allows each quantized weight to update independently in its own gradient direction while preserving the compact VQ storage format.

## Results

On ImageNet classification, SSVQ improves DeiT-tiny by 12% absolute top-1 accuracy over conventional VQ at a 21× compression ratio, and outperforms MVQ on MobileNet-v2 (65.9% vs 65.1% at 16× CR). On COCO object detection, SSVQ achieves 35.2 AP50:95 at 20× CR versus 30.0 for VQ and 27.7 for LSQ+BNR at 16×. For semantic segmentation on VOC, SSVQ reaches 67.7 mIoU at 19× CR, surpassing MVQ (66.5) and conventional VQ (65.7). On Llama3.2-1B at 2-bit, SSVQ reduces WikiText-2 PPL from 63.1 (VQ) to 13.9, approaching the full-precision 9.7.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSVQ consistently outperforms all VQ baselines: on MobileNet-v2 classification it beats MVQ (65.9% vs 65.1% at 16×) and HQ (63.1% vs 58.8% at 20×); on VOC segmentation it surpasses MVQ by 1.2 mIoU (67.7 vs 66.5 at 19×) and dramatically outperforms PvQ (67.7 vs 19.2 mIoU). Against UQ methods, SSVQ at 20× CR achieves 35.2 AP50:95 on COCO detection versus 27.7 for LSQ+BNR at 16×, a 7.5-point gain at a higher compression ratio. In image generation (Stable Diffusion v1.4, 2-bit), SSVQ reduces FID-to-FP from 24.5 (VQ) to 13.6, while scalar UQ methods PCR (311.0) and EMF (173.4) fail catastrophically; SSVQ also achieves a 25% higher CLIP score than the best mix-precision UQ baselines Quest (23.0) and MPQ-DM (25.1). On Llama3.2-1B, SSVQ at 2-bit achieves 13.9 WikiText-2 PPL versus 63.1 for VQ, a 4.5× reduction in perplexity. The paper does not report any setting where SSVQ underperforms the compared baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the vector quantization (VQ) compression paradigm by identifying and addressing a fundamental fine-tuning limitation: all weight subvectors assigned to the same codeword are forced to update in a shared direction, with a few high-magnitude gradients dominating the cluster update. The sign-splitting mechanism (decoupling sign bits from the codebook) is a novel architectural modification to VQ, while the iterative freezing strategy for learnable signs directly adapts the EMA-based oscillation tracking and periodic freezing from Nagel et al. (Osqat, ICML 2022), replacing sign-EMA with majority sign voting and a cosine-scheduled threshold. The work also builds on the broader VQ literature (BGD, PQF, DKM, MVQ) and the Straight-Through Estimator for handling non-differentiable operations, but departs from all of them by making the sign mask a learnable parameter rather than a fixed attribute.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SSVQ addresses a practical bottleneck that limits the utility of VQ in real deployment pipelines where fine-tuning after quantization is essential. The consistent 4–12% accuracy gains over conventional VQ across classification, detection, segmentation, diffusion, and LLM tasks, combined with a validated 3× hardware speedup, make this a practically relevant improvement for edge deployment. Open questions include how the 1-bit sign overhead interacts with extremely aggressive compression (e.g., below 2-bit effective), whether the approach extends naturally to activation quantization, and whether the freezing schedule can be made adaptive per-layer rather than global. The method's generality across architectures (CNNs, ViTs, diffusion UNets, transformers) suggests broad adoptability, though the hardware validation is limited to a single model (DeiT-tiny) on a cycle-accurate simulator rather than silicon.
