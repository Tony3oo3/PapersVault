## Problem

Existing trajectory prediction methods assume clean historical observations, yet real-world sensing is inevitably corrupted by noise from adverse weather, occlusions, or tracking imperfections, causing significant performance degradation even for state-of-the-art models. No prior approach handles arbitrary noise distributions in a unified, plug-and-play manner for trajectory prediction.

## Input / Output

The method takes noisy 2D position sequences X_obs = {x_obs^1, ..., x_obs^T_obs} (each x_obs^i ∈ R²) as input, where noise is applied via an arbitrary function f_N to clean trajectories. It produces K plausible future trajectories {Ŷ_fut^k}_{k=1}^K (K=20 on ETH/UCY and SDD, K=1 or 6 on Argoverse). The framework is plug-and-play: the denoising module sits in front of any existing prediction backbone (GraphTern, EqMotion, HiVT), and at inference only the denoised observations are fed to the backbone.

## Method

NATRA consists of a Trajectory Denoise Model (TDM) and a Trajectory Prediction Backbone (TPB). The TDM denoises via a mutual information objective: it maximizes MI between denoised observations and clean ground-truth future trajectories (lower-bounded by MINE) while minimizing MI between denoised observations and noisy inputs (upper-bounded by CLUB), inspired by the Information Bottleneck principle. A masked-reconstruction loss (randomly masking M positions and reconstructing them with L2 distance) preserves trajectory structure that pure MI optimization would destroy. A ranking loss then enforces that predictions from denoised observations are closer to ground truth than those from noisy observations, jointly optimizing both TDM and TPB. The total loss is L_pred + βL_rank + δL_rec + γL_MI, and because no noise-distribution-specific module is used, the framework is agnostic to the type of noise.

## Results

On ETH/UCY with Gaussian noise (σ=0.4), NATRA+EqMotion achieves the best average ADE of 0.35 m and FDE of 0.48 m, outperforming the unmodified EqMotion backbone (0.42/0.59) by 17% and 19% respectively. On SDD under the same noise level, NATRA+EqMotion reaches ADE 11.92 px and FDE 17.65 px, improving over EqMotion alone (13.46/19.60) by 11% and 10%. On Argoverse (σ=4), NATRA+HiVT attains ADE 2.78 m / FDE 5.69 m (K=1), a 12% ADE reduction over the HiVT baseline (3.16/6.22). NATRA consistently outperforms the Wavelet and EMA denoising baselines across all three datasets and both noise levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NATRA+EqMotion dominates all six trajectory prediction baselines on both ETH/UCY and SDD under both noise levels. Against the strongest backbone baseline EqMotion, NATRA reduces average ADE from 0.42 to 0.35 (σ=0.4, ETH/UCY) and from 13.46 to 11.92 px (σ=0.4, SDD). Against SocialVAE, the gap is even larger: 0.42→0.35 ADE on ETH/UCY and 13.73→11.92 on SDD at σ=0.4. MID and SocialImplicit suffer the most degradation under noise (e.g., SocialImplicit jumps to 18.07 ADE on SDD at σ=0.4), and NATRA outperforms them by 30–50% in ADE. Against the two denoising baselines (Wavelet and EMA), NATRA consistently wins by 0.03–0.04 ADE on ETH/UCY and 0.4–0.9 px on SDD at σ=0.4, confirming that the mutual information + reconstruction mechanism is more effective than simple signal-processing filters. On Argoverse (σ=4), NATRA+HiVT beats HiVT by 0.38 m ADE (K=1) and 0.06 m ADE (K=6), and also outperforms both Wavelet+HiVT and EMA+HiVT.

## Relation to Prior Work

NATRA builds on the Information Bottleneck principle and operationalizes it through two established mutual information estimators: CLUB for the upper bound (minimizing dependence on noisy observations) and MINE for the lower bound (maximizing dependence on clean future trajectories). It departs from prior robust trajectory prediction work (MTP, affinity-based methods, adversarial-robustness approaches) that targets specific noise sources like tracking errors or adversarial perturbations, by formulating a noise-agnostic denoising objective that makes no assumption about the noise distribution. The masked reconstruction component borrows from the autoencoder/masked-modeling paradigm, while the ranking loss is a novel training signal specific to this paper's two-branch (denoised vs. noisy) prediction setup.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real-world sensors introduce noise into trajectory observations, yet nearly all trajectory prediction methods assume clean inputs, and the demonstrated performance collapse (Figure 1) is severe. The plug-and-play, noise-agnostic design means practitioners can retrofit existing backbones (GraphTern, EqMotion, HiVT) without re-architecting them, which lowers adoption cost. Limitations include the supervised requirement (ground-truth future trajectories needed during training), the reliance on potentially loose MI bounds, and evaluation limited to 2D pedestrian/drone trajectories with moderate noise levels; whether the approach transfers to 3D autonomous-driving settings with higher-dimensional state spaces or to fully unsupervised regimes remains open.
