## Problem

Diffusion-based image generators lack explicit conditioning mechanisms for perceptual quality, making it difficult to control the quality level of generated images. While IQA models can score image quality, their knowledge has not been systematically transferred into generative architectures, leaving a gap between quality assessment and quality-aware synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251815/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and one or more normalized IQA/IAA quality scores (standardized to zero mean, unit variance over the training set) as conditioning signals, projected into 2 tokens via a linear layer and LayerNorm. In reference-based mode, it additionally accepts intermediate-layer activations from a pretrained ARNIQA model applied to a reference image, extracting content-agnostic qualitative embeddings. The output is a generated image whose perceptual quality is shifted toward the target level—ranging from high-fidelity (99th percentile) to progressively degraded (1st percentile)—while preserving the semantic content specified by the text prompt.

## Method

IQA-Adapter introduces a lightweight adapter that conditions a frozen SDXL U-Net on quality scores via a Separate Qualitative Attention mechanism: an additional cross-attention layer is added alongside each existing text cross-attention layer, sharing the query projection so quality features are applied in a content-agnostic manner. A Qualitative Negative Guidance extension modifies classifier-free guidance by subtracting predictions conditioned on an opposite quality level (q_neg = -δ·q), amplifying the quality shift at inference. Training follows the standard DDPM denoising objective on (image, text, quality-score) triplets, updating only adapter weights without backpropagating through IQA models, which enables use of non-differentiable metrics. The Reference-based variant replaces scalar scores with ARNIQA intermediate activations that encode distortion type and strength while discarding semantic content, enabling targeted quality transfer from a reference image.

## Results

Under high-quality conditioning (99th percentile), IQA-Adapter achieves an average 7–9% relative gain across multiple IQA/IAA metrics over the SDXL base model, with up to 10% improvement on individual metrics. In a subjective study with 1,017 users and 22,300+ responses, the high-quality condition achieves a 60% pairwise win rate against the base model (32% for base, ~7% ties). On the GenEval benchmark, overall scores remain close to the base model (56.29% base vs. 56.76% best adapter), confirming generative capability is preserved. For reference-based distortion transfer on KADID-10k, the Reference-based IQA-Adapter reaches SROCC 0.80 (T2I) and 0.69 (I2I) in quality alignment, substantially outperforming IP-Adapter (0.41/0.24) and StyleCrafter (0.53/0.24).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the gradient-based IQA guidance baseline, IQA-Adapter clearly wins on cross-metric transferability: the gradient method inflates only the target IQA score while other metrics degrade (adversarial exploitation), whereas IQA-Adapter trained on a single metric still improves 7–9% on unrelated metrics. Among generation-quality-improvement methods, IQA-Adapter outperforms Q-Refine, BeautifulPrompt, and Prompt Weighting (Compel) on average relative gain across Lexica.art prompts; the best single result is a combination of IQA-Adapter with DiffusionDPO and BeautifulPrompt, indicating complementarity rather than direct competition. On the reference-based distortion transfer task (KADID-10k), the Reference-based IQA-Adapter dominates IP-Adapter and StyleCrafter on SROCC (0.80 vs. 0.41 and 0.53 in T2I; 0.69 vs. 0.24 and 0.24 in I2I) and IQA embedding similarity (0.91 vs. 0.79 and 0.86), while also showing lower semantic leakage (CLIP-I with distortion reference: 58.62 vs. 68.40 for IP-Adapter). IP-Adapter and StyleCrafter retain an edge in CLIP-I alignment with the real source image, reflecting their design for content/style transfer rather than quality transfer. On GenEval, all IQA-Adapters roughly match or slightly exceed the base model's overall score (56.29%), with HYPER-IQA-trained adapter reaching 56.39% and TOPIQ(FLIVE) reaching 56.76%, confirming no meaningful degradation in compositional generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper inverts the recent trend of transferring generative priors into IQA models (DP-IQA, GenZIQA) by instead injecting IQA knowledge into a diffusion generator. Architecturally, it follows the adapter pattern established by IP-Adapter and ControlNet—projecting a conditioning signal into tokens consumed via cross-attention—but repurposes it for a fundamentally different input: scalar quality scores rather than spatial maps or image features. The gradient-based baseline directly adapts the classifier-guidance formula from Dhariwal & Nichol, replacing log-probabilities with IQA scores, while the main IQA-Adapter departs from this by learning the image-to-quality mapping implicitly during training, avoiding the adversarial exploitation that plagues direct score optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IQA-Adapter provides a lightweight, composable mechanism for quality-conditioned generation that can be stacked with existing techniques (DiffusionDPO, BeautifulPrompt) without modifying base-model weights, making it practically attractive for production pipelines. The cross-metric transferability—training on one IQA model improving scores on others—suggests the adapter captures a somewhat universal quality manifold rather than metric-specific artifacts, though this remains to be validated at scale and across architectures beyond SDXL. Open questions include whether the learned quality features truly generalize to video generation, whether the reference-based variant can serve as a general-purpose degradation simulator for data augmentation, and how the method behaves under adversarial probing of the underlying IQA models.
