## Problem

Existing training-free cache reuse methods for diffusion Transformers rely on inter-step output similarity to decide when to skip computation, which systematically concentrates reuse in later sampling steps where outputs are most similar but denoising strength is weakest. This leads to irrecoverable trajectory deviations and quality degradation, particularly in video generation models with fewer sampling steps. The paper identifies that local similarity metrics diverge from the truly optimal cache placement strategy and proposes a global, trajectory-based alternative.

## Input / Output

OmniCache is a training-free inference-time acceleration layer applied on top of existing DiT models without modifying their weights. For text-to-video it consumes a text prompt and produces videos at 480p/2s (OpenSora, 30 steps) or 512×512/2s (Latte, 50 steps); for image-to-video it takes a reference image and generates up to 1080P (768×1360) video (CogVideoX-5b-I2V-distill, 16 steps); for class-conditional image generation it takes a class label and outputs 256×256 images (DiT-XL/2, 250 steps). The method requires a one-time calibration pass to compute trajectory curvature and noise-correlation coefficients before actual sampling begins.

## Method

OmniCache projects each sampling trajectory into a 3D subspace via PCA and computes per-step curvature, then selects low-curvature (straight-segment) time steps for cache reuse, where the diffusion model's inherent self-correction can absorb the introduced noise. During sampling, the method estimates cache-induced noise at the current step by scaling the previous step's measured noise with a Taylor-expansion-derived correlation coefficient γ_t, and subtracts this estimate from the denoising output to correct the trajectory. A frequency-aware filtering stage applies low-pass filtering to the estimated noise in early steps (protecting high-frequency detail) and high-pass filtering in later steps (protecting low-frequency structure). A hard constraint prohibits cache reuse on three consecutive steps to keep the noise-correlation approximation valid. The result is a 2–2.5× speedup on 30–50-step video models and a 1.45× speedup on the 16-step distilled CogVideoX model, where prior similarity-based methods cause model collapse.

## Results

On text-to-video generation, OmniCache-slow achieves a 2.00× speedup with only a 0.16–0.36% VBench drop (78.83% on OpenSora, 77.24% on Latte), while OmniCache-fast reaches 2.50× speedup with a 0.31–0.31% VBench reduction (78.50% on OpenSora, 77.09% on Latte). On the distilled CogVideoX-5b-I2V-distill model (16 steps), OmniCache achieves a 1.45× speedup with Q-Align of 0.792 and Aesthetic Quality of 0.621, both matching or exceeding the unaccelerated baseline (0.79, 0.59), whereas AdaCache degrades Q-Align to 0.77. On ImageNet class-conditional generation (DiT-XL/2), OmniCache-fast attains FID 2.69 and sFID 5.86 at 2.50× speedup, comparable to ToCa (FID 2.68, sFID 5.81 at 2.75×).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniCache consistently outperforms all seven baselines on VBench across both OpenSora and Latte. On Latte, the advantage is most dramatic: OmniCache-slow (77.24%) beats AdaCache-slow (77.07%), TeaCache-fast (76.69%), PAB-slow (76.32%), and T-GATE (75.42%), while Δ-DiT collapses entirely (52.00%). On OpenSora, OmniCache-slow (78.83%) edges out PAB-slow (78.51%), TeaCache-fast (78.48%), and ToCa (78.34%), with a 2.00× speedup matching or exceeding their 1.20–2.36× range. On the challenging 16-step CogVideoX-5b-I2V-distill model, OmniCache (Q-Align 0.792, Aesthetic 0.621) surpasses AdaCache (0.77, 0.57) at a higher speedup (1.45× vs 1.33×), while Δ-DiT, PAB, and Learn-to-Cache fail entirely due to model collapse. On ImageNet (DiT-XL/2), OmniCache-fast (FID 2.69, sFID 5.86) matches ToCa (FID 2.68, sFID 5.81) while FORA lags slightly (FID 2.80, sFID 6.13–6.21). Overall, OmniCache achieves the best quality–speed trade-off among all compared methods, particularly in the 2–2.5× speedup regime where most baselines show noticeable VBench degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the trajectory regularity analysis of Chen et al. (ICML 2024), which established that diffusion sampling trajectories can be faithfully approximated in a 3D PCA subspace. It departs fundamentally from the dominant cache-reuse paradigm (AdaCache, TeaCache, ToCA, PAB, T-GATE, Δ-DiT), which selects cache points based on inter-step output similarity, by instead using trajectory curvature—a global geometric property—as the criterion for cache placement. The key conceptual departure is the finding that high similarity in later sampling steps (which existing methods exploit) coincides with diminished denoising capacity, making those steps the worst candidates for cache reuse rather than the best.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for video generation deployment: OmniCache achieves 2–2.5× speedup on 30–50-step models and, notably, still works on heavily distilled 16-step models (CogVideoX-5b-I2V-distill) where competing cache-reuse methods cause outright model collapse. The noise-correction module (Taylor-expansion-based estimation plus frequency-selective filtering) is a clean, training-free mechanism that could be adapted to other caching or approximation strategies in iterative generative models. The main limitation is the no-three-consecutive-caches constraint, which caps maximum acceleration; whether more sophisticated noise tracking could relax this remains open. Broader open questions include whether the curvature-based strategy generalizes to non-DiT architectures (e.g., UNet-based video models) and to text-to-speech or 3D generation settings.
