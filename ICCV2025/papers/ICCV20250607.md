## Problem

Vision language models process all video tokens uniformly, incurring massive computational overhead on long-form videos where only sparse frames are relevant to a given query. Existing solutions either prune tokens independently per frame (ignoring temporal dependencies) or hard-select keyframes (discarding all tokens from non-keyframes and breaking contextual continuity). No prior method adaptively allocates token budgets across frames based on query relevance while preserving the temporal structure needed for video reasoning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a long video uniformly sampled into up to 128 frames (grouped into clips of 8) and a debiased text query as input. It produces a frame-specific pruning rate vector that, when applied to the vision token sequence, yields a compressed token set (up to 80% reduction) fed to the LLM backbone for answer generation. The pruning rates are computed at inference time by a lightweight SigLIP-based predictor, making the module plug-and-play with any existing token pruning pipeline (PruMerge, ToMe, FastV, etc.) without modifying the backbone LLM.

## Method

KVTP fine-tunes a pretrained SigLIP encoder into a query-frame relevance predictor using a modified contrastive loss (element-wise sigmoid loss between logits and mean-normalized GPT-4o-labeled relevance scores) augmented with a context fusion head that performs cross-attention between each frame embedding and both its local clip embedding and the global video embedding. At inference, the predictor outputs per-frame relevance logits, which are passed through a temperature-controlled softmax and scaled by a hyperparameter to yield frame-specific pruning rates: keyframes retain most tokens while less relevant frames retain only a small fraction, preserving temporal and contextual coherence. This soft-selection mechanism is the key differentiator from hard keyframe selection, as it retains a residual token budget from every frame. The resulting pruning rates are then integrated with existing image-level token pruning algorithms (e.g., PruMerge's attention-based selection) to produce the final compressed token sequence, achieving 64% FLOPs reduction with minimal accuracy loss on long-video QA benchmarks.

## Results

On the SparseKV-QA benchmark (subsets of VideoMME, EgoSchema, and NeXT-QA), KVTP integrated with PruMerge at 36% FLOPs (64% reduction) achieves 63.29% on VideoMME, 54.71% on EgoSchema, and 76.76% on NeXT-QA with LLaVA-Video-7B, surpassing the full unpruned model (62.63 / 54.17 / 78.51) on two of three subsets while using only 36% of the original FLOPs. The same trend holds at 72B scale, where PruMerge+KVTP at 23% FLOPs reaches 67.34 / 64.12 / 81.21 versus the full model's 69.52 / 65.76 / 83.20. The method prunes up to 80% of video tokens while maintaining or exceeding baseline accuracy, demonstrating that query-aware soft frame selection recovers performance lost by uniform token pruning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KVTP consistently outperforms all token-pruning baselines at the same FLOPs budget: PruMerge+KVTP beats PruMerge alone by +3.32 (VideoMME), +1.95 (EgoSchema), and +0.88 (NeXT-QA), and beats ToMe by +4.08, +2.41, and +3.39 respectively. Against the hard-selection keyframe method KeyVideoLLM, KVTP gains a substantial +11.97 / +7.93 / +12.43 across the three subsets, confirming that soft frame selection preserving contextual tokens is far superior to discarding low-relevance frames entirely. Notably, PruMerge+KVTP at 36% FLOPs surpasses the full unpruned LLaVA-Video-7B on VideoMME (63.29 vs. 62.63) and EgoSchema (54.71 vs. 54.17), while remaining within 1.75 points on NeXT-QA. FastV, which prunes only 50% of tokens at layer 2 (×64% FLOPs), achieves 61.79 / 52.42 / 77.89; KVTP at a more aggressive 36% FLOPs still matches or exceeds FastV on VideoMME and EgoSchema. At 72B scale, the same pattern holds with PruMerge+KVTP at 23% FLOPs reaching 67.34 / 64.12 / 81.21, within 2.2 points of the full 72B model on all three subsets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established threads: vision token pruning (DynamicViT, PruMerge, FastV, ToMe), which reduces spatial redundancy per frame but ignores inter-frame temporal structure, and keyframe selection (KeyVideoLLM, VideoTree, Koala), which identifies query-relevant frames but discards others entirely via hard selection. KVTP departs from both by converting query-frame relevance scores into per-frame pruning rates, effectively performing soft selection that retains a small token budget from less relevant frames to preserve temporal and contextual coherence. The method is built on SigLIP as a fine-tuned relevance predictor and is designed as a plug-and-play module that composes with existing token pruning pipelines (PruMerge, ToMe) rather than replacing them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 80% token reduction with 64% FLOPs savings while matching or exceeding full-model accuracy on sparse-event video QA is a practically significant efficiency gain for deploying VLMs on long-form video (surveillance, video analytics, content summarization). The SparseKV-QA benchmark fills a gap by providing a structured evaluation setting where keyframe sparsity is the dominant characteristic, which existing long-video benchmarks do not isolate. Limitations include evaluation only on LLaVA-Video (7B/72B), reliance on GPT-4o for ground-truth relevance labeling during data construction, and the somewhat ad hoc temperature-based softness control at inference. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other VLM architectures, streaming/online video settings, and whether the context fusion head's cross-attention design is optimal versus simpler alternatives.
