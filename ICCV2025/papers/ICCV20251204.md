## Problem

Existing 3D test-time adaptation methods either perform expensive per-point spatial optimizations (N×3 parameters for N>1024 points) or require access to source training data, making them impractical for online inference on irregular point clouds. This paper identifies that the graph spectral domain captures global structural properties with ~90% fewer parameters than the spatial domain, and that Laplacian eigenmaps provide domain-independent descriptors that complement potentially source-biased deep features during early adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251204/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a batch of unlabeled 3D point clouds (N×3) from a shifted target distribution and a pre-trained classification backbone (DGCNN, CurveNet, or PointNeXt), and outputs class labels. Rather than adapting all N×3 spatial coordinates, the method constructs an outlier-aware kNN graph per point cloud and optimizes only the lowest M=100 graph Fourier coefficients (the lowest ~10% of frequency components), which the paper shows concentrate 95% of spectral energy. Adaptation proceeds iteratively: 4 steps of spectral adjustment followed by 1 step of model parameter update, repeated for 10 total cycles per batch.

## Method

GSDTTA comprises two iteratively coupled modules. The Graph Spectral Driven Point Shift (GSDPS) module converts each point cloud into an outlier-aware graph (RBF-weighted kNN edges with degree-threshold outlier removal), computes the Graph Fourier Transform, applies a learnable spectral adjustment ΔX̂ to only the lowest-frequency coefficients, and reconstructs the adapted point cloud via inverse GFT. The Graph Spectral Guided Model Adaptation (GSGMA) module updates the backbone parameters θ. Both are optimized through an eigenmap-guided self-training strategy that generates pseudo-labels as a convex combination (weighted by α) of cosine similarities between each sample's global deep descriptor and its global spectral descriptor (derived from max-pooled Laplacian eigenmap embeddings) against class centroids. The combined loss includes cross-entropy with pseudo-labels, entropy minimization, divergency loss (information maximization), and a one-directional Chamfer distance regularizing the adapted point cloud to remain close to the original.

## Results

On ModelNet40-C, GSDTTA achieves the highest mean accuracy across all three backbones: 79.07% (DGCNN), 82.63% (CurveNet), and 82.51% (PointNeXt), outperforming the best 2D TTA method SHOT by 1.71%, 1.39%, and 2.05% respectively. On the more challenging real-scanned ScanObjectNN-C benchmark, GSDTTA achieves 61.83% (DGCNN), 62.59% (CurveNet), and 60.84% (PointNeXt) mean accuracy, surpassing the previous SOTA 3D TTA method CloudFixer by 1.10%, 3.63%, and 1.83%. GSDTTA shows particularly strong gains on semantic corruptions (e.g., background: 88.57% vs. 74.55% for CloudFixer with DGCNN on ModelNet40-C) while CloudFixer retains an edge on basic noise corruptions (uniform, gaussian, impulse, upsampling) due to its diffusion-based denoising.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GSDTTA outperforms all 2D TTA baselines (BN, PL, DUA, TENT, SHOT) across both datasets and all three backbones, with the largest gap over SHOT (the strongest 2D TTA method) being 1.39–2.05% mean accuracy on ModelNet40-C. Against the 3D-specific baselines, GSDTTA surpasses CloudFixer (the previous SOTA) by 2.53%, 4.72%, and 6.47% on ModelNet40-C and by 1.10%, 3.63%, and 1.83% on ScanObjectNN-C, winning on 11–12 of 15 corruption types. GSDTTA also substantially outperforms BFTT3D (by ~13–14% mean accuracy) and 3DD-TTA (by ~7–11%), both of which require access to source training data and thus do not strictly adhere to the TTA setting. The one area where GSDTTA trails is basic noise corruptions (uniform, gaussian, impulse, upsampling) on ScanObjectNN-C, where CloudFixer's diffusion-based denoising provides an average 8.35% advantage; however, GSDTTA compensates with stronger performance on semantic corruptions (shear, cutout, background), yielding a net mean improvement of +2.19% over CloudFixer on that dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

GSDTTA extends the test-time adaptation (TTA) paradigm from 2D images to 3D point clouds, but departs fundamentally from prior 3D TTA methods (CloudFixer, 3DD-TTA) that rely on diffusion models and require access to source training data. Instead of adapting in the spatial domain, the paper repurposes graph spectral analysis—specifically the Global Point Signature framework [40] and Graph Fourier Transform—by treating low-frequency spectral coefficients as a compact, domain-independent representation for adaptation. The eigenmap-guided self-training strategy combines this spectral descriptor with standard deep-feature pseudo-labeling, drawing on the information maximization losses from TENT/SHOT while adding a Chamfer Distance constraint to preserve geometric fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that 3D TTA can be performed with roughly 90% fewer optimization parameters by operating in the spectral domain, which is practically significant for streaming or data-limited inference scenarios. However, scalability remains a clear limitation: the global eigendecomposition of the graph Laplacian becomes prohibitive for large point clouds, and the authors acknowledge this without providing a solution. An open question is whether multi-scale or local spectral analysis can retain the domain-invariance benefits while reducing computational cost. The honest reporting that diffusion-based CloudFixer still outperforms GSDTTA on basic noise corruptions (by ~8%) underscores that spectral adaptation is complementary rather than a universal replacement for denoising approaches.
