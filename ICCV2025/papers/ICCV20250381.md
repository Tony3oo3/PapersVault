## Problem

Existing referring visual grounding methods rely on direct vision-language interaction (self-attention or cross-attention between visual and linguistic tokens), which suffers from three limitations: a tendency to lock onto a single prominent target (failing on multi-object or no-object scenes), uncontrolled linguistic noise that degrades fine-grained visual representations, and quadratic time complexity that scales poorly with language length. The paper asks whether a mediated interaction strategy can simultaneously improve multi-target localization, suppress cross-modal redundancy, and reduce computational cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250381/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes an RGB image (processed by a ViT-B or Swin-B backbone into multi-scale visual features) and a natural language reference expression (encoded by BERT into a sequence of T tokens). It produces either a bounding box (REC) or a pixel-level segmentation mask (RES/GRES), with the image partitioned into N=16 regions each carrying an independent object-presence score; when all region scores are negative, the model predicts no target. Training is multi-task, jointly optimizing detection and segmentation losses on the same input pair.

## Method

RaAM introduces N learnable region-aware anchors that alternately interact with language (via cross-attention in Linguistic Anchor Learning) and vision (via bidirectional cross-attention in Visual Anchor Learning) across K=4 hierarchical stages, with anchors explicitly initialized only in the first J=2 stages and propagated forward thereafter. A Language-to-Vision (L2V) gate, implemented as a two-layer MLP with Tanh, adaptively rescales anchor elements to suppress low-frequency linguistic noise before it reaches the visual modality. The anchors act as cross-modal buffers, reducing time complexity to linear in the number of visual tokens (M ≫ T > N) compared to direct self- or cross-attention. At prediction time, each anchor is element-wise multiplied with its corresponding regional feature, and separate MLP heads output per-region object presence, bounding boxes, and segmentation masks. Training uses a composite loss combining region-level cross-entropy (for presence), pixel-level focal + boundary-weighted dice loss (for segmentation), and smooth-L1 + GIoU loss (for detection), with the boundary weight β=1.2 applied to dilated-eroded edge regions to sharpen mask boundaries.

## Results

On REC (Precision@0.5), RaAM-RVG with ViT-B achieves 91.45/93.42/88.71 on RefCOCO val/testA/testB, 84.78/89.23/79.24 on RefCOCO+, and 86.78/87.71 on G-Ref, setting new SOTA across all splits. On RES (oIoU), the same model reaches 79.35/81.22/77.81 on RefCOCO and 71.30/72.09 on G-Ref, surpassing the previous best (EEVG) by 1.1 and 2.2 points respectively. On GRES (cIoU/gIoU), RaAM-RVG achieves 67.35/70.02 on gRefCOCO Val, outperforming ReLA by 3.15/4.52 points and CGFormer by 5.07/7.01 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RaAM-RVG (ViT-B) outperforms the previous SOTA PolyFormer (Swin-B) by 1.07/0.53/1.55 points on RefCOCO val/testA/testB and by 0.95/1.80 points on G-Ref val/test for REC. On RES, RaAM-RVG (ViT-B) surpasses EEVG (ViT-B) by 1.12 points on RefCOCO val oIoU and by 3.04 points on RefCOCO+ testA, while also exceeding PolyFormer by 3.39 points on RefCOCO val. For GRES, RaAM-RVG achieves 67.35 cIoU on gRefCOCO Val, beating ReLA (64.20) by 3.15 points and CGFormer (62.28) by 5.07 points, with similar margins on TestA and TestB. The applicability experiments (Table 4) confirm that replacing the interaction module in LAVT, PolyFormer, HyperSeg, and GSVA with RaAM consistently improves cIoU by 3.8–4.4 points, demonstrating broad transferability. Notably, RaAM-RVG achieves these gains with lower computational cost than self-attention-based alternatives, as the anchoring mechanism reduces time complexity to linear scale relative to the number of visual tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant Direct Interaction paradigm in Transformer-based RVG methods (where vision and language tokens interact via self-attention or cross-attention) by introducing learnable region-aware anchors as explicit cross-modal intermediaries. Conceptually, it repurposes the idea of learnable queries (seen in DETR-style detection and prompt-based segmentation) but repositions them as region-level object-presence indicators that alternately attend to language and vision rather than serving as final prediction queries. The L2V gate for controlling linguistic-to-visual information flow and the boundary-weighted pixel loss are novel design elements not present in prior RVG architectures like LAVT, PolyFormer, or EEVG, which all rely on direct or prompt-mediated cross-modal attention without an explicit buffering mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The efficiency argument—reducing interaction complexity from quadratic (in visual token count) to linear—is practically significant for deploying RVG in real-time or resource-constrained settings, and the demonstrated plug-and-play applicability (replacing interaction modules in LAVT, PolyFormer, and integrating into MLLMs like HyperSeg and GSVA) broadens its relevance beyond a single model. The GRES results (67.35 cIoU vs. 64.20 for ReLA) are notable because GRES with multi-object and no-object scenarios remains an underexplored and practically important setting. Open questions include whether the linear-complexity advantage persists at larger backbone scales (the paper only tests ViT-B and Swin-B), how the fixed N=16 region partition generalizes to very high-resolution or dense scenes, and whether the anchor-based approach can be extended to video or 3D grounding without losing its efficiency edge.
