## Problem

Autoregressive LLM-based video generation excels at causal and semantic modeling but suffers from low visual fidelity and error accumulation, while diffusion-based generation produces high-quality visuals but lacks explicit causal structure and semantic interpretability. No existing framework successfully combines the strengths of both paradigms to achieve coherent, semantically faithful, and visually compelling video generation simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250569/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (encoded via T5-XXL) along with two control conditions—a frames condition specifying the desired video length and a motion score (0–1) controlling the degree of motion—and produces an RGB video at approximately 480×720 resolution and 8 fps. For long video generation, the diffusion stage operates in a chunk-wise streaming fashion, using the first half of each chunk as a prompt to generate the second half, enabling arbitrarily long sequences without full-sequence conditioning.

## Method

LanDiff employs a coarse-to-fine two-stage pipeline: a 2B LLaMA-based language model autoregressively generates compact 1D semantic tokens (achieving ~14,000× compression via a query-based VQ-VAE tokenizer over Theia SSL features with MP4-inspired I/P-frame grouping), and a streaming MMDiT-based diffusion model (initialized from CogVideoX-2B) refines these tokens into VAE latent features using ControlNet-style conditioning. The tokenizer compresses 3D visual features into 1D discrete representations by using a small number of query tokens per frame group, with I-frames receiving more tokens than P-frames to exploit temporal redundancy. The diffusion model is trained with a chunk-wise streaming strategy where the first half of each chunk serves as a clean prompt (with 20% random masking) to maintain appearance continuity across chunks. This division of labor lets the LLM handle causal/semantic structure at low bit-rate while the diffusion model adds perceptual detail at high bit-rate, avoiding each paradigm's respective weaknesses.

## Results

On the VBench T2V benchmark, LanDiff (5B) achieves a Total Score of 85.43, Quality Score of 86.13, and Semantic Score of 82.61, surpassing all open-source models including the 13B HunyuanVideo (83.24) and commercial systems such as Sora (84.28), Hailuo (83.41), and Kling (81.85). On VBench-Long for long video generation, LanDiff achieves a Total Score of 68.34 with a Dynamic Degree of 72.86 and Aesthetic Quality of 60.96, outperforming all open-source long-video baselines by at least 3 points. An ablation with a 7B DiT model trained on identical data (81.85) confirms that the hybrid LLM+Diffusion architecture contributes approximately 3.6 points over a pure diffusion approach.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LanDiff (5B) outperforms every open-source baseline on VBench total score, beating the largest open-source model HunyuanVideo (13B, 83.24) by 2.19 points while using less than half the parameters. Among commercial closed-source systems, LanDiff surpasses Sora (84.28) by 1.15 points, Hailuo (83.41) by 2.02 points, Gen-3 (82.32) by 3.11 points, and Kling (81.85) by 3.58 points. The closest hybrid competitor, ARLON (5B, LLM+Diffusion), scores 82.31, trailing LanDiff by 3.12 points, with the gap driven primarily by Dynamic Degree (92.71 vs. 72.22) and Semantic Score (82.61 vs. 77.27). A 7B DiT control trained on the same data achieves only 81.85, confirming that the coarse-to-fine LLM+Diffusion decomposition—not merely model scale or data—accounts for the performance gain. On VBench-Long, LanDiff (68.34) leads ARLON (65.09) by 3.25 points, OpenSora-V1.2 (64.24) by 4.10 points, and StreamingT2V (62.92) by 5.42 points, while maintaining a favorable balance between Dynamic Degree (72.86) and Subject Consistency (95.41) that no single baseline matches.

## Relation to Prior Work

LanDiff carves a hybrid position between two competing T2V paradigms: autoregressive LLM-based generation (VideoPoet, Emu3, VideoGPT) and diffusion-based generation (CogVideoX, HunyuanVideo, Sora). It draws the query-based compression mechanism from TiTok [63] and the zero-initialized conditioning injection from ControlNet [67], but departs conceptually from both lines by assigning them to different stages of a coarse-to-fine pipeline: the LLM generates compact semantic tokens (low-bit, MP4 I-frame/P-frame inspired grouping) while a streaming diffusion model refines them into perceptual VAE latents (high-bit). This rate-distortion-inspired division of labor is the paper's core architectural departure from prior work that uses either paradigm end-to-end.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a 5B hybrid model outperforms a 13B pure-diffusion model (HunyuanVideo) and commercial systems (Sora, Kling, Hailuo) on VBench suggests that architectural synergy can substitute for raw parameter scale, which is practically significant for resource-constrained deployment. The ~14,000× compression ratio and the MP4-inspired frame grouping offer a concrete recipe for reducing token sequences in video generation. Open questions include: whether the approach generalizes beyond the 200M-pair internal dataset (not publicly released), how the three separately trained components (tokenizer, LLM, diffusion) interact at scale, and whether the chunk-wise streaming strategy degrades gracefully for very long (>60s) videos.
