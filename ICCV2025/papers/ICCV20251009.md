## Problem

Previous lecture slides datasets focused on narrow sub-tasks (text detection, segmentation, retrieval) and lacked both large-scale data and comprehensive understanding benchmarks covering global summarization and fine-grained QA. Moreover, existing methods ignore the complex spatial and semantic text relations within slides, which are critical for grasping internal logic and generating coherent responses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251009/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a lecture chunk (average 3.2 slides, up to hundreds) as RGB images plus pre-annotated OCR text with 2D bounding boxes, and optionally a natural-language query for the QA task. For Lecture Summary, it outputs a free-form paragraph (average 124.6 words) integrating key information across all slides in the chunk; for Lecture QA, it outputs a short answer (average 4.1 words) grounded in the specific slide relevant to the question. The dataset spans 15 disciplines with 370,078 slides and 25,542 lectures, segmented into 114,472 topic-aligned chunks.

## Method

SlideParser builds on a UDOP-initialized multi-modal Transformer Encoder that fuses text tokens (with 1D word and 2D layout position embeddings) and image patches, followed by a T5 decoder for text generation. The key contribution is a Text Relation Prediction Module with two sub-components: a Relation Prediction Head (RPH) that computes a relation matrix by combining a 12-D spatial box-delta vector (processed through an MLP) with a semantic attention score from text embeddings, and a Relation Attention Layer (RAL) that converts the predicted relation matrix into a binary attention mask (assigning −∞ to unrelated pairs) applied in N self-attention layers to enhance attention between related texts and suppress it between unrelated ones. To supervise RPH without ground-truth annotations, a weak-supervision pipeline called DL-Cluster generates pseudo relation labels by dynamically determining the optimal number of clusters per slide via K-means++ and the Silhouette method (K=8), then combining spatial cluster-assignment scores with Sentence Transformer cosine similarity (β₁=0.7, β₂=0.3) and binarizing at 0.5. The total training objective is the sum of a cross-entropy loss for text generation and a binary cross-entropy loss for relation prediction.

## Results

On the Lecture Summary task in LecSlides-370K, SlideParser achieves ROUGE-1 of 42.2, ROUGE-L of 25.3, and METEOR of 31.3 on the validation set, surpassing the strongest baseline UDOP (36.1 / 22.1 / 26.4) by 6.1, 3.2, and 4.9 points respectively. On Lecture QA, SlideParser reaches EM 23.7 and F1 33.0 (val), outperforming the best NLP baseline T5-large (20.9 / 27.4) by 2.8 EM and 5.6 F1. On the external SlideVQA benchmark, SlideParser attains EM 37.5 and F1 43.9 (test), and with pretraining on LecSlides-370K (SlideParser†) further improves to EM 42.4 and F1 47.7, beating the previous best M3D (33.5 / 41.7) by 8.9 EM and 6.0 F1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SlideParser outperforms all 13 baselines across both LecSlides-370K tasks and SlideVQA. Among NLP-only methods, T5-large (R-1 35.1, F1 27.1) and BART-large (R-1 35.0, F1 26.3) are the strongest text-only baselines but trail SlideParser by 6.6 ROUGE-1 on Summary and 5.0 F1 on QA, confirming that textual content alone is insufficient. Vision-language models that process only visual modality (Vid2Seq, mPLUG) perform worst, with Vid2Seq scoring only 17.8 ROUGE-1 and 6.7 EM, indicating that ignoring OCR text severely degrades slide understanding. Among document understanding models, UDOP (the architectural backbone) is the closest competitor at 35.3 ROUGE-1 and 18.7 EM, yet SlideParser's Text Relation Prediction Module adds 6.4 ROUGE-1 and 4.9 EM over it. On SlideVQA, SlideParser beats M3D (the prior SOTA) by 4.0 EM and 2.2 F1 without pretraining, and by 8.9 EM and 6.0 F1 with LecSlides-370K pretraining, while also surpassing LayoutT5 (31.7 EM), FiD (30.4 EM), and LayoutLMv2 (21.4 EM) by wide margins.

## Relation to Prior Work

SlideParser is architecturally built on UDOP (Tang et al., CVPR 2023), reusing its pre-trained Transformer Encoder and T5-based Text Decoder, and extends it with a Text Relation Prediction Module (RPH + RAL) that explicitly models and leverages complex text-to-text relations within slides. This departs from prior document understanding methods (LayoutLMv3, Donut) that rely solely on 2D positional embeddings to capture layout, arguing that slides require explicit relation modeling due to their more complex and flexible text arrangements. On the data side, the paper extends the slides understanding literature beyond SlideVQA's single QA task by introducing a Lecture Summary task and scaling the dataset to 9.7× more lectures and 7.1× more slides across 15 disciplines. The DL-Cluster pseudo-label pipeline also departs from traditional fixed-k clustering (K-means, HAC) or density-based methods (DBSCAN) used in prior document layout work by dynamically selecting cluster count per slide and jointly incorporating spatial and semantic signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LecSlides-370K fills a clear gap as the largest multi-task slides understanding benchmark, and its demonstrated utility as a pre-training corpus (SlideParser† gains ~5 EM on SlideVQA) suggests it will become a standard resource for the document/slides understanding community. The finding that explicit text relation prediction and attention masking provide consistent gains over UDOP's positional-embedding-only approach offers a practical recipe for handling visually complex layouts beyond slides. Open questions include whether the weakly-supervised relation labels generalize to unseen layout styles, whether the method transfers to other document types (scientific papers, forms), and whether the Slideshare-sourced data introduces domain bias that limits real-world applicability.
