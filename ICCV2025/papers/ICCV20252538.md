## Problem

Training multi-agent, multi-frame, end-to-end frameworks for cooperative perception and prediction is unstable: one-time training fails due to complex spatiotemporal feature interactions and task conflicts, while existing solutions rely on manually designed multi-stage pipelines that are fragile, hard to generalize, and require extensive monitoring. Compounding this, V2X datasets are expensive to collect, making it difficult to scale supervised learning across multiple tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252538/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw LiDAR point clouds from the ego agent and up to N cooperative agents (all projected into the ego coordinate system), along with historical frame sequences, map context, and agent metadata (poses, timestamps). A 3D encoder produces BEV features per frame, a temporal module captures cross-frame dynamics, and a multi-agent fusion module aggregates features before feeding two task heads. Outputs are 3D bounding boxes with class labels (detection) and future trajectories (prediction), evaluated jointly via the EPA metric that penalizes both missed detections and prediction errors.

## Method

TurboTrain comprises two stages. In the Pretrain stage, a masked reconstruction objective trains the full 3D backbone, temporal module, and multi-agent fusion module simultaneously (unlike prior work that pretrains only the backbone), using a dual decoder that reconstructs both raw point clouds (Chamfer distance) and sparse voxel occupancy (focal loss) from highly masked BEV grids, with the ego agent reconstructing both its own and collaborators' point clouds to learn a task-agnostic 4D representation. In the Balance stage, a conflict-suppressing gradient aligner detects negative inner products between task gradients and projects out the conflicting component before updating shared parameters, but to avoid the 1.5× GPU overhead of per-step balancing, it alternates n free-training steps with m conflict-suppressing steps (optimal at n=2000, m=1000), combining the stability of gradient correction with the exploration benefits of random weighting.

## Results

On the V2XPnP-Seq-VC dataset, TurboTrain applied to V2XPnP achieves AP@0.5 of 72.2% and EPA of 45.5%, improving over the manual multi-stage training strategy (AP 70.8%, EPA 41.8%) by +1.4 AP and +3.7 EPA. In the V2V setting, TurboTrain yields AP 68.6% and EPA 38.5% versus 66.3% and 36.2% for manual training. The pretraining stage alone (without the Balance stage) reaches AP 70.3% and EPA 43.4%, already surpassing the full manual pipeline, while the Balance stage adds a further +2.1 EPA over the no-balance setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TurboTrain consistently outperforms all spatial-fusion baselines (F-Cooper, DiscoNet, CoBEVFlow, FFNet) on both AP and EPA across VC and V2V settings; the largest gap is over No Multi-Agent Fusion (+13.1 AP, +10.3 EPA in VC). Against the strongest baseline V2XPnP with manual multi-stage training, TurboTrain gains +1.4 AP and +3.7 EPA in VC and +2.6 AP and +2.3 EPA in V2V, while also reducing FDE (2.75 vs. 3.07 m in VC). In the pretraining ablation (Table 2), TurboTrain's multi-agent spatiotemporal pretraining surpasses CooPre by +5.5 AP and +3.8 EPA, CORE by +7.3 AP, and T-MAE by +9.1 AP, confirming that both multi-agent and temporal dimensions are necessary. For the Balance stage (Table 3), TurboTrain's conflict-suppressing gradient achieves the best EPA (45.5%) and lowest ADE (1.49 m) and MR (35.0%), edging out CAGrad (EPA 44.1%, AP 73.1%) on overall prediction quality despite CAGrad's slightly higher AP, while GradNorm and MoCo underperform significantly (EPA 34.2% and 38.2% respectively), indicating that gradient conflict rather than dominance is the primary bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

TurboTrain builds directly on the V2XPnP spatiotemporal fusion architecture [79] and adapts the PCGrad-style gradient projection [72] into a hybrid free/balanced training schedule. It departs from prior multi-agent pretraining (CooPre, CORE) by jointly training the 3D backbone, temporal module, and multi-agent fusion module with dual point-level and voxel-level reconstruction, rather than pretraining only the backbone or reconstructing compressed BEV features. It also extends prior MTL balancing methods (CAGrad, GradNorm, MoCo) by introducing a periodic free-training phase that avoids the 1.5× GPU overhead of per-step gradient correction, positioning itself as a training paradigm rather than a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical bottleneck in V2X research: multi-agent end-to-end models currently require fragile, manually designed multi-stage training pipelines that are sensitive to task configuration and architecture changes. By showing that task-agnostic spatiotemporal pretraining plus periodic gradient conflict suppression can replace these pipelines, TurboTrain lowers the barrier to adding new tasks or agents to cooperative driving systems. However, evaluation is limited to a single dataset (V2XPnP-Seq) and primarily one backbone (V2XPnP), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to other V2X benchmarks, larger agent counts, or additional downstream tasks such as planning. The data-efficiency results (25–75% sampling) are promising but would benefit from validation on larger-scale datasets.
