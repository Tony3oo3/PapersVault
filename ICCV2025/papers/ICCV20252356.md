## Problem

Memory-based semi-supervised VOS methods rely heavily on temporal consistency of pixel- and object-level features while neglecting the inherent geometric structure of objects, causing failures when targets are occluded or visually similar to the background. Existing approaches that incorporate edge information either require expensive per-frame edge prediction networks or use edges only for coarse tasks like motion magnitude estimation, leaving fine-grained boundary refinement unaddressed in real-time VOS.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252356/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video frame sequence (480×480 crops) with a binary object mask on the first frame, plus the previous n frames and their predicted masks for memory construction. Each frame is also processed by a Canny filter (thresholds 50/200) to produce a 1-channel rough edge map. The primary output is a per-frame binary segmentation mask, with an intermediate object-level structure map (in logit form, not binarized) that encodes target-specific boundary and layering information at the original spatial resolution.

## Method

OASIS introduces a lightweight structure refinement pipeline: Canny edges are fused into multi-scale ResNet-18 features via Hadamard product (Eq. 3), then a small transposed-convolution decoder—guided by 30×30 object memory features from the Cutie-style memory encoder—predicts a target-specific structure map (Eq. 4). This structure map is fed back into the image features through a second Hadamard product (Eq. 5) to amplify boundary regions before the final memory-based decoder produces the mask. To handle high uncertainty in occluded/overlapping regions, the paper introduces Evidential Deep Learning with a Dirichlet distribution over class probabilities, adding an evidential loss (Eq. 6) with KL regularization to the standard CE + Dice objective. Training proceeds in two stages: pre-training on static images with pseudo-video sequences (excluding the structure decoder and EDL loss for robustness), followed by main training on video data with point supervision (12,544 sampled points) over 125K iterations.

## Results

OASIS achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four SVOS benchmarks: J&F of 91.6 (F) on DAVIS-17 validation, J&F of 87.0 (F) on DAVIS-17 test-dev, J&F of 66.3 (F) on MOSE, and G of 86.6 on YouTubeVOS-2019 validation. On the DAVIS-17 validation set, OASIS outperforms the previous SOTA (Cutie) by 1.5 in J&F, 1.2 in J, and 1.9 in F. The method maintains a competitive inference speed of 48 FPS on DAVIS while using only 31M parameters (ResNet-18 backbone + ~2M structure decoder).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OASIS outperforms all nine compared baselines across every benchmark. Against the closest competitor Cutie (CVPR'24), OASIS gains +1.5 J&F on DAVIS-17 Val (88.3 vs. 86.8), +0.4 J&F on DAVIS-17 Test (83.2 vs. 82.8), +0.9 J&F on MOSE (62.1 vs. 61.2), and +0.4 G on YouTubeVOS-2019 (86.6 vs. 86.6). The largest margin is on DAVIS-17 Val F-score (+1.9 over Cutie), reflecting the structure refinement module's impact on boundary accuracy. On the more challenging MOSE benchmark (dense objects, heavy occlusion), OASIS leads Cutie by +1.0 in F-score, confirming the benefit of edge-guided structure modeling in complex scenes. Earlier methods like XMem (ECCV'22) and DEVA (ICCV'23) trail by 2.1 and 1.5 J&F respectively on DAVIS-17 Val, while STCN and RDE lag by 2.9 and 4.1 J&F, indicating that memory-matching and efficiency-focused designs sacrifice structural fidelity. Under the Trained-with-MOSE setting (Table 2), OASIS extends its lead on DAVIS-17 Test to +1.7 J&F over Cutie, suggesting the structural prior generalizes well when trained on more complex data.

## Relation to Prior Work

OASIS builds directly on Cutie's (CVPR 2024) memory-based SVOS architecture, reusing its memory encoder, object-query mechanism, and decoder design, while introducing a structure refinement module that fuses Canny edge priors with stored object features to produce a target-specific structure map. The method also adapts Evidential Deep Learning (Sensoy et al., NeurIPS 2018) for the first time in SVOS, adding a Dirichlet-based uncertainty loss to handle occluded and high-similarity regions. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior edge-assisted segmentation work—which typically requires learned edge networks or complex convolutional layers—by showing that a classical Canny filter provides sufficient global edge priors for a lightweight structure decoder to refine, avoiding the inference cost of end-to-end edge prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent practical limitation in SVOS—degradation under occlusion and inter-object similarity—while maintaining real-time speed (48 FPS), making it relevant for downstream applications like autonomous driving and video editing. The first application of evidential learning to SVOS opens a direction for uncertainty-aware segmentation, though the ablation shows its individual contribution is modest (~0.2–0.3 J&F). Open questions include whether the Canny-based structure prior generalizes to highly dynamic or low-contrast scenes, whether the approach extends naturally to multi-object or referring segmentation settings, and whether the structure map could be learned end-to-end without the classical edge detector as a crutch.
