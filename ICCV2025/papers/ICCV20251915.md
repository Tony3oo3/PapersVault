## Problem

Encoder-free VLMs that learn visual perception from scratch within a unified decoder-only backbone suffer from severe vision-language interference: prior decoupling strategies (re-parameterization, MoE on FFN only) leave significant weight shifts in the LLM's attention and normalization layers, limiting both visual learning and linguistic retention. The paper asks what architectural decomposition and training recipe are actually needed to build a competitive encoder-free VLM without pre-trained vision encoders or visual feature supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251915/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Images of arbitrary aspect ratio (up to ~2.5M pixels) are encoded by a two-layer convolutional patch embedding (stride-16 Conv → GELU → stride-2 Conv) producing up to 2,500 lossless 3584-dim patch tokens, with a <CLS> token prepended and <SPL> row-separator tokens inserted. Text is tokenized via the Qwen2.5 tokenizer into the same 3584-dim space. The model outputs autoregressive text (greedy decoding, zero-shot) for VQA, captioning, OCR, chart reading, and multi-step visual reasoning tasks.

## Method

EVEv2.0 introduces a Divide-and-Conquer (DaC) architecture: every transformer layer (multi-head attention Q/K/V projections, both LayerNorms, and the FFN) is split into modality-specific parameter sets for vision and text tokens, while cross-modal interaction still occurs through the shared softmax attention computation over the concatenated sequence. This full decomposition—rather than partial MoE on FFN or re-parameterized weights—eliminates representational conflicts and allows the LLM backbone to remain frozen during visual pre-training. Training proceeds in four stages: (1) freeze the LLM and train only the patch embedding on 10M re-captioned pairs; (2.1) unfreeze vision-specific layers and train on 48M pairs with progressive resolution (800²→1600²); (2.2) unfreeze all layers on 15M multi-task QA data; (3) SFT on 7M instruction data. A DenseFusion++ captioning engine (built on LLaVA-1.6 7B with multiple vision experts) generates high-quality synthetic annotations, replacing the need for visual feature supervision and enabling efficient data scaling with only ~100M total training samples.

## Results

EVEv2.0 (7B, 92M pre-training + 7.3M fine-tuning data, 2500 visual tokens) achieves 39.3 on MMMU, 71.4 on SEEDBench-IMG, 96.2 on ScienceQA-IMG, 71.1 on TextVQA, and 702 on OCRBench, outperforming all encoder-free counterparts including EVE (32.6 MMMU, 64.9 SQA) and Mono-InternVL (33.7 MMMU, 93.6 SQA). Against encoder-based models of similar capacity, EVEv2.0 is competitive with LLaVA-1.6 (7B), surpassing it on MMMU (39.3 vs 35.1), SEED (71.4 vs 64.7), SQA (96.2 vs 70.2), TextVQA (71.1 vs 64.9), ChartQA (73.9 vs 54.8), and OCRBench (702 vs 532), while trailing on MME (1709 vs 1842) and GQA (62.9 vs 64.2). The model uses only 100M publicly available training samples, demonstrating strong data efficiency for an encoder-free architecture trained from scratch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against encoder-free baselines, EVEv2.0 achieves clear and consistent wins: it surpasses EVE by +6.7 on MMMU, +31.3 on SQA-IMG, +14.3 on TextVQA, and +304 on OCRBench; it outperforms Mono-InternVL (1.8B) on MMMU (+5.6), SEED (+4.0), SQA (+2.6), and AI2D (+6.2), though Mono-InternVL retains advantages on MME (1875 vs 1709), TextVQA (72.6 vs 71.1), and OCRBench (767 vs 702) due to 13× more pre-training data. Against encoder-based models of similar 7B capacity, EVEv2.0 is competitive with LLaVA-1.6, winning on MMMU (+4.2), SEED (+6.7), SQA (+26.0), TextVQA (+6.2), ChartQA (+19.1), and OCRBench (+170), but trailing on MME (−133) and GQA (−1.3). Against the stronger Cambrian (10B+ data), EVEv2.0 loses on MMMU (−3.4), MMBench (−9.6), and SEED (−3.3) but wins on SQA (+15.8) and OCRBench (+88). Against the top encoder-based LLaVA-OV (10B+ data, 7290 tokens), EVEv2.0 trails on most perception benchmarks (MMMU −8.0, MMBench −15.4, MME −289) but remains close on OCRBench (702 vs 697), confirming that the remaining gap is primarily driven by data scale rather than architectural limitations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct successor to EVE (Diao et al., 2024), which first demonstrated that a single transformer block can replace a pre-trained vision encoder in a VLM. The key architectural departure from EVE and from concurrent encoder-free work like Mono-InternVL (which uses MoE to partially decouple modalities) is the full Divide-and-Conquer design: modality-specific parameters for attention, feed-forward, and layer-normalization within a unified decoder-only backbone. The DenseFusion++ captioning engine is built on LLaVA-1.6, repurposing it as a multi-expert annotation tool rather than a VLM baseline. The paper systematically ablates re-parameterization (EVEv1.2), MoE (EVEv1.5), and full decomposition (EVEv2.0) to isolate which level of modality separation is actually necessary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EVEv2.0 provides the most thorough empirical roadmap to date for building encoder-free VLMs from scratch, with clear architectural and training-recipe guidance that practitioners can follow. The finding that full modality-wise decomposition (not just FFN or attention) is required to prevent representational interference is a concrete design principle for future unified multimodal architectures. However, the 7B model trained on only ~100M samples still trails strong encoder-based systems (LLaVA-OV, Cambrian) by a meaningful margin, and the authors explicitly frame this as a baseline study rather than a SOTA claim. Open questions include whether the architecture gap closes at larger scale, whether the modality-specific parameter overhead becomes prohibitive, and how the approach extends to video and audio modalities.
