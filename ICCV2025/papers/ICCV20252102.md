## Problem

Existing 3D semi-supervised object detection methods rely on a single teacher network to generate pseudo-labels, which struggles to handle the wide variation in object aspect ratios and sizes across categories (e.g., vehicles vs. pedestrians vs. cyclists). Additionally, the inherent sparsity of LiDAR point clouds limits the teacher's ability to learn from objects that are far away or occluded, degrading pseudo-label quality and ultimately capping student network performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252102/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes LiDAR point clouds (3D coordinates plus additional channels such as intensity and ring, from 64- or 128-channel sensors) and produces 3D bounding boxes with 9 degrees of freedom (center, dimensions, orientation) along with class labels. During semi-supervised training, the framework jointly processes a small labeled set and a large unlabeled set; the PointGen module additionally operates on the point cloud geometry by partitioning it into angular pie-shaped sectors around the LiDAR center to identify and augment sparse regions before feeding data to the teacher networks.

## Method

MultipleTeachers replaces the conventional single-teacher paradigm with multiple category-specialized teacher networks (grouped by similar aspect ratios: vehicles, pedestrians, cyclists) that collaboratively generate pseudo-labels for a shared student. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) Burn-In pre-trains the baseline 2-stage detector (PV-RCNN or Voxel-RCNN) on labeled data; (2) Fine-Tuning specializes each teacher on its assigned category group while treating other categories as background; (3) Mutual Learning applies weak augmentations to unlabeled data, each teacher predicts category-specific pseudo-labels, the aggregated candidates are filtered via dynamic confidence/IoU thresholds, and the student is trained on the combined labeled + pseudo-labeled loss. Teacher weights are updated via a category-wise EMA (C-EMA) that uses an Adjust function to split the student's anchor-head weights by class index ranges. A complementary PointGen module divides the point cloud into pie-shaped sectors, scores each sector by a weighted combination of confidence and point density, and transplants points from dense sectors into sparse ones to enrich the teacher's input and improve pseudo-label quality for distant or occluded objects.

## Results

On KITTI, MultipleTeachers achieves 64.6, 72.2, and 71.9 mAP at 1%, 2%, and 15% labeled splits, respectively, outperforming all prior 3D SSOD methods. On Waymo Open Dataset (WOD), the method reaches 47.5 mAP (Level 1) with 1% labeled + 99% unlabeled data, surpassing the previous best (PTPM, 46.8 mAP) and A-Teacher (46.6 mAP). On the newly introduced LiO dataset, MultipleTeachers achieves 84.0 mAP with 15% labeled data on the 112K-frame training set, exceeding HSSDA by 23.2 mAP over the 16K-frame setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MultipleTeachers outperforms all 10 compared baselines across every dataset and labeled split. On KITTI, it surpasses HINTED (60.5 mAP) by 4.1 mAP and HSSDA (59.5 mAP) by 5.1 mAP at the 1% split, with gains of 1.4 and 3.6 mAP respectively at 2%. On WOD (Large split), it beats PTPM (46.8 mAP L1) by 0.7 mAP and A-Teacher (46.6 mAP L1) by 0.9 mAP, while the gap widens to 6.7 mAP over A-Teacher on the Small split. On the LiO dataset, the advantage is most pronounced: MultipleTeachers exceeds HSSDA by 1.3–8.4 mAP across 1%, 2%, and 15% splits, and by 23.2 mAP when comparing the 112K-frame setting (84.0 vs. 60.8 mAP). The supervised baselines PV-RCNN and Voxel-RCNN are outperformed by 14–21 mAP on KITTI and 11–24 mAP on LiO, confirming the substantial benefit of the semi-supervised framework. The method also generalizes across detector backbones, consistently exceeding HSSDA by 0.4–3.6 mAP on PV-RCNN, Voxel-RCNN, and SECOND.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the teacher-student paradigm for 3D semi-supervised object detection (established by Mean Teacher and adapted by HSSDA, A-Teacher, PTPM, and others) by replacing the single teacher with multiple category-specialized teacher networks that collaboratively generate pseudo-labels. It departs from prior threshold-based pseudo-label filtering strategies (3DIoUMatch, DDS3D, HSSDA) by instead addressing the root cause of poor pseudo-labels—LiDAR sparsity—through the PointGen pseudo-points generator, which redistributes points from dense to sparse angular sectors. The category-wise EMA (C-EMA) update mechanism is a targeted modification of standard EMA that accounts for the differing output dimensions of category-specialized anchor heads, and the framework is demonstrated to be detector-agnostic across PV-RCNN, Voxel-RCNN, and SECOND.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent 3–8 mAP gains over strong baselines (HSSDA, A-Teacher, PTPM) across three datasets, combined with a new 117K-frame 128-channel LiDAR dataset (LiO) covering seven object classes, provide both a practical method upgrade and a much-needed benchmark for 3D SSOD in diverse urban settings. The finding that multiple teachers without C-EMA actually underperform a single teacher with standard EMA (68.0 vs. 68.6 mAP in the ablation) highlights that the architectural split alone is insufficient—the category-aware weight synchronization is the critical enabler, a nuance that may inform future multi-expert SSL designs. Open questions include whether the approach scales gracefully to more than three category groups, the computational overhead of maintaining multiple full teacher networks, and whether PointGen's angular-sector heuristic generalizes to non-circular LiDAR geometries or multi-sensor fusion setups.
