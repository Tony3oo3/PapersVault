## Problem

Existing VLN scene representations either lack explicit 3D geometric structure (topological graphs, 2D semantic maps) or rely on dense volumetric sampling that is computationally redundant and fails to capture object boundaries. Moreover, most methods are trained in closed-vocabulary settings, limiting their ability to generalize to unseen scenes with diverse and novel semantics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252638/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At each navigable node, the agent receives a 360-degree panoramic observation of K RGB-D views (resized to 224×224 for efficiency) and a natural language instruction. The RGB-D observations are back-projected into sparse pseudo-lidar point clouds to initialize the 3D Gaussian Map, which is constructed online in ~0.07 seconds per step. The output is a next-step action selected from neighboring nodes, backtracked nodes, or a STOP token, with the 3D Gaussian Map serving as an intermediate structured representation.

## Method

The method constructs a unified 3D Gaussian Map through three components. First, the Egocentric Scene Map (ESM) initializes differentiable 3D Gaussian primitives from sparse pseudo-lidar point clouds and refines them via tile-based differentiable rendering supervised by RGB, depth, and semantic losses. Second, Open-Set Semantic Grouping (OSG) uses SAM2 for automatic 2D segmentation and CLIP for compact semantic embeddings, then assigns each Gaussian a scalar semantic parameter optimized through the same rendering pipeline, grouping Gaussians by object instance or stuff membership without manual 3D annotations. Third, Multi-Level Action Prediction (MAP) scores candidate waypoints at three granularities—scene (global average pooling over all Gaussians), view (Gaussians in the forward-facing observation), and instance (per-object aggregated Gaussians)—each processed by a multi-layer transformer and combined via nearest-neighbor mapping to the action space. Training follows a two-stage protocol: pretraining with MLM and SAP objectives (plus object grounding for REVERIE), followed by DAgger fine-tuning.

## Results

On R2R val unseen, the method achieves 77% SR and 66% SPL, outperforming the previous best (BEVBert at 75% SR, 64% SPL) by 2 points on both metrics. On REVERIE val unseen, it reaches 36.73% RGS and 26.74% RGSPL, surpassing BEVBert (34.71% RGS, 24.44% RGSPL) by 2.02% and 2.30% respectively. On R4R val unseen, the method attains 47% SR, 60% CLS, 52% nDTW, and 35% SDTW, improving over HAMT by 2% in SR/CLS/nDTW and 3% in SDTW. The 3D Gaussian Map construction at each waypoint takes approximately 0.07 seconds, compatible with real-time robotic execution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 22 compared baselines across all three benchmarks. On R2R val unseen, it beats BEVBert [1] (75% SR, 64% SPL) by 2% in both SR and SPL, and surpasses BSG [47] (74% SR, 62% SPL) by 3% SR and 4% SPL. On REVERIE val unseen, it leads BEVBert [1] by 2.02% RGS (36.73 vs. 34.71) and 2.30% RGSPL (26.74 vs. 24.44), while also improving over DUET [13] (32.15% RGS, 23.03% RGSPL) by 4.58% and 3.71% respectively. On R4R val unseen, it exceeds HAMT [12] (45% SR, 58% CLS, 50% nDTW, 32% SDTW) by 2% in SR/CLS/nDTW and 3% in SDTW, and outperforms RelGraph [29] (36% SR, 34% SDTW) by 11% SR and 1% SDTW. The ablation baseline (row #1 in Table 4, matching DUET's numbers) confirms that the full pipeline (ESM + OSG + MAP) contributes a cumulative +5% SR on R2R and +4.58% RGS on REVERIE over the DUET backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework (Kerbl et al., ACM TOG 2023) for scene representation and the DUET navigation architecture (Chen et al., CVPR 2022) for action prediction and topological memory. It departs from prior VLN scene representations—topological graphs, top-down semantic maps, and dense NeRF-based volumetric maps—by introducing sparse, adaptive 3D Gaussians initialized from pseudo-lidar point clouds, which preserve geometric priors without the redundancy of uniform volumetric sampling. A key conceptual departure is the integration of open-set semantics via SAM2 segmentation and CLIP embeddings, moving beyond the closed-vocabulary settings that dominate existing VLN methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in VLN: the disconnect between geometric fidelity and semantic richness in scene representations. By unifying both in a single differentiable 3D Gaussian Map with open-set semantics, it offers a more generalizable and computationally efficient alternative to dense volumetric approaches. However, the method remains tied to discrete-node navigation on Matterport3D-style environments, and the 2–3% improvements over strong baselines (BEVBert, DUET) are incremental rather than transformative. Open questions include whether the approach scales to continuous navigation, larger-scale outdoor scenes, and whether the CLIP-based open-set semantics truly generalize beyond the distribution of CLIP's training data.
