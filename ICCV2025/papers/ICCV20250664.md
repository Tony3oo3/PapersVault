## Problem

Existing all-in-one weather restoration models rely on fixed network structures that cannot adapt to the distinct characteristics of different weather degradations (rain, snow, haze, raindrops), and they lack the iterative, observation-driven refinement process that human experts use for progressive image restoration. This limits both feature diversity across layers and the model's ability to capture degradation-specific patterns effectively.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250664/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image (e.g., 256×256 patches during training) affected by one or more adverse weather conditions and produces a restored, weather-free image. A routing prompt module first extracts degradation-specific instructions from the input, which then guide the restoration network. The restoration is refined iteratively over T sequential steps, with each step adjusting the routing prompt based on VLM-evaluated quality feedback before producing the next intermediate output.

## Method

MOERL combines a Mixture-of-Experts architecture with reinforcement learning for progressive weather image restoration. The weather restoration network is a U-Net built from MoE blocks, each containing a gate network that selects among two expert types—spatial experts (FocalNet-inspired context aggregation at full and downsampled resolutions) and channel experts (low-rank channel compression at varying rates)—guided by a routing prompt generated from the input image. The RL component uses a PPO actor-critic framework where the actor (routing prompt module + restoration network) adjusts degradation-relevant parameters and the critic estimates restoration quality, trained with a reward signal from the Q-ALIGN vision-language model that scores both image quality and degradation removal. This sequential optimization over T steps allows the model to iteratively refine its routing decisions, mimicking the progressive refinement process of human experts.

## Results

MOERL achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four benchmarks: 38.01 dB PSNR / 0.9748 SSIM on Snow100K-S, 32.98 dB / 0.9412 on Snow100K-L, 32.70 dB / 0.9428 on Outdoor-Rain, and 33.21 dB / 0.9450 on RainDrop. It surpasses the best all-in-one baseline (Histoformer) by up to 0.82 dB PSNR on Snow100K-L and 0.62 dB on Outdoor-Rain, and outperforms the general-purpose Restormer by 2.67 dB on deraining & dehazing. In terms of efficiency, MOERL uses only 12.52M parameters, 67.86G MACs, and 8,718.76 MB memory with 0.049 ms/image inference — 24% fewer parameters, 30% lower FLOPs, and 31% less memory than the next best method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOERL outperforms all 25 compared baselines across all four evaluation benchmarks. Against the strongest all-in-one competitor Histoformer [44], MOERL gains +0.60 dB PSNR on Snow100K-S, +0.82 dB on Snow100K-L, +0.62 dB on Outdoor-Rain, and +0.15 dB on RainDrop. It also surpasses weather-specific specialists: e.g., DDMSNet [63] (34.34 dB on Snow100K-S) and Restormer [60] (30.03 dB on Outdoor-Rain), the latter by a substantial 2.67 dB margin. Among all-in-one methods, the gap over the previous best (Histoformer) is consistent but modest (0.15–0.82 dB), while the gap over earlier all-in-one models like TransWeather [47] and WeatherDiff [37] is much larger (2–8 dB). On efficiency, MOERL (12.52M params, 67.86G MACs) is lighter than Restormer (26.10M, 140.99G), GridFormer (30.12M, 251.35G), and Histoformer (16.92M, 96.99G), achieving the fastest inference at 0.049 ms/image.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOERL builds on the Restormer Transformer architecture for feature refinement within its MoE blocks, adopts the sparsely-gated TopK routing mechanism from Shazeer et al. for expert selection, and leverages Q-ALIGN as a VLM-based reward signal for RL training. It departs from prior all-in-one weather restoration models (All-in-One, TransWeather, WeatherDiff, GridFormer, Histoformer) by replacing their fixed network structures with a Mixture-of-Experts layer containing two distinct expert types (channel-wise and spatial modulation), and by framing optimization as a sequential, progressive RL problem inspired by human expert refinement rather than a single-pass supervised training objective. This is the first work to combine MoE routing with PPO-based actor-critic optimization specifically for adverse weather image restoration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a compact 12.52M-parameter MoE+RL model can outperform larger all-in-one weather restoration networks (up to 30M parameters) while using 30% fewer MACs and 31% less memory, suggesting that adaptive expert routing is more effective than brute-force capacity scaling for multi-degradation restoration. The VLM-as-reward approach (Q-ALIGN) is the single largest contributor in ablations, opening a path toward human-aligned, metric-free optimization for low-level vision tasks. However, the RL component's marginal contribution (~0.3 dB over supervised-only training) raises questions about whether the sequential progressive framing is truly necessary or whether a simpler multi-stage training schedule could achieve similar gains. Open questions include scalability to additional degradation types, the sensitivity of the VLM reward to prompt design, and whether the RL training overhead justifies the modest performance delta in deployment settings.
