## Problem

The standard point cloud winding number formulation w_U diverges as the query point approaches a surface point, producing values far outside [0,1] near the input surface. This poor local approximation forces downstream methods like GCNO and WNNC to rely on ad-hoc heuristics—Voronoi-vertex-only query sampling and distance-truncation smoothing—to avoid optimization failure, limiting their accuracy and efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250443/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unoriented 3D point cloud (N points with no normal or orientation information) and produces a globally consistent outward-pointing unit normal at each point, which can then be fed to SPSR for mesh reconstruction. Point areas are estimated via KNN or Voronoi cell cross-sections and are jointly optimized with the normals. The winding number field is evaluated at query points that may be the input points themselves, random samples, or Voronoi vertices depending on the variant.

## Method

The core contribution is a bounded winding number w_B derived via a two-step projection: first project the unknown surface patch onto the tangent plane at the surface point, then project that planar region onto the sphere centered at the query point assuming radial symmetry, yielding a spherical-cap area that is inherently bounded by half the sphere's surface area. Algebraically, w_B is a nonlinear saturation of w_U: w_B = sign(x)/2 · (1 − 1/√(|4x|+1)), which correctly limits to ±1/2 as the query approaches the surface point and to 0 at the point itself. The paper integrates w_B into two distinct optimizers: (1) sGCNO, which replaces GCNO's expensive Voronoi diagram and L-BFGS with stochastic gradient descent over randomly sampled query points, and (2) WNNC variants, where the original linear system A(μ,ω)=b becomes the nonlinear system A_B(μ,ω)=b solved by Levenberg-Marquardt (for small clouds) or steepest descent on the original linear system with w_B used only in the WNNC gradient-alignment step (for large clouds).

## Results

On the GCNO dataset (18 shapes, 3k points), the best proposed variant WNNC(LM, A_B, G_B, Ω₁) achieves 99.944% PGP, 10.158° angular RMSE, and 0.140 chamfer distance in 1.5 s, substantially outperforming the original WNNC (14.516° RMSE) and GCNO (15.926° RMSE, 9123 s). The lightweight WNNC(SD, A, G_B, Ω₁) variant reaches 10.798° RMSE in just 0.4 s. On the harder Top20SION dataset (100k points), WNNC(SD, G_B, Ω₀) achieves a mean angular RMSE of 21.10° and mean IoU of 0.9724, improving over the original WNNC (22.52° RMSE) and matching iPSR (22.29° RMSE, 0.9709 IoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GCNO [49], sGCNO w_B(q) improves PGP from 99.783% to 99.913%, reduces angular RMSE from 15.93° to 12.77°, cuts chamfer distance from 0.204 to 0.140, and is ~285× faster (32 s vs. 9123 s). Against the original WNNC (Ω₁) [32], replacing only the WNNC step gradient with G_B (SD, A, G_B) reduces angular RMSE from 14.52° to 10.80° at the same 0.4 s runtime, while the full LM variant (LM, A_B, G_B, Ω₁) further improves to 10.16° RMSE. Against iPSR [17] and PGR [31], the proposed WNNC variants dominate on all three metrics (PGP, ∠RMSE, CD) on the GCNO dataset. On Top20SION [28], WNNC(SD, G_B, Ω₀) achieves a mean IoU of 0.9724, surpassing SAP [45] (0.7616) and PG-SDF [28] (0.9153), while sGCNO w_B(q) underperforms (0.9460 IoU), indicating SGD struggles on these complex 100k-point shapes. The key finding is that the bounded formulation w_B consistently reduces angular error by 3–5° over w_U across all WNNC variants, with the largest gains appearing when the WNNC consistency step is included.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the point cloud winding number formulation of Barill et al. [4], identifying that their approximation w_U diverges as the query point approaches a surface point, and derives a bounded alternative w_B via a two-step projection (tangent plane then sphere). It then integrates w_B into two existing winding-number-based normal estimation pipelines: GCNO [49], which regularizes the field via L-BFGS over Voronoi vertices, and WNNC [32] (building on PGR [31]), which solves an underdetermined linear system with steepest descent and gradient-alignment steps. A key conceptual contribution is showing that several design choices in these methods—Voronoi vertex sampling in GCNO and the smoothing width ω in WNNC—were implicitly compensating for the divergence of w_U rather than being fundamentally necessary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bounded formulation provides a theoretically grounded fix that removes the need for ad-hoc heuristics, yielding consistent improvements in angular RMSE (e.g., ~14.5° → ~10.8° for WNNC) and chamfer distance across both tested datasets. The diagnostic insight that Voronoi sampling and smoothing width were workarounds for divergence, rather than intrinsic requirements, reframes how practitioners should think about these methods and may guide future designs. Limitations include the symmetry assumption in the two-step projection (which may not hold for highly anisotropic local geometry), the fact that the LM-based variant does not scale to 100k-point clouds due to Jacobian memory, and that sGCNO underperforms iPSR on the harder Top20SION shapes, suggesting SGD optimization remains a bottleneck for complex geometries.
