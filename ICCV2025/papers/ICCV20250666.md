## Problem

Existing unsupervised camouflaged object detection methods rely on intra-image feature similarity to separate foreground from background, but this fails because camouflaged objects are visually nearly indistinguishable from their surroundings within a single image. Dataset-level contextual information that could resolve this ambiguity remains largely untapped, and prompt-based alternatives still require some form of supervision or task-specific prompts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250666/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unlabeled COD training set (3,040 COD10K + 1,000 CAMO images, resized to 476×476) and produces per-image pseudo binary segmentation masks at the original resolution. Features are extracted from the last layer of DINOv2-ViT-L14, and KNN retrieval uses K=512 prototypes queried via FAISS. During Multi-View KNN Retrieval, each image is additionally processed in five augmented views (horizontal/vertical flips and 90°/180°/270° rotations), with inverse-transformed masks combined by voting before upsampling to full resolution.

## Method

RISE operates in two stages. First, Clustering-then-Retrieval (CR) builds foreground and background prototype libraries from the unlabeled dataset: spectral clustering yields a coarse mask per image, mask-averaged pooling produces global category features, and cross-category retrieval selects the local feature least similar to the *opposite* category's global feature as the prototype (maximizing inter-class distinction). A histogram-based adaptive threshold then filters out images whose foreground/background global features are too similar, removing noisy prototypes. Second, Multi-View KNN Retrieval (MVKR) assigns each pixel feature to foreground or background by voting over its top-K nearest prototypes, and aggregates results across five geometrically transformed views to suppress artifacts in the DINOv2 feature maps without any fine-tuning. The resulting pseudo-masks are used to train a standard COD model (SINet-V2), making RISE a training-free pseudo-label generator that leverages global dataset semantics rather than single-image similarity.

## Results

RISE with DINOv2-ViT-L14 achieves Sα=0.822, Eφ=0.884, Fβω=0.720 on CHAMELEON and Sα=0.763, Eφ=0.840, Fβω=0.600 on COD10K, outperforming the best unsupervised baseline (ProMerge) by at least 8% in Eφ and 9% in Fβω on COD10K. When integrated with SAM, RISE reaches Sα=0.790, Eφ=0.854, Fβω=0.643 on COD10K and Sα=0.825, Eφ=0.874, Fβω=0.736 on NC4K, surpassing the strongest prompt-based method (WS-SAM) on most metrics. The method also demonstrates strong efficiency, generating pseudo-masks for the full 4,040-image training set in hours versus days for prompt-based alternatives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest unsupervised baseline ProMerge (DINOv2-ViT-L14), RISE achieves substantial gains on COD10K: Sα 0.763 vs 0.674 (+8.9%), Eφ 0.840 vs 0.714 (+12.6%), and Fβω 0.600 vs 0.435 (+16.5%), with similar margins on NC4K (Sα +7.9%, Fβω +14.7%). All other unsupervised baselines (TokenCut, MaskCut, VoteCut, DeepSpectral, LOST, FOUND, DiffCut) are outperformed by even larger margins. In the prompt-based setting, RISE with SAM surpasses WS-SAM on COD10K (Eφ 0.854 vs 0.838, Fβω 0.643 vs 0.622) and NC4K (Eφ 0.874 vs 0.867, Fβω 0.736 vs 0.727), though WS-SAM retains a slight edge on NC4K Sα (0.829 vs 0.825). RISE also clearly outperforms GenSAM and ProMac across all four datasets, with the largest gaps on COD10K where GenSAM lags by over 15% in Fβω. The paper notes that RISE completes pseudo-mask generation for the full training set in hours with far less GPU memory, compared to days for LLM/diffusion-based prompt methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

RISE carves new ground at the intersection of unsupervised segmentation (which relies on intra-image feature similarity via spectral clustering or normalized cut) and retrieval-augmented semantic segmentation (which mines prototypes from external data sources). Rather than importing prototypes from diffusion models or external datasets, RISE repurposes the COD training set itself as the prototype source, using a Clustering-then-Retrieval mechanism to extract foreground and background prototypes in an annotation-free manner. The Multi-View KNN Retrieval component draws on the observation that DINOv2 feature-map artifacts shift with viewpoint, offering a fine-tuning-free alternative to register tokens. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant intra-image paradigm by arguing that dataset-level cross-image similarity is the key signal for separating highly similar foreground and background in camouflage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that a camouflaged object indistinguishable within a single image becomes separable when contextualized against a dataset-level prototype library—offers a generalizable principle for any task where foreground-background similarity is extreme. The plug-and-play results (Table 4) showing ~10% gains when RISE is grafted onto existing unsupervised methods suggest broad applicability beyond COD. Practical efficiency is a significant advantage: pseudo-mask generation completes in hours versus days for LLM/diffusion-based prompt methods, with far lower GPU memory. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other high-similarity segmentation tasks (e.g., salient object detection in cluttered scenes), whether the spectral clustering bottleneck can be replaced by learned segmentation, and how performance scales when the training and test distributions diverge substantially.
