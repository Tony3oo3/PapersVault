## Problem

Existing generative models for art operate at the level of individual images or discrete style categories, leaving the broader question of how stylistic distributions evolve over centuries unaddressed. No ground-truth correspondences exist between artworks from different eras, and prior art datasets lack the scale, unified metadata, and historical depth needed to model long-range temporal progression.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251064/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a style embedding (CLIP or CSD) of a source artwork together with its creation year y_a and a target year y_b = y_a + Δy, all in a compact 1D latent space rather than pixel space. It produces the style embedding projected onto the target era's distribution, which is then decoded to a visible image via unCLIP for visualization. The year conditioning is continuous (not discrete style labels), enabling arbitrary temporal offsets from ±20 to ±100 years or more.

## Method

A single 1D transformer velocity field v_θ(x_t, t, y) is trained with a linear stochastic interpolant (α_t = 1−t, σ_t = t) and a standard flow-matching regression objective, conditioned on the artwork's creation year y. At inference, the Dual Diffusion Implicit Bridge mechanism pushes the source embedding forward to structured noise using the source-year condition, then backward to data using the target-year condition, implicitly defining an optimal transport plan between era-conditioned distributions without any paired supervision. The key insight is that the ODE trajectories of independently trained conditional models share a structured noise space, so the composition of forward and backward ODE solves with different year conditions yields meaningful cross-era correspondences. A threshold parameter t_th controls how far into noise space the forward pass goes, trading off content preservation against stylistic flexibility. The approach is supported by a newly curated dataset of 650,000+ artworks (1500–2021) with normalized metadata including year, artist, style, medium, and location.

## Results

On the temporal style-matching task (Table 1), the proposed method achieves R@1 of 72.5/65.0/58.5 and R@5 of 85.1/76.3/69.3 across Rococo, Impressionism, and Realism, with compactness δ ≈ 0.024–0.025, far outperforming the best pixel-space baseline SDEdit (fine-tuned) which reaches only R@1 = 37.9/34.1/27.5 and δ = 0.236–0.272. On century-conditioned synthesis (Table 2), the method achieves FID scores of 72.0–90.7 across the 16th–20th centuries, substantially lower than fine-tuned SD 1.5 (88.8–150.7) and SDXL (114.8–186.6). The method also attains the highest triplet consistency τ (68.6–84.8%), indicating strong preservation of local stylistic structure after temporal transport.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all four pixel-space editing baselines on the core temporal-matching task. SDEdit (fine-tuned on the Art-fm dataset) is the closest competitor, reaching R@1 ≈ 27–38 and δ ≈ 0.24–0.27, but still trails the proposed method by roughly 2× in recall and 10× in compactness. LEDITS++ and RF-inversion achieve even lower recall (R@1 ≤ 15) and much higher δ (0.51–0.75), though RF-inversion retains competitive triplet consistency (τ ≈ 66–84%) due to its high pixel-level fidelity. On century-conditioned synthesis (Table 2), the method achieves FID 72.0–90.7, beating fine-tuned SD 1.5 (88.8–150.7) by 10–70 points and SDXL (114.8–186.6) by 30–100 points; FLUX.1-schnell (122.8–199.2) and SD 2.1 (134.2–214.4) perform comparably to SDXL, all substantially worse than the proposed approach. The consistent gap across all three evaluated styles (Rococo, Impressionism, Realism) and all five centuries confirms that operating in a compact style latent space with flow-matching transport is fundamentally more effective than pixel-level editing for capturing historically grounded stylistic evolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of flow matching / stochastic interpolants (Albergo et al. 2023; Ma et al. 2024) and dual diffusion implicit bridges (Zhou et al. 2024), repurposing these generative modeling tools for a cultural-history task rather than standard image synthesis. It departs from the dominant style-transfer literature (Gatys et al., AdaIN, CSD) by reframing style as a probability distribution over a learned latent space and modeling its temporal evolution via optimal transport, rather than performing discrete image-to-image style mapping. The linear interpolant schedule from rectified flow (Liu et al. 2023) and the SiT training objective provide the concrete architectural backbone, while the DDIB insight—that two independently trained ODEs implicitly define an OT plan through structured noise—enables unpaired temporal transport without any ground-truth correspondences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 650K-artwork dataset spanning 1500–2021 with unified chronological metadata fills a clear gap left by WikiArt, BAM, and LAION-Styles, and will likely become a standard resource for computational art-history research. The finding that pixel-space editing methods (SDEdit, LEDITS++, RF-inversion) fundamentally fail at temporally faithful style transport—while a compact latent-space flow model succeeds—suggests that semantic-level representation is essential for modeling cultural evolution, a principle that may generalize to other domains with long-range structural change. Open questions include whether the distributional (rather than sample-level) evaluation can be tightened, how the method handles non-linear or discontinuous stylistic ruptures (e.g., the jump from Impressionism to Cubism), and whether the approach extends to other cultural artifacts beyond paintings.
