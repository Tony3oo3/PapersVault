## Problem

Existing automated graphic design methods fall into two inadequate categories: two-stage layout generation requires all visual assets to be pre-designed by users, limiting creativity and increasing labor, while diffusion-based text-to-image methods produce flat, non-editable images with poor legibility in rendered text. No prior approach can generate fully editable, multimodal graphic design files (text, image, and graphic layers) directly from natural language instructions in a single stage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252252/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

In single-modal mode, IGD accepts only a natural language instruction and outputs a complete design file containing text layers, image layers, and graphic layers in a Penpot-compatible standardized token sequence. In multimodal mode, the user additionally provides image content, and the model outputs only text-based elements. The output is structured as an ordered sequence of special tokens encoding each layer's type, position, size, stacking order, and content attributes; simple images (solid colors, abstract patterns) are represented as SVG vector paths rather than raster pixels, and a bucket strategy handles the wide range of design resolutions and aspect ratios.

## Method

IGD integrates Qwen2.5 (as the MLLM) with Stable Diffusion in an end-to-end architecture where the LLM autoregressively predicts layer attributes and sequencing, and upon encountering an <image_gen> token, pauses to feed learnable condition-query embeddings through a linear projection into SD for image synthesis, then appends the generated image to continue prediction. Simple graphic elements are converted to SVG via k-means color clustering and Potrace path tracing, reducing them to compact text-token representations that avoid inflating sequence length. Training proceeds in two stages: first, augmented pre-training with random and semantically coherent text substitutions injects design-reasoning and layout skills into the LLM; second, joint end-to-end fine-tuning of the LLM, SD, and both projection layers (L_gen for image generation, L_com for visual comprehension via CLIP ViT-L/14) optimizes cross-layer spatial and stylistic coherence. This parameter-prediction-and-rendering paradigm, rather than pixel-level diffusion for the entire design, ensures crisp text legibility and attribute-level editability while retaining the creative image generation capability of the diffusion model.

## Results

On GPT-4o evaluation (English), IGD achieves 81.2 Quality, 88.8 Harmony, 81.4 Accuracy, 72.0 Layout, and 68.2 Innovation, and is the only method that simultaneously supports instruction following, editability, and creativity. In OCR-based text rendering (English), IGD attains 94.35% character precision and 86.83% F-measure, substantially outperforming SD3.5 (73.83% F) and DALL-E3 (42.81% F). On graphic layout metrics, IGD achieves the lowest alignment error (R_ali = 0.0541) and FID of 56.35, nearly halving SD3.5's FID of 107.42.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IGD outperforms all five baselines on the Accuracy metric (81.4 vs. 38.6 for SD3.5, 52.8 for DALL-E3, 30.2 for AnyText, 54.0 for CanvaGPT, 65.6 for OpenCOLE), reflecting its superior instruction adherence. Against DALL-E3, IGD trades some Harmony (88.8 vs. 96.2) and Layout (72.0 vs. 82.4) for a decisive Accuracy advantage and, critically, full layer-level editability that DALL-E3 lacks. Compared to CanvaGPT, IGD wins on every GPT-evaluated dimension while also following instructions (CanvaGPT does not), though CanvaGPT's template-based approach gives it a slight edge in perceived typographic consistency. Versus OpenCOLE, IGD leads on all five GPT metrics and on OCR F-measure (86.83% vs. 58.76%), alignment error (0.0541 vs. 0.1941), and FID (56.35 vs. 122.20), while both support editability and instruction following. AnyText and SD3.5 are clearly dominated by IGD across all metrics, with IGD's parametric rendering paradigm eliminating the text-rendering failures (repetition, omission, distortion) that plague pure diffusion approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

IGD departs from both two-stage layout generation methods (CGL-GAN, LayoutGPT, Graphist) that require pre-designed elements, and single-stage diffusion approaches (SD3.5, DALL-E3, AnyText) that produce non-editable flat images with poor text rendering. It also extends COLE/OpenCOLE, which supports only a fixed two-layer structure (background image + text), by introducing a unified parametric-rendering paradigm where an MLLM predicts layer attributes and a diffusion model generates image assets, trained end-to-end following the DreamLLM recipe. The standardized Penpot-based file format is a new data infrastructure contribution that unifies multi-scenario design files into a single LLM-friendly token sequence, enabling cross-domain generalization that prior layout or diffusion methods did not support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IGD demonstrates that instruction-driven, editable, multi-scenario graphic design generation is feasible in a single model, addressing a practical gap where designers currently rely on manual element curation or accept non-editable diffusion outputs. The Penpot-based standardized format and the MLLM+diffusion end-to-end training recipe provide reusable infrastructure for the creative-AI community. Open questions include scalability to multi-page or highly complex documents, the quality ceiling of the diffusion component for photorealistic assets within designs, and whether the bucket strategy generalizes to arbitrary aspect ratios beyond the pre-defined set. The paper's evaluation on 1k samples per language, while reasonable, leaves room for broader stress-testing of instruction adherence and layout quality at scale.
