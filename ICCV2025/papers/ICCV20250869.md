## Problem

Existing 3D point trackers are either monocular (suffering from depth ambiguity and occlusion) or multi-camera but require 20+ views and per-sequence optimization, making them impractical for real-world deployment. No feed-forward, data-driven model existed that could track arbitrary 3D points across a practical number of cameras (e.g., four) with known poses, handling occlusions and varying camera setups without test-time optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250869/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized RGB frames from V cameras (1–8 views) over T frames (24–150), along with known intrinsics/extrinsics and per-pixel depth maps (from sensors like Kinect or estimators like DUSt3R/VGGT), plus N query points specified as (t_q, x, y, z) in world coordinates. It outputs per-point 3D trajectories (x_t, y_t, z_t) for all t ≥ t_q and binary visibility labels indicating whether the point is occluded in all views. The model runs online at 7.2 FPS with RGB-D input, processing sequences in overlapping sliding windows of 12 frames with 6-frame overlap.

## Method

MVTracker fuses per-view CNN feature maps into a unified 3D feature point cloud by unprojecting pixels with depth, then performs multi-scale kNN-based correlation directly in 3D space (retrieving K nearest neighbors at 4 scales around each tracked point's current estimate, concatenating dot-product similarity with explicit 3D offset vectors). A spatiotemporal transformer with temporal self-attention and cross-attention over learned virtual tracks iteratively refines position and appearance features over M steps, recomputing kNN correlations after each update. This avoids the projection collisions of triplane representations and the irrelevant background matching of 2D-grid correlation. Training is fully supervised on 5K synthetic Kubric multi-view sequences (200K steps, 8 GPUs, AdamW, d=128, 6-head attention, hidden size 256) with a weighted L1 position loss and balanced BCE visibility loss, using unrolled windowed updates so each iteration learns to correct prior windows.

## Results

On Panoptic Studio, MVTracker achieves an AJ of 86.0, δ_avg of 94.7, OA of 92.3, and a median trajectory error (MTE) of 3.1 cm. On DexYCB (with DUSt3R-estimated depth), it reaches AJ 71.6, δ_avg 80.6, OA 91.3, and MTE 2.0 cm. On the synthetic Multi-View Kubric validation set, it attains AJ 81.4, δ_avg 90.0, OA 93.7, and an exceptionally low MTE of 0.7 cm. The method runs at 7.2 FPS with RGB-D input and is fully feed-forward, requiring no per-sequence optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVTracker outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest single-view 3D tracker TAPIP3D, it gains +1.7 AJ on Panoptic Studio (86.0 vs 84.3) and +9.2 AJ on DexYCB (71.6 vs 38.8), while matching its MTE of 3.1 cm on Panoptic Studio. Against the closest multi-view baseline (Triplane Baseline, which uses the same MV-Kub training data but a triplane representation instead of kNN point-cloud correlation), MVTracker improves by +14.1 AJ on DexYCB (71.6 vs 57.5) and +6.7 AJ on Multi-View Kubric (81.4 vs 74.7), confirming that the kNN-based 3D correlation is the key architectural advantage. Optimization-based multi-view methods (Dynamic 3DGS, Shape of Motion) are substantially worse—Dynamic 3DGS scores only 45.7 AJ on DexYCB versus MVTracker's 71.6—and additionally require 20+ cameras and per-sequence optimization. Single-view 2D trackers (CoTracker3, LocoTrack, CoTracker2) suffer the largest degradation when their 2D trajectories are lifted to 3D via estimated depth, with MTEs of 8.6–22.0 cm on DexYCB compared to MVTracker's 2.0 cm. On the synthetic MV-Kub validation set, MVTracker achieves the lowest MTE (0.7 cm) and highest OA (93.7%), indicating strong generalization from synthetic training to held-out synthetic scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

MVTracker extends the CoTracker family of transformer-based point trackers (sliding-window inference, temporal self-attention with virtual tracks) from 2D to 3D and from monocular to multi-view input. Its key architectural departure is from SpatialTracker's triplane representation: instead of projecting features onto fixed 2D planes (which causes destructive collisions and requires a pre-defined scene bounding box), MVTracker fuses multi-view features into a unified 3D point cloud and performs kNN-based correlation directly in world space. This positions the work between the optimization-based multi-view methods (Dynamic 3DGS, Shape of Motion, which need 20+ cameras and per-sequence fitting) and the monocular feed-forward 3D trackers (SpatialTracker, DELTA, TAPIP3D), offering a practical middle ground with only 4 cameras and no test-time optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a feed-forward model with just four cameras can achieve 3.1 cm median trajectory error on Panoptic Studio and 2.0 cm on DexYCB, MVTracker makes multi-view 3D point tracking practical for robotics, AR, and 4D reconstruction pipelines that previously required 27-camera rigs or slow per-sequence optimization. The main open limitation is the dependence on depth quality: without reliable sensor or estimated depth, tracking degrades, pointing toward future work on joint depth-tracking estimation or integrated 4D foundation models. The paper also highlights the scarcity of large-scale real-world multi-view tracking data as a field-wide bottleneck, with self-supervised approaches (BootsTAP, CoTracker3) as a promising but unexplored direction for this setting.
