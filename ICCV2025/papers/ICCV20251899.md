## Problem

Current medical vision-language models assign high L2 norms to background tokens and produce global image tokens that are poorly aligned with small, fine-grained disease regions, causing weakly-supervised visual grounding methods to mislocalize pathology. This paper identifies these two failure modes empirically and proposes a remedy, while also establishing the first comprehensive weakly-supervised medical VG benchmark across three chest X-ray datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251899/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 chest X-ray image and a text disease description (capped at 77 tokens, sampled from a GPT-4o-generated and radiologist-verified description set) as input, and produces a pixel-level segmentation map localizing the referenced disease region. During training, an additional explainability map extracted from the frozen VLM serves as a disease-aware prompt; at inference, only the original image and text query are required, making the approach annotation-free at test time.

## Method

Disease-Aware Prompting (DAP) extracts a 2D explainability map from the pretrained VLM's cross-attention for the given text query, then multiplies this map onto intermediate ViT token features to amplify disease-relevant patches and suppress high-norm background tokens in the feature space. Training uses a hierarchical objective: a global contrastive loss aligns the prompted [IMG] token with the text [CLS] token (serving as a proxy for disease patches), a local contrastive loss directly pulls foreground tokens toward [CLS] while pushing background tokens away (with FG/BG selected by thresholding the prompt map), and a Dice loss trains the pixel decoder using the prompt as a pseudo-label. The key distinction from pixel-space masking (e.g., ZSeg) is that feature-space prompting preserves global context while consistently reducing background norms across all ViT layers, and the prompt is discarded at inference so the model grounds on the original image.

## Results

On MS-CXR, DAP achieves a CNR of 1.254, PG of 0.457, Dice of 0.352, and IoU of 0.245, outperforming all baselines. On RSNA Pneumonia, DAP reaches CNR 1.630, PG 0.747, Dice 0.474, and IoU 0.328. On the COVID Rural dataset, DAP attains CNR 1.018, PG 0.625, Dice 0.391, and IoU 0.267. The headline improvement is a 20.74% CNR gain over g++ on RSNA and a 15.89% CNR gain over Self-EQ on MS-CXR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAP outperforms all 13 baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against the strongest VLM baseline BioViL [6], DAP improves CNR by 7.73% on MS-CXR (1.254 vs 0.941) and by 48.72% on RSNA (1.630 vs 1.096). Against the most competitive weakly-supervised VG method Self-EQ [23], DAP gains 15.89% CNR and 9.36% PG on MS-CXR, and 51.4% CNR on RSNA (1.630 vs 1.075). The largest single-metric gain is 20.74% CNR over g++ [47] on RSNA (1.630 vs 1.350). On the COVID Rural dataset, DAP surpasses VPT [36] by 17.7% PG (0.625 vs 0.531) and Self-EQ [23] by 33.26% PG (0.625 vs 0.469). Notably, g [48] and g++ [47] achieve competitive Dice/IoU on RSNA but are substantially outperformed on CNR and PG, indicating their grounding maps are less disease-focused despite reasonable overlap with ground truth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the weakly-supervised visual grounding literature (Self-EQ, g, g++, VPT) to the medical domain by diagnosing two specific failure modes in medical VLMs—high-norm background tokens and non-representative global [IMG] tokens—that prior general-domain methods do not address. The core mechanism repurposes the Trans-MM explainability approach [8] not as a pseudo-label generator (as in prior WSVG methods) but as a feature-space prompt that reweights ViT token activations before the global token is computed. This departs from pixel-space masking (ZSeg) and learnable prompt tuning (CoOp, CoCoOp, Visual Prompt Tuning) by using an informative, disease-specific signal rather than randomly initialized or hard-masked prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnostic findings—that background regions carry higher L2 norms than disease regions and that the global image token aligns more with background than foreground—provide a concrete explanation for why medical VLMs underperform on fine-grained grounding, which should inform future medical VLM pre-training. The paper also establishes the first comprehensive weakly-supervised medical VG benchmark across three chest X-ray datasets, filling a gap in evaluation infrastructure. Limitations include restriction to chest X-rays, dependence on the quality of the explainability map (which is acknowledged as imperfect), and evaluation on relatively small test sets (1,162 images for MS-CXR). Whether the feature-space prompting strategy generalizes to other modalities (CT, MRI) or to multi-disease grounding with overlapping regions remains open.
