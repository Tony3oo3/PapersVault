## Problem

Existing forgery image detectors face a fundamental trade-off: specialized deep networks achieve high accuracy on seen generative models but fail to generalize to unseen ones, while frozen pre-trained encoders (e.g., CLIP-ViT) generalize well but produce general-purpose features laden with forgery-irrelevant information, limiting detection accuracy. Moreover, most methods require large training datasets, making rapid adaptation to newly emerging forgery techniques impractical.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single RGB image, randomly cropped to 224×224 during training (with horizontal flipping) and center-cropped during testing, and outputs a binary real/fake class label via a linear classifier applied to the final Focus CLS token. No text prompts or auxiliary modalities are required, distinguishing it from methods like FatFormer and C2P-CLIP that leverage both image and text encoders.

## Method

ForgeLens introduces two lightweight trainable modules around a frozen CLIP-ViT backbone. The Weight-Shared Guidance Module (WSGM) is a bottleneck structure (expansion → ReLU → middle linear → ReLU → compression) inserted between the MHSA and MLP sub-layers of each ViT block; a small number of WSGMs are weight-shared across all blocks, guiding the frozen encoder to emphasize forgery-specific features with minimal parameters. The FAFormer concatenates the CLS tokens from every ViT stage with a newly introduced Focus CLS token and processes them through standard Transformer blocks, integrating low-level texture artifacts with high-level semantic cues into a unified forgery-aware representation. Training follows a two-stage schedule: first the WSGM is trained with the CLIP-ViT frozen, then FAFormer is trained with all prior components frozen, ensuring stability while keeping total trainable parameters very small.

## Results

On UniversalFakeDetect (19 generative model subsets spanning GANs and diffusion models), ForgeLens achieves 94.99% average accuracy and 98.83% average AP, outperforming all baselines. Compared to the frozen-CLIP-ViT base model UniFD, ForgeLens improves average accuracy by 13.61% and average AP by 8.69%. Notably, even with only 1% of the training data, ForgeLens maintains performance comparable to the full-data setting, demonstrating strong data efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ForgeLens outperforms all 12 baselines on average accuracy across UniversalFakeDetect. Against the frozen-CLIP-ViT base model UniFD [39], ForgeLens gains +13.61% Avg.Acc (94.99% vs. 81.38%) and +8.69% Avg.AP (98.83% vs. 90.14%), confirming that the WSGM+FAFormer guidance mechanism effectively suppresses forgery-irrelevant information. Among specialized methods, FreqNet [49] is the strongest (85.09% Avg.Acc) but degrades sharply on diffusion model subsets; ForgeLens surpasses it by +9.9% Avg.Acc. Among preprocessing-based methods, NPR [50] leads at 87.56% Avg.Acc, still 7.43% below ForgeLens. Among frozen-model methods, ForgeLens beats the prior SOTA C2P-CLIP [48] by +1.2% Avg.Acc (94.99% vs. 93.79%) and RINE [29] by +3.68%, while using a simpler image-encoder-only architecture (no text encoder). In the WSGM vs. fine-tuning comparison (Table 4), WSGM achieves 94.52% Avg.Acc, outperforming LoRA (86.96%) and Adapter (85.49%) by 7.56% and 9.03% respectively, validating the guidance mechanism over parameter-adjustment approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

ForgeLens extends the frozen pretrained model paradigm for forgery detection (UniFD, FatFormer, RINE, C2P-CLIP), which uses a frozen CLIP-ViT backbone with a lightweight classifier to avoid overfitting to model-specific artifacts. The key departure is the introduction of WSGM, a bottleneck guidance module inserted between MHSA and MLP in each ViT block, which actively steers the frozen encoder toward forgery-specific features rather than passively consuming its general-purpose output. This is conceptually distinct from standard parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA, Adapter), which the paper explicitly compares against and shows to be inferior, positioning WSGM as a 'guidance' mechanism rather than an 'adaptation' one. FAFormer further differentiates the method by integrating multi-stage CLS tokens through a small Transformer, drawing on the observation that shallow features carry important low-level forgery cues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in data efficiency: achieving near-optimal performance with just 1% of training data means a detector can be rapidly deployed against newly emerging generative models without collecting large labeled datasets. This addresses a real deployment bottleneck in the arms race between forgery generation and detection. However, the two-stage training strategy and FAFormer's sensitivity to hyperparameters add operational complexity, and the significant accuracy drops under Gaussian blur and noise (12–13%) reveal vulnerability to common real-world image degradation. Open questions include whether the WSGM guidance mechanism transfers to other frozen backbones (e.g., DINOv2, SAM), whether it extends to video or 3D forgery detection, and whether the data-efficiency advantage persists as generative models produce increasingly artifact-free outputs.
