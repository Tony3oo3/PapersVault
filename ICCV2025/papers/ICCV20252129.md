## Problem

Sparsely annotated remote sensing object detection suffers from a fundamental misalignment in Dense Pseudo-Label (DPL) methods: underrepresented categories exhibit artificially high confidence scores while well-represented categories show low confidence, causing the top-k selection mechanism to systematically over-select rare classes and under-train common ones. This confidence-semantic inconsistency, compounded by dense object distributions and category imbalance inherent to aerial imagery, undermines pseudo-label reliability and hinders effective learning under minimal annotation budgets.

## Input / Output

The method consumes remote sensing aerial images tiled into 1024×1024 patches with sparse annotations (1%–10% of instances labeled per category), alongside unlabeled images. For the LLM-based semantic prediction, high-resolution imagery is processed through CLIP-ViT (L-14) at an expanded 504×504 resolution (1296 patches), with an MLP adaptor projecting 1024-dim visual embeddings into a 4096-dim space compatible with Vicuna-v1.5 (7B). The output consists of oriented bounding boxes (center, width, height, rotation angle) with class labels, evaluated via mAP on the DOTA (15-class) and HRSC2016 (ship) benchmarks.

## Method

The framework employs a Multi-Branch Input (MBI) teacher-student architecture built on Rotated FCOS with a ResNet-50 backbone. In the unsupervised branch, an LLM-Assisted Semantic Prediction (LSP) module uses CLIP-ViT + Vicuna-v1.5 to offline-generate per-image class prompts identifying which foreground categories are present, which then drive a Class-Aware Label Assignment (CLA) mechanism that selects pixel-level pseudo-labels through a three-stage process: (1) filtering pixels whose predicted class matches the LLM prompt and exceeds a confidence threshold, (2) selecting top-k highest joint-confidence pixels, and (3) selecting top-k_j pixels per category for balanced representation, with ground-truth pixels additionally included for sparsely labeled data. In the supervised branch, an Adaptive Hard-Negative Reweighting (AHR) module modifies the focal loss by applying an additional down-weighting factor w to negative samples whose predicted confidence exceeds a threshold, thereby reducing the adversarial influence of unannotated objects misclassified as background. The cyclic optimization alternates between refining the teacher's dense pseudo-labels and adapting the student detector, with the LLM prompts progressively refined by incorporating annotation information from labeled data.

## Results

On the DOTA benchmark (OBB detection), the proposed method achieves mAP of 56.64%, 59.37%, 63.95%, and 65.11% at 1%, 2%, 5%, and 10% annotation rates respectively, outperforming all supervised, semi-supervised, and sparsely-annotated baselines at every rate. The largest gains are over Dense Teacher (ECCV 2022), with improvements of +11.65% (2%), +13.13% (5%), and +6.98% (10%). On HRSC2016, the method reaches 66.80% mAP, surpassing Dense Teacher (64.86%) by 1.94% and ReDet (63.80%) by 3.00%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across every annotation rate on DOTA. Against the strongest sparsely-annotated baseline S²A-Net w/ PECL (NeurIPS 2024), it gains +6.25% mAP at 1% and +2.62% at 10%. Among semi-supervised methods, the largest margins are over ARSL (CVPR 2023) at +12.76% (1%) and MCL at +14.74% (1%), while over Dense Teacher (ECCV 2022) the gap is +13.13% at 5%. Compared to the best fully supervised detector (ReDet, CVPR 2021), the method still leads by +5.92% at 1% and +3.32% at 10%, demonstrating that the LLM-guided pseudo-labeling strategy effectively compensates for missing annotations. On HRSC2016, the method surpasses Dense Teacher by 1.94% mAP and ReDet by 3.00% mAP, though it trails ReDet by 0.1% on AP75, indicating a slight trade-off in localization precision at stricter IoU thresholds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dense pseudo-label (DPL) paradigm from Dense Teacher and ARSL to the sparsely annotated oriented object detection (SAOD) setting in remote sensing, where only a fraction of objects per image are labeled. Its key departure is the introduction of an offline LLM-based semantic prediction module (CLIP-ViT + Vicuna) that generates class-specific prompts to resolve the misalignment between per-pixel confidence scores and true category distributions—a problem that prior DPL methods (top-k selection, divide-and-rule) address only heuristically. The supervised branch's Adaptive Hard-Negative Reweighting (AHR) module builds on focal-loss-style reweighting but applies it specifically to the logit-space negatives that arise from missing annotations in SAOD, a setting where one-stage detectors are particularly vulnerable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that even a modest offline LLM call (one per image, ~0.5 s) can meaningfully improve pseudo-label quality in a domain where annotation is prohibitively expensive, offering a practical recipe for label-efficient remote sensing detection. However, the ablation reveals that the AHR module contributes the bulk of the gain (~10.8 mAP at 5% labels) while the LLM-guided assignment adds only ~2 mAP, raising the question of whether simpler class-frequency priors could substitute for the LLM. The method is validated on only two datasets (DOTA, HRSC2016) with a single backbone (ResNet-50 + Rotated FCOS), leaving open whether the LLM component generalizes to larger category spaces, multi-sensor imagery, or different detector architectures. The 'gt-prompt' upper bound in Table 7 also suggests substantial headroom if the semantic prior could be made more reliable.
