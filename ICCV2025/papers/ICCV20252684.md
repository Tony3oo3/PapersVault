## Problem

3D-based avatar methods (NeRF/3DGS + parametric models) preserve identity well but cannot model pose-driven non-rigid deformations like cloth motion without expensive per-subject pose-rich video capture. Diffusion-based animation methods learn pose-driven deformations from large-scale data but fail to maintain consistent identity across poses. No prior framework combines both strengths to produce a personalized, animatable 3D avatar with natural deformations from just a single image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252684/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image of a person as the sole user-provided input. From it, SMPL-X identity parameters (shape, etc.) are extracted and combined with pre-computed target 3D poses to drive MimicMotion, generating pose-rich training videos that supplement the input image. The output is an animatable 3D Gaussian avatar (SMPL-X template mesh with per-vertex isotropic Gaussians, constant opacity, triplane features) equipped with MLP-predicted mean offsets for pose-driven deformation, drivable by arbitrary SMPL-X body poses and facial expression parameters and renderable from any viewpoint via Mip-Splatting.

## Method

PERSONA optimizes a hybrid SMPL-X + 3DGS avatar on a training set consisting of the input image plus diffusion-generated pose-rich videos, using two key mechanisms. Balanced sampling oversamples the input image during optimization to anchor identity, detects seam boundaries via Sobel filtering on rendered positional maps to prevent color leakage between body parts, and uses albedo supervision to avoid baking shadows into textures. Geometry-weighted optimization assigns low image-loss weights and high geometry-loss weights (binary masks, depth, normals, part segmentations from SAM/Sapiens) when training the deformation MLPs, so the model learns non-rigid motion from stable geometric signals rather than artifact-prone generated textures. Pose-driven deformations are modeled exclusively through mean offsets on isotropic Gaussians (no scale or RGB offsets), preserving texture sharpness by shifting positions without altering Gaussian shape or color.

## Results

On the NeuMan test set (single-image setting), PERSONA achieves 29.20 PSNR, 0.974 SSIM, and 0.021 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest 3D-based baseline AniGS (28.27 PSNR) by 0.93 dB and the best diffusion-based baseline Champ (27.27 PSNR) by 1.93 dB. On X-Humans (single-image, averaged over 3 subjects), PERSONA reaches 27.38 mean PSNR, 0.968 mean SSIM, and 0.039 mean LPIPS, surpassing AniGS (25.41 PSNR) and StableAnimator (24.41 PSNR). The ablation without pose-driven deformations drops to 28.02 PSNR on NeuMan, confirming the contribution of the deformation module.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PERSONA outperforms all 15 baselines in the single-image setting on both NeuMan and X-Humans. Against 3D-based single-image methods, it beats AniGS by 0.93 dB PSNR (NeuMan) and LHM by 2.98 dB, while also achieving lower LPIPS than AniGS (0.021 vs. 0.027), indicating better perceptual quality. Against diffusion-based methods, the margins are larger: PERSONA surpasses Champ by 1.93 dB PSNR, StableAnimator by 2.62 dB, and MimicMotion by 3.08 dB on NeuMan, with consistent SSIM gains of 0.004–0.006. Compared to 3D reconstruction methods (TeCH, SiTH), PERSONA leads by 5–6 dB PSNR, reflecting the advantage of an animatable Gaussian representation over static mesh reconstruction. Against video-based 3D methods (ExAvatar, GaussianAvatar, Vid2Avatar), PERSONA is competitive or superior in the single-image regime despite using far less input data, e.g., matching ExAvatar's video-based PSNR of 34.80 is not expected, but PERSONA's 29.20 PSNR from a single image exceeds ExAvatar's single-image result of 24.95 by 4.25 dB. The ablation 'Ours w/o pose-driven deform.' (28.02 PSNR) sits between AniGS and PERSONA, confirming that the pose-driven deformation module contributes ~1.2 dB beyond a standard 3DGS avatar.

## Relation to Prior Work

PERSONA bridges two established but complementary paradigms for 3D human avatar creation: 3D-based methods (NeRF/3DGS + SMPL-X) that preserve identity but lack pose-driven non-rigid deformations, and diffusion-based methods that capture natural clothing motion but fail at identity preservation. It builds directly on ExAvatar's hybrid mesh-Gaussian representation and fitting pipeline, adopts MimicMotion as the diffusion video generator, and follows SCANimate's 4-ring joint locality strategy for the deformation MLPs. The key conceptual departure is repurposing a diffusion model not as the final rendering engine but as a per-subject data augmentation tool that synthesizes pose-rich training videos, which then supervise a standard 3DGS optimization—something neither prior family has attempted in this specific configuration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single photo now suffices to produce an animatable avatar with natural, pose-dependent clothing deformations, removing the need for costly multi-pose video capture per subject. However, the pipeline remains per-subject (requiring optimization) and inherits quality limitations from the underlying diffusion model (MimicMotion), so identity fidelity in extreme poses or with unusual clothing may still degrade. Open questions include whether the geometry-weighted optimization strategy transfers to other 3D representations (e.g., NeRF), whether stronger or multi-diffusion ensembles could reduce the need for balanced sampling, and whether the approach extends to multi-person or scene-interactive settings.
