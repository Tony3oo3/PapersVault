## Problem

Text-guided zero-shot counting methods provide limited visual detail for novel categories, leading to poor counting accuracy, while exemplar-guided few-shot methods require costly manual annotation of bounding boxes that scales poorly with image density and object scale variation. CountSE bridges this gap by automatically generating soft exemplars from image features guided solely by text, achieving near few-shot performance at zero-shot annotation cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251057/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized so the shortest side is 800 px) and a short text description (e.g., 'tomato', 'car') as input, requiring no manual exemplar annotations. It outputs a single object count per image. Internally, the image is encoded by a frozen Swin Transformer producing four multi-scale feature maps (H/8, H/16, H/32, H/64) and the text by a frozen BERT encoder; the total number of soft exemplars N is fixed at 18 and distributed adaptively across the four scales.

## Method

CountSE builds on the GroundingDINO framework and introduces two parameter-free modules that operate between the encoders and the detection decoder. First, Semantic-guided Exemplar Selection (SES) computes a similarity map between text features and each stage's image features via matrix multiplication, extracts the top-M candidate features per stage, and adaptively allocates the total exemplar budget N across stages proportional to each stage's maximum similarity score. Second, Clustering-based Exemplar Filtering (CEF) applies spectral clustering to the candidate exemplars at each stage and retains only the top-Ni features from the largest cluster, discarding semantically similar but visually inconsistent outliers. The filtered exemplars are averaged per stage and concatenated with the original text features to form an enriched text representation T' that is fed into the GroundingDINO enhancer and decoder. Training freezes both the Swin Transformer and BERT encoders, optimizes remaining parameters with Adam (lr=1e-4, 30 epochs, batch size 4), and uses a combined L1 localization loss and focal classification loss with Hungarian matching.

## Results

On FSC-147, CountSE achieves MAE 8.51 (val) and 7.84 (test), outperforming the best text-only zero-shot method GroundingREC (MAE 10.06 val, 10.12 test) by 15.4% and 22.5% respectively. It also surpasses the best visual-exemplar few-shot method DAVE (MAE 8.91 val, 8.66 test) by 4.4% and 9.4% in MAE. On CARPK, CountSE achieves MAE 2.79, beating the previous best CountGD (MAE 3.83) by 24.1%. On ShanghaiTech-A, CountSE reaches MAE 129.7, improving over the best open-set method CountGD (MAE 141.9) by 8.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CountSE (text-only) outperforms all seven text-guided zero-shot baselines on FSC-147, with the largest gap over GroundingREC (MAE 10.06→8.51, −15.4% val) and GroundingDINO (12.07→8.51, −29.5% val). It also beats all six visual-exemplar few-shot methods, including DAVE (8.91→8.51 val, 8.66→7.84 test) and CACViT (10.63→8.51 val), despite requiring no manual exemplar annotations. The only method that outperforms CountSE is CountGD with both text and visual exemplars (MAE 7.10 val, 5.74 test), a gap of 1.41 and 2.10 in MAE respectively. On CARPK, CountSE (MAE 2.79) surpasses every baseline including CountGD with visual exemplars (3.68) and text-only (3.83). On ShanghaiTech-A, CountSE (MAE 129.7) beats all open-set methods (best: CountGD at 141.9) and all specific-category methods (best: CrowdClip at 217.0), demonstrating strong cross-dataset generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

CountSE builds directly on the GroundingDINO vision-language framework as its counting backbone, adopting its Transformer architecture, enhancer, and decoder. It inherits the text-feature concatenation strategy and loss formulation from CountGD, and borrows the spectral clustering mechanism from DAVE for exemplar filtering. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of 'soft exemplars'—dynamically selected image feature patches guided by text semantics—positioned between the information-poor text-only zero-shot methods and the annotation-heavy hard-exemplar few-shot methods, effectively automating what previously required manual bounding-box annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in closing the efficiency-performance gap: CountSE achieves near-few-shot accuracy with only a text prompt, eliminating the costly manual exemplar annotation that limits real-world deployment of open-set counting. However, it still trails CountGD (which uses both text and annotated exemplars) by a noticeable margin, and the authors acknowledge failure in complex scenes where objects resemble backgrounds. Open questions include whether the spectral clustering step (which yields only a 0.05 MAE gain on the val set) is truly necessary, whether the approach extends to video or 3D counting, and whether the fixed N=18 exemplar budget generalizes across vastly different scene densities.
