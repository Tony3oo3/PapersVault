## Problem

Existing Consistency Distillation (CD)-based methods for text-to-3D generation (e.g., CDS, GCS) suffer from improper conditional guidance due to an inherent imbalance between self-consistency and cross-consistency in their distillation losses, leading to semantically inconsistent details or overexposure artifacts. Additionally, these methods enforce consistency across the entire PF-ODE trajectory, resulting in a large distillation error upper bound that limits generation fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251696/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt as input and produces a 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) asset that can be rendered from arbitrary camera views. The 3D representation is initialized from a Point-E point cloud and optimized for 5,000 iterations on a single A100 GPU, yielding a complete 3D asset in approximately 32 minutes with Classifier-Free Guidance or 38 minutes with Perp-Neg. The underlying 2D prior is Stable Diffusion 2.1, and the generated 3DGS can be rendered at real-time speeds for downstream use.

## Method

SegmentDreamer reformulates Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) through Segmented Consistency Trajectory Distillation (SCTD), which partitions the PF-ODE trajectory into N_s sub-intervals and enforces consistency only within each segment, explicitly defining the relationship between self-consistency and cross-consistency constraints. This segmentation yields a theoretically tighter distillation error upper bound of O(Δt)(s_{m+1}−s_m) compared to O(Δt)T for CDS. The loss is further simplified by omitting the generative prior term and applying stop-gradient operations, reducing the number of conditional U-Net evaluations per iteration. A dynamic sampling strategy adaptively switches between one-step and two-step unconditional deterministic sampling based on a time threshold t_τ, and the unconditional consistency function is approximated by the segment start point z_{s_m} to eliminate U-Net Jacobian computation. The 3D representation is a 3DGS initialized by Point-E, optimized with the Adam optimizer over 5,000 iterations.

## Results

On 40 prompts evaluated across 120 camera views, SegmentDreamer achieves the best CLIP-L score (30.88), ImageReward score (0.020), and FID (110.45) among all compared methods. It also requires the shortest generation time at 38 minutes on a single A100 GPU, compared to 45 min (LucidDreamer), 60 min (DreamFusion), 80 min (ConnectCD), and 140 min (Consistent3D). In a 40-volunteer user study, SegmentDreamer is ranked first (lowest rank) on all three criteria: text alignment (1.57), object realism (1.47), and object details (1.33).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SegmentDreamer outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest CD-based competitor ConnectCD [22], it improves CLIP-L by 0.15 (30.88 vs. 30.73), IR by 0.002 (0.020 vs. 0.018), FID by 2.16 (110.45 vs. 112.61), and cuts generation time nearly in half (38 vs. 80 min). Against the SD-based DreamFusion [31], the gains are larger: +2.41 CLIP-L, +0.024 IR, and −30.39 FID, while also halving the training time. LucidDreamer [23] is faster than DreamFusion but still trails SegmentDreamer by 0.89 CLIP-L and 11.35 FID. Consistent3D [48] is the slowest baseline (140 min) and lags SegmentDreamer by 0.28 CLIP-L and 3.16 FID. In the user study, SegmentDreamer achieves the best (lowest) average rank on all three criteria, with the largest margin over ConnectCD on object details (Q3: 1.33 vs. 1.93), reflecting fewer artifacts and overexposure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a corrective to the recent line of CD-based text-to-3D methods (Consistent3D/CDS and Connect3D/GCS), identifying a shared flaw: an imbalance between self-consistency and cross-consistency that causes improper conditional guidance under CFG. It draws directly from segmented consistency models in 2D image generation (Hyper-SD, Phased Consistency Model) to partition the PF-ODE trajectory into sub-trajectories, reformulating the original SDS loss from DreamFusion into the proposed SCTD. The theoretical contribution—a tighter distillation error bound of O(Δt)(s_{m+1}−s_m) versus O(Δt)T for CDS—builds on Lipschitz-based analyses from the consistency model literature but applies them to the 3D distillation setting for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By explicitly decoupling self- and cross-consistency within each sub-trajectory segment, SCTD offers a principled fix for the overexposure and semantic inconsistency artifacts that plague existing CD-based text-to-3D pipelines, while the tighter error bound provides a theoretical justification for the quality gains. The practical impact is significant: ~32–38 minute generation on a single A100 (vs. 80–140 min for competitors) with 3DGS makes high-fidelity text-to-3D more accessible. However, the method remains limited to single-instance generation and performs suboptimally on multi-object prompts, and the reliance on a fixed noise ε* and heuristic segmentation strategies (equal vs. monotonically increasing) leaves open questions about optimal trajectory partitioning for diverse object categories.
