## Problem

Existing AI-generated video detectors rely on learned classifiers that lack interpretability and fail to deeply analyze temporal artifacts from a physical-theory perspective. As generation quality improves, pixel-level and region-specific artifact detectors lose generalization, yet no prior work has grounded detection in the second-order dynamics (acceleration) that real-world motion inherently exhibits.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251251/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a short video clip (up to 2 seconds), samples frames at 8 fps, crops 10% of the longer edge, and resizes each frame to 224×224 JPEG. A pre-trained visual encoder (default XCLIP-B/16, but any ViT or CNN backbone works) produces per-frame embeddings, from which a single scalar — the standard deviation of second-order temporal features — is computed as the real-vs-generated score. No training data, generated samples, or learned classifier parameters are required at inference time.

## Method

D3 is a training-free pipeline that exploits a physical insight: real videos follow second-order Newtonian dynamics and thus show high-volatility acceleration in their temporal features, whereas current generators produce unnaturally flat second-order patterns. Concretely, it (1) encodes each frame with a frozen visual encoder to obtain zero-order features, (2) computes first-order features as L2 distance (or cosine similarity) between consecutive frame embeddings, (3) applies the second-order central difference formula to the first-order sequence to obtain per-frame acceleration values, and (4) reports the standard deviation of these second-order values as the detection score. The entire procedure is a closed-form mathematical computation on top of a single forward pass through a pre-trained encoder, requiring no training, no generated-video data, and no learned decision boundary, yet it generalizes across 40 test subsets from four out-of-distribution benchmarks.

## Results

On the primary GenVideo benchmark (10 subsets), D3 achieves 98.46% mAP, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art DeMamba (81.66%) by 10.39% absolute mAP and the best image-level baseline FID (88.07%) by 10.39%. Across the three additional challenging datasets, D3 achieves 98.87% mAP on EvalCrafter (14 subsets), 99.16% mAP on VideoPhy (10 subsets), and 88.46% mAP on VidProM (6 subsets), consistently surpassing all baselines. D3 is entirely training-free, requiring only a pre-trained visual encoder (XCLIP-B/16) and L2 distance computation, with inference taking 56 seconds per 1,000 video samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D3 decisively outperforms all 9 baselines on GenVideo (98.46% vs. 81.66% for DeMamba, 88.07% for FID, 78.31% for XCLIP), with the largest margins over video-level deepfake detectors designed for facial forgery (MINITIME 57.38%, TALL 62.25%, FTCN 71.94%). On EvalCrafter, D3 leads FID (98.87% vs. 95.59%) and DeMamba (98.87% vs. 76.37%) by 3.28% and 22.50% respectively. On VideoPhy, D3 achieves 99.16% mAP, surpassing FID (94.69%) by 4.47% and DeMamba (51.47%) by a striking 47.69%. On VidProM, D3 (88.46%) essentially ties FID (88.49%) but still beats DeMamba (81.72%) by 6.74%; the only notable weakness is on the T2VZ subset (45.11%), attributed to T2VZ's very low generation quality producing chaotic, non-dynamic outputs. Image-level baselines (FID, NPR) show stronger cross-dataset generalization than video-level deepfake detectors, but D3's second-order temporal analysis consistently surpasses both categories across 40 test subsets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in AI-generated video detection, where methods like DeMamba, DeCoF, and AIGVDet train deep classifiers on paired real/generated video data. Instead, it repurposes the second-order central difference formula from Newtonian mechanics and control theory as a training-free detection signal, using pre-trained visual encoders (XCLIP, DINOv2, etc.) solely as feature extractors rather than learned detectors. The conceptual lineage connects to prior work on temporal artifacts (DeCoF) and generalization-focused image detectors (FID, NPR), but the key departure is grounding the detection criterion in a physical model of motion dynamics rather than learned statistical patterns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of D3 eliminates the need for curated generated-video training data, which is a significant practical advantage as new generators emerge continuously. The physical interpretation (second-order dynamics / acceleration) offers interpretability that trained black-box classifiers lack, and the finding that even lightweight encoders (MobileNet-V3) retain strong performance suggests the signal is robust across feature spaces. Limitations include degraded performance on low-quality generators (T2VZ at 45.11% AP) and the open question of whether improved generators that better model second-order dynamics will close this gap. Future work could extend the physical analysis to additional dimensions (RGB color dynamics, bitrate characteristics) to build a more comprehensive detection framework.
