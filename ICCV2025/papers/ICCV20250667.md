## Problem

Existing human image animation methods are designed for single-person scenarios and fail in multi-identity settings: they cannot reliably associate each individual's appearance with the correct driving pose when multiple people swap positions, nor can they model the 3D spatial relationships needed for realistic human-human and human-object interactions (e.g., objects floating, blurring, or vanishing during motion).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250667/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image containing up to N humans, a sequence of 2D OpenPose skeletons per frame, camera extrinsics, and per-frame SAM2 tracking masks that label which pixel belongs to which identity. It outputs a 48-frame multi-human RGB video at 896×512 or 512×896, jointly with per-frame depth and surface-normal maps. Notably, depth and normals are predicted outputs rather than required inputs, and normal supervision is restricted to human-mask regions because the Sapiens estimator degrades on backgrounds.

## Method

Built on CamAnimate (Stable Diffusion 1.5 + AnimateDiff + ControlNet pose guider + CameraCtrl Plücker embedding), the framework introduces two mechanisms. First, ID-Specific Embedding Learning assigns N learnable query vectors (analogous to DETR object queries) to identities; per-frame tracking masks scatter the appropriate embedding into each spatial location to form an ID-aware feature map, which is injected into the denoising UNet via a zero-initialized convolution so training starts from the single-person baseline. Second, Latent Structural Video Diffusion replaces the single-modality UNet with a multi-branch architecture: shared middle layers enforce cross-modality correlation while modality-specific entry/exit convolutions keep RGB, depth, and noise predictions spatially aligned; a parallel reference UNet warps depth and normals from the reference image to reduce the denoiser's geometric inference burden. Training uses v-prediction with a summed L2 loss over all three modalities at a shared timestep, supervised by DepthCrafter depth maps and Sapiens normals (human-mask only), over a 45K-video dataset (HumanVid + 25K new Multi-HumanVid clips) in two stages (full-parameter then frozen-backbone camera/motion fine-tuning).

## Results

On the Multi-HumanVid test set (80 interaction-centric videos, 48 frames each), Structural Video Diffusion achieves SSIM 0.691, PSNR 20.685, LPIPS 0.233, FVD 878.2, and FID 30.57. The most striking gain is in FID, where the method reduces the score from 54.09 (CamAnimate) to 30.57, a 43% improvement, indicating substantially better perceptual quality. FVD also improves by 10.6% over CamAnimate (878.2 vs. 982.1) and by 15.8% over MimicMotion (878.2 vs. 1042.6), reflecting better temporal coherence in complex multi-identity scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: MimicMotion [71] and CamAnimate [60], both of which are single-person human image animation methods. Structural Video Diffusion outperforms both on all five metrics (SSIM, PSNR, LPIPS, FVD, FID). Against CamAnimate (the closest baseline, sharing the same AnimateDiff foundation), the proposed method gains +0.042 SSIM, +1.13 dB PSNR, −0.032 LPIPS, −103.9 FVD, and −23.52 FID. Against MimicMotion, the gaps are even larger in FVD (−164.4) and FID (−28.54), partly because MimicMotion cannot follow camera trajectories. The authors note that CamAnimate, despite sharing the same video generation backbone, degrades significantly in multi-identity interaction scenarios, confirming that the ID-embedding and structural (depth/normal) learning components are the key differentiators. A user study with 20 participants further corroborates the quantitative gains, with 91.25% preference for the proposed method over CamAnimate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-person pose-guided human video generation line (Animate Anyone, MagicAnimate, Champ, MimicMotion, CamAnimate) to the multi-identity interaction setting, building directly on CamAnimate's camera-controlled diffusion framework and AnimateDiff's motion module. The two key departures are: (1) learnable ID-specific embeddings tied to SAM2 tracking masks to disambiguate which appearance belongs to which pose in multi-person scenes, and (2) a multi-branch denoising UNet that jointly predicts RGB, depth, and surface-normal maps to capture 3D spatial relationships during human-object interactions. The authors also extend the HumanVid dataset with 25K additional multi-human interaction videos, creating the Multi-HumanVid benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic attempt at multi-identity human video generation with interactions, filling a clear gap where all prior methods were limited to single subjects. The Multi-HumanVid dataset (45K total videos with depth, normal, and mask annotations) provides a practical resource for the community. However, significant limitations remain: the method is constrained to the SD 1.5 UNet architecture rather than modern DiT-based video models, the surface-normal modality actually degrades performance due to poor estimation quality, and the evaluation set is small (80 videos) with only two baselines. Open questions include whether the ID-embedding and structural learning ideas transfer to larger video diffusion transformers and whether better normal estimation would unlock the full potential of the multi-modality approach.
