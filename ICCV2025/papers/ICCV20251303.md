## Problem

Estimating physical properties (density, elasticity, friction, hardness) from visual data remains under-explored because intrinsic properties are not directly observable, ground-truth labels are scarce, and prediction is inherently ambiguous from limited visible surfaces. Existing approaches either require task-specific training data, depend on multi-view video sequences, or demand manual assignment of material properties to 3D representations, limiting practical deployment in AR, robotics, and physics simulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251303/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images (30 views for 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction, 10 views for property estimation) of a single object and produces 3D Gaussians annotated with per-part physical properties including density, Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, material type, friction coefficient, and thickness. These annotated Gaussians directly yield material segmentation maps (evaluated via mIoU), adaptive grasping force ranges (F_min to F_max) for a robotic gripper, and MPM-compatible parameters for physics-based dynamic simulation. The entire pipeline is training-free, relying on GPT-4V API calls and SAM inference rather than learned weights.

## Method

GaussianProperty is a training-free framework that couples SAM's part-level segmentation (via a 32×32 grid of point prompts) with GPT-4V's recognition capability through a global-local reasoning module: the model first sees the full object, then a highlighted partial segmentation, then the isolated part, with gradual prompt guidance instructing it to describe the part, identify its material from a curated library of 15 families and 600+ materials, and output specific physical property values. The 2D property estimates are lifted to 3D by projecting each reconstructed Gaussian onto all visible views and applying a frequency-based voting strategy to select the most consistently predicted attribute per Gaussian, avoiding single-view errors. For downstream applications, the annotated Gaussians feed directly into PhysGaussian's MPM solver for dynamic simulation, and a closed-form grasping force model computes safe force bounds from the predicted density, friction, Young's modulus, and thickness of the contact part.

## Results

On material segmentation (mIoU), GaussianProperty achieves 55.83% average on ABO and 34.83% average on MVImgNet, substantially outperforming NeRF2Physics (25.59% and 4.02% respectively). The largest gains appear on ABO where the method more than doubles the baseline (e.g., 78.40% on Ceramic vs. 38.44%, 67.57% on Fabric vs. 40.26%). For robotic grasping on 16 real-world objects, the proposed adaptive force strategy achieves a 93.75% success rate (93.75% picked-up, 100% no-damage), compared to 68.75% for the best fixed-force baseline (MaxGF).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole direct method baseline is NeRF2Physics [50], which uses language-embedded point clouds with zero-shot kernel regression for physical property estimation. GaussianProperty outperforms NeRF2Physics by a wide margin on material segmentation mIoU: 55.83% vs. 25.59% average on ABO (a 2.2× improvement) and 34.83% vs. 4.02% on MVImgNet (an 8.7× improvement). The gap is particularly stark on MVImgNet, where NeRF2Physics struggles with diverse material categories (e.g., 1.15% on Glass, 0.38% on Foam), while GaussianProperty benefits from GPT-4V's broad open-world material knowledge. For robotic grasping, the method is compared against three fixed-force baselines (MinGF at 15, MidGF at 60, MaxGF at 100 normalized input). GaussianProperty achieves 93.75% success rate with 100% no-damage, beating MaxGF (68.75% success, 75% no-damage) by 25 percentage points in success and 25 in no-damage, while matching its 93.75% picked-up rate. The only failure case is an object exceeding the robot's mechanical force limit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: LMM-based visual reasoning (GPT-4V), part-level segmentation (SAM), and explicit 3D representation (3D Gaussian Splatting). It departs from NeRF2Physics, which uses LLMs with a language-embedded point cloud and kernel regression for property estimation, by instead leveraging GPT-4V's direct visual recognition in a training-free, zero-shot pipeline. It also addresses a key limitation of PhysGaussian and related physics-based Gaussian simulation methods, which require manual assignment of material properties to each Gaussian point, by automating this step through multi-view 2D-to-3D projection with frequency-based voting. The global-local reasoning module (combining whole-object context with SAM-segmented part details) is a novel architectural choice not present in prior LMM-based property estimation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for two downstream applications: it eliminates the tedious manual property annotation bottleneck in physics-based Gaussian simulation (PhysGaussian), and it enables material-sensitive robotic grasping from visual input alone without tactile sensors. However, the method's reliance on GPT-4V API calls introduces cost, latency, and reproducibility concerns, and the authors acknowledge difficulty with ambiguous material surfaces. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to complex multi-object scenes, whether the 15-family/600-material candidate library is sufficient for industrial applications, and whether the voting strategy degrades when objects have highly heterogeneous materials visible from limited viewpoints.
