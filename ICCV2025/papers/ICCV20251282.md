## Problem

Existing deformable 3D Gaussian methods learn motion patterns from individual timestamps in isolation or from local neighboring-frame constraints, neglecting global cross-time relationships. This limitation makes reconstruction of complex dynamic scenes—featuring violent movement, extreme-shaped geometries, or reflective surfaces—particularly challenging, as the deformation field cannot capture long-range temporal dependencies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251282/cropped_page0_idx7.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes calibrated multi-view video sequences (e.g., N3DV) or monocular dynamic videos (e.g., HyperNeRF, NeRF-DS) along with camera intrinsics and extrinsics. It outputs a canonical 3D Gaussian representation with a time-variant deformation field that enables real-time novel view rendering. A key design constraint is that TimeFormer operates exclusively during training; at inference, only the base deformation field is used, preserving the original rendering speed of the underlying 3DGS pipeline.

## Method

TimeFormer introduces a Cross-Temporal Transformer Encoder that treats B randomly sampled timestamps as a 'time batch': each Gaussian's canonical position is replicated B times, concatenated with position-encoded timestamps, and processed through M self-attention layers to produce per-timestamp position offsets. These offsets are added to the canonical positions before the shared deformation field, so the gradient at each timestamp is influenced by all other timestamps in the batch (an additional ∂P/∂μ term in the chain rule), enabling global motion pattern learning. A two-stream optimization strategy shares deformation field weights between the TimeFormer-augmented branch and the base branch, transferring learned motion knowledge so that TimeFormer can be entirely removed at inference. The method is plug-and-play, requiring no optical flow or other external priors, and is validated as a drop-in enhancement for 4DGS, STGS, and DeformGS.

## Results

On the N3DV multi-view dataset, TimeFormer improves 4DGS mean PSNR from 31.10 to 31.84 (+0.74) and STGS from 31.62 to 32.23 (+0.61), outperforming all baselines including K-Plane (31.63) and 4D-Rotor (31.06). On the HyperNeRF monocular dataset, TimeFormer boosts DeformGS mean PSNR from 26.09 to 27.03 (+0.94) and 4DGS from 27.14 to 28.03 (+0.89), with SSIM gains of +0.028 and +0.019 respectively. On NeRF-DS (dynamic specular objects), TimeFormer-enhanced DeformGS achieves 24.10 mean PSNR vs. 23.59 for the original. Additionally, TimeFormer reduces Gaussian count (e.g., 172.3k→135.5k for 4DGS on HyperNeRF) while increasing FPS (35.7→40.9), demonstrating more efficient canonical space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TimeFormer consistently outperforms all compared baselines across all three datasets. On N3DV, STGS+TimeFormer (32.23 PSNR) surpasses K-Plane (31.63), 4D-Rotor (31.06), GS4D (30.07), and MixVoxels (30.80), while 4DGS+TimeFormer (31.84) beats 4D-Rotor (31.06) and GS4D (30.07) but falls slightly below K-Plane (31.63) in PSNR, though K-Plane achieves higher SSIM (0.960 vs. 0.941). On HyperNeRF, both TimeFormer-enhanced methods dominate: 4DGS+TimeFormer (28.03 PSNR) exceeds DeformGS (26.09) by nearly 2 dB, and DeformGS+TimeFormer (27.03) improves over the original by 0.94 dB. On NeRF-DS, DeformGS+TimeFormer (24.10 PSNR) edges out NeRF-DS (23.57) and the original DeformGS (23.59), though NeRF-DS retains a higher SSIM (0.895 vs. 0.859). The paper also shows TimeFormer reduces Gaussian count by 20–50% while increasing FPS (e.g., DeformGS 30.1→58.9 FPS on HyperNeRF), a unique advantage over all baselines that do not offer such efficiency gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

TimeFormer extends the deformable 3D Gaussian Splatting lineage (4DGS, STGS, DeformGS) by inserting a Cross-Temporal Transformer Encoder before the deformation field, drawing conceptual inspiration from BatchFormer's batch-dimension attention. It departs from prior motion-modeling approaches that rely on explicit optical-flow regularization or local neighboring-frame constraints by instead learning cross-time relationships globally via self-attention over randomly sampled timestamps. The two-stream weight-sharing strategy is a novel training trick that transfers the learned temporal knowledge into the base deformation field, allowing the module to be discarded at inference without quality loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, training-only design makes TimeFormer broadly applicable to any existing deformable 3DGS backbone without architectural modification, lowering the barrier for practitioners to adopt global temporal modeling. The observation that the module simultaneously improves reconstruction quality and reduces the number of Gaussians (thereby increasing FPS) suggests that global temporal attention acts as an implicit regularizer on the canonical space, an insight that could inform future efficient 4D representations. Limitations include roughly doubled training time, modest PSNR gains (~0.6–0.9 dB), and evaluation on only three datasets with limited scene diversity; open questions include scalability to very long video sequences and whether the benefit persists for highly non-rigid or topologically changing scenes.
