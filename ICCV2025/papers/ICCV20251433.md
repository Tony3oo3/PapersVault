## Problem

Existing 3D head stylization methods built on Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) suffer from severe identity collapse: different input faces are stylized into nearly identical outputs, losing the unique features of each subject. Additionally, most approaches rely on EG3D-based generators that only synthesize near-frontal views, limiting 360-degree consistency. Prior work addressed SDS's diversity loss by adding auxiliary regularizers rather than modifying the distillation objective itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251433/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a real head image (inverted into PanoHead's W+ latent space via optimization with the original generator) and a free-form text prompt describing the target style. It produces a fine-tuned 3D triplane representation that can be rendered from arbitrary 360-degree camera poses, yielding 512×512 stylized head images. The PanoHead pipeline internally renders 64×64 triplane views that are upscaled by a convolutional super-resolution network, and the diffusion model operates on 512×512 latent-space images (4-channel VAE latents).

## Method

The core contribution replaces SDS with negative log-likelihood distillation (LD) adapted from PlacidDreamer to the GAN setting, which is diversity-seeking (it does not subtract ground-truth noise ε) and avoids the mode-collapse that causes identity loss. Three key mechanisms enhance the base LD: (1) rank-weighted score tensors, where the 4×4 SVD of the score along the VAE latent channel is reweighted with linearly decaying coefficients W=diag(1, 0.75, 0.5, 0.25) to suppress color-tint artifacts; (2) mirror gradients, which exploit yaw-symmetry of human heads to share a single score estimate between mirror poses and mirror the backpropagated gradient, enforcing 3D consistency without extra diffusion queries; and (3) multi-view grid denoising, where four rendered views are arranged in a 2×2 grid fed to the denoising UNet (with a depth-conditioned ControlNet) so the score correlates across poses, with gradients injected before the super-resolution layer to avoid resolution mismatch. Training fine-tunes PanoHead for 10k iterations (batch 1, Adam lr=1e-4, CFG=7.5) per prompt on a single A100 GPU.

## Results

Across five stylization prompts (Pixar, Joker, Werewolf, Sketch, Statue) evaluated on ~100 FFHQ identities, the proposed method achieves the best FID (77.6–144.5), CLIP similarity (0.77–0.89), ArcFace ID similarity (0.55–0.75), and multi-view depth consistency ΔD (0.002–0.014) compared to all baselines. The most dramatic gains are in identity preservation: e.g., for the Joker prompt, ID similarity is 0.56 vs. 0.47 (DiffusionGAN3D) and 0.41 (StyleGANFusion), while FID drops from 110.7 to 67.7. The method also outperforms 2D editing baselines (InstructPix2Pix, InstantID) on every metric, confirming that 3D-aware domain adaptation with LD distillation is superior to per-view 2D editing for multi-view consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all seven baselines on every metric (FID, CLIP, ID, ΔD) across all five prompts. Against the strongest 3D domain-adaptation competitor, DiffusionGAN3D (SDS + relative distance loss), the gains are most pronounced in identity preservation: ID similarity improves from 0.46→0.69 (Pixar) and 0.47→0.56 (Joker), while FID drops from 189.3→77.6 (Pixar) and 110.7→67.7 (Joker). StyleGANFusion (SDS + directional regularizer) similarly suffers from identity collapse, producing near-identical faces for different inputs (ID 0.41 on Joker), whereas the proposed LD-based approach maintains distinct identities. CLIP-guided methods (StyleCLIP, StyleGAN-NADA) achieve moderate stylization quality but lag in both FID and ID, with StyleGAN-NADA reaching ID 0.61 on Pixar versus 0.69 for the proposed method. The 2D editing baselines (InstructPix2Pix, InstantID) are weakest on multi-view consistency (ΔD up to 0.066) and stylization fidelity (FID up to 248.8), confirming that per-view 2D editing cannot match 3D-aware domain adaptation. Instruct-NeRF2NeRF is discussed qualitatively and in runtime comparisons (~1 h NeRF fitting + 3 h stylization) but is not included in the quantitative table; the proposed method requires ~3 h tuning per prompt and ~0.5 s inference per image.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on PanoHead's 360-degree triplane GAN backbone and adapts the negative log-likelihood distillation (LD) objective from PlacidDreamer to the GAN domain-adaptation setting. It departs from the dominant SDS-based paradigm (DreamFusion, StyleGANFusion, DiffusionGAN3D) where prior work addressed identity collapse by adding auxiliary regularizers (directional losses, relative distance losses); here, the distillation objective itself is replaced, and the authors argue this is the more principled fix. The method further extends LD with three GAN-specific architectural modifications—mirror-pose gradient sharing, multi-view grid denoising with depth-conditioned ControlNet, and SVD-based rank weighting on the 4-channel VAE latent score tensor—that have no direct counterpart in the 2D or NeRF-based distillation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear conceptual distinction (LD is diversity-seeking and sharp; SDS is mode-seeking and blurry) that should inform future distillation-based domain adaptation for one-step generators, and the rank-weighting trick is a lightweight, transferable regularization that could apply to other latent-space distillation tasks. Limitations remain: earrings and hair accessories are still lost, output resolution is bounded by PanoHead's 512×512 SR network, and the method requires ~3 hours of per-prompt generator tuning on an A100. The authors point toward extending the framework to class-agnostic large reconstruction models, which could unlock broader 3D editing applications beyond heads.
