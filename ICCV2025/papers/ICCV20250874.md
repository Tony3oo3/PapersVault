## Problem

Existing online HD mapping methods treat mapping as a single-pass process, unable to leverage multi-traversal knowledge from repeated visits to the same scene. Two critical capabilities—precise localization within a crowdsourced prior-map and robust detection of road structural changes—are typically solved in isolation, preventing a unified framework that simultaneously maintains map freshness, aligns current observations with prior spatial data, and serves downstream driving modules in real time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250874/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The onboard model consumes calibrated surround-view camera frames and a truncated local crowdsourced HD map (fetched via a GPS meters-level initial pose over a [-36,36]×[-18,18] m perceptual range), producing vectorized map elements annotated with per-vertex Laplace-distribution parameters (location and scale), a 6-DOF SE(3) pose relative to the prior-map, and a partition of elements into matched, outdated, and newly observed sets. A separate cloud-side module asynchronously fuses multi-traversal observations using union-find vertex tracking and Gaussian-mixture density refinement to update the global crowdsourced prior-map.

## Method

RTMap introduces hybrid queries in a transformer decoder that combine prior-map embeddings (Q_prior) and sensor-derived queries (Q_new), then differentiates Q_prior into Q_map (reliable correspondences) and Q_fake (outdated elements) via an existence-aware matching strategy that pre-attributes only Q_map during training, leaving Q_fake to exhibit unstable reference-point behavior and low category confidence at inference. Vertex-level uncertainty is modeled with univariate Laplace distributions supervised by a negative log-likelihood loss combined with Manhattan distance, enabling both probabilistic-aware MAP localization (Levenberg-Marquardt minimization of Mahalanobis residuals with Gaussian-mixture covariance) and noise-aware cloud-side crowdsourcing. A pose auxiliary loss provides additional decoder supervision for correcting initial pose misalignment, and the entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with ResNet50 image features, synthetic map-change perturbations, and Hungarian matching for new-element assignment.

## Results

On the TbV crowdsourcing task, RTMap achieves 57.6% mAP (straight) and 55.4% mAP (turning) after 3 traversal cycles, substantially outperforming the best single-traversal baselines (MapTRv2: 37.1%/26.0%, MapTracker: 39.9%/28.0%). For map-based localization on nuScenes, the optimization-based pose estimator T^R achieves 0.121 m lateral, 0.586 m longitudinal, and 0.368° yaw mean error. On change detection, RTMap reaches 57.4% mAcc, surpassing the TbV baseline (54.1%) with notably higher recall on changed events (48.9% vs 40.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RTMap dominates all three mapping baselines (MapTRv2, HRMapNet, MapTracker) on the TbV crowdsourcing task, with the gap widening across traversal cycles: at cycle 3, RTMap reaches 57.6% mAP (straight) and 55.4% (turning) versus 37.1%/26.0% for MapTRv2, 39.2%/27.8% for HRMapNet, and 39.9%/28.0% for MapTracker — a 17–29 point margin. The advantage stems from RTMap's ability to exploit the prior-map for localization and change detection, which single-traversal methods cannot do. On change detection (Table 2), RTMap improves mAcc from 54.1% to 57.4% over the TbV baseline, with a large gain in changed-event recall (48.9% vs 40.0%) at a small cost in unchanged-event accuracy (66.0% vs 68.2%). For localization on nuScenes, the optimization-based estimator T^R (0.121 m lat, 0.368° yaw mean) outperforms the end-to-end regressed pose T^E (0.142 m lat, 0.521° yaw), and both benefit from the proposed L_pose and L_pts auxiliary losses.

## Relation to Prior Work

RTMap extends the MapTR/MapTRv2 encoder-decoder paradigm for online vectorized HD mapping by introducing hybrid queries (Q_map, Q_fake, Q_new) and an existence-aware matching scheme that departs from MapEX's pre-attribution strategy to explicitly separate matched, outdated, and newly observed elements. It incorporates Laplace-distribution-based vertex uncertainty modeling from Gu et al. (CVPR 2024) not merely for downstream prediction but as a first-class signal for both MAP-based 6-DOF localization and noise-aware multi-traversal crowdsourced map fusion. The key conceptual departure from prior single-traversal methods (HDMapNet, MapTR, MapTracker, HRMapNet) is the persistent, self-evolutional crowdsourced prior-map that is asynchronously updated in the cloud, turning mapping from a one-shot perception task into a recursive, multi-agent memory system.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By tightly coupling localization, change detection, and prior-aided mapping in a single end-to-end onboard model, RTMap removes the need for standalone localization modules and offline map-update pipelines, which is practically significant for fleet-scale deployment where map freshness directly affects safety. However, the evaluation is limited to two datasets (TbV with ~200 scenarios and nuScenes), the change detection accuracy remains modest (~49% on the 'changed' class), and the crowdsourcing is simulated via spatially clustered clips rather than true multi-agent deployment. Open questions include scaling to city-level maps, incorporating LiDAR for multi-modal robustness, distinguishing transient occlusions from permanent structural changes, and validating the probabilistic fusion under genuinely heterogeneous sensor fleets.
