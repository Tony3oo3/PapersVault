## Problem

Existing GCN-based skeleton action recognition methods model joint relationships exclusively through binary pairwise edges, failing to capture the multi-joint collaborative patterns that define human actions (e.g., coordinated hand-leg movements). Although a few hyper-graph approaches have been explored, they rely on fixed, manually designed hyper-edge structures that cannot adapt to the diverse joint combinations across different action categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250338/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 4-stream skeleton sequences (joint coordinates, bone coordinates, joint motion, bone motion) with a fixed number of joints per frame (e.g., 25 for NTU-120), processed over a temporal window. Beyond the physical joints, the network introduces learnable virtual hyper-joints (3 per layer in the best configuration) that are shared across all frames and manually connected to every physical joint, effectively augmenting the input topology. The output is a single action class label predicted via cross-entropy loss, with a 4-stream ensemble used for final evaluation.

## Method

Hyper-GCN replaces the standard adjacency-matrix graph convolution with a multi-head hyper-graph convolution (M-HGC) that splits channel features into 8 parallel heads, each independently constructing an Adaptive Non-uniform Hyper-graph (A-NHG) via Euclidean-distance-based K-nearest selection in a learned feature subspace, yielding hyper-edges of variable size that capture action-specific multi-joint synergies. The normalized incidence matrix (D_v^{-1} H W D_e^{-1} H^T) is fused with the physical skeleton adjacency through a learnable scalar α per head. Virtual hyper-joints are injected as learnable parameters at each layer and regularized by a Divergence Loss that penalizes cosine similarity among them, preventing homogenization while allowing them to carry global semantic information. The full architecture comprises 9 spatio-temporal blocks in 3 stages with dense connections, where each block pairs M-HGC with multi-scale temporal convolution, and the entire network is trained end-to-end with label-smoothed cross-entropy plus the divergence loss.

## Results

On NTU RGB+D 120, the base version of Hyper-GCN (1.1M params, 1.63 GFLOPs) achieves 90.5% (X-Sub) and 91.7% (X-Set), while the large version (2.3M params, 2.88 GFLOPs) reaches 90.9% and 92.0%. On NTU RGB+D 60, the large version attains 93.7% (X-Sub) and 97.8% (X-View), and on NW-UCLA it scores 97.6%. The base version already surpasses all GCN-based and hyper-graph-based SOTA methods, and the large version achieves 1st place on 4 of 5 benchmarks with only 2.3M parameters and 2.88 GFLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hyper-GCN (Base) outperforms all 12 GCN-based baselines across every benchmark; the closest GCN competitor, BlockGCN [57], scores 93.1/97.0/90.3/91.5/96.9 versus Hyper-GCN-B's 93.3/97.4/90.5/91.7/97.2. Against the three hyper-graph-based baselines (Hyper-GNN, Selective-HCN, DST-HCN), Hyper-GCN-B leads by 1.0–3.7 points on NTU60 X-Sub and 1.7–2.9 points on NTU120 X-Sub, confirming the advantage of adaptive over fixed hyper-graph construction. On NTU120, Hyper-GCN-B surpasses the strongest Transformer (SkateFormer [8]) by 0.7 points (X-Sub) and 0.3 points (X-Set) while using fewer parameters (1.1M vs 2.0M) and lower GFLOPs (1.63 vs 3.62). However, on NTU60 X-Sub and X-View, SkateFormer edges out Hyper-GCN-B by 0.2 and 0.4 points respectively, and on NW-UCLA SkateFormer leads by 1.1 points (98.3 vs 97.2). The large version (2.3M params) closes these gaps, matching or exceeding SkateFormer on all five benchmarks (93.7/97.8/90.9/92.0/97.6 vs 93.5/97.8/89.8/91.4/98.3), achieving 1st place on 4 of 5 benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GCN-based skeleton action recognition paradigm by replacing pairwise (binary) joint connections with an adaptive non-uniform hyper-graph, building directly on the HGNN+ incidence-matrix formulation for hyper-graph convolution. It departs from prior hyper-graph approaches (Hyper-GNN, Selective-HCN) that rely on fixed, manually-defined hyper-edge structures, instead learning the hyper-graph topology end-to-end via a K-nearest-neighbor construction in a learned feature subspace. The temporal processing reuses CTR-GCN's multi-scale temporal convolution, and the input embedding follows InfoGCN, positioning the contribution squarely within the lightweight GCN family rather than the heavier Transformer line.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving competitive or SOTA accuracy with extremely low parameter counts (1.1M base, 2.3M large) and GFLOPs, making the approach attractive for deployment-constrained settings where Transformer-based methods are prohibitive. The virtual hyper-joints with a Divergence Loss offer a principled way to separate local and global semantic roles, an idea that could transfer to other graph-based recognition tasks. Open questions include whether the Euclidean-distance-based hyper-edge construction generalises beyond skeleton data, whether the non-uniform structure provides consistent gains over well-tuned uniform hyper-graphs at larger scale, and whether the adaptive topology remains stable under distribution shift.
