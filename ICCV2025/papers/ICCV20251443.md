## Problem

Most Video-LLMs rely on a pretrained vision encoder to extract frame-wise features before feeding them to an LLM, incurring prohibitive computational costs that scale poorly with frame count, imposing fixed-resolution biases, and limiting fine-grained spatiotemporal interaction modeling. While encoder-free approaches have been explored for images (Fuyu, EVE), extending this paradigm to video remains largely untapped due to video's high dimensionality and temporal dependencies, making it a critical open frontier for efficient video-language understanding.

## Input / Output

ELVA consumes raw video frames at their native resolution and aspect ratio (no resizing or cropping), split into patches with special <FRAME> and <LINE> tokens marking temporal and spatial boundaries. During inference, a hybrid-resolution strategy mixes high-resolution frames (longest edge 672 px) with low-resolution frames (longest edge 336 px) within a single video to balance fidelity and cost. The model produces autoregressive text outputs—captions, QA answers, or free-form responses—conditioned on the video and a text prompt.

## Method

ELVA eliminates the vision encoder entirely by introducing a lightweight 9M-parameter video patch embedding layer that uses cross-attention over <FRAME> and <LINE> tokens to build spatiotemporal relationships before the LLM. A hierarchical token merging mechanism progressively drops and merges temporally redundant tokens across LLM layers using cosine similarity (threshold 0.6), achieving 50% compression while preserving salient features. A video guidance supervisor distills spatiotemporal knowledge from a pretrained SigLIP teacher via a tube-wise MSE alignment loss and a frame-wise contrastive loss, complementing the standard generative caption loss. Training follows a three-stage progressive paradigm (spatial warm-up → spatial-temporal pretraining → SFT) on 7M recaptioned video-text pairs, and inference leverages hybrid-resolution frame mixing to further reduce FLOPs by up to 95% and latency by 92% relative to encoder-based baselines.

## Results

ELVA (7B) achieves 47.1 on VideoMME, 51.8 on MLVU, and 46.1 on CinePile, matching or exceeding the best encoder-based 7B models (VideoLLaMA2: 46.6/48.5/44.6) on long-video benchmarks. The 1.5B variant reaches 41.8 on VideoMME and 47.6 on MLVU, competitive with 7B encoder-based models like Video-LLaVA (39.9/47.3). In terms of efficiency, ELVA reduces FLOPs by up to 95% and time-to-first-token (TTFT) by 92% compared to the LLaVA-Next-Video encoder-based baseline, with TTFT dropping from 15.18s to 0.56s at 128 frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ELVA (7B) outperforms VideoLLaMA2 on VideoMME (47.1 vs 46.6), MLVU (51.8 vs 48.5), and CinePile (46.1 vs 44.6), and surpasses VideoChat2 on VideoMME (47.1 vs 42.3) and MLVU (51.8 vs 47.9), but trails VideoChat2 on MVBench (51.2 vs 60.4) and EgoSchema (48.3 vs 54.4). On short-video tasks, ELVA (7B) scores 65.2 on MSVD and 48.7 on ActivityNet, below VideoLLaMA2 (70.9/50.2) and VideoChat2 (70.0/49.1), indicating a remaining gap in fine-grained spatial reasoning. Against encoder-free baselines, ELVA dominates: it exceeds Fuyu (8B) by 8.4 points on VideoMME and 20.7 on MLVU, and surpasses EVE (7B) by 17.8 on VideoMME and 15.0 on MLVU. The 1.5B ELVA already matches or exceeds 7B encoder-based models like Video-LLaVA on MLVU (47.6 vs 47.3) and CinePile (41.2 vs 22.5). In inference efficiency (Table 5), ELVA with hierarchical merging and hybrid resolution reduces FLOPs by 95% and TTFT by 92% at 32 frames, and by 96% on both metrics at 128 frames, compared to the LLaVA-Next-Video encoder-based baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

ELVA extends the encoder-free VLM paradigm established by Fuyu (native raster-scan tokenization with <LINE> tokens) and EVE (visual representation supervision) from the image domain to video, addressing the additional challenges of temporal modeling and high-dimensional spatiotemporal redundancy. It departs from the dominant encoder-decoder Video-LLM architecture (Video-LLaVA, VideoChat2, LLaMA-VID) by eliminating the pretrained vision encoder entirely, instead learning spatiotemporal representations directly within the LLM via a lightweight video patch embedding layer and a SigLIP-based guidance supervisor. The hierarchical token merging mechanism draws on recent token compression work (FrameFusion, DyCoke) but applies it progressively across LLM layers rather than as a one-shot pooling operation, and the hybrid-resolution inference strategy is novel to the video setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ELVA demonstrates that competitive video-language understanding is achievable without a dedicated vision encoder, reducing FLOPs by 95% and inference latency by 92%—a practical win for real-time and long-form video applications where encoder-side latency becomes the bottleneck. The power-law scaling results suggest the encoder-free paradigm can benefit from further scale-up in both model size and data, opening a path toward more flexible, resolution-agnostic video models. However, the model still relies on a pretrained video encoder (SigLIP) as a teacher during training, so the 'encoder-free' claim applies only to inference; whether fully encoder-free training (without any visual teacher) can match these results remains an open question. Generalization to much longer videos (beyond 128 frames) and to other modalities (audio, 3D) also remains unexplored.
