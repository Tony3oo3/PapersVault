## Problem

Existing photorealistic avatar models built on 3D Gaussian Splatting or NeRF capture face and hand appearance in isolation but ignore the non-rigid deformations, self-cast shadows, and skin-contact dynamics that occur during hand-face interactions — a behavior people perform 600–800 times per day. Rigid or static representations produce visibly unnatural results when a hand touches the face, and no prior method jointly models pose-dependent hand articulation effects (wrinkles, shading) and interaction-induced facial geometry changes in a single animatable avatar.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view video (7 training views, 1 held-out test view) with known camera intrinsics/extrinsics, from which FLAME and MANO template parameters plus coarse PBD-based per-vertex deformation offsets are extracted via multi-view template fitting. Hand and face regions are segmented with SAM2 and cropped to 512×512 patches using tracked bounding boxes. The output is a fully animatable 3D Gaussian avatar that renders photorealistic novel views and supports both self-reenactment (driving with held-out poses) and cross-actor reenactment (transferring FLAME/MANO parameters from a source subject to a target avatar).

## Method

InteractAvatar anchors 3D Gaussian kernels to FLAME (face) and MANO (hand) template mesh facets, so that Gaussians deform rigidly with the mesh during animation. A Dynamic Gaussian Hand module uses two MLPs (geometry and appearance) conditioned on canonical Gaussian position, hand pose, and relative hand-face orientation to predict per-Gaussian offsets in position, scale, rotation, color, and opacity, capturing articulation-dependent effects like self-cast shadows and wrinkles that static parameters cannot represent. A separate Hand-Face Interaction MLP takes the PBD-derived coarse deformation offset, PointNet++-extracted global geometric features (computed over a heuristic subset of non-rigid facial Gaussians), and relative hand-face pose to refine both geometry and appearance in the contact region, with distance-weighted blending to localize updates near the hand. Training follows a two-stage schedule (100k steps of static Gaussian optimization, then MLP fine-tuning with zero-initialized final layers) and includes scale and position regularization to prevent spiky Gaussians, plus a patch perceptual loss targeting the small hand/interaction region to strengthen the weak training signal from those pixels.

## Results

On the DECAF dataset, InteractAvatar achieves 29.85 PSNR and 0.0338 LPIPS on novel view synthesis, outperforming GaussianAvatar (26.71 PSNR, 0.0542 LPIPS) and SplattingAvatar (25.90 PSNR, 0.0584 LPIPS) by 3.1 and 3.9 dB in PSNR respectively. On self-enactment, InteractAvatar reaches 28.17 PSNR and 0.0396 LPIPS, again leading both baselines by 2.7–2.7 dB in PSNR. The largest relative gains appear in LPIPS (perceptual quality), where InteractAvatar reduces the score by ~38% compared to GaussianAvatar on novel views, reflecting its superior capture of dynamic hand details and hand-face interaction effects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InteractAvatar is compared against two recent hybrid mesh-Gaussian head avatar methods: GaussianAvatar (Qian et al., CVPR 2024) and SplattingAvatar (Shao et al., CVPR 2024), both naively extended to model hands via MANO. On novel view synthesis, InteractAvatar wins decisively on all three metrics, gaining +3.14 dB PSNR and −0.0204 LPIPS over GaussianAvatar, and +3.95 dB PSNR and −0.0246 LPIPS over SplattingAvatar. On self-enactment, InteractAvatar again leads on PSNR (+2.66 dB over GaussianAvatar, +1.99 dB over SplattingAvatar) and LPIPS (−0.0182 and −0.0150 respectively), but trails GaussianAvatar by 0.0037 on SSIM, which the authors attribute to MLP-induced noise in global facial structure during generalization. Qualitatively, both baselines fail to capture non-rigid hand-face deformation and pose-dependent hand appearance (self-cast shadows, wrinkles), while InteractAvatar reproduces these effects faithfully. The ablation (Table 2) confirms that removing the hand MLPs drops PSNR by 2.88 dB and LPIPS by 0.0236, and removing the interaction MLPs drops PSNR by 1.62 dB, validating each component's contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hybrid mesh-Gaussian avatar line (GaussianAvatar, SplattingAvatar) by adding a hand representation anchored to the MANO template and, critically, by introducing dynamic MLP-based refinement modules for pose-dependent hand appearance and non-rigid hand-face interaction. For the interaction component, it builds on DECAF's position-based-dynamics pipeline for coarse mesh deformation but departs from prior hand-face interaction work (DECAF, DICE) by shifting the focus from mesh recovery to photorealistic Gaussian rendering with learned appearance corrections. The dynamic hand module is conceptually distinct from MANUS, which anchors Gaussians to a skeleton with learned skinning weights rather than to mesh facets, and from LiveHand/LISA, which use implicit NeRF representations without explicit interaction modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first photorealistic avatar model to jointly render dynamic hands and non-rigid hand-face contact, addressing a gap that limits immersion in teleconferencing, gaming, and AR/VR where people touch their faces hundreds of times daily. Key limitations include reliance on the small DECAF dataset (4 subjects, 7 views), the need for multi-view capture with known calibration, and the fact that generalization to truly novel interaction configurations remains untested. Open questions include whether the approach can work from monocular video, whether the interaction MLPs generalize beyond the limited gesture vocabulary in the training data, and how to scale to full-body interactions beyond hand-face.
