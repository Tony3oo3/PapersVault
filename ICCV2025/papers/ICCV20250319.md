## Problem

Deep functional maps rely heavily on axiomatic regularizations such as Laplacian commutativity and orthogonality penalties, which limit accuracy and generalization to shape pairs where these assumptions do not hold. No prior work has replaced both the in-network regularization and the training loss with data-driven alternatives, restricting methods to scenarios where axiomatic models are valid.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250319/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of unregistered 3D triangular meshes as input and produces a point-to-point correspondence via a 30×30 functional map matrix in the Laplace-Beltrami spectral domain. At test time, a DiffusionNet feature extractor computes per-vertex descriptors, and the pipeline optimizes these descriptors zero-shot (no category-specific training) before converting the final functional map to pointwise correspondences via aligned eigenfunctions and nearest-neighbor search.

## Method

The paper trains a spectral diffusion model (DiT-S architecture, EDM formulation) on ~40,000 ground-truth 30×30 functional maps extracted from D-FAUST registrations, operating on absolute-valued maps to be sign-agnostic. The key technical contribution is a novel distillation strategy that converts the trained denoiser into a sparsity-promoting mask M_σ by averaging (|C|_σ − D(|C|_σ; σ)) / (2σ²|C|_σ) over positive noise samples, replacing axiomatic masks derived from Laplacian or Resolvent commutativity. At test time, the pipeline estimates a raw functional map from features, distills the mask from it, solves the regularized map equation, applies Zoomout to project into proper map space, and optimizes the feature extractor by minimizing a combined loss of L2 distance to the proper map and a Score Distillation Sampling term on the absolute raw map. This entirely data-driven regularization generalizes category-agnostically from human shapes to humanoids and animals without any retraining.

## Results

On near-isometric human benchmarks, DiffuMatch achieves geodesic errors of 1.9 (FAUST), 4.4 (SCAPE), and 3.9 (SHREC19), approaching the trained Simplified Fmaps baseline (1.7/2.3/3.4) while remaining fully zero-shot. On cross-category animal data, the advantage is dramatic: DiffuMatch scores 10.1 on SMAL and 2.9 on TOSCA, versus 42.1/5.1 for Simplified Fmaps and 54.6/32.8 for 3D-CODED. On the DT4D-Inter humanoid challenge, DiffuMatch (8.6) outperforms all other methods including SNK (9.0) and Simplified Fmaps (8.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest zero-shot competitor SNK [5], DiffuMatch wins on 6 of 7 datasets, with the largest margins on SHREC19 (3.9 vs 5.8) and TOSCA (2.9 vs 3.6); SNK edges ahead only on FAUST (1.8 vs 1.9) and SMAL (9.1 vs 10.1). Versus the strongest learned baseline Simplified Fmaps [45], DiffuMatch is slightly behind on in-domain human data (e.g., SCAPE 4.4 vs 2.3) but dominates on cross-category animal data, where Simplified Fmaps degrades to 42.1 on SMAL versus DiffuMatch's 10.1. Deformation-based methods 3D-CODED [30] and Neural Jacobian Fields [1] are far behind on all non-human categories (e.g., 45.0–61.4 on DT4D vs 1.8–8.6 for DiffuMatch), confirming their category-specific limitations. Among axiomatic baselines, Smooth Shells [24] is competitive on FAUST (2.5) but was not evaluated on all datasets; DiffuMatch's distilled mask alone (Ini+Zoomout with our mask) already outperforms both Laplacian and Resolvent mask initializations across the board.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of deep functional maps (Donati et al., 2020) and score-based generative modeling, repurposing the Score Distillation Sampling framework from DreamFusion (Poole et al., 2023) for a fundamentally different domain: regularizing spectral shape correspondences rather than guiding 3D scene generation. The key conceptual departure is replacing axiomatic regularizations (Laplacian commutativity, orthogonality, bijectivity) that have underpinned functional map pipelines since Ovsjanikov et al. (2012) with a single data-driven mask distilled from a diffusion model trained on ground-truth maps. Zoomout (Melzi et al., 2019) remains a pipeline component for proper-map projection, but the regularization signal itself is now learned rather than derived from geometric assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most significant implication is that structural properties of good functional maps (near-isometry, bijectivity, properness) can be captured implicitly by a generative model, potentially unifying what were previously separate hand-crafted penalties into a single learned prior. The category-agnostic generalization from human training data to animals and humanoids, if it holds at scale, points toward a foundation-model paradigm for shape correspondence. However, the diffusion model is trained on only ~40,000 maps from a single human dataset (D-FAUST), and the authors acknowledge limitations with highly non-isometric and partial shapes. A key open question is whether scaling training data diversity (multiple categories, more shape types) would yield a truly universal functional map prior, and whether the mask distillation strategy generalizes beyond the 30×30 spectral resolution used here.
