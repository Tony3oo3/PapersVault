## Problem

Equirectangular projection (ERP) introduces severe, latitude-dependent distortions in panoramic images, with the most extreme warping occurring at the poles. Conventional perspective-based optical flow methods degrade substantially in these polar regions, and no prior panoramic optical flow method explicitly compensates for this polar distortion, leaving a critical gap in wide-field motion estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250736/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of consecutive ERP panoramic frames (e.g., 2048×1024) as input and produces a dense 2D optical flow field in the primitive ERP coordinate system. Internally, the primitive frames are also warped into an orthogonal view via a 90° spherical rotation around the x-axis, so the network simultaneously processes two ERP projections of the same spherical scene. The final output is the primitive-view flow, with horizontal boundary continuity enforced via modulo wrapping.

## Method

PriOr-Flow is a dual-branch iterative framework built on top of existing recurrent optical flow backbones (RAFT, GMA, SKFlow). The key insight is that a 90° spherical rotation produces an orthogonal ERP view whose distortion pattern is complementary to the primitive view—minimal at the poles where the primitive view is worst. Two novel modules exploit this: (1) DCCL performs joint correlation lookup from both the primitive and orthogonal cost volumes using the current flow estimate, suppressing distortion noise in polar regions during cost retrieval; (2) ODDC computes group-wise correlation confidence maps for both branches and uses a confidence-guided motion encoder to fuse orthogonal motion features into the primitive branch's ConvGRU hidden state, explicitly compensating polar distortions. Training uses spherical-area-weighted L1 loss with exponentially increasing iteration weights, initialized from perspective-pretrained weights and fine-tuned on panoramic datasets.

## Results

On MPFDataset, PriOr-RAFT achieves an overall EPE of 2.22 and SEPE of 4.06, surpassing the previous best (SLOF) by 30.0% in EPE and 20.9% in SEPE. On FlowScape, PriOr-RAFT achieves an overall EPE of 2.33 and SEPE of 3.49, improving over PanoFlow (RAFT) by 29.6% in EPE and 21.4% in SEPE. In the critical polar regions of FlowScape, PriOr-RAFT reduces EPE from 6.25 (PanoFlow) to 4.13, a 39.7% improvement, while maintaining comparable equatorial performance (0.53 vs. 0.52 EPE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PriOr-RAFT decisively outperforms all ERP-based panoramic flow methods: it beats SLOF (RAFT) by 30.0% EPE on MPFDataset (2.22 vs. 3.17) and PanoFlow (RAFT) by 29.6% EPE on FlowScape (2.33 vs. 3.38). Against tangent-plane methods, PriOr-RAFT surpasses TanImg (RAFT) by 40.7% EPE on MPFDataset (2.22 vs. 3.76) and by 87.4% on FlowScape (2.33 vs. 18.3), highlighting the severe inter-plane discontinuity problem. Weight-transformation methods (SphereNet+RAFT) are far behind, with EPE of 10.7 on MPFDataset and 12.9 on FlowScape. In the polar regions specifically, PriOr-RAFT reduces EPE from 6.25 (PanoFlow) to 4.13 on FlowScape, a 39.7% gain, while equatorial performance is essentially matched (0.53 vs. 0.52 EPE). The method also generalizes to real-world panoramic videos (OmniPhotos, ODVista) without retraining, showing qualitatively superior flow in polar regions compared to PanoFlow.

## Relation to Prior Work

PriOr-Flow builds directly on the RAFT iterative cost-volume + ConvGRU architecture, extending it to the panoramic setting by introducing a dual-branch design where a second branch processes a 90°-rotated (orthogonal) ERP view. Within the panoramic optical flow literature, it departs from prior ERP-based methods (SLOF, MPF-Net, PanoFlow) that attempt to compensate for distortion within a single view, instead exploiting the complementary distortion pattern of the orthogonal view to explicitly correct polar errors. The DCCL and ODDC modules are novel integration mechanisms that allow the orthogonal branch's low-distortion cues to flow into the primitive branch during iterative refinement, and the authors demonstrate the framework's universality by plugging these modules into GMA and SKFlow as well.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a specific and previously unaddressed gap—severe polar-region errors in panoramic optical flow—by showing that a simple geometric rotation provides a free, low-distortion reference that can be fused into any iterative flow network. The ~30% EPE reduction over prior SOTA and the 39.7% polar-region improvement over PanoFlow are practically meaningful for applications like autonomous driving and 3D reconstruction in wide-FOV settings. Limitations include a slight equatorial-region trade-off, evaluation primarily on synthetic data with only qualitative real-world validation, and the approach being specific to ERP projection; whether the orthogonal-view strategy transfers to other projection formats (cubemap, fisheye) or to related tasks (panoramic stereo, depth) remains open.
