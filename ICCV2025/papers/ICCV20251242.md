## Problem

Unsupervised point cloud denoising methods either rely on iterative inference procedures that are computationally expensive or fail to generalize across different datasets and noise levels without retraining. Additionally, real-world point clouds often have unknown noise parameters, making it difficult to apply existing methods directly. This paper addresses both the efficiency and generalization gaps in learning-based unsupervised denoising.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251242/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes only the 3D coordinates of a noisy point cloud (10K–50K points for Gaussian noise, 3K–120K for LiDAR noise) as input, with no normals, colors, or other auxiliary features. It produces a denoised point cloud of the same cardinality in a single forward pass. When the noise standard deviation σ is unknown, it is estimated automatically by minimizing a proposed Total Variation for Point Cloud (TV_PC) metric over a range of candidate σ values, making the pipeline fully self-contained for real-world data.

## Method

Noise2Score3D decouples score estimation from denoising: a KPConv encoder-decoder (24.3M parameters, 5 encoder stages with 14 blocks, 4 decoder blocks) is trained to predict the score function S(y) = ∇_y log p(y) using only noisy data, with the AR-DAE loss that minimizes ‖σ_t·S(y′) + u‖² over perturbed points. At inference, Tweedie's formula for additive Gaussian noise yields the clean estimate in one step as x̂ = y + σ²·S(y), eliminating the iterative gradient-descent loops used by prior score-based methods. The training uses linear annealing of σ_t from 0.031 to 0.001 over 400 epochs, enabling a single trained model to handle multiple noise levels. A novel TV_PC metric (weighted sum of local geometric differences over k-nearest neighbors) is introduced to estimate unknown σ by minimizing TV_PC(x̂(σ)) over a candidate range, completing a fully unsupervised pipeline.

## Results

On ModelNet-40 with 50K points and Gaussian noise, Noise2Score3D achieves CD of 1.881, 4.393, and 8.917 (×10⁻⁴) at 1%, 2%, and 3% noise levels respectively, outperforming the best unsupervised baseline Score-U (2.696, 10.153, 26.169) by 30–66% in CD. On the PU-Net generalization test, the method achieves CD of 0.833, 1.532, and 2.434 (×10⁻⁴) at 1%, 2%, 3% noise (50K points), again leading all unsupervised methods. Inference time is 2.10 s (10K) and 5.83 s (50K), making it 15–50× faster than competing learning-based methods (e.g., TotalDn at 61.83 s / 6198.95 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Noise2Score3D outperforms all five unsupervised learning baselines (TotalDn, DMR-U, Score-U) across every noise level and point density on both ModelNet-40 and PU-Net, with the largest margins at high noise (3%): CD of 8.917 vs. 26.169 for Score-U and 22.627 for TotalDn on 50K ModelNet-40 points. Against the best classical method GLR, Noise2Score3D is competitive at low noise (50K 1%: CD 1.881 vs. 1.860) but clearly superior at 2% and 3% noise (CD 4.393 vs. 6.147; 8.917 vs. 17.462). On the PU-Net generalization test (trained only on ModelNet-40), the method achieves the best CD among all methods at 50K 1% noise (0.833 vs. 0.767 for GLR, 0.918 for Score-U), confirming cross-dataset transfer. In inference speed, Noise2Score3D (2.10 s / 5.83 s) is roughly 15× faster than Score-U (33.39 s / 308.42 s) and over 1000× faster than TotalDn (61.83 s / 6198.95 s) on 10K/50K points, owing to its single-step Tweedie-based denoising versus iterative gradient updates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Noise2Score framework (Kim & Ye, NeurIPS 2021) from 2D image denoising to 3D point clouds, transplanting the Bayesian score-estimation-plus-Tweedie's-formula pipeline into a point cloud setting. For the score estimation network, it adopts the AR-DAE loss (Lim et al., ICML 2020) and implements it on a KPConv encoder-decoder backbone. It departs from the closest prior unsupervised point cloud method, ScoreDenoise (Luo & Hu, ICCV 2021), in two key ways: replacing iterative gradient-based point updates with a single-step Tweedie formula, and grounding the score in a proper likelihood-based formulation rather than displacement-based gradients, which unlocks the use of Bayesian tools for noise parameter estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-step denoising via Tweedie's formula yields a 15–50× inference speedup over existing unsupervised methods while matching or exceeding their accuracy, making unsupervised point cloud denoising practically viable for real-time or large-scale applications. The proposed TV_PC metric closes a practical gap by enabling automatic noise-level estimation from data alone, removing a key assumption that limits real-world deployment. However, the method is currently restricted to exponential-family noise with a uniform level across the cloud, and generalization to multiplicative, spatially varying, or sensor-specific noise models remains open. Extending the score-estimation framework to color point clouds, upsampling, or completion tasks is identified as a natural next step.
