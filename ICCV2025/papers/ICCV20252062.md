## Problem

MLLMs for document understanding operate as black boxes with opaque reasoning, limiting their use in high-stakes domains like legal, financial, and medical analysis. Existing Chain-of-Thought approaches rely on static reasoning templates trained via supervised fine-tuning, which suffer from catastrophic forgetting, poor adaptability, and limited generalization across diverse document types. RL-based reasoning for document understanding remains largely unexplored, particularly in designing reward functions that jointly optimize reasoning adaptability and explainability.

## Input / Output

The model takes a document image (processed at 336² or 1536² resolution) paired with a free-form text question. Output is structured in XML-like tags: a free-form reasoning trace enclosed in <think>..., followed by a JSON object in <answer>...</answer> containing three fields — a rephrased question that clarifies the original query, a 2D bounding box (bbox_2d) localizing the document region supporting the answer, and the final predicted answer. This dual-modality output (textual rephrasing + visual grounding) is what distinguishes DocThinker's explainability from standard CoT that only produces a linear text trace.

## Method

DocThinker applies Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to a Qwen2.5-VL base model, replacing SFT with rule-based RL for inference-time reasoning. For each question, the old policy generates G=6 candidate structured responses, which are scored by a multi-objective verifiable reward combining four components: XML/JSON format compliance, exact-match final answer accuracy, RoI IoU ≥ 0.5 against ground-truth bounding boxes, and a soft rephrase-question score (cosine similarity to the original query plus a word-diversity ratio, gated on answer correctness). Rewards are normalized within the group to compute relative advantages, and the policy is updated with a KL-divergence penalty (β=0.04) against a reference model to prevent catastrophic forgetting. Notably, the framework trains on only 4k–8k samples across document and general VQA datasets, yet outperforms SFT baselines trained on 438k samples, demonstrating that RL with verifiable rewards is substantially more data-efficient for document understanding.

## Results

On the Visual CoT benchmark, DocThinker-7B (1536², 8k RL) achieves the best overall performance, scoring 0.802 on DocVQA, 0.836 on TextVQA, 0.757 on TextCaps, and 0.814 on SROIE (zero-shot). Even with only 4k training samples, DocThinker-7B (1536², 4k RL) reaches 0.795 on DocVQA and 0.827 on TextVQA, surpassing the SFT-trained Qwen2.5VL-7B (1536², 4k) which scores 0.784 and 0.801 respectively. On the TextREC visual grounding task, DocThinker-7B achieves 82.4% Precision@1, outperforming the specialist model TAMN (80.8%) and MDETR (63.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DocThinker-7B (1536², 4k RL) outperforms the same-base Qwen2.5VL-7B (1536², 4k SFT) on every document-oriented task: DocVQA 0.795 vs. 0.784, TextVQA 0.827 vs. 0.801, TextCaps 0.738 vs. 0.725, and SROIE 0.806 vs. 0.714, confirming that RL with multi-objective rewards yields consistent gains over SFT with identical data. Against VisCoT-7B (336², 438k SFT), DocThinker-7B (336², 4k RL) surpasses it on all non-zero-shot tasks (e.g., DocVQA 0.579 vs. 0.476, TextVQA 0.802 vs. 0.775) while using only ~1% of the training data. On zero-shot benchmarks, DocThinker-7B (1536², 8k) improves DUDE from 0.492 (Qwen2.5VL-7B zero-shot) to 0.568 and SROIE from 0.708 to 0.814. On the TextREC grounding task, DocThinker-7B (82.4% P@1) beats the strongest specialist baseline TAMN (80.8%) and substantially outperforms MDETR (63.3%) and TransVG (54.0%). The 8k-data variant further extends the lead over the 4k variant on general VQA and relational reasoning (GQA 0.737 vs. 0.686, VSR 0.768 vs. 0.721), indicating that broader domain coverage in training data amplifies RL's generalization benefits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO-based RL paradigm demonstrated in DeepSeek-R1 and MedVLM-R1 from text-only and medical imaging settings to multimodal document understanding, where both layout and visual content must be jointly reasoned over. It departs from static CoT/VoT/MVoT approaches (ReFocus, Visual CoT, MVoT) that rely on fixed reasoning templates by replacing them with policy-learned, adaptive inference-time reasoning guided by verifiable multi-objective rewards. The base model (Qwen2.5-VL) and the Visual CoT benchmark are shared with prior SFT-based document MLLMs, making the comparison between RL and SFT training strategies the central methodological contrast.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that rule-based RL with only 4k training samples can match or exceed SFT models trained on 438k samples for document understanding, suggesting a significant data-efficiency advantage for RL in this domain. The multi-objective reward design (format, accuracy, RoI IoU, rephrased question) provides a practical template for injecting explainability into MLLM training without human preference labels. However, the evaluation is limited to a single model family (Qwen2.5-VL 3B/7B) and primarily the Visual CoT benchmark; whether the approach scales to larger models, more diverse document types (legal, medical), or benefits from the RL signal beyond the specific reward formulation remains open. The 'explainability' is largely structural (XML tags, bounding boxes) rather than a fundamentally new reasoning paradigm, which may limit its impact in truly high-stakes verification settings.
