## Problem

Video diffusion models (VDMs) enable high-quality novel view synthesis from sparse inputs but require many iterative denoising steps, making them too slow for real-time or large-scale 3D reconstruction. Existing diffusion distillation methods (DMD, DMD2, CausVid) suffer from training instability and mode collapse when applied to the relatively small multi-view video datasets needed for view synthesis, limiting their practical utility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250028/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of sparse input views (e.g., 5 views) with known camera poses and a target camera trajectory as input. DUSt3R constructs a point cloud from the first and last input frames, which is then rendered at intermediate camera poses to serve as 3D guidance alongside CLIP embeddings. The output is a sequence of L novel-view images (up to 16 frames at 512×320 resolution) generated in as few as 4 denoising steps in the VAE latent space, compared to the teacher's multi-step sampling.

## Method

FVGen distills a multi-step teacher VDM (ViewCrafter) into a 4-step student via a two-stage procedure. First, the student (initialized with teacher weights) is trained under a GAN objective where the frozen teacher model itself serves as the discriminator via a 3D CNN classifier on its UNet middle-block features, and real dataset images (not teacher-generated samples) define the real distribution—this avoids capping the student at teacher quality. Second, the student is further refined by minimizing a Soften Reverse KL-divergence (Soften RKL), which computes KL(0.5·p_real + 0.5·p_fake ‖ p_real) instead of standard reverse KL, preserving mode-seeking behavior while penalizing the student for ignoring non-dominant modes of the teacher distribution. The GAN-trained discriminator also provides a direct density-ratio estimate r(x,t) ≈ f(D(x,t))/(1−f(D(x,t))) used in the Soften RKL gradient. A fake score network is dynamically updated with standard diffusion loss to track the evolving student distribution, and the full pipeline is trained on 20,000 multi-view video pairs from DL3DV-10K in approximately one day on 8 H100 GPUs.

## Results

On MipNeRF360, FVGen achieves PSNR 16.28, SSIM 0.352, LPIPS 0.429, and FID 17.44 in 5.1 s, matching ViewCrafter's quality (PSNR 16.35, FID 16.28) while reducing inference time from 66.3 s to 5.1 s (~92% speedup). On Tanks-and-Temples, FVGen slightly surpasses ViewCrafter on PSNR (18.72 vs 18.69), SSIM (0.411 vs 0.402), and FID (23.64 vs 23.94) in 5.0 s versus 65.9 s. In the distillation comparison (Table 2), FVGen outperforms both VC+DMD2 and VC+CausVid on all four metrics across both datasets, e.g., PSNR 18.72 vs 10.27 (DMD2) and 17.33 (CausVid) on TNT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ViewCrafter (the multi-step teacher), FVGen matches or marginally exceeds it on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS on both datasets while cutting inference time by >90% (5.0–5.1 s vs. 65.9–66.3 s); FID is slightly worse on MipNeRF360 (17.44 vs. 16.28) but slightly better on TNT (23.64 vs. 23.94). Against DepthSplat, FVGen wins decisively on every quality metric (e.g., PSNR 16.28 vs. 11.23 on MipNeRF360) at roughly 1.2× the inference time. Against MVSplat360, FVGen is superior on all quality metrics and ~17× faster (5.1 s vs. 87.2 s). In the distillation-specific comparison (Table 2), FVGen outperforms VC+DMD2 by a large margin (PSNR 16.28 vs. 9.29 on MipNeRF360; 18.72 vs. 10.27 on TNT), as DMD2 training collapses. FVGen also beats VC+CausVid on all four metrics (e.g., PSNR 16.28 vs. 15.77, FID 17.44 vs. 19.27 on MipNeRF360), attributed to the GAN-based initialization and soften reverse KL-divergence replacing CausVid's ODE-pair regression initialization and standard DMD loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

FVGen builds directly on ViewCrafter's video-diffusion-based novel view synthesis pipeline and the Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) framework for accelerating diffusion models. It departs from CausVid's ODE-regression initialization by instead using a GAN objective with the frozen teacher as discriminator, and from DMD/DMD2's pure reverse KL-divergence by proposing a 'soften reverse KL' that mixes the student and teacher distributions before computing divergence. The separation of GAN initialization and DMD refinement into distinct training phases, along with the use of a 3D convolutional discriminator for video data, distinguishes it from prior image-domain distillation methods like SDXL-Lightning and LADD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× speedup (from ~13 s to ~1 s per 16-frame sequence) makes VDM-based novel view synthesis practical for dynamic 3D reconstruction and large-scale scene generation where many views must be synthesized repeatedly. However, the method inherits ViewCrafter's limitations on extremely sparse inputs and is constrained to 16-frame sequences due to the computational cost of running three video diffusion models simultaneously. Open questions include whether the soften RKL formulation generalizes to longer video sequences or other VDM architectures, and whether the GAN-based initialization strategy transfers to image diffusion models trained on similarly small, domain-specific datasets.
