## Problem

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models enable end-to-end robotic control but have not been systematically evaluated for adversarial vulnerabilities. Conventional pixel-level attacks fail to produce meaningful disruptions in robotic systems because they ignore the physical dynamics, 7-DoF action discretization, and temporal dependencies inherent to robotic execution. This paper is the first to define attack objectives tailored to VLA-based robotic systems and demonstrate that small adversarial patches can cause up to 100% task failure.

## Input / Output

The attack pipeline takes an RGB image from a fixed shoulder-view camera and a text instruction as input to the victim VLA model (OpenVLA-7B). The optimization produces a small, colorful adversarial patch (3–10% of image area) that is composited onto the input image; when the patched image is fed to the VLA model, it outputs a 7-DoF action vector [ΔP_x, ΔP_y, ΔP_z, ΔR_x, ΔR_y, ΔR_z, gripper] where each continuous DoF is discretized into 256 uniform bins. The patch is designed to be physically printable and robust to viewpoint changes via geometric augmentation during training.

## Method

The paper introduces three attack objectives: (1) UADA maximizes action discrepancy by computing a soft action via Hadamard product of model output probabilities with normalized bin labels, then minimizing squared distance to the farthest action bound; (2) UPA disrupts 3D positional trajectory by combining cosine similarity (direction) and inverse L2 distance (magnitude) between adversarial and ground-truth position vectors; (3) TMA steers the model toward a specific target action using cross-entropy loss. All three are optimized via a patch-based framework (Algorithm 1) that uses k inner-loop gradient steps per outer iteration and random affine/rotation transformations (shear + rotation) to ensure physical-world robustness. The authors also propose Normalized Action Discrepancy (NAD) as a fine-grained metric measuring the ratio of applied to maximum possible action deviation per DoF, complementing the coarse Failure Rate metric.

## Results

On the LIBERO simulation benchmark, UADA targeting DoF1~3 achieves a 100% average Failure Rate (FR) across all four task suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), compared to 23.5% for benign and 78.8% for the UMA baseline. UADA also attains the highest Normalized Action Discrepancy (NAD) of 32.6% (DoF1) and 27.7% (DoF1~3), outperforming UMA by 5.9 and 3.9 percentage points respectively. In the physical-world transfer setting (patches generated on BridgeData V2, evaluated on LIBERO), UADA DoF1~3 still achieves 100% FR on the in-domain Long suite. Real-world evaluation on a UR10e robot yields a 43% attack success rate across 100 trials on three manipulation tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its proposed attacks (UADA, UPA, TMA) against two baselines: UMA (adapted from Tramber et al. [66], which maximizes negative cross-entropy to push predictions away from ground truth) and Random Noise (Gaussian patches with no learned intent). UADA dominates UMA on both NAD and FR: for DoF1, UADA achieves 32.6% NAD vs. 26.7% for UMA (+5.9 pp) and 96.8% avg FR vs. 73.8% (+23 pp); for DoF1~3, UADA reaches 100% FR vs. UMA's 78.8% (+21.2 pp). UPA achieves a higher NAD than UMA (26.9% vs. 23.8%) but a lower average FR (70.7% vs. 78.8%), suggesting its geometric-direction objective produces larger per-step deviations but less consistent task-level failure. Both proposed methods substantially outperform Random Noise (28.3% avg FR), confirming that structured adversarial optimization is essential. For TMA, targeting all DoFs to zero yields 100% avg FR in simulation and 88.6% in the physical transfer setting, far exceeding the 23.5% benign baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial patch attack paradigm (Athalye et al., ICML 2018) from image classification to the action-discretized output space of Vision-Language-Action models, introducing three robot-specific objectives (UADA, UPA, TMA) that exploit the 7-DoF action structure and 3D geometric constraints of robotic arms. It departs from prior adversarial attacks on robotic systems, which typically target perception modules or use generic misclassification losses, by formulating objectives directly in the action space where bin-level discrepancies map to physical motion. The UMA baseline is adapted from Tramber et al. (NeurIPS 2020), but the paper's core contribution is the geometric and action-magnitude-aware loss design rather than the patch generation mechanism itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100% failure rate in simulation and 43% in physical-world trials on OpenVLA expose a critical safety gap before VLA models are deployed in shared human-robot environments, where induced erratic movements pose direct physical hazards. The observation that generated patches structurally resemble robotic arm joints suggests a learned representation bias from narrow training robot diversity, raising open questions about whether defenses must address multi-robot training or physical-constraint-based anomaly detection. Limitations include evaluation on a single model family (OpenVLA) and a single robot platform (UR10e), leaving generalizability to other VLA architectures and robot morphologies untested.
