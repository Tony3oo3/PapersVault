## Problem

Assembling a coherent multi-shot video from candidate shots is challenging because existing text-to-video retrieval methods select shots independently, often producing semantically inconsistent sequences (e.g., a snow scene amid summer footage). No dedicated metric quantifies multi-shot narrative coherence, and the k^n combinatorial search space makes exhaustive assembly infeasible for practical shot counts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252083/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes n shot positions, each with k candidate shots (typically 5, retrieved via CLIP-based queries from a stock footage pool), and optionally per-shot text captions. Each shot is consumed as 5 uniformly sampled frames encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32, with a maximum sequence length of 50 shots. The output is an assembled n-shot sequence selected via beam search to maximize the LCA coherence score, a real-valued metric in the range calibrated to 1–5 human ratings.

## Method

SKALD introduces the Learned Clip Assembly (LCA) score, computed by a 4-layer transformer encoder (8 heads, 512 hidden dim) that ingests shot-level CLIP embeddings with positional encodings and a learnable [CLS] token, producing a 768-d sequence representation. Training is two-stage: first, contrastive learning with NCE loss distinguishes coherent sequences (light frame-level augmentations) from incoherent ones (40% of shots replaced with visually disjoint content), requiring no shot-level labels; second, the frozen encoder feeds a linear regression head trained on ~500 human-rated samples with MSE loss. At inference, a beam search of size m (default 20) guided by the LCA score navigates the combinatorial space in O(k³ + (n−3)mk) time, with a sequence-length decorrelation penalty ensuring score invariance across sequence lengths. The SKALD-text variant adds a CLIP text-image similarity term weighted by γ=0.5 to incorporate optional captions.

## Results

On the MSV3C test set, SKALD-text achieves the best IoU of 0.328 and R@1 of 0.494, outperforming the strongest text-retrieval baseline CLIP4Clip (IoU 0.269, R@1 0.424) by 22% and 16% respectively. On VSPD, SKALD-text reaches IoU 0.214 and SMS 0.167, surpassing the prior best text-guided method RATV (IoU 0.144, SMS 0.090) while reducing inference time by 43% (4.67s vs. 6.67s per video). The visual-only SKALD variant also beats RATV on VSPD (IoU 0.158 vs. 0.144) at 43% lower inference cost, confirming that the LCA score captures coherence beyond what text matching provides.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SKALD-text decisively outperforms all five baselines on both datasets. On MSV3C, it beats CLIP4Clip (the strongest zero-shot retrieval baseline) by 0.059 IoU and 0.070 R@1, and Frozen-in-Time by 0.107 IoU. On VSPD, SKALD-text surpasses RATV (the best text-guided assembly method) by 0.070 IoU and 0.077 SMS while running 30% faster (4.67s vs. 6.67s). The visual-only SKALD variant wins on VSPD against both RATV and Transcript-to-Video in IoU and SMS, but underperforms the text-based zero-shot methods on MSV3C (IoU 0.117 vs. 0.269 for CLIP4Clip), highlighting that the LCA score alone is weaker than strong text-video alignment when high-quality captions are available. The 43% inference speedup over RATV on VSPD stems from the beam-search strategy replacing sequential text-matching. In the user study, 45% of participants preferred SKALD-assembled videos over CLIP4Clip (22%), with 33% rating them as comparable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant text-guided shot assembly paradigm (RATV, Transcript-to-Video, Write-a-Video), which encodes text and video into a shared space and retrieves shots by caption matching, by instead learning a purely visual coherence metric (the LCA score) that scores multi-shot sequences directly. It also distinguishes itself from no-reference video quality assessment (VQA) methods, which evaluate individual shot aesthetics or technical quality, by explicitly modeling inter-shot temporal and semantic continuity. The architecture repurposes CLIP ViT-B/32 frame embeddings and a lightweight transformer encoder, but the key conceptual shift is treating assembly as a sequence-level ranking problem solvable via beam search rather than a per-shot retrieval problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SKALD addresses a practical bottleneck in short-form video creation by reducing dependence on expensive shot-level text annotations, making automated assembly more accessible to non-expert users. The two-stage training (contrastive pretraining + light regression) demonstrates that a useful coherence metric can be learned from only ~500 human-rated samples, which is a meaningful reduction in annotation cost. However, the visual-only SKALD variant underperforms text-based retrieval (CLIP4Clip) on MSV3C when high-quality captions are available, suggesting the LCA score complements rather than replaces text cues. Open questions include scalability to longer-form content, incorporation of audio, and generalization beyond the relatively narrow MSV3C and VSPD evaluation domains.
