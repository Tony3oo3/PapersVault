## Problem

Model merging allows free-riders to cheaply inherit a defender's fine-tuned capabilities by linearly combining parameters, yet existing protections (watermarking, fingerprinting) are purely passive and can only detect infringement after the fact. No proactive defense exists that renders a model unmergeable while preserving its standalone performance, leaving a critical gap in IP protection for open-sourced fine-tuned models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the fine-tuned model's MLP weight matrices (W1, W2, b1) and attention head parameters (Q, K, V, W_O) alongside the corresponding pretrained checkpoint parameters. It outputs a parameter-modified model that is a drop-in replacement: functionally identical for standalone inference but positioned far from the shared parameter basin. The permutation matrices are optimized via linear assignment to maximize L2 distance from pretrained MLP parameters, while attention scaling factors are drawn from a uniform distribution U(s_min, s_max) per head.

## Method

PaRaMS exploits the observation that model merging works because fine-tuned models from the same pretrained checkpoint converge to a shared basin in parameter space, making linear interpolation effective. The defense pushes the model out of this basin using two functional-equivalence transformations: (1) MLP Parameter Rearrangement applies a permutation matrix P such that W1'=PW1, b1'=Pb1, W2'=W2P^T, preserving the MLP output exactly while reordering hidden units; the permutation is optimized to maximize distance from pretrained parameters via a linear assignment problem. (2) Random Multi-head Scaling applies per-head diagonal matrices A and B so that Q'=QA, K'=KA^{-1}, V'=VB, W_O'=B^{-1}W_O, preserving the attention output while altering parameter magnitudes. Together, these transformations guarantee exact functional equivalence (Eq. 1) while ensuring that any linear combination with another fine-tuned model produces a merged model with severely degraded performance (Eq. 2), as the interpolated parameters no longer lie in a low-loss region.

## Results

On image classification with ViT-B-32 and Task Arithmetic (λ=0.8), PaRaMS reduces merged-model accuracy from 67–99% (MMP−) to 0.5–13% (MMP+) across all 21 dataset pairs, with the largest gap of 65.16 percentage points (GTSRB→DTD: 67.82%→2.66%). Unmerged model accuracy is preserved exactly (UMP− = UMP+). On text classification with Llama2, MMP+(TA) drops from 97.6% to 21.4% on Emotion and from 95.3% to 35.8% on Twitter, while UMP−/UMP+ remain at 99.8%/99.7%. For text-to-image generation with SD1.5, CLIP alignment scores for MMP+ fall to 0.13 and 0.08 versus 0.34 and 0.34 for MMP−.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PaRaMS is evaluated against six merging methods: Task Arithmetic (TA), TIES-Merging, Weight Average (WA), AdaMerging (ADA), DARE (as a plug-in combined with each of the above), and PEM-TA for LoRA-based merging. Across all eight merging configurations (four base methods ± DARE) and three ViT architectures (B-16, B-32, L-14), PaRaMS consistently degrades merged-model accuracy to near-random levels while leaving unmerged accuracy unchanged (Figure 3). For PEM-TA on Llama2 (Table 3), the degradation is smaller (96.1%→52.7% on Emotion) because only the LoRA scaling module applies, but the drop is still substantial. The paper does not compare against other IP-protection or watermarking defenses; instead, it positions PaRaMS as the first proactive (pre-merging) defense, contrasting with passive post-merging detection methods [6,30]. Against adaptive free-riders who attempt to reverse the permutation/scaling, PaRaMS alone is bypassed (80.32% recovery), but adding dropout-based pruning limits recovery to 64.35%, and the ultimate knowledge-distillation attack is deemed impractical due to its cost exceeding that of simply retraining from scratch.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the permutation symmetry exploited by Git Re-Basin [2] for model merging as a defensive tool, inverting its purpose: instead of aligning models to merge them, it maximizes parameter distance to prevent merging. It departs from prior model-security work on watermarking and fingerprinting [6, 30], which are inherently passive (post-merging detection), by introducing the first proactive defense that makes merging fail at the parameter level. The theoretical grounding draws on the shared-basin explanation for why merging works [35], and the method is validated against the full landscape of parameter-level merging techniques (Task Arithmetic, TIES, WA, AdaMerging, DARE).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As model merging becomes a standard practice (nearly 30,000 merged models on Hugging Face), the IP-infringement risk from free-riders is increasingly real, yet prior defenses only detect misuse after the fact. PaRaMS offers a practical, training-free, architecture-agnostic protection that preserves full model functionality while rendering merging ineffective, which is directly deployable by any model owner. Key limitations include the adaptive free-rider who can recover ~80% performance by solving an optimization problem (mitigated partially by dropout-based pruning), and the fact that the defense only covers parameter-level merging, not knowledge distillation or other transfer mechanisms. Open questions include owner-controlled selective merging and whether the approach extends to non-Transformer architectures or cross-checkpoint merging scenarios.
