## Problem

In Mamba-based state space models, the forget-gate (A matrix) that controls temporal memory propagation remains static and input-independent, even though other key parameters (input gate, output gate, time-step) are already input-dependent. This limitation is particularly detrimental for long-term dense action anticipation, where the relevance of past information varies dramatically across semantic contexts and where padded zero sequences representing unknown future frames must be selectively ignored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251221/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes pre-extracted per-frame video features (I3D for Breakfast and 50Salads, TSM for Assembly101) from the first P observed frames, which are concatenated with zero-padded future frame slots and the current diffusion latent along the channel dimension. It outputs S=25 stochastic per-frame action label sequences spanning both observed and future frames, generated via DDIM sampling from a diffusion process, with evaluation using Mean MoC (average over all samples) and Top-1 MoC (best-matching sample) accuracy.

## Method

MixANT introduces the S6+ algorithm, which replaces the single fixed A matrix in each Mamba block with a set of E=5 expert A matrices and a softmax gating vector computed from the projected mean of observed-frame tokens, selecting one expert per forward pass via argmax. The full architecture is a hybrid of K=15 blocks: the first K0=3 are standard bidirectional Mamba blocks for foundational feature extraction, while the remaining 12 are MixMamba blocks with the mixture mechanism. Training combines the standard diffusion L2 reconstruction loss with a novel load-balancing KL divergence loss that penalizes non-uniform expert utilization across the batch, ensuring all experts receive adequate gradient signal. The gating is conditioned exclusively on observed-frame features (not the zero-padded future region), and a unified router shares the same expert selection between forward and backward SSM paths to preserve bidirectional consistency.

## Results

MixANT achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three datasets and both metrics (Mean MoC and Top-1 MoC). On Breakfast, it reaches 36.2% Mean MoC and 60.7% Top-1 MoC at α=0.3, β=0.1, surpassing MANTA by 2.0 and 1.1 points respectively. On the more challenging Assembly101 (202 action classes), MixANT achieves 8.7% Mean MoC and 20.3% Top-1 MoC at α=0.3/0.2, β=0.1, showing relative gains of up to 32% over MANTA at longer horizons. On 50Salads, it attains 33.4% Mean MoC and 72.9% Top-1 MoC at α=0.3, β=0.1, consistently leading across all observation and anticipation ratios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MixANT outperforms all four baselines across every dataset and evaluation setting. Against the closest competitor MANTA [36] (bidirectional Mamba + diffusion), MixANT gains 1.9–2.1 points on Mean MoC and 1.1–1.7 points on Top-1 MoC on Breakfast, 1.3–2.1 points on Mean MoC and 1.9–3.4 points on Top-1 MoC on Assembly101, and 1.7–2.1 points on Mean MoC and 1.2–3.7 points on Top-1 MoC on 50Salads. Against GTDA [35] (gated temporal diffusion), the margins are larger: up to 5.6 points on Breakfast Top-1 MoC and 2.3 points on Assembly101 Top-1 MoC. DiffAnt [43] (diffusion + FUTR encoder) is only evaluated on Breakfast, where MixANT surpasses it by 4.9 points on Mean MoC and 23.3 points on Top-1 MoC at α=0.3, β=0.1. The earliest baselines, Tri-gram and UAAA [8], are substantially outperformed (by 10–20+ points), confirming that the mixture-of-experts A-matrix mechanism provides meaningful gains over both RNN-based and prior diffusion-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MANTA [36], which applied bidirectional Mamba blocks within a diffusion framework for stochastic long-term dense action anticipation, by making the previously static forget-gate (A matrix) input-dependent through a mixture-of-experts mechanism (S6+). It departs from prior MoE-in-Mamba work (BlackMamba [3], MoE-Mamba [27], Mixture-of-Mamba [20]) that applied expert routing to MLPs or modality selection outside the SSM core, instead targeting the state-transition matrix itself. The diffusion training pipeline and evaluation protocol follow GTDA [35] and MANTA [36], while the bidirectional SSM structure follows VideoMamba [19].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges Mamba's original design hypothesis that input-dependence on Δ alone suffices, showing that for very long sequences with mixed observed/padded inputs, the A matrix must also adapt to context. The emergent finding that expert selection patterns cluster by high-level activity category (despite training only on atomic actions) suggests the mixture mechanism learns semantically meaningful temporal memory strategies. Open questions include whether the optimal expert count (5) scales with dataset size, whether the hybrid static-then-mixture block design generalizes to other SSM applications, and whether the load-balancing loss is necessary at larger training budgets.
