## Problem

Existing GAN-based unpaired dehazing methods are limited by the generator's inability to model complex transport mappings, producing artifacts on images with intricate structures. Diffusion models offer richer transport but require many sampling steps, making them computationally expensive. The paper asks whether the Schrödinger Bridge can provide an efficient, few-step optimal transport mechanism directly between hazy and clear image distributions in an unpaired setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252210/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single hazy RGB image as input and produces a dehazed RGB image of the same resolution. At inference, the number of function evaluations (NFEs) is adjustable, allowing a trade-off between quality and speed (the framework uses N=5 time intervals by default). Training requires only unpaired collections: over 7,000 real hazy images from RESIDE and over 10,000 clear images from SOTS and ADE20K, with no pixel-level correspondence needed between the two sets.

## Method

DehazeSB discretizes the unit interval [0,1] into N=5 steps and learns a single time-conditioned generator G: q_θ(x₁|x(tᵢ)) that directly predicts the target clear image from any intermediate state, leveraging the Static Schrödinger Bridge's self-similarity property for recursive interpolation via conditional Gaussians. Distribution alignment is enforced by a dual-discriminator adversarial loss combining a Markovian patch-level discriminator (for local texture fidelity) and a CLIP-based global discriminator (for scene coherence). A detail-preserving regularization suite—PatchNCE contrastive loss, a physical-prior term that reconstructs the hazy image via the Atmospheric Scattering Model using a UNet-refined transmission map, and high-frequency losses (DFT, SSIM, Sobel)—enforces pixel-level structural consistency without requiring paired data. Additionally, a learned haze-aware text prompt is optimized via binary cross-entropy against a frozen CLIP model, then used during SB training to push generated dehazed images away from the haze representation in vision-language space.

## Results

On RTTS, DehazeSB achieves the best no-reference scores across all four metrics: FID 53.12, NIQE 3.76, MUSIQ 60.11, and MANIQA 0.153, outperforming the strongest unpaired baseline UNSB (FID 56.67, MUSIQ 55.05) and all paired methods. On Haze2020, it similarly leads with FID 69.80, MUSIQ 59.26, and MANIQA 0.150, while matching KANet on NIQE (3.74 vs. 3.74). On OHAZE (full-reference), DehazeSB attains PSNR 18.83 dB, SSIM 0.838, LPIPS 0.223, and VSI 0.961, surpassing the best unpaired method RefineDNet (PSNR 18.69, SSIM 0.755) and the prior-based DCP (PSNR 17.01).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DehazeSB outperforms all 14 baselines on FID, MUSIQ, and MANIQA across both RTTS and Haze2020, with the largest margins over paired methods (e.g., FID 53.12 vs. 64.96 for KANet on RTTS) and over the closest unpaired competitor UNSB (FID 53.12 vs. 56.67, MUSIQ 60.11 vs. 55.05 on RTTS). On NIQE (Haze2020), DehazeSB (3.743) is essentially tied with KANet (3.742), a trade-off the authors attribute to their physical prior regularization. On OHAZE with full-reference metrics, DehazeSB leads all methods: PSNR 18.83 dB vs. 18.69 (RefineDNet), SSIM 0.838 vs. 0.819 (DCP), LPIPS 0.223 vs. 0.242 (DCP), and VSI 0.961 vs. 0.954 (RefineDNet). Among unpaired GAN-based methods (CUT, YOLY, RefineDNet, D4, D4+), DehazeSB consistently achieves the best or near-best scores, confirming that the Schrödinger Bridge transport formulation overcomes the limited mapping capability of GAN generators. The paired diffusion-based Diff-Plugin and OneRestore, despite their generative flexibility, still trail DehazeSB by 11–15 FID points on RTTS, underscoring the advantage of direct optimal-transport bridging over iterative denoising.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Neural Schrödinger Bridge (UNSB, Kim et al. ICLR 2024) from general unpaired image translation to the specific domain of real-world image dehazing, leveraging the Static Schrödinger Bridge formulation for entropy-regularized optimal transport between hazy and clear image distributions. It departs from prior unpaired dehazing methods (CycleDehaze, D4/D4+, RefineDNet, YOLY) that rely on GAN generators with limited transport mapping capability, and from diffusion-based approaches that require many sampling steps, by using SB to achieve optimal transport in just a few steps. The PatchNCE regularization is borrowed from CUT (Park et al. ECCV 2020), and the CLIP-based discriminator follows the ensembling strategy of Kumari et al. (CVPR 2022), but the haze-aware prompt learning and the combination of physical prior + high-frequency detail regularization as a lightweight alternative to CycleGAN are novel contributions specific to the dehazing setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in real-world dehazing: GAN-based unpaired methods produce artifacts on complex structures, while diffusion-based methods are computationally expensive. The Schrödinger Bridge formulation offers a principled middle ground with fewer function evaluations, and the detail-preserving regularization (PatchNCE + DCP + DFT/SSIM/Sobel) provides a cheaper structural-consistency constraint than CycleGAN. However, the method's dependence on a learned haze-aware prompt makes it sensitive to training data diversity, and the authors acknowledge struggles with dense haze and fine-grained details. Open questions include whether the SB framework generalizes to other atmospheric degradations (rain, fog) and whether the prompt learning component can be replaced by more robust scene-conditioned signals.
