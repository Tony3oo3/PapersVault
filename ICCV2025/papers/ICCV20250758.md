## Problem

Client-side neural architecture search (NAS) for personalized federated learning (PFL) remains underexplored and suffers from two critical limitations: inefficient local search caused by data isolation and restricted cross-client architecture access, and slow supernet convergence due to naive federated averaging of undertrained local supernets. This paper targets cross-company PFL scenarios where privacy is paramount, as opposed to server-side NAS which exposes client architectures and performance metrics to the server.

## Input / Output

Each client receives supernet weights from the server and local non-i.i.d. image data (600 training + 600 test samples per client, partitioned via Dirichlet distribution with α=0.1 or 0.3). The conditional diffusion model is pre-trained once per search space using 500K sampled architectures and a meta-predictor trained on ImageNet-1K data features (extracted via ResNet-18 + Set Transformer) to avoid dataset leakage. The method outputs a personalized subnet architecture per client (from DARTS, MobileNetV3, or NASViT search spaces) and updated supernet weights uploaded to the server each round.

## Method

PerFedSDE-NAS employs a predictor-guided SDE-based diffusion model (using CATE as the score network) to generate 100 candidate architectures per iteration, conditioned on client-specific data features, with a Radial Basis Function surrogate selecting only the top-10 for actual evaluation—yielding 7,500 evaluated architectures per client over 1,500 rounds, 100× more than prior methods. For local supernet training, an archive-driven mechanism maintains the top-k performing architectures and samples training instances from this archive rather than the full population, mitigating multi-task forgetting. A novel supernet aggregation formula (Eq. 5) reweights FedAvg by the fraction of clients that actually updated each weight position, reducing the dilution effect of unmodified supernet parameters and accelerating global convergence. The diffusion model and predictor are trained once per search space and reused across all FL runs, making the fixed pre-training cost negligible relative to repeated FL experiments.

## Results

PerFedSDE-NAS achieves the highest average test accuracy across all three search spaces. On MobileNetV3, it reaches 82.62±5.21% on CIFAR-10 (α=0.3) and 64.15±4.91% on CIFAR-100 (α=0.1); on NASViT (transformer), it reaches 85.79±4.53% and 65.94±4.42% respectively. The method also outperforms the server-side NAS baseline PerFedRLNAS (82.02% / 63.85% on MobileNetV3) while operating under stricter privacy constraints (only supernet weights are uploaded). Ablation shows removing all three components (diffusion, archive, Eq.5 aggregation) drops accuracy by 4.58% on CIFAR-10 and 8.20% on CIFAR-100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PerFedSDE-NAS outperforms all fixed-model PFL baselines by a wide margin: FedAvg (74.44%), FedBABU (75.00%), and FedRep (74.75%) on CIFAR-10/MobileNetV3, with gaps of 7.6–8.0 percentage points. Against client-side NAS baselines, it beats the evolutionary-operator baseline by 2.03% (CIFAR-10) and 1.86% (CIFAR-100), and substantially outperforms FedNAS (79.88%) and SPIDER (71.14%) on DARTS/CIFAR-10. On the transformer search space (NASViT), it surpasses FedTP (80.27%) by 5.52% on CIFAR-10. Notably, PerFedSDE-NAS also edges out the server-side NAS method PerFedRLNAS (82.02% → 82.62% on CIFAR-10/MobileNetV3; 85.02% → 85.79% on CIFAR-10/NASViT), though the authors caution this comparison is inherently unfair since server-side NAS has access to client architectures and test accuracies. The method maintains competitive runtime (~17.6 h on MobileNetV3) comparable to the evolutionary baseline, confirming that the diffusion-based generation does not add significant overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of NAS-based personalized federated learning (PFL) by shifting the architecture search from the server to the client side, building on prior client-side methods like FedNAS and SPIDER while addressing their limited search capacity. It adapts the predictor-guided SDE-based diffusion framework from DiffusionNAG (centralized NAS) to the federated setting, where data heterogeneity and local supernet training introduce unique challenges absent in the original formulation. The novel supernet aggregation rule (Eq. 5) departs from standard FedAvg by reweighting updates based on which weight positions were actually modified during local NAS, a problem specific to parameter-sharing supernets that prior FL aggregation methods do not handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe for privacy-preserving client-side NAS in cross-company FL scenarios, where server-side methods are infeasible due to information leakage. The 100× increase in evaluated architectures (3.75M vs. ~7,500 in prior methods) and the archive-driven supernet training offer concrete mechanisms to close the performance gap between client-side and server-side NAS. However, experiments are limited to CIFAR-10/100 with 100 clients, and the comparison with server-side baselines is acknowledged as inherently unfair; open questions include scalability to ImageNet-scale data, larger client populations, and whether the diffusion-based generation advantage persists under tighter compute budgets.
