## Problem

Existing INR-based video codecs are outperformed by both conventional codecs (e.g., VVC VTM) and autoencoder-based neural codecs (e.g., DCVC-FM) under standard Random Access coding configurations, primarily because their encoding latency limits the number of consecutive frames that can be jointly modeled with a single set of learnable parameters. This paper tackles the gap by architecturally scaling the INR framework's capacity for long-range spatiotemporal dependency modeling to full-GOP level.

## Input / Output

The encoder takes a GOP of T=32 consecutive YUV 4:2:0 frames at 1920×1080 resolution and stochastically overfits a set of hierarchical latent grids (with 3D token size 4×8×8) to the sequence, producing an entropy-coded bitstream via a context model. The decoder reconstructs frames through a per-token denoising diffusion process that unrolls an INR-based denoiser across K representation levels, conditioned on HGLA-generated tokens and positional embeddings, yielding the final pixel-space reconstruction.

## Method

GIViC integrates a conditional implicit diffusion model with a novel Hierarchical Gated Linear Attention (HGLA) transformer within an INR-based compression framework. The implicit diffusion reformulates standard diffusion into a spatiotemporal pyramidal process where per-token denoising at constant token size progresses from coarse full-sequence scales to fine local details, trained with a consistency objective that enforces path-independence across resolution scales. HGLA maintains fixed-size 2D hidden states per representation level, updated recurrently along both scale and sequence axes with data-dependent gating, achieving linear complexity in context length while capturing full-GOP dependencies. The spatiotemporal encoder is inflated from a pretrained image autoencoder, and the overall system is optimized end-to-end with a GOP-level rate-distortion Lagrangian, using a multi-stage pretraining procedure followed by sequence-specific overfitting at encoding time.

## Results

Under the Random Access (RA) configuration (YUV 4:2:0, GOPSize=32), GIViC achieves BD-rate savings of 15.94% (PSNR) and 16.19% (MS-SSIM) over VTM 20.0, 22.46% and 27.23% over DCVC-FM, and 8.52% and 4.92% over NVRC on the UVG test set. On JVET-B, the corresponding PSNR BD-rate savings are 7.71%, 22.34%, and 16.13% over VTM, DCVC-FM, and NVRC respectively. GIViC also outperforms the autoencoder-based DCVC-DC by 36.68% BD-rate (PSNR) on UVG and the INR-based PNVC by 34.87%. Notably, GIViC remains competitive even without sequence-specific overfitting (3.37% BD-rate on UVG PSNR), while the full overfitting pipeline achieves the best results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GIViC outperforms all seven baselines across all three test sets (UVG, MCL-JCV, JVET-B) in both PSNR and MS-SSIM BD-rate. Against the strongest conventional codec VTM 20.0 (RA), GIViC saves 15.94% bitrate on UVG and 7.71% on JVET-B (PSNR), making it the first INR-based codec to surpass VTM under RA constraints. Among neural codecs, GIViC beats DCVC-FM by 22.46% (UVG) and 22.34% (JVET-B) in PSNR BD-rate, and the autoencoder-based DCVC-DC by 36.68% on UVG. Among INR-based methods, GIViC outperforms NVRC by 8.52% on UVG and 16.13% on JVET-B (PSNR), and PNVC by 34.87% on UVG. The trade-off is computational: GIViC's 225.9M parameters and 2399 kMACs/px are substantially higher than PNVC (21.8M, 102 kMACs/px) or NVRC (16.8M, 582 kMACs/px), and its encoding speed (0.03 FPS) is slower than DCVC-FM (0.93 FPS) and NVRC (4.4 FPS), though decoding at 9.79 FPS is competitive with conventional codecs.

## Relation to Prior Work

GIViC extends the INR-based video compression line (NeRV, HNeRV, PNVC, NVRC, HiNeRV) by jointly integrating a conditional diffusion model and a linear-attention transformer backbone into the implicit representation framework — a combination absent from prior INR codecs. The implicit diffusion process generalizes multi-resolution diffusion ideas (Relay Diffusion, Cold Diffusion, cascaded diffusion) into a unified spatiotemporal pyramidal chain with a novel consistency objective, while HGLA adapts gated linear attention (HGRN2, Mamba, RWKV) to dual-factorize dependency modeling along both scale and sequence axes. The spatiotemporal encoder is inflated from a pretrained image autoencoder, and the Quincunx token ordering is borrowed from product-quantized masked image modeling, but the overall architecture and training pipeline are new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GIViC marks the first INR-based video codec to surpass VTM under the Random Access configuration, breaking a long-standing performance ceiling that had kept INR methods behind both conventional and generic neural codecs at practical latencies. The HGLA architecture and implicit diffusion formulation are potentially reusable components for other long-context generative or compression tasks. However, the very high encoding latency (0.03 FPS) and 226M parameter count severely limit near-term practical deployment, and the paper itself acknowledges that real-time decoding remains out of reach. Open questions include whether the complexity can be reduced (via fewer diffusion steps, smaller models, or state reuse across GOPs) without sacrificing the coding gains, and whether the approach scales to longer sequences or higher resolutions.
