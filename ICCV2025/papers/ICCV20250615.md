## Problem

Existing image-to-LiDAR pretraining methods either perform single-frame spatial alignment or short-term frame-to-frame temporal propagation, both of which fail to capture long-range temporal dependencies and global scene evolution. This limits the model's ability to encode extended motion trajectories and structural variations that are critical for robust perception in dynamic driving environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250615/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes LiDAR point clouds (N×4: x, y, z, intensity) and multi-view RGB images (H×W×3) from 6 consecutive frames, with the 2D backbone being a DINOv2-pretrained ViT (S/B/L) and the 3D backbone being MinkUNet-34. The output is a pretrained LiDAR feature representation (N×C) distilled via L2 loss, which is then fine-tuned with task-specific heads for semantic segmentation (mIoU) or 3D object detection (mAP/NDS) using VoxelNet+SECOND or CenterPoint. No additional computational overhead is incurred at inference time since the memory bank and temporal propagation are pretraining-only mechanisms.

## Method

LiMA introduces a memory-bank-based long-term distillation framework with three components. First, Cross-View Aggregation resolves multi-view ambiguity by averaging pixel-aligned features from all V cameras for each LiDAR point, producing a unified feature F_u^t. Second, Long-Term Feature Propagation maintains a FIFO memory bank of the past k=6 frames' unified features, warps them into the current ego-vehicle coordinate frame via temporal calibration, and average-pools them with the current frame to yield a temporally enriched representation F_d^t, which is distilled into the LiDAR encoder via L2 loss. Third, Cross-Sequence Memory Alignment blends two LiDAR scenes from distinct sequences using LaserMix/PolarMix and enforces structural coherence with the original sequences' memory banks, improving cross-domain generalization. The key efficiency insight is that the memory bank stores compact unified features rather than re-extracting from raw historical images, keeping pretraining under 20 hours on 8 GPUs even with 8 frames.

## Results

On nuScenes with ViT-B backbone, LiMA (6 frames) achieves 56.65% mIoU under linear probing, 51.29% at 1% labels, and 76.91% with full fine-tuning, outperforming the strongest baseline ScaLR (51.90% LP, 48.90% 1%, 74.15% Full) by 4.75%, 2.39%, and 2.76% respectively. On SemanticKITTI 1%, LiMA reaches 50.44% mIoU vs. ScaLR's 47.77%, and on Waymo Open 1% it achieves 51.35% vs. 49.38%. For 3D object detection (CenterPoint, 5% labels), LiMA attains 46.5 mAP / 56.4 NDS, surpassing the best prior method CSC (45.3 / 54.2) by 1.2 mAP and 2.2 NDS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LiMA outperforms all 10 baselines across every evaluation setting. Against the closest competitor ScaLR (CVPR'24, 1 frame), LiMA gains +4.75% mIoU in LP, +2.39% at 1% labels, and +2.76% at full fine-tuning on nuScenes (ViT-B), with similar margins on KITTI (+2.67%) and Waymo (+1.97%). Compared to the temporal methods Seal (2 frames) and SuperFlow (3 frames), LiMA improves LP by 10.06% and 8.99% respectively, demonstrating that long-term propagation (6 frames) substantially exceeds short-term frame-to-frame consistency. On 3D object detection (Table 4), LiMA surpasses CSC (CVPR'24) by +1.2 mAP / +2.2 NDS at 5% labels and +1.4 / +1.0 at 20%, while also outperforming ScaLR by +2.2 mAP / +3.1 NDS at 5%. On the nuScenes-C robustness benchmark (Table 3), LiMA achieves the best mCE (91.43) and mRR (82.57) under full fine-tuning, and the best average mIoU (44.92) under LP, edging out SuperFlow (63.68 vs. 62.93 average mIoU in full setting) and ScaLR (59.82). The only setting where LiMA does not lead is the 25% fine-tuning on nuScenes with ViT-L, where SuperFlow (70.01) slightly trails LiMA (70.59) by a negligible 0.58%.

## Relation to Prior Work

LiMA extends the image-to-LiDAR knowledge distillation paradigm established by SLidR and refined by ScaLR, which aligns LiDAR features with pretrained vision foundation model (DINOv2/ViT) features via spatial projection. Where prior temporal extensions (Seal, SuperFlow) propagate features over only 2–3 adjacent frames using contrastive objectives, LiMA introduces a FIFO memory bank that aggregates up to 6 historical frames through ego-motion warping and average pooling, shifting from pairwise temporal contrast to cumulative long-horizon feature distillation. The cross-view aggregation module addresses a practical multi-camera redundancy issue that prior single-view or naive multi-view methods overlook, and the cross-sequence memory alignment repurposes existing mixing strategies (LaserMix, PolarMix) to enforce inter-sequence consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The memory-bank formulation offers a practical recipe for incorporating long-range temporal context into LiDAR pretraining without proportional computational cost—6 frames in ~18 hours versus Seal's 2 frames in ~27 hours—making long-horizon distillation accessible under standard training budgets. The consistent gains across 11+ datasets and both segmentation and detection tasks, plus robustness under sensor corruptions, suggest the learned representations transfer broadly. Open questions include whether the optimal 6-frame window generalizes to higher-frequency LiDAR or longer driving sequences, whether the simple averaging aggregation would be outperformed by learned fusion at larger scale, and how the approach extends to 4D occupancy or end-to-end driving pipelines.
