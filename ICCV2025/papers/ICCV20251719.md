## Problem

Echocardiography video segmentation requires modeling long-range spatiotemporal dependencies across the cardiac cycle while maintaining real-time inference speed, yet existing approaches face a fundamental trade-off: CNNs have limited receptive fields, Transformers incur quadratic computational cost, and space-time memory networks risk error propagation and high memory overhead. No prior method simultaneously achieves efficient global temporal modeling, robust local spatial precision under heavy speckle noise, and clinically deployable inference speed.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 10 uniformly sampled frames from each echocardiography video, resized to 256×256 (CAMUS) or 128×128 (EchoNet-Dynamic). The value encoder jointly encodes the current frame and the model's own previous prediction mask (not ground truth), simulating a clinical deployment scenario where annotations are unavailable at inference. Output is per-frame segmentation masks for the left ventricular endocardium, epicardium, and left atrium, from which LV ejection fraction is estimated via Simpson's rule.

## Method

GDKVM replaces quadratic softmax attention with a Linear Key-Value Association (LKVA) that maintains a fixed-size matrix hidden state S_t ∈ R^{Cv×Ck}, reducing complexity to O(t·Cv·Ck). The Gated Delta Rule (GDR) introduces two data-dependent matrices projected from the previous state: β_t controls writing strength (how much new key-value information replaces old associations) and α_t controls multiplicative decay (how aggressively stale memory is forgotten), yielding the update S_t = S_{t-1}(α_t(I − β_t K_t K_t^T)) + β_t V_t K_t^T. The Key-Pixel Feature Fusion (KPFF) module gates between a fused local+global key feature (via global average pooling) and a pixel-level feature to preserve high-frequency boundary details lost to over-smoothing. A ResNet-50 backbone extracts key features, and the model is trained with combined cross-entropy and soft-dice loss over 1500 iterations, predicting the first and last frames before computing loss to mimic the clinical no-ground-truth-at-inference setting.

## Results

On CAMUS, GDKVM achieves a mean Dice of 95.11%, mIoU of 92.97%, HD of 3.05, and ASD of 1.98, outperforming all baselines. On EchoNet-Dynamic, it reaches 93.46% mDice, 90.86% mIoU, HD 2.38, and ASD 1.36. For the clinical metric LV_EF on CAMUS, GDKVM attains the highest Pearson correlation (0.904) and the lowest bias±std (−0.19 ± 11.3%). The model runs at 37 FPS with 35.2M parameters, enabling real-time inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GDKVM outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across both datasets. The closest competitor is DSA [22], which trails by 0.86 mDice points on CAMUS (94.25 vs. 95.11) and 0.55 on EchoNet-Dynamic (92.91 vs. 93.46); GDKVM also leads DSA on HD (3.05 vs. 3.27) and ASD (1.98 vs. 2.37) on CAMUS. Among task-specific echocardiography methods, PKEchoNet [40] and MemSAM [10] are the next strongest, with GDKVM exceeding them by 1.62 and 1.48 mDice points respectively on CAMUS. The general-purpose video segmentation baselines (XMem++, Cutie, VideoMamba, Vision LSTM) lag further behind, with XMem++ [3] showing the largest gap at 5.73 mDice points on CAMUS. For the clinical LV_EF metric, GDKVM achieves the highest Pearson correlation (0.904) and the tightest bias±std (−0.19 ± 11.3%), surpassing SimLVSeg (0.895, 1.83 ± 13.8%) and DSA (0.891, 0.86 ± 13.4%). Notably, GDKVM attains these gains while running at 37 FPS with only 35.2M parameters, whereas MemSAM and XMem++ incur substantially higher computational overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

GDKVM sits at the intersection of two research threads: linear-attention/state-space sequence models (delta rule [41], gated delta networks [42], Mamba [12], VideoMamba [19]) and space-time memory networks for video segmentation (XMem [5], XMem++ [3], Cutie [7]). It departs from softmax-attention-based STM networks by replacing the quadratic attention with a linear key-value recurrence, and from SSM-based video models by introducing two data-dependent gating matrices (α_t for decay, β_t for write strength) that are projected from the hidden state itself rather than from the input. The Key-Pixel Feature Fusion module borrows the multi-scale fusion idea from xLSTM [2] but adapts it to the specific noise and contrast challenges of ultrasound imaging.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The linear-complexity formulation (O(t·C_v·C_k)) makes real-time processing of long echocardiography sequences practical in clinical settings, and the 37 FPS / 35.2 M-parameter footprint is competitive with heavier baselines. However, the non-square state matrix currently prevents chunkwise parallelization and hardware acceleration, and the model's tendency to over-rely on its own predictions in difficult frames (Fig. 10) suggests a gap in handling truly ambiguous cardiac anatomy. Open questions include whether the GDR mechanism generalizes to other medical video segmentation tasks (e.g., 3D echo, fetal ultrasound), whether fully- or semi-supervised settings change the optimal memory strategy, and whether a square-matrix redesign can unlock further speedups without sacrificing the adaptive forgetting behavior.
