## Problem

Unsupervised federated learning has been limited to representation learning and clustering because the absence of labels precludes direct classification. While CLIP's zero-shot capability makes label-free classification feasible, naively applying it in a federated unsupervised setting suffers from globally imbalanced pseudo labels (due to CLIP's inherent bias and unknown per-client label distributions) and conflicting model update directions under label skew, rendering standard aggregation ineffective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250824/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client holds a local set of unlabeled RGB images and the full set of class names for the target task; no ground-truth labels are available at any client. The frozen CLIP model (ViT-B/32 or ViT-B/16 image encoder) produces per-class probability scores via cosine similarity between the image embedding and text embeddings of prompted class names. The final output is a predicted class label, and during training the method also produces a redistributed pseudo-label budget per client per class that governs which samples enter local training.

## Method

FedCoPL combines two mechanisms. First, cooperative pseudo labeling: each client filters its unlabeled data by confidence and entropy thresholds to estimate a local pseudo-label distribution, uploads this distribution to the server, and the server globally allocates a fixed budget M of pseudo labels per class to each client proportionally to its estimated share, ensuring balanced and representative training data across all categories. Second, partial prompt aggregation: only visual prompts (which capture general image representations and remain similar across clients) are uploaded and aggregated at the server via a sample-count-weighted average, while textual prompts (which encode category-specific boundaries and diverge sharply under label skew) are retained locally for personalization. The overall objective minimizes cross-entropy over the redistributed pseudo-labeled sets, jointly optimizing the shared visual prompt and each client's private textual prompt.

## Results

Under Dirichlet-based label skew (β=0.1) with ViT-B/32, FedCoPL achieves an average accuracy of 72.49% across six datasets, substantially outperforming the best baseline (pFedPrompt+CPL at 62.45%) and zero-shot CLIP (60.19%). On individual datasets, FedCoPL reaches 95.38% on CIFAR-10, 75.76% on RESISC45, and 73.20% on UCF101. Under the more severe β=0.05 setting, FedCoPL further improves its average to 74.06%, while most baselines degrade. Under quantity-based label skew, FedCoPL maintains a 72.31% average, again far exceeding the best baseline (pFedPrompt+CPL at 63.55%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedCoPL outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and both skew settings. Against zero-shot CLIP, FedCoPL gains 12.3% on average (72.49% vs. 60.19%), with the largest margin on RESISC45 (+21.25%) and UCF101 (+12.20%). Compared to PromptFL (best variant 62.48% avg with CPL), FedCoPL leads by 10.01% on average; notably, on CUB all PromptFL variants fall below zero-shot CLIP, while FedCoPL exceeds it by 4.81%. PromptProx (best 62.27% avg) is surpassed by FedCoPL by 10.22% on average. pFedPrompt (best 62.65% avg with FPL) trails FedCoPL by 9.84% on average, with the gap widening to 13.77% on RESISC45. FedOPT with FPL (63.06% avg) is the strongest baseline but still lags FedCoPL by 9.43% on average; with CPL, FedOPT degrades severely to 57.17%, falling below zero-shot CLIP on four of six datasets, highlighting the fragility of existing personalized methods under noisy pseudo labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of three previously separate threads: unsupervised federated learning (which had been limited to representation learning and clustering, e.g., FedU, FedGaLA, Orchestra), federated prompt learning for VLMs (which had been exclusively supervised, e.g., PromptFL, pFedPrompt, FedOPT), and centralized unsupervised CLIP fine-tuning via pseudo labeling (e.g., UPL, FPL, CPL). By leveraging CLIP's zero-shot capability to generate pseudo labels in a federated, label-free setting, the authors introduce a new problem formulation that none of these prior lines addressed. The method's two components—cooperative pseudo labeling with global allocation and partial (visual-only) prompt aggregation—draw on ideas from centralized pseudo labeling and federated aggregation respectively, but their combination and the motivation for splitting prompt aggregation by modality are novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work opens a practically important direction: enabling federated classification without any labels by exploiting the zero-shot prior of a pre-trained VLM, which is highly relevant for privacy-sensitive domains where annotation is infeasible. The finding that naive pseudo labeling in a federated setting can be *worse* than zero-shot (e.g., FedOPT+CPL underperforms CLIP zero-shot on CUB and RESISC45) is a useful cautionary result for practitioners. Open questions include the lack of theoretical guarantees (convergence, privacy, fairness), the reliance on a uniform joint label distribution assumption, and whether the approach generalizes beyond CLIP to other VLMs or to multi-modal tasks. The partial prompt aggregation insight (visual prompts are more transferable, text prompts are more client-specific) may inform broader federated fine-tuning strategies beyond this specific setting.
