## Problem

Existing text-guided 3D shape editing methods (e.g., ChangeIt3D, Spice-E) perform global modifications that fail to preserve the input shape's structural identity outside the intended edit region. The core challenge is enabling fine-grained, localized edits that faithfully follow a text prompt while keeping all non-target geometry unchanged, a task that prior supervised editing models struggle with due to their global editing bias.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252604/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a point cloud (1024 points at the generator stage, upsampled to 4096), a natural-language edit prompt, and a binary mask identifying the region to modify. The mask is derived automatically by prompting Llama3 to extract a part name from the instruction, then running PointNet (trained on PartNet) for semantic segmentation. The output is an edited point cloud satisfying the constraint (1−M)⊙x = (1−M)⊙x_e, i.e., all unmasked coordinates remain identical to the input.

## Method

BlendedPC reframes localized shape editing as text-guided inpainting. The Inpaint-E model is built on the Point-E diffusion backbone, modified with Cross-Entity attention blocks (following Spice-E) so that a masked point cloud (with masked coordinates zeroed and RGB channels set to white) can serve as structural guidance alongside the text prompt during denoising. It is fine-tuned in a self-supervised manner on l-ShapeTalk, a curated subset of ShapeTalk containing only part-specific edit prompts. The key contribution is an inversion-free coordinate blending algorithm at inference: two parallel denoising passes run simultaneously—one reconstructing the full shape (conditioned on the complete point cloud and an empty prompt) and one performing text-conditioned inpainting (conditioned on the masked point cloud and the edit prompt)—and their outputs are blended per-coordinate according to the mask at each step from a transition timestep t_r=20 down to 0. This avoids artifact-prone inversion while preserving identity both outside and within the edited region.

## Results

On the l-ShapeTalk test set (localized editing subset), BlendedPC achieves a Geometric Distance (GD) of 0.29 and localized-GD of 0.05, substantially outperforming ChangeIt3D (GD 0.87, l-GD 0.20) and Spice-E (GD 2.26, l-GD 0.39). The Fréchet Point Distance (FPD) drops to 13.51 compared to 217.28 (ChangeIt3D) and 487.71 (Spice-E), indicating far superior structural fidelity. On edit fidelity, BlendedPC attains the highest CLIP similarity (0.27) and the only positive CLIP direction similarity (0.99 vs. 1.01–1.02 for baselines), confirming that edits align with the target prompt. A user study with 60 participants further confirms a 75% preference rate for BlendedPC outputs over the two baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BlendedPC is compared against two supervised text-guided shape editing methods: ChangeIt3D (Achlioptas et al., CVPR 2022), which learns direct source-to-target shape mappings from ShapeTalk pairs, and Spice-E (Sella et al., SIGGRAPH 2024), which fine-tunes a Point-E diffusion model with cross-entity attention for structural editing. BlendedPC outperforms both baselines on every reported metric across both the full ShapeTalk and the localized l-ShapeTalk test sets. The most dramatic gains appear in structural fidelity: FPD improves by roughly 16× over ChangeIt3D (13.51 vs. 217.28) and 36× over Spice-E (13.51 vs. 487.71) on l-ShapeTalk, while Class Distortion drops from 0.19/0.26 to 0.04. Identity preservation (l-GD) improves from 0.20 (ChangeIt3D) and 0.39 (Spice-E) to 0.05, reflecting the benefit of the inference-time coordinate blending mechanism that preserves unedited regions. On edit fidelity, BlendedPC achieves the highest CLIP similarity (0.27 vs. 0.21/0.25) and is the only method with a positive CLIP direction score (0.99), whereas both baselines score above 1.0 (indicating edits that deviate from the intended direction). The user study (60 participants, 15 questions each) confirms these quantitative findings with a 75% preference rate for BlendedPC versus 9% for ChangeIt3D and 16% for Spice-E.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the 2D blended diffusion paradigm (Blended Latent Diffusion) to 3D point clouds, reframing localized text-guided shape editing as a semantic inpainting problem. Architecturally, it builds on Point-E as a foundational point cloud diffusion backbone and adopts Spice-E's cross-entity attention mechanism to inject partial-shape structural guidance into the denoising process. The key departure from prior text-guided editing methods (ChangeIt3D, Spice-E) is the introduction of a binary edit mask that constrains modifications to a specific region, combined with a novel inversion-free coordinate blending inference algorithm that leverages the model's own reconstruction capability to preserve identity within the edited region.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The inversion-free coordinate blending mechanism offers a practical alternative to computationally expensive and artifact-prone inversion methods, making localized 3D editing more accessible. However, the approach is currently limited to PartNet-defined part categories (via PointNet segmentation) and three ShapeNet classes (Chair, Table, Lamp), raising open questions about generalization to arbitrary regions, multi-part simultaneous edits, and more complex shape categories. The reliance on an LLM to extract part names from free-form instructions is a potential bottleneck for edits that don't map cleanly to a single named part. Broader impact depends on whether the inpainting formulation and blending strategy transfer to other 3D representations (SDF, mesh, Gaussian splatting).
