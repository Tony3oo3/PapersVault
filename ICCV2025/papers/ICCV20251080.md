## Problem

Existing synthetic image detection datasets rely on outdated early-GAN generators, cartoon-style content, and sparse point annotations, making them poorly suited for detecting modern photorealistic synthetic images. Current detection methods also lack artifact-level textual interpretability and predominantly target tampered images rather than fully synthetic ones, where artifacts are more complex and less constrained by real-world references. Furthermore, detection has been framed solely as a 'defender' role, ignoring its potential to guide generators toward producing more realistic outputs.

## Input / Output

LEGION takes a single RGB image as input and simultaneously produces three outputs: a binary real/fake classification (via a two-layer MLP on the CLIP CLS token), pixel-level artifact segmentation masks (binary, produced by a SAM-style decoder triggered by <SEG> tokens in the LLM output), and free-form natural language explanations describing each artifact's location, type (physics, distortion, or structure), and specific anomaly. The segmentation masks correspond to irregular polygon regions rather than full object contours, reflecting the partial and contextually blended nature of synthetic artifacts.

## Method

LEGION builds on the GLaMM architecture with four components: a ViT-H/14 CLIP global image encoder, a vicuna-based LLM for explanation generation, a pretrained SAM encoder as grounding image encoder, and a SAM-style pixel decoder. The LLM appends a <SEG> token after each artifact description; a language-to-prompt projection maps the corresponding text embedding into the decoder's feature space to produce the binary mask. Training is two-stage: Stage 1 fine-tunes GLaMM with LoRA (α=8, lr=1e-4) on SynthScars using a weighted combination of BCE, Dice, and CE losses for localization and explanation; Stage 2 trains the detection MLP on ProGAN with CE loss. Beyond detection, LEGION is deployed as a 'Controller' in two training-free refinement pipelines: an iterative prompt-revision loop for image regeneration (where artifact explanations enrich the T2I prompt) and a region-by-region inpainting loop (where detected masks and explanations condition SDXL to selectively repair anomalous areas while preserving the rest of the image).

## Results

On the SynthScars benchmark, LEGION achieves SOTA artifact localization across all four content categories, with mIoU of 54.62 (Object), 54.52 (Animal), 60.82 (Human), and 53.67 (Scene), and F1 scores of 29.90, 27.43, 39.44, and 24.51 respectively. It also leads on the unseen-domain benchmarks LOKI (mIoU 48.66, F1 16.71) and RichHF-18K (mIoU 50.07, F1 17.41). For explanation generation, LEGION attains ROUGE-L of 39.50 and CSS of 72.60 on SynthScars, substantially outperforming the best baseline (LLaVA-v1.6: 29.61 / 61.75). On the UniversalFakeDetect detection benchmark, LEGION achieves the highest accuracy on GANs (97.01%), CRN (90.78%), and IMLE (98.93%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest traditional expert PAL4VST, LEGION gains +10.65 F1 on SynthScars Object (29.90 vs 19.25) and +3.72 F1 on Human (39.44 vs 35.70), while also leading on LOKI mIoU (48.66 vs 47.34) and RichHF-18K mIoU (50.07 vs 49.88). Compared to the fine-tuned segmentation VLM LISA-v1-7B, LEGION surpasses it by a wide margin on SynthScars (e.g., 54.62 vs 35.49 mIoU on Object) and LOKI (48.66 vs 31.10 mIoU), though LISA edges out LEGION on RichHF-18K F1 (21.94 vs 17.41). Object-grounding VLMs (Ferret, Griffon) and general-purpose MLLMs (Qwen2-VL, InternVL2, DeepSeek-VL2) exhibit extreme failure modes—either predicting almost no foreground or over-segmenting most of the image as artifact—resulting in low mIoU scores (21–42) far below LEGION's 53–61 range. On explanation generation (Table 3), LEGION outperforms all baselines including GPT-4o (ROUGE-L 39.50 vs 22.43; CSS 72.60 vs 53.55 on SynthScars). For detection (Table 4), LEGION achieves the best accuracy on GANs (97.01%), CRN (90.78%), and IMLE (98.93%), but trails FreqNet on Deepfakes (63.37 vs 97.40) and NPR on SAN (57.76 vs 98.63), indicating the detection head is less competitive on certain generator families.

## Relation to Prior Work

LEGION builds directly on GLaMM's pixel-grounding MLLM architecture, repurposing its SAM-based segmentation decoder and LLM backbone for the forgery analysis domain. It departs from traditional CNN/transformer-based localization methods (PAL4VST, TruFor, HiFi-Net) that rely on low-level structural cues and lack interpretability, and from prior MLLM-based forgery work (FFAA, FakeShield, ForgeryGPT, SIDA) that predominantly targets tampered images rather than fully synthetic ones. The paper also extends the 'defender' framing of detection research into a 'controller' role, where artifact explanations and masks feed back into generation pipelines (Stable Diffusion 3.5, SDXL) for iterative refinement—a direction only briefly explored by prior inpainting-localization work like PAL4Inpaint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SynthScars fills a concrete gap by providing 12K high-quality fully synthetic images with irregular polygon masks, artifact-type labels, and human-written explanations—addressing the outdated-generator and sparse-annotation problems of prior benchmarks. The controller paradigm (using detection feedback to guide regeneration and inpainting) opens an interesting co-evolution loop between detection and generation, though the HPS gains are modest (≈7% for regeneration, ≈2% for inpainting) and the pipeline is training-free and limited to two specific generation backends. Open questions include whether the controller approach scales to newer, more diverse generators, whether the two-stage training (localization/explanation first, then detection) is optimal, and how well the framework generalizes as generative models continue to evolve rapidly.
