## Problem

Existing voxel-based 3D Semantic Scene Completion methods treat voxels as the sole interaction unit, which inherently limits the exploitation of class-level information critical for fine-grained completion. Instance categories suffer from class omission and semantic ambiguity due to occlusion and projection errors, while scene categories exhibit structural incoherence from out-of-view regions, yet unified voxel modules cannot address these divergent challenges simultaneously. The paper identifies three specific barriers: voxel construction disrupts category structure, a single module cannot handle both instance and scene needs, and 3D spatial interactions impose prohibitive computational costs for further class-level integration.

## Input / Output

The method consumes single-frame multi-camera RGB images (front and left views) and a pretrained MobileStereoNet depth estimate to compute 3D projected point positions. In the BEV plane, instance queries are sparsely placed at detected object locations while scene queries are densely tiled as patches, and the final output is a 3D semantic voxel grid produced by fusing category-decoupled height distributions (H_ins and H_scn) with their respective BEV feature maps via broadcasted element-wise multiplication, followed by a local-global aggregator.

## Method

DISC replaces uniform voxel queries with a Discriminative Query Generator (DQG) that initializes separate instance and scene queries with geometric and semantic priors: instance queries are located via image-space segmentation with a parallelizable neighbor-suppression voting strategy projected to BEV, while scene queries are patch-based BEV features upsampled to single vectors. A Coarse-to-fine BEV generation module refines LSS-lifted voxel features using depth-guided surface voxels to mitigate projection errors. The Dual-Attention Class Decoder (DACD) then processes each stream with tailored layers: the Adaptive Instance Layer transforms queries into pillar-like representations with learned height sampling and deformable cross-attention to multi-level image features plus scene-context cross-attention, while the Global Scene Layer aggregates masked image-query features via cross-attention and expands receptive field through self-attention. Finally, category-decoupled height prediction combines the two streams in 3D space, and training uses a composite loss including SceneClass Affinity Loss, class-frequency weighted cross-entropy, BEV segmentation/height auxiliary losses, and explicit depth supervision.

## Results

On SemanticKITTI (hidden test), DISC achieves IoU 45.30, mIoU 17.35, InsM 7.25, and ScnM 28.56, outperforming all competing methods on every metric. On SSCBench-KITTI-360, DISC reaches mIoU 20.55 and InsM 13.47, setting new SOTA for both metrics. Notably, DISC is the first single-frame method to surpass multi-frame SOTA approaches in IoU, mIoU, and InsM on SemanticKITTI, while improving instance mIoU by 17.9% over the previous best single-frame method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DISC outperforms the strongest camera-based baseline CGFormer [50] on mIoU (20.55 vs. 20.05) and InsM (13.47 vs. 12.08) on SSCBench-KITTI-360, though CGFormer retains a slight edge in IoU (48.07 vs. 47.35) and ScnM (28.01 vs. 27.63). On SemanticKITTI, DISC surpasses CGFormer on all four aggregate metrics, including a 17.9% relative gain in instance mIoU. Against Symphonies [13] (CVPR 2024), the prior SOTA single-frame method, DISC improves mIoU from 18.58 to 20.55 and InsM from 13.11 to 13.47 on SSCBench-KITTI-360. Compared to earlier voxel-based methods (VoxFormer, TPVFormer, MonoScene), DISC achieves substantially higher scores across all metrics, with mIoU gains of 5–9 points. DISC also surpasses LiDAR-based methods (SSCNet, LMSCNet) in mIoU and InsM despite using only camera input, though SSCNet retains a higher overall IoU (53.58) due to its direct 3D sensor access. The paper highlights that DISC is the first single-frame method to beat multi-frame SOTA fusion approaches in IoU, mIoU, and InsM on SemanticKITTI.

## Relation to Prior Work

DISC departs from the dominant voxel-based paradigm in 3D SSC (VoxFormer, Symphonies, CGFormer) by replacing uniform voxel queries with discriminative class-specific queries (instance vs. scene) in BEV space. It builds on Lift-Splat-Shoot for BEV feature generation and adopts a FlashOcc-style baseline (2D backbone + BEV encoder + 3D decoder), but introduces a dual-stream architecture with specialized decoding layers (Adaptive Instance Layer and Global Scene Layer) that address the distinct failure modes of each category. The key conceptual shift is from treating voxels as atomic interaction units to exploiting class-level geometric and semantic priors for targeted feature propagation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DISC demonstrates that a single-frame camera-based method can outperform multi-frame SOTA in mIoU, IoU, and instance mIoU, suggesting that class-aware architectural design can compensate for the absence of temporal information. The 17.9% instance mIoU gain over prior SOTA highlights that instance categories (vehicles, pedestrians, cyclists) have been systematically under-served by unified voxel-based approaches. Open questions include whether the dual-stream paradigm generalizes to other sensor modalities (LiDAR, multi-sensor fusion), whether the reliance on a separate depth estimator (MobileStereoNet) limits robustness in adverse conditions, and whether the fixed instance/scene category split can be made adaptive per scene.
