## Problem

Dermatology AI lags behind other medical domains due to a critical data gap: existing vision-language datasets for skin conditions are tiny (SkinCAP has only 4,000 image-text pairs), predominantly offer single-label annotations without rich textual descriptions, and lack the clinical context (patient history, symptoms, skin tone) and structured ontology knowledge needed for real-world diagnostic workflows spanning hundreds of conditions.

## Input / Output

The DermLIP models consume dermoscopic or clinical skin images paired with multi-granular text descriptions averaging 41 tokens, which include disease names, 130 standardized visual concepts (morphology, color, border, distribution), and meta-information such as body site, symptoms, and skin tone. Outputs span four task types: zero-shot disease classification over 390 conditions organized in a 4-level hierarchy, per-concept AUROC scores for 130 clinical concepts and dermoscopic artifacts, cross-modal retrieval rankings (Recall@10), and joint image-text embeddings for downstream linear probing at 1%, 10%, and 100% label ratios.

## Method

Derm1M is constructed through a five-stage pipeline: (1) multi-source collection from YouTube videos, PubMed articles, medical forums, educational materials, and public datasets; (2) preprocessing with FFmpeg keyframe extraction, Whisper speech-to-text, DINO subfigure detection, and EfficientNetV2-S feature extraction; (3) parallel image-text cleaning using DenseNet121 classifiers, K-means clustering, and GPT-4o terminology correction; (4) temporal and caption-based image-text pairing; and (5) knowledge-enhanced text augmentation that generates three text variants (ontology-based, concept-based, refined medical) grounded in a 390-disease, 130-concept ontology collaboratively built by four senior dermatologists and expanded via LLM-guided hierarchical placement. The DermLIP model family is trained with CLIP's contrastive learning objective, pairing a vision encoder (ViT-B16 or the self-supervised PanDerm-B) with a text encoder (GPT-2/77, BiomedBERT/77, or PubMedBERT/256). The key finding is that domain-specific pre-training in both modalities combined with longer text context (256 tokens) yields the strongest zero-shot and few-shot performance, outperforming general and biomedical VLMs by 14.7% average accuracy and 48.1% retrieval recall.

## Results

On zero-shot skin disease classification, DermLIP (PanDerm-B/PMB256) achieves 58.79% average accuracy across four datasets (HAM10000, Fitzpatrick17K, PAD-UFES-20, Daffodil), outperforming the best biomedical baseline BiomedCLIP (44.08%) by 14.71% and the dermatology-specific MONET (40.01%) by 18.78%. In few-shot linear probing at 1% data, DermLIP (PanDerm-B/PMB256) reaches 58.6% average accuracy, surpassing MONET by 5.6%. For cross-modal retrieval on the Derm1M holdout set, DermLIP (PanDerm-B/PMB256) achieves Recall@10 of 59.98% (I2T) and 59.30% (T2I), exceeding BiomedCLIP by over 43 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DermLIP (PanDerm-B/PMB256) decisively outperforms all general VLMs (CLIP, SigLIP, CoCa) and biomedical VLMs (PMC-CLIP, BiomedCLIP) on zero-shot classification, with the largest gap over BiomedCLIP (58.79% vs. 44.08% average). Against the dermatology-specific MONET (ViT-L14), DermLIP wins by 18.78% on zero-shot classification and 5.6% on 1% linear probing, despite using a smaller vision encoder (ViT-B16 vs. ViT-L14). On cross-modal retrieval, DermLIP (PanDerm-B/PMB256) achieves 59.98% Recall@10 (I2T) on the holdout set versus BiomedCLIP's 16.57% and MONET's 12.90%, a >43-point margin. On the external SkinCAP retrieval benchmark, DermLIP outperforms MONET by 5.97% (I2T) and 6.36% (T2I). In full-shot (100%) linear probing, PanDerm-L (80.2%) slightly edges DermLIP (79.3%), but DermLIP leads at 1% (58.6% vs. 56.7%) and 10% (69.6% vs. 68.5%), indicating stronger data efficiency. For zero-shot concept identification on SkinCon, DermLIP achieves 73.1% AUROC, surpassing MONET by 1.1% overall and 2.3% on SkinCon specifically.

## Relation to Prior Work

Derm1M extends the vision-language pretraining paradigm (CLIP, SigLIP, CoCa) into dermatology by scaling the only prior dermatology VL dataset (SkinCAP, 4K pairs) by 257×, while drawing methodological inspiration from Quilt1M's use of YouTube educational content and PMC-15M's PubMed mining. Its key conceptual departure is the integration of an expert-curated hierarchical disease ontology (390 conditions, 4 levels) and 130 standardized clinical concepts into the text augmentation pipeline, a structural element absent from broader biomedical VL datasets like PMC-15M and MedTrinity-25M. The training models (DermLIP) reuse the standard CLIP contrastive objective but leverage domain-specific encoders (PanDerm for vision, PubMedBERT for text), positioning the work at the intersection of self-supervised dermatology foundation models and contrastive language-image pretraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Derm1M fills a critical gap: no comparable large-scale, ontology-aligned VL dataset existed for dermatology, a domain where primary-care diagnostic accuracy is as low as 24% and hundreds of conditions must be differentiated. The 130-concept annotation and four-level hierarchical structure enable multi-granular diagnostic AI and explainable concept-based models (CBMs) that were previously infeasible at scale. Key limitations include residual noise from educational sources, 19 diseases unplaced in the ontology, and the absence of ablations isolating the ontology's contribution beyond raw data volume. Open questions include whether the hierarchical structure genuinely improves fine-grained generalization over flat labels at scale, and whether the pipeline generalizes to other underserved medical specialties.
