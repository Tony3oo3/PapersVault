## Problem

Existing in-generation watermarking methods for diffusion models (e.g., Gaussian Shading) rely on DDIM inversion to recover embedded watermarks, but this inversion is highly sensitive to pixel-level modifications, making them fragile against common image manipulations such as cropping, inpainting, and logo insertion. Additionally, no prior in-generation framework simultaneously provides both copyright verification and proactive tamper localization, leaving a gap in protecting AI-generated content from malicious edits while preserving generation quality.

## Input / Output

At generation time, the method takes a text prompt, a 256-bit copyright message, a ChaCha20 encryption key, and a fixed localization seed, producing a 512×512 watermarked image whose 4×64×64 latent space encodes both watermarks. At extraction time, it accepts the (potentially tampered and degraded) watermarked image and outputs the decoded copyright message plus a binary tampering localization mask in image space. The localization watermark is a Bernoulli(0.5) tensor of the same spatial dimensions as the latent, and the copyright message is expanded via replication and ChaCha20 encryption to fill the 4×64×64 grid.

## Method

TAG-WM embeds dual watermarks via a Dual-Mark Joint Sampling (DMJS) algorithm that partitions the real line into three intervals (−∞,a), [a,b), [b,+∞) and maps the four (w_c, w_l) bit-pair combinations onto them in a reordered sequence, provably preserving the N(0,I) distribution of the initial noise so generation quality is lossless. At extraction, VAE encoding followed by DDIM inversion recovers the watermarked pseudo-noise, which is reverse-sampled to reconstruct both W_cop and W_loc. A Dense Variation Region Detector (DVRD) exploits the fact that tampering randomizes the Bernoulli bit distribution at affected latent positions (error probability 0.5 vs. intrinsic 0.145), using a UNet to refine the initial XOR-based tamper mask into a spatially accurate localization map. Finally, Tamper-Aware Decoding (TAD) excludes watermark bits falling within the detected tampered regions and applies majority voting on the remaining reliable bits, substantially improving copyright recovery under manipulation.

## Results

In non-tampering scenarios (Table 2), TAG-WM achieves T-TPR of 1.000 (clean) and 0.997–0.999 (degraded) across SD V1.4/V2.0/V2.1, matching Gaussian Shading and Stable Signature, while maintaining a CLIP Score of 0.3631±0.0005 (t-value 0.6870, statistically indistinguishable from watermark-free SD). In tampering scenarios (Table 3), TAG-WM outperforms EditGuard by ~10% in Bit Accuracy (92.3% vs 82.6% clean; 86.7% vs 62.6% degraded) and by ~0.5 in T-TPR (0.989 vs 0.477 clean; 0.907 vs 0.129 degraded) across all four inpainting methods tested. Against Gaussian Shading in background tampering, TAG-WM achieves D-TPR of 0.987 vs GS's 0.611 and T-TPR of 0.980 vs GS's 0.084 in clean settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against post-processing baselines (DwtDct, DwtDctSvd, RivaGAN), TAG-WM dominates on all metrics: degraded T-TPR of ~0.998 vs 0.17–0.71, and degraded Bit Acc of ~0.975 vs 0.56–0.91, while maintaining identical CLIP Score to watermark-free SD. Against in-generation baselines (Stable Signature, Tree-Ring, Gaussian Shading), TAG-WM matches or slightly exceeds them on T-TPR and Bit Acc in clean/degraded non-tampering settings (e.g., degraded T-TPR 0.998 vs GS 0.997), with no measurable quality loss. The critical differentiation emerges in tampering scenarios: TAG-WM outperforms EditGuard by ~10 percentage points in Bit Accuracy and ~0.5 in T-TPR under degradation (e.g., 86.7% vs 62.6% Bit Acc, 0.907 vs 0.129 T-TPR on ControlNet inpainting), while EditGuard retains a marginal ~0.7% Dice advantage in clean localization. Against Gaussian Shading in background tampering, TAG-WM's tamper-aware decoding yields D-TPR of 0.987 vs GS's 0.611 and T-TPR of 0.980 vs GS's 0.084, a gap that widens under degradation (0.893 vs 0.378 D-TPR). In foreground tampering at high ratios (≥0.7), TAG-WM's advantage over GS narrows due to weaker localization on large tampered regions, an acknowledged limitation tied to training data distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAG-WM extends Gaussian Shading (GS), which achieved provably quality-lossless watermarking via distribution-preserving latent-space sampling, by generalizing its single-watermark mechanism to a dual-mark joint sampling scheme that simultaneously embeds copyright and localization watermarks. It departs from GS's fundamental vulnerability to pixel-level manipulations by exploiting DDIM inversion sensitivity as a tamper-detection signal, a property GS does not leverage. The approach also contrasts with EditGuard, which performs dual-watermark (copyright + tamper localization) as a post-processing pipeline; TAG-WM integrates both functions directly into the diffusion generation process, eliminating the quality-robustness trade-off inherent in post-hoc embedding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first in-generation watermarking framework to unify copyright protection and tamper localization within a single diffusion pipeline, offering 4× capacity and 6.3× speed advantages over post-processing alternatives while maintaining lossless visual quality. Key limitations include dependence on deterministic DDIM inversion (incompatible with SDE schedulers), vulnerability to full-image adversarial noise distortion, and a fixed-seed localization watermark that could be targeted by informed attackers. Open questions include whether attention-guided watermark allocation can improve fine-grained detection, whether adaptive dynamic strategies can mitigate the fixed-seed weakness, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation pipelines.
