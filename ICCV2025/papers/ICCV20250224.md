## Problem

Existing video semantic segmentation methods predominantly follow a reference-target interaction paradigm that captures only local temporal contexts while neglecting global temporal information across the entire video. Additionally, complex dynamics and varying lighting introduce inter-frame intra-class discrepancies in feature representations, leading to unstable predictions. The standard Video Consistency (VC) metric also fails to account for segmentation accuracy and is insensitive to small-object errors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250224/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 4-frame video clip with frames sampled at {t−9, t−6, t−3, t} (where t is the target frame) and produces a pixel-level semantic segmentation mask for the target frame. A shared MiT backbone (pre-trained on ImageNet-1K) extracts multi-level features from all frames, which are fused into a single encoded representation per frame. The framework is evaluated on VSPW (124 semantic categories) and Cityscapes (19 categories), with the number of cluster centers tuned per backbone size (e.g., 200 for MiT-B1 on VSPW, 45 for MiT-B1 on Cityscapes).

## Method

DTERN models both local and global temporal information through learnable cluster-center exemplars in an end-to-end framework. The Local Temporal Exemplar Module (LTEM) is applied sequentially N times; each instance contains a Cluster Assignment Module (with parallel depthwise convolutions for multi-scale texture and axial decomposition for spatial features), a Selection and Aggregation Module that merges cluster assignments across adjacent LTEMs via attention steering and constructs local exemplars by similarity-weighted aggregation of per-frame cluster centers, and a Feature Enhancement Module that updates target features via attention over the local exemplars. The Global Temporal Exemplar Module (GTEM) introduces learnable frame-level and video-level cluster-center tokens; the video-level centers progressively aggregate local temporal context from the updated target features to derive global temporal information, which is then used to further refine the target features before the segmentation head. This exemplar-based design reduces attention complexity from quadratic to linear (since the number of clusters M is much smaller than the spatial token count) and avoids the multi-stage training and non-learnable exemplar limitations of prior work like CFFM++.

## Results

On VSPW, DTERN achieves 35.9% mIoU (MiT-B0), 40.3% (MiT-B1), 46.1% (MiT-B2), and 50.3% (MiT-B5), consistently outperforming the image-only SegFormer baseline by 2.6–2.8% mIoU. DTERN also attains the best VEC₈ and VEC₁₆ scores across all backbone sizes (e.g., 48.1/49.3 with MiT-B5), indicating superior temporal consistency. On Cityscapes, DTERN reaches 74.5% mIoU (MiT-B0) and 76.0% (MiT-B1), surpassing CFFM++ by 0.2% and 0.3% respectively. The model operates at 31.3 FPS (MiT-B0) and 10.6 FPS (MiT-B5) on a single RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the image-only SegFormer baseline, DTERN gains 2.8% mIoU (MiT-B0) and 2.6% (MiT-B1) on VSPW, confirming the benefit of temporal modeling. Compared to the self-attention-based CFFM, DTERN improves mIoU by 0.7% (MiT-B0), 2.0% (MiT-B1), 1.1% (MiT-B2), and 1.0% (MiT-B5), while also achieving the best VEC scores in every setting. Against CFFM++—the closest competitor that also models global temporal context—DTERN matches mIoU on MiT-B0 (35.9%) with 4.4× fewer parameters (8.2M vs. 35.9M) and surpasses it by 0.4–0.6% mIoU on MiT-B1/B2/B5, while also leading on VEC₈ and VEC₁₆ across all backbones. On Cityscapes, DTERN edges out CFFM++ by 0.2% (MiT-B0: 74.5 vs. 74.3) and 0.3% (MiT-B1: 76.0 vs. 75.7) mIoU. DTERN also outperforms the clustering-based image segmentation methods ClusterFormer (38.0%) and PaCaViT (39.7%) in the ablation comparison (Table 6), achieving 40.3% mIoU with its tailored clustering strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

DTERN extends the reference-target interaction paradigm dominant in video semantic segmentation (CFFM, MRCFA, STT) by introducing a dual-exemplar framework that models both local and global temporal contexts. Its closest predecessor, CFFM++, also uses global exemplars but relies on non-learnable cluster centers from a pre-trained encoder within a multi-stage training pipeline; DTERN replaces this with fully learnable local and global exemplars trained end-to-end. The feature selection mechanism in the SAM module is borrowed from TOAST [30], and the clustering-based representation draws conceptual inspiration from image segmentation work like ClusterFormer and PaCa-ViT, repurposed here for temporal modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VEC metric addresses a genuine gap in video segmentation evaluation by coupling temporal consistency with per-class segmentation accuracy, making it more informative than the standard VC metric (which can score a trivial all-background prediction as perfect). The end-to-end learnable exemplar approach removes the multi-stage training burden of CFFM++ and offers a cleaner architectural template for future work on temporal context mining. However, the mIoU gains over CFFM++ are modest (0.2–0.7%), FPS is lower than several baselines, and the framework is validated only on 4-frame clips with MiT backbones, leaving open questions about scalability to longer sequences, larger backbones, and real-time deployment.
