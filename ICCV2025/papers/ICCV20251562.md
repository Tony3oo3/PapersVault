## Problem

Procedural videos (cooking, assembly, furniture building) span minutes to hours and require both long-term semantic memory of completed steps and fine-grained short-term visual context of the present action. Existing streaming video understanding methods store all past observations as visual tokens, leading to quadratic memory and compute scaling that makes real-time inference on long-form procedural videos infeasible, while offline VideoLLMs cannot operate without access to future frames.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251562/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes streaming video frames sequentially (typically at 4 fps for detection tasks) along with text instructions such as 'Provide what I am doing now' or 'Provide the next 10 steps I need to do.' Predictions are emitted per-frame without access to future observations, with a fixed cache budget of N_S=64 short-term frames (~16 s) and N_L=5 long-term verbalized steps. The lightest variant (ProVideLLM-1B/5) uses only 5 visual tokens per frame and 2 GB GPU memory, achieving 10 FPS per-frame inference and 25 FPS streaming dialogue on a single A6000.

## Method

ProVideLLM's core contribution is a multimodal interleaved cache that stores two token types in a single FIFO queue: verbalized text tokens (compressed summaries of long-term step sequences, e.g. 'get → crack → whisk eggs') and visual tokens (DINOv2 patch features for short-term fine-grained context). A novel DETR-QFormer connector, pretrained with hand and object-in-contact queries plus a DETR bounding-box loss, compresses DINOv2 patch tokens into a small set of hand-object-focused visual tokens while suppressing background distractors. Interleaving both token types in one cache eliminates the attention recomputation cost (O(N²) → O(N)) that arises when converting short-term visual tokens into long-term text, enabling sublinear scaling of memory and compute with video length. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) alignment pre-training of DETR-QFormer with language modeling and hand-object detection losses on EPIC-KITCHENS, and (2) instruction tuning with LoRA (r=128) on task-specific prompts including interleaved <L> tokens for long-term cache entries.

## Results

ProVideLLM-8B/11+ achieves 67.3% Top-1 accuracy on COIN Step Recognition, surpassing the previous best VideoLLM-MoD (63.4%) by 3.9 points. On COIN Next Step Forecasting, the model with long-term verbalization reaches 53.6% Top-1, outperforming VideoLLM-MoD (49.7%) by ~4 points. On EgoExo4D Fine-grained Keystep Recognition, ProVideLLM-8B/11 scores 44.36/50.74% (Val/Test), beating VideoLLM-MoD (42.62% Val) by 1.74 points. On Ego4D Goal-Step Online Step Detection, the lightweight ProVideLLM-1B/5 with verbalization achieves 13.0 per-frame mAP, exceeding LSTR (8.9) by over 4 points at a similar temporal span.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProVideLLM outperforms all 16 baselines across every evaluated task. Against the strongest VideoLLM competitors, VideoLLM-MoD [83] and VideoLLM-online [14], ProVideLLM-8B/11+ gains 3.9 points on COIN Step Recognition (67.3 vs 63.4/63.1), 3.9 points on COIN Next Step Forecasting (53.6 vs 49.7/49.1), and 1.74 points on EgoExo4D Keystep Recognition (44.36 vs 42.62). Against the dedicated procedural model VEDIT [42], the margin is 4.6 points on COIN Step Recognition. On Ego4D Goal-Step Online Step Detection, ProVideLLM-1B/5 with verbalization (13.0 mAP) beats LSTR [86] (8.9) by 4.1 points and EgoOnly [78] (10.3) by 2.7 points, while using only 22 long-term tokens to encode 128 s of past versus LSTR's full visual cache. On Assembly101 Action Anticipation, ProVideLLM-8B/11 (13.8 Top-5) surpasses +Goal [88] (12.0) by 1.8 points using a single view versus 12 views. In the multi-task COIN benchmark, ProVideLLM-8B/11+ leads all baselines on all five tasks (e.g., 66.9 vs 63.4 Step, 50.5 vs 49.7 Next), and in cross-dataset generalization it achieves 12.4% Ego accuracy versus 8.3% (LLaVA-OneVision [38]) and 3.6% (VideoLLaVA [41]).

## Relation to Prior Work

ProVideLLM extends the LLaVA-style encoder-connector-decoder architecture to the streaming setting, departing from prior VideoLLMs (VideoLLM-online, VideoLLM-MoD) that process offline or use computationally prohibitive online approaches. Its core architectural departure from LSTR-style long-short-term streaming is replacing visual long-term tokens with verbalized text summaries, achieving 22× token compression while preserving causal step dependencies. The DETR-QFormer connector is a novel fusion of BLIP-2's Q-Former compression and DETR's bounding-box regression, specifically pretrained to focus on hand-object interaction regions, and the choice of DINOv2 over CLIP/SigLIP is motivated by a newly identified 'temporal collapse' phenomenon in language-aligned encoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to unify step recognition, next-step forecasting, and procedural planning in a single real-time model with a 2GB GPU footprint and 10 FPS per-frame inference, making streaming procedural assistance (e.g., AR cooking or assembly guidance) practically deployable. The multimodal interleaved cache is a generic architectural contribution applicable beyond procedural videos, and the verbalization strategy opens a direction for compressing long videos on the fly. However, cross-dataset generalization remains modest (12.4% on EgoExo4D when trained on Ego4D), the model lacks real-time feedback on execution quality ('how' rather than 'what'), and DETR-QFormer relies on pseudo ground-truth bounding boxes that could introduce bias.
