## Problem

Unified vision-language models process the same input under different task instructions, yet existing adversarial attack evaluations measure success rates per task in isolation, failing to capture whether a single perturbation can simultaneously fool all tasks. This paper identifies that cross-task transferability within a unified VLM is a distinct and largely unexplored security dimension, and that prior cross-task attack studies lack a rigorous metric for simultaneous multi-task deception.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, the bounding box of a source object, and a source-to-target category pair (e.g., 'bicycle' → 'motorcycle') as input, and produces a single adversarial image I_adv = I + δ with ‖δ‖∞ ≤ 16/255. The perturbation is spatially concentrated on the token grid region corresponding to the source object rather than spread uniformly. Success is measured by whether the same adversarial image causes the target category to appear (and the source category to disappear) simultaneously across four tasks: image captioning, object detection, region categorization, and object localization, with IoU thresholds of 0.6 for box-based tasks.

## Method

CRAFT (Cross-task Region-based Attack Framework with Token-alignment) exploits the shared representation space of unified VLMs by operating on localized region tokens rather than the full image. It first projects the source object's bounding box onto the model's patch-token grid to identify the relevant token indices, then extracts the visual features of those tokens and the text embeddings of the target (positive) and source/negative categories. A contrastive alignment loss pushes the region features toward the target category embedding while repelling them from all non-target categories, with a margin hyperparameter τ = 0.9. The perturbation is optimized via PGD (100 iterations, step size 4/255) under the ℓ∞ constraint, and the key design choice of using all non-target categories as negatives (rather than only the source) combined with region token localization yields the strongest cross-task transferability, as confirmed by ablation studies.

## Results

On the CrossVLAD benchmark (3,000 samples from MSCOCO), CRAFT achieves CTSR-4 scores of 0.471 (Florence-2), 0.485 (UnifiedIO-2), and 0.286 (OFA), substantially outperforming the strongest baseline Attack-Bard (0.213, 0.098, 0.087 respectively). CRAFT also leads on CTSR-3 (at least 3 tasks fooled simultaneously) with 0.609, 0.652, and 0.429 on the three models. The average per-task success rate for CRAFT reaches 0.707 on Florence-2 and 0.601 on UnifiedIO-2, while maintaining balanced performance across all four tasks (IC, OD, RC, OL) rather than excelling on one task at the expense of others.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRAFT decisively outperforms all four baselines on the cross-task metrics (CTSR-4 and CTSR-3) across all three unified VLMs. On Florence-2, CRAFT achieves CTSR-4 of 0.471 versus 0.213 for Attack-Bard (the strongest baseline), 0.264 for MF-ii, 0.139 for MF-it, and 0.067 for Mix.Attack — a 2.2× improvement over the best baseline. The gap is even more dramatic on UnifiedIO-2, where CRAFT's CTSR-4 of 0.485 exceeds Attack-Bard's 0.098 by a factor of nearly 5×. On individual task success rates, Attack-Bard dominates Image Captioning (0.935 on Florence-2, 0.934 on UnifiedIO-2) but collapses on other tasks (e.g., 0.101 on OL for OFA), confirming that its high per-task scores do not translate to cross-task consistency. CRAFT sacrifices some peak per-task performance (e.g., 0.765 vs. 0.935 on IC for Florence-2) in exchange for substantially more balanced and simultaneously successful manipulation across all four tasks, which is the core objective of the cross-task attack setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two lines of prior adversarial attack work on VLMs: multi-task attacks that generate separate adversarial examples per task, and cross-task attacks that evaluate success independently on each task. The key conceptual departure is the evaluation paradigm—requiring a single adversarial example to simultaneously fool all four tasks in a unified VLM, rather than reporting per-task success rates. Methodologically, CRAFT repurposes contrastive alignment in the unified representation space (a technique common in VLM pretraining) as an attack objective, combined with region-token localization to concentrate perturbations, which is a new combination not seen in prior cross-task or cross-model attack literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CTSR metrics expose a significant evaluation gap: methods that appear effective on individual tasks (e.g., Attack-Bard achieving 0.935 on captioning) drop to 0.213 CTSR-4, revealing that per-task success rates overstate real-world cross-task vulnerability. This matters as unified VLMs are deployed in instruction-controlled settings where the attacker cannot predict which task will process their input. Open questions include whether findings generalize beyond object-change attacks to other manipulation objectives, whether the 3,000-sample benchmark is sufficient for robust conclusions, and what defenses (if any) can restore cross-task consistency. The paper does not explore defensive strategies, leaving the security implications largely unaddressed.
