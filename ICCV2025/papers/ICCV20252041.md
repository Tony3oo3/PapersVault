## Problem

Existing image-based world models for robotic manipulation lack explicit 3D geometric and spatial understanding, making them vulnerable to visual variations (lighting, camera pose, textures) and prone to failures in capturing fine contact dynamics critical for manipulation. While 3D Gaussian Splatting offers efficient explicit 3D scene representation, its reliance on offline per-scene optimization makes it impractical for real-time policy learning and model-based RL. The paper addresses how to build a scalable, feed-forward 3D world model that combines Gaussian Splatting with generative dynamics modeling for robotic manipulation.

## Input / Output

The method takes single or two-view unposed RGB images along with robot actions and states as input. Images are first converted to 3D Gaussian representations via a feed-forward pipeline (Mast3R stereo reconstruction + Splatt3R Gaussian prediction), then encoded into a fixed-length latent embedding of N×D dimensions via Farthest Point Sampling and cross-attention. The output is a predicted future 3D Gaussian scene (renderable to novel views) for world modeling, or robot action sequences for downstream policy execution in imitation learning and RL settings.

## Method

GWM combines a 3D Gaussian Variational Autoencoder with a latent Diffusion Transformer (DiT) to model action-conditioned scene dynamics. The 3D Gaussian VAE uses FPS to downsample variable-count Gaussians to a fixed set, then a cross-attention encoder maps them to compact latent embeddings, with a mirrored transformer decoder reconstructing Gaussians (trained with Chamfer + rendering loss). The dynamics model follows EDM preconditioning, where a DiT with RoPE positional encoding, AdaLN-modulated time embeddings, and cross-attention on robot actions denoises the future state latent conditioned on history states and actions. This architecture enables GWM to serve dual roles: as a self-supervised visual encoder that improves imitation learning policies (BC-Transformer, Diffusion Policy), and as a neural simulator within an MBPO-style model-based RL loop that generates synthetic rollouts to augment the replay buffer.

## Results

On action-conditioned scene prediction, GWM outperforms iVideoGPT on both Meta-World (FVD 73.0 vs 75.0, LPIPS 9.0 vs 9.5) and Franka-PNP (FVD 61.5 vs 63.2, LPIPS 4.5 vs 4.9). For model-based RL on six Meta-World tasks, GWM converges approximately 2× faster than iVideoGPT and achieves a 16.25% average improvement in final performance. In real-world Franka PNP experiments, GWM-enhanced diffusion policy reaches 65% success (13/20) versus 35% (7/20) for vanilla diffusion policy, with the gap widening on novel distractors (e.g., 3/5 vs 0/5 on table distractor).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against iVideoGPT [82], GWM wins on all four prediction metrics (FVD, PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) in both synthetic (Meta-World) and real-world (Franka-PNP) settings, and further dominates in model-based RL where it converges ~2× faster and reaches higher asymptotic returns across all six Meta-World tasks (16.25% average improvement). Against BC-Transformer [59] on the 24-task RoboCasa benchmark, GWM achieves an average success-rate gain of 10.5% with 50 human demos and 7.6% with 3000 generated demos, though individual task results are mixed (e.g., +28 on CoffeePress Button H-50, but −6 on PnP CounterToCab G-3000). Against vanilla Diffusion Policy [9] in the real-world Franka PNP task, GWM more than doubles the success rate from 7/20 to 13/20, with the largest margin on the table-distractor condition (3/5 vs 0/5), demonstrating superior generalization to unseen visual perturbations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of image-based world models for robotics (iVideoGPT, diffusion-based pixel-space models) by replacing the 2D latent representation with explicit 3D Gaussian Splatting primitives. It builds on recent feed-forward 3D reconstruction methods (Splatt3R, Mast3R) to obtain Gaussians from unposed images without per-scene optimization, and on Diffusion Transformers with EDM preconditioning for the dynamics model. The 3D Gaussian VAE, inspired by 3D shape VAEs (3DShape2VecSet), bridges the variable-count Gaussian representation to a fixed-length latent space suitable for diffusion-based generation. The key conceptual departure is treating the world state as a set of 3D geometric primitives whose propagation under actions is predicted, rather than predicting pixel-level future frames.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit 3D geometric structure (Gaussian Splatting) provides a consistent and meaningful advantage over 2D image-based world models for manipulation, with the ablation showing the 3D component alone lifts success rate from 4% to 18%. This challenges the implicit assumption that increasingly powerful 2D video generative models are sufficient for world modeling in robotics. Open questions include whether the approach scales to long-horizon, multi-object, or deformable-object manipulation, how sensitive it is to the quality of the feed-forward reconstruction backbone, and whether the 3D representation advantage persists as 2D video models continue to improve in scale and physical fidelity. The real-world evidence, while encouraging (30% improvement), is based on only 20 trials across a single task family.
