## Problem

Methods commonly labeled 'unsupervised' anomaly detection actually assume all training samples are nominal, requiring manual curation that is costly, bias-prone, and unrealistic in industrial settings where undetected anomalies inevitably contaminate training sets. This paper addresses the gap by enabling deep anomaly detection models to learn directly from uncurated data where nominal and anomalous samples coexist, without any explicit labels or filtering step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251793/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (resized to 256×256) from uncurated training sets that may contain a mixture of nominal and anomalous samples, with no labels or filtering required. It produces both an image-level anomaly score (used for I-AUROC) and a pixel-level anomaly score (used for P-AUROC). The framework is model-agnostic: it wraps any gradient-descent-trainable anomaly detection backbone (demonstrated with a normalizing-flow model using pretrained AlexNet features and a CNN discriminator using frozen WideResNet-50 features), applying the same training strategy regardless of the underlying architecture.

## Method

CoMet combines Soft Confident Learning (SCL) with Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning (MAML) in a two-loop training procedure. SCL assigns each training sample a confidence weight w_i = min(1, t/a_θ(x_i)), where t is an IQR-based threshold on anomaly scores, so that samples near or beyond the decision boundary receive lower weights in the loss. Model uncertainty is quantified via the determinant of the covariance matrix between per-sample training and validation losses, yielding an adaptive L2 regularization coefficient λ(Σ) = λ_0(1 + γ·det(Σ)) that strengthens regularization when train–val disagreement is high. In the MAML inner loop, the model is updated on one task subset using the confidence-weighted AD loss plus adaptive regularization; in the outer loop, parameters are updated using validation performance on the remaining tasks, so that parameter updates which generalize across tasks are reinforced. This combination prevents overfitting to boundary or anomalous samples while preserving sensitivity to true anomalies, and the entire procedure is plug-and-play for any differentiable anomaly detection loss.

## Results

On MVTec-AD, CoMet-SN achieves 99.7% I-AUROC and 98.3% P-AUROC, setting a new state-of-the-art and outperforming the SimpleNet baseline (99.6%/98.1%) and PatchCore (99.1%/98.1%). On the more challenging VIADUCT dataset, CoMet-SN reaches 90.3% I-AUROC / 97.0% P-AUROC, surpassing all 12 baselines on average (next best: SimpleNet at 87.1%/92.5%). On KSDD2, CoMet-NF achieves the best I-AUROC of 94.9% / 93.4% P-AUROC, edging out MLD-IR (94.3%/92.6%) and the DifferNet baseline (91.5%/92.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoMet consistently outperforms all 12 baselines across the three benchmarks. Against its direct backbones, CoMet-NF improves DifferNet by +4.3% I-AUROC on MVTec-AD (99.2 vs 94.9), +7.3% on VIADUCT (83.5 vs 76.2), and +3.4% on KSDD2 (94.9 vs 91.5); CoMet-SN improves SimpleNet by +0.1% on MVTec-AD (99.7 vs 99.6), +3.2% on VIADUCT (90.3 vs 87.1), and +0.5% on KSDD2 (92.2 vs 91.7). Against feature-embedding SOTA, CoMet-SN beats PatchCore by +0.6% I-AUROC on MVTec-AD and +7.2% on VIADUCT, and surpasses PaDiM by +3.9% on MVTec-AD. On VIADUCT, CoMet-SN outperforms EfficientAD (+7.7%) and MSFlow (+7.9%) on average I-AUROC. On KSDD2, CoMet-NF edges out MLD-IR (+0.6%) and DSR (+7.7%), while in the noise-robustness experiment (Figure 5), CoMet maintains near-constant I-AUROC up to 10% anomalous contamination in training, whereas IRP and MLD-IR degrade more sharply.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoMet sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning (MAML, Finn et al. 2017), which provides the inner/outer-loop optimization structure, and Confident Learning (Northcutt et al. 2021), whose confident-joint concept is adapted here to an unsupervised setting via IQR-based anomaly-score thresholding. The paper's key conceptual move is reclassifying what the field calls 'unsupervised' anomaly detection as actually semi-supervised (since it assumes all training samples are nominal), then proposing a training strategy that removes this assumption. Unlike prior robustness approaches that iteratively filter or re-weight training data with statistical heuristics (e.g., IRP, MLD-IR), CoMet integrates the re-weighting directly into a meta-learning objective, making the confidence estimation and parameter update jointly optimized rather than sequential.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: in real industrial pipelines, guaranteeing a perfectly clean training set is infeasible, and even a few leaked anomalies can degrade detection. CoMet's model-agnostic design means it can be bolted onto any gradient-trained anomaly detector, which broadens its applicability. However, the performance gains are modest on already-saturated benchmarks (0.1% on MVTec-AD for SimpleNet), and the noise-robustness experiment only tests up to 10% contamination, leaving open how the method behaves at higher anomaly fractions or on datasets with more subtle, near-boundary defects. A key open question is whether the IQR-based confidence threshold generalizes across domains without per-class tuning, and whether the meta-learning overhead is justified when the base model is already near ceiling performance.
