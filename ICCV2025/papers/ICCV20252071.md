## Problem

Previous layout-to-image (L2I) methods are built on UNet-based architectures (SD1.5, SDXL), while Multimodal Diffusion Transformers (MM-DiTs) like SD3 and FLUX.1 have demonstrated superior generation quality but lack layout control. Enabling MM-DiT for L2I is non-trivial because layout must be introduced, integrated, and balanced as a third modality alongside image and text without suffering from 'modality competition' in the attention mechanism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252071/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The generation model takes a global text prompt and a layout condition of N entities, where each entity is a (region caption, bounding box) pair; bounding boxes are encoded via Fourier embedding and concatenated with text-encoder outputs before an MLP produces layout tokens. At inference, layout-conditioned denoising is applied only in the first 30% of diffusion steps. The LayoutDesigner component additionally accepts coarse-grained inputs (center points, masks, scribbles, or a rough idea) and outputs a harmonious set of bounding boxes with region descriptions. Training resolution is 1024×1024 on LayoutSAM and 512×512 on COCO.

## Method

SiamLayout treats layout as an independent modality with its own set of transformer parameters and decouples the three-way M³-Attention into two parallel siamese MM-Attention branches—one for image-text and one for image-layout interaction—then fuses the resulting image tokens by summation, thereby eliminating modality competition. The pre-trained MM-DiT (SD3 or FLUX.1) is frozen; only the newly introduced layout parameters are trained with a region-aware loss that upweights pixels inside bounding boxes and a biased time-step sampling strategy that oversamples large timesteps where structural content is formed. The paper also contributes LayoutSAM (2.7M image-text pairs, 10.7M entities with fine-grained region descriptions derived from the SAM dataset via an automated VLM/LLM annotation pipeline) and LayoutDesigner, a Llama-3.1-8B model fine-tuned with LoRA on 180K layout-design pairs to convert diverse user inputs into optimized bounding-box layouts.

## Results

On the fine-grained open-set LayoutSAM-Eval benchmark, SiamLayout-FLUX achieves 95.67 spatial accuracy, 80.71 color, 83.53 texture, and 82.80 shape, surpassing the previous best (InstanceDiff/HiCo) by +7.68, +11.52, +10.75, and +11.70 points respectively. On the coarse-grained COCO 2017 benchmark, SiamLayout-SD3 reaches 47.8 AP, 33.32 CLIP, 29.27 FID, and 30.26 IS, outperforming all baselines on global quality metrics. On T2I-CompBench, incorporating layout via SiamLayout boosts SD3's spatial adherence from 32.00 to 47.36 and numeracy from 58.34 to 62.15. For layout planning, LayoutDesigner achieves 100% format accuracy across all three input granularities, compared to 76.60–99.96% for GPT-4-Turbo.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On LayoutSAM-Eval, SiamLayout-FLUX dominates all six prior methods (GLIGEN, Ranni, MIGC, InstanceDiff, Be Yourself, HiCo) across every region-wise metric, with the largest gaps over HiCo and InstanceDiff on color (+11.52) and shape (+11.70). On COCO 2017, SiamLayout-SD3 trails InstanceDiff on spatial metrics (AP 47.8 vs. 51.9, AR 42.1 vs. 43.7) but clearly wins on global quality (CLIP 33.32 vs. 30.04, FID 29.27 vs. 29.94, IS 30.26 vs. 27.23), which the authors attribute to InstanceDiff's per-entity generation strategy trading off efficiency for spatial precision. On T2I-CompBench, SiamLayout-SD3 improves over vanilla SD3 on all five compositional dimensions, most notably spatial (47.36 vs. 32.00) and numeracy (62.15 vs. 58.34), while also surpassing DALL·E-3 on spatial and numeracy. For layout planning, LayoutDesigner achieves 100% format accuracy on all three input types (caption, center point, bounding box), substantially outperforming GPT-4-Turbo (76.60% on caption input) and Llama-3.1-8B (50.11%), while also yielding higher IR quality scores (66.46 vs. 60.03 on caption input). The ablation confirms that the siamese decoupling is critical: M³-Attention (which merges all three modalities in one attention) underperforms SiamLayout by 3.5–4.3 points on spatial/attribute metrics due to modality competition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MM-DiT lineage (SD3, FLUX.1), which treats text as an independent modality interacting with image via MM-Attention, by introducing layout as a third modality. Prior layout-to-image methods (GLIGEN, MIGC, InstanceDiff, HiCo) were built on UNet-based architectures and injected layout through cross-attention adapters between self-attention and cross-attention layers; the authors show this paradigm does not transfer cleanly to MM-DiT, where the absence of a dedicated cross-attention slot and the equal-status treatment of modalities create a 'modality competition' problem. The siamese-branch design (decoupling image-text and image-layout into parallel MM-Attention streams) is the paper's core architectural departure, and the LayoutSAM dataset departs from COCO-derived closed-set datasets by providing open-set, fine-grained region descriptions at 10.7M-entity scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of layout control in MM-DiT architectures, which are now the dominant backbone for high-quality text-to-image generation (SD3, FLUX.1, Playground-v3). The LayoutSAM dataset (2.7M pairs, 10.7M entities) and LayoutSAM-Eval benchmark fill a notable gap: prior L2I datasets were either closed-set (COCO) or lacked fine-grained attribute descriptions, limiting evaluation of complex rendering (color, texture, text). The 'modality competition' diagnosis—showing that a newly introduced modality without large-scale pre-training alignment is systematically outcompeted by text in joint attention—provides a useful design principle for future multi-modal diffusion architectures. Open questions include end-to-end integration of layout planning with generation (currently a two-stage LLM + diffusion pipeline), scalability to very large entity counts, and whether the siamese decoupling generalizes beyond bounding-box layouts to other spatial conditioning formats.
