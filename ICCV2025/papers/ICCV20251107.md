## Problem

Existing video generation benchmarks primarily assess static content quality, with dynamic evaluation largely limited to motion smoothness, making it difficult to capture spatiotemporal inconsistencies and violations of physical laws. Moreover, current motion prompts in benchmarks are limited in diversity, covering far fewer motion categories than needed for comprehensive evaluation. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing the first benchmark explicitly designed to evaluate video motion quality from the perspective of human perception alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251107/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes text prompts (1,050 curated prompts spanning six movement modes: fluid dynamics, biological motion, mechanical motion, weather phenomena, collective behavior, and energy transfer) and generates one video per prompt using each of six T2V models, yielding 6,300 total videos. For human-aligned validation, 200 videos per model (1,200 total) are sampled and rated by three domain experts on a 1–5 Likert scale across five dimensions. The evaluation produces five scalar scores per video (CAS, MSS, OIS, PAS, TCS) and is validated via Spearman rank correlation and pairwise preference accuracy against human judgments.

## Method

The core contribution is a five-metric Perception-Driven Motion Metrics (PMM) framework: CAS uses a VideoMAEv2 classifier trained on VideoReward-derived preference labels to score commonsense adherence; MSS detects frame-to-frame quality drops via Q-Align aesthetic scores with scene-aware adaptive thresholds; OIS checks anatomical constraint violations using MMPose keypoints with statistically derived tolerance thresholds; PAS separates subject motion from camera motion via GroundingDINO localization, GroundedSAM masking, and CoTracker keypoint displacement; and TCS detects anomalous object disappearance/reappearance using GroundedSAM2 tracking with CoTracker trajectory analysis and rule-based filtering for legitimate transitions. Complementary to the metrics, the Meta-Guided Motion Prompt Generation (MMPG) pipeline extracts Subject-Place-Action metadata from multiple datasets, generates and iteratively refines prompts with GPT-4o, and validates them through Human-LLM joint review with DeepSeek-R1, producing the most diverse motion prompt set (969 categories) to date.

## Results

On VMBench, PPM (the proposed Perception-Driven Motion Metrics) achieves an average Spearman correlation of 62.2% with human annotations, outperforming the best MLLM baseline (InternVideo2.5 at 26.9%) by 35.3 percentage points and the best rule-based method (Dover Technical at 20.6%) by 41.6 points. PPM leads on all five evaluation dimensions: CAS (69.9%), MSS (77.1%), OIS (65.8%), PAS (65.2%), and TCS (54.5%). In pairwise preference accuracy, PPM achieves 70.6% alignment with human judgments, with ablation showing that removing CAS causes the largest single-metric drop (to 64.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPM decisively outperforms all 12 baselines across every evaluation dimension. Among rule-based methods, Dover Technical [84] is the strongest (20.6% avg ρ) but still trails PPM by 41.6 points; SSIM [82], RAFT [73], and Warping Error [39] produce near-zero or negative correlations, indicating they are fundamentally misaligned with human motion perception. CLIP [59] and DINO [13] show moderate performance on CAS and OIS (21.5% and 11.0% for CAS; 31.7% and 27.4% for OIS) but fail catastrophically on PAS (-42.7% and -45.8%), confirming that feature-similarity metrics cannot capture motion amplitude. AMT [42] (18.1% on MSS) and Warping Error [39] (-19.1% on MSS) demonstrate that interpolation-based and warping-based smoothness measures are unreliable for complex deformations. Among MLLM baselines, InternVideo2.5 [81] leads at 26.9% avg ρ, with its strongest dimension being PAS (44.3%), yet it still lags PPM by 35.3 points on average; LLaVA-NEXT-Video [95], MiniCPM-V-2.6 [91], InternVL2.5 [19], and Qwen2.5-VL [9] all cluster between 15–19% avg ρ, with no single MLLM exceeding 44.3% on any dimension. The consistent gap—PPM exceeding the best MLLM by 2–5× on most dimensions—supports the paper's claim that neither rule-based heuristics nor general-purpose MLLM prompting can substitute for purpose-built, perception-aligned motion metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

VMBench carves new ground at the intersection of video generation evaluation and human perception modeling. Prior benchmarks (VBench, EvalCrafter) assess video quality but limit dynamic evaluation to motion smoothness via optical flow or frame interpolation, while feature-based metrics (FVD, FID) conflate spatial quality with temporal dynamics. This paper repurposes a pipeline of existing perception tools—VideoMAEv2 for commonsense scoring, MMPose for anatomical integrity, GroundingDINO/GroundedSAM/CoTracker for subject-aware motion tracking, and Q-Align for artifact detection—into a unified five-dimensional motion evaluation framework (PMM) explicitly designed to mirror hierarchical human perceptual processing. The prompt generation strategy (MMPG) extends the structured prompt curation of VBench by grounding prompts in physics and cognitive science taxonomies and validating them through LLM-human collaboration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VMBench provides the first standardized, perception-aligned framework for diagnosing motion-specific failures in T2V models, filling a gap that static-content-focused benchmarks leave unaddressed. The finding that state-of-the-art video MLLMs achieve only 10–30% Spearman correlation on most motion dimensions—despite strong general video understanding—challenges the assumption that scaling multimodal models will automatically yield reliable motion quality assessment. However, the absolute alignment of even the best PMM metrics (54–77% correlation) remains moderate, and the authors acknowledge that individual perceptual differences are not captured. Open questions include whether the five dimensions generalize to longer or higher-resolution videos, whether the 1,050-prompt set adequately covers the full motion space, and whether the metrics can serve as differentiable training signals rather than post-hoc evaluation.
