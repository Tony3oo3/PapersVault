## Problem

GaussianImage (GI) achieves fast 2D Gaussian Splatting-based image representation but suffers from two practical limitations: random initialization of Gaussian primitives necessitates lengthy training (50,000+ iterations) to converge, and the fixed number of Gaussians per image cannot adapt to varying information entropy across different images. This paper addresses both issues by proposing a generalizable network that produces a high-quality Gaussian representation in a single feedforward pass, followed by only minimal fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (tested at resolutions from 768×512 on Kodak to 408×1020–1020×1020 on DIV2K×2) and produces a variable-size set of 2D Gaussian primitives, each parameterized by position, 2D covariance (via scaling and rotation), and color (sampled from the image and modulated by predicted opacity). The number of Gaussians is self-adaptive—determined by the image's information entropy via a learned Position Probability Map—rather than being fixed a priori. The final output is a rendered image produced by accumulated blending-based rasterization of the Gaussians, achieving >1000 FPS decoding.

## Method

Instant-GI employs a ConvNeXt-based UNet to extract image features, from which an MLP-based Position Field predicts a Position Probability Map (PPM) indicating where Gaussians should be placed. The PPM is discretized via Floyd-Steinberg dithering over k×k patches, yielding an adaptive number of sampling points whose density reflects local image entropy. Delaunay Triangulation is then applied to these points, and ellipses are fitted to each triangle to provide geometric reference values for scaling and rotation. Dedicated MLP fields (BC Field for barycentric position, Σ Field for scaling/rotation adjustments, Opacity Field for opacity) predict all Gaussian attributes from concatenated geometric and sampled image features. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with differentiable rasterization, supervised by a reconstruction loss (L2 + D-SSIM) and a Focal MSE loss on the PPM, after which only a few fine-tuning steps are needed to match or exceed GI's quality achieved through 50,000 iterations.

## Results

On DIV2K×2, Instant-GI achieves 42.80 PSNR and 0.9982 MS-SSIM with 615.05K parameters, outperforming GI† (same Gaussian count, random init) by 2.54 dB and GI (560K params, full training) by 3.27 dB. On Kodak, the method reaches 42.92 PSNR with 342.86K parameters, surpassing GI† (41.44) by 1.48 dB but trailing fully-trained GI (44.08, 560K params) by 1.16 dB due to the lower resolution reducing the adaptive Gaussian count by 61%. In terms of training speed, network-based initialization achieves 46.68 PSNR at 2 s on image 0844×2, exceeding the 45.90 PSNR that random initialization reaches only after 20 s — a >10× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest baseline GI† (same Gaussian count, random initialization), Instant-GI wins on both datasets: +1.48 dB on Kodak (42.92 vs 41.44) and +2.54 dB on DIV2K×2 (42.80 vs 40.26), confirming that network-based initialization substantially reduces optimization difficulty. Against fully-trained GI (560K Gaussians, 50K iterations), Instant-GI achieves SOTA on DIV2K×2 (42.80 vs 39.53, +3.27 dB) but trails on Kodak (42.92 vs 44.08, −1.16 dB), which the authors attribute to Kodak's lower resolution causing a 61% reduction in the adaptive Gaussian count. Compared to INR-based methods (WIRE, SIREN, I-NGP, NeuRBF), Instant-GI dominates on both datasets in PSNR and MS-SSIM while using fewer parameters than 3DGS (615K vs 4130K on DIV2K×2). The speed advantage is the most striking: at 2 s, network initialization already surpasses random initialization's 20 s result on two of three test images (>10× speedup), and even on the hardest high-entropy image the gap is 5×. Overall, the method trades a small Kodak PSNR deficit (under lower-resolution conditions) for a large DIV2K×2 gain and a one-order-of-magnitude reduction in training time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GaussianImage (GI), which itself adapts 3D Gaussian Splatting to 2D image representation via accumulated blending rasterization. The key departure is replacing GI's purely random Gaussian initialization with a generalizable ConvNeXt-UNet that predicts a Position Probability Map, discretized via Floyd-Steinberg dithering to yield an entropy-adaptive Gaussian count. Geometric structure is further imposed through Delaunay Triangulation and ellipse fitting, drawing on Scaffold-GS's anchor-based prediction philosophy. The work sits at the intersection of INR-based image representation and Gaussian Splatting, specifically addressing GI's two practical limitations: slow convergence from random init and a fixed, non-adaptive number of primitives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~10× reduction in training time (2s vs. 20s+ to reach comparable quality) makes Gaussian-based image representation far more practical for real-time or resource-constrained pipelines, and the entropy-adaptive Gaussian count eliminates the need to manually tune primitive counts per image. However, the method still requires a fine-tuning stage after network initialization, incurs notable CPU overhead from Delaunay Triangulation, and does not surpass GI on lower-resolution images (Kodak). Open questions include whether the CPU bottleneck can be eliminated, whether the approach scales to gigapixel or video representations, and whether the learned PPM generalizes beyond the DIV2K training distribution.
