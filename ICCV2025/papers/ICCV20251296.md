## Problem

Per-point manual labeling for LiDAR Moving Object Segmentation is costly and labor-intensive, while existing self-supervised pre-training approaches based on LiDAR observation forecasting are either unnecessarily complex for MOS or learn noisy implicit 'flow' representations that reduce transferability. The paper asks whether a simpler, geometry-grounded self-supervised objective can produce spatiotemporal representations specifically suited to distinguishing moving from static objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251296/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder consumes a sequence of 6 LiDAR scans (current plus 5 past) at 2 Hz, all transformed into the current scan's local frame via sensor poses, with each point concatenated with its timestamp before sparse 4D convolution. During pre-training, the model outputs 3-class occupancy probabilities (free/occupied/unknown) for pre-computed temporal overlapping points and for sampled points along current beams; during fine-tuning, it outputs a binary per-point moving/static label. The temporal overlapping points themselves are not learned but are geometrically pre-computed from beam intersections using the LiDAR's known divergence angle.

## Method

TOP pre-trains a sparse 4D UNet encoder by predicting the occupancy states of temporal overlapping points—locations along current-scan beams that are also intersected by adjacent-scan beams—where relative sensor/object motion causes the same spatial location to exhibit different occupancy at different times. A key pre-processing step uses the physical beam divergence angle to identify coplanar beam pairs, compute their intersections (a single point when the inter-beam angle exceeds divergence, or a sampled segment when it does not), and assign occupancy labels via an exponential confidence-decay model along each beam. A shallow MLP decoder predicts occupancy from the sum of a sine-cosine 4D positional encoding of the query point and the encoded feature of the corresponding current-scan LiDAR point. The total loss combines a confidence-weighted cross-entropy on overlapping-point predictions with a cross-entropy reconstruction of the current scan's occupancy, which acts as a regularizer. Unlike forecasting-based pre-training, TOP avoids learning an implicit or explicit flow between past and future observations by relying on pre-computed geometric correspondences, yielding cleaner spatiotemporal features that transfer more effectively to few-shot MOS.

## Results

On nuScenes few-shot MOS (10% labeled data), TOP achieves 28.03% Recall_obj and 36.95% IoU_w/o, outperforming the training-from-scratch baseline (24.98% / 36.44%) by +3.04 and +0.51 points respectively. On the cross-dataset SemanticKITTI transfer (10% data), TOP reaches 44.68% Recall_obj and 43.29% IoU_w/o, a +7.46 and +2.07 point gain over the no-pretrain baseline. The paper reports a relative improvement of up to 28.77% over training-from-scratch, and TOP also generalizes to semantic segmentation, surpassing the scratch baseline by 0.42% mIoU on nuScenes with 10% data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TOP consistently outperforms all three self-supervised pre-training baselines (ALSO, 4DOcc, UnO) on both Recall_obj and IoU_w/o across every data fraction on nuScenes. On the 10% nuScenes setting, TOP leads UnO (the closest competitor) by +1.65 points in Recall_obj (28.03 vs 26.38) and +0.39 in IoU_w/o (36.95 vs 36.56). The gap widens dramatically on the cross-dataset SemanticKITTI transfer: on 10% data, TOP achieves 44.68% Recall_obj while ALSO and 4DOcc collapse to 9.21% and 8.54% respectively, and UnO reaches only 36.66%. On the 20% SemanticKITTI setting, TOP's Recall_obj of 58.45% exceeds UnO (45.48%) by 13 points, confirming that forecasting-based pre-training (4DOcc, UnO) learns noisy 'flow' representations that do not transfer well, whereas TOP's simpler occupancy-prediction objective yields more robust spatiotemporal features. On the auxiliary semantic segmentation task, TOP also beats UnO (50.97 vs 48.06 mIoU) and 4DOcc (50.97 vs 47.49 mIoU), further supporting the claim that avoiding explicit flow learning improves generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

TOP departs from the dominant line of LiDAR self-supervised pre-training that relies on observation forecasting (4DOcc, UnO) by arguing that predicting future scans is unnecessarily complex for moving object segmentation and introduces noisy 'flow' representations that hurt transferability. It repurposes the LiDAR occupancy measurement formalism from UnO/ALSO but redirects it toward a simpler, geometry-grounded objective: predicting occupancy states of temporally overlapping points identified via beam-divergence-aware coplanarity. The sparse 4D UNet backbone and the overall pre-then-fine-tune paradigm follow established MOS practice (Mersch et al.), while the temporal overlapping prediction loss and the dual-metric evaluation (Recall_obj, IoU_w/o) are novel contributions that carve out a more targeted pre-training signal for dynamic perception.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TOP demonstrates that a deliberately simpler pre-training objective can outperform more complex forecasting-based methods, particularly in cross-dataset transfer where forecasting baselines degrade catastrophically (e.g., ALSO drops from 37.22 to 9.21 Recall_obj on SemanticKITTI 10%). This challenges the implicit assumption that richer generative pre-training always yields better representations for downstream tasks. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other temporal tasks (tracking, scene flow), whether the beam-divergence pre-processing generalizes to non-Velodyne sensors with different divergence profiles, and whether the gains persist at full-data scale where the annotation bottleneck is less acute. The proposed evaluation metrics (Recall_obj, IoU_w/o) also address a real gap in how MOS performance is reported, as conventional IoU is heavily biased by ego-vehicle points and large objects.
