## Problem

Unified Multimodal LLMs (MLLMs) that jointly handle understanding and generation fail at Text-to-Image In-Context Learning (T2I-ICL), where models must infer implicit patterns (e.g., a shared style or attribute) from interleaved text-image demonstrations and apply them to a new query. Existing models produce disorganized reasoning or simply ignore contextual relationships, resulting in generated images that miss key compositional elements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252236/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a sequence of interleaved text-image demonstration pairs followed by a text query (e.g., 'a leather-bound book' + image, 'a leather apple' + image, then query 'a box'). Inference is explicitly two-stage: the model first outputs a free-form reasoning chain (ImageGen-CoT) that articulates the inferred pattern, then the original input is concatenated with this chain plus a mandatory <image> token to force image generation. This separation is necessary because single-pass prompting frequently fails to produce the image at all.

## Method

The framework introduces ImageGen-CoT, a reasoning chain generated before image synthesis, and an automated dataset construction pipeline where an MLLM iteratively acts as Generator (producing N CoT+prompt pairs), Selector (picking the best image from FLUX.1-schnell outputs), Critic (assessing image-prompt alignment), and Refiner (revising the prompt) until a quality threshold is met. The collected (CoT, image) pairs are used to fine-tune unified MLLMs (SEED-X, SEED-LLaMA) with standard language-modeling loss on the CoT generation split and MSE loss on visual embeddings for the image generation split. At inference, a hybrid test-time scaling strategy generates multiple diverse CoT chains via high-temperature decoding and then samples multiple images per chain, achieving bidirectional expansion across both comprehension and generation dimensions that outperforms single-axis scaling (varying only seeds or only chains).

## Results

On CoBSAT, SEED-X fine-tuned with the ImageGen-CoT dataset achieves an average score of 0.658, an 88.5% relative improvement over the SEED-X baseline (0.349). On DreamBench++, the same model reaches a CP·PF score of 0.403, a 114.4% gain over the SEED-X baseline (0.188). With the proposed Hybrid test-time scaling strategy (N=16), performance further improves to 0.909 on CoBSAT and 0.543 on DreamBench++. SEED-LLaMA shows more modest gains: 0.291 on CoBSAT (+14.6%) and 0.101 on DreamBench++ (+29.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two unified MLLM baselines: SEED-LLaMA (discrete visual tokens) and SEED-X (continuous visual embeddings). On CoBSAT, SEED-X fine-tuned with ImageGen-CoT (0.658) substantially outperforms both the SEED-X baseline (0.349, +88.5%) and the SEED-LLaMA fine-tuned variant (0.291). On DreamBench++, the gap is even wider: SEED-X + ImageGen-CoT FT (0.403) surpasses the SEED-X baseline (0.188, +114.4%) and SEED-LLaMA + ImageGen-CoT FT (0.101) by a large margin. Notably, SEED-X also outperforms its own GT-image fine-tuned variant (0.592) by 11.1% on CoBSAT, indicating that the ImageGen-CoT dataset is more effective than ground-truth images for training. The larger gains on SEED-X versus SEED-LLaMA are attributed to SEED-X's stronger base comprehension, which allows it to leverage the reasoning chain more effectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the chain-of-thought reasoning paradigm from NLP into the text-to-image generation domain, specifically for the T2I-ICL task established by CoBSAT and DreamBench. It builds on unified MLLMs (SEED-X, SEED-LLaMA) that already handle interleaved text-image input, but departs from prior work by inserting an explicit reasoning chain before image synthesis and by constructing a dedicated fine-tuning dataset via an automated Generator-Selector-Critic-Refiner pipeline. The test-time scaling section draws on NLP's Best-of-N paradigm but extends it to a bidirectional setting where both reasoning diversity and generation diversity are scaled simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that explicit reasoning before image generation yields 80%+ gains on T2I-ICL provides a practical recipe for improving unified MLLMs without architectural changes, and the validated hypothesis that 'better understanding leads to better generation' offers a clear diagnostic for future multimodal generation work. However, the approach relies on an external diffusion model (FLUX) for dataset construction and a two-stage inference protocol, raising questions about whether the gains transfer to end-to-end single-pass generation. Open questions include whether ImageGen-CoT generalizes beyond in-context learning to broader compositional generation, and whether the hybrid scaling strategy's marginal advantage over single-axis scaling persists at larger N or on more diverse tasks.
