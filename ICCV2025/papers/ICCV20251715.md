## Problem

Generating temporally coherent 3D hand motion from monocular video remains challenging, especially during hand-object interactions with severe occlusions. Existing motion refinement methods learn deterministic mappings from initial frame-wise estimates to refined sequences, which cannot capture the inherent uncertainty in noisy image-based predictions, and they largely ignore the physical principles that govern natural hand-object interaction dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251715/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of T=16 video frames and their corresponding per-frame 3D hand estimates (MANO pose θ∈R^{15×3} and shape β∈R^{10} parameters) produced by any frame-wise reconstruction model. It outputs a refined 3D hand motion sequence as MANO parameters, trained exclusively on motion capture data without any image supervision. At inference, the model is agnostic to the upstream frame-wise method and can be plugged in as a post-processing refinement stage.

## Method

The core contribution is a conditional diffusion model that learns p(x_{1:T}|y_{1:T}) rather than a deterministic mapping, using a residual-shifting forward process that progressively transforms clean motion toward initial estimates via their residuals plus Gaussian noise. The reverse denoising network combines a MeshCNN for spatial hand geometry features, a 4-layer Transformer for temporal dependencies, and autoregressive prediction where each frame's estimate conditions on all prior predictions. The key differentiator is the integration of intuitive physics: the model predicts four discrete hand-object interaction states (reaching, stable grasping, manipulation, releasing) via a Gumbel-Softmax layer, and enforces state-specific constraints—a least-kinetic-motion loss penalizing direction reversals during reaching/releasing and a stability loss penalizing finger joint variation during stable grasping. Training uses randomly perturbed ground-truth motion as initial estimates, enabling plug-and-play compatibility with any frame-wise reconstruction method.

## Results

On HO3Dv2 (zero-shot, trained only on DexYCB), the method achieves P-MJE 8.0 mm, P-MVE 8.3 mm, F@5 59.7%, and F@15 97.6%, outperforming all video-based baselines including Deformer (P-MJE 9.4 mm) and VIBE (P-MJE 9.9 mm). On DexYCB, when refining HaMer's initial estimates, the method reduces MJE from 18.9 mm to 17.5 mm and ACCL from 7.95 to 1.01 mm/frame², substantially surpassing PoseBERT (MJE 18.0 mm, ACCL 2.38 mm/frame²). When refining K-Hand, MJE drops from 24.4 mm to 21.5 mm and ACCL from 14.41 to 1.17 mm/frame², again outperforming PoseBERT (MJE 22.3 mm, ACCL 3.33 mm/frame²).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PoseBERT (the closest motion-refinement competitor), the method wins on every metric for both frame-wise backbones: on HaMer it improves MJE by 0.5 mm (17.5 vs 18.0) and ACCL by 2.37 mm/frame² (1.01 vs 2.38); on K-Hand it improves MJE by 0.8 mm (21.5 vs 22.3) and ACCL by 2.16 mm/frame² (1.17 vs 3.33). Against video-based methods on HO3Dv2, the method beats Deformer by 1.4 mm in P-MJE (8.0 vs 9.4) and 1.7% in F@5 (59.7 vs 54.6), and outperforms VIBE, TCMR, and TempCLR by 1.9–3.4 mm in P-MJE. The method also surpasses the plain HaMer frame-wise baseline by 0.1 mm in P-MJE and 1.7% in F@5, confirming that the diffusion refinement adds value even on top of a strong per-frame model. Heuristic baselines (Gaussian smoothing, constant-acceleration loss) are clearly inferior: SmoothFilter worsens MJE to 25.3 mm while Constant Accl. Loss only reaches 21.7 mm, versus 21.5 mm for the full method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the motion refinement paradigm established by PoseBERT [4] and PhysPT [75], which learn deterministic Transformer mappings from noisy frame-wise estimates to clean motion sequences. The key departure is replacing the deterministic mapping with a conditional diffusion model that captures the full posterior p(x|y), following the residual-shifting formulation of Reshift [72] and the clean-state prediction strategy of Human Motion Diffusion [55]. Additionally, it draws on the intuitive-physics line of work for human body reconstruction [56, 60] but adapts it to the hand domain by identifying four discrete interaction states (reaching, stable grasping, manipulation, releasing) and encoding them as differentiable training constraints rather than as a separate physics simulator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design—train once on motion capture data, then refine outputs from any frame-wise model—addresses the persistent data-scarcity bottleneck for video-based hand reconstruction and makes the approach broadly applicable. The zero-shot transfer from DexYCB to HO3Dv2 (outperforming methods trained on HO3Dv2) suggests the physics priors provide genuine generalization beyond dataset-specific patterns. Open questions include whether the four-state taxonomy and two constraint losses generalize to bimanual interactions, tool use, or non-rigid objects, and whether richer contact dynamics (friction, compliance) could be incorporated without requiring per-object modeling. The self-defined KIN and STA metrics, while useful for ablation, limit independent verification of the physics claims.
