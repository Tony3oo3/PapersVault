## Problem

Despite OpenAI's CLIP being the default vision backbone for multimodal foundation models, its training data and recipe remain proprietary, it is available in only two parameter scales, and it exhibits documented weaknesses in spatial reasoning and object counting. No fully open, from-scratch vision encoder of comparable capacity has consistently matched or surpassed CLIP when integrated into multimodal frameworks like LLaVA, leaving a critical gap in transparency and flexibility for the research community.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250957/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The vision encoder consumes RGB images at resolutions ranging from 84×84 to 384×384 pixels, tokenized via patches of size 8 or 16, and outputs a sequence of visual feature embeddings (one per patch). After pre-training, the text tower and auxiliary decoder are discarded, leaving only the vision backbone whose output tokens are projected through a lightweight MLP and concatenated with language tokens for downstream MLLM inference. The released family spans 5.9M to 632.1M parameters, enabling deployment from edge devices to high-capacity servers.

## Method

OpenVision builds on the CLIPS framework, which extends standard CLIP's two-tower contrastive architecture with a multi-positive loss (treating both original and LLM-generated synthetic captions as positives) and a jointly trained auxiliary text decoder for generative caption prediction. Pre-training uses Recap-DataComp-1B, a billion-scale corpus re-captioned with LLaVA+Llama-3, under a three-stage progressive resolution curriculum (e.g., 84→224→336/384 for large models) with 12.8B, 1.024B, and 256M image-text pairs per stage. Ablations reveal three key insights: (1) the auxiliary decoder's generative objective provides essential semantic supervision beyond contrastive learning alone, (2) high-quality synthetic captions substantially outperform noisy web-crawled originals, and (3) progressive resolution training achieves better multimodal performance at roughly 3× less pre-training compute than high-resolution training from scratch.

## Results

Under the LLaVA-1.5 framework, OpenVision-L/14-336 achieves 61.2 on TextVQA, 15.7 on ChartQA, 339 on OCR-Bench, and 87.2 on POPE, outperforming OpenAI-CLIP-L/14-336 (59.1, 13.8, 201, 85.7) across nearly all multimodal benchmarks. The larger OpenVision-SoViT-400M/14-384 variant reaches 62.4 TextVQA, 16.1 ChartQA, and 357 OCR, matching or exceeding SigLIP-SoViT-400M/14-384 (62.6, 14.5, 338) on most tasks. Under the higher-compute Open-LLaVA-Next setting, OpenVision-L/14-336 attains 68.3 TextVQA, 68.0 ChartQA, 547 OCR, and 45.3 MMVet, surpassing OpenAI-CLIP-L/14-336 on OCR and MMVet while remaining competitive on TextVQA (68.3 vs. 69.4). The family spans 5.9M to 632.1M parameters, with the Tiny variant retaining 87% of CLIP-L/14's average score at ~50× fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OpenAI-CLIP, OpenVision wins decisively on multimodal tasks: at L/14-336, it gains +2.1 on TextVQA, +1.9 on ChartQA, +138 on OCR, and +1.5 on POPE, while also leading on MME, SEED, MMVet, and GQA; however, it trails on CLIP-Bench ImageNet classification (78.8 vs. 76.6 is actually a win, but retrieval is far ahead at 55.9/75.2 vs. 37.1/57.9). Versus SigLIP-SoViT-400M/14-384, OpenVision is competitive but slightly behind on TextVQA (62.4 vs. 62.6) and SQA (72.4 vs. 76.7), while clearly winning on ChartQA (+1.6), OCR (+19), GQA (+0.5), and POPE (+1.0). Against the open-source CLIP variants (LAION-2B-CLIP, DataComp-1B-CLIP, DFN-2B-CLIP), OpenVision-L/14-224 dominates on nearly every multimodal benchmark—for example, it leads DFN-2B-CLIP by +4.5 on TextVQA and +69 on OCR—despite DFN-2B-CLIP achieving higher ImageNet classification (81.4 vs. 78.4). Compared to MetaCLIP-5B, OpenVision-L/14-224 is competitive, winning on OCR (+2), GQA (+1.6), and POPE (+1.0) but trailing on SQA (73.6 vs. 78.0) and MMVet (35.2 vs. 34.6 is actually a win). The paper's key finding is that CLIP-Bench metrics (classification, retrieval) do not predict multimodal performance: DFN-2B-CLIP has the best ImageNet score (81.4) yet underperforms OpenVision on most downstream VQA tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

OpenVision builds directly on the CLIPS training framework (multi-positive contrastive loss with an auxiliary text decoder) and the Recap-DataComp-1B re-captioned dataset, while adopting the progressive-resolution curriculum from CLIPA. It departs from prior open-source CLIP efforts (OpenCLIP, DataComp-CLIP, DFN-CLIP) that achieved strong zero-shot retrieval/classification but underperformed in multimodal MLLM settings, by explicitly optimizing for downstream multimodal quality rather than CLIP-benchmark scores. The paper positions itself as the first fully-open (data, code, recipe) vision encoder family that matches or exceeds proprietary CLIP/SigLIP when integrated into LLaVA-style multimodal models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By releasing 25+ checkpoints spanning 5.9M–632.1M parameters with full training transparency, OpenVision removes the dependency on proprietary encoders for multimodal research and enables edge-deployable vision backbones. The finding that standard CLIP benchmarks (ImageNet accuracy, MSCOCO retrieval) poorly predict multimodal MLLM performance challenges the community's default evaluation practice and suggests new evaluation criteria are needed. Open questions include whether the specific Recap-DataComp-1B captions are truly necessary versus other high-quality synthetic sources, and whether the auxiliary decoder's benefit persists at much larger scales or with different LLM backbones.
