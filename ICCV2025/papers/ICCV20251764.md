## Problem

Denoising diffusion models have become the state of the art for image synthesis, yet interactive, localized editing workflows remain underdeveloped. Existing DM-based editing methods lack intuitive non-destructive layering (a cornerstone of tools like Photoshop), struggle with seamless blending in sequential edits, and are too slow for real-time creative iteration. This paper addresses the gap by enabling professional-style, layer-based, non-destructive editing at interactive speeds using standard diffusion models without any additional training.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (512×512), a text edit prompt, a soft mask (values in [0,1]^{H×W}) specifying the edit region, a random seed for stochastic variation, and a strength parameter α ∈ [0,1]. For real images, the input is first converted to a noisy latent via Direct Inversion. The output is an edited image plus a self-contained layer parameter tuple (seed, mask, strength, cached regeneration and blending latents, prompt, denoising step count, visibility flag) that fully defines the edit non-destructively, allowing it to be toggled, reordered, or deleted without re-running the full diffusion process.

## Method

LDB is a training-free framework that reuses intermediate denoising states from the initial generation to reduce each edit to only n=8 steps (vs. N=25 for full generation), achieving 140 ms per edit on consumer GPUs. The core mechanism caches two intermediate latents: a regeneration latent Z_r at step r=N−n (partially denoised, retaining global structure but malleable for edits) and a blending latent Z_b at step b=N−2 (more refined, used for seamless background integration). To perform an edit, new noise scaled to match Z_r's variance is injected into Z_r under the mask and strength α, then denoised for n steps; at step b, the result is blended with Z_b using the mask to preserve unedited regions. Each layer is formalized as a self-contained parameter tuple, and for overlapping regions the regeneration latent is recomputed by inverting the previous layer's output, ensuring correct propagation of edits across the stack. This design enables stacking, toggling, and deletion of layers without cross-interference, with recomputation of affected layers completing in milliseconds to seconds thanks to the cached latents.

## Results

On PIE-Bench, LDB achieves the best scores in six of nine metrics: Image Reward (86.02 vs. 72.77 for BrushNet), Aesthetic Score (6.51 vs. 6.17), PSNR (184.57 vs. 181.58), LPIPS (1.91 vs. 3.89), CLIP Score (31.66 vs. 31.50), and inference time (0.25 s vs. 1.41–13.44 s for baselines). On MagicBrush, LDB leads in IR (7.74 vs. −0.04 for BrushNet), AS (5.74), and PSNR (12.85). LDB is 53× faster than BrushNet (0.25 s vs. 7.51 s per edit on an RTX 4090), and with as few as n=4 denoising steps achieves 140 ms latency while maintaining reasonable quality (HPS 0.34, CS-D 0.35).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LDB decisively outperforms all five baselines on speed, completing each edit in 0.25–0.26 s versus 1.41 s (BLD), 1.72–1.84 s (IP2P/SDI), 7.49–7.51 s (BrushNet), and 12.85–13.44 s (HDP). On PIE-Bench, LDB leads in Image Reward (86.02 vs. 72.77 for BrushNet, the next best), Aesthetic Score (6.51 vs. 6.17), PSNR (184.57 vs. 181.58 for SDI), LPIPS (1.91 vs. 3.89 for SDI), and global CLIP Score (31.66 vs. 31.50 for BrushNet). However, BrushNet narrowly edges LDB on HPS (26.66 vs. 26.60) and masked-region CLIP (CS-L: 22.80 vs. 22.76), while BLD achieves a slightly higher CS-D (17.22 vs. 16.74). On MagicBrush, LDB wins on IR (7.74 vs. −0.04 for BrushNet) and PSNR (12.85 vs. 12.64 for BLD), but BrushNet leads on CS (31.16 vs. 31.04), CS-L (22.17 vs. 22.07), and CS-D (12.92 vs. 9.54), and BLD marginally wins on HPS (22.80 vs. 22.65). Overall, LDB trades small (<0.1) deficits in a few text-alignment sub-metrics for large gains in image quality, background preservation, and a 30–50× speed advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

LDB builds directly on the Latent Diffusion Model (Stable Diffusion) architecture and adapts the mask-guided latent injection technique from Blended Latent Diffusion, but repositions it within a non-destructive, layer-based editing paradigm that prior DM editing methods lack. The latent caching strategy is adapted from Approximate Caching for T2I serving, repurposed here to reduce each edit to a handful of denoising steps rather than a full forward pass. Unlike prior layered editing approaches (LayeringDiff, Collage Diffusion, Text2Live) that require per-image training or assume pre-layered inputs, LDB defines layers as self-contained parameter sets (seed, mask, strength, prompt) operating on a shared cached latent, enabling order-agnostic stacking without cross-interference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 53× speedup over BrushNet (140 ms per edit on consumer hardware) makes interactive, iterative diffusion-based editing practically viable for creative workflows, a regime previously limited to 1–2 edits per minute. The training-free, plug-and-play design means the technique can be dropped into existing LDM pipelines without retraining, and the demonstrated extension to video editing (SVD) suggests broader applicability. Open questions include whether adaptive strength/step-count tuning can eliminate the remaining manual parameter coupling, how the approach handles semantically implausible edits (e.g., placing objects in physically inconsistent locations), and whether the layered paradigm scales to very large numbers of overlapping edits without quality degradation.
