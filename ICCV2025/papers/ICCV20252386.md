## Problem

Existing multimodal models rely on global scene representations and predefined sound categories, making them unable to distinguish the subtle acoustic differences produced by different objects in an interaction (e.g., a spoon hitting hardwood versus carpet). This paper introduces the sounding object detection task—predicting which specific object in a scene is producing a given interaction sound—and a manually annotated benchmark to evaluate it, filling the gap between coarse audiovisual localization and fine-grained, object-centric sound understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252386/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes video frames, an audio waveform, a language narration, and binary object segmentation masks (computed automatically via hand-object interaction detection + SAM2 for training, or manually annotated for evaluation). At inference, a pool of candidate object masks is generated by prompting OWLv2 with 521 Ego4D taxonomy nouns and segmenting with SAM2; the model outputs a per-object cosine similarity score (top-1 for sounding object detection) or a binary sounding/non-sounding decision (sounding action discovery). Notably, the segmentation mask is applied at the patch-embedding level rather than to raw pixels, preserving background context through self-attention before masking.

## Method

The visual encoder is a slot attention model (7 slots, pretrained on COCO) with frozen encoder and slots but a trainable decoder, providing an object-centric prior; audio uses a pretrained AST encoder and language uses frozen CLIP. Object-aware masking patchifies the segmentation mask and applies it element-wise to patch embeddings, then mean-pools only the non-zero (object) features into a single visual vector. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) an align stage using symmetric InfoNCE contrastive loss across all three modality pairs, (2) a refine stage adding the MC3 consensus coding loss that forces pairwise modality scores to agree, and (3) a finetune stage introducing hard negatives sampled from 50% of non-object background regions of the same clip, which explicitly penalizes the model for attending to irrelevant areas. This hard-negative finetuning is the key mechanism that converts the global contrastive representation into a localized, object-discriminative one.

## Results

On the primary sounding object detection task, the proposed method achieves 49.6% top-1 accuracy on Epic Kitchens and 43.9% on Ego4D, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art SoundingActions [6] by 8.6 and 8.8 percentage points respectively. On the secondary sounding action discovery task, the method reaches 0.617 AUC-ROC (AV) and 0.630 AUC-ROC (AL), surpassing SoundingActions (0.592 / 0.616) and zero-shot multimodal models ImageBind and LanguageBind (≤0.551 AUC-ROC). Ablations confirm that both object-aware masking and the slot attention encoder contribute independently, with their combination yielding the best performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines on both datasets for sounding object detection. Against the closest competitor SoundingActions [6] (same MC3 pretraining loss and data), the object-aware framework gains 8.6 pp on Epic Kitchens (49.6 vs 41.0) and 8.8 pp on Ego4D (43.9 vs 35.1), demonstrating the value of localized object-centric representations over global features. Among zero-shot audiovisual localization methods, SSLAlign [34] is the strongest (39.5 / 33.9), followed by SLAVC [28] (34.8 / 33.0 zero-shot; 35.8 / 31.5 finetuned) and DenseAV [20] (26.4 / 29.7), all of which trail the proposed method by 10+ points. On sounding action discovery, the method improves over SoundingActions by 2.5 pp AUC-ROC (AV) and 1.4 pp (AL), while zero-shot ImageBind [16] and LanguageBind [47] remain near chance level (≤0.551 AUC-ROC), confirming that task-specific contrastive training with object-aware masking is essential for this subtle discrimination task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SoundingActions framework [6], which introduced the MC3 contrastive-consensus loss for learning action-sound correspondences from egocentric videos, by adding an object-aware visual encoding pipeline. Where SoundingActions and prior multimodal methods (ImageBind, LanguageBind) operate on global scene representations, this work introduces segmentation-mask-guided patch selection and a pretrained slot attention encoder to enforce an object-centric prior. It also draws on the audiovisual localization literature (SLAVC, SSLAlign, DenseAV) but deliberately reframes the problem: rather than predicting continuous spatial boundaries, the model selects from a discrete set of candidate objects, shifting the focus from localization geometry to semantic object-sound association.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces a well-motivated new task (sounding object detection) with a manually annotated benchmark, filling a gap between global sounding action discovery and pixel-level audiovisual localization. The automatic mask annotation pipeline (HOI detection + SAM2) is practical and could be reused for other object-centric audiovisual tasks. However, absolute performance remains modest (~50% top-1), the benchmark is small (~1.2K sounding clips), and the two-object-per-interaction assumption limits generality. Open questions include whether the approach scales to scenes with many candidate objects, non-hand-mediated interactions, and whether the object prior from slot attention transfers to 3D or multi-view settings.
