## Problem

Existing dataset condensation methods assume clean, well-curated labels, but real-world datasets inevitably contain noisy labels that cause severe performance degradation in the condensed synthetic set. Two-stage approaches that apply noise-cleaning before condensation are resource-intensive, incomplete, and risk discarding valuable information. This paper presents the first end-to-end dataset condensation method that is robust to label noise without requiring separate preprocessing.

## Input / Output

The method takes a large RGB image dataset (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64) with potentially corrupted labels and a target images-per-class (IPC) budget, and produces a small synthetic dataset of learned image parameters with fixed, clean labels. The synthetic images are optimized as learnable tensors rather than generated by a separate model, and the method is modular: it can be plugged on top of any existing condensation framework (IDM, Acc-DD, MTT, DATM) by adding the GMC loss to the condensation objective. Evaluation trains a classifier from scratch on the condensed set and measures test accuracy on the clean test split.

## Method

RDC integrates supervised contrastive learning (SupCon) into the condensation loop, exploiting the fact that synthetic labels are fixed ground truth to enforce class-wise feature separation as implicit regularization. The key novelty is Golden MixUp Contrast (GMC): a golden set is extracted from the noisy dataset by fitting a bimodal GMM to log-transformed accumulated losses (identifying clean samples) and relabeling high-confidence mislabeled samples (softmax > 0.95), then MixUp is applied between synthetic and golden-set images. The contrastive loss uses bidirectional alignment—positive/negative pairs are defined once relative to the synthetic label and once relative to the golden-set label, weighted by the MixUp coefficient—preserving consistency between mixed-synthetic and mixed-real representations. Additionally, the base model is trained with a semi-supervised objective (DivideMix + cross-entropy on the golden set) to extract noise-resilient features. The final condensation objective is the sum of the base method's condensation loss and the GMC loss, making RDC a drop-in enhancement for any existing condensation pipeline.

## Results

On noisy CIFAR-10 (10 Img/Cls, ResNet18), RDC achieves 41.35% accuracy under 40% asymmetric noise, substantially outperforming the base IDM method (30.61%) and the two-stage DivideMix+IDM approach (33.29%). On CIFAR-100 (10 Img/Cls), RDC reaches 26.73% on clean data and 15.60% under 40% asymmetric noise, compared to 18.94% and 12.42% for IDM respectively. Notably, RDC even surpasses the clean-dataset accuracy of the base method in several settings (e.g., 47.28% vs. 45.10% on clean CIFAR-10), attributed to the regularization effect of supervised contrastive learning. Results are consistent on Tiny-ImageNet, where RDC achieves 11.09% under 40% asymmetric noise (10 Img/Cls) versus 6.38% for IDM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RDC is compared against three baselines: (1) the base condensation method IDM, (2) a two-stage approach that first applies DivideMix for noise cleaning before condensation, and (3) random image selection. RDC outperforms IDM across all noise types and levels on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, with the largest gains under heavy asymmetric noise (e.g., +10.74 pp on CIFAR-10 at 40% asymmetric, +3.18 pp on CIFAR-100). Against the two-stage DivideMix+IDM baseline, RDC wins in most settings, most notably at 40% asymmetric noise where the two-stage approach fails to recover (33.29% vs. 41.35% on CIFAR-10), demonstrating that end-to-end integration of noise handling is superior to separate preprocessing. On CIFAR-100, RDC shows the most dramatic improvement over both baselines (e.g., 26.73% vs. 18.94% for IDM and 18.97% for two-stage on clean data), reflecting the greater challenge of condensing 100 classes. RDC also generalizes to the Acc-DD gradient-matching base, where it achieves 60.99% on clean CIFAR-10 (10 Img/Cls), exceeding Acc-DD's clean accuracy of 57.18%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of dataset condensation and noisy-label learning, building directly on two condensation backbones (IDM for distribution matching and Acc-DD for gradient matching) and the supervised contrastive learning framework of Khosla et al. The key conceptual departure is from prior contrastive approaches like MixCo, which the authors show fails under label noise by enforcing contrast on incorrect labels; their golden MixUp contrast (GMC) instead constructs a verified 'golden set' via GMM-based loss clustering and high-confidence relabeling before mixing. The semi-supervised training component borrows from DivideMix, but the critical novelty is integrating all noise-handling steps end-to-end within the condensation loop rather than as a separate preprocessing stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical gap: real-world datasets inevitably contain label noise, yet all prior condensation methods assume clean labels, making them unreliable in deployment. The finding that an end-to-end approach outperforms the natural two-stage alternative (DivideMix cleaning → condensation) at high noise levels (40% asymmetric) suggests that noise handling must be co-optimized with the condensation objective rather than treated as a preprocessing step. Limitations include evaluation only on CIFAR-10/100 and Tiny-ImageNet with relatively shallow backbones (ResNet18, VGG11, ConvNet), leaving open whether the GMM-based golden set selection and the contrastive regularization scale to ImageNet-level datasets or deeper transformers. The plug-in design (compatible with any condensation method) increases its practical utility, but the reliance on a fixed confidence threshold (0.95) for relabeling may not transfer cleanly across domains.
