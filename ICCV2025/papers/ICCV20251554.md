## Problem

Weakly-supervised audio-visual video parsing (AVVP) suffers from two intertwined limitations: noisy pseudo-labels in the LLP dataset prevent models from learning diverse segment-level event combinations, and Transformer-based backbones struggle with the ~2000-token visual sequences while also failing to model single-frame spatial structure effectively. Additionally, cross-modal interaction often introduces noise from the irrelevant modality rather than providing complementary information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251554/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

A 10-second video is decoded at 8 fps into 10 non-overlapping segments; audio features (128-dim) are extracted by a frozen VGGish and visual features (2048-dim) by frozen ResNet-152 and R(2+1)D. The model outputs per-segment one-hot predictions over 25 event categories for each of the three label types (audio, visual, audio-visual), which are then aggregated into event-level predictions with temporal boundaries. As an auxiliary input, frozen CLIP/CLAP text encoders process natural-language captions derived from pseudo-labels to provide semantic constraints on the predictions.

## Method

MUG combines a data-augmentation strategy (CMRC) with a Mamba-Transformer architecture. CMRC manually corrects obviously wrong pseudo-labels from VALOR, then randomly pairs the visual track of one video with the audio track of another, assigning the union of unimodal pseudo-labels as the new label; this is applied in five batches (1,585–12,096 samples) to balance data quality against overfitting. The AV-Mamba network processes features through four stages: (1) Mamba-based Attention (MBA), a CBAM-like module implemented with Mamba blocks to enhance per-segment feature expressiveness; (2) Adaptive Mamba Fusion (AMF), which runs forward, backward, and dynamic-start SSM scans with the state-transition matrix B shared across modalities to capture cross-modal similarity while preserving unimodal independence; (3) Mamba Feature Enhancement (MFE), which performs element-wise cross-modal multiplication followed by sigmoid-gated channel enhancement to amplify shared features and suppress modality-specific noise; and (4) a Pseudo-Label Semantic Interaction Module (PLSIM) that fuses CLIP/CLAP text embeddings with audio/visual features via learned scale-and-bias MLPs to constrain unimodal predictions. A final HAN (Transformer) block captures long-range spatial dependencies, and the entire system is trained end-to-end with AdamW (lr 3e-4, batch 64, 20 epochs) under weak video-level supervision.

## Results

On the LLP dataset, MUG achieves state-of-the-art performance across all segment-level and event-level F1 metrics. At the segment level, MUG scores 65.4 (Audio), 66.5 (Visual), 59.9 (AV), 63.9 (Type@AV), and 64.7 (Event@AV); at the event level, it scores 59.5 (Audio), 63.9 (Visual), 55.3 (AV), 59.6 (Type@AV), and 57.7 (Event@AV). Compared to the previous SOTA CoLeaF, MUG gains +2.1% on Visual Segment-level, +1.2% on Audio Segment-level, +2.2% on Event@AV Segment-level, and +2.1% on Event@AV Event-level. The model uses only 7.6M parameters, substantially fewer than the 19.3M-parameter Transformer variant while outperforming it on every metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MUG outperforms all ten baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest competitor CoLeaF (ECCV'25), MUG leads by +2.1% on Visual Segment-level F1 (66.5 vs. 64.4), +1.2% on Audio Segment-level (65.4 vs. 64.2), +2.2% on Event@AV Segment-level (64.7 vs. 62.5), and +2.1% on Event@AV Event-level (57.7 vs. 55.6). Against VALOR (NeurIPS'23), which serves as the direct architectural baseline, MUG improves Visual Segment-level by +0.6% and Audio Segment-level by +3.6%, with the largest gap on Event-level Audio (+4.1%). The older HAN baseline (ECCV'20) trails MUG by 5.3 points on Visual Segment-level and 11.7 points on Event-level Audio, reflecting the cumulative gains from pseudo-labeling, Mamba-based sequence modeling, and cross-modal fusion. Notably, MUG achieves these gains with only 7.6M parameters versus 19.3M for the Transformer variant, confirming the efficiency advantage of the Mamba architecture for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the HAN framework (Tian et al., ECCV 2020) for weakly-supervised AVVP and on VALOR's (Lai et al., NeurIPS 2023) CLIP/CLAP-based pseudo-label extraction, which the authors repurpose to enable a previously infeasible data augmentation strategy (cross-modal random combination). Architecturally, it departs from the Transformer-dominated AVVP literature by introducing Mamba (selective state space models) as the primary sequence-modeling backbone, drawing on Vision Mamba's bidirectional scanning and CBAM-style attention design, while retaining a final HAN Transformer layer for long-range spatial dependencies. The text-modality integration via frozen CLIP/CLAP encoders for noise exclusion extends the pseudo-labeling line of work (VALOR, LEAP) into a feature-fusion role rather than a pure supervision signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CMRC data augmentation strategy is a practical contribution that could transfer to other weakly-supervised multimodal tasks where unimodal pseudo-labels are available but cross-modal combinations are underrepresented. However, the evaluation is confined to the single LLP dataset, and the gains over the prior SOTA (CoLeaF) are modest (1–2% F1), limiting confidence in broader generalizability. The finding that excessive augmentation (Batch5, ~12K samples) degrades the PLSIM module highlights an open question about the noise ceiling in pseudo-label-driven augmentation. The authors themselves note that future work should validate MUG on larger, more diverse datasets beyond LLP.
