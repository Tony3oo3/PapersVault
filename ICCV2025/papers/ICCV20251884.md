## Problem

Current video generation models produce high-quality clips of only 5–10 seconds and cannot generate long sequences that convey a coherent, complete narrative over time. This limitation is compounded by the scarcity of video data with clear, structured narratives suitable for both training and evaluation—existing datasets are either too coarse, lack fine-grained action annotations, or focus on image-based story generation rather than real-world video.

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form text query (e.g., 'How to cook a tuna sandwich?') plus an initial image-text pair as input and produces a long narrative video at 448×448 resolution, composed of sequential ~9.5-second clips. Intermediate outputs include a structured sequence of (action, caption, visual embedding) triplets and rendered keyframes, which together form a storyboard that conditions the final video generation. The narrative is domain-specific (cooking), with each video containing an average of 763.8 words of dense textual annotation across ~6 clips.

## Method

VideoAuteur is a three-stage pipeline: (1) an interleaved autoregressive director (initialized from SEED-X 7B) generates a coherent sequence of actions, captions, and CLIP-Diffusion visual embeddings via next-token prediction, supervised with cross-entropy for text and a combined cosine-similarity + MSE loss for visual latents; (2) a rolling-context conditioned renderer (initialized from FLUX.1 Fill) synthesizes high-quality keyframes by conditioning on a dynamic rolling window of 1–3 prior reference images, tiled global captions, and predicted visual embeddings, trained with flow matching loss; (3) a visual-conditioned video generation model produces final clips conditioned on both VAE keyframe embeddings and CLIP latents, with regularization (Gaussian noise, 25% random masking, random shuffling) to handle imperfect regressed embeddings. The key architectural insight is that CLIP latents provide semantic alignment while VAE embeddings from the rolling context preserve fine visual details (clothing, food textures, character identity), and the layered action→caption→visual-state conditioning enforces narrative coherence across the full sequence.

## Results

On the YouCook2 validation set, VideoAuteur with Rolling Context Conditioning (RCC) achieves the best CLIP-T score of 28.0 and FID of 29.4 for long narrative keyframe generation, outperforming all six baselines. In human evaluation (5-tier scale), it attains top scores across all four criteria: aesthetics (4.8), realism (4.5), visual consistency (4.8), and narrative coherence (4.6). For visual-conditioned video generation, using regressed visual embeddings with regularization improves CLIP-T from 25.9 to 26.4 (YouCook2) and 26.6 to 27.3 (HowTo100M), while reducing FVD from 557.7 to 512.6 and 541.1 to 520.7 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoAuteur (w/ RCC) wins on every reported metric against all six baselines. Against IC Lora (the strongest text-conditioned competitor), it improves CLIP-T by 0.1 (28.0 vs. 27.9), FID by 4.7 points (29.4 vs. 34.1), and human-rated consistency by 0.1 (4.8 vs. 4.7), while matching aesthetics (4.8 vs. 4.7) and surpassing realism (4.5 vs. 4.1). Against Seed-Story (the only other interleaved visual-embedding method), VideoAuteur gains 3.9 CLIP-T points (28.0 vs. 24.1), 2.7 FID points (29.4 vs. 32.1), and 2.9 aesthetics points (4.8 vs. 1.9), though Seed-Story's realism (4.1) is close to VideoAuteur's (4.5). Vlogger and StoryDiffusion are clearly outperformed: Vlogger has the worst FID (45.5) and lowest realism (2.4), while StoryDiffusion trails on CLIP-T (25.9) and FID (36.4). The text-only baselines (SD-XL, FLUX.1-s) cannot produce narrative scores and lag on consistency (3.3–3.4 vs. 4.8), confirming that interleaved visual conditioning is essential for coherent long-form generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of interleaved image-text autoregressive modeling (building on SEED-X, a 7B multimodal LLM) and pretrained diffusion transformers (building on FLUX.1 Fill), creating a unified pipeline that prior narrative generation work has not attempted. It departs from the dominant line of narrative visual generation research (StoryDiffusion, Vlogger, Seed-Story, IC Lora), which targets consistent *image* sequence generation, by explicitly targeting coherent *video* narrative with temporal dynamics. The rolling-context conditioning strategy for keyframe rendering is a practical extension of in-context conditioning in DiTs, while the visual-embedding-conditioned video generation (beyond standard I2V keyframe conditioning) represents a novel architectural choice that leverages the CLIP latent space as a continuous conditioning signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CookGen dataset (~200K clips, 39M frames) is the first large-scale, structured benchmark specifically designed for long narrative video generation, filling a gap the authors identify as a key bottleneck in the field. However, the cooking-domain restriction, while justified by unambiguous procedural narratives, limits generalizability to open-ended storytelling or multi-character scenes. Open questions include whether the interleaved autoregressive + DiT pipeline scales to significantly longer narratives (beyond ~10 clips), how evaluation of narrative coherence can be made more objective than human rating, and whether the approach transfers to domains without strict procedural structure. The regularization strategy for noisy visual embeddings is a practical technique likely to be adopted in other autoregressive-to-diffusion bridging pipelines.
