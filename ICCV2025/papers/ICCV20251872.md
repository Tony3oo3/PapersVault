## Problem

Previous human reaction generation works condition the reactor's motion on the actor's motion sequence, which restricts interactions to human-human pairs and discards emotional cues (e.g., facial expressions) that influence how a person should react. This paper proposes generating 3D human reactions directly from RGB videos, thereby covering animal-human and scene-human interactions and naturally incorporating the actor's emotional state visible in the footage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251872/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is an RGB video V ∈ R^{T×H×W×3} captured from the reactor's perspective (i.e., what the reactor sees), and the output is a single 3D reactive motion M ∈ R^{N×D} in HumanML3D pose format at 20 FPS with a maximum duration of 10 seconds. Unlike prior reaction-generation tasks that require aligning actor and reactor motions in a shared coordinate system, HERO produces one self-contained reactive motion sequence without any explicit actor pose input.

## Method

HERO consists of three modules: a frozen TC-CLIP video encoder that yields per-frame local and pooled global visual representations; an RVQ-VAE that discretizes motions into multi-layer token sequences; and a masked Transformer decoder that generates motion tokens via iterative parallel prediction. The key architectural contribution is a global-local cross-attention mechanism (Interaction Intention Extraction) where the global video representation serves as query over frame-level keys/values, allowing the model to proactively weight key frames and distill a refined interaction-intention embedding rather than passively consuming a pooled signal. This intention embedding is injected via intention-conditioned self-attention, while a separate motion-frame cross-attention (Dynamic Information Exploitation) lets the decoder attend back to individual video frames to capture temporal dynamics. A residual Transformer then predicts the remaining RVQ layers to refine the base-layer tokens, and the full token sequence is decoded into the final motion.

## Results

On the ViMo test set, HERO achieves a FID of 0.427±0.014, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline MoMask (0.856±0.015) by nearly 2×, and the next-best BAMM (0.930±0.031) by over 2×. HERO also attains the highest diversity score (7.801±0.061), closest to the real-motion reference (7.954±0.074), indicating well-learned reaction patterns. On multimodality, HERO scores 1.614±0.040, surpassing MoMask (1.567) but trailing diffusion-based baselines such as MDM (2.117) and MLD (2.102), which the authors note is a secondary metric per [28].

## Baselines & Comparisons

HERO outperforms all five baselines on the primary FID metric, with the largest gap over the diffusion-based MDM (0.427 vs. 1.688, a 4× improvement) and MLD (0.427 vs. 1.565, a 3.7× improvement). Against the closest masked-modeling competitor MoMask [28], HERO nearly halves the FID (0.427 vs. 0.856) and also edges ahead on diversity (7.801 vs. 7.394) and multimodality (1.614 vs. 1.567). HERO also beats BAMM [71] on FID (0.427 vs. 0.930) and diversity (7.801 vs. 7.619), though BAMM retains a higher multimodality score (1.885 vs. 1.614). The autoregressive T2M-GPT [115] is intermediate, with HERO improving FID by 2.7× (0.427 vs. 1.154) and diversity by 0.08. The authors note that multimodality is a secondary metric per MoMask's own analysis, and that HERO's advantage on FID and diversity—metrics directly reflecting fidelity to the real reaction distribution—is the more meaningful signal of performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established human reaction generation paradigm (InterFormer, ReGenNet, ReMoS, PhysReaction) that conditions reactor motion on structured actor motion sequences, instead using raw RGB video as input to enable broader interaction categories (animal-human, scene-human) and emotional cues. Architecturally, it builds on the masked motion modeling backbone from MoMask and MMM, the TC-CLIP video encoder, and the residual VQ-VAE tokenization from BAMM, but introduces a novel interaction intention extraction module (global-local cross-attention) that actively refines the video's global representation by re-weighting frame-level features, distinguishing it from text-to-motion pipelines where the condition signal is used passively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By shifting the input from structured motion to video, this work opens human reaction generation to interaction types and emotional contexts that were previously inaccessible, with direct implications for humanoid robotics, VR NPCs, and embodied AI. The ViMo dataset (3,500 video-motion pairs across 32 subcategories) is a useful but modest resource; the authors acknowledge the dataset size and category coverage need expansion. Open questions include whether the simple cross-attention-based intention extraction scales to longer or more complex interactions, whether physical constraints can be integrated without degrading the generative quality, and whether the approach generalizes to multi-person or object-centric reactions.
