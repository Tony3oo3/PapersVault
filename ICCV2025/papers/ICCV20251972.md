## Problem

Despite combining powerful vision encoders with LLM decoders, state-of-the-art VLMs—including the proprietary GPT-4o—lack a fundamental in-context learning ability for visual tasks: they cannot localize a specific object instance in a query image by attending to annotated in-context examples. This paper identifies and benchmarks this gap, showing that existing instruction-tuning data (curated from generic image collections) teaches models to reason about individual instances but never forces them to extract identity from visual context across multiple shots.

## Input / Output

The model receives a structured multi-turn conversation: N in-context messages, each containing an RGB image, a category label (or a randomly assigned pseudo-name like 'Elizabeth'), and explicit bounding-box coordinates in (x_min, y_min, x_max, y_max) format, followed by a final query image with no annotation. The model must output a single bounding-box coordinate pair localizing the same object instance in the query. The pseudo-name mechanism is the key I/O constraint: because the label carries no semantic content, the model is forced to ground its prediction entirely in the visual appearance shown by the in-context bounding boxes rather than in pre-trained category knowledge.

## Method

IPLoc is a data-centric fine-tuning strategy that constructs instruction-tuning dialogues from three video object-tracking datasets (TAO, LaSOT, GOT-10k), sampling frames at maximum intervals from a single tracked object within one video sequence to ensure semantic coherence and appearance variation. Two design choices drive context-awareness: (1) all shots in a conversation focus on the same object instance across different frames, and (2) true category names are replaced with random pseudo-names, stripping away any shortcut to pre-trained object knowledge. The VLM (primarily Qwen2-VL at 7B/72B) is then fine-tuned with LoRA under standard next-token-prediction loss, where the assistant turn must generate the target bounding-box coordinates conditioned on the preceding in-context examples. This parameter-efficient approach yields 14–28% mIoU gains over base models while retaining <1% degradation on general VLM benchmarks (GQA, SEED, POPE), confirming that the model learns to attend to visual context rather than over-specializing.

## Results

On the few-shot personalized localization task, IPLoc(72B)(Real+Pseudo) achieves the best average mIoU of 55.79% across PDM, PerSeg, and ICL-LASOT benchmarks, substantially outperforming GPT-4o (27.63%), InternVL2-76B (41.32%), and the base Qwen2-VL-72B (34.21%). The 7B variant (IPLoc(7B)(Real+Pseudo)) reaches 43.31% average mIoU, a 11.54% gain over the base Qwen2-VL-7B (31.77%). On ICL-LASOT specifically, IPLoc(72B) achieves 67.63% mIoU at 4-shot, far exceeding the best few-shot object detector (CD-ViTO at 34.90%) and the open-vocabulary detector OwlViT (50.18%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IPLoc(72B)(Real+Pseudo) outperforms all compared VLMs by a wide margin: it beats GPT-4o (27.63%) by 28.16%, InternVL2-76B (41.32%) by 14.47%, and the base Qwen2-VL-72B (34.21%) by 21.58% in average mIoU. The 7B variant (IPLoc(7B)(Real+Pseudo), 43.31%) also surpasses GPT-4o (27.63%) by 15.68% and LLaVA-OV (20.64%) by 22.67%, despite being smaller than GPT-4o. Against vision-only few-shot object detectors on ICL-LASOT, IPLoc(72B) achieves 63–68% mIoU versus CD-ViTO's 32–35% and TFA's 26–31%, a gap of over 30 points. Compared to the open-vocabulary detector OwlViT, IPLoc wins on PerSeg (51.62 vs. 40.71) and ICL-LASOT (65.30 vs. 50.18) but trails slightly on PDM (45.15 vs. 48.47), yielding a higher overall mean (54.02 vs. 46.45). Notably, GPT-4o's underperformance is attributed to its tendency to generate descriptive reasoning rather than structured bounding-box outputs, even under multiple prompting strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLM instruction-tuning paradigm (LLaVA, Qwen2-VL) by repurposing video object tracking datasets (TAO, LaSOT, GOT-10k) to construct semantically coherent, single-object-focused conversations that instill in-context localization ability. It departs from prior VLM ICL work (Flamingo, LLaVA-ICL, MTV, MMICL) which targeted classification and VQA, by introducing a localization-specific task and a pseudo-name regularization that forces reliance on visual context rather than pre-trained category knowledge. The fine-tuning strategy (LoRA) follows standard parameter-efficient adaptation, but the data curation—sampling frames from the same tracking sequence to simulate personalization—is the key methodological departure from generic instruction-tuning corpora.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a fundamental gap in even the strongest proprietary VLMs (GPT-4o) for context-driven object localization, suggesting that current instruction-tuning data does not adequately train spatial reasoning from in-context examples. The pseudo-name regularization is a simple, transferable technique that any VLM fine-tuning pipeline could adopt to reduce reliance on pre-trained category priors. Key limitations include single-object-only localization (multi-object mIoU drops to ~5% on COCO) and evaluation on relatively small test sets (154–1952 samples), leaving open whether the approach scales to dense, multi-object scenes. The work lays groundwork for personalized vision-language applications (e.g., tracking a specific pet or product across video streams) but the practical ceiling remains unclear.
