## Problem

Existing controllable street view generation methods (e.g., BEVControl, MagicDrive) integrate 3D annotation conditions through basic cross-attention mechanisms, which fail to fully exploit detailed geometric layout information for precise attention map manipulation. This results in imprecise object-level control, where generated objects misalign with their 3D annotation positions, particularly problematic for data production in autonomous driving where annotation accuracy is critical.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252061/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D bounding box annotations (projected to 8 2D corner points per box), perspective-projected street maps (encoding road markings and obstacles), and free-form textual scene descriptions as controlling conditions. It produces multi-view street view images at 256×384 or 256×704 resolution for NuScenes (6 cameras) and 384×1280 for KITTI (single camera), constrained by the U-Net architecture of Stable Diffusion. The 3D boxes are encoded via Fourier embedding and the street maps via a frozen ConvNext encoder, while text descriptions use the CLIP text encoder.

## Method

PerLDiff introduces a PerL-based Controlling Module (PerL-CM) that augments standard cross-attention with perspective layout masking maps rendered directly from 3D annotations. The key mechanism adds binary masking maps (road mask M_s for non-empty road regions and box mask M_b for interior regions of each projected 3D box) as additive terms to the attention logits before softmax, weighted by learnable coefficients λ_s and λ_b. This geometric prior is injected during training rather than inference, so the network learns to align attention responses with object locations without disrupting the denoising process. The module is integrated into pre-trained Stable Diffusion and fine-tuned, with a gating operation (similar to GLIGEN) dynamically modulating condition contribution, and view cross-attention ensuring multi-camera consistency.

## Results

On NuScenes validation with BEVFormer, PerLDiff achieves mAP 25.10 and NDS 36.24, substantially outperforming BEVControl* (16.48 / 28.08) and MagicDrive* (15.21 / 28.79), while maintaining a competitive FID of 13.36. With BEVFusion, PerLDiff reaches mAP 15.24 and NDS 24.05 versus 9.98 / 19.61 for BEVControl*. On KITTI, PerLDiff achieves 11.04 / 7.44 / 6.03 mAP (Easy/Mod/Hard) with MonoFlex, dramatically surpassing BEVControl*'s 0.33 / 0.29 / 0.39. For BEV segmentation (CVT), PerLDiff attains Road mIoU 61.26 and Vehicle mIoU 27.13, the best among all generative methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PerLDiff consistently outperforms both BEVControl* and MagicDrive* across all detection and segmentation metrics on NuScenes. Against BEVControl* (the closest architectural baseline, differing only in the PerL-based cross-attention mechanism), PerLDiff gains +8.62% mAP and +8.16% NDS with BEVFormer, and +5.26% mAP / +4.44% NDS with BEVFusion. Against MagicDrive*, PerLDiff improves mAP by +2.94% and NDS by +0.73% (BEVFormer), while also reducing FID by 2.84 points (13.36 vs. 15.92), indicating better generation quality alongside superior controllability. On KITTI, where data scarcity amplifies the importance of geometric priors, PerLDiff achieves 11.04 Easy mAP versus BEVControl*'s 0.33—a 33× improvement—attributed to more precise object sizing via perspective layout masks. For BEV segmentation, PerLDiff leads BEVControl* by +0.52% Road mIoU and +4.66% Vehicle mIoU, and MagicDrive* by +5.70% and +2.32% respectively, confirming that the PerL masking maps improve both scene-level and object-level alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

PerLDiff extends the diffusion-based street view generation line initiated by BEVControl and MagicDrive, which integrate 3D annotation conditions via standard cross-attention (inheriting the GLIGEN/ControlNet paradigm). The key departure is the introduction of perspective layout masking maps as geometric priors directly embedded in the cross-attention computation during training, rather than relying solely on learned conditional feature extraction or inference-time attention manipulation (as in BoxDiff or ZestGuide). The framework is built atop Stable Diffusion's latent space, with ConvNext and CLIP encoders handling road map and text conditions respectively, and a Fourier-embedded 3D box representation for object geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By baking geometric layout constraints into the training process rather than post-hoc inference adjustments, PerLDiff addresses a fundamental misalignment problem in controllable generation that limits downstream perception performance on synthetic data. The practical impact is significant for autonomous driving data pipelines: synthetic validation sets generated by PerLDiff nearly match the performance gains of real validation data when augmenting 3D detectors. However, the FID score remains comparable to (not better than) the BEVControl* baseline, suggesting the geometric constraints trade off some background realism for foreground precision, and the approach has only been validated on two datasets with limited object classes. Whether the PerL-based cross-attention generalizes to more complex 3D scenes or video generation remains an open question.
