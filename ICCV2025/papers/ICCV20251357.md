## Problem

Natural language queries in video grounding datasets are often vague, incomplete, or poorly worded, limiting the precision of temporal localization. While video captioning and grounding are dual tasks, prior work has largely treated them in isolation without exploiting how richer descriptions could improve grounding accuracy. Existing LLM-based grounding methods represent timestamps as raw text or special tokens, missing the opportunity to leverage the LLM's descriptive capabilities to refine queries before localizing them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251357/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an untrimmed video (T frames, tokenized into R visual tokens via a ViT-G/14 + Q-Former encoder) and one or more text queries as input. It produces an enriched natural-language query (generated autoregressively by the LLM) followed by a predicted temporal interval parameterized as (center, width), decoded from the hidden state of a special <INT> token. For paragraph grounding, multiple enriched queries are interleaved with <INT> tokens; for article grounding, the model must also determine which queries are groundable.

## Method

ED-VTG performs temporal grounding as a two-stage cascaded process within a single multimodal LLM (VideoLLaMA-7B backbone with LoRA rank 32): first, the LLM enriches the input query by adding video-grounded details, then emits an <INT> token whose hidden state is decoded by a lightweight interval decoder (2 transformer layers + MLP) into a (center, width) interval. The decoder receives the concatenation of a linearly projected <INT> embedding and the full video token sequence, enabling detection-style losses (L1 + gIoU) that are infeasible with token-based timestamp prediction. Training uses a multiple-instance learning framework: two forward passes (one with the original query, one with a pseudo-labeled enriched query from LLaVA OneVision 72B) are run in teacher-forcing mode, and gradients flow only through the pass yielding the lower grounding loss, allowing the model to autonomously decide when enrichment is beneficial. The overall loss combines cross-entropy language modeling with the selected grounding loss, and the model is pre-trained on 136K samples across 8 datasets before task-specific fine-tuning.

## Results

In zero-shot single-query temporal grounding (STG), ED-VTG achieves mIoU of 40.2 on Charades-STA, 35.2 on ActivityNet-Captions, and 12.7 on TACoS, outperforming the best prior LLM-based method (HawkEye) by 6.5 and 2.5 mIoU points respectively, and specialist zero-shot models (LT-ZVG) by 4.2 mIoU on Charades. In fine-tuned STG, ED-VTG reaches mIoU 52.6 (Charades), 44.9 (ActivityNet), and 32.4 (TACoS), surpassing all generalist LLM-based models by 3.3–14.4 mIoU while remaining competitive with specialist detectors (e.g., EMB/ELA at 62.2/56.2/48.4 mIoU). On video paragraph grounding (VPG), ED-VTG sets new SOTA on Charades-CD-OOD (mIoU 45.0) and YouCook2 (mIoU 31.5), and on question grounding (QG) achieves mIoP 34.7 on NExT-GQA, beating the prior best (HawkEye) by 0.9 mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ED-VTG outperforms all 47 compared baselines in the zero-shot STG setting, with the largest margins over LLM-based generalists: +6.5 mIoU over HawkEye on Charades-STA, +2.5 mIoU on ActivityNet, and +7.2 mIoU over ChatVTG on TACoS. In fine-tuned STG, ED-VTG surpasses all generalist LLM-based methods (e.g., +11.7 R@0.3 over HawkEye on ActivityNet, +14.4 mIoU over VTimeLLM on TACoS) but remains below the strongest specialist detectors (EMB/ELA: 62.2 vs 52.6 mIoU on Charades, 56.2 vs 44.9 on ActivityNet, 48.4 vs 32.4 on TACoS). On VPG, ED-VTG beats the best LLM-based baseline TimeChat by 6.7 mIoU on Charades-CD-OOD and 4.9 mIoU on YouCook2, and also exceeds specialist models like SiamGTR (38.9) and SVPTR (55.9 on ANet). On QG (NExT-GQA), ED-VTG achieves SOTA mIoP of 34.7, surpassing HawkEye (25.7 mIoU) and SeViLA (21.7 mIoU). On AG (HT-Step), ED-VTG improves over TimeChat by +3.5 mAP@[0.3-0.7] (seen) and +2.9 (unseen), and outperforms specialist ActionFormer-T on the unseen split (21.6 vs 20.4 mAP@[0.3-0.7]).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior LLM-based temporal grounding methods (VTimeLLM, TimeChat, HawkEye) that represent timestamps as raw text tokens or special vocabulary entries, instead introducing a lightweight interval decoder that enables detection-style losses (L1, gIoU) while keeping the LLM focused on language generation. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate research threads—video captioning and temporal grounding—by exploiting their duality: the LLM's captioning ability is repurposed to enrich coarse queries before grounding. The multiple-instance learning framework, borrowed from weakly-supervised vision, is repurposed here to handle noisy pseudo-labels from an external captioner (LLaVA-OneVision), allowing the model to dynamically choose between enriched and original queries during training rather than relying on offline data augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ED-VTG is the first LLM-based model to match or surpass specialist temporal grounding models in zero-shot evaluation, narrowing a gap that has persisted across prior generalist approaches. The enrichment-then-detect paradigm suggests a broader principle: leveraging a model's generative capacity to improve its discriminative performance, which could extend to other grounding or retrieval tasks. Open questions include whether the two-stage process scales to very long videos (the fixed number of input frames remains a bottleneck), whether the MIL selection mechanism generalizes beyond the binary enriched/original choice, and whether the reliance on an external 72B captioner for pseudo-labels limits reproducibility. The fine-tuned results, while strong, still trail the best specialist models (e.g., EMB/ELA on Charades), indicating the gap is not fully closed in the supervised regime.
