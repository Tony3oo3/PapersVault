## Problem

Human motion estimation and generation have been treated as separate tasks requiring distinct specialized models, despite sharing common representations of temporal dynamics and kinematics. This separation limits cross-task knowledge transfer and prevents a single model from simultaneously performing precise video-conditioned reconstruction and diverse text/music-conditioned synthesis. Additionally, existing methods struggle to handle arbitrary combinations of multimodal conditions at different time intervals within variable-length sequences without complex post-processing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250272/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a variable-length set of multimodal condition signals—video features, 2D skeletons, text descriptions, music clips, 3D keyframes, camera motion, and 2D bounding boxes—each accompanied by a binary temporal mask indicating per-frame availability, allowing conditions to start and stop at arbitrary frames. The output is a unified per-frame motion token comprising SMPL joint angles and shape parameters, gravity-view global trajectory (6D orientation + 3D root velocity), camera pose (6D orientation + 3D translation), and 6D hand/foot contact labels, all produced in a single feedforward diffusion pass without post-processing.

## Method

GENMO reformulates motion estimation as constrained motion generation within a diffusion framework, using a dual-mode training paradigm: an estimation mode that feeds pure Gaussian noise at the maximum diffusion timestep T to enforce maximum-likelihood regression, and a generation mode following standard DDPM training to learn diverse conditional distributions. The architecture is a RoPE-based Transformer with a novel multi-text injection block that applies masked multi-head attention to restrict each text prompt's influence to a user-specified time window, enabling arbitrary numbers of text inputs at different temporal positions. An estimation-guided training objective leverages in-the-wild 2D annotations by first generating pseudo-clean motions via the estimation mode, then applying a 2D reprojection loss during the generation mode's forward diffusion process. Sliding-window attention over relative positions supports variable-length sequences, while per-condition binary masks enable seamless transitions between different modalities at different time intervals.

## Results

On global motion estimation, GENMO achieves W-MPJPE₁₀₀ of 202.1 mm on EMDB (vs. 222.4 mm for TRAM) and 118.6 mm on RICH (vs. 126.3 mm for GVHMR), setting new state-of-the-art with a single feedforward pass. For local motion estimation on 3DPW, GENMO reports PA-MPJPE of 34.6 mm, outperforming GVHMR (36.2 mm) and WHAM (35.9 mm). On music-to-dance generation (AIST++), the generalist model achieves PFC of 0.3702 and BAS of 0.2708, substantially surpassing EDGE (PFC 1.5363) and GCDance (BAS 0.2346). On text-to-motion (Motion-X), GENMO reduces FID from 2.389 (MDM) to 0.207 while improving R-Precision from 0.313 to 0.472.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GENMO outperforms all 22 baselines on global motion estimation: it beats the closest competitor TRAM by 20.3 mm in W-MPJPE₁₀₀ on EMDB (202.1 vs. 222.4) and by 7.7 mm on RICH (118.6 vs. 126.3 for GVHMR), while also achieving the lowest RTE (1.2% vs. 1.4% for TRAM). For local motion estimation on 3DPW, GENMO (PA-MPJPE 34.6 mm) surpasses GVHMR (36.2 mm), WHAM (35.9 mm), and TRAM (35.6 mm), with the 2D-training variant further improving over the no-2D variant (34.6 vs. 35.2 mm). On music-to-dance, the generalist GENMO achieves the best PFC (0.3702) and BAS (0.2708), beating EDGE (PFC 1.5363) and GCDance (BAS 0.2346), though its FID (40.91) is higher than the specialized music-only variant (16.10), which the authors attribute to training on more heterogeneous data. On text-to-motion, GENMO substantially outperforms MDM on Motion-X (FID 0.207 vs. 2.389; R@3 0.472 vs. 0.313) but trails EMDM and MoMask on HumanML3D-specific metrics (FID 0.216 vs. 0.112/0.204), a gap the authors attribute to the SMPL-vs-HumanML3D representation mismatch rather than model quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

GENMO builds on the diffusion model framework (DDPM) and the gravity-view coordinate system for world-grounded motion representation, while extending the motion diffusion paradigm (MDM) to jointly handle estimation and generation. It departs from prior motion estimation methods (WHAM, GVHMR, TRAM) that rely on optimization or separate post-processing by formulating estimation as constrained generation within a single feedforward diffusion pass. The dual-mode training paradigm (regression at maximum timestep + standard diffusion) and the estimation-guided 2D training objective are novel contributions that bridge the gap between deterministic reconstruction and stochastic generation, whereas prior work treated these as fundamentally incompatible objectives requiring separate models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying estimation and generation, GENMO demonstrates bidirectional synergy: generative priors improve robustness under occlusions and challenging conditions, while diverse in-the-wild video data (via 2D annotations) enriches generative diversity without requiring expensive 3D mocap. This reduces the need to maintain separate specialized models and enables flexible multi-modal conditioning (video, text, music, keyframes) in a single pass. Limitations include the SMPL representation mismatch that caps performance on HumanML3D-specific metrics, higher FID than a specialized music-only variant, and the reliance on SLAM for camera estimation at inference. Open questions include whether the dual-mode approach generalizes to other embodied agents or longer-horizon tasks, and whether the single-pass variable-length generation scales to very long sequences without quality degradation.
