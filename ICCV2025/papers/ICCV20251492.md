## Problem

Existing visual text generation methods condition on pre-rendered glyph images that discard font style and color information, forcing multi-branch architectures with auxiliary positional-encoding and style-control modules. This information degradation in the conditioning signal is the root cause of blurred glyphs, poor small-text rendering, and architectural bloat in current ControlNet-based approaches like AnyText and GlyphDraw2.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251492/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a user text prompt and an optional LLM-generated layout specification (bounding boxes, font tokens, color tokens) as input, and produces an image with rendered visual text at arbitrary resolutions (inherited from FLUX.1-dev's resolution flexibility). The key conditioning signal is not a rendered glyph image but a pixel-level segmentation mask combined with the original image's glyph regions via an adaptive blending strategy: large glyphs (average character area > 4900 px) use Canny edges plus the masked original image, while small glyphs use a Gaussian-blurred crop to compensate for segmentation inaccuracies at small scales.

## Method

UniGlyph replaces rendered glyph conditions with unified segmentation masks that preserve shape, position, font, and color at full spatial resolution, enabling a single ControlNet branch on a FLUX.1-dev DiT (flow-matching) backbone without auxiliary style or positional modules. A fine-tuned bilingual SAM-TS model (from Hi-SAM, trained on BTS) extracts pixel-accurate text masks, and an adaptive glyph condition constructs the ControlNet input by blending Canny edges with the original image for large glyphs or using blurred crops for small ones. Training combines a standard flow-matching loss with a novel glyph region loss (MSE applied only within glyph masks, weighted λ=1, activated after 100K steps) to sharpen text fidelity. The system is trained on 300K steps over GlyphMM-3M and Anyword-3M, then fine-tuned for 160K steps on Poster-100K using 8 A800 GPUs, achieving state-of-the-art results with substantially fewer training samples than prior methods.

## Results

On the AnyText-benchmark, UniGlyph achieves 0.8267 Sen.Acc and 0.8976 NED for Chinese, and 0.9018 Sen.Acc and 0.9582 NED for English, surpassing the next-best method (CharGen at 0.7499/0.8609 Chinese, 0.8096/0.9205 English) by a wide margin. On the more challenging GlyphMM-benchmark with complex multi-line layouts, UniGlyph leads with 0.5056 Sen.Acc (Chinese) and 0.4522 Sen.Acc (English), outperforming Glyph-ByT5 (0.4300/0.2812) and GlyphDraw2 (0.4232/0.3079). On the MiniText-benchmark for small-character generation, UniGlyph achieves 0.7925 Sen.Acc and 0.9537 NED, nearly doubling the best competitor Glyph-ByT5 (0.3881/0.8268) while all other methods score below 0.02 Sen.Acc.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniGlyph outperforms all eight baselines on both Sen.Acc and NED across every benchmark and language setting. On the AnyText-benchmark, it leads CharGen (the closest competitor) by +7.7 points in Chinese Sen.Acc (0.8267 vs. 0.7499) and +9.2 points in English Sen.Acc (0.9018 vs. 0.8096). On the harder GlyphMM-benchmark with complex multi-line layouts, the gap widens: UniGlyph beats Glyph-ByT5 by +7.6 points (Chinese) and +17.1 points (English) in Sen.Acc, and GlyphDraw2 by +8.2 and +14.4 points respectively. On the MiniText-benchmark for small characters, UniGlyph (0.7925 Sen.Acc) more than doubles Glyph-ByT5 (0.3881) and is the only method above 0.3, while AnyText-V1.1, GlyphDraw2, and GlyphControl all score below 0.02. The only metric where UniGlyph does not lead is ClipScore on the AnyText-benchmark (0.7915 Chinese vs. AnyText-V1.1's 0.8341), suggesting a slight trade-off in overall image-text semantic alignment, though HPSv2 (perceptual quality) remains competitive. Notably, UniGlyph achieves these gains with substantially less training data (~7.36M effective samples vs. tens of millions for GlyphControl/TextDiffuser), underscoring the efficiency of the segmentation-conditioned approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniGlyph departs from the dominant ControlNet-based paradigm for visual text generation (AnyText, GlyphDraw2, GlyphControl) by replacing pre-rendered glyph images with pixel-level segmentation masks as the conditioning signal, arguing that rendered glyphs irreversibly discard font style and color information. The segmentation component is fine-tuned from Hi-SAM's SAM-TS module for bilingual (Chinese-English) text, and the diffusion backbone is a DiT-ControlNet initialized from FLUX.1-dev with flow matching. The glyph region loss and adaptive blending strategy are novel additions that address the specific failure mode of segmentation inaccuracy on small characters, while the LLM-based layout module follows the approach established by GlyphDraw2 and TextDiffuser-2.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that segmentation masks preserve the full typographic signal (shape, position, font, color) at pixel resolution, eliminating the need for auxiliary style/position modules—offers a cleaner architectural path for visual text generation that could be adopted by subsequent work. The introduction of GlyphMM-3M (3M+ high-resolution bilingual images) and two new benchmarks (GlyphMM-benchmark for complex layouts, MiniText-benchmark for small text) addresses a real data and evaluation gap in the field. However, the ClipScore is consistently lower than several baselines, suggesting a trade-off between text accuracy and overall image quality that remains unresolved; the paper also acknowledges using only a subset of its constructed dataset due to compute constraints, leaving open whether full-scale training would further close the gap on aesthetic metrics.
