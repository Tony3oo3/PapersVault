## Problem

Existing 3D language fields are confined to small indoor scenes and support only simple object localization, making them unsuitable for city-scale urban environments exceeding 1 km². Two fundamental gaps remain: (1) scalability to high-fidelity city-scale 3D reconstructions without memory overflow, and (2) compositional reasoning capabilities—spatial relationships, counting, measurement, and comparison—that urban applications demand but current methods cannot provide.

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural language query (e.g., 'How many cars are there between Little Stadium and Huiyi building?') and a pre-trained GCLF scene (hierarchical 3D Gaussians georeferenced to real-world coordinates via OpenStreetMap, covering up to 2 km²). The LLM generates an executable Python program that chains GV-API calls; the program's output is task-dependent: a pixel-level segmentation mask (GRD), an integer count (CNT), a distance or height in meters (MES), a yes/no verdict (SPR), or a building name (CMP). The 3D scene is rendered on-the-fly at dynamic resolution—only Gaussians projecting smaller than one pixel are rendered—enabling real-time inference over vast urban spaces.

## Method

GeoProg3D combines two components: (i) GCLF, a tree-structured 3D Gaussian representation where each node pairs a Gaussian primitive with a CLIP-aligned language embedding, trained in three stages (coarse 3D-GS → hierarchical tree → language embedding via autoencoder on SAM-segmented masks), and georeferenced to real-world coordinates using 20+ manually selected OpenStreetMap landmarks; and (ii) nine Geographical Vision APIs (GV-APIs) that operate directly on the 3D Gaussian space, including landmark/structure segmentation, directional and proximity segmentation, distance/height measurement, and GroundingDINO-based object detection on rendered views. An LLM (GPT-3.5-turbo-instruct) generates Python programs by combining these APIs via in-context learning with 10–15 examples, decomposing complex queries into sequential subtasks without any additional training. The hierarchical tree structure is key to scalability: it reduces rendering cost by dynamically selecting the appropriate level of detail, allowing GCLF to handle scenes with 37M+ Gaussians (where vanilla 3D-GS baselines like LangSplat run out of memory) while maintaining rendering speeds under 21 ms.

## Results

On the GeoEval3D benchmark, GeoProg3D achieves the best performance across all five tasks on both GoogleEarth and UrbanScene3D. On GoogleEarth, it reaches 64.00% accuracy on spatial reasoning (SPR), 59.73% on comparison (CMP), 2.00 MAE on counting (CNT), 45.24 m MAE on height measurement (MES-H), and 49.28 m MAE on distance measurement (MES-D). On the larger UrbanScene3D scene (2 km²), it achieves 60.87% SPR, 2.51 MAE CNT, and 139.51 m MAE MES-D. For the grounding (GRD) task, GeoProg3D attains 45.20% localization accuracy on GoogleEarth and 30.23% on UrbanScene3D, where competing 3D language fields (LangSplat, LERF) fail entirely due to out-of-memory errors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoProg3D outperforms all nine VLM/VLGFM baselines on every task and both datasets. The closest competitor is TEOChat, which leads in SPR among baselines (59.04% on GoogleEarth, 57.99% on UrbanScene3D) but is still surpassed by GeoProg3D by ~5 points; on CMP, TEOChat reaches 48.11% versus GeoProg3D's 59.73%. For distance measurement (MES-D), the gap is most dramatic: GeoProg3D achieves 49.28 m MAE on GoogleEarth versus 89.34 m for the best baseline (GeoChat) and 195.29 m for GPT-4o Vision, and 139.51 m versus 427.94 m (Llama-3.2) on UrbanScene3D. Among 3D language field baselines on the GRD task, LangSplat (14.15% on GoogleEarth) and LERF (11.44%) are far behind GeoProg3D (45.20%), and both fail with out-of-memory errors on the 2 km² UrbanScene3D scene, whereas GeoProg3D still achieves 30.23%. GPT-4o Vision performs worst overall (24.77% SPR, 2.63% CMP on GoogleEarth), indicating that general-purpose VLMs struggle substantially with city-scale 3D geographic reasoning even when given top-down views.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoProg3D extends LangSplat's 3D language Gaussian splatting from indoor-scale scenes to city-scale environments by adopting the hierarchical tree-structured 3D Gaussian representation from Shuai et al. (2024), which enables memory-efficient rendering of scenes exceeding 1 km². The compositional reasoning mechanism follows the Visual Programming paradigm (Gupta & Kembhavi, 2022), where an LLM generates executable Python programs, but departs from prior visual programming work by operating on 3D Gaussian space rather than 2D images, and by integrating geographic information (OpenStreetMap georeferencing, directional segmentation, real-world distance measurement) into the API layer. The key conceptual departure from existing 3D language fields (LERF, LangSplat, LEGaussians) is the addition of geographic APIs that enable spatial reasoning, counting, and measurement tasks beyond simple object localization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GeoProg3D is the first framework to enable natural-language compositional reasoning over high-fidelity city-scale 3D reconstructions, and the GeoEval3D benchmark (952 queries, 5 tasks, >3 km²) provides a much-needed evaluation standard for urban-scale 3D understanding that prior indoor-focused benchmarks cannot address. The practical significance is underscored by the OOM failures of LangSplat and LERF on the 5 km² UrbanScene3D scene, demonstrating that hierarchical 3D Gaussians are necessary (not merely preferable) for city-scale language fields. Open questions include whether the semi-automatic georeferencing (20+ manual landmark points) scales to truly continental-scale scenes, whether the GPT-3.5 dependency for program generation can be replaced by smaller or local models, and whether the 64% spatial reasoning accuracy reflects a fundamental limitation of the API decomposition or can be improved with better LLM reasoning.
