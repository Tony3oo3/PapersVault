## Problem

Robotic manipulation in open environments faces a dual dilemma: data-driven trajectory fitting methods (procedural skill) degrade catastrophically under environmental variation, while vision-language models (declarative skill) lack embodied experience and produce physically implausible action sequences. Existing approaches force VLMs to generate low-level control commands, sacrificing both cognitive reasoning and execution robustness. The paper asks how to let large multimodal models and reinforcement learning each operate at their full capability without mutual constraint.

## Input / Output

The system consumes an RGB image from a wrist-mounted camera (first-person view) and a third-person camera, a natural language instruction, and depth maps. The HCP outputs a sequence of primitive actions (e.g., reach, grasp, place) each annotated with an Invariant Operable Representation (IOR) comprising an action-type one-hot vector, 3rd-view segmentation masks for gripper/object/destination, 1st-view masked depth channels, and directional constraints. The GEA then maps each IOR to continuous low-level end-effector actions, with the IOR updated at high frequency by TrackAnything and re-planned at low frequency by GPT-4o success/failure checks.

## Method

RoBridge is a three-tier hierarchical architecture: a High-level Cognitive Planner (GPT-4o + GroundingDINO + SAM + TrackAnything) decomposes instructions into primitive actions and generates IORs; the IOR serves as an invariant symbolic bridge with physical intuition but no execution-specific details; and a Guided Embodied Agent (GEA) converts IORs into robot actions. The GEA is trained in three stages: (1) per-task RL experts (DRQ-v2) with domain randomization on object shape, arm placement, and camera pose; (2) imitation learning on expert-generated data with additional augmentations (depth warping, Gaussian blur, random masking, mask offsets/cropping) to build robustness; and (3) adaptive offline DAgger that resamples tasks by a reward-weighted function and injects expert corrections for failures. A closed-loop controller runs TrackAnything at high frequency to update masks/depth and GPT-4o at low frequency to verify action success and trigger replanning.

## Results

On the Metaworld benchmark (50 tasks), RoBridge achieves a mean success rate of 82.12%, outperforming the best baseline ManipGen (70.84%) by 11.28 percentage points, with particularly strong generalization under unseen camera poses (74.8% vs. 62.8%). In real-world experiments on a Kinova Gen3 arm, RoBridge reaches an 83.3% average success rate across four manipulation tasks using only five demonstrations per task, compared to 49.2% for the next-best method (ReKep). On five zero-shot unseen tasks, RoBridge achieves a 75% mean success rate versus 28% for ReKep, and on a four-stage long-horizon task it completes an average of 3.0 stages versus 1.7 for ReKep.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoBridge outperforms all eight baselines across every evaluation setting. In simulation (Metaworld), it surpasses ManipGen (70.84%) by 11.28 points and PSL (45.24%) by 36.88 points, with the largest gap appearing under unseen camera poses where ManipGen drops to 62.8% while RoBridge retains 74.8%. In real-world experiments, RoBridge (83.3% mean) nearly doubles the performance of ReKep (49.2%) and RAM (44.17%), and substantially outperforms end-to-end VLA models π_0 (18.3%) and RDT (7.5%), which struggle with tasks requiring physical reasoning like pressing a button or opening a drawer. On zero-shot unseen tasks, RoBridge (75%) achieves 2.7× the success rate of ReKep (28%) and 4.2× that of ManipGen (18%), while DRQ-v2, SayCan, and PSL cannot handle these tasks at all due to their architectural limitations. The long-horizon task further highlights RoBridge's advantage: it completes 3.0 of 4 stages on average versus 1.7 for ReKep and 0.5 for π_0, demonstrating that the hierarchical IOR-based planning avoids the compounding errors that plague end-to-end approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoBridge departs from two dominant paradigms in robotic manipulation: (1) end-to-end vision-language-action models (RDT, π0) that force VLMs into low-level control via data-driven trajectory fitting, and (2) keypoint/pose-based planning methods (ReKep, OmniManip) that require VLMs to produce physically grounded spatial outputs without embodied experience. Drawing on Central Pattern Generator theory from cognitive neuroscience, the paper introduces an Invariant Operable Representation (IOR) as a symbolic bridge, combining GPT-4o-based task decomposition with DRQ-v2-trained RL experts and DAgger-based imitation learning. The architecture is conceptually closest to PSL/ManipGen (hierarchical skill decomposition) but replaces their language-only or keypoint-based interfaces with a richer, physically intuitive representation (masked depth + 3rd-view masks + constraints) that is explicitly designed for domain invariance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 83% sim-to-real success rate with only five real-world demonstrations per task and 75% zero-shot performance on unseen tasks demonstrate a practical path toward reducing the data burden in robotic deployment. However, the system still requires per-task RL expert training, relies on GPT-4o API calls for planning and success detection (raising cost and latency concerns), and handles the 'reach' primitive via classical motion planning rather than learned control. Open questions include whether the IOR generalizes to articulated objects, multi-robot settings, or tasks requiring force/tactile feedback, and whether the adaptive DAgger sampling strategy scales to hundreds of tasks without combinatorial expert training.
