## Problem

Multi-head self-attention in Vision Transformers obscures which input patches causally contribute to a model's output, and prior work shows that editing MHSA attention maps does not change VLM predictions—no causal link between attention and output has been established. This limits both interpretability and the ability for users to debug or intervene on vision-language model decisions at test time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250430/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a pair of RGB images, each patchified into n spatial patches plus a [CLS] token (n=49 for ViT-B/32, n=196 for ViT-B/16), and produces a natural-language change caption along with a single per-patch attention map (the [CLS] row of the 1-head co-attention). The attention map is constrained so that total attention over all image patches lies in [0,1]; for no-change pairs the ideal output is an all-zero attention map, which causes the VLM to emit a generic 'no change' response. Unlike post-hoc attribution heatmaps, this attention map is directly editable at inference time.

## Method

TAB is a 1-head co-attention layer inserted after the standard MHSA encoder in CLIP4IDC, with four key modifications: (1) cross-attention is replaced by co-attention where K and V are swapped between the two image branches, (2) the number of heads is reduced from 12 to 1 to yield a single interpretable attention map, (3) the residual skip connection is removed to block information leakage around the bottleneck, and (4) a dynamic gating mechanism zeroes the [CLS] output when total patch attention is zero. Training proceeds in two stages: a CLIP-style contrastive adaptation loss aligns visual and textual representations, followed by a captioning stage that combines cross-entropy next-token prediction with a cosine-distance attention supervision loss against groundtruth bounding-box-derived binary attention maps. This architecture makes the attention a true information gate—zeroing it forces a no-change caption, and correcting it to groundtruth values causally improves the predicted object name—enabling user debugging that is impossible with standard MHSA.

## Results

On OpenImages-I (the primary real-world dataset), TAB4IDC with attention supervision (ViT-B/16) achieves BERTScore 96.6, CIDEr 335.6, and BLEU-4 92.4, outperforming CLIP4IDC (BERTScore 95.1, CIDEr 328.7, BLEU-4 87.5) by +1.5 BERTScore. On CLEVR-Change, TAB4IDC (B/16) reaches BERTScore 75.8 and CIDEr 153.0, surpassing CLIP4IDC (74.2, 144.7) and all RNN/CNN-Transformer baselines. On the multi-change Spot-the-Diff dataset, TAB4IDC (B/16) achieves CIDEr 51.3 and BERTScore 28.3, outperforming CLIP4IDC (47.6, 23.0) and large VLMs like LLaVA-1.5-13B (45.8, 25.9), though OneDiff (CIDEr 56.6) remains stronger due to its larger L/14 + 7B architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIP4IDC (the direct architectural baseline), TAB4IDC wins on OpenImages-I by +1.5 BERTScore (96.6 vs 95.1) and +6.9 CIDEr at B/16, and on Spot-the-Diff by +5.3 CIDEr (51.3 vs 47.6) and +5.3 BERTScore (28.3 vs 23.0) at B/16, while roughly tying on CLEVR-Change (75.8 vs 74.2 BERTScore). Against RNN-attention and CNN-Transformer baselines (DUDA, MCCFormer-D/S, IDC-PCL) on CLEVR-Change, TAB4IDC leads by a wide margin in BERTScore (75.8 vs 67.4 for DUDA) and CIDEr (153.0 vs 112.3 for DUDA). Against large generalist VLMs fine-tuned on Spot-the-Diff (LLaVA-1.5-13B, Mini-Gemini-7B, InternVL2-8B), TAB4IDC outperforms all three in CIDEr (51.3 vs 45.8/53.3/32.2) and BERTScore (28.3 vs 25.9/26.4/25.3). The only baseline that beats TAB4IDC is OneDiff on Spot-the-Diff (CIDEr 56.6 vs 51.3), attributed to its substantially larger L/14 vision encoder and 7B language model. For change localization (PG+ accuracy), TAB with attention supervision achieves 99.19% mean on OpenImages-I (B/16), far exceeding CLIP4IDC's 52.98%, and 96.75% on CLEVR-Change vs CLIP4IDC's 84.47%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CLIP4IDC, a SotA change captioning VLM, by replacing its final multi-head cross-attention layer with a single-head co-attention bottleneck (TAB) inspired by ViLBERT's co-attention and CrossViT's image-comparison design. It departs from the broader interpretability literature (GradCAM, attention-flow aggregation, concept bottleneck models) by arguing that post-hoc attribution maps lack causal grounding, and from the model-editing literature (which edits facts in LLMs or neurons in classifiers) by being the first to enable test-time editing of a vision encoder's attention. The attention supervision loss, which forces the [CLS] row of the attention map to match groundtruth bounding boxes, is a new training objective not present in prior change captioning methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TAB provides the first architectural mechanism that establishes a causal (not merely correlational) link between a vision encoder's attention values and a VLM's text output, enabling users to debug, audit, and correct model predictions at inference time. Key limitations include reliance on attention supervision for accurate localization, softmax spreading that dilutes attention values in multi-change scenes, and the need for smaller patches (B/16) to resolve small changes. Open questions include whether TAB generalizes to general-purpose VLMs like LLaVA (which lack [CLS] tokens), whether the bottleneck can be applied to classification or image-matching tasks, and whether a VLM itself can be used to audit TAB's attention values.
