## Problem

Cross-view segmentation between egocentric and exocentric perspectives remains an open challenge due to drastic viewpoint transformations, scale variations, occlusions, and domain shifts from differing camera optics. Existing approaches either perform direct pixel-level segmentation across views or rely on geometric feature matching, both of which struggle under the extreme view variance inherent to ego-exo pairs. This paper asks whether the problem can be simplified by reformulating it as a mask matching task over pre-segmented candidates rather than predicting masks from scratch.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251456/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a synchronized image pair (one egocentric, one exocentric) and a query object mask in the source view, with no access to semantic labels, object names, or camera pose. FastSAM generates N dense mask candidates in the destination view, and the model selects the single best-matching candidate as output. The task is evaluated on the Ego-Exo4D Correspondences benchmark (1.8M synchronized masks at 1 FPS across 1335 videos), with total inference time of 250 ms (70 ms for FastSAM extraction).

## Method

O-MaMa reformulates cross-view segmentation as object mask matching: FastSAM produces N candidate masks in the destination view, and a Mask-Context Encoder computes per-candidate descriptors by average-pooling DINOv2 ViT-B/14 dense features (upsampled ×4) over both the mask pixels (object descriptor) and an extended bounding box (context descriptor). A Hard Negative Adjacent Mining strategy constructs a Delaunay Triangulation graph over mask centroids to identify 1st- and 2nd-order neighbors as hard negatives, forcing the model to disambiguate nearby objects sharing similar context. An Ego↔Exo Cross Attention module then refines each candidate's object descriptor by attending to the other view's full feature map, injecting global cross-view semantic information. Training uses an InfoNCE-based Mask Matching Contrastive Loss that aligns the positive cross-view pair while pushing apart hard negatives in a shared latent space via a shallow MLP, requiring only 11.6 M trainable parameters.

## Results

On the Ego-Exo4D Correspondences v2 test split, O-MaMa achieves 42.6 IoU (Ego2Exo) and 44.1 IoU (Exo2Ego), with a total IoU of 43.4, representing relative gains of +22.1% and +76.4% over the best official baseline (XMem+XSegTx: 34.9 / 25.0 IoU). On the v1 validation split, O-MaMa scores 50.1 and 54.2 IoU, surpassing the previous SOTA (ObjectRelator: 44.3 / 50.9 IoU) by +13.1% and +6.5% while using only 11.6M trainable parameters versus 1587.3M. The method also achieves the best Location Error (0.033 Ego2Exo, 0.082 Exo2Ego) and Contour Accuracy (0.590, 0.524) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

O-MaMa decisively outperforms all baselines on both evaluation splits. Against the official Ego-Exo4D challenge baselines (XSegTx, XMem, XMem+XSegTx), O-MaMa gains +22.1% (Ego2Exo) and +76.4% (Exo2Ego) relative IoU over the strongest combination XMem+XSegTx, while using only 11.6M trainable parameters versus 67.1M. On the v1 validation split, O-MaMa surpasses the previous SOTA ObjectRelator [19] by +5.8 and +3.3 absolute IoU points (50.1 vs. 44.3 Ego2Exo; 54.2 vs. 50.9 Exo2Ego) with roughly 1% of the trainable parameters (11.6M vs. 1587.3M). Even the untrained k-NN baseline (31.9 / 30.9 IoU) already exceeds XMem+XSegTx on Ego2Exo, demonstrating that the mask-matching reformulation itself is highly effective. PSALM in zero-shot mode (7.4 / 2.1 IoU) and CMX (6.8 / 12.0 IoU) are far behind, confirming that direct cross-view segmentation without the matching reformulation struggles with the extreme viewpoint variance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant cross-view segmentation paradigm (XSegTx, XMem, ObjectRelator/PSALM) by reframing ego-exo correspondence as a retrieval problem over FastSAM-generated mask candidates rather than a pixel-level prediction task. It builds on DINOv2's dense self-supervised features for object-level descriptors and on InfoNCE-style contrastive learning for cross-view alignment, but introduces a novel Hard Negative Adjacent Mining strategy (via Delaunay triangulation) and an Ego↔Exo Cross-Attention module specifically designed for the ego-exo setting. The approach is conceptually closer to feature matching literature (RoMa, SuperGlue) than to segmentation, yet operates at the object-mask level rather than the keypoint level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway is that a simple contrastive matching pipeline with only 11.6M trainable parameters outperforms a 1587M-parameter LLM-based segmentation model (ObjectRelator) on the Ego-Exo4D Correspondences benchmark, suggesting that cross-view correspondence may be more tractable as a retrieval problem than as a generation problem. Limitations include dependence on FastSAM's candidate quality (partial segmentations for complex objects) and degraded performance on very small objects where mask descriptors become unreliable. Open questions include whether this matching formulation generalizes to unsynchronized or multi-object correspondence, and whether the approach transfers to other cross-view settings beyond ego-exo.
