## Problem

Building a versatile, robust 3D whole-body human pose prior remains challenging: existing priors (GMMs, VAEs, NDFs) either generate implausible poses, enforce restrictive Gaussian latents, or fail to generalize across high-dimensional pose manifolds. Moreover, most prior models target body-only poses, neglecting the whole-body domain (hands, face) that is critical for detailed human modeling. The potential of diffusion models to serve as a universal pose prior benefiting diverse downstream tasks via test-time optimization remains largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method consumes degraded measurements in various forms—2D keypoints from monocular images, partially masked pose vectors, noisy 3D joint positions, or noisy 60-frame joint sequences—and recovers full 3D pose parameters in parametric model space: SMPL joint angles (θ ∈ R^{3×21}) and shape (β ∈ R^{10}) for body, MANO joint angles (θ_hand ∈ R^{3×15}) for hands, and FLAME expression coefficients plus jaw pose (θ_face ∈ R^{103}) for face. Unlike single-pass regression, DPoser operates via iterative test-time optimization (Algorithm 1), where the pose is refined over N steps by jointly minimizing a task-specific measurement loss and the diffusion-based regularization, ultimately yielding a 3D human mesh.

## Method

DPoser trains an unconditional diffusion model (fully connected network, ~8.28M params) on normalized joint-angle parameters using sub-VP SDE parameterization, then formulates each pose task as an inverse problem solved via variational diffusion sampling. The key regularization is a one-step denoising L2 loss (L_DPoser) that pulls the current pose toward the diffusion model's denoised estimate at a chosen timestep, making DPoser a drop-in regularizer for any task-specific measurement loss. A critical insight drives the truncated timestep scheduling: pose refinement concentrates at later timesteps (t ≤ 0.3), so the optimization restricts t to a narrow interval (e.g., [0.15, 0.05]) rather than sweeping the full [1.0, 0.0] range, yielding consistent gains over uniform, random, or fixed scheduling. For whole-body modeling, DPoser-X introduces a mixed training strategy that treats part-only data (body, single-hand, two-hand, face) as incomplete whole-body samples with loss applied only to available parts, while randomly masking 20% of whole-body data to force prediction of missing parts—balancing inter-part correlation learning with generalization across diverse data sources.

## Results

On whole-body mesh recovery (ARCTIC), DPoser-X achieves 60.98 PA-MPVPE (All) from scratch, outperforming VPoser-X (66.74) and GMM+L2 (67.25), and further refines SMPLer-X initialization to 24.65 PA-MPVPE (vs. 25.41 with VPoser-X). On body HMR (EHF), DPoser reaches 56.05 PA-MPJPE from scratch, beating GAN-S (57.26) and NRDF (57.38). For motion denoising on AMASS, DPoser achieves 19.87 MPJPE, surpassing HuMoR (22.69) and Pose-NDF (22.13). In hand IK (sparse setting, ReInterHand), DPoser-hand reduces MPJPE to 3.21 mm, a >50% improvement over VPoser (8.25) and NRDF (8.66).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPoser-X consistently outperforms VPoser-X [31] (the primary whole-body prior) across all evaluated tasks: 60.98 vs. 66.74 PA-MPVPE on whole-body HMR, 24.65 vs. 25.41 when refining SMPLer-X, and 21.81 vs. 37.34 min-MPVPE on whole-body pose completion (ARCTIC). Against body-only priors, DPoser beats GMM [1] (56.05 vs. 58.32 PA-MPJPE on EHF), GAN-S [8] (56.05 vs. 57.26), Pose-NDF [43] (56.05 vs. 57.87), and NRDF [15] (56.05 vs. 57.38) on HMR. In motion denoising, DPoser surpasses the specialized motion prior HuMoR [33] (19.87 vs. 22.69 MPJPE on AMASS) and MVAE [24] (19.87 vs. 26.80). For hand IK, DPoser-hand dramatically outperforms VPoser-hand and NRDF-hand by over 50% in the sparse setting (3.21 vs. 8.25/8.66 MPJPE). On face reconstruction (NOW), DPoser-face achieves 11.68/14.58/12.17 MPVPE from scratch, beating VPoser-face (12.23/15.13/12.71) and L2 prior (12.21/15.15/12.74), and when refining MICA [45] initialization, DPoser-face reduces mean error to 10.78 mm vs. MICA's 11.12 mm, while L2 and VPoser actually degrade MICA's results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the score-based diffusion framework (Song et al., 2020) and the variational diffusion sampling formulation for inverse problems (Mardani et al., 2023), repurposing both for the pose domain. It departs from the established line of pose priors—GMMs (SMPLify), VAEs (VPoser), and neural distance fields (Pose-NDF, NRDF)—by arguing that diffusion models offer superior expressiveness without the Gaussian latent bottleneck or limited manifold generalization of prior approaches. While diffusion models have been applied to specific pose tasks (GFPose, DiffPose, motion generation), this work is the first to position them as a universal, task-agnostic prior via a unified inverse-problem formulation, and introduces a pose-specific truncated timestep scheduling and a mixed training strategy for whole-body modeling that prior body-only priors did not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DPoser-X provides the first whole-body (body + hands + face) pose prior, filling a gap where prior methods were limited to body-only modeling, and the mixed training strategy offers a practical recipe for leveraging heterogeneous part-only datasets to improve whole-body generalization. The truncated timestep scheduling is a useful domain-specific insight that could inform other structured-data diffusion applications. However, the test-time optimization paradigm remains computationally expensive per instance, limiting real-time deployment, and the approach's reliance on parametric models (SMPL/MANO/FLAME) constrains its applicability to non-parametric or novel body representations. Open questions include whether the scheduling insight generalizes beyond pose, whether the prior can be distilled for faster inference, and how it performs on out-of-distribution body shapes or non-humanoid morphologies.
