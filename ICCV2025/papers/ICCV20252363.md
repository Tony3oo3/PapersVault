## Problem

Existing VLM-based facial expression recognition methods rely on coarse text templates (e.g., 'a photo of [class]') that fail to capture fine-grained visual cues distinguishing subtle expressions, while fine-tuning the CLIP image encoder risks overfitting and erodes the pretrained model's generalization. No prior work has explored a fully frozen CLIP backbone for FER with explicit external semantic guidance injected into the prompt learning process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252363/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 224×224 RGB facial image as input and produces a facial expression class label (7 or 8 categories depending on the dataset split). The output logits are computed as the sum of a global image-text cosine similarity and a sparse top-k local similarity that selectively aggregates only the k=16 most expression-relevant spatial patches, effectively suppressing background noise. All images are resized to 224×224 with standard augmentations (random cropping, erasing) during training.

## Method

MPA-FER keeps the CLIP backbone (ViT-B/16 or ViT-L/14) fully frozen and introduces three alignment mechanisms. First, an LLM (ChatGPT) generates detailed class-specific visual descriptions that form multi-granularity hard prompts; soft prompts are regularized toward these hard prompts via contrastive losses at both token-level and prompt-level (soft-hard prompt alignment). Second, learnable visual prompt tokens are appended to the token sequence at every frozen encoder layer, and the resulting prompted features are anchored to class-specific prototypes computed as the mean of frozen CLIP features over a training subset (prototype-guided visual alignment). Third, a cross-modal global-local alignment module computes output logits by combining the global CLS-token similarity with a sparse top-k similarity over local patches, forcing the model to attend only to expression-relevant facial regions. The total loss is a weighted sum of the cross-modal classification loss, the soft-hard textual alignment loss, and the prototype-guided visual alignment loss, with only ~0.2–0.4 MB of learnable parameters.

## Results

Using the ViT-L/14 CLIP backbone, MPA-FER achieves 93.74% top-1 accuracy on RAF-DB, 91.81% on FERPlus, 68.89% on AffectNet-7, and 63.74% on AffectNet-8, setting new state-of-the-art on all four benchmarks. With the smaller ViT-B/16 backbone, the method still reaches 92.51% on RAF-DB and 91.15% on FERPlus while training only 0.218 MB of learnable parameters (vs. 0.443 MB for ViT-L/14). The full CLIP image and text encoders remain frozen throughout training; only visual prompts, soft textual prompts, and a small set of alignment parameters are updated.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPA-FER outperforms all 15 compared baselines across every dataset where a head-to-head comparison is available. Among CNN-based methods, the strongest is KTN (88.07% on RAF-DB), which MPA-FER surpasses by 5.67 points; among Transformer-based methods, APViT (91.98%) is the closest, trailing by 1.76 points on RAF-DB. In the CLIP-based category, the nearest competitors are E2NT (92.63% on RAF-DB, 91.18% on FERPlus) and CEPrompt (92.43% on RAF-DB, 67.29% on AffectNet-7); MPA-FER leads E2NT by 1.11 points on RAF-DB and 0.63 points on FERPlus, and leads CEPrompt by 1.60 points on AffectNet-7 and 1.00 points on AffectNet-8. On the large-scale AffectNet benchmarks, MPA-FER improves over the previous best (POSTER at 67.31% on AffectNet-7) by 1.58 points and over CEPrompt (67.29%) by 1.60 points, confirming robustness under in-the-wild conditions. Notably, MPA-FER achieves these gains while keeping the entire CLIP backbone frozen, whereas most competing CLIP-based methods (CLIPER, FER-former, E2NT, CEPrompt) fine-tune the image encoder, incurring higher training cost and overfitting risk.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CoOp prompt-learning paradigm for CLIP by introducing a fully frozen image encoder with learnable visual prompts, departing from the fine-tuning approach adopted by most prior VLM-based FER methods (CLEF, CLIPER, FER-former, E2NT, CEPrompt). The key conceptual departure is the soft-hard prompt alignment mechanism, where LLM-generated class-specific descriptions serve as external semantic anchors to regularize soft prompt learning at both token and prompt levels. Additionally, the prototype-guided visual feature alignment and sparse top-k cross-modal global-local alignment are novel regularization and matching strategies not present in prior prompt-learning FER work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a fully frozen CLIP backbone with only ~0.2 MB of learnable parameters can match or exceed fine-tuned VLM-based FER methods offers a practical, parameter-efficient alternative for deploying FER in resource-constrained settings. The LLM-as-description-generator pipeline is straightforward to replicate and could transfer to other fine-grained recognition tasks where class distinctions are subtle. Open questions include whether the LLM-generated descriptions are truly necessary (vs. simpler enriched templates), how the approach scales to open-set or compound expressions, and whether the frozen-encoder assumption holds when domain shift is extreme (e.g., cross-ethnicity or cross-age generalization).
