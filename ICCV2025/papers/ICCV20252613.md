## Problem

Existing open-world detection and segmentation methods suffer from three architectural deficiencies: late-stage fusion of text, visual, and backbone features that restricts cross-modal alignment in ambiguous scenarios; query selection mechanisms that treat text and visual prompts independently without optimizing their structural alignment; and reliance on caption-derived or fixed vocabularies that inherently constrain generalization to novel or composite objects beyond the training entity set.

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image together with one or more visual prompts (points, boxes, or masks) and/or text prompts (words, phrases, or short sentences), supporting both in-image and cross-image prompting configurations. Visual prompts are encoded via a 6-layer deformable attention transformer (hidden dim 256), while text prompts are encoded by a fine-tuned CLIP-L text encoder. The model outputs instance segmentation masks, bounding boxes, and panoptic segmentation predictions, operating at both object-level and part-level granularity depending on prompt specificity.

## Method

Prompt-DINO builds on the DINO/MaskDINO architecture with three key innovations. First, an early fusion layer integrates text, visual, and backbone features at the initial encoding stage using a gated cross-attention mechanism with a learnable background token that suppresses hallucination when query-key similarity falls below an adaptive threshold. Second, an order-aligned query selection loss based on Kendall's tau coefficient (approximated via tanh for differentiability) enforces ordinal consistency between queries ranked by visual and text prompt similarity during DETR decoding, ensuring semantic-spatial alignment between the two prompt modalities. Third, a generative data engine powered by RAP (a 0.5B-parameter MLLM that assigns semantic labels to image-mask pairs) combined with a dual-path cross-verification pipeline (top-down via Qwen-VL tagging + bottom-up via RAP+SAM) synthesizes 0.5B training instances while filtering 80.5% of noisy annotations through Hungarian matching and BGE-based similarity scoring.

## Results

On in-domain COCO, Prompt-DINO (visual prompt, ViT-L) achieves 51.0 mask AP and 58.0 box AP, surpassing DINOv by 0.6 and 3.8 points respectively. On LVIS, the text-prompt variant reaches 56.5 mask AP and 60.1 box AP, leading APE by 3.5 and 0.5 points. On out-domain ADE20K, the visual-prompt variant achieves 35.9 PQ, 28.0 mask AP, and 32.7 box AP, improving over DINOv by 12.7 PQ, 12.9 mask AP, and 18.4 box AP. On SegInW (25 datasets), Prompt-DINO attains 56.4 AP-avg and 59.1 AP-med, outperforming DINOv by 15.8 and 14.5 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest text-prompt baseline APE [40], Prompt-DINO leads on LVIS by 3.5 mask AP (56.5 vs 53.0) and 0.5 box AP (60.1 vs 59.6), and on ADE20K by 2.4 PQ (29.6 vs 27.2) and 0.2 mask AP (24.6 vs 24.4). Against the strongest visual-prompt baseline DINOv [22], Prompt-DINO achieves substantially larger gains on out-domain ADE20K: +12.7 PQ (35.9 vs 23.2), +12.9 mask AP (28.0 vs 15.1), and +18.4 box AP (32.7 vs 14.3), while on in-domain COCO the margin is smaller (+0.6 mask AP, +3.8 box AP). On SegInW, Prompt-DINO (visual) surpasses DINOv by 15.8 AP-avg and 14.5 AP-med, and surpasses APE (text) by 6.8 AP-avg and 6.9 AP-med. On Cityscapes, Prompt-DINO improves over APE by 1.0 PQ (34.3 vs 33.3) and 8.0 mIoU (52.2 vs 44.2). Prompt-DINO does not surpass the close-set kMaX-DeepLab [50] on COCO mask AP (51.0 vs 58.1), which is expected given the open-world setting, but it is the only method evaluated under both text and visual prompting with competitive in-domain and strong out-domain performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

Prompt-DINO extends the DINO/MaskDINO detection architecture by integrating multimodal prompting (text + visual) into the segmentation pipeline, departing from prior hybrid-prompt methods (T-Rex2, SEEM, DINOv) that process text, visual, and backbone features in separate subspaces and merge them only at later stages. The key architectural departure is an early-fusion mechanism with gated cross-attention that unifies all modalities at the initial encoding stage, combined with a Kendall's-tau-based order alignment loss that explicitly optimizes structural consistency between text-selected and visual-selected queries during DETR decoding. On the data side, the paper moves beyond caption-derived vocabulary approaches (limited to ~2560 classes in SA-1B) by introducing RAP, a 0.5B MLLM that generates semantic labels for arbitrary masks, paired with a dual-path cross-verification pipeline that replaces single-path pseudo-label propagation used in T-Rex2 and YOLO-World.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in open-world segmentation: the fixed-vocabulary constraint of caption-derived training data, and demonstrates that a generative data engine can substantially expand semantic coverage while reducing label noise by 80.5%. The strong out-domain generalization (e.g., 54.7% PQ improvement over DINOv on ADE20K with visual prompts) suggests the unified text-visual prompting paradigm is more robust than either modality alone. Open questions include whether the dual-path verification pipeline scales efficiently to video or 3D segmentation, whether the gated cross-attention mechanism generalizes beyond the ViT-L/CLIP-L configuration tested here, and whether the 0.5B RAP model can be further distilled for real-time annotation pipelines.
