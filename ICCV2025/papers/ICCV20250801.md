## Problem

Grounded video caption generation—producing a natural language caption with temporally dense bounding boxes for multiple noun phrases—lacks both large-scale training data and a dedicated model. Existing video grounding datasets localize only a single spatio-temporal tube per description, annotate bounding boxes on only a few sparse frames, or are restricted to specific domains, making it impossible to train models that handle multi-object, temporally consistent grounding with occlusions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250801/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a video clip of T frames (typically ~8 seconds at 5 fps, 455×256 resolution) and produces a single video-level caption alongside N spatio-temporal bounding box tracks—one per grounded noun phrase—computed at every frame, plus a per-frame temporal objectness score that flags when an object is occluded or has left the frame. Unlike image-based grounded captioning, the caption is generated once for the entire video rather than per frame, and the bounding boxes must remain temporally coherent despite viewpoint and scale changes.

## Method

GROVE extends the image-based GLaMM model to video by interleaving spatio-temporal adapter layers (with learnable scaling parameters initialized to zero, following the Flamingo design) between pre-trained image encoder layers, enabling efficient temporal modeling without retraining the backbone. A bounding box decoder adapted from SAM's mask decoder applies cross-attention in a frame-wise fashion to predict per-frame boxes for each detection token, while a dedicated temporal objectness head predicts per-frame visibility to handle occlusions and objects leaving the frame. Training follows a two-stage recipe: large-scale pre-training on HowToGround1M (1M automatically annotated instructional video clips from HowTo100M, generated via a three-stage pipeline using GLaMM for frame-level grounded captions, Llama2 for video-level caption aggregation from SVO triplets, and LLM-based text classification for temporally consistent object labeling), followed by fine-tuning on the manually annotated iGround dataset (3,513 videos with 230K+ dense bounding boxes).

## Results

On the newly introduced iGround test set, GROVE with pre-training on HowToGround1M followed by fine-tuning (PT+FT) achieves METEOR 21.4, CIDEr 83.5, AP50 40.0, and Recall 28.7 in the all-frames evaluation, substantially outperforming the GLaMM image-based baseline (CIDEr 29.9, AP50 20.8) and the automatic annotation pipeline (CIDEr 40.0, AP50 27.1). On VidSTG declarative sentences, GROVE PT+FT reaches m_sIoU 63.7, surpassing the previous best (DenseVOC, 61.9). On ActivityNet-Entities, GROVE PT+FT achieves F1_loc of 45.04, nearly doubling the GVD baseline (23.80).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GROVE PT+FT outperforms all nine baselines across every evaluated benchmark. On iGround, it beats the GLaMM image-based model [32] by 53.6 points in CIDEr (83.5 vs 29.9) and 19.2 points in AP50 (40.0 vs 20.8), demonstrating the critical benefit of temporal modeling. On VidSTG declarative sentences, GROVE (63.7 m_sIoU) surpasses DenseVOC [52] (61.9), STCAT [14] (61.7), TubeDETR [44] (59.0), and STVGBert [37] (47.3). On VidSTG interrogative sentences, GROVE (55.5 mSIOU) leads VideoGLaMM [27] (39.7), GLaMM+SAM2 [32,33] (38.6), and PG-V-L [26] (35.1) by wide margins. On ActivityNet-Entities, GROVE nearly doubles GVD [51] in F1_loc (45.04 vs 23.80) and F1_all (13.39 vs 7.10). The paper also reports large-margin improvements over prior SOTA on GroundingYouTube and YouCook-Interactions (details in the extended version).

## Relation to Prior Work

GROVE directly extends GLaMM, a state-of-the-art still-image grounded captioning model, to the video domain by introducing spatio-temporal adapters, a frame-wise bounding box decoder, and a temporal objectness head. The automatic annotation pipeline repurposes GLaMM for frame-level grounding and leverages Llama2 for video-level caption aggregation and temporally consistent object labeling, building on the broader trend of using LLMs to synthesize large-scale training data. Compared to prior video grounding work (VidSTG, HC-STVG) that localizes a single spatio-temporal tube for a given query, or ActivityNet-Entities that grounds multiple objects but only on a single frame, this paper formulates the task as jointly generating a caption and producing temporally dense, multi-object bounding boxes across all frames.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical data bottleneck in grounded video captioning by demonstrating that large-scale automatic annotations (HowToGround1M, 1M videos) followed by fine-tuning on a small high-quality manual set (iGround, 3.5K videos) is an effective recipe, validated across multiple benchmarks. The iGround dataset provides the first proper benchmark for multi-object, temporally dense grounded video captioning, enabling rigorous evaluation that was previously impossible. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer videos or more complex multi-person scenes, whether mask-level grounding (as explored by concurrent VideoGLaMM) would yield better downstream utility, and whether the automatic annotation noise fundamentally limits the ceiling of pre-training quality.
