## Problem

Vector quantized models suffer from representation collapse: as codebook size grows, only a small fraction of codes receive gradient updates, leaving most codes permanently inactive and capping scalability well below LLM vocabulary sizes. The paper identifies the root cause as disjoint optimization—only the nearest code vector is updated per sample—while existing fixes either reduce latent dimensionality (sacrificing model capacity) or depend on external pre-trained features (limiting generalization).

## Input / Output

For images, 128×128 RGB inputs are encoded into 16×16 spatial tokens of 128-dim latent vectors, quantized against a codebook of up to 262,144 entries, and decoded back to pixel space. For audio, 1-second waveform windows are processed through the same encoder-decoder pipeline with codebook sizes from 4,096 to 262,144. The discrete output is the index of the nearest code vector; reconstruction quality is measured via rFID/LPIPS/PSNR/SSIM for images and UTMOS/PESQ/STOI for audio.

## Method

SimVQ reparameterizes the codebook C ∈ ℝ^{K×d} as the product CW, where W ∈ ℝ^{d×d} is a single learnable linear basis matrix and C is frozen (initialized from a Gaussian). During training, only W receives gradients from the commitment loss, so the entire codebook is jointly rotated and stretched toward the encoder's output distribution rather than updating only the nearest individual code. The paper proves that with C frozen and W optimized, the update rule for W involves the expectation E[q_k^T q_k] = I (for Gaussian-initialized codes), guaranteeing all elements of W are updated and preventing the disjoint-optimization 'cocoon effect.' This asymmetric design also reduces backpropagation memory from O(Kd) to O(d²), making large codebooks (K ≫ d) computationally cheap. Vanilla VQ is recovered as the special case W = I.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k (128×128, 65,536 codebook), SimVQ achieves 100% codebook utilization with rFID 2.24, LPIPS 0.12, PSNR 24.15, and SSIM 78.4, outperforming all baselines. Scaling the codebook to 262,144 further improves rFID to 1.99 and SSIM to 80.3 while maintaining 100% utilization. On LibriTTS audio reconstruction at 0.9 kbps, SimVQ reaches UTMOS 4.00/3.51 (test-clean/test-other) and PESQ 2.33/2.08, surpassing WavTokenizer (UTMOS 3.74/3.43) at the same bandwidth while sustaining 100% codebook utilization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimVQ outperforms all baselines on rFID at the standard 128-dim latent space: 2.24 vs. VQGAN-LC-CLIP (2.40), VQGAN-FC at 8-dim (2.63), LFQ (2.88), and FSQ (2.80). Traditional VQGAN and VQGAN-EMA suffer severe representation collapse (1.4% and 4.5% utilization respectively), yielding rFID of 3.74 and 3.23. Methods that achieve 100% utilization (FSQ, LFQ, VQGAN-FC) do so by reducing latent dimension to 6–16, sacrificing model capacity; SimVQ maintains full 128-dim capacity while reaching 100% utilization. VQGAN-LC-CLIP relies on an external CLIP model for initialization and shows performance degradation when scaling codebook from 100k to 200k (rFID worsens from 2.62 to 2.66), whereas SimVQ consistently improves with larger codebooks. On audio (LibriTTS), SimVQ at 0.9 kbps beats WavTokenizer (UTMOS 4.00 vs. 3.74) and at 0.975 kbps matches WavTokenizer's 1.05 kbps performance (UTMOS 4.03 vs. 4.00) while maintaining 99.4% utilization versus WavTokenizer's 27%.

## Relation to Prior Work

SimVQ extends the VQ-VAE/VQGAN lineage by identifying the root cause of representation collapse as disjoint codebook optimization—only the nearest code vectors receive gradient updates—and proposing a reparameterization that decouples the codebook into a frozen coefficient matrix C and a learnable linear basis W. This departs from two dominant prior strategies: (1) reducing latent dimensionality (FSQ, LFQ, VQGAN-FC), which sacrifices model capacity, and (2) complex optimization tricks (stochastic quantization, codebook reset, CLIP-based initialization), which add training complexity or external dependencies. The key conceptual move is that optimizing only W (with C frozen) turns the disjoint per-code update into a joint update of the entire latent space, a distinction the authors formalize through gradient analysis and toy experiments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SimVQ offers a drop-in, one-linear-layer fix for representation collapse that preserves full latent dimensionality, requires no external pre-trained models, and scales cleanly from 1k to 262k codebook entries with consistent 100% utilization—addressing a bottleneck that has limited VQ-based tokenizers from matching LLM vocabulary sizes. The O(d²) backpropagation memory (vs. O(Kd)) makes large codebooks practically feasible. Open questions remain: the paper acknowledges that improved VQ reconstruction does not automatically translate to better downstream generative performance, and the requirement to freeze C (rather than jointly optimize C and W, which the authors show leads to collapse) constrains the method's flexibility. Whether SimVQ's benefits persist in autoregressive generation pipelines with very long sequences is left for future work.
