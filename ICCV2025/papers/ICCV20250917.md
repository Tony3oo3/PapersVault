## Problem

Existing CNN-based overlay map generation methods in semiconductor manufacturing struggle to capture contextual information from equipment logs, leaving a critical gap in leveraging operational data for accurate multilayer alignment. Additionally, conventional diffusion transformers (DiT) suffer from slow convergence, making them impractical for the iterative training demands of production-grade overlay prediction. The paper addresses both gaps by proposing a pure transformer architecture that natively processes text and image tokens, combined with a self-supervised distillation strategy to accelerate DiT training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250917/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes five heterogeneous inputs: categorical IDs (equipment, scanner exposure chuck, reticle), free-text equipment logs (5,781 unique tokens), and RGB overlay maps from previous lithography layers at 512×512×3 resolution. All inputs are encoded into a unified joint embedding space via a pre-trained image-text discriminative model trained with unified contrastive learning. The output is a 512×512×3 overlay map for the current lithography layer, generated in latent space (64×64×4 via an 8×-downsampling VAE) and decoded back to pixel space, with the generation conditioned on all five input modalities simultaneously.

## Method

IKDDiT is a latent diffusion transformer that employs a teacher-student architecture with implicit knowledge distillation: the teacher DiT encoder processes all tokens while the student encoder sees only 50% visible tokens, and an implicit discriminator D_φ aligns student and teacher embeddings in a shared space via a combined InfoNCE and adversarial discriminator loss. The DiT blocks use a novel Gated Cross-Attention mechanism where visual patch embeddings serve as queries and conditional text/label embeddings serve as keys and values, with a learnable tanh-gated scalar (initialized to zero) modulating cross-modal signal flow for training stability. Training jointly optimizes three objectives: denoising score matching on unmasked tokens, MAE-style reconstruction of masked tokens, and the discriminator alignment loss, weighted by hyperparameters λ₁ and λ₂. The student decoder, built on MaskDiT+MAE with Adaptive Layer Normalization, handles the full token sequence to predict noise and diagonal covariance for sampling. This design achieves FID 6.8 at 500k iterations—nearly 2× faster convergence than prior masked DiT variants—while reducing overlay misregistration error from 6.8% (pilot run) to 3.2%.

## Results

On the MPOM dataset, IKDDiT achieves the best generative quality among all compared DiT variants: FID 6.64, sFID 5.24, IS 197.39, and Precision 0.81 without classifier-free guidance (CFG), and FID 2.68, sFID 6.12, IS 189.00, Precision 0.76, Recall 0.67 with CFG. In practical semiconductor terms, IKDDiT-G reduces the overlay misregistration error (OME) rate to 3.2% (vs. 6.8% for Pilot Run and 9.2% for GAGAN), increases effective lot size to 24.1 wafers, and cuts scanner loss to 2.3%. Training convergence is also markedly faster: IKDDiT reaches FID 11.6 at 250k iterations, roughly half the iterations needed by DiT (33.1), MDT (25.8), MaskDiT (24.4), or SD-DiT (21.9) at the same checkpoint.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IKDDiT outperforms all four DiT-based baselines (DiT, MDT, MaskDiT, SD-DiT) on FID, sFID, IS, and Precision in both standard and CFG settings; the only metric where it trails is Recall without CFG (0.62 vs. MDT's 0.64), a gap that disappears under CFG where IKDDiT-G leads on all five metrics. Against the domain-specific GAGAN [2], IKDDiT-G reduces the OME rate from 9.2% to 3.2%, increases effective lot size from 21.9 to 24.1 wafers, and lowers scanner loss from 3.8% to 2.3%, attributed to GAGAN's inability to incorporate equipment logs due to its CNN-based architecture. In training efficiency, IKDDiT converges to FID 6.8 at 500k iterations while DiT, MDT, MaskDiT, and SD-DiT reach only 14.1, 12.2, 11.9, and 9.5 respectively at the same budget, indicating roughly 2× faster convergence. The ablation on the discriminative objective confirms that the proposed Implicit Discriminator (FID-15k 24.66) substantially outperforms the self-supervised discrimination used in SD-DiT (FID-15k 30.39), validating the core architectural choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

IKDDiT extends the DiT (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023) and Latent Diffusion (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) framework by integrating masked autoencoder-style training (MAE, MaskDiT) with a novel implicit knowledge distillation mechanism that decouples discriminative and generative objectives via a teacher-student architecture. It departs from prior CNN-based overlay prediction (GAGAN) by adopting a pure transformer architecture that natively processes both image patches and text tokens, and from standard DiT training by introducing an implicit discriminator for inter-token alignment in a unified contrastive embedding space. The gated cross-attention mechanism and UCE-based multimodal conditioning represent architectural departures from vanilla DiT blocks, positioning the work at the intersection of masked generative modeling, contrastive representation learning, and domain-specific industrial AI.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a high-stakes industrial problem where overlay misregistration directly causes wafer rework and scanner capacity loss, and demonstrates a substantial practical improvement (OME rate reduced from 6.8% to 3.2%) over both pilot-run and prior GAN-based approaches. However, all evaluation is confined to a single industry dataset (MPOM), making it difficult to assess whether the implicit distillation and gated cross-attention mechanisms generalize to other diffusion transformer applications or domains. Open questions include whether the ~2× convergence speedup holds at larger scales or with different noise schedules, and whether the implicit discriminator's advantage over self-supervised discrimination (SD-DiT) persists beyond the 150k-iteration ablation budget. The work also raises the broader question of how effectively textual operational logs can be fused with visual data in industrial generative pipelines.
