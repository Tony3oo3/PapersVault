## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods such as LoRA are designed for two-dimensional weight matrices and, when naively extended to higher-dimensional parameters like 4D convolutional kernels, disrupt the inherent spatial locality and structural relationships within those tensors. The paper asks whether matrix-based PEFT can be generalized to arbitrary higher-dimensional parameter spaces while preserving their geometric structure, rather than requiring bespoke strategies for each parameter type.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251743/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained model weights (4D convolutional kernels of shape C_in×C_out×k×k or 2D linear matrices) and task-specific training data, producing updated parameters through a multiplicative (Hadamard) update rather than the conventional additive one. The low-rank factors A and B are optimized in the Lie algebra (a linear vector space), and the resulting perturbation is mapped back to the Lie group via an element-wise exponential map, ensuring the updated kernel remains in the valid parameter space (all entries nonzero). A first-order Taylor approximation (exp(ΔW) ≈ I + ΔW) is used in practice to reduce the update to W + W⊙ΔW, making it computationally equivalent to standard LoRA in cost.

## Method

LieRA constructs an Abelian Lie group G over nonzero tensors with the Hadamard product as the group operation, whose Lie algebra is the full linear space R^{C_out×C_in×k×k}. Low-rank perturbations ΔW = AB are parameterized in the Lie algebra, then lifted back to the group via the element-wise exponential map, yielding the multiplicative update W → W ⊙ exp(ΔW). A first-order Taylor approximation simplifies this to W + W⊙ΔW, which is computationally efficient while preserving the group structure. Theoretically, because the pre-trained weights are nearly full-rank, the Hadamard product W⊙AB achieves full rank capacity min(n,m) versus LoRA's rank-r capacity, giving LieRA greater expressiveness. The framework is model-agnostic and composable, as demonstrated by coupling with PISSA and DoRA to further improve performance on both convolutional and linear layers.

## Results

On the VTAB-1K benchmark with ConvNeXt-V2-B, LieRA (r=16) achieves 75.5% average accuracy, outperforming LoRA (r=16) at 74.1% and approaching the full fine-tuning upper bound of 78.2%. On MS COCO object detection and instance segmentation, LieRA (r=16) reaches 42.3 AllAvg mAP versus LoRA's 38.4, a 3.9-point gain. For commonsense reasoning with LLaMA-7B, LieRA (r=16) scores 75.2% average, surpassing LoRA (r=16) by 4.3 points, and with LLaMA3-8B achieves 85.3% (r=32) versus LoRA's 80.8%. On GLUE with DeBERTaV3-base, LieRA (r=2) attains 88.97 average, exceeding LoRA (r=2) at 88.13 while using only 0.18% of parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LieRA consistently outperforms LoRA [23] across all evaluated tasks and rank settings: +1.4 pts on VTAB-1K (r=16), +3.9 pts on MS COCO (r=16), +4.3 pts on LLaMA-7B commonsense reasoning (r=16), and +0.84 pts on GLUE (DeBERTaV3-base, r=2). Against adapter-based baselines, LieRA (r=16, 0.42% params) beats Series [22] (0.99% params, 70.8 avg) and Parallel [19] (3.54% params, 72.2 avg) on LLaMA-7B commonsense reasoning. On LLaMA3-8B, LieRA (r=32, 0.70% params) surpasses PISSA [35] (75.4 avg) and MiLoRA [59] (81.9 avg) by 9.9 and 3.4 points respectively. In the coupling experiments (Table 7), LieRA's framework improves PISSA from 56.5 to 59.1 and DoRA [33] from 56.6 to 59.0 on the VTAB+COCO average, confirming its compatibility with other PEFT variants. The full fine-tuning baselines (ConvNeXt-V2-B [64] at 78.2 on VTAB, DeBERTaV3 [21] at 88.27/91.36 on GLUE) remain the upper bound, with LieRA closing the gap to within 2.7 and 0.8–2.4 points respectively using only 1.4–14.5% of parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LoRA lineage of low-rank PEFT methods from two-dimensional weight matrices to higher-dimensional parameter spaces (specifically 4D convolutional kernels) by framing parameter updates within a Lie group structure. Rather than designing bespoke strategies for each tensor type (as FLoRA's Tucker decomposition or Atom-filter's filter atoms do), it provides a general mechanism: treat parameters as elements of an Abelian Lie group under Hadamard product, represent perturbations in the corresponding Lie algebra, and map back via the exponential map. The first-order Taylor approximation then reduces the update to a simple element-wise multiplicative correction (W ⊙ (I + ΔW)), connecting the Lie-theoretic formulation back to a computationally tractable operation. The rank capacity analysis further distinguishes LieRA from LoRA by showing the Hadamard product with a near-full-rank pretrained weight yields full-rank updates regardless of the low-rank factor's rank.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, architecture-agnostic recipe for porting any matrix-based PEFT method to higher-dimensional parameters while preserving structural properties like spatial locality, which is practically important as vision foundation models (ConvNeXt, Stable Diffusion) rely heavily on convolutional layers. However, the Lie group construction is somewhat artificial—restricting to non-zero entries and using Hadamard product as the group operation—and the first-order approximation means the method is essentially a multiplicative update, raising the question of whether the full Lie-theoretic machinery is necessary or merely a justification. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes beyond 4D tensors, whether the rank capacity advantage translates to consistent gains at very low ranks, and whether the modest empirical improvements (typically 0.3–1.5%) justify the added conceptual complexity over simpler multiplicative baselines.
