## Problem

Existing diffusion-based Real-ISR methods (e.g., SeeSR, OSEDiff) produce semantically inaccurate and physically unnatural reconstructions in complex or heavily degraded scenes because they lack global content understanding and local structural perception. This paper proposes, for the first time, to adapt a pre-trained autoregressive multimodal generative model (Lumina-mGPT) into a Real-ISR framework that explicitly perceives degradation, understands scene semantics, and restores the image in a unified autoregressive pipeline.

## Input / Output

The model takes a low-quality RGB image (128×128 for 4× or 256×256 for 2× super-resolution) and a fixed text instruction as input. The LQ image is discretized into image tokens via a VQGAN encoder (downsampling factor f=8, codebook size N=16,384), while the instruction is tokenized via BPE; both token sequences are concatenated and fed to the transformer. The model autoregressively outputs three sequential segments: discrete degradation level labels (small/medium/large for noise and blur), a free-form semantic description of the scene, and finally the HQ image tokens which are decoded to a 512×512 restored image.

## Method

PURE fine-tunes Lumina-mGPT via instruction tuning so that a single decoder-only Transformer (with RMSNorm, QK-Norm, SwiGLU, and RoPE) sequentially predicts degradation scores, semantic captions, and HQ image tokens conditioned on the LQ image tokens. The vision tokenizer is upgraded from Lumina-mGPT's original VQGAN (f=16, N=8192) to LlamaGen's VQGAN (f=8, N=16384) to reduce information loss critical for restoration, with the BPE vocabulary expanded from 65,536 to 73,728 to accommodate the larger codebook. Degradation labels come from a pre-trained DEM module (MM-RealSR+), and semantic descriptions are generated by LLaVA on GT images to provide text-visual supervision. At inference, the key innovation is an entropy-based dynamic Top-k sampling strategy: the Top-k value for each image token is computed as k_t = k_min + (k_max − k_min)·σ(H_t − μ), where H_t is the token's prediction entropy, so that low-entropy (edge) regions use small k for structural fidelity while high-entropy (texture) regions use large k for detail richness.

## Results

On full-reference metrics, PURE consistently trails SD-based baselines due to the inherent fidelity loss of VQGAN tokenization (e.g., PSNR 24.73 vs. PASD's 29.73 on DrealSR; SSIM 0.6459 vs. 0.8326). However, on no-reference perceptual metrics, PURE is competitive or best: it achieves the highest CLIPIQA (0.7259) and MANIQA (0.5251) on DrealSR, and the best TOPIQ (0.7751) and CLIPIQA (0.7711) on RealLQ250. In a user study with 50 real-world images, PURE receives the highest preference rate (38.9%), surpassing all five diffusion-based methods. Inference cost is substantially higher: 256.87 s per image with 7,080 M parameters, versus 0.35–11.50 s and 1,410–2,524 M for SD-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PURE underperforms all five SD-based baselines (StableSR, DiffBIR, SeeSR, PASD, OSEDiff) on every full-reference metric across all three reference-based test sets, with the largest gaps on PSNR (e.g., 24.73 vs. PASD's 29.73 on DrealSR, a 5 dB deficit) and SSIM (0.6459 vs. 0.8326). This gap is partly attributable to VQGAN's discrete tokenization losing 0.71–2.61 dB in PSNR upper bound relative to the VAE used by diffusion methods (Table 1). On no-reference metrics, PURE wins on CLIPIQA and TOPIQ for OST-Val (0.7581/0.8148) and RealLQ250 (0.7711/0.7751), and on MANIQA for DrealSR (0.5251 vs. DiffBIR's 0.5007), but trails on NIQE and MUSIQ in most settings. The user study is the strongest evidence in PURE's favor: it earns a 38.9% preference rate, exceeding each individual diffusion baseline, suggesting that human perceptual judgment aligns more with PURE's semantically coherent and structurally natural outputs than with pixel-level fidelity scores. Inference speed is the clearest weakness: PURE requires 256.87 s per 512×512 image on an A100, roughly 7× slower than the next slowest method (StableSR at 11.50 s) and ~730× slower than OSEDiff (0.35 s), with 7,080 M parameters versus 1,410–2,524 M for baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

PURE departs from the dominant Stable Diffusion-based Real-ISR paradigm (StableSR, SeeSR, OSEDiff, PASD, DiffBIR), which relies on VAE latent spaces and parallel diffusion denoising, by instead adapting the autoregressive multimodal model Lumina-mGPT with a LlamaGen VQGAN tokenizer. The perception-understanding-restoration pipeline draws on MM-RealSR+'s degradation estimation module and LLaVA-generated captions as training supervision, but unifies these into a single autoregressive sequence rather than separate conditioning branches. The entropy-based dynamic Top-k sampling strategy is a novel inference-time technique not present in prior diffusion-based or autoregressive image generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that an autoregressive multimodal generative model can produce competitive perceptual-quality Real-ISR results, opening a new research direction that leverages the strong local-structure modeling and global semantic understanding inherent in autoregressive token prediction. However, the practical gap is severe: 256 s inference (vs. 0.35 s for OSEDiff) and 7 B parameters make deployment infeasible, and full-reference fidelity (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) is consistently worse than all diffusion baselines due to the VQGAN tokenization ceiling. Key open questions include whether distillation or parallel decoding can close the speed gap, whether better discrete vision tokenizers can reduce the ~2.6 dB PSNR ceiling loss relative to VAEs, and whether the perception-understanding pipeline generalizes beyond the specific degradation types tested.
