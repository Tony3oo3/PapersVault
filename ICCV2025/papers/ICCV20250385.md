## Problem

Existing parametric body models (SMPL, SMPL-X, STAR, SUPR) derive internal skeletal joints from surface vertices, creating spurious correlations that prevent independent control of bone lengths, body height, and shoulder width without disturbing soft-tissue attributes. This vertex-centric paradigm also causes keypoint fitting to produce unwarranted soft-tissue deviations and limits shape expressivity due to entangled parameter spaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250385/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes four parameter groups—surface shape (β^s, 128 components), facial expression (β^f), skeletal attributes (β^k, 16 components over 76 individual controls), and Euler 3DoF pose (θ)—to produce a posed 3D mesh at up to 115,834 vertices (16× SMPL resolution), with each vertex influenced by up to 8 joints. For the single-image fitting pipeline, a single RGB image is the sole input, and the output is the full set of ATLAS parameters (shape, skeleton, pose, expression) optimized via a multi-stage procedure that decouples keypoint/depth fitting from mask-based shape fitting.

## Method

ATLAS explicitly separates the surface and skeleton into two independent parameter spaces: surface vertices are first customized via linear blendshapes (shape + expression) and sparse non-linear pose correctives while aligned to a fixed template skeleton, then the mesh is simultaneously scaled and posed via LBS using 76 controllable skeletal attributes (15 body-part scales + 61 bone-length translations). The pose correctives combine a lightweight MLP over local joint-group rotations (6D representation) with a sparse, geodesic-initialized linear mapping whose activations are enforced sparse via ReLU masking and L1 regularization, avoiding spurious cross-joint correlations while retaining non-linear expressivity. Training uses a two-stage registration on 600K high-resolution multi-view scans (Goliath dataset, 130 subjects, 240 cameras) plus CAESAR and SizeUSA: skeletal parameters are first fit to triangulated keypoints, then surface shape is optimized for soft-tissue attributes, and autoencoders learn the decoupled shape and skeletal spaces.

## Results

On 3DBodyTex, ATLAS achieves 21.6% lower vertex-to-vertex error than SMPL-X at 32 fitting components, demonstrating superior generalization to unseen identities. On the held-out Goliath-Test set (100 unseen scans from 10 subjects), ATLAS achieves 2.34 mm fitting error versus SMPL-X's 2.78 mm. For single-image mesh fitting, ATLAS reduces vertex error from 87.7 mm (SMPLify-X) to 55.4 mm and joint error from 73.2 mm to 53.7 mm. Non-linear pose correctives further reduce fitting error from 1.82 mm to 1.61 mm compared to linear correctives on the SMPL dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ATLAS is compared against four state-of-the-art parametric body models: SMPL, STAR, SMPL-X, and SUPR. On 3DBodyTex, ATLAS outperforms all four baselines in vertex-to-vertex error across all component counts, with the largest gap (21.6% reduction) against SMPL-X at 32 components. On Goliath-Test, ATLAS achieves 2.34 mm error versus SMPL-X's 2.78 mm, with qualitative improvements concentrated at joints (elbows, knees, shoulders) where baselines exhibit blending artifacts. For single-image fitting, ATLAS substantially outperforms SMPLify-X (SMPL-X's fitting pipeline), reducing vertex error by 37% and joint error by 27%, with the decoupled skeleton-shape optimization preventing keypoint-induced soft-tissue hallucinations. In runtime, ATLAS is faster than SMPL-X at equivalent vertex counts (2.47 ms vs 3.74 ms at 10,475 vertices) and scales to 115,834 vertices at only 5.37 ms. The non-linear pose correctives also outperform the linear variant (1.61 mm vs 1.82 mm), validating the sparse non-linear design over both SMPL's dense linear correctives and GHUM's dense non-linear approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

ATLAS extends the SMPL/SMPL-X/STAR/SUPR lineage of vertex-centric parametric body models by explicitly decoupling the internal skeleton from the external surface, a direction previously explored by BLSM and SKEL but without pose correctives or full finger control. For pose-dependent deformations, it reconciles two competing design choices in the literature—STAR's sparse linear correctives (which avoid spurious joint-vertex correlations) and GHUM's dense non-linear correctives (which capture complex joint behavior)—by introducing sparse non-linear correctives via a lightweight MLP over local joint groups. The single-image fitting pipeline builds on SMPLify-X's optimization framework and incorporates Sapiens for relative depth, while the 600k-scan Goliath dataset represents a substantial scale-up over prior training sets (SMPL: 1.8k, GHUM: 60k).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled skeleton-shape formulation enables precise, independent control of bone lengths and body-part sizes without perturbing soft-tissue attributes, which is critical for downstream applications like VR avatar customization, motion retargeting, and character generation where skeletal and surface edits must not interfere. The Goliath dataset (600k high-resolution scans from 130 subjects across 157 pose identities) is a significant open resource that could accelerate the field. However, the authors acknowledge that 15k subjects do not span the full range of human variation, and high-resolution multi-view capture remains a bottleneck for further scaling. Open questions include whether the decoupled formulation generalizes to even more diverse populations and whether the non-linear correctives can be made more computationally efficient at the 115k-vertex resolution.
