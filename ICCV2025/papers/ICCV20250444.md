## Problem

Existing creative sketch generation methods produce pixels as a whole or as segmented parts, overlooking the intrinsic shape and spatial relationships among individual strokes, resulting in blurry local regions and scattered or isolated strokes. More recent stroke-point approaches like SketchKnitter lack the concept of a stroke entity, performing poorly on complex creative sketches. The paper addresses this by treating each stroke as a discrete entity with a compact vector-quantized representation that captures shape, position, and semantics jointly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250444/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each stroke is a 256×256 rasterized image with an axis-aligned bounding box (half-width, half-height, center x, center y) and a one-hot part label. At inference, the generator starts from a special STR token (unconditional), a class index token, a CLIP-embedded text token, or an initial stroke for completion, and autoregressively emits a sequence of (shape code index, position code index, label) triplets that are assembled into the final 256×256 sketch. The discrete codes are drawn from learned codebooks of size 8192 with 512-dimensional features for both shape and position.

## Method

VQ-SGen uses a two-stage framework. In stage one, each stroke's shape and location are decoupled: the shape is encoded by a CNN autoencoder (with CoordConv and distance-field supervision) into a latent embedding, then compressed via a separate VQ codebook; the position quaternion is similarly compressed into its own codebook, so the shape codebook learns purely shape variation without positional interference. In stage two, two cascaded decoder-only Transformers operate autoregressively: the first (label Transformer) predicts the next stroke's part label given all previous shape/position codes and labels, and the second (code Transformer) predicts the shape and position code indices conditioned on that label and the fused context from the first Transformer. The key insight is that semantic-aware clustering emerges implicitly in the discrete shape code space (verified via UMAP), providing a compressed, semantically structured sampling space that enables high-fidelity, structurally coherent generation without explicit semantic constraints during codebook training.

## Results

On the Creative Birds dataset, VQ-SGen achieves the best FID of 15.78 (vs. 17.48 for DoodleFormer) and the best Generation Diversity of 18.92, while on Creative Creatures it leads on all four metrics: FID 17.61, GD 17.42, CS 0.57, and SDS 1.86. The method narrows the gap to the training-data reference (FID ~19.4/18.1) more closely than any baseline, indicating generated sketches are both more faithful and more diverse. A user study with 50 participants confirms VQ-SGen is preferred over DoodleFormer on 4 of 5 subjective criteria for both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VQ-SGen outperforms DoodleFormer on FID by 1.70 (CB) and 2.82 (CC) and on GD by 1.09 (CB) and 1.19 (CC), while DoodleFormer retains a slight edge on CS for Creative Birds (0.57 vs. 0.53), which the authors attribute to DoodleFormer's simpler, more Inception-friendly outputs. Against DoodlerGAN, VQ-SGen achieves dramatically lower FID (15.78 vs. 39.95 on CB; 17.61 vs. 43.94 on CC) and higher GD, though DoodlerGAN scores higher on CS for Creative Birds (0.69 vs. 0.53). SketchKnitter is clearly the weakest baseline, with FID values of 74.42 and 64.34 and CS as low as 0.14 on Creative Birds, indicating its stroke-point rectification approach struggles with complex creative sketches. On Creative Creatures, VQ-SGen achieves the best score on every metric, including SDS (1.86 vs. 1.68 for DoodleFormer), confirming superior semantic diversity. The user study further validates these quantitative gains, with VQ-SGen preferred over DoodleFormer on creativity, human-likeness, stroke integration, and overall preference for both datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the stroke-entity concept from ContextSeg [32] and the shape/location decoupling from SketchXAI [20], but repurposes these ideas for generation rather than segmentation or explainability. The core technical contribution is adapting the VQ-VAE framework [28] to rasterized stroke images, creating a discrete codebook space for stroke shapes and positions separately. It departs from pixel-level generators (DoodlerGAN, DoodleFormer) and stroke-point-sequence methods (SketchKnitter) by treating each stroke as a compact, quantized token, enabling autoregressive Transformer-based generation over a compressed discrete space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VQ stroke representation opens practical avenues for conditional generation (text-to-sketch via CLIP, class-conditioned generation, sketch completion) that pixel-based methods struggle with, and the emergent semantic clustering in the code space—achieved without explicit semantic supervision—suggests a useful inductive bias for stroke-level modeling. However, evaluation is limited to the relatively small CreativeSketch dataset (8K–9K sketches per category), and the CS metric does not consistently favor the proposed method, raising questions about whether the gains generalize to larger or more diverse sketch corpora. Open questions include whether the VQ stroke paradigm scales to multi-page or 3D sketch generation and whether the discrete code space can support more complex compositional reasoning.
