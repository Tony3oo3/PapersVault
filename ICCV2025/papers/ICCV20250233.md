## Problem

Extending diffusion-based video generation beyond short clips to minute-long sequences faces a fundamental tension: auto-regressive and tuning-free methods accumulate errors or lack long-range priors, while direct long-sequence modeling is computationally prohibitive. Existing hierarchical approaches (keyframe + interpolation) sacrifice fine-grained motion and appearance consistency across adjacent clips. The paper addresses how to jointly model spatial-temporal distributions for long videos while maintaining both per-clip semantic fidelity and global content coherence without excessive memory overhead.

## Input / Output

For generation, the method takes a single text prompt (optionally split into 24 segments via GPT-4o for multi-prompt guidance) and produces a coherent video of up to 1176 frames (~2 minutes at 49 frames per chunk). For editing, it accepts a source video plus a target text prompt and outputs a structurally preserved but semantically transformed long video. Internally, the source video is compressed by a 3D-VAE (8×8×4 ratio) and further condensed by a Resampler into semantic tokens of shape 4×8×12×16, achieving roughly a 3×8×8 compression over the original latent representation.

## Method

TokensGen decomposes long video generation into a two-stage pipeline operating in a condensed token space. First, a To2V model (built on CogVideoX-5B) is trained with a Video Tokenizer (3D-VAE + Patchify + Resampler) that encodes 49-frame clips into 4×8×12×16 semantic tokens; a Cross-Attention Branch with Sem AdaLN injects these tokens into the diffusion transformer alongside text and video embeddings for precise per-clip control. Second, a T2To model (same architecture as CogVideoX but with 80% of 3D-RoPE channels allocated to the temporal axis) is trained to generate the full sequence of semantic tokens for all N=24 chunks simultaneously from text, enforcing global content consistency. At inference, an adaptive FIFO-Diffusion strategy replaces the original FIFO's frame-replication padding: partitions with fewer than f_s frames are denoised jointly, while full partitions use lookahead denoising that updates only the noisier trailing frames, preventing distributional artifacts at clip boundaries.

## Results

On the MiraData test set (100 prompts), TokensGen achieves the highest VBench scores for Motion Smoothness (98.08) and Dynamic Degree (78.95), and dominates the human study with 75.69% Text-Visual Alignment and 75.74% Motion & Content Consistency, far exceeding all baselines. On VBench's Subject Consistency (84.57) and Background Consistency (92.2), TokensGen is slightly below the AP-FIFO+CogVideoX baseline (86.22 and 92.89 respectively), which the authors attribute to VBench's tendency to reward less dynamic videos. The method generates videos up to 2 minutes (1176 frames) using CogVideoX-5B as the backbone with a ~3×8×8 compression ratio on semantic tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TokensGen decisively outperforms Video-Infinity [32] and DiTCtrl [7] on all human-study metrics (75.69% vs 0.0% Text-Visual Alignment; 75.74% vs 0.69%/1.0% Motion & Content Consistency) and on Dynamic Degree (78.95 vs 31.0 and 75.0), indicating that multi-prompt tuning-free methods fail to maintain coherent motion and prompt adherence over minute-long sequences. Against the closest baseline, AP-FIFO+CogVideoX (FIFO-Diffusion with adaptive padding on the same CogVideoX backbone), TokensGen wins on Motion Smoothness (98.08 vs 97.48), Dynamic Degree (78.95 vs 78.57), and both human-study dimensions (75.69% vs 24.31%; 75.74% vs 22.57%), but trails slightly on Subject Consistency (84.57 vs 86.22), Background Consistency (92.2 vs 92.89), and Imaging Quality (63.31 vs 64.10). The authors note that these VBench consistency metrics can favor static content, and the human study confirms TokensGen's superior long-term controllability. Kling [1] is compared qualitatively only (Figure 5), where it shows erratic subject motions and scene discontinuities that TokensGen avoids.

## Relation to Prior Work

TokensGen builds directly on CogVideoX as its pre-trained backbone and extends FIFO-Diffusion's diagonal denoising strategy with an adaptive padding mechanism to fix boundary artifacts. Conceptually, it departs from both auto-regressive clip-by-clip generation (which accumulates errors) and hierarchical keyframe-based methods (which lose fine-grained spatial-temporal detail) by introducing a token-space intermediate: a Video Tokenizer condenses short clips into compact semantic tokens, and a separate T2To transformer generates all tokens for a minute-long video simultaneously, providing global consistency without frame-level autoregression. This positions the work between tuning-free noise-scheduling methods (which lack long-range priors) and full autoregressive approaches, offering a modular middle ground where each subtask—inner-clip control, long-term consistency, and inter-clip smoothness—is handled by a dedicated component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular two-stage design (To2V + T2To + adaptive FIFO) provides a practical, resource-efficient pathway to minute-scale video generation by operating in a compressed token space rather than raw frame space, and its components can be swapped or combined with other short-term control strategies. However, the authors acknowledge that condensed tokens sacrifice fine-grained detail, causing gradual foreground drift over long sequences, and the evaluation is limited to gameplay and landscape videos on a single dataset (MiraData). Open questions include whether multi-scale or hybrid token representations can recover lost detail, whether the approach generalizes to diverse content domains, and whether the T2To model's global token generation can scale beyond ~2 minutes without additional architectural changes.
