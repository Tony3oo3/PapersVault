## Problem

Unpaired image dehazing methods based on contrastive learning introduce haze-unrelated content information and overlook the frequency-domain property that haze degradation is primarily manifested in the amplitude spectrum. Meanwhile, existing diffusion-model-based restoration approaches either require paired data or suffer from unpredictable artifacts and high computational cost when applied directly to full image generation, leaving the unpaired dehazing setting unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252295/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes both a hazy RGB image and an unpaired clear RGB image (256×256 patches, batch size 8) to extract amplitude residuals and supervise the diffusion model. At inference, only the hazy RGB image is required: its amplitude spectrum serves as the conditioning signal for the DM, which generates amplitude residuals from Gaussian noise in just 8 denoising steps. The final output is a dehazed RGB image at the same spatial resolution as the input.

## Method

FrDiff reframes dehazing as frequency-domain amplitude reconstruction: instead of generating a full clear image, a diffusion model reconstructs only the amplitude residual (the distributional gap between hazy and clear amplitude spectra) conditioned on the hazy image's amplitude spectrum. An Amplitude Residual Encoder (ARE) computes this residual parameter-free by normalizing the hazy amplitude to match the clear amplitude's mean and standard deviation, then taking the element-wise difference. A Phase Correction Module (PCM) refines the phase spectrum via a lightweight GAP-SoftMax-Conv attention to remove texture artifacts. The dehazing backbone is a NAFNet-style UNet with Frequency Compensation Layers at multiple scales that add the DM output to the amplitude spectrum before IFFT. Training proceeds in two stages: first, the dehazing network is trained with ARE-provided residuals under adversarial and PatchNCE losses; second, the DM (a 5-ResBlock denoiser) is jointly trained to reconstruct those residuals from noise, adding an L1 diffusion loss. This design requires only 8 diffusion iterations and 8.76M parameters, substantially fewer than full-image DM approaches.

## Results

On the SOTS-Indoor dataset, FrDiff achieves 27.43 dB PSNR and 0.957 SSIM, outperforming the best unsupervised baseline ODCR (26.32/0.945) by 1.11 dB. On SOTS-Outdoor and HSTS-Synth, FrDiff scores 22.75/0.914 and 23.24/0.923 respectively, surpassing the previous best D4 (20.96/0.859 and 22.84/0.923). On real-world no-reference evaluation (HSTS-Real, Fattal's, URHI), FrDiff achieves the lowest FADE and BRISQUE scores (e.g., 0.8732/26.972 on HSTS-Real vs. ODCR's 1.2410/35.800). FrDiff uses only 8.76M parameters and 54.56G FLOPs, the fewest parameters among all compared unsupervised methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FrDiff outperforms all 17 compared baselines on SOTS-Indoor (27.43 dB vs. ODCR's 26.32 dB, +1.11 dB) and SOTS-Outdoor (22.75 dB vs. D4's 20.96 dB, +1.79 dB), and on HSTS-Synth (23.24 dB vs. D4's 22.84 dB, +0.40 dB). On I-HAZE, FrDiff (15.35/0.763) trails D4 (15.61/0.780) by 0.26 dB PSNR, the only dataset where it does not lead. On real-world no-reference metrics (Table 2), FrDiff achieves the best FADE and BRISQUE on all three datasets (HSTS-Real, Fattal's, URHI), with the largest margin on Fattal's (0.2435 FADE vs. ODCR's 0.3871). In terms of efficiency, FrDiff uses 8.76M parameters—roughly 23% fewer than CycleGAN-based methods (11.38M)—while maintaining comparable FLOPs (54.56G vs. 56.89G). The supervised FFANet achieves much higher PSNR on SOTS-Indoor (36.36 dB) but requires paired data and incurs 5.3× more FLOPs (288.3G), highlighting FrDiff's advantage in the unpaired setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

FrDiff builds on the conditional denoising diffusion framework established by DDPM [20] and the two-stage training paradigm for image restoration introduced by HiDiff [9] and DiffIR [58], but departs from them by operating on unpaired data and targeting frequency-domain amplitude residuals rather than full images or paired latent representations. Conceptually, it diverges from the dominant unpaired dehazing approaches—CycleGAN-like methods that enforce bijective domain mapping and contrastive learning methods (CUT, ODCR, UCL-Dehaze) that maximize mutual information between hazy and clear features—by grounding the dehazing objective in the well-known frequency-domain property that haze degradation is primarily encoded in the amplitude spectrum. The Amplitude Residual Encoder's parameter-free distribution alignment (mean/std matching) is a lightweight alternative to the learned encoders in prior contrastive methods, while the Phase Correction Module addresses a texture-fidelity gap that prior frequency-domain dehazing work [61] left unaddressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FrDiff is the first to demonstrate that a diffusion model can be effectively integrated into an unpaired restoration pipeline by reconstructing a compact, illumination-only signal (amplitude residual) rather than a full image, reducing the required denoising steps to just 8 and keeping the total parameter count (8.76M) below most competitors. The frequency-domain framing offers a principled alternative to contrastive learning for unpaired dehazing, avoiding the introduction of haze-unrelated content that plagues MI-maximization approaches. Open questions include whether this amplitude-residual DM strategy generalizes to other unpaired restoration tasks (deblurring, low-light enhancement) where degradation is not purely amplitude-driven, and whether the reliance on unpaired clear images of comparable scene content limits real-world applicability when such data is unavailable.
