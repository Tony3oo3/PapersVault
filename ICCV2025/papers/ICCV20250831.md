## Problem

Visual RL agents suffer from poor sample efficiency when environments contain visual variations (lighting, color, background shifts), and existing disentangled representation learning methods must learn from scratch without any prior world knowledge. This paper asks whether semantic disentanglement capability can be extracted from accessible action-free distracting videos and transferred to downstream control tasks, even across domains with different dynamics, action spaces, and reward structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250831/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pretraining stage consumes 1M frames of action-free distracting videos (64×64 RGB) collected from a potentially different domain (e.g., DMC Reacher Easy videos for MuJoCo Pusher), while the online finetuning stage ingests 64×64 visual observations paired with actions and rewards from the target environment. The model produces 20-dimensional disentangled latent codes, an actor-critic policy over those latents, and predicted future observations, rewards, and discount factors for latent imagination. Notably, the source and target domains can differ in visual appearance, physical dynamics, action space dimensionality, and reward range, as formalized in the cross-domain setup.

## Method

DisWM follows a three-stage pretrain–distill–adapt pipeline. First, an action-free β-VAE video prediction model is pretrained offline on distracting videos with a disentanglement loss (KL to isotropic Gaussian) to extract well-separated semantic factors. Second, an offline-to-online latent distillation loss (KL divergence between the pretrained latent z_disen and the world model latent z_task) transfers the disentanglement capability rather than raw features, mitigating domain mismatch. Third, a DreamerV2-style action-conditioned world model is finetuned online with a combined loss that includes image/reward/discount reconstruction, a disentanglement regularizer (β-weighted KL), and the distillation term (η-weighted, annealed from 0.1 to 0.01). The key insight is that incorporating actions and rewards during online adaptation enriches observation diversity, which in turn strengthens the disentangled representation beyond what the offline video pretraining alone could achieve.

## Results

The paper evaluates DisWM on six cross-domain transfer tasks (Cheetah Run→Walker Walk, Reacher Easy→Cheetah Run, Cheetah Run→Hopper Stand, Finger Spin→Reacher Easy, Finger Spin→Cartpole Swingup, and Reacher Easy→Pusher) using episode return curves over 1×10⁶ environment steps. DisWM consistently achieves the highest sample efficiency and final performance across all tasks, outperforming all baselines. The advantage is most pronounced in large domain-shift settings (e.g., DMC→MuJoCo Pusher), where competing finetuning methods suffer significant performance degradation. No single numerical summary table is provided; results are reported as training curves in Figure 4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DisWM outperforms all four baselines across the six cross-domain tasks. Against TED [7] (temporal disentanglement on top of RAD, the strongest distractor-specific baseline), DisWM achieves better final returns and faster convergence, particularly in tasks with large visual domain gaps. DV2 Finetune (DreamerV2 initialized from distracting-video pretraining) is the second-best method but shows a significant sample-efficiency drop under large distribution shifts (e.g., DMC→MuJoCo), whereas DisWM's latent distillation mitigates this. APV [29] (action-free video pretraining with stacked latent prediction) initially benefits from pretraining but eventually degrades in downstream performance because it lacks environment-specific disentanglement constraints for visual distractors. CURL [18] (contrastive unsupervised RL) struggles to learn effective policies, especially on Hopper Stand, and is consistently the weakest baseline. The ablation in Figure 7 confirms that both the latent distillation term and the disentanglement constraint are individually necessary for DisWM's advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of disentangled representation learning (β-VAE, TED, Dunion et al.) and video-based transfer RL (APV, IPV, PreLAR), but departs from both in a key way: prior DRL methods learn disentangled features from scratch within a single environment, while prior video-pretraining methods transfer general world knowledge without explicitly targeting disentanglement. DisWM bridges these by pretraining a β-VAE-based video prediction model on cross-domain distracting videos and then transferring the disentanglement capability to an action-conditioned world model via KL-based latent distillation, building on the DreamerV2 world-model architecture for behavior learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is that practitioners can leverage any accessible videos with visual variations—regardless of domain match—to improve sample efficiency in downstream VRL, reducing the costly environment interactions needed for robust policies. The cross-domain results (DMC→MuJoCo with different dynamics, action spaces, and reward ranges) suggest the disentanglement prior is domain-agnostic, which is valuable for real-world deployment where matching pretraining data is impractical. However, the authors acknowledge that disentangled representation learning struggles in complex environments, and the approach has not been tested on non-stationary or time-varying distractions, leaving open how well it scales to truly dynamic real-world settings.
