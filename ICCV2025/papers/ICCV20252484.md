## Problem

Existing feed-forward 3D Gaussian splatting models (e.g., GS-LRM) are limited to 1–4 input images with narrow viewing spans, making them unable to reconstruct large real-world scenes that require dozens of images for wide coverage. The core obstacle is that 32 high-resolution images produce a token sequence of ~250K, far exceeding what standard transformer architectures can process due to their quadratic time complexity, leaving optimization-based methods (10+ minutes per scene) as the only viable option for scene-level reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252484/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 32 RGB images at 960×540 resolution along with their Plücker ray embeddings, which are channel-wise concatenated and patchified (patch size 8) into a single ~250K-token sequence. It outputs per-pixel 3D Gaussian parameters (position, scale, rotation, opacity, spherical-harmonic color coefficients) for every pixel across all input views (~17M Gaussians before pruning), which are then rendered into photorealistic novel-view images or, in the 2D GS variant, geometrically accurate depth maps. A Gaussian pruning step retains only the top 50% of Gaussians by opacity at inference to keep rendering tractable.

## Method

Long-LRM uses a hybrid backbone of 24 blocks arranged as three repetitions of {7 Mamba2 blocks + 1 global-attention transformer block}, exploiting Mamba2's linear-complexity state-space recurrence for most of the sequence while preserving the transformer's superior long-range context aggregation at one block per group. A lightweight token-merging module inserted before the 9th block reshapes the token grid, applies a 2×2 strided convolution, and reduces the sequence length by 4× while expanding token dimensionality from 256 to 1024, making ultra-resolution training feasible where all-Mamba or all-Transformer variants run out of memory. Training follows a three-stage curriculum (256→512→960×540) with a composite loss combining MSE+perceptual rendering loss, a scale-invariant depth supervision from DepthAnything to suppress floater artifacts, and an L1 opacity penalty that drives ~60% of Gaussians below the pruning threshold. The Mamba2 blocks employ bidirectional scanning over the concatenated multi-view token sequence, and the entire pipeline runs in ~1 second on a single A100 GPU for a full 360° scene reconstruction.

## Results

On DL3DV-140 with 32 input images at 960×540, Long-LRM achieves PSNR 24.10 / SSIM 0.783 / LPIPS 0.254 in just 1 second, compared to 3D GS at 23.60 / 0.779 / 0.213 in 13 minutes — an 800× speedup with comparable or better PSNR. With 10 post-prediction optimization steps (37 sec total), Long-LRM reaches PSNR 25.60, surpassing all optimization-based baselines by over 1 dB. On the sparse-view RealEstate10K benchmark (2 views, 256×256), Long-LRM achieves PSNR 28.54, outperforming GS-LRM (28.10), MVSplat (26.39), and pixelSplat (25.89).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three optimization-based 3D GS baselines (3D GS, Mip-Splatting, Scaffold-GS) on DL3DV-140 with 32 views, Long-LRM (1 sec) matches or exceeds 3D GS and Mip-Splatting on PSNR (24.10 vs. 23.60 and 23.32) but trails Scaffold-GS (24.77) by ~0.7 dB; all optimization-based methods lead on LPIPS (0.205–0.217 vs. 0.254), indicating better perceptual quality. On Tanks&Temples, Long-LRM wins PSNR over 3D GS (18.38 vs. 18.10) but lags on SSIM (0.601 vs. 0.688) and LPIPS (0.363 vs. 0.269), suggesting weaker geometric fidelity on that dataset. With just 10 post-prediction steps (37 sec), Long-LRM surpasses all three optimization baselines on PSNR for both datasets (25.60 on DL3DV, 18.90 on T&T). In the sparse-view RealEstate10K setting (2 views, 256×256), Long-LRM outperforms pixelSplat by 2.65 dB PSNR, MVSplat by 2.15 dB, and GS-LRM by 0.44 dB, confirming the hybrid Mamba2+Transformer architecture's advantage even at low resolution. For the 2D GS variant on DL3DV-140, the direct feed-forward result (PSNR 23.24) trails optimization-based 2D GS (24.35) by 1.1 dB, but 10 post-prediction steps close the gap and slightly exceed it (24.74 vs. 24.35).

## Relation to Prior Work

Long-LRM extends GS-LRM's feed-forward 3D Gaussian splatting framework from 2–4 low-resolution views to 32 high-resolution (960×540) views, a ~60× increase in input scale. To handle the resulting ~250K-token sequence, it departs from GS-LRM's pure transformer design by adopting a hybrid Mamba2 + Transformer architecture (7:1 ratio, inspired by Jamba), augmented with a lightweight token-merging module and Gaussian pruning. The method also builds on 3D GS as the scene representation, Plücker ray embeddings for camera conditioning, and DepthAnything for soft depth supervision, while exploring compatibility with the 2D GS variant for geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Long-LRM is the first feed-forward method to achieve wide-coverage, scene-level 3D GS reconstruction in ~1 second, an 800× speedup over optimization-based 3D GS, with PSNR within ~0.5 dB of 30K-step optimization. This opens the door to instant, generalizable scene reconstruction for applications like AR/VR and robotics where per-scene optimization is prohibitive. Limitations include a persistent LPIPS gap versus optimization-based methods (suggesting perceptual quality still lags), dependence on DL3DV training distribution, and the fact that the 2D GS variant's depth quality remains below its optimization-based counterpart. Open questions include whether the hybrid architecture and token-merging strategy generalize to even longer sequences or video-length inputs, and whether post-prediction optimization can be further compressed without quality loss.
