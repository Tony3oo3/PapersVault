## Problem

While test-time scaling (TTS) has been shown to significantly improve LLM reasoning and image diffusion quality by allocating more inference-time compute, its potential for video generation remains unexplored. Video generation introduces unique challenges absent in these settings: strong temporal continuity across frames, multi-step iterative denoising in diffusion models, and the high computational cost of generating all frames simultaneously. The paper asks: if a video generation model is allowed to use non-trivial inference-time compute, how much can it improve generation quality for challenging text prompts?

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and N independently sampled Gaussian noise vectors as input, where N is the test-time scaling factor (ranging from 1 to 30 for linear search, 1 to 7 for ToF). The output is a single selected video (H×W×C×T frames) chosen from the search space. The framework is model-agnostic, operating on six pre-trained video generators (0.6B–5B parameters) spanning both diffusion-based (CogVideoX, OpenSora) and autoregressive (NOVA, Pyramid-Flow) architectures, with evaluation on VBench's 16-dimension benchmark.

## Method

Video-T1 reinterprets test-time scaling for video generation as a path-search problem: finding the best trajectory from Gaussian noise space to the target video distribution. Two search algorithms are proposed: (1) Random Linear Search, a Best-of-N baseline that denoises N noise candidates in parallel and selects the highest-scoring video, with O(TN) complexity; and (2) Tree-of-Frames (ToF) Search, an autoregressive tree search that adaptively expands and prunes frame branches at each timestep using test-time verifiers for heuristic scoring, reducing practical complexity to O(N+T). ToF incorporates three key techniques: image-level alignment (progressively evaluating partially denoised frames to skip uninformative early steps), hierarchical prompting (stage-specific verifier prompts for first-frame semantics, intermediate motion continuity, and final text-video alignment), and heuristic pruning (retaining only top-k nodes per level via heap sorting). A multi-verifier ensemble (VisionReward, VideoScore, VideoLLaMA3) with weighted rank aggregation provides robust final selection.

## Results

On VBench, test-time scaling (TTS) consistently improves total scores across all six evaluated video generation models, with gains ranging from +1.58% (NOVA) to +5.86% (Pyramid-Flow FLUX). The largest per-dimension improvements appear in Multiple Objects (up to +53.8% for Pyramid-Flow SD3) and Scene (up to +35.2%), while Motion Smoothness and Temporal Flickering show minimal gains. The ToF search algorithm achieves comparable quality to random linear search at roughly 1/3 the computational cost (e.g., 1.62×10⁷ vs. 5.22×10⁷ GFLOPs for Pyramid-Flow FLUX).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates TTS on four families of video generation models. Pyramid-Flow (FLUX) benefits most from TTS, improving its VBench total score from 81.61 to 86.51 (+5.86%), with dramatic gains in Multiple Objects (+45.6%) and Semantic Score (+18.6%). Pyramid-Flow (SD3) also shows strong improvements (+4.39% total, +53.8% on Multiple Objects). CogVideoX-5B gains +3.44% total with notable Object Class improvement (+16.6%), while CogVideoX-2B achieves +3.68%. OpenSora-v1.2 shows a more modest +2.37% total gain. NOVA, the smallest model (0.6B), sees only +1.58% improvement, suggesting that TTS benefits scale with model capacity. In terms of efficiency, ToF search reduces inference cost by roughly 3× compared to random linear search (e.g., 1.62×10⁷ vs. 5.22×10⁷ GFLOPs for Pyramid-Flow FLUX) while achieving comparable final quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly ports the test-time scaling paradigm from LLMs (best-of-N, tree search, process reward models as in DeepSeek-R1 and OpenAI o1) and from recent image diffusion TTS work [9, 33] into the video generation domain. The key conceptual adaptation is reinterpreting multi-step video denoising as a path-search problem over a tree of frames, where test-time verifiers (VisionReward, VideoScore, VideoLLaMA3) serve as reward signals analogous to LLM process reward models. The Tree-of-Frames (ToF) algorithm is the video-specific counterpart to MCTS-style search in LLMs, exploiting the autoregressive temporal structure of video to prune low-quality branches early rather than denoising all N candidates to completion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic evidence that inference-time compute can improve video generation quality across six diverse models without any retraining, with the ToF algorithm cutting computational cost by roughly 3× compared to linear best-of-N search. However, improvements on dimensions requiring fine-grained temporal control (Motion Smoothness, Temporal Flickering) remain modest, suggesting that current VLM-based verifiers cannot yet assess these properties reliably. Open questions include whether TTS gains compound with longer video lengths, whether the approach transfers to video editing or conditioning tasks, and whether the verifier bottleneck can be addressed with dedicated temporal quality models.
