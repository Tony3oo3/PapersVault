## Problem

Cost volumes in optical flow estimators have quadratic computational and memory complexity with input resolution, accounting for over 50% of FLOPS and requiring 4 GB of memory at Full HD resolution. Despite their dominance in compute cost, their actual contribution to final accuracy in modern RAFT-based pipelines with powerful context encoders has never been rigorously analyzed, leaving open whether they can be eliminated entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252578/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB frames as input, which are stacked and processed by the context network to produce feature maps at 1/8 of the input resolution. The output is a dense 2D optical flow field at full input resolution. Models are trained on crops up to 960×432 but generalize to Full HD (1920×1080) and even 4K (3840×2160) without additional resizing strategies, with the fastest model (ReCoVEr-MN) processing Full HD at 20 FPS using only 500 MB of GPU memory.

## Method

ReCoVEr follows a RAFT/SEA-RAFT architecture with two parallel branches: a feature network (feature extractor + cost volume) and a context network (convolutional backbone producing initial flow and context features), whose outputs are combined by a GRU-based refinement network. The key training strategy is a scheduled cut-off: the full network is trained normally through the TartanAir and FlyingChairs stages, then at the transition to FlyingThings, the feature network is removed and the refinement module is modified to no longer consume cost volume inputs, after which training continues on the shrunk architecture until convergence. This exploits the empirical finding that the refinement network benefits from cost volume features during early training but can learn to function without them at inference if the removal occurs at the right point. Three models are instantiated with different context backbones—MobileNetV3-L (ReCoVEr-MN), ResNet-34 (ReCoVEr-RN), and ConvNeXt-t (ReCoVEr-CX)—trading off speed, memory, and accuracy, with ReCoVEr-CX achieving state-of-the-art EPE while being 1.2× faster and using 6× less memory than SEA-RAFT.

## Results

On the Spring dataset (full resolution, no fine-tuning), ReCoVEr-CX achieves the best total EPE of 0.51, outperforming SEA-RAFT (0.54) while running 1.2× faster (144 ms vs. 170 ms) and using 6× less GPU memory (1.24 GB vs. 8.22 GB). The fastest variant, ReCoVEr-MN, processes Full HD frames in 50 ms with only 0.49 GB memory at a cost of 0.99 EPE, still more than 2× more accurate than PWC-Net (2.31 EPE) at comparable speed. ReCoVEr-RN sits in the middle with 0.62 EPE, 65 ms runtime, and 0.93 GB memory, matching RAFT's accuracy while being 2.5× faster and using less than 1/8th the memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReCoVEr-CX outperforms SEA-RAFT on total Spring EPE (0.51 vs. 0.54) while being 1.2× faster and using 6× less memory, making it strictly superior in both accuracy and efficiency. Against GMA (0.55 EPE, 381 ms, 13.29 GB) and GMFlow (0.65 EPE, 1004 ms, 8.28 GB), ReCoVEr-CX wins on all three axes—accuracy, speed, and memory. Compared to the original RAFT (0.66 EPE, 161 ms, 8.00 GB), ReCoVEr-CX improves accuracy by 0.15 EPE while reducing memory by 85%. Against PWC-Net, ReCoVEr-CX is 3.5× more accurate (0.51 vs. 2.31) at roughly 3× the runtime, while ReCoVEr-MN matches PWC-Net's speed (50 vs. 44 ms) with 2.3× better accuracy (0.99 vs. 2.31). The only scenario where a baseline wins is raw speed: PWC-Net remains the fastest method, but at substantially lower accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the RAFT/SEA-RAFT architecture family, which has been the dominant paradigm for optical flow estimation since 2020. It departs from a core assumption inherited from FlowNet (2015) and FlowNet2 (2017) that explicit cost volumes (or equivalent cross-attention layers) are indispensable for matching pixels across frames. The key conceptual shift is leveraging modern CNN backbones (ConvNeXt, MobileNetV3) that were unavailable during the original FlowNet-era analyses, combined with a simple training strategy (cutting off the feature network mid-training) that allows the context and refinement networks to internalize the matching information previously provided by the cost volume.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cost volumes account for 53–61% of FLOPS and dominate memory usage in current SOTA optical flow estimators, making high-resolution processing (Full HD, 4K) impractical on consumer hardware. By eliminating them, ReCoVEr enables 20 FPS Full HD inference with only 500 MB of GPU memory and processes 4K frames that would require 62 GB with a cost volume. Open questions include whether the approach extends to transformer-based flow estimators (where cross-attention plays the analogous role), whether the refinement network can also be removed (Fig. 6 suggests minimal gains for ConvNeXt), and whether the MobileNetV3 variant's failure on large motions can be addressed without reintroducing explicit matching.
