## Problem

Garment production requires converting flat sketches into realistic sample images while allowing text-based attribute modifications (fabric, color, localized details), but existing diffusion-based methods either lack structural precision (text-to-garment) or cannot support text-guided editing (sketch-driven synthesis). Two specific challenges remain unsolved: fabric attributes are guided solely by text, providing insufficient visual supervision for fine-grained texture rendering, and flat sketches and text prompts may conflict, requiring the model to selectively preserve or modify attributes while maintaining structural coherence.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D flat sketch image (resized to 512×512) and a free-form text prompt specifying garment attributes (color, fabric type, pattern, structure, style) as input, and produces a photorealistic garment image as output. The text prompt can reference any of 150 fabric types in a vector database, 23 color categories, and localized component descriptions. A key design choice is that the output can be either image-biased (faithfully reproducing the flat sketch) or text-biased (applying text-specified modifications), controlled dynamically by the degree of modality alignment.

## Method

HiGarment builds on Stable Diffusion v1.5 with two novel modules. The Multi-modal Semantic Enhancement (MMSE) module extracts visual features via a linear projection trained on MMDGarment and text features via CLIP, then retrieves a fabric sample image from a 150-entry vector database using NER-based label normalization; a Q-Former processes the retrieved fabric image and label to produce a fine-grained fabric embedding, which is cross-attended into both visual and textual features to inject texture details absent from either modality alone. The Harmonized Cross-Attention (HCA) module computes cosine similarity between the enhanced visual and text embeddings, maps it through a sigmoid with a floor parameter λ=0.6 to produce a weight α∈[0.6,1], and scales the attention output (query from visual features, key/value from concatenated visual+text features) by α, so higher alignment favors the sketch while lower alignment shifts control to text. The entire adapter contains 85.15M trainable parameters and is trained with MSE loss for 30 hours on L40 GPUs.

## Results

On the MMDGarment test set (1,000 text-image pairs), HiGarment achieves a CLIPScore of 0.8054, FID of 8.8109, and LPIPS of 0.6008, outperforming the strongest baseline IP-Adapter by 3.7%, 20.3%, and 14.5% respectively. In MLLM-based evaluation (ChatGPT-4o scoring structure, color, fabric, and details), HiGarment scores 6.996 versus 6.355 for IP-Adapter. In the user study with professional garment designers, HiGarment receives an expert score of 7.735 compared to 6.958 for IP-Adapter and 4.799 for DEADiff.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiGarment outperforms all five baselines across all three metrics (CLIPScore, FID, LPIPS). The closest competitor is IP-Adapter, which achieves a CLIPScore of 0.7768 and FID of 11.0334; HiGarment improves these to 0.8054 and 8.8109, respectively. DEADiff achieves the best LPIPS among baselines (0.6780) due to its perceptual-similarity focus, but still trails HiGarment (0.6008) and scores lower on CLIPScore (0.6459) and FID (14.5238), indicating weaker semantic alignment. Uni-ControlNet, which uses the flat sketch as a structural condition, performs worst overall (CLIPScore 0.6869, FID 16.9257, LPIPS 0.8369), confirming that structure-only conditioning is insufficient for realistic garment synthesis. SSR-Encoder and Versatile Diffusion fall in the middle, with SSR-Encoder scoring lowest on CLIPScore (0.6220) and Versatile Diffusion showing moderate performance across all metrics. In the human and MLLM evaluation (Table 2), HiGarment also leads, with expert designers rating it 7.735/10 versus 6.958 for IP-Adapter and 4.799 for DEADiff.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiGarment extends the controllable diffusion generation lineage (Stable Diffusion, IP-Adapter, ControlNet) by introducing a task-specific architecture for garment synthesis. It repurposes BLIP-2's Q-Former as a fabric-retrieval-enhanced semantic module (MMSE) and modifies IP-Adapter's decoupled cross-attention into a dynamically weighted harmonized cross-attention (HCA) that uses a cosine-similarity-driven sigmoid to balance image and text influence. Conceptually, it departs from prior garment synthesis work (Diffcloth, ARMANI, FoldGen) that treats either text or sketch as the sole conditioning signal, instead framing flat sketches and text as complementary but potentially conflicting modalities requiring explicit arbitration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in the fashion production pipeline by formalizing the FS2RG task and releasing MMDGarment, the largest open-source multi-modal garment dataset (20K images with flat sketches, close-ups, and structured annotations), which should lower the barrier for future garment generation research. The dynamic alpha mechanism offers a simple, interpretable knob for controlling image-vs-text fidelity, which could generalize beyond garments to other multi-condition generation tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-garment scenes or 3D garment generation, whether the 150-entry fabric database is sufficient for industrial diversity, and whether the method generalizes beyond Stable Diffusion v1.5 to newer architectures like SDXL or DiT-based models.
