## Problem

Existing DCT-domain JPEG enhancement methods underperform because DCT coefficients are spatially decorrelated (unlike pixel values), making standard convolution and attention operators ineffective. The paper identifies that two latent correlation structures—block-based and point-based—exist within DCT coefficients but have not been explicitly exploited by prior methods, leaving a gap between DCT-domain and pixel-domain enhancement quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251941/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method reads the JPEG bitstream directly from disk, performs only Huffman (entropy) decoding to obtain quantized YCbCr DCT blocks, and dequantizes them using the quantization matrix—completely bypassing the inverse DCT and color-space conversion required by pixel-domain methods. The internal pixel-domain model operates on a 64× reduced input size (coefficient maps or block features) rather than full-resolution pixels. Output is enhanced DCT coefficients that can be entropy-encoded directly into a JPEG file for storage/transmission, or optionally decoded to RGB for display.

## Method

AJQE introduces two enhancement modules that exploit distinct correlation structures in DCT coefficients. EMBC captures block-based correlations via a learnable block-based convolution (kernel and bias generated from the quantization matrix and chroma DCT blocks), applies a pixel-domain model to the resulting block features, and maps back with a second block-based convolution. EMPC captures point-based correlations by extracting 64 coefficient maps (one per DCT frequency position across all blocks), compacting them via low-frequency retention or learnable selection, enhancing them with a pixel-domain model, and expanding back to the original coefficient layout. A feature fusion module integrates outputs from multiple EMBCs and one EMPC, and a UMEP module performs chroma upsampling in the DCT domain using a closed-form matrix derived from DCT basis properties (Lemma 1), ensuring equivalence to pixel-domain nearest-neighbor upsampling. Training uses L1 loss directly in the DCT domain against QF-100 ground-truth coefficients, with the embedded pixel-domain model fine-tuned end-to-end.

## Results

On color JPEG enhancement (BSDS500, LIVE-1, ICB at QF 10–40), the AJQE-adapted DCT-domain models achieve an average 0.35 dB PSNR improvement over their pixel-domain counterparts and 0.41 dB over state-of-the-art DCT-domain methods (QGAC, JDEC). For example, AJQE w/ ARCRL reaches 32.49 dB PSNR on BSDS500 at QF=40, surpassing JDEC (31.96 dB) and QGAC (31.42 dB). In terms of efficiency, the DCT-domain models deliver 60.5% higher enhancement throughput, 38.0% lower training time, and 45.9% fewer FLOPs compared to their pixel-domain versions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five pixel-domain models (AR-CNN, DCAD, DnCNN, FBCNN, ARCRL) and two DCT-domain methods (QGAC, JDEC). AJQE-adapted DCT-domain models consistently outperform their pixel-domain counterparts by 0.1–0.9 dB PSNR across all datasets and QF levels, with the largest gains seen for AR-CNN (up to +0.90 dB on ICB at QF=10) and smaller gains for more advanced models like ARCRL (+0.12 to +0.32 dB). Against the DCT-domain baselines, AJQE w/ ARCRL surpasses JDEC by 0.32–0.55 dB and QGAC by 0.56–0.86 dB on BSDS500, with similar margins on LIVE-1 and ICB. Notably, AJQE w/ FBCNN and AJQE w/ ARCRL achieve the best PSNR across nearly all settings, while AJQE w/ DnCNN occasionally edges out FBCNN at higher QF (e.g., 31.26 vs. 31.55 on LIVE-1 at QF=30). The method also wins on efficiency: 60.5% higher throughput and 45.9% fewer FLOPs than pixel-domain counterparts, and at least 45.9% fewer FLOPs than QGAC and JDEC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against two lines of work: pixel-domain JPEG enhancement (AR-CNN, DnCNN, FBCNN, ARCRL) and earlier DCT-domain methods (QGAC, JDEC). It builds on the block-based convolution idea from Deguerre et al. and Ehrlich et al. by making the 8×8 kernel and bias learnable, and on the coefficient-map representation from Ouyang et al. to construct its EMPC module. The key conceptual departure is diagnosing *why* prior DCT-domain methods underperform—spatial decorrelation of DCT coefficients—and then identifying two overlooked correlation structures (block-based and point-based) that allow standard pixel-domain CNNs to be repurposed in the DCT domain via two adapter modules (EMBC, EMPC).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployment: skipping the IDCT step yields a 60.5% throughput increase and 45.9% FLOP reduction while simultaneously improving PSNR by 0.35 dB over the pixel-domain counterparts, making DCT-domain enhancement both faster and more accurate. The modular design (EMBC + EMPC as adapters around any pixel-domain backbone) offers a reusable recipe that could extend to other transform-domain restoration tasks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other transforms (wavelet, learned), whether the 64× input-size reduction in the inner pixel-domain model limits expressiveness for very high-quality restoration, and whether the method extends to video or progressive JPEG.
