## Problem

Existing diffusion-based image morphing methods require per-instance fine-tuning (LoRA modules, text embedding optimization) that takes ~30 minutes per pair and generalizes poorly to images with differing semantics or layouts. Tuning-free alternatives that simply interpolate latent features suffer from non-directional transitions and identity loss due to the non-linear multi-step denoising process and biases inherited from the pre-trained model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251048/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two 768×768 RGB images as input, automatically generates text captions via LLaVA, and produces J=5 intermediate morphing images in under 30 seconds on a single A100 GPU. The output sequence is designed to transition smoothly and directionally from the left image to the right image, with the two original images serving as the endpoints of the sequence.

## Method

FreeMorph operates entirely within a pre-trained Stable Diffusion (v2.1) UNet without any parameter updates. Its first key component, guidance-aware spherical interpolation, modifies self-attention by replacing K/V features with a blend of those extracted from both input images at each denoising step (spherical feature aggregation), while a prior-driven self-attention mechanism during forward diffusion averages attention across all J intermediate latents to preserve identity. The second component, a step-oriented variation trend, linearly shifts attention weights from the left image to the right image via α_j = j/(J+1), enforcing a consistent directional transition. These mechanisms are scheduled across the DDIM forward and reverse processes using four hyperparameters (λ1–λ4) that assign different attention strategies to different step ranges, and high-frequency Gaussian noise is injected via FFT after forward diffusion to relax overly rigid constraints.

## Results

On the combined MorphBench and Morph4Data benchmarks, FreeMorph achieves the best scores across all three metrics: LPIPS-sum 162.99, FID-mean 152.88, and PPL-sum 4192.82 (all lower is better). Compared to the strongest baseline DiffMorpher, FreeMorph reduces LPIPS-sum by 13.8%, FID-mean by 26.9%, and PPL-sum by 10.0%. In a user study with 30 participants, FreeMorph is preferred 60.13% of the time versus 17.16% for IMPUS and 14.89% for DiffMorpher. The method completes a full morphing sequence in under 30 seconds on an A100 GPU, making it 50× faster than IMPUS and 10× faster than DiffMorpher.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeMorph outperforms both baselines on every metric across both evaluation datasets. Against IMPUS [45], FreeMorph reduces overall LPIPS-sum from 265.40 to 162.99 (−38.6%), FID-mean from 174.76 to 152.88 (−12.5%), and PPL-sum from 6462.93 to 4192.82 (−35.1%), while also being 50× faster (no per-instance training). Against DiffMorpher [46], the gains are more modest but consistent: LPIPS-sum drops from 189.13 to 162.99 (−13.8%), FID-mean from 209.10 to 152.88 (−26.9%), and PPL-sum from 4658.25 to 4192.82 (−10.0%), with a 10× speed advantage. The spherical-interpolation-only baseline (Slerp) is clearly inferior to both trained methods, confirming that naive latent-space interpolation without attention-level guidance is insufficient. In the user study, FreeMorph receives 60.13% preference votes, nearly 4× the combined preference for the two strongest baselines (32.05% total), indicating that the quantitative gains translate into perceptually meaningful improvements in transition smoothness and identity preservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreeMorph builds on the latent diffusion model framework (Stable Diffusion + DDIM) and draws directly from tuning-free image editing techniques (P2P, PnP, MasaCtrl) that manipulate self-attention at inference time. It departs from the dominant line of diffusion-based morphing work (Wang & Golland, IMPUS, DiffMorpher), which requires per-instance LoRA fine-tuning or text-embedding optimization, by instead injecting guidance through spherical interpolation of key/value features and a step-oriented blending schedule. The paper also introduces Morph4Data as a new evaluation benchmark to complement MorphBench, addressing the bias toward semantically similar pairs in prior benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating per-instance training entirely, FreeMorph makes high-quality image morphing practical for real-time or interactive applications (under 30 seconds vs. ~30 minutes for IMPUS), while preserving the full generalizability of the underlying diffusion model. The approach also reframes text-guided image editing as a special case of morphing, suggesting a unifying perspective. Limitations include reliance on Stable Diffusion 2.1's UNet architecture, a fixed number of intermediate frames (J=5), and 768×768 resolution; open questions include whether the attention-modification strategy transfers to DiT-based models, whether it extends to video morphing or multi-image blending, and how it scales to more than two source images.
