## Problem

State-of-the-art Multimodal Diffusion Transformers (MM-DiTs) like FLUX and SD3.5 still produce images with poor text-image alignment, particularly in spatial relationships and attribute binding. The paper identifies two root causes in the unified self-attention mechanism: (1) cross-modal attention suppression arising from the large numerical imbalance between visual and text tokens (e.g., 4096 vs. 512 in FLUX), which dilutes textual guidance in the shared softmax, and (2) timestep-insensitive QK weighting that fails to prioritize text-visual interaction during the early denoising steps where global layout is established.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a drop-in modification to existing MM-DiT models (FLUX.1-Dev, SD3.5-Medium), taking a standard text prompt as input and producing a generated RGB image (1024×1024 for FLUX). The temperature scaling is applied only to the visual-text attention logits within the unified self-attention computation during the denoising loop, with no changes to the input pipeline or output format. The LoRA fine-tuning stage uses 10K image-text pairs from LAION with LLaVA-refined captions, training only on early timesteps (t ≥ 970 out of 1000) to focus on layout formation.

## Method

TACA introduces a modality-specific temperature coefficient γ > 1 that scales the visual-text attention logits (s_vt) within the MM-DiT's unified softmax, effectively boosting the relative weight of cross-modal interactions against the dominant visual-visual terms. To account for the denoising dynamics, γ is made timestep-dependent via a piecewise function: γ(t) = γ₀ (e.g., 1.2) for t ≥ t_thresh (early steps where layout is formed) and γ(t) = 1 for t < t_thresh (later detail-refinement steps), requiring no new learnable parameters. To suppress artifacts caused by the shifted attention output distribution, the authors apply LoRA (rank 16 or 64) fine-tuning to the attention layers using a velocity-prediction loss under the flow-matching paradigm, restricted to early timesteps. The approach is parameter-efficient, adding only two hyperparameters (γ₀ and t_thresh) that are robust across a reasonable range (γ₀ ∈ [1.15, 1.25], t_thresh ∈ [930, 970]).

## Results

On T2I-CompBench, TACA (r=64) applied to FLUX.1-Dev improves Spatial Relationship from 0.2066 to 0.2405 (+16.4% relative) and Shape from 0.5064 to 0.5362 (+5.9% relative), while also gaining on Color (0.7678→0.7843) and Texture (0.6756→0.6872). For SD3.5-Medium, TACA (r=64) boosts Spatial Relationship from 0.2087 to 0.2678 (+28.3% relative) and Shape from 0.5770 to 0.5938 (+2.9% relative). Image quality is preserved or slightly improved, with MUSIQ increasing from 0.7186 to 0.7212 (FLUX) and MANIQA from 0.5149 to 0.5292 (FLUX). A user study with 50 participants shows 76.42% preference for TACA-generated images overall and 82.25% for text-image alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares TACA against two baselines: FLUX.1-Dev and SD3.5-Medium, both state-of-the-art MM-DiT text-to-image models. TACA consistently outperforms both baselines across all six T2I-CompBench metrics (Color, Shape, Texture, Spatial, Non-Spatial, Complex) for both model families. The largest relative gains appear in Spatial Relationship: +16.4% for FLUX.1-Dev (0.2066→0.2405) and +28.3% for SD3.5-Medium (0.2087→0.2678), indicating that the temperature-scaled cross-modal attention most strongly addresses spatial composition errors. For SD3.5-Medium, the lower-rank LoRA (r=16) actually achieves the best Complex score (0.4505 vs. 0.4470 for r=64), suggesting that less aggressive fine-tuning can be preferable for holistic prompt adherence. Image quality metrics (MUSIQ, MANIQA) confirm that TACA does not degrade visual fidelity, with slight improvements on both models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the MM-DiT architecture (Stable Diffusion 3), which unified text and visual tokens into a single self-attention sequence, replacing the unidirectional cross-attention of earlier designs like CrossDiT (PixArt-α). It departs from prior alignment methods (Attend-and-Excite, layout-guided approaches) that were designed for U-Net cross-attention blocks and do not translate to DiT-based architectures. The key conceptual departure is diagnosing the unified softmax as inherently suppressing cross-modal attention due to the N_vis ≫ N_txt token imbalance, and proposing a minimal temperature-scaling fix (TACA) combined with LoRA fine-tuning rather than architectural redesign.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TACA offers a parameter-free, drop-in modification to any MM-DiT model that yields meaningful alignment gains (up to 28.3% relative improvement in spatial relationships for SD3.5) with negligible computational overhead, making it practically attractive for practitioners using FLUX or SD3.5. However, the paper acknowledges two open limitations: a dilution effect when extending LoRA training to text-to-video, and the inability to adaptively select the scaling factor based on the actual degree of misalignment in a given prompt. Whether this temperature-scaling principle generalizes beyond image generation to video or 3D synthesis, and whether learned adaptive γ(t) schedules could replace the fixed piecewise function, remain open questions.
