## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary detection and segmentation methods either target a single prompt type (text, visual, or prompt-free) or, when unified, incur prohibitive training and inference costs that preclude real-time deployment. No single efficient model simultaneously supports diverse open prompt mechanisms for both detection and segmentation while maintaining the speed characteristic of YOLO-family architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252640/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image plus one of three prompt modalities: free-form text strings (encoded by MobileCLIP-B(LT)), visual cues formalized as binary masks over bounding boxes, or no explicit prompt (prompt-free mode using a built-in vocabulary of 4,585 category names). For each anchor point, it outputs a bounding box, a segmentation mask (via YOLACT-style prototype and coefficient prediction), and a category label obtained by contrasting the anchor's object embedding against the prompt embedding. In prompt-free mode, a specialized embedding first filters anchor points containing objects, and only those are lazily matched against the vocabulary for category retrieval.

## Method

YOLOE builds on YOLOv8/YOLO11 and introduces three complementary strategies. For text prompts, RepRTA uses a single SwiGLU FFN block to refine cached CLIP text embeddings during training, then re-parameterizes this auxiliary network into the classification head at inference, yielding an architecture identical to standard YOLO with zero additional overhead. For visual prompts, SAVPE decouples processing into a semantic branch (prompt-agnostic multi-scale features) and an activation branch (grouped prompt-aware weights in A=16 low-dimensional channels), aggregating them into the prompt embedding with minimal complexity. For prompt-free detection, LRPC reformulates the task as retrieval rather than generation: a trained specialized embedding identifies object-containing anchor points via a threshold, and only those points are matched against the built-in vocabulary, avoiding costly language-model dependency and achieving 1.7× speedup over direct vocabulary contrast.

## Results

On LVIS minival (zero-shot detection), YOLOE-v8-S/M/L achieve 27.9/32.6/35.9 AP with text prompts and 26.2/31.0/34.2 AP with visual prompts, outperforming YOLO-Worldv2-S/M/L by 3.5/0.2/0.4 AP while training 3× faster (12.0/17.0/22.5 h vs. 41.7/60.0/80.0 h on 8 GPUs). Inference speedups of 1.4×/1.3×/1.3× on T4 GPU and 1.3×/1.2×/1.2× on iPhone 12 are achieved over YOLO-Worldv2. For prompt-free detection, YOLOE-v8-L reaches 27.2 AP with 6.3× fewer parameters and 53× higher FPS than GenerateU (Swin-T). On COCO transfer (full tuning), YOLOE-v8-L surpasses closed-set YOLOv8-L by 0.6 AP^b and 0.4 AP^m with nearly 4× fewer training epochs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against YOLO-Worldv2 (the closest same-architecture baseline), YOLOE wins at every scale: +3.5 AP for S, +0.2 AP for M, +0.4 AP for L on LVIS, while training 3× faster and running 1.3–1.4× faster at inference. Against the large-vocabulary text-prompted detectors (GLIP-T 26.0, GLIPv2-T 29.0, GDINO-T 27.4, DetCLIP-T 34.4, G-1.5 Edge 33.5), YOLOE-v8-L (35.9 AP) leads all of them by 1.5–9.9 AP with far fewer parameters (45M vs. 155–232M). For visual prompts, YOLOE-v8-L (34.2 AP) trails T-Rex2 (37.4 AP) by 3.2 AP overall, but the paper attributes this to T-Rex2 using 2× more training data (3.1M vs. 1.4M images) and 16 A100 GPUs; on rare categories YOLOE-v8-L actually exceeds T-Rex2 by 3.3 AP_r. In the prompt-free setting, YOLOE-v8-L (27.2 AP, 25.3 FPS) beats GenerateU-Swin-T (26.8 AP, 0.48 FPS) by 0.4 AP with 6.3× fewer parameters and 53× higher throughput. For COCO downstream transfer, YOLOE-v8-L (53.0 AP^b, 42.7 AP^m) surpasses closed-set YOLOv8-L (52.4, 42.3) by 0.6 AP^b and 0.4 AP^m while training 80 vs. 300 epochs (~4× less time).

## Relation to Prior Work

YOLOE extends the YOLO-World line of work on open-vocabulary detection within the YOLO architecture, but departs from its reliance on cross-modality fusion by introducing RepRTA, a re-parameterizable auxiliary network that refines text embeddings during training and vanishes at inference. For visual prompts, it replaces the transformer-heavy encoders in T-Rex2 and DINOv with a lightweight decoupled two-branch design (SAVPE), and for prompt-free detection it reframes the generative approach of GenerateU and DINO-X as a retrieval problem (LRPC) over a built-in vocabulary. The overarching contribution is unifying all three prompt mechanisms in a single CNN-based model, whereas prior efforts either target one prompt type or, in the case of DINO-X, achieve unification at the cost of prohibitive training and inference overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

YOLOE makes open-vocabulary detection and segmentation genuinely deployable on edge hardware (iPhone 12, T4 GPU) by eliminating the transformer and language-model dependencies that have kept prior open-set methods in the research lab. The re-parameterization trick—where the text-alignment network folds into the classification head, yielding an architecture identical to vanilla YOLO at inference—offers a template for adding open-vocabulary capability to any YOLO variant with zero deployment penalty. Limitations include a small AP_f drop from multi-task learning (detection + segmentation jointly), dependence on MobileCLIP for text encoding during training, and the LRPC vocabulary being fixed at 4,585 categories, which constrains truly open-ended scenarios. Open questions include whether the retrieval-based prompt-free approach can match generative methods on long-tail or compositional categories, and whether the unified multi-prompt formulation generalizes beyond the YOLO family to DETR-style architectures.
