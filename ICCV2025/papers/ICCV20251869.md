## Problem

Current Language-guided Affordance Segmentation (LASO) methods memorize category-specific cues rather than learning generalizable affordance knowledge, causing significant performance drops when evaluated on unseen object categories. The paper identifies two root causes: underutilized generalizable geometric and linguistic patterns in intermediate backbone layers that are isolated by late-stage fusion, and the inability to handle substantial structural variability in affordance regions across categories due to a lack of geometric priors about the target region.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language question (tokenized via RoBERTa into N_Q tokens) and a 3D object point cloud (tokenized via Farthest Point Sampling into N_F local patches encoded by PointNet++). During decoding, the point cloud is upsampled 64× with RepKPU and rendered into L=4 multi-view images via Mitsuba3, which are segmented by an off-the-shelf VLM (LISA or GLaMM) to produce 2D binary masks. The output is a per-point binary mask over the original N_P points, obtained by mean-pooling dynamic-kernel convolutions followed by a sigmoid.

## Method

GLANCE introduces two components on top of the LASO pipeline. First, a Cross-Modal Connector (CMC) is a lightweight MLP-based adapter inserted into the last 4 transformer blocks of frozen Uni3D and RoBERTa backbones, bridging MHA and FFN layers via shared linear projections into a common 64-dim space and a shared MLP, enabling joint gradient flow between modalities without the computational cost of attention over dense 3D tokens. Second, a Geometry-Aware Query Generator (GAQG) projects FPS-sampled points onto multi-view 2D VLM segmentation masks and selects K key points by combining intra-view reliability (exponential decay of distance to mask center) and cross-view consistency (sum of per-view scores), then uses their encoded features as geometric queries. A DETR-style transformer decoder takes the concatenated geometric queries and text embedding as queries against point-wise features as keys/values, producing dynamic kernels that convolve with point features to yield the final affordance mask, trained with Dice + BCE loss.

## Results

On the LASO benchmark, GLANCE achieves 23.3% mIoU in the Seen setting and 18.7% mIoU in the Unseen setting, outperforming the previous best method GEAL (22.0% / 16.7%) by 1.3 and 2.0 points respectively. The Unseen-to-Seen mIoU gap for GLANCE is 4.6 points, substantially smaller than GEAL's 5.3-point gap, indicating better cross-category generalization. On LangSHAPE, GLANCE reaches 63.8% mIoU, surpassing PointRefer (60.5%) and IAGNet (59.2%) by 3.3 and 4.6 points, while also achieving the best AUC (92.6), SIM (0.751), and lowest MAE (0.132).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLANCE outperforms all six baselines on both the LASO and LangSHAPE benchmarks across all four metrics. On LASO Seen, GLANCE leads GEAL (the closest competitor) by 1.3 mIoU points (23.3 vs. 22.0) and PointRefer by 2.5 points (23.3 vs. 20.8). The advantage is more pronounced in the Unseen setting, where GLANCE gains 2.0 mIoU over GEAL (18.7 vs. 16.7) and 4.1 over PointRefer (18.7 vs. 14.6), confirming that the cross-modal connector and geometry-aware query generator specifically address the generalization gap. On LangSHAPE, GLANCE achieves 63.8% mIoU versus 60.5% for PointRefer and 59.2% for IAGNet, with a 2.0-point MAE reduction over PointRefer (0.132 vs. 0.152). Notably, GLANCE's Unseen SIM (0.532) is lower than GEAL's (0.567), suggesting that while mIoU and AUC improve, the spatial overlap quality in the Unseen regime still lags slightly behind the prior best on that particular metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LASO framework [16] by targeting its underexplored 'unseen category' evaluation setting, where models must transfer affordance knowledge (e.g., 'grasp') to object categories withheld from training. Methodologically, it draws on parameter-efficient adapter designs (Houlsby et al., Clip-Adapter) but deliberately departs from attention-based fusion in favor of lightweight MLP connectors, motivated by the high token count and data scarcity of 3D point clouds. For geometric priors, it builds on VLM-assisted 3D segmentation work (PartDistill, PartSLIP) but bypasses 2D feature projection entirely, instead using multi-view VLM binary masks as reliability-weighted voting signals for 3D key-point selection. The query-based decoder follows the DETR-style formulation already used in LASO itself, augmented with geometry-aware queries derived from the VLM masks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap for embodied agents: current LASO models memorize category-specific patterns and degrade sharply on novel objects, limiting real-world deployment. The diagnosis that late-stage cross-modal fusion and absence of structural priors are the root causes is well-supported by ablations, and the proposed remedies (intermediate MLP connectors + VLM-guided geometric queries) are modular and could be adopted by other 3D language-grounded tasks. However, the approach introduces a dependency on external VLM segmentation quality (LISA vs. GLaMM comparison shows sensitivity), and the unseen-setting improvement, while consistent, remains modest (~2 mIoU over the prior best). Open questions include whether the VLM dependency can be internalized, whether the method scales to more complex sequential affordances, and whether the geometric prior strategy generalizes beyond the relatively simple affordance types in LASO.
