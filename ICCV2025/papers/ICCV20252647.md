## Problem

Existing 3D head reconstruction methods rely on parametric template models (e.g., 3DMM) that oversimplify non-facial regions like hair, and require post-processing alignment with 2D landmarks or head pose that introduces errors under perspective projection, particularly in multi-view settings. Additionally, dense facial correspondence search and geometry reconstruction have been treated as separate problems. WarpHE4D unifies both tasks by directly regressing per-pixel 4D vectors without any template fitting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252647/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB head image resized to 448×448 (no resolution augmentation) and produces a dense 4D head map W ∈ R^(h×w×4) where each pixel encodes FLAME UV coordinates (2D), a nose-centered normalized depth (1D), and a confidence score (1D). The nose-centered normalization scales raw depth so the nose tip sits at z=0 and the image plane spans [-1,1], ensuring consistent scale across subjects, hairstyles, and camera parameters. The output can be back-projected into a colored point cloud with UV mapping, which is directly convertible to a topology-unified mesh or used for confidence-weighted multi-view texture optimization.

## Method

The core insight is that DINOv2 features remain consistent across head poses, occlusions, and even unwrapped FLAME texture maps, enabling image-to-UV correspondence without explicit 3DMM priors. The architecture employs two encoders (image and fixed UV texture) each combining frozen DINOv2 for coarse features and trainable VGG19 for fine features, followed by a coarse ViT decoder that predicts quantized B=64-bin distributions for U, V, and D at 1/14 resolution, and a fine CNN decoder that progressively upsamples and refines the head map over four levels using grid sampling to warp UV-domain features into the image domain. A novel nose-centered depth normalization replaces scale-invariant or average-depth formulations to avoid non-uniform scaling. Training uses a composite loss: BCE on quantized bins (coarse), Charbonnier regression (fine), a keypoint loss leveraging 68 2D facial landmarks to correct UV accuracy around eyes and mouth, and BCE on the confidence map.

## Results

On the FaceScape benchmark, WarpHE4D achieves 2.82 mm average chamfer distance on FS-Wild and 1.44 mm on FS-Lab, outperforming the best baselines (HRN at 2.91 mm and PRNet at 3.56 mm respectively). On the Multiface benchmark, the method achieves 0.9115 3DAUC on the facial region (vs. DECA's 0.8936) and 0.7091 3DAUC on the non-facial region (vs. DECA's 0.6317), without benefiting from Procrustes alignment. On FaceVerse, WarpHE4D maintains UVAUC above 0.85 across all azimuth angles (0°–90°) and elevation angles (0°–30°), whereas DECA drops to 0.63–0.74, demonstrating superior pose robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WarpHE4D outperforms all template-based baselines (PRNet, 3DDFA v2/v3, HRN) by a large margin on both facial and non-facial regions, with UVAUC gains of 0.09–0.58 over these methods. Against the strongest baselines DECA and EMOCA, the method trades a small facial UVAUC deficit (−0.021 vs. DECA) for a clear 3DAUC win (+0.018) and a substantial non-facial region advantage (+0.084 UVAUC, +0.077 3DAUC over DECA), highlighting its ability to reconstruct ears and hair that template models oversimplify. Critically, all baselines benefit from Procrustes alignment to the ground-truth mesh before evaluation, while WarpHE4D does not, making the facial-region parity even more notable. On FaceVerse, the gap widens dramatically under pose variation: at 90° azimuth, WarpHE4D achieves 0.8737 UVAUC versus DECA's 0.7391, a 0.135 margin that grows with head rotation. The ablation with Sapiens (a human-centric foundation model) performs far worse than even the no-SSL variant, confirming that DINOv2's general-purpose features are specifically suited for this task rather than domain-specific human models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 3DMM-based paradigm (DECA, EMOCA, 3DDFA) by reframing head reconstruction as a per-pixel regression that directly outputs a 4D head map (UV coordinates + nose-centered depth + confidence), eliminating template fitting and post-processing alignment. Architecturally, it extends the ROMA dense matching framework by replacing its generic feature matching with DINOv2 self-supervised features as a shared semantic-geometric prior between the image and a fixed UV texture map, and by jointly regressing depth alongside UV. Conceptually, it follows FlowFace's UV-to-image flow idea but inverts the direction (image-to-UV) and grounds the correspondence in self-supervised features rather than parametric model constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the 3DMM fitting step, the method achieves full-head coverage (including hair and ears) and robustness to extreme poses (>90°) without Procrustes alignment, which is critical for multi-view and uncalibrated-camera avatar pipelines. The ablation showing that Sapiens—a larger, human-specific foundation model—performs worse than no self-supervised features at all challenges the assumption that domain-specific scale always helps, suggesting DINOv2's general-purpose 3D consistency is more transferable than specialized human priors. A key open question is how to represent highly variable non-facial regions (especially hair) beyond mesh-based UV mapping, which the authors acknowledge as an inherent limitation.
