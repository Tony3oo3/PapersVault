## Problem

Current Dataset Ownership Verification (DOV) methods are evaluated against oversimplistic evasion attacks (basic regularization, fine-tuning), creating a false sense of security. The paper asks whether DOV is truly robust against strong, universal evasion and reveals that it is not, by demonstrating that all 11 tested DOV methods can be simultaneously evaded with minimal generalization loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251648/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a copyright dataset D (the protected/stolen data), a large-scale OOD gallery set G (e.g., LSVRC-2012) with no class overlap to D, a VLM (MobileCLIP) for zero-shot image classification, and an LLM (GPT-4o mini) for generating category descriptions. It produces a surrogate student model (ResNet-18) trained exclusively on a curated OOD transfer set via distillation, never directly on D, along with the curated transfer set itself. The student must simultaneously maintain task accuracy and fall below all DOV verification thresholds (VSR < 30% for backdoor watermarks, p-value > 0.01 for non-poisoning methods).

## Method

The core insight is that DOV verification behaviors are both exclusive (unique to the protected dataset) and subtle (orthogonal to the task distribution), making them unlikely to be triggered by OOD samples. A teacher model is first trained on D to absorb domain knowledge, then knowledge is transferred to a student via an OOD transfer set T, so verification identifiers are not activated or transmitted. Transfer Set Curation uses an LLM-enhanced VLM to assign gallery samples to D's classes, ranks them by proximity to per-class distribution centroids in CLIP feature space, and applies a consensus filter requiring teacher predictions to match VLM assignments. Selective Knowledge Transfer (SKT) augments standard distillation with teacher-generated adversarial perturbations (using adaptive L0/L2/L∞ norm projection) and a genetic-algorithm-selected corruption chain from ImageNet-C, promoting student invariance to the teacher's worst-case perturbations while encouraging predictive divergence to suppress spurious verification behaviors.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, Escaping DOV evades all 11 tested DOV methods (4 backdoor watermarks, 5 non-poisoning watermarks, 2 fingerprints) while maintaining test accuracy within 1% of the vanilla teacher (e.g., 93.46% vs. 94.44% for BadNets). On the harder Tiny ImageNet (with no overlapping gallery classes), accuracy drops by only 2–3% while all VSR values fall below 13% and all p-values exceed 0.1, well past the 30% VSR / 0.01 p-value detection thresholds. In the head-to-head comparison against 9 SOTA evasion attacks (Table 2), Escaping DOV is the only method that simultaneously evades all four representative DOV methods (BadNets, Narcissus, Isotope, Dataset Inference) while achieving the highest accuracy across every setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Escaping DOV outperforms all 9 baselines on every DOV method tested. Against the strongest backdoor-defense baselines, BCU achieves the closest VSR on BadNets (1.56% vs. 1.36%) but lags by 1.1% in accuracy (92.75% vs. 93.46%); on the harder clean-label Narcissus watermark, Escaping DOV reduces VSR to 4.59% while BCU still sits at 61.72% and NAD at 75.41%, a gap of over 57 percentage points. For non-poisoning DOV, Escaping DOV achieves the highest p-values on both Isotope (0.2845 vs. IPRemoval's 0.1916) and Dataset Inference (0.4759 vs. ABD's 0.3696), while also maintaining the best accuracy (93.99% and 93.97% respectively). Distillation-based baselines (NAD, BCU, ABD) partially suppress backdoor VSR but fail entirely on non-poisoning watermarks and fingerprints (p-values below 0.1), whereas Escaping DOV is the only method that simultaneously evades all four DOV categories. Data sanitization (Meta-Sift) and input purification (ZIP) baselines are ineffective, with VSR values above 50% on Narcissus and p-values below 0.1 on Isotope, confirming that pre-training or test-time defenses cannot handle the diversity of modern DOV mechanisms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on standard knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015) as the transfer mechanism and on universal adversarial training (Shafahi et al., 2020) as the basis for its Selective Knowledge Transfer module, but fundamentally repositions both: distillation is applied through an OOD intermediary rather than in-distribution data, and UAT is decoupled from the student by pre-generating perturbations with the teacher. It departs sharply from prior DOV evasion work, which relied on simple regularization, fine-tuning, and generic backdoor defenses (data sanitization, robust learning, unlearning, input purification) that the authors show are largely ineffective against advanced DOV. The key conceptual departure is the observation that DOV identifiers are 'exclusive and subtle'—orthogonal to task semantics—making them naturally absent from OOD samples, which prior evasion work never exploited.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that all 11 tested DOV methods (spanning backdoor, non-poisoning, and fingerprint categories) are simultaneously evadable with minimal accuracy loss, revealing that prior robustness evaluations gave a false sense of security. The authors explicitly position Escaping DOV as a benchmark for future DOV assessments, arguing that any new DOV method must survive this attack to be considered practical. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-vision modalities (the authors mention planned cross-modal experiments), how defenders can design verification behaviors that survive OOD-mediated transfer, and whether the VLM/LLM curation step introduces its own vulnerabilities. The practical impact is significant for anyone deploying dataset copyright protection, as it suggests current DOV is not yet deployment-ready.
