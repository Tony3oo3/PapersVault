## Problem

Creating high-quality animated 3D models from text remains challenging: existing 4D generation methods either rely on slow per-scene optimization (SDS-based) or multi-view video generation followed by per-scene 4D reconstruction, both of which are computationally expensive and produce view inconsistencies. No feed-forward, universal solution exists for animating arbitrary 3D meshes of any topology in a single forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252606/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a static 3D mesh of arbitrary topology (vertex counts ranging from 4K to 20K+) and a free-form text prompt as input, and produces a temporally coherent animated mesh sequence of 16 or 32 frames as output. The mesh is consumed as vertex positions and face connectivity; the model adapts to varying mesh complexity by dynamically adjusting Farthest Point Sampling density at inference. Inference completes in approximately 6 seconds on a single A800 GPU for a 20K-vertex mesh.

## Method

The core architecture, DyMeshVAE, decomposes each dynamic mesh into initial-frame vertex positions (shape) and relative trajectories (motion), then compresses them into a fixed 512-token latent space using topology-aware self-attention (mesh adjacency matrix as attention mask) and Farthest Point Sampling on neighborhood-enhanced features. A cross-attention decoder reconstructs trajectories by using initial-mesh vertex features as queries to retrieve motion information from the compressed latents. For text-conditional generation, a Shape-Guided Text-to-Trajectory Model built on the MMDiT architecture learns the posterior distribution of trajectory latents conditioned on CLIP text embeddings and initial-mesh features, trained with a Rectified Flow objective (velocity-field MSE) and sampled via ODE integration with classifier-free guidance (γ=3.0). The key design insight is that decomposing shape from motion and encoding topology into vertex features enables a single model to handle meshes of arbitrary vertex count and topology while preventing trajectory entanglement between semantically distinct but spatially proximate regions.

## Results

On the DyMesh test set (10 objects across multiple categories), AnimateAnyMesh achieves the best scores across all VBench metrics: I2V Subject Similarity 0.954, Motion Smoothness 0.995, and Aesthetic Quality 0.539. In the user study (5-point Likert scale), it scores 4.505 on text-to-motion alignment, 4.700 on motion naturalness, and 4.790 on shape preservation, substantially outperforming all baselines. Inference takes approximately 6 seconds on a single A800 GPU, compared to ~14 minutes for Animate3D and ~10 minutes for DG4D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnimateAnyMesh outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest competitor Animate3D [25], it improves I2V Subject Similarity from 0.936 to 0.954, Aesthetic Quality from 0.526 to 0.539, and user-study scores by roughly 1.3–1.6 points on a 5-point scale (e.g., User.Ta 4.505 vs. 2.850), while reducing inference time from ~14 min to ~6 s. L4GM [57] is the fastest baseline (~30 s) but lags significantly on I2V (0.844 vs. 0.954) and all user-study dimensions. DG4D [56] performs worst overall, with the lowest I2V (0.811), lowest user-study scores (e.g., User.Ta 2.130), and a ~10 min runtime, attributed to its SDS-based optimization causing object drifting and poor shape preservation. The qualitative analysis further notes that Animate3D's multi-stage pipeline (mesh→Gaussian→video→ARAP) accumulates errors, while AnimateAnyMesh's direct vertex-trajectory prediction avoids this and preserves local geometric detail.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in 4D generation—per-scene optimization via SDS (DG4D) or multi-view video diffusion followed by 4D reconstruction (Animate3D, L4GM)—by reframing the problem as animating existing 3D meshes rather than generating 4D content from scratch. Architecturally, it builds on MMDiT (Esser et al., 2024) as the backbone for its Shape-Guided Text-to-Trajectory Model and adopts Rectified Flow (Liu et al., 2022) for training in the compressed latent space. The key conceptual departure is the DyMeshVAE, which introduces topology-aware attention and trajectory decomposition specifically for dynamic mesh sequences, a representation-level innovation not present in prior 4D generation work that relies on 3DGS or NeRF.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating per-scene optimization, AnimateAnyMesh reduces mesh animation from ~10 minutes to ~6 seconds, making 4D content creation practical for interactive applications like gaming and VR. The DyMesh Dataset (4M+ annotated sequences) addresses a critical data bottleneck that has hindered general-purpose 4D generation. However, the current formulation is limited to short sequences (16–32 frames) and moderate mesh complexity, and the evaluation relies on a small 10-object test set with single-view rendering metrics, leaving open questions about long-horizon temporal coherence, physics plausibility, and scalability to highly detailed meshes.
