## Problem

Existing training-free open-vocabulary semantic segmentation methods focus on modifying CLIP architectures or generating prototypes but overlook two fundamental issues caused by overcomplete vocabularies: class redundancy (predicting classes absent from the image) and visual-language ambiguity (semantically similar classes competing for the same spatial region). These issues produce spurious activations and misclassifications that degrade segmentation accuracy, particularly as vocabulary size grows.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 448px shorter side, 560px for Cityscapes) and an overcomplete set of K semantic class names as input, processed via a 384px sliding window with 112px stride. It outputs a pixel-level segmentation mask obtained by argmax over the purified class activation maps. No pixel-level annotations, image-text pairs, or additional training data are required; fine-grained LLM-generated text descriptions for each class are precomputed offline and retrieved at inference time.

## Method

FreeCP is a training-free, plug-and-play purification module built on CLIP's ViT encoder. It first generates class-specific activation maps via CAM-style softmax over image-text cosine similarity, then refines them by multiplying with an image self-affinity matrix (averaged self-attention across CLIP layers). Redundancy Purification removes classes whose intra-class spatial consistency (IoU between original and refined maps) falls below a threshold T_rp. Ambiguity Purification then identifies groups of mutually overlapping classes via inter-class IoU thresholding and DFS; for each group, the ambiguous region is cropped, encoded by CLIP, and disambiguated against LLM-generated fine-grained text descriptions (Vicuna-13b) via cosine similarity, zeroing out losing classes locally. The final mask is produced by argmax over the surviving refined activations.

## Results

FreeCP is a plug-and-play module that, when combined with existing training-free OVSS methods, yields substantial mIoU gains across eight benchmarks. Specifically, MaskCLIP+FreeCP achieves 41.2% average mIoU (vs. 30.3% for MaskCLIP alone, +10.9%), while GEM+FreeCP, ClearCLIP+FreeCP, and SCLIP+FreeCP reach 42.0%, 42.2%, and 42.1% respectively. The best per-dataset results include 65.8% on VOC21 (SCLIP+FreeCP), 84.3% on VOC20 (SCLIP+FreeCP), and 26.1% on COCO Stuff (ClearCLIP+FreeCP). These results are competitive with methods that incorporate additional vision foundation models such as FreeDA (43.3% avg) and ProxyCLIP (42.3% avg), despite FreeCP requiring no extra models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeCP consistently outperforms its host baselines across all eight benchmarks: MaskCLIP improves by +10.9% avg mIoU (30.3→41.2), GEM by +3.7% (38.3→42.0), ClearCLIP by +4.1% (38.1→42.2), and SCLIP by +3.9% (38.2→42.1). Among the four FreeCP variants, ClearCLIP+FreeCP achieves the highest average (42.2%) and leads on PC60 (35.7), PC59 (39.3), ADE (18.9), and Stuff (26.1), while SCLIP+FreeCP dominates on VOC21 (65.8) and VOC20 (84.3). Compared to methods that incorporate additional vision foundation models, FreeCP's best average (42.2%) is within 1.1% of FreeDA (43.3%) and slightly above ProxyCLIP (42.3%), despite not requiring DINOv2, SAM, or Stable Diffusion. On Cityscapes, ProxyCLIP (38.1) and FreeDA (36.7) still outperform all FreeCP variants (best: GEM+FreeCP at 35.7), indicating a remaining gap on structured urban scenes. The paper also shows that FreeCP is robust to the quality of the base method: MaskCLIP, which starts from the weakest baseline (30.3%), benefits the most from FreeCP, suggesting the purification strategy is effective regardless of initial segmentation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreeCP builds on the CLIP dual-encoder architecture and the class activation mapping (CAM) paradigm for dense localization, extending the affinity-based refinement technique from weakly-supervised segmentation (Lin et al., 2023) into the open-vocabulary setting. It departs from prior training-free OVSS methods (MaskCLIP, SCLIP, GEM, ClearCLIP) that focus on modifying attention mechanisms or generating prototypes to improve activation quality; instead, FreeCP operates as a post-hoc purification stage that diagnoses and removes redundant or ambiguous classes using spatial consistency between original and refined CAMs. The method also leverages LLM-generated fine-grained descriptions (Vicuna) for disambiguation, borrowing the bounding-box localization strategy from Lin et al. (2023) to crop ambiguous regions for re-scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and previously underappreciated failure mode in OVSS: the overcomplete vocabulary necessary for open-vocabulary operation introduces class redundancy and visual-language ambiguity that systematically degrade segmentation, and the standard affinity-refinement technique actually amplifies this problem (Table 1 shows mIoU dropping from 59.4 to 24.0 on VOC without GT filtering). The plug-and-play design means FreeCP can be stacked onto any existing training-free OVSS method, making it broadly applicable. Limitations include dataset-specific threshold tuning (T_rp, T_ap), dependence on LLM-generated descriptions for the ambiguity-resolution step, and the fact that the method is inherently a post-hoc filter rather than a learned representation improvement. Open questions include whether the spatial-consistency diagnostic generalizes to video or 3D segmentation, and whether the LLM description step could be replaced by a learned fine-grained embedding.
