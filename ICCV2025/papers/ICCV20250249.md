## Problem

Existing relightable 3D Gaussian Splatting methods for inverse rendering require both densely sampled input views and static illumination conditions, which are prohibitively expensive and often impractical in real-world capture. Under sparse views with unconstrained (unknown and varying) illuminations, standard 3DGS suffers from overfitting and contradictory color gradients that mislead geometry optimization, while inter-view photometric pseudo-labels become unreliable due to illumination discrepancies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250249/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a set of sparse RGB images (as few as 6, 12, or 24 views) with known camera extrinsics and intrinsics, captured under unknown and varying illumination conditions. It produces a full relightable 3DGS representation from which novel views can be rendered with RGB color, albedo, surface normals, material parameters (metallic, roughness), and environment illumination under arbitrary new lighting. The 2D foundation model used for semantic feature extraction is frozen during training, and the per-image appearance is modeled via a GLO latent vector rather than explicit lighting parameters.

## Method

SU-RGS introduces three coupled modules. First, a Varying Appearance Rendering MLP takes the observed ray direction, 0th-order SH base color, Gaussian position (both positionally encoded), and a per-image GLO appearance embedding to produce view- and illumination-dependent colors, enabling each Gaussian to exhibit different appearances under different lighting while still converging to a consistent geometry via standard volume rendering. Second, Sparse Inverse Rendering constructs inter-view hierarchical semantic pseudo-labels by re-projecting pixels between view pairs and computing squared-error losses on multi-level features from a frozen 2D foundation model, providing illumination-robust extra supervision that compensates for the scarcity of photometric ground truth. Third, a Transient Object Perception module computes an adaptive transience coefficient from the normalized depth discrepancy between re-projected and rendered depth, modulated by hierarchical semantic cosine similarity, to attenuate or reject gradients from transient objects. Training proceeds in two end-to-end stages: 30K iterations of volume rendering with varying appearance, followed by 5K iterations of PBR-based rendering to refine geometry and learn BRDF and illumination, with a combined loss of photometric, D-SSIM, and semantic feature terms all weighted by the transience coefficient.

## Results

On NeRF-OSR (real-world outdoor scenes), SU-RGS achieves PSNR 19.95, SSIM 0.876, and LPIPS 0.244 with 24 input views, outperforming the strongest baseline GSIR (18.59/0.726/0.352) by +1.36 dB PSNR. On TensoIR Synthetic, SU-RGS reaches PSNR 33.05, SSIM 0.976, LPIPS 0.033, and MAE 5.17° with 24 views, surpassing GSIR (29.29/0.896/0.125/8.15°) by +3.76 dB PSNR and −2.98° MAE. The advantage persists even at 6 views, where SU-RGS maintains PSNR 16.04 (NeRF-OSR) and 28.05 (TensoIR Synthetic) while baselines degrade substantially (e.g., GSIR drops to 14.54 and 27.18 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SU-RGS outperforms all five baselines across every metric and every view-count setting on both NeRF-OSR and TensoIR Synthetic. Against the strongest baseline GSIR (CVPR 2024), SU-RGS gains +1.36 dB PSNR and +0.150 SSIM on NeRF-OSR (24 views), and +3.76 dB PSNR, +0.080 SSIM, and −2.98° MAE on TensoIR Synthetic (24 views). R3DG (ECCV 2024) and TensoIR (CVPR 2023) trail GSIR by roughly 1–2 dB PSNR, while NeRF-OSR (ECCV 2022) is the weakest baseline, scoring below 16.2 dB PSNR on NeRF-OSR at 24 views. The gap widens as views decrease: at 6 views, SU-RGS retains 16.04 dB PSNR on NeRF-OSR versus 14.54 for GSIR and 11.22 for TensoIR, a 1.5–4.8 dB advantage. On TensoIR Synthetic at 6 views, SU-RGS achieves 28.05 dB PSNR and 12.32° MAE, while GSIR drops to 27.18 dB and 13.99° MAE. WildGaussians is compared only on NeRF On-the-go for transient object handling, where SU-RGS's geometry-semantics cooperative module provides qualitative improvements without extra rendering cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

SU-RGS builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting as the scene representation and on GS-IR's physically-based rendering formulation for decomposing BRDF and illumination. It departs from prior relightable 3DGS methods (GSIR, R3DG, LumiGauss) that assume dense views and/or static illumination by introducing a varying-appearance MLP (with GLO per-image embeddings) to handle illumination variation, and by replacing photometric inter-view pseudo-labels with hierarchical semantic features from a frozen 2D foundation model to compensate for sparse supervision. The transient object perception module extends the geometry-semantic consistency idea from NeRF On-the-go into a differentiable, gradient-weighting mechanism integrated into the inverse rendering pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combination of sparse views and unconstrained illumination is a practically critical but previously unaddressed setting for 3DGS inverse rendering, especially for outdoor scenes where lighting changes continuously during capture. Achieving SOTA relighting with as few as 6 views (25% of baselines) makes the approach far more deployable in real-world pipelines. Open questions remain around unposed inputs, dynamic scenes, and whether the reliance on a frozen 2D foundation model for semantic features limits generalization to domains where such models are weak. The authors note future work on unposed-view relighting, suggesting the current method still assumes known camera parameters.
