## Problem

Existing texture synthesis methods face a three-way tension: 2D diffusion-based approaches produce global inconsistencies (seams, ghosting, Janus problem) due to lack of 3D awareness, while 3D-native methods discard 2D diffusion priors and are impractical for real-world objects. Multi-view diffusion models partially address consistency but rely on soft cross-view attention constraints that cannot guarantee pixel-level overlap, and the dual-modality conditioning of both reference images and geometric features introduces conflicts that make texture synthesis ill-posed when geometry and image are misaligned.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a reference RGB image, a 3D mesh (from which a Canonical Coordinate Map and Normal Map are rendered per view), and optionally a text prompt. Geometry is injected by concatenating CCM and normal-map VAE latents with the denoised noise latents, while the reference image enters through a frozen-weight reference attention branch. The model generates 6 views during training and 6–12 views at inference, which are then baked into a UV texture map for the input mesh.

## Method

RomanTex extends Stable Diffusion 2 with a parallel multi-attention architecture comprising three blocks: a frozen self-attention (SA) for 2D generative fidelity, a multi-view attention (MVA) with a novel 3D-aware Rotary Positional Embedding that maps downsampled CCM voxel indices into rotational position encodings (bypassing VAE compression to preserve geometric precision), and a reference attention (RefA) for image conditioning. A key training insight is the decoupled dropout strategy: during training, the MVA branch (λ_mv) and the reference image are independently dropped, allowing each attention component to specialize without entangling consistency and image-following objectives. At inference, a geometry-related Classifier-Free Guidance scheme applies orthogonal CFG over both the geometry condition and the reference image, with a projection variant that dynamically weights geometry adherence by the sharpness of geometric activations, gracefully handling misalignment between generated meshes and reference images.

## Results

On the image-to-texture task, RomanTex achieves the best CLIP-FiD (24.78), CMMD (2.191), and LPIPS (0.121) among all compared methods, outperforming the strongest baseline HY3D-2.0 (26.44, 2.318, 0.126) by 6.3%, 5.5%, and 4.0% respectively. On the text-to-texture task, RomanTex similarly leads with CLIP-FiD 24.78 and LPIPS 0.121, while matching HY3D-2.0 on CLIP-T (0.308 vs 0.307). In the user study, RomanTex is preferred by 63.9% of participants for overall quality, 62.1% for image following, and 64.4% for cross-view consistency, compared to 20.7%, 27.6%, and 21.5% for the next-best method (HY3D-2.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RomanTex outperforms all four image-to-texture baselines on CLIP-FiD, CMMD, and LPIPS. Against HY3D-2.0 (the closest competitor), RomanTex reduces CLIP-FiD from 26.44 to 24.78 and LPIPS from 0.126 to 0.121, while nearly matching its CLIP-I score (0.891 vs 0.889). Paint3D-IPA achieves the highest CLIP-I (0.998) due to its IP-Adapter-based image injection, but RomanTex surpasses it on all other metrics, indicating better overall texture quality and geometric alignment. SyncMVD-IPA and TexGen are clearly outperformed across all four metrics, with TexGen additionally suffering from seam artifacts at UV boundaries. In the user study, RomanTex dominates with 63.9% overall quality preference versus 20.7% for HY3D-2.0, confirming that the quantitative gains translate to perceptual improvements in texture coherence and image fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

RomanTex extends the multi-view diffusion framework for texture synthesis established by Hunyuan3D 2.0, which itself builds on Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion architecture. The key conceptual departure is the adaptation of 2D Rotary Positional Embedding (RoPE) to 3D voxel indices, injecting geometry-aware spatial priors directly into the multi-view attention without VAE encoding. Additionally, the paper introduces a decoupled parallel attention structure (self-attention, multi-view attention, reference attention) with a dropout training strategy that separates generative fidelity from cross-view consistency, and a geometry-conditioned CFG scheme that balances image and geometric guidance during inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses three persistent failure modes in 3D texture synthesis—global inconsistency (Janus problem), multi-view ghosting, and image-geometry misalignment—that have limited the practical adoption of diffusion-based texturing in production pipelines. The 3D-aware RoPE and decoupled attention are lightweight architectural modifications that could be integrated into existing multi-view diffusion systems, and the geometry-related CFG provides a practical inference-time control for handling imperfect geometry-image alignment. A notable limitation is the baked-in illumination in generated textures, which the authors acknowledge requires extension to physically-based rendering for full production readiness. The large user-study preference gap (62–64% vs. 20–27% for the next-best method) suggests the improvements are perceptually significant even where quantitative metrics show only moderate gains.
