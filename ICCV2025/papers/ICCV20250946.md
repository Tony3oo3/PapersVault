## Problem

Aerial vision-and-language navigation over real-world cities remains underexplored because existing aerial VLN datasets rely on synthetic 3D scenes (AerialVLN) or 2D satellite imagery (AVDN), and integrating real-world geographic information with visual observations for UAV navigation is difficult. No large-scale dataset provides human demonstration trajectories over actual cityscapes where agents must interpret spatial relationships between real landmarks and a textually specified goal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250946/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent operates in a 5D pose space (x, y, z, pitch, yaw) within a 3D point-cloud environment rendered at up to 200 m altitude, receiving at each step an RGB image, a depth image, a natural-language goal description, and a geographic semantic map (GSM) — a binary mask with five categories (current field of view, explored area, landmarks, potential goals, surrounding objects) aligned to a 2D OpenStreetMap view. The output is a discrete action from {move-forward (5 m), turn-left/right (30°), ascend/descend (2 m), stop}, and an episode succeeds if the agent stops within a 20 m radius of the goal.

## Method

The paper introduces CityNav, a dataset of 32,637 human demonstration trajectories collected via Amazon MTurk over 4.65 km² of Cambridge and Birmingham using SensatUrban 3D point clouds rendered in a Potree-based web simulator (CityFlight). The key technical contribution is the geographic semantic map (GSM) representation: landmark polygons from OpenStreetMap, GNSS-derived explored-area masks, and Grounding DINO detections of potential goals and surrounding objects are composited into a five-channel binary mask, encoded by a small five-layer CNN with max-pooling and ReLU, and appended to the [z_RGB, z_depth] input sequence of the GRU module in three baseline models (Seq2Seq, CMA, AerialVLN). Models are trained via imitation learning on human trajectories using Adam (lr 1.5×10⁻³, batch 12, 5 epochs) with ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50 for RGB and PointGoalNav-pretrained ResNet-50 for depth. Experiments show GSM improves SR from ~1–2% to 6–10% on test-unseen, and that training on human demonstrations outperforms shortest-path trajectories, especially under positional noise.

## Results

On the test-unseen split, AerialVLN+GSM achieves the best performance among all models with NE=85.1 m, SR=6.72%, OSR=18.21%, and SPL=5.16%. Incorporating the geographic semantic map (GSM) representation yields substantial gains across all three baselines: Seq2Seq improves from SR=1.50% to 3.81%, CMA from 0.82% to 4.68%, and AerialVLN from 1.79% to 6.72% on test-unseen. Human annotators achieve SR of 87.86–89.31% across all splits, revealing a large remaining gap between current models and human-level navigation in real-world aerial environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates three representative aerial VLN models—Seq2Seq, CMA, and AerialVLN—each with and without the proposed geographic semantic map (GSM) representation. On the test-unseen split, AerialVLN+GSM outperforms all other model configurations, achieving NE=85.1 m and SR=6.72%, compared to CMA+GSM (NE=94.6 m, SR=4.68%) and Seq2Seq+GSM (NE=98.1 m, SR=3.81%). Without GSM, all three models perform poorly: AerialVLN drops to NE=187.7 m and SR=1.79%, CMA to NE=252.6 m and SR=0.82%, and Seq2Seq to NE=245.3 m and SR=1.50%, demonstrating that the GSM representation is critical for leveraging real-world geographic context. The relative ranking (AerialVLN > CMA > Seq2Seq) is consistent with prior findings on synthetic aerial VLN, confirming CityNav's alignment with existing benchmarks. Despite these gains, all models remain far below human performance (SR≈88%), highlighting the substantial challenge of real-world aerial navigation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the aerial VLN line initiated by LANI, AVDN, and AerialVLN by moving from synthetic or small-scale environments to large-scale real-world 3D cityscapes built on SensatUrban point clouds. Methodologically, it builds directly on the Seq2Seq and CMA architectures from Anderson et al. (CVPR 2018) and the look-ahead guidance mechanism from AerialVLN (ICCV 2023), integrating a novel geographic semantic map (GSM) representation as an auxiliary modality into their GRU modules. The key conceptual departure is the requirement that agents interpret spatial relationships between real-world landmarks (retrieved from OpenStreetMap) and the navigation destination, a challenge absent in prior synthetic-scene benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CityNav establishes the first large-scale real-world benchmark for aerial VLN (32,637 trajectories over 4.65 km²), filling a critical gap that has limited progress in this subfield. The GSM representation is a simple, broadly applicable technique that yields large performance gains across three architectures, suggesting that integrating 2D map context is an underexploited direction for embodied navigation more generally. However, the enormous performance gap between the best model (~10% SR) and humans (~88% SR) underscores that real-world aerial navigation remains far from solved, and the dataset's geographic coverage is limited to two UK cities. Open questions include whether the GSM approach generalizes to other cities, whether multi-agent collaboration can close the human-model gap, and how to unify aerial, ground-level, and indoor navigation in a single framework.
