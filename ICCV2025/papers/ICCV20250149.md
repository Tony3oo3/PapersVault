## Problem

Combining structured pruning with Winograd convolution on GPUs is inefficient: filter pruning preserves GPU-friendly regularity but its pruning unit (p²×R^{1×c}) is too large, causing severe accuracy drops at high pruning ratios, while Winograd weight pruning achieves fine-grained sparsity but produces irregular data patterns that require index computation and defeat GPU throughput. No prior method simultaneously achieves small pruning unit size, regular data layout, and balanced workload across GPU streaming multiprocessors.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained CNN (e.g., ResNet-18, ResNet-50, VGG-16) and produces a pruned model whose 3×3 convolution layers operate in the Winograd BGEMM form with vector-wise removed rows from sub-GEMM weight matrices. At inference, the pruned model accepts standard RGB images and outputs class labels, with the pruned weights processed by standard dense GEMM kernels (cuBLAS/CUTLASS) rather than sparse libraries, since the structured form eliminates the need for index computation. For 1×1 convolutions, WINS-AB optionally pairs with BCBP or FPGM as a complementary pruning strategy.

## Method

WINS applies vector-wise structured pruning directly in the Winograd-transformed domain after EC2B conversion, removing entire row vectors (R^{1×c}) from each of the 16 sub-GEMM weight matrices using an ℓ₂-norm threshold, yielding a pruning unit p² times smaller than filter pruning while preserving the regular layout that standard GPU GEMM kernels exploit. WINS-B enforces equal pruning ratios across all 16 sub-GEMMs by computing thresholds independently per sub-GEMM, eliminating load imbalance that causes idle SMs and delays subsequent layers. WINS-AB introduces an adaptive auto-algorithm that, per layer, selects between WINS and WINS-B by measuring balanced speedup and outlook accuracy (via 100-iteration Adaptive Batch Normalization evaluation), then iteratively adjusts a selection threshold α over β iterations to find the best speedup-accuracy trade-off before fine-tuning the winner.

## Results

On ImageNet, WINS-AB achieves up to 2.82× speedup on ResNet-18 (84% pruning ratio, 69.00% top-1, −0.70% GAP) and 3.47× speedup on ResNet-50 (80% PR, 76.43% top-1, +0.33% GAP) while preserving or even improving accuracy over the unpruned baseline. On DenseNet-121, WINS-AB reaches 85% PR with only −0.40% top-1 drop and 1.66× speedup. On SqueezeNet-1.0, WINS-AB achieves 28.1% PR with −1.20% GAP and 1.23× speedup, outperforming the l2-norm filter pruning baseline at a much lower PR (9.7%, −2.90% GAP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WINS-AB consistently outperforms filter pruning baselines at substantially higher pruning ratios: on ResNet-18, FPGM [20] and PFP [35] suffer −1.80% and −2.40% top-1 drops at ~42–44% PR, while WINS-AB at 84% PR incurs only −0.70% and achieves 2.82× speedup. On ResNet-50, WINS-AB (80% PR, +0.33% GAP, 3.47×) dominates Torque [14] (64.5% PR, −1.49% GAP, 2.35×) and APIB [13] (58% PR, −0.78% GAP, 2.07×) on both accuracy and speed. Against SOSP [46] on ResNet-18 at comparable ~40% PR, WINS-AB improves accuracy by 0.40% (69.90% vs 69.60%) with equivalent speedup. On DenseNet-121, WINS-AB at 85% PR (−0.40% GAP, 1.66×) far exceeds C-SGD [16] (29.9% PR, −0.80%, 1.14×) and LWP [67] (35.2% PR, −1.80%, 1.33×). Against Winograd Weight Pruning (WWP) [39] on VGG-16, WWP retains 2.0% higher accuracy at 70% PR but is slower than the unpruned model due to irregular sparse memory access, whereas WINS-AB achieves 2.01× speedup at 84% PR. UDSP [9] and ATO [59] show small accuracy gains at low PR but offer far less speedup (1.26–1.29×) compared to WINS-AB's 2.82–3.47×.

## Relation to Prior Work

WINS sits at the intersection of Winograd convolution (Lavin & Gray, 2016) and structured pruning, but departs from both prior Winograd-compatible pruning lines: filter pruning (FP) uses a pruning unit p² times too large, causing severe accuracy drops, while Winograd Weight Pruning (WWP) operates at element granularity, producing irregular sparse matrices that are actually slower than dense execution on GPUs. The paper's key conceptual move is to identify the BGEMM sub-GEMM vector (R^{1×c}) as the natural pruning unit after EC2B conversion, yielding a structured sparsity pattern that is both GPU-friendly and fine-grained enough to preserve accuracy. The WINS-AB variant further builds on EagleEye's adaptive batch normalization evaluation (Li et al., 2020) to automatically select per-layer pruning strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical deployment gap: industrial GPUs already use Winograd convolution, and structured pruning is the standard compression tool, yet combining them efficiently had been an open engineering problem. The 2.8× practical speedup on ResNet-18 with <1% accuracy loss demonstrates that GPU-aware pruning granularity can unlock the full theoretical speedup of Winograd without the accuracy penalty of coarse filter pruning. Limitations include the method's dependence on specific GPU execution models (SM-level BGEMM assignment), its focus on 3×3 CNN convolutions, and the heuristic nature of the WINS-AB search (α=30, β=5). Open questions include whether the vector-wise BGEMM pruning unit generalizes to larger Winograd tiles (e.g., 4×4 or 6×6), to non-CNN architectures, or to emerging hardware with different parallelism models.
