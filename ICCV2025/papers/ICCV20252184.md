## Problem

Existing efficient Vision Transformers overlook two critical issues: at the micro level, attention variants that reduce theoretical FLOPs inadvertently introduce substantial runtime overhead from memory-bound operations (reshape, copy, LayerNorm) that dominate actual inference latency; at the macro level, placing spatial attention blocks in the final network stage is suboptimal because features there have shifted from spatial-dominant to semantic-dominant, making channel-wise recalibration more effective than global spatial attention.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252184/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard RGB images at 224×224 for classification, 320×320 for object detection (SSDLite), and 512×512 for semantic segmentation (DeepLabv3). Outputs are class labels (1000-way ImageNet), bounding boxes with class labels (COCO), or pixel-wise segmentation masks (PASCAL VOC). All variants are designed for TinyML deployment, with parameter counts as low as 1.0M, and are benchmarked on edge hardware including NVIDIA Jetson Nano and ARM Cortex-A57.

## Method

TinyNeXt introduces Lean Single-Head Self-Attention (LeanSHSA), which reduces the standard four projection matrices (W_q, W_k, W_v, W_o) to two merged matrices (W_1 = W_q·W_k^T and W_2 = W_v·W_o) while maintaining full head dimension d_h = d to avoid the rank deficiency that bottlenecked single-head variants suffer from. This eliminates reshape operations and halves softmax memory, yielding ~2× CPU throughput over MHSA. At the macro level, the pyramid architecture assigns stage-specific modules matching feature characteristics: inverted-residual ConvBlocks in early stages for local texture, SpatialBlocks (LeanSHSA + 3×3 DWConv + MLP) in the middle stage for long-range spatial dependencies, and ChannelBlocks (SE module replacing attention) in the final stage where semantic rather than spatial information dominates. Three variants (T/S/M) scale depth, width, and expansion ratio, achieving 71.5%–75.3% top-1 on ImageNet-1K with 1.0M–2.3M parameters.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, TinyNeXt-T achieves 71.5% top-1 accuracy with only 1.0M parameters and 259M FLOPs, while TinyNeXt-S and TinyNeXt-M reach 72.7% and 75.3% top-1 with 1.3M and 2.3M parameters respectively. On edge hardware (NVIDIA Jetson Nano), TinyNeXt-S and TinyNeXt-M achieve 14.3 ms and 19.4 ms latency, running 2.1× and 2.6× faster than MobileViT-XXS and MobileViT-XS at comparable accuracy. For object detection (MS COCO with SSDLite), TinyNeXt-S/M achieve 22.4/25.0 mAP, and for semantic segmentation (PASCAL VOC with DeepLabv3), TinyNeXt-M reaches 76.9 mIOU with 4.9M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At the 1.0M-parameter scale, TinyNeXt-T outperforms MobileNetv1 (70.6%) and PVTv2-b0 (70.5%) by +0.9% and +1.0% top-1 accuracy while using 3.2M and 2.7M fewer parameters, respectively, and delivering 1.4× and 2.8× higher GPU throughput. At 1.3M parameters, TinyNeXt-S (72.7%) surpasses MobileViT-XXS (69.0%) by +3.7%, EdgeNeXt-XXS (71.2%) by +1.5%, and EMO-1M (71.5%) by +1.2%, with 1.9× and 1.83× GPU throughput gains over the latter two; it also beats MobileNetv2-1.0 (72.0%) by +0.7% with 37% fewer parameters. At 2.3M parameters, TinyNeXt-M (75.3%) edges out MobileViT-XS (74.8%) by +0.5%, EdgeNeXt-XS (75.0%) by +0.3%, and EMO-2M (75.1%) by +0.2%, while achieving 6.4× higher CPU throughput than MobileViT-XS and 1.84× higher GPU throughput than EMO-2M. On edge devices (Jetson Nano), TinyNeXt-S runs in 14.3 ms versus 30.1 ms for MobileViT-XXS and 26.2 ms for EMO-1M; TinyNeXt-M runs in 19.4 ms versus 50.8 ms for MobileViT-XS and 35.7 ms for EMO-2M. For object detection, TinyNeXt-S (22.4 mAP) beats MobileViTv2-0.5 (21.2) by +1.2 mAP with 1.2× throughput, and TinyNeXt-M (25.0 mAP) matches EMO-2M (25.2) within 0.2 mAP while running 1.6× faster. For segmentation, TinyNeXt-M (76.9 mIOU) exceeds MobileNetV2 (75.7) by +1.2 mIOU with 0.7G fewer FLOPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

TinyNeXt positions itself as a corrective to two blind spots in the efficient-ViT literature: at the micro level, prior attention variants (Swin, MobileViT, SHViT) reduced theoretical FLOPs but introduced costly memory-bound operations (reshape, copy, LayerNorm) that dominate actual runtime; at the macro level, existing hybrid CNN-Transformer designs (LeViT, EfficientFormer, EMO) uniformly place spatial attention in the deepest stage despite evidence that features there are semantically rather than spatially dominant. The architecture repurposes well-known components—MobileNetV2-style inverted residual blocks for early stages, a simplified single-head attention (LeanSHSA) for the middle stage, and a Squeeze-and-Excitation module for the final stage—rather than introducing a fundamentally new primitive, but the stage-specific module assignment and the rank-based justification for merging projection matrices (W_q W_k^T → W_1) are the paper's conceptual contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For TinyML practitioners, TinyNeXt offers a clean, hardware-friendly backbone with consistent 1.8–2.8× speedups on Jetson Nano and ARM Cortex-A57 over comparable models, making it a practical drop-in replacement for edge deployment. The macro-level finding that channel-wise attention outperforms spatial attention in the deepest stage (where spatial resolution is 7×7) is a useful architectural principle that could inform future lightweight ViT designs beyond this specific family. The main open limitation is that LeanSHSA retains quadratic token complexity, restricting applicability to high-resolution dense-prediction tasks; the authors note that dynamic attention mechanisms with minimal memory-bound overhead remain an open direction.
