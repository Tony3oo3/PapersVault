## Problem

IP protection for already-released diffusion models remains under-explored because external watermarking methods require retraining and are inapplicable to open-source models, while existing intrinsic-fingerprint methods are designed for deterministic networks. Diffusion models are inherently stochastic and temporal (each denoising step samples from a conditional distribution), and fine-tuning introduces non-negligible shifts in feature representations, making direct representation comparison unreliable for determining whether a suspect model was derived from a victim model.

## Input / Output

Given a suspect and a victim diffusion model, the method consumes 200 text prompts (from PartiPrompts) and N=20 random seeds per prompt, collecting intermediate latent representations at every denoising step to form a fingerprint sequence per model. The output is a normalized similarity score in [0,1] (via an exponential of the dynamic-programming total distance divided by √(T_s·T_v)), where high values indicate the suspect model is likely derived from the victim model. No retraining or modification of either model is required.

## Method

DiffIP operates in two sub-phases. In sub-phase 1, for each pair of denoising steps, it estimates the stochastic feature distributions via multi-seed sampling and then solves for an optimal linear transformation M(X)=QSX−h (orthogonal, scaling, translation) that reverts the suspect model's representation back to the victim model's state, formulated as a Procrustes-like problem solved by alternating closed-form updates over Q and S (ALS-style). In sub-phase 2, it constructs a cost matrix of step-wise distances and applies a dynamic-programming path-finding algorithm (allowing right, up, and diagonal moves) to find the order-preserving alignment between the two fingerprint sequences that minimizes total cumulative distance, yielding the final similarity score. The framework is inherently robust to dimension permutation and column-wise scaling camouflage because permutation matrices are isometric and scalar scaling absorbs into the S operator.

## Results

With SD15 as the victim model, DiffIP achieves an average similarity score of 0.9464 across all derived models (ranging from 0.8065 to 0.9953) while keeping unrelated model scores low (average 0.0807, max 0.1001). Against external-watermark baselines, DiffIP maintains a true positive rate (TPR) of 0.9186 even after 10,000 LoRA fine-tuning steps, whereas Stable Signature drops to 0.0094 and AquaLoRA to 0.0000. DiffIP also achieves the best model fidelity (FID 24.26) since it requires no retraining, compared to 24.77 (Stable Signature) and 24.88 (AquaLoRA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against REEF [71], DiffIP substantially outperforms on both detection and rejection: DiffIP achieves 0.9464 average similarity on derived models versus REEF's inconsistent ~0.59, while keeping unrelated model scores at 0.0807 versus REEF's dangerously high ~0.47 (indicating severe false positives). Against PCS [68], DiffIP wins on consistency—PCS scores as low as 0.0682 on some derived models (Earth) while DiffIP maintains ≥0.8065 across all derived models; PCS does achieve near-zero scores on unrelated models but at the cost of missing many true derivatives. Against external-watermark methods, DiffIP dominates on fine-tuning robustness: at 10,000 LoRA fine-tuning steps, DiffIP retains TPR 0.9186 while Stable Signature [73] collapses to 0.0094 and AquaLoRA [24] to 0.0000. DiffIP also achieves the best FID (24.26 vs. 24.77 and 24.88) since it requires no model retraining, making it applicable to already-released models unlike both watermark baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the intrinsic-fingerprint-identification paradigm (REEF, PCS) from deterministic networks to diffusion models, which are inherently stochastic and temporal. It repurposes the Procrustes problem and dynamic programming for sequence alignment to handle the unique challenges of diffusion model fingerprints: fine-tuning-induced distribution shifts (addressed via a constrained linear reversion using orthogonal, scaling, and translation operators) and temporal misalignment across denoising steps (addressed via a DP-based alignment). Unlike external-watermark methods (Stable Signature, AquaLoRA) that require retraining and are inapplicable to already-released models, DiffIP operates purely on feature representations collected at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As diffusion models become commercially critical (FLUX, DeepFloyd, SD), the inability to verify IP ownership of already-released models is a significant gap in model security. DiffIP provides the first practical white-box fingerprinting method for this setting, with demonstrated robustness to aggressive fine-tuning (10,000+ steps) and camouflage attacks (permutation, scaling) that defeat existing watermark-based approaches. Open questions include whether the linear-approximation assumption for fine-tuning effects holds under more extreme adaptations (e.g., full retraining vs. LoRA), whether the method extends to video diffusion or other generative modalities, and how it performs when only partial model access is available.
