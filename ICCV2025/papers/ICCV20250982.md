## Problem

Existing pre-trained model-based CIL methods that rely on lightweight modules (e.g., prompts) suffer from two critical weaknesses: (1) key-query matching for module selection is fragile and prone to selecting irrelevant modules under task ambiguity or distribution shift, and (2) task-specific modules capture only narrow task-discriminative features while ignoring shared general knowledge, causing confusion between visually similar classes across different tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250982/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and outputs a class label from the cumulative set of all previously encountered classes. It operates in an exemplar-free setting—no stored samples from prior tasks are used during training or inference. The frozen ViT backbone (ViT-B/16-IN1K or IN21K) processes the image, and the final prediction is produced by summing logits from the selected task-specific adapter and the universal adapter before argmax.

## Method

TUNA trains a separate bottleneck adapter (down-projection → ReLU → up-projection) per incremental task on a frozen ViT, regularized by an orthogonal L1 loss on up-projection weights to enforce non-redundant task features. At inference, an entropy-based selection mechanism routes each input to the adapter yielding the lowest prediction entropy, replacing brittle key-query matching. A universal adapter is constructed by fusing all task-specific adapters via sign-summation (voting on parameter direction) followed by max-absolute-value selection (preserving the strongest magnitude consistent with the consensus sign), inspired by model-merging techniques. The final prediction ensembles the logits of the selected task-specific adapter and the universal adapter, combining specialized and shared knowledge to improve discrimination of similar classes across tasks.

## Results

Using ViT-B/16-IN21K as the frozen backbone, TUNA achieves the best average accuracy (A) and last-stage accuracy (A_B) across all four benchmarks: 94.44/90.74 on CIFAR-100 (B0 Inc5), 84.22/79.42 on ImageNet-R (B0 Inc20), 73.78/64.78 on ImageNet-A (B0 Inc20), and 76.46/66.32 on ObjectNet (B0 Inc20). The largest gains over the second-best method appear on ImageNet-A (+2.95 A over SSIAT) and ObjectNet (+2.70 A_B over MOS). In the exemplar-free setting, TUNA also outperforms exemplar-based methods (20/class) such as TagFex by 2.67 A on ImageNet-R and 2.88 A on CIFAR-100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TUNA outperforms all 10 PTM-based baselines on every dataset and metric. The closest competitors are SSIAT (adapter-based) and RanPAC (random-projection-based): on CIFAR-100, TUNA leads SSIAT by 0.92 A and RanPAC by 0.44 A; on ImageNet-R, it leads RanPAC by 1.24 A and SSIAT by 1.02 A; on the more challenging ImageNet-A, the margin widens to +2.95 A over SSIAT and +4.46 A over RanPAC; on ObjectNet, TUNA surpasses MOS by 1.77 A and SSIAT by 2.81 A. Prompt-based methods (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt) trail by 5–10 A on average, confirming the fragility of key-query matching. Against exemplar-based CIL methods (iCaRL, DER, FOSTER, MEMO, TagFex) in Table 2, TUNA—using zero stored exemplars—still achieves higher average accuracy than TagFex (20 exemplars/class) by 2.67 A on ImageNet-R and 2.88 A on CIFAR-100, highlighting its memory efficiency advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

TUNA builds directly on the adapter-based PTM fine-tuning paradigm introduced by APER [64], but departs from the dominant prompt-based CIL line (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt) that relies on key-query matching for task selection. The paper repurposes model-merging techniques (sign summation and max-absolute-value selection from [24, 36, 54]) to construct a universal adapter from task-specific ones, and replaces brittle key-query retrieval with an entropy-based selection criterion. The orthogonal loss on up-projection weights is a new regularization specifically designed to enforce task disentanglement in the adapter space, complementing the fusion mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By operating entirely exemplar-free while outperforming exemplar-based methods (Table 2), TUNA addresses a practical memory constraint that limits deployment of CIL systems in resource-limited settings. The dual-adapter inference strategy (task-specific + universal) offers a principled way to balance specialization and generalization, a tension that prior prompt-based methods handle only implicitly. The main open limitation is the multiple forward passes required for entropy-based adapter selection, which scales linearly with the number of tasks and could become prohibitive in long incremental sequences; the authors acknowledge this and flag speedup as future work.
