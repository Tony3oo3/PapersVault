## Problem

Existing 3D human motion generation methods produce realistic but offline, non-interactive sequences that cannot respond in real time to external forces or user commands. The paper identifies that human motion is dominated by physics over short intervals (~0.5 s) and by semantics over longer horizons, and that prior autoregressive models conditioned on multi-frame histories overfit to training semantics, limiting generalization as a foundation for interactive motor control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252448/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single-frame initial state (267-dim vector of SMPL-X root translation, 6D root/joint rotations, and 22 joint positions and velocities) plus optional control signals: induced velocity impulses on selected joints, a target mean speed vector and facing direction (via CBG), an integer action label, or a 2D pelvis target location. It outputs 15-frame (0.5 s) SMPL-X parameter segments that are chained autoregressively to produce unbounded, real-time avatar animation. Notably, the model is trained entirely on unlabeled mocap segments with no foot-contact annotations, and at test time the body shape β can be drawn from N(0,I) to generalize to unseen identities.

## Method

PRIMAL uses a two-stage paradigm. In pretraining, a transformer-based autoregressive diffusion model (DDPM with x₀-prediction) is trained fully unsupervised on 15-frame segments from AMASS, conditioning via in-context concatenation of the initial state embedding with the noisy motion tokens; the loss combines the standard DDPM term with forward-kinematics losses on joint positions and velocities, and no scheduled sampling or contact labels are used. The key architectural choice is conditioning on a single initial state (positions + velocities) rather than a multi-frame history, which decouples short-term physics from long-term semantics and makes the model responsive to velocity perturbations. For real-time speed/direction control, classifier-based guidance losses on mean joint velocity and facing direction are used with analytically derived gradients to avoid autodifferentiation overhead. In the adaptation stage, a ControlNet-like adaptor is inserted at every transformer block, adding a task-specific control embedding (action label or 2D target) to the block input while keeping the base model frozen, enabling efficient few-shot personalization and spatial target reaching.

## Results

On the motion realism benchmark (Table 1), PRIMAL-InContext achieves an averaged skating ratio (ASR) of 0.024 and an averaged neural Riemannian distance (ARD) of 0.074×10⁻³, outperforming all baselines on both metrics. In perceptual studies, participants prefer PRIMAL over CLoSD (9.17% baseline preference), DiP (20.81%), and DART (34.88%). For responsiveness to induced impulses (Table 2), PRIMAL achieves ASR 0.088 and ARD 0.511×10⁻³, beating DiP (ASR 0.126, ARD 0.645) and DART (ASR 0.159, ARD 2.259). In few-shot semantic action generation (Table 3), the ControlNet-based adaptation reaches 0.96 R-precision with ASR 0.215, surpassing both direct finetuning (0.87) and OmniControlNet (0.90).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLoSD/DiP [71], PRIMAL achieves a lower ASR (0.024 vs. 0.019 for CLoSD, 0.067 for DiP off-the-shelf) and a substantially lower ARD (0.074 vs. 1.994 and 7.373), with perceptual preference strongly favoring PRIMAL (only 9.17% and 20.81% of raters prefer CLoSD and DiP respectively). Against MotionLCM [13], PRIMAL reduces foot skating by a factor of ~6 (ASR 0.024 vs. 0.149). Against DART [89] re-implemented under identical pretraining conditions, PRIMAL wins on ASR (0.024 vs. 0.072) and BPR (34.88% prefer DART), while ARD is nearly tied (0.074 vs. 0.077). In the adaptation setting, PRIMAL's block-wise ControlNet adaptor outperforms OmniControlNet [81] on both ASR (0.215 vs. 0.299) and R-precision (0.96 vs. 0.90) for few-shot semantic action generation, while also beating direct finetuning on ASR (0.215 vs. 0.253) and R-precision (0.96 vs. 0.87). Overall, PRIMAL wins or ties on every reported metric across all four baseline comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRIMAL extends the autoregressive diffusion model line (DiP/CLoSD, DART, CAMDM, AMDM) by conditioning on a single-frame initial state (joint positions and velocities) rather than a multi-frame motion history, and by restricting the generation horizon to 0.5 seconds where the authors argue physics dominates over semantics. The two-stage pretrain-then-adapt paradigm follows the foundation-model recipe popularized by OMG [44], but departs in using a ControlNet-style block-wise adaptor for generic control signals (action labels, spatial targets) rather than a mixture-of-experts text fusion module. Unlike physics-based methods (PhysDiff, CLoSD, PACER++) that couple diffusion with simulation for physical plausibility, PRIMAL is purely data-driven and learns contact-like behavior implicitly from unlabeled AMASS segments without contact annotations or scheduled sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a short-horizon, single-state-conditioned diffusion model can serve as a general-purpose 'motor system' for interactive avatars, responding to external impulses and continuous control signals in real time without any physics engine. This opens a path toward replacing hand-crafted motion graphs in games with learned, reactive motor controllers, though the authors note computational cost remains too high for that application today. Key open questions include extending control to text/speech inputs, adding scene and obstacle awareness, and reducing the occasional non-physical artifacts (e.g., the avatar 'flying' when getting up). The clean separation of motor control (stage 1) from semantic behavior (stage 2) via a generic adaptor is a reusable architectural pattern for future interactive character systems.
