## Problem

Visual Reinforcement Learning agents overfit to specific domain features during training, causing significant performance degradation in unseen environments. Existing data augmentation methods rely on predefined pixel transformations or limited external dataset distributions, inherently constraining the extent of domain variability they can introduce. While Stable Diffusion can generate highly diverse domains, directly applying it to VRL requires carefully regulating the degree of domain shift to align with the agent's learning progress and maintain training stability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a visual state (RGB image) sampled from the replay buffer and the agent's current episode return (scalar) to produce an augmented visual sample of the same spatial resolution as the original state, along with an updated initial Gaussian noise vector in Stable Diffusion's latent space. The augmented sample is a pixel-wise Hadamard blend of the original state and a diffusion-generated domain image, weighted by a saliency mask derived from the value network's gradient. Generation uses distilled Stable Diffusion v1.4 with TinyAutoEncoder at only 3 denoising steps for computational efficiency.

## Method

DGA2 combines two mechanisms: Adaptive Domain Shift (ADS) and saliency-guided augmentation. ADS dynamically modulates the initial noise in Stable Diffusion's latent space based on episode return changes: when return improves, the noise is updated as a weighted blend with fresh random noise where the weight v = K·(R_e − R_{e−1})/(R_max − R_{e−1}) controls shift magnitude; when return stagnates, the domain is held constant; when return reaches R_max, a completely new noise vector is sampled. For augmentation, a saliency map is extracted by computing the absolute gradient of the value network with respect to the state, normalizing it, and applying an exponential transform (γ=0.3) to emphasize task-relevant regions. Stable Diffusion then generates a diverse background domain from the current noise vector, and the final augmented sample is formed by masking: the saliency-weighted original state is combined with the diffusion-generated domain in non-salient regions, preserving task-critical pixels while replacing background context.

## Results

On DMControl-GB (SAC), DGA2 achieves average episode returns of 876 (Color Easy), 834 (Color Hard), 844 (Video Easy), and 616 (Video Hard), improving over NoAug by 22%, 31%, 74%, and 353% respectively. On Procgen, DrAC+DGA2 attains an average rank of 1.50 across 8 games, compared to 3.63 for DrAC alone and 4.75 for PPO. On Adroit (Door task), DGA2 reaches 61.0% success rate on Color Hard and 53.0% on Video Hard, outperforming PIE-G (56.3% and 41.3%) and SVEA (14.7% and 9.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGA2 outperforms all augmentation baselines on DMControl-GB: on Video Hard (SAC), it achieves 616 vs. Overlay (351), SRM (157), and CNSN (150), with the gap widening as domain shift severity increases. On Procgen, DrAC+DGA2 (rank 1.50) beats DrAC (3.63), MixStyle (3.63), SAR (5.38), RAD (5.38), and RADIAL (7.00), while PPO+DGA2 (3.50) surpasses PPO (4.75) and RAD (5.38). On Adroit, DGA2 wins on the Door task across all four domain shifts (e.g., 61.0% vs. PIE-G 56.3% on Color Hard; 53.0% vs. SVEA 9.7% on Video Hard), but loses to PIE-G on the Hammer task under Color Hard (36.0% vs. 49.3%) and Video Hard (22.0% vs. 45.7%). SRM and CNSN sometimes degrade SAC performance below NoAug (e.g., SRM 675 vs. NoAug 717 on Color Easy), whereas DGA2 consistently improves over NoAug in every setting. The key differentiator is that DGA2's adaptive domain shift and saliency-based masking preserve task-relevant information while generating genuinely novel backgrounds, whereas static or normalization-based methods (SRM, CNSN, MixStyle) are constrained to the training distribution's manifold.

## Relation to Prior Work

DGA2 sits at the intersection of two research threads: diffusion-model-based data augmentation (building on Stable Diffusion's latent-space denoising pipeline) and saliency-guided representation learning in VRL (adopting the gradient-based saliency extraction from SGQN). It departs from prior augmentation methods (RAD, SRM, MixStyle, CNSN, SAR) by replacing fixed or normalization-based transformations with a generative model that can produce genuinely novel domain images, and by introducing an Adaptive Domain Shift mechanism that modulates the degree of domain variation based on the agent's episode return rather than applying a static or random augmentation schedule. This adaptive control is the paper's key conceptual departure: prior methods either augment uniformly or rely on learned normalization parameters, but none tie the augmentation strength directly to the agent's learning trajectory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work offers a practical recipe for improving VRL generalization that is algorithm-agnostic (validated with SAC, DrQ, SVEA, PPO, and DrAC) and does not require training an additional generative model from scratch, since it leverages a pre-trained Stable Diffusion checkpoint. However, the computational overhead of running diffusion denoising steps during training, the reliance on a fixed sensitivity parameter K, and the observed underperformance on the Adroit Hammer task in hard settings (where PIE-G still leads) suggest that the adaptive mechanism may not yet be robust enough for all task types. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-resolution or multi-frame visual inputs, whether the saliency-based masking remains effective when task-relevant regions are spatially diffuse, and whether the FID-based domain distance metric adequately captures the semantic shifts that matter for RL generalization.
