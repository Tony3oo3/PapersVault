## Problem

Existing large multimodal models for autonomous driving are typically fine-tuned on a single dataset targeting specific tasks (e.g., corner-case perception or road understanding), resulting in poor generalization across diverse driving scenarios and unseen benchmarks. No prior work has demonstrated a unified model that simultaneously handles multiple input modalities (single/multi-view images and videos) and the full spectrum of AD tasks (perception, prediction, planning) while maintaining strong zero-shot transfer to new datasets.

## Input / Output

The model accepts visual signals at 384×384 resolution (single-view images, multi-view images, single-view videos, or multi-view videos from cameras or LiDAR) alongside a text instruction, and produces structured text outputs including object bounding boxes normalized to a 0–100 coordinate range, camera-perspective-tagged grounding regions (e.g., [CAM_BACK, x_min, y_min, x_max, y_max]), motion predictions, action decisions, and free-form driving reasoning. A key design detail is the perspective-aware prompt: different placeholders distinguish image from video tokens, numerical labels identify each camera perspective, and explicit text descriptions name the specific sensor, enabling the LLM to reason about spatial relationships across views.

## Method

RoboTron-Drive adapts the LLaVA architecture (SigLIP vision encoder → 2-layer MLP projector → Llama-3.1-8B LLM) with a perspective-aware prompt that encodes camera identity and data format directly into the instruction, and applies 2×2 spatial pooling on video features for computational efficiency. Training follows a four-stage curriculum: (1) projector-only alignment on LCS-558K, (2) full-model single-image pretraining on image-text pairs, (3) multi-capability pretraining on visual instruction tuning data plus grounding/perception data across diverse formats, and (4) driving fine-tuning on six augmented and standardized AD datasets simultaneously. A critical data-engineering contribution is QA augmentation (using GPT-4o-mini to diversify fixed-template questions and convert open-ended QAs to multiple-choice) and QA standardization (unifying object representation formats and normalizing coordinates across heterogeneous datasets), which enables mutual improvement across datasets rather than overfitting to any single one.

## Results

RoboTron-Drive achieves state-of-the-art average scores across all six AD benchmarks: 64.18 on CODA-LM, 76.67 on MAPLM, 61.30 on DriveLM, 69.20 on LingoQA, 50.25 on OmniDrive, and 46.30 on NuInstruct. The most dramatic improvement is on NuInstruct (+26.17 over the specialist model) and MAPLM (+4.91), where multi-view and detection-heavy tasks benefit most from the all-in-one training. On zero-shot generalization to unseen datasets, RoboTron-Drive scores 43.10 (BDD-X), 53.32 (DRAMA), and 61.06 (DriveBench), outperforming all specialist models fine-tuned on individual datasets. On the nuScenes open-loop planning task, it achieves 0.33 m average L2 error and 26% collision rate, surpassing UniAD (0.46 m / 37%) and VAD-Base (0.37 m / 33%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the six specialist models (each fine-tuned on a single dataset), RoboTron-Drive wins on five of six benchmarks, with the largest margin on NuInstruct (+26.17) and MAPLM (+4.91), while trailing Drive-OV by 0.90 on LingoQA (69.20 vs. 70.10) due to Drive-OV's extensive video pre-training. Compared to zero-shot GPT-4o, RoboTron-Drive leads by 15.39 on CODA-LM, 53.55 on MAPLM, 20.09 on DriveLM, 50.80 on LingoQA, 31.03 on OmniDrive, and 44.35 on NuInstruct, confirming that domain-specific fine-tuning is critical for AD tasks. On DriveBench (Table 5), RoboTron-Drive-8B (61.06 avg) surpasses GPT-4o (51.96), Qwen2-VL-72B (48.01), InterVL2-8B (46.43), and DriveLM-7B (43.17) across all four task categories, including Planning (79.44 vs. GPT-4o's 75.75). On the nuScenes planning task, RoboTron-Drive reduces average L2 error to 0.33 m and collision rate to 26%, outperforming both UniAD (0.46 m / 37%) and VAD-Base (0.37 m / 33%). The only comparison where RoboTron-Drive does not lead is LingoQA vs. Drive-OV, which the authors attribute to Drive-OV's large-scale video pre-training advantage on simple scene-description tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoboTron-Drive builds directly on the LLaVA architecture (vision encoder + projector + LLM) but departs from the dominant paradigm in AD LMMs where each model is fine-tuned on a single dataset for a narrow set of tasks (e.g., CODA-LM for corner cases, MAPLM for road understanding, NuInstruct for prediction). The key conceptual departure is the all-in-one formulation: a single model trained via curriculum learning on six heterogeneous datasets simultaneously, augmented with a perspective-aware prompt to disambiguate multi-view inputs. This contrasts with prior specialist approaches (DriveLM, OmniDrive, LingoQA, etc.) that each target one input modality and a subset of perception/prediction/planning tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to evaluate an AD LMM comprehensively across six public benchmarks and three unseen datasets, establishing a more realistic evaluation protocol than the single-dataset benchmarking that has dominated the field. The finding that multi-dataset training yields mutual improvement (generalist outperforms all specialists on every benchmark) validates the all-in-one approach and suggests future AD LMMs should be trained on diverse data rather than domain-specific subsets. Limitations include reliance on GPT-4o as an evaluator for some benchmarks, open-loop planning evaluation only, and text-based outputs that do not yet produce direct control signals. Open questions include whether the curriculum schedule is truly necessary versus simply having more diverse data, and whether the approach scales to closed-loop, real-time deployment.
