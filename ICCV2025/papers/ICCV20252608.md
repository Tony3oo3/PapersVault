## Problem

Traditional LIF neurons in SNNs suffer from substantial information loss during spike transmission because the recursive membrane potential update means each spiking decision is based on only partially integrated input history. Additionally, the per-time-step sequential computation and single-bit spike storage prevent efficient use of modern GPU parallelism, resulting in O(T) time and space complexity that scales poorly with the number of time steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252608/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet, COCO, ADE20K) or event streams (CIFAR10-DVS, DVS-Gesture, N-Caltech101) and produces class labels, bounding boxes with class labels, or pixel-wise segmentation masks. Internally, the network compresses the full T-step binary spike matrix into a single integer vector s_zip per neuron via multiplication with the decay vector q = [τ^(T-1), …, τ^0]^T, so the membrane time constant τ doubles as a quantization parameter that controls the effective bit-width of the spike representation.

## Method

SpikePack replaces the recursive LIF membrane potential with a global potential v_g^l = w^l · S^{l-1} · q computed as a single GeMM over the compressed spike vector s_zip^{l-1} = S^{l-1}·q, aggregating the entire input sequence before any spiking decision is made. The output spike sequence is then decoded from v_g^l using a dynamic threshold θ_t = θ/τ^(t-T), and the total spike count over T steps is obtained in closed form as s_zip^l = ⌈v_g^l/θ⌉_τ, reducing both time and space complexity from O(T) to O(1). For training, gradients propagate directly through the compressed representation with the quantization derivative approximated as 1, eliminating the need for BPTT or temporal unrolling. The model preserves biological features (membrane reset, leaky integration) while remaining compatible with standard ANN architectures, enabling near-lossless ANN-to-SNN conversion with as few as 6–8 time steps.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k with ResNet-34 and 4 time steps, SpikePack achieves 73.4% top-1 accuracy with only 8.1 min/epoch training time and 24.3 MB/image memory, outperforming all compared SNN training methods. Scaling to ResNet-50 (T=4) yields 78.7% accuracy, and with Spikeformer-8-512 (T=8) reaches 80.1% while maintaining O(1) memory (150.5 MB/img) regardless of T. For ANN-to-SNN conversion, SpikePack achieves near-lossless performance with just 8 time steps: 77.81% on ResNet-34 (ANN: 80.72%) and 88.22% on ViT-L/14 (ANN: 88.27%), compared to 256+ steps required by prior methods. On COCO detection, DINO-r50 with SpikePack reaches 50.1% mAP@50:95 at T=10, and on ADE20K segmentation, Segformer-b2 achieves 45.6% mIoU at T=10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpikePack (ResNet-34, T=4) achieves 73.4% top-1 accuracy, surpassing SEW ResNet [8] (67.0%), MS ResNet [15] (69.4%), Parallel SNN [10] (70.5%), TEBN [7] (64.3%), TET [6] (68.0%), OTTT [47] (64.2%), SLTT [33] (66.2%), and STBP-tdBN [56] (63.7%) by 2.9–9.7 percentage points, while also reducing memory from 71–267 MB/img to 24.3 MB/img. Against T-RevSNN [14] (73.2% with 29.8M params), SpikePack matches accuracy with fewer parameters (21.8M) and lower memory (24.3 vs 85.7 MB/img). For ANN-to-SNN conversion, SpikePack at T=8 (77.81% ResNet-34, 88.22% ViT-L/14) substantially outperforms RMP [13] (55.65%), Opt [5] (70.06%), Calib [25] (74.61%), and SNM [44] (69.31%) at T=256, and approaches STA [17] (82.79% ViT-B/32 at T=256) with only 1/32 the time steps. On object detection, SpikePack with DINO-r50 (50.1% mAP@50:95, T=10) exceeds SpikeYOLO [30] (48.9%, T=4) and EMS-YOLO [43] (30.1%, T=4), while Meta-SpikeFormer [51] reports only mAP@50 without mAP@50:95. On neuromorphic datasets, SpikePack achieves 84.6% on CIFAR10-DVS (T=16) and 97.4% on DVS-Gesture (T=10), confirming versatility across static and event-based inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpikePack builds directly on the LIF neuron's membrane potential dynamics (Dayan & Abbott) but departs fundamentally from the recursive per-step update by aggregating the entire input spike sequence into a single global potential before spike generation. It contrasts with prior information-transmission approaches (ternary spikes, LIFB, integer-valued neurons) that expand spike representations, and with training-efficiency methods (OTTT, SLTT, T-RevSNN) that reduce memory but retain O(T) temporal dependencies. The key conceptual departure is treating the membrane time constant τ as a quantization parameter and compressing the full spike sequence into a bitwise integer (s_zip), which enables standard GeMM operations and O(1) complexity in both time and space with respect to T.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-lossless ANN-to-SNN conversion with only 6–8 time steps (versus 32–256 for prior methods) and the O(1) complexity claim could substantially lower the barrier to deploying SNNs on both GPUs and neuromorphic hardware, making SNNs competitive with ANNs for practical workloads. The FPGA validation on an xczu3eg chip demonstrates cross-platform viability, though the hardware experiments are limited to ResNet-34/50 at a single clock frequency. Open questions include whether the global-potential formulation degrades in very deep or recurrent architectures, whether the τ-as-quantization interpretation holds under non-stationary input distributions, and whether the O(1) memory claim persists when batch normalization or other stateful layers are introduced.
