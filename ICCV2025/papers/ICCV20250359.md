## Problem

Existing Vision Mamba methods for image restoration suffer from two coupled limitations: (1) computational complexity and parameter count scale linearly with the number of scanning sequences, making it costly to add scanning directions, and (2) the two-dimensional scanning strategy causes local pixel forgetting, where spatially adjacent pixels become distantly separated in the flattened 1D token sequence, degrading the preservation of local spatial relationships critical for restoration tasks.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a degraded RGB image of shape H×W×3 and outputs a restored high-quality RGB image of the same spatial dimensions via residual learning (the network predicts a residual that is added to the input). FLOPs are reported at a fixed 256×256 resolution, and training employs a progressive patch-size schedule from 128×128 up to 384×384 with batch sizes decreasing from 64 to 8. The same architecture is applied across four degradation types (Gaussian noise, downsampling, motion blur, haze) with task-specific training data.

## Method

EAMamba introduces a Multi-Head Selective Scan Module (MHSSM) that partitions feature channels into n groups and performs selective scanning independently on each group, then concatenates the outputs—this allows multiple scanning directions to be processed without increasing FLOPs or parameter count beyond a single scan. On top of MHSSM, an all-around scanning strategy combines horizontal, vertical, diagonal, and flipped-diagonal scans (with reversed orientations) to capture holistic spatial context and mitigate local pixel forgetting. The overall architecture is a four-level UNet-like encoder-decoder composed of MambaFormer blocks, where each block pairs MHSSM (as the token mixer) with a channel MLP (Simple FFN) under Layer Normalization and residual connections. The model is trained end-to-end with AdamW, cosine-annealed learning rate, and L1 loss over 450K iterations, achieving 31–89% FLOPs reduction over prior Vision Mamba baselines while matching or exceeding their PSNR across denoising, super-resolution, deblurring, and dehazing benchmarks.

## Results

EAMamba achieves a 31–89% reduction in FLOPs compared to existing Vision Mamba methods while maintaining competitive or superior image quality across four restoration tasks. On synthetic Gaussian denoising (CBSD68, σ=50), EAMamba reaches 28.62 dB PSNR using only 137G FLOPs versus MambaIR's 1290G (11% of the compute). On RealSR ×4 super-resolution, EAMamba attains the best PSNR (29.60 dB) among all Vision Mamba methods with the lowest FLOPs (137G vs. 230G for MambaIR-UNet and 200G for VMambaIR). On GoPro deblurring, EAMamba achieves 33.58 dB PSNR, surpassing the second-best method (SFNet, 33.27 dB) by 0.31 dB, and on RESIDE SOTS-Indoor dehazing it reaches 43.19 dB, outperforming DehazeFormer-L (40.05 dB) by over 3 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary Vision Mamba baseline MambaIR-UNet [57], EAMamba reduces FLOPs by 41% (137G vs. 230G) on SIDD real-world denoising with only a 0.02 dB PSNR drop (39.87 vs. 39.89), and by 40% on RealSR while matching or exceeding PSNR at ×4 (29.60 vs. 29.53). Compared to VMambaIR [58], EAMamba uses 31% fewer FLOPs (137G vs. 200G) and achieves higher PSNR at ×4 super-resolution (29.60 vs. 29.56). On GoPro deblurring, EAMamba outperforms all non-Mamba baselines including SFNet (33.58 vs. 33.27 dB), Stripformer (33.08 dB), and Restormer (32.92 dB), while using fewer FLOPs than MPRNet (137G vs. 760G) and MAXIM-3S (137G vs. 339G). On RESIDE dehazing, EAMamba surpasses DehazeFormer-L by 3.14 dB on SOTS-Indoor (43.19 vs. 40.05) and DeHamer by 1.16 dB on SOTS-Outdoor (36.34 vs. 35.18), with substantially fewer parameters (25.3M vs. 132.5M for DeHamer). On synthetic denoising (Table 1), EAMamba matches or slightly exceeds MambaIR's PSNR across all noise levels while using only 11% of its FLOPs (137G vs. 1290G), and outperforms Restormer by 0.03 dB at σ=50 on CBSD68 with comparable FLOPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

EAMamba extends the Vision Mamba lineage (Mamba → VMamba → MambaIR) by replacing the full-channel 2D Selective Scan (2DSS) with a Multi-Head Selective Scan (MHSS) that splits channels into groups, each processed by an independent selective scan. This channel-splitting design decouples computational cost from the number of scanning directions, a key departure from MambaIR and VMambaIR where adding scan patterns linearly increases FLOPs and parameters. The all-around scanning strategy (2D + diagonal) directly addresses the local pixel forgetting limitation identified in MambaIR, building on the ERF analysis from that work to motivate additional diagonal coverage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 31–89% FLOPs reduction while maintaining or slightly improving PSNR across four restoration tasks makes Vision Mamba architectures substantially more practical for high-resolution and real-time deployment, where the quadratic cost of attention and the linear-but-large cost of multi-direction 2DSS were prohibitive. The MHSS channel-splitting pattern is a general architectural primitive that could transfer to other Vision Mamba applications beyond restoration. Open questions include whether the efficiency advantage persists at much higher resolutions (e.g., 1024×1024+), whether the all-around scanning benefit generalizes to non-restoration tasks like segmentation or detection, and whether further scan combinations (e.g., adding Z-order or Hilbert) yield diminishing returns as the ablation in Table 7 suggests.
