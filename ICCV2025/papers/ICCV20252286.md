## Problem

Existing single-image 3D generation methods produce RGB views but lack spatially-varying PBR material parameters and surface normals, making relighting and controlled appearance editing impossible or requiring separate, multi-view-inconsistent estimation steps. SViM3D addresses this by being the first camera-controllable multi-view diffusion model that jointly generates fully spatially-varying PBR parameters, RGB color, and normals in a single forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252286/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image and a user-defined camera orbit of 21 poses (elevation/azimuthal angle pairs) and produces an 11-channel orbital video at 576×576: 3 channels for RGB radiance, 5 for Cook-Torrance PBR parameters (3-channel basecolor, 1-channel roughness, 1-channel metallic), and 3 for camera-space surface normals. Material parameters and normals are encoded as images via the pretrained VAE using an ORM channel layout, so the network operates on a 16-channel latent stack. Downstream, the multi-view outputs serve as pseudo-ground-truth for a 3D reconstruction pipeline that yields a textured mesh with PBR materials, or can be directly relit in 2.5D image space.

## Method

SViM3D extends the SV3D latent video diffusion architecture by treating PBR material maps and normals as images encoded through the same VAE, then augmenting the UNet's input/output channels from 4 to 16 (four 4-channel latents for RGB, basecolor, roughness-metallic, and normals) with weight initialization by copying RGB channel parameters. Training uses a synthetic multi-illumination multi-view dataset rendered with Blender Cycles, with a two-phase schedule: first freezing temporal attention blocks for ~100k steps on all data, then fine-tuning the full UNet for 60k steps on the highest-quality PBR subset to preserve temporal coherence while adapting to material generation. For 3D reconstruction, the generated views are used as pseudo-ground-truth in a four-phase pipeline (illumination pre-optimization → Instant-NGP → DMTet → mesh extraction), augmented with view-dependent masking weighted by normal-view alignment, per-view learnable homography correction to compensate for residual multi-view inconsistencies, and a fast differentiable split-sum environment lighting module using precomputed illumination pyramids with filtered importance sampling.

## Results

On the Poly Haven test set, SViM3D achieves 19.57 PSNR / 0.85 SSIM / 6.93 FID for multi-view RGB radiance (21 frames), outperforming SV3D (18.41 / 0.83 / 7.8). For multi-view albedo generation, SViM3D reaches 18.27 PSNR and 9.42 FID, substantially beating SV3D+IID (15.62 / 28.41) and SV3D+RGB↔X (15.15 / 22.13). On single-frame material prediction, SViM3D attains 28.68 PSNR for basecolor and 25.36 PSNR for roughness-metallic, far exceeding IID (8.62 / 11.80) and RGB↔X (16.01 / 17.4). On Stanford Orb, SViM3D achieves 19.34 PSNR for NVS and 21.86 PSNR for relighting, surpassing SF3D (16.81 / 20.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SViM3D outperforms all baselines on the core multi-view material generation task. Against SV3D [93] (the underlying video diffusion model without material outputs), SViM3D improves RGB NVS PSNR from 18.41 to 19.57 and FID from 7.8 to 6.93, showing that joint PBR generation also aids RGB quality. For albedo, SViM3D (18.27 PSNR, 9.42 FID) beats the best pipeline baseline SV3D+StableMaterial [63] (18.12 PSNR, 17.22 FID) and substantially outperforms SV3D+IID [54] (15.62 PSNR, 28.41 FID) and SV3D+RGB↔X [103] (15.15 PSNR, 22.13 FID), with the FID gap being particularly large (9.42 vs. 17–28). On single-frame material prediction (Table 1), SViM3D dominates: basecolor PSNR 28.68 vs. 20.59 (SM), 16.01 (RGB↔X), and 8.62 (IID); roughness-metallic PSNR 25.36 vs. 19.1, 17.4, and 11.80 respectively. On Stanford Orb [55], SViM3D beats SF3D [14] by 2.53 PSNR on NVS (19.34 vs. 16.81) and 1.76 PSNR on relighting (21.86 vs. 20.1), confirming superior 3D reconstruction quality with physically-based materials.

## Relation to Prior Work

SViM3D extends SV3D (a latent video diffusion model for camera-controlled multi-view synthesis) by augmenting its output channels to jointly predict spatially-varying PBR parameters (albedo, roughness, metallic) and surface normals alongside RGB. The key architectural departure is treating material maps and normals as images that can be encoded by the existing VAE, avoiding the need for a separate latent representation. This contrasts with prior approaches (IID, MaterialFusion, RGB↔X) that decouple material estimation from multi-view generation, and with 3D reconstruction methods (DMTet, NeRF-based pipelines) that require per-object optimization. The 3D reconstruction stage builds on Instant-NGP and DMTet but introduces view-dependent masking, learnable homography correction, and fast differentiable environment-map lighting to handle residual multi-view inconsistencies in the generated pseudo-ground-truth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SViM3D is positioned as the first foundational model that unifies multi-view 3D reconstruction and PBR material understanding in a single camera-controllable diffusion framework, enabling relightable 3D asset generation from a single image without per-object optimization. The practical impact for AR/VR, gaming, and media production is significant, as it removes the need for multi-view captures or text prompts to obtain physically-based materials. However, the method is limited to object-centric scenes and cannot represent complex materials like transparency, and the baselines compared (IID, SM, RGB↔X) address somewhat different sub-tasks, making direct comparison imperfect. Open questions include whether the VAE-as-image trick generalizes to more complex material models (clearcoat, sheen, transmission) and whether the approach scales to full scenes.
