## Problem

Existing human-scene interaction (HSI) modeling methods rely on generative approaches conditioned on text, action labels, or past motions, which lack the controllability and flexibility needed for practical applications like keyframe-guided animation or refining noisy real-world HSI data. While motion in-betweening has been studied for isolated motions, it has not been formally addressed in the scene-aware setting, and prior scene-aware methods use conditional VAEs with limited expressivity. Additionally, real-world keyframes from IMU sensors or video extraction are inherently noisy, a challenge overlooked by existing in-betweening methods that assume precise inputs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D scene (voxelized at 0.1 m resolution into a 48×24×48 occupancy grid), a sparse set of key poses in SMPL parameter space (201-dim vectors comprising 22 joint positions, 6D root orientation, and 21×6 local pose parameters), a binary key-pose mask, and 7-dim body shape features (inter-joint distances). Keyframes may be noisy, and the keyframe interval is typically 60 frames (2 s) for sparse settings or 3 frames for dense noisy settings. The output is a dense 121-frame motion sequence in the same SMPL parameter space, satisfying both keyframe constraints and scene collision avoidance.

## Method

SceneMI is a conditional diffusion model (U-Net with AdaGN and 1D convolutions) that synthesizes full motion sequences from sparse keyframes within a 3D scene. Scene context is encoded at two scales: a global occupancy voxel grid processed by a ViT into a 512-dim vector, and local Basis Point Set features (64 anchor points on the SMPL mesh, with nearest-scene-point offsets of 64×3) computed at each keyframe and embedded via MLP. During training, keyframe positions in the noisy diffusion sample are imputed with clean values, and a two-phase denoising strategy handles noisy keyframes: from timestep T to T*+1, noisy keyframes guide alignment, while from T* to 1 the full sequence is iteratively denoised (T=1000, T*=20). Classifier-free guidance (10% global-scene dropout during training, scale w=2.5 at inference) and auxiliary joint-position and velocity losses via forward kinematics further improve output quality.

## Results

On the TRUMANS dataset with noise-free keyframes (60-frame interval), SceneMI achieves the best performance across all metrics: FID of 0.123 (vs. 0.371 for the next-best OmniControl), MJPE Key of 0.006 m, MJPE All of 0.023 m, collision frame ratio of 0.113, and penetration max of 0.043 m. On the real-world GIMO dataset, SceneMI reduces foot skating from 0.261 to 0.163 (37.5% reduction) and jerk from 0.573 to 0.249 (56.5% reduction) relative to the original noisy data, while maintaining a collision frame ratio (0.060) comparable to the ground-truth (0.057).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SceneMI outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric in the primary TRUMANS evaluation. Against the closest competitor OmniControl [74], SceneMI improves FID by 3× (0.123 vs. 0.371), reduces MJPE Key by 36× (0.006 vs. 0.217 m), and halves the collision frame ratio (0.113 vs. 0.211). The scene-agnostic diffusion baselines MDM [64] and StableMoFusion [26] struggle with keyframe alignment (MJPE Key 0.568 and 0.412 m respectively) because they model motion distributions rather than in-betweening. SceneDiffuser [25] and Wang et al. [66], though scene-aware, produce poor motion quality (FID 1.397 and 3.243) due to reliance on CVAE or global point-cloud features with limited expressivity. CondMDI [10], the only other diffusion-based in-betweening method, lags on both motion quality (FID 0.943) and keyframe alignment (MJPE Key 0.452 m), partly because it requires velocity features unavailable in practice. On the out-of-domain GIMO dataset, SceneMI further demonstrates robustness: it reduces foot skating and jerk substantially below all baselines (e.g., jerk 0.249 vs. 0.828 for MDM) while keeping collision rates near the ground-truth level.

## Relation to Prior Work

SceneMI sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: scene-agnostic motion in-betweening (CondMDI, OmniControl, Harvey et al.) and scene-aware motion synthesis (TRUMANS, SceneDiffuser, HUMANISE). It departs from both by reformulating HSI modeling as a keyframe-conditioned in-betweening task within 3D scenes, a combination neither line had addressed. The method borrows the U-Net/AdaGN denoiser from Karunratanakul et al. [33], the BPS local encoding from Prokudin et al. [56], the ViT-based global voxel encoding from TRUMANS [30], and the noise-as-diffusion-timestep insight from MotionMix [24] and ROHM [84] to handle imperfect keyframes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SceneMI provides a practical, controllable alternative to the dominant text- or category-conditioned HSI generation paradigm, enabling direct keyframe-guided animation and post-hoc refinement of noisy real-world HSI data (37.5% foot-skating and 56.5% jitter reduction on GIMO). Its robust generalization from synthetic TRUMANS scenes to smartphone-scanned GIMO scenes suggests the dual global/local scene encoding is not overfit to a single scene source. Key limitations remain: the method requires full-pose keyframes (no partial-pose support), lacks text conditioning for higher-level controllability, and relies on feature-level rather than model-level scene fusion, which may cap expressivity for complex interactions.
