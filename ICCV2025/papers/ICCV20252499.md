## Problem

Visual self-supervised learning (SSL) models have underperformed CLIP on multimodal tasks like VQA, but this gap has been confounded by differences in training data scale and distribution—SSL was typically trained on ImageNet or LVD-142M while CLIP used billion-scale web image-text pairs. The paper asks whether language supervision is truly necessary for strong multimodal visual representations, or whether the gap is an artifact of data rather than paradigm.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252499/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The vision encoder takes RGB images at 224×224 (main experiments) or up to 518×518 (high-resolution adaptation) and produces a visual feature embedding. These features are consumed as a frozen encoder in a two-stage visual instruction tuning pipeline: first an MLP adapter projects features into Llama-3 8B Instruct's dimensionality, then both adapter and LLM are finetuned jointly. Evaluation spans 16 VQA benchmarks from Cambrian-1 (General, Knowledge, OCR & Chart, Vision-Centric) plus classic vision tasks (ImageNet-1k linear probe, ADE20K segmentation, NYUv2 depth).

## Method

The paper trains DINOv2 (Web-DINO) and MAE (Web-MAE) ViT models from 1B to 7B parameters using 2 billion unlabeled images from MetaCLIP (MC-2B), the same dataset used for state-of-the-art CLIP, with off-the-shelf training recipes and no architectural modifications. This controlled setup isolates the effect of training paradigm from data distribution. The key finding is that visual SSL exhibits continued log-linear improvement with both model size and examples seen (up to 8B), while CLIP saturates beyond ~3B parameters. A secondary contribution shows that filtering MC-2B to retain only text-containing images (1.3% via SmolVLM2) yields a Web-DINO model that outperforms full-data CLIP on OCR & Chart by +4.3%, demonstrating that data composition can substitute for language supervision. Representational alignment analysis with LLMs confirms that scaling SSL naturally develops text-sensitive features without explicit language signals.

## Results

Web-DINO ViT-7B (trained on 8B MC-2B samples) achieves 59.9 average VQA at 518px resolution, surpassing SigLIP2 ViT-SO400M (62.0 at 384px) and MetaCLIP ViT-G (54.8 at 224px). At the standard 224px setting, Web-DINO reaches 55.2 average VQA, matching SigLIP2 (56.3) despite seeing 5× less data and receiving no language supervision. On classic vision tasks, Web-DINO achieves 86.5% ImageNet-1k linear probe accuracy and 42.6 ADE20K mIoU (mask scoring), outperforming MetaCLIP ViT-G (86.4%, 46.7%) and DINOv2 ViT-g (86.0%, 53.0). Web-DINO also outperforms all off-the-shelf visual SSL baselines (DINOv2, MAE, I-JEPA) across every VQA category.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIP (Radford et al.), Web-DINO matches or exceeds average VQA performance at 5B+ parameters (55.2 vs. ~53.0 for CLIP ViT-7B at 224px), while CLIP performance saturates beyond 3B parameters and Web-DINO continues improving log-linearly. Against SigLIP/SigLIP2 (Zhai et al.), Web-DINO at 224px (55.2 AVG) nearly matches SigLIP2 (56.3) despite training on 5× fewer samples (8B vs. 45B) and receiving zero language supervision; at 518px Web-DINO (59.9) approaches SigLIP2 at 384px (62.0). Against MetaCLIP ViT-G (Xu et al.), Web-DINO wins on both VQA (55.2 vs. 54.8 at 224px) and classic vision (86.5% vs. 86.4% IN1k, 52.6 vs. 46.7 ADE20K ms.). Against off-the-shelf visual SSL baselines, Web-DINO substantially outperforms MAE ViT-H (55.2 vs. 45.2 AVG VQA), I-JEPA ViT-H (55.2 vs. 44.7), and DINOv2 ViT-g (55.2 vs. 47.9), with the largest gains in OCR & Chart (39.4 vs. 20.6/21.2/21.2). Notably, a Web-DINO ViT-2B trained on only 1.3% of MC-2B (heavily filtered for text-containing images) surpasses full-data CLIP ViT-2B on OCR & Chart by +4.3%, demonstrating that data composition can substitute for language supervision on text-centric tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper takes off-the-shelf visual SSL methods (DINOv2, MAE) and CLIP, and trains them under a controlled protocol on the same billion-scale MetaCLIP web data, removing the confound of different training distributions that has separated the two lines of work. Architecturally, it extends the ViT-g configuration from DINOv2 to 1B–7B parameter models, and adopts the Cambrian-1 VQA evaluation suite as a diverse testbed for vision encoders. The contribution is explicitly empirical: no new pretraining objective or architecture is proposed; the paper's value lies in the controlled scaling study and the data-composition ablation (filtering for text-containing images) that isolates why SSL closes the OCR gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that pure visual SSL can match or exceed language-supervised CLIP on VQA (including OCR & Chart) when trained on the same web-scale data, and that SSL continues to improve past 7B while CLIP saturates—challenges the prevailing assumption that linguistic supervision is necessary for multimodal visual representations. This opens a vision-centric path for MLLM design that avoids the data-engineering burden of image-text pairing. Limitations include the 224px comparison (no high-resolution adaptation), the reliance on a single LLM backbone (Llama-3 8B) for VQA evaluation, and the fact that the data-filtering result uses multiple epochs on a small subset. Open questions include whether the scaling advantage persists beyond 7B, whether more aggressive data curation can close remaining gaps in Knowledge VQA, and whether these findings transfer to video or 3D modalities.
