## Problem

Existing single-image fluid animation methods rely on 2D motion estimators or data-only 3D motion predictors that produce physically implausible results, such as fluids penetrating occluding boundaries. No prior method generates 4D fluid scenes (3D geometry + temporally consistent motion) from a single image while enforcing fundamental physical constraints like the Navier-Stokes equations and no-through boundary conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251903/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB fluid image, a user-specified camera trajectory, a monocular depth map, and a binary fluid-area mask as input, and produces a T-frame video that captures both fluid motion and camera movement from arbitrary viewpoints. The depth map is used to lift pixels into 3D Gaussians, and the fluid mask defines the region where physics-informed velocity prediction is applied; the output video is rendered from feature-based 3D Gaussians through a decoder, enabling novel-view synthesis beyond the input camera pose.

## Method

The core contribution is a conditional physics-informed neural network (PINN) that predicts a 3D velocity field u(x,t) from image features extracted by a CNN, supervised jointly by dense 3D scene flows (lifted from RAFT optical flow + monocular depth) and a physics-aware loss derived from simplified incompressible Navier-Stokes equations (inviscid, pressure-free) plus a divergence-free constraint and a boundary penalty that discourages velocities exiting the fluid region. An auxiliary network head predicts the external force f_g used only in the physics loss. The predicted velocities animate feature-based 3D Gaussians (derived from Layered Depth Images via agglomerative clustering on the depth map) by iteratively displacing Gaussian centers, with symmetric splatting to fill holes; the animated Gaussians are rendered via a tile-based rasterizer and decoded into RGB frames. This data-driven + physics-guided formulation is trained on large-scale video data to learn a generalizable fluid motion prior, in contrast to prior per-scene PINN approaches.

## Results

On the Holynski et al. [18] validation set (162 clips, 40 scenes), the proposed method achieves PSNR 24.98, SSIM 0.78, LPIPS 0.20, and VMAF 44.40 for input-view video generation, outperforming 3D-Cinemagraphy (PSNR 22.81) and Holynski et al. (PSNR 22.11). For novel-view generation, the method reaches PSNR 24.34 and VMAF 45.71, surpassing Make-it-4D (PSNR 21.40, VMAF 22.36) and 3D-Cinemagraphy (PSNR 22.46, VMAF 37.20). In a user study with 30 participants, the proposed method is preferred over all baselines by 42% on average (e.g., 75.5% vs. 24.5% over Holynski et al.).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: Holynski et al. [18] (2D Eulerian motion field warping), 3D-Cinemagraphy [22] (3D point-cloud animation from a single image), and Make-it-4D [31] (longer-trajectory extension of 3D-Cinemagraphy). The proposed method wins on every metric across both input-view and novel-view settings. Against 3D-Cinemagraphy, the improvement is +2.17 PSNR and +3.66 VMAF (input view) and +1.88 PSNR and +8.51 VMAF (novel view). Against Make-it-4D, the gap is larger: +2.94 PSNR and +23.35 VMAF on novel views, reflecting Make-it-4D's degradation on longer trajectories. The user study confirms these quantitative gains, with the proposed method preferred 69.4% of the time over 3D-Cinemagraphy and 71.7% over Make-it-4D. A key qualitative advantage is that the physics-informed dynamics prevent fluid from penetrating occlusions (e.g., rocks), whereas 3D-Cinemagraphy's data-prior-only motion predictor produces physically impossible interpenetration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of physics-informed neural networks (Raissi et al., 2019) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023), applying both to the single-image fluid animation task first formalized by 3D-Cinemagraphy (Li et al., 2023). The key departure from 3D-Cinemagraphy and Make-It-4D is replacing their purely data-driven motion estimators with a conditional PINN supervised by both scene flow and simplified Navier-Stokes residuals, following the loss formulation from Chu et al. (2022). Unlike prior per-scene physics-informed fluid reconstruction methods, this work learns a generalizable velocity prior from large-scale video data, making it applicable to arbitrary unseen fluid images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that embedding fundamental PDE constraints into a learned motion model yields physically plausible fluid animations that respect boundary occlusions — a capability absent from purely data-driven baselines, as shown by the boundary-editing experiment where fluids correctly separate around an added rock. Limitations include the inviscid, incompressible assumption (no viscosity or pressure terms), reliance on monocular depth estimation, and evaluation on a single dataset of 162 clips. Open questions include whether the approach extends to viscous or compressible fluids, more complex multi-body interactions, and whether the physics-informed loss can be made more scalable as the number of boundary constraints grows.
