## Problem

Prior self-supervised representation learning relies on static image datasets like ImageNet that lack temporal context and cannot capture object state changes, while existing video datasets are not object-centric, suffer from long-tail class distributions, and contain cluttered scenes that dilute object-specific signals. The paper asks whether a large-scale, object-centric video dataset combined with an adapted contrastive objective can learn image-level representations that encode both object identity and dynamic state.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252482/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes video object tracks—temporal sequences of cropped bounding boxes around a single tracked object—sampled at random timesteps with a temporal gap of δ=2 frames. Each track is further augmented with spatial crops (random resized cropping, horizontal flip) and color transforms, with an adaptive Gaussian blur whose strength scales inversely with crop resolution to prevent the model from exploiting category-specific track sizes as a shortcut. The output is a 256-dimensional image-level representation from a ViT-Base/16 backbone, used directly for nearest-neighbor classification, few-shot linear probing, or as spatial initialization for video models like TimeSformer.

## Method

The paper introduces TRACKVERSE, a 31.9M-track dataset built by running an automated DETIC (open-vocabulary detector) + ByteTrack pipeline over 600K HD-VILA-100M videos, with class-balanced subsets (up to 3.8M tracks) obtained by capping high-frequency LVIS classes. For pretraining, the authors extend MoCo-v3 with variance-aware contrastive learning: the predictor h_{b→t} is conditioned on spatio-temporal augmentation parameters v=(t, x_c/W, y_c/H, w/W, h/H) encoded via a 2-layer MLP, so the model learns invariance to color-preserving augmentations while remaining sensitive to spatial and temporal shifts that alter object state. This relaxation of the standard view-invariance objective adds only 0.9% extra parameters over vanilla MoCo, ensuring gains stem from the data's dynamics rather than model capacity. The full pipeline—scene segmentation, cartoon/static filtering, detection, tracking, and class-balanced curation—is fully automated and scalable.

## Results

The best TRACKVERSE configuration (3.8M-CB8000, variance-aware MoCo) achieves 50.1% NN accuracy on LVIS-IN, 71.1% FSL on TRACKVERSE, 61.8% top-1 on SSv2, 61.0% object NN on MIT-States, and 67.1% J&Fm on DAVIS-2017, outperforming ImageNet-1K MoCo pretraining (48.8%, 47.7%, 56.8%, 55.8%, 64.8% respectively) under an equivalent 220M forward-backward pass budget. The largest gains appear on state-sensitive tasks: SSv2 action recognition improves by +5.0 points and MIT-States attribute NN by +6.4 points over the ImageNet baseline. Scaling from 0.4M to 3.8M tracks yields consistent improvements, with FSL rising from 55.3% to 71.1% and LVIS-IN NN from 39.6% to 50.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRACKVERSE (3.8M-CB8000) outperforms ImageNet-1K MoCo on every downstream task, with the largest margins on state-sensitive evaluations: +5.0 on SSv2 (61.8 vs 56.8), +6.4 on MIT-States attribute NN (45.3 vs 38.9), and +2.7 on DAVIS Fm (70.1 vs 67.4). Against TAO, the largest tracking dataset available, TRACKVERSE leads by a wide margin on all tasks, e.g., +35.3 on LVIS-IN NN (50.1 vs 14.8) and +51.3 on TrackVerse FSL (71.1 vs 19.8), reflecting TAO's limited scale (17K tracks vs 3.8M). Walking Tours, a non-object-centric urban video dataset, trails TRACKVERSE by 33+ points on LVIS-IN NN and 55 points on FSL, confirming that object-centricity is critical. Scene Clips (the same source videos without object tracking) is the closest non-TrackVerse competitor, yet TRACKVERSE still leads by +14.8 on LVIS-IN NN, +29.6 on FSL, and +9.8 on MIT-States Pair Ft, demonstrating that explicit object tracking and cropping over raw scene context yields substantially better representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MoCo/MoCo-v3 contrastive learning framework (He et al., 2020; Chen et al., 2021) by introducing a variance-aware predictor that conditions on spatio-temporal augmentation parameters, relaxing the strict view-invariance objective to capture state changes in objects over time. It departs from prior SSL representation learning that relies on static image datasets (ImageNet) or non-object-centric natural video (Walking Tours, VideoNet, TAO) by constructing TRACKVERSE, the first large-scale object-centric video track dataset (31.9M tracks, 1203 LVIS classes) built via an automated DETIC+ByteTrack pipeline. The paper positions itself against the video representation learning literature that typically encodes entire clips for action recognition, arguing instead for image-level object representations learned from individual object tracks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TRACKVERSE fills a critical gap as the only large-scale, open-source, object-centric video dataset with full temporal dynamics, enabling research on state-aware object representations that neither static images nor cluttered natural video can provide. The finding that dynamic tracks prevent overfitting at longer training budgets while static frames plateau suggests a practical path to scaling SSL beyond the ImageNet paradigm. Limitations include the automated pipeline's moderate label precision (52.3% on the class-balanced subset), reliance on a single ViT-Base/16 architecture, and YouTube-sourced data that may limit domain generalization. Open questions include whether the variance-aware objective generalizes to other SSL paradigms (e.g., masked image modeling) and whether further scaling the fully automated pipeline yields continued gains.
