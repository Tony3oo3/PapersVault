## Problem

State-of-the-art image-to-3D generators (e.g., TRELLIS, Hunyuan3D 2.0) frequently produce 3D objects that topple under gravity, even when conditioned on images of stable objects, with failure rates of 15–30%. Existing remedies (Atlas3D, PhysComp) rely on differentiable physics simulators for test-time optimization, which are slow, numerically fragile, and prone to local optima. The paper asks whether a feed-forward 3D generator can be aligned with physical soundness using only non-differentiable simulation feedback, eliminating test-time optimization entirely.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image of an object (renderings from Objaverse or synthetic images from FLUX) and outputs a 3D mesh decoded from TRELLIS's structured 3D latents via marching cubes. Stability is defined operationally as a tilting angle below 20° at equilibrium in MuJoCo when the mesh is placed upright on flat ground. The fine-tuned model operates in a single feed-forward pass at test time (~seconds), with no per-shape optimization or additional decoding steps beyond the base model's standard pipeline.

## Method

DSO fine-tunes a pre-trained rectified-flow 3D generator (TRELLIS) by aligning it with binary stability labels from a non-differentiable MuJoCo simulator, using LoRA on the coarse-geometry transformer. The key technical contribution is DRO (Direct Reward Optimization), a novel loss that re-parameterizes the reward signal through the optimal reverse diffusion process and directly encourages the model to denoise stable samples well while denoising unstable samples poorly, without requiring pairwise preference data or querying the reference model during training. Training data is self-generated: the base model produces 3D assets from curated image prompts, each is simulated in MuJoCo to obtain a binary stability label, and the resulting preference dataset (312k models) is used for fine-tuning. This self-improving loop eliminates the need for ground-truth 3D supervision and can operate on entirely synthetic 2D images. The DRO objective converges faster and achieves higher stability (99.0% vs. 95.1%) than the DPO alternative, while also slightly improving geometric fidelity (F-Score 76.17 vs. 73.12) over the base model.

## Results

On the full 65-object evaluation set, TRELLIS+DSO with the DRO objective achieves 99.0% physical stability (vs. 85.1% for base TRELLIS) with a mean rotation angle of 1.88° (vs. 14.14°), while simultaneously improving geometric fidelity (Chamfer Distance 0.0440 vs. 0.0485; F-Score 76.17 vs. 73.12). On the harder 11-object subset where TRELLIS originally fails, DRO reaches 95.5% stability with 100% output rate, compared to only 54.5% for base TRELLIS. The DPO variant also improves stability to 95.1% but with less geometric improvement (F-Score 73.62). All DSO results are obtained in a single feed-forward pass with no test-time optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSO (DRO) decisively outperforms all three baselines on physical stability: 99.0% vs. 85.1% (TRELLIS), 69.4% (Atlas3D), and 80.3% (PhysComp) on the full set. Atlas3D, which uses differentiable simulation for test-time optimization, actually produces *less* stable objects than the unmodified TRELLIS (69.4% vs. 85.1%) and has a lower output rate (95.4%), likely due to numerical instability in differentiable ODE solving. PhysComp, which refines TRELLIS outputs via test-time optimization, achieves 80.3% stability on the 11-object subset but at the cost of a 46.2% output rate (failing to produce a mesh in over half of runs) and severely degraded geometry (F-Score 53.73 vs. 72.48 for TRELLIS). DSO matches or exceeds all baselines on geometry metrics as well, with DRO improving F-Score from 73.12 to 76.17 and CD from 0.0485 to 0.0440, suggesting that the reward-based alignment does not sacrifice geometric fidelity. Crucially, DSO operates entirely in a single feed-forward pass (~seconds), whereas Atlas3D and PhysComp require minutes of per-object test-time optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on TRELLIS, a rectified-flow-based image-to-3D generator, and adapts the Diffusion-DPO alignment framework (Wallace et al., CVPR 2024) to the 3D domain. It departs from prior physically-sound 3D generation methods (Atlas3D, PhysComp) that rely on slow, numerically fragile differentiable physics simulators at test time, instead framing physical soundness as a reward-based alignment problem solvable at training time with a non-differentiable simulator (MuJoCo). The key methodological departure is the introduction of Direct Reward Optimization (DRO), a novel loss that aligns diffusion models with a scalar reward without requiring pairwise preference data or querying the reference model during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DSO converts a slow, unstable test-time optimization problem into a fast feed-forward inference, making physically stable 3D generation practical for fabrication and simulation pipelines. The DRO objective, which outperforms DPO in this setting, is potentially transferable to other diffusion alignment tasks where pairwise preferences are hard to obtain. The self-improving pipeline—where the model generates its own training data and a simulator labels it—removes the bottleneck of collecting large 3D datasets with ground-truth stability annotations. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other physical properties (articulation, scene decomposition), whether DRO's advantage over DPO holds across architectures and tasks, and whether the modest data requirement (1/16 of the full set suffices) extends to rarer physical attributes.
