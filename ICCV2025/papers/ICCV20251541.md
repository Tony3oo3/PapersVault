## Problem

Existing controllable generation frameworks handle single conditions effectively, but combining multiple conditions (e.g., a subject reference image, a spatial layout map, and a text prompt simultaneously) remains unsolved. Prior multi-conditional methods like UniControl and UniControlNet are restricted to spatial conditions only, while Ctrl-X supports subject+spatial but with unsatisfactory quality and limited condition combinations. Additionally, no public dataset exists for training or benchmarking multi-conditional generative models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251541/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

UniCombine accepts any arbitrary combination of up to N conditions—text prompts, depth/canny spatial maps, subject reference images, and inpainting masks with background images—concatenated into a unified embedding sequence S = [T; X; C₁; …; Cₙ]. The output is a single 512×512 RGB image that simultaneously satisfies all provided constraints. Unlike fixed-pair approaches, the number and type of conditions are not restricted at inference time, enabling novel combinations such as subject + inpainting mask + depth map + text all at once.

## Method

Built on FLUX.1-schnell (MMDiT + Rectified Flow), UniCombine introduces a Conditional MMDiT Attention (CMMDiT) mechanism that replaces the original MMDiT Attention: when the denoising or text branch serves as query, it attends globally over the full sequence [T; X; C₁; …; Cₙ], while each conditional branch attending as query is restricted to the subsequence [T; X; Cᵢ], preventing cross-condition feature entanglement and reducing attention complexity from O(N²) to O(N). A LoRA Switching module applies one-hot gating to activate the appropriate pre-trained Condition-LoRA (from OminiControl) on the denoising branch for each condition type, enabling a training-free multi-conditional version. For the training-based version, a small trainable Denoising-LoRA (rank 4) is added to the denoising branch and trained for 30k steps on the newly constructed SubjectSpatial200K dataset while all Condition-LoRAs remain frozen, allowing the denoising branch to learn better attention-score distribution across multiple conditions.

## Results

On the Subject-Insertion task, UniCombine (training-based) achieves a FID of 4.55, substantially outperforming AnyDoor (26.07) and ObjectStitch (26.86), while also attaining the highest subject consistency scores (CLIP-I 97.14, DINO 92.96). On Subject-Depth generation, UniCombine (training-based) reduces FID from 52.37 (Ctrl-X) and 29.93 (ControlNet+IP-Adapter) to 6.66, and improves DINO subject consistency from 50.83/62.26 to 90.31. On Multi-Spatial generation, UniCombine (training-based) achieves FID 6.82 and MSE 165.90, compared to UniControl (FID 44.17, MSE 1346.02) and UniControlNet (FID 20.96, MSE 1231.06). The training-free version also outperforms all baselines across every task without any task-specific training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniCombine decisively outperforms all six baselines across every task and metric. On Multi-Spatial generation, UniCombine (TB) reduces FID by 3.0× over UniControlNet (6.82 vs 20.96) and 6.5× over UniControl (6.82 vs 44.17), while cutting MSE by 7.4× and 8.1× respectively. On Subject-Insertion, UniCombine (TB) achieves FID 4.55 versus 26.07 (AnyDoor) and 26.86 (ObjectStitch), with DINO subject consistency of 92.96 vs 86.04 and 82.34. On Subject-Depth, the gap is most dramatic: UniCombine (TB) scores DINO 90.31 versus 50.83 (Ctrl-X) and 62.26 (ControlNet+IP-Adapter), a 30+ point margin over Ctrl-X. On Subject-Canny, UniCombine (TB) achieves DINO 92.59 versus 54.31 (Ctrl-X) and 60.19 (ControlNet+IP-Adapter). Notably, even the training-free version of UniCombine surpasses all baselines on every task, indicating that the Conditional MMDiT Attention mechanism and LoRA Switching alone provide substantial gains without task-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniCombine directly extends OminiControl's single-conditional MMDiT framework by introducing a LoRA Switching module and a Conditional MMDiT Attention mechanism that allow multiple pre-trained Condition-LoRA branches to coexist without cross-condition interference. It departs from prior multi-conditional methods (UniControl, UniControlNet, Cocktail) that are restricted to spatial conditions, and from Ctrl-X which attempts joint structure-appearance control but with limited condition combinations and inferior performance. The architectural foundation is the MMDiT attention from SD3/FLUX with Rectified Flow training, and the paper explicitly positions OminiControl as a special case of its single-conditional setting, making the multi-conditional extension a natural generalization rather than a redesign.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a practical gap by providing the first DiT-based framework that handles arbitrary combinations of text, spatial, and subject conditions in a unified pipeline, with the notable advantage that the training-free version requires only 29M additional parameters versus ~1.7B for stacking ControlNet and IP-Adapter. The SubjectSpatial200K dataset addresses a real absence of public multi-conditional training data. However, evaluation is limited to 512×512 resolution on FLUX.1-schnell alone, and the training-based version still requires task-specific data; open questions include scalability to higher resolutions, more than four conditions, and compatibility with other DiT backbones beyond FLUX.
