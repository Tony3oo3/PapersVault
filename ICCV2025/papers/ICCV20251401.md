## Problem

Conventional visual pipelines require transferring full images from sensor to processor, creating bandwidth bottlenecks, high power consumption, and privacy risks. While Pixel Processor Arrays (PPAs) enable in-pixel computation, prior PPA-based feature methods were limited to very short descriptors constrained by the 22-bit digital memory of a single Processing Element. This paper addresses how to generate, store, and match long binary descriptors (128–256 bits) entirely within the PPA focal plane, so that neither images nor descriptors ever leave the sensor.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a 256×256 grayscale image captured directly into the SCAMP PPA's PE array, along with previously stored keyframe descriptors occupying rows of PEs. Output is reduced to sparse events: 4 events (8 bytes) per keypoint match, encoding the keypoint position, best-match score, and match ID. No full image or raw descriptor data is ever read out; the only off-sensor communication is keypoint coordinates for the row planner and final match events, yielding a >100× reduction in data transfer compared to transmitting a full gray-level image.

## Method

The core contribution is a mechanism to spread binary descriptor storage across multiple PEs horizontally (one bit per PE in a row), enabling 128- and 256-bit BRIEF descriptors despite each PE having only 22 digital registers. Descriptor generation exploits the PPA's SIMD architecture: analogue pixel data is shifted to co-locate comparison pairs within each PE, pairwise intensity differences are computed, and results are binarized—all in parallel across the array. An Off-Sensor Row Planner assigns each detected keypoint a unique PE row, and On-Sensor Descriptor Movement iteratively shifts each generated bit to its correct row and column position. For matching, a query descriptor is replicated across all rows, bitwise compared against stored keyframe descriptors in parallel, and scored using either a digital stacking method (MultiSandCastleSum) or an analogue diffusion method that averages bit-match results across each row. Two post-filtering strategies—Max ratio and Cross check—further validate matches, with the entire pipeline achieving ~210 fps at 128-bit descriptors on SCAMP hardware.

## Results

On the Oxford Affine Covariant Features dataset, the PPA digital matching method achieves recognition rates up to 94.0% (Wall, mild viewpoint change) and 100% (Ubc, compression), closely matching the PC digital baseline (100% on most sequences). On the Oxford Robotic Car re-localization task, the system reaches 99.2% Img2Img and 100.0% Seq2Seq accuracy with 256-bit descriptors and digital scoring. The system operates at ~210 fps (128-bit, analogue scoring) or ~209 fps (64-bit, digital scoring), with a >100× reduction in data transfer compared to transmitting full grayscale images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external descriptor or matching methods; instead, it benchmarks its in-pixel PPA implementation against a standard PC implementation of the same BRIEF+FAST pipeline. The PPA digital method closely tracks the PC digital baseline on most sequences (e.g., Bikes: 98.5% vs 97.8% on 1|2; Ubc: 100% vs 100%), with the largest gaps appearing on the challenging Graf sequence (52.7% vs 55.2% on 1|2) and Wall under large viewpoint changes (81.7% vs 83.7% on 1|4). The PPA analogue summation method consistently underperforms the digital method by 5–20 percentage points due to inherent analogue noise, with the gap widening under illumination change (Leuven: 88.0% vs 95.4% on 1|2). On the Oxford Robotic Car re-localization task, the PPA system achieves 99.2% (Img2Img) and 100.0% (Seq2Seq) with 256-bit digital descriptors, demonstrating that in-pixel matching is viable for practical localisation. The key practical advantage is a >100× reduction in sensor-to-processor data transfer (8 bytes per keypoint vs 65,536 bytes for a full image) and operation at ~210 fps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of PPA-based visual processing (Bose et al. 2017 for visual odometry, Murai et al. 2020 for BIT-VO) by moving from off-sensor descriptor processing to fully in-pixel descriptor storage and matching. It builds on the SandCastleSum algorithm (Bose et al. 2021) for digital register-plane summation, extending it to MultiSandCastleSum for parallel scoring of multiple smaller descriptors. The key departure from prior PPA work is the horizontal spreading of descriptors across multiple PEs to achieve 128–256-bit lengths, and the introduction of an analogue diffusion-based matching summation that exploits SCAMP's unique analogue register capabilities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that full binary descriptor matching can occur without any image or descriptor data leaving the sensor plane has direct implications for privacy-preserving vision in consumer devices (VR/AR headsets, robots) and for bandwidth-constrained embedded systems, achieving >100× data reduction. However, the approach is tightly coupled to the SCAMP PPA architecture, relies on the unstable FAST detector, and shows significant accuracy degradation under large viewpoint changes (Graf sequence drops to ~53%). Open questions include whether improved in-pixel keypoint detectors can close the gap with off-sensor pipelines, and whether the analogue matching path can be extended beyond 128-bit descriptors as hardware matures.
