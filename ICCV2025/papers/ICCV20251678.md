## Problem

While minimal problems for calibrated-camera SfM with point-line arrangements had been fully classified (yielding 30 problems), the analogous uncalibrated (projective) camera case remained unexplored, and prior classification works relied on numerical evidence rather than formal proofs for non-minimality. This paper closes both gaps by providing a complete catalog of all minimal point-line problems for uncalibrated pinhole cameras and introducing a stabilizer-group-based algebraic strategy that yields rigorous certificates of non-minimality.

## Input / Output

The method takes m images (m ≥ 2), each containing p points and l lines with prescribed incidence relations, under the complete-visibility assumption that every point and line appears in every view. Cameras are modeled as full-rank 3×4 projective matrices up to scaling (11 degrees of freedom each), and the output is the set of camera matrices plus 3D point coordinates and Plücker-coordinate line coordinates, determined up to a global PGL₄ transformation. For each minimal problem the paper also reports its algebraic degree (generic number of solutions), which ranges from 1 to 114 across the 291 catalogued problems.

## Method

The classification proceeds in three stages: (1) a dimension-counting argument using the fiber-dimension theorem identifies all *balanced* PLPs where domain and codomain of the joint camera map have equal dimension, reducing the search to a finite list of 434 candidates; (2) minimality is verified by checking that the Jacobian of the joint camera map is invertible at a single point (computed over large finite fields for efficiency); (3) for the 149 balanced PLPs that fail the Jacobian test, a novel stabilizer-group criterion provides formal non-minimality proofs by identifying subarrangements whose stabilizer subgroups overconstrain the remaining camera parameters, violating a necessary dimension inequality. Degrees are then computed via monodromy (numerical homotopy continuation) and cross-verified with symbolic Gröbner bases for problems up to 4 views. The stabilizer framework also yields a practical factorization strategy: minimal problems can be decomposed into smaller (often linear) subproblems solved sequentially, as demonstrated by splitting a degree-6 problem into subproblems of degrees 2 and 3.

## Results

The paper completely classifies all minimal point-line problems (PLPs) for uncalibrated pinhole cameras under complete visibility, finding 291 minimal problems in total. Of these, 73 have degree 1 (unique solution, solvable linearly), and 2 infinite families allow an arbitrary number of views while all others have at most 9 cameras. The degrees of the 285 finite-view problems range from 1 to 114, with 70 of them having degree 1. All minimal problems involve at most 7 points and at most 12 lines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical classification paper with no empirical baselines or benchmark comparisons. The primary comparison is to the calibrated-camera analogue [Duff et al., IEEE TPAMI 2023], which found only 30 minimal problems with at most 6 views. The uncalibrated setting yields 291 minimal problems (nearly 10× more) with smaller degrees overall, which the authors attribute to the linear (projective) structure of the uncalibrated camera space versus the nonlinear calibrated camera space. Additionally, the uncalibrated case admits minimal problems with arbitrarily many views (2 infinite families), whereas the calibrated case is bounded at 6 views. The paper also provides formal non-minimality certificates via stabilizer subgroups, in contrast to prior works [12, 13] that relied on numerical evidence alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct uncalibrated-camera extension of Duff et al. [12] (IEEE TPAMI 2023), which classified all minimal point-line problems for calibrated cameras under complete visibility. The entire methodological pipeline—balanced PLP enumeration via dimension counting, Jacobian-rank minimality checks, and monodromy-based degree computation—is inherited from that work. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of stabilizer-subgroup-based formal proofs of non-minimality, which supersedes the purely numerical evidence used in [12] and [13], and provides a systematic factorization strategy that goes beyond the Galois/monodromy decomposition approach of Duff et al. [11].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The complete catalog of 291 minimal problems (73 of them linear) gives SfM practitioners a definitive reference for selecting the most efficient RANSAC subproblems when line matches are available alongside point matches. The discovery of two infinite families admitting arbitrary numbers of views with unique linear solutions is a structural result with no calibrated analogue, and the stabilizer-based non-minimality criterion offers a reusable formal tool applicable to other algebraic inverse problems. The main limitation is the complete-visibility assumption; classifying minimal problems under partial visibility for uncalibrated cameras remains open and is explicitly deferred to future work.
