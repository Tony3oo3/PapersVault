## Problem

All existing RGB-only category-level pose estimation methods are two-stage pipelines that use separate models and representations for detection and pose estimation, making them vulnerable to error propagation from detection failures and requiring maintenance of two independent networks. This paper asks whether a single unified model with a shared representation can jointly solve both tasks from RGB images alone, without depth or pre-computed bounding boxes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250955/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and camera intrinsics as input and outputs a set of detected objects, each described by a category label, 3D size vector, rotation matrix in SO(3), and 3D translation (a full 9D pose). Unlike two-stage baselines that require a separate detector to provide bounding boxes or segmentation masks, this method produces its own foreground segmentations via cross-attention on decoder features and handles multiple instances of the same category within a single image without any a-priori count or class information.

## Method

The framework uses Neural Mesh Models as 3D object prototypes, where each category has a prototypical mesh with learned vertex features. A DINOv2 ViT backbone with low-rank adapters and a geometric feature decoder produces two 2D feature maps (category-level and instance-level) that are jointly trained with the 3D vertex features via a von Mises-Fisher contrastive loss, establishing dense 2D/3D correspondences. At inference, these correspondences are fed into Progressive-X (a multi-model RANSAC PnP algorithm) to simultaneously detect all object instances and estimate their initial 6D poses. A subsequent 9D refinement step optimizes a per-instance deformation parameter along principal axes by minimizing reprojection error, recovering instance-specific sizes and improving rotation/translation accuracy. Foreground segmentation is obtained emergently from cross-attention between learned foreground tokens and decoder features, supervised with dice loss against rendered prototype masks.

## Results

On REAL275 with scale-agnostic metrics, the unified method achieves NIoU@50 of 53.7%, 5°/0.2d of 25.1%, and 10°/0.5d of 66.1%, outperforming the previous RGB-only state-of-the-art (LaPose) by 22.9% on average across all scale-agnostic metrics. On CAMERA25, the method achieves the highest score on 9 out of 11 metrics, with the two losses (NIoU@25 and 10°) attributed to smaller objects yielding fewer correspondences. In robustness experiments, the single-stage approach degrades by only ~14% under image corruptions, compared to 19–37% for two-stage baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all four RGB-only baselines on every reported scale-agnostic metric on REAL275. Against the closest competitor LaPose [59], gains range from +4.5 points (NIoU@25: 75.2 vs 70.7) to +9.4 points (5°/0.2d: 25.1 vs 15.7), with the largest relative improvement on joint rotation-translation thresholds. DMSR [52] trails by a wider margin (e.g., 53.7 vs 38.4 on NIoU@50), while OLD-Net [15] and MSOS [30] are substantially weaker, particularly on rotation-sensitive metrics. On CAMERA25, the method wins 9 of 11 metrics but is narrowly outperformed by LaPose on NIoU@25 (73.9 vs 74.1) and 10° (80.8 vs 82.7), which the authors attribute to smaller objects in that dataset providing fewer 2D/3D correspondences. In the robustness study (Table 4), the single-stage approach degrades by only ~14% under 8 image corruptions, whereas two-stage baselines lose 19–37% (mean), confirming that detection failures in the first stage are a dominant error source for decoupled pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural mesh model lineage (NEMO, Ma et al.) from single-object, per-category pose estimation to a unified multi-object detection-and-pose framework. The key architectural departure is replacing the dominant two-stage paradigm (off-the-shelf detector → single-object pose estimator) with a shared DINOv2+adapter backbone that jointly learns 2D image features and 3D vertex features via contrastive learning, then uses Progressive-X multi-model RANSAC PnP at inference to simultaneously detect instances and estimate 6D poses. The vMF contrastive loss and geometric feature decoder are inherited from NEMO, while the PEFT adapter strategy and foreground segmentation via cross-attention tokens are novel additions that enable the single-stage formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the detection stage in two-stage pipelines is a dominant error source, and that a single shared representation can outperform specialized per-task models on both accuracy (22.9% over LaPose on REAL275) and robustness to image corruptions (14% vs. 19–37% degradation). This challenges the common assumption that dedicated detectors and pose estimators should be more robust than a unified model. Limitations include weaker absolute-scale performance (no 2D bounding boxes at inference), evaluation on only six object categories, and the reliance on a non-differentiable PnP solver that may limit end-to-end optimization. Open questions include scalability to open-vocabulary or larger category sets, real-time inference speed, and whether the approach transfers to RGB-D or multi-view settings.
