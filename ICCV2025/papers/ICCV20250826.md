## Problem

Few-step distillation of video diffusion models faces a fundamental tradeoff: VSD-based methods (e.g., DMD) achieve high fidelity but suffer mode collapse, while CD-based methods (e.g., LCM) preserve diversity but produce lower-quality, overly smooth outputs. Additionally, distilled students are typically upper-bounded by the teacher, and reward-based fine-tuning in pixel space is prohibitively memory-intensive for video models (requiring >90 GB VRAM for backpropagation through large reward models and decoders).

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt as input and generates a 10-second video (128 frames at 12 FPS) at 192×320 resolution. Generation occurs in a compressed latent space: a pretrained 3D VAE encodes 16-frame chunks into 5 latent embeddings, which are patchified into sequences of length 9600 for the DiT backbone. The distilled student produces the full video in as few as 1, 2, or 4 diffusion steps (versus 50 DDIM steps for the teacher), with CFG weight 7.5 applied during inference.

## Method

DOLLAR combines three objectives in a single training loop: (1) a variational score distillation (VSD) loss that matches the student's sample distribution to the teacher's via KL divergence, providing fidelity; (2) a generalized consistency distillation (CD) loss that enforces self-consistency of the student's predictions across a multi-step teacher denoising trajectory (Denoise^m with m=5), providing diversity; and (3) a latent reward model (LRM) fine-tuning loss that directly optimizes the student via reward gradients in latent space. The LRM is a compact CNN (≈189K–763K parameters) trained to predict pixel-space reward values (e.g., HPSv2, PickScore) from latent representations, bypassing the 632M-parameter pixel-space reward model and the VAE decoder entirely. This eliminates the need for differentiable reward functions and reduces backward-pass VRAM from >90 GB to <30 MB, enabling gradient-based reward optimization for video models that was previously infeasible.

## Results

On VBench (946 long prompts, 5 videos each), DOLLAR with HPSv2 reward achieves a total score of 82.57, surpassing Gen-3 (82.32), Kling (81.85), T2V-Turbo (81.01), and the 50-step DDIM teacher (80.25). The semantic alignment score of 77.51 is the highest among all compared models, indicating superior text-video alignment. The 4-step student also achieves ×15.6 speedup over the teacher's 50-step inference, while 1-step distillation reaches ×278.6 acceleration. In human evaluation, DOLLAR (HPSv2) is preferred over the teacher by 51.1% and over DDPO by 57.3% in general preference, with visual quality rated significantly higher than both teacher (58.4%) and Gen-3 (55.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DOLLAR (HPSv2) outperforms all full-step commercial models on total VBench score: Gen-3 (82.32), Kling (81.85), T2V-Turbo (81.01), and Pika (80.40), while also surpassing its own 50-step teacher (80.25) by 2.32 points. Against distillation baselines, DOLLAR clearly beats DMD/VSD (total 80.08) and LCM/VideoLCM (CD-based, which produces blurry, lower-fidelity samples), with the combined VSD+CD+LRM loss yielding both higher quality (83.83 vs. 80.95 for VSD alone) and higher diversity (Vendi pixel score 1.60 vs. 1.49). In the reward fine-tuning comparison (Table 5), DOLLAR with HPSv2 achieves a total score of 82.57 and reward of 0.277, outperforming VSD+DDPO (81.29, 0.271) and VSD+LRM without CD (81.96, 0.276), confirming that the CD component is critical for maintaining semantic alignment during reward optimization. Notably, DOLLAR loses to Gen-3 on the Quality sub-score (83.83 vs. 84.11), which the authors attribute to Gen-3's lower dynamics degree inflating frame-consistency metrics; DOLLAR compensates with a much higher dynamics degree (81.67 vs. 60.14) and superior semantic alignment. In human evaluation, DOLLAR (HPSv2) is preferred over Gen-3 in visual quality (55.9%) but performs comparably in general preference (45.6%), suggesting the automatic VBench total score slightly favors DOLLAR's motion richness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate distillation paradigms for diffusion models—variational score distillation (VSD, as in DMD) for fidelity and consistency distillation (CD, as in LCM/VideoLCM) for diversity—into a single training objective, replacing the adversarial loss used in DMD with CD plus a novel latent reward model (LRM) fine-tuning term. The LRM departs from both pixel-space reward-gradient methods (ReFL, DRaFT) that require differentiable reward models and backpropagation through large decoders, and policy-gradient methods (DDPO) that lack rich gradient signals, by learning a compact differentiable surrogate in latent space. The multi-step teacher denoising (Denoise^m) generalization of CD is a practical extension of the original consistency model formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LRM approach is practically significant: a 189K-parameter model replaces 632M-parameter pixel-space reward models, reducing backward VRAM from >90 GB to ~18 MB, making reward-based fine-tuning of large video diffusion models feasible on standard hardware. The finding that CD loss provides the signal for the student to exceed teacher quality (beyond VSD's distribution-matching ceiling) challenges the common assumption that distillation is upper-bounded by the teacher. Limitations include evaluation on a single architecture (DiT/Open-Sora variant) at 192×320 resolution and 128 frames, and the paper acknowledges that reward fine-tuning can over-optimize specific metrics at the expense of overall quality. Open questions include whether the LRM approach generalizes to longer/higher-resolution videos, other modalities, and whether the VSD+CD combination scales to even larger teacher models.
