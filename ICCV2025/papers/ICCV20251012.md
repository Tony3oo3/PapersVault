## Problem

Continual Test-Time Adaptation (CTTA) must balance adaptability (updating all parameters to track new domains) against stability (preserving source knowledge to avoid catastrophic forgetting). Full Tuning is flexible but unstable and slow, while Efficient Tuning is stable and fast but under-adapts to large domain shifts. No prior method dynamically decides, per instance, which tuning strategy is appropriate for the current domain shift magnitude.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251012/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequential stream of unlabeled RGB target images (960×544 for ACDC, 1024×1024 for Cityscapes/OnDA; dimensions must be multiples of the 32-pixel mask patch size), processing each image exactly once in an online fashion with no access to source data or ground-truth labels. The output is a per-pixel semantic segmentation mask. Internally, the student encoder receives a randomly masked version of the target image while the teacher encoder receives the unmasked original to generate pseudo-labels, so the input is consumed in two complementary forms simultaneously.

## Method

Hybrid-TTA operates a teacher-student framework where the teacher (EMA of student weights) produces pseudo-labels and the student is updated per instance. Two synergistic modules drive adaptation: (1) Dynamic Domain Shift Detection (DDSD) computes the cross-entropy between the student's prediction and the teacher's pseudo-label, then compares it against a dynamically evolving threshold (itself an EMA of past losses); if the loss exceeds the threshold, Full Tuning updates all parameters, otherwise only lightweight AdaptMLP adapter parameters (0.1% of total) are trained. (2) Masked Image Modeling Adaptation (MIMA) randomly masks the target image, passes it through the student encoder, and trains two decoders in parallel — a segmentation decoder supervised by the teacher pseudo-label and a reconstruction decoder supervised by L1 loss on the masked patches — providing a self-supervised auxiliary signal that stabilizes pseudo-label quality without requiring test-time augmentation. The reconstruction decoder is randomly initialized and requires no pre-training, keeping the additional overhead minimal.

## Results

On the Cityscapes-to-ACDC benchmark (10 rounds), Hybrid-TTA achieves 62.2% mean mIoU, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art SVDP (61.0%) and ViDA (~61.0%) by over 1.2 percentage points, and CoTTA (58.7%) by 3.5%p. On the OnDA benchmark (5 rounds), Hybrid-TTA reaches 66.7% mean mIoU, outperforming CoTTA (65.8%) and SVDP (65.7%). In terms of efficiency, Hybrid-TTA runs at 2.004 FPS, approximately 20× faster than CoTTA (0.100 FPS) and ~34× faster than SVDP (0.058 FPS), while maintaining superior accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hybrid-TTA outperforms all compared CTTA baselines on both benchmarks. On Cityscapes-to-ACDC, it beats SVDP (61.0%) by 1.2%p and CoTTA (58.7%) by 3.5%p in mean mIoU, while being ~20–34× faster in FPS. On OnDA, it leads CoTTA (65.8%) and SVDP (65.7%) by ~1%p, with notably better performance in the hardest domain (200mm rain: 64.3% vs. 62.1% for SVDP). Against the lightweight TENT-continual (52.5%), Hybrid-TTA gains 9.7%p, confirming that limited adaptability of pure ET methods is a major bottleneck. Compared to HAMLET's Adaptive Domain Detection (ADD) on OnDA, DDSD achieves 66.7% vs. 64.7% mIoU, demonstrating that dynamic loss thresholding outperforms fixed-threshold domain shift detection. The only method exceeding Hybrid-TTA is its own augmented variant Hybrid-TTA++ (63.4%), which adds test-time augmentation at a 15× FPS cost (0.136 FPS).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three CTTA research threads: (1) full-parameter adaptation methods like CoTTA and SVDP that update all weights with regularization or TTA to prevent catastrophic forgetting, (2) parameter-efficient methods like ViDA and VDP that update only a small subset (adapters, prompts) to preserve source knowledge, and (3) masked image modeling for adaptation, building on MIC's UDA formulation but re-purposing it for the single-pass, source-free CTTA setting. The key conceptual departure is treating the FT-vs-ET choice not as a fixed design decision but as a per-instance dynamic decision governed by a temporal-correlation-based domain shift detector (DDSD), which contrasts with prior methods that commit to one strategy or use fixed schedules. The MIMA component extends MIC by adding a reconstruction loss alongside segmentation, adapting the UDA MIM recipe to the constraints of online, single-image CTTA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~20× FPS improvement over the fastest competing CTTA methods (SVDP, CoTTA) while matching or exceeding their accuracy is practically significant for real-time deployment in autonomous driving, where per-frame latency is critical. The dynamic switching mechanism offers a principled way to navigate the plasticity-stability trade-off that has been addressed ad hoc in prior work (fixed regularization, fixed adapter subsets, fixed TTA schedules). Limitations include evaluation on a single architecture (SegFormer MiT-B5), a single primary task (semantic segmentation), and cyclic domain sequences that may not reflect truly arbitrary real-world domain ordering; whether the dynamic thresholding generalizes to non-cyclic or highly irregular domain sequences remains open. The randomly initialized reconstruction decoder also raises questions about whether a pre-trained MIM decoder would further improve stability.
