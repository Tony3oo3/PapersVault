## Problem

Developing 3D-capable Large Multimodal Models is bottlenecked by the scarcity of large-scale 3D vision-language datasets and the absence of powerful pre-trained 3D point cloud encoders analogous to CLIP in 2D. Existing 3D LMMs either rely on complex pipelines with off-the-shelf 3D segmentors or manually crafted 2D segmentation to construct scene features, making them computationally intensive and difficult to scale. The paper asks whether one can directly extend a well-trained 2D LMM to 3D scene understanding with minimal architectural changes, bypassing these obstacles entirely.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view egocentric RGB images along with known camera intrinsics/extrinsics and nearest-neighbor depth maps to compute per-patch 3D world coordinates; it also accepts natural language instructions and, for object-centric tasks, explicit 3D coordinate tokens. Outputs include free-form natural language (QA answers, dense object captions with spatial relations) and, for grounding tasks, precise 3D bounding boxes decoded from a learned location token via a dedicated grounding head rather than emitted as text. A distinctive user-facing feature is that users can click on individual 2D image views to trigger 3D captioning or grounding for the corresponding object.

## Method

LLaVA-3D extends LLaVA-Video by injecting 3D spatial context into 2D CLIP patches: per-patch 3D world coordinates (recovered from depth and camera parameters) are encoded through a learnable two-layer MLP into 3D position embeddings, which are added element-wise to the projected 2D patch tokens to form '3D patches.' When token counts exceed the LLM context window, a parameter-free 3D-aware pooling stage (voxelization or Farthest Point Sampling) compresses patches while preserving scene structure. For 3D grounding, a lightweight decoder uses FPS-sampled instance queries that perform multi-scale 3D k-NN cross-attention over the 3D patches followed by distance-adaptive self-attention with a location token, then a two-layer MLP predicts 3D bounding boxes—eliminating the need for off-the-shelf 3D segmentors. Training is two-stage: joint 2D+3D instruction tuning (on LLaVA-Video data plus the curated LLaVA-3D-Instruct-860K collection) followed by decoder-only fine-tuning on 3D grounding data, which yields 3.5× faster convergence than prior 3D LMMs while preserving 2D video understanding at near-parity with the base model.

## Results

On ScanQA, LLaVA-3D achieves CIDEr 103.1, BLEU-4 16.4, METEOR 20.8, ROUGE 49.6, and EM@1 30.6, surpassing the best prior 3D LMM (LEO: CIDEr 101.4, EM@1 24.5) and all task-specific methods. On SQA3D, LLaVA-3D reaches 60.1 EM@1, outperforming Chat-3D v2 (54.7) and ChatScene (54.6). On MMScan QA (GPT-4 evaluation), LLaVA-3D scores 55.4 overall as a generalist model, exceeding LEO fine-tuned on the full dataset (47.8) and LL3DA fine-tuned (38.5). On 3D dense captioning (Scan2Cap), LLaVA-3D achieves CIDEr@0.5 of 84.1 vs. ChatScene's 77.2, and on MMScan Captioning it scores 78.8 overall vs. LEO's 51.3. On 2D video benchmarks, LLaVA-3D retains near-identical performance to LLaVA-Video (MVBench 58.1 vs. 58.6; VideoMME 62.8 vs. 63.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLaVA-3D outperforms all 24 compared baselines across every benchmark. Against task-specific 3D QA models (ScanQA [3], 3D-VisTA [54], ClipBERT [25]), LLaVA-3D leads by 9.5 EM@1 on ScanQA (30.6 vs. 22.4) and 11.6 EM@1 on SQA3D (60.1 vs. 48.5). Among 3D LMMs, LLaVA-3D surpasses LEO [18] by 5.6 EM@1 on ScanQA (30.6 vs. 24.5) and 10.1 EM@1 on SQA3D (60.1 vs. 50.0), and beats ChatScene [17] by 9.0 EM@1 on SQA3D (60.1 vs. 54.6). On MMScan QA, LLaVA-3D as a generalist (55.4) exceeds LEO fine-tuned on the full dataset (47.8) by 7.6 points, demonstrating superior training efficiency. For 3D dense captioning on Scan2Cap, LLaVA-3D (CIDEr@0.5 84.1) leads ChatScene [17] (77.2) and LEO [18] (68.4) by 6.9 and 15.7 points respectively. On 3D visual grounding, the two-stage LLaVA-3D† achieves 63.9 Acc@0.25 on ScanRefer and 68.1 on Multi3DRefer, surpassing Chat-Scene [17] (55.5 / 57.1) and all task-specific methods (best: ReGround3D [53] at 53.1 on ScanRefer). Notably, LLaVA-3D also outperforms zero-shot 2D LMMs including GPT-4V (CIDEr 59.6) and Gemini (68.3) on ScanQA, while maintaining 2D video understanding nearly identical to its LLaVA-Video base (MVBench 58.1 vs. 58.6).

## Relation to Prior Work

LLaVA-3D extends the LLaVA-Video 2D LMM by injecting 3D spatial context into existing CLIP patch features via learnable 3D position embeddings, a design directly inspired by ODIN's unified 2D/3D segmentation framework. It departs from prior 3D LMMs (3D-LLM, LL3DA, LEO, Chat-Scene) that rely on point-cloud encoders or off-the-shelf 3D instance segmentors to construct scene representations, instead operating purely on multi-view RGB images with camera parameters. The grounding decoder builds on the authors' own prior work on 3D visual grounding with reasoning, adapting its location-token and k-NN cross-attention mechanism to decode 3D bounding boxes directly from 3D patches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: by eliminating the need for 3D point clouds, 3D encoders, and offline 3D segmentation, LLaVA-3D offers a drop-in recipe for equipping any 2D LMM with 3D capabilities at a fraction of the training cost (3.5× faster convergence). The observation that zero-shot LLaVA-Video already performs competitively on ScanQA/SQA3D raises questions about whether current 3D QA benchmarks truly measure spatial understanding. Open questions include scalability to larger or unstructured scenes, real-time deployment for embodied agents, and whether the approach generalizes beyond indoor ScanNet-style environments to outdoor or dynamic 3D settings.
