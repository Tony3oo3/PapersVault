## Problem

As embodied navigation systems grow more complex, they become opaque black-boxes, yet prior work shows a persistent trade-off where adding explainability degrades navigation performance. A key obstacle is that reinforcement learning lacks ground-truth language descriptions of actions, making it difficult to incorporate action verbalization as a training signal the way imitation learning can use human-written instructions. This paper asks whether action description can be integrated into RL navigation as an auxiliary task that simultaneously improves performance and provides human-interpretable explanations.

## Input / Output

The RL agent consumes RGBD images (128×128×7, including semantic segmentation channels), auditory observations, agent posture, and the previous action at each timestep; in VLN it additionally receives a natural-language instruction and operates on a graph of panoramic observations. The ADGenerator additionally receives a sequence of past (or future) visual observations and one-hot action vectors to produce a free-form word sequence. The model outputs a discrete navigation action (MoveForward, TurnLeft, TurnRight, Stop) alongside a natural-language action description generated autoregressively by a transformer decoder, with task embeddings distinguishing the policy head from the description head.

## Method

DescRL is a two-phase framework. In Phase 1, an ADGenerator (a CNN+Transformer encoder-decoder, or a pre-trained VLM such as VideoLLaMA2 or Qwen2.5-VL) is trained to verbalize navigation observations into action descriptions, either from the human-annotated R2R dataset or via knowledge distillation from a VLM to eliminate reliance on human data. In Phase 2, an ADPredictor module is attached to the navigation policy (SAVi, KSAVEN, DUET, ScaleVLN, or SMT) and shares transformer encoder and decoder layers with the policy network; task embeddings route inputs to either the action-decision or description-prediction head. The ADPredictor is trained with cross-entropy loss against the ADGenerator's output (teacher-forcing), added to the RL or IL loss with a weighting coefficient λ. Three description types—past, future, and past-and-future—are supported by feeding different observation windows to the ADGenerator at train time, while at test time the ADPredictor only accesses past observations. The shared encoder/decoder architecture is the key mechanism: it forces the navigation policy to encode semantically rich representations that support both action selection and language generation, turning the description task into a regularizer that improves rather than harms navigation performance.

## Results

On the most challenging task, Semantic Audio-Visual Navigation (SAVNav), SAVi with Past-DescRL achieves state-of-the-art performance across all metrics: SR 37.4, SPL 32.4, SNA 28.0, DTG 8.4, and SWS 19.1 in the Heard setting, and SR 31.4, SPL 26.9, SNA 22.5, DTG 8.7, SWS 15.1 in the Unheard setting. On Object-Goal Navigation, SMT with Past-DescRL (with pre-training) improves SR from 17.9 to 26.7 and SPL from 7.7 to 9.7 over the SMT baseline. On Vision-and-Language Navigation, DUET with Past-DescRL slightly improves SR from 79.73 to 81.00 and SPL from 74.78 to 76.27 (Val Seen), while ScaleVLN with Past-DescRL maintains comparable performance (SR 82.66 vs. 82.57).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On SAVNav, SAVi with P-DescRL outperforms all five baselines on every metric in both Heard and Unheard settings, achieving the largest gains over SAVi (SR +5.8, SPL +3.9, SWS +6.6 in Heard) and KSAVEN (SR +12.3, SPL +14.3 in Heard). On ObjNav, SMT with Past-DescRL (with pre-training) substantially improves over the SMT baseline (SR 26.7 vs. 17.9, SPL 9.7 vs. 7.7) and the simpler GRU-based method (SR 26.7 vs. 7.4). On VLN, DUET with Past-DescRL slightly outperforms DUET on all three metrics (SR 81.00 vs. 79.73, SPL 76.27 vs. 74.78, NE 2.12 vs. 2.26), while ScaleVLN with Past-DescRL matches or marginally improves over ScaleVLN (SR 82.66 vs. 82.57, though SPL drops slightly from 77.09 to 76.46). The Seq2Seq baseline is far behind all transformer-based methods (SR 37.45 vs. 81.00 for DUET w/ Past-DescRL). Notably, the method never degrades the main navigation task performance, and the gains are most pronounced on the hardest task (SAVNav), where the agent must navigate after the sound source stops, making the auxiliary description task particularly beneficial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the explainable RL (XRL) literature, which has traditionally treated explainability as a separate concern that trades off against main-task performance, by integrating action description generation directly into the policy network as an auxiliary task. It departs from prior VLN instruction-prediction work (Zhu et al. [80], Hejna et al. [32]) that relied solely on imitation learning with ground-truth trajectory-instruction pairs, by using knowledge distillation from pre-trained VLMs (VideoLLaMA2, Qwen2.5-VL) to synthesize supervision signals compatible with RL. The method is applied as a modular addition to existing navigation architectures (SAVi, KSAVEN, DUET, ScaleVLN, SMT), making it architecture-agnostic rather than proposing a new end-to-end model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the long-standing explainability-performance trade-off in RL navigation can be broken: adding an action-description auxiliary task simultaneously improves navigation metrics and produces human-interpretable outputs, with the largest gains on the particularly challenging SAVNav task where sound stops mid-episode. The knowledge-distillation route from VLMs removes dependence on expensive human-annotated data, making the approach more scalable. Open questions include whether the method generalizes to non-navigation RL tasks, how it behaves when the robot morphology differs from the VLM's training assumptions, and whether the domain gap between VLN training data and other navigation tasks (evidenced by stopping-precision failures) can be closed with task-specific VLM prompts.
