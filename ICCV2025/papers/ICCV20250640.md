## Problem

Existing deepfake video detectors learn manipulation-specific artifacts (boundary inconsistencies, blinking anomalies, texture irregularities) that perform well on known forgery types but degrade severely against unseen manipulation techniques. This poor cross-domain generalization stems from a constrained decision boundary in feature space where known forgeries occupy only a localized region, while novel manipulations introduce artifacts in arbitrary, previously unobserved locations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250640/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes face-cropped video clips as input: four temporal segments of 12 consecutive frames each, with faces detected via RetinaFace and standardized to 224×224 pixels. Internally, the model performs a triplet classification (Real / Fake / Outlier) using a CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone with ST-Adapter layers, but at inference time the Fake and Outlier predictions are merged into a single binary real/fake decision, so the final output is a standard binary classification score.

## Method

FakeRadar introduces two coupled components. First, Forgery Outlier Probing (FOP) performs dynamic subcluster modeling on CLIP features using GMMs with split/merge proposals (inspired by DeepDPM) to partition real and fake samples into fine-grained subclusters, then generates cluster-conditional outlier samples near subcluster boundaries via ε-likelihood-region sampling to simulate unseen forgeries. Second, Outlier-Guided Tri-Training (OGTT) optimizes a triplet-class classifier (Real/Fake/Outlier) with two losses: an InfoNCE-style outlier-driven contrastive loss that maximizes separation between samples, subcluster centers, and generated outliers, and an outlier-conditioned cross-entropy loss that enforces clear three-way decision boundaries. The backbone is a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 with lightweight ST-Adapter layers (384-dim bottleneck, 3D convolutions) for parameter-efficient spatio-temporal fine-tuning, and during inference the Outlier class is simply relabeled as Fake.

## Results

On cross-dataset evaluation (trained on FF++ HQ, tested on four external datasets), FakeRadar achieves video-level AUC of 91.7% on Celeb-DF v2, 88.5% on DFDC Preview, 84.1% on DFDC, and 96.2% on DFD, setting new state-of-the-art on all four benchmarks. On the in-domain FF++ test set, it reaches 99.1% AUC, comparable to the best baselines (TALL: 99.9%). In cross-manipulation evaluation on FF++, FakeRadar achieves the best average AUC across all four training configurations (82.23–86.75%), with the largest margin (7.41% over DCL) when trained only on Face2Face manipulations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FakeRadar outperforms all 16 baselines on the three most challenging cross-dataset benchmarks: it leads on DFDC by 3.6% over UCF (84.1 vs. 80.5) and 3.7% over LTTD (84.1 vs. 80.4), on DFDCP by 2.2% over SeeABLE (88.5 vs. 86.3), and on Celeb-DF v2 by 0.6% over LSDA (91.7 vs. 91.1). On DFD, it edges out StyleFlow by 0.1% (96.2 vs. 96.1) and LSDA by 0.6%. On the in-domain FF++ test set, FakeRadar (99.1%) trails TALL (99.9%) and FTCN/NACO (99.8%) by less than 1%, indicating no meaningful in-domain cost for the generalization gains. In cross-manipulation evaluation (Table 2), FakeRadar achieves the best average AUC in all four training configurations, with the largest advantage (7.41% over DCL) when trained on Face2Face, suggesting the outlier-probing mechanism is most beneficial when the training manipulation is atypical. Frame-level methods (Xception, Face X-Ray, PCL+I2G) are consistently 5–20 points below video-level methods on cross-dataset tests, underscoring the importance of temporal modeling for generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

FakeRadar builds on two distinct lines of work: (1) DeepDPM's dynamic clustering with unknown cluster counts via split/merge proposals (Ronen et al., CVPR 2022), which is adapted here for category-specific subcluster modeling of real and fake video features, and (2) virtual outlier synthesis near cluster boundaries (Du et al., ICLR 2022), repurposed to generate 'forgery outliers' that simulate unseen manipulation patterns. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant paradigm in deepfake detection—passively learning manipulation-specific artifacts (frequency anomalies, boundary inconsistencies, blink patterns)—by instead proactively probing the CLIP feature space for distributional gaps, framing detection as anomaly discovery rather than pattern matching. The triplet-class (Real/Fake/Outlier) training objective with outlier-driven contrastive and cross-entropy losses is a novel combination not seen in prior deepfake detectors, which typically perform binary classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cross-domain generalization remains the central unsolved challenge in deepfake detection, and FakeRadar's consistent improvements across four external datasets (up to 3.7% AUC over prior best on DFDC) and particularly strong cross-manipulation results (7.4% over second-best in F2F-trained setting) suggest that feature-space outlier probing is a viable strategy for handling the open-ended nature of future manipulation techniques. However, the approach is limited to face-centric deepfakes and relies on CLIP's pre-trained feature space, raising open questions about whether the GMM-based subcluster modeling scales to truly novel generative paradigms (e.g., diffusion-based face generation) or non-face manipulations. The method also introduces additional training complexity (clustering network, outlier cache, triplet classifier) that may complicate deployment, and the reliance on a frozen backbone with lightweight adapters constrains the model's capacity to adapt to fundamentally different artifact types.
