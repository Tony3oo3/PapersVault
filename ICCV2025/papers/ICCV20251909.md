## Problem

Existing animation colorization methods are limited to short-term sequences (≤100 frames) and rely on local paradigms that fuse overlapping features between adjacent segments, neglecting global color relationships. This causes progressive color drift in long animations (e.g., a character's hat changing color by frame 100–300), making automated colorization of real production shots (300–1000 frames) impractical.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251909/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference key-frame image (which need not be the first frame of the sequence), a sequence of binarized line-art sketch frames (thresholded at 200), and a text prompt as input, and produces a colorized animation video at 1024×576 resolution. Generation proceeds autoregressively in 81-frame segments (CogVideoX-1.5-5B capacity), with the reference image randomly sampled from historical keyframes during training to enhance robustness. The system targets sequences averaging ~500 frames, roughly 5× longer than prior methods.

## Method

LongAnimation builds on CogVideoX-1.5-5B and introduces three components. SketchDiT is a 6-layer lightweight DiT that extracts hybrid features from the reference image, sketches, and text, injected into even-numbered layers of the frozen base model via skip-layer control. The Dynamic Global-Local Memory (DGLM) module uses the Video-XL long-video-understanding model to dynamically compress all historical frames: CLIP-based change estimation selects variable compression granularities (2/4/8 frames), and middle-layer KV-cache tokens of the MLLM serve as compressed global and local memory, which are adaptively fused with current SketchDiT features through M cross-attention layers (query = current features, key/value = compressed history). A Color Consistency Reward, optimized via non-gradient reinforcement learning, aligns the generated video's KV-cache features with those of the reference video to refine low-frequency color details. During inference, a late-stage-only latent fusion (t_st=20) smooths segment boundaries without disrupting brightness and fine-grained details.

## Results

On the Sakuga-42M test set, LongAnimation achieves FVD of 187.48 (short-term, 14 frames) and 240.57 (long-term, avg. 500 frames), improving FVD by 35.1% and 49.1% over the best prior method (LVCD*) respectively. Frame similarity (LPIPS) improves by 57.1% (short-term) and 58.0% (long-term), while FID drops to 37.80 and 40.75, the best across all methods. The long-term gains are substantially larger than short-term gains, confirming the effectiveness of the Dynamic Global-Local Memory mechanism for extended sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LongAnimation outperforms all three baselines (ToonCrafter, LVCD, AniDoc) and the stronger LVCD* variant on every metric in both short-term and long-term settings. Against the closest competitor LVCD* (FVD 288.70 short / 473.02 long), LongAnimation reduces FVD to 187.48 and 240.57 respectively, a 35.1% and 49.1% improvement. AniDoc is the strongest baseline on LPIPS among the original three (0.142 short / 0.169 long), yet LongAnimation still halves that gap to 0.054 and 0.068. ToonCrafter and LVCD lag substantially on both frame similarity and video quality, with ToonCrafter's FVD exceeding 560 (short) and 750 (long). The margin of improvement is notably larger in the long-term setting (49.1% FVD gain vs. 35.1% short-term), indicating that the Dynamic Global-Local Memory mechanism provides the most benefit as sequence length increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends reference-based line art video colorization from the UNet-based short-term paradigm (LVCD, ToonCrafter, AniDoc, all limited to ≤100 frames with local segment fusion) to a DiT-based long-term setting by introducing a dynamic global-local memory mechanism. It repurposes Video-XL, a long video understanding model originally designed for hour-scale video comprehension, as a dynamic feature compressor that extracts color-consistent global features from historical generated segments. The SketchDiT module provides a lightweight control interface for CogVideoX, and the Color Consistency Reward adapts non-gradient RL from image generation to refine animation coloring, collectively forming a pipeline that no prior animation colorization method attempted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Long animation colorization (300–1000 frames) is a genuine bottleneck in the animation industry, and this paper demonstrates a 5× extension in stable generation length with substantial quality gains, making automated long-sequence coloring practically viable. The dynamic global-local paradigm—using a video understanding model to adaptively retrieve relevant historical color features—is a generalizable concept that could transfer to other long controllable video generation tasks beyond colorization. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-animation domains, whether the reliance on Video-XL's KV cache scales to even longer sequences, and whether the DiT-vs-UNet architecture gap inflates the reported improvements over prior methods.
