## Problem

Tracking tiny drones (≤20×20 pixels) in infrared videos against highly complex backgrounds remains unsolved: existing anti-UAV datasets feature predominantly large targets on simple scenes, and current Siamese trackers rely almost exclusively on spatial template matching, causing them to lose targets when they blend into cluttered backgrounds or undergo pose changes over long sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251364/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes infrared video frames at 640×480 or 640×512 resolution (25 FPS) captured at 200–2000 m range, where targets span only 5×5 to 30×30 pixels (average 28×16). It takes an initial target template and a dynamically updated template as ROI feature maps (7×7×256 via ROI Align on a ResNet-50 backbone), and outputs a single bounding box (x₁, y₁, x₂, y₂) with a confidence score per frame. The dynamic template is refreshed only when consecutive-frame confidence ≥ 0.95 and center displacement ≤ 20 pixels, preventing error accumulation.

## Method

MCATrack integrates two modules. First, a Bio-inspired Magno-motion Enhancement module implements a magnocellular-pathway temporal high-pass filter (a first-order difference equation with Z-transform transfer function, τ=2) to amplify the local signal-to-noise ratio of tiny moving targets while suppressing static background clutter; the resulting motion response is combined with the original frame. Second, a Dynamic Cross-Guided (DCG) module performs global search in three stages: (1) Dynamic Target Cross-Guidance fuses the initial template with the updated dynamic template via cross-attention to produce enhanced target features, which then drive a Siamese-branching RPN to generate candidate proposals; (2) Enhanced Feature Confidence Generation refines each proposal through a Hadamard-product correlation with the enhanced target features, followed by standard RCNN classification and regression; (3) the highest-confidence box is selected. Training uses binary cross-entropy and smooth-L1 losses for both the RPN and RCNN heads, with IoU thresholds of 0.7/0.3 for positive/negative samples.

## Results

On the newly introduced TDTIV dataset, MCATrack achieves a State Accuracy (SA) of 54.97, surpassing the previous best tracker SiamDT (49.89) by 5.08 points and GlobalTrack (46.98) by 7.99 points. On the established Anti-UAV410 benchmark, MCATrack scores 69.18 SA, edging out SiamDT (68.19) by 0.99 points and DropTrack (60.15) by 9.03 points. The paper also reports absolute gains of 6.7% in precision and 5.1% in success rate over SiamDT on TDTIV, and 3% in precision and 1% in success rate on Anti-UAV410.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCATrack outperforms all 20 competing trackers on both TDTIV and Anti-UAV410. On TDTIV, the margin over the strongest baseline SiamDT is 5.08 SA points (54.97 vs. 49.89), and the gap widens to over 10 points against transformer-based trackers like OStrack-384 (44.62) and DropTrack (42.70). On Anti-UAV410, the advantage over SiamDT narrows to 0.99 SA points (69.18 vs. 68.19), while the lead over GlobalTrack (66.45) and DropTrack (60.15) remains substantial at 2.73 and 9.03 points respectively. The ablation study attributes 7.13 SA points of the TDTIV gain to the Magno-motion module and an additional 0.62 to the Feature Enhancement (Dynamic Cross-Guided) module, indicating that motion-based contrast enhancement is the dominant contributor. Notably, the larger performance gap on TDTIV (the authors' own, harder dataset with smaller targets averaging 28×16 px) compared to Anti-UAV410 (39×24 px) suggests the motion-centric design is particularly effective for extremely small targets in cluttered infrared scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

MCATrack extends the Siamese R-CNN [38] architecture by replacing the static initial-template-only guidance with a Dynamic Cross-Guided module that fuses updated target features via cross-attention, addressing pose drift in long-term tracking. The Bio-inspired Magno-motion Enhancement module draws on the magnocellular pathway's temporal high-pass filtering (formalized as a simple recursive difference equation) to amplify target-background contrast, departing from prior drone trackers (SiamDT, DFSC, SiamFusion) that rely predominantly on spatial template matching. The TDTIV dataset extends the Anti-UAV410 benchmark by pushing target sizes smaller (28×16 px average vs. 39×24) and backgrounds more complex, filling a gap the authors identify in existing anti-UAV datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TDTIV benchmark (290k frames, 280k annotations) provides the anti-UAV community with a substantially harder evaluation setting where most prior trackers degrade significantly, making it a useful stress test for future methods. The motion-centric design addresses a practical failure mode—targets blending into cluttered backgrounds—where purely appearance-based Siamese trackers lose the target, and the 5+ point SA gain over SiamDT on TDTIV validates this. Open questions include whether the bio-inspired motion filter generalizes beyond infrared to visible-light or multimodal settings, whether the approach scales to sub-10-pixel targets, and whether the dynamic template update strategy remains stable under prolonged occlusion or rapid 3D pose changes.
