## Problem

Existing anti-noise 3DGS reconstruction methods rely on fixed metric thresholds to separate noisy labels from clean data, but these thresholds fail to generalize across scenes with varying noise proportions, types, and observation completeness. Areas with fewer camera observations exhibit higher reconstruction errors even without actual noise, causing naive classifiers to misattribute observation insufficiency as label noise.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251998/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images with known camera poses (from RobustNeRF and On-the-go datasets) and produces a clean 3D Gaussian Splatting scene alongside auxiliary maps: a per-pixel clean background mask, an observation completeness map, and a per-pixel uncertainty map. Feature extraction uses frozen SAM and DINOv2 networks whose outputs are concatenated with positional and image-ID encodings before being fed to two lightweight MLPs, so the heavy semantic backbone is never updated during training.

## Method

OCSplats introduces an Observation Completeness (OC) metric computed iteratively per Gaussian primitive by tracking the variance of observing camera positions and the count of effective observations (gradient > 1e-7), then renders OC via alpha blending analogous to color. A hybrid noise assessment combining reconstruction residual and learned uncertainty is corrected by an Observation Correction Ratio (OCR) that suppresses errors in under-observed regions. Two MLPs—one predicting uncertainty β (with gradient detached from Gaussian parameters to prevent 3DGS collapse) and one predicting the clean mask—are trained self-supervised using labels generated by a dynamic anchor threshold that interpolates between Otsu's maximum inter-class variance and the background noise mean, eliminating the need for per-scene threshold tuning. An Observation Completeness Pruning (OCP) step removes Gaussians with OC < 0.03 and fewer than 3 observations, reducing primitive count by ~10% while stabilizing reconstruction.

## Results

On the RobustNeRF dataset, OCSplats achieves 25.17 PSNR / 0.843 SSIM / 0.071 LPIPS on Android, 35.21 / 0.958 / 0.068 on Crab, 35.37 / 0.961 / 0.069 on Yoda, and 23.13 / 0.863 / 0.101 on Statue, outperforming the closest 3DGS-based competitor SpotLessSplats by 0.34–0.89 dB PSNR across all four scenes. On the more challenging On-the-go dataset (6 scenes spanning low to high occlusion), OCSplats leads in every scene, with the largest gains in high-occlusion settings: 26.32 PSNR on Spot (vs. 24.15 for SpotLessSplats) and 23.61 on Patio high (vs. 22.27). The method also reduces LPIPS to 0.068–0.101 on RobustNeRF, the lowest among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OCSplats outperforms all eight baselines across both datasets. Against the closest 3DGS-based competitor SpotLessSplats, OCSplats gains 0.34–2.17 dB PSNR, with the largest margin on high-occlusion scenes (Spot: +2.17 dB, Patio high: +1.34 dB), demonstrating the advantage of dynamic thresholding over fixed thresholds. Compared to the vanilla 3DGS baseline, OCSplats improves by 0.47–7.87 dB PSNR, with the most dramatic gains on occluded scenes (Patio: +5.32 dB, Patio high: +7.87 dB), where 3DGS suffers severe degradation. Among NeRF-based methods, OCSplats surpasses NeRF On-the-go by 1.30–2.21 dB and RobustNeRF by 3.91–7.87 dB, confirming that the 3DGS representation combined with observation completeness correction is more effective than NeRF-based uncertainty losses. Wild-GS, the other 3DGS-based anti-noise method, is outperformed by 0.53–2.39 dB, with OCSplats particularly stronger in high-occlusion settings where Wild-GS's fixed-threshold approach struggles. On the lower-occlusion RobustNeRF scenes (Android, Crab, Yoda), the margins over SpotLessSplats are smaller (0.34–0.78 dB), consistent with the paper's observation that the method's advantages are most pronounced in complex, heavily occluded scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the anti-noise 3DGS reconstruction line initiated by SpotLessSplats and WildGaussians, which both rely on fixed-threshold metric classification to separate noisy labels. It departs from these methods in two key ways: (1) introducing observation completeness (OC) as an epistemic-uncertainty-inspired correction that reweights noise assessment in under-observed regions, drawing on triangulation principles and building on NeRF uncertainty work (Bayes' Rays, Shen et al.) but adapted to 3DGS's iterative training; and (2) replacing fixed thresholds with a dynamic anchor scheme (Otsu maximum inter-class variance + background mean) that adapts per-scene without manual tuning. The noise classifier itself is a novel architectural choice, using frozen DINOv2/SAM features fed to lightweight MLPs rather than operating purely on reconstruction residuals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dynamic threshold and OC correction together address a practical pain point: existing anti-noise 3DGS methods require per-scene hyperparameter retuning, limiting real-world deployment. The OC-based pruning (removing ~10% of Gaussians) is a useful side benefit that stabilizes reconstruction. However, the method adds substantial complexity (frozen SAM + DINOv2 feature extraction, two additional MLPs, per-Gaussian OC tracking with iterative variance computation), and evaluation is limited to two relatively small datasets (RobustNeRF, On-the-go) with modest scene counts. Open questions include scalability to large-scale outdoor scenes, applicability to dynamic or multi-object settings, and whether the frozen-feature approach generalizes to domains where DINOv2/SAM features are less informative.
