## Problem

Current text-to-motion frameworks rely on coarse global action descriptions (e.g., 'run'), creating a many-to-many ambiguity between text and motion that prevents fine-grained control over individual body parts. A single term like 'running' encompasses wide variations in speed, arm swing, and direction, while multiple distinct phrases can describe the same motion, making it impossible for existing models to generate or edit specific kinematic components independently.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250605/cropped_page0_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a global action text prompt (e.g., 'a person walks forward in a straight line') and optionally a target-joint trajectory for trajectory control. Internally, a Motion Reasoner (fine-tuned LLaMA-3) expands the global prompt into 6 group-level descriptions (torso, head, left/right arm, left/right leg) and 15 pairwise interaction-level descriptions, which are encoded into hierarchical embeddings. The output is a 3D human motion sequence decoded from discrete VQ-VAE tokens, aligned text-motion embeddings in a shared co-embedding space for retrieval, or an edited motion sequence when specific body-part descriptions are modified.

## Method

KinMo reformulates motion as a linear transformation of standard joint representations into six kinematic groups, each characterized by group position, limb angles, and group velocity, plus pairwise interaction terms (relative position, connecting-joint angles, relative velocity). A semi-supervised annotation pipeline uses PoseScript for per-frame spatial descriptions, sBERT-based keyframe selection for temporal segmentation, and GPT-4o-mini to generate natural-language group and interaction descriptions, enriching HumanML3D into the KinMo dataset. Hierarchical Text-Motion Alignment (HTMA) encodes the three description levels progressively via cross-attention layers (global → group → interaction) with VAE-based ACTOR encoders, trained with InfoNCE contrastive loss at each level. For generation, a coarse-to-fine procedure extends MoMask by sequentially conditioning a shared masked Transformer generator on μc, μg, and μi, where each stage re-feeds intermediate tokens to refine the output, enabling fine-grained body-part generation and editing.

## Results

On HumanML3D text-to-motion generation, KinMo (HTMA) achieves the best R-Precision Top-1 of 0.532, Top-3 of 0.821, lowest FID of 0.039, and lowest MM-Dist of 2.901, outperforming MoMask (0.521/0.807/0.045/2.958) and all other baselines. For text-motion retrieval (protocol 'All'), KinMo with RoBERTa achieves R@1 of 9.05 and MedR of 16.00, substantially surpassing TMR (R@1 5.68, MedR 28.00) and TEMOS (R@1 2.12, MedR 173.0). In the user study, KinMo receives the highest Mean Opinion Scores across Realness, T2M Alignment, and Overall Impression compared to MoMask, MMM, and STMC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KinMo (HTMA) outperforms all 12 generation baselines on R-Precision (Top-1/2/3) and MM-Dist, with the largest margins over diffusion-based methods: MDM (FID 0.544 vs. 0.039) and FG-MDM (FID 0.618 vs. 0.039). Against the closest masked-modeling competitors, KinMo improves over MoMask by +0.011 R-Prec Top-1 and −0.006 FID, and over MMM by +0.028 R-Prec Top-1 and −0.041 FID. ParCo, which decomposes motion into only 2 body parts, achieves a higher MModality (1.382 vs. 1.321) but worse FID (0.109 vs. 0.039) and R-Prec Top-1 (0.515 vs. 0.532), confirming that the 6-group kinematic decomposition is more effective. For retrieval, KinMo (RoBERTa) nearly doubles TMR's R@1 (9.05 vs. 5.68) and reduces MedR from 28 to 16, while TEMOS and the original HumanML3D baseline remain far behind (R@1 2.12 and 1.80 respectively). In the user study, KinMo achieves the highest MOS on all three criteria (Realness, T2M Alignment, Overall Impression) compared to MoMask, MMM, and STMC.

## Relation to Prior Work

KinMo builds directly on MoMask's masked token generation architecture and TMR's contrastive text-motion alignment framework, extending both with a three-level hierarchical description (global action → kinematic group → group interaction) that replaces the flat global-action conditioning used in prior work. Where earlier fine-grained approaches (LGTM, FG-MDM, FineMoGen) rely on LLMs to produce unstructured supplementary text, and ParCo decomposes the body into only two parts, KinMo formalizes a six-group kinematic decomposition (torso, head, left/right arm, left/right leg) and uses cross-attention to progressively integrate descriptions at each level. The annotation pipeline repurposes PoseScript for per-frame spatial descriptions and sBERT for keyframe selection, feeding structured inputs to GPT-4o-mini rather than relying on free-form LLM generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The KinMo dataset and its hierarchical kinematic representation offer a reusable, structured resource for fine-grained motion understanding that goes beyond the global-action bottleneck limiting current text-motion systems. The coarse-to-fine generation procedure and the demonstration that structured kinematic descriptions outperform both unstructured LLM text and coarser (2-part) decompositions provide a clear recipe for improving body-part-level controllability. Open questions include whether the fixed six-group decomposition generalizes to non-humanoid or multi-agent scenarios, whether the LLM-annotation pipeline scales to larger or more diverse motion corpora without quality degradation, and whether the retrieval gains transfer to downstream tasks like motion planning or robotics where real-time inference constraints apply.
