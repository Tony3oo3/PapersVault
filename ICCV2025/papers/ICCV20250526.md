## Problem

Generic event boundaries are inherently subjective, with human annotators disagreeing on where boundaries occur, yet all prior GEBD methods produce a single deterministic prediction per video, ignoring the diversity of plausible solutions. This paper addresses the gap by formulating GEBD as a generative task, enabling a single model to produce multiple diverse yet plausible boundary predictions that better reflect human judgment variability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250526/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video uniformly sampled to 100 RGB frames and produces a binary boundary label sequence of length 100 (1 = boundary present, 0 = absent). Unlike deterministic methods that yield one output, DiffGEBD generates N_P diverse predictions from a single trained model by varying the initial Gaussian noise, with the classifier-free guidance weight w controlling the diversity-fidelity trade-off (higher w → more deterministic, lower w → more diverse). The model is trained on Kinetics-GEBD (5 annotations per video, top-4 selected by F1 consistency) and evaluated on both Kinetics-GEBD and TAPOS.

## Method

DiffGEBD consists of a ResNet-50 backbone extracting per-frame features, a temporal self-similarity encoder (1D conv + sliding-window TSM + FCN + 2D pooling) producing temporal embeddings E, and a 6-layer Transformer decoder that denoises noisy boundary labels conditioned on E via FiLM-injected timestep embeddings. Training uses standard diffusion MSE loss with classifier-free guidance: the conditioning E is randomly dropped with probability p=0.1, jointly training conditional and unconditional prediction paths. At inference, DDIM sampling (32 steps) iteratively denoises from random Gaussian noise, and multiple diverse predictions are obtained by different initial noise seeds. The paper also introduces a diversity-aware evaluation protocol with symmetric F1 (harmonic mean of Pred-to-GT and GT-to-Pred alignment scores) and a pairwise diversity score to properly assess many-to-many prediction-annotation alignment.

## Results

On the conventional F1@0.05 evaluation, DiffGEBD achieves 78.4 on Kinetics-GEBD and 65.8 on TAPOS, outperforming all prior methods on TAPOS (next best: EfficientGEBD at 63.1) and remaining competitive on Kinetics-GEBD (next best: DyBDet at 79.6). Under the proposed diversity-aware evaluation on Kinetics-GEBD, DiffGEBD attains the best symmetric F1 (74.0), best GT-to-Pred F1 (72.9), and best diversity score (20.4) among all compared methods, while achieving a Pred-to-GT F1 of 75.6 (slightly below EfficientGEBD's 76.0). The CFG weight w=0.6 is identified as optimal for the diversity-aware setting, balancing fidelity and diversity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffGEBD achieves the best TAPOS score (65.8) among all 14 compared methods, surpassing the previous best EfficientGEBD (63.1) by 2.7 points and DyBDet (62.5) by 3.3 points. On Kinetics-GEBD, DiffGEBD (78.4) trails only DyBDet (79.6) by 1.2 points while matching or exceeding all other baselines including SC-Transformer (77.7) and EfficientGEBD (78.3). Under the novel diversity-aware evaluation (Table 1), DiffGEBD leads on F1_sym (74.0 vs. 72.9 for SC-Transformer), F1_g2p (72.9 vs. 71.6 for SC-Transformer), and diversity (20.4 vs. 18.9 for SC-Transformer), though EfficientGEBD edges it on F1_p2g (76.0 vs. 75.6). The key distinction is that DiffGEBD generates all 5 diverse predictions from a single trained model by varying initial noise, whereas deterministic baselines (Temporal Perceiver, SC-Transformer, BasicGEBD, EfficientGEBD) require five separate training runs to produce multiple predictions. The ablation in Table 2 confirms that temporal self-similarity features (E) as diffusion conditioning substantially outperform raw backbone features (F), yielding a 5.5-point F1_sym improvement (74.0 vs. 68.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes generic event boundary detection (GEBD) from a deterministic regression problem into a generative one by applying denoising diffusion (Sohl-Dickstein et al., Song et al.) to the boundary label sequence. It adopts the temporal self-similarity encoder from BasicGEBD/EfficientGEBD (Zheng et al., 2024) as its conditioning backbone and integrates classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2021) to explicitly control prediction diversity. Conceptually, it aligns with the broader trend of using diffusion for ambiguous prediction tasks (Probabilistic U-Net for medical segmentation, GTDA for action anticipation), but is the first to apply this paradigm to video event boundary detection. The key departure from prior GEBD work (UBoCo, DDM-Net, SC-Transformer, DyBDet) is the shift from a single deterministic output to a distribution over plausible boundaries, accompanied by new diversity-aware evaluation metrics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The generative reframing addresses a genuine limitation of prior GEBD methods: event boundaries are inherently subjective, yet all previous approaches force a single 'correct' answer. The proposed symmetric F1 and diversity score metrics fill a real evaluation gap for multi-prediction settings and could be adopted by the broader GEBD community. However, the empirical gains are modest—DiffGEBD does not surpass the best conventional baseline (DyBDet) on Kinetics-GEBD F1, and the diversity advantage over retrained deterministic models is the primary differentiator. Open questions include whether the generated diversity genuinely reflects human perceptual variability or merely captures training noise, and whether the approach scales to longer videos or more complex boundary taxonomies.
