## Problem

Existing Cross Modal Generalization (CMG) methods assume a closed-set setting where source and target modalities share identical class sets, yet real-world deployment frequently encounters unseen categories in the target modality. Prior multimodal unified representation work also tends to align features at only a single granularity level (either holistic or temporal), and self-supervised approaches like MMJP are sensitive to modality-specific semantics, undermining the construction of a truly modality-agnostic unified representation. This paper introduces the Open-set Cross Modal Generalization (OSCMG) task and a method to address both the open-set detection challenge and the multi-granularity alignment gap.

## Input / Output

The model consumes paired multimodal inputs (video clips, audio signals, text descriptions, and optionally optical flow) extracted by modality-specific backbones (Swin-V2-L, HTS-AT, SlowOnly) and projected into a uniform feature tensor of shape T×D (time steps × 256-dim latent). A sample-specific binary mask is applied identically across all modalities of a pair, and the masked features are vector-quantized into a shared 400-entry codebook. Downstream, a frozen encoder plus a linear decoder produces class labels (with known/unknown separation for OSCMG), temporal event boundaries for localization, or ranked retrieval results for zero-shot cross-modal retrieval.

## Method

MICU combines two complementary self-supervised objectives. First, FCMI applies masked contrastive learning at two granularities simultaneously: a fine-grained InfoNCE that contrasts masked features at each timestep against unmasked features at the same timestep from the paired modality (with other timesteps as hard negatives), and a coarse-grained InfoNCE that contrasts the masked sample-level feature against the complete paired-modality feature (with other samples as negatives); both losses permit cross-modal and intra-modal pairs. Second, CUJP performs a modality-agnostic jigsaw puzzle on the quantized codebook indices: the discrete sequence is split into O segments, each segment is randomly drawn from any modality, the segments are shuffled, and a small classifier predicts the permutation index—requiring only O! permutations rather than the (O·M)! of MMJP, drastically reducing computation. The total training objective weights L_fine + L_coarse (λ₁=1), L_cujp (λ₂=2), reconstruction (λ₃=1), and commitment (λ₄=1) losses, with codebook updates via EMA for robustness.

## Results

On the newly proposed OSCMG task, MICU achieves the best HOS (harmonic mean of known-class accuracy and unknown-class accuracy) in 11 out of 12 evaluation settings across AVE, UCF, and UCF(v)↔VGG(a) datasets. The largest gains appear on UCF(v)↔VGG(a) Split1 V→A, where MICU reaches 87.09 HOS versus 83.58 for the previous SOTA (DCID), and on AVE Split1 A→V, where MICU scores 45.43 versus 38.36 for DCID. On the established CMG task, MICU improves all 8 metrics over DCID, with the smallest margin being +2.0% (AVE V→A: 56.1 vs 54.1) and the largest being +8.2% (UCF(v)↔VGG(a) V→A: 75.3 vs 67.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MICU outperforms all four baselines (CODIS, TURN, CMCM, DCID) on the vast majority of OSCMG HOS metrics, with the only exception being UCF(v)↔VGG(a) Split2 A→V where it scores 61.17 versus DCID's 62.49. The closest competitor is DCID [42], the prior SOTA for CMG; MICU improves over DCID by 2.85 HOS on AVE Split1 V→A (54.39 vs 51.54), 7.34 on AVE Split1 A→V (45.43 vs 38.36), 5.36 on UCF Split1 V→A (39.82 vs 34.46), and 3.51 on UCF(v)↔VGG(a) Split1 V→A (87.09 vs 83.58). CMCM [22] and CODIS [12] trail significantly, with CODIS achieving the lowest HOS in most settings (e.g., 24.91 on UCF Split1 V→A vs MICU's 39.82). On the CMG task (Table 3), MICU leads all baselines across all 8 metrics, with the largest gap over DCID being +8.2 on UCF(v)↔VGG(a) V→A (75.3 vs 67.1) and +7.4 on AVVP A→V (48.2 vs 40.8). On zero-shot retrieval (Table 4), MICU achieves R@1 of 1.30 on MSCOC (V↔T) and 2.44 on Clotho (A↔T), surpassing DCID (0.80 and 2.06) and CMCM (0.50 and 1.62).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Cross Modal Generalization (CMG) framework of Xia et al. (DCID) from closed-set to open-set evaluation, building on its discrete codebook architecture and cross-modal contrastive prediction. It adapts the Jigsaw Puzzle mechanism from Carlucci et al. and its multimodal extension MMJP (Dong et al.), but critically departs by operating on modality-agnostic unified discrete representations rather than modality-specific features, and introduces a fine-coarse masked contrastive loss (FCMI) that complements the holistic alignment of CMCM (Liu et al.) with temporal-level discrimination. The masking strategy draws from open-set segmentation work (Mask2Anomaly, SemSeg), repurposing it for cross-modal representation learning rather than anomaly detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OSCMG provides a more realistic evaluation protocol for multimodal unified representations, since real-world target modalities inevitably contain categories absent from the source domain. The finding that modality-specific jigsaw (MMJP) actually underperforms no jigsaw at all in unified representation tasks is a useful negative result that clarifies when cross-modal complementarity helps versus hurts. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to truly novel (never-seen) categories rather than held-out known ones, how it scales beyond three modalities, and whether the open-set evaluation via class splitting adequately captures the difficulty of genuine open-world deployment.
