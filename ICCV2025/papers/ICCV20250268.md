## Problem

Complex CNN-Transformer hybrid models achieve state-of-the-art spatiotemporal forecasting accuracy but incur quadratic computational cost and high memory usage, making them impractical for edge deployment. Existing knowledge distillation methods designed for classification or generic regression fail on spatiotemporal signals because they ignore the multi-band spectral structure, leading to either over-smoothing of high-frequency transients or loss of low-frequency trends in the student model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250268/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a spatiotemporal sequence X = {X_t ∈ R^{H×W×C}}_{t=1}^T where H×W is the spatial grid (ranging from 32×64 on WeatherBench to 128×128 on TaxiBJ+) and C is the number of physical variables (1–3). It produces a future state sequence Y = {X_{T+τ}}_{τ=1}^Δ with prediction horizons from 10 to 36 steps depending on the dataset. The student model is constrained to match the teacher's input-output shapes exactly, and its hidden dimension is set to only 20–30% of the teacher's, yielding up to 5× parameter reduction.

## Method

SDKD operates in two stages. First, a frequency-decoupled teacher (ST-AlterNet) is pretrained with an encoder–latent evolution–decoder architecture where the latent module runs a CNN block (acting as an implicit high-pass filter via local gradient operators) in parallel with a Transformer block (acting as a low-pass filter via attention), fused by a residual connection. Second, a lightweight student (U-Net, ResNet, or MLP-Mixer) is trained with a frequency-aligned distillation loss that separately aligns high-frequency and low-frequency spectral components extracted from the teacher's latent space, weighted by a hyperparameter α. For multi-teacher settings, the A2D (Agree to Disagree) gradient-space optimization adaptively weights each teacher's contribution per mini-batch, avoiding blind averaging when teachers conflict. The overall objective combines the standard forecasting MSE with the spectral distillation loss scaled by λ, and the trained student performs single-pass inference with O(Nd) or O(Kd²) complexity instead of the teacher's O(N²d).

## Results

On WeatherBench, the U-Net student with AEKD (MSE loss) achieves MAE=0.8457, PSNR=31.69, and SSIM=0.8849, closing the gap to the ST-AlterNet teacher (MAE=0.7287, PSNR=33.32, SSIM=0.9493) by roughly 14% in MAE over the undistilled student (MAE=0.9822). On TaxiBJ+, the same configuration yields MSE=0.0715 and MAE=0.1228, compared to the undistilled U-Net's MSE=0.0831 and MAE=0.1354. On the Navier-Stokes dataset, the best distillation method (CAMKD) reduces MSE from 0.141 to 0.136 and MAE from 0.239 to 0.234 relative to the student baseline, while inference speed improves by 2.28× over the teacher.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three distillation baselines: AVER-MKD (simple teacher-output averaging, based on Hinton [14]), AEKD (adaptive ensemble KD via gradient-space weighting from Du et al. [9]), and CAMKD (the proposed frequency-aligned variant). On WeatherBench, AEKD with MSE loss (MAE=0.8457) outperforms AVER-MKD with AB loss (MAE=0.8541) by ~1% in MAE and ~0.24 dB in PSNR for the U-Net student, while on TaxiBJ+ AEKD (MSE loss) achieves the lowest MSE (0.0715) versus AVER-MKD (0.0728). The ST-AlterNet teacher consistently outperforms the SimVP teacher across both datasets (e.g., WeatherBench MAE 0.7287 vs. 0.7475), confirming that the CNN-Transformer spectral decoupling design yields stronger distillation signals. On the Navier-Stokes dataset (Table 4), CAMKD achieves the best SSIM (0.667) but AVER-MKD edges it out slightly on MAE (0.233 vs. 0.234), indicating that the multi-teacher spectral alignment provides marginal gains over simple averaging for this particular fluid-dynamics task. Overall, the proposed frequency-aligned distillation consistently improves all three student architectures (U-Net, ResNet, MLP-Mixer) over their undistilled baselines, with the largest relative gains observed for the weakest student (MLP-Mixer: MAE reduced from 1.1826 to 1.1428 on WeatherBench).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends knowledge distillation from its traditional classification/regression settings to spatiotemporal forecasting by introducing an explicit frequency-domain decomposition of the teacher's latent space. It builds on the hybrid CNN-Transformer teacher architectures (ST-AlterNet, SimVP) and the multi-teacher distillation framework of Agree-to-Disagree (A2D), but departs from prior frequency-aware KD methods (e.g., FOLK's static spectral masks for classification) by treating the spectral properties of spatiotemporal signals as non-stationary and architecture-independent. The conceptual framing—CNN as implicit high-pass filter and Transformer as low-pass filter—draws on neural tangent kernel theory (Jacot et al.) and prior analyses of spectral bias in deep networks, but repurposes these observations as a distillation mechanism rather than a diagnostic tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation of deploying complex spatiotemporal forecasters on resource-constrained devices is well-motivated, and the spectral-decoupling perspective offers a more interpretable distillation target than generic feature matching. However, the experimental evidence is mixed: the abstract's claimed 81.3% MSE reduction on Navier-Stokes is not reflected in Table 4 (which shows only ~4% improvement), and on the RBC dataset several distillation variants (notably AEKD with AB Loss) actually degrade performance below the undistilled student baseline. Open questions include whether the frequency-aligned loss generalizes beyond the three student architectures tested, whether the modest 2.28× speedup justifies the distillation overhead in practice, and whether the spectral priors remain valid under distribution shift (e.g., different physical regimes or spatial resolutions).
