## Problem

Existing video instance segmentation methods rely on instance center features for cross-frame object matching, which degrades significantly under heavy occlusion or when multiple visually similar objects coexist. The surrounding contextual information around each object—what is adjacent to it in the scene—remains largely untapped for disambiguating and tracking instances across frames.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250395/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of T video frames and produces per-frame instance segmentation masks, class scores, and consistent tracking IDs. During training, the tracking network operates on 3 adjacent frames (the optimal window found via ablation), and context features are extracted using a fixed 9×9 average filter applied to pixel-decoder feature maps, masked by Laplacian-processed boundary scores. The segmentation backbone is Mask2Former with ResNet-50, Swin-L, or ViT-L encoders, and the tracking head is a 6-block transformer operating at the sub-clip level.

## Method

CAVIS introduces two key components. First, the Context-Aware Instance Tracker (CAIT) extracts surrounding features by averaging pixel-decoder features within a Laplacian-filtered mask boundary, concatenates them with core instance query features, and projects the result through a 3-layer MLP to form context-aware instance embeddings. A modified cross-attention in the tracking transformer uses these context-aware features as both query and key while retaining standard instance features as value, enabling the network to match objects based on their local scene context rather than appearance alone; context features are aligned across frames via Hungarian matching on cosine similarity. Second, the Prototypical Cross-frame Contrastive (PCC) loss constructs per-instance prototypes by averaging pixel embeddings within predicted masks and applies a contrastive objective across frames to enforce temporal consistency of these prototypes. Both losses are combined with the standard VIS training objective, and the full system is trained end-to-end with supervised segmentation plus contrastive tracking losses.

## Results

On the ViT-L backbone, CAVIS achieves 57.1 AP on OVIS, 69.4 AP on YouTube-VIS 2019, and 65.3 AP on YouTube-VIS 2021 (with temporal refiner), outperforming the previous best (DVIS+++) by 3.7, 1.1, and 1.4 AP points respectively. On the ResNet-50 backbone, CAVIS reaches 37.6 AP on OVIS, surpassing the prior best (VISAGE at 36.2) by 1.4 AP. On VIPSeg for video panoptic segmentation, CAVIS attains 58.5 VPQ and 56.1 STQ with ViT-L, exceeding DVIS++† (58.0 VPQ, 56.0 STQ).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAVIS achieves the best OVIS AP across all three backbones, with the largest margin on ViT-L (53.2 vs. DVIS++'s 49.6, a +3.6 AP gain) and with the temporal refiner (57.1 vs. DVIS+++ at 53.4, +3.7 AP). On YouTube-VIS 2019, CAVIS leads all baselines on every backbone (e.g., 68.9 vs. DVIS++'s 67.7 on ViT-L; 55.7 vs. VISAGE's 55.1 on ResNet-50). However, on YouTube-VIS 2021 with ResNet-50, CAVIS (50.5 AP) trails VISAGE (51.6 AP) by 1.1 points, and with Swin-L it is marginally below TCOVIS (61.1 vs. 61.3 AP). On VIPSeg, CAVIS† outperforms DVIS++† by 1.1 VPQ and 2.5 STQ with ResNet-50, and by 0.5 VPQ and 0.1 STQ with ViT-L, with the largest gain in VPQ^Th (things) of +3.0 and +1.9 respectively. Overall, CAVIS wins or ties on 11 of 12 backbone-dataset combinations for VIS, with the sole loss being YTVIS21-ResNet-50.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAVIS builds directly on the Mask2Former query-based segmentation backbone and the transformer-based tracking architecture from GenVIS/DVIS, which learn inter-frame associations via cross-attention between instance features. The key departure is the introduction of context-aware instance features: rather than relying solely on instance-center embeddings (as in IDOL, CTVIS, or DVIS), CAVIS extracts boundary-surrounding features via a Laplacian-then-average pipeline and fuses them with core features through an MLP, then uses these enriched features in a modified cross-attention for tracking. The Prototypical Cross-frame Contrastive (PCC) loss extends the contrastive learning paradigm from IDOL by constructing instance-wise prototypes from pixel embeddings, adding an intra-frame consistency constraint that prior contrastive VIS methods lacked. Compared to full-context approaches like CAROQ, CAVIS is deliberately memory-efficient, focusing only on the immediate neighborhood of each object rather than a global memory bank.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent and practically important failure mode in VIS—tracking breakdown under occlusion and among visually similar objects—by showing that local contextual cues around object boundaries carry discriminative signal that instance-center features alone miss. The particularly strong gains on OVIS (up to 3.7 AP with ViT-L) suggest the approach is most valuable in the hardest, most occlusion-heavy scenarios, which are also the most common in real-world deployment. Open questions include whether the fixed 9×9 context window generalizes to very small or very large objects, whether the assumption of stable surrounding information across adjacent frames holds in high-motion or scene-transition settings, and whether the PCC loss's prototype construction could be extended to longer temporal spans without the performance degradation observed when increasing the number of training frames.
