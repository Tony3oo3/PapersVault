## Problem

Self-contact human poses (e.g., rubbing belly, touching face) are inherently constrained by the subject's body shape, yet existing self-contact datasets contain only 1–4K poses with inaccurate body shape registration, making it impossible to conclusively study the pose–shape dependency. This gap limits both the modeling of shape-dependent self-contact distributions and the accuracy of single-view pose estimation in contact scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251671/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The diffusion model takes 300-dimensional SMPL-X body shape parameters as conditioning and generates part-wise pose parameters (face: R^(3+10), each hand: R^(15×3), body: R^(21×3)) using 6D rotation representation, with global orientation and translation discarded. For single-view refinement, the method additionally consumes an initial SMPL-X pose estimate (from regressors like SMPLer-X or HybrIK-X) and 2D keypoints in COCO-WholeBody format (from Sapiens), producing a refined 3D pose by running only the last 10% of diffusion steps (from step 100 to 1) with 2D keypoint gradient guidance at each iteration.

## Method

PAPoseDiff is a latent diffusion model that operates in a learned low-dimensional space via part-wise auto-encoders, rather than denoising raw pose parameters directly. A self-attention transformer serves as the denoising network, where query, key, and value vectors are formed by concatenating embeddings across face, both hands, body, diffusion time, and shape, enabling the model to capture cross-part interactions (e.g., hand-to-torso contact). A key design choice is shape-conditional perturbation: instead of standard conditional dropout, shape parameters are augmented with small Gaussian noise (30% probability, scale 1e-4) to encourage generalization to unseen subjects while preserving identity-specific signals. Training combines L1 losses in pose space, mesh vertex space, and a ray-tracing-based collision loss restricted to hand vertices. At inference, DDIM sampling with anti-collision guidance produces random self-contact poses, while the refinement procedure (Algorithm 1) starts from a diffused initial pose and iteratively denoises with 2D keypoint gradient steps, optionally using blended pose denoising to refine only selected body parts.

## Results

On the Goliath-SC eval set (unseen subjects), the proposed shape-conditional diffusion refinement (PAPoseDiff) reduces average MPJPE to 31.8 mm when applied to SMPLer-X initial estimates, compared to 42.0 mm for the fine-tuned SMPLer-X† regressor alone and 58.0 mm for the unfine-tuned SMPLer-X. For pose generation, the method achieves FID 1.25 and KID 0.430×10⁻³ with shape conditioning, substantially outperforming BUDDI* (FID 2.66, KID 1.12×10⁻³) and VPoser* (FID 9.16, KID 0.882×10⁻³), while also achieving the highest recall (0.708 vs. 0.488 and 0.005). The refinement is robust across initializers: it reduces Hand4Whole's 126.3 mm average error to 35.3 mm and HybrIK-X's 82.3 mm to 32.4 mm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BUDDI* (the concurrent diffusion-based contact prior), PAPoseDiff wins decisively across all three initializers: with SMPLer-X, BUDDI* actually degrades performance (71.7 mm vs. 42.0 mm for SMPLer-X†), while Ours achieves 31.8 mm; with HybrIK-X, BUDDI* gives 65.0 mm vs. Ours at 32.4 mm; with Hand4Whole, BUDDI* gives 74.5 mm vs. Ours at 35.3 mm. The paper attributes BUDDI*'s failure on well-initialized poses to its lack of latent diffusion and part-wise self-attention, which limits its capacity to refine already-reasonable estimates. Against the VAE-based VPoser* in generation, Ours achieves a 53% FID reduction (1.25 vs. 9.16) and dramatically higher recall (0.708 vs. 0.005), indicating VPoser* overfits to a narrow pose subset. Against the fine-tuned SMPLer-X† regressor (42.0 mm), the diffusion refinement further reduces error to 31.8 mm, with the largest gains on hands (56.7→54.6 mm) and body (31.9→24.7 mm), confirming that the shape-dependent contact prior corrects residual hand-placement errors that regression alone cannot resolve. Shape conditioning provides a consistent but modest improvement over the unconditional variant (e.g., 31.8 vs. 33.7 mm for SMPLer-X), with the largest relative benefit on hands and body parts that correlate more strongly with body shape.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based human contact modeling line (BUDDI, InterHandGen) by shifting from joint pose-shape distribution modeling to explicit shape-conditioned pose generation, and by moving from direct pose-space diffusion to latent diffusion with part-aware self-attention (PAPoseDiff). It builds on the Goliath multi-camera capture infrastructure to produce Goliath-SC, a self-contact dataset two orders of magnitude larger than prior efforts (HumanSC3D, MTP), and on the SMPL-X parameterization for full-body shape encoding. The refinement pipeline departs from regression-based estimators (SMPLer-X, HybrIK-X) by treating the learned diffusion prior as a post-hoc correction step that fits 2D keypoints while preserving contact plausibility, rather than training an end-to-end image-to-pose regressor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Goliath-SC dataset (383K poses, 130 subjects, precise SMPL-X shape registration) fills a critical gap that has limited self-contact research to small, imprecise collections, and will likely become a standard benchmark. The demonstration that shape conditioning is not merely helpful but essential for capturing the self-contact pose manifold (confirmed by ablation) provides a clear modeling principle for future body-aware generative work. Open questions remain: hand-hand interpenetration persists due to high articulation, the method only handles in-contact scenarios (not non-contact), captures use scripted actions limiting naturalness, and extending the prior to temporal sequences or multi-person settings is left as future work.
