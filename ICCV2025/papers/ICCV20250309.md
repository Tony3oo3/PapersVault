## Problem

Existing 3D city generation methods either rely on neural rendering (NeRF/Gaussian Splatting) that produces 2D images with inherent 3D structural ambiguity, or use diffusion on dense point clouds that cannot scale beyond street-level scenes. No prior framework jointly generates city-scale explicit 3D geometry and appearance from a single satellite observation without requiring auxiliary inputs like segmentation maps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250309/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single noised satellite height map (simulating real digital surface models with contrast-scaled noise) as its sole conditioning input, cropped to 300×300 pixel segments at ~0.93 m²/pixel ground sampling distance. It outputs a colorized sparse voxel grid (VDB structure) encoding both geometry (occupancy + trilinearly splatted normals) and per-vertex appearance (RGB colors), which can be extracted as a textured mesh. Notably, no segmentation map or multi-view images are required, and generation completes in approximately one minute.

## Method

Sat2City builds on XCube's sparse voxel + latent diffusion architecture but introduces three key mechanisms for joint geometry-appearance generation. First, a Triplet Bottleneck VAE encodes the input into three parallel latent streams: a dense neck (for explicit occupancy discrimination against noisy height-map conditioning), a sparse neck (for fine geometric structure), and a Re-Hash neck that hierarchically coarsens the appearance latent by factor-of-2 voxel resampling with trilinear interpolation across n levels to capture multi-scale color context. Second, inverse sampling provides implicit per-vertex color supervision by decoding appearance features at each Re-Hash level, trilinearly sampling them at the original colorized point cloud positions, and concatenating all levels through an MLP—avoiding the blending conflicts of direct splatting. Third, a three-stage cascaded DDIM diffusion pipeline progressively generates dense geometry latent (conditioned on the height map), then sparse geometry latent (conditioned on the dense output), then multi-level appearance latents (conditioned on the sparse structure's pruning decisions), with each stage's output structuring the next.

## Results

On the Sat2City dataset, Sat2City achieves 100% COV (CD) and 60% COV (EMD) for geometry, reducing MMD (CD) by 49.4% and MMD (EMD) by 37.9% over the strongest unconditional baseline (BlockFusion). In the user study, Sat2City scores 7.35 TPQ and 8.03 TSC for textured appearance, surpassing the best 2D rendering baselines (CityDreamer: 6.40 TPQ, 6.63 TSC; Sat2Scene: 6.17 TPQ, 5.90 TSC). For geometry-only perception, Sat2City achieves 6.27 GPQ and 7.02 GSC, substantially outperforming the 3D-reconstructed versions of baselines (CityDreamer 3D: 3.60 GPQ, 3.38 GSC; Sat2Scene 3D: 3.83 GPQ, 4.05 GSC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sat2City is compared against four baselines spanning two categories: unconditional 3D scene generators (NFD, BlockFusion) for geometry, and rendering-based city generators (Sat2Scene, CityDreamer) for appearance. Against BlockFusion (the strongest geometry baseline), Sat2City nearly halves MMD (CD: 0.0165 vs. 0.0326) and more than doubles COV (CD: 100% vs. 50.49%), though the comparison is between a conditional and an unconditional pipeline. Against Sat2Scene, the most directly comparable method (also satellite-conditioned, diffusion-based), Sat2City wins decisively on all four user-study metrics: 7.35 vs. 3.18 TPQ and 8.03 vs. 3.30 TSC when Sat2Scene is retrained on the same dataset, and 6.27 vs. 3.03 GPQ for geometry. Against CityDreamer, Sat2City outperforms both its native 2D rendering scores (7.35 vs. 6.40 TPQ) and its MASt3R-reconstructed 3D version (7.35 vs. 4.48 TPQ, 6.27 vs. 3.60 GPQ). The paper notes that Sat2Scene's failure on this dataset stems from its dependence on high point-cloud density (~400 pts/m²) versus the ~14 pts/m² available here, highlighting the scalability advantage of the sparse voxel representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

Sat2City builds directly on XCube's sparse voxel grid + latent diffusion architecture for large-scale 3D generation, extending it from geometry-only to joint geometry-and-appearance synthesis at city scale. It departs from the dominant neural rendering paradigm (CityDreamer, InfiniCity, GaussianCity) that produces 2D views with inherent 3D ambiguity, and from Sat2Scene's point-cloud diffusion approach that is limited to street-level scenarios with fixed geometry. The key architectural departures are the Re-Hash multi-scale coarsening at the VAE bottleneck, inverse sampling for implicit color supervision, and a three-stage cascaded diffusion pipeline (dense geometry → sparse geometry → multi-level appearance), none of which appear in prior city generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to produce explicit 3D geometry with city-scale appearance from a single satellite image, addressing a gap where prior methods either rendered 2D views (CityDreamer) or colored pre-existing geometry (Sat2Scene). The synthetic Blender-based dataset, while enabling controlled training, limits immediate real-world applicability; the authors acknowledge the open question of transferring to real satellite–3D Tiles pairs. The Re-Hash and inverse-sampling mechanisms are potentially transferable to other large-scale 3D generation tasks requiring multi-scale appearance, and the finding that direct 3D appearance learning can outperform 2D rendering-based baselines in user studies challenges the assumption that image-space supervision is necessary for photorealistic textures.
