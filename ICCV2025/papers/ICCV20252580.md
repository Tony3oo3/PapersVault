## Problem

Existing AIGI evaluation benchmarks either lack fine-grained MOS annotations (only 3–6 per image), cover only one of perceptual quality or text-image correspondence, or omit task-specific accuracy annotations. This makes it difficult to holistically assess both the generation quality and prompt alignment of large multimodal T2I models in a way that aligns with human perception.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252580/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes an AI-generated image (resized to 1024×1024, tiled into 448×448 patches with pixel-unshuffle reducing visual tokens to one-quarter), the corresponding text prompt, and 20 task-specific yes/no questions. It produces two continuous scores (perception quality and T2I correspondence on a 0–100 scale), a text-defined quality level label (bad/poor/fair/good/excellent), and binary answers to each task-specific question, all from a single unified model.

## Method

LMM4LMM builds on InternVL2.5-8B (InternViT vision encoder + LLM) with a two-MLP projector and a quality regression head. Training is two-stage: first, instruction tuning aligns the projector and trains the LLM to output text-defined quality levels and QA answers (language loss); second, LoRA (r=8) adapters are applied to both the vision encoder and the LLM while a regression module trained with L1 loss maps last-hidden-state features to numerical scores. The key design choice is converting continuous MOS values into five categorical text levels (following Q-Align) before numerical regression, exploiting the LLM's stronger grasp of textual than numerical data. A pixel-unshuffle operation enables processing of high-resolution 1024×1024 inputs without excessive token counts.

## Results

On EvalMi-50K, LMM4LMM achieves SRCC of 0.8863 (perception) and 0.8969 (correspondence), with PLCC of 0.9094 and 0.9162 respectively, outperforming the best prior methods by +5.5% SRCC in perception (vs. Q-Align at 0.8311) and +9.61% SRCC in correspondence (vs. fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL at 0.8008). In zero-shot cross-dataset evaluation, LMM4LMM trained on EvalMi-50K achieves the best SRCC on all 7 external benchmarks, including 0.8011 on AGIQA-3K, 0.7514 on AIGCIQA2023, and 0.7082 on GenAI-Bench. The model also achieves 87.9% QA accuracy on task-specific challenges, surpassing the best fine-tuned LMM baseline (89.5% for Qwen2.5-VL on single-class tasks but only 72.6% on counting tasks).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LMM4LMM outperforms all 34 baselines on both perception and correspondence dimensions of EvalMi-50K. In perception quality, it surpasses the strongest deep-learning IQA baselines (StairIQA at 0.8268 SRCC, Q-Align at 0.8311, MUSIQ at 0.7985) by 5.5–8.8% SRCC, and dominates fine-tuned LMM baselines (DeepSeekVL2 at 0.7899, Qwen2.5-VL at 0.6990) by 9–27% SRCC. In T2I correspondence, the advantage is even larger: LMM4LMM (0.8969 SRCC) beats the best fine-tuned LMM (Qwen2.5-VL at 0.8008) by 9.6% SRCC and the best non-fine-tuned method (FGA-BLIP2 at 0.6755) by 22.1% SRCC. Handcrafted IQA methods (NIQE, BRISQUE, QAC) and vision-language pre-training scores (CLIPScore, PickScore, HPSv2) perform poorly on both dimensions, with SRCC values generally below 0.65, confirming that AIGI-specific evaluation requires dedicated modeling. In zero-shot cross-dataset evaluation (Table 5), LMM4LMM trained on EvalMi-50K achieves the best SRCC on all 7 external benchmarks (e.g., 0.8011 on AGIQA-3K vs. FGA-BLIP2's 0.7793, 0.7514 on AIGCIQA2023 vs. 0.7432), demonstrating strong generalization without dataset-specific fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the InternVL2.5-8B LMM as its backbone and adopts Q-Align's strategy of converting continuous quality scores into discrete text-defined levels (bad/poor/fair/good/excellent) as an intermediate training signal before numerical regression. It departs from prior AIGI evaluation work by unifying three previously separate evaluation dimensions—perceptual quality (addressed by IQA methods like MUSIQ, StairIQA), text-image correspondence (addressed by CLIPScore, VQAScore, FGA-BLIP2), and task-specific accuracy (addressed by GenEval, T2I-CompBench)—into a single all-in-one LMM-based metric. The dataset itself extends the line of AIGI evaluation databases (AGIQA-3K, AIGCIQA2023, AIGIQA-20K) by scaling to 50K images across 24 models and 20 fine-grained tasks with 16 annotators per image, addressing the limited scale and dimension coverage of prior benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EvalMi-50K is likely to become a standard reference benchmark for AIGI evaluation given its scale (50K images, 24 models, 20 tasks, 2M+ annotations) and the fact that it covers dimensions that prior benchmarks addressed in isolation. The finding that fine-tuning on a coarser-scoring dataset (EvalMuse-40K) actually degrades zero-shot generalization compared to fine-tuning on the finer-grained EvalMi-50K highlights an underexplored question about how annotation granularity affects model transferability. Open questions include whether the all-in-one paradigm scales to video generation evaluation, whether the 20-task taxonomy is sufficient for emerging T2I capabilities, and whether the LoRA-based adaptation strategy generalizes to much larger LMM backbones beyond 26B parameters.
