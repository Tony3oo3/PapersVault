## Problem

Existing NLOS reconstruction methods rely on empirical physical priors—most notably a single fixed path compensation coefficient—that fail when multiple surface materials coexist in a scene, causing Radiometric Intensity Fall-off (RIF) to be inadequately corrected. Additionally, these methods exhibit poor generalization under low signal-to-noise ratio conditions, where Poisson-distributed noise photons introduce high-frequency aliasing that degrades both traditional and learning-based approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250585/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes time-resolved transient measurements (256×256 spatial resolution, 512 temporal bins at 33 ps bin width, 2 m × 2 m scanning area) captured by a SPAD-based confocal imaging system and produces an albedo volume, a 2D intensity image, and a depth map. A key design choice is that the network is trained entirely on synthetic data (2704 samples from the LFE dataset) and then evaluated zero-shot on real-world measurements from different imaging systems, including self-captured data with 10-minute acquisition times.

## Method

The approach integrates two novel modules into the LFE framework. The Learnable Path Compensation (LPC) module initializes features with physics-based predefined compensation weights (r = 1, 2, 4 corresponding to different surface material attenuation), then uses a small CNN with Softmax to learn per-scanning-point probabilities over these weights, enabling adaptive RIF correction across heterogeneous materials in a single scene. The Adaptive Phasor Field (APF) module transforms features into the frequency domain along the temporal axis, convolves across spatial and spectral dimensions, and predicts a per-measurement standard deviation for the Gaussian illumination window, dynamically selecting the effective 3 dB bandwidth to suppress noise while preserving signal. The full pipeline is trained end-to-end with a combined L2 intensity and depth loss on synthetic data, requiring no real-world supervision.

## Results

On the Seen synthetic test set, the proposed method achieves 24.08 dB PSNR and 0.8704 SSIM for intensity reconstruction, and 0.0867 RMSE / 0.0307 MAD for depth estimation, outperforming all six baselines across every metric. On the Unseen test set under low SNR (25 dB), the method retains 18.11 dB PSNR and 0.2445 SSIM, while the strongest competitor NLOST drops to 17.59 dB and 0.0998 SSIM. The method also uses only 16 GB memory and 0.24 s inference time, compared to 38 GB and 0.38 s for NLOST.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines on every metric in both the Seen and Unseen test sets. Against the strongest learning-based competitor NLOST [13], it gains 0.34 dB PSNR and reduces depth MAD by 10.23%, while using less than half the memory (16 GB vs. 38 GB) and 37% less inference time (0.24 s vs. 0.38 s). Compared to I-K [31], the improvement is 0.64 dB PSNR with a 35% reduction in depth MAD (0.0307 vs. 0.0476). Over LFE [4], the gain is 0.81 dB PSNR and a 37% MAD reduction. Against the traditional wave-based RSD [18], the method achieves a 2.34 dB PSNR advantage and dramatically lower depth error (RMSE 0.0867 vs. 0.5677). The largest margins are over LCT [23] (+4.57 dB PSNR) and FK [17] (+2.39 dB PSNR), confirming that the learnable physical priors (LPC and APF) substantially close the gap between physics-driven and learning-based reconstruction, especially under low-SNR and unseen-scene conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LFE framework [4] for its overall pipeline (feature extraction, wave propagation, rendering) and on the phasor-field virtual wave optics formulation of Liu et al. [18] for the illumination function and Rayleigh-Sommerfeld diffraction integral. The key departure is replacing two fixed empirical physical priors—uniform path compensation coefficients (quadratic or quartic, chosen per material type) and a hand-tuned Gaussian window standard deviation—with learnable, per-point and per-measurement counterparts (LPC and APF). This positions the work within the physics-informed learning trend in NLOS imaging, alongside LFE, I-K, and NLOST, but explicitly targets the limitation that prior methods apply a single material assumption across heterogeneous scenes and a single spectral band across varying SNR conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to train exclusively on synthetic data and generalize across multiple real-world datasets from different imaging systems at low SNR is practically valuable for NLOS applications where labeled real data is scarce and acquisition time is constrained. However, the improvements over the strongest baselines (NLOST, I-K) are modest (≈0.3–0.6 dB PSNR), and the self-captured scenes, while adding diversity, are relatively simple. Open questions remain around extending the confocal formulation to non-confocal systems and closing the gap between the Poisson SPAD model and real sensor behavior, both acknowledged by the authors as future work.
