## Problem

Existing single-stage gait recognition models encode sequences into a single global feature, which limits discrimination when hard negatives (similar gait patterns) occupy the top of the ranked list. This creates a large gap between Rank-1 and Rank-5 accuracy, yet re-ranking—a two-stage refinement widely used in person re-ID and image retrieval—has received very limited attention in gait recognition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250762/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The re-ranker consumes strip-wise feature maps (s × d, where s is the number of horizontal body strips and d is the feature dimension) extracted by a frozen pre-trained gait model, operating on each probe–candidate pair within the top-K (K=10) shortlist. It outputs updated probe–candidate distances (average Euclidean distance over corresponding strips in the new space) that re-order the candidate list. The method is model-agnostic: input dimensions adapt to whatever feature map the backbone produces, while the cross-attention module itself (8 heads, 256-d hidden) remains fixed.

## Method

CarGait applies bidirectional multi-head cross-attention between the probe and each candidate's strip feature maps: the probe strips serve as Query with candidate strips as Key/Value (and vice versa), producing two conditioned representations that capture fine-grained inter-strip correlations. Residual connections preserve the pre-trained feature space, and a new distance is computed as the average Euclidean distance between corresponding strips in this refined space. Training uses a ranking loss (sigmoid-based triplet penalty with a damping factor β for already-correctly-ranked pairs) combined with a cross-entropy classification loss as regularization, optimizing the re-ranker on top-v (v=30) nearest-neighbor pairs generated from the frozen backbone. The key distinction from prior re-rankers (KR, LBR, GCR) is that CarGait modifies the representations themselves through pairwise strip-level attention rather than merely re-weighting global similarity scores, enabling it to handle scenarios with a single positive in the gallery.

## Results

CarGait consistently improves Rank-1 accuracy across all seven gait models and three datasets. The largest gains appear on challenging in-the-wild benchmarks: GaitBase on GREW improves from 60.1% to 67.2% R1 (+7.1%), and GaitSet on Gait3D from 36.7% to 41.5% R1 (+4.8%). On the strongest model (SG++), CarGait lifts R1 from 85.8% to 88.2% on GREW and R5 from 92.6% to 94.6%. Inference time is 6.52 ms per probe, substantially faster than competing re-rankers (KR: 214 ms, LBR: 19.81 ms, GCR: 1866 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CarGait outperforms all three competing re-rankers (KR, LBR, GCR) on every model and dataset tested. On Gait3D, CarGait achieves the best R1 for all seven models, with margins of 1.7–9.5 points over the strongest competitor (e.g., GaitSet: 41.5% vs. GCR's 35.7%; GaitBase: 66.1% vs. LBR's 63.8%). On OU-MVLP, where the gallery contains only a single positive per probe, KR and LBR severely degrade initial results (e.g., GaitSet drops from 87.1% to 65.5% with KR), while CarGait maintains or slightly improves them (87.5%). In terms of speed, CarGait's 6.52 ms inference per probe is 33× faster than KR (214 ms), 3× faster than LBR (19.81 ms), and 286× faster than GCR (1866 ms). The paper also shows that a naive binary-classifier baseline (concatenating probe and candidate features into an MLP) actually degrades R1 from 75.0% to 71.6%, confirming that the strip-wise cross-attention mechanism is essential for the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

CarGait builds on the strip-based gait representation introduced by GaitPart and GaitSet, where gait sequences are decomposed into horizontal body strips carrying fine-grained spatio-temporal information. It departs from prior re-ranking methods (KR, LBR, GCR) designed for person re-identification or image retrieval by introducing a pairwise cross-attention mechanism that operates directly on probe-candidate strip interactions rather than refining global feature similarities or graph structures. The paper positions its contribution as bringing the well-established two-stage retrieval paradigm (common in image retrieval and visual place recognition) to gait recognition, which has been almost exclusively single-stage, while also distinguishing itself from prior cross-attention use in gait (e.g., Cui et al.'s multi-modal fusion) by applying it specifically to pairwise sequence comparison for re-ranking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that re-ranking is a viable and underexplored avenue for improving gait recognition, with consistent Rank-1 gains across seven diverse models and three benchmarks, and a practical inference cost of ~6.5ms per probe. The model-agnostic design means practitioners can adopt CarGait on top of any existing single-stage gait model without retraining the backbone. Limitations include modest absolute gains (typically 1–7% Rank-1), the need to train a separate re-ranker per base model, and reduced benefit on controlled datasets like OU-MVLP where initial performance is already saturated. Open questions include whether the cross-attention approach extends to model-based (skeleton/pose) gait methods, whether it scales to very large galleries beyond top-10, and whether the strip-level granularity can be further exploited with hierarchical or part-aware attention.
