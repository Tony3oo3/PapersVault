## Problem

Existing preference alignment datasets (e.g., Pick-a-Pic) capture only aggregated, population-level preferences, while individual-level personalization of text-to-image models remains under-explored due to the absence of large-scale, fine-grained, in-the-wild user preference annotations. Prior personalization efforts are limited to zero-shot approaches requiring user input at inference time, making them expensive and unable to leverage cross-user similarity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250527/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 4.9M safety-filtered images with 2.9M unique prompts, 242K LoRA checkpoints, and interaction logs from 57K users, structured as a ternary relational database linking images, LoRAs, and users. For the W2W personalization task, input is a set of user-created LoRA weight matrices (reduced via SVD to top-1 singular component and flattened) plus binary preference labels per user-model pair; output is an edited LoRA weight vector θ_edit = θ + αv in a PCA-reduced latent space. For recommendation, input is a user's historical interaction sequence and output is a ranked list of images or LoRA models with VLM-generated textual justifications.

## Method

The paper introduces three applications on the ImageGem dataset. First, aggregated preference alignment fine-tunes SD1.5 with DiffusionDPO using preference pairs curated via HDBScan clustering of prompt CLIP embeddings followed by min-max pairing on HPSv2 scores. Second, a two-stage retrieval-ranking pipeline uses FAISS-based collaborative filtering (ItemKNN, Item2Vec, two-tower, SASRec) to retrieve candidates, then Pixtral-12B VLM to caption user preference profiles and rank items with explainable scores. Third, the key novelty is Generative Model Personalization in a LoRA Weights2Weights (W2W) space: each LoRA is reduced via SVD to its top-1 component, flattened, and projected via PCA into a low-dimensional basis; a linear classifier trained on user preference labels yields a direction vector v, and personalized models are produced by translating any LoRA along v (θ_edit = θ + αv) without retraining, enabling bidirectional style and preference editing.

## Results

For aggregated preference alignment (DiffusionDPO on SD1.5), ImageGem subsets outperform Pick-a-Pic across all three topics: on Cars, ImageGem Small achieves Pick Score 0.2004 vs. 0.1993, HPSv2 0.2741 vs. 0.2690, and CLIP Score 0.3745 vs. 0.3607; on Dogs, ImageGem reaches 0.2069/0.2789/0.3683 vs. 0.2058/0.2739/0.3617; on Scenery, ImageGem Large reaches 0.1961/0.2747/0.3427 vs. 0.1936/0.2676/0.3289. For image retrieval on 1M items, the two-tower model achieves the best Recall@10000 of 0.5157, outperforming Item2Vec (0.5032) and ItemKNN (0.4705). For model retrieval, SASRec achieves Recall@100 of 0.2845 and NDCG@10 of 0.1239, substantially outperforming UserKNN (0.232/0.0705) and ItemKNN (0.1282/0.057). In VLM-based generative recommendation, the VLM achieves the best average rank (2.40 for images, 3.94 for models) and highest Recall@5 (0.95 for images, 0.72 for models) compared to ItemKNN and SASRec.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Pick-a-Pic [19], ImageGem consistently wins on all three preference alignment topics (Cars, Dogs, Scenery) across all three metrics (Pick Score, HPSv2, CLIP Score), with the largest margin on Scenery where CLIP Score improves from 0.3289 to 0.3427 and HPSv2 from 0.2676 to 0.2747. For image retrieval, the two-tower model [39] outperforms both ItemKNN [7] (Rec@10000: 0.5157 vs. 0.4705) and Item2Vec [2] (0.5157 vs. 0.5032), with the gap widening at lower k values. For model retrieval, SASRec [18] substantially outperforms ItemKNN [7] (Rec@100: 0.2845 vs. 0.1282; NDCG@10: 0.1239 vs. 0.057) and UserKNN (0.2845 vs. 0.232), demonstrating that sequential modeling of user interactions is critical for generative model recommendation. In the VLM-based generative recommendation setting, the VLM (Pixtral-12B) outperforms both ItemKNN and SASRec on ranking quality for both image and model recommendation, achieving Recall@5 of 0.95 (images) and 0.72 (models) versus 0.75/0.25 and 0.85/0.50 respectively, while also providing human-readable explanations for its rankings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two distinct lines of work: DiffusionDPO [32] for preference-aligned diffusion model training, and the Weights2Weights (W2W) framework [10] for navigating LoRA weight spaces. The key departure is repurposing the W2W space from identity editing (face/subject customization) to individual preference editing, and sourcing preference signals from in-the-wild interaction logs rather than curated human annotations. Unlike prior preference datasets (Pick-a-Pic, RichHF) that capture aggregated population-level preferences, ImageGem records individual-level user preferences from 57K real users on Civitai, enabling a shift from 'what does everyone like' to 'what does this specific user like.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset fills a critical gap: no prior large-scale resource captures fine-grained, individual-level user preferences for generative models from real-world usage. The generative model personalization paradigm (editing LoRA in a latent weight space to match a user's style) is a promising direction that avoids the cost of per-user fine-tuning, but the current evidence is preliminary—limited to the human-figure domain, relying on PCA-restricted low-rank models, and validated primarily through qualitative comparisons. Open questions include whether the W2W approach generalizes to non-human domains (scenery, objects), whether it scales to larger base models like Flux, and whether alternative weight-space constructions (beyond PCA+SVD) can capture richer preference geometry.
