## Problem

Training 3D Gaussian Splatting with high Gaussian counts (2M–5M) requires GPU memory up to 4× the model size, making it infeasible on resource-constrained platforms. Post-training codebook compression reduces storage but not training memory, while pruning-based methods reduce memory at the cost of reconstruction quality. The unsolved challenge is training directly on codebook-compressed representations, where the discrete Gaussian-to-codebook indices are non-differentiable and cannot be optimized by standard gradient descent.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images with known camera poses and can initialize Gaussians from a random point cloud (no SfM required) or from an SfM point cloud. The output is a codebook-compressed 3DGS model in which each Gaussian stores only its position, opacity, and two integer indices (g2sh, g2sr) pointing into a shared SH codebook (spherical harmonics coefficients) and SR codebook (scaling + quaternion), rather than full per-Gaussian parameter vectors. This compressed model renders novel-view images at higher FPS than uncompressed 3DGS due to reduced memory bandwidth.

## Method

ContraGS formulates codebook-compressed 3DGS training as Bayesian inference over a posterior distribution p(G,C) ∝ exp(−L), where L combines the photometric reconstruction loss with penalties on codebook sizes (|SH|, |SR|) to incentivize compact codebooks. Because the discrete Gaussian-to-codebook indices are non-differentiable, SGLD (used by 3DGS-MCMC) is inapplicable; instead, ContraGS uses Metropolis-Hastings MCMC with three transition types: a parameter update step (98% probability, SGLD-like gradient step on continuous parameters), a split step (1%, allocating a new codebook vector offset by a Gaussian perturbation), and a merge step (1%, combining two previously split codebook vectors). The acceptance probabilities for split and merge incorporate the codebook-size penalty and the ratio of proposal densities, enabling the sampler to explore the full state space of codebook mappings while preferentially accepting configurations with smaller codebooks. This jointly learns both the continuous codebook vector values and the discrete assignment of Gaussians to those vectors, achieving 3.49× peak memory reduction with less than 0.3 PSNR degradation at 2M Gaussians.

## Results

On MipNerf360, ContraGS-2M achieves 30.06 PSNR / 0.899 SSIM / 0.175 LPIPS with 130 MB peak memory, compared to 3DGS-MCMC-2M's 30.71 PSNR at 473 MB (3.78× less memory). On Tanks and Temples, ContraGS-2M reaches 24.35 PSNR (matching MCMC-2M's 24.32) while using only 128 MB vs. 473 MB. ContraGS-5M achieves 31.01 PSNR on MipNerf360 (vs. MCMC-5M's 32.72) at 276 MB (4.3× less), and 26.18 PSNR on T&T (vs. 26.63) at 338 MB. Training is 1.36× faster and rendering 1.88× faster than MCMC-2M on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the state-of-art 3DGS-MCMC [26], ContraGS-2M stays within 0.3 PSNR on average (30.06 vs. 30.71 on MipNerf360, 24.35 vs. 24.32 on T&T) while reducing peak training memory by 3.78× (130 MB vs. 473 MB); ContraGS-5M narrows the gap to 0.8 PSNR on average with 3.5× less memory. Against memory-efficient baselines that prune Gaussians, ContraGS-2M clearly wins on quality: it outperforms EAGLES (30.06 vs. 28.70 PSNR on MipNerf360), SpeedySplat (30.06 vs. 28.33), Taming-GS (30.06 vs. 29.39), and Scaffold-GS (30.06 vs. 29.22) while using comparable or lower peak memory (130 MB vs. 159, –, 478, 305 MB respectively). Reduced-GS [46] is the closest competitor on MipNerf360 (29.03 PSNR) but still trails ContraGS-2M by 1.03 PSNR and uses 11× more memory (1432 MB). On the Blender synthetic dataset (Table 2), ContraGS-2M achieves 36.84 PSNR, substantially outperforming all baselines (best prior: Reduced-GS at 33.80). The main trade-off is that ContraGS-5M incurs a 1.5 PSNR drop on MipNerf360 relative to MCMC-5M, the largest gap observed across all configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

ContraGS sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: (1) the Bayesian/MCMC reformulation of 3DGS training by 3DGS-MCMC [26], which replaces gradient descent with SGLD sampling, and (2) post-training codebook compression of 3DGS parameters as in C3DGS [47] and LightGaussian [10]. The key departure is that SGLD requires a differentiable log-posterior, which breaks when Gaussian-to-codebook indices are discrete; ContraGS substitutes the Metropolis-Hastings algorithm with split/merge proposals to navigate the joint continuous-discrete state space. Unlike prior memory-efficient training methods (EAGLES, SpeedySplat, Taming-GS, Scaffold-GS) that reduce Gaussian count and sacrifice quality, ContraGS preserves the full Gaussian count while compressing the parameter storage, making it the first method to train directly on a codebook-compressed 3DGS representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.49× peak-memory reduction and 1.36×/1.88× training/rendering speedups make high-Gaussian-count 3DGS feasible on resource-constrained platforms (mobile, web browsers) that were previously infeasible, with only a small quality gap (<0.3 PSNR at 2M Gaussians). The Bayesian framework is stated to be extensible to other point-based representations (RadiantFoam, ADOP, Deformable Beta Splatting, LinPrim), suggesting a broader applicability beyond 3DGS. Open questions include how the split/merge proposal rates (1% each) and codebook-size penalties (λ_SH, λ_SR) should be tuned for very large or dynamic scenes, and whether the quality gap to full-precision MCMC can be closed with longer sampling or adaptive proposal distributions.
