## Problem

High-fidelity facial albedo estimation and unbiased skin tone estimation have been treated as separate challenges: methods achieving one typically sacrifice the other, and both rely on expensive light-stage captured data. No prior work simultaneously recovers pore-level albedo detail and produces racially fair skin tones without requiring such captured supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250853/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts one or more aligned RGB facial images (1024×1024) of the same individual, optionally grouped by similar age to handle temporal appearance variation. It outputs a diffuse albedo UV map that is illumination-invariant and directly renderable on a predicted 3D mesh (via HRN). Multi-image inference (3–6 images optimal) progressively refines skin tone accuracy by averaging over different lighting conditions, while single-image inference remains functional but suffers from albedo-illumination ambiguity.

## Method

HUST is a three-stage pipeline built on a VQGAN backbone. First, a high-fidelity texture codebook (1024 codes) is trained on 501K high-resolution web faces in image space. Second, the encoder is fine-tuned under a dual discriminator—operating jointly in latent space (for UV topology consistency) and image space (for pore-level photorealism via differentiable rendering from original and random viewpoints)—to unwrap any input image into a coherent UV texture. Third, a cross-attention module with a learnable query latent aggregates variable-length texture latents from multiple images of the same person into a fixed-length albedo latent, trained with a novel group identity loss that extends single-image identity constraints to the entire batch using pairwise face-similarity weights. The key insight is treating albedo estimation as Monte Carlo sampling: the shared albedo latent learned across differently-lit images of one person converges to the true diffuse albedo, enabling unsupervised domain adaptation from texture to albedo without any captured albedo data.

## Results

On the FAIR benchmark, HUST achieves the lowest average ITA error (11.20) and bias score (1.58) among all compared methods, with a total score of 12.78 (avg. ITA + bias). On CelebAMask-HQ, HUST attains PSNR 29.14, SSIM 0.9114, LPIPS 0.1109, and identity similarity 0.7594, substantially outperforming TRUST (PSNR 21.63) and ID2Albedo (PSNR 23.72). Notably, HUST achieves these results without using any ground-truth albedo supervision, relying solely on in-the-wild web images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HUST outperforms all six methods that do not use albedo data (Deep3D, GANFIT, MGCNet, DECA, INORig, CEST) by large margins on both ITA error and bias, with the closest non-albedo-data competitor MGCNet still trailing by over 10 ITA points (21.41 vs. 11.20). Against the two strongest albedo-data-based baselines, HUST beats TRUST [16] on average ITA (11.20 vs. 13.87), bias (1.58 vs. 2.79, a 43.3% reduction), and total score (12.78 vs. 16.67), though TRUST achieves a lower MAE (18.41 vs. 21.24). HUST also surpasses ID2Albedo [36] on ITA (11.20 vs. 12.07), bias (1.58 vs. 4.91), and MAE (21.24 vs. 23.33), and outperforms ID2Reflectance [38] on all four headline metrics. On per-skin-type ITA, HUST wins on five of six Fitzpatrick types (II–VI) but is marginally worse on the lightest type I (11.61 vs. 11.90 for TRUST). On CelebAMask-HQ, HUST dominates both TRUST and ID2Albedo across PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and identity similarity, with a 5.4 dB PSNR gain over TRUST and a 0.23 improvement in identity score.

## Relation to Prior Work

HUST builds on VQGAN (Esser et al., CVPR 2021) as its generative backbone but departs fundamentally from prior albedo estimation pipelines that rely on expensive light-stage or 3D-scanned albedo data (TRUST, ID2Albedo, AvatarMe). The key conceptual departure is reframing albedo estimation as a Monte Carlo sampling problem over multiple in-the-wild images of the same person, enabling unsupervised domain adaptation from texture to albedo via a cross-attention module and group identity loss. The dual discriminator (joint latent-space and image-space adversarial supervision) for UV texture unwrapping is a novel architectural contribution not present in prior VQGAN-based face reconstruction work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HUST is the first method to simultaneously achieve high-fidelity pore-level detail and unbiased skin tone estimation without any captured albedo data, addressing a long-standing trade-off in the field. The elimination of light-stage dependency dramatically lowers the barrier to training high-quality albedo models, making the approach accessible to groups without access to specialized capture hardware. Open questions include how well the Lambertian assumption holds for glossy or textured skin, whether the age-based grouping strategy generalizes to rapidly aging subjects, and whether the Monte Carlo convergence behavior (3–6 images) holds under extreme lighting variation or makeup.
