## Problem

Existing unified multimodal models that perform both visual understanding and generation require substantial architectural modifications and extensive pretraining on billions of image-text pairs. This paper asks whether a pretrained LLM can be efficiently finetuned to generate visual tokens with the same data and compute efficiency as visual instruction tuning, and whether understanding and generation are synergistic or orthogonal capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251277/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts arbitrary interleaved sequences of text tokens and continuous visual tokens (images encoded by SigLIP ViT-SO400M-14@384, interpolated to 64 tokens per image, or video frames at 1 FPS). It autoregressively outputs a mixture of discrete text tokens and continuous visual tokens, with special <image_start> and <image_end> tokens delimiting visual segments. Generated visual tokens are mapped to pixel space by a separately finetuned latent diffusion model conditioned on the vision encoder's embedding space.

## Method

Visual-Predictive Instruction Tuning (VPiT) extends standard visual instruction tuning by attaching a separate vision head (a projection layer from LLM dimension to vision encoder dimension) alongside the original text head, training the LLM to predict both discrete text tokens (cross-entropy loss) and continuous visual tokens (cosine similarity loss) autoregressively. The key empirical finding is that visual generation emerges as a byproduct of visual understanding: joint training with understanding data unlocks generation with as few as 200k image-text pairs, whereas generation-only training requires millions and still underperforms. Understanding data contributes more to both capabilities than generation data, and the synergy transfers across LLM backbones (LLaMA-3 8B, LLaMA-3.1 8B, LLaMA-3 70B). The final MetaMorph model is built on LLaMA-3.1 8B with SigLIP, trained on ~7M VQA samples plus 1M generation pairs and auxiliary video/editing data, with a diffusion autoencoder for pixel-space visualization.

## Results

MetaMorph (LLaMA-3.1 8B) achieves an average VQA score competitive with GPT-4V on MMBench (75.2 vs. 75.8) and SEED (71.8 vs. 69.1), while also producing a COCO FID of 11.8—comparable to dedicated text-to-image models like DALL-E 2 (10.4) and Stable Diffusion 1.5 (9.6). On video understanding (MV-Bench), MetaMorph scores 48.8, outperforming all other unified models. The model also achieves 83.2 on ScienceQA and 41.8 on MMMU, though it trails GPT-4V on knowledge-intensive and OCR-heavy benchmarks (MMMU 41.8 vs. 56.8; TextVQA 60.5 vs. 78.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaMorph outperforms all other unified models on visual understanding: it leads EMU-3 (FID 12.8) by 16.7 points on MMBench (75.2 vs. 58.5) and 10.2 on MMMU (41.8 vs. 31.6), while also achieving a lower FID (11.8 vs. 12.8). Against Chameleon-7B, MetaMorph dominates across every reported understanding benchmark (e.g., MMBench 75.2 vs. 35.7) and improves FID by 14.9 points (11.8 vs. 26.7). Compared to VILA-U256, MetaMorph gains 8.6 points on MMBench and 9.8 on MMMU, with a substantially better FID (11.8 vs. 19.6). On generation quality, MetaMorph (FID 11.8) trails Janus (8.5) and Transfusion (6.7), though both of those models either use a smaller base LLM (Janus, DeepSeek 1.3B) or do not report understanding scores (Transfusion). Relative to GPT-4V, MetaMorph is competitive on MMBench (75.2 vs. 75.8) and SEED (71.8 vs. 69.1) but lags significantly on OCR/knowledge-heavy tasks like ChartQA (37.1 vs. 78.5) and TextVQA (60.5 vs. 78.0).

## Relation to Prior Work

VPiT extends visual instruction tuning (LLaVA) by adding a vision head that predicts continuous visual tokens alongside discrete text tokens, using the same next-token prediction paradigm without architectural changes. It departs sharply from prior unified models (LWM, Show-o, Chameleon, EMU-3, Janus, Transfusion) that require billions of image-text pairs for extensive pretraining or heavy architectural modifications (discrete tokenization, diffusion objectives, decoupled modes). For mapping predicted tokens to pixels, the paper repurposes the Diffusion Autoencoder concept, finetuning a latent diffusion model to condition on vision encoder embeddings rather than learning a new decoder from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that visual generation emerges as a byproduct of visual understanding—and that understanding data contributes disproportionately more than generation data—offers a practical recipe for building unified models with far less generation data than prior approaches. However, MetaMorph's generation quality (FID 11.8 on COCO) remains behind dedicated T2I systems (Stable Diffusion 1.5 at 9.6, Imagen at 7.3), and the implicit reasoning demonstrations, while compelling, are limited to a small set of hand-crafted puzzles. Open questions include whether this data-efficiency advantage persists at larger scales, whether the asymmetry holds for higher-fidelity generation, and whether the approach generalizes to video generation or other modalities.
