## Problem

Existing multi-plane panorama generation methods rely on field-of-view (FoV) overlapping to reduce seam artifacts, but this introduces computational redundancy, reduces effective resolution, and causes depth ambiguity in non-image domains like Z-depth and latent space. The paper asks whether seam inconsistencies in multi-plane generation can be resolved without FoV overlapping, and identifies the root cause as translational inequivalence of standard neural operators in the omnidirectional domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251961/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single-view RGB-D image (one cubemap face at 512×512) and multi-view text prompts as conditions, producing a complete 6-face RGB-D cubemap (512×512×6) with Z-depth (not Euclidean depth). The conditional face is kept noise-free throughout the diffusion process, while the other five faces are jointly denoised. The generated RGB-D cubemap can be directly lifted into a 3D Gaussian scene, and the multi-plane synchronization component can also be applied standalone to existing depth estimators like Marigold for panoramic depth estimation.

## Method

The core contribution is multi-plane synchronization, which adapts three spatial operators in a U-Net-based diffusion model to be translation-equivalent across cubemap faces: (1) synced self-attention reshapes tokens from (BM)×(HW)×C to B×(MHW)×C so attention operates across all six faces simultaneously; (2) synced 2D convolutions replace zero-padding at face boundaries with geometrically projected pixels from adjacent faces; and (3) synced group normalization computes statistics globally across all planes rather than per-face. On top of this, DreamCube jointly models RGB and depth by concatenating both latent streams with XYZ-based omnidirectional positional encodings (projecting each pixel onto a unit sphere) and a binary mask, trained with a v-prediction objective. The model uses Z-depth instead of Euclidean depth for better alignment with 2D diffusion priors, and employs a depth rescaling strategy (scaling conditional depth to [-s,+s] with s<1) to prevent generated depths from exceeding the diffusion's in-domain range.

## Results

On the in-domain Structured3D dataset, DreamCube achieves a FID of 12.58 and IS of 5.50 for RGB panorama generation, outperforming all baselines (best competitor: PanoDiffusion at FID 16.20). On the out-of-domain SUN360 dataset, DreamCube reaches FID 66.56 and IS 5.35, again ranking first overall (average rank 1.8). For depth panorama generation, DreamCube achieves δ-1.25 of 0.787, AbsRel of 0.133, RMSE of 0.256, and MAE of 0.211, surpassing the strongest depth baseline DAC (δ-1.25 0.751, AbsRel 0.139) on all four metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamCube outperforms all seven RGB panorama baselines on FID across both datasets, with the largest margins over OmniDreamer (FID 12.58 vs. 97.46 on Structured3D) and PanoDiffusion (12.58 vs. 16.20). On Inception Score, DreamCube (5.50) trails PanFusion (6.18) and LDM3D-Pano (6.13) on Structured3D but leads on SUN360 (5.35 vs. 4.98), yielding the best overall average rank of 1.8. Notably, Diffusion360 and OmniDreamer include SUN360 in their training data, yet DreamCube still achieves a lower FID (66.56 vs. 63.03 and 128.17) and higher IS on that out-of-domain set. For depth generation, DreamCube beats DAC (the strongest depth baseline) by 0.036 on δ-1.25 and 0.006 on AbsRel, and also surpasses equirectangular-based RGB-D methods LDM3D-Pano and PanoDiffusion on all four depth metrics. Qualitatively, DreamCube also outperforms Depth Anywhere in maintaining continuity at left-right seams, where Depth Anywhere exhibits visible discontinuities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the cubemap-based multi-plane generation paradigm introduced by CubeDiff and the depth estimation repurposing of Marigold, but departs from both by eliminating the FoV-overlapping strategy that prior multi-plane methods (CubeDiff, MVDiffusion) relied on to mitigate seam artifacts. The key conceptual departure is identifying that seam inconsistencies stem from the translational inequivalence of standard neural operators (self-attention, 2D convolution, group normalization) in the omnidirectional domain, rather than from the generation process itself. By proposing multi-plane synchronization—a unified adaptation of these three operators—the paper subsumes previously scattered techniques (flattened attention, projective resampling, tiled normalization) into a single systematic framework that requires no fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-plane synchronization is a general, plug-and-play technique demonstrated across multiple pretrained models (SD2, SDXL, Marigold), making it immediately applicable to any future 2D diffusion model for multi-plane tasks beyond panoramas. The shift from Euclidean to Z-depth for panoramic geometry modeling addresses a practical domain-gap issue that has limited prior RGB-D panorama methods. Open questions include scalability to higher resolutions and longer sequences (video panoramas), whether the approach extends to other multi-plane representations (e.g., spherical tiles), and how well the generated geometry supports downstream 3D reconstruction beyond simple Gaussian lifting.
