## Problem

Existing reference segmentation methods (FSS, CD-FSS) predominantly rely on meta-learning paradigms that require extensive episodic meta-training on massive datasets, incurring significant data and computational overhead. The paper asks whether the inherent correspondence between reference-target image pairs can be directly exploited by interactive video object segmentation (iVOS) models like SAM2, bypassing meta-training entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251340/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image with its pixel-level mask (1-shot or 5-shot setting) and an unlabeled target image, producing a binary segmentation mask for the target. Unusually, the reference-target pair is not processed as two independent images but is expanded into a 9-frame pseudo video sequence via diffusion-based interpolation, and the reference mask is further augmented to generate additional prompts. The output mask is derived from SAM2's spatio-temporal mask prediction over this constructed sequence, with the final target segmentation read from the last frame.

## Method

CAV-SAM reframes reference segmentation as video object segmentation by constructing a pseudo video from the reference-target pair. The Diffusion-Based Semantic Transition (DBST) module, built on DiffMorpher, generates intermediate frames by linearly interpolating LoRA parameters fitted to each image and spherically interpolating their DDIM-inverted latent noises, producing a smooth semantic transition sequence that bridges intra-class semantic discrepancies. The Test-Time Geometric Alignment (TTGA) module then performs lightweight fine-tuning of only SAM2's FPN neck using the single reference image and an augmented version, trained with an Augmentative Cyclic Consistency (ACC) loss that enforces the prototype vector to correctly segment both the original and augmented reference. The refined prototype vector activates all pseudo video frames to produce pseudo-labels, which are fed as additional mask prompts to SAM2 on the first half of the sequence, enabling the iVOS model to track geometric variations across the transition and produce the final target segmentation.

## Results

CAV-SAM achieves an average mIoU of 64.06 on the 1-shot CD-FSS benchmark (averaged over FSS-1000, DeepGlobe, ISIC2018, and Chest X-ray), representing approximately a 5% improvement over the strongest SOTA meta-learning-based methods. The concatenation baseline (SAM2 with reference-target images simply concatenated, no DBST or TTGA) already reaches 60.68 mIoU, demonstrating the inherent potential of iVOS for reference segmentation. Adding DBST lifts performance to 62.68, and the full CAV-SAM (DBST + TTGA) reaches 64.06. The largest gains are observed on the Chest X-ray dataset, while DeepGlobe (region-level segmentation) remains the weakest dataset for this object-segmentation-oriented approach.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAV-SAM outperforms all 12 compared baselines by approximately 5% in average mIoU on the 1-shot CD-FSS benchmark, without requiring any meta-training. The strongest meta-learning CD-FSS baselines (IFA [27], DR-Adaptor [35], Adapt-Before-Compare [11]) and SAM-based reference segmentation methods (APSeg [10], VRP-SAM [36]) all rely on extensive episodic meta-training on large datasets, whereas CAV-SAM uses only a single reference image for lightweight test-time fine-tuning of the FPN neck. Among traditional FSS methods (HPA [4], Base&Meta [18], IPMT [22], HDCD [29]), CAV-SAM also leads, confirming that the iVOS-as-video paradigm surpasses prototype- and matching-based approaches. The one notable exception is DeepGlobe, where CAV-SAM does not achieve the best result, likely because SAM2 is designed for object-level segmentation while DeepGlobe requires region-level segmentation. The ablation further shows that even the simple concatenation baseline (60.68) already rivals SOTA CD-FSS methods, and heuristic alternatives (mixup: 52.21, affine: 56.84) actually underperform the baseline, validating the need for the diffusion-based semantic transition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant meta-learning paradigm in reference segmentation (FSS, CD-FSS) by repurposing SAM2's interactive video object segmentation capability to treat reference-target image pairs as a pseudo video sequence. It builds directly on SAM2 (Ravi et al., 2024) as the iVOS backbone and on DiffMorpher (Zhang et al., CVPR 2024) for diffusion-based semantic interpolation, while adapting prototype-vector-based segmentation (masked average pooling, Otsu thresholding) from classical FSS methods into a test-time adaptation loop. The key conceptual departure is that instead of learning class-agnostic segmentation through extensive episodic meta-training, the method exploits the temporal tracking inductive bias of video segmentation models to capture intra-class correspondence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for large-scale meta-training, CAV-SAM offers a practical pathway to adapt large vision models to novel domains with only a single annotated reference image, reducing both data and compute requirements substantially. The modular design (any iVOS model + any diffusion model) suggests broad applicability, though the diffusion-based generation step introduces non-trivial inference latency that limits real-time deployment. An open question is whether the pseudo-video framing generalizes beyond object-level segmentation to region or part-level tasks, as evidenced by the weaker results on DeepGlobe. The finding that simple concatenation of reference-target images already approaches SOTA performance raises the question of how much of the gain truly comes from the diffusion-based transitions versus the iVOS model's inherent robustness.
