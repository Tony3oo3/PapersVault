## Problem

Existing 6D pose estimation methods extract features directly from RGB images via CNNs, making them highly sensitive to environmental variations such as illumination changes, background clutter, and occlusion, which severely limits generalization to unseen scenes. Self-supervised approaches partially address the synthetic-to-real domain gap but still cannot fully account for the extensive real-world variability in uncontrolled environments. No prior work has explored generating environment-independent object representations as an intermediate step for pose estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252335/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single RGB image of an object in an arbitrary environment (cropped by a YOLOv4 detector) and produces a 6D pose consisting of a rotation quaternion and a 3D translation vector (decomposed via SITE into 2D center offset and depth). Internally, the pipeline generates a 64×64×3 latent representation of the object free from environmental factors, which is then decoded by a pose head. During training, a pre-rendered pure object image (same pose, blank background, no occlusion) is required to supervise the VAE and diffusion model, but this is not needed at test time.

## Method

EA6D replaces direct feature extraction with a generative paradigm: a VAE encodes pure object images into latent representations, and an Environment Decoupling Diffusion Model (EDDM) recovers these representations from a noisy mixture of the input image's latent code and Gaussian noise. The key innovation is the forward diffusion process, which transitions from the clean object latent g₀ toward the input image latent r₀ (rather than toward zero), introducing an explicit 'environment decoupling gradient' (r₀ − g₀) that guides the model to strip away environmental factors. The reversed process starts from g₁ = r₀ + n instead of pure noise, enabling accurate generation in as few as one step. The EDDM uses a U-Net with one encoder and two decoders, with a pre-trained Swin Transformer providing multi-level conditional features from the input image via attention-based fusion. A separate pose decoder (ResNet + rotation/translation heads) is trained on the generated latent representations, and the entire system is trained exclusively on synthetic PBR data in three sequential stages (VAE → EDDM → pose decoder).

## Results

On Linemod, EA6D achieves 96.3% ADD(-S) recall using only synthetic training data, surpassing the best fully-supervised method (SO-Pose, 96.0%) and the best synthetic-only self-supervised method (RKHSPose, 78.2%) by 18.1%. On Linemod-Occluded, EA6D reaches 87.8% ADD(-S), exceeding the previous synthetic-only SOTA (RKHSPose, 54.3%) by 33.5% and all fully-supervised methods. On YCB-V, EA6D attains 93.1% ADD-S AUC and 88.3% ADD(-S) AUC, outperforming all baselines. On the self-made DiverseScenes dataset (unseen environments), EA6D achieves 83.8% mean ADD(-S), leading the next-best method (TexPose, 72.3%) by 11.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EA6D, trained exclusively on synthetic PBR data, outperforms all 16 baselines across every dataset and metric. Against fully-supervised methods (R+S), EA6D exceeds SO-Pose (96.0%) on Linemod and surpasses 6D-Diff (79.6%), CheckerPose (77.5%), and ZebraPose (76.9%) on Linemod-Occluded by 8.2–10.9%. Among self-supervised methods requiring unannotated real images (S+R⁻), EA6D beats TexPose (91.7%) and SMOC-Net (91.3%) on Linemod, and TexPose (66.7%) on Linemod-Occluded by 21.1%. Against methods using depth (S+R⁻+D), EA6D surpasses RKHSPose (95.9%) on Linemod and (68.7%) on Linemod-Occluded by 19.1%. Most strikingly, compared to synthetic-only baselines, EA6D improves over RKHSPose (78.2%) by 18.1% on Linemod and (54.3%) by 33.5% on Linemod-Occluded, and over Self6D++ (77.4%) by 18.9% on Linemod. On the unseen-environment DiverseScenes dataset, EA6D (83.8%) leads TexPose (72.3%), GDR-Net (64.5%), and Self6D++ (59.2%) by 11.5–24.6%, confirming superior generalization to novel backgrounds and lighting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the decoupled diffusion model (DDM) framework [15] by reformulating the forward process as an environment-decoupling gradient (r₀ − g₀) rather than a simple image-to-noise transition, and by initializing the reverse process with the input image's latent representation plus noise instead of pure noise. It departs from the self-supervised 6D pose estimation line (Self6D, TexPose, SMOC-Net) that attempts to close the synthetic-to-real domain gap via unannotated real images, instead proposing to eliminate the gap entirely by generating environment-independent object representations via a VAE-encoded latent space. The pose decoding head reuses the SITE translation parameterization from CDPN [22], and the conditional encoder leverages a pre-trained Swin Transformer, but the core architectural novelty lies in the EDDM's dual-decoder U-Net that separates object identity from environmental context during denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central claim—that a model trained exclusively on synthetic PBR renders can outperform methods trained on real+synthetic+depth data by 18–34% on standard benchmarks—challenges the prevailing assumption that the domain gap must be bridged rather than circumvented. This opens a path toward pose estimation in truly uncontrolled environments (AR, autonomous driving) without collecting scene-specific real data. Key open questions include: whether the approach scales to textureless or highly deformable objects, the practical cost of requiring CAD models for every target object, and whether the 1-step inference (0.23 s) remains stable under extreme occlusion or multi-object clutter beyond the tested scenarios. The self-made DiverseScenes dataset, while useful, is small (1,950 images) and evaluated against re-implemented baselines, leaving some uncertainty about the robustness of the generalization claims.
