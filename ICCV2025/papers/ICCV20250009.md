## Problem

Polyp segmentation in colonoscopy requires accurate boundary detection across highly variable polyp sizes, yet fully supervised methods demand extensive expert annotation and suffer from domain shifts. While SAM2 offers strong zero-shot segmentation, its reliance on manual prompts makes it impractical for clinical automation. Existing one-shot SAM-based methods (PerSAM, Matcher, ProtoSAM) further struggle with morphological size variations and employ rigid prompting strategies—either a single point or all points at once—that fail to balance prompt sufficiency against noise.

## Input / Output

The method requires only a single support image with its binary polyp mask (one-shot) and an arbitrary query colonoscopy image, both processed as RGB. The support image is internally augmented by cutting out the polyp, resizing it to extra-large and extra-small scales, and pasting it back with inpainting to fill gaps. DINOv2 (ViT-L/14) encodes features at 560×560 resolution while SAM2 (hiera-large) generates masks at 1024×1024. The output is a binary polyp segmentation mask for the query image, produced entirely without any training or fine-tuning.

## Method

OP-SAM is a training-free pipeline with three modules. First, Correlation-based Prior Generation (CPG) transfers semantic labels from support to query via patch-level cross-correlation of frozen DINOv2 features, then refines the coarse prior through iterative multiplication with a Sinkhorn-normalized self-correlation matrix extracted from DINOv2's last attention block (ρ rounds). Second, Scale-cascaded Prior Fusion (SPF) generates three priors from original, zoomed-in, and zoomed-out support images, evaluates each via a reverse-transfer mechanism that projects query features back onto the support image and computes confidence-weighted IoU (cIoU), then adaptively fuses them with normalized cIoU weights to suppress noisy priors. Third, Euclidean Prompt Evolution (EPE) iteratively prompts SAM2 by computing the Euclidean Distance Transform on the uncovered prior region to locate the geometrically central prompt point, evaluating coverage and IoU after each round, expanding from tight to loose prior thresholds when coverage is insufficient, and injecting negative prompts to suppress over-segmentation—maintaining a cumulative prompt list that evolves across rounds until convergence or a five-round cap.

## Results

On Kvasir, OP-SAM achieves 76.93% IoU and 84.53% Dice, surpassing the best baseline (Matcher, 65.49% IoU) by 11.44% and even exceeding the Oracle baseline (71.88% IoU) that uses ground-truth prompt points. Across the four PolypGen centers, OP-SAM consistently leads with IoU ranging from 68.65% to 71.44%, outperforming Matcher by 3.6–6.4 points. On CVC-ClinicDB and CVC-ColonDB, OP-SAM reaches 67.75% and 60.81% IoU respectively, maintaining a 5–6 point advantage over the strongest competitor. On the challenging Kvasir-H (extreme-size polyps) and Piccolo datasets, OP-SAM improves over the second-best method by 10.26% and 6.05% IoU respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OP-SAM outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and metric. The largest margin is on Kvasir, where OP-SAM (76.93% IoU) beats Matcher (65.49%) by 11.44 points and ProtoSAM (64.27%) by 12.66 points; it also surpasses the Oracle baseline (71.88%) that randomly samples three points from ground-truth masks, demonstrating that iterative prompt evolution is more effective than even oracle-level point placement. On PolypGen, OP-SAM leads Matcher by 3.6–6.4 IoU points across the three centers, while remaining within 0.5–6.8 points of the Oracle, indicating near-oracle prior quality. On CVC-ClinicDB and CVC-ColonDB, OP-SAM maintains a consistent 5–6 IoU advantage over Matcher and ProtoSAM. On the challenging Kvasir-H dataset (extreme-size polyps), OP-SAM (57.31% IoU) exceeds the second-best Matcher (33.23%) by 24 points, and on Piccolo (flat, infiltrative polyps) it leads Matcher (59.43%) by 6.05 points. The cross-domain few-shot methods (SpecIFA, SegGPT) and training-free prompt methods (EviPrompt, SEGIC) are substantially outperformed, with gaps exceeding 30 IoU points on most datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SAM2 as a frozen segmentation backbone and DINOv2 as a frozen feature encoder, positioning itself within the one-shot SAM-based segmentation lineage (PerSAM, Matcher, ProtoSAM). It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) replacing rigid single-shot prompt placement with an iterative, feedback-driven prompt evolution loop (EPE) that mimics SAM's interactive training paradigm, and (2) introducing scale-cascaded prior fusion with a reverse-transfer quality assessment to handle polyp size variability, rather than relying on a single support image at fixed scale. The self-correlation refinement step draws on the CLIP-based segmentation insight from Lin et al. [15], adapted here to DINOv2's attention maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, single-annotation requirement makes this framework practically deployable in clinical settings where large-scale polyp annotation is infeasible and manual prompting per image is impractical. The finding that iterative prompting with EDT-based point selection outperforms even an oracle that randomly samples three points from ground truth suggests that prompt *strategy* matters more than prompt *access*, a principle likely transferable to other SAM-based medical segmentation tasks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond polyps to other endoscopic or histopathological segmentation tasks, whether the iterative loop's computational cost is acceptable for real-time colonoscopy, and whether the method's reliance on DINOv2's self-correlation structure is robust to domain shifts beyond those tested.
