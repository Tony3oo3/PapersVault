## Problem

In Single Positive Multi-Label Learning (SPML), each image carries only one confirmed positive label, and the standard Assume Negative assumption treats all other labels as negative, producing many false negatives. Existing pseudo-labeling strategies either rely on fixed per-image pseudo-label vectors that cannot recover from initial misclassifications, or introduce additional noise that prior loss functions do not adequately mitigate. The paper addresses both gaps: designing a loss that robustly learns from noisy external pseudo-labels and a dynamic pseudo-labeling scheme that re-evaluates each image across epochs.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 448×448) with a single confirmed positive label as training input, and additionally feeds the image (globally and as overlapping augmented patches) plus per-class text prompts (e.g., 'a photo of a {class}') into a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 encoder to generate pseudo-labels. The trainable ResNet-50 backbone outputs a C-dimensional probability vector via sigmoid activation, where C ranges from 20 (VOC) to 312 (CUB). Pseudo-labels are a ternary vector l ∈ {−1, 0, 1}^C that changes every epoch, distinguishing confirmed positives, model-assumed negatives, CLIP-confirmed negatives, and CLIP-suggested positives.

## Method

The AEVLP framework combines two components. First, the GPR Loss generalizes the GR Loss by introducing four distinct loss-and-weight pairs for the four label states (confirmed positive, undefined, pseudo-negative, pseudo-positive), where the pseudo-positive term uses a softened BCE with a bounded Gaussian weight v⁴(p;α) to temper overfitting to noisy labels, and a regularization term R constrains the expected number of positive predictions. Theorem 4.1 proves GPR reduces to GR when pseudo-label confidence vanishes. Second, DAMP generates dynamic pseudo-labels by: (a) enhancing CLIP text embeddings with a frozen GCN that encodes label-to-label similarity, (b) partitioning each image into overlapping patches with random augmentation to produce multiple local views, (c) aggregating local CLIP scores via a max/min rule gated by a threshold tied to the confirmed positive's global score, and (d) assigning positive pseudo-labels from the top-k of the averaged global+aggregated scores and negative pseudo-labels from the lowest Δ_neg% of averaged scores. Because patches and augmentations are resampled each epoch, the pseudo-label vector for each image varies over training, increasing coverage of true positives while GPR Loss absorbs the residual noise.

## Results

AEVLP achieves state-of-the-art mAP on all four SPML benchmarks: 90.46 on VOC, 73.54 on COCO, 50.70 on NUS-WIDE, and 24.89 on CUB-200-2011. Notably, on VOC it surpasses the fully-labeled BCE baseline (89.42) despite using only one positive label per image. The method uses a smaller CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone (149.62M params) while outperforming VLPL, which relies on the larger ViT-L/16 (428M params). On the most challenging CUB dataset (312 labels), AEVLP improves over GR Loss by 3.25 mAP points and over VLPL by 0.87 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AEVLP outperforms all eight compared baselines across all four datasets. Against the foundational Assume Negative and ROLE methods [5], AEVLP gains 4.57 and 2.69 mAP on VOC respectively, with even larger margins on CUB (6.58 and 11.23). Compared to the entropy-based EM/EM+APL [34], AEVLP leads by 1.27–1.51 mAP on VOC/COCO and by 3.86–4.05 on CUB. Against the recent loss-design methods BoostLU+LL-R [14] and MINE [19], AEVLP improves by 1.17–5.09 mAP depending on dataset. The closest competitor is VLPL [27], which also leverages CLIP pseudo-labels; AEVLP surpasses it by 1.36 mAP on VOC, 2.09 on COCO, 1.15 on NUS, and 0.87 on CUB, while using a substantially smaller CLIP backbone. GR Loss [2], the direct predecessor that AEVLP generalizes, is beaten by 0.63 mAP on VOC and 3.25 on CUB. The LL-Ct pseudo-labeling strategy [13] benefits from GPR Loss (57.32 vs 56.98 average mAP in Table 2), confirming the loss's generality across different pseudo-label sources.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GR Loss framework (Chen et al., IJCAI 2024), which handles noise from the Assume Negative assumption in SPML, by generalizing it to incorporate external pseudo-labels from any method, with a formal theorem showing GPR reduces to GR under specific conditions. For pseudo-label generation, it builds on the CLIP-driven aggregation strategy from CDUL (Abdelfattah et al., ICCV 2023) but departs from VLPL (Ding et al., CVPRW 2024) by replacing fixed per-image pseudo-labels with a dynamic, multi-focus approach that re-samples local patches each epoch. The overall positioning is a unification of the loss-design line (GR Loss) and the vision-language pseudo-labeling line (VLPL) into a single framework, AEVLP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework achieves SOTA on all four standard SPML benchmarks and notably surpasses fully-labeled multi-label classification on VOC, suggesting that external vision-language knowledge can compensate for the information lost in single-positive annotation. The GPR Loss's generality—consistently improving mAP when paired with any pseudo-labeling strategy (random, VLPL, LL-Ct, DAMP)—makes it a drop-in robustness layer for future SPML methods. Open questions include whether the dynamic multi-focus pseudo-labeling scales efficiently to very large label spaces (beyond CUB's 312 labels), whether the approach generalizes to non-CLIP vision-language models, and how sensitive performance is to the grid size and patch overlap hyperparameters in practice.
