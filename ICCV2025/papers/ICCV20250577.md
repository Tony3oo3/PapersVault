## Problem

Existing protective perturbation methods (anti-customization) inject adversarial noise to distort fine-tuned diffusion model outputs, but diffusion-based purification can remove these perturbations before fine-tuning, rendering protection ineffective in the Purification-Customization (P-C) workflow. This paper formalizes the anti-purification task—designing perturbations that survive purification—and identifies three core challenges that make it harder than anti-customization: the purification UNet lacks vulnerable components, its parameters are frozen and training-free, and high-timestep denoising locks in global structure.

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean 512×512 RGB image and a frozen unconditional DDPM purification model (e.g., GrIDPure with 2 rounds × 20 iterations at t^p=10) as inputs, and produces an imperceptible l∞-bounded perturbation δ^adv that, when added to the image, persists through purification and degrades subsequent DreamBooth or LoRA fine-tuning outputs. The perturbation is generated via PGD over the purification model's denoising pipeline, with attack timesteps constrained to [1, t^p] to align with the purification schedule. Evaluation uses FID, ISM (ArcFace cosine similarity), BRISQUE, and LPIPS to measure output distortion and perceptual imperceptibility respectively.

## Method

AntiPure maximizes a composite PGD loss L_pgd = E[L_ddpm + λ₁·e^{ᾱ_t−1}·L_fre + λ₂·e^{L_err-t}] against the frozen purification UNet. Patch-wise Frequency Guidance (L_fre) unfolds the UNet's predicted clean image x̂₀ into s×s patches, applies DCT to each, and sigmoid-normalizes the mean of the bottom-right (high-frequency) quarters, thereby amplifying high-frequency components where purification exerts weaker control while preserving low-frequency semantic structure. Erroneous Timestep Guidance (L_err-t) minimizes the L2 distance between the UNet's noise prediction at the correct timestep t and at a fixed erroneous timestep t_err, forcing the model to produce similar outputs regardless of timestep input and disrupting its ability to select appropriate denoising actions. The exponential scaling e^{ᾱ_t−1} on L_fre increases its influence at lower timesteps, while e^{L_err-t} provides more aggressive optimization given the small MSE values. This design targets the purification model directly rather than trying to preserve anti-customization perturbations through purification, exploiting the fact that frozen DDPM parameters prioritize low-frequency priors and cannot finely control high-frequency details.

## Results

On the P-C workflow (GrIDPure purification followed by DreamBooth fine-tuning), AntiPure achieves the highest FID (81.15 on CelebA-HQ, 90.77 on VGGFace2), lowest ISM (0.6112 and 0.5475), and highest BRISQUE (43.60 and 46.01) among all five methods, indicating the strongest post-purification distortion. AntiPure also attains the lowest LPIPS (0.1392 Alex / 0.2843 VGG on CelebA-HQ; 0.1758 Alex / 0.3884 VGG on VGGFace2), confirming the most imperceptible perturbation. Notably, AntiPure's effectiveness *increases* with more purification iterations (FID rises from 54.45 at 10 iterations to 78.21 at 40), whereas Anti-DB's degrades (FID drops from 124.62 to 77.30).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AntiPure outperforms all four baselines (AdvDM, Mist, Anti-DB, SimAC) on every metric (FID, ISM, BRISQUE) across both CelebA-HQ and VGGFace2 under the P-C workflow. The most notable gap is in ISM: on VGGFace2, AntiPure reduces identity similarity to 0.5475 versus 0.5923–0.6053 for the baselines, a 4–10% relative improvement. Anti-DB, the strongest prior method, achieves FID 90.29 on VGGFace2 but its ISM (0.5938) remains substantially higher than AntiPure's (0.5475), and its BRISQUE (38.35) is 7.66 points lower. SimAC shows the largest performance drop after purification (FID 67.37 on CelebA-HQ, the lowest of all methods), attributed to its smoother perturbation edges being more easily removed. In the purification-iteration ablation (Table 3), AntiPure uniquely *gains* robustness as iterations increase (FID 54.45→78.21 from 10→40 iterations), while Anti-DB's FID drops from 124.62 to 77.30, confirming that AntiPure's anti-purification design is fundamentally different from anti-customization approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the protective perturbation / anti-customization line of work (AdvDM, Mist, Anti-DB, SimAC) by identifying a critical gap: existing methods target the customization model (SD/DreamBooth/LoRA) but are nullified when diffusion-based purification (DiffPure, GrIDPure) is applied before fine-tuning. Rather than making perturbations survive purification, AntiPure reframes the problem as a direct attack on the purification model itself (a frozen DDPM), introducing two novel guidance losses—Patch-wise Frequency Guidance and Erroneous Timestep Guidance—specifically designed to exploit the purification model's limited control over high-frequency components and its sensitivity to timestep inputs. The method builds on PGD as the optimization backbone and GrIDPure as the representative purification setting, but the conceptual target shifts from LDM fine-tuning to DDPM denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The P-C workflow (purify-then-customize) is a realistic and increasingly common pipeline, yet prior protective perturbations were largely ineffective under it; this paper provides the first systematic formalization and a working method for that setting. The characterization of why anti-purification is harder than anti-customization (no vulnerable encoder, frozen parameters, fixed high-timestep denoising) gives the field a clearer map of where purification is genuinely robust versus where it remains exploitable. Key limitations include the white-box assumption, evaluation restricted to face datasets (CelebA-HQ, VGGFace2), and the method's self-described role as a 'diagnostic' stress test rather than a deployment-ready defense. Open questions include black-box transferability, generalization to non-face content and other purification variants, and whether the frequency-domain attack strategy extends to latent-space purification.
