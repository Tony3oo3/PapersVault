## Problem

Multi-task learning suffers from task conflicts where gradients of different tasks diverge in direction or magnitude, degrading performance relative to single-task baselines. While sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) empirically mitigates these conflicts by flattening the loss landscape, integrating SAM into MTL raises two unresolved questions: how to combine global (average-loss) and local (task-specific) gradient information in the perturbation, and how to avoid the O(K) additional backpropagations that task-specific SAM incurs.

## Input / Output

SAMO operates on standard multi-task training data (RGB images for Cityscapes/NYU-v2/CelebA/Office-Home, molecular graphs for QM9) and takes as input the current model parameters, the K task loss functions, and a tunable weight alpha balancing global vs. local perturbation. The output is an updated parameter vector that, when applied to the shared backbone, yields task-specific predictions (segmentation masks, depth maps, surface normals, class labels, or molecular properties). Unusually, the local task gradients needed for perturbation are never computed via backpropagation; instead they are estimated from paired forward-pass evaluations at theta plus/minus mu*z, then rescaled layerwise to match the global gradient magnitude.

## Method

SAMO introduces a joint global-local perturbation that blends the average-loss gradient with each task's gradient via a weighted combination, capturing both shared positive transfer and task-specific variation in a single perturbation direction. To avoid K extra backward passes, the task-specific gradients are approximated via the SPSA zeroth-order estimator using only forward passes, requiring 2K additional forward evaluations per step. A novel layerwise normalization then rescales each estimated local gradient to match the norm of the corresponding layer's global gradient, stabilizing the high-variance zeroth-order estimate. The SAMO gradient is obtained by evaluating the task loss gradient at the perturbed point, and the final update is produced by plugging all K SAMO gradients into any existing gradient-manipulation MTL method (LS, MGDA, FairGrad). The total overhead is C_b + 2K*C_f, matching the cost of global-only SAM and far below F-MTL's K*C_b + C_gm.

## Results

On Cityscapes (2-task), SAMO-FairGrad achieves a Δm% of −0.62, improving over FairGrad (3.90) and F-MTL (0.67), and outperforming all other baselines including Nash-MTL (6.82) and MoCo (9.90). On NYU-v2 (3-task), SAMO-FairGrad reaches −6.55 Δm%, surpassing FairGrad (−4.96) and F-MTL (−4.77). On CelebA (40-task), SAMO-FairGrad achieves −0.74 Δm% versus FairGrad's 0.37, and on QM9 (11-task) it reaches 53.0 versus FairGrad's 57.9. On Office-Home (4-task, multi-input), SAMO-FairGrad attains 77.66% average accuracy, the best among all tested methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAMO-FairGrad consistently outperforms all 13 baselines across every dataset. On Cityscapes, it improves Δm% from 3.90 (FairGrad) to −0.62, a 4.5-point gain, and beats F-MTL (0.67) by 1.29 points; it also surpasses the next-best non-SAMO method Nash-MTL (6.82) by 7.4 points. On NYU-v2, SAMO-FairGrad (−6.55) edges out FairGrad (−4.96) by 1.59 points and F-MTL (−4.77) by 1.78 points. On CelebA, the improvement over FairGrad is from 0.37 to −0.74 (1.11 points), and on QM9 from 57.9 to 53.0 (4.9 points). Against gradient-manipulation baselines like PCGrad (18.29 on Cityscapes), CAGrad (11.64), and MGDA (44.14), SAMO-FairGrad achieves substantially lower Δm%, confirming that the sharpness-aware perturbation complements rather than replaces existing gradient balancing. Notably, SAMO also improves weaker base methods: SAMO-LS on Cityscapes (14.30) still trails SAMO-FairGrad but beats standalone LS (22.60) by 8.3 points, and SAMO-MGDA (4.30) improves over MGDA (44.14) by nearly 40 points, demonstrating broad applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAMO extends Sharpness-Aware Minimization (Foret et al., 2021) from single-task to multi-task optimization, departing from the only prior MTL-SAM work (F-MTL, Phan et al., 2022) by replacing per-task backpropagation with a joint global-local perturbation and a zeroth-order SPSA gradient estimator (Spall, 1992). Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of the gradient-manipulation MTL literature (MGDA, PCGrad, CAGrad, FairGrad, Nash-MTL) and the sharpness-aware optimization line, introducing a layerwise normalization strategy to stabilize the SPSA approximation. The key departure from F-MTL is treating the average-loss gradient as a shared base perturbation and augmenting it with cheaply estimated task-specific perturbations, rather than applying SAM independently to each task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAMO offers a practically important efficiency gain: it replaces K additional backpropagations (as in F-MTL) with forward-pass-only SPSA estimates, making sharpness-aware MTL feasible at scale without prohibitive memory overhead. The empirical demonstration that SAM flattens the loss landscape and increases task-gradient cosine similarity provides a useful mechanistic explanation for why sharpness-awareness helps MTL, beyond the standard generalization narrative. Open questions include whether the SPSA approximation quality degrades with very large model dimensions or task counts, and whether the layerwise normalization generalizes to architectures without clear layer boundaries (e.g., transformers). The authors note future directions toward sparsity and low-rank approximations for further scalability.
