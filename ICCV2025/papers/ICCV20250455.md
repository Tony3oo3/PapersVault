## Problem

Dense point tracking (equivalent to long-range optical flow) in a streaming, frame-by-frame setting remains challenging: classical optical flow models suffer from appearance drifting without temporal consistency, while recent point tracking methods rely on sliding windows with offline processing that are slow and less effective for long-range propagation. The paper asks whether both high accuracy and efficiency can be achieved using only past observations (causal processing) without future frames.

## Input / Output

SPOT consumes a streaming video causally, processing each frame sequentially with access only to all prior frames {I_1, ..., I_t}. Internally it operates at 4× downsampled resolution (H/4 × W/4) and tracks all points in the first frame to the current frame, producing a dense long-range optical flow field (H×W×2) and a per-pixel visibility mask in [0,1]^{H×W}. At 512×512 resolution it runs at 12.4 FPS on a single H100 GPU with 4.15 GB memory, using only 8.7M parameters.

## Method

SPOT decomposes long-range information propagation into two efficient steps: (1) visibility-guided splatting forward-warps the first-frame feature onto recent-frame coordinates using the already-predicted flow, and (2) a cross-attention memory reading module retrieves relevant features from a small FIFO memory bank (length 3) to enhance the current frame via feature similarity. A fusion convolution layer inpaints disocclusion artifacts in the readout before feeding the enhanced feature into a standard RAFT-style flow decoder. The decoder performs N iterative GRU refinements and is augmented with a sensory memory (updated by an additional GRU on the final motion feature) that models short-term motion dynamics. The memory bank is updated each frame by appending the visibility-normalized splatting result as a new value entry, while the query feature of the current frame serves as the key, keeping the architecture lightweight and unifying optical flow and point tracking under one decoder.

## Results

On the CVO Extended benchmark (48-frame videos), SPOT achieves an EPE of 4.77 (all) / 3.58 (visible) / 6.96 (occluded) with 88.6% occlusion accuracy, outperforming the best online baseline DOT† (12.1 EPE) by 60.5% and even beating the best offline method DOT (5.12 EPE). On TAP-Vid DAVIS (First), SPOT reaches 61.5 AJ, surpassing the best online method Online TAPIR (56.2 AJ) by 9.4% and matching or exceeding offline methods like CoTracker2 (60.8 AJ). SPOT achieves all this with only 8.7M parameters (10× fewer than DOT's 56.5M) and runs at 12.4 FPS on 512×512 video on a single H100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPOT decisively outperforms all online baselines on CVO: it reduces EPE from 12.1 (DOT†) to 4.77 on Extended (60.5% reduction), and from 21.4 (MFT) to 4.77 (77.6% reduction). Against offline methods, SPOT beats CoTracker2 (5.45→4.77 EPE) and DOT (5.12→4.77) on CVO Extended, though DOT retains a slight OA edge (88.9 vs 88.6). On TAP-Vid, SPOT surpasses the best online tracker Online TAPIR by 5.3 AJ on DAVIS (61.5 vs 56.2) and 11.3% relative AJ improvement on Kinetics (50.2 vs 49.6), while slightly trailing the offline TAPTR (63.0 AJ) on DAVIS First. SPOT also outperforms the chaining-based MFT and AccFlow by large margins on both CVO and TAP-Vid, confirming that memory-based propagation is more effective than flow chaining for long-range tracking. On RoboTAP, SPOT achieves 60.7 AJ vs. 57.9 for Online TAPIR and 61.5 for offline DOT, placing it competitively with offline methods while remaining fully causal.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPOT builds directly on the RAFT optical flow decoder as its regression backbone, unifying the architectures of optical flow and point tracking. It departs from the dominant sliding-window paradigm of recent sparse trackers (TAPIR, CoTracker2, DOT) and from chaining-based long-range flow methods (MFT, AccFlow) by introducing a streaming memory bank that stores first-frame features propagated via visibility-guided splatting. The two-step information propagation—splatting for long-range transfer followed by attention-based reading for short-range retrieval—is the core architectural departure, drawing on splatting from video frame interpolation and memory mechanisms from video object segmentation, but repurposed for dense pixel-level correspondence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPOT demonstrates that online (causal, frame-by-frame) dense point tracking can match or exceed offline methods on CVO while using 10× fewer parameters and running 2× faster, opening a practical path toward real-time dense motion perception for robotics and video understanding. The main limitation is the small memory bank (L=3), which means extreme long-duration occlusions degrade performance to pairwise-level accuracy; the authors suggest persistent object-level memory as a future direction. Broader open questions include whether the splatting-plus-attention propagation strategy generalizes to 3D tracking or to scenarios with extreme fast motion and texture-less regions, where the paper acknowledges failures.
