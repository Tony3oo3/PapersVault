## Problem

In weakly supervised affordance grounding, models trained without pixel-level annotations tend to latch onto class-specific discriminative patterns (e.g., bicycle frames for 'ride') rather than true affordance-relevant parts (e.g., seat or handlebars), because affordance cues are not always visually distinct and existing part-level distillation is applied only intermittently. This leaves training dominated by classification objectives that favor common but affordance-irrelevant features, particularly hurting performance on unseen objects.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes an egocentric image (object-focused, single view) paired with three exocentric images (third-person human-object interaction examples) and an action class text prompt; at inference, only the egocentric image and action class are required. The output is a class activation map (CAM) over the egocentric image localizing affordable parts, which is post-processed by a Hadamard product with a binarized CLIP-derived object affinity map to suppress Gaussian-like activation spread beyond object boundaries. DINO ViT-S/16 and CLIP ViT-B/16 are used as the visual and text encoders respectively.

## Method

The core contribution is a selective contrastive learning framework that adaptively operates at object or part granularity depending on clue reliability. First, CLIP (via ClearCLIP) generates object affinity maps in both egocentric and exocentric views to identify action-associated objects; part clues in exocentric images are extracted by refining LOCATE's K-means-based segmentation with object affinity filtering, while part clues in egocentric images are derived by exploiting CLIP's stronger response to prominent (larger-scale) objects. Prototypical contrastive learning then constructs positive/negative prototypes: when a reliable part is found, part prototypes serve as targets; otherwise, object prototypes are used, ensuring the model consistently distinguishes target objects from background. Pixel contrastive learning supplements this by binarizing egocentric object affinity maps using a threshold derived from exocentric activations, creating positive/negative pixel sets that enforce fine-grained separation of affordance-relevant from irrelevant pixels within each image. The total loss combines cross-entropy classification, prototypical contrastive loss, and pixel contrastive loss with equal weighting.

## Results

On AGD20K-Seen, the proposed method achieves KLD 1.124, SIM 0.433, and NSS 1.280, outperforming the strongest prior WSAG method (WSMA) by 0.052 KLD, 0.017 SIM, and 0.033 NSS. On the more challenging AGD20K-Unseen split, the gains are larger: KLD 1.243 vs. 1.335 (WSMA), SIM 0.405 vs. 0.382, and NSS 1.368 vs. 1.220. On HICO-IIF, the method reaches KLD 1.358, SIM 0.378, and NSS 1.231, surpassing WSMA (1.465 / 0.358 / 1.012) and LOCATE (1.593 / 0.327 / 0.966) across all three metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 13 baselines on every reported metric across both AGD20K (seen and unseen) and HICO-IIF. Against the closest WSAG competitors—WSMA (DINO+CLIP) and INTRA (DINov2+ALBEF+GPT-4)—the gains on AGD20K-Unseen are most pronounced: KLD drops from 1.335/1.365 to 1.243, SIM rises from 0.382/0.375 to 0.405, and NSS jumps from 1.220/1.209 to 1.368, indicating substantially better generalization to novel objects. Compared to LOCATE (the direct architectural predecessor), the method improves KLD by 0.162 on AGD20K-Unseen and by 0.235 on HICO-IIF, while also using a simpler backbone (DINO+CLIP vs. DINO alone for LOCATE). Against heavier multi-model pipelines such as WorldAfford (DINO+CLIP+SAM+GPT-4) and Rai et al. (DINO+CLIP+GPT-3.5T), the proposed approach achieves lower KLD and higher SIM/NSS without requiring SAM or any LLM, suggesting that the selective contrastive objectives capture affordance-relevant structure more effectively than prompt-based or segmentation-based part discovery. The largest absolute margin is over Cross-view-AG [28] on AGD20K-Seen (KLD 1.538→1.124, SIM 0.334→0.433), reflecting the benefit of part-level contrastive learning over pure cross-view distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the weakly supervised affordance grounding (WSAG) line initiated by Cross-view-AG and refined by LOCATE, which introduced part-level distillation from exocentric to egocentric views. The key conceptual departure is replacing pairwise distillation (used in LOCATE and WSMA) with prototypical contrastive learning that explicitly models negative relationships between prototypes, and adding a pixel-level contrastive objective for fine-grained localization. The object affinity map from ClearCLIP serves as a consistent grounding signal throughout, and the 'selective' mechanism adaptively switches between object-level and part-level supervision depending on clue reliability. Notably, the paper positions itself against recent foundation-model-heavy approaches (WorldAfford, AffordanceLLM, INTRA) that leverage LLMs/VLMs for part-level knowledge, claiming competitive or superior performance using only DINO and CLIP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental practical limitation in WSAG: when affordance-relevant parts are not visually distinguishable, training collapses to classification and the model latches onto class-specific but affordance-irrelevant details. The selective contrastive framework provides a principled solution by always offering a contrastive signal (object-level when parts are unreliable, part-level when they are), which is particularly valuable for unseen-object scenarios critical in real-world robotics. A notable practical implication is that a DINO+CLIP pipeline outperforms heavier DINO+CLIP+SAM+GPT-4 or DINov2+ALBEF+GPT-4 systems, suggesting that well-designed contrastive objectives may be more effective than simply adding foundation-model components. Open questions include whether the multi-threshold part discovery pipeline (γ₁, α, ρ, γ₂) generalizes to more diverse or cluttered scenes, and whether the approach extends to video-based or 3D affordance grounding.
