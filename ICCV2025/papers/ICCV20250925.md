## Problem

Existing HOT prediction methods rely solely on single-modality images, leading to over-segmentation in low-interaction areas and a failure to maintain category consistency within predicted regions (i.e., spurious classes appearing inside a correctly identified contact area). No prior work has leveraged textual or depth information to guide attention toward relevant body parts or to suppress irrelevant background regions around the human.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250925/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as primary input and internally generates auxiliary signals: 17 body-part text prompts encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder, a human mask from SAM, and a monocular depth map from ZoeDepth. The output is an 18-class HOT segmentation map (background + 17 body parts) at H/4 × W/4 resolution, matching the 4× downsampled ground-truth convention used by DHOT and PIHOT rather than producing full-resolution predictions.

## Method

P3HOT combines three mechanisms around a ResNet-50 encoder and a U-Net-style decoder. First, a semantic-driven prompt mechanism computes cosine similarity between the CLIP image embedding and 17 frozen text embeddings (one per body part), then multiplies the corresponding decoder channels by these similarity scores to bias attention toward likely contact regions. Second, a Human Proximal Perception (HPP) module computes each person's average depth from the ZoeDepth map and SAM mask, then uses a learnable threshold τ to construct a differentiable depth-range filter (via ReLU on a depth-interval indicator) that is downsampled and added to the decoder features, suppressing regions far from the human. Third, a Regional Joint Loss (RJLoss) penalizes intra-region category inconsistency through two terms: a Local Joint Loss that flags pixels where a non-GT class appears inside a GT class region, and a Global Joint Loss that uses connected-component analysis to detect and penalize other-class regions fully enclosed within a predicted class region. The decoder progressively fuses all four ResNet-50 block outputs during upsampling to recover fine texture, and the full loss combines cross-entropy, RJLoss, and a binary cross-entropy image-text alignment term.

## Results

On HOT-Annotated, P3HOT achieves SC-Acc. 46.0, mIoU 25.6, wIoU 30.2, and AD-Acc. 42.3, outperforming the strongest baseline PIHOT (45.3, 23.6, 28.6, 31.3) by +0.7, +2.0, +1.6, and +11.0 respectively. On HOT-Generated, P3HOT reaches SC-Acc. 35.2, mIoU 18.0, wIoU 23.1, and AD-Acc. 30.6, again leading PIHOT (34.9, 16.9, 21.2, 25.4) on all four metrics. The most substantial gain is in the newly proposed AD-Acc. metric, where P3HOT surpasses PIHOT by 11.0 points on HOT-Annotated and 5.2 points on HOT-Generated, reflecting better handling of negative (non-contact) regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

P3HOT outperforms PIHOT [26] on SC-Acc. (+0.7), mIoU (+2.0), wIoU (+1.6), and AD-Acc. (+11.0) on HOT-Annotated, and on all four metrics on HOT-Generated (SC-Acc. +0.3, mIoU +1.1, wIoU +1.9, AD-Acc. +5.2). However, P3HOT trails PIHOT on C-Acc. (74.9 vs. 80.7 on HOT-Annotated; 70.8 vs. 76.3 on HOT-Generated), a gap the authors attribute to C-Acc.'s known flaw of rewarding over-prediction of contact classes across the entire human body. Against DHOT (ResNet-50) Full-OF [5], P3HOT leads by +5.9 SC-Acc., +3.5 mIoU, +5.2 wIoU, and +11.3 AD-Acc. on HOT-Annotated, with even larger margins on HOT-Generated. The generic segmentation baselines (ResNet+UperNet, ResNet+PPM) are substantially weaker, confirming that task-specific HOT architectures are necessary. The authors argue that AD-Acc. is a more reliable indicator of true performance than C-Acc., and under this metric P3HOT's advantage over PIHOT is the most pronounced.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the HOT prediction task introduced by DHOT (Chen et al., CVPR 2023) by adding three orthogonal components: CLIP-based text prompt guidance (first multimodal HOT approach), a depth-aware Human Proximal Perception module using SAM masks and ZoeDepth, and a Regional Joint Loss for intra-region category consistency. It departs from PIHOT by avoiding occlusion-specific restoration networks in favor of a lighter depth-filtering strategy, and from DHOT by leveraging multi-scale encoder features in the decoder rather than relying solely on the final layer. The text-guided mechanism borrows the CLIP attention-pooling paradigm from vision-language pretraining but repurposes it as a channel-wise attention gate over 17 body-part prompts rather than for open-vocabulary classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of AD-Acc. as an evaluation metric addresses a genuine gap: the standard C-Acc. can be trivially maximized by predicting a single contact class over the entire image, making it unreliable for comparing methods. The text-guided HOT framework opens a path toward multi-modal contact understanding that could incorporate richer linguistic context in future work. However, the approach remains 2D and pseudo-3D (monocular depth), depends on three external pre-trained models (CLIP, SAM, ZoeDepth) that add inference overhead, and the gains on standard metrics (SC-Acc., mIoU, wIoU) over PIHOT are modest (0.7, 2.0, 1.6 points). Open questions include whether the text guidance generalizes beyond the fixed 17-part vocabulary and whether the RJLoss's connected-component logic scales efficiently to higher-resolution outputs.
