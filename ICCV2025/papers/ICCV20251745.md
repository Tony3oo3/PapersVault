## Problem

Scaling up motion generation to large multi-source datasets introduces severe distributional heterogeneity—differences in motion types, recording devices, and annotation styles cause optimizing for one dataset to degrade another. Additionally, the field lacks both a dedicated pretrained motion backbone (existing approaches repurpose LLMs with a significant domain gap) and a unified large-scale motion dataset with diverse textual annotations. GenM³ tackles all three gaps simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251745/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt (encoded by a CLIP text encoder into token embeddings and a global feature) and, for completion tasks, a partially observed motion sequence. Motion is discretized by MEVQ-VAE into a sequence of indices from an 8192-entry, 32-dimensional codebook at 4× downsampling, with all data standardized to 22 joints at 30 fps on a default SMPL skeleton. At inference, all motion tokens start as [Mask] and are generated via parallel decoding with iterative re-masking of low-confidence tokens, producing a full 3D joint-position sequence.

## Method

GenM³ comprises a Multi-Expert VQ-VAE (MEVQ-VAE) and a Multi-path Motion Transformer (MMT). MEVQ-VAE replaces standard 1D convolutions with layers where all experts fire simultaneously and their outputs are combined via learned per-expert weights, allowing a single shared codebook to accommodate heterogeneous dataset distributions. MMT uses an 18-layer Transformer: the first 9 layers are standard self-attention blocks for cross-modal interaction, while the last 9 layers split the FFN into three parallel pathways (motion, text, cross-modal), each containing densely activated experts with input-dependent gating. A Motion Descriptor module produces context tokens by attention-weighted aggregation of motion embeddings scored against the CLIP text embedding, injecting a high-level motion summary into the text branch. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) MEVQ-VAE reconstruction, (2) masked-token pretraining on all 11 unified datasets (~220 hours) with all pathways consuming motion tokens, and (3) text-conditional fine-tuning on motion-text pairs, where the motion pathway remains in masked-completion mode conditioned on visible text and motion tokens.

## Results

On HumanML3D (20 FPS evaluator), GenM³ achieves a state-of-the-art FID of 0.035, substantially outperforming the next-best method MoMask (0.045) and MMM (0.080). R-Precision (Top-3) reaches 0.795, slightly below MoMask's 0.807, while Diversity is 9.341. On the zero-shot IDEA400 benchmark, GenM³* (trained on all integrated text-motion pairs) achieves FID 4.232, beating MMM (6.001) and T2M-GPT (7.947) by large margins, demonstrating strong generalization to unseen motion distributions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenM³ achieves the lowest FID (0.035) among all 15 compared methods on HumanML3D, beating the closest competitor MoMask (0.045) by 22% and the prior VQ-VAE+Transformer baseline MMM (0.080) by 56%. On R-Precision Top-3, MoMask (0.807) retains a slight edge over GenM³ (0.795), while GenM³ matches or exceeds all other baselines (MMM 0.794, MoMamba 0.792, MotionChain 0.790). Diversity is a relative weakness: GenM³ scores 9.341, below T2M-GPT (9.722), MoMamba (9.871), and MotionLLM (9.838), suggesting the multi-expert architecture may slightly constrain output variety. On the zero-shot IDEA400 benchmark, GenM³* (FID 4.232) outperforms MMM (6.001) and T2M-GPT (7.947) by 30% and 47% respectively, and also leads on R-Precision Top-1 (0.136 vs. 0.126 and 0.122). Among diffusion-based baselines (MDM, MLD, MotionDiff, OMG), GenM³'s FID advantage is particularly pronounced, with OMG (0.381) and MDM (0.544) trailing by over 10× in FID.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VQ-VAE + masked Transformer paradigm established by MoMask and MMM, but departs from the LLM-backbone approach of MotionGPT and MotionChain by arguing that the structural and contextual gap between text and motion makes pretrained language models suboptimal for motion representation. Its key architectural departure is the introduction of a Multi-Expert VQ-VAE (all experts densely activated with learned weights) to handle distributional heterogeneity across 11 integrated datasets, and a Multi-path Motion Transformer with modality-specific expert pathways inspired by VLMO's mixture-of-modality-experts design. The Motion Descriptor, which compresses motion features into context tokens via text-conditional attention, draws on LLaMA-VID's information compression strategy but applies it to the motion domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in motion generation: training on multiple high-quality datasets simultaneously without one dataset's distribution degrading another's performance. The integrated 220-hour dataset with LLM-augmented and human-verified text annotations is a useful community resource, and the dense MoE finding (outperforming sparse MoE for cross-dataset adaptation) offers a counterpoint to the efficiency-driven sparse activation trend in MoE literature. Open questions include whether the multi-expert design scales to even more heterogeneous sources, whether the 30 FPS standardization loses information from higher-rate captures, and whether the zero-shot generalization to IDEA400 holds for truly out-of-distribution motion types not represented in any training dataset.
