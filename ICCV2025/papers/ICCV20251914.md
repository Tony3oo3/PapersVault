## Problem

Existing zero-shot personalized text-to-image generation methods (e.g., ELITE, IP-Adapter, BLIP-Diffusion) tightly couple their visual encoders to the cross-attention layers of a specific diffusion UNet, requiring costly re-training—sometimes infeasible for distilled models—whenever the base diffusion architecture changes. This paper tackles the previously unaddressed problem of model-agnostic personalized generation: can a single trained subject encoder be plugged into arbitrary unseen diffusion backbones with different channel dimensions, block counts, and functionalities without any additional optimization?

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB subject image and a free-form text prompt containing a virtual token 'S' that references the subject (e.g., 'a S wearing a santa hat'). The subject image is encoded by a frozen CLIP image encoder, and the prompt by a CLIP text encoder; no test-time optimization or per-subject fine-tuning is performed. The output is a generated image at the resolution supported by the target diffusion model (e.g., 512×512 for SD-v1.4, 1024×1024 for SD-XL) that preserves the subject's identity and local appearance while following the compositional text prompt. The same trained encoder can be loaded into SD-v1.4, SD-XL, SD-3-Medium, distilled LCM variants, InstructPix2Pix, LaVie, and MVDream without modification.

## Method

UniversalBooth introduces three key designs. First, it replaces per-layer key/value projections with shared square transformation matrices T_k and T_v that are learned once and applied across all cross-attention blocks at the same resolution scale via composition with the target model's native W_k, W_v (i.e., Ŵ_k = T_k W_k), decoupling the encoder from channel dimensions and block counts. Second, a hierarchical cross-attention mechanism combines a holistic Q-Former branch (following BLIP-Diffusion) that maps global subject semantics into virtual words with a fine-grained branch (following ELITE) that encodes local patch features; the fine-grained attention scores multiplicatively divide the holistic attention column for the primary subject word, balancing text adherence and appearance preservation without a tunable injection weight λ. Third, an optimal transport regularization (Eq. 4) penalizes uneven attention mass distribution across fine-grained tokens during training, providing a generic prior that guides layout coherence when the model is deployed on unseen architectures. Training uses the standard noise-prediction loss on OpenImages with Stable Diffusion v1.4 as the seen backbone.

## Results

On unseen architectures, UniversalBooth consistently outperforms all baselines across all three metrics. On LCM (a distilled model), it achieves CLIP-T 0.307, CLIP-I 0.694, and DINO-I 0.532, surpassing the next-best IP-Adapter (0.291, 0.679, 0.449) by a notable margin on DINO-I (+8.3 points). The gap widens dramatically on Base Diffusion where DINO-I reaches 0.500 vs. 0.210 for IP-Adapter and 0.050 for ELITE. On the seen architecture (SD-v1.4), UniversalBooth achieves comparable performance: CLIP-T 0.302, CLIP-I 0.772, DINO-I 0.667, slightly trailing IP-Adapter on CLIP-I (0.796) but leading on the other two metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BLIP-Diffusion, UniversalBooth wins on every metric across all four unseen architectures, with the largest gaps on DINO-I (e.g., 0.532 vs. 0.201 on LCM, 0.482 vs. 0.360 on Small Diffusion), reflecting BLIP-Diffusion's inability to preserve fine-grained textures when the backbone changes. Against IP-Adapter, UniversalBooth also dominates on unseen models (e.g., DINO-I 0.500 vs. 0.210 on Base Diffusion), but on the seen architecture it trades a small CLIP-I loss (0.772 vs. 0.796) for gains in CLIP-T (0.302 vs. 0.295) and DINO-I (0.667 vs. 0.629). Against ELITE, the most direct baseline, UniversalBooth achieves the largest relative improvements on unseen architectures—DINO-I jumps from 0.050 to 0.500 on Base Diffusion and from 0.093 to 0.482 on Small Diffusion—while on seen models it leads on CLIP-T (0.302 vs. 0.255) and DINO-I (0.667 vs. 0.652) with a marginal CLIP-I edge (0.772 vs. 0.762). The paper attributes the slight CLIP-I trade-off on seen models to the shared KV mapping design, which sacrifices some architecture-specific fidelity for cross-model portability.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniversalBooth builds directly on ELITE for its fine-grained mapping network and on BLIP-Diffusion for its holistic Q-Former-based mapping, inheriting the feed-forward visual-to-textual encoder paradigm that replaced test-time optimization. It departs from these and from IP-Adapter by identifying the architectural coupling between the subject encoder and the diffusion UNet's cross-attention layers as the root cause of poor cross-model transfer, and addresses it through shared square KV mapping matrices that make the encoder independent of channel dimensions and block counts. The hierarchical cross-attention (holistic + fine-grained) and the optimal transport prior are novel additions not present in any of the prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to enable zero-shot personalized generation across unseen diffusion architectures without retraining, addressing a practical pain point as the diffusion model landscape evolves rapidly (SD-v1.4 → SD-XL → SD-3, distilled variants, editing models). The shared KV mapping design is simple and potentially adoptable by other personalization frameworks. Limitations include slightly lower CLIP-I on seen architectures compared to ELITE, reliance on CLIP-based text encoders (requiring holistic-only mode for multi-encoder models like SD-XL/SD-3), and untested generalization to fundamentally different backbones such as DiT-based architectures. Open questions include whether the optimal transport prior can be generalized beyond the UNet multi-scale structure and whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation.
