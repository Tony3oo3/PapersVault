## Problem

Palmprint recognition models perform well on constrained datasets but degrade significantly on challenging samples with geometric distortions (positional shifts, size/angle variations) and textural degradations (overexposure, blur, shadows). Existing data augmentation methods—whether random, automatic, or adversarial—either fail to simulate palmprint-specific variations or disrupt identity consistency, leaving a gap in generating diverse yet identity-preserving challenging samples for robust training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251945/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 112×112 palmprint RGB images along with a learnable control vector z = [t, s], where t encodes geometric parameters (translation, rotation, scaling) and s is a 16-dimensional L2-normalized style code. The augmentation module outputs modified palmprint images that retain the original identity while exhibiting geometric and textural variations. The downstream recognition network (ResNet-50 with ArcFace loss) then produces identity class labels and cosine-similarity matching scores for verification (TAR@FAR) and identification (rank-1/rank-5) evaluation.

## Method

UAA unifies a differentiable spatial transformer (affine transformation with constrained rotation <0.25 rad, scaling <0.2, translation <0.2) and a StyleGAN2-inspired identity-preserving generation network (dual-encoder extracting style codes and spatial hierarchical identity features, with AdaIN modulation and channel-concatenated identity injection) into a single augmentation module governed by a shared control vector. The framework employs an adversarial training paradigm: the control vector is optimized via PGD to maximize the recognition network's classification loss (generating harder samples), then the recognition network is updated by minimizing loss on both original and augmented samples. A momentum-based dynamic sampling strategy replaces static Gaussian initialization by shifting the sampling mean toward previously optimized control vectors (z_t^0 ~ N(β·z_{t-1}^* + (1-β)·μ, Σ)), enabling progressively more targeted exploration of the augmentation parameter space.

## Results

On the three challenging datasets (MPD, XJTU-UP, BJTU) under the open-set protocol, UAA-s achieves TAR@FAR@1e-5 of 0.8075, 0.9332, and 0.8192 respectively, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline ARoFace (0.6367, 0.7640, 0.6510) by 17.1, 16.9, and 16.8 percentage points. On constrained datasets (TongJi, PolyU, CASIA), UAA-p/UAA-s maintain high accuracy (0.9867, 0.9858, 0.9841 at TAR@FAR@1e-5) while still exceeding the best baselines. For identification, UAA-s reaches rank-1 accuracy of 0.9526 on MPD and 0.9911 on XJTU-UP, compared to 0.8763 and 0.9642 for the best competing method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UAA outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and metric. On the most challenging MPD dataset, UAA-s (TAR@FAR@1e-5 = 0.8075) surpasses the strongest baseline ARoFace (0.6367) by 17.1 points and the automatic augmentation method MADAug (0.6042) by 20.3 points. On XJTU-UP, UAA-s (0.9332) leads MADAug (0.8402) by 9.3 points, while on BJTU it beats MADAug (0.6644) by 15.5 points. Among adversarial-example methods, AdvProp and Fast AdvProp perform poorly on challenging datasets (e.g., 0.4992 and 0.5310 on MPD), confirming that pixel-level perturbations are insufficient for palmprint-specific distortions. On constrained datasets (TongJi, PolyU, CASIA), UAA still leads by 0.9–2.9 points over the best baselines, demonstrating that the gains on hard samples do not come at the cost of accuracy on easier data. The sequential configuration (UAA-s) consistently edges out the parallel one (UAA-p) on challenging sets, suggesting that applying geometric then textural augmentation in sequence yields more effective hard samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate augmentation paradigms—differentiable spatial transformers (from Jaderberg et al.) and GAN-based image synthesis (inspired by StyleGAN2 and prior palmprint generators like RPG-Palm/PCE-Palm)—under a single adversarial optimization framework. It departs from automated augmentation methods (RandAugment, TrivialAugment, AdaAug, MADAug) that rely on fixed operation pools and search-based policies, instead using PGD-style gradient feedback from the recognition network to optimize a continuous control vector. It also extends facial-recognition augmentation ideas (CFSM's GAN-based adversarial synthesis, ARoFace's spatial alignment) to the palmprint domain, where identity preservation is more constrained by crease geometry. The momentum-based dynamic sampling strategy is a novel initialization scheme that replaces the static Gaussian sampling used in prior adversarial augmentation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical gap in contactless palmprint payment systems (WeChat Pay, Amazon) where geometric distortions and textural degradations from mobile capture severely degrade recognition. The fact that UAA improves performance on both challenging (MPD, XJTU-UP, BJTU) and constrained (TongJi, PolyU, CASIA) datasets without sacrificing accuracy on easy samples is notable, as many augmentation strategies trade off robustness for baseline performance. Key limitations include the reliance on a global affine transformer that cannot model local non-planar deformations (e.g., palm bending), and the identity-preserving generation module's dependence on a frozen pre-trained recognition network as the identity encoder, which couples the augmentation quality to the backbone's feature space. Future work on 3D hand-model-based augmentation and combining UAA with synthetic-data pre-training (BezierPalm) are natural extensions.
