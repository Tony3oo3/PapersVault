## Problem

Video diffusion models generate visually impressive sequences but lack 3D consistency, making them unsuitable for direct 3D scene synthesis. Existing approaches either stitch generated multi-view images with post-hoc reconstruction (suffering from inconsistencies) or use custom architectures that cannot leverage pre-trained video diffusion backbones. The core challenge is integrating an explicit 3D representation into a pre-trained latent video diffusion model whose compressed latent space is not inherently 3D-consistent and whose v-prediction objective conflicts with directly modeling 3D geometry.

## Input / Output

The model takes one or more posed reference images (with known camera extrinsics and intrinsics) and generates a 3D scene parameterized by per-pixel Gaussian splats in feature space. For the autoregressive variant, it additionally conditions on renderings of the current 3D scene to maintain consistency across generation steps. The output is a set of Gaussian splats (mean, covariance, opacity, color, and feature values) that can be rendered to multi-view images at 256×256 or decoded into a full 3D radiance field; the 3D decoder produces per-pixel splats in image space that can optionally be refined with 5K iterations of Splatfacto optimization for a more compact representation.

## Method

GGS integrates a 3D Gaussian splatting representation into the final upsampling block of a pre-trained U-Net video diffusion backbone, operating in feature space rather than latent space to remain compatible with v-prediction. An epipolar transformer correlates features along epipolar lines across neighboring views, and two prediction heads output per-pixel Gaussian splat parameters (depth as a Gaussian distribution via reparameterization, plus covariance, opacity, color, and feature). The rendered feature maps pass through a refinement block with skip connections to produce the v-prediction target, while additional L1 reconstruction losses on rendered low-resolution images for both input and novel viewpoints regularize the 3D representation at marginal computational cost. A separate 3D decoder (D_3D) with a convolutional upsampler directly maps epipolar transformer features to image-space Gaussian splats, bypassing the 2D decoder to improve 3D consistency. The model is trained in two stages—first fine-tuning temporal attention and the CameraCtrl-style Plücker camera encoder, then integrating the 3D representation—with optional depth supervision that replaces the discrete depth-bucket sampling with a continuous Gaussian depth prior.

## Results

On RealEstate10K (single reference image), GGS achieves PSNR 19.2, LPIPS 0.277, FVD 267.4, and TSED 0.992, outperforming all baselines on 3D consistency. On ScanNet++, GGS+depth reaches TSED 0.899, a substantial improvement over Ours-No3D (0.596) and ViewCrafter (0.871). For 3D scene quality (Table 3), GGS+depth+FT achieves FID 47.3 on RealEstate10K and 111.0 on ScanNet++, roughly 20% better FID than the no-3D variant (57.8 / 146.2) and nearly 2× better than ViewCrafter (96.1 / 140.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The most critical comparison is GGS vs. Ours-No3D (same backbone without 3D representation): adding Gaussian splats improves TSED from 0.596 to 0.896 on ScanNet++ (+50%) and from 0.984 to 0.992 on RealEstate10K, confirming that the 3D representation is the key driver of consistency gains. Against CameraCtrl, GGS wins on PSNR (19.2 vs. 17.7), LPIPS (0.277 vs. 0.332), FVD (267.4 vs. 366.7), and TSED (0.992 vs. 0.885) on RealEstate10K. Against ViewCrafter, GGS achieves higher TSED on both datasets (0.992 vs. 0.947 on RE10K; 0.896 vs. 0.871 on ScanNet++), though ViewCrafter edges out on PSNR and LPIPS on ScanNet++ (18.3/0.280 vs. 18.0/0.317), likely because ViewCrafter relies on DUSt3R trained with ground-truth depth. For 3D scene quality (Table 3), GGS+depth+FT achieves FID 47.3 on RealEstate10K, nearly 2× better than ViewCrafter (96.1) and ~20% better than Ours-No3D (57.8), demonstrating that the explicit 3D output avoids the artifacts that accumulate when reconstructing from inconsistent 2D generations. GGS also clearly surpasses PNVS and MultiDiff on all metrics across both datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

GGS sits at the intersection of pose-conditional video diffusion (CameraCtrl, ViewCrafter, CAT3D) and 3D-aware generative models (GeNVS, WildFusion, NeuralField-LDM). It departs from prior 3D generative models by leveraging a pre-trained latent video diffusion backbone rather than a custom architecture, and from prior pose-conditional diffusion by integrating an explicit 3D Gaussian splat representation directly into the U-Net's feature space. The key architectural departure from PixelSplat and similar regression-based splat predictors is the use of v-prediction in a generative diffusion setting, which necessitates placing the 3D representation in feature space (decoded to latent space) rather than in the compressed latent space itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GGS provides a practical recipe for combining pre-trained video diffusion priors with explicit 3D representations, a combination that prior 3D generative models could not achieve due to architectural incompatibility. The ~20% FID improvement from adding 3D structure, and the large TSED gains on challenging ScanNet++ trajectories, demonstrate that 3D consistency is a first-order concern for generative scene synthesis rather than a post-hoc reconstruction problem. Limitations include still-trailing image fidelity compared to regression-based NVS methods, dependence on posed inputs, and the 3D decoder's moderate degradation of visual quality. Open questions include whether this feature-space 3D integration generalizes to outdoor scenes, dynamic content, or text-conditioned generation without camera poses.
