## Problem

Current multi-organ segmentation models, despite strong aggregate metrics, frequently produce anatomically implausible predictions such as fragmented organs or mixed vertebrae labels because they struggle to learn global anatomical patterns from local scan features alone. Existing remedies—broadening the effective receptive field with data-hungry transformers or imposing topological/template constraints—either require large training sets or scale poorly to multi-organ settings with complex shapes and relative positions.

## Input / Output

The network consumes a 3D CT volume (intensity-truncated, spacing-normalized to [1.5,1.5,2.0], zero-padded to [288,288,512]) at a downsampled global resolution (factor [3,3,2]) and a 128×128×128 local crop, producing a per-voxel multi-organ probability map with C_cls channels. Final evaluation predictions are obtained via sliding-window inference on the full-resolution volume, and the method is task-agnostic across organ, vertebrae, and rib subgroups of the TotalSegmentator dataset.

## Method

AIC-Net introduces a learnable Anatomical Prior tensor (one channel per organ) that is spatially deformed per-patient by a Deform block combining a translation-only affine transform (aligning organ centroids) and a Thin-Plate Spline warp (matching organ shapes), with deformation parameters predicted from concatenated vision-encoder and prior-encoder features. The deformed prior is injected into each decoder layer via Squeeze-and-Excitation residual blocks that gate and fuse it with intermediate decoder embeddings, steering predictions toward anatomically consistent outputs. A novel centroid loss (per-class L2 between prior and ground-truth centroids) is added to prevent the flat loss landscape that arises when the deformed prior has no overlap with the target, ensuring stable gradient flow for the deformation parameters. The global-local process is cascaded: the global deformed prior is cropped and upsampled to serve as the local prior, which is further refined by a second Deform block on the 128³ patch. Training alternates optimization of the prior tensor and network weights every 500 iterations to avoid the correlation-induced optimization confusion between prior shape and deformation magnitude.

## Results

On the TotalSegmentator benchmark, AIC-Net consistently outperforms both Vanilla and Cascaded baselines across all four backbones and three tasks (abdominal organs, vertebrae, ribs). The most striking gains appear in HD95: for example, UNETR-Swin on vertebrae improves from 6.59 (Vanilla) to 1.76, and on ribs from 19.9 to 4.58. DSC improvements are more modest (typically 0.1–1.0 points), confirming that the anatomical prior primarily corrects spatial misplacements rather than improving overlap. External validation on the WORD dataset shows AIC-Net maintaining its HD95 advantage over baselines across all backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the UNet backbone, AIC-Net reduces HD95 by 1.27 on organs (6.39 vs 7.66) and by 9.4 on vertebrae (1.90 vs 11.3), while matching or slightly exceeding DSC on all three tasks. Against DeepLabV3+, AIC-Net achieves the largest organ-segmentation gain (HD95 4.28 vs 7.56, a 43% reduction) and wins on DSC for all tasks. Against UNETR, AIC-Net dramatically improves vertebrae HD95 (12.6 vs 52.2) and ribs HD95 (16.5 vs 39.4), with DSC gains of 6.7 and 3.2 points respectively. Against UNETR-Swin, AIC-Net wins on HD95 for all three tasks (most notably ribs: 4.58 vs 19.9) but concedes 0.9 DSC points on vertebrae (89.3 vs 90.2), suggesting the prior slightly smooths fine-grained vertebral boundaries while correcting gross positional errors. Overall, AIC-Net wins or ties on HD95 in 11 of 12 backbone–task combinations and on DSC in 11 of 12, with the sole DSC loss being the UNETR-Swin vertebrae case.

## Relation to Prior Work

AIC-Net departs from three established strategies for incorporating anatomical knowledge into segmentation: broadening effective receptive fields (Transformers, SSMs), mesh-deformation of fixed templates, and topological regularization via persistent homology. Rather than relying on data-hungry self-attention or discrete topological constraints that scale poorly to multi-organ settings, it introduces a learnable volumetric prior that is spatially deformed per-patient using differentiable affine and Thin-Plate Spline (TPS) transforms, then injected into decoder layers via Squeeze-and-Excitation blocks. The cascaded global-to-local design and the novel centroid loss (which addresses flat loss landscapes in deformation-based prediction) are the paper's core methodological departures from prior atlas-based and topology-regularized approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper targets a clinically meaningful failure mode—segmentation outputs that are statistically accurate but anatomically implausible (e.g., mixed vertebrae labels, fragmented adrenal glands)—and shows that a lightweight learnable prior with differentiable deformation can reduce such errors across four diverse backbones without requiring template meshes or topological losses. Key limitations include the doubled model size and memory from the cascaded architecture, and the external validation on the WORD dataset shows mixed generalization (e.g., UNETR-Swin HD95 worsens from 4.1 to 5.5). Open questions raised by the authors include whether the prior can be learned without the global-view guidance, and whether more flexible deformation functions could better represent fine-grained structures, suggesting the current affine+TPS pipeline is a starting point rather than a final solution.
