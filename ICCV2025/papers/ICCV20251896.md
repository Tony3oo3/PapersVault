## Problem

Existing feature-caching methods for video diffusion transformers treat all transformer blocks uniformly, overlooking the heterogeneous semantic roles of individual blocks. This uniform treatment leads to suboptimal reuse decisions that degrade output quality, particularly for dynamic foreground content. The paper identifies that blocks exhibit consistent foreground-vs-background attention preferences and that noise prediction stability evolves non-uniformly across denoising steps, but prior work has not exploited this structure for caching decisions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251896/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is a training-free inference-time acceleration layer applied to pretrained video diffusion models (HunyuanVideo at 720×1280, 129 frames, 50 steps; Wan2.1 at 480p, 81 frames). A text prompt conditions the diffusion process, and the output is a generated video at the same resolution and frame count as the base model. During a one-time profiling phase, SAM2 is used to generate foreground masks from PCA-projected latent visualizations to classify blocks; at inference time, only the text prompt and the pretrained model weights are required.

## Method

ProfilingDiT implements a dual-granularity caching strategy. At the block level, it computes a foreground attention ratio R_attn for each transformer block using attention maps and SAM2-derived masks, then partitions blocks into foreground-focused (full computation) and background-focused (delta caching) sets; a delta list scheme extends Delta-DiT to handle non-consecutive background blocks by caching per-block deviations (h_out − h_in) and accumulating them across steps. At the step level, it monitors the L1 distance between consecutive noise predictions to set an adaptive caching interval T_s that decreases linearly from T_max to T_min over the denoising schedule: early high-variance steps disable caching entirely, mid steps use larger reuse intervals, and final steps tighten the interval for fine-grained updates. On cache-utilization steps, background blocks reuse their accumulated delta while foreground blocks execute full forward passes, yielding a 2.01× speedup on Wan2.1 with LPIPS 0.1256.

## Results

On HunyuanVideo (720P, 129 frames), ProfilingDiT achieves a 1.87× speedup (932 s vs. 1745 s baseline) while substantially outperforming TeaCache on reconstruction quality: LPIPS 0.1203, PSNR 26.44, SSIM 0.8445, and FID 41.10. On Wan2.1 (480P, 81 frames), the method reaches a 2.01× speedup (247 s vs. 497 s) with LPIPS 0.1256, PSNR 22.02, SSIM 0.7899, and FID 62.56. VBench scores are slightly below the unaccelerated baseline (0.7642 vs. 0.7703 on HunyuanVideo; 0.7615 vs. 0.7582 on Wan2.1), indicating a small trade-off in holistic video quality for the speed gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TeaCache (slow) on HunyuanVideo, ProfilingDiT wins decisively on all reconstruction metrics—LPIPS drops from 0.1720 to 0.1203, PSNR rises from 21.91 to 26.44, SSIM improves from 0.7456 to 0.8445, and FID nearly halves from 77.67 to 41.10—while also reducing latency (932 s vs. 1052 s). Against TeaCache (fast), the quality gap is even larger (e.g., FID 41.10 vs. 83.85), though TeaCache (fast) remains faster in wall-clock time (753 s vs. 932 s) and marginally higher on VBench (0.7677 vs. 0.7642). On Wan2.1, ProfilingDiT dominates TeaCache (fast) across every metric including VBench (0.7615 vs. 0.7604), LPIPS (0.1256 vs. 0.2913), PSNR (22.02 vs. 16.17), SSIM (0.7899 vs. 0.5685), and FID (62.56 vs. 117.61), while matching its speed (247 s vs. 249 s). The consistent pattern is that ProfilingDiT trades a small VBench drop (≈0.006 on HunyuanVideo) for large gains in pixel-level and perceptual fidelity, suggesting the cached background blocks preserve fine detail that TeaCache's uniform reuse discards.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free feature caching paradigm for video diffusion transformers, building directly on Delta-DiT's block-level deviation caching mechanism while departing from similarity-based routing strategies like TeaCache. The key conceptual departure is introducing semantic awareness: rather than treating all transformer blocks uniformly or relying on input-output similarity thresholds, the authors use SAM2-derived foreground masks to profile which blocks attend to foreground versus background regions, then cache only background-focused blocks. The step-level adaptive caching interval is inspired by the observed L1 distance trajectory of predicted noise across denoising steps, a dynamic also exploited (in a different form) by TeaCache's timestep embedding routing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A ~2× speedup for high-resolution video generation (720p, 129 frames) with *improved* perceptual quality over TeaCache is practically significant for deployment of video diffusion models, which are currently prohibitively slow. The attention-profiling finding that individual DiT blocks exhibit stable foreground/background specialization across all denoising steps offers a useful interpretability lens for the architecture. However, the method requires a one-time SAM2-based profiling pass per model, and generalizability beyond HunyuanVideo and Wan2.1 remains unverified; whether the foreground/background split holds for architectures without explicit spatial tokenization or for non-photorealistic content is an open question.
