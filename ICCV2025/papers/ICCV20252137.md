## Problem

Sequential non-rigid registration—registering a source mesh onto a long sequence of sparse or partial point clouds—suffers from two compounding issues: optimization-based methods get trapped in local minima under noisy or partial inputs, and frame-wise neural registration methods accumulate errors and lose temporal consistency over long sequences. No existing feed-forward approach efficiently handles both robustness to partial observations and temporal coherence across extended sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252137/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a dense source point cloud (N_s points) and a temporal sequence of T sparse or partial target point clouds (each with N_i points, often depth-sampled from a single camera view) as input. It produces dense per-frame registered point clouds by predicting a deformation graph of B=256 nodes (positions, radii, and SE(3) transformations per frame), which is then expanded to all source points via RBF-based linear blend skinning. The target point clouds can be arbitrarily sparse or partial, and the source and targets may be in arbitrary poses with large inter-frame motions.

## Method

ERNet uses a two-stage coarse-to-fine pipeline over a sparse deformation graph (256 nodes sampled via farthest point sampling from the source). A shared triplane encoder compresses point clouds into three 256×256 feature planes, then a spatial transformer (node-to-frame matching, 6 iterations) provides robust per-frame initialization, followed by a spatio-temporal transformer that refines node trajectories in overlapping sliding windows of 8 frames (6 iterations per window) to enforce temporal consistency. Rather than directly regressing SE(3) transformations, the method exploits local rigidity: it groups nearby nodes whose relative distances remain stable over time and solves their rigid transformation via Procrustes analysis (SVD). Dense deformation is then applied through RBF-weighted linear blend skinning, where per-node radii are predicted by a separate transformer to define blending weights. Training is two-stage: the encoder and matching network are first pre-trained on single-frame pairs, then frozen while the spatio-temporal transformers are optimized end-to-end on longer sequences.

## Results

On D-FAUST, ERNet achieves an ATE3D of 0.016, δ0.01 of 0.313, and δ0.05 of 0.863, outperforming the best baseline C-NDP (ATE3D 0.041, δ0.01 0.057, δ0.05 0.522) by a large margin. On the unseen DT4D-H dataset, ERNet records ATE3D 0.037, δ0.01 0.238, and δ0.05 0.678, again surpassing all baselines. In terms of efficiency, ERNet registers at 0.178 s/frame on D-FAUST and 0.188 s/frame on DT4D-H, delivering over 4× speedup compared to the fastest baseline C-NDP (0.808 s and 0.832 s respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper constructs three chained baselines—C-NICP, C-NSFP, and C-NDP—by iteratively applying pairwise non-rigid registration methods frame-by-frame, which is the standard approach for sequential registration prior to this work. ERNet outperforms all three baselines on every metric (ATE3D, δ0.01, δ0.05, Tavg) across both D-FAUST and DT4D-H. On D-FAUST, ERNet reduces ATE3D from 0.041 (C-NDP, the best baseline) to 0.016, a 61% improvement, while increasing δ0.05 from 0.522 to 0.863. On the held-out DT4D-H dataset (never seen during training), ERNet still achieves the best ATE3D of 0.037 versus 0.058 for C-NDP, demonstrating strong generalizability. In terms of speed, ERNet runs at 0.178–0.188 s/frame, more than 4× faster than the fastest baseline C-NDP (0.808–0.832 s/frame) and roughly 8–11× faster than C-NICP and C-NSFP, which require per-sequence optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

ERNet departs from the dominant paradigm of optimization-based non-rigid registration (NICP, ARAP) and implicit neural deformation fields (CaDeX, OccupancyFlow) by adopting explicit deformation graphs as a feed-forward regression target, drawing on the classical deformation graph representation from Sederberg et al. [35]. Its triplane point cloud encoder directly adapts the spatial encoding scheme from SpatialTracker [39], while the sliding-window temporal refinement extends CoTracker's [13] overlapping-segment strategy from 2D video tracking to 3D node trajectory prediction. The key conceptual departure is the two-stage coarse-to-fine pipeline: a frame-wise matching network provides robust initialization that prevents error accumulation, followed by a spatio-temporal transformer that enforces temporal consistency—addressing a gap left by prior feed-forward methods that operate frame-by-frame without explicit temporal modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4× speedup over the previous best method while simultaneously achieving SOTA accuracy on both DT4D-H and D-FAUST makes sequential non-rigid registration practical for real-time applications in robotics, dynamic reconstruction, and scene understanding. The demonstrated generalization to the unseen DT4D-H dataset (trained only on D-FAUST and DT4D-A) suggests the deformation graph representation captures transferable motion priors. However, the method is limited by the scarcity of annotated 4D correspondence data, and its applicability to broader settings (dynamic scene editing, autonomous driving, open-world objects) remains unexplored. An open question is whether the fixed node count (B=256) and RBF skinning can scale to objects with highly non-uniform deformation complexity without per-object tuning.
