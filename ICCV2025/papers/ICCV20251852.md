## Problem

High-fidelity co-speech gesture video generation suffers from a severe one-to-many mapping between audio and visual content, and existing public datasets (13 h PATS, 3 h TED-talks) are too small and undiverse to train competitive models. Industry solutions rely on 200–2,200 hours of private data and high-end GPUs, leaving the research community without adequate resources to advance this task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251852/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a speech audio signal (16 kHz) and a single reference image of the target speaker (512×512) as input, and produces a 5–15 second co-speech gesture video at 512×512 and 25 FPS. The reference image is consumed twice: once to extract a 133-keypoint 2D full-body skeleton (COCO-Whole-Body format) that conditions the diffusion model, and once as the identity anchor for the downstream video generator. The framework is evaluated in both paired (audio and image from the same source video) and unpaired (audio and image from different videos) settings, the latter being more practical for real-world use.

## Method

The framework is a two-stage pipeline: a lightweight DiT-based diffusion model predicts a sequence of 2D full-body skeletons from audio, which is then fed into an off-the-shelf human video generator (StableAnimator or EchoMimicV2) together with the reference image. Two key design choices ensure high-fidelity skeleton prediction: (1) the reference-image skeleton s_r is concatenated with the noisy skeleton x_t along the frame dimension so self-attention layers enforce body-shape consistency, and (2) fine-grained wav2vec 2.0 audio segment embeddings (768-dim, one per frame) are concatenated with skeleton embeddings along the feature dimension, creating a strict one-to-one temporal correspondence that outperforms cross-attention conditioning. The model uses x0-prediction (following Human Motion Diffusion Model) and classifier-free guidance (10% audio dropout), and keypoints are represented in a local motion coordinate system (nose tip, wrists, and neck as root nodes) to disentangle global body motion from subtle facial and hand articulation. The audio-to-skeleton model is trained on the newly released CSG-405 dataset (405 hours, 147,550 clips, 71 speech types, 17,787 speakers) with 4×V100 GPUs for 2M steps.

## Results

In the unpaired setting on CSG-405, Ours†(StableAnimator) achieves the best FID (62.93) and FVD (984.13), substantially outperforming StableAnimator (FID 94.94, FVD 1532.74) and EchoMimicV2 (FID 90.01, FVD 1444.00). Ours(EchoMimicV2) attains the best audio-lip synchronization on CSG-405 with Sync-C 6.59 and Sync-D 8.04. On PATS and TED-talks, the method similarly leads in FID and FVD in the unpaired setting (e.g., FID 70.38 vs. 91.83 for StableAnimator on PATS; FVD 650.20 vs. 890.48 on TED-talks). In the paired setting, StableAnimator with ground-truth skeletons still achieves the highest SSIM/PSNR, as expected, but the proposed method narrows the gap significantly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the unpaired setting (the more practical scenario), the proposed method with StableAnimator (Ours†) achieves the best FID and FVD across all three datasets, reducing FVD by 35% over StableAnimator (984 vs. 1533 on CSG-405) and by 42% over MimicMotion (984 vs. 1751). With EchoMimicV2 as the video generator, the method achieves the best Sync-C (6.59) and Sync-D (8.04) on CSG-405, surpassing EchoMimicV2 alone (6.53/8.09) and StableAnimator (5.13/9.50). Warping-based methods (S2G-MDDiffusion, DiffTED) are clearly inferior, with FID values 2–3× higher and poor Sync-C scores (e.g., DiffTED's Sync-C of 0.66 on CSG-405). In the paired setting, StableAnimator with ground-truth skeletons still leads in SSIM/PSNR (0.83/24.27 on CSG-405), but the proposed method narrows the gap to 0.67/16.62 while achieving superior identity consistency (CSIM 0.82 vs. 0.67). Qualitative comparisons with VLOGGER and CyberHost (via demo videos) show the proposed method produces more natural and diverse gestures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against two lines of co-speech gesture video generation: warping-based methods (S2G-MDDiffusion, DiffTED) that produce artifacts under large pose changes, and recent diffusion-based approaches (VLOGGER, CyberHost, EchoMimicV2) that rely on private large-scale data and auxiliary conditions like 3D motions or hand-only skeletons. The key conceptual departure is using a lightweight 2D full-body skeleton as an intermediate representation predicted by a DiT-based diffusion model, with audio conditioning via feature-level concatenation rather than the standard cross-attention mechanism. The framework then delegates final video synthesis to off-the-shelf human video generators (StableAnimator, EchoMimicV2, MimicMotion), making the approach modular and avoiding end-to-end training on expensive hardware.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primary practical impact is CSG-405, a 405-hour public dataset with 17,787 speakers and 71 speech types that is roughly 30× larger than the previous largest public resource (PATS, 13 hours), directly addressing the data scarcity that has limited academic progress in this area. The finding that feature-level concatenation of audio and skeleton embeddings dramatically outperforms cross-attention for audio-visual synchronization (Sync-C: 6.28 vs. 0.77) offers a useful design principle for future audio-conditioned generation models. Open questions include whether the two-stage pipeline's error propagation (skeleton artifacts → video artifacts) can be mitigated, whether the approach scales to longer sequences beyond 15 seconds, and whether the unpaired setting (audio and image from different sources) can be further improved without sacrificing identity consistency.
