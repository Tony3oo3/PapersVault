## Problem

Video-based tracking-any-point (TAP) methods assume slow linear motion during the blind time between frames, causing point loss under large displacements or nonlinear motion. Event cameras offer continuous microsecond-resolution motion cues, but their spatial sparsity (events only at brightness changes) and speed-dependent event frequency introduce ambiguities in appearance-based matching that existing event trackers do not address.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252618/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes event streams encoded as Time Surfaces (two channels for positive/negative polarity, recording the most recent event timestamp per pixel) at 512×640 resolution, along with 2D query point coordinates. The temporal window length is matched to the ground-truth frequency with untruncated timestamps to preserve full motion trajectories. Output is an iteratively refined 2D trajectory (x, y positions at each of T=48 time bins) per query point, updated via a transformer-predicted displacement at each iteration.

## Method

MATE comprises two modules operating within an iterative tracking loop. The Motion-Guidance Module (MGM) fits a tangent plane via SVD to the local time-surface neighborhood of each query point, yielding 2D kinematic vectors (inverse of spatial gradient of the event surface); an MLP then corrects boundary ambiguities where overlapping objects produce multiple motion states. The Variable Motion Aware (VMA) module addresses the fact that event update frequency is proportional to speed (f ∝ v·∇I/C) by sampling feature patches from three offset time steps {0, t−4, t−2}, fusing them with kinematic vectors via cross-attention, and processing through parallel short-term (1D temporal convolutions) and long-term (temporal attention) branches to produce speed-invariant correlation maps. A transformer then combines point positions, kinematic vectors, and correlation maps to predict the displacement ΔX for the next iteration. The full system is trained end-to-end with a weighted MAE loss (γ=0.8) that penalizes later time steps more heavily, using a residual encoder on the time surface and only 6.6M parameters.

## Results

On Ev-PointOdyssey, MATE achieves δ_avg=0.362, MTE=25.63 px, and Survival_50=0.560, improving Survival_50 by 17.9% over the best event-only baseline (Ev-PIPs++, 0.475) and reducing MTE by 55% compared to Ev-PIPs++ (57.36 px). On Ev-Davis, it reaches δ_avg=0.747, MTE=3.47 px, and Survival_16=0.928, outperforming all video-based and event-based methods. For feature tracking, MATE attains FA=0.713/EFA=0.626 on EDS and FA=0.885/EFA=0.875 on EC, surpassing all event-only and event+video baselines. The model uses only 6.6M parameters, roughly 3–4× fewer than competing methods (17.6M–25.4M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against event-only baselines, MATE dominates: it improves Survival_50 from 0.475 (Ev-PIPs++) and 0.428 (Ev-Cotracker3) to 0.560, and cuts MTE from 57.36/42.55 px to 25.63 px on Ev-PointOdyssey. Versus video-based methods, MATE matches or exceeds them on Survival (0.560 vs. 0.505 for PIPs++, 0.409 for Cotracker3) and MTE (25.63 vs. 26.95/26.84), while using only event input and 6.6M parameters. On Ev-Davis, MATE leads all methods on every metric, including Survival_16 (0.928 vs. 0.913 for Cotracker3) and MTE (3.47 vs. 3.73). In feature tracking (Table 2), MATE outperforms the strongest event-only baseline ETAP (FA 0.713 vs. 0.701 on EDS; 0.885 vs. 0.881 on EC) and substantially surpasses event+video methods like FE-TAP (FA 0.713 vs. 0.676 on EDS) and DeepEvT (0.713 vs. 0.576 on EDS), demonstrating that motion-guided event-only tracking can rival or exceed multi-modal approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

MATE extends the video-based Tracking Any Point (TAP) paradigm—established by PIPs++, Cotracker, and TAP-Net—to the event camera modality, departing from prior event-based TAP attempts (FE-TAP, ETAP) that rely solely on appearance-based matching. The key conceptual departure is the use of kinematic vectors derived from time-surface plane fitting (via SVD) to construct a dynamic-appearance matching space, addressing the spatial sparsity and speed-dependent event density issues that make direct porting of video TAP methods to events problematic. The variable motion aware module further distinguishes MATE by explicitly modeling the non-stationary nature of event streams, where event update frequency is proportional to local velocity, a property absent in frame-based representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first comprehensive event-based TAP framework that explicitly tackles both spatial sparsity and motion sensitivity, and introduces two simulated event datasets (Ev-PointOdyssey, Ev-Davis) that fill a critical benchmarking gap for the nascent event-based TAP field. The finding that a simple geometric operation (plane fitting on time surfaces) yields kinematic cues sufficient to outperform video-based methods with 3–4× fewer parameters suggests that motion structure in events can substitute for texture information. However, all evaluation is on simulated events, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to noisy real event streams, and the limited number of event-based TAP baselines (only two) makes the competitive landscape hard to assess. Future work will need to validate on real event hardware and explore whether the kinematic-guidance principle transfers to other sparse or asynchronous sensing modalities.
