## Problem

Autoregressive visual generation faces a fundamental dilemma: discrete tokens enable simple cross-entropy prediction but suffer from information loss and tokenizer training instability, while continuous tokens preserve visual fidelity but require complex distribution modeling (diffusion heads, GMMs) beyond standard categorical prediction. No existing approach achieves both the representational capacity of continuous tokens and the modeling simplicity of discrete tokens.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250563/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label (ImageNet category) as conditioning input and generates a 256×256 RGB image. Internally, a pretrained VAE encoder maps the image to a 16×16 grid of 16-dimensional continuous latent vectors, which are then discretized into tokens with B=64 levels per dimension. At inference, the autoregressive model predicts discrete indices sequentially, de-quantizes them back to continuous features, and feeds them through the pretrained VAE decoder to produce the final image.

## Method

TokenBridge decouples discretization from tokenizer training by applying training-free, dimension-wise quantization to a fully pretrained continuous VAE. Each of the 16 feature dimensions is independently quantized using Gaussian-based non-uniform boundaries (B=64 equal-probability intervals), exploiting the bounded, near-Gaussian distribution of KL-regularized VAE features to achieve near-lossless reconstruction without an explicit codebook. The resulting exponentially large token space (64^16 per spatial location) is handled by a lightweight autoregressive head that decomposes each token's prediction into a sequence of 16 smaller B-way classification problems, conditioned on previously predicted dimensions and spatial context from a masked autoregressive Transformer backbone. Training uses standard cross-entropy loss over these dimension-wise predictions, and a frequency-based ordering (via FFT analysis) prioritizes structurally important dimensions early in the generation sequence.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, TokenBridge-H (910M params) achieves FID 1.55 and IS 313.3, matching the FID of MAR-H (943M, FID 1.55) while achieving higher IS (313.3 vs. 303.7) and Recall (0.65 vs. 0.62) with fewer parameters. TokenBridge-L (486M) reaches FID 1.76, outperforming MAR-L (479M, FID 1.78) and substantially beating GIVT (304M, FID 3.35) which uses Gaussian Mixture Models for continuous token prediction. Notably, TokenBridge-H surpasses FlowAR-H (1.9B, FID 1.65) by 0.10 FID with nearly half the parameters, and outperforms VAR (2.0B, FID 1.73) by 0.18 FID, all while using only standard cross-entropy loss for categorical prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest direct comparison MAR [18] (which uses diffusion-based token prediction on continuous VAE features), TokenBridge-H matches FID exactly (1.55) while achieving higher IS (313.3 vs. 303.7) and Recall (0.65 vs. 0.62) with 33M fewer parameters; TokenBridge-L similarly matches MAR-L (FID 1.76 vs. 1.78). TokenBridge-H clearly outperforms FlowAR-H [29] (FID 1.55 vs. 1.65) with less than half the parameters (910M vs. 1.9B), and substantially beats GIVT [39] (FID 1.76 vs. 3.35) which relies on Gaussian Mixture Models. Among discrete-token methods, TokenBridge-L (486M) outperforms LlamaGen [36] (3.1B, FID 2.18) and Open-MAGVIT2-L [20] (804M, FID 2.51) by large margins, and TokenBridge-H beats VAR [38] (2.0B, FID 1.73) by 0.18 FID. The one notable loss is to VAR [38] on IS (313.3 vs. 350.2) and to MDTV2-XL/2 [9] on IS (313.3 vs. 314.7), though TokenBridge-H achieves a slightly better FID than MDTV2 (1.55 vs. 1.58). Overall, the paper demonstrates that standard cross-entropy categorical prediction on post-training quantized tokens can match or exceed methods requiring diffusion heads, GMMs, or flow matching for continuous token distribution modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

TokenBridge inverts the conventional tokenization paradigm: rather than embedding quantization within the tokenizer training loop (as VQ-VAE, LFQ, and MAGVIT2 do), it applies training-free dimension-wise quantization to a fully pretrained continuous VAE (from LDM [31]) after the fact. The spatial autoregressive backbone is adopted directly from MAR [18], which itself demonstrated that continuous tokens can be generated autoregressively but required a diffusion head for distribution modeling. TokenBridge's key conceptual departure is that by discretizing post-hoc, the generation model can use standard cross-entropy loss over categorical tokens, eliminating the need for diffusion heads, GMMs, or flow-matching modules that MAR, GIVT, and FlowAR all require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is a simpler generation pipeline: standard categorical prediction replaces specialized distribution modeling, reducing architectural complexity and training overhead while matching state-of-the-art FID on ImageNet-256. The confidence-guided generation capability (Fig. 8) is a unique advantage of the discrete formulation that continuous approaches like MAR cannot offer, potentially enabling foreground-focused or compositional generation. Limitations include reliance on a fixed pretrained VAE (no end-to-end joint optimization), evaluation only on class-conditional ImageNet-256, and the need for careful hyperparameter tuning of quantization range and granularity. Open questions include whether this post-training quantization strategy transfers to video generation, text-conditioned settings, or higher resolutions where the VAE latent space may have different statistical properties.
