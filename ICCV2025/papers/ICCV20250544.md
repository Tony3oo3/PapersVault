## Problem

Building a realistic, animatable whole-body talking avatar from a single image remains unsolved: existing methods require minutes of multi-view or self-rotating video for supervision, and even those struggle to generalize to novel gestures and facial expressions. The core difficulties are (1) modeling complex full-body dynamics with only one image as ground truth, and (2) extending the avatar to poses and expressions far outside the single observed configuration without identity drift or geometric artifacts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250544/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image of a person (cropped to 9:16 aspect ratio) and produces a fully animatable 3DGS-mesh avatar parameterized by SMPL-X pose, shape, and expression codes. The avatar uses ~150,000 3D Gaussians on a 512-resolution UV map and renders at 50+ FPS at 1K resolution. Animation is driven by arbitrary SMPL-X pose and expression sequences, enabling cross-identity motion transfer and novel-view synthesis within roughly ±30° of the input viewpoint.

## Method

The core contribution is a tightly coupled 3DGS-mesh hybrid representation where 3D Gaussians are initialized on the SMPL-X canonical surface via UV parameterization, and two deformation fields (Gaussian offset dX and mesh vertex offset dT) are jointly optimized with a soft mesh-Gaussian consistency constraint (‖dX − Bary(dT)‖₁) plus Laplacian smoothing and normal-consistency regularizers. To overcome the single-image supervision gap, the pipeline generates imperfect pseudo-frames using MimicMotion (body) and Portrait4D-v2 (face) driven by diverse TED Gesture Dataset pose sequences, then re-tracks each pseudo-frame to recover accurate SMPL-X parameters, correcting 2D diffusion misalignment. Crucially, pseudo-frames are supervised with LPIPS perceptual loss rather than pixel-level losses to tolerate residual artifacts, while the single input image receives standard L1+SSIM+mask supervision. This hybrid training strategy—perceptual supervision on noisy generative frames plus pixel-accurate supervision on the one real image—enables complete, expressive whole-body animation from a single photograph.

## Results

On the self-driven motion reenactment task, the proposed method achieves PSNR 29.311, SSIM 0.943, LPIPS 0.0299, and MSE 1.216×10⁻³, outperforming all baselines across every metric. Notably, the one-shot method surpasses ExAvatar-40shot (which uses 40 input images) by 5.1 dB in PSNR and 0.019 in SSIM, and beats the 2D diffusion-based StableAnimator by 1.96 dB in PSNR. The method also achieves the lowest LPIPS (0.0299), indicating superior perceptual quality over ELICIT (0.0660) and ExAvatar (0.0409).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines spanning NeRF, 3DGS, and 2D diffusion approaches. Against ELICIT (one-shot NeRF with SMPL geometry), the proposed method wins decisively on all metrics (PSNR +6.75 dB, LPIPS −0.036), largely because ELICIT's SMPL-based geometry cannot model hands or facial expressions. Against ExAvatar-40shot (mesh-based 3DGS requiring 40 input images), the one-shot method still achieves PSNR 29.31 vs. 24.22 and SSIM 0.943 vs. 0.924, demonstrating that the pseudo-frame guidance strategy compensates for the 40× reduction in input data. Against StableAnimator (SOTA 2D pose-guided video diffusion), the method gains 1.96 dB PSNR and 0.008 SSIM, attributed to 3D consistency and identity preservation that 2D diffusion lacks. Make-Your-Anchor (2D diffusion fine-tuned on a short video) is compared only qualitatively in Fig. 4, where it struggles with fine details and gestures outside its fine-tuning distribution, while the proposed method preserves identity and local details more faithfully.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three active research threads: 3D Gaussian Splatting-based human avatars (ExAvatar, HUGS, GaussianAvatar), pose-guided human video diffusion (MimicMotion, Animate Anyone, StableAnimator), and parametric mesh-driven animation (SMPL-X). It departs from prior 3DGS avatar methods that require dense multi-view or self-rotating video input by instead using diffusion-generated pseudo-frames as imperfect supervision, and departs from prior diffusion-based animation that operates purely in 2D by grounding the output in a coupled 3DGS-mesh representation with explicit geometric constraints. The key conceptual departure from SDS-based diffusion guidance is the argument that pixel-aligned losses are suboptimal for pose-critical avatar tasks, motivating the use of LPIPS perceptual loss on pseudo-frames combined with strict per-pixel supervision only on the single input image.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that a single photograph, augmented with imperfect diffusion-generated pseudo-frames, is sufficient to reconstruct a photorealistic, fully animatable whole-body talking avatar with precise control over hands, face, and body — a practical capability that prior methods required minutes of video capture to achieve. The coupled 3DGS-mesh architecture with mesh-Gaussian consistency loss and Laplacian smoothing provides a reusable pattern for stabilizing Gaussian deformation under sparse supervision. Key limitations remain: large viewing angles (>30°) degrade significantly, finger-level mismatches between the input image and SMPL-X registration can cause optimization failures, and the 3-hour training time (due to re-tracking) limits scalability. Open questions include whether full 360° reconstruction is achievable with current diffusion priors and how semantic information from LLMs or 3D reconstruction priors could further reduce the reliance on imperfect pseudo-frames.
