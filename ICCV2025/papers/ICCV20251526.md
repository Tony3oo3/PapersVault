## Problem

Existing 3D/4D scene generation pipelines handle geometric alignment separately in either the video generation stage or the scene reconstruction stage, making cross-stage misalignments difficult to correct. This indistinct link between stages means inconsistencies introduced in one stage cannot be fully addressed by the other, degrading the final scene quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251526/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

SteerX accepts a text prompt or a single RGB image as input and produces geometrically consistent multi-view video frames (25 frames at 480×480) that are directly lifted into 3D Gaussian splatting scenes or 4D dynamic point-cloud scenes. Only 8 of the generated frames are used for the actual feed-forward reconstruction, and the method is model-agnostic, plugging into any pre-trained video generative model (Mochi, HunyuanVideo, CogVideoX, SplatFlow, DimensionX) without retraining.

## Method

SteerX is a zero-shot inference-time steering method that integrates pose-free feed-forward scene reconstruction (MV-DUSt3R+ for 3D, MonST3R for 4D) directly into the reverse diffusion sampling loop as a geometric reward signal. Two reward functions are defined: GS-MEt3R measures cosine similarity between DINO features of input frames and their 3DGS renderings, while Dyn-MEt3R unprojects background features from half the frames and reprojects them onto the other half to score dynamic consistency. The steering is performed via Sequential Monte Carlo (Feynman-Kac Steering) with max potentials and DPM-solver++ as the proposal kernel, resampling particles at selected timesteps to tilt the data distribution toward geometrically aligned samples. A theoretical guarantee (Proposition 1) shows that with bounded approximation errors from the Tweedie estimate and the ODE solver, the algorithm samples from the desired tilted distribution. The approach requires no training and scales favorably with the number of particles, enabling test-time scaling for 3D/4D scene generation.

## Results

On T3Bench (Text-to-3D), SteerX(k=4) with SplatFlow achieves BRISQUE 13.1, NIQE 4.30, and CLIPScore 33.4, outperforming SplatFlow alone (BRISQUE 19.6, NIQE 4.24, CLIPScore 33.2) and Director3D (BRISQUE 32.3, NIQE 4.35, CLIPScore 32.9). On PenguinVideoBenchmark (Text-to-4D), SteerX(early) with HunyuanVideo improves Dyn-MEt3R from 0.911 (baseline) to 0.964, while also boosting Aesthetic Score from 0.549 to 0.555 and Subject Consistency from 0.967 to 0.980. On VBench-I2V (Image-to-3D), SteerX(early) with DimensionX reduces BRISQUE from 37.3 to 29.7 and improves GS-MEt3R from 0.708 to 0.749. Performance scales consistently with the number of particles k across all four generation tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SteerX substantially outperforms all optimization-based text-to-3D baselines (DreamFusion, Magic3D, LatentNeRF, SJC, Fantasia3D, ProlificDreamer) by large margins on BRISQUE (13.1 vs. 61.5–92.8) and NIQE (4.30 vs. 7.07–11.20), reflecting the advantage of feed-forward reconstruction over per-scene optimization. Against the two most competitive camera-free baselines, SteerX(k=4) beats Director3D on BRISQUE (13.1 vs. 32.3) and CLIPScore (33.4 vs. 32.9) while matching on NIQE (4.30 vs. 4.35). Compared to SplatFlow alone, SteerX reduces BRISQUE by 6.5 points (13.1 vs. 19.6) and improves CLIPScore by 0.2, though NIQE is marginally higher (4.30 vs. 4.24). Notably, the best-of-N baseline (k=4) degrades CLIPScore to 32.3 and increases NIQE to 4.41, confirming that geometric steering via SMC resampling is more effective than simple selection. Across all four generation tasks (Text/Image-to-3D/4D), SteerX consistently surpasses both the unsteered baseline and best-of-N on geometric consistency metrics (GS-MEt3R, Dyn-MEt3R) while maintaining or improving perceptual quality scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

SteerX builds on the Feynman-Kac Steering (FKS) framework [55] for inference-time particle-based guidance of diffusion models, and extends the MEt3R multi-view consistency metric [1] into two new geometric reward functions (GS-MEt3R and Dyn-MEt3R) tailored for 3D and 4D scene generation. It departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm—where video generation and scene reconstruction are optimized separately for geometric alignment—by embedding pose-free feed-forward reconstruction models (MV-DUSt3R+, MonST3R) directly into the reverse diffusion sampling loop as reward signals. Unlike gradient-based guidance methods that require differentiable rewards and substantial memory, SteerX uses sequential Monte Carlo resampling, enabling non-differentiable rewards and favorable test-time scaling with particle count.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying generation and reconstruction into a single zero-shot inference-time process, SteerX eliminates the cross-stage misalignment that has plagued camera-free 3D/4D generation pipelines, and its test-time scaling property (more particles → better geometric consistency) opens a previously underexplored axis of improvement for scene generation. The framework's modularity—compatible with any video generative model and any pose-free reconstruction model—makes it a practical drop-in enhancement rather than a monolithic system. Open questions include the computational cost of evaluating geometric rewards at multiple timesteps for long video sequences, whether the DINO-feature-based consistency metric captures all relevant geometric errors, and how the approach behaves when the base video model's camera trajectories are highly non-rigid or involve complex scene dynamics beyond what MonST3R handles well.
