## Problem

Unifying visual understanding and generation in a single multimodal framework is difficult because the two tasks demand representations at different granularities: understanding requires coarse-grained semantic features while generation requires fine-grained intrinsic imagery features. Existing unified models rely on VQ or VAE encoders pre-trained for pixel-level reconstruction, which capture local textures but lack the high-level visual semantics needed for effective understanding, creating an inherent trade-off between the two capabilities.

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the model takes a natural language caption as input and produces a 256×256 or 512×512 RGB image via K=64 masked autoregressive forward passes with a cosine-decaying mask schedule and classifier-free guidance (weight 3.0). For image understanding, the model takes a full image (all patches, no masking) plus a question and produces a text answer via standard next-token prediction. Both tasks share the same MAR encoder, which processes image patches alongside buffer embeddings, with the encoder output routed through the LLM before reaching either the MAR decoder (generation) or the text head (understanding).

## Method

Harmon employs a shared MAR (masked autoregressive) encoder as the visual backbone for both generation and understanding, motivated by the finding that MIM-trained MAR encoders learn rich visual semantics (validated via linear probing and GradCAM++ visualization) unlike VQGAN or VAE encoders. The architecture routes the MAR encoder output through a Qwen2.5 LLM: for generation, the LLM-processed encoder features are fed to a MAR decoder that predicts masked patches using a reverse-diffusion loss; for understanding, all image patches (unmasked) are encoded and the LLM performs next-token prediction with cross-entropy loss. Training follows a three-stage progressive recipe: (I) vision-language alignment on 22M image-text pairs with only the MAR encoder/decoder trainable, (II) comprehensive multimodal training unlocking the LLM with 20M QA samples and 50M T2I images, and (III) high-quality fine-tuning at 512×512 resolution with filtered and synthetic data. A key empirical finding is that co-training with the understanding loss actively boosts generation quality (especially on GenEval), demonstrating synergy through the shared representation that a decoupled Janus-like design (separate SigLIP + MAR encoders) does not exhibit.

## Results

On the GenEval benchmark, Harmon-1.5B achieves an overall score of 0.76, surpassing all unified models (Janus-Pro-1.5B: 0.73, OmniGen: 0.70, D-DiT: 0.65) and even approaching generation-only SD3-Medium (0.74). For visual quality, Harmon-1.5B attains FID 5.15 on MJHQ-30K and 8.39 on MSCOCO-30K, outperforming all unified baselines (Janus-Pro: 9.53/16.08, Show-o: 15.18/9.24). On image understanding, Harmon-1.5B scores 87.6 on POPE, 321 on MME-Cognition, and 38.9 on MMMU, matching or exceeding Janus-Pro-1.5B (86.2/268/36.3) which uses a dedicated SigLIP encoder, while substantially outperforming VQGAN/VAE-based unified models (e.g., LWM: 75.2 POPE, Show-o: 80.0 POPE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Harmon-1.5B (0.76 GenEval) outperforms the strongest unified baseline Janus-Pro-1.5B (0.73) by 0.03, and surpasses OmniGen (0.70) and D-DiT (0.65) by larger margins. On MJHQ-30K FID, Harmon-1.5B (5.15) beats Janus-Pro-1.5B (9.53) by 4.38 points and Show-o (15.18) by nearly 10 points, establishing a clear visual quality advantage. For image understanding, Harmon-1.5B matches or slightly exceeds Janus-Pro-1.5B on POPE (87.6 vs 86.2) and MMMU (38.9 vs 36.3) despite using a shared MAR encoder rather than a dedicated SigLIP encoder, while trailing on MME-P (1155 vs 1444) and MMB (65.5 vs 75.5). Harmon decisively outperforms VQGAN/VAE-based unified models: LWM scores only 75.2 on POPE and 0.47 on GenEval, Show-o achieves 80.0 POPE and 0.53 GenEval, and Chameleon reaches just 0.39 GenEval. Notably, the smaller Harmon-0.5B (0.71 GenEval, 6.08 MJHQ FID) already rivals the larger Janus-Pro-1.5B, suggesting the shared MAR encoder provides superior vision-language alignment at lower parameter cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the MAR (Masked Autoregressive) encoder [39], originally designed for image generation via masked image modeling, as a shared visual backbone for both understanding and generation within a single LLM-based framework. It departs from prior unified approaches (Janus, Show-o, LWM, Chameleon) that rely on VQGAN or VAE encoders—compression models that sacrifice semantic richness for pixel-level fidelity—or from decoupled designs that maintain separate encoders per task. The conceptual lineage traces to MIM [29], which demonstrated that mask-and-reconstruct pretraining yields semantically rich representations, and to the autoregressive LLM paradigm, making MAR a natural bridge between the two tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a generation-trained MAR encoder matches a contrastive vision-language encoder (SigLIP) on understanding benchmarks challenges the prevailing assumption that unified models must use dedicated semantic encoders or accept a significant understanding penalty. The observed synergy—where understanding supervision improves generation quality—suggests that shared representations can foster cross-task co-evolution rather than merely splitting the difference. Open questions include whether this approach scales to higher resolutions (beyond 512×512) and larger model sizes, whether it extends to video or 3D generation, and whether the three-stage training recipe generalizes to other LLM backbones or MAR variants.
