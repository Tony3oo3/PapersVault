## Problem

Existing DPO-based diffusion alignment methods suffer from two critical flaws: (1) they estimate intermediate-step rewards using the full-trajectory Q-value rather than the step-wise difference ΔQ, leading to biased preference ordering at each denoising step; and (2) the standard DPO loss can inadvertently increase the sampling probability of dispreferred samples as long as the relative preferred-to-dispreferred ratio grows, introducing safety risks and harming generalization.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a text-to-image diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1.5 or SDXL) conditioned on a text prompt, generating 512×512 RGB images via a 20-step DDIM or DDPM denoising schedule. During fine-tuning, only 5 uniformly sampled steps (t = 20, 16, 12, 8, 4) are optimized using LoRA adapters, and at each step two candidate denoised samples are drawn from the current policy to form a preference pair scored by a reward model (e.g., Aesthetic scorer, ImageReward, HPSv2, PickScore).

## Method

RDPO introduces two modifications to the standard step-wise DPO loss for diffusion models. First, it replaces the biased intermediate-step reward r(c, x̂₀(x_t)) — which is actually a cumulative Q-value over the entire denoising trajectory — with the unbiased step-wise reward r* = ΔQ ≈ r(c, x̂₀(x_t)) − r(c, x̂₀(x_{t−1})), capturing only the reward change attributable to the denoising action at step t. Second, it adds a penalty term Ψ = min(0, log(π_ref(x_{t−1}^l | x_t, c) / π_θ(x_{t−1}^l | x_t, c))) weighted by hyperparameter λ into the DPO objective, which is zero when the dispreferred sample's likelihood ratio is ≤ 1 and penalizes the model whenever that ratio exceeds 1, thereby preventing the DPO gradient from simultaneously inflating both preferred and dispreferred sample probabilities. The full loss is L_RDPO = −E[log σ(δ − λ·Ψ)] where δ is the standard DPO log-likelihood ratio difference, and the method is implemented on top of TailorPO-G with LoRA fine-tuning.

## Results

On the Animal Prompts dataset with SD v1.5, RDPO achieves the best scores across all six reward metrics: Aesthetic scorer 7.80, ImageReward 1.48, HPSv2 28.42, PickScore 21.10, Compressibility −2.37, and VP-Score 26.93. On the Pick-a-pic dataset, RDPO similarly leads with Aesthetic 5.90, ImageReward 0.37, HPSv2 25.71, PickScore 20.27, Compressibility −69.63, and VP-Score 24.32. The closest competitor, TailorPO-G, trails RDPO by 0.84 on Aesthetic scorer and 0.22 on ImageReward (Animal Prompts), while the weakest baseline SPO lags by 1.91 on Aesthetic scorer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RDPO outperforms all four baselines (DDPO, D3PO, SPO, and TailorPO/TailorPO-G) on every reward metric in both the Animal Prompts and Pick-a-pic evaluations. Against the strongest baseline TailorPO-G, RDPO gains +0.84 Aesthetic, +0.22 ImageReward, +0.39 HPSv2, +0.42 PickScore, and +0.66 VP-Score on Animal Prompts, and +0.32 Aesthetic, +0.12 ImageReward, +1.04 HPSv2, +0.41 PickScore, and +0.45 VP-Score on Pick-a-pic. D3PO and DDPO, which rely on the stronger Assumption 1 (final-image preference transfers to all intermediate steps), show substantially larger gaps: RDPO exceeds D3PO by +1.34 Aesthetic and +0.53 ImageReward on Animal Prompts. SPO, which uses a step-preference reward model, is the weakest baseline, trailing RDPO by +1.91 Aesthetic and +0.53 ImageReward. The ablation (RDPO* vs. RDPO) further confirms that both the revised ΔQ reward and the penalty term Ψ contribute independently, with the penalty term adding an additional +0.52 Aesthetic and +0.09 ImageReward on top of the improved reward estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO-for-diffusion line initiated by Diffusion-DPO and D3PO, which adapted the LLM DPO loss (Rafailov et al.) to step-wise denoising, and builds directly on TailorPO's intermediate-step preference ranking framework. It departs from prior work on two theoretical fronts: (1) showing that the commonly used step-wise reward r(c, x̂₀(x_t)) is actually a cumulative Q-value rather than a true per-step reward, and proposing ΔQ (the difference between consecutive Q-values) as the correct estimator; and (2) identifying that the standard DPO gradient can increase the log-likelihood of dispreferred samples whenever the preferred/dispreferred ratio grows, and introducing a penalty term Ψ to prevent this.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that DPO fine-tuning can raise the generation probability of dispreferred (potentially harmful) samples is practically important for safe deployment of aligned diffusion models, especially in distilled or few-step variants where such probability mass is amplified. The ΔQ correction and penalty term are simple, drop-in modifications that improve alignment quality, training stability, and out-of-distribution generalization across multiple base models (SD v1.5, SDXL) and schedulers (DDIM, DDPM). Open questions include whether the Q-value interpretation and penalty formulation transfer cleanly to video diffusion, autoregressive image models, or settings with multiple simultaneous reward objectives, and whether the approach remains effective as base model scale increases further.
