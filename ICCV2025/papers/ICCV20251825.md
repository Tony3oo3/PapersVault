## Problem

Light Field Displays (LFDs) require multi-perspective images with strict spatial consistency across dense viewpoints, refracted through cylindrical lens gratings to produce autostereoscopic 3D. Existing image/video generation models and radiance field methods cannot directly produce display content that satisfies the specific optical and geometric constraints of LFD hardware from a single 2D resource, creating a critical content scarcity barrier to widespread LFD adoption.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251825/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single 2D image, a video clip, or a text prompt (converted to an image via text-to-image generation) and produces 40 off-axis perspective views spanning −20° to +20°, which are then interlaced according to the target LFD's grating parameters to yield the final autostereoscopic display frame. Unlike conventional novel-view synthesis that uses circular camera trajectories, the output views are rendered from cameras positioned along a single horizontal line with shifted principal points, matching the off-axis geometry required by LFD optics. The field of view is constrained to less than 90°, so only front-facing scene content needs to be synthesized.

## Method

EYE³ is a three-stage pipeline: (1) monocular depth estimation (Depth Anything V2 for images, DepthCrafter for video) followed by inverse perspective projection to build a colored point cloud; (2) off-axis perspective rendering of the point cloud from 40 camera positions along a shared horizontal line, where each camera's principal point is shifted along x so all optical axes converge at a common point, producing multi-view images and binary masks marking occluded/missing regions; (3) a fine-tuned AnimateDiff V3 video diffusion model performs mask-guided inpainting to fill only the occluded regions while preserving already-rendered content. The key architectural choice is using a discrete point cloud rather than a mesh, which avoids depth-adhesion artifacts and yields clean, accurate inpainting masks. Training data is synthesized by applying edge detection and dilation to RealEstate10K video frames to approximate the spatial distribution of point-cloud rendering gaps, and the mask is concatenated as an additional channel into the denoising U-net.

## Results

On the Blender-rendered evaluation dataset, EYE³ achieves the highest PSNR (22.40 indoor, 20.31 outdoor) and SSIM (0.734 indoor, 0.541 outdoor) among all compared methods, outperforming the next-best ViewCrafter (off-axis & fine-tuned) by ~2.5 dB in indoor PSNR. In the user study (30 participants, 0–10 scale), EYE³ obtains a weighted score of 7.583, substantially exceeding CameraCtrl (5.413) and ViewCrafter off-axis (5.377). On the warp-error consistency metric for real-world images without ground truth, EYE³ achieves the lowest error (11.38–12.77) among all methods except 3D-Photo-Inpainting (1.62–2.57), which trades off consistency for weaker 3D effect and image quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EYE³ decisively outperforms all four baselines on PSNR and SSIM for both indoor and outdoor Blender-rendered scenes, with the largest gap over 3D-Photo-Inpainting (~7 dB indoor PSNR) and SV3D (~6 dB). Against SV3D, the advantage is particularly pronounced because SV3D's training on single-object Objaverse data limits its ability to handle multi-object scenes and small angular ranges typical of LFDs. CameraCtrl, while achieving moderate PSNR/SSIM, suffers from imprecise camera pose control (left-right reversal, incorrect rotation angles) due to its reliance on Plücker coordinates alone, resulting in a lower user-study weighted score (5.413 vs. 7.583). ViewCrafter (both original and off-axis fine-tuned) achieves competitive PSNR/SSIM but lacks mask-guided inpainting, causing it to redraw the entire image and introduce spatial inconsistencies in already-consistent regions; its warp-e values (20–76) are 2–7× higher than EYE³'s. The sole exception is 3D-Photo-Inpainting, which achieves the lowest warp-e (best multi-view consistency) due to its mesh-based explicit geometry, but it loses decisively on user-study scores (weighted 4.967 vs. 7.583) owing to weak 3D effect, mesh adhesion artifacts, and poor image quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of novel view synthesis and light field display (LFD) content generation, departing from prior NVS methods (SV3D, CameraCtrl, ViewCrafter) that rely on Plücker coordinates or 1D extrinsic parameters for camera control—approaches that cannot satisfy the off-axis perspective and dense-viewpoint constraints imposed by LFD grating optics. It builds on the LDM/AnimateDiff video diffusion architecture for occluded-region inpainting and borrows the interlacing pipeline from DirectL, but introduces a point-cloud-based explicit 3D representation rendered via off-axis perspective as the core geometric backbone, replacing the mesh-based representation of 3D-Photo-Inpainting to avoid depth-adhesion artifacts. The key conceptual departure is treating LFD content generation as a constrained NVS problem where camera poses are fully determined by hardware parameters, shifting the generative burden entirely to inpainting rather than view synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck—content scarcity for LFDs—by providing an end-to-end pipeline from arbitrary 2D inputs (images, videos, text) to display-ready autostereoscopic content, which could lower the barrier to LFD adoption in entertainment and advertising. However, the method's quality is tightly coupled to monocular depth estimation accuracy, and the per-frame video processing with multiple denoising iterations makes real-time or long-sequence generation impractical with current compute. Open questions include whether the point-cloud representation can be replaced by more compact radiance-field alternatives that still guarantee the off-axis consistency LFDs require, and whether the mask-guided inpainting strategy generalizes to wider angular ranges or dynamic scenes with significant parallax.
