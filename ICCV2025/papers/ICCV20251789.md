## Problem

Vision Transformers are as vulnerable to adversarial attacks as CNNs, and their large parameter counts make them especially prone to adversarial overfitting, degrading both clean and adversarial accuracy. While Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) can mitigate overfitting in principle, applying it globally across the full ViT architecture impedes convergence, and CNN-based layer-selection methods (CLAT, RiFT) fail on ViTs due to their diverse layer types. The paper addresses the gap of identifying which specific ViT layers are most susceptible to overfitting and targeting only those for regularization.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for Imagenette) and outputs class labels, but its distinctive input is a pre-trained ViT model that has already undergone initial adversarial training. Layer sharpness is computed using a very small batch (as few as 50 adversarial examples, ~0.001% of training data) per evaluation round, adding only ~0.2% overhead to the overall training pipeline. The output is a refined ViT with selectively updated weights in ~5% of its layers, evaluated under PGD-20 and AutoAttack at ε=0.03 (ℓ∞).

## Method

SAFER introduces a per-layer sharpness metric (Equ. 6) computed as the L2 norm of the adversarial loss gradient with respect to each layer's weights, summed over a batch, which identifies layers most prone to overfitting in a single backward pass. The top-K sharpest layers (~5% of total) are selected for fine-tuning while all other layers are frozen. On the selected layers, SAFER applies a SAM-style objective: the adversarial loss is evaluated at both the current weights and a perturbed version (w_i + ε, where ε is the normalized gradient direction scaled by ρ), and the maximum of the two is minimized. This process is iterated—sharpness is re-measured every 10 epochs to update the layer selection, allowing the method to track shifting overfitting hotspots. The approach is also integrated with PEFT frameworks (LoRA, DoRA), where SAM-based layer-selective updates are applied to the low-rank adaptation matrices rather than full weights.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, SAFER achieves 68.50% PGD-20 adversarial accuracy with DeiT-Ti, a 20.4-point improvement over PGD-AT(SGD) (48.10%) and 14.1 points over ARD+PRM (50.33%). For larger models, SAFER reaches 71.95% PGD-20 with ViT-B and 53.65% with Swin-B, consistently outperforming all baselines by 5–20 points on adversarial accuracy. Clean accuracy also improves substantially, e.g., 82.36% (SAFER) vs. 75.46% (PGD-AT(SGD)) for DeiT-Ti on CIFAR-10. Gains are consistent across CIFAR-100 and Imagenette, with SAFER achieving 40.15% PGD-20 on CIFAR-100 (DeiT-Ti) and 68.36% on Imagenette (DeiT-Ti).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAFER dominates all baselines on adversarial accuracy across every architecture tested. Against PGD-AT(SGD) [Madry et al.], SAFER gains 14–20 points on PGD-20 (e.g., 68.50% vs. 48.10% for DeiT-Ti on CIFAR-10). Against PGD-AT(SAM) [Foret et al.], which applies SAM globally as the optimizer, SAFER still leads by 10–18 points on PGD-20, demonstrating that layer-selective SAM is far more effective than full-model SAM for ViTs. Against ARD+PRM [Mo et al.], the strongest transformer-specific adversarial training recipe, SAFER improves PGD-20 by 14–18 points (e.g., 71.95% vs. 53.80% for ViT-B). Against CNN-oriented layer-selective methods RiFT [Zhu et al.] and CLAT [Gopal et al.], SAFER's advantage is even more dramatic on ViTs: on DeiT-Ti, SAFER achieves 68.50% PGD-20 versus 44.32% (RiFT) and 46.29% (CLAT), a gap of over 22 points, confirming that CNN-based criticality metrics fail for transformer architectures. Against Tian et al. [27], SAFER improves PGD-20 by 1.7–2.5 points when used as a post-hoc fine-tuning step ([27]+SAFER), and by 16+ points when applied as the primary training method.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAFER extends Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) from a global optimizer into a layer-selective fine-tuning strategy, drawing on the CNN literature on critical-layer identification (RiFT, CLAT) but replacing feature-based criticality indices with a gradient-norm sharpness metric suited to ViTs' heterogeneous layer types. It departs from prior SAM-for-adversarial-training work (Zhang et al., 2024), which applies SAM uniformly across all parameters and still suffers convergence issues on large ViTs, by freezing the majority of layers and restricting SAM to the top-K sharpest layers. The method also extends naturally to PEFT frameworks (LoRA, DoRA), positioning it within the broader parameter-efficient fine-tuning literature for large transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: SAFER adds only ~0.2% overhead to standard adversarial training while delivering consistent 5–20% gains in both clean and adversarial accuracy across ViT, DeiT, ConViT, and Swin architectures, and it composes with existing baselines (ARD+PRM, Tian et al.) and PEFT methods. A key open question the authors flag is *why* particular ViT layers are intrinsically prone to adversarial overfitting—no architectural or theoretical explanation is offered, and the sharpness metric remains an empirical proxy. The scope is limited to empirical (PGD/AutoAttack) robustness on image classification; generalization to other tasks, larger-scale models, or certified robustness settings is unexplored.
