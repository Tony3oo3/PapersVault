## Problem

Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) append a fixed, large number of visual tokens (e.g., 576 for LLaVA-1.5, 2048 for Video-LLaVA) as prefix context to the LLM, causing quadratic growth in attention computation cost. Prior efficiency efforts focused on shrinking the LLM backbone or applying quantization, but no work addressed the visual token count itself as a source of inefficiency, despite the fact that many visual tokens are spatially redundant.

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images (336×336 for LLaVA-1.5) or video clips (8 frames, 2048 tokens for Video-LLaVA) paired with a text prompt. The CLIP-ViT encoder produces visual tokens that are adaptively reduced before entering the LLM; the number of retained tokens is not fixed but varies per image based on content complexity (e.g., ~32 tokens on average for LLaVA-1.5, ~256 for Video-LLaVA). The output is a standard text response, and the method is plug-and-play: it can be applied training-free at inference or with a single epoch of LoRA fine-tuning.

## Method

PruMerge exploits the sparsity of attention scores between the [CLS] token and spatial visual tokens in the penultimate layer of CLIP-ViT to adaptively select important tokens via Interquartile Range (IQR) outlier detection, so that complex images retain more tokens and simple images retain fewer. A Token Supplement step then clusters pruned tokens into the retained ones using k-nearest-neighbor matching on key-vector similarity and updates each retained token via a weighted average, preserving information from discarded tokens. PruMerge+ extends this with spatially uniform sampling of additional tokens to complement the outlier-selected set, achieving a 4× reduction with minimal performance loss. The approach is orthogonal to LLM-level optimizations like quantization and requires no architectural changes to the LLM or visual encoder.

## Results

On six visual QA/reasoning benchmarks, LLaVA-1.5+PruMerge+ (using only 25% of visual tokens on average) achieves 76.8 on VQAv2, 68.3 on ScienceQA, 57.1 on TextVQA, 84.0 on POPE, 1462.4 on MME, and 64.9 on MMBench, closely matching the full-token LLaVA-1.5 (78.5/66.8/58.2/85.9/1510.7/64.3). The more aggressive PruMerge variant (5.5% tokens, ~32 tokens/image) still achieves 72.0/68.5/56.0/76.3/1350.3/60.9. In efficiency terms, PruMerge reduces LLM prefill FLOPs from 9.3 TB to 0.91 TB (10× reduction) and prefill time from 88.6 ms to 15.3 ms for Vicuna-7B in FP16. On video benchmarks, PruMerge+ applied training-free to Video-LLaVA improves ActivityNet-QA accuracy from 45.3 to 47.7 while reducing visual tokens from 2048 to 512.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the full-token LLaVA-1.5 baseline, PruMerge+ (25% tokens) matches or exceeds performance on ScienceQA (68.3 vs 66.8) and MMBench (64.9 vs 64.3), while remaining within 1.7 points on VQAv2 and 1.9 on POPE. Compared to other LMMs, PruMerge+ outperforms BLIP-2 by 35.8 points on VQAv2 and 168.6 on MME, and beats InstructBLIP-13B on SQA (68.3 vs 63.1) and MME (1462.4 vs 1212.8). Against Qwen-VL-Chat, PruMerge+ wins on MMBench (64.9 vs 60.6) but trails slightly on VQAv2 (76.8 vs 78.2) and MME (1462.4 vs 1487.5). In the dedicated token-reduction comparison (Table 7), PruMerge+ at 25% ratio dramatically outperforms uni-modal methods ToMe (66.0), ATS (66.7), and EViT (65.5) on VQAv2, and surpasses the concurrent multimodal method CrossGet at 50% ratio on SQA (68.5 vs 66.7) and VQAv2 (77.6 vs 77.3). On video QA, PruMerge+ applied training-free to Video-LLaVA improves ActivityNet-QA accuracy from 45.3 to 47.7 and MSRVT-QA score from 3.5 to 3.6, demonstrating that token reduction can actually enhance video understanding by removing redundant tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes a property of CLIP-ViT—the sparse attention distribution between the [CLS] token and spatial patch tokens—as an adaptive signal for visual token pruning in LMMs, a use not explored by prior token reduction work. It explicitly departs from uni-modal ViT token reduction methods (ToMe, ATS, EViT), which target internal ViT efficiency, by instead targeting the LLM backbone's quadratic attention cost from prefix visual tokens. The k-NN clustering for token merging is positioned as a simpler O(n) alternative to CrossGet's O(n²) graph-matching approach, and the IQR outlier-detection mechanism for adaptive token count selection is borrowed from classical statistics rather than learned pruning criteria.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a 4–10× FLOPs reduction in LMM prefill with minimal performance loss, and the method is plug-and-play (training-free on Video-LLaVA), making it immediately deployable. The finding that only ~5.5% of visual tokens carry most of the information challenges the assumption that LMMs need large fixed visual token budgets. Open questions include whether the IQR threshold generalizes across vision encoders and resolutions, whether the approach degrades on tasks requiring fine-grained spatial detail (POPE shows a notable drop), and whether the sparsity signal remains informative for higher-resolution or longer video inputs where token counts grow further.
