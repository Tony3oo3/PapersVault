## Problem

Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) for video generation incur substantial computational and memory costs due to quadratic attention over long timesteps, making real-time deployment impractical. Existing acceleration techniques—quantization, feature caching, and layer pruning—are typically applied in isolation with static heuristics (fixed bit-widths, predefined cache schedules) that fail to adapt to the dynamic importance of layers and timesteps during the iterative denoising process.

## Input / Output

QuantCache takes a text prompt as input and generates a 64-frame, 512×512 video via Open-Sora 1.2 over 100 denoising timesteps. The framework is entirely training-free and operates at reduced precision (8/8 or 4/6 bits for weights/activations), with all adaptive decisions (cache refresh, bit-width assignment, layer pruning) computed online at inference time with minimal overhead. Optimized CUDA kernels with kernel fusion handle the quantization-rotation-caching pipeline on a single NVIDIA A800-80GB GPU.

## Method

QuantCache jointly optimizes three complementary acceleration mechanisms driven by a shared feature-divergence signal. (1) Hierarchical Latent Caching (HLC) computes a per-layer, per-timestep divergence score combining L1 feature change and inter-frame gradients, then adaptively sets the cache-refresh interval (long skip for low divergence, short skip for high divergence). (2) Adaptive Importance-Guided Quantization (AIGQ) allocates a total bit-width budget across layers based on sensitivity to quantization error, and dynamically modulates activation bit-widths per timestep using the same divergence metric—high-redundancy steps use fewer bits while critical transition steps retain higher precision. (3) Structural Redundancy-Aware Pruning (SRAP) computes cosine similarity between consecutive layer outputs within a timestep and prunes layers exceeding a redundancy threshold, with pruning probability further modulated by cumulative feature variation across prior timesteps. The three tiers operate at different granularities (cross-timestep caching, per-timestep precision, intra-timestep layer omission) and are co-optimized so that post-skip steps receive higher precision to compensate for cached features, yielding a 6.72× end-to-end speedup with negligible quality degradation.

## Results

On VBench, QuantCache (8/8) achieves an Overall Consistency score of 26.97, marginally surpassing the FP16 Open-Sora baseline (26.89) and ViDiT-Q (26.74). In the more aggressive 4/6 bit-width setting, QuantCache retains an Overall Consistency of 26.77, outperforming all competing methods at 4/8 bit-width (best competitor: ViDiT-Q at 26.61). On CLIP/Dover metrics, QuantCache (8/8) achieves the best CLIP-Temporal score (0.9989) and competitive VQA-Aesthetic (60.19) and VQA-Technical (52.39) scores. The full framework delivers a 6.72× end-to-end speedup on a single NVIDIA A800-80GB GPU, compared to 2.24× for the best prior method (AdaCache-fast).

## Baselines & Comparisons

QuantCache (8/8) achieves the highest Overall Consistency (26.97) among all 8-bit methods, edging out ViDiT-Q (26.74) and Q-DiT (26.77), while also leading in Motion Smoothness (98.52 vs. 98.28 for ViDiT-Q). In the low-bit regime, QuantCache at 4/6 substantially outperforms all 4/8 competitors: ViDiT-Q (4/8) scores 26.61 Overall Consistency, while Q-DiT (4/8) collapses to 5.11 and PTQ4DiT (4/8) to 11.76, demonstrating that QuantCache's adaptive bit allocation preserves quality far better than uniform 4-bit quantization. On CLIP/Dover metrics, QuantCache (8/8) achieves the best CLIP-Temporal score (0.9989) and VQA-Technical (52.39), slightly trailing ViDiT-Q on VQA-Aesthetic (60.19 vs. 59.91). For speedup, QuantCache (6.72×) dramatically surpasses AdaCache-fast (2.24×), ViDiT-Q (1.71×), PAB (1.34×), and T-Gate (1.10×), with the gap attributable to the joint use of low-bit quantization, adaptive caching, and layer pruning. The ablation confirms each component contributes: HLC alone gives 4.12×, adding AIGQ reaches 6.33×, and SRAP pushes to the final 6.72×.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by jointly optimizing three previously isolated acceleration techniques—feature caching (building on AdaCache and Δ-DiT), post-training quantization (building on ViDiT-Q and SmoothQuant), and layer pruning (building on ShortGPT and dynamic LLM slicing)—into a unified, content-adaptive framework for Diffusion Transformers. The key conceptual departure is that each component's decisions (cache refresh, bit-width allocation, layer skipping) are driven by shared, timestep-aware feature-divergence metrics rather than static schedules or uniform heuristics, creating a self-adaptive compute allocation strategy. Unlike prior work that applies quantization, caching, or pruning independently, QuantCache couples them so that caching decisions inform quantization precision and pruning thresholds, exploiting synergies that isolated methods miss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6.72× end-to-end speedup with training-free deployment on Open-Sora is practically significant for making high-fidelity video generation accessible on resource-constrained hardware, and the joint-optimization framework offers a template for combining multiple efficiency techniques in a principled way. However, the evaluation is limited to a single model (Open-Sora 1.2) and a single benchmark suite (VBench + CLIP/Dover), leaving open whether the adaptive thresholds and cosine-similarity pruning generalize across DiT architectures, video lengths, or resolutions. The modest quality degradation in the 4/6 configuration (e.g., Aesthetic Quality dropping from 60.07 to 58.63) and the small marginal gain from SRAP over HLC+AIGQ (6.33× → 6.72×) suggest that further gains may require more aggressive or learned adaptation strategies.
