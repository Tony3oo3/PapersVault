## Problem

Existing personalized federated learning methods rely on serial architectures (shared backbone followed by a local classifier) where the global model is directly used for local training without assessing its impact on local performance. This serial processing creates a conflict between personalization and generalization, as information loss or degradation occurs when one component overshadows or fails to convey details needed by the other, particularly under highly heterogeneous data distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251831/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives image inputs (32×32 RGB for CIFAR-10/100, grayscale for FEMNIST, variable-size for ImageNet) and produces a class label via the sum of predictions from two parallel branches: a locally-trained supervisor and a shared inter-learning model. The supervisor architecture is client-specific and never transmitted to the server—only the inter-learning model (a LeNet-5 scaled to roughly one-sixth of the full model size) is uploaded for aggregation. This design allows clients with varying computational resources to choose different supervisor architectures (e.g., Transformer, CNN, or LeNet-5) without affecting the federated communication protocol.

## Method

FedSimSup replaces the serial backbone-classifier structure with a parallel architecture consisting of a local supervisor (trained locally, never uploaded) and an inter-learning model (uploaded and aggregated on the server). During local training, the supervisor is first updated for τ_s epochs to adapt to the incoming global information, then the inter-learning model is updated for τ_θ epochs under the supervisor's guidance, ensuring the model remains aligned with the local data distribution. For non-participating clients, the server aggregates their inter-learning models using a similarity-weighted scheme based on cosine similarity of label distributions, modulated by a data-volume ratio λ_i and a round-dependent decay factor β^t that decreases after a threshold to ensure convergence. The paper provides a convergence guarantee (Theorem 1) showing the average gradient norm decays at a rate dependent on the participation rate, data heterogeneity, and the hyperparameters C and γ controlling the global-information incorporation schedule.

## Results

FedSimSup achieves the highest accuracy across all four datasets and all Non-IID settings. On CIFAR-10 (Dirichlet α=0.1, 100 clients), it reaches 89.2% accuracy, outperforming the strongest baselines FedAs (87.4%) and pFedFda (87.8%). On CIFAR-100 (Dirichlet α=0.1, 100 clients), FedSimSup attains 50.3% versus 46.7% for FedAs and 46.3% for pFedFda. On FEMNIST and ImageNet (Dirichlet α=0.1), it scores 84.3% and 22.4% respectively, leading all competitors by 3.1 and 1.2 percentage points over the next-best methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedSimSup outperforms all eight baselines on every dataset and every Non-IID setting tested. Against the two strongest recent methods, FedAs (CVPR 2024) and pFedFda (NeurIPS 2025), FedSimSup gains 1.8 pp on CIFAR-10 Dir(0.1)/100 clients (89.2% vs 87.4%) and 3.6 pp on CIFAR-100 Dir(0.1)/100 clients (50.3% vs 46.7%). The gap widens under more heterogeneous settings: on CIFAR-100 Dir(0.1)/100 clients, FedSimSup leads FedRep by 4.9 pp and FedProto by 9.6 pp. Per-FedAvg collapses on CIFAR-100 (0.3–10.3% accuracy), confirming that meta-learning-based personalization struggles with high-class-count tasks. On FEMNIST, FedSimSup (84.3%) surpasses FedRep (81.2%) by 3.1 pp, while on ImageNet it edges out pFedFda (21.2%) by 1.2 pp. Under the Pathological distribution (Table 3), FedSimSup maintains its lead, e.g., 46.4% vs 44.3% (pFedFda) on CIFAR-100 Shard(20)/100 clients.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant serial parameter-decoupling paradigm in personalized federated learning (e.g., FedRep, FedProto, FedPac, FedAs), where a shared backbone is trained first and a local classifier is trained on top of it. Instead, FedSimSup introduces a parallel architecture with a locally-trained supervisor and a server-aggregated inter-learning model, arguing that serial processing risks information loss when one component overshadows the other. The similarity-based aggregation for non-participating clients extends the standard FedAvg weighted average by incorporating cosine similarity of label distributions, and the convergence analysis follows the standard smoothness/bounded-gradient assumptions used in prior FL theory (FedProx, Per-FedAvg).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parallel supervisor-inter-learning design offers a practical flexibility that serial decoupling methods lack: clients with heterogeneous computational resources can adopt different supervisor architectures (Transformer, CNN, LeNet-5) without affecting the shared inter-learning model, which is validated experimentally. However, the similarity metric relies solely on label distribution proportions rather than feature-level similarity, which may be insufficient for more subtle heterogeneity patterns. Open questions include how the approach scales to larger backbones (the experiments use LeNet-5), whether the supervisor's role generalizes beyond classification, and how the method performs under extreme heterogeneity where label distributions overlap significantly.
