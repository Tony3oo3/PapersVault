## Problem

Existing radar-based state estimation methods rely on CFAR-processed point clouds, which suffer from severe sparsity, ghost points from multi-path effects, and limited angle resolution in single-chip radars, making reliable scan-matching and velocity estimation difficult. No prior work has fused raw radar signal spectra with inertial data in a self-supervised manner, leaving the complementary information between exteroceptive radar and proprioceptive IMU largely unexploited for robust localization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250365/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw Range-Azimuth Spectra (RAS) from a single-chip mmWave radar (TI AWR1843), interpolated from 128×128 to 256×256 with speckle noise filtering, alongside synchronized IMU accelerometer and gyroscope streams. It outputs per-frame ego pose (position, velocity, orientation) and sub-pixel geometry-consistent landmark points with 248-dimensional descriptors, which are further integrated into a spatially coherent map. Notably, the RAS is used directly as a 2D signal spectrum rather than being reduced to sparse point clouds, and the entire pipeline is self-supervised without requiring localization ground truth.

## Method

S³E exploits the key insight that inter-frame rotation manifests as a linear translation along the azimuth axis in the RAS, enabling a Rotation-based Cross Fusion module that shifts and blends adjacent spectra using IMU-derived rotation to enhance spatial structure. A U-Net backbone with multi-head outputs (sub-pixel location, score, and 248-dim descriptor) extracts and associates landmarks across frames via differentiable attention-based matching. A differentiable velocity estimation module solves instantaneous ego velocity from Doppler measurements of stationary landmarks using a closed-form least-squares solution, which also serves as a RANSAC criterion to filter dynamic targets and clutter. Training is fully self-supervised through three jointly optimized losses: a Mahalanobis-distance geometry constraint aligning landmark pairs with IMU pre-integration, a kinematic constraint enforcing the cosine relationship between Doppler velocities and ego velocity, and a velocity alignment loss bridging IMU and radar velocity estimates. The network is initialized from a RadarHD pre-trained model and fine-tuned on the ColoRadar dataset.

## Results

On the ColorRadar dataset (7 scenarios), S³E achieves the lowest average pose error with a translation error of 3.10% and a rotation error of 4.05 deg/100m, outperforming the supervised Milliego baseline by 22.2% in translation and 16.8% in rotation. On the self-collected dataset (7 unseen sequences), S³E further demonstrates strong generalization, reducing translation error to 2.35–6.92% versus 8.95–18.6% for Milliego, while model-based baselines (EKF-RIO, PG-RIO, 4D-RIO) fail entirely on outdoor sequences. Relative pose error analysis shows S³E reduces rotation and translation errors by 11% and 5.7% respectively over Milliego at the 90% confidence level.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S³E outperforms all four baselines on the ColorRadar dataset. Against the supervised learning baseline Milliego [26], S³E achieves 22.2% lower average translation error (3.10% vs. ~4.0%) and 16.8% lower rotation error (4.05 vs. ~4.86 deg/100m), while also producing geometry-consistent landmark points that Milliego cannot extract. Against the model-based EKF-RIO [10], PG-RIO [41], and 4D-RIO [39], S³E wins on most scenarios; these CFAR-based methods fail entirely on the longboard sequence (sparse point clouds) and show large errors on dynamic scenes (e.g., PG-RIO reaches 14.7 deg/100m rotation error on outdoors). On the self-collected dataset, S³E generalizes to unseen scenes without retraining, achieving 2.35–6.92% translation error, while Milliego degrades to 8.95–18.6% and all three model-based baselines fail on outdoor sequences. The ablation confirms that the Rotation-based Cross Fusion module contributes an additional 11–19% improvement in both state estimation and landmark extraction quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of radar-inertial odometry that relies on CFAR-extracted point clouds (EKF-RIO, PG-RIO, 4D-RIO) or supervised point-cloud-to-pose regression (Milliego), instead operating directly on Range-Azimuth Spectra (RAS) as a richer, denser representation. Conceptually, it repurposes the attention mechanism and U-Net architecture from vision tasks for spectral landmark extraction, and introduces a rotation-based cross-fusion that maps IMU-derived angular increments into linear spectral translations—a geometric insight not previously exploited in radar state estimation. The self-supervised loss design (geometry, kinematic, and velocity-alignment constraints) draws on factor-graph-style constraints from VIO literature but applies them without any localization ground truth, distinguishing it from Milliego's supervised training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By bypassing CFAR point extraction, S3E addresses a fundamental bottleneck in low-resolution single-chip radar odometry (sparsity, ghost points, missed detections) and demonstrates that spectral representations can yield more geometry-consistent landmarks and more robust pose estimation. The self-supervised formulation is practically significant because it eliminates the need for LiDAR-based ground truth, enabling deployment in environments where such supervision is unavailable. However, the method is limited to 2D range-azimuth spectra from a single-chip radar, relies on RANSAC-based static landmark filtering only at test time, and has not been validated on 4D imaging radars or in highly dynamic outdoor scenes—open questions that limit immediate broad adoption.
