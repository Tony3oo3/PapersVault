## Problem

Gaussian Splatting methods achieve high-fidelity novel view synthesis but require a large number of primitives because geometry and appearance are entangled in each Gaussian's parameters. This entanglement forces excessive densification in texture-rich regions (overparameterizing geometry) while underparameterizing the high-frequency texture itself, and produces needle-like artifacts as Gaussians shrink to capture sharp color edges.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251952/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images with known camera poses and produces novel-view RGB renderings via differentiable splatting. A distinctive output is an explicit textured mesh: after extracting the 2D Gaussian surface as a mesh and unwrapping it to a UV atlas, the trained hash-grid feature field is queried at each UV pixel's world coordinate to bake a conventional texture map. Each Gaussian primitive stores only 10 floating-point values (position, 2D scale, rotation, opacity), with all appearance information residing in a shared hash table of L×T×F parameters.

## Method

NeST-Splatting fully decouples geometry from appearance by replacing per-Gaussian spherical harmonic colors with a global multi-resolution hash grid (Instant-NGP) that encodes a continuous 'shell texture' over the scene. 2D Gaussian surfels serve purely as geometric samplers: at each ray-splat intersection (u,v), the world-space point is computed via the surfel's homogeneous transform and used to query the hash grid for a feature vector. Rendering uses a deferred strategy—alpha-blended feature vectors are accumulated in screen space into a feature image, which a small MLP then decodes into RGB, avoiding per-intersection MLP calls. Training employs feature-level annealing (progressively activating higher hash-grid resolutions) and backpropagates gradients through the hash encoding to the surfel parameters, enabling stable joint optimization of geometry and texture with a combined photometric and regularization loss.

## Results

On NeRFSynthetic, NeST-Splatting achieves PSNR 33.50 / SSIM 0.967 / LPIPS 0.032 using only 73k Gaussian primitives (vs. 288k for 3DGS and 207k for SuperGS). On DTU, it reaches PSNR 33.96 / LPIPS 0.042 with 80k points, and on Mip-NeRF 360-indoor it attains PSNR 30.59 / LPIPS 0.174 with 356k points—roughly 4× fewer primitives than 3DGS (1457k) and 2.5× fewer than 2DGS (876k). For geometry reconstruction on DTU, the method achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.83 mm with 80k points, comparable to 2DGS (0.80 mm, 214k points) while using fewer than half the primitives. The method consistently achieves the best LPIPS scores across all three datasets, indicating superior perceptual quality in texture-rich regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3DGS, NeST-Splatting uses 4–5× fewer primitives across all datasets while matching or exceeding PSNR (e.g., 33.50 vs. 33.34 on NeRFSyn, 33.96 vs. 33.77 on DTU) and achieving a notably lower LPIPS on MipNeRF360 (0.174 vs. 0.188). Compared to 2DGS, the method reduces point count by 28–63% (73k vs. 102k on NeRFSyn, 80k vs. 214k on DTU, 356k vs. 876k on MipNeRF360) while improving PSNR on all three datasets and LPIPS on MipNeRF360 (0.174 vs. 0.189). Versus SuperGS, which also uses spatially varying colors, NeST-Splatting requires 2.8–4.9× fewer primitives and achieves a substantially better LPIPS on MipNeRF360 (0.174 vs. 0.188), though SuperGS retains a slight PSNR edge on NeRFSyn (33.71 vs. 33.50). Against GsTex, the method uses 1.4–2.3× fewer points and achieves the best LPIPS on MipNeRF360 (0.174 vs. 0.204), with comparable PSNR on NeRFSyn and DTU. For geometry reconstruction on DTU, NeST-Splatting achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.83 mm with 80k points, slightly behind 2DGS (0.80 mm, 214k points) but well ahead of 3DGS (1.96 mm, 359k points) and GaussianSurfel (0.88 mm, 168k points), while matching NeuS (0.84 mm) at a fraction of the primitive count.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 2DGS for its 2D Gaussian surfel geometry representation and backward ray tracing, and on Instant-NGP's multi-resolution hash encoding for the texture field. It departs from prior spatially-varying-color GS methods (SuperGS, GsTex, Textured-GS) that still bind per-primitive texture to individual Gaussians, instead introducing a global neural shell texture queried at ray-splat intersections. This achieves a full geometry-appearance decoupling that prior per-primitive texture approaches could not, since those methods still require densification in texture-rich regions and suffer from discontinuous gradient fields.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean separation of geometry (explicit 2D surfels) from appearance (implicit hash-grid field) offers a principled path to reducing primitive counts by ~3x while improving high-frequency texture fidelity, and enables straightforward textured mesh extraction. However, the significant speed penalty (5x slower training, 3x slower rendering than 2DGS) limits immediate practical deployment, and the paper acknowledges this as a key limitation. Open questions include whether the decoupling extends to dynamic scenes, whether more efficient texture field architectures can close the speed gap, and whether the approach generalizes to unbounded or large-scale scenes beyond the indoor Mip-NeRF 360 evaluation.
