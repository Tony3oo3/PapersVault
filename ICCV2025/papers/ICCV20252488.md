## Problem

Existing online point trackers fail to re-establish correspondences after long-term occlusions because reappearing points undergo large viewpoint and scene-context changes that local feature correlation cannot bridge. Meanwhile, the strongest window-based trackers (CoTracker3, TAPTRv2) require access to future frames for joint optimization, making them unsuitable for latency-sensitive online applications such as SLAM and robotic manipulation. No prior method combines robust retracking after prolonged occlusions with a strictly online (no-future-frame) operating mode.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video stream of RGB frames (resized to 256×256) and a set of 2D query points in the initial frame, which may be floating (non-grid) coordinates sampled via grid interpolation on the feature map. It produces, for each newly captured frame, the 2D location of every query point along with per-point occlusion and certainty scores. The global stage outputs coarse locations from a 64×64 distribution map (4096 classification anchors per point), which the local refinement stage then corrects iteratively at three resolution scales (16×, 8×, 2×).

## Method

ReTracker is a two-stage online tracker. The global tracking module uses a DINOv2 backbone to extract dense features from the initial and current frames, then bridges the temporal context gap via a recurrently updated memory-token mechanism (cross-attention between current features and compact history tokens) followed by a spatial attention layer with a global receptive field inspired by two-view matchers (LoFTR, RoMa). A transformer decoder predicts a per-point distribution map whose argmax yields coarse correspondences. The key training insight is a two-stage curriculum: the spatial attention and decoder are first pretrained on large-scale two-view matching data (MegaDepth, ScanNet++) to learn general matching priors, then jointly fine-tuned with the temporal attention module on synthetic tracking data (Kubric) using Focal Loss. The multi-scale local refinement module crops feature patches around the coarse locations, computes 4D correlations against up to 12 history frames, and applies interleaved spatial-temporal attention to iteratively regress sub-pixel offsets, occlusion, and certainty scores at three resolution levels.

## Results

On the Ego-ReTrack dataset (the paper's novel occlusion-heavy benchmark), ReTracker achieves AJ=44.3, δ_avg=56.6, and OA=82.2 in online mode, outperforming the best online baseline BootsTAPIR (AJ=38.1, δ_avg=50.9) by 5.7 points in δ_avg and remaining competitive with the best offline method CoTracker3‡ (AJ=46.0, δ_avg=56.0). On standard TAP-Vid benchmarks, ReTracker achieves δ_avg of 67.0 (Kinetics), 77.5 (DAVIS), 80.7 (RGB-Stacking), and 78.1 (RoboTAP) in online mode, surpassing all online baselines and even exceeding offline CoTracker3 on DAVIS (77.5 vs 76.2) and RoboTAP (78.1 vs 73.4). The method is trained on Kubric synthetic data plus two-view pretraining on MegaDepth and ScanNet++, without the 15M+ real videos used by BootsTAPIR or the 15K real sequences used by CoTracker3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReTracker outperforms all online baselines across every benchmark and metric. On Ego-ReTrack, it beats BootsTAPIR-online by 5.7 points in δ_avg (56.6 vs 50.9) and 6.2 points in AJ (44.3 vs 38.1), while also surpassing CoTracker3 with step-size=1 (43.5 δ_avg) by 3.1 points. On DAVIS, ReTracker's online δ_avg of 77.5 exceeds even the offline CoTracker3 (76.2) and offline TAPTRv2 (75.9), and on RoboTAP it reaches 78.1 δ_avg versus 73.4 for offline CoTracker3. On Kinetics, ReTracker (67.0 δ_avg) is competitive with offline BootsTAPIR (68.5) and CoTracker3+15K (67.8) despite using far less training data. The main gap is on RGB-Stacking, where offline CoTracker3+15K reaches 85.2 δ_avg versus ReTracker's 80.7, likely due to the textureless robotic scenes benefiting from the larger real-video training sets. The authors note that scaling with more real-world data and longer sequences could further close this gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReTracker bridges two previously separate research threads: sliding-window point trackers (TAPIR, CoTracker3, TAPTRv2) that achieve robust offline tracking through iterative local refinement, and detector-free two-view image matchers (LoFTR, RoMa, MatchAnything) that use global-receptive-field architectures trained on large-scale matching data. The key departure is repurposing the matching architecture and its large-scale pretraining strategy for the sequential tracking problem, augmented with a recurrent memory-token temporal attention module to bridge the appearance gap between the initial query frame and the current frame. Unlike prior trackers that rely on future frames within a sliding window, ReTracker operates strictly online, and unlike prior matchers it must handle temporal accumulation of context across many intermediate frames rather than a single image pair.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to retrack points after long-term occlusion in an online (no-future-frame) setting directly addresses a practical gap for latency-sensitive applications such as SLAM and robotic manipulation, where waiting for future frames is infeasible. The two-view pretraining strategy offers a general recipe for overcoming the well-known scarcity of diverse, occlusion-heavy tracking annotations by borrowing priors from the much larger matching data ecosystem. Limitations include a small new benchmark (30 sequences), reliance on the computationally heavy DINOv2 backbone, and a residual gap to the strongest offline methods on some metrics; the authors themselves note that scaling with more real-world video data could close this gap. An open question is whether the global-matching-plus-temporal-attention decomposition generalizes to 3D point tracking or to even longer occlusion periods beyond the tested 300–600-frame sequences.
