## Problem

Vision-language models suffer from high inference latency because visual tokens dominate compute and memory, yet existing token-pruning methods face a fundamental trade-off: query-agnostic pruning loses fine-grained details, while query-aware pruning permanently discards tokens needed for subsequent turns in multi-turn conversations. Moreover, most prior work optimizes only the prefill stage, ignoring the decoding stage that dominates latency in real-world applications involving long generations and multi-round dialogue.

## Input / Output

SparseVILA operates on the KV cache of a frozen VLM backbone (e.g., LLaVA-NeXT-7B, LongVILA-7B, Qwen2-VL), taking native-resolution images or up to 256 video frames (64K context tokens) plus a text query as input and producing a text response. The visual context is prefilled once per conversation, and for each subsequent query the method retrieves a sparse subset of visual KV entries to participate in generation. A partial KV-cache eviction strategy removes only the previous question/answer entries between turns to prevent information leakage while retaining the visual context.

## Method

SparseVILA introduces a decoupled sparsity framework that separates visual token reduction into two stages. During prefill, query-agnostic pruning uses mean-column-sum attention scores from the visual encoder to remove redundant tokens before the LLM, preserving multi-turn capability since the selection is query-independent. During decoding, a query-aware retrieval mechanism computes attention between the current query and remaining visual KV entries at each layer, selecting a compact subset that participates in all generation steps for that turn. The key design choice is migrating the majority of sparsity into the decode stage (e.g., 90% decode sparsity vs. 67% prefill sparsity), which retains more context tokens for multi-turn accuracy while still achieving significant decoding speedup. Implementation builds a compact dense KV cache per query to avoid non-contiguous sparse access, uses FlashAttention2 throughout, and employs a custom Triton kernel that streams attention-weight normalization without materializing the full S×S matrix, reducing peak memory by 39.3% on long-context video tasks.

## Results

On LLaVA-NeXT-7B, SparseVILA achieves a 1.44× end-to-end speedup (67% prefill + 90% decode sparsity) while maintaining or exceeding baseline accuracy on most benchmarks: DocVQA 54.4 vs. 74.4 baseline, GQA 63.4 vs. 64.2, POPE 86.1 vs. 85.9, and MME 1827.8 vs. 1846.1. On long-context video (LongVILA-7B, 64K context), SparseVILA achieves 3.6× prefill and 1.7× decode speedup (2.2× end-to-end) with VideoMME overall 60.3 vs. 59.7 baseline and MLVU m-avg 74.4 vs. 72.4. On the VideoChatGPT free-form generation benchmark, SparseVILA delivers lossless quality (mean 2.41 vs. 2.33) at 2.2× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against query-aware baselines at comparable speedup (~1.37–1.38×), SparseVILA (67%/50%) achieves DocVQA 60.8 vs. FastV 54.4 and SparseVLM 54.4, a 6.4-point gain, while also leading on ChartQA (41.8 vs. 37.3/37.7) and InfoVQA (33.3 vs. 31.8/31.4). Against query-agnostic baselines at ~1.43–1.44× speedup, SparseVILA (67%/90%) outperforms PruMerge (DocVQA 54.4 vs. 36.9, +17.5), HIRED (54.4 vs. 41.6, +12.8), and VisionZip (54.4 vs. 51.2, +3.2), with the largest margins on text-centric benchmarks (ChartQA 39.9 vs. 26.9/29.7/34.1). On long-context video (Table 2), SparseVILA (75%/94%, 2.2× E2E) surpasses HIRED (VideoMME 60.3 vs. 57.4) and VisionZip (60.3 vs. 57.5) while also exceeding the unpruned LongVILA baseline (60.3 vs. 59.7), whereas FastV and SparseVLM cannot scale to 64K context due to OOM. The paper reports a +5.9% average accuracy improvement over prior methods on image-centric benchmarks, attributed to retaining more context tokens by migrating sparsity into the decode stage.

## Relation to Prior Work

SparseVILA synthesizes two previously separate lineages of VLM token pruning—query-agnostic methods (PruMerge, VisionZip, HIRED) that reduce visual redundancy based on encoder saliency, and query-aware methods (FastV, SparseVLM, PDrop) that use text-visual attention to guide pruning—into a unified decoupled framework. The key departure is the architectural decision to apply query-agnostic pruning only during prefill (preserving multi-turn context) and defer query-aware selection to the decode stage via KV-cache retrieval, rather than committing to a single pruning point as prior work does. This design also draws on KV-cache compression ideas from SnapKV and Quest, but avoids their limitations (SnapKV discards the full cache, Quest re-runs selection per token) by computing a compact dense cache once per query.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: most VLM acceleration work optimizes only the prefill stage, yet real-world applications (multi-turn conversation, video captioning, long-form generation) are dominated by decode latency. By demonstrating that migrating sparsity to the decode stage yields a 5.9% accuracy gain on image benchmarks and lossless performance at 2.2× end-to-end speedup on long-context video, the paper provides a clear recipe for practitioners deploying VLMs interactively. Open questions include whether layer-wise or head-aware sparsity schedules could further improve the speedup-accuracy tradeoff, and whether the decoupled framework extends naturally to audio or 3D modalities. The released multi-turn evaluation protocol also fills a methodological gap, as prior benchmarks masked the degradation of query-aware pruning by using single-turn (re-prefill) evaluation.
