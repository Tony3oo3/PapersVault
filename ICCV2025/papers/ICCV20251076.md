## Problem

Existing Mamba-based anomaly detectors (e.g., MambaAD) suffer from two coupled limitations: their spatial-domain scanning inadequately models diverse local features, causing missed or mislocalized subtle anomalies, and the fixed spatial-wise scanning disrupts the spatial continuity of large-scale anomalies, yielding incomplete localization. The paper asks whether decomposing features into frequency bands and applying direction-specific state space scanning to each band can simultaneously resolve both issues within a single multi-class framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251076/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image resized to 256×256 and produces a pixel-level anomaly score map (obtained by summing cosine similarities of multi-scale features across three decoder stages) and an image-level anomaly score (the maximum of the average-pooled anomaly map). A single pretrained model handles all classes across MVTec-AD, VisA, and MPDD without per-class retraining, and inference runs at 58.8 FPS with 1142 MB memory on an RTX A6000.

## Method

Wave-MambaAD is a pyramid autoencoder that applies a Haar wavelet transform to decoder features, splitting them into high-frequency (horizontal, vertical, diagonal) and low-frequency subbands. The high-frequency state space (HFSS) model applies directional selective scanning—each subband is serialized along its corresponding axis and processed by a VSSM-style Mamba block—so that subtle, directionally-aligned anomalies are captured in their native orientation. The low-frequency state space (LFSS) model groups channels and applies a Channel Mamba block that compresses spatial dimensions via axis-wise global average pooling and models inter-channel dependencies with a bidirectional channel SSM, preserving global structural coherence for large-scale anomalies. A dynamic spatial enhancement (DSE) block then restores spatial continuity lost in the frequency domain through channel shuffle and a GELU-based adaptive gating mechanism. Training uses MSE loss across three feature scales with a frozen ResNet-34 encoder and a Half-FPN bottleneck, requiring only 22.3 M parameters and 7.5 GFLOPs.

## Results

Wave-MambaAD achieves an average mAD of 81.2 across MVTec-AD (85.6), VisA (79.0), and MPDD (79.1), outperforming the prior SoTA MambaAD (80.0) by 1.2 points. On MVTec-AD, it reaches 98.2% image-level AU-ROC and 58.3% pixel-level F1_max; on the challenging MPDD dataset, it achieves 92.5% image-level AU-ROC and 41.5% pixel-level AP. In terms of efficiency, Wave-MambaAD uses only 22.3M parameters and 7.5G FLOPs with 1142 MB inference memory, while running at 58.8 images/s—faster and lighter than MambaAD (25.7M params, 8.3G FLOPs, 1484 MB, 52.6 img/s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary SoTA baseline MambaAD, Wave-MambaAD wins on mAD across all three datasets (85.6 vs 85.4 on MVTec-AD, 79.0 vs 78.3 on VisA, 79.1 vs 76.3 on MPDD), with the largest gap on MPDD (+2.8) where it improves pixel-level AP from 33.6 to 41.5. The only metric where Wave-MambaAD trails MambaAD is pixel-level AU-PRO on MVTec-AD (93.1 vs 93.4) and pixel-level F1_max on VisA (43.8 vs 44.0), both by less than 0.3 points. Compared to diffusion-based DiAD, Wave-MambaAD leads by 10.5 mAD points on average (81.2 vs 70.7) while using 59× fewer parameters (22.3M vs 1331M) and 60× less inference memory (1142 MB vs 20306 MB). Against embedding-based methods (CFLOW-AD, PyramidalFlow) and augmentation-based SimpleNet, Wave-MambaAD achieves 7–25 mAD point advantages, with PyramidalFlow being the weakest baseline at 56.6 mAD. RealNet, a feature-selection approach, lags by 14.4 mAD points (66.8 vs 81.2), confirming that reconstruction-based SSM architectures substantially outperform feature-selection strategies for multi-class anomaly detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MambaAD's spatial-domain state space anomaly detector by moving the scanning mechanism into the wavelet frequency domain. It retains the VSSM architectural framework from VMamba for the high-frequency branch but replaces the Hilbert-curve spatial scanning with directional (horizontal, vertical, diagonal) selective scanning aligned to Haar wavelet subbands, and introduces a novel channel-adaptive dynamic scanning mechanism for the low-frequency branch. The multi-class reconstruction paradigm follows UniAD and DiAD, but the core departure is the frequency-aware dual state space design that decouples subtle (high-frequency) and large-scale (low-frequency) anomaly modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The wavelet-domain decomposition offers a principled way to address two failure modes of spatial SSM scanning—missed subtle defects and fragmented large-scale localization—while simultaneously reducing parameters (22.3M vs. 25.7M) and improving inference speed over MambaAD. However, the performance gains over the strongest baseline (MambaAD) remain modest (≤0.7 mAD on MVTec/VisA), and the approach is validated only on three industrial datasets with a fixed Haar wavelet; whether other wavelet bases, deeper SSM stacks, or non-industrial domains would benefit similarly remains open. The channel-adaptive scanning idea for low-frequency components could be of broader interest beyond anomaly detection.
