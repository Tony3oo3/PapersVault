## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting training depends on sharp images and accurate COLMAP poses, both of which degrade or fail under motion blur common in UAV and robotics capture. While event cameras offer high temporal resolution to counteract blur, prior event-assisted 3D reconstruction methods are NeRF-based and lack real-time rendering, leaving a gap for efficient, event-guided deblurring within the 3D-GS framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes K motion-blurred RGB images, their corresponding continuous asynchronous event streams (recorded by a DAVIS346 or simulated via ESIM/V2E), and initial camera poses derived from COLMAP on EDI-processed latent images. It produces a trained 3D Gaussian Splatting representation plus recovered blur-formation camera trajectories (learnable offsets on n=9 latent poses per view), enabling real-time novel view synthesis at full resolution. The event streams are consumed as integrated event maps over sub-exposure intervals and as continuous intensity signals that extend supervision beyond the exposure window.

## Method

EvaGaussians first uses the Event-based Double Integral (EDI) model to derive n latent images per blurry frame, initializes 3D-GS and camera poses via COLMAP on these, then jointly optimizes learnable pose offsets and Gaussian parameters in a bundle-adjustment loop where n rendered images are averaged to simulate the blur formation. An event reconstruction loss converts the rendered image sequence into event maps via a differentiable event simulator and enforces L1 agreement with ground-truth integrated events over m sub-intervals. Two event-assisted geometry regularizers—intensity reconstruction (deriving grayscale images from events at arbitrary times between frames) and intensity-aware depth regularization (Sobel-weighted depth gradients)—stabilize geometry beyond the exposure window. Training is progressive (coarse-to-fine resolution, event loss introduced after a 3,000-iteration warmup, no densification), and the total loss combines blur photometric, event, intensity, and depth terms with fixed weights.

## Results

On the synthetic EvaGaussians-Blender dataset, EvaGaussians achieves PSNR of 26.02 / 30.47 / 30.24 dB on large-scale, medium-scale, and object-level scenes respectively, outperforming the strongest baseline (BAGS at 24.93 dB on large-scale) by over 1 dB. On the real-world EvaGaussians-DAVIS dataset, the method attains the best NR-IQA scores across all five metrics (e.g., BRISQUE 53.96 vs. 58.58 for BAGS; NIQE 8.371 vs. 9.011 for EvDNeRF; MetaIQA 0.213 vs. 0.198 for BAGS). The method also maintains real-time rendering while reducing training time compared to NeRF-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvaGaussians outperforms all 10 baselines across every scene scale and every metric. Against the strongest non-event 3D-GS baseline BAGS (24.93 PSNR on large-scale), EvaGaussians gains +1.09 dB PSNR and +0.061 SSIM; against the strongest event-assisted NeRF baseline EvDNeRF (28.91 PSNR on medium-scale), it gains +1.56 dB PSNR and −0.017 LPIPS. On real-world scenes, the advantage is most pronounced on BRISQUE (53.96 vs. 58.58 for BAGS, a 4.62-point improvement) and NIQE (8.371 vs. 9.011 for EvDNeRF). The image-deblurring-then-splatting baselines (UFP-GS, EDI-GS, EFN-GS) consistently underperform direct blurry-image training (B-3DGS) on large-scale scenes, confirming that pre-deblurring corrupts 3D consistency. NeRF-based methods (B-NeRF, BAD-NeRF, E²NeRF) are uniformly worse than their 3D-GS counterparts and suffer from non-real-time rendering, while EvaGaussians maintains real-time inference with 50k-iteration training on a single RTX 4090.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: (1) deblurring reconstruction via bundle adjustment (BAD-NeRF, BAD-Gaussians, BAGS), which jointly optimizes scene representation and camera trajectories during exposure, and (2) event-assisted 3D reconstruction (E2NeRF, EvDNeRF), which leverages the high temporal resolution of event cameras to recover sharp geometry from blurry frames. EvaGaussians is the first to unify both: it uses 3D-GS as the scene representation (enabling real-time rendering, unlike NeRF-based predecessors) and integrates event streams not only as a deblurring signal but also as a source of geometry regularization beyond the exposure window. The EDI model is repurposed here not as a standalone deblurring step but as an initialization mechanism whose outputs are subsequently refined through differentiable bundle adjustment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By bringing event-assisted deblurring into the 3D-GS paradigm, this work makes high-fidelity novel view synthesis from motion-blurred captures practical in real-time settings (e.g., UAVs, robotics) where NeRF-based methods are too slow. The two contributed datasets (synthetic Blender scenes and real DAVIS346 captures) fill a gap in benchmarking for hybrid frame-event deblurring reconstruction. However, the authors acknowledge that extremely intricate textures from severely blurred images remain challenging, and the method's reliance on a synchronized event camera limits its applicability to setups where such hardware is available. An open question is whether the event-assisted geometry regularization terms can be adapted to dynamic scenes or to settings where event streams are sparse or noisy.
