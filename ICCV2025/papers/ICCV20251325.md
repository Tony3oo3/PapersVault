## Problem

Existing MLLM-based reward models for text-to-image evaluation rely on lengthy system prompts and text-response analysis, making them slow to train and inefficient at inference, while CLIP-based alternatives suffer from bag-of-words behavior and poor generalization to complex image-text relationships. There is no efficient, flexible reward model that can produce fine-grained, continuous scores across multiple evaluation perspectives (alignment, fidelity, safety) without requiring instruction-following data or discrete rating tokens.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251325/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt and a generated RGB image as a pair, processes them through a frozen MLLM backbone, and extracts the hidden state at the EOS token position rather than generating any text output. The reward head then produces either a scalar reward (under Bradley-Terry), a preference probability P(i_c ≻ i_r | t), or a 2-dimensional vector reward embedding (under GPM) depending on the training objective. No resolution constraints are specified beyond standard MLLM input, and the same architecture supports both paired preference data and unpaired binary-labeled data through different loss functions.

## Method

LLaVA-Reward repurposes a pretrained decoder-only MLLM (Phi-3.5-vision 4.2B) as a reward model by discarding its text-generation head and instead reading the final-layer hidden state at the EOS token. The key architectural addition is the SkipCA module, a cross-attention layer that uses early-layer visual tokens (extracted right after the visual projector, before they dilute in deeper layers) as keys/values and the final-layer text hidden state as query, thereby restoring bidirectional visual-textual interaction that causal attention alone cannot provide. LoRA adapters (8% additional parameters) are trained per evaluation perspective, with the visual encoder and language model weights frozen. Training uses Bradley-Terry pairwise ranking loss for preference data or cross-entropy for binary safety labels, and optionally employs GPM's skew-symmetric preference operator to produce vector rewards that capture richer preference structure than scalar scores.

## Results

On MJ-Bench, LLaVA-Reward-Qwen (8.2B) achieves the best overall performance: 67.5% accuracy (w/tie) on alignment, 59.2% (w/tie) / 93.6% (w/o tie) on safety, and 94.3% on artifact/fidelity. On TIFA 160, the 4.2B variant attains 71.1 Pearson correlation and 71.3% pairwise accuracy, outperforming VQAScore-11B (66.9 / 71.6%) and LLaVA-score-13B (66.6 / 70.9%). For safety F1, LLaVA-Reward-4.2B scores 87.2 on UnsafeDiffusion and 75.7 on SMID, surpassing ImageGuard-7B (84.1 / 73.4) and LlavaGuard-7B (84.5 / 69.5). In diffusion inference-time scaling (FK steering with SDXL), LLaVA-Reward yields the highest overall GenEval score of 0.645, compared to 0.627 for ImageReward and 0.592 for CLIPScore.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIP/BLIP-based scorers (HPS-v2.1, ImageReward, PickScore-v1, CLIPScore), LLaVA-Reward-Phi improves alignment accuracy by 31–43% and safety w/tie accuracy by 2–3×, confirming the benefit of an MLLM backbone over bag-of-words-style CLIP similarity. Against VQA-based MLLM judges (EVALALIGN, ImageGuard, LlavaGuard), LLaVA-Reward dominates: ImageGuard collapses to 0% safety accuracy on MJ-Bench (all ties), while LLaVA-Reward-Phi reaches 55.2% w/tie; on TIFA 160, LLaVA-Reward-4.2B (71.1 Pearson) beats VQAScore-11B (66.9) and LLaVA-score-13B (66.6) despite being 2–3× smaller. On safety F1, LLaVA-Reward-4.2B outperforms both ImageGuard-7B and LlavaGuard-7B on both UnsafeDiffusion and SMID. Against closed-source MLLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-4-vision), LLaVA-Reward-Qwen matches or exceeds them on alignment (67.5 vs. 66.1/61.5 w/tie) and artifact (94.3 vs. 90.4/97.6 w/tie), though GPT-4o retains a slight edge on safety w/o tie (100.0 vs. 93.6). In inference-time scaling, LLaVA-Reward as the FK-steering reward model achieves the best overall GenEval score (0.645 SDXL, 0.622 SD v2.1), surpassing ImageReward (0.627/0.613) and CLIPScore (0.592/0.562), and is the only model that correctly renders text in DrawBench generations.

## Relation to Prior Work

LLaVA-Reward departs from two dominant paradigms in MLLM-based T2I evaluation: VQA-style judges (EvalAlign, ImageGuard, LlavaGuard) that require lengthy system prompts and generate text rationales, and token-probability methods (VQAScore, LLaVA-score) that extract scores from discrete assessment tokens. Instead, it directly reads hidden states from a frozen MLLM (Phi-3.5-vision) through a lightweight SkipCA reward head, drawing architectural inspiration from the cross-modality attention in ImageGuard but repurposing it as a skip connection between early visual-projector tokens and final-layer text embeddings. The preference objective follows the Bradley-Terry framework and the General Preference Model (GPM) formulation, while LoRA adapters enable per-perspective customization without retraining the base model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in efficiency: at 0.35 s per evaluation, LLaVA-Reward is an order of magnitude faster than VQA-based judges (4–7 s), making it viable as a reward signal inside RLHF or inference-time scaling loops where thousands of evaluations are needed. The demonstration that a 4.2B model with LoRA adapters outperforms 7–13B MLLMs and even approaches closed-source models (GPT-4o) on multi-perspective T2I evaluation suggests that reward modeling does not require the full generative capacity of large MLLMs. Open questions include whether a single jointly-trained adapter can handle all perspectives simultaneously (the paper trains separate LoRA weights per perspective), and whether the SkipCA design generalizes to more complex compositional evaluations beyond the three tested.
