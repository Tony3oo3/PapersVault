## Problem

Existing multi-modal anomaly detection (MAD) methods struggle in few-shot settings where only a handful of normal training samples are available. Feature-fusion approaches risk modality interference, while cross-modal alignment methods suffer from high false-positive rates due to the inherent distribution gap between 2D and 3D representations. No prior method effectively combines fine-grained intra-modal localization with cross-modal inconsistency detection under data scarcity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251360/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a 3D point cloud per sample; the point cloud is organized into a 2D grid and converted to a 3-channel surface-normal map, which is then treated as an RGB-style input (normalized with ImageNet statistics) and resized to 224×224 alongside the RGB image. At inference, the model produces a pixel-level anomaly map (a weighted combination of intra-modal and cross-modal error maps) and an image-level anomaly score derived from the aggregated anomaly response.

## Method

FIND employs a dual-student architecture with two symmetric branches (2D and 3D), each containing a frozen ViT-B/8 DINO teacher, a cross-modal student (encoder-decoder that maps features from the other modality to the teacher's feature space), and an intra-modal student (multi-stage decoder performing layer-wise reverse distillation back to image resolution). A key design choice is using surface-normal maps as the 3D representation, which allows a single shared backbone for both modalities and substantially reduces the modality gap that causes false positives in prior cross-modal methods. Training jointly minimizes a bidirectional cosine-similarity cross-modal loss and a multi-layer intra-modal loss (cosine similarity at each hidden layer plus L2 image reconstruction). At test time, anomaly maps from all four students are averaged within each modality pair and then combined via a weighted sum (α=0.5 for full-shot, α=0.8 for few-shot) to yield the final pixel-level anomaly map.

## Results

On MVTec-3D-AD (full-shot), FIND achieves 97.8% I-AUROC, 99.5% P-AUROC, and 98.3% AUPRO@30%, outperforming the strongest baseline CFM (95.4% I-AUROC, 99.3% P-AUROC, 97.1% AUPRO@30%). In the challenging 5-shot setting, FIND reaches 89.9% I-AUROC on MVTec-3D-AD and 84.0% on Eyecandies, surpassing the next-best methods (M3DM at 82.2% and CFM at 81.1% on MVTec-3D-AD) by a substantial margin. On Eyecandies (full-shot), FIND attains 95.5% I-AUROC and 98.3% P-AUROC, exceeding M3DM (88.2% I-AUROC) and CFM (88.1% I-AUROC) by over 7 points in image-level detection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FIND outperforms all seven baselines across every shot setting and both datasets. Against the strongest multi-modal baselines CFM [5] and M3DM [31], FIND gains +2.4 and +3.3 points in I-AUROC respectively on MVTec-3D-AD (full-shot), and +7.4 and +7.3 points on Eyecandies (full-shot), with the gap widening in few-shot regimes (e.g., +8.8 over CFM at 5-shot on MVTec-3D-AD). The classical 3D method BTF [11] and the asymmetric T-S method AST [24] trail FIND by 11–25 points in I-AUROC across all settings, confirming the advantage of learned multi-modal representations. 3D-ADNAS [21] reports only I-AUROC (no P-AUROC or AUPRO), yet FIND still exceeds it by 2.7 points on MVTec-3D-AD (full-shot) and 8.9 points at 5-shot. In the per-category full-shot comparison (Table 2), FIND also surpasses the more recent Shape Guided [4] (97.8% vs. 94.7% mean I-AUROC) and LSFA [29] (97.8% vs. 97.1%), with the largest per-category gains on Cable Gland (+7.6 over CFM) and Foam (+9.1 over CFM), indicating superior handling of fine-grained geometric anomalies.

## Relation to Prior Work

FIND unifies two previously separate lines of work—single-modality reverse distillation (RD, RD4AD) and cross-modal feature mapping (CFM)—into a single dual-student teacher-student framework, decoupling intra-modal reconstruction from inter-modal correspondence learning to avoid the modality interference that plagues fusion-based methods like MMRD and M3DM. A key departure from CFM and prior MAD methods is the use of surface-normal maps as a geometric proxy fed through a shared ViT-B/8 DINO backbone, rather than separate 2D/3D encoders or depth maps, which the authors argue substantially narrows the modality gap. The few-shot setting extends the RGB-only few-shot AD literature (FastRecon, WinCLIP, FCOT) into the multi-modal regime, a combination the authors explicitly identify as unaddressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is strong: industrial inspection pipelines often have only a handful of normal multi-modal samples per class, and FIND's consistent gains across 5/10/50-shot settings on two benchmarks suggest a deployable recipe for data-scarce factories. The surface-normal-as-geometric-proxy trick is simple and could transfer to other cross-modal distillation settings beyond anomaly detection. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond RGB+geometry (e.g., thermal, LiDAR), whether the shared-backbone assumption holds for more heterogeneous modality pairs, and whether the fixed α weighting in the final anomaly map could be learned or made category-adaptive.
