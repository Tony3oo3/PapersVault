## Problem

Current deep learning-based weather forecasting models are fundamentally physics-agnostic, relying solely on data correlations and potentially producing unphysical outputs such as negative humidity. While radiative transfer is a well-defined physical process that drives Earth's weather and climate systems, no prior deep learning forecasting framework has explicitly incorporated physical process modeling as a training constraint. FuXi-RTM addresses this gap by being the first to integrate a differentiable radiative transfer surrogate into a DL weather forecasting pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250817/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes two consecutive 6-hourly ERA5 atmospheric state cubes (83 variables on a 721×1440 global lat-lon grid at 0.25° resolution) and autoregressively predicts the next 6-hour state, extending to 10-day forecasts. The frozen DLRTM additionally processes 11 variables per grid column across 13 pressure levels (50–1000 hPa) to produce four radiative fluxes (SW/LW up/down) at each level, with dynamic masking to exclude non-physical 'ghost levels' below the surface. During training, a sunlit-region-centered (SRC) sampling strategy selects 250×250 patches from randomly chosen sunlit locations for the physics loss, rather than computing over the full global grid.

## Method

FuXi-RTM couples a trainable FuXi-base forecasting model (30 Swin Transformer V2 blocks in an encoder-processor-decoder architecture, ~1.1B parameters) with a pre-trained, frozen Bi-LSTM DLRTM surrogate (3 stacked Bi-LSTM layers, 96/128 feature dims) that emulates the RRTMG radiation scheme. The DLRTM is first trained on 2 years of RRTMG-generated fluxes, then frozen and used as a differentiable physics regularizer: its radiative flux outputs computed on FuXi's predicted state are compared against ERA5-derived fluxes in a Charbonnier L1 loss weighted by λ=10⁻³, added to the standard latitude-weighted forecast loss. This design requires no architectural modification to FuXi and no additional training of the DLRTM during the main forecasting training, while the SRC sampling strategy concentrates gradient signal on physically meaningful sunlit regions. The DLRTM achieves roughly 440× speedup over RRTMG (22 min on 8 CPUs → ~3 s on one H100 GPU) through column-wise parallel processing.

## Results

FuXi-RTM outperforms its unconstrained counterpart FuXi-base in 88.51% of 3,320 variable-and-lead-time combinations (6-hour intervals up to 10 days) on the 5-year ERA5 test set (2018–2022). For radiation-coupled variables, the win rate rises to 95.38% for cloud cover (CC) and 93.46% for specific humidity (Q), while surface albedo (FAL) shows nRMSE improvements exceeding 7% across the full 10-day horizon. For the derived instantaneous surface net solar radiation (ISSRD), FuXi-RTM achieves improvements approaching 100% in nRMSE, and it wins on 100% of radiative flux combinations. The DLRTM surrogate also delivers an orders-of-magnitude speedup over the traditional RRTMG scheme, reducing global-grid radiation computation from ~22 minutes on 8 CPUs to ~3 seconds on a single H100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is FuXi-base, the same FuXi architecture trained without any radiative physics constraint. FuXi-RTM wins on 88.51% of all 3,320 variable × lead-time combinations in globally-averaged, latitude-weighted nRMSE, with the largest gains concentrated in radiation-sensitive variables: CC (95.38% win rate), Q (93.46%), CLWC (>2% nRMSE improvement above 200 hPa), and FAL (>7% nRMSE improvement over the full 10-day range). On the DLRTM-derived radiation outputs, FuXi-RTM achieves near-100% nRMSE improvement for ISSRD and modest but consistent gains for SWDFLX/SWUFLX at the surface and 50 hPa, despite only explicitly supervising surface-level shortwave fluxes. FuXi-base does retain a slight edge on specific humidity at 1000 hPa for the first ~1.25 days, likely due to training dynamics. In the global total atmospheric energy conservation check (Fig. 6), FuXi-RTM shows smaller energy drift relative to ERA5, with the gap widening beyond 10-day forecasts, confirming that the radiative constraint improves long-horizon physical fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on FuXi [10] as its primary forecasting backbone (Swin Transformer V2 encoder-processor-decoder) and on Yao et al. [56] for the Bi-LSTM architecture underlying the radiative transfer surrogate. It departs from prior physics-guided weather forecasting work [30, 50, 55], which embedded primitive equations via ODE solvers, by instead using a frozen deep-learning radiative transfer model (DLRTM) as a differentiable loss regularizer rather than a forward-pass component. This positions FuXi-RTM as the first DL weather model to explicitly model a specific physical process (radiation) rather than treating the network as a holistic black-box parameterization, bridging the gap between NWP parameterization schemes and data-driven forecasting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular 'trainable forecaster + frozen physics surrogate as loss term' architecture provides a practical template for incrementally adding physical processes (convection, PBL, cloud microphysics) to DL weather models without retraining the entire system, which is a significant step toward physically consistent operational forecasting. However, the current implementation constrains only surface shortwave fluxes, excludes u/v wind components, and demonstrates improvements that, while consistent (88.51% of combinations), are modest in absolute magnitude for most conventional variables. Open questions include whether multiple physics surcharges can be combined without conflicting gradients, whether the approach generalizes to extreme or out-of-distribution conditions, and whether the energy conservation gains beyond 10 days translate to operational subseasonal skill.
