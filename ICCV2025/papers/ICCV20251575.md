## Problem

Transformers achieve strong SR performance but suffer from excessive memory access caused by materializing self-attention score matrices and memory-bound operations like tensor reshaping and window masking, making them impractical on resource-constrained devices. The paper identifies that self-attention's similarity modeling and extracted features exhibit high inter-layer consistency (89% and 87% average), revealing redundancy that can be exploited to replace most self-attention layers with cheaper alternatives without sacrificing representational power.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251575/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes a low-resolution RGB image (H×W×3) and produces a high-resolution RGB image at upscaling factors ×2, ×3, or ×4 for classic SR, arbitrary scales up to ×30 via LTE, and real-world SR with unknown degradations. Notably, the architecture operates without a patchify stem or downsampling stage, meaning it processes full-resolution feature maps directly, which is precisely what makes the memory bottleneck of self-attention so severe in the SR setting. Three variants are provided: ESC and ESC-light prioritize latency reduction, while ESC-FP targets FLOPs and parameter size reduction.

## Method

The ESC network replaces most self-attention layers with a Convolutional Attention (ConvAttn) module that emulates self-attention's two key properties: long-range modeling via a single shared 13×13 large kernel (LK) applied across the entire network on a 16-channel subset, and input-dependent weighting via per-layer dynamic depth-wise kernels (DK) generated through global average pooling and 1×1 convolutions. Each ESCBlock retains one window self-attention layer (with Flash Attention enabling a 32×32 window at 16× lower latency and 12.2× lower memory than naive attention) followed by M ConvAttn iterations. The shared LK avoids parameter explosion and optimization difficulty, while the layer-specific DKs capture instance-dependent features that the static LK cannot. This design reduces reliance on memory-intensive self-attention while preserving the large receptive field, data scalability, and representational capacity of Transformers.

## Results

On the DIV2K-trained classic SR task, ESC achieves 32.68/0.9011 PSNR/SSIM on Set5×4 and 27.07/0.8144 on Urban100×4, setting new state-of-the-art among lightweight methods while running at 21.9 ms latency (8.7× faster than ATD-light at 189.7 ms) and 215 MB memory (3.5× less than ATD-light's 753 MB). At ×2, ESC reaches 38.35/0.9619 on Set5 and 33.46/0.9395 on Urban100, outperforming all Transformer and SSM baselines. The ESC-FP variant, targeting FLOPs/parameter reduction, achieves 26.90/0.8098 on Urban100×4 with only 60.8G FLOPs and 539K parameters, surpassing MambaIRV2-light by 0.08 dB while using 20% fewer FLOPs and 32% fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESC outperforms all 13 baselines on Urban100×4, achieving 27.07 dB PSNR versus ATD-light (26.97), HiT-SRF (26.80), RCAN (26.82), and MambaIRV2-light (26.82), while running 8.7× faster than ATD-light and using 3.5× less memory. On Set5×4, ESC leads with 32.68 dB, edging out ATD-light and RCAN (both 32.63) and HiT-SRF (32.55). The ESC-light variant surpasses ELAN-light by 0.29 dB on Urban100×2 (33.05 vs. 32.76) while being 1.4× faster, and ESC-FP beats MambaIRV2-light on Urban100×4 (26.90 vs. 26.82) with 20% fewer FLOPs and 32% fewer parameters. On the DFLIP large-scale dataset (Table 4), ESC extends its lead to 0.38 dB over HiT-SRF on Urban100×4 (27.45 vs. 27.07), confirming that the ConvAttn module preserves Transformer data-scalability. For arbitrary-scale SR (Table 5), ESC+LTE achieves the best PSNR at all tested scales (×2 through ×30), marginally outperforming HiT-SRF+LTE and ATD-lt+LTE. The only notable trade-off is that ESC-light (13.3 ms) sacrifices ~0.3 dB on Urban100×4 relative to full ESC, while ESC-FP (21.7 ms) trades ~0.17 dB for substantially lower FLOPs and parameter count.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads in efficient SR: (1) Transformer-based methods (SwinIR, HiT-SRF, ATD, ELAN) that reduce FLOPs via windowed or permuted self-attention but still suffer from memory-bound operations like score-matrix materialization; (2) large-kernel CNNs (ShuffleMixer, LKDNet) and SSMs (MambaIR, MambaIRV2) that attempt to replicate long-range modeling without attention; and (3) Flash Attention, which avoids materializing the score matrix but had not been applied to lightweight SR. The key departure is the empirical observation that self-attention's extracted features and attention maps are highly similar across layers (~89% CKA), motivating a hybrid design where only one self-attention per block is retained (with Flash Attention enabling 32×32 windows) and the rest are replaced by a ConvAttn module combining a shared 13×13 large kernel with layer-specific dynamic depth-wise kernels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deploying Transformer-quality SR on resource-constrained hardware: 16× latency and 12.2× memory reduction at 32×32 window size, and 3.7×/6.2× reductions over HiT-SRF at ×4, while matching or exceeding SOTA PSNR. The first successful integration of Flash Attention into lightweight SR opens a path for other restoration tasks to adopt larger attention windows without prohibitive memory costs. Open questions include whether the shared large kernel generalizes to substantially larger receptive fields or different degradation models, whether the inter-layer similarity observation holds for deeper or differently-structured architectures, and whether the approach extends cleanly to video SR or other dense prediction tasks where feature maps are even larger.
