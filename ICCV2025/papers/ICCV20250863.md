## Problem

Most knowledge distillation methods assume teacher and student share similar architectures (e.g., both CNNs), limiting both the range of usable teachers and flexibility in domain-specific settings. Cross-architecture distillation (CAKD) is severely challenged by large feature gaps between heterogeneous models, caused by fundamentally different inductive biases (local convolution vs. global attention) and module functions, which make naive feature alignment ineffective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250863/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images at 32×32 (CIFAR-100) or 224×224 (ImageNet-1K), producing class-label logits. For CIFAR-100, images are upsampled to 224×224 to accommodate patch-based MSA/MLP models. The fused model consumes the same input image as both teacher and student, and knowledge transfer occurs only on final-stage features (after average pooling) and output logits, rather than intermediate feature maps.

## Method

FBT introduces an adaptive fused model that merges the first three CNN stages (from the student) with the final MSA/MLP stage (from the teacher), connected by a local-to-global (L2G) projector containing a patch embedding and a Swin attention block. This fused model bridges complementary inductive biases (local + global) and aligns heterogeneous module functions within a single pipeline, introducing negligible extra parameters through weight-sharing. Training follows a three-level Teacher→Fused→Student scheme with loss L_FBT = L(K_t,K_s) + L(K_t,K_f) + L(K_f,K_s). Supervision uses a spatial-agnostic InfoNCE loss on average-pooled final features (replacing pixel-wise MSE, which fails for spatially diverse heterogeneous features) and an OFA loss on logits that modulates target-class information to prevent learning incorrect teacher predictions.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, FBT achieves an average top-1 accuracy gain of +8.38% over from-scratch training and +2.06% over the prior SOTA CAKD method OFA across 12 heterogeneous teacher-student pairs, with a maximum gain of 11.47% (ViT-S → MobileNetV2: 82.10% vs. 73.68% scratch). On ImageNet-1K, FBT delivers an average +2.31% improvement over from-scratch across 14 cross-architecture pairs, with a peak gain of 3.67% (ConvNeXt-T → DeiT-T: 75.26% vs. 72.17% scratch). In homogeneous SAKD on ImageNet-1K, FBT remains competitive, achieving 72.29% (ResNet34→ResNet18) and 73.45% (ResNet50→MobileNet), matching or slightly exceeding FCFD and OFA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FBT outperforms all 12 baselines on average across both CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-1K. Feature-based methods (FitNet, CC, RKD, CRD) show negative or near-zero average improvements on CIFAR-100 (−5.21%, −0.33%, −1.39%, −0.02% respectively), with FitNet collapsing to 24.06% on ConvNeXt-T→Swin-P due to its pixel-wise MSE loss being incompatible with spatially diverse features. Logits-based methods (KD, DKD, DIST) achieve modest positive gains (+3.12%, +3.16%, −2.31%) but remain 2–6% below FBT. The closest competitor, OFA (the prior SOTA for CAKD), achieves +6.19% average on CIFAR-100 but is consistently outperformed by FBT by ~2.06% on average and by up to 3.8% on individual pairs (e.g., ConvNeXt-T→Swin-P: 80.73% vs. 78.32%). In homogeneous SAKD (Table 3), FBT matches or slightly exceeds FCFD (+0.05 to +0.08%) and OFA (+0.19 to +0.17%), confirming that the fusion strategy also benefits same-architecture distillation. Notably, FBT uses only final features (after average pooling) whereas OFA leverages four intermediate feature levels, yet FBT still wins, suggesting the fused-model bridge is more effective than multi-level feature alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cross-architecture knowledge distillation (CAKD) paradigm introduced by OFA (Hao et al., NeurIPS 2023), which first demonstrated that heterogeneous teachers (e.g., ViT) can outperform homogeneous ones (e.g., ResNet152) for a given student. FBT departs from the direct Teacher→Student (T→S) transfer used by OFA and most prior KD methods by inserting an adaptive fused model (T→F→S) that merges CNN and MSA/MLP modules from both teacher and student, drawing on the hybrid-architecture literature (CoaTNet, ConvMLP, Park & Kim 2021) showing that local and global inductive biases are complementary. It also replaces the pixel-wise MSE loss common in feature-based KD (FitNet, FCFD) with a spatial-agnostic InfoNCE loss, motivated by the observation that heterogeneous features have fundamentally different spatial distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FBT addresses a practical gap: in domain-specific or emerging-model scenarios, a suitable homogeneous teacher may simply not exist, and the method provides a generic, low-cost bridge that works across arbitrary CNN/MSA/MLP combinations with minimal extra parameters. However, the authors acknowledge that for well-studied pairs (e.g., ResNet18 student), a strong homogeneous teacher still beats a heterogeneous one, and the fusion disrupts spatial alignment in ways that could be further mitigated. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dense prediction tasks (detection, segmentation) and NLP, and whether the three-term loss (T→F, F→S, T→S) can be simplified without sacrificing the consistent gains observed.
