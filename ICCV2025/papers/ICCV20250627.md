## Problem

Composed pose retrieval (CPR) requires transition descriptions between reference and target poses, but existing datasets rely on costly human annotations or rigid rule-based generation from 3D keypoints, both of which limit scalability, diversity, and expressiveness. No prior work has explored using multimodal LLMs to automatically generate pose transition descriptions tailored for CPR.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250627/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The description generation stage takes a pair of RGB pose images (reference and target) and produces structured, body-part-level transition descriptions that are then integrated into natural-language narratives; by default three paraphrased descriptions are generated per pair, expanding to twelve with swap and mirror augmentations. The retrieval stage takes a reference pose image concatenated with a transition description as the composed query and outputs a ranked list of target pose images from a gallery. The MLLM (GPT-4o) processes the image pair directly without requiring 3D pose data, making the pipeline applicable to any 2D human image dataset.

## Method

AutoComPose generates transition descriptions in three stages: (I) an MLLM produces fine-grained, body-part-specific motion descriptions (head, arms, torso, legs, etc.) by comparing corresponding anatomical landmarks across the pose pair; (II) these are synthesized into a single coherent narrative with multiple paraphrased variants to capture linguistic diversity; (III) swapping (time reversal) and mirroring (left-right flip) of the image pair yield additional consistent descriptions without extra annotation. For training, the paper introduces a cyclic consistency loss that enforces the composed features (reference image + forward transition) to be recoverable back to the reference image features via the reverse transition description, mitigating MLLM hallucinations. The retrieval model uses a batch-based InfoNCE loss with cosine similarity over CLIP encoders (RN50/RN101/ViT-B/32/ViT-B/16), optionally with a Combiner feature merger, and fine-tunes only the text encoder to preserve pretrained visual representations.

## Results

On FIXMYPOSE (gallery size 7106), AutoComPose-generated descriptions consistently outperform human annotations across all four CLIP backbones and both feature mergers, with the most dramatic gain on CLIP-RN50 where R@1 jumps from 0.20 (human) to 8.24 (AutoComPose) and R@50 from 13.53 to 63.53. On PoseFixCPR (gallery size 2376), AutoComPose achieves R@1 of 61.36–64.14 (CLIP) and 80.72–84.09 (CLIP w/ Combiner), surpassing human annotations by 4–19 points in R@1 depending on architecture. Against the rule-based PoseFix baseline on PoseFixCPR, AutoComPose improves R@1 by 8–19 points (e.g., 61.36 vs. 42.00 with CLIP-RN50; 84.09 vs. 73.15 with Combiner-ViT-B/16).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against human-annotated descriptions from FIXMYPOSE [Kim et al., AAAI 2021], AutoComPose wins in every single configuration (4 backbones × 2 mergers × 2 datasets = 16 comparisons), with the largest margin on CLIP-RN50/FIXMYPOSE where R@1 improves from 0.20 to 8.24 (a 41× gain) and R@50 from 13.53 to 63.53. On PoseFixCPR, the human-annotation baseline is stronger (R@1 ≈ 49–68), yet AutoComPose still leads by 4–19 R@1 points across all architectures. Against the rule-based PoseFix pipeline [Delmas et al., ICCV 2023], which relies on predefined 3D keypoint 'paircodes' and template sentences, AutoComPose achieves consistent R@1 gains of 8–19 points on PoseFixCPR (e.g., 61.36 vs. 42.00 with CLIP-RN50; 84.09 vs. 73.15 with Combiner-ViT-B/16). The paper also shows that applying paraphrasing and cyclic training to the rule-based baseline narrows but does not close the gap (55.05 vs. 61.36 R@1), confirming that the advantage stems from AutoComPose's unconstrained, MLLM-generated descriptions rather than solely from the training tricks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the CLIP-based composed image retrieval framework of Baldrati et al. [3], adopting its batch-based classification loss and Combiner feature merger as the retrieval backbone. It departs from prior CPR work (FixMyPose [20], PoseFix [10]) that relied on human-annotated or rule-based transition descriptions by introducing MLLM-driven automatic annotation as the first such approach for pose retrieval. The cycle consistency loss draws conceptual inspiration from cycle-consistent adversarial translation [53] and domain adaptation [15], but is the first application of such a constraint to composed pose retrieval, enabled by the automatic generation of reverse transition descriptions via image-pair swapping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that MLLM-generated, structured pose transition descriptions consistently outperform both human annotations and rule-based baselines across all tested architectures, the paper offers a practical path to scaling CPR training data without the cost and inconsistency of manual labeling. The release of AIST-CPR and PoseFixCPR provides much-needed standardized benchmarks for a field that previously lacked large-scale evaluation sets. Open questions remain: whether the approach generalizes to more complex multi-person or occluded scenarios, whether the cycle constraint can be extended beyond CLIP-style encoders, and whether the notably lower AIST-CPR performance (R@1 ≈ 3–7%) reflects a fundamental limitation of text-based transition descriptions for highly dynamic dance motions.
