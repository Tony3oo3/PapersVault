## Problem

Applying state-of-the-art image relighting models (e.g., IC-Light) frame-by-frame to video sequences produces severe flickering due to unstable lighting sources and inconsistent relighted appearances across frames. Dedicated video relighting methods are limited by the scarcity of high-quality video lighting datasets and prohibitive training costs, leaving a gap for a practical, temporally consistent video relighting solution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252433/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video (or a foreground sequence with a text prompt for background generation), a text prompt describing the desired lighting (e.g., 'neon light', 'sunlight through blinds'), and a lighting map that encodes spatial light intensity as the initial latent for the relighting model. The source video is noised to 50% and processed through a video diffusion model (AnimateDiff or CogVideoX) with 25 denoising steps. The output is a temporally smooth relighted video that preserves the source motion while aligning illumination to the specified condition.

## Method

Light-A-Video is a training-free pipeline that couples an image relighting model (IC-Light) with a video diffusion model (VDM) via two key mechanisms. First, a Consistent Light Attention (CLA) module modifies each self-attention layer in IC-Light with a dual-stream design: an original stream computes standard frame-by-frame attention, while an average stream feeds temporally averaged features to suppress high-frequency lighting fluctuations; the two outputs are blended with a trade-off parameter γ=0.5 to stabilize the generated background light source across frames. Second, a Progressive Light Fusion (PLF) strategy exploits the linearity of light transport to blend the VDM's denoising target (Consistent Target) with the CLA-relighted target (Relight Target) in pixel space, using a fusion weight λ_t = 1 − t/T_m that decreases as denoising progresses, so the relighting influence is gradually injected rather than applied all at once. A detail compensation term from the first denoising step is added to preserve fine appearance details. The fused target replaces the original denoising target at each step, effectively steering the VDM's denoising direction toward the desired relighting while its motion priors enforce temporal coherence.

## Results

On a 73-video test set (DAVIS + Pixabay), Light-A-Video achieves the best temporal consistency (CLIP score 0.9667), the best motion preservation (optical flow discrepancy 1.833), and the highest user preference scores across all three criteria (LPA 3.752, VS 3.502, IP 3.656). In terms of relighted image quality (FID against frame-by-frame IC-Light), Light-A-Video scores 29.63, outperforming IC-Light+SDEdit-0.6 (62.61) and IC-Light+AnyV2V (32.73) but trailing IC-Light+SDEdit-0.2 (13.79). The method is evaluated with both AnimateDiff and CogVideoX as the video diffusion backbone, confirming model-agnostic compatibility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Light-A-Video decisively outperforms all three baselines on temporal consistency (CLIP 0.9667 vs. 0.9040–0.9483) and motion preservation (optical flow discrepancy 1.833 vs. 5.959–8.854), with the largest margin over IC-Light+AnyV2V (8.854) and IC-Light+SDEdit-0.6 (7.544). On user preference, Light-A-Video leads all three criteria (LPA, VS, IP) by 0.4–1.6 points over the best baseline, indicating clearly perceived improvements in lighting alignment, smoothness, and identity preservation. The one metric where Light-A-Video does not lead is FID (29.63 vs. 13.79 for IC-Light+SDEdit-0.2), reflecting a trade-off: SDEdit-0.2 preserves single-frame relighting fidelity more closely to the IC-Light reference but at the cost of severe temporal flickering (CLIP 0.9199, motion discrepancy 5.959). IC-Light+SDEdit-0.6 and IC-Light+AnyV2V both suffer from identity shifts and poor motion preservation, respectively, confirming that simply adding noise or propagating first-frame appearance is insufficient for coherent video relighting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two established lines of work: diffusion-based image relighting (primarily IC-Light, which enforces consistent light transport to edit illumination while preserving albedo) and video diffusion models (AnimateDiff, CogVideoX) that encode temporal motion priors. Rather than training a dedicated video relighting model, it repurposes the image relighting model's self-attention layers with a Consistent Light Attention module and injects relighting guidance into the VDM's denoising trajectory via Progressive Light Fusion, grounded in the linearity of light transport. It departs from prior video editing approaches (SDEdit, AnyV2V, COVE) that address temporal consistency through noise scheduling or feature correspondence, instead leveraging the physical decomposition of appearance into light transport and illumination to guide the fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free formulation makes video relighting accessible without the prohibitive cost of collecting paired video relighting datasets or training new models, and the plug-and-play design generalizes across UNet-based and DiT-based video backbones. However, the CLA module is explicitly limited to stabilizing static background lighting and cannot model dynamic lighting changes, which is a significant practical gap for real-world applications like film production. The method's quality is inherently bounded by the underlying image relighting model and VDM, and the evaluation relies on relatively weak baselines (frame-by-frame IC-Light, SDEdit at two fixed noise levels, AnyV2V), leaving open whether the approach scales to more challenging dynamic-lighting scenarios or longer sequences.
