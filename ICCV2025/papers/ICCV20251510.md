## Problem

3D assembly completion requires retrieving missing parts and predicting their 6-DoF poses from a partial assembly, but existing methods rely on unrealistic assumptions: part-level segmentation of the partial assembly and a known/pre-aligned image view. This paper tackles the more practical setting where the partial assembly is an unsegmented point cloud and the reference image view is undetermined, making it difficult to establish which image regions correspond to existing parts versus missing ones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251510/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unsegmented partial assembly point cloud (resampled to 2048 points), a single 128×128 texture-less reference image of the complete assembly from an arbitrary unknown view, and N=20 candidate part point clouds (k true missing parts plus N−k distractors from other assemblies). It autoregressively outputs k retrieved missing parts with their 6-DoF poses (quaternion rotation and translation), assembled iteratively into the completed assembly. Notably, no part segmentation labels or view annotations are required at inference time.

## Method

The framework jointly optimizes two core modules: (1) a self-supervised view alignment module that uses a ResNet-based predictor to estimate the relative view (quaternion + scale) between the partial assembly and image, supervised by a projection loss between the differentially rendered completed assembly and the image silhouette; and (2) a missing-oriented feature fusion module that computes a differential attention map D = ‖S_I − R(T_v(A))‖ to highlight missing regions in the image, then uses this map to modulate cross-modal attention between PointNet++-encoded 3D patches and MLP-encoded image patches. The fused feature conditions a part decoder that performs cross-attention with candidate part features to predict binary retrieval scores and 6-DoF poses. Completion proceeds autoregressively: at each step the highest-scoring candidate is assembled into the partial assembly and removed from the pool, with view alignment re-optimized throughout. Training combines cross-entropy classification, translation/rotation pose losses, shape Chamfer distance, symmetry loss, and the self-supervised projection loss in an end-to-end manner.

## Results

On PartNet (medium difficulty, k=3 missing parts), the method achieves 39.84% Part Accuracy (PA) and 49.43% Match Accuracy (MA) on chairs, with a Completion Chamfer Distance (CCD) of 0.99×10⁻², outperforming all assembly-based and generative baselines. The advantage grows with difficulty: in hard mode (k=5), the method reaches 23.14% PA and 35.64% MA on chairs versus 1.30–1.34% PA and 20.08–22.26% MA for the strongest baselines. On CCD, the method achieves 0.73×10⁻² (chair, hard) compared to 4.83–5.25×10⁻² for generative methods like AdaPoinTr and XMFnet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against assembly-based baselines, the method dominates: in medium mode it achieves 49.43% MA on chairs versus 37.66% (FiT) and 38.70% (3DPA), and in hard mode the gap widens to 35.64% vs. 22.26% (FiT) and 20.08% (3DPA). FiT's reliance on part-level instance segmentation causes significant PA/MA degradation when segmentation is unavailable, while 3DPA fails to capture accurate 2D-3D correspondence. Against generative methods (Table 2), the method achieves substantially lower CCD: 0.99×10⁻² (chair, medium) vs. 8.85 (AdaPoinTr), 8.57 (XMFnet), and 4.87 (EGIInet). XMFnet underperforms because it assumes the image view is known and pre-aligned, a condition violated in this paper's setting. TRELLIS results are reported only in the supplementary material, so a direct numerical comparison is not available in the main text.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D assembly completion (FiT, PhysFiT, ComplementMe) by introducing a single-view image as guidance while simultaneously removing the part-segmentation assumption those methods rely on. It also departs from view-guided point cloud completion (ViPC, XMFnet), which requires the image view to be pre-aligned, by learning the view self-supervisedly through differential rendering. The core architectural novelty is a differential attention mechanism that computes a missing-region map from the silhouette difference between the image and the rendered partial assembly, directing cross-modal fusion toward precisely the absent parts rather than the full image.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a more realistic deployment scenario (unsegmented scans, unknown camera view) and demonstrates practical utility through component suggestion and IKEA furniture restoration with real photos. However, it still struggles with extremely geometrically similar parts, elongated stripe-like orientations, and integrated parts that differ from the fine-grained decomposition learned during training. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to real-scanned point clouds with noise, whether connectivity or physical constraints can be incorporated for more robust completion, and whether the autoregressive strategy scales efficiently to assemblies with many missing parts.
