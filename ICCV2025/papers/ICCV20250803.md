## Problem

Multimodal models frequently encounter missing modalities at deployment due to hardware failures, budget constraints, or data collection limitations. Existing solutions either synthesize missing inputs via generative imputation (suffering from hallucination and high compute cost) or rely on complex, task-specific architectures that constrain backbone choices and scale poorly with the number of modalities. The paper asks whether a simple, architecture-agnostic framework can handle arbitrary missing-modality patterns without imputation while guaranteeing that adding modalities never degrades performance.

## Input / Output

The framework accepts any arbitrary subset of the M available modalities per sample; missing modalities are simply zeroed out and their gating values set to −∞ so they contribute zero weight after softmax. For BraTS 2018 the input is 3D MRI volumes (FLAIR, T1, T1ce, T2) and the output is a per-voxel segmentation mask for three tumor subregions plus per-modality gating weights; for classification tasks (UPMC Food-101, avMNIST) the output is a class label. The gating weights are produced at the logit/output level rather than in a shared latent space, so experts can have heterogeneous output dimensions and no projection layers are required.

## Method

SimMLM combines two components: (1) a Dynamic Mixture of Modality Experts (DMoME) architecture where each modality is processed by its own dedicated expert network (e.g., nnUNet, Inception V3, LeNet-5) and a lightweight gating network produces per-modality weights over the available subset, with missing modalities masked to zero weight via −∞ gating; and (2) a More-vs-Fewer (MoFe) ranking loss that, at each training step, samples a modality-rich subset x⁺ and a modality-poor subset x⁻ (x⁻ ⊂ x⁺) from the same sample and penalizes the network whenever the task loss on x⁺ exceeds that on x⁻, directly regularizing the loss landscape so that more modalities yield equal or better accuracy. Training is two-stage: experts are first trained independently with the task loss to avoid modality dominance, then co-trained jointly with the gating network under the combined task + MoFe objective. Because each modality passes through only its own expert (unlike MoMKE which routes every modality through all experts), FLOPS scale linearly rather than quadratically with the number of modalities.

## Results

On BraTS 2018 (segmentation), SimMLM achieves the highest average Dice scores across all 15 modality settings: 67.16% (ET), 80.20% (TC), and 87.67% (WT), outperforming MoMKE (65.56/78.58/86.69), ShaSpec (61.58/77.45/85.92), and mmFormer (59.85/72.97/82.94), while using only 80% of labeled data. On UPMC Food-101, SimMLM reaches 94.99% accuracy with both modalities (vs. 92.73% ShaSpec, 92.71% MoMKE) and 84.81% average across settings. On avMNIST, SimMLM achieves 99.27% with both modalities and 94.52% average. SimMLM also reduces FLOPS by 4× compared to MoMKE (123G vs. 490G) while using the same 7.8M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimMLM consistently outperforms all four baselines across every dataset and modality setting. On BraTS 2018, it surpasses MoMKE (the strongest prior method) by +1.60/+1.62/+0.98 Dice points on ET/TC/WT averages, and leads ShaSpec by +5.58/+2.75/+1.75 points; it ranks first or second in 44 of 45 individual configurations. On UPMC Food-101, SimMLM gains +2.26% over ShaSpec and +1.74% over MoMKE in the full-modality setting (94.99% vs. 92.73%/92.71%), with the largest gap in the image-only setting (+2.98% over ShaSpec). On avMNIST, gains are smaller but consistent: +0.56% over MoMKE and +0.56% over ShaSpec in the full-modality setting. In terms of efficiency, SimMLM (7.8M params, 123G FLOPS) is 4× cheaper in FLOPS than MoMKE (490G) and uses 13–24× fewer parameters than mmFormer (106M) and ShaSpec (187.7M), while achieving higher accuracy than all three.

## Relation to Prior Work

SimMLM departs from the dominant paradigms for missing-modality learning—generative imputation and complex feature-alignment architectures (mmFormer, ShaSpec, Robust-MSeg)—by proposing a deliberately simple mixture-of-experts design where each modality has a dedicated expert rather than passing through all experts as in MoMKE. The MoFe ranking loss draws conceptually from preference-based ranking in reinforcement learning and comparative learning in cognitive science, but applies the ranking at the task-loss level rather than the confidence level used by prior calibration work (Noh et al. 2023, Ma et al. 2023). The two-stage training (independent expert pretraining followed by cooperative gating training) is a practical simplification over end-to-end joint training used by most prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's main practical value is its generality and efficiency: the DMoME architecture is backbone-agnostic (nnUNet, Inception V3, LeNet-5) and achieves a 4× FLOPS reduction over MoMKE with 13× fewer parameters than mmFormer/ShaSpec, making it attractive for resource-constrained deployments. The MoFe loss is task-agnostic and could be grafted onto existing multimodal frameworks to improve robustness and calibration without architectural changes. Open questions include scalability to many more modalities, handling training-time missingness (only test-time is addressed), and whether the gating mechanism remains interpretable and stable in high-dimensional or highly heterogeneous modality spaces.
