## Problem

Generating smooth, semantically coherent transition videos conditioned on first and last frames plus text prompts remains largely underexplored. Existing approaches either handle only a single transition type (e.g., object morphing via deep interpolation or frame interpolation via optical flow) or rely on task-specific architectures, leaving no unified framework that can address object morphing, motion prediction, concept blending, and scene transition with minimal adaptation.

## Input / Output

VTG takes two RGB images (the first and last frames of the desired transition) and two corresponding text captions as input, and produces a sequence of N−2 intermediate video frames that form a temporally coherent transition. The input frames are encoded into latent space via a VAE and inverted through DDIM to obtain endpoint latent noises; the two captions are embedded separately and interpolated frame-by-frame. The output is a full-resolution video clip where the first and last frames match the inputs and intermediate frames exhibit smooth, rational content evolution.

## Method

VTG builds on the DynamiCrafter image-to-video diffusion backbone and introduces three complementary mechanisms. First, interpolation-based initialization uses spherical linear interpolation (SLERP) between the DDIM-inverted latent noises of the two endpoint frames, combined with two LoRA adapters (one per input frame) that are linearly blended to capture object-level semantics, and frame-aware SLERP on text embeddings to produce hybrid captions for intermediate frames. Second, Bidirectional Motion Prediction fine-tunes only the value and output matrices of temporal attention layers on a small video collection, rotating self-attention maps 180° to predict reversed motion, then fuses forward and backward noise predictions via linear interpolation to eliminate motion-reversal ambiguity. Third, Representation Alignment Regularization distills DINOv2 patch-level features into the denoising trajectory through a lightweight MLP projector, explicitly aligning diffusion latents with high-frequency semantic representations to reduce blur and improve fidelity.

## Results

On MorphBench, VTG achieves the best FID on both Metamorphosis (67.39) and Animation (39.16) subsets, and ties for best PPL on Animation (5.14), though it trails DiffMorpher on Metamorphosis PPL (22.80 vs. 18.19). On TC-Bench (motion prediction), VTG attains the highest TCR and TC-Score across all three categories (Attribute: 42.78/0.893; Object: 33.46/0.849; Background: 50.00/0.883). For concept blending, VTG achieves the best Smoothness scores on both CIFAR-10 (0.7932) and LAION-Aesthetics (0.8215), outperforming AID-I by 0.0061 and 0.0063 respectively. In the user study, VTG receives the highest preference rate across all four transition tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VTG outperforms all four video-diffusion baselines (DiffMorpher, TVG, SEINE, DynamiCrafter) on FID for both MorphBench subsets, with the largest margin over DynamiCrafter (67.39 vs. 87.32 on Metamorphosis FID). The sole exception is Metamorphosis PPL, where DiffMorpher (18.19) beats VTG (22.80), likely because DiffMorpher's image-level interpolation produces fewer perceptual jumps at the cost of temporal coherence. On TC-Bench, VTG dominates all six TCR/TC-Score metrics, with the most notable gap over SEINE on Object TCR (33.46 vs. 10.48) and Background TCR (50.00 vs. 7.96). For concept blending, VTG surpasses the strongest image-diffusion baseline AID-I by a small but consistent margin (0.7932 vs. 0.7861 on CIFAR-10; 0.8215 vs. 0.8152 on LAION-Aesthetics). Generative Inbetweening is compared only qualitatively and in the user study, where VTG is preferred for semantic coherence and fidelity. Overall, VTG wins or ties on every reported quantitative metric except one PPL cell, establishing consistent superiority across all four transition tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

VTG builds directly on pretrained image-to-video diffusion models (DynamiCrafter) and repurposes techniques from image morphing (SLERP latent interpolation, LoRA-based semantic blending) into a video context. It departs from prior transition-generation methods (SEINE's random masking, TVG's channel concatenation, Generative Inbetweening's forward-backward noise fusion) by unifying four previously separate tasks—object morphing, motion prediction, concept blending, and scene transition—under a single framework with minimal task-specific adaptation. The bidirectional motion prediction draws on the forward-backward fusion idea from Generative Inbetweening but extends it with a 180° temporal-attention rotation and lightweight fine-tuning, while the DINOv2-based representation alignment regularizes the denoising trajectory in a way not previously applied to video diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified formulation and the introduction of TransitBench (the first benchmark for concept blending and scene transition) lower the barrier for future work on general-purpose transition generation, which has been hindered by the lack of public evaluation data. Practically, VTG offers a single pipeline for video/film production workflows that previously required separate tools for morphing, interpolation, and scene blending. Limitations include reliance on a small 150-video training set, dependence on a specific pretrained backbone (DynamiCrafter), and evaluation on relatively short sequences; open questions include whether the approach scales to longer transitions, more diverse motion types, or higher resolutions without additional fine-tuning.
