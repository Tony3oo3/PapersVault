## Problem

Existing compressive video capture methods based on rolling-shutter diffuser measurements rely on sparse scene priors and fail to recover dense scenes with bright, detailed backgrounds. Data-driven video interpolators, while effective on in-distribution content, generalize poorly to chaotic motions at the >100× frame-rate acceleration regime needed for true high-speed imaging. The paper addresses this gap by fusing complementary rolling-shutter and global-shutter measurements with a learned reconstruction model that handles non-sparse scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252620/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes one spatially multiplexed RS-diffuser long-exposure measurement (encoding temporal dynamics via row-by-row exposure and a large structured PSF) and three short-exposure GS-lens key frames captured at known times during the RS exposure. The output is a high-speed RGB video of 231 frames at an effective 4,807 fps. Due to GPU memory constraints on the coordinate-network grid, the working resolution is downsampled by 16× in both space and time relative to the raw sensor, and the system requires well-lit scenes because the diffuser's large PSF footprint reduces light throughput.

## Method

The Space-Time Fusion Model (STFM) represents the video as an alpha-blend of a static background INR F_θ^S:(x,y)→(R,G,B) and a dynamic foreground INR F_θ^D:(x,y)→(R,G,B,α), with a separate motion INR F_θ^M:(x,y,t)→(u_x,u_y) predicting a time-varying warp field that displaces the spatial query coordinates before evaluating the dynamic network. All three networks use SIREN (sinusoidal activation) backbones with positional encoding. The final video is computed as v = α·v^S + (1−α)·v^D, where the time-varying alpha map handles occlusions. The inverse problem is solved by per-scene optimization of the INR parameters θ to minimize the data fidelity term ||Av_θ − b||² (fusing both RS and GS forward models) plus an anisotropic 3D total-variation regularizer on the motion field U with a temporal weighting factor β, which explicitly constrains local spatio-temporal consistency and is the key mechanism enabling dense-scene recovery without large training datasets.

## Results

RnGCam reconstructs 230 frames of high-speed video at an effective 4,807 fps from just 4 measurements (1 RS-diffuser long exposure + 3 GS-lens key frames), using consumer-grade hardware that costs ~10× less than prior compressive video systems (which require >$20k dual-shutter sCMOS cameras). On a complex simulated scene (smoke plume from a gun barrel), the method achieves the highest full-video PSNR among all compared methods, with notably stable per-frame PSNR across intermediate frames, whereas video interpolators degrade sharply between the three GS key frames. On real experimental data (spinning propeller with checkered background, tennis ball with complex background), the method recovers both static and dynamic components at high fidelity at 4,807 Hz.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3DTV-based reconstruction (single shutter [48] and dual shutter [1]), RnGCam+STFM clearly wins on dense scenes: 3DTV fails on early frames due to time-varying field-of-view blind spots and produces strong artifacts in intermediate frames, while STFM recovers these frames at high fidelity with the highest full-video PSNR. Against data-driven video interpolators (SuperSloMo [16] and EMA-VFI [54]), RnGCam wins decisively on complex, chaotic motions (e.g., the gun-barrel smoke scene in Fig. 7), where interpolators hallucinate and fail to recover information present early in the video since they only rely on the three GS key frames. However, on sparse, simple motions resembling their training distribution (e.g., a bird wing flap in Fig. 11), video interpolators perform comparably or even slightly better than RnGCam, which is expected given their large-scale training. The unified pyramid recurrent network [17] is listed among the interpolator baselines but is not individually highlighted in the main comparisons. Overall, the key differentiator is that RnGCam's RS-diffuser measurement provides genuine temporal information between GS key frames, giving it a structural advantage over pure interpolation methods for out-of-distribution, high-chaos scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RS-diffuser compressive video line (Antipa et al. 2019; Weinberg & Katz 2020), which encoded high-speed dynamics into a single rolling-shutter measurement but relied on sparsity priors (3DTV) that fail on dense scenes. It also builds on the dual-shutter RS+GS sensing concept from Sheinin et al. (CVPR 2022), which combined the two sensor types for vibration sensing but did not address video recovery. The key departure is the introduction of a Space-Time Fusion Model (STFM) that uses an INR (SIREN) to explicitly decompose the video into static and dynamic components with a learned motion-warping field, replacing the 3DTV regularizer with anisotropic TV on the motion field — a formulation that naturally handles dense backgrounds without requiring sparsity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that three short GS exposures can anchor an INR reconstruction of a dense scene, the paper makes kHz-rate high-speed video accessible with consumer-grade hardware at roughly 10× lower cost than prior compressive video systems. The explicit static/dynamic factorization and motion-warping regularization are inductive biases that could transfer to other compressive imaging inverse problems. Open questions include whether the method scales to higher spatial/temporal resolutions (currently limited by GPU memory and small RS pixel pitch), whether video-specific INRs (e.g., WIRE) would further improve quality, and how the approach handles scenes that violate the static-background assumption (sudden object appearance, camera motion).
