## Problem

Existing referring expression segmentation (RES) and generalized RES (GRES) models accept only text prompts and produce at most one mask per query, making them unable to handle real-world instructions that reference visual entities (e.g., 'all objects on <mask-ref>') or require selecting a group of related masks. The paper introduces omnimodal referring expression segmentation (ORES), a new task that accepts both text and mask-based visual prompts and outputs an arbitrary group of segmentation masks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250118/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image, a free-form text prompt, and optionally one or more reference masks (visual prompts) as input. A segmentation foundation model (SAM or Co-DETR) first proposes a pool of candidate masks over the image; the model then outputs a binary selection over these candidates, yielding a variable-size group of segmentation masks. Reference masks in the prompt are encoded as mask tokens with a distinct special token (<mask-ref-pre>) to differentiate them from candidate mask tokens (<mask-pool-pre>), allowing the LLM to reason about spatial and semantic relationships between the reference and the targets.

## Method

RAS extends LLaVA-1.5 (Vicuna-13B) with a mask-centric LMM that encodes each candidate mask as a single token by pooling features from an ensemble of four visual encoders (CLIP, SigLIP, ConvNeXt-CLIP, DINOv2) within the mask region and projecting the concatenated features into the language space. The key architectural choice is non-autoregressive decoding: instead of generating mask embeddings sequentially, the model feeds all candidate mask tokens through the LLM in one pass and applies a binary selection classifier on each output hidden state, converting the set-prediction problem into per-mask binary classification. Training is two-stage: first, the mask projector is pretrained via a captioning pretext task using only mask tokens (no global image tokens); second, all modules except visual encoders are fine-tuned on MASKGROUPS-2M (2M auto-generated samples) and optionally MASKGROUPS-HQ (100K human-annotated samples) with a weighted binary cross-entropy loss that up-weights positive candidates to handle class imbalance.

## Results

On the novel ORES task (MASKGROUPS-HQ), RAS with ORES finetuning achieves 73.13 overall cIoU (64.77 gIoU), substantially outperforming the best prior GRES model GSVA (49.55 cIoU on text-only prompts only, with no mask-ref capability). On the standard RES benchmark, RAS with Co-DETR and RES finetuning reaches 77.8 average cIoU across RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg, setting a new state of the art. On GRES (gRefCOCO), RAS with GRES finetuning achieves 71.79 average cIoU, surpassing the previous best SAM4MLLM (67.82) by nearly 4 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the novel ORES task, RAS with ORES finetuning (73.13 overall cIoU) outperforms GSVA (49.55 cIoU, text-only only) by 23.6 points and is the sole method capable of processing mask-ref prompts, where it reaches 68.77 cIoU versus no baseline support. On RES, RAS with Co-DETR and RES finetuning (77.8 avg cIoU) surpasses PSALM 1.3B (77.1), SAM4MLLM 8B (76.4), UNINEXT-H (76.6), and u-LLaVA 7B (75.9), with the largest margin over Groundhog 7B (74.2) and ReLA (68.3). On GRES, RAS with GRES finetuning (71.79 avg cIoU) leads SAM4MLLM 7B (67.82) by 3.97 points and GSVA 13B (67.46) by 4.33 points, while also improving N-acc. to 69.05 on val versus 66.08 for SAM4MLLM. Notably, RAS without task-specific finetuning already matches or exceeds several baselines (e.g., 75.0 avg on RES vs. GSVA's 74.8), indicating strong generalization from the ORES training alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAS builds on LLaVA-1.5 as its LMM backbone and adopts the multi-encoder visual feature ensemble strategy from Cambrian-1, but departs from both by replacing patch-level visual tokens with mask-level tokens and replacing autoregressive text generation with per-mask binary classification. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of segmentation foundation models (SAM, Co-DETR) for candidate proposal and LMMs for semantic comprehension, bridging a gap that prior grounding LMMs (Groundhog, GSVA, LISA) address only partially—those models either lack mask-based visual prompts or rely on autoregressive decoding. The paper explicitly positions ORES as extending GRES by adding reference-mask inputs and emphasizing group-level output, filling a capability gap identified in Table 1 where no prior paradigm supports all four prompt/target dimensions simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of ORES and the MASKGROUPS-2M/HQ datasets provides the community with a new benchmark for evaluating vision-language segmentation that more closely mirrors real user interactions (e.g., 'select all objects on <mask-ref>'). The finding that non-autoregressive per-mask binary classification substantially outperforms autoregressive mask-embedding prediction (both in accuracy and 4× latency) offers a practical design principle for future LMM-based segmentation systems. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-round interactive refinement, whether the 13B LLM can be replaced by a more compact model without significant quality loss, and how well the mask-centric formulation generalizes to video or 3D segmentation.
