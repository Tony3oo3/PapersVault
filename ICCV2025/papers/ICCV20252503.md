## Problem

Existing brain decoding methods produce only coarse one-sentence captions that lack fine-grained details on objects, attributes, spatial relationships, and locations, leading to imprecise visual reconstructions. Additionally, no reliable evaluation framework exists for assessing long, detailed brain-decoded captions, as traditional n-gram metrics are style-sensitive and model-based metrics cannot handle long sequences. This paper addresses both gaps by systematically exploring which vision feature spaces within MLLMs best support fine-grained brain decoding and by introducing a dedicated benchmark with structured evaluation metrics.

## Input / Output

The method takes raw fMRI brain signals (L_s-dimensional vectors from the Allen 7T dataset, 4 subjects × 24,980 training samples) and produces multimodal outputs through an MLLM: detailed multi-sentence descriptions, bounding box coordinates for concept localization, and multiple-choice answers for salient question-answering. Critically, the brain signals are not mapped directly to text but are first aligned to pre-trained visual encoder features (CLIP-224/336, DINOv2-224, or SigLIP-384), which are then passed through the MLLM's connector and LLM for instruction-following, enabling zero-shot decoding without any external textual or spatial annotations during training.

## Method

VINDEX trains a lightweight brain encoder to predict visual features from fMRI signals, using a denoiser-based alignment objective that combines regression loss with a masked diffusion denoising loss to regularize against overfitting on scarce brain data. The key design choice is which feature space to align to: the paper systematically compares four options—Single Encoder (CLIP), Mixture of Encoders (CLIP+DINOv2 interleaved), Aggregated Feature (multi-layer SigLIP channels concatenated), and Nested Features (hierarchical coarse-to-fine token sets via sequential 2×2 pooling, yielding 144→36→9→1 tokens). The brain encoder's predicted features are fed into a frozen MLLM (Shikra/LLaVA) for zero-shot multimodal interaction. A central empirical finding is that 9-token nested features (NF9) outperform both dense single-encoder features and larger token counts, because brain signals encode less visual information than real images and fewer tokens better match this information bottleneck. The denoising objective (a one-layer MLP with 1024 channels predicting noise on masked feature positions) improves stability and performance over pure MSE regression at no additional training cost.

## Results

On BrainHub concise captioning, VINDEX achieves BLEU-1 of 60.00, CIDEr of 60.32, and SPICE of 12.84, outperforming all zero-shot baselines (UMBRAE: BLEU-1 59.44, CIDEr 61.06; MEVOX: BLEU-1 58.56, CIDEr 54.37) and subject-specific methods. On the proposed MG-BrainDUB benchmark, the NF9 (9-token nested feature) configuration achieves the best CAPTURE score of 0.5021, Object F1 of 59.74, and Salient QA accuracy of 83.83%, substantially surpassing NeuroVLA (CAPTURE 0.4356, SQA 75.50%). For concept localization, VINDEX reaches acc@0.5 of 18.10 and IoU of 20.89 across all categories, edging out UMBRAE (18.03 / 20.63) and surpassing all image-reconstruction-based grounding results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VINDEX outperforms UMBRAE [53] on most concise captioning metrics (BLEU-1 60.00 vs. 59.44, SPICE 12.84 vs. 12.79, CLIP-S 68.26 vs. 67.78), though UMBRAE retains a slight edge on CIDEr (61.06 vs. 60.32). Against MEVOX [55], VINDEX leads on every metric, with the largest gap in CIDEr (60.32 vs. 54.37). VINDEX substantially surpasses subject-specific methods: SDRecon [49] (BLEU-1 36.21), OneLLM [13] (47.04), and BrainCap [12] (55.96), as well as cross-subject methods NeuroVLA [43] (57.19) and MindEye2 [42] (54.82), despite being trained on only four subjects without external annotations. For concept localization, VINDEX (acc@0.5 = 18.10) beats all brain-based baselines including UMBRAE (18.03) and UMBRAE-S (14.31), and also surpasses image-reconstruction-based grounding from BrainDiffuser [35] (17.49), MindEye [41] (15.34), and DREAM [54] (16.21). On MG-BrainDUB descriptive captioning, NF9 achieves the best CAPTURE (0.5021) and SQA accuracy (83.83%), while NeuroVLA [43] trails at CAPTURE 0.4356 and SQA 75.50%. The Shikra-img upper bound [7] (BLEU-1 82.38) remains well above all brain-based methods, confirming the gap between direct image input and brain-signal decoding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on UMBRAE, which established zero-shot multimodal brain decoding by aligning fMRI signals with CLIP features and feeding them into MLLMs. VINDEX departs from UMBRAE in two conceptual directions: it systematically explores multiple vision feature spaces (single encoder, mixture of encoders, aggregated multi-layer features, and Matryoshka-style nested features) rather than relying on a single CLIP representation, and it replaces the deterministic L2 regression alignment with a denoising-diffusion objective to improve generalization under data scarcity. The feature-space exploration draws on recent MLLM findings about dense connectors, mixture-of-encoders, and hierarchical token representations, repurposing these ideas for the brain-decoding setting where data is orders of magnitude scarcer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The counterintuitive finding that 9 visual tokens outperform 144 tokens (and that 729-token aggregated features perform worst) challenges the prevailing assumption in MLLMs that richer visual features always improve downstream tasks, suggesting brain signals encode a fundamentally different and more limited information profile than natural images. The MG-BrainDUB benchmark and its structured object/attribute/relation metrics address a real gap in evaluating long, detailed brain-decoded captions where n-gram and CLIP-based metrics break down. Open questions include whether the token-count sweet spot generalizes across fMRI acquisition protocols, subject populations, and stimulus types, and whether the denoising objective's benefits persist at larger data scales where regression overfitting becomes less severe.
