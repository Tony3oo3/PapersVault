## Problem

Traditional V2V cooperative driving methods rely on rigid optimization protocols or learned policies that generalize poorly to unseen interaction patterns, while existing LLM-based driving approaches cannot produce executable control signals or handle real-time inference latency. No prior system integrates full-pipeline LLM-based language negotiation with real-time waypoint-level driving control for multi-vehicle cooperation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250864/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes point clouds (encoded via PointPillars), front camera images, BEV occupancy maps and features, navigation/speed intentions, and broadcast information (speed, intention, position) from surrounding vehicles within a 70 m communication range. It produces 20 waypoints at 5 Hz for the control module, high-level driving intentions (navigation + speed), and natural-language negotiation messages exchanged over up to 3 rounds. The high-level negotiation pipeline runs asynchronously at low frequency while the low-level perception-planning-control loop operates at high frequency, decoupling LLM inference latency from real-time control.

## Method

CoLMDriver employs a parallel architecture with two pipelines: a high-level guidance pipeline (LLM negotiation + VLM intention planning) and a low-level perception-planning-control pipeline. The LLM-based negotiation module (Qwen2.5-3B) uses a critic-feedback paradigm inspired by Actor-Critic RL: a negotiation quality evaluator scores each round on consensus (via LLM judgment), safety, and efficiency (via waypoint-based formulas), then provides textual criticism that is fed back into the next negotiation round to accelerate convergence. A dynamic graph grouping mechanism constructs a spatiotemporal vehicle graph using safety-score thresholds and DFS, merging intersecting groups across time to form stable negotiation clusters. The intention-guided waypoint planner is a Transformer-based model that fuses BEV features (via MotionNet encoder) with goal-oriented inputs (target points, navigation/speed intentions) through an MLP fuser, then uses cross-attention between waypoint queries and environmental/guidance contexts to generate executable waypoints. The VLM intention planner (InternVL2-4B with LoRA fine-tuning) handles non-cooperation periods by synthesizing multi-modal context into driving intentions, and the entire system is trained via transfer learning on expert driving data collected from the V2Xverse platform.

## Results

On the proposed InterDrive benchmark (92 tasks across 10 interactive scenarios), CoLMDriver achieves a Driving Score of 88.53, Route Completion of 94.05%, Infraction Score of 0.90, and Success Rate of 80.4%, outperforming the next-best baseline (Rule-based, DS=78.38) by over 10 points in DS. On the public Town05 Long benchmark, CoLMDriver reaches a Driving Score of 81.49, surpassing ReasonNet (73.22) by 11%. The system also demonstrates robustness to inference latency, with only a 6.62% DS drop when running on an RTX 3090 versus ideal zero-latency conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoLMDriver outperforms all six baselines on the InterDrive benchmark in Driving Score, with the largest margins over single-vehicle methods: VAD (25.37) and UniAD (35.17) suffer from low infraction scores (0.33, 0.38) due to frequent collisions without negotiation, while LMDrive (48.83) achieves a high IS (0.85) but poor RC (58.02%) because vehicles stall in dual-yielding deadlocks. Among cooperative baselines, TCP (73.68) and CoDriving (74.13) show moderate DS but lower SR (50% and 57% respectively), indicating inconsistent performance across scenarios. The Rule-based method (78.38) is the closest competitor, leveraging traffic rules to order vehicle passage, but CoLMDriver still leads by 10.15 DS points and 8.4 SR points, demonstrating that language negotiation generalizes better than fixed rules. On Town05 Long, CoLMDriver (81.49) beats both InterFuser (68.31) and ReasonNet (73.22), confirming that receiving neighbor intentions improves even single-ego planning in non-interactive settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of LLM-based driving and cooperative (V2V) autonomous driving. Prior LLM-driven cooperative works (AgentsCoDriver, CoDrivingLLM) are restricted to discrete decision-making without executable control signals, while optimization-based and MARL-based cooperative methods lack generalization to unseen interaction patterns. CoLMDriver repurposes the Actor-Critic paradigm from reinforcement learning as a negotiation quality evaluator, and introduces a parallel high-level/low-level pipeline architecture to bridge the latency gap between LLM inference and real-time control — a design absent from prior LLM-driving systems. The intention-guided waypoint planner draws on the MotionNet encoder and Transformer decoder architectures from end-to-end driving, but augments them with language-derived intention conditioning as a novel input modality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first full-pipeline (sensor-to-control) LLM-based cooperative driving system, CoLMDriver demonstrates that language negotiation can practically resolve multi-vehicle conflicts in real time, with the InterDrive benchmark providing a much-needed evaluation suite for highly interactive V2V scenarios. The critic-feedback mechanism for accelerating negotiation convergence is a transferable idea applicable to other multi-agent LLM coordination tasks. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to CARLA simulation with small models (3B/4B), limited diversity of language interaction demonstrations, and unaddressed questions about scalability beyond 8 vehicles or robustness to communication failures. Whether the parallel architecture generalizes to production-grade latency constraints and heterogeneous vehicle fleets remains an open question.
