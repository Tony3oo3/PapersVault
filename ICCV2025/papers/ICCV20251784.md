## Problem

Federated training of Vision Transformers on resource-constrained clients remains largely unexplored, as ViTs' lack of spatial inductive biases makes them far more computationally demanding than CNNs. Existing resource-constrained FL methods (partial parameter aggregation or training) are designed around CNN architectures and do not exploit ViTs' patch-based processing to fundamentally reduce client-side computation.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images resized to 224×224 and patchifies them into 16×16 patches. During local training, a configurable masking ratio (default 75%) randomly discards most patches, so only ~25% of patches are processed on the client; at inference, full images are used. The client uploads balanced patch features (after median sampling to equalize per-class counts) to the server rather than raw images or gradients, and the final output is a class label over the task's label set.

## Method

EFTViT splits a ViT-B backbone (pretrained on ImageNet-21K) into a lightweight local module (M=2 Transformer layers + classification head) trained on each client and a larger global module (N=10 Transformer layers) trained centrally on the server, enabling full-parameter optimization while keeping client computation minimal. The key mechanism is patch-wise masked training: images are randomly masked at ratio r_m, and only unmasked patches pass through the local module, reducing forward FLOPs by up to 5.6× with negligible accuracy loss. Instead of aggregating parameters, clients upload intermediate patch features to the server, which trains the global module on these features to learn universal representations across heterogeneous data. A median sampling strategy balances the number of uploaded patch features per class to the median, erasing client data-distribution information and providing content privacy since features encode only a small fraction of each image.

## Results

EFTViT achieves the highest accuracy among all resource-constrained federated methods across three datasets and two heterogeneity levels (β=0.1, 1.0). On the most challenging setting (UC Merced Land-Use, β=0.1), EFTViT reaches 98.80% accuracy, outperforming the second-best method FedRA (96.43%) by 2.37%. On CIFAR-100 with high heterogeneity (β=0.1), EFTViT achieves 90.02%, surpassing FEDBFPT (88.91%) and FedRA (87.89%). Additionally, EFTViT reduces local training GFLOPs by up to 5.6× (2.997 vs 16.982 GFLOPs) and cuts local training time by up to 3.1× compared to FedRA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EFTViT outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and heterogeneity setting. Against FedRA (ECCV 2024), the closest competitor, EFTViT gains 2.37% on UC Merced Land-Use (β=0.1: 98.80 vs 96.43) and 0.74% on CIFAR-100 (β=1.0: 90.81 vs 90.07). Against FEDBFPT (IJCAI 2023), EFTViT leads by 1.11% on CIFAR-100 (β=0.1) and 0.85% on UC Merced Land-Use (β=0.1). The PEFT-based baselines (Fed-LoRA, Fed-Prompt, Fed-Bias) show substantially larger gaps under high heterogeneity: Fed-LoRA drops to 70.23% on CIFAR-100 (β=0.1) versus EFTViT's 90.02%, a 19.79% deficit. EFTViT also converges dramatically faster, reaching 85% accuracy in 6 rounds on UC Merced Land-Use versus 163 rounds for Fed-Prompt (27.1× speedup) and 55 rounds for FedRA (9.1× speedup).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the masked-image concept from self-supervised pre-training (MAE and related work) for a fundamentally different purpose: reducing federated training cost rather than learning representations. It departs from the two dominant paradigms in resource-constrained FL—Partial Parameter Aggregation (HeteroFL, AdaptiveFL) and Partial Parameter Training (FedGKT, FedEKT)—by exploiting ViT's patch-based architecture to split computation between clients and server while still training all parameters. Unlike PEFT-based FL methods (Fed-LoRA, Fed-Prompt, Fed-Bias) that limit trainable parameters and sacrifice model capacity, EFTViT enables full-parameter optimization through hierarchical division, positioning itself as a middle ground between full local training and parameter-efficient fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5.6× computational reduction and 3.1× training-time reduction make ViT-based federated learning viable on genuinely resource-constrained edge devices, a setting where prior CNN-oriented methods were the only practical option. The dual privacy mechanism (masked patches obscuring content, median sampling obscuring distribution) is a practical contribution for deployment scenarios where both data content and statistical properties are sensitive. Open questions include whether the 75% masking ratio and M=2 local layers generalize to larger ViTs (ViT-L/H), denser tasks like detection or segmentation, and whether the approach extends to non-classification federated settings. The evaluation is limited to three relatively small datasets and a single ViT-B backbone, leaving scalability untested.
