## Problem

Event cameras offer high temporal resolution for feature tracking but lack fine-grained texture, causing error accumulation over time. Fusing event data with conventional image frames to compensate is non-trivial because the two streams arrive asynchronously at different rates, exhibit different uncertainty distributions, and must be fused without sacrificing the high-frequency advantage of events. Prior approaches either use naive position-replacement fusion or align events to image frame rates, both of which degrade performance or temporal resolution.

## Input / Output

The method takes a grayscale reference image (from which small patches are extracted at query points), a preprocessed event stream (SBT-Max format at 100 Hz for EC or 200 Hz for EDS), and grayscale image frames (24 Hz or 75 Hz). The event module operates on small patches (a few dozen pixels) centered at the last predicted position, while the image module processes full frames. Output is a continuous stream of tracked point displacements at 80+ FPS (multimodal) or 100+ FPS (event-only), each accompanied by a 2×2 covariance matrix representing positional uncertainty.

## Method

BlinkTrack employs two parallel prediction modules—an event module (shallow U-Net pyramid encoder + dual ConvLSTM for feature and displacement propagation) and an image module (pyramid encoder + MLP-Mixer prediction head)—each producing a displacement estimate and a learned uncertainty covariance. A differentiable Kalman filter with a constant-velocity state model fuses these asynchronous predictions by weighting each observation according to its predicted uncertainty, naturally handling arbitrary timestamp offsets between event and image measurements. The key insight is that the Kalman filter's internal state evolves continuously, so it can accept observations from either modality at any time without explicit alignment, while the learned uncertainty (supervised via visibility labels and displacement loss through the filter) lets the network adaptively trust or discount each measurement. Training is two-stage for the event module: first the displacement predictor is trained with direct L1 loss, then the uncertainty predictor is trained end-to-end through the differentiable Kalman filter with an additional cross-entropy visibility loss.

## Results

On the EC dataset (event-only), BlinkTrack with Kalman filter achieves 0.835 FA and 0.820 Exp FA, outperforming Deep-EV-Tracker (0.795/0.787) and EKLT (0.811/0.775). On EDS (event+image with Kalman), it reaches 0.653 FA and 0.550 Exp FA, surpassing D-Tracker+KLT (0.594/0.503) and FF-KDT (0.526*/0.431*). On the occlusion-augmented EC-occ dataset, the Kalman filter yields a 149% relative improvement in δ^occ (28.5 vs. 5.8 for D-Tracker+KLT) and a 22.3% gain in δ^all (43.4 vs. 35.5 without Kalman). The method runs at >80 FPS with multi-modality data and >100 FPS with preprocessed event data, compared to 50 FPS for Deep-EV-Tracker and 7 FPS for FF-KDT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HASTE (pure event, no frames), BlinkTrack dominates on both EC (0.835 vs. 0.442 FA) and EDS (0.569 vs. 0.096 FA), reflecting the advantage of learned features over hand-crafted multi-hypothesis tracking. EKLT (no neural network) is competitive on EC event-only (0.811 vs. 0.835) but falls far behind on EDS (0.325 vs. 0.569), where the learned event module's larger receptive field matters. Deep-EV-Tracker is the closest event-only competitor: BlinkTrack wins on EC (0.835 vs. 0.795 FA) but slightly trails D-Tracker(E2VID) on EDS (0.569 vs. 0.579 FA), a gap the authors attribute to E2VID's richer training data. In the event+image setting, the naive D-Tracker+KLT fusion (replacing initial positions) underperforms both single-modality results on EC (0.735 FA), while BlinkTrack with Kalman filter achieves 0.851 FA, matching FF-KDT's 0.852* but at 124 predictions/sec vs. FF-KDT's 24. On EDS, BlinkTrack (E+I w.K) clearly leads with 0.653 FA vs. 0.594 (D-Tracker+KLT) and 0.526* (FF-KDT). The most dramatic advantage appears on occlusion data: on EC-occ, δ^occ jumps from 5.8 (D-Tracker+KLT) to 28.5 (Ours E+I w.K), a 149% relative gain, confirming the Kalman filter's role in suppressing erroneous measurements during occlusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

BlinkTrack builds directly on Deep-EV-Tracker's event-patch matching architecture and PIPs/CoTracker-style frame-based point tracking, but departs from both the naive initialization-replacement fusion used by Deep-EV-Tracker and the attention-based implicit alignment explored in concurrent work (FF-KDT, Gehrig et al.). The key conceptual departure is repurposing the classical Kalman filter as a differentiable, learnable fusion layer that naturally handles asynchronous multi-modal observations without sacrificing event-camera temporal resolution. The paper also introduces a synthetic data pipeline (MultiTrack, EC-occ, EDS-occ) that fills a gap left by existing benchmarks, which lack occlusion ground truth and are too simple to stress-test fusion strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 80+ FPS multimodal tracking speed makes event-image fusion practically viable for latency-sensitive applications like autonomous driving and drone navigation, where prior fusion methods (FF-KDT at 7 FPS, E2VID at 153 ms/frame) were too slow. The differentiable Kalman filter provides a principled, lightweight alternative to attention-based fusion that scales well and requires minimal coupling between modalities. However, the occlusion experiments rely on synthetic data, and the authors acknowledge that network capacity and receptive field limit long-range tracking; scaling up while preserving the >100 FPS budget remains an open challenge. Whether the Kalman-filter fusion paradigm generalizes beyond 2D point tracking to higher-level tasks (e.g., 3D tracking, SLAM) is unexplored.
