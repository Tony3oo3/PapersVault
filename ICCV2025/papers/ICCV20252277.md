## Problem

Feature coding for large models remains largely unexplored: existing work targets small-scale CNN features, focuses almost exclusively on visual discriminative tasks, and lacks a public dataset or unified evaluation protocol. Without standardized test conditions and representative features from Transformer-based, LLM, and diffusion models, fair comparison across feature coding methods is impossible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252277/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes intermediate features at predefined split points from DINOv2 (257×1536 or multi-layer 2×4×1611×1536), Llama3 (N×4096 token embeddings), and SD3 (16×128×128 VAE-decoder latents), truncates them to task-specific value ranges, quantizes to 10-bit integers, and packs into 2D YUV-400 frames. The codec outputs a compressed bitstream measured in Bits Per Feature Point (BPFP) and a decoded floating-point feature that is fed through a fixed task head (linear classifier, VAE decoder, etc.) to report task accuracy.

## Method

The paper establishes a benchmark with two baselines adapted from image coding: a handcrafted VTM-23.3 Intra codec (QPs 22–42) and a learned Hyperprior model modified to single-channel 2D input with a loss of BPFP + λ·MSE. Both share a pre/post-processing pipeline of range truncation, uniform 10-bit quantization, and 2D packing (e.g., 2740×1536 for Seg, 512×512 for TTI), with inverse operations at decode time. Unified test conditions are formalized, including the BPFP bitrate metric (replacing BPP to handle non-visual features) and fixed task heads with specific accuracy metrics (Accuracy, mIoU, RMSE, FID). The authors further analyze rate-accuracy-drop curves, distortion-accuracy linearity, encoding/decoding complexity, and cross-task generalizability of the learned codec to identify open challenges for future large-model feature coding.

## Results

The VTM baseline achieves 99.80% accuracy at 1.94 BPFP on Image Classification (vs. 100% at 32 BPFP original), 80.42% mIoU at 1.76 BPFP on Segmentation, and 6.14 FID at 1.41 BPFP on Text-to-Image Synthesis. The Hyperprior baseline reaches 93.20% accuracy at 2.01 BPFP on Cls, 77.96% mIoU at 1.71 BPFP on Seg, and 25.25 FID at 1.44 BPFP on TTI. Both baselines achieve over 90% bitrate reduction (from 32 BPFP to ~1–2 BPFP) while retaining most task accuracy, with VTM consistently outperforming Hyperprior at comparable bitrates. The D-A analysis (Table 5) shows VTM achieves higher distortion-accuracy correlation coefficients (e.g., 0.9413 vs. 0.9296 on Cls; 0.7490 vs. 0.4713 on CSR), confirming its superior adaptability to large model features.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against prior feature coding methods; instead, it introduces two baselines (VTM handcrafted codec and Hyperprior learned codec) as the first benchmarks for large model feature coding. VTM consistently outperforms Hyperprior on most tasks at comparable bitrates: on Cls, VTM achieves 99.80% accuracy at 1.94 BPFP versus Hyperprior's 93.20% at 2.01 BPFP; on Seg, VTM reaches 80.42% mIoU at 1.76 BPFP versus 77.96% at 1.71 BPFP; on TTI, VTM achieves 6.14 FID at 1.41 BPFP versus 25.25 FID at 1.44 BPFP. However, Hyperprior achieves a slightly lower RMSE on Dpt (0.4256 vs. 0.5021) at comparable bitrate, and requires far less encoding time (milliseconds vs. seconds-to-hours). The generalizability analysis (Table 6) reveals that Hyperprior models trained on one task perform poorly on others (e.g., CSR-trained model drops to 0.20% accuracy on Seg at 0.78 BPFP), while VTM, being handcrafted, is inherently task-agnostic. The D-A analysis shows feature MSE is a poor proxy for semantic distortion, especially for textual features (CSR correlation coefficient only 0.7490 for VTM, 0.4713 for Hyperprior), highlighting a key open challenge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature coding line of work (originating in the Coding for Machines framework) from small-scale CNNs (ResNet, MaskRCNN) to large Transformer-based models (DINOv2, Llama3, SD3). It repurposes two well-established image/video codecs—VVC/VTM (handcrafted) and Hyperprior (learned)—as baselines by adding pre/post-processing modules (truncation, quantization, 2D packing) to adapt them to feature characteristics. The key departure is the introduction of a unified evaluation framework (BPFP metric, standardized task heads) and a multi-model, multi-task, multi-modality dataset, addressing the lack of standardized test conditions that has hampered fair comparison in prior feature coding studies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper establishes the first comprehensive benchmark for large model feature coding, providing a dataset, unified test conditions, and baseline results that future work can build upon. The finding that feature MSE poorly correlates with task accuracy for textual features (CSR) highlights a fundamental gap in how semantic distortion is measured, suggesting that current rate-distortion frameworks may be inadequate for non-visual features. Open questions include developing codecs with strong cross-task generalizability, designing semantic distortion metrics appropriate for large model features, and creating lightweight codecs that balance accuracy, speed, and adaptability—particularly as distributed deployment of large models becomes more prevalent.
