## Problem

Embodied AI navigation policies trained in simulation struggle to transfer to real-world deployment due to the sim-to-real gap: synthetic environments lack photorealism, while high-fidelity real-world reconstructions (e.g., Matterport3D, HM3D) require expensive capture hardware and labor-intensive pipelines, making per-environment personalization impractical. This paper asks whether low-effort, consumer-grade iPhone video captures can generate sufficient 3D scene reconstructions to enable effective policy training and real-world transfer for indoor image-goal navigation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250435/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline ingests 20–30 minute iPhone 13 Pro Max RGB-D recordings (captured via Polycam) and processes them through Nerfstudio into 1000 aligned RGB-depth frames with camera poses. These frames feed DN-Splatter to produce a 3D Gaussian Splat, which is then converted to a Poisson-reconstructed .ply mesh and exported as .glb for Habitat-Sim. At deployment, the trained policy consumes a current RGB observation and a goal image, outputting discrete navigation actions (forward, turn left/right, stop) for a Stretch robot, with episodes capped at 100 steps in the real world.

## Method

The core pipeline reconstructs navigable meshes from casual phone captures using DN-Splatter, which regularizes 3D Gaussian Splatting training with sensor depth (λ_d=0.2) and monocular surface normals from Metric3D-V2, followed by Poisson surface reconstruction to extract a watertight mesh. This mesh is loaded into Habitat-Sim, where ImageNav episodes are generated on the largest navmesh island (1000 training / 100 validation per scene). Policies use a VC-1 visual encoder with an LSTM action head (following Silwal et al.), pre-trained on HM3D or HSSD and then fine-tuned for only 20M steps on the personalized scene mesh. A key empirical finding is that even overfitting a from-scratch policy on a single reconstructed scene yields non-trivial real-world success (50% with Polycam meshes), and fine-tuning pre-trained policies on these meshes achieves 70% real-world success versus 50% zero-shot, with a sim-vs-real correlation of 0.87–0.97.

## Results

On the real-world lounge scene (10 episodes, 100-step cap), fine-tuning the HM3D pre-trained policy on reconstructed meshes improves success rate from 50% (zero-shot) to 70% (+20% absolute), while fine-tuning the HSSD policy improves from 10% to 50% (POLYCAM) or 40% (DN) (+30–40% absolute). In simulation, fine-tuning for only 20M steps pushes success rates to 90%+ across all captured scenes, compared to ~60% average for zero-shot HM3D and <15% for zero-shot HSSD. The sim-to-real correlation coefficient (SRCC) between simulation and real-world performance reaches 0.87–0.97 across reconstructed meshes, confirming that simulation gains reliably predict real-world improvements.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four configurations: zero-shot HM3D (real-world dataset), zero-shot HSSD (synthetic dataset), fine-tuned variants of both, and overfitted policies trained from scratch without any large-scale pre-training. Fine-tuning on captured-scene meshes consistently outperforms zero-shot baselines: the HM3D fine-tuned policy achieves 70% real-world SR versus 50% zero-shot (+20%), and the HSSD fine-tuned policy reaches 50% (POLYCAM) versus 10% zero-shot (+40%). Between the two pre-training datasets, HM3D (photorealistic) substantially outperforms HSSD (synthetic) in both zero-shot (50% vs. 10%) and fine-tuned (70% vs. 50%) settings, confirming that real-world pre-training data is critical for transfer. Between mesh types, DN and POLYCAM meshes yield nearly identical fine-tuned real-world performance (both 70% for HM3D), but POLYCAM meshes preserve more visual detail and give the overfitted (no pre-training) policy a 50% vs. 10% real-world advantage, suggesting visual fidelity matters more when the policy has not seen diverse scenes. In simulation, both mesh types reach 90%+ after fine-tuning, with no clear winner, and the SRCC of 0.87–0.97 indicates simulation performance is a strong predictor of real-world outcomes regardless of mesh type.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper composes existing components—DN-Splatter for depth/normal-regularized Gaussian Splat meshing, Habitat-Sim for embodied training, and the VC-1-based policy architecture from Silwal et al.—into a new end-to-end pipeline for personalized sim-to-real navigation. It departs from SplatNav (modular, single-environment, no end-to-end learning) and GaussNav (simulation-only, no real-world deployment) by demonstrating full real-to-sim-to-real transfer with end-to-end policies on indoor ImageNav. The iPhone-capture approach is inspired by Phone2Proc and MuSHRoom, but differs in reconstructing full room-scale meshes rather than generating layout variations, and in fine-tuning pre-trained policies rather than training from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pipeline dramatically lowers the barrier to scene collection (20–30 min with an iPhone vs. hours with Matterport cameras), making personalized agent training accessible to labs without expensive capture infrastructure. The strong sim-to-real correlation (0.87–0.97) and the finding that overfitting on a single scene reconstruction yields non-trivial real-world performance (50% SR) without large-scale pre-training suggest a practical path to rapid deployment. However, real-world validation is limited to one scene (lounge) with 10 episodes on a single robot platform, and the approach has not been tested on more complex tasks (object navigation, rearrangement, mobile manipulation) or larger multi-room environments, leaving open whether the pipeline scales beyond the 1–3 room scale demonstrated here.
