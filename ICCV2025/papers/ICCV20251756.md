## Problem

Sparse-view 3D reconstruction models typically require precise camera poses as input, yet recovering these poses from sparse uncalibrated images remains difficult. Existing pose-free approaches either rely on implicit NeRF representations with inefficient volume rendering (PF-LRM, LEAP) or produce sparse point clouds (DUSt3R) that lack the fidelity needed for high-quality novel view synthesis. The paper asks whether one can directly predict dense, pixel-aligned 3D Gaussian maps from uncalibrated images to simultaneously achieve high-fidelity reconstruction and fast camera pose recovery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251756/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N uncalibrated RGB images (no extrinsics or intrinsics required) at 512×512 resolution and produces N pixel-aligned 3D Gaussian maps in a unified reference frame, where each image pixel corresponds to one q-dimensional Gaussian primitive (position, rotation, scale, opacity, SH coefficients). Camera poses (4×4 matrices) and a shared focal length are then recovered via PnP-RANSAC on the predicted Gaussian positions, enabling immediate novel view rendering without any per-scene optimization.

## Method

FreeSplatter uses a streamlined single-stream transformer (24 self-attention layers, 8×8 patch size) that jointly processes multi-view image tokens, with learnable reference/source view embeddings to designate the first image's camera frame as the unified coordinate system. Rather than using depth-based unprojection (which requires known poses), the model directly predicts full 3D Gaussian positions in the reference frame, with a dedicated pixel-alignment loss (cosine similarity between predicted Gaussian positions and camera rays) enforcing that Gaussians lie on their corresponding viewing rays. Training follows a two-stage strategy: an initial position-supervision phase (L_pos) stabilizes convergence by anchoring Gaussian locations to ground-truth depth unprojection, followed by a rendering-and-alignment phase that removes the position loss to avoid degrading quality from noisy depths. Two variants are trained—FreeSplatter-O on Objaverse for object-centric reconstruction and FreeSplatter-S on BlendedMVS/ScanNet++/CO3Dv2 for scene-level reconstruction—differing mainly in how occluded regions are handled (free placement vs. target-view masking).

## Results

On object-centric reconstruction, FreeSplatter-O achieves PSNR 30.44 on GSO and 31.93 on OmniObject3D, surpassing pose-dependent LGM (24.46/24.85) and InstantMesh (25.42/24.08) by over 5 dB despite not using ground-truth camera poses. On scene-level reconstruction, FreeSplatter-S reaches PSNR 25.81 on ScanNet++ and 20.41 on CO3Dv2, outperforming pose-dependent pixelSplat (24.97) and MVSplat (22.60) as well as pose-free Splatt3R (21.01/18.07). For camera pose estimation, FreeSplatter-O achieves RRE of 3.90° on GSO (vs. MASt3R's 59.63°), while FreeSplatter-S matches or exceeds MASt3R on translation error (TE 0.066 vs. 0.356 on ScanNet++; TE 0.190 vs. 0.299 on CO3Dv2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeSplatter-O decisively outperforms both pose-dependent LRMs (LGM and InstantMesh) on object-centric reconstruction, gaining >5 dB PSNR on GSO and >7 dB on OmniObject3D despite those methods receiving ground-truth poses. Against the pose-free baseline PF-LRM, FreeSplatter-O wins on OmniObject3D (PSNR 22.83 vs. 21.77 at GT poses; 26.49 vs. 25.86 at predicted poses) but trails on PF-LRM's GSO evaluation set, which the authors attribute to domain alignment between PF-LRM's training and evaluation data. For scene-level reconstruction, FreeSplatter-S surpasses pose-dependent pixelSplat and MVSplat on ScanNet++ PSNR (25.81 vs. 24.97 and 22.60) and significantly outperforms pose-free Splatt3R on both ScanNet++ (25.81 vs. 21.01) and CO3Dv2 (20.41 vs. 18.07). In pose estimation, FreeSplatter-O dramatically outperforms MASt3R on object-centric data (RRE 3.90° vs. 59.63° on GSO), while FreeSplatter-S matches MASt3R on rotation accuracy but achieves substantially lower translation error on ScanNet++ (0.066 vs. 0.356) and CO3Dv2 (0.190 vs. 0.299), though it lags slightly on RRE for Re10K (3.51° vs. 2.34°).

## Relation to Prior Work

FreeSplatter sits at the intersection of two research threads: pose-free reconstruction (PF-LRM, DUSt3R, MASt3R, Splatt3R) and feed-forward 3D Gaussian reconstruction (GS-LRM, LGM, GRM). It departs from PF-LRM's NeRF-based implicit representation (which suffers from inefficient volume rendering) and from DUSt3R/MASt3R's point-cloud paradigm (which is too sparse for high-fidelity rendering) by directly predicting pixel-aligned 3D Gaussian maps in a unified reference frame via a single-stream transformer. Unlike Splatt3R and InstantSplat, which bootstrap Gaussian positions from DUSt3R/MASt3R point clouds and then optimize remaining attributes, FreeSplatter learns Gaussian positions end-to-end without any external geometric prior, using a dedicated pixel-alignment loss to enforce ray consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is eliminating camera pose estimation as a prerequisite for sparse-view 3D reconstruction, which streamlines 3D content creation pipelines (text/image-to-3D) and removes a major failure point in real-world applications where SfM breaks down. The finding that a pose-free model can outperform pose-dependent LRMs by >5 dB PSNR on object-centric benchmarks challenges the prevailing assumption in the LRM literature that accurate poses are essential for high-quality reconstruction. Open questions include whether this advantage persists in more challenging uncontrolled environments, how the method scales to very large or dynamic scenes, and whether the unified-reference-frame assumption (shared focal length, single reference camera) limits generalization to highly diverse multi-view captures.
