## Problem

Existing VLM-based open vocabulary HOI detection methods rely on holistic, coarse-grained visual features from VLMs, which contradicts the fine-grained instance-level nature of detection tasks. Additionally, one-stage methods typically fuse VLM and HOI detector features only at the output stage (late fusion), lacking early interaction between the two components and making effective feature alignment difficult.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252357/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and outputs all <human, verb, object> triplets with their bounding boxes, supporting both closed-set and open-vocabulary interaction recognition. During training, the model also consumes GPT-4o-generated image captions for the LSG loss, but at inference the LLM component (OPT-2.7B) is entirely dropped, so only the BLIP-2 Vision Tower and the DETR-based HOI detector are active. The interaction classifier is constructed from BLIP-2's text encoder to support arbitrary verb-object combinations at test time.

## Method

BC-HOI establishes bilateral collaboration between a DETR-based HOI detector and BLIP-2 through three mechanisms. First, Early Fusion concatenates the CNN backbone and BLIP-2 Vision Tower feature maps along the spatial dimension before a shared transformer encoder, enabling fine-grained cross-backbone correlation. Second, Attention Bias Guidance (ABG) duplicates the ViT cls_token to match the number of HOI queries and injects the interaction decoder's cross-attention maps as additive bias into the self-attention of each duplicated token (and into the Q-Former), steering the VLM to produce instance-level interaction embeddings semantically aligned with the HOI decoder. Third, LLM-based Supervision Guidance (LSG) feeds a separate set of ABG-guided embeddings into BLIP-2's OPT-2.7B LLM to auto-regressively predict GPT-4o-generated captions, applying a weighted cross-entropy loss that up-weights noun and verb tokens; since BLIP-2 parameters are frozen except for a small set of learnable queries, gradients from this loss primarily optimize the HOI detector's attention maps, creating a feedback loop that improves ABG quality.

## Results

On HICO-DET (closed setting), BC-HOI achieves 43.01 mAP (Full), 45.76 mAP (Rare), and 42.18 mAP (Non-Rare), outperforming the previous best one-stage method UniHOI by 2.95%, 5.85%, and 2.07% respectively. On V-COCO, it reaches 68.20 mAP#1role and 70.61 mAP#2role, surpassing all prior methods. In the open vocabulary NF-UC setting on HICO-DET, BC-HOI achieves 33.01 mAP on unseen categories (vs. 28.45 for UniHOI) and 36.40 mAP overall, demonstrating strong generalization to unseen verb-object compositions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BC-HOI outperforms all 17 compared baselines across both HICO-DET and V-COCO in both closed and open vocabulary settings. The closest competitor is UniHOI [14] (NeurIPS 2023), which also uses BLIP-2; BC-HOI surpasses it by 2.95% Full mAP on HICO-DET closed, 4.56% on NF-UC unseen, and 2.62% on V-COCO mAP#1role. Among two-stage methods, BCOM [82] (CVPR 2024) is the strongest at 39.34 Full mAP, still trailing BC-HOI by 3.67%. In the open vocabulary RF-UC setting, BC-HOI leads the second-best DP-HOI [52] by 11.82% on unseen categories (42.31 vs. 30.49), a particularly large margin indicating the attention bias guidance effectively handles unseen verb-object compositions. On V-COCO, BC-HOI beats the prior best DP-HOI [52] by 1.6% mAP#1role (68.20 vs. 66.60). The method also shows the largest gains on rare HOI categories (45.76 vs. 39.91 for UniHOI), confirming that the bilateral collaboration mitigates data scarcity for underrepresented interactions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the GEN-VLKT HOI detector architecture (CNN backbone, DETR-style detection and interaction decoders) and the BLIP-2 VLM (ViT encoder, Q-Former, OPT-2.7B LLM), but departs fundamentally from the unidirectional, late-fusion paradigm used by prior VLM-based HOI methods (HOICLIP, UniHOI, MP-HOI). Where those works extract features independently from the detector and VLM and fuse them via element-wise addition at the end, BC-HOI establishes a bidirectional loop: the HOI detector's cross-attention maps guide the VLM's self-attention (ABG), and the VLM's LLM provides token-level caption supervision back to the detector (LSG). The early-fusion strategy—concatenating CNN and VLM feature maps before the shared transformer encoder—further distinguishes it from the late-fusion approach in all compared baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine tension in VLM-assisted detection: VLM visual features are trained for holistic image-caption alignment and thus lack the instance-level granularity that detection requires. The bilateral collaboration framework offers a principled way to reconcile generalizability (from the VLM) with fine-grained spatial precision (from the detector), and the token-level LSG supervision is a more targeted alternative to holistic feature distillation. Limitations include the increased GPU memory cost during training (3.8 GB vs. 12 GB) and the modest improvement on unseen-object categories (0.22%), suggesting the method's strength lies in interaction understanding rather than object recognition. An open question is whether pre-training the LSG component on large-scale image-text pairs with diverse object categories could close the unseen-object gap.
