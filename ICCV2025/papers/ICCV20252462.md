## Problem

Monocular depth estimation captures rich contextual priors but lacks geometric precision, while stereo matching achieves metric accuracy yet fails on ambiguous regions like reflective or textureless surfaces. Despite their clear complementarity, existing methods treat these paradigms as disjoint pipelines with post-hoc fusion heuristics, never unifying contextual reasoning and geometric matching during feature learning. This gap fundamentally limits robustness in unconstrained real-world scenarios where both cues are indispensable.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a calibrated stereo image pair (left + right) at arbitrary resolution; the reference image is internally resized by a factor of 14/16 to align with DINOv2's 14×14 patch grid. It produces two simultaneous outputs: a full-resolution metric disparity map from the stereo branch and a full-resolution relative depth map from the monocular branch, both decoded through a shared 3-layer MLP followed by pixel unshuffle. The dual-output design serves both practical (metric reconstruction) and diagnostic (validating cross-modal alignment) purposes.

## Method

BridgeDepth introduces a bidirectional latent alignment framework that iteratively synchronizes frozen monocular contextual features (from DAv2's DINOv2 encoder + DPT decoder) with stereo hypothesis embeddings (from a DPN-pruned cost volume retaining only K=2 disparity candidates per pixel). Each alignment step comprises two cross-attention phases: a monocular-readout phase where stereo embeddings query monocular features to inject structural priors before cost aggregation via Neural Message Passing, and a monocular-update phase where monocular features query the aggregated stereo embeddings to absorb geometrically verified constraints. A content-adaptive positional bias (learnable position table with query/key role separation) preserves pixel-wise spatial alignment throughout. A coarse-to-fine cascade upsamples stereo embeddings to H/4 resolution while halving the monocular window size, recovering thin structures without full-resolution processing. Training uses dual losses—L1 for disparity and affine-invariant loss for relative depth—applied across all iterations with exponential weighting, and the monocular branch remains frozen throughout to preserve DAv2's pretrained generalization.

## Results

On the Scene Flow test set, BridgeDepth achieves 0.37 EPE and 3.67% BP-1, outperforming the prior best (Mocha-Stereo at 0.41 EPE / 5.07% BP-1). For zero-shot generalization (trained only on Scene Flow), BridgeDepth reduces Middlebury BP-2 from 7.5 (NMRF) to 4.3 (a 42.7% improvement) and ETH3D BP-1 from 3.8 to 1.3 (a 65.8% improvement). On fine-tuned KITTI 2015, it achieves 1.40 D1-ALL, the best among all compared methods, with a runtime of 0.13 s on a GTX 3090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BridgeDepth outperforms its direct stereo baseline NMRF [13] on every benchmark: zero-shot Middlebury BP-2 drops from 7.5 to 4.3 (−42.7%) and ETH3D BP-1 from 3.8 to 1.3 (−65.8%), while fine-tuned KITTI 2015 D1-ALL improves from 1.59 to 1.40. Against the strongest concurrent hybrid methods, BridgeDepth beats IGEV++ [52] (KITTI-15 D1: 4.5 vs 5.9; ETH3D BP-1: 1.3 vs 4.1), MonSter [6] (KITTI-15 D1: 4.5 vs 4.9; ETH3D: 1.3 vs not reported but KITTI-12 D1 3.6 vs 5.66), and DEFOM-Stereo [21] (KITTI-15 D1: 4.5 vs 4.9; ETH3D: 1.3 vs not reported). On zero-shot KITTI-12, FoundationStereo [49] edges out BridgeDepth (3.2 vs 3.6 D1), but BridgeDepth wins on the other three zero-shot benchmarks (KITTI-15, Middlebury, ETH3D). Among pure stereo methods, BridgeDepth surpasses RAFT-Stereo [29], Selective-IGEV [47], Mocha-Stereo [5], and IGEV-Stereo [51] on all reported metrics, with the largest margins on Middlebury (4.3 vs 9.4/4.4/4.1/8.8 BP-2) and ETH3D (1.3 vs 3.3/3.3/4.1/4.0 BP-1). Runtime is competitive at 0.13 s (KITTI fine-tuned) and 0.044 s (ablation baseline), slightly slower than NMRF (0.09 s) but far faster than FoundationStereo and MonSter.

## Relation to Prior Work

BridgeDepth builds directly on NMRF [13] for its stereo branch (Disparity Proposal Network and Neural Message Passing) and on DepthAnythingV2 [54] as a frozen monocular feature extractor, but departs from prior hybrid methods (MaGNet, LoS) that treat monocular and stereo streams as separate pipelines with post-hoc fusion. Concurrent works (FoundationStereo, MonSter, DEFOM-Stereo) also integrate monocular priors into stereo matching, but do so through secondary cost volumes, scale-alignment, or residual refinement applied after the main stereo pipeline. BridgeDepth's key conceptual departure is performing iterative bidirectional cross-attention *during* cost aggregation, so that monocular structural priors and stereo geometric hypotheses mutually refine each other at the feature level rather than being combined at the output level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The >40% zero-shot error reduction on Middlebury and ETH3D demonstrates that coupling a frozen foundation-model monocular branch with a lightweight stereo matcher can substantially close the domain gap that has limited stereo generalization, while the 0.13 s runtime shows this comes at modest computational cost. The dual-output design (metric disparity + relative depth) is positioned as a generalizable template for any task requiring coupled geometric and contextual reasoning (e.g., optical flow), though the paper does not yet validate this beyond depth. A noted limitation is potential accuracy degradation at ≥2K resolutions where large disparity ranges stress the top-k hypothesis pruning in the DPN, and the zero-shot evaluations are conducted on training sets of real-world benchmarks rather than fully held-out test splits, which slightly tempers the generalization claims.
