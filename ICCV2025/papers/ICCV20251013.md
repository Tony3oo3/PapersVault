## Problem

Existing goal-oriented VLN agents make single-step decisions based solely on historical observations from visited areas, ignoring the long-term consequences of each action. While world-model-based approaches can anticipate multi-step futures, they require expensive multi-round rollouts prone to compounding errors, and neighborhood-synthesis methods only capture immediate local hints. The paper seeks an efficient mechanism to predict long-horizon, semantic-level future information in a single forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251013/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each step the agent receives 36 discrete panoramic views at the current node, a natural-language instruction describing the target object, and a maintained graph of visited and candidate nodes. The Q-model operates in a text-feature space (CLIP embeddings of scene captions) rather than raw RGB, producing one Q-feature vector per candidate action that encodes the discounted aggregation of all reachable future node descriptions. The final output combines history-based scores (from the global encoder) with future-sensitive scores (from the future encoder) to select the next navigation node, alongside an object grounding prediction at the current location.

## Method

NavQ introduces a Q-model (4-layer Transformer) trained self-supervised on randomly sampled trajectories without instruction annotation, where the Q-feature for a state-action pair is defined as a γ-discounted sum of text-based node features over all reachable future nodes under a shortest-path-biased rollout policy. This policy ensures each future node's feature is accumulated into exactly one candidate action's Q-feature, preserving discriminability across actions. A cross-modal future encoder (Graph Transformer) then interacts the task-agnostic Q-features with the instruction to produce goal-oriented future scores, which are fused with the baseline DUET's history-based scores in an A*-inspired manner. Training proceeds in three stages: MAE warm-up followed by Q-feature regression on random trajectories, agent pre-training with DUET's auxiliary tasks plus progress-estimation subtasks that decouple the history and future branches, and DAgger fine-tuning on online data.

## Results

On REVERIE Val Unseen, NavQ achieves 53.22% SR, 38.89% SPL, 36.84% RGS, and 27.12% RGSPL, improving over the DUET baseline by +6.24, +5.16, +4.69, and +4.09 points respectively. On the Test Unseen split, NavQ reaches 53.29% SR and 39.50% SPL. On the more challenging SOON benchmark (Val Unseen), NavQ attains 39.09% SR and 26.65% SPL, outperforming the DUET baseline by +2.81 and +4.07 points. The pre-trained Q-model is shared across both REVERIE and SOON, demonstrating task-agnostic generalizability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NavQ consistently outperforms the DUET baseline across all five metrics on both REVERIE and SOON, with the largest gains on RGSPL (+4.09 on REVERIE Val, +1.76 on SOON Val), indicating that foresighted Q-features most improve navigation efficiency. Against the strongest recent methods, NavQ surpasses GOAT [117] by +2.19 RGSPL and +2.20 SPL on REVERIE Val, and exceeds VER [80] by +3.42 RGS and +3.42 RGSPL, though VER retains a higher SR (55.98 vs. 53.22) due to its volumetric representation. On SOON, NavQ leads all baselines on every metric, achieving the best OSR (58.79), SR (39.09), SPL (26.65), and RGSPL (5.51) on Val Unseen. When additional unlabeled scenes from HM3D and Gibson are used for Q-model pre-training (without any speaker annotation), NavQ further improves to 54.10% SR and 39.22% SPL on REVERIE Val, matching or exceeding methods that rely on additional annotated trajectories such as AutoVLN [15] and ScaleVLN [123]. The shared Q-model across REVERIE and SOON confirms that the learned future semantics are task-agnostic and transferable.

## Relation to Prior Work

NavQ builds directly on DUET's dual-scale graph transformer architecture, adding a parallel future-prediction branch (Q-model + future encoder) alongside the existing history-based global encoder. Conceptually, it adapts the Q-learning Bellman equation from classical RL but replaces the scalar Q-value with a Q-feature in latent text space, enabling unsupervised pre-training on unlabeled trajectories. This departs from prior future-information approaches in VLN that either synthesize single-step neighborhood observations (dVAE, NeRF-based) or require expensive multi-step world-model rollouts, by predicting aggregated long-horizon semantics in a single forward pass. The A*-inspired fusion of historical cost and future heuristic also echoes the Q* algorithm for LLM planning, but is grounded in embodied navigation rather than symbolic reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a training paradigm that decouples the Q-model from instruction annotations, allowing it to be pre-trained on large-scale unlabeled scenes (1,351 scenes from HM3D/Gibson) and shared across different VLN benchmarks (REVERIE and SOON) without retraining. This addresses a well-known data-scarcity bottleneck in VLN. However, the method remains confined to discrete graph-based navigation (MP3D), and the authors note that extending to continuous environments is future work. The frozen Q-model design also limits adaptability: the model cannot update its future predictions based on task-specific feedback during navigation, which may cap performance in highly novel or dynamic scenes.
