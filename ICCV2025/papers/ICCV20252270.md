## Problem

Radar-camera fusion 3D object detection still lags behind LiDAR-based detectors, and existing knowledge distillation and temporal fusion approaches fail to account for sensor-specific uncertainties (camera depth ambiguity, radar angular imprecision) and temporal misalignment caused by dynamic object motion. Prior KD methods apply uniform circular Gaussian masks that cannot capture the anisotropic error distributions inherent to radar-camera fusion, and temporal fusion methods struggle with error propagation from independently moving objects without incurring prohibitive latency.

## Input / Output

The student model consumes 9 frames (8 historical + 1 current) of radar point clouds and multi-view camera images (6 cameras at 256×704 or 512×1408 resolution on nuScenes; single forward stereo camera on VoD), while the frozen LiDAR teacher (CenterPoint with SECOND backbone) processes single-frame point clouds. The student produces 3D bounding boxes with class labels and heading angles via a detection head on high-level BEV features. The teacher model is used exclusively during training and is entirely omitted at inference, preserving the student's 26.2 FPS real-time speed.

## Method

RCTDistill distills knowledge from a LiDAR teacher (CenterPoint) to a temporal radar-camera student (BEVFusion-R with adaptive gating and streaming temporal fusion) through three complementary modules. RAKD replaces circular Gaussian distillation masks with elliptical ones whose axes are scaled by object size and ego-vehicle distance (following DORN), explicitly modeling the anisotropic range-azimuth uncertainty of radar-camera fusion. TKD processes historical BEV features through an HA-Net (large-kernel 2D CNN) to suppress inter-frame variation, then applies trajectory-aware elliptical masks whose centers are shifted opposite to object velocity and whose major axes are elongated to cover the motion path, enabling targeted temporal alignment of dynamic objects. RDKD computes cosine-similarity affinity maps over K foreground positions (selected by classification confidence) in both teacher and student high-level features, and minimizes the L1 difference between these maps to transfer discriminative foreground-background relational structure. The total loss combines the standard 3D detection loss with weighted RAKD, TKD, and RDKD terms, trained for 60 epochs with frozen teacher weights.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set with ResNet-50, RCTDistill achieves 62.2% NDS and 55.2% mAP at 26.2 FPS, surpassing the previous best radar-camera method SpaRC (62.0% NDS, 54.5% mAP) while running 37% faster. On the nuScenes test set, RCTDistill reaches 67.4% NDS and 60.3% mAP, outperforming SpaRC (67.1% NDS, 60.0% mAP) and CRT-Fusion (64.9% NDS, 58.3% mAP). On the View-of-Delft dataset, RCTDistill achieves 62.37% EAA AP and 82.25% RoI AP, exceeding the prior best SGDet3D by 2.62% and 4.83% respectively. The method also improves over its own baseline (without KD) by 4.9% NDS and 4.7% mAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RCTDistill outperforms all radar-camera fusion baselines on nuScenes validation: it beats SpaRC (the closest competitor) by 0.2% NDS and 0.7% mAP while running 37% faster (26.2 vs 19.1 FPS), and surpasses CRT-Fusion by 2.5% NDS and 4.4% mAP. Against KD-based methods, RCTDistill improves over CRKD by 7.3% NDS and 12.0% mAP, and over X3KD by 8.4% NDS and 12.9% mAP, demonstrating the benefit of temporal fusion combined with sensor-aware distillation. On the nuScenes test set, RCTDistill edges out SpaRC (67.4 vs 67.1 NDS) and leads CRT-Fusion by 2.5% NDS. On View-of-Delft, RCTDistill achieves 62.37% EAA AP and 82.25% RoI AP, outperforming SGDet3D (59.75/77.42) and HGSFusion (58.96/79.46). The only method that exceeds RCTDistill is the LiDAR-based teacher CenterPoint (67.1% NDS), which is expected given the higher sensor fidelity; RCTDistill closes this gap to within 4.9% NDS using only radar and camera inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

RCTDistill sits at the intersection of two active research threads: cross-modal knowledge distillation for 3D detection (BEVDistill, CRKD, X3KD) and temporal fusion for radar-camera perception (CRN, CRT-Fusion, SOLOFusion). It builds directly on BEVFusion as the student architecture, adopts CRKD's adaptive gating for modality fusion, and follows SOLOFusion's streaming temporal mechanism. The key departure is that prior KD methods operated on single-frame setups, while RCTDistill is the first to integrate KD with temporal radar-camera fusion, introducing sensor-specific elliptical Gaussian masks (RAKD), velocity-aware temporal alignment via HA-Net (TKD), and region-decoupled relational distillation (RDKD) as a unified framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in closing the performance gap between radar-camera and LiDAR-based detectors while maintaining real-time inference (26.2 FPS), which is critical for cost-sensitive autonomous driving deployments. The ablation confirms that spatial, temporal, and relational distillation are complementary rather than redundant, suggesting the framework can be extended to other sensor pairs or longer temporal windows. Open questions include whether the elliptical mask design generalizes to 4D radar or other sparse sensors, whether HA-Net's fixed-kernel temporal alignment scales beyond 8 historical frames, and whether the approach transfers to datasets with different sensor geometries or object densities.
