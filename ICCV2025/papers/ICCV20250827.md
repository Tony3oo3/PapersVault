## Problem

Existing data-free quantization methods for Vision Transformers produce synthetic images that suffer from semantic distortion (features deviate substantially from real-image semantics) and semantic inadequacy (large dull regions with oversimplified textures). These issues are especially detrimental under low-bit quantization, where severely reduced model capacity demands highly informative training images to recover performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250827/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained full-precision ViT/DeiT/Swin model and initializes 32 synthetic images from standard Gaussian noise at the model's input resolution. During synthesis, the pre-trained model's attention maps and classification logits are used as supervision signals; during the quantized learning stage, the generated images are fed through both the full-precision and quantized networks block-by-block. The final output is a low-bit (W4A4 through W6A6) quantized ViT model fine-tuned exclusively on the 32 synthetic images.

## Method

SARDFQ introduces three complementary mechanisms. Attention Priors Alignment (APA) generates structured attention priors via Gaussian Mixture Models (1–5 2D Gaussians per head) and applies a depth-weighted MSE loss between the classification-token attention of synthetic images and these priors in deeper blocks, steering attention toward semantically discriminative regions. Multi-Semantic Reinforcement (MSR) randomly crops m (1–4) non-overlapping patches from each synthetic image and backpropagates gradients only to the corresponding patch regions, forcing each sub-region to capture distinct semantics rather than homogeneous dull content. Soft-Label Learning (SL) replaces the one-hot cross-entropy with a soft cross-entropy against a multi-target soft label (constructed by sampling uniform values for all m patch classes plus the global class), providing consistent supervision for the multi-semantic images produced by MSR. The synthesis objective combines APA, SL, and total-variation losses; the quantized model is then fine-tuned block-wisely with a reconstruction loss matching full-precision block outputs.

## Results

On ImageNet, SARDFQ achieves consistent top-1 accuracy improvements across all ViT, DeiT, and Swin models at W4A4, W5A5, and W6A6 bit-widths. The most striking gain is 15.52% on ViT-B W4A4 (51.84% vs. 36.32% for PSAQ-ViT), while improvements range from 1.20% to 4.58% across other models at W4A4. At higher bit-widths (W5A5, W6A6), gains narrow to 0.82%–2.72%, confirming that semantic alignment matters most when model capacity is severely constrained. SARDFQ also outperforms the real-data upper bound in several W6A6 settings (e.g., DeiT-B: 80.15% vs. 80.90% real, Swin-B: 83.03% vs. 82.64% real).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SARDFQ outperforms all three baselines (PSAQ-ViT, PSAQ-ViT V2, and SMI) across every model and bit-width configuration tested. Against PSAQ-ViT [47], the largest gap is 15.52% on ViT-B W4A4 (51.84% vs. 36.32%), with typical W4A4 gains of 3–5% on other models; at W6A6 the gap narrows to 1.2–2.5%. Against PSAQ-ViT V2 [48], which adds adversarial training, SARDFQ still leads by 4.58% on Swin-B W4A4 and 3.08% on ViT-S W4A4, with smaller margins (0.8–2.3%) at W5A5/W6A6. Against SMI [33], which uses sparse generation to remove hallucinated backgrounds, SARDFQ wins by 25.99% on ViT-B W4A4 (51.84% vs. 25.85% dense) and by 1.55–4.58% on Swin models at W4A4; at W6A6 the gap is minimal (0.82–1.46%), suggesting SMI's sparse strategy is more competitive when quantization pressure is lower. Notably, SARDFQ approaches or even slightly exceeds the real-data upper bound at W6A6 for DeiT-B (80.15% vs. 80.90%) and Swin-B (83.03% vs. 82.64%), indicating that well-aligned synthetic data can be as informative as real data under mild quantization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data-free quantization (DFQ) line for Vision Transformers initiated by PSAQ-ViT (Li et al., ECCV 2022), which first addressed the absence of batch normalization statistics in ViTs by introducing patch similarity entropy loss, and its successor PSAQ-ViT V2 (Li et al., TNNLS 2023) which added adversarial learning. SARDFQ departs from these methods by diagnosing two specific failure modes—semantic distortion (synthetic features diverging from real-image feature distributions) and semantic inadequacy (large dull, low-content regions)—and addressing them through attention-prior alignment via GMM-generated structural priors and localized multi-patch optimization, respectively. The quantized network learning stage follows the block-wise reconstruction strategy from BRECQ (Li et al., ICLR 2021), shared with all compared baselines for fair evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 15.52% top-1 accuracy gain on W4A4 ViT-B (36.32% → 51.84%) is substantial and consistent across ViT, DeiT, and Swin architectures, demonstrating that attention-structure alignment and local semantic reinforcement are effective levers for low-bit DFQ where model capacity is most damaged. However, a significant gap to real-data quantization remains (e.g., 51.84% vs. 68.16% for W4A4 ViT-B), and the authors acknowledge the lack of a theoretical foundation for why GMM-generated attention priors and localized patch optimization should work. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-classification tasks (detection, segmentation results are deferred to appendix), whether the heuristic hyperparameter choices (α₁, K_APA, K_MSR) transfer across model scales without re-tuning, and whether a principled theory can explain the interaction between attention alignment and quantization error recovery.
