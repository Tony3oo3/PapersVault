## Problem

While MLLMs achieve strong performance on multimodal tasks, the internal representation shifts that occur during fine-tuning remain largely unexplored. Existing interpretability work typically examines models in isolation at their final trained state, overlooking how latent concepts are reshaped, refined, or replaced by fine-tuning. This gap limits both our understanding of what fine-tuning actually changes and our ability to cheaply control model behavior at inference time.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes RGB images and text prompts fed through an MLLM (e.g., LLaVA with CLIP encoder + Vicuna LLM), extracting residual stream activations at a chosen layer for a specified token of interest. It requires access to both the original and fine-tuned model weights to compute per-concept shift vectors, but once computed, steering only requires the original model plus the precomputed vectors. Outputs include a K-concept dictionary with dual image/text groundings, per-concept shift vectors, and steered model outputs (answers, captions, or safety-refusal responses) produced without any weight modification.

## Method

The method applies K-Means dictionary learning to residual stream representations to extract K interpretable concepts, grounding each in image space (top-activating samples) and text space (top vocabulary logits via the unembedding matrix). Concept shift vectors are computed as the mean representation difference between original and fine-tuned models over samples where each original concept is most activated, enabling recovery of fine-tuned concepts via a simple additive shift (u_k^s = u_k^a + α·Δ_k) without accessing the fine-tuned model at inference. For steering, two granularities are defined: coarse-grained vectors (mean difference between target and source representation sets) applied globally to all samples, and fine-grained vectors (u_j − u_i) that redirect specific concepts toward target concepts. Both are injected additively into the residual stream at a chosen layer (typically the last), requiring no retraining and only negligible per-token computation overhead.

## Results

For fine-grained VQA steering on VQAv2, the method converts 828 'Yes' answers to 'No' (Yes→No), 144 '1' answers to '3' (1→3), and 441 'White' answers to 'Black' (White→Black), while accuracy on non-targeted answer types drops by less than 3 percentage points. For gender debiasing on COCO, fine-grained steering converts 632/794 gendered terms to neutral on LLaVA-1.5, 315/815 on Idefics2, and 300/926 on Qwen2-VL-Instruct, substantially outperforming coarse steering (23, 23, and 13 respectively). For safety alignment, steering reduces the attack success rate (ASR) on MM-SafetyBench from 45/100 to 5/100 on Qwen2-VL-Instruct. For concept recovery, shifted original concepts achieve higher text-grounding overlap with fine-tuned concepts than the unshifted originals across all three fine-tuning tasks (places, colors, sentiments).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a methodology paper that introduces a training-free steering framework rather than competing against external baselines; the primary comparison is between the unsteered model and the steered model, as well as between coarse-grained and fine-grained steering strategies. Fine-grained steering consistently outperforms coarse steering for targeted interventions: in gender debiasing, fine-grained steering converts 27× more gendered terms than coarse on LLaVA-1.5 (632 vs. 23) and 23× more on Qwen2-VL-Instruct (300 vs. 13). For VQA answer steering, the unsteered baseline produces 0 target answers, while fine-grained steering produces 828 (Yes→No), 144 (1→3), or 441 (White→Black) target answers with minimal collateral damage to other answer-type accuracies (≤3 pp drop). For concept recovery, the shifted-concept approach improves text-grounding overlap with fine-tuned concepts over the unshifted original-concept baseline across all fine-tuning tasks, with the improvement correlating positively with the consistency of individual representation shifts. The paper does not compare against other steering methods (e.g., ReFT, ICL-based approaches) in a head-to-head quantitative evaluation, leaving that as future work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the concept-based explainability framework of CoX-LMM [47] (same group) from post-hoc analysis of a single model state to tracking how latent concepts evolve during fine-tuning, introducing concept shift vectors to quantify and recover those changes. It simultaneously extends the activation-addition steering paradigm—previously applied to text-only LLMs for factuality, sentiment, and refusal control [62, 66]—to the multimodal setting, where no prior steering work existed. The coarse-grained steering vector (difference of mean representations) follows the latent steering vector extraction approach of Subramani et al. [62], while the fine-grained per-concept steering is a novel combination of dictionary-learned concept directions with additive intervention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a training-free, computationally cheap mechanism for both interpreting and controlling MLLM behavior, with immediate practical applications in gender debiasing, safety alignment, and caption style control that avoid the cost of retraining. A key open question is the reliance on linear separability of concepts in latent space: the authors acknowledge (citing [14]) that not all features are linearly represented, which limits the generality of the shift-vector recovery. Whether the approach scales to more complex fine-tuning regimes (e.g., multi-task, multi-modal shifts) or to models where concept geometry is more entangled remains to be explored.
