## Problem

Video diffusion models can generate realistic videos but lack the spatial and temporal controllability needed to reconstruct coherent 3D/4D scenes from a single image. Existing approaches either operate at the object level with mesh renderings or rely on time-intensive per-scene optimization, leaving the generation of photorealistic, scene-level 3D and 4D content from a single image an open challenge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252412/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (or two sparse-view images for the sparse-view 3D setting) and produces a 3D scene as a Gaussian splatting representation or a 4D dynamic scene as a deformable Gaussian splatting. Intermediate outputs include spatial-variant videos (camera moves, objects static), temporal-variant videos (objects move, camera fixed), and combined spatial-temporal videos. The base video diffusion model is extended from 49 to 145 frames via RoPE positional embedding modification to cover the multi-view coverage needed for full scene reconstruction.

## Method

The core contribution is ST-Director, which decouples spatial and temporal priors in a DiT-based video diffusion model (CogVideoX) by training two orthogonal LoRA modules: S-Director on spatial-variant data (camera motion through static scenes) and T-Director on temporal-variant data (object motion under fixed camera). A training-free composition strategy called Switch-Once exploits the observation that spatial structure forms earlier than temporal dynamics during denoising, starting with S-Director for the first few steps then switching to T-Director to inject motion. For 3D generation, a trajectory-aware mechanism with early-stopped S-Director training and adaptive trajectory planning handles diverse camera poses; for 4D generation, an identity-preserving denoising strategy shares reference video latents across frames and applies SDEdit-style appearance refinement to enforce multi-view consistency before deformable 3DGS reconstruction.

## Results

On single-view 3D scene generation, DimensionX achieves PSNR of 17.11 (Tank and Temples), 18.91 (MipNeRF360), 20.38 (LLFF), and 18.28 (DL3DV), outperforming ViewCrafter by 1.9–3.6 dB PSNR across all four benchmarks. In the sparse-view (two-view) setting, the gains are even larger: 20.42 PSNR on Tank and Temples vs. 18.76 for ViewCrafter and 18.70 for InstantSplat, with LPIPS dropping to 0.067 on LLFF (vs. 0.149 for InstantSplat). For decoupled video generation, DimensionX scores 97.69 Subject Consistency and 47.06 Dynamic Degree on VBench, surpassing both CogVideoX (93.56 / 11.76) and Dream Machine 1.6 (93.69 / 38.24).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DimensionX outperforms all baselines across every dataset and metric in both single-view and sparse-view 3D generation. In the single-view setting, it beats ViewCrafter [69] by 1.9–3.6 dB PSNR (e.g., 20.38 vs. 17.56 on LLFF) and ZeroNVS [40] by 4.8–5.9 dB PSNR, with LPIPS roughly halved compared to ViewCrafter (0.199 vs. 0.319 on Tank and Temples). In the sparse-view setting, DimensionX surpasses the strongest baseline ViewCrafter by 1.7–3.5 dB PSNR and InstantSplat [13] by 1.7–3.4 dB, with the largest margin on LLFF (25.11 vs. 22.33 PSNR, 0.067 vs. 0.149 LPIPS). DNGaussian [23] is consistently the weakest baseline, trailing DimensionX by 8+ dB PSNR on most datasets. For video generation (Table 2), DimensionX leads CogVideoX [65] by 4.1 points in Subject Consistency and 35.3 points in Dynamic Degree, and also surpasses the closed-source Dream Machine 1.6 on all three VBench metrics. For 4D generation (Table 4), DimensionX with both reference video sharing and appearance refinement achieves 834.3 Mat. Pix.(K) view synchronization, more than 2.5× the 294.3 of SV4D [62] and 2.6× the 323.0 of multi-view images + CogVideoX.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on CogVideoX (a DiT-based video diffusion model) as its base architecture and on 3D/4D Gaussian Splatting for scene reconstruction, but its core conceptual departure is the decoupling of spatial and temporal priors within video diffusion via dimension-aware LoRAs (ST-Director). Prior approaches to 3D/4D generation from video diffusion either operate at the object level (SV3D, Diffusion4D), rely on time-intensive per-scene optimization (4Real), or inject explicit control signals like camera poses and Plücker coordinates. DimensionX instead learns orthogonal spatial and temporal 'directors' on curated dimension-variant datasets and composes them through a training-free Switch-Once strategy, positioning itself as a scalable, generalizable alternative to both signal-injection and per-scene-optimization paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that video diffusion models can serve as a unified backbone for both 3D and 4D scene generation from a single image without per-scene optimization, which is practically significant given the scarcity of large-scale 3D/4D training data. The empirical observation that spatial structure is resolved before temporal dynamics during denoising provides a principled basis for the Switch-Once composition and could inform future controllable video generation systems. However, the 4D baselines are relatively weak (SV4D, multi-view images + CogVideoX), the method still requires a separate 3DGS/4DGS reconstruction step, and the authors acknowledge limitations in fine detail and computational efficiency. A key open question is whether the decoupled video diffusion prior can be integrated end-to-end with 3D/4D representation learning, eliminating the current two-stage pipeline.
