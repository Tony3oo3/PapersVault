## Problem

Public histopathology datasets contain either image-text pairs or image-mask pairs, but no dataset provides both text and mask annotations for the same images. Existing diffusion-based synthesis methods condition on a single modality (text or mask), limiting simultaneous control over high-level semantics and fine-grained spatial structure. This paper addresses the gap by learning a unified generative model from unpaired text and mask data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252671/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text prompt (embedded via PLIP pathology CLIP), a cell-type segmentation mask (embedded via a shallow CNN), or both simultaneously, and produces 256×256 H&E-stained histopathology image patches. At inference, either condition can be set to a null token (empty string for text, invalid-label mask for spatial), enabling flexible generation from text-only, mask-only, or joint conditioning. A shared VAE (from PathLDM, trained on TCGA-BRCA) encodes and decodes all images into a common latent space.

## Method

PathDiff jointly trains a single latent diffusion model on two independent datasets—image-text pairs (PathCap) and image-mask pairs (PanNuke, CoNIC, MoNuSAC)—by alternating samples with probability p_split and setting the absent modality to a null token. Following ControlNet, parts of the U-Net's downsampling and middle layers are duplicated with zero-convolution layers to inject mask conditioning, while text embeddings from PLIP are cross-attended into U-Net layers. The key insight is that training with complementary null tokens forces the model to learn a shared latent space where both conditions are independently informative, so at inference classifier-free guidance can combine both text and mask signals even though they were never observed together during training. Only the copied U-Net encoder and the shallow mask embedder are trained; the VAE, text encoder, and original U-Net remain frozen.

## Results

On Mask-to-Image generation, PathDiff achieves the best FID and KID across all three datasets: PanNuke (FID 7.21, KID 0.0415), CoNIC (FID 5.54, KID 0.0488), and MoNuSAC (FID 6.99, KID 0.0758) on test splits. For downstream nuclei segmentation with synthetic data augmentation (Real+Synth), PathDiff yields the highest Dice scores: 0.8164 on PanNuke, 0.8356 on CoNIC, and 0.7221 on MoNuSAC, outperforming the real-data-only baselines by 3.3%, 4.3%, and 1.9% respectively. On Text-to-Image alignment, PathDiff attains a PLIP cosine similarity of 24.66 (vs. 21.76–23.27 for ControlNet) and FID as low as 13.72 on CoNIC test, compared to ControlNet's 28.66.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PathDiff is compared against three baselines: Diffmix [32] (mask-only diffusion for imbalanced nuclei data), SDM [44] (semantic diffusion model), and ControlNet [47] (spatial control added to a text-to-image diffusion backbone). PathDiff outperforms all three on every reported metric. Against Diffmix, PathDiff reduces test FID from 8.32→7.21 (PanNuke), 8.58→5.54 (CoNIC), and 16.78→6.99 (MoNuSAC), with the largest gain on MoNuSAC where Diffmix struggles most. Against SDM, PathDiff improves FID by 1.6–12.8 points and KID by 0.03–0.27 across datasets, and also achieves higher faithfulness scores (FS1 0.7437 vs. 0.7025 on PanNuke). Against ControlNet, PathDiff shows the most dramatic advantage on PanNuke (FID 7.21 vs. 15.36; KID 0.0415 vs. 0.7722) and MoNuSAC (FID 6.99 vs. 20.56), while on CoNIC the gap is smaller (FID 5.54 vs. 6.79). For downstream segmentation, PathDiff-augmented training beats ControlNet-augmented training by 0.7% Dice on PanNuke (0.8164 vs. 0.8093), 2.8% on CoNIC (0.8356 vs. 0.8076), and 2.8% on MoNuSAC (0.7221 vs. 0.6940), and also surpasses the real-data-only baseline on all three datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

PathDiff builds directly on the ControlNet architecture (Zhang et al., 2023) for spatial mask conditioning via duplicated U-Net encoder layers with zero convolutions, and on Latent Diffusion Models (Rombach et al., 2021) for efficient latent-space denoising with classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022). The key conceptual departure from prior histopathology synthesis work—where text-conditioned methods (PathLDM, Graikos et al.) and mask-conditioned methods (Diffmix, SDM) operate in isolation—is the joint training strategy that alternates between unpaired text-image and mask-image datasets using a probabilistic switch, allowing a single model to learn both conditioning modalities without requiring paired triplets. This positions PathDiff as a unification of two previously separate lines of conditional generation research in computational pathology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in leveraging existing, separately-annotated datasets (PathCap for text, PanNuke/CoNIC/MoNuSAC for masks) without the prohibitively expensive creation of paired text+mask+image annotations, directly addressing the data scarcity bottleneck in histopathology. The consistent downstream improvements in nuclei segmentation and classification (e.g., Dice 0.8164 on PanNuke vs. 0.7834 baseline) demonstrate that the synthetic data is diagnostically meaningful, not merely visually plausible. Open questions include whether the shared VAE assumption holds across more diverse staining protocols or organ types, how the approach scales to whole-slide image generation, and whether the silver-standard mask evaluation protocol (using DeepCMorph) adequately captures the quality of unified text+mask conditioning in the absence of gold-standard triplets.
