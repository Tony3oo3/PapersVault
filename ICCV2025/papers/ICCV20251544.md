## Problem

Current diffusion-based dataset distillation methods focus exclusively on enhancing image fidelity (via diffusion training or latent clustering) while overlooking the downstream classification evaluation objective. This leads to two critical inconsistencies: Objective Inconsistency, where generated images may be misclassified relative to their conditioned labels, and Condition Inconsistency, where the conditional likelihood of each generated sample is not maximized due to imperfect diffusion model training. These gaps result in suboptimal distilled datasets that fail to capture the discriminative properties needed for accurate classification.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained conditional diffusion model (DiT at 256×256 resolution) and a lightweight pre-trained classifier (ResNet-18) as fixed components, with class labels as the conditioning signal. It produces a compact distilled dataset of synthetic RGB images paired with hard labels at user-specified IPC (1, 10, or 50), where each image is a latent-space representation refined through optimization. The output dataset is model-agnostic and can be evaluated with any architecture (ResNet-18/50/101, EfficientNet-B0, MobileNet-V2) without re-distillation.

## Method

CaO₂ is a two-stage framework that aligns distillation with the evaluation objective without retraining the generative backbone. Stage 1 (Objective-guided Sample Selection) generates an m×IPC image pool per class via the diffusion model, then uses a lightweight classifier to retain only correctly-classified samples, selecting the most confident (low IPC) or least confident (high IPC) among them to match task difficulty. Stage 2 (Condition-aware Latent Optimization) perturbs each selected latent with Gaussian noise and iteratively minimizes the diffusion loss with respect to the latent while keeping the diffusion model frozen, regularized by an L∞ norm constraint to prevent drift into other-class regions. The optimization condition is task-adaptive: true class labels guide easy tasks, while unconditional (classifier-free guidance) labels are used for harder tasks, effectively embedding conditional information into the latent itself. The framework extends to masked autoregressive (MAR) models by replacing noise perturbation with random token masking.

## Results

On ImageWoof (ResNet-18, IPC=10), CaO₂ achieves 45.6% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the best baseline Minimax Diffusion (40.1%) by 5.5% and SRe²L (20.2%) by 25.4%. On ImageNette (ResNet-18, IPC=10), CaO₂ reaches 65.0%, outperforming Minimax (61.4%) and RDED (61.4%) by 3.6%. Scaling to ImageNet-1K (ResNet-18, IPC=10), CaO₂ achieves 46.1%, beating Minimax (44.3%), RDED (42.0%), and D⁴M (27.9%). Across all datasets and IPC settings, CaO₂ surpasses the best-performing baseline by an average of 2.3% accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CaO₂ consistently outperforms all four baselines across every dataset, IPC, and architecture setting. Against SRe²L [41], the uni-level matching method, CaO₂ wins by large margins (e.g., 45.6% vs. 20.2% on ImageWoof IPC=10 with ResNet-18), confirming that diffusion-based generation fundamentally surpasses gradient-matching approaches at scale. Against Minimax Diffusion [9], the closest diffusion-based competitor, CaO₂ gains 1.6–5.5% on ImageWoof and 2.0–4.3% on ImageNette, with the gap widening at lower IPC where sample quality matters most. Against RDED [34], which selects real image crops rather than generating synthetic ones, CaO₂ achieves comparable or slightly better results at IPC=50 (e.g., 68.9% vs. 68.5% on ImageWoof) but pulls ahead more clearly at IPC=10 and IPC=1. Against D⁴M [33], evaluated only on ImageNet-1K, CaO₂ leads by a substantial margin (46.1% vs. 27.9% at IPC=10 with ResNet-18), suggesting that prototype-based clustering in latent space is less effective than the proposed two-stage selection-and-optimization pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a corrective to the diffusion-based dataset distillation paradigm introduced by Minimax Diffusion and D4M, which repurpose pre-trained diffusion models (DiT, text-to-image models) as distribution learners for generating compact surrogate datasets. Rather than proposing a new generative architecture, CaO2 diagnoses two specific misalignments—Objective Inconsistency (distillation ignores the classification evaluation objective) and Condition Inconsistency (generated latents do not maximize conditional likelihood under their assigned labels)—and addresses them with a post-hoc classifier-based sample filter and a latent-space optimization step that minimizes the diffusion ELBO with respect to the image rather than the model. The method also extends the latent optimization idea to masked autoregressive (MAR) generation, showing the framework is backbone-agnostic, and can be plugged into fine-tuned diffusion models like Minimax Diffusion without architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By explicitly aligning the distillation pipeline with the downstream classification objective, CaO2 offers a practical, plug-and-play recipe that improves upon strong diffusion-based baselines across ImageNet and its subsets, and the two-stage design (select-then-refine) is simple enough to adopt alongside existing methods. However, the selection protocol remains heuristic (top-k confident/least-confident correct samples), the latent optimization is limited to a small number of denoising steps and a fixed noise realization, and the paper does not explore whether the gains persist under extreme compression (IPC=1 on ImageNet-1K yields only ~7% accuracy). Open questions include whether more principled selection criteria (e.g., influence functions or diversity-aware sampling) could replace the ad-hoc confidence-based filter, and whether the latent optimization could be extended to multi-step or adversarial settings for stronger robustness.
