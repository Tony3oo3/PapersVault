## Problem

Existing privacy-utility trade-off methods for visual data require white-box access to the utility model (e.g., retraining an action recognition network on anonymized inputs), which is infeasible when the utility model is a proprietary cloud LMM like GPT-4V whose parameters and internals are inaccessible. This paper is the first to tackle visual privacy protection under the constraint that the LMM is a frozen black box providing only final label outputs, creating a hybrid black-box utility / white-box privacy optimization problem that prior adversarial-learning approaches cannot handle.

## Input / Output

The anonymization model (a U-Net) takes raw RGB images or video frames as input and produces pixel-level anonymized outputs that remove identifiable attributes (faces, skin color, gender cues, landmarks) while preserving semantic content sufficient for the frozen black-box LMM to perform its task. The LMM (Video-LLaVA, LLaVA, or GPT-4V) is queried only for its final prediction, and a separate ResNet-50 privacy evaluation model scores the anonymized output for residual privacy leakage via class-wise mAP. A utility budget τ=0.9 (i.e., ≥90% of the LMM's raw-data accuracy) defines the binary 'satisfactory' threshold that constrains all optimization.

## Method

The PABP framework reframes the black-box utility constraint as a binary 'satisfactory vs. unsatisfactory' classification and exploits the decision boundary between these two states in the anonymization model's parameter space as an optimization guide. The Gradually-Expanding-Probing (GEP) scheme navigates the model from an initial 'unsatisfactory' region by uniformly sampling points on a sphere centered at the current parameters, querying the black-box LMM at each point, and expanding the sphere radius exponentially (2^m·d) until a 'satisfactory' point is found, after which the model jumps to that point. The Prior-Guided-Probing (PGP) scheme then handles subsequent privacy-gradient steps that would cross the boundary: it uses a second-order Taylor expansion of the privacy loss (with the Hessian diagonal approximated by the Fisher Information Matrix) to analytically derive the contour line of constant privacy loss passing through the would-be gradient-descent point, samples candidate updates along this contour, and selects the one that the black-box LMM confirms as still 'satisfactory', thereby achieving equivalent privacy improvement without degrading utility.

## Results

On the PPAR benchmarks with Video-LLaVA as the black-box LMM, PABP achieves 46.7% top-1 action accuracy on HMDB51-VISPR and 47.9% on UCF101-VISPR, while reducing privacy cMAP to 59.7 and 54.3 respectively (raw data: 50.3/54.4% accuracy, 70.1/64.4 cMAP). With GPT-4V, the method reaches 55.1% and 65.2% action accuracy with 59.4 and 54.9 cMAP. On VQA (OK-VQA) with GPT-4V, PABP attains 58.1% VQA accuracy while dropping privacy cMAP to 43.6 (OK-VQA) and 48.2 (VISPR), compared to 63.6% accuracy and 59.3/64.4 cMAP for raw data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PABP outperforms all six baselines on both utility and privacy across every benchmark. On HMDB51-VISPR with Video-LLaVA, PABP achieves 46.7% action accuracy, surpassing the strongest baseline SelectivePrivacy [25] (35.2%) by 11.5 points and the surrogate-based adversarial methods (SPAct 32.4%, VITA 31.6%, BDQ 33.1%, MPPAR 32.6%, STPrivacy 32.3%) by 13–15 points, while simultaneously achieving the lowest privacy cMAP (59.7 vs. 62.5–63.5). The same pattern holds on UCF101-VISPR (47.9% vs. 39.8% for SelectivePrivacy) and with GPT-4V (55.1% vs. 49.5% on HMDB51; 65.2% vs. 61.2% on UCF101). On VQA with GPT-4V, PABP reaches 58.1% accuracy versus 55.9% for SelectivePrivacy and 55.6% for MPPAR, while achieving the best privacy cMAP of 43.6 (OK-VQA) and 48.2 (VISPR). The gap is most pronounced against the obfuscation-based baselines (Downsample, Obf-Blackening/StrongBlur/WeakBlur from SPAct [16]), which sacrifice 10–25 points of utility for only marginal privacy gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of privacy-preserving action recognition work (SPAct, VITA, BDQ, MPPAR, STPrivacy) that all require white-box gradient access to the utility model for adversarial training. The key conceptual departure is reframing the black-box LMM utility constraint as a binary 'satisfactory/unsatisfactory' classification problem, then exploiting the decision boundary between these states as an optimization guide — drawing on decision-boundary ideas from SVMs and label-only adversarial attacks, and on exponential search for efficient boundary probing. The surrogate-model initialization strategy borrows from the common practice of using a small white-box proxy when the target model is inaccessible, but the subsequent boundary-probing optimization (GEP and PGP schemes) is novel in this context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to address visual privacy protection specifically for black-box LMM services, a setting that is increasingly relevant as GPT-4V and similar models are deployed as cloud APIs where users must upload personal images and videos. The practical impact is significant: the framework enables privacy-preserving use of LMMs without requiring access to model internals or retraining. Open questions include whether the binary satisfactory/unsatisfactory utility framing is sufficient for more nuanced LMM tasks (e.g., open-ended generation quality), the computational cost of repeated black-box queries during probing, and whether the approach generalizes to multi-turn conversational settings where privacy leakage accumulates over dialogue.
