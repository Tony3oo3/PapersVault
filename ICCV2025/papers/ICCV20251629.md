## Problem

Existing human-centric evaluation metrics for text-to-image generation (HPS, ImageReward, PickScore, MPS) are constrained by three gaps: their training data covers only a narrow quality spectrum dominated by diffusion-model outputs, they rely on CLIP/BLIP encoders that produce suboptimal multimodal representations, and they use deterministic ranking losses that ignore annotation uncertainty. These limitations make them unreliable for evaluating newer model families (diffusion transformers, autoregressive models) and for distinguishing subtle quality differences.

## Input / Output

The model takes a pair of RGB images (resized to 448×448 while preserving aspect ratio) and a shared text prompt, producing a scalar preference score per image and a pairwise preference probability via a Gaussian-integrated sigmoid. For the CoHP refinement pipeline, the input is a text prompt plus a reference image blended with noise, and the output is a progressively refined generated image at 1024×1024. All model parameters of the Qwen2-VL-7B backbone are trainable, distinguishing this from prior frozen-encoder approaches.

## Method

HPSv3 uses Qwen2-VL-7B as a fully trainable VLM backbone to extract joint image-text features, followed by an MLP head. The key architectural innovation is an uncertainty-aware ranking loss: instead of outputting a single scalar, the final two linear layers predict μ and σ, modeling each image's score as a 1-D Gaussian N(μ, σ). The pairwise preference probability is then computed by double-integrating sigmoid(r₁−r₂) over both Gaussians, and training minimizes the negative log-likelihood of the correct ordering. This is trained on HPDv3 (1.08M text-image pairs, 1.17M pairwise comparisons spanning GAN, diffusion, autoregressive models, and high-quality real photographs) for 2 epochs on 48 A800 GPUs at lr 2×10⁻⁶. A complementary CoHP pipeline uses HPSv3 as a reward model in two iterative stages—Model-wise (selecting the best generator) and Sample-wise (iteratively refining via img2img with denoising strength 0.8→0.5)—to improve generation quality without additional training data.

## Results

HPSv3 achieves state-of-the-art preference prediction accuracy of 76.9% on HPDv3, 85.4% on HPDv2, 72.8% on PickScore, and 66.8% on ImageReward, outperforming all prior methods on every test set. In terms of correlation with human rankings, HPSv3 attains Spearman r = 0.94 and Kendall τ = 0.82, substantially exceeding HPSv2 (0.87/0.76) and PickScore (0.81/0.63). On the challenging HPDv3 benchmark, HPSv3 gains 19.3 percentage points over HPSv2 and 17.2 pp over PickScore, while remaining remarkably stable across datasets (only 8.5 pp drop from HPDv2 to HPDv3, versus 14.2 pp for PickScore and 19.1 pp for MPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HPSv3 outperforms all seven baselines on every test set. Against the closest competitor HPSv2 (same authors, prior version), HPSv3 gains 11.6 pp on HPDv3 (76.9% vs. 65.3%) and 2.1 pp on HPDv2 (85.4% vs. 83.3%), with the gap widening dramatically on the wider-spectrum HPDv3 dataset. PickScore, the strongest external baseline, scores 65.6% on HPDv3 (11.3 pp below HPSv3) and suffers a 14.2 pp drop from HPDv2 to HPDv3, indicating poor generalization to high-quality real images. MPS (64.3% on HPDv3) and HPS (63.8%) show similar degradation patterns. ImageReward (58.6%) and the Aesthetic Score Predictor (59.9%) trail by 17–18 pp on HPDv3, while CLIP (48.6%) is the weakest by a wide margin, confirming that raw CLIP features are insufficient for fine-grained preference ranking. Notably, HPSv3's cross-dataset stability (only 8.5 pp drop from HPDv2 to HPDv3) contrasts sharply with the 14–19 pp drops seen in PickScore and MPS, underscoring the benefit of training on a wide-spectrum dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

HPSv3 extends the HPSv2 line of work by replacing the CLIP-based backbone with Qwen2-VL (a 7B VLM) and introducing an uncertainty-aware ranking loss inspired by probabilistic reward modeling [30], moving from deterministic scalar scores to Gaussian-distributed outputs. The dataset HPDv3 deliberately departs from prior preference datasets (HPDv1/v2, ImageRewardDB, Pick-a-Pic) by incorporating high-quality real photographs as an upper quality bound alongside outputs from 16 generative models spanning GAN, diffusion, and autoregressive architectures. The CoHP component is a novel contribution that repurposes Chain-of-Thought reasoning for iterative image refinement, using the preference model as a reward signal to guide multi-round generation without additional training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HPDv3 establishes the first wide-spectrum benchmark that includes real photographs as a quality ceiling, exposing the limitations of prior metrics that only compared generated images against each other; the 19+ point accuracy drops for existing methods on HPDv3 versus HPDv2 underscore how much harder the task becomes when the full quality range is covered. CoHP offers a practical, training-free refinement pipeline that could be integrated into production T2I systems, though its compute cost (multiple generation rounds across multiple models) limits immediate adoption. Open questions include whether the VLM backbone advantage persists as smaller VLMs improve, whether uncertainty-aware ranking generalizes to video or 3D generation preference scoring, and whether the 90%+ annotator convergence threshold adequately captures the full diversity of human aesthetic judgment.
