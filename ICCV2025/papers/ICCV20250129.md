## Problem

Autoregressive image generation models rely on causal attention to predict visual tokens sequentially, but existing tokenizers encode only local image patches into latent tokens, leaving the model without explicit global information. This absence of holistic representation makes it difficult for AR models to maintain global coherence, leading to degraded generation quality and poor performance on tasks requiring whole-image consistency such as inpainting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250129/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tokenizer is trained on 336×336 RGB images from ImageNet and produces 569 discrete tokens per image: 128 holistic tokens (from learnable queries) followed by 441 patch-level tokens (21×21 grid at 16× downsampling). For class-conditional generation, the AR model takes a class embedding as conditioning and sequentially generates the full 569-token sequence—holistic tokens first, then patch tokens in raster-scan order—which is decoded into a 256×256 image. The tokenizer also supports zero-shot style transfer (swapping holistic tokens between images) and zero-shot inpainting (using partial token sequences as prefix prompts).

## Method

Hita introduces a holistic-to-local tokenization scheme built on VQGAN. A set of 128 learnable queries interacts with patch embeddings via a transformer, with DINOv2 features injected to enrich semantic content; a causal transformer then reorganizes holistic and patch features into a sequential order (holistic first, patches in raster-scan) to align the latent space with AR's causal nature. Holistic and patch features are quantized with separate codebooks (16,384 entries each, 8-dim for patches, 12-dim for holistic). A key design in the token fusion module prevents holistic codebook collapse: after causal-attention fusion, only the last k=4 holistic tokens are combined with the first (441−k) patch tokens to form the 2D grid for decoding, forcing patch tokens to depend on holistic information rather than bypassing it via skip connections. A standard Llama-based AR transformer then generates the token sequence autoregressively, with holistic tokens serving as a global prefix prompt that guides subsequent patch-token generation.

## Results

On the ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation benchmark, Hita-2B (2B parameters, 50 epochs) achieves an FID of 2.59 and IS of 281.9, outperforming LlamaGen-3B (3B parameters) which reports FID 2.61 and IS 251.9. At the 1.4B scale, Hita-XXL reaches FID 2.70 and IS 274.8, surpassing LDM-4 (400M params) by 0.9 FID and 27.1 IS. Hita also accelerates convergence: reaching FID 4.22 requires 2.1× less training time compared to the vanilla tokenizer baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary AR baseline LlamaGen, Hita wins at every matched parameter scale: Hita-B (FID 5.85) beats LlamaGen-B (FID 8.31) by 2.46 FID, and Hita-XXL (FID 2.70) beats LlamaGen-XXL (FID 2.89) by 0.19 FID while also improving IS from 236.2 to 274.8. Hita-2B (2B params) surpasses LlamaGen-3B (3B params) on both FID (2.59 vs 2.61) and IS (281.9 vs 251.9) with 33% fewer parameters. Against diffusion models, Hita-XXL outperforms LDM-4 (400M) by 0.9 FID and 27.1 IS, though DiT-XL/2 (675M) still leads on FID (2.27 vs 2.70) while Hita-2B edges it on IS (281.9 vs 278.2). Compared to other AR tokenizers, Hita substantially outperforms TiTok at both 111M (FID 5.85 vs 9.19) and 343M (FID 3.75 vs 5.66) scales, and also beats VIM-L-re (FID 2.70 vs 3.04) and RQTran.-re (FID 2.59 vs 3.80). At extended 300-epoch training, Hita-B (FID 4.33) and Hita-L (FID 2.86) continue to lead over LlamaGen-B (6.09) and LlamaGen-L (3.07) respectively, confirming the gap widens with more training.

## Relation to Prior Work

Hita builds directly on the VQGAN tokenizer architecture and the Llama-based AR generation pipeline established by LlamaGen, but departs from the prevailing design of encoding only local patch-level tokens. It is conceptually related to VAR's multi-scale prediction and TiTok's query-based compression, yet differs by explicitly separating holistic (global) tokens from patch tokens, injecting DINOv2 semantic features into learnable queries, and using a causal token-fusion module to prevent holistic codebook collapse. Rather than modifying the AR model with bidirectional attention (as in MAR or Show-o), Hita addresses the global-coherence limitation at the tokenizer level, keeping the downstream AR transformer completely vanilla.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a well-designed tokenizer can partially compensate for the inherent limitations of causal attention in AR image generation, offering a simpler alternative to architectural modifications like bidirectional attention. The emergent zero-shot style transfer and inpainting capabilities, along with a 2× training speedup, suggest the holistic-token paradigm could be a practical building block for future AR generators. However, the evaluation is limited to class-conditional ImageNet 256×256; whether the approach transfers to text-to-image generation, higher resolutions, or video remains open. The holistic tokens capture primarily low-level global properties (texture, color, material) rather than high-level semantics, as confirmed by the linear probing gap with DINOv2, leaving room for richer semantic integration.
