## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models consistently generate content that is strongly entangled with specific keywords (e.g., a mustache with 'Charlie Chaplin'), even when explicitly instructed to exclude it. Existing suppression methods—whether they weaken attention to negative tokens, apply noise-level guidance, or regularize [EOT] embeddings—fail in this regime because the entangled content is encoded within the keyword's own embedding rather than in a separate negative token.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252327/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt and a negative content word (e.g., 'glasses') as input; for the optimization variant on personalized models, it additionally requires a subject image and a binary mask delineating the negative content region. The output is a generated image in which the specified negative content is suppressed while the rest of the scene is preserved. The delta vector itself is a 768-dim vector (or n_w × 768 for multi-token words) obtained either zero-shot from the text encoder or via optimization, and is applied at the embedding level before cross-attention projection.

## Method

The core mechanism introduces a delta vector δ (the text embedding of the negative content, mean-pooled if multi-token) that is added to or subtracted from the keyword's word embedding to directly modulate the entangled content. The proposed SSDV method inserts a competing token into cross-attention: a key-side embedding e_k* = e_w + α_k·δ (α_k > 0) identifies the spatial region where the negative content would appear via softmax competition, while a value-side embedding e_v* = e_w + α_v·δ (α_v < 0) suppresses the visual features in that region. For personalized models (DreamBooth, CustomDiffusion) where prior knowledge is weakened, the delta is optimized via DDIM inversion with a combined loss—L_latent aligns the generated content with the mask in latent space, and L_attn ensures the cross-attention map of e_k* matches the mask—yielding a more precise suppression vector than the zero-shot embedding.

## Results

On the SEP-Benchmark (Stable Diffusion), SSDV achieves the best scores across all four metrics: CLIP 15.92 (vs. 16.46 for Negative Prompt, the second-best), IFID 87.34 (vs. 47.50 for Inst-Inpaint), DetScore 0.113 (vs. 0.220 for Inst-Inpaint), and human preference 86.47% (vs. 4.54% for Negative Prompt). On DreamBooth-based personalized models, SSDV with the zero-shot delta achieves CLIP scores of 14.14–15.65 and human preference scores of 90.15–95.45%, substantially outperforming all baselines. The optimization-based delta further improves identity preservation, with 73.2% of users preferring it over the zero-shot delta (26.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSDV outperforms all four baselines on every metric in both the Stable Diffusion and DreamBooth settings. On SEP-Benchmark, SSDV reduces CLIP similarity to 15.92 (vs. 16.46 for Negative Prompt, 17.14 for Inst-Inpaint, 17.16 for SEGA, 18.34 for P2P, and 18.41 for SuppressEOT) and achieves a DetScore of 0.113, roughly half that of the next-best Inst-Inpaint (0.220). In human preference, SSDV receives 86.47% vs. 4.54% for Negative Prompt and 3.52% for SEGA, a dramatic margin. On personalized DreamBooth models, SSDV achieves 90–95% human preference while baselines score below 9%; the sole exception is Inst-Inpaint on S*toy duck-yellow, which achieves a lower CLIP (12.22) but does so by erasing the subject itself rather than just the color, as confirmed by its 0.75% preference score versus SSDV's 95.45%. P2P and SuppressEOT fail to suppress entangled content at all (CLIP scores near or above the original generation), confirming their inability to handle strongly entangled concepts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of diffusion-based content suppression methods (P2P, SuppressEOT, SEGA, Inst-Inpaint, ESD) by shifting the intervention point from attention weights, noise-level guidance, or model fine-tuning to a direct modification of the text embedding itself via a 'delta vector.' The key conceptual departure is that prior methods target the negative content's own token or its attention influence, which fails when the content is strongly entangled with a keyword (e.g., 'mustache' with 'Charlie Chaplin'); here, the delta vector is subtracted from the keyword's embedding to break the entanglement at its source. The SSDV mechanism further builds on the cross-attention structure of LDMs by introducing competitive tokens that exploit softmax normalization for spatially selective suppression, and the optimization-based delta extends the approach to personalized models (DreamBooth, CustomDiffusion) where prior knowledge is weakened.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to suppress strongly entangled content is practically critical for mitigating copyright infringement and malicious outputs in deployed T2I systems, and this paper provides the first method that handles the entanglement case where all prior baselines fail. The zero-shot delta vector is appealingly simple (no training required) and the extension to personalized models addresses a gap where existing suppression methods are entirely ineffective. Limitations include the need to know the negative content in advance, the requirement of a mask for the optimization variant, and evaluation on a relatively small benchmark (10 prompt-content pairs) with a single diffusion architecture. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multiple simultaneous negative contents, to non-LDM architectures, and whether the delta vector can be learned in a fully automatic manner without user-specified content.
