## Problem

Event camera denoising and motion estimation have been treated as separate, sequential tasks, yet motion is intrinsic to event data since scene edges cannot be sensed without it. Existing denoising methods either require unavailable ground-truth noise labels or rely on spatio-temporal heuristics that ignore the motion structure present in the data. This paper asks whether denoising and motion estimation can be formulated as a single joint problem that benefits both simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only a raw asynchronous event stream (pixel coordinates, timestamps, polarity) with no auxiliary inputs such as frames, IMU data, or GT labels. The sole user-specified parameter is a target noise ratio η, which sets the threshold for the signal/noise split. Outputs include the classified signal and noise event sets, the estimated motion (either a 3-DOF angular velocity or a dense 2Np-DOF optical flow field), and the image of warped events (IWE) built with a Gaussian kernel (default σ=1 px) that serves as the edge-strength measure for scoring.

## Method

The core mechanism is an alternating optimization that iterates between one step of CMax-based motion estimation (using only the current signal events) and a per-event reclassification step. In the classification step, all events are warped to a reference time using the current motion estimate, and each event receives a local-contrast score c_k equal to the IWE value at its warped location; events are then ranked and thresholded at the (1−η)-quantile to update the signal/noise partition. The key insight is that signal events warped by the true motion concentrate at scene edges (producing high IWE values), while noise events scatter uniformly and contribute little to edge sharpness, making local contrast a natural signal/noise discriminator. The framework is motion-estimator-agnostic: the CMax step can be replaced by any motion estimator (e.g., the DNN-based E-RAFT), and the per-iteration cost is O(N_p + N_e log N_e), only ~5% overhead over vanilla CMax. Initialization uses a random signal/noise split, and convergence is flagged by stabilization of the motion parameters.

## Results

On the E-MLB denoising benchmark (MESR↑), the proposed method achieves 0.938–0.986 across Day ND1–ND64 and 0.932–1.037 across Night ND1–ND64, ranking first or second among all model-based methods and competitive with learning-based approaches (e.g., EDformer) that require GT noise labels for training. On DND21, it reaches MESR 0.992, outperforming all model-based baselines (best prior: TS at 0.985). For motion estimation, the method reduces angular velocity RMS from 20.0° (CMax without init) to 8.17° on ECD dynamic_rot, and improves optical flow EPE from 2.676 to 2.517 px on MVSEC indoor1, demonstrating that joint denoising and motion estimation benefits both tasks simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among model-based denoisers, the proposed method outperforms BAF [9] on all four E-MLB Day conditions (e.g., 0.986 vs 0.876 on ND16) and on DND21 (0.992 vs 0.920), but trails BAF on E-MLB Night ND4/ND16/ND64 (0.961/0.945/0.932 vs 0.973/0.992/0.942). Against the best learning-based method EDformer [29], Ours wins on E-MLB Day ND16 (0.986 vs 0.956), ND64 (0.950 vs 0.942), and DND21 (0.992 vs 1.041 is actually a loss—correction: EDformer scores 1.041 on DND21, so Ours loses there), while losing on most Night conditions where EDformer benefits from GT-label training. For motion estimation on ECD, the method reduces angular velocity RMS from 20.0° (CMax, no init) to 8.17° (dynamic_rot) and from 124.6° to 20.6° (boxes_rot), marginally beating BAF+init (8.25°/20.55°) and substantially outperforming CMax without denoising. On MVSEC optical flow, Ours improves EPE over MultiCM [48] on all four sequences (e.g., 2.517 vs 2.676 on indoor1; 1.865 vs 1.960 on outdoor1), confirming that simultaneous noise removal directly benefits flow accuracy. The method also reduces the sensitivity to initialization: with a good init, CMax alone reaches 8.275° RMS, while Ours without init reaches 8.170°, showing that joint denoising can compensate for poor initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate pipelines—event denoising and motion estimation—into a single alternating optimization loop built on the Contrast Maximization (CMax) framework (Gallego et al., CVPR 2018). The per-event scoring mechanism is adapted from motion segmentation by motion compensation (Stoffregen et al., ICCV 2019), where local contrast (the count of warped events at each pixel) is repurposed as an unsupervised signal/noise criterion. Unlike prior denoising methods (BAF, spatiotemporal filters, or learning-based classifiers requiring GT noise labels) that operate independently of motion, and unlike ProgressiveMotionSeg which applied similar weighting only to low-DOF warps without evaluating denoising efficacy, this work supports arbitrary motion models (3-DOF rotation, dense optical flow, or DNN-based estimators) and explicitly treats noise as uncorrelated with motion from first principles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for GT noise labels or additional sensor inputs, the method opens a practical path to denoising in real-world deployments where such annotations are infeasible, while simultaneously improving the robustness of CMax-based motion estimation by relaxing its sensitivity to initialization. Key open questions remain: the method requires a user-specified target noise ratio (η), and the authors acknowledge that automatic noise-level estimation is unsolved; additionally, the ESR denoising metric itself depends on the motion parameter, suggesting the field needs better label-free evaluation tools. The flexibility to swap in learned motion estimators (e.g., E-RAFT) hints at a broader co-design space between denoising and learning-based perception that the paper leaves for future work.
