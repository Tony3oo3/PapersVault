## Problem

Existing pose-free novel view synthesis methods still rely on ground-truth camera poses during training, confining them to datasets with pose annotations and limiting scalability to large-scale unposed data. The few self-supervised pose-free approaches that eliminate this dependency use separate modules for pose estimation and scene reconstruction, producing inconsistent features, poor geometric alignment, and an unstable feedback loop between pose errors and reconstruction quality. This paper asks whether ground-truth poses can be removed entirely from both training and inference while matching or exceeding SOTA pose-required and supervised pose-free methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250575/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N sparse unposed RGB images (typically 2, extendable to 10) at 256×256 resolution along with optional camera intrinsics, and produces pixel-aligned 3D Gaussian primitives (centers, rotation quaternions, scales, opacities, SH coefficients) in a canonical space where the first input view defines the reference frame, plus relative camera poses as 10D vectors (4D homogeneous translation + 6D rotation). During training, a target view is additionally fed through a separate branch solely for pose estimation, while Gaussian prediction uses only context views to prevent information leakage. Inference is a single feed-forward pass with no iterative optimization.

## Method

SPFSplat uses a shared ViT backbone (ViT-Large encoder, ViT-Base cross-attention decoder) with three specialized heads: two DPT heads for Gaussian centers and parameters, and a lightweight 3-layer MLP pose head, all operating in a canonical space anchored to the first input view. The key architectural insight is that the shared backbone forces pose estimation and Gaussian prediction to learn from the same geometric features, breaking the destabilizing feedback loop that plagues methods with decoupled modules. Training is fully self-supervised: the model estimates target-view poses itself and uses them to render novel views for the L2+LPIPS rendering loss, eliminating any ground-truth pose requirement. A pixel-wise reprojection loss is added to project predicted 3D Gaussian centers back into 2D using the estimated context poses, explicitly enforcing pixel-to-point alignment and preventing the training collapse that occurs when context views are naively included in the rendering loss. The model is initialized from pretrained MASt3R weights, and the pose head is initialized to approximate the identity rotation for stable convergence.

## Results

On RE10K (the primary evaluation dataset), SPFSplat achieves an average PSNR of 25.484, SSIM of 0.847, and LPIPS of 0.153 across small/medium/large overlap settings, outperforming all pose-required (MVSplat: 24.012 PSNR), supervised pose-free (NoPoSplat*: 25.033 PSNR), and self-supervised pose-free (PF3plat: 21.042 PSNR) baselines. Notably, SPFSplat without evaluation-time pose alignment (EPA) already surpasses NoPoSplat with EPA by 0.45 PSNR, and the gap widens to 4.44 PSNR over the best self-supervised baseline (PF3plat). For relative pose estimation on RE10K, SPFSplat achieves AUC@5° of 0.617, AUC@10° of 0.755, and AUC@20° of 0.845, outperforming MASt3R (0.281/0.494/0.671) and DUSt3R (0.336/0.541/0.702) despite being trained without any geometric priors. Reconstruction time is 0.044 s for two 256×256 images, comparable to NoPoSplat (0.042 s) and ~27× faster than PF3plat (1.171 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPFSplat outperforms all pose-required baselines (pixelSplat, MVSplat) by 1.47–1.63 PSNR on average RE10K, demonstrating that ground-truth SfM poses are not necessary for competitive NVS. Against the strongest supervised pose-free method NoPoSplat, SPFSplat wins by 0.45 PSNR without EPA and 0.81 PSNR with EPA, while matching its inference speed (0.044 s vs. 0.042 s). The gap over self-supervised baselines is substantial: SPFSplat exceeds PF3plat by 4.44 PSNR and SelfSplat by 6.33 PSNR on average, with the largest margins in the small-overlap regime (e.g., 4.54 PSNR over PF3plat at small overlap). For pose estimation, SPFSplat (AUC@5°=0.617 on RE10K) surpasses MASt3R (0.281) and DUSt3R (0.336) by a wide margin, and also beats NoPoSplat (0.572) and SelfSplat (0.207), despite being trained without any geometric supervision. The one area where SPFSplat trails is cross-dataset generalization to DTU (object-centric), where NoPoSplat* achieves 17.899 PSNR vs. SPFSplat's 16.550, though SPFSplat still outperforms all other baselines on DTU.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPFSplat extends the canonical-space 3D Gaussian splatting paradigm established by Splatt3R and NoPoSplat, which predict Gaussians in a reference view's coordinate frame, but removes the ground-truth pose dependency that those supervised pose-free methods still require during training. It departs from prior self-supervised pose-free approaches (PF3plat, SelfSplat) by replacing their separate pose-estimation and scene-reconstruction modules with a single shared ViT backbone, and introduces a reprojection loss that enforces pixel-Gaussian alignment without any pose supervision. The architecture is initialized from MASt3R/DUSt3R weights, inheriting their joint 3D-point-and-pose prediction philosophy, but augments it with a rendering loss on estimated target poses to close the self-supervised loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating ground-truth poses from both training and inference, SPFSplat opens the door to training on large-scale unposed real-world imagery (e.g., web-scraped video) without the expensive and unreliable SfM pipeline, which is a significant practical bottleneck for sparse-view reconstruction. The finding that a self-supervised method can match or exceed pose-required SOTA (MVSplat, pixelSplat) challenges the field's working assumption that accurate camera poses are a prerequisite for high-quality NVS. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions and more complex scenes, how it handles truly texture-less or highly dynamic environments, and whether the 0.044s feed-forward inference can be further reduced for real-time deployment.
