## Problem

Compositional visual reasoning methods that use frozen LLMs as planners lack tool awareness, producing workflows with ineffective tool usage. Existing fine-tuning approaches (e.g., VisRep) either use single-turn frameworks or apply naive SFT that clones entire workflows—including steps where tools returned incorrect results—causing the model to learn noise. The core gap is the absence of a mechanism to assess individual tool-usage effectiveness during workflow generation and to selectively train only on effective actions.

## Input / Output

The system takes an RGB image and a natural-language query as input, and produces a text answer derived from a multi-turn agentic workflow. The workflow is a sequence of three action types—Thought (natural-language reasoning), Code (Python calls to perception tools such as BLIP2, LLaVA-1.5, or GroundingDINO), and Done (final answer)—executed iteratively with environmental feedback appended at each step. During training, the ground-truth answer is provided as prior knowledge to the policy, a condition not available at inference time.

## Method

DWIM comprises two coupled components. First, Discrepancy-aware Training Workflow Generation conditions the LLM policy on the ground-truth answer during data collection, enabling it to detect factual discrepancies between tool outputs and expected results; when a discrepancy is found, the LLM emits a 'Rethink' Thought action describing the error and proposing an alternative tool call, which increases the fraction of successful workflows (data utilization rises from ~48% to ~68% on GQA). Second, Instruct-Masking Fine-tuning operates at the semantic (action) level rather than the token level: each effective action (κ_t=1) is replaced by a context-level mask plus an instruction to regenerate it, while ineffective actions and their Rethink descriptions remain unmasked so the model learns to recognize failure patterns. Training minimizes a reward-weighted negative log-likelihood loss over the masked workflow, implemented via LoRA (r=64) on LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct, and the resulting model requires zero in-context examples at inference time.

## Results

DWIM achieves state-of-the-art performance across all six visual reasoning tasks, with an average absolute improvement of 9.73% over the best compositional baseline (HYDRA). Headline numbers include 69.3% ACC on GQA, 62.8% on OKVQA, 74.6% on SugarCREPE, 13.6% group-level ACC on NaturalBench, 82.7% IoU on RefCOCO, and 72.0% ACC on TallyQA — all evaluated with 0 in-context examples, whereas baselines require 10-shot prompting. On cross-dataset generalization (trained on GQA, tested on OKVQA/A-OKVQA/SugarCREPE/Winoground), DWIM achieves ~4.8% average improvement over HYDRA and VisRep without any in-context examples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DWIM outperforms all compositional baselines on every task. Against HYDRA (the strongest compositional baseline), DWIM gains +7.0% on GQA, +2.2% on OKVQA, +16.4% on SugarCREPE, +1.3% on NaturalBench, +19.7% IoU on RefCOCO, and +11.8% on TallyQA — while using 0 in-context examples versus HYDRA's 10. Against VisRep, the margins are even larger, with DWIM surpassing it by up to +27.5% IoU on RefCOCO and +16.4% on SugarCREPE. Compared to frozen LLaMA-3.1-8B with 10-shot prompting, DWIM improves by 11–31% across tasks, and the frozen model's performance saturates quickly as in-context examples increase (Figure 4). Against E2E reference models, DWIM exceeds LLaVA-1.5 on GQA (+7.2%), SugarCREPE (+21.9%), and RefCOCO (+47.8% IoU), and surpasses GPT-4o on GQA (+10.8%) and RefCOCO (+52.2% IoU), though GPT-4o retains a slight edge on NaturalBench (16.8 vs. 13.6) and TallyQA (76.4 vs. 72.0). DWIM also uses fewer tool calls per successful sample (1.74 vs. 2.87 for frozen few-shot and 3.26 for HYDRA), indicating superior tool-use efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

DWIM extends the agentic LLM framework lineage from ReAct and CodeAct by introducing a multi-turn, discrepancy-aware workflow generation loop specifically designed for visual reasoning, where perception tools (BLIP2, GroundingDINO, etc.) are unreliable and introduce factual errors. It departs from prior compositional VR methods (VisRep, HYDRA, ViperGPT) in two key ways: (1) conditioning workflow generation on the ground-truth answer to detect and refine tool-usage discrepancies, and (2) replacing naive SFT with instruct-masking that selectively trains only on effective actions while exposing failure cases in unmasked context. The instruct-masking technique itself adapts BERT-style masking to the semantic (action) level and combines it with instruction-tuning principles, creating a hybrid training signal absent from prior VR or agent-training literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical bottleneck in compositional VR: the inability of frozen LLMs to develop tool awareness, and the data scarcity caused by unreliable perception tools corrupting workflow generation. The ~17% data utilization gain and the ability to train a small 8B model to outperform much larger frozen models (including GPT-4o on SugarCREPE) with zero in-context examples suggest that selective, discrepancy-aware training is more effective than scaling prompt engineering. Open questions include whether the approach scales to substantially larger or more heterogeneous tool libraries, whether the discrepancy-detection mechanism generalizes to domains where tool errors are more subtle (e.g., numerical approximations vs. categorical errors), and whether the instruct-masking strategy can be extended to multi-agent or hierarchical planning settings.
