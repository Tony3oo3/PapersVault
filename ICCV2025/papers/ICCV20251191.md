## Problem

Multimodal LLMs pre-trained on object-centric Internet images are inherently weak at specialized vision tasks (charts, tables, receipts, reports) and typically require large-scale task-specific datasets for adaptation. While distilling Chain-of-Thought reasoning from a third-party MLLM can improve few-shot adaptation, the intermediate reasoning steps frequently contain factual errors (e.g., misread prices or numbers) that degrade training quality and limit generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251191/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes specialized visual documents (bar/line/pie charts, tabular math problems, scanned receipts, financial report tables) at their native resolution paired with a natural-language question, and produces a grounded chain-of-thought in which key extracted entities (numbers, labels, objects) are inline-annotated with normalized bounding-box coordinates [x1, y1, x2, y2], followed by a final answer. The bounding boxes serve as self-verifiable visual evidence: the model can crop each region and check whether the enclosed content matches the referenced target, enabling automatic filtering of hallucinated reasoning steps.

## Method

GCoT is a three-stage pipeline: (1) a base MLLM (VisCoT-7B) is pre-trained on visual grounding data to acquire bounding-box localization ability; (2) a bootstrapping loop iteratively prompts the model to generate bounding boxes for key nouns/numbers extracted from LLaMA-3.2-distilled CoT, then self-verifies each box by cropping the image region and asking the model to recognize its content, retaining only boxes whose recognized text matches the target; (3) the model is fine-tuned (LoRA, rank 16) on the resulting grounded CoT, where correct bounding boxes are appended inline after their corresponding targets. The bootstrapping loop is repeated so that each iteration's verified boxes improve the model's grounding accuracy for the next round, and three verified GCoT samples per question are generated for data augmentation. The key insight is that grounding transforms unverifiable text reasoning into checkable visual evidence, enabling automatic rejection of factually incorrect reasoning steps without human annotation.

## Results

GCoT consistently outperforms all baselines (Zero-shot, Fine-tuning, Distillation) across all five datasets (ChartQA, TabMWP, SROIE, DVQA, TAT-QA) and all sample sizes (8–128). With only 8 training samples, GCoT beats Zero-shot by approximately 2% in average accuracy, and the margin over Fine-tuning and Distillation widens as sample size increases. On TabMWP, GCoT reaches 33.93% accuracy at 128 samples versus 22.78% average for Distillation across all source models (Table 3), a +3.76 percentage-point improvement. The advantage is most pronounced on TAT-QA, the most challenging dataset, where GCoT still leads Fine-tuning and Distillation despite uniformly lower absolute accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares GCoT against three baselines: Zero-shot (no parameter updates), Fine-tuning (LoRA on original Q-A pairs), and Distillation (LoRA on CoT distilled from LLaMA 3.2). GCoT wins on all five datasets at every sample size. Against Zero-shot, GCoT gains roughly 2% average accuracy at 8 samples, with the gap growing at larger sample sizes. Against Fine-tuning, GCoT leads by a significant and consistent margin; notably, Fine-tuning accuracy plateaus or even decreases (e.g., on TabMWP at 128 samples), indicating that simple Q-A pairs provide limited new knowledge. Against Distillation, GCoT achieves an average improvement of +3.76 percentage points (26.54% vs. 22.78% across five source models in Table 3), and also reduces variance from 2.37 to 0.91, indicating more stable training. Distillation itself generally outperforms Fine-tuning, confirming that CoT data is beneficial, but the factual errors in distilled CoT limit its ceiling—precisely the gap GCoT addresses through self-verified grounding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Chain-of-Thought reasoning paradigm (Wei et al., 2022) to the multimodal setting by injecting self-verified bounding boxes into reasoning steps, building directly on VisCoT-7B (Shao et al., 2024) as the grounding-capable backbone. It departs from prior chart/document understanding work (ChartLLaMA, ChartAssistant, ChartGemma) that relied on large-scale task-specific datasets, instead proposing a bootstrapping strategy that requires only 8–128 labeled examples. The self-verification mechanism via bounding-box cropping extends NLP CoT verification methods (SelfCheck, deductive verification) to the visual domain, where correctness can be checked by re-reading localized image regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that distilled CoT from strong third-party models contains systematic factual errors in intermediate steps, and that these errors actively harm downstream fine-tuning, is practically important for anyone using distillation pipelines for MLLM adaptation. The GCoT bootstrapping loop offers a concrete, low-cost recipe for data-efficient adaptation to specialized vision tasks, though it is currently limited to text/number-rich formats (charts, tables, receipts) and depends on an external model for initial CoT generation. Open questions include whether reinforcement learning can replace the external distillation dependency, whether the approach generalizes to abstract visual elements (lines, icons), and whether the grounding-verification loop scales to higher-resolution or more complex document layouts.
