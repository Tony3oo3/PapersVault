## Problem

Speculative Decoding (SD) achieves only modest speedups (~2×) in autoregressive image generation compared to its ~70% acceptance rate in text, because image tokens exhibit inherent redundancy and diversity—multiple tokens convey valid semantics—causing low, near-uniform next-token probabilities and high total variation between draft and expert distributions. Existing SD-based methods for image AR either provide limited acceleration or require additional training of a draft model.

## Input / Output

The method operates at inference time on a pre-trained 7B autoregressive image model (Lumina-mGPT) with a ~50,000-token VQ codebook, taking a text prompt as input and producing a 768×768 RGB image. It is entirely training-free: no additional parameters are learned, and the method is applied as a drop-in replacement for the standard token-by-token decoding loop, using Top-K sampling (K=2000, τ=1) as the base decoding setting.

## Method

GSD modifies the SD acceptance criterion to evaluate clusters of visually valid tokens rather than a single token: a draft token x is accepted if the grouped probability ratio p′(C(x))/q′(C(x)) passes a uniform random threshold, where p′ and q′ are probability masses summed over disjoint clusters C. The paper proves (Theorem 1) that this guarantees α_GSD ≥ α_SD via the triangle inequality on Total Variation. For clustering, they show static embedding-distance grouping is ineffective due to the near-uniform distribution of codebook embeddings, and instead propose dynamic context-aware clustering that sorts tokens by the expert model's predicted probability p(x) at each decoding step, grouping the G nearest tokens while filtering out those exceeding embedding-distance (d) or probability-difference (δ) thresholds. The drafting mechanism is Speculative Jacobi Decoding (SJD), which reuses early Jacobi-iteration predictions as drafts within a single parallel forward pass, eliminating the need for a separate draft model.

## Results

On Parti-Prompt (1,600 prompts), GSD with G=50 achieves a 4.65× latency speedup (24.13s vs. 112.29s for vanilla AR) and 3.76× NFE reduction (636.75 vs. 2392) while maintaining a CLIP score of 32.075 (vs. 32.091 for vanilla). On MS-COCO 2017 (5,000 prompts), GSD with G=25 achieves a 3.77× latency speedup (32.52s vs. 122.45s) with a CLIP score of 31.24 (vs. 31.308) and FID of 33.55 (vs. 30.79). At the conservative G=3 setting, GSD already outperforms vanilla AR on CLIP score (32.125 vs. 32.091 on Parti-Prompt; 31.33 vs. 31.308 on MS-COCO) while delivering 2.38–2.54× speedup, indicating that clustering smooths prediction noise and slightly improves quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla AR (Lumina-mGPT), GSD achieves 2.4–4.9× latency speedup while matching or slightly exceeding the CLIP score (e.g., 32.125 vs. 32.091 at G=3 on Parti-Prompt), showing that clustering actually improves quality by smoothing prediction noise. Compared to the lossless SJD baseline (2.15–2.25× speedup), GSD delivers an additional 1.1–1.63× speedup (reaching 3.76–3.91× NFE acceleration at G=50–100) with negligible CLIP degradation (≤0.02). Against naive lossy SD variants (Amplify, Addition), GSD achieves comparable or better speed (e.g., 3.76× vs. 4.08× NFE at G=50 vs. Amplify k=3) but preserves CLIP score far more closely (32.075 vs. 31.774), whereas the lossy methods degrade CLIP by 0.2–0.3 points and FID by 4–9 points on MS-COCO. Jacobi Decoding provides essentially no speedup (1.03–1.04× NFE), making it a weak baseline that GSD decisively outperforms. Overall, GSD dominates the Pareto front: at G=3 it is both faster and higher-quality than all lossless baselines, and at G=50–100 it matches the speed of the most aggressive lossy methods while retaining near-lossless quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Speculative Decoding (SD) framework of Leviathan et al. [10] and the training-free Speculative Jacobi Decoding (SJD) of Teng et al. [22] to the autoregressive image generation setting. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that image tokens—unlike text tokens—exhibit inherent redundancy and diversity, causing standard single-token acceptance criteria to suffer excessive false-negative rejections due to high total variation across a large (~50K) vocabulary. GSD addresses this by shifting the acceptance decision from individual tokens to clusters of semantically valid tokens, and further departs from prior relaxed-SD methods like LANTERN [9] by requiring no training and modifying both numerator and denominator of the acceptance ratio rather than only the numerator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AR image models are increasingly positioned as flexible alternatives to diffusion models, but their sequential token-by-token generation creates a significant latency bottleneck that limits practical deployment. GSD's training-free 3.7–3.8× speedup with a theoretical guarantee of no quality degradation (and in some settings, slight quality improvement) makes it immediately applicable to existing AR image pipelines. Open questions include whether the dynamic clustering strategy generalizes to other AR modalities (video, audio, 3D), whether it scales to higher resolutions or larger codebooks, and whether the approach can be combined with other acceleration techniques (e.g., parallel decoding heads). The paper is limited to a single model (Lumina-mGPT 7B) at 768×768, so broader architectural validation remains to be done.
