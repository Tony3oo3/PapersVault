## Problem

In mainstream LVLMs following the LLaVA paradigm, the mechanism by which MLP adapters bridge the vision-language modality gap remains poorly understood, and the adapter must be fully retrained whenever switching LLM backbones due to embedding dimension mismatches and distributional shifts. This paper investigates how MLPs actually learn cross-modal alignment and proposes an explicit, transferable alternative.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251377/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (processed by CLIP-ViT-L/14 at 336px resolution into patch-aligned visual features) and a text prompt. Rather than producing visual tokens via a direct dimension-mapping MLP, LangBridge outputs visual tokens as weighted linear combinations of the target LLM's vocabulary embeddings, which are then concatenated with text embeddings and fed to the LLM to generate a text response. The adapter's intermediate output is a probability distribution over a fixed shared vocabulary of 19,200 tokens, decoupling it from any specific LLM's embedding dimension.

## Method

LangBridge is grounded in the empirical finding that MLP adapters progressively learn to project visual features into subspaces spanned by semantically related text embeddings. The architecture operates in three stages: a Vision Transformer extracts patch features, a two-layer MLP followed by a linear layer maps these features to a probability distribution over a shared vocabulary (19,200 high-frequency tokens common to LLaMA and Qwen), and the final visual token is computed as the weighted sum of the target LLM's vocabulary embeddings using those probabilities. Because the adapter only learns combination weights over a fixed shared vocabulary rather than a direct dimension-to-dimension mapping, a LangBridge pretrained on one LLM (e.g., Qwen2-0.5B) can be directly reused on a different LLM (e.g., LLaMA3-8B or Qwen2.5-14B) during SFT without any re-pretraining, simply by re-weighting the same probability distribution against the new LLM's embedding table.

## Results

In the standard (non-transfer) setting, LangBridge matches or slightly exceeds the standard MLP connector on LLaMA3-8B across 6 of 7 benchmarks (e.g., GQA 64.00 vs 63.24, MME 1775 vs 1736, MMBench 72.6 vs 71.0), with only a minor SciQA drop (77.98 vs 78.61). On Qwen2.5-7B, LangBridge achieves comparable overall performance with a notable MMVeT gain (36.7 vs 34.8, +5.46%) but a larger MME drop (1874 vs 1939). The headline contribution is pretraining-free transfer: a LangBridge pretrained on Qwen2-0.5B transfers to Qwen2.5-14B retaining 97–101% of the baseline on most benchmarks, and a Qwen2-0.5B connector transfers cross-architecture to LLaMA3-8B with gains on GQA (+1.42%), TextVQA (+1.87%), and MMVeT (+9.68%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the standard two-layer MLP connector from LLaVA, trained with the same two-stage pipeline (pretraining + SFT) on identical datasets. On LLaMA3-8B, LangBridge wins on 6 of 7 benchmarks (GQA +0.76, TextVQA +0.89, MME +39, MMBench +1.6, MMVeT +0.1, POPE +0.6) and loses only on SciQA (−0.63). On Qwen2.5-7B, the comparison is more mixed: LangBridge wins on GQA (+0.18), MMVeT (+1.9), and SciQA (+0.07), ties on MMBench, and loses on MME (−65 points, −3.35%) and TextVQA (−0.02). The critical differentiator is not raw accuracy but transferability: the MLP baseline must be fully retrained for each new LLM backbone, whereas LangBridge pretrained on Qwen2-0.5B transfers to LLaMA3-8B, Qwen2-7B, Qwen2.5-7B, and Qwen2.5-14B with 97–101% of baseline performance (and occasional gains up to +9.68% on MMVeT) without any additional pretraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LLaVA's ViT + MLP connector + LLM architecture and its two-stage (pretraining + SFT) training paradigm, but departs from the standard MLP connector by proposing LangBridge, which explicitly decomposes visual features into linear combinations of LLM vocabulary embeddings rather than learning a direct dimension mapping. It positions itself against other connector designs (Flamingo's cross-attention, BLIP-2's Q-former, Ovis's structural embedding alignment) by offering an interpretable, vocabulary-grounded alternative. The empirical analysis of how MLPs progressively learn cross-modal alignment across training stages is a novel diagnostic contribution that motivates the architectural choice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pretraining-free adapter transfer across different LLM backbones (e.g., Qwen2-0.5B → LLaMA3-8B or Qwen2.5-14B) offers a practical compute saving by eliminating the expensive cross-modal pretraining stage when switching LLMs, which is increasingly relevant as the LLM ecosystem diversifies. The interpretability of visual tokens as weighted combinations of named vocabulary embeddings opens a path for diagnosing and controlling what the model 'sees.' Open questions include whether the 19,200-token shared vocabulary bottleneck limits performance on specialized or multilingual domains, whether the approach extends to video or higher-resolution inputs, and whether the probability distributions can be further exploited for fine-grained visual grounding or hallucination mitigation.
