## Problem

Existing Image Difference Captioning (IDC) datasets suffer from limited breadth (confined to narrow scenes like fixed-viewpoint surveillance, bird species, or simple tabletop renders) and insufficient depth (average captions of only 8 words, e.g., IEdit), making them inadequate for capturing the complexity of real-world visual changes. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a comprehensive benchmark and a dedicated model architecture for fine-grained difference description.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251728/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of similar RGB images (real-world photographs or 3D-rendered scenes) as input and produces a structured natural-language difference caption averaging 60 words. Each caption is organized into a referring part that localizes the changed object (e.g., 'a man in a blue shirt standing behind a desk on the left') and a change part that describes the specific modification (e.g., 'changed from facing the window to facing the sofa'). The dataset covers 12 distinct change types including viewpoint, illumination, addition, disappearance, removal, substitution, size, color, orientation, pose, OCR text changes, and counting.

## Method

M³Diff extends LLaVA-OneVision 7B (SigLIP visual encoder + Qwen2 LLM) with a plug-and-play Multi-scale Differential Perception (MDP) module that operates on features from four intermediate encoder layers (17, 20, 23, 26). The MDP module first computes gated differential features via sigmoid-weighted channel-wise subtraction between the two image streams, then fuses these differences back into the original features through a two-stage cross-attention mechanism (self-attention on the difference, followed by cross-attention between difference and original features). Multi-scale integration uses learned per-layer fusion scores (computed via token-wise mean pooling and MLP) to aggregate low-level detail with high-level semantics. Training employs a single-stage fine-tuning strategy: LoRA (rank 128) on the LLM with full fine-tuning of the visual encoder, projector, and MDP module, on 896K QA pairs drawn from OmniDiff plus five public IDC datasets.

## Results

On the proposed OmniDiff benchmark, M³Diff achieves BLEU-4 of 14.3 / METEOR 18.9 / ROUGE-L 32.9 / CIDEr 31.3 on real-world scenes and 15.7 / 19.9 / 35.3 / 28.3 on 3D-rendered scenes, substantially outperforming the best prior MLLM (FINER-MLLM: 8.9 / 13.8 / 25.9 / 11.7 on Real) and zero-shot GPT-4o (3.1 / 13.6 / 21.0 / 5.2). On public benchmarks, M³Diff sets new SOTA: CIDEr 71.1 on Spot-the-Diff (vs. 61.8 for FINER-MLLM), CIDEr 136.6 on IEdit (vs. 109.6 for OneDiff), and BLEU-4 60.6 / CIDEr 109.4 on CLEVR-DC (vs. 51.4 / 84.1 for DIRL). On CLEVR-Change, M³Diff leads in BLEU-4 (57.1), METEOR (41.9), and SPICE (33.8) but trails CLIP4IDC on CIDEr (130.5 vs. 150.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

M³Diff achieves the best score on every metric across all five benchmarks except CIDEr on CLEVR-Change, where CLIP4IDC (150.7) edges out M³Diff (130.5). On the paper's own OmniDiff benchmark, M³Diff surpasses the strongest non-MLLM baseline (CARD) by 5.2 BLEU-4 and 22.1 CIDEr on Real, and outperforms the best MLLM baseline FINER-MLLM by 5.4 BLEU-4 and 19.6 CIDEr on Real. On Spot-the-Diff, M³Diff leads FINER-MLLM by 1.5 BLEU-4 and 9.3 CIDEr, and OneDiff by 1.6 BLEU-4 and 14.5 CIDEr. On IEdit, M³Diff dominates OneDiff by 4.0 BLEU-4 and 27.0 CIDEr, and the best non-MLLM method (DIRL) by 22.7 BLEU-4 and 102.5 CIDEr. On CLEVR-DC, M³Diff outperforms the previous best (DIRL) by 9.2 BLEU-4 and 25.3 CIDEr, with no other MLLM baseline reported. Zero-shot evaluations of GPT-4o, LLaVA-OneVision-7B, and Qwen-2.5-VL-7B all score far below fine-tuned methods on OmniDiff, confirming that general-purpose MLLMs struggle with fine-grained difference captioning without task-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Image Difference Captioning (IDC) task from traditional encoder-decoder pipelines (DUDA, M-VAM, DIRL) and early MLLM adaptations (FINER-MLLM, OneDiff) by introducing a dedicated Multi-scale Differential Perception (MDP) module as a plug-and-play component on top of LLaVA-OneVision 7B. Conceptually, it departs from prior MLLM-based IDC methods that rely on dual intra-/inter-image constraints or siamese encoders, instead performing explicit channel-wise subtraction and cross-attention fusion at multiple visual encoder layers. On the data side, OmniDiff generalizes prior IDC benchmarks (Spot-the-Diff, CLEVR-Change, IEdit) by combining real-world and 3D-rendered scenes with 12 change types and substantially longer captions (60 words vs. 8–19 words in prior work).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OmniDiff addresses a concrete gap in IDC evaluation: prior datasets were either scene-restricted (single viewpoint, tabletop, or bird-only) or caption-shallow, making it difficult to assess whether models truly understand fine-grained differences in complex environments. The finding that even GPT-4o achieves only 3.1 BLEU-4 zero-shot on OmniDiff-Real underscores that current general-purpose MLLMs remain far from competent at this task, motivating task-specific architectures. Open questions include whether the MDP module's multi-scale fusion generalizes to video-based or temporally continuous difference captioning, and whether the 60-word annotation standard is the right granularity for downstream applications like surveillance or environmental monitoring.
