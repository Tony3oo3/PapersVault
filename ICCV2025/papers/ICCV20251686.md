## Problem

Existing transformer-based moment retrieval models over-associate text queries with background frames rather than distinguishing the actual target moment, a spurious correlation that causes inaccurate temporal span predictions. This paper identifies this spurious correlation as the root cause of the performance gap and proposes a dynamic learning framework to mitigate it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251686/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes pre-extracted video features (SlowFast + CLIP vision encoder) as L vision tokens and a CLIP text encoder representation of W words. It outputs a normalized temporal span (center and duration) predicted by a 3-layer MLP, a foreground/background classification score, and token-wise saliency scores. The video is processed as a sequence of clip-level tokens, and the model also handles paired synthesized videos simultaneously within a batch.

## Method

TD-DETR introduces two complementary strategies within a DETR encoder-decoder framework. First, a Video Synthesizer for Dynamic Context (VSDC) selects the most similar video pair from each training batch via cosine similarity and synthesizes new videos by sampling tokens from both videos at ratio α while preserving ground-truth span completeness, forcing the model to localize moments across varying backgrounds. Second, a Temporal Dynamics Enhancement Module (TDEM) computes element-wise differences between consecutive tokens to extract dynamic representations, then applies cross-attention between the text query and both the visual and dynamic token sequences, combining the results via weighted addition (β=0.7) to inject text-guided temporal information. The enhanced representations feed into a standard DETR encoder-decoder with Hungarian matching and L1/gIoU/CE losses for moment retrieval plus margin/contrastive/negative losses for highlight detection.

## Results

On QVHighlights test, TD-DETR achieves R1@0.7 of 50.37 and average mAP of 46.69, outperforming the previous SOTA BAM-DETR (R1@0.7=48.64, avg mAP=45.36) by 1.73 and 1.33 points respectively. On Charades-STA test, TD-DETR reaches R1@0.5=60.89 and R1@0.7=40.35, surpassing BAM-DETR (59.95, 39.38) by 0.94 and 0.97 points. The spurious correlation evaluation (Table 3) further shows that integrating TD-DETR into BAM-DETR reduces Spurious R1@0.9 from 1.87 to 0.52 while simultaneously boosting standard mAP@0.75 from 48.56 to 49.62.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TD-DETR outperforms all 15 compared baselines on both QVHighlights and Charades-STA. On QVHighlights test, it leads BAM-DETR (the closest competitor) by 1.73 in R1@0.7 and 0.99 in mAP@0.75, and surpasses CG-DETR by 1.97 in R1@0.7 and 4.52 in mAP@0.75. The gap over earlier DETR-based methods is larger: QD-DETR is beaten by 5.39 in R1@0.7 and 7.44 in mAP@0.75, while Moment-DETR trails by 17.35 in R1@0.7. On Charades-STA, TD-DETR edges out BAM-DETR by 0.94 (R1@0.5) and 0.97 (R1@0.7), and leads SnAG by 5.17 and 13.03 respectively. The spurious correlation evaluation (Table 3) confirms that TD-DETR consistently reduces spurious predictions across all three tested architectures (QD-DETR, CG-DETR, BAM-DETR) while simultaneously improving standard mAP, e.g., reducing BAM-DETR's Spurious R1@0.9 from 1.87 to 0.52 (72.7% reduction) and boosting its mAP@0.75 from 48.56 to 49.62.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DETR-based moment retrieval lineage (Moment-DETR, QD-DETR, BAM-DETR) by identifying spurious correlation between text queries and background frames as a root cause of inaccurate span predictions. While prior analyses (Otani et al. on query-dependent biases, Zhang et al. on moment distribution imbalance) touched on related issues, this work specifically isolates the text-background association problem and proposes a dynamic learning framework combining video synthesis for contextual variation with temporal dynamics enhancement via cross-attention. The method is architecture-agnostic, demonstrated by integration into both DETR-like and hybrid (SnAG) backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of a Spurious mAP evaluation protocol (masking target moments and measuring residual prediction confidence) provides the field with a diagnostic tool to quantify how much models rely on background cues rather than actual content. The finding that SOTA models still confidently predict masked segments reveals a previously underappreciated failure mode. However, the video synthesis strategy depends on batch-level similarity sampling, which may be less effective in diverse or small-batch settings, and the spurious evaluation, while informative, is a somewhat artificial stress test rather than a natural distribution shift. Open questions include whether the temporal dynamics tokenizer generalizes to longer videos or more complex multi-moment queries.
