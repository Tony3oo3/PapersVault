## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models inherit and amplify demographic biases (gender, race) from training data, yet existing debiasing methods either require expensive annotated reference datasets for retraining or depend on additional attribute classifiers whose accuracy caps debiasing performance. No prior approach achieves strong debiasing while being simultaneously lightweight, plug-and-play, and free of both reference datasets and external classifiers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250560/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a text prompt (e.g., 'a photo of a CEO') and a prescribed attribute distribution (e.g., uniform over male/female) as input, and produces a set of generated images whose attribute composition matches the prescribed distribution. The learned artifact is a set of lightweight 1-dim adapter vectors (one per attribute category) attached to cross-attention layers, plus a one-hot distribution indicator that selects which adapter to activate per image. The adapters are trained once per base model and can be transferred to other model versions without retraining.

## Method

FairGen attaches a 1-dim adapter (two vectors p, q) to each cross-attention layer of the denoising U-Net, one adapter per attribute category (e.g., male, female). Adapters are trained via a self-discovering noise composition objective: the adapter-equipped model's noise prediction is regressed toward a composed target ε_θ(X_t, g_t, t) + η(ε_θ(X_t, d, t) − ε_θ(X_t, g_t, t)), derived from Bayes' rule and Tweedie's formula, requiring no reference dataset or external classifier. At inference, a one-hot distribution indicator sampled from the prescribed distribution selects the corresponding adapter, which is added to the U-Net weights with a scaling factor α. For intersectional debiasing across multiple attributes, an orthogonal regularization loss enforces that adapter subspaces for different attributes remain orthogonal, preventing interference. The entire pipeline is plug-and-play: the base diffusion model remains frozen, and the adapters can be transferred across model versions with minimal performance loss.

## Results

On gender debiasing with Stable Diffusion v2.1, FairGen achieves a Fairness Discrepancy (FD) of 0.003 under a uniform target distribution (0.5, 0.5), reducing the original SD bias (FD = 0.424) by over 99%, while maintaining CLIPsim at 0.38 and BRISQUE at 38.46, matching the original model. For racial debiasing, FairGen reaches FD = 0.095 (uniform) versus 0.384 for original SD, and for intersectional (gender + race) debiasing, FD drops to 0.047 versus 0.214. The method also transfers across SD versions (v1.4–v2.1) with only marginal FD increases (≤0.005).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FairGen outperforms all five baselines on FD across every debiasing task. On gender debiasing (uniform), FairGen achieves FD = 0.003, beating the closest competitor ITI-GEN (0.093) by a factor of ~31×, and F4Fair (0.165) by ~55×. On racial debiasing, FairGen (FD = 0.095) surpasses ITI-GEN (0.131) and H Guidance (0.150), while UCE (0.290) and InterDiff (0.150) trail further behind. For intersectional debiasing, FairGen (FD = 0.047) nearly halves the best baseline ITI-GEN (0.098) and more than quadruples the improvement over F4Fair (0.145). Crucially, FairGen maintains CLIPsim at 0.38 (gender) and 0.46 (racial), matching or exceeding the original SD, whereas ITI-GEN and H Guidance sacrifice 0.05–0.07 CLIPsim. BRISQUE scores for FairGen (38.46–39.50) are at or above the original SD, while UCE and ITI-GEN degrade image quality by 1–2 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

FairGen builds on the 1-dim adapter architecture from Lyu et al. (2024) and the noise-composition framework derived from classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022), repurposing both for a debiasing objective rather than concept erasure or precise concept control. It departs from training-based debiasing methods (F4Fair, ITI-GEN) that require curated reference datasets, and from Distribution Guidance (Parihar et al.) that depends on an externally trained attribute classifier, by learning attribute latent directions entirely through self-discovery in the model's own latent space. The orthogonal regularization for multi-attribute debiasing extends the single-adapter formulation to handle intersectional bias without cross-attribute interference, a setting not addressed by prior closed-form editing or h-space manipulation approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for annotated reference datasets or auxiliary classifiers, FairGen substantially lowers the barrier to deploying fairer text-to-image generation in production settings, and its plug-and-play, transferable design means a single training run on one model version can be applied across others. However, the evaluation is confined to the Stable Diffusion family (v1.3–v2.1) and to gender/race attributes on occupational prompts, leaving open whether the self-discovering noise-composition objective generalizes to other model architectures (e.g., DiT-based models), more complex or continuous attributes, and non-English prompts. A key open question is whether the learned latent directions remain stable as base models are updated or fine-tuned, and whether the approach can be extended to mitigate biases that are not cleanly separable into discrete categories.
