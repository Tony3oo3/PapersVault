## Problem

Existing RVOS methods struggle with complex compositional descriptions, relative spatial relationships, and dynamic motion attributes due to limited vision-language understanding from small-scale training data. Foundational visual grounding models like GroundingDINO possess strong region-level language-vision alignment but lack pixel-level dense prediction and cross-modal spatiotemporal reasoning, making direct application to RVOS impractical. Prior attempts to combine grounding models with segmentation (e.g., GroundingDINO + SAM2) are non-differentiable ensembles that prevent end-to-end task-specific refinement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251500/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a T-frame video (evaluated at 360P) and a natural-language text description as input, producing per-frame binary segmentation masks, bounding boxes, and classification scores for each of N_s object queries. At inference, the best object trajectory is selected by highest average classification score across frames; for multi-object datasets like MeViS, all trajectories exceeding a score threshold (0.3) are retained. The entire pipeline is end-to-end differentiable, allowing segmentation gradients to propagate back into the grounding box head for collaborative learning.

## Method

ReferDINO builds on pretrained GroundingDINO (Swin-T/B backbone + BERT text encoder, fine-tuned with LoRA rank 32) and adds three components. First, a grounding-guided deformable mask decoder cascades box prediction as a location prior into a deformable cross-attention mechanism (using the predicted box center as the reference point for adaptive sampling on a shared FPN feature map) followed by cross-modal attention with text features, forming a differentiable grounding→deformation→segmentation pipeline. Second, an object-consistent temporal enhancer aligns objects across frames via a memory-augmented tracker (Hungarian matching + momentum update weighted by text relevance) and performs inter-frame interaction through a cross-modal temporal decoder that uses time-varying [CLS] sentence embeddings as queries against object embeddings. Third, a confidence-aware query pruning strategy progressively retains only 1/k high-confidence queries at each decoder layer (using a composite score from self-attention and cross-attention weights), reducing 900 initial queries to a compact set and cutting 40.6% FLOPs while achieving 51 FPS.

## Results

With a Swin-B backbone, ReferDINO achieves 69.3% J&F on Ref-YouTube-VOS (+2.1% over the previous SOTA LoSh at 67.2%), 68.9% J&F on Ref-DAVIS17, 61.1 mAP on A2D-Sentences, and 74.2 mAP on JHMDB-Sentences. On MeViS, it reaches 49.3% J&F, surpassing the prior best (DsHmp at 46.4% with Swin-T) by 2.9%. With the smaller Swin-T backbone, ReferDINO still leads by 3.8% J&F on Ref-YouTube-VOS (67.5 vs. 63.7) and 1.6% on MeViS (48.0 vs. 46.4). The confidence-aware query pruning strategy reduces FLOPs by 40.6% and memory by 41.3% while maintaining performance, achieving 51.0 FPS (a 10× speedup over the unpruned 4.9 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReferDINO outperforms all eight baselines across every evaluated dataset and both backbone sizes. Against the strongest prior RVOS methods (LoSh, DsHmp, SOC), it gains +2.1–2.2% J&F on Ref-YouTube-VOS (Swin-B), +1.4–4.0% J&F on Ref-DAVIS17, +1.3 mAP on A2D-Sentences, and +0.3–0.4 mAP on JHMDB-Sentences. On MeViS, it surpasses DsHmp (46.4% J&F, Swin-T) by 1.6% and the GroundingDINO+Dynamic-Head baseline (47.9%, Swin-B) by 1.4%. The two GroundingDINO baselines (G-DINO+SH, G-DINO+DH) that simply attach a mask head and temporal self-attention to the pretrained detector achieve competitive but inconsistent results (e.g., G-DINO+DH reaches 67.6% J&F on Ref-DAVIS17, close to ReferDINO's 68.9%), confirming that trivial adaptation cannot fully exploit the pretrained grounding knowledge. Earlier methods (ReferFormer, HTML, SgMg, MTTR, LMPM) trail by 4–17% J&F depending on the dataset, highlighting the gap between DETR-based RVOS models and grounding-foundation-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReferDINO builds directly on GroundingDINO, a DETR-based visual grounding foundational model pretrained on large-scale image-text data, and adapts it end-to-end to referring video object segmentation. It departs from prior RVOS methods (MTTR, ReferFormer, SOC, DsHmp) that rely on smaller-scale vision-language pretraining and struggle with compositional descriptions, and from prior attempts to combine GroundingDINO with video tasks (e.g., Video-GroundingDINO, which merely inserts temporal self-attention) by introducing a grounding-guided deformable mask decoder and an object-consistent temporal enhancer that are jointly trained with the grounding backbone. The confidence-aware query pruning strategy further distinguishes the work by addressing the computational bottleneck of large query sets in foundational detectors during video-scale processing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates a viable paradigm for transferring open-world visual grounding knowledge into pixel-level video segmentation, potentially reducing the data bottleneck that has limited RVOS progress. The 10× inference speedup (4.9→51 FPS) with negligible quality loss makes real-time referring video segmentation practically feasible, which is important for interactive video applications. Open questions include whether this adaptation strategy generalizes to other foundational models (e.g., SAM-based or diffusion-based), whether the query pruning principle transfers to other DETR-family architectures, and whether the approach scales to longer videos or more complex multi-object referring scenarios beyond the current benchmarks.
