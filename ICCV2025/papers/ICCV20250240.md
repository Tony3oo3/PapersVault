## Problem

The self-attention mechanism in transformers suffers from inherent ill-conditioning that hampers gradient-based optimization, yet its role in attention remains largely unexplored compared to feedforward networks. This paper establishes a direct theoretical link between the condition number of the first-layer self-attention matrix and that of the embedded token matrix, revealing that ill-conditioned embeddings propagate into poorly conditioned attention computations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250240/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates as a drop-in modification at the embedding stage: standard inputs (RGB images split into 16×16 or 4×4 patches, sub-word text tokens, or flattened long-range sequences) are embedded into high-dimensional vectors as usual, but a correction matrix C derived from the SVD of the embedded token matrix X is added before the first transformer layer. The output format is unchanged from the base architecture—class labels, bounding boxes with masks, or next-token predictions—so the method is architecture-agnostic and compatible with any attention variant (standard, cross-covariance, Nyström-approximated, dual-attention).

## Method

The paper proves that the condition number of linear self-attention satisfies κ(LA(X)) ≤ κ(W_Q)κ(W_K)κ(W_V)κ(X)³, and for softmax attention κ(A(X)) ≤ κ(softmax(XW_QW_K^TX^T))κ(X)κ(W_V), establishing that the embedded token condition number κ(X) is the dominant controllable factor. Theorem 4.4 then shows that whenever κ(X) > 2, a correction matrix C exists such that κ(X+C) ≤ 2, with C constructed from the singular value decomposition of X to selectively adjust singular values. In practice, this correction is computed once from the embedded token matrix and added before the first transformer layer, after which the modified tokens propagate through all subsequent layers, empirically reducing condition numbers throughout the network. The approach requires no architectural changes, no additional learnable parameters, and no modification to the attention computation itself, making it a simple additive enhancement validated across ViT, DeiT, Swin, XCiT, DaViT, Crammed BERT, GPT-2, and Nyströmformer.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k image classification, conditioned embedded tokens improve top-1 accuracy across all five vision transformer architectures tested: ViT-Base (80.3→81.3%), DeiT-Base (81.6→82.5%), Swin-Base (83.1→83.9%), XCiT-Medium (82.2→82.9%), and DaViT-Base (83.6→84.6%). On the GLUE benchmark, Crammed BERT with conditioned tokens improves over the original on all 8 tasks (e.g., MRPC 85.0→86.3, CoLA 48.9→53.7, GLUE average 78.6→79.7). On the LRA benchmark, the Nyströmformer with conditioned tokens outperforms the original on all 5 tasks, with the largest gain on Pathfinder (72.9→80.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares conditioned embedded tokens against the unmodified (original) version of each architecture, serving as a direct ablation of the proposed correction term. On ImageNet-1k, the method consistently wins: ViT-Base improves by +1.0 pp, DeiT-Base by +0.9 pp, Swin-Base by +0.8 pp, XCiT-Medium by +0.7 pp, and DaViT-Base by +1.0 pp. On COCO object detection/segmentation, XCiT-S with conditioned tokens gains +0.8 AP^b and +0.3 AP^m, while XCiT-M gains +0.5 AP^b and +0.6 AP^m. On GLUE, Crammed BERT with conditioned tokens outperforms the original on every task, with the largest margin on CoLA (+4.8) and MRPC (+1.3). On the LRA benchmark, the Nyströmformer with conditioned tokens beats the original on all five tasks, most dramatically on Pathfinder (+7.1 pp, 72.9→80.0). The method never loses to any baseline, confirming its role as a universal drop-in enhancement across diverse attention mechanisms (standard self-attention, shifted-window, cross-covariance, dual-attention, and Nyström-approximated attention).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on conditioning neural network components for improved optimization—specifically the weight-matrix conditioning of Agarwal et al. [20] and the NTK conditioning framework of Liu et al. [14]—from feedforward architectures to the self-attention mechanism in transformers. The key conceptual departure is targeting the embedded token matrix rather than the learnable weight matrices: the authors show that the condition number of the first-layer self-attention matrix is bounded by a power of the condition number of the embedded tokens, so an SVD-derived correction to the tokens propagates through the attention computation. Unlike prior conditioning work that modifies internal weights, this approach operates at the input boundary of the transformer, making it a drop-in compatible with diverse attention variants (Swin, XCiT, DaViT, Nyströmformer) without altering their internal structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a previously underappreciated source of optimization difficulty in transformers—the ill-conditioning of the self-attention matrix driven by the embedded tokens—and provides a simple, architecture-agnostic fix. However, a significant theoretical gap remains: the link between reduced condition number and improved gradient-based convergence is only empirically demonstrated, as a full proof would require analyzing the NTK of a transformer, which the authors acknowledge is 'a notoriously difficult task.' The propagation of improved conditioning to deeper layers is also shown only empirically. Open questions include whether the SVD-based correction can be made learnable or adaptive during training, and how the conditioning interacts with the NTK in the infinite-width limit for transformers specifically.
