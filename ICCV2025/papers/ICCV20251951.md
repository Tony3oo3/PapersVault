## Problem

Vehicle detectors trained on aerial imagery from one geographic region degrade significantly when deployed in another due to domain shifts in urban layout, lighting, resolution, and vehicle types. Off-the-shelf generative models lack sufficient aerial imagery representation to synthesize diverse, high-quality annotated training data for small objects like vehicles, and existing domain adaptation and open-set detection methods achieve unsatisfactory performance on aerial views.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251951/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes fully annotated source-domain aerial patches (112×112 px, GSD 12.5 cm/px, with bounding boxes) and target-domain patches with only binary image-level labels (vehicle present or absent), plus text prompts encoding domain identity. It produces a fully annotated synthetic target-domain dataset where bounding boxes are derived automatically from stacked multi-channel cross-attention maps, and a trained detector that can be evaluated with standard AP50 metrics after converting location labels to 42.36 px pseudo-bounding boxes.

## Method

The pipeline fine-tunes Stable Diffusion V1.4's U-Net on both source and target aerial images using distinct text prompts, then introduces three learnable tokens—[V1] for the object concept, [V2] for source background, and [V3] for target background—trained with a novel cross-attention regularization loss that minimizes total variation distance between the object token's attention map and the category word's map while maximizing distance between background tokens and the category map. Training proceeds in two stages: jointly optimizing the U-Net and tokens, then freezing the tokens and re-fine-tuning the U-Net to align with the learned prompt distribution. For automatic labeling, cross-attention maps from all three tokens are stacked into multi-channel grayscale inputs; a detector is first trained on synthetic source-domain attention maps with pseudo labels from a source-trained detector, then applied to target-domain attention maps to produce bounding boxes, with a classifier refinement step filtering intermediate-confidence predictions to improve label reliability.

## Results

On the cross-domain vehicle detection task (LINZ→UGRC and DOTA→UGRC), the proposed method achieves AP50 of 75.4% and 75.7% using YOLOv8, and 69.3% and 75.5% using Faster-RCNN. These results surpass the best supervised source-only baseline (YOLOv8: 62.9%/71.4%) by 4–23%, the best weakly supervised method (OCUD: 63.1%/65.3%) by 6–10%, the best unsupervised domain adaptation method (CycleGAN-Turbo: 56.0%/60.8%) by 7–40%, and the best open-set detector (OWLV2: 17.9%) by more than 50%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against supervised source-only detectors, the method improves AP50 by 4–23% depending on backbone and source domain (e.g., YOLOv8: 75.4% vs. 62.9% for LINZ→UGRC; Faster-RCNN: 75.5% vs. 52.5% for DOTA→UGRC). Open-set detectors (GLIP-T, OmDet-Turbo, OWLV2) perform very poorly on aerial imagery (8.7–17.9% AP50), and the proposed method exceeds them by over 50 points, confirming that foundational models lack sufficient aerial-domain representation. Among unsupervised domain adaptation methods, CycleGAN-Turbo (56.0%/60.8%) and TIA (49.2%/42.4%) are the strongest baselines; the proposed method outperforms them by 12.8%/7.7% (YOLOv5) and 20.1%/33.1% (Faster-RCNN) respectively. For weakly supervised cross-domain detection, OCUD (63.1%/65.3%) and H2FA R-CNN (61.8%/68.3%) are the closest competitors; the proposed method surpasses them by 6.2–12.3% (LINZ→UGRC) and 7.2–10.4% (DOTA→UGRC) with Faster-RCNN and YOLOv8 backbones. Notably, the method's gains are consistent across all four detector architectures tested (Faster-RCNN, YOLOv5, YOLOv8, ViTDet), indicating the synthetic data augmentation is architecture-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of diffusion-based synthetic data generation (DatasetDM, Dataset Diffusion, DiffusionEngine) from general-purpose benchmarks like COCO and VOC to the underexplored domain of aerial vehicle detection, where off-the-shelf generative models fail due to insufficient aerial imagery in their training data. It builds on the cross-attention map interpretation of Stable Diffusion (Tang et al.) and the learnable token mechanism from AttnDreamBooth, but departs by introducing a two-stage fine-tuning with separate object and background tokens, a novel total-variation-distance regularization loss on attention maps, and a multi-stage knowledge transfer pipeline that trains a detector on style-less cross-attention maps to bridge the source-to-target domain gap. Unlike prior cross-domain detection work that relies on feature alignment or adversarial training, this approach treats the generative model as a domain-bridging data engine rather than a direct adaptation mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of two large-scale, manually annotated aerial datasets (LINZ with 2.07M images and UGRC with 2.68M images) fills a significant gap in publicly available aerial vehicle detection benchmarks, and the consistent 4–23% AP50 gains over source-only supervised baselines across four detector architectures validate the generative augmentation strategy. However, the method is constrained to 112×112 px patches to work around Stable Diffusion's limited cross-attention resolution, and it struggles with overlapping objects where attention maps merge. An open question is whether the pipeline can be made fully unsupervised by replacing the weak binary target labels with VLLM-based vehicle presence detection, as the authors suggest for future work.
