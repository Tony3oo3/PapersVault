## Problem

Existing 3D human-object interaction (HOI) methods model only one-way conditionals—either human pose given an object or object pose given a human—requiring a separate tailored model, architecture, and training procedure for each direction. No prior work captures the full joint distribution P(H, O, I) of human, object, and interaction simultaneously, limiting flexibility for applications like scene population, partial-observation completion, and unconditional generation.

## Input / Output

The only mandatory input is the object's canonical geometry (PointNeXt features plus a one-hot class label); human pose (SMPL+H θ, β, 6-DoF global pose), object 6-DoF pose, and interaction (a 128-dim latent code derived from either a text description via CLIP or a binary 690-vertex contact map) are optional conditions whose presence is controlled by setting their diffusion timestep to 0. The model outputs all three modalities jointly: full-body SMPL+H parameters, a 6-DoF object transform, and the interaction latent, from which a contact map can be decoded. The interaction representation is the key design choice: text and contact maps are mapped into a shared 128-dim latent space so users can control interactions intuitively via language while the model retains spatial contact information.

## Method

TriDi formulates a three-way (trilateral) diffusion process over tokenized representations of human, object, and interaction, extending the UniDiffuser paradigm from two to three modalities. A single transformer with token-level self-attention attends across all three modality tokens, learning fine-grained cross-modal dependencies; the object's canonical geometry is injected as a fixed conditioning signal. The interaction modality is encoded by a dedicated Contact-Text module: two encoders (one for binary contact maps, one for CLIP text embeddings) are jointly trained with a shared 128-dim latent space and a contact-map decoder, using binary cross-entropy and latent-space similarity losses. Training is supervised with an L2 x0-prediction loss over all three modalities plus a distance-based spatial alignment term, and data is augmented by mirroring through the ZY plane to double the dataset and remove right-handed bias. At inference, a reconstruction-guidance step (following Ho et al.) perturbs the denoised sample at each diffusion step using a supervising function that penalizes non-contact vertices, enforcing physical contact between the predicted human and object meshes.

## Results

On BEHAVE (H,I|O), TriDi achieves 1-NNA of 67.89, COV of 47.81%, and MMD of 1.352, outperforming ObjPOP+cVAEGNet (COV −40.71%, MMD 1.789) by up to 47% in coverage. On GRAB (H,I|O), TriDi reaches MPJPE of 15.3 cm and MPJPE-PA of 11.1 cm, substantially better than GNet (26.7 / 15.5 cm). For object prediction (O,I|H), TriDi achieves E_c of 5.0 cm and E_v2v of 6.9 cm on GRAB, beating the regressive ObjPOP (7.7 / 9.4 cm). Contact accuracy reaches 98.0–99.0% across both datasets, with the diffused interaction modality matching or exceeding mesh-inferred contacts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TriDi is compared against two specialized one-way baselines: GNet [69] for human+interaction generation given an object (H,I|O), and Object Pop-up [58] (ObjPOP) for object+interaction generation given a human (O,I|H). On the H,I|O task, TriDi substantially outperforms GNet in spatial accuracy: MPJPE drops from 35.6 to 20.8 cm on BEHAVE and from 26.7 to 15.3 cm on GRAB, with similar gains in MPJPE-PA (12.3 vs 14.6; 11.1 vs 15.5). On the O,I|H task, TriDi beats the regressive ObjPOP on object center error (E_c: 15.3 vs 22.6 cm on BEHAVE; 5.0 vs 7.7 cm on GRAB) and vertex-to-vertex error on GRAB (6.9 vs 9.4 cm), while matching or slightly exceeding ObjPOP on BEHAVE E_v2v (28.0 vs 27.5). In distribution quality (Table 1), TriDi achieves markedly higher COV (up to 47% improvement) and lower MMD than ObjPOP+cVAEGNet across all four dataset/mode combinations, indicating better sample diversity and alignment. Notably, TriDi also matches or slightly exceeds its own single-mode ablations (s-TriDi-HI, s-TriDi-OI), confirming that joint three-way training does not sacrifice per-task performance and in fact improves generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

TriDi extends the UniDiffuser multi-modal diffusion paradigm [3] from two to three modalities, applying it to the joint distribution of human pose, object pose, and interaction. It departs from the dominant one-way HOI literature—where separate models handle human-from-object (GNet [69]) or object-from-human (Object Pop-up [58])—by formulating a single trilateral diffusion that covers all 2³−1 = 7 conditional and joint distributions simultaneously. The contact-text joint embedding space, which unifies CLIP text encodings with binary body contact maps in a shared 128-d latent, is a novel representation not present in prior HOI work, which typically uses either text or contact maps exclusively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By collapsing seven previously separate modeling tasks into one network, TriDi offers a practical unified tool for scene population, interaction reconstruction from partial observations, and synthetic data generation in AR/VR pipelines. The paper's ablations confirm that joint training does not degrade per-mode performance relative to single-mode variants, and the left-right mirroring augmentation (addressing a right-handed bias no prior method tackled) meaningfully improves distribution coverage. Open questions remain around scaling to multi-person/multi-object interactions, handling objects with genuinely novel affordances (wheelchairs, bicycles), and extending beyond single-frame to temporal sequences, all of which the authors flag as future work.
