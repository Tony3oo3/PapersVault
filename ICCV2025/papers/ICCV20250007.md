## Problem

Work zones are a critical long-tail scenario for autonomous driving where foundation models (open-vocabulary detectors, VLMs, text spotters) perform poorly, yet no large-scale public dataset comprehensively covers work zone perception and navigation. Existing driving datasets contain fewer than 1000 work zone images and lack fine-grained annotations such as sign text, scene descriptions, and drivable pathways, leaving the full pipeline from recognition through navigation unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The benchmark spans four tasks with distinct I/O: (1) instance segmentation takes RGB images (smartphone-captured, known intrinsics) and outputs bounding boxes and pixel masks for 15 work zone object categories; (2) sign text reading takes cropped TTC sign regions and outputs transcriptions from 360 text types, evaluated at sentence/word/character level; (3) scene description takes an RGB image optionally augmented with detector predictions and outputs a natural-language work zone description; (4) pathway prediction takes an RGB image plus the observed ego-vehicle trajectory and outputs goal and pathway heatmaps as spatial probability distributions in image space (not BEV), evaluated by angular error thresholds against a ~50° field of view. Pathway ground truth is extracted automatically via COLMAP 3D reconstruction of driving videos, projecting camera poses onto the ground plane.

## Method

The core contribution is the ROADWork dataset (4375 videos, 9650 annotated keyframes, 129K pathway frames from ~5000 work zones across 18 US cities), curated through a three-stage bootstrapping pipeline: manual capture in Pittsburgh, semi-automatic mining from 45M frames in the Michelin Mobility Intelligence dataset, and in-the-wild discovery from BDD100K/Mapillary. Beyond the dataset, the paper demonstrates that simple composition techniques close the gap left by foundation models: SAM2-based video label propagation augments training with 11,959 additional frames (+2.6 AP for segmentation), a crop-rescaling strategy that feeds detector-localized TTC sign crops into the Glass text spotter improves 1-NED by +14.2%, and appending work zone detection predictions as context to a LoRA-fine-tuned LLaVA reduces hallucinations (+3.9 SPICE). For pathway prediction, YNet is trained with work zone semantic segmentations (vs. Cityscapes pretraining), improving angular error < 0.5° by +9.9% for goals and +8.1% for pathways, with the key insight that work zone-specific object semantics are necessary because BEV representations cannot capture the irregular, multi-modal navigational constraints of construction zones.

## Results

On work zone object instance segmentation (coarse vocabulary), zero-shot open-vocabulary foundation models achieve only 2.9–4.2 AP, while Mask DINO fine-tuned on ROADWork reaches 36.4 AP, and adding SAM2 video label propagation pushes this to 39.0 AP. For fine-grained sign segmentation (49 classes), Mask DINO with video propagation achieves 32.8 AP versus 1.1 AP for Detic zero-shot. For scene description generation, fine-tuned LLaVA-1.5-7B with ground-truth object context reaches 49.9 SPICE, compared to 9.9 SPICE for the pretrained model. For pathway prediction, incorporating ROADWork work-zone segmentations yields 53.6% of goals with angular error < 0.5° (+9.9% over Cityscapes-pretrained segmentation).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All three zero-shot open-vocabulary segmentation baselines (OpenSeeD, Detic, OMG-LLaVA) are decisively outperformed by supervised models trained on ROADWork: Mask DINO with video propagation achieves 39.0 AP versus 2.9 (OpenSeeD), 4.2 (Detic), and 0.2 (OMG-LLaVA) AP on coarse work zone objects, and 32.8 AP versus 1.0, 1.1, and 0 AP on fine-grained sign graphics. For sign text reading, the specialized Glass text spotter (65.4 1-NED zero-shot) substantially outperforms GPT-4o (40.3) and Gemini 2.5 Flash (22.7), and the paper's crop-rescaling composition further improves Glass to 79.6 1-NED (+14.2%). For scene descriptions, pretrained BLIP-361M (3.9 SPICE) and LLaVA-1.5-7B (9.9 SPICE) are both far below GPT-4o (16.4 METEOR) and Gemini 2.5 (13.2 METEOR) in zero-shot quality, but fine-tuned LLaVA with ground-truth object context (49.9 SPICE) surpasses all. For work zone discovery on BDD100K and Mapillary, Mask R-CNN trained on ROADWork finds 12.8× and 4.5× more work zone images than Detic with +32.5% and +34.0% higher precision respectively. For pathway prediction, the YNet model using ROADWork work-zone segmentations consistently outperforms the Cityscapes-pretrained variant at all angular error thresholds, with the largest gap (−15.4%) on high-curvature goal predictions at 0.5°.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new domain (work zones) that existing autonomous driving datasets (BDD100K, NuScenes, Waymo, Cityscapes) and long-tail benchmarks (CODA, WildDash) largely ignore, with fewer than 1000 work zone images across all common datasets combined. Methodologically, it repurposes standard tools—Mask R-CNN, Mask DINO, SAM/SAM2 for label propagation, Glass for text spotting, LLaVA/BLIP for captioning, and YNet for pathway prediction—rather than proposing new architectures, positioning the contribution as data-driven. The cognitive framing (recognize → observe → analyze → drive) is a novel organizational lens that maps to existing sub-tasks but has not been previously applied to work zones as a unified benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Work zones cause over 700 deaths annually in the US and are a known failure mode for deployed robotaxis, yet no public dataset has addressed them comprehensively. The paper demonstrates that data scarcity, not model capacity, is the bottleneck: foundation models trained on hundreds of millions of images achieve single-digit AP on work zone objects, while a simple fine-tuned Mask R-CNN reaches 30+ AP. Key limitations include smartphone-only capture (no multi-camera rigs), absence of 3D perception, and incomplete traversable path coverage, leaving 3D work zone understanding and multi-sensor fusion as open challenges. The dataset also enables broader long-tail research, including geographic domain adaptation and label unification between common and rare object vocabularies.
