## Problem

Instruction-based image editing models face a data bottleneck (training datasets like AnyEdit ~2.5M and UltraEdit ~4M are far smaller than T2I corpora like LAION-5B) and require architectural modifications that break compatibility with the broader T2I ecosystem (ControlNet, IP-Adapter). The paper asks whether re-framing editing as description-driven text-to-image generation—describing the desired final state rather than the transformation—can unify editing with T2I while preserving generative quality and plug-and-play compatibility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250652/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference RGB image and a free-form edit description (e.g., 'fireworks are blooming in the sky' rather than 'add fireworks to the sky') as input, producing a single edited RGB image. The edit description can be user-authored or auto-generated from an instruction via an MLLM such as QWen-VL. Both images are processed in the latent space of a frozen Stable Diffusion v1.5 VAE, and the reference image is fed at a fixed clean timestep (s=0) while the edited image undergoes standard diffusion denoising.

## Method

DescriptiveEdit introduces a Cross-Attentive UNet that pairs a frozen denoising UNet with a separate Ref-UNet (weight-copied) to encode the reference image. At each self-attention layer, a newly added cross-attention bridge takes Q from the Ref-UNet and K,V from the denoising UNet's self-attention, injecting low-level structural cues from the reference into the generation process. A zero-initialized learnable linear mapping (Eq. 5) adaptively balances the reference-enhanced feature Z′ against the generative prior Z, preventing the reference from overpowering the edit. Training uses LoRA (rank 64) on the attention bridge layers only (~75M trainable parameters vs. ~860M for IP2P), applies noise exclusively to the edited image (inspired by Diffusion Forcing), and drops conditions at 5% for classifier-free guidance. At inference, a modified guidance formulation (Eq. 7) with separate strengths λ_I and λ_T independently controls reference-image fidelity and text adherence, and the framework extends to DiT-based models like Flux without architectural changes.

## Results

On the Emu Edit benchmark, DescriptiveEdit achieves the best scores on 6 of 8 metrics: L1 (0.065), L2 (0.011), LPIPS (0.139), PSNR (20.99), DINO-I (0.843), and CLIP-I (0.874). It is narrowly outperformed on SSIM by FPE (0.664 vs. 0.661) and on CLIP-T by RF-Edit (0.319 vs. 0.315). Among training-based methods, it leads all consistency and quality metrics while remaining competitive on instruction adherence, demonstrating a stronger overall trade-off than both training-free and training-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DescriptiveEdit outperforms all five training-free baselines (MasaCtrl, RF-Edit, PnPInversion, FPE, TurboEdit) on every consistency and quality metric, with the largest gaps over RF-Edit (LPIPS 0.139 vs. 0.317; DINO-I 0.843 vs. 0.553) and TurboEdit (PSNR 20.99 vs. 17.56). Among training-based methods, it beats AnyEdit† (the closest competitor) on L1 (0.065 vs. 0.067), LPIPS (0.139 vs. 0.147), PSNR (20.99 vs. 19.81), DINO-I (0.843 vs. 0.809), and CLIP-I (0.874 vs. 0.832), and substantially outperforms BrushEdit (L1 0.065 vs. 0.143; PSNR 20.99 vs. 13.51). The only two metrics where it does not rank first are SSIM (FPE: 0.664 vs. 0.661, a 0.003 gap attributed to FPE's spatial-structure preservation bias) and CLIP-T (RF-Edit: 0.319 vs. 0.315, a 0.004 gap the authors attribute to training-free methods prioritizing instruction adherence at the cost of visual consistency). Overall, DescriptiveEdit achieves the best or near-best score on all eight metrics, confirming that the description-based paradigm with the Cross-Attentive UNet yields a more balanced editing result than either training-free or prior training-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes semantic image editing from an instruction-following problem (IP2P, MagicBrush, AnyEdit) into a reference-image-conditioned text-to-image generation problem, leveraging the generative prior of pretrained T2I models without architectural modification. Architecturally, it borrows the separate-UNet feature extraction paradigm from ControlNet but replaces channel-wise concatenation with a lightweight cross-attention bridge, and adapts IP2P's multi-branch classifier-free guidance for inference. The training protocol draws on Diffusion Forcing's independent-noise-level idea to avoid corrupting reference information, while LoRA-based tuning keeps the adaptation parameter-efficient (~75M vs. ~860M for full UNet fine-tuning).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying editing and T2I generation under a single computational framework, DescriptiveEdit sidesteps the data bottleneck of instruction-labeled datasets and preserves full compatibility with the existing diffusion ecosystem (ControlNet, IP-Adapter, Flux/DiT), which is a practical advantage for practitioners building on top of Stable Diffusion or Flux. However, the approach still depends on the quality of the edit description—whether user-written or auto-generated by an MLLM like QWen-VL—and the paper does not deeply explore failure modes when descriptions are ambiguous or when edits require precise spatial reasoning beyond what a global attention bridge can capture. Open questions include whether the description-based paradigm scales to multi-step or compositional edits, and whether the attention-bridge mechanism generalizes to video or 3D editing.
