## Problem

Existing implicit 3D morphable models for head modeling (e.g., NPHM, imFace) rely on entangled latent spaces that prohibit localized facial editing, and are trained on datasets of fewer than 300 identities, limiting their ability to capture real-world shape diversity. Traditional PCA-based 3DMMs, while scalable, are linear and cannot represent high-frequency geometric details or complex full-head topology without labor-intensive correspondence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250203/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model is an auto-decoder that takes a compact 256-dimensional identity latent code and an expression latent code as conditioning, and predicts the signed distance field at any queried 3D point. For fitting, a raw 3D scan (point cloud) is warped into canonical space via a backward deformation network, eliminating the iterative root-finding step required by forward-deformation baselines. The decomposition network produces K=39 region-specific embeddings that can be independently sampled or swapped to perform localized edits without affecting the rest of the head.

## Method

imHead introduces a two-level disentanglement strategy: a single compact global identity latent z_id is decomposed via a linear DecNet into K=39 local region embeddings, each conditioning a dedicated Local-Part network that operates in its own landmark-centered canonical space. A LandmarkNet regresses per-region keypoints from the local embeddings, and a FusionNet aggregates the resulting local features using distance-weighted blending (rather than direct field blending) to predict a smooth global SDF, avoiding discontinuities during editing. For expressions, a backward warping network maps observed posed points to canonical space, enabling direct fitting without iterative optimization. The model is trained on a curated 4,000-identity full-head dataset (10× larger than prior implicit head models) assembled from MimicMe frontal scans via NPHM fitting and NICP registration, and optimized with SDF reconstruction, Eikonal, landmark, symmetry, and latent regularization losses.

## Results

On the MimicMe test set (250 identities), imHead-Full achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.533 mm, Normal Consistency of 0.986, and F-score@5mm of 0.873, outperforming the previous best implicit model monoNPHM† (CD 0.593, NC 0.968, F@5mm 0.829) and all explicit 3DMMs. On the smaller NPHM test set (23 identities), imHead-Full reaches CD 0.459, NC 0.988, and F@5mm 0.898. The model also achieves a highly compact 256-dim identity latent, which is 8.5× smaller than monoNPHM (2176-dim) and 5× smaller than NPHM, while enabling 3× faster fitting (40 s vs. 138 s) thanks to backward expression warping.

## Baselines & Comparisons

imHead-Full outperforms all eight baselines on both the NPHM and MimicMe test sets across all three metrics (CD, NC, F@5mm). Against the closest implicit competitors, imHead-Full reduces Chamfer Distance by ~0.06 mm over monoNPHM† (0.533 vs. 0.593) and NPHM† (0.533 vs. 0.598) on MimicMe, while improving F@5mm by ~0.044 over both. The gap widens substantially against NPM† (CD 0.672) and imFace (CD 0.946), which model only 5 frontal regions. Among explicit 3DMMs, FLAME (CD 1.336) and LSFM (CD 1.231) are the strongest but remain far behind all implicit methods, while BFM (CD 2.794) and PCA (CD 1.621) show the weakest reconstruction. Notably, even imHead trained only on the small NPHM dataset (imHead-NPHM, CD 0.571 on MimicMe) already surpasses NPHM† and monoNPHM†, demonstrating that the architectural contribution (DecNet + FusionNet) is effective independent of data scale, and the full 4K-identity dataset provides an additional ~0.04 CD improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

imHead extends the NPHM (Giebenhain et al., CVPR 2023) architecture for neural parametric head models, which introduced local-part networks conditioned on landmark-defined regions and a global identity latent. The key departure is replacing NPHM's direct latent partitioning (splitting identity into K local vectors plus a global vector) with a single compact global identity latent that is decomposed via a lightweight DecNet into region-specific embeddings, preserving both compactness and local editability. The implicit SDF auto-decoder paradigm follows DeepSDF (Park et al., 2019), and the backward expression warping contrasts with the forward deformation used in NPHM/NPM. The paper also draws on SPAGHETTI's intermediate part-level representation concept, though applied to a single global latent rather than independent part latents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By scaling to 4,000 identities (10× prior implicit head datasets), imHead moves implicit 3DMMs closer to the representational coverage of large-scale explicit models like LSFM, addressing a key barrier to real-world deployment in gaming and VR avatar pipelines. The localized editing capability—emerging naturally from the DecNet architecture without additional constraints—opens practical workflows for per-region face manipulation that were previously infeasible in implicit models. However, the model still inherits implicit-representation limitations (slow inference, difficulty capturing hair strands), and the curated dataset retains racial biases inherited from NPHM and MimicMe. Open questions include whether the approach scales to texture modeling, real-time inference, and whether the 39-region partitioning is optimal or should be learned end-to-end.
