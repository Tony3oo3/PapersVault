## Problem

Prior instruction-based image editing methods either fine-tune diffusion models end-to-end (lacking interpretability and struggling with abstract or multi-step instructions) or chain separate single-modality models for understanding and generation, which restricts editing quality on complex real-world scenes. The paper addresses the gap of bridging scene understanding and generation through a unified multi-modality reasoning pipeline that can handle abstract concepts, multi-action instructions, and precise spatial localization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252257/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (processed at 256×256) and a natural-language editing instruction as input, optionally augmented with a global image description for the planning stage. It produces a final edited image as output, with intermediate artifacts including a chain of decomposed sub-prompts (up to K=3) and per-sub-instruction editing region masks generated by the MLLM reasoner. The editing is applied iteratively: each sub-instruction edits the previous step's output, so the pipeline is sequential rather than parallel.

## Method

The framework has three stages: (1) a CoT Planner using DeepSeek's reasoning model decomposes the user instruction into concrete sub-prompts via chain-of-thought prompting, incorporating the editing network's capabilities as prior knowledge; (2) an MLLM reasoner (LLaVA-7B with LoRA adapters + SAM decoder, trained on MagicBrush masks) localizes the editing region for each sub-prompt by cross-modal reasoning over the image and instruction; (3) a hint-guided Stable Diffusion U-Net (initialized from InstructPix2Pix) performs the actual edit by conditioning on the foreground image (image × mask), background image (image × (1−mask)), and text instruction simultaneously, with classifier-free guidance extended to all three conditions. The foreground/background split is encoded into latent space and concatenated as spatial conditions to the denoising network, requiring only modification of the first convolution layer. Training uses the MagicBrush dataset (4,600 pairs augmented 5× to 78,000) with ground-truth masks, while inference uses the MLLM-predicted masks.

## Results

On the MagicBrush test set, the full framework achieves a total score of 0.5904, outperforming MagicBrush (0.5853), InstructDiffusion (0.5754), InstructPix2Pix (0.5457), and HIVE (0.5493). The method leads on image-similarity metrics (CLIP-I: 0.9172, DINO-I: 0.8658) but trails MagicBrush slightly on text-similarity metrics (CLIP-T Global: 0.2995 vs. 0.3035; CLIP-T Local: 0.2789 vs. 0.2855). On the HQEdit-Abstract user study, the full framework achieves the highest abstract-concept score (29.41% vs. 23.01% for HQEdit and 22.76% for MagicBrush), though its editing-quality score (27.10%) is marginally below the no-planning variant (28.64%) due to cumulative quality degradation across iterative edits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against InstructPix2Pix and HIVE, the proposed method wins decisively on every metric (total score 0.5904 vs. 0.5457 and 0.5493 respectively), confirming that the MLLM-reasoned editing regions and CoT planning provide substantial gains over end-to-end instruction editing without explicit spatial control. Compared to InstructDiffusion, the method also leads across all metrics (0.5904 vs. 0.5754 total). The closest competitor is MagicBrush: the proposed method surpasses it on CLIP-I (0.9172 vs. 0.9080) and DINO-I (0.8658 vs. 0.8443), indicating better preservation of non-edited regions, but falls slightly behind on CLIP-T Global (0.2995 vs. 0.3035) and CLIP-T Local (0.2789 vs. 0.2855), suggesting the iterative multi-step editing introduces minor text-alignment drift. On the HQEdit-Abstract user study, the full framework achieves the highest abstract-concept score (29.41%) by a wide margin over HQEdit (23.01%) and MagicBrush (22.76%), demonstrating the value of CoT planning for instantiating abstract instructions, though its editing-quality vote (27.10%) is slightly below the no-planning ablation (28.64%) due to quality loss from sequential generation steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against two lines of prior work on instruction-based image editing: end-to-end fine-tuning of diffusion models (InstructPix2Pix, MagicBrush) and MLLM-augmented editing that replaces text embeddings with multi-modal language model outputs (SmartEdit, Fu et al.). It departs from both by explicitly decomposing the task into a three-stage pipeline—CoT planning, MLLM-based editing region reasoning (inspired by LISA's reasoning segmentation), and a hint-guided diffusion editor—treating the MLLM as a bridge between understanding and generation rather than a direct conditioning signal. The hint-guided editing network, which concatenates foreground and background latents as spatial conditions in the denoising U-Net, is a new architectural choice distinct from ControlNet-style adapters or attention-modulation approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular, interpretable decomposition makes editing hints (sub-prompts, masks) inspectable and user-editable, which is practically valuable for interactive editing products. However, the paper acknowledges that iterative generation degrades image quality, and the MLLM reasoner still produces inaccurate editing regions (ground-truth masks outperform learned ones in ablation). Open questions include whether the framework scales to more complex multi-object edits without quality loss, and whether the CoT planner's gains on abstract concepts generalize beyond the small 100-sample HQEdit-Abstract evaluation. Extending the full pipeline to Flux-based backbones is noted as future work.
