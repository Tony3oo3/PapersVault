## Problem

Current LLMs excel at structured 'if A, then B' reasoning (math, code) but struggle with implicit, non-physical, heuristic reasoning that connects concrete visuals to abstract concepts. No comprehensive video QA benchmark existed for the advertising domain, where understanding requires interpreting marketing logic, persuasive strategies, and audience engagement beyond surface-level physical content. This paper introduces AdsQA to probe this reasoning boundary and proposes a method to close the gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252010/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an ad video (15–120 seconds, drawn from 1,544 unique ads totaling 22.7 hours) and a free-text question as input. During training, each video is decomposed into a modality-interleaved sequence of SSIM-sampled keyframes, Video-LLM-generated clip descriptions, and Whisper-extracted ASR transcripts, alongside creator-provided metadata. The model outputs a structured response containing a free-text reasoning process within <think> tags followed by a final answer within <answer> tags, evaluated across five open-ended tasks (Visual Concept Understanding, Emotion Recognition, Theme Extraction, Persuasion Strategy Mining, Audience Modeling) using GPT-4o-based strict and relaxed accuracy scoring.

## Method

ReAd-R is a DeepSeek-R1–styled reinforcement learning reasoner built on Qwen2-VL-7B (frozen visual backbone, fine-tuned language model) that learns ad-video reasoning through outcome-reward-driven optimization rather than supervised chain-of-thought. The reward model is a rule-guided LLM evaluator applying inclusion rules (generated answer must incorporate elements of the ground-truth answer, with partial credit of 0.5) and exclusion rules (penalizing hallucinated content not inferable from metadata), plus a format reward enforcing the <think>/<answer> structure. Training uses GRPO: the policy generates n distinct responses per question, the reward model scores them, advantages are computed via group-normalized rewards, and the policy is updated with KL regularization against a reference model. Notably, ReAd-R is trained on only 500 curated QA pairs from 100 videos, and the paper shows that SFT on the same data actually degrades performance while RL generalizes better, and that fixed COT templates (e.g., Video-of-Thought) accumulate errors and underperform the free-form RL-trained reasoning.

## Results

On AdsQA, ReAd-R (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) achieves 25.0% strict accuracy and 51.5% relaxed accuracy overall, outperforming the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (23.0%/48.9%) and all reasoning-based baselines (VOT: 17.0%/40.2%, EvolAgent: 18.3%/42.6%, MCTSr: 17.1%/43.4%). GPT-4o reaches 29.4% strict / 56.6% relaxed, and Gemini-2.5-Pro leads among commercial models at 35.9%/60.7%, while human annotators score 51.3%/71.4%. The largest gains from ReAd-R appear on Emotion Recognition (+4.5% strict over Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and Persuasion Strategy Mining (+1.6% strict), confirming that RL-based reasoning helps most on tasks requiring implicit, non-physical interpretation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReAd-R (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) outperforms all same-size open-source Video-LLMs: it beats Qwen2.5-VL-7B by +2.0% strict / +2.6% relaxed, Qwen2-VL-7B by +7.8% / +10.5%, and LLaVA-OneVision-7B by +11.0% / +12.4%. Against reasoning-based methods built on Qwen2-VL-7B, ReAd-R (Qwen2-VL-7B) surpasses VOT by +1.6% strict / +4.7% relaxed, EvolAgent by +0.3% / +2.3%, and MCTSr by +1.5% / +1.5%, despite using only 500 QA pairs for RL training. SFT on the same data actually degrades Qwen2-VL-7B by 2.2% strict (14.1% vs. 17.2%), confirming that supervised fine-tuning overfits rather than generalizes. However, ReAd-R still trails the larger commercial models: GPT-4o leads by 4.4% strict / 5.1% relaxed, and Gemini-2.5-Pro by 10.9% / 9.2%, indicating that model scale and proprietary training data remain significant advantages. The paper positions ReAd-R as SOTA among open-source 7B models and all reasoning-augmented 7B variants, with the gap to 72B Qwen2.5-VL (6.0% strict) suggesting room for further scaling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DeepSeek-R1/GRPO reinforcement learning paradigm for reasoning from math and code domains to open-ended video question answering, specifically the advertising domain. It departs from the dominant chain-of-thought ('if A, then B') reasoning framework that underpins methods like VOT and MCTSr, arguing that ad understanding requires associative, heuristic reasoning that resists step-wise logical templates. The multi-agent annotation pipeline builds on EvoAgent's role-playing framework, while the benchmark itself fills a gap left by the image-only Pit dataset and general-purpose Video QA benchmarks (NextQA, ActivityQA, VideoMME) that focus on physical-content perception rather than implicit marketing logic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AdsQA is the first comprehensive video QA benchmark for the advertising domain, providing a testbed that exposes a clear gap between frontier models' physical perception and their ability to reason about implicit, non-physical content (persuasion strategies, audience modeling, emotional framing). The finding that SFT degrades performance while RL on only 500 pairs improves it, and that longer chain-of-thought actively hurts, challenges the assumption that structured reasoning universally benefits multimodal understanding. Key limitations include reliance on GPT-4o as both evaluator and (indirectly) data generator, a single base-model family (Qwen2-VL), and very low absolute accuracy across all models, leaving open whether the benchmark is measuring model capability or evaluation noise. Future work should validate findings across architectures and with human-only evaluation.
