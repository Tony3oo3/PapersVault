## Problem

Existing Domain Generalizable Face Anti-Spoofing (DGFAS) methods, such as SAFAS, align classifier weight directions across domains but leave bias terms misaligned, resulting in inconsistent classification thresholds and degraded performance on unseen target domains. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a framework that jointly aligns both weights and biases of local decision boundaries across multiple source domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252269/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB face crops (extracted via MTCNN with 0.6 padding) and produces a live/spoof decision by computing cosine similarity between a learned text embedding (serving as the classifier weight vector) and the image embedding in a CLIP-based text-image space. During training, the model also consumes domain labels to construct group-wise loss groups (Y×E) and to define positive pairs for the domain-specific contrastive objective.

## Method

GD-FAS builds on a CLIP-style text-image embedding architecture and introduces two complementary modules. Group-wise Scaling Risk Minimization (GS-RM) applies a sigmoid-based, loss-adaptive scaling factor to each (class × domain) group's cross-entropy loss, up-weighting high-loss groups and down-weighting low-loss groups so that all domains contribute equally to gradient updates, thereby aligning classifier bias terms. Feature Orthogonal Decomposition (FOD) uses the Gram-Schmidt process to project the image embedding onto the text-embedding basis, yielding a domain-invariant component f_I, and subtracts it to obtain a domain-specific component f_S that is provably orthogonal to f_I; a contrastive InfoNCE loss trained with domain labels then shapes f_S so that domain-specific variation is confined to a subspace orthogonal to the invariant direction, ensuring the inner product f_S·w^T = 0 and thus a consistent zero bias across domains. The total training objective combines the GS-scaled image-text similarity loss, the FOD contrastive loss, an image-image InfoNCE loss for representation learning, and a standard classification loss, all optimized end-to-end.

## Results

On the standard leave-one-out protocol (no extra source data), GD-FAS achieves an average HTER of 1.83% and average AUC of 99.46% across four domain-shift scenarios, outperforming the second-best method (TF-FAS, 2.19% HTER) by 0.36 points. In the OCI→M setting, GD-FAS reaches 0.42% HTER / 99.88% AUC, and with an additional CelebA-Spoof source it achieves a perfect 0.00% HTER on OCI→M and 0.19% on OMI→C (average HTER 0.95%). The method also reports the lowest Expected Calibration Error (ECE) among all compared approaches in 3 of 4 scenarios (e.g., 2.37% on OCM→I vs. 2.62% for FLIP-MCL), confirming better bias-term alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the standard (no-extra-data) protocol, GD-FAS achieves the lowest average HTER (1.83%) among all 11 baselines, beating TF-FAS (2.19%), BUDoPT (2.62%), and CFPL (3.60%); it is best on OCI→M (0.42% vs. 0.95% for BUDoPT) and OMI→C (0.93% vs. 0.81% for TF-FAS, a narrow loss). However, GD-FAS trails TF-FAS on OCM→I (3.33% vs. 2.24%) and ICM→O (2.64% vs. 2.26%), and TF-FAS retains the highest average AUC (99.96% vs. 99.46%). With the extra CelebA-Spoof source, GD-FAS† achieves a perfect 0.00% HTER on OCI→M and the best OMI→C (0.19%), but BUDoPT† edges it out on average HTER (0.91% vs. 0.95%) and on OCM→I (1.38% vs. 1.67%). Older baselines (MMD-AAE, MADDG, SDA, SSDG-R, SSAN-R, PatchNet, SAFAS) are substantially outperformed, with SAFAS showing the largest ECE gap (7.77–18.12% vs. GD-FAS's 2.37–3.39%), confirming the bias-misalignment limitation the paper targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-based DGFAS framework of FLIP-MCL [22] by explicitly decomposing the text-image embedding space into orthogonal domain-invariant and domain-specific subspaces via Gram-Schmidt projection (FOD), rather than relying solely on text embeddings as implicit invariant guidance. It departs from SAFAS [23] by replacing the PG-IRM classifier projection—which aligns weight directions but leaves bias terms misaligned—with a loss-adaptive group-wise scaling mechanism (GS-RM) inspired by class-wise calibration scaling [10], thereby addressing the bias-alignment gap that SAFAS acknowledged but could not resolve. The theoretical grounding draws on Invariant Risk Minimization [2], but the practical relaxation through per-group sigmoid scaling makes the optimization tractable without the hard invariance constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete, previously underexplored failure mode in DGFAS—bias-term misalignment across domain-specific classifiers—providing a principled geometric explanation (orthogonal subspace decomposition) for why weight-only alignment is insufficient. Introducing ECE as a reliability metric for FAS opens a new axis of evaluation beyond HTER/AUC, which could influence how the community assesses cross-domain robustness. Limitations include dependence on CLIP text embeddings as the invariant anchor (raising questions about transferability to non-CLIP backbones), evaluation restricted to four standard FAS datasets, and the observation that FOD alone yields lower ECE than the full model, suggesting a non-trivial trade-off between calibration and accuracy that remains unresolved.
