## Problem

Existing diffusion-based material transfer methods either depend on text-prompt-bound fine-tuning (incurring high training costs and overfitting risk) or employ parallel reference networks like IP-Adapter and ControlNet that cause hierarchical decoupling between material and structural information in the latent space. This decoupling leads to material-geometry misalignment and illumination-reflection inconsistency, while also inflating inference time through additional encoders and DDIM inversion steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251699/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two RGB images as input: a target image (I_input) providing structure and a material image (I_M) providing texture. From the target, it internally derives a depth map and an illumination-preserving grayscale composite (foreground grayscale + background original) without any external segmentation or text prompts. The output is a single 1024×1024 material-transferred image generated in 8 diffusion steps with a classifier-free guidance scale of 30, requiring no fine-tuning, no text conditioning, and no auxiliary reference networks.

## Method

MaTe projects the material, depth, and illumination images into the same VAE latent space as the noise tokens, then processes all four token streams jointly through FLUX.1's multi-modal attention blocks via Unified-Sequence Processing—concatenating condition tokens with image tokens rather than adding them as ControlNet-style residuals. A novel Cross-Bias Modulation mechanism injects a log(γ) bias into the attention matrix to control cross-modal interaction strength at test time without retraining, while a lightweight 620M-parameter depth LoRA (replacing a 1.59B ControlNet) provides geometric guidance. Background-Preserving Blending iteratively re-injects the noised input latent outside the foreground mask at each diffusion step to maintain background consistency. The entire pipeline is training-free and zero-shot, leveraging only the pre-trained FLUX.1 DiT architecture with its existing multi-modal attention and RoPE positional encoding.

## Results

On the MTB dataset, MaTe achieves the best scores across all quantitative metrics: SSIM 0.8617 (vs. 0.8263 for MaterialFusion), LPIPS 0.1319 (vs. 0.1565), and CLIP 0.8825 (vs. 0.8627 for ZeST). In the user study (16 evaluators, 1,632 votes), MaTe dominates with 63.42% preference for texture, 73.71% for structure, and 83.82% for overall quality, far exceeding the next-best method (MaterialFusion at 11.21%/20.04%/10.48%). MaTe also runs in 11 seconds per inference on a single A100 GPU, roughly 4× faster than MaterialFusion (43 s), and is the only method requiring no fine-tuning, no text prompts, and no reference networks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaTe outperforms all five baselines on every quantitative metric (SSIM, LPIPS, CLIP) and every user-study dimension. Against the closest competitor MaterialFusion [9], MaTe gains +0.035 SSIM, −0.025 LPIPS, +0.030 CLIP, and a 4× speedup (11 s vs. 43 s), while also eliminating the need for text prompts and DDIM inversion. ZeST [3] achieves the second-best CLIP score (0.8627) but trails MaTe by 0.020 and is far behind in structure preservation (0.18% vs. 73.71% user preference). IP-Adapter SDXL [43] is competitive on SSIM (0.8152) but lags on LPIPS (0.1876 vs. 0.1319) and requires a reference network. Fine-tuning-based methods (ProSpect [49], U-VAP [42]) show the weakest material transfer effects, with user-study overall preferences below 2%, and additionally incur 200–522 s of preprocessing time for fine-tuning. MaTe is the only method that simultaneously avoids fine-tuning, text conditions, and auxiliary reference networks while achieving the best results.

## Relation to Prior Work

MaTe departs from the dominant paradigm in diffusion-based material transfer, which relies on parallel injection of conditions through separate modules (IP-Adapter for material features, ControlNet for depth, text prompts for semantic guidance) as seen in MaterialFusion, ZeST, and U-VAP. Instead, it follows the emerging trend of eliminating text and auxiliary encoders in image generation (CatVTON, Ctrl-X) by projecting all image conditions—material, illumination, and depth—into the same VAE latent space and processing them as a unified token sequence through FLUX.1's existing multi-modal attention. The only learned component is a lightweight depth LoRA (620M params) replacing a full ControlNet (1.59B params), and a novel Cross-Bias Modulation mechanism provides test-time control over cross-modal attention strength without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing fine-tuning, text prompts, and reference networks entirely, MaTe substantially lowers the barrier to practical material transfer, making it accessible as a zero-shot, single-image tool for design workflows. The released MTB benchmark (60 materials × 30 targets) provides a standardized evaluation that prior work lacked. Key open questions include whether the unified-sequence approach generalizes beyond FLUX.1 to other DiT backbones, how it handles 3D-consistent material transfer (the current method is 2D-to-2D), and whether the semantic-entanglement limitation (material semantics leaking into the target) can be addressed without reintroducing separate encoders.
