## Problem

Existing geospatial foundation models either focus on a narrow set of modalities, rely on contrastive or diffusion-based pretraining, or support only unimodal generation. No prior model achieves any-to-any generative capability across a diverse set of Earth observation modalities (optical, radar, elevation, land cover, vegetation index, text, geolocation) while simultaneously delivering state-of-the-art discriminative performance. TerraMind addresses this by introducing the first fully generative, multimodal model for EO that unifies generation and understanding in a single architecture.

## Input / Output

TerraMind consumes 264×264 pixel patches at 10 m resolution across up to six pixel-level modalities (S-2 L1C, S-2 RGB, S-1 GRD, S-1 RTC, DEM, and optionally LULC/NDVI) and eight token-level modalities (discrete FSQ tokens for each image-like modality plus WordPiece tokens for captions and discretized coordinates). The dual-scale design means the same 16×16 spatial region is represented both as a single discrete integer (token) and as a floating-point patch embedding, so the model simultaneously ingests compressed semantic codes and raw sensor values. Outputs span generated images (via diffusion decoders), generated text (autoregressive token decoding), segmentation masks, class labels, and geolocation strings, depending on the downstream head or generation target.

## Method

TerraMind employs a symmetric Transformer encoder-decoder (following 4M) with a dual-scale early-fusion strategy: each 16×16 patch is represented by both a discrete FSQ token and a learnable linear patch embedding, so the model learns cross-modal correlations at the token level while preserving spatial nuance at the pixel level. Pretraining uses masked token reconstruction—randomly sampled input tokens and pixel patches condition the decoder to predict masked target tokens via a cross-entropy loss, reducing the objective to a cross-modal patch classification problem. Modality-specific autoencoder tokenizers (FSQ bottleneck + diffusion decoder) compress image-like modalities into 16K-vocabulary discrete codes, while a modified WordPiece tokenizer handles captions and geolocations. A key additional contribution is Thinking-in-Modalities (TiM), a recursive technique where the model generates synthetic modalities (e.g., LULC from S-2) and concatenates them with the original input during finetuning or inference, mimicking chain-of-thought reasoning to improve downstream accuracy by up to 2 pp mIoU. The full model (TerraMindv1-B/L) is pretrained on 500 B tokens from the 9-million-sample TerraMesh dataset on 32 A100 GPUs.

## Results

On the PANGAEA benchmark (9 datasets), TerraMindv1-B achieves an average mIoU of 58.35% and average rank of 3.94, outperforming all other geospatial foundation models by at least 3pp avg. mIoU. TerraMindv1-L further improves to 59.57% avg. mIoU (rank 3.44), surpassing even the task-specific UNet baseline (57.58%) that is trained from scratch per task. In few-shot 1-shot 5-way classification, TerraMindv1-B reaches 70.83% on EuroSAT and 33.90% on METER-ML, exceeding CLIP-ViT-B/16 by over 13pp on EuroSAT. For zero-shot water body mapping on Sen1Floods11, TerraMindv1-B achieves 45.40% IoU without any task-specific fine-tuning, compared to 31.25% for a fine-tuned baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the PANGAEA benchmark, TerraMindv1-B (58.35% avg. mIoU) outperforms CROMA (55.29%) by ~3pp, GFM-Swin (52.42%) by ~6pp, and RemoteCLIP (51.66%) by ~7pp, with the largest gains on multimodal datasets like PASTIS (+7.2pp over CROMA) and CTM-SS (+6.4pp). TerraMindv1-L extends this lead to 59.57%, surpassing even the task-specific UNet baseline (57.58%) that is trained from scratch per task — a notable distinction as no other pretrained foundation model achieves this. In few-shot 1-shot 5-way classification, TerraMindv1-B (70.83% on EuroSAT, 33.90% on METER-ML) beats CLIP-ViT-B/16 (57.00%, 29.15%) by 13.8pp and 4.8pp respectively, and also outperforms Prithvi 2.0 300M (61.06%, 28.26%). For zero-shot water body mapping, TerraMindv1-B (45.40% IoU) exceeds the fine-tuned Sen1Floods11 baseline (31.25%) by 14pp without any task-specific training, while the fine-tuned TerraMindv1-B (82.26%) matches the SOTA DeCUR (82.17%). The Thinking-in-Modalities ablation shows that generating auxiliary LULC data during fine-tuning adds up to 2pp mIoU over standard unimodal fine-tuning (91.14% vs. 89.70% on S-2 input).

## Relation to Prior Work

TerraMind builds directly on the 4M massively multimodal masked modeling framework (Mizrahi et al., 2023), extending its token-only encoder-decoder with a dual-scale early fusion that simultaneously ingests pixel-level sensor data alongside discrete tokens. For tokenization, it adopts finite-scalar quantization (Mentzer et al., 2023) with diffusion decoders, departing from the vector-quantization approaches common in prior geospatial foundation models (DeCUR, Prithvi, CROMA). The key conceptual departure from prior EO foundation models is the unification of eight heterogeneous modalities—including text, elevation, land use, vegetation index, and geolocation—under a single any-to-any generative objective, whereas prior work (MetaEarth, AnySat, DOFA) handles either a narrow modality set or discriminative-only tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TerraMind establishes a practical template for building truly multimodal generative EO models, and the TerraMesh dataset (9M spatiotemporally aligned samples across 8 modalities) provides a much-needed shared pretraining resource for the community. The Thinking-in-Modalities technique, while showing only modest gains (~2pp mIoU), opens a promising direction where a model's own generative output serves as auxiliary input, analogous to chain-of-thought in LLMs. Open questions include whether the dual-scale benefit persists at larger scales, how generation quality (notably the asymmetric radar-to-optical vs. optical-to-radar fidelity and noisy DEM outputs) can be improved, and whether the approach extends to multi-temporal and hyperspectral data as the authors themselves note.
