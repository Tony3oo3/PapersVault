## Problem

Generating full 3D body pose from a single head-mounted device (one IMU + one egocentric camera) is highly ambiguous because first-person views rarely show the wearer's body and the sensor signals are extremely sparse. Prior methods either require additional wearable sensors, rely on diffusion-based generation that is computationally expensive, or lack sufficient training data due to the scarcity of paired egocentric video with body pose annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250035/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of egocentric video frames and synchronized 6-channel high-frequency head IMU data; head pose is either provided directly or estimated from the video via HeadNet (SLAM-based). Body parts visible in frames are first segmented by NExT-Chat before feature extraction. The output is a continuous 3D full-body pose sequence, represented in SMPL parameters for AMASS/KinPoly or COCO 17-joint format for EgoExo4D, generated auto-regressively frame by frame.

## Method

Head2Body combines three feature streams—IMU features from a Best-RQ encoder self-supervised on 1,700+ hours of Ego4D head-IMU data (with a 1D-CNN front-end replacing the original speech spectrogram extractor), visual features from ResNet applied to NExT-Chat-segmented body parts, and head pose—into a single concatenated sequence fed to VQ-Poser. VQ-Poser is a vanilla Transformer encoder-decoder that auto-regressively predicts discrete motion tokens from a residual VQ-VAE codebook learned on body pose sequences, bypassing the costly diffusion denoising loop used by prior generative approaches. A final pose refinement module (P_pose) adjusts the decoded output for positional and scaling accuracy. Training uses a combined cross-entropy loss on next-token prediction (with teacher forcing) and an L1 reconstruction loss on joint positions, velocities, and accelerations, which together enforce both structural consistency and kinematic smoothness.

## Results

On AMASS, Head2Body achieves 90.5 mm MPJPE, 12.1 mm head translation error, and 7.4 mm/s² acceleration error, outperforming the best baseline (MocapEvery) by 11.2% in MPJPE and 5.1% in acceleration error. On KinPoly, it reaches 66.1 mm MPJPE and 2.9 mm/s² ACC, a 13.8% and 12.1% improvement over MocapEvery respectively. On the EgoExo4D Body Pose Estimation challenge (test split), Head2Body achieves 13.0 cm MPJPE and 0.58 m/s MPJVE, surpassing the previous leaderboard top (EgoCast) by 9.5% in MPJPE and 1.6% in MPJVE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Head2Body outperforms all eight baselines across every metric on both AMASS and KinPoly. Against the primary vision-based baseline EgoEgo [25], it reduces MPJPE by 17% (90.5 vs. 109 mm on AMASS) and 13% (66.1 vs. 76.0 mm on KinPoly), while also cutting head translation error by 67% and 81% respectively. Against the primary IMU-based baseline SAGE [8], improvements are 19.5% in MPJPE on AMASS and 30.3% on KinPoly. Against the primary Vision+IMU baseline MocapEvery [23], Head2Body achieves 11.2% and 13.8% lower MPJPE on AMASS and KinPoly, plus 5.1% and 12.1% lower acceleration error. On EgoExo4D, Head2Body beats the leaderboard-leading EgoCast [7] by 9.5% in MPJPE (13.0 vs. 14.4 cm) and 1.6% in MPJVE (0.58 vs. 0.59 m/s), and outperforms MocapEvery by 10.3% in MPJPE. Notably, even when ground-truth head pose is unavailable and replaced by HeadNet estimates, Head2Body (107.9 mm MPJPE) still outperforms MocapEvery with ground-truth head pose (109.9 mm).

## Relation to Prior Work

Head2Body extends the EgoEgo two-stage paradigm (head pose → body pose generation) by enriching the conditioning signal with pre-trained IMU features and LVLM-segmented visual cues, all from a single head-mounted device. It repurposes Best-RQ, a self-supervised speech pre-training method, for 6-channel head-IMU representation learning, and adapts the residual VQ-VAE from audio codec design (Soundstream) to discretize body poses into tokens for auto-regressive generation. Unlike prior multi-sensor approaches (Trans-Pose, PIP, TIP) or hand-only visual methods (MocapEvery, DSPoser), it uses only one device and leverages NExT-Chat to segment all visible body parts, not just hands. The key architectural departure from contemporaries is replacing diffusion-based pose generation with a vanilla auto-regressive transformer over VQ tokens, reducing inference cost while improving accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-device constraint makes this approach directly applicable to consumer AR/VR headsets and smart glasses, where multi-sensor setups are impractical. The 6–14% gains over strong baselines across three diverse datasets (lab mocap, single-lab egocentric, in-the-wild EgoExo4D) suggest the multi-sensory fusion strategy generalizes well. However, the visual component's benefit is limited to frames where body parts are visible (more common in procedural than physical activities), and the LVLM segmentation can fail when other people appear in frame. Open questions include real-time inference latency of the LVLM + transformer pipeline, robustness to domain shift beyond the Ego4D pre-training distribution, and whether the VQ tokenization approach scales to longer sequences or more complex multi-person scenarios.
