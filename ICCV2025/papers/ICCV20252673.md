## Problem

Zero-shot metric monocular depth estimation for ground vehicles is hindered by the entanglement between depth and camera parameters, causing models to overfit to the specific camera setup in training data. Existing zero-shot methods (ZeroDepth, UniDepth, Metric3D) require large-scale multi-dataset training to cover diverse camera geometries, while the vertical image position cue—arguably the most fundamental depth cue for ground vehicles—is highly susceptible to overfitting without proper canonical modeling. The paper asks whether a single-dataset, single-camera training setup can achieve comparable zero-shot accuracy by exploiting the fixed camera-to-ground geometry constraint.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image at arbitrary resolution (trained with H ∈ [200, 500], W preserving aspect ratio) along with camera intrinsic parameters (f_y, c_y) and extrinsic parameters (pitch θ, height h). It produces a metric depth map in [0 m, 80 m] range at half the input resolution, along with per-pixel aleatoric uncertainty estimates for both the object-size and vertical-position cues. The model is fully resolution-agnostic at inference, adapting to any input size without resizing or padding, unlike UniDepth or Metric3D which require native training resolution.

## Method

The core contribution is a Vertical Canonical Transform (VCT) that replaces direct depth regression with prediction of vertical image positions of ground-projection points, which are then mapped to metric depth via the known ground-plane depth and camera parameters—effectively disentangling the vertical position cue from specific camera geometry. A ConvNext-Large encoder feeds two lightweight UNets that separately predict focal canonical depth (FCT, from Metric3D) and vertical canonical position (VCT), each with an accompanying uncertainty map. The two depth estimates are fused via an uncertainty-weighted combination (Eq. 4), allowing the model to adaptively rely on whichever cue is more reliable per region. Training uses only a single dataset with geometric augmentation (random crop/resize) to simulate diverse camera perspectives, combined with SI_log loss, uncertainty-weighted L1 loss, and a geometric consistency loss across augmented views—requiring far fewer data and compute than competing zero-shot methods.

## Results

GVDepth, trained on a single dataset (e.g., DrivingStereo, 168K samples), achieves A.Rel of 6.96% and δ₁ of 92.7% on KITTI zero-shot transfer, outperforming Metric3D (7.91%, 93.0%), ZeroDepth (11.1%, 87.9%), and UniDepth (13.6%, 88.0%) without requiring resolution alignment. On DDAD, GVDepth (DDAD-trained) reaches A.Rel 9.67% and δ₁ 90.3%, beating Metric3D (12.0%, 87.7%) and UniDepth (13.0%, 84.4%). When UniDepth and Metric3D are given their native training resolution (†), they marginally surpass GVDepth on KITTI (A.Rel 5.41–5.61% vs. 5.67–6.96%), but GVDepth remains competitive while operating at arbitrary resolutions without padding overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against domain-dependent methods (Monodepth2, DIFFNet, NeWCRFs, iDisc), GVDepth achieves dramatically better cross-domain transfer: while iDisc reaches A.Rel 5.33% in-domain on KITTI, it degrades to 27.4% on DDAD, whereas GVDepth (DDAD-trained) maintains 9.67% on DDAD and 12.3% on KITTI. Relative depth methods (MiDaS, LeReS) require shift-and-scale alignment and still trail GVDepth on both datasets (e.g., MiDaS A.Rel 13.2% on KITTI vs. GVDepth 6.96%). Among zero-shot metric methods, GVDepth (DrivingStereo-trained) outperforms Metric3D (6.96% vs. 7.91% A.Rel on KITTI) and UniDepth (6.96% vs. 13.6%) without resolution alignment, and beats both on DDAD (12.0% vs. 12.0%/13.0%). ZeroDepth is the closest competitor, slightly ahead on DDAD (8.62% vs. 9.67% A.Rel) but behind on KITTI (11.1% vs. 6.96%). When UniDepth and Metric3D are given their native high-resolution training setup (‡), they marginally surpass GVDepth on KITTI (5.41–5.61% vs. 5.67–6.96%), but GVDepth avoids the computational overhead of padding and remains fully resolution-agnostic. Against the ground-plane method GEDepth, GVDepth's Vertical cue achieves substantially better cross-domain accuracy (e.g., 10.43% vs. 18.25% A.Rel on DrivingStereo test), confirming that the canonical formulation generalizes beyond road pixels to all object classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the canonical-transform paradigm introduced by Metric3D (Focal Canonical Transform for object-size cues) by proposing a second, geometry-grounded canonical representation — the Vertical Canonical Transform — that exploits the fixed camera-to-ground relationship inherent in ground vehicles. It departs from prior ground-plane methods (GEDepth, PlaneDepth, GroCo) that restrict the ground constraint to road pixels, instead using the vertical image position of ground-contact points as a global depth cue disentangled from camera parameters. The probabilistic fusion of the two cues via aleatoric uncertainty is a novel architectural contribution, and the single-dataset training strategy with geometric augmentation contrasts sharply with the multi-dataset, multi-million-sample training of concurrent zero-shot methods (ZeroDepth, UniDepth, Metric3D).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for resource-constrained autonomous driving and robotics: achieving zero-shot metric depth comparable to methods trained on 3–17M samples while using only 23K samples from a single camera setup dramatically lowers the data and compute barrier. The resolution-agnostic design (no padding or resizing to a fixed training resolution) is directly relevant for real-time deployment on embedded hardware. Open questions include whether the approach extends to unknown or varying camera parameters (it currently requires known height and pitch), whether it generalizes beyond ground vehicles to aerial or indoor settings where the ground-plane prior is absent, and whether multi-dataset scaling can further close the remaining gap to the best zero-shot methods at their native resolutions.
