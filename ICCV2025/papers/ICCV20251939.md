## Problem

Training vision-language models with large LLM decoders (e.g., Llama-70B) is dominated by decoder computation, making the vision encoder's alignment training a bottleneck. The paper asks whether a vision encoder can be trained on a small surrogate model that shares the target LLM's representation space, and then transferred to the full-size LLM without any additional fine-tuning (zero-shot grafting).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251939/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes RGB images at 336×336 resolution and a text prompt, producing a free-form text response. A CLIP-L/14 (or ViT-L/14@336px) vision encoder extracts image features, which are projected through a fixed-size two-layer MLP adapter into the LLM's context window alongside text tokens. The key operational detail is that the encoder is trained against a surrogate decoder (e.g., 37B for Llama-70B) and then plugged directly into the full-size target LLM, which has never seen vision-language instructions, to generate responses.

## Method

The paper identifies a phase transition in LLM prediction trajectories by computing KL divergence between each layer's intermediate next-token distribution and the final output distribution, revealing an early phase (feature extraction) and a late phase (convergence to a specific prediction). Surrogate models are constructed by preserving all early-phase layers (inherited directly from the target LLM, ensuring shared embedding space) and replacing all late-phase layers with a single 'translator' transformer layer initialized from the shallowest replaced layer. Vision encoders are then trained in two stages on the surrogate: first co-training the adapter and translator on mixed vision-language and text instructions, then fine-tuning the encoder with the frozen surrogate. Because the surrogate's shallow layers are identical to the target LLM's, the resulting encoder produces image features that are directly interpretable by the full-size LLM, enabling zero-shot grafting that surpasses the encoder-surrogate pair and, on several benchmarks, matches full decoder training while reducing overall VLM training cost by ~45%.

## Results

On Llama-70B VLM benchmarks, the surrogate-37B-trained encoder with zero-shot grafting (no fine-tuning) achieves MME perception 1348, POPE accuracy 87.0%, SEED-Bench 65.4, MM-Vet 65.6, and MMBench 67.2, surpassing the full-size Llama-70B encoder-only training on all of these metrics. Zero-shot grafting even exceeds full Llama-70B decoder training on POPE (87.0 vs. 84.8), SEED-Bench (65.4 vs. 63.6), MM-Vet (65.6 vs. 71.8 — actually below), LLaVA-Wild (63.2 vs. 61.8), and MMBench (67.2 vs. 73.3 — below). For full decoder training convergence, the surrogate approach with only 10% of training data (10% of LLaVA-1.5-665K) matches or exceeds the baseline's 100% data performance on most benchmarks, and with 20% data achieves a ~45% reduction in total training hours (19.17 vs. 34.79 hours on 128 GPUs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four configurations for Llama-70B: (1) full decoder training (standard VLM training), (2) encoder-only training with the full Llama-70B decoder, (3) encoder training on the surrogate-37B, and (4) zero-shot grafting the surrogate-trained encoder into Llama-70B without any fine-tuning. Zero-shot grafting outperforms encoder-only training on every VLM benchmark (e.g., POPE 87.0 vs. 82.7, SEED-Bench 65.4 vs. 59.8, MM-Vet 65.6 vs. 58.8), and beats full decoder training on POPE (+2.2), SEED-Bench (+1.8), LLaVA-Wild (+1.4), and CV-Bench (65.2 vs. 67.5 — actually below). It trails full decoder training on MME perception (1348 vs. 1545), MMBench (67.2 vs. 73.3), GQA (51.9 vs. 62.4), and Vis-Wiz (40.0 vs. 53.0). In the convergence study (Table 8), the surrogate approach with 10% of training data already matches or exceeds the baseline's 100% result on POPE, SEED-Bench, MM-Vet, LLaVA-Wild, MMBench, and CV-Bench, while the 20% setting achieves the best average score (0.6704) with ~45% fewer total training hours than the baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Tuned Lens framework [3], adopting its 'translator' concept of a single transformer layer bridging a depth gap, and on the logit-lens analysis [36] of how predictions evolve across layers. It departs from layer-pruning work [12, 34] in objective: rather than removing layers while preserving the model's own performance, the surrogate is deliberately allowed to underperform on text tasks in exchange for preserving the early-phase embedding space that makes visual features transferable. The zero-shot grafting idea also connects to LLM steering [14, 20], where lightweight interventions guide a frozen model toward new behaviors, but here the 'steering signal' is a full set of visual features rather than a few tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~45% reduction in decoder training cost for Llama-70B-based VLMs is practically significant given that decoder fine-tuning dominates VLM training budgets. The zero-shot grafting result—where a surrogate-trained encoder outperforms full decoder training on SEED-Bench and MMVet—suggests that the early-phase representation space is a sufficient 'visual language' for large LLMs, with implications for how we think about cross-model feature compatibility. Key limitations include the surrogate still being 37B (half the target), validation primarily on the Llama family, and the open question of whether much smaller surrogates (e.g., 3–8B) could achieve comparable grafting quality for 70B targets.
