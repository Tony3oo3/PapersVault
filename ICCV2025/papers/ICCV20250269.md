## Problem

Clothes-Changing ReID (CC-ReID) requires recognizing individuals across clothing changes, but existing RGB-only methods depend on external clothing annotations or computationally expensive auxiliary modalities (gait, face, body shape) to learn clothing-invariant features. This paper asks whether color information—readable directly from raw pixels without any annotation—can serve as a lightweight proxy for appearance bias, and how to explicitly disentangle it from identity-relevant ReID features within a single transformer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250269/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 RGB images (or n=4 video frames) with no external attributes, annotations, or auxiliary models. It produces two outputs: a 1024-dim ReID embedding (f_ReID) used for gallery matching at inference, and a color embedding (f_CO) that regresses a flattened color histogram (8000-dim Pixel Binning or 3072-dim RGB-uv projection) computed on-the-fly from the input pixels. Only f_ReID is used at test time; f_CO exists solely to enable disentanglement during training.

## Method

CSCI appends a dedicated Color token alongside the standard ReID (class) token to an EVA-02 ViT-L backbone, so both tokens attend to the same spatial tokens but learn separate embeddings. The key architectural contribution is S2A Self-Attention: each transformer block runs two independent self-attention passes—one excluding the Color token and one excluding the ReID token—with spatial token outputs averaged across the two steps, preventing direct information exchange between color and identity cues while avoiding the computational cost of a full dual-branch design. Training combines cross-entropy on identity logits, triplet loss on f_ReID, MSE regression of f_CO toward the ground-truth color histogram, and a disentanglement loss that maximizes the cosine distance between f_ReID and f_CO. For video ReID, CSCI-V integrates EZ-CLIP temporal tokens via an alternating two-step schedule (vanilla video ReID without the Color token, then image-style CSCI training with the Color token on sampled frames), keeping temporal and color tokens from interacting simultaneously.

## Results

On image CC-ReID, CSCI-RGB-uv achieves 47.8% R-1 and 24.4% mAP on LTCC (CC protocol) and 66.2% R-1 and 61.3% mAP on PRCC (CC protocol), improving over the EVA-02 baseline by +2.9% and +4.6% Top-1 respectively. On video CC-ReID, CSCI-V-RGB-uv reaches 90.8% R-1 and 91.3% mAP on CCVID (CC) and 79.1% R-1 and 55.6% mAP on MeVID, gaining +1.0% and +2.5% Top-1 over the EVA-02+EZ-CLIP video baseline. All improvements are achieved without any external attributes (pose, body shape, clothing labels, text descriptions, or diffusion models).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSCI-RGB-uv outperforms all 18 compared baselines on both image CC-ReID benchmarks without using any external attributes. On LTCC (CC), it achieves 47.8% R-1, surpassing the strongest prior methods IRM (46.7%, uses clothing labels + text + body parsing) and RLQ (46.4%, uses clothing labels + pose + body parsing) by 1.1% and 1.4% respectively. On PRCC (CC), CSCI reaches 66.2% R-1, beating the closest RGB-only competitor FIRe² (65.0%, unsupervised pseudo-attributes) by 1.2% and the multi-attribute RLQ (64.0%) by 2.2%. For video ReID, CSCI-V-RGB-uv leads on CCVID with 90.8% R-1 (CC), exceeding GBO (86.9%, which uses diffusion + pose + body parsing) by 3.9%, and on MeVID with 79.1% R-1, surpassing the prior best ShARc (59.5%, uses silhouettes + body shape + pose) by 19.6%. The key differentiator is that CSCI requires zero additional supervision or external models, whereas most competing methods rely on clothing labels, pose estimators, 3D body shape, text descriptions, or diffusion-based augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the EVA-02 ViT-L backbone and the EZ-CLIP video adaptation strategy, introducing a dedicated Color token (analogous to the ViT class token) and a novel S2A self-attention mechanism that sits between full-overlap traditional attention and fully separate dual-branch architectures. It departs from prior CC-ReID methods (CAL, CCFA, AIM, CLIP3D) that rely on external clothing annotations, pose, body shape, or text-based fine-grained attributes by using raw color histograms (pixel binning or RGB-uv projections) as a free, annotation-free proxy for appearance bias. The RGB-uv projection is adapted from Afifi et al.'s illumination estimation work, repurposed here as a learnable color representation target rather than a style-transfer tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CSCI offers a practically attractive, low-overhead alternative to the expensive external modalities and manual clothing annotations that dominate CC-ReID, with only 2.77% parameter overhead and 4.39% FLOP increase. The validation that learned color embeddings cluster by clothing label (Fig. 6) supports color as a legitimate proxy when explicit labels are unavailable, though the paper acknowledges residual information leak (similar poses/faces appearing in color clusters) that a fully separate branch might resolve at higher computational cost. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more extreme appearance changes (seasonal clothing, accessories), whether the S2A mechanism transfers to other disentanglement settings beyond color, and whether the modest 2–5% gains hold under larger-scale or more diverse real-world deployments.
