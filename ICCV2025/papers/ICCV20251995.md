## Problem

Existing cooperative perception methods rely on single-stage fusion (early, intermediate, or late), forcing a rigid trade-off between communication bandwidth and detection accuracy. Additionally, inevitable calibration errors and pose noise during inter-agent information exchange produce misaligned features and misleading detections that degrade collaborative performance. No prior framework dynamically routes information across multiple fusion stages to simultaneously minimize communication cost and maximize robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251995/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent encodes its local LiDAR point cloud via a shared PointPillars encoder into a BEV feature map (C×H×W), and broadcasts its coordinates and heading angle so collaborators can project into the ego frame. The output is a set of 3D bounding boxes (position, size, yaw) with confidence scores after NMS. Uniquely, the transmitted payload is selectively partitioned: high-confidence locations send only 7-dim bounding boxes, moderate-confidence locations send raw intermediate BEV features, and low-confidence locations are suppressed entirely—reducing communication volume by factors of 9153×, 156×, and 18305× over comparable SOTA methods.

## Method

mmCooper introduces an adaptive multi-stage collaboration strategy governed by a Confidence-based Filter Generation (CFG) Module that uses a CNN to produce per-location confidence maps, applies Gumbel-Softmax for differentiable discrete stage assignment, and top-p% spatial filtering to decide whether each location transmits a bounding box (late stage), an intermediate feature patch, or nothing. For robustness to misalignment, the Multi-scale Offset-aware Fusion (MOF) Module performs cross-attention over s×s neighborhoods at three feature scales, incorporating spatially adjacent context to absorb calibration noise. In the late stage, a BBox Filtering & Calibration (BFC) Module employs Deformable BBox Attention to query the ego's fused intermediate features for each received bounding box, producing quality scores (for discarding erroneous boxes) and positional offsets (for refining noisy ones) via a transformer decoder with Tanh-bounded outputs. The total loss combines smooth-L1 regression, focal classification, offset, and score losses, trained end-to-end with supervised learning on PointPillars backbones.

## Results

On the three benchmark datasets (OPV2V, DAIR-V2X, V2XSet) with 100 ms delay, 0.2 m localization error, and 0.2° heading error, mmCooper achieves AP@0.7/0.5 of 78.11/88.93, 48.27/65.12, and 65.86/84.40, respectively. These represent improvements of 7.29%, 1.31%, and 2.09% in AP@0.7 over the second-best SOTA methods. In terms of communication efficiency, mmCooper requires only 1/9153, 1/156, and 1/18305 of the communication volume used by comparable methods to achieve optimal performance, and on OPV2V and V2XSet it even uses less bandwidth than late fusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

mmCooper outperforms all six SOTA baselines across all three datasets in both AP@0.7 and AP@0.5. The closest competitor is ERMVP, which leads on DAIR-V2X (46.96 vs. 48.27 AP@0.7, a 1.31% gap) and is second-best on OPV2V (69.71 vs. 78.11, an 8.40% gap). Where2comm is the strongest baseline on V2XSet (63.77 vs. 65.86, a 2.09% gap) and competitive on OPV2V (69.73 vs. 78.11). V2X-ViT achieves 70.06 on OPV2V but drops to 40.43 on DAIR-V2X, showing sensitivity to real-world conditions. When2com and Select2col are consistently the weakest baselines, with When2com even falling below the No Fusion baseline on DAIR-V2X under 0.2 m localization error. The multi-stage design gives mmCooper a clear advantage: it combines the information richness of intermediate features with the communication efficiency of late-stage bounding boxes, while the MOF and BFC modules provide robustness that single-stage methods lack.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the single-stage fusion paradigm that dominates cooperative perception (Where2comm, ERMVP, V2X-ViT, DiscoNet), where agents share information at exactly one level—raw data, features, or bounding boxes. It repurposes the confidence-based filtering idea from Where2comm but extends it into a two-stage decision: high-confidence regions transmit lightweight bounding boxes (late fusion) while moderate-confidence regions transmit intermediate features, with low-confidence regions suppressed entirely. The multi-scale offset-aware fusion and deformable bounding-box attention modules draw on standard cross-attention and deformable attention machinery (as in DETR-family detectors) but apply them specifically to mitigate calibration misalignment in multi-agent settings, a problem previously addressed only at one fusion stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic communication reduction (up to ~9000× less bandwidth than comparable SOTA methods while achieving higher AP) is practically significant for real V2X deployment where channel capacity is severely limited. The consistent robustness gains under increasing localization noise and transmission delay address a key barrier to field deployment that prior methods handle only partially. Open questions include whether the multi-stage strategy scales gracefully to larger agent counts or heterogeneous sensor suites, whether the Gumbel-Softmax-based stage selection remains stable in highly dynamic scenes, and whether the approach transfers to camera-only or multi-modal cooperative settings beyond LiDAR point clouds.
