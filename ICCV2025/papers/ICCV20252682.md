## Problem

GLOM, a part-whole hierarchy architecture, suffers from two practical limitations: current implementations fail to produce embeddings that reliably converge into distinct 'islands' of agreement representing parse-tree nodes, and the model is computationally expensive because it maintains equal spatial resolution at every level, creating redundancy at the top. This paper tackles both the vector-agreement problem and the computational overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252682/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 28×28 for MNIST/FashionMNIST, 96×96 for SmallNORB) and converts them into patch embeddings via a convolutional tokenizer, producing N = h×w columns each containing K-level embeddings of dimension d. At inference, the top-level embeddings are downsampled by the dissimilarity-focused head H_d from h×w to p×p spatial resolution before being passed to a contrastive head and a linear classifier, yielding a class label. The intermediate K-level embeddings themselves form the interpretable part-whole hierarchy.

## Method

The core contribution is a Contrastive Agreement Enhancer (CAE) that, at each level and time step, computes pairwise cosine similarity across all N embeddings, partitions pairs into positive (S ≥ threshold) and negative (S < threshold) sets, then aggregates positive pairs via softmax-weighted averaging while subtracting the negative-pair contribution to push dissimilar embeddings apart. The threshold is level-dependent (0 at the bottom, −0.2 at the top), so higher levels tolerate more angular spread and form larger, smoother islands. A second contribution, the dissimilarity-focused head H_d, performs sliding-window downsampling where each embedding's weight is proportional to exp(1 − S_ij) summed over its window neighbors, so embeddings that are *less* similar to their neighbors receive higher weight, preserving informative content while discarding redundant agreement. Training is two-phase: supervised contrastive pre-training on augmented image pairs, followed by frozen-backbone linear classification with cross-entropy loss.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the proposed model achieves 10.90% error with only 2M trainable parameters, compared to 11.15% for the Agglomerator (72M params) and 10.6% for CapsNet (6.8M params). On CIFAR-100, it reaches 41.31% error, dramatically outperforming CapsNet (82.00%) while using 3.4× fewer parameters. On simpler datasets, it matches or beats CapsNets: 0.01% on SmallNORB (vs. 2.70% CapsNet), 0.30% on MNIST, and 5.10% on FashionMNIST (vs. 6.38% CapsNet). The model is trained on a single GPU, unlike ViT-L/16 and ConvMLP-L which require TPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest GLOM implementation (Agglomerator [8]), the proposed model ties on SmallNORB (0.01%) and MNIST (0.30%), wins on FashionMNIST (5.10% vs. 7.43%) and CIFAR-10 (10.90% vs. 11.15%), but is marginally worse on CIFAR-100 (41.31% vs. 40.97%)—all while using 36× fewer parameters (2M vs. 72M). Compared to CapsNet [23], the method achieves substantially lower error on SmallNORB (0.01% vs. 2.70%), FashionMNIST (5.10% vs. 6.38%), and especially CIFAR-100 (41.31% vs. 82.00%), at the cost of slightly higher error on MNIST (0.30% vs. 0.25%) and CIFAR-10 (10.90% vs. 10.6%). Against other CapsNets (Matrix-CapsNet, Capsule VB, Res-CapsNet), the method is competitive or superior on CIFAR-10 and dramatically better on CIFAR-100, where CapsNets fail to scale. Versus convolutional baselines (ResNet-110, VGG), the method wins on MNIST and FashionMNIST but trails on CIFAR-10/100, though those baselines are ImageNet-pre-trained (marked *). Against large transformer/MLP models (ViT-L/16, ConvMLP-L, MLP-Mixer), the method is far less accurate on CIFAR-10/100 but uses 21–316× fewer parameters and trains on a single GPU rather than TPU.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Hinton's GLOM theory (2023) and its most complete implementation, the Agglomerator (Garau et al., CVPR 2022), which uses regularizers to encourage embedding agreement across levels. The key conceptual departure is replacing the multi-level regularizer balancing problem with a contrastive agreement enhancer (CAE) inspired by contrastive learning, which promotes positive-pair similarity and negative-pair separation without requiring hyperparameter tuning across levels. The dissimilarity-focused head (H_d) further departs from standard pooling by using within-window similarity as a negative weighting signal, positioning it as a redundancy-aware alternative to max or average pooling in hierarchical architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GLOM offers a principled path to interpretable part-whole representations that capsule networks and transformers lack, but its practical adoption has been stunted by the very issues this paper addresses: unreliable island formation and excessive parameter counts. The 72M→2M parameter reduction while maintaining competitive accuracy on small datasets makes GLOM more tractable, and the TV_n analysis revealing that the Agglomerator's top-level embeddings are actually *less* smooth than bottom-level ones (contradicting GLOM's theoretical expectation) is a useful diagnostic for the field. However, the evaluation is limited to small-scale datasets (CIFAR-10/100, MNIST variants, SmallNORB), and the CIFAR-100 error of ~41% remains far from competitive, leaving open whether the approach scales to ImageNet-level complexity or whether the 'islands' truly correspond to semantically meaningful parts at scale.
