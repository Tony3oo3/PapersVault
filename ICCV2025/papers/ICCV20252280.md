## Problem

Existing scanpath prediction models produce deterministic, averaged gaze trajectories that fail to capture the inherent stochasticity and individual variability of human visual exploration. Moreover, no prior diffusion-based approach unifies free-viewing and goal-directed visual search within a single architecture that supports variable-length scanpath generation and textual task conditioning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252280/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (resized to 518×518, yielding 37×37 DINOv2 patches) and a textual viewing task (an empty string for free-viewing or an object label like 'laptop' for visual search) as input. It outputs a variable-length sequence of up to 16 fixations, each comprising 2D spatial coordinates scaled to [0,1] and a fixation duration in seconds. Multiple diverse scanpaths can be generated for the same input by sampling different initial noise vectors, and the scanpath length is predicted per-sample by a validity classifier rather than being fixed.

## Method

ScanDiff embeds ground-truth scanpaths into a latent space via a learned linear projection, then applies a standard DDPM forward process (T=1000 steps, squared-root noise schedule) to corrupt the sequence. The reverse denoising process uses a 6-layer encoder-only Transformer (8 heads, d=512) augmented with a cross-attention layer that conditions on a joint multimodal embedding built from DINOv2 visual features and CLIP textual features projected into a shared space and concatenated along the channel dimension. Unlike prior diffusion scanpath works that directly concatenate the noisy gaze sequence with image embeddings, ScanDiff fuses them only through cross-attention, enabling dynamic modulation between gaze dynamics and visual-semantic information. A dedicated length-prediction head outputs per-token validity probabilities to determine the scanpath length, and training combines a variational lower bound loss, L1 reconstruction loss, binary cross-entropy validity loss, and a diffusion prior alignment loss (L_T) that penalizes residual mean bias at the final timestep.

## Results

On COCO-Search18 (target-present), ScanDiff achieves a KL divergence of 0.048 on MM, representing a 71.3% reduction over the second-best model (GazeXplain at 0.167), with SM w/ Dur = 0.037, SS w/ Dur = 0.019, and SemSS w/ Dur = 0.072. On the more challenging target-absent setting, ScanDiff further reduces MM to 0.020 and SM w/ Dur to 0.005. On free-viewing benchmarks, ScanDiff attains MM Avg = 0.078 and SM w/ Dur = 0.015 on COCO-FreeView, and MM Avg = 0.040 and SM w/ Dur = 0.041 on MIT1003, outperforming all re-trained baselines on duration-aware metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ScanDiff outperforms all 12 baselines across every metric on both target-present and target-absent COCO-Search18 settings. The closest competitor, GazeXplain [15] (re-trained), achieves MM = 0.167 on target-present, while ScanDiff reduces this to 0.048 (a 71.3% improvement); on target-absent, the gap widens further (0.037 vs. 0.020). Among traditional methods, Itti-Koch [33] and CLE [7] are far behind, with MM values exceeding 0.5 on COCO-FreeView. GAN-based PathGAN [2] and the G-Eymol [70] baseline show consistently poor performance (MM > 0.5 on search tasks). Among deep learning baselines, ChenLSTM [13] and ChenLSTM-ISP [14] are competitive on SM but lag significantly on MM and SemSS; HAT [69] reports strong RSS but lacks duration-aware metrics. TPP-Gaze [23], the most recent temporal point process approach, is outperformed by ScanDiff on all four metrics in both search conditions, with particularly large gaps on SM (e.g., 2.317 vs. 0.037 on TP). Gazeformer [48] shows moderate performance but is surpassed by ScanDiff on every reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

ScanDiff extends the diffusion model paradigm (DDPM, Ho et al. 2020) from image synthesis and 360°/text scanpath generation (DiffGaze, ScanTD, ScanDL) to natural-scene scanpath prediction, where prior work has relied on autoregressive or deterministic Transformer architectures (Gazeformer, HAT, GazeXplain). Architecturally, it departs from earlier diffusion-based gaze models by using cross-attention for multimodal conditioning (image + text) rather than direct concatenation, and introduces a length-prediction head that prior diffusion scanpath methods lacked. The unified free-viewing/task-driven framing builds on the Gazeformer and HAT lineage but reframes the output as a stochastic distribution over scanpaths rather than a single point prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By explicitly modeling scanpath variability through stochastic sampling, ScanDiff addresses a recognized gap: most predictors converge to an averaged gaze pattern that fails to capture individual differences relevant to clinical, HCI, and robotics applications. The proposed DSS metric and the RSS analysis open a new evaluation axis (diversity) that standard KL-divergence-based metrics cannot capture, potentially reshaping how the community benchmarks scanpath models. Open questions include whether the generated diversity genuinely reflects individual cognitive traits or merely adds calibrated noise, whether the approach scales to longer or multi-image scanpaths, and whether the DSS metric will gain community adoption as a standard evaluation criterion.
