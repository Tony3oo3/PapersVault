## Problem

Existing VLM acceleration methods based on visual token pruning suffer from two major shortcomings: (i) considerable accuracy drop because early-layer attention signals are insensitive to the task, leading to poor token selection, and (ii) limited speedup during the decoding stage, which dominates inference time for long responses (e.g., >30 tokens). The paper asks whether both limitations can be addressed within a single unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250144/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (or video) and a text prompt as input, which are encoded into M visual tokens and N textual tokens concatenated into a single sequence. During inference, visual tokens are pruned at an early layer (K=2) down to R retained tokens, and all visual tokens are removed after layer K_f=24 (FinalWipe). The output is a standard autoregressive text response; the SSD strategy guarantees the output is bit-identical to the unpruned base VLM, so accuracy loss comes solely from the token pruning step.

## Method

TwigVLM appends a lightweight twig block (T=3 transformer layers) after the K-th layer of a frozen base VLM, initializing the twig weights from the corresponding backbone layers and fine-tuning only the twig parameters (~10% of base training cost). During the prefilling stage, a Twig-Guided Token Pruning (TTP) strategy uses the attention map from the last twig layer—closer to the prediction head—to select which visual tokens to retain, yielding more task-sensitive selection than early backbone attention. During the decoding stage, a Self-Speculative Decoding (SSD) strategy uses the shallow twig model as a draft model to generate multiple tokens autoregressively, which are then verified in parallel by the deep base VLM as the target model; the two sub-networks share the first K layers' computation and KV-cache for further efficiency. A FinalWipe strategy additionally removes all visual tokens after layer K_f=24, reformulating the average retained token count to allow a larger R at the same overall pruning ratio.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B, TwigVLM preserves 96.0% relative accuracy (RelAcc) after pruning 88.9% of visual tokens (retaining only 64 out of 576), substantially outperforming the best prior method VisionZip‡ (95.2%) and FastV (77.0%). At a milder 66.7% pruning ratio, TwigVLM retains 99.2% RelAcc, achieving near-lossless compression. On generation speed, TwigVLM delivers 154% relative speedup on long-response benchmarks (MM-Vet), compared to ~104% for FastV and ~106% for VisionZip, thanks to its self-speculative decoding strategy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TwigVLM outperforms all six baselines across every pruning ratio and benchmark. At the aggressive 88.9% pruning ratio, TwigVLM (96.0% RelAcc) surpasses the closest competitor VisionZip‡ (95.2%) by 0.8 points and FastV (77.0%) by a dramatic 19 points, indicating that early-layer attention signals are far less informative than twig-layer attention for token selection. Against SparseVLM (89.9%), PDrop (87.6%), MustDrop (89.6%), and FasterVLM (89.1%), TwigVLM leads by 6–9 points in RelAcc. On generation speed for long responses (MM-Vet), TwigVLM achieves 154% relative speedup versus ~104% for FastV and ~106% for VisionZip, a gap attributable to its self-speculative decoding strategy that accelerates the decoding stage where token pruning alone provides minimal benefit. On the video domain (Video-LLaVA-7B), TwigVLM also leads VisionZip by 3.1% RelAcc (96.3% vs. 93.2%), confirming generalization beyond image-based VLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual token pruning line (FastV, SparseVLM, VisionZip, MustDrop) by replacing early-layer attention signals with attention from a lightweight 'twig' block trained on an early VLM layer, arguing that proximity to the prediction head yields more task-sensitive pruning signals. It simultaneously repurposes the same twig as a draft model for self-speculative decoding (SSD), a technique previously applied to LLMs via LayerSkip and Kangaroo but not integrated with VLM token pruning. The key architectural departure from prior work is that a single shallow module serves dual roles—guiding token selection during prefilling and generating draft tokens during decoding—rather than treating these as separate acceleration problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper highlights a practical gap that prior VLM acceleration work largely ignored: token pruning accelerates prefilling but provides limited speedup during decoding, which dominates inference time for responses longer than ~30 tokens. By unifying TTP and SSD in one architecture, TwigVLM delivers a combined 96% accuracy retention at 88.9% pruning and 154% speedup on long responses, a result that is directly relevant to real-world deployment scenarios (GUI automation, document understanding, video QA). Limitations include the need for a short fine-tuning phase (~10% of base training time) and the fact that SSD's speedup depends on token acceptance rate, which may degrade for highly diverse or creative outputs. Open questions include scalability to much larger VLMs (e.g., 70B+), multi-turn dialogue settings where KV-cache management interacts with the shared first-K layers, and whether the twig depth T=3 optimum holds across architectures.
