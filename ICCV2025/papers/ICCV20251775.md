## Problem

SAM and SAM2 produce overly smooth masks with coarse boundaries when segmenting objects with fine structures or sharp edges, limiting their utility in precision-critical applications. Existing high-precision interactive segmentation methods face a fundamental trade-off: enhancing local detail perception degrades the model's responsiveness to user prompts, undermining the interactive paradigm that makes SAM-family models practical.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251775/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image (or video frame) together with visual prompts (points, boxes, or coarse masks). Internally, the input image is additionally split into four non-overlapping sub-images that are upsampled to full resolution and fed alongside the original into the SAM2 Hiera encoder, effectively quintupling the encoder's input batch. The output is a high-resolution segmentation mask at 1024×1024, upsampled from the standard 256×256 SAM2 decoder output, with the architecture natively supporting SAM2's streaming memory pipeline for frame-by-frame video segmentation.

## Method

SAM2Refiner inserts three modules into the frozen SAM2 backbone. (1) Localization Augment (LA) feeds four sub-images alongside the original to the encoder, then applies multi-granularity average pooling (receptive fields 2, 4, 8) on the concatenated local features and performs global-local cross-attention to inject fine-grained spatial cues into the global representation. (2) Prompt Retargeting (PR) first augments the object embedding with a multi-branch RFB-style convolution (kernels 1×3, 1×5, 1×7) to sharpen small-patch discriminability, then constructs a 3-channel dense prompt map (positive clicks, negative clicks, mask at 256×256) encoded by a small CNN and added element-wise to the augmented embedding, followed by four attention steps (self-attention on sparse prompt tokens, cross-attention to/from the dense embedding, MLP) to restore prompt sensitivity. (3) Mask Refinement (MR) employs a UNet-like decoder that fuses the retargeted object embedding with multi-scale hierarchical features (256×256, 128×128, 64×64) from the Hiera encoder via channel-concatenation and upsampling, finishing with two transposed convolutions to reach 1024×1024 resolution. Training fine-tunes only the three new modules on HQSeg-44K with BCE + Dice loss and intermediate supervision at each decoder block, using a learning rate of 0.001 over 20 epochs on 8 V100 GPUs.

## Results

On four fine-grained image segmentation benchmarks (DIS, COIFT, HR-SOD, ThinObject), SAM2Refiner with the hiera_l backbone achieves an average mIoU of 93.1% and mBIoU of 88.9%, surpassing the previous best HQ-SAM (vit_h) by 4.9 and 8.6 points respectively. On video object segmentation, SAM2Refiner attains 80.9% J&F on LVOS-v1 and 79.9% J&F on LVOS-v2, outperforming SAM2 (77.2% / 78.3%) and HQ-SAM2 (79.6% / 79.1%). The method also demonstrates strong prompt responsiveness, achieving 90.0% mIoU with only 5 point prompts versus 84.2% for HQ-SAM and 73.9% for SAM2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAM2Refiner (hiera_l) outperforms all baselines on every image segmentation benchmark. Against the closest competitor HQ-SAM (vit_h), it gains +4.9 mIoU and +8.6 mBIoU on average, with the largest margin on DIS (+7.3 mIoU) and ThinObject (+7.4 mIoU). Compared to the SAM2 backbone it builds upon, the improvement is +14.6 mIoU and +17.4 mBIoU on average, confirming that the three proposed modules (LA, PR, MR) substantially close the fine-grained gap. On video segmentation (LVOS-v1/v2), SAM2Refiner beats HQ-SAM2 by +1.3 and +0.8 J&F respectively, and surpasses SAM2 by +3.7 and +1.6 J&F. Against older video baselines (XMem, RDE, LWL, CFBI, STCN, SwinB-DeAOT-L), the gains are much larger, reaching +16.4 to +32.0 J&F points, though these methods were not designed for prompt-based interactive segmentation. The prompt-response analysis (Table 3) shows SAM2Refiner maintains a consistent 5–10 mIoU advantage over HQ-SAM and 15–20 mIoU over SAM/SAM2 across 1 to 10 point prompts, validating the Prompt Retargeting module's role in preserving prompt sensitivity.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAM2Refiner is built directly on the SAM2 backbone (Hiera hierarchical encoder + streaming memory pipeline), extending it with three targeted modules to address fine-grained segmentation. It departs from HQ-SAM, which augments SAM with extra input tokens and multi-level feature fusion but remains constrained by SAM's original architecture, and from Pi-SAM, which adds a separate high-resolution mask decoder at the cost of diminished prompt responsiveness. The key conceptual departure is the explicit recognition that enhancing local detail perception (via sub-image features) actively degrades prompt sensitivity, necessitating a dedicated Prompt Retargeting module to re-align spatially-aware prompt features with the enhanced object embedding — a trade-off neither HQ-SAM nor Pi-SAM addresses directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since SAM and SAM2 serve as foundational models for a wide range of downstream applications (image editing, 3D reconstruction, medical imaging, AR/VR, robotics), the fine-grained boundary and detail limitation is a practical bottleneck that constrains real-world deployment. SAM2Refiner demonstrates that this limitation can be substantially mitigated while preserving zero-shot generalization and stable prompting, and the seamless integration into SAM2's video streaming pipeline extends the benefit to temporal segmentation. Open questions include whether the sub-image cropping strategy generalizes to very high-resolution or non-square inputs, whether the approach transfers to other SAM variants (e.g., SAM with ViT backbones), and whether the computational overhead of encoding five images per frame is acceptable for real-time video applications.
