## Problem

Masked autoregressive (MAR) models for image generation suffer from two limitations: (1) a training-inference discrepancy where ground-truth tokens are used as unmasked inputs during training but only predicted noisy tokens are available at inference, and (2) the MLP-based diffusion head models each masked token's distribution conditioned solely on its own conditional token, under-exploiting the interdependency across all masked and unmasked tokens. These issues constrain the quality of token prediction and limit the scalability of autoregressive models for visual generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250226/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates in the continuous latent space of a pre-trained VAE (KL-16 tokenizer from MAR), encoding 256×256 RGB images into a sequence of N = h·w continuous-valued visual tokens. Conditioning is provided either as a class token (ImageNet, 1K classes) or as a sequence of CLIP text embeddings (MS-COCO, five captions per image). At inference, the model progressively reduces the masking ratio from 1.0 to 0 via a cosine schedule, predicting multiple tokens per step, and the final token sequence is decoded back to 256×256 pixels by the VAE decoder.

## Method

De-MAR augments the MAR Transformer with two novel heads. First, a denoising head injects Gaussian noise into unmasked tokens during training (following the DDPM forward process) to mimic inference conditions, then uses a two-branch MLP—token refinement (predicts the denoised token) and token assessment (estimates quality scores for both the noisy and refined tokens)—to progressively refine unmasked tokens and adaptively fuse them at inference. Second, a Transformer-based diffusion head replaces MAR's MLP head, using cross-attention over all conditional tokens (both masked and unmasked positions) to model each masked token's probability distribution via a standard denoising objective, with shared adaptive normalization layers to reduce parameter count. At inference, the diffusion head generates masked tokens through a reverse diffusion process conditioned on the full token context, while the denoising head refines previously predicted tokens, yielding a 45% inference speedup over DiT-XL/2 with state-of-the-art FID scores.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 for class-conditional generation, De-MAR-H (995M params) achieves a state-of-the-art FID of 1.47 with classifier-free guidance (CFG) and 2.19 without CFG, outperforming the closest masked AR baseline MAR-H (FID 1.55 w/ CFG) and the strongest diffusion baseline DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.27 w/ CFG). On MS-COCO 256×256 for text-to-image generation, De-MAR achieves FID 5.27, surpassing the best masked AR baseline AutoNAT-S (FID 5.36) and the best diffusion baseline U-ViT-S/2-Deep (FID 5.48). The paper also reports a 45% inference speedup over DiT-XL/2 while maintaining superior FID.

## Baselines & Comparisons

De-MAR consistently outperforms all baselines across both datasets and all four model categories (GAN, diffusion, AR, masked AR). On ImageNet with CFG, De-MAR-H (FID 1.47) beats the strongest diffusion model DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.27) by 0.80 and the closest masked AR competitor MAR-H (FID 1.55) by 0.08; even the smallest De-MAR-B (FID 2.04) surpasses MAR-B (FID 2.31) by 0.27. On MS-COCO, De-MAR (FID 5.27) edges out AutoNAT-S (FID 5.36) by 0.09 and U-ViT-S/2-Deep (FID 5.48) by 0.21, while substantially outperforming MAR (FID 6.36) by 1.09. Among GAN baselines, StyleGAN-XL (FID 2.30 w/o CFG) is the strongest but still trails De-MAR-H (FID 2.19 w/o CFG). The ablation (Table 4) confirms both the denoising head and the upgraded Transformer-based diffusion head contribute independently, with the full De-MAR-B achieving FID 2.04 versus 2.31 for the base MAR. The paper also highlights a 45% inference speedup over DiT-XL/2, positioning De-MAR as both higher quality and faster than leading diffusion transformers.

## Relation to Prior Work

De-MAR directly extends the Masked Autoregressive (MAR) model of Li et al. (2024), which replaced discrete VQ tokens with continuous-valued representations and an MLP-based diffusion head for masked token prediction. The key architectural departure is twofold: (1) a new denoising head that injects Gaussian noise into unmasked tokens during training and progressively refines them at inference, addressing the training-inference discrepancy inherent in MAR; and (2) an upgraded Transformer-based diffusion head that replaces MAR's per-token MLP with cross-attention across all conditional tokens, capturing global interdependencies. Conceptually, the paper sits at the intersection of autoregressive sequence modeling (inheriting the 'next set-of-tokens prediction' paradigm from MAR/MaskGIT) and denoising diffusion (borrowing the DDPM forward/reverse formulation), positioning itself as a hybrid that retains AR's parallelizable multi-token prediction while gaining diffusion's iterative refinement capability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 45% inference speedup over DiT-XL/2 while achieving a lower FID (1.47 vs. 2.27 on ImageNet with CFG) is practically significant, suggesting that well-designed autoregressive models can now compete with diffusion models on both quality and efficiency for image generation. The denoising head's treatment of the training-inference gap (ground-truth vs. predicted noisy tokens) is a clean, generalizable fix that could benefit other masked/autoregressive generative models. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions (beyond 256×256), video generation, and whether the speed advantage persists as model scale increases; the paper also does not explore whether the denoising head's benefit diminishes at larger model sizes where the MAR Transformer itself may already produce high-quality unmasked tokens.
