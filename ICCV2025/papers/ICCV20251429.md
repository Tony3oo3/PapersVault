## Problem

Long-Tailed Class-Incremental Learning suffers from a harmful synergy between sequential forgetting and class-imbalance-driven gradient dominance, but existing CIL methods overlook a subtle geometric misalignment: randomly initialized new-class classifier weights create directional errors that induce gradient competition between old/new classes and between head/tail classes. This misalignment permanently degrades feature separability, particularly for under-represented tail classes, and no prior work addresses it through principled initialization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251429/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

GPA consumes RGB images and the frozen feature extractor weights from the previous incremental session (no access to old-class data is required). It produces standard class-label predictions alongside geometrically initialized and dynamically anchored classifier weight vectors. The method is model-agnostic and plug-and-play, requiring only a few lines of code to integrate into any existing CIL framework (replay-based, prompt-based, or representation-based), with no changes to the backbone architecture or training schedule.

## Method

GPA operates in three phases: (1) frozen prototype estimation computes per-class centroids using the previous session's feature extractor to avoid distortion from imbalanced optimization; (2) geometric weight initialization projects each prototype onto a unit hypersphere and sets bias terms inversely proportional to class frequency, explicitly decoupling magnitude imbalance from angular discriminability and encoding Fisher's linear discriminant criterion at initialization; (3) dynamic anchoring adds a regularization loss during incremental training that continuously pulls classifier weights toward the evolving (moving-average) class centroids, preserving geometric consistency as the feature space drifts. The total loss combines standard cross-entropy, the anchoring term weighted by λ, and the base method's auxiliary loss. Theoretically, this initialization reduces the initial angular deviation from the optimal boundary from ~π/4 (random) to <π/6, accelerating convergence by ~2.7× and yielding decision boundaries that approach Fisher-optimality in high-dimensional regimes.

## Results

On CIFAR-100-LT (shuffled, 5 tasks), GPA integrated with DualPrompt achieves 75.00% average accuracy and 71.78% final-task accuracy, outperforming the base DualPrompt by +7.58% and +9.58% respectively. Across all three benchmarks (CIFAR-100-LT, ImageNet-Subset-LT, ImageNet-R) and both 5-task and 10-task settings, GPA yields consistent gains of 0.8%–10.75% in average incremental accuracy over the base method, with the largest improvement seen in CODA-Prompt+GPA reaching 79.20% Acc on CIFAR-100-LT (+13.85%). The method also reduces the average forgetting rate by 6.38% and narrows the head-tail (Many vs. Few) accuracy gap by up to 18.6%, with tail-class (Few-shot) accuracy improving by as much as +12.4% absolute (e.g., PODNET Few: 25.7% → 38.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPA consistently outperforms all replay-based baselines: LUCIR+GPA reaches 44.68% Acc vs. LUCIR's 35.09% (+9.59%) on CIFAR-100-LT 5-task, surpassing both LWS (39.40%) and GVAlign (42.80%); PODNET+GPA achieves 43.85% vs. PODNET's 36.64% (+7.21%). Among prompt-based methods, the gains are most dramatic: CODA-Prompt+GPA jumps from 65.35% to 79.20% (+13.85%) and DualPrompt+GPA from 67.42% to 75.00% (+7.58%) on CIFAR-100-LT 5-task, while DynaPrompt+GPA improves from 67.74% to 73.65% (+5.91%). The one notable exception is L2P, where GPA slightly reduces 5-task CIFAR-100-LT accuracy (64.85% vs. 65.83%) but recovers on 10-task (61.15% vs. 60.47%) and on ImageNet-R 10-task (68.38% vs. 66.34%), suggesting L2P's prompt mechanism partially compensates for initialization bias in short sequences. For representation-based methods, EASE+GPA leads with 89.23% (+2.11%) and RPAC+GPA reaches 87.28% (+1.93%) on CIFAR-100-LT 5-task, confirming that even strong prototype-aware baselines benefit from explicit geometric alignment. On ImageNet-R, GPA delivers the largest absolute gains (e.g., CODA-Prompt: 78.59% → 88.68%, +10.09%), indicating the geometric initialization is particularly effective under domain shift and higher-dimensional ViT feature spaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

GPA repurposes class prototypes—long used as auxiliary inference components in CIL methods like iCaRL, RPAC, and EASE—as the primary geometric anchor for classifier weight initialization, a role no prior method assigns them. Conceptually, it connects to Fisher linear discriminant analysis by showing that hyperspherical projection of prototypes approximates Fisher-optimal weight directions in high-dimensional regimes, but it departs from existing LT-CIL strategies (LWS, GradRew, DynaPrompt) that address imbalance through loss reweighting, resampling, or gradient manipulation by intervening at the initialization stage instead. The paper positions itself as orthogonal to and composable with all three major CIL paradigms (replay, regularization, dynamic architecture), requiring only a few lines of code to integrate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of GPA (sub-10-line integration) makes it immediately applicable to any existing CIL pipeline, and the theoretical grounding (Fisher-optimality, convergence acceleration, generalization bounds) provides a principled explanation for why geometric initialization helps tail classes specifically. However, the method depends on a well-trained frozen feature extractor from the previous session, and the authors acknowledge mild sensitivity on high-dimensional ViT backbones. Open questions include whether scale-invariant normalization or adaptive anchoring can extend the approach to Transformer architectures without the observed sensitivity, and whether the gains persist under more extreme imbalance ratios or larger class counts beyond the 100-class benchmarks tested.
