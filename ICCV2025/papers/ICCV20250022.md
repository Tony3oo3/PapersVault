## Problem

Existing low-light image enhancement methods treat frequency-domain features uniformly and rely on global color/semantic losses that fail to preserve instance-level consistency. Additionally, wavelet-based approaches do not fully exploit the directional structure of wavelet sub-bands, limiting their ability to recover fine-grained high-frequency details. The paper addresses both gaps by introducing a causal inference framework that explicitly separates semantic content (causal factors) from illumination and color degradations (non-causal confounders).

## Input / Output

The network takes a single low-light RGB image as input (randomly cropped to 256×256 patches during training) and produces a corresponding enhanced bright RGB image. Beyond the primary image stream, the training pipeline additionally consumes text prompts (for CLIP-based instance-level semantic alignment) and segmentation maps from a frozen HRNet pretrained on PASCAL-Context to compute the local causal consistency loss. The architecture is a U-Net-like encoder-decoder with 16 base channels and asymmetric block configurations of [1,3,4,3,1] and [1,2,2,2,1] for the low- and high-frequency branches respectively.

## Method

CWNet decomposes each feature map via 2D wavelet transform into low-frequency (L) and three high-frequency (H, V, D) sub-bands, then processes them through a Hierarchical Feature Restoration Block (HFRB) at each scale. The Feature Extraction stage uses WTConv for the low-frequency band and direction-aligned depthwise separable convolutions for high-frequency bands, cross-compensating missing information from L into H/V/D. The High-Frequency Enhancement Block (HFEB) applies direction-specific 2D Selective Scanning Modules (H-2D-SSM, V-2D-SSM, D-2D-SSM) that match the scanning order to each wavelet sub-band's orientation, rather than using a generic 2D-SSM as in prior Mamba-based methods. The Low-Frequency Enhancement Block (LFEB) uses Fast Fourier Convolution with 5×5 local convolutions to capture global context for brightness and color recovery. Training combines a standard L2+SSIM+perceptual loss with two causal-consistency terms: a global causally-guided metric learning loss that pulls the anchor (enhanced image features) toward its normal-light positive while pushing away counterfactual negatives generated via physics-based light degradation and color anomaly interventions, and a local instance-level CLIP loss that verifies each HRNet-segmented sub-instance retains correct semantic identity relative to its text prompt.

## Results

CWNet achieves the best SSIM and LPIPS across all four evaluation datasets: SSIM 0.8496 / LPIPS 0.0648 on LOL-v1, SSIM 0.9005 / LPIPS 0.0383 on LOL-v2-Real (cross-dataset, trained on LOL-v1), SSIM 0.9362 / LPIPS 0.0195 on LOL-v2-Syn, and SSIM 0.6397 / LPIPS 0.1562 on LSRW-Huawei. On PSNR, CWNet is competitive but not uniformly best: it leads on LSRW-Huawei (21.50 dB) but trails Wave-Mamba (27.87 dB) on LOL-v2-Real, RetinexMamba (25.89 dB) on LOL-v2-Syn, and MIRNet (24.14 dB) on LOL-v1. The model is highly efficient at 1.23M parameters and 11.3G FLOPs, far lighter than MIRNet (31.79M) or SNR-Aware (39.12M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CWNet achieves the best SSIM and LPIPS on all four datasets, indicating superior structural and perceptual fidelity. On PSNR, it is not uniformly best: Wave-Mamba leads on LOL-v2-Real (27.87 vs. 27.39 dB), RetinexMamba on LOL-v2-Syn (25.89 vs. 25.50 dB), and MIRNet on LOL-v1 (24.14 vs. 23.60 dB), though CWNet still ranks second or third in each case. On LSRW-Huawei, CWNet wins all three metrics, outperforming Retinexformer (21.23 dB) and Wave-Mamba (21.19 dB) in PSNR while also leading in SSIM and LPIPS. Compared to the closest Mamba-based competitors, CWNet's advantage is most pronounced in LPIPS (e.g., 0.0195 vs. 0.0389 for RetinexMamba on LOL-v2-Syn), suggesting better perceptual quality even when pixel-level PSNR is slightly lower. In terms of efficiency, CWNet (1.23M params, 11.3G FLOPs) is comparable to Wave-Mamba (1.26M, 7.22G) and far lighter than RetinexMamba (3.59M, 34.76G) or SNR-Aware (39.12M, 26.35G), while matching or exceeding their quality on perceptual metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

CWNet sits at the intersection of three research threads: wavelet-based LLIE (Wave-Mamba, wavelet diffusion), state-space model architectures (VMamba, RetinexMamba, LLE-Mamba), and causal inference in computer vision (CIIM, DCIN). It departs from prior wavelet-SSM methods by replacing the generic 2D-SSM scanning with direction-specific H/V/D-2D-SSM modules aligned to wavelet sub-band orientations, and from prior CLIP-guided enhancement (SCLLE, SKF) by operating at the instance level via HRNet segmentation. The causal inference framing—treating semantic content as causal factors and illumination/color anomalies as confounders—is the paper's primary conceptual departure, as no prior LLIE work formalizes the task through a Structural Causal Model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The causal-inference lens offers a principled vocabulary for what LLIE should preserve (semantics) versus what it should correct (brightness, color), and the ATE analysis provides a diagnostic tool for understanding region-specific degradation sensitivity. However, the practical gains are modest and mixed: CWNet does not uniformly dominate on PSNR across all four benchmarks (e.g., Wave-Mamba still leads on LOL-v2-Real PSNR), and the authors acknowledge failure under composite degradations (blur + haze + low light simultaneously). The open question of how to extend the causal framework to multi-degradation scenarios, and whether the causal framing yields benefits beyond what well-tuned frequency-domain losses already provide, remains unresolved.
