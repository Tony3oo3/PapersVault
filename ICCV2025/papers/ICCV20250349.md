## Problem

Localizing traffic surveillance cameras within a 3D LiDAR reference map is an under-explored problem that differs fundamentally from in-vehicle I2P registration: images and point clouds are captured at different times from largely different viewpoints, traffic cameras use variable focal length lenses with frequently changing intrinsics, and no large-scale intersection dataset exists for training. Existing I2P methods struggle with cross-modal matching under these large viewpoint differences, and prior traffic-camera-specific approaches require manual intervention or panoramic/rendering image acquisition.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB traffic camera image (1920×1080, 90° horizontal FOV, captured at 6–8 m height with 15–30° pitch) and a 3D LiDAR point cloud accumulated from multiple locations, along with camera intrinsics (either known or predicted by DUSt3R for uncalibrated cameras). It outputs a 6-DoF relative transformation (rotation matrix and translation vector) between the image and point cloud coordinate frames, derived from predicted point-to-pixel correspondences via EPnP-RANSAC. The key constraint is that the image and point cloud are captured at different times and from substantially different viewpoints, with no guaranteed overlap or initial pose guess.

## Method

TrafficLoc follows a coarse-to-fine pipeline: it first extracts 2D patch features (ResNet-18 + DUSt3R ViT) and 3D point group features (PointNet + FPS + Point Transformer), fuses them via a Geometry-guided Feature Fusion (GFF) Transformer, performs coarse patch-to-group matching, then refines to pixel-level correspondences. Three novel components distinguish it from prior I2P methods: (1) Geometry-guided Attention Loss (GAL) supervises cross-attention maps using camera projection geometry (angular radius for I2P, ray-to-point distance for P2I) with a BCE loss, forcing the network to attend only to geometrically corresponding regions; (2) Inter-intra Contrastive Learning (ICL) constructs negative pairs within each modality in addition to inter-modal pairs, increasing feature separability among same-modality patches/groups; (3) Dense Training Alignment (DTA) uses a soft-argmax operator to back-propagate gradients to all image patches rather than only sparsely sampled correspondences, providing global supervision. The final pose is estimated via EPnP-RANSAC on the predicted point-pixel pairs.

## Results

On the proposed Carla Intersection dataset, TrafficLoc (with GT intrinsics) achieves median RRE of 0.66° and RTE of 0.51 m on Test_T1-T7, 2.64°/1.13 m on the harder viewpoint-variation split Test_T1-T7hard, and 2.53°/2.69 m on the fully unseen Test_T10 — reducing RRE by up to 86% over the previous SOTA CoFiI2P. On KITTI Odometry, TrafficLoc reaches 0.87° mean RRE and 0.19 m mean RTE with 100% registration recall, and on NuScenes it achieves 1.38°/0.78 m with 99.45% recall, both setting new SOTA. Inference runs at 0.85 s per frame with GT intrinsics and 1.74 s when intrinsics are predicted by DUSt3R.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TrafficLoc outperforms all five baselines across every dataset. On Carla Intersection, the gap is largest: CoFiI2P (the prior SOTA) achieves 4.24°/2.82 m RRE/RTE on Test_T1-T7 versus TrafficLoc's 0.66°/0.51 m (an 85% RRE reduction), and the advantage widens to 17.78°→2.53° (86% reduction) on the fully unseen Test_T10 where CoFiI2P and CFI2P both degrade severely. CFI2P is the second-strongest baseline on Carla Intersection (4.46°/1.92 m on T1-T7) but still trails TrafficLoc by 3.8× in RRE. On KITTI Odometry, TrafficLoc improves RRE from 1.14° (CoFiI2P) to 0.87° and RTE from 0.29 m to 0.19 m, while matching the 100% recall; CFI2P is close at 1.38°/0.54 m. On NuScenes, TrafficLoc edges out CoFiI2P (1.48°/0.87 m/98.67%) with 1.38°/0.78 m/99.45%, and CFI2P is comparable at 1.47°/1.09 m/99.23%. DeepI2P variants and CorrI2P are substantially weaker on Carla Intersection (RRE > 9°) but remain competitive on KITTI/NuScenes, confirming that the traffic-camera viewpoint shift is the primary differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

TrafficLoc extends the coarse-to-fine I2P registration paradigm established by CoFiI2P and CFI2P, reusing their Transformer-based fusion architecture and EPnP-RANSAC pose estimation backbone, while drawing on EP2P-Loc's dual-branch feature extraction design. The key conceptual departures are three targeted modules: a Geometry-guided Attention Loss that supervises cross-attention maps via camera projection geometry (addressing the limited geometric awareness of standard Transformers under large viewpoint changes), an Inter-intra Contrastive Learning scheme that adds intra-modality negatives to standard contrastive learning, and a Dense Training Alignment strategy using soft-argmax to propagate gradients to all patches rather than only sparsely sampled pairs. The paper also introduces the Carla Intersection dataset, filling a gap left by existing I2P benchmarks (KITTI, NuScenes) that are limited to in-vehicle camera perspectives and lack traffic-intersection scenarios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Traffic camera localization is a prerequisite for city-scale cooperative perception, enabling fusion of roadside and onboard sensors for broader situational awareness than any single vehicle can achieve. The Carla Intersection dataset (75 intersections, 8 worlds) provides the first large-scale benchmark specifically designed for this under-explored setting, and the demonstrated Sim2Real transfer (training only on Carla, evaluating on uncalibrated real-world USTC and OpenTrafficCam cameras) suggests practical deployability. Open questions include scalability to dense city-wide camera networks, handling of dynamic scene changes between LiDAR capture and camera observation, and whether the geometry-guided supervision generalizes to non-pinhole or multi-camera traffic sensor configurations.
