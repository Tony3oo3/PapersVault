## Problem

Existing joint RGB-depth generation methods (JointNet, LDM3D, UniCon) rely on U-Net-based diffusion architectures with limited receptive fields, producing geometrically inaccurate depth maps and low-fidelity images. Moreover, prior joint models struggle to simultaneously support joint generation, depth estimation, and depth-conditioned image generation within a single framework without task-specific fine-tuning, because their joint distributions are not solidly modeled across all noise levels of each modality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250742/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes text prompts (generated by LLaVA) and, depending on the task configuration, either a clean RGB image, a clean depth map, or neither. It outputs a 512×512 RGB image and a corresponding depth map. Task selection is controlled purely by the timesteps of each branch: t_x=0, t_y=0 for joint generation; t_x=1, t_y=0 for depth estimation from a given image; t_x=0, t_y=1 for depth-conditioned image generation. No additional guidance or conditioning network is required at inference time.

## Method

JointDiT extends the Flux diffusion transformer (MM-DiT + P-DiT blocks) with a parallel depth branch parameterized by LoRAs (rank 64) and joint cross-attention modules that exchange queries between the RGB and depth branches within each DiT block. The training objective is a Joint Conditional Flow Matching (JCFM) loss with two independent timesteps t_x and t_y, enabling the network to learn the joint vector field v_{t_x,t_y}(x,y|x_1,y_1). Two key techniques ensure solid joint distribution coverage: (1) adaptive scheduling weights, which apply sigmoid-gated coefficients to the joint cross-attention outputs so that the noisier branch receives stronger structural guidance from the cleaner one, and (2) an unbalanced timestep sampling strategy that draws t_x and t_y from two different distributions (f(t) and g(t)) with 50% probability and from the same distribution with 50% probability, ensuring adequate coverage of extreme timestep combinations. Only the LoRAs and joint connection modules are trained (backbone frozen), and the model is trained on 50k image-depth-text pairs for 75k iterations on a single H100 GPU.

## Results

On depth estimation (Table 1), JointDiT achieves AbsRel of 5.7 on NYUv2, 6.6 on ScanNet, 10.3 on KITTI, 27.3 on DIODE, and 16.5 on ETH3D, outperforming joint generation baselines JointNet (13.7/14.7/20.9/35.0/27.1) and UniCon (7.9/9.2) while remaining competitive with dedicated generative depth estimators Marigold and GeoWizard on all datasets except ETH3D. After 50k additional iterations of fine-tuning on synthetic data (Ours+ft), the model reaches AbsRel 5.0 on NYUv2 and 5.6 on ScanNet, surpassing both Marigold and GeoWizard. For depth-conditioned image generation (Table 2), JointDiT achieves the lowest FID (12.62) and AbsRel (6.99) on OpenImages 6K, beating UniCon (FID 13.21, AbsRel 9.26), ControlNet (FID 13.68, AbsRel 9.85), and Readout-Guidance (FID 18.72, AbsRel 23.19).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against joint generation baselines, JointDiT substantially outperforms JointNet on all five depth estimation benchmarks (e.g., AbsRel 5.7 vs. 13.7 on NYUv2, 6.6 vs. 14.7 on ScanNet) and also beats UniCon (AbsRel 5.7 vs. 7.9 on NYUv2, 6.6 vs. 9.2 on ScanNet), which only reports two datasets. Compared to dedicated generative depth estimators Marigold and GeoWizard, JointDiT is competitive on NYUv2 (5.7 vs. 5.5/5.2), ScanNet (6.6 vs. 6.4/6.1), and DIODE (27.3 vs. 30.8/29.7, where it actually wins), but trails on KITTI (10.3 vs. 9.9/9.7) and ETH3D (16.5 vs. 6.5/6.4); the ETH3D gap is attributed to using Depth Anything V2 pseudo-labels for training. After fine-tuning on synthetic data (Ours+ft), the model surpasses both Marigold and GeoWizard on NYUv2 (5.0 vs. 5.5/5.2), ScanNet (5.6 vs. 6.4/6.1), and DIODE (26.6 vs. 30.8/29.7). For depth-conditioned image generation, JointDiT achieves the best FID (12.62) and AbsRel (6.99) on OpenImages 6K, outperforming UniCon (13.21/9.26), ControlNet (13.68/9.85), and Readout-Guidance (18.72/23.19), with the AbsRel advantage indicating superior geometric fidelity of generated images. For 3D lifting visualization, JointDiT produces markedly more geometrically plausible point clouds than LDM3D and JointNet, which the authors attribute to the superior image prior of the Flux diffusion transformer backbone over the U-Net-based Stable Diffusion used by those baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

JointDiT extends the joint RGB-depth diffusion modeling line (LDM3D, JointNet, UniCon) by replacing the U-Net backbone with the Flux diffusion transformer and introducing a parallel depth branch connected via joint cross-attention modules. It builds on the flow matching framework of Lipman et al. to formulate a Joint Conditional Flow Matching loss with two independent timesteps, and adopts the joint cross-attention mechanism from UniCon. The key methodological departure is the introduction of adaptive scheduling weights and unbalanced timestep sampling to enable solid joint distribution learning across all noise levels, which prior joint models did not explicitly address. Conceptually, the paper positions joint distribution modeling as a unified alternative to separate conditional generation pipelines (ControlNet for depth-to-image, Marigold/GeoWizard for depth estimation).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single joint model, with only LoRA and joint connection modules trainable (a small fraction of parameters), can match fully fine-tuned generative depth estimation models on most benchmarks, suggesting joint distribution modeling is a practical alternative to maintaining separate conditional models. The observed complementary behavior—depth branch capturing structure, RGB branch capturing texture—offers a mechanistic explanation for why joint modeling generalizes to stylized domains where pure depth estimation fails. However, the 19.8% parameter overhead and 2.9× sampling time increase limit immediate deployment, and the authors note that adapting the approach to lightweight diffusion transformers remains an open direction. Whether the unbalanced timestep sampling and adaptive scheduling weights generalize beyond the RGB-depth pair to other multi-modal combinations is unexplored.
