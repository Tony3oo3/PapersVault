## Problem

Existing 4D avatar generation methods either require extensive multiview video inputs with camera pose estimation, or produce incomplete 360-degree views, or lack the ability to model facial expressions. No prior method simultaneously achieves full 360-degree coverage, expression animation, 3D multi-view consistency, and high visual quality from a single image, making high-quality 4D avatar creation impractical for widespread use.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250371/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB face image (no occlusions required, though it handles cartoons and old photographs) and produces an animatable 4D avatar represented as 3D Gaussians bound to a FLAME parametric mesh. The output supports real-time rendering at 156 FPS at 512×512 resolution, full 360-degree view synthesis, and arbitrary expression/pose animation via FLAME parameters. The full generation pipeline takes approximately 2.5 hours per avatar, with the final model being a compact per-identity representation rather than a generalizable network.

## Method

FaceCraft4D synthesizes personalized multiview training data using three sequential priors, then fits an explicit 3D model. First, 3D-GAN inversion on PanoHead yields a coarse geometry prior with approximate texture. Second, a 2D diffusion model (Cosmicman) enhances multiview textures using cross-view mutual attention (replacing self-attention K/V with reference-image features) and depth-guided warping with epipolar constraints to enforce geometric consistency across views. Third, LivePortrait generates synchronized multiview expression videos using the enhanced images as sources and diverse driving clips from NerSemble. The final 4D representation is a GaussianAvatar (3D Gaussians rigged to FLAME), trained with a novel COIN strategy that jointly optimizes a view-consistent base model (supervised by LPIPS) and a lightweight two-layer MLP encoding per-view color offsets (supervised by L1/SSIM), effectively isolating cross-view inconsistencies into the delta representation to prevent blur while preserving sharp high-frequency details.

## Results

On static 3D head generation (Table 2), FaceCraft4D achieves the best ID score (0.5082) and FID (174.36) among all baselines, while PanoHead leads on CLIP-I (0.8233 vs. 0.8053). On 3D head animation (Table 3), the method outperforms both AniPortrait and Portrait-4D-V2 across all three metrics, achieving CLIP-I 0.5737, ID 0.4602, and FID 201.76. The full pipeline takes approximately 2.5 hours to generate a single 4D avatar and renders at 156 FPS at 512×512 resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FaceCraft4D outperforms all four static-generation baselines on identity preservation (ID) and image quality (FID), with the largest margin over Portrait3D (ID 0.5082 vs. 0.3719; FID 174.36 vs. 302.74). PanoHead is the closest competitor, achieving a slightly higher CLIP-I (0.8233 vs. 0.8053) but trailing on ID by 0.084 and FID by 20.9 points, indicating better cross-view consistency but weaker identity fidelity. SV3D and GaussianCube, designed for general objects, show substantially weaker head-specific performance (FID 234.86 and 258.81 respectively). In the animation comparison, FaceCraft4D beats Portrait-4D-V2 on CLIP-I (0.5737 vs. 0.5236) and FID (201.76 vs. 248.36) while matching its ID score (0.4602 vs. 0.4592), and dominates the 2D-based AniPortrait across all metrics (FID 201.76 vs. 364.99), confirming the advantage of a pure 3D representation for multi-view consistency during large-angle rotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

FaceCraft4D composes three established prior models—PanoHead (3D-GAN geometry prior), Cosmicman (image diffusion prior), and LivePortrait (video prior)—into a unified pipeline that no prior work has assembled for the single-image-to-4D-avatar task. The cross-view mutual attention mechanism adapts MasaCtrl's tuning-free attention control to enforce identity consistency across synthesized viewpoints, while the depth-guided warping with epipolar constraints departs from purely generative approaches by injecting geometric structure into the diffusion process. The COIN training paradigm, which jointly optimizes a consistent GaussianAvatar base and a view-dependent MLP for inconsistencies, is the paper's most original methodological contribution, drawing on robust regression ideas but applying them spatially via a per-view embedding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for multi-view video capture, this work makes high-quality animatable 3D head avatars accessible from a single photograph, with practical implications for gaming, film, and virtual production. The COIN training strategy is a reusable technique for any reconstruction task where training data is synthetically generated and inherently inconsistent across views. Key limitations include the ~2.5-hour per-avatar generation time, reliance on multiple large pretrained models (PanoHead, Cosmicman, LivePortrait) that must be kept in sync, and evaluation limited to non-reference metrics on FFHQ without ground-truth 3D scans. Open questions include whether the pipeline generalizes to subjects with complex hair, accessories, or extreme expressions, and whether the multi-stage pipeline can be compressed into a more efficient end-to-end formulation.
