## Problem

Existing deepfake detectors either focus on a single modality (ignoring audio-visual correspondence) or model only cross-modal alignment (overlooking within-modality inconsistencies), and most are single-stage supervised frameworks that overfit to specific training data and generalize poorly to unseen manipulations. The paper addresses the gap of jointly capturing intra-modal temporal continuity and cross-modal synchronization in a way that generalizes across manipulation types and datasets without task-specific fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251002/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes video clips with synchronized audio, where video is represented as a sequence of RGB frames (T_v × C × H × W) and audio as a log-mel spectrogram (T_a × B). In the classification task, it outputs a single real/fake logit; in the localization task, it outputs per-frame confidence scores and boundary-matching confidence maps (position-aware, channel-aware, and position-channel-aware) that identify the temporal segments containing deepfake manipulation. The pretraining stage requires only real videos, while the classification and localization stages operate on the same input format without any architectural changes to the pretrained encoders.

## Method

The framework is two-stage: (1) self-supervised pretraining on real videos using three synchronization models (visual-visual, audio-audio, and visual-audio), each an attention-based module that predicts frame-level temporal alignment between a primary encoding and temporally shifted secondary encodings across 2τ+1 offsets, trained with MSE loss against Gaussian targets (truncated normal distributions centered at 0 for cross-modal, ±1 for intra-modal) rather than InfoNCE; (2) a classification or localization head that fuses the pretrained synchronization features with unimodal embeddings via alternating cross-attention layers, requiring no fine-tuning of the pretrained models. Two attention variants are proposed: a decoder with a k-band local attention mask, and a sparse encoder replacing self-attention with window-level depthwise+pointwise convolutions (inspired by TriDet), which achieves comparable accuracy at 4× faster training. The localization stage additionally employs a boundary module (adopted from BA-TFD+) that predicts position-aware, channel-aware, and position-channel-aware confidence maps fused across modalities for temporal proposal generation.

## Results

On FakeAVCeleb cross-manipulation detection, the best variant (Decoder, VoxCeleb2) achieves 99.6% AP and 97.4% AUC on average across the three Fake Visual categories, and 98.6% AP / 97.9% AUC on the Real Visual–Fake Audio (RVFA) category, outperforming AVFF by 5.3% AP and 5.5% AUC on RVFA. On the cross-dataset KoDF benchmark, the same variant reaches 98.9% AP and 99.0% AUC, surpassing AVFF (93.1% AP, 95.5% AUC) and RealForensics (95.7% AP, 93.6% AUC). For temporal localization on LAV-DF, the model achieves 87.4% AP@0.5 and 66.8% AP@0.75 with only 18.8M parameters, competitive with ActionFormer (85.2% / 59.1%) but trailing the much larger BA-TFD+ (96.3% / 85.0%) and UMMAFormer (98.8% / 95.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against visual-only baselines (Xception, LipForensics, FTCN, RealForensics), the proposed model matches or exceeds them on all Fake Visual categories while additionally handling the RVFA (audio-only manipulation) category where visual-only methods cannot operate. Compared to the closest audio-visual competitor AVFF [40], the proposed model wins on RVFA by 5.3% AP and 5.5% AUC and on AVG-FV AP by 1.4 points, while AVFF edges ahead on FVFA-FS and FVFA-GAN AUC by ~2–3 points; however, AVFF requires fine-tuning its pretrained model for classification, whereas the proposed method does not. On cross-dataset KoDF, the proposed model (98.9% AP) outperforms AVFF (93.1%) by 5.8% AP and RealForensics (95.7%) by 3.2% AP. For temporal localization on LAV-DF, the proposed model (87.4% AP@0.5) surpasses ActionFormer (85.2%) and TriDet (86.3%) but trails the substantially larger BA-TFD+ (96.3%, ~150M params) and UMMAFormer (98.8%), with the gap attributed to model size rather than feature quality. The single-stage supervised AV-DFD [59] shows highly inconsistent performance (58.4% AP on FVFA-GAN vs. 100% on FVFA-WL), highlighting the generalization advantage of the two-stage self-supervised pretraining approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the audio-visual synchronization pretraining paradigm introduced by Feng et al. (AVAD, CVPR 2023) and Chen et al. (2021) from a single cross-modal model to three parallel synchronization models (visual-visual, audio-audio, visual-audio), capturing both intra-modal temporal continuity and cross-modal alignment. It departs from the InfoNCE contrastive loss used in prior synchronization work by proposing Gaussian-targeted MSE loss that models relative synchronization across all 2τ+1 shifted pairs simultaneously. For the localization stage, it adopts the boundary-matching architecture of BA-TFD+ (Cai et al., 2023) and borrows the window-level convolution attention from TriDet (Shi et al., 2023) to replace expensive self-attention, reducing training time by 4× while maintaining comparable accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a two-stage framework where pretrained synchronization features require no fine-tuning for either classification or temporal localization, simplifying deployment and enabling easy task transfer. By jointly modeling intra-modal and cross-modal consistencies, the approach addresses a gap in prior multimodal detectors (AVAD, AVFF) that focus solely on cross-modal correspondence and may miss unimodal manipulations. However, the model still trails the strongest localization baselines (UMMAFormer, BA-TFD+) at high IoU thresholds, and the ablation revealing that intra-modal synchronization features are not directly useful in stage 2—only their pretraining losses indirectly benefit shared encoders—suggests the mechanism of benefit is not fully understood. Open questions include whether combining synchronization with other self-supervised objectives (e.g., masked autoencoding) could yield richer features, and whether the approach scales to longer videos or non-speech audio-visual content.
