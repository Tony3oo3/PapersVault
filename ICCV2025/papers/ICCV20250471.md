## Problem

HDR gain maps, which encode the per-pixel transformation between SDR and HDR renderings, are conventionally stored as 8-bit images compressed with JPEG, HEIC, or JPEG-XL. This standard compression pipeline introduces visible artifacts (banding, haloing, blockiness) even at moderate quality settings, and increasing quality inflates file size. No prior method leverages the readily available SDR image as a conditioning signal to encode the residual more efficiently.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250471/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network receives a 5-D vector per pixel — spatial coordinates (x, y) and the corresponding SDR RGB values (r, g, b) — and outputs a 3-channel gain or gamma map value at that location. The SDR image is 8-bit BT.709/sRGB; the reconstructed HDR target is BT.2020. The entire model is stored as 10 KB of MLP weights, independent of image resolution, and reconstruction is obtained by applying the predicted map to the decoded SDR image (multiplicatively for gain maps, exponentially for gamma maps).

## Method

A two-layer ReLU MLP (16 neurons per layer, 10 KB total) is trained per image to predict the SDR-to-HDR residual. Each of the five input dimensions is expanded via a 24-D sinusoidal embedding, yielding a 120-D network input; the network is optimized with MSE loss over 1000 Adam iterations (~4 s on a Quadro RTX 6000) using 65,536 randomly sampled pixel locations per batch. Two key design choices drive performance: (1) conditioning on SDR color values makes the target highly correlated with the input, enabling fast convergence compared to coordinate-only INRs; (2) replacing the multiplicative gain map with an exponential (gamma) residual — g = log(H+ε)/log(S+ε) — better approximates the non-linear structure of tone mapping operators. A meta-initialization step pre-trains the MLP for 10,000 iterations on 50 synthetic chromatic noise images generated from spatiochromatic natural image statistics, accelerating per-image optimization and improving reconstruction quality.

## Results

On the pooled Cyriac et al. and Chen et al. datasets, Gamma-MLP (the proposed method with exponential residual) achieves the best PSNR of 48.53 dB, lowest ΔE₀₀ of 0.78, highest SSIM of 0.993, and lowest ΔE_IPT of 3.91, while using only 10 KB of metadata. The gain-map variant (Gain-MLP) also outperforms all traditional compression methods (JPEG, HEIC, JPEG-XL) by a wide margin (e.g., 47.60 vs. 42.21 PSNR for the best traditional method, Gamma-HEIC). The rate-distortion analysis (Fig. 5) confirms that exponential gamma-map residuals consistently yield superior reconstructions at very low bit rates across both traditional and MLP-based encoding paradigms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Direct-MLP [24] (Le et al., CVPR 2023), Gamma-MLP wins on every metric at the same 10 KB model size: PSNR improves from 46.30 to 48.53 dB, ΔE₀₀ drops from 0.96 to 0.78, SSIM rises from 0.988 to 0.993, and ΔE_IPT falls from 4.66 to 3.91. Against MLP-ITM [26] (Liu et al., MM 2024), Gamma-MLP outperforms on PSNR (48.53 vs. 47.25), ΔE₀₀ (0.78 vs. 0.87), SSIM (0.993 vs. 0.991), and ΔE_IPT (3.91 vs. 4.28), while MLP-ITM edges ahead on HDR-VDP3 by a negligible 0.02 (9.13 vs. 9.11). Critically, Gamma-MLP achieves these results at only 10 KB versus MLP-ITM's 34 KB — a 3.4× reduction in metadata size. All MLP-based methods substantially outperform the best traditional compression baseline (Gamma-HEIC at 42.21 PSNR, 18.2 KB), with Gamma-MLP gaining over 6 dB in PSNR while using less than half the storage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of implicit neural representations (INRs) for image encoding and the gain map framework standardized by Adobe, Android UltraHDR, and ISO 21496 for HDR metadata. It builds directly on Le et al.'s GamutMLP [24], which first proposed using (x,y,r,g,b) inputs to an MLP for color recovery, and Liu et al.'s MLP-ITM [26], which introduced spatial-color network pairs for embedded inverse tone mapping. The key departures are: (1) adopting the exponential residual (gamma map) formulation from the authors' prior work [5] instead of the standard multiplicative gain map, and (2) introducing a meta-initialization procedure using chromatic noise images generated from spatiochromatic natural image statistics, which accelerates convergence and improves reconstruction quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is a fixed 10 KB metadata footprint that requires no per-image-size or per-quality-factor adjustments, making it attractive for real-time HDR display pipelines where traditional compression introduces block artifacts and banding. The finding that the exponential residual consistently improves reconstruction across both traditional and MLP-based encoders suggests the choice of residual formulation is more impactful than the compression method itself. However, the fixed model size limits scalability to higher resolutions, and the MLP has limited ability to reconstruct clipped information within practical training times. An open question is whether adaptive bit-rate allocation (varying MLP size with image resolution) can maintain the quality advantage at 4K and beyond.
