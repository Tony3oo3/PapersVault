## Problem

Existing promptable segmentation models (SAM, SAM2) operate exclusively on images or videos, leaving LiDAR point clouds unaddressed despite their critical role in autonomous driving. No prior work unifies 2D and 3D segmentation across synchronized camera and LiDAR streams with cross-modal prompting and temporal consistency, and the annotation cost for multi-modal 4D segmentation remains prohibitive.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250427/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes synchronized camera image sequences (768×768, encoded by Hiera-S) and LiDAR point cloud sequences (voxelized at 0.15 m, encoded by MinkUNet via TorchSparse), together with sparse or dense prompts (points, boxes, masks) that may originate from either modality. It outputs 2D segmentation masks for each image frame and 3D segmentation masks for each LiDAR scan, propagated as temporally consistent masklets across the full sequence. A key property is cross-modal prompting: a single prompt in one modality (e.g., an image click) simultaneously drives segmentation in the other (e.g., LiDAR voxels).

## Method

SAM4D extends SAM2's streaming architecture to the multi-modal domain with two core modules. Unified Multi-modal Positional Encoding (UMPE) lifts image patches into 3D via depth estimation (Lift-Splat-Shoot style) and applies a dual-stage encoding—sinusoidal positional encoding in native space plus an MLP-based 3D embedding—to both image and LiDAR tokens, aligning them in a shared spatial domain while preserving modality-specific structure. Motion-aware Cross-modal Memory Attention (MCMA) performs intra-modal self-attention, inter-modal cross-attention, and temporal cross-attention against a FIFO memory bank, with ego-motion compensation (SE(3) transformation from vehicle odometry) that re-projects stored past-frame features into the current coordinate system before retrieval. For training data, a three-step data engine generates Waymo-4DSeg (300k+ camera-LiDAR masklets) by propagating VFM-derived video masklets (Grounding-DINO + SAM + SAM2) through 4D voxel reconstruction and cross-modal masklet fusion with DBSCAN-based noise filtering.

## Results

On Waymo-4DSeg under the semi-supervised stream object segmentation setting with ground-truth mask prompts, SAM4D achieves 69.8% image mIoU (80.1% J&F) and 55.7% LiDAR mIoU, with 280 and 582 NMP respectively. Cross-modal prompting is effective: a single 3-click image prompt yields 53.1% LiDAR mIoU, while a 3-click LiDAR prompt yields 64.2% image mIoU. On the unseen nuScenes dataset, SAM4D achieves 58.4% image mIoU and 25.9% LiDAR mIoU zero-shot, improving to 67.5% and 44.8% after fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is SAM2+Project, which runs SAM2 video segmentation and projects the resulting 2D masks onto per-frame LiDAR point clouds. SAM4D substantially outperforms this baseline on LiDAR segmentation, achieving 55.7% mIoU versus 32.0% (a 23.7-point gain) and reducing NMP from 383 to 280 on the image side. On image segmentation the two methods are close (69.8% vs. 68.2% mIoU), confirming that SAM4D's advantage lies in its native 3D LiDAR branch and cross-modal attention rather than in the image pathway. Against its own single-modality ablations, the full SAM4D improves LiDAR mIoU from 47.0% (SAM4D-L) to 55.7% and image mIoU from 68.6% (SAM4D-C) to 69.8%, demonstrating that cross-modal interaction contributes meaningfully to both output modalities. The ego-motion ablation (Table 6) further shows that motion-aware memory alignment reduces LiDAR NMP from 746 to 582, a 22% decrease in tracking mismatches, underscoring the importance of the MCMA module for long-horizon consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAM4D extends the SAM2 video segmentation framework (memory attention, streaming architecture, Hiera encoder) into the multi-modal domain by adding a LiDAR branch (MinkUNet/TorchSparse) and a unified 3D positional encoding that lifts image patches via depth estimation (following Lift-Splat-Shoot) into the same spatial domain as LiDAR voxels. It departs from prior LiDAR segmentation work (SAL, PointSAM) that operates frame-by-frame on a single modality, and from multi-modal perception methods (BEV fusion, voxel fusion) that produce only 3D outputs without 2D-3D joint segmentation or cross-modal prompting. The data engine repurposes the VFM annotation pipeline (Grounding-DINO + SAM + SAM2) but adds a 4D voxel reconstruction and DBSCAN-based filtering step to transfer video masklets onto LiDAR point clouds, a step absent from prior VFM-to-3D labeling approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying promptable segmentation across camera and LiDAR in a single model, SAM4D addresses a practical bottleneck in autonomous driving annotation where 2D and 3D labels must be kept instance-consistent over time. The Waymo-4DSeg dataset (300k+ masklets, 1000 clips) provides the first large-scale benchmark for this task, and the cross-modal prompting capability (a single click on one sensor yielding usable masks on the other) could substantially reduce labeling effort. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to additional sensors (radar, event cameras), whether the depth-estimation-based lifting degrades under adverse weather, and whether the FIFO memory bank scales to very long sequences without ego-motion drift accumulation. The zero-shot transfer to nuScenes (25.9% LiDAR mIoU) suggests meaningful but still limited cross-dataset generalization.
