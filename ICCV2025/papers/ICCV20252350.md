## Problem

Single-pass anchor-based lane detectors struggle in challenging scenarios (curved roads, low visibility, heavy occlusion) because they lack a mechanism to iteratively correct uncertain or erroneous predictions. While diffusion models offer iterative refinement, standard 'hot' diffusion injects Gaussian noise that drives lane anchors into geometrically unrealistic positions, violating the strong structural priors of road lanes. No prior work has applied diffusion to lane detection, leaving a gap between the need for progressive refinement and the constraints of lane geometry.

## Input / Output

The method takes a cropped driving-scene RGB image (CULane: 1640×590; TuSimple: 1280×720) and a set of N_s predefined straight-line lane anchors (each parameterized by starting position (x_p, y_p) and slope θ_p) distributed uniformly across the image width. At each diffusion step t, the image is additionally rescaled by a resolution factor γ_t, so the model sees progressively sharper inputs. The output is a set of parameterized lane boundaries, each described by five parameters (x_p, y_p, θ_p, l, δ_x) that encode starting position, initial slope, total length, and incremental horizontal deviations, along with a per-lane confidence score; only the final step's length, deviation, and confidence values are retained.

## Method

CDiffLane replaces stochastic noise corruption with a deterministic cold-diffusion degradation that interpolates between a ground-truth lane and a predefined anchor: a^t = α_t · a_gt + (1−α_t) · a, where α_t decreases monotonically so the degraded representation transitions from the anchor toward the target. A resolution scheduling mechanism (γ_t) feeds the restoration network R_θ (built on CLRNet with DLA-34/ResNet + FPN) images of increasing sharpness across diffusion steps, enabling a genuine coarse-to-fine progression in image space. A learnable α schedule introduces a per-step variation magnitude v_t = α_t − α_{t−1} that weights the regression loss, allowing the model to suppress corrections when image information is insufficient. Training uses focal classification loss plus a v_t-weighted regression loss (smooth L1 on lane parameters and lane-IoU loss), with anchors dynamically updated from the model's own predictions to mitigate sampling drift. At inference, the model starts from the full anchor set on a blurred image and iteratively refines lane positions over T steps as the input image sharpens, converging to precise lane geometry.

## Results

On the CULane test set, CDiffLane (DLA-34) achieves F1@50 of 81.26 and F1@75 of 65.31, with an mF1 of 58.06, outperforming all prior methods. The most notable gain is at the stricter F1@75 threshold, where CDiffLane surpasses the previous best (CLRerNet, 64.30) by 1.01 points and GSENet (64.08) by 1.23 points. On TuSimple, CDiffLane (ResNet-34) reaches F1 of 97.71 with a false positive rate of 1.62%, the lowest among all compared methods. CDiffLane also records the fewest cross-scenario false positives on CULane (893 vs. 1036 for the next best), indicating improved robustness under challenging conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct baseline CLRNet (DLA-34), CDiffLane gains +0.79 on F1@50 and +2.53 on F1@75, confirming that the cold-diffusion refinement loop adds substantial localization precision beyond a single-pass anchor regression. Compared to the strongest recent competitors GSENet (DLA-34) and CLRerNet (DLA-34), CDiffLane leads by +1.23 and +1.24 points respectively at F1@75, while the F1@50 margins are smaller (+0.13 and +0.14), consistent with the paper's observation that most lanes are already detected at the looser threshold. On TuSimple, CDiffLane (ResNet-34) edges out CANet (ResNet-34) by +0.27 F1 and reduces the false positive rate from 2.32% to 1.62%, though it accepts a slightly higher false negative rate (3.01% vs. 2.79%). The ablation in Table 3 further shows that hot diffusion (Gaussian noise on anchors) actually underperforms the CLRNet baseline (79.25/60.77 vs. 80.47/62.78), validating the paper's core claim that deterministic cold degradation is essential for the geometrically constrained lane structure. Overall, CDiffLane wins on every reported metric in both main tables, with the largest relative gains at the stricter IoU threshold and in cross-scenario false-positive suppression.

## Relation to Prior Work

CDiffLane builds on two distinct lineages: the anchor-based lane detection framework of CLRNet (which provides the backbone, FPN feature extraction, and IoU-based regression loss) and the cold diffusion paradigm of Bansal et al. (NeurIPS 2024), which replaces stochastic Gaussian noise with deterministic degradation operators. The key conceptual departure from prior diffusion-for-perception work (e.g., DiffusionDet, DDP) is the rejection of 'hot' noise injection in favor of structured interpolation between predefined lane anchors and ground-truth lanes, motivated by the geometric constraints of lane shapes on the ground plane. Two additional mechanisms—multi-resolution image scheduling and a learnable α interpolation schedule—are introduced to create a genuine coarse-to-fine conditioning signal, addressing the limitation that static image conditioning causes each diffusion step to produce near-identical estimates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first application of diffusion models to lane detection, the paper establishes that deterministic (cold) degradation is essential for structured prediction tasks where random perturbation violates physical constraints—a finding that could generalize to other geometrically constrained perception problems (e.g., 3D pose, molecular conformation). The practical limitation is the added inference cost from iterative refinement, though the authors note that even a single diffusion step exceeds 140 FPS and still outperforms GSENet. Open questions include whether progressive distillation can collapse the multi-step process into a single forward pass without accuracy loss, and whether the resolution-scheduling + learnable-α recipe transfers to other anchor-based detection tasks beyond lanes.
