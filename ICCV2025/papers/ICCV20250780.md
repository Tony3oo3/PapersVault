## Problem

Existing feedforward image-to-3D methods rely on 2D multi-view diffusion that cannot enforce 3D consistency and collapses when the prompt view direction changes, or on triplane-NeRF-based 3D diffusion that is slow and resolution-limited. Moreover, prior work is largely restricted to object-centric generation with synthetic data, leaving large-scale scene reconstruction underexplored and dependent on monocular depth estimators that fail under occlusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250780/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a single RGB image (256×256 or 512×512) plus relative camera poses for N target views, and outputs a fixed-size set of pixel-aligned 3D Gaussians ((N+1)×H×W primitives, each with 3D position, 3×3 covariance, opacity, and RGB color) that can be rasterized to render novel views in ~6 seconds via 30-step DDIM. During training, the input additionally includes N noisy views and M clean novel views with their camera extrinsics/intrinsics, and supervision is provided entirely through 2D renderings (L2 + VGG-19 perceptual loss) rather than explicit 3D ground truth.

## Method

DiffusionGS bakes 3D Gaussian Splatting directly into the diffusion denoiser: instead of the standard ε-prediction, a Vision Transformer backbone (with adaptive layer-norm timestep conditioning) predicts per-pixel 3D Gaussian primitives at every timestep, and the predicted Gaussians are rendered via differentiable rasterization to produce multi-view images that supervise the network. A key architectural choice is the Reference-Point Plücker Coordinate (RPPC) camera conditioning, which replaces the moment vector in standard Plücker rays with the point on each ray closest to the world origin, providing richer depth and geometry cues that flow through skip connections to the Gaussian decoder. To handle the domain gap between object and scene data, the paper introduces a scene-object mixed training strategy that constrains viewpoint selection via two angle constraints (position and orientation) and uses dual Gaussian decoders with different depth ranges ([0.1, 4.2] for objects, [0, 500] for scenes), followed by task-specific finetuning. A point-distribution loss during warm-up encourages concentrated Gaussian placement for object-centric generation.

## Results

On the GSO object generation benchmark, DiffusionGS achieves 22.07 PSNR, 0.8545 SSIM, 0.1115 LPIPS, and 11.52 FID, surpassing the strongest 3D diffusion baseline DMV3D by 1.25 dB in PSNR and 21.96 in FID. On ABO, the gains are 2.20 dB in PSNR (25.89 vs. 23.69) and 23.25 in FID (9.03 vs. 32.28) over DMV3D. For single-view scene reconstruction on RealEstate10K, DiffusionGS reaches 21.63 PSNR and 15.87 FID, outperforming Flash3D by 1.34 dB in PSNR and 19.16 in FID, all without a depth estimator. Inference takes approximately 6 seconds on a single A100 GPU, over 5× faster than DMV3D (31.4 s) and 10× faster than 12345++ (60 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffusionGS outperforms all 11 baselines across every evaluated metric and dataset. Among 3D diffusion methods, DMV3D [75] is the closest competitor, trailing by 1.25–2.20 dB in PSNR and 21.96–23.25 in FID on GSO/ABO; DiffSplat [35] scores lower in the user study (3.96 vs. 5.89) and is not directly compared on ABO/GSO. Among 2D multi-view diffusion methods, LGM [69] and CRM [72] produce over-smoothed or geometrically distorted outputs, with LGM achieving only 14.27 PSNR on GSO; 12345++ [38] scores 4.56 in the user study but requires 60 s inference. The SDS-based DreamGS [68] is the weakest baseline (2.93 user-study score, 120 s runtime). For scene reconstruction on RealEstate10K, DiffusionGS beats Flash3D [67] (20.29 PSNR, 35.03 FID) by 1.34 dB and 19.16 FID, and substantially outperforms PixelNeRF [80] (17.46 PSNR) and Splatter-Image [66] (18.21 PSNR), which produce blurry or artifact-laden novel views. The 2D view-synthesis method PhotoNVS [82] achieves only 15.31 PSNR with a 2417 s post-hoc Gaussian fitting step, versus DiffusionGS's 21.63 PSNR in 6 s total. GS-LRM [87] lags DiffusionGS by 7.11 dB on ABO and 4.37 dB on GSO in PSNR, confirming that the single-stage 3DGS-diffusion design yields substantially better geometry and texture fidelity than feed-forward Gaussian reconstruction alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm (2D multi-view diffusion followed by 3D reconstruction) and from triplane-NeRF-based 3D diffusion methods by baking 3D Gaussian Splatting directly into the diffusion denoiser, producing pixel-aligned Gaussian primitives at every timestep. It builds on the DDPM framework for the forward/reverse process, adopts the pixel-aligned Gaussian parameterization from Splatter Image for a fixed-count output, and introduces a novel Reference-Point Plücker Coordinate (RPPC) camera conditioning that replaces the standard moment vector with the ray's closest point to the world origin. The scene-object mixed training strategy is a new contribution addressing the domain gap between synthetic object datasets (Objaverse, MVImgNet) and real scene datasets (RealEstate10K, DL3DV10K), which prior single-view 3D methods have not jointly exploited.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiffusionGS offers a practical single-stage pipeline that handles both object generation and scene reconstruction without a monocular depth estimator, at ~6 s inference on one A100—over 5× faster than the prior SOTA 3D diffusion (DMV3D) and 10× faster than multi-view diffusion methods. The scene-object mixed training strategy and RPPC are reusable components that could benefit other 3D generation or reconstruction pipelines. Open questions include whether the pixel-aligned Gaussian formulation scales to very large or unbounded scenes, whether the 30-step DDIM sampling can be further compressed (e.g., via consistency distillation), and whether the approach generalizes to dynamic or 4D content.
