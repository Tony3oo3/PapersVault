## Problem

Existing global SfM methods for multi-camera systems either accumulate drift through incremental registration or jointly estimate internal and global rotations in a highly non-convex manner that is sensitive to outlier relative rotations. These approaches also rely on biased distance-based translation objectives that fail in degenerate collinear configurations, limiting their applicability to large-scale real-world multi-camera reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251087/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a view graph built from a multi-camera image sequence (stereo, 4-camera, or 6-camera rigs) and per-camera intrinsics, where each edge carries a relative pose (rotation + normalized translation) and inlier feature matches. It outputs global camera poses decomposed into rigid-unit poses and fixed internal camera poses, plus a 3D point cloud, all refined through multi-camera bundle adjustment. The rigid-unit decomposition reduces the unknown parameter space and enforces fixed internal geometry across all images captured simultaneously.

## Method

MGSfM decouples multi-camera rotation averaging into two sequential steps: first, conventional rotation averaging estimates all global camera rotations without multi-camera constraints, from which internal camera rotations are derived per rigid unit and robustly aggregated via geodesic median; second, rigid unit rotations are computed on Lie algebra using the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula with the now-fixed internal rotations, enabling linear optimization. For translation averaging, the method employs a hybrid strategy: camera positions are initialized with a convex L1 distance-based objective over relative translations, 3D points are triangulated via a convex cross-product L1 formulation, and then both are jointly refined with a non-bilinear L2 angle-based objective that incorporates feature tracks (camera-to-point constraints) alongside relative translations. This hybrid design avoids the sensitivity of random initialization used by bilinear formulations while eliminating the bias of pure distance-based objectives, and the multi-camera bundle adjustment fixes internal rotations in the first round before jointly optimizing all parameters.

## Results

On the KITTI-360 dataset (4-camera system, 10 sequences up to 11,508 images), MGSfM achieves median rotation errors of 0.3°–1.1° and median position errors of 0.8 m–2.0 m, substantially outperforming COLMAP (which fails with 20.7°/88.4 m on data 0007) and MMA (10.7°/30.5 m on data 0002). On the KITTI Odometry stereo dataset (11 sequences), MGSfM achieves median rotation errors of 0.1°–0.4° and median position errors of 0.1 m–1.3 m, matching or exceeding all baselines. In terms of efficiency, MGSfM is more than 10× faster than the incremental MCSfM on KITTI-360, and reconstructs a 29,000-image six-camera dataset in approximately one hour.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MGSfM outperforms COLMAP (incremental SfM) by a wide margin on KITTI-360, where COLMAP's error accumulation produces 20.7° median rotation and 88.4 m median position errors on data 0007, versus 0.6°/2.0 m for MGSfM. Against GLOMAP (global SfM), MGSfM is competitive on well-constrained sequences (e.g., data 0009: both achieve 0.4°/0.9 m) but clearly superior on sequences with many outliers or weak loop closures (data 0007: 0.6°/2.0 m vs. 1.1°/100 m; data 0010: 1.1°/1.8 m vs. 1.8°/200 m). MMA (multi-camera global) suffers from its joint rotation estimation being sensitive to outlier relative rotations, yielding 10.7°/30.5 m on data 0002 and 11.4°/39.7 m on data 0006, while MGSfM achieves 0.7°/1.2 m and 0.7°/1.5 m respectively. MCSfM (multi-camera incremental) is the closest competitor, matching MGSfM on most sequences but only winning on the small-scale data 0007 (0.3°/1.4 m vs. 0.6°/2.0 m); however, it is more than 10× slower. The ablation DMRA+MGP (same decoupled rotations but GLOMAP's bilinear translation objective) confirms the importance of the hybrid non-bilinear refinement: it fails catastrophically on data 0007 (700 m median position error) and is slightly worse on data 0010 (2.1 m vs. 1.8 m).

## Relation to Prior Work

MGSfM extends the multi-camera global SfM framework introduced by MMA [12], which decomposes camera poses into rigid-unit poses and internal camera poses, but departs from it in two critical ways: it decouples the highly non-convex joint rotation estimation into two sequential standard rotation averaging problems (using Chatterjee & Govindu's Lie-algebraic method [7] for both steps), and it replaces MMA's biased distance-only translation averaging with a hybrid pipeline that combines convex L1 distance-based initialization (building on Tao et al. [46]) with a non-bilinear angle-based refinement (inspired by but distinct from GLOMAP's bilinear formulation [41]). The decoupling strategy removes the requirement for overlapping fields of view between cameras within a rigid unit, a limitation that constrained MMA's applicability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As multi-camera rigs (6-camera Insta360, 4-camera street-view, stereo) become standard in autonomous driving and robotics, a robust and efficient global SfM pipeline that exploits their fixed internal geometry is practically essential. MGSfM's ability to handle 29,000+ image datasets in under an hour while outperforming both incremental (COLMAP, MCSfM) and global (GLOMAP, MMA) baselines on degenerate collinear trajectories makes it a strong candidate for production-grade mapping pipelines. Open questions include whether the rigid-unit assumption generalizes to loosely-coupled or dynamically reconfigurable camera arrays, and whether the two-stage rotation decoupling can be further accelerated for real-time or near-real-time applications.
