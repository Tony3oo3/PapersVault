## Problem

Existing shadow removal methods treat the task as a monolithic end-to-end problem, yet shadows introduce three intertwined degradations—reduced brightness, texture deterioration, and color distortion—that are difficult to address simultaneously. Retinex-based decomposition, commonly borrowed from low-light enhancement, fails in shadow regions because the reflectance map exhibits substantial per-channel color bias (especially in red and blue), violating the illumination-invariance assumption. This paper identifies that a clean separation of luminance/texture from color is needed before targeted restoration can succeed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single shadow RGB image and outputs a shadow-free RGB image. The input is first converted to YCbCr space, where the Y (luminance) channel is fed to LRNet and the Cb/Cr (chrominance) channels serve as conditioning input to CRNet; the two processed components are recombined via the inverse YCbCr transform. Training uses 384×384 random crops with shadow masks (from DHAN) as auxiliary supervision, and evaluation is performed at 256×256 resolution on full-resolution inference outputs.

## Method

ShadowHack decomposes the problem via an invertible YCbCr split into a luminance restoration subtask (LRNet) and a color regeneration subtask (CRNet). LRNet is a U-shaped transformer using Local Range Blocks in shallow layers and a novel RecTified Attention (RTA) module in deeper layers; RTA computes two attention maps—one where Q/K are formed from concatenated luminance and color features, another from color features alone—and subtracts the second (scaled by λ=0.7) from the first to cancel chrominance-induced distraction in the attention matrix. CRNet is a dual-encoder U-shaped network that injects a ConvNeXt-v2 atto color encoder (pre-trained on ImageNet-21k, 2M params) to extract multi-scale Cb/Cr features, which are then fused into skip connections via cross-attention modules where restored luminance features act as query/key and color features act as value. A multiple-checkpoint training strategy randomly samples early LRNet checkpoints during CRNet training to bridge the train-test luminance gap without extra inference cost.

## Results

On ISTD+, ShadowHack achieves the best overall PSNR of 36.31 dB and lowest RMSE of 2.48 (LAB), surpassing the previous best (RASM: 36.16 dB, 2.53 RMSE). On SRD, it attains 35.94 dB PSNR and 2.90 RMSE, outperforming the prior best (HomoFormer: 35.37 dB, 3.33 RMSE) by a notable 0.57 dB and 0.43 RMSE respectively. In the shadow region specifically, ShadowHack reaches 40.56 dB PSNR on ISTD+ and 39.47 dB on SRD, with the largest RMSE gains concentrated in the color-sensitive shadow areas.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ShadowHack outperforms all 12 baselines on overall (ALL) PSNR and RMSE for both ISTD+ and SRD. On ISTD+, it edges out RASM (the closest competitor) by 0.15 dB PSNR (36.31 vs 36.16) and 0.05 RMSE (2.48 vs 2.53), though RASM retains a slight 0.17 dB advantage in the shadow-region PSNR (40.73 vs 40.56). On SRD, the margin is substantially larger: ShadowHack beats HomoFormer by 0.57 dB PSNR and 0.43 RMSE overall, and by 0.66 dB in the shadow region. Against diffusion-based methods (ShadowDiffusion, DeS3), ShadowHack leads by 0.59–1.21 dB PSNR and 0.73–1.03 RMSE on SRD, while also being far more computationally efficient. OmniSR+GM reports only overall PSNR (34.20 on ISTD+, 34.56 on SRD) and trails ShadowHack by over 2 dB, suggesting its semantic-attention approach does not fully resolve the color-bias problem that the luminance-color decomposition directly targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

ShadowHack carves new ground by repurposing the YCbCr color-space decomposition as a divide-and-conquer strategy for shadow removal, departing from the dominant Retinex-based illumination-reflectance split used in prior low-light and shadow work. The authors argue that Retinex's illumination-invariant reflectance assumption breaks in shadow regions due to channel-specific color bias (predominantly in R and B), motivating a luminance-first, color-second pipeline. Architecturally, LRNet builds on the U-shaped transformer lineage (Restormer, Uformer, RASM, HomoFormer) but introduces a rectified attention mechanism inspired by differential transformers to counteract a 'distraction' phenomenon in deep attention layers, while CRNet borrows the cross-attention color-injection paradigm from exemplar-based colorization and uses a pre-trained ConvNeXt-v2 as a frozen color encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean separation of luminance recovery from color regeneration offers a principled alternative to end-to-end shadow removal, and the diagnostic showing that Retinex reflectance is contaminated by shadow color bias is a useful correction to a widely-used assumption. However, the performance gains over the strongest baselines (RASM, HomoFormer) are modest—roughly 0.15 dB PSNR on ISTD+ and 0.57 dB on SRD—limiting the practical impact. Open questions include whether the YCbCr decomposition generalizes to other illumination degradations (e.g., mixed lighting, indoor scenes), whether the rectified attention mechanism transfers to other restoration tasks, and whether the reliance on (even refined) shadow masks constrains real-world deployment.
