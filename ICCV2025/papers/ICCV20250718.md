## Problem

Current vision-based ML models, trained predominantly on able-bodied individuals, fail to accurately detect and analyze people with transfemoral prosthetic legs due to the unique visual appearance and atypical movement patterns of prosthetic limbs. No existing dataset simultaneously provides video, segmentation, pose, and gait-level annotations for prosthetic users testing multiple prosthetic configurations, leaving a critical gap for both model development and clinical gait analysis.

## Input / Output

The dataset consists of paired frontal and sagittal video clips (1920×1080, 30 fps, 8–20 s inside parallel bars, 2–40 s outside) from four above-knee amputees across 144 walking trials with multiple prosthetic types. The benchmark pipeline consumes bounding boxes and segmentation masks from YOLO11 tracking, feeds cropped regions into RTMPose for 23-keypoint estimation (17 body + 6 foot per COCO-WholeBody), and passes 12 lower-body keypoint coordinate sequences into an LSTM for gait classification. Gait annotations additionally include four textual components—category, specific deviation, corrective adjustment, and clinical reasoning—enabling downstream LLM-based applications.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is the ProGait dataset (412 clips, 4 subjects, multiple prosthetic knee types and configurations) annotated via a human-in-the-loop pipeline: GroundingDINO localizes the subject, SAM2 generates and tracks segmentation masks with manual correction for occlusions, and an iteratively fine-tuned RTMW model produces pose keypoints with <25% of frames requiring manual fix. For benchmarks, YOLO11 is fine-tuned for VOS (evaluated by mIoU), RTMPose is fine-tuned for 2D HPE (evaluated by AP@[0.5,0.95]), and a custom 128-dim LSTM classifies 9 gait categories from temporal keypoint sequences (evaluated by Top-1 and balanced accuracy). All models use a 70/20/10 split with strict subject-level separation between train and test to prevent data leakage. A key empirical finding is that the sagittal view alone yields substantially higher gait classification accuracy (82.6% Top-1) than combining both views, and that 12 lower-body keypoints suffice, suggesting gait is fundamentally driven by lower-body dynamics.

## Results

On the ProGait dataset, the fine-tuned RTMPose-ProGait achieves 0.947 AP@[0.5,0.95] on 2D pose estimation, outperforming the best pre-trained baseline (RTMPose at 0.855) by ~11%. For lower-body keypoints (knee, ankle, foot), RTMPose-ProGait reaches 0.918 AP-leg versus 0.804 for the original RTMPose, a 14% gain. In video object segmentation, YOLO11-ProGait achieves 0.847 mIoU, improving over the pre-trained YOLO11 (0.784) by 8%. For gait classification, a custom LSTM using sagittal-view pose sequences attains 82.6% Top-1 accuracy (81.2% balanced accuracy with 12 lower-body keypoints) across 9 gait categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For 2D pose estimation, RTMPose-ProGait (0.947 AP) decisively outperforms all four pre-trained baselines: HRNet (0.750), ViPNAS (0.761), ViTPose (0.830), and the original RTMPose (0.855), with the largest gap over HRNet and ViPNAS (~19–20%). On the clinically critical lower-body keypoints (knee, ankle, foot), the advantage widens further: RTMPose-ProGait (0.918) beats RTMPose (0.804) by 11.4 points and HRNet (0.625) by 29.3 points, confirming that pre-trained models struggle most with prosthetic limbs. In video object segmentation, YOLO11-ProGait (0.847 mIoU) improves over pre-trained YOLO11 (0.784) by 8%, though Grounded SAM2 with the prompt 'a human with prosthetic leg' reaches 0.964 mIoU—albeit with occasional tracking failures on the prosthetic leg. For gait classification on the full dataset, the fine-tuned GPGait (0.457) and GaitGraph2 (0.440) slightly outperform the paper's LSTM-ProGait (0.372), but all methods show substantial gains over their pre-trained counterparts (e.g., GPGait: 0.388 → 0.457), validating the dataset's utility for adaptation. The LSTM's sagittal-view-only configuration (82.6% Top-1) far exceeds its whole-dataset performance (37.2%), indicating that the frontal view introduces noise for gait pattern discrimination.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of vision-based gait analysis and prosthetic rehabilitation. Prior gait datasets (GAVD, Health&Gait) and the kinematic/kinetic dataset of 18 above-knee amputees [11] either exclude prosthetic limbs entirely or lack raw video, leaving a gap that ProGait fills by providing paired frontal/sagittal video with segmentation, pose, and clinical gait annotations for transfemoral prosthesis users. Methodologically, the annotation pipeline repurposes existing tools—GroundingDINO, SAM2, and RTMW—within a human-in-the-loop framework, while the benchmark baselines (YOLO11, RTMPose, LSTM) are standard architectures fine-tuned on the new domain rather than novel architectures. The paper thus positions itself as a dataset-and-benchmark contribution that exposes and quantifies the domain gap between able-bodied training data and prosthetic-user inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ProGait is, to the authors' knowledge, the first multi-purpose video dataset that simultaneously supports segmentation, pose estimation, and clinically annotated gait classification for transfemoral prosthesis users, directly addressing a gap that hampers scalable, non-invasive gait assessment in clinical settings. The demonstrated 9–30% improvements over zero-shot SOTA models after fine-tuning validate that domain-specific data can meaningfully close the prosthetic-detection gap. However, the dataset's scale (4 subjects, 412 clips, single institution) limits generalizability, and the gait classification accuracy (~81% sagittal-only) leaves room for improvement before clinical deployment. Open questions include whether the textual gait annotations can be effectively leveraged with LLMs for automated prosthesis alignment recommendations, and whether the findings transfer to transtibial (below-knee) amputees or outdoor environments.
