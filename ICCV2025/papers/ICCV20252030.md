## Problem

Existing deepfake detection methods are designed for single-face scenarios and ignore the social contextual cues—scene-motion coherence, inter-face appearance compatibility, gaze alignment, and face-body consistency—that humans naturally use to spot fakes in group settings. No established benchmark or method addresses frame-level complete multi-face detection, where every face in a frame must be correctly classified to preserve the full interpretation of a social scene.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes multi-face video frames at 720×1280 (for the scene-motion module, which requires temporal sequences) and 224×224 crops (for the appearance, gaze, and body-face modules); image-only datasets like OpenForensics are replicated into pseudo-sequences to feed M1. Outputs include per-face fake/real labels, a frame-level complete detection score that is correct only when all faces in the frame are classified correctly, and a natural-language explanation generated by invoking ChatGPT with the four module scores as prompts.

## Method

HICOM comprises four modules, each grounded in a specific human-cognitive cue identified through a two-phase crowdsourced study (2,000 samples in phase 1; 920 manipulated samples in phase 2). M1 extracts multi-scale face-and-context features across frames via RoIAlign and a spatio-temporal dynamic inference network, trained jointly with face-level and frame-level cross-entropy losses. M2 uses a Transformer to encode face crops and applies a combined cross-entropy plus contrastive loss to penalize appearance inconsistencies among co-occurring faces. M3 trains a ResNet gaze-locking classifier (pretrained on Columbia Gaze-DataSet) and applies a majority-vote decision rule that flags a face only when most others look at the camera. M4 independently predicts age and gender from face crops and from body regions with the face occluded by GaussianBlur, flagging mismatches. The four module outputs are fused with an XOR operation so that any single detected anomaly triggers a fake-face prediction, and the module weights reflect the relative prevalence of each cue in the human study (M1 and M2 primary; M3 and M4 auxiliary).

## Results

On in-dataset evaluation, HICOM achieves 91.3% FCAC / 92.1% FCAU on FFIW, 97.8% FCAC / 98.9% FCAU on OpenForensics, and 93.5% FCAC / 94.6% FCAU on DF-Platter, improving average FCAC by 3.3% and FCAU by 3.1% over the next-best method. Under six unseen real-world perturbations (OpenForensics), HICOM gains 2.8% in FCAU over the previous best. On the unseen ManualFake dataset (cross-dataset generalization), HICOM outperforms all baselines by 5.8% in frame-level complete multi-face detection accuracy (FCAC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HICOM outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and metric. Among multi-face methods, the closest competitors are FILTER (93.6/93.7 FCAC/FCAU on OpenForensics) and COMISC (93.7/94.8 on OpenForensics), but HICOM leads by 4.2 and 4.1 points in FCAC/FCAU respectively on that dataset, and by 4.0/4.0 on FFIW. Single-face baselines (SBI, TALL, Li et al.) degrade substantially in frame-level complete detection (e.g., SBI drops from 94.0% FAC to 84.1% FCAC on FFIW), confirming that per-face classification alone is insufficient for multi-face scenarios. On the unseen ManualFake dataset, HICOM achieves 66.3% and 70.9% FCAC (trained on DF-Platter and FFIW respectively), surpassing the best baseline (TALL at 59.9% and 65.8%) by 6.4 and 5.1 points. Under perturbations, HICOM's contextual cues (gaze, body-face consistency) remain detectable, yielding 78.6% FCAC versus FILTER's 74.3% and COMISC's 73.0%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from existing multi-face deepfake detection methods (FILTER, COMISC, MoNFAP, S-MIL) that rely on researcher-designed heuristics or black-box classifiers, instead grounding its architecture in cues derived from systematic human studies. It repurposes established components—spatio-temporal dynamic inference networks for group activity recognition, ResNet-based age/gender classifiers, and Transformer-based contrastive learning—reorganizing them around four human-identified cognitive cues (scene-motion coherence, inter-face appearance, gaze alignment, face-body consistency) and fusing them via a simple XOR operation. The frame-level complete multi-face detection metric (FCAC/FCAU) is introduced as a new evaluation criterion absent from prior multi-face benchmarks, which typically report only face-level or video-level accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes frame-level complete multi-face detection as a distinct and arguably more practically relevant evaluation paradigm, since missing even one fake face in a social scene can mislead viewers about what actually occurred. The human-study-driven design offers interpretability (each module maps to a named cognitive cue) and the LLM explanation layer makes predictions more transparent to end users. However, the modular XOR fusion is not end-to-end optimized, some cues (gaze, body-face) have limited applicability outside deepfake contexts, and the generalization evidence rests on a single unseen dataset (ManualFake). Open questions include whether these human-derived cues remain sufficient as generation techniques evolve and whether the modular design can be made end-to-end trainable without losing interpretability.
