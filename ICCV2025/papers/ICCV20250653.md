## Problem

Diffusion Transformers (DiT) rely on quadratic-complexity self-attention, making high-resolution image generation prohibitively slow and memory-intensive on resource-limited hardware. While linear attention has been validated in visual recognition, its suitability for denoising diffusion—where locality matters for noise prediction—remains under-explored. The paper asks: how can a fully converged, pre-trained DiT be converted to a linear-attention DiT with minimal performance loss and without complex architectural modifications?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250653/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For class-conditional generation, the model takes an ImageNet class label and a noisy latent (encoded via SDXL VAE) and produces a 256×256 or 512×512 RGB image over 250 sampling steps. For text-to-image, it consumes a text prompt (up to 300 tokens, encoded by Flan-T5-XXL) and a noisy latent to synthesize 512px or 1K-resolution images in 20 sampling steps. The 1K model can be deployed offline on a Windows 11 laptop with 8-bit quantized text encoder and fp16 diffusion weights.

## Method

LiT replaces every self-attention block in DiT or PixArt-Σ with a simple ReLU linear attention augmented by a single depth-wise convolution (kernel 5) on the value matrix, which compensates for linear attention's lack of locality. A key empirical finding is that using very few heads (2–4) increases theoretical GMACs without increasing measured latency—a 'free lunch' that raises the model's compute budget and generation quality. All pre-trained DiT/PixArt-Σ weights are inherited except the Q, K, V, and O projection matrices of the attention module, which are randomly initialized. Training employs a hybrid distillation loss that supervises the student not only on predicted noise (MSE against the teacher DiT) but also on the variance of the reverse diffusion process, with the variance term kept moderate (λ₂=0.05). This combination allows LiT to reach comparable FID to DiT using only 20–33% of the original training steps.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, LiT-XL/2-G (cfg=1.50) achieves FID 2.32 and IS 265.20 using only 20% of DiT-XL/2's training steps (1.4M vs. 7M), closely matching DiT-XL/2-G (FID 2.27). On ImageNet 512×512, LiT-XL/2-G (cfg=1.50) reaches FID 3.69 with ~23% of DiT's training steps, compared to DiT's FID 3.04. For text-to-image generation, LiT-0.6B (1024px) achieves a GenEval overall score of 0.48, comparable to PixArt-Σ (0.52) while reducing 2K-resolution latency by ~55% (14.59s vs. 32.16s). At smaller scales, LiT-S/B/L/2 trained for only 100K steps already outperforms the corresponding DiT trained for 400K steps across all FID metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LiT-XL/2-G (cfg=1.50) achieves FID 2.32 on ImageNet 256×256, narrowly trailing DiT-XL/2-G (2.27) and SiT-XL (2.06) but outperforming DiM-H (2.40), DiM-L (2.64), Simple Diffusion U-ViT (2.77), and LDM-4-G (3.60). On 512×512, LiT-XL/2-G (FID 3.69) is competitive with DiT-XL/2-G (3.04) despite using only ~23% of the training steps, and surpasses ADM-G (7.72) and Simple Diffusion U-Net (4.28). In text-to-image generation, LiT-0.6B (1024px) scores 0.48 on GenEval overall, slightly below PixArt-Σ (0.52) but above Lumina-Next (0.46), LlamaGen (0.32), and SDv1.5 (0.43). At smaller model scales (S/B/L), LiT trained for just 100K steps outperforms the corresponding DiT trained for 400K steps on FID (e.g., LiT-L/2: 17.80 vs. DiT-L/2: 23.33). In efficiency, LiT reduces 2K-resolution generation latency by ~55% compared to PixArt-Σ (14.59s vs. 32.16s) and reduces GPU memory from ~14 GB to 4 GB at 2048px.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a systematic, minimal-modification study of converting pre-trained Diffusion Transformers (DiT) into linear-attention variants, deliberately departing from prior efficient-attention diffusion works (DiG, DiM, SANA, CLEAR, Mediators) that introduce complex architectural changes such as gated linear attention, state-space models, or additional mediator tokens. It borrows the depth-wise convolution remedy from Flatten Transformer (Han et al., ICCV 2023) for visual recognition and adapts it to the denoising context, while introducing a hybrid distillation objective (noise + reverse-process variance) that differs from the step-reduction distillation common in diffusion literature. The training strategy of inheriting all non-attention weights from a converged DiT teacher and randomly initializing only the linear-attention projections is a novel transfer-learning recipe specific to this architectural swap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, low-risk recipe for any team deploying DiT-based generators at high resolution: replace self-attention with 2-head ReLU linear attention + DWC, inherit weights, and apply hybrid distillation to recover performance in ~20% of the original training budget. The 9× latency reduction and 3.5× memory savings at 2048px make edge and high-resolution deployment substantially more feasible. Open questions include whether the guidelines generalize beyond ImageNet-class-conditional and single text-to-image setups, whether the FID gap at 512px (3.69 vs 3.04) closes with more training, and whether the approach extends to video or 4K generation as the authors note as future work.
