## Problem

No real-world UDC video dataset exists that captures the full complexity of under-display camera degradation, including spatially variant flares, temporally variant flares, and realistic noise patterns. Existing synthetic UDC video datasets (e.g., VidUDC33K, PexelsUDC-T/P) rely on PSF convolution and pixel scaling that produce unrealistic, light-source-invariant flares and fail to model the actual diffraction physics of display panels, rendering models trained on them ineffective on real UDC footage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250924/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 647 paired video clips (116,460 total frames) at 1900×1060×3 resolution and 60 fps, where each pair consists of a UDC-degraded frame and its ground-truth counterpart captured simultaneously through a beam splitter. For restoration, the input is a UDC-degraded video sequence and the output is the restored sequence; for face recognition, the system takes two face crops (one GT, one degraded or restored) and outputs a same/different-person decision. The dataset exclusively features human motions (walking, hand waving, thumbs-up, body swaying) from 22 subjects, making it directly applicable to Face ID-style pair-matching tasks.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a purpose-built video-capturing system that simultaneously acquires clean and UDC-degraded video of the same scene: a Samsung Galaxy Z-Fold 5 UDC display panel is mounted on a non-polarizing cube beam splitter, with two Arducam Hawk-Eye (IMX686) cameras on 6-axis kinematic mounts capturing both optical paths, synchronized via MPI barriers on a Raspberry Pi 5 to within 8 msec. Residual sub-pixel misalignment between paired frames is corrected by iterative DFT-based shift-rotate-crop optimization minimizing a composite loss of spatial MSE, frequency-domain amplitude L1, and phase L1 (with λ₁=λ₃=1, λ₂=0), achieving PCK alignment accuracy of 92–99.7%. The paper then benchmarks six CNN-based restoration models (DISCNet, UDC-UNet, FastDVDNet, EDVR, ESTRNN, DDRNet) trained and tested on UDC-VIT versus VidUDC33K, and evaluates seven face-recognition models from the DeepFace library to quantify how restoration quality (PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS) translates into practical FR accuracy gains.

## Results

On UDC-VIT, the input (degraded) frames have PSNR 16.26, SSIM 0.7366, and LPIPS 0.4117, substantially harder than VidUDC33K's input (PSNR 26.22, SSIM 0.8524, LPIPS 0.2642). The best restoration on UDC-VIT is achieved by UDC-UNet with PSNR 28.00, SSIM 0.8911, and LPIPS 0.1779, followed by ESTRNN (PSNR 25.38) and DISCNet (PSNR 24.70). DDRNet, the only UDC-specific video restoration model, achieves the best result on synthetic VidUDC33K (PSNR 31.91, SSIM 0.9313, LPIPS 0.1306) but drops to PSNR 24.68 on UDC-VIT, falling below UDC-UNet and ESTRNN. The performance rankings of the six models are inconsistent across the two datasets, confirming that synthetic degradations do not reflect real-world UDC characteristics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates six restoration models (re-trained on each dataset) to demonstrate the synthetic-to-real gap. DDRNet [27], the only model purpose-built for UDC video restoration, dominates on synthetic VidUDC33K (PSNR 31.91, LPIPS 0.1306) but drops to PSNR 24.68 on real UDC-VIT, losing to UDC-UNet [29] (PSNR 28.00, LPIPS 0.1779) and ESTRNN [56] (PSNR 25.38, LPIPS 0.2216). UDC-UNet, a still-image UDC restoration model, becomes the top performer on UDC-VIT, suggesting its residual U-Net architecture handles complex real-world flares better than task-specific video models. DISCNet [17] (PSNR 24.70) and FastDVDNet [48] (PSNR 23.89) occupy the middle tier on UDC-VIT, while EDVR [51] (PSNR 23.55) performs worst, indicating that deformable-convolution-based temporal alignment is less effective under severe real UDC degradation. The inconsistent rankings across datasets (e.g., ESTRNN is 2nd on VidUDC33K but 2nd on UDC-VIT, while DDRNet goes from 1st to 5th) are the paper's central empirical argument that synthetic UDC video datasets fail to capture actual degradation characteristics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the real-world UDC data collection paradigm established by UDC-SIT (Ahn et al., NeurIPS'23) from still images to video, using a beam-splitter-based dual-camera system for synchronized capture and DFT-based frame alignment. It departs from the two existing synthetic UDC video datasets (VidUDC33K and PexelsUDC-T/P), which model degradation via PSF convolution or GAN generation, by capturing actual optical degradation including spatially and temporally variant flares. The empirical evaluation draws on both UDC-specific restoration models (DISCNet, UDC-UNet, DDRNet) and general video restoration architectures (FastDVDNet, EDVR, ESTRNN) to benchmark against the synthetic baseline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UDC-VIT is the first real-world UDC video dataset and will likely become the standard benchmark for UDC video restoration, filling a critical gap where all prior video data was synthetic. The finding that DDRNet—the purpose-built UDC video restoration model—performs near the bottom on real data while a general image restoration model (UDC-UNet) leads, underscores that synthetic degradation models are fundamentally misaligned with real optics. Key limitations include hardware specificity (Samsung Galaxy Z-Fold 5 panel), exclusion of fast-moving objects, and the inherent hardware-awareness of UDC degradation, leaving open the question of generalizable, device-agnostic restoration.
