## Problem

Existing training-free classifier-guidance methods for diffusion models face a critical trade-off: direct optimization approaches (e.g., DOODL) require backpropagation through multiple denoising steps, incurring prohibitive GPU memory costs, while estimation-based methods (e.g., FreeDoM, TFG) rely on Tweedie's formula to approximate clean data, which produces visually incomplete outputs in early denoising steps and misdirects the sampling process. These limitations make it impractical to apply guidance to memory-intensive text-to-video diffusion models on consumer-grade hardware.

## Input / Output

TITAN-Guide conditions a pretrained T2V diffusion model (AnimateDiff or CogVideoX) on a text prompt plus an auxiliary signal: reference audio for audio-video alignment, a reference image for style transfer, a DOVER aesthetic target, or masked frames for interpolation. The method operates in latent space at 256×256 (16 frames, AnimateDiff) or 256×384 (32 frames, CogVideoX), and uniquely extends to 384×384 resolution where all baselines exceed 90 GB and fail on a single H100 GPU. The output is a guided video whose content aligns with the specified condition while preserving text-prompt fidelity.

## Method

TITAN-Guide replaces backpropagation with forward-mode automatic differentiation to compute gradient signals for updating diffusion latents, eliminating the memory overhead of storing and reversing the computation graph. Instead of estimating clean data via Tweedie's formula at each step, the method samples the full denoising trajectory from the current noisy latent Z_t all the way to t=0, decodes the clean latent through the VAE decoder, and evaluates a classifier loss (ImageBind, DOVER, or Style-CLIP) on the decoded video. The gradient is then approximated via a forward gradient descent step G_t = ⟨∇f, V_t⟩ V_t, where the direction vector V_t can be drawn from an isotropic Gaussian (random guess), taken as the normalized denoiser output ε_θ(Z_t, t, c) (score-based guess), or computed on a small frame subset and expanded (sampled-gradient guess). This design reduces peak memory to ~20.6 GB—roughly half that of the most efficient baselines—while the full sampling-to-zero strategy yields cleaner guidance signals than single-step Tweedie estimates, improving FVD by over 30 points on audio-video alignment.

## Results

On the VGG-Sound audio-video alignment task (AnimateDiff, 256×256), TITAN-Guide with sampled gradient guesses achieves the best FVD of 331.11 and KVD of 26.19, outperforming DOODL (FVD 334.21) and TFG (FVD 365.19), while using only 20.6 GB of GPU memory versus 41–67 GB for all baselines. For aesthetic guidance, TITAN-Guide attains the highest DOVER score (0.65) compared to DOODL (0.62) and FreeDoM (0.60). On frame interpolation, TITAN-Guide achieves the lowest FVD (168.88) and highest frame consistency (0.934). At 384×384 resolution, TITAN-Guide still operates within 42 GB, whereas baselines exceed 90 GB (beyond H100 capacity).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TITAN-Guide (sampled gradient guesses) outperforms all five baselines on the primary audio-video alignment FVD metric (331.11 vs. 334.21 for DOODL, 365.19 for TFG, 366.02 for MPGD, 390.21 for FreeDoM, and 392.23 for Seeing-and-Hearing), while also achieving the best KVD (26.19), AV-Align (0.480), and frame consistency (0.961). On aesthetic guidance, TITAN-Guide leads with DOVER 0.65, surpassing DOODL (0.62), FreeDoM (0.60), MPGD (0.58), and TFG (0.52). For style guidance, TFG achieves a slightly lower ED (8.92 vs. 9.16), but TITAN-Guide compensates with a higher IB-TV score (0.29 vs. 0.28), indicating better text preservation during style transfer. On frame interpolation, TITAN-Guide edges out DOODL (FVD 168.88 vs. 169.21) and clearly beats TFG (170.89) and FreeDoM (170.77). The most striking advantage is memory: TITAN-Guide requires only 20.6 GB, roughly half of MPGD/TFG (~41 GB) and one-third of DOODL (67.2 GB) or FreeDoM (60.2 GB), making it the only method feasible on a 24 GB consumer GPU.

## Relation to Prior Work

TITAN-Guide sits at the intersection of two lines of work: classifier guidance for diffusion models (Ho et al., FreeDoM, MPGD, TFG) and forward gradient methods as an alternative to backpropagation (Baydin et al., 2022). It adopts the 'sample all the way to t=0' strategy from DOODL, which avoids the inaccurate Tweedie's clean-data estimates used by FreeDoM/MPGD/TFG in early denoising steps, but replaces DOODL's backpropagation through the full sampling chain with forward-mode automatic differentiation. This combination is the paper's core conceptual move: the full-sampling quality of DOODL becomes feasible for memory-intensive T2V models precisely because forward gradients eliminate the need to store and traverse the computation graph backward.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: TITAN-Guide reduces peak GPU memory from 40–67 GB (baselines) to ~20.6 GB for 256×256 video guidance, making classifier-guided T2V feasible on a single consumer GPU, and extends to 384×384 where baselines exceed even H100 capacity. The three gradient-guess strategies (random, score-based, sampled-gradient) offer a useful design space, with sampled-gradient guesses consistently yielding the best quality at the same memory cost. Open questions include whether forward gradients scale to longer videos (beyond 32 frames) or higher resolutions without quality degradation, whether the approach transfers to autoregressive or flow-matching video models, and whether the modest quality gap over DOODL on some metrics (e.g., IB-AV) narrows with better gradient approximations or more denoising steps.
