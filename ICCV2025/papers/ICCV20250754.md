## Problem

Part-level 3D editing in Gaussian Splatting remains unsolved because (1) 2D part segmentation is inherently view-inconsistent due to occlusions and appearance variation, producing unreliable 3D masks, and (2) Score Distillation Sampling is an implicit objective that cannot isolate specific parts or drive semantically low-probability modifications (e.g., changing only one eye's color). Existing methods handle instance-level edits well but fail on fine-grained, localized part modifications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250754/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D Gaussian Splatting scene (either reconstructed from images or generated via text-to-3D), a free-form text edit prompt specifying the desired change, and a part segmentation prompt (e.g., 'left eye') that guides which region to modify. It outputs a fully edited 3D Gaussian Splatting scene where only the target part is altered while the rest of the scene is preserved. The segmentation prompt is consumed via a diffusion model's cross-attention map as a pseudo 2D mask, and the editing is performed entirely through test-time optimization of per-Gaussian parameters (positions, colors, opacities, and the new label parameter).

## Method

RoMaP has two core components. First, 3D-GALP introduces a learnable SH-coefficient parameter r_i per Gaussian to model view-dependent label variation; it is optimized via L1 loss against attention maps from a rendered view, then refined by an anchor-based neighbor consistency loss where anchors are selected by label softness (entropy of cosine-similarity probabilities × variance of SH color across views), enabling precise boundary segmentation. Second, a regularized SDS loss replaces the standard implicit SDS with three regularizers: (a) Gaussian prior removal, which replaces dominant color priors with neutral values to break fixation on the original appearance; (b) explicit 3D/2D masking that blocks gradient updates outside the target region; and (c) an L1 anchor loss against SLaMP-edited images, where SLaMP performs 2D part editing via scheduled latent mixing—starting with a low blending coefficient to generate new context, then sharply increasing it at a tuned timestep t_s to preserve global identity while confining drastic changes to the masked region.

## Results

On the IN2N and NeRF-Art scenes (75 editing prompts per scene), RoMaP achieves the best scores across all four metrics: CLIP 0.277, CLIP_dir 0.205, B-VQA 0.723, and TIFA 0.674. The largest margins over the strongest baseline (DGE) appear in CLIP_dir (0.205 vs. 0.095, more than 2×) and B-VQA (0.723 vs. 0.497, +45%). In the user study, RoMaP scores 0.372 for editing and 0.365 for generation, substantially ahead of the next-best editing method (DGE at 0.224) and generation method (RFDS at 0.234).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoMaP outperforms all six 3D editing baselines (three NeRF-based, three Gaussian-based) on every metric. Among Gaussian Splatting methods, DGE is the closest competitor, trailing RoMaP by 0.076 in CLIP, 0.110 in CLIP_dir, 0.226 in B-VQA, and 0.109 in TIFA; GaussianEditor and GaussCtrl lag further behind, particularly on CLIP_dir (0.087 and 0.044 vs. 0.205). Among NeRF methods, IN2N leads on CLIP (0.248) and TIFA (0.634) but is still surpassed by RoMaP on all four metrics, with the gap most pronounced in CLIP_dir (0.072 vs. 0.205) and B-VQA (0.142 vs. 0.723). In the user study, RoMaP's editing score (0.372) exceeds DGE (0.224) by 66% relative, and its generation score (0.365) surpasses RFDS (0.234) by 56% relative, confirming that the regularized SDS + SLaMP pipeline enables edits (e.g., 'goat's face', 'butterfly lips') that prior methods fail to produce.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) as its scene representation, repurposing the existing SH coefficients to model view-dependent label softness for part segmentation (3D-GALP) rather than solely for view-dependent color. It departs from prior 3D editing methods (DGE, GaussianEditor, GaussCtrl, IN2N) that rely on 2D multi-view segmentation projected onto 3D and SDS-only optimization, arguing that both are fundamentally unreliable for part-level edits due to inconsistent 2D part masks and the implicit, context-biased nature of SDS. The regularized SDS loss with SLaMP scheduled latent mixing and Gaussian prior removal represents a new loss formulation that explicitly anchors optimization to a 2D-edited target while breaking the diffusion model's prior toward the original appearance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RoMaP demonstrates that drastic, semantically unlikely part-level edits (e.g., 'emerald nose,' asymmetric eye colors) are achievable in 3D Gaussian scenes, a capability that prior methods simply cannot provide, which is practically important for 3D content pipelines in AR/VR and robotics. The SH-based label prediction is a lightweight, elegant reuse of existing Gaussian parameters that could be adopted by other 3DGS-based segmentation or editing pipelines. Open questions include scalability to very large scenes with many parts, whether the test-time optimization cost is acceptable for interactive use, and whether the approach extends to 4D or video Gaussian representations. The method still depends on a user-provided segmentation prompt and a 2D diffusion model for SLaMP editing, limiting fully autonomous operation.
