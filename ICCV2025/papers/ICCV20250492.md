## Problem

Prior depth-aware relative pose solvers either used only relative depths (discarding full depth information), failed to model unknown shifts in monocular depth estimates, or relied on computationally expensive hybrid RANSAC with Ceres-based optimization. This left it unclear whether modern monocular depth estimation (MDE) methods genuinely improve pose estimation over traditional 2D-2D point-based solvers, and which solver variants are preferable under different depth quality and camera calibration conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250492/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 2D point correspondences between two images along with monocular depth estimates for those points in both images, where depths may be metric, scale-invariant, or affine-invariant (unknown scale and/or shift). Unlike standard relative pose solvers where translation is estimated up to an unknown scale, here the translation is a full 3-DOF vector because the depth scale is jointly estimated, increasing the problem to 9–11 DOF depending on calibration. The solvers are minimal, requiring only 3–4 point correspondences (versus 5–8 for point-based methods), and output the relative pose, depth scale/shift parameters, and focal length(s) when unknown.

## Method

The core formulation substitutes affine-invariant depth expressions (ηᵢ = s₁(αᵢ+u), λᵢ = s₂(βᵢ+v)) into the 3D-3D projection equation, then eliminates translation using rigid motion constraints (distance preservation under rotation) to reduce the system to a small polynomial system in {s, u, v, f}. For the calibrated case, three points yield a 3×6 Gauss-Jordan elimination followed by a closed-form quartic in u; for shared focal length, four points yield a 24×32 G-J elimination with an 8×8 eigenvalue extraction; for different focal lengths, four points yield a 20×24 G-J elimination with a 4×4 eigenvalue problem. A key empirical finding is that solvers modeling only scale (3PT_s00) often outperform those modeling both scale and shift (4PT_suv), contradicting prior claims that shift modeling is always necessary. All solvers are evaluated within both standard LO-RANSAC (PoseLib) and the hybrid RANSAC framework from MADPose, with Sampson or reprojection error for scoring and local optimization.

## Results

On the ETH3D calibrated case with real (ground-truth) depth and SP+LG matches, the proposed 3PTsuv solver achieves a median pose error of 0.80° and mAA of 88.60% at 29.6 ms, outperforming the 5-point baseline (0.91°, 87.67%, 48.1 ms) in both accuracy and speed. With the hybrid RANSAC scheme, accuracy improves further to 0.52° / 91.42% mAA, though at ~20× the runtime (543 ms). For the shared-focal-length case, the 3PTs00f solver (which does not model shift) achieves 1.75° / 79.17% mAA at 25.1 ms with real depth, beating the MADPose 4PTsuvf solver (1.83° / 78.86% at 112.3 ms) by 4.5× in speed. The 3PTsuv solver runs in 1.46 μs per hypothesis versus 4.45 μs for the MADPose equivalent (Table 2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the 5-point solver [51] (no depth), the proposed 3PTsuv improves median pose error from 0.91° to 0.80° (12% reduction) and mAA from 87.67% to 88.60% with real depth, while also being 39% faster (29.6 vs 48.1 ms). Against MADPose's 3PTsuv(M) [69], the proposed solver achieves comparable accuracy (0.80° vs 0.79°) but runs 30% faster (29.6 vs 41.8 ms); with hybrid RANSAC both reach 0.52° but the proposed solver is slightly faster (543 vs 550 ms). Against Rel3PT [2], which uses only relative depth, the proposed solver is dramatically better with DA v2 depth (0.91° vs 5.57°) and MoGe (0.89° vs 4.74°), confirming that modeling full affine-invariant depth is critical. Against P3P [18], the proposed solver is slightly less accurate with real depth (0.80° vs 0.83°) but faster (29.6 vs 29.7 ms), and significantly better with noisy MDEs (e.g., 0.91° vs 0.90° for DA v2 where P3P degrades). For the shared-focal-length case, the 3PTs00f solver (no shift modeling) outperforms MADPose's 4PTsuvf(M) [69] by 4.5× in speed (25.1 vs 112.3 ms) with comparable or better accuracy (1.75° vs 1.83° with real depth). The 7-point solver [29] and 4p4d [20] baselines for the different-focal-length case are substantially outperformed: 3PTs00f1,2 achieves 2.23° / 69.50% mAA at 12.1 ms versus 8.03° / 38.14% at 24.8 ms for 7PT and 7.58° / 39.34% at 23.7 ms for 4p4d with real depth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of depth-aware relative pose estimation initiated by Astermark et al. (Rel3PT, 3DV 2024) and Ding et al. (3p3d/4p4d, ECCV 2024), which used only relative depths and ignored unknown shifts. It departs from the concurrent MADPose work (Yu et al., CVPR 2025) by deriving smaller, faster solvers that jointly estimate depth scale/shift parameters alongside pose, and by covering more variants of known/unknown/same/different scale and shift configurations. The solver derivations build on the rigid-motion constraint from P3P (Ding et al., CVPR 2023) and the Gröbner basis / syzygy-based elimination framework (Larsson et al., CVPR 2017) for the focal-length cases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first comprehensive, multi-dataset, multi-MDE comparison that answers when monocular depth actually helps relative pose estimation versus traditional 2D-2D solvers, giving practitioners concrete guidance (e.g., use reprojection error with good depth for focal-length problems; fall back to 5PT when depth is noisy). A key practical finding is that modeling unknown shift is not always beneficial—contradicting MADPose's conclusion—which simplifies the solver choice. Remaining limitations include the computational cost of running MDEs, persistent degenerate cases (coplanar points, pure translation), and the fact that hybrid RANSAC, while most accurate, is an order of magnitude slower than standard RANSAC.
