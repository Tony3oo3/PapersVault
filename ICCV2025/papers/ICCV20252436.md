## Problem

Medical videos suffer from camera shake, jitter, and abrupt frame transitions that cause large optical flow errors, making standard alignment-based propagation unreliable. Additionally, the continuous, nuanced tissue structures in medical imagery are highly sensitive to the artifacts, shape distortions, and texture removal introduced by existing VSR models that rely on stacked local CNNs with limited receptive fields.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252436/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

MedVSR takes three consecutive LR medical video frames as input and operates recurrently to produce super-resolved HR frames at a specified scaling factor. The degradation model uses 4× bicubic downsampling with Gaussian noise (covariance 15), and training uses 256×256 HR patches. The model is evaluated on endoscopy (HyperKvasir, LDPolyp), surgical (EndoVis18), and cataract surgery (Cataract-101) videos, with 4 propagation branches in the recurrent architecture.

## Method

MedVSR introduces two core modules built on state-space models (Mamba/Mamba2). Cross State-Space Propagation (CSSP) avoids directly aligning distant frames; instead, it projects the distant (t-2) frame into the SSM's output matrix C and the neighboring (t-1) frame into the input x and input matrix B, so the distant frame *controls* the selective scanning of the neighbor, yielding consistent propagated features without requiring precise optical-flow alignment. Local window partitioning (16×16) and a learnable 2D position embedding mitigate the forgetting problem and preserve spatial relations during recurrent scanning. Inner State-Space Reconstruction (ISSR) then applies SSM over concatenated forward/backward propagated features for long-range spatial modeling (using concatenation rather than gating to preserve fine-grained detail), followed by 7×7 large-kernel separable blocks for short-range aggregation, jointly reducing artifacts and enhancing tissue structure fidelity.

## Results

On HyperKvasir, MedVSR achieves 32.10 PSNR / 0.9069 SSIM, outperforming the second-best VSRT by 0.32 dB while using 3.5× fewer parameters and 10.9× fewer FLOPs. On LDPolyp and EndoVis18, MedVSR reaches 31.83 PSNR / 0.8673 SSIM and 30.83 PSNR / 0.8960 SSIM respectively, both leading all baselines. On Cataract-101, MedVSR attains 36.23 PSNR / 0.9308 SSIM, surpassing IART by 0.15 dB. Inference latency is 1.15 s per 50-frame clip on an RTX 4090, making it 6.0× faster than VSRT, 12.7× faster than RVRT, and 18.7× faster than IART.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MedVSR outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the strongest CNN baseline BasicVSR++ [6], MedVSR gains +0.37 dB PSNR on HyperKvasir, +0.16 dB on LDPolyp, and +0.04 dB on EndoVis18, while matching its parameter count (7.16M vs 7.32M) and FLOPs (9.46T vs 9.47T). Against the strongest transformer-based baselines, MedVSR surpasses VSRT [4] by +0.32 dB PSNR on HyperKvasir with 3.5× fewer parameters and 10.9× fewer FLOPs, and beats IART [59] by +0.13 dB on LDPolyp with 3.6× fewer FLOPs. RVRT [32] is the weakest performer on HyperKvasir (29.26 PSNR) and also the slowest at 16.02 s latency, whereas MedVSR runs in 1.15 s. On Cataract-101, MedVSR leads IART [59] by +0.15 dB PSNR and TCNet [33] by +1.63 dB PSNR while using 0.3× fewer parameters and 3.4× fewer FLOPs than TCNet. The consistent wins across endoscopy, surgical instrument, and cataract surgery scenarios indicate robust generalization across distinct medical imaging conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

MedVSR builds directly on the BasicVSR/BasicVSR++ lineage of bidirectional recurrent feature propagation for VSR, but departs from the core assumption that higher-order propagation of aligned distant frames is beneficial. Instead of warping and propagating the (t-2)-th frame features (as BasicVSR++ does), it repurposes distant frames as control matrices within a state-space model to guide the selective scanning of neighboring frames, drawing on Mamba/Mamba2's input-dependent SSM parameters. The reconstruction side combines SSM-based long-range spatial modeling with large-kernel separable convolutions, a design that contrasts with the stacked small-kernel CNNs in prior VSR work. Prior Mamba-in-vision efforts (VideoMamba, VMamba, etc.) use SSMs for intra-sequence modeling; MedVSR is among the first to exploit SSMs for inter-frame feature propagation in a recurrent VSR pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete failure mode of standard VSR propagation in medical videos—optical flow errors from camera shake and abrupt transitions that make direct alignment of distant frames unreliable—and shows that reframing distant frames as SSM control signals mitigates this without sacrificing temporal information. The practical efficiency (CNN-level latency with transformer-competitive quality) makes the approach deployable in clinical settings where real-time processing matters. Limitations include evaluation only at 4× scale, use of a single Charbonnier loss without perceptual metrics, and testing on four relatively small medical datasets; whether the SSM-based cross-frame propagation generalizes to other modalities (ultrasound, CT video) or to larger scale factors remains open.
