## Problem

Radar point clouds are inherently sparse, especially at long range (>175 m), making it difficult to distinguish true objects from noise and to estimate shape, size, and orientation. Standard temporal aggregation with ego-motion compensation increases density but introduces significant spatial scatter from dynamic objects whose velocities are unknown, degrading detection accuracy. The paper asks how to exploit the per-point Doppler measurement already present in radar data to enhance point cloud density while minimizing this scatter.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-frame radar point clouds in which each point carries a 3D position, a Doppler velocity, and a reflection intensity, together with the ego-vehicle velocity (from GPS-INS, odometry, or radar-based estimation). It outputs an aggregated point cloud in which each retained point is represented by six features: 3D position in the current frame's radar coordinate system, the dynamic Doppler component, reflection intensity, and the original frame time index. Because each point receives a unique aggregation duration, roughly 11 % of dynamic points are discarded relative to fixed-duration aggregation, trading a small loss in point count for substantially reduced scatter.

## Method

DoppDrive is a Doppler-driven temporal aggregation preprocessing step that is detector-agnostic. For every point from a previous frame, the ego-speed Doppler component is subtracted to isolate the dynamic Doppler (the object's radial velocity v), and the point is shifted radially toward the current-frame position by v·(T₀−Tₖ), which eliminates range scatter. Because the tangential velocity is unknown, the method instead imposes a per-point aggregation-duration constraint T₀−Tₖ ≤ D/(|v|·g(θ)) that bounds the expected tangential offset below a tolerance D, where g(θ) is the expectation of tan(θ+α) over the heading-angle distribution. This yields a unique aggregation window for each point rather than a single fixed duration, and points violating the constraint are simply discarded. The resulting dense, low-scatter point cloud is then fed to any standard point-cloud detector (PointPillars-based, FPN-based, or sparse-convolution-based) without architectural modification.

## Results

DoppDrive improves Average Precision (AP) for vehicle detection across all four detectors and three datasets. On the sparsest dataset (aiMotive), SMF gains +7.4 AP over standard aggregation (89.1 vs. 81.7) and +14.5 AP over no aggregation (89.1 vs. 74.6). On the high-resolution RADIal dataset, gains are smaller but consistent (e.g., SMF: 95.8 vs. 92.5 standard, 91.3 no aggregation). At long range (>175 m) on LRR-Sim, DoppDrive's advantage widens again: SMF reaches 69.2 AP vs. 63.1 for standard aggregation and 61.2 for no aggregation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DoppDrive is compared against two baselines: (1) standard temporal aggregation with ego-motion compensation (0.7 s window) and (2) no aggregation (single frame). DoppDrive outperforms both baselines on every detector–dataset combination. The largest gains appear on aiMotive, whose low angular resolution yields the sparsest point cloud: SMF improves by +7.4 AP over standard aggregation (89.1 vs. 81.7) and +14.5 AP over no aggregation. On the denser RADIal dataset, gains shrink to +3.3 AP (SMF: 95.8 vs. 92.5), and on LRR-Sim to +2.2 AP (93.3 vs. 91.1). At long range (>175 m) on LRR-Sim, where sparsity is most severe, DoppDrive's lead over standard aggregation widens to +6.1 AP for SMF (69.2 vs. 63.1), confirming that the method's benefit scales with point-cloud sparsity. The ablation (Table 3) further shows that the radial Doppler shift contributes +3.8 AP and the per-point variable aggregation duration adds another +3.9 AP on top of standard ego-motion aggregation.

## Relation to Prior Work

DoppDrive builds directly on the Doppler decomposition framework of Tan et al. [38], which separates the ego-speed and dynamic components of radar Doppler measurements, and on the standard temporal aggregation with ego-motion compensation used across radar detection pipelines (SMF, K-Radar, Nvradarnet, etc.). The key departure is repurposing the dynamic Doppler component not merely as an additional input feature to the detection network, but as a geometric signal to physically shift prior-frame points along their radial direction, thereby eliminating range scatter from dynamic objects. The per-point variable aggregation duration (Eq. 11) further distinguishes the method from fixed-window aggregation, and the LRR-Sim dataset extends the evaluation range to 300 m, well beyond the 175 m ceiling of existing annotated radar datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a fundamental bottleneck in long-range radar perception—extreme point sparsity at 200–300 m—by increasing density with minimal scatter, and its plug-and-play nature (compatible with any point-cloud detector) makes it immediately adoptable. The LRR-Sim dataset fills a critical gap for benchmarking beyond 175 m, where LIDAR and camera annotation become unreliable. Open questions include how well the simulation-to-real transfer holds for actual long-range automotive radars, whether the tangential approximation (which is bounded but not eliminated) becomes a limiting factor at very high relative velocities, and whether the method extends naturally to multi-radar or 4D radar configurations with elevation Doppler.
