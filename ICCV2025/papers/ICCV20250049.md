## Problem

Existing 3D single object tracking benchmarks (KITTI, NuScenes) are limited to 8–23 object classes, restricted to traffic scenarios, and only support 7DoF boxes, making them inadequate for developing generic trackers that handle arbitrary-pose objects in diverse environments. The paper introduces GSOT3D, a 620-sequence, 123K-frame benchmark covering 54 classes across indoor and outdoor scenes with multi-modal (PC, RGB, depth) data and 9DoF annotations, along with a new progressive tracker to establish a strong baseline.

## Input / Output

PROT3D consumes the current-frame point cloud plus K=3 historical point clouds and an initial 3D bounding box, and outputs a 9DoF 3D box (6D pose + 3D size) expressed as translation, rotation, and size offsets relative to the previous frame, along with per-point targetness scores. Unlike most prior trackers that predict a 7DoF box (4D pose + 3D size), the 9DoF formulation captures full 3D orientation, which is critical for non-rigid or arbitrarily oriented objects. The GSOT3D benchmark additionally provides synchronized RGB images and depth maps per frame, enabling RGB-PC and RGB-D tracking tasks beyond the PC-only focus of the experiments.

## Method

PROT3D is a class-agnostic, progressive spatial-temporal tracker with N=2 cascaded stages. In each stage, a spatial-temporal Transformer (two layers of cross- and self-attention plus feed-forward) fuses a memory feature built from K=3 historical frames into the current search-region feature, and an MLP predicts candidate centers, a targetness mask, and proposal scores. Farthest Point Sampling then refines the point cloud, and a Feature Transformation Block (borrowed from MBP-Track) combined with a 1D convolution on the scores produces a more discriminative search-region feature that is forwarded to the next stage. After the final stage, a second MLP regresses the 9DoF box parameters and targetness scores, and the highest-scoring point's box is selected as the tracking result. The key design insight is that each stage's localization output progressively enriches the feature representation, enabling the final stage to distinguish the target from background more reliably than a single-pass approach.

## Results

On the GSOT3D test set (185 sequences, 39,740 frames), PROT3D achieves 21.97% mAO, 19.76% mSR50, and 5.22% mSR75 for 3D-SOT_PC, outperforming the second-best MBPTrack (20.54% mAO) by 1.43 points and the third-best M2-Track (20.26% mAO) by 1.71 points. All eight evaluated baselines suffer severe degradation on GSOT3D compared to KITTI (e.g., MBPTrack drops from 71.95% to 20.54% mAO), highlighting the difficulty of generic 3D tracking across 54 categories and diverse scenarios. Without GSOT3D training (KITTI-only pretraining), PROT3D still leads with 4.87% mAO versus 3.65% for M2-Track, confirming its robustness even under domain shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PROT3D outperforms all eight baselines on every metric (mAO, mSR50, mSR75) under both training settings. The closest competitor is MBPTrack [32], which PROT3D beats by 1.43% mAO, 3.21% mSR50, and 2.65% mSR75 (with GSOT3D training); the gap widens to 2.65× on mSR75 (5.22% vs 2.57%), indicating PROT3D's progressive refinement is particularly effective for precise localization. M2-Track [35] ranks second on mAO (20.26%) but trails PROT3D by 5.42 points on mSR50, suggesting it struggles with the stricter success threshold. M3SOT [18] (17.40% mAO) and CXTrack [31] (14.29% mAO) show moderate performance, while P2B [24], BAT [34], PTT [25], and SeqTrack3D [17] all fall below 15% mAO, with BAT [34] the weakest at 6.56% mAO. Notably, retraining on GSOT3D improves all baselines substantially (e.g., MBPTrack gains ~17 points in mAO over KITTI-only), yet none close the gap to PROT3D, underscoring both the value of the diverse benchmark and the effectiveness of the progressive architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

PROT3D builds directly on MBP-Track [32], borrowing its feature transformation block (FTB) and the strategy of leveraging historical frame memory for robust tracking, while extending the architecture with a progressive multi-stage cascade that iteratively refines search region features via spatial-temporal Transformers. The benchmark itself departs from autonomous-driving-centric datasets (KITTI, NuScenes) that offer only 7–8 object classes in traffic scenes, and from Track-it-in-3D [33] which is RGB-D-only and smaller in scale, by providing a unified multi-modal (PC, RGB, depth) platform with 54 classes across diverse indoor and outdoor environments. The 9DoF bounding box formulation extends the standard 7DoF (4D pose + 3D size) used in prior 3D SOT work to full 6D pose + 3D size, enabling tracking of arbitrarily oriented objects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GSOT3D is likely to become the de facto benchmark for generic 3D SOT, as it is the first to simultaneously support PC, RGB-PC, and RGB-D tracking tasks at a scale (620 sequences, 123K frames) sufficient for deep learning. The evaluation reveals a stark reality: all eight representative trackers drop from ~70% mAO on KITTI to ~20% on GSOT3D, underscoring that current 3D tracking methods are far from robust in the wild and that significant algorithmic advances are still needed. Key open questions include whether the progressive refinement strategy generalizes to longer sequences (GSOT3D averages only 198 frames), whether multi-modal fusion (RGB-PC, RGB-D) can close the gap left by point-cloud-only methods, and whether 9DoF prediction yields meaningful gains once deformable and non-rigid objects become more prevalent in future datasets.
