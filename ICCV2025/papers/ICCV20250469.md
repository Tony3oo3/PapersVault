## Problem

Egocentric vision systems capture rich environmental and behavioral data but cannot access the wearer's physiological state, which is critical for modeling attention, stress, and situational responses. While remote photoplethysmography (rPPG) can estimate heart rate from facial videos, it has never been applied to the inward-facing eye tracking cameras already present in unmodified egocentric headsets, and existing rPPG methods degrade severely under the heavy motion and wide HR ranges (44–164 bpm) of real-world activities.

## Input / Output

The method consumes monochrome eye tracking video at 320×240 pixels per eye (downsampled to 48×128) at 30 fps, represented as consecutive standardized frame differences, fused with 3-axis IMU data from the headset. The output is a continuous photoplethysmogram (BVP) signal, from which heart rate in bpm is extracted via a 0.5–2.8 Hz Butterworth bandpass filter and peak detection. Notably, the model is trained to ignore the eye regions themselves (which blink and move) and instead extract the BVP from the relatively stable skin surrounding the eyes.

## Method

PulseFormer builds on a PhysNet 3D CNN backbone (T=128 frames, ~12M parameters) and introduces two key mechanisms tailored to the egocentric setting. First, a Motion-Informed Temporal Attention (MITA) module uses cross-attention where IMU-derived embeddings serve as queries and ResNet-18 image embeddings serve as keys/values, producing per-frame temporal weights that down-weight frames heavily affected by motion. Second, Spatial Attention (SA) modules are inserted before each pooling layer to learn a spatial map that suppresses the high-motion eye regions and emphasizes the lower-motion periorbital skin. Training uses five-fold participant-level cross-validation with MSE loss over 100 epochs, and targeted augmentations (random rotation ±20°, horizontal cropping, flipping) account for individual variation in glasses fit and visible skin coverage.

## Results

On egoPPG-DB, PulseFormer achieves a heart rate estimation MAE of 7.67 bpm with a Pearson correlation of r=0.85, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art FactorizePhys (MAE=10.07 bpm, r=0.67) by 23.8% in MAE. The model also demonstrates robustness across activities, with MAE ranging from 5.52 bpm (video watching) to 12.91 bpm (exercise bike). For the downstream proficiency estimation task on EgoExo4D, integrating PulseFormer's HR predictions into the TimeSFormer baseline improves overall accuracy from 39.69% to 45.29% (a 14.1% relative gain) when using egocentric videos plus HR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PulseFormer outperforms all 10 compared rPPG baselines on every metric (MAE, RMSE, MAPE, and Pearson r) on egoPPG-DB. The closest competitor is FactorizePhys [38] (MAE=10.07 bpm), which PulseFormer beats by 2.40 bpm (23.8% reduction); PhysFormer [93] (MAE=10.71) and PhysNet [92] (MAE=12.09) follow. The signal-processing baselines—using the skin region (MAE=12.40) or eye region (MAE=14.60)—confirm that the skin around the eyes is a more reliable BVP source than the eyes themselves, a finding corroborated by PulseFormer's learned spatial attention maps. Older deep-learning rPPG methods (DeepPhys, TS-CAN, Yue et al.) perform substantially worse (MAE 26–30 bpm), indicating they are not robust to the heavy motion and HR variability present in egocentric recordings. For the downstream EgoExo4D proficiency estimation task, PulseFormer's HR features improve accuracy in 5 of 6 activity scenarios (all except Soccer), with the largest gains in cooking (+20 pp) and dancing (+9.8 pp) over the video-only baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the rPPG literature, which has focused on facial videos captured by stationary cameras of seated users, to the fundamentally different setting of inward-facing eye tracking cameras in body-worn egocentric headsets. Architecturally, PulseFormer builds on the PhysNet 3D CNN backbone and the frame-difference input representation from DeepPhys, but introduces two key departures: a motion-informed temporal attention (MITA) module that fuses IMU data via cross-attention to down-weight motion-corrupted frames, and spatial attention modules that learn to exclude the highly mobile eye regions in favor of the more stable periorbital skin. The downstream application to EgoExo4D proficiency estimation via TimeSFormer is a novel integration of continuous physiological signals into an existing egocentric video understanding pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that unmodified egocentric headsets (e.g., Project Aria) already contain sufficient signal for continuous HR estimation, opening a path to physiological awareness in large existing datasets like EgoExo4D and Nymeria without new hardware. The 14.1% proficiency-estimation improvement is a concrete demonstration that physiological state is a useful signal for behavior modeling, though absolute accuracy remains modest (45.29%). Key limitations include a small cohort (25 participants, ages 19–32, skin types II–V), indoor-only capture, and a significant performance drop at the 10 fps frame rate used by most large-scale egocentric datasets. Open questions include generalization to outdoor lighting, broader demographics, and 'stop-and-go' activities not represented in the training protocol.
