## Problem

Existing unlearning methods for diffusion models are designed for single-concept forgetting and become unstable when applied iteratively: previously erased concepts resurface, generation quality degrades, and outputs for forgotten prompts collapse into meaningless noise rather than semantically coherent alternatives. Multi-concept methods that do exist struggle to balance thorough forgetting with preservation of unrelated generative capability and prompt-level semantic consistency.

## Input / Output

The method fine-tunes the U-Net of Stable Diffusion 1.4 using text prompts for the target concept to be forgotten and its designated superclass (or opposing class), paired with training images from a forgetting dataset and a superclass dataset. The dynamic mask is applied to U-Net weight gradients at a fixed sparsity level (default 50% on cross-attention layers), and the output is a modified set of U-Net weights such that prompts referencing the forgotten concept produce superclass-aligned images while all other prompts remain unaffected.

## Method

The framework combines two mechanisms. First, a Dynamic Mask adaptively selects which weight gradients are active at each training step: it initializes from accumulated warmup gradients, then periodically drops a fraction of currently-masked weights and adds new ones with the highest accumulated gradient magnitudes, governed by a cosine-decay update schedule to stabilize training. Second, a Concept-Aware Loss has three terms: an unlearning loss that minimizes the MSE between noise predictions under the target-concept condition and the superclass condition, an alignment loss that enforces standard diffusion training on superclass data, and a knowledge-distillation regularization loss that anchors the current model's predictions to a frozen teacher checkpoint from the prior unlearning step, preventing previously forgotten concepts from reappearing. The total objective is L_unlearn + α·L_align + β·L_reg (α=β=0.25), and the gradient of this loss drives which weights the dynamic mask activates, coupling the loss design with the selective update mechanism.

## Results

On the 10-class Imagenette multi-concept unlearning task, the proposed method achieves a Total Accuracy of 0.084 (lower is better), substantially outperforming the next-best methods SalUn (0.226) and ESD-x (0.297), while maintaining a CLIP score of 26.43 that exceeds ESD-x (25.04) and SalUn (25.37). On the NSFW unlearning task (I2P dataset), the method detects only 47 NSFW elements total (vs. 743 for unmodified SD v1.4), the lowest among all baselines, while preserving a competitive FID of 14.11 and CLIP score of 30.79. In the 3-class unlearning setting, the method achieves Total Accuracy of 0.093 with 0.900 accuracy on non-target classes, outperforming SepME (0.140 / 0.710) and SPM (0.443 / 0.898).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method achieves the lowest Total Accuracy (0.084) across all 10 Imagenette classes, beating single-concept methods FMN (0.789), AC (0.607), ESD-x (0.297), and SalUn (0.226), as well as multi-concept methods MACE (0.732) and SPM (0.529). The only method with a lower Total Accuracy is ESD-u (0.0), but its CLIP score of 22.52 indicates it produces entirely meaningless noisy backgrounds rather than semantically coherent replacements. On CLIP score, the method (26.43) trails FMN (29.87) and MACE (29.62), reflecting a trade-off where those methods preserve prompt fidelity less aggressively at the cost of incomplete forgetting. For NSFW unlearning on I2P, the method detects only 47 NSFW elements total, outperforming MACE (111), ESD-u (123), and SPM (229), while maintaining a CLIP score of 30.79 that exceeds MACE (29.41) and ESD-u (30.21). In the 3-class setting, the method (Total Acc 0.093, Others Acc 0.900) outperforms SepME (0.140 / 0.710) and SPM (0.443 / 0.898) on both forgetting and preservation metrics, though SalUn achieves a slightly lower Total Acc (0.426) at the cost of much lower Others Acc (0.872). Overall, the method consistently wins on forgetting effectiveness while remaining competitive on generation quality, with the primary trade-off being a modest CLIP score reduction relative to methods that forget less thoroughly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two previously separate research threads—dynamic sparse training (DST) and concept-aware loss design—into a unified multi-concept unlearning framework for diffusion models. It departs from single-concept unlearning methods (ESD, FMN, SalUn, AC) that fail when applied iteratively due to instability and relearning, and from existing multi-concept approaches (MACE, SPM, SepME) that struggle to balance thorough forgetting with generation quality. The dynamic mask mechanism is directly inspired by DST literature, repurposed here to selectively modulate gradient updates during unlearning rather than for model compression, while the concept-aware loss introduces superclass alignment and knowledge-distillation regularization as novel loss components for the unlearning objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-concept unlearning is a practically critical need for deploying diffusion models responsibly (removing copyrighted, biased, or harmful content simultaneously), and this paper provides a principled framework that addresses the sequential instability problem that plagues iterative application of single-concept methods. However, the evaluation is limited to SD 1.4 on small-scale datasets (Imagenette's 10 classes, I2P), leaving open whether the approach scales to larger models (SDXL, PixArt-α) and hundreds of concepts. The authors also identify adversarial robustness of unlearned concepts and hierarchical/compositional forgetting as important future directions, suggesting the current solution is not yet irreversible or fine-grained enough for production deployment.
