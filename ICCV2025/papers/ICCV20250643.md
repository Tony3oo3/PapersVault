## Problem

Inversion-based generative image watermarking frameworks (e.g., GauShad, TreeRings) achieve strong robustness against non-geometric distortions but fail under common geometric distortions such as rotation, scaling, and translation. The root cause is that geometric distortions introduce structural pixel misalignment, making it extremely difficult—or possibly impossible—to construct universally geometric-invariant watermark features. This gap prevents practical deployment of inversion-based watermarking in real-world pipelines where geometric transformations are routine.

## Input / Output

At injection, the method takes a binary watermark sequence (64 bits for GauShad-SynTag) and a text prompt, producing a 512×512×3 watermarked image with invisible SynTag features embedded via a fine-tuned VAE decoder. At extraction, a distorted 512×512×3 image is fed to the SynTag predictor, which outputs 4 transformed coordinate points defining a homography matrix; after correction and dither compensation, DDIM inversion yields candidate watermark bit sequences and a binary detection decision (or source-model identification in tracing). The latent space operates at 4×64×64, and the dither compensation generates up to (2r+1)² × n candidate latents (with r=2 and n=4 pixel-level transforms) before final extraction.

## Method

SynTag's core insight is to abandon the search for geometric-invariant features and instead embed a geometric-sensitive synchronization template that evolves predictably under distortion, enabling the distortion trajectory to be estimated and reversed. Concretely, the VAE decoder is fine-tuned (as D_Syn) to inject an imperceptible template feature while preserving visual quality (enforced via MSE + VGG + SSIM reconstruction loss against the original decoder output), and a prediction network (P_Syn) is jointly trained to regress 4 transformed corner coordinates from the distorted image, from which a 3×3 homography matrix is constructed for geometric correction. During extraction, after homography correction, a two-level dither compensation mechanism—pixel-level (small rotations ±3° and scalings 0.9–1.1×) and latent-level (zero-padding with sliding-window resampling at stride 1)—generates multiple candidate latents to absorb residual correction bias. The method is designed as a drop-in plugin: it replaces only the VAE decoder in any existing inversion-based pipeline (demonstrated with GauShad and TreeRings), requiring no changes to the watermark embedding or DDIM inversion logic.

## Results

GauShad-SynTag achieves a geometric distortion TPR of 0.980 (SD-v1.4) / 0.988 (SD-v2.1) and bit accuracy of 0.938/0.940, while maintaining non-geometric TPR of 0.967/0.970 and bit accuracy of 0.938/0.939. TreeRings-SynTag reaches geometric TPR of 0.928/0.932 and non-geometric TPR of 0.950/0.949. Visual quality is preserved, with FID of 25.21±0.17 and CLIP-Score of 0.3615±0.0006 for GauShad-SynTag, comparable to raw Stable Diffusion (FID 25.28±0.17, CLIP-Score 0.3628±0.0006). The paper reports a 19% higher overall geometric TPR and 8% higher bit accuracy over the most competitive state-of-the-art method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GauShad-SynTag dramatically outperforms the original GauShad [34] on geometric distortions (TPR 0.980 vs. 0.016, bit accuracy 0.938 vs. 0.633) while preserving its non-geometric robustness (TPR 0.967 vs. 0.963). Similarly, TreeRings-SynTag improves over TreeRings [31] geometric TPR from 0.548 to 0.928. Against fine-tune-based methods, GauShad-SynTag surpasses LaWa [25] (geometric TPR 0.980 vs. 0.764) and Stable Signature [13] (0.980 vs. 0.760) on geometric distortions, while also matching or exceeding their non-geometric performance. Post-hoc methods show a clear trade-off: StegaStamp [29] and RoSteALS [6] achieve high non-geometric TPR (0.897–0.920) but very poor geometric TPR (0.240), whereas DwtDctSvd [8] and RivaGAN [35] are weak on both axes. MBRS [16] is competitive on geometric TPR (0.744) but lags significantly on non-geometric (0.346). Overall, GauShad-SynTag is the only method that simultaneously achieves >0.96 TPR on both geometric and non-geometric distortions, with no degradation in FID or CLIP-Score relative to unwatermarked Stable Diffusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the inversion-based generative watermarking paradigm (GauShad, TreeRings) by addressing its critical weakness: geometric distortion vulnerability. Rather than following the established strategy of constructing distortion-invariant features (as in Stable Signature, LaWa, and prior DNN watermarking), SynTag departs conceptually by embedding a geometrically-sensitive synchronization tag that evolves with the distortion, enabling trajectory estimation and correction via homography. The VAE decoder fine-tuning mechanism follows the Stable Signature paradigm, while the DDIM inversion extraction pipeline is inherited from the inversion-based lineage. The dither compensation mechanism (pixel-level and latent-level exhaustive search) is a novel refinement not present in prior watermarking work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Geometric robustness is the primary barrier between lab-validated generative watermarking and practical deployment, where rotation, cropping, and scaling are ubiquitous in real-world image sharing. SynTag's modular design—compatible with any inversion-based backbone—makes it a drop-in upgrade rather than a replacement, lowering adoption friction. However, purification attacks at high strength (f=0.7) still degrade TPR to zero, and the homography-based correction assumes planar geometric transforms, leaving non-rigid or perspective distortions unaddressed. Open questions include whether the sensitive-template strategy generalizes to video watermarking, 3D content, or adversarially crafted geometric attacks specifically designed to corrupt the SynTag feature.
