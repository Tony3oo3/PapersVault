## Problem

Domain-incremental object detection in remote sensing faces two compounding challenges absent in natural-image settings: severe data distribution shifts caused by heterogeneous sensors, altitudes, and geographic locations, and feature misalignments driven by extreme foreground-background ratios and multiscale object characteristics. Existing incremental detection methods (knowledge distillation, exemplar replay, adapters) were designed for natural images and do not jointly address both the data-level and feature-level shifts that degrade performance across remote sensing domains.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB remote sensing images (DOTA images are cropped to 1024×1024) and produces bounding boxes with class labels (ship, airplane, oil tank after class pruning). Training proceeds sequentially over four domains (HRRSD → LEVIR → DIOR → DOTA or other orderings), where at each step the model must detect objects in the new domain while retaining accuracy on all previously seen domains, evaluated via a joint test across all four datasets.

## Method

Active-DDC builds on Deformable DETR and introduces two complementary modules. ALER (Data-based Active Learning Example Replay) scores candidate samples in a memory buffer using a weighted combination of image information entropy and a risk function derived from the average foreground-background ratio, then retains the highest-scoring samples in a fixed-size memory bank for joint training with new-domain data. ADSC (Query-based Active Domain Shift Control) first preselects query vectors whose predicted boxes exceed an IoU threshold relative to the frozen teacher model, then solves an entropy-regularized optimal transport problem (via the Sinkhorn algorithm) with a cost matrix combining IoU and cosine similarity to establish many-to-many query correspondences between student and teacher. The final training loss is the sum of the standard Deformable DETR detection loss and a cross-entropy matching loss computed over the OT-aligned query pairs.

## Results

On the four remote sensing datasets (HRRSD, LEVIR, DIOR, DOTA), Active-DDC achieves the best mAP of 49.5%, 29.1%, 32.6%, and 35.9% respectively, and the highest Joint Test mAP of 36.2% with AP50 of 67.8% and AP75 of 35.0%. In the Joint Test (evaluating knowledge retention across all domains), the method outperforms the strongest baselines by 3.8–7.5 percentage points in AP50. The method also narrows the gap to Joint Training (upper bound) substantially, e.g., from 60.3% (Fine-tuning) to 67.8% AP50 in the Joint Test.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Active-DDC outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and the Joint Test. In the Joint Test AP50, it leads CL-DETR [28] by 5.9 pts (67.8 vs 61.9), Incre-DETR [8] by 3.8 pts (67.8 vs 64.0), DyQ-DETR [43] by 4.6 pts (67.8 vs 63.2), and LDB* [37] by 4.5 pts (67.8 vs 63.3). Against the Faster-RCNN-based baselines, the margin is even larger: ORE [16] is beaten by 7.5 pts in Joint Test AP50 and Faster-ILOD [31] by 5.6 pts. The method also surpasses the ER-based ABR* [27] and RPC [43] variants in the dedicated ER ablation (Table 4), and outperforms DETRDistill [4] and KD-DETR [39] in the KD ablation (Table 5), confirming that both the ALER replay strategy and the ADSC query-matching distillation contribute independently to the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Deformable DETR as its base detector and extends the incremental object detection literature (CL-DETR, Incre-DETR, DyQ-DETR) from class-incremental settings to a domain-incremental formulation specifically for remote sensing. It departs from standard example replay by incorporating active learning criteria (information entropy and foreground-background ratio) tailored to remote sensing's extreme class imbalance, and from standard knowledge distillation by replacing one-to-one Hungarian matching with optimal transport-based many-to-many query alignment. The work also draws on the risk-assessment scoring from DyQ-DETR but reweights it to emphasize the difficulty inherent in remote sensing imagery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Remote sensing object detection faces more severe domain shifts than natural image detection due to heterogeneous sensors, altitudes, and geographic coverage, making this a practically important but underexplored setting. The dual decomposition into data-level (ALER) and feature-level (ADSC) control provides a clean conceptual framework that could inform other domain-incremental tasks. However, the evaluation is limited to only three object classes (ship, airplane, oil tank) across four datasets, raising open questions about scalability to the full class sets of DIOR (20 classes) or DOTA (18 classes) and to other remote sensing modalities such as SAR or multispectral imagery.
