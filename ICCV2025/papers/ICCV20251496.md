## Problem

Existing VLM-based end-to-end autonomous driving methods struggle in closed-loop evaluation because the semantic reasoning space of VLMs is fundamentally misaligned with the purely numerical trajectory output required in the action space. Prior approaches either force VLMs to output trajectories as text (poor numerical reasoning), use meta-actions to guide decoupled E2E planners (preventing collaborative optimization), or decode special tokens via MLPs (limited trajectory diversity). This gap prevents VLMs from fully leveraging their causal reasoning to produce correct, diverse driving decisions in interactive scenarios.

## Input / Output

ORION takes multi-view camera images (resized to 640×640), a discrete navigation command (one of 6 modes), and a structured text instruction (scene description, history review, scene analysis, action reasoning) as input. It is fully HD map-free and anchor-free, requiring no LiDAR or target-point conditioning. The system outputs 6-mode multi-modal trajectory predictions (decoded by a GRU from a VAE latent space), VQA text responses, plus auxiliary outputs for object detection, traffic state classification, and dynamic-agent motion prediction.

## Method

ORION comprises three tightly coupled components: (1) a QT-Former that uses scene queries, perception queries, and a set of history queries interacting with a FIFO memory bank to compress multi-view features and aggregate long-term temporal context; (2) a Vicuna v1.5 LLM (fine-tuned with LoRA, rank 16) that performs hierarchical driving-scenario reasoning and emits a single special planning token encoding the full reasoning context; and (3) a generative planner that projects both the planning token and the ground-truth trajectory into Gaussian latent variables via two-layer MLPs, aligning the reasoning and action spaces through a KL-divergence loss, then decodes the trajectory with a GRU. The key insight is that the VAE's shared Gaussian latent space makes the reasoning-to-action mapping differentiable, enabling unified end-to-end optimization of VQA and planning objectives. Training jointly optimizes detection, traffic-state, motion-prediction, cross-entropy (LLM), VAE alignment, MSE, collision, and boundary losses across all components.

## Results

On the Bench2Drive closed-loop benchmark, ORION achieves 77.74 Driving Score (DS) and 54.62% Success Rate (SR), surpassing the previous SOTA DriveTransformer by +14.28 DS and +19.61% SR. In open-loop evaluation, ORION attains 0.68 m average L2 error and 0.47% average collision rate. On the Multi-Ability metric, ORION scores 54.72% mean ability, with particularly strong performance in Overtaking (71.11%), Emergency Brake (78.33%), and Traffic Sign (69.15%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ORION outperforms all nine baselines on the Bench2Drive closed-loop benchmark. Against the previous SOTA DriveTransformer (ICLR 25), ORION gains +14.28 DS (77.74 vs. 63.46) and +19.61% SR (54.62% vs. 35.01%), while also improving mean ability by +16.12%. Compared to DriveAdapter (ICCV 23), which uses both camera and LiDAR inputs plus expert feature distillation, ORION still leads by +13.52 DS and +21.54% SR using camera-only input. Over classic E2E methods like VAD (42.35 DS) and UniAD-Base (45.81 DS), ORION achieves roughly 35 DS improvement. The largest relative gap is against AD-MLP (18.05 DS), where ORION's VLM-based reasoning provides a 59.69 DS advantage. ORION also leads in open-loop L2 error (0.68 m vs. 0.62 m for DriveTransformer, the only baseline slightly better) and collision rate (0.47% vs. 0.96% for the diffusion ablation). The one area where ORION trails is Merging (25.00% vs. 28.82% for DriveAdapter) and Give Way (30.00% vs. 50.00%), attributed to the diverse decision-making timing in lane-changing scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

ORION extends the VLM-for-driving line initiated by OmniDrive (Q-Former3D for scene compression) and EMMA/DriveVLM/Senna (text or meta-action interfaces), but departs fundamentally from their decoupled reasoning-to-action pipelines. It repurposes the GRU decoder from GenAD and the loss design from VAD, while introducing a VAE-based generative planner that creates a shared Gaussian latent space to make the VLM's planning token directly condition trajectory generation end-to-end. The QT-Former further extends OmniDrive's Q-Former3D with a FIFO memory bank and history queries for long-term temporal context, going beyond single-frame or short-window temporal modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 14.28 DS / 19.61% SR margin over the prior SOTA (DriveTransformer) on Bench2Drive is the largest gap reported on this benchmark to date, and the ablations convincingly attribute it to the reasoning-action alignment rather than auxiliary supervision alone. However, the framework is evaluated only in CARLA's Bench2Drive, and the authors acknowledge weakness in lane-changing decisions (Merging, Give Way), suggesting the causal-reasoning benefit is scene-dependent. Open questions include whether the VAE-based bridging generalizes to real-world multi-sensor settings, whether the approach scales to longer-horizon planning, and whether the Chat-B2D auto-annotation pipeline transfers to other driving domains.
