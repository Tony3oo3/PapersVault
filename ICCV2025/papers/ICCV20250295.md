## Problem

Existing domain generalization methods (Mixup, JiGen, IBN-Net) achieve strong gains on single-modal data but yield significantly smaller improvements when naively transferred to multimodal settings, because applying them independently per modality produces divergent generalization directions and modality competition. No prior work has explored constructing a unified multimodal representation space as a bridge to make single-modal DG techniques applicable to MMDG in a synchronized manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250295/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired multimodal inputs—video clips, audio clips, and optical flow frames—each potentially spanning multiple source domains, and outputs a single class label per sample. All DG operations (Mixup, JiGen, IBN) are applied at the learned representation level rather than on raw inputs, which the authors found to outperform raw-input augmentation in the multimodal setting. The model is trained exclusively on source domains and evaluated on unseen target domains under both multi-source and single-source DG protocols.

## Method

The framework first encodes each modality into two components via separate encoders: a general representation z capturing category-level semantics and a specific representation z̄ capturing domain- and modality-specific features. A supervised contrastive loss aligns z across all modalities and domains at the category level, while a CLUB-based mutual information minimization term decouples z from z̄, and an MSE reconstruction loss (decoding x from z and z̄) preserves semantic integrity. DG methods are then applied exclusively to the unified general space: UR-Mixup interpolates z across modalities with a shared λ, UR-JiGen shuffles segments of z across modalities as a jigsaw task, and UR-IBN applies instance/batch normalization to z. The augmented general representation is recombined with the specific representation through the decoder to produce domain-aware features for classification, effectively reformulating MMDG as a standard DG problem in a shared semantic space.

## Results

On the EPIC-Kitchens dataset (V-A-F, multi-source DG), UR-Mixup achieves 67.79% average top-1 accuracy, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art CMRF (67.36%) and significantly outperforming direct DG transfers (Mixup 63.42%, JiGen 62.81%, IBN 60.98%). On the HAC dataset, UR-Mixup reaches 73.40% average accuracy, exceeding CMRF (72.44%) by nearly 1 point. UR-JiGen also matches or exceeds CMRF, achieving 67.04% on EPIC-Kitchens and 72.52% on HAC, without requiring MMDG-specific architectural modifications.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UR-Mixup outperforms the previous state-of-the-art CMRF [14] on both datasets: 67.79% vs. 67.36% on EPIC-Kitchens and 73.40% vs. 72.44% on HAC (average top-1 accuracy). UR-JiGen is competitive with CMRF, achieving 67.04% vs. 67.36% on EPIC-Kitchens and 72.52% vs. 72.44% on HAC, while requiring no MMDG-specific architecture. Both UR methods substantially outperform direct DG transfers: Mixup [49] (63.42%/67.78%), JiGen [4] (62.81%/67.49%), and IBN [37] (60.98%/65.39%) on EPIC-Kitchens/HAC respectively, with gains of 3.4–6.8 points. UR-IBN (64.72%/68.57%) improves over its direct-transfer counterpart IBN (60.98%/65.39%) but remains below CMRF. Compared to MMDG-specific methods SimMMDG [11] (64.20%/70.13%) and RNA-Net [39] (59.81%/65.78%), both UR-JiGen and UR-Mixup achieve clear margins, demonstrating that the unified representation bridge generalizes well across DG method categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of single-modal Domain Generalization (DG) and multi-modal representation learning, arguing that the three canonical DG families—data manipulation (Mixup), learning strategies (JiGen), and representation learning (IBN-Net)—degrade when naively transferred to multi-modal settings due to modality asynchrony. Prior MMDG work (SimMMDG, CMRF, RNA-Net) designed task-specific architectures to handle multi-modal domain shift, but none provided a general mechanism for porting arbitrary DG techniques. The paper's conceptual departure is to use supervised contrastive learning and CLUB-based mutual information decoupling to construct a unified representation space where standard DG methods can be applied synchronously, effectively reformulating MMDG as a single-space DG problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a reusable recipe: any future DG technique can be lifted into the MMDG setting by operating on the disentangled general-information space, reducing the need for bespoke multi-modal architectures. However, the evaluation is limited to two datasets (EPIC-Kitchens, HAC) and a fixed modality triple (video, audio, optical flow), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to unpaired modalities, larger modality sets, or non-classification tasks. A key open question is how the unified representation quality degrades when domain shift is extreme or when modalities are only weakly correlated, and whether the disentanglement objective remains stable at scale.
