## Problem

Diffusion models can effortlessly replicate copyrighted images while circumventing existing protection measures such as invisible watermarks, visible trademarks, and signatures, yet no systematic framework exists to characterize this 'neural plagiarism' threat or to demonstrate how it can be executed in a controllable, gradient-based manner. The paper exposes that both forgery attacks (removing watermarks) and ambiguity attacks (embedding competing watermarks) are trivially achievable, undermining the practical utility of current copyright protection schemes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single watermarked copyrighted image at 512×512 resolution as input, along with user-selected timesteps at which to apply perturbations, and operates entirely within a pre-trained Stable Diffusion pipeline (DPM solver, 50 timesteps). The attacker has no knowledge of the embedded watermark. The output is a visually similar replica image that either has the original watermark removed (forgery) or carries an alternative watermark creating ownership ambiguity; the degree of semantic alteration is controlled by which timesteps receive shims—earlier timesteps produce larger changes (e.g., hairstyle, clothing) while later timesteps yield subtle, near-indistinguishable replicas.

## Method

The core contribution is an 'anchors and shims' optimization framework that decouples the latent trajectory to avoid the ~100 GB memory cost of backpropagating through all diffusion steps. First, the target image is inverted through a deterministic DPM solver to obtain anchor latents at every timestep. Then, at user-selected timesteps, a shim perturbation δ_t is searched on the CLIP text embedding fed into the cross-attention mechanism, jointly optimizing three losses: a norm loss enforcing a minimum perturbation magnitude, a semantic loss maximizing cosine similarity to the empty-string embedding (preserving unconditional generation), and an alignment loss keeping the predicted next latent close to its anchor. Because each timestep's shim is optimized independently against the frozen anchor trajectory, the method requires no model training or fine-tuning and uses only a single forward pass per gradient step. The coarse-to-fine nature of diffusion means shims at large timesteps induce major semantic shifts while shims at small timesteps produce fine-grained modifications, giving the attacker a controllable dial over how much the replica deviates from the original.

## Results

On MS-COCO with post-hoc invisible watermarks (DwtDctSvd and RivaGAN, 32 bits), the proposed method reduces bit accuracy to ~0.52–0.56 and accuracy to 0.01–0.02 (near random guessing), achieving complete watermark removal (T@1%F = 0.00) while maintaining PSNR of 25.27–25.29 and SSIM of 0.73. For diffusion-based Stable Signature watermarking, the method also achieves T@1%F = 0.00 with the highest SSIM (0.80) among all methods. However, for Tree-Ring latent watermarking, the method fails to remove the watermark (T@1%F = 0.98), though it still produces the best image quality (SSIM = 0.86, FID = 33.50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two regeneration-based baselines: Regen [59] (single noise-add/denoise step) and Rinse [3] (repeated noise-add/denoise). On post-hoc watermarks (DwtDctSvd, RivaGAN), the proposed method achieves the lowest bit accuracy (0.52 vs. 0.64 for Regen, 0.54 for Rinse on DwtDctSvd) and lowest accuracy (0.01 vs. 0.15 and 0.04), indicating more thorough watermark removal, while maintaining higher PSNR (25.27 vs. 26.21 and 23.68) and SSIM (0.73 vs. 0.75 and 0.68) than Rinse. For Stable Signature (diffusion-based), all three methods achieve complete removal (T@1%F = 0.00), but the proposed method yields the best SSIM (0.80 vs. 0.78 and 0.72). The method loses on Tree-Ring watermarking, where it cannot remove the watermark (T@1%F = 0.98 vs. 1.00 for both baselines), because Tree-Ring encodes in the Fourier domain and local latent shifts do not disrupt the pattern. Overall, the proposed method wins on watermark removal effectiveness and image quality for post-hoc and Stable Signature watermarks, but is outperformed by baselines on Tree-Ring removal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the deterministic inversion capability of DPM-Solver to obtain anchor latents, and extends the regeneration-attack line of work (Regen, Rinse) by replacing their simple noise-and-denoise pipeline with a structured, gradient-based optimization in latent space. The 'anchors and shims' framework is a novel decoupling strategy that avoids the ~100 GB memory cost of backpropagating through all timesteps, while the attention-based perturbation on cross-attention text embeddings provides a semantic search mechanism absent from prior regeneration attacks. Conceptually, the paper unifies two previously separate threat categories—forgery (watermark removal) and ambiguity (competing ownership claims)—under a single 'neural plagiarism' threat model, drawing on the underexplored ambiguity-attack literature [54] to show that even watermarks that resist removal (e.g., Tree-Ring) can be undermined by co-embedded alternative keys.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that current invisible watermarking schemes, widely deployed as the primary technical defense for AI-generated and copyrighted content, are vulnerable to both removal and ownership-ambiguity attacks using only a pretrained diffusion model and no additional training. The ambiguity-attack results—where both the original and a forged watermark are simultaneously detected with high confidence (T@1%F ≈ 0.99–1.00)—are particularly concerning because they render third-party arbitration unreliable even when the watermark technically survives. Open questions include whether Tree-Ring-style Fourier-domain watermarks can be made robust to multi-key ambiguity, whether the approach generalizes to video or 3D content, and what practical defenses (beyond timestamps) can distinguish the rightful owner when multiple watermarks coexist.
