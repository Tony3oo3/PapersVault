## Problem

Real-world images frequently exhibit complex degradations—arbitrary mixtures of low resolution, motion blur, defocus blur, and noise—yet existing restoration methods either train on simulated single degradations (poor generalization) or rely on adapter-based diffusion models that suffer from content inconsistency and hallucination. Additionally, no publicly available benchmark properly reflects the challenge of restoring images with multiple co-occurring real-world degradations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250151/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-quality RGB image (512×512 for ×1 restoration, 128×128 for ×4 upscaling) and produces a restored high-quality image at the target resolution. At inference, the LQ image latent is concatenated with the noisy latent as conditioning, and the model generates six parallel noise predictions (blind restoration, SR, motion deblur, defocus deblur, denoise, and DownLQ) that are combined via per-image weights. These weights are either user-specified or found by grid search over [−0.2, 1.2] optimizing MUSIQ, with at most one negative weight allowed.

## Method

UniRes fine-tunes a pre-trained WebLI text-to-image LDM (865M parameters) via multi-task learning on four isolated degradation tasks (super-resolution, motion deblur, defocus deblur, denoising), each distinguished by a constant text prompt. Unlike adapter-based methods, it conditions on the LQ image through latent concatenation with the noisy latent, which preserves pixel structure and only adds parameters to the first UNet convolution layer. At inference, the core mechanism is a weighted sum of the K task-specific noise predictions (Eq. 2), extending classifier-free guidance to allow flexible combination of specialized restoration knowledge. A DownLQ term—SR prediction conditioned on a ×4 downsampled-then-upscaled LQ image—serves as a fidelity-quality trade-off knob, encouraging more detail generation without excessive hallucination. The model is trained for 200K steps with DDPM loss, random condition dropping (rate 0.1) for classifier-free guidance, and task sampling probabilities of 0.32/0.28/0.18/0.22.

## Results

On the novel DiversePhotos ×1 benchmark (160 images with complex real-world degradations), UniRes achieves the best scores on all three no-reference metrics: ClipIQA 0.6519, MUSIQ 68.22, and ManIQA 0.5021, with a particularly large MUSIQ margin over the second-best StableSR (61.39). On the single-degradation Real60 benchmark, UniRes remains competitive with MUSIQ 75.15 (best) and ClipIQA 0.7894, though SUPIR edges ahead on ClipIQA (0.8217) and ManIQA (0.6716). For ×4 upscaling on DiversePhotos ×4, UniRes leads on MUSIQ (62.40 vs. 54.09 for DiffBIR) and ManIQA (0.4656 vs. 0.4551), while DiffBIR slightly wins on ClipIQA (0.6190 vs. 0.6050).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the complex-degradation DiversePhotos ×1 benchmark, UniRes decisively outperforms all four baselines on every metric, with the largest gap over StableSR (+6.83 MUSIQ) and DACLIP-IR (+22.06 MUSIQ), confirming its advantage when multiple degradation types co-occur. On the single-degradation Real60 set, UniRes wins on MUSIQ (75.15) and ClipIQA (0.7894 vs. 0.7593 for StableSR) but trails SUPIR on ClipIQA (0.8217) and ManIQA (0.6716), indicating SUPIR's stronger detail generation when low-resolution is the sole degradation. For ×4 upscaling on DiversePhotos ×4, UniRes leads on MUSIQ (62.40 vs. 54.09 for DiffBIR) and ManIQA, while DiffBIR narrowly wins on ClipIQA (0.6190 vs. 0.6050). On the specialized super-resolution benchmarks RealSR ×4 and DRealSR ×4, UniRes achieves balanced full-reference and no-reference scores but is outperformed by DiffBIR and SUPIR on perceptual metrics (e.g., DiffBIR reaches MUSIQ 69.28 on RealSR ×4 vs. UniRes's 65.46), suggesting the method trades some peak quality on isolated degradations for robustness on complex ones. DACLIP-IR consistently underperforms across all settings, with MUSIQ scores 15–25 points below UniRes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the line of diffusion-based image restoration methods (StableSR, DiffBIR, SUPIR) that leverage pre-trained LDMs as generative priors, but departs from the dominant adapter/ControlNet-based conditioning in favor of latent concatenation, which the authors argue better preserves pixel structure. Conceptually, it draws on Mixture-of-Experts and multi-task learning to train a single LDM on several well-isolated restoration tasks (SR, motion deblur, defocus deblur, denoise), then combines their latent predictions at inference via a weighted sum — an end-to-end alternative to iterative all-in-one pipelines like AutoDIR or RestoreAgent. The fidelity-quality trade-off is handled through a novel 'DownLQ' inference term rather than the adapter-based pull-toward-LQ mechanisms used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of DiversePhotos fills a genuine gap: no prior benchmark specifically targets arbitrary mixtures of multiple real-world degradations, which is the dominant scenario in the wild. The weighted-combination framework is flexible and extensible, potentially applicable to any set of restoration tasks, and the finding that a simple convex combination of task-specific diffusion predictions outperforms more elaborate iterative or adapter-based approaches on complex degradations is practically useful. Open questions include whether the grid-search weight optimization (1512 combinations) scales to more degradation types, whether learned weight predictors (the Random Forest result is promising but preliminary) can match per-image optimization, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the four camera-based degradations considered here.
