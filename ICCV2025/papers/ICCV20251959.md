## Problem

Existing MLLMs are trained on global or object-level semantics and lack the precise localization capability needed for point-level reasoning such as keypoint detection. Traditional 3D keypoint detection methods rely heavily on annotated 3D datasets and supervised training, limiting their applicability to new categories. This paper asks whether the pixel-level reasoning embedded in recently trained MLLMs (specifically Molmo, trained with curated pixel-level annotations) can be exploited for zero-shot, category-agnostic 3D keypoint detection without any 3D ground-truth labels.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D mesh of arbitrary category as input, renders it from 26 viewpoints (ablations test 6 and 46 views), and uses GPT-4o to generate a candidate list of keypoint names from the shape class label. For each view and each keypoint name, Molmo is prompted to return 2D pixel coordinates. The output is a set of named 3D keypoint locations on the mesh surface, obtained after back-projection and clustering. The method is fully zero-shot and category-agnostic, requiring no 3D annotations or task-specific training.

## Method

ZeroKey is a three-stage pipeline: (1) GPT-4o is prompted with a front-facing rendering and the shape class to generate a JSON list of candidate keypoint names; (2) Molmo, an MLLM trained with pixel-level annotation tasks (PixMo), is prompted per-view and per-keypoint-name to detect 2D coordinates, leveraging its natural-language localization capability; (3) 2D detections are back-projected to 3D using cached depth from rasterization, with soft voting weights computed from Gaussian kernels over h×h patches to stabilize sharp-angle intersections. Multi-view 3D points are aggregated via HDBSCAN clustering (minPts=10) to filter noise and resolve ambiguity from Molmo's occasional multiple-point outputs, yielding final 3D keypoint locations. The key insight is that Molmo's specialized pixel-level training provides the fine-grained localization that standard MLLMs (GPT-4o, PaliGemma) lack, and that multi-view 3D consistency through clustering compensates for per-view detection noise.

## Results

On the KeypointNet dataset (airplane, chair, table classes), ZeroKey achieves 79.43% IoU at a 0.10 geodesic distance threshold, substantially outperforming all zero-shot MLLM baselines (GPT-4o: 20.73%, CLIP-DINOiser: 25.56%, PaliGemma 2: 11.65%, RedCircle: 18.50%). ZeroKey also surpasses the strongest few-shot method B2-3D (70.57%) at thresholds ≥ 0.06, and outperforms all fully supervised methods (e.g., FSKD: 57.03%, UKPGAN: 46.49%) across all thresholds. At the tighter 0.01 threshold, ZeroKey scores 13.16% IoU, still exceeding all MLLM-based baselines by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZeroKey decisively outperforms all four zero-shot MLLM-based baselines: at the 0.10 threshold it scores 79.43% IoU versus GPT-4o (20.73%), CLIP-DINOiser (25.56%), RedCircle (18.50%), and PaliGemma 2 (11.65%), confirming that pixel-level training (as in Molmo) is essential for point-level 3D reasoning. Against the unsupervised StablePoints baseline (38.22% at 0.10), ZeroKey more than doubles the IoU, and it also surpasses all classical supervised 3D keypoint detectors (Harris-3D, SIFT-3D, ISS, USIP, D3Feat, UKPGAN, FSKD) at every threshold. The closest competitor is the few-shot B2-3D method (70.57% at 0.10), which leads ZeroKey at tighter thresholds (≤ 0.05) but is overtaken by ZeroKey from 0.06 onward (62.06% vs 61.61%), demonstrating that the zero-shot semantic approach becomes more accurate as the tolerance for localization error increases. The paper attributes ZeroKey's relative weakness at very small thresholds (e.g., 0.001: 9.12% vs B2-3D's 12.41%) to the inherently semantic, name-driven nature of the method, which prioritizes high-level part identification over sub-millimeter precision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Molmo [12], an MLLM trained with pixel-level annotation tasks, repurposing its 2D point-localization capability for a 3D task via multi-view rendering and back-projection. It departs from the 3D keypoint detection literature (KeypointNet, B2-3D, UKPGAN) which relies on supervised or few-shot 3D training, and from 3D LLMs (3D-LLM, ULIP, PointLLM) that inherit the limited localization of standard vision-language backbones. The multi-view 2D-to-3D lifting strategy connects to the broader zero-shot 3D segmentation literature (SATR, Segment3D), but the target here is a single fine-grained 3D point per text query rather than a segmentation mask.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first zero-shot, category-agnostic 3D keypoint detection method, removing the need for 3D annotations entirely and opening the door to keypoint-based shape manipulation, correspondence, and deformation on arbitrary unseen categories. The Schelling Points and describability analyses reveal that MLLM 3D understanding is tightly coupled to linguistic salience—a point that is hard to name is hard to retrieve—highlighting a fundamental limitation of language-grounded spatial reasoning. Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the three evaluated classes (airplane, chair, table), whether 3D multi-view consistency can be fed back to improve MLLM pixel-level understanding, and whether the method scales to more complex or articulated shapes.
