## Problem

LoRA's low-rank assumption creates an approximation gap when the desired fine-tuning weights are not actually low-rank, limiting both performance and the ability to simultaneously achieve ultra-parameter-efficiency. Additionally, LoRA's simple matrix decomposition offers limited flexibility in adjusting the fine-tuning budget or compressing large weight matrices.

## Input / Output

The method fine-tunes the UNet attention layers of Stable Diffusion XL (for subject-driven generation) and Stable Diffusion v1.5 (for controllable generation). For subject-driven generation, a text prompt is consumed to produce images of a previously unseen subject; for controllable generation, a text prompt paired with a control signal (face landmarks, segmentation maps, or Canny edges) conditions the output image. The method achieves ultra-parameter-efficiency, fine-tuning SDXL with as few as 0.4M trainable parameters while matching or exceeding LoRA at 1.45M.

## Method

TLoRA decomposes the fine-tuning into two parts: a full-rank dense transform (parameterized as a Tensor-Ring Matrix, TRM) applied to the pre-trained weight, and a compact low-rank residual (parameterized as a Tensor-Ring, TR) added on top. The TRM transform adaptively aligns the pre-trained weight toward the target weight, reducing the effective rank of the residual so that the much smaller TR structure can approximate it with lower error. The TRM provides a dense, full-rank transform with O(DI^{2/D}R²) memory cost, avoiding the sparsity limitations of block-diagonal or butterfly OFT variants. For the TR residual, the authors introduce a zero-initialization strategy (B¹ set to zero, other cores from a μP-scaled Gaussian) that preserves the pre-trained model at initialization, unlike prior TR-based PEFT methods that use random Gaussian initialization. Identity and orthogonal regularizations on the transform are computed efficiently on the small core tensors rather than the full matrix.

## Results

On controllable generation (SD v1.5), TLoRA*_(2,4) with 0.94M parameters achieves the best S2I mIoU of 29.23 and aACC of 69.21, outperforming LoRA_r=4 (27.72 mIoU, 64.99 aACC) and OFT_r=32 (28.52 mIoU, 66.04 aACC) with fewer parameters. On C2I, TLoRA*_(2,4) achieves the highest IoU (0.2033e-4) and F1 (0.3375e-4). On subject-driven generation (SDXL, DreamBooth), TLoRA with only 0.40M parameters achieves the best overall Pareto front across CLIP-I, CLIP-T, DINOv2, and LPIPS metrics, surpassing LoRA (1.45M), DoRA (2.12M), and BOFT (3.81M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TLoRA*_(2,4) (0.94M params) outperforms LoRA_r=4 (0.80M) on all S2I metrics (mIoU 29.23 vs 27.72, aACC 69.21 vs 64.99) and C2I metrics (IoU 0.2033e-4 vs 0.1803e-4, F1 0.3375e-4 vs 0.3055e-4), demonstrating that the TRM transform adaptation adds substantial value over pure low-rank residual. Against DoRA_r=4, TLoRA*_(2,4) wins on every metric, with the largest gap on S2I Error (5.32 vs 6.43) and C2I F1 (0.3375e-4 vs 0.2487e-4), confirming that a learnable dense transform is more effective than DoRA's diagonal magnitude decomposition. Compared to OFT_r=32 (1.50M) and BOFT_(m=2,r=32) (2.41M), TLoRA*_(2,4) achieves better S2I Error (5.32 vs 7.35/7.64) and aACC (69.21 vs 66.04/66.19) with roughly half the parameters, showing that dense TRM transforms are superior to orthogonal or butterfly-structured transforms. ETHER+ (0.40M) is competitive on C2I but lags on S2I (mIoU 27.66 vs 29.23, aACC 65.76 vs 69.21). LoRETTA_(32,8) (0.41M) is catastrophically unstable, with S2I Error of 118.14 versus 5.32 for TLoRA, attributed to its non-zero initialization destroying pre-trained information. On subject-driven generation, TLoRA (0.40M) achieves the best Pareto front across CLIP-I, DINOv2, CLIP-T, and LPIPS, outperforming all baselines including LoRA (1.45M) and BOFT (3.81M) with 3–10× fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

TLoRA sits at the intersection of two PEFT lineages: low-rank additive adaptation (LoRA, DoRA) and transform-based adaptation (OFT, BOFT, ETHER). It departs from OFT/BOFT by replacing their sparse or orthogonal transforms with a learnable dense full-rank transform parameterized via tensor-ring matrix (TRM), and from LoRA by adding this transform to reduce the rank of the residual before approximating it with a compact tensor-ring (TR) structure. The paper further shows that DoRA's diagonal magnitude scaling and Fourier-based LoRA's fixed DFT projection are special cases of the proposed transform-plus-residual framework, unifying them under a single learnable dense transform. The TR residual component builds on LoRETTA's tensor-train adaptation but introduces a μP-inspired zero-initialization strategy to avoid the training instability observed in prior TR-based PEFT methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method achieves ultra-parameter-efficiency (0.4M trainable parameters for SDXL) while matching or exceeding LoRA and DoRA on subject-driven and controllable generation, making personalized diffusion fine-tuning accessible on consumer hardware. The transform-plus-residual framework provides a principled explanation for why DoRA and Fourier-based methods work (they implicitly apply a transform to reduce residual rank) and suggests that learnable dense transforms should outperform fixed or sparse ones. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to LLMs, vision transformers, and convolutional layers (where tensor decomposition is more natural), and whether the identity regularization on the TRM transform is sufficient to prevent catastrophic forgetting at scale. The paper is limited to two diffusion model families and two task types, so broader validation is needed.
