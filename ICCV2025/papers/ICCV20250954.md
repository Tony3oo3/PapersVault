## Problem

Existing ID-preserving video generation methods either require per-identity fine-tuning or produce static copy-and-paste results with limited facial motion, failing to balance identity consistency with dynamic, natural expressions. A key architectural barrier is that state-of-the-art video diffusion transformers (DiTs) use full-attention cross-modal paradigms that are incompatible with the decoupled cross-attention conditioning mechanisms used in image-based ID personalization, making direct integration of identity features non-trivial.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250954/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference identity image (RGB, arbitrary resolution) and a free-form text prompt as input, producing a temporally consistent identity-preserved video. The reference image is consumed through a dual-branch perceiver that decouples high-level identity embeddings (via ArcFace) from fine-grained structural features (via learnable queries), and the identity embedding is injected into the text embedding space via a token-level masked replacement targeting identity-relevant tokens (e.g., 'man', 'woman'). Evaluation uses 50 identities from PubFig with 80 prompts (40 single-character, 40 action-specific), generating four personalized videos per identity.

## Method

MagicMirror introduces a dual-branch facial feature extractor (identity perceiver using ArcFace-conditioned Q-Former queries, structural perceiver using learnable Q-Former queries) that decouples identity from facial structure. A lightweight cross-modal adapter integrates these features into the CogVideoX-5B mm-DiT backbone via two mechanisms: (1) Conditioned Adaptive Normalization (CAN), which predicts modality-specific distribution shifts for video and text latents conditioned on the identity embedding, acting as a distribution prior through residual-modulated normalization factors; and (2) a decoupled cross-attention branch that supplements the full self-attention with facial key-value projections. Training follows a two-stage strategy: image pre-training on synthetic identity pairs (generated via PhotoMaker-V2 with ArcFace-based filtering at cosine similarity > 0.65) to learn robust identity representations, followed by video fine-tuning to ensure temporal consistency, with a face-region-restricted denoising loss applied to 50% of samples.

## Results

MagicMirror achieves the best overall preference score (7.315) and highest dynamic degree (0.705) among all compared methods, while maintaining strong identity preservation (Average ID Similarity 0.922, Similarity Decay 0.002). It leads on face motion metrics (FM_ref 0.730, FM_inter 0.610), indicating more natural and dynamic facial expressions than all baselines. In the user study, MagicMirror scores highest on Visual Quality (6.97), Text Alignment (8.88), and Dynamic Degree (7.02), confirming its advantage in perceptual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MagicMirror outperforms ID-Animator [20] on nearly every metric, most dramatically on Dynamic Degree (0.705 vs. 0.140) and FM_inter (0.610 vs. 0.181), showing that ID-Animator's strong identity constraint (ID Sim. 0.923) comes at the cost of producing near-static, copy-paste-like faces. Against ConsisID [69], MagicMirror wins on Dynamic Degree (0.705 vs. 0.615), FM_ref (0.730 vs. 0.652), and Overall Preference (7.315 vs. 6.640), while ConsisID retains a slight edge on Inception Score (11.09 vs. 10.59). Compared to the I2V baselines (CogVideoX-I2V, DynamiCrafter, EasyAnimate), MagicMirror achieves substantially higher face motion scores (FM_ref 0.730 vs. 0.413/0.237/0.262) and better identity similarity (0.922 vs. 0.901/0.896/0.903), confirming that its dual-branch design and CAN module effectively inject identity without sacrificing motion diversity. In the user study, MagicMirror leads all baselines on Visual Quality, Text Alignment, and Dynamic Degree, with the closest competitor being CogVideoX-I2V on Visual Quality (6.86 vs. 6.97).

## Relation to Prior Work

MagicMirror extends the CogVideoX mm-DiT video generation backbone by introducing a lightweight cross-modal adapter that injects facial identity conditions through a novel Conditioned Adaptive Normalization (CAN) module, departing from the simpler decoupled cross-attention injection used in U-Net-based methods like ID-Animator and IP-Adapter. The dual-branch feature extraction repurposes PhotoMaker's stacked ID embedding (ArcFace + CLIP via Q-Former perceivers) to decouple high-level identity from fine-grained structural features, a separation not present in prior ID-preserving video methods. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against the co-current ConsisID (also DiT-based) by arguing that modulation layers in full-attention DiTs are the correct injection point for identity, rather than frequency decomposition or additional cross-attention heads alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CAN mechanism offers a general recipe for integrating new conditioning modalities into full-attention DiT architectures with minimal parameter overhead, a technique likely transferable beyond face ID to other control signals (pose, style, object identity). The paper's two-stage training strategy (image pre-training on synthetic pairs → video fine-tuning) provides a practical template for addressing the data scarcity problem in personalized video generation. Key limitations remain: single-identity only, facial features exclusively (no clothing or body attributes), and the approach has not been validated on multi-person or long-sequence scenarios. Open questions include whether CAN generalizes to non-facial identity attributes and whether the synthetic data pipeline can be replaced with smaller curated datasets.
