## Problem

Large text-to-image models deliver superior generation quality but incur high cloud serving costs, while lightweight edge models are cheap but often produce inferior results for complex prompts. No prior work has studied predictive routing for T2I generation, and two fundamental challenges make direct adaptation of LLM routing infeasible: image quality lacks a single unified metric (unlike text correctness), and the vast image output space makes it difficult to predict quality from a short text prompt before generation.

## Input / Output

The routing model takes a user text prompt as a sequence of tokens and outputs a 10-dimensional quality prediction vector (definition, detail, clarity, sharpness, harmony, realism, color, consistency, layout, integrity) along with a scalar Pareto Relative Superiority score. The routing strategy then applies a threshold α ≤ 0.5 on the predicted PRS to produce a binary decision: prompts with PRS below α are sent to the cloud model, others stay on the edge. The final output is the generated image from whichever model is selected, with the constraint that the fraction of prompts routed to the cloud does not exceed a preset routing rate ρ_r.

## Method

RouteT2I introduces a dual-gate token selection Mixture-of-Experts architecture embedded in a Transformer to predict multi-metric image quality from prompts. A token selection gate computes an affinity matrix between token embeddings and expert embeddings (one expert per quality metric), then selects top-K tokens per expert to focus on quality-relevant words while suppressing noise. A dual-gate mechanism uses separate positive and negative gates to capture opposing token influences on each metric; within each expert, a shared score matrix evaluates both aspects via low-rank projection matrices, reducing parameters from O(hd) to O(l(h+d)). Multiple prediction heads output per-metric scores, which are combined into a Pareto Relative Superiority score that quantifies the normalized quality gap between edge- and cloud-generated images. The routing strategy sets a threshold on predicted PRS so that only prompts where the cloud model shows substantial quality advantage are routed there, maximizing overall quality under a cost budget.

## Results

At a 50% routing rate (half of prompts sent to cloud), RouteT2I achieves a relative performance improvement (ΔP) of 83.97%, meaning it recovers 84% of the quality gap between the edge model (SD2.1) and the cloud model (SD3) while only using the cloud for half the requests. On normalized win rate improvement, RouteT2I reaches 30.60% at a 40% routing rate, exceeding the best baseline (ZOOTER at 26.77%) by 3.83 percentage points. On cost efficiency, to achieve a 40% ΔP target, RouteT2I reduces cloud calls by 71.81% compared to random routing, versus 69.28% for ZOOTER.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RouteT2I outperforms all three adapted LLM routing baselines on the primary metric ΔP (relative performance improvement): 83.97% vs. ZOOTER (77.95%), Hybrid LLM (73.49%), RouteLLM-BERT (71.51%), and RouteLLM-MF (69.90%), a margin of at least 6 percentage points over the strongest baseline. On individual quality metrics, RouteT2I leads on 6 of 10 dimensions (notably Realism at 0.5804 vs. 0.5788 for ZOOTER, and Harmony at 0.5975 vs. 0.5966), while trailing slightly on a few others (e.g., Detail: 0.6786 vs. 0.6796 for ZOOTER), reflecting its multi-objective optimization trade-off. On cost efficiency (Table 4), RouteT2I achieves 71.81% cost saving at a 40% ΔP target versus 69.28% for ZOOTER and 62.06% for Hybrid LLM. On normalized win rate improvement (Table 3), RouteT2I leads at every routing rate from 40% to 80%, with the largest gap at 40% routing (30.60% vs. 26.77% for ZOOTER). The baselines were adapted from LLM routing by replacing their single-objective quality scoring with the average across the 10 image quality metrics, which the authors note limits their ability to balance multiple quality dimensions simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by being the first to formulate predictive model routing specifically for text-to-image generation, departing from the LLM routing literature (RouteLLM, ZOOTER, Hybrid LLM) in two fundamental ways: the quality objective is multi-dimensional rather than single-objective, and the output space (images) is vastly larger than the input space (text prompts), making pre-generation quality prediction inherently harder. Architecturally, it repurposes the mixture-of-experts paradigm—specifically token selection and expert-choice routing from LLM MoE literature—into a dual-gate structure where experts align with quality metrics and a token selection gate identifies prompt tokens that most influence each metric. The multi-metric quality assessment draws on CLIP-based contrastive scoring (positive/negative prompt pairs) previously used for image quality evaluation, but extends it into a Pareto relative superiority framework for cross-model comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With large T2I models like SD3.5 costing $65K per million API calls, the practical motivation for reducing cloud invocations while preserving quality is strong and timely. The paper's main limitation is evaluation on COCO 2014 captions, which are relatively short and structured, leaving open how well the routing generalizes to long-form, creative, or adversarial prompts in production settings. Additionally, all 18 model pairs tested are from a small set of open-source architectures (Stable Diffusion family, PixArt, Infinity), so the framework's transferability to proprietary or fundamentally different T2I systems (e.g., autoregressive vs. diffusion) remains untested. An open question is whether the multi-metric quality framework and PRS can be extended to interactive or iterative generation settings where user feedback modifies the prompt mid-generation.
