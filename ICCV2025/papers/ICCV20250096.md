## Problem

Existing 3D motion estimation methods rely on local spatial or temporal smoothness assumptions that break down when depth variation causes heterogeneous spatio-temporal deformations in image space. While event cameras provide microsecond temporal resolution to observe these deformations, no prior framework explicitly models the resulting non-stationary, locally heterogeneous motion trajectories, leaving a gap between fine temporal observation and physically consistent 3D motion representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250096/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes asynchronous event streams (pixel-level brightness changes with polarity) at 320×960 resolution over 100 ms windows, which are projected into two complementary representations: an Event Voxel (spatially aggregated) and an Event Kymograph (x-t and y-t decoupled with continuous Gaussian temporal kernel at ~10 μs resolution). Outputs include a continuous NURBS trajectory T(t,x,y)→R² enabling optical flow queries at arbitrary timestamps, a motion-in-depth field derived from trajectory temporal gradients, and a full 3D scene flow via depth-normalized decomposition, all produced with only 5.61 M parameters and 40 ms inference on an A6000 GPU.

## Method

EMoTive constructs dual cost volumes—spatial (2D conv encoder on Event Voxel with multi-resolution inner-product matching) and temporal (1D conv encoder on Event Kymograph subblocks with cross-subblock correlation fused via tensor product)—to guide iterative trajectory refinement. The core representation is a non-uniform rational B-spline (NURBS) trajectory whose knot vector and control-point weights are adaptively set by a density-aware mechanism: top-n temporal indices are extracted from a 3D-pooled event density tensor, converted to normalized timestamps for knot placement, and softmax-normalized densities determine curve weights. Control points are updated iteratively via a GRU that fuses temporal cost volumes at adaptively sampled timestamps, spatial cost volume at end-time displacement, and context features. Motion in depth is computed from the trajectory's temporal gradient under a rigid-body perspective model, with a multi-view temporal stability constraint (M_k = (1/k)Σ(t_k/t_i)(M_i−1)+1) aggregating observations across adjacent timestamps for robustness. Training uses a multi-task loss combining exponentially weighted L1 flow and depth supervision with a first-order temporal gradient regularization term (λ=10⁻⁷) to suppress non-physical trajectory distortions.

## Results

On the CarlaEvent3D dataset, EMoTive achieves 2.547 EPE and 22.866 f1 for optical flow (dense labels), and 113.593 log-mid for motion in depth, outperforming all image-based baselines by 43.5% in EPE and 33.6% in log-mid. On the DSEC real-world benchmark, EMoTive reaches 0.439 EPE, 0.742% f1, and 152.722 log-mid, surpassing TPCV by 43.9% in EPE and Expansion by 14.7% in log-mid. For 3D scene flow on DSEC, EMoTive achieves 0.205 cm EPE_3D and 25.5% ACC_0.05 using only event input, outperforming monocular event-based methods by 58% in EPE_3D. The model uses only 5.61M parameters and runs at 40 ms inference on an A6000 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMoTive outperforms all spatial-correlation-based baselines (Expansion, TPCV, ScaleFlow, Scale++) by large margins on both CarlaEvent3D and DSEC, with 43.5% lower EPE and 33.6% lower log-mid on the synthetic dataset, attributed to event data's low spatial redundancy degrading frame-based methods. Against E-RAFT, EMoTive achieves slightly better EPE (2.547 vs 2.781) and f1 (22.866 vs 24.604) on CarlaEvent3D while additionally providing motion-in-depth estimation that E-RAFT cannot. The event-per-event method ETTCM performs dramatically worse (27.596 EPE vs 2.850 on sparse labels), confirming that per-event processing struggles with complex motion. On DSEC scene flow (Table 3), EMoTive (0.205 EPE_3D, 25.5% ACC_0.05) substantially outperforms monocular event-based methods (Expansion: 0.489, Scale++: 0.603) but trails multimodal fusion methods CamLiFlow (0.120) and RPEFlow (0.104) that leverage LiDAR point clouds for privileged depth. The non-uniform trajectory design contributes an additional 4.6% EPE and 6.9% log-mid improvement over the uniform B-spline variant, validating the density-aware adaptation mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

EMoTive sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) event-based optical flow estimation, where it adopts the cost-volume and iterative GRU refinement architecture from E-RAFT/RAFT; (2) depth-normalized 3D motion estimation via optical expansion (Yang & Ramanan, ScaleFlow/Scale++), from which it inherits the decomposition into optical flow and motion-in-depth; and (3) parametric curve modeling of event-based motion (Time Lens++, Dense Continuous-Time Optical Flow), which it extends from uniform B-splines to event-guided non-uniform NURBS with adaptive knot vectors and weights. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of the Event Kymograph—a decoupled x-t/y-t projection with continuous Gaussian temporal kernels—as a complementary representation to the standard Event Voxel, enabling fine-grained temporal observation that frame-based or coarsely-quantized event representations cannot provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The trajectory-centric formulation offers a principled way to query pixel motion at arbitrary timestamps and to derive depth motion from temporal derivatives, addressing the fundamental inconsistency between local spatial smoothness and depth-induced deformation that plagues frame-based 3D motion estimation. However, the method remains substantially behind multimodal LiDAR+RGB+Event approaches on DSEC (58% worse EPE_3D), and the primary training dataset (CarlaEvent3D) is synthetic, raising questions about generalization to real-world sensor noise and long-tail scenarios. Open questions include whether the NURBS trajectory paradigm scales to multi-object or non-rigid motion, how it integrates with predictive control loops, and whether the density-aware adaptation mechanism generalizes beyond the specific event-rate distributions encountered in driving scenarios.
