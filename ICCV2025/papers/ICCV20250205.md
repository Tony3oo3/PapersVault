## Problem

Existing 4D driving simulation methods based on NeRF, 3D Gaussian Splatting, or video generation cannot independently control spatial viewpoint and temporal progression within a single coherent scene. They struggle with large camera viewpoint shifts, long-term temporal consistency under static views, and multi-view synchronization, making it impossible to, for example, translate the camera while freezing time or advance time while fixing the viewpoint.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250205/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes six surround-view RGB images (576×1024) along with calibrated camera intrinsics/extrinsics and ego-vehicle pose trajectories to construct a 4D point cloud, which is then projected into sparse 2D keyframe sequences under user-specified camera pose and time controls. The output is a continuous 4D simulation video (H×W×t×3) at 25 fps with synchronized six panoramic views, supporting operations like viewpoint translation/rotation at frozen time, temporal advancement at frozen space, and selective object removal.

## Method

Stag reconstructs aligned 4D point clouds from surround-view data using R3D3-based per-frame depth estimation followed by iterative fine alignment that minimizes point-to-reference distance across consecutive frames. The key insight is spatial-temporal decoupling: the 4D point cloud is projected into sparse 2D keyframes under independently controlled camera pose C_p and time T_t, so fixing one dimension while varying the other yields consistent keyframe sequences. A video diffusion model (fine-tuned from ViewCrafter) then synthesizes continuous video from these sparse keyframe projections, trained in two stages: a time-focused stage on single-view temporal sequences and a spatial-focused stage that adds cross-view attention (spatial attention over 6 views and temporal attention over frames) to enforce multi-view consistency. The conditioning signal is the projected sparse point cloud rather than a full reference image, and CLIP text embeddings provide additional sparse semantic guidance.

## Results

On the Waymo-NOTR dataset (zero-shot), Stag achieves PSNR 32.91 / SSIM 0.934 / LPIPS 0.104 for scene reconstruction and PSNR 28.08 / SSIM 0.864 / LPIPS 0.129 for novel view synthesis, outperforming all baselines including S3Gaussian (PSNR 31.35) and 3DGS (PSNR 28.47). For the core 4D simulation task, Stag achieves FID 34.9 and FVD 91.5 under frozen-time conditions, substantially outperforming Vista (FID 89.7, FVD 356.8) and ViewCrafter (FID 71.8, FVD 218.6). On StreetGaussian, Stag reaches PSNR 35.23 and LPIPS 0.043, the best across all seven compared methods. Inference time is 104.7 s total (1.7 s point cloud reconstruction + 103 s video generation), competitive with ViewCrafter (119 s) and faster than Vista (127 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Stag outperforms all eight baselines across every reported metric. Against 3DGS-based methods (3DGS, S3Gaussian, StreetGaussian), Stag gains 1.5–4.5 dB PSNR in reconstruction and 0.6–1.1 dB in novel view synthesis on Waymo-NOTR, with the largest margin over 3DGS (e.g., 32.91 vs. 28.47 PSNR on D32). Against NeRF-based methods (EmerNeRF, MARS), the advantage is even more pronounced, with Stag exceeding EmerNeRF by ~4.7 dB PSNR on D32 reconstruction. On the 4D simulation task (Table 4), Stag's FID of 34.9 under frozen-time is 2.6× lower than Vista (89.7) and 2.1× lower than ViewCrafter (71.8), and its FVD of 91.5 is roughly 4× lower than Vista (356.8). On the Waymo Open Dataset (Table 3), Stag achieves 31.23 PSNR in the front view, surpassing StreetGaussian (30.80) and EmerNeRF (30.28), and leads in multi-view synthesis (26.03 PSNR) over FreeVS (24.96). The only metric where Stag does not lead is SSIM* on D32 NVS (0.644 vs. 3DGS's 0.753), suggesting slightly weaker structural fidelity for dynamic objects in novel views, though it still leads on PSNR* and LPIPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

Stag sits at the intersection of two research threads: 4D scene reconstruction for autonomous driving (NeRF, 3DGS, S3Gaussian, EmerNeRF, StreetGaussian) and controllable video generation (Vista, ViewCrafter). It builds directly on ViewCrafter as the video diffusion backbone (fine-tuned for 5,000 iterations) and on R3D3 for per-frame depth estimation to construct point clouds. The key conceptual departure is the explicit spatial-temporal decoupling via 4D point cloud keyframe projection: rather than treating 4D simulation as a monolithic generation task, Stag uses aligned point clouds as an intermediate representation that independently parameterizes camera pose (spatial) and time, then conditions a cross-view diffusion model on the resulting sparse 2D projections. This bridges the gap between geometric reconstruction methods (which lack generative flexibility) and pure video generation methods (which lack explicit 3D/4D structure).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to independently freeze time while translating/rotating the camera, or freeze the camera while advancing time, provides a practical tool for autonomous driving validation that neither pure reconstruction nor pure generation methods offer. The multi-view consistency across six surround views is particularly relevant for real vehicle testing pipelines. However, the paper acknowledges a significant limitation: controlling individual vehicle or pedestrian motion via rigid-body clustering of point clouds is left to future work, which limits the method's utility for closed-loop scenario testing. Open questions include scalability to longer temporal sequences, handling of occluded or newly-appearing objects, and whether the two-stage training (time-focused then spatial-focused) can be unified.
