## Problem

Existing 3D multimodal LLMs can perform scene-level captioning or object-level grounding but cannot produce fine-grained, part-aware segmentation masks linked to material-aware textual descriptions. No dataset or model supports the task of generating grounded descriptions where each part-level phrase is tied to a point-wise segmentation mask, a capability essential for robotic interaction with real-world objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252568/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 3D point cloud (N×6 coordinates) and a natural-language instruction as input, and produces a grounded description in which part-level phrases are wrapped in special <p>…</p> tokens, each followed by a [SEG] token whose hidden state drives a corresponding point-wise segmentation mask. Part annotations are capped at six per object (selected by largest visible area in rendered views), and the system supports three task variants: multi-part grounded description (PaPGD), direct single-part segmentation, and reasoning-based single-part segmentation.

## Method

Kestrel couples a Uni3D-g point encoder and a Vicuna-7B LLM (fine-tuned with LoRA, rank 8) with a PointNet++-based Point Feature Propagation Module (PFPM) and a 3D segmentation decoder. The key mechanism is a two-stage query refinement: [SEG] token hidden states from the LLM are projected to initial queries q₀, then at each of two upsampling levels the queries are cross-attended against intermediate point features using binary attention masks (mᵢ) computed by thresholding sigmoid dot-products, progressively sharpening spatial localization. Training uses an auto-regressive cross-entropy loss for text generation combined with BCE and Dice losses for mask prediction (weights 1.0, 2.0, 0.5), and the model is pretrained on PointLLM instructions plus 3DCoMPaT-GrIn point-cloud–text pairs before fine-tuning on the full 111K-sample dataset for 5 epochs.

## Results

On the Part-Aware Point Grounded Description task (3DCoMPaT-GrIn), Kestrel achieves the highest mIoU of 86.70, AP50 of 51.27, and Recall of 67.37 for segmentation grounding, while also scoring the best GPT-based language evaluation (50.10) and METEOR (32.88). On Direct Segmentation, Kestrel reaches 78.78 mIoU and 63.91 AP50; on Reasoning Segmentation, it achieves 71.80 mIoU and 51.05 AP50. On the external RPSeg3D benchmark, Kestrel outperforms PARIS3D by 15.77 mIoU (72.54 vs. 56.77).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Kestrel outperforms SegPoint (the strongest segmentation-capable baseline) across all metrics on PaPGD: +3.19 mIoU (86.70 vs. 83.51), +4.11 AP50 (51.27 vs. 47.16), and +6.71 Recall (67.37 vs. 60.66), while also leading on language metrics (METEOR 32.88 vs. 27.62, GPT EVAL 50.10 vs. 42.80). Against PARIS3D, Kestrel achieves a dramatic 39.44 mIoU improvement (86.70 vs. 47.26) and 39.37 AP50 gain, attributed to PARIS3D's reliance on 2D-rendered views that miss occluded parts. On the external RPSeg3D benchmark, Kestrel surpasses PARIS3D by 15.77 mIoU (72.54 vs. 56.77) after one epoch of fine-tuning. PointLLM-7B and ShapeLLM-7B cannot produce segmentation masks; Kestrel matches or exceeds their language scores (e.g., METEOR 32.88 vs. 29.51/30.05) while adding full part-level grounding. The Mask3D Baseline (a 3D variant of GLaMM using Mask3D) underperforms Kestrel by 28.80 mIoU, confirming the benefit of the query refinement mechanism over a single-stage decoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

Kestrel extends the 2D segmentation-grounding paradigm of LISA and GLaMM (which attach a segmentation decoder to an MLLM via [SEG] tokens) into the 3D point-cloud domain, replacing SAM with a PointNet++-based feature propagation module and a Mask3D-style decoder. It departs from PARIS3D, which performs part segmentation by uplifting 2D segmentations onto 3D (suffering domain shift), by operating purely in 3D space. The dataset builds directly on 3DCoMPaT's part and material annotations, but augments them with GPT-4o-generated grounded descriptions and multi-part segmentation masks, filling a gap that prior 3D vision-language datasets (ScanRefer, Instruct3D, RPSeg3D) leave open at the part level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3DCoMPaT-GrIn dataset (111K+ samples) and the PaPGD task formulation provide the first large-scale resource for training and evaluating multi-part, material-aware 3D grounded description, a capability directly relevant to robotic manipulation and scene interaction. The 3D-CALC evaluation metric offers a more fine-grained compositional assessment than BLEU/ROUGE, though its reliance on GPT-4o scoring introduces subjectivity. Key limitations include the single-object scope of all training point clouds, dependence on synthetic 3DCoMPaT geometry, and the fact that the query refinement mechanism, while effective, is a relatively straightforward multi-scale attention design; whether the approach scales to open-vocabulary or truly multi-object real-world scenes remains an open question.
