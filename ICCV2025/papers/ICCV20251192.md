## Problem

Video-LLMs struggle with precise temporal localization because existing temporal expression paradigms either conflate time with text-based numbers, rely on discrete temporal tokens with long-tailed distributions, or require heavy specialized grounding heads that re-input visual information. Compounding this, large-scale training data with fine-grained event boundaries (beyond shot-level or fixed-interval granularity) is scarce, limiting models' ability to learn accurate temporal grounding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251192/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes uniformly sampled video frames (16 for InternVL2.5, up to 32 for LLaVA-OneVision) at original resolution alongside a text query (event description or question). During autoregressive generation, a single <TIME_STAMP> token is emitted and decoded into normalized start/end timestamps in [0,1]; these are then re-encoded as Gaussian-distributed time tokens and spliced back into the sequence to update temporal context for subsequent steps. The final output replaces <TIME_STAMP> with concrete (st, et) values concatenated with generated text, yielding time-stamped event descriptions or grounded answers.

## Method

DisTime introduces a single learnable <TIME_STAMP> token that occupies a dedicated position in the LLM vocabulary, creating a continuous embedding space for time distinct from numerical text tokens. A lightweight MLP-based Distribution-based Time Decoder maps the token's final-layer hidden state to two probability distributions (start and end) over reg_max+1 uniformly spaced anchor bins via softmax, then computes continuous timestamps as anchor-weighted sums, inspired by DFL's treatment of boundary ambiguity. A Distribution-based Time Encoder performs the inverse: it projects timestamps into fixed-variance Gaussian distributions, discretizes them into bins, and maps the concatenated distributions back to an LLM-compatible embedding via MLP. During autoregression, decoded timestamps are re-encoded and substituted for the raw <TIME_STAMP>, implementing Iterative Time Refinement that normalizes ambiguous multi-modal distributions into standardized Gaussian forms. Training uses a combined loss of distribution focal loss, 1D-IoU regression loss, and next-token prediction loss, with LoRA applied to the LLM while the vision backbone remains frozen; the time encoder/decoder add only ~0.3–0.8% of total parameters.

## Results

On Charades-STA (zero-shot), DisTime-InternVL-8B achieves 81.0 R@1 (IoU=0.3), 60.3 R@1 (IoU=0.5), and 53.1 mIoU, surpassing all dedicated temporal grounding models and Video-LLMs. On ANet-Caption, DisTime-InternVL-8B reaches 72.9 R@1 (IoU=0.3) and 50.5 mIoU, outperforming TimeMarker (67.4 / 49.5) and MrBLIP (53.92 / –). On NExT-GQA, DisTime-InternVL-8B attains the best scores across all metrics including 80.3 Acc@QA and 35.9 mIoU. On YouCook2 dense video captioning, DisTime-InternVL-8B achieves 26.4 F1 and 31.0 CIDEr, substantially exceeding VTG-LLM (17.5 / 5.0) and VTimeLLM (– / 27.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Charades-STA (zero-shot), DisTime-InternVL-8B (81.0 R@1@0.3) surpasses the strongest dedicated model UniVTG (72.6) by +8.4% and the best Video-LLM TimeMarker (73.5) by +7.5%; even the 1B variant (78.1) beats TimeMarker by +4.6%. On R@1@0.5, DisTime-InternVL-8B leads TimeMarker by +8.4% (60.3 vs 51.9) and LLaVA-MR (70.7, dedicated) is the only model that exceeds it. On ANet-Caption, DisTime-InternVL-8B (72.9 R@1@0.3, 50.5 mIoU) outperforms TimeMarker (67.4, 49.5) and MrBLIP (53.92, –), though MrBLIP retains a slight edge on R@1@0.5 (53.92 vs 53.2). For dense video captioning on YouCook2, DisTime-InternVL-8B (F1=26.4, CIDEr=31.0) substantially outperforms VTG-LLM (17.5, 5.0) and VTimeLLM (–, 27.2), while on ANet-Caption it trails VTimeLLM (CIDEr 21.3 vs 27.2) due to fewer input frames (32 vs 100). On NExT-GQA, DisTime-InternVL-8B achieves the best scores in all eight metrics (e.g., 80.3 Acc@QA vs 70.8 for FrozenBILM; 35.9 mIoU vs 21.7 for SeVLLA). On general video understanding, DisTime-InternVL-8B matches InternVL2.5-8B on MVBench (72.0) and LongVideoBench (60.0), confirming that temporal specialization does not degrade general capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

DisTime builds directly on the Distribution Focal Loss (DFL) framework from Li et al. (NeurIPS 2020), transplanting the idea of representing boundaries as probability distributions over discrete bins from 2D object detection into the temporal grounding domain of Video-LLMs. It departs from prior temporal expression strategies in Video-LLMs—text-based numerics (GroundingGPT, TimeMarker), multi-token discretization (Momentor, VTG-LLM), and heavy specialized grounding heads (InternVideo2.5/CGDETR)—by using a single learnable token with a lightweight distribution-based decoder/encoder pair. The automated annotation pipeline also extends prior unsupervised temporal data expansion (VTimeLLM, InternVid-MR, Momentor) by replacing shot-boundary or fixed-interval constraints with dedicated temporal grounding models (UniMD, Mr.Blip, TFVTG) for fine-grained boundary detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The InternVid-TG dataset (1.25M events across 179K videos, 55× larger than ActivityNet-Caption) addresses a critical data bottleneck for temporal grounding in Video-LLMs, and the lightweight DisTime module (adding <1% parameters) offers a practical, plug-and-play solution for existing Video-LLMs. The zero-shot Charades-STA results surpassing dedicated temporal grounding models suggest that distribution-based temporal representation within an LLM can rival task-specific architectures. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to very long videos (>1 hour), whether the single-token constraint limits expressiveness for multi-event or hierarchical temporal reasoning, and whether the pseudo-label quality from the ensemble annotation pipeline degrades at scale beyond the 179K videos tested.
