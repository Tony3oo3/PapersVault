## Problem

Robustness to spurious correlations and model compressibility are both critical for deploying ML systems under distribution shift and resource constraints, yet it remains unclear whether these objectives are inherently in conflict or can be jointly achieved. No prior work has systematically identified a single training hyperparameter that simultaneously promotes both properties, nor has the mechanism by which large learning rates confer SC robustness been formally characterized.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 256×256 for ImageNet-1k, 64×64 for CelebA/Waterbirds) as well as binary feature vectors for synthetic parity and moon-star datasets. Beyond the predicted class label, the evaluation produces structured-pruning retention scores (κ-Prunability at κ = 0.1–0.9 on an unbiased test set), Integrated Gradients attribution maps for core vs. spurious feature analysis, and representation-level metrics (class-separation R², class-selectivity index, activation sparsity) computed on penultimate-layer activations.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is an extensive empirical and theoretical study demonstrating that large learning rates (up to the divergence threshold) simultaneously increase OOD robustness to spurious correlations, network compressibility, and favorable representation properties (core feature utilization, class separation, activation sparsity) across FCNs, CNNs (VGG11, ResNet18/50, Wide ResNet-101-2), and Swin Transformers. The mechanism is formalized in Proposition 1: under cross-entropy loss, up-scaled logits from fast norm growth at high LRs cause confident mispredictions of bias-conflicting samples, making their loss dominate the mini-batch gradient (scaling as O(ke^{αk})), which implicitly reweights training toward these harder examples and repeatedly resets spurious subnetworks in a lottery-ticket-like dynamic. The authors validate this by showing unbiased test accuracy strongly correlates with the maximum bias-conflicting loss ratio during training, and that high-LR models progressively shift attributions from spurious to core features. Large LRs are compared against batch size, momentum, L1/L2 regularization, focal loss, and ASAM, and shown to uniquely achieve the joint combination of OOD robustness, compressibility, and core feature utilization, with the additional finding that their documented success in standard classification tasks is attributable to addressing hidden spurious correlations in naturalistic training data.

## Results

Across 11 datasets (parity, moon-star, Colored MNIST, Corrupted CIFAR-10, MNIST-CIFAR, Double MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet-1k, CelebA, Waterbirds) and 6 architectures (FCN, VGG11-variant, ResNet18, ResNet50, Wide ResNet-101-2, Swin Transformer), increasing the learning rate consistently and simultaneously improves unbiased test accuracy (robustness to spurious correlations), network prunability (compressibility), core feature utilization (CSI), and class separation (R²). The paper reports results with normalized y-axes per figure (exact min/max values in supplementary material), but the key quantitative finding is that unbiased test accuracy strongly correlates with the maximum bias-conflicting loss ratio encountered during training (Fig. 12), confirming the proposed mechanism. Effects hold under SGD, Adam, PSGD, and various LR schedulers (cosine annealing, WSD), with at least 3 random seeds per configuration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares large learning rates against focal loss (FL, γ varied) and adaptive sharpness-aware minimization (ASAM, ρ varied), along with batch size, momentum, L1, and L2 regularization (Fig. 6, ResNet18 on semi-synthetic SC datasets). Large LRs consistently achieve a combination of OOD robustness, network compressibility, and core feature utilization that is 'on par with or surpassing' both FL and ASAM individually. Importantly, the paper shows in Fig. 9 that FL and ASAM create robustness through qualitatively different dynamics in the loss and feature-utilization landscape compared to large LRs, suggesting the mechanisms are complementary rather than redundant. The authors further note that L1/L2 regularization can be combined with high LRs to achieve even better trade-offs than either alone. No single baseline dominates large LRs across all three target properties simultaneously, which is the paper's central comparative claim.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads: the inductive bias of large learning rates in standard classification (Li et al. 2019, Lewkowycz et al. 2020, Mohtashami et al. 2023), robustness to spurious correlations (Sagawa et al. 2020, Geirhos et al. 2020, Puli et al. 2023), and model compressibility (Barsbey et al. 2021, Diffenderfer et al. 2021). While each thread has been studied independently, no prior work systematically examined how a single hyperparameter (LR) simultaneously governs both SC robustness and network prunability, or provided a mechanistic explanation linking the two. The proposed mechanism—confident mispredictions of bias-conflicting samples under up-scaled logits dominating the gradient—extends the catapult mechanism (Lewkowycz et al.) and gradient starvation (Pezeshki et al.) into the spurious-correlation setting, where it had not been previously formalized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a single, easily-tuned hyperparameter (learning rate) can jointly address two goals—robustness to distribution shift and resource efficiency—that were previously assumed to be in tension, offering a low-cost intervention for practitioners deploying models under compute constraints. The reframing that large-LR benefits in standard classification may stem from addressing hidden/rare SCs in training data opens a new diagnostic lens for understanding generalization gaps. However, the authors acknowledge the work is not an end-to-end theoretical account of optimization dynamics and loss landscapes, and leave open how multiple or hierarchical spurious correlations interact with training dynamics, as well as how to design explicit training procedures that exploit these effects more directly.
