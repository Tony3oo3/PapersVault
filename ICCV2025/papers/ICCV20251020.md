## Problem

Activity-biometrics requires identifying individuals across diverse daily activities, but identity cues become entangled with motion dynamics and appearance variations, making reliable biometric feature learning difficult. Existing approaches either depend on additional visual modalities (silhouettes, skeletons) that are unreliable under real-world conditions, or use CLIP-based contrastive learning that lacks identity-specific feature separation and temporal consistency, leading to misidentifications when appearance or activity changes.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB video as input, from which 8 frames are randomly sampled with stride 4 and resized to 224×224. During training only, a frozen VLM (LLaVA 1.5) generates three structured text prompts (biometrics, motion, non-biometrics) from a key-frame, which are encoded by a frozen BERT text encoder to provide language supervision. At inference, only the visual pipeline is used. The output is a combined identity similarity score computed via an adaptive MLP-weighted fusion of biometrics and motion cosine similarities, used for ranked identity retrieval in a probe-gallery matching setup.

## Method

DisenQ is a Disentangling Q-Former adapted from BLIP-2's Q-Former that replaces the single set of learnable queries with three independent query sets (biometrics z_b, motion z_m, non-biometrics z_b̂), each performing self-attention followed by cross-attention with the global visual feature F concatenated with its corresponding textual supervision embedding. This explicit separation ensures each query attends to distinct information without interaction, preserving disentangled feature representations. Training uses a combination of cross-entropy and triplet loss on biometrics features, cross-entropy on motion features, and an orthogonality constraint between biometrics and non-biometrics features to enforce independence. At retrieval, a lightweight MLP adaptively weights the biometrics and motion cosine similarities, allowing the model to prioritize motion cues when they are discriminative and fall back on stable biometric traits otherwise. The visual backbone is a frozen ViT-G/14 from EVA-CLIP, and DisenQ is initialized from InstructBLIP pre-trained weights.

## Results

On the three activity-biometrics benchmarks, DisenQ achieves state-of-the-art performance across all evaluation protocols. On NTU RGB-AB, it reaches 82.2% R@1 and 43.8 mAP (Same) and 80.9% R@1 and 41.3 mAP (Cross); on PKU MMD-AB, 89.2% R@1 and 59.3 mAP (Same) and 84.1% R@1 and 56.9 mAP (Cross); on Charades-AB, 49.9% R@1 and 34.8 mAP (Same) and 48.4% R@1 and 32.5 mAP (Cross). On the traditional video-based MEVID benchmark, DisenQ achieves 60.7% R@1, 70.3% R@5, and 30.4 mAP, outperforming the best visual-only method (ShARc, 59.5% R@1) and all visual+language baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DisenQ outperforms all 22 baselines on every evaluation protocol across the three activity-biometrics datasets. The most direct comparison is with ABNet [3], the only prior activity-biometrics method: DisenQ improves R@1 by +3.4% (NTU Same), +2.4% (PKU Same), and +4.1% (Charades Same), with mAP gains of +3.5, +2.0, and +3.2 respectively. Among visual+language baselines, DisenQ beats the strongest (Instruct-ReID [22]) by +4.0% R@1 on NTU Same, +4.9% on PKU Same, and +5.1% on Charades Same, indicating that structured language-guided disentanglement substantially outperforms global image-text contrastive alignment. On the traditional MEVID benchmark, DisenQ achieves the best R@1 (60.7%) and mAP (30.4), edging out ShARc [62] (59.5% R@1, 29.6 mAP) and ABNet (58.3% R@1, 30.1 mAP), while surpassing all visual+language methods by 6–7% R@1. No baseline is competitive with DisenQ on any of the six activity-biometrics evaluation settings (3 datasets × 2 protocols).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes BLIP-2's Q-Former architecture (originally designed for image-text alignment) into a feature-disentanglement module for video person identification, introducing three separate learnable query sets (biometrics, motion, non-biometrics) instead of a single unified query. It departs from the prior activity-biometrics method ABNet [3], which relied on unreliable silhouette extraction for feature separation, by replacing additional visual modalities with structured language supervision from a frozen VLM. The approach also extends CLIP-based person ReID methods by adding explicit temporal modeling and identity-specific feature disentanglement, addressing their inability to separate motion from biometric cues in video settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The language-guided disentanglement strategy offers a practical path toward activity-aware person identification without the fragility of auxiliary visual modalities (silhouettes, skeletons), which is important for real-world deployment in surveillance and smart environments. The finding that the model generalizes to traditional walking-based ReID (MEVID) suggests the disentanglement principle is broadly applicable beyond activity-specific settings. Open questions include how the approach scales to open-world activity vocabularies, whether the VLM-generated prompts remain reliable under significant domain shift, and whether the adaptive motion weighting can be further refined for activities with highly variable motion signatures.
