## Problem

Existing Partially Relevant Video Retrieval (PRVR) methods operate entirely in Euclidean space, whose flat geometry distorts the intrinsic hierarchical structure of untrimmed videos—embeddings with distant hierarchical relations can be spatially close, making it difficult to disentangle informative moments from background. This geometric limitation leads to suboptimal temporal modeling and weakens cross-modal matching under partial relevance. The paper introduces the first hyperbolic modeling framework for PRVR to compensate for these Euclidean shortcomings.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language text query (encoded via RoBERTa into a d-dimensional sentence embedding) and an untrimmed video (encoded via pre-trained I3D or ResNet152+I3D features) as input, with no moment timestamps available. The video is processed through two parallel branches: a gaze branch that densely samples M_f frames for fine-grained relevance and a glance branch that sparsely samples M_c clips via mean pooling for coarser-grained moment perception. The output is a ranked list of videos scored by a weighted combination of frame-level and clip-level max cosine similarities between the query embedding and the respective video embeddings.

## Method

HLFormer's core is a dual-branch HLFormer Block that processes video embeddings in both hyperbolic and Euclidean spaces. The Lorentz Attention Block projects Euclidean features onto the Lorentz model via the exponential map, then applies Lorentz linear layers and a Lorentz self-attention mechanism with Gaussian distance-based scoring to capture multi-scale hierarchical structure; the Euclidean Attention Block uses standard Gaussian attention for fine-grained local features. A Mean-Guided Adaptive Interaction Module (MAIM) dynamically fuses outputs from N_L parallel Lorentz blocks and N_E Euclidean blocks by computing per-timestep aggregation weights from a globally pooled query via cross-attention. For cross-modal matching, a Partial Order Preservation (POP) loss geometrically confines the text embedding within a hyperbolic entailment cone anchored by the video embedding in an auxiliary Lorentzian manifold, explicitly enforcing the 'text ≺ video' semantic hierarchy. Training combines a standard similarity retrieval loss, a query diversity loss, and the POP loss with learnable balancing coefficients.

## Results

HLFormer achieves state-of-the-art PRVR performance across all three benchmarks: SumR of 154.9 on ActivityNet Captions, 78.7 on Charades-STA, and 187.7 on TVR. On ActivityNet Captions, it attains R@1=8.7%, R@5=27.1%, R@10=40.1%, R@100=79.0%; on TVR, R@1=15.7%, R@5=37.1%, R@10=48.5%, R@100=86.4%. The method outperforms the strongest PRVR baseline DL-DKD by 4.9% SumR on ActivityNet Captions and 4.3% on TVR, and exceeds PEAN by 5.4% SumR on Charades-STA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HLFormer consistently outperforms all six PRVR baselines across all three datasets. Against the strongest PRVR competitor DL-DKD, it gains +4.9% SumR on ActivityNet Captions (154.9 vs. 147.6) and +4.3% on TVR (187.7 vs. 179.9); on Charades-STA where DL-DKD reports no results, HLFormer beats the next-best PEAN by +5.4% SumR (78.7 vs. 74.7). Compared to GMMFormer, the gains are +8.9% SumR on ActivityNet, +5.8% on Charades-STA, and +11.1% on TVR. Against the original PRVR baseline MS-SL, improvements reach +14.8% (ActivityNet), +10.3% (Charades-STA), and +15.3% (TVR) in SumR. HLFormer also surpasses the best T2VR method (Cap4Video) and best VCMR method (JSG) by substantial margins, confirming that task-specific PRVR modeling with hyperbolic geometry yields superior retrieval over general-purpose video-text or moment-retrieval approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PRVR task established by MS-SL [14] and the Gaussian attention mechanism from GMMFormer [57] by introducing a hyperbolic (Lorentz) branch alongside the existing Euclidean branch, drawing the Lorentz linear layer design from Fully Hyperbolic Neural Networks [6]. It departs from all prior PRVR methods (MS-SL, GMMFormer, DL-DKD, PEAN, BGM-Net), which operate exclusively in Euclidean space, by arguing that the flat geometry distorts the intrinsic frame→moment→video hierarchy of untrimmed videos. The Partial Order Preservation Loss is inspired by the entailment-cone idea in MERU [10] for image-text retrieval but is adapted here to enforce a 'text ≺ video' partial order specific to the PRVR setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first hyperbolic framework for PRVR, this work opens a direction for leveraging non-Euclidean geometry in long-video understanding, and the hybrid Euclidean–Lorentz design offers a practical template for combining hierarchical and fine-grained representations. However, the approach relies on pre-extracted I3D/RoBERTa features and is validated on only three mid-scale datasets, leaving open whether the hyperbolic advantage persists at longer video durations or with end-to-end learned backbones. The modest ablation gain from the POP loss (~1% SumR) suggests the geometric constraint, while conceptually clean, may not be the primary driver of the overall improvement, raising questions about its transferability to other hierarchical retrieval tasks.
