## Problem

Adapting foundation models for downstream tasks typically requires either sharing the model with data owners or sharing data with the model owner, but in cross-silo settings both parties have strict privacy constraints: the LSP must retain ownership of the FM, and clients must not reveal their data to the LSP or to each other. No existing framework enables collaborative FM adaptation under this 'double-blind' constraint while remaining computationally feasible.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (384×384 for ViT-Base) as input, which are FHE-encrypted by the client before transmission to the server. The server processes encrypted intermediate representations (577×768 tensors per transformer block) and returns them block-by-block for client-side decryption and re-encryption. The final output is a class label prediction, with the client computing the loss locally from encrypted predictions and the server's encrypted average loss.

## Method

BlindFed redesigns a ViT-Base into an FHE-friendly model by replacing Softmax, GELU, and LayerNorm division with low-degree polynomial approximations (Taylor series for exp, quadratic for GELU, Goldschmidt's algorithm for inverse) and appending a low-rank parallel adapter (LoSA) that avoids backpropagation through the backbone. Training proceeds in two stages: offline knowledge distillation from the original FM to the approximated blocks on an auxiliary dataset, followed by online encrypted inference where the server evaluates one transformer block at a time (the client decrypts and re-encrypts between blocks to manage multiplicative depth). For model privacy, the server applies per-block sample permutations and stochastic block sampling (returning only a subset of block outputs with no two consecutive blocks sampled) to prevent a client from reconstructing individual block parameters from intermediate representations.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (centralized), BlindFed achieves 94.28% balanced accuracy, compared to 95.92% for LoRA and 96.35% for full fine-tuning, while outperforming the only other double-blind baseline (linear probing) by 2.02 points. On CIFAR-100, BlindFed reaches 79.30% vs. 83.49% for LoRA and 74.76% for linear probing. On SVHN, BlindFed achieves 92.32%, substantially outperforming linear probing (59.38%) and adapter tuning (52.01%), though trailing LoRA (96.59%) and full fine-tuning (96.80%). Under federated settings with extreme heterogeneity (Dirichlet α=0.01), BlindFed maintains 85.40% on CIFAR-10, compared to 89.79% for LoRA and 74.47% for linear probing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BlindFed is compared against LoRA (sequential low-rank adapter) and adapter tuning (sequential bottleneck adapter), both of which are NOT double-blind because they require backpropagation through the FM. On CIFAR-10 centralized, BlindFed (94.28%) trails LoRA (95.92%) by 1.64 points but substantially outperforms adapter tuning (89.92%) by 4.36 points. On CIFAR-100, the gap to LoRA widens to 4.19 points (79.30% vs. 83.49%), while BlindFed still beats adapter tuning by 13.36 points. On SVHN, BlindFed (92.32%) is 4.27 points below LoRA (96.59%) but 40.31 points above adapter tuning (52.01%). Among double-blind methods, BlindFed consistently outperforms linear probing by 2.0–33.0 points across datasets, with the largest margin on SVHN (92.32% vs. 59.38%), confirming that the parallel adapter design captures task-specific features that a single linear head cannot.

## Relation to Prior Work

BlindFed sits at the intersection of three established research threads: FHE-based private inference (polynomial approximations for Softmax, GELU, and LayerNorm via Goldschmidt's algorithm), parameter-efficient fine-tuning via parallel adapters (specifically LoSA from Mercea et al., 2024), and federated learning with secure aggregation (MPC-based FedAvg). The paper's conceptual contribution is assembling these components into a unified two-stage split-learning protocol—offline knowledge distillation (following TinyBERT's schedule) followed by online encrypted inference—where the client never backpropagates through the FM and the server never sees plaintext data. This departs from prior private inference work (which typically protects a single user's query) and prior federated PEFT work (which assumes the model is shared with clients) by enforcing both data and model privacy simultaneously in a multi-client cross-silo setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BlindFed is the first framework to demonstrate practical feasibility of double-blind federated FM adaptation, addressing a real deployment barrier in healthcare, finance, and other regulated domains where neither the model owner nor the data owners can trust each other. However, the framework carries substantial practical limitations: the per-block decrypt-re-encrypt loop incurs high communication overhead (≈2.8× FHE expansion per intermediate representation), the server-side encrypted inference is computationally expensive, and the privacy guarantees rely on heuristic mechanisms (permutation + SBS) rather than formal cryptographic proofs. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger FMs (ViT-Huge, 22B+ parameters), whether stronger formal model-privacy guarantees can be achieved, and how the framework behaves under malicious (rather than semi-honest) collaborators.
