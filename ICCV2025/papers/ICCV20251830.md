## Problem

Existing long-video understanding methods operate at the frame level, limiting them to short clips (≤64 frames) due to quadratic attention complexity and prohibitive GPU memory requirements for longer sequences. Additionally, annotated long-video datasets are scarce, making supervised pre-training on extended content impractical. This paper asks whether long-range temporal dependencies across minutes-to-hours of video can be learned efficiently and in a self-supervised manner without being constrained by the number of input frames.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251830/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a long video (20+ minutes) and segments it into 5-second clips, each of which is encoded by a frozen multimodal model (LanguageBind or InternVideo2) into a single d-dimensional embedding vector. LV-MAE then operates on this sequence of embedding tokens (capped at 256 via padding), so a 2-minute video requires only 24 tokens versus ~11,760 for frame-level approaches. The output is a high-level long-video representation (the encoder's latent Z), which is probed with a single transformer layer (attentive probing) or a linear head to yield class labels or regression scores for downstream tasks.

## Method

LV-MAE decouples short-span and long-span modeling: off-the-shelf multimodal encoders capture within-segment semantics, while a self-supervised masked-embedding autoencoder learns cross-segment dependencies. The architecture follows MAE's asymmetric encoder-decoder design but operates on sequences of embedding vectors rather than image patches—masking 40–50% of tokens, encoding the visible subset, and reconstructing masked positions via shared learnable mask tokens and positional encodings, trained with MSE loss. A semantic masking strategy (masking embeddings with lowest cosine similarity to their predecessor) is proposed to target more salient transitions. Pre-training uses dynamic sampling over 1,000+ movies/TV series plus FineVideo, MovieClips, and ActivityNet, requiring only 20 hours on 8×A10 GPUs. The key distinction from prior MAE variants is the granularity shift from frame-level patches to clip-level embeddings, which eliminates the frame-count bottleneck and enables processing of arbitrarily long videos.

## Results

On the LVU benchmark, LV-MAE with LanguageBind embeddings achieves 63.4% average top-1 accuracy across classification tasks, outperforming the previous best (VideoMamba at 58.1%) by 5.3 percentage points, with particularly large gains on Director (77.57% vs. 67.29%) and Writer (64.28% vs. 52.98%). On COIN, LV-MAE with InternVideo2 reaches 92.72% top-1 accuracy, surpassing VideoMamba (90.4%) and Turbo (87.5%). On Breakfast, LV-MAE with InternVideo2 achieves 93.24%, placing second behind VideoMamba (96.9%) but ahead of Turbo (91.3%) and ViS4mer (88.2%). Notably, LV-MAE uses a frozen backbone with only attentive or linear probing, whereas all compared methods fine-tune their entire model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LV-MAE with LanguageBind outperforms all full-fine-tuning baselines on the LVU benchmark, achieving 63.4% average accuracy versus VideoMamba's 58.1% (+5.3%) and ViS4mer's 53.7% (+9.7%), while using only a frozen backbone with attentive probing. On COIN, LV-MAE with InternVideo2 (92.72%) beats VideoMamba (90.4%), Turbo (87.5%), and ViS4mer (88.4%), establishing a new state of the art. On Breakfast, VideoMamba (96.9%) retains the top spot, with LV-MAE InternVideo2 (93.24%) in second place, ahead of Turbo (91.3%) and ViS4mer (88.2%). Compared to raw-embedding baselines (LanguageBind-Baseline at 38.9% avg, InternVideo2-Baseline at 32.9% avg on LVU), the LV-MAE pre-training yields dramatic improvements—e.g., Writer accuracy jumps from 5.95% to 64.28% with LanguageBind—demonstrating that the masked-embedding autoencoder captures high-level temporal context beyond what individual clip embeddings provide. The paper also shows that end-to-end training of transformer or Mamba models directly on LanguageBind embeddings (56.8% avg) underperforms the self-supervised LV-MAE approach (63.4%), validating the MAE pre-training strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

LV-MAE adapts the asymmetric encoder-decoder architecture of MAE (He et al., CVPR 2022) from image patches to sequences of clip-level embedding vectors, and borrows BERT's padding and attention-masking strategies for variable-length inputs. It departs from frame-level video masked autoencoders (VideoMAE, VideoMAE V2) and SSM-based long-video models (ViS4mer, VideoMamba) by decoupling low-level representation extraction (delegated to frozen off-the-shelf multimodal encoders like LanguageBind or InternVideo2) from long-range dependency modeling, operating on one token per five-second segment rather than per-frame patches. The authors claim this is the first application of masked autoencoders to sequences of embedding vectors rather than spatio-temporal image patches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic token reduction (24 vs. 11,760 for a two-minute video) makes self-supervised pre-training on 20+ minute videos computationally feasible on modest hardware (20 hours on 8×A10 GPUs), lowering the barrier for long-video research. A key limitation is the dependence on frozen off-the-shelf encoders: if the short-video model misses content, that deficiency propagates into the long-range representation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to hour-long or multi-hour content, whether jointly training the short-video encoder would further improve quality, and whether the embedding-space reconstruction objective captures all relevant long-range structure or merely surface-level semantic similarity.
