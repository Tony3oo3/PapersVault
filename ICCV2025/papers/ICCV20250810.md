## Problem

Existing self-supervised 3D occupancy estimation methods rely on ground truth 6D ego pose from sensors to compute multiview photometric losses at real-world scale, limiting their generality. Additionally, volume rendering for 3D voxel representation learning requires dense sampling (e.g., ~108M points for a 180×320 image), making it computationally expensive and less effective than necessary. The paper asks whether Gaussian splatting can simultaneously solve both the scale-aware training problem and the rendering efficiency problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250810/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 6 surround-view RGB images, known camera extrinsics, and 2D pseudo semantic maps generated by open-vocabulary segmentation models (no ground truth occupancy labels or GT 6D pose required). It outputs a 300×300×24 3D semantic occupancy grid, per-view depth maps, and a learned 6D ego pose. The 2D-to-3D lifting produces a voxel grid at 512×512×32 resolution (upsampled from 256×256×16), and the perception range is 80 m on nuScenes and 200 m on DDAD.

## Method

GaussianOcc is a two-stage fully self-supervised pipeline. In Stage 1, a U-Net (NewCRFs/Swin Transformer) predicts per-pixel Gaussian attributes (depth, scale, rotation) in 2D image space; cross-view Gaussian splatting between adjacent cameras—using an overlap mask that masks out one side of each overlap region—produces a rendered image whose photometric loss provides scale information, enabling joint training with a 6D pose network without any GT pose. In Stage 2, 2D features are lifted into a 3D voxel grid where each vertex is treated as a 3D Gaussian with fixed position, scale, and rotation; only semantic and opacity attributes are optimized, and the scene is rendered via fast Gaussian splatting rather than dense volume rendering. This voxel-Gaussian formulation reduces rendering time by ~5× and training time by ~2.7× compared to volume rendering, while achieving competitive mIoU (11.26 on Occ3D) without GT occupancy or GT pose.

## Results

On the Occ3D benchmark (nuScenes), GaussianOcc achieves 11.26 mIoU* (9.94 mIoU) for 3D occupancy estimation without requiring ground truth occupancy labels or ground truth 6D ego pose, outperforming the previous best self-supervised methods OccNeRF (10.81 mIoU*) and SelfOcc (10.54 mIoU*). For depth estimation in Stage 1, GaussianOcc achieves 0.258 Abs Rel on nuScenes and 0.212 on DDAD without GT pose, while in Stage 2 the rendered depth reaches 0.197 Abs Rel on nuScenes, surpassing OccNeRF (0.202) and SelfOcc (0.215) which rely on GT pose. The method is also 2.7× faster in training and 5× faster in rendering compared to volume-rendering-based approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among self-supervised 3D occupancy methods (no GT occupancy labels), GaussianOcc (11.26 mIoU*) outperforms OccNeRF (10.81), SelfOcc (10.54), and SimpleOcc (7.99) while being the only method that also eliminates the need for GT 6D ego pose. RenderOcc (24.53 mIoU*) substantially outperforms GaussianOcc but is weakly supervised, relying on GT depth and semantic maps rather than being fully self-supervised. For depth estimation on nuScenes, GaussianOcc† in Stage 2 (0.197 Abs Rel) beats both OccNeRF (0.202) and SelfOcc (0.215), which use GT pose, while GaussianOcc‡ in Stage 1 (0.258) is competitive with SurroundDepth (0.280) and VFF (0.289) that also avoid GT pose. On DDAD, GaussianOcc‡ (0.212 Abs Rel) is competitive with SurroundDepth (0.208) and SA-FSM (0.187), though R3D3 (0.162) remains stronger as it uses multi-frame offline optimization. The learned 6D pose from Stage 1 yields depth results (0.211 Abs Rel) close to GT pose (0.214) in Table 4, confirming the pose network's quality, and the two-stage training is essential—single-stage training collapses to 0.946 Abs Rel due to local optimization in voxel space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) in two novel ways for self-supervised 3D occupancy estimation: as a cross-view projection mechanism (GSP) that replaces direct bilinear warping for scale-aware depth training, and as a voxel-space rendering engine (GSV) that replaces dense volume rendering. It builds directly on the self-supervised occupancy paradigm established by SimpleOcc, OccNeRF, and SelfOcc, which all rely on volume rendering and require ground-truth 6D ego pose; GaussianOcc eliminates the pose dependency by learning scale through adjacent-view Gaussian splatting in a joint training loop with a 6D pose network. The depth backbone (NewCRFs/Swin Transformer) and 2D-to-3D lifting follow prior work, positioning the contribution squarely in the rendering and scale-learning components rather than the feature extraction pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the need for ground-truth 6D ego pose, GaussianOcc makes self-supervised 3D occupancy estimation genuinely end-to-end trainable from surround images and 2D semantic pseudo-labels alone, which is a meaningful step toward deployment in settings where sensor-level pose is unavailable or unreliable. The 5× rendering speedup and resolution-independent cost of splatting rendering versus volume rendering are practically significant for iterative training and high-resolution occupancy grids. However, the mIoU gain over OccNeRF (11.26 vs. 10.81) is modest, the method still depends on open-vocabulary 2D semantic maps, and the two-stage training pipeline adds complexity. Open questions include whether the learned pose generalizes to dynamic or degraded-sensor conditions, and whether the voxel-Gaussian formulation extends naturally to 4D (temporal) occupancy.
