## Problem

Existing streaming video generation methods based on conditional diffusion must solve a full denoising ODE from random noise for every new frame, making them computationally expensive. A direct frame-to-frame ODE flow would be faster but is unstable because it was never trained to recover from accumulated errors. The paper addresses how to combine the efficiency of direct frame-to-frame transport with the robustness of denoising-based error correction in a single unified model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251782/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB frame (128×128, or 64×64 for MINERL/MAZES) as the starting condition and produces the next frame via an ODE solve, enabling arbitrarily long streaming video generation with finite memory. The network is a 2D UNet with no dedicated temporal modules; it receives a 3-channel frame and outputs a 6-channel joint field (3 channels for temporal evolution, 3 for denoising). Generation is unconditional and Markovian—each frame depends only on the immediately preceding one.

## Method

The core contribution is a joint ODE flow field trained via a bilinear flow-matching objective: during training, consecutive frame pairs are linearly interpolated in time (parameter t) and additionally corrupted with Gaussian noise (parameter α), and the network learns to predict both the temporal direction (x₁ − x₀) and the noise direction (n) simultaneously. At inference, a joint sampling pattern follows a characteristic curve in the (t, α) plane from (0, ε) to (1, 0), simultaneously advancing to the next frame and removing accumulated drift in a single ODE solve. This replaces the expensive noise-to-frame diffusion trajectory with a short frame-to-frame path while the denoising branch acts as a built-in corrector. The approach uses a 2D UNet with an adaptive Heun solver, requiring roughly half the steps of a conditional diffusion baseline while achieving comparable or better FVD across six video datasets.

## Results

On six video datasets, Bi-FLOW achieves the best FVD on 5 out of 6 datasets while using roughly half the ODE solver steps of the conditional diffusion baseline (CONDIFF). For example, on SKY, Bi-FLOW reaches FVD 526 with 14.57 steps versus CONDIFF's FVD 1271 with 46.18 steps; on CARLA, Bi-FLOW achieves FVD 345 with 23.51 steps versus CONDIFF's FVD 947 with 53.77 steps. The ablation FLOW (without the bilinear denoising term) is fastest (7–10 steps) but suffers severe error accumulation, yielding FVDs 3–10× higher than Bi-FLOW. CONDIFF wins only on MAZES (FVD 424 vs. 605), where the simpler scene dynamics favor its noise-based generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Bi-FLOW against two baselines: FLOW (the same architecture trained without the bilinear denoising objective) and CONDIFF (a conditional diffusion model that generates each frame from noise conditioned on the previous frame). Bi-FLOW decisively outperforms FLOW on all six datasets in FVD (e.g., 526 vs. 2144 on SKY, 3753 vs. 14010 on BIKING), confirming that the denoising branch is essential for preventing error accumulation during streaming. Against CONDIFF, Bi-FLOW achieves lower FVD on five of six datasets (SKY: 526 vs. 1271; BIKING: 3753 vs. 11465; RIDING: 1552 vs. 4668; CARLA: 345 vs. 947; MINERL: 834 vs. 1149) while requiring roughly 40–55% fewer solver steps (e.g., 14.57 vs. 46.18 on SKY; 23.51 vs. 53.77 on CARLA). CONDIFF wins only on MAZES (FVD 424 vs. 605), a dataset with very simple, low-variation dynamics. Under a fixed 20-step budget (Table 2), Bi-FLOW also consistently outperforms CONDIFF, e.g., FVD 491 vs. 994 on SKY and 307 vs. 829 on CARLA, further validating the efficiency advantage of starting from the previous frame rather than from noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reformulates the flow matching objective (Lipman et al., 2023) and the coupled-transport principle from rectified flow (Liu et al., 2023) to define a neural ODE that maps directly from a previous frame distribution to a next-frame distribution, rather than from noise to a frame. It departs from the dominant conditional-diffusion paradigm (e.g., GameNGen, RIVER, SVP) by eliminating the noise-to-frame denoising step entirely, and from full-sequence diffusion by operating frame-by-frame in a streaming fashion. The bilinear training extension, which jointly learns a temporal-evolution field and a denoising field as a first-order PDE in the (t, α) plane, is the paper's core architectural novelty, drawing on the characteristic-curve method from PDE theory to enable a unified predictor-corrector sampling pattern.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method demonstrates that streaming video generation can be made substantially more efficient (roughly 3–4× fewer ODE solver steps) than conditional diffusion while maintaining comparable FVD, opening a path toward real-time interactive video synthesis (e.g., game engines). However, the Markovian one-frame context is a significant limitation that causes visible artifacts and scene drift over longer horizons, and the manual noise-level hyperparameter ε lacks an adaptive mechanism. Open questions include whether autoregressive multi-frame conditioning can be integrated into the bi-flow framework, whether the approach scales to higher resolutions and conditional (text/image-prompted) generation, and whether the joint PDE formulation generalizes to other correlation dimensions such as multi-view or multi-illumination settings.
