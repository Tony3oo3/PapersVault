## Problem

Existing text-to-video methods cannot simultaneously and independently control the 6D poses of both camera and objects in a 3D-aware manner, primarily due to the absence of datasets providing complete 6D pose annotations for both elements. Image-space trajectory annotations entangle camera and object motions (e.g., a rightward trajectory is ambiguous between a moving object and a moving camera), and prior synthetic datasets are limited to static cameras, human-centric assets, or uncontrollable motion patterns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251302/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts 6D camera poses (rotation and translation per frame), 6D object poses for up to N objects in a global coordinate system, and a text content description. Users can alternatively specify motion by drawing 3D curves or selecting motion types, which are converted to trajectories via a rule-based algorithm. The output is a 16-frame video at 256×384 resolution. Camera poses are encoded via Plücker embeddings, and object poses are consumed relative to the camera with a Gaussian-blurred coarse mask (kernel size approximated from object size and camera distance) rather than requiring precise segmentation.

## Method

FMC introduces two decoupled controllers on top of AnimateDiff V3: a Camera Motion Controller (CMC) that encodes Plücker embeddings of initial and relative camera poses and modulates temporal block features via a Camera Adapter, and an Object Motion Controller (OMC) that encodes 6D object poses (duplicated within each object's region, zero elsewhere) concatenated with a foreground mask, then modulates spatial features from downsampled blocks. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) Domain LoRA adapts spatial blocks to the synthetic rendering style, (2) CMC is trained with a background-weighted camera loss L_cam to isolate camera-driven motion, and (3) OMC is trained with a foreground-weighted object loss L_obj to isolate object-driven motion, with all other parameters frozen. The region-specific losses are the key mechanism for disentangling global camera dynamics from local object dynamics, enabling independent or joint 3D-aware control at inference time.

## Results

FMC achieves the best text–video alignment (CLIPSIM 31.01 vs. 29.33 for AnimateDiff, 29.21 for CameraCtrl, 26.83 for MotionCtrl) and dramatically outperforms MotionCtrl on object motion fidelity (ObjTransErr 42.25 vs. 80.66; ObjRotErr 0.96 vs. 1.77), roughly halving both errors. On camera motion, FMC is on par with CameraCtrl and MotionCtrl (CamTransErr 18.12, CamRotErr 1.03). FMC's FID (133.42) is slightly higher than MotionCtrl's (125.52) but lower than CameraCtrl (137.96) and AnimateDiff (149.61), while its FVD (846.51) is competitive with CameraCtrl (805.25) and well below MotionCtrl (952.31).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AnimateDiff, FMC wins on every reported metric (FID 133.42 vs. 149.61, FVD 846.51 vs. 868.97, CLIPSIM 31.01 vs. 29.33) while adding explicit camera and object pose control that AnimateDiff lacks. Against CameraCtrl, FMC improves CLIPSIM (31.01 vs. 29.21) and FID (133.42 vs. 137.96) and adds full 6D object control; CameraCtrl retains a slight edge on FVD (805.25 vs. 846.51) and CamRotErr (0.94 vs. 1.03). Against MotionCtrl, FMC's most decisive wins are on object motion: ObjTransErr drops from 80.66 to 42.25 (≈48 % reduction) and ObjRotErr from 1.77 to 0.96 (≈46 % reduction), while CLIPSIM improves from 26.83 to 31.01 and FVD from 952.31 to 846.51; MotionCtrl retains a modest FID advantage (125.52 vs. 133.42). In the user study (Table 4), FMC scores highest on quality (0.91), text similarity (0.95), and object motion fidelity (0.98 vs. MotionCtrl's 0.53), confirming the quantitative gains. Direct-a-Video is compared qualitatively: FMC maintains a stationary camera when only object motion is specified and preserves object orientation, whereas Direct-a-Video exhibits camera drift and poor orientation fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on AnimateDiff as its base diffusion architecture and adopts CameraCtrl's Plücker embedding for camera pose representation. It departs from prior motion control methods (MotionCtrl, Direct-a-Video, DragNUWA) that rely on 2D image-space trajectories entangling object and camera dynamics, by introducing a 3D-aware formulation with explicit 6D pose conditioning for both elements. The key conceptual departure is the SynFMC dataset, which provides ground-truth 6D poses for both camera and objects simultaneously—something no prior real or synthetic dataset (360°-Motion, HumanVid-Syn, BEDLAM, SynBody) offers with comparable motion diversity and category breadth. The Domain LoRA mechanism for bridging the synthetic-to-real domain gap is a practical addition not seen in prior camera/object control methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SynFMC is the first dataset to provide complete 6D pose annotations for both camera and objects, filling a critical gap that has prevented 3D-aware disentangled motion control in video generation. The demonstrated ~2× improvement in object motion error (ObjTransErr 42.25 vs. 80.66) and the large user-study gap (0.98 vs. 0.53 object motion score) validate that 3D pose conditioning fundamentally outperforms 2D trajectory approaches. However, the method's ability to handle complex multi-object motions remains limited, and the authors acknowledge the need for better evaluation metrics for object motion. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer videos, more objects, and whether additional input modalities (e.g., reference images) can further improve controllability.
