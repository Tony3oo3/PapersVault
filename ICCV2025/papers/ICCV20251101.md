## Problem

Existing physics-based generative motion editing methods are limited to simple translational dragging, operate on single-view images without multi-view consistency guarantees, and typically require resource-intensive retraining or reliance on specific field representations (NeRF, 3DGS). No training-free, zero-shot approach exists that can handle complex motions such as rotation and stretching while maintaining geometric and appearance consistency across multiple views.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251101/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images at 512×512 resolution along with reconstructed 3D point clouds (from ScanNet200 or via DUSt3R from sparse views) and user-interactive motion priors (object selection via Mask Clustering queries plus a GUI-defined motion mode and parameters). It produces multi-view motion-edited images and the corresponding multi-view optical flows, all generated through a single-GPU inference pass using a frozen SD-1.5 model with 500 DDIM steps and classifier-free guidance scale 7.5.

## Method

MotionDiff is a two-stage training-free inference pipeline. In the Multi-view Flow Estimation Stage (MFES), a proposed Point Kinematic Model (PKM) estimates post-motion 3D points for four motion modes (translation, scaling, rotation, stretching) by projecting user-defined single-view optical flows into 3D space, then computes multi-view optical flows via camera projection. In the Multi-view Motion Diffusion Stage (MMDS), the core insight is to decouple the motion process into static background, motion objects, and occluded regions, each handled by a dedicated strategy: DDIM inversion preserves the static background; a Flow Guidance Strategy (FGS) uses RAFT-predicted flow loss and warp-based color loss as classifier-free guidance gradients; a Latent Space Fusion (LSF) module inverse-warps the input, encodes it through the VAE, adds schedule-matched noise, and fuses it with the predicted latents to preserve texture fidelity; and a Background Grid Constrain (BGC) reshapes the 4×4 latent grid into a single 128×128 tensor before U-Net denoising to enforce cross-view consistency of the occluded region without extra computation.

## Results

On ScanNet200, MotionDiff achieves the best performance across all three metrics (MPA↓, ATF↓, MVC↑) for all four motion modes. For translation, MotionDiff attains MPA 10.8, ATF 4.6, and MVC 34.38, substantially outperforming Motion Guidance (59.5/25.1/29.31) and DiffEditor (14.7/10.5/33.51). For stretching, the gains are most pronounced: MPA 3.1 and ATF 3.4 versus MagicFixup's 6.2/7.0, while MVC reaches 38.62 (vs. 37.78 for MagicFixup). Across all modes, MotionDiff reduces MPA by 2–5× and ATF by 2–5× compared to the strongest baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MotionDiff outperforms all three baselines on every metric across all four motion modes. Against Motion Guidance [12], the largest gaps appear in MPA (e.g., 10.8 vs. 59.5 for translation; 6.7 vs. 53.0 for scaling) and ATF (4.6 vs. 25.1 for translation), reflecting MG's inability to preserve texture and accurately follow complex motions. Against DiffEditor [26], MotionDiff improves MPA by roughly 30–60% (e.g., 10.8 vs. 14.7 for translation, 3.1 vs. 7.4 for stretching) and MVC by 1–3 dB (e.g., 30.02 vs. 27.45 for rotation), showing that drag-based editing degrades under rotation and multi-view settings. Against MagicFixup [2], which is the closest competitor, MotionDiff still leads on MPA (3.1 vs. 6.2 for stretching; 10.8 vs. 26.5 for translation) and MVC (38.62 vs. 37.78 for stretching; 34.38 vs. 33.24 for translation), with the advantage attributed to the Background Grid Constrain enforcing cross-view consistency. The ablation (Table 3) further confirms that removing BGC drops MVC from 34.92 to 30.22, while removing LSF degrades ATF from 5.1 to 5.3 and MVC from 34.92 to 31.79, validating each component's contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

MotionDiff extends the flow-guided diffusion editing paradigm introduced by Motion Guidance (Geng & Owens, ICLR 2024), which first demonstrated that optical flow can serve as a differentiable motion prior for diffusion-based image editing. However, Motion Guidance operates on single-view images and lacks texture preservation for moving objects. MotionDiff departs from this by (1) introducing a Point Kinematic Model (PKM) to synthesize multi-view optical flows from static 3D point clouds without any motion data, (2) decoupling the motion process into static background, motion object, and occluded region for targeted guidance, and (3) adding a Background Grid Constrain that reshapes the latent tensor to enforce cross-view consistency at zero additional compute. The classifier-guidance inference framework of Dhariwal & Nichol (NeurIPS 2021) underpins the Flow Guidance Strategy, while DDIM inversion and RAFT-based flow prediction are repurposed from their original contexts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, zero-shot nature of MotionDiff removes the retraining bottleneck that plagues prior physics-based editing methods (e.g., DiffEditor, MagicFixup), making complex multi-view motion editing accessible without paired datasets or NeRF/3DGS representations. The decoupled motion representation and PKM are modular components that could be repurposed for AR, VR, and HCI applications where interactive scene manipulation is needed. However, PKM is currently limited to four rigid/affine motion modes (translation, scaling, rotation, stretching), and the authors acknowledge that generalizing to arbitrary deformations requires new kinematic models. An open question is whether the grid-based multi-view constraint (BGC) generalizes beyond the 4×4 view configuration tested here, and whether the approach scales to larger view counts or higher resolutions without degrading consistency.
