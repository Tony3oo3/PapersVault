## Problem

Existing MonoSSC methods rely on CNNs whose fixed-weight encoding and local receptive fields cannot adapt to the non-uniform distribution of projected voxel centers caused by perspective projection, nor can they capture the long-range 3D dependencies needed to recover information lost during 3D-to-2D unprojection. This limits both 2D feature quality and 3D geometric reconstruction, particularly on object classes with complex structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250759/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular RGB image and outputs a 3D semantic occupancy volume of shape N_c × H × W × L, where N_c is the number of semantic classes (13 for NYUv2, 12 for Occ-ScanNet). Evaluation is performed at downsampled resolutions for computational efficiency: 60×36×60 for NYUv2 (from original 240×144×240) and 60×60×36 for Occ-ScanNet. The geometric decoder additionally produces a 2D depth map that is converted to 3D occupancy for auxiliary supervision.

## Method

GA-MonoSSC is a hybrid Transformer–Mamba architecture with two key components. The Dual-Head Multi-Modality Encoder uses a Transformer backbone to extract multi-scale 2D tokens, then decodes them into separate geometric and semantic feature maps via two parallel decoders (with geometry injected into the semantic path via elementwise addition), enforcing 3D occupancy supervision on the geometric head to preserve 3D spatial awareness. The Frustum Mamba Decoder unprojects both feature sets into 3D voxel space via FLoSP, fuses them through channel-attention-based multi-scale weighting and elementwise addition, then processes the unified volume with a 3D U-Net whose encoder stages employ Frustum Mamba Layers. The critical design choice is the Frustum Reordering Strategy: instead of flattening voxels by 3D coordinates (which introduces discontinuities from the 2D-to-3D unprojection), voxels are reordered along frustum rays where features are more structured along the image plane and highly similar along depth, yielding a more continuous sequence that aligns with Mamba's sequential scan mechanism.

## Results

On NYUv2, GA-MonoSSC achieves 47.51 IoU and 32.32 mIoU, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art ISO (47.11 IoU, 31.25 mIoU) by +0.4 IoU and +1.07 mIoU. On the larger Occ-ScanNet dataset, the improvement is substantially larger: GA-MonoSSC reaches 48.59 IoU and 35.65 mIoU versus ISO's 42.16 IoU and 28.71 mIoU, a gain of +6.43 IoU and +6.94 mIoU. The method also outperforms all other baselines (MonoScene, NDC-Scene, 3DSketch, AICNet, LMSCNet) on both datasets across both metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GA-MonoSSC outperforms the previous state-of-the-art ISO on both datasets: +0.4 IoU / +1.07 mIoU on NYUv2 and +6.43 IoU / +6.94 mIoU on Occ-ScanNet. The gap widens dramatically on Occ-ScanNet (nearly 60× more training samples), which the authors attribute to the global-context modeling of Transformer + Mamba scaling better than CNN-based architectures on large datasets. On NYUv2, GA-MonoSSC wins on all object classes (chair, bed, sofa, table, furniture, objects) but slightly trails ISO on the three 'stuff' classes (ceiling, floor, wall), which have simple planar geometry well-suited to local CNN receptive fields. Against earlier monocular methods, the gains are larger: NDC-Scene is beaten by +3.34 IoU / +3.29 mIoU on NYUv2, and MonoScene by +5.0 IoU / +5.38 mIoU on NYUv2 and +6.99 IoU / +11.03 mIoU on Occ-ScanNet. Methods relying on auxiliary 3D inputs (LMSCNet with occupancy, AICNet with depth, 3DSketch with TSDF) all underperform the monocular-only GA-MonoSSC, suggesting that richer 3D supervision does not compensate for weaker architectural capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MonoSSC lineage initiated by MonoScene (Cao & De Charette, CVPR 2022), which established the FLoSP unprojection and 3D U-Net decoder framework, and the subsequent refinements by NDC-Scene and ISO that improved depth-aware unprojection. The key conceptual departure is replacing the CNN-based 2D encoder and 3D decoder with a Transformer (for global 2D context) and a Mamba/SSM-based decoder (for linear-complexity 3D long-range modeling), motivated by the non-uniform distribution of projected voxel centers under perspective projection. The Frustum Reordering Strategy is a novel contribution specific to adapting SSMs to 3D voxel data, addressing discontinuities introduced by the 2D-to-3D unprojection that prior 3D SSM works (e.g., Hilbert-curve or octree ordering for point clouds) did not encounter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~15% IoU and ~24% mIoU gains on Occ-ScanNet over the previous SOTA (ISO) are substantial and suggest that global context modeling is a critical bottleneck for MonoSSC that CNN-based methods fundamentally cannot resolve, especially at scale. The frustum reordering strategy is a practical, transferable insight for any application of SSMs to 3D voxel data, potentially benefiting occupancy prediction and other 3D completion tasks. Limitations include evaluation restricted to indoor scenes and the increased computational cost of the hybrid Transformer+Mamba architecture relative to pure CNN baselines; whether this approach scales to outdoor autonomous-driving scenarios with much larger voxel grids remains an open question.
