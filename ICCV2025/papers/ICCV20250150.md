## Problem

While diffusion model features have been shown to be effective for discriminative tasks like classification and segmentation, there is no mechanistic understanding of how visual semantic information is organized across layers, denoising timesteps, and architectures. Prior interpretability efforts (attention maps, PCA) operate on a per-image basis and fail to provide a holistic, dataset-level account of what each layer actually encodes, leaving it unclear which features to select for transfer learning or why certain design choices affect representational quality.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes RGB images (randomly cropped and resized to 512×512) and passes them through a pretrained diffusion model's VAE encoder to obtain latent representations, then extracts spatially-pooled activations (d=1280) from specific U-Net layers (bottleneck, up_ft0, up_ft1, up_ft2) or DiT blocks at chosen denoising timesteps, conditioned on an empty prompt by default. The k-SAE produces sparse monosemantic neuron activations (top-32 of an expanded hidden layer), while Diff-C outputs class probabilities for fine-grained or coarse-grained classification, and the LLaVA integration produces free-form text responses for visual reasoning benchmarks.

## Method

The paper employs two complementary tools: (1) k-sparse autoencoders (k-SAE) trained with a TopK(k=32) activation and MSE reconstruction loss on spatially-pooled diffusion activations, which decompose high-dimensional layer features into interpretable monosemantic visual concepts (e.g., specific dog breeds, grass textures, camera angles); and (2) Diff-C, a lightweight 4-convolutional-layer classifier (40M parameters) that directly maps 2D diffusion feature maps to class labels without distillation, hypernetworks, or synthetic data. The key empirical finding is that representation granularity oscillates non-linearly with model depth—bottleneck captures coarse spatial layout, up_ft1 captures fine-grained class-specific details, and up_ft2 reverts to high-frequency texture—shifting depending on task granularity, denoising timestep (t=25 optimal for fine-grained, t=200 for coarse), and architecture (U-Net preserves spatial inductive biases absent in DiT). This mechanistic interpretation directly informs feature selection, yielding 4-orders-of-magnitude inference speedup over prior diffusion-based classifiers while matching or exceeding their accuracy.

## Results

On Oxford-IIIT Pet, Diff-C (using SD 1.5 up_ft1 features with an empty prompt) achieves 88.69% top-1 accuracy, and on FGVC-Aircraft it reaches 65.07%, outperforming the prior best diffusion-feature classifier [33] by +1.39% and +39.03% respectively. With a CLIP-inferred prompt, Diff-C further improves to 90.97% on Oxford-IIIT Pet. In the visual reasoning setting (LLaVA-Bench In-the-Wild), interleaving CLIP with SD 1.5 up_ft1 features at t=25 yields a relative score of 59.9, a +3.3% improvement over CLIP alone (56.6). Diff-C also achieves a 4-orders-of-magnitude inference speedup (~0.13 s/sample vs. ~24 s/sample) compared to the prior diffusion classifier.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the prior diffusion-feature classifier of Li et al. [33], Diff-C wins decisively: +1.39% on Oxford-IIIT Pet (88.69 vs. 87.3) and a massive +39.03% on FGVC-Aircraft (65.07 vs. 26.04), while using only 40M parameters versus 900M and running ~185× faster at inference. Compared to the SD-2.0 features baseline (520M ResNet-like architecture) from the same paper, Diff-C gains +15.07% on Pet and +29.87% on Aircraft. Against zero-shot CLIP ResNet-50 [49], Diff-C leads by +3.29% on Pet and +45.77% on Aircraft, though OpenCLIP ViT-H/14 [14] still edges out Diff-C on Pet (94.39 vs. 88.69) while Diff-C substantially outperforms it on the harder FGVC-Aircraft task (65.07 vs. 42.75). In the visual reasoning comparison, CLIP+SD 1.5 up_ft1 (59.9) beats CLIP+DINOv2 (47.0) by +12.9 relative score on LLaVA-Bench, indicating diffusion features complement CLIP more effectively than DINOv2. Across architectures, SD 1.5 up_ft1 and DiT block 14 tie at 88.61% on Pet, while both outperform SD 2.1 (84.74%) and pixel-space DeepFloyd-IF (84.08%), suggesting latent-space U-Net and mid-transformer blocks are the most transferable representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mechanistic interpretability toolkit from LLMs (sparse autoencoders for monosemantic feature discovery [11, 16, 23]) and CLIP vision features [17, 22] to the visual internals of diffusion models, applying k-SAEs systematically across layers, timesteps, and architectures. It departs from prior diffusion interpretability efforts (Plug-and-Play PCA [63], per-image attention analysis [64, 37, 45]) by operating at the dataset level to recover interpretable monosemantic features rather than visualizing individual image activations. For the transfer-learning side, it builds on the observation that diffusion features are useful for discriminative tasks [33, 68, 36] but replaces expensive distillation or hypernetwork training with a lightweight convolutional classifier (Diff-C), and adopts the multi-encoder interleaving strategy from [62] within the LLaVA framework [34] for visual reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic, architecture-agnostic mechanistic interpretation of diffusion model internals, providing practitioners with concrete guidance on which layer and timestep to extract features for a given task granularity (fine-grained vs. coarse). The finding that a 40M-parameter classifier on a single diffusion layer outperforms a 520M-parameter pipeline by 15–30% while being 4 orders of magnitude faster has immediate practical value for representation learning. Open questions remain: why text conditioning sometimes degrades performance (particularly on domain-specific datasets like FGVC-Aircraft), why SD 1.5 encodes more class-specific information than the larger SD 2.1, and how to leverage the identified monosemantic features for controllable editing or dense prediction tasks.
