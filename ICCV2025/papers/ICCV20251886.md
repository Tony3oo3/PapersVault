## Problem

Deterministic centerline graph learning methods (e.g., CGNet) struggle with occluded or invisible lane centerlines and ambiguous visual cues in complex driving scenes. While generative models offer probabilistic reasoning to handle such ambiguity, prior work (PolyDiffuse) directly generates vectorized outputs, introducing complexity in predetermining output counts and relying on external pre-trained models for initialization. No prior work has applied diffusion models to generate lane centerline priors at the BEV feature level for topology-aware graph learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes surround-view RGB images (6 cameras on nuScenes, 7 cameras on Argoverse2) and produces a centerline graph consisting of non-overlapping vectorized centerline segments (each an ordered set of 2D points) and an adjacency matrix encoding topological connectivity. Internally, the pipeline operates on BEV features of shape 256×200×100 over a perception range of ±15 m (X) and ±30 m (Y), and both a segment-level graph G=(V,E) and a fine-grained point-level graph are formulated to support different evaluation metrics. During inference, the diffusion sampling is executed three times and the generated features are averaged for stability.

## Method

LaneDiffusion introduces two modules: a Lane Prior Injection Module (LPIM) that encodes lane GT centerlines via sinusoidal positional embeddings and a 6-layer transformer encoder, then cross-attends the encoded priors into intermediate BEV features (SMERF-style) to construct the diffusion target; and a Lane Prior Diffusion Module (LPDM) that models this target using a DDPM with a ResShift-inspired residual-shifting forward process (transitioning from the prior-injected feature x₀ toward the original BEV feature x_c rather than to Gaussian noise), reducing required sampling steps to T=15. The denoising network is a Swin Transformer UNet trained to predict x₀ from noisy x_t conditioned on x_c, with the ELBO simplified to a weighted L2 loss. A three-stage training procedure first optimizes LPIM with CGNet's lane losses, then freezes LPIM to train LPDM, and finally freezes LPDM to train a standard lane decoder. A lane prior refinement encoder-decoder fuses the generated and original BEV features before decoding, and the entire pipeline is designed as a plug-in add-on to existing BEV-based architectures.

## Results

On nuScenes, LaneDiffusion achieves 58.9 GEO F1, 46.8 TOPO F1, 38.8 JTOPO F1, 37.1 APLS, and 10.6 SDA on point-level metrics, and 58.6 IoU, 41.6 mAPcf, 28.8 DET_l, and 3.4 TOP_ll on segment-level metrics. These represent improvements of 4.2%, 4.6%, 4.7%, 6.4%, and 1.8% (point-level) and 2.3%, 6.4%, 6.8%, and 2.1% (segment-level) over the CGNet baseline. On Argoverse2, LaneDiffusion achieves 47.0 TOPO F1, 36.4 JTOPO F1, 25.8 APLS, 14.4 SDA, and 1.8 TOP_ll, outperforming CGNet by 2.5%, 1.8%, 2.2%, 0.7%, and 1.3% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaneDiffusion outperforms all six baselines across every reported metric on both nuScenes and Argoverse2. Against the primary baseline CGNet (ECCV 2024), the gains are most pronounced on APLS (+6.4%) and DET_l (+6.8%), indicating that the diffusion-based prior generation particularly benefits lane continuity and segment detection. Compared to MapTR and TopoNet, LaneDiffusion leads by 5.6–6.1 points on TOPO F1 and 10.3–12.2 points on APLS, showing that the generative paradigm captures topological structure more effectively than deterministic graph-based or query-based approaches. Against the earliest baselines STSU and HDMapNet, the margins are even larger (e.g., +25.9 GEO F1 over STSU), reflecting the overall progress in the field. Notably, LaneDiffusion achieves these gains with the same 24-epoch training budget as CGNet, confirming that the improvement stems from the diffusion module rather than additional training compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaneDiffusion positions itself as the first generative (diffusion-based) approach to centerline graph learning, departing from the deterministic paradigm that dominates the field (CGNet, TopoNet, MapTR, VectorMapNet). Conceptually, it draws from ResShift's residual-shifting diffusion to avoid the high sampling cost of standard DDPMs, and from SMERF's cross-attention mechanism for injecting prior knowledge into feature representations. It explicitly contrasts with PolyDiffuse, which generates vectorized map elements directly via diffusion, arguing that operating at the BEV feature level simplifies the problem to an image-restoration-like task and avoids the difficulty of predetermining the number of vectorized outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular, add-on design means LaneDiffusion can be plugged into any existing BEV-based architecture without redesign, lowering the barrier for adoption. The self-contained nature (no external SD maps, satellite imagery, or trajectory data needed at inference) addresses a practical limitation of prior prior-injection methods. However, the approach still requires three stochastic sampling passes at inference for stability, and the authors acknowledge GPU memory constraints that prevented exploration of stronger fusion mechanisms (e.g., cross-attention). Open questions include whether the BEV-level diffusion paradigm generalizes to other vectorized map elements (boundaries, crosswalks) and whether the sampling cost can be further reduced for real-time deployment.
