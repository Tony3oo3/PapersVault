## Problem

Existing FSCIL methods freeze feature extractors and rely on Nearest Class Mean classifiers to prevent catastrophic forgetting, but this rigidity prevents adaptation to new feature distributions, causing spatial overlap between old and new classes. While semi-supervised FSCIL alleviates overfitting from limited labeled data, prior work overlooks the quality of unlabeled sample selection, leading to prototype drift and degraded inter-class separability in later sessions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252519/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB images as input and outputs a class label over an expanding class space. During the base session, the model is trained on a full dataset (e.g., 60 classes on CIFAR-100/miniImageNet, 100 on CUB-200). In each incremental session, the model receives a small labeled set (N-way K-shot, e.g., 5-way 5-shot or 10-way 5-shot) plus a large unlabeled pool (e.g., 350 samples over 35 iterations on CIFAR-100), from which POE selectively filters high-quality samples for self-training. A small exemplar set from previous sessions is retained for distillation.

## Method

Flexi-FSCIL integrates three complementary strategies into a semi-supervised incremental framework. Adaptive Gated Residual Fusion (AGRF) maintains a frozen backbone branch alongside a trainable residual branch, with a per-layer learnable gating parameter and threshold function that selectively fuses old and new parameters, avoiding uniform-weight contamination of low-level features. Attention-Guided Dynamic Hybrid Distillation (ADHD) computes bidirectional cross-attention maps between old and new model intermediate features for soft structural alignment, and conditionally activates a direct L1 feature-matching loss only when the pooled attention similarity falls below an adaptive threshold, preventing over-constraint on new-task learning. Prototype Offset Equilibrium (POE) filters unlabeled samples via two geometric criteria: Feature Affinity requires the sample's embedding to be most similar to its pseudo-labeled class prototype, and Separation Preservation rejects samples whose incorporation would increase mean inter-class prototype similarity beyond a threshold, thereby preventing prototype drift while maintaining class separability.

## Results

On CUB200 (10-way 5-shot, 10 incremental sessions), Flexi-FSCIL achieves a ΔF of +57.77% and a performance degradation (PD) of only 12.97%, outperforming the strongest frozen FSCIL method FACL (ΔF=+56.23%, PD=18.04%) and the best Semi-FSCIL method UaD-CIE (ΔF=+52.25%, PD=14.45%). On CIFAR100, it reaches ΔF=+57.13% and PD=19.76%, and on miniImageNet, ΔF=+58.29% and PD=19.76%. When embedded into existing frozen methods (FACT, SAVC, FACL), Flexi-FSCIL further improves their PD by 1.23%, 0.63%, and 1.20% respectively on CUB200, demonstrating plug-and-play compatibility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Flexi-FSCIL outperforms all 13 baselines on all three datasets in both ΔF and PD. On CUB200, it surpasses the strongest frozen FSCIL method FACL by 1.54% in ΔF and 5.07% in PD, and beats the best Semi-FSCIL method UaD-CIE by 5.52% in ΔF and 1.48% in PD. On CIFAR100, the margin over FACL is 1.58% in ΔF and 8.24% in PD, while over UaD-CIE it is 5.28% in ΔF and 1.29% in PD. On miniImageNet, Flexi-FSCIL leads FACL by 0.21% in ΔF but by a substantial 7.44% in PD (19.76 vs. 27.20), and exceeds UaD-CIE by 9.17% in ΔF and 2.07% in PD. The paper also shows that embedding Flexi-FSCIL into frozen methods (FACT+Flexi, SAVC+Flexi, FACL+Flexi) consistently improves their PD by 1.2–1.23% on CUB200 without sacrificing base-session accuracy, confirming that the AGRF+ADHD+POE pipeline is complementary to existing freezing strategies rather than a replacement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant frozen-feature-extractor + NCM paradigm in FSCIL (FACT, SAVC, FACL) by introducing a trainable residual branch with learnable gating (AGRF), while retaining the frozen backbone for stability. It extends knowledge distillation in incremental learning from output-logit alignment to intermediate feature alignment via bidirectional cross-attention (ADHD), a direction enabled by the trainable components. On the semi-supervised side, it builds on the recently formalized Semi-FSCIL paradigm (SS-iCaRL, Us-KD, UaD-CIE) but introduces a principled geometric sample-selection criterion (POE) that prior semi-supervised FSCIL methods lacked, addressing prototype drift from noisy pseudo-labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the stability-plasticity tradeoff in FSCIL can be partially resolved by selectively unfreezing parameters rather than fully freezing or fully fine-tuning, and that unlabeled data in few-shot incremental settings is only beneficial when filtered by geometric quality criteria. The POE finding that excessive unlabeled data without filtering degrades performance is practically important for practitioners considering semi-supervised augmentation in low-data regimes. Limitations include evaluation only on ResNet-18/20 backbones and three mid-scale datasets, modest ΔF gains over the strongest baselines (≤1.5%), and the added architectural complexity of three interacting components whose hyperparameters (τ, α, β, λ, σ) require careful tuning. Open questions include whether the gating mechanism scales to deeper architectures (ResNet-50/101) and whether POE generalizes to non-prototype classifiers.
