## Problem

Existing trajectory-controllable video generation methods support only a single control signal format (points, optical flow, boxes, or masks), making them inflexible across diverse user scenarios. Additionally, no publicly available large-scale dataset or unified benchmark exists for this task, and prior work neglects the impact of the number of moving objects on generation quality and trajectory accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252669/cropped_page0_idx43.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB input image (first frame) and a set of trajectory maps C ∈ R^{T×H×W×3} in one of three formats: dense segmentation masks, per-frame bounding boxes, or sparse boxes (fewer than 10 annotated frames). The first frame of the trajectory condition is always a segmentation mask to specify which foreground objects should move. The output is a T-frame video where the specified objects animate along the given paths while preserving identity and visual quality, generated via a DiT-based model (CogVideoX-5B or Wan2.1 1.3B) with 3D VAE encoding at T/4 × H/8 × W/8 spatial resolution.

## Method

MagicMotion builds a Trajectory ControlNet—a trainable copy of all DiT blocks—on top of a pretrained DiT-based I2V model, encoding trajectory maps via a 3D VAE and injecting them into the base model through zero-initialized convolution layers, following the ControlNet paradigm. A key design choice is a three-stage progressive training procedure (masks → boxes → sparse boxes) where each stage initializes from the previous, allowing the model to retain dense shape knowledge even when trained with sparse conditions. To further compensate for the shape information lost in sparse trajectories, a Latent Segmentation Loss is introduced: a lightweight Panoptic-FPN-inspired head predicts latent segmentation masks directly from concatenated DiT block features, adding a dense supervision signal (weighted 0.5) during stages 2 and 3 without requiring pixel-space decoding. The model is trained on MagicData (23K videos) curated from Pexels using Llama3.1 for object extraction and GroundedSAM2 for mask/box annotation, with optical-flow-based filtering to ensure meaningful foreground motion.

## Results

On MagicBench, MagicMotion (Stage1-CogVideoX) achieves the best FID of 15.06, FVD of 112.69, MaskIoU of 91.57%, and BoxIoU of 87.75%, substantially outperforming the strongest baselines (Tora: FID 26.27; SG-I2V: FVD 168.82, M_IoU 68.78%). On DAVIS, the same configuration attains MaskIoU 81.33% and BoxIoU 84.97%, exceeding the best prior method (DragNUWA: M_IoU 41.22%, B_IoU 52.61%) by a wide margin. The Stage2-CogVideoX variant (sparse-box control) still leads on FVD (107.21) and BoxIoU (81.45%) on MagicBench, confirming robustness under sparser trajectory conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MagicMotion outperforms all seven baselines across every metric on both MagicBench and DAVIS. On MagicBench, the closest competitor is Tora [91] for FID (26.27 vs. 15.06) and SG-I2V [35] for FVD (168.82 vs. 112.69) and BoxIoU (74.39% vs. 87.75%); MagicMotion leads by 11+ FID points, 56+ FVD points, and 13+ BoxIoU points respectively. On DAVIS, the gap is even more pronounced: MagicMotion (Stage1-CogVideoX) achieves 81.33% MaskIoU versus the best baseline DragNUWA [84] at 41.22%, a 40-point margin. Among baselines, SG-I2V [35] and DragAnything [71] are the strongest on MagicBench trajectory accuracy, while Tora [91] leads on DAVIS FID (51.75) and FVD (766.76), yet all are surpassed by MagicMotion. The paper also reports that MagicMotion maintains its advantage across all object-count categories (1 to >5 objects) on MagicBench, whereas baselines degrade more sharply as the number of controlled objects increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DiT-based video generation paradigm (CogVideoX, Wan2.1) and adapts the ControlNet architecture for trajectory conditioning, extending it from 2D image control to 3D video latents. It departs from prior trajectory-controllable video generation methods (MotionCtrl, Tora, DragAnything, LeViTor, SG-I2V) that each support only a single control signal format, by unifying dense (masks) and sparse (boxes, sparse boxes) conditions through a progressive training strategy. The latent segmentation loss draws on the observation that diffusion features encode rich semantic information, but operates in latent space rather than pixel space to reduce computational cost, and the data pipeline repurposes LLM-based object extraction (Llama3.1) and grounded segmentation (GroundedSAM2) to automate trajectory annotation at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of MagicData (23K annotated videos) and MagicBench (600 videos, categorized by object count) fills a critical gap: prior trajectory-controllable video generation work relied on private datasets or small benchmarks like DAVIS, making fair comparison impossible. The progressive dense-to-sparse training strategy and latent segmentation loss offer a practical recipe for maintaining shape fidelity under sparse user input, which is the more realistic interaction pattern. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 3 objects, whether the 49-frame evaluation window is sufficient for longer videos, and whether the benchmark's SAM2-based evaluation protocol introduces systematic bias. The framework's reliance on a specific base model (CogVideoX/Wan2.1) also raises questions about generalizability to future architectures.
