## Problem

Generating photorealistic 3D cities directly from satellite imagery remains challenging because satellite views provide only a top-down perspective, lacking building facades, street-level details, and architectural styles. Existing geometry-prior methods preserve structural accuracy but produce low-quality textures with limited style diversity, while image-prior diffusion methods generate realistic textures but suffer from geometric inconsistencies across views that worsen at city scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251373/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single top-down satellite image as input and produces two outputs: a set of 60 photorealistic multi-view city images (rendered at 1920×1080 along diverse camera trajectories including orbital, forward-facing, and spline paths) and a 3D city representation in 3D Gaussian Splatting format. The satellite image is preprocessed to extract instance segmentation, depth maps, and CLIP texture descriptors, which are then lifted into a 3D volume and rendered from N camera positions to form the geometric conditioning signal.

## Method

MagicCity employs a three-stage pipeline: (1) a Dual Encoder separately encodes 3D geometry (segmentation + depth rendered from multiple viewpoints) and CLIP texture features into embeddings via cross-attention injection at each UNet block, with an asymmetric training strategy that freezes the texture branch while training the geometry branch; (2) a City-scale Multi-view Diffusion model based on LDM generates consistent views using a multi-branch architecture with Inter-Frame Consistency Attention for cross-frame feature sharing, Hierarchical Geometric-Aware Attention (dual-path: 4-head scene-level + 8-head object-level) in middle layers, and a progressive generation strategy that synthesizes key frames first, evaluates them with DINO-based instance-level consistency scores, and only proceeds to remaining frames once a threshold (0.85) is met; (3) a Consistency Score-guided 3D Gaussian Splatting pipeline uses per-instance consistency scores to weight both color initialization and per-point rendering loss during optimization, suppressing artifacts from pixel-level inconsistencies in the generated views.

## Results

On the CityVista test set, MagicCity achieves the best scores across all six evaluation metrics: FID 86.10, KID 0.087, NIQE 4.55, BRISQUE 28.02, Depth Error (DE) 0.137, and Camera Error (CE) 0.072. Compared to the strongest baseline DreamScene, MagicCity improves FID by 17.7% (86.10 vs. 104.63), KID by 30.4% (0.087 vs. 0.125), and NIQE by 24.3% (4.55 vs. 6.02), while also achieving the lowest geometric consistency errors (DE 0.137 vs. 0.223, CE 0.072 vs. 0.371). The method is evaluated on 500 satellite images with 60 rendered frames per scene at 1280×720 resolution, using both the CityVista and OSM datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MagicCity outperforms all three main baselines (CityDreamer, DimensionX, DreamScene) on every reported metric. Against CityDreamer (geometry-prior-based), MagicCity reduces FID by 44.5% (86.10 vs. 155.39) and BRISQUE by 67.7% (28.02 vs. 86.77), while also improving DE (0.137 vs. 0.157) and CE (0.072 vs. 0.083), showing that explicit geometric constraints in a diffusion framework surpass purely geometry-driven texture synthesis. Against DimensionX (video-diffusion-based), MagicCity achieves substantially better visual quality (FID 86.10 vs. 126.89, NIQE 4.55 vs. 6.60) and is the only method reporting geometric consistency scores, since DimensionX's frame-by-frame generation lacks 3D constraints. Against DreamScene (multi-view diffusion with formation pattern sampling), MagicCity leads on all six metrics, with the largest gaps in geometric consistency (DE 0.137 vs. 0.223, CE 0.072 vs. 0.371), confirming that the proposed IFCA and HGA modules effectively enforce city-scale structural coherence. In the supplementary multi-view generation comparison (Figure 5), MagicCity also outperforms Wonder3D, ViewCrafter, and DimensionX on 360° scene synthesis, maintaining building proportions and texture consistency where baselines exhibit distortion. For 3D reconstruction (Figure 4), the consistency-score-guided strategy (CSR) produces fewer artifacts than ReconFusion, particularly in regions with pixel-level color inconsistencies across views.

## Relation to Prior Work

MagicCity sits at the intersection of two established lines of 3D city generation: geometry-prior methods (InfiniCity, CityDreamer, GaussianCity) that build 3D structure from satellite semantic/depth maps but produce limited texture quality, and image-prior methods (CAT3D, DimensionX, DreamScene) that leverage diffusion models for photorealistic textures but lack explicit geometric constraints. The paper builds on the LDM framework for its diffusion backbone, adopts CAT3D's progressive key-frame-then-remaining-frames generation strategy, and extends 3D Gaussian Splatting with consistency-score-guided color initialization and adaptive optimization. Its key architectural departure is the Dual Encoder that jointly encodes CLIP texture features and rendered geometric features, injected via decoupled cross-attention, combined with Inter-Frame Consistency Attention to enforce cross-view coherence during parallel multi-view generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in digital-twin and gaming pipelines: the inability of prior methods to simultaneously achieve photorealistic textures and geometric fidelity at city scale. The CityVista dataset (500 scenes with paired satellite, multi-view, and 3D annotations) fills a notable gap, as no prior dataset provided all three modalities together. However, the method still depends on the quality of upstream depth/segmentation predictions, the progressive generation with iterative consistency checking adds latency, and the 3DGS reconstruction retains artifacts in low-consistency regions. Open questions include scalability to truly unbounded or real-world cities beyond the 500m orbital trajectories tested, and whether the consistency-score mechanism generalizes to dynamic or seasonal city changes.
