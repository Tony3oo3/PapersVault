## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has become a dominant 3D scene representation, yet its robustness to data poisoning attacks targeting visible illusion injection remains unexplored. Prior work (IPA-NeRF) demonstrated that implicit NeRF representations are vulnerable to view-dependent poisoning, but 3DGS's explicit geometry and multi-view consistency effectively neutralize naive adaptations of such attacks. This paper is the first to systematically analyze why existing poisoning methods fail on 3DGS and to propose a successful attack that embeds clearly visible illusory objects from a target view while preserving innocent-view fidelity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard multi-view image dataset for 3DGS training, an illusory object image (e.g., a vehicle or dog) to be injected, and the camera parameters of the target (poisoned) view. The illusory object is projected onto the image plane of the target view, and rays are cast from the camera center through its pixels to determine 3D placement. The output is a modified 3D Gaussian point cloud (poisoned G) that, when rendered from the target view, displays the illusory object with PSNR > 25 on the masked region, while renderings from all other views remain visually indistinguishable from the clean reconstruction (PSNR drop ≤ 3 dB).

## Method

The attack combines two complementary strategies. First, a Density-Guided Point Cloud Attack uses Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) over the initial Gaussian point cloud to compute a continuous 3D density field; rays cast from the target-view camera through the illusory object's pixels are sampled to find minimum-density positions (x_min), where new Gaussian points are inserted with colors sampled from the illusory object, exploiting the fact that low-density regions are weakly constrained by multi-view consistency. Second, a View Consistency Disruption Attack adds adaptive Gaussian noise (η ~ N(0, σ_t²)) exclusively to innocent training views during 3DGS optimization, with σ_t decaying over iterations (linear, cosine, or square-root schedules) to weaken multi-view consistency early in training while allowing clean reconstruction to recover later. The KDE bandwidth (h) controls placement precision, and the initial noise strength (σ₀) balances illusion persistence against innocent-view degradation; the ablation shows both components are necessary for a 7/7 attack success rate.

## Results

On single-view attacks across three datasets, the proposed density-guided method achieves V-ILLUSORY PSNR of 27.04 (Mip-NeRF 360), 21.33 (Tanks & Temples), and 26.66 (Free), substantially outperforming the best baseline (IPA-NeRF Instant-NGP) by 9.4, 5.3, and 7.7 dB respectively. V-TEST PSNR remains competitive with the unpoisoned 3DGS baseline (e.g., 27.76 vs. 29.45 on Mip-NeRF 360), confirming minimal degradation on innocent views. The method achieves a 7/7 Attack Success Rate (PSNR > 25 on V-ILLUSORY with V-TEST drop ≤ 3) when combining all attack components, versus 6/7 for density-guided alone and 0/7 for image poisoning alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: IPA-NeRF (Nerfacto), IPA-NeRF (Instant-NGP), and IPA-Splat (a direct adaptation of IPA-NeRF to 3DGS). The proposed method decisively outperforms all baselines on V-ILLUSORY PSNR across every dataset, with the largest margin over IPA-NeRF (Instant-NGP) being +9.4 dB on Mip-NeRF 360 (27.04 vs. 17.60) and +7.7 dB on Free (26.66 vs. 18.94). Notably, IPA-Splat—the most natural 3DGS adaptation—performs nearly identically to unpoisoned 3DGS on V-ILLUSORY (13.23 vs. 13.21 PSNR on Mip-NeRF 360), confirming that 3DGS's multi-view consistency effectively neutralizes naive poisoning. On V-TEST, the proposed method incurs a modest PSNR drop (1.7 dB on Mip-NeRF 360, 3.0 dB on Tanks & Temples, 1.6 dB on Free) relative to unpoisoned 3DGS, while IPA-NeRF baselines suffer much larger drops (7.5–10.5 dB), indicating they degrade innocent views far more severely. In multi-view attacks (2–4 poisoned views), the proposed method maintains V-ILLUSORY PSNR above 26.9 while keeping V-TEST PSNR above 27.5, whereas all baselines drop below 20 on V-ILLUSORY.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the view-dependent illusion injection paradigm from IPA-NeRF (Jiang et al., ECAI 2024), which targeted NeRF's implicit MLP representation, to the explicit Gaussian point cloud representation of 3DGS (Kerbl et al., TOG 2023). The key conceptual departure is that 3DGS's multi-view consistency and densification processes fundamentally resist the direct image-level poisoning strategies that work on NeRF, necessitating a new attack strategy built on KDE-based density estimation for point placement and adaptive noise scheduling to disrupt consistency. The paper also distinguishes itself from the concurrent Poison-Splat (Lu et al., 2024), which targets computational cost rather than visible illusion embedding, positioning this as the first work on visible-content poisoning of 3DGS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As 3DGS becomes integral to safety-critical applications (autonomous driving, AR/VR, digital twins), demonstrating that view-dependent illusory objects can be stealthily embedded with minimal impact on innocent views highlights a concrete and previously unaddressed vulnerability. The KDE-based evaluation protocol for quantifying attack difficulty across camera configurations provides a reusable benchmark for future defense research. However, the method's limitations with highly overlapping views and complex trajectories suggest that 3DGS's multi-view consistency remains a partial defense, and open questions include whether scalable detection or sanitization methods can identify such density-guided injections without degrading legitimate scene quality.
