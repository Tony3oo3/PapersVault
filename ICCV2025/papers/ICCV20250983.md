## Problem

Existing LLM-driven data generation methods for embodied agents are designed for single-agent settings and cannot handle the additional dimensions of multi-agent collaboration: logical consistency in task allocation, spatial collision avoidance in shared physical space, and temporal scheduling for efficient parallel execution. Moreover, no benchmark exists for evaluating embodied multi-agent manipulation with both high-level planning and low-level control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250983/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes RGB observations from one global camera plus ego-centric cameras per agent, a text task instruction, previous subgoal sets, and constraint violation feedback strings from the checker. RoboBrain outputs per-agent sub-goals, three sets of textual compositional constraints (logical, spatial, temporal), and unconstrained trajectory sequences generated via visual programming over predefined motion primitives. RoboChecker then validates these trajectories against physical constraint interfaces—interaction position/direction annotations, 5 cm voxel 3D occupancy grids built from depth maps and joint states, and temporal occupancy representations—returning a boolean pass/fail with a violation description that feeds back into re-planning.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-module framework (RoboBrain + RoboChecker) that operationalizes compositional constraints for automated multi-agent data generation. RoboBrain, powered by GPT-4o, decomposes a global task into per-agent sub-goals and emits textual constraints, then generates trajectories by invoking predefined motion primitives through visual programming. RoboChecker translates each textual constraint into a concrete physical interface: logical constraints are enforced via annotated interaction positions and directions on 3D assets, spatial constraints via depth-estimated 3D occupancy grids (5 cm voxels) tracking arm joint coordinates, and temporal constraints via dynamic occupancy modeling that detects irrational scheduling and enforces synchronization windows. Validated trajectories and observation sequences form the RoboFactory benchmark (11 tasks, 1–4 Franka Emika Panda arms on ManiSkill3, 150 demonstrations per task). The paper then evaluates Diffusion Policy under four architecture variants (shared vs. separate policy × global vs. local view) and ablates each constraint type, showing that spatial and temporal constraints are critical for both data generation success rate and episode efficiency.

## Results

On the RoboFactory benchmark, Diffusion Policy (DP) with 150 demonstrations achieves average success rates of 49% (1-agent), 27.5% (2-agent), 20.5% (3-agent), and 10% (4-agent), showing a clear performance degradation as the number of collaborating agents increases. The Long Pipeline Delivery task (4-agent) achieves 0% success rate across all data budgets, highlighting limitations in learning long-term temporal dependencies. Among four multi-agent architecture variants tested on two representative tasks, Local View + Separate Policy (Arch4) yields the best results: 58% on Lift Barrier and 20% on Place Food, outperforming the Shared Policy + Global View baseline (49% and 5%) by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external multi-agent manipulation baselines; instead, it conducts internal architectural and ablation comparisons. Among four imitation learning architectures, Local View + Separate Policy (Arch4) dominates: it achieves 58% on Lift Barrier vs. 49% (Shared+Global), 4% (Shared+Local), and 26% (Separate+Global), and 20% on Place Food vs. 5%, 0%, and 17% respectively. The ablation on compositional constraints shows that using all three constraints (logical + spatial + temporal) yields the highest data generation success rate (97.5% on Lift Barrier, 98.9% on Three Robots Stack Cube, 88.2% on Take Photo) and shortest episode lengths (80.7, 424, 204 steps), compared to 80.2%, 62.5%, and 37.1% success with only logical constraints. Spatial constraints contribute the largest single gain in success rate (e.g., 80.2%→95.2% on Lift Barrier), while temporal constraints primarily improve data quality by reducing episode length (e.g., 685→424 steps on Three Robots Stack Cube).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of LLM-driven automated data generation for embodied agents (e.g., RoboCodex, RoboCasa) from single-agent to multi-agent settings by introducing compositional constraints (logical, spatial, temporal) as a principled framework for safe multi-agent data collection. For low-level control, it adapts Diffusion Policy to multi-agent scenarios, exploring four architecture variants (shared/separate policy × global/local view) that go beyond the single-agent formulation. The constraint interface design draws from visual programming and constraint-aware monitoring (CaM, Code-as-Monitor) but repurposes them specifically for multi-agent physical collision avoidance and temporal scheduling, a setting absent from prior visual programming work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first benchmark for embodied multi-agent manipulation, RoboFactory provides a much-needed evaluation platform that exposes a severe performance cliff: success rates drop from 49% (1-agent) to 10% (4-agent), and the 0% on Long Pipeline Delivery reveals that current imitation learning methods fundamentally struggle with long-horizon multi-agent temporal dependencies. The compositional constraints framework offers a reusable conceptual tool for any multi-agent embodied system, but the paper's own results suggest the field is still far from deploying such systems in practice. Open questions include whether more sophisticated multi-agent architectures (e.g., shared latent spaces, communication channels) can close the gap, and whether the constraint framework generalizes beyond the simulated ManiSkill environment to real-world hardware.
