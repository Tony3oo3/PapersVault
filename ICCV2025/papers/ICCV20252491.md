## Problem

Vision Transformers are computationally expensive on edge devices due to quadratic self-attention complexity and resource-intensive Softmax operations, yet existing linear attention alternatives suffer from significant accuracy degradation. No prior method simultaneously eliminates Softmax, achieves linear complexity, and maintains performance comparable to standard softmax-based attention across diverse vision tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252491/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MixA operates on standard RGB images at an edge-friendly 448×448 resolution, which yields 1024 tokens (patch size 14) for DeiT. The method is applied post-hoc to pretrained ViT backbones (DeiT-T/S, Swin-T/S) and produces task-specific outputs: class labels for ImageNet-1K classification, bounding boxes via Faster R-CNN for COCO detection, and pixel-level masks via Semantic FPN for ADE20K segmentation. No architectural changes to the backbone are required beyond swapping attention modules.

## Method

MixA is a mixed-attention strategy with two components. First, a novel importance score (the trace of the product between the loss gradient w.r.t. each attention head and the perturbation induced by replacing softmax with linear attention) identifies the top-k most critical layers, which are assigned ReLU-based quadratic attention (eliminating Softmax while preserving O(N²) expressiveness). Second, remaining layers are replaced with SteLLA, a ReLU-based linear attention that applies ReLU independently to Q and K, enabling the computation reordering Q(K^T V) to reduce complexity from O(N²d) to O(Nd²). SteLLA's key innovation over prior ReLU linear attention (e.g., CosFormer) is theoretically motivated normalization: a √d scaling on the attention matrix (Theorem 5.3) and an N scaling on the output (Theorem 5.2), both with learnable multipliers, which prevents the variance explosion caused by row-sum normalization. The modified model is fine-tuned for 150 epochs with knowledge distillation from the original pretrained ViT as teacher.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, MixA achieves 74.54% top-1 accuracy with DeiT-T (vs. 74.66% Softmax baseline) while reducing inference time from 12.57 ms to 10.30 ms on Apple M1 (22.04% speedup) and from 346.76 ms to 299.90 ms on Raspberry Pi (15.63% speedup). For the larger Swin-S model, MixA delivers a 28.20% speedup on Apple M1 (79.06 ms → 61.67 ms) with only a 0.71% accuracy drop (83.59% → 82.88%). On ADE20K semantic segmentation, MixA-Swin-T actually improves mIoU from 35.3% to 35.8% while achieving a 28.72% speedup on Apple M1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Softmax-based DeiT baselines [28], MixA-DeiT-T loses only 0.12% top-1 accuracy (74.54% vs. 74.66%) while gaining 22% inference speedup on Apple M1; MixA-DeiT-S drops 0.17% (80.59% vs. 80.76%) with a 14.5% speedup. Against Swin Transformer baselines [17], MixA-Swin-T sacrifices 0.48% accuracy (81.28% vs. 81.76%) for a 22.3% speedup, and MixA-Swin-S drops 0.71% (82.88% vs. 83.59%) for a 28.2% speedup. For dense prediction tasks, MixA-DeiT-Adapter-T [4] loses 0.9% mIoU on ADE20K (33.8% vs. 34.7%) and 0.4% mAP on COCO (33.5% vs. 33.9%), while MixA-Swin-T actually improves mIoU by 0.5% (35.8% vs. 35.3%) on ADE20K. In the linear-attention comparison (Table 5), SteLLA outperforms CosFormer [23] by 4.66% (73.71% vs. 69.05%), Efficient Attention [26] by 1.91% (73.71% vs. 71.80%), and SimA [15] by 0.98% (73.71% vs. 72.73%) on DeiT-T, demonstrating that the theoretically motivated normalization is the key differentiator over prior ReLU-based linear attention.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the ReLU-based attention line initiated by Wortsman et al. (replacing softmax with ReLU in quadratic attention) and the ReLU-based linear attention formulations in CosFormer and EfficientViT. Its key departure is twofold: (1) it identifies that the row-sum normalization used in prior ReLU linear attention causes variance explosion and instability, replacing it with theoretically motivated variance-based scaling factors derived from theorems on dot-product variance growth; and (2) it introduces a mixed-attention strategy where an importance score (combining gradient sensitivity and attention perturbation) determines which layers retain quadratic ReLU attention versus receiving the cheaper linear SteLLA module. This positions MixA as a post-hoc modification of pretrained ViTs rather than a from-scratch architecture, distinguishing it from Performer, Nystromformer, and other linear attention designs that require retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners deploying ViTs on edge hardware (phones, embedded devices), the 12–28% inference speedup with sub-0.5% accuracy loss is a practical and drop-in improvement that requires no architectural redesign—just a forward/backward pass to compute importance scores followed by fine-tuning. The theoretical grounding for the normalization terms (variance scaling by √d and N) provides a principled alternative to the ad-hoc row-sum normalization that has plagued prior ReLU linear attention, potentially informing future attention designs. Open questions include whether the importance score generalizes across tasks and resolutions without recomputation, whether the approach scales to larger ViT variants (the paper only tests tiny/small models), and whether the mixed strategy can be extended to other activation functions beyond ReLU.
