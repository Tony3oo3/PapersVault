## Problem

While MLLMs have been extensively evaluated on 2D visual perception and semantic question answering, their capacity for precise, quantitative spatial-temporal understanding—such as estimating object dimensions, computing displacement and velocity, and performing visual odometry—remains largely unexamined. No existing benchmark systematically assesses MLLMs on metric-level spatial reasoning and physical motion analysis across diverse real-world scenarios, leaving the readiness of these models for Embodied AI and Autonomous Driving uncertain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250178/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance consists of a uniformly sampled set of 30 video frames (20 for Claude-3.7-Sonnet due to API limits) paired with a multiple-choice text question offering five answer options. The 2,064 QA pairs span three real-world scenarios (Desktop from Omni6DPose, Indoor from ScanNet, Outdoor from Waymo) and eight task types covering both static spatial measurement and dynamic motion analysis. Distractor options are constructed with scenario-specific error ranges—millimeter-level for desktop, centimeter-to-decimeter for indoor, and decimeter-to-meter for outdoor—using logarithmic sampling to ensure numerically challenging but reasonable alternatives.

## Method

STI-Bench is constructed via a pipeline that aggregates frame-by-frame camera intrinsics/extrinsics and 3D bounding box annotations from Waymo, ScanNet, and Omni6DPose, from which ground-truth numerical values (distances, velocities, poses) and textual trajectory descriptions are computed. MLLMs are then prompted with the ground-truth data, object descriptions, and task-specific requirements to generate diverse questions and answer options, followed by multi-round human filtering via a dedicated website to remove inaccurate descriptions, unreasonable options, and cases where video alone lacks sufficient information. A fine-grained adjustment step applies a weighted-average formula to reposition distractors so that the minimum distance to the correct answer equals a logarithmically sampled error value within the scenario-specific range, ensuring evaluation robustness. The benchmark evaluates 10 state-of-the-art MLLMs (4 proprietary, 6 open-source) in a 5-option multiple-choice format where a random-guess baseline yields 20% accuracy, and error analysis on ~200 sampled failures per task identifies three dominant failure modes: inaccurate spatial quantification, flawed temporal dynamics understanding, and weak cross-modal grounding.

## Results

On STI-Bench (2,064 QA pairs across 300+ videos), the best-performing model Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves only 41.4% average accuracy, barely doubling the 20% random-guess baseline. Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL-72B leads at 40.7%, followed by InternVL2.5-78B (38.5%) and VideoChat-Flash (36.3%). Performance is weakest on precise quantitative tasks: Dimensional Measurement peaks at 38.7% (Gemini-2.5-Pro) and Displacement & Path Length at 33.9%, while relative tasks like Spatial Relation (53.8%) and Pose Estimation (62.6% by Claude-3.7-Sonnet) are comparatively easier.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gemini-2.5-Pro (41.4%) edges out Qwen2.5-VL-72B (40.7%) and Claude-3.7-Sonnet (40.5%) by a narrow margin, with all three proprietary/open-source leaders clustered within 1 percentage point. Notably, GPT-4o ranks only 8th (34.8%), trailing even the 7B VideoLLaMA3-7B (35.2%), suggesting its video-spatial reasoning is weaker than specialized video MLLMs. Gemini-2.5-Pro dominates six of eight tasks (Dim. Meas., Spatial Relation, 3D Grounding, Disp. & Path, Ego Orient., Traj. Desc.), while Claude-3.7-Sonnet wins Pose Estimation (62.6%) and Speed & Acceleration (38.8%), and Qwen2.5-VL-72B takes 3D Video Grounding (39.1%). The gap between the best (41.4%) and worst (26.9%, MiniCPM-V-2.6) model is ~14.5 points, but even the top model remains far below the reliability threshold needed for embodied or driving applications. Across scene types, outdoor is easiest (best: 50.6% by Qwen2.5-VL-72B) while desktop is hardest (best: 35.8% by Gemini-2.5-Pro), indicating that finer-grained spatial precision remains the primary bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in benchmarking by positioning itself against a family of spatial/embodied MLLM benchmarks (VSI-Bench, EmbSpatial-Bench, EmbodiedBench, WorldSense) that the authors argue are limited in scene coverage, lack numerical ground truth, or omit dynamic spatial-temporal tasks. Rather than building on a specific prior method, STI-Bench repurposes existing real-world video datasets (Waymo, ScanNet, Omni6DPose) with their 3D annotations to construct a unified evaluation spanning desktop, indoor, and outdoor scales. The conceptual departure from prior work is the insistence on precise quantitative answers (distances in cm/m, speeds, angular changes) rather than descriptive or multiple-choice semantic judgments, and the inclusion of both ego-centric and allocentric perspectives across all three scales simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the best-performing model (Gemini-2.5-Pro) achieves only 41.4% average accuracy—barely double the 20% random baseline—quantifies a gap that the embodied AI and autonomous driving communities have informally suspected but lacked a systematic measure for. The three identified failure modes (inaccurate spatial quantification, flawed temporal dynamics, weak cross-modal grounding) provide a concrete diagnostic framework for future model development. Open questions include whether architectural changes (e.g., explicit 3D representations, physics-informed priors) or data strategies (more metric-scale training data) can close this gap, and whether the benchmark's multiple-choice format with logarithmically-spaced distractors adequately captures the continuous nature of spatial-temporal estimation.
