## Problem

Existing planar affine rectification methods each fail on certain image types: line-based vanishing-point estimators struggle with patterns lacking prominent linear structures (e.g., polka dots), while scale-based methods fail on line-dominated scenes. Prior approaches that combine multiple cues either require expensive affine-invariant feature detectors (ASIFT, AffNet) or impose restrictive assumptions such as feature grouping into consistent classes. The paper asks whether cheap, separately extractable scale and orientation features can be jointly exploited to achieve robust rectification across all pattern types without segmentation or inter-feature dependencies.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and extracts two types of local features: point-and-scale features (e.g., SIFT keypoints with their scale) and oriented line segments (e.g., from LSD or DeepLSD). The output is a two-parameter rectifying homography Ĥ = [[1,0,0],[0,1,0],[h7,h8,1]] that maps the plane's vanishing line to the line at infinity, producing an affinely rectified image. No minimum image resolution is specified, but experiments use 800×600 images; the method is detector-agnostic and works with any feature extractor that provides local scale or orientation.

## Method

The core contribution is a novel linear constraint (Eq. 11) that relates the intersection point of two oriented lines to the rectifying homography parameters: if two orientation features define a vanishing point v, then [vx, vy, 0]·[h7, h8, α]ᵀ = vw, which is linear in the unknowns. This is combined with the modified scale constraint from Chum et al. (Eq. 8), [x, y, σ^(1/3)]·[h7, h8, α]ᵀ = 1, to form a 3×3 linear system solvable by the Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse, requiring only two scale and two orientation features. A hybrid RANSAC framework scores candidate models using log-scale residuals for scale features and angular residuals for orientation features, with a geometric degeneracy test that discards samples whose vanishing point falls inside the convex hull of the four feature points. For images with multiple scale classes, an iterative strategy progressively assigns scale features to classes while reusing orientation inliers, eliminating the need for pre-classification.

## Results

The paper evaluates on three synthetic patterns (checkerboard, polka-dot, linear) with homography parameters h7, h8 sampled in [-0.2, 0.2] at step 2×10⁻⁵, running 100 iterations per setting. The metric is the average pixel-wise rectification error over a grid of sampled points in an 800×600 image. Results are presented as 3D median-error surface plots (Fig. 6) and recall curves (Fig. 7) rather than tabulated numbers; median errors exceeding 0.1 are truncated for visualization. The proposed hybrid method (SIFT + LSD) consistently matches the best-performing baseline on each pattern type, while individual baselines fail on at least one pattern. No quantitative real-world evaluation is provided due to the absence of suitable ground-truth datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: (1) the standard vanishing-point-based estimator from the GC-RANSAC library [1,2], and (2) the scale-based rectification method of Chum et al. [5], both integrated into the same pipeline for fair comparison. The vanishing-point baseline excels on checkerboard and linear patterns (where strong orientation cues exist) but fails entirely on the polka-dot pattern, which lacks linear structures. Conversely, the Chum et al. scale-based method performs well on checkerboard and polka-dot patterns (where scale variations are reliable) but fails on the purely linear pattern. The proposed hybrid method (SIFT + LSD) matches or exceeds the best baseline on every pattern type, demonstrating that combining scale and orientation constraints yields robustness across diverse image structures. In the multi-scale experiment (Fig. 8), the proposed hybrid estimation reduces high-error peaks compared to the adapted Chum et al. method. Learned detectors (SuperPoint + SelfScaleOri + DeepLSD) produce less accurate rectifications than SIFT-based features, indicating that classical detectors remain preferable for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the scale-based affine rectification framework of Chum et al. [5] by deriving a novel linear constraint on local orientation changes, showing that two oriented points define a vanishing point whose incidence with the vanishing line yields a constraint linear in the homography parameters. Unlike prior line-based methods (vanishing point estimation from parallel lines) or affine-invariant feature approaches (ASIFT, AffNet), the method requires no feature segmentation, no grouping into consistent classes, and no expensive multi-viewpoint simulation. The hybrid RANSAC framework draws on the robust estimation literature (GC-RANSAC, MAGSAC++) but adapts it to jointly handle two heterogeneous feature types—scale points and orientation pairs—within a single scoring function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in providing a single, unified rectification pipeline that is robust across fundamentally different image structures (line-dominated, scale-dominated, mixed), eliminating the need to select a method per image type. The orientation constraint is elegant in its simplicity—requiring only two oriented points—and the overall system needs just two scale features and two orientation features for a minimal solve, making it computationally lightweight. Limitations include the dependence on reliable local scale/orientation estimates (learned detectors like SuperPoint underperform SIFT here), the restriction to planar scenes, and the lack of quantitative evaluation on real-world images due to the absence of suitable ground-truth datasets. Open questions include whether the framework extends to non-planar or multi-plane scenes and whether the orientation constraint can be generalized to higher-order geometric invariants.
