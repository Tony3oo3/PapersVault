## Problem

Existing implicit degradation estimation-based blind super-resolution (IDE-BSR) methods produce implicit degradation representations (IDRs) with poor discriminability across degradation types, forcing them to compensate with increasingly complex adaptation networks (e.g., large Transformer blocks) that inflate parameters and computation. The paper asks whether strengthening IDR discriminability itself can enable high-quality BSR with a lightweight adaptation module, circumventing the need for heavy architectural stacking.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single low-resolution RGB image (64×64 patches during training, evaluated at 256×256) with unknown degradation—either isotropic or anisotropic Gaussian blur (kernel size 21×21, width range [0.2, 4.0]) optionally combined with additive Gaussian white noise (σ ∈ [0, 25])—and outputs a 4× upscaled high-resolution image. At inference, only the LR image is required; no degradation parameters or HR supervision are needed, and the entire pipeline is a 3.1M-parameter CNN.

## Method

LightBSR employs a two-stage knowledge-distillation training framework. In the teacher stage, a degradation-prior-constrained contrastive learning technique (MoCo-style with a momentum branch and negative sample queue) is used, where degradation reference priors (DRPs) are constructed by PCA-projecting blur kernels and concatenating noise levels, then stretched to patch dimensions and concatenated with LR patches as input; this forces the IDR-estimator to learn discriminative degradation-specific representations. In the student stage, feature-alignment distillation (L2 loss for spatial IDRs, KL divergence + L1 loss for channel IDRs) transfers the teacher's degradation knowledge to a structurally identical but DRP-free student that takes only the LR image. The architecture consists of an IDR Estimation Module (six-layer conv estimator + spatial/channel converters) and a hierarchical IDR Adaptation Module where each IDR-AB splits LR features 1:3 for spatial-wise and channel-wise modulation via Sigmoid-gated gating, followed by ConvNeXt fusion and residual links across eight IDR-AGs, yielding SOTA PSNR with only 3.1M parameters and 206 GFLOPs.

## Results

Under Degradation Setting 1 (isotropic Gaussian blur, ×4 SR), LightBSR (3.1M params, 206.16 GFLOPs) achieves the best or near-best PSNR across all four benchmarks (Set5, Set14, B100, Urban100) at kernel widths {1.2, 2.4, 3.6}, e.g., 32.47 dB on Set5 (kw=1.2) and 26.50 dB on Urban100 (kw=1.2). Under Degradation Setting 2 (anisotropic blur + Gaussian noise), LightBSR achieves SOTA PSNR on all 18 kernel-noise combinations on B100, exceeding the best IDE-BSR baseline CDFormerS by 0.05 dB on average while using only 26% of its parameters. The model reduces parameters by 78% and computation by 59% compared to the best EDE-BSR method (DCLS) while matching or exceeding its performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LightBSR (3.1M params, 206 GFLOPs) outperforms the strongest Transformer-based IDE-BSR methods CDFormerS (11.9M, 632 GFLOPs) and DSAT (15.6M, 895 GFLOPs) on nearly all 12 evaluation conditions in Setting 1, using only 26% and 20% of their parameters respectively; the sole exception is Set5 at kernel width 1.2 where DSAT edges ahead by 0.04 dB (32.51 vs 32.47). Against the best EDE-BSR method DCLS (13.6M, 497 GFLOPs), LightBSR matches or exceeds PSNR on 8 of 12 conditions while cutting parameters by 78% and FLOPs by 59%. Among same-scale CNN baselines, LightBSR surpasses KDSR (5.8M) by 0.05–0.13 dB on average and DASR (5.8M) by 0.4–0.8 dB across benchmarks. Under the more challenging Setting 2 (anisotropic blur + noise), LightBSR achieves SOTA on all 18 kernel-noise combinations on B100, exceeding CDFormerS by 0.05 dB on average and DnCNN+DCLS by over 0.9 dB. On the real-world RealWorld38 benchmark, LightBSR qualitatively restores line textures more clearly than all compared methods under unknown complex degradations.

## Relation to Prior Work

LightBSR extends the IDE-BSR paradigm by shifting the design focus from complex adaptation mechanisms (as in DSAT and CDFormer, which stack large Transformer blocks) to the discriminability of the implicit degradation representation itself. It inherits the six-layer Conv IDR-estimator and two-stage knowledge distillation framework from KDSR, and borrows the PCA-based dimensionality stretching strategy from prior multi-degradation SR work to construct degradation reference priors. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of MoCo-style contrastive learning with degradation-prior constraints during teacher training, followed by feature-alignment distillation to a lightweight student—repurposing representation learning techniques from self-supervised vision for the specific goal of separating degradation types in latent space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides evidence that the bottleneck in IDE-BSR is not the capacity of the adaptation module but the quality of the degradation representation, offering a principled alternative to the trend of scaling up Transformer backbones. The 3.1M-parameter CNN matching or exceeding 11.9–15.6M-parameter Transformer methods is practically significant for deployment-constrained settings. Open questions include whether the contrastive + DRP training recipe transfers to other restoration tasks (denoising, deblurring), whether the approach generalizes to truly unknown real-world degradations beyond the tested RealWorld38 sample, and whether the modest PSNR margins (≈0.05–0.1 dB over CDFormer) persist at higher resolution scales or with more diverse training data.
