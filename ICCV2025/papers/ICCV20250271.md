## Problem

Existing video generation methods face a fundamental trade-off: T2V approaches lack precise spatial layout control, while I2V methods are restricted to real RGB images as first-frame inputs, limiting editability. ControlNet-based solutions introduce spatial conditioning but require expensive per-modality training and do not support user-defined motion trajectories. No prior training-free framework simultaneously accepts arbitrary modality inputs (e.g., meshes, point clouds) and enables fine-grained trajectory-based motion control.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single conditional image in any modality (mesh, point cloud, edge, depth, skeleton, segmentation, or mixed-modality combinations) as the first-frame spatial constraint, along with user-defined bounding box trajectories specifying where objects should move in subsequent frames, a text prompt for content guidance, and optional LoRA weights for style editing. The output is a multi-frame video where the first frame preserves the structural layout of the input while subsequent frames animate objects along the specified trajectories. Unlike prior I2V methods that require photorealistic RGB inputs, AnyI2V directly consumes structural representations without an intermediate ControlNet rendering step.

## Method

AnyI2V is a training-free pipeline built on AnimateDiff's 3D U-Net that operates in three stages. First, it performs DDIM inversion on the conditional image (with temporal modules removed) to extract features at timestep t_α=201, then applies patchified AdaIN to debias appearance information from residual hidden states while preserving structural layout, injecting both the debiased residual hidden and the spatial self-attention query into the generation process. Second, it achieves zero-shot trajectory control by identifying—via PCA analysis—that the query in spatial self-attention uniquely combines high temporal consistency with entity-level semantic coherence, then optimizes latents to align PCA-reduced query features across frames within user-defined bounding boxes. Third, it generates dynamic semantic masks by computing cosine similarity between salient points in the first frame and features in subsequent frames, followed by K-Means binary clustering, producing masks that adapt to object deformation rather than relying on static regions. Keys and values of subsequent frames are also set to match the first frame to enforce structural coherence, and the entire process requires no gradient updates to model weights.

## Results

As a training-free method, AnyI2V achieves FID 104.53, FVD 569.89, and ObjMC 16.39 on the VIPSeg-based evaluation, outperforming all other training-free baselines (FreeTraj, TrailBlazer, ObjCtrl-2.5D) on every metric. It also surpasses the AnimateDiff + SparseCtrl baseline (FID 141.95, FVD 970.26, ObjMC 38.26) by a wide margin. Among trained methods, AnyI2V is competitive with DragNUWA (FID 105.02, FVD 575.78, ObjMC 15.02) but trails DragAnything (FID 95.83, ObjMC 13.60) and MOFA-Video (FID 95.63) on FID and ObjMC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among training-free methods, AnyI2V clearly dominates: it improves FVD by 51–103 points and ObjMC by 6.7–7.6 points over FreeTraj, TrailBlazer, and ObjCtrl-2.5D, while also achieving the best FID (104.53 vs. 111.82–128.78). Against the AnimateDiff + SparseCtrl baseline, AnyI2V reduces FVD from 970.26 to 569.89 and ObjMC from 38.26 to 16.39, demonstrating the benefit of explicit trajectory control over sparse frame conditioning. Compared to trained methods, AnyI2V is closest to DragNUWA (FVD 575.78 vs. 569.89; ObjMC 15.02 vs. 16.39), slightly trailing on ObjMC but leading on FVD. However, DragAnything (FID 95.83, ObjMC 13.60) and MOFA-Video (FID 95.63) still outperform AnyI2V on FID by roughly 9 points and on ObjMC by 2.8 and 1.3 points respectively, indicating that trained methods retain an edge in visual fidelity and fine-grained motion accuracy. The paper notes that for a fair comparison, all methods' first-frame inputs are converted to structural representations (canny, HED, depth, normal, segmentation), with AnyI2V using them directly while others require a ControlNet preprocessing step.

## Relation to Prior Work

AnyI2V sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) training-free spatial control in diffusion models (PnP, FreeControl), (2) drag-based latent optimization for motion control (DragGAN, DragDiffusion), and (3) I2V generation with structural conditioning (AnimateDiff + ControlNet/SparseCtrl). It departs from ControlNet-based video methods by eliminating training entirely, and from drag-based I2V methods (DragNUWA, DragAnything) by accepting arbitrary modalities (mesh, point cloud, depth, skeleton) rather than only RGB images. The PCA-based analysis of temporal consistency in spatial self-attention queries is a new empirical contribution that motivates the cross-frame alignment strategy, distinguishing it from prior work that optimizes full feature maps or point-level latents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free design removes the need for modality-specific ControlNet training and retraining when the base model changes, making spatially- and motion-controlled video generation accessible without large aligned datasets. The ability to accept mixed-modality inputs and edit content via LoRA/text prompts broadens the practical utility beyond what prior I2V methods offer. However, the authors acknowledge limitations: large motion ranges and ambiguous occlusions remain challenging, and first-frame fidelity is inferior to ControlNet-level control. Open questions include whether the PCA-based alignment generalizes to longer videos or more complex multi-object interactions, and whether lightweight fine-tuning could close the remaining gap with trained methods.
