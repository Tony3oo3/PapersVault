## Problem

Trajectory prediction models struggle with rare, high-risk driving behaviors that are underrepresented in datasets, yet prior work lacks a precise multi-criteria definition of what constitutes a long-tail trajectory and tends to identify tail samples solely via model-specific prediction error. Moreover, existing approaches that boost tail accuracy often degrade head performance, creating an unresolved trade-off between rare and common behavior prediction.

## Input / Output

The model consumes the target agent's past trajectory (position, speed, heading), surrounding agents' trajectories, and vectorized HD map (lane nodes and lines) as input, with the target trajectory additionally passed through four stochastic augmentations (Simplify, Shift, Mask, Subset) to generate augmented views. Output is a set of multimodal future trajectories parameterized by a Laplace Mixture Density Network, yielding per-mode position, scale, and probability values over a 2 s horizon (nuScenes) or 4.8 s horizon (ETH/UCY).

## Method

AMD combines an unsupervised contrastive branch (MoCo-DT) with a supervised contrastive branch (DCL) in a dual-layer framework. MoCo-DT extends standard MoCo with stage-dependent momentum coefficients (0.95/0.99/0.999 for early/middle/late training) and Top-K hard negative mining to force the encoder to discriminate challenging tail samples. DCL applies L2-regularized decoupled weighting to balance intra- and inter-class attraction, preventing head-class dominance. An online iterative K-means clustering strategy periodically re-assigns pseudo-labels from encoder features, allowing the supervised branch to adapt to shifting tail distributions during training. The backbone uses a hierarchical MLP → Transformer Encoder → GRU encoder for trajectories, a GAT for map structure, and a cross-attention scene interaction module with latent-variable mode tokens, feeding a single-layer GRU decoder with Laplace MDN head.

## Results

On the nuScenes long-tail subset (Top 1% hardest samples by prediction error), AMD achieves minADE/minFDE of 1.08/1.66, reducing error by 14.9% and 33.6% over the closest competitor (FEND: 1.21/2.50). On the full nuScenes test set (6 s horizon), AMD attains minADE₅ = 1.23 and MR₅ = 0.50, outperforming the next-best model (SeFlow: 1.38 / 0.60) by 10.9% and 5.7% respectively. On ETH/UCY, AMD achieves an average minADE/minFDE of 0.18/0.27 across all five scenes, surpassing the second-best method (PPT: 0.20/0.31) by 5.3% and 12.9%. Inference time is 14 ms on an RTX 3090, roughly 2–8× faster than competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMD outperforms all long-tail-specific baselines (FEND, TrACT, Traj++ EWTA+contrastive) on every Top 1%–5% subset for both nuScenes and ETH/UCY, with the largest gains on the hardest 1% samples (e.g., 33.6% minFDE reduction over FEND on nuScenes Top 1%). On the full nuScenes set, AMD achieves the best minFDE (0.21) and MR₅ (0.50), though SeFlow edges it slightly on minADE₁₀ (0.98 vs. 1.06). On ETH/UCY, AMD leads all seven compared methods (PECNet, AgentFormer, Trajectron++, NPSN, MID, TUTR, PPT) in average minADE and minFDE across all five scenes. Under the risk-metric criterion (Table 2), AMD beats Q-EANet by 8.5% minADE and 25.8% minFDE on the Top 1% highest-collision-risk samples. Under the vehicle-state criterion (Table 3), AMD improves over Q-EANet on all four maneuver categories (Rapid Acceleration, Rapid Deceleration, Sharp Lane Change, Sharp Turn), with the largest margin on Sharp Lane Change (14.0% lower minFDE). AMD also runs 2–8× faster at inference (14 ms vs. 38–116 ms for competitors), making it suitable for real-time deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the momentum contrast (MoCo) framework [16] with adaptive, stage-dependent momentum coefficients and Top-K hard negative mining (MoCo-DT), and pairs it with decoupled contrastive learning (DCL) [49] as a supervised complement, creating a dual-layer contrastive architecture not previously combined in trajectory prediction. It departs from prior long-tail trajectory prediction methods (FEND [46], TrACT [51], Hi-SCL [19]) that relied on single contrastive strategies or model-specific prediction errors to identify tail samples, instead proposing a multi-criteria definition (prediction error, inverse TTC, vehicle state) and an online iterative clustering mechanism for dynamic pseudo-label updates. The trajectory augmentation strategies (Simplify, Shift, Mask, Subset) draw inspiration from contrastive trajectory similarity learning [6] but are tailored to short-term driving trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-criteria definition of long-tail trajectories (combining error, risk, and state-based criteria) offers a more principled and reproducible way to evaluate long-tail performance than the single-metric approach used by prior work, potentially becoming a standard evaluation protocol. The 14 ms inference time—roughly 2.7× faster than Trajectron++ while achieving better accuracy—makes the framework practically attractive for real-time autonomous driving deployment. Open questions include whether the online iterative clustering strategy scales to larger, more diverse datasets, whether the specific combination of unsupervised and supervised contrastive losses is optimal versus simpler alternatives, and whether the multi-criteria long-tail definition generalizes beyond the two datasets tested (nuScenes, ETH/UCY).
