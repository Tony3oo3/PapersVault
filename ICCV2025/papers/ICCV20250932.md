## Problem

Existing text-to-3D head avatar methods represent hair as holistic meshes or NeRFs, failing to capture the internal strand-level geometry needed for realistic appearance and downstream applications like physics-based simulation. The most relevant prior work, HAAR, requires a large-scale paired dataset of 9,825 haircuts with descriptions, limiting its ability to generate diverse hairstyles beyond the training set. The paper asks whether human-specific 2D generative priors can be distilled to produce realistic, diverse 3D hair strands from text without such costly paired data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250932/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single free-form text prompt (e.g., 'A handsome American man with slicked-back sandy blonde hair') and produces a fully disentangled 3D head avatar: a FLAME-aligned bald head represented as a DMTet with texture field, plus 3D hair strands represented as Neural Scalp Textures decoded into individual polylines (each with position, orientation, and curvature per point). The strands can be converted to watertight prismatic meshes for rendering and imported into graphics engines (e.g., Blender) for physics-based simulation, enabling strand-level transfer, editing, and animation that holistic representations cannot support.

## Method

StrandHead introduces a differentiable prismatization algorithm that converts each hair strand polyline into a watertight prismatic mesh with K lateral edges and radius R, solving the critical problem of ambiguous normals in non-watertight quad meshes that destabilize SDS-based optimization. This enables stable gradient backpropagation from HumanNorm's human-specific 2D diffusion models (normal-adapted and depth-adapted) through a mesh-based differentiable renderer to the underlying Neural Scalp Texture representation. Two prior-driven losses regularize the geometry: an orientation consistency loss enforcing high cosine similarity between neighboring strand directions (validated at >95% above 0.9 across 343 USC-HairSalon hairstyles), and a curvature regularization loss constraining mean strand curvature to a target value derived from the prompt's curliness description. The three-stage pipeline first generates a FLAME-aligned bald head via enhanced HumanNorm, then optimizes strand geometry using the combined SDS and prior-driven losses, and finally generates strand-aware texture using a normal-conditioned diffusion model with MSDS to avoid color oversaturation.

## Results

For head generation, StrandHead achieves the best BLIP-VQA score (0.8500) and ties with HeadArtist on BLIP2-VQA (0.9667), while dominating user-study metrics: GQP 53.67% vs. 34.33% (TECA) and TAP 58.00% vs. 28.33% (TECA). For hair generation, StrandHead ties MVDream on BLIP-VQA (0.9000) and ranks second to LucidDreamer on BLIP2-VQA (0.9000 vs. 0.9333), but substantially outperforms all baselines on user-study preferences: GQP 57.67% (vs. 24.67% for MVDream) and TAP 60.33% (vs. 20.00% for MVDream). The evaluation uses BLIP-VQA and BLIP2-VQA for text-image alignment, plus GQP (generation quality preference) and TAP (text-image alignment preference) from a 30-volunteer user study.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against head-generation baselines, StrandHead wins on all four metrics: it surpasses HeadStudio (0.7833) and HeadArtist (0.7667) on BLIP-VQA, ties HeadArtist on BLIP2-VQA (0.9667), and leads TECA by a wide margin on user-study GQP (53.67% vs. 34.33%) and TAP (58.00% vs. 28.33%). For hair generation, StrandHead ties MVDream on BLIP-VQA (0.9000) and trails LucidDreamer slightly on BLIP2-VQA (0.9000 vs. 0.9333), but dominates every method on user-study preferences—GQP 57.67% vs. 24.67% (MVDream) and TAP 60.33% vs. 20.00% (MVDream). Compared to the closest text-to-strand baseline HAAR, StrandHead improves BLIP-VQA by 0.267 (0.9000 vs. 0.6333) and BLIP2-VQA by 0.700 (0.9000 vs. 0.2000), while GQP jumps from 1.33% to 57.67%. General text-to-3D methods (GaussianDreamer, RichDreamer) perform poorly on hair-specific metrics, confirming that domain-agnostic approaches struggle to capture strand-level structure. TECA, which uses NeRF for hair, is outperformed on both head and hair tasks, underscoring the advantage of explicit strand geometry over implicit volumetric representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

StrandHead builds directly on HumanNorm's human-specific 2D diffusion models (normal-adapted, depth-adapted, and normal-conditioned) and the Neural Scalp Textures strand representation from Neural Strands, repurposing them for a task neither was designed for: strand-level hair generation from text. It departs from HAAR, the closest prior text-to-strand method, by replacing a dedicated text-conditioned hair-map diffusion model (trained on 9,825 paired haircuts) with score distillation from pre-trained 2D human priors, and from TECA/NeuralHaircut by replacing NeRF or non-watertight quad meshes with a novel differentiable prismatization that produces watertight prismatic meshes for stable gradient flow. The two prior-driven regularization losses (orientation consistency and curvature) are grounded in statistical observations from the USC-HairSalon dataset, adding a 3D geometric prior layer absent from SDS-only pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

StrandHead demonstrates that large-scale 3D hair-text paired data is not necessary for realistic strand generation, opening a path toward diverse, text-controllable hair that adapts to arbitrary head shapes—something HAAR's fixed dataset cannot achieve. The differentiable prismatization is a reusable technical contribution for any pipeline needing to bridge strand representations and mesh-based differentiable rendering. However, the method remains limited to hairstyles representable by the underlying strand generator (failing on dreadlocks, ponytails), and the SDS-based optimization is computationally expensive, restricting practical deployment. Open questions include whether richer hairstyle datasets or learned 3D hair priors can extend the representable space, and whether the distillation cost can be reduced for interactive applications.
