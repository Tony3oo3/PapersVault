## Problem

Real-world datasets commonly suffer from both long-tailed class imbalance and label noise simultaneously (LTNLL), yet existing methods address these issues in isolation or with limited success. Current SOTA LTNLL methods like SFA overlook low-confidence tail instances and lack targeted tail-class operations, while RCAL over-calibrates tail classes by indiscriminately using all head-class instances, introducing false inter-class correlations. The paper identifies that semantically related instances across classes can boost representations, but no prior work exploits label-space information (training labels) to achieve this transfer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251826/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet-LT and WebVision) paired with noisy one-hot class labels from long-tailed distributed datasets. Rather than producing a single prediction, IBC constructs three shot-specific soft label vectors per instance (one each for head, medium, and tail class groups) and trains three corresponding expert classifiers that share a common feature encoder. The final output is a class label obtained by integrating the three expert predictions, while the soft labels themselves serve as the supervisory signal during training.

## Method

IBC first pre-trains a backbone with MoCo contrastive learning to obtain noise-robust features, then computes dynamic class prototypes via exponential moving averages. A two-component GMM over instance-prototype distances separates clean from noisy instances; noisy ones are handled as unlabeled data via MixMatch. For clean instances, the key mechanism is Instance-Prototype Similarity (IPS): each instance's cosine similarity to all class prototypes identifies top-k semantically related classes, which are partitioned into head/medium/tail groups. Probability mass is then redistributed from the noisy one-hot label to these semantically related classes, weighted by similarity, producing three shot-specific soft labels. Three expert classifiers (E_h, E_m, E_t) are trained on these labels with progressively stronger rebalancing losses (standard CE, balanced CE with log n_k, and modified balanced CE with log n_k²), and their predictions are ensembled for final classification.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with noise ratio 0.5 and imbalance ratio 200, IBC achieves 69.80% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the previous SOTA SFA (64.81%) by +4.99%. On CIFAR-100 under the same setting, IBC reaches 35.60% vs SFA's 32.85% (+2.75%). Across all 12 noise/imbalance settings on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, IBC consistently outperforms every baseline with improvements ranging from +1.89% to +4.99%. On ImageNet-LT (noise 0.5), IBC achieves 38.35% overall accuracy and 19.95% on tail classes, surpassing SFA by +1.93% and +4.80% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IBC outperforms all 26 baselines across every dataset and setting tested. The closest competitors are SFA [11] and RCAL [41], the two prior SOTA LTNLL methods. On CIFAR-10 (noise 0.5, IR 100), IBC achieves 78.17% vs SFA's 74.06% (+4.11%) and RCAL's 64.01% (+14.16%); on CIFAR-100 (noise 0.5, IR 100), IBC reaches 41.96% vs SFA's 39.73% (+2.23%) and RCAL's 35.04% (+6.92%). On the real-world WebVision dataset (IR≈6.78), IBC achieves 79.76% top-1 on WebVision-50, beating SFA (78.96%) by +0.80% and RCAL (76.68%) by +3.08%, with an even larger top-5 gap (+1.68% over SFA). On Clothing1M, IBC reaches 76.15%, surpassing the best NLL baseline RCAL+ (74.97%) by +1.18%. NLL methods (DivideMix, DISC) degrade severely under high imbalance, while LTL methods (cRT, RIDE, SADE) collapse under high noise, whereas IBC maintains consistent performance across all configurations with minimal degradation as noise and imbalance ratios increase.

## Relation to Prior Work

IBC builds on the LTNLL problem space established by SFA (stochastic feature averaging) and RCAL (representation calibration), but departs from both: unlike RCAL, which calibrates tail classes using all head-class macro-statistics (causing over-calibration), IBC targets semantically related instances via instance-prototype similarity; unlike SFA, which relies on high-confidence instances for prototype estimation, IBC uses MoCo's unsupervised features for noise-robust representations. The method also extends the label smoothing paradigm by replacing random probability redistribution with similarity-weighted redistribution to top-k semantically related classes, and introduces a shot-specific multi-expert ensemble framework that has no direct precedent in prior LTNLL work. MixMatch is repurposed to exploit noisy instances as unlabeled data, and a GMM-based distance criterion handles clean/noisy sample separation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that label-space rectification (softening noisy labels toward semantically related classes) is a viable and effective complement to representation-space calibration for LTNLL, offering a unified mechanism that simultaneously mitigates overfitting to noise and under-representation of tail classes. The shot-specific ensemble finding—that a soft label targeting one shot improves that shot but degrades others, and that fusing all three experts yields the best overall performance—provides a practical design principle for multi-granularity classification. Limitations include dependence on MoCo pre-training quality, the added complexity of GMM-based sample selection, and evaluation primarily on standard benchmarks; open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-long-tailed imbalance patterns (e.g., multi-modal class distributions) and whether the shot-specific framework scales to very large class counts where head/medium/tail boundaries become ambiguous.
