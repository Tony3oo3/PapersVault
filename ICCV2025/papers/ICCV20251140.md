## Problem

Current multimodal robotic manipulation models predominantly operate on 2D image understanding, limiting their ability to interact with the physical 3D world. Additionally, existing multi-dataset efforts like Open X-Embodiment lack critical 3D information (multi-view images, camera parameters, depth maps) and proper spatial alignment across datasets, causing direct data fusion to actually underperform single-dataset training (e.g., RT-1-X < RT-1). The paper addresses both gaps by proposing a 3D-aware multimodal architecture and a unified dataset with standardized input/output spaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251140/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes H historical frames from N camera perspectives, a natural-language instruction, and camera intrinsic/extrinsic parameters. The primary output is a per-frame delta 6D pose (Δpos, Δrot) plus a 1-DoF gripper action, produced at every time step via the Frame-by-Frame Action mechanism rather than only at the final frame. Two optional auxiliary outputs—predicted next-frame static and wrist images, and a full 3D occupancy grid with position and RGB color—can be enabled or disabled at inference time through the Modality-Isolation-Mask, making the architecture flexible for datasets where depth or multi-view sensors are unavailable.

## Method

RoboTron-Mani stacks four components: a CLIP-based Vision Encoder extracts multi-view features; a UVFormer 3D Perception Adapter uses camera parameters and learnable unified-view queries to project features into a shared L×B×P 3D grid, unifying spatial understanding across views; an OpenFlamingo-based Feature Fusion Decoder fuses visual and text features via cross-attention, with a novel Modality-Isolation-Mask (MIM) that controls attention connectivity between modality-specific tokens during training and allows selective modality omission at inference; and separate Multimodal Decoders (attention-based image decoder, 3D-convolution occupancy decoder, MLP or DiT action decoder) produce the respective outputs. Training minimizes a weighted sum of action MSE+BCE loss, pixel-level L2 image loss, and occupancy position+RGB loss, with any term excludable when the corresponding modality is absent. The companion RoboData dataset unifies 10 heterogeneous datasets (70K episodes, 7M samples) by rotating all coordinate systems to a common X-right/Y-forward/Z-up frame, translating workspaces to a fixed range, and regenerating all actions using the Composite Rotation Matrix Method, enabling the first generalist policy to be jointly trained and evaluated across all datasets simultaneously.

## Results

RoboTron-Mani achieves state-of-the-art or near-SOTA success rates across five datasets: 91.7% on LIBERO (vs. 89.8% QueST), 47.4% on RoboCasa (vs. 40.9% GR00T-N1-2B+IDM), 93.8% on CALVIN (vs. 93.7% MDT), 80.1% on Meta-World (vs. 80.4% PRISE), and 60.0% on RT-1 (vs. 60.7% RT-2-X 55B). On CALVIN, the full model with DiT action head boosts the average completed task sequence length from 1.7 (baseline) to 3.51. The model is trained jointly on all five datasets (2.1M samples, 10 epochs, 32×A100 GPUs, ~50 hours) with a 4B-parameter architecture in bf16 precision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoboTron-Mani outperforms all expert baselines on LIBERO (91.7% vs. 89.8% QueST, 89.1% π-Fast*, 87.3% π0*), RoboCasa (47.4% vs. 40.9% GR00T-N1-2B+IDM), and CALVIN (93.8% vs. 93.7% MDT, 93.0% HULC++). On Meta-World, it achieves 80.1%, narrowly trailing PRISE (80.4%) but substantially exceeding PAD (72.5%) and GR-1 (57.4%). On the RT-1 real-world benchmark, RoboTron-Mani reaches 60.0%, slightly below the 55B-parameter RT-2-X (60.7%) but surpassing RT-1-X (53.4%), HPT (35.0%), OpenVLA (24.8%), and Octo-Base (16.9%). Critically, as a generalist policy trained jointly on all five datasets, RoboTron-Mani surpasses the prior generalist RoboFlamingo* by 19.6 points on LIBERO (91.7% vs. 72.1%), 14.8 points on Meta-World (80.1% vs. 65.3%), 50.0 points on CALVIN (93.8% vs. 43.8%), and 31.8 points on RoboCasa (47.4% vs. 15.6%), demonstrating that its 3D perception and data alignment strategy enables a single model to match or exceed task-specific expert models across all benchmarks simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoboTron-Mani builds directly on OpenFlamingo's cross-attention architecture for multimodal fusion and RoboUniView's UVFormer for unified 3D view representation, extending both into a unified robotic manipulation framework. It departs from prior VLA models (RT-1, RT-2, Octo, OpenVLA) that operate purely on 2D image features by incorporating camera parameters and occupancy supervision for explicit 3D spatial perception. The Modality-Isolation-Mask (MIM) is a novel architectural addition that enables flexible multimodal outputs (actions, images, occupancy) without requiring all modalities at inference time. On the data side, RoboData explicitly addresses the spatial misalignment problem identified in Open X-Embodiment (where RT-1-X underperforms RT-1 due to inconsistent coordinate systems), unifying 10 datasets into a single 3D-aligned training and evaluation space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first generalist policy that simultaneously evaluates across multiple datasets and surpasses per-dataset expert models, offering a more realistic and fair evaluation protocol than the standard single-dataset fine-tuning paradigm. RoboData's 3D space alignment (unifying coordinate systems and action representations across 10 heterogeneous datasets) is a practically important contribution that the field has lacked, and the ablation showing ~16-point performance drops without alignment underscores its necessity. However, the current scale is moderate (70K episodes, primarily simulation, 4B parameters), and the RT-1 evaluation relies on supplemented sensor data, leaving open whether the 3D perception advantage and cross-embodiment generalization hold at internet-scale real-world data. A key open question is whether the occupancy and next-frame prediction auxiliary losses continue to provide gains as the primary action task scales to larger, more diverse real-world datasets.
