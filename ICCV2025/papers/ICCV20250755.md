## Problem

Existing driving world models struggle with two critical deficiencies: instance-level temporal consistency (object colors and textures shift across frames) and spatial geometric fidelity (instances deviate from bounding box control signals and occlusion hierarchies are violated). Prior methods rely on global temporal attention without explicit per-instance control, and lack a proper BEV-to-camera view transformation with depth ordering, making generated videos unreliable for downstream perception, tracking, and planning tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250755/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a rich set of control conditions—3D bounding boxes projected into the camera's first-person view, road maps and lane markings, camera intrinsic/extrinsic parameters, text prompts, per-instance tracking IDs, and optionally an initial frame—all injected via ControlNet into the denoising network. It outputs multi-view driving videos at 16×256×448 resolution (6 camera views × 16 frames per clip), stably generating over 200 frames. The motion map for instance flow is encoded through the same VAE as the video, achieving 8× spatial downsampling to a compact latent representation before injection.

## Method

InstaDrive builds on OpenSora V1.1's ST-DiT backbone with ControlNet (13 duplicated blocks) and introduces two lightweight modules. The Instance Flow Guider computes per-instance positional offsets from the last visible frame (handling occlusion-reappearance), renders them as an RGB motion map (x→R, y→G, z→B), encodes via VAE, and injects via ControlNet so that temporal attention layers can retrieve and propagate instance semantic features (color, texture, category) forward without external feature extractors. The Spatial Geometric Aligner projects 3D bounding boxes to 2D pixel space using camera intrinsics/extrinsics for precise localization, and explicitly encodes occlusion hierarchy by applying Fourier embedding + MLP to the depth (z_c) of each box's eight corner points, then fusing depth-order tokens with box projections via cross-attention. Multi-view consistency is enforced through a parameter-free view-inflated attention that reshapes the spatial dimension to treat all views as a single wide frame.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, InstaDrive achieves an FID of 3.96 and FVD of 38.06, substantially outperforming all baselines on both realism and temporal consistency metrics. In the multi-object tracking task (Table 2), InstaDrive reduces ID switches to 532 (vs. 687 for the oracle real-data baseline) and achieves AMOTA of 0.496, surpassing DriveDreamer2 (AMOTA 0.313, IDS 593). For perception validation (Table 3), InstaDrive reaches 86.38% of oracle NDS (40.51 vs. 46.90), compared to 78.51% for MagicDrive-V2 and 68.00% for Panacea. When used for data augmentation (Table 4), InstaDrive-generated data boosts StreamPETR's NDS by +3.6 points over real-data-only training, outperforming Panacea's +2.3-point gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InstaDrive outperforms all six baselines on both FVD and FID. The closest competitor, DriveDreamer2 [55], achieves FVD 55.7 and FID 11.2, while InstaDrive reduces FVD by 32% (38.06) and FID by 65% (3.96). DriveScape [48] is competitive on FID (8.34) but trails significantly on FVD (76.39 vs. 38.06), indicating weaker temporal consistency. MagicDrive-V2 [10] and Panacea [47] show larger gaps, with FVDs of 94.84 and 139 respectively, and both exhibit visible instance-level color shifts and occlusion errors that InstaDrive avoids. On the downstream MOT task, InstaDrive reduces ID switches by 155 compared to the oracle real-data baseline, whereas DriveDreamer2 only reduces them by 94. For perception, InstaDrive's generated data yields a higher NDS (40.51, 86.38% of oracle) than MagicDrive-V2 (36.82, 78.51%) and Panacea (32.10, 68.00%), confirming superior spatial geometric fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

InstaDrive builds directly on the OpenSora V1.1 stack (ST-DiT backbone, VAE, T5) and adapts ControlNet for multi-condition injection into the diffusion transformer, extending the controllable video generation paradigm to the driving domain. It departs from prior driving world models (MagicDrive-V2, DriveDreamer2, Panacea) that rely on implicit temporal attention for global coherence by introducing explicit instance-level control: the Instance Flow Guider propagates per-instance motion and semantic features across frames without external feature extractors (unlike GEM's DINOv2 dependency), and the Spatial Geometric Aligner uses 3D bounding box projection with Fourier-embedded depth ordering rather than the 2D BEV layouts used by BEVGen and BEVControl. The CARLA-based procedural scenario pipeline is a practical extension that repurposes simulator outputs as control conditions for the generative model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that instance-aware mechanisms can close a meaningful gap between synthetic and real driving data for downstream tasks (perception NDS reaches 86.4% of oracle, and mixed real+generated training outperforms real-only by 3.6 NDS points), suggesting generated data is a viable augmentation source rather than merely a visualization tool. The CARLA integration opens a path toward systematic long-tail scenario generation for safety evaluation, though the pipeline is currently limited to nuScenes-scale scenes and requires 3D bounding box annotations as input. Open questions include whether the instance flow mechanism generalizes to domains without clean track IDs (e.g., crowded pedestrian scenes), how performance scales to longer video horizons beyond ~200 frames, and whether the approach transfers to other sensor modalities or datasets without retraining.
