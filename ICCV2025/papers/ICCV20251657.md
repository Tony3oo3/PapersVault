## Problem

Existing universal 3D avatar prior models treat the face and hair as a holistic, inseparable entity, making hairstyle transfer and compositional editing nontrivial. A fundamental obstacle is the absence of paired multi-view captures of the same person with and without hair, which are essential for learning disentangled face and hair representations. Without such data, models cannot naturally separate facial identity from hairstyle, limiting flexibility in avatar customization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251657/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes UV-mapped geometry and albedo texture maps extracted from tracked meshes (FLAME topology), along with gaze direction, from multi-view studio captures (110 cameras, 460 LEDs at 4096×2668). It produces two independent sets of relightable 3D Gaussians (face and hair) plus a predicted bald head mesh, enabling face and hair components to be independently controlled or swapped across identities. For few-shot personalization, a single monocular video suffices as input, with only the last hypernetwork layer fine-tuned in ~1 hour on one GPU.

## Method

HairCUP introduces explicit compositionality as an inductive bias by learning separate latent spaces for face and hair, enabled by a synthetic bald image pipeline that registers a bald mesh per subject and optimizes its UV texture via reconstruction loss on visible face regions plus SDS loss with a diffusion prior to hallucinate the occluded scalp. The architecture employs separate expression and hair-motion encoders (operating on UV-masked delta geometry), separate identity-conditioned hypernetworks generating bias maps for face and hair Gaussian decoders, and a bald geometry predictor that anchors all Gaussians to a shared scalp surface. Training uses a compositional rendering loss (face+hair vs. original), a face-only rendering loss (face vs. synthetic bald image), and a boundary-free segmentation loss that stops supervising near the face-hair boundary to allow natural blending. Anchoring hair Gaussians to the bald mesh rather than the haired mesh is critical for transfer, as it prevents hairstyle-specific offsets from being baked into the geometry, enabling seamless hairstyle swapping across avatars with different head shapes.

## Results

On 8 unseen identities evaluated with monocular front-view head rotation sequences, HairCUP (fine-tuned) achieves L1 = 0.0223, PSNR = 27.040 dB, SSIM = 0.817, and LPIPS = 0.0131, outperforming DELTA on all four metrics. The PSNR improvement of ~3.3 dB and the near-halving of LPIPS (0.0131 vs. 0.0241) indicate substantially better perceptual fidelity in both facial and hair regions. Qualitative comparisons further show that HairCUP produces more natural hairstyle transfer with seamless face-hair blending, whereas DELTA exhibits rigid segmentation boundaries and blocky facial artifacts from its parametric face model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DELTA [10], HairCUP wins decisively on every quantitative metric: L1 drops from 0.0344 to 0.0223, PSNR rises from 23.775 to 27.040 dB, SSIM improves from 0.790 to 0.817, and LPIPS nearly halves from 0.0241 to 0.0131. Qualitatively, DELTA's reliance on a parametric face mesh produces blocky facial artifacts, and its LBS-based hair deformation degrades fine hair details; its hairstyle transfer also enforces rigid face-hair boundaries via segmentation masks, yielding unnatural transitions. HairCUP's boundary-free segmentation loss and fully Gaussian representation produce smoother, more photorealistic results in both single-avatar reconstruction and cross-avatar hairstyle swapping. Against URAvatar [26] (the holistic prior that HairCUP extends), the comparison is qualitative: during avatar interpolation, URAvatar's entangled face-hair representation causes ears to morph into hair and distort facial structure, while HairCUP's disentangled latent spaces yield smooth, semantically coherent transitions in both face-only and hair-only interpolation, confirming the benefit of explicit compositionality.

## Relation to Prior Work

HairCUP extends URAvatar's universal relightable Gaussian codec avatar framework by replacing its holistic face-and-hair modeling with explicitly disentangled face and hair latent spaces, each conditioned on separate identity hypernetworks. It departs from prior compositional avatar methods (DELTA, MeGA) that rely on hybrid mesh+NeRF representations and per-subject optimization, instead learning a single universal prior over fully Gaussian-based avatars. The synthetic bald image generation pipeline—combining a registered bald mesh, SDS-guided diffusion inpainting, and compositing—repurposes 2D diffusion priors to solve the 3D paired-data scarcity problem that has blocked face-hair disentanglement in universal priors. The boundary-free segmentation loss adapts GALA's Gaussian segmentation idea to enforce soft face-hair transitions rather than hard boundaries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit compositionality in a universal 3D Gaussian avatar prior enables practical hairstyle transfer and dehairing without per-subject optimization, a capability that holistic priors like URAvatar cannot support. The synthetic bald data pipeline is a reusable recipe for creating paired supervision from existing studio captures, potentially applicable beyond avatars. However, limitations remain: large hair dynamics are unaddressed, the single-frame bald texture optimization introduces color inconsistencies, and occluded scalp regions resist relighting. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to highly dynamic or voluminous hairstyles, and whether the fixed UV-map anchoring can be relaxed to handle extreme geometric variation across subjects.
