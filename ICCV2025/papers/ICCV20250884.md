## Problem

Existing visual prompt tuning (VPT) methods insert learnable prompt tokens into frozen Vision Transformers with a fixed distribution (shallow or deep) across all downstream tasks, ignoring that the importance of each Transformer block varies significantly by task. This paper identifies that adaptively optimizing the prompt distribution (ADO) is critical but its formal definition and optimization strategy remain unexplored, particularly whether distribution adjustments should be one-shot or iterative relative to prompt updates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250884/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images and produces class labels for classification (primary), bounding boxes for detection (COCO), and pixel-level masks for segmentation (ADE20K). The key structural detail is that a fixed set of N lightweight prompt tokens is inserted into the input of selected Transformer blocks of a frozen ViT; the distribution of which blocks receive prompts is itself a learned, task-specific variable that changes during training via prompt relocation, rather than being predetermined.

## Method

PRO-VPT formulates ADO and VPT as a nested optimization problem where the outer loop optimizes the prompt distribution D and the inner loop tunes the prompt values P, creating an iterative closed loop of distribution adjustment → prompt tuning → evaluation → new adjustment. The prompt relocation (PR) strategy decomposes each relocation into two steps: (1) pruning, which identifies idle prompts in prompt-saturated blocks using an idleness score approximated via first-order Taylor expansion of the loss (Ĩ_k ≈ g_k^T p_k), and (2) allocation, which uses PPO reinforcement learning to assign the freed prompt to the most prompt-needed block, with the reward approximated by the loss improvement minus the idleness score. This decomposition reduces the RL action space from L² (all block pairs) to L (candidate destination blocks only), and the non-stationary nature of the environment motivates using full RL (PPO) rather than simpler bandit methods. The framework is backbone- and pre-training-agnostic, demonstrated on ViT-B/L/H and Swin-B with supervised, MAE, and MoCo-v3 pre-training.

## Results

On VTAB-1k (19 classification tasks), PRO-VPT achieves a state-of-the-art 78.0% global average accuracy with 0.61M trainable parameters, surpassing the previous best prompt-based method iVPT (77.1%) and the best adapter-based method Adapter+ (77.6%). On the FGVC benchmark (5 fine-grained datasets), PRO-VPT reaches 91.7% average accuracy with 0.86M parameters, outperforming Adapter+ and SPT-Deep (both 91.4%). The method yields consistent gains of +1.6 pp over VPT-Deep on VTAB-1k and +2.0 pp on FGVC, while also improving over the one-shot optimization method NOAH by +2.5 pp on VTAB-1k.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRO-VPT outperforms all 10 compared baselines on both VTAB-1k and FGVC. Against the direct VPT baseline [22], it gains +1.6 pp on VTAB-1k (78.0% vs. 76.4%) and +2.0 pp on FGVC (91.7% vs. 89.7%), confirming the benefit of adaptive distribution over fixed deep prompting. It surpasses the previous best prompt-based method iVPT [71] by 0.9 pp on VTAB-1k (78.0% vs. 77.1%) and 1.8 pp on FGVC (91.7% vs. 89.9%), and beats the one-shot prompt search method NOAH [67] by a substantial 2.5 pp on VTAB-1k (78.0% vs. 75.5%). Among adapter-based methods, PRO-VPT edges out Adapter+ [51] by 0.4 pp on VTAB-1k (78.0% vs. 77.6%) and 0.3 pp on FGVC (91.7% vs. 91.4%), establishing prompt-based methods as competitive with or superior to adapter approaches. It also leads SPT-Deep [58] by 2.4 pp on VTAB-1k and 0.3 pp on FGVC, and outperforms reparameterization-based methods (LoRA [20] at 75.6%, FacT-TK [23] at 76.5%, SSF [34] at 75.7%, Consolidator [15] at 76.5%, SPT-Adapter [16] at 76.4%) by 1.4–2.5 pp on VTAB-1k.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) by Jia et al. (ECCV 2022), which inserts learnable prompt tokens into frozen ViT blocks with a fixed distribution (shallow or deep). It draws conceptual inspiration from layer-wise fine-tuning literature (SpotTune, AutoLR, Surgical Fine-Tuning) that demonstrates per-block importance varies across tasks, and repurposes this insight for the prompt-tuning setting. Unlike prior pruning-based adaptive methods (E²VPT, NOAH) that only remove redundant prompts from a hyper-distribution, PRO-VPT introduces a relocation mechanism that simultaneously addresses prompt-saturated and prompt-deficient blocks, framed within a novel nested optimization formulation where distribution adjustment and prompt tuning are co-optimized iteratively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that prompt distribution is a critical, previously underexplored degree of freedom in VPT, and provides a principled nested optimization framework that could accommodate various adjustment strategies beyond the specific PR strategy proposed. The consistent gains across ViT scales, Swin-B, and multiple pre-training paradigms (supervised, MAE, MoCo-v3) suggest the approach is broadly applicable. Open questions include whether the RL-based allocation component (PPO) is truly necessary versus simpler heuristics, the computational overhead of iterative relocation during training, and whether the nested optimization formulation generalizes to other PEFT paradigms beyond prompt-based methods.
