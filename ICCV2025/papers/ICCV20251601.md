## Problem

Existing video LLMs handle temporal grounding and textual response in a coupled manner, mixing special timestamp tokens with text generation without clear logical structure. More critically, these tokens focus solely on timestamp representation while overlooking event-level visual semantics that should serve as crucial context for subsequent answer generation. This paper addresses both issues from a factorized learning perspective, decoupling the two tasks while preserving their inherent dependency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251601/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a video (encoded via ViT-G/14 + Q-Former into one token per frame) and a textual question, producing a two-stage autoregressive response: first a sequence of <evi> evidence tokens that localize temporal intervals and aggregate visual semantics from salient frames, followed by an interleaved text-evidence answer where <evi> tokens are regenerated to reference the previously grounded evidence. Exact timestamps are recovered from frame indices of high-similarity video tokens rather than being generated as discrete text tokens.

## Method

D²VLM decomposes generation into a pure grounding stage (producing <evi> tokens that capture both temporal location and event-level visual semantics via similarity-based aggregation from salient frame tokens) and an interleaved text-evidence answering stage, with a consistency loss (L_cons) enforcing alignment between evidence tokens across stages. The training objective combines standard SFT loss, a binary cross-entropy grounding loss on frame-level similarity, and the consistency constraint. On top of this, Factorized Preference Optimization (FPO) extends DPO by adding an explicit temporal grounding probability term—computed as the product of per-frame similarity scores over the grounded interval—into the log-probability of the response, enabling preference learning over both grounding accuracy and textual quality. A synthetic data pipeline generates dispreferred samples by applying factorized perturbations (temporal shifts, event deletion/addition, semantic distortion, repetition) at the sub-video event level, providing structured preference pairs without manual annotation.

## Results

D²VLM-3.8B achieves 42.3% average F1 on E.T. Bench Grounding, surpassing the strongest baseline Qwen2.5-VL-7B (35.3%) by 7.0 points, and 37.5% average F1 / 21.8% average Sim on E.T. Bench Dense Captioning. On Charades-STA, it reaches 50.3% R@1 (IoU=0.5) and 26.0% R@1 (IoU=0.7), outperforming the previous best VideoChat-T-7B (48.7% / 24.0%). On YouCook2, it attains 26.4% F1, 10.6 CIDEr, and 3.2 SODA_c, exceeding TRACE-7B by 4.0% F1 and 2.5 CIDEr. Notably, the 3.8B model outperforms most 7B–13B competitors across all four benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D²VLM-3.8B wins against all 15 baselines on every benchmark. On E.T. Bench Grounding, it leads Qwen2.5-VL-7B (the strongest 7B baseline) by 7.0% Avg F1 and E.T.Chat-3.8B (same 3.8B base model) by 8.8%, with the largest gap on the Episodic Memory sub-task (14.4 vs. 9.3 for Qwen2.5-VL). On E.T. Bench Dense Captioning, it surpasses VTG-LLM-7B by 7.0% Avg F1 and 5.3% Avg Sim, and outperforms LITA-13B (a 13B model) by 7.1% Avg F1. On Charades-STA, D²VLM beats VideoChat-T-7B (ICLR'25) by 1.6% R@1(IoU=0.5) and E.T.Chat-3.8B by 4.4%, while on YouCook2 it exceeds TRACE-7B (ICLR'25) by 4.0% F1, 2.5 CIDEr, and 1.0 SODA_c. The consistent margin over same-size (3.8B) and larger (7B–13B) models highlights both the effectiveness and efficiency of the factorized learning design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the E.T. Bench framework (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2024) for both architecture initialization and evaluation, and extends DPO (Rafailov et al., NeurIPS 2023) by incorporating explicit probabilistic temporal grounding into the preference optimization objective. The key conceptual departure from prior video LLMs (TimeChat, VTG-LLM, LITA, etc.) is the factorized learning perspective: rather than interleaving special timestamp tokens with text tokens in a coupled generation sequence, D²VLM enforces a strict 'ground first, then answer with evidence referencing' decomposition, and introduces evidence tokens that capture event-level visual semantics rather than merely encoding timestamps. The factorized preference data synthesis pipeline is also novel, addressing the absence of preference datasets that explicitly account for temporal grounding errors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a structural weakness in how video LLMs learn temporal grounding and textual response simultaneously, offering a principled decomposition that yields consistent gains across grounding and captioning tasks with a lightweight 3.8B model. The factorized preference optimization (FPO) is the first to incorporate explicit temporal grounding probability into a DPO-style objective, opening a path for preference-based alignment in temporally grounded video understanding. However, limitations remain: episodic memory F1 is still only 14.4%, the synthetic preference data generates only negative samples (no diverse positive augmentation), and the approach is validated primarily on a single base model and training dataset, leaving open whether the factorized paradigm generalizes across architectures and scales.
