## Problem

Inference-time scaling for text-to-image diffusion models has been limited to best-of-N sampling, where many random images are generated and a judge selects the best one. Diffusion Transformers condition solely on the input prompt and cannot reference or learn from their own past generations, preventing them from iteratively correcting errors in object count, spatial positioning, or attribute binding. This paper asks whether equipping DiTs with in-context reflection—using past generations and natural language feedback as conditioning—can surpass naive random sampling for inference-time scaling.

## Input / Output

At each iteration, the DiT receives the original text prompt plus up to K=3 past image-feedback pairs as additional context. Past images are encoded by SigLIP-Large into 24×24 feature maps, downsampled to 8×8 (64 tokens per image), and feedback text is encoded by Gemma2-2B; both are concatenated and processed by a 2-layer Context Transformer before being appended to the prompt embeddings in the DiT's cross-attention layers. The output is a single refined image per iteration, with the full trajectory of up to N=20 images returned for selection. The VLM judge (Qwen2.5-VL 3B) produces concise structured feedback (e.g., 'There should be 3 cats, but only 2 exist') or null feedback to signal convergence.

## Method

Reflect-DiT introduces a verification-reflection loop between a fine-tuned VLM judge and a modified SANA-1.0-1.6B DiT. The key architectural addition is a lightweight 2-layer Context Transformer that fuses vision embeddings (from SigLIP-Large, downsampled to 8×8) and text feedback embeddings (from Gemma2-2B) into a unified context representation, which is concatenated after the standard prompt embeddings and injected into the DiT's cross-attention layers. Training uses the flow-matching objective with a logit-normal weighting scheme, fine-tuning the DiT and Context Transformer end-to-end for 5,000 steps (lr 1e-5, batch 48) on 78.5k synthetic image-feedback pairs generated from 6,000 GenEval-style prompts. The VLM judge is separately fine-tuned on structured feedback templates derived from object-detection ground truth. At inference, the loop iterates: generate an image, obtain VLM feedback, update the reflection context (stochastically sampling K=3 pairs when context exceeds the limit), and regenerate—stopping when the VLM reports no further issues or N=20 iterations are reached.

## Results

On the GenEval benchmark, Reflect-DiT (SANA-1.0-1.6B + 0.1B Context Transformer, N=20) achieves a new state-of-the-art overall score of 0.81, surpassing the previous best of 0.80 (SANA-1.5-4.8B with N=2048 best-of-N sampling) while using 102× fewer samples. Compared to the SANA-1.0-1.6B baseline (0.62), Reflect-DiT yields a +0.19 overall gain, with particularly large improvements in Position (+0.47), Counting (+0.22), and Two-object composition (+0.13). On DPG-Bench, Reflect-DiT improves the SANA-1.0 baseline from 84.1 to 86.1 (All) and from 24.2 to 51.8 on the hardest subset (Hard-56), a +27.6 point gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Reflect-DiT (0.81) outperforms all listed baselines on GenEval overall, including the much larger SANA-1.5-4.8B + Best-of-2048 (0.80), SD3 (0.74), DALLE 3 (0.67), Flux-Dev (0.68), and SDXL (0.55). Against the same-size SANA-1.0-1.6B + Best-of-20 baseline (0.75), Reflect-DiT gains +0.06 overall, with the largest per-category gains in Position (+0.12) and Counting (+0.07). On DPG-Bench, Reflect-DiT (86.1 All / 51.8 Hard-56) beats SANA-1.5 (85.0), SD3-Medium (84.1), DALLE 3 (83.5), and Flux-Dev (84.0), with a particularly large +27.6 point margin over the SANA-1.0 baseline on the hardest prompts. In the finetuning comparison (Table 2), Reflect-DiT with only 4 samples (0.776) surpasses both SFT (0.767) and Diffusion-DPO (0.765) with 20 samples, saving 80% of the inference compute budget. In the human evaluation on PartiPrompts, Reflect-DiT is preferred 53.3% of the time versus 32.3% for best-of-N sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

Reflect-DiT draws a direct conceptual bridge from LLM inference-time scaling (particularly DeepSeek-R1's self-verification and reflection) to text-to-image diffusion models, where prior work has been limited to naive best-of-N sampling (SANA-1.5, Ma et al.) or scaling denoising steps. It departs from LLM-agent-based self-correction frameworks (SLD, GenArtist) that rely on predefined operation sets, and from controllable generation methods (ControlNet, GLIGEN) that use conditioning for spatial/subject control rather than iterative feedback. The key architectural novelty is a lightweight Context Transformer that fuses vision and text feedback embeddings into the DiT's cross-attention, enabling the diffusion model to condition on its own past generations and natural-language critiques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100× reduction in samples needed to match or exceed prior SOTA (20 vs. 2048) makes inference-time scaling practically viable for real-world deployment, and the approach is model-agnostic in principle. However, the method inherits limitations from both the base DiT and the VLM judge (hallucinated feedback, poor small-object detection), and the strongest evidence comes from GenEval's structured prompts (counting, positioning, attributes) rather than open-ended generation. Open questions include whether the reflection loop generalizes to aesthetic or compositional quality, whether the fine-tuned VLM judge can be replaced by a frozen frontier model, and whether the approach scales to higher-resolution or video generation.
