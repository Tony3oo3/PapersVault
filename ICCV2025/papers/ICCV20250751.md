## Problem

Open-Vocabulary Multi-Object Tracking (OVMOT) lacks a continuously annotated video training dataset: the TAO benchmark provides annotations only every 30 frames, forcing prior methods to rely on synthetic image pairs or IoU-based pseudo-labels that fail to capture true temporal motion and appearance evolution. This data gap, combined with the unreliable equal-weight fusion of appearance, location, and semantic cues in prior work (e.g., SLAck), limits tracking robustness especially for novel categories.

## Input / Output

The method consumes video frames processed by a frozen Faster R-CNN (ResNet-50) open-vocabulary detector trained on LVIS base classes, extracting per-object appearance embeddings (RoI features through a conv+MLP head), location embeddings (normalized bounding-box coordinates through a projection layer), and semantic embeddings (distilled CLIP text+image fusion). At inference, up to 80 class-agnostic NMS-filtered detections per frame are associated across time using bisoftmax matching (threshold 0.35, memory queue 30), producing continuous trajectories with bounding boxes, identities, and category labels evaluated via TETA (LocA, ClsA, AssocA) on base and novel classes separately.

## Method

COVTrack introduces a dual-perspective confidence mechanism to adaptively fuse three tracking cues. Intra-frame confidence is computed by a Self-attentive Gated Network (SGN) that takes concatenated appearance, location, and semantic embeddings and outputs sigmoid-gated weights for location and semantic cues, learning their relative reliability within a single frame. Inter-frame confidence is derived from a cycle-consistency self-correction: pairwise similarity matrices between consecutive frames are temperature-scaled, softmax-normalized, and self-multiplied (S·Sᵀ), with diagonal entries yielding per-object temporal stability scores for each cue. During fusion, location and semantic features are scaled by their product of intra- and inter-frame confidences, concatenated with the appearance feature, and passed through a Multi-cue Aggregation Network (MAN); the result is then adaptively blended with the raw appearance embedding using the appearance's own inter-frame confidence as a gate. All five confidence scores are trained end-to-end via a cross-entropy association loss on the final fused similarity matrix, requiring no explicit supervision for the gating weights. The framework is trained on the newly constructed C-TAO dataset, a frame-by-frame re-annotation of TAO that increases annotated frames by over 26× and reduces inter-frame object displacement from 101 to 6.6 pixels.

## Results

On the TAO validation set, COVTrack achieves a novel TETA of 34.3 and base TETA of 39.6, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art SLAck (31.1 / 37.2) by 3.2 and 2.4 points respectively. The association accuracy gains are particularly pronounced: 41.3 novel AssocA and 42.0 base AssocA, improving over SLAck by 3.5 and 4.4 points. On the TAO test set, COVTrack reaches 28.9 novel TETA and 37.9 base TETA, with a 6.5-point improvement in base AssocA (42.1 vs. 35.6) over SLAck, demonstrating strong generalization to unseen evaluation data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COVTrack outperforms all nine baselines on both TAO validation and test sets. Against the closest OVMOT competitor SLAck (ECCV 2024), COVTrack gains +3.2 novel TETA and +4.4 base AssocA on validation, and +1.8 novel TETA and +6.5 base AssocA on test, with the largest gap appearing in association accuracy where the multi-cue confidence mechanism provides the most benefit. Compared to OVTrack (CVPR 2023), the improvement is even larger: +6.5 novel TETA and +7.4 novel AssocA on validation, confirming that the C-TAO continuous dataset plus adaptive fusion substantially surpasses the appearance-only, image-pair-trained approach. MASA (CVPR 2024), which learns universal appearance via unlabeled image pairs, is outperformed by +4.3 novel TETA and +6.9 base AssocA on validation. Closed-set MOT baselines (ByteTrack, OC-SORT, DeepSORT, Tracktor++) trail significantly, with OC-SORT achieving only 23.7 novel TETA versus COVTrack's 34.3, underscoring the difficulty of open-vocabulary tracking. Notably, COVTrack also improves ClsA for novel classes (3.5 vs. SLAck's 1.3 on validation), suggesting the semantic cue with confidence gating helps classification of rare categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the OVMOT line initiated by OVTrack (CVPR 2023), which relied solely on appearance features from synthetic image pairs (LVIS), and SLAck (ECCV 2024), which added semantic and location cues but fused them via simple summation. The key architectural departure is a dual-perspective confidence estimation strategy (intra-frame SGN gating + inter-frame cycle-consistency self-correction) that adaptively weights appearance, location, and semantic cues rather than treating them equally. The dataset contribution—C-TAO, a frame-by-frame re-annotation of TAO—addresses a gap both prior works worked around: OVTrack avoided video continuity entirely, and SLAck approximated it with IoU-based pseudo-labels that lack temporal consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

C-TAO is the first continuously annotated training dataset for OVMOT, and the empirical finding that even minimal continuity (P-TAO, one extra frame per TAO interval) yields most of the benefit (13.7% AssocA gain for OVTrack) provides a practical, low-cost path for the community to improve tracking without full re-annotation. The confidence-based fusion is a more principled alternative to SLAck's summation, and the ablations clearly isolate each component's contribution. Open questions include whether the cycle-consistency mechanism generalizes to longer temporal gaps or different backbones, and whether the frozen-detector assumption limits ceiling performance as OV detectors improve.
