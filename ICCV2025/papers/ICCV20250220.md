## Problem

Existing frequency-domain pose estimation methods using periodic patterns suffer from significant spectral leakage bias when only a few pattern periods are visible in the image, making frequency and phase estimates inaccurate and highly sensitive to the pattern's initial frequency and phase. This limits their practical operating range and prevents reliable absolute positioning or large-displacement measurement. Additionally, spatial-domain methods like PnP degrade under defocus and are incompatible with the telecentric lenses required in high-precision micro/nano metrology.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single grayscale image (640×480 pixels, 9.9 μm pixel pitch) of a periodic checkerboard or grid pattern (pitches of 150, 450, or 750 μm) captured through a low-distortion industrial lens (28 mm focal length) under quasi-orthographic projection. It outputs the full 6DoF pose: in-plane translations (t_x, t_y), out-of-plane translation (t_z), in-plane rotation (α), and two out-of-plane rotations (β, γ), achieving nanometer-level translation and microradian-level rotation accuracy. The pattern must contain at least ~6 periods along one axis for reliable estimation.

## Method

The core contribution is a 2D interpolated DFT (2D-IpDFT) algorithm that estimates the frequency and phase of two orthogonal sinusoidal components in the pattern image. It applies a 2D maximum sidelobe decay window (Hanning) to the sampled image, then evaluates the DFT at three adjacent bins (k−1, k, k+1) around each spectral peak in both dimensions. By formulating a linear system from these DFT values and enforcing a zero-determinant condition, it solves for the exact (non-integer) frequency parameters while accounting for both positive and negative frequency contributions, thereby eliminating the spectral leakage bias that dominates existing methods. The extracted frequencies and phases are then mapped analytically to the 6DoF pose through closed-form equations derived under the quasi-orthographic projection model, where the in-plane rotation follows from a ratio of frequency components, out-of-plane rotations from a quadratic in a depth-dependent parameter η, and translations from the phase terms.

## Results

On single-axis motion with a 450 μm checkerboard pattern, the proposed 2D-IpDFT (Hanning window) achieves RMSE of 21 nm (t_x), 27 nm (t_y), 244 nm (t_z), 8 μrad (R_z), 15 μrad (R_y), and 15 μrad (R_x). For 2D 'ICCV' trajectory tracking, in-plane accuracy reaches 5 nm (t_x) and 7 nm (t_y), and for a 3D helical trajectory, 34 nm (t_x), 37 nm (t_y), and 1664 nm (t_z). The method also maintains sub-500 nm in-plane accuracy over a ±1.5 mm defocus range and achieves 6.0 nm / 8.9 nm / 108.6 nm RMSE in closed-loop vision-based position control.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines: QSE [20], linear regression (André et al. [2]), and zero-padding at 4× and 8× signal length. The proposed 2D-IpDFT outperforms all baselines by a wide margin on every metric and every experimental condition. On single-axis motion, it reduces t_x RMSE from 152–493 nm (baselines) to 21 nm, and R_z from 86–497 μrad to 8 μrad. On the 'ICCV' 2D trajectory, it achieves 5 nm / 7 nm versus 132–354 nm / 430–577 nm for baselines — roughly a 25–70× improvement. The advantage is most dramatic for out-of-plane rotation (R_x, R_y), where linear regression and QSE produce errors of 1000s of μrad while the proposed method stays at 15 μrad. The method also wins under defocus blur (197 nm vs. 342–653 nm for light blur; 348 nm vs. 1037–5668 nm for heavy blur) and across all pattern pitches (6.4 to 32 periods), confirming that its spectral-leakage mitigation is the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on André et al. (2022), which established the mathematical framework linking spectral parameters (frequency and phase) of periodic patterns to 6DoF pose under quasi-orthographic projection. The key departure is the proposed 2D-IpDFT algorithm, which addresses the critical limitation of existing frequency-domain methods—spectral leakage bias when only a few pattern periods are visible in the image—by incorporating negative frequency compensation and windowing. The paper also positions itself against spatial-domain approaches (PnP, template matching, optical flow) that suffer from defocus sensitivity and incompatibility with telecentric lenses, and against other frequency-domain estimators (QSE, linear regression, zero-padding) that remain biased under the same low-period conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstrated nanometer-level translation and microradian-level rotation accuracy on a low-cost industrial camera setup makes high-precision 6DoF pose estimation accessible for semiconductor alignment, nanoscale manipulation, and micro-robotics without expensive interferometric hardware. The method's robustness across a wide defocus range (±1.5 mm) and its computational efficiency (~12 ms per frame) suggest practical viability for real-time closed-loop control, as validated by the feedback control experiment. Open questions include generalization to non-periodic or irregular patterns, performance under more complex scene conditions (occlusion, illumination variation), and whether the quasi-orthographic assumption limits applicability to shorter working distances.
