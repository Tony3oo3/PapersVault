## Problem

Depth-based multi-view 3D object detection transformers suffer from three interrelated quality issues: (1) reliance on high-level image features for depth prediction causes boundary discontinuity and poor small-object discrimination due to lost local details; (2) sparse projected LiDAR supervision becomes increasingly insufficient at higher input resolutions, leaving distant regions under-constrained; and (3) independent per-view feature extraction neglects cross-view consistency and scale invariance, degrading depth reliability across cameras.

## Input / Output

The method consumes six calibrated surround-view RGB images (resolutions from 256×704 up to 640×1600) along with their camera intrinsics and extrinsics, plus LiDAR point clouds used solely as training supervision. It produces per-pixel depth maps at multiple spatial scales, which are back-projected into 3D LiDAR coordinates to form positional embeddings; these depth-aware 3D features are then fed to a StreamPETR-style transformer decoder that outputs 3D bounding boxes (10 nuScenes categories). During training, dense pseudo relative-depth labels from DepthAnything supplement the sparse LiDAR depth to provide implicit distribution supervision, particularly benefiting long-range detection beyond 40 m.

## Method

FreqPDE introduces three coupled modules. The Frequency-aware Spatial Pyramid Encoder (FSPE) replaces FPN's simple interpolation with a wavelet-based fusion: low-level features are decomposed via DWT into LL/LH/HL/HH sub-bands, the LL component is augmented with adaptively filtered low-frequency semantics from high-level features (learned spatially-varying low-pass kernels), and the result is reconstructed via iDWT to preserve high-frequency edges while injecting global context. The Cross-view Scale-invariant Depth Predictor (CSDP) performs hierarchical multi-scale depth estimation, inserting a Cross-view Width Attention block to enforce consistency in overlapping regions between adjacent cameras and an Efficient Channel Attention block conditioned on scaled camera intrinsics to ensure scale invariance; depth is predicted as a fusion of regressed and categorical (pixel-wise refined bin) components. The Positional Depth Encoder (PDE) back-projects the predicted depth through camera parameters into 3D LiDAR space, applies sine positional encoding, and adds it element-wise to image features to form depth-aware 3D features for the transformer query decoder. Training uses a hybrid loss combining smooth-L1 on sparse LiDAR depth and MSE on mean-variance-normalized reciprocal depth against DepthAnything pseudo labels, balancing explicit metric and implicit distribution supervision.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, FreqPDE achieves 56.2% NDS and 46.3% mAP with ResNet50 (256×704 input), surpassing the StreamPETR baseline by 1.2% NDS and 1.3% mAP. With the stronger VoVNet-99 backbone at 320×800 input, performance improves to 58.6% NDS and 50.6% mAP (+1.4% NDS, +2.4% mAP over baseline). On the nuScenes test set at 640×1600 resolution, FreqPDE reaches 64.2% NDS and 56.0% mAP, setting a new state-of-the-art among camera-only methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreqPDE outperforms all 12 compared baselines on both NDS and mAP across every backbone/resolution setting. Against the direct baseline StreamPETR [50], FreqPDE gains +1.2% NDS / +1.3% mAP (ResNet50), +1.4% NDS / +2.4% mAP (V2-99), and +0.9% NDS / +1.5% mAP (ResNet101). Among the strongest competitors at the ResNet50 level, FreqPDE beats SparseBEV [30] by 0.4% NDS and Sparse4Dv2 [29] by 2.4% NDS. At the high-resolution ResNet101 setting, FreqPDE surpasses Far3D [19] (the prior SOTA) by 0.7% NDS and 0.9% mAP. On the nuScenes test set (640×1600, V2-99), FreqPDE leads Sparse4Dv2 [29] by 0.4% NDS, SparseBEV [30] by 0.6% NDS, and BEVDepth [25] by 4.2% NDS, confirming consistent gains over both BEV-based and transformer-based paradigms.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreqPDE builds directly on the StreamPETR transformer decoder architecture and the 3DPPE positional encoding scheme, but rethinks how 2D image features are prepared before depth prediction and 3D feature construction. Its core departure from prior depth-based detectors (BEVDepth, CaDDN, BEVDet) is the frequency-domain perspective on feature pyramid construction: rather than the standard FPN top-down interpolation that smooths boundaries, FSPE uses Discrete Wavelet Transform to losslessly preserve high-frequency edge details while adaptively extracting low-frequency semantics via learned spatial-variant low-pass filters. The cross-view width attention and camera-aware channel attention mechanisms extend the independent per-view processing paradigm in prior work by explicitly modeling inter-view consistency and scale invariance during depth prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-identified bottleneck in depth-based 3D detection—sparse LiDAR supervision and loss of high-frequency details during downsampling—through a combination of frequency-aware feature engineering and hybrid supervision (sparse metric + dense pseudo depth from DepthAnything). The practical finding that dense pseudo labels yield substantially larger gains at long range (>40 m: +1.9% NDS vs. +0.6% for sparse alone) provides a concrete recipe for improving distant-object detection without additional sensor data. Limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (nuScenes), a ~20 MB / 20 ms computational overhead for the full model, and dependence on an external foundation model for pseudo labels. Open questions include whether the frequency-aware pyramid generalizes to other 3D perception tasks (segmentation, tracking) and whether the cross-view attention mechanism scales gracefully beyond six cameras.
