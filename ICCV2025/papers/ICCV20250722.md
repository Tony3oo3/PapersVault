## Problem

All existing vectorized online mapping methods require ground-truth HD maps for supervised training, which are expensive to produce, labor-intensive to annotate, and often lack geographic diversity needed for reliable generalization. This paper asks whether online mapping models can be trained entirely from unlabeled sensor data by generating pseudo-labels through road surface reconstruction, eliminating the HD map dependency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250722/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pseudo-label generation pipeline consumes multi-view camera images, a pre-trained Mask2Former 2D segmentation (trained on Mapillary Vistas V2), ego/sensor poses, and optionally LiDAR for elevation supervision. It produces vectorized map elements (lane dividers, road boundaries, pedestrian crossings) as 2D polylines/polygons in BEV, but these are inherently incomplete with masked regions covering only ~40% (single-trip) to ~65% (multi-trip) of the BEV range. The downstream online model is camera-only at inference (ResNet-50 backbone, 6 cameras) and predicts the same vectorized elements, with LiDAR used only for auxiliary depth supervision during training.

## Method

PseudoMapTrainer generates pseudo-labels by modeling the road surface as a meshgrid of flat Gaussian surfels (3D Gaussian splatting with scaling restricted to Diag([s_x, s_y, 0])), initialized along vehicle trajectories and optimized to match both RGB and semantic camera observations. After optimization, a virtual orthographic BEV camera renders a semantic segmentation at each ego pose, which is postprocessed (morphological filtering, skeletonization, RDP vectorization) into vectorized map elements. The key training contribution is a mask-aware one-to-many assignment: predictions are split at BEV mask boundaries into subsegments, and a binary integer linear program jointly solves one-to-one and one-to-many matching between prediction subsegments and pseudo-label elements. The loss combines a differentiable rendering loss (Dice on aggregated rasterizations of all matched pseudo-labels), a self-supervised direction loss, and a PV segmentation loss derived directly from the 2D segmentation rather than projected 2D GT maps, which avoids misalignment. This enables training on low-coverage samples (τ_m=30%) that would otherwise be discarded, and supports semi-supervised pre-training on large-scale pseudo-labeled data followed by fine-tuning on limited ground-truth labels.

## Results

On the geographically disjoint nuScenes validation split, PseudoMapTrainer (multi-trip pseudo-labels) achieves a mean AP of 13.2 (ped. 18.1, div. 4.1, bdry. 17.4), outperforming VectorMapNet trained on ground truth (mean 14.0) on pedestrian crossings and road boundaries despite never seeing a ground-truth map. The best ground-truth-trained baseline, StreamMapNet, reaches a mean AP of 26.1, leaving a notable gap—particularly for lane dividers (4.1 vs. 23.0). The mask-aware assignment and loss contribute the largest single gain in the ablation, lifting single-trip mean AP from 6.4 to 8.2. In semi-supervised settings, pre-training on multi-trip pseudo-labels followed by fine-tuning on a fraction of ground-truth labels yields substantial improvements over purely supervised training, especially when fewer than 20% of labels are available.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PseudoMapTrainer (multi-trip) beats VectorMapNet [26] on pedestrian crossings (18.1 vs. 13.7) and road boundaries (17.4 vs. 14.9) despite being trained exclusively on pseudo-labels, but falls well short on lane dividers (4.1 vs. 13.5), dragging the mean AP below VectorMapNet's 14.0. Against MapTR [19] and MapVR [51] (ground-truth trained, mean 19.0 and 20.3), PseudoMapTrainer trails by 5.8 and 7.1 mean AP points respectively, with the gap concentrated in lane dividers. The strongest ground-truth baseline, StreamMapNet [50] (mean 26.1), outperforms PseudoMapTrainer by 12.9 mean AP, reflecting the inherent quality gap between pseudo-labels and curated HD maps. Compared to naive MapTRv2 [20] trained on the same pseudo-labels, PseudoMapTrainer's mask-aware assignment and loss yield consistent gains: +1.5 mean AP for single-trip (6.7→8.2) and +2.7 for multi-trip (10.5→13.2), with the largest improvement on road boundaries (+1.3 and +2.9 respectively). The semi-supervised experiments further show that PseudoMapTrainer pre-training narrows the gap to fully supervised MapTRv2 most dramatically when only 5–20% of ground-truth labels are available for fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: 3D Gaussian splatting for road surface reconstruction (building on RoGs and 2D Gaussian surfels) and DETR-style vectorized online mapping (HDMapNet → VectorMapNet → MapTR → MapTRv2 → MapVR). The key departure is replacing ground-truth HD map labels with pseudo-labels generated by rendering a Gaussian surfel road model, and adapting the standard one-to-one Hungarian assignment to a mask-aware one-to-many scheme solved via integer linear programming. The semi-supervised pre-training strategy extends prior semi-supervised BEV segmentation work to the vectorized mapping setting, where no such method had been proposed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a vectorized online mapping model can be trained entirely without ground-truth HD maps, opening a path to leveraging large-scale crowdsourced sensor data that is far cheaper and more geographically diverse than curated annotations. The semi-supervised results show meaningful gains in low-label regimes, suggesting practical value for deployment scenarios where annotation budgets are limited. However, a substantial performance gap remains versus fully supervised methods (13.2 vs. 25.4 mean AP), particularly for lane dividers, and the multi-trip approach requires precise relative pose alignment that is non-trivial with consumer-grade sensors. Open questions include whether self-supervised pre-training can close the gap, whether the approach extends to 3D mapping and centerlines, and how robust it is to road changes between trips.
