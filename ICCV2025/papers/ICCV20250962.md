## Problem

Traditional multimodal models rely on modality-specific encoders (CNNs for images, transformers for text, etc.) combined via late fusion, which limits flexibility when extending to new modalities or task combinations. This paper asks whether all modalities can be unified into a single visual representation so that a single model, with no modality-specific components, can handle diverse tasks through one shared mechanism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250962/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

All inputs are converted into 64×64 RGB video frames: text is tokenized on spaces and punctuation with each token rendered in a fixed-width font, images are resized to 64×64, audio is converted to a spectrogram frame, and video frames are used directly. A separator frame (|) marks the boundary between input and output within the sequence. Outputs are likewise rendered as frames—class labels or answers as text frames, colorized video frames, or frames with overlaid bounding boxes—so the model always predicts the next frame in a unified pixel space.

## Method

The core contribution is a task-reformulation framework that maps every multimodal task (text classification, image classification, video QA, audio classification, object tracking, video colorization) into a single next-frame prediction problem over a 64×64 RGB video sequence. The model is a 41M-parameter pure transformer inspired by ViT and TimeSformer, using non-overlapping patch partitioning with local space-time attention blocks separated by U-Net-style patch merge/unmerge operations, followed by a global space-time transformer at reduced effective resolution to capture multiscale features while limiting self-attention cost. Temporal causality is enforced via masking so only past and present frames inform prediction, and inference proceeds autoregressively by concatenating each predicted frame to the input. Training is fully end-to-end with SSIM loss and no pretrained weights, requiring only a single A100 GPU for up to 7 days per task, demonstrating that the unified video-frame representation is sufficient to learn cross-modal reasoning without any modality-specific architecture.

## Results

Across seven task types, the unified next-frame prediction model (41M params, no pretraining) achieves: 76.8 F1 on SST-2 text classification, 89.1% accuracy on CIFAR-10, 30.4 macro-F1 on TinyVIRAT video classification, 97.1% accuracy on AudioMNIST, 52.5% accuracy on CLEVRER VQA, 52.3% accuracy on TGIF-QA, 0.63 IoU on LaSOT object tracking, and 42.4 PSNR / 73.1 colorfulness on video colorization. The model is trained end-to-end with SSIM loss on a single A100 for at most 7 GPU days per task, with no pretrained weights or modality-specific encoders. All text outputs are decoded via Tesseract OCR from the predicted 64×64 frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The model wins on most comparisons against non-pretrained or task-specific baselines: it outperforms PCANet (89.1 vs 77.1 acc) and ViT-base (89.1 vs 71.3 acc) on CIFAR-10, surpasses AlexNet on AudioMNIST (97.1 vs 95.8 acc), beats both LSTM (52.5 vs 34.7) and LSTM+CNN (52.5 vs 51.8) baselines on CLEVRER VQA, edges out LSTM+Attn on TGIF-QA (52.3 vs 51.9 acc), and exceeds Dai et al. on LaSOT tracking (0.63 vs 0.60 IoU). It also slightly beats ResNet50 on TinyVIRAT macro-F1 (30.4 vs 29.1) and outperforms Yang et al. on colorization (CDC 0.02 vs 0.03, colorfulness 73.1 vs 72.0). However, it trails pretraining-heavy models: Vega v1 (F1 91.3 vs 76.8 on SST-2), ViT-huge (99.5 vs 89.1 on CIFAR-10), and ResNet-101 (90.0 vs 89.1 on CIFAR-10). On video colorization, Zhang et al. achieves higher colorfulness (82.2 vs 73.1) while matching the CDC score. The authors note that the SST-2 gap narrows to F1 80.0 when restricting to inputs ≤20 tokens, and that CLEVRER accuracy is limited by subsampling to 4 frames at 64×64 resolution due to hardware constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the task-reformulation paradigm established in NLP (where diverse tasks like classification and QA are unified under a single language-modelling supertask) to the multimodal domain, proposing next-frame prediction as the universal supertask. Architecturally, it builds on TimesFormer's spatiotemporal attention and ViT's patch-based representation, adding a U-Net-style progressive patch-merging mechanism for multi-scale processing. It departs from prior multimodal models (UNITER, UniT, FLAVA, GPT-4V) that rely on modality-specific encoders and late fusion, instead rendering all inputs—text, audio, images, video—as 64×64 RGB frames processed by a single transformer. The visual-representation-of-text line of work (rendering tokens as images for vision models) is a direct prerequisite for the text modality in this framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single 41M-parameter transformer trained from scratch can handle seven distinct multimodal tasks without any modality-specific components, validating the conceptual feasibility of a truly unified multimodal interface. However, performance remains well below pretrained SOTA on most tasks, the 64×64 resolution and OCR-based evaluation introduce significant limitations, and no multitask joint training is attempted. The key open question is whether this paradigm scales: the authors suggest pretraining on unstructured next-frame-prediction tasks (language modelling, image captioning) before fine-tuning, mirroring the NLP recipe, but this remains untested. For practitioners, the main takeaway is architectural simplification at the cost of current performance, positioning this as a proof-of-concept for future unified foundation models rather than a deployable system.
