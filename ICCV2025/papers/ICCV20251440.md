## Problem

State-of-the-art video diffusion models fail to synthesize egocentric traffic accident videos with causal-entity fidelity: when a text prompt changes the collision participant (e.g., pedestrian → motorbike), existing models either omit the new object entirely or generate it without reflecting the actual collision dynamics. The core difficulty is that causal entities in ego-view accidents are tiny, appear suddenly, and their accident-related behaviors are hard to ground from text alone, compounded by a modality gap between textual cause descriptions and visual accident appearances.

## Input / Output

In video-to-video (V2V) mode, the model takes a 16-frame video clip (224×224) plus an accident reason-collision text prompt and generates 16 output frames depicting the near-crash-to-crashing process; in text-to-video (T2V) mode, only the text prompt is provided. During training, the model additionally consumes driver gaze fixation maps (from the newly constructed Drive-Gaze dataset, 1.54M frames) and multi-choice accident reason Q&A pairs, but these are removed at inference so that only video or text prompts are needed. The CTS and CTG modules are plug-and-play and can be grafted onto other backbones (e.g., CogVideoX-2B at 720×480, Latte at 512×512) with minimal parameter overhead (as low as 2.9% for CogVideoX).

## Method

Causal-VidSyn operates at two progressive levels on a 3D-Unet diffusion backbone initialized from Stable Diffusion. At the diffusion-recipe level, Reciprocal Prompted Frame Diffusion (RPFD) contrasts two shared-weight pathways: forward diffusion conditioned on accident-reason text with forward-order frames, and backward diffusion conditioned on accident-prevention text with reversed-order frames, linked by a negative cosine-similarity loss that forces the two noise reconstructions to capture different visual content. At the knowledge level, Causal-Prone Token Selection (CTS) blocks are injected after each temporal-attention layer in the 3D-Unet, where a Gumbel-Softmax gated fusion of CLIP-encoded vision tokens with position-embedded driver gaze tokens selects the top-quarter causal-prone tokens per frame, pulling them forward layer by layer. A Causal Token Grounding (CTG) block at the final layer performs token-level accident-reason answering: causal tokens are classified against the correct reason-answer text via cross-entropy, background tokens are classified against a uniform distribution, and a Kullback-Leibler divergence term measures the effect of randomly masking background tokens. Training proceeds in three 10K-step stages (forward diffusion → RPFD → CTS+CTG), and all causal-aware modules are discarded at inference, leaving a standard diffusion sampler (DDIM) conditioned only on video or text prompts.

## Results

On the N2A task (BDD-A, 2000 clips), Causal-VidSyn achieves the best CLIP_s (26.5) and FVD (6192.3), outperforming the closest competitor A-OAVD (CLIP_s 25.8, FVD 6378.9). On the T2V task (500 clips), Causal-VidSyn attains the highest CLIP_s (27.5) but the lowest TempC (0.944) among top methods, while CogV-X* leads in TempC (0.994). On the AEdit task (DADA-2000, 3000 clips), Causal-VidSyn achieves the best CLIP_s (28.7), FVD (5352.9), and the newly proposed affordance metric Afd (55.4%), surpassing A-OAVD (Afd 49.4%) and LAMP (Afd 34.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Causal-VidSyn outperforms all 10 baselines on CLIP_s across N2A, T2V, and AEdit tasks, with the largest margin over training-free methods (e.g., +2.3 over T2V-zero on N2A CLIP_s, +3.4 over Free-bloom on T2V CLIP_s). On FVD, Causal-VidSyn achieves the best score on both N2A (6192.3) and AEdit (5352.9), beating the closest fine-tuned competitors A-OAVD (6378.9 / 5358.2) and LAMP (6208.2 / 6191.6). The key differentiator is the AEdit task: Causal-VidSyn's Afd of 55.4% substantially exceeds A-OAVD (49.4%) and LAMP (34.7%), confirming superior causal-entity sensitivity. However, Causal-VidSyn trails CogV-X* and AVD2 on T2V TempC (0.944 vs. 0.994 and 0.976), and loses to LAMP on AEdit TempC (0.940 vs. 0.971), suggesting a trade-off between causal alignment and frame-level temporal smoothness. The DiT-based baselines (Latte, CogV-X) suffer from a 'large object issue' that inflates their CLIP_s without genuine causal grounding, while Causal-VidSyn's Unet backbone with CTS/CTG modules achieves both higher fidelity and causal sensitivity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Abductive-OAVD (Fang et al., CVPR 2024), adopting its 3D-Unet diffusion backbone and MM-AU accident video dataset, but departs by introducing causal-entity grounding mechanisms (CTS and CTG blocks) that prior accident video synthesis works lack. It extends the broader video diffusion literature (CogVideoX, Latte, Stable Diffusion) by showing that causal grounding can be injected as plug-and-play modules into both U-Net and DiT backbones, rather than requiring architecture-specific redesign. The reciprocal prompted frame diffusion (RPFD) training recipe is novel, contrasting forward and backward time-order diffusion with semantically reciprocal text prompts to create a counterfactual intervention signal, which differs from prior causal diffusion work focused on counterfactual estimation or contrastive causal-relation discovery in latent spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Drive-Gaze dataset (1.54M fixation frames across 9,727 accident videos) fills a critical gap as the largest driver gaze resource in accident scenarios, enabling future work on accident anticipation, driver attention prediction, and scanpath analysis. The plug-and-play nature of CTS&CTG (requiring only 2.9% additional parameters for CogVideoX) makes causal grounding accessible to practitioners working with existing video diffusion models without retraining from scratch. Limitations include the low 224×224 resolution for main experiments, generation of only 16 frames, and the fact that gaze and ArA signals are training-only, raising questions about whether the model truly internalizes causal structure or merely memorizes gaze patterns. The authors note future directions toward accident anticipation and other understanding tasks, but the narrow scope (egocentric, 16-frame, accident-specific) limits immediate generalizability.
