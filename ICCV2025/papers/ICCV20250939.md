## Problem

Multimodal Diffusion Transformers (MM-DiTs) such as FLUX and HunyuanVideo unify text and vision tokens in a single Multimodal Self-Attention (MM-SA) operation, making traditional U-Net-based attention manipulation methods (e.g., P2P, PnP) ineffective for editing. Directly decomposing the MM-SA map into quadrants and applying existing cross-attention or self-attention editing techniques produces undesirable artifacts, leaving a gap in how to perform precise text-guided editing within these architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250939/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source image or video (real or synthetic), a source prompt describing the original content, and a target prompt specifying the desired edit. For real inputs, an inversion step recovers the vision query and key embeddings; for synthetic inputs, these are recorded during generation. The manipulation is applied only during the first 30% of diffusion steps for images and the first 20% for videos, supporting resolutions from 320×320 (65 frames) up to 1280×720 (129 frames) for video.

## Method

QK-Edit is a training-free framework that exploits the quadrant structure of MM-SA: the 2nd quadrant (Q_text K_vision^T) has negligible impact on output, while the 3rd (Q_vision K_text^T) governs text-vision semantic binding and the 4th (Q_vision K_vision^T) defines spatial/temporal layout. Rather than directly modifying attention maps (infeasible under optimized kernels like flash-attention), the method substitutes the vision components of Q and K in the target generation with those saved from the source, which is mathematically equivalent to replacing the 3rd and 4th quadrants. Concretely, during source generation or inversion, Q_vision^src and K_vision^src are cached at selected timesteps; during target denoising, the concatenated [Q_text^tgt, Q_vision^tgt] and [K_text^tgt, K_vision^tgt] are replaced with [Q_text^tgt, Q_vision^src] and [K_text^tgt, K_vision^src], preserving source structure while allowing the target prompt to guide semantic changes. The approach requires only a few lines of code modification and generalizes seamlessly from image to video editing.

## Results

On PIE-Bench for real image editing, QK-Edit achieves the best scores across all three metrics: CLIP-T 0.264, DINO 0.776, and PickScore 22.33, outperforming the strongest baseline RF-Solver (0.257, 0.769, 21.98). For generative video editing on TGVE+, QK-Edit reaches CLIPframes 0.951, PickScore 21.24, ViCLIPdir 0.217, and ViCLIPout 0.268, surpassing DiTCtrl on every metric. For real video editing on TGVE+, QK-Edit attains CLIPframes 0.921, PickScore 20.52, and ViCLIPout 0.258, leading all baselines on three of four metrics (Flatten edges it slightly on ViCLIPdir at 0.204 vs. 0.196).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On PIE-Bench (real image editing), QK-Edit outperforms all four baselines on every metric, with the largest margin over LEDITS++ (PickScore 22.33 vs. 19.88) and a consistent lead over the closest competitor RF-Solver (CLIP-T 0.264 vs. 0.257, DINO 0.776 vs. 0.769, PickScore 22.33 vs. 21.98). For generative video editing on TGVE+, QK-Edit dominates DiTCtrl across all four metrics, with particularly large gains in ViCLIPdir (0.217 vs. 0.141) and CLIPframes (0.951 vs. 0.904). In real video editing on TGVE+, QK-Edit beats CCEdit, AnyV2V, and Flatten on PickScore, CLIPframes, and ViCLIPout, though Flatten narrowly edges it on ViCLIPdir (0.204 vs. 0.196). On the newly introduced HiLo-Benchmark for high-resolution long-video editing, QK-Edit (QK_replace) achieves the best CLIPframes (0.908) and PickScore (20.08) among all QKV manipulation strategies, confirming that replacing both Q and K yields the optimal trade-off between prompt fidelity and structural preservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the attention-manipulation editing paradigm (P2P, PnP) from U-Net diffusion models to the newer MM-DiT architecture (FLUX, HunyuanVideo), where cross-attention and self-attention are unified into a single Multimodal Self-Attention mechanism. The key departure is an empirical quadrant analysis of MM-SA that reveals text-to-vision attention is negligible while vision-to-text and vision-to-vision attention jointly govern editing, leading to a simple QK vision-component replacement strategy rather than direct attention-map surgery. The work also extends the broader U-Net-to-Transformer transition in diffusion models by showing that training-free editing techniques must be re-derived for the unified attention structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MM-DiT-based models (FLUX, HunyuanVideo, SD3, CogVideo-X) become the dominant generative architecture, a training-free editing method that requires only a few lines of code is practically valuable for rapid deployment without fine-tuning. The introduced HiLo-Benchmark (43 high-resolution, long-duration videos) addresses a gap in evaluating editing at 1K+ resolution and 100+ frames. Open questions remain around real-video inversion accuracy in complex scenes, unintended modifications in unedited regions, and whether mask-based refinement can further improve locality without sacrificing the training-free property.
