## Problem

Recent detector-free feature matching methods achieve strong accuracy but are computationally expensive, as they rely on shallow-wide CNNs that prioritize high-resolution local features and incur heavy costs in feature transformation and fine-level keypoint localization. This limits their deployment in real-time applications such as SLAM and visual localization, where inference latency is critical.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252121/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images (resized to 640×480 for ScanNet evaluation or 1152×1152 for MegaDepth validation) and produces subpixel-level correspondences as coarse grid-center matches at 1/8 scale plus regressed X/Y offsets, along with per-match confidence scores derived from the predicted distribution scale σ. Coarse matches are selected via Top-K scoring with a probability threshold, and bidirectional fine matches are filtered by retaining only the more confident direction above a fine-level threshold, yielding a fixed-size output suitable for mini-batch inference.

## Method

EDM replaces the conventional shallow-wide backbone with a deeper ResNet-18 (fewer channels) to extract features at 1/32 and 1/8 scales, then applies a Correlation Injection Module that alternates self- and cross-attention (with QKNA and RoPE) L=2 times on the low-resolution deep features to model inter-image correlations, followed by two Injection Layers that progressively gate and fuse global context into local features. Coarse matching uses an efficient dual-softmax implementation with Top-K row-max selection instead of mutual nearest neighbors. For fine matching, a lightweight bidirectional axis-based regression head (ABRHead) with Soft Coordinate Classification (16 bins per axis, soft-argmax for location, sigmoid for scale) directly regresses subpixel offsets from latent features, supervised by RLE loss with a Laplace base distribution, entirely avoiding explicit keypoint localization on high-resolution heatmaps.

## Results

On the MegaDepth dataset, EDM achieves AUC@5° of 57.5, AUC@10° of 73.2, and AUC@20° of 84.2 for relative pose estimation, outperforming all semi-dense baselines including ELoFTR (56.4/72.2/83.5) and ASpanFormer (55.3/71.5/83.1). On ScanNet, EDM reaches 19.8/37.5/54.4 AUC at 5°/10°/20° thresholds while running at 16.7 ms per image pair on a single NVIDIA 3090 GPU — 2.3× faster than ELoFTR (39.0 ms) and 4.3× faster than LoFTR (71.8 ms). For homography estimation on HPatches, EDM achieves the best AUC at all thresholds (68.5/78.1/86.6 at 3/5/10 px), surpassing ASpanFormer (67.4/76.9/85.6) and ELoFTR (66.5/76.4/85.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDM outperforms all semi-dense baselines on MegaDepth relative pose estimation, beating the closest competitor ELoFTR by +1.1 AUC@5° (57.5 vs 56.4) while being 2.3× faster (16.7 ms vs 39.0 ms). On ScanNet, EDM achieves the best AUC@5° (19.8) and AUC@20° (54.4), though ASpanFormer edges it slightly on AUC@10° (37.7 vs 37.5). For homography estimation on HPatches, EDM leads all methods at every threshold, gaining +1.1 AUC@3px over ASpanFormer and +2.0 over ELoFTR. In visual localization, EDM is competitive but not dominant: on InLoc DUC1 it trails ELoFTR by ~1–2 points at tighter thresholds, while on DUC2 it surpasses ELoFTR at 0.25 m and 0.5 m; on Aachen, EDM matches or slightly trails ELoFTR on Day but wins at 0.5 m on Night (92.1 vs 91.1). Dense matchers (DKM, RoMa) achieve higher absolute accuracy but at 11–19× the inference cost of EDM, making them impractical for real-time use.

## Relation to Prior Work

EDM extends the semi-dense coarse-to-fine matching paradigm established by LoFTR and refined by EfficientLoFTR, but departs fundamentally from their shallow-wide CNN + FPN upsampling design. Instead of transforming features at 1/8 resolution, EDM uses a deeper ResNet-18 with fewer channels to extract features at 1/32 scale, then progressively injects global correlations into local features via a Correlation Injection Module inspired by FPN-style aggregation. The fine matching stage repurposes techniques from human pose estimation—RLE loss, SimCC-style coordinate classification, and soft-argmax—to regress subpixel offsets directly from latent features, avoiding the expensive high-resolution heatmap localization used by prior semi-dense methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EDM addresses a practical bottleneck: existing semi-dense matchers like LoFTR and ELoFTR are too slow for real-time deployment in SfM, SLAM, and visual localization pipelines. By achieving 2.3× speedup over ELoFTR while matching or slightly exceeding accuracy, EDM makes competitive feature matching viable in latency-sensitive applications. The core insight—that excessively high-resolution local features are computationally superfluous and that deeper, narrower networks capturing high-level context can substitute—challenges the prevailing design assumption in the field. Limitations include a moderate decline in relative efficiency at very high resolutions and training exclusively on outdoor MegaDepth data, leaving open whether the approach generalizes equally well to other domain shifts.
