## Problem

Existing controllable motion synthesis methods require dense, frame-wise spatial control signals with exact per-frame timing, which are impractical for animators to specify manually. This paper addresses the gap by enabling motion generation from highly sparse and flexible keyjoint inputs, including time-agnostic trajectories and goal-driven constraints, while maintaining high motion quality.

## Input / Output

The method accepts extremely sparse control signals—down to 0.454% of total joint signals (one frame every 1.5 s, 50% of six keyjoints)—along with a binary indicator mask, a text description or action label, and optionally a differentiable objective function or a time-agnostic 3D trajectory with only start/end times. The output is a full-body 3D motion sequence in the HumanML3D representation (263-dim per frame at 20 FPS), where the six keyjoints (hands, feet, pelvis, head) are expressed in global coordinates and the full pose in root-relative coordinates.

## Method

The framework decomposes motion synthesis into two diffusion stages: a keyjoint trajectory model that synthesizes low-dimensional (6-joint) movements from sparse control via imputation-based training with random spatio-temporal masks, and a full-body completion model that generates the complete 263-dim pose conditioned on the dense keyjoint trajectories. Both stages use a U-Net denoiser trained with an imputation strategy (replacing masked/noisy components with clean values) and classifier-free guidance at inference. For implicit control, the same keyjoint model is reused with diffusion latent optimization, back-propagating gradients of a user-defined differentiable objective through the denoising process. The time-agnostic control formulates a novel arc-length reparametrization loss using cubic splines to align generated trajectories with target paths independent of temporal indexing, eliminating the need for per-frame timestamps.

## Results

Under a highly sparse control setting (only 0.454% of total joint signals as input), the proposed method achieves a Control Error of 0.036 m, FID of 0.224, and Foot Skating of 0.061 on HumanML3D, outperforming all baselines on spatial precision and motion quality. The method also achieves the best Critic Score (0.253) on the Walking Narrow implicit-control task and the lowest Constraint Error (0.0036) on Hand-to-Head, demonstrating superior constraint satisfaction while preserving motion naturalness. With DDIM-10 sampling, the method retains its advantage (FID 0.254, Control Error 0.036 m) while reducing inference time to 1.4 s per sequence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method dominates on spatial control precision: it achieves a Control Error of 0.036 m, roughly 3× lower than OmniControl (0.111 m) and 14× lower than CondMDI (0.507 m), while also improving FID from 0.689 (OmniControl) and 0.498 (CondMDI) to 0.224. Against TLControl, the gap is most dramatic on FID (0.224 vs. 4.637) and R-precision (0.673 vs. 0.473), though TLControl edges out on Foot Skating (0.058 vs. 0.061). MotionLCM is the fastest baseline (0.034 s) but sacrifices control accuracy (0.418 m) and motion quality (FID 1.013, Foot Skating 0.141); the proposed DDIM-10 variant (1.4 s) still outperforms it on every metric. On implicit control tasks (Table 3), the decomposed pipeline reduces Constraint Error by 17% over DNO on Walking Narrow (0.0084 vs. 0.0101) and by 57% on Hand-to-Head (0.0036 vs. 0.0084), while also improving the Critic Score from −2.024 to +0.253 on Walking Narrow, indicating substantially more natural motion. The only metric where a baseline slightly leads is R-precision: OmniControl scores 0.689 vs. 0.673 for the proposed method, a marginal 0.016 gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on diffusion-based motion synthesis with imputation (CondMDI [10], MDM [62]) and joint-level spatial control (OmniControl [68], TLControl [64], DNO [31]), but departs from them by decomposing the problem into a low-dimensional keyjoint trajectory stage followed by a full-body completion stage. Prior joint-level methods require dense frame-wise control signals with exact timing; here, the keyjoint-first decomposition naturally accommodates sparse, multi-joint, and even time-agnostic inputs without architectural changes. The time-agnostic control formulation via arc-length reparametrization and cubic splines is a new contribution not present in prior controllable motion synthesis work, and the unified goal-driven network spanning reaching, climbing, and sitting tasks extends single-task goal-reaching approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for animation workflows: animators can specify only a handful of keyjoint positions without per-frame timestamps, removing a major usability barrier that dense-signal methods impose. The decomposition cleanly separates controllability from motion quality, and the 0.454% control-signal sparsity achieving 0.036 m control error demonstrates that very little explicit specification is needed when the right intermediate representation is used. Key limitations include the lack of real-time performance (0.7 s with DDIM5, 7.1 s with DDPM) and the reliance on a fixed keyjoint set (hands, feet, pelvis, head), which may not generalize to all motion types. Open questions include whether autoregressive or consistency-model sampling can close the real-time gap, and whether the keyjoint set can be learned or adapted per task.
