## Problem

Multi-modal semantic segmentation models exhibit unimodal bias, disproportionately relying on easily learnable modalities (e.g., RGB) while under-utilizing complementary sensors, causing severe performance degradation when the dominant modality is missing or corrupted. Existing fusion architectures lack explicit mechanisms to regulate how each modality contributes to predictions, leaving models vulnerable in safety-critical applications where sensor availability is unreliable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251168/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method processes a mini-batch of multiple visual modalities (RGB, depth, event, LiDAR, NIR, DoLP, AoLP) where each modality is fed independently through the same segmentation backbone without any cross-modal fusion module. Fusion is achieved by simple averaging of per-modality predictions within the mini-batch, and random modality dropout is applied during training to expose the model to diverse input combinations. The output is a pixel-wise semantic segmentation map, with the regularization operating on both multi-scale intermediate features (from 4 transformer blocks) and final prediction logits.

## Method

The core contribution is a parameter-free, plug-and-play regularization term derived from functional entropy, bounded via the log-Sobolev inequality by the inverse of functional Fisher information. This term is decomposed across modalities (Eq. 3) so each modality's contribution is independently assessed, and applied at two levels: a prediction-level term (R_p) that penalizes cross-modality prediction inconsistency, and a multi-scale feature-level term (R_f) that enforces smoothness and balance across 4 feature scales. The total loss combines standard cross-entropy supervision with both regularization terms (L = L_sup + R_p + R_f), with hyperparameters λ_p=0.3 and λ_f=0.02. Unlike prior work that adds complex fusion connectors or asymmetric branches, this approach treats all modalities symmetrically within the backbone and relies entirely on the entropy-based regularization to prevent any single modality from dominating the learned representations.

## Results

On MUSES (3 modalities: Frame, Event, LiDAR), the method achieves a mean mIoU of 47.80%, a +13.94% improvement over the previous best (Any2Seg, 33.86%). On DELIVER (4 modalities: RGB, Depth, Event, LiDAR), it reaches 48.29% mean mIoU (+3.25% over Any2Seg), and on MCubeS (4 modalities: Image, AoLP, DoLP, NIR), it achieves 41.64% (+3.64% over MAGIC). A key advantage is balanced performance across all modality subsets: on DELIVER, single-modality mIoUs range from 32.40% (Event) to 57.74% (Depth), whereas baselines like MAGIC drop below 1% on Event and LiDAR alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all six baselines across all three datasets. Against CMNeXt [1], the gains are largest: 47.80% vs. 10.80% mean mIoU on MUSES and 48.29% vs. 20.77% on DELIVER, as CMNeXt heavily relies on RGB and collapses when it is absent. Against MAGIC [2], the method wins by +14.46% on MUSES (47.80% vs. 33.34%), +7.80% on DELIVER (48.29% vs. 40.49%), and +3.64% on MCubeS (41.64% vs. 38.00%); MAGIC still exhibits strong unimodal bias, scoring below 3% on Event and LiDAR alone. Against Any2Seg [18], the closest competitor, the method leads by +13.94% on MUSES, +3.25% on DELIVER, and +3.64% on MCubeS, with the most consistent advantage in single-modality and mixed-modality settings where Any2Seg still drops sharply (e.g., 2.07% on Event alone vs. 32.40% for Ours). On the dual-modality DELIVER subset (Table 1), the method reaches 60.37% mean mIoU, surpassing the best prior method (CMNeXt with MMS, 56.45%) by +3.92%. The method also outperforms CMX [77] (19.30% on MUSES) and MultiMAE [75] (52.25% on DELIVER dual-modality) by wide margins, confirming that the entropy-based regularization provides a more effective balance mechanism than architectural fusion or masked autoencoding approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the functional entropy regularization introduced by Gat et al. (NeurIPS 2020) for multi-modal VQA (image + text) to the substantially harder pixel-level multi-modal semantic segmentation setting with multiple visual modalities. It departs from the dominant paradigm in multi-modal segmentation (CMNeXt, MAGIC, Any2Seg), which relies on complex fusion architectures and dedicated training objectives, by instead treating all modalities symmetrically within a mini-batch (simple averaging) and enforcing balance through a parameter-free, plug-and-play entropy-based regularizer applied at multiple scales. The key conceptual extension over [21] is the decomposition of functional entropy across individual modalities and the multi-scale application at both feature and prediction levels, which the authors argue is necessary for pixel-wise tasks where spatial and contextual integration across modalities is required.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Unimodal bias is a critical failure mode in safety-critical applications (autonomous driving, robotics) where sensor degradation or failure is routine; this paper provides a theoretically grounded, zero-parameter remedy that can be dropped into existing segmentation backbones without architectural changes. The large single-modality gains (e.g., +30% on Event in MUSES) demonstrate that the regularization genuinely prevents collapse to a dominant modality rather than merely improving the all-modality case. However, the fusion strategy (simple averaging of predictions) is extremely basic and its scalability to highly heterogeneous modality pairs (e.g., text + 3D point clouds) or to larger backbones remains untested; the evaluation is confined to three datasets with at most four modalities. Open questions include whether the log-Sobolev bound remains tight for deeper transformer features and whether the approach generalizes beyond the tested sensor combinations.
