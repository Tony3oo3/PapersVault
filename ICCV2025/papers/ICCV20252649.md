## Problem

Existing weakly supervised Dynamic Scene Graph Generation (WS-DSGG) methods depend on off-the-shelf external object detectors trained on static, object-centric images to produce pseudo labels. These detectors are ill-suited for dynamic, relation-aware video scenarios, suffering from inaccurate localization (39.83% of errors) and low-confidence missed detections (59.43% of errors), which severely degrades pseudo scene graph quality and limits final DSGG performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video sequence of N_v RGB frames plus a single unlocalized scene graph (a set of subject-object-predicate category triplets) from one annotated frame as the sole training supervision. It produces a fully localized scene graph for every frame, with object bounding boxes and predicted relational edges between object pairs. The external detector (VinVL with ResNeXt-152 C4 backbone) processes each frame independently, and the TRKT pipeline refines its per-frame proposals before pseudo scene graph construction.

## Method

TRKT mines relation- and motion-aware knowledge via a transformer encoder-decoder that uses separate object tokens and relation tokens to generate class-sensitive attention maps from image-level labels, fusing them through a similarity-weighted combination so that object regions are highlighted alongside their interactive boundaries. Inter-frame Attention Augmentation (IAA) then warps the previous frame's attention maps using RAFT optical flow to produce pseudo-attention maps that are robust to motion blur and occlusion. A Dual-stream Fusion Module transfers this knowledge to external detections: the Localization Refinement Module (LRM) converts attention maps into internal proposals and fuses them with external boxes via Weighted Box Fusion, while the Confidence Boosting Module (CBM) selectively amplifies confidence scores for high-probability categories by injecting detection-box attention into the class-specific attention map before re-extracting proposals. The entire fusion is applied sequentially—first with relation-aware maps, then with motion-aware pseudo maps—to compound both localization and confidence improvements before constructing the final pseudo scene graphs for fully supervised DSGG training.

## Results

On the Action Genome dataset for WS-DSGG, TRKT achieves R@10 of 17.56 (With Constraint) and 18.76 (No Constraint), outperforming the prior SOTA PLA by an average of 1.72% and 2.42% respectively across R@10/R@20/R@50. For the underlying object detection task, TRKT more than doubles the Average Precision from 11.4 to 23.0 (maxDets=1) and improves Average Recall from 37.6 to 43.8 (maxDets=10) over PLA's external detector. On the VidVRD dataset for traclet-level WS-DSGG, TRKT improves mAP from 17.17% to 17.93% and R@100 from 10.26% to 11.92% over UCML.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRKT outperforms all five baselines across every reported metric. Against the closest competitor PLA [3] (the only prior WS-DSGG method using the same pseudo-label pipeline), TRKT gains +2.11 on R@10 and +1.66 on R@50 under With Constraint, and +2.89 on R@10 and +2.23 on R@50 under No Constraint, attributable to its improved object detection (AP nearly doubled from 11.4 to 23.0). Against NL-VSGG [12], which uses video captions for weaker supervision, TRKT leads by +7.05 on R@50 (With Constraint) and +10.71 on R@50 (No Constraint), demonstrating that relation-aware detection refinement surpasses caption-based pseudo-label construction. The zero-shot methods RLIP [33] and RLIPv2 [34] trail TRKT by over 12 points on R@10 (With Constraint), confirming the necessity of temporal context and task-specific relation knowledge. On the traclet-level VidVRD setting, TRKT improves over UCML [27] by +0.76% mAP and +1.66% R@100, validating generalization beyond the frame-level formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on PLA (Chen et al., ICLR 2023), which first formulated Weakly Supervised DSGG and established the pipeline of using an external detector to generate pseudo-localized scene graphs for fully-supervised DSGG training. TRKT departs from this pipeline by identifying the external detector as the critical bottleneck and proposing to refine its outputs using relation-aware and motion-aware knowledge mined from the same weak supervision signal (unlocalized scene graph labels). The approach also draws on weakly supervised object detection literature (e.g., H²FA-RCNN, C-MIDN) for the attention-map-based localization strategy, while explicitly distinguishing itself from WS-OD by leveraging the ability to refine external detections rather than training a detector from scratch. The temporal augmentation via optical flow connects to broader video understanding work but is applied here specifically to rescue attention maps from motion blur and occlusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's most valuable contribution is the empirical diagnosis that object detection quality, not relation prediction, is the dominant bottleneck in WS-DSGG (61.9% gain from oracle detections vs. 0.9% from oracle relations), which redirects community attention toward improving the detection stage. The proposed TRKT framework provides a practical, plug-in solution that requires no retraining of the external detector, making it applicable to existing WS-DSGG pipelines. However, the actual DSGG improvements are modest (~2% R@K), the method is evaluated primarily on a single dataset (Action Genome), and the reliance on a specific external detector (VinVL) and optical flow computation limits generality. Open questions include whether the knowledge-mining component could be made end-to-end with the DSGG model, whether the sequential relation-then-motion knowledge injection is optimal, and whether the approach transfers to other video datasets with different motion characteristics.
