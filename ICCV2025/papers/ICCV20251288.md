## Problem

Generating accurate rewards for robotic manipulation from VLMs without fine-tuning is challenging because pre-trained VLMs lack domain-specific robotic knowledge, and querying them at every timestep incurs prohibitive computational costs that preclude real-time use. Moreover, relying solely on single spatial observations makes it difficult for VLMs to reliably determine goal completion, especially under occlusions or dynamic scene changes. This paper addresses both the accuracy and efficiency gaps by incorporating temporal information to correct VLM-derived spatial estimates while drastically reducing the number of VLM queries.

## Input / Output

At the start of each episode, the method takes a single RGB image and a natural-language task description, which the VLM uses to decompose the task into subgoals and produce an initial binary completion vector. During execution, video frames are processed by SAM 2 to extract per-object bounding box trajectories, which serve as the temporal observations for the particle filter. The output is a scalar reward at each decision interval τ, computed as a weighted sum of absolute differences in the estimated subgoal hidden state (an N-dimensional continuous vector in [0,1] per subgoal) between consecutive decision steps.

## Method

T²-VLM queries the VLM only once per episode to obtain spatially-aware subgoals and an initial completion estimate, then tracks goal completion over time using a particle filter initialized with that estimate. SAM 2 segments and tracks task-relevant objects in the video stream to produce bounding box trajectories, which feed the observation model. The key innovation is a VLM-coding affordance module: for each subgoal, the VLM is prompted to generate coding functions f_VLM¹(i) and f_VLM⁰(i) that output expected object positions when the subgoal is satisfied or unsatisfied, respectively; the observation likelihood is then the minimum mean distance between observed bounding boxes and these affordance positions. Particles propagate via a constant-velocity motion model (α=0.7, β=0.04), weights are updated by multiplying per-subgoal error terms, and systematic resampling maintains the particle set. The final reward at each decision step is σ(ĥ_t − ĥ_{t−τ}), where ĥ_t is the weighted-average hidden state across all particles, making the entire pipeline training-free and requiring no VLM queries after the initial episode setup.

## Results

On recovery performance across four failure types (Occupied, Closed, Drop, Placement), RL trained with T²-VLM achieves a 93% average recovery success rate, compared to 53% for REFLECT and 23% for SayCan. In recovery meta-steps, T²-VLM requires only 9.73 steps on average (vs. 17.03 for REFLECT and 18.53 for SayCan), approaching the optimal of 7.50. In the Stack-tower placement failure, T²-VLM achieves 100% recovery vs. 25% for REFLECT (a 75% absolute improvement). In reward accuracy on CLIPort, T²-VLM outperforms step-by-step VLM scoring by 25–41% while reducing VLM queries by 77–88%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VLM-CaR [36] and RL-VLM-F [38] in RL training (Figure 5), T²-VLM achieves goal completion ratios comparable to ground-truth environmental rewards and clearly outperforms both baselines; RL-VLM-F in particular shows poor performance on CLIPort and high variance on CALVIN due to its learned reward model instability. In recovery evaluation (Table 1), T²-VLM dominates SayCan [1] by a wide margin (93% vs. 23% average success rate; 9.73 vs. 18.53 meta-steps), with SayCan failing entirely on Drop and Placement failures. Against REFLECT [19], T²-VLM improves average recovery success rate from 53% to 93% and reduces meta-steps from 17.03 to 9.73, with the largest gap in the Placement failure (100% vs. 25%). The advantage over REFLECT is attributed to T²-VLM's use of temporal bounding-box trajectories (via SAM 2) rather than single-image inputs, enabling robust reward signals under occlusion. In reward accuracy benchmarks (Tables 2–4), T²-VLM consistently surpasses step-by-step VLM scoring by 25–41% across all three tested VLMs (Claude-3.5-Sonnet, Qwen2-VL-72B, Qwen2-VL-7B) while cutting VLM query counts by 77–88%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of VLM-based reward generation and classical Bayesian state estimation in robotics. It departs from prior VLM reward methods (VLM-CaR, RL-VLM-F) that query VLMs at every timestep or generate reward functions upfront, instead querying the VLM only once per episode for subgoal decomposition and initial completion estimates, then relying on a particle filter adapted from latent-space object tracking [43] to propagate and correct the goal state over time. The VLM-coded affordance module—where the VLM outputs coding functions that map subgoal states to expected object positions—is a novel repurposing of VLM reasoning capabilities as a likelihood function within a Bayesian filter, rather than as a direct reward oracle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic reduction in VLM queries (77–88%) while simultaneously improving reward accuracy addresses a key practical bottleneck for deploying VLM-based rewards in real-time robotic settings, making the approach more accessible for resource-constrained labs. However, the method's reliance on SAM 2 bounding box quality and the VLM's spatial reasoning (which still struggles with relations like 'next to') limits its applicability to more complex or occluded scenes. Open questions include whether the binary subgoal hidden state formulation generalizes to continuous or hierarchical goal structures, and whether the particle filter approach scales to tasks with many interacting objects or 3D manipulation.
