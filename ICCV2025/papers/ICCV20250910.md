## Problem

Existing score distillation methods for text-guided editing (DDS, CSD) suffer from unstable optimization, loss of source content structure, and insufficient editing strength. DDS relies on a source branch that removes model bias but lacks explicit preservation of unedited regions, while CSD's dual-classifier design amplifies noise and introduces overlapping objectives that hinder convergence. These limitations stem from complex auxiliary structures that generate conflicting optimization signals and prevent precise, localized edits.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a parametric representation (NeRF or 3D Gaussian Splatting for 3D scenes, or a 2D image in latent space) together with a source prompt describing the original content and a target prompt specifying the desired edit, with an optional binary mask to restrict editing to specific regions. It produces an edited 3D scene or 2D image optimized via gradient descent on the parametric representation. The framework operates within the Stable Diffusion pipeline without requiring LoRA adapters or fine-tuning, and uses non-increasing timestep sampling to reduce optimization to approximately 3,000 iterations for NeRF and 1,500 for Gaussian splatting.

## Method

SSD decomposes the editing gradient into three terms: (1) a cross-prompt term w_p(ε_φ(z_t, y) − ε_φ(z_t, ŷ)) that uses a single CFG-anchored classifier to provide a smooth transition direction from source to target prompt, eliminating the need for DDS's auxiliary source branch; (2) a cross-trajectory term w_t(ε_φ(z_t, ŷ) − ε_φ(ẑ_t, ∅)) that introduces a null-text branch on the source latent to explicitly regularize the optimization and preserve original structure, acting as a 'reconstruction' constraint; and (3) a prompt-enhancement term w_e(ε_φ(z_t, y) − ε_φ(z_t, ∅)) that amplifies editing strength, particularly for style transformations. An additional ID regularization term w(t)·(x_t − ẑ_t) using the noisy source latent suppresses gradient explosion in local 3DGS regions. The final loss L_final = L_ssd + L_align + L_ID is optimized via standard gradient descent, and the paper shows that the InstructPix2Pix one-step reverse sampling equation is a simplified special case of the SSD formulation.

## Results

On 3D scene editing (IN2N, LLFF, Mip-Nerf360), SSD achieves the best CLIP Similarity (0.1846), CLIP Directional Similarity (0.0773), and User Study score (41.81%), outperforming all baselines. On 2D image editing (PIE-Bench), SSD achieves the highest CLIP Similarity (26.94) among all methods, confirming the effectiveness of the prompt-enhancement branch. When combined with CDS for structure preservation, SSD+CDS achieves the best structure distance (6.90×10³), LPIPS (32.15×10³), and MSE (24.21×10⁴) while maintaining competitive CLIP score (25.12).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In 3D scene editing, SSD outperforms all four baselines (IN2N, DDS, GS-Editor, DGE) on every metric: CLIP Similarity (0.1846 vs. 0.1780 for DDS), CLIP Directional Similarity (0.0773 vs. 0.0563 for DGE), and User Study preference (41.81% vs. 23.63% for DGE). In 2D editing, SSD achieves the highest CLIP Similarity (26.94) over DDS (25.86) and DDS+CDS (24.96), demonstrating stronger prompt alignment, particularly for style editing. However, standalone SSD has higher structure distance (28.13) than DDS+CDS (7.15), reflecting a trade-off between editability and preservation; combining SSD with CDS resolves this, yielding the best structure metrics (Distance 6.90, LPIPS 32.15, MSE 24.21) while retaining a competitive CLIP score (25.12). Optimization-based methods (NT+P2P, StyleD+P2P) achieve lower structure distance but suffer from limited editability due to their reliance on P2P, yielding CLIP scores below 25. Inversion-based methods (DDIM+P2P, DDIM+PNP, DDIM+MasaCtrl) perform worst on both structure and editability, with DDIM+P2P showing the highest distance (69.43) and LPIPS (208.80).

## Relation to Prior Work

SSD sits at the intersection of three prior threads: DreamFusion's SDS optimization, DDS's source-branch bias removal, and CSD's dual-classifier cross-prompt editing. The key departure is a simplification: rather than CSD's two classifiers or DDS's separate source branch, SSD anchors a single classifier to the source prompt via the CFG equation and introduces a null-text branch as a constant 'reconstruction' term. This yields a cross-trajectory regularization that constrains structural drift without the conflicting optimization signals of dual-branch architectures. The paper also reveals a conceptual link to InstructPix2Pix, showing that IP2P's one-step reverse sampling is a simplified instance of the same cross-prompt + cross-trajectory decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SSD provides a cleaner, more interpretable editing gradient that achieves SOTA on both 2D (PIE-Bench) and 3D (NeRF/3DGS) editing with fewer components and faster convergence (~3,000 iterations for NeRF, ~1,500 for 3DGS), without requiring LoRA or fine-tuning. The prompt-enhancement branch addresses a practical gap in DDS-based methods—insufficient editing strength for style transfer—making the framework broadly applicable. However, the optimization-based nature remains a bottleneck compared to emerging one-step or few-step methods (e.g., LCM, SD-Turbo), and the paper itself flags acceleration as the primary open direction. The cross-trajectory term's effectiveness is well-supported by ablations, but the method's sensitivity to the w_t and w_e hyperparameters across diverse scenes is not exhaustively characterized.
