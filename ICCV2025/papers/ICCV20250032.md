## Problem

Existing multi-view diffusion models condition on a single-view image without 3D-aware guidance, making it difficult to control unseen regions, edit material properties, or generate views from text alone. No prior method supports flexible, simultaneous conditioning on both image and text inputs for controllable multi-view synthesis, leaving a gap in user-friendly 3D content creation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250032/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a single-view RGB image (arbitrary focal length and pose), a 3D-aware text prompt (global description + local features + material attributes like metallic/roughness), or both simultaneously. Output is a 2×2 tiled grid of four orthogonal orthographic views (front, left, back, right) at 512×512 each, rendered at a fixed 5° elevation angle. During training, a condition switcher randomly drops the text (replaced by an empty string) or the image (replaced by a black image) to support all three input modes.

## Method

FlexGen builds on Stable Diffusion 2.1 and introduces two key components. First, GPT-4V generates 3D-aware global-to-local captions by reasoning over a 2×2 tiled image of four orthogonal views, capturing spatial relationships, textures, and material properties that single-view captioning (e.g., Cap3D) misses. Second, an adaptive dual-control module extends the reference attention mechanism: the user prompt is encoded by CLIP into per-token embeddings, which are fused with the reference image features via cross-attention before the resulting self-attention K/V matrices are appended to the denoising UNet's attention layers. Training uses 147k curated Objaverse objects rendered in Blender, with a condition switcher that samples image-only, text-only, dual-mode, or no-condition inputs at 0.3/0.3/0.3/0.1 probability respectively, enabling the single model to handle all input configurations at inference.

## Results

On the GSO dataset for novel view synthesis and sparse-view 3D reconstruction, FlexGen achieves PSNR of 22.31, LPIPS of 0.12, Chamfer Distance of 0.076, and FS@0.1 of 0.928, outperforming all baselines across every metric. For text-to-multi-view generation, FlexGen attains FID of 35.56, Inception Score of 13.41, and CLIP score of 0.83, surpassing MVDream (FID 44.42, IS 12.98, CLIP 0.79) and approaching ground-truth quality (IS 13.81, CLIP 0.89).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlexGen outperforms all three single-image-to-multi-view baselines (Zero123++, Era3D, SyncDreamer) on every reported metric for novel view synthesis and 3D reconstruction. The largest margin is over Era3D, which suffers a CD of 0.245 versus FlexGen's 0.076 and an FS@0.1 of 0.713 versus 0.928, indicating substantially worse geometric consistency. Zero123++ is the closest competitor (PSNR 18.83 vs. 22.31, LPIPS 0.16 vs. 0.12), but still trails by roughly 3.5 dB in PSNR. For text-to-multi-view generation, FlexGen beats MVDream by a wide margin on FID (35.56 vs. 44.42), Inception Score (13.41 vs. 12.98), and CLIP score (0.83 vs. 0.79), demonstrating superior both image quality and text-image alignment. The ablation further confirms that removing the 3D-aware caption (replacing with Cap3D) or the cross-attention module degrades performance, validating each component's contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlexGen extends the multi-view diffusion model lineage (MVDream, Zero123++, SyncDreamer) by introducing joint image-and-text controllability, building directly on the reference attention mechanism [52] for image conditioning. It departs from Cap3D's per-view BLIP-2 captioning by leveraging GPT-4V to produce 3D-aware global-to-local descriptions from tiled multi-view inputs, and generalizes Instant3D's text-only pipeline to support image, text, or both as conditioning. The adaptive dual-control module is the key architectural novelty, injecting CLIP text embeddings via cross-attention into the reference attention pathway to enable simultaneous multi-modal control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FlexGen addresses a practical gap in 3D content creation pipelines by making multi-view generation controllable through natural language, enabling users to edit unseen regions, adjust material properties (metallic/roughness), and modify textures without re-optimization. The material-editing capability via simple prompt suffixes is a useful practical addition for game and animation workflows. However, the authors acknowledge that complex user-defined instructions remain challenging due to dataset limitations, and the approach is constrained to a fixed 2×2 orthographic view layout at 512×512 resolution. Open questions include whether the GPT-4V captioning pipeline scales to more diverse object categories and whether the controllability generalizes to arbitrary view configurations beyond the four orthogonal views.
