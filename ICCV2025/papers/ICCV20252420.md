## Problem

Existing approaches to unify vision and language in a single model either train from scratch at enormous computational cost or fine-tune the LLM backbone, which degrades the model's original language capabilities through catastrophic forgetting. The paper asks whether new modalities (e.g., vision) can be introduced to a pretrained LLM while keeping all language weights completely frozen, and what training data strategies best support this frozen-LLM paradigm.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252420/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts interleaved sequences of text tokens (from the LLM's native tokenizer) and image tokens (256×256 RGB images encoded by the SD1.5 VAE, then compressed via a trainable 2×2 linear patchify layer). For text-to-image generation, a text prompt is fed in and a 256×256 image is produced via flow-matching denoising; for image-to-text understanding, an image is fed in and a caption is generated autoregressively. The same model handles both directions within a single forward pass over the interleaved token sequence.

## Method

X-Fusion introduces a dual-tower architecture where each transformer layer contains a frozen text block (original LLM weights) and a trainable vision block (initialized by copying the corresponding LLM layer's self-attention, MLP, and normalization parameters). At each layer, text tokens are decoded from the text tower's output and image tokens from the vision tower's output, so modality-specific processing occurs at every intermediate layer rather than only at input/output boundaries. Training jointly minimizes an autoregressive cross-entropy loss (λ=0.2) for text and a flow-matching diffusion loss (λ=1) for images, using LLaMA-3 as the frozen backbone. An optional X-Fuse operation further fuses cross-tower features with learnable scalars at a FLOPs cost. Key empirical findings: providing clean (noise-free) images for image-to-text samples improves both understanding and generation, and adding understanding data boosts generation quality while the reverse asymmetry does not hold.

## Results

On the architecture comparison (Table 1, LLaMA-3.2-1B backbone, 0.08T tokens), X-Fusion's Dual Tower achieves the best text-to-image FID of 14.20 (vs. 19.10 for Single Tower, a 23% reduction) and the highest image-understanding scores (CLIP 22.81, BLIP-ITM 31.3). Critically, Dual Tower preserves the frozen LLM's MMLU score at 32.2, whereas the Single Tower baseline degrades to 25.0 (near chance level). The Gated Tower variant performs worst overall (FID 24.51, BLIP 14.5), confirming that simple additive gating is insufficient for multimodal integration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Single Tower baseline (equivalent to Transfusion initialized from a pretrained LLM), Dual Tower reduces FID from 19.10 to 14.20 (23% improvement) while matching its CLIP and BLIP-ITM scores, and—most importantly—preserves MMLU at 32.2 versus Single Tower's collapse to 25.0. Compared to Dual Projection (which uses modality-specific QKV projections with joint attention), Dual Tower achieves a lower FID (14.20 vs. 20.22) and slightly better understanding metrics, attributed to its flexibility in generating new QKV representations for cross-modal attention. The Gated Tower (additive residual with a learnable gate) is the weakest variant, scoring FID 24.51 and BLIP 14.5, indicating that simple element-wise addition cannot adequately separate text and vision processing. All Dual Tower variants (including Dual Projection) that freeze the language weights retain the original MMLU score of 32.2, confirming that the frozen-text-tower design successfully prevents catastrophic forgetting of language knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

X-Fusion builds directly on the Transfusion [28] training recipe—combining autoregressive next-token prediction for text with diffusion loss for images—but departs fundamentally by initializing from a frozen pretrained LLM (LLaMA-3) rather than training a unified model from scratch. Architecturally, it diverges from the visual-instruction-tuning line (LLaVA, MiniGPT-4) that fine-tunes the LLM backbone to align with vision encoders, and from LLM-plus-diffusion pipelines (DreamLLM, GILL) that concatenate separate models, by introducing a per-layer dual-tower design where a trainable vision transformer block runs in parallel to the frozen text block. The concurrent LMFusion [49] shares the high-level frozen-LLM-plus-vision-weights idea but uses joint cross-modal attention, whereas X-Fusion's dual-tower allows asymmetric tower designs and modality-specific QKV projections. The paper's data-centric ablations (noise level, task ratio) represent a distinct empirical contribution not present in prior unified-model work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frozen-LLM dual-tower design offers a practical path to adding modalities without the catastrophic forgetting that plagues fine-tuning approaches (MMLU drops from 32.2 to 25.0 with single-tower fine-tuning but stays at 32.2 with X-Fusion), making it attractive for organizations that cannot afford to retrain large LLMs. The finding that clean (noise-free) images for understanding data improve both understanding and generation quality challenges the Transfusion convention of adding partial noise to I2T samples, and the asymmetric data-ratio insight (understanding data helps generation but not vice versa) provides concrete training guidance. Limitations include evaluation at only 256×256 resolution, 0.08T training tokens, and a single added modality (vision); open questions include whether the dual-tower design scales to higher resolutions, additional modalities (audio, video), and whether the clean-image finding holds at larger model and data scales.
