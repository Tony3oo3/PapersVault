## Problem

Autoregressive image generators underperform diffusion and masked-transformer models because causal attention restricts context to a unidirectional scan order, whereas visual data inherently requires bidirectional context modeling. Prior fixes such as VAR (next-scale prediction) and MAR (masked AR) reintroduce bidirectionality but deviate from the standard next-token-prediction AR formulation, breaking compatibility with the language-modeling framework that underpins unified multimodal architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250778/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model is conditioned on an ImageNet-1K class label (1000 classes, dropped with probability 0.1 during training) and autoregressively generates a sequence of 256 discrete VQ tokens (codebook size 1024) that are decoded by the MaskGIT-VQGAN tokenizer into a 256×256 RGB image. At inference time, sampling follows the fixed raster-scan order with classifier-free guidance (power-cosine schedule), and the target-aware positional embedding is merged into the standard positional embedding so the model is indistinguishable from a conventional AR image generator.

## Method

RAR modifies only the training objective of a standard causal-attention ViT: at each step, the token sequence is randomly permuted with probability r, and the model is trained with next-token prediction under that permuted order, so in expectation every token is predicted under every possible context, capturing bidirectional dependencies while preserving the AR factorization. A linearly decaying schedule (randomness annealing) starts with r=1 (fully random permutations) and ends at r=0 (pure raster order), letting the model first learn rich bidirectional representations and then specialize to the empirically best scan order. To resolve ambiguity when two permutations yield identical preceding-token features but different targets, the authors add a target-aware positional embedding that encodes the index of the token being predicted, which is discarded at inference. The backbone is a plain ViT (24–40 layers, 768–1408 width) with QK LayerNorm and adaLN class conditioning, trained for 400 epochs with AdamW on pre-tokenized ImageNet-1K, achieving FID 1.48 at 1.5B parameters on ImageNet-256.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K 256×256, RAR-XXL (1.5B params) achieves a new state-of-the-art FID of 1.48, IS of 326.0, Precision of 0.80, and Recall of 0.63. The smallest variant RAR-B (261M params) already reaches FID 1.95, outperforming LlamaGen-3B-384 (3.1B, FID 2.18) and Open-MAGVIT2-XL (1.5B, FID 2.33) with 91% and 81% fewer parameters respectively. RAR also demonstrates strong sampling speed: RAR-XL generates 8.3 images/sec (vs. 0.7 for MaskBit and 0.3 for MAR-H at comparable FID), leveraging KV-cache optimization inherited from the autoregressive framework.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAR-XXL (FID 1.48) outperforms all prior autoregressive generators: LlamaGen-3B-384 (FID 2.18, 3.1B params), Open-MAGVIT2-XL (FID 2.33, 1.5B), and VIM-L-re (FID 3.04, 1.7B). It also surpasses the two recent methods that modify the AR formulation for vision—VAR-d30-re (FID 1.73, 2.0B) and MAR-H (FID 1.55, 943M)—while retaining full compatibility with standard language-modeling architectures. Against leading diffusion models, RAR-XXL beats MDTv2-XL/2 (FID 1.58) and DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.27), and RAR-B (261M) already matches or exceeds SiT-XL (FID 2.06, 675M) with only 39% of the parameters. Among masked transformers, RAR-XL (FID 1.50) edges out MaskBit (FID 1.52, 305M) while being 3× faster in sampling (8.3 vs. 0.7 images/sec). The key differentiator is that RAR achieves these gains without altering the autoregressive next-token prediction objective or architecture, making it directly compatible with LLM optimization techniques like KV-cache and vLLM.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAR builds directly on the standard next-token-prediction autoregressive framework used by LlamaGen, Open-MAGVIT2, and GPT-2-style image generators, which all process tokens in a fixed raster-scan order. It departs from recent attempts to fix AR for vision—VAR (next-scale prediction) and MAR (masked autoregressive with diffusion loss)—by preserving the exact AR formulation and causal attention mask, instead introducing a randomized permutation training objective with a linearly annealing schedule. The permuted objective draws on the NLP literature (NADE, BERT-style pretraining), but the key novelty is the randomness annealing strategy that transitions from fully random to raster order, which the paper shows is critical: pure random-order training yields only marginal gains over raster, while annealing produces a large FID improvement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RAR is the first language-modeling-compatible AR image generator to outperform both leading diffusion models (DiT, SiT, MDTv2) and masked transformers (MaskBit, MAGVIT-v2) on ImageNet-256, which is significant for the push toward unified multimodal models that rely on a single next-token-prediction backbone. The technique is remarkably simple—a training-schedule change with no architectural modification—making it easy to adopt in existing LLM pipelines. The paper acknowledges that during generation the model still lacks full global context (tokens are produced sequentially), and suggests resampling or refinement as future directions; it also notes that the target-aware positional embedding is only needed during training and can be merged at inference. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to text-to-image generation, higher resolutions, and continuous-token (VAE-based) settings.
