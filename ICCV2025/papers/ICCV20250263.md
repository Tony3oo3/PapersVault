## Problem

Linear Attention achieves O(N) complexity by rearranging the computation order of Q, K, and V, but suffers from significant performance degradation compared to Softmax Attention. The paper identifies the root cause: in Linear Attention's reformulated computation, the magnitude of φ(Q) cancels out entirely, leaving only the directional component, so the attention score distribution cannot dynamically concentrate on relevant keys as the Query scales—unlike Softmax Attention where increasing Query magnitude produces increasingly spiky distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250263/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

MALA is a drop-in attention replacement within existing architectures, so inputs and outputs are task-determined: RGB images at 224×224 (classification) or 512×512 (segmentation) yield class labels, bounding boxes, instance masks, or pixel-wise segmentation maps; text sequences yield token predictions; audio waveforms yield transcripts; and noise tensors yield generated images. The kernel function φ(·) (default ELU(·)+1) maps Q and K into non-negative space, and the attention scores are guaranteed to sum to 1 per query via the addition-based normalization, though individual scores can theoretically be negative (observed to never occur in practice).

## Method

The key insight is that in standard Linear Attention, decomposing φ(Q_i) into magnitude ||φ(Q_i)|| and direction α_i causes the magnitude to cancel in the numerator and denominator of the attention computation, fixing the score distribution regardless of Query scale. MALA replaces the division-based normalization with an addition-based scheme: Attn(Q_i, K_j) = βφ(Q_i)φ(K_j)^T − γ, where β = 1 + 1/(φ(Q_i)Σ_m φ(K_m)^T) and γ = φ(Q_i)Σ_m φ(K_m)^T / N, ensuring scores still sum to 1. When the magnitude of φ(Q_i) scales by factor a, the attention score ratio between two keys changes from p to p_m > p (fractional growth), mimicking Softmax's concentration behavior but with a gentler rate than Softmax's exponential growth, yielding a more balanced distribution. The full output is Y_i = βφ(Q_i)Σ_j φ(K_j)^T V_j − γ Σ_j V_j, preserving O(N) complexity. Based on MALA, the authors build MAViT (Magnitude-Aware Vision Transformer) and validate the mechanism across classification, detection, segmentation, NLP, speech, and generation tasks.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification, MAViT achieves 82.9% (Tiny), 84.7% (Small), 85.7% (Base), and 86.0% (Large) top-1 accuracy, setting new state-of-the-art among linear-attention models at every scale. On COCO object detection (Cascade Mask R-CNN, 3×+MS), MAViT-B reaches 55.5 AP^b / 48.0 AP^m and MAViT-L reaches 56.0 AP^b / 48.4 AP^m, surpassing the larger CSwin-B (53.9/46.4). On ADE20K semantic segmentation (UperNet, 160K), MAViT-L achieves 53.6 mIoU. In NLP, MALA matches or exceeds Transformer on LAMB (31.0 vs 31.0) and WinoGrande (51.9 vs 50.4) while matching Mamba on PIQA (65.0). For speech recognition, MALA reduces WER to 2.4/5.3 (testclean/testother, without LM), outperforming both Conformer (2.7/6.3) and vanilla Linear Attention (3.4/10.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet-1K, MAViT outperforms the closest linear-attention competitor RAVLT by +0.1% (Tiny), +0.3% (Small), +0.2% (Base), and +0.2% (Large), and surpasses the strongest softmax-attention baselines (RMT, SECViT) by 0.2–0.7% at each scale. Among other linear-attention methods, MAViT-L (86.0%) leads InLine-CSwin-B (84.5%) by 1.5% and SOFT-Huge (83.3%) by 2.7%. On COCO detection (3×+MS), MAViT-B (55.5 AP^b) beats the larger CSwin-B (53.9 AP^b) by 1.6 and RMT-B (54.5) by 1.0; MAViT-L (56.0 AP^b) extends this lead. On ADE20K (UperNet), MAViT-L (53.6 mIoU) surpasses RMT-L (52.8) by 0.8 and MILA-B (51.9) by 1.7. In NLP, MALA ties Transformer on LAMB (31.0) and WinoGrande (51.9 vs 50.4) while matching Mamba on PIQA (65.0), though Mamba edges ahead on HellaSwag (35.4 vs 34.5). For speech recognition, MALA improves over Conformer by 0.3/1.0 WER (without LM) and 0.2/0.8 WER (with LM), and dramatically outperforms vanilla Linear Attention (2.4 vs 3.4 WER on testclean). In image generation, MALA achieves FID 49.62 and IS 32.18, substantially beating DiT-S/2 (FID 68.40), DiG-S/2 (FID 62.06), and DiC-S/2 (FID 58.68) while also delivering the highest throughput (5.6 images/s).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Vision Transformer (ViT) and Swin Transformer architectures, replacing their Softmax Attention with a modified Linear Attention mechanism. It departs from prior linear attention improvements (MILA, Flatten Transformer, InLine, RAVLT) that typically add auxiliary components—convolutions, macro architectures, or rank-enhancement—to compensate for linear attention's weaknesses. Instead, MALA identifies a single root cause in the linear attention formulation itself: the complete cancellation of Query magnitude during the algebraic rearrangement that achieves linear complexity. The fix is a minimal two-term modification (scaling factor β and offset γ) that restores magnitude sensitivity without reintroducing quadratic cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of magnitude neglect as the fundamental reason for linear attention's performance gap provides a clean theoretical lens through which to evaluate all existing and future linear attention variants. The practical impact is significant: MALA is a drop-in replacement that achieves SOTA or near-SOTA results across seven diverse tasks (classification, detection, segmentation, NLP, speech, generation) while maintaining linear complexity, making it immediately adoptable. Open questions include whether the theoretically possible negative/zero attention scores could emerge in other settings, whether the fractional (vs. exponential) concentration rate is truly optimal, and how the approach interacts with other efficiency techniques like token reduction or low-rank factorization.
