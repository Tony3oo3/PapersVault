## Problem

Open-source MLLMs achieve only 30–50% accuracy on challenging chart understanding benchmarks, and existing synthetic chart training datasets (PlotQA, ChartBench, ReachQA, etc.) suffer from limited visual diversity, restricted chart type coverage, and inauthentic styles that reduce their similarity to real scientific plots. This gap between synthetic training data and real-world chart complexity limits the effectiveness of fine-tuning on these datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250756/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes chart images rendered from Python/Matplotlib/Seaborn code (single plots or multi-subplot figures with up to 4+ subplots) along with their generation code and underlying data tables, and produces two types of QA pairs: descriptive questions whose ground truth is a single final answer, and reasoning questions whose ground truth includes a step-by-step rationale followed by the final answer. The final ECD contains 10,535 chart images spanning 29 chart types, 25 themes, and 252 type combinations, paired with 321,544 QA pairs. A confidence-based filter (GPT-4o self-assigned score of 5 out of 5) removes approximately 7.8% of initially generated QA pairs to eliminate ambiguous or erroneous entries.

## Method

The core contribution is a five-step modular data synthesis pipeline that decouples chart generation into separable stages to maximize both data complexity and visual realism. First, GPT-4o generates data tables and plot parameters (titles, axis labels, markers) conditioned on human-defined chart functions (29 types) and a chart theme, rather than generating full plotting code, which allows richer data distributions. Second, multi-subplot figures are produced via conditional sequential generation, where each new subplot is informed by all prior subplots to maintain thematic coherence. Third, a diversification step prompts GPT-4o to modify the underlying Python code with randomly selected visual enhancements (annotations, arrows, area shading, zoom-in insets, font/axis modifications, Seaborn styling) to break the uniformity of template-based rendering. Fourth, GPT-4o scores each chart on visual clarity and semantic coherence, and charts scoring below the dataset mean are discarded (~37.4% removed). Fifth, GPT-4o generates both descriptive and reasoning QA pairs with self-assigned confidence scores, retaining only the highest-confidence entries. Fine-tuning is performed via LoRA (rank 8–64) or full-parameter SFT on four open-source MLLMs with the vision tower frozen, and the resulting ECD consistently outperforms fine-tuning on ChartQA, ChartBench, or ReachQA across all six evaluation benchmarks.

## Results

Fine-tuning four open-source MLLMs with ECD yields consistent improvements across six benchmarks. LLaVA-Next-Llama3-8B sees the largest gains: CharXiv average accuracy rises from 35.06% to 51.60% (+16.54 pp), ChartX from 27.69% to 46.61% (+18.92 pp), and ECDBench from 10.95% to 31.58% (+20.63 pp). Qwen2.5-VL-7B, the strongest open-source baseline, improves from 61.36% to 67.40% on CharXiv and from 38.19% to 50.86% on ECDBench. Even after ECD fine-tuning, all four open-source models remain below proprietary GPT-4o (76.98% CharXiv avg) and Claude 3.5 Sonnet (79.48% CharXiv avg), but the gap narrows substantially.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against proprietary models, ECD-fine-tuned open-source models still trail: GPT-4o achieves 76.98% CharXiv avg vs. the best open-source result of 67.40% (Qwen2.5-VL-7B + ECD), and Claude 3.5 Sonnet leads at 79.48%. Among the four open-source base models, ECD fine-tuning produces the largest absolute gains on LLaVA-Next-Llama3-8B (+16.54 pp CharXiv, +18.92 pp ChartX) and Phi-3-Vision (+6.36 pp CharXiv, +3.91 pp ChartX), while Qwen2.5-VL-7B shows smaller but still positive gains (+6.04 pp CharXiv, +3.03 pp ChartX) with minor drops on CharXiv reasoning (41.20→40.20) and ReachQA recognition (71.90→70.80). In the training-set comparison (Table 3), ECD consistently outperforms ChartQA, ChartBench, and ReachQA as training data: on LLaVA-Next-Llama3-8B, ECD achieves 51.60% CharXiv vs. 35.16% (ChartQA), 32.86% (ChartBench), and 30.68% (ReachQA), and 46.61% ChartX vs. 31.51%, 37.33%, and 39.24% respectively. The key distinction is that ECD improves across all five test sets simultaneously, whereas each alternative training set boosts its own distribution at the expense of others (e.g., ChartBench training yields 75.13% on ChartBench but drops to 32.86% on CharXiv).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of synthetic chart dataset construction (PlotQA, ChartBench, ReachQA, ChartX) by introducing a modular five-stage pipeline that separates data generation from chart-function rendering, uses conditional sequential generation for multi-subplot coherence, and applies GPT-4o at multiple stages for diversification and QA synthesis. It departs from prior template-based or single-pass code-generation approaches by treating chart creation as a decomposed, quality-controlled process, and explicitly targets the realism gap (measured via FID against CharXiv) that limits earlier synthetic datasets. The work also builds on the broader MLLM fine-tuning paradigm (LoRA, SFT) but contributes primarily at the data level rather than the model architecture level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ECD provides a practical, reproducible resource (10.5k images, 321k QA pairs, 29 chart types, 252 type combinations) that consistently improves four open-source MLLMs across six benchmarks, outperforming fine-tuning on existing chart datasets like ChartQA, ChartBench, and ReachQA which show mixed cross-benchmark results. The ECDBench adds a human-verified test set that better reflects real scientific figure complexity. Open questions include whether the pipeline scales to pre-training (the authors note gains saturate beyond 20k images on CharXiv), whether the 2:3 descriptive-to-reasoning ratio finding generalizes, and whether the approach transfers to other programmable visualizations beyond charts.
