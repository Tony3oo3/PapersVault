## Problem

Existing vector mapping methods are designed for small image patches (typically 512×512) and, when applied to large-size remote sensing imagery exceeding 10,000×10,000 pixels, resort to a patch-based strategy that discards critical contextual information and produces fragmented vector outputs at patch boundaries. This 'large-size challenge' is compounded by significant scale variations across geographic object categories (e.g., continuous water bodies vs. scattered buildings), for which no unified holistic vectorization framework existed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252396/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single large-size RGB remote sensing image (10,000×10,000 to 12,800×12,800 pixels at 0.075–0.3 m spatial resolution) as input and directly produces complete vectorized polygon contours with identified vertex coordinates for geographic objects (buildings, water bodies, roads) without any patching or stitching. The output is structured as ordered point sequences per polygon, where each point carries a vertex/non-vertex label, enabling direct use in GIS vector formats. Unlike prior methods that process 512×512 crops independently, HoliTracer perceives the full image context in a single forward pass.

## Method

HoliTracer is a three-stage pipeline: (1) a Context Attention Net (CAN) builds a multi-scale image pyramid (downsampling rates d₁<d₂<…<dₙ) and fuses features across scales via a local-to-global attention mechanism where the bottom-layer feature serves as query and upper-layer features as key/value, feeding a UperNet decoder for complete segmentation; (2) a Mask Contour Reformer (MCR) extracts dense contours via TC89-KCOS, simplifies them with Douglas-Peucker, then reconstructs regularized polygons by interpolating at a fixed distance, using a bidirectional matching mechanism to establish one-to-one correspondence with ground truth during training; (3) a Polygon Sequence Tracer (PST) performs iterative offset refinement (inspired by DeepSnake) through a transformer regressor, computes multi-scale angle features at each point, and classifies vertices via a transformer predictor supervised by Smooth L1 offset loss, BCE vertex loss, and an angle penalty loss that encourages high vertex probability at sharp angles below a 135° threshold.

## Results

On WHU-building, HoliTracer achieves CIoU 82.30, IoU 91.60, and AP 61.07, outperforming the best baselines (UniVec at CIoU 62.26, LCF-ALE at IoU 85.67, HiSup at AP 56.77). On GLH-water, it reaches CIoU 59.24, IoU 85.68, and AP 20.84, with a particularly large margin over the next-best AP (DeepSnake at 2.54). On VHR-road, HoliTracer attains IoU 46.48 and CIoU 6.10, surpassing the best baselines (HiSup at IoU 36.40, DeepSnake at CIoU 4.67). Across all three datasets, HoliTracer achieves the best score on every reported metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HoliTracer outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across all three datasets. On WHU-building, the closest competitor is HiSup [33] (segmentation-based), which HoliTracer surpasses by 20.04 points on CIoU (82.30 vs. 42.23) and 5.81 points on IoU (91.60 vs. 85.79); UniVec [37] (graph-based) is the best vectorization baseline at CIoU 62.26 but still trails by 20 points. On GLH-water, the advantage is most dramatic: HoliTracer's AP of 20.84 exceeds the next-best DeepSnake [25] (2.54) by over 8×, and its IoU of 85.68 beats LCF-ALE [23] (70.74) by 14.94 points, reflecting the severe fragmentation that patch-based methods suffer on large water bodies. On VHR-road, HiSup [33] is the strongest baseline (IoU 36.40), and HoliTracer leads by 10.04 points on IoU and 1.43 on CIoU, though absolute scores remain low due to road network complexity. The two pure segmentation methods (TS-MTA [7], LCF-ALE [23]) produce the worst vector metrics (CIoU < 30 on water, < 20 on buildings) because their TC89-KCOS + DP post-processing cannot recover geometric fidelity. Contour-based methods (DeepSnake [25], E2EC [38]) and the graph-based UniVec [37] show intermediate vector quality but still fall well short of HoliTracer's holistic tracing.

## Relation to Prior Work

HoliTracer synthesizes ideas from three distinct lines of work: segmentation-based vectorization (FFL, HiSup, Wei et al.), iterative contour refinement (DeepSnake's offset regression), and multi-scale large-image processing (TS-MTA, LCF-ALE). Rather than building on a single prior method, it repurposes the segmentation-then-vectorization paradigm by replacing the patch-based segmentation stage with a multi-scale pyramid and context attention mechanism, and the contour refinement stage with a sequence-based tracer that incorporates geometric angle features. The key conceptual departure is treating the large-size RSI as a holistic input rather than decomposing it into independent patches, which prior vectorization methods (UniVec, HiSup, E2EC) all require due to their 512×512 input constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical gap: as satellite and UAV imagery resolution increases, vector mapping pipelines that rely on patch-based processing produce fragmented, context-poor results that are unusable for downstream applications like navigation and urban planning. The introduction of the VHR-road dataset (treating roads as planar elements at 12,500×12,500 resolution) fills a notable benchmark void for large-scale road vectorization. However, road vectorization remains substantially harder than buildings or water (AP of only 1.58, CIoU of 6.10), and the method's reliance on a frozen Swin-L encoder plus multi-scale pyramid slicing raises open questions about computational cost and scalability to even larger scenes or real-time deployment.
