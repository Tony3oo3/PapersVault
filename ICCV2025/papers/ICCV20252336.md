## Problem

Existing NeRF-based segmentation and view synthesis methods rely solely on RGB data, which captures color and geometry but lacks intrinsic material properties, leading to ambiguities such as metamerism where distinct materials appear identical under a given illumination. While spectral imaging provides rich per-wavelength signatures that naturally encode material identity, prior hyperspectral NeRF extensions treat spectral bands as independent output channels without exploiting the structured linear-mixing nature of real-world spectral signatures (endmembers and abundances). No prior view synthesis framework jointly performs high-fidelity spectral novel view generation and physically grounded material decomposition.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view hyperspectral images with calibrated camera poses; spectral band counts vary by dataset (21 bands at 450–650 nm for NeSpoF, 128 bands at 370–1100 nm for Surface Optics, 140 bands at 400–1110 nm for BaySpec). The number of endmembers K is set as a heuristic approximation of the number of distinct materials in the scene. Outputs include a full hyperspectral novel view, an RGB image obtained via a 3×B camera spectral response matrix, per-ray abundance vectors (K-dim, sum-to-one) for unsupervised material segmentation, and a learned K-column endmember dictionary that can be directly edited for material-based scene manipulation.

## Method

UnMix-NeRF embeds the Extended Linear Mixing Model (ELMM) at every 3D point within a NeRF volumetric rendering pipeline. A learnable global endmember dictionary E ∈ R^{B×K} is jointly optimized with the network (optionally initialized via VCA), while per-point MLPs predict K scaling factors (sigmoid-activated, modeling local illumination variability) and K abundances (temperature-scaled softmax, enforcing non-negativity and sum-to-one). The diffuse reflectance is computed as c_d = E·S̃·ã, and a separate view-dependent specular branch (motivated by Ref-NeRF) predicts c_s modulated by a learned tint factor h, yielding the final per-point spectral radiance c = c_d + h·c_s under the dichromatic model. Volume rendering accumulates both spectral radiance and abundance vectors along each ray, and a spectral-to-RGB projection matrix M converts the rendered hyperspectral signature to sRGB. Training minimizes a weighted sum of hyperspectral L2 and RGB L2 losses (λ_spec=5, λ_rgb=1), with endmembers clamped to [0,1] each iteration; material segmentation is then obtained post-hoc by a cluster probe that computes normalized inner products between rendered spectral signatures and the learned endmembers.

## Results

On the BaySpec dataset, UnMix-NeRF achieves the best performance across all three scenes (Pinecone, Caladium, Anacampseros), reaching PSNR of 27.13, 30.08, and 28.20 respectively, with the lowest SAM (0.0287, 0.0237, 0.0154) and RMSE (0.0429, 0.0312, 0.0392). On the Surface Optics dataset, it obtains 28.91 and 29.21 PSNR on Rosemary and Basil, outperforming HyperGS by +2.14 and +3.91 PSNR. On NeSpoF, the method achieves 33.2 average PSNR in 44 minutes, matching NeSpoF's 33.0 PSNR while reducing training time from 11.9 hours to under 1 hour. For unsupervised material segmentation, the method achieves an average F1 of 0.41 and mIoU of 0.28 across all NeSpoF scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UnMix-NeRF outperforms all five baselines across every metric on all three datasets. Against HyperGS (the strongest competitor), it gains +3.15 PSNR on Caladium (30.08 vs 27.70), +2.14 PSNR on Rosemary (28.91 vs 26.77), and +3.91 PSNR on Basil (29.21 vs 25.30), while also achieving lower SAM and RMSE. On NeSpoF, UnMix-NeRF matches NeSpoF's average PSNR (33.2 vs 33.0) but reduces training time from 11.9 hours to 44 minutes per scene. HS-NeRF, which treats spectral bands as independent channels, is consistently outperformed by 5–10+ PSNR across scenes, confirming the benefit of explicit spectral unmixing over per-pixel spectral regression. Standard NeRF variants (NeRF, MipNeRF, TensoRF, Nerfacto, MipNeRF360) adapted for hyperspectral data lag significantly behind, with Nerfacto achieving the worst PSNR (15.36 on Pinecone), highlighting the difficulty of modeling spectral structure without unmixing constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NeRF volumetric rendering framework by embedding the Extended Linear Mixing Model (ELMM) from hyperspectral unmixing directly into the scene representation, replacing the standard per-wavelength radiance output with a decomposition into learned global endmembers and per-point abundances. Prior hyperspectral NeRF methods (HS-NeRF, SpectralNeRF, HyperGS) treat spectral bands as independent output channels, ignoring the structured material signatures encoded in the data; UnMix-NeRF departs from this by explicitly modeling the linear mixing structure. The specular component draws on the dichromatic model and tint-factor modulation from Ref-NeRF, while the volumetric rendering and Nerfacto backbone follow standard NeRF practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By tying spectral reconstruction to a physically grounded unmixing model, the framework yields unsupervised material segmentation and endmember-based scene editing as natural byproducts, addressing the metamerism limitation of RGB-only approaches that is critical for robotics, AR, and simulation. However, the segmentation metrics remain modest (F1 ≈ 0.41, mIoU ≈ 0.28), the number of endmembers K must be set heuristically per scene, and the method requires multi-view hyperspectral data that is expensive to acquire. Open questions include whether the linear mixing assumption generalizes to strongly nonlinear material interactions, how the approach scales to scenes with many distinct materials, and whether the learned endmembers remain stable under large viewpoint changes.
