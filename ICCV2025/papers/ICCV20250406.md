## Problem

Existing affordance prediction methods reduce the task to coarse object-part segmentation or task-agnostic interaction hotspots, failing to capture the precise, task-specific regions humans actually use when interacting with objects. Moreover, no prior work addresses bimanual affordance prediction—where two hands coordinate across one or more objects—and all existing datasets rely on manual annotation that lacks the precision of real hand-object contact regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250406/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a single RGB image and a free-form natural language task prompt (e.g., 'pour tea from kettle') and outputs two pixel-wise binary segmentation masks (left-hand and right-hand affordance regions) along with a three-class bimanual taxonomy label (unimanual-left, unimanual-right, or bimanual). Unlike prior methods that predict heatmaps or point labels, the output is a precise per-pixel mask, and the text prompt is an open-vocabulary narration rather than a fixed affordance class from a predefined taxonomy.

## Method

The pipeline has two components. First, a data extraction procedure generates the 2HANDS dataset (278K images) by propagating sparse hand-object masks across video frames with XMem, inpainting out occluding hands using the video-based VIDM model, completing the un-occluded object masks via SAM2 propagation, and intersecting the completed object masks with hand masks to yield precise affordance regions labeled by task narrations. Second, the 2HandedAfforder prediction model uses a frozen LLaVA-13B VLM (fine-tuned via LoRA) to process the image and text prompt jointly, producing a [SEG] token that encodes task-relevant reasoning; this token is combined with features from a frozen SAM image encoder and fed into two SAM-style mask decoders (one per hand) plus an MLP taxonomy head. Training uses a combination of dice loss and focal cross-entropy for mask prediction and cross-entropy for taxonomy, with the loss weight for a hand's mask set to zero when the task does not involve that hand.

## Results

On the combined ActAffordance benchmark, 2HandedAfforder achieves the best precision (0.130) and mAP (0.104) among all model-based methods, with a directional Hausdorff distance of 202. On the ActAffordance-Cropped benchmark (where images are cropped to target objects), 2HandedAfforder further improves to precision 0.269 and mAP 0.240, outperforming all baselines. The extraction pipeline (AffExtract) achieves a much higher precision of 0.420 on the combined benchmark, confirming alignment with human-annotated affordance regions. Notably, 2HandedAfforder generalizes to Ego4D (precision 0.137) despite no Ego4D data being used in training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

2HandedAfforder outperforms all five baselines on the combined ActAffordance benchmark in precision and mAP, achieving 0.130 precision vs. LISA's 0.050 and AffordanceLLM's 0.013. On the ActAffordance-Cropped benchmark, 2HandedAfforder leads with 0.269 precision and 0.240 mAP, beating 3DOI (0.224 precision, 0.180 mAP) and LISA (0.122 precision, 0.116 mAP). LISA is the strongest reasoning-segmentation baseline, benefiting from its ability to identify the correct object in the scene, but it cannot predict precise affordance sub-regions. 3DOI performs competitively on precision in the cropped setting (0.224) thanks to its query-point mechanism, but lags in mAP. AffordanceLLM achieves surprisingly low HD and Dir. HD in the cropped benchmark (155, 76) because it is 'optimistic' and always predicts some region, whereas other methods sometimes produce no mask at all. VRB, a task-agnostic hotspot method, performs worst overall (0.088 precision), confirming that task-specific affordance prediction is substantially harder than generic interaction-region detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reasoning segmentation paradigm of LISA [22] by replacing its single mask decoder with dual left/right hand affordance decoders and a bimanual taxonomy classifier, while leveraging SAM/SAM2 as a frozen vision backbone. It departs fundamentally from prior affordance detection work (IIT-AFF, AGD20K, 3DOI, VRB) by arguing that manually-labeled affordances reduce the problem to imprecise object-part segmentation, and instead proposes auto-extracting precise, task-specific affordance masks from egocentric human activity videos via a hand-inpainting (VIDM) and mask-completion (SAM2) pipeline. The bimanual dimension—where two hands coordinate on one or more objects—is explicitly positioned as absent from all prior affordance datasets and methods, making this the first work to formulate and address task-specific bimanual affordance region prediction from text prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2HANDS dataset (278K images) and ActAffordance benchmark fill a critical gap: no prior resource provides precise, task-oriented, bimanual affordance masks, and the benchmark's design (showing annotators interaction sequences rather than static images) better captures the multimodal nature of affordance prediction. However, the extremely low IoU scores across all methods (≤0.09 combined) reveal that prompt-based precise affordance segmentation remains far from solved, and the gap between the extraction pipeline's precision (0.42) and model predictions (0.13) highlights a significant learning bottleneck. Open questions include whether the VLM reasoning component provides meaningful gains over simpler CLIP-based approaches (the margin is modest), whether the kitchen-centric data generalizes to broader domains, and whether the low absolute performance reflects inherent task difficulty or insufficient model capacity.
