## Problem

Reconstructing a complete high-resolution image from pixels sampled at irregular, non-grid positions remains challenging because standard CNNs require regular 2D array inputs, and prior approaches either rely on fixed interpolation kernels with limited adaptability or force irregular pixels onto a finite-resolution grid with zero-padding. This paper presents the first end-to-end deep network that directly consumes an unordered list of irregularly sampled pixels (with decimal coordinates) and produces a continuous high-resolution image without any grid-mapping step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252430/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes a list of N irregularly sampled pixels, each represented as a 5-dimensional vector (x, y, R, G, B) where coordinates are arbitrary decimal values not constrained to integer grid positions. During training, N=2304 pixels are randomly sampled per 48×48 patch with sampling ratios drawn from U(0.0625, 1). The output is a high-resolution RGB image of arbitrary spatial size, generated by querying the learned continuous image function at any desired coordinate, enabling single-model inference across all upscaling scales (×1.5, ×2, ×2.5, ×3, ×4).

## Method

ISP2HRNet hierarchically extracts gradient structure information inspired by Taylor expansion. A Gradient Derivation (GD) module estimates first-order gradients at each irregular pixel by forming K triangles from its K nearest neighbors and computing a weighted average of triangle normal vectors. A Local Gradient Structure Encoder (GSE) then feeds per-triangle geometric and photometric vertex descriptors through a shared MLP and aggregates with a symmetric function to obtain higher-order structural features per pixel. An Irregular-to-Regular Conversion (IRC) module maps these features onto a regular grid using a dual-branch content-dependent weight generator that separately produces softmax-normalized weights for pixel values and element-wise weights for structural features. An EDSR-baseline encoder extracts latent codes on the regular grid, and a LIIF-style MLP decodes (latent code, relative coordinate) pairs into pixel values, yielding a continuous image representation that supports arbitrary output resolutions.

## Results

ISP2HRNet achieves the best PSNR and SSIM across all five test datasets (Set5, Set14, B100, Urban100, DIV2K Val) at every sampling ratio tested (0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8). At the most challenging ratio of 0.2, it reaches 30.94 dB PSNR on Set5 and 27.43 dB on Urban100, surpassing the strongest baseline (DPIR) by 1.24 dB and 1.09 dB respectively. At ratio 0.6, it achieves 37.80 dB on Set5 and 34.72 dB on Urban100. The method also generalizes to super-resolution scales (×1.5 to ×4) and to truly irregular (decimal-coordinate) sampling, where it reports 30.92 dB on Set5 at r=0.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ISP2HRNet outperforms all ten baselines on every dataset and sampling ratio in the main reconstruction task (Table 1). The closest competitor is DPIR [56], which leads among baselines at higher sampling ratios; at r=0.2 on Set5, ISP2HRNet gains 1.24 dB over DPIR (30.94 vs 29.70), and the margin narrows to ~0.65 dB at r=0.8 (41.93 vs CODE's 41.28). Inpainting-oriented methods (Partial Conv, RFR, MISF) and the transformer-based CODE [57] trail by 1–4 dB, indicating that treating irregular sampling as a standard inpainting problem is suboptimal. In the super-resolution task (Table 2), ISP2HRNet wins on most scale/dataset combinations but is slightly outperformed by DPIR+EDSR on Urban100 at ×2 (25.34 vs 25.69) and ×3 (22.87 vs 23.15), suggesting the INR decoder is less effective on highly structured urban textures at moderate scales. Overall, the hierarchical gradient learning + dual-branch IRC design consistently yields the strongest reconstruction quality, with the largest absolute gains at the lowest sampling ratios where information is most scarce.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: classical image reconstruction from irregular samples (spline interpolation, kernel regression), deep image inpainting/restoration (DPDNN, DPIR, RRSNet), and implicit neural representation for continuous image modeling (LIIF). It borrows the PointNet paradigm of shared MLPs with symmetric aggregation to process unordered irregular pixels, and adopts LIIF's coordinate-conditioned MLP decoder for arbitrary-resolution output. The key departure is the hierarchical gradient learning pipeline—deriving first-order gradients via local triangle normals and learning higher-order structural features via a GSE module—inspired by Taylor expansion, which no prior deep method for irregular sampling has attempted. The dual-branch content-dependent weight generator for irregular-to-regular conversion is a novel mechanism that replaces the ad hoc zero-padding strategy used by prior deep restoration networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes the first end-to-end deep learning framework for HR image reconstruction from truly irregular (non-integer, non-grid) pixel coordinates, a setting that prior methods either ignored or handled with lossy discretization. The ability to handle arbitrary numbers of input pixels and arbitrary output resolutions with a single model has practical relevance for sensor-based imaging, remote sensing, and medical microscopy where sampling is inherently irregular. Limitations include reliance on bicubic interpolation to generate training targets at irregular coordinates (a somewhat circular assumption), evaluation limited to 48×48 patches, and comparison primarily against inpainting baselines rather than purpose-built irregular-sampling methods. Open questions include scalability to full-resolution images, robustness to non-uniform sampling densities, and extension to other modalities (e.g., hyperspectral, 3D volume data).
