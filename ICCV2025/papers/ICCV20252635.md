## Problem

Generalizable 3D reconstruction faces a fundamental trade-off in multi-view correspondence: explicit cost-volume methods achieve geometric precision but fail under occlusions, textureless regions, and repetitive patterns, while implicit attention-based methods provide robustness but converge slowly. Additionally, prevailing semantic-aligned visual encoders (e.g., DINOv2) sacrifice the pixel-level spatial fidelity that 3D reconstruction demands, creating a representation mismatch that limits reconstruction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252635/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The base H3R model takes 2 sparse RGB context images at 256×256 with known camera poses and produces pixel-aligned 3D Gaussian primitives (2×256×256×1) rendered via rasterization. H3R-α extends to 2–8 context views at 256×256 and additionally accepts target-view camera poses (without images) to generate Gaussians for unobserved regions, while H3R-β operates on 2 views at 512×512 for high-resolution output. The Gaussian parameterization follows GS-LRM: 3-channel RGB, 3-channel scale, 4-channel rotation quaternion, 1-channel opacity, and 1-channel ray distance, with centers derived from ray distances and camera intrinsics.

## Method

H3R integrates two complementary correspondence modules: (1) a Volumetric Latent Fusion stage that warps multi-view features onto uniformly sampled inverse-depth planes via differentiable homography and concatenates them into a cost-free latent volume (no explicit similarity metric), and (2) a Camera-aware Transformer that embeds 6D Plücker ray coordinates as positional encodings and applies self-attention across all flattened view tokens to implicitly refine cross-view correspondences. The fused features are decoded into Gaussian parameters by a hierarchical CNN with sequential upsampling ResBlocks. A key empirical finding is that spatial-aligned encoders (SD-VAE, MAE) consistently outperform semantic-aligned ones (DINOv2, CLIP, DeiT-III), with SD-VAE achieving the best quality-to-parameter ratio. Training uses MSE + LPIPS + gradient-MAE losses, and the hybrid design converges 2× faster than TranSplat (150k vs. 300k steps) while achieving superior PSNR across RealEstate10K, ACID, and DTU.

## Results

On RealEstate10K, H3R achieves 27.60 PSNR / 0.891 SSIM / 0.114 LPIPS from two 256×256 context views, outperforming the strongest baseline (TranSplat, 26.69 PSNR) by +0.91 dB. The high-resolution variant H3R-β (512×512) further reaches 28.03 PSNR, surpassing DepthSplat (27.44) by +0.59 dB. On ACID, H3R-β achieves 28.71 PSNR / 0.853 SSIM, and in cross-dataset transfer (trained on RealEstate10K, zero-shot on ACID), H3R-β reaches 29.23 PSNR, a +1.06 dB gain over TranSplat. On DTU (object-centric, 2 context views), H3R achieves 15.15 PSNR, improving +0.22 dB over TranSplat (14.93).

## Baselines & Comparisons

H3R decisively outperforms all NeRF-based baselines (pixelNeRF, GPNR, AttnRend, MuRF) by 1.5–7.2 dB PSNR on RealEstate10K, confirming the advantage of 3DGS representations. Among 3DGS methods, H3R beats pixelSplat by +1.51 dB and MVSplat by +1.21 dB on RealEstate10K, and edges out pixelSplat (+0.02 dB) and MVSplat (+0.04 dB) on ACID. The closest competitors are TranSplat and eFreeSplat: H3R wins on RealEstate10K by +0.91 and +1.15 dB respectively, but trails by a narrow margin on ACID (−0.06 dB vs. eFreeSplat, −0.06 dB vs. TranSplat); the H3R-α and H3R-β variants close this gap, reaching 28.44 and 28.71 PSNR on ACID. In cross-dataset transfer (Table 2), H3R-β achieves 29.23 PSNR on ACID (+1.06 dB over TranSplat) and 15.15 PSNR on DTU (+0.22 dB over TranSplat), demonstrating stronger generalization than all baselines. MVSplat360 and latentSplat are only evaluated on RealEstate10K, where H3R surpasses them by +1.19 and +3.72 dB respectively. DepthSplat, which benefits from additional pre-training data, is the strongest single-dataset baseline at 27.44 PSNR, still below H3R-β's 28.03.

## Relation to Prior Work

H3R sits at the intersection of two established lines in generalizable 3D reconstruction: explicit cost-volume methods (MVSNet, MVSplat) that enforce geometric consistency through epipolar warping, and implicit attention-based methods (TranSplat, eFreeSplat, GS-LRM) that learn cross-view correspondences via Transformers with Plücker ray encoding. Rather than choosing one paradigm, H3R unifies both into a single pipeline—volumetric latent fusion for geometric precision followed by a camera-aware Transformer for adaptive refinement—addressing the explicit/implicit trade-off that prior work treats as a binary choice. The Gaussian parameterization and rendering pipeline follow GS-LRM, while the plane-sweep depth sampling directly adapts MVSNet's formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic demonstration that spatial-aligned encoders (SD-VAE, MAE) outperform the widely adopted semantic-aligned DINOv2 for 3D reconstruction is a practically important finding that could redirect encoder choices across the field. The 2× convergence speedup and robust scaling with additional views (where MVSplat degrades) make the approach more deployable in real-world settings with variable inputs. Open questions include whether the spatial-aligned encoder advantage persists at larger model scales or for tasks requiring stronger semantic understanding (e.g., semantic scene completion), and whether the hybrid explicit-implicit design generalizes beyond pixel-aligned Gaussian representations to other 3D primitives.
