## Problem

Recent empirical results show that whole-class training (WCT) achieves comparable few-shot accuracy to meta-learning, raising the question of whether meta-learning's bi-level optimization is redundant. However, this comparison is unfair because WCT consumes more annotation entropy (distinguishing all classes simultaneously) than meta-training. The paper introduces an entropy-limited supervised setting to enable fair comparison and asks: under matched annotation budgets, when and why does meta-learning genuinely outperform WCT?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250714/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes entirely unlabeled data: a support set and query set of images (or 3D point clouds projected to 2D via MVCNN) with no ground-truth class labels. Pseudo-labels are generated on-the-fly by DBSCAN clustering of the base-learner's embeddings, so the number of clusters (i.e., the 'way' of the task) varies per sampled task, making tasks inherently heterogeneous. At test time, the model outputs a class prediction for each query sample, with the option to perform or skip inner-loop fine-tuning depending on whether the task is few-shot or zero-shot.

## Method

MINO is a bi-level meta-learning framework with three key components. First, DBSCAN replaces fixed K-means for task construction, dynamically determining the number of clusters per task and dropping small clusters (via min_samples) to avoid sampling bias, thereby creating heterogeneous tasks that reduce meta-overfitting. Second, a dynamic head using the grouping classification trick partitions its classifier layer into groups matching the per-task cluster count, enabling a single architecture to handle variable-way tasks. Third, a stability-based meta-scaler σ_i = SVCCA(f'_θ_t(T_i), f'_θ_{t-1}(T_i)) modulates the outer-loop meta-gradient, suppressing updates when the head's representation is destabilized by noisy pseudo-labels while preserving cross-task body stability. The theoretical contribution proves that under the entropy-limited setting, meta-learning achieves a tighter generalization error bound than WCT whenever C₂²·k < C₁, a condition readily satisfied in few-shot regimes.

## Results

On unsupervised few-shot classification, MINO achieves 93.75% accuracy on Omniglot 5-way 1-shot, 94.69% on 20-way 5-shot, 60.38% on Mini-ImageNet 5-way 5-shot, and 62.14% on Tiered-ImageNet 5-way 5-shot, outperforming the second-best method PsCo by 2.85% on average across 10 tasks. On unsupervised zero-shot classification, MINO reaches 73.15% on CIFAR-10, 43.34% on CIFAR-100, and 86.45% on Tiny-ImageNet, improving over Meta-GMVAE by 1.70% on average across 6 datasets. The gap to the supervised MAML upper bound narrows to ~0.7% on Omniglot 5-way 1-shot (93.75% vs. 94.46%), demonstrating that meta-learning can nearly close the supervision gap under entropy-limited conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MINO outperforms all unsupervised few-shot baselines across all 10 task configurations. Against PsCo (the strongest prior method), MINO gains +0.50% on Omniglot (20,1), +3.68% on Omniglot (20,5), +5.51% on Mini-ImageNet (5,5), +4.28% on Mini-ImageNet (5,20), and +5.41% on Tiered-ImageNet (5,5), with the largest margins on higher-shot and higher-way settings. Against Meta-GMVAE, MINO leads by +3.68% on Omniglot (20,5) and +5.51% on Mini-ImageNet (5,5), while matching or slightly trailing on Omniglot (5,1) (93.75 vs. 93.81). The CACTUs variants (all four configurations) and UMTRA are substantially weaker, with CACTUs-Pr-Bi trailing MINO by up to 44.57% on Omniglot (20,5), attributed to their heavy reliance on pseudo-label quality without noise-robustness mechanisms. In the zero-shot setting (Table 5), MINO beats Meta-GMVAE by +1.42% on CIFAR-10, +2.08% on CIFAR-100, and +1.80% on Tiny-ImageNet, and surpasses ReSSL (the best non-meta baseline) by +2.88% on CIFAR-10 and +2.26% on STL-10. The supervised MAML upper bound remains 0.71–2.15% above MINO, confirming that the entropy-limited gap is small but nonzero.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the MAML bi-level optimization framework (Finn et al., 2017) and the dynamic-head grouping classification trick from Any-way meta learning (Lee et al., 2024), repurposing them for an unsupervised setting. Its key conceptual departure is the 'entropy-limited supervised setting,' which reframes the widely-cited finding that whole-class training (WCT) matches meta-learning in supervised few-shot benchmarks as an unfair comparison due to unequal annotation budgets. The method MINO further integrates DBSCAN-based adaptive clustering (replacing fixed K-means) and an SVCCA-based meta-scaler (Raghu et al., 2017) to address label noise and task heterogeneity, positioning meta-learning as specifically advantageous when supervision is limited or noisy rather than universally superior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The entropy-limited framework provides a principled, theoretically grounded criterion (C₂²·k < C₁) for when meta-learning should outperform WCT, giving practitioners a clear decision rule rather than relying on empirical benchmark comparisons. However, the practical gains of MINO over strong unsupervised baselines (PsCo, Meta-GMVAE) are modest (~2–3%), and the entropy model assumes balanced classes and a specific annotation process that may not hold in real-world pipelines. Open questions include whether the stability-based meta-scaler generalizes to non-classification tasks (e.g., segmentation, detection), whether the theoretical bound tightens with more realistic (unbalanced, multi-modal) task distributions, and whether the DBSCAN hyperparameters (eps, min_samples) remain stable at scale beyond the datasets tested here.
