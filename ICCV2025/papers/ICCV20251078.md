## Problem

Whether strict rotation equivariance is necessary for aerial object detection remains an open question. Existing rotation-equivariant detectors (ReDet, FRED) either only achieve approximate equivariance because stride-2 downsampling on even-dimensional feature maps breaks the symmetry, or rely on single-sided zero-padding that may introduce misalignment. No prior work has quantitatively compared strict versus approximate rotation equivariance on aerial detection benchmarks or tracked how equivariance error evolves during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251078/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The detector consumes aerial RGB images resized to 1024×1024 patches (DOTA) or 800×800 (DIOR-R) and outputs oriented bounding boxes with five parameters (center x, y, width, height, rotation angle) plus a class label per instance. Internally, the rotation-equivariant backbone produces feature maps with N=8 orientation dimensions in the channel axis; the multi-branch head splits these into 8 groups of C/8 channels each, processes them in parallel branches, and concatenates the N branch outputs for final prediction.

## Method

MessDet builds on RTMDet's CSPNeXt backbone and CSPNeXtPAFPN neck, replacing standard convolutions with E2CNN rotation-equivariant convolutions. Three key mechanisms enable strict equivariance with an advanced architecture: (1) a tuning layer (k=4, p=1, s=1) inserted before each stride-2 downsampling converts even-sized feature maps to odd sizes, preventing the sampling-point mismatch that breaks equivariance; (2) a rotation-equivariant channel attention (RE-CA) module that computes only C/N attention weights and repeats each N times, preserving the cyclic group symmetry while reducing the FC layer's output dimension by a factor of N; (3) a multi-branch head that routes the N orientation groups into separate lightweight convolutional branches before concatenation, cutting head parameters from 2.4M to 1.5M while improving mAP. The paper further tracks rotation equivariance error (Eq. 5) across backbone stages during training, showing it decreases on aerial detection data—contrasting with classification—thereby confirming that equivariance is beneficial rather than a constraint to be relaxed.

## Results

On DOTA-v1.0, the strictly rotation-equivariant MessDet (Str. MessDet) achieves 79.12% mAP with only 18.1M parameters, outperforming the RTMDet baseline (78.85% mAP, 52.3M params) while using roughly one-third the parameters. The approximately rotation-equivariant variant (Appr. MessDet) reaches 78.45% mAP with just 15.3M parameters. On DOTA-v1.5, Str. MessDet attains 73.14% mAP, and on DIOR-R it achieves 68.19% mAP, both setting new state-of-the-art results among single-stage detectors with the lowest parameter count reported in the literature.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Str. MessDet (79.12% mAP, 18.1M params) surpasses the strongest single-stage baseline RTMDet (78.85% mAP, 52.3M params) by 0.27% mAP while using only 35% of its parameters, and outperforms the best two-stage method PKINet (78.39% mAP, 30.8M params) by 0.73% mAP with fewer parameters. Among prior RE-Net-based detectors, Str. MessDet improves over ReDet (76.25% mAP) by 2.87% and over FRED (75.56% mAP) by 3.56%, demonstrating that the proposed strict equivariant downsampling and multi-branch head yield substantial gains over earlier approximate approaches. Appr. MessDet (78.45% mAP, 15.3M params) trails RTMDet by 0.40% mAP but achieves this with 3.4× fewer parameters, making it the most parameter-efficient detector in the comparison. On DOTA-v1.5, Str. MessDet (73.14%) leads RTMDet (71.80%) by 1.34% mAP, and on DIOR-R it achieves 68.19% versus RTMDet's 66.86%, a 1.33% margin. The paper further shows that the strict variant consistently outperforms the approximate variant by 0.4–0.8% mAP across all three datasets, confirming that eliminating rotation equivariance error during downsampling provides a measurable detection benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RE-Net line of work initiated by ReDet and FRED by implementing a strictly rotation-equivariant backbone and neck built on the more advanced CSPNeXt/CSPNeXtPAFPN architecture from RTMDet, rather than the simpler backbones used in prior RE-Net detectors. It departs from FRED's single-side padding strategy for maintaining strict equivariance by introducing a tuning layer that converts even-dimensional feature maps to odd dimensions before stride-2 downsampling, and adapts SENet's channel attention into a rotation-equivariant variant (RE-CA) that preserves the group structure. The multi-branch head exploits the inherent N-way grouping of RE-Net features to reduce parameters while improving accuracy, a design not present in ReDet or FRED.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first quantitative comparison of strict versus approximate rotation equivariance in aerial detection, showing that strict equivariance yields a modest but consistent mAP gain (~0.7 points) and that equivariance error decreases during training (opposite to the classification setting), confirming that rotational structure is genuinely beneficial for this task. The practical impact is significant: MessDet achieves SOTA mAP on DOTA-v1.0, DOTA-v1.5, and DIOR-R with roughly half the parameters of competing methods (15–18M vs. 30–52M), making RE-Nets more accessible for resource-constrained deployment. Open questions include whether the tuning-layer approach generalizes to other group-equivariant settings (e.g., reflection, scaling) and whether the parameter-efficiency advantage persists when scaling to larger backbones or more orientation dimensions.
