## Problem

Generating long high-resolution videos with Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) is bottlenecked by quadratic attention computation, consuming ~500 PFLOPs for an 8s 720p video. Existing sparse attention methods—static patterns, offline searches, and online approximate searches—fail to capture the hierarchical, blockified, and input-dependent sparsity structure inherent in DiTs, leading to significant quality degradation or limited speedups.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252526/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

AdaSpa is a plug-and-play module that intercepts the self-attention computation inside any DiT (tested on HunyuanVideo 13B and CogVideoX1.5-5B), operating on Q, K, V tensors of length L = f·h·w + t (video spatial-temporal tokens plus text tokens). It takes a text prompt and produces a generated 720p video (8s or 10s, 50 denoising steps), requiring only a one-line code change to enable. The method is training-free and data-free, with default hyperparameters γ=0.8 (sparsity), B=64 (block size), and search steps T={10,30}.

## Method

AdaSpa is the first sparse attention method combining a Dynamic Pattern with Online Precise Search for DiTs. It represents sparsity via a blockified pattern (partitioning the L×L attention matrix into B×B blocks), motivated by the observation that DiT sparsity is hierarchical across modalities and frames, making continuous patterns (col, diag) ineffective. For finding optimal sparse indices, AdaSpa exploits the step-invariance of sparse patterns and LSE distributions across denoising steps: at the first search step it runs a fused kernel that simultaneously computes full attention output and caches LSE values; at subsequent search steps it reuses the cached LSE to compute block-summed attention weights in O(L²/B²) time and applies a Top-k operator to select the highest-weight blocks, reducing search overhead to <5% of full attention. A Head-Adaptive strategy then adjusts per-head sparsity—increasing γ for high-recall heads and decreasing it for low-recall heads—to balance kernel load while preserving quality. Two additional optimizations (Text Sink to retain all text-related interactions, and Row-Wise uniform selection for temporal continuity) further improve fidelity.

## Results

On HunyuanVideo (13B, 8s 720p), AdaSpa achieves a VBench score of 80.13% (vs. 80.10% for full attention) with a 1.78× speedup (1810s vs. 3214s), while also attaining the best PSNR (29.07), SSIM (0.8905), and LPIPS (0.1478) among all sparse methods. On CogVideoX1.5-5B (10s 720p), AdaSpa reaches 81.90% VBench (vs. 81.16% full attention) with a 1.66× speedup (1888s vs. 3135s). Across varying sparsity levels (γ from 0.7 to 0.95), AdaSpa maintains quality with speedups ranging from 1.59× to 2.04×, and scales to 4.01× at 24-second video duration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaSpa outperforms all three baselines on both models across every quality and speed metric. On HunyuanVideo, AdaSpa (VBench 80.13%, 1.78×) beats Sparse VideoGen (79.39%, 1.58×) by 0.74 VBench points and 0.20× speedup, and MInference (79.17%, 1.27×) by 0.96 VBench points and 0.51× speedup. On CogVideoX1.5-5B, the gap widens: AdaSpa (81.90%, 1.66×) surpasses Sparse VideoGen (79.40%, 1.52×) by 2.50 VBench points and MInference (76.79%, 1.25×) by 5.11 VBench points, with MInference suffering the largest quality degradation due to its approximate online search failing on DiTs' dispersed attention patterns. The Frame region-wise baseline (79.53%/79.79%) underperforms AdaSpa by 0.6–2.1 VBench points, confirming that hierarchical sparsity breaks frame-level continuity. In terms of similarity metrics, AdaSpa achieves the best PSNR (29.07/23.25), SSIM (0.8905/0.8267), and lowest LPIPS (0.1478/0.2067) on both models, while MInference lags significantly (e.g., LPIPS 0.3550/0.4335), indicating substantial perceptual quality loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaSpa extends the sparse attention lineage from LLMs (StreamingLLM, MInference, DSA) to Diffusion Transformers with 3D full attention, where prior static-pattern (Sparse VideoGen) and approximate-search (MInference) methods fail due to DiTs' hierarchical, blockified, and input-adaptive sparsity. It builds directly on FlashAttention's block-wise computation for the fused LSE-cached search kernel and on Guo et al.'s Block Sparse Attention for the head-adaptive execution. The key conceptual departure is identifying the blockified pattern as the natural representation for DiT sparsity and combining it with an online *precise* (not approximate) search, enabled by the observed step-invariance of sparse patterns and LSE distributions across denoising steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As video generation models scale to longer durations and higher resolutions, attention dominates compute (≈83% of FLOPs for 8s 720p HunyuanVideo), making efficient sparse attention a practical necessity. AdaSpa's plug-and-play, training-free design (one-line integration, no data profiling) lowers the adoption barrier, and the 4× speedup at 24s with near-linear multi-GPU scaling suggests strong practical relevance for production video pipelines. Open questions remain: sparsity γ must be manually tuned, efficiency degrades for short videos, and the V-matrix sparsity within attention is unexplored. Whether the step-invariance assumption holds for future architectures with more aggressive temporal mixing is also unclear.
