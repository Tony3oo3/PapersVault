## Problem

Standard InfoNCE contrastive loss is ill-suited for multi-task multimodal retrieval because it treats all tasks and samples uniformly, suffering from biased gradients across tasks of varying difficulty, poor handling of noisy pairs, and no principled mechanism to balance competing objectives. While recent LLM-based embedding approaches (VLM2VEC, MM-Embed, NV-Embed) have shown promise, they lack task-adaptive weighting and a structured way to extract informative embeddings from a decoder-only LLM backbone.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a unified token sequence composed of a task-specific instruction, optional image tokens (32 tokens from ViT-g-14 + Q-Former), and text tokens, all mapped into the e5-Mistral-7B vocabulary. A [SOS] token is prepended and 16 [EOS] tokens are appended; the final embedding is the mean-pooled representation of these 16 EOS tokens, which the ablations show captures complementary semantic aspects compared to a single token. The model supports eight retrieval task types (T→I, T→T, T→(I,T), I→T, (I,T)→T, (I,T)→I, (I,T)→(I,T)) and also produces class predictions for zero-shot classification.

## Method

M3T-UEM uses e5-Mistral-7B-Instruct as a shared encoder for all modalities, with a BLIP-2 Q-Former adapter projecting ViT-g-14 image features into the LLM token space. The key loss innovation is a task-aware contrastive objective that introduces task-pair weights w_{τi,τk} (capturing inter-task importance) and sample-pair weights w̃_ik (capturing intra-task noise), both regularized with Gamma priors and inferred via a stochastic EM algorithm that alternates between sampling weights from their posteriors and updating LLM parameters with SGD. Training proceeds in two LoRA stages: Stage 1 (109M params, 7K steps) aligns the vision encoder with the LLM token space, and Stage 2 (200M params, 14K steps) refines multimodal representations across eight retrieval tasks. An auxiliary autoregressive language-modeling loss (λ=0.1) regularizes the embedding quality, and the 16-token EOS summarization mechanism replaces single-token pooling to preserve richer multi-faceted representations.

## Results

On the M-BEIR multimodal retrieval benchmark (8 tasks), M3T-UEM with task-aware loss (TA) achieves an average Recall@5 of 53.9%, outperforming the best LMM-based method MM-Embed (52.7%) by 1.2 points and the best encoder-based multi-task variant CLIPsf-MT (50.6%) by 3.3 points. On the ICinW zero-shot classification benchmark (20 datasets), M3T-UEM attains 67.5% mean accuracy, edging out OpenCLIP ViT-g-14 (67.2%) and MM-GEM (66.3%). In multilingual zero-shot retrieval (XTD200), M3T-UEM achieves 72.9% average Recall@5 (T→I) versus only 41.4% for OpenCLIP ViT-g-14, with particularly large gaps in Japanese (43.4% vs 9.8%) and Swedish (81.5% vs 26.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

M3T-UEM (TA) achieves the best overall M-BEIR average (53.9%), surpassing MM-Embed (52.7%) by 1.2 points and NV-Embed (47.2%) by 6.7 points, while also exceeding the strongest encoder-based multi-task baseline CLIPsf-MT (50.6%) by 3.3 points. On zero-shot image classification (ICinW), M3T-UEM (67.5%) narrowly beats OpenCLIP ViT-g-14 (67.2%) and ViT-H-14 (67.3%), and clearly outperforms MM-GEM (66.3%). In compositional reasoning (SUGARCREPE + WinoGround), M3T-UEM achieves 69.9% average image-to-text accuracy versus 64.0% for OpenCLIP ViT-g-14, with the largest gap on WinoGround (34.5% vs 28.0%). The most dramatic advantage appears in multilingual retrieval: on XTD200, M3T-UEM reaches 81.5% Recall@5 (T→I) in Swedish versus 26.6% for ViT-g-14, and 43.4% in Japanese versus 9.8%, reflecting the LLM backbone's inherent cross-lingual knowledge. On Flickr30K zero-shot retrieval, M3T-UEM (95.9% avg) marginally edges out VLM2VEC (95.7%) and MM-GEM (95.8%), while substantially outperforming LLM2CLIP (88.9%) and E5V (86.6%). Notably, M3T-UEM loses to MM-Embed on the WebQA text-to-text task (80.5% vs 95.9%) and to CLIPsf-MT on NIGHTS (29.8% vs 32.0%), indicating that the LLM backbone does not uniformly dominate all retrieval sub-tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

M3T-UEM sits within the recent wave of LLM-backed multimodal embedding models (VLM2VEC, MM-Embed, NV-Embed, MM-GEM) that replace dual-encoder architectures with a single pretrained LLM as a unified representation space. Its primary methodological departure is the task-aware contrastive loss, which extends the sample-wise probabilistic weighting of Qin et al. (NeurIPS 2024) by adding a task-level Bayesian weight and optimizing both via a stochastic EM procedure grounded in the Gamma identity of Chen et al. (NeurIPS 2022). Architecturally, it follows the BLIP-2 Q-Former + LLM pattern but introduces a 16-token EOS summarization mechanism and an auxiliary language-modeling regularizer, positioning itself as a more principled multi-task variant of the standard InfoNCE training recipe used by all concurrent LMM-based retrievers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically impactful finding is that an English-only fine-tuned LLM backbone transfers multilingual retrieval capability essentially for free, achieving 81.5% Recall@5 on Swedish and 43.4% on Japanese versus 26.6% and 9.8% for OpenCLIP ViT-g-14 — a gap that no amount of CLIP-scale contrastive pretraining has closed. However, the core M-BEIR retrieval gains over other LMM methods are modest (~1.1% average), and the task-aware loss contributes only 0.6% in ablation, suggesting the architectural choice (LLM backbone + Q-Former) does most of the heavy lifting. Open questions include whether the Bayesian task weighting scales to dozens of tasks, whether the two-stage training protocol is necessary at larger compute budgets, and how the framework extends to additional modalities (audio, video) that the authors mention but do not evaluate.
