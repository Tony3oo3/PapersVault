## Problem

Existing diffeomorphic transformation-based cortical surface reconstruction methods rely on fixed-step Neural ODEs designed for adult brains, where intermediate deformation states carry no biological meaning. In neonates, the cortex undergoes rapid morphological change across post-menstrual age (PMA), and longitudinal imaging data is extremely scarce (mostly single time-point scans), making it impossible to model developmental trajectories with conventional fixed-step approaches or to train on sufficient paired data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250241/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single T2-weighted neonatal brain MRI (aligned to MNI152-1mm template), a smooth initial template mesh, and the subject's PMA (29–45 weeks) as input. From this single scan, it produces white matter, midthickness, and pial surface meshes at the subject's PMA, plus intermediate surface meshes at every preceding week (e.g., a 40-week scan yields surfaces at weeks 29–40), forming a complete developmental trajectory. All surfaces are represented as triangular meshes with ~150k vertices, and the ODE is solved with adaptive step sizes where each discrete step maps to a specific PMA week rather than a uniform time increment.

## Method

The core contribution is reinterpreting the diffeomorphic ODE deformation process as brain development: an Age-Conditioned ODE (ACDE) with adaptive step sizes replaces the fixed-step scheme, so each intermediate state corresponds to a biologically meaningful PMA. A 3D U-Net encodes the MRI into a multi-static velocity field, and an MLP generates attention weights conditioned on both PMA and current step index to dynamically aggregate the velocity at each Euler integration step. To handle severe data imbalance (single-time-point scans vastly outnumber longitudinal pairs), a Progressive Curriculum Incremental Learning (PCIL) strategy gradually introduces single-point data into training, followed by a self-knowledge distillation loop where a teacher model generates pseudo-longitudinal trajectories refined via EMA. Stage 2 extends the trajectory to pial surfaces using two cascaded fully-connected networks (white→midthickness→pial) trained with a cycle-consistent semi-supervised loss that constrains the midthickness surface to the midpoint of white matter and pial, plus a global cortical thickness loss.

## Results

On the dHCP dataset (877 T2-weighted neonatal MRIs, PMA 29–45 weeks), the proposed method achieves state-of-the-art performance on both developmental trajectory generation and cortical surface reconstruction. For developmental trajectory (Table 1), it attains PCC 0.965 / MAE 0.451 / COS 0.963 on sulcal depth and PCC 0.866 / MAE 0.173 / COS 0.987 on cortical thickness, improving MAE by 17% over the second-best method (DITSAA). For surface reconstruction (Table 3), it achieves ASSD 0.109 mm and HD 0.221 mm on white matter surfaces and ASSD 0.121 mm and HD 0.263 mm on pial surfaces, outperforming all three diffeomorphic baselines while maintaining minimal self-intersection rates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For developmental trajectory generation, the proposed method outperforms all three feature-level baselines (S-UNet, S-Transformer, DITSAA) on every metric. The largest gap is in sulcal depth MAE: 0.451 vs. 0.532 (S-UNet), 0.565 (S-Transformer), and 0.578 (DITSAA), a 17% improvement over the best baseline. Notably, the proposed method also exhibits the lowest variance (e.g., ±0.026 PCC vs. ±0.061 for S-UNet), indicating more consistent predictions across subjects. For cortical surface reconstruction, the proposed method beats CoTAN (the strongest reconstruction baseline) on white matter ASSD (0.109 vs. 0.112 mm) and HD (0.221 vs. 0.227 mm), and on pial ASSD (0.121 vs. 0.124 mm) and HD (0.263 vs. 0.269 mm). It also surpasses Corticalflow and CFPP on all geometric metrics. The only trade-off is a slightly higher pial SIF (0.064% vs. 0.001% for CoTAN), though this remains well below the 0.097% of Corticalflow. A key qualitative advantage is that the proposed method generates multi-time-point surfaces in a single ODE pass, whereas all baselines predict only a single time point per inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffeomorphic transformation-based cortical surface reconstruction line (CorticalODE, CoTAN) by reinterpreting the fixed-step ODE deformation process as a biologically meaningful developmental trajectory, where each intermediate state corresponds to a specific post-menstrual age. The key architectural departure is the Age-Conditioned ODE (ACDE) with adaptive step sizes, which replaces the uniform integration scheme designed for adult brains with a PMA-conditioned velocity field that captures the rapid morphological changes in neonates. The training strategy (PCIL + self-knowledge distillation) and the cycle-consistent semi-supervised extension from white matter to pial surfaces are novel contributions that address the severe longitudinal data scarcity in neonatal imaging, going beyond the feature-level developmental prediction of prior methods (S-UNet, S-Transformer, DITSAA) to operate directly on triangular meshes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to produce full surface-level (triangular mesh) developmental trajectories from a single neonatal MRI scan, enabling multi-timepoint cortical analysis without requiring repeated scans—a significant practical advance given the difficulty of longitudinal neonatal imaging. The dual-task framing (reconstruction + development in one ODE pass) is elegant and computationally efficient. However, the 'adaptive step size' is effectively a fixed mapping of 17 discrete PMA values to ODE time points rather than truly continuous adaptation, and validation is limited to the dHCP dataset (877 subjects, 29–45 weeks PMA). Open questions include whether the generated intermediate surfaces are biologically faithful or merely smooth interpolations, whether the approach generalizes beyond the neonatal window, and whether the modest SIF increase on pial surfaces (0.064% vs. 0.001% for CoTAN) matters for downstream analysis.
