## Problem

CTC-based scene text recognition models offer fast inference but suffer from two key limitations: they struggle with text irregularity (distortions, rotated layouts) because CTC alignment assumes near-canonical left-to-right reading order, and they lack linguistic context modeling that encoder-decoder methods exploit. This paper closes the accuracy gap between CTC and encoder-decoder models while preserving the simple, fast inference architecture of CTC.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251228/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a cropped scene text image and resizes it to one of four predefined sizes based on its aspect ratio (R<1.5 → 64×64, 1.5≤R<2.5 → 48×96, 2.5≤R<3.5 → 40×112, R≥3.5 → 32×⌊R⌋×32), avoiding the severe distortion caused by fixed-size resizing. It outputs a recognized text string decoded via CTC from a sequence of per-position character probabilities. The character set is 94 for English and 6624 for Chinese; training uses a maximum length of 25 but the model handles longer sequences (up to 35) without the length cap imposed by attention-based decoders.

## Method

SVTRv2 combines three modules on top of a three-stage visual backbone (alternating local grouped-convolution and global multi-head self-attention mixing blocks). Multi-Size Resizing (MSR) adaptively selects the target resolution from four aspect-ratio buckets to minimize text distortion. The Feature Rearrangement Module (FRM) maps 2D visual features into a 1D CTC-aligned sequence via a two-step soft permutation: horizontal rearrangement (row-wise self-attention) followed by vertical rearrangement (column-wise cross-attention with a selecting token), making the model sensitive to rotated and irregular layouts. The Semantic Guidance Module (SGM) is a training-only component that, for each target character, encodes its left and right string context into a query token and uses cross-attention over the visual features to force the backbone to embed linguistic context; at inference SGM is discarded so the model remains a pure CTC pipeline. Training minimizes a weighted sum of CTC loss (λ₁=0.1) and SGM cross-entropy loss (λ₂=1).

## Results

SVTRv2-B achieves 96.57% average accuracy on the six common benchmarks (Com), 86.14% on Union14M-Benchmark (U14M), 80.0% on Occluded Scene Text (OST), and 50.2% on the Long Text Benchmark (LTB), while running at 189 FPS with only 19.8M parameters. On U14M, it surpasses the previous best EDTR MAERec by 0.97% accuracy while being 8× faster (189 vs. 17.1 FPS). On OST, it matches PARSeq (80.0% vs. 79.9%) using only a single visual model without any language decoder. The only scenario where SVTRv2-B trails is LTB, where SMTR achieves 55.5% vs. SVTRv2-B's 50.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SVTRv2-B outperforms all 24 compared EDTRs on U14M (86.14% vs. MAERec's 85.17%, a +0.97% gain) and on Com (96.57% vs. IGTR-AR's 96.48%), while also achieving the highest OST accuracy (80.0% vs. PARSeq's 79.9%) despite using no language decoder at inference. On LTB, SVTRv2-B (50.2%) is outperformed by SMTR (55.5%), the only method specifically designed for long text via sub-string matching. In terms of speed, SVTRv2-B runs at 189 FPS, making it 8× faster than MAERec (17.1 FPS), 1.5× faster than CPPD (125 FPS), and 3.6× faster than PARSeq (52.6 FPS), while maintaining a smaller model size (19.8M vs. 35.7M for MAERec). On the irregular text subsets, SVTRv2-B achieves 90.6% on Curve and 89.0% on Multi-Oriented, surpassing MAERec (89.1% and 87.1%) and IGTR-AR (90.4% and 91.2% on MO, where it slightly trails). The ablation in Table 4 further shows that replacing SVTRv2's MSR+FRM with a TPS rectification module or an attention-based decoder (MAERec*) degrades performance on both irregular text and long text, confirming that the proposed modules are more effective than these alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

SVTRv2 is a direct successor to SVTR (Du et al., IJCAI 2022), retaining its single-visual-model CTC architecture but replacing absolute positional encoding with grouped convolutions to support variable input sizes. It departs from the dominant EDTR paradigm—where rectification modules (TPS) or attention-based decoders handle irregular text and external language models provide linguistic context—by instead using a multi-size resizing strategy, a feature rearrangement module for CTC alignment, and a training-only semantic guidance module. The paper also introduces U14M-Filter, a rigorously de-duplicated version of Union14M-L, to address data leakage that invalidates prior results reported on Union14M-Benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CTC-based recognizers are the workhorse of commercial OCR engines due to their speed and simplicity, yet have been considered accuracy-limited compared to EDTRs; SVTRv2 demonstrates that this gap can be closed (or reversed) while retaining 8–10× faster inference, making it a strong candidate for real-time deployment. The U14M-Filter benchmark corrects a systemic data-leakage problem that has affected reported results across the field, providing a more reliable evaluation standard. Open questions include whether the SGM's training-only linguistic guidance can be further strengthened (e.g., with larger context windows or multi-lingual settings), and whether the approach extends to video text or extremely low-resolution inputs where visual features are inherently degraded.
