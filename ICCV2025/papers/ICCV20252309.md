## Problem

Existing backdoor defenses rely on heuristic partitioning of training data into clean and poisoned subsets, which are vulnerable to adaptive attacks and cause information loss through label pruning. No prior defense treats the clean labels as unobserved latent variables to be recovered through principled probabilistic inference, leaving a gap between the statistical structure of the poisoning problem and the tools used to solve it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252309/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard RGB image dataset (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, ImageNet at 224×224) where a fraction of samples have been poisoned with triggers and corrupted labels, and produces a robust classifier mapping images to clean class labels. Crucially, the method does not require knowing which samples are poisoned or what the poisoning rate is; it operates directly on the corrupted dataset. In the transfer-learning variant, the feature extractor is frozen (e.g., DINOv2 ViT-G/14) and only the classification heads are optimized.

## Method

VIBE frames clean labels as latent variables and recovers their posterior via an EM algorithm. The E-step infers clean pseudolabels by solving an entropy-regularized optimal transport problem over the coupling between observed corrupted labels and latent clean classes, efficiently computed with the Sinkhorn-Knopp matrix scaling algorithm. The M-step updates the classifier parameters via gradient descent on a cross-entropy objective between the pseudolabels and the model's clean-class posterior, which is parameterized as a von Mises-Fisher mixture over L2-normalized features with learnable class prototypes. A separate corrupted-label posterior models the mapping from clean to corrupted labels, enabling reconstruction of the attacker's poisoning rules. Self-supervised pre-training (All4One) on the poisoned data jumpstarts the EM optimization, and the modular design allows swapping in frozen foundation-model extractors for transfer-learning setups.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, VIBE-SS-F achieves an average ASR of 3.2% (vs. 16.0% for the best baseline ABL) while maintaining 94.3% clean accuracy, a 12.8pp absolute ASR improvement. On CIFAR-100, VIBE-SS-F reaches 0.5% average ASR (vs. 15.5% for ASD) with 73.9% accuracy. On ImageNet-30, both VIBE variants attain 0.1% average ASR with 96.8% accuracy, achieving near-complete resilience. In the transfer learning setup with DINOv2 (VIBE-FM), the method delivers 0.1% ASR on CIFAR-10 and 0.0% on CIFAR-100 with 99.3% and 92.3% accuracy respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIBE-SS-F outperforms all five baselines on every dataset. Against ABL (the strongest baseline on CIFAR-10 at 16.0% ASR), VIBE-SS-F achieves 3.2% ASR, a 12.8pp improvement; on CIFAR-100 the gap widens to 24.7pp (0.5% vs. 25.2%). CBD is the weakest defense, failing catastrophically with 84.0% ASR on CIFAR-10 and 93.0% on CIFAR-100, while VIBE-SS-F stays below 4% in both cases. ASD (15.5% ASR on CIFAR-100) and VaB (20.6% ASR on CIFAR-100) are also clearly beaten by VIBE-SS-F (0.5%). On ImageNet-30, VIBE-SS-A and VIBE-SS-F both reach 0.1% ASR, marginally outperforming VaB (0.2%) and ABL/DBD (0.5%), while ASD (5.5%) and CBD (74.2%) remain substantially worse. Notably, VIBE maintains or improves clean accuracy relative to baselines (e.g., 94.3% vs. 87.3% for ABL on CIFAR-10), whereas several baselines sacrifice accuracy for defense.

## Relation to Prior Work

VIBE departs from the dominant paradigm in backdoor defenses that relies on heuristic partitioning of training data into clean and poisoned subsets (ABL, DBD, ASD, VaB) or post-hoc trigger inversion. Instead, it reframes the problem through the lens of latent variable models: clean labels are treated as unobserved variables, and their posterior is recovered via an EM algorithm whose E-step is an entropy-regularized optimal transport problem solved with the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm (Cuturi, 2013). The M-step updates a classifier parameterized with von Mises-Fisher distributions on L2-normalized features, and the entire pipeline is jumpstarted by self-supervised pre-training on the poisoned data, building on the observation that self-supervised features reduce sample complexity for downstream tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VIBE is the first backdoor defense to achieve near-complete resilience (≤0.1% ASR) consistently across visible, invisible, clean-label, and all-to-all attacks on datasets ranging from CIFAR-10 to ImageNet-1k, without requiring any clean validation data or heuristic data partitioning. Its modular design allows seamless integration with frozen foundation models (DINOv2, CLIP), making it relevant as the field shifts toward transfer learning with large pretrained encoders. A unique byproduct is the ability to reconstruct the attacker's systematic poisoning rules from the learned corrupted-label posterior, enabling human audit. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-visual modalities, how it performs against truly adaptive attacks designed to exploit the variational framework itself, and whether the optimal transport E-step scales efficiently beyond ~1M samples without GPU memory constraints.
