## Problem

End-to-end autonomous driving models require diverse, high-quality data, but existing simulators cannot simultaneously deliver photorealistic sensor rendering, real-time efficiency, and bidirectional traffic interaction. Game-engine simulators suffer from sim-to-real gaps, NeRF- and diffusion-based methods are too slow for large-scale generation, and the only prior 3DGS-based simulator (HugSim) lacks reasonable agent-to-ego interaction modeling. No prior work has systematically studied how synthetic data from a photorealistic closed-loop environment affects the generalization of imitation-learning-based E2E models trained on real-world data.

## Input / Output

SceneCrafter consumes HD map topology and labeled road data from the Waymo Open Dataset, real-world sensor images and LiDAR point clouds (for scene reconstruction and ground height fitting), and configurable world setup parameters (agent count, behavior modes, spawn strategies). It produces spatially and temporally consistent multi-camera driving images at 2 Hz (matching E2E model input frequency) alongside ego vehicle states updated at 10 Hz, interactive agent trajectories, and closed-loop evaluation metrics (Route Completion, Vehicle Collision Rate, Layout Collision Rate). Unlike prior photorealistic simulators, the output includes both rendered sensor observations and physically grounded ego dynamics, making it usable for both training data generation and closed-loop policy evaluation.

## Method

SceneCrafter is a unified 3DGS-based simulator with two coupled components. The Scene Controller initializes agents via route-based or trigger-based spawning on HD map topology, then updates their states each step using bidirectional interaction rules (ego-to-agent and agent-to-ego) and an Adaptive Kinematic Model (AKM) that adds two learnable parameters (u₁, u₂) to the standard bicycle model, calibrated on real IMU data to correct velocity and yaw discrepancies. The Scene Renderer composites a static Street-Gaussians background with dynamic foreground vehicles built from BlenderNeRF and 3DRealCar assets (both re-trained as 3DGS), augmented by a RANSAC+MLP ground-height model to prevent z-axis floating/sinking and a directional shadow Gaussian model selected per-frame from pre-estimated global illumination. Two simulation modes share the same pipeline: synthetic data generation uses a privileged expert planner to drive the ego along its original trajectory while agents react, while closed-loop evaluation replaces the expert with a learned E2E policy (VAD or GenAD) that consumes rendered images, a control command, and ego status to produce future actions.

## Results

In closed-loop evaluation on unseen Waymo validation scenes, fine-tuning VAD with SceneCrafter synthetic data (6k samples) improves Route Completion from 42.01% to 53.03% (+11.02 pts) and reduces Vehicle Collision Rate from 7.55% to 3.43%. GenAD similarly benefits, with RC rising from 44.87% to 49.65% and LCR dropping from 8.50% to 5.76%. In open-loop data augmentation, adding 2k synthetic samples to a small real set (8k) reduces average planning L2 error from 1.39 m to 1.18 m and collision rate from 0.52 to 0.45, while the gain is more marginal with a larger real set (28k: L2 1.09→1.04 m). The Adaptive Kinematic Model (AKM) produces ego trajectories closer to planning outputs than the standard Bicycle Model, with 3s L2 of 2.06 m vs. 2.43 m.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not perform direct quantitative comparisons against other photorealistic simulators (NeuroNCAP, UniSim, DriveArena, HugSim), explaining in Appendix E that those systems cannot generate synthetic driving logs with both sensor inputs and ego dynamics. Instead, the primary comparison is real-data-only training versus real+synthetic (SceneCrafter) training. SceneCrafter consistently wins: VAD gains +11.02 pts RC and halves VC in closed-loop; GenAD gains +4.78 pts RC and reduces LCR by 2.74 pts. Against the standard Bicycle Model kinematics, the proposed Adaptive Kinematic Model reduces 3s planning L2 from 2.43 m to 2.06 m and improves 3D detection AP0.5 from 0.170 to 0.185. Qualitatively, Table 1 positions SceneCrafter as the only platform combining real-time rendering, spatial-temporal consistency, real-image fidelity, interactive traffic flow, and E2E data generation—capabilities no single existing simulator (CARLA, SUMO, MetaDrive, NeuroNCAP, UniSim, DriveArena, HugSim) fully provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

SceneCrafter sits at the intersection of 3D Gaussian Splatting-based scene reconstruction (building on 3DGS and Street Gaussians for background, BlenderNeRF/3DRealCar for foreground assets) and interactive traffic simulation (drawing on CARLA-style agent control and actor-critic behavior models). It departs from prior photorealistic simulators (NeuroNCAP, UniSim, DriveArena) by replacing NeRF or diffusion rendering with 3DGS for real-time efficiency, and from HugSim by introducing bidirectional ego-agent interaction modeling rather than only agent-to-ego responses. The Adaptive Kinematic Model, trained on real IMU data, is a novel refinement over the standard bicycle model used in most driving simulators, addressing the sim-to-real dynamics gap that prior 3DGS-based simulators ignored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first study to systematically evaluate how synthetic data from a photorealistic, interactive simulator affects imitation-learning-based E2E driving models trained on real data, bridging a gap where prior work either generated synthetic data for perception tasks or evaluated models in non-photorealistic game engines. The 18% Route Completion improvement in closed-loop evaluation is practically significant for reducing reliance on expensive real-world data collection. However, the current scope is limited to vehicles on the Waymo dataset (no pedestrians, cyclists, or diverse weather beyond what Waymo provides), and the benefit of synthetic data diminishes with larger real datasets, raising open questions about whether the approach scales to truly long-tail scenarios or requires complementary generative methods (e.g., local diffusion) for unseen content.
