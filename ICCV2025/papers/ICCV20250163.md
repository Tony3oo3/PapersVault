## Problem

Most prior work on object hallucination in LVLMs attributes the issue to language-model biases (statistical co-occurrence, prior knowledge overriding visual input) and applies fixes at the decoding level. This paper asks a complementary question that had been largely unexplored: how do specific image tokens in the visual input directly drive hallucinated object generation? The answer reveals that a tiny subset of image tokens (as few as 1.5% of all image tokens) are the primary visual cause of object hallucination, a finding that opens a new intervention point at the image-token level.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image (split into 576 fixed-size patches for LLaVA-1.5, processed through ViT and a linear projector into image tokens) and a text prompt as input. It produces two outputs: (1) a hallucination detection label for each object token in the generated text (real vs. hallucinated), and (2) a mitigated text response with hallucinated objects removed. The entire pipeline is training-free and requires only access to intermediate attention scores from a single middle layer (e.g., layer 15 of 32 in LLaVA-1.5) and the ability to re-run inference with modified image-token embeddings.

## Method

EAZY exploits the observation that, in middle-to-late transformer layers, generated object tokens concentrate their attention on specific image regions (quantified via the OL-VAR metric), and that hallucinated objects—lacking a true visual anchor—instead attend to visually related but incorrect image tokens. The algorithm first generates an initial response, extracts object tokens via POS tagging and dependency parsing, then for each object identifies its top-K most-attended image tokens (candidate HITs). It zeros out the union of all candidate HITs and regenerates; any object that disappears is classified as hallucinated. A second zero-out pass targets only the HITs associated with detected hallucinated objects, producing the final mitigated response. The zero-out operation replaces the selected image-token embeddings with zero vectors in the LLM input space, and the approach generalizes across LLaVA-1.5, LLaVA-Next, and Shikra without any model-specific tuning.

## Results

On the CHAIR hallucination mitigation benchmark (lower is better), EAZY achieves CHAIR_S of 38.8 and CHAIR_I of 11.4 on LLaVA-1.5, 26.6/8.9 on Shikra, and 26.8/8.3 on LLaVA-NEXT, outperforming all six baselines by large margins (e.g., 10.8 points lower CHAIR_S than Greedy on LLaVA-1.5, and 29.2 points lower than Greedy on Shikra). On the POPE benchmark, EAZY attains the best average accuracy (84.97%) and F1 (85.78%) on LLaVA-1.5. For hallucination detection on Hall-COCO, EAZY reaches 78.77% accuracy and 80.82% F1, improving accuracy by 16.21% over the best baseline (IC at 62.56%). On general MLLM benchmarks, EAZY preserves model utility with MME 1496.2 and MMBench 64.1, close to the unmodified Greedy baseline (1510.7 / 64.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EAZY outperforms all six baselines on every CHAIR metric across all three LVLMs. The largest gains are over Greedy decoding (10.8 CHAIR_S reduction on LLaVA-1.5, 29.2 on Shikra) and over VCD, which actually performs slightly worse than Greedy on LLaVA-1.5 (49.2 vs 49.6 CHAIR_S). Among the dedicated hallucination mitigation methods, OPERA and SID are the strongest baselines, but EAZY still leads by 6.4 and 5.4 CHAIR_S points respectively on LLaVA-1.5, and by 18.2 and 18.0 points on Shikra. On POPE, EAZY achieves the best average accuracy and F1 across all three models, though the margin over SID and OPERA is modest (0.15–1.16 F1 points), which the authors attribute to POPE's binary Yes/No format limiting the impact of object-token-level interventions. For hallucination detection on Hall-COCO, EAZY surpasses both UT (50.57% Acc) and IC (62.56% Acc) by 16.21 and 16.21 percentage points in accuracy respectively, with F1 of 80.82 vs 70.42 for IC. On general MLLM benchmarks (MME, MMBench), EAZY scores slightly below Greedy (1496.2 vs 1510.7 MME; 64.1 vs 64.3 MMBench) but remains within the range of all other baselines, confirming that hallucination mitigation does not significantly degrade general model capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of hallucination mitigation work that targets the language-model decoding process (contrastive decoding in VCD, generation backtracking in OPERA, self-introspective decoding in SID, layer-contrast in Dola) by shifting the intervention to the image input side. It builds on prior attention-analysis studies of LVLMs (DAMRO, Woo et al.) that identified middle-layer attention as informative, but goes further by establishing a causal link between specific high-attention image tokens and hallucinated object generation. The Logit Lens technique is repurposed not for interpretability per se but as a diagnostic tool to confirm that the identified tokens are semantically associated with the hallucinated objects, grounding the 'Hallucinatory Image Token' concept in the model's own vocabulary-space representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a tiny fraction (~1.5%) of image tokens are sufficient to drive object hallucinations offers a new, input-side lever for hallucination control that is complementary to existing decoding-side methods and requires no retraining. Practical limitations include the need for three forward passes (initial, detection, final), per-model tuning of the K parameter, and a more modest improvement on POPE's binary question format compared to open-ended CHAIR evaluation. Open questions include whether the HIT phenomenon generalizes to other hallucination types (attribute, spatial, relational), whether it persists in larger or next-generation LVLMs with different vision encoders, and whether the zero-out operation can be replaced by a learned soft suppression to reduce the computational overhead.
