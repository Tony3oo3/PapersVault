## Problem

Gradient-based explanations for ReLU networks are inherently noisy due to the network's reliance on high-frequency information, yet existing post-hoc smoothing methods (e.g., SmoothGrad, GradCAM) reduce this complexity by creating implicit surrogates at an unquantified cost to faithfulness. No principled, unified framework exists to simultaneously measure explanation complexity and the faithfulness loss introduced by surrogate-based smoothing, making the complexity-faithfulness trade-off opaque and method-dependent.

## Input / Output

The framework operates on RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/Fashion-MNIST, 112×112 or 224×224 for Imagenette, 224×224 for ImageNet) paired with a trained classifier (feedforward CNN, ResNet-50, or ViT-B16). It produces three outputs: a saliency map from any gradient-based explanation method, an Expected Frequency (EF) score computed as the radial-average integral of the spatial power spectrum of the input gradient, and an Explanation Gap (ΔEF) defined as the absolute difference in EF between VanillaGrad and the surrogate-based method. EF values are averaged over 1K images per dataset, and gradient magnitudes are normalized via inverse transformation to make results independent of absolute scale.

## Method

The paper establishes a formal spectral connection (Theorem 1) showing that in high-correlation data domains like images, the tail of a network's power spectrum (TPS) is directly proportional to the tail of the spatial power spectrum of its input gradient (TSPS), thereby linking network architecture to explanation complexity. Building on this, they introduce Expected Frequency (EF) as a hyperparameter-free summary statistic of the TSPS tail and the Explanation Gap (ΔEF) as a Fourier-domain proxy for the L² distance between original and surrogate gradients, both derived via Parseval's theorem. The key practical mechanism is Smooth Parameterization (SP) of ReLU, implemented as SoftPlus with precision parameter β (Eq. 8), which convolves ReLU with a Gaussian to control the network's high-frequency reliance; Lemma 1 proves this yields a faster-decaying power spectrum tail, and β→∞ recovers standard ReLU. By varying β during training, the framework enables a continuous trade-off curve where lower EF (simpler explanations) is achieved without the faithfulness penalty of post-hoc surrogates, as validated across four datasets and multiple architectures.

## Results

On Imagenette-CNN (scaled by 10⁴), the Smooth Parameterization SP(β=0.9) reduces Expected Frequency (EF) from 0.390 (ReLU VanillaGrad) to 0.202 while maintaining a zero explanation gap (ΔEF = 0.000), a ~48% reduction in explanation complexity with no faithfulness loss. Among post-hoc methods on ReLU networks, GradCAM achieves the lowest EF (0.293) but introduces the largest explanation gap (ΔEF = 0.097), while SmoothGrad (EF 0.286, ΔEF 0.104) and Guided Backprop (EF 0.300, ΔEF 0.090) show similar trade-offs. On ImageNet with ResNet-50, GradCAM again shows the largest gap (ΔEF = 0.130) with the lowest EF (0.133), whereas IntGrad (ΔEF = 0.010) and DeepLIFT (ΔEF = 0.009) preserve faithfulness with only modest complexity reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates seven gradient-based explanation methods along two axes: EF (complexity, lower is better) and ΔEF (faithfulness gap, lower is better). VanillaGrad serves as the zero-gap reference (ΔEF = 0 by definition) but has the highest EF on ReLU networks (0.390). GradCAM achieves the lowest EF (0.293 on ReLU, 0.133 on ResNet-50) but at the cost of the largest explanation gap (ΔEF = 0.097 and 0.130 respectively), making it the least faithful method. SmoothGrad and Guided Backprop occupy a middle ground, reducing EF to ~0.29–0.30 while introducing moderate gaps (ΔEF ≈ 0.09–0.10). IntGrad, DeepLIFT, and LRP remain highly faithful (ΔEF ≤ 0.019) but offer minimal complexity reduction on ReLU networks. The key finding is that SP(β=0.9) shifts the entire trade-off curve: VanillaGrad on SP(β=0.9) achieves EF = 0.202 with zero gap, matching or beating the complexity of SmoothGrad/GradCAM on ReLU while preserving full faithfulness, and most post-hoc methods on SP(β=0.9) show near-zero gaps (ΔEF ≤ 0.025).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spectral analysis of gradient-based explanations established by Mehrpanah et al. (ECCV 2024), which showed that post-hoc methods act as low-pass filters, by connecting the spectral properties of explanations directly to the spectral properties of the network itself. It builds on the spectral bias literature (Rahaman et al., 2019) to formalize how ReLU-induced sharp transitions in a network's power spectrum manifest as high-frequency content in input gradients. Unlike prior spectral explanation work (Kolek et al.) that uses optimization to impose spectral properties on explanations, this paper uses the spectral view purely as a measurement and diagnostic tool, introducing Expected Frequency and the Explanation Gap as unified, hyperparameter-free metrics for the complexity-faithfulness trade-off.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled, unified spectral framework for simultaneously quantifying explanation complexity and faithfulness, reframing the ante-hoc vs. post-hoc explainability dichotomy as two orthogonal axes (EF and ΔEF) with a continuum of behaviors. The practical implication—that training with a smooth parameterization of ReLU can reduce explanation complexity while maintaining zero explanation gap—offers a concrete path toward inherently more explainable networks. However, the framework relies on kernel-method assumptions and continuity in the spatial domain, and its extension to other architectural choices (skip connections, batch norm, attention) remains largely empirical. Open questions include whether the spectrum tail can serve as an optimization objective in neural architecture search and how the framework discretizes for practical resolutions.
