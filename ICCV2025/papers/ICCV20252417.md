## Problem

Existing active mapping systems either fail to leverage multimodal LLMs for long-horizon exploration planning or ignore localization uncertainty, which is critical when a robot explores texture-less or poorly reconstructed regions. Prior radiance-field-based active mapping methods model only scene reconstruction uncertainty and assume ground-truth poses, leaving the risk of localization failure unaddressed in practical embodied agents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252417/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The robot receives 800×800 RGB-D images at each step and outputs a discrete 3DOF action (MOVE FORWARD 5 cm, TURN LEFT/RIGHT 5°) over a 2000-step episode. The multimodal LLM is queried with a bird's-eye-view rendering synthesized from the current 3DGS map, annotated with the robot's trajectory and frontier candidates, plus a text prompt containing task context and step budget. The system maintains a continuously updated 3DGS scene representation and per-frame camera pose estimates via photometric optimization.

## Method

The system operates in two phases: (1) a multimodal LLM (GPT-4o) performs zero-shot long-horizon planning by receiving chain-of-thought-prompted queries over annotated 3DGS bird's-eye renderings and frontier candidates, selecting a coarse exploration goal; (2) an information-theoretic path planner computes Expected Information Gain (EIG) over 3DGS parameters using the full Fisher Information matrix, with the model's information matrix estimated via Monte-Carlo sampling over free-space poses rather than the empirical Fisher approximation. Path selection minimizes a combined cost that subtracts η·log(EIG) from the localization cost C_local, where C_local is derived from the Cramér-Rao bound on the full (non-diagonal) Fisher Information of the camera pose — computable without Laplace approximation because the pose Jacobian is linear in the number of Gaussians. This dual objective balances exploration of information-rich regions against the risk of localization drift in poorly constrained areas.

## Results

On the Gibson dataset, the proposed method achieves PSNR 23.28, SSIM 0.8067, Depth MAE 0.0696, RMSE ATE 0.0226, and 84.38% completeness, outperforming the strongest baseline (FBE) by 1.83 dB in PSNR, 0.045 in SSIM, 32.5 percentage points in completeness, and a 7.4× reduction in tracking error. On HM3D, the method reaches PSNR 19.86, SSIM 0.7127, Depth MAE 0.1666, RMSE ATE 0.0336, and 49.76% completeness, again leading all baselines on every metric. The only metric where the method does not lead is LPIPS on Gibson (0.2507 vs. FBE's 0.2126), though it still substantially outperforms all other baselines on that metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines: ANS (learned active SLAM, ICLR 2020), UPEN (uncertainty-driven planner, ICRA 2022), ExplORB (pose-graph-based active SLAM, 2022), and FBE (classical frontier-based exploration, IROS 1997). On Gibson, the proposed method dominates all four baselines on PSNR, SSIM, Depth MAE, RMSE ATE, and completeness, with the largest gaps over ANS (+7.0 dB PSNR, +49 pp completeness) and UPEN (+6.8 dB PSNR, +62 pp completeness). FBE is the closest competitor, achieving the best LPIPS (0.2126 vs. 0.2507) and competitive PSNR (21.45), but the proposed method still leads by 1.83 dB in PSNR and 32.5 pp in completeness while reducing tracking error by 7.4×. On HM3D, the advantage is even more pronounced: the method outperforms FBE by 4.06 dB in PSNR and reduces RMSE ATE from 1.2004 to 0.0336 (a 36× improvement), while ExplORB (run with ground-truth poses) trails by 2.05 dB in PSNR. Notably, UPEN and ANS both suffer localization failures on several scenes, further widening the gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Fisher Information framework for active perception (FisherRF, ECCV 2024) in two key ways: it adds a localization uncertainty term via the Cramér-Rao bound that prior active mapping methods ignored, and it replaces the empirical Fisher approximation with Monte-Carlo sampling over the free space. It builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) as the scene representation and on Gaussian Splatting SLAM (Matsuki et al., CVPR 2024) for pose Jacobians, but departs from both by computing the full Fisher Information matrix for localization parameters without Laplace approximation. The integration of a multimodal LLM (GPT-4o) as a zero-shot long-horizon planner over bird's-eye-view renderings is a novel combination not present in prior active mapping or LLM-for-robotics work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first active mapping system using 3D Gaussian Splatting that operates without ground-truth camera poses, which is a critical prerequisite for real-world deployment where pose sensors are noisy or unavailable. The dual objective balancing information gain against localization cost addresses a practical failure mode (drift in textureless regions) that prior information-theoretic methods overlooked. Limitations include evaluation only in simulation on 3-DOF ground robots, reliance on a specific LLM (GPT-4o) for planning, and the need for an occupancy grid that may not transfer directly to unstructured outdoor environments. Open questions include scaling to higher-DOF manipulation, task-driven exploration, and whether the LLM planning component generalizes beyond indoor scenes.
