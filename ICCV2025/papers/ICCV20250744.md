## Problem

Joint 3D human-object interaction reconstruction from monocular images remains challenging because existing optimization-based methods overemphasize physical constraints at the expense of image-level fidelity, while regression-based single-step refinements lack robustness under occlusion and depth ambiguity. The core gap is the absence of a principled mechanism that simultaneously leverages rich interaction priors and enforces physical plausibility (contact, collision, floor support) during reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250744/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a cropped RGB image, corresponding human and object segmentation masks, and a coarse 64-point object template (predefined canonical shape). It outputs a 331-dimensional parameter vector comprising SMPL-H pose (52 joints in 6D rotation format), 10 shape coefficients, object 6D rotation, and 3D translation, from which the full body mesh, object mesh, and per-vertex contact masks (human-object, object-floor) are derived. The object template constrains the shape space, limiting generalization to unseen object geometries.

## Method

ScoreHOI uses a score-based diffusion model as a differentiable optimizer: an initial coarse estimate from an affordance-aware regressor is inverted into a noisy latent via DDIM inversion, then denoised through guided DDIM sampling where the noise prediction is augmented with a physical guidance term (human-object contact, object-floor contact, and SDF-based penetration penalty) computed on the denoised intermediate estimate. An IG-Adapter (cross-attention block inspired by IP-Adapter) injects both image features (ResNet-50) and object geometry features (frozen PointNeXt) as conditions to make the denoising process controllable. A contact-driven iterative refinement loop (N=10 iterations) updates contact masks at each round, re-inverting and re-sampling to progressively improve physical plausibility. Training is two-stage: the regressor and contact predictor are supervised on BEHAVE/InterCap, then the diffusion model is trained on IMHD data with the frozen image backbone to augment generative capability.

## Results

On BEHAVE, ScoreHOI achieves the best Chamfer distance for both human (4.85 cm) and object (7.86 cm) reconstruction, along with the highest contact F-Score of 0.609, representing a 9% improvement over the previous best (CONTHO, 0.554). On InterCap, ScoreHOI attains CD_human of 5.56 cm, CD_object of 8.75 cm, and a contact F-Score of 0.578. In terms of inference efficiency, ScoreHOI runs at 0.29 FPS on a single RTX 4090, which is roughly 8× faster than VisTracker (0.036 FPS) and over 80× faster than CHORE (0.0035 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ScoreHOI outperforms all four baselines on the majority of metrics across both benchmarks. Against PHOSA [66], the gains are substantial: on BEHAVE, CD_human drops from 12.17 to 4.85 cm and contact F-Score rises from 0.317 to 0.609; on InterCap, CD_object improves from 20.57 to 8.75 cm. Compared to CHORE [58], ScoreHOI reduces CD_human by ~0.7 cm and CD_object by ~2.8 cm on BEHAVE while boosting contact F-Score from 0.523 to 0.609. The closest competitor is CONTHO [37]: on BEHAVE, ScoreHOI wins on every metric (notably contact recall 0.586 vs 0.496), while on InterCap CONTHO retains a slight edge in contact precision (0.661 vs 0.627) but ScoreHOI leads in CD_human (5.56 vs 5.96), CD_object (8.75 vs 9.50), contact recall (0.590 vs 0.432), and contact F-Score (0.578 vs 0.522). In the efficiency comparison (Table 2), ScoreHOI at 0.29 FPS surpasses VisTracker [60] (0.036 FPS) by ~8× and CHORE (0.0035 FPS) by over 80×, while also achieving lower Chamfer distances than both.

## Relation to Prior Work

ScoreHOI extends the score-guided diffusion framework from Stathopoulos et al. (CVPR 2024), which applied diffusion priors to human mesh recovery, to the joint human-object interaction reconstruction setting. It departs from the dominant optimization-based paradigm (CHORE, VisTracker) that iteratively refines parameters with Adam under physical constraints, and from the single-step regression refinement of CONTHO, by instead using DDIM inversion and guided sampling as the optimization loop. The physical guidance formulation follows the diffusion posterior sampling assumption from Chung et al. (ICLR 2023), and the conditioning mechanism (IG-Adapter) is directly inspired by IP-Adapter's cross-attention design. The contact-driven iterative refinement strategy is the paper's key architectural departure, updating contact masks across multiple DDIM sampling passes rather than relying on a single forward prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 9% contact F-Score improvement on BEHAVE and the 1–2 order-of-magnitude speedup over Adam-based optimizers (0.29 FPS vs. 0.0035 FPS for CHORE) make diffusion-based refinement a practically viable alternative for real-time or near-real-time HOI reconstruction pipelines. The N=2 'faster' variant reaching 2 FPS with minimal quality loss further strengthens this practical case. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is the reliance on pre-defined canonical object templates, which restricts generalization to unseen object shapes; the authors identify template-free reconstruction of arbitrary objects as the primary open problem. Broader implications include whether score-guided diffusion can serve as a general-purpose physics-aware optimizer beyond HOI, e.g., for human-scene or multi-agent interaction reconstruction.
