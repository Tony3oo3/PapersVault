## Problem

Forecasting rotational trajectories on SO(3) for rigid bodies with unknown moment of inertia and external forces remains unsolved: existing methods either assume energy conservation (excluding non-conservative dynamics), rely on constant-velocity priors, or are brittle to sensor noise. No prior approach simultaneously handles unknown inertial parameters, arbitrary external torques, and noisy observations while respecting the manifold geometry of SO(3).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252395/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a discrete sequence of noisy 3×3 rotation matrices (SO(3) elements) at irregular or regular timestamps, typically produced by a 6D pose estimator on RGB images, with noise up to ~9° (δ = 0.05π). It outputs forecasted rotation matrices for future timesteps beyond the observed horizon (0.8–1.2 s in simulation, 0.3 s on real data), recovered from a 6D representation via Gram-Schmidt orthonormalization. The model is trained on 1.2 s input segments and regularized on the next 0.8 s, then rolled forward at inference to extrapolate unseen rotations.

## Method

The core architecture is a Neural CDE whose hidden state is integrated with respect to a control path constructed directly on SO(3) via a Savitzky-Golay filter: a second-order polynomial is regressed in the Lie algebra so(3) over a sliding window of noisy observations, then mapped back to the manifold via the exponential map. A learnable weight matrix W is introduced to suppress boundary artifacts that would otherwise degrade extrapolation, and a second-order CDE term (d²X) injects explicit angular acceleration information to prevent temporal phase shifts. The 9D rotational derivatives from the Savitzky-Golay regression serve as the integration path for the latent state, which is projected to a 6D rotation representation at output. Training minimizes the geodesic distance (Frobenius norm of Log(y·x⁻¹)) between predicted and ground-truth rotations, and the entire pipeline is agnostic to energy or momentum conservation, enabling it to model dissipative, externally-forced, and variable-dynamics systems from noisy observations alone.

## Results

On simulated SO(3) trajectories with 0.05π input noise, SG-nCDE achieves the lowest rotational geodesic error (RGE) across all five dynamics scenarios at both 0.8 s and 1.2 s extrapolation horizons, ranging from 0.42° (velocity damping, 0.8 s) to 1.31° (variable dynamics, 1.2 s). On the Oxford Motion Dataset (OMD) with real Vicon tracking data, SG-nCDE attains 2.18–2.32° mean prediction error over 0.3 s, outperforming the SO(3)-GRU baseline (2.83–3.04°) and the SO(3)-nCDE baseline (5.43–7.82°). The method also requires roughly half the function evaluations of the baseline SO(3)-nCDE during training, indicating more efficient numerical integration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SG-nCDE consistently outperforms all four baselines across every scenario and both extrapolation horizons. Against the Conservational method [41], which assumes energy conservation, SG-nCDE reduces RGE by roughly 2× at 0.8 s (e.g., 0.87° vs. 2.63° on freely rotating) and by ~3× at 1.2 s (1.28° vs. 4.09°); the gap widens further in non-conservative settings where the Conservational method cannot model external torques. LEAP [23], which learns the CDE control path in an unstructured way, performs worst overall (7.67–15.12° RGE) and is highly unstable, confirming that a geometrically grounded control signal is essential. The SO(3)-GRU baseline is the closest competitor, achieving 1.65° vs. 0.87° at 0.8 s on freely rotating trajectories, but SG-nCDE maintains a 1.5–2× advantage at the longer 1.2 s horizon (1.28° vs. 2.67°). On the real-world OMD dataset, SG-nCDE (2.18–2.32°) beats SO(3)-GRU (2.83–3.04°) by ~0.6° and the SO(3)-nCDE spline baseline (5.43–7.82°) by more than 3×, demonstrating robustness to unseen dynamics and sensor noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of neural CDEs [29] and geometric filtering on Lie groups, repurposing the SO(3) Savitzky-Golay filter [25] as a control signal for a neural CDE rather than as a standalone denoising tool. It departs from conservative dynamics approaches (e.g., Mason et al. [41]) that assume energy/momentum conservation and from standard spline-based CDE paths (cubic, Hermite) that ignore SO(3) geometry, instead constructing the integration path natively on the manifold via Lie algebra polynomial regression. The weighted formulation extends the Euclidean weighted SG filter [52] with learnable parameters, and the second-order CDE term leverages the closed-form acceleration available from the SG regression—neither of which is accessible to spline-based alternatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical gap: real-world rotational tracking involves dissipative forces, external torques, and noisy sensor readings, yet prior SO(3) prediction methods either assume conservation or handle only interpolation. By decoupling dynamics learning from pose estimation, the approach can be plugged into existing 6D pose pipelines (demonstrated with GDR-NPP) without re-annotating dynamic objects. The main limitation is restriction to SO(3); extending to SE(3) with coupled rotation-translation dynamics (centripetal, Coriolis effects) remains open, and the authors note that naive SE(3) filtering risks decoupling the two components. Open questions include whether the SG control path generalizes to higher-dimensional Lie groups or to systems with discontinuous external forces.
