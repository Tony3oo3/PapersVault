## Problem

Existing 3D interactive instance segmentation methods face a trade-off: explicit click fusion (e.g., AGILE3D) struggles to generalize to unseen objects and domains, while implicit click fusion (e.g., Point-SAM) achieves better generalization but relies on heavy ViT encoders and point-group tokenization that lack a universal metric definition, leading to inconsistent cross-dataset performance. No prior method combines the efficiency and domain-independence of voxel-based representations with the generalization benefits of implicit click fusion.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud scene (N_P points, each with 3D position and RGB color, i.e., 6 features) and a set of N_C user clicks (each with 3D coordinates and a positive/negative label, i.e., 4 features). The scene is voxelized at a fixed 5 cm resolution for processing, and the output is a per-point binary mask (N_P values) indicating which points belong to the target object. The interaction is iterative: after each prediction, the user can add additional positive or negative clicks to refine the mask, with the method designed to converge within a small number of clicks (≤3 for real-time VR use).

## Method

Easy3D encodes the voxelized scene with a sparse U-Net (SpConv) and encodes user clicks via positional + label embeddings (following SAM). A Two-Way Transformer decoder alternates self- and cross-attention between scene and click embeddings to update both representations. The key architectural choice is implicit click fusion: a learned output embedding participates in the attention operations alongside the clicks, and the final mask is obtained by a single dot product between the updated scene and output embeddings, rather than predicting per-click masks and combining them with a max operation. The paper's novel contribution is integrating a learned negative embedding (originally from AGILE3D's explicit fusion) into this implicit scheme, where a second negative output embedding's mask is subtracted from the positive one to better model background. Training uses a simulated interaction protocol (10 clicks placed at object center then largest error region) with DICE + Cross Entropy losses, and the voxel-based representation provides a domain-independent, metrically explicit encoding that generalizes across indoor, outdoor, and Gaussian Splatting scenes.

## Results

On the primary in-domain benchmark ScanNet40, Easy3D achieves IoU@1 of 68.2 and IoU@3 of 77.3, outperforming AGILE3D (63.0 / 75.1) and InterObject3D (40.8 / 63.9). The advantage is most pronounced on the unseen outdoor domain KITTI-360, where Easy3D reaches IoU@1 of 46.3 and IoU@10 of 83.6 versus AGILE3D's 34.8 and 49.6, a gap of over 34 points at IoU@10. On the high-fidelity ScanNet++ dataset, Easy3D leads at IoU@1 (54.8 vs. 49.5 for AGILE3D) while AGILE3D retains a small edge at IoU@10 (74.2 vs. 71.3). All models are trained exclusively on ScanNet40 with a 5 cm voxel resolution and no pretraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Easy3D consistently outperforms AGILE3D [37] on IoU@1 across all four test datasets (ScanNet40: 68.2 vs. 63.0; S3DIS: 65.7 vs. 58.5; KITTI-360: 46.3 vs. 34.8; ScanNet++: 54.8 vs. 49.5), with the largest margin on the unseen outdoor domain KITTI-360 where the gap widens to 34 points at IoU@10 (83.6 vs. 49.6). Against Point-SAM [39], Easy3D wins on every reported metric for S3DIS and KITTI-360, with particularly large gains on KITTI-360 IoU@10 (83.6 vs. 80.8) and S3DIS IoU@1 (65.7 vs. 38.8). Compared to the earlier InterObject3D [15], Easy3D improves IoU@1 by 27–44 points depending on the dataset. The one setting where AGILE3D retains an edge is high-click in-domain evaluation: on ScanNet40 at IoU@10, AGILE3D scores 83.5 versus Easy3D's 81.7, and on ScanNet++ at IoU@10, 74.2 versus 71.3, suggesting explicit click fusion can be slightly beneficial when many clicks are available in a familiar domain. Against the non-interactive Mask3D [24], Easy3D achieves 56.1 mAP on ScanNet20→ScanNet20 and 39.2 mAP on ScanNet20→ScanNet40 (seen + unseen), surpassing Mask3D's 51.5 and 5.3 respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

Easy3D sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: the 2D interactive segmentation paradigm from SAM (Two-Way Transformer decoder, positional/label click encoding, implicit click fusion) and the 3D interactive instance segmentation methods AGILE3D (voxel-based sparse U-Net encoder, learned negative embeddings, simulated interaction training) and Point-SAM (point-group tokenizer, ViT encoder, implicit fusion). The paper's core conceptual move is to combine AGILE3D's voxel-based sparse encoder with SAM-style implicit click fusion, and to be the first to apply learned negative embeddings within the implicit fusion framework. This departs from AGILE3D's explicit max-based fusion and from Point-SAM's less interpretable point-group representation, positioning Easy3D as a deliberately simpler and more generalizable architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The targeted ablation isolating implicit vs. explicit click fusion provides practitioners with a clear design principle: implicit fusion is substantially more robust on unseen domains (e.g., +73% IoU@10 on KITTI-360), while explicit fusion can marginally win in-domain with many clicks. The voxel-based representation's advantage over point groups for cross-domain generalization is also well-supported empirically. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-object or open-vocabulary settings, how it performs at real-time frame rates on large-scale outdoor scenes, and whether the learned negative embedding can be replaced by a more interpretable background prior. The method's simplicity (sparse convolutions + lightweight transformer, no pretraining) makes it a practical baseline for 3D interactive segmentation in VR/AR and robotics pipelines.
