## Problem

Existing temporal fusion paradigms for 3D occupancy prediction (recurrent or stacked frame queues) are memory- and computation-heavy due to the dense voxel-level representation, limiting the number of historical frames that can be exploited. Additionally, voxel-level uncertainty from occlusion and lighting changes, and misalignment from dynamic instances, accumulate during naive temporal fusion, degrading prediction robustness and temporal consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250842/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (6 cameras at 256×704) and ego vehicle pose in a streaming video fashion, processing frames sequentially with a variable number of historical frames. It produces a 100×100×8 voxel occupancy grid (18 semantic classes, 0.4 m resolution) in ego-vehicle coordinates per frame, while simultaneously maintaining a scene-centered spatiotemporal memory that accumulates historical representations, class activations, log variance, and occupancy flow across the entire sequence.

## Method

ST-Occ introduces a unified temporal modeling paradigm that replaces the per-frame feature queue with a single scene-centered spatiotemporal memory, reducing memory from k full representations to one. The memory stores not only the historical occupancy representation but also temporal attributes: a class activation vector updated with exponential decay, a log variance for uncertainty, and a 2D occupancy flow for dynamic compensation. A memory attention module conditions the current ego-centered occupancy on the memory via deformable attention, where an uncertainty score (from an MLP over class activation, log variance, and feature similarity) weights the blend between current and historical features, and the occupancy flow shifts query positions to compensate for dynamic instance motion. Built on FB-OCC with a ResNet50 backbone and three temporal self-attention layers, the framework is trained end-to-end with focal, Lovász softmax, Gaussian NLL, and L1 flow losses, achieving 3 mIoU improvement and 29% reduction in temporal inconsistency over prior state-of-the-art.

## Results

On the Occ3D benchmark, ST-Occ achieves 42.13 mIoU with a ResNet-50 backbone, outperforming the direct baseline FB-OCC (39.11 mIoU) by 3.02 mIoU and ViewFormer (41.44 mIoU) by 0.69 mIoU. The method also reduces temporal inconsistency (mSTCV) from 12.18% to 8.68%, a 29% decrease. In terms of temporal modeling effectiveness, ST-Occ gains 4.74 mIoU from temporal fusion versus only 1.72 mIoU for FB-OCC, making it 2.8× more effective. ST-Occ also achieves the best efficiency: 10.90 GB training memory, 24 ms fusion time, and 8.65 FPS, compared to 12.89 GB / 705 ms / 5.95 FPS for recurrent and 19.02 GB / 84 ms / 5.42 FPS for stacked paradigms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ST-Occ outperforms all seven baselines on mIoU. Against the direct ResNet-50 baselines, it beats FB-OCC by 3.02 mIoU (42.13 vs. 39.11) and ViewFormer by 0.69 mIoU (42.13 vs. 41.44), with consistent gains across all 18 semantic classes. Against the ResNet-101 baselines (TPVFormer 27.83, OccFormer 21.93, BEVFormer 26.88, CTF-Occ 28.53), ST-Occ leads by 13.6–20.2 mIoU, though these use a larger backbone. On temporal consistency (mSTCV), ST-Occ reduces inconsistency from 12.18% (FB-OCC) to 8.68%, a 29% improvement. In efficiency, ST-Occ uses 15% less training memory and 29× less fusion time than the recurrent paradigm (VoxFormer-style), and 43% less training memory than the stacked paradigm (FB-OCC-style), while achieving the highest FPS (8.65). When plugged into ViewFormer's architecture, ST-Occ adds 4.5 mIoU over ViewFormer without temporal fusion, a 25% more effective temporal modeling gain than ViewFormer's own temporal module.

## Relation to Prior Work

ST-Occ builds directly on FB-OCC's occupancy encoder and head architecture, and on BEVFormer's temporal self-attention (TSA) layer as the core conditioning mechanism. It departs from the dominant ego-vehicle-centered temporal fusion paradigms (recurrent in BEVFormer, stacking in BEVDet4D/FB-OCC) by introducing 'unified temporal modeling'—a scene-centered spatiotemporal memory that stores one persistent representation rather than a queue of k aligned frames. The paper also extends prior occupancy work by explicitly incorporating uncertainty (via log-variance and class-activation history) and dynamic awareness (via occupancy flow) into the temporal attention, factors that prior methods largely ignored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3 mIoU gain over SOTA combined with 2.8× more effective temporal utilization and lower memory/compute makes ST-Occ a practically attractive option for real-time autonomous driving perception, where both accuracy and efficiency matter. The proposed mSTCV metric fills a gap in evaluating temporal consistency of occupancy predictions, which is critical for downstream planning. Open questions include whether the occupancy flow can be derived end-to-end from temporal information rather than relying on nuScenes bounding-box annotations, and whether the unified temporal modeling paradigm extends cleanly to sparse query-based occupancy methods.
