## Problem

Minibatch optimal transport (OT) coupling straightens flow paths in unconditional flow matching, enabling fewer integration steps at inference. However, the paper shows that naively extending OT to conditional generation paradoxically degrades performance because the OT coupling disregards conditions, creating a train-test gap: during training the prior becomes conditionally skewed (q(x₀|c) ≠ q(x₀)), but at test time the full unbiased prior must be used, causing the model to encounter previously unseen (x₀, c) pairs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a condition (a discrete class label for CIFAR-10, or a text caption encoded into a feature vector via a CLIP-like DFN text encoder for ImageNet) and a noise sample drawn from a standard Gaussian prior. At test time, the noise is always sampled from the full prior regardless of condition, and the learned velocity field is integrated via an ODE solver (single-step Euler or adaptive Dormand-Prince) from t=0 to t=1 to produce a generated image (32×32 in image space for CIFAR-10/ImageNet-32, or 256×256 decoded from latent space for ImageNet-256) or a 2D data point on the synthetic dataset.

## Method

C²OT modifies the minibatch OT cost matrix by adding a condition-aware penalty term: for discrete conditions, an infinite penalty forbids transport between samples with different class labels (forcing OT to be computed independently within each class); for continuous conditions, a cosine-distance penalty scaled by a weight w is used. The weight w is found adaptively per minibatch via binary search to maintain a target ratio r_tar of 'potential OT candidates,' making the method invariant to distance scaling across datasets. The key theoretical insight is that this construction enforces independence between the prior x₀ and the condition c in the joint distribution π(x₀, x₁, c) = q(x₀)q(c)π_ot(x₁|x₀, c), guaranteeing that the marginal prior at test time remains the full q(x₀) rather than a skewed q(x₀|c). To counteract the reduced effective OT batch size caused by the conditional restriction, the method oversamples: the OT batch size b is set much larger than the network batch size b_n (e.g., 6400 vs. 256 for ImageNet), with OT computation offloaded to CPU data workers to avoid wall-clock overhead.

## Results

On the 8gaussians→moons synthetic dataset, C²OT achieves a 2-Wasserstein distance (W2²) of 0.048 (Euler-1) and 0.018 (adaptive) for binary conditions, dramatically outperforming FM (2.573 / 0.059) and OT (0.483 / 0.462). On CIFAR-10 class-conditioned generation, C²OT reaches FID 2.904 with the adaptive solver (vs. FM 2.922, OT 4.144) and FID 64.694 with just 2 Euler steps (vs. FM 105.481, OT 64.417), while also achieving the best condition-adherence CE of 2.264 at Euler-2. On ImageNet-256×256 in latent space, C²OT attains FID 3.369 (adaptive) and CLIP 0.139, beating OT (FID 5.726, CLIP 0.125) and matching or slightly exceeding FM (FID 3.498, CLIP 0.140).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FM (independent coupling), C²OT wins decisively in few-step settings: on CIFAR-10 Euler-2, C²OT achieves FID 64.69 vs. FM's 105.48, and on ImageNet-256×256 Euler-10, C²OT reaches FID 10.00 vs. FM's 10.00 (essentially tied) while matching CLIP scores. In the adaptive (many-step) setting, C²OT matches or slightly beats FM on FID (e.g., 2.904 vs. 2.922 on CIFAR-10) and achieves the best condition-adherence CE (0.279 vs. 0.276). Against OT (unconditional minibatch optimal transport), C²OT wins clearly in the conditional setting: on CIFAR-10 adaptive, C²OT achieves FID 2.904 vs. OT's 4.144, and CE 0.279 vs. OT's 0.372, showing OT fails to follow conditions. On ImageNet-256×256, C²OT achieves FID 3.369 vs. OT's 5.726 and CLIP 0.139 vs. OT's 0.125. The key finding is that OT, which excels in unconditional generation, paradoxically degrades in the conditional setting due to a train-test prior mismatch, while C²OT corrects this and recovers OT's straight-path benefits without the condition-induced skew.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends minibatch optimal transport (OT) coupling for flow matching from the unconditional setting (Tong et al. 2024; Pooladian et al. 2023) to conditional generation. The key departure is a formal diagnosis of why naive OT extension fails: the OT coupling correlates prior samples with conditions, skewing the training-time prior q(x₀|c) away from the test-time prior q(x₀), creating a train-test gap. The proposed C²OT addresses this by adding a condition-aware penalty to the OT cost matrix, effectively computing OT independently within each condition while preserving the flow-straightening benefit. The method is orthogonal to other flow-straightening approaches (reflow, variational flow matching) and consistency models, and can be combined with them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, formal explanation for a previously unaddressed failure mode of OT in conditional generation, and the fix (a single weighting term in the cost matrix) is simple enough to adopt with minimal engineering overhead. However, the authors honestly note that benefits are less pronounced in high-dimensional image data, where the prior skew is less detrimental due to data entanglement. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other coupling strategies beyond OT, whether the adaptive weight-finding heuristic generalizes to very high-dimensional or multi-modal conditions, and whether the modest high-dimensional gains can be amplified by combining C²OT with other flow-straightening or consistency techniques.
