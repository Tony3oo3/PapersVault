## Problem

Multi-frame optical flow methods improve accuracy by exploiting temporal cues, but their RAFT-style correlation volumes scale quadratically with resolution, consuming 8–19 GB of GPU memory at 1080p and making them impractical on consumer hardware. Existing memory-efficient approaches either sacrifice accuracy or operate only on two frames, leaving no principled solution for high-resolution multi-frame estimation that avoids downsampling or tiling.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three consecutive FullHD (1920×1080) RGB frames and outputs two dense flow fields: one mapping the center frame to the previous frame and one to the next frame. Internally, all correlation volumes and iterative flow refinement operate at 1/16 of input resolution (120×210), with the final flow upsampled back to full resolution via convex upsampling. For video sequences, previously computed feature maps and correlation volumes are cached and reused across overlapping frame triplets to avoid redundant computation.

## Method

MEMFOF extends SEA-RAFT from two to three frames by predicting bidirectional flows (center-to-previous and center-to-next) through a shared context network and dual correlation volumes, with a single recurrent update block refining both flows simultaneously. The key memory reduction comes from lowering the correlation volume resolution from 1/8 to 1/16 of input, compensated by increasing feature dimension D_f from 256 to 1024 and update-block dimension D_c from 128 to 512, cutting inference memory from 8.19 GB to 2.09 GB at 1080p. A high-resolution training strategy upsamples all non-Spring datasets by 2× and trains on full frames (not crops) to align motion-magnitude distributions with FullHD inference, preventing underfitting on large displacements. The architecture also reintroduces the GMA module with a resolution-adaptive attention scale (log₃(HW)/√D_c), and inference is further accelerated by feature-network caching, late convex upsampling, and correlation-volume reuse across consecutive frames.

## Results

On the Spring benchmark (fine-tuned), MEMFOF achieves a 1px outlier rate of 3.289, EPE of 0.355, Fl of 1.238, and WAUC of 95.186, ranking first among all submissions. On Sintel (clean), it leads with an EPE of 0.963, and on KITTI-2015 it achieves the best Fl-all of 2.94% among non-scene-flow methods. Critically, all results are obtained with only 2.09 GB of GPU memory at 1080p inference, compared to 8.19 GB for SEA-RAFT and 18.97 GB for StreamFlow, while also being faster (472 ms vs. 286 ms for SEA-RAFT-M and 1403 ms for StreamFlow).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MEMFOF outperforms all 14 compared baselines on the Spring benchmark across every metric (1px, EPE, Fl, WAUC) in both zero-shot and fine-tuned settings. Against the closest two-frame competitor SEA-RAFT(M), MEMFOF improves 1px by 10.9% (3.289 vs. 3.686) and EPE by 2.2% (0.355 vs. 0.363) while using 4× less memory (2.09 vs. 8.19 GB). Among multi-frame methods, MEMFOF beats MemFlow (1px: 3.289 vs. 5.759 zero-shot) and StreamFlow (1px: 3.289 vs. 5.215 zero-shot) by large margins, and uses 4–9× less GPU memory. On Sintel clean, MEMFOF (EPE 0.963) surpasses VideoFlow-MOF (0.991), StreamFlow (1.041), and MemFlow (1.046), though VideoFlow-MOF retains a slight edge on Sintel final (1.649 vs. 1.907). On KITTI-15, MEMFOF achieves the best Fl-all (2.94%) among all non-scene-flow methods, beating CrocoFlow (3.64%), VideoFlow-MOF (3.65%), and MemFlow-T (3.88%). Notably, FlowFormer and VideoFlow-MOF cannot even run at 1080p without OOM on a 24 GB GPU, whereas MEMFOF operates within 2.09 GB.

## Relation to Prior Work

MEMFOF extends SEA-RAFT's two-frame RAFT-style architecture to three frames following VideoFlow's bidirectional flow formulation, while incorporating GMA for motion coherence. Its key architectural departure is reducing the correlation volume resolution from 1/8 to 1/16 of input (compensated by quadrupling feature dimensions), which cuts memory by ~4× and is the enabling factor for multi-frame processing at FullHD. The high-resolution training strategy—2× upsampling of standard datasets to match FullHD motion distributions—represents a novel empirical protocol not explored in prior RAFT-family work, addressing a known but unaddressed train-test resolution mismatch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By requiring only 2.09 GB at 1080p inference, MEMFOF makes multi-frame optical flow practical on consumer GPUs for the first time, removing the need for tiling or downsampling that degrades motion boundary quality. The finding that 2× upsampling of training data is critical (not merely helpful) for high-resolution generalization provides a reusable recipe for the broader optical flow community. Open questions include whether the correlation-volume-reduction strategy generalizes beyond RAFT-like architectures, whether the 5-frame degradation (attributed to insufficient context network capacity) can be resolved with deeper modules, and whether the approach scales to 4K or real-time settings.
