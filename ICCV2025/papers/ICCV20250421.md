## Problem

Existing remote sensing foundation models face a fundamental trade-off: CNN-based backbones lack global receptive fields needed for large-scale RS objects, while attention-based models incur quadratic computational complexity that becomes prohibitive on high-resolution imagery. Additionally, current RSFMs offer no physical interpretability for how object features propagate across the image, making it difficult to reason about or adjust learning behavior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250421/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts paired or unpaired optical (RGB, H×W×3) and SAR (single-channel, H×W×1) images, which are first decomposed via 2D DCT into high- and low-frequency components using a random sector mask (20–30% masking rate) before entering the encoder. Both modalities are projected into a shared thermal space where heat conduction is simulated. Downstream outputs are task-specific: segmentation masks (UPerNet head), bounding boxes (YOLOX head), scene class labels, and binary change masks, evaluated at resolutions from 224×224 (pre-training) up to 1024×1024.

## Method

RS-vHeat replaces the standard attention-based encoder with a Heat Conduction Operator (HCO) that computes the general solution of the 2D heat equation in the frequency domain: U^t = IDCT₂D(DCT₂D(U⁰)·e^{−k(ωx²+ωy²)t}), yielding O(N^1.5) complexity with a global receptive field. Learnable Frequency Value Embeddings (FVEs) predict an adaptive per-frequency diffusion coefficient k, while lightweight Spatial Correction Embeddings (SCEs) add content-aware adjustments to the temperature field in the spatial domain to sharpen object boundaries. Pre-training uses a frequency-domain hierarchical masking strategy (DCT-based sector masking) combined with multi-domain reconstruction (spatial L1 + frequency L1) and cross-modal contrastive loss, trained on 3M+ optical/SAR entries with a Swin-B-style 4-stage architecture (2, 2, 18, 2 blocks). This design reduces memory by 84%, FLOPs by 24%, and improves throughput by 2.7× over attention-based RSFMs on 1024×1024 inputs while matching or exceeding their accuracy across segmentation, detection, classification, and change detection.

## Results

On semantic segmentation, RS-vHeat achieves 92.82 mF1 on Potsdam and 68.72 mIoU on iSAID, surpassing all CNN-based and ViT-based RSFMs while using fewer FLOPs (921G) than ViT-based methods (1536G). On object detection (DIOR), it reaches 82.30 mAP50, outperforming SkySense (78.73) by 3.57% with 6.3× fewer FLOPs (266G vs >1679G). On change detection (LEVIR-CD), it attains 93.48 F1, exceeding SkySense by 0.9 points. Across all tasks, RS-vHeat reduces memory usage by 84% and improves throughput by 2.7× compared to Swin-B-based RSFMs at 1024×1024 resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RS-vHeat outperforms all CNN-based RSFMs (GASSL, SeCo, SSL4EO, CACo, TOV, SAMRS) on both Potsdam mF1 and iSAID mIoU, with the closest competitor TOV at 92.03/66.24 vs. RS-vHeat's 92.82/68.72. Against ViT-based methods, it surpasses Scale-MAE by 1.28% mF1 and 2.95% mIoU while using 56% fewer FLOPs (921G vs 1536G). Among Swin-based models, RS-vHeat matches SatLas on iSAID mIoU (68.72 vs 68.71) and exceeds RingMo, GFM, and CMID, but trails SkySense by 1.17% mF1 and 2.19% mIoU—however, SkySense uses >702M parameters and >2708G FLOPs versus RS-vHeat's 148M and 921G. On DIOR object detection, RS-vHeat (82.30 mAP50) beats SkySense (78.73) by 3.57% with 6.3× lower FLOPs. On SAR-specific tasks, RS-vHeat leads all baselines: 83.9 OA on WHU-OPT-SAR (vs. 82.9 for MCANet), 57.46 mIoU on AIR-PolSAR-Seg (vs. 51.93 for DANet), and 87.1 mAP50 on SAR-AIRcraft-1.0 (vs. 77.7 for SA-Net). On LEVIR-CD change detection, it achieves 93.48 F1, exceeding SkySense (92.58) and Scale-MAE (92.07) with comparable FLOPs (46G vs 45G for Swin-B baselines).

## Relation to Prior Work

RS-vHeat directly extends vHeat [63], a heat conduction-based vision model, by adapting its Heat Conduction Operator (HCO) to the remote sensing domain and embedding it within a self-supervised pre-training pipeline. It departs from the dominant RSFM paradigm of attention-based backbones (ViT, Swin) and CNN-based backbones (ResNet) by replacing self-attention with a physics-inspired diffusion process of O(N^1.5) complexity, while retaining a Swin-B-style hierarchical structure. The self-supervised strategy builds on masked image modeling (SimMIM, RingMo, Scale-MAE) and contrastive learning (GASSL, SeCo, CACo, SkySense), but introduces frequency-domain hierarchical masking and multi-domain (spatial + frequency) reconstruction as a novel pre-training objective tailored to preserve small RS objects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 84% memory reduction and 2.7× throughput improvement over attention-based RSFMs at 1024×1024 resolution is practically significant for processing the very large, high-resolution images common in satellite and aerial imagery, where quadratic attention becomes prohibitive. The multi-modal (optical + SAR) support in a single unified model addresses a real gap, as most RSFMs handle only optical data. However, the interpretability claims remain somewhat thin—the heat conduction metaphor provides a physical narrative but the paper does not demonstrate that the learned diffusion coefficients k(ωx,ωy) yield actionable insights about object structure beyond the analogy. Open questions include whether the O(N^1.5) advantage persists at extreme resolutions (4K+), how the method generalizes to hyperspectral or LiDAR modalities, and whether the modest accuracy gap versus SkySense (Swin-H) narrows with larger pre-training data.
