## Problem

Existing CLIP-based zero-shot anomaly detection methods (WinCLIP, AnomalyCLIP, AdaCLIP, etc.) rely solely on spatial-domain visual features and text prompts, overlooking the frequency information of images. The authors observe that anomaly regions often exhibit distinctive frequency patterns—high-frequency components corresponding to fine details such as soft borders—that are absent in surrounding normal regions, yet no prior method explicitly mines this signal for anomaly detection.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (processed at 336×336 resolution by the frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 visual encoder) and two fixed text prompts ('A photo of a normal object' / 'A photo of a damaged object') as input. It produces an image-level anomaly score and a pixel-level anomaly segmentation map, both computed by averaging predictions across four intermediate block levels of the visual encoder. The segmentation map is initially at patch-token resolution (H×W) and is upsampled to full image resolution for evaluation.

## Method

FE-CLIP injects frequency information into the CLIP visual encoder via two complementary adapters inserted at four block levels while keeping the backbone frozen. The Frequency-aware Feature Extraction (FFE) adapter splits patch tokens into non-overlapping 3×3 windows, applies DCT → linear layer + GELU → inverse DCT to capture frequency-aware patterns in the spatial domain. The Local Frequency Statistics (LFS) adapter applies a dense Sliding Window DCT (3×3) to extract localized frequency responses, then averages across the Q×Q frequency groups to produce a multi-channel spatial map that directly encodes frequency statistics in the frequency domain. The two adapter outputs are fused with the original features via a weighted sum (λ=0.1) before feeding into the next block. Training optimizes only the adapter parameters using a combined binary cross-entropy (classification) and focal + Dice (segmentation) loss on MVTec AD test data, with the CLIP text and visual encoders fully frozen.

## Results

On the ZSAD task (Table 1), FE-CLIP achieves the best or tied-best AUROC/AP on 6 out of 8 datasets, with standout margins on DTD-Synthetic (98.3/99.4 vs. AnomalyCLIP's 93.5/97.0), BTAD (90.3/90.0 vs. 88.3/87.3), and BrainMRI (94.8/93.8 vs. 90.3/92.2). On the ZSAS task (Table 2), FE-CLIP attains the highest AUROC/PRO on 7 out of 8 datasets, with the largest gap on Kvasir (79.8/60.6 vs. AnomalyCLIP's 78.9/45.6) and DTD-Synthetic (99.0/97.4 vs. 97.9/92.3). Under the few-normal-shot setting (Table 5), FE-CLIP outperforms all baselines at 1-, 2-, and 4-shot on both MVTec AD and VisA, e.g., 96.1/90.9 (pixel) and 95.6/98.1 (image) at 1-shot on MVTec AD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FE-CLIP outperforms AnomalyCLIP (the strongest CLIP-based baseline) on 6 of 8 ZSAD datasets, with the largest gains on DTD-Synthetic (+4.8 AUROC, +2.4 AP), BTAD (+2.0 AUROC, +2.7 AP), and BrainMRI (+4.5 AUROC, +1.6 AP); it ties AnomalyCLIP on DAGM (97.5/92.3) and slightly trails on Br35H AP (93.8 vs. 94.7). Against WinCLIP, FE-CLIP wins on all 8 ZSAD datasets, with the most dramatic improvement on BTAD (+22.1 AUROC) and MPDD (+14.4 AUROC). On ZSAS, FE-CLIP beats AnomalyCLIP on 7 of 8 datasets (losing only on DAGM to VCP-CLIP by 0.9 AUROC / 1.7 PRO) and surpasses AdaCLIP on every dataset where both report numbers. Under few-normal-shot settings, FE-CLIP dominates all baselines (PatchCore, WinCLIP, AnomalyGPT, PromptAD, InCTRL) at 1/2/4-shot on both MVTec AD and VisA, e.g., 96.7/91.7 pixel-level and 96.6/98.4 image-level at 4-shot on MVTec AD. The paper notes that FE-CLIP's advantage is more consistent on segmentation than detection because frequency features are injected directly into patch tokens (used for segmentation) rather than only implicitly into the global class token (used for detection) via self-attention.

## Relation to Prior Work

FE-CLIP extends the CLIP-based zero-shot anomaly detection lineage initiated by WinCLIP and advanced by AnomalyCLIP, ClipSAM, VCP-CLIP, and AdaCLIP, all of which adapt the frozen CLIP visual encoder via prompt learning or cross-modal interaction modules. The key departure is the explicit injection of frequency-domain information through two lightweight adapters (FFE and LFS) applied at multiple block levels of the ViT encoder, a signal source that all prior CLIP-based ZSAD methods overlooked. Rather than modifying the text side or adding cross-modal interaction, FE-CLIP operates purely on the visual feature stream, using DCT to extract and re-inject frequency patterns while keeping the CLIP backbone entirely frozen.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that frequency-domain cues (particularly high-frequency components associated with fine-grained texture defects) provide a complementary signal for zero-shot anomaly detection that is orthogonal to the semantic matching already captured by CLIP's text-visual alignment. The consistent gains across 10 diverse datasets spanning industrial inspection and medical imaging suggest the frequency signal is domain-agnostic, though the authors honestly note it may fail when anomalies do not alter local frequency statistics (e.g., a squashed dumpling that looks like a meat pie). Open questions include whether the benefit persists under more extreme domain shifts, whether other frequency representations (wavelets, Fourier) offer further gains, and whether the approach transfers to video or 3D anomaly detection where temporal/spectral frequency patterns may be even more informative.
