## Problem

Industrial anomaly inspection suffers from severe scarcity of defective samples, and existing training-based generation methods (GANs, fine-tuned diffusion models) fail to handle complex, multi-defect, or fine-grained anomalies—especially in the one-shot setting where only a single reference sample per defect type is available. No prior approach generates diverse, structurally aligned industrial defects in a fully training-free manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251337/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a normal background image, a single defect reference image with its segmentation mask, and a target mask (randomly positioned within a pre-defined affected object area) as input. Defect regions are cropped and enlarged before generation to focus on fine details, then the result is seamlessly composited back into the full-resolution normal image. The output is a generated defect image with a corresponding mask, produced entirely at inference time without any model retraining.

## Method

TF-IDG builds on the Anydoor zero-shot image-customization framework (Stable Diffusion V2.1 + ControlNet + DINOv2) and introduces three training-free guidance modules applied during the DDIM denoising loop. (1) Feature Alignment computes a Sinkhorn Distance (entropic optimal transport) between feature vectors extracted from the generated and reference defect regions, pulling the generated features toward the real defect distribution in the first 3/5 of sampling steps. (2) Adaptive Anomaly Mask detects small or narrow regions where the generated image still resembles the normal sample (via a pixel-level difference map thresholded at τ) and applies an additional Sinkhorn-based loss specifically to those overlooked areas. (3) Texture Preservation injects background style by concatenating keys and values from the normal image's DDIM inversion into the self-attention module, and applies AdaIN at the latent level in later steps for color and texture harmonization. All three losses are backpropagated through the frozen U-Net as gradient guidance on the noise prediction, requiring no parameter updates.

## Results

On MVTec AD (few-shot), TF-IDG achieves 99.6% image-level AUROC and 95.8% pixel-level Pro, outperforming the next-best method AnoDiff (99.2% / 94.0%). On VisA, it reaches 97.4% image-level AUROC and 91.6% Pro, a 6.0% and 3.1% improvement over AnoDiff respectively. In terms of generation quality, TF-IDG attains the highest Local IS scores (3.32 on MVTec AD, 3.90 on VisA) and the best average anomaly classification accuracy of 79.84% (few-shot) and 71.92% (one-shot), surpassing all training-based and training-free baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TF-IDG outperforms all six generation baselines (Crop-Paste, DFMGAN, AnoDiff, AnoGen, DIAG, Anydoor) on both MVTec AD and VisA across image-level and pixel-level detection metrics. The closest competitor is AnoDiff, which achieves 99.2% image-level AUROC on MVTec AD (vs. 99.6% for TF-IDG) and 97.4% pixel-level AUROC on VisA (vs. 97.6%), but TF-IDG leads by 3.1% in Pro on VisA and 1.8% in pixel-level AP on MVTec AD. In the one-shot setting (Ours†), TF-IDG still surpasses AnoDiff on MVTec AD (98.2 vs. 93.6 image-level AUROC) and VisA (93.7 vs. 91.4), demonstrating robustness with a single reference. For generation quality (Table 1), TF-IDG achieves the highest Local IS on both datasets (3.32 MVTec, 3.90 VisA), while IC-LPIPS scores are competitive but not always best (tied at 0.32 on MVTec, tied at 0.30 on VisA). When integrated with downstream detectors, TF-IDG improves GLASS by 3.4% Pro on VisA and UniAD by 5.4% image-level AUROC on VisA, confirming that the generated samples provide meaningful augmentation beyond what hand-crafted or training-based synthesis offers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Anydoor (CVPR 2024), a zero-shot object-level image customization framework that combines DINOv2 feature retrieval with ControlNet structure preservation, repurposing it from general object compositing to industrial defect synthesis. It departs from Anydoor by identifying three specific failure modes in the industrial context—geometric misalignment in complex reconstructions, neglect of small/narrow defect regions, and poor background texture integration—and addresses each with targeted gradient-guided losses (Sinkhorn-based feature alignment, adaptive anomaly masking, and AdaIN-based texture preservation). The paper also positions itself against training-based anomaly generators (DFMGAN, AnoDiff, AnoGen) that require fine-tuning per defect type and struggle in the extreme one-shot regime, arguing that a training-free pipeline eliminates this bottleneck entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, one-shot capability is practically significant for manufacturing settings where new defect types may appear with only a single example and retraining is infeasible. The method's integration with existing detection models (GLASS, UniAD) demonstrates plug-and-play utility, with consistent gains across both one-to-one and multi-class architectures. However, the performance gap between one-shot and few-shot settings remains notable (e.g., VisA i-AUC drops from 97.4 to 93.7), and evaluation is limited to two datasets, leaving open questions about generalization to more diverse industrial domains, 3D inspection, or extremely subtle defects. The reliance on a pre-defined target mask and a reference image per defect type also constrains fully autonomous deployment.
