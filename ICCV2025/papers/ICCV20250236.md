## Problem

Existing 3D vision-language models handle either scene understanding or human motion understanding in isolation, leaving a critical gap in jointly modeling human-scene interactions in 3D space. No benchmark or foundation model exists for answering questions that require simultaneous comprehension of a human's state, behavior, and their surrounding 3D environment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250236/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 3D scene represented as a point cloud (P×6 with 3D coordinates and RGB), a temporal sequence of T SMPL human poses, and a natural language question. The scene is pre-segmented into N objects whose point clouds are individually encoded, while the motion sequence is mapped to discrete VQ-VAE tokens. The output is a free-form natural language answer, evaluated by GPT-4 scoring on a 0–2 scale across 16 sub-tasks spanning perception, reasoning, prediction, dialogue, and planning.

## Method

HIS-GPT encodes scene and motion separately using a frozen Uni3D encoder (object-level features from segmented point clouds) and a frozen motion VQ-VAE, then fuses them through two novel modules before feeding into a Vicuna-1.5 LLM. The Auxiliary Interaction (AInt) module injects interaction-aware knowledge via three auxiliary losses—activity classification (fusing motion with k-nearest scene objects), spatial relation detection (8-class human-object relation), and contact detection (per-joint binary contact)—trained jointly with the LLM loss. The Layout-Trajectory Position Encoding (LTP) module generates position embeddings via spatial and temporal Fourier transforms, encoding each object's 3D location and each motion frame's 3D location plus timestamp, so both modalities receive aligned spatiotemporal context. Training proceeds in two stages: modality alignment with auxiliary losses on 60k captions, followed by instruction tuning on 700k QA samples, with only projection layers, AInt, and LTP fine-tuned while encoders and LLM remain frozen.

## Results

On HIS-Bench (800 questions across 16 sub-tasks, scored 0–100 per dimension), HIS-GPT achieves an average score of 48.7, outperforming the strongest baseline GPT-4o (31.3) by 17.4 points. HIS-GPT shows particular strength in spatial relationship tasks (e.g., Human Position: 37.0 vs. GPT-4o's 9.7) and human-object interaction (Contact Part: 55.5 vs. 31.2). Among baselines, vision LLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-4v, Qwen-VL-max) substantially outperform 3D scene LLMs (LL3DA: 6.7, Chat-Scene: 8.2) and caption-based pipelines (5.0–17.9), confirming that direct visual processing is critical for HIS understanding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HIS-GPT (48.7 avg) decisively outperforms all 11 baselines across every category. The strongest baseline, GPT-4o (31.3), is surpassed by 17.4 points on average; the gap widens dramatically on spatial tasks (HIS-GPT 42.1 vs. GPT-4o 25.8) and human-object interaction (55.5 vs. 36.6). Among vision LLMs, GPT-4o leads (31.3), followed by GPT-4v (24.2) and Qwen-VL-max (23.5), all of which rely on rendered video rather than native 3D input. 3D scene LLMs (LL3DA: 6.7, Chat-Scene: 8.2) and caption-based pipelines (LL3DA+AvatarGPT+GPT-4: 5.0) perform poorly, confirming that neither scene-only 3D understanding nor text-mediated descriptions capture human-scene interactions adequately. Notably, GPT-4v scores 58.7 on Dialogue (likely leveraging strong conversational priors) but drops to 7.2 on Human Position, illustrating the fragility of 2D vision models on 3D spatial reasoning. HIS-GPT's advantage is most pronounced in tasks requiring joint human-scene modeling: Human Position (37.0 vs. 9.7 for GPT-4o), Contact Part (55.5 vs. 31.2), and Intent Prediction (50.5 vs. 23.3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: 3D scene-language models (Chat-Scene, LL3DA, 3D-LLM) that understand environments but ignore human agents, and 3D human-motion language models (M3GPT, MotionGPT, AvatarGPT) that interpret poses and motions without scene context. HIS-GPT directly reuses Chat-Scene's Uni3D-based scene encoder and M3GPT's motion VQ-VAE as its two modality encoders, but departs from both by introducing the Auxiliary Interaction (AInt) module and Layout-Trajectory Position Encoding (LTP) module, which are specifically designed to inject cross-modal human-scene interaction cues that neither parent architecture provides. The paper positions itself as the first to jointly model 3D scene and human motion in a single LLM, filling a gap that prior situated-scene work (SQA3D, EmbodiedScan) only partially addressed through text-based or first-person-view proxies rather than explicit 3D human pose.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HIS-Bench (800 questions, 31 scenes, 500 motion segments) is the first dedicated benchmark for human-in-scene understanding, and the finding that even GPT-4o scores only 31.3/100 average confirms this is a genuinely underexplored capability gap in current VLMs. The work is important for embodied AI and household robotics, where agents must simultaneously track what a person is doing and where they are relative to objects. Key limitations include the small benchmark scale, reliance on GPT-4 as automatic evaluator, and the fact that the model is evaluated only on rendered video inputs for baselines rather than true 3D interaction. Open questions include whether the AInt and LTP mechanisms generalize to more diverse scene types, whether the approach scales to real-time inference for deployed robots, and how to extend from question-answering to closed-loop action planning.
