## Problem

Existing 3D scene reconstruction methods treat scenes as undifferentiated wholes, while open-set segmentation methods fail to produce complete instances from cluttered environments, and category-specific generative models struggle to generalize or maintain physical alignment with real-world scenes. No unified system can simultaneously decompose arbitrary object instances from complex scenes and reconstruct each one with complete geometry and appearance that faithfully aligns with the original scene context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251233/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a posed RGB image sequence, first reconstructs it into a 2D Gaussian Splatting scene (with 16-dim randomly initialized per-Gaussian embeddings), and uses EntitySeg to generate class-agnostic 2D instance masks as supervision. The output is a set of complete 3D instances in 2DGS format that can be individually extracted and placed back into the scene with preserved geometry and appearance, along with a learned instance-level feature field for segmentation. The 'in-situ' constraint means reconstructed instances must match the original scene's scale, shape, and texture rather than producing a generic object.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages. For decomposition, the paper introduces spatial contrastive learning: it traces Gaussian rasterization to build spatial trackers per 2D mask, computes view consensus rates to cluster masks across views and filter under-segmentations, then applies DBSCAN to form robust 3D instance masks. A contrastive loss is jointly applied on 2D intra-view masks, 2D cross-view masks, and 3D Gaussian points, allowing noisy 2D supervision and sparse 3D supervision to mutually guide each other into a dense, distinctive feature field. For completion, the paper proposes in-situ generation built on MVDFusion: it selects occlusion-aware viewpoints around each instance, feeds re-rendered views as alternated conditions through the diffusion denoising process, and warps known latent features onto target views using rendering depths and surface normals to enforce geometric consistency. The generated views are then jointly fine-tuned with source observations to refine the instance's 2DGS, yielding a complete object aligned with the physical scene.

## Results

On the LERF-Mask dataset, InstaScene achieves 85.6% average mIoU for instance segmentation, substantially outperforming LangSplat (60.6%) and GSGrouping (58.1%), with the largest gains on the complex Kitchen scene (77.3% vs. 50.7% and 43.1%). For in-situ generation on Replica-CAD, the method achieves 32.57/29.02 PSNR (known/unknown views), 0.716 Volume IoU, and 0.016 Chamfer Distance, surpassing the best generative baseline SpaRP (25.09/23.03 PSNR, 0.590 Vol IoU, 0.037 CD) by a wide margin while also exceeding the original 2DGS reconstruction in unknown-region quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For instance segmentation, InstaScene (85.6% avg mIoU) decisively outperforms both LangSplat (60.6%) and GSGrouping (58.1%), with the gap widening on cluttered scenes (Kitchen: 77.3% vs. 50.7%/43.1%), attributed to the spatial contrastive learning producing more distinguishable feature fields. For complete reconstruction, InstaScene dominates all four generative baselines: it exceeds the original 2DGS reconstruction (Vol. IoU 0.716 vs. 0.361) by nearly doubling the recovered geometry while maintaining comparable known-view fidelity (PSNR 32.57 vs. 31.67). Among generative methods, SpaRP is the strongest baseline (Vol. IoU 0.590, CD 0.037) but still trails InstaScene by 0.126 in Volume IoU and 0.021 in Chamfer Distance, with a 7.5 dB PSNR gap on unknown views. Single-view methods MVDFusion and InstantMesh perform substantially worse, with MVDFusion achieving only 0.150 F1-Score and 17.46 PSNR on unknown views, indicating that generic image-to-3D pipelines struggle to maintain scene alignment. The paper also notes that SpaRP fails to recover geometry for certain objects (e.g., a teapot) due to domain gap between generative priors and real-world scenes, whereas InstaScene's geometry-aware feature warping imposes explicit consistency constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) as the scene representation and MVD-Fusion as the 3D generative prior, but departs from both in significant ways. For segmentation, it moves beyond prior Gaussian-based methods (LangSplat, GSGrouping) that rely on VLM features or video tracking—both of which degrade in cluttered scenes—by introducing spatial contrastive learning guided by traced Gaussian rasterization, which creates robust cross-view spatial trackers. For completion, it departs from generic image-to-3D pipelines (InstantMesh, SpaRP) that treat the object in isolation, instead proposing in-situ generation that conditions the diffusion process on all known multi-view observations and geometric cues from the surrounding scene, ensuring the completed object aligns physically with its original context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified decomposition-plus-completion pipeline addresses a practical gap for robotics and scene editing: prior methods either segment but leave objects incomplete, or complete objects but lose alignment with the scene. The in-situ generation concept—using the scene's own geometry and multi-view observations as omni-conditions rather than a single canonical view—is a useful paradigm for any task requiring object-level manipulation within a reconstructed environment. Key limitations include inability to handle dynamic, transparent, or highly reflective objects, and the pipeline's dependence on 2DGS reconstruction quality. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 4D scenes, whether the spatial contrastive learning generalizes beyond Gaussian-based representations, and how it scales to scenes with hundreds of instances.
