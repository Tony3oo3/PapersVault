## Problem

Recent point trackers are trained primarily on synthetic data due to the difficulty of collecting pixel-accurate tracking annotations for real videos, creating a sim-to-real gap that limits performance. Additionally, it is unclear whether the millions of unlabeled real videos and complex semi-supervised protocols used by prior work (e.g., BootsTAPIR's 15M videos) are truly necessary, and whether the increasingly complex transformer architectures can be simplified without sacrificing quality.

## Input / Output

The model takes a video sequence of T frames (resized to 256×256) and a set of N query points specified as (t^q, x^q, y^q) tuples, and iteratively refines per-frame track coordinates (x_t, y_t), visibility V_t ∈ [0,1], and confidence C_t ∈ [0,1] for all frames. It is available in two modes: an online (causal) variant that processes a sliding window of T' frames forward-only and can track indefinitely, and an offline variant that processes the entire video in one window, enabling bidirectional tracking but bounded by memory. During evaluation, predicted visibility is multiplied by predicted confidence and thresholded to produce a binary occlusion decision.

## Method

CoTracker3 computes multi-scale convolutional features per frame, extracts 4D correlation volumes between query-point neighborhoods and current track estimates (simplified from LocoTrack by replacing its ad-hoc module with a plain MLP), and iteratively refines tracks, confidence, and visibility via a transformer that applies factorized time attention, cross-track (group) attention over N points, and proxy tokens for efficiency. The architecture removes the global matching stage used by TAPIR, BootsTAPIR, and LocoTrack, and uses only Fourier-encoded per-frame displacements plus correlation features as token inputs. For training, the model is first pre-trained on synthetic Kubric with Huber + BCE losses, then fine-tuned on real videos annotated by an ensemble of four frozen teacher trackers (CoTracker3 online/offline, CoTracker, TAPIR) sampled randomly per batch, with SIFT-based query point sampling to filter ambiguous tracks. This pseudo-labeling protocol requires only ~15k real videos—1,000× fewer than BootsTAPIR—yet outperforms it on all TAP-Vid benchmarks, and the student surpasses all its teachers through ensembling and complementary strengths.

## Results

CoTracker3 (online, fine-tuned on 15k real videos) achieves a mean TAP-Vid score of 76.1 (AJ) / 73.3 (δ_vis_avg) / 90.8 (OA), outperforming BootsTAPIR (74.8 / 64.9 / 86.3) which was trained on 15M real videos — 1,000× more data. On Dynamic Replica, CoTracker3 offline achieves 42.3 δ_occ for occluded points, substantially ahead of LocoTrack (29.8) and BootsTAPIR (28.0), demonstrating superior occlusion handling. Even trained solely on synthetic Kubric data, CoTracker3 online (74.4 mean) already surpasses CoTracker (71.8) and TAPIR (68.7), while using 2× fewer parameters than CoTracker and running 27% faster than LocoTrack.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoTracker3 (Kub+15k, online) outperforms all six baselines on the mean TAP-Vid score. Against BootsTAPIR [8] (the strongest prior method, trained on 15M real videos), CoTracker3 gains +1.3 mean AJ and +8.4 δ_vis_avg while using 1,000× fewer real videos and a simpler training protocol without augmentations, EMA, or loss masks. Against LocoTrack [4], CoTracker3 wins on mean score (76.1 vs 74.6) and dramatically on occluded tracking on Dynamic Replica (40.1 vs 29.8 δ_occ), attributed to cross-track attention enabling joint reasoning over visible and occluded points. Against CoTracker [16], CoTracker3 improves mean AJ by +4.3 (76.1 vs 71.8) while using half the parameters (25M vs 45M). Against TAPIR [7], the gain is +7.4 mean AJ (76.1 vs 68.7), with the largest improvement on DAVIS (76.3 vs 70.0 δ_vis). Against TAPTR [19] and PIPs++ [44], CoTracker3 leads by +3.8 and +11.6 mean AJ respectively, confirming that the simplified transformer architecture with 4D correlation and cross-track attention is more effective than detection-style or correlation-only designs.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoTracker3 sits at the intersection of several recent point-tracking architectures: it inherits iterative refinement and convolutional features from PIPs, cross-track attention and proxy tokens from CoTracker, and 4D correlation from LocoTrack, while deliberately removing the global matching stage that TAPIR, BootsTAPIR, and LocoTrack all include. Its key conceptual departure is in training: rather than BootsTAPIR's complex self-training recipe (augmentations, EMA, loss masks, 15M videos), CoTracker3 uses a simple multi-teacher pseudo-labeling protocol on a small collection of real videos, treating off-the-shelf synthetic-trained trackers as complementary annotators. The paper also contributes a systematic ablation of which architectural components (global matching, cross-track attention, 4D correlation) are actually necessary, positioning itself as a simplification study as much as a new model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1000× data-efficiency result (15k vs. 15M real videos) is practically significant: it makes real-data fine-tuning of point trackers accessible to groups without massive video collections, and the multi-teacher pseudo-labeling protocol is simple enough to adopt broadly. The finding that global matching is redundant while cross-track attention is critical for occlusion handling provides clear architectural guidance for future tracker design. Open questions include whether the student can continue improving beyond the teachers' ceiling with more data (the scaling curve plateaus around 30k videos), whether the protocol transfers to other correspondence tasks (optical flow, dense tracking), and whether the online variant's inability to track occluded points as well as the offline variant can be closed with longer context windows.
