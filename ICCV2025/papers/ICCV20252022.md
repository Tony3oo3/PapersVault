## Problem

Existing 4D medical video frame interpolation methods rely on a simplified linear-motion hypothesis to regress intermediate frames via optical flow, but realistic respiratory motions are nonlinear and quasi-periodic with specific frequencies. This mismatch causes temporal inconsistency and spatial distortion in interpolated volumes, particularly for midmost frames farthest from the reference endpoints.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of boundary 4D volumes (e.g., end-diastolic and end-systolic cardiac MRI at 128×128×32, or end-inspiratory and end-expiratory lung CT at 128×128×128) with 6–16 frames per sequence, and produces the full set of intermediate 4D frames. During inference, only the two endpoint frames are available; the remaining frames are zero-masked and fed through a fine-tuned VAE to jointly adapt the Fourier bases and produce a coarse inpainted video that conditions the diffusion model.

## Method

FB-Diff resolves temporal interpolation from a frequency perspective by extracting Fourier motion bases in a VAE feature space via a Fourier Motion Operator that combines learnable physiology motion priors (frequency embeddings encoding generic respiratory discipline) as the phase component with case-specific FFT-derived spectral features as the magnitude component. A frame-inpainting-based basis synthesis step jointly optimizes the adapted Fourier bases and a coarse inpainted video using a fine-tuned VAE, so the bases are domain-adapted to the specific masked sequence. The core generator is a latent diffusion model (lightweight UNet) conditioned on the adapted Fourier bases, VAE-encoded endpoint frames, and the coarse inpainted video; a Basis Interaction Operator injects the Fourier bases by selectively enhancing video feature representations according to per-region motion intensity, guiding the denoising process toward temporally consistent, frequency-aware intermediate frames.

## Results

On ACDC cardiac, FB-Diff achieves the best perceptual metrics: LPIPS 1.452, FID 21.5, and FVD 88.9, while maintaining a competitive PSNR of 30.95 dB. On 4D-Lung, it similarly leads in LPIPS (2.066), FID (37.7), and FVD (113.6) with PSNR 30.18 dB. Compared to the strongest flow-based baseline UVI-Net, FB-Diff improves LPIPS by 0.210 and FVD by 5.3 on ACDC, and by 0.145 and 8.1 on 4D-Lung. For midmost frames (the hardest interpolation case), FB-Diff reduces FID by 4.3 and FVD by 8.2 over UVI-Net on ACDC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FB-Diff achieves the best LPIPS, FID, and FVD on both ACDC and 4D-Lung, outperforming all 10 baselines on these perceptual metrics. On PSNR, UVI-Net [30] remains the strongest flow-based method (32.16 dB on ACDC, 31.57 dB on 4D-Lung), exceeding FB-Diff by ~1.2 dB, but FB-Diff surpasses it by 0.210 LPIPS and 5.3 FVD on ACDC. Among diffusion-based baselines, FB-Diff dominates across all four metrics: LDMVFI [10] scores 27.11 dB PSNR and 2.943 LPIPS on ACDC versus FB-Diff's 30.95 and 1.452, and LDDM [7] trails by 6.4 dB PSNR and 1.18 LPIPS. The flow-based methods SVIN [13], MPVF [51], and IFRNet [31] are competitive on PSNR (30.6–31.5 dB) but consistently lag FB-Diff by 0.1–0.3 LPIPS and 5–10 FVD points. DDM [29], a diffusion deformable model, underperforms all other methods on FVD (110.3 on ACDC, 165.5 on 4D-Lung), highlighting the benefit of Fourier-based conditional guidance over geodesic-path generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant linear-motion-hypothesis paradigm in 4D medical VFI (SVIN, MPVF, UVI-Net), which regresses intermediate frames via optical-flow or deformation-field interpolation, by reframing respiratory motion as a quasi-periodic oscillatory process decomposable into Fourier bases. It also extends the diffusion-based VFI line (LDMVFI, LDDM, Conditional Diffusion) by introducing structured conditional guidance—learnable physiological motion priors combined with case-specific FFT spectral features—rather than relying on implicit temporal learning from start/end frames alone. The VAE feature-space formulation and the Basis Interaction Operator for injecting frequency-domain motion patterns into a latent diffusion UNet are the primary architectural novelties.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frequency-domain conditioning offers an interpretable and physically grounded mechanism for modeling periodic anatomical motion, which could generalize to other quasi-periodic clinical scenarios (e.g., cardiac cycles, peristalsis). However, the evaluation is limited to two datasets (ACDC cardiac MRI, 4D-Lung CT) with relatively small sequence lengths (6–16 frames), and the authors themselves note that highly unstable breathing would require additional signals such as ECG. An open question is whether the Fourier-basis conditioning remains effective for longer, more irregular motion sequences or for multi-organ 4D volumes where motion is less strictly periodic.
