## Problem

No existing benchmark, dataset, or method addresses language-guided 3D object placement in reconstructed real-world scenes. Unlike 3D visual grounding (single correct answer) or synthetic scene generation (privileged metadata available), this task requires simultaneously resolving one-to-many placement ambiguity, intrinsic 3D geometric reasoning, and joint understanding of scene geometry, asset shape, and language intent—all from noisy point clouds with no scene graphs, layouts, or object properties at test time.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a colored 3D scene point cloud (N×6, from ScanNet reconstructions), a 3D asset point cloud (from PartObjaverse-Tiny), and a natural-language prompt containing one or more spatial, rotational, or visibility constraints. It outputs a single 3D translation vector and a yaw rotation angle (discretized into 8 bins of 45°). The task is inherently one-to-many: multiple placements can satisfy the same prompt, and the evaluation protocol checks each constraint individually as well as collectively, with physical plausibility (no mesh intersection, resting on a surface) enforced in every sample.

## Method

PLACEWIZARD adapts the Reason3D 3D-LLM architecture with four key modifications for placement. First, it replaces superpoint pooling with uniform spatial pooling via farthest point sampling to preserve fine-grained geometric detail needed for accurate placement. Second, it encodes the 3D asset with a Point-BERT encoder (pretrained on Objaverse) plus explicit size embeddings, projected into the LLM space via an MLP. Third, the LLM is instructed to emit three special tokens—[LOC], [ANC], [ROT]—whose embeddings feed a transformer decoder performing self-attention followed by cross-attention with both asset and pooled scene features. Fourth, three prediction heads produce a dense placement mask over the scene point cloud, a per-point rotation validity mask (8 discrete yaw angles), and an auxiliary anchor-localization mask. Training uses BCE+Dice losses for the placement and anchor masks, BCE for rotation, and cross-entropy for the LLM's token sequence, all trained end-to-end on the 100K-example PLACEIT3D-dataset.

## Results

On the PLACEIT3D-benchmark (3,500 examples), PLACEWIZARD achieves 54.9% Global Constraint Accuracy, 25.9% Language Adherence Success, and 15.0% Complete Placement Success. The strictest metric, Complete Placement Success (requiring both physical plausibility and full language constraint satisfaction), remains low across all methods (1.6%–15.0%), underscoring the inherent difficulty of the task. PLACEWIZARD leads on every reported metric, with the largest margins over baselines on Spatial (56.6%) and Rotational (17.3%) constraint accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PLACEWIZARD outperforms both OpenMask3D baselines and the adapted Reason3D across all seven metrics. Against the rule-based OpenMask3D baseline, PLACEWIZARD gains +25.7 points on Global Constraint Accuracy (54.9% vs. 29.2%) and +28.0 on Spatial (56.6% vs. 28.6%), though the rule-based system retains a slight edge on Physical plausibility (61.6% vs. 58.8%) due to its explicit collision checks. The LLM-based OpenMask3D baseline is the weakest overall (26.7% Global, 1.6% Complete Placement Success), primarily because GPT-4o lacks direct access to 3D geometry and frequently produces physically implausible placements. Compared to the adapted Reason3D baseline, PLACEWIZARD improves Global Constraint Accuracy by +10.1 points (54.9% vs. 44.8%) and Rotational accuracy by +10.7 points (17.3% vs. 6.6%), with the largest single-metric gain on Spatial (+19.1 points). The uniformly low Complete Placement Success scores (1.6%–15.0%) across all methods highlight that the task remains far from solved.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Reason3D, a 3D LLM for dense visual grounding, repurposing its Q-Former + LLM + mask-decoder pipeline for the fundamentally different task of object placement. It departs from 3D grounding (which admits a single correct answer) by formulating placement as a one-to-many problem, and from synthetic scene generation by operating on noisy real ScanNet point clouds without privileged metadata like scene graphs or clean geometry. The method introduces three key architectural modifications to Reason3D: uniform spatial pooling (replacing superpoints that over-merge planar surfaces), a Point-BERT asset encoder with scale embeddings, and a rotation prediction head, while retaining the LLM-based constraint reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first benchmark and large-scale training dataset for language-guided 3D object placement in real reconstructed scenes, filling a gap between 3D grounding and synthetic scene generation. The very low Complete Placement Success (15% for the best method) signals that the task is far from solved, and the authors acknowledge key limitations: restriction to horizontal surfaces, synthetic rule-based annotations without human verification, and a specialist model that has not been integrated into a generalist framework. Open questions include extending to arbitrary contact geometries (e.g., wall-mounted objects), handling inconsistent or underspecified language instructions, and whether the one-to-many ambiguity can be resolved with richer preference signals.
