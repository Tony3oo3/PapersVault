## Problem

Zero-shot CLIP suffers severe accuracy drops under common image corruptions (e.g., CIFAR-100 accuracy plummets from 49% to 10.79% under Gaussian noise at severity 5), yet existing test-time adaptation methods for CLIP are unimodal—adapting only the vision encoder or only text prompts—preventing the model from jointly adjusting both modalities to restore image-text alignment in the shared feature space.

## Input / Output

The method consumes streaming batches of unlabeled corrupted images (batch size 200 for CIFAR-10C/100C, 64 for ImageNet-C) along with a single fixed prompt template ('a photo of a <CLS>.') to generate text features. It outputs class labels via softmax over cosine similarities between adapted visual features and adapted text features, with the model reset to pre-trained weights after each corruption task to prevent catastrophic forgetting. Adaptation requires only one forward pass per batch, making it suitable for real-time deployment.

## Method

BATCLIP jointly adapts both CLIP's vision and text encoders by updating only their LayerNorm parameters (~0.044% of total parameters), optimizing three synergistic losses: (1) entropy minimization for confident predictions, (2) a projection matching loss that maximizes the scalar projection of class prototypes (mean visual features grouped by pseudo-labels) onto their corresponding normalized text features, and (3) an inter-class separability loss that maximizes cosine distance between all pairs of class prototypes. The key insight is that unimodal adaptation leaves one encoder frozen and unaware of the test distribution, whereas bimodal adaptation lets both encoders co-adjust to produce domain-specific, mutually aligned features. Class prototypes are used instead of individual image features to mitigate noise in pseudo-labels under corruption, and the single-step online update per batch avoids the catastrophic forgetting and computational cost of multi-iteration methods like WATT.

## Results

On CIFAR-10C (severity 5, ViT-B/16), BATCLIP achieves 73.85% mean accuracy across 15 corruption types, outperforming the strongest baseline SAR (67.37%) by 6.48 points and zero-shot CLIP (61.16%) by 12.69 points. On CIFAR-100C, BATCLIP reaches 42.09% mean accuracy vs. SAR's 41.19% and zero-shot CLIP's 35.79%. On ImageNet-C, BATCLIP achieves 30.72% mean accuracy, surpassing SAR (29.73%), VTE (25.60%), and zero-shot CLIP (24.51%). The method also generalizes to domain generalization benchmarks (OfficeHome, PACS, VLCS, Terra Incognita), with the largest gains on Terra Incognita (e.g., L38: 37.33% vs. 20.30% zero-shot).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BATCLIP outperforms all nine baselines across all three corruption benchmarks. Against the strongest non-CLIP TTA method SAR, BATCLIP gains +6.48 points on CIFAR-10C (73.85 vs. 67.37), +0.90 on CIFAR-100C (42.09 vs. 41.19), and +0.99 on ImageNet-C (30.72 vs. 29.73). Among CLIP-specific TTA methods, BATCLIP surpasses WATT-S* (the best CLIP baseline) by +1.04 on CIFAR-10C and +5.38 on CIFAR-100C, and outperforms VTE by +9.70 on CIFAR-10C and +6.48 on CIFAR-100C. TENT and RPL, which rely solely on entropy minimization, often perform near or below zero-shot CLIP on CIFAR-100C (37.96% and 38.47% vs. 35.79% ZS), while BATCLIP consistently improves over ZS. On ImageNet-C, BATCLIP exceeds StatA (the only other method evaluated there) by +6.05 points over γ=0.1 and +6.05 over γ=-1. On domain generalization datasets, BATCLIP matches or exceeds WATT-S* on most domains but shows the largest advantage on Terra Incognita (e.g., L38: 37.33% vs. 29.08% for WATT-S*), while WATT-S* retains a slight edge on some OfficeHome and PACS domains due to its multi-template prompt strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

BATCLIP extends the online test-time adaptation (TTA) line of work (TENT, RoTTA, SAR) by moving from unimodal, vision-only normalization-layer updates to a bimodal scheme that jointly adapts both CLIP's vision and text encoders. It departs from prior CLIP-specific TTA methods (TPT, VTE, WATT) that either tune text prompts alone, use prompt ensembles without parameter updates, or require multiple prompt templates and gradient steps. The method draws on LayerNorm fine-tuning for efficient multimodal adaptation (Zhao et al.) and prototypical networks for class-prototype computation, but introduces a novel projection matching loss and inter-class separability loss that explicitly enforce image-text alignment and visual feature discriminability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: CLIP's well-known zero-shot capability does not transfer to corrupted inputs, which is critical for safety-critical deployments (autonomous driving, medical imaging). By showing that both encoders must be jointly adapted and that inter-class separability of prototypes is the dominant loss component, the work provides a principled recipe for lightweight, single-step CLIP adaptation that is fast (0.2s/batch) and avoids catastrophic forgetting. Limitations include reliance on potentially noisy pseudo-labels for prototype computation, adaptation restricted to LayerNorm parameters (~0.044%), and evaluation limited to a single prompt template; whether the approach generalizes to other VLMs or more complex, compound corruptions remains open.
