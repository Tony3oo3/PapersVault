## Problem

Existing pathological image restoration methods rely heavily on GANs, which suffer from training instability, loss of structural details, and artifact generation, while also struggling to handle composite degradation (low resolution, blur, noise) in a unified framework. The large scale of whole slide images further complicates efficient restoration, as prior models require substantial computational resources and memory to process high-resolution pathological content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250467/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded 256×256 H&E-stained pathological image patch as input and produces a restored high-quality patch of the same resolution. Internally, the degraded image is encoded into multi-scale discrete tokens (as small as 8×8) via a VQVAE encoder with a shared codebook of size 4096, and the autoregressive model generates the corresponding high-quality token sequence from coarse to fine scales. Notably, the model can restore satisfactory images even when only a limited set of degraded sparse tokens (e.g., 8×8) is provided as conditioning, demonstrating efficient compression of pathological content.

## Method

CVARPath introduces a conditional next-scale prediction framework where a transformer-based autoregressive model generates high-quality image tokens scale-by-scale, guided by degraded conditional tokens from the current scale. A novel attention mask ensures each predicted token attends only to its prefix and the corresponding conditional tokens, enabling pixel-wise constraint on generation. The VQVAE encoder is pre-trained with a contrastive learning objective that pulls quantized latent features of degraded and original images closer in embedding space, learning a consistent sparse vocabulary across degradation types. During training, the frozen encoder produces multi-scale token sequences for both clean and degraded images, and the transformer is optimized with cross-entropy loss to predict the next-scale tokens given all lower-scale tokens and the current-scale conditional tokens. This design allows the model to handle super-resolution, denoising, and deblurring within a single unified architecture without task-specific modifications.

## Results

On the super-resolution task (×8), CVARPath achieves FID of 41.10 on MIDOG2022 and 78.12 on TIGER2021, representing up to a 70-point FID reduction over the next-best method (SwinIR at 145.94 on MIDOG2022). For ×4 super-resolution, CVARPath reaches PSNR 27.05 / SSIM 0.866 / LPIPS 0.093 / FID 37.53 on MIDOG2022, outperforming all baselines on every metric. Across denoising and deblurring tasks, CVARPath similarly leads on perceptual metrics (LPIPS, FID) while remaining competitive on distortion metrics (PSNR, SSIM), with the largest FID gains observed under heavy degradation (e.g., FID 42.51 vs. 90.47 for SwinIR at noise density 0.5 on MIDOG2022).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CVARPath outperforms all five baselines (Pix2Pix, CycleGAN, BSRGAN, SwinIR, DiffBIR) on FID and LPIPS across every degradation setting and both datasets, with the most dramatic margins against GAN-based methods (e.g., FID 41.10 vs. 144.35 for CycleGAN on MIDOG2022 SR×8). Against the strongest baseline SwinIR, CVARPath achieves substantially lower FID (37.53 vs. 49.13 on MIDOG2022 SR×4; 41.10 vs. 145.94 on SR×8) and lower LPIPS, while matching or exceeding PSNR in most settings (the sole exception being TIGER2021 SR×4 where SwinIR edges ahead by 0.17 dB). DiffBIR, the diffusion-based baseline, is consistently outperformed by CVARPath on both perceptual and distortion metrics, with FID gaps of 20–80 points depending on the task. BSRGAN and Pix2Pix/CycleGAN are clearly inferior, with FID values 1.5–4× higher than CVARPath, confirming that autoregressive token-level generation with conditional guidance captures fine-grained pathological textures that GAN and diffusion pipelines miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Visual Autoregressive (VAR) framework for next-scale prediction to the conditional image restoration setting, specifically for pathological images. It builds on VQVAE-based multi-scale tokenization (with residual quantization from Lee et al.) and introduces two key modifications: a new attention mask that allows each predicted token to attend to its prefix and the corresponding degraded conditional token, and a contrastive pre-training strategy that aligns the latent representations of high-quality and degraded pathological images. The work departs from prior GAN- and diffusion-based restoration methods (Pix2Pix, BSRGAN, DiffBIR) and transformer-based methods (SwinIR) by framing restoration as sequential token generation guided by degraded tokens rather than direct pixel-space mapping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Pathological image restoration is clinically critical for cancer diagnosis, and this paper provides a unified autoregressive framework that handles super-resolution, denoising, and deblurring in a single model, addressing the fragmentation of prior methods that target individual degradation types. The demonstration that high-quality 256×256 patches can be restored from as few as 8×8 conditional tokens suggests practical efficiency gains for processing large whole-slide images. However, the evaluation is limited to 256×256 H&E-stained patches on two datasets, and generalization to other staining protocols, tissue types, or full WSI-scale restoration remains untested. Open questions include whether the contrastive pre-training advantage persists with larger codebooks or more diverse pathological domains, and how the method compares to emerging foundation models for computational pathology.
