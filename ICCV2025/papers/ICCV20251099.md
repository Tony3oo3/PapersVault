## Problem

Videos captured in low-light environments suffer from three simultaneous degradations—low visibility, motion blur from long exposure, and low frame rate—yet no prior work addresses all three jointly. While individual tasks (deblurring, frame interpolation, low-light enhancement) and partial combinations have been studied, the unified problem of transforming low-frame-rate, motion-blurred, low-visibility video into high-frame-rate, sharp, well-lit video remains unexplored and is ill-posed from frame-based information alone.

## Input / Output

The network takes three consecutive low-light blurred RGB frames (21 fps, 24 ms exposure, 1/64 ND filter, receiving only 0.39% of scene light) and three corresponding event voxel grids (32 temporal bins) from shutter intervals t−1, t, and t+1. It outputs N=8 normal-light sharp frames within the central shutter interval t, split into E frames from the 24 ms exposure period and R frames from the 24 ms readout dead time, effectively reconstructing 166 fps-equivalent content. The shutter interval (48 ms total) defines the temporal window over which interpolation and enhancement occur.

## Method

The framework is a CNN-based encoder-decoder (9.4 M parameters) with two core modules. The MM-ISTFF module fuses multi-scale RGB and event features across adjacent shutter intervals using pixel-wise dynamic filters for content-adaptive extraction and region-wise sigmoid attention to suppress noise-corrupted regions, operating bidirectionally (past and future) to produce spatio-temporally fused features. The IS-TFF module first generates per-timestep template features by dividing the event voxel grid into N equal temporal units and concatenating each with the fused features, then applies a lightweight bidirectional encoder that splits channel dimensions to compute per-timestep attention and fuses adjacent timesteps, reinforcing temporal dependencies within the shutter interval at three scales. A dual-decoder design with separate weights for exposure-time and readout-dead-time frames tailors reconstruction to the distinct physical processes of each sub-interval.

## Results

On the RELBFI dataset, the proposed method achieves 31.62 dB PSNR and 0.902 SSIM for the joint low-light enhancement, deblurring, and frame interpolation task, outperforming the best event-based joint baseline REFID by 2.13 dB and the best cascaded pipeline (ELEDNet+CBMNet) by 2.34 dB. For the joint LLE and deblurring sub-task (without interpolation), the method reaches 31.65 dB PSNR, surpassing ELEDNet by 1.17 dB. The method also achieves the fastest runtime (0.148 s per frame, 1.184 s for 8 frames) with only 9.4 M parameters, compared to 15.9 M parameters and 1.656 s for REFID.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all eight baselines on both the full joint task (LLE + deblurring + VFI) and the sub-task (LLE + deblurring). Against the strongest event-based joint baseline REFID [41], the method gains 2.13 dB PSNR (31.62 vs. 29.49) while using 40% fewer parameters (9.4 M vs. 15.9 M) and running 11% faster. Frame-based joint methods DEMFI [30] and BiT [63] trail by 7.40 dB and 5.06 dB respectively, confirming that frame-only information is insufficient for this severely degraded setting. Among cascaded pipelines, the best combination (ELEDNet [16] + CBMNet [13]) reaches 29.28 dB, still 2.34 dB below the unified approach, while the weakest cascade (LEDNet [65] + TimeLens [44]) lags by 7.20 dB. For the LLE + deblurring sub-task, the method leads ELEDNet [16] by 1.17 dB (31.65 vs. 30.48) and REFID [41] by 2.57 dB, with frame-based methods (LEDNet, DEMFI, BiT) falling 2.57–7.5 dB behind.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by defining a unified task that simultaneously addresses low-light enhancement, motion deblurring, and frame interpolation—three degradations that prior work has only tackled in pairs (e.g., deblurring+VFI in REFID/BiT, LLE+deblurring in ELEDNet/LEDNet). Conceptually, it repurposes event-camera-based fusion ideas from individual sub-tasks (cross-modal attention in EFNet, event-guided LLE in ELEDNet, event-based VFI in TimeLens/CBMNet) into a single end-to-end pipeline, and introduces the MM-ISTFF module (building on pixel-wise dynamic filters [64] and region-wise attention) and the IS-TFF module (a lightweight alternative to the heavier temporal propagation in BasicVSR++/EDVR) as the architectural backbone. The RELBFI dataset extends the lineage of event-frame hybrid datasets (HighREV, RELED) by being the first to capture real-world low-light blur, low-light events, and normal-light ground truth simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RELBFI dataset and the clearly defined three-in-one task formulation provide the community with a concrete benchmark that was previously absent, enabling fair comparison for future work on combined low-light video restoration. The large PSNR gaps between cascaded and joint approaches (2.3–4.6 dB) underscore that error accumulation in multi-degradation pipelines is severe, motivating further research into unified architectures. Limitations include reliance on a single (albeit real-world) dataset, a complex hybrid camera rig that is difficult to replicate, and a CNN-only architecture that may not scale to higher resolutions or more complex scenes. Open questions include whether the template-feature-generation strategy generalizes to arbitrary frame-rate ratios beyond the 8× setting, and whether transformer-based temporal modeling could further improve the IS-TFF module.
