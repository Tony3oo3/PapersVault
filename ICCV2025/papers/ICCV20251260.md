## Problem

Covariate shifts—subtle perturbations like blurring or corruption within the same semantic and domain context—are often conflated with domain shifts in OOD detection literature, yet they pose unique challenges that degrade model performance without obvious semantic or domain changes. Existing OOD detection methods are either too large and slow for real-time deployment or lack specific mechanisms to detect these fine-grained distributional shifts. The paper asks whether the batch statistics inherent to Batch Normalization in adversarially-trained networks can be systematically exploited to tighten the ID/OOD boundary for covariate shift detection.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 RGB image, crops it into N random 64×64 patches (N=64 in the main configuration), and processes them through the discriminator at inference. A custom PatchNorm2D layer ensures each group of patches from the same image is normalized independently, so the final anomaly score is the mean of per-patch discriminator outputs. Only the discriminator (not the VAE) is used at inference, yielding a compact 25MB model with latency an order of magnitude lower than transformer-based baselines.

## Method

DisCoPatch is an unsupervised Adversarial VAE in which the discriminator is trained on three types of patches: real, VAE-reconstructed (lacking high-frequency detail), and VAE-generated (exhibiting severe high-frequency artifacts). This dual negative-sample strategy tightens the discriminator's frequency-spectrum boundary, making it sensitive to both low- and high-frequency corruptions characteristic of covariate shifts. The key architectural insight is that Batch Normalization's reliance on current-batch statistics (momentum set to 1) creates a natural separation between ID and OOD domains, since clean and adversarial samples exhibit distinct means and variances in BN layers. A patch-based strategy ensures that at inference, all patches in a batch come from the same image, keeping batch statistics consistent with a single underlying distribution. The VAE loss combines standard ELBO (reconstruction + KL) with an adversarial term that pushes both reconstructions and generations toward realism, while the discriminator loss penalizes all non-real patches, progressively refining the ID boundary.

## Results

DisCoPatch-64 achieves state-of-the-art performance on Near-OOD detection (95.8% AUROC on SSB-hard, 94.3% on NINCO) and Covariate Shift detection (97.2% AUROC on ImageNet-1K(-C)), substantially outperforming all baselines by 6.4 and 5.9 AUROC points respectively on the hardest Near-OOD and Covariate Shift benchmarks. On Far-OOD, DisCoPatch is competitive but not SOTA, reaching 99.1% AUROC on iNaturalist (vs. 99.9% for NNGuide-RegNet) and 96.4% on DTD (vs. 98.0% for FDBD). The model is only 25MB and up to 19× faster than the largest baselines, achieving near-25% AUROC advantage over the next-smallest model (NNGuide-MobileNetV2) on Near-OOD while running an order of magnitude faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DisCoPatch-64 decisively outperforms all six baselines on the two tasks it targets: it leads SSB-hard by 6.4 AUROC over the best baseline (RankFeat, 89.4%) and ImageNet-1K(-C) by 5.9 AUROC over RankFeat (91.3%), with FPR95 gaps of 28.1 and 28.1 points respectively. On NINCO, DisCoPatch edges out NNGuide-RegNet (93.7%) by 0.6 AUROC while achieving a much lower FPR95 (39.0 vs. 28.9 — actually NNGuide is better on FPR95 here, so DisCoPatch wins on AUROC but not FPR95). On Far-OOD, DisCoPatch is outperformed by NNGuide-RegNet on iNaturalist (99.9 vs. 99.1), DTD (98.0 FDBD vs. 96.4), and OpenImage-O (97.7 vs. 94.4), though the gaps are modest (≤3.3 AUROC) and DisCoPatch is at least 10× faster and substantially smaller than those models. Compared to the lightweight NNGuide-MobileNetV2, DisCoPatch gains 31.5 AUROC on SSB-hard, 18.8 on NINCO, and 19.1 on ImageNet-1K(-C) while remaining comparable in model size. The key trade-off is that DisCoPatch sacrifices a few AUROC points on Far-OOD (where large transformer-based models like MOODv2 and NNGuide-RegNet excel) in exchange for dominant performance on covariate shift and near-OOD detection at a fraction of the latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

DisCoPatch extends the Adversarial VAE (A-VAE) framework by repurposing the discriminator not as a generation quality tool but as the primary OOD detector, trained adversarially against both VAE reconstructions and generated samples. Its conceptual foundation is the 'two-domain hypothesis' from adversarial training literature, which showed that Batch Normalization layers assign distinct statistics to clean versus adversarial inputs; DisCoPatch deliberately exploits this bias by structuring inference batches as patches from a single image so that batch statistics remain distributionally consistent. This departs from the dominant OOD detection paradigm of feature/logit-based scoring (MOODv2, NNGuide, ASH) and from likelihood-based generative methods, instead distilling boundary information into a lightweight CNN discriminator during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 25MB model can match or exceed much larger transformer-based OOD detectors while running an order of magnitude faster, making real-time OOD screening practical on resource-constrained hardware. The ~6-point AUROC gap on Covariate Shift detection (97.2 vs. 91.3) is the largest margin reported across the benchmarks, suggesting that frequency-spectrum sensitivity induced by adversarial VAE training is a genuinely underexploited signal. Open questions include whether the BN-statistics mechanism generalizes to architectures without BatchNorm2D, whether more robust generators (DDPMs, VQ-GANs) would further tighten the boundary, and whether the approach transfers to high-stakes domains like medical imaging where covariate shifts carry safety implications.
