## Problem

Existing multimodal 3D Gaussian Splatting methods represent all modalities (RGB, thermal, language) with the same set of Gaussians and a shared opacity, ignoring the inherent property disparities (e.g., language requires far higher feature dimensionality than RGB) and granularity disparities (e.g., thermal is coarser than RGB) among modalities. This causes modality conflicts where gradients from different modalities cancel each other during optimization, leading to suboptimal performance for every modality and redundant scene representations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB and thermal image pairs, text queries for open-vocabulary tasks, a sparse SfM point cloud, and camera poses. Language ground-truth features are precomputed using SAM ViT-H masks and OpenCLIP ViT-B/16 embeddings. At inference, each modality can be selectively activated or deactivated via its modality indicator, enabling flexible rendering of any subset of modalities from a single trained scene representation. The output includes novel-view RGB and thermal renderings, per-pixel semantic segmentation masks from text queries, and object localization results.

## Method

MMOne introduces two key mechanisms built on 3DGS. First, a modality modeling module assigns each modality its own feature vector and a dedicated modality indicator (α^m ∈ [0,1]) that replaces the shared opacity in alpha-blending, allowing each modality to be independently weighted and selectively deactivated (acting as a rendering 'switch' implemented by skipping that modality in the CUDA rasterizer). Second, a multimodal decomposition mechanism operates during the densification phase: when the norm of the gradient difference between two modalities on the same Gaussian exceeds a threshold (0.0002), the multi-modal Gaussian is split into separate single-modal Gaussians optimized independently. A complementary 'Soft Prune' strategy removes a specific modality from a Gaussian (by zeroing its indicator) rather than deleting the entire Gaussian, preserving information for other modalities. Together, these components disentangle the scene into shared and modality-specific Gaussian subsets, yielding a representation using fewer than one-third of the Gaussians of joint-training baselines while improving all modalities simultaneously.

## Results

On the RGBT-Scenes dataset (10 scenes), MMOne achieves an average RGB PSNR of 24.89 dB and thermal PSNR of 25.89 dB, outperforming ThermalGaussian (24.38 / 25.51 dB) and standalone 3DGS (23.27 / 24.11 dB) while using only one-third of the Gaussians. On the LERF dataset (4 scenes), MMOne reaches 24.35 dB RGB PSNR and 56.6 mIoU for open-vocabulary segmentation, surpassing both LangSplat (24.02 dB / 47.6 mIoU) and the joint-training baseline LS-J (23.23 dB / 55.3 mIoU). In the three-modality RGB-Thermal-Language setting, MMOne improves RGB PSNR by +0.87 dB and thermal PSNR by +0.86 dB over the joint-training baseline, while also achieving 48.1 mIoU vs. 45.1 mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3DGS (trained separately per modality), MMOne wins on every metric for both RGB and thermal, with the largest gains in thermal PSNR (+1.78 dB) and thermal SSIM (+0.031), confirming that joint multimodal training with modality-specific indicators is superior to independent single-modality reconstruction. Against ThermalGaussian (T-GS), which shares a single opacity across RGB and thermal, MMOne improves RGB PSNR by +0.51 dB and thermal PSNR by +0.38 dB on average, while using roughly one-third as many Gaussians; the only metric where T-GS edges out MMOne is RGB LPIPS (0.204 vs. 0.209), a negligible difference. Against LangSplat (LS*) on the RGB-Language task, MMOne achieves higher RGB PSNR (+0.33 dB) and substantially better language metrics (56.6 vs. 47.6 mIoU, 76.5% vs. 72.4% localization accuracy), showing that joint training with modality disentanglement benefits both modalities. The joint-training baseline LS-J (shared opacity) degrades RGB PSNR by 0.79 dB relative to LS* while improving mIoU, illustrating the modality conflict; MMOne avoids this trade-off, beating LS-J on both RGB PSNR (+1.12 dB) and mIoU (+1.3). In the three-modality setting, the joint baseline MM-J loses 0.56 dB RGB PSNR and 0.52 dB thermal PSNR when language is added, whereas MMOne actually gains +0.07 dB on both, demonstrating that the multimodal decomposition mechanism prevents modality conflicts from degrading existing modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

MMOne builds directly on the 3DGS framework (Kerbl et al., 2023) for its Gaussian representation, densification, and rendering pipeline, and adopts thermal rendering conventions from ThermalGaussian (Lu et al., 2025) and language feature registration from LangSplat (Qin et al., 2024). The key conceptual departure is from prior dual-modal works that share a single opacity and the same Gaussian set across all modalities; MMOne replaces the shared opacity with per-modality indicators and introduces a decomposition mechanism that splits multi-modal Gaussians into single-modal ones, explicitly addressing property and granularity disparities that prior methods ignore. This positions the work as a generalization from the dual-modal setting (RGB+thermal or RGB+language) to a scalable multi-modal (>2) framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that naive joint training of multiple modalities actively degrades existing modalities (Table 4 shows ~0.5 dB PSNR loss in RGB/thermal when language is added), and that the proposed decomposition not only prevents this degradation but enables slight mutual improvement across all three modalities. The practical efficiency gain—achieving superior quality with roughly one-third the Gaussians of baselines—makes the approach attractive for memory-constrained applications. However, the work is limited to static scenes with three modalities, does not learn camera poses, and the performance margins over strong baselines remain modest (0.4–0.5 dB), leaving open whether the decomposition strategy scales to more than three modalities or to dynamic scenes.
