## Problem

Deep neural networks trained on high-quality images suffer substantial performance degradation on low-quality inputs (fog, rain, low light) due to distribution shifts in feature space. Existing normalization layers (BN, IN, GN, LN, PONO) learn static, quality-agnostic parameters that fail to align feature distributions across quality domains, and no prior adaptive normalization method explicitly conditions on image quality as a guiding signal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252442/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Q-Norm takes an intermediate feature tensor x ∈ R^{C×H×W} from any backbone layer and a quality feature vector f_Q extracted by a frozen, pre-trained IQA model (CONTRIQUE by default, ~28M params). It outputs a normalized feature map of the same shape, with dynamically generated per-channel or per-spatial-location scale (γ) and shift (β) parameters. The module is plug-and-play: it can replace an existing normalization layer or be inserted at arbitrary positions in the network without architectural redesign.

## Method

Q-Norm generates normalization parameters via a mapping θ(f_Q) that converts quality features into γ and β, then applies standard affine normalization: x̂ = γ·(x−μ)/√(σ²+ε) + β. Two variants are provided: channel-wise Q-Norm uses two fully connected layers followed by reshape to produce per-channel parameters, while spatial-wise Q-Norm uses two convolutional layers followed by bilinear interpolation to produce spatially-varying parameters. The key insight is that conditioning on quality implicitly performs cross-domain feature alignment—migrating the HQ-to-LQ distribution gap—without requiring explicit domain-adversarial losses or paired data. The approach is biologically motivated by HVS contrast gain control and attention-based feature selection, and the quality model is kept frozen so Q-Norm adds no training overhead beyond the small parameter-generating network.

## Results

On the PASCAL VOC dataset with YOLOv11, Q-Norm (channel-wise) achieves 64.00% average mAP50 across quality levels (HQ through LQ-5), compared to 38.50% for the official BN model and 63.30% for data augmentation. On the HazyDet foggy-detection benchmark, Q-Norm (spatial-wise) improves RT-DETR's average mAP50 from 45.40% to 48.00% (+2.60) and YOLOv11 from 63.00% to 63.70% (+0.70). On CODaN classification, Q-Norm (spatial-wise) reaches 73.3% Top-1 accuracy on daytime images (vs. 68.6% for BN) and 35.6% on nighttime images (vs. 30.8% for BN). Across multi-task scene understanding on PASCAL-Context, Q-Norm (channel-spatial) achieves 76.46% mIoU on semantic segmentation, outperforming data augmentation (76.40%) and the official LN model (69.73%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Q-Norm consistently outperforms all five compared normalization methods on low-quality images. Against BN (Ioffe & Szegedy, ICML 2015), the gains are most dramatic: on VOC, Q-Norm ch lifts average mAP50 from 38.50% to 64.00% (+25.5 points), and on CODaN nighttime Top-1 from 30.8% to 35.6% (+4.8 points). Against PONO (Li et al., NeurIPS 2019), Q-Norm sp achieves 73.3% vs. 68.5% Top-1 on CODaN daytime and 63.70% vs. 62.90% mAP50 on HazyDet (YOLOv11). Against IN (Ulyanov et al., 2016), Q-Norm sp edges ahead on HazyDet (63.70% vs. 63.60% mAP50) and CODaN daytime (73.3% vs. 67.3% Top-1). Against GN (Wu & He, ECCV 2018), Q-Norm sp leads by 6.5 points on CODaN daytime Top-1 (73.3% vs. 66.8%). Against LN (Ba et al., 2016), Q-Norm sp achieves 63.70% vs. 57.80% mAP50 on HazyDet with YOLOv11. Notably, Q-Norm also slightly improves HQ performance (e.g., 76.70% vs. 75.70% mAP50 on VOC), confirming it does not sacrifice high-quality accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

Q-Norm sits at the intersection of two well-established lines of work: adaptive normalization (AdaIN, SPADE, Dynamic Instance Normalization, GRANOLA) and blind image quality assessment (CONTRIQUE, BRISQUE, MUSIQ). While prior adaptive normalization methods condition their γ/β parameters on style, semantics, depth, or raw input content, none use image quality as the conditioning signal. The paper repurposes a frozen, pre-trained IQA model as a lightweight quality encoder whose features drive a small mapping network (linear or convolutional layers) to produce per-sample normalization parameters, drawing a biological analogy to HVS contrast gain control. Conceptually, it is closest to AdaBN/SPADE in spirit (external signal modulates normalization) but departs by making that signal a quality descriptor rather than a semantic or stylistic one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design (a few linear/conv layers plus a frozen IQA backbone) makes Q-Norm easy to adopt in existing pipelines, and the consistent gains across classification, detection, segmentation, and translation tasks suggest broad applicability. However, the improvements are modest in many settings (e.g., +0.7 mAP50 for YOLOv11 on HazyDet, and channel-wise Q-Norm actually underperforms BN on CoDaN Night), and the method inherits the domain bias of whichever IQA model is chosen. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video, to degradation types outside the tested blur/fog/low-light spectrum, and whether end-to-end joint training of the IQA encoder with the downstream task could yield larger gains than the current frozen-encoder setup.
