## Problem

Foreground-conditioned inpainting requires generating a text-aligned background while strictly preserving the shape of a given foreground subject. Existing T2I-based inpainting methods suffer from subject shape expansion, distortion, or degraded text-image alignment because conventional condition injection via cross-attention creates conflicts between text and subject features, and standard feature extractors (CLIP, VAE) cannot capture fine contour details needed for strict shape preservation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250043/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a subject image (RGB with background removed), a binary mask, a text prompt describing the desired background, a depth map, and a Sobel edge image as conditioning signals, and produces a 1024×1024 inpainted image. A two-stage compositing step overlays the original subject onto the generated image within the unmasked region to guarantee pixel-level foreground fidelity. Training uses 88K real-world photographs with dynamically cropped aspect ratios (1:1, 16:9, 9:16), and evaluation spans 1,800 generation tasks (300 items × 3 prompts × 2 seeds).

## Method

Pinco is a plug-and-play adapter (≈11–13% trainable parameters) that integrates three modules into a frozen DiT backbone. (1) A Self-Consistent Adapter computes subject-aware cross-attention between latent queries and subject keys/values, then adds this to the base model's self-attention output (rather than the text cross-attention), with Zero-init Tanh Gating for progressive injection; for MM-DiT backbones like FLUX.1, it instead concatenates subject tokens into the MM-Attention key/value sequence. (2) A Decoupled Image Feature Extractor separately encodes semantic features via the pre-trained VAE encoder and shape features via a convolutional network over the mask, depth, and Sobel channels, then fuses them with an MLP. (3) A Shared Positional Embedding Anchor reuses the base model's RoPE and adds it to subject features before key projection, causing attention weights to decay with distance from the subject region and concentrating subject-aware attention on the contour area. This combination mitigates text-subject conflicts, prevents shape expansion, and accelerates convergence compared to cross-attention injection.

## Results

On the DiT backbones, HY-Pinco achieves OER(SAM2.1) of 11.51% and OER(BiRefNet) of 10.00%, substantially outperforming HY-ControlNet (11.78% / 11.95%) while using only 11.37% trainable parameters versus 31.20%. Flux-Pinco further reduces OER(SAM2.1) to 7.87% and OER(BiRefNet) to 6.84%, compared to Flux ControlNet's 28.11% / 22.30%, with a VQAScore of 0.918 and FV2Score of 2.99%. Across all 12 compared methods, Pinco achieves the best or near-best scores on foreground consistency (OER), text alignment (VQAScore, FV2Score), and composition rationality (GPT-4o score of 4.790 for HY-Pinco), while maintaining the lowest trainable parameter ratio among DiT-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the same-backbone ControlNet variants, Pinco delivers dramatic gains in foreground consistency: Flux-Pinco reduces OER(SAM2.1) from 28.11% to 7.87% (a 3.6× improvement) and OER(BiRefNet) from 22.30% to 6.84%, while HY-Pinco improves OER(BiRefNet) from 11.95% to 10.00% with 64% fewer trainable parameters (11.37% vs 31.20%). On text alignment, HY-Pinco achieves VQAScore 0.901 vs HY-ControlNet's 0.877 and FV2Score 3.16% vs 5.00%, confirming that the Self-Consistent Adapter's self-attention injection better preserves prompt fidelity. Compared to the strongest UNet-based baselines (Kolors Inpainting, BrushNet-SDXL), HY-Pinco leads on OER(BiRefNet) (10.00% vs 14.40% and 18.91%), VQAScore (0.901 vs 0.891 and 0.870), and GPT-4o rationality (4.790 vs 4.418 and 4.621). Flux-Pinco achieves the best overall scores across nearly all metrics in the table, with the lowest OER values (7.87% / 6.84%), highest VQAScore (0.918), lowest FV2Score (2.99%), and highest GPT-4o score (4.735). The user study (31 participants, 40 image pairs each) further confirms that Pinco is preferred over BrushNet, Kolors-IP, Flux-IP, and SDXL-IP across foreground consistency, text alignment, and overall quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

Pinco builds on the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, 2023) and integrates with specific DiT backbones—Hunyuan-DiT and FLUX.1—while drawing on the IP-Adapter's decoupled cross-attention mechanism for image-conditioned generation. Its key departure from prior conditional-injection methods (ControlNet, BrushNet) is relocating subject-aware attention from the cross-attention or side-branch pathway into the self-attention layer, motivated by the observation that self-attention is more closely aligned with spatial layout. The decoupled feature extraction (VAE for semantics, convolution for shape) extends beyond the CLIP-or-VAE-only extractors used in prior controllable T2I models, and the Shared Positional Embedding Anchor repurposes the base model's RoPE to spatially constrain subject attention—a technique not previously applied in this inpainting context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Pinco offers a lightweight (≈11–12% trainable parameters) plug-and-play adapter that works across two major DiT architectures, making it a practical option for practitioners who want foreground-conditioned inpainting without the parameter overhead of ControlNet-style side branches or full model fine-tuning. The self-attention injection insight—that subject features are better integrated where spatial layout is already encoded—could inform future adapter designs beyond inpainting. However, the method still relies on a two-stage compositing step for perfect foreground preservation, and evaluation is limited to 300 everyday items with GPT-4o-generated prompts, leaving open questions about robustness on complex multi-subject scenes, video inpainting, or very large foreground regions.
