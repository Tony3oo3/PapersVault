## Problem

Source-free object detection (SFOD) adapts a pre-trained detector to an unlabeled target domain without source data, but operates under a closed-set assumption that only pre-defined source classes exist in the target. This prevents the detector from recognizing novel, undefined objects (e.g., pedestrians or animals in autonomous driving), which is critical for real-world safety. The paper introduces SFUOD, a new scenario requiring simultaneous adaptation to known objects and detection of unknown objects, and identifies two core challenges: knowledge confusion from source-dependent weights and ineffective pseudo-labeling for unknown objects.

## Input / Output

The method takes unlabeled RGB images from the target domain (e.g., Foggy Cityscapes or BDD100K) as input, with no access to source data during adaptation. The pre-trained Deformable-DETR detector (ResNet-50 backbone) outputs bounding boxes with class labels: either one of the predefined known vehicle classes (Car, Truck, Bus) or a single 'unknown' category (class K+1) for any novel object. During training, pseudo-labels are generated by a teacher model and refined by the proposed PAUL module to distinguish true unknown objects from background proposals.

## Method

CollaPAUL builds on the Mean Teacher framework (with DRU as base) and introduces two components. First, collaborative tuning employs an auxiliary target encoder that applies truncated SVD reconstruction to extract target-dependent features, then fuses them with source-dependent features from the student's DETR encoder via a cross-domain attention mechanism using identical object queries; collaborative layers are inserted between decoder layers (L=3) to propagate integrated knowledge. Second, PAUL (Principal Axis-based Unknown Labeling) computes principal axes from known proposal features, projects remaining proposals onto these axes, and scores objectness via cosine similarity against known projections; an objectness mask (threshold δ) is combined with a confidence mask (threshold ε) to select reliable unknown proposals for pseudo-label assignment. This dual mechanism mitigates knowledge confusion for known objects while enabling effective detection of unknown objects from background.

## Results

On the weather adaptation benchmark (Cityscapes → Foggy Cityscapes), CollaPAUL achieves 32.32% Known mAP, 10.59% U-Recall, and 15.95% H-Score, outperforming the best SFOD baseline (SF-UT) by 6.14% in Known mAP and the best MT-based baseline (Mean Teacher) by 7.07% in H-Score. On the cross-scene adaptation benchmark (Cityscapes → BDD100K), CollaPAUL reaches 28.21% Known mAP, 8.57% U-Recall, and 13.15% H-Score, surpassing the strongest baselines by 1.53%, 1.71%, and 3.26% respectively. Notably, all prior SFOD methods either fail to detect unknown objects (U-Recall = 0.00 for SF-UT) or suffer severe knowledge confusion that degrades known-object performance (e.g., Mean Teacher drops Truck mAP to 0.00).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CollaPAUL outperforms all four SFOD baselines on every metric in both benchmarks. Against Mean Teacher [28], the most direct MT-based comparison, CollaPAUL gains +15.41% Known mAP and +7.07% H-Score on weather adaptation, while Mean Teacher catastrophically fails on Truck (0.00 mAP) due to knowledge confusion. Against DRU [14] (the base model), CollaPAUL improves Known mAP by +9.35% and U-Recall by +6.99%, showing that collaborative tuning and PAUL add substantial value beyond the base framework. Against SF-UT [18], which preserves source knowledge but detects zero unknown objects, CollaPAUL matches or exceeds known-object performance while achieving 10.59% U-Recall. Against PET [23], the gap is largest in H-Score (+11.42%), reflecting PET's severe degradation in both known and unknown detection. In the unknown-labeling ablation (Table 7), PAUL also outperforms the attention-driven labeling from OW-DETR [8] by +6.59% U-Recall and +8.83% H-Score, confirming that principal-axis-based objectness estimation is more effective than attention-based selection for identifying unknown proposals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of source-free object detection (SFOD) and open-set recognition. SFOD methods (SED, PET, SF-UT, DRU) adapt a source-trained detector to an unlabeled target domain but assume a closed-set class space, while open recognition methods (OW-DETR, SOMA/AOOD) handle unknown objects but require access to source data. CollaPAUL departs from both lines by introducing the SFUOD scenario—source-free adaptation with unknown object detection—and builds on DRU's dynamic retraining-updating mean teacher as its base pipeline. The two proposed mechanisms, collaborative tuning (SVD-based target encoder + cross-domain attention) and PAUL (principal-axis projection for objectness estimation), are novel contributions not present in either prior line of work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SFUOD addresses a practically important gap: real-world detectors (e.g., in autonomous driving) must handle unforeseen objects without retraining on source data, yet prior SFOD methods are architecturally incapable of this. The paper's diagnosis of 'knowledge confusion'—where source-dependent representations actively hinder unknown-object learning—provides a useful conceptual framework for future open-set domain adaptation work. However, absolute performance remains low (32.32 known mAP, 10.59 U-Recall), the evaluation is limited to two benchmarks with a narrow known-class set (vehicles only), and scalability to richer class taxonomies or more diverse unknown categories remains an open question.
