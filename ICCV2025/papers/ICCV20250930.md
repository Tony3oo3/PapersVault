## Problem

Existing AI-generated image detectors target GAN high-frequency artifacts or diffusion iterative noise patterns, but autoregressive (AR) models generate images through discrete token prediction, producing distinctive artifacts in the vector-quantized latent space that are invisible to conventional pixel-level analysis. No comprehensive benchmark or detection method specifically addressing AR-generated images existed prior to this work.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250930/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 RGB image, which is processed by a frozen VQVAE encoder (16× downsampling, codebook size 16,384) to produce a discrete token map and a quantization error feature, while a frozen CLIP-ViT encoder processes a 224×224 crop for global semantic features. During training, the system dynamically accumulates codebook frequency statistics separately for real and fake batches, which are then used to compute a distribution discrepancy vector. The output is a binary real/fake classification score produced by an MLP over concatenated aligned features.

## Method

D³QE exploits the fact that AR models' finite codebooks cannot fully capture the long-tail distribution of natural images, causing generated samples to exhibit concentrated probability mass in high-frequency codebook entries. The method computes the quantization error (z_q − z) as a local feature and tracks per-codebook-entry frequency statistics for real and fake images during training, yielding a normalized discrepancy vector ΔD. A Discrete Distribution Discrepancy-Aware Transformer (D³AT) incorporates ΔD into self-attention by adding learned projection terms Q_D·K_D^T/α to the standard attention scores, enabling the model to modulate token interactions based on distributional bias. The D³AT output is average-pooled and concatenated with CLIP semantic features (both projected through alignment MLPs) before a final classification MLP. The VQVAE encoder, codebook, and CLIP backbone remain frozen; only the D³AT, alignment modules, classifier, and codebook statistics are trained.

## Results

On the ARForensics benchmark (7 autoregressive models), D³QE achieves a mean detection accuracy of 82.11% and mean average precision of 92.07%, outperforming the strongest baseline UnivFD (79.56% Acc, 90.44% AP) by 2.55% and 1.63% respectively. On the cross-paradigm GAN test set (ForenSynths), D³QE reaches 83.73% Acc and 92.23% AP, surpassing UnivFD (80.70%, 86.12%). On the diffusion test set (GenImage), D³QE attains 78.61% Acc and 89.60% AP, marginally edging out UnivFD (78.42%, 89.58%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

D³QE outperforms all six baselines on mean accuracy across the 7 AR models in ARForensics. The closest competitor is UnivFD (79.56% Acc, 90.44% AP), which D³QE surpasses by 2.55% in accuracy and 1.63% in AP; NPR is the second-best on AP (91.43%) but trails D³QE by 0.64%. Traditional frequency-domain and CNN-based detectors (CNNSpot, FreDect, Gram-Net, LNP) all score near or below 67% mean accuracy, with several dropping to ~50% (chance level) on architecturally distinct models like VAR and RAR, indicating poor cross-architecture generalization. Notably, D³QE achieves 85.33% Acc on VAR (scale-based AR) and 91.69% on RAR (random-scan-order), where most baselines fall to 50–57%, demonstrating that the discrete distribution discrepancy mechanism captures architecture-agnostic statistical signatures. On the cross-paradigm GAN test set, D³QE leads all baselines by a wide margin (83.73% vs. 80.70% for UnivFD), while on diffusion models it matches UnivFD almost exactly (78.61% vs. 78.42% Acc), confirming that the codebook-distribution signal transfers to non-AR generators without degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of AI-generated image detection that targets pixel-level or frequency-domain artifacts (CNNSpot, FreDect, NPR for GANs; UnivFD, DIRE for diffusion models) by shifting the detection signal to the discrete latent space inherent to autoregressive models. It repurposes the VQVAE tokenizer (from LlamaGen) not as a generative component but as a forensic probe, exploiting the quantization residual and codebook frequency statistics as discriminative features. The D3AT module is a novel architectural contribution that injects dynamic codebook distribution discrepancy into self-attention, a mechanism absent from prior detection transformers. The paper also introduces ARForensics, the first benchmark purpose-built for AR-generated image detection, filling a gap the authors identify in existing datasets like ForenSynths and GenImage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ARForensics benchmark and the codebook-distribution detection paradigm provide the field with its first systematic tool for evaluating and detecting AR-generated content, a rapidly growing generative family that prior detectors handle poorly (often near chance-level on VAR, RAR, Switti). The cross-paradigm generalization to GANs and diffusion models, while not state-of-the-art there, suggests that discrete distribution concentration is a more universal forensic signal than previously recognized. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video AR models, how it performs against future AR architectures with larger or adaptive codebooks, and whether the moderate accuracy drops on certain AR models (Infinity at 62.9%, Open-MAGVIT2 at 70.1%) reflect fundamental limitations of the fixed-codebook assumption or are addressable with model-specific adaptation.
