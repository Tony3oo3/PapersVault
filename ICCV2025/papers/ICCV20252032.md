## Problem

Existing dynamic scene reconstruction methods face a fundamental trade-off: approaches that scale to large environments (hundreds of meters to kilometers) cannot model complex dynamics, while methods that capture non-rigid motion (e.g., pedestrians) are restricted to small-scale or fragmented scenes. No prior work simultaneously achieves high-fidelity reconstruction of large-scale dynamic street scenes with arbitrary rigid and non-rigid elements while maintaining real-time rendering speeds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252032/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes long sequences of calibrated RGB images (thousands of frames at 10 Hz covering 600 m–1+ km scenes), sparse LiDAR point clouds for initialization and depth supervision, and per-object motion priors (rotation and translation) extracted by off-the-shelf trackers (SAM 2, SAMURAI). The output is a BVH-organized hierarchical UGP tree where each leaf primitive is a 4D Gaussian with element-specific properties, enabling real-time rendering at 21–102 FPS depending on scene scale and LOD threshold, with sub-scenes merged at 50% spatial overlap to avoid boundary artifacts.

## Method

Hierarchy UGP organizes the scene into a three-level tree (Root → Sub-scenes → Primitives) where each primitive is a 4D Unified Gaussian Primitive parameterized by a 4D mean, 4D covariance (decomposed via two isotropic quaternions), opacity, and spherical harmonics. The key design insight is element-specific UGP properties: sky primitives optimize only opacity and SH, background/rigid objects have infinite temporal scale and zero temporal mean, while non-rigid objects use a frame-rate-derived temporal scale initialization (Eq. 5) to concentrate temporal influence around each frame. A BVH tree with iterative diameter-based LOD selection enables real-time rendering, and a Block-wise Objects training strategy prevents cross-sub-scene interference for objects spanning multiple partitions. Training uses a composite loss combining L1+SSIM photometric, LiDAR-projected depth, and virtual warping view supervision, with sub-scenes trained in parallel and merged at the root level.

## Results

On the Dynamic City dataset, Hierarchy UGP achieves PSNR 27.01 / SSIM 0.862 / LPIPS 0.180 on Large 001 and PSNR 25.25 / SSIM 0.844 / LPIPS 0.198 on Large 002, outperforming all baselines while maintaining real-time rendering (32 and 21 FPS respectively). On sub-scenes, it reaches PSNR 27.81 (Sub-scene 001) and 27.61 (Sub-scene 002) with up to 102 FPS. On Waymo, the method achieves PSNR 30.20 (scene 014) and 34.10 (scene 023) for reconstruction, with pedestrian-region PSNR of 36.10 and 37.16, substantially exceeding Street GS (34.01 / 32.14) and OmniRe (32.93 / 36.40).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hierarchy UGP outperforms all four baselines across every metric on both large-scale and sub-scene settings of Dynamic City. Against the closest competitor, Hierarchical GS [15], it gains +0.27 dB PSNR on Large 001 and +0.11 dB on Large 002, while also achieving higher FPS (32 vs. 28, 21 vs. 15). Street GS [40] and OmniRe [4] trail by 1.4–2.5 dB PSNR on large scenes, with OmniRe showing the largest LPIPS gap (0.336 vs. 0.180 on Large 001). On Waymo, Hierarchy UGP leads Street GS by +6.87 dB PSNR on scene 014 reconstruction and by +2.33 dB on pedestrian-region PSNR (36.10 vs. 33.77 implied), while OmniRe is competitive on scene 023 reconstruction (36.40 vs. 37.16 pedestrian PSNR) but falls behind on novel-view synthesis. The method also achieves the best FID scores in extrapolation (68.27 and 63.36 on Large 001/002), beating the next-best Street GS (90.99 and 79.67) by a wide margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of 3D Gaussian work: hierarchical large-scale scene representations (Kerbl et al. 2024, Liu et al. 2025, Ren et al. 2024) that handle millions of static Gaussians via BVH trees and LOD, and 4D dynamic Gaussian splatting (Yang et al. 2024, Duan et al. 2024) that models temporal deformation through a 4D covariance parameterized by two quaternions. Prior dynamic-scene methods (Street Gaussians, OmniRe, PVG) were limited to small or fragmented scenes, while large-scale methods could not model arbitrary dynamics. Hierarchy UGP departs from both by embedding 4D Unified Gaussian Primitives within a three-level hierarchical tree (root → sub-scenes → primitives), enabling a single framework to handle tens of millions of primitives with mixed rigid and non-rigid motion at real-time rates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to jointly address large-scale scene reconstruction, arbitrary dynamic elements (rigid vehicles and non-rigid pedestrians), and real-time rendering in a unified framework, which is critical for autonomous driving simulation and VR applications. The planned release of the Dynamic City dataset (600 m–1 km street sequences at 10 Hz) fills a notable gap, as no public dataset currently covers large-scale dynamic street scenes. Open questions remain: the method still depends on off-the-shelf trackers for motion priors, performance degrades when pedestrian motion is entirely unobserved (as noted on Waymo test frames), and scalability beyond ~1 km scenes or to more complex multi-agent interactions is untested.
