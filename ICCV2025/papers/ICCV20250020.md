## Problem

Existing multimodal large language models handle mathematical problems in 'image-text-separation-form,' where the textual statement and math figures are fed separately. In real-world scenarios, however, problem statements and figures are captured together in a single image ('vision-form'), and no prior model has been designed to solve such problems end-to-end, leading to significant accuracy drops (e.g., GPT-4o mini drops from 58.1% to 53.4% on geometry when switching formats).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250020/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image containing both the rendered textual problem statement (including LaTeX-style formulas) and accompanying math figures (geometry diagrams, function graphs) as input, processed through InternViT-300M-448px with up to 9 image tiles and a 4096-token sequence limit. The output is a structured chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a final answer in the format '{CoT process}. Therefore, the answer is \boxed{final answer}', supporting both English and Chinese problem types spanning geometry, algebra, and functions.

## Method

VisionMath builds on a Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct LLM with an InternViT-300M-448px visual encoder and a two-layer MLP projector, trained via a three-stage progressive multimodal reasoning alignment strategy. Stage 1 establishes mathematical OCR by training on document OCR datasets plus rendered math problem images, with 25% text-form Numbia data interleaved to prevent catastrophic forgetting. Stage 2 enhances figure comprehension through a dual-path approach: figure captioning (Geo170k, MAVIS) for structural parsing, and mathematical educational video frames (Multimodal-Textbook) for property-level understanding of real-world figures. Stage 3 performs end-to-end instruction tuning on the newly constructed VisionMath-IT dataset (1.96M English + 0.62M Chinese vision-form problems with CoT annotations), unifying solution formats to focus learning on reasoning rather than style. The key design insight is the hierarchical progression from perception (OCR) → understanding (figure structure and properties) → integrated reasoning, with each stage building on the previous one's capabilities.

## Results

On the Chinese VisionMath-Bench, VisionMath (7B) achieves 49.0% on plane geometry, 43.3% on analytic geometry, 44.7% on solid geometry, 56.9% on algebra, and 44.4% on function problems, substantially outperforming the best open-source general-purpose model Qwen2-VL (33.0/18.9/35.0/38.9/34.8%) and the best math-specialized model MultiMath (5.0/6.7/10.4/8.3/12.6%). On the English Vision-MathVista benchmark, VisionMath scores 70.3% on Geometry Problem Solving and 69.4% on Math Word Problems, surpassing GPT-4o (59.6%/67.2%). On Vision-MathVerse, VisionMath achieves 58.0% on Text-Diagram, 48.0% on Text-Language, 45.3% on Visual-Image, 44.2% on Visual-Diagram, and 39.6% on Visual-Other, trailing Gemini-1.5-pro (61.7/56.2/52.8/52.2/53.3%) but leading GPT-4o on most sub-tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VisionMath decisively outperforms all 13 open-source baselines across every benchmark and problem type. On VisionMath-Bench, it leads the strongest open-source general-purpose model Qwen2-VL by 16.0% (plane geometry), 24.4% (analytic geometry), and 9.7% (solid geometry), and exceeds the best math-specialized model MultiMath by 44.0%, 36.6%, and 34.3% on the same geometry sub-tasks. On Vision-MathVista, VisionMath (70.3% GPS, 69.4% MWP) surpasses GPT-4o (59.6%, 67.2%) by 10.7 and 2.2 points respectively, and also beats the OCR-tool-augmented pipelines (OCR + InternVL2.5: 48.1/54.9; OCR + URSA: 34.6/42.5). On Vision-MathVerse, VisionMath outperforms GPT-4o on four of five sub-tasks (TD 58.0 vs 53.9, TL 48.0 vs 47.1, VI 45.3 vs 45.1, VD 44.2 vs 42.1) but trails on VO (39.6 vs 42.4), and overall remains behind Gemini-1.5-pro (e.g., TD 58.0 vs 61.7, VO 39.6 vs 53.3). The paper also notes that math-specialized models like URSA and MultiMath suffer catastrophic degradation on vision-form inputs compared to their image-text-separation-form performance, indicating overfitting to separated figure-text formats.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LLaVA-style architecture (visual encoder + MLP projector + LLM) with Qwen2.5-Math as the base language model and InternViT as the visual encoder, but departs from prior math-specialized MLLMs (G-LLaVA, MathLLaVA, URSA, MultiMath, Math-PUMA) that exclusively target image-text-separation-form problems where text and figures are input separately. The key conceptual departure is reframing the task around 'vision-form' problems—where the problem statement and figures are integrated into a single image—requiring a progressive training curriculum (OCR → figure comprehension → integrated reasoning) rather than the direct instruction tuning used by prior math MLLMs. The use of mathematical educational videos for figure property understanding is a novel data source not explored in prior math-specialized MLLM work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real-world math problems (textbooks, exams, homework) are almost always in vision-form, yet all prior MLLMs were trained and evaluated on separated inputs. The finding that math-specialized models catastrophically underperform general-purpose models on vision-form problems (e.g., URSA at 2.0% vs. Qwen2-VL at 33.0% on plane geometry) reveals a dangerous overfitting risk in domain-specific MLLM training. However, absolute performance remains modest (49% on plane geometry, 43% on analytic geometry), and the approach is limited to K-12 level mathematics; whether the progressive strategy generalizes to university-level or research-level math, and whether the 7B scale is sufficient for harder problems, remain open questions.
