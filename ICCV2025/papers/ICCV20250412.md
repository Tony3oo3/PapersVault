## Problem

While image diffusion models have been shown to learn useful representations for downstream perception tasks, the representational power of video diffusion models remains largely unexplored, and no prior work has directly compared video versus image diffusion pre-training objectives using the same architecture. This leaves open the question of how much temporal information in the generation objective contributes to learned visual representations for tasks spanning semantics, motion, and 3D geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250412/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The frozen WALT backbone consumes 17-frame video clips or single images, first encoded by a MAGVIT-v2 3D CNN tokenizer into a shared latent space (5 temporal latents, 8× spatial compression). A single forward pass through the transformer at a chosen noise level (t=0–200 optimal) with a null text embedding yields intermediate block activations (blocks 11–16 optimal out of 24), from which lightweight cross-attention readout heads produce task-specific outputs: class labels, per-pixel depth maps, 6D relative camera pose matrices, normalized point/box coordinates with visibility and certainty scores.

## Method

The paper trains two instances of the identical WALT windowed-attention latent transformer: V-WALT, which uses spatio-temporal windowed attention to model motion across 17 frames, and I-WALT, which replaces those blocks with spatial-only windowed attention of matched parameter count, trained on randomly sampled frames from the same dataset. Both models serve as frozen backbones in a probing framework where a single denoising forward pass (not the full iterative sampling loop) extracts intermediate features, and task-specific readout heads (attentive cross-attention + MLP for classification/pose, Scene Representation Transformer decoder for depth, MooG-style recurrent predictor for tracking) are trained on top. The study ablates noise level, extraction block depth, model scale (284 M vs. 1.9 B), and training budget (checkpoints at 20%–100% of pre-training), revealing that video pre-training yields consistent gains across all tasks with the largest improvements on motion-sensitive tasks (PointTracks +68%, Cam. Pose +60%, SSv2 +42%) while semantic classification sees only marginal gains.

## Results

V-WALT (video diffusion pre-training) consistently outperforms I-WALT (image diffusion pre-training) across all 9 downstream tasks, with relative improvements ranging from +0.6% on Places365 to +68% on PointTracks. The largest gains appear on motion-sensitive tasks: Cam. Pose (+60%), SSv2 (+42%), and Obj. Tracks (+23%), while purely semantic tasks show modest gains (ImageNet +1.8%, Places365 +0.6%). Scaling V-WALT from 284M to 1.9B parameters further improves most tasks, with the most notable boosts on classification tasks with many classes (K700, Inat2018). On the frozen-readout comparison with standard SSL models (Figure 6), V-WALT is competitive with video models on depth and motion tasks but is dominated by SigLIP and DINOv2 on semantic classification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is V-WALT vs. I-WALT (same architecture, different pre-training objective), where V-WALT wins on all 9 tasks with the margin scaling with motion sensitivity: negligible on semantic tasks (Places365 +0.6%, ImageNet +1.8%) but large on spatio-temporal tasks (PointTracks +68%, Cam. Pose +60%, SSv2 +42%). Against standard SSL baselines in Figure 6, V-WALT (284M) is competitive with VideoMAE and V-JEPA on depth estimation and motion understanding tasks, but is clearly dominated by SigLIP and DINOv2 on semantic image classification (ImageNet, Places365, Inat2018), revealing a core weakness of generative diffusion representations in the frozen-readout setting. Scaling V-WALT to 1.9B narrows the gap on classification tasks (especially K700 and Inat2018) but does not close it against SigLIP/DINOv2, and slightly hurts PointTracks. MAE and JEPA (image-only models) perform below I-WALT on video tasks, confirming that video pre-training is essential for spatio-temporal understanding. The concurrent work [3] (SVD vs. SD) also finds video diffusion outperforms image diffusion, but the architecture size disparity (865M vs. 1.5B) weakens that comparison, whereas this paper's controlled WALT setup isolates the temporal attention contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the growing line of work on extracting representations from diffusion models for visual understanding (DDPM-Seg, DIFT, DINOv2+diffusion) by performing the first direct comparison of the same architecture trained under video versus image generation objectives. It builds on the WALT hybrid transformer with windowed attention, whose design naturally supports both image and video modes, enabling a controlled ablation of the temporal objective that prior comparisons (e.g., SVD vs. SD) could not achieve due to architecture size disparities. The probing framework follows Carreira et al.'s 4D representation evaluation protocol, and the comparison with self-supervised baselines (DINOv2, SigLIP, MAE, JEPA, VideoMAE, V-JEPA) situates diffusion features within the broader representation learning landscape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The striking range of video-over-image improvement (from +0.6% on Places365 to +68% on PointTracks) provides a quantitative map of where temporal information in diffusion pre-training adds value, which is directly actionable for practitioners choosing pre-training objectives. However, the study is limited to a single architecture (WALT) at one scale (284M), and the finding that V-WALT is dominated by SigLIP and DINOv2 on semantic tasks reveals that generative diffusion features remain weaker than contrastive/alignment features for pure semantics. Open questions include whether the video-over-image gap persists across other unified architectures as they emerge, and whether the observed trade-off between semantic and spatio-temporal performance can be addressed through architectural or training modifications.
