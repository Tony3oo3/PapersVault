## Problem

Existing 3D visual grounding methods struggle with two compounding issues: (1) complex multi-anchor queries entangle target-anchor spatial relationships, making it difficult for models to correctly identify which object is the target versus a reference anchor, and (2) spatial descriptions (e.g., 'to the right of') become inconsistent under viewpoint changes, as the same relative position flips when observed from the opposite direction. Prior multi-view approaches address only isolated aspects of these problems without a unified mechanism for both query simplification and perspective-aware feature alignment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3D point cloud scene in XYZ+RGB format rendered from N=4 simulated viewpoints (rotated around the scene's central axis) along with a natural language referring expression. The LLM-based SRD preprocessing step runs at inference time to decompose the query into single-anchor sub-sentences before encoding. The output is a 3D bounding box (center + dimensions) localizing the target object, evaluated via IoU thresholds (Acc@0.25, Acc@0.5) on ScanRefer or exact box matching on Nr3D/Sr3D.

## Method

ViewSRD formulates 3DVG as a structured multi-view decomposition with three stages. First, the Simple Relation Decoupling (SRD) module uses a pre-trained classifier to identify target and anchor words, then an LLM (DeepSeek-R1) with predefined prompt templates to generate k single-anchor candidate sentences per anchor, from which a sentence-matching algorithm (weighted label + semantic consistency) selects the best simplification. Second, the Multi-view Textual-Scene Interaction (Multi-TSI) module introduces Cross-modal Consistent View Tokens (CCVTs)—learnable, shared tokens of dimension D that are jointly optimized with both the BERT text encoder and PointNet++ scene encoder; the text module applies cross-attention where view tokens are reweighted by their cosine similarity to each sentence's [CLS] token, while the scene module concatenates CCVTs with per-view object features and processes them through L Transformer layers, retaining only object tokens at the end. Third, a Textual-Scene Reasoning module performs cross-attention (scene features as query, text features as key/value) followed by view aggregation (mean + max pooling across N views) and a prediction head. Training uses a combined loss of parallel referential loss (localizing both target and anchors simultaneously), object-level loss, and sentence-level loss.

## Results

On Nr3D, ViewSRD achieves 69.9% overall grounding accuracy, surpassing the best prior methods MiKASA and CoT3DRef (both 64.4%) by 5.5 percentage points. The advantage is most pronounced under view-dependent evaluation, where ViewSRD reaches 68.6% versus 61.9% for CoT3DRef (+6.7%). On Sr3D, ViewSRD attains 76.0% overall accuracy, outperforming MiKASA (75.2%) and CoT3DRef (73.2%). On ScanRefer, ViewSRD scores 45.4% Acc@0.25 and 36.0% Acc@0.5, while the SRD module alone boosts BUTD-DETR by 14.9% and EDA by 9.3% in Acc@0.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViewSRD outperforms all ten baselines on both Nr3D and Sr3D. Against the two strongest competitors, MiKASA and CoT3DRef (both 64.4% on Nr3D), ViewSRD gains 5.5% overall and 6.7% on the view-dependent subset, indicating that the CCVT mechanism specifically addresses perspective-induced inconsistencies. On Sr3D, the margin over MiKASA narrows to 0.8% overall but ViewSRD still leads on the Hard split (+3.3%) and View-Independent split (+0.8%). Against earlier multi-view methods ViewRefer (56.0%) and MVT (55.1%), ViewSRD achieves gains of 13.9% and 14.8% respectively on Nr3D, reflecting the combined benefit of query decomposition and cross-modal view tokens. Notably, ViewSRD is the only method where multi-anchor queries (71.5%) outperform single-anchor ones (69.5%), whereas MVT and CoT3DRef both degrade on multi-anchor inputs, confirming that the SRD module effectively resolves the entanglement that hampers prior approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViewSRD extends the multi-view 3D visual grounding line initiated by MVT (CVPR 2022) and ViewRefer (ICCV 2023), which introduced multi-perspective scene encoding but lacked explicit mechanisms for handling viewpoint-dependent language. It departs from prior multi-view methods (MVT, MiKASA, ViewRefer) by introducing two orthogonal innovations: an LLM-based query decomposition step (SRD) that restructures multi-anchor sentences into single-anchor statements before encoding, and shared Cross-modal Consistent View Tokens (CCVTs) that inject viewpoint identity into both the textual and scene branches. The loss formulation follows MVT and LanguageRefer, while the parallel referential loss for simultaneous target-and-anchor localization is adapted from CoT3DRef (ICLR 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The model-agnostic SRD module is practically valuable: it can be plugged into existing 3DVG pipelines (demonstrated on MVT, BUTD-DETR, EDA) without architectural changes, yielding up to 18% gains on ScanRefer. The counterintuitive finding that properly decomposed multi-anchor queries outperform single-anchor ones (71.5% vs. 69.5%) suggests that the field has been treating multi-anchor complexity as purely a source of noise rather than a source of additional grounding signal. A key limitation is the assumption that queries decompose cleanly without overlapping relational dependencies, and the authors acknowledge that adaptive decomposition preserving contextual interdependencies remains open. Future work on adaptive view selection (beyond the fixed N=4) and more nuanced LLM-based query restructuring could further close the gap on view-dependent scenarios.
