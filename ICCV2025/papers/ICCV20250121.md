## Problem

Existing evaluations of multimodal reasoning in MLLMs either reduce assessment to isolated abilities (visual grounding, VQA) in constrained settings, or focus solely on final task completion in open environments without analyzing the intermediate reasoning process. This leaves a critical gap: we lack a comprehensive, quantitative understanding of how MLLMs actually coordinate multiple multimodal abilities—visual perception, spatial awareness, visual search, and target deduction—during free-form exploration in interactive 3D environments.

## Input / Output

Models receive sequential RGB visual observations from a first-person 3D room environment, a text task instruction (escape the room), and environment interaction feedback (e.g., object descriptions upon grabbing). They output a sequence of actions from a defined space (move forward, horizontal/vertical rotation, aim at coordinates, grab, use, read, input) along with a reasoning rationale at each step. Evaluation produces both a final escape rate and five intermediate process metrics (prop gain, average steps, grab success rate, grab ratio, intent-outcome consistency), with step limits of 50/75/100 for difficulty levels 1/2/3. A post-game debriefing task additionally requires the model to reconstruct the full narrative from collected clues, scored by an LLM evaluator for consistency with ground-truth.

## Method

The paper introduces EscapeCraft, a customizable 3D room environment built by extending ProcTHOR and Legent, which supports automatic large-scale scene generation with configurable room scale, furniture, and interactable objects. The core task mechanism is the Prop Chain—a singly linked list defining the ordered sequence of items (keys, locked boxes, password notes) and interactions required to escape, where chain length determines difficulty (1-hop: no props; 2-hop: one key or password; 3-hop: both). Unlike prior benchmarks, no step-by-step instructions are provided; models must autonomously explore, deduce short-term goals, and coordinate multiple abilities in a free-form manner. The benchmark includes 63 scenes across four room styles and three difficulty levels, plus multi-room combinations where the second room contains two doors requiring spatial disambiguation. A key analytical contribution is the intent-outcome consistency metric (C_IO), which uses GPT-4o to score whether a model's stated reasoning rationale aligns with the actual environment feedback, exposing accidental successes that inflate raw task-completion scores.

## Results

On the MM-Escape benchmark (standard single-room setting), GPT-4o achieves the highest average escape rate of 81.36%, followed by Gemini-1.5-pro (61.06%) and Claude 3.5 Sonnet (52.60%). Performance drops sharply with difficulty: GPT-4o's escape rate falls from 100% (Difficulty-1) to 71.36% (Difficulty-3), while smaller models like Llama-3.2-11b-vision and Qwen-VL-Max drop to 0% and 9.52% respectively at Difficulty-3. Human testers achieve 100% escape rate across all difficulties in far fewer steps (5.73 vs. 11.27 for GPT-4o at Difficulty-1). The intent-outcome consistency metric reveals that even GPT-4o achieves only 26.51% consistency on Difficulty-3, indicating many successful sub-goals are accidental rather than the result of valid reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a benchmark paper evaluating seven MLLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5-pro, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Doubao 1.5 Pro, Llama-3.2-11b-vision, Qwen-VL-Max, Phi-3-vision-128k) against human performance on the proposed MM-Escape benchmark. GPT-4o leads all models across every difficulty level, achieving 100% escape rate at Difficulty-1 and 71.36% at Difficulty-3, while also using the fewest steps (11.27 vs. 21.18 for Gemini at Difficulty-1). Gemini-1.5-pro shows the strongest visual search ability (highest Prop Gain at 90.91% on Difficulty-2) but suffers from spatial awareness issues, getting stuck in corners and using more steps. Claude 3.5 Sonnet and Llama-3.2-11b-vision show moderate Difficulty-1 performance but collapse at Difficulty-2 and beyond (0% ER for Llama-3.2). All models fall substantially short of the 100% human escape rate, with GPT-4o requiring roughly 2x the steps of humans even at the easiest level. The intent-outcome consistency analysis reveals that even top-performing models achieve only ~26% consistency, meaning most successful prop acquisitions are accidental rather than the product of coherent reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the embodied-agent evaluation line (ProcTHOR, Legent, AI2-THOR) by constructing a customizable room-escape environment (EscapeCraft) that supports free-form multimodal exploration, departing from both text-only escape benchmarks (EscapeBench, TextWorld) and controlled video-QA settings (OpenEQA, EgoSchema). Unlike open-world game benchmarks (MineRL, ALFRED, OSWorld) that emphasize final task completion or rely on structured knowledge libraries, MM-Escape introduces a prop-chain mechanism to define extensible reasoning difficulty and evaluates the intermediate reasoning process alongside outcome. The intent-outcome consistency metric (C_IO) is a novel diagnostic that has no direct counterpart in prior embodied or multimodal evaluation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even top-performing MLLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5-pro) exhibit severe degradation as reasoning chains lengthen, with distinct and model-specific failure modes (repetitive trajectories, spatial disorientation, ineffective prop use) that are invisible under outcome-only metrics. The C_IO finding—that a large fraction of 'successful' sub-goal interactions are accidental rather than reasoned—challenges the field's reliance on task success as a proxy for reasoning quality and opens a new axis for diagnosing multimodal agents. Open questions include whether the extensible prop-chain framework generalizes to richer 3D environments, whether the observed failure modes can be addressed through architectural changes (e.g., explicit spatial memory), and how post-game debriefing scales as model capabilities improve.
