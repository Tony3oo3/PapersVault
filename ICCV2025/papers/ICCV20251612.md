## Problem

Synchronizing multiple videos of the same action from different scenes requires nonlinear temporal alignment, yet existing pairwise methods incur O(N×L²) retrieval cost and produce no globally consistent alignment across the full video collection. Additionally, no prior approach addresses synchronization of AI-generated videos depicting the same action, where temporal misalignment is even more pronounced due to diverse visual styles and execution timing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251612/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of N variable-length video clips of the same action, each converted to a per-frame embedding sequence U_i ∈ R^{C×L_i} via any pretrained feature extractor (e.g., DINO-ViT [CLS] tokens, OpenCLIP, or CARL). It outputs per-video CPAB warping parameters θ_i, a shared 1D temporal prototype of fixed median length, and—upon alignment—synchronized frame sequences and transferred phase annotations. A key design constraint is that the prototype length is set to the median of all input video lengths to prevent the 'shrinking' artifact observed with variable-length multivariate data.

## Method

TPL introduces a Diffeomorphic Multitasking Autoencoder (D-MTAE) that simultaneously performs dimensionality reduction and joint alignment: a TCN encoder compresses each C-channel embedding sequence to a univariate latent Z_i, a DTAN-style alignment module (InceptionTime backbone) predicts CPAB warping parameters θ_i for each video, and a TCN decoder reconstructs the original embeddings from the warped latents. The training objective combines an Inverse-Consistency Averaging Error (ICAE) loss—enabling regularization-free joint alignment—weighted by a sigmoid annealing schedule, with a misalignment-invariant reconstruction loss that prevents trivial collapse of the latent. The 1D bottleneck is the critical architectural choice: it discards channel-specific temporal variations that would otherwise hinder multivariate alignment, while the median-length prototype constraint preserves temporal scale. At inference, new videos are aligned by predicting their warping parameters and mapping frames to the shared prototype axis, enabling O(L) frame retrieval instead of O(N×L²) nearest-neighbor search.

## Results

On Penn Action with DINO-ViT features, TPL achieves CBC=0.788 and PLP=0.803, outperforming the best DTAN variant (ICAE: CBC=0.578) and DBA (CBC=0.773). On the GenAI-MVS dataset, TPL reaches CBC=0.953 (DINO) and 0.942 (OpenCLIP), surpassing SoftDTW (0.890/0.926) and DBA (0.886/0.918). TPL is ~10× faster than DBA (534 s vs. 5415 s on Penn Action) and ~5× faster than SoftDTW (534 s vs. 3010 s). For frame retrieval, TPL reduces runtime from 30.1 s (full KNN) to 0.24 s, a 125× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TPL outperforms all three DTAN variants (WCSS, WCSS+Reg., ICAE) by a wide margin on every dataset and feature extractor; for example, on Penn Action with DINO, TPL achieves CBC 0.788 versus 0.578 for DTAN+ICAE, the strongest DTAN variant. Against the optimization-based baselines DBA and SoftDTW, TPL gains 1–3 points in CBC (e.g., 0.953 vs. 0.890 on GenAI-MVS with DINO) while running 5–10× faster (534 s vs. 5415 s for DBA on Penn Action). On the phase-classification proxy task (Table 3), TPL-aligned features reach 93.31% accuracy and Kendall's τ = 0.990 on Penn Action, marginally surpassing VSP (93.12%, τ = 0.986) and CARL (93.07%, τ = 0.985), and clearly outperforming earlier pairwise methods TCC (74.39%), GTA (78.90%), LAV (78.68%), and VAVA (84.48%). Notably, DTAN+ICAE with OpenCLIP features degrades severely on GenAI-MVS (CBC drops to 0.535), whereas TPL maintains robust performance (0.942), highlighting TPL's advantage in handling the high-variance embeddings of AI-generated content.

## Relation to Prior Work

TPL extends the Diffeomorphic Temporal Alignment Net (DTAN) line of work, which learns CPAB-based warps for joint alignment of time series, by introducing a univariate autoencoder bottleneck that reduces high-dimensional multivariate video embeddings to a 1D latent before alignment. This departs from prior DTAN formulations, which were designed for univariate sequences and suffer from a 'shrinking' effect on variable-length multivariate data; TPL addresses this by fixing the prototype to the median video length. The framework also builds on the regularization-free ICAE objective and CPAB transformation family, while positioning itself against pairwise video alignment methods (TCC, GTA, VAVA, CARL) that scale as O(N×L²) and lack global consistency across a video collection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TPL provides a practical, linear-time synchronization pipeline that works with off-the-shelf pretrained features (DINO, OpenCLIP), making it accessible without per-dataset training. The introduction of the GenAI-MVS dataset and demonstration of synchronization on AI-generated videos opens a new evaluation domain as generative video becomes more prevalent. However, the method's effectiveness is tightly coupled to the quality of the initial embeddings, and the GenAI dataset is small (82 videos, 5 classes), leaving open how well the approach scales to very large video collections or to actions with highly variable phase structure. A key open question is whether the 1D bottleneck assumption holds for actions where multiple independent sub-processes evolve in parallel.
