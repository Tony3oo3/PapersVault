## Problem

Existing driving world models face a fundamental trade-off: video diffusion approaches (e.g., Vista) model joint distributions over fixed-length frame sequences, preventing variable-length long-horizon prediction and trajectory planning, while GPT-style autoregressive methods support temporal flexibility but degrade visual fidelity through discrete tokenization. No prior framework unifies high-fidelity continuous visual generation, flexible-length autoregressive rollout, and end-to-end motion planning in a single model.

## Input / Output

The model takes 10 consecutive front-view camera frames at 512×1024 resolution along with their corresponding per-step driving actions (Δθ, Δx, Δy in ego-coordinates) as conditioning context. It autoregressively produces one future frame at a time (up to 600 frames / 2 minutes) and a 3-second vehicle pose trajectory; the trajectory stream can be run independently at up to 20 Hz without invoking the visual generation module. A user-supplied trajectory can optionally replace the predicted one to enable trajectory-controlled video synthesis.

## Method

Epona decomposes world modeling into three modules: a 12-layer Multimodal Spatiotemporal Transformer (MST) with interleaved causal temporal attention and multimodal spatial attention encodes the historical context into a compact latent F; a 2-layer Trajectory Planning DiT (TrajDiT) and a 12-layer Next-frame Prediction DiT (VisDiT) then asynchronously denoise the future trajectory and next-frame image latents respectively, both using rectified-flow objectives conditioned on the shared F. This decoupled spatiotemporal factorization—causal GPT-style temporal modeling in compressed latent space plus separate diffusion-based spatial rendering—enables flexible-length autoregressive generation in continuous representations. A chain-of-forward training strategy periodically performs multi-step forward passes using one-step denoised self-predictions to simulate inference-time error accumulation, mitigating autoregressive drift. A temporal-aware DCAE decoder (spatiotemporal self-attention prepended to a deep compression autoencoder) further improves inter-frame consistency during video decoding.

## Results

On the NuScenes video generation benchmark, Epona achieves a state-of-the-art FVD of 82.8, improving 7.4% over the previous best (Vista, 89.4) while extending maximum generation duration from 15 s (150 frames) to 120 s (600 frames) — an 8× increase. On the NAVSIM planning benchmark, Epona attains the highest overall PDMS of 86.2 using only front-camera input, surpassing DRAMA (85.5, Camera+Lidar) and PARA-Drive (84.6, Camera). On NuScenes planning, Epona achieves the lowest 1-second collision rate (0.01%) and a competitive average L2 error of 1.25 m without any auxiliary supervision (no maps, 3D boxes, or occupancy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

For video generation (Table 1), Epona outperforms Vista [18] by 7.4% in FVD (82.8 vs 89.4) and DrivingWorld [26] by 9.0% (82.8 vs 90.9), while Vista retains a slight FID edge (6.9 vs 7.5). The most striking gap is in generation length: Epona produces 120 s of coherent video versus 15 s for Vista and 40 s for DrivingWorld. For motion planning on NAVSIM (Table 4), Epona (PDMS 86.2, Camera only) beats DRAMA (85.5, Camera+Lidar), PARA-Drive (84.6, Camera), LAW (84.6, Camera), UniAD (83.4, Camera), and TransFuser (84.0, Camera+Lidar), making it the top performer despite using fewer sensor modalities. On NuScenes planning (Table 3), Epona trails UniAD (L2 1.03 m vs 1.25 m; collision 0.31% vs 0.36%) but achieves the lowest 1-second collision rate (0.01%) and matches DoE-1 [70] in average L2 (1.25 vs 1.26 m) while using no auxiliary supervision and only a front camera, whereas UniAD relies on maps, 3D boxes, motion, tracklets, and occupancy. Overall, Epona wins on the primary video-generation metric (FVD) and the NAVSIM planning score, ties or slightly trails on NuScenes planning L2, and trades a small FID deficit for a large duration and FVD gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

Epona positions itself as a synthesis of two competing paradigms for driving world models: video diffusion approaches (Vista, DriveGAN) that model fixed-length joint spatiotemporal distributions, and GPT-style autoregressive methods (GAIA-1, DrivingWorld) that sacrifice visual fidelity through discrete tokenization. It builds on rectified flow objectives (Liu et al. 2022), the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie 2023), and the DCAE deep compression autoencoder (Chen et al. 2024), but recombines them into a decoupled spatiotemporal factorization where a causal temporal transformer handles dynamics in latent space while separate diffusion transformers handle spatial rendering and trajectory generation. The chain-of-forward training strategy draws inspiration from noise augmentation in GameNGen and Diffusion Forcing but generalizes the idea to any autoregressive diffusion loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in unifying long-horizon video generation (120 s, 8× Vista) and real-time motion planning (20 Hz) in a single 2.5 B-parameter model that requires no perception inputs (3D boxes, lanes) or auxiliary supervision. The observation that the model implicitly learns traffic rules (e.g., stopping at red lights) purely from self-supervised next-frame prediction suggests a path toward world models that acquire driving knowledge without explicit annotation. Open questions include whether the chain-of-forward strategy scales beyond 600 frames without drift, whether the front-camera-only setting generalizes to multi-view or adverse-weather scenarios, and whether the shared-latent coupling between video and trajectory prediction holds at larger model scales or with pre-trained video backbones.
