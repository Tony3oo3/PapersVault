## Problem

Open-vocabulary occupancy prediction requires dense voxel-to-text correspondences for supervision, but existing pipelines produce noisy or coarse labels: image features from vision-language models conflate semantics with appearance, and voxel-based model-view projections ignore occlusion and yield sparse correspondences. This paper tackles the core problem of generating dense, fine-grained 3D language occupancy ground truth without labor-intensive human annotation.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes six surround RGB images (256×704 or 512×1408) and produces a binary occupancy map (200×200×16 voxels at 0.4 m resolution) plus a 3D language volume where each occupied voxel carries a compressed 128-dim language feature. During the offline pseudo-label generation stage, unlabeled LiDAR point clouds and the same surround images are consumed by a separate pipeline (LVLM + OV-Seg + projection + voxelization) to produce the training ground truth. At evaluation, each voxel's language feature is matched against predefined CLIP text embeddings via cosine similarity to assign open-vocabulary semantics.

## Method

The core contribution is a semantic transitive labeling pipeline that propagates text labels through three stages: (1) a large vision-language model (Qwen-VL) extracts per-frame vocabularies via chain-of-thought prompting, with vocabularies consolidated across consecutive frames to compensate for missed classes; (2) an open-vocabulary segmentation model (SAN) assigns each pixel a text label by cosine similarity in a shared feature space; (3) LiDAR points are projected onto the image plane to inherit pixel labels, then multi-frame point clouds are merged in world coordinates and voxelized via majority-voting to produce dense 3D language occupancy ground truth. The LOcc framework then replaces the prediction head of standard supervised occupancy backbones (BEVFormer, BEVDet, BEVDet4D) with a geometry head (binary occupancy, supervised by cross-entropy) and a language head (supervised by cosine similarity against CLIP text embeddings). A language autoencoder compresses 512-dim CLIP embeddings to 128-dim to reduce memory cost during training. The key distinction from prior work is that labels are transferred transitively (image text → LiDAR point → voxel) rather than relying on image features as intermediates or voxel-based projections, and occlusion is handled by selecting the nearest visible camera per point.

## Results

On the Occ3D-nuScenes zero-shot benchmark, LOcc-BEVDet4D achieves an mIoU of 23.84 at 512×1408 resolution and 22.95 at 256×704, substantially outperforming all prior zero-shot methods. Notably, even the simpler LOcc-BEVDet (ResNet-50, 256×704) reaches 20.29 mIoU, surpassing the best previous zero-shot approach (POP-3D-BEVDet4D at 512×1408, 15.64 mIoU) by over 4.6 points. In the open-vocabulary setting (Table 4), LOcc-BEVDet4D achieves 26.91 mIoU with competitive performance on unseen classes (e.g., 23.77 on motorcycle, 23.93 on traffic cone).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LOcc decisively outperforms all prior zero-shot occupancy prediction methods. Against POP-3D (NeurIPS 2024), the strongest previous zero-shot baseline, LOcc-BEVDet4D at 256×704 (22.95 mIoU) exceeds POP-3D-BEVDet4D at 512×1408 (15.64 mIoU) by 7.31 points, and LOcc-BEVDet at 256×704 (20.29) already surpasses it by 4.65 points. Against VEON (arXiv 2024), LOcc-BEVDet4D (22.95) outperforms VEON-L (15.14) by 7.81 points, with particularly large gains on dynamic objects (car: 38.90 vs. 12.70; pedestrian: 13.46 vs. 6.50). Against image-only self-supervised methods SelfOcc (9.30) and OccNeRF (10.81), LOcc achieves roughly 2× the mIoU. Against fully supervised baselines (BEVFormer 39.04, CVT-Occ 40.34), LOcc naturally trails, but the gap is narrowed to ~16 points at 512×1408, demonstrating that pseudo-labeled supervision recovers most of the performance lost by removing human annotations. The ablation (Table 2) confirms that the semantic transitive labeling pipeline is the key driver: replacing it with image-feature intermediates (setting a) drops mIoU from 22.95 to 19.37, and voxel-based model-view projection (setting e) causes a catastrophic drop to 15.41.

## Relation to Prior Work

LOcc builds directly on existing supervised occupancy architectures (BEVFormer, BEVDet, BEVDet4D) by replacing their prediction heads with a geometry head and a language head, while the core novelty lies in the semantic transitive labeling pipeline that generates pseudo-labeled 3D language occupancy ground truth. It departs from prior open-vocabulary occupancy work (POP-3D, VEON) that relied on noisy image features as intermediates or coarse voxel-based model-view projections, instead using LVLMs (Qwen-VL) for vocabulary extraction and open-vocabulary segmentation (SAN) for pixel-to-text association, followed by LiDAR point projection and majority-voting voxelization. A language autoencoder compresses CLIP embeddings to reduce computational cost during alignment. The framework is deliberately architecture-agnostic, validating the same pipeline across forward-projection (BEVDet, BEVDet4D) and backward-projection (BEVFormer) paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The semantic transitive labeling pipeline offers a practical path toward reducing the labor-intensive human annotation bottleneck in 3D occupancy datasets, and its generalizability across multiple architectures makes it a drop-in improvement for existing occupancy pipelines. However, the pseudo-labeled ground truth still lags significantly behind human annotations (mIoU ~25.5 vs. ~40 for supervised baselines), indicating substantial residual noise from LVLM vocabulary extraction errors and segmentation imperfections. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more diverse or dynamic scenes, how sensitive performance is to the quality of the LVLM, and whether the gap to fully supervised methods can be closed with better foundation models or larger vocabulary sets.
