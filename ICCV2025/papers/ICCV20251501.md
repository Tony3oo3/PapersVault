## Problem

End-to-end Tracking-by-Attention (TbA) multi-object tracking methods, such as MOTR, use a single shared attention decoder to simultaneously detect new objects and associate existing tracklets, creating competing optimization objectives that degrade both tasks. This functional ambiguity is particularly harmful in crowded scenes where new-target detection is already difficult, leading to significant performance drops on benchmarks like MOT17.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251501/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB video frames resized to a shorter side of 800 px (longer side ≤ 1536 px) along with tracklet queries propagated from the previous frame. Training uses fixed three-frame clips as batches with frames sampled at random intervals of 1–10. At inference, the model outputs per-frame object bounding boxes and tracklet trajectories; objects whose association score exceeds 0.5 are initialized as new trajectories, while matched objects update existing tracklets.

## Method

LA-MOTR decouples the TbA pipeline into three specialized modules built on a DAB-Deformable-DETR backbone with ResNet-50. First, Separated Object-Tracklet Detection (SOTD) feeds previous-frame tracklet queries and blank object queries into a single decoder to produce independent detection embeddings for existing tracklets and all current-frame objects. Second, Spatial-Guided Learnable Association (SGLA), based on an Edge-Augmented Graph Transformer, computes pairwise bounding-box differences between objects and tracklets, embeds them via an MLP, and injects these relative spatial cues into a multi-layer Spatial-Weighted Attention that iteratively refines an association matrix. Third, a lightweight Tracklet Update step forms the next frame's tracklet queries as a weighted sum of current tracklet and object outputs (Q_{t+1}^{tck} = w·O_t^{tck} + (1−w)·O^{obj}), requiring no additional learnable parameters. Training uses a decoupled loss combining detection, track, and association terms with Hungarian-algorithm matching, and the model is initialized from COCO pre-trained weights.

## Results

On DanceTrack, LA-MOTR achieves 71.1% HOTA, 93.5% MOTA, and 84.4% DetA, setting new state-of-the-art among end-to-end methods and surpassing the best two-stage method OC-SORT (55.1% HOTA) by 16.0 points. On SportsMOT, it reaches 72.4% HOTA and 61.8% AssA, outperforming all end-to-end baselines. On MOT17 (with CrowdHuman augmentation), LA-MOTR attains 62.6% HOTA and 80.7% MOTA, leading all end-to-end approaches while remaining competitive with two-stage methods like OC-SORT (63.2% HOTA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DanceTrack, LA-MOTR (71.1% HOTA) outperforms the strongest end-to-end baselines MOTRv3 (70.4%), MOTIP (70.0%), and MOTRv2 (69.9%) by 0.7–1.7 points, and surpasses the best two-stage method OC-SORT (55.1%) by 16.0 points in HOTA. On SportsMOT, LA-MOTR (72.4% HOTA, 61.8% AssA) beats MOTIP (71.9%), LAID (71.7%), and MeMOTR (70.0%), and also edges out two-stage methods OC-SORT (71.9%) and DiffMOT (72.1%). On MOT17 with CrowdHuman augmentation, LA-MOTR (62.6% HOTA) leads all end-to-end methods (next best: MOTRv2 at 62.0%) but trails the two-stage OC-SORT (63.2%) by 0.6 points, which the authors attribute to MOT17's small training set (~5K frames) causing overfitting in Transformer-based models. Notably, LA-MOTR achieves the highest DetA (84.4%) on DanceTrack without requiring an auxiliary detector, unlike MOTRv2/v3 which incorporate YOLOx. The method also maintains comparable parameter count (50.29M) and inference speed (11.96 FPS) to MeMOTR (50.13M, 12.33 FPS) despite adding the SGLA module.

## Relation to Prior Work

LA-MOTR directly extends the Tracking-by-Attention (TbA) lineage initiated by MOTR, which uses a single Transformer decoder to jointly perform object detection and tracklet association. The key architectural departure is decoupling this shared decoder into a Separated Object-Tracklet Detection (SOTD) module and a Spatial-Guided Learnable Association (SGLA) module, the latter drawing on the Edge-Augmented Graph Transformer from 3DMOTFormer. This positions LA-MOTR as a response to the MOTRv2/MOTRv3/MeMOTR line of work, which mitigates detection-association conflict through auxiliary detectors or reweighted supervision while retaining the shared decoder, whereas LA-MOTR eliminates the conflict structurally.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled design offers a principled resolution to the task-interference problem in end-to-end MOT without requiring an external detector (unlike MOTRv2/v3), making it a practical and interpretable alternative for the TbA community. The finding that a simple weighted combination of current-frame outputs suffices for tracklet updates—matching or exceeding learned update mechanisms like TIM—suggests the association module itself carries sufficient temporal information, potentially simplifying future architectures. However, the paper acknowledges that long-term memory components still provide accuracy gains, and the modest HOTA margins over strong baselines (e.g., +0.7 over MOTRv3 on DanceTrack) leave open whether the decoupling advantage persists at larger scale or on more challenging benchmarks like MOT20.
