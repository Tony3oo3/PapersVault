## Problem

Existing training-free controllable T2I diffusion models such as Ctrl-X and FreeControl struggle to simultaneously preserve spatial structure and appearance fidelity, particularly in class-invariant setups where the structure and appearance images belong to different object categories or exhibit significant structural mismatch. These methods suffer from appearance leakage and fail to capture fine-grained pose and layout conditions, leaving no universally reliable solution for cross-category structure-appearance fusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250334/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts three inputs: a structure image (which can be a 3D mesh, point cloud, skeleton, Canny edge map, or natural image), an appearance image (RGB), and a text prompt describing the desired generation. It operates at 512×512 resolution on SDXL and produces a single generated image that fuses the structural layout of the structure image with the visual identity of the appearance image while following the text prompt. The structure image is consumed via convolutional features and self-attention maps injected into the diffusion backbone, while the appearance image is encoded into a clean latent that serves as a fixed-point anchor during recursive refinement.

## Method

DRF is a training-free dual recursive feedback loop built on the Ctrl-X injection pipeline. At each of 20 intermediate diffusion steps (after the first 5), it computes two guided noise estimates: an appearance score ε_θ^a from a modified stochastic latent z̃_t^a (constructed by re-noising the clean appearance latent z_0^a with the per-step noise schedule ratio α_t/α_{t-1} to avoid excessive guidance at high noise levels), and a generation score ε_θ^g from the current generation latent z_t^g. The appearance feedback loss minimizes the distance between the posterior mean z_{0|t}^a and the clean appearance latent z_0^a, while the generation feedback loss minimizes the distance between z_{0|t}^g and the previous iteration's posterior mean z_prev^g, treating it as a recursive fixed point. The two losses are combined with an exponential weighting schedule that progressively shifts emphasis from appearance to generation feedback across N recursive iterations, and the shared injection noise ε is updated via gradient descent on the combined DRF loss, yielding a refined generation latent that honors both structural and appearance constraints.

## Results

DRF achieves the highest Successive Rate (0.9859) across all four structure types, outperforming the next-best baseline T2I-Adapter+IP-Adapter (0.9718) and Ctrl-X (0.9577). On the Pose and Point-cloud structure sets, DRF attains the best scores on all three metrics (Self-Sim, CLIP, DINO-I), e.g., CLIP 0.3503 and DINO-I 0.7391 on Pose. On Mesh, DRF leads in CLIP (0.3492) and DINO-I (0.7342), while on Canny it achieves the best Self-Sim (0.0473) and CLIP (0.3102). In the user study, DRF is preferred over all baselines in every category: 35.52% for text alignment, 43.73% for structure preservation, and 33.72% for appearance retention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRF outperforms all six baselines on the Successive Rate metric (0.9859 vs. 0.9718 for T2I-Adapter+IP-Adapter, 0.9577 for Ctrl-X, 0.9152 for FreeControl, 0.8873 for ControlNet+IP-Adapter, and 0.7605 for Uni-ControlNet), confirming superior joint structure-appearance fidelity. On the Pose and Point-cloud structure sets, DRF achieves the best scores on all three metrics, with the largest margins over Uni-ControlNet (e.g., DINO-I 0.7391 vs. 0.6450 on Pose). Against its direct backbone Ctrl-X, DRF improves CLIP by 0.007–0.008 and DINO-I by 0.016–0.027 on Mesh and Pose, while also reducing Self-Sim on Pose (0.2294 vs. 0.2332) and Point-cloud (0.1114 vs. 0.1205). FreeControl remains competitive on Mesh Self-Sim (0.1503 vs. 0.1532) and Canny DINO-I (0.7381 vs. 0.7135), but trails DRF substantially on Successive Rate and user preference. In the user study, DRF is preferred at roughly 2× the rate of the next-best method (Ctrl-X) for text alignment (35.52% vs. 17.37%) and structure preservation (43.73% vs. 12.90%), indicating a clear perceptual advantage beyond the aggregate metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRF builds directly on Ctrl-X [14], which provides the backbone for injecting structure and appearance images into a pretrained T2I diffusion model via feed-forward feature replacement and self-attention, and on IDS [12], whose fixed-point regularization (FPR) loss structure is adapted here. The key departure from IDS is that DRF generates from initial noise over a full denoising schedule rather than optimizing a single latent at a random timestep, and it introduces a modified stochastic latent to prevent excessive appearance guidance at large timesteps. The dual-feedback design—combining an appearance-preservation loss with a generation-alignment loss applied recursively—goes beyond prior single-objective score distillation methods (SDS, DDS, IDS) and addresses the class-invariant setting where structure and appearance images belong to different categories, a regime where Ctrl-X and FreeControl degrade.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DRF offers a training-free, plug-and-play mechanism that can be layered onto existing controllable T2I pipelines (demonstrated with ControlNet + IP-Adapter) to improve both structural fidelity and appearance preservation, particularly in the challenging class-invariant regime (e.g., human skeleton → tiger). The scheduler-agnostic design and cross-backbone compatibility (SD 1.5, SD 2.0, SDXL) make it broadly applicable. However, the recursive iterations introduce meaningful computational overhead (roughly 2× the latency of Ctrl-X at N=1, 3.7× at N=3), and the method still struggles with fine-grained identity details (e.g., faces of non-celebrity individuals) that the pretrained model has not seen. Open questions include whether the dual-feedback loop can be distilled into a single forward pass or whether it extends naturally to video and 3D generation.
