## Problem

Existing visual-only hand-object reconstruction methods struggle to capture occluded contact details and object deformation at the contact interface, as visual perception inherently lacks information in regions hidden by the hand. While distributed tactile sensors can provide fine-grained force data, prior visual-tactile approaches like ViTaM simply fuse tactile and visual features with neural networks, ignoring the fundamental information mismatch between force signals and point cloud geometry. This paper addresses how to effectively integrate distributed tactile sensing with state-of-the-art visual reconstruction to refine contact modeling and deformation tracking.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of 3D point clouds (downsampled to 1024 points per frame) derived from single-view depth images, along with distributed tactile sensor readings organized into 22 hand regions (2 thumb, 3 finger, 8 palm areas). It produces MANO hand parameters (shape β∈R¹⁰, pose θ∈R⁵¹), an object mesh reconstructed from predicted SDF values via Marching Cubes, and a refined contact state. A key design choice is the two-stage consumption of inputs: point clouds drive the initial visual reconstruction, while tactile readings are only used in a post-hoc physics-based optimization stage rather than being fused into the network.

## Method

ViTaM-D operates in two stages. Stage 1 is a visual dynamic tracking network that predicts inter-frame point cloud flow via a PointNet++ backbone and transformer fusion module, then decodes object geometry (SDF via 3D-UNet + Marching Cubes) and hand pose (MANO parameters via a PVN3D-inspired voting mechanism), with a contact constraint that forces SDF values to zero at tactile-confirmed contact locations. Stage 2 introduces DF-Field, a distributed force-aware contact representation that models each hand-object point pair with a relative potential energy term (κl²) encoding deformation and a barrier energy term (adapted from IPC) preventing penetration; the force parameter κ is computed as the ratio of regional tactile force to point-pair distance. The hand pose is then refined by minimizing the total DF-Field energy plus regularization losses (joint twist penalties and pose proximity) via Adam gradient descent for 100 iterations per frame. This decoupled design—using tactile data in a physics-based optimization rather than neural feature fusion—allows the method to leverage any off-the-shelf visual tracker while correcting penetrations and suboptimal contact states.

## Results

On DexYCB (rigid objects), ViTaM-D without force optimization achieves the best IoU (90.1%), Chamfer Distance (9.6 mm), MPJPE (13.2 mm), and Contact IoU (35.4%), outperforming gSDF, HOTrack, and Zhang et al. On the HOT dataset (deformable objects), the visual tracking stage matches ViTaM's IoU (81.0% vs 80.5%), and the force-aware optimization further reduces MPJPE to 11.3 mm, Penetration Depth to 7.3 mm, and raises Contact IoU to 40.3%, substantially improving contact fidelity over both ViTaM and prior refinement methods (CPF, TOCH).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DexYCB, ViTaM-D (visual stage only) beats gSDF by 3.3 points in IoU (90.1 vs 86.8) and 3.8 mm in CD (9.6 vs 13.4), and outperforms HOTrack by 1.9 points in IoU and 12.5 mm in MPJPE (13.2 vs 25.7). It also surpasses Zhang et al. [40] in IoU (90.1 vs 88.1) and CIoU (35.4 vs 30.5), though gSDF retains a slight edge in Penetration Depth (8.9 vs 9.9 mm). On HOT, the visual tracking stage marginally outperforms ViTaM in IoU (81.0 vs 80.5) and CD (10.9 vs 11.5 mm), while the force-aware optimization stage yields the largest gains: MPJPE drops from 13.6 to 11.3 mm and CIoU jumps from 29.8% to 40.3%, surpassing both CPF (CIoU 37.5%) and TOCH (CIoU 34.1%) as refinement strategies. The tactile-based force representation also outperforms a fixed-force ablation (M=0.5), confirming that distributed tactile readings provide more accurate interaction forces than empirical settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ViTaM visual-tactile reconstruction system [15] by replacing its direct neural fusion of tactile and visual features with a two-stage pipeline: a visual dynamic tracking network (building on PointNet++ backbones, transformer fusion, and ConvOccNet-style SDF decoding) followed by a force-aware hand-pose optimization stage. The key conceptual departure is DF-Field, a contact representation that borrows the barrier energy formulation from Incremental Potential Contact [17] and couples it with a relative potential energy term to model both hand motion and object deformation, rather than treating contact as a purely geometric constraint as in CPF [33] or TOCH [42]. Unlike prior visual-only methods (gSDF, HOTrack) that struggle with occluded contact regions, ViTaM-D explicitly leverages distributed tactile sensor readings to refine local contact geometry, positioning itself at the intersection of tactile sensing and SDF-based reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HOT dataset (600 sequences, 30 deformable objects, 8 views, FEM-based ground truth) addresses a genuine gap: prior hand-object benchmarks (DexYCB, ARCTIC) focus on rigid or articulated objects and lack accurate deformation ground truth, making deformable interaction reconstruction difficult to evaluate. The force-aware optimization is designed as a plug-in refinement step compatible with existing visual reconstruction pipelines, which lowers the adoption barrier. However, real-world validation is limited to a single qualitative example, the tactile hardware requirement (ViTaM glove) restricts accessibility, and the simulation-to-real generalization remains largely untested. Open questions include whether DF-Field scales to bimanual or multi-object interactions, and whether the 22-region hand discretization is sufficient for fine-grained contact in dexterous manipulation.
