## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting assumes geometric consistency across input views, a condition violated by real-world captures containing dynamic objects, occlusions, and illumination shifts. Existing robustness methods attempt to filter or down-weight inconsistent regions, but this discards supervision and leaves reconstruction artifacts unresolved. The paper asks whether generative models can actively reconstruct occluded content during 3DGS optimization rather than merely ignoring it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252209/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view in-the-wild RGB images at 1024×1024 resolution along with known camera extrinsics and an SfM-initialized point cloud. During training, it additionally queries three frozen pretrained models—DINOv2 (ViT-B/14) for semantic features, DIFT (from SDXL) for geometric-correspondence features, and FLUX-1. lite as the rectified-flow DiT for region-adaptive inpainting. The output is a standard 3DGS parameter set (positions, scales, rotations, colors, opacities) optimized for 30k iterations, from which novel views are rendered via differentiable splatting.

## Method

RogSplat iteratively detects and repairs distractor regions during 3DGS optimization. At each iteration, it renders the current 3DGS model, extracts pixel-aligned DINOv2 and DIFT features from both the rendered and ground-truth images, L2-normalizes and concatenates them, then computes a per-pixel cosine similarity map S that flags low-consistency (occluded) regions. The RF-Refiner module applies SDEdit on FLUX with a region-adaptive denoising schedule: the similarity map is inverted and clamped to [s_min, s_max], then mapped to per-region timesteps so that low-similarity pixels receive stronger noise and more denoising steps while high-similarity pixels remain nearly untouched. The FLUX DiT processes the entire latent simultaneously but selectively updates only the tokens whose assigned timestep has been reached, enabling coherent multi-region editing in a single forward pass. The refined images replace the raw GT images as supervision for the standard 3DGS loss (L1 + D-SSIM), closing the loop without any additional architectural changes to the Gaussian primitives themselves.

## Results

On the RobustNeRF dataset, RogSplat achieves a mean PSNR of 31.05, SSIM of 0.934, and LPIPS of 0.087, outperforming the strongest baseline SpotLessSplats (30.16 / 0.900 / 0.095) by +0.89 dB in PSNR. On the NeRF on-the-go dataset, RogSplat leads across all three occlusion levels, reaching a mean PSNR of 24.32 under high occlusion (vs. 23.05 for SpotLessSplats) and 26.03 under medium occlusion (vs. 26.19 for SpotLessSplats on the Corner scene specifically, but 26.03 overall mean). The method consistently achieves the best LPIPS scores, indicating superior perceptual quality in occluded regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RogSplat outperforms all seven baselines on both the RobustNeRF and NeRF on-the-go datasets across PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS. The closest competitor is SpotLessSplats, which RogSplat surpasses by +0.89 dB mean PSNR on RobustNeRF (31.05 vs. 30.16) and by up to +1.27 dB on the high-occlusion scenes of NeRF on-the-go (24.32 vs. 23.05 mean). WildGS is the second-strongest baseline, trailing RogSplat by approximately 2.2 dB mean PSNR on RobustNeRF. The NeRF-based methods (RobustNeRF and NeRF On-the-go) are significantly outperformed, with RogSplat achieving roughly 4–5 dB higher mean PSNR, highlighting the advantage of the 3DGS representation combined with generative refinement. On the NeRF on-the-go dataset, RogSplat also leads in perceptual quality (LPIPS) across all occlusion levels, e.g., 0.103 vs. 0.157 for SpotLessSplats under high occlusion, confirming that the RF-Refiner produces more visually coherent reconstructions in severely occluded regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

RogSplat builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as its scene representation and departs from the dominant 'remove-based' paradigm in robust 3DGS (WildGS, SpotLessSplats, GS-W) that discards or down-weights distractor regions. Instead, it repurposes rectified flow models (FLUX) and SDEdit-style guided denoising to actively regenerate occluded content, using fused DINOv2 and DIFT features for region-aware similarity scoring. This positions the work at the intersection of robust novel-view synthesis and generative image editing, extending the 3DGS optimization loop with an iterative inpainting step that prior robust-3DGS methods do not perform.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By replacing distractor removal with generative reconstruction, RogSplat preserves full supervision signal from in-the-wild images, which is particularly valuable when occlusions are dense and removal would leave large unsupervised gaps. The similarity-guided denoising schedule (region-adaptive timesteps) is a clean, training-free mechanism that could transfer to other 3D reconstruction pipelines. Open questions include computational cost (FLUX inference per training iteration limits scalability to large scene collections), generalization to more complex dynamic scenes (e.g., multiple moving objects with parallax), and whether the approach can be made differentiable end-to-end to avoid the current two-stage render-then-refine loop.
