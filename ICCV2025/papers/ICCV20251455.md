## Problem

Current vision-language models produce unstructured, error-prone reasoning chains on complex visual QA tasks, frequently presenting conclusions prematurely and then attempting to justify them. The lack of a systematic, multi-stage reasoning process means that once an erroneous intermediate step is generated, the model continues along a flawed path with no mechanism for self-correction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251455/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image paired with a text question and produces a single-pass, four-stage tagged response: <SUMMARY> (task outline), <CAPTION> (relevant image description), <REASONING> (step-by-step logical analysis), and <CONCLUSION> (final answer). The first three stages are internal reasoning that the user does not need to act on, while the conclusion adapts its verbosity to the user's request. No external prompting or intervention is required between stages; the model transitions autonomously in one inference pass.

## Method

LLaVA-CoT decomposes reasoning into four tagged stages (Summary, Caption, Reasoning, Conclusion) and is trained via full-parameter supervised fine-tuning of Llama-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct on the LLaVA-CoT-100k dataset, which was constructed by prompting GPT-4o to generate structured multi-stage annotations over ~99k QA pairs drawn from 10 general and science-targeted VQA benchmarks. At test time, the model employs SWIRES (Stage-wise Retracing Search), a beam search that operates at stage boundaries rather than fixed token intervals: at each stage it generates M candidates, scores them with a reward model (InternLM-XComposer2.5-Reward), and retains the top N to expand in the next stage. Crucially, if all candidates at a stage fall below a reward threshold, the algorithm retraces to the previous stage to regenerate its output, providing a self-correction loop that addresses errors propagated from earlier stages. This retracing mechanism, combined with the structured stage format, yields stronger test-time scaling than best-of-N or standard stage-wise beam search.

## Results

LLaVA-CoT (11B) achieves an average score of 63.1 across six multimodal reasoning benchmarks (MMStar-R, MMBench-R, MMVet-R, MathVista, AI2D, HallusionBench), improving over its base model Llama-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct (56.9) by 6.2 points. With stage-wise retracing search (SWIRES) at test time, the average rises to 66.3, a 9.4-point gain over the base model. The largest per-benchmark improvements come on MMStar-R (46.6→63.0) and HallusionBench (40.3→49.1), while MathVista shows a more modest gain (48.6→57.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

With SWIRES test-time scaling, LLaVA-CoT (11B, avg 66.3) surpasses GPT-4o-mini (63.8) by 2.5 points and Gemini-1.5-Pro (63.6) by 2.7 points on average, despite being an 11B open-source model. It also outperforms the much larger Llama-3.2-90B-Vision-Instruct (62.3) by 4.0 points and narrowly edges out DeepSeek-VL2 (66.0) and Qwen2-VL-7B (65.9) by 0.3 and 0.4 points respectively. Against the top closed-source models, LLaVA-CoT still trails GPT-4o (71.8) by 5.5 points and GLM-4v-Plus (72.5) by 6.2 points, and is marginally below Claude 3.5 Sonnet (66.7) by 0.4 points. The most striking per-benchmark wins are on MMStar-R, where LLaVA-CoT (63.0) beats GPT-4o-mini (54.9) by 8.1 points and Llama-3.2-90B (51.1) by 11.9 points, and on HallusionBench, where it leads all open-source comparables. On MMBench-R, DeepSeek-VL2 (80.2) and Qwen2-VL-7B (77.6) still hold the edge over LLaVA-CoT (75.8), indicating that structured reasoning does not uniformly dominate every sub-skill.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends chain-of-thought prompting from a single undifferentiated reasoning chain into a structured four-stage format (Summary, Caption, Reasoning, Conclusion) with explicit special tags, building on the CoT paradigm established by Wei et al. and its VLM adaptations (Prism, Visual CoT, MSG). For test-time scaling, it departs from standard beam search and best-of-N by introducing stage-wise beam search with a retracing mechanism (SWIRES), which aligns search granularity with semantic reasoning stages rather than fixed token/sentence intervals. The approach is trained via supervised fine-tuning on a GPT-4o-generated structured dataset (LLaVA-CoT-100k), positioning it within the broader trend of distilling structured reasoning into smaller VLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical recipe—100k structured samples, four tagged stages, and SWIRES at test time—provides a low-cost pathway to substantially boost VLM reasoning without scaling model parameters, which is relevant for resource-constrained deployments. The SWIRES retracing mechanism offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc test-time scaling that continues to improve where best-of-N and standard beam search plateau. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the single base model (Llama-3.2-11B) tested, whether the GPT-4o-generated training data introduces distributional biases, and whether reinforcement learning (suggested by the authors) could replace the reward-model-based search for more efficient scaling.
