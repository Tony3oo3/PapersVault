## Problem

Deep learning-based image denoisers overfit to the specific noise distribution in their training data, severely limiting generalization to unseen noise types and levels. Existing remedies—self-supervised training, synthetic noise modeling, pretrained priors, and masked training—each carry their own limitations in robustness, computational cost, or fine-grained texture preservation. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a lightweight dynamic kernel prediction approach that adapts filtering to local image context while preventing memorization of training noise patterns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250540/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single noisy RGB image (any resolution, processed as 128×128 patches during training) and outputs a denoised RGB image of the same size. Despite being trained exclusively on i.i.d. Gaussian noise at σ=15, the model generalizes to six synthetic noise types (Gaussian, spatially correlated Gaussian, Poisson, speckle, salt-and-pepper, mixture), real-world sensor noise from smartphones and DSLRs, and Monte Carlo rendering noise at 64–128 spp. The model is extremely compact at ~0.04M parameters, and inference supports both fixed (T=10) and adaptive iteration counts via early termination.

## Method

IDF iteratively applies a shared-weight Dynamic Image Denoising (DID) block that, at each iteration, unfolds the current estimate into overlapping K×K patches and predicts per-pixel denoising kernels via four sub-modules: a Feature Extraction Module (RMS normalization followed by two 3×3 convolutions for noise-level-invariant features), a Global Statistics Module (mean/std of inter-iteration residuals), a Local Correlation Module (Pearson correlation within patches), and a Kernel Prediction Module (channel-attention fusion followed by a 3×3 convolution). Predicted kernels are regularized with power normalization (p=3) instead of softmax, enforcing a sum-to-one constraint that makes each kernel a weighted-averaging operator and reduces degrees of freedom to discourage noise memorization. An adaptive Dynamic Iteration Control (DIC) mechanism monitors the convergence of kernel center values across iterations and terminates early when the spatially averaged confidence map drops below a threshold (κ=0.015), reducing iterations by ~30% without quality loss.

## Results

Trained exclusively on Gaussian noise at σ=15, IDF (0.04M params) achieves strong OOD generalization across six synthetic noise types on four benchmarks. On CBSD68, the full model (Ours) attains 25.73 dB PSNR on Gaussian σ=50, 27.87 dB on Spatial Gaussian σ=55, 27.47 dB on Poisson α=3.5, 33.38 dB on Salt-and-Pepper d=0.02, 29.18 dB on Speckle, and 28.11 dB on Mixture Level 4. On real-world SIDD, IDF reaches 32.08 dB PSNR / 0.7578 SSIM, and on Monte Carlo rendering at 64 spp it achieves 28.09 dB PSNR, outperforming all baselines in most settings while using ~17× fewer parameters than DnCNN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDF decisively outperforms DnCNN, SwinIR, and Restormer across all noise types and datasets, with gaps of 5–10 dB PSNR on high-noise settings (e.g., 25.73 vs. 15.87 dB on Gaussian σ=50 CBSD68). Against CODE, IDF wins on most benchmarks but CODE edges ahead on PolyU (38.11 vs. 37.93 dB PSNR) and Nam (39.78 vs. 38.96 dB), suggesting CODE overfits to those specific sensor domains. Compared to MaskedDenoising, IDF leads by 1–4 dB PSNR on most noise types, with the largest gap on Poisson (27.47 vs. 24.17 dB) and Mixture noise (28.11 vs. 25.60 dB). The closest competitor is CLIPDenoising, which slightly edges IDF on Gaussian σ=50 (25.84 vs. 25.73 dB on CBSD68) and Salt-and-Pepper SSIM, but IDF surpasses it on Spatial Gaussian, Poisson, Speckle, Mixture, and all real-world datasets (e.g., 32.08 vs. 30.19 dB on SIDD). Notably, IDF achieves these results with only 0.04M parameters and 8.02–11.5 GFLOPs, compared to 19.55M params and 8.77 GFLOPs for CLIPDenoising, 26.1M for Restormer, and 12.2M for CODE.

## Relation to Prior Work

IDF sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: dynamic kernel prediction (Kernel-Predicting CNN, Kernel Prediction Network, ADFNet) and iterative refinement (DBPN, ResShift, IDR), neither of which had previously been combined in a denoising context. It departs from the dominant trend toward large transformer-based denoisers (SwinIR, Restormer) by arguing that architectural inductive bias—pixel-wise adaptive kernels with sum-to-one constraints and RMS normalization—can substitute for model scale in achieving OOD generalization. The power-normalization choice for kernel weighting and the Pearson-correlation-based local structure cue are borrowed from attention and self-supervised denoising literature respectively, but their specific combination within a compact iterative CNN is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 0.04M-parameter model that outperforms 20–26M-parameter transformers on OOD denoising across six synthetic noise types, four real-world sensor datasets, and Monte Carlo rendering noise, all while being trained on a single Gaussian noise level. This challenges the implicit assumption that generalization requires either massive training data diversity or large model capacity, suggesting that structured inductive biases (dynamic kernels, iterative refinement, normalization) are the key lever. Limitations include slower inference than DnCNN due to the PyTorch unfolding operation, a fixed DIC threshold (κ=0.015) that may not transfer across domains, and evaluation restricted to denoising rather than broader restoration tasks. Open questions include whether the dynamic-kernel-plus-iteration recipe extends to video denoising, super-resolution, or other low-level tasks, and whether the unfolding bottleneck can be resolved with custom CUDA kernels to make the efficiency advantage fully realized.
