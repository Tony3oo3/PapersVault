## Problem

Existing prompt-based methods for multi-modal visual recognition under missing modalities suffer from two critical limitations: static prompts are instance-agnostic and cannot adapt to varying missing-data conditions, and basic prompt tuning lacks cross-layer synergy, failing to exploit hierarchical multi-modal dependencies. This leads to significant performance degradation when critical modalities are absent in real-world deployments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250715/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 RGB images (split into 16×16 ViT-B/16 patches) and text sequences (max 77 CLIP tokens), with missing modalities replaced by zero-filled tensors of dimension 512. Learnable prompts of length 36 are injected at 6 transformer layers. The output is a class label or score depending on the task: multi-label genre scores (F1-Macro) for MM-IMDb, single-label food category (top-1 accuracy) for UPMC Food-101, or binary hateful-meme detection (AUROC) for Hateful Memes.

## Method

SyP introduces a Dynamic Adapter that concatenates image and text feature vectors, passes them through a bottleneck MLP (with reduction ratio r, ReLU, and sigmoid) to produce a per-sample scaling factor S_d ∈ [0,1], which is then applied via element-wise multiplication to modality-specific base prompts to generate input-adaptive dynamic prompts. A Synergistic Prompting Strategy combines these dynamic prompts with static prompts (a shared cross-modal prompt projected into image and text spaces via learned linear maps) through element-wise addition, balancing modality-specific adaptation with stable pre-trained knowledge. The resulting synergistic prompts propagate layer-wise through the frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone using bottleneck MLPs with GeLU and Layer Normalization. Training updates only the prompts and a final FC classification head under a summed task-specific loss, keeping the entire CLIP encoder frozen for parameter efficiency.

## Results

On the most challenging 'both modalities missing' condition at 50% missing rate (testing set), SyP achieves 55.02 F1-Macro on MM-IMDb, 86.17% top-1 accuracy on UPMC Food-101, and 68.16 AUROC on Hateful Memes, outperforming the strongest baseline DCP by 2.70, 0.93, and 2.14 points respectively. At the extreme 90% missing rate (both missing), SyP still leads with 49.63 F1-Macro (MM-IMDb), 81.03% accuracy (Food-101), and 68.93 AUROC (Hateful Memes), maintaining a 1.59–2.15 point margin over DCP. SyP wins in all 27 dataset–rate–condition combinations reported in Table 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SyP outperforms all five baselines across every dataset, missing rate, and missing condition in Table 1. The strongest baseline, DCP (NeurIPS 2025), is consistently beaten by 1.5–2.7 points on MM-IMDb, 0.9–1.2 points on Food-101, and 2.1–2.9 points on Hateful Memes, with the largest gaps appearing at high missing rates (90%) where DCP's static correlated prompts degrade most. DePT (CVPR 2024) ranks second but trails SyP by 2–4 points, indicating that decoupling base knowledge alone is insufficient without dynamic adaptation. MaPLe (CVPR 2023) and MMP (CVPR 2023) occupy the middle tier, with MMP sometimes outperforming MaPLe on Hateful Memes but underperforming on MM-IMDb. CoOp (IJCV 2022), which uses only input-level prompts, is the weakest baseline in nearly all settings, confirming that layer-wise and dynamic prompting are critical for missing-modality robustness. Notably, on Hateful Memes at 90% both-missing, SyP (68.93 AUROC) exceeds DCP (66.78) by over 2 points, while CoOp drops to 64.32—a 4.6-point gap that underscores the importance of the dynamic adapter's adaptive scaling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt-based paradigm for missing-modality visual recognition established by MMP (Lee et al., CVPR 2023), whose layer-wise prompt propagation architecture is directly adopted as the backbone structure. It departs from the static-prompt lineage (CoOp, MMP, MaPLe, DePT) by introducing a dynamic adapter that computes input-specific scaling factors from concatenated modality features, and further distinguishes itself from the strongest prior method DCP (Shi et al., NeurIPS 2025) through a synergistic strategy that explicitly combines static and dynamic prompts via element-wise addition rather than relying solely on correlated or decoupled prompt designs. The conceptual contribution is the dual-prompt mechanism: static prompts preserve pre-trained cross-modal knowledge while dynamic prompts adapt per-instance to the specific missing-modality condition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, parameter-efficient solution (only prompts and an FC layer are trained) to a common real-world deployment problem, and the consistent gains across three datasets and three missing rates suggest the dynamic adapter generalizes beyond a single scenario. However, the evaluation is limited to two modalities, a single CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone, and three relatively small datasets, leaving open whether the synergistic prompting strategy scales to three or more modalities, larger backbones, or higher-dimensional feature spaces. The paper itself acknowledges extension to multi-modality settings as future work, and the moderate (though consistent) margins over DCP suggest the remaining headroom in this prompt-based family may be narrowing.
