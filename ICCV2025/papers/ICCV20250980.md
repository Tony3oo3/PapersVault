## Problem

Black-box adversarial attacks on T2I models' safety mechanisms have relied on word-substitution search over limited candidate spaces, yielding suboptimal success rates. Gradient-based training would offer superior optimization over the full prompt space, but black-box settings block access to internal parameters, and safety mechanisms return black images upon detecting NSFW content, causing conventional gradient estimation to vanish entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250980/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target text prompt (containing sensitive semantics) and a random prompt as inputs. The target prompt is encoded into a sensitive embedding via a two-layer projection, while the random prompt is processed through a prompt encoder where the sensitive embedding is injected at a middle layer. The resulting learnable embedding is concatenated with the target prompt and fed into a PLM (BERT or T5) to produce a single adversarial text prompt. An auxiliary T2I model without safety mechanisms also generates a reference target image from the target prompt, which is used only for loss computation and not as a direct input to the attack pipeline.

## Method

PLA consists of three components: (1) Sensitive Knowledge Guided Encoding, where a SKE module projects the target prompt's CLIP text embedding into a sensitive embedding via low-then-high projection layers, and this embedding is additively injected into a middle layer of a prompt encoder processing a random prompt to produce a learnable embedding; (2) a PLM (BERT or T5) that takes the concatenation of the learnable embedding and target prompt to generate the adversarial prompt; and (3) a multimodal loss combining CLIP-based text-image cosine similarity (target prompt vs. generated image) and image-image cosine similarity (auxiliary-model target image vs. generated image). For gradient optimization under black-box constraints, PLA extends standard Zeroth-Order Optimization with a history-gradient retention mechanism (Eq. 9) that prevents gradient vanishing when both perturbed parameter evaluations yield black images, and a 'restart' strategy that substitutes Gaussian noise for black images when the gradient collapses to zero at the first optimization step.

## Results

On the I2P nudity dataset, PLA-BERT achieves average ASR-4 of 91.45% (SDv1.5), 90.57% (SDXLv1.0), and 90.82% (SLD) across three post-hoc safety checkers, while PLA-T5 reaches 86.56%, 86.54%, and 90.61% respectively. On the violence dataset, PLA-T5 slightly edges out PLA-BERT, achieving average ASR-4 of 90.41%, 88.95%, and 90.80% across the three victim models. On online services (Stability.ai and DALL-E 3), PLA-BERT and PLA-T5 achieve ASR-4 of 62.15%/54.83% (nudity) and 55.68%/69.70% (violence) on Stability.ai, and 45.09%/38.22% (nudity) and 36.77%/51.98% (violence) on DALL-E 3, outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PLA consistently outperforms all five baselines across every victim model and dataset. The strongest baseline, MMA-Diffusion (white-box, CVPR'24), achieves average ASR-4 of 77.76% on SDv1.5 and 73.30% on SDXLv1.0 for nudity, while PLA-BERT surpasses it by 13.69 and 17.27 percentage points respectively. UnlearnDiffAtk (ECCV'24) is the second-strongest baseline at 73.27% (SDv1.5, nudity), still trailing PLA-BERT by 18.18 points. Ring-A-Bell (ICLR'24) and SneakyPrompt (S&P'24) achieve 50.53% and 37.89% ASR-4 on SDv1.5 nudity, falling 40+ and 53+ points behind PLA-BERT. QF-Attack (CVPR'23) is the weakest baseline at 24.71% ASR-4 on SDv1.5 nudity. On the violence dataset, PLA-T5 slightly outperforms PLA-BERT (e.g., 90.41% vs. 88.62% on SDv1.5), suggesting different PLM 'preferences' for different content categories, but both variants still lead all baselines by 10–20+ points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of black-box adversarial attacks on T2I models (SneakyPrompt, QF-Attack) by replacing their word-substitution search paradigm with gradient-based training, which prior work (MMA-Diffusion, UnlearnDiffAtk) had restricted to white-box settings. The gradient estimation builds directly on Zeroth-Order Optimization (ZOO), but introduces a history-gradient mechanism and a Gaussian-noise 'restart' strategy to handle the unique challenge of black images returned by safety checkers. The multimodal loss (CLIP-based text-image and image-image cosine similarity) and the sensitive knowledge encoding module (projecting target prompt embeddings into a learnable prompt encoder) are the paper's key architectural departures from prior search-based or white-box gradient attacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that current prompt filters and post-hoc safety checkers in widely deployed T2I systems (Stable Diffusion, SDXL, DALL-E 3, Stability.ai) are highly vulnerable to gradient-based black-box attacks, with ASR-4 exceeding 90% in many settings. This has direct practical implications for T2I model developers who must now consider gradient-based adversarial training in their defense design. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video generation or multimodal LLMs, whether the reliance on an auxiliary model without safety mechanisms limits real-world applicability, and whether the large performance gap over search-based baselines reflects a fundamental advantage of gradient methods or simply the weakness of the baselines tested.
