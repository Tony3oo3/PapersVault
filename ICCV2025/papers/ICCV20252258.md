## Problem

In multi-task dense prediction, sufficient cross-task interaction is essential for strong performance, but existing methods face a fundamental trade-off: pairwise interactions preserve full task information at O(n²) complexity, while linear-complexity bridge representations inevitably compress and discard task-specific details. The paper asks how to achieve comprehensive, information-preserving cross-task interaction while maintaining linear computational complexity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252258/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (resized to 448×576 for NYUD-V2 or 512×512 for PASCAL-Context) and simultaneously produces pixel-level predictions at the same spatial resolution for up to six tasks: semantic segmentation, monocular depth, surface normal estimation, object boundary detection, human parsing, and saliency detection. A pretrained Swin-Large Transformer (ImageNet-22K) extracts hierarchical features at four scales, and the Mamba decoder refines them at three scales (H/16, H/8, H/4) before task-specific heads upsample to full resolution.

## Method

BIM employs a Transformer-encoder / Mamba-decoder architecture where the decoder consists of three Mamba Feature Refinement (MFR) blocks, each containing task-specific MSST blocks and a shared BCFR block. The core novelty is the Bidirectional Interaction Scan (BI-Scan), which serializes all task features into a single sequence using two complementary modes—Task-First (spatial serialization per task, then task concatenation) and Position-First (task serialization per spatial position, then spatial concatenation)—and processes them through forward and backward SSM passes, preserving full task information at O(n) complexity. The second contribution, Multi-Scale Scan (MS-Scan), splits channels into N branches with different scanning scales (e.g., {1,4}), tokenizing each branch into non-overlapping patches of varying size before four-way SS2D scanning, then fusing multi-scale outputs back to full resolution. This multi-granularity scene modeling addresses the diverse spatial resolution needs of different tasks while enabling finer cross-task interactions.

## Results

On NYUD-V2, BIM achieves 57.40 mIoU for semantic segmentation, 0.4733 RMSE for depth estimation, 18.55° mErr for surface normal estimation, and 78.72 odsF for boundary detection, outperforming the previous best MTMamba (55.82 mIoU, 0.5066 RMSE, 18.63° mErr, 78.70 odsF) by +1.58 mIoU and −0.0333 RMSE. On PASCAL-Context, BIM reaches 81.25 mIoU (Semseg), 73.15 mIoU (Parsing), 84.14 maxF (Saliency), 14.13° mErr (Normal), and 79.05 odsF (Boundary), surpassing MTMamba on all five tasks. The full BIM model uses 547G FLOPs and 388M parameters, while the lightweight DBIM variant reduces these to 495G FLOPs and 290M parameters while still achieving 56.41 mIoU on NYUD-V2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BIM outperforms the closest competitor MTMamba [16] on every task across both benchmarks: on NYUD-V2 it gains +1.58 mIoU (Semseg), −0.0333 RMSE (Depth), −0.08° mErr (Normal), and +0.02 odsF (Boundary); on PASCAL-Context it gains +0.14 mIoU (Semseg), +0.53 mIoU (Parsing), ties on Saliency (84.14 maxF), −0.01° mErr (Normal), and +0.25 odsF (Boundary). Against Transformer-based methods, BIM leads TaskPrompter [36] by +2.10 mIoU on NYUD-V2 Semseg and +0.36 mIoU on PASCAL-Context Semseg, while also improving Depth RMSE by 0.0419. Compared to CNN-based baselines (Cross-Stitch, PAD-Net, MTI-Net, ATRC), BIM achieves substantially larger margins of 11+ mIoU on Semseg and 2+° on Normal mErr. The lightweight DBIM variant (495G FLOPs, 290M params) still surpasses MTMamba (541G FLOPs, 308M params) on Semseg mIoU (56.41 vs 55.82) and Depth RMSE (0.4789 vs 0.5066) with fewer parameters and lower FLOPs. On PASCAL-Context, BIM also outperforms ASTMT [20] by +13.25 mIoU on Semseg and +11.53 mIoU on Parsing, and exceeds ATRC-BMTAS [2] by +13.58 mIoU on Semseg and +10.22 mIoU on Parsing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MTMamba architecture (Transformer-encoder + Mamba-decoder for multi-task dense prediction) by replacing its single-scale SS2D scanning with two novel mechanisms: BI-Scan for cross-task interaction and MS-Scan for multi-scale scene modeling. The key conceptual departure from prior work is addressing the fundamental trade-off between interaction completeness and computational efficiency: pair-wise methods (Cross-Stitch, PAD-Net) preserve full task information at O(n²) cost, while bridge-representation methods (MTL, Efficient Computation Sharing) achieve O(n) cost but compress task features into a shared bottleneck. BIM's BI-Scan resolves this by constructing task representations as bidirectional sequences that maintain full per-task information while scanning at linear complexity, building directly on VMamba's SS2D four-way scanning pattern and Mamba's selective state space formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the completeness-vs-efficiency trade-off in cross-task interaction is not a hard constraint, which could influence how future multi-task architectures are designed. However, the practical impact is tempered by the modest gains over MTMamba (+1.52 mIoU on NYUD-V2, +0.14 mIoU on PASCAL-Context) and evaluation on only two small-scale datasets (795/654 and ~5K images). Open questions include whether the bidirectional scanning strategy generalizes to larger task sets (beyond 4–5 tasks), whether it transfers to other SSM variants or non-Mamba decoders, and whether the multi-scale scanning benefit persists at higher resolutions or on outdoor-centric benchmarks. The dilated-sampling variant (DBIM) offering competitive performance at reduced FLOPs is a practically useful finding for deployment-constrained settings.
