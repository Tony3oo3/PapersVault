## Problem

Multispectral demosaicing from 4×4 MSFA mosaics is inherently harder than RGB demosaicing from 2×2 Bayer mosaics due to sparser spatial sampling, yet existing MS demosaicing methods ignore the higher spatial fidelity available from co-captured RGB images in dual-camera smartphone setups. Additionally, no publicly available dataset provides high-fidelity ground-truth demosaiced MS images paired with their mosaic inputs, making it impossible to train or fairly evaluate RGB-guided MS demosaicing.

## Input / Output

The method takes two single-channel mosaic raw images at 1440×2160 resolution in camera raw space: a 4×4 MSFA-patterned MS mosaic and a 2×2 Bayer-patterned RGB mosaic, both captured with a 1 cm baseline disparity and Poisson-Gaussian noise (ISO 400). It produces a 16-channel demosaiced MS image at the same spatial resolution. A second scenario extends this to 4× super-resolution, where the MS mosaic is at one-quarter the RGB resolution and the output is a full-resolution 16-channel MS image.

## Method

The framework operates in two stages. First, NAFNet-based networks independently demosaic the MS and RGB mosaics. Second, a cross-spectral multi-scale fusion module transfers high-frequency RGB details into the MS image: a precalibrated 16×3 color conversion matrix maps the demosaiced MS image into a proxy RGB space, and a pre-trained RAFT network estimates dense optical flow between the proxy and the actual RGB image to resolve geometric disparity. A Spectral Alignment Layer (SAL) then applies deformable convolution to multi-scale RGB feature maps, simultaneously compensating for the flow-based geometric offset and adapting to spectral differences, before a NAFNet-based fusion network integrates the aligned RGB features into the MS representation. Training is two-stage: demosaicing networks are trained independently with L2 loss, then the fusion module is trained with L2 loss while the demosaicing networks and flow estimator remain frozen.

## Results

On the Dual-camera RGB-MS Dataset (Scenario 1: asymmetric CFA, same resolution), NAFNet+Ours achieves the best overall performance with PSNR 41.92, SSIM 0.981, and SAM 2.422, outperforming the strongest single-input baseline NAFNet (40.89 PSNR, 2.604 SAM) by +1.03 dB in PSNR. In Scenario 2 (4× MS super-resolution with asymmetric sensor resolution), NAFSR+Ours reaches 37.67 PSNR and 3.567 SAM, surpassing the best single-input baseline NAFSR-L (32.95 PSNR, 4.439 SAM) by +4.72 dB. The method is plug-and-play: integrating the cross-spectral fusion module into MCAN, NAFNet, or Restormer consistently improves all three metrics across both scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against seven baselines across two scenarios. NAFNet+Ours (41.92 PSNR, 2.422 SAM) outperforms all single-input baselines: SSMT (33.97 PSNR, 6.723 SAM) by a large margin, MCAN (39.02, 3.947) by +2.9 dB, NAFNet alone (40.89, 2.604) by +1.03 dB, and Restormer (40.61, 2.643) by +1.31 dB. The hybrid RGB-guided methods DCT (38.44 PSNR) and HSIFN (36.50 PSNR) underperform even the best single-input baseline, suggesting their RGB guidance strategy is less effective for MS demosaicing than the proposed cross-spectral fusion. In Scenario 2 (4× super-resolution), NAFSR+Ours (37.67 PSNR, 3.567 SAM) beats NAFSR-L (32.95, 4.439) by +4.72 dB, and MCAN+Ours (37.55) outperforms MCAN-L (32.87) by +4.68 dB, confirming the plug-and-play benefit. Notably, capacity-increased baselines (NAFNet-L, Restormer-L) actually degrade in Scenario 1 due to overfitting on sparse MS mosaics, whereas in Scenario 2 the additional capacity helps, highlighting the difficulty of the MS demosaicing task without RGB guidance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MS demosaicing task (previously addressed by methods like MCAN and SSMT operating on a single MS mosaic) by introducing a co-captured RGB mosaic as a guidance signal, drawing conceptual parallels to hybrid hyperspectral reconstruction approaches that use RGB to guide HS super-resolution. Methodologically, it repurposes NAFNet as a demosaicing backbone, RAFT for cross-spectral optical flow, and deformable convolution (via a proposed Spectral Alignment Layer) to simultaneously handle geometric disparity and spectral mismatch during multi-scale fusion. The paper also carves new ground on the data side: unlike existing MS datasets (e.g., Glatt et al. 2024, Zhou et al. 2024) that lack high-fidelity ground-truth MS images or suffer from extreme inter-camera disparity, the authors construct a controlled dual-camera dataset with realistic 1 cm baseline and pixel-shift-based ground truth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is significant as dual RGB+MS camera modules begin appearing in mobile devices, and this work provides both a training dataset and a plug-and-play fusion module that can be attached to existing demosaicing backbones (MCAN, NAFNet, Restormer) to yield consistent ~1 dB PSNR gains. However, the method's applicability is currently limited to the narrow scenario of co-located RGB and MS sensors with small, known disparity, and the computational cost (2.53 T MACs for the full pipeline) raises questions about real-time feasibility on resource-constrained mobile hardware. Open questions include generalization to larger disparities, different MSFA configurations beyond 4×4, and whether the cross-spectral flow estimation remains robust under significant illumination or spectral differences between the two cameras.
