## Problem

Existing safety measures for text-to-image models—prompt blacklists, output classifiers, and concept-removal fine-tuning—are either easily bypassed or scale poorly, degrading generative quality beyond ~100 removed concepts. No prior method achieves safety alignment at the scale needed to cover hundreds of harmful concepts across multiple categories without sacrificing image quality on safe prompts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251841/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the aligned model accepts a standard text prompt and produces a single RGB image at the resolution of the underlying diffusion backbone (512×512 for SD v1.5/v2.1, 1024×1024 for SDXL). For unsafe prompts, the output is a safe image that preserves the global layout and composition of the requested scene while removing the specific harmful visual traits (e.g., a knife scene without blood, a bar scene without nudity). No additional inference-time computation is required beyond the standard diffusion sampling loop with the merged LoRA applied.

## Method

AlignGuard trains category-specific LoRA safety experts (rank 4) using a modified DPO objective with two terms: L_align discourages the unsafe image for the unsafe prompt, while L_con preserves generative fidelity by also preferring the safe image for the safe prompt. Training data (CoProV2, 23,690 pairs) is fully synthetic: an LLM generates unsafe prompts from 723 concepts across 7 categories, a second LLM pass strips the unsafe concept to produce a minimally-different safe prompt, and the target T2I model renders both images. The N trained LoRA experts are then combined via Co-Merge, a data-aware strategy that, for each neuron position, selects the weight from whichever expert exhibits the highest activation frequency across K=100 sampled unsafe prompts, yielding a single merged LoRA that generalizes across all safety categories without inter-expert interference.

## Results

On SD v1.5, AlignGuard achieves an Inappropriate Probability (IP) of 0.07 on CoProV2, 0.11 on I2P, and 0.16 on UD, substantially outperforming the next-best baseline ESD-u (0.22, 0.25, 0.21 respectively). Generative quality is well preserved, with FID of 70.96 and CLIPScore of 32.32 on COCO, close to the unaligned baseline (69.77 / 33.52). The method generalizes across backbones: SD v2.1 reaches IP 0.12 on CoProV2 and SDXL reaches IP 0.09, confirming applicability to arbitrary T2I models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AlignGuard dominates all three concept-removal baselines on every IP metric. On CoProV2, AlignGuard (IP 0.07) reduces inappropriate generation by 3× compared to ESD-u (0.22), 4× compared to SLD (0.27), and 5× compared to UCE (0.33). The gap persists on out-of-distribution sets: on I2P, AlignGuard scores 0.11 versus 0.19 (SLD), 0.25 (ESD-u), and 0.30 (UCE); on UD, 0.16 versus 0.21, 0.30, and 0.38. Notably, all baselines collapse when trained on the full 723-concept set, so the comparison uses only 7 category names for baselines while AlignGuard uses the full dataset. For merging strategies, Co-Merge (IP 0.07, FID 70.96, CLIP 32.32) outperforms TIES (0.09, 79.79, 28.85), Model Soups (0.14, 69.83, 33.40), Task Vectors (0.25, 179.60, 4.80), and Git Re-Basin (0.30, 69.98, 33.40), achieving the best safety while preserving generative quality. In adversarial robustness (Table 4), AlignGuard maintains IP below 0.12 across four attack methods, compared to 0.21–0.43 for ESD-u.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DPO-based alignment (originally for LLMs, then adapted to diffusion models by Wallace et al.) to the safety domain for T2I, departing from the refusal-based supervision used in LLM alignment by instead training on paired safe/unsafe images. It builds directly on the CoPro dataset and prompt synthesis pipeline from Latent Guard (Liu et al., ECCV 2024), regenerating prompts with a stronger LLM to improve the inappropriate probability of generated images. The expert-decomposition strategy departs from prior concept-removal methods (ESD, UCE, SLD, MACE) that operate on the full model or use segmentation masks and collapse beyond ~100 concepts, while the Co-Merge procedure introduces a data-aware, activation-frequency-based merging that contrasts with data-agnostic strategies like TIES or Task Vectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

T2I models are widely deployed with minimal safety guardrails, and this paper provides the first practical recipe for removing hundreds of harmful concepts without degrading generation quality, validated across SD 1.5, 2.1, and SDXL. The expert-per-category decomposition plus lightweight LoRA training makes the approach accessible (single-GPU, ~2 hours per expert for SD), and the released CoProV2 dataset lowers the barrier for future safety-alignment research. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video diffusion or to entirely novel safety categories not covered by the 7 predefined groups, and whether the activation-frequency merging heuristic remains optimal as the number of experts grows beyond 7.
