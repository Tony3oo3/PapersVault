## Problem

Existing post-hoc backdoor defenses fail to generalize across diverse trigger types, with no successful defense demonstrated against semantic trigger attacks (e.g., sunglasses, tattoos, masks on faces) that use realistic, naturally-occurring perturbations. Prior methods also show significant performance degradation when attack configurations change (poisoning rate, trigger variance, target mapping), limiting their practical robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251111/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a backdoored model M_B (preAct-ResNet18 or ResNet50), a small clean training subset D_S (~5% of training data), and optionally the target class t (inferred via Neural Cleanse if unknown). It outputs a sanitized model M_C after 35 fine-tuning epochs. An optional large-model mapping module (trained for 5 epochs using DINOv1 ViT-Base 384-dim features) lifts activation-space prototypes into a richer geometric space before PAV computation, after which the large model is no longer needed at inference.

## Method

PGBD computes Prototype Activation Vectors (PAVs) as the displacement from each class prototype to the target class prototype in an intermediate convolutional layer's activation space. During fine-tuning on clean data, a novel sanitization loss L_S penalizes the cosine alignment between the gradient of a prototype loss (MSE distance to the correct class prototype) and the PAV, thereby discouraging sample movement toward the target class while preserving correct-class clustering. The total loss is L = L_o + λL_S (λ=10), combining standard cross-entropy with this directional penalty. Two variants extend the base method: NT-PGBD cycles through all classes as pseudo-targets to eliminate the need for t, and ST-PGBD uses synthesized triggers to construct class-specific PAVs. Large-model mapping via a lightweight autoencoder enriches the activation geometry, improving CA retention at a slight ASR cost.

## Results

PGBD achieves the best Defense Efficacy Measure (DEM, Γ) across all eight attack types averaged over five datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, GTSRB, Tiny-ImageNet, ROF), with mean Γ values of 0.95 (Badnet), 0.90 (Trojan), 0.90 (Blended), 0.97 (Signal), 0.98 (Wanet), 0.86 (Sunglass), 0.98 (Tattoo), and 0.94 (Mask). The method achieves 95–99.9% average ASR reduction across attacks while retaining only ~7% average CA loss. On the new ROF semantic attack dataset, PGBD is the only defense achieving consistent performance across all three triggers (sunglasses, tattoo, mask) with an average DEM of 0.93, compared to 0.84 for the next-best method (FT-SAM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PGBD outperforms all four baselines on DEM (Γ) across every attack type in the averaged results. Against NAD [36], PGBD achieves substantially lower ASR (e.g., 6.99 vs. 69.86 on Trojan; 4.92 vs. 53.85 on Blended) while retaining higher CA. Against I-BAU [77], PGBD wins on both CA and ASR for most attacks (e.g., Signal: ASR 0.31 vs. 18.03; Trojan: CA 70.96 vs. 67.93), though I-BAU is competitive on Wanet (Γ 0.98 vs. 0.98). Against FT-SAM [81], PGBD achieves better ASR reduction on all attacks (e.g., Trojan: 6.99 vs. 68.86; Blended: 4.92 vs. 61.63) while FT-SAM retains slightly higher CA on some settings. Against MCL [76], PGBD matches or beats ASR while retaining significantly more CA (e.g., Badnet: CA 75.17 vs. 64.96; Sunglass: CA 71.67 vs. 33.33), and PGBD is the only method that handles all three semantic triggers consistently (MCL drops to 33.33% CA on Sunglass and 21.10% on Mask). On the Signal (clean-label) attack, PGBD achieves near-perfect ASR reduction (0.31%) where FT-SAM and I-BAU leave 37.50% and 18.03% ASR respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

PGBD adapts the CAV-based debiasing loss from Gupta et al. [22] to the backdoor defense setting, replacing concept-specific activation vectors with class-specific Prototype Activation Vectors (PAVs) that encode the direction from each class prototype toward the target class prototype. Among post-hoc sanitization methods, it is closest in spirit to MCL [76], which also operates in latent/activation space but uses a contrastive loss; PGBD instead uses a directional cosine penalty on the prototype-loss gradient. The paper also builds on the CAV interpretability framework of Kim et al. [30] and leverages large pre-trained model feature spaces (DINOv1) for richer geometric reasoning, following the mapping strategy of Gupta et al. [22].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most significant contribution is the first demonstrated defense against semantic trigger attacks (sunglasses, tattoos, masks), a category where all prior post-hoc methods failed, along with a new public dataset (ROF semantic attack) to benchmark this setting. PGBD's single geometric mechanism scales uniformly across patch, functional, and semantic triggers without per-attack tuning, which is practically valuable for deployment. Limitations include the need for a small clean data subset and (in the base variant) knowledge of the target class, and the method is restricted to supervised classification. Open questions include whether the PAV-alignment loss generalizes to uneven class distributions, self-supervised or reinforcement learning settings, and whether the large-model mapping step is necessary at scale.
