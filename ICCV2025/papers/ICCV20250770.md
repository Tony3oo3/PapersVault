## Problem

Existing LMM-based segmentation methods either serialize masks as boundary point sequences (losing accuracy on complex shapes) or use special tokens whose hidden states are decoded by an external image-conditioned mask decoder (e.g., SAM or Mask2Former). These approaches prevent the LLM from natively learning spatial localization, create an inconsistency between how masks are consumed as input versus generated as output, and introduce heavy architectural dependencies on off-the-shelf segmentation foundation models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250770/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (base resolution 448×448, high-resolution in stage 3) and a text instruction identifying the target object. It autoregressively generates up to 32 discrete mask tokens (drawn from a 1024-entry codebook) that a lightweight mask de-tokenizer converts into a 256×256 binary mask without requiring the original image. Bounding boxes can also be produced directly by the LLM, and the model supports bidirectional conversion between mask tokens and box coordinates within the same generation sequence.

## Method

HiMTok represents a segmentation mask as a sequence of up to 32 hierarchical 1D tokens using a mask tokenizer with causal attention (each token conditioned on all preceding ones), a vector quantization layer, and a mask de-tokenizer, inspired by TiTok's compact 1D image tokenization. A Hierarchical Mask Loss (HML) provides explicit coarse-to-fine supervision by de-tokenizing partial token prefixes at multiple levels and comparing against Gaussian-blurred ground-truth masks at corresponding granularities. Training follows a 3-stage recipe: (1) pre-train HiMTok on mask image reconstruction, (2) jointly train the LMM (InternVL 2.5) and HiMTok at low resolution with mixed segmentation and general data, and (3) fine-tune only the LMM at high resolution with reduced segmentation data to preserve general capabilities. Mask tokens are treated as a new vocabulary for the LLM, enabling native autoregressive segmentation without any external segmentation foundation model, while bidirectional mask-to-box and box-to-mask token ordering in training data leverages multi-task synergy.

## Results

On the primary RES benchmarks (RefCOCO/+/g), LMM_HiMTok-8B achieves 81.1/77.1/75.8 cIoU zero-shot (without any Segmentation Foundation Model), already surpassing SFM-based methods like PSALM (83.6/72.9/73.8) and u-LLaVA (83.0/77.1/77.1) on RefCOCO+ and RefCOCOg. After in-domain finetuning, LMM_HiMTok-8B(ft) reaches 85.0/79.7/80.0 cIoU, setting new state-of-the-art on all three benchmarks without any SFM. On ReasonSeg, the method achieves 60.7 gIoU / 67.0 cIoU (val) and 60.8 gIoU / 66.2 cIoU (test), outperforming CoReS-7B (59.4 gIoU val, 52.4 gIoU test). On open-vocabulary segmentation, it attains 25.0 mIoU on ADE20K-A150 and 82.0 mIoU on PASCAL VOC-20, both best among compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The zero-shot LMM_HiMTok-8B (no SFM) already outperforms all SFM-based hidden-state methods on RefCOCO+ (77.1 vs. PSALM 72.9, u-LLaVA 77.1) and RefCOCOg (75.8 vs. PSALM 73.8, SAM4MLLM 75.5), while remaining competitive on RefCOCO (81.1 vs. PSALM 83.6). After finetuning, LMM_HiMTok-8B(ft) achieves new SOTA on all three RES benchmarks without any SFM: 85.0 cIoU on RefCOCO (vs. PSALM 83.6, u-LLaVA 83.0), 79.7 on RefCOCO+ (vs. u-LLaVA 77.1, PSALM 72.9), and 80.0 on RefCOCOg (vs. u-LLaVA 77.1, SAM4MLLM 75.5). On ReasonSeg, the method surpasses CoReS-7B by 1.3 gIoU on val (60.7 vs. 59.4) and by a large 8.4 gIoU on test (60.8 vs. 52.4), indicating stronger generalization to short, sophisticated instructions. On open-vocabulary segmentation, LMM_HiMTok-8B leads on ADE20K-A150 (25.0 vs. PSALM 18.2 mIoU) and PASCAL VOC-20 (82.0 vs. PSALM 81.3), but trails PSALM on PASCAL Context-59 (43.9 vs. 48.5). On REC (visual grounding), the method improves over its InternVL2.5-8B baseline by 2.6 Acc@0.5 on RefCOCO val (92.9 vs. 90.3) and 2.4 on RefCOCO+ val (87.6 vs. 85.2), demonstrating that joint segmentation training enhances detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant paradigms for LMM-based segmentation—boundary-point sequences (PolyFormer, VistaLLM) and hidden-state prompting of external segmentation foundation models (LISA, GSVA, PSALM, SAM4MLLM)—by introducing a third paradigm where the LLM directly generates discrete mask tokens autoregressively, treating them as a new vocabulary. The tokenizer architecture is derived from TiTok's 1D compact image tokenization, repurposed for binary mask images with a causal attention mechanism that enforces coarse-to-fine hierarchy. The hierarchical mask loss and bidirectional box-mask information flow are novel training strategies not present in prior LMM segmentation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for an image-conditioned mask decoder (SAM, Mask2Former) and its associated original-image re-encoding, HiMTok offers a significantly simpler and more unified architecture for LMM-based segmentation, which could lower the barrier for integrating pixel-level tasks into multimodal models. The finding that mask tokens preceding box predictions act as a 'visual chain-of-thought' that improves grounding accuracy at strict IoU thresholds suggests a broader principle: intermediate dense representations can serve as spatial reasoning steps. Open questions include whether the 32-token budget suffices for very complex multi-object scenes, whether the approach extends to video or 3D segmentation, and whether the performance gap on open-vocabulary tasks (e.g., PC-59) reflects a fundamental limitation of single-class prompting versus joint multi-class modeling.
