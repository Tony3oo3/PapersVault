## Problem

Deploying person ReID models on edge devices with heterogeneous resource constraints requires multiple compact models of different sizes, yet conventional compression methods (distillation, pruning) demand a separate, computationally expensive training run for each target size. This leads to repetitive and cumbersome computations when a family of models spanning a range of widths is needed, with no existing approach that generates all sizes from a single computation pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251518/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained teacher model (ResNet50 from LUPerson or ViT-B/ViT-S from PASS) and person ReID training data (MSMT17, Market1501, CUHK03) as input. It produces a weight chain—an intermediate knowledge carrier with the same depth as the teacher but substantially reduced per-layer width—which can be expanded in O(1) time (by stacking rows and averaging normalization parameters) to yield a student model of any width between the chain's minimum and the teacher's full width, without any additional training. The resulting student models are standard person ReID classifiers deployable on edge hardware.

## Method

OSKT refines teacher knowledge into a weight chain through two stages: (1) row clustering, where weight rows in each teacher layer are clustered (Euclidean distance for CNNs, cosine for ViTs) and cluster centers initialize the chain's rows, and (2) progressive refinement, where the teacher and the smallest student (S-Student, matching the chain's width) are jointly trained with ID loss, hard triplet loss, and an MSE loss that tightens intra-cluster row alignment, with gradients backpropagating through the shared weight chain. The key insight is that training only the two endpoints of the 'bridge' (teacher and S-Student) implicitly trains all intermediate-width models, analogous to lifting a string of beads by its ends. Student generation is a deterministic O(1) operation: refined rows are stacked proportionally to reach the target width, corresponding column weights in subsequent layers are summed to preserve functionality, and normalization (γ,β) pairs are averaged—no further training is required. The framework unifies CNNs and ViTs by abstracting feature-generating units as rows and feature-processing units as columns, making it architecture-agnostic.

## Results

On Market1501, OSKT achieves 75.7 mAP / 89.4 R1 for the smallest ResNet50 student (Res-50-S1), outperforming the best baseline DepGraph (61.3/80.7) by 14.4 mAP. For ViT-based students, OSKT reaches 81.6/91.9 (ViT-B-S1) and 74.2/87.1 (ViT-S-S1), substantially exceeding all baselines. In cross-scenario transfer (e.g., MSMT17→CUHK03), OSKT maintains a consistent 10–20 mAP advantage over DepGraph across all student sizes, with the gap widening as model size decreases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OSKT decisively outperforms all three baselines across every student size and architecture. Against Weight Selection (WTSel), OSKT gains 27.2 mAP on Res-50-S1 (75.7 vs. 48.5) and a staggering 64.8 mAP on ViT-B-S1 (81.6 vs. 16.8), indicating WTSel's row-selection strategy fails to preserve functional knowledge in narrow models. Against KD++ (knowledge distillation), the advantage is even more pronounced: OSKT achieves 74.2 mAP on ViT-S-S1 versus KD++'s 22.6, a 51.6 mAP gap, showing that logit/feature-level distillation is far less effective than direct weight-level transfer for ReID. Against DepGraph (structural pruning), OSKT leads by 14.4 mAP on the smallest ResNet student (75.7 vs. 61.3) and by 66.3 mAP on ViT-B-S1 (81.6 vs. 15.3), though the gap narrows to ~1.3 mAP on the largest ResNet student (Res-50-S5: 87.6 vs. 86.3), suggesting DepGraph performs competitively when the student is close to teacher size. In cross-scenario settings (Tables 3–4), OSKT consistently leads by 10–20 mAP over DepGraph, with the advantage growing as student width shrinks.

## Relation to Prior Work

OSKT carves new ground at the intersection of weight-based knowledge transfer (Net2Net, Weight Selection, Learngene) and model compression for person ReID, but departs from both lines by introducing the weight chain as an intermediate, expandable knowledge carrier. Unlike distillation-based methods (KD++) or pruning-based methods (DepGraph) that require separate, costly training for each target model size, OSKT performs a single refinement pass to produce a weight chain that can be expanded to any intermediate width via an O(1) stacking-and-averaging operation. The method also repurposes the neural identity transformation property (merging identical rows while summing downstream columns) as a theoretical foundation for why the weight chain can faithfully approximate the teacher's function at reduced width.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-shot computation property directly addresses a practical pain point in edge deployment: generating multiple model sizes for heterogeneous devices without repeating expensive training for each size. The scalability analysis (Figure 7) reveals that for very large size ranges, multiple weight chains are needed, which partially qualifies the 'one-shot' claim. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond person ReID to other vision tasks, how it handles non-uniform width scaling across layers, and whether the weight chain concept can be combined with other compression techniques (quantization, sparsity) for further efficiency gains.
