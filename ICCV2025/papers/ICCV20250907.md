## Problem

Existing WSSS methods primarily address under-activation of class activation maps but largely ignore over-activation (bias), where the classifier incorrectly activates non-target regions (e.g., a cabinet as a TV). Because all current approaches ultimately trust the image-level supervision from the CNN classifier, semantic bias propagates into prototypes and persists throughout training. No prior method explicitly models the pixel feature distribution across the entire dataset to detect and correct such bias.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250907/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images with only image-level multi-class labels (no pixel annotations) and produces dense pixel-level segmentation masks via a DeepLabV2 decoder. Internally, it also generates class activation maps (CAMs) at multiple stages: classifier CAMs, prototype CAMs, and GMM CAMs. The pipeline additionally consumes probability maps from a prior WSSS method (e.g., CLIP-ES or SFC) as a BaseSeed for guidance, and operates on ResNet50 feature maps of dimension H×W×D extracted at multiple scales.

## Method

BRNF trains two normalizing flows in a semi-supervised manner: f₁ maps pixel features to a K-component Gaussian mixture (one per class) using labeled pixels (high-activation regions from prototype CAMs) and unlabeled pixels (intermediate-activation regions), while f₂ models a unified single-component foreground distribution. Three mechanisms exploit these distributions: (1) CCNF uses f₁'s GMM posterior as a pixel classifier to generate GMM CAMs that calibrate the classifier CAMs via L1 consistency loss, injecting global dataset-level context to counter image-specific bias; (2) FSCL samples reliable low-bias features from the high-density interval of each class's Gaussian (Mahalanobis distance ≤ d) and inserts them as additional positive anchors in a memory-bank-based InfoNCE contrastive loss, pulling biased features toward the true class centroid; (3) BSDNF treats background noise in the BaseSeed as an outlier detection problem, fitting a 2-component GMM to f₂ likelihoods and re-weighting pixels with high background posterior probability. The overall objective combines classification loss, BaseSeed guidance loss, GMM calibration loss, and contrastive loss, forming a positive feedback loop where improved CAMs yield better pseudo-labels for the normalizing flow, which in turn produces better GMM CAMs.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC 2012, CLIP-ES+BRNF achieves 75.9% mIoU on the validation set and 76.2% on the test set, setting a new state-of-the-art for image-level WSSS. On MS COCO 2014, the method reaches 47.9% mIoU on the validation set. When applied to SFC, the method yields 74.8% (VOC Val) and 47.7% (COCO Val), also surpassing the original SFC by 3.6% and 0.9% respectively. The method also improves seed CAM quality to 72.1% and pseudo-label mask quality to 79.2% mIoU on VOC, outperforming all prior methods in localization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLIP-ES+BRNF outperforms the previous SOTA PSDPM by 1.8% on VOC Val (75.9% vs 74.1%) and 1.3% on VOC Test (76.2% vs 74.9%), and by 0.7% on COCO Val (47.9% vs 47.2%). It also surpasses CPAL (CVPR'24) by 1.4% on VOC Val and 1.1% on COCO Val. Compared to its own base method CLIP-ES, the BRNF module adds 4.8% on both VOC splits and 2.5% on COCO, demonstrating the substantial contribution of the normalizing-flow-based calibration and feature sampling. SFC+BRNF similarly improves over SFC by 3.6% on VOC Val and 0.9% on COCO Val. All other baselines (SEAM, SIPE, MCTformer, ReCAM, CLIMS, SAS, OCR+SEAM, ACR, BECO) are outperformed by at least 3.8% on VOC Val, confirming that the gap to older methods is substantial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the WSSS pipeline that has evolved from CAM-based localization through prototype methods (SIPE, LPCAM) and contrastive learning (RCA) by introducing a distribution-modeling perspective. It builds directly on RCA's memory-bank contrastive learning framework and on prior semi-supervised normalizing flow work (FlowGMM, DDFP), but repurposes the flow as a dataset-wide pixel feature distribution model rather than a generative or OOD-detection tool. The key departure from prior WSSS is treating over-activation/bias as a first-class problem: while existing methods focus on expanding under-activated regions, BRNF uses the learned Gaussian mixture to calibrate classifier CAMs, sample reliable positive anchors, and denoise baseline outputs, forming a positive feedback loop between the CNN and the flow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a recognized but under-explored failure mode in WSSS—semantic bias from image-level labels that persists throughout training—and offers a self-contained correction mechanism that does not require external models or re-annotation. Its plug-and-play design (applicable to any baseline via BaseSeed guidance) makes it practically attractive, and the 4.8% mIoU gain over CLIP-ES on VOC is meaningful. Open questions include whether the normalizing flow's computational overhead scales well to very large or open-vocabulary settings, whether the circular dependency between prototype CAMs and flow training can degrade in early epochs, and whether the distribution-based bias correction generalizes beyond standard closed-set benchmarks.
