## Problem

Large Reconstruction Models (LRMs) for single-view 3D reconstruction are trained exclusively on multi-view synthetic data, which is expensive to scale and biased toward artist-friendly shapes, creating a domain gap with real-world objects. Single-view real images are abundant and representative of the true shape distribution but lack the multi-view supervision that standard LRM training requires, making them unusable without a new training paradigm.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (no camera pose or intrinsics conditioning) and outputs a 3D triplane representation that is volume-rendered at 224×224 resolution from arbitrary SE(3) camera poses. The triplane can be converted to a mesh via marching cubes at 256 resolution. The model operates under a fixed canonical camera pose (identity rotation, fixed translation) shared across all inputs, meaning the input image is always interpreted from the same viewpoint.

## Method

Real3D initializes a TripoSR-based LRM on synthetic multi-view data (Objaverse) with standard rendering losses, then jointly trains on 300K curated real single-view images (WildImages) using two novel unsupervised losses. The pixel-level loss enforces cycle consistency: the model reconstructs the input, renders a novel view, reconstructs again from that view, and renders back to the original viewpoint, with a stop-gradient on the intermediate rendering to prevent trivial solutions and a curriculum that gradually increases the sampled pose variation from 15° to 90°. The semantic-level loss computes CLIP similarity between the input and rendered novel views, but applies it only to the hardest negative (least similar view among m candidates) to avoid the multi-head degenerate solution where all views collapse to copies of the input. An automatic data curation pipeline using DECOLA instance segmentation and Depth Anything monocular depth estimation filters out occluded instances by checking boundary depth consistency, ensuring only unoccluded objects contribute to self-training.

## Results

On MVImgNet (Table 1), Real3D achieves 20.53 dB PSNR, 0.871 SSIM, and 0.107 LPIPS on GT novel views, outperforming the directly comparable TripoSR (19.81/0.864/0.116) and all generative-prior methods. Across all four evaluation sets, Real3D reports average relative improvements of 4.2% PSNR (15% MSE), 6.3% LPIPS, and 13.5% FID over TripoSR. For mesh quality, Real3D achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.275, a 14.3% improvement over TripoSR (0.321) and 30.4% over InstantMesh (0.395). On the in-the-wild WildImages test set (no GT novel views), Real3D leads with 0.892 CLIP similarity, 0.144 LPIPS, and 106.5 FID on semantic metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Real3D outperforms all five baselines on every metric across all four evaluation sets. Against the directly comparable deterministic baseline TripoSR [75], Real3D gains +0.72 dB PSNR (19.80→20.53 on NVS), −0.018 LPIPS, and +0.032 CLIP similarity on MVImgNet, with similar margins on CO3D (+0.74 dB) and OmniObject3D (+0.74 dB). Among generative-prior methods, InstantMesh [91] is the strongest on CLIP (0.873) but lags significantly on NVS PSNR (14.70 vs. 20.53) and FID (153.5 vs. 116.9), while CRM [83] and LGM [73] trail by 2–5 dB PSNR on novel views. OpenLRM [20], the other deterministic baseline, is the closest competitor on NVS PSNR (19.75 vs. 20.53) but still loses by 0.78 dB and 0.005 LPIPS. The paper also shows that Real3D's single-view real-data self-training yields a larger performance gain (+0.72 dB PSNR) than multi-view real-data training of LRM (+0.41 dB), despite using far fewer images (300K vs. ~8M).

## Relation to Prior Work

Real3D builds directly on the LRM family of feed-forward single-view 3D reconstruction models, using a fine-tuned TripoSR as its base architecture with triplane representation and volume rendering. Its key departure from prior LRMs is replacing the reliance on multi-view synthetic supervision with a self-training framework that leverages single-view real images through cycle-consistency pixel losses and CLIP-based semantic losses. This positions the work at the intersection of LRM training and unsupervised 3D learning from real images, but unlike prior unsupervised methods that learn from scratch, Real3D initializes from synthetic multi-view data and jointly trains with real data, preserving 3D accuracy while expanding the shape distribution. The automatic data curation pipeline (DECOLA + Depth Anything for occlusion detection) is a practical addition not seen in prior LRM training recipes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Real3D addresses the fundamental scalability bottleneck of LRMs: multi-view synthetic data is expensive to generate and biased toward artist-friendly shapes, while single-view real images are abundant and representative of real-world geometry. The finding that 300K curated single-view images with self-training yield larger performance gains than 8M multi-view real images with standard rendering losses challenges the assumption that more views per object always provides a stronger training signal. Open questions include whether the self-training framework generalizes to more complex scenes (beyond single objects), how the data curation pipeline scales to truly massive collections, and whether the curriculum and stop-gradient tricks remain necessary as model capacity grows. The approach is likely to influence how future LRMs incorporate real-world data beyond synthetic renderings.
