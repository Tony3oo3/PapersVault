## Problem

Existing ophthalmic MLLMs suffer from annotation granularity fragmentation: global disease labels and fine-grained lesion annotations exist in isolation across different dataset subsets, forcing a 'single-image single-dimension' flat mapping that prevents models from establishing cross-scale semantic associations. This leads to spatial perception errors (e.g., optic disc localization failures) and disrupted diagnostic correlations, because models are never guided through the progressive clinical reasoning pathway of region localization → feature analysis → diagnostic inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250277/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single fundus color image alongside sequential text instruction prompts structured as multi-turn dialogues that progressively build a cognitive chain (e.g., first asking for abnormal region analysis, then requesting diagnostic integration, then verifying fine-grained evidence). Outputs include natural-language diagnostic reports with embedded positional references (e.g., 'optic disc located at [box]'), bounding box coordinates for five anatomical/lesion categories (optic cup, optic disc, hard exudates, cotton-wool spots, microaneurysms), disease classification labels, and multi-turn reasoning responses that explicitly link spatial observations to diagnostic conclusions.

## Method

Fundus-Engine constructs the FundusGen dataset via a three-stage collaborative annotation pipeline: (1) collecting 200K expert-annotated fundus images with fine-grained labels, (2) generating bounding boxes through semi-supervised nnU-Net segmentation followed by DBSCAN spatial clustering, and (3) using GPT-4o to expand discrete annotations into clinically standardized multi-turn dialogue texts that encode a region-localization → feature-analysis → diagnostic-reasoning cognitive chain. FundusExpert is then obtained by fully fine-tuning InternVL2.5-8B (InternViT 300M visual encoder + InternLM 7B language encoder) on this curated instruction data, enabling region-semantic self-reference without external tools. A key empirical finding is a power-law scaling relationship (L ∝ N^0.068, R²=0.972) between data volume and model accuracy, demonstrating that cognitive-aligned annotations substantially improve data utilization efficiency over flat classification-guided annotations.

## Results

FundusExpert (8B) achieves 69.7% accuracy on Fundus-MMBench and 66.7% on GMAI-MMBench (fundus subset), surpassing the 40B MedRegA by 26.6% and GPT-4o by 28.1% on Fundus-MMBench. In zero-shot object localization, FundusExpert reaches IoU of 0.738 on optic disc and 0.632 on optic cup, substantially outperforming MedRegA (0.543/0.302) and InternVL2.5 (0.077/0.036). For medical report generation, FundusExpert attains 77.0% clinical consistency, exceeding GPT-4o's 47.6% by 29.4 percentage points. The paper further reveals a power-law scaling relationship (L ∝ N^0.068, R²=0.972) between data volume and model accuracy on FundusGen.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FundusExpert (8B) decisively outperforms all specialist ophthalmic models: it surpasses MedRegA (40B) by 26.6% on GMAI-MMBench (66.7% vs 40.1%) and by 29.4% on Fundus-MMBench (69.7% vs 40.3%), despite being 5× smaller. Against the CLIP-based RetiZero (59.2% GMAI-MMBench), FundusExpert leads by 7.5% on GMAI-MMBench and 27.7% on Fundus-MMBench, demonstrating that contrastive pretraining alone cannot match instruction-tuned cognitive-chain reasoning. Among generalist models, FundusExpert exceeds GPT-4o (41.6%/57.4%) by 28.1%/9.3% and Gemini-2.0-pro (46.1%/59.0%) by 23.6%/7.7% on the two benchmarks. In zero-shot localization (Table 2), FundusExpert's IoU on optic disc (0.738) is 1.36× that of MedRegA (0.543) and 9.6× that of InternVL2.5 (0.077), while for hard exudates it reaches 0.194 versus 0.038 for MedRegA. In clinical report generation, FundusExpert's 77.0% clinical consistency exceeds GPT-4o's 47.6% by 29.4 points, confirming domain-specific advantage over general-purpose commercial models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'single-image single-dimension' annotation paradigm in ophthalmic MLLMs (VisionFM, RetiZero, MedDr) by integrating global disease classification, local object detection, and fine-grained feature analysis within a single fundus image through the Fundus-Engine system. It builds on the InternVL2.5 vision-language architecture and the nnU-Net segmentation pipeline, but introduces a clinically aligned cognitive chain (region localization → feature analysis → diagnostic reasoning) as the training signal, which prior ophthalmic models lack. The approach also extends region-aware medical MLLMs (MedDr, VisionFM) by achieving region-semantic self-reference without external tools, moving from a decoupled 'detect-then-describe' paradigm to an integrated reasoning process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The revealed scaling law (L ∝ N^0.068, R²=0.972) demonstrating that 10% of cognitively-annotated FundusGen matches 100% of flat classification-annotation data provides a quantitative argument for annotation quality over data volume in medical MLLM construction, with implications beyond ophthalmology. The finding that a lightweight 8B domain-specific model outperforms GPT-4o in generating training data suggests a self-improving data flywheel for specialized medical AI. However, the scaling law is demonstrated on a single dataset and base model, the cognitive chain is somewhat rigid in its multi-turn structure, and generalization to other medical imaging modalities remains untested. Open questions include whether the cognitive chain approach transfers to 3D imaging (CT, MRI) and whether reinforcement learning post-training can further enhance the reasoning depth.
