## Problem

Existing event-based human motion estimation methods predict a fixed, discrete sequence of poses, making them computationally expensive at high temporal resolutions and unable to query poses at arbitrary timestamps. Frame-based cameras further suffer from aliasing and motion blur during fast human motion. This paper asks whether a continuous-time human motion field can be learned directly from an event stream, enabling parallel pose queries at any resolution without re-optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250953/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a stream of asynchronous events from a static event camera, discretized into a 3D event volume tensor E ∈ R^(H×W×T) via bilinear sampling, and assumes the SMPL shape parameter β and initial pose θ₀ are known (e.g., from an RGB-based initializer). It outputs a pair of latent codes (z_l, z_g) computed once from the full event stream, which are then decoded by a frozen time-continuous MLP to yield SMPL body poses θ_t and root translations τ_t at any requested timestamp, producing a full 3D mesh at arbitrary temporal resolution without re-running the encoder.

## Method

EvHuman encodes event volume slices with a CNN, aggregates them through a GRU recurrent network, and projects the terminal hidden state to local and global latent codes in the space of a pre-trained NeMF VAE decoder. The frozen NeMF decoder is a time-implicit MLP that maps (t, z) → θ_t, providing a smooth continuous motion prior; a separate Global Motion Predictor MLP estimates root velocity from predicted poses, joints, and their temporal derivatives, integrated via Euler's method. A key training innovation is an event-based contrast maximization loss that renders vertex optical flow from the continuous motion field, warps events by this flow, and maximizes the variance of the resulting Image of Warped Events, providing self-supervised motion supervision without ground-truth flow. The full loss combines supervised pose/translation/joint losses, a flow cosine loss against a pre-trained event flow network, and the contrast maximization term, trained in a three-stage schedule (GMP pre-training, event encoder training, GMP fine-tuning).

## Results

On MMHPSD, EvHuman achieves PA-MPJPE of 39.16 mm and PEL-MPJPE of 52.23 mm, outperforming the best event-based baseline EventHPE (43.90 / 54.96 mm) and the best image-based method HMR 2.0 (60.63 / 79.53 mm). On the new BEAHM dataset at 120 FPS, EvHuman reaches MPJPE 49.74 mm, PA-MPJPE 30.05 mm, PEL-MPJPE 41.06 mm, and PCKh@0.5 of 0.92, improving joint errors by 23.8% over prior event-based methods while reducing computational time by 69%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvHuman outperforms all five baselines on both datasets across every reported metric. Against the strongest event-based learning baseline EventHPE (which uses both images and events), EvHuman improves PA-MPJPE by 4.74 mm on MMHPSD (39.16 vs. 43.90) and by 8.86 mm on BEAHM (30.05 vs. 38.91), while also achieving a higher PCKh@0.5 (0.92 vs. 0.86 on BEAHM). Against the best image-based method HMR 2.0, EvHuman reduces PA-MPJPE by 21.47 mm on MMHPSD and 21.07 mm on BEAHM, demonstrating that event-based continuous-time modeling captures fast motions that frame-based cameras miss due to motion blur and limited temporal resolution. The optimization-based EventCap baseline lags significantly (PA-MPJPE 62.62 on MMHPSD, 65.81 on BEAHM) and is up to 6750× slower than real-time. DHP19, which relies on ground-truth depth for 3D lifting, achieves competitive PCKh@0.5 (0.86 on BEAHM) but higher PA-MPJPE (42.39 vs. 30.05), confirming that EvHuman's end-to-end event-based regression without depth priors is more accurate in 3D joint localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on NeMF [19], which introduced a neural motion field (an MLP decoder in a VAE latent space) for continuous-time human motion representation, and repurposes its pretrained decoder as a frozen motion prior. It departs from prior event-based HMR methods (EventCap, EventHPE, DHP19) that predict a fixed sequence of discrete SMPL poses, instead encoding the entire event stream once into latent codes and decoding poses at arbitrary timestamps. The novel event-based contrast maximization loss extends the Image-of-Warped-Events framework from generic optical flow to the structured flow induced by a parametric human mesh, providing self-supervised motion supervision without ground-truth flow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The continuous-time formulation eliminates the linear scaling of inference cost with the number of query poses, making high-frame-rate human motion estimation from events practically feasible for the first time. The BEAHM dataset, with hardware-synchronized 120 FPS mesh labels and 40 diverse motions, fills a critical gap in high-speed event-based human motion benchmarks that prior datasets (15–100 FPS, software-synced) could not address. Open questions remain around the reliance on voxelized event representations, the need for initial pose/shape estimation, and the static-camera assumption; extending to dynamic cameras and reducing end-to-end latency are natural next steps.
