## Problem

Most data-driven trajectory predictors directly regress or classify future positions without explicitly reasoning about an agent's behavioral intentions, limiting interpretability and prediction confidence in multimodal scenarios. The paper asks whether trajectory prediction can be reframed from a planning perspective—where high-level intention reasoning precedes low-level motion generation—and whether RL-based paradigms (specifically Inverse Reinforcement Learning) can provide the reward heuristics needed to make such intention reasoning tractable and efficient for onboard systems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250501/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes vectorized agent states (positions, velocities, headings over T_h past timestamps) and HD map polylines (lane centerlines with segment attributes), all normalized into a target-centric coordinate system via translation and rotation. Beyond the K multimodal trajectories and their probabilities, the pipeline produces intermediate structured outputs: a dense H×W reward distribution over a grid map, L sampled Grid-based Reasoning Traversal (GRT) sequences as discrete intention priors, and binary spatial-temporal occupancy grid maps over T_f future timestamps. The GRT sequences are quantized to a grid resolution d and sampled with L ≫ K to capture the full multimodal intention distribution before clustering down to K final modes.

## Method

FiM follows a 'First Reasoning, Then Forecasting' pipeline with three core components. First, a Query-centric IRL (QIRL) module aggregates vectorized scene features into learnable grid-shaped queries via cross-attention, then applies a grid-based MaxEnt IRL algorithm to infer a reward distribution; policy rollouts over this reward heuristic sample L plausible GRT intention sequences, whose associated grid tokens serve as behavior priors. Second, a hierarchical DETR-like decoder uses these priors to generate anchor-free trajectory proposals via cross-attention, clusters them into K modes with K-means, and refines each mode through anchor-based DETR-style decoding. Third, a Bi-Mamba decoder with dual CLS tokens (one prepended, one appended to the trajectory query) performs bidirectional selective state-space scanning to capture sequential dependencies in trajectory states, followed by a mode self-attention module for multimodal interaction. An auxiliary U-Net-based S-T OGM head predicts future binary occupancy from the fused grid tokens and reward, providing dense interaction supervision that improves feature fusion. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a combined loss of IRL, focal BCE (OGM), Huber regression, and max-margin classification.

## Results

On the Argoverse 1 test set (single model), FiM achieves MR6=0.1087, minADE6=0.7795, minFDE6=1.1199, and a Brier score of 0.5732, the best Brier score among all single-model entries. With model ensembling, FiM further improves to MR6=0.1250 and Brier score=0.6218, outperforming HPNet (0.5912) and Wayformer (0.5792). On the nuScenes leaderboard, FiM achieves minADE5=0.88 and MR10=0.23, surpassing all prior entries including UniTraj (minADE5=0.96) and Goal-LBP (minADE5=1.02). On the customized Argoverse 2 benchmark, FiM w/GRT-L reaches minFDE6=0.528 and Brier score=0.603, beating QCNet (0.551/0.629) and DeMo (0.543/0.626).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Argoverse 1 (single model), FiM achieves the best Brier score (0.5732) and MR6 (0.1087), outperforming HiVT (0.6729/0.1267), SceneTransformer (0.6547/0.1255), and DSP (0.6398/0.1303) by substantial margins on confidence-aware metrics, though HPNet retains a slight edge on minFDE6 (1.0856 vs. 1.1199). In the ensemble group, FiM's Brier score of 0.5732 beats HPNet (0.5912), Wayformer (0.5792), and MultiPath++ (0.5788), confirming the reasoning module's advantage in prediction confidence. On Argoverse 2, all FiM variants (GRT-S/M/L) surpass both QCNet and DeMo on minFDE6 and Brier score, with longer-term intention supervision (GRT-L, 60 timestamps) yielding the best Brier score of 0.603 versus 0.629 for QCNet. On nuScenes, FiM dominates the leaderboard with minADE5=0.88, beating the previous best UniTraj (0.96) by 8.3% and Goal-LBP (1.02) by 13.7%, while also achieving the lowest MR10 (0.23 vs. 0.27 for Goal-LBP). Overall, FiM's primary advantage is in confidence-aware metrics (Brier score, brier-minFDE6) rather than raw displacement error, consistent with the paper's emphasis on reliable multimodal forecasting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repositions trajectory prediction within the planning literature by introducing a 'First Reasoning, Then Forecasting' pipeline, where a MaxEnt IRL module (Ziebart et al., 2008) adapted into a query-centric form (QIRL) infers a reward distribution that serves as an intention prior before trajectory generation. Architecturally, it combines the DETR-style hierarchical decoder (Carion et al., 2020) with a bidirectional Mamba state-space model (Gu & Dao, 2023) for trajectory refinement, while inheriting the vectorized, query-centric scene encoding paradigm from QCNet (Zhou et al., 2023). The key departure from prior motion predictors is the explicit use of IRL-derived reward heuristics to sample multiple grid-based intention sequences (GRTs) via policy rollouts, rather than relying solely on data-driven regression or endpoint classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a recognized gap in motion forecasting: most SOTA predictors achieve strong displacement metrics but offer limited interpretability and weaker confidence calibration, both critical for safety-critical autonomous driving. The consistent Brier score improvements across Argoverse 1, Argoverse 2, and nuScenes suggest that explicit intention reasoning genuinely improves prediction reliability beyond what direct regression achieves. Open questions remain about whether the grid-level abstraction (GRT) captures sufficiently rich intention structure for highly complex or out-of-distribution scenarios, and whether the IRL component truly encodes causal behavioral reasoning or merely learns a compact reward landscape correlated with training distributions. The computational cost of L parallel policy rollouts and the inner-loop IRL optimization also raise practical deployment questions for real-time onboard systems.
