key_contribution: COIN is a three-step annotation-free framework that recovers error-free cell instances via unsupervised semantic segmentation with optimal transport, scores instance-level confidence through model-SAM consistency (eliminating the need for ground-truth annotations), and progressively expands confident instances via recursive self-distillation, achieving state-of-the-art performance that surpasses semi- and weakly-supervised methods on MoNuSeg and TNBC without any image-related annotations.
limitations: The method incurs a 50% increase in training time (e.g., 8→14 hours on MoNuSeg) due to the recursive self-distillation loop and SAM-based scoring, and its reliance on SAM's point-prompt sensitivity—though mitigated by the consistency scoring—remains a potential failure mode. Evaluation is limited to 2D H&E-stained nuclei-scale instances across six datasets, with no assessment on whole-slide images, multi-class cell typing, or 3D volumetric data.

surprise_factor: The most notable finding is that an annotation-free method surpasses point- and box-annotated semi/weakly-supervised baselines across all metrics on MoNuSeg and TNBC, which inverts the typical supervision-quality tradeoff. ## Problem

Existing unsupervised cell instance segmentation (UCIS) methods, such as SSA and PSM, fail to produce even a single prediction with 100% accuracy—a phenomenon the authors term the 'absence of error-free instances.' Without reliable self-identification of correct predictions, these models cannot effectively self-improve, leading to missed detections and incomplete cell boundaries. The paper asks whether error-free instances can be recovered and leveraged for progressive self-improvement without any image-related annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251197/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB histopathological images (H×W×3) as input and is model-agnostic, operating on top of any pre-trained UCIS baseline (e.g., SSA or PSM). It produces three outputs: a cell instance segmentation mask, a cell boundary (edge) mask via a Canny-based edge decoder, and per-instance confidence scores. During training, a tri-valued pseudo-mask is constructed where 1 marks confident cells, 0 marks confident background, and −1 marks rejected instances that are excluded from the loss computation.

## Method

COIN is a three-step annotation-free framework. Step 1 performs pixel-level cell propagation by combining unsupervised semantic segmentation (MAE/DINOv2 features with class-level average pooling) and optimal transport (OT) to refine overlapping activations, ensuring at least one error-free instance exists per image. Step 2 applies instance-level confidence scoring: instances are extracted via watershed, SAM is prompted with each instance's center point to generate a pseudo-GT mask, and the IoU between the model's propagated mask and the SAM output serves as a confidence score; a non-parametric threshold (mean + std of all scores) selects only highly consistent instances. Step 3 performs recursive self-distillation using binary and edge pseudo-masks from the accepted instances, with cross-entropy and Dice losses, iteratively expanding the set of confident cells as the model parameters update. The key insight is that model-SAM consistency acts as a proxy for ground-truth accuracy, enabling error-free instance identification without any manual annotations.

## Results

On the MoNuSeg test set, SSA+COIN achieves AJI 0.580, PQ 0.536, IoU 0.776, and Dice 0.794, more than doubling the AJI of the SSA baseline (0.259) and surpassing the best weakly-supervised method InstaSAM (AJI 0.574) and the best semi-supervised method TextDiff (AJI 0.510). On TNBC, SSA+COIN reaches AJI 0.568 and Dice 0.774, outperforming TextDiff (AJI 0.464, Dice 0.666) by +10.4%p in AJI. PSM+COIN similarly improves over PSM by +10.8%p in AJI on MoNuSeg (0.471→0.579). Across four additional datasets (BRCA, CPM-17, CryoNuSeg, PanNuke), COIN consistently improves SSA by +8.3 to +16.6%p in AJI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two primary UCIS baselines, COIN delivers the largest gains: SSA's AJI more than doubles on MoNuSeg (0.259→0.580, +32.1%p) and TNBC (0.273→0.568, +29.5%p), while PSM's AJI improves by +10.8%p on MoNuSeg (0.471→0.579). Among weakly-supervised methods that require point or box annotations, COIN (annotation-free) surpasses InstaSAM (AJI 0.574) by +0.6%p and PROnet (AJI 0.555) by +2.5%p on MoNuSeg, and outperforms SPPNet (AJI 0.497) by +8.3%p. Against semi-supervised baselines, SSA+COIN beats TextDiff (mask+text supervision) by +7.0%p AJI on MoNuSeg and +10.4%p on TNBC, and edges out CDCL (mask supervision) on Dice (0.794 vs. 0.782). General-purpose unsupervised instance segmentation methods (MaskCut, CutLER, ProMerge) perform near-zero on cell benchmarks (AJI ≤ 0.002), confirming that cell-specific propagation and scoring are essential. Older annotation-free tools (CellProfiler, Fiji, Hou et al.) trail COIN by 8–35%p in AJI on MoNuSeg.

## Relation to Prior Work

COIN builds directly on two existing unsupervised cell instance segmentation (UCIS) baselines—SSA (Sahasrabudhe et al., MICCAI 2020) and PSM (Chen et al., MICCAI 2023)—which rely on scale classification and geometric augmentation respectively, and on SAM (Kirillov et al., 2023) as a prompt-based segmentation oracle. The key departure is that prior SAM-based cell segmentation methods (InstaSAM, SPPNet) require manual point or box prompts, whereas COIN repurposes SAM in a fully annotation-free loop: the model's own propagated masks generate the point prompts, and a consistency score between the model prediction and SAM's output serves as a proxy for ground-truth quality. The pixel-level propagation step further departs from prior UCIS by combining unsupervised semantic segmentation (MAE/DINOv2) with optimal transport to refine overlapping activations, addressing what the authors term the 'absence of error-free instances' that plagues augmentation-based self-supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COIN demonstrates that a fully annotation-free pipeline can match or exceed semi- and weakly-supervised methods (which still require point or box annotations) on standard cell segmentation benchmarks, which is practically significant given the cost of pixel-level histopathology annotation. The confidence-scoring mechanism offers a reusable pattern for uncertainty estimation in iterative self-training loops beyond cell segmentation. However, the 50% increase in training time, the dependence on SAM's prompt sensitivity (mitigated but not eliminated by scoring), and evaluation limited to nuclei-scale instances in 2D H&E images leave open questions about scalability to whole-slide images, multi-class cell typing, or 3D volumetric data.
