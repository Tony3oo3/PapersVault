## Problem

Existing Video VAEs that compress temporal redundancy still fail to faithfully reconstruct videos with large motion, suffering from motion ghosting, temporal flickering, blurring of fine details (faces, hands, text), and motion stuttering. This limitation degrades the latent representations fed to downstream Latent Video Diffusion Models, particularly in high-dynamic scenarios. The paper asks whether architecture design choices and cross-modal information can jointly resolve these large-motion reconstruction failures.

## Input / Output

The encoder takes an RGB video tensor of shape C×T×H×W (16-frame clips sampled at stride 4 during training) and optionally a text caption, producing a two-level latent: a spatially compressed Z1 (c×T×H/8×W/8) and a further temporally compressed Z2 (c'×T/4×H/8×W/8). The decoder inverts this to reconstruct the full-resolution video. At inference the text guidance is optional. The model also accepts single RGB images by masking the temporal autoencoder, enabling joint image-video operation. Evaluation uses 256×256 (WebVid), 480×864 (Inter4K), and a self-constructed large-motion set of 80 videos selected by optical-flow score.

## Method

VideoVAE+ introduces a two-stage spatiotemporal architecture: a temporal-aware spatial autoencoder (SD VAE inflated to 3D convolutions with kernel (1,3,3) plus an extra temporal conv and a temporal attention in the middle block) compresses only the spatial dimension 8×, followed by a lightweight asymmetric temporal autoencoder that compresses the temporal dimension 4×. Cross-modal text guidance is injected via cross-attention layers at every ResNet block (visual tokens as Q/V, Flan-T5 embeddings as K, with patch sizes 8×8→1×1 across layers), improving fine-detail recovery and training convergence without requiring text at inference. The model is trained jointly on images and videos (8:2 ratio) using standard Conv3D (no causal convolution), with a combined loss of pixel+perceptual reconstruction, KL divergence on the innermost latent Z2 only, and a 3D GAN adversarial term. Initialization is from SD1.4 (4-channel) or SD3.5 (16-channel), and the GAN loss is enabled after a 50K-step warmup.

## Results

On the 16-channel setting, Cross-Modal VAE achieves PSNR 34.44 dB, SSIM 0.9403, LPIPS 0.0271, and FVD 6.36 on WebVid, outperforming the strongest baseline VidTok (PSNR 34.35, FVD 9.66) and HunyuanVAE (PSNR 33.44, FVD 8.77). On the challenging Large Motion test set, Cross-Modal VAE achieves FVD 43.49, substantially below VidTok (52.95), HunyuanVAE (51.47), and IV-VAE (59.54). In the 4-channel setting, Cross-Modal VAE reaches PSNR 30.35 dB and FVD 18.11 on WebVid, improving over VidTok (PSNR 30.25, FVD 34.00) by over 1 dB in PSNR and nearly halving FVD. User study MOS scores confirm the advantage: 4.5/4.4 for temporal smoothness/overall quality (16-ch) vs. 4.06/4.02 for the best baseline EasyAnimate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the 16-channel setting, Cross-Modal VAE outperforms all 8 baselines on WebVid PSNR (34.44 vs. VidTok's 34.35, HunyuanVAE's 33.44) and FVD (6.36 vs. VidTok's 9.66, HunyuanVAE's 8.77), with the largest FVD gap over CogVideoX-VAE (14.06) and Cosmos-Tokenizer (24.71). On the Large Motion test set, the advantage is most pronounced: Cross-Modal VAE achieves FVD 43.49, beating VidTok (52.95) by ~9 points and HunyuanVAE (51.47) by ~8 points, while also leading in PSNR (32.29) and LPIPS (0.0408). In the 4-channel setting, Cross-Modal VAE leads on WebVid PSNR (30.35 vs. VidTok 30.25) and dramatically reduces FVD (18.11 vs. VidTok 34.00, Open-Sora 90.89). The Video VAE without cross-modal guidance still beats most baselines, confirming the architecture's strength, but the text-guided variant adds a consistent 0.1–0.3 dB PSNR and 1–3 FVD improvement across all settings. In the user study, both 4-channel (4.6/4.54) and 16-channel (4.5/4.4) models receive the highest MOS for temporal smoothness and overall quality, surpassing EasyAnimate (4.06/4.02) and CogVideoX-VAE (3.86/3.48) in the 16-channel group.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of video VAEs that inflate pre-trained Stable Diffusion 2D VAEs into 3D video autoencoders (Open Sora, Open Sora Plan, CV-VAE, CogVideoX, Easy Animate). It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) a two-stage spatiotemporal architecture that separates temporal-aware spatial compression from a dedicated temporal autoencoder, rather than performing simultaneous or purely sequential compression; and (2) the introduction of cross-modal text guidance via Flan-T5 embeddings injected through cross-attention layers, making it the first text-conditioned video VAE. Notably, the architecture avoids causal convolutions used by MagVitV2, OD-VAE, and OPS-VAE, relying instead on standard 3D convolutions with a joint image-video training strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Video VAEs are a foundational bottleneck in the latent video diffusion pipeline, and the paper demonstrates that large-motion reconstruction—a persistent failure mode across existing VAEs—can be substantially improved through architectural design and cross-modal conditioning. The text-guided VAE concept is particularly interesting as it suggests that auxiliary semantic information can compensate for information lost during aggressive compression, potentially enabling higher compression ratios without quality degradation. Open questions include whether text guidance remains beneficial at inference when captions are unavailable, how the approach scales to higher resolutions and longer videos, and whether the two-stage design generalizes to other compression ratios beyond 4×8×8 and 8×8×8.
