## Problem

Current MLLMs perform significantly worse on composite images (charts, collages, diagrams, tables, code, image-text composites) than on natural images, yet high-quality image-caption training data exists almost exclusively for natural images. Existing CI datasets (ChartQA, ScienceQA) are formatted as QA pairs rather than captions, leaving a critical gap in the vision-language alignment stage of MLLM training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250176/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The CompCap framework consumes heterogeneous raw data—image-caption pairs, tabular data, code snippets, Mermaid diagram code, and free text—along with randomly sampled configuration parameters (layout grids, chart types, color schemes, margins) to synthesize composite images. The output is a paired composite image and a detailed LLM-generated caption structured in bullet-point format that describes each visual element, its spatial position, and inter-element relationships. The resulting CompCap-118K dataset spans six CI categories with average caption lengths ranging from 221 characters (Image-Text) to 2044 characters (Diagram).

## Method

CompCap is a modular synthesis framework with six type-specific pipelines, each combining automated rendering tools (OpenCV/PIL for collages, Matplotlib/Plotly for charts, Mermaid+Selenium for diagrams, Carbon+Selenium for code, Augraphy for image-text) with LLM-based caption generation. Captions are produced via carefully structured prompts that include a coordinate system for spatial reference, bullet-point layout metadata, and in-context examples to enforce accuracy and detail. For collages specifically, three retrieval strategies (random, similarity-based via Dino-v2/CLIP embeddings, entity-based) and two layout schemes (grid and auto) create diverse compositions, while randomization of margins, padding, and backgrounds adds visual variety. The key training insight is that adding CompCap-118K to the SFT stage (replacing an equal number of original SFT samples to keep total data constant) yields consistent gains of 1.7–2.9% across eleven benchmarks, with the largest improvements on CI-heavy tasks like ChartQA, DocVQA, and InfoVQA, confirming that caption data drives vision-language alignment more effectively than instruction data alone.

## Results

Across eleven MLLM benchmarks, CompCap-4B/7B/13B achieve average scores of 58.9%, 64.5%, and 68.5%, respectively, outperforming their same-architecture baselines (xGen-MM-inst.-4B*, LLaVA-NeXT-Vicuna-7B/13B) by 1.7%, 2.0%, and 2.9% on average. Gains are most pronounced on CI-heavy benchmarks: ChartQA improves by +2.6/+5.4/+5.4 points, WebSRC by +5.2/+3.3/+4.0 points, and LLaVABench by +2.8/+3.1/+6.3 points across the 4B/7B/13B models. Notably, despite no math-specific data in CompCap-118K, MathVista scores improve by +2.4/+2.1/+2.6 points, suggesting transferable CI understanding benefits mathematical reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against same-architecture models trained on identical PT data and equivalent SFT sample counts, isolating the effect of adding CompCap-118K caption data. CompCap-4B beats xGen-MM-inst.-4B* on 10 of 11 benchmarks, with the largest margin on WebSRC (+5.2) and LLaVABench (+2.8). CompCap-7B outperforms LLaVA-NeXT-Vicuna-7B on 10 of 11 benchmarks (slight dip on SEEDBench: 70.5 vs 71.2), with the strongest gains on ChartQA (+5.4), OCRBench (+3.4), and LLaVABench (+3.1). CompCap-13B dominates LLaVA-NeXT-Vicuna-13B on all 11 benchmarks, with the most substantial improvements on LLaVABench (+6.3), ChartQA (+5.4), and WebSRC (+4.0). Against SoTA models of similar scale (e.g., Cambrian-8B at 65.9 avg, Cambrian-13B at 67.2 avg), CompCap-7B (64.5) and CompCap-13B (68.5) are competitive despite using 10–100× fewer training samples. The consistent pattern across all three model sizes is that CI-focused benchmarks (ChartQA, DocVQA, InfoVQA, WebSRC, OCRBench) see larger absolute gains than NI-focused benchmarks (SEEDBench, TextVQA, MMBench), confirming that the caption data specifically targets the CI understanding gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the synthetic data generation pipeline for MLLMs (LLaVA, BLIP, DVQA, PlotQA, MapQA, Donut) by shifting focus from instruction/VQA pair synthesis to high-quality caption generation specifically for composite images. It builds directly on the LLaVA-NeXT and xGen-MM architectures and the established finding (ShareGPT4V, MM1) that caption quality drives vision-language alignment, but identifies and addresses the gap that such caption data has been almost exclusively curated for natural images. The CompCap framework departs from prior synthetic data work by using LLMs to generate detailed, structured captions for programmatically rendered CIs (charts, collages, diagrams, code, tables, image-text composites) rather than template-based or GPT-generated instructions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, extensible recipe for generating CI-caption training data that MLLM developers can adopt to improve their models' handling of real-world synthetic visuals (screenshots, infographics, charts). The ablation showing that caption data outperforms instruction data for CI understanding, and that caption-derived knowledge transfers across CI types (ChartQA → DocVQA/InfoVQA), offers a useful data-mixing principle. However, the absolute gains are modest (1.7–2.9% average), the dataset is entirely synthetic and may not capture the full messiness of real-world CIs, and the framework's scalability to novel CI types remains to be demonstrated. Open questions include whether larger or more diverse CI caption data yields diminishing returns, and whether the approach generalizes to video or multi-page document understanding.
