## Problem

Category-agnostic pose estimation (CAPE) has traditionally required support images with annotated keypoints, introducing inconsistency and impractical annotation overhead. Even recent text-based methods like CapeX still depend on skeletal structure representations and limited text encoders that cannot leverage rich semantic priors. The paper asks whether detailed text descriptions alone can fully condition CAPE, eliminating all auxiliary support queries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250680/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB query image (resized to 224×224) and a text prompt containing manually crafted spatial and relational descriptions of target keypoints (e.g., 'next to the fifth wheel and in front of the second wheel'), with no support images or skeletal graphs. Output is 2D keypoint coordinates as normalized floating-point values in the format [0.abc, 0.def], decoded autoregressively token-by-token over K=3 decimal digits per coordinate. Multiple keypoints are predicted per image in a multi-round conversation format, with a default of 4 rounds per instruction.

## Method

CapeLLM pairs a DINOv2 visual encoder with LLaMA 3.1 via a linear projection layer, feeding visual tokens prepended to text tokens into the LLM, with LoRA (rank 8) applied to both modules. Two training strategies are introduced: Fixed-Round (grouping all category keypoints into fixed-size sets paired with each image) and Dynamic-Round (variable keypoint counts per pair to encourage cross-keypoint reasoning). At inference, a cumulative reasoning mechanism prepends previously predicted keypoint coordinates to subsequent prompts, allowing the model to exploit spatial context from already-estimated joints. The floating-point token-by-token decoding implicitly models an arbitrary conditional density over keypoint positions rather than a fixed-variance Gaussian, and the paper shows that detailed spatial/relational descriptions in the text prompt yield up to 6%p mPCK improvement over vague descriptions.

## Results

On the MP-100 benchmark, CapeLLM achieves a mean PCK@0.2 of 92.60 across five splits, outperforming the 5-shot GraphCAPE (92.04) by 0.56%p and CapeX (91.62) by ~1%p, all while being completely support-free. In the 'Only Query Images' setting (unique queries without support pairs), CapeLLM scores 92.60 versus CapeX's 91.59. The method also achieves 78.43 PCK@0.05 and 91.98 mPCK under the default fixed-round training configuration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CapeLLM is compared against three baselines: GraphCAPE [9] (support-based, 1-shot and 5-shot), CapeX [26] (text-based but still relies on skeletal structure), and LocLLM-style [31] (category-specific LLM approach). Against GraphCAPE, CapeLLM surpasses the 1-shot variant (90.52) by over 2%p and the 5-shot variant (92.04) by 0.56%p in mean PCK@0.2, despite providing strictly less input information (no support images or annotations). Against CapeX, CapeLLM gains approximately 1%p (92.60 vs. 91.62) in the support-query setting and a similar margin in the query-only setting, while being fully free from skeletal representations. Against the LocLLM-style baseline, CapeLLM's fixed-round training yields an 8%p improvement in mPCK (91.98 vs. 84.00) and a 2.13%p gain in PCK@0.20 (96.98 vs. 94.85), demonstrating that the tailored training strategy is critical for category-agnostic generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

CapeLLM adapts the visual-encoder + linear-projection + LLM architecture pattern established by Visual Instruction Tuning (Liu et al.) and LocLLM (Wang et al.) to the category-agnostic pose estimation setting, replacing the category-specific human keypoint task with a fully open-vocabulary one. It departs fundamentally from the dominant CAPE paradigm (GraphCAPE, CapeX) that conditions on support images, skeletal graphs, or at minimum keypoint names via a text encoder; instead, it uses a full MLLM to consume rich natural-language spatial descriptions as the sole conditioning signal. The theoretical justification for its floating-point token decoding as a flexible density model draws on the decoding-based regression framework of Song and Bahri (2025), distinguishing it from the Gaussian heatmap regression used by virtually all prior CAPE methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that detailed text descriptions alone can match or exceed support-based CAPE methods, CapeLLM challenges the field's assumption that auxiliary support images or skeletal structures are necessary for robust keypoint estimation on unseen categories. The cumulative-reasoning inference mechanism and the implicit distribution modeling via token decoding offer practical tools that could transfer to other dense-prediction tasks. However, all evidence is confined to the MP-100 benchmark with a single LLM (LLaMA 3.1) and visual encoder (DINOv2), leaving open questions about scalability to larger category spaces, 3D pose, and real-time deployment. The modest margin over 5-shot GraphCAPE (~0.5%p) and the reliance on manually crafted descriptions limit immediate generalizability.
