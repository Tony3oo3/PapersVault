## Problem

End-to-end MM-LLMs for long video understanding are overwhelmed by redundant frames as video length grows to hours, while existing hierarchical key-frame extraction methods disrupt temporal coherence and struggle to adapt to complex tree structures. The paper tackles two coupled challenges: mitigating interference from massive irrelevant content in hour-long videos, and enabling a model to dynamically navigate hierarchical structures while precisely grounding key frames relevant to a query.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250137/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a long video (uniformly sampled into N frames, spanning tens of minutes to hours) and a free-form text question as input. It produces a set of grounded key frames (sampled to 32 frames to match baseline protocols) and a final text answer generated by a VLM. The video is consumed hierarchically—first as scene-level segments, then as clustered event nodes, and finally as individual frames—rather than as a flat frame sequence, preserving temporal ordering throughout the tree.

## Method

VideoMiner iteratively applies scene segmentation (via Bhattacharyya distance on normalized grayscale histograms), question-conditioned VLM captioning, and DBSCAN clustering to build a hierarchical tree from coarse video segments to fine-grained frames. T-GRPO, a tree-adapted variant of GRPO, trains a policy model that outputs accept/continue/delete decisions at each node, guided by event captions, the user question, and node depth. The reward function decomposes into node-level components (format compliance, Gaussian-shaped length reward, and action-specific rewards) multiplied by a tree-level correctness reward, enabling the policy to weigh intermediate decisions against final outcomes. A tree growth auxin parameter (λ_auxin) adaptively regulates expansion depth by modulating the relative reward for continue versus accept/delete actions, balancing exploration thoroughness against efficiency. Training with T-GRPO spontaneously induces chain-of-thought reasoning in the policy model, improving key-frame localization accuracy across all long-video benchmarks.

## Results

VideoMiner (Qwen2-VL-7B) achieves state-of-the-art performance across all long-video understanding sub-tasks: 66.2 on EgoSchema, 52.2 on Video-MME Long, 58.6 on LongVideoBench (180–600s), 49.3 on LongVideoBench (900–3600s), and 65.1 on MLVU M-Avg. On shorter video segments (Video-MME Short, LongVideoBench 8–60s), end-to-end baselines like LLaVA-Video retain a small edge due to their dedicated video pre-training, but VideoMiner still outperforms all external-structure baselines. The performance gap over the strongest existing baseline (LifelongMemory, GPT-4) widens with video length, reaching +4.1 on EgoSchema and +7.3 on LongVideoBench (900–3600s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest existing external-structure baseline, LifelongMemory (GPT-4), VideoMiner leads by +2.1 on EgoSchema, +5.6 on Video-MME Long, +8.3 on LongVideoBench (180–600s), +7.3 on LongVideoBench (900–3600s), and +11.2 on MLVU M-Avg. VideoTree (CVPR'25), the closest architectural competitor, is outperformed by +6.4 on EgoSchema, +12.9 on Video-MME Long, +10.2 on LongVideoBench (180–600s), and +13.7 on MLVU. Among end-to-end LVLMs, LLaVA-Video (Qwen2-7B) retains an advantage on short-video segments (e.g., 72.0 vs. 65.6 on Video-MME Short; 69.8 vs. 65.1 on LongVideoBench 8–15s), but VideoMiner surpasses it on all long-video segments (e.g., 52.2 vs. 49.3 on Video-MME Long; 49.3 vs. 45.5 on LongVideoBench 900–3600s; 65.1 vs. 62.1 on MLVU). The paper notes that the short-video gap is partly attributable to different base models and dedicated video pre-training, and that with the same base model VideoMiner's plug-in architecture delivers a clear gain. Overall, VideoMiner achieves the best score in every long-video column of Table 1 while remaining competitive on short-video tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoMiner builds directly on GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) by adapting its group-relative reward mechanism to a hierarchical tree structure, introducing node-level and tree-level rewards that guide key-frame exploration. It is most closely related to VideoTree, which also constructs a tree for long-video reasoning, but departs from it by replacing heuristic correlation scoring with a reinforcement-learning policy model that adaptively decides node expansion. The scene-segmentation and DBSCAN clustering pipeline draws on standard video processing techniques, while the tree-growth-auxin mechanism (regulating exploration depth via reward ratios) is a novel control strategy not present in prior tree-based video methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Long-video understanding remains a bottleneck for MM-LLMs, and VideoMiner offers a practical plug-in architecture that achieves SOTA across four major benchmarks without requiring end-to-end retraining of the base model. The spontaneous emergence of chain-of-thought reasoning from T-GRPO training is an intriguing side effect that suggests RL-based key-frame selection may serve as a lightweight proxy for reasoning enhancement. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-hour videos with highly dynamic content, how sensitive performance is to the VLM used for captioning, and whether the RL-trained policy generalizes across domains without retraining.
