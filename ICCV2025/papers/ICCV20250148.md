## Problem

Existing 3D scene interpolation methods rely on a small-motion assumption that allows correspondences to be found via local nearest-neighbor search between adjacent timesteps. When inter-frame motion is large—as in athletes in motion, vehicles on a highway, or sparse temporal captures—this assumption breaks down: local neighborhoods become unreliable and global point-to-point matching becomes fundamentally ill-posed, causing methods to produce criss-cross matches and implausible trajectories.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view images at two timesteps (t=0 and t=1), from which 3D Gaussian Splatting models are pre-trained for 30k iterations. DINO features are extracted from the images and PCA-projected to 4 dimensions, serving as semantic input alongside 3D Gaussian means and RGB colors. The output consists of interpolated novel-view renderings at arbitrary intermediate timesteps, dense per-point SE(3) motion trajectories, and—uniquely among baselines—extrapolated renderings beyond the observed timesteps (t<0 or t>1).

## Method

GMC replaces direct point-to-point matching with two unary potential fields (MLPs), one per timestep, that predict SE(3) transformations (quaternion rotation + translation) mapping each set of Gaussians into a shared canonical space where corresponding points occupy identical locations. The MLPs take PCA-DINO features and Gaussian means as input, leveraging the inductive bias of neural networks to ensure semantically similar points receive coherent transformations, thereby resolving the ill-posed global matching problem. Training uses a bidirectional energy-based loss that minimizes a weighted combination of color, feature, and canonical-space positional distances between nearest neighbors, combined with a local isometry loss to preserve local geometric rigidity. A joint refinement stage then backpropagates an L1+LPIPS rendering loss through both the Gaussian models and the potential fields simultaneously. Interpolation is performed via SLERP on relative rotations and linear interpolation on relative translations, with extrapolation achieved by extending the interpolation parameter beyond [0,1].

## Results

On synthetic global-motion scenes (Table 1), GMC achieves average SI-FID of 224.42, SI-EMD of 149.38, and SI-MPED of 16.47, substantially outperforming Dynamic Gaussian (283.53 / 521.51 / 824.50) and PAPR in Motion (315.75 / 246.71 / 114.45). On real-world global-motion scenes (Table 3), GMC scores 166.33 SI-FID, 70.85 SI-EMD, and 32.78 SI-MPED, again leading all baselines by wide margins. On the local-motion setting (Table 2), GMC matches or slightly exceeds PAPR in Motion (112.62 vs 117.94 SI-FID; 9.27 vs 9.38 SI-MPED), confirming no regression under small motion. Notably, 4DGS and Deformable 3DGS fail to produce valid results on most large-motion scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GMC is compared against four baselines: two deformation-field methods (4DGS and Deformable 3DGS) and two iterative-refinement methods (Dynamic Gaussian and PAPR in Motion). The deformation-field baselines fail to converge on most large-motion scenes (marked "–" in Table 1), making direct comparison impossible; where they do succeed (e.g., Boat, Microwave, Seagull), GMC still achieves lower SI-FID and SI-MPED. Against Dynamic Gaussian, GMC wins on all three metrics across all settings: on synthetic global-motion, SI-MPED drops from 824.50 to 16.47 (a 50× improvement), and on real-world scenes SI-EMD drops from 530.10 to 70.85. Against PAPR in Motion, GMC also dominates: SI-FID improves from 315.75 to 224.42 and SI-MPED from 114.45 to 16.47 on synthetic scenes, and from 390.02 to 32.78 on real-world scenes. On the local-motion setting where the small-motion assumption holds, GMC matches PAPR in Motion closely (SI-FID 112.62 vs 117.94; SI-MPED 9.27 vs 9.38), confirming no performance regression when motion is small.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and Feature 3DGS as the underlying geometric representation, but departs fundamentally from all prior dynamic scene interpolation methods (4DGS, Deformable 3DGS, Dynamic Gaussian, PAPR in Motion) by rejecting their shared small-motion assumption. Instead of predicting per-point displacements via deformation fields or iteratively refining point positions from adjacent timesteps, GMC learns SE(3) unary potential fields (MLPs) that map Gaussians into a shared canonical space, where correspondence becomes a well-posed nearest-neighbor problem. The use of PCA-projected DINO features as semantic input to these fields extends the 2D semantic correspondence literature into the 3D dynamic setting, where prior feature-based methods have not been applied.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GMC addresses a practical gap: real-world capture of fast-moving objects (athletes, vehicles) routinely violates the small-motion assumption that underpins all existing 3DGS-based interpolation methods, and the complete failure of deformation-field approaches on such scenes (they cannot even converge) highlights a fundamental limitation in the field. The extrapolation capability is genuinely new and opens the door to predicting future states from sparse captures, though the paper only demonstrates this qualitatively. Open questions include whether the two-timestep canonical-space formulation scales to multi-timestep sequences, whether the method handles occlusion and topology changes, and whether the proxy evaluation metrics (SI-FID, SI-EMD, SI-MPED) adequately capture perceptual quality when no ground-truth trajectory exists.
