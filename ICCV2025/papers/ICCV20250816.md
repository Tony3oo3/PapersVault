## Problem

Distribution shifts among clients significantly degrade Federated Learning performance, and most existing remedies operate during the FL training loop (sampling, aggregation, local training). Pre-training approaches such as dataset distillation or synthetic data generation are computationally expensive, limited in scope, and incompatible with other FL stages. This paper asks whether a lightweight, one-time client index generated *before* training can simultaneously improve all three key FL stages without altering the training pipeline itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250816/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client's local image-label pairs are first encoded by a frozen CLIP model into image embeddings D_{i,j} and label embeddings L_{i,j}; the DSA-IGN network then operates on these pre-computed embeddings rather than raw pixels. The output is a fixed 2d_i-dimensional client index β_i = [β_i^f; β_i^l] per client (feature and label sub-vectors), computed as the mean of per-sample indices. This index is generated once and remains static throughout all subsequent FL communication rounds, serving as a lookup key for sampling probabilities, aggregation weights, and local-training orthogonality constraints.

## Method

Client2Vec leverages CLIP's cross-modal alignment to separate label-relevant from client-specific information: the CLIP image embedding D_{i,j} is decomposed by a three-layer transformer (DSA-IGN) into a sample feature index u_{i,j}^f (client-specific, label-independent) and a data encoding z_{i,j} (label-sensitive), enforced by an orthogonality loss, a cosine-alignment loss between z_{i,j} and the CLIP label embedding, a reconstruction loss on D_{i,j}, and a diversity loss to prevent index collapse. The client index is the average of all sample indices, and DSA-IGN can be trained either centrally (GLOBAL: one batch per client uploaded to server) or via FedAvg (FEDERATED). The resulting index then drives three enhancements: (1) a greedy client-sampling rule that preferentially selects clients similar to those sampled in the previous round, (2) MWU-inspired aggregation weights that up-weight clients with high historical similarity, and (3) a local-training objective that projects local features into the index space and enforces orthogonality to the client feature index, thereby stripping distribution-bias information from learned representations.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18), Client2Vec with all three case studies (sampling + aggregation + local training) yields the largest gains: FedAvg improves from 42.24% to 59.29% (FEDERATED) or 58.28% (GLOBAL), FedAvgM from 42.56% to 63.48%/69.37%, and FedDyn from 37.22% to 69.10%/70.59%. On DomainNet (MobileNetV2), improvements are more moderate, e.g., FedAvg rises from 46.31% to 56.43% (FEDERATED) or 57.43% (GLOBAL), while FedIIR goes from 49.32% to 52.74%/51.86%. On Shakespeare (LSTM), gains are small (0.45–0.63 percentage points), reflecting the lower heterogeneity of the text task. The maximum improvement (MI) column ranges from 0.45 (Shakespeare/Moon) to 33.37 (CIFAR-10/FedDyn), confirming that the benefit scales with the severity of distribution shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Client2Vec is not a standalone FL algorithm but an orthogonal enhancement applied on top of existing baselines, so every comparison is 'baseline vs. baseline + Client2Vec.' Across all six baselines (FedAvg, FedAvgM, FedDyn, Moon, FedLC, FedIIR) and all three datasets, Client2Vec with all three case studies consistently outperforms the unmodified baseline. The largest relative gains appear on CIFAR-10 with label-shift partitioning: FedDyn jumps 33.37 points (37.22→70.59 GLOBAL), FedAvgM gains 26.81 points, and FedAvg gains 17.05 points, all driven primarily by the improved local training (case study iii). On DomainNet with feature shifts, FedAvgM benefits most (+12.84), while FedIIR—a method already designed for feature shift—shows the smallest gain (+7.73), suggesting partial overlap in what the two methods address. On Shakespeare, all baselines improve by only 0.45–0.63 points, indicating limited headroom when heterogeneity is mild. Notably, the FEDERATED index-generation strategy matches or nearly matches the GLOBAL strategy in final accuracy for local training and aggregation, but the GLOBAL strategy gives a slight edge in client sampling (e.g., 50.40 vs. 50.51 for FedAvg on Shakespeare), attributed to its more discriminative inter-client distances.

## Relation to Prior Work

Client2Vec draws a direct conceptual lineage from Word2Vec (entity-to-vector mapping in NLP) and variational domain indexing (VDI) in domain generalization, repurposing the idea of a learned identity vector to represent each FL client's local distribution. It departs from the dominant FL literature that modifies training stages (sampling, aggregation, local SGD) by introducing a decoupled pre-training mechanism, and from prior 'before-training' approaches (dataset distillation, synthetic pseudo-data) by being lightweight, compatible with all training stages simultaneously, and requiring only a single index vector per client. The DSA-IGN network leverages CLIP's cross-modal alignment as a fixed encoding space, with a transformer-based decomposition that enforces orthogonality between label-relevant and client-specific components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular design means Client2Vec can be plugged into any existing FL algorithm without modifying its core, and the orthogonality-based local training trick (case study 3) offers a simple, interpretable regularization that yields the largest gains. Key limitations include the information bottleneck of compressing all client distributional information into a single vector, the dependence on a pre-trained cross-modal model (CLIP) for encoding, and evaluation limited to three datasets with at most 100 clients. Open questions raised by the authors include whether client indices can benefit personalization and clustering in FL, and how to overcome the representational bottleneck for highly heterogeneous client populations.
