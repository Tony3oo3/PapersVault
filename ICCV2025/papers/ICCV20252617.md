## Problem

Deep Gaussian denoisers trained on a fixed noise distribution generalize poorly to out-of-distribution noise (e.g., Poisson, real-world camera noise), while existing single-image denoising methods either require thousands of training iterations or sacrifice accuracy by shrinking the network to two layers. The paper asks whether a pre-trained Gaussian denoiser can be rapidly adapted to arbitrary unknown noise with minimal test-time computation, without requiring clean references or noise-type knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252617/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single noisy RGB image (tested at 256×256 patches) with unknown noise type and level, and outputs a denoised image of the same spatial dimensions. During adaptation, the noisy image is processed to build a pixel bank tensor of size h×w×c×p, from which pseudo-instance pairs are randomly sampled each iteration; the final denoised output is produced by running the fine-tuned network on the original noisy image. No clean reference, noise statistics, or external dataset is required at test time.

## Method

The framework has three stages: (1) self-supervised pre-training of a Neighbor2Neighbor CNN on ImageNet with σ=25 Gaussian noise, establishing a 'deep denoising prior'; (2) for each test image, constructing a pixel bank via non-local self-similarity search — for every pixel, a k×k patch is extracted, M similar non-local patches are found within a W×W window, the patches are arranged into a k²×c×M matrix, and the p rows most similar to the center row are selected to yield p similar pixels per location; (3) fine-tuning the pre-trained denoiser using the Noise2Noise loss by randomly sampling pairs of pseudo-instances from the pixel bank (C(p,2) pairs per spatial location per iteration). This self-similarity-based sampling produces training pairs with high clean-content similarity and diverse random perturbations, enabling convergence in ~10 iterations (vs. 2000+ for prior methods) while resisting overfitting, and the pre-trained initialization provides a strong prior that accelerates adaptation to both in-distribution and out-of-distribution noise.

## Results

On Gaussian denoising (σ=25, Kodak24), the proposed method achieves 30.75 dB PSNR, outperforming MPI (30.37 dB), ZS-N2N (29.07 dB), and the pre-trained NB2NB model (29.70 dB) without requiring knowledge of the noise level. For out-of-distribution Poisson noise (λ=25, Kodak24), it reaches 28.89 dB, surpassing MPI (27.98 dB), ZS-N2N (27.49 dB), and S2S (28.31 dB). On real-world PolyU camera noise, the method achieves 36.71 dB, the best among all compared methods, edging out MPI (36.55 dB) and S2S (35.97 dB). The method also achieves 35.49 dB on SIDD, competitive with MPI (35.68 dB) and MASH (35.34 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all dataset-free baselines (DIP, S2S, ZS-N2N, MPI, BM3D) across all noise types and levels without requiring knowledge of the noise distribution. Against MPI (the strongest competitor), the method gains +0.38 dB on Gaussian σ=25 (Kodak24), +2.06 dB on Gaussian σ=50, +0.91 dB on Poisson λ=25, and +2.41 dB on Poisson λ=10, with the largest margins at higher noise levels where MPI degrades. Compared to ZS-N2N, the method improves by +1.68 dB (Gaussian σ=25) and +1.72 dB (Poisson λ=25) on Kodak24, while also being significantly faster (0.07 s vs. 9.04 s per image). On real-world PolyU noise, the method achieves 36.71 dB, beating MPI (36.55 dB) and S2S (35.97 dB); on SIDD, it scores 35.49 dB, slightly below MPI (35.68 dB) but well above ZS-N2N (28.16 dB) and S2S (30.18 dB). The method also surpasses the supervised DnCNN (σ known) on OOD settings: e.g., 30.75 vs. 17.95 dB on Gaussian σ=50 (Kodak24), demonstrating that test-time adaptation with a pre-trained prior far exceeds what a fixed supervised model can achieve under distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of self-supervised denoising (Neighbor2Neighbor, Noise2Noise), single-image denoising (DIP, Self2Self, ZS-N2N, MPI), and test-time adaptation (TENT, SS-TTA). It builds directly on Neighbor2Neighbor's architecture and training recipe for the pre-trained Gaussian denoiser and on the Noise2Noise framework for fine-tuning, but departs from prior single-image methods by replacing downsampling-based pair construction with a non-local self-similarity search that builds a 'pixel bank' of similar pixels. Conceptually, it extends the plug-and-play denoising idea (Venkatakrishnan et al.)—where a Gaussian model serves as a reusable prior within iterative algorithms—into the deep-learning TTA setting, reframing a noise-specific denoiser as a transferable image prior that needs only a handful of fine-tuning steps to handle new noise distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: a single Gaussian denoiser pre-trained on one noise level can be rapidly adapted (10 iterations, ~0.07 s) to Poisson, real-world, or higher-level Gaussian noise without retraining, reducing the need for noise-specific models. The pixel-bank construction also addresses a known weakness of downsampling-based methods (overfitting, limited training diversity), as shown by the sustained performance in Table 3 where ZS-N2N degrades sharply after 50 iterations while the proposed method plateaus. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger images or video, whether it generalizes to transformer-based backbones, and how the pixel-bank search cost behaves on high-resolution inputs. The method still depends on a quality pre-trained model and the non-local search adds a preprocessing step that may be non-trivial at scale.
