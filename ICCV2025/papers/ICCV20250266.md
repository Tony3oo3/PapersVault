## Problem

Generating professional-quality house-tour videos requires both smooth, human-like 3D camera trajectories and spatially-grounded real estate descriptions, yet existing vision-language models lack geometric reasoning and existing 3D vision-language datasets focus on object-level captions rather than architectural layout, materials, and ambiance. This paper introduces the joint task of spatially-aware 3D camera trajectory and textual summary generation from sparse posed images, filling a gap between trajectory planning and scene-level language generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250266/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse set of Nc RGB images with known camera poses (3D translation + quaternion rotation) as input, where poses are sampled every 5th–15th frame of a ground-truth tour video. It produces a dense N-frame camera trajectory (N > Nc) anchored to the known poses, a scene-level real estate text summary emphasizing architectural features and spatial layout, and a rendered video via 3D Gaussian Splatting. The text summary is conditioned on both the visual observations and 3D spatial features extracted from the trajectory generation process, making it a trimodal (language + vision + 3D localization) output.

## Method

The system has two coupled components. First, a Residual Diffuser extends the Diffuser framework by learning residuals over a Catmull-Rom spline (translations) and SLERP (rotations) interpolation rather than absolute trajectories, which is critical because each property has a different floor plan. A U-Net with 1D convolutions denoises the residual signal conditioned on sparsely known poses (treated as an inpainting mask via Dirac constraints), trained with a custom loss combining L2 distance on densely sampled spline points for translations and geodesic distance on SO(3) for rotations. Second, Qwen2-VL-3D integrates 3D spatial information as a third modality into the VLM: bottleneck features from the Residual Diffuser are concatenated with denoised camera poses, passed through a linear adapter, and inserted as special spatial tokens alongside visual tokens. Training proceeds in two stages—LoRA fine-tuning for real estate language style, then spatial token integration—allowing the model to generate summaries that reference specific architectural elements at their correct spatial locations.

## Results

On the HouseTour test set, HouseTour (Residual Diffuser + Qwen2-VL-3D) achieves a Spatio-Linguistic Score (SLS) of 76.0, outperforming the composed baseline (Catmull-Rom Spline + Qwen2-VL-7B SFT) at 71.7. For trajectory generation, the Residual Diffuser reduces Euclidean distance error to 73.9 cm versus 106.2 cm (Catmull-Rom) and 145.8 cm (Linear Interpolation), with R@1m recall of 69.4% vs. 64.7% and 59.8% respectively. For scene summarization, Qwen2-VL-3D achieves a Bradley-Terry preference score of 0.79, surpassing the fine-tuned Qwen2-VL-7B (0.71) and zero-shot baselines (0.04).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Catmull-Rom Spline (the strongest trajectory baseline), the Residual Diffuser wins on all translation and rotation metrics: Euclidean distance drops from 106.2 cm to 73.9 cm (−31%), R@1m recall improves from 64.7% to 69.4%, and geodesic rotation error decreases from 0.10 to 0.09 rad. The advantage grows with sparser pose sampling (every 15th frame: 371.2 cm vs. 510.9 cm Euclidean distance). For scene summarization, Qwen2-VL-3D outperforms both zero-shot LLaVA-OneVision-7B (BT 0.04) and zero-shot Qwen2-VL-7B (BT 0.04) by a wide margin, and also beats the supervised-fine-tuned Qwen2-VL-7B (BT 0.71) with a Bradley-Terry score of 0.79, confirming that 3D spatial conditioning adds value beyond language-style fine-tuning alone. The end-to-end SLS metric (76.0 vs. 71.7) further validates that jointly modeling trajectory and text generation yields gains over composing the best per-task methods independently.

## Relation to Prior Work

The method extends the Diffuser framework [31] for trajectory planning but departs in two key ways: it models human-like motion as a residual to spline interpolation rather than learning absolute trajectories (necessary because each property has a unique layout), and it operates purely in observation space with a custom loss incorporating geodesic distance on SO(3) for rotations. For language generation, it builds on Qwen2-VL [56] via LoRA fine-tuning and introduces a third spatial modality by injecting Residual Diffuser bottleneck features as positional tokens, going beyond prior 3D vision-language work (ScanRefer, ReferIt3D, Scan2Cap) that focuses on object-level relationships rather than scene-level architectural narratives. The rendering pipeline leverages 3D Gaussian Splatting [35] to visualize trajectories, and the dataset fills a gap left by existing 3D visio-linguistic benchmarks whose trajectories are optimized for reconstruction (close-up, jerky) rather than human-like navigation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work enables automated, professional-quality house-tour video creation from sparse smartphone photos, removing the need for expert videographers and high-end equipment—a practical pipeline for the $3.43T U.S. real estate market. The HouseTour dataset (1,200+ videos with verified 3D reconstructions and professionally written descriptions) addresses a clear gap in 3D visio-linguistic resources that previously lacked human-like trajectories and real-estate-style narratives. Open questions include whether VLM outputs can jointly guide the trajectory diffusion process, and how to extend Gaussian Splatting to fill gaps between sparse views without hallucinating non-existent content—both acknowledged as future work by the authors.
