## Problem

Existing text-guided image inpainting methods are constrained to 1K resolution (SDXL-inpainting) or at most 2K (HD-Painter), and as resolution increases, models exhibit diminished attention to unmasked regions and produce inconsistent, artifact-laden completions. This paper tackles the scalability gap by enabling 4K+ resolution inpainting while simultaneously preserving global content coherence and text-prompt alignment—two challenges that intensify with resolution and texture complexity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251769/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a masked RGB image, a binary mask, a hierarchical text prompt (a global scene-level prompt plus VLM-generated patch-specific prompts), and a dynamically selected reference patch as inputs, and produces a 4K+ resolution inpainted image. Operationally, high-resolution images (2048×2048) are split into 1024×1024 patches for Stage 2 processing, with the reference patch chosen per target patch via CLIP cosine similarity against the Stage 1 output. The output preserves the unmasked regions exactly (via Blended Diffusion) while synthesizing new content in masked areas at native resolution.

## Method

Patch-Adapter is a two-stage, parameter-efficient framework grafted onto a frozen SDXL-inpainting backbone. Stage 1 introduces a Dual Context Adapter (DCA) that augments each attention layer with learnable Q/K/V projections: it first isolates unmasked background features via element-wise masking to form a background-enhanced query, then computes two complementary attention maps (unmask-guided text attention and text-refined mask attention) whose outputs are residually added to the original attention. Stage 2 introduces a Reference Patch Adapter (RPA) that, for each target patch, dynamically selects the most semantically similar reference patch via CLIP, propagates it through the Stage 1 U-Net to extract reference features, and injects them as ref-conditioned key/value projections with residual addition—enabling cross-patch contextual awareness without full-model fine-tuning. A hierarchical text prompting mechanism (global scene prompt + VLM-generated local patch prompts) and ControlNet-based structural guidance further enforce inter-patch consistency during high-resolution refinement.

## Results

On the photo-concept-bucket dataset (2,000 high-resolution images), Patch-Adapter achieves the best scores across all four metrics: FID of 14.594, Aesthetic Score of 6.021, CLIP Score of 26.806, and LPIPS of 0.153. The closest competitor, SDXL Inpainting, records FID 17.660, Aesthetic 6.012, CLIP 26.771, and LPIPS 0.208. On OpenImages (Stage 1, 1K resolution), the method also leads all baselines in both random-mask/global-prompt and segmentation-mask/local-prompt settings, e.g., FID 12.167 vs. 13.326 (SDXL Inpainting) and FID 9.427 vs. 9.565 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Patch-Adapter outperforms all four baselines on every metric in the main high-resolution comparison (Table 1). Against the strongest baseline, SDXL Inpainting, it reduces FID from 17.660 to 14.594 (−17.4%), lowers LPIPS from 0.208 to 0.153 (−26.4%), and marginally improves Aesthetic Score (6.021 vs. 6.012) and CLIP Score (26.806 vs. 26.771). The gap widens substantially against earlier methods: HD-Painter (FID 22.262, LPIPS 0.230), PowerPaint (FID 23.652, LPIPS 0.308), and BrushNet (FID 36.334, LPIPS 0.307) all trail by large margins, with BrushNet showing the most pronounced degradation in both perceptual quality and reconstruction fidelity. On the OpenImages Stage-1 evaluation (Table 2), the advantage over SDXL Inpainting is smaller but consistent (e.g., FID 12.167 vs. 13.326 under random masks; FID 9.427 vs. 9.565 under segmentation masks), confirming that the Dual Context Adapter alone already improves contextual coherence at 1K resolution before the patch-based refinement stage is applied.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SDXL-inpainting as its frozen backbone, extending the line of adapter-based inpainting methods (BrushNet, PowerPaint, IP-Adapter) that graft lightweight trainable parameters onto pretrained diffusion models. It departs from prior work by introducing a two-stage hierarchical pipeline: a Dual Context Adapter for global structural coherence at 1K, followed by a Reference Patch Adapter that performs patch-wise 4K refinement with cross-patch attention—a mechanism absent from all prior inpainting methods. The hierarchical text prompting (global scene prompt + VLM-generated local patch prompts) and the use of ControlNet for structural guidance in Stage 2 further distinguish it from single-prompt, single-resolution approaches like HD-Painter, which achieves only 2K via a separate super-resolution stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that 4K+ text-guided inpainting is achievable through a lightweight adapter rather than full model retraining or a separate super-resolution pipeline, which has practical implications for deployment cost and flexibility. The patch-based reference mechanism addresses a genuine scalability gap—prior methods degrade in consistency as resolution increases—but the authors acknowledge the trade-off of increased inference time from processing multiple patches. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other base models beyond SDXL, how it behaves with very large or irregularly shaped masks spanning many patches, and whether the inference overhead can be reduced without sacrificing cross-patch coherence.
