## Problem

Knowledge distillation for multimodal sentiment analysis with missing modalities suffers from two critical issues: representation inconsistency between the full-modality teacher and partial-modality student (naive MSE fails to preserve high-order semantic relationships), and optimization instability where easier modality combinations (e.g., language+video) dominate training while harder ones (e.g., audio-only) are underutilized. Existing reconstruction-based methods require training 2^m−1 separate models, and prior KD approaches do not jointly address both the correlation structure gap and the modality difficulty imbalance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted per-modality features (BERT or GloVe for language, COVAREP or wav2vec for audio, OpenFace for video) along with a binary indicator vector marking which modalities are present; missing modalities are zeroed out. The modality combination is scalarized via binary-to-decimal encoding (e.g., [1,1,1]→7) to index per-combination weights. Output is a sentiment score (regression, k=1, evaluated with MAE) or an emotion class label (classification, k>1, evaluated with cross-entropy), supporting both MSA and MER tasks within a single unified model.

## Method

CMAD comprises a Perceiver-based modality encoder, a transformer fusion network, and a classifier, trained via knowledge distillation from a full-modality teacher (TEA). The Correlation-Aware Feature Distillation (CAFD) module enforces multi-level alignment: in addition to standard MSE feature matching, it computes cosine-similarity correlation matrices and applies a positive-pair contrastive loss (normalizing diagonal discrepancies) plus an all-pair KL-divergence loss with temperature smoothing between student-teacher and teacher-teacher correlations. The Modality-Aware Regularization (MAR) module implements a two-stage curriculum: during the first G epochs all modality combinations are weighted equally while per-sample difficulties are recorded; thereafter, a difficulty metric (student loss minus teacher loss, clipped at zero to eliminate intrinsic sample difficulty) is aggregated per modality combination to compute adaptive weights Ψ that up-weight harder combinations in both the task loss and the DKD-based auxiliary loss, preventing easy modalities from overshadowing difficult ones.

## Results

On MOSEI, CMAD achieves an average ACC/F1 of 77.0/76.4 across all seven modality combinations, outperforming the best customized baseline UMDF (76.0/75.1) and the unified baseline MMANet (75.9/73.2). On IEMOCAP, CMAD reaches 79.2/77.8 average, surpassing the teacher model TEA (78.1/76.7) and MMANet (76.4/72.6) by a notable margin. On CHERMA, CMAD attains 65.6/65.1, exceeding the second-best UMDF (64.2/63.6) by 1.4 points. The paper reports average improvements of 1.0% (MOSEI), 4.4% (IEMOCAP), 1.9% (MUStARD), 0.5% (UR-FUNNY), and 1.9% (CHERMA) over existing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMAD outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and modality combination. Among customized (per-combination) models, the reconstruction-based methods MCTN, GCNet, and MPLMM trail significantly on average (74.6–75.3 ACC on MOSEI) and suffer large F1 drops when language is absent (e.g., MPLMM drops to 49.4 F1 on audio-only MOSEI). The KD-based customized baselines UMDF and CorrKD are the strongest per-combination competitors, with UMDF reaching 76.0/75.1 average on MOSEI and 73.4 F1 on IEMOCAP, yet CMAD still exceeds them by 1.0 and 4.4 points respectively. Among unified (single-model) baselines, MMANet [42] is the closest competitor but underperforms CMAD by 1.1 ACC / 3.2 F1 on MOSEI and 2.8 ACC / 5.2 F1 on IEMOCAP, particularly struggling on language-only and audio-only settings. Notably, CMAD also surpasses the shared teacher model TEA on average (77.0 vs. 76.1 on MOSEI; 79.2 vs. 78.1 on IEMOCAP), demonstrating that the distillation framework with CAFD and MAR can exceed the full-modality teacher when modalities are missing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the knowledge distillation line of work for multimodal sentiment analysis with missing modalities, building directly on CorrKD [17] (correlation-decoupled KD) and MMANet [42] (margin-aware distillation with modality-aware regularization). It departs from reconstruction-based approaches (MCTN, GCNet, MPLMM) that require up to 2^m−1 separate models per modality combination, instead proposing a single unified student model. The key methodological departure is the joint use of a two-level correlation alignment loss (positive-pair contrastive + all-pair KL divergence) in CAFD, combined with a curriculum-inspired dynamic reweighting scheme (MAR) that explicitly eliminates sample difficulty before computing modality-combination weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified single-model formulation is practically important for deployment, avoiding the combinatorial explosion of per-combination models. The finding that eliminating sample difficulty (CMAD_w) consistently outperforms including it (CMAD_t) across datasets provides a useful design principle for curriculum-based reweighting in KD. However, the authors acknowledge that the framework does not fully account for the varying contributions of each modality within the teacher model, leaving language dominance as an open issue. Broader questions remain about whether the correlation-alignment mechanism generalizes beyond three modalities or to tasks where modality reliability is less hierarchical.
