## Problem

Reliable uncertainty quantification for Vision-Language Models remains an open challenge: the standard Maximum Concept Matching (MCM) score assigns high confidence to failures and degrades on fine-grained or open-vocabulary tasks, while existing learning-based methods (LVU) model only visual uncertainties and ignore conceptual ambiguities in the text modality. No prior approach provides a loss-agnostic, post-hoc failure predictor that explicitly captures cross-modal interactions between an image and the full set of candidate concepts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250893/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ViLU consumes a frozen VLM's visual embedding z_v ∈ R^d and K textual embeddings Z_t ∈ R^{K×d} of candidate concepts (class names or captions), requiring no access to model weights or internal logits. It outputs a single scalar failure score in [0,1] via a sigmoid, where values near 1 indicate a likely misclassification. The number of candidate concepts K can vary at inference, supporting both fixed-label classification (e.g., 1000 ImageNet classes) and dynamic image-caption retrieval where the concept set changes per batch.

## Method

ViLU constructs a multi-modal uncertainty embedding by concatenating three components: the visual embedding z_v, the predicted caption embedding z_t̂, and an image-conditioned textual representation z_t^α obtained via a cross-attention module where z_v acts as the query and the K textual embeddings serve as keys and values. A multi-layer perceptron with a sigmoid head maps this concatenated embedding to a failure probability. Training frames the task as binary classification (correct vs. incorrect prediction) using a weighted binary cross-entropy loss with a dynamic weight computed from the VLM's empirical per-batch accuracy, making the method fully loss-agnostic. The architecture is a consistent generalization of MCM: with a bilinear form on the ViLU embedding it reduces to the unnormalized MCM score, but the learned non-linear transformation captures fine-grained cross-modal ambiguities that MCM cannot detect.

## Results

On the average of 13 image-label datasets, ViLU achieves 93.2% AUC and 29.9% FPR95, substantially outperforming MCM (81.8% AUC, 70.6% FPR95), BayesVLM (84.2% AUC, 65.1% FPR95), and LVU (85.0% AUC, 57.4% FPR95). On large-scale image-caption datasets, ViLU reaches 95.2% AUC / 25.2% FPR95 on CC12M and 97.3% AUC / 21.2% FPR95 on LAION-400M, surpassing BayesVLM by 21.5 and 5.2 FPR95 points respectively. ViLU ranks first across all 16 datasets and both metrics, with the largest gains on fine-grained and low-accuracy settings (e.g., EuroSAT: 98.8% AUC vs. MCM's 64.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViLU outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest non-learning baseline MCM [30], ViLU improves average FPR95 by 40.7 points (29.9 vs. 70.6) and AUC by 11.4 points (93.2 vs. 81.8). The most competitive learned baselines are LVU [8,19,47] (85.0 AUC / 57.4 FPR95) and BayesVLM [2] (84.2 AUC / 65.1 FPR95); ViLU beats LVU by 8.2 AUC and 27.5 FPR95, and BayesVLM by 9.0 AUC and 35.2 FPR95 on average. On image-caption datasets (Table 2), LVU collapses below MCM (e.g., 69.3 AUC vs. 83.9 on CC3M), while ViLU maintains a clear lead over BayesVLM (91.4 vs. 87.1 AUC on CC3M; 95.2 vs. 90.9 on CC12M). Entropy [38], Doctor [14], and Rel-U [13] all underperform MCM on average, confirming that task-agnostic or vision-only uncertainty signals are insufficient for VLM failure prediction. Temperature scaling [15] applied to MCM yields only marginal gains (82.0 vs. 81.8 average AUC), further highlighting the need for the multi-modal contextualization that ViLU provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViLU positions itself as a consistent generalization of MCM (Maximum Concept Matching), the standard zero-shot UQ baseline for VLMs, by showing that its predictor reduces to the unnormalized MCM score under a bilinear form. It departs from the Learning Visual Uncertainties (LVU) line of work (ConfidNet, Pretrained Visual Uncertainties) in two key ways: it is loss-agnostic (training as a binary classifier rather than regressing the model's loss) and it explicitly models textual/conceptual ambiguity via cross-attention over all candidate captions. Compared to the closest VLM-specific method, BayesVLM, which applies a Laplace approximation over embedding similarities, ViLU instead directly targets the misclassification decision, making its objective aligned with the downstream failure-prediction metric.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As VLMs are increasingly deployed in safety-critical and open-vocabulary settings, a practical post-hoc UQ method that requires no access to model weights and no retraining of the backbone is highly relevant to practitioners. The finding that LVU methods actually underperform the simple MCM baseline on image-caption datasets challenges the assumption that visual-only uncertainty is sufficient when the concept set is dynamic. Open questions include whether the approach extends to domain adaptation and test-time adaptation of VLMs, and whether the cross-attention mechanism scales efficiently to very large candidate concept sets (e.g., hundreds of thousands of classes).
