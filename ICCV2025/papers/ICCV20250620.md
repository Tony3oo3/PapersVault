## Problem

Existing world models for VLN-CE rely on discrete state dynamics, static pretrained architectures, or expensive pixel-level future prediction, leaving them unable to model continuous action-state transitions or adapt online to novel environments. This paper addresses the absence of a self-evolving world model that can continuously refine its latent representations during deployment while maintaining efficient, feature-level foresight planning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250620/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent consumes sequential monocular RGB-D frames (or 12 panoramic views at 30° intervals) paired with a natural language instruction, and at each timestep predicts a waypoint as a position delta (Δposition_t) mapped to a d_a-dimensional embedding, which is translated into low-level Habitat Simulator actions (MOVE-FORWARD, TURN-LEFT/RIGHT, STOP). The world model additionally outputs predicted visual embeddings x̂_t at the feature level (not pixel level) to provide scene-aware foresight for subsequent action planning over T_p future steps.

## Method

NavMorph builds a tailored RSSM with two coupled networks: a World-Aware Navigator (inference) that encodes observations into a deterministic history h_t and stochastic state s_t, and a Foresight Action Planner (prediction) that rolls out latent states and decodes predicted visual embeddings for multi-step action planning. The key architectural novelty is the Contextual Evolution Memory (CEM), a bank of N_m scene-contextual vectors that retrieves top-K features via cosine similarity to enrich the recurrent state, then updates them through a simple forward interpolation (v_k ← (1−β)v_k + βh_t) rather than gradient backpropagation, enabling zero-cost self-evolution during online testing. Training minimizes a variational lower bound combining visual-embedding reconstruction, action prediction, KL divergence between posterior and prior, and an NDTW-based temporal alignment regularizer, all combined with a DAgger-style imitation learning objective. The framework is modular and plugs into existing VLN-CE base models (VLN-3DFF, ETPNav, HNR) without modifying their core navigation policies.

## Results

On R2R-CE Val Unseen (monocular, VLN-3DFF base), NavMorph achieves 47.9% SR and 33.2% SPL, improving over the base model by +4.1% SR and +3.8% SPL. On R2R-CE Val Unseen (panoramic, HNR base), NavMorph reaches 64% SR and 53% SPL, surpassing HNR by +3% SR and +2% SPL. On the more challenging RxR-CE Val Unseen (monocular), NavMorph attains 30.76% SR and 22.84% SPL, outperforming VLN-3DFF by +4.1% SR and +2.7% SPL. In the panoramic RxR-CE setting (HNR base), NavMorph achieves 58.02% SR, 48.98% SPL, 64.77 NDTW, and 48.85 SDTW, setting new state-of-the-art across all four metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NavMorph consistently outperforms all 16 compared baselines on the primary metrics (SR, SPL) across both R2R-CE and RxR-CE in both monocular and panoramic settings. In the monocular R2R-CE setting, NavMorph (47.9% SR) surpasses the strongest prior monocular method VLN-3DFF (43.8% SR) by 4.1 points, and also beats WS-MGMap (38.9%) and NaVid (37.4%) by substantial margins. In the panoramic R2R-CE setting, NavMorph with HNR base (64% SR, 53% SPL) exceeds HNR itself (61%, 51%) and ETPNav (57%, 49%), while matching DREAMW's SR (59%) but exceeding it by 9 points in SPL. On RxR-CE, NavMorph (monocular) improves over VLN-3DFF by 4.1% SR and 2.7% SPL, and in the panoramic setting (HNR base) achieves 58.02% SR vs. HNR's 56.48%, also leading on NDTW (64.77 vs. 63.43) and SDTW (48.85 vs. 47.38). The only near-tie is with DREAMW on R2R-CE panoramic Val Unseen SR (59 vs. 59), where NavMorph still wins decisively on SPL (50 vs. 44). Against the online adaptation method FSTTA [16], NavMorph wins on SR (47.9 vs. 43.9), SPL (33.2 vs. 29.6), and NDTW (44.86 vs. 42.76) while also being faster (21.22s vs. 27.34s per instruction).

## Relation to Prior Work

NavMorph extends the RSSM-based world model lineage (PlaNet, Dreamer series) to the VLN-CE setting, departing from prior navigation world models (PathDreamer, DreamWalker, Navigation World Models) that rely on discrete state dynamics or pixel-level future prediction. It introduces a continuous latent action-state transition model with feature-level reconstruction, and augments the recurrent module with a Contextual Evolution Memory (CEM) that performs forward-update self-evolution rather than gradient-based parameter updates. The framework is designed as a plug-in enhancement for existing VLN-CE base models (VLN-3DFF, ETPNav, HNR), positioning it as an orthogonal module rather than a standalone navigation system.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: existing navigation world models lack mechanisms for continuous online adaptation, which is critical for real-world deployment where agents face distributional shifts. The CEM mechanism offers a practical, computationally efficient alternative to gradient-based test-time adaptation (outperforming FSTTA in both accuracy and speed), making it potentially adoptable in other embodied AI settings requiring online memory. Open questions remain around designing explicit navigational reward functions for planning within world models and developing modular, composable world model architectures. A noted limitation is that CEM accumulates test-environment information, which the authors defend under the online VLN paradigm but which could raise evaluation fairness concerns in other contexts.
