## Problem

Transformer-based large multimodal models (LMMs) cannot effectively process hour-long videos because the quadratic complexity of causal self-attention over thousands of video tokens makes training and inference prohibitively expensive. Existing token-compression approaches mitigate this by reducing the number of vision tokens, but they incur information loss, still scale quadratically with remaining tokens, and add overhead. VAMBA pursues an orthogonal direction: replacing the expensive self-attention over video tokens with a linear-complexity architecture rather than reducing the token count.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251649/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a sequence of video frames (resized to 640×360) concatenated with a text prompt or question, and autoregressively generates a text answer or caption. A key practical distinction is that VAMBA can encode over 1024 frames on a single 80 GB GPU, whereas the Qwen2-VL-7B baseline is limited to 256 frames under the same hardware. During inference, the pre-filling stage (processing all input tokens before generation begins) is where the efficiency gains are most pronounced, with 30–50% FLOPs reduction and over 50% GPU memory savings for long inputs.

## Method

VAMBA is a hybrid Mamba-Transformer decoder initialized from Qwen2-VL-7B that splits the causal self-attention into two cheaper components: (1) a self+cross-attention module where text tokens attend to themselves (self-attention) and to all video tokens (cross-attention), with a learnable scalar α balancing the two paths, and (2) Mamba-2 blocks that update video tokens recurrently with linear O(d²M) complexity instead of the quadratic O(dM²) self-attention. Because video tokens M are typically 100× more numerous than text tokens N, the overall pre-filling complexity drops from O(d(M+N)²) to O(dMN + d²M). Cross-attention weights are initialized from the corresponding self-attention layers of the pretrained transformer, which ablations show is critical for preserving multimodal understanding. Training uses a two-stage paradigm: a pretraining stage that freezes all pretrained components and trains only the new cross-attention and Mamba layers on image captioning data with a standard language-modelling loss, followed by a full instruction-tuning stage on ~7M image and video QA pairs.

## Results

On the hour-long video benchmark LVBench, VAMBA (10B) achieves 42.1%, surpassing all efficient video LMMs by at least 4.3% over the best prior method (LongVU at 37.8%) and matching the transformer-based Qwen2-VL-7B (42.0%). On medium-length video benchmarks, VAMBA ranks first among efficient LMMs on Video-MME (57.8), MLVU (65.9), and LongVideoBench (55.9), while also achieving top scores on short-video tasks NExT-QA (78.1) and DREAM-1K (28.1). In terms of efficiency, VAMBA reduces GPU memory by over 50% during training and inference for long videos, nearly doubles training speed per step, and can encode 1024 frames on a single A800 GPU versus only 256 for Qwen2-VL-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four efficient video LMMs (LongVU, Video-XL, LongLLaVA, LLaVA-Mini), VAMBA wins on nearly all benchmarks: it leads on LVBench (42.1 vs. 37.8/36.8/31.2/17.6), Video-MME (57.8 vs. 55.3/55.5/51.6/40.3), MLVU (65.9 vs. 65.4/64.9/53.3/44.3), and LongVideoBench (55.9 vs. 53.5/50.7/42.1/19.3). The one notable loss is on MVBench (short video), where LongVU scores 66.9 vs. VAMBA's 60.4, suggesting VAMBA's Mamba-based video token processing is less effective for very short clips. Compared to the transformer-based Qwen2-VL-7B baseline, VAMBA matches on LVBench (42.1 vs. 42.0) but trails on Video-MME (57.8 vs. 63.3) and MVBench (60.4 vs. 67.0), though it achieves this with >50% less GPU memory and 4× more frames per GPU. Against proprietary models (GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5-Pro), VAMBA remains competitive on hour-long video (LVBench 42.1 vs. 34.7/33.1) but falls behind on medium-length video (Video-MME 57.8 vs. 71.9/75.0). Overall, VAMBA establishes a new state-of-the-art among open-source efficient video LMMs across long and medium-length video understanding while being substantially more computationally efficient than its transformer-based counterparts.

## Relation to Prior Work

VAMBA departs from the dominant token-compression paradigm (Q-Former, adaptive chunking, importance-based dropping) by taking an orthogonal architectural route: replacing the quadratic causal self-attention over video tokens with linear-complexity Mamba-2 blocks, while retaining a lightweight cross-attention mechanism for text-to-video interaction. It builds directly on Qwen2-VL as the base transformer LMM and on Mamba-2 as the sequence-processing backbone, and it addresses a known limitation of prior cross-attention-based LMMs (Flamingo, OpenFlamingo, Idefics) — the absence of vision token updates — by explicitly incorporating Mamba layers to transform video representations. The design is positioned as complementary to, rather than competitive with, token-reduction methods, since the two axes (fewer tokens vs. cheaper per-token processing) are independent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a 50%+ reduction in GPU memory and near-2× training speedup enable hour-long video processing on a single A800 (1024 frames vs. 256 for the transformer baseline), lowering the barrier for long-video research and deployment. The finding that the approach is orthogonal to token compression opens a clear path for future work combining both strategies for even greater efficiency. Key open questions include whether the hardware under-optimization of Mamba kernels will erode the practical speedup as SSM libraries mature, and whether the two-stage training recipe (freeze-then-finetune) generalizes to larger model scales or different base LMMs beyond Qwen2-VL.
