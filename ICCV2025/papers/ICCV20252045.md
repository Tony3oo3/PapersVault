## Problem

Existing text-guided image editing methods either rely on heavy diffusion models that produce hallucinations and cannot handle high-resolution images in real-time, or cover only a narrow set of restoration tasks. Camera ISPs are deterministic, offering a single uncontrollable output, so photographers depend on external software for the 40+ professional editing operations (exposure, white balance, color grading, denoising, etc.) that define photo-finishing. No prior method introduces natural-language control over this full range of photorealistic image processing operations at high resolution and real-time speed.

## Input / Output

The model takes an sRGB image (up to 24 MP, sensor-agnostic) and a free-form natural language prompt describing the desired edit (e.g., 'make it warmer and brighter', 'apply shade light'). It outputs a single edited RGB image at the same resolution. The model processes 12 MP images in under 1 second on consumer 24 GB GPUs, and the text prompts can vary in tone from novice to expert photographer language. Unlike diffusion-based editors, PixTalk does not generate new content but applies deterministic, interpretable transformations to the input.

## Method

PixTalk couples a lightweight text encoder (BGE-micro-v2 sentence transformer with a LoRA adapter projecting to 256-d embeddings, plus a task-classification FFN head) with a 5-block Mixture-of-Experts image pipeline. The controller uses a sigmoid (rather than softmax) gating mechanism so multiple blocks can be simultaneously active, and each block's output is computed as w_i·f_i(prev) + (1−w_i)·y, where y is the original image—this residual formulation lets zero-weight blocks be skipped entirely at inference. The five expert blocks are: (i) illumination/tone mapping via a conditional NILUT with 16 channels, (ii) white balance via a learnable dictionary of N gain triplets linearly combined by instruction-conditioned weights, (iii) color enhancement via pixel-wise dynamic convolution over 16 learned color-correction matrices, (iv) denoising via a simplified UNet, and (v) sharpening/refinement via an adapted SCSRN. The entire image model has only ~0.1 M parameters, trained end-to-end with supervised paired-image loss on ~106 K 1 MP crops from 8 camera models processed in Adobe Lightroom.

## Results

On the proposed PixTalk testset (40 images, 12MP, ~10,000 1MP cropped pairs), PixTalk achieves a mean PSNR of 37.80 dB and SSIM of 0.950 using only 0.1M parameters, improving the baseline PSNR by 8.3 dB and DeltaE by 6.23 on average. The average per-task DeltaE is below 2.5, indicating that the difference between PixTalk outputs and professional Adobe Lightroom edits is imperceptible to the human eye. PixTalk is the only method in the comparison that processes full 12MP images in real-time (under 1 second) on a standard 24 GB consumer GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PixTalk (37.80 dB, 0.1M params) is within 0.3 dB of InstructIR (38.10 dB, 1.8M params) while using 18× fewer parameters and being the only method capable of real-time 12MP processing. Against the diffusion-based zero-shot baselines, PixTalk dominates by a wide margin: InstructPix2Pix scores only 22.68 dB / 0.680 SSIM and Ledits++ 24.68 dB / 0.743 SSIM, reflecting hallucination artifacts and inability to handle high resolution. Among the adapted ISP baselines (CycleISP 30.20, InvISP 30.92, STAR 32.57, CNILUT 28.07), PixTalk leads by 5.2–9.7 dB in PSNR. The LLaVA-1.5 controller variant (37.90 dB, 7B params) matches PixTalk's own 0.1M-parameter controller almost exactly, confirming that the lightweight sigmoid-routed MoE controller is sufficient for task selection. Overall, PixTalk achieves the best efficiency–performance trade-off: near-state-of-the-art fidelity at two orders of magnitude fewer parameters than the closest text-guided competitor.

## Relation to Prior Work

PixTalk extends the text-guided image editing paradigm established by InstructPix2Pix (diffusion-based) and InstructIR (non-generative, 7 tasks) by reframing the problem as explicit control over a deterministic, multi-task image processing pipeline. Rather than generating new pixels, it routes a text embedding through a sigmoid-gated mixture-of-experts controller that activates specialized blocks (NILUT tone mapping, dynamic-convolution color correction, SCSRN sharpening) drawn from the same group's prior work on mobile ISP components. The key conceptual departure from diffusion-based editors is the rejection of generative modeling in favor of a shallow, interpretable, and extremely parameter-efficient (0.1M) processing chain that mirrors professional photo-finishing workflows in Adobe Lightroom.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PixTalk is the first method to bring language-based control to the full spectrum of professional photography operations (40+ transformations) while maintaining real-time processing of 12MP images on consumer GPUs, bridging the gap between deterministic ISP pipelines and interactive user experience. The accompanying dataset (~106K pairs from 8 diverse cameras) and benchmark fill a gap left by MIT5K and other limited resources, enabling future research on multi-modal image processing. Open questions include robustness to ambiguous or out-of-distribution prompts, extension to RAW-domain processing, and practical deployment of the language controller on mobile devices where the image pipeline already runs but the LLM component remains the bottleneck.
