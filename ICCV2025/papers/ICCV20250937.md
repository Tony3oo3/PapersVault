## Problem

Diffusion models frequently generate images with incomplete objects—fragmented or missing parts—yet this issue has been largely overlooked in the literature. The paper identifies RandomCrop, a standard data augmentation technique used during training, as the primary cause, and shows that existing remedies (layout-based methods, SDXL's coordinate conditioning) either introduce placement artifacts or fail to eliminate the problem entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250937/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a specified object category X as input and produces a generated image where the target object is rendered completely. It operates as a training-free post-hoc modification on pretrained Stable Diffusion models (SDv1.5, SDv2.1, SDXL), intervening only during the first 5 of 50 denoising steps by adjusting the latent representation in-place. Attention maps are extracted at 16×16 resolution, and K=10 cluster centers are used to select representative self-attention maps, requiring no additional model parameters or external spatial signals.

## Method

The core contribution is a training-free 'dispelling constraint' that penalizes activation values at image boundaries during early denoising to implicitly push objects toward the central region. Cross-attention maps for the target object are first extracted to capture semantic localization; these are then Gaussian-smoothed and clustered to identify K spatially distributed key points, whose corresponding self-attention maps are averaged to obtain a spatial guidance signal. A dispelling loss (Eq. 1) is computed by subtracting activation values in a randomly selected internal region from those in the surrounding boundary region, applied separately to both the cross-attention and self-attention maps. The combined loss is minimized via gradient descent on the latent z_t during timesteps t > T₁ (with T₁=45 out of T=50), while all model parameters remain frozen. The ablation shows that cross-attention alone captures semantics but fails at spatial structure, while self-attention alone refines position but loses fine detail—hence both are necessary for effective boundary repulsion.

## Results

On SDv2.1, the proposed method reduces the Human-Evaluated Object Incompleteness Rate (HOIR) from 45.7% (baseline Stable Diffusion) to 17.3%, and the LMM-Evaluated Object Incompleteness Rate (LOIR) from 32.0% to 11.7%, outperforming all 8 layout-based baselines. The method also achieves the best human-preference scores: PickScore 23.41, HPSv2 25.66, and ImageReward 0.327, while maintaining a CLIP-IQA of 0.703 (close to the baseline's 0.714). The additional time cost is negligible at 5.75s per image versus 5.51s for vanilla Stable Diffusion, compared to 8.54–24.02s for competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all 8 baselines on both incompleteness metrics: HOIR drops to 17.3% versus the best baseline SLD at 27.1% (a 9.8-point gap), and LOIR drops to 11.7% versus SLD's 23.1% (an 11.4-point gap). Among non-LLM layout methods, GLIGEN (HOIR 34.2%) and Boxdiff (HOIR 39.1%) are the closest competitors but still trail by over 16 points. Among LLM-based methods, SLD (HOIR 27.1%, LOIR 23.1%) is the strongest, yet the proposed method surpasses it by roughly 10 points on both metrics without requiring any LLM inference. On human-preference metrics, the method leads all baselines: PickScore 23.41 vs. SLD's 23.07, HPSv2 25.66 vs. SLD's 25.17, and ImageReward 0.327 vs. SLD's 0.253. The only slight trade-off is CLIP-IQA (0.703 vs. vanilla SD's 0.714) and a 0.24s increase in generation time, both negligible relative to the completeness gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by identifying RandomCrop—a standard data augmentation technique—as the root cause of object incompleteness in diffusion models, a problem previously dismissed as inherent randomness or categorized merely as a generation failure. Methodologically, it repurposes the cross-attention and self-attention mechanisms already exploited by prompt-to-prompt editing [14], Attend-and-Excite [5], and MasaCtrl [4], but redirects them toward a spatial boundary-penalization objective rather than semantic binding or attribute control. It explicitly positions itself as orthogonal to SDXL's training-time fix (incorporating crop coordinates) and as a lightweight alternative to layout-based methods like GLIGEN [21] and BoxDiff [57] that require external spatial signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a ubiquitous augmentation step (RandomCrop) systematically biases models toward incomplete objects reframes how practitioners should think about training data pipelines for generative models, and the training-free remedy is immediately deployable on any pretrained Stable Diffusion variant with ~0.24s overhead. However, the method requires knowing the target object category in the prompt to extract the relevant cross-attention map, limiting its applicability to open-ended or multi-subject scenes where the 'object' is ambiguous. Open questions include whether the RandomCrop effect persists in video or 3D diffusion models, whether the boundary-penalization strategy generalizes to non-rectangular or irregular object shapes, and whether the 45% incompleteness baseline varies substantially across object scales and aspect ratios.
