## Problem

Existing post-training quantization methods for diffusion models overlook channel-wise activation outliers that stretch the quantization range, causing severe performance degradation at low bit-widths such as W4A6. The iterative denoising process compounds this problem because quantization errors accumulate across hundreds of timesteps, and early steps—despite having smaller immediate errors—disproportionately affect final image quality. Naive application of Equivalent Scaling (e.g., SmoothQuant) from LLMs fails for diffusion models because the large activation-to-weight magnitude ratio produces scaling factors that amplify weight quantization errors at every sampling step.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained diffusion model (LDM-4, LDM-8, or Stable Diffusion v1.4) and a small calibration set, and produces a quantized model with per-channel weight quantization and per-tensor activation quantization at W4A6, W4A8, or W8A8. The quantized model accepts the same inputs as the original—random noise, class label plus noise, or text prompt plus noise—and generates images at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution. The LES scaling factors are fused into static weights and activation scales at calibration time, while PTS factors are applied as bit-shifts during weight loading, so inference incurs no additional overhead beyond the bit-shift operation.

## Method

DMQ combines two complementary techniques. First, Learned Equivalent Scaling (LES) optimizes channel-wise scaling factors τ by minimizing the quantized output error, redistributing outlier difficulty bidirectionally between activations and weights; an adaptive timestep weighting loss (inspired by focal loss) dynamically up-weights timesteps with lower accumulated error to prioritize early denoising steps whose small errors compound over the full sampling trajectory. Second, channel-wise Power-of-Two Scaling (PTS) directly removes extreme activation outliers in layers with high inter-channel variance (e.g., skip connections) by scaling each channel by 2^δ, which is implemented as a left-bit-shift on weights at kernel load time. A robust voting algorithm selects δ values by requiring statistical consensus across calibration samples (agreement ratio above threshold κ), preventing overfitting to anomalies in the small calibration set. LES is applied to all layers with zero inference overhead, while PTS is selectively applied only to outlier-prone layers, keeping the bit-shift cost minimal.

## Results

On FFHQ 256×256 at W4A6, DMQ achieves FID 26.38 and sFID 20.01, surpassing full-precision (FID 31.34) and outperforming the best baseline (EDA-DM, FID 29.62) by a wide margin. On ImageNet 256×256 at W4A6, DMQ reaches FID 7.81 and IS 320.6, again beating full precision (FID 11.34, IS 366.8) and the strongest baseline TFMQ-DM (FID 9.61, IS 225.6). On MS-COCO text-to-image at W4A6, DMQ achieves FID 29.58 and CLIP 30.67, while TFMQ-DM collapses to FID 104.95. Across all three generation settings and bit-widths (W8A8, W4A8, W4A6), DMQ consistently attains the best or near-best FID and sFID.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMQ outperforms all four baselines across nearly every setting. At W4A6 on FFHQ, Q-Diffusion (FID 71.16) and PTQD (FID 74.18) collapse catastrophically, while EDA-DM (FID 29.62) and TFMQ-DM (FID 29.76) remain competitive but still trail DMQ (FID 26.38) by ~3 FID points. On ImageNet W4A6, the gap widens dramatically: DMQ achieves FID 7.81 vs. TFMQ-DM's 9.61, Q-Diffusion's 36.49, and EDA-DM's 48.23, with IS of 320.6 vs. TFMQ-DM's 225.6. On MS-COCO text-to-image at W4A6, TFMQ-DM degrades to FID 104.95 while DMQ maintains FID 29.58, a 3.5× improvement. The one exception is LSUN Church at W4A8, where EDA-DM (FID 12.31) slightly edges out DMQ (FID 11.16 is actually better—correction: DMQ wins at 11.16 vs. 12.31), and at W4A6 where EDA-DM (FID 14.41) is marginally ahead of DMQ (FID 14.28 is actually better). In fact, DMQ wins on LSUN Church at all bit-widths. Overall, DMQ is the only method that maintains stable, high-quality generation at W4A6 across all three datasets and all three generation paradigms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the equivalent channel-wise scaling paradigm from LLM quantization (SmoothQuant, AWQ, Outlier Suppression+) to diffusion models, but departs from prior work in two key ways: it learns the scaling factors end-to-end (LES) rather than using heuristic magnitude ratios, and it augments scaling with Power-of-Two Scaling (PTS) to directly remove extreme activation outliers in skip connections. It also builds on the PTQ-for-diffusion-models line (Q-Diffusion, PTQD, EDA-DM, TFMQ-DM) by identifying that these methods overlook channel-wise outliers, and introduces an adaptive timestep weighting loss inspired by focal loss to account for error accumulation across denoising steps. The voting algorithm for PTS factor selection draws on statistical robustness ideas to avoid overfitting on small calibration sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: W4A6 quantization of diffusion models was essentially infeasible before this work (FID 70–100+ for prior methods), and DMQ brings it close to full-precision quality (FID ~26–30), enabling deployment on resource-constrained hardware. The finding that early denoising steps with small immediate quantization errors disproportionately affect final output quality provides a useful diagnostic for future quantization research. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to DiT-based architectures (where equivalent scaling is more naturally applicable), video diffusion models, and whether the voting algorithm's threshold κ can be made architecture-agnostic. The method remains PTQ (no retraining), which is its main practical advantage over QAT alternatives.
