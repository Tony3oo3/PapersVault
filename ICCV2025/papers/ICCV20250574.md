## Problem

Existing unified MLLMs that extend LLMs with vision vocabularies require hundreds of millions to billions of image-text pairs for alignment, making training prohibitively expensive. Additionally, whether the discriminative (understanding) and generative (image creation) capabilities of a unified model can synergistically enhance each other remains largely unexplored, with prior experiments showing no obvious mutual benefit from joint training alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250574/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

For understanding, the model consumes RGB images encoded by the UNIT vision encoder into continuous 256-token representations (224×224 in pretraining, up to 448×448 with a 9-slice patchify strategy in SFT) alongside text. For generation, the LLM autoregressively predicts 256 discrete tokens from a 16,384-entry codebook, which are decoded through a Stable Diffusion model to produce 512×512 images using classifier-free guidance. The self-enhancement module additionally produces structured consistency assessments (object accuracy, count, color, spatial relations) comparing self-generated images against their source text prompts.

## Method

ILLUME builds on Vicuna-7B with a continuous-input/discrete-output design: the UNIT encoder provides semantic features for understanding, while a novel semantic VQ tokenizer quantizes images in feature space (supervised by feature reconstruction loss rather than pixel reconstruction) for generation, with Stable Diffusion recovering high-fidelity images from the quantized semantic features. A three-stage training procedure—visual embedding initialization with an image reconstruction task, unified image-text alignment on only 15M samples, and task-specific SFT—reduces alignment data needs by 4× compared to Janus. The key differentiator is a self-enhancing multimodal alignment scheme: the model self-generates images from training captions, GPT-4o produces structured assessments of text-image consistency, and these 50K assessment examples are incorporated into SFT, teaching the model to identify its own generation failures and refine both its interpretive and generative accuracy.

## Results

On visual understanding, ILLUME (Vicuna-7B) achieves 88.5 on POPE, 75.1 on MMBench, 72.9 on SEED-Bench, 1445.3 on MME-P, and 38.2 on MMMU, ranking first or second on 10 out of 12 benchmarks. On text-to-image generation, ILLUME achieves a FID of 7.76 on MJHQ-30K and the best GenEval overall score of 0.61, outperforming both generation-only diffusion models (SDXL: FID 9.55) and unified models (Janus: FID 10.1). On image editing (Emu Edit benchmark), ILLUME scores 0.879 CLIP-I and 0.260 CLIP-T, competitive with dedicated editing models like Emu Edit (0.859 CLIP-I, 0.231 CLIP-T) despite being a unified model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest unified MLLM, Janus (1.3B), ILLUME (7B) achieves substantial gains: +5.7 on MMBench (75.1 vs 69.4), +9.2 on SEED (72.9 vs 63.7), +107.3 on MME-P (1445.3 vs 1338.0), and +7.7 on MMMU (38.2 vs 30.5). Compared to the best understanding-only model LLaVA-NeXT, ILLUME wins on 10 of 11 shared benchmarks (e.g., +7.7 MMBench, +8.2 SEED, +3.1 MMMU) but trails on MM-Vet (37.0 vs 43.9). Against Emu3-Chat (8B from scratch), ILLUME leads on MMBench (+16.6) and MMMU (+6.6) but slightly lags on OCRBench (669 vs 687). On generation, ILLUME's FID of 7.76 on MJHQ-30K beats Janus (10.1), Show-o (15.18), and LWM (17.77), and its GenEval overall of 0.61 surpasses SDXL (0.55) and Janus (0.61, tied). On image editing, ILLUME outperforms the autoregressive PUMA on CLIP-I (0.879 vs 0.846) and DINO (0.791 vs 0.785) but trails on CLIP-T (0.260 vs 0.270), while remaining competitive with diffusion-based specialists like Emu Edit.

## Relation to Prior Work

ILLUME builds directly on the LLaVA/LLaVA-NeXT architecture for visual understanding (vision adapter mapping encoder features into LLM input space) and the unified next-token prediction paradigm established by Chameleon, AnyGPT, Emu3, Show-o, VILA-U, and Janus. Its key methodological departure is the use of a semantic vision tokenizer that quantizes images in a pretrained encoder's feature space (supervised by feature reconstruction loss) rather than in pixel space (as in VQGAN), paired with a Stable Diffusion detokenizer to recover high-fidelity images. The paper also introduces a self-enhancing multimodal alignment scheme—a training procedure where the model self-assesses consistency between text and its own generated images—which has no direct precedent in prior unified MLLM work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4× data-efficiency gain (15M vs. 65M image-text pairs for Janus) is practically significant for teams with limited compute, and the semantic-tokenizer ablation provides a clear design principle for future unified MLLMs. However, the self-enhancing alignment yields only modest improvements (e.g., MMBench 61.7→63.1, GenEval overall 0.56→0.59), and the paper itself acknowledges that natural synergy between understanding and generation does not emerge from joint training alone. Open questions include whether the self-assessment paradigm scales to more complex generation criteria (aesthetics, multi-object composition), whether the approach extends to video or 3D, and whether the 512×512 generation ceiling can be lifted without sacrificing the discrete-token efficiency.
