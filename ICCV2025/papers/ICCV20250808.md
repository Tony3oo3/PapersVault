## Problem

Medical imaging lacks the large-scale labeled datasets that have driven progress in general computer vision, due to privacy regulations, logistics, and high annotation costs. Existing datasets are either too small or lack anatomical diversity to support generalizable 3D segmentation models. This paper addresses both the data scarcity and the label-quality challenge that arises when scaling automatic labeling to over 50,000 scans.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250808/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3D volumetric scans (MRI or CT) cropped to 96×96×96 voxels, with UKBOB scans at 224×174 axial resolution and four MRI sequences (fat-only, water-only, in-phase, out-of-phase). Output is a per-voxel softmax probability map over 72 organ classes, yielding a 3D segmentation mask. The ETTA module additionally produces an entropy map over the prediction that guides batch-normalization refinement at inference time.

## Method

The paper introduces UKBOB, a 51,761-scan, 72-organ MRI segmentation dataset labeled automatically by TotalVibeSegmentator, then cleaned via a Specialized Organ Label Filter (SOLF) that flags samples where at least two of three geometric features (normalized volume, sphericity, eccentricity) fall outside organ-specific percentile bounds derived from full-body statistics. A foundation model (Swin-BOB) is pre-trained on the filtered UKBOB using Swin-UNetr with binary cross-entropy and Dice loss, then fine-tuned on downstream benchmarks. To further mitigate residual label noise and domain shift, the authors propose Entropy Test-Time Adaptation (ETTA), which at inference time minimizes prediction entropy by updating only batch-normalization parameters while freezing all other weights, making the adaptation architecture-agnostic and computationally lightweight.

## Results

On the BRATS brain tumor MRI benchmark, Swin-BOB achieves a mean Dice Score of 0.894 and mean Hausdorff Distance of 8.650, outperforming the previous best baseline SegResNet (0.890 Dice) by 0.4% and Swin-UNetr (0.886) by 0.8%. On the BTCV abdominal CT benchmark, Swin-BOB reaches 0.889 mean Dice while using only 24.19 GB GPU memory and 23.90 GPU hours—42% less memory than nnU-Net ResEnc L (41.85 GB) and 4.53× faster than MedNeXt (108.27 GPU hrs), though MedNeXt (0.926) and nnU-Net ResEnc L (0.921) achieve 1–2% higher Dice. In zero-shot transfer from UKBOB MRI to external datasets, Swin-BOB with SOLF filtering achieves 0.840 mean Dice on AMOS and 0.882 on BTCV, substantially surpassing the TotalVibeSegmentator baseline (0.796 and 0.792 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On BRATS, Swin-BOB (0.894 Dice, 8.650 HD) outperforms all 9 baselines, beating SegResNet (0.890) by 0.4% and Swin-UNetr (0.886) by 0.8%, with the largest margin over UNet (0.544) and V-Net (0.842). On BTCV, Swin-BOB (0.889 avg Dice) trails MedNeXt (0.926) and nnU-Net ResEnc L (0.921) by 3.7% and 3.2% respectively, but surpasses MedSegDiff (0.879) by 1.0%, Swin-UNetr (0.869) by 2.0%, and all CNN-based baselines (nnUNet 0.802, TransUNet 0.838, UNetr 0.856) by 3–9%. The efficiency trade-off is stark: Swin-BOB uses 24.19 GB memory and 23.90 GPU hours versus 41.85 GB / 55.69 hrs for nnU-Net ResEnc L and 31.93 GB / 108.27 hrs for MedNeXt. In zero-shot MRI-to-CT transfer (Table 5), Swin-BOB with SOLF filtering (0.882 on BTCV, 0.840 on AMOS) substantially outperforms the TotalVibeSegmentator zero-shot baseline (0.792, 0.796) by 9% and 5% respectively. For test-time adaptation, ETTA consistently improves all three tested networks (nn-UNet, Swin-UNetr, Swin-BOB) over the augmentation-based TTA baseline [82] on all three datasets, with the largest gain on AMOS (Swin-BOB: 0.847→0.864 Dice, 8.105→7.191 HD). The TotalVibeSegmentator [17], used for label generation, also serves as a zero-shot baseline and is consistently outperformed by Swin-BOB across all evaluation settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Swin-UNetr as the architectural backbone for its Swin-BOB foundation model and on TotalVibeSegmentator as the automatic labeling engine that generates the 1.37 billion masks. Conceptually, it departs from prior medical imaging datasets (BRATS, BTCV, AbdomenAtlas, TotalSegmentator) by scaling from hundreds to tens of thousands of 3D samples and from single-region to full-body coverage, while introducing a statistical quality-control pipeline (SOLF) that has no direct precedent in the medical segmentation literature. The Entropy Test-Time Adaptation (ETTA) extends the TENT line of work by restricting adaptation to batch-normalization parameters only, making it architecture-agnostic and more robust under noisy-label training conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UKBOB is the largest labeled 3D medical segmentation dataset to date and will likely become a standard pre-training resource for the field, much as ImageNet did for 2D vision. The SOLF filtering mechanism and ETTA offer practical, lightweight solutions to the label-noise and domain-shift problems that plague large-scale automatic labeling, and the zero-shot MRI-to-CT transfer results validate that scale plus quality control can substitute for modality-specific training. Open questions include whether the approach extends to CT and other modalities, whether the 72-organ coverage is sufficient for clinical deployment, and whether the modest SOTA gains (0.4–1.3% Dice) will grow as downstream tasks become more demanding. The reliance on a single automatic labeler (TotalVibeSegmentator) for all labels remains a potential single point of failure for systematic biases.
