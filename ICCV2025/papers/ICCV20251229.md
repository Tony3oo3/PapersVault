## Problem

Existing blind deblurring methods, both traditional and deep learning-based, degrade significantly under strong unknown noise because noise corrupts blur kernel estimation and amplifies artifacts during non-blind deconvolution. The fundamental challenge is the trade-off between deblurring and denoising: aggressive noise removal destroys structural details needed for accurate kernel estimation, while focusing on deblurring leaves the image heavily corrupted. This paper targets the specific gap of achieving accurate kernel estimation in the presence of high-level noise (e.g., Gaussian σ=0.1) without requiring training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image degraded by an unknown spatially invariant blur kernel plus additive noise (Gaussian, Poisson, or mixed) as input, with no requirement for noise level or type to be known a priori. It outputs both an estimated blur kernel and a fully restored deblurred image. The pipeline is entirely training-free, operating on a coarse-to-fine image pyramid where the number of scales depends on image size, and the final deconvolution step uses a non-blind method with nonlocal-means denoising.

## Method

The core contribution is a Residual Guidance Strategy (RGS) embedded within a coarse-to-fine image pyramid framework. At each finer scale, the method computes a residual between the current-scale blurry image and the upsampled convolution of the previously estimated kernel and intermediate latent image from the coarser scale. A guided filter is then applied to this residual (rather than to the image itself) to suppress noise while preserving edge structure, and the filtered residual is added back to the upsampled result to produce a corrected blurry image for kernel estimation. This residual-based filtering constrains the denoising scope, avoiding the detail degradation that occurs when filtering the full image directly. Kernel estimation at each scale uses a surface-aware prior solved via half-quadratic splitting and FFT, and the final deblurred image is obtained through non-blind deconvolution with NLM denoising. The strategy is modular and can be plugged into other blind deblurring methods to improve their noise robustness.

## Results

At Gaussian noise σ=0.1, the proposed RGS method achieves 21.41 dB PSNR and 0.77 MNC on the Lai et al. dataset, outperforming the best baseline (Li et al. [24] at 18.48 dB / 0.33 MNC) by nearly 3 dB and 0.44 MNC. On the Zhang et al. face dataset, it reaches 24.73 dB PSNR and 0.66 MNC (vs. 23.37 / 0.38 for the next best), and on RealBlur it attains 25.07 dB PSNR and 0.72 SSIM. The method also achieves the lowest LPIPS (0.18 on Lai et al.) indicating superior perceptual quality, and requires no training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed RGS method wins on every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, MNC) across all four datasets against all ten baselines. The largest margins are against deep-learning-based methods: VDIP [14] lags by 5.5 dB PSNR on Lai et al., DeblurGAN-v2 [19] by 3.6 dB, and Zhang et al. [41] by 5.1 dB, reflecting their inability to handle σ=0.1 noise. Among traditional methods, Anger et al. [1] is the closest competitor on RealBlur (24.06 vs. 25.07 dB) and on MNC for Lai et al. (0.53 vs. 0.77), but still trails by ~1 dB PSNR and 0.24 MNC. The kernel-estimation advantage is most pronounced: MNC of 0.77 on Lai et al. versus 0.53 (Anger) and 0.33 (Li et al.), confirming that RGS's residual-guided refinement directly improves kernel accuracy. The method also outperforms the noise-robust Lee et al. [21] (ECCV 2024) by 3.8 dB PSNR and 0.31 MNC on Lai et al., demonstrating that explicit residual processing in the image pyramid is more effective than learned noise-robust kernel estimation under strong noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the surface-aware blind deblurring framework of Liu et al. (PAMI 2019), adopting its optimization formulation, half-quadratic splitting solver, and coarse-to-fine image pyramid scheme, and uses Zhong et al.'s directional-filter non-blind deconvolution as the final restoration step. It departs from prior noise-handling strategies—whether pre/post denoising (Tai et al.), robust regularization (Anger et al.), or learned denoising stages (BDCNN, SelfDeblur)—by introducing a Residual Guidance Strategy that filters inter-scale residuals with a guided filter rather than the blurred image itself, thereby preserving structural detail while suppressing noise. The approach is positioned as a plug-in correction module applicable to existing blind deblurring pipelines, demonstrated on Dong et al.'s method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a training-free, optimization-based method can substantially outperform multiple deep learning approaches (by 2–5 dB PSNR) under high Gaussian noise (σ=0.1), challenging the assumption that learned methods are necessary for robust restoration in noisy regimes. The modularity of RGS—improving kernel estimation in any coarse-to-fine deblurring pipeline—makes it a practical tool for practitioners working with noisy real-world captures. Limitations include the assumption of linear (non-dynamic) blur, reliance on a specific guided filter and non-blind solver that could be replaced, and evaluation primarily at a single high noise level; open questions include whether learned or adaptive filters could further close the gap and whether the strategy extends to other degradation types such as motion blur or sensor-specific noise.
