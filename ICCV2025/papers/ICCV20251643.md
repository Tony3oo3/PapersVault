## Problem

Existing video color grading and photorealistic style transfer methods reduce the task to matching color distributions between input and reference, failing to capture the high-level artistic attributes—mood, emotion, tone, and atmosphere—that professional colorists actually transfer. Additionally, prior approaches lack a mechanism for users to express additional creative preferences (e.g., 'increase contrast') after the initial grading pass, and frame-by-frame generation methods introduce temporal flickering and structural degradation.

## Input / Output

The method takes an input video clip and a reference image or video (ideally from the same genre), and optionally a free-text prompt for post-grading retouching. Frames are processed at 512×512 resolution; key-frame pairs are selected by sampling one frame per second and maximizing CLIP cosine similarity. The primary output is a 16×16×16×3 3D LUT (represented as a 64×64×3 latent) that is applied uniformly to every frame in the video, guaranteeing temporal consistency and preserving all structural details of the original footage.

## Method

The framework has two diffusion-based components. First, a GS-Extractor (a U-Net initialized from Stable Diffusion weights, trained with a paired image-editor denoising U-Net using spatial attention) learns to extract high-level subjective features from a reference frame. Second, an L-Diffuser generates the LUT as a variation ΔL from an identity LUT in the VAE latent space, conditioned on the feature difference C = G(reference) − G(input) computed by the frozen GS-Extractor. This explicit LUT generation—rather than direct frame synthesis—ensures that the output is a deterministic color mapping applied to all frames, eliminating structural loss and flickering. A final user-preference stage matches a text prompt against GPT-4-generated LUT descriptions via CLIP text embeddings to select an additional LUT for fine retouching.

## Results

On the Condensed Movie dataset, the proposed method achieves PSNR 24.55, SSIM 0.8445, and LPIPS 0.1457, outperforming the strongest baseline NLUT (PSNR 21.41, SSIM 0.7398, LPIPS 0.3028) by a wide margin. On Adobe5k, it reaches PSNR 18.78, SSIM 0.7966, and LPIPS 0.2689, again leading all baselines. Inference time is also the fastest at 12.10 s for 480 frames, compared to 21.24 s for NLUT and 130.08 s for WCT2. In the user study, the method scores highest on mood matching (4.14/5) and intent matching (3.97/5) while having the lowest artifact rate (0.16).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all seven baselines on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across both datasets, with the largest gaps over WCT2 (PSNR +4.58 on Condensed Movie) and PhotoNAS (PSNR +7.47). NLUT is the closest competitor on Condensed Movie (PSNR 21.41 vs. 24.55) but still trails by over 3 dB; on Adobe5k the gap narrows slightly (16.76 vs. 18.78). CAP-VST is the only baseline that beats the proposed method on one metric: BRISQUE on Adobe5k (16.08 vs. 20.72), though it lags substantially on PSNR and LPIPS. Deep Preset achieves competitive PSNR on Condensed Movie (20.70) but fails to capture subtle tonal adjustments and produces the highest BRISQUE (44.63). CCPL and HistoGAN show the weakest overall performance, with CCPL scoring the lowest SSIM (0.6195) and HistoGAN the highest BRISQUE (45.68) on Condensed Movie. In the user study, the proposed method achieves the lowest artifact selection rate (0.16 vs. 0.33–0.57 for baselines) and the highest mood-matching score (4.14 vs. 3.11–3.80), confirming that the LUT-based approach better preserves structural detail while transferring artistic intent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the ReferenceNet architecture from Animate Anyone [30] (two U-Nets with spatial attention) and the latent diffusion framework of Stable Diffusion [60] (VAE, DDIM sampling, pre-trained weights) to construct its GS-Extractor and L-Diffuser modules. It departs from prior photorealistic style transfer and video retouching methods (WCT2, HistoGAN, CCPL, CAP-VSTNet) that frame color grading as low-level color distribution matching, and from LUT-based methods (NLUT, AdaCM) that treat LUTs as implicit, input-dependent operators. The key conceptual shift is explicitly generating a generalized LUT via a diffusion model conditioned on high-level feature differences (mood, atmosphere, emotion) extracted by a dedicated network, rather than regenerating frames or fitting per-pair LUTs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LUT-based formulation offers a practical path to professional-grade video color grading with guaranteed temporal consistency and structural preservation, addressing a real workflow gap between amateur editing tools and professional colorist pipelines. The user-study validation (mood/intent matching scores) and the text-prompt retouching step make the system more accessible than prior methods. Open questions remain around scene-change handling (a single LUT is applied across the entire video) and spatially-variant grading, both acknowledged as limitations. The approach could influence how deterministic color operators are generated in broader video editing and VFX pipelines.
