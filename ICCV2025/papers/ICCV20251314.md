## Problem

Existing VTG-specific Video-LLMs process all task tokens—timestamps, saliency scores, and captions—through a single static reasoning backbone with shared parameters, treating fundamentally distinct subtasks identically. This uniform processing causes task interference and prevents the model from specializing in the different feature representations each subtask demands, limiting fine-grained temporal localization performance.

## Input / Output

The model takes a natural language instruction and a sequence of video frames, each encoded by a ViT-based visual encoder (438M params) into 128 or 256 visual tokens at medium/high resolution, then compressed via slot-based token compression to 8 tokens per frame. The output is a structured causal event sequence where each event interleaves timestamp tokens (11 number tokens + separator), saliency score tokens (same format), and free-text caption tokens, with a sync token marking sequence end. Time and score tokens use dedicated encoders/decoders separate from the text tokenizer.

## Method

TimeExpert replaces the single LLM decoder with a fine-grained Mixture-of-Experts architecture featuring two key mechanisms: (1) task-aware dynamic gating, which computes cosine similarity between each token and expert representations, adds a task-specific activation-rate bias, and applies a learnable per-expert threshold to produce a binary (not top-k) activation mask; and (2) token-adaptive routing, which dynamically adds new experts initialized from aggregated embeddings of underrepresented tokens when they fail to activate any expert, and prunes experts whose activation rate falls below a threshold. A task-dependent auxiliary loss combines a concentration term (aligning expert activation counts with assigned token counts) and an L2 regularization on expert representations to drive specialization without collapse. Training proceeds in three stages—task module pretraining, MoE decoder pretraining with z-loss, and full supervised fine-tuning—on ~5.1M curated samples, built atop the ARIA MoE Video-LLM base.

## Results

In zero-shot evaluation, TimeExpert (adaptive-k, ~5.9B/3.5B/4.8B activated params) achieves SODA_c 2.5, CIDEr 8.2, and F1 23.6 on YouCook2 (DVC); R@1 IoU=0.5 of 42.8 and R@1 IoU=0.7 of 20.3 on Charades-STA (MR); and mAP 29.6, HIT@1 46.9 on QVHighlights (VHD). These are the best results across all three VTG tasks while activating fewer parameters than the 7B baselines. After fine-tuning on YouCook2 and Charades-STA, TimeExpert further improves to SODA_c 7.2, CIDEr 39.0, F1 33.5 (DVC) and R@1 IoU=0.5 64.1, R@1 IoU=0.7 43.3 (MR), setting new state-of-the-art on both benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TimeExpert outperforms all nine baselines across every reported metric. Against the strongest prior VTG-specific Video-LLM, TRACE [16], TimeExpert gains +0.3 SODA_c, +1.2 F1 on YouCook2; +2.5 R@1 IoU=0.5, +0.9 R@1 IoU=0.7 on Charades-STA; and +2.8 mAP, +4.2 HIT@1 on QVHighlights, while activating fewer parameters (~5.9B vs 7B). Against VTG-LLM [15], the margin is larger: +1.0 SODA_c, +6.1 F1 (DVC), +9.0 R@1 IoU=0.5 (MR), and +13.4 HIT@1 (VHD). TimeChat [40] and HawkEye [49] trail by even wider margins (e.g., TimeChat scores only 12.6 F1 vs TimeExpert's 23.6 on YouCook2). Among traditional non-LLM baselines in the fine-tuned setting (Table 3), CM2 [22] and Vid2Seq [57] achieve competitive DVC scores (CIDEr 31.7 and 25.3) but lack Moment Retrieval capability, whereas TimeExpert achieves CIDEr 39.0 and R@1 IoU=0.5 of 64.1 simultaneously. The ablation further confirms that removing token-adaptive routing drops HIT@1 from 46.9 to 42.6, and vanilla MoE with k=6 experts matches TimeExpert's performance only at the cost of fixed, non-adaptive activation.

## Relation to Prior Work

TimeExpert builds directly on TRACE's causal event modeling framework, which first structured VTG outputs as interleaved sequences of timestamps, saliency scores, and captions. However, it departs from the single-static-LLM paradigm shared by all prior VTG-specific Video-LLMs (TimeChat, VTimeLLM, HawkEye, VTG-LLM, TRACE) by replacing the shared reasoning backbone with a Mixture-of-Experts decoder featuring task-aware dynamic gating and token-adaptive routing. The dynamic routing mechanism draws on recent MoE literature on variable expert activation (e.g., Dynamic MoE, AdaMoE) but is the first to apply adaptive expert addition and pruning specifically to video-language task tokens. The base architecture inherits from ARIA, an open multimodal MoE model, and uses slot-based token compression from VTG-LLM for visual token efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine structural limitation in Video-LLMs for VTG: treating timestamps, scores, and text as homogeneous tokens ignores their fundamentally different representational needs. The consistent gains across three distinct VTG subtasks (DVC, MR, VHD) with fewer activated parameters than 7B baselines suggest the MoE decomposition is a practical path toward multi-task video understanding without task interference. Open questions include whether the dynamic expert management (addition/pruning) scales to longer videos or more diverse task taxonomies, and whether the observed implicit task preference in vanilla MoEs (Figure 4) generalizes beyond VTG to other structured-output tasks. The modest but consistent improvements over TRACE also raise the question of how much further headroom exists before the bottleneck shifts from token routing to visual encoding or temporal resolution.
