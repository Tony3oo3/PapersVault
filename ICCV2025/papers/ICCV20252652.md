## Problem

Existing video-to-audio synthesis methods struggle to simultaneously achieve high audio fidelity, precise event-level temporal synchronization, and computational efficiency. In particular, no prior work provides an explicit mechanism to condition generation on discrete auditory events (footsteps, percussive impacts, object interactions), and naively applying representation alignment (à la REPA) to audio latents is non-trivial due to noise-schedule sensitivity and sequence-length mismatches between latent tokens and pretrained audio encoder outputs.

## Input / Output

The model takes a silent video clip (truncated to 8 seconds) as input, extracting frame-level visual features at 4 FPS via a frozen Diff-Foley visual encoder and frame-wise onset cues at 15 FPS via a frozen SyncFusion onset detector. The output is an 8-second audio waveform, produced by decoding a latent mel-spectrogram (8×F/r×T/r, tokenized with patch size 2) through a frozen AudioLDM2 VAE decoder and vocoder. Inference runs in 0.58 s on a single A100 GPU using 25 SDE Euler-Maruyama steps with a CFG scale of 8.0.

## Method

TARO is a 12-block, 768-dim flow-based multimodal transformer (built on MMDiT) that jointly processes visual feature tokens and audio latent tokens through concatenated joint attention, with per-modality AdaLN to handle scale differences. Its first innovation, Timestep-Adaptive Representation Alignment (TRA), injects frozen EAT encoder features at the 4th transformer block and applies a cosine-similarity alignment loss weighted by w(t)=sigmoid(log(α_t/(σ_t+ε))), so constraints are weak when noise dominates and strengthen as the latent converges; a 1D convolutional projection resolves the sequence-length mismatch between latent tokens and encoder patches. Its second innovation, Onset-Aware Conditioning (OAC), projects frame-wise onset events through MLPs, sums them with timestep embeddings, and modulates both visual and audio branches via AdaLN (γ·x+β) rather than cross-attention, providing lightweight event-timing anchors without extra attention cost. Training minimizes the conditional flow matching loss plus λ_align·L_align (λ=0.5) with 10% classifier-free guidance dropout, using a linear interpolant (α_t=1−t, σ_t=t) and AdamW at lr=1e-4 for 500k steps.

## Results

On VGGSound, TARO achieves the best scores across nearly all metrics: FD 0.47, FAD 0.94, FID 8.21, Alignment Accuracy 97.19%, CLIP Score 14.10, and the highest human-rated MOS-Q (3.87) and MOS-A (4.16). It also maintains fast inference (0.58 s) with a compact 258 M parameter model. On the zero-shot Landscape dataset, TARO leads in FD (2.53), Alignment Accuracy (98.20%), and CLIP Score (10.68), while FoleyCrafter retains a lower FAD (3.82 vs. 5.41). Overall, TARO delivers 53% lower FD and 29% lower FAD relative to the strongest prior flow-based method (Frieren).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TARO outperforms all seven baselines on VGGSound in FD (0.47 vs. 0.99–3.34), FAD (0.94 vs. 1.33–9.37), FID (8.21 vs. 10.55–24.30), Alignment Accuracy (97.19% vs. 57.20–97.15%), CLIP Score (14.10 vs. 6.37–13.59), and both MOS scores. Against the closest flow-based competitor Frieren, TARO reduces FD by 61% (0.47 vs. 1.22) and FAD by 29% (0.94 vs. 1.33) while matching its inference speed (0.58 s vs. 0.52 s) with fewer parameters (258 M vs. 372 M). MDSGen is the most parameter-efficient baseline (142 M) but trails TARO on FD (1.04 vs. 0.47), FAD (1.34 vs. 0.94), and CLIP (11.41 vs. 14.10). Diffusion-based methods (Diff-Foley, See & Hear, FoleyCrafter) are 5–30× slower at inference and use 3–5× more parameters, yet still fall short on FD and FAD. On the zero-shot Landscape dataset, TARO leads in FD (2.53), Accuracy (98.20%), and CLIP (10.68), though FoleyCrafter achieves a lower FAD (3.82 vs. 5.41), suggesting TARO's audio fidelity advantage is most pronounced in the in-domain VGGSound setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

TARO extends the MMDiT flow-based transformer architecture (Esser et al., ICML 2024) to video-to-audio synthesis, adapting the stochastic interpolant framework of SIT (Ma et al., ECCV 2024) and the conditional flow matching objective (Lipman et al., 2022). Its key conceptual departure is from REPA (Yu et al., ICLR 2025), which applies fixed-strength representation alignment throughout training; TARO instead introduces timestep-adaptive weighting that scales alignment strength according to the noise schedule, preventing over-regularization in early, noise-dominated timesteps. For temporal synchronization, TARO departs from cross-attention-based conditioning used in DiT-style methods by employing lightweight AdaLN modulation driven by onset cues extracted via a pretrained onset detection model (SyncFusion, Comunità et al., ICASSP 2024), avoiding the computational overhead of token-level concatenation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TARO demonstrates that flow-based transformers with targeted representation alignment and event-driven conditioning can match or exceed heavier diffusion-based and masked-diffusion baselines while using fewer parameters (258M) and achieving sub-second inference (0.58s), making high-fidelity video-to-audio synthesis more practical for near-real-time applications. The timestep-adaptive alignment principle—gradually tightening constraints as the latent representation converges—is a transferable design insight applicable to other conditional generation tasks where pretrained feature alignment is used. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer video sequences, more diverse acoustic domains beyond VGGSound and natural landscapes, and whether the onset-detection dependency introduces a bottleneck for truly real-time deployment.
