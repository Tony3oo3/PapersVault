## Problem

Existing camera calibration methods either treat intrinsic (distortion) and extrinsic (pose) parameters independently, rely on pre-rectified images or calibration patterns, or condition on high-level semantic features that fail to capture local optical aberrations. No prior diffusion-based framework jointly models complex, arbitrary ray profiles and camera pose while grounding the model in authentic lens designs rather than simplified distortion models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251783/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N multi-view RGB images at 448×448×3 resolution from a video sequence, along with geometric aberration cues extracted by a line-segment detection network (ULSd). It outputs 6D camera ray bundles (origin + direction) stored on a grid over image patches in world space, from which camera extrinsics (rotation R, translation t) and per-pixel distortion warping flow maps are derived; the flow maps can be applied to produce undistorted images. The first camera serves as a coordinate anchor with unit translation and identity rotation, so all other cameras are expressed relative to it.

## Method

AlignDiff uses a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone, initialized from a 3D shape generation model, to denoise ray bundles conditioned on multi-view image latents (from TIPS) and geometric aberration cues. Two key architectural additions shift the model from semantic to structural reasoning: (1) line-segment embeddings from ULSd are injected at a single middle block as plug-and-play conditioning, and (2) an edge-aware attention mechanism adds Canny-edge-derived patch embeddings to the Query vector in self-attention, amplifying features along geometrically prominent boundaries. A separate DistortionNet (MAE-Tiny) regresses per-pixel warping flow from the denoised rays to a pinhole reference, enabling direct image undistortion. Training combines a standard DDPM denoising loss (T=100 steps) with an angular ray-direction loss and a distortion flow regression loss, while data augmentation with ~3000 ray-traced lens profiles from the LensView database grounds the model in real optical distortion characteristics.

## Results

On the distorted CO3D dataset, AlignDiff reduces mean ray angular error to 3.4–4.1° (2–8 input views) for seen categories and 4.6–8.2° for unseen categories, compared to 8.7–13.2° for RayDiffusion and 18.4–26.2° for RayRegression. On the out-of-distribution Aria Digital Twins egocentric dataset, AlignDiff achieves 13.8–24.9° angular error versus 28.4–45.6° for RayDiffusion. For camera pose on distorted CO3D (seen classes), AlignDiff reaches 90.6–93.1% rotation accuracy at 15° and 84.6–100% camera center accuracy at 0.1 scale, outperforming all baselines by 6–19 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AlignDiff consistently outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and metric. Against RayDiffusion [63] (the closest competitor, sharing the same ray representation), AlignDiff reduces angular error by roughly 6–14° on CO3D and by 13–21° on Aria Digital Twins, while also improving rotation accuracy by 6–19 points on distorted CO3D. Against RayRegression [63], the gap is even larger (15–20° angular error reduction), confirming that the diffusion formulation plus geometric conditioning is critical. Against PoseDiffusion [55] and RelPose++ [33], AlignDiff leads by 15–25 points in rotation accuracy on distorted CO3D, demonstrating that explicit aberration modeling via ray bundles is substantially more effective than parameterized distortion models. Against COLMAP [48] (evaluated only on rectified CO3D), AlignDiff achieves 92.3–93.6% rotation accuracy versus COLMAP's 26.5–30.6%, though on rectified images the gap over RayDiffusion narrows to ~0.5–1.5 points, suggesting the primary advantage of AlignDiff lies in handling unrectified, aberrated captures.

## Relation to Prior Work

AlignDiff extends the ray-based diffusion framework of RayDiffusion [63] by replacing its purely semantic image conditioning with geometric cues (line-segment embeddings from ULSd and edge-aware attention), and by grounding the model in real ray-traced lens profiles from the LensView database rather than predefined distortion models. The DiT/DDPM backbone [16] is inherited from prior 3D shape generation work, with the latent space inflated to accommodate concatenated noised rays and image latents. Conceptually, the paper departs from the broader camera calibration literature—which typically treats intrinsic and extrinsic parameters separately or assumes fixed distortion models—by unifying both under a generic ray camera representation and explicitly modeling local, asymmetric aberrations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical gap: real-world lenses exhibit complex, local optical distortions that pinhole or radial-tangential models cannot capture, and prior deep-learning calibration methods rely on semantic features that conflate object content with geometric aberration. The ~8.2° angular error reduction and strong out-of-distribution generalization (Aria Digital Twins) suggest that geometric conditioning plus physical lens grounding is a viable path toward robust in-the-wild calibration. Open questions include handling scale ambiguity in outdoor scenes, integrating temporal consistency natively rather than as post-processing, and whether the approach scales to multi-lens rigs or dynamic scenes with significant lighting variation.
