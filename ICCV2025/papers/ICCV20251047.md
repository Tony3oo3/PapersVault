## Problem

Collecting large-scale real-world robot manipulation demonstrations is prohibitively expensive, while existing physics simulators suffer from the sim-to-real gap and require manual reprogramming even for trivial scene changes (e.g., swapping an object's color). Although 3D Gaussian Splatting offers photo-realistic reconstruction and per-primitive editability, no systematic pipeline has integrated these capabilities into a practical, automated robotic simulation framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251047/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes short demonstration videos (a few viewpoints of a manipulation scene) and free-form natural language commands (e.g., 'change the cup color to blue', 'remove the distractor object'), optionally supplemented by 3D Gaussian assets retrieved from databases like ShapeSplat/uCO3D or synthesized via feed-forward generators (GRM, LGM). It outputs photo-realistic, view-consistent simulation videos rendered from arbitrary camera poses, along with the edited 4D semantic Gaussian scene itself, which can be directly used as training data for manipulation policies such as RVT, RVT-2, or SAM2Act.

## Method

RoboPearls extends 3DGS to a 4D dynamic representation with SAM-supervised identity encodings, enabling per-object manipulation of time-conditioned Gaussians. Two key modules power the editing operators: Incremental Semantic Distillation (ISD), which fine-tunes only the identity encoding of retrieved Gaussians to handle fine-grained object parts not covered during initial training, and a 3D regularized NNFM Loss that transfers texture/style from a reference image while preserving background geometry by optimizing only target-object spherical harmonics under a reconstruction constraint. A multi-agent LLM pipeline (Simulation Manager, Grounding, Scene Operation, 3D Asset Management, Scene Refiner, Scene Renderer) decomposes natural language commands into concrete operator calls, automating the entire simulation production workflow. Finally, a VLM analyzes failure keyframes from robot learning, diagnoses causes (object position, color, background), and generates targeted simulation commands, closing the loop between evaluation and data generation.

## Results

On the COLOSSEUM benchmark, RoboPearls-RVT2 achieves 75.4% average success across 13 perturbations, improving over RVT (51.7%) by +17.5% and over RVT-2 (64.6%) by +10.8%. On RLBench, RoboPearls-SAM2Act reaches 88.5% average success, surpassing SAM2Act (83.8%) by +4.7%, with particularly large gains on Stack Cups (+16.4 over RVT-2) and Put in Cupboard (+23.0 over RVT-2). On a real-world Kinova Gen3 robot, RoboPearls achieves 14/20 success on unseen Pick-up objects versus only 4/20 for RDT, demonstrating strong generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoboPearls consistently outperforms all baselines across both simulation benchmarks. On COLOSSEUM, RoboPearls-RVT2 beats RVT by +17.5% and RVT-2 by +10.8% in average success across all 13 perturbations, with the largest gains on texture and color changes (e.g., MO-Texture: 73.8% vs. 58.1% for RVT-2). On RLBench, RoboPearls-SAM2Act surpasses SAM2Act by +4.7% average success, with the most dramatic improvements on challenging tasks: Stack Cups (68.0% vs. 63.2%) and Put in Cupboard (72.1% vs. 60.8%). When paired with RVT-2, RoboPearls achieves +16.4 on Stack Cups and +23.0 on Put in Cupboard over the unmodified RVT-2 baseline. On the real-world Kinova Gen3 robot, RoboPearls dramatically outperforms RDT on unseen objects (e.g., 14/20 vs. 4/20 on Pick-up, 12/20 vs. 1/20 on Place-in), confirming that the simulation-generated training data transfers effectively to physical manipulation. The ablation against IP2P (a 2D image-space editing method) further validates that 3D view-consistent simulation is critical, as IP2P yields only marginal gains (e.g., 18.4% vs. 14.5% on Stack Cups) compared to RoboPearls' 37.7%.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoboPearls sits at the intersection of 3D Gaussian Splatting scene editing and robotic manipulation simulation. It builds directly on 3DGS/4DGS for dynamic scene reconstruction, SAM-based semantic Gaussians (Feature Splatting, Gaussian Grouping) for object-level decomposition, and ARF's NNFM loss for texture transfer, but departs from prior 3DGS editing work by integrating these individual operators into a systematic, LLM-orchestrated pipeline specifically targeting robotic data generation. The multi-agent LLM architecture draws conceptual parallels to editable scene simulation for autonomous driving [71], while the VLM-based failure analysis extends prior binary failure detectors (e.g., RoboFail) into a generative, closed-loop simulation demand system. Unlike traditional physics-engine simulators (Isaac Sim, MuJoCo), RoboPearls uses real demonstration video as the simulation substrate, sidestepping the need for manual scene reprogramming.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core bottleneck in robot learning: the cost and inflexibility of collecting diverse real-world demonstrations. By making simulation editable via natural language and closing the loop with VLM-driven failure analysis, it offers a practical path toward scalable, user-friendly data augmentation without full physics-engine reprogramming. However, the approach still requires initial multi-view demonstration video, the physics simulation component remains limited to MPM-based rigid/soft body dynamics, and the LLM agent pipeline's reliability on complex multi-step commands is not deeply stress-tested. Open questions include whether the framework scales to long-horizon, multi-object manipulation tasks and whether the video-based simulation can match the physical fidelity of engine-based simulators for contact-rich interactions.
