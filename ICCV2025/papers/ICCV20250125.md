## Problem

Existing scene text image super-resolution methods rely on text probability distribution priors from text recognizers to guide reconstruction, which capture general character structures but are insensitive to specific text style variations such as font, stereoscopic effects, and spatial transformations. This results in restored images that lose stylistic consistency, particularly for artistic characters or logos, degrading both visual quality and downstream recognition performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250125/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution scene text image (degraded by blur, distortion, or low focal length) and produces a super-resolution image at the target resolution. In addition to the LR image, the network consumes text probability priors from a pre-trained CRNN recognizer and style features extracted from the LR image itself via ResNet18. The output is an HR scene text image that preserves both character stroke structure and the original stylistic attributes (e.g., shadows, 3D effects, font characteristics).

## Method

StyleSRN introduces a dual-branch architecture: a Text Style Embedding Branch that extracts style features via ResNet18, refines them through stacked Style Enhancement Blocks (multi-scale 1D convolutions with varying kernel sizes for cross-channel attention, extending ECA), and maps the resulting latent vector through a StyleGAN-style 8-layer MLP to produce per-channel scaling and bias parameters; and a Super-Resolution Branch that aligns spatially deformed text via STN, then fuses style and content through stacked Style Content Fuse Blocks where AdaIN modulates shallow image features with the style parameters while a TP Interpreter embeds text priors, followed by a Sequential Residual Block. A novel Text Style Loss computes Gram matrices from CRNN feature maps (rather than VGG) and applies MSE between HR and SR Gram matrices to supervise style-level reconstruction. The overall training objective combines pixel-level MSE, a text-focus loss, and the proposed style loss with balancing coefficients α=0.5 and β=1.0.

## Results

On the TextZoom benchmark, StyleSRN achieves average recognition accuracy of 57.4% (CRNN), 64.1% (MORAN), and 67.3% (ASTER), outperforming all 11 baselines across all three recognizers. In terms of image quality, StyleSRN attains the highest average PSNR of 21.82 dB and an SSIM of 0.7778, surpassing TATT (21.52 dB) and LEMMA (20.88 dB) in PSNR. On five generalization datasets (IC13, IC15, CUTE80, SVTP, IIIT5K), StyleSRN achieves the best CRNN accuracy on every dataset, reaching 80.99% on IC13 and 65.63% on IIIT5K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StyleSRN outperforms all 11 baselines on average recognition accuracy across CRNN, MORAN, and ASTER. The closest competitors are RTSRN (57.0/63.2/66.2) and LEMMA (56.3/63.2/66.0), with StyleSRN leading by 0.4–1.3 points on average. On image quality, StyleSRN achieves the best PSNR (21.82 dB) but trails TATT in SSIM (0.7778 vs. 0.7930), suggesting TATT preserves structural similarity slightly better at the cost of style. On the Hard subset of TextZoom, TCDM edges out StyleSRN on CRNN accuracy (42.7% vs. 42.4%), likely due to TCDM's diffusion-based approach handling extreme blur differently. On the five generalization datasets, StyleSRN leads every baseline on all five benchmarks, with the largest margins on SVTP (+2.79% over LEMMA) and IC15 (+0.77% over LEMMA). Earlier methods without text priors (TSRN, TBSRN) lag significantly, confirming that both content and style guidance are necessary for competitive performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established research threads: scene text image super-resolution (STISR) methods that leverage text probability priors from recognizers (TSRN, TPGSR, TATT, LEMMA), and style transfer techniques (Gatys-style Gram matrices, AdaIN, StyleGAN's style mapping network). Prior STISR work has focused almost exclusively on preserving character structure via text priors, implicitly treating style as an irrelevant byproduct. StyleSRN departs from this by explicitly encoding and embedding style features (channel correlations) into the SR pipeline, borrowing the AdaIN modulation and Gram-matrix supervision from the style transfer literature and adapting them to the text-specific context using a CRNN backbone rather than VGG.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and previously under-addressed failure mode: text-prior-guided SR methods systematically destroy stylistic details (font, stereoscopic effects, shadows), which degrades both visual quality and downstream recognition. The Text Style Loss using a text recognizer for Gram-matrix computation is a reasonable domain-specific adaptation, but the paper itself acknowledges a significant open problem — there is no standardized quantitative metric for evaluating style preservation in STISR, making rigorous comparison across methods difficult. Additionally, the method still struggles with extreme degradations (uneven illumination, severe blur), and the modest gains over strong baselines like TATT and LEMMA suggest that style embedding alone may not be sufficient for the hardest cases.
