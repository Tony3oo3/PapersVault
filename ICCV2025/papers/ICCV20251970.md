## Problem

The multimodal domain lacks a large-scale, high-quality agent tuning dataset that simultaneously supports Chain-of-Thought reasoning, error reflection, and dynamic tool usage. Existing datasets such as LLaVA-Plus (117K) and MM-Traj (20K) suffer from homogeneous task distributions, absence of reflection mechanisms to correct tool-induced errors, and inflexible reasoning paradigms, limiting their utility for real-world multimodal agent applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251970/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image and a text question, with tool API outputs (scene-graph-based image captions, open-vocabulary detection boxes, OCR text, face regions) and RAG-retrieved web snippets either dynamically invoked across multiple turns (RR format) or pre-computed and prepended in brackets to the input (ORR format). The output is a structured rationale containing step-by-step reasoning thoughts, explicit tool invocation commands, reflection corrections when logical inconsistencies are detected, and a final answer. ORR consolidates all reasoning into a single generation turn for faster inference, while RR preserves the multi-turn adaptive tool-calling trajectory.

## Method

The paper introduces a four-stage data engine that synthesizes 1,090,263 QA pairs from five public multimodal sources (Visual CoT, LLaVA-CoT, The Cauldron, TabMWP, InfoSeek). Stage 1 unifies prompt formats; Stage 2 uses GPT-4o to generate iterative CoT trajectories with dynamic API calls—Image Caption via CCoT scene graphs, OVD via Grounding DINO, OCR via PaddleOCR, Face Detection via DeepFace, and RAG via Google Search (top-3); Stage 3 applies a reflection mechanism where GPT-4o detects and corrects two failure modes: 'step skipping' in mathematical derivations and 'reasoning cheating' where the rationale does not logically entail the answer; Stage 4 optionally compresses multi-turn RR dialogues into a one-turn ORR format by prepending tool results to the input. Open-source MLLMs (InternVL2.5-2B/4B/8B, MiniCPM-V-2.6, Llama-3.2-11B-Vision) are fine-tuned via LoRA SFT for one epoch at learning rate 4e-5, and the resulting models achieve up to 2.7% average improvement across eight benchmarks and 8.8% on Dyn-VQA over their baselines.

## Results

Fine-tuning InternVL2.5-8B on MMAT-1M with the RR strategy raises the average score across eight benchmarks from 60.7 to 63.4 (+2.7%), while ORR yields 62.4 (+1.7%). On the RAG benchmark Dyn-VQA, InternVL2.5-8B-RR improves from 27.0 to 36.8 (F1-Recall, +8.8 absolute), and Llama-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct-RR jumps from 29.4 to 38.0 (+29.3% relative). The RR strategy consistently outperforms ORR across all model scales, with the largest relative gains observed on smaller models (e.g., Llama-3.2-11B: 52.2→55.3, +5.9% relative).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-4o, InternVL2.5-8B-RR wins on MMStar (65.3 vs. 65.1), MathVista (64.8 vs. 60.0), and OCRBench (839 vs. 806), but trails on MMMU (57.3 vs. 70.7), MathVision (21.7 vs. 30.4), AI2D (84.2 vs. 84.9), and HallusionBench (55.8 vs. 56.2). On the Dyn-VQA RAG benchmark, GPT-4o (52.0 Query / 61.5 Golden Query F1-Recall) substantially outperforms InternVL2.5-8B-RR (36.8 / 44.0), indicating that the open-source model still lags on knowledge-intensive retrieval. Compared to OmniSearch (GPT-4V), which scores 50.0 on Dyn-VQA Query, InternVL2.5-8B-RR (36.8) remains behind, though the gap narrows considerably relative to the untrained baseline (27.0). Overall, the RR strategy delivers the largest and most consistent gains across all five open-source model families tested, with relative improvements ranging from 2.7% (InternVL2.5-8B) to 5.9% (Llama-3.2-11B) on the eight-benchmark average.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multimodal agent tuning line initiated by LLaVA-Plus (117K tool-use instructions) and T3-Agent (20K multimodal trajectories) by scaling to one million samples and integrating three previously disconnected capabilities: CoT reasoning, structured reflection, and dynamic RAG-based retrieval. It builds on CCoT for scene-graph-based image captioning and on OmniSearch/Dyn-VQA for the RAG evaluation protocol, while drawing on the LUMOS one-turn compression idea for the ORR format. The key conceptual departure from prior datasets is the explicit reflection stage that corrects 'step-skipping' and 'reasoning cheating' artifacts in GPT-4o-generated rationales, a mechanism absent from all comparable multimodal agent datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMAT-1M fills a clear gap: prior multimodal agent tuning datasets were small (20K–117K), lacked reflection, and did not support RAG, limiting their transfer to knowledge-intensive and multi-hop tasks. The consistent 2.7–5.9% average gains across InternVL2.5, MiniCPM-V, and Llama-3.2-Vision, plus the 8.8% Dyn-VQA improvement, demonstrate that scale and reflection compound to close part of the gap with GPT-4o on open-source models. Open questions include whether the GPT-4o-generated rationales (89% quality) introduce systematic biases, whether the OCRBench degradation under RR reflects a fundamental tension between reflection and OCR fidelity, and how the dataset generalizes to unseen tool types beyond the five operators used during construction.
