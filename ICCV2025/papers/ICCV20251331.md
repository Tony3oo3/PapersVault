## Problem

Deep stereo matching models achieve strong benchmark performance but fail to generalize to real-world scenes due to the scarcity of annotated real-world stereo pairs. Existing synthesis-based approaches either fill occluded regions with heuristic pixel copying (causing structural inconsistencies) or rely on NeRF reconstruction (requiring multi-view inputs and degrading for distant objects), leaving a gap in generating high-quality, diverse training data from arbitrary single images.

## Input / Output

The pipeline accepts a single RGB image of arbitrary resolution as the left image. It produces a synthesized right image at 512×512 (in latent space), a pseudo disparity map scaled adaptively to the image width, and a per-pixel confidence map derived from horizontal-flip invariance of relative depth. The disparity scaling factor is sampled from a three-region distribution (small/center/large) parameterized by center c=0.1, radius r=0.05, and probabilities p_c=0.8, p_s=p_l=0.1, ensuring the disparity-to-width ratio remains physically plausible across resolutions.

## Method

ZeroStereo fine-tunes Stable Diffusion V2 Inpainting (SDv2I) with text conditioning disabled on synthetic stereo datasets (Scene Flow, Tartan Air, CREStereo, VKITTI 2) to handle the irregular, structured occlusion masks unique to stereo warping—unlike standard inpainting which restores localized objects. Training-Free Confidence Generation exploits the spatial invariance of relative depth under horizontal flipping: the confidence is computed as the normalized agreement between the original and flipped depth predictions, requiring no auxiliary network. Adaptive Disparity Selection samples the disparity scaling factor from a piecewise uniform distribution over three intervals to prevent foreground distortion at low resolutions while preserving perceptible parallax at high resolutions. The final ZeroStereo Loss combines a confidence-weighted L1 disparity loss with a (1−confidence)-weighted non-occlusion photometric loss (SSIM + L1), allowing the model to learn from reliable pixels via supervision and unreliable pixels via photometric consistency.

## Results

On the zero-shot generalization benchmark (real-world data without GT), Zero-RAFT-Stereo achieves 4.53 EPE (>3px) on KITTI-15, 12.40 (>2px) on Middlebury, and 2.75 (>1px) on ETH3D, outperforming all baselines on KITTI and Middlebury. Zero-IGEV-Stereo achieves the best ETH3D non-occlusion score of 1.90 (>1px), surpassing even SceneFlow-trained IGEV-Stereo (3.60). Notably, these results are obtained with only 35K synthesized samples (MfS35K), a dataset volume comparable to Scene Flow, while outperforming methods trained on 200K–1106K synthetic samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Zero-RAFT-Stereo outperforms NS-RAFT-Stereo [47] (the closest real-data baseline) by 0.88 px on KITTI-15 (4.53 vs 5.41), 3.98 px on Middlebury (12.40 vs 16.38), and 0.20 px on ETH3D (2.75 vs 2.95), with the largest relative gain (>20%) on Middlebury. Against MfS-PSMNet [54], the improvement is even more dramatic: 0.65 px on KITTI, 14.02 px on Middlebury, and 5.42 px on ETH3D, confirming that diffusion-based inpainting and confidence-aware training substantially surpass naive forward-warping with random background filling. Compared to SceneFlow-trained methods, Zero-RAFT-Stereo beats RAFT-Stereo [25] on KITTI (4.53 vs 5.47) and Middlebury (12.40 vs 15.63) but trails slightly on ETH3D (2.75 vs 2.60); Zero-IGEV-Stereo* beats IGEV-Stereo [58] on ETH3D (2.00 vs 3.60) and KITTI (4.89 vs 6.03). Among all SceneFlow-trained baselines (DSMNet, CFNet, GraftNet, ITSA, HVT-PSMNet, NMRF-Stereo, Mocha-Stereo, DKT-RAFT, Former-RAFT), Zero-RAFT-Stereo achieves the best KITTI-15 and Middlebury scores, while Zero-IGEV-Stereo achieves the best ETH3D non-occlusion score (1.90), demonstrating that the synthesized MfS35K dataset is more effective for zero-shot transfer than 5–30× larger synthetic datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZeroStereo extends the single-view stereo training paradigm initiated by MfS-Stereo [54], which used monocular depth estimation and forward warping to synthesize right images from single photos. The key departure is replacing MfS-Stereo's heuristic occlusion filling (nearest-neighbor or random background) with a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion V2 Inpainting model [39] specifically adapted to the irregular, structured occlusion masks inherent to stereo pairs. The paper also departs from NeRF-Stereo [47], which required multi-view inputs and 3D scene reconstruction, by remaining fully single-image-based while adding Training-Free Confidence Generation (exploiting horizontal-flip invariance of relative depth) and Adaptive Disparity Selection to improve training stability and disparity coverage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 35K diverse real-image-derived samples outperform 200K–1.1M synthetic samples (Falling Things, CREStereo, Tartan Air, FoundationStereo) on zero-shot benchmarks challenges the prevailing assumption that larger synthetic datasets are necessary for generalization, suggesting scenario diversity is the critical factor. The pipeline is practical and efficient (1.9s per image vs. 31.2s for StereoDiffusion at 512×512), making it accessible for broader adoption. Open questions remain: forward warping still fails on transparent or net-like structures, the fine-tuned inpainting model can introduce color inconsistencies, and whether the approach transfers to even more extreme domain shifts (e.g., underwater, aerial) is untested.
