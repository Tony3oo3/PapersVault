## Problem

Open-world egocentric activity recognition requires inferring activities from an unconstrained, partially observed search space, but current VLM-based approaches rely on exhaustive enumeration that becomes computationally infeasible as the space grows. Additionally, the field lacks a principled taxonomy for defining and comparing different levels of openness, making it difficult to assess generalization. This paper addresses both gaps by proposing a hierarchical openness taxonomy (L0–L3) and a structured search framework that avoids brute-force evaluation.

## Input / Output

The method takes an egocentric video clip as input, encoded into a fixed-dimensional feature vector v by a VLM backbone (EGO-VLP or LAVILA). The output is a single activity label expressed as an action-object pair (e.g., 'take fork'), selected from a search space that can range from a few hundred to over 195,000 candidate phrases depending on the openness level. A key practical detail is that ProbRes reduces VLM likelihood queries by 10–20× compared to exhaustive evaluation (e.g., 3,000 vs. 29,100 calls on EK100), with an average inference time of ~2 seconds per video on a single RTX 3090.

## Method

ProbRes performs a jump-diffusion-style stochastic search over the VLM text-embedding space of activity phrases, guided by structured commonsense priors from ConceptNet. The search proceeds in three phases: (1) an exploration phase that samples candidates from a mixture of the ConceptNet-derived prior and a uniform distribution, (2) an exploitation phase that shifts to likelihood-guided sampling using VLM scores, and (3) a deterministic residual refinement that decomposes top-k candidates into action and object components and re-ranks them via separate alignment scores. The search space is pre-structured by sorting embeddings by Euclidean distance to an anchor point, ensuring that stochastic jumps remain semantically local. The key insight is that structured priors from a knowledge graph can replace most VLM queries, enabling efficient navigation of spaces too large for exhaustive evaluation while maintaining or improving WUPS and phrase-level accuracy.

## Results

On L1 evaluation, LAVILA+ProbRes achieves the highest overall WUPS across all four datasets (53.34 on GTEA Gaze, 53.82 on GTEA Gaze+, 43.55 on EK100, 40.91 on Charades-Ego) while reducing VLM calls by 3–10× (e.g., 110 vs. 380 on GTEA Gaze; 3,000 vs. 29,100 on EK100). Activity-level WUPS improves by 4.8 points over LAVILA on GTEA Gaze (33.80 vs. 29.02) and by 2.4 points on Charades-Ego (13.71 vs. 11.34). Under L2/L3 settings, ProbRes maintains or improves WUPS while cutting VLM calls from 37,191 to 1,500 (L2) and from 195,714 to 5,000 (L3), confirming that structured priors scale to unconstrained search spaces.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProbRes consistently outperforms both VLM baselines (LAVILA, EGOVLP) and neuro-symbolic baselines (KGL, ALGO) on all four L1 datasets in overall WUPS. Against LAVILA, ProbRes gains +2.03 WUPS on GTEA Gaze (53.34 vs. 51.31) and +1.15 on Charades-Ego (40.91 vs. 39.76) while using only 29% and 24% of the VLM calls, respectively. Against EGOVLP, the gains are larger: +2.48 WUPS on GTEA Gaze and +0.52 on EK100, again with 71–90% fewer VLM queries. Neuro-symbolic methods KGL and ALGO are substantially weaker (e.g., KGL scores only 35.83 WUPS on GTEA Gaze vs. 53.34 for ProbRes), confirming that fixed knowledge graphs without adaptive likelihood refinement struggle in open-world settings. The ALGO+LAVILA hybrid is the closest competitor on GTEA Gaze+ (53.38 vs. 53.82 WUPS) but still requires frame-wise CLIP querying without the structured search efficiency of ProbRes.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProbRes sits at the intersection of neuro-symbolic open-world activity recognition (KGL, ALGO) and VLM-based video-language pretraining (EGO-VLP, LAVILA), but departs from both by replacing fixed knowledge-graph inference or exhaustive embedding matching with a stochastic jump-diffusion search over a structured activity space. The ConceptNet-based prior construction follows KGL's lineage, while the likelihood estimation reuses off-the-shelf VLM embeddings; the novelty lies in the probabilistic search mechanism that balances prior-guided exploration with likelihood-driven exploitation, and in the L0–L3 openness taxonomy that formalizes previously informal distinctions in 'open-world' settings. The paper also repurposes ideas from stochastic search and Bayesian inference (exploration-exploitation tradeoffs, residual refinement) into a discrete combinatorial setting over action-object pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The L0–L3 taxonomy provides the field with a principled, comparable framework for evaluating open-world egocentric recognition, addressing a long-standing ambiguity in how 'openness' is defined. The 70–97% reduction in VLM queries while maintaining or improving WUPS is practically significant for real-time deployment in assistive robotics and wearable AI. Key limitations remain: reliance on VLM embedding quality (which the authors show is insufficient for fine-grained affordance structure), the need for careful curation of LLM-generated search spaces at L2/L3, and the observation that neuro-symbolic baselines underperform even naive VLMs—suggesting that static knowledge graphs are poorly suited to dynamic, context-dependent activity spaces. Open questions include whether hyperbolic or other structured embeddings could replace the current Euclidean VLM space, and whether hierarchical or adaptive search strategies could further reduce computational cost at scale.
