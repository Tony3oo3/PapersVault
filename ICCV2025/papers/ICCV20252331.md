## Problem

Existing diffusion-based image editing methods attempt to handle object transformation, source-region inpainting, and target-region refinement simultaneously in a single optimization loop, which creates competing demands that degrade quality under large or geometrically complex transformations. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a decoupled pipeline that separates these subtasks and a training-free refinement mechanism that can handle both inpainting and structural completion without dedicated training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252331/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB source image, a binary object mask (generated via SAM with a few user clicks), a text editing instruction specifying the geometric transformation (e.g., 'rotate 30° along z-axis'), and an optional structural completion mask for controlled content generation. It produces a final edited image where the object has been repositioned, reoriented, or reshaped with a clean inpainted background and a refined target region that blends seamlessly. The pipeline internally generates intermediate artifacts—a coarse transformed image with target mask, an inpainted background, and a composite image—that feed sequentially into the refinement stage.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-step decoupled pipeline (object transformation → source inpainting → target refinement) where both refinement steps are handled by FreeFine, a training-free diffusion framework built on Stable Diffusion v1.5. FreeFine combines three modules: (1) Temporal Contextual Attention (TCA), which linearly blends mask-guided mutual self-attention (MMSA) with full self-attention over denoising steps via a temporally decreasing weight α_τ, allowing large structural edits early and fine detail refinement late; (2) Local Perturbation (LP), which selectively applies DDPM stochastic updates only within user-defined masked regions while retaining deterministic DDIM updates elsewhere, enabling dramatic local changes without global disruption; and (3) Content-specified Generation (CG), which injects text-conditioned cross-attention and classifier-free guidance exclusively within specified regions to steer newly generated content. The source image is DDIM-inverted once and its latent is reused for both inpainting and refinement, with the composite image additionally inverted to initialize the refinement step. For 3D edits, the transformation step leverages depth estimation (DepthAnything) or single-image 3D lifting (SV3D) to reproject the object after geometric manipulation.

## Results

On the GeoBench benchmark, FreeFine achieves the best or near-best results across all three task categories (2D edits, 3D edits, and structure completion). For 2D edits, FreeFine attains the highest Subject Consistency (0.907) and Background Consistency (0.971), along with the lowest Warp Error (0.055) and Mean Distance (9.25), outperforming the strongest competitor DesignEdit (WE 0.098, MD 10.15) by roughly 44% and 9% respectively. For 3D edits, FreeFine leads on FID (150.89), SUBC (0.786), WE (0.079), and MD (20.32), beating GeoDiffuser by 18% on MD. In structure completion, FreeFine surpasses BrushNet on FID (184.84 vs. 186.93), WE (0.056 vs. 0.060), and MD (9.56 vs. 11.31) without requiring any task-specific training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeFine outperforms all 2D editing baselines on edit precision metrics (WE and MD), achieving 0.055 WE and 9.25 MD versus DesignEdit's 0.098/10.15 and GeoDiffuser's 0.166/34.88, while also leading on subject and background consistency (SUBC 0.907, BC 0.971). However, GeoDiffuser retains a slight edge on image quality (FID 33.89 vs. 34.72; DINOv2 437.75 vs. 478.18), suggesting FreeFine trades a small amount of global texture fidelity for substantially better structural accuracy. In 3D edits, FreeFine dominates on all precision and consistency metrics, with MD of 20.32 compared to GeoDiffuser's 34.34 (41% lower) and Diffusion Handles' 26.10, while DragDiffusion and DesignEdit either underperform or do not support 3D rotation at all. For structure completion, FreeFine beats the training-based BrushNet on FID (184.84 vs. 186.93), WE (0.056 vs. 0.060), and MD (9.56 vs. 11.31), though BrushNet marginally leads on DINOv2 (2516.52 vs. 2526.38) and KD (0.971 vs. 0.982). The user study (35 participants, 2,622 votes) corroborates these quantitative findings, with FreeFine receiving 70.2% preference on 2D edits and 88.8% on 3D edits, far exceeding the closest competitor DragonDiffusion (20.8% on 2D).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MMSA (Mask-guided Mutual Self-Attention) mechanism from Masactrl [Cao et al., ICCV 2023] by introducing a temporal blending schedule (TCA) that transitions from mask-restricted attention to full self-attention across diffusion steps, addressing MMSA's inability to generate novel structural content. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant paradigm of unified single-loop editing (DragDiffusion, DragonDiffusion, RegionDrag) by proposing a decoupled three-stage pipeline that separates object transformation, source inpainting, and target refinement. The training-free refinement framework (FreeFine) draws on the broader line of diffusion-prior-based restoration work (RePaint, DDRM, DreamClean) but repurposes it for geometric editing by combining selective DDPM perturbation with localized classifier-free guidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of GeoBench provides the first dedicated benchmark for geometric image editing with 2D and 3D scenarios at varying difficulty levels, filling a gap where prior methods were evaluated on ad-hoc or drag-specific benchmarks. The finding that a training-free pipeline can match or exceed purpose-trained inpainting models (BrushNet, SD-inpainting) on structural completion is practically significant, lowering the barrier to high-quality geometric editing. Open questions include whether the decoupled approach scales to multi-object or scene-level edits, whether depth estimation errors in 3D transformations compound at larger rotation angles, and whether the 512×512 resolution constraint limits real-world applicability.
