## Problem

As Latent Video Diffusion Models scale to higher resolutions and longer durations, the Video VAE's encoding and decoding become a critical computational bottleneck, with existing models consuming tens of GB of memory for modest inputs. Prior approaches either inflate 2D convolutions into heavy 3D structures or adopt transformer backbones with quadratic complexity in resolution, leaving no Video VAE that simultaneously achieves ultra-low FLOPs and competitive reconstruction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250396/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB video of (T+1)×H×W×3 (or a single image with T=0) and produces a compressed latent z of shape (T'+1)×H'×W'×d with a fixed 4×8×8 spatiotemporal compression ratio and d ∈ {4, 16} latent channels. The first frame is processed with a 2D Haar wavelet transform while subsequent frames use a 3D Haar wavelet, enabling joint image-video encoding. The latent space is causal in the temporal dimension, and the decoder reconstructs the full-resolution video from the latent.

## Method

LeanVAE applies a non-overlapping patch operation (2×4×4 for video, 4×4 for image) directly in the Haar wavelet frequency domain, splitting each patch into low-frequency and high-frequency embeddings that are processed by separate branches of a lightweight Neighborhood-Aware Feedforward (NAF) backbone—each NAF layer combines a 3×3×3 depthwise convolution with a feedforward network and residual connections. The two branches are concatenated and fused by additional NAF layers to produce a 512-channel feature, which is then compressed to d channels via an ISTA-Net+ compressed-sensing bottleneck (2 unfolding iterations with learnable step sizes and thresholds) rather than a conventional two-linear-layer autoencoder. The decoder mirrors the encoder symmetrically, and causal padding in the temporal dimension ensures each frame depends only on past frames. Training uses L1 reconstruction in both RGB and wavelet domains, VGG-based LPIPS, PatchGAN adversarial loss, and KL regularization, with the key finding that omitting LayerNorm on pixel patches avoids grid-like block artifacts.

## Results

LeanVAE (40M params) achieves competitive video reconstruction with 4 latent channels: PSNR 26.04 / SSIM 0.7629 / LPIPS 0.0899 / rFVD 322.46 on DAVIS and PSNR 31.12 / SSIM 0.8957 / LPIPS 0.0432 / rFVD 162.55 on TokenBench, outperforming all 4-channel baselines on LPIPS and rFVD. With 16 latent channels, it reaches PSNR 30.15 / LPIPS 0.0461 / rFVD 119.48 (DAVIS) and PSNR 35.71 / LPIPS 0.0173 / rFVD 44.63 (TokenBench), approaching the best baseline VidTok. In video generation (Latte backbone), LeanVAE with 4 channels achieves FVD 49.59 (SkyTimelapse) and 164.45 (UCF101) at 6.64 samples/sec, a 315% throughput increase over original Latte. The model delivers up to 50× fewer FLOPs and 44× faster inference than VidTok while maintaining near-identical PSNR at 768² resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

With 4 latent channels, LeanVAE (40M) outperforms all baselines—CV-VAE (182M), Open-Sora VAE (393M), OD-VAE (239M), and VidTok (157M)—on LPIPS and rFVD across both DAVIS and TokenBench, while maintaining competitive PSNR/SSIM (within ~0.5 dB of the best). With 16 latent channels, LeanVAE trails VidTok (157M) by ~0.9 dB PSNR and ~0.003 LPIPS on DAVIS, but surpasses CogVideoX-VAE (206M), WF-VAE (316M), and Cosmos-Tokenizer (101M) on LPIPS and rFVD. In video generation (Table 2), LeanVAE-equipped Latte with 4 channels achieves FVD 49.59 on SkyTimelapse and 164.45 on UCF101, substantially beating original Latte (59.82 / 477.97) and LVDM (95.20 / 372.0), while matching or exceeding StyleGAN-V (79.52 / 1431.0) and VideoGPT (222.7 / 2880.6). The efficiency advantage is stark: LeanVAE uses 50× fewer FLOPs and runs 8–44× faster than VidTok across resolutions, completing a 17-frame 768² encode-decode in 0.46 s versus VidTok's 20.26 s with tiling. Notably, increasing LeanVAE to 16 channels improves reconstruction but degrades generation FVD (45.56 and 10.88 points worse), suggesting high-channel latent spaces require stricter regularization for diffusion training.

## Relation to Prior Work

LeanVAE departs from the dominant lineage of Video VAEs that inflate 2D Stable Diffusion convolutions to 3D (OD-VAE, CV-VAE, SliceVAE) and from transformer-based tokenizers (OmniTokenizer, ViTok) that suffer quadratic complexity. It selectively borrows wavelet transforms from WF-VAE and Cosmos Tokenizer but repurposes them for input-space enrichment rather than as the primary compression mechanism, and introduces ISTA-Net+ compressed sensing as a channel bottleneck—a technique previously unexplored in Video VAEs. The NAF backbone (depthwise convolution + feedforward) and non-overlapping frequency-domain patching represent a deliberate move away from both heavy 3D convolutions and global attention, positioning the model as a lightweight CNN-based alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50× FLOPs reduction and 44× inference speedup directly address the VAE encoding bottleneck that limits large-scale LVDM training, making high-resolution video pre-training substantially more tractable. The discovery that LayerNorm on pixel patches induces block artifacts in reconstruction is a broadly applicable insight for low-level vision models. However, the paper is limited to a fixed 4×8×8 compression ratio, does not explore text-to-video generation, and the finding that 16-channel latents degrade generation quality (despite better reconstruction) remains unresolved, pointing to an open question about the reconstruction–generation tradeoff in high-channel latent spaces.
