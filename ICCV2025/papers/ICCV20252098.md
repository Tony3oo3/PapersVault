## Problem

Dynamic street scene reconstruction for novel view synthesis typically relies on ground-truth object poses or 3D object trackers to position moving vehicles, but 3D trackers generalize poorly due to the scarcity of large-scale 3D annotated datasets (fewer than 4,000 scenes across major benchmarks). This paper asks how to leverage the strong generalization of 2D foundation-model trackers to achieve robust 3D dynamic object reconstruction without any 3D tracker or manual pose annotation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252098/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three frontal RGB camera views at 960×640, synchronized LiDAR point clouds, and camera intrinsics/extrinsics, with 2D segmentation masks from a pretrained GLEE tracker (not trained on Waymo or KITTI) providing the only object-level signal. It produces a 3D Gaussian scene representation with per-point deformation fields, from which novel views can be rendered at arbitrary camera poses and time steps at ~100 FPS. No ground-truth object poses or 3D tracking labels are required at any stage.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-part pipeline: (1) a robust 3D object tracking module that re-projects LiDAR points into each camera view, segments them using 2D tracker masks, associates partial point clouds across views (>50 shared points), and incrementally builds a canonical object model frame-by-frame via ICP alignment with overlap thresholds (≥30% updates the model, 10–30% records pose only, <10% discards the frame as a tracking failure); and (2) a motion learning module using a multi-resolution HexPlane (six 2D planes at base resolution 64, upsampled ×2 and ×4) whose features are decoded by two MLPs to predict per-point displacement ΔX_t and color change ΔC_t. A motion loss (Eq. 7) explicitly supervises the learned displacement against the ICP-derived trajectory for the first 40% of training, then is removed so the implicit field can autonomously correct pose errors and interpolate missed detections. Dynamic points use isotropic Gaussian marbles (identity rotation, uniform scale, 3-dim SH) to constrain deformation purely to translation and color. Training proceeds in two stages—static-only (20K iterations) then full-scene (50K iterations)—with a composite loss of L1 RGB, LiDAR depth, SSIM, grid TV, color regularization, and the temporary motion term.

## Results

On Waymo-NOTR, the proposed method achieves 32.56 PSNR / 0.936 SSIM / 0.059 LPIPS for scene reconstruction and 28.85 PSNR / 0.867 SSIM / 0.088 LPIPS for novel view synthesis, setting new state-of-the-art across all metrics. For dynamic-object-specific evaluation (DPSNR/DSSIM), it reaches 28.51/0.868 (reconstruction) and 25.58/0.779 (NVS), outperforming the closest self-supervised baseline S3Gaussian by 2.66 dB in DPSNR and 0.099 in DSSIM on novel view synthesis. On KITTI-75%, the method attains 25.49 PSNR / 0.889 SSIM / 0.063 LPIPS using only a 2D tracker, matching or exceeding Street Gaussians (which uses ground-truth tracking annotations) on SSIM and LPIPS while trailing by 0.3 dB in PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all six baselines on every metric in the Waymo-NOTR evaluation. Against S3Gaussian (the strongest self-supervised baseline requiring no extra input), it gains 2.66 dB in DPSNR and 0.099 in DSSIM for novel view synthesis, demonstrating that explicit motion supervision from the 2D tracker trajectory is critical for handling rapid vehicle motion. Against Street Gaussians (which relies on a 3D tracker, VoxelNext+SimpleTrack, pretrained on nuScenes), it improves PSNR by 1.87 dB and DPSNR by 0.96 dB on NVS, attributed to the superior generalization of the 2D foundation model (GLEE) over 3D trackers in unseen environments. On KITTI-75%, the method slightly trails Street Gaussians by 0.3 dB in PSNR (25.49 vs 25.79) but leads by 0.045 in SSIM and 0.018 in LPIPS; notably, Street Gaussians uses ground-truth tracking annotations on KITTI while the proposed method uses only a generalized 2D tracker. Against NeRF-based methods (EmerNeRF, MARS, NSG), the gap is substantial—over 3 dB in PSNR and 0.1+ in SSIM—confirming the advantage of the 3DGS representation for real-time street scene rendering. The ablation further shows that replacing the 2D tracker with a 3D tracker (VoxelNext+SimpleTrack) degrades DPSNR by 0.97 dB, and removing the motion loss causes a further 1.04 dB drop, validating both design choices.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework for dynamic street scenes by replacing the 3D object tracker used in Street Gaussians [89] with a 2D foundation model (GLEE) combined with LiDAR reprojection and ICP-based incremental reconstruction in canonical space. For motion modeling, it builds on the HexPlane deformation architecture from 4DGS [81] and the isotropic Gaussian marble formulation from [74], but departs from S3Gaussian [32] by introducing an explicit trajectory-based motion supervision loss that guides the deformation network during early training. The key conceptual departure is from the rigid-pose-transform paradigm (used by Street Gaussians and Driving Gaussian) toward a learned per-point motion in an implicit feature space that can autonomously correct tracking errors and handle non-rigid deformations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating both ground-truth pose annotations and 3D trackers, this work addresses a practical bottleneck in deploying street scene reconstruction to new, unannotated environments — a critical need for closed-loop autonomous driving simulation. The near-parity with GT-pose baselines (within ~0.1 dB PSNR) suggests that 2D foundation models, trained on orders of magnitude more data, can effectively substitute for 3D trackers in this pipeline. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond driving scenes (e.g., indoor or pedestrian-heavy environments), whether LiDAR can be replaced with monocular depth estimation, and how the method scales to very long sequences or high-velocity objects where ICP alignment may fail.
