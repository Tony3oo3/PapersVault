## Problem

Existing MLLMs lack proven ability to comprehend complex driving scenes, and the field suffers from three intertwined gaps: driving VQA datasets are either small, front-view-only, or restricted to instance-level grounded questions from nuScenes; no dedicated multi-view evaluation benchmark exists for driving scene understanding; and MLLMs are not natively designed to process multi-view camera inputs. This paper addresses all three by introducing a 1M-pair multi-view training dataset, an 8K multiple-choice evaluation benchmark spanning nine subtasks, and a BEV-integrated baseline model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252130/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes time-sequential multi-view camera images (6 cameras from nuPlan) spanning 1.5 seconds (3 frames at ~2 Hz), vehicle control signals (velocity, steering wheel angle), and a natural-language question. For training, 90% of the 1M QA pairs are free-form text answers generated via GPT-4o with chain-of-thought prompting; for evaluation, the 8K benchmark uses 4-option multiple-choice format. The output is a single text response, scored by accuracy on the benchmark.

## Method

BEV-LLM builds on LLaMA-3.2-Vision-11B by inserting a BEV-Fusion module between the vision encoder and the language model. Multi-view images are first encoded into per-frame visual tokens; BEV features are then constructed using BEVFormer's temporal self-attention to incorporate motion-aware context from prior timesteps. A spatio-temporal fusion attention (SFA) step cross-attends visual tokens (as queries) against the temporally-enriched BEV embeddings (as keys/values), transferring spatial and temporal awareness into the visual features without explicit temporal self-attention on the image side. Multi-scale features sampled every fourth layer are concatenated and projected into the LLaMA embedding space, where cross-attention layers in the decoder act as the multi-modal adapter. Training proceeds in two stages: first, only the projector is trained on front-view multi-timestep images to adapt the single-timestep LLaMA-3.2-Vision to video inputs; then the BEV-Fusion module is activated and the full model is fine-tuned on NuPlanQA-1M.

## Results

On NuPlanQA-Eval (multi-frame input), BEV-LLM achieves 78.7% overall accuracy, the highest among all open-source models, outperforming the second-best open-source model (LLaVA-OV 7B at 77.8%) by 0.9%. GPT-4o leads overall at 81.5%, but BEV-LLM surpasses all other models in all three ego-centric reasoning subtasks (ego-vehicle maneuver: 79.1%, situation assessment: 83.2%, action recommendation: 83.4%). Traffic light detection remains the hardest subtask for nearly all models, where BEV-LLM achieves 61.1%—surpassing the second-best open-source model by 12.7%. The ablation (Table 3) shows BEV-Fusion adds +13.2% overall accuracy over the no-BEV baseline under multi-view, multi-frame settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BEV-LLM (78.7%) trails GPT-4o (81.5%) by 2.8% overall but wins in 6 of 9 subtasks among open-source models, with the largest gaps in ego-centric reasoning (e.g., action recommendation: 83.4% vs. GPT-4o's 82.4%). Against Gemini-1.5-Pro (78.1%), BEV-LLM edges ahead by 0.6% overall, driven by stronger performance in ego-vehicle maneuver (79.1% vs. 73.8%) and situation assessment (83.2% vs. 73.8%). Among open-source video MLLMs, BEV-LLM outperforms VideoLLaMA2 7B (73.3%) by 5.4%, LLaVA-OV 7B (77.8%) by 0.9%, LLaVA-NV 32B (71.3%) by 7.4%, LLaVA-NV 7B (56.8%) by 21.9%, InternVL-1.5 20B (55.9%) by 22.8%, and Qwen2.5-VL 7B (41.8%) by 36.9%. Notably, BEV-LLM achieves the best traffic light accuracy (61.1%) among all open-source models, a subtask where even GPT-4o (68.5%) and Gemini (64.5%) struggle relative to their other scores. The paper notes that spatial relations recognition is the weakest skill area for all models, with even GPT-4o scoring 6.9% below its own average in that category.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the driving VQA line of work (NuScenes-QA, DriveLM, LingoQA, VLAAD) by addressing two key gaps: the reliance on single front-view images and the restriction to instance-level, fixed-format questions. Architecturally, BEV-LLM repurposes BEVFormer's temporal self-attention to construct motion-aware bird's-eye-view features, then fuses them into LLaMA-3.2-Vision via a Flamingo-style cross-attention adapter, creating a bridge between multi-view camera inputs and language generation that prior driving MLLMs lacked. The hierarchical nine-subtask taxonomy (Road Environment Perception → Spatial Relations Recognition → Ego-Centric Reasoning) departs from the coarser perception/prediction/planning groupings used in prior datasets, enabling finer-grained diagnostic evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1M-scale multi-view dataset and 8K multiple-choice benchmark fill a critical gap: no prior resource combined large scale, multi-view coverage, free-form answers, and comprehensive task breadth for driving VQA. The finding that even GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5-Pro score 4–7% below their own averages on spatial relations recognition, and that traffic light interpretation (not detection) is the single hardest subtask across nearly all models, provides actionable diagnostic signal for the field. Open questions include whether BEV fusion generalizes beyond LLaMA backbones, how to close the simulated-to-real gap, and whether the 1.5-second temporal window is sufficient for more complex driving decisions. The benchmark's multiple-choice format, while robust, may understate free-form generation quality that matters in real deployment.
