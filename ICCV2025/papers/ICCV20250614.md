## Problem

Transductive zero-shot learning with VLMs like TransCLIP exploits image-image structure within a dataset but largely ignores the rich semantic structure of the label space. Existing methods do not leverage class-specific attributes to improve class separation, align text semantics with image features, or enable dataset-specific model adaptation, leaving significant accuracy gains on the table especially in fine-grained domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250614/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unlabeled set of RGB images, a list of class names, and API access to a language model (Llama-3.1-8B or GPT-4o) to generate text attributes of the form '[domain] with [attribute]'. In the zero-shot setting no labeled data is required; in few-shot, a small number of labeled examples per class are incorporated by fixing their assignment vectors. The output includes per-image class labels, fine-tuned CLIP image and text encoders, a per-class attribute set that grows dynamically over iterations, and Gaussian mixture prototypes (mean and diagonal covariance) for each class.

## Method

GTA-CLIP iterates three steps for T=30 rounds: (1) it identifies the most confused class pairs by thresholding the top-2 probability gap in current assignments, then queries an LLM to generate discriminative attributes specific to those pairs, expanding the attribute space; (2) it performs attribute-augmented transductive inference by optimizing a joint objective combining a GMM clustering term, a Laplacian regularizer over image affinities, and a KL divergence term that aligns assignments with softmax similarities between image features and the mean of per-class attribute embeddings (rather than a single class-name prompt); (3) it fine-tunes both CLIP encoders using the top-k inferred labels per class with the AdaptCLIPZS objective, which handles class-level (not instance-level) supervision. The key insight is that these steps are complementary: dynamic attributes alone yield only marginal gains over static ones, but when paired with transductive inference and encoder adaptation they produce a 9.5% average improvement over CLIP and 4.0% over TransCLIP across 12 datasets.

## Results

On the 12-dataset zero-shot benchmark, GTA-CLIP achieves an average top-1 accuracy of 73.81% with ViT-B/16, improving over CLIP (64.35%) by 9.46% and over TransCLIP (69.80%) by 4.01%. With the larger ViT-L/14 encoder, the average reaches 79.22%, a 7.78% gain over CLIP and 3.22% over TransCLIP. The largest single-dataset improvement is on EuroSAT (+18.87% over CLIP with ViT-B/32), while Food-101 is the most challenging with only a modest 0.14% average gain. In the few-shot setting (ViT-B/16), GTA-CLIP outperforms TransCLIP-FS by 3.41%, 3.86%, and 3.01% for 1-, 4-, and 16-shot respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GTA-CLIP consistently outperforms the base CLIP zero-shot baseline [36] by 8.6–9.5% average top-1 accuracy across all three encoder sizes, with the largest gains on fine-grained datasets (e.g., +18.87% on EuroSAT with ViT-B/32). Against the state-of-the-art transductive method TransCLIP [61], GTA-CLIP wins on 11 of 12 datasets for each encoder, achieving +3.96% (B/32), +4.01% (B/16), and +3.22% (L/14) average improvements; the sole exception is UCF101 with ViT-L/14 where TransCLIP edges ahead (81.73 vs. 81.28). In the zero-shot-with-seen-classes setting (Table 3), GTA-CLIP surpasses all inductive adaptation baselines—CoOp [67], CoCoOp [66], CLIP-A [11], VDT [24], and AdaptCLIPZS [40]—on all five fine-grained datasets, e.g., 64.74% vs. 55.63% on CUB and 82.17% vs. 75.78% on Cars. In the few-shot setting, GTA-CLIP beats TransCLIP-FS [61] in every k-shot configuration (1/4/16), with the largest margin of +3.86% at 4-shot. Notably, zero-shot GTA-CLIP outperforms 1-shot TransCLIP, indicating that the attribute-guided transduction and adaptation pipeline can substitute for even minimal human labeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

GTA-CLIP unifies two previously separate lines of work: TransCLIP's transductive inference (GMM + Laplacian regularizer + KL divergence over image-text probabilities) and AdaptCLIPZS's LLM-generated attribute fine-tuning of CLIP. The key departure is making attribute generation *dynamic* and *confusion-driven*—iteratively querying an LLM for discriminative attributes only for the most confused class pairs—rather than using a static per-class attribute set. The paper frames its contribution as the first demonstration that attribute generation, transductive inference, and encoder adaptation are complementary when combined iteratively, whereas prior work explored each in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 9.5% average gain over CLIP and 4% over TransCLIP across 12 diverse datasets, achieved in 10–20 minutes on a single A100, offers a low-cost path to better labeling accuracy for domain experts (ecologists, remote-sensing scientists) who already have a fixed image collection. The finding that zero-shot GTA-CLIP outperforms 1-shot TransCLIP is practically valuable, as it reduces human labeling effort. Open questions include robustness to non-Gaussian class structure, applicability in streaming or online settings where the full test set is unavailable, and whether the LLM-hallucination risk becomes problematic in more fine-grained or out-of-domain scenarios.
