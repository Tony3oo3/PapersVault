## Problem

When upgrading a cross-modal retrieval system to a new, stronger Vision-Language Pretraining (VLP) model, the old gallery's embeddings become incompatible, forcing a costly full re-embedding (backfilling) of the entire dataset. Prior Backward-compatible Training (BT) work addressed only vision-only (image-to-image) retrieval; the cross-modal image-text setting with VLP models like CLIP remained entirely unexplored, and naively training from scratch would require hundreds of millions of supervised image-text pairs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained new VLP model (e.g., CLIP-ViT-L14 or H14), a frozen old VLP model (e.g., CLIP-ViT-B32), a large text-only corpus (~67M captions from BLIP) for pretraining the projection module, and a much smaller supervised image-text set (~4M pairs) for XBT fine-tuning. All embeddings are L2-normalized, and the projection module φ maps new-model embeddings into the old model's embedding dimensionality K. The output is a set of backward-compatible embeddings from the new model that can directly retrieve against the old model's fixed gallery, evaluated via R@K in both text→image and image→text directions on NoCaps, Flickr, and COCO.

## Method

XBT uses a two-stage procedure. First, a small 3-layer MLP projection module φ (Linear-LN-GELU-Linear-LN-GELU-Linear, hidden dim = 4× output dim) is pretrained using only text samples with a contrastive loss that aligns new text embeddings to old text embeddings, with Gaussian noise injected to prevent overfitting to the text domain. Second, the new VLP encoders are fine-tuned end-to-end with φ via a cross-modal contrastive loss on ~4M image-text pairs, where φ generates synthetic old-space embeddings for both images and texts. Crucially, the old model's encoders are not needed during this second stage. To preserve the new model's zero-shot capability and enable easy reversion, only layer-normalization parameters of the encoders are updated, augmented with LoRA (rank 16) on both encoders and 10 soft prompts on the vision encoder alone. This parameter-efficient design means a single epoch of training suffices, and removing the added parameters restores the original new-to-new retrieval behavior.

## Results

On NoCaps, XBT with B32→L14 achieves R@1 of 48.02% (text→image) and 75.02% (image→text), surpassing the old B32 model (45.14% and 71.38%) and the new L14 model's original performance (47.77% and 73.29%). For the larger L14→H14 upgrade, XBT reaches R@1 of 63.48% (text→image) and 77.22% (image→text), exceeding the old L14 baseline (47.77% and 73.29%) by 15.7 and 3.9 points respectively. On Large-Flickr and Large-COCO, XBT consistently outperforms the Base baseline by 3–7 R@1 points across all retrieval directions, confirming robustness at scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares XBT against three direct backward-contrastive-learning baselines (Full-tune, LoRA-only, and Base) that all use a randomly initialized projection module without text-only pretraining. Full-tune significantly underperforms the old model on all metrics (e.g., 41.60 vs. 45.14 R@1 for B32→L14 text→image), showing that full fine-tuning destroys the new model's representational power. LoRA-only and Base improve over Full-tune (43.40 and 43.48 R@1 respectively) but still fall short of the old B32 model (45.14), failing to meet the backward-compatibility criterion. XBT decisively outperforms all three baselines, achieving 48.02 R@1 (B32→L14) and 63.48 R@1 (L14→H14) for text→image retrieval, exceeding the old model by 2.9 and 15.7 points respectively. The gap between XBT and Base is particularly large for the L14→H14 case (63.48 vs. 56.18 R@1), demonstrating that the text-only pretrained projection module φ is the critical component enabling cross-modal backward compatibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Backward-compatible Training (BT), originally formulated for vision-only image retrieval [34], to the cross-modal (image-text) retrieval setting with Vision-Language Pretraining models like CLIP. It departs from prior BT approaches that train from scratch with a compatibility loss by introducing a text-only pretrained projection module φ that aligns new and old model embeddings without requiring the old model during training. The method also incorporates parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA and soft prompts) to preserve the new model's zero-shot capability, a design choice absent from earlier BT work that typically modifies the full model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

XBT addresses a practical and costly problem in production retrieval systems: the need to re-embed entire galleries when upgrading to a new VLP model. The text-only pretraining trick—learning a projection from text embeddings alone and applying it to images—is an elegant efficiency gain that reduces data requirements to ~1% of VLP pretraining scale. However, the approach is validated only on CLIP-family models and three datasets, and the core assumption that text embedding distributions mirror image distributions remains untested for more diverse or domain-specific VLP models. Open questions include whether XBT generalizes to non-CLIP architectures, video/audio modalities, and whether the projection module degrades when old and new models differ substantially in training data scale (e.g., 400M vs. 2B).
