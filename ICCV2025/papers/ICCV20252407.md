## Problem

Existing 3D object detection methods for autonomous driving face an inherent accuracy-efficiency trade-off: multimodal LiDAR-camera fusion models achieve high accuracy but impose substantial computational overhead that makes real-time inference infeasible on resource-constrained edge platforms, while lightweight single-modality models sacrifice detection quality under challenging conditions (distant, occluded, or sparse-voxel objects). No prior system adaptively allocates computational resources based on scenario difficulty to simultaneously meet the 100 ms perception-latency requirement and maintain high detection accuracy across diverse driving conditions.

## Input / Output

The system consumes raw LiDAR point clouds and synchronized RGB camera images. The LiDAR branch (UNet + sparse CNN) always runs to produce proposal regions with per-region confidence scores, which drive the routing decision; the RGB branch (ResNet) is invoked only when the dispatcher selects the Accuracy-Prioritized Expert, making image processing conditional rather than always-on. Outputs are 3D bounding boxes with class labels and confidence scores, evaluated under KITTI's R40 protocol (IoU 0.7 for cars, 0.5 for pedestrians/cyclists) and nuScenes' NDS metric with center-distance matching thresholds from 0.5 m to 4.0 m.

## Method

EMC2 is a scenario-aware Mixture-of-Experts system with three components: an Adaptive Multimodal Data Bridge (AMDB) that extracts LiDAR features and generates proposal regions, a Scenario-Adaptive Dispatcher that routes each scene to one of three experts based on object distance (threshold D) and proposal confidence (threshold C), and three specialized experts—LPE (2D CNN on BEV for close/clear scenes), VEE (3D sparse CNN for mixed-visibility scenes), and APE (3D CNN with LiDAR-camera fusion via Multiscale Pooling for distant/uncertain scenes). Training employs a hierarchical triple back-propagation strategy (supervising both AMDB branches and expert outputs separately) to stabilize early-stage learning, combined with balanced sub-dataset sampling and an adaptive learning-rate optimizer to mitigate MoE long-tail effects. For edge deployment, the system uses a custom 3D sparse convolution library under ONNX Runtime (reducing computation by 65–80% over dense convolutions), Multiscale Pooling to compress image features before 3D fusion, and system-level optimizations including overlapping communication/computation, staged thread management, prefix-sum acceleration, model pruning, quantization, and computational graph fusion.

## Results

On KITTI (Hard difficulty), EMC2 achieves 66.81% AP for pedestrians, 88.15% for cars, and 77.62% for cyclists, outperforming all 15 baselines. On nuScenes, it reaches 0.7241 mAP and 0.7316 NDS with only 87M active parameters. On the Jetson AGX Orin edge platform, EMC2 runs at 372.5 ms (KITTI) and 229.3 ms (nuScenes), delivering a 159.06% inference speedup over the slowest comparable baseline (PV-RCNN at 1787 ms) while maintaining superior accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMC2 outperforms all 15 baselines on KITTI Hard difficulty across every object class. Against the strongest multimodal competitor TED [53], EMC2 gains +3.18% AP on pedestrians (66.81 vs 63.63), +1.42% on cars (88.15 vs 86.73), and +6.03% on cyclists (77.62 vs 71.59). Against the best LiDAR-only method 3ONet [49], the margins are even larger: +6.59% (pedestrians), +3.98% (cars), and +6.44% (cyclists). On nuScenes, EMC2 surpasses FocalFormer [55] by +6.01% mAP (0.7241 vs 0.6640) and +2.26% NDS, and BEVFusion [56] by +3.89% mAP and +1.78% NDS, while using fewer active parameters (87M vs 156M and 189M). Critically, EMC2 is the only method in Table 1 with a reported Jetson latency (372.5 ms), running 2.6× faster than Voxel-RCNN multimodal (965 ms) and 4.8× faster than PV-RCNN (1787 ms), making it the sole method combining SOTA accuracy with demonstrated edge-deployable speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

EMC2 repurposes the Mixture-of-Experts paradigm—well established in large language models for token-level routing—into the domain of multimodal 3D object detection, where prior work (BEVFusion, FocalFormer, Voxel-RCNN, PV-RCNN) relies on a single monolithic architecture to handle all driving scenarios uniformly. Rather than building on a specific prior detection backbone, the paper composes existing components (UNet for LiDAR feature extraction, ResNet for image features, sparse 3D CNNs) under a novel scenario-adaptive dispatcher that routes based on object distance and proposal confidence. The system-level contributions (customized 3D sparse convolution, multiscale pooling, ONNX graph fusion) extend the edge-compilation literature (TensorRT, TVM, XLA) with detection-specific optimizations, positioning EMC2 as an algorithm-hardware co-design rather than a purely architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: most SOTA multimodal 3D detectors are too slow for real-time edge deployment on automotive-grade hardware, and the 159% inference speedup on Jetson AGX Orin (372.5 ms vs. 965–1787 ms for comparable-accuracy baselines) is directly relevant to the 100 ms perception-latency budget cited in the literature. However, the dispatcher relies on fixed, empirically chosen distance and confidence thresholds per dataset, limiting generalizability to unseen sensor configurations or novel driving environments; the authors themselves flag fully adaptive expert selection as future work. Open questions include whether the MoE routing generalizes beyond KITTI/nuScenes to more diverse or adversarial scenarios, and whether the hierarchical training strategy scales to more than three experts or to end-to-end driving stacks.
