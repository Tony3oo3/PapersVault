## Problem

CLIP-based prompt learning methods (e.g., CoOp) operate under a closed-set assumption and cannot detect unknown samples because unknown class names and their corresponding samples are unavailable. Existing workarounds—generating synthetic image-text pairs (LMC) or manually selecting specialized words from WordNet (M-Tuning)—are inflexible, unstable across diverse class compositions, and sensitive to the number of known classes. The paper addresses how to effectively extend CLIP-based models to few-shot open-set recognition using only known samples, without requiring unknown data or large generative models.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input in a few-shot setting (e.g., 16-shot per known class) and produces either a known class label or an 'unknown' flag. During training, only known-class images and their class names are available; the model learns N unknown text prompts (default N=5, up to 60 for large-scale benchmarks) alongside context prompts. At inference, unknown detection is performed by thresholding the maximum softmax probability over known classes, where the learned unknown text in the denominator regularizes confidence scores downward for out-of-distribution inputs.

## Method

UTL introduces two core components for learning unknown text without unknown samples. First, Universal Unknown Words Optimization (U²WO) constructs a constrained space for unknown text by performing PCA on word embeddings of 1000 base class names (filtered to exclude potential unknown classes), then representing each unknown text as a linear combination of the top-k principal component basis vectors (F=100), avoiding the overfitting risk of unconstrained learning. Second, Unknown Label Smoothing (ULS) performs contrastive learning between unknown text and known samples by assigning a small constant ε to all unknown class labels in the cross-entropy loss, which pushes unknown text embeddings away from known visual features without requiring actual unknown images. A dual-context strategy adds a separate context prompt for known classes to resolve optimization conflicts between known-class discriminability and unknown-class regularization, combined via a weighted loss (λ=0.9). The full predicted probability incorporates both known-class similarities and N unknown-text similarities in the softmax denominator, effectively widening the confidence gap between known and unknown samples.

## Results

On standard FSOR benchmarks (16-shot, ViT-B/16), UTL achieves an average AUROC of 93.34%, outperforming the strongest baseline M-Tuning (89.57%) by 3.77% and CoOp (88.68%) by 4.66%. On challenging large-scale benchmarks (iNaturalist, ImageNet-Hard, ImageNet-LT), UTL reaches 85.28% average AUROC, surpassing LoCoOp (80.60%) by 4.68% and M-Tuning (76.70%) by 8.58%. For closed-set accuracy (Table 2), UTL achieves 92.16% average ACC, marginally exceeding CoOp (92.07%) and CoCoOp (91.80%), confirming that unknown text learning does not degrade known-class classification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UTL consistently outperforms all 8 baselines across both standard and challenging benchmarks. Against the closest competitor M-Tuning (which uses manually selected WordNet words as unknown text), UTL gains 3.77% average AUROC on standard benchmarks and 8.58% on challenging ones, demonstrating that learned universal unknown words (U²WO) are more flexible than hand-picked open words. Compared to CoOp and CoCoOp (closed-set prompt learning), UTL improves AUROC by 4.66% and 4.14% respectively, confirming that the closed-set assumption fundamentally limits open-set performance. The gap over OOD-detection methods is even larger: UTL exceeds ID-like by 19.65% and NegPrompt by 12.25% on average, highlighting the instability of augmentation-based or negative-prompt approaches. On the most challenging datasets (iNaturalist, ImageNet-Hard, ImageNet-LT), UTL surpasses the second-best method (LoCoOp) by 4.59%, 9.5%, and 10.11% respectively, indicating that the advantage grows with task difficulty. For closed-set accuracy, UTL matches or slightly exceeds CoOp and CoCoOp (92.16% vs. 92.07% and 91.80%), showing that unknown text learning does not compromise known-class recognition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP prompt-learning lineage (CoOp, CoCoOp) to the few-shot open-set recognition (FSOR) setting, where prior prompt-learning methods operate under a closed-set assumption. The most direct predecessor is M-Tuning, which manually selects 'open words' from WordNet as unknown text; UTL replaces this with a learned universal unknown words space (U²WO) constructed via PCA over base-class embeddings, removing the need for hand-curated vocabulary. It also departs from LMC, which relies on ChatGPT and DALL-E to synthesize unknown image-text pairs, by learning unknown text entirely from known samples through a novel Unknown Label Smoothing (ULS) loss that assigns a small constant probability to all unknown classes during contrastive training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UTL provides a practical, self-contained recipe for making CLIP-based few-shot classifiers open-set-aware without requiring additional large generative models, synthetic data, or unknown samples—addressing a gap that has limited the adoption of prompt learning in realistic deployment. The dual-context strategy and the observed ACC-vs-AUROC conflict under unified context learning highlight a previously underexplored tension in joint known/unknown optimization. Open questions include whether the uniform ε constraint can be relaxed per-class, whether the PCA-based basis construction is optimal versus learned bases, and whether the approach generalizes to multi-modal or video settings where unknown text semantics are even less constrained.
