## Problem

Existing inherently interpretable image classifiers each satisfy only a subset of desirable explanation properties: B-cos networks provide faithful pixel-level attributions but lack global explanations, NW-Head offers case-based reasoning without pixel-space maps, and ProtoPNet's latent-space prototypes have been shown to produce spatially misaligned and potentially misleading explanations. No prior model simultaneously delivers local, global, and axiomatically faithful explanations for image classification.

## Input / Output

SIC takes a standard RGB image (any resolution compatible with the chosen backbone) and produces class logits via summed similarity scores, supporting both single-label and multi-label prediction. Beyond the class label, it outputs three complementary explanation types: (i) per-support-vector similarity scores (support evidence) quantifying how much each training exemplar contributed to the decision, (ii) pixel-level RGBA contribution maps for the test image (test contribution) showing which input regions aligned with the support vectors, and (iii) pixel-level RGBA contribution maps for each support image (support contribution) revealing what the model encoded in each support vector. The B-cos forward pass is summarized as a single input-dependent weight matrix, from which all contribution maps are derived in one pass.

## Method

SIC couples a B-cos feature extractor (DenseNet121, ResNet50, or Hybrid ViT) with a Nadaraya-Watson-style evidence predictor. During training, the model extracts class-specific support vectors by k-means clustering the non-negative latent features of all training samples per class, then replacing each centroid with the nearest actual training sample to preserve case-based reasoning. At inference, the test image's latent vector is converted to a non-negative vector, and the class logit is computed as the sum of temperature-normalized B-cos similarity scores between this vector and the class's support vectors plus a fixed bias. The B-cos transform (which replaces standard dot products with an alignment-enforcing variant) makes the entire forward pass piecewise-linear, so each prediction reduces to a single weight matrix whose rows directly yield faithful pixel-level contribution maps satisfying the axioms of completeness, sensitivity, implementation invariance, dummy, linearity, and symmetry preservation. Training uses binary cross-entropy loss to increase alignment pressure, and the architecture is agnostic to the backbone, supporting CNNs and vision transformers alike.

## Results

On Pascal VOC (multi-label) and RSNA (pathology detection), SIC matches black-box backbone accuracy within ±0.65%, while on Stanford Dogs (fine-grained, 120 classes) it trails by 3.7% accuracy. SIC outperforms 9 out of 14 compared models overall, with ProtoPNet and BagNet performing worse than all competing methods. On the FunnyBirds explainability benchmark, SIC achieves a background independence of 0.99, contrastivity of 0.95, completeness of 0.97, and correctness of 0.72, outperforming ProtoPNet and B-cos in 8 out of 9 metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ProtoPNet [8], SIC wins on both accuracy (ProtoPNet performs worse than all competing methods) and explainability (SIC outperforms it in 8/9 FunnyBirds metrics), while also providing faithful pixel-level attributions that ProtoPNet lacks. Against BagNet [6], the comparison is mixed: BagNet leads on FunnyBirds accuracy (+1%), correctness (+4%), and contrastivity (+4%), but SIC surpasses it on the remaining six explainability metrics and uniquely provides global explanations. Against NW-Head [31], SIC adds faithful pixel-level contribution maps (NW-Head only shows similar training images) and satisfies axiomatic faithfulness, which NW-Head does not. Against B-Cos [5], SIC inherits B-cos's faithful local explanations while adding global case-based reasoning via support vectors, and outperforms B-cos in 8/9 FunnyBirds explainability metrics. Overall, SIC is the only model in the comparison that simultaneously provides local, global, and faithful explanations (Table 1).

## Relation to Prior Work

SIC combines two previously separate lines of work: B-cos networks (Bohle et al.), which provide faithful pixel-level local explanations through weight-input alignment but lack global interpretability, and the Nadaraya-Watson head (Wang and Sabuncu), which enables case-based reasoning via similarity to class-specific support vectors but offers no pixel-space attributions. The paper positions itself against ProtoPNet and its extensions, which provide prototypical part-based explanations but have been shown to suffer from spatial misalignment between latent and input spaces. The key conceptual departure is that SIC is the first inherently interpretable architecture to simultaneously satisfy local, global, and faithful explanation criteria (Tab. 1), a combination no prior model achieved.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For decision-critical domains like medicine, the ability to debug a model by inspecting its support vectors and their pixel-level contributions is practically valuable, and the axiomatic proof that SIC's explanations are faithful strengthens the case for deploying inherently interpretable models over post-hoc methods. Limitations include a ~3.7% accuracy drop on fine-grained classification (Stanford Dogs) relative to black-box counterparts, and the k-means-based support vector extraction introduces computational overhead that may scale poorly with very large class spaces. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other modalities (e.g., video, 3D medical volumes) and whether the fixed support vector set can be made adaptive at inference time without sacrificing interpretability guarantees.
