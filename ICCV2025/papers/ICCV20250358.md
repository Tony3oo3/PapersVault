## Problem

Existing depth completion methods are supervised by imperfect ground truth depth maps from sensors, which lack high-frequency details in reflective, fine-structured, or weak-signal regions, leading to blurred or fragmented predictions. While depth foundation models (e.g., Depth Anything v2) excel at generating such high-frequency details from RGB alone, their outputs suffer from inconsistent metric scaling, making them unsuitable as direct supervision. The paper addresses this gap by selectively distilling only the high-frequency components from a frozen depth foundation model into a depth completion student.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a sparse depth map (from LiDAR or structured light) as input and produces a dense metric depth map as output. The sparse depth serves as metric anchors to ensure correct scale, while the RGB provides structural cues. The student model uses a DINOv2 encoder with a DPT multi-scale prediction head that progressively outputs depth maps at resolutions from 1/32 up to full resolution, with distillation applied at each scale layer.

## Method

HFD-Teacher is a teacher-student framework where a frozen Depth Anything v2 ViT-L acts as the teacher and a DINOv2+DPT student learns to complete depth. The key mechanism is Adaptive Local Wavelet Transform (ALWT): each depth map is partitioned into local blocks, and the DWT decomposition level (1–3, using bior3.3 wavelet) is assigned per block based on its variance and gradient magnitude relative to batch running means, enabling deeper decomposition in complex regions and shallower in flat areas. High-frequency subbands are isolated by zeroing the low-frequency component and reconstructing via inverse DWT, then aligned between teacher and student via an L1 distillation loss over valid high-frequency pixels. A second innovation, topological constraints, applies persistent homology to the student's high-frequency map, penalizing short-lived features (persistence below threshold θ) in the persistence diagram to suppress spurious edges. The total training loss combines the frequency distillation loss (α=0.5), topological loss (β=0.3), and standard L1 ground-truth supervision.

## Results

On NYUD-v2, HFD-Teacher (ViT-L) achieves the best high-frequency boundary error (ε_acc = 0.603 px, ε_comp = 1.211 px), substantially outperforming the next-best baselines MSPN (1.020 / 2.041) and BP-Net (1.028 / 2.872). On depth fidelity, it reaches RMSE = 0.087 m and AbsRel = 0.010, matching TPVD (0.086 / 0.010) while dominating on edge accuracy. On KITTI-DC, the ViT-L model attains the lowest RMSE (679.98 mm) and MAE (193.67 mm). Zero-shot evaluation on iBims-1 and DDAD confirms strong generalization, with ε_acc dropping from ~4.7–5.6 px (baselines) to 1.112 px and RMSE improving by over 3× on iBims-1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HFD-Teacher (ViT-L) achieves a decisive win on high-frequency boundary metrics across all 14 baselines on NYUD-v2: ε_acc of 0.603 px is 40% lower than the best baseline (MSPN, 1.020 px) and 66% lower than CSPN (1.747 px); ε_comp of 1.211 px is 41% lower than MSPN (2.041 px). On depth fidelity, the method is essentially tied with TPVD (RMSE 0.087 vs 0.086 m; AbsRel 0.010 for both; δ₁ 99.7% for both), while clearly surpassing all other baselines (e.g., BP-Net 0.089 m, NLSPN 0.092 m). On KITTI-DC, HFD-Teacher wins RMSE (679.98 mm) and MAE (193.67 mm) over all 14 baselines, with the closest competitor LP-Net at 684.71 mm / 186.63 mm (LP-Net edges out on MAE by 7 mm but trails on RMSE by 5 mm). In zero-shot settings on iBims-1 and DDAD, the method dominates all three compared baselines (NLSPN, CFormer, BP-Net) by large margins—e.g., RMSE 0.662 m vs 4.07–4.85 m on iBims-1, and RMSE 313.5 mm vs 702–919 mm on DDAD—demonstrating that the high-frequency distillation transfers effectively to unseen domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of depth completion, depth foundation models, and frequency-domain analysis. Prior depth completion methods (CSPN, NLSPN, BP-Net, etc.) rely on imperfect ground truth supervision, which inherently limits high-frequency detail recovery; frequency-aware approaches like RigNet and SEHLNet attempt to address this but operate on RGB-guided features rather than depth-specific frequency content. The key conceptual departure is repurposing a frozen depth foundation model (Depth Anything v2) as a 'partial teacher' that contributes only high-frequency subbands via an adaptive local wavelet decomposition, rather than serving as a full supervision signal. The topological constraint via persistent homology is borrowed from image segmentation literature [15] and applied here for the first time to regularize depth high-frequency maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine and underexplored limitation in depth completion: the quality ceiling imposed by noisy or incomplete ground truth in high-frequency regions. The teacher-student framework with foundation models is timely and likely to be adopted as more capable depth foundation models emerge, though the current reliance on a single frozen teacher (DAv2 ViT-L) and the added computational overhead of multi-level wavelet decomposition are practical constraints. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other foundation models, whether the topological constraint can be made differentiable and more expressive, and whether the zero-shot gains (impressive on iBims-1 and DDAD) hold against a broader set of baselines beyond the three compared. The method's dependence on the teacher's quality for high-frequency regions means it inherits any systematic biases in the foundation model's edge predictions.
