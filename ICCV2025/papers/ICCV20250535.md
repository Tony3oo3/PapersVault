## Problem

Existing autoregressive mesh generation methods suffer from two fundamental limitations: they produce manifold defects (surface holes, overlapping faces, missing components) due to inadequate modeling of local mesh structure, and they are constrained to low face counts because naive tokenization produces excessively long sequences. The paper identifies the neglect of manifold mesh locality—where adjacent faces share edges and face clusters converge at central vertices—as the root cause of both issues.

## Input / Output

Nautilus accepts either a point cloud (4,096 uniformly sampled points) or a single-view RGB image as conditioning input and produces a triangle mesh of up to 5,000 faces. The mesh is generated as an autoregressive sequence of discrete 2D coordinate tokens (u, v pairs from a 1024×2048 codebook) that are detokenized into interconnected faces via the Nautilus shell structure. Generation of a 5,000-face mesh takes approximately 4 minutes on a single GPU using probabilistic sampling at temperature 0.5.

## Method

Nautilus introduces a shell-based tokenization that organizes mesh faces around central vertices, so each new face is added by appending a single vertex rather than three, compressing sequence length to 1/4 of vanilla flattening while preserving spatial neighbor proximity. A continuous shell traversal strategy selects the next shell's center as the highest-degree neighbor of the last traversed vertex, maintaining short-distance dependencies across shells. A Dual-stream Point Conditioner provides multi-scale geometric guidance: a global encoder (Michelangelo) supplies cross-attention features for overall shape, while a local PointConv encoder captures fine-grained vertex-fan geometry injected directly into token embeddings. A 24-layer, ~500M-parameter transformer decoder autoregressively predicts the next coordinate token under cross-entropy loss, and for image conditioning the pretrained Michelangelo image encoder is integrated into the unified feature space with the point cloud encoder frozen.

## Results

On a 500-sample test set for point cloud conditioned mesh generation, Nautilus achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.087 and Hausdorff Distance of 0.176, substantially outperforming MeshAnything (0.133 / 0.293) and MeshAnythingV2 (0.106 / 0.248). In a user study, Nautilus receives an 88.68% satisfaction rate versus 10.27% for MeshAnything and 13.17% for MeshAnythingV2. For tokenization efficiency, Nautilus achieves a compression ratio of 0.275 (vs. 0.462 for AMT and 0.474 for EdgeRunner) and a local ratio of 0.554 (vs. 0.378 and 0.461), enabling generation of meshes up to 5,000 faces in 4 minutes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Nautilus decisively outperforms both MeshAnything and MeshAnythingV2 on all three metrics: it reduces Chamfer Distance by 35% (0.087 vs. 0.133) and 18% (0.087 vs. 0.106) respectively, and cuts Hausdorff Distance by 40% and 29%. The user study gap is particularly stark—Nautilus achieves 88.68% satisfaction versus 10.27% and 13.17%, a roughly 7× improvement over the strongest baseline. Against EdgeRunner, Nautilus is compared primarily on tokenization efficiency (Table 1) and image-conditioned qualitative results (Figure 7); Nautilus achieves a 42% lower compression ratio (0.275 vs. 0.474) and a higher local ratio (0.554 vs. 0.461), and produces sharper, more manifold-consistent meshes in the image-conditioned setting. The paper attributes its advantage to the Nautilus-style shell tokenization preserving local neighbor proximity and the Dual-stream Point Conditioner providing fine-grained geometric guidance, whereas prior methods suffer from excessive sequence lengths that limit training to meshes under 1,600 faces.

## Relation to Prior Work

Nautilus extends the autoregressive mesh generation paradigm established by MeshGPT and MeshAnything, which tokenize mesh faces into sequences for GPT-style decoding. Its key departure is the Nautilus-style shell tokenization, which reorganizes faces around shared central vertices to preserve spatial neighbor proximity and compress sequence length to 1/4, addressing the locality neglect in prior flat or strip-based serializations (MeshAnything, EdgeRunner). For conditioning, it builds on Michelangelo's multi-modal latent space for image input and introduces a Dual-stream Point Conditioner combining a global encoder with a local PointConv module, going beyond the single-stream point cloud conditioning in MeshAnything.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By pushing direct mesh generation to 5,000 faces with artist-like structural fidelity, Nautilus narrows the gap between automated and hand-crafted 3D assets, which is critical for scalable 3D content pipelines in games, AR/VR, and digital twins. The locality-aware tokenization is a general, architecture-agnostic technique that other autoregressive mesh generators could adopt. Open questions include whether the shell traversal degrades on highly non-manifold or extremely sparse topologies, whether the approach scales beyond 5,000 faces, and how it extends to text-conditioned or multi-view generation without relying on a separate pretrained shape encoder.
