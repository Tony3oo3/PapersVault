## Problem

CLIP zero-shot classification is severely undermined by spurious correlations, where the model disproportionately attends to background or contextual features rather than the core object. Existing robustification methods either require prior annotations of spurious features, domain expertise to craft debiasing prompts, or access to training-set statistics—information that is typically unavailable in real-world zero-shot deployment. The paper asks whether unknown spurious correlations can be mitigated at test time using only the test samples and a task-related text prompt.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB test image and a general text prompt (e.g., "a photo of a bird" rather than a class-specific prompt) at inference time. Internally, it maintains a sliding queue of up to 30 historical images to stabilize PCA-based semantic structure estimation, then produces k=3 segmentation masks over the image patches. The final output is a standard zero-shot class label computed exclusively on the identified core-object segment, with all other regions zero-masked to eliminate spurious signal.

## Method

TTR exploits an emergent object-localization capability in CLIP's image encoder: patch-level embeddings (excluding [CLS]) carry strong semantic structure that can be recovered via PCA to 3 dimensions followed by K-means clustering (k=3) with Gaussian smoothing. A queue of recent test images is maintained to stabilize the PCA subspace across samples. Among the resulting segments, the one most aligned (by inner product) with a general text prompt is selected as the core object, and zero-shot classification is performed only on that masked region. The approach requires no auxiliary segmentation model, no spurious-feature annotations, and no training-set access—operating entirely at test time with the pretrained CLIP weights frozen.

## Results

On the average of four general benchmark datasets (Waterbirds, CelebA, Metashifts, UrbanCars), TTR achieves 70.58% worst-group accuracy and 80.16% average accuracy, with the smallest WG–Avg gap of 9.58 points among all methods. This represents a 5.05% improvement in worst-group accuracy over the strongest baseline (TIE*, 65.53%) while requiring no spurious annotations or training statistics. On the biomedical ISIC dataset, TTR reaches 64.28% WG with only a 4.33-point gap, and on CXR it achieves 55.26% WG with a 7.99-point gap, both substantially outperforming baselines in worst-group robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TTR outperforms all nine baselines on worst-group accuracy across the four general datasets, with the largest margin over TIE* (+5.05% WG) and Orth-Cali (+7.89% WG), both of which require spurious text prompts. Among methods requiring no additional annotations (MTA, TENT, TTLRA, Zero-Shot), TTR leads by 16.07% WG over MTA and 15.35% over Zero-Shot, while also achieving the highest average accuracy (80.16%) and the smallest robustness gap (9.58). On the biomedical ISIC dataset, TTR achieves 64.28% WG versus 61.11% for TIE* and 60.18% for TTLRA, with a dramatically smaller gap (4.33 vs. 10.57 and 6.81). On CXR, TTR reaches 55.26% WG, surpassing the next-best Orth-Cali (42.11%) by 13.15 points, though TTLSA achieves higher average accuracy (80.77%) at the cost of a 70.24-point gap, indicating it predominantly predicts one class. Notably, TTR is the only method that simultaneously achieves the best WG, best Avg, and smallest Gap on the general dataset average, and it does so without any spurious annotations, training statistics, or auxiliary models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of spurious-correlation mitigation for VLMs (Orth-Cali, ROBOSHOT, TIE*, BEND-VLM), which all require prior identification or annotation of spurious features, and from test-time adaptation methods (TTLSA, MTA, TTLRA) that need training-set statistics or fail under severe spurious correlation. Instead of debiasing the text space or calibrating predictions, it repurposes a standard dimensionality-reduction-plus-clustering pipeline (PCA + K-means) on CLIP's patch-level image embeddings to perform test-time object localization, drawing on the observation that CLIP's contrastive pre-training yields an emergent semantic segmentation capability. The approach is conceptually inspired by human recognition-by-components (localize-then-classify) but requires no auxiliary detection or segmentation model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TTR offers a practical, annotation-free recipe for robust zero-shot CLIP classification that is broadly deployable in settings where spurious attributes are unknown and training data is inaccessible—a common real-world constraint. The discovery that CLIP's image encoder can segment core objects via simple PCA + K-means on patch embeddings is a useful empirical insight that may inform future work on leveraging foundation-model internal representations for downstream tasks. Key limitations include dependence on CLIP's segmentation quality (failing when spurious cues are abstract, e.g., texture) and sensitivity to prompt quality in complex scenarios; extending TTR to other multimodal tasks and to settings with multiple overlapping objects remains open.
