## Problem

Existing video understanding MLLMs decouple spatial and temporal processing, performing spatio-temporal interaction only at the high-level token or query stage. By the time temporal interaction occurs, fine-grained spatial details of rapidly moving objects have already been lost through motion blur and feature compression (pooling, Q-former, clustering), causing temporally critical regions to be underemphasized and degrading comprehension in dynamic scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251219/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video clip decomposed via MPEG-4 into I-frames (keyframes) and P/B-frames (non-keyframes), along with a text question. Four DynImgs are synthesized per video, each comprising one high-resolution keyframe with four resized non-keyframes concatenated beneath it as temporal prompts, yielding 576 visual tokens total (vs. 2304 for PLLaVA). Outputs are free-form text answers for open-ended QA (MSVD, MSRVTT, TGIF, ActivityNet) and multiple-choice selections for MVBench's 20 spatiotemporal reasoning categories.

## Method

DynImg advances spatio-temporal interaction to the pixel level by overlaying non-keyframes as temporal prompts directly onto keyframes before visual feature extraction, so the encoder's self-attention can link keyframe patches to their deformed counterparts in the prompts and learn to upweight dynamic regions. A novel 4D Rotary Position Embedding over (Height, Width, Temporal, Sequence) coordinates is introduced to preserve correct spatio-temporal adjacency within the composite DynImg format; the temporal coordinate is 0 at the keyframe and increases symmetrically for preceding/following prompts, while θ_h, θ_w, θ_t are initialized to zero and learned during training to avoid disrupting the pre-trained LLM. The full pipeline uses a SigLIP-so400m384 visual encoder, a feedforward projection with adaptive average pooling (16×12×12), and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct as the LLM, with all three modules trained end-to-end on a mixture of video captioning, classification, conversation, reasoning, and QA data.

## Results

On open-ended video QA, DynImg achieves 78.6% accuracy (score 4.2) on MSVD, 64.1% (3.5) on MSRVTT, 57.9% (3.6) on ActivityNet, and 77.5% (4.0) on TGIF, surpassing the previous best (PLLaVA) by approximately 2% on each benchmark. On the MVBench multi-choice benchmark, DynImg reaches 55.8% average accuracy using only 576 visual tokens (4 DynImgs), outperforming ST-LLM (54.9% with 256 tokens) and PLLaVA (46.6% with 2304 tokens). Gains are most pronounced on motion-sensitive subtasks: Moving Direction (+21.0%), Moving Count (+15.0%), and Moving Attribute (+26.5%) over the next-best method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynImg outperforms all 13 baselines on the four main open-ended QA benchmarks. Against the closest competitor PLLaVA [49], DynImg gains +2.0% on MSVD (78.6 vs 76.6), +2.1% on MSRVTT (64.1 vs 62.0), +1.6% on ActivityNet (57.9 vs 56.3), and matches on TGIF (77.5 vs 77.5), while also improving the Video-ChatGPT average score from 3.12 to 3.25. On MVBench, DynImg (55.8% avg) beats ST-LLM (54.9%) by 0.9% and VideoChat2 (51.1%) by 4.7%, despite using fewer visual tokens (576 vs 256 and 1536 respectively). The largest margins appear on motion-sensitive subtasks: Moving Direction 44.5% vs ST-LLM's 42.5%, Moving Count 57.0% vs ST-LLM's 56.5%, and Moving Attribute 78.5% vs ST-LLM's 78.5% (tied) but far ahead of VideoChat2's 58.5%. DynImg also surpasses the GPT-4V-based IG-VLM [20] on MSVD (78.6 vs 76.3) and ActivityNet (57.9 vs 57.0) while using a 7B open-source LLM rather than a proprietary model. Notably, DynImg achieves these gains with only 4 DynImgs (576 tokens) versus PLLaVA's 16 frames (2304 tokens), yielding a ~4× reduction in visual token count.

## Relation to Prior Work

DynImg departs from the dominant paradigm in video MLLMs (Video-ChatGPT, Video-LLaMA, VideoChat) that decouples spatial and temporal processing into separate modules operating on compressed tokens. Instead, it draws on the visual-prompt literature from image understanding (bounding boxes, labels that shift encoder attention) and repurposes non-keyframes as pixel-level temporal prompts that guide the vision encoder's self-attention before any spatial compression occurs. The training recipe and adaptive average pooling are inherited from PLLaVA, while the 4D Rotary Position Embedding extends standard 1D RoPE to handle the novel keyframe-plus-prompt composition format.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that motion blur causes fine-grained spatial details of rapidly moving objects to be under-represented during feature extraction, and that this loss is irrecoverable at the token level—provides a principled justification for advancing spatio-temporal interaction to the pixel stage. The token-efficiency result (4 DynImgs matching 16-frame PLLaVA) is practically significant for deployment. Open questions include how the approach scales to very long videos, whether the MPEG-4 I-frame selection strategy generalizes to non-MPEG sources, and whether the 4D RoPE can be replaced by simpler positional schemes as LLMs become more robust to novel input formats.
