## Problem

Existing EgoVQL methods rely solely on the given visual query as the target cue, which is insufficient under severe appearance variations (pose change, motion blur, occlusion) and cluttering backgrounds typical of first-person videos. Because the query is cropped outside the search video, a significant appearance gap exists between the query and the in-video target, causing localization to degrade in complex scenes. The paper asks whether target-relevant information mined directly from the video itself can supplement the query cue to improve robustness.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single visual query image (a crop outside the search video) and a 30-frame first-person video clip (frames randomly selected to cover at least part of the response track), both resized to 480×480. It outputs the most recent spatio-temporal tube: per-frame target bounding boxes with temporal confidence scores, from which a peak is selected and a bidirectional track is generated. Feature extraction uses a shared DINOv2-pretrained ViT backbone, and the final prediction heads (regression + classification with multi-scale anchors) follow the VQLoC design.

## Method

PRVQL is a K-stage (K=3) progressive architecture where each non-final stage extracts two types of target knowledge from the video and uses them to refine features for the next stage. Appearance Knowledge Generation (AKG) predicts per-frame target regions via classification/regression heads on target-aware video features, selects the top-n (n=3) highest-confidence boxes above a threshold τ=0.7, and extracts appearance knowledge via RoIAlign (size 5) from the original video features. Spatial Knowledge Generation (SKG) combines the cross-attention maps (target-aware) and the diagonal of the masked self-attention maps (temporal-aware) through bilinear interpolation and a weighted sum (α=0.5) to produce spatial knowledge. The appearance knowledge refines the query feature via a cross-attention block (QFR), while the spatial knowledge refines the initial video feature via pixel-wise multiplication with a residual connection (VFR, β=0.1). This progressive loop—knowledge → refined features → better knowledge—gradually sharpens both query and video representations, and the final stage applies the standard prediction heads for localization.

## Results

On the Ego4D validation set, PRVQL achieves 0.35 tAP25, 0.27 stAP25, 47.87% recovery, and 57.93% success, outperforming the previous best method VQLoC (0.31 tAP25, 0.22 stAP25) by 4% and 5% respectively. On the Ego4D test set, PRVQL further improves to 0.37 tAP25 and 0.28 stAP25, with 59.43% success. PRVQL runs at 30 FPS, slightly slower than VQLoC (36 FPS) but significantly faster than multi-stage methods like SiamRCNN, NFM, and CocoFormer (3 FPS each).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRVQL outperforms all five baselines on every metric across both the Ego4D validation and test sets. Against the closest competitor VQLoC (NeurIPS 2023), PRVQL gains 4% on tAP25 (0.35 vs. 0.31) and 5% on stAP25 (0.27 vs. 0.22) on validation, and 5% and 4% respectively on the test set, while also improving recovery by 0.82% and success by 2.04%. Against CocoFormer (CVPR 2023), PRVQL leads by 9% on tAP25 and 8% on stAP25 on validation. NFM (VQ2D Challenge 2022) trails PRVQL by 9% on tAP25 and 8% on stAP25. SiamRCNN (CVPR 2020) and STARK (ICCV 2021) are substantially weaker, with STARK achieving only 0.10 tAP25 and 12.41% recovery, reflecting the limitations of multi-stage detection-and-tracking pipelines. In terms of speed, PRVQL (30 FPS) is comparable to VQLoC (36 FPS) and far faster than the 3 FPS methods (SiamRCNN, NFM, CocoFormer), offering a favorable accuracy-speed tradeoff.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRVQL builds directly on VQLoC [14], the single-stage end-to-end Transformer framework for EgoVQL, adopting its backbone (DINOv2/ViT), prediction heads, and inference strategy. It departs from all prior EgoVQL methods (STARK, SiamRCNN, NFM, CocoFormer, VQLoC) that treat the given visual query as the sole localization cue, by additionally mining target-relevant appearance and spatial knowledge from the video itself. The progressive multi-stage refinement architecture draws conceptual inspiration from cascade detection and progressive segmentation networks, but applies this paradigm to EgoVQL for the first time, using cross-attention and masked self-attention to iteratively refine query and video features guided by extracted knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PRVQL addresses a practical limitation in egocentric video understanding—severe appearance variation and cluttering backgrounds that degrade query-only localization—by showing that the video itself contains exploitable target information. The 4–5% gains over VQLoC on Ego4D are meaningful for downstream applications in AR/VR and robotics where robust localization is critical. Limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (Ego4D), a modest speed tradeoff (30 vs. 36 FPS), and the progressive architecture's sensitivity to the number of stages (K=4 degrades performance). Open questions include whether the knowledge-guided refinement strategy transfers to other video grounding or tracking tasks, and whether richer knowledge types (e.g., semantic or motion-based) could further close the gap.
