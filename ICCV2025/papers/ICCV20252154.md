## Problem

Conventional GANs suffer from training instability and mode collapse due to the adversarial game between separate generator and discriminator networks, while standard diffusion models optimize each denoising timestep independently under a Markov assumption, limiting semantic information exchange across steps. Existing hybrid approaches that combine GANs with diffusion models require a separate discriminator, which increases memory overhead and reintroduces GAN-specific instabilities. This paper addresses how to integrate adversarial training directly into the diffusion denoising process without a separate discriminator network.

## Input / Output

The framework operates in the latent space of Stable Diffusion 2.1, accepting text prompts, text with bounding boxes (GLIGEN), single RGB images (2D-to-3D), or small image sets with text (Textual Inversion) as conditioning signals, and producing 512×512 generated images or multi-view consistent image sets. The adversarial regularizer is applied at every denoising timestep during training, with the margin parameter m set empirically by computing average noise-prediction error on the training set every 5 epochs, and the adversarial weight fixed at λ_adv = 0.01 across all experiments.

## Method

GAD unifies the generator and discriminator into a single U-Net by recognizing the structural analogy between the energy-based GAN auto-encoder discriminator and the LDM noise-prediction U-Net. The generator G(z₀,t) is the U-Net's noise prediction on a perturbed latent, while the discriminator D(z,t) measures the L1 discrepancy between predicted and true noise; the generator loss is therefore identical to the standard LDM denoising objective. An adversarial regularizer L_adv = [m − ‖ε_θ(√ᾱ_t z₀ + √(1−ᾱ_t)ε, t) − ε‖]₊ is added to enforce a margin between noise predictions on real (noisy) latents at step t and denoised latents from step t+1, promoting cross-timestep semantic exchange. Rather than alternating generator and discriminator updates as in conventional GANs, the total loss L_gad = L_ldm + λ_adv·L_adv is optimized jointly in a single backward pass, eliminating the need for a separate discriminator and its associated memory cost while mitigating mode collapse.

## Results

GAD consistently improves all evaluated baselines across three task families. On text-to-image (Stable Diffusion), FID drops from 13.52 to 9.68 and CLIP score rises from 0.3143 to 0.3471. On conditional generation, GLIGEN's FID improves from 12.26 to 9.12 and Textual Inversion's FID drops from 13.74 to 8.71 (CLIP similarity 0.712→0.764). On 2D-to-3D, SyncDreamer's PSNR increases from 20.25 to 22.81 and Era3D's from 22.74 to 24.12, with corresponding SSIM and LPIPS gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAD outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against Stable Diffusion [36], it reduces FID by 28.4% (13.52→9.68) and raises CLIP score by 10.4% (0.3143→0.3471). Against GLIGEN [24], FID drops from 12.26 to 9.12 (−25.6%) and CLIP score improves from 0.292 to 0.311. Against Textual Inversion [15], the FID improvement is the largest in the paper (13.74→8.71, −36.6%), with CLIP similarity rising from 0.712 to 0.764, indicating stronger concept preservation under data-scarce conditions. In 2D-to-3D, SyncDreamer [26] gains +2.56 dB PSNR (20.25→22.81) and Era3D [22] gains +1.38 dB (22.74→24.12), with SSIM improvements of 0.060 and 0.054 respectively and LPIPS reductions of 0.027 and 0.024. No baseline is matched or exceeded by GAD; the method is strictly superior across all five comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work: energy-based GANs (EBGANs), which use an auto-encoder as a margin-based discriminator, and latent diffusion models (LDMs), which use a U-Net for noise prediction in latent space. The key departure from prior GAN-diffusion hybrids (e.g., Diffusion-GAN, D3PM-GAN, adversarial distillation methods) is the elimination of a separate discriminator entirely: the same U-Net serves as both generator and discriminator, and the adversarial term reduces to a simple margin-based regularizer added to the standard diffusion loss. This is conceptually distinct from approaches that attach an independent discriminator in image or latent space, which the authors argue reintroduces GAN instabilities and memory overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is that GAD is a drop-in regularizer requiring no architectural changes, no additional parameters, and no alternating GAN training loop, making it easy to retrofit onto existing diffusion pipelines (Stable Diffusion, GLIGEN, Textual Inversion, SyncDreamer, Era3D). The consistent FID/CLIP/SSIM/LPIPS gains across text-to-image, conditional generation, and 2D-to-3D tasks suggest the adversarial margin is a broadly useful inductive bias, particularly under data-scarce or multi-constraint settings. However, the authors acknowledge that coupling generation and discrimination into one network limits independent tuning of each role, and the theoretical justification for why the margin term improves cross-timestep semantic flow remains informal. Open questions include whether the fixed λ_adv = 0.01 generalizes across model scales and whether the approach interacts well with recent fast-sampling or distillation techniques.
