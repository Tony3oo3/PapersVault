## Problem

Existing Video-LLMs rely on uniform frame sampling, which is query-agnostic, treats all frames equally, and becomes increasingly sparse for long videos, causing critical temporal transitions and fine-grained spatial details to be missed. Additionally, fixed-resolution processing wastes computational budget on less informative frames while under-resolving frames that contain crucial details like small objects or text.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video downsampled to T candidate frames (e.g., 128) via uniform sampling and a natural-language text query. It outputs a selected set of N frames (e.g., 4 high + 8 medium + 32 low resolution) with per-frame resolution assignments following the ratio r^(1) = 4·r^(2) = 16·r^(3), constrained by the token budget equation K + M/4 + N/16 = 8. The selected multi-resolution frames are then fed as visual tokens into a standard Video-LLM for autoregressive answer generation.

## Method

Q-Frame is a training-free, plug-and-play pipeline with three stages. First, Cross-modal Query Retrieval (CQR) encodes the query and all candidate frames with a pre-trained CLIP model and computes per-frame relevance scores via inner product. Second, Query-Aware Frame Selection (QFS) converts these scores into a softmax probability distribution (with temperature scaling) and applies the Gumbel-Max trick to stochastically sample the top-K frames, introducing controlled randomness that improves robustness over deterministic top-K selection. Third, Multi-Resolution Adaptation (MRA) assigns high, medium, or low resolution to frames based on their rank within the selected set, preserving fine details in the most query-relevant frames while reducing token cost for less critical ones. The entire framework requires no fine-tuning of the Video-LLM and is model-agnostic, applicable to both open-source (VILA-V1.5, Qwen2-VL) and closed-source (GPT-4o) backbones.

## Results

Q-Frame consistently improves over uniform frame sampling across all three backbone models. With Qwen2-VL (fixed token budget, 4+8+32 frames at varying resolutions), Q-Frame achieves 65.4% on MLVU, 58.4% on LongVideoBench, and 58.3/61.8% on Video-MME (w/o/w subtitles), surpassing the Qwen2-VL baseline by +8.5, +4.9, and +4.6/+2.4 points respectively. With VILA-V1.5 (fixed 8 frames), Q-Frame reaches 54.4% on MLVU and 50.7/55.0% on Video-MME, outperforming both the uniform baseline (46.3%, 47.5/50.0%) and FRAME-VOYAGER (49.8%, 50.5/53.6%). GPT-4o + Q-Frame attains the best Video-MME overall score of 63.8/66.5% and 58.6% on LongVideoBench.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Q-Frame outperforms uniform frame sampling on every backbone model and every benchmark: VILA-V1.5 gains +8.1 on MLVU and +3.2/+5.0 on Video-MME; GPT-4o gains +5.3 on LongVideoBench and +1.9/+2.0 on Video-MME; Qwen2-VL gains +8.5 on MLVU, +4.9 on LongVideoBench, and +4.6/+2.4 on Video-MME. Against the closest competing keyframe-selection method, FRAME-VOYAGER (which trains a frame-ranking model via loss-driven combinatorial optimization), Q-Frame with VILA-V1.5 achieves 54.4% vs. 49.8% on MLVU and 50.7/55.0% vs. 50.5/53.6% on Video-MME, while remaining entirely training-free. Qwen2-VL + Q-Frame attains the highest MLVU score (65.4%) among all reported methods, surpassing Video-XL (64.9%) and LLaVA-OneVision (64.7%). GPT-4o + Q-Frame achieves the best Video-MME overall accuracy (63.8/66.5%), exceeding the next-best LongVA (52.6/54.3) by over 11 points. The gains are most pronounced on longer videos (15–60 min on LongVideoBench: +7.5 to +10.5 points) and on fine-grained tasks such as OCR, counting, and reasoning, confirming that query-aware selection and multi-resolution adaptation are particularly beneficial when temporal sparsity and spatial detail matter most.

## Relation to Prior Work

Q-Frame sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: query-aware keyframe selection (KeyVideoLLM's top-K retrieval, Frame-Voyager's trained combinatorial optimizer) and any-resolution processing (Qwen2-VL's Native Dynamic Resolution, InternVL's patch partitioning). It departs from prior keyframe selection by replacing learned or motion-based criteria with a training-free CLIP similarity score plus the Gumbel-Max trick for stochastic top-K sampling, and it departs from prior any-resolution work by extending variable-resolution tokenization from Image-LLMs to Video-LLMs for the first time. The combination of these two mechanisms into a single plug-and-play pipeline that requires no fine-tuning of the downstream Video-LLM is the paper's core conceptual contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that Q-Frame can be dropped onto any Video-LLM (open-source or API-based) without retraining, making it immediately deployable for long-video understanding where token budgets are tight. A notable limitation is that the multi-resolution component (MRA) only applies to models with native dynamic-resolution support (e.g., Qwen2-VL), and the CLIP-based matching may struggle with queries requiring fine-grained temporal reasoning beyond single-frame semantics. Open questions include whether the Gumbel-Max stochasticity introduces undesirable variance across runs, whether the approach generalizes to video generation or editing tasks, and whether more sophisticated temporal modeling (beyond per-frame CLIP scores) could further improve selection quality for long-horizon queries.
