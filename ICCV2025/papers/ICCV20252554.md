## Problem

Vision foundation models produce features at 16× or greater spatial downscale relative to input images, limiting pixel-level understanding. Existing task-agnostic upsamplers (FeatUp, LiFT) compute their training loss at low resolution, leaving high-resolution features severely under-constrained and producing blurry outputs. The paper identifies that both the upsampler architecture and the training objective must be jointly optimized to achieve sharp, fine-grained features.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image resized to a fixed VFM input resolution (224×224 or 336×336) and the corresponding low-resolution VFM feature map (e.g., 16×16 for DINOv2-S/14) as inputs, and produces a high-resolution feature map at the full input image resolution. Because the architecture is coordinate-based, the output resolution is decoupled from the input: adjusting the coordinate grid allows generating features at arbitrary scales (56, 112, 224, or 448) without retraining, unlike multi-layer upsamplers restricted to fixed scaling factors.

## Method

LoftUp is a coordinate-based cross-attention transformer that encodes full-resolution pixel coordinates with sinusoidal positional embeddings, concatenates them with RGB values, and projects the result via a convolutional layer to form queries. An L-block cross-attention transformer then attends these high-resolution queries to the low-resolution VFM features (as keys and values), enabling global content-aware upsampling in a single step rather than through multiple interpolation layers. Training uses a two-stage objective: Stage 1 supervises the upsampler against mask-refined bicubic features (pseudo-GT constructed by blending SAM class-agnostic masks with bicubic-upsampled features), and Stage 2 applies self-distillation where an EMA-updated teacher processes high-resolution crops while the student processes full images, with the teacher's mask-refined output serving as full-resolution pseudo-GT. This full-resolution supervision signal is the key differentiator from prior work, which trains at 1/16 or 1/8 of the target resolution.

## Results

On semantic segmentation, LoftUp achieves 61.11 mIoU on COCO-Stuff and 53.10 mIoU on Cityscapes, outperforming the best prior upsampler (FeatUp) by 7.3% and 15.6% relative improvement, respectively. For depth estimation on NAVI, LoftUp reduces RMSE to 0.0921 (vs. 0.1071 for low-res) and boosts recall to 91.35%, the only method to significantly beat the low-resolution baseline. On video object segmentation (DAVIS2017), LoftUp achieves 60.25 J&F Mean, a 39.6% increase in J Mean and 97.6% increase in F Mean over the low-res baseline, representing a nearly 50% improvement over prior SoTA upsamplers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoftUp consistently outperforms all four baselines across every evaluated task. Against FeatUp (ICLR 2024), LoftUp gains +4.81 mIoU on COCO-Stuff (61.11 vs. 56.30), +8.91 mIoU on Cityscapes (53.10 vs. 44.19), and +15.95 J&F Mean on DAVIS2017 video object segmentation (60.25 vs. 44.30). Against LiFT (ECCV 2024), the margins are even larger: +7.76 mIoU on COCO, +17.30 mIoU on Cityscapes, and +18.02 J&F Mean on DAVIS, with LiFT notably failing on interactive segmentation (29.66 IoU on GrabCut vs. LoftUp's 78.49). Against LIIF (CVPR 2021), LoftUp achieves +8.87 mIoU on COCO and +12.74 mIoU on Cityscapes, while LIIF's local-only interactions cause it to underperform even the low-res baseline on depth (79.85 vs. 89.08 recall). Against resize-conv (Distill 2016), LoftUp gains +5.06 mIoU on COCO and +7.16 mIoU on Cityscapes, with resize-conv actually degrading depth and normal estimation relative to the low-res baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the recent line of task-agnostic feature upsampling for Vision Foundation Models initiated by FeatUp (ICLR 2024) and LiFT (ECCV 2024), which first demonstrated that general-purpose upsamplers can be trained without task-specific supervision. It departs from both in two key ways: architecturally, it replaces multi-layer interpolation/deconvolution stacks (and FeatUp's per-image implicit optimization) with a coordinate-based cross-attention transformer inspired by 3D reconstruction (NeRF-style coordinate encoding), enabling global content-aware upsampling in a single forward pass; and in training, it moves from low-resolution proxy losses to full-resolution pseudo-ground-truth constructed via SAM class-agnostic masks and self-distillation. The coordinate-based formulation also connects to LIIF's local implicit image functions, but LoftUp's cross-attention mechanism enables global interactions that LIIF's local design cannot.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Feature resolution is a fundamental bottleneck limiting VFMs like DINOv2 and CLIP in any pixel-level application, and LoftUp offers a lightweight, plug-and-play solution (<20% parameter overhead, inference speed comparable to bilinear) that substantially narrows this gap across segmentation, depth, normal estimation, and video tasks. The self-distillation pseudo-GT strategy is broadly applicable and could inform training objectives for other dense prediction heads. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very high resolutions (beyond 448px feature maps), whether the cross-attention cost becomes prohibitive for very large feature maps, and whether the dependence on SAM-quality masks for Stage 1 training limits applicability to domains where segmentation foundation models are less reliable.
