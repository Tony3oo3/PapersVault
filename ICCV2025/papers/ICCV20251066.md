## Problem

Event cameras offer microsecond temporal resolution and high dynamic range but produce sparse, asynchronous data that is extremely challenging for depth estimation. The field is further hampered by the absence of large-scale datasets with dense ground-truth depth annotations, since collecting precise depth for event streams remains costly and technically demanding. This paper addresses both challenges by proposing a cross-modal distillation paradigm that transfers knowledge from image-based Vision Foundation Models to the event domain, and by adapting VFMs directly to process event data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes event streams encoded as Tencode representations (a 3-channel RGB image where R and B channels encode positive and negative event polarities, and G encodes the relative timestamp within the slice), with events sliced using either a fixed 50 ms time window or a fixed event count. For the distillation setup, spatially and temporally aligned RGB frames (e.g., from a DAVIS346B sensor) are additionally required to generate proxy labels via the VFM teacher. The output is a monocular depth map at the event sensor resolution (346×260 for MVSEC, 640×320 after center crop for DSEC), predicted up to an unknown scale and shift in affine-invariant mode, or in metric units when trained for metric depth.

## Method

The core contribution is a cross-modal distillation paradigm where a pre-trained VFM teacher (Depth Anything v2 ViT-Large, fine-tuned on EventScape) processes aligned RGB frames to produce dense proxy depth labels that supervise any event-based student network, eliminating the need for expensive LiDAR depth annotations. The student is trained with a scale-invariant loss plus a multi-scale gradient regularization term. To adapt VFMs to events, the authors propose DepthAnyEvent (vanilla DAv2 ViT-Small fine-tuned on Tencode inputs) and DepthAnyEvent-R, a novel recurrent variant that inserts ConvLSTM modules at each scale of the DINOv2 encoder's multi-scale feature maps, allowing the model to accumulate temporal context from successive event stacks via hidden states before hierarchical fusion and depth decoding. The Tencode representation is deliberately chosen because it encodes both spatial and temporal event information within the RGB format expected by pre-trained image models, minimizing architectural modifications.

## Results

On the in-domain evaluation (Table 2), DepthAnyEvent-R achieves the best performance across all metrics on both MVSEC (Abs Rel 0.365, RMSE 6.465, δ<1.25 0.489) and DSEC (Abs Rel 0.191, RMSE 8.618, δ<1.25 0.691). The vanilla DepthAnyEvent variant is competitive, achieving Abs Rel 0.373 on MVSEC and 0.201 on DSEC. In the zero-shot setting (Table 1), both proposed models also lead, with DepthAnyEvent-R reaching Abs Rel 0.276 and RMSE 10.942 on DSEC without any fine-tuning on real data. The distillation paradigm (Table 3) shows that models trained with VFM proxy labels approach or occasionally match fully supervised performance, e.g., DepthAnyEvent-R distilled achieves Abs Rel 0.226 vs. 0.191 supervised on DSEC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two event-based depth estimation baselines: E2Depth [15] (recurrent, Voxel Grid input) and EReFormer [21] (recurrent transformer, Image-like input). DepthAnyEvent-R dominates both baselines on every metric across both datasets. On MVSEC, it reduces Abs Rel from 0.420 (E2Depth) and 0.511 (EReFormer) to 0.365, and lowers RMSE from 7.268/8.373 to 6.465. On DSEC, the gains are even more pronounced: Abs Rel drops from 0.253 (E2Depth) to 0.191, and RMSE falls from 10.119 to 8.618, while δ<1.25 improves from 0.574 to 0.691. The vanilla DepthAnyEvent (non-recurrent) also outperforms both baselines on DSEC but is slightly behind DepthAnyEvent-R on MVSEC, confirming the benefit of the recurrent ConvLSTM extension for temporal event processing. In the zero-shot setting, the advantage over baselines is even larger, with DepthAnyEvent-R achieving 22% lower RMSE than E2Depth on DSEC (10.942 vs. 13.258).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: image-based monocular depth estimation via Vision Foundation Models (MiDaS → DPT → Depth Anything v2) and event-based depth estimation (E2Depth, EReFormer). It departs from prior event-depth methods, which were trained from scratch or with limited pretraining on event-specific architectures, by repurposing a web-scale pre-trained VFM (DAv2/DINOv2) and introducing a cross-modal distillation loop where the VFM generates dense proxy labels from aligned RGB frames to supervise event-based student networks. The recurrent extension (DepthAnyEvent-R) adds ConvLSTM temporal memory to the DAv2 decoder, a design choice absent from both the original VFM and prior event-depth architectures that used simpler recurrent modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cross-modal distillation paradigm directly addresses the core bottleneck in event-based depth estimation—the absence of large-scale dense depth annotations—by showing that proxy labels from a frame-based VFM can match or occasionally exceed supervised ground-truth training, eliminating the need for costly LiDAR annotation. This opens a practical path for deploying depth estimation on event cameras in resource-constrained or rapidly changing environments where conventional cameras fail. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond Tencode representations, whether larger VFMs (beyond ViT-Small) yield further gains, and how well the method handles scenes with very low event activity where temporal context is sparse.
