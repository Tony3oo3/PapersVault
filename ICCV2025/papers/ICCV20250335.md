## Problem

Generalist Large Multi-modal Models (LMMs) trained on web-scale natural images underperform on domain-specific tasks such as chart/table understanding, mathematical reasoning, and document extraction. Post-training on domain data is impractical due to proprietary datasets, and naively concatenating expert encoder features into a well-aligned LMM causes representational misalignment and imbalanced optimization that prevents effective knowledge transfer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250335/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image (charts, tables, math diagrams, or documents) paired with a free-form text instruction. A linear router reads the CLS token of the general visual encoder to decide whether to invoke a domain expert (table, chart, math, or document) or proceed with general features only. The LLM input sequence concatenates masked general visual tokens, optionally expert visual tokens, and text embeddings; the output is either a free-form text response (reasoning, QA) or a structured extraction (table HTML, chart description, document layout).

## Method

Chimera integrates multiple frozen expert encoders (StructEqTable, ChartVLM, Math-CLIP, or GOT) into a generalist LMM (InternVL2) via a lightweight linear router that selects the appropriate expert per image based on the CLS token. The key mechanism is Generalist-Specialist Collaboration Masking (GSCM): during training, a random subset of general visual tokens is masked (attention set to False), forcing the LLM to attend to expert features rather than over-relying on the already well-aligned general encoder. Training follows a progressive two-stage recipe—first aligning projectors and router with frozen encoders/LLM on domain perception tasks, then unfreezing the LLM for full visual instruction tuning with GSCM. The objective combines token-wise cross-entropy for generation with a classification loss on the router, and optional DPO post-training further boosts reasoning performance.

## Results

On MathVista, Chimera-8B achieves 64.9% overall accuracy, setting a new SOTA among open-source LMMs under 70B parameters and surpassing GPT-4o (63.8%) by 1.1 points. On MathVerse, Chimera-8B reaches 32.4% overall accuracy, outperforming the 110B LLaVA-NeXT by 7.9 points. With Direct Preference Optimization (Chimera†), the model further improves to 68.3% on MathVista, exceeding InternVL-MPO (67.3%) and Ovis1.6-Gemma2 (67.2%). On visual structural extraction, Chimera achieves AP@high of 87.6 on ChartQA-SE, TEDS of 0.740 with Edit Distance 0.165 on Table-SE, and Edit Distance of 0.304 (English) on Doc-SE, matching or surpassing dedicated expert models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MathVista, Chimera-8B (64.9%) outperforms GPT-4o (63.8%) by 1.1 points, Qwen2-VL-7B (58.2%) by 6.7 points, and its own baseline InternVL2-8B (61.6%) by 3.3 points, while trailing only LLaVA-OneVision-72B (67.5%) and InternVL2-Pro (66.8%) among larger/closed models. Against domain-specific expert models, Chimera-8B leads Math-LLaVA (46.6%) by 18.3 points and Math-PUMA (44.7%) by 20.2 points on MathVista, and surpasses MAVIS (27.5%) and Math-PUMA (31.8%) on MathVerse. After DPO post-training, Chimera† (68.3%) exceeds both InternVL-MPO (67.3%) and Ovis1.6-Gemma2 (67.2%) on MathVista. On extraction tasks, Chimera matches or beats the GOT expert: it achieves higher AP@high on ChartQA-SE (87.6 vs. 86.7) and PlotQA-SE (71.0 vs. 64.0), lower Edit Distance on Table-SE (0.165 vs. 0.257) and Doc-SE English (0.304 vs. 0.355), though it is marginally behind GOT on ChartQA-SE AP@strict (74.1 vs. 74.7). On MathVerse, Chimera-8B (32.4%) still trails GPT-4V (39.4%) by 7 points, indicating that the gap to top closed-source models on vision-intensive math reasoning remains.

## Relation to Prior Work

Chimera builds directly on the InternVL2 family as its generalist backbone and departs from both the 'One for All' paradigm (scaling a single LMM on web-scale data) and the 'One for One' paradigm (training separate expert models per domain). Conceptually, it is closest to mixture-of-experts approaches like SPHINX and MoME, but operates at the visual encoder level rather than the language model level, routing between pre-trained domain-specific vision encoders (StructEqTable, ChartVLM, Math-CLIP, GOT) via a lightweight linear router. The key methodological departure is the Generalist-Specialist Collaboration Masking (GSCM) mechanism, which addresses an underexplored failure mode: when a well-aligned general encoder is concatenated with specialist features, the LLM over-relies on the general path and under-utilizes expert tokens, a problem not addressed by prior multi-encoder fusion work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Chimera offers a practical, low-cost recipe for upgrading existing LMMs with domain expertise without requiring access to proprietary training data, which is a significant barrier in practice. The GSCM mechanism is a simple, generalizable technique that could be adopted by other multi-encoder or multi-modal fusion pipelines. However, the router's 95.4% accuracy (with all errors being general-vs-expert confusions) and the observation that the table expert actually degrades the 8B model's table-domain performance (62.9 vs. 67.1 baseline) suggest that naive expert integration is not always beneficial and that explicit per-image domain annotation or more sophisticated routing may be needed. Open questions include scalability beyond four domains, whether the approach transfers to audio/video modalities, and whether the progressive two-stage training is strictly necessary versus a single joint training phase.
