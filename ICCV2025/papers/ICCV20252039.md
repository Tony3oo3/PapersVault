## Problem

Existing feature importance-based adversarial attacks (e.g., FIA, RPA) rely on single-granularity patches—either pixel-level or fixed-size squares—and single-stage training with a fixed DNN layer, leading to inaccurate aggregate gradient estimation and failure to exploit transferable generic features from shallow layers. This results in suboptimal transferability, particularly in cross-model black-box scenarios where the target architecture is unknown.

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean RGB image (224×224 from the 1000-image ImageNet-compatible NIPS-17 benchmark), its ground truth class label, and a surrogate CNN (ResNet-152, Inception-ResNet-v2, or VGG-16) to produce a single adversarial example bounded by an ℓ∞-norm of ε=16 over 10 iterations. The patch generation step internally partitions the image into deformable superpixel-like regions via linear iterative clustering, and the aggregate gradient is computed over M=60 randomly color-patched variants of the input. The output adversarial example is evaluated for transferability against 14 unseen target models (undefended/defended CNNs and ViTs).

## Method

SMP-Attack introduces two coupled innovations within an MI-FGSM momentum framework. First, it replaces single-granularity regular patches with semantics-aware multi-granularity deformable patches: each pixel is assigned to the nearest patch centroid under a combined spatial-plus-color distance metric D_sc = d_spatial + (d/c)·d_color, yielding irregular patches of varying sizes and shapes that respect semantic boundaries; the aggregate gradient is then averaged over M=60 randomly transformed images parameterized by both patch size d and shape parameter c. Second, it replaces single-stage training with a multi-stage optimization that divides T iterations into S stages, each targeting a different DNN layer (shallow → intermediate) with stage-specific patch settings—smaller, more regular patches for shallow layers capturing generic features, and larger, more irregular patches for intermediate layers capturing mid-level features—while carrying the adversarial example forward as initialization between stages. This two-stage variant (shallow + intermediate layer) is shown to subsume FIA and RPA as special cases and is compatible with transformation-based augmentations (PIM, DIM) for further gains.

## Results

On the ImageNet-1000 benchmark (ε=16, T=10), SMP-Attack achieves an average black-box ASR of 93.4% (Res152-v1 surrogate), 84.8% (IncRes-v2), and 97.9% (Vgg-16) against undefended CNNs, outperforming the strongest baseline MFAA by 1.4–9.5 percentage points depending on the surrogate. With PIM+DIM augmentation (PIDI-SMP), the average black-box ASR rises to 96.5%, 86.9%, and 98.4% respectively. Against defended CNNs and ViTs, SMP-Attack improves over MFAA by 3.93% (defended CNNs) and 6.47% (ViTs) on average across surrogates, with PIDI-SMP reaching 91.3% on defended CNNs and 86.3% on ViTs (Vgg-16 surrogate).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMP-Attack consistently outperforms all seven baselines (FIA, RPA, NAA, DANAA, NEAA, BFA, MFAA) across all three surrogate models and all target model categories. The largest gains are over MFAA (the strongest prior method): +1.4 pp on undefended CNNs (Res152 surrogate), +8.4 pp (IncRes-v2 surrogate), and +6.6 pp on ViTs (Res152 surrogate). Against the older single-granularity methods FIA and RPA, improvements range from 2–10 pp depending on the surrogate and target. Neuron-attribution methods (NAA, DANAA, NEAA) and BFA are substantially weaker, with ASRs 10–25 pp below SMP on most targets. The PIDI variant (combining PIM+DIM transformations) further boosts all methods, but PIDI-SMP maintains its lead over PIDI-MFAA by 1.1 pp (undefended CNNs) and 5.4 pp (ViTs), confirming that the multi-granularity and multi-stage components provide complementary gains beyond transformation augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature importance-based adversarial attack lineage from FIA (pixel-level patchout) and RPA (fixed-size square patches) by introducing multi-granularity deformable patches that vary both spatial size and shape, guided by a semantics-aware color-distance criterion. It departs from prior multi-layer approaches (FDA, MFAA) that jointly optimize losses from multiple DNN layers within a single training stage, instead proposing a sequential multi-stage strategy that first exploits shallow-layer generic features before refining with intermediate-layer features. The optimization backbone remains MI-FGSM, and the method is explicitly designed as a drop-in replacement for the patch generation and training schedule in existing feature-based attacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-stage optimization strategy is presented as a general, plug-and-play component that can be integrated with other feature-based attacks (FIA, RPA, MFAA) to improve their performance, which has practical value for the adversarial ML community. The consistent 3–6% ASR gains across 14 target models (CNNs, defended CNNs, and ViTs) suggest the approach is robust rather than architecture-specific. Open questions include whether the multi-granularity ensemble (M=60 forward passes per iteration) scales to larger datasets or higher-resolution inputs, whether the shallow-to-intermediate layer progression generalizes to non-classification tasks (detection, segmentation), and whether the semantics-aware patch boundary has any measurable effect beyond what a simpler random-shape perturbation would achieve.
