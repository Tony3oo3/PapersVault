## Problem

Conventional attention-based MIL for WSI classification aggregates patch features in a complex, scattered feature space, causing substantial information loss of subtle morphological patterns during slide-level pooling. The reliance on bag-level labels alone further limits the model's discriminative capacity, as it lacks fine-grained instance-level insight needed to distinguish positive from negative patches within a bag.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251679/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted, frozen patch features (from pathology foundation models) organized as bags of N non-overlapping patches per WSI, with only a single bag-level label per slide. It produces both a bag-level class prediction (via attention-weighted aggregation in the LSES) and instance-level pseudo-labels (via GMM posterior probabilities combined with attention scores), which enable patch localization maps on the original slide. The dual-branch design shares a single projector between the bag-level and instance-level pathways, so the two branches interact through the common intermediate representation.

## Method

Flow-MIL maps intermediate patch features into a Latent Semantic Embedding Space (LSES) via a cascading of invertible Flow Blocks, where each block splits the input into two halves, applies a neural network to one half, and recombines via an invertible transformation T, ensuring full reversibility. An inverse flow reconstructs the original features, and a reconstruction loss (MSE) prevents the latent space from degrading. Within the LSES, each class is modeled by a k-component Gaussian Mixture Model (Latent Semantic Prototypes, LSP) that captures class-specific pathological sub-distributions. A dual-branch optimization strategy then trains a bag-level classifier (attention MIL + LSP alignment cross-entropy) and an instance-level classifier (supervised by ProtoScore pseudo-labels that blend LSP posterior probabilities with attention weights), with the two branches optimized alternately to progressively refine both slide-level and patch-level predictions.

## Results

On CAMELYON16, Flow-MIL achieves a bag-level AUC of 0.9437 and an instance-level AUC of 0.9277, outperforming the strongest baselines (IBMIL at 0.8991 bag-level and WENO at 0.9271 instance-level). On TCGA-NSCLC, Flow-MIL reaches a bag-level AUC of 0.9859, surpassing TransMIL (0.9830) and RRT-MIL (0.9794). On the multi-class PANDA dataset, Flow-MIL attains bag-level AUCs of 0.9121, 0.9005, and 0.9012 for Gleason scores 3, 4, and 5 respectively, with instance-level AUCs of 0.9678, 0.9415, and 0.9636.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Flow-MIL achieves the best bag-level AUC across all three datasets, with the largest margin on CAMELYON16 where it surpasses the next-best method IBMIL (0.8991) by 4.5 points and FR-MIL (0.8989) by 4.5 points. On instance-level AUC for CAMELYON16, Flow-MIL (0.9277) narrowly edges out WENO (0.9271) and clearly beats FR-MIL (0.9103) and RRT-MIL (0.9000), though it remains below the fully supervised upper bound (0.9644). On TCGA-NSCLC, Flow-MIL (0.9859) outperforms TransMIL (0.9830) and RRT-MIL (0.9794) by small but consistent margins. On PANDA, Flow-MIL is competitive but does not uniformly dominate: it leads on Gleason 3 instance-level AUC (0.9678 vs. 0.9525 for DSMIL) but trails the fully supervised method (0.9862), and its bag-level results are close to WENO and IBMIL. Overall, the normalizing-flow-based LSES and GMM-based LSP components yield consistent gains over attention-only MIL baselines (ABMIL, TransMIL) and more recent re-weighting or distillation approaches (FR-MIL, RRT-MIL, DTFD-MIL).

## Relation to Prior Work

Flow-MIL extends the standard attention-based MIL pipeline (ABMIL, TransMIL, WENO) by inserting a normalizing-flow module between feature extraction and aggregation, mapping patch embeddings into a Latent Semantic Embedding Space (LSES) via invertible neural networks. The GMM-based Latent Semantic Prototypes draw conceptual inspiration from ProtoFlow [5] and PANTHER [40], but are applied within the flow-transformed latent space rather than the raw feature space, and are coupled with a reconstruction loss to prevent information degradation. The dual-branch bag/instance optimization strategy is reminiscent of WENO [34], but here the instance-level pseudo-labels are refined by prototype posterior probabilities rather than purely by attention scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-recognized bottleneck in WSI-MIL—information loss during slide-level aggregation—by showing that an invertible flow can restructure the feature space to improve separability before aggregation, yielding a ~4–5 point bag-level AUC gain on CAMELYON16 over prior SOTA. The LSP prototypes offer a degree of interpretability (each prototype maps to a distinct morphological pattern), which is valuable for clinical trust. Open questions include whether the flow module scales to very large bags (thousands of patches), whether the frozen-backbone assumption limits gains when paired with newer pathology foundation models, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the three tested datasets to more heterogeneous or multi-label clinical settings.
