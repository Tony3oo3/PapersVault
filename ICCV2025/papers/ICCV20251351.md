## Problem

Existing multimodal pretraining methods predominantly target vision-language pairs (e.g., CLIP) or a limited set of modalities, lacking scalable architectures and sufficient paired data to achieve coherent understanding across diverse modalities such as video, audio, depth, and normal maps. The paper identifies four key barriers: architectural inflexibility for modality extension, scarcity of large-scale multimodal paired datasets, absence of effective cross-modal context modeling, and unclear understanding of how disparate modalities interact synergistically.

## Input / Output

The pretraining pipeline consumes 720p (1280×720) video clips of 5–30 seconds from HD-VILA, along with their synchronized audio tracks and subtitle captions; frames are extracted as images, and depth/normal maps are generated via pretrained monocular estimation models, yielding 1.7M paired clips (~510M frames) split into 1M/10M/110M/334M subsets. The shared ViT backbone processes all non-text modalities (image, video, audio, depth, normal) into a joint embedding space, while a separate BERT or Llama-2-7B branch handles text. Downstream, the universal embedding supports retrieval rankings, CIDEr-scored captions, QA answers, and zero-shot classification across 10 modalities including thermal, IMU, time-series, graph, and hyperspectral data.

## Method

MiCo employs a two-branch architecture inspired by the brain's dual-channel multimedia cognition: a shared Vision Transformer encodes all 'knowledge modalities' (image, video, audio, depth, normal maps) into a common embedding space, while a dedicated text encoder (BERT) or LLM (Llama-2-7B) serves as the 'interface modality' branch for language alignment and generation. The key mechanism is a top-down multimodal context construction where all modality tokens share position embeddings for cross-modal fusion, are tagged with modality-specific embeddings to preserve type identity, and use intra-modal context embeddings for within-modality sequential relationships; for cross-dataset joint sampling (e.g., combining text-image and text-audio pairs), additional sampling context embeddings link otherwise unrelated data. Pretraining combines three complementary objectives—InfoNCE contrastive loss for alignment, feature matching (logits) for fine-grained correspondence, and causal generation for captioning—whose combination yields consistent gains over any single objective. The framework scales across modalities (I→I+V+A+3D), data volume (1M→334M pairs), and model size (ViT-B 86M→ViT-g 1.3B parameters), with the full modality set and largest data scale producing the strongest universal representations.

## Results

MiCo (ViT-g) achieves state-of-the-art or near-SOTA performance across 10 modalities: 68.9% MMLU, 89.8% ImageNet-1K top-1, 91.6% K700 accuracy, 64.3% MSR-VTT R@1, 84.6% NYU-D, 50.5% AudioSet mAP, 80.3% SYSU thermal, 77.2% Ego4D IMU, 98.5% Indian Pines hyperspectral, and 0.913 Fraud F1. On 25 cross-modal retrieval/captioning/QA benchmarks, MiCo sets 20 new SOTAs, with the largest gains on YouCook2 video-audio retrieval (+20.0 R@1) and MSRVTT video-audio retrieval (+9.9 R@1). On 18 MLLM benchmarks, MiCo-Chat-7B (Qwen2-7B) reaches 69.5% GQA, 82.5% VQAv2, 79.3% ScienceQA, 1644.8 MME, 81.3% MMBench, 59.5% MMVet, and 77.4% SEED, outperforming all prior vision-specialist and multimodal-generalist LLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ImageBind (ViT-H), MiCo (ViT-g) wins on all 10 modalities, with the largest margins on Ego4D IMU (77.2% vs. 25.0%), NYU-D depth (84.6% vs. 54.0%), and MMLU text (68.9% vs. 43.6%). Against Meta-Transformer (ViT-L), MiCo also dominates across all 10 modalities, e.g., K700 video (91.6% vs. 33.2%) and MSR-VTT retrieval (64.3% vs. 31.5%). MiCo surpasses the absolute SOTA on 6 of 10 modalities (NYU-D, AS-2M, SYSU, Ego4D, Indian Pines, Fraud) but trails on MMLU (68.9% vs. 90.0%), ImageNet-1K (89.8% vs. 91.0%), PCQM4M (0.742 vs. 0.123 MAE), and Global Weather (7.834 vs. 7.602 MAE). On cross-modal tasks (Table 5), MiCo sets 20 of 25 SOTAs, with the most dramatic wins over VALOR [9] on YouCook2 retrieval (+20.0 R@1) and MSRVTT video-audio retrieval (+9.9 R@1), and over WavCaps [71] on ClothoV1 audio captioning (+7.3 CIDEr). On MLLM benchmarks (Table 6), MiCo-Chat-7B with Qwen2-7B outperforms LLaVA-OneVision-7B by +10.7 on MMBench (81.3 vs. 80.8 is marginal, but +2.0 on MME 1644.8 vs. 1580) and by large margins on MMVet (59.5 vs. 57.5) and SEED (77.4 vs. 75.4), while also surpassing OneLLM-7B on every reported metric (e.g., GQA 69.5 vs. 59.5, SQA 79.3 vs. 63.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

MiCo extends the CLIP contrastive pretraining paradigm and BEiT-3's masked-generation objective to an omni-modal setting, building on the architectural foundations of both while departing from the modality-specific encoder designs of ImageBind and Meta-Transformer. The key conceptual departure is the brain-inspired dual-channel architecture that separates 'knowledge modalities' (image, video, audio, depth, normal) from the 'interface modality' (language), with a shared ViT for all non-text inputs and a dedicated LLM branch for text. The multimodal context construction—using shared position embeddings across modalities, modality-specific context embeddings, and cross-dataset joint sampling with sampling context embeddings—represents a new formulation for how to model relationships between tokens from different modalities within a single sequence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MiCo provides a practical, scalable recipe for omni-modal pretraining that sets 37 SOTA results across 10 modalities, and the finding that MiCo-pretrained ViTs transfer to SigLIP with only 25% of training data challenges the assumption that vision-language alignment is tightly bound to a specific model pair. The unexpected +6% spatial understanding improvement in downstream VLMs from video-audio pretraining (via camera-motion descriptions in captions) suggests that temporal dynamics in language carry geometric information not previously exploited. Open questions include whether the dual-channel architecture is truly necessary versus simpler unified encoders, how the framework scales to genuinely heterogeneous modalities like IMU or event-camera data, and whether the reliance on pretrained depth/normal estimators introduces systematic biases into the learned representations.
