## Problem

Existing agent models train separate networks for visual representation, world state prediction, and action prediction, failing to capture the intrinsic causal and temporal dependencies among these abilities. Cognitive science (Common Coding Theory) suggests perception and action operate within a shared representational space and are mutually reinforcing, yet no unified framework has jointly learned all three from egocentric observations and 3D body motions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250143/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes interleaved sequences of egocentric video frames (one frame sampled every 5 frames) and synchronized 3D body poses (all 5 poses per time step), encoded into image tokens and action tokens respectively. It produces three outputs: a future world state as a continuous semantic embedding (retrievable against a gallery of frame features), a 3D human motion prediction (15-frame pose sequence), and a visual feature representation usable for classification or policy learning. The world state is represented in a learned continuous feature space rather than pixel-level or discrete VQGAN tokens, aligning with abstract human reasoning.

## Method

EgoAgent formulates human-world interaction as an interleaved 'image–action query–action–state query' token sequence processed by a causal transformer (InternLM backbone, trained from scratch). A temporally asymmetric predictor-observer architecture (JEAP) is used: the predictor branch forecasts the next action and future world state, while the observer branch (EMA-updated) extracts target world states from raw images, with a stop-gradient preventing conflicting gradients. Three losses are jointly optimized: L1 loss for action prediction, DINO loss for state prediction (predictor vs. observer), and DINO loss for self-supervised representation learning from multi-view crops. Learnable query tokens (q_a, q_s) are inserted at causally meaningful positions to explicitly prompt the model to predict actions from observations and states from actions, decoupling shared representation components from action-specific ones.

## Results

On Ego-Exo4D world state prediction, EgoAgent-1B achieves 46.43% Top-1 accuracy and 61.96% mAP, outperforming the strongest baseline DoRA (30.15% Top-1, 45.01% mAP) by +16.28% and +16.95% respectively. For 3D human motion prediction at 30 fps, EgoAgent-1B attains the lowest MPJPE of 12.51 cm, beating siMLPe (13.33 cm) and HumanMAC (19.21 cm). On ImageNet-1K k-NN classification, EgoAgent-1B reaches 35.84% Top-1 accuracy, surpassing DoRA (34.52%) by +1.32%. On the TriFinger embodied manipulation benchmark, EgoAgent-1B achieves 57.66% success rate on Move Cube, exceeding DoRA (48.13%) by +9.53%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EgoAgent decisively outperforms all egocentric representation baselines (VIP, R3M, DINO, DoRA) on world state prediction, with EgoAgent-1B exceeding DoRA by +16.28% Top-1 and +16.95% mAP, a gap the authors attribute to DoRA's reliance on object tracking that fails under dynamic egocentric motion. On 3D human motion prediction, EgoAgent-1B achieves the best MPJPE (12.51 cm) at 30 fps, narrowly beating siMLPe (13.33 cm) by 0.82 cm and substantially outperforming HumanMAC (19.21 cm) and both Diffusion Policy variants (25.92–28.07 cm). For visual representation, EgoAgent-1B edges out DoRA by +1.32% Top-1 on ImageNet-1K and +1.40% on ImageNet-100, while the retrained DoRA* (trained on the same combined dataset) actually degrades to 31.46% Top-1, confirming that DoRA's tracker cannot handle Ego-Exo4D's dynamic scenes. On the TriFinger embodied manipulation benchmark, EgoAgent-1B achieves 57.66% Move Cube success rate versus DoRA's 48.13% (+9.53%) and DINO's 47.42%, demonstrating that joint action prediction yields representations more transferable to visuomotor control. Notably, EgoAgent is the only method evaluated across all three task categories simultaneously, and it wins or ties on every metric where it is directly comparable.

## Relation to Prior Work

EgoAgent extends the JEPA (Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture) framework from LeCun and Garrido et al. by adding an action-prediction branch and formulating the problem as an interleaved state-action sequence processed by causal attention, effectively turning a passive world model into a reactive agent model. It also unifies three previously isolated research threads—egocentric visual representation learning (DoRA, VIP, R3M), world state prediction, and 3D human motion prediction—into a single transformer trained from scratch on synchronized egocentric video and 3D pose data. The key architectural departure from prior JEPA work is the temporally asymmetric predictor-observer design with learnable query tokens that explicitly encode the causal ordering of perception → action → next-state, and the use of continuous semantic embeddings rather than discrete VQGAN tokens for state representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides strong ablation evidence that representation, prediction, and action tasks mutually reinforce each other in a unified model, offering a principled argument for joint training over task-specific models in agent learning. The finding that continuous semantic feature space substantially outperforms pixel-level VQGAN latents for world state prediction (37.77% vs 20.62% Top1) challenges the common practice of using reconstruction-based tokenizers in world models. Limitations include the absence of fine-grained hand representations (only whole-body 3D poses), no long-term memory mechanism, and evaluation limited to near-future prediction (4 time steps). Open questions include whether this joint formulation scales to longer horizons, more complex action spaces (e.g., object manipulation), and whether the mutual reinforcement effect persists with larger models or more diverse action modalities.
