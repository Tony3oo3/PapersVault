## Problem

Existing template-free hand-object reconstruction methods assume full object visibility, an assumption that breaks in real-world short videos where fixed camera viewpoints and static grips leave parts of the object unobserved due to hand-induced or self-occlusions. This incomplete observation leads to implausible reconstructions of occluded object regions and unreliable hand-object spatial alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250591/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a short monocular RGB video (typically 30-frame clips) as input, which is first segmented into hand and object masks using Cutie. It produces a 3D object surface represented as an implicit signed distance field (SDF) with texture, and a 3D hand mesh parameterized by MANO (pose, shape, rotation, translation). The object is reconstructed in a canonical Zero-1-to-3 coordinate space, and the hand is aligned to it via visible contact constraints, yielding metric-scale hand-object spatial relationships.

## Method

MagicHOI integrates the Zero-1-to-3 novel view synthesis diffusion model as a category-agnostic geometry prior to regularize unobserved object regions during SDF-based volumetric reconstruction. After SfM camera calibration and MVS depth estimation, the method creates an object-only reference image via InpaintAnything, aligns the SfM coordinate frame to the Zero-1-to-3 canonical space via a rigid transformation estimated from 3D correspondences, and then optimizes an implicit SDF supervised by RGB/segmentation losses on observed views plus a Score Distillation Sampling loss on randomly sampled novel views. A key novelty is a visibility-aware weighting strategy that computes a per-view visibility ratio β from a coarse voxel grid and modulates the NVS loss weight as μ = e^{−β²/0.6}, ensuring the diffusion prior strongly regularizes unobserved regions while minimally interfering with observed surfaces. Finally, hand-object alignment is performed using only visible contact pairs (fingertip vertices whose projections fall within the hand mask and are not occluded by other fingertips), optimizing hand translation and scale while keeping pose fixed to avoid degradation.

## Results

On the HO3D-v3 dataset (14 sequences, 30-frame clips), MagicHOI achieves a Chamfer distance of 0.87 cm, F5 of 69.72%, F10 of 92.15%, MPJPE of 4.62 mm, hand-relative CD of 2.39 cm, and Relative Scale of 0.11 — all best-in-class among the four template-free baselines. The most dramatic gains are in hand-object alignment (CDh: 2.39 cm vs. 19.55–33.33 cm for prior-driven baselines) and hand pose accuracy (MPJPE: 4.62 mm vs. 16.02–30.79 mm), reflecting the benefit of NVS-regularized object geometry for stable contact-based alignment. The method also generalizes qualitatively to bimanual rigid-object sequences from ARCTIC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MagicHOI outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against the geometry-driven HOLD [16], MagicHOI reduces CD from 1.31 to 0.87 cm (−33%) and F10 from 80.23% to 92.15%, while dramatically improving MPJPE from 30.79 mm to 4.62 mm — HOLD's lack of any prior leaves unobserved object regions empty, causing large hand-object misalignment (CDh 21.28 cm). Among prior-driven methods, EasyHOI [42] (which uses a different NVS model) achieves the second-best CD (1.86 cm) but still lags MagicHOI by 1.14× in CD and 8.2× in CDh (19.55 vs. 2.39 cm), with the paper noting EasyHOI often hallucinates unrealistic shapes and produces hand-object penetrations. DiffHOI [83] and iHOI [81] rely on category-specific priors and show the worst CDh values (33.33 and 25.45 cm respectively), indicating that category-level supervision introduces distortions in unobserved regions that destabilize alignment. The Relative Scale metric (0.11) confirms MagicHOI's object size estimate is closest to ground truth, whereas HOLD's RS of 0.62 reflects a 62% size deviation due to incomplete geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

MagicHOI extends the template-free hand-object reconstruction line (HOLD, iHOI, EasyHOI, DiffHOI) by replacing category-specific or geometry-only priors with a category-agnostic novel view synthesis diffusion model (Zero-1-to-3) as a geometric prior for unobserved object regions. The score-distillation sampling loss mechanism is borrowed from DreamFusion-style text-to-3D work, but here it is conditioned on a single inpainted reference image rather than a text prompt, and is combined with direct RGB supervision via a visibility-aware weighting strategy that has no direct analogue in prior hand-object or object reconstruction methods. The hand-object alignment step departs from prior work (e.g., HOLD) by restricting contact constraints to a carefully filtered set of visible fingertip contacts, rather than using all closest vertex pairs, which is critical when the object surface is incompletely reconstructed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: short monocular videos from the internet (product reviews, social media clips) rarely provide full 360° object views, yet they are the primary data source for scaling robotic grasping demonstrations. The finding that a general-purpose NVS diffusion model outperforms category-specific priors (iHOI, DiffHOI) for completing occluded object regions suggests a path toward truly object-agnostic hand-object reconstruction without per-category training. Limitations include per-scene optimization (not real-time), dependence on the quality of the Zero-1-to-3 prior for novel object categories, and evaluation on only 14 HO3D sequences plus a small in-the-wild set. Open questions include whether the approach extends to articulated objects, multi-object scenes, and whether the visibility-aware weighting can be learned rather than heuristic.
