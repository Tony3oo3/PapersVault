## Problem

Generating high-fidelity dynamic 3D content (4D) from video remains largely unexplored due to two fundamental barriers: constructing large-scale 4D training data is prohibitively expensive (per-instance 4DGS fitting requires tens of minutes), and the high dimensionality of jointly representing 3D shape, appearance, and motion (typically exceeding 100K tokens) makes direct diffusion modeling intractable. Existing video-to-4D methods either rely on slow optimization-based approaches or suffer from temporal inconsistency due to multiview 2D generation artifacts.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single in-the-wild video sequence as input, from which DINOv2 visual features are extracted per frame. The output is a temporally coherent sequence of 3D Gaussian Splatting models (4DGS) that jointly encode shape, appearance, and motion. The canonical GS for the first frame is generated separately via a pretrained 3D diffusion model (TRELLIS), while the diffusion model produces only the per-frame attribute variations (positions, scales, rotations, colors, opacities) relative to the canonical frame. Training uses 24-frame sequences at 512×512 resolution; inference extends to 32 frames, with total generation time of ~4.5 seconds on a single A100 GPU.

## Method

The framework decomposes 4D generation into canonical 3DGS creation and Gaussian Variation Field (GVF) modeling. A Direct 4DMesh-to-GS VAE encodes mesh animation sequences into a compact 512-dimensional latent space without per-instance fitting: it computes per-point displacement fields from mesh animations, uses a mesh-guided interpolation mechanism with adaptive radius to generate motion-aware query vectors for a perceiver-style cross-attention encoder, and is trained with a novel mesh-guided loss that aligns predicted Gaussian displacements with pseudo ground-truth from mesh interpolation. The GVF diffusion model is built on the DiT architecture with dedicated temporal self-attention layers for coherent motion, conditioned on DINOv2 video features and farthest-sampled canonical GS via cross-attention, and augmented with positional embeddings from canonical GS positions to strengthen spatial correspondence awareness during denoising. The velocity-parameterized diffusion is trained with a simple L2 loss on the compact latent, reducing the modeling space from 8192 points to 512 latent tokens.

## Results

On the combined test set of 100 objects (7 from Consistent4D + 93 from Objaverse-XL), the method achieves PSNR 18.47, LPIPS 0.114, SSIM 0.901, CLIP 0.935, and FVD 476.83, outperforming all five baselines on every metric. Generation takes 4.5 seconds on a single A100 GPU (3.0 s for canonical GS + 1.5 s for variation field diffusion), comparable to the feedforward L4GM (3.5 s) and orders of magnitude faster than optimization-based methods (15 min to 1.5 hr). The evaluation is conducted at 512×512 resolution with 4 novel views rendered per timestep.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method achieves the best score on all six metrics against every baseline. Against the closest competitor L4GM (a feedforward 4D reconstruction model), it improves PSNR by 1.44 dB (18.47 vs. 17.03), reduces LPIPS by 0.014 (0.114 vs. 0.128), and lowers FVD by 52.27 (476.83 vs. 529.10), while adding only 1.0 s to generation time. Against optimization-based methods, the gains are substantially larger: PSNR improves by 1.62–3.23 dB over STAG4D, Consistent4D, SC4D, and DreamGaussian4D respectively, and FVD drops by 322–532 points. The largest absolute margin is over DreamGaussian4D (PSNR +3.23, FVD −322.73), while the smallest is over L4GM, which is the only feedforward baseline. In terms of speed, the method is roughly 20× faster than SC4D (~20 min), 13× faster than DreamGaussian4D (~15 min), and 20× faster than Consistent4D (~1.5 hr), making it practical for interactive use.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TRELLIS [80] for canonical 3DGS generation (reusing its Mesh-to-GS autoencoder and SLAT latent) and on the DiT architecture [51] for the diffusion backbone, while drawing on 3DShape2VecSet [89] for the perceiver-style cross-attention encoding of point clouds. It departs from prior video-to-4D methods (Consistent4D, SC4D, STAG4D, DreamGaussian4D) that rely on per-instance score distillation optimization, and from the feedforward L4GM [56] that reconstructs 4DGS from 2D-generated multiview images, by instead directly encoding 4D mesh animations into a compact Gaussian Variation Field latent via a VAE and then applying diffusion in that latent space. The key conceptual departure is the decomposition of 4D generation into a frozen canonical GS component plus a learned temporal variation field, coupled with a mesh-guided interpolation mechanism that bridges mesh-based ground-truth motion to Gaussian-level supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that high-fidelity video-to-4D generation can be done in ~4.5 seconds (vs. minutes to hours for optimization-based methods) while outperforming the closest feedforward baseline (L4GM) on all quality metrics, and notably generalizes to in-the-wild video inputs despite training exclusively on synthetic Objaverse data. The compact 512-dimensional latent space makes 4D diffusion tractable and the decomposition strategy (canonical + variation) is a reusable architectural pattern for other dynamic 3D tasks. Open questions include scalability to longer sequences beyond 32 frames, handling of topological changes, and whether the synthetic-to-wild generalization holds for more complex or multi-object motions. The reliance on a pretrained 3D diffusion model for the canonical component means quality is bounded by that upstream model.
