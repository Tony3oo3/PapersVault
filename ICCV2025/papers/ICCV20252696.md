## Problem

Completing partially occluded objects consistently across video frames remains unsolved for unstructured, in-the-wild videos. Existing image-level amodal completion methods ignore temporal context, while video inpainting approaches require explicit masks that presuppose the occluded object's shape and lack object-awareness. No prior method achieves spatio-temporally consistent amodal synthesis that generalizes to diverse real-world scenarios without simulator data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252696/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an occluded video clip (T×H×W×3) and a visual prompt (a point or mask) identifying the target object; SAM2 converts the prompt into per-frame visible masks that are VAE-encoded into latent space. The output is a temporally consistent unoccluded object video (T×H×W×3) showing the complete object in every frame. For videos exceeding the fixed 14-frame generation window, a sliding-window mechanism (5-frame overlap) iteratively synthesizes and reinserts completed objects into subsequent frames to maintain long-sequence consistency.

## Method

TACO fine-tunes Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) by replacing its single-reference-image conditioning with full-video conditioning: the input video latent z^(v) and visible-mask latent z^(m) are channel-concatenated with the noisy object latent z^(o) as U-Net input, while frame-wise CLIP embeddings are injected via cross-attention for high-level semantics. This design gives the denoiser a broad receptive field over all frames simultaneously, which is critical for cross-frame consistency. Training follows a progressive paradigm: Phase 1 uses OvO-Easy (~200k pairs with 0.3–0.7 occlusion rate and linear occluder motion), then Phase 2 introduces OvO-Hard (0.4–0.8 occlusion, persistent occlusion via bounding-box-coupled motion, and image feathering for contact realism). The dataset is curated model-in-the-loop, where identified failure modes (severe occlusion, swift motion, close contact) guide the generation of harder augmentation, and a third phase fine-tunes on Bdd100k to validate cross-domain transfer.

## Results

On the four VAC benchmarks, TACO achieves the best image alignment (PSNR 26.11 on OvO-Easy, 22.57 on OvO-Hard, 23.96 on Kubric-Static, 23.05 on Kubric-Dynamic) and the lowest FVD (142.4, 291.5, 162.9, 209.3 respectively), substantially outperforming all baselines. In the user study, TACO scores 4.65/5 on Content Quality, 4.63/5 on Content Consistency, and 4.64/5 on Content Plausibility, roughly doubling the best baseline (pix2gestalt at 2.88 CQ). For zero-shot Video Amodal Segmentation, TACO (Top-3) reaches 77.8 mIoU on MOVi-B, surpassing EoRaS (75.8), though EoRaS retains an edge on MOVi-D (69.4 vs. 61.3). On downstream tasks, TACO improves 6-DoF pose estimation Rot-Acc from 26.5% to 34.7% and reduces Chamfer Distance for object reconstruction from 1.97 (pix2gestalt) to 1.26, approaching the GT amodal bound of 1.05.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TACO decisively outperforms all baselines on the two self-curated datasets (OvO-Easy, OvO-Hard), leading every metric by large margins (e.g., +5.4 PSNR and −247 FVD over pix2gestalt on OvO-Easy). On the Kubric benchmarks, TACO wins on PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and FVD against all five baselines, but Diffusion-VAS [9] edges it out by a narrow margin on IoU (84.3 vs. 83.9 on Kubric-Static; 77.8 vs. 77.4 on Kubric-Dynamic), likely because Diffusion-VAS explicitly predicts amodal masks in a two-stage pipeline. Among image-level methods, pix2gestalt [68] and PCNet [115] produce plausible single-frame content but suffer severe cross-frame inconsistency (FVD 389–429 vs. TACO's 142–291), while video inpainting (E²FGVI) and video editing (Pix2Video) baselines fail to generate coherent amodal content at all. For zero-shot VAS, TACO (Top-3) surpasses EoRaS [19] on MOVi-B (77.8 vs. 75.8 mIoU) but trails on MOVi-D (61.3 vs. 69.4), suggesting EoRaS's task-specific training still holds an advantage on that particular distribution. On downstream tasks, TACO improves 6-DoF pose Rot-Acc by 8.2 points over the no-completion baseline and reduces reconstruction Chamfer Distance by 36% relative to pix2gestalt, approaching the GT amodal upper bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

TACO repurposes Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) for a task it was not originally designed for, following the broader trend of leveraging pre-trained video diffusion models for downstream perception tasks (depth estimation, novel view synthesis). It departs from image-level amodal completion (pix2gestalt, PCNet) by operating natively in the video domain to exploit temporal context, and from video inpainting (E²FGVI, Pix2Video) by requiring only a visual prompt rather than explicit occlusion masks and by maintaining object-level awareness. The progressive fine-tuning strategy is directly inspired by SAM2's curriculum learning, and the data curation pipeline adapts pix2gestalt's occlusion-overlay idea to the video setting with a model-in-the-loop difficulty escalation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TACO demonstrates that a single video diffusion model can serve as a general-purpose 'see-through' module, directly improving downstream tasks like 3D reconstruction and 6-DoF pose estimation without task-specific retraining, which positions it as a practical building block for embodied AI and autonomous driving pipelines. The OvO dataset and progressive training paradigm offer a reusable recipe for fine-tuning video diffusion models on occlusion-related tasks. Key limitations include dependence on SVD's fixed frame count (mitigated by a sliding window), resolution constraints that prevent joint training across datasets with different resolutions, and the need for SAM2 to extract visible masks as an intermediate step. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-object scenes, longer unbounded videos, and whether the progressive curriculum generalizes beyond occlusion to other video completion tasks.
