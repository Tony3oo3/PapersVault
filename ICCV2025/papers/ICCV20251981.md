## Problem

Dynamic 3D reconstruction and 3D point tracking are typically treated as disconnected tasks, with reconstruction methods (DUSt3R, MonST3R) ignoring temporal correspondence and tracking methods (SpatialTracker, CoTracker) operating only in camera coordinates. This separation prevents a unified understanding of scene dynamics that decouples camera motion from scene motion in a consistent world frame, and existing approaches rely heavily on 4D ground-truth supervision that is unavailable for in-the-wild videos.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images (frames I_i and I_j at different timestamps) and outputs two 3D pointmaps (H×W×3) both expressed in the world coordinate frame established by the first frame: a tracking pointmap showing where frame i's content has moved to at time j, and a reconstruction pointmap showing frame j's geometry at its own timestamp. For a full video, the first frame serves as a fixed anchor, and each subsequent frame is paired with it, yielding accumulated world-frame trajectories and per-frame point clouds. Camera intrinsics and extrinsics are also derived differentiably from the pointmap outputs. The model is trained on 24-frame sequences but generalizes to arbitrarily long videos at inference, running at 30 FPS on an RTX 4090.

## Method

The key insight is introducing a time-dependent pointmap representation: given frames I_i and I_j, the network predicts two pointmaps both in frame i's coordinate system but at frame j's timestamp — X_j^i (tracking: where frame i's points are at time j) and X_j^j (reconstruction: frame j's geometry at its own time) — using a DUSt3R-like siamese ViT encoder with dual cross-attention decoder. This minimal redefinition of the DUSt3R/MonST3R output unifies reconstruction and tracking, since chaining predictions from the anchor frame naturally yields long-range 3D correspondences. Training proceeds in two stages: full 4D supervision on synthetic data (Point Odyssey, Dynamic Replica, Kubric) using mesh vertices and depth maps, followed by reprojection-based self-supervision on real videos that leverages differentiable PnP to recover camera poses, then aligns projected 3D points with CoTracker 2D trajectories and MoGe monocular depth via scale-invariant losses. Test-time adaptation fine-tunes only the tracking branch on a target sequence, preserving the pretrained view-alignment capability of the frozen reconstruction branch.

## Results

On the new WorldTrack benchmark for 3D tracking in world coordinates, St4RTrack achieves the best APD₃D across all four datasets (PO, DR, ADT, PStudio) for both all points and dynamic points, with headline values of 67.95, 73.74, 76.01, and 69.67 respectively. For dynamic points specifically, margins are even larger (e.g., 68.72 on PO vs. 58.61 for the best baseline). On 3D reconstruction, St4RTrack achieves EPE of 0.2406 and APD of 78.73 on Point Odyssey, and EPE of 0.1854 and APD of 83.42 on TUM-Dynamics, outperforming all baselines including those with global alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

St4RTrack outperforms all four baselines on every dataset in the WorldTrack 3D tracking benchmark. Against SpatialTracker (the strongest camera-frame 3D tracker), St4RTrack gains +7.1 APD on PStudio (69.67 vs. 62.59), a fair comparison since PStudio has a fixed camera; on PO the gap widens to +29.4 (67.95 vs. 38.54). Against MonST3R (dynamic reconstruction without explicit tracking), St4RTrack leads by +34.5 on PO (67.95 vs. 33.47) and +15.7 on DR (73.74 vs. 58.06), though MonST3R is competitive on ADT (74.35 vs. 76.01) where scene motion is minimal. The combinational baselines (SpaTracker + RANSAC-Procrustes or SpaTracker + MonST3R) remain 15–25 APD points behind St4RTrack, confirming that ad-hoc pipelines cannot match the unified formulation. On 3D reconstruction (Table 2), St4RTrack beats MonST3R+GA by +6.4 APD on PO (78.73 vs. 72.31) and DUSt3R by +37.6 APD on TUM-Dynamics (83.42 vs. 72.26), demonstrating that joint tracking and reconstruction yields better geometry than either task alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

St4RTrack directly extends the DUSt3R → MonST3R lineage of two-pointmap 3D reconstruction by introducing a time-dependent pointmap definition: both pointmaps are predicted at the same timestamp in the same world frame, rather than each at its own timestamp as in MonST3R. This minimal redefinition of the geometric interpretation—without any architectural change to the siamese cross-attention decoder—unifies dynamic reconstruction and 3D point tracking into a single feed-forward pass. In contrast to prior 3D tracking methods (SpatialTracker, CoTracker3) that operate in camera coordinates and lack explicit camera-motion disentanglement, St4RTrack works entirely in world coordinates, and unlike optimization-based 4D methods (MoSca, Shape of Motion), it is a learned, generalizable framework that also supports test-time adaptation via a reprojection loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying reconstruction and tracking in a single world-frame representation, St4RTrack provides a practical path toward task-agnostic 4D perception from monocular video, and the new WorldTrack benchmark fills a gap left by camera-frame-only tracking evaluations. The reprojection-based adaptation scheme (using off-the-shelf 2D trackers and monocular depth as pseudo-labels) is a broadly applicable technique for bridging the synthetic-to-real gap without 4D supervision. Open questions include whether the reliance on CoTracker and MoGe pseudo-labels limits ceiling performance, whether the approach scales to very long sequences beyond the 64-frame evaluation, and whether the world-frame formulation generalizes to multi-camera or egocentric settings beyond the current benchmarks.
