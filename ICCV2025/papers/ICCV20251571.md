## Problem

Existing interpretability methods for black-box image classifiers either provide passive explanations (Grad-CAM, CRAFT) or enable only sample-level interventions (CBM, Post-hoc CBM, Beyond CBM) without modifying the model's parameters. CBM-based approaches sacrifice classification accuracy relative to the original black-box model, and their intervention mechanisms are constrained to adjusting concept weight matrices that depend heavily on human priors, leaving backbone biases uncorrected. The paper asks whether one can automatically identify and remove detrimental concepts from a black-box model's reasoning and transfer the correction back into the model's parameters, achieving both interpretability and accuracy gains without additional human annotation.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images as input and processes them through a pre-trained black-box classifier (CNN or ViT backbone). Internally, CRAFT extracts n visual concepts from the model's hidden-layer activations, and CLIP's image encoder maps these into a 512-d shared space where they are scored against a ChatGPT-3.5-Turbo-generated natural language concept bottleneck via CLIP's text encoder. The outputs are threefold: interpretable concept scores per image, a modified concept weight matrix (with detrimental concepts zeroed), and a retrained black-box model whose parameters have been updated via knowledge distillation to correct misclassifications on confused class pairs.

## Method

CBM-HNMU operates in three stages. First, confused class pairs are identified from validation-set misclassification frequencies, and a CBM with a learnable concept weight matrix W ∈ R^{N_Γ×N_c} is trained to approximate the black-box model's predictions on those classes using concept scores computed via CRAFT + CLIP cross-modal mapping. Second, gradient-based attribution (S_nT for concepts suppressing the true class, S_pF for concepts reinforcing the false class) is accumulated over the validation set to automatically rank and select the top-q most detrimental concepts per confused class, whose weights are zeroed in W. Third, the intervened CBM's predictions on confused classes are combined with frozen original predictions on non-confused classes (via a probability residual coefficient) to form a teacher signal, and the black-box model is fine-tuned by minimizing cross-entropy against this teacher, transferring the corrective knowledge back into the original model's parameters. The entire pipeline is label-free, requiring no human annotation beyond the automatically generated concept bottleneck.

## Results

CBM-HNMU achieves consistent global accuracy improvements across all five datasets and three primary architectures (NFResNet50, ViT-Small, GCVit). The largest single-dataset gain is 2.64% on CIFAR-10 with NFResNet50 (80.92% → 83.56%), while ViT-Small attains the highest average improvement of 1.03% across all five datasets. On fine-grained benchmarks, improvements range from 0.26% (CUB-200, NFResNet50) to 1.60% (FGVC-Aircraft, GCVit), confirming that the intervention is most effective where class distinctions are subtle.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CBM-HNMU outperforms both CBM and Post-hoc CBM on all three fine-grained datasets. On Flower-102, CBM-HNMU reaches 95.36%, surpassing CBM (92.57%) by 2.79% and Post-hoc CBM (93.58%) by 1.78%, while also exceeding the original black-box baseline (94.28%) by 1.08%. On CUB-200, the gap is most pronounced: CBM-HNMU (62.67%) beats CBM (54.53%) by 8.14% and Post-hoc CBM (58.20%) by 4.47%, and slightly edges the black-box baseline (62.41%) by 0.26%. On FGVC-Aircraft, CBM-HNMU (64.94%) leads CBM (56.31%) by 8.63% and Post-hoc CBM (64.45%) by 0.49%, again exceeding the black-box baseline (64.63%) by 0.31%. The key takeaway is that CBM-HNMU is the only method that simultaneously maintains interpretability (via the concept bottleneck) and matches or exceeds the original black-box accuracy, whereas both CBM and Post-hoc CBM sacrifice accuracy for interpretability.

## Relation to Prior Work

CBM-HNMU extends the Concept Bottleneck Model (Koh et al., 2020) and its Post-hoc variant (Yuksekgonul et al., 2022) by replacing their human-prior-dependent, sample-level concept weight adjustments with an automated, global gradient-contribution-based identification of detrimental concepts. It departs from model-approximation methods (VRX, DiConStruct) and Beyond CBM (Marcinkevics et al., 2024) by not merely explaining black-box behavior but directly modifying the black-box parameters through reverse knowledge distillation. The cross-modal concept alignment pipeline—combining CRAFT's NMF-based visual concept extraction, CLIP's shared embedding space, and ChatGPT-generated natural language bottlenecks—repurposes existing tools into a label-free intervention framework that prior CBM-based methods did not integrate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: most interpretability methods diagnose errors but offer no mechanism to correct them at the model-parameter level, while CBM-based interventions remain constrained to sample-level concept weight edits. The label-free pipeline (LLM + CLIP + unsupervised extraction) removes the annotation bottleneck that limits prior concept-based methods. However, the improvements are modest (≤2.64% accuracy gain, ~1% average), the approach requires a validation set for intervention, and the concept bottleneck quality depends on ChatGPT's generation—open questions include scalability to larger class spaces, robustness to concept bottleneck errors, and applicability beyond image classification.
