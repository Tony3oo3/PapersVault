## Problem

Diffusion-based super-resolution methods apply a uniform number of sampling steps across all image regions, ignoring that patches with sparse structural details can be reconstructed in fewer steps while texture-rich patches require more. This one-size-fits-all sampling wastes computation on easy regions and still incurs high inference costs, especially at high resolutions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251040/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB image, encodes it into latent space via a frozen VAE encoder, and produces a high-resolution RGB image after patch-level denoising and VAE decoding. During training, LR images are synthesized from LSDIR and HQ-50K using the Real-ESRGAN degradation pipeline with a latent patch size of 16. Evaluation covers ×4 SR at 512→2048 resolution, with LR inputs at 128×128 (LSDIR-Test, RealSR) or arbitrary resolutions (RealSet110).

## Method

PatchScaler introduces a patch-independent diffusion pipeline with three key components. First, a Global Restoration Module (GRM) removes degradations from the LR latent and simultaneously predicts a confidence map estimating per-region reconstruction difficulty. Second, Patch-adaptive Group Sampling (PGS) quantizes the confidence map into three difficulty groups (simple/medium/hard) and assigns each group a different shortcut point τ and number of sampling steps N along the diffusion trajectory, so simple patches start denoising from a later (less noisy) point with fewer steps while hard patches use the full trajectory. Third, a Patch-DiT backbone (modified from DiT) refines each coarse HR patch, conditioned on the original patch via concatenation and on a texture prompt retrieved from a pre-built Reference Texture Memory (RTM) of 2,000 representative texture patches selected via farthest-point sampling. The RTM retrieval uses a pre-trained texture classifier to compute semantic similarity, and the retrieved texture priors are injected through a time-independent similarity-aware encoder with cross-attention, avoiding per-step recomputation.

## Results

On the RealSet110 dataset, PatchScaler achieves the best non-reference scores across all three metrics: ManIQA 0.5442, CLIPIQA 0.8213, and MUSIQ 70.97, outperforming the next-best methods (DiffBIR at 0.4933 ManIQA, SwinIR-GAN at 0.8009 CLIPIQA, and OSEDiff at 70.26 MUSIQ). On the synthetic LSDIR-TEST set, PatchScaler leads with ManIQA 0.6261, CLIPIQA 0.7145, and MUSIQ 73.23. In terms of efficiency, PatchScaler runs in 3.41 s on the ×4 (512→2048) task, only 0.23× the runtime of the second-fastest diffusion method ResShift (14.89 s), and 10× faster than DiffBIR (78.12 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PatchScaler achieves the best ManIQA and MUSIQ scores across all three evaluation datasets, with the largest margins on RealSet110 (ManIQA +0.0509 over DiffBIR; MUSIQ +0.71 over OSEDiff) and LSDIR-TEST (ManIQA +0.0855 over DiffBIR; MUSIQ +1.07 over OSEDiff). On CLIPIQA, PatchScaler leads on LSDIR-TEST (0.7145 vs. OSEDiff's 0.7070) and RealSet110 (0.8213 vs. SwinIR-GAN's 0.8009), but is slightly behind OSEDiff on RealSR (0.5839 vs. 0.5890). Among diffusion-based multi-step methods, PatchScaler outperforms ResShift, StableSR, DiffBIR, and PASD on all non-reference metrics while running 4.4×–23× faster. Against one-step methods, PatchScaler surpasses OSEDiff on ManIQA and MUSIQ on all datasets and on CLIPIQA on two of three, while SinSR trails significantly on all perceptual metrics. On full-reference metrics (PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS), PatchScaler is competitive but not the best—LDM leads PSNR (24.14) and BSRGAN leads SSIM (0.5748)—which the authors note is expected for generative diffusion models that prioritize perceptual realism over pixel-level fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

PatchScaler builds on the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023) as its denoising backbone and the latent diffusion framework of Stable Diffusion (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) for encoding, while adapting the uncertainty-driven loss (Ning et al., NeurIPS 2021) to train its Global Restoration Module. Conceptually, it departs from prior diffusion-based SR accelerators (ResShift, SinSR, OSEDiff) that uniformly reduce sampling steps across all image regions, instead introducing a patch-adaptive shortcut that allocates different denoising budgets based on per-patch reconstruction difficulty. The texture prompt mechanism also diverges from the standard text-conditioned paradigm in diffusion models, replacing CLIP/BLIP-2 text embeddings with retrieved texture priors from a Reference Texture Memory to better address the image-text misalignment problem in SR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4.3× speedup over ResShift (0.23× runtime) while matching or exceeding perceptual quality metrics makes diffusion-based SR substantially more practical for high-resolution tasks (512→2048), and the plug-and-play demonstration on StableSR suggests the PGS strategy could accelerate other diffusion restoration pipelines without retraining. The texture prompt as an alternative to text conditioning is a useful direction for SR-specific diffusion models where local texture fidelity matters more than semantic alignment. However, real-time inference remains out of reach due to model size, and the authors acknowledge that integrating sampling distillation or engineering optimizations is needed for deployment. An open question is whether the three-group quantization of reconstruction difficulty is optimal or whether continuous per-patch scheduling could yield further gains without the combinatorial complexity of many discrete groups.
