## Problem

Different dual-encoder VLMs (CLIP, BLIP, BLIP2, EVA-CLIP) produce significantly inconsistent quality scores for the same image-text pairs, with standard deviations reaching ~60% of the upper bound. This disparity causes data filtering, weighting, and re-captioning to yield nearly disjoint subsets, and no single scoring model dominates across all downstream tasks—each exhibits task-specific biases. The paper addresses the lack of a robust, unified quality metric that mitigates these individual biases.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-computed score matrix of shape [N, M], where N is the number of image-text pairs and M is the number of scoring models (up to 18 in experiments), with each entry being a cosine similarity in [0, 1] extracted from a dual-encoder VLM. It outputs a single scalar ensemble quality score per image-text pair, computed in roughly 2 minutes for 1M pairs on one A800 GPU. The scores are one-time computations that can be reused across multiple data processing strategies (filtering, weighting, re-captioning) without re-scoring.

## Method

MoS performs a data-adaptive weighted ensemble over M pre-computed quality scores per image-text pair. For each score, it computes a 'density' (negative average L1 distance to all other scores), which measures consensus among scoring models—higher density means the score is less of an outlier. A per-sample temperature τ_i is set linearly proportional to the standard deviation of that sample's M scores, so high-uncertainty data receives a smoother (flatter) weight distribution. The final weight for each score is the softmax of density divided by temperature, and the MoS score is the weighted sum of all M scores. The entire computation requires only three lines of vectorized PyTorch code, with default hyperparameters τ_min=0.5 and τ_max=1.5, and L1 distance as the default metric.

## Results

On CC3M with CLIP ViT-B/32, MoS (ensembling all 18 baseline scores) achieves the best performance across all 8 evaluation benchmarks under all three data processing strategies. Under data filtering, MoS reaches 24.3 R@1 on Flickr30K I2T, 12.4 R@1 on MSCOCO I2T, 15.6% on ImageNet-1K, and 31.1% on CIFAR-100, outperforming the best single baseline by +0.8 to +1.6 points and the average baseline by +2.0 to +2.5 points. Under image re-captioning, the gains are even larger: MoS achieves 43.2 R@1 on Flickr30K I2T (+4.2 over best baseline) and 17.6% on ImageNet-1K (+2.4 over best baseline). MoS is the first method to simultaneously achieve the best score on every evaluation task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MoS against 18 individual quality scores from four model families: CLIP (R50, ViT-B/32, ViT-L/14), EVA-CLIP (ViT-L/14, ViT-G/14), BLIP (ViT-B/16, ViT-L/16), and BLIP-2 (ViT-L/14, ViT-G/14). No single baseline dominates all tasks: CLIP-family scores lead on fine-grained recognition (ImageNet-1K, ImageNet-R), while BLIP-family scores lead on retrieval (Flickr30K, MSCOCO) and common-category recognition (CIFAR-100, VOC2007). MoS outperforms the best single baseline on every benchmark under all three processing strategies, with the largest margins under image re-captioning (e.g., +4.2 R@1 on Flickr30K I2T, +2.4 on ImageNet-1K). Compared to the average of all baselines, MoS gains +4.3 R@1 on Flickr30K I2T under data filtering. The paper also shows that ensembling only the top-4 performing scores (e.g., CLIP-R50, EVA-CLIP-ViT-L/14, BLIP-ViT-B/16-pt, BLIP2-ViT-G/14-pt) can match or exceed the full 18-score ensemble, confirming that diversity across architectures matters more than sheer quantity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the ensemble learning paradigm (softmax-weighted combination) for the well-established task of image-text quality scoring via dual-encoder VLMs like CLIP, BLIP, BLIP2, and EVA-CLIP. Rather than proposing a new scoring architecture, it treats the existing per-model cosine-similarity scores as a discrete distribution and introduces a data-adaptive weighting scheme based on score density (consensus) and per-sample standard deviation (uncertainty). The key conceptual departure from prior work is the explicit identification of 'quality score disparity' as a first-class problem and the observation that different scoring models exhibit complementary task biases, motivating ensemble rather than single-model selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that filtered subsets from different scoring models have near-zero intersection (for ρ ≤ 50%) and that no single score dominates across all benchmarks directly challenges the common practice of picking one 'best' CLIP variant for data curation. The three-line implementation makes this trivially adoptable for any pipeline already computing multiple quality scores, and the heuristic recipes for selecting fewer but key scores address practical efficiency concerns. Open questions include whether the complementarity structure holds for next-generation scoring models (e.g., LLM-based captioners as scorers), whether the density-based weighting generalizes beyond cosine-similarity scores, and whether the approach transfers to other modalities (audio-text, video-text).
