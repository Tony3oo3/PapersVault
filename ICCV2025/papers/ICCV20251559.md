## Problem

Existing V2X collaborative perception datasets are limited in scope, diversity, and quality: they are collected exclusively in right-hand traffic countries, use homogeneous LiDAR configurations across all connected vehicles, and often suffer from poor point cloud alignment due to localization errors. This paper introduces Mixed Signals, the first real-world V2X dataset with heterogeneous CAV LiDAR configurations (differing in both position and orientation) collected in a left-hand traffic country (Australia), addressing these gaps to better reflect real-world deployment conditions.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides synchronized LiDAR point clouds at 10 Hz from three CAVs (two EVs with OS1-128 LiDARs tilted 15° downward at 1.63 m height, and one urban vehicle with a horizontal OS1-128 at 1.9 m) plus a roadside unit with dual LiDARs (OS1-64 TOP and OSDome-128 DOME at 2.5 m), totaling 45.1k point clouds. Annotations consist of 240.6k 3D bounding boxes (center, dimensions, quaternion rotation) across 10 fine-grained classes grouped into 3 detection classes (Vehicle, Bike, Pedestrian), with VRU classes comprising 50.3% of all boxes. The evaluation region is a 102.4 m × 102.4 m square in the TOP LiDAR coordinate frame, with a visibility threshold of 5 LiDAR points per object.

## Method

The core contribution is a carefully engineered data pipeline: GPS-synchronized LiDAR streams (≤50 ms timestamp mismatch) are localized via NDT scan-matching against dense pre-built point cloud maps, achieving ≤15 cm positioning and ≤0.4° heading error, yielding superior cross-agent alignment compared to prior V2X datasets. Professional annotators (FlipSideAI) label keyframes at 1 Hz using SegmentsAI, with linear interpolation for non-keyframes, and annotations are validated through multi-view consistency checks. The paper defines two benchmark tasks—Collaborative Object Detection (shared model across all agents) and Object Detection Enhanced by Communication to RSU (frozen RSU model, CAVs adapt to its messages)—and evaluates eight fusion methods (Early, Intermediate via Attentive Fusion/V2V-Net/F-Cooper/V2X-ViT/V2V-AM/Where2Comm, Late, and Laly Fusion) using AP at IoU 0.3/0.5/0.7 in BEV, alongside bandwidth consumption. A domain-gap experiment adapts models from the right-hand-traffic V2V4Real dataset, showing significant performance degradation that underscores the dataset's unique left-hand-traffic and heterogeneous-sensor characteristics.

## Results

On the Collaborative Object Detection task, V2X-ViT achieves the highest Vehicle AP@0.5 (0.84) and Pedestrian AP@0.5 (0.70), while V2V-AM leads on Bike AP@0.5 (0.79). All fusion methods substantially outperform the No Fusion baseline (Vehicle 0.42, Bike 0.19, Pedestrian 0.41), confirming the benefit of collaboration. Laly Fusion achieves near-competitive performance (Vehicle 0.61, Bike 0.68, Pedestrian 0.62) at only 0.11 MB bandwidth—roughly 1/100th of intermediate fusion methods—making it a strong candidate for real-world deployment. Models adapted from the right-hand-traffic V2V4Real dataset degrade severely (Vehicle AP drops from 0.65 to 0.24 for Early Fusion), highlighting the domain gap of left-hand traffic.

## Baselines & Comparisons

V2X-ViT [41] achieves the best overall detection (Vehicle 0.84, Pedestrian 0.70) but at the highest bandwidth cost (19.36 MB), while V2V-AM [21] leads on Bike detection (0.79) with moderate bandwidth (16.78 MB). Attentive Fusion [42] offers the best performance-to-bandwidth ratio among intermediate methods (Vehicle 0.82 at 5.26 MB), outperforming V2V-Net [36] on Pedestrian (0.68 vs. 0.32) and F-Cooper [6] on Vehicle (0.82 vs. 0.75). Where2Comm [14] shows a striking weakness on Pedestrian (0.18) despite competitive Vehicle (0.77) and Bike (0.74) scores, suggesting its spatial confidence maps are less effective for small, dynamic VRUs. Laly Fusion [7] is the standout for bandwidth efficiency, achieving 0.61/0.68/0.62 AP at just 0.11 MB—comparable to Early Fusion (0.65/0.65/0.67) at 1/70th the communication cost. The domain-gap experiment shows that models transferred from V2V4Real [43] (right-hand traffic) collapse to 0.24 (Early) and 0.12 (Late) Vehicle AP, far below their native-performance counterparts, confirming that left-hand traffic and heterogeneous sensor configurations introduce a substantial distribution shift not captured by prior V2X datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the existing V2X dataset landscape (OPV2V, V2X-Set, DAIR-V2X, V2X-Real, V2V4Real, TUMTrafV2X), identifying two critical gaps: all prior real-world datasets use homogeneous CAV LiDAR configurations, and all are collected in right-hand traffic countries. Rather than building on a specific prior method, Mixed Signals carves new ground by introducing heterogeneous sensor configurations (differing in both LiDAR height and tilt angle) and left-hand traffic dynamics, while benchmarking the full spectrum of existing collaborative perception methods (Early/Intermediate/Late fusion, V2V-Net, V2X-ViT, F-Cooper, Laly Fusion) under these new conditions. The 'Object Detection Enhanced by Communication to RSU' task extends standard V2I detection by requiring the CAV model to adapt to a frozen, independently-trained RSU model, introducing a cross-domain communication challenge not present in prior V2I benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As V2X deployment moves toward real-world fleets with diverse sensor suites, the absence of heterogeneous-sensor and left-hand-traffic data has limited the generalizability of collaborative perception research. Mixed Signals addresses this by providing 45.1k point clouds with 50.3% VRU annotations (far exceeding prior datasets), precise sub-15cm localization, and professional-grade annotations consistent across time and viewpoints. Key open questions remain: VRU detection still underperforms substantially across all fusion methods, the domain gap from RHT to LHT is severe (models predict incorrect vehicle headings), and no current method handles heterogeneous LiDAR modalities (e.g., missing intensity on EVs) effectively. The single-intersection, single-city scope limits geographic generalizability, and the 37-scene scale, while sufficient for benchmarking, is modest compared to large-scale driving datasets.
