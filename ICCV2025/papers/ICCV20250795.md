## Problem

Mainstream VLMs (InternVL2, LLaVA-v1.5, MiniGPT-4) lack visual noise augmentation during training, leaving them vulnerable to even simple Gaussian noise that degrades both helpfulness and safety alignment. Existing safety fine-tuning datasets like VLGuard do not address noise-augmented inputs or image-text misalignment scenarios, and prior defenses (JailGuard, CIDER) either incur high inference costs or noticeably harm model helpfulness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250795/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline accepts an RGB image (potentially corrupted with Gaussian noise or PGD-optimized adversarial perturbations at pixel constraints ε ∈ {16/255, 32/255, 64/255}) paired with a text prompt. DiffPure-VLM first applies a diffusion model (DDPM) with a small timestep t* ∈ [50, 150] to shift adversarial noise into a Gaussian-like residual, then feeds the purified image into the noise-augmented VLM, which produces a safe text response. During training, images are augmented with Gaussian noise at a randomly sampled σ ∈ [0.01, 0.15] with 70% probability of application.

## Method

The defense has two complementary components. First, Robust-VLGuard is a 6,467-sample dataset combining 4,467 GPT-4V-refined general instructions (spanning QA, OCR, math, spatial reasoning) with 2,000 safety instructions split into image-text aligned pairs (from VLGuard) and image-text misaligned pairs (text-only safety prompts paired with random COCO images or kept text-only). VLMs are fine-tuned on this dataset using LoRA on the vision encoder only, with Gaussian noise augmentation, over 3 epochs (~3 hours on one A100). Second, DiffPure-VLM leverages the distribution-shifting property of diffusion models: applying DiffPure at a small timestep (t*=50) transforms non-Gaussian adversarial perturbations into a Gaussian-like residual (verified via kurtosis and Q-Q deviation metrics), which the noise-augmented VLM is specifically trained to tolerate. This two-stage design—preprocessing to convert arbitrary adversarial noise into the trained noise distribution, followed by a robust VLM—outperforms both JailGuard (mutation-based detection) and standalone DiffPure on attack success rates across all three base models.

## Results

On the MM-Vet helpfulness benchmark, Robust-VLGuard fine-tuning preserves performance far better than VLGuard: InternVL2-8B drops only 3.7 points (56.2 vs. 59.9) versus VLGuard's 17.0-point drop (42.9), and MiniGPT-4-13B actually improves by 0.2 points. On the RealToxicityPrompts safety benchmark under Gaussian noise, Robust-VLGuard reduces attack success rate from 44.1% to 16.5% for MiniGPT-4-13B and from 57.2% to 34.5% for InternVL2-8B. For optimization-based adversarial attacks (ε=32/255), the full DiffPure-VLM pipeline reduces attack success rate from 70.6% to 33.4% (InternVL2-8B) and from 53.7% to 13.6% (MiniGPT-4-13B), nearly restoring clean-image safety levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VLGuard [51], Robust-VLGuard wins decisively on both axes: it preserves helpfulness (InternVL2 MM-Vet drop of −3.7 vs. −17.0; MiniGPT-4 +0.2 vs. −9.2) while achieving lower or comparable attack success rates under noise (MiniGPT-4: 16.5% vs. 43.7%; LLaVA: 42.3% vs. 52.3%). Against JailGuard [49], the paper reports that DiffPure-VLM (t*=50) outperforms JailGuard on LLaVA-RobustVLGuard (43.9% vs. 43.9% attack success rate, a tie) and more substantially on InternVL2-8B-RobustVLGuard where DiffPure-VLM reaches 33.4% versus JailGuard's higher rates; however, on LLaVA-VLGuard, JailGuard (51.1%) actually outperforms standalone DiffPure (62.5%), indicating DiffPure's advantage is specifically unlocked when paired with noise-augmented safety fine-tuning. Against standalone DiffPure [33] without noise-augmented fine-tuning (Table 3), applying DiffPure to Gaussian-noised images yields no improvement (44.3% vs. 44.1% attack success rate), confirming that the distribution-shifting property of DiffPure is only effective in concert with the Gaussian-noise-tolerant VLMs produced by Robust-VLGuard fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two distinct lines of work: (1) DiffPure (Nie et al., ICML 2022), which used diffusion models to purify adversarial images for classifiers, is repurposed here not as a complete noise remover but as a distribution shifter that converts adversarial perturbations into Gaussian-like noise; and (2) VLGuard (Zong et al., ICML 2024), a safety fine-tuning dataset for VLMs, is extended with image-text misaligned pairs, general instructions, and Gaussian noise augmentation during training. The key conceptual departure from prior VLM defense work (e.g., JailGuard, CIDER) is the unification of noise robustness and adversarial defense into a single mechanism: if the model is trained to tolerate Gaussian noise, and a diffusion model converts adversarial noise into that same distribution, one pipeline handles both threats.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that even trivial Gaussian noise (σ=0.1) significantly degrades both helpfulness and safety alignment across all three evaluated VLMs is practically important, as it exposes a gap that existing safety training pipelines do not address. The proposed defense is remarkably cheap (LoRA on the vision encoder only, ~3 hours on a single A100), making it accessible for practitioners deploying VLMs. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other noise distributions or more sophisticated multi-step attacks, whether integrating noise augmentation into pretraining (rather than post-hoc fine-tuning) would yield stronger guarantees, and whether the diffusion-based preprocessing introduces its own failure modes at higher timesteps or with conditional diffusion models.
