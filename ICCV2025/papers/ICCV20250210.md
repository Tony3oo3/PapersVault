## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting requires accurate camera poses and high-fidelity point clouds, typically obtained from time-consuming SfM pipelines like COLMAP. Real-world multi-camera SLAM systems, however, produce noisy device poses, imprecise intrinsics, and low-quality point clouds due to sensor drift, asynchronous capture, and imperfect calibration. This paper addresses the gap of jointly refining camera parameters and reconstructing 3D scenes directly from such imprecise SLAM outputs without any SfM support.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multi-camera SLAM sequence (4 fisheye RGB cameras, IMU, and Lidar in a rigid body) producing coarsely-estimated per-frame device poses, approximate per-camera intrinsics, and a noisy fused point cloud used to initialize the 3DGS. It outputs a refined 3DGS scene representation together with corrected camera intrinsics (focal length, principal point) and extrinsics (decomposed into shared camera-to-device and per-frame device-to-world transforms), enabling high-fidelity novel view synthesis. A test-time adaptation step further refines poses for held-out images using a frozen 3DGS.

## Method

The core mechanism decomposes each camera pose into a shared camera-to-device extrinsic and a per-frame device-to-world pose, reducing 60k degrees of freedom to approximately 15k and enabling stable joint optimization. Refinement is applied via right-multiplication of small SE(3) deltas to avoid world-origin rotation instability, with analytical gradients computed for intrinsic parameters. Two optimization constraints prevent degenerate solutions: a sensitivity-based pre-conditioning strategy (inspired by Levenberg-Marquardt) that scales per-parameter learning rates using the diagonal of the inverse square root of the Jacobian product, and a log-barrier penalty that confines parameters to empirically set feasible bounds (e.g., plus/minus 2% for intrinsics, plus/minus 0.625 and 2.5 degrees for rotations). Two geometric regularizers, a soft epipolar constraint via Sampson distance on LoFTR keypoint matches and a depth-aware reprojection error extending bundle adjustment into the 3DGS training loop, provide strong priors on relative camera geometry complementary to the photometric loss.

## Results

On the authors' four-scene dataset (Cafeteria, Office, Laboratory, Town), the proposed method achieves PSNR of 29.05, 26.07, 28.64, and 24.52 with SSIM of 0.917, 0.885, 0.910, and 0.826 respectively, outperforming 3DGS-COLMAP (which requires 4–12 hours of pre-processing) in all scenes while needing only 5 minutes of preparation. On the GarageWorld and Waymo public benchmarks, the method achieves the best score in 12 out of 15 metric-scene combinations, surpassing multimodal baselines LetsGo and Street-GS. The method also improves SSIM by 6.8% over the pose-optimization baseline when the log-barrier constraint is applied, and reduces mean epipolar line error from 2.70 to 0.75 pixels via geometric constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all nine baselines across all four scenes in the authors' dataset. Against the strongest COLMAP-based baseline (3DGS-COLMAP), the method wins on PSNR in every scene (e.g., 29.05 vs. 17.03 on Cafeteria where COLMAP fails due to repetitive patterns, and 28.64 vs. 28.30 on Laboratory) while reducing pre-processing time from 4–12 hours to 5 minutes. The rig-based variant 3DGS-COLMAP△, which is the most relevant baseline, is surpassed on all scenes (e.g., SSIM 0.9168 vs. 0.8379 on Cafeteria). SLAM-based 3DGS methods (CF-3DGS, MonoGS) perform substantially worse, with SSIM dropping to 0.31–0.75, due to their reliance on high covisibility that the noisy multi-camera dataset violates. On the public benchmarks (Table 2), the method achieves the best PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS in 12 of 15 metric-scene combinations against LetsGo and Street-GS, with the largest gains on GarageWorld Group6 (PSNR 23.76 vs. 21.23 for 3DGS) and Waymo Scene002 (PSNR 29.75 vs. 27.96 for Street-GS). InstantSplat, which leverages a foundation model for relative poses, is the closest non-COLMAP competitor but still trails by 2–9 dB PSNR across scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) training pipeline by jointly refining camera intrinsics, extrinsics, and scene geometry from noisy multi-camera SLAM initialization, without relying on COLMAP. It departs from prior pose-optimization methods (BARF, iComMa, GS-CPR) that assume a pre-trained high-quality 3DGS model or single-camera setups, and from multimodal 3DGS approaches (LetsGo, Street-GS) that still depend on COLMAP for pose refinement. The key structural contribution is decomposing multi-camera poses into shared camera-to-device and per-frame device-to-world transformations, combined with sensitivity-based pre-conditioning (inspired by Levenberg-Marquardt) and log-barrier constraints to manage the over-parameterized optimization landscape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: replacing a 4–12 hour COLMAP preprocessing step with a 5-minute constrained optimization pipeline while matching or exceeding rendering quality removes a major bottleneck for real-world 3DGS deployment, particularly in industrial scanning and autonomous driving. The new multi-camera SLAM dataset (four fisheye cameras, IMU, Lidar) fills a gap in public benchmarks for noisy, rapidly-acquired scans. Open questions include scalability to very large outdoor scenes beyond the 85×45 m Town scene, whether the approach extends to real-time or online settings, and how sensitive the method is to the number of cameras and the quality of the initial SLAM trajectory.
