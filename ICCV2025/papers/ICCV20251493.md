## Problem

Label-free multiview pedestrian detection remains challenging under sparse viewpoints and severe occlusions: stereo-based methods fail to reconstruct geometry with wide camera baselines, while monocular depth estimation methods lack cross-view consistency, producing misaligned point clouds that inflate false positives. Existing label-free approaches (e.g., UMPD) are sensitive to pixel-level errors and struggle in crowded, sparse-view scenes, resulting in inconsistent projections and degraded localization accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251493/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sparse multiview RGB images (e.g., 4–7 calibrated cameras at 1080×1920), 2D pedestrian segmentation masks from YOLOv11, and camera intrinsic/extrinsic parameters. It produces 3D pedestrian locations as DBSCAN cluster centers with confidence scores, segmented 3D Gaussians with consistent cross-view IDs, and multiview-consistent depth maps. Notably, the pipeline requires no 3D annotations during training and achieves 1.2 FPS inference on an RTX 4090, with human modeling construction taking only 0.1 seconds.

## Method

DCHM introduces an iterative three-stage training loop: (1) superpixel-wise Gaussian Splatting generates pseudo-depth labels, using a novel 3D superpixel-based initialization (uniform ray sampling + background filtering) that bypasses the SfM failure in sparse views, optimized with a composite loss of superpixel photometric, mask, depth-variance, and opacity terms; (2) the filtered pseudo-depth (validated via cross-view foreground and depth-consistency checks) fine-tunes an off-the-shelf monocular depth network (Depth Anything v2); (3) multiview detection compensation projects source-view detections into a reference view and uses SAM with point-box prompts to recover occluded pedestrians. At inference, the fine-tuned mono-depth backprojects to a global point cloud converted to Gaussians, which are segmented via multiview label matching (assigning consistent IDs by projecting Gaussians onto reference views) and localized via DBSCAN clustering with NMS. The superpixel-wise supervision is key: it aggregates local features to mitigate pixel-level inconsistency in overlapping regions, improving MODA from 71.7% (pixel-wise) to 84.2%.

## Results

On the Wildtrack dataset, DCHM achieves 84.2% MODA and 80.3% MODP, outperforming the strongest label-free baseline UMPD (76.6% MODA, 61.2% MODP) by 7.6 and 19.1 percentage points respectively. On Terrace, DCHM reaches 80.1% MODA and 73.9% MODP, surpassing UMPD (73.8%, 59.0%) by 6.3 and 14.9 points. On MultiviewX, DCHM attains 78.4% MODA and 82.3% MODP, exceeding UMPD (67.5%, 79.4%) by 10.9 and 2.9 points. In the human modeling ablation (Table 1), DCHM's reconstruction (84.2% MODA, 80.3% MODP) outperforms the best off-the-shelf depth estimator, Depth Pro (72.8%, 64.1%), by 11.4 and 16.2 points under the same label-free localization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCHM outperforms all ten compared baselines across every dataset and metric where they overlap. Against the strongest label-free competitor UMPD [25], DCHM gains +7.6 MODA and +19.1 MODP on Wildtrack, +6.3 MODA and +14.9 MODP on Terrace, and +10.9 MODA and +2.9 MODP on MultiviewX. The earlier label-free methods (RCNN&clustering, POM-CNN, Pre-DeepMCD, BP&BB+CC) trail DCHM by 27–73 MODA points on Wildtrack, confirming that depth-consistent reconstruction is the key differentiator. In the human-modeling ablation (Table 1), DCHM's superpixel-wise GS reconstruction (84.2/80.3) beats every off-the-shelf depth estimator—Depth Pro (72.8/64.1), Depth Anything v2 (68.7/55.1), Mast3R (63.2/60.2), Metric3D v2 (65.2/59.8), and Marigold (58.1/47.0)—under the identical label-free localization, isolating the reconstruction quality as the source of the overall gain. The paper also reports that integrating DCHM's human modeling with the label-based cardboard method [29] yields state-of-the-art results among supervised approaches, suggesting the reconstruction module is broadly transferable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the label-free multiview pedestrian detection line (UMPD, POM-CNN, BP&BB+CC) by replacing their direct 2D-to-3D projection or volume-rendering strategies with an iterative Gaussian Splatting pipeline that self-generates pseudo-depth labels. It repurposes 3D Gaussian Splatting—not as a final rendering tool but as a geometric pseudo-label generator for monocular depth fine-tuning—and introduces superpixel-wise optimization to overcome the sparse-view initialization problem that breaks standard SfM-based GS. The multiview detection compensation via SAM prompting and the cross-view depth-consistency filtering are novel components that depart from prior label-free methods' reliance on heuristic standing points or single-pass volume regression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DCHM is the first framework to jointly perform 3D pedestrian reconstruction and multiview segmentation in sparse-view, large-scale, crowded scenes without any 3D annotations, addressing a gap where prior label-free methods produced inconsistent projections and inflated false positives. The superpixel-wise GS ablation (71.7 → 84.2 MODA) reveals that pixel-level photometric supervision is fundamentally unreliable under wide-baseline sparse views, a conceptual insight that may transfer to other sparse-view reconstruction tasks. Open questions include scalability beyond pedestrian-scale objects, sensitivity to segmentation quality (YOLOv11 masks drive the entire pipeline), and whether the three-iteration convergence limit holds in more extreme occlusion or view-count regimes.
