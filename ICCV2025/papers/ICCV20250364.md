## Problem

Few-step distilled diffusion models (student models) still exhibit a noticeable quality gap compared to their full-step teacher counterparts, due to accumulated errors in multi-step sampling and distribution shifts between training and inference. Prior remedies rely on additional real training data, which introduces further distribution mismatch on out-of-distribution prompts. No prior work has explored using the teacher model to guide the student's sampling trajectory at inference time, i.e., after training is complete.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250364/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference time, the method consumes a text prompt, the student model's intermediate denoised estimate at an early sampling step (e.g., the first of 4–8 steps), and a frozen pre-trained teacher diffusion model (e.g., SDXL). It produces a refined clean-image estimate that replaces the student's original estimate before the remaining student sampling steps proceed. The teacher is invoked for only 1–2 initial steps, and both models share the same VAE, so no additional data, fine-tuning, or architectural modification is required; peak GPU memory stays ≤24 GB.

## Method

Distillation++ recasts each student sampling step as a proximal optimization problem regularized by a score distillation sampling (SDS) loss computed with the teacher model. Concretely, the student's denoised estimate x̂₀^θ(t) is renoised to a nearby noise level s (using a decreasing schedule s = t−1), denoised by the teacher to obtain x̂₀^ψ(s), and then the student's estimate is updated by a convex interpolation x̂_new = (1−λ)x̂₀^θ(t) + λx̂₀^ψ(s), which is equivalent to one gradient step on the SDS loss while bypassing the intractable score Jacobian via the Decomposed Diffusion Sampling trick. This update is integrated directly into the DDIM/Euler iterate so that the refined estimate is renoised and fed into the next student step. The framework is solver-agnostic (compatible with Euler, DPM++ 2S Ancestral, and iterative consistency-model sampling), requires no additional training data or parameter updates, and can be applied to a broad range of student architectures including LCM, LCM-LoRA, SDXL-Lightning, DMD2, and SDXL-Turbo.

## Results

On MS-COCO 10K with 4-step student sampling plus 1 step of teacher-guided inference-time distillation, Distillation++ consistently improves FID, ImageReward, and PickScore across all five distillation baselines. The largest FID gains appear for SDXL-Lightning LoRA (25.304 → 24.429) and SDXL-Lightning (24.506 → 23.876), while the smallest is for SDXL-Turbo (18.612 → 18.481). PickScore (user preference) improves by 0.01–0.036 across all models, with the most notable jump for SDXL-Lightning LoRA (0.482 → 0.518). The additional wall-clock cost of one teacher step is comparable to a single student step (e.g., 1.987 s vs. 1.996 s for LCM 4+1 vs. 5-step), and peak GPU memory stays ≤ 24 GB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Distillation++ outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric (FID, ImageReward, PickScore) with only a single additional teacher-model evaluation step. Against LCM, it reduces FID from 20.674 to 20.149 and lifts PickScore from 0.494 to 0.505; against LCM-LoRA the FID drop is larger (20.300 → 19.815) and PickScore gains 0.020. The most substantial relative improvements come from the SDXL-Lightning family: FID drops by 0.63 (Lightning) and 0.88 (Lightning LoRA), while PickScore jumps by 0.036 for the LoRA variant (0.482 → 0.518), the largest single-metric gain in the table. DMD2 sees a modest but consistent improvement (FID 21.238 → 20.937, PickScore 0.490 → 0.510). SDXL-Turbo, already the strongest baseline in FID (18.612), shows the smallest absolute gains (FID 18.481, PickScore 0.501), suggesting diminishing returns when the student is already well-aligned with the teacher. Notably, the method also surpasses simply adding an extra student sampling step (e.g., LCM 4+1 step FID 20.149 vs. 5-step FID 20.732), confirming that teacher guidance is more effective than additional student iterations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Score Distillation Sampling (SDS), originally a training-time loss in DreamFusion and subsequent distillation methods (DMD, DMD2), as an inference-time guidance mechanism that steers student model sampling trajectories toward the teacher's distribution. The derivation draws on Decomposed Diffusion Sampling (DDS) to bypass intractable Jacobian computation, and the resulting update is shown to be structurally analogous to classifier-free guidance, with the teacher model playing the role of the 'conditional' branch. Unlike prior distillation approaches (LCM, SDXL-Lightning, DMD2) that compress the PF-ODE integral during training, Distillation++ operates entirely post-training and data-free, positioning itself as a complementary sampling correction rather than a replacement for the distillation procedure itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in its simplicity and low cost: a single teacher evaluation at the first sampling step (comparable wall-clock time to one student step due to parallel CFG computation) yields consistent FID, ImageReward, and PickScore gains across five different distillation baselines without any retraining or additional data. This opens a viable post-training correction path for the persistent quality gap between few-step students and their teacher counterparts, and the framework is solver-agnostic (Euler, DPM++ 2S Ancestral, iterative consistency sampling). Open questions include whether the approach extends to video diffusion distillation, whether multi-step teacher guidance compounds gains, and how it interacts with emerging flow-matching architectures. A limitation is the need to load both teacher and student models simultaneously, though peak memory remains under 24 GB.
