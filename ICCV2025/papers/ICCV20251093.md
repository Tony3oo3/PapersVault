## Problem

Existing trajectory refinement methods model vehicle interactions through implicit feature learning in latent space, lacking explicit topological structure to guide information fusion. While braid theory has been applied to enable interaction only between crossing trajectories (BeTop), this approach neglects non-crossing interactions (e.g., one vehicle decelerating for another), ignores temporal dynamics, and cannot characterize *how* trajectories interact. The paper addresses these gaps by introducing a soft-braid topology that captures spatio-temporal relationships among all trajectory pairs.

## Input / Output

SRefiner takes as input the initially predicted multi-mode future trajectories Y₀ ∈ R^{K×N×T₊×2} from any baseline predictor, the vectorized HD map lanes (M centerlines with coordinates and semantics), and the agents' historical trajectories. It outputs refined trajectories Y = Y₀ + R(Y₀, L) through I=3 iterative refinement steps, where each step produces a trajectory offset added to the previous iteration's output. The method is plug-and-play: it operates on the K-mode multi-hypothesis output of the baseline and refines all N agents simultaneously in a single forward pass, unlike methods such as SmartRefine that process agents independently.

## Method

The core mechanism is Soft-Braid Attention, which defines 'soft intersection points' as the pair of closest points on two trajectories at the same time t_ij = argmin_t ‖y_i(t) − y_j(t)‖. The soft-braid topology between trajectories i and j encodes both agents' velocities and accelerations at these points (in each agent's local coordinate frame), plus the distance and orientation of the connecting line, forming a rich spatio-temporal descriptor that replaces the binary crossing indicator of classical braid topology. This topology is injected into multi-head cross-attention as additive features on keys and values, enabling topology-guided information fusion among trajectories within a local radius (τ_a=50m). The approach extends to trajectory-lane interactions by defining the closest waypoint to a lane as a soft intersection point, encoding the agent's motion state and spatial offset relative to the lane. A progressive refinement strategy uses the topology computed from the previous iteration's refined trajectories to guide the current iteration, and the entire pipeline is trained with a joint winner-takes-all Huber loss across all I iterations.

## Results

On Argoverse v2 (Forecast-MAE baseline), SRefiner achieves avgMinFDE of 1.477, avgMinADE of 0.658, and actorMR of 0.183, outperforming the strongest prior refinement method MTR++ (1.495 / 0.670 / 0.183) while running at 28 ms latency versus MTR++'s 54 ms. On INTERACTIONS (FJMP baseline), SRefiner reaches minJointFDE of 0.579, minJointADE of 0.170, and minJointMR of 0.110, again beating MTR++ (0.585 / 0.172 / 0.111) at 18 ms. When plugged into four different baselines, SRefiner yields consistent relative improvements of 1.8%–10.7% in FDE across both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRefiner outperforms all five prior refinement methods (DCMS, R-Pred, QCNet, SmartRefine, MTR++) on both Argoverse v2 and INTERACTIONS, achieving the lowest FDE, ADE, and MR on every metric. The margin over the closest competitor MTR++ is 0.018 in avgMinFDE on Argoverse v2 (1.477 vs 1.495) and 0.006 in minJointFDE on INTERACTIONS (0.579 vs 0.585), while also being roughly 2× faster in inference latency (28 ms vs 54 ms; 18 ms vs 30 ms). As a plug-in refiner, SRefiner improves FJMP by 9.6% avgMinFDE on Argoverse v2 and 8.1% minJointFDE on INTERACTIONS, Forecast-MAE by 10.1% on Argoverse v2, AutoBots by 10.5% on INTERACTIONS, and HPNet by 1.8% on INTERACTIONS. Against the braid-topology baseline BeTop (Table 4), SRefiner's soft-braid topology reduces avgMinFDE from 1.512 to 1.477, confirming that the richer spatio-temporal encoding of soft intersection points provides a meaningful gain over binary crossing/non-crossing gating.

## Relation to Prior Work

SRefiner extends the braid-topology-based trajectory interaction modeling introduced by BeTop [14] (NeurIPS 2024), which enables information fusion only between crossing trajectories. The key conceptual departure is replacing the binary crossing/non-crossing signal with 'soft intersection points'—the closest points on two trajectories at the same time—thereby capturing spatio-temporal dynamics even in non-crossing interactions (e.g., one vehicle decelerating for another). Within the broader trajectory refinement literature (QCNet, R-Pred, MTR++, SmartRefine), SRefiner shifts from implicit attention-based interaction modeling to explicit topological feature encoding, positioning itself as a more interpretable refinement mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit topological representation offers interpretability advantages over purely implicit attention, and the soft-braid formulation provides a principled way to encode *how* trajectories interact rather than merely *whether* they do. However, the method inherits error propagation from the baseline predictor (topology is computed from predicted, not ground-truth, trajectories), and the 28 ms latency, while competitive, is higher than lightweight refiners like DCMS (5 ms). Open questions include whether the soft-braid formulation generalizes to 3D trajectories or non-vehicle agents, and whether the progressive topology-update strategy yields further gains with more iterations or in denser traffic scenarios.
