## Problem

CLIP's attention mechanism produces outlier image tokens that are over-activated across all input tokens, and these outliers originate in intermediate layers and propagate through subsequent layers, degrading spatial perception for dense prediction. Existing training-free methods either only modify the final layer's attention or rely on empirical parameter tuning to remove a fixed number of outliers, leaving propagated outliers unaddressed and limiting segmentation accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 336 px short side, or 560 px for Cityscapes) and text prompts constructed from standard ImageNet templates combined with class names. Inference uses sliding-window processing with 224×224 windows and 112×112 stride over the CLIP ViT-B/16 encoder. The output is a pixel-level segmentation mask at the original image resolution, produced by computing cosine similarity between the purified visual feature map and text features, followed by upsampling.

## Method

SFP introduces three parameter-free modules applied to a frozen CLIP ViT. First, a Self-adaptive Outlier Mitigator (SOM) is inserted after every transformer layer: it detects outliers by flagging image tokens whose self-attention weight (diagonal entry) is lower than their attention weight to the class token, then replaces each outlier's feature with the mean of its 8 spatial neighbours, preventing propagation without altering the attention map. Second, a Semantic-aware Attention Enhancer (SAE) replaces the final transformer layer, computing a self-self attention map (QQᵀ + KKᵀ scaled by λ=0.5) on the purified feature and adding it back via a residual FFN connection to boost activation in semantically relevant regions. Third, a Hierarchical Attention Integrator (HAI) aggregates attention maps from all preceding layers after zeroing out outlier columns, applies iterative row-wise and column-wise normalization for an object-centric distribution, and multiplies the result with the SAE feature to produce the final visual representation for cosine-similarity-based segmentation.

## Results

SFP achieves an average mIoU of 44.0% across eight segmentation benchmarks (V21, PC60, Object, V20, PC59, Stuff, ADE20K, Cityscapes) using CLIP ViT-B/16 without any additional backbone or training. The headline gains are 63.9% mIoU on PASCAL VOC 2012 (V21) and 41.1% on Cityscapes, surpassing the previous training-free SOTA NACLIP by 4.6% average mIoU and 5.6% on Cityscapes. SFP also outperforms methods that rely on extra backbones (e.g., ProxyCLIP with DINO) by 1.7% average mIoU and training-based methods with extra backbones (e.g., DINOiser) by 3.7% average mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among training-free methods without extra backbones, SFP outperforms the previous SOTA NACLIP [16] by 4.6% average mIoU (44.0 vs. 39.4), with the largest per-dataset gains on Cityscapes (+5.6%) and PASCAL VOC 2012 (+5.0%). SFP also beats SCLIP [45] by 4.9% and ClearCLIP [24] by 5.2% average mIoU, and CLIPtrase [40] by 6.5%. Against methods that require an additional backbone, SFP surpasses ProxyCLIP [25] (which uses DINO) by 1.7% average mIoU and by 3.0% on Cityscapes specifically, and outperforms DINOiser [47] (which requires both training and DINO) by 3.7% average mIoU. Compared to vanilla CLIP [38], SFP improves average mIoU by 29.9 points (44.0 vs. 14.1), demonstrating that the feature purification pipeline substantially recovers spatial information lost in CLIP's classification-oriented attention. SFP also maintains its advantage across different CLIP backbones (ViT-B/32, ViT-L/14), gaining 1.3–1.8% over ProxyCLIP in those settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free CLIP-based OVSS line of work (MaskCLIP, CLIPtrase, SCLIP, ClearCLIP, NACLIP) by shifting the focus from last-layer attention modification to multi-layer outlier suppression. It builds on the observation from Darcet et al. (2024) that outliers emerge in intermediate transformer layers, but departs from prior methods that only patch the final layer or rely on fixed-count empirical tuning. The key conceptual departure is treating outlier propagation as a sequential, layer-by-layer problem and introducing a parameter-free detection criterion (self-attention weight vs. class-token attention weight) that adapts automatically across layers and backbones, combined with a self-self attention enhancer and hierarchical attention integration for spatial refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4.6% average mIoU gain over the previous SOTA (NACLIP) across eight benchmarks, achieved without any extra backbone or training, demonstrates that feature purification is a critical and previously under-addressed bottleneck in training-free OVSS. The parameter-free, backbone-agnostic design (validated on ViT-B/32, B/16, and L/14) makes the approach broadly applicable and easy to adopt. Open questions include whether the 8-neighbour replacement strategy is optimal for outlier mitigation, whether the detection criterion generalizes to non-CLIP VLMs (e.g., SigLIP, DINOv2-based encoders), and whether the per-layer computational overhead becomes prohibitive for very deep or high-resolution settings.
