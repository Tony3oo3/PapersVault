## Problem

Existing input transformation-based adversarial attacks compute gradients on inputs where the target object remains at a fixed position, thereby activating only a single behavioral pattern of the surrogate model. This neglects the spatial invariance property of DNNs—where class-relevant weights are distributed across multiple image regions—resulting in less diverse adversarial perturbations and reduced transferability to unseen target models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251717/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 RGB image from ImageNet and its ground-truth class label, and produces an adversarial example under an L∞ norm constraint (ε=16). During gradient computation, the input is transformed N=20 times per iteration via multi-scale downsampling (ratio 1−β·n/N), random padding to restore original dimensions, horizontal flipping, and block-level fusion, so the gradient is averaged over an ensemble of spatially and spectrally diverse views of the same image.

## Method

SID (Spatial Invariance Diversity) is an input transformation operator T(·) integrated into MI-FGSM that activates diverse DNN behavioral patterns across spatial scales and positions. The transformation divides the image into k×k blocks; for each block, with probability p, either a linear fusion (weighted combination of the globally rescaled image and the local block) or a frequency fusion (DCT-based combination of high-frequency content from the rescaled image with low-frequency content from the local block) is applied. The fused image is then downsampled at a scale that decreases with iteration count, randomly padded back to the original size, and optionally horizontally flipped. This multi-scale, multi-position, multi-fusion pipeline generates an ensemble of transformed inputs whose averaged gradient captures class-relevant patterns from many spatial locations, yielding perturbations with broader transferability than single-position baselines.

## Results

On the ImageNet-compatible 1,000-image benchmark, SID achieves the highest average attack success rate across nine target models when crafted on any of four source models (IncV3, IncV4, IncResV2, Res50). On normally trained models, SID improves over SIA and BSR by 3.5% and 4.0% on average, respectively. The gains are most pronounced on adversarially trained models (IncV3_ens3, IncV3_ens4, IncResV2_ens), where SID outperforms SIA by 16.8% and BSR by 13.7% on average. Against eight defense methods (Table 4), the combined SID-SI-TIM variant achieves 84.3% average attack success rate, surpassing the best baseline (SSA-TI-DIM) by 4.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SID is compared against five input-transformation baselines (DIM, TIM, SSA, SIA, BSR), all combined with MI-FGSM. Against the two earliest methods, DIM and TIM, SID achieves substantially higher transferability across all target models (e.g., 97.3% vs. 71.5% on IncV4 and 77.3% vs. 31.6% on IncV3_ens3 when crafted on IncV3). Against SSA, SID improves by roughly 9–12 percentage points on ViT-B and 15–20 points on adversarially trained models. Against the two strongest recent baselines, SIA and BSR, SID leads by 3.5% and 4.0% on average over normally trained CNN models, but the gap widens dramatically on adversarially trained models (16.8% over SIA, 13.7% over BSR) and on ViT-B (e.g., 58.1% vs. 46.4% for SIA and 47.0% for BSR when crafted on IncV3). In ensemble settings (Table 3), SID-SI-TIM reaches 98.1% average attack success rate, exceeding SIA-TI-DIM by over 2.2%. Against eight defense methods (Table 4), SID-SI-TIM achieves 84.3% average success rate, outperforming the best baseline SSA-TI-DIM (79.5%) by 4.8%, with the largest margin (11.4%) against the diffusion-based defense DiffPure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MI-FGSM (Dong et al., CVPR 2018) as its gradient optimization backbone, inserting a novel transformation operator T(·) into the iterative loop. It positions itself within the input transformation-based attack family (DIM, TIM, SSA, SIA, BSR) but departs conceptually by arguing that prior methods activate behavioral patterns at only a single spatial position, whereas DNNs exhibit spatial invariance across multiple positions. The key novelty is combining multi-scale downsampling with positional randomization and local image fusion (linear and DCT-based frequency fusion) to simultaneously activate diverse behavioral patterns at different scales and positions within a single surrogate model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that spatial invariance exploitation yields substantially larger gains against adversarially trained models (16.8% over SIA) than against normally trained ones (3.5–4.0%) suggests that the behavioral patterns at non-central positions are particularly informative for breaking through adversarial training defenses. The method is modular and composable, as demonstrated by its integration with Nesterov accelerated gradients, TIM, and DIM, making it a practical building block for future attack pipelines. Open questions include whether the multi-position, multi-scale strategy generalizes to non-classification tasks (segmentation, detection) and whether the specific fusion parameters (k=2, p=0.5, ω=0.5) transfer across datasets or require re-tuning.
