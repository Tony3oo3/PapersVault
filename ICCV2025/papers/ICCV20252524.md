## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting relies on an image-wise loss that computes gradients from a single viewpoint per optimization step, lacking the multi-view constraints essential for accurate 3D appearance and geometry. While NeRF's ray-wise sampling provides multi-view gradients, it discards structural information across distant pixels. The tile-based splatting pipeline in 3DGS offers a natural but underexplored mechanism for integrating multi-view constraints without sacrificing rendering efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252524/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images and an SfM point cloud for Gaussian initialization, but unlike standard 3DGS, it loads the dataset as 16×16 tiles rather than full images. Each training batch consists of N randomly sampled tiles drawn from V different views (default V=5), with the total pixel count matching one full image to ensure fair batch-size comparison. The output is a 3D Gaussian scene representation rendered via tile-based rasterization at real-time speeds (93–135 FPS on RTX 4090).

## Method

RT-Loss replaces the image-wise loss by computing a hybrid objective over a random-tile batch: a per-tile MAE color loss (L_RT-MAE) plus a structural loss (L_RT-SSIM) that averages SSIM across independent non-overlapping tiles from different views, preserving positional relationships within each tile while integrating multi-view supervision. The tile (16×16) is chosen as the minimal loss unit because it aligns with the splatting rasterization pipeline, enabling parallel computation with negligible overhead. A tile-based adaptive densification strategy (Iteration-Count Densification) weights NDC gradients by the ratio of a Gaussian's projected area to the tile area, preventing the increased observation frequency in tile-wise training from suppressing densification. The approach is plug-and-play: only the sampling and loss computation change, while all other 3DGS parameters (learning rates, densification threshold) remain default, and it generalizes to Scaffold-GS and 4D-GS.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF360, the proposed RT-Loss achieves 29.66 PSNR, 0.892 SSIM, and 0.171 LPIPS, outperforming 3D-GS (28.69/0.870/0.182) and Scaffold-GS (28.84/0.848/0.220). On Tanks&Temples, it reaches 25.13 PSNR and 0.192 LPIPS, surpassing 3D-GS (24.36/0.213) and Scaffold-GS (24.81/0.205). On Deep Blending, it attains 30.15 PSNR, 0.911 SSIM, and 0.224 LPIPS. Combining RT-Loss with Scaffold-GS yields the best overall results (29.86 PSNR on Mip-NeRF360, 25.32 on Tanks&Temples, 30.49 on Deep Blending) while using fewer Gaussians and achieving higher rendering FPS (e.g., 93 vs. 78 FPS on Mip-NeRF360-ROOM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3D-GS [19], RT-Loss consistently wins on all three static datasets: +0.97 dB PSNR on Mip-NeRF360, +0.77 dB on Tanks&Temples, and +0.74 dB on Deep Blending, while also improving SSIM and LPIPS across the board. Against Scaffold-GS [31], RT-Loss achieves higher PSNR on Mip-NeRF360 (+0.82 dB) and Tanks&Temples (+0.32 dB); on Deep Blending, PSNR is marginally lower (30.15 vs. 30.21) but SSIM and LPIPS are clearly better (0.911 vs. 0.906, 0.224 vs. 0.254). Against NeRF-based baselines (Mip-NeRF360, Instant-NGP, Plenoxels), the gains are substantial, with PSNR improvements of 0.43–6.04 dB depending on dataset. On the dynamic Neural 3D Video Dataset, RT-Loss applied to 4D-GS [10] improves PSNR from 31.15 to 31.74, reduces LPIPS from 0.049 to 0.043, and increases rendering speed from 30 to 34 FPS while reducing storage from 90 MB to 78 MB. The paper also demonstrates that combining RT-Loss with Scaffold-GS yields the strongest overall results, suggesting the tile-based paradigm is complementary to structural Gaussian representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two established training paradigms: NeRF's ray-wise loss (which provides multi-view gradients but discards structural context) and 3DGS's image-wise loss (which preserves structure but cannot integrate multi-view constraints in a single step). It draws direct conceptual inspiration from S3IM's stochastic SSIM on random rays for NeRF, but adapts the idea to the tile-based rasterization pipeline of Gaussian Splatting, where the 16×16 tile is the natural minimal rendering unit. The tile-based adaptive densification strategy further departs from prior multi-view GS work (e.g., MVS-based initialization or image-batch accumulation) by operating at the tile granularity rather than the image level, and the approach is demonstrated to be composable with Scaffold-GS and 4D-GS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in a drop-in training modification that consistently improves PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across static and dynamic benchmarks while reducing storage and increasing FPS, making it an attractive upgrade for existing 3DGS pipelines. The finding that the convergence gap widens substantially on data-rich scenes (e.g., SMERF with ~10× more views) and that RT-Loss is more robust to poor SfM initialization suggests the image-wise paradigm is a hidden bottleneck that becomes more severe as scene complexity grows. The acknowledged limitation on large texture-less regions and the suggestion that finer multi-view constraint allocation (matching identical content across views) could further improve geometry point to clear next steps. Broader open questions include whether tile-wise training generalizes to other splatting variants (e.g., 2DGS, hexplane-based methods) and whether the approach interacts favorably with learned or generalizable Gaussian representations.
