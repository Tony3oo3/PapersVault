## Problem

Existing co-speech gesture generation methods model body regions (hands, upper body, legs) as independent motion features, failing to capture the spatial interactions between them and producing disjointed, unnatural full-body movements. Additionally, their diffusion-based or autoregressive pipelines require dozens of inference steps, making real-time deployment impractical for digital avatars and embodied agents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252598/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes speech audio (low-level onset features) and a text transcript (BERT-encoded semantics) as input, fusing them via cross-attention where gesture features serve as queries and speech features as keys/values. The output is a full-body 3D SMPL gesture sequence represented as discrete RVQ tokens across three body regions (hands, upper body, lower body), with an optional facial expression component. Inference runs at 0.039 s per sentence on a single A100 GPU using 8 flow-matching steps, and the generated 3D poses can be projected to 2D keypoints for downstream avatar animation.

## Method

GestureLSM is a flow-matching-based generator that operates in a compact quantized gesture latent space (RVQ tokens for each body region) rather than continuous pose space. A transformer decoder applies spatial attention (across body regions within a single frame) followed by temporal attention (along the time axis per region) to explicitly model inter-region coherence and motion dynamics, with dedicated positional encodings for each axis. The key innovation is the Latent Shortcut Model: instead of standard rectified-flow velocity prediction, the network predicts a shortcut f_θ(a, x_t, t, d) that directly jumps from state x_t to x_{t+d}, trained with a self-consistency constraint that decomposes larger steps into smaller ones. To overcome the suboptimal quality of vanilla flow matching in this domain, the authors introduce a left-skewed beta distribution (α=2, β=1.2) for timestep sampling during training, which counteracts the observed degradation in velocity prediction as t→1. This combination of spatial-temporal attention, latent shortcut learning, and beta-schedule training enables the model to outperform diffusion baselines while achieving 1–8 step inference.

## Results

On BEAT2, GestureLSM achieves the best Fréchet Gesture Distance (FGD) of 4.088 (×10⁻¹), outperforming the previous best SynTalker (4.687) by 12.8%. It simultaneously achieves the fastest inference speed at 0.039 s/sentence (AIST), which is 75× faster than SynTalker (2.942 s) and 2.9× faster than the next-fastest DiffSHEG (0.112 s). The Beat Constancy (BC) of 0.714 is closest to the ground-truth value of 0.703, indicating superior speech-gesture synchronization. With the optional face module, GestureLSM(+Face) reports a facial vertex MSE of 1.021 (×10⁻⁷), substantially lower than EMAGE (7.680) and TalkShow (7.791).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GestureLSM achieves the lowest FGD (4.088) among all nine baselines, beating the closest competitor SynTalker (4.687) by 0.60 and ProbTalk (5.040) by 0.95, confirming that explicit spatial-temporal body-region modeling yields more coherent full-body gestures. On inference speed, GestureLSM (0.039 s/sentence) is dramatically faster than every baseline: 75× faster than SynTalker (2.942 s), 17× faster than CaMN (0.675 s), and 2.9× faster than DiffSHEG (0.112 s), making it the only method combining top quality with real-time speed. For speech-gesture synchronization (BC, where ground truth is 0.703), GestureLSM (0.714) is closest to the reference, whereas MambaTalk (0.781) and EMAGE (0.772) over-estimate beat constancy. On diversity, GestureLSM (13.24) is competitive with TalkShow (13.47) and ProbTalk (13.27), while the +Face variant reaches the highest diversity (13.76). For facial expression accuracy, GestureLSM(+Face) reports an MSE of 1.021, roughly 7.5× lower than EMAGE (7.680) and TalkShow (7.791), indicating near-ground-truth facial fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the latent shortcut model framework from Frans et al. (2024) and the rectified flow ODE formulation from Liu et al. (2022) to the co-speech gesture generation domain, operating in a quantized (RVQ) gesture latent space rather than pixel space. It departs from prior gesture generators (EMAGE, TalkShow, DiffSHEG, MambaTalk, SynTalker) that treat body regions as independent motion features by introducing explicit spatial-temporal attention to model inter-region interactions within a unified transformer. The beta-distribution time-sampling strategy is a novel training-time modification motivated by an observed denoising pattern specific to audio-conditioned gesture generation, contrasting with the middle-emphasized schedules used in image-domain flow matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× reduction in inference time (0.039s vs. 0.112–2.942s per frame) makes high-quality full-body gesture generation viable for real-time digital avatars and embodied agents, a practical bottleneck that prior diffusion- and autoregressive-based methods could not overcome. The finding that left-skewed (t→1-emphasized) time sampling outperforms uniform or middle-emphasized schedules in the gesture domain—opposite to the 'lost in the middle' pattern observed in image generation—suggests domain-specific flow-matching training strategies may be broadly applicable. Open questions include whether the spatial-temporal attention design generalizes to longer sequences or more complex multi-person interactions, and whether the single-dataset (BEAT2) evaluation limits confidence in the reported gains.
