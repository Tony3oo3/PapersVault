## Problem

Novel space-time view synthesis from a monocular video is highly ill-posed because frame-based methods lack inter-frame motion cues and must rely on simplistic linear motion assumptions, which fail on complex motions like deformation or nonlinear trajectories. Event cameras capture these inter-frame cues at high temporal resolution, but their inherent noise and sparsity prevent existing event-based methods (e.g., DE-NeRF) from accurately estimating 3D motion trajectories, leaving a gap in handling high-speed or complex dynamic scenes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB video (e.g., 12 frames sampled from 45 timestamps on the NVIDIA dataset) and the corresponding asynchronous event streams from a hybrid camera system, along with camera poses and monocular depth estimates (MiDaS). It produces rendered novel space-time views (at unobserved time points and viewpoints), novel views at observed times, and explicit 3D scene flow trajectories for dynamic objects. On the NVIDIA dataset, events are synthesized from video; on HS-ERGB and HC-ERGB, both frames and events are captured by a DAVIS346C sensor.

## Method

E-NeMF represents the dynamic scene with two MLPs (static background and dynamic foreground) and introduces three key mechanisms to handle event noise and sparsity. First, a Parametric Motion Representation models each spatial point's trajectory as a Bezier curve controlled by MLP-predicted control points, holistically fitting noisy events across the temporal domain and enforcing bidirectional curve consistency. Second, a Flow Prediction Module decomposes optical flow into ego-motion (computed from camera poses and depth) and non-rigid residual flow (extracted by a pretrained DCEIFlow network from event voxel grids), converting sparse events into dense flow that supervises the scene flow via reprojection loss. Third, Temporal-Neighbor Alignment warps density, color, and dynamic-region blending weights from neighboring time steps back to the anchor time, enforcing appearance and motion consistency across temporal domains. The full model is optimized with a combined loss including photometric, event, reprojection, curve-consistency, alignment, and regularization terms.

## Results

On the NVIDIA Dynamic Scene Dataset, E-NeMF achieves 26.51 PSNR / 0.8542 SSIM / 0.087 LPIPS for novel view synthesis and 28.79 PSNR / 0.8907 SSIM / 0.100 LPIPS for novel space-time view synthesis, outperforming all frame-based and event-based baselines on PSNR and SSIM. The closest competitor, NSFF, reaches 25.37 PSNR (novel view) and 27.50 PSNR (novel space-time), while the prior event-based method DE-NeRF lags significantly at 18.51 and 22.67 PSNR respectively. E-NeMF also achieves the best LPIPS (0.087) on novel view synthesis, tying with NSFF, though NSFF retains a slight LPIPS edge (0.087 vs. 0.100) on novel space-time synthesis.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E-NeMF outperforms all eight baselines on PSNR and SSIM for both novel view and novel space-time view synthesis. Against the strongest frame-based competitor NSFF, E-NeMF gains +1.14 PSNR (26.51 vs. 25.37) and +0.0085 SSIM on novel views, and +1.29 PSNR (28.79 vs. 27.50) and +0.0106 SSIM on novel space-time views, though NSFF retains a marginal LPIPS advantage on space-time synthesis (0.087 vs. 0.100). The prior event-based method DE-NeRF is substantially outperformed, with E-NeMF achieving +8.00 PSNR on novel views and +6.12 PSNR on novel space-time views, confirming that the parametric motion representation and flow prediction module effectively mitigate event noise and sparsity. Deformation-based methods (Nerfies, HyperNeRF) and the 3DGS-based D3DGS trail by 10+ PSNR on novel space-time synthesis, reflecting their inability to model complex non-linear motion without inter-frame cues. DVS, while competitive on novel views (25.09 PSNR), degrades sharply on novel space-time views (20.85 PSNR), highlighting the limitation of its motion model for unseen timestamps.

## Relation to Prior Work

E-NeMF builds directly on the deformation-based NeRF framework of DVS [14] and the event-guided dynamic NeRF of DE-NeRF [33], but departs from both in how motion is modeled. Frame-based methods (NeRFies, HyperNeRF, NSFF, DVS, Deformable 3DGS) rely on linear or latent-space interpolation assumptions that break down under complex motion, while DE-NeRF uses raw event streams without addressing their inherent noise and sparsity. E-NeMF introduces a Bezier-curve parametric motion representation to holistically fit noisy events across time, and a Flow Prediction Module (leveraging DCEIFlow) to convert sparse events into dense optical flow for spatial supervision, effectively addressing both failure modes simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that event cameras can meaningfully improve novel space-time view synthesis for complex motion (deformation, rotation, high-speed movement) where frame-based methods fundamentally fail due to missing inter-frame cues. The practical impact is significant for applications like VR, slow-motion generation, and autonomous driving where complex motion is common. However, limitations remain: event noise still introduces color artifacts, extremely high-frequency motions (e.g., tuning fork vibrations) exceed the capacity of low-degree Bezier curves, and the method requires a hybrid event+RGB camera system. Open questions include whether the parametric motion representation can be made more adaptive in degree, whether real-time rendering is feasible, and how the approach generalizes to longer sequences or more complex multi-object scenes.
