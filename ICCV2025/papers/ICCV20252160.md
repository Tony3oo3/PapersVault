## Problem

Existing frequency-based deepfake video detectors apply 2D spatial Fourier transforms per frame and stack the resulting spectra across time, which captures only changes in spatial artifacts but ignores pixel-wise temporal variations. This makes them blind to the fine temporal flickering and unnatural per-pixel movements that deepfake synthesis models frequently introduce, especially in localized regions such as the eyes and mouth.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252160/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a facial video clip of 32 consecutive frames (C×32×H×W), preprocessed by median-filter subtraction and grayscale conversion before a 1D FFT is applied along the time axis at every pixel, yielding a T/2 × H × W magnitude spectrum. The APM additionally proposes five 44×44 spatial patches for part-based analysis. The output is a binary real/fake video-level classification score.

## Method

The core mechanism is a per-pixel 1D Fourier transform along the temporal axis, producing a pixel-wise temporal frequency spectrum that is fed into a 2D ResNet-50 (with T/2 input channels) to extract global frequency features. An Attention Proposal Module (APM), trained end-to-end without localization labels, regresses five center coordinates from multi-level ResNet features and the raw frequency map, cropping 44×44 patches that are re-processed by the same ResNet for part-based features. A Joint Transformer Module then fuses these frequency features with spatio-temporal context from a frozen 3D SlowFast network: a Feature Blender injects 1×1-convolved frequency features into each 3D ResNet block, and a Spatial Transformer Encoder (STE) and Temporal Transformer Encoder (TTE) separately integrate part-based and global frequency representations with the blended features before a final classifier. Training uses auxiliary binary classifiers on global, part-based, and STE features, with the final-classifier loss weight λ ramped from 0 to 1 after 4 epochs to let the APM and STE stabilize before joint optimization.

## Results

Trained solely on FaceForensics++, the method achieves 92.2% average video-level AUC across five unseen datasets (CDF, DFDC, FSh, DFo, DFD), outperforming the strongest baselines AltFreezing (91.1%) and StyleFlow (90.8%). On individual datasets, it reaches 89.7% on CDF, 75.2% on DFDC, 99.3% on FSh, 99.4% on DFo, and 97.3% on DFD. In cross-synthesis experiments (Table 5), it achieves 99.9% AUC on DF and 99.8% on FS when trained on the other three FF++ methods, surpassing AltFreezing (99.8%/99.7%) and FTCN (99.8%/99.3%). The method also demonstrates superior robustness to perturbations (saturation, blur, resizing) and achieves the best EER on DFDC (0.2919) and FSh (0.0286) among compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 19 baselines on the average AUC across unseen datasets (92.2% vs. 91.1% for AltFreezing, 90.8% for StyleFlow, and 90.6% for FTCN). Among image-based detectors, it surpasses the strongest RGB-based method Face X-ray (84.0% avg) by 8.2 points and the best spatial-frequency method SFDG by a wide margin, confirming that pixel-wise temporal frequency captures artifacts invisible to spatial analysis. Among video-based RGB methods, it beats FTCN by 1.6 points on average and RealForensics by 3.4 points, with the largest gap on DFD (97.3% vs. 94.4% and 82.2% respectively). Against stacked spatial-frequency video methods (MGL, FCAN, Two-branch), it achieves substantially higher CDF AUC (89.7% vs. 88.8%, 83.5%, 76.7%). In cross-synthesis (Table 5), it edges out AltFreezing on DF (99.9 vs. 99.8) and FS (99.8 vs. 99.7) but trails slightly on F2F (97.1 vs. 98.6). In cross-deepfake-type evaluation (Table 4), it dominates on the hardest transfer (DF→F2F: 75.0% vs. 63.0% for StyleFlow, 59.2% for FTCN) and (DF→NT: 82.7% vs. 69.2% and 66.3%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant spatial-frequency paradigm in deepfake detection (HFF, F3Net, SFDG, and stacked-spatial-frequency methods like FCAN and MGL) by shifting the Fourier analysis from the spatial plane to the temporal axis, applying a 1D FFT per pixel across frames. It also builds on the temporal-consistency line of work (CNN-GRU, FTCN, LipForensics) but operates in frequency space rather than raw RGB, and introduces an Attention Proposal Module inspired by recurrent attention networks to localize where temporal artifacts concentrate. The joint transformer module for fusing temporal-frequency features with 3D-CNN spatio-temporal context is a new architectural combination not seen in prior deepfake detectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete gap—temporal artifacts that spatial-frequency stacking methods structurally cannot capture—and demonstrates that pixel-wise temporal frequency is a complementary and sometimes superior signal for cross-domain and cross-synthesis generalization. The APM's ability to discover artifact-rich regions (nose, mouth) end-to-end without localization labels is a practical contribution for interpretable detection. A notable limitation is the vulnerability to strong video compression, which distorts the very temporal signal the method relies on; this remains an open challenge. Broader implications include whether temporal-frequency features can be combined with other forensic cues (audio, metadata) for multi-modal deepfake detection.
