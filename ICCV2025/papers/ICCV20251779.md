## Problem

Existing infrared-visible image fusion methods suffer from insufficient cross-modal feature interaction and incomplete multimodal fusion, often relying on self-attention restricted to local windows or quadratic-complexity global attention that does not exploit the structural priors inherent in the source images themselves. This paper identifies that infrared images possess an intrinsic 'attention property'—their heat distribution naturally encodes region importance in a manner structurally analogous to Grad-CAM visualizations—yet no prior fusion method has directly leveraged this physical prior to guide cross-domain information exchange.

## Input / Output

The method takes a registered infrared-visible image pair as input and produces a single fused grayscale image. Unusually, the raw source images (after a lightweight CBSM channel-boosting and spatial-mapping step) are used directly as attention queries rather than being passed through deep feature extractors first, while the other modality's intermediate features (48-channel, produced by a Restormer Block) serve as keys and values. Training uses 128×128 random crops from MSRS augmented with 200 RoadScene pairs; evaluation is performed at full resolution on MSRS, M³FD (resized to half), and TNO without fine-tuning.

## Method

The core mechanism is a source-image-prior-guided cross-attention: I-SCA treats the CBSM-processed infrared image (and its negative transform) as the Query, while the visible branch's Restormer intermediate features are projected into Key and Value via depthwise-separable convolutions, enabling heat-distribution-prior-guided global cross-modal interaction with linear complexity. V-SCA symmetrically extends this to the visible modality, using visible image priors (edges, textures, contrast) as queries against infrared features. CBSM (two 3×3 convolutions + SE + activation) boosts the single-channel source image to 48 channels and maps its feature space to reduce the gap with deep intermediate features while suppressing redundant or misleading content. The overall pipeline is a serial SE-ResNet → Restormer → I-SCA/V-SCA structure per branch, with four source-oriented feature maps concatenated and refined by a final SE-ResNet Block. Training uses a combined intensity loss (L1 against element-wise max of inputs) and gradient loss (Sobel for edges + Laplacian for noise suppression), optimized with Adam over 60 epochs.

## Results

On the MSRS test set, the proposed method achieves the best score across all seven metrics: VIF 1.061, SCD 1.700, MI 5.111, Q_abf 0.715, SSIM 0.981, MS-SSIM 0.975, and FMI 0.933. On the unseen M³FD dataset (no fine-tuning), it leads in VIF (0.759), Q_abf (0.654), SSIM (0.964), MS-SSIM (0.923), and FMI (0.883), trailing only on MI (3.771 vs. SwinFusion's 4.097). On TNO, it secures four first-place positions (VIF, MI, Q_abf, FMI) and two runner-up spots (SCD, SSIM). The method also achieves the highest inference speed at 132 FPS on MSRS, roughly 3.3× faster than CDDFuse (39.7 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The strongest baseline is CDDFuse (CVPR'23), which also uses a Restormer-based hybrid architecture; the proposed method outperforms it on all seven MSRS metrics (e.g., VIF 1.061 vs. 1.051, SSIM 0.981 vs. 0.967) and on five of seven M³FD metrics, though it trails on MI (3.771 vs. 3.999). SwinFusion (JAS'22), the best SwinTransformer-based method, is surpassed on MSRS across all metrics and on M³FD for VIF, Q_abf, SSIM, MS-SSIM, and FMI, but retains an edge on MI (4.097 vs. 3.771) and SSIM on TNO (0.937 vs. 0.932). DATFuse (TCSVT'23) shows a clear gap, with the proposed method leading by 0.155 in VIF and 0.026 in SSIM on MSRS. Among CNN-based baselines, MetaFusion (CVPR'23) is the closest, yet still trails by 0.335 in VIF and 0.069 in SSIM on MSRS; LRRNet, CoCoNet, and TUFusion are substantially weaker, with TUFusion scoring only 0.569 VIF and 0.229 Q_abf on MSRS. Notably, the proposed method also achieves 132 FPS on MSRS versus 39.7 FPS for CDDFuse and 0.624 FPS for SwinFusion, reflecting the linear-complexity advantage of its dconv-based cross-attention design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Restormer's linear-complexity attention (MDTA + GDFN) as its architectural backbone, but departs from prior transformer-based fusion methods (ViT, SwinFusion, CDDFuse) by using the raw source image itself—rather than learned intermediate features—as the query in cross-modal attention. The conceptual departure is the observation that infrared images structurally resemble Grad-CAM saliency maps, which the authors formalize as an inherent 'attention property' that can be exploited without training. This positions the work between CNN-based fusion (SE-ResNet for feature extraction) and attention-based fusion (I-SCA/V-SCA for cross-modal interaction), while explicitly rejecting the local-window restriction of SwinTransformer in favor of true global modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'source image as attention prior' paradigm offers a physically grounded alternative to learned attention in multimodal fusion, potentially reducing the need for large training sets to discover saliency patterns. The 3× efficiency gain over CDDFuse (132 vs ~40 FPS) makes the method practical for real-time applications. However, the ablation (Table 3) reveals that replacing I/V-SCA with plain Restormer blocks yields nearly identical performance, raising questions about how much the cross-attention mechanism itself contributes versus the overall architecture. Open questions include whether this source-prior-guided attention generalizes to other fusion tasks (e.g., medical, remote sensing) and whether the Grad-CAM analogy holds for visible images, which lack the explicit physical saliency of infrared.
