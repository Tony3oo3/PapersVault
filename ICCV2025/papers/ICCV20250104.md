## Problem

Existing image fusion methods are task-specific (a VIF model cannot handle MEF or MFF), fail on degraded real-world inputs, operate in pixel space where Transformer attention is prohibitively expensive, and lack user-interaction capabilities. No prior framework unifies multi-task fusion, multi-degradation restoration, and language-guided control within a single efficient model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250104/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a pair of degraded images (from VIF, MEF, or MFF tasks, with 1–3 compositional degradations such as noise, blur, rain, haze, or snow) and a natural-language prompt (e.g., 'please remove the noise') as input. Images are encoded to 16-channel latents at 1/8 spatial resolution via Cosmos VAE, and the prompt is encoded by FlanT5; the output is a single clean fused image decoded back to pixel space. Two inference modes are provided: a deterministic regression variant (~0.1 s per 1024×1024 image) for mild degradations and a flow-matching variant (<0.6 s, 5 midpoint steps with velocity reuse) for severe cases.

## Method

MMAIF builds a modified Diffusion Transformer (DiT) in latent space that jointly performs restoration and fusion, conditioned on the degraded latent pair and a FlanT5 prompt embedding via LoRA-decomposed AdaLN. Key architectural modifications include: (1) Mixture-of-Experts GLU replacing the FFN (4 routed experts + 1 shared, top-2 routing with load-balancing loss) to expand capacity without proportional FLOPs; (2) 2D rotary positional embedding plus per-block learnable absolute PE for resolution extrapolation; (3) attention value residual (learnable Lerp between current and previous-layer values) to mitigate vanishing gradients; and (4) NAFNet blocks before each attention layer to inject inductive bias for blur restoration. Training uses either a direct MSE regression loss with an auxiliary pixel-space fusion loss, or a flow-matching velocity-prediction loss; the latter is sampled at inference with a custom midpoint SDE sampler that reuses the first-half velocity to cut NFEs from 100 to 5. A composable degradation pipeline (n∈{1,3} random degradations per pair, modality-aware for VIF) and ChatGPT-generated prompts produce 157,500 training pairs spanning all three fusion tasks.

## Results

On the LLVIP VIF dataset, MMAIF (Flow Matching) achieves the best VIF score of 0.604, outperforming the strongest restoration+fusion baseline (ERN+SwinFusion, 0.571) and the all-in-one TextIF (0.456) by 5.8% and 32.5% respectively. On M3FD and MSRS, MMAIF(FM) leads in BRISQUE (35.70 and 32.08 vs. 42.40 and 43.68 for the best baselines) and MUSIQ (55.42 and 40.41), while DRMF retains a slight edge on VIF for M3FD (0.506 vs. 0.464). For MEF (SICE) and MFF (RealMFF), MMAIF(FM) achieves the lowest Q_cv (268.07 and 105.97) and highest MUSIQ (68.07 and 52.77) among all methods. Inference latency for the regression variant is 0.108 s/image at 1024×1024, roughly 53× faster than the SwinIR+EMMA pipeline (5.78 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMAIF(FM) outperforms all eight restoration+fusion pipelines (ERN+U2Fusion, DeFuse, SwinFusion, CDDFuse, DDFM, TC-MoA, TTD, EMMA) on VIF, BRISQUE, MUSIQ, and CLIPIQA for LLVIP, with the largest margin over ERN+U2Fusion (VIF 0.604 vs. 0.429) and ERN+DDFM (BRISQUE 27.54 vs. 46.04). Against the two all-in-one baselines, MMAIF(FM) beats TextIF by 32.5% in VIF (0.604 vs. 0.456) and DRMF by 78.2% in VIF (0.604 vs. 0.339) on LLVIP, though DRMF retains a narrow VIF edge on M3FD (0.506 vs. 0.464) while MMAIF still leads in perceptual metrics (MUSIQ 55.42 vs. 48.90). On MEF (SICE), MMAIF(FM) achieves the best Q_cv (268.07 vs. 395.88 for ERN+PSLPT) and MUSIQ (68.07 vs. 58.59), and on MFF (RealMFF) it leads in all five metrics including Q^A/BF (0.503 vs. 0.490 for ERN+PSLPT). The regression variant (MMAIF Reg) is competitive under mild degradation but consistently trails the flow-matching variant by 4–8% in VIF, confirming the generative model's advantage for severe degradations. In latency, MMAIF(Reg) at 0.108 s/image is 53× faster than SwinIR+EMMA (5.78 s) and 180× faster than DA-CLIP+EMMA (19.45 s), while MMAIF(FM) with 5-step midpoint sampling stays under 0.6 s.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DiT architecture (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) and flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022), adapting both to the image fusion domain by operating in a Cosmos VAE latent space and conditioning on degraded image pairs plus text prompts. It departs from prior degradation-aware fusion work (TextIF, DRMF) by unifying multiple fusion tasks (VIF, MEF, MFF) and multiple degradation types into a single model, rather than handling one task or a limited set of degradations. Architecturally, it introduces MoE GLU, 2D RoPE, value-residual attention, and NAFNet inductive-bias blocks as principled modifications to the DiT backbone, drawing on LLM design choices (DeepSeek V3-style MoE, RoPE) that had not previously been applied to low-level vision fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical path toward a single deployable model for degraded multi-task image fusion, reducing inference latency from ~6 s (restoration+fusion pipelines) to ~0.1 s (regression) or <0.6 s (flow matching) at 1024×1024, which is significant for real-time applications. Open questions include whether the simulated degradation pipeline generalizes to truly unseen real-world degradations, whether the MoE capacity strategy scales to additional fusion tasks or higher resolutions, and whether the language-guided interaction provides meaningful user control beyond the tested prompt set. The reliance on pre-trained fusion networks (SwinFusion, DeFuse) to generate ground truth for training also raises questions about error propagation and the ceiling of the approach.
