## Problem

Decoding video from EEG signals is hindered by three intertwined challenges: the nonstationarity and noise of raw EEG, the scarcity of annotated EEG-video pairs, and the variability in channel montages (number and scalp position) across datasets. Existing pre-training methods either assume a fixed montage, pad channels by replication, or ignore spatial relations entirely, making it impossible to leverage the large amounts of unlabeled EEG data available in the wild. Meanwhile, fMRI-based video decoding suffers from low temporal resolution that cannot capture rapid brain dynamics.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 2-second multi-channel EEG segment (arbitrary number of channels at arbitrary scalp positions per the international 10-10 system) and produces a 2-second video clip at 512×288 resolution and 3 FPS. EEG channels are segmented into non-overlapping patches via a sliding window (default 500 ms), and the framework explicitly handles heterogeneous montages without any channel padding or replication, so a 62-channel SEED recording and a 32-channel FACED recording can be processed by the same encoder.

## Method

EEGMirror is a three-stage framework. In stage 1, a Transformer-based brain encoder is pre-trained via masked autoencoding: EEG patches are embedded by a 1-D convolutional patch embedder, quantized into discrete tokens via a VQ-VAE-style neural codebook (trained to reconstruct Fourier amplitudes), and the encoder is trained to predict codebook indices under three masking strategies (random, channel, and temporal). The key architectural novelty is Montage-Agnostic Position Embeddings (MAPE), which encodes each channel's spatial location as sinusoidal/cosine functions of its x-y proportion on the skull plus a relative temporal embedding, enabling a single encoder to process any montage without learnable per-channel parameters. In stage 2, the pre-trained encoder is fine-tuned with multimodal contrastive learning: an MLP decoder aligns EEG with CLIP text embeddings (SoftCLIP + MSE loss) for semantic information, while a Seq2Seq Transformer decoder predicts VAE latent frames for low-level perceptual alignment. In stage 3, a Tune-A-Video-style inflated Stable Diffusion model (with sparse temporal attention) is co-trained with the alignment modules to generate the final video conditioned on both the predicted text embedding and frame-level latent representations.

## Results

On the SEED-DV benchmark, EEGMirror achieves 82.1% video-level 2-way semantic accuracy and 0.261 SSIM, outperforming the previous EEG-based SOTA EEG2Video (79.8% and 0.256) and the fMRI-based MinD-Video (78.8% and 0.185). At the 40-way video-level semantic metric, EEGMirror reaches 17.2% versus 15.9% (EEG2Video) and 15.1% (MinD-Video). Frame-level 2-way semantic accuracy is 78.6% for EEGMirror compared to 77.4% (EEG2Video) and 75.6% (MinD-Video). The SSIM gap over MinD-Video is particularly large (0.261 vs. 0.185), reflecting the advantage of EEG's high temporal resolution for capturing dynamic visual content.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EEGMirror outperforms both baselines across all five metrics. Against EEG2Video (the closest EEG-based competitor), EEGMirror gains +2.3 points on video 2-way semantic accuracy (82.1% vs. 79.8%), +1.3 points on video 40-way (17.2% vs. 15.9%), +1.2 points on frame 2-way (78.6% vs. 77.4%), +1.6 points on frame 40-way (15.4% vs. 13.8%), and +0.005 SSIM (0.261 vs. 0.256). Against MinD-Video (an fMRI-based method), the advantage is much larger, especially on pixel-level SSIM where EEGMirror scores 0.261 versus 0.185, a 41% relative improvement, attributed to EEG's superior temporal resolution for capturing rapid visual dynamics. The paper attributes the gains over EEG2Video primarily to the montage-agnostic pre-training on heterogeneous EEG datasets (SEED + FACED), which the ablation confirms: removing pre-training drops SSIM to 0.223 and video 2-way to 78.5%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the EEG-to-video reconstruction task first established by EEG2Video (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2024) by addressing its core limitation: all modules were trained subject-dependently from scratch, preventing effective feature extraction. The methodological lineage draws from VQ-VAE-style neural quantization for handling EEG nonstationarity, masked autoencoder pre-training for self-supervised representation learning, and the Tune-A-Video network inflation technique for video generation. The key conceptual departure is the Montage-Agnostic Position Embedding (MAPE), which replaces the fixed or learnable channel embeddings used in prior EEG pre-training works (LaBraM, EEGPT, DreamDiffusion) with a continuous spatial coordinate system based on the international 10-10 system, enabling a single encoder to process heterogeneous EEG montages without padding or channel-specific parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The montage-agnostic pre-training framework directly addresses the practical bottleneck in brain decoding: the extreme scarcity of annotated EEG-video pairs. By demonstrating that pre-training on emotion EEG datasets (SEED, FACED) with different channel configurations transfers effectively to the video decoding task, the paper opens a path toward leveraging the much larger pool of in-the-wild EEG data. However, the empirical gains over EEG2Video are modest (82.1% vs 79.8% video 2-way accuracy; 0.261 vs 0.256 SSIM), evaluation is limited to a single dataset (SEED-DV) at low resolution (512×288, 3 FPS, 2-second clips), and the approach still relies on a frozen CLIP/BLIP text-semantic bottleneck that may constrain fidelity. Open questions include whether MAPE scales to higher-resolution, longer-duration video reconstruction and whether the quantization bottleneck introduces information loss that limits ceiling performance.
