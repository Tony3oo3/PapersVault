## Problem

Existing handwritten text generation methods focus on isolated words, which cannot capture inter-word style relationships such as vertical alignment and horizontal spacing that define a coherent text line. The two prior text-line GAN methods (TS-GAN, CSA-GAN) suffer from content-style interference during joint optimization and struggle to maintain character-level accuracy across long lines averaging ~42 characters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251058/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a Unicode text string (arbitrary length, including letters, digits, punctuation) and a single style reference text-line image (one-shot) as input, and produces a full handwritten text-line image at 64-pixel height with variable width (padded or resized to 64×1024). The style reference is a raw text-line sample from an unseen writer, and the generated output must simultaneously match that writer's calligraphic style while faithfully rendering the specified content.

## Method

DiffBrush is a conditional diffusion model (U-Net denoiser with DDIM sampling) whose condition vector is built by fusing content features (from a ResNet18 + Transformer encoder) with two style features extracted via a content-decoupled style module. The key style-learning insight is directional masking: column-wise masking on spatial style features preserves vertical alignment while erasing horizontal content, and row-wise masking preserves horizontal spacing while erasing vertical content; each masked feature is trained with a Proxy-NCA loss that clusters same-writer features and separates different-writer features. For content accuracy, a multi-scale adversarial loss combines a line-level discriminator (3D CNN over sliced segments of the generated image concatenated with a style-free guidance image, enforcing global character order) and a word-level discriminator (ASTER-inspired CNN-LSTM attention to locate and crop individual words, enforcing local structural correctness). The total generator objective is L_diff + L_style + λ·L_content with λ=0.05, and classifier-free guidance (scale 0.2) is used at inference.

## Results

On IAM, DiffBrush achieves HWD 1.41 (18.02% lower than the second-best DiffusionPen at 1.72), DCER 8.59, and DWER 28.60, substantially outperforming all baselines in both style fidelity and content accuracy. On CVL, it further reduces HWD to 1.06 (19.69% improvement over DiffusionPen's 1.32) and DCER to 20.92. DiffBrush also attains the best FID (8.69 on IAM, 7.57 on CVL), IS (1.85, 1.70), and GS (2.35×10⁻³, 2.96×10⁻²) among all compared methods, confirming superior visual quality in style-agnostic generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffBrush wins across all six metrics (HWD, D_CER, D_WER, FID, IS, GS) on both IAM and CVL against all five baselines. Against the closest competitor One-DM [7] (also one-shot, diffusion-based), DiffBrush reduces HWD by 21.7% on IAM (1.80→1.41) and 28.0% on CVL (1.47→1.06), while cutting D_CER from 20.91 to 8.59 and D_WER from 54.27 to 28.60 on IAM. Against DiffusionPen [40] (few-shot, diffusion-based), the content accuracy gap is dramatic: D_CER drops from 54.75 to 8.59 on IAM and from 55.94 to 20.92 on CVL, attributed to DiffusionPen's lack of content supervision. Against GAN-based text-line methods TS-GAN [9] and CSA-GAN [23], DiffBrush improves HWD by 33.2% and 37.3% on IAM respectively, and achieves far lower D_CER (8.59 vs. 44.20 and 42.27), demonstrating that the content-decoupled style learning and multi-scale discriminators overcome the style-content interference inherent in GAN approaches. Against VATr [44] (word-level, few-shot), DiffBrush achieves better HWD (1.41 vs. 1.87) and dramatically better content metrics (D_CER 8.59 vs. 28.80), highlighting the advantage of direct text-line generation over word-level assembly.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffBrush extends the line of GAN-based text-line generation (TS-GAN, CSA-GAN) by replacing the generative backbone with a conditional diffusion model, while also drawing on recent diffusion-based word-level methods (One-DM, DiffusionPen) for style and content conditioning. The key conceptual departure is the content-decoupled style learning strategy: rather than jointly optimizing style and content from a single output (as in prior GAN approaches), the paper uses directional spatial masking (column-wise for vertical alignment, row-wise for horizontal spacing) to selectively disrupt content while preserving style patterns, combined with Proxy-NCA losses for writer-level style clustering. The multi-scale content discriminator (3D CNN at line level, attention-based at word level) departs from prior single-scale recognition losses by providing hierarchical content supervision without interfering with style learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first diffusion model applied to full text-line generation, addressing the practical limitation that word-level methods cannot capture inter-word relationships (vertical alignment, spacing) that define realistic handwriting. The content-decoupled masking strategy offers a generalizable principle for disentangling style from content in any spatially-structured generation task. Limitations include reliance on a single style reference, evaluation limited to English and Chinese scripts, and the need for a trained OCR system to measure content accuracy (D_CER/D_WER), which introduces an indirect evaluation dependency. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-line paragraphs, mixed-script documents, or real-time interactive generation.
