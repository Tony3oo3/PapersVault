## Problem

Training vision-language-action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation requires large-scale, user-specific indoor data that exposes sensitive personal information (preferences, health conditions, home layouts), yet no privacy-preserving federated framework exists for this multi-modal, task-heterogeneous setting. Existing federated learning methods like FedAvg naively average models across clients, ignoring the fundamental task distinctions and multi-modal complexity inherent to VLA learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251584/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives an RGB observation (128×128 from three fixed cameras in simulation; 1280×720 from a RealSense D435i at ~30 Hz in real-world), a natural-language task instruction, and robot proprioception, and produces a predicted action sequence optimized with Huber loss. The IOSP module preprocesses the raw image by decomposing it into object-level token groups (target, surrounding, background) via YOLOv8 detection and CLIP-based instruction alignment before feeding the structured tokens into the transformer trunk.

## Method

FedVLA introduces three coupled mechanisms for privacy-preserving VLA training. First, Instruction-Oriented Scene-Parsing (IOSP) uses NER to extract target objects from the instruction, YOLOv8 to detect foreground/background objects, and CLIP cosine similarity to assign image tokens into three groups, each refined through a local MoE block. Second, Dual Gating Mixture-of-Experts (DGMoE) replaces fixed top-k expert selection with a bidirectional process: a token-side gate (soft router with cross-layer residual) scores expert relevance, while a self-aware expert-side gate applies a learnable threshold to accept or reject tokens, yielding sparse, task-adaptive activation (average ~1.22 experts per token vs. a fixed k). Third, Expert-Driven Aggregation (EDA) computes per-layer cosine similarity between clients' expert activation count vectors and uses these similarities as aggregation weights, so that only the trunk module is merged at the server while stem and head remain client-specific, preserving task personalization while transferring shared expert knowledge.

## Results

In simulation (Meta-World, 4 tasks), FedVLA achieves a 63.3% average success rate, closely approaching the 65.0% of centralized training and substantially outperforming FedAvg (51.7%). In real-world experiments (UR3 arm, 4 household tasks), FedVLA reaches 63.3% average success, nearly matching centralized training (63.4%) while FedAvg drops to 53.3%. Notably, FedVLA surpasses centralized on individual tasks: Close Drawer in simulation (80.0% vs. 73.3%) and Clean Up in real-world (53.3% vs. 46.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares FedVLA against two baselines: centralized training (all data pooled on one server, serving as a performance upper bound) and FedAvg [22] (standard federated averaging of local model updates). Against FedAvg, FedVLA wins decisively in both settings: 63.3% vs. 51.7% average success in simulation (a 11.6-point gap) and 63.3% vs. 53.3% in real-world (a 10.0-point gap), with the largest per-task margins on Open Drawer (80.0% vs. 60.0%) and Trash Collection (46.7% vs. 40.0%). Against centralized training, FedVLA nearly ties in real-world (63.3% vs. 63.4%) and comes within 1.7 points in simulation (63.3% vs. 65.0%), while actually exceeding centralized on two individual tasks (Close Drawer in simulation, Clean Up in real-world). The authors attribute FedAvg's underperformance to its uniform averaging ignoring task heterogeneity in multi-modal VLA settings, whereas FedVLA's Expert-Driven Aggregation selectively weights updates by expert-selection similarity across clients.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FedAvg federated learning paradigm to the multi-modal VLA domain, where prior FL approaches (FedHCA2, MAS) were limited to single-modal or single-task settings. Architecturally, it builds on Mixture-of-Experts designs from LLMs (DeepSeekMoE, LLaVA-MoE) but introduces a bidirectional gating mechanism where experts can reject token assignments, departing from the fixed top-k routing standard in prior MoE work. The Instruction-Oriented Scene-Parsing module repurposes CLIP and YOLOv8 for task-aware object decomposition, a technique borrowed from vision-language alignment but applied here specifically to structure federated client inputs. The Expert-Driven Aggregation strategy is a novel server-side modification to the standard FedAvg averaging step, using expert activation statistics to weight contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to address the privacy concerns inherent in VLA training, where user-specific home environment data (images, interaction trajectories) would otherwise be centralized. The practical motivation is strong: as VLA models move toward consumer robotics, federated training becomes a near-necessity for deployment. However, the experimental validation is limited to four tasks with small datasets (30–80 episodes per task) and a single-task-per-client setup, leaving open questions about scalability to dozens of heterogeneous tasks, communication overhead in large client populations, and robustness to non-stationary environments. The DGMoE's ~1.22 experts-per-token efficiency gain, while directionally correct, is modest and would need validation on larger-scale VLA models to confirm practical significance.
