## Problem

Existing CLIP-based CZSL methods suffer from a persistent modality gap that blurs boundaries between semantically similar compositions, and from the inherent lack of fine-grained visual cues in single-modal textual prototypes. These two limitations cause misclassification of visually similar pairs (e.g., 'ripe' vs. 'unripe' apples) because text-only class centers cannot capture the subtle visual distinctions that define compositional semantics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250127/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image, encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 image encoder with AdaptFormer adapters, and produces a composition label (attribute-object pair). Prediction operates under either a closed-world setting (candidate set = seen + known unseen compositions) or an open-world setting (candidate set = full Cartesian product of all attributes and objects). The three-branch output (attribute, object, composition) is fused by summing per-branch probabilities before argmax selection over the prediction space.

## Method

VP-CMJL introduces learnable visual proxies in the image feature space, initialized with CLIP text embeddings to provide semantically grounded starting points, then refined via contrastive cross-entropy losses that enforce intra-category compactness and inter-category separability. In parallel, textual prototypes are learned through CLIP text prompts with multi-head cross-attention decoupling modules (AD-CA/OD-CA) that align image features to text prototypes while extracting attention scores that augment the probability computation. A KL-divergence constraint (Cross-Modal Joint Learning) aligns the probability distributions produced by textual prototypes and visual proxies during training, treating text as the target distribution and visual proxies as the approximation. At inference, the final score for each composition is the sum of text-image similarity and image-visual-proxy similarity across all three branches, dynamically balancing semantic alignment with fine-grained visual discrimination.

## Results

In the closed-world setting, VP-CMJL achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three standard CZSL benchmarks: HM of 34.9% on C-GQA (+5.5% over Troika), 58.5% on UT-Zappos (+3.9% over IMAX), and 40.4% on MIT-States (+1.1% over CDS-CZSL), with corresponding AUC gains of +3.9%, +6.2%, and +1.2%. In the open-world setting, VP-CMJL leads on UT-Zappos (HM 54.5%, +6.7%) and C-GQA (HM 15.5%, +4.6%) while remaining competitive on MIT-States (HM 22.0% vs. CDS-CZSL's 22.1%). On the large-scale VAW-CZSL dataset, VP-CMJL improves HM by +3.6% and AUC by +3.5% over the previous best (CAILA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VP-CMJL outperforms all ten baselines on every metric in the closed-world setting. The closest competitors are Troika [7] (HM 29.4 on C-GQA, 54.6 on UT-Zappos) and IMAX [10] (HM 54.2 on UT-Zappos, AUC 40.6), with VP-CMJL exceeding them by 5.5 and 3.9 HM points respectively. In the open-world setting, VP-CMJL leads on UT-Zappos (HM 54.5 vs. CDS-CZSL's 48.2, a +6.7% margin) and C-GQA (HM 15.5 vs. CDS-CZSL's 11.6, +4.6%), while on MIT-States it is essentially tied with CDS-CZSL (22.0 vs. 22.1 HM), which benefits from dedicated label-noise pruning. Earlier prompt-based methods (CSP, DFSP, CoOp) and distribution-matching approaches (DLM, ProLT) trail by 5–15 HM points in closed-world, confirming that the visual proxy mechanism provides a substantial gain over purely text-prototype-based classification. On VAW-CZSL, VP-CMJL surpasses CAILA [18] by +3.6% HM and +3.5% AUC, the largest gap among all evaluated datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the three-path (attribute/object/composition) prediction framework introduced by Troika (CVPR 2024) and the learnable prompt paradigm from CoOp (IJCV 2022), using frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 with AdaptFormer adapters as the backbone. Its key conceptual departure is the introduction of learnable visual proxies—class centers in the visual space initialized from text embeddings and refined via contrastive learning—drawing on the intra-modal proxy learning idea from Qian et al. (NeurIPS 2024) but applying it to CZSL for the first time. The Cross-Modal Joint Learning (CMJL) strategy, which uses KL divergence to align text-prototype and visual-proxy probability distributions, represents a new loss formulation that goes beyond the standard text-image similarity matching used by prior CLIP-based CZSL methods (CSP, DFSP, CDS-CZSL, IMAX).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two persistent limitations in CLIP-based CZSL—the modality gap that blurs boundaries between semantically similar compositions, and the lack of fine-grained visual cues in single-modal textual prototypes—providing a principled mechanism (text-guided visual proxies) to tackle both simultaneously. The SOTA results across four benchmarks, particularly the +5.5% HM gain on C-GQA, suggest that explicitly learning visual class centers is a meaningful improvement over pure text-image matching. However, open questions remain: the open-world setting still shows competitive-but-not-dominant performance (e.g., MIT-States open-world HM of 22.0 vs. CDS-CZSL's 22.1), and it is unclear whether the visual proxy approach scales to much larger composition spaces or benefits from stronger backbones beyond CLIP ViT-L/14.
