## Problem

Diffusion-based super-resolution achieves strong perceptual quality but requires dozens to hundreds of inference steps. Existing one-step distillation methods (e.g., SinSR) inherit the teacher model's performance ceiling and incur high training costs from multi-step sampling to generate training pairs. The paper asks whether a distillation-free, backbone-independent one-step generative SR model can be trained directly while still producing photo-realistic results.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252206/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single low-resolution RGB image as input and produces a high-resolution RGB image in one forward pass. During training, the LR image is perturbed with a residual term (y₀ − x₀) and Gaussian noise according to a ResShift-style forward schedule, so the network learns to denoise from a noisy LR state rather than pure noise. The architecture uses pixel unshuffle and nearest-neighbor upsampling instead of a VQ-GAN encoder/decoder, keeping the model lightweight and trainable from scratch.

## Method

CTMSR trains in two stages. Stage 1 applies Consistency Training directly to the SR task: a UNet with Swin Transformer blocks is trained to map any point on a PF-ODE trajectory (from noisy LR to HR) to the final HR solution, using a consistency loss between outputs at two adjacent trajectory points with a stopgrad target. Stage 2 introduces Distribution Trajectory Matching (DTM): the converged model is frozen and used to construct both a 'real ODE' trajectory (starting from ground-truth HR) and a 'fake ODE' trajectory (starting from the model's SR output), and the gradient of the squared L2 distance between these two trajectories is backpropagated to the training model, omitting the expensive Jacobian term as in score distillation. This trajectory-level matching aligns the SR output distribution with the natural image distribution more effectively than point-level score distillation, improving perceptual quality without sacrificing fidelity.

## Results

On ImageNet-Test, CTMSR-1 achieves the best perceptual quality across all non-reference metrics: LPIPS 0.197, CLIPIQA 0.691, MUSIQ 60.14, MANIQA 0.486, and NIQE 5.66, while maintaining competitive fidelity (PSNR 24.73, SSIM 0.666). On real-world datasets, CTMSR-1 leads in MUSIQ (64.80 on RealSR, 67.17 on RealSet65) and NIQE (4.65, 4.51), outperforming SinSR-1 by 5.45 and 4.42 MUSIQ points respectively. Inference latency is 48 ms per 64×64 image on a single RTX 3090, making it 26% faster than SinSR-1 (65 ms) and 4.4× faster than ResShift-4 (210 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest one-step competitor SinSR-1 (distilled from ResShift), CTMSR-1 wins decisively on all perceptual metrics: LPIPS 0.197 vs 0.218, CLIPIQA 0.691 vs 0.611, MUSIQ 60.14 vs 53.63, MANIQA 0.486 vs 0.416, and NIQE 5.66 vs 6.29, while matching PSNR (24.73 vs 24.70) and SSIM (0.666 vs 0.663). Compared to multi-step ResShift-4, CTMSR-1 trades a small fidelity gap (PSNR 24.73 vs 25.02, SSIM 0.666 vs 0.683) for substantially better perceptual quality (MUSIQ +8.12, CLIPIQA +0.091, NIQE −1.68) and 4.4× faster inference. On real-world datasets (RealSR, RealSet65), CTMSR-1 achieves the best MUSIQ and NIQE scores, with MUSIQ gains of +5.45 and +4.42 over SinSR-1, though it trails SinSR-1 slightly on CLIPIQA (0.645 vs 0.663 on RealSR). GAN-based baselines (ESRGAN, BSRGAN, SwinIR, RealESRGAN) are consistently outperformed on perceptual metrics by a wide margin, confirming the advantage of the diffusion-based approach. Multi-step diffusion baselines (StableSR-200, LDM-15) are dominated by CTMSR-1 on both quality and speed, with StableSR-200 requiring 268× more inference time (12889 ms vs 48 ms) while scoring worse on most metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Consistency Models (Song et al., 2023) and diffusion-based super-resolution, adapting the CT strategy—originally designed for unconditional image generation—to the conditional SR setting by formulating a PF-ODE from noisy LR images to HR images. It departs from the dominant distillation paradigm (SinSR, Progressive Distillation, DMD) by training the one-step model from scratch, eliminating the teacher-student bottleneck. The Distribution Trajectory Matching loss is conceptually inspired by Score Distillation Sampling (DreamFusion, 2022) but operates at the trajectory level rather than the point level, matching full PF-ODE paths from the LR distribution rather than individual denoised estimates. The forward process and UNet-Swin architecture are inherited directly from ResShift, positioning CTMSR as a training-strategy upgrade rather than an architectural one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the distillation step, CTMSR eliminates both the high training cost of generating multi-step teacher outputs and the performance ceiling imposed by the teacher model, making one-step generative SR more accessible and scalable to smaller backbones. The DTM loss offers a principled, trajectory-level alternative to SDS for distribution alignment in SR, and the ablation showing DTM consistently outperforms SDS in both fidelity and perceptual quality suggests trajectory matching may be a more effective paradigm for conditional generation tasks. Open questions include whether the two-stage training (CT pre-training followed by DTM fine-tuning) can be unified, whether the approach extends to other restoration tasks (denoising, inpainting), and whether the gains persist at higher resolution scales or with more complex degradation models beyond the RealESRGAN pipeline.
