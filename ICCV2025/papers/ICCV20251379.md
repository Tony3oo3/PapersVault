## Problem

No public dataset provides the essential triplet of realistic map priors, current sensor data, and up-to-date ground-truth HD maps, forcing existing prior-aided mapping methods to rely on synthetic perturbations (noise, dropout, warping) that create a significant sim2real gap. Furthermore, standard evaluation metrics like mAP cannot distinguish whether a model merely preserves unchanged map elements or genuinely detects and integrates real-world changes, obscuring the stability-versus-adaptability tradeoff.

## Input / Output

The network takes multi-camera images (processed into BEV features via a ResNet-50 backbone) and a prior HD map encoded as 10 2D-points per left boundary, right boundary, and centerline, plus one-hot class labels and 7-type lane marking attributes, concatenated into a polyline sequence of shape n_prior × (30·d + 2·c_mark). The map is cropped to a 50×50 m² region. Output is a vectorized HD map in Argoverse 2 format (lane segments and pedestrian crossings) augmented with per-element change status: a primary label from {No Change, Insertion, Deletion, Other} and, when 'Other' is predicted, secondary binary flags for geometry and marking modifications.

## Method

The core contribution is a bijective change mapping framework that decomposes large-scale structural road updates (shape, appearance, function, lane graph, lane number) into unique compositions of atomic element-level edits (geometry, markings, type, connectivity, insertion/deletion), enforced via a disambiguation rule on the underlying lane graph topology. The baseline model extends LaneSegNet with a prior encoder that injects map elements as an additional cross-attention modality alongside BEV features, and a two-stage explainability module: a primary multi-class head assigns mutually exclusive change statuses, while secondary binary heads (trained with Focal Loss) flag co-occurring geometry and marking edits. Training uses a multi-task loss combining vector regression, segmentation (CE + Dice), classification, type prediction, and change-detection terms. The ArgoTweak dataset itself is constructed by applying realistic structural modifications to Argoverse 2 ground-truth maps under the bijective framework for training, while the test set uses real-world priors from the Argoverse 2 Map Change Dataset with newly annotated ground truth, enabling the first standardized evaluation of prior integration with a change-aware dual metric (mAPC + mACC).

## Results

On the ArgoTweak test set, the full model with atomic change annotations achieves mAP 78.8, mAPC 6.3, and mACC 64.6. The headline finding is the sim2real gap: training on ArgoTweak reduces ΔmACC from −36.0 (rule-based synthetic priors) to −3.5, a more than 10× reduction. On standard mAP, ArgoTweak-trained models score 77.7 vs. 76.7 (rule-based), 73.5 (continuous noise), and 71.5 (discrete dropout), but the paper argues these near-identical mAP values mask large qualitative differences in change-handling behavior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against other prior-aided mapping methods; instead, it compares its ArgoTweak dataset against three categories of synthetic prior generation (continuous noise, discrete element dropout/shift, and rule-based scripted edits from [12]). On mAP, ArgoTweak leads by only 1.0 point over rule-based priors (77.7 vs. 76.7), which the authors deliberately highlight to argue that mAP is an inadequate metric for prior-aided mapping. The decisive comparison is the sim2real gap: the rule-based-trained model shows ΔmACC = −36.0 between its synthetic validation and real-world test splits, while the ArgoTweak-trained model narrows this to ΔmACC = −3.5, demonstrating that realistic priors are essential for generalization. Qualitatively, only the ArgoTweak-trained model captures complex multi-element road updates, whereas rule-based-trained models overfit to lane-marking changes and noise-trained models support only minor geometric corrections. The ablation (Table 3) further shows that adding full atomic change annotations (model 6) yields the best combined mAP (78.8) and mACC (64.6) compared to binary change labels (model 3: mAP 77.7, mACC 70.5) or no annotations (model 1: mAP 77.7).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prior-aided HD mapping line of work (M3TR, PriorDrive, ExelMap) by addressing a fundamental data gap: no public dataset previously provided the triplet of realistic map priors, current sensor data, and up-to-date ground-truth maps. It builds on LaneSegNet as the lane-segment backbone, adopts the prior encoding scheme from Luo et al., and extends the authors' own ExelMap framework from binary insertion/deletion to a full vocabulary of atomic changes (geometry, markings, type, connectivity). The key conceptual departure is the bijective change mapping framework, which imposes a structured, near-injective correspondence between high-level structural updates and element-level edits, replacing the ad-hoc synthetic perturbations (noise, dropout, scripted rules) used by all prior prior-aided mapping work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ArgoTweak fills a critical data gap that has constrained the entire prior-aided HD mapping subfield, and the demonstrated 10× reduction in sim2real gap (ΔmACC from −36.0 to −3.5) provides concrete evidence that realistic priors are necessary rather than a nice-to-have. The finding that standard mAP scores are nearly identical (71–77) across qualitatively very different model behaviors is a conceptual challenge to the field's default evaluation practice, motivating the proposed mAPC/mACC dual metric. Open questions include whether the soft bijection generalizes to 3D maps or more complex topologies, whether the approach scales beyond the Argoverse 2 format, and whether the LaneSegNet backbone is the right architecture for capturing the full diversity of real-world changes.
