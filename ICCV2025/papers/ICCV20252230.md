## Problem

Existing reward models for multimodal LLMs suffer from three limitations: they rely on labor-intensive manual annotation for CoT quality signals, they use only a single final-outcome reward that ignores intermediate reasoning steps, and they provide only one-dimensional (correctness) scores rather than multi-dimensional assessments. This 'non-automatic, results-oriented, single-dimensional paradigm' fails to capture the quality of step-by-step reasoning in complex visual tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252230/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a visual task (single image, multi-image, or video) paired with a text query and instruction prompt, and produces step-level reward scores across three dimensions (relevance, logic, attribute) for each CoT step. The CoT steps fed to the reward model are interleaved multimodal sequences where image regions are encoded as bounding-box textual descriptions within a dictionary structure, making each step a self-contained unit of text plus referenced visual evidence. The intermediate artifacts include Python code block trees (with X=2 branching per step) and their execution intermediate variables.

## Method

SVIP leverages visual programming to automatically generate step-level, multi-dimensional CoT annotations: it uses least-to-most prompting to build a tree of Python code blocks, executes them to obtain intermediate variables, and evaluates each block on three axes—Relevance (compilation success/failure), Logic (PropTest-based format and logical-operation validation), and Attribute (De-fine cross-validation of external function return values). These code-level labels are then mapped onto natural-language CoT steps synthesized by an MLLM, creating training pairs without manual annotation. The reward model (SVIP-Reward) is trained with TriAtt-CoT, a tri-head attention layer that extracts features for each dimension independently to avoid objective conflict, followed by a contrastive loss against the three-dimensional labels and a scoring head that replaces the standard decoder to output per-step multi-dimensional reward scores. The approach is demonstrated as plug-and-play across data cleaning, RL training, and inference-time candidate selection for MLLMs.

## Results

On the introduced SVIP-Test benchmark, Qwen2-VL-7B(SVIP-Reward) achieves 70.7% average step-level accuracy across three dimensions (Relevance 92.3%, Logic 61.0%, Attribute 58.9%), outperforming GPT-4o (64.6%), IXC-2.5-Reward-7B (67.7%), and its own Tuning (63.4%) and Zero-shot (57.1%) variants. On MJ-Bench, SVIP-Reward scores 73.7% vs. GPT-4o's 65.8% and IXC-2.5-Reward-7B's 69.2%. For downstream MLLM training (Table 3), Qwen2-VL-7B with SVIP-Reward reward signals improves MME from 2374 to 2466 and MMMU from 58.5 to 61.8, while InternVL-2.5-2B gains 2.7% on MMMU-Pro over its Tuning variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SVIP-Reward (Qwen2-VL-7B) achieves the highest average step-level accuracy of 70.7% on SVIP-Test, surpassing GPT-4o (64.6%) by 6.1 points and IXC-2.5-Reward-7B (67.7%) by 3.0 points. On MJ-Bench, SVIP-Reward (73.7%) edges out Qwen2-VL-7B Tuning (73.1%) and GPT-4o (65.8%), while on VL-RewardBench it leads all baselines at 68.0% vs. GPT-4o and IXC-2.5-Reward-7B (both 65.8%). The smaller InternVL-2.5-2B backbone with SVIP-Reward (66.5% avg) also outperforms its Tuning (61.9%) and Zero-shot (59.6%) variants by 4.6 and 6.9 points respectively. In downstream RL training (Table 3), SVIP-Reward consistently improves MLLM performance: Qwen2-VL-7B gains +92 MME points and +3.3 MMMU over the no-reward baseline, while InternVL-2.5-2B gains +188 MME and +2.6 MMMU. For inference-time scaling (Table 4), SVIP-Reward further boosts Qwen2-VL-7B to 2472 MME and 61.6 MathVista, exceeding the no-reward baseline by +145 MME and +3.4 MathVista.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of visual programming (ViperGPT, Gupta & Kembhavi) and CoT reward model training (Math-Shepherd, PRMBench). It repurposes the interpretability of Python code execution—leveraging PropTest for logic validation and De-fine for attribute cross-validation—as an automatic annotation pipeline for step-level, multi-dimensional CoT labels, a connection not previously exploited for reward model training. The key departure from prior MLLM reward models (VL-RewardBench, InternLM-XComposer2.5-Reward) is the shift from single-dimensional, final-answer scoring to three-dimensional (relevance, logic, attribute) step-level evaluation, enabled by the TriAtt-CoT multi-head attention architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of SVIP-Train/SVIP-Test as the first step-level, multi-dimensional CoT benchmark for MLLMs provides a much-needed evaluation standard that existing benchmarks (RMB, PRMBench, MJ-Bench, VL-RewardBench) lack. The 83% human agreement with SVIP-annotated labels validates the automated pipeline's credibility, reducing the annotation bottleneck that has limited reward model research in the multimodal domain. Open questions include whether the three dimensions (relevance, logic, attribute) are sufficient to capture all failure modes in complex reasoning, whether the approach generalizes beyond the 24 tasks covered, and whether the reliance on GPT-4o mini as the code generator introduces a ceiling on label quality.
