## Problem

3D visual question answering remains significantly behind its 2D counterpart due to the scarcity of language-annotated 3D data and the high cost of manual 3D scene labeling. Existing 3D LMMs typically require explicit geometric inputs (point clouds, depth maps, or meshes) that are unavailable in many real-world settings, while image-only 2D LMMs lack explicit 3D spatial reasoning and struggle with queries about objects that never co-occur in a single view.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251446/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N posed RGB images (100 views uniformly sampled around a scene) and a free-form text question as input, requiring no explicit depth, point cloud, or 3D surface reconstruction. It produces a free-form text answer via a pretrained LLM, with an intermediate 3D-Language Gaussian Field that can be independently queried, cropped to a 3D region, or rendered from a novel view. The 3D tokens fed to the LLM form an unordered set (analogous to a point cloud) with no positional encoding or fixed sequence order, since spatial relationships are encoded within the features themselves through the rendering and fusion process.

## Method

SplatTalk operates in three stages: (1) a generalizable autoencoder compresses high-dimensional (3584-dim) LLaVA-OV visual tokens—extracted after the multimodal projector to ensure LLM compatibility—into a 256-dim unit hypersphere; (2) a feed-forward 3DGS model (extending FreeSplat) jointly learns RGB rendering parameters and semantic features per Gaussian in a single self-supervised pass, using the compressed tokens as pseudo ground-truth; (3) at inference, features are extracted at each Gaussian's mean position (justified via an EM-equivalence argument showing the optimal 3D feature is a weighted aggregation of 2D pseudo-ground-truth across views), projected back to the original embedding space via the autoencoder decoder, and entropy-adaptive sampling selects the top-k most informative Gaussians to match the LLM's visual token budget. The key architectural choices—using post-projector tokens, joint RGB-semantic training rather than sequential, and a single cross-scene autoencoder—distinguish SplatTalk from prior per-scene or object-centric 3DGS-language methods.

## Results

On ScanQA (val), SplatTalk's zero-shot base model achieves 61.7 CIDEr and 17.1 EM@1, outperforming LLaVA-OV (50.0 CIDEr, 15.6 EM@1) and GPT-4V (59.6 CIDEr) using only posed RGB images. After LoRA fine-tuning on combined 3D VQA data (SplatTalk-3DVQA-FT), performance rises to 77.5 CIDEr and 22.4 EM@1 on ScanQA, and 47.6 EM@1 / 49.4 EM@1-R on SQA3D, surpassing specialist models like 3D-VisTA (69.6 CIDEr) and approaching finetuned 3D LMMs like LEO (101.4 CIDEr, 50.0 EM@1 on SQA3D). On MSR3D (zero-shot), SplatTalk achieves 41.8 overall LLM-Match score, roughly doubling LLaVA-OV's 24.0 on most question categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SplatTalk's zero-shot base model outperforms all 2D LMM-based baselines on ScanQA CIDEr (61.7 vs. GPT-4V's 59.6, Claude's 57.7, LLaVA-OV's 50.0), demonstrating that the 3D Gaussian representation adds value over raw multi-image input. After fine-tuning, SplatTalk-3DVQA-FT surpasses all specialist models (3D-VisTA 69.6, ScanQA 64.9 CIDEr) and approaches finetuned 3D LMMs like Scene-LLM (80.0 CIDEr) and LEO (101.4 CIDEr), though it trails LEO by ~24 CIDEr points. On SQA3D, SplatTalk-3DVQA-FT (47.6 EM@1) surpasses specialist models (ScanQA 47.2, 3D-VisTA 48.5) but remains below LEO (50.0) and Chat-Scene (54.6). On MSR3D in zero-shot, SplatTalk (41.8 overall) roughly doubles LLaVA-OV (24.0) on existence, attributes, spatial, and navigation questions, while LEO (7.8) struggles due to lack of fine-tuning. The paper notes that fine-tuning on ScanQA/SQA3D actually degrades MSR3D performance (SplatTalk-3DVQA-FT drops to 26.5), suggesting domain mismatch between these benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

SplatTalk extends the FreeSplat generalizable 3DGS framework by adding a language-feature decoder head, enabling joint RGB-semantic optimization rather than the sequential (RGB-first, then language) pipeline used by ChatSplat and LangSplat. It departs from per-scene 3DGS language methods by training a single autoencoder across all scenes to compress LLaVA-OneVision's post-projector visual tokens into a compact hypersphere, making the approach fully self-supervised and generalizable. Conceptually, it bridges the gap between 2D LMM token-based VQA (which arranges tokens by image grid) and 3D LMMs requiring explicit geometry, by consolidating tokens in 3D space via Gaussian splatting before feeding them to an LLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first self-supervised 3DGS-based method for zero-shot 3D VQA that requires only posed RGB images, removing the dependency on point clouds, depth maps, or per-scene optimization that limits prior 3D LMMs. The EM-based justification for mean-feature extraction and the entropy-adaptive sampling strategy provide principled tools that could transfer to other 3D-language tasks. However, performance still trails the strongest 3D LMMs (e.g., LEO) by a few points on SQA3D, and the approach is validated only on indoor ScanNet scenes with posed multi-view inputs, leaving open questions about scalability to outdoor environments, unposed inputs, and whether the 3D representation's advantage persists as LLM context windows grow.
