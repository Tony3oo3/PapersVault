## Problem

Mainstream visual relation detection models overfit to frequent in-distribution predicates, cannot infer novel unseen relations, and produce coarse annotations that miss fine-grained relational semantics. While prompt tuning of VLMs has been explored, it relies on handcrafted prompts tailored to a limited set of known relations, failing to generalize to complex or unseen relational concepts. The paper asks how to adapt VLMs for VRD so they learn diverse, fine-grained relations while preserving broad generalization to out-of-distribution data.

## Input / Output

Given an RGB image with pre-specified subject and object bounding boxes and their category labels, the model generates a natural-language yes/no response indicating whether a queried relation (e.g., 'Is there a prominent spatial relation between the person and the hat?') holds, optionally naming the specific predicate. The instruction-tuning format wraps each subject-object pair into a question-response template that includes both positive and counter-negative examples, and the LLM component uses beam search (width M) to produce multiple candidate sequences, from which entropy and similarity scores are computed for the adaptive sampling loop.

## Method

ART reframes VRD as instruction tuning of a VLM (InstructBLIP with Vicuna-7B or FlanT5) in two stages. First, relation-tuning data creation converts VRD triplets into Q&A prompts annotated with high-level relation categories (spatial, semantic, possessive) and applies counter-negative mining, where negatives are assigned based on category exclusivity rules (e.g., spatial relations serve as negatives for possessive queries but not for semantic ones). Second, adaptive relation tuning runs multiple sampling iterations: a balanced sampling module initializes training with even predicate coverage, then an adaptive module allocates per-predicate budgets inversely proportional to recall and selects samples using entropy (for uncertain TPs and both high/low-entropy FNs) and cosine similarity between predicted and ground-truth embeddings (for semantically distant FPs). Thresholds are adapted per predicate by fitting normal distributions to the entropy/similarity statistics, and iteratively relaxed if the budget is unmet, ensuring the model focuses on the most informative and misleading examples while avoiding overfitting to the full training set.

## Results

On GQA (in-distribution with unseen predicates), Vicuna-7B+ART achieves mR@20/50 of 18.9/19.4%, a >40% relative improvement over the strongest VLM baseline (Naive-RT balanced random, 13.2/14.1%) and substantially above mainstream VRD models (Motifs 12.7%, VETO 10.9%). On the out-of-distribution OI-v4, Vicuna-7B+ART reaches mR@20/50 of 26.0/26.2%, nearly doubling the balanced-random baseline (11.8/12.4%) and far exceeding all non-VLM baselines (≤13.4%). On the most challenging OI-v6, FlanT5+ART achieves a 50% mR improvement over the second-best baseline, while mainstream models collapse (e.g., PENET drops to 0.1% mR). On the downstream deictic segmentation task, DeiSAM+ART (with bbox) achieves 99.98% mAP on VG and 97.96% on GQA, versus 7.87% for LISA and 35.04% for DeiSAM+GT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ART (Vicuna-7B) dominates all mainstream VRD baselines on mR across every test set: on GQA it reaches 18.9% mR@20 versus Motifs (12.7%), VTransE (9.8%), PENET (10.2%), and VETO (10.9%); on OI-v4 it achieves 26.0% versus at most 13.4% (Motifs); and on OI-v6 it scores 9.5% versus at most 4.0% (Motifs). Against the VLM-based Naive-RT baselines, ART yields a >40% relative mR gain on GQA (18.9% vs. 13.2% balanced-random) and a >120% gain on OI-v4 (26.0% vs. 11.8%), while also improving gR and gmR consistently. On the downstream deictic segmentation task, DeiSAM+ART (with bbox) achieves 99.98% mAP on VG and 97.96% on GQA, massively outperforming LISA (7.87% / 18.92%) and DeiSAM+GT (35.04% / 43.84%), demonstrating that ART's richer relational predictions directly translate into superior segmentation quality. ART also maintains strong performance under noisy object detections from Detectron2 and zero-shot RAM, where it still leads its sampling baselines by 10–20 mR points on OI-v4.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes visual relation detection (VRD) as an instruction tuning problem for vision-language models, departing from prior prompt-tuning approaches (He et al. 2022, Li et al. 2024) that rely on handcrafted prompts tailored to in-distribution relation cues. It builds on the InstructBLIP/BLIP-2 VLM family and the broader instruction-tuning paradigm (Wei et al. 2021, Dai et al. 2024), but introduces a novel adaptive sampling strategy inspired by active learning that operates on fully labeled data to select informative samples via entropy and cosine-similarity-based uncertainty. The counter-negative mining scheme, grounded in high-level relation categories (spatial, semantic, possessive), is a new data-construction technique not present in prior VRD or SGG work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ART addresses a critical gap in VRD: the inability of mainstream models to generalize to unseen relations, which is essential for safety-critical applications like autonomous driving. The finding that only ~12% of training data with adaptive sampling outperforms full-data training challenges the assumption that more data is always better for VLM fine-tuning. Limitations include the focus on relation classification (given bounding boxes) rather than full scene graph generation, and the mutual-exclusivity assumptions in counter-negative mining that may not hold for all relation types. Open questions include whether the adaptive sampling strategy transfers to other VLM architectures, scales to full SGG, and whether the entropy-based uncertainty from beam search generalizes to non-autoregressive decoders.
