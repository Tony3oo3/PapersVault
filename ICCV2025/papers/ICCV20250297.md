## Problem

Existing multi-view generation (MVG) evaluation relies on comparing generated images to ground truth views, which is inappropriate for generative tasks where multiple valid solutions exist and fails to capture 3D consistency across views. Different methods are trained on different camera setups, synthetic data, and lightings, making cross-method comparisons unreliable, while robustness to input perturbations and generalization to real images remain largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250297/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark feeds a single RGB image (rendered at each method's own optimal camera setup for synthetic data, or a manually annotated front view for real data) plus N target camera poses to an MVG model, which outputs N generated views. These views are split into two disjoint subsets, each independently optimized into a 3D Gaussian Splatting, and the discrepancy between the two 3DGSs yields the consistency scores. For real images where 3DGS alignment is ambiguous due to varying object scales, ICP with uniform scaling aligns reconstructions to a reference before scoring.

## Method

The core contribution is a 3D self-consistency metric that splits generated multi-views into two subsets, fits separate 3DGS to each, and measures geometric (Chamfer distance on covariance-resampled points, rendered depth error) and texture (cPSNR, cSSIM, cLPIPS) discrepancy between the two reconstructions—requiring no ground truth and enabling fair comparison across different camera setups. The suite additionally evaluates image quality via per-object FID (oFID) and VLM-based scoring, and semantic consistency (class, color, style) via VLM binary questions validated against human judgments. Leveraging systematic ablations across 12 SoTA MVGs, the authors propose ViFiGen, which builds on SV3D's video diffusion backbone by replacing the CLIP input encoder with ConvNextV2 for finer detail preservation and adding CaPE camera positional encoding from EscherNet, achieving the best 3D consistency among all evaluated methods.

## Results

On GSO (synthetic), ViFiGen achieves the best 3D consistency with CD=3.15 and cPSNR=28.93, outperforming SV3D-tune (CD=3.24, cPSNR=27.95) and SyncDreamer (CD=2.99, cPSNR=26.83). On CO3D (real), ViFiGen again leads in 3D consistency (CD=3.02, cPSNR=25.82) while matching SV3D-tune on image quality (IQ-vlm=0.29). A clear trade-off exists: Vivid123 achieves the highest IQ-vlm on CO3D (0.49) but the worst 3D consistency (CD=9.81), and no method simultaneously achieves top scores on both dimensions. Performance drops substantially on real images, with IQ-vlm falling from ~0.8 (GSO) to ~0.3 (CO3D) for most methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViFiGen outperforms all 12 baselines on 3D consistency (cPSNR) on both GSO and CO3D, with the closest competitor being SV3D-tune (28.93 vs. 27.95 cPSNR on GSO; 25.82 vs. 24.70 on CO3D). SyncDreamer achieves a slightly lower CD on GSO (2.99 vs. 3.15) but trails significantly on cPSNR (26.83 vs. 28.93) and image quality (IQ-vlm 0.53 vs. 0.82). Vivid123 is the only method that beats ViFiGen on IQ-vlm for real images (0.49 vs. 0.29 on CO3D), reflecting its stronger generalization to real data since it was not fine-tuned on Objaverse, but it sacrifices 3D consistency severely (CD 9.81 vs. 3.02). Video-based methods (SV3D, Hi3D, V3D) generally achieve a better consistency-quality balance than image-based methods (Zero123, Vivid123, EpiDiff), with Zero123 and Zero123-xl showing the worst 3D consistency overall (CD > 10 on GSO). The ablation in Table 3 confirms that replacing CLIP with ConvNextV2 and adding CaPE camera encoding to SV3D yields the largest individual gains, while adding SyncDreamer's 3D convolution on top provides only marginal improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as the first unified evaluation framework for multi-view generation models (MVGs), departing from prior works that rely on pairwise comparison against ground-truth views (PSNR, SSIM) or 3D ground-truth alignment. It builds directly on SV3D's video-diffusion architecture and EscherNet's CaPE camera embedding to construct ViFiGen, while introducing a 3D self-consistency metric based on 3D Gaussian Splatting that eliminates the need for ground-truth references. The benchmark also draws on VLM-based evaluation paradigms from image and video generation benchmarks, adapting them to the multi-view setting with object-level FID and semantic attribute checks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MVGBench fills a critical gap in a field producing 20+ papers per year with no standardized evaluation, enabling fair cross-method comparison and revealing that no existing model simultaneously achieves top 3D consistency, image quality, and robustness. The finding that explicit 3D convolution (SyncDreamer-style) adds little on top of video-based spatial-temporal attention challenges a widely adopted design assumption and redirects research toward better camera embeddings and input encoders. Open questions remain around the persistent synthetic-to-real performance gap, robustness to elevation and lighting perturbations, and handling of fine-grained geometric structures, all of which limit current MVGs for practical 3D content creation.
