## Problem

Single-image 3D scene reconstruction is fundamentally ill-posed, causing existing feed-forward methods to render incoherent, blurry views from novel camera angles—especially in regions far from the input view. Existing generative priors (text-guided inpainting, 2D diffusion distillation) either lack 3D correspondence awareness or produce temporally inconsistent results across views, making them unsuitable for large-viewpoint-change scene refinement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252597/cropped_page0_idx7.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (rendered at 256×384), camera intrinsics, and a sequence of test-camera trajectory extrinsics, along with coarse Gaussians from a pre-trained feed-forward model (Flash3D). It outputs refined 3D Gaussian splat parameters and high-fidelity novel view renderings. The entire refinement is self-supervised—requiring no 2D or 3D ground truth—and is post-hoc compatible with any existing reconstruction backbone, making it dataset-agnostic.

## Method

UAR-Scenes iteratively refines coarse Gaussians using a camera-controllable Latent Video Diffusion Model (MotionCtrl) that generates temporally consistent pseudo views as pseudo-2D supervision. A semantic uncertainty quantification module computes per-pixel Shannon entropy via BLIP-2 object tagging followed by open-vocabulary segmentation, producing uncertainty maps that weight the reconstruction loss (Hadamard product with confidence) so the optimizer focuses on high-confidence pixels while discarding uncertain regions. Fourier Style Transfer is applied on-the-fly between the input image and LVDM outputs to correct over-saturated textures. Adaptive Densification and Pruning controls Gaussian density, preserving primitives in observed regions and introducing new ones in unseen areas, with scale optimization constrained to a fixed range to prevent gradient explosion.

## Results

On RealEstate-10K, UAR-Scenes achieves 27.81 PSNR / 0.887 SSIM / 0.107 LPIPS on the wide U[-30,30] baseline, improving over the Flash3D baseline by +2.88 dB PSNR and reducing LPIPS from 0.160 to 0.107. On the narrower 5-frame and 10-frame settings, gains are more modest (+0.21 and +0.60 dB PSNR respectively), confirming that the refinement is most impactful when the camera moves far from the input view. On the out-of-domain KITTI-v2 dataset, UAR-Scenes reaches 22.31 PSNR / 0.844 SSIM, outperforming Flash3D (21.96 / 0.826) and all other baselines. In extrapolation on RealEstate-10K, UAR-Scenes achieves the best FID of 2.55, substantially lower than Flash3D (4.02) and LatentSplat (2.97).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UAR-Scenes outperforms all 12 baselines across every metric and baseline range in Table 1. The most dramatic gain is on the wide U[-30,30] setting, where it surpasses Flash3D by +2.88 dB PSNR (27.81 vs. 24.93) and MINE by +3.06 dB, indicating that the diffusion-guided refinement is most beneficial when large unseen regions must be synthesized. On the narrow 5-frame setting, UAR-Scenes edges out Flash3D (28.67 vs. 28.46 PSNR) and MINE (28.45), with the closest competitor being Flash3D itself. In the interpolation/extrapolation evaluation (Table 2), UAR-Scenes matches MVSplat on interpolation PSNR (26.37 vs. 26.39) but clearly wins on extrapolation FID (2.55 vs. 3.87 for MVSplat, 4.02 for Flash3D). On the out-of-domain KITTI-v2 dataset (Table 3), UAR-Scenes achieves the best PSNR (22.31) and SSIM (0.844), beating Flash3D by +0.35 dB and MINE by +0.41 dB, while MINE retains a slight LPIPS edge (0.112 vs. 0.128). Overall, the method consistently wins or ties on PSNR and SSIM across all settings, with the largest margins appearing in wide-baseline and extrapolation scenarios where single-image reconstruction is most challenged.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a post-hoc refinement layer atop existing feed-forward single-image-to-3D reconstruction models (primarily Flash3D), combining the geometric structure from a regressive backbone with the generative prior of a camera-controllable latent video diffusion model (MotionCtrl). It departs from prior diffusion-based 3D reconstruction work (CAT3D, GeNVS, ReconFusion) that fine-tunes or conditions diffusion models on specific datasets, instead directly optimizing Gaussian splat parameters with pseudo-2D supervision. The uncertainty quantification module, which leverages an MLLM (BLIP-2) to tag objects and an open-vocabulary segmentation model to compute per-pixel Shannon entropy, is a novel departure from prior uncertainty approaches in NeRF (BayesRays, variational inference) that operate at the 3D representation level rather than the 2D semantic level. The use of Adaptive Densification and Pruning from Gaussian Splatting SLAM to selectively add or remove primitives based on confidence is a practical repurposing of a SLAM mechanism for scene refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental limitation of single-image 3D reconstruction—degradation in unseen regions—by showing that a generative prior can fill in plausible completions while an uncertainty module prevents the model from overfitting to hallucinated details. Its post-hoc, self-supervised design means it can be bolted onto any existing reconstruction pipeline without retraining, which has practical appeal for the broader 3D reconstruction community. However, the approach inherits dependencies on LVDM quality (over-saturation, mode collapse) and MLLM object detection accuracy, and the iterative refinement (1000 steps per scene on a 48 GB GPU) limits scalability to very large or numerous scenes. Open questions include whether the segmentation-entropy uncertainty proxy generalizes to scenes with few distinct objects, and whether the FST alignment is sufficient for cross-domain texture transfer beyond the tested datasets.
