## Problem

Neural 3D modeling and novel view synthesis with NeRF or 3DGS typically require multi-view images with wide baselines and accurate camera poses, but scenarios with accidental camera motion (tiny involuntary movements from hand shaking or breathing) remain unexplored. Standard SfM methods fail under such conditions due to extremely small triangulation angles, and existing pose-free methods either depend on initial pose estimates or assume motion patterns incompatible with accidental motion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252660/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unposed RGB image sequence with minimal inter-frame displacement (accidental motion) as input, requiring no camera poses, intrinsics, or depth priors. It jointly recovers camera poses, focal length, a 3D Gaussian scene representation, and per-pixel depth maps, from which novel view renderings can be produced in real time. Optical flow between frames is computed via GMFlow to provide 2D correspondences for the geometric optimization.

## Method

AccidentalGS employs a two-stage pipeline. In the initialization stage, a GPU-differentiable bundle adjustment uses a small-angle rotation matrix approximation and inverse-depth parameterization to jointly optimize focal length, poses, and reference-view depth via a Huber-robust reprojection loss guided by GMFlow optical flow. In the joint optimization stage, the 3DGS field, camera poses, and depth are simultaneously refined using a combined objective of geometric (reprojection) and photometric (L1 + D-SSIM) losses, with the focal length frozen. The key novelty is an online adaptive depth-consistency loss that uses the continuously optimized reprojection depth as pseudo-ground-truth to supervise the α-blending rendered depth from 3DGS, bridging geometric and neural surface representations to prevent the Gaussian model from overfitting to the near-duplicate input views.

## Results

On the Replica-Accidental dataset, AccidentalGS achieves an average PSNR of 36.38 dB, SSIM of 0.96, and LPIPS of 0.09 for novel view synthesis, outperforming the closest competitor InstantSplat (35.60 dB, 0.95, 0.13) by 0.78 dB in PSNR. For camera pose estimation, AccidentalGS achieves average RPE_t of 0.160, RPE_r of 0.494°, and ATE of 0.179 (all scaled ×1000), substantially outperforming InstantSplat (1.75, 5.2°, 2.157) and FlowMap (0.792, 1.415°, 1.518). On the real-world Orbbec-Accidental dataset, the method reaches 32.65 dB average PSNR, and on DfUSMC it achieves 28.79 dB, consistently leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AccidentalGS outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and metric. The closest competitor is InstantSplat [8], which uses DUSt3R for initialization; AccidentalGS beats it by 0.78 dB PSNR (36.38 vs. 35.60) and 0.03 LPIPS (0.09 vs. 0.13) on Replica-Accidental, and by a larger margin on pose estimation (ATE 0.179 vs. 2.157, a 12× improvement). CF-3DGS [9] is the second-closest NVS baseline at 34.02 dB, trailing AccidentalGS by 2.36 dB. Among NeRF-based methods, UP-NeRF [25] (32.91 dB) and NoPe-NeRF [1] (31.97 dB) are the strongest but still fall 3.5–4.4 dB short. COLMAP [40] and BARF [32] perform worst (both 27.47 dB), confirming that standard SfM and bundle-adjusting NeRF struggle under tiny-baseline accidental motion. FlowMap [42] (32.67 dB) and NeRFmm [50] (30.53 dB) also lag significantly. On the real-world Orbbec-Accidental dataset, AccidentalGS (32.65 dB) leads InstantSplat (31.84 dB) and CF-3DGS (30.55 dB), and on DfUSMC it achieves 28.79 dB vs. InstantSplat's 28.46 dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

AccidentalGS builds on the 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering framework (Kerbl et al., 2023) and the simplified small-angle rotation model from accidental-motion bundle adjustment literature (Yu et al., 2016; Ha et al., 2016), but departs from both pose-free NeRF methods (BARF, NeRFmm, NoPe-NeRF, UP-NeRF) and SfM-free 3DGS methods (CF-3DGS, InstantSplat), which are designed for wide-baseline or sparse-view settings and fail under the extremely small triangulation angles of involuntary camera shake. The key conceptual departure is the joint optimization of geometric (reprojection via optical flow) and photometric (3DGS rendering) errors within a single differentiable pipeline, combined with a novel online adaptive depth-consistency loss that bridges the geometric depth from bundle adjustment with the α-blending depth of the Gaussian field to prevent overfitting to the near-duplicate input views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work opens a practically important but previously unaddressed scenario—reconstructing 3D scenes from the tiny, involuntary camera motions that occur when taking photos with smartphones—demonstrating that even telescope-like images of the Moon can yield usable 3D models. The online adaptive depth-consistency loss is a transferable technique for any 3DGS pipeline where input views are highly correlated and overfitting is a risk. However, the method assumes a static scene and relies on optical flow quality, limiting its applicability to dynamic or transparent-object-heavy scenes; extending it to handle motion and more robust correspondence estimation remains an open question.
