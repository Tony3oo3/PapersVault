## Problem

Existing 3D reconstruction methods (NeRF, 3DGS) produce floaters and view-dependent artifacts when scenes contain dynamic elements, and prior distractor-free approaches rely on careful initialization and stable optimization that break down on highly dynamic egocentric videos with continuous human-scene interactions, severe motion blur, and rapid illumination changes. No prior method robustly handles both casual image collections and long, interaction-rich egocentric recordings within a single self-supervised framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251230/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB video frames (2800–5000 frames for egocentric sequences, with 1-in-5 held out) or casual image collections (several hundred images) along with camera poses from COLMAP or Aria sensor perception. It outputs a decoupled 3D Gaussian scene split into a static background branch and a deformable dynamic foreground branch, plus a per-pixel probabilistic dynamic-static mask and a brightness control map. The foreground Gaussians carry three extra per-primitive attributes (foreground probability, background probability, brightness control) beyond standard position, scale, rotation, opacity, and SH color, and training proceeds in two stages: a coarse phase without deformation (1000 iterations or sequence-length iterations) followed by a fine end-to-end phase (20k–120k iterations).

## Method

DeGauss introduces a decoupled foreground-background Gaussian Splatting design in which dynamic elements are modeled by foreground Gaussians with a Hexplane-based spatio-temporal encoder and per-attribute MLP decoders (predicting deformations for position, rotation, scale, opacity, color, and the three mask attributes), while static content is captured by background Gaussians. A probabilistic composition mask is rasterized directly from the foreground Gaussians' mask attributes, and the final image is composed *after* independent rendering of each branch (C = P_f·C_f + P_b·B̂·C_b), enabling full gradient flow and gradual mask formation during training. A piecewise-linear brightness control mask boosts the background branch's non-Lambertian capacity to resolve illumination-induced dynamic-static ambiguities. The loss is split into a main term (driving Gaussian densification) and a utility term (SSIM, entropy, brightness, scale regularization) to separately control floater spawning and utility-Gaussian count, and a 50% partial opacity reset stabilizes training and prunes floaters without the instability of full resets.

## Results

On the NeRF-on-the-go distractor-free reconstruction benchmark, DeGauss achieves a mean PSNR of 23.91, SSIM of 0.819, and LPIPS of 0.113, outperforming the previous SOTA SpotlessSplats (23.42 / 0.813 / 0.145) by +0.49 dB PSNR and −0.032 LPIPS. On the Neu3D dynamic modeling benchmark, DeGauss attains the best LPIPS of 0.047 (vs. 4DGS at 0.058 and SWinGS at 0.095) while remaining competitive on PSNR (31.52) and SSIM (0.942). For egocentric video sequences (ADT, AEA, Hot3D, EPIC-Fields), the paper reports qualitative comparisons only due to the absence of clean reference views, showing visually superior distractor-free static backgrounds and accurate foreground separation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the primary NeRF-on-the-go benchmark, DeGauss outperforms all six baselines on all three metrics, with the largest gap over SpotlessSplats (previous SOTA): +0.49 dB PSNR, +0.006 SSIM, and −0.032 LPIPS. The advantage over DeSplat is even more pronounced on LPIPS (0.113 vs. 0.130), indicating better perceptual quality in occluded regions. On Neu3D dynamic modeling, DeGauss achieves the best LPIPS (0.047) among all eight methods, beating 4DGS (0.058) and SWinGS (0.095), though KPlanes retains a slight edge on PSNR (31.63 vs. 31.52) and SSIM (0.964 vs. 0.942). For egocentric videos (ADT, AEA, Hot3D, EPIC-Fields), qualitative comparisons in Figure 4 show DeGauss producing cleaner static backgrounds and more accurate foreground separation than 3DGS, SpotlessSplats, and NeuralDiff, which all exhibit visible floaters or over-segmentation. On HyperNeRF, DeGauss qualitatively outperforms 4DGS by reducing unregularized Gaussian movement artifacts while preserving fine dynamic details (Figure 8).

## Relation to Prior Work

DeGauss builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting as its base representation and adopts the 4DGS/HexPlane paradigm (spatio-temporal encoder + per-attribute MLP decoders) for the foreground deformation module. It departs from the distractor-free reconstruction line (NeRF-on-the-go, WildGaussians, SpotlessSplats), which suppresses transients via loss residuals or semantic features, and from self-supervised NeRF decomposition methods (NeRF-W, NeuralDiff, D²NeRF), which jointly model dynamic and static branches with compositional rendering. The key conceptual departure is a fully decoupled foreground-background Gaussian design where a probabilistic mask rasterized from the foreground branch controls composition *after* independent rendering, enabling full gradient flow and avoiding the early ray-termination and local-minima issues of prior compositional approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying distractor-free static reconstruction and dynamic scene modeling in a single framework, DeGauss provides a practical baseline for the increasingly important egocentric video setting, where prior methods either over-segment dynamics or leave floaters in the static scene. The decoupled design with probabilistic mask rasterization is a clean architectural choice that could be adopted in other Gaussian-based pipelines. Open questions include whether the heuristic brightness control mask generalizes to extreme illumination changes, whether the method scales to very long unbounded sequences without the 120k-iteration budget, and whether the probabilistic mask can be replaced by learned segmentation for even cleaner separation.
