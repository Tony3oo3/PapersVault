## Problem

Existing language-driven DGSS methods use fixed context prompts (handcrafted templates or a single learnable embedding) to decorate class names for VLM-based query initialization, but these prompts are optimized only on the source domain and cannot adapt to the visual characteristics of unseen target domains. This rigidity causes semantic misalignment between textual and visual features under severe domain shifts (e.g., synthetic-to-real, day-to-night), limiting the model's ability to fully exploit VLM semantic knowledge.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (cropped to 512×512) and produces a pixel-level segmentation map of shape K×H×W, where K is the number of semantic classes, derived by multiplying per-query mask predictions (N×H×W) with per-query class scores (N×K). During training, a texture-perturbed copy of each image is generated via photometric transformations and processed alongside the original to form a paired batch, enabling both contrastive prompt learning and consistency supervision.

## Method

DPMFormer extends Mask2Former with a frozen CLIP/EVA02-CLIP text encoder that initializes object queries from class-name text features. The core novelty is a domain-aware prompt learning module: a small auxiliary network (BatchNorm-Linear-ReLU-Linear) maps the CLIP class token of the input image to a domain-specific prompt embedding π_x, which is added to a learnable context prompt p to form p_x = p + π_x before text encoding, so the resulting text queries reflect the input's domain characteristics. A domain-aware contrastive loss trains this auxiliary network to produce embeddings that cluster by domain (original vs. augmented) rather than by instance. Additionally, texture perturbation (color jitter, Gaussian blur, noise) synthesizes novel-domain training images, and a domain-robust consistency loss—combining binary cross-entropy on masks and Jensen-Shannon divergence on class predictions—is applied at every transformer decoder layer to enforce prediction stability across the original–augmented image pair.

## Results

On the synthetic-to-real benchmark (GTAV source), DPMFormer with a CLIP-pretrained ViT-B backbone achieves 58.14% average mIoU across Cityscapes (59.00%), BDD100K (51.80%), and Mapillary (63.62%), surpassing the previous state-of-the-art TQDM by 3.17% on average. With the larger EVA02-CLIP backbone, DPMFormer reaches 67.07% average mIoU, outperforming TQDM (66.05%) and Rein (63.60%). In the real-to-real setting (Cityscapes source), DPMFormer attains 70.44% average mIoU with EVA02-L, matching TQDM, and 61.27% with ViT-B, exceeding TQDM by 3.13%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPMFormer consistently outperforms all 13 compared baselines on the primary synthetic-to-real (GTAV) benchmark. Against the closest competitor TQDM [36], DPMFormer gains +3.17% avg mIoU with ViT-B (58.14 vs. 54.97) and +1.02% with EVA02-L (67.07 vs. 66.05), with the largest per-domain gains on BDD100K (+4.14% ViT-B) and Mapillary (+3.17% ViT-B). Against VLM-based methods VLTSeg [21] and Rein [55], DPMFormer leads by 8.97% and 3.47% respectively with EVA02-L. In the real-to-real (Cityscapes source) setting, DPMFormer with ViT-B beats TQDM by 3.13% avg (61.27 vs. 58.14), while with EVA02-L it ties TQDM at 70.44% avg but edges ahead on Mapillary (76.67 vs. 76.15) and slightly trails on BDD100K (64.2 vs. 64.72). Traditional DGSS baselines (SAN-SAW, WildNet, SHADE, TLDR) lag by 10–16% in average mIoU, highlighting the advantage of VLM-driven approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

DPMFormer extends the Mask2Former-based DGSS line initiated by TQDM, which first introduced textual-query-driven object queries initialized from VLM text encoders. The key departure is from TQDM's fixed single context prompt (a learnable embedding optimized on one source domain), which the authors argue causes semantic misalignment when target domains differ substantially in appearance. The paper also draws on domain randomization literature (SHADE, RobustNet) for its texture perturbation strategy and on contrastive prompt learning (CoCoOp, MaPLe, PromptSRC) to motivate a domain-aware contrastive objective that is explicitly designed to separate domain-specific prompt embeddings rather than instance-specific ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The domain-aware prompt mechanism offers a principled way to condition VLM textual knowledge on the visual domain of the input, a direction that could transfer to other VLM-driven segmentation or detection tasks beyond DGSS. However, the approach remains constrained to a single-source setting with relatively simple photometric perturbations, leaving open whether the learned domain prompts generalize to structurally different shifts (e.g., sensor modality changes, novel camera geometries) that texture perturbation cannot simulate. The consistency loss applied at every decoder layer is a practical design choice whose interaction with deeper or differently-structured decoders remains unexplored.
