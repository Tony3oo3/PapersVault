## Problem

VAE-based UHD restoration methods compress degraded images indiscriminately, losing both background and degradation features due to their inherent coupling. This causes uncontrollable information loss during encoding and uncontrollable information gain during compensation, resulting in restored images with detail loss and residual degradation artifacts. The paper tackles the lack of controllability in what information is discarded versus preserved during the VAE encoding process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251602/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded UHD image (4K resolution, RGB) and produces a restored UHD image at the same resolution. Internally, the input is decomposed into a degradation-dominant latent vector (downsampled by 2^L in spatial dimensions) and L multi-scale background-dominant feature maps. Restoration is performed in two parallel paths—latent-space mapping for degradation and invertible multi-scale fusion for background—before the decoder reassembles the final output. The full pipeline operates at native 4K resolution with only ~1M parameters.

## Method

D²R-UHDNet implements an Active Discarding–Targeted Restoration paradigm via three components. First, the CD²-VAE encoder uses Hierarchical Contrastive Disentanglement Learning (Hi-CDL), a multi-scale contrastive loss that progressively pushes background features out of the latent encoding while retaining degradation-relevant features, combined with an Orthogonal Gated Projection Module (OrthoGate) that enforces Stiefel-manifold orthogonality on pointwise convolutions to mathematically minimize mutual information between decoupled subspaces. Second, the Complex Invertible Multiscale Fusion Network (CIMF-Net) processes the multi-scale background features through GLOW-based invertible modules operating in the complex domain, guaranteeing lossless cross-scale information transfer. Third, a lightweight Latent Restoration Network (LaReNet, based on SFHformer) maps the degradation-dominant latent to a clean latent. The three-stage training (CleanVAE pretraining → CD²-VAE disentanglement training → full D²R-UHDNet restoration training) enables the VAE to actively discard easily recoverable background information while concentrating the latent capacity on harder-to-recover degradation components.

## Results

On the all-in-one UHD restoration benchmark (four degradation types), D²R-UHDNet achieves an average PSNR of 28.20 dB, SSIM of 0.860, and LPIPS of 0.2523 with only 1.0M parameters and 4.0G FLOPs, outperforming the closest VAE-based competitor DreamUHD (27.43 dB, 1.46M params) by 0.77 dB. On individual tasks, the method leads by 0.22 dB on low-light enhancement (27.94 vs. 27.72), 0.68 dB on dehazing (25.37 vs. 24.69), 0.33 dB on deblurring (29.84 vs. 29.51), and 0.68 dB on demoiréing (23.92 vs. 23.24) over the respective best baselines. The method also surpasses much heavier all-in-one models (e.g., HAIR at 29M params, 41G FLOPs) by 0.84 dB average PSNR while using ~29× fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D²R-UHDNet achieves the best PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across all six evaluated UHD restoration tasks (low-light, dehazing, deblurring, demoiréing, denoising at three noise levels, and all-in-one), outperforming every baseline. Against the closest VAE-based competitor DreamUHD [18], the gains range from +0.22 dB (low-light) to +0.68 dB (dehazing and demoiréing), with a consistent ~0.3 dB advantage on deblurring. Compared to the ultra-lightweight UHDformer [27] (0.33M params), the method improves average PSNR by 1.67 dB while using only 3× more parameters. Against heavy all-in-one restoration models such as HAIR [2] (29M params, 41G FLOPs) and adaIR [6] (28.7M params, 147G FLOPs), D²R-UHDNet surpasses them by 0.84 and 1.84 dB average PSNR respectively, while using 29–29× fewer parameters and 10–37× fewer FLOPs. The method also outperforms UHDDIP [29] (0.81M params) by 1.57 dB average PSNR, demonstrating that the feature disentanglement strategy provides substantially better information retention than prior dual-interaction or high-frequency injection approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends DreamUHD's VAE-based UHD restoration framework, departing from its High Frequency Injection strategy (which compensates for loss post-hoc) by instead making the VAE's information loss itself controllable through active feature disentanglement. The CD²-VAE introduces Hierarchical Contrastive Disentanglement Learning and an Orthogonal Gated Projection (constrained on the Stiefel manifold) to selectively discard background features while encoding degradation-dominant information into the latent space. The CIMF-Net repurposes GLOW's invertible 1×1 convolution architecture into a complex-domain variant for multi-scale background fusion, while the latent restoration backbone (SFHformer) is a plug-in component. Conceptually, the paper shifts the paradigm from 'compress then compensate' to 'decouple then restore separately,' a divide-and-conquer framing not present in prior VAE-based UHD methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Active Discarding–Targeted Restoration paradigm offers a principled answer to the long-standing tension in VAE-based restoration between compression efficiency and information fidelity, and the 1M-parameter footprint across six UHD tasks is practically attractive for deployment. However, the performance gains over DreamUHD (the closest VAE-based baseline) are modest in most settings (0.2–0.6 dB PSNR), and the complex-domain GLOW's advantage over simpler cross-attention fusion (Set3 in Table 7) is narrow, raising questions about whether the added architectural complexity is justified. Open questions include whether the disentanglement strategy generalizes to unseen degradation types or video restoration, and whether the Stiefel-manifold constraint on OrthoGate is essential versus a simpler orthogonal initialization.
