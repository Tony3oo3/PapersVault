## Problem

Activation-based zero-cost proxies (AZP) such as NWOT and SWAP exhibit a previously unreported negative correlation phenomenon: their Spearman correlation with true architecture accuracy degrades sharply and becomes negative as the number of convolutions in the search space increases (e.g., NWOT drops to −0.734 on the 77-convolution subspace of NAS-Bench-201). The paper identifies the root cause as high non-linearity from accumulated ReLU transformations, which dramatically reduces the magnitude of AZP scores and inverts the ranking signal, yet no prior work had analyzed or addressed this mechanism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252180/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a candidate neural architecture (defined by its cell topology and operation choices) together with a small batch of unlabeled input images from the target dataset, all evaluated at random initialization with no gradient descent. It produces a scalar ranking score per architecture (e.g., a modified NWOT log-determinant or a modified SWAP unique-vector count), from which the highest-scoring architecture is selected as the search output. The masking probability α is the sole hyperparameter, and the method is insensitive to its exact value across search spaces.

## Method

NCD (Negative Correlation-Defied) augments any existing AZP with two lightweight modifications. First, Stochastic Activation Masking (SAM) multiplies each input activation tensor by a Bernoulli(1−α) mask tensor before the convolution, so that only a random subset of activation values participates in each filter computation, directly reducing the non-linearity accumulated through weighted summation of ReLU outputs. Second, Non-linear Rescaling (NIR) replaces Batch Normalization with Layer Normalization during AZP evaluation; the paper proves (Theorem 4.1 vs. 4.2) that BN's batch mean is a weighted sum of B·C·K² ReLU values (amplifying non-linearity), whereas LN's spatial mean weights each activation by the expectation of the kernel weight, which is approximately zero at initialization, thereby suppressing non-linearity without altering the relative ranking of architectures. Together, SAM and NIR restore positive correlation across all convolution-count subspaces and improve test accuracy by up to 0.93% on NAS-Bench-201 and 2.4% on MobileNet-like search, while also reducing runtime.

## Results

On NAS-Bench-201, NCD-NWOT (α=0.95) achieves 94.00% test accuracy on CIFAR-10, 71.99% on CIFAR-100, and 45.27% on ImageNet16-120, outperforming the previous SOTA (AZ-NAS) by 0.51%, 1.66%, and 0.93% respectively, while running 2.4× faster (29.01 ms/arch vs. 69.43 ms/arch). On NAS-Bench-101, NCD-NWOT reaches 93.49% accuracy with the fewest runtime (29.01 ms). On the DARTS search space, NCD-NWOT achieves 2.44% CIFAR-10 test error and 76.9% ImageNet Top-1 accuracy, surpassing all training-free baselines. The method also resolves the negative correlation issue, improving Spearman's ρ from −0.734 (NWOT) to 0.679 on the 77-convolution subspace of NAS-Bench-201.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NCD-NWOT consistently outperforms all training-free baselines across all four datasets in Table 2: it beats NWOT by 2.05% (CIFAR-10), 3.11% (CIFAR-100), and 2.96% (ImageNet16-120), and beats SWAP by 3.20%, 3.81%, and 9.74% respectively. Against the strongest non-AZP baselines, NCD-NWOT surpasses Synflow by 0.33% on CIFAR-10 and 1.88% on ImageNet16-120, and ZiCo by 0.31% and 3.85% on the same datasets. On the DARTS search space (Table 3), NCD-NWOT achieves 2.44% CIFAR-10 test error (vs. 4.27% for NWOT and 2.48% for SWAP) and 76.9% ImageNet Top-1 (vs. 76.0% for SWAP and 75.9% for IS-DARTS), while using only 0.002 GPU-days—3× less than SWAP. On the MobileNet-like search space, NCD-NWOT reaches 78.0% Top-1, exceeding the previous SOTA RLNAS by 2.4%. The method also generalizes to transformers, achieving 78.6% Top-1 on AutoFormer-T with 1.5× faster search than NWOT. Notably, NCD-NWOT is the fastest method overall (29.01 ms/arch), outpacing even ZenNAS (30.07 ms) and NWOT (36.54 ms), thanks to the computational savings from stochastic activation masking.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the activation-based zero-cost proxy line of work initiated by NWOT (Mellor et al., ICML 2021) and SWAP (Peng et al., ICLR 2024), which score architectures by measuring dissimilarity or uniqueness of binary activation patterns at initialization. Rather than proposing a fundamentally new proxy, the paper diagnoses a specific failure mode—negative correlation between AZP scores and true accuracy that worsens with convolutional depth—and attributes it to non-linearity accumulation from stacked ReLU transformations. The proposed NCD method (Stochastic Activation Masking + Non-linear Rescaling via BN→LN substitution) is a targeted modification of existing AZP scoring rather than a new proxy family, and the authors frame their four design principles as generalizable to other zero-cost proxy types (gradient-based, theory-based).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The negative correlation diagnosis is practically important because it explains why training-free NAS methods silently fail on deeper search spaces (e.g., 65–77 convolutions on NAS-Bench-201), where practitioners would expect AZP to at least remain neutral. The fix is lightweight (a Bernoulli mask and a normalization swap), making it easy to adopt alongside existing NWOT/SWAP pipelines. Open questions include whether the α hyperparameter can be made search-space-agnostic (the paper acknowledges it varies across topologies), whether the approach extends to non-convolutional backbones beyond the limited AutoFormer experiment, and how the method interacts with other proxy families (gradient-based, theory-based) that the design principles nominally cover but are not empirically validated.
