## Problem

Dense self-supervised learning in images relies on augmentations that implicitly preserve pixel-level correspondences, but this breaks down in videos where object motion, camera shifts, and occlusions destroy such correspondences. Existing video SSL methods either propagate object masks (which fail under occlusions and rapid motion) or use local frame-based learning that cannot capture long-range temporal consistency, resulting in degraded and incoherent dense representations over time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251718/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes video clips (e.g., 12 frames at 224×224 resolution) that are patchified into p×p tokens and passed through a ViT-S/14 or ViT-B/14 backbone. A binary mask randomly hides a fraction of tokens for the student network, while the teacher sees all patches. Additionally, N points are sampled on a regular grid (16×16) in the first frame and tracked across all frames by a frozen CoTracker-v3 point tracker, producing continuous 2D trajectories. The output is the teacher network's dense patch-level feature map (n×d per frame), which serves as a frozen feature extractor for downstream segmentation, detection, and in-context retrieval tasks.

## Method

MoSiC clusters patch features in the first frame using Sinkhorn-Knopp optimal transport against K learnable prototypes, then propagates these cluster assignments along motion trajectories extracted by CoTracker-v3 to all subsequent frames. The key mechanism is a cross-entropy loss that aligns the student's soft cluster scores (computed at t₀) with the teacher's propagated one-hot assignments at each later frame t, but only for points whose trajectories remain visible (gated by a visibility flag v_{t,i}). This enforces that all patches along a tracked trajectory—representing the same object part across time—are consistently assigned to the same cluster, implementing the Gestalt principle 'what moves together belongs together' at fine spatial granularity. The framework uses a DINO-style EMA teacher-student ViT, initialized from DINOv2 and fine-tuned solely on YouTube-VOS video data, with simple augmentations (cropping and masking) sufficient for effective learning.

## Results

MoSiC achieves state-of-the-art dense self-supervised performance across four benchmarks. On in-context scene understanding (Pascal VOC, full data), MoSiC-S14 reaches 78.2 mIoU vs. DINOv2's 77.0, and MoSiC-B14 reaches 80.5 vs. DINOv2's 77.1; in the low-data regime (1/128), MoSiC-S14 gains 6.5 mIoU over DINOv2 (62.5 vs. 56.0). On unsupervised video segmentation, MoSiC outperforms the closest video-based baseline TimeT by 8.7 mIoU on DAVIS (58.9 vs. 49.5) and 9.4 mIoU on YouTube-VOS (60.6 vs. 50.2). For linear segmentation, MoSiC-B14 achieves 81.5 mIoU on Pascal VOC and 43.6 on ADE20K, surpassing DINOv2 by 1.2 and 1.0 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoSiC consistently outperforms all 17 baselines across every benchmark. Against the strongest image-pretrained baseline DINOv2, MoSiC gains 1–6 mIoU depending on dataset and data regime (e.g., +6.5 on Pascal VOC 1/128, +1.2 on Pascal VOC full data for linear segmentation). Against DINOv2-R (registers variant), MoSiC leads by up to 4 mIoU on Pascal VOC linear segmentation and ~10 mIoU in the 1/128 in-context setting. The closest video-based competitor, TimeT [50], is surpassed by 8.7 mIoU on DAVIS and 9.4 mIoU on YouTube-VOS for unsupervised video segmentation, and by 15.9 mIoU on Pascal VOC in-context (1/128). Among image-trained dense methods, MoSiC beats CrIBo by 5.7–6.6 mIoU on frozen clustering (K=500) and Leopart by 13.7 mIoU on Pascal VOC in-context (1/128). Older baselines (DINO, CrOC, SelfPatch, MAE, iBOT, STEGO, MaskContrast, DeepSpectral, DINOSAUR, COMUS) are all outperformed by 10+ mIoU on most benchmarks. On object detection (COCO), MoSiC matches or slightly exceeds DINOv2 (e.g., 46.4 vs. 46.1 AP^box for ViT-B/14), confirming that video-only fine-tuning preserves and slightly enhances detection capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoSiC extends the DINO/DINOv2 teacher-student framework with Sinkhorn-Knopp optimal-transport clustering to the video domain, departing from prior video SSL methods (TimeTuning, DoRA, MooG) that rely on mask propagation or local frame-based learning. The key conceptual departure is the use of an off-the-shelf dense point tracker (CoTracker-v3) to establish long-range spatiotemporal correspondences, which are then used to propagate cluster assignments along motion trajectories rather than relying on static augmentations or short-range temporal cues. This operationalizes the Gestalt principle 'what moves together belongs together' at patch granularity, bridging the gap between object-centric clustering (CrIBo, Leopart) and motion-aware video learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that motion trajectories from a frozen tracker can serve as a powerful implicit supervisory signal for dense SSL, improving DINOv2 by 1–6% across six datasets using only video data—suggesting videos are an underexploited complementary resource to image pretraining. A practical limitation is the dependency on tracker quality: CoTracker-v3's drift accumulation over long sequences could degrade the signal, and the method's performance on very long or highly dynamic scenes remains untested. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 3D or multi-view settings, whether learned (rather than frozen) trackers could further improve the signal, and whether the motion-based clustering loss can be integrated into the initial pretraining phase rather than applied as a fine-tuning step.
