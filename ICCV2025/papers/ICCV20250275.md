## Problem

Current prompt engineering methods for text-to-video generation suffer from three critical gaps: Modality-Inconsistency (image-oriented prompts emphasize static attributes like composition and color rather than dynamic video requirements such as motion fluency and temporal coherence), Cost-Discrepancy (video models lack the mature prompt platforms available for image generation), and Model-Unaware (existing LLM-based prompt expansion does not align with the specific preferences of a target video diffusion model). The paper asks how to build a model-aware, labor-free system that produces video-centric, preference-aligned prompts automatically.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250275/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a concise user text prompt (e.g., a short sentence describing a scene) and outputs a refined, detailed natural-language prompt optimized for a specific video diffusion model. The refined prompt is then consumed by the target generator—CogVideoX (4-second, 480×720) or Open-Sora 1.2 (4-second, 720×1080) for video, or Stable Diffusion v1.4 for image—to produce the final visual output. Unlike image prompt boosters that append quality modifiers, the output prompt restructures the description with richer motion cues, scene dynamics, and model-specific phrasing rather than generic aesthetic suffixes.

## Method

Prompt-A-Video is a two-stage LLM optimization pipeline built on LLaMA3-Instruct with LoRA fine-tuning. Stage 1 (SFT) uses a reward-guided prompt evolution pipeline inspired by evolutionary algorithms: GPT-4o acts as the evolutionary operator, iteratively generating, evaluating (via a multi-dimensional reward system combining VideoScore's five video-level metrics—VQ, TC, DD, TVA, FC—and image-level Aesthetic Predictor and MPS scores), selecting top-N prompts, and producing refined candidates over 4 iterations, yielding ~3,000 original-to-refined prompt pairs for supervised fine-tuning. Stage 2 (DPO) generates 5 candidate prompts per input from the SFT model, scores the resulting videos with the full reward suite, and constructs {prompt, chosen, rejected} triplets to train with Direct Preference Optimization, repeated over 2 iterations for continued alignment. The key insight is that the evolutionary data engine simultaneously solves the data-scarcity problem (no manual prompt curation) and the model-awareness problem (rewards are computed on the actual target generator's outputs), while DPO further sharpens the LLM's output distribution toward the specific model's preference landscape.

## Results

On the in-domain WebVid test set, Prompt-A-Video improves the average VideoScore from 2.989 to 3.056 for CogVideoX and from 3.116 to 3.318 for Open-Sora 1.2. On the out-of-domain VBench benchmark, the method raises the overall Quality Score from 80.98 to 83.28 (+2.30) for CogVideoX and from 78.26 to 79.78 (+1.52) for Open-Sora 1.2. In the text-to-image generalization experiment on HPSv2, Prompt-A-Video achieves an average score of 27.09, surpassing the base Stable Diffusion 1.4 (26.73) by +0.36 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Promptist (Hao et al., NeurIPS 2024), a text-to-image prompt boost model, Prompt-A-Video wins decisively on both video models: for CogVideoX it improves the WebVid average from 2.873 to 3.056 and VBench QS from 80.74 to 83.28, while for Open-Sora 1.2 it lifts the average from 2.959 to 3.318 and QS from 79.05 to 79.78; Promptist actually degrades quality relative to original prompts, confirming the modality-inconsistency issue. Against GLM-4 (Zeng et al., 2024), the LLM used by CogVideoX for prompt expansion, Prompt-A-Video achieves a +0.060 gain in WebVid average (3.056 vs 2.996) and a +1.28 gain in VBench QS (83.28 vs 82.00), with particularly large improvements in Dynamic Degree (+8.34) and Aesthetic Quality (+1.53). Against GPT-4o (Achiam et al., 2023), the LLM used by Open-Sora, Prompt-A-Video improves the WebVid average by +0.239 (3.318 vs 3.079) and VBench QS by +0.55 (79.78 vs 79.23). In the text-to-image generalization test on HPSv2, Prompt-A-Video (27.09) outperforms both PAE (Mo et al., CVPR 2024; 26.73) and Promptist (26.85), with the largest margin on photorealistic prompts where prior methods degrade below the base model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt optimization lineage from text-to-image (Promptist, PAE, OPT2I) to text-to-video, identifying three specific gaps—modality-inconsistency, cost-discrepancy, and model-unawareness—that make direct transfer of image prompt techniques ineffective. Methodologically, it combines evolutionary-algorithm-inspired prompt search (using GPT-4o as the evolutionary operator, building on EvoPrompt-style work) with a two-stage LLM training pipeline (SFT followed by DPO, building on Rafailov et al.'s preference optimization framework). The key conceptual departure is treating prompt refinement as a model-specific, reward-guided data generation problem rather than a generic text expansion task, producing a fine-tuned LLM that internalizes a particular video diffusion model's prompt preferences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic, automated framework for video-centric prompt optimization, removing the need for expert-crafted prompts or iterative manual trial-and-error when using text-to-video models. The finding that GPT-4o and GLM-4 (the very LLMs used by Open-Sora and CogVideoX for prompt expansion) fail to improve over original prompts, while a purpose-trained small LLM (LLaMA3 + LoRA) consistently outperforms them, suggests that generic in-context expansion is insufficient and model-specific preference alignment is necessary. Limitations include evaluation on only two video models, reliance on GPT-4o for data generation (introducing cost and potential bias), and the observation that DPO beyond two iterations causes metric degradation (e.g., factual consistency dropping sharply), raising open questions about stable multi-round preference optimization and scalability to future, more capable video models.
