## Problem

Existing 3D semantic Gaussian methods treat semantic segmentation and image rendering as separate branches, relying exclusively on 2D feature supervision while ignoring the intrinsic 3D geometry of the Gaussian representation. Additionally, adaptive Gaussian allocation strategies depend solely on rendering gradients, which are insufficient in textureless or geometrically subtle regions, leading to poor coverage and inefficient resource use.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251989/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images with known camera poses and pre-trained 2D semantic features (from LSeg) as distillation targets. It produces a 3D semantic Gaussian representation where each Gaussian carries an extended parameter set including a semantic feature vector, enabling dense per-pixel semantic segmentation from arbitrary viewpoints alongside photorealistic novel-view rendering at real-time frame rates (up to 164 FPS on RTX 3090). The local encoding operates within the current camera frustum to keep computation bounded.

## Method

The core contribution is an anisotropic 3D Gaussian Chebyshev descriptor that builds a discrete Laplace-Beltrami operator on Gaussian centers using a metric derived from each Gaussian's anisotropic covariance, then extracts spectral shape features via Chebyshev polynomials on scaled eigenvalues across multiple rotation angles to capture directional sensitivity. A transformer-based encoder aggregates these local descriptors, and a covariance-aware weighted propagation fuses the resulting shape encoding with semantic features through a learned gating mechanism. For the rendering branch, the fused semantic-shape features guide both Gaussian densification/pruning (via a soft mask with straight-through estimator) and per-Gaussian SH level selection (using differentiable soft histograms of local color complexity). A cross-scene knowledge transfer module maintains a low-rank pattern basis for transformer projection matrices, updated via SVD when projection residuals and local shape variance exceed a threshold, enabling faster convergence on new scenes without relearning shape priors.

## Results

On the Replica dataset, the method achieves 81.1 mIoU and 95.9 OA for segmentation, and 36.58 PSNR for rendering, outperforming Feature 3DGS (78.2 mIoU, 36.20 PSNR). On ScanNet open-vocabulary segmentation, it reaches 65.1 mIoU and 80.5 mAcc with the cross-scene knowledge transfer module, surpassing Semantic Gaussians (62.0 mIoU) and Feature 3DGS (59.2 mIoU). On Deep Blending, the method attains 50.6 mIoU, 29.86 PSNR, and 164 FPS, simultaneously improving segmentation accuracy over Feature 3DGS (46.2 mIoU) and rendering speed over EAGLES (119 FPS) and Compact 3DGS (152 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3DGS-based baselines, the method consistently wins: on ScanNet open-vocabulary it beats Semantic Gaussians by 2.1–3.1 mIoU and Feature 3DGS by 5.1–5.9 mIoU, while on Deep Blending it improves mIoU by 4.4 points over Feature 3DGS and gains 54 FPS over it (164 vs. 110). Against NeRF-based methods (Panoptic Lifting, LERF, PVLFF, DM-NeRF), the method achieves higher segmentation accuracy (65.1 vs. 52.9 mIoU on ScanNet open-vocab; 68.0 vs. 65.2 on closed-set) but trails on rendering PSNR in the closed-set ScanNet setting (27.49 vs. 28.50 for Panoptic Lifting), which the authors attribute to inherent 3DGS-vs-NeRF rendering differences on that dataset. Against image-based baselines (OpenSeg, LSeg, Mask2Former), the method substantially outperforms on 3D-consistent segmentation (65.1 vs. 56.1 mIoU on ScanNet; 67.4 vs. 46.7 on closed-set). On Deep Blending rendering speed, the method achieves 164 FPS, exceeding EAGLES (119 FPS) and Compact 3DGS (152 FPS) while matching EAGLES' PSNR (29.86) and surpassing Compact 3DGS (29.79). The cross-scene knowledge transfer module provides a consistent but modest boost of ~0.8 mIoU and ~0.8 mAcc on ScanNet, confirming the value of reusing learned shape patterns across scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3DGS semantic segmentation line (Feature 3DGS, Semantic Gaussians, LangSplat) by departing from the prevailing reliance on 2D feature distillation alone, instead injecting 3D shape information via anisotropic Laplace-Beltrami operator (ALBO)–based Chebyshev descriptors computed over Gaussian covariance. For the rendering branch, it moves beyond gradient-only adaptive strategies (EAGLES, Compact 3DGS, Scaffold-GS) by coupling semantic and shape cues to Gaussian pruning, densification, and spherical harmonics level selection. The cross-scene knowledge transfer module adapts the core knowledge space (CKS) framework from continual learning to 3D shape pattern reuse, a repurposing not previously seen in the 3DGS literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The joint semantic-rendering enhancement framing is practically useful: semantic cues that improve segmentation also make Gaussian management more targeted, yielding a 49% FPS gain over Feature 3DGS while improving mIoU. However, the rendering quality on ScanNet (PSNR 27.49) remains below established NeRF-based methods (28.50), and the cross-scene transfer contributes only ~0.8 mIoU, suggesting limited generalization beyond indoor scenes with shared geometry. Open questions include whether ALBO-based spectral encoding scales to very large outdoor scenes with millions of Gaussians, and whether the 2D supervision dependency (LSeg teacher) can be further reduced without sacrificing open-vocabulary performance.
