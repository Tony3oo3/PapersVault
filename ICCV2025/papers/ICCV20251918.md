## Problem

Existing 3D editing methods are constrained to predetermined viewing angles (typically the front view), making it impossible to perform fine-grained edits on occluded or less-visible regions of a 3D asset. Video 3D Diffusion Models (V3DMs) generate consistent dense multi-view sequences but accept only a single front-view input, so any edit must be applied to that front view before generation—preventing modifications at arbitrary viewpoints while preserving the object's identity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251918/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Edit360 accepts an editing instruction (text) plus one of three input formats: a text prompt (synthesized to a front view via a text-to-image model), a single RGB image, or an existing 3D model (rendered via Blender). The user also specifies or auto-selects (via LLM) an anchor view for the edit. The output is a dense multi-view image sequence (21 views at 576×576 with SV3D, or 18 views at 512×512 with V3D) that is reconstructed into an edited 3D asset using NeuS or 3D Gaussian Splatting. All inference is tuning-free and runs on a single RTX 4090 GPU.

## Method

Edit360 introduces an Anchor-View Editing Propagation mechanism that runs two parallel camera trajectories through a V3DM: an unedited front-view trajectory (preserving identity) and an anchor-view trajectory (carrying the 2D edit). Spatial Progressive Fusion (SPF) aligns the two trajectories via a circular-shift operation so that corresponding views share the same camera position, then blends them at each denoising timestep using a proximity-based weight α^i that decays with cyclic distance from the anchor view, prioritizing edits near the edited region. Cross-View Alignment (CVA) resolves residual structural conflicts by injecting key-value features from the front-view trajectory into the self-attention layers of the anchor-view trajectory, preventing ghosting artifacts when the two trajectories disagree on local geometry. In later diffusion stages, SPF additionally integrates edge and texture priors to mitigate over-smoothing. The entire pipeline requires no training or fine-tuning of the underlying V3DM.

## Results

On the GSO dataset, Edit-SV3D with dual-view input (v⁰ & vⁱ) achieves LPIPS 0.07, PSNR 22.17, and SSIM 0.90, outperforming the single-view SV3D baseline (LPIPS 0.09, PSNR 21.14, SSIM 0.87). With two ground-truth views as input, Edit-SV3D reaches PSNR 26.32 and SSIM 0.93 on GSO, demonstrating the upper bound of the framework. On OmniObject3D, Edit-SV3D (v⁰ & vⁱ) achieves LPIPS 0.08, PSNR 20.44, SSIM 0.89, again surpassing all single-view baselines. In the 3D editing user study, Edit360 scores 4.56/5 on geometric consistency and 4.49/5 on texture detail, substantially exceeding Tailor3D (3.32 and 2.77 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Edit360 outperforms all image-diffusion-based novel view synthesis baselines (Zero123, Zero123XL, Stable Zero123, Free3D, EscherNet) by a wide margin on both GSO and OmniObject3D, with PSNR gains of 3–6 dB over the best image-diffusion method (Stable Zero123: 18.34 vs. 22.17 on GSO). Against the closest video-diffusion baselines SV3D and V3D, Edit360 with dual-view input improves PSNR by ~1 dB (22.17 vs. 21.14 on GSO) and reduces LPIPS from 0.09 to 0.07, while also eliminating the Janus problem observed in both SV3D and V3D (e.g., duplicated front-view content on the back of a monitor). In the 3D editing user study, Edit360 substantially surpasses Tailor3D across all three dimensions: geometric consistency (4.56 vs. 3.32), texture detail (4.49 vs. 2.77), and overall quality (4.52 vs. 3.02), with the gap attributed to Edit360's ability to edit from arbitrary viewpoints rather than being restricted to front/back views. Qualitative comparisons in Figure 8 further show that Edit360 generates richer back-view details and avoids the multi-faced artifacts that plague SV3D and V3D.

## Relation to Prior Work

Edit360 builds directly on Video 3D Diffusion Models (V3DMs) such as SV3D and V3D, which generate dense 360-degree orbit sequences from a single front-view image, and repurposes their multi-view consistency for editing rather than generation. It departs from prior 3D editing methods (Tailor3D, Control3D, Generic 3D) that are restricted to fixed front/back viewpoints by introducing an arbitrary anchor-view trajectory and a dual-stream fusion mechanism (SPF + CVA) that merges edited and unedited view sequences at inference time. The approach is tuning-free, requiring no retraining of the underlying V3DM, and leverages off-the-shelf 2D editing models for the anchor-view modification step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling editing from any viewpoint without retraining, Edit360 removes a key practical bottleneck in 3D asset customization for animation, gaming, and VR pipelines. The tuning-free design means the framework can be immediately applied to any new V3DM as they emerge, without costly fine-tuning. Limitations include dependence on the quality of the base V3DM and the 2D editing model, and the evaluation is limited to single-object scenes with relatively simple edits; scaling to complex multi-object scenes or simultaneous multi-region edits remains open. The large gap in the '2GT input' setting (PSNR 26.32 vs 22.17) suggests significant headroom when higher-quality multi-view inputs are available, pointing toward future integration with stronger view synthesis or 3D-aware editing.
