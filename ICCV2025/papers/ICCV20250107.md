## Problem

Existing vision-based 3D occupancy prediction methods decompose the 2D-to-3D transformation into independently optimized modules—proxy depth supervision, fixed camera parameters, and static lifting maps—causing cascading errors and a failure mode the authors term *semantic ambiguity*, where 2D features of one class are erroneously projected to 3D locations of another. This modularity makes the pipeline vulnerable to real-world camera perturbations and prevents the transformation from being jointly optimized for the final semantic occupancy objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250107/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 6 surround-view RGB images at 256×704 resolution along with their camera intrinsic and extrinsic parameters, and produces a dense 3D semantic occupancy grid spanning −40 m to 40 m in X/Y and −1 m to 5.4 m in Z at 0.4 m voxel resolution with 18 semantic classes (17 object categories plus empty). It operates in single-frame mode (no temporal fusion) and is designed as a drop-in replacement for the LSS-based 2D-to-3D lifting stage within existing pipelines such as BEVDet and ALOcc, leaving the backbone and 3D decoder unchanged.

## Method

The core contribution is a gradient-based *causal loss* that enforces 2D-to-3D semantic causal locality: for each semantic class, the gradient of its aggregated 3D voxel features is backpropagated to the 2D feature map, yielding a per-class saliency map that is supervised with the corresponding 2D segmentation mask via binary cross-entropy; an unbiased single-class sampling strategy reduces the cost from S backward passes to one. To make the pipeline fully differentiable and stable under this supervision, the authors propose the Semantic Causality-Aware Transformation (SCAT) with three components: (i) Channel-Grouped Lifting, which replaces LSS's uniform depth weighting with distinct learnable weights per channel group to disentangle semantics; (ii) Learnable Camera Offsets, where two small networks predict a global camera-parameter offset and per-pixel coordinate offsets, trained implicitly through the causal loss via soft-filling (trilinear interpolation) for coordinate differentiability; and (iii) Normalized Convolution, a depthwise-pointwise decomposition with softmax-normalized spatial and channel kernels that densifies sparse lifted features while constraining gradient magnitudes to [0,1] for stable causal-loss optimization.

## Results

On the Occ3D benchmark (single-frame, ResNet-50, 256×704 input), ALOcc+Ours achieves the best mIoU of 40.9 (+0.8 over ALOcc) and mIoU_D of 35.5 (+1.1), while BEVDetOcc+Ours reaches mIoU 38.3 (+1.2 over BEVDetOcc) and IoU 71.2 (+1.2). Under Gaussian noise (σ=0.1) added to camera parameters, the method dramatically reduces performance degradation: BEVDetOcc+Ours drops only −7.3% mIoU versus −32.3% for vanilla BEVDetOcc, and ALOcc+Ours drops −3.3% versus −21.9% for ALOcc. Training latency increases modestly (e.g., 466 ms vs. 416 ms for BEVDetOcc on an RTX 4090).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is integrated into two LSS-based baselines (BEVDetOcc and ALOcc) and consistently outperforms them across all three metrics. ALOcc+Ours (40.9 mIoU) surpasses the strongest single-frame baseline ProtoOcc (39.6 mIoU) by 1.3 points and COTR (39.1 mIoU) by 1.8 points. BEVDetOcc+Ours (38.3 mIoU) beats FB-Occ (35.7), FlashOCC (32.0), and DHD-S (36.5) by 2.6, 6.3, and 1.8 mIoU respectively. The most striking comparison is under camera perturbation: vanilla BEVDetOcc suffers a −32.3% mIoU drop under Gaussian noise, while BEVDetOcc+Ours drops only −7.3%; similarly, ALOcc drops −21.9% versus −3.3% for ALOcc+Ours. The method does not directly compete with attention-based or rendering-based approaches (e.g., TPVFormer, MonoScene) as it targets the LSS lifting pipeline specifically, but its plug-and-play nature means it could in principle be combined with those architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Lift-Splat-Shoot (LSS) paradigm used in BEVDet and ALOcc for vision-based 3D occupancy prediction, departing from the standard modular design where depth estimation, camera calibration, and feature lifting are each supervised or fixed independently. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a gradient-based causal loss that treats 2D image semantics as the 'cause' of 3D semantic predictions, thereby making the entire 2D-to-3D transformation differentiable and end-to-end trainable. The SCAT module (Channel-Grouped Lifting, Learnable Camera Offsets, Normalized Convolution) is a targeted redesign of the LSS lifting step, drawing on soft-filling from ALOcc and depthwise/pointwise convolution decompositions from MobileNet/ConvNeXt, but unified under the constraint that gradient flow must remain in [0,1] for stable causal supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the dramatic robustness improvement under camera parameter noise (mIoU drop reduced from -32.3% to -7.3% on BEVDet), which is directly relevant to real-world autonomous driving where sensor calibration drifts. The theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) formalizes why fixed 2D-to-3D mappings inherently limit gradient-based optimization, providing a principled justification for end-to-end learnable transformations. However, absolute performance gains are modest (1.2 mIoU on BEVDet, 0.8 on ALOcc), evaluation is limited to a single benchmark (Occ3D) with two backbone architectures, and the causal loss's applicability to attention-based 2D-to-3D methods (e.g., TPVFormer) or multi-frame settings remains unexplored. An open question is whether the causal loss principle generalizes beyond LSS-style lifting to other geometric transformation paradigms.
