## Problem

Existing physical adversarial camouflage methods for vehicle detection rely on pre-built mesh models and simulator-constructed environments (e.g., CARLA) that inevitably diverge from real-world conditions, and their limited training backgrounds cause optimization to fall into sub-optimal solutions with poor multi-view robustness. Additionally, vanilla 3D representations suffer from cross-view rendering inconsistencies due to mutual and self-occlusion, further degrading the physical deployability of generated camouflage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250765/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of multi-view RGB images of the target object (e.g., 282 drone-captured images for a 1:1 vehicle) plus a set of camera viewpoints at varying distances, pitch, and azimuth angles, along with SAM segmentation prompts to isolate the target region. It outputs optimized zero-order spherical harmonics coefficients on the 3D Gaussian primitives, from which an adversarial camouflage mesh and printable texture are extracted for physical deployment via stickers. Only surface color is optimized while geometry and shape remain fixed, making the output directly transferable to physical fabrication.

## Method

PGA replaces mesh-based differentiable renderers with 3D Gaussian Splatting as the rendering backbone, enabling rapid scene reconstruction from a few images and fast photo-realistic multi-view rendering during iterative attack. To resolve cross-view inconsistency, the framework applies SuGaR regularization to align Gaussians with the object surface (preventing mutual occlusion) and restricts optimization to only the zero-order spherical harmonic term (preventing self-occlusion from higher-order color variation). The core attack uses a min-max optimization: at each iteration, I-FGSM adds bounded pixel-level noise to the background to maximize detection loss, then the camouflage is updated to minimize that loss, filtering out non-robust adversarial features. Sequential per-viewpoint optimization with early stopping prevents over-fitting to easier viewpoints, while EoT augmentations, Non-Printability Score, and K-means-based primary color regularization further improve physical robustness and visual naturalness.

## Results

In the digital domain (CARLA), PGA achieves the lowest AP@0.5 across all four distances (5 m–20 m), with average values of 23.46 %, 21.78 %, 25.53 %, and 20.73 % respectively, compared to the next-best baseline RAUCA at 37.16 %, 39.25 %, 51.30 %, and 49.50 %. In physical experiments on a 1:24 scale toy car, PGA attains 28.69 % and 28.26 % average AP@0.5 at 50 cm and 100 cm, outperforming all baselines. On a 1:1 real vehicle (GOLF Sportsvan), PGA reduces Faster R-CNN AP@0.5 from 88.48 % to 25.67 % using only printed stickers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PGA outperforms all six baselines (DAS, FCA, DTA, ACTIVE, TAS, RAUCA) on every distance and weather setting in the digital domain. The closest competitor is RAUCA, yet PGA still achieves substantially lower AP@0.5: at 5 m the gap is 13.7 percentage points (23.46 % vs 37.16 %), and it widens to 25.8 points at 15 m (25.53 % vs 51.30 %) and 28.8 points at 20 m (20.73 % vs 49.50 %), indicating PGA's advantage grows with distance. On the white-box Faster R-CNN, PGA reduces AP@0.5 to near-zero at all distances (4.52 %, 1.40 %, 1.95 %, 1.85 %), while the best baseline (RAUCA) only reaches 21.71 % at 5 m and 37.29 % at 20 m. In the 1:24 physical experiment, PGA again leads with 28.69 % and 28.26 % average AP@0.5 at 50 cm and 100 cm, beating RAUCA (36.69 %, 37.25 %) by roughly 8–9 points. Transferability to YOLO-V5, Mask R-CNN, and Deformable-DETR follows the same pattern, with PGA consistently achieving the lowest scores across all four detectors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to employ 3D Gaussian Splatting as the differentiable rendering backbone for physical adversarial camouflage, replacing the mesh-based renderers (Neural 3D Mesh Renderer, DTA) and NeRF-based approaches (Adv3D, Huang et al.) used by prior methods like DAS, FCA, DTA, ACTIVE, TAS, and RAUCA. It inherits surface-alignment regularization from SuGaR to address 3DGS-specific occlusion artifacts, and introduces a min-max background perturbation strategy (via I-FGSM) that has no direct analogue in the prior camouflage literature, which typically relies on simple EoT augmentations. Conceptually, the work sits at the intersection of 3D scene reconstruction and adversarial optimization, repurposing a rendering technology originally designed for novel view synthesis as an attack tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the dependency on pre-built mesh models and simulator environments, PGA lowers the practical barrier to generating physical adversarial camouflage for arbitrary objects, which is a meaningful step toward real-world threat assessment for autonomous driving. The multi-view robustness gains (AP@0.5 dropping to ~20–25% vs. ~45–75% for baselines) are substantial, but the physical validation is limited to a single 1:1 vehicle and a 1:24 toy car, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to diverse object categories, pedestrian detection, or adversarially trained detectors. A key open question is whether the min-max background optimization, which adds computational overhead per viewpoint, scales to the hundreds of viewpoints needed for truly universal camouflage, and whether defenders can exploit the zero-order spherical harmonic constraint to develop targeted defenses.
