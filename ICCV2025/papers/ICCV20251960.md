## Problem

Existing talking head generation methods are limited to single-signal control (audio or facial motion), and applying both simultaneously causes control conflicts where one signal dominates overlapping facial regions. Additionally, standard attention-based integration of control signals in video diffusion models processes temporal and spatial dimensions separately, missing cross-dimensional interactions and suffering from quadratic token complexity for longer videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251960/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source image (encoded via VAE and ArcFace for identity), an audio segment (embedded via Whisper), a sequence of facial motion images (embedded via a pre-trained motion encoder), and a face mask, and produces a talking head video of arbitrary length within memory constraints. A key design choice is that the gate status is manually set at inference time to select audio-only, motion-only, or combined audio-visual control, while during training the gate is randomly sampled across all three configurations to learn flexible controllability.

## Method

ACTalker builds on Stable Video Diffusion and introduces a Parallel-Control Mamba Layer (PCM) in each block, consisting of two parallel branches (audio and motion), each containing a Mask-SSM unit. The Mask-SSM flattens spatio-temporal feature tokens, applies a mask-drop strategy to retain only tokens within the signal's designated facial region, concatenates them with the driving signal embedding, and processes them through a selective state-space model that aggregates information across both spatial and temporal dimensions in linear time. A gating mechanism randomly activates one or both branches during training, and the branch outputs are combined via addition and residual connection. An identity embedding is injected into each branch to preserve the subject's identity during region-specific manipulation, and the mask-drop strategy ensures that audio only affects the mouth region while motion controls the broader expression, resolving control conflicts.

## Results

On CelebV-HQ, ACTalker (Audio-Visual) achieves the best lip-sync scores (Sync-C 5.737, Sync-D 7.510) and the lowest FVD-Inc (230.125) and FID (29.977) among all methods, outperforming the second-best Memo by ~32 points on FVD-Inc. On RAVDESS, the Audio-Visual variant similarly leads with Sync-C 5.511, Sync-D 7.311, FVD-Inc 190.125, and FID 15.977. For face reenactment on VFHQ, ACTalker achieves the best Expression Similarity (0.57 vs. 0.48 for X-Portrait) and ID Similarity (8.64 vs. 8.57), while also attaining the lowest LMD (0.14) and LPIPS (0.24).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACTalker outperforms all six audio-driven baselines (SadTalker, Hallo, VExpress, EDTalk, EchoMimic, Memo) on every metric for both CelebV-HQ and RAVDESS. The closest competitor is Memo, which achieves the best FVD-Res (7.992) on CelebV-HQ but trails ACTalker by ~32 points on FVD-Inc (264.596 vs. 232.374) and by 1.36 on Sync-C (3.958 vs. 5.317). EDTalk is the only method that approaches ACTalker on Sync-C (5.124 vs. 5.317) but falls significantly behind on FVD-Inc (430.906 vs. 232.374) and FID (50.428 vs. 30.721). For face reenactment on VFHQ, ACTalker beats X-Portrait (the strongest reenactment baseline) by 9% in Expression Similarity (0.57 vs. 0.48) and by 0.07 in ID Similarity (8.64 vs. 8.57), while also achieving the lowest LMD (0.14 vs. 0.24) and LPIPS (0.24 vs. 0.27). LivePortrait and AniPortrait lag further behind on expression fidelity (0.38 and 0.46 respectively) and LMD (0.49 and 0.68). The Audio-Visual variant consistently edges out the Audio-only variant across all metrics, confirming the benefit of multi-signal control.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) as its backbone and introduces the Mamba selective state-space model as a replacement for attention-based cross-attention layers to aggregate spatio-temporal feature tokens with driving signals. It departs from prior talking-head methods (Hallo, X-Portrait, Loopy, Memo) that handle only a single driving modality at inference time, by proposing a parallel-control mamba layer with a gating mechanism that enables simultaneous audio and facial-motion control. The mask-drop strategy is a novel architectural choice that assigns disjoint spatial regions to each signal branch, directly addressing the control-conflict problem that prior unified frameworks (e.g., EmoPortraits, StyleHEAT) leave unresolved.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Simultaneous multi-signal control (audio for lip sync + expression for full-face dynamics) is a practical requirement for production virtual avatars and human-computer interaction, yet prior methods force a choice between signals at inference. The mask-drop + parallel-branch design offers a clean, interpretable mechanism for signal disentanglement that could generalize to additional control modalities (e.g., gaze, hand gestures). Limitations include reliance on manually specified masks, the SVD backbone's resolution constraints, and evaluation on relatively small test sets (100 videos from CelebV-HQ); whether the SSM-based aggregation scales to longer videos or higher resolutions remains open.
