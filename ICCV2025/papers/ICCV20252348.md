## Problem

Existing training-free image editing methods (inversion-denoising, attention modification, improved samplers) can only achieve approximate background similarity rather than pixel-perfect preservation, while training-based inpainting models that do preserve backgrounds incur expensive training costs. The paper identifies three root causes of background drift—discretization error accumulation, new text conditions, and new foreground content—and asks whether a training-free approach can eliminate all three simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252348/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, a binary user-provided mask delineating the foreground (editing) region from the background (preservation) region, and a text prompt describing the desired edit. It operates in the latent space of a DiT-based flow model (FLUX.1-dev) over 28 timesteps (24 effective with skip), and produces an edited image in which masked-out background pixels are bit-identical to the input while the masked-in foreground contains newly generated content aligned with the prompt. No additional training or fine-tuning is required; the method is plug-and-play for any DiT-based generative model.

## Method

KV-Edit implements a KV cache mechanism within DiT's attention blocks to preserve background tokens exactly. During inversion, the key-value pairs of background tokens are cached at every timestep and every attention layer; during denoising, only foreground tokens are processed as queries, while their keys and values are concatenated with the cached background KV, so the background is never regenerated. This attention decoupling is enabled by DiT's pure-attention architecture (unlike UNet's convolutions, which spatially fuse foreground and background). Two optional enhancements address object removal: mask-guided inversion (an attention mask prevents foreground information from leaking into background KV during inversion) and reinitialization (fusing the inverted noise with fresh Gaussian noise to disrupt residual object information). A memory-efficient inversion-free variant reduces KV cache storage from O(N) to O(1) by computing and discarding KV pairs after a single step rather than storing them across all timesteps.

## Results

On PIE-Bench, KV-Edit achieves the best background preservation among all editing methods with PSNR 35.87, LPIPS 9.92, and MSE 4.69, substantially outperforming the next-best training-based methods (BrushEdit: PSNR 32.16, LPIPS 17.22; FLUX Fill: PSNR 32.53, LPIPS 25.59). For image quality, KV-Edit scores HPS 27.21 and AS 6.49, ranking third overall behind RF-Inversion (HPS 27.99) and RF-Edit (HPS 27.60), while the +NS+RI variant reaches the best HPS of 28.05. For text alignment, the +NS+RI variant achieves the highest Image Reward score of 9.15, compared to 5.63 for the base method and 5.71 for FLUX Fill. In the user study, KV-Edit wins 94.8% of background preservation comparisons against RF-Inversion and 66.7% against BrushEdit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KV-Edit decisively outperforms all training-free baselines on background preservation: PSNR of 35.87 vs. 17.86 (P2P), 22.20 (MasaCtrl), 20.20 (RF-Inversion), and 24.44 (RF-Edit), with LPIPS of 9.92 versus 105–208 for those methods. Against training-based inpainting models, KV-Edit still leads on background metrics (PSNR 35.87 vs. 32.16 for BrushEdit and 32.53 for FLUX Fill; LPIPS 9.92 vs. 17.22 and 25.59), while being competitive on image quality (HPS 27.21 vs. 25.81 and 25.76). On text alignment, the base KV-Edit method (IR 5.63) is slightly below FLUX Fill (5.71) but the +NS+RI variant (IR 9.15) surpasses all baselines. RF-Inversion achieves the highest image quality (HPS 27.99, AS 6.74) but completely fails at background preservation (PSNR 20.20), confirming the paper's claim that prior methods trade off quality against consistency. In the user study, KV-Edit wins 85.1% overall satisfaction against RF-Inversion and 70.2% against BrushEdit, with background preservation being the dominant factor in user preference.

## Relation to Prior Work

KV-Edit departs from the dominant inversion-denoising paradigm (P2P, MasaCtrl, RF-Edit) that achieves only approximate background similarity through attention modification or improved samplers, and from training-based inpainting models (BrushEdit, FLUX-Fill) that enforce consistency at the cost of expensive fine-tuning. Instead, it borrows the KV cache mechanism from LLM autoregressive generation and applies it to DiT-based flow models, treating background tokens as fixed context that is never regenerated. The memory-efficient variant further builds on the inversion-free editing framework of FlowEdit and related work, reducing KV storage from O(N) to O(1) by computing and discarding cached values after a single step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that perfect background preservation is achievable by simply not regenerating background tokens, rather than fighting the model to produce similar output—reframes a long-standing trade-off in editing as an architectural choice enabled by DiT's pure attention structure. This opens the door to composable, training-free editing pipelines on any DiT-based model without hyperparameter tuning per case. Limitations include content-retention artifacts in the inversion-free variant, quality degradation when enhancement strategies (no-skip, reinitialization, attention mask) are applied to large masks, and evaluation restricted to a single base model (FLUX) and one benchmark (PIE-Bench). The authors suggest extensions to video editing and multi-concept personalization, but these remain unexplored.
