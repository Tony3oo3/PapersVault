## Problem

3D reconstruction and simulation have fundamentally different representation requirements: reconstruction demands a flexible 3D representation adaptable to diverse scenes, while simulation requires a structured representation to model deformation principles. Existing methods either lack surface and physical priors (implicit NeRF-based approaches) or sacrifice rendering quality and speed (explicit mesh-based approaches), making it difficult to achieve a unified framework that excels at both tasks simultaneously for dynamic objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251222/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video as input for reconstruction and user-defined deformations (via ARAP, SMPL, or soft-body simulation tools like Blender/Taichi) for simulation. It produces a temporally consistent triangular mesh (~1K faces, deliberately kept small), Mesh-adsorbed 3D Gaussians parameterized by barycentric coordinates and normal offsets on mesh facets, and rendered novel views or simulation results. Evaluation resolutions are 400×400 for D-NeRF/DG-Mesh and 1080×1080 for PeopleSnapshot; for PeopleSnapshot, optimized SMPL models from InstantAvatar serve as guide meshes rather than TSDF-extracted geometry.

## Method

MaGS introduces a hybrid mesh-Gaussian representation where each 3D Gaussian is parameterized by barycentric coordinates (b) and a normal-direction offset (h) on a specific mesh facet, allowing Gaussians to 'roam' near the surface (Gaussian Hover) rather than being rigidly anchored. Three networks handle deformation: MPE-Net extracts pose embeddings from Poisson-disk-sampled handle vertices of the guide mesh (eliminating temporal inputs), RMD-Net predicts relative vertex displacements to refine mesh deformations, and RGD-Net predicts per-Gaussian changes in barycentric coordinates and offset to maintain rendering fidelity under mesh constraints. The entire pipeline is jointly optimized end-to-end via differentiable splatting rendering with L1+SSIM loss, and the learned networks are reused at simulation time to refine arbitrary user-defined mesh deformations without requiring temporal information, enabling generalization to out-of-distribution motions.

## Results

On the D-NeRF dataset, MaGS achieves an average PSNR of 44.06, MS-SSIM of 0.9985, and LPIPS of 0.0040, surpassing the second-best method SC-GS (43.31 PSNR) by 0.75 dB and Grid4D (42.56 PSNR) by 1.5 dB. On DG-Mesh, MaGS attains 40.76 PSNR and the best EMD (0.1106) while using only ~1K mesh faces (vs. ~170K for DG-Mesh and ~1.4M for Dynamic 2DGS) and optimizing in 47.6 minutes. On PeopleSnapshot, MaGS delivers an approximately 2 dB average PSNR improvement over SplattingAvatar, and on Nerfies it outperforms SC-GS by 1.1 dB on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaGS outperforms all 13 baselines on the D-NeRF average PSNR, with the closest competitor being SC-GS (43.31) and Grid4D (42.56); the gap over SC-GS is 0.75 dB and over Grid4D is 1.5 dB. Among mesh-based methods, MaGS dominates: it beats D-MiSo by 2.44 dB, DynaSurfGS by 8.43 dB, and DG-Mesh by 14.18 dB in PSNR, while also achieving the best LPIPS (0.0040 vs. 0.0087, 0.0221, 0.0543 respectively). On DG-Mesh, MaGS achieves the best PSNR (40.76) and EMD (0.1106) but is slightly behind Dynamic 2DGS in Chamfer Distance (0.6662 vs. 0.5254), though it uses ~1,450× fewer faces and trains 1.5× faster. On PeopleSnapshot, MaGS wins all four subjects in PSNR and SSIM over SplattingAvatar (the strongest baseline), with gains of 2.46, 1.21, 1.95, and 2.58 dB respectively. On Nerfies, MaGS leads on three of four scenes (Tobysit, Broom, Curls) but trails D-3DGS slightly on Tail (24.2 vs. 24.6), while decisively outperforming SC-GS by 5.8 dB on average.

## Relation to Prior Work

MaGS extends the mesh-Gaussian binding paradigm established by SuGaR, DG-Mesh, and GaMeS, but departs from their fixed-anchoring design by introducing a learnable relative displacement (RGD-Net) that lets Gaussians roam near rather than stick to mesh facets. It builds on deformation-field-based dynamic 3DGS (Deformable-GS, 4D-GS) for the rendering backbone and on TSDF integration for initial mesh extraction, but replaces temporal encoding with a geometry-based Mesh Pose Embedding Network (MPE-Net) to decouple deformation from video timestamps. This design choice is the key conceptual departure: prior unified reconstruction-simulation methods (D-MiSo, GaMeS, Mani-GS) either rely on temporal cues or fixed mesh-Gaussian bindings, whereas MaGS learns motion priors from mesh geometry alone, enabling generalization to user-defined deformations outside the training video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying high-fidelity dynamic reconstruction and physics-based simulation in a single differentiable pipeline, MaGS removes the need for separate reconstruction and simulation stages, and its compatibility with ARAP, SMPL, and soft-body simulation makes it a practical tool for content creation and virtual interaction. The finding that a ~1K-face mesh suffices to guide deformations (versus 170K–1.4M faces in prior mesh-GS methods) suggests that mesh complexity is far less critical than previously assumed for deformation guidance. However, the method cannot handle topology-changing deformations (e.g., fluid dynamics), and its reliance on a pre-extracted temporally consistent mesh limits applicability to scenes with large topological changes. Open questions include whether the MPE-Net generalizes to multi-object scenes and whether the Gaussian Hover mechanism can be extended to handle occluded or thin structures.
