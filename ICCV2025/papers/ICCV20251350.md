## Problem

Classifier-free guidance (CFG) in text-to-image models requires two forward passes per denoising step, and when combined with complex sampling strategies like W2SD or Z-Sampling, inference cost escalates to roughly 6× that of a single Euler step. Existing guidance distillation methods either introduce extra network parameters (e.g., an MLP to encode the guidance scale), are overly complex to implement at scale, or have not been validated on industrial-grade DiT architectures such as SD3 and FLUX.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251350/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the distilled student model accepts a text prompt and a user-chosen guidance magnitude w (trained over the range [2, 14]) and produces a single generated image using only one forward pass with simple Euler sampling—no CFG, no reflection. The guidance magnitude w is not fed as a separate conditioning channel but is instead injected into the text embedding via a sinusoidal encoding, so the model's input interface remains architecturally identical to the undistilled model. Training uses high-resolution LAION-5B images (aesthetic score > 6) with AdamW at an effective batch size of 128.

## Method

TeEFusion's key insight is that linear combinations of text embeddings in the embedding space can effectively merge or suppress semantic components of a prompt, validated by a masking experiment showing that additively fusing embeddings of preserved and masked prompt parts reproduces (or surpasses) the original prompt's output. The method moves the CFG linear combination from the prediction space—(1+w)ε(x_t,c) − wε(x_t)—forward into the text embedding space, yielding a fused embedding ẑ = G(ψ(t)) + F(c) + G(ψ(w))·F(c−∅), where the extra term encodes w via the same sinusoidal timestep encoder G(ψ(·)) and multiplies it with the conditional–unconditional embedding difference. This design introduces zero additional parameters or hyperparameters; the student architecture is identical to the teacher. The student is trained with an L2 loss to match the teacher's denoised outputs produced under a complex reflection-based sampling strategy (W2SD+CFG), thereby simultaneously distilling both the guidance signal and the sampling procedure into a single Euler step.

## Results

On HPS v2 aesthetic scores, TeEFusion (1× inference cost) matches or exceeds the W2SD+CFG teacher (6× cost) across all four prompt categories for both SD3 and In-house T2I — e.g., on In-house T2I, TeEFusion scores 32.39/31.34/31.27/29.96 (Anime/Concept-Art/Paintings/Photo) versus the teacher's 32.23/31.29/31.22/29.93. On DPG-Bench, TeEFusion achieves 84.56% overall (SD3) and 84.13% overall (In-house T2I), surpassing DistillCFG (84.13% and 83.52%) but remaining slightly below the W2SD+CFG teacher (86.56% and 85.20%). The student model runs at 1× forward-pass cost versus the teacher's 6×, yielding up to 6× faster inference while preserving image quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole quantitative baseline is DistillCFG [20], which encodes the guidance scale via an additional MLP. TeEFusion outperforms DistillCFG on every reported metric for both SD3 and In-house T2I: on HPS v2, TeEFusion leads by 0.5–1.6 points per category (e.g., 32.39 vs. 30.80 on Anime for In-house T2I); on DPG-Bench overall, TeEFusion scores 84.56% (SD3) and 84.13% (I.-T2I) versus DistillCFG's 84.13% and 83.52%; and on CLIP score, TeEFusion achieves 34.63 (SD3) and 33.81 (I.-T2I) versus 34.53 and 33.38. Against the W2SD+CFG teacher itself, TeEFusion matches or slightly exceeds HPS scores at 1/6 the inference cost, while trailing by roughly 1–2 points on DPG-Bench overall composition. The paper also notes that TeEFusion is more stable at high guidance scales (w > 10), where W2SD+CFG exhibits periodic artifacts, explaining part of the aesthetic advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

TeEFusion builds directly on Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) by Ho & Salimans, whose linear combination of conditional and unconditional predictions (Eq. 2) is the core formulation that TeEFusion relocates from the output space into the text embedding space. The most direct prior work is DistillCFG (Meng et al., CVPR 2023), which encodes the guidance scale via an additional MLP; TeEFusion departs from this by eliminating all extra parameters and instead fusing conditional and unconditional text embeddings with a sinusoidally-encoded guidance coefficient. A key conceptual departure from the broader distillation literature (progressive distillation, DMD, etc.) is that TeEFusion explicitly distills complex teacher sampling strategies (W2SD, Z-Sampling) into a simple Euler student, a scenario prior methods largely ignored by assuming identical samplers for teacher and student.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6× inference speedup with near-lossless quality on industrial-scale DiT models (SD3, 2B params) makes this a practically attractive option for deployment where latency is critical, and the zero-parameter, zero-hyperparameter design lowers the barrier to adoption significantly. The paper validates the approach across multiple benchmarks (HPS, DPG-Bench, CLIP, PickScore, ImageReward) and two model architectures, which is notably more thorough than prior distillation work that lacked large-scale validation. Limitations include occasional semantic inconsistencies (mismatched attributes) and imperfect alignment with the teacher, and the method's scalability to even larger models or more complex multi-step sampling strategies remains to be tested. An open question is whether the near-linear structure of text embeddings that enables this fusion generalizes to other conditioning modalities (e.g., image conditioning, multi-modal prompts).
