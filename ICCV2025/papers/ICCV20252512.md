## Problem

Neural video codecs (NVCs) have advanced to near state-of-the-art performance for perspective video but remain entirely unexplored for the increasingly important 360-degree video format. Two key obstacles hinder progress: the absence of large-scale 360-degree training datasets and the lack of methods that exploit the spherical geometry of 360-degree content. The paper demonstrates that naive finetuning on 360-degree data significantly underperforms a purpose-built optimization framework, motivating a structured approach to 360-degree NVC design.

## Input / Output

Training consumes 256×256 patches from 9-frame sequences in equirectangular projection (ERP) format at resolutions spanning 640×320 to 7680×4320, with 7 consecutive frames per sample (first frame intra-coded) in RGB color space. At inference, the positional feature encoding is independently constructed by both encoder and decoder from the known frame height and width, requiring zero additional bitstream transmission. Evaluation is performed in YUV color space using WS-PSNR for 360-degree sequences and conventional PSNR for perspective sequences, with Bjontegaard Delta rate as the primary metric.

## Method

The framework extends DCVC-HEM with three components: the UGC360 dataset (6866 user-generated 360-degree clips), flow-guided reprojection (FGR) for spherical data augmentation, and a positional feature encoding in the entropy model. FGR augments training by sampling a source patch center within a flow guide (SPyNet optical flow magnitude above a threshold) and a target patch center uniformly across the image, projecting both onto the unit sphere, computing a 3D rotation matrix to align them, and reprojecting the source frame with mipmapped bilinear interpolation to extract the target patch. The positional feature encoding adds a single non-learnable channel to the entropy model input, computed as cos((v−V/2)·π/V) for ERP (encoding each pixel's relative area on the unit sphere) and set to 1 for perspective video, extending convolution kernels by one channel. This lightweight addition (~5K parameters) enables the entropy model to perform dynamic bitrate allocation, spending fewer bits on geometrically distorted polar regions while preserving quality in equatorial viewports, yielding ~8% rate savings on 360-degree video and >3% on perspective video with negligible complexity overhead.

## Results

Using DCVC-HEM-360 (with positional feature encoding) and flow-guided reprojection, trained on UGC360+vimeo90k, the paper achieves a BD-Rate saving of 7.73% on the 360-degree JVET360 dataset and 3.40% average on perspective video (HEVC B/C/D/E + UVG), relative to the DCVC-HEM baseline finetuned on vimeo90k. The complexity overhead is negligible: only 5K additional parameters (17.528M vs 17.523M) and 1 ms extra decoding time (90 ms vs 89 ms) on an RTX 3090 at 1024×512. The ablation confirms flow-guided reprojection is essential—disabling it costs 1.53% (360°) to 6.88% (perspective) in BD-Rate depending on configuration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the DCVC-HEM baseline (finetuned on vimeo90k), the proposed DCVC-HEM-360 with flow-guided reprojection achieves 7.73% BD-Rate savings on JVET360 and 3.40% average savings across all perspective datasets, turning what would be losses (up to 11.74% on HEVC-B without FGR) into consistent gains. Compared to the traditional HEVC standard (HM-18.0), the neural codec is dramatically superior on both 360-degree video (−7.73% vs +22.83%) and perspective video (−3.40% vs +8.27% average). Against the VVC standard (VTM-22.2), the neural codec is still substantially behind on perspective video (−3.40% vs −20.34% average), but narrows the gap considerably on 360-degree video (−7.73% vs −10.12%), suggesting the neural approach is more competitive in the 360-degree domain where VVC's planar coding tools are less effective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DCVC-HEM neural video codec (Li et al., ACM MM 2022) to the 360-degree video domain, a setting the authors identify as entirely unexplored in the NVC literature. It departs from prior 360-degree image compression work (Li et al. 2022, Zheng et al. 2024, Gungordu & Tekalp 2024) by addressing the temporal dimension and by ensuring no regression on perspective video, whereas those methods were 360-degree-only. The flow-guided reprojection data augmentation is novel, leveraging the spherical geometry of ERP content in a way that has no direct analogue in perspective video training pipelines, while the positional feature encoding draws on the WS-PSNR weighting function but repurposes it as a learnable-free input channel to the entropy model rather than as a loss function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The UGC360 dataset (6,866 clips, 0.5K–8K) fills a critical gap that has blocked NVC research on 360-degree video, and the flow-guided reprojection technique is broadly applicable to any NVC trained on spherical content. The finding that joint 360-degree + perspective training yields mutual gains (≈8% savings on 360-degree, >3% on perspective) challenges the assumption that domain-specific optimization must sacrifice performance on the other domain. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to newer NVC architectures (DCVC-DC, DCVC-FM) whose training procedures remain closed, and whether it generalizes to other projection formats beyond ERP, which the authors flag as future work.
