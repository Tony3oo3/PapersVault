## Problem

Text-guided graphics program synthesis (e.g., generating TikZ code from captions) requires aligned caption-program pairs for end-to-end training, but such data is extremely scarce—fewer than 170k of over 450k available TikZ programs have captions. Meanwhile, large pools of unaligned graphics programs and captioned images exist separately, yet prior methods cannot exploit them jointly, limiting model performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251516/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text caption describing a scientific figure and autoregressively generates a complete TikZ graphics program as output. Internally, the caption is encoded by a LLaMA 3.2 1B text encoder, and the inverse graphics VLM processes images at 420×420 pixels through a SIGLIP SoViT vision encoder. The generated TikZ code is compiled to a raster image for evaluation, with resampling triggered if compilation fails (tracked via Mean Token Efficiency).

## Method

TikZero decouples graphics program generation from text understanding via a two-stage architecture. First, a VLM (LLaMA 3.1 8B decoder + SIGLIP vision encoder, initialized from PaliGemma weights) is trained as an inverse graphics model that generates TikZ programs from image patch embeddings. Second, a lightweight adapter network (2B parameters, 400M trainable) with sigmoid-gated cross-attention layers inserted before each vision encoder layer is trained to map caption embeddings to synthetic patch embeddings via knowledge distillation from the frozen vision encoder, using only captioned images (ArxivCap, 6.4M pairs) and no caption-program pairs. A trainable probe replaces image input, and the text decoder is not loaded during adapter training, enabling efficient training. At inference, the adapter converts a caption into patch-level embeddings that condition the text decoder, achieving zero-shot text-guided synthesis. An optional fine-tuning stage (TikZero+) further incorporates the small caption-program intersection with weight resetting to surpass GPT-4o on several metrics.

## Results

On the DATikZv3 test set (1k captioned samples), TikZero (Cos) achieves the best zero-shot AVG score of 85.6, outperforming the strongest end-to-end baseline AUTOMATIKZv2 (LLM) at 82.4 by 3.2 points. When augmented with end-to-end fine-tuning and weight resetting (TikZero+), the model reaches an AVG of 87.0, surpassing GPT-4o (79.0) by 8 points and Qwen2.5-Coder 32B (48.6) by 38.5 points. On individual metrics, TikZero+ leads on cBLEU (1.988 vs. GPT-4o's 0.327), KID (1.831 vs. 2.844), and DREAMSIM (56.3 vs. 56.5, near-tie), while GPT-4o retains an edge on CLIPScore (31.8 vs. 24.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TikZero (Cos) zero-shot outperforms IDEFICS3 (8B) by ~19 pp and AUTOMATIKZ (13B) by ~22 pp on AVG, with particularly large margins on cBLEU (1.60 vs. 0.66 and 0.39 respectively), confirming that patch-level conditioning yields substantially better code generation than token-level conditioning. Against the directly comparable end-to-end baseline AUTOMATIKZv2 (LLM), TikZero wins on DREAMSIM (+2.3 pp), cBLEU (+1.0), and MTE (+0.5 pp) but loses on KID (−1.6 pp) and CLIPScore (−5.7 pp); the CLIPScore gap is shown to be largely an artifact of string copying rather than genuine visual quality. The enhanced TikZero+ (Weight Resetting) surpasses GPT-4o by 8 pp on AVG, leading on cBLEU (1.99 vs. 0.33), KID (1.83 vs. 2.84), and TED (59.0 vs. 58.5), while GPT-4o retains advantages on CLIPScore (31.8 vs. 24.2) and MTE (97.7 vs. 93.1). Against Qwen2.5-Coder (32B), TikZero+ leads by 38.5 pp on AVG, with GPT-4o's estimated 1.8T parameters being 180× larger than TikZero's 10B, underscoring the data-efficiency of the decoupled training strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

TikZero builds directly on DeTikZify's inverse graphics model (a VLM that generates TikZ programs from rasterized images) and adapts Flamingo's gated cross-attention architecture as a text-conditioned adapter that mimics the vision encoder's patch-level output space. Conceptually, it follows the two-stage prior-and-decoder paradigm of DALL-E 2 but operates at patch-level embeddings rather than global CLIP latents, which the authors argue is critical for inverse graphics models that require fine-grained spatial detail. The key departure from AutoTikZ (the prior text-guided TikZ synthesis work) is the decoupling of training: rather than requiring scarce caption-program pairs, TikZero trains the inverse graphics model on all available programs and the text adapter on all available captioned images independently, reconciling them only at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DATikZv3 dataset (450k+ TikZ programs, ~170k captioned) is a substantial open resource that addresses a genuine data bottleneck in scientific figure generation, and the decoupled training principle—aligning representation spaces rather than requiring aligned data—generalizes beyond TikZ to any low-resource multimodal synthesis task. TikZero+ matching GPT-4o on key metrics with a 10B model (vs. an estimated ~1.8T parameters) demonstrates that task-specific architectural design can rival brute-force scale. Open questions include whether contrastive alignment strategies could replace distillation, how the approach scales to larger corpora (ArxivCap covers only 572k papers vs. 200M+ indexed), and whether the latent-space editing capability the authors envision is practically viable.
