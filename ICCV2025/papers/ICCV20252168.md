## Problem

Existing spherical point pattern registration methods rely on spherical cross-correlation in the frequency domain, incurring computational complexity of O(N_r^3 log(N_r)) with respect to rotation space discretization and degrading in accuracy beyond ~75° rotation errors. The paper addresses the need for a correspondence-free, outlier-robust solution to the Wahba problem on S² that operates in linear time and handles the full SO(3) rotation space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252168/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts two sets of 3D unit vectors on the unit sphere (S²-points), which can be derived from 3D point clouds via Extended Gaussian Image (EGI) or Centroid Aware Spherical Embedding (CASE), or from spherical images via intensity thresholding (threshold 0.21 on a 180×360 pixel grid). It outputs a 3×3 rotation matrix aligning the source to the template, and for point cloud registration, additionally a translation vector estimated via voxel-based adaptive voting followed by translation-only ICP. The method is correspondence-free and handles unequal set sizes (M ≠ N) with up to 90% outliers.

## Method

The key insight is reformulating spherical rotation estimation as a 1D circular cross-correlation problem over binary histograms rather than a 3D spherical cross-correlation. SPMC aligns both sets' mean directions to the North Pole, projects them into 360×180 binary histograms (thresholded to suppress density bias), sums over polar bins to obtain 1D azimuthal profiles, and finds the optimal rotation via a single 1D circular cross-correlation (O(1) over fixed 360 bins). FRS iteratively computes axis-direction-angle histograms for each of the three axes, performs 1D cross-correlations to extract per-axis shifts, applies the combined rotation, and repeats until convergence (typically ~11 iterations). The hybrid SPMC+FRS uses SPMC as initialization for FRS, combining SPMC's global robustness with FRS's local refinement, achieving O(n) overall complexity and median angular errors below 1° across the full SO(3) space.

## Results

On the Robust Vector Alignment Dataset, SPMC+FRS achieves a median rotational error below 1° across all five template patterns and seven noise/outlier configurations, while FPFH+QUASAR exhibits median errors exceeding 100° on several datasets and a median runtime of ~23.18 s versus under 1 s for the proposed methods. On ModelNet40 point cloud registration, SPMC with CASE embedding achieves a median rotational error of 0.13° in the complete-to-complete setting, and SPMC+FRS reaches ~2° in the partial-to-complete setting (65% overlap), whereas ICP and TEASER++ (FPFH) report median errors around 10°. For spherical image rotation estimation with up to 19.3% clutter, the method achieves a median angular error of 0.89° (1.32° at the highest clutter level).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FPFH+QUASAR [73] on the Robust Vector Alignment Dataset, SPMC+FRS is over 10× faster (<1 s vs. ~23.18 s median) and over 10× more accurate (median <1° vs. errors exceeding 100° on datasets A3–A5), because QUASAR's certifiably optimal backend is only as good as the FPFH correspondences it receives, and FPFH struggles with the uniform, depth-free geometry of spherical point clouds. In the ModelNet40 point cloud registration experiment, SPMC with CASE embedding achieves a 0.13° median rotational error in the complete-to-complete setting, outperforming ICP [8] and TEASER++ with FPFH [75] (both ~10° median) by roughly two orders of magnitude; in the harder partial-to-complete setting (65% overlap), SPMC+FRS reaches ~2° while ICP and TEASER++ fail to converge to good registrations. The deep learning baselines—PCRNet [62], PointNetLK [2], and RMN-Net [77]—perform poorly because they were pretrained on 1024-point inputs but evaluated on 2500-point clouds, exposing a generalization gap. Overall, the proposed correspondence-free methods dominate every comparison on both accuracy and speed, with the only noted weakness being SPMC's sensitivity to very high outlier ratios (median error rising to 11.32° at 90% outliers on Dataset A5), which the FRS refinement step corrects in the hybrid SPMC+FRS pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant spherical cross-correlation paradigm (Makadia & Daniilidis, Sorgi & Daniilidis), which relies on spherical harmonic transforms and incurs O(N_r³ log N_r) complexity, by reformulating rotation estimation between spherical patterns as a 3D unit-vector alignment problem (the Wahba problem) solved via binary histograms and 1D circular cross-correlation. It also contrasts with correspondence-based pipelines (FPFH + QUASAR, TEASER++) and deep learning registrars (PCRNet, PointNetLK, RPM-Net), positioning itself as a correspondence-free, training-free alternative. The two novel algorithms (SPMC and FRS) and their hybrid are original contributions; the paper does not directly extend a single prior method but rather repurposes the Wahba-problem lens and histogram-based correlation to a setting where spectral methods have been the standard tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(n) complexity and the reported 10× speedup over FPFH+QUASAR make correspondence-free spherical registration viable in real-time and large-scale settings where spectral cross-correlation is infeasible. However, the method is restricted to 3D unit vectors, the FRS component is sensitive to initialization on shapes with localized features, and the quality of the spherical embedding (EGI vs. CASE) remains a critical but underexplored factor. Open questions include extension to higher-dimensional unit-vector alignment, design of more robust spherical embeddings, and whether the histogram-based approach generalizes to non-uniform or extremely sparse point distributions beyond the tested regimes.
