## Problem

Existing unsupervised Shape-from-Template methods that handle severe occlusions and fine details rely on differentiable physics simulation (e.g., φ-SfT), which is prohibitively slow—up to 30 hours for 50–60 frames. The paper asks whether correspondence-free, unsupervised SfT can be made both dramatically faster and more accurate by replacing explicit physics with purely image-driven optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250692/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a textured triangle-mesh template (with UV map), a calibrated RGB video sequence, and camera intrinsics; camera extrinsics are not required since reconstruction is per camera frame. Silhouette masks are optional and can be auto-generated by SAM2 from a single user prompt. The output is a per-frame 3D deformed mesh (vertex positions), optimized frame-by-frame with the previous frame's solution used as initialization for the next.

## Method

A small MLP deformation network f_θ(x₀, t) predicts per-vertex offsets from the template, producing the deformed shape x_t = x₀ + f_θ(x₀, t). A differentiable renderer (nvdiffrast) projects this mesh into an RGB image and silhouette, which are compared against the video frame using three vision losses—RGB, silhouette, and Sobel image gradient—all wrapped in an adaptive data-loss structure that exponentially amplifies large pixel-wise errors to handle shading variations. A mesh inextensibility regularizer (covariance-based, from [6]) constrains local geometry without enforcing strict isometry, accommodating both rigid and elastic surfaces. Optimization is frame-wise: each frame is solved independently with AdamW (500 warmup + 200 iterations), and the converged parameters initialize the next frame, yielding O(TN) complexity versus O(T²N) for prior physics-based methods while implicitly enforcing temporal coherence.

## Results

On the φ-SfT real dataset (9 cloth sequences), the proposed method achieves an average chamfer distance of 3.95×10⁴, outperforming φ-SfT (9.27×10⁴) and PGSfT (9.75×10⁴) on all 9 sequences. The largest margin is on R1 (0.66 vs. 9.36 for φ-SfT) and R6 (3.37 vs. 15.46 for PGSfT). Runtime is approximately 2.5 minutes per frame, matching PGSfT and achieving a 400× speedup over φ-SfT (2800 s/frame). On the synthetic φ-SfT dataset, the method achieves the best error on 2 of 4 sequences (S1: 0.0229, S4: 0.0031) but trails φ-SfT on S2 and S3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against φ-SfT [17] (physics-based, unsupervised), the proposed method wins on all 9 real sequences by large margins (e.g., 0.66 vs. 9.36 on R1; 3.37 vs. 9.95 on R6) while running 400× faster (≈2.5 min vs. 2800 s per frame). Against PGSfT [49] (self-supervised neural cloth model), the method also wins on all 9 sequences (e.g., 0.66 vs. 6.05 on R1; 2.12 vs. 9.18 on R9) with comparable runtime (~2.5 min vs. ~2.4 min per frame). On the synthetic φ-SfT dataset, the method beats PGSfT on all 4 sequences and φ-SfT on S1 and S4, but φ-SfT retains an edge on S2 (0.0230 vs. 0.0254) and S3 (0.0330 vs. 0.0357). On the Kinect Paper dataset, the method outperforms traditional SfT [3] and supervised DeepSfT [11] in depth-map RMSE, though it is slightly worse than TD-SfT [10] on frames with high inter-image motion. The paper notes that supervised baselines (DeepSfT, TD-SfT) cannot be fairly compared on the φ-SfT sequences because they require ~100K training samples, far exceeding the 50-frame sequences available.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the physics-based unsupervised SfT line (φ-SfT [17], PGSfT [49]) by replacing differentiable thin-shell simulation with a lightweight MLP deformation network guided solely by image observations (RGB, gradients, silhouettes). It borrows the mesh inextensibility regularizer from GAPS [6] but applies it as a soft constraint rather than a strict isometric one, enabling both rigid and elastic surfaces. The frame-wise optimization strategy (O(TN) vs. O(T²N) joint optimization) is a key architectural departure that leverages temporal continuity implicitly through parameter warm-starting rather than explicit temporal losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 400× speedup over φ-SfT while simultaneously outperforming it on fine details and severe occlusions makes unsupervised SfT practically deployable for long video sequences, removing the primary barrier to real-world adoption. The finding that explicit physics simulation is unnecessary—simple image cues plus inextensibility suffice—opens the door to extending the approach to object categories where physical parameters are hard to estimate. Limitations include inability to handle textureless or specular surfaces (requiring shape-from-shading), performance degradation under high inter-frame motion, and the current restriction to triangle meshes; the authors note that window-wise optimization and alternative representations (implicit fields, point clouds) are natural next steps.
