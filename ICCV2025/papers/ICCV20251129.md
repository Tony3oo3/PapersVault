## Problem

Traditional DRM solvers rely on chromaticity analysis or energy minimization and fail to recover missing content under strong specular highlights, producing artifacts like color distortion and black blocks on achromatic surfaces. Existing deep learning methods, while more powerful, treat the task as a direct image-to-image translation without fully exploiting the physical DRM variable set (diffuse chromaticity, diffuse intensity ratio, specular chromaticity), limiting their controllability and accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251129/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces a diffuse reflection image, a specular reflection image, and a binary specular reflection mask. HDNet and ACENet operate on shuffle-down features at one-quarter resolution (scale factor s=4), while RNet operates at the original resolution (capped at 512p). The high-resolution diffuse and specular images are reconstructed by upsampling the low-resolution intermediate variables (chromaticity, alpha, specular chromaticity) and multiplying by the full-resolution intensity sum, rather than directly upsampling the final images.

## Method

The pipeline consists of three networks: HDNet detects specular highlights using Contextual Contrasted Feature Extraction Blocks (CCFEBs) that compute the difference between local (dilation=1) and context (dilation=3) convolutions; ACENet estimates the DRM variables (diffuse chromaticity, alpha, spatially-varying specular chromaticity) using Transformer Blocks guided by CCFGBs, which inject HDNet's multi-level spatial cues via Q-K-V-induced attention; and RNet refines the reconstructed images in high-resolution space using ConvNeXt blocks without assuming any physical model. The key design insight is that DRM variables are piecewise smooth and low-frequency, so they can be estimated at low resolution and upscaled, with high-frequency details preserved in the intensity map that bypasses the network. Training uses cross-entropy loss for the mask, L2 loss on reconstructed diffuse/specular images for ACENet, and L2 loss on refined outputs for RNet, all supervised end-to-end with Adam at lr 1e-4→1e-6 over 100 epochs.

## Results

On SHIQ, the proposed neural DRM solver achieves 34.501 PSNR and 0.979 SSIM, outperforming the best prior method DHAN-SHR (33.810 PSNR, 0.975 SSIM). On PSD, it reaches 29.803 PSNR and 0.943 SSIM, surpassing HighlightRNet (29.787 PSNR, 0.922 SSIM) by a notable margin in SSIM. On synthetic SSHR, it attains 34.637 PSNR and 0.965 SSIM, exceeding DHAN-SHR (34.103 PSNR, 0.959 SSIM). On the high-resolution Hypersim dataset, the method achieves 23.158 PSNR and 0.820 SSIM, and on the user-study Wild dataset it obtains the highest PtA score of 18.53%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 10 baselines on every dataset and metric. Among traditional methods, Fu19 [8] is the strongest (15.746 PSNR on SHIQ) yet still trails the proposed method by nearly 19 dB, reflecting the fundamental limitation of non-learning solvers on real images. Among deep learning baselines, DHAN-SHR [13] is the closest competitor on SHIQ (33.810 vs. 34.501 PSNR) and SSHR (34.103 vs. 34.637 PSNR), while HighlightRNet [39] is closest on PSD (29.787 vs. 29.803 PSNR) but lags by 0.021 in SSIM. JSHDR [10] achieves a surprisingly high 34.131 PSNR on SHIQ but drops to 21.516 on PSD, indicating poor generalization across datasets. TSHRNet [11] and SpecularityNet [33] show moderate performance (25.575 and 23.420 PSNR on SHIQ respectively), with visible blurriness and specular leakage noted qualitatively. On the user-study Wild dataset, the proposed method achieves the highest PtA (18.53%) versus SpecularityNet (17.42%) and DHAN-SHR (16.93%), confirming human-perceived superiority on complex real-world scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Shafer's Dichromatic Reflection Model and Kim et al.'s RG-chromaticity-space formulation, which prior work solved via energy minimization or chromaticity analysis. It departs from existing deep learning methods (JSHDR, SpecularityNet, TSHRNet, DHAN-SHR) that learn an end-to-end mapping from input to diffuse/specular images, by instead explicitly estimating the full set of DRM variables (diffuse chromaticity, diffuse intensity ratio α, specular chromaticity) with dedicated networks and then reconstructing images from those variables. The architectural contribution—the Contextual Contrasted Features Guidance Block (CCFGB) with Q-K-V-induced attention for cross-network feature transfer, combined with a low-resolution estimation strategy motivated by the piecewise-smooth nature of DRM variables—represents a new way to integrate physical model structure into a neural pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly parameterizing a physical model with neural networks and exploiting the low-frequency nature of its variables can outperform both traditional solvers and end-to-end deep learning baselines while reducing computational cost (50p intermediate estimation vs. full resolution). The CCFGB mechanism for injecting detection cues into a variable-estimation network is a reusable architectural pattern. Limitations include reliance on supervised ground truth (the Wild dataset evaluation is user-study-only), the assumption that scenes approximately follow DRM, and the fact that the refinement network is model-free and thus may not generalize beyond training distributions. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-image or video settings, and how it handles scenes with strong spatially varying illumination that violate the uniform specular chromaticity assumption.
