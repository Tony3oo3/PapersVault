## Problem

Synthetic traffic scenario generation methods produce only path-level outputs without designating an ego vehicle or generating the sensor inputs (multi-camera images, LiDAR) required by real-world E2E AD training pipelines. This incompatibility has confined synthetic data to virtual simulators, leaving real-world E2E AD models limited to the narrow scenario diversity of expensive real-world datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251631/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes two input types: multi-camera images (processed by a frozen BEVFormer) and ego-centric 2D map representations containing road layout, vehicle bounding boxes, and ego vehicle dimensions (processed by the Map-to-BEV Network). At test time, only multi-camera images are used. Outputs include a planned driving trajectory, multi-hypothesis agent motion forecasts with associated probabilities, and future occupancy maps. Notably, the ego vehicle's width and height vary across synthetic scenarios, a condition absent from standard real-world datasets.

## Method

SynAD integrates three components: (1) an ego-centric scenario generator using conditional diffusion with guided sampling (collision, map-collision, and speed-limit objectives) that selects the longest-traveling agent as ego and projects all trajectories into an ego-relative coordinate frame; (2) a Map-to-BEV Network—a Transformer encoder with learnable queries—that converts ego-centric maps into BEV features aligned to a frozen BEVFormer via L2 loss, eliminating the need for sensor inputs on synthetic data; and (3) a selective integration strategy that feeds map-derived BEV features only into the motion forecasting and planning modules (not occupancy prediction, which requires richer 3D spatial information), using real scenarios projected onto maps as a domain bridge. At inference, the model operates on image-only inputs, ensuring practical deployability.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, SynAD achieves the lowest average collision rate (0.11%) among all E2E AD baselines, outperforming Paradrive (0.25%), OCCWorld (0.24%), VAD (0.22%), and UniAD (0.17%). The average L2 planning error is 0.80 m, slightly higher than Paradrive (0.48 m) but competitive with UniAD (0.76 m) and VAD (0.72 m). On motion forecasting, SynAD attains the best minADE (0.69 m), minFDE (1.01 m), and miss rate (0.154), surpassing Paradrive, UniAD, and VAD. For occupancy prediction, SynAD achieves IoU-n of 60.5 and IoU-f of 39.6, comparable to UniAD (61.9 / 39.7) and VAD (not reported).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SynAD achieves the lowest collision rate across all horizons (0.04% at 1s, 0.10% at 2s, 0.20% at 3s), beating the next-best Paradrive (0.14/0.23/0.39%) and OCCWorld (0.06/0.21/0.47%) by a wide margin. On L2 planning error, SynAD (0.80 m avg) trails Paradrive (0.48 m) and VAD (0.72 m) but is competitive with UniAD (0.76 m); the paper attributes this trade-off to the broader behavioral distribution introduced by synthetic scenarios, which prioritizes safety over exact GT-path replication. Against the older ST-P3 and OCCNet baselines, SynAD improves L2 by roughly 2.6× and collision rate by 6–7×. On motion forecasting (Table 3), SynAD outperforms all baselines on minADE (0.69 vs. 0.73–0.78), minFDE (1.01 vs. 1.08–1.11), and miss rate (0.154 vs. 0.162–0.169), while UniAD retains a slight edge on occupancy IoU-n (61.9 vs. 60.5). In scenario generation (Table 1), SynAD achieves the best rule-compliance score (0.330) and the highest relative-realism score (0.324) among all generators, confirming that the synthetic data is both physically valid and behaviorally diverse.

## Relation to Prior Work

SynAD sits at the intersection of two previously disconnected research threads: conditional diffusion-based traffic scenario generation (CTG, CTG++, KING) and vision-centric end-to-end autonomous driving (UniAD, VAD, ST-P3, Paradrive). Prior scenario generators produce path-level multi-agent trajectories but lack ego-vehicle designation and sensor inputs, while E2E AD methods rely exclusively on real multi-camera or LiDAR data. The paper's core architectural departure is the Map-to-BEV Network, which aligns map-derived BEV features with frozen BEVFormer outputs via L2 distillation, thereby eliminating the sensor-input bottleneck that had confined synthetic scenarios to simulator-only pipelines. The E2E AD backbone follows UniAD's modular design (motion forecasting, occupancy prediction, planning) with deformable cross-attention, but selectively injects map-based features only into the prediction and planning modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a handful of synthetic scenes (500, roughly 7.5× the real data volume) can reduce collision rate from 0.25% to 0.11% without degrading test-time image-only inference, SynAD offers a practical, low-cost path to safer E2E AD models without additional data collection. The finding that 2D map features help motion forecasting and planning but actively harm occupancy prediction provides a useful design principle for future multi-source E2E architectures. Key limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (nuScenes), a modest synthetic scene count, and a slight L2 regression relative to UniAD, suggesting the approach may need validation at larger scale and across diverse driving conditions. The authors explicitly leave open the extension of this integration strategy to other E2E AD frameworks and to richer guide functions (LLM-based, retrieval-based).
