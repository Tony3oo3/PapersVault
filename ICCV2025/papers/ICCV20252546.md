## Problem

Automatic 3D semantic occupancy annotation remains heavily dependent on LiDAR point clouds, manual pre-annotations (over 4,000 human hours for nuScenes), and labor-intensive post-processing, while being confined to predefined closed-set or open-set categories. Real-world driving scenes contain open-ended objects (e.g., collapsed poles, plastic sheets) that fall outside any fixed vocabulary, and existing pipelines struggle with dynamic objects and cross-scene generalization.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a multi-view image sequence (900×1600 for Occ3D-nuScenes, 370×1226 for SemanticKITTI) along with a fixed text prompt fed to a VLM, and optionally LiDAR sweeps as geometric priors. It outputs a 3D semantic occupancy voxel grid at an arbitrary voxel resolution, where each voxel carries an open-ended semantic label determined by weighted Gaussian splatting—no human labels, predefined categories, or post-processing steps are required.

## Method

AutoOcc introduces VL-GS, a semantic-aware variant of 3D Gaussian Splatting where each Gaussian carries a semantic attribute vector and an adaptive scaling factor tied to the occupied range of its ellipsoid, allowing sparse large-scale Gaussians for backgrounds and dense small-scale Gaussians for fine objects. A VLM (InternVL) generates per-category semantic attention maps via aggregated transformer decoder attentions, which simultaneously prompt SAM for instance-level masks and UniDepth for semantic-guided monocular depth, eliminating manual annotations. A self-estimated 3D flow module uses Chamfer distance between temporally paired same-semantic Gaussians to detect and track dynamic objects, assigning them dedicated dynamic Gaussians to avoid trailing artifacts. Final occupancy is produced by a cumulative GS-to-voxel splatting that weights each Gaussian's contribution by its occupied depth, opacity, and softmax semantic probability, with an optional LiDAR geometry-aware loss constraining Gaussian distributions to match projected point anchors.

## Results

On Occ3D-nuScenes, AutoOcc-V (camera-only) achieves 83.01 IoU and 20.92 mIoU, while AutoOcc-M (camera + LiDAR) reaches 88.62 IoU and 25.84 mIoU. These represent substantial gains over the strongest camera-only baseline VEON (57.92 IoU, 14.51 mIoU) and the strongest LiDAR-assisted baseline OpenOcc* (70.59 IoU, 17.76 mIoU). On SemanticKITTI zero-shot cross-dataset evaluation, AutoOcc maintains competitive mIoU on both base and novel classes, whereas self-supervised baselines suffer significant degradation on unseen categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoOcc-V (camera-only) outperforms all camera-only self-supervised baselines by a wide margin: +31.8 IoU over GaussianOcc (51.22→83.01), +36.5 IoU over LangOcc (46.55→83.01), and +25.1 IoU over VEON (57.92→83.01), while also improving mIoU by 6–9 points. Against LiDAR-assisted pipelines, AutoOcc-M surpasses OpenOcc* (70.59 IoU) by +18.0 IoU and the VLM-LiDAR projection baseline (73.28 IoU) by +15.3 IoU, despite the latter using direct 2D-to-3D projection. OVIR-3D, a 2D-to-3D projection method, trails AutoOcc-M by 34.3 IoU, confirming that naive projection loses critical 3D geometric detail. Notably, AutoOcc-V with only camera input already exceeds the LiDAR-only SurroundOcc* (68.87 IoU) by 14.1 IoU, demonstrating that the vision-language guided reconstruction captures more complete scene geometry than sparse LiDAR point clouds alone. On zero-shot SemanticKITTI evaluation, self-supervised methods (GaussianOcc, OVO) degrade significantly on novel classes, while AutoOcc maintains robust open-ended annotation without retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

AutoOcc builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) as its geometric backbone and on training-free VLM attention extraction (Lin et al., 2024) for open-ended semantic query generation, but departs from both by coupling them into a differentiable reconstruction pipeline specifically designed for occupancy annotation. It explicitly rejects the dominant LiDAR-voxelization paradigm (SurroundOcc, OpenOcc) and 2D-to-3D projection approaches (OVIR-3D, SAMPro3D), arguing that sparse point clouds and naive projection cannot ensure 3D semantic-geometric consistency. Compared to self-supervised occupancy estimators (GaussianOcc, LangOcc, VEON), AutoOcc introduces semantic-aware scalable Gaussians and a self-estimated flow module to handle dynamic objects, while leveraging VLMs to eliminate the need for any predefined category vocabulary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pipeline eliminates the 4000+ human annotation hours required for benchmarks like nuScenes and achieves open-ended (not merely open-set) semantic labeling, which is critical for real-world driving where novel objects (collapsed poles, plastic sheets) appear outside any fixed taxonomy. The 5 GB memory footprint and ~30 GPU-hour cost versus 32–73 GB and 1000+ GPU hours for LiDAR-based pipelines make large-scale annotation economically viable. Open questions remain: the method requires per-scene optimization (not feed-forward), depends on the quality of multiple foundation models (InternVL, SAM, UniDepth) whose errors compound, and the 'open-ended' capability is bounded by the VLM's vocabulary coverage. Whether the approach scales to longer sequences or higher-resolution scenes without proportional compute growth is untested.
