## Problem

Existing V2X cooperative perception methods focus on single-frame spatial fusion across agents, ignoring temporal cues across sequential frames that are essential for locating previously visible objects and predicting future trajectories. The broader challenge of efficiently aggregating multi-agent and multi-frame information to support long-term temporal tasks such as motion prediction remains largely unexplored, with no comprehensive framework addressing what to transmit, when to transmit, and how to fuse across both spatial and temporal dimensions.

## Input / Output

Each agent provides LiDAR point clouds over a 2-second history at 2 Hz, HD vector map polylines (with position and lane-type attributes per waypoint), and relative pose/extrinsic metadata; all point clouds are transformed into the ego agent's coordinate frame before processing. The ego agent evaluates surrounding agents within a 50 m communication range (x ∈ [−70, 70] m, y ∈ [−40, 40] m). Outputs are 3D bounding boxes (x, y, z, w, l, h, yaw, confidence) and 3-second future trajectories at 2 Hz for predefined anchor boxes, produced jointly by a shared detection and prediction head on the fused spatio-temporal BEV feature.

## Method

V2XPnP adopts intermediate fusion within a one-step communication strategy: each agent internally fuses its own historical BEV features (extracted via PointPillar) into a single condensed feature map using a Transformer-based temporal attention module, then transmits only this compact representation (achieving ~32× compression over multi-step) rather than per-frame features. A heterogeneous multi-agent spatial attention module then fuses features across agents, with separate learnable weights for each interaction pair type (V-V, V-I, I-V, I-I) to account for different sensor capabilities and deployment positions. Self-spatial attention uses multi-scale windowed MHSA (local, intermediate, global windows) to capture intra-agent spatial interactions at different resolutions. HD map polylines are encoded per BEV grid via MLP and injected through a map-BEV cross-attention with sinusoidal positional encoding. The final fused feature feeds a shared detection head (2D conv for box regression and focal-loss classification) and a prediction head (2D conv for per-timestep trajectory offsets accumulated into full trajectories), trained end-to-end with a weighted sum of smooth-L1 regression, focal classification, and L2 trajectory losses.

## Results

On the V2XPnP Sequential Dataset (VC mode, up to 4 agents), V2XPnP achieves 71.6% AP@0.5 and 48.2% EPA, outperforming the strongest baseline V2X-ViT* (69.6% AP, 44.7% EPA) by +2.0 AP and +3.5 EPA. Across all four collaboration modes (VC, IC, V2V, I2I), V2XPnP consistently achieves the best AP@0.5 (69.2–71.6%) and EPA (40.6–48.2%). The one-step communication strategy further improves over multi-step by +5.0% AP and +12% EPA while compressing transmission data by 32×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

V2XPnP outperforms all seven baselines across every collaboration mode and every metric. Against the strongest intermediate-fusion competitor V2X-ViT*, V2XPnP gains +2.0 AP and +3.5 EPA in VC mode, +1.7 AP and +2.7 EPA in IC mode, +5.9 AP and +3.9 EPA in V2V mode, and +3.1 AP and +1.2 EPA in I2I mode. FFNet* and CoBEVFlow* (both leveraging 0.5 s historical features for asynchrony mitigation) trail V2XPnP by 7.0 and 8.3 AP respectively in VC mode, confirming that longer temporal horizons (2 s) with proper spatio-temporal fusion yield substantially better results. Early fusion (60.3% AP) consistently beats late fusion (58.1% AP) in VC mode, validating that transmitting intermediate features is more informative than final detections. The single-agent baselines (No Fusion at 43.9% AP, No Fusion-FaF* at 53.4% AP) are far behind all cooperative methods, underscoring the value of multi-agent information sharing. In V2V mode, V2XPnP's advantage over V2X-ViT* is largest (+5.9 AP), suggesting the unified Transformer's temporal attention is particularly beneficial when only two vehicle agents are available.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: single-frame V2X cooperative perception (V2X-ViT, FFNet, CoBEVFlow, OPV2V) and end-to-end temporal perception-and-prediction (FaF, PnPNet, V2VNet). Prior V2X work aggregates spatial information across agents but treats each frame independently, while temporal PnP methods operate on a single vehicle's history. V2XPnP deliberately unifies these by extending the three classical fusion strategies (early, late, intermediate) into the spatio-temporal domain and introducing one-step vs. multi-step communication as an orthogonal design axis, then benchmarking 11 models across all four V2X collaboration modes. The architectural choice—a unified Transformer with separate temporal, self-spatial, multi-agent spatial, and map attention blocks—repurposes standard attention mechanisms rather than introducing a fundamentally new module, but the heterogeneous multi-agent spatial attention with per-interaction-pair weights (V-V, V-I, I-V, I-I) is a practical departure from homogeneous fusion in prior V2X-ViT-style work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The V2XPnP Sequential Dataset is the first real-world sequential V2X dataset covering all collaboration modes (VC, IC, V2V, I2I) with temporally consistent trajectories and vector maps, filling a clear gap left by non-sequential datasets like Dair-V2X and V2X-Real. The comprehensive 11-model benchmark across four modes provides the field with the first systematic comparison of what/when/how to transmit in V2X spatio-temporal settings, which should guide future system design. Open questions include whether the one-step communication advantage persists at larger agent counts or longer history windows, whether the framework extends to camera-based or radar-based V2X, and how performance degrades under realistic C-V2X bandwidth constraints beyond the 32× compression tested. The EPA metric for joint perception-prediction evaluation is a useful addition but has not yet been adopted as a standard.
