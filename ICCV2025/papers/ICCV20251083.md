## Problem

Existing 3D LiDAR-based single object tracking methods are category-specific, requiring separate models per object type, which is impractical for real-world deployment and fails to generalize to novel categories. When these methods are naively trained on all categories together, they suffer severe performance degradation (up to 14.2%) due to drastic geometric disparities in scale, motion patterns, and structural complexity across categories. The paper asks how to learn geometry-aware yet category-agnostic representations by transferring large-scale pretrained 3D point cloud models without introducing manual biases.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 128-point template and search regions (after farthest point sampling), a target-oriented mask (0.8 for object, 0.2 for background), a uniform search mask (0.5), and a learnable temporal token, all concatenated into a single token sequence of length 1+N_t+N_s. The output is a 3D bounding box with only four predicted parameters (x, y, z, yaw), since target dimensions are inherited from the template, plus an encoded temporal token that is propagated to the next frame. The unified modeling framework processes template and search tokens jointly within shared Transformer blocks rather than using separate Siamese branches.

## Method

TrackAny3D transfers the RECON pretrained 3D encoder (frozen) for category-agnostic tracking via three mechanisms. First, a two-path adapter is inserted into every Transformer layer: an adaptation path (down-project → GeLU → up-project) computes the feature shift, while a gated scoring path (linear → ReLU) produces a per-token scaling factor that modulates the adaptation intensity element-wise. Second, a Mixture-of-Geometry-Experts (MoGE) module with 8 FFN experts and Top-4 routing is placed at even-numbered layers, adaptively activating specialized subnetworks based on input geometry rather than class labels. Third, temporal context is modeled through a learnable temporal token that accumulates historical encoder outputs across frames, combined with a dynamic mask weighting mechanism that applies learnable per-point weights β to the input masks, jointly suppressing temporal noise and adapting mask emphasis to category-specific characteristics without manual tuning.

## Results

On KITTI under the category-unified setting, TrackAny3D achieves a mean success rate of 67.1% and precision of 85.4%, surpassing the previous best unified method MoCUT (65.8%/85.0%) by 1.3% in success. On NuScenes, it reaches 54.57%/66.25% mean, outperforming MoCUT (51.19%/64.63%) by 3.4% in success. On Waymo Open Dataset (cross-dataset generalization from KITTI-trained model), TrackAny3D achieves 64.0%/73.3% mean, beating MoCUT (61.9%/69.7%) by 2.1% in success and 3.6% in precision. The method uses only 5.30M tunable parameters with the RECON backbone frozen, compared to 18.30M for CXTrack.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TrackAny3D outperforms all category-unified baselines across all three datasets. On KITTI, it beats MoCUT (the closest unified competitor) by 1.3% success (67.1% vs. 65.8%) and by 6.0% on the Car category (73.4% vs. 67.6%), while surpassing MBPTrack-unified by 11.0% and CXTrack-unified by 9.9% in mean success. On NuScenes, the gap over MoCUT widens to 3.4% in success (54.57% vs. 51.19%), and TrackAny3D even surpasses category-specific MBPTrack on the Bus category (61.01% vs. 55.41%). On Waymo (cross-dataset, KITTI-trained), TrackAny3D leads MoCUT by 2.1% success and 3.6% precision, and exceeds category-specific MBPTrack (61.9%/71.9%) by 2.1% in success. Against the strongest category-specific methods (StreamTrack 70.8%, M3SOT 70.3% on KITTI), TrackAny3D trails by 2.7–3.7% in success, confirming that the unified paradigm still has a small accuracy gap but eliminates the need for per-category models. The method achieves these results with only 5.30M tunable parameters (adapters + MoGE + temporal token), versus 18.30M for CXTrack and 7.38M for MBPTrack, while keeping the RECON backbone fully frozen.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D SOT lineage (SC3D, STNet, MBPTrack, etc.) by introducing a pretraining-transfer paradigm that was previously unexplored in this domain. It builds directly on RECON as a frozen pretrained encoder and adopts the unified single-branch modeling framework from SyncTrack to maintain input-format consistency with pretraining tasks. The key conceptual departure is from the dominant category-specific training paradigm: rather than training separate networks per object class, TrackAny3D uses parameter-efficient adapters and a Mixture-of-Geometry-Experts module to adapt a single shared model across all categories, drawing on PEFT techniques (adapters, prompt tuning) well-established in 2D vision and NLP but largely absent from 3D point cloud tracking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: real-world autonomous driving and robotics systems cannot afford to train and store dozens of category-specific trackers, and the demonstrated 14%+ performance drops when existing methods are forced into unified training underscore the urgency of this problem. The finding that a single PEFT-adapted model can match or exceed category-specific baselines (e.g., 67.1% vs. 70.3% mean success on KITTI) suggests pretrained 3D geometric priors are a viable substitute for category-specific overfitting. Open questions include whether the approach scales to open-vocabulary or truly unseen categories, whether it extends to multi-object tracking or other sensor modalities (RGB-D, camera), and whether the 3-frame temporal window is sufficient for long-horizon tracking in highly dynamic scenes.
