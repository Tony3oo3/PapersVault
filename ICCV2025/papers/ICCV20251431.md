## Problem

Reconstructing hand-held objects in 3D from a single RGB image remains challenging: implicit-representation methods (SDF, DDF) produce overly smooth surfaces and require Marching Cubes post-processing, while the recent diffusion-based D-SCO achieves high fidelity but is extremely slow (>13 s) due to multi-step denoising. No existing method simultaneously delivers high-resolution explicit geometry and fast feed-forward inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251431/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 224×224 monocular RGB image cropped to the hand-object region, along with an estimated MANO hand mesh (778 vertices, 16 joints) and camera intrinsics/extrinsics from an off-the-shelf hand pose estimator. It outputs a dense 3D point cloud of 16,384 points in the hand-palm coordinate system plus a 3D translation vector locating the object relative to the palm; no rotation is predicted because many everyday objects are symmetric.

## Method

HORT follows a coarse-to-fine pipeline with four modules. An image encoder (DINOv2-Large, last 12 layers fine-tuned) and a hand encoder (PointNet over 778 vertices transformed into 22 local joint/fingertip/palm coordinate systems) extract complementary visual and geometric features. A sparse decoder uses a unified 10-layer transformer with 2,049 learnable tokens to jointly predict the hand-relative translation and a 2,048-point sparse cloud, exploiting mutual dependencies between pose and shape. A dense decoder then upsamples to 16,384 points via two blocks of kNN-based self-attention (k=16) and bilinear upsampling, where each sparse point is projected onto the image plane to retrieve pixel-aligned DINOv2 patch features that provide local visual context for refinement. The entire network is trained end-to-end with L1 pose loss and Chamfer Distance losses on both sparse and dense point clouds.

## Results

On the synthetic ObMan dataset, HORT achieves FS@5=0.66, FS@10=0.88, and CD=1.0 cm with predicted hands, outperforming the previous best D-SCO (FS@5=0.61, FS@10=0.81, CD=1.1). On real-world HO3D, HORT reaches FS@10=0.72 and CD=2.6 cm, and on DexYCB it attains FS@10=0.85 and CD=1.1 cm. Inference speed is 16.9 fps (4.8 fps with meshing), roughly 200× faster than D-SCO (0.08 fps) and 35–48× faster than implicit methods (0.35–0.48 fps), while requiring only 258 GFLOPs versus >6000 for implicit approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HORT outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the closest competitor D-SCO [25] (diffusion-based point cloud), HORT improves FS@10 by 7 percentage points on ObMan (0.88 vs 0.81) and by 9 points on HO3D (0.72 vs 0.63), while also reducing CD from 1.1 to 1.0 cm on ObMan and from 3.4 to 2.6 cm on HO3D. On DexYCB, HORT matches D-SCO on FS@5 (0.63) but leads on FS@10 (0.85 vs 0.82) and CD (1.1 vs 1.3). Against implicit-function methods (gSDF, DDF-HO, IHOI, AlignSDF, GF), HORT achieves substantially lower CD (1.0 vs 1.4–13.9 cm on ObMan) and higher FS@10 (0.88 vs 0.51–0.67). On the in-the-wild MOW dataset, HORT trained on multiple datasets achieves FS@10=0.57 and CD=5.4, far surpassing MCC-HO (FS@10=0.31, CD=15.2) and WildHOI (FS@10=0.20, CD=27.2). The most striking advantage is inference speed: HORT runs at 16.9 fps versus 0.08 fps for D-SCO and 0.35–0.48 fps for implicit methods, a 200× speedup over the prior state of the art.

## Relation to Prior Work

HORT positions itself as a departure from both implicit-function methods (SDF/DDF) that produce overly smooth surfaces and require Marching Cubes post-processing, and from the recent diffusion-based D-SCO that achieves high-resolution point clouds but at prohibitive inference cost (>13 s). Conceptually, it repurposes the coarse-to-fine point cloud generation paradigm (sparse → dense upsampling) within a unified transformer backbone, replacing the multi-step denoising of diffusion with a single feed-forward pass. The hand-encoding strategy extends prior work that used only sparse joint positions by transforming MANO vertices into 22 local coordinate systems, and the pixel-aligned feature retrieval for the dense decoder draws on projection-based conditioning ideas from single-image 3D reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~200× speedup over D-SCO (16.9 fps vs 0.08 fps) while simultaneously improving reconstruction quality makes high-fidelity hand-held object reconstruction practical for real-time applications such as robotics manipulation and human-computer interaction. A key limitation is that the method only predicts hand-relative translation (not rotation) due to object symmetry, and it depends on an external hand pose estimator whose errors propagate. Open questions include whether the coarse-to-fine transformer strategy extends to two-handed or multi-object interactions, whether rotation can be recovered for asymmetric objects, and whether the fine-grained multi-coordinate-system hand encoding generalizes beyond the MANO parameterization.
