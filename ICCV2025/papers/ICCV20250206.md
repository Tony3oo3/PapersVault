## Problem

Existing data pruning and online batch selection methods for training acceleration rely on gradient back-propagation or proxy model training to score sample importance, introducing computational overhead that scales poorly with dataset size. This paper asks whether sample importance can be assessed at minimal cost—using only shallow-layer features of the target model itself—while still preserving the diversity and generalization of the retained training subset.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250206/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each training iteration, the method consumes a batch of RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet, up to 2048×1024 for Cityscapes) and the target model partitioned into a shallow sub-network (e.g., ResNet stage-1/2 or Swin stage-1) and a deep sub-network. Shallow features are spatially average-pooled and channel-downsampled to a 1×D vector (D=128 by default) for density estimation. The output is a binary pruning mask per sample (retained vs. discarded) and updated model parameters; pruned samples never enter the deep sub-network, so their forward and backward passes are entirely skipped.

## Method

PFB computes a probability-density-based importance score for each sample using only shallow-layer features: importance is inversely proportional to the estimated PDF at the sample's feature location, so rare (low-density) samples are prioritized. The PDF is estimated via a lightweight Kernel Density Estimation with a fixed number of centroids (N_C=64) that are updated each iteration using an exponential moving average over retained samples assigned to each centroid via k-means-style clustering, enabling adaptive tracking of distribution shift without storing all features. A small random term (α·max density, α~U(0,0.01)) is added to the denominator to prevent deterministic pruning and further encourage diversity. Pruned samples are blocked immediately after the shallow sub-network, eliminating their deep-layer forward and backward computation, while retained samples reuse their already-computed shallow features to continue the forward pass—reducing total cost to a full forward on retained samples plus a shallow forward on pruned ones, with no proxy model or gradient computation required.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18, PFB achieves 79.1% top-1 accuracy at 30% pruning (exceeding the 78.2% full-data baseline) and maintains 77.9% even at 70% pruning. On ImageNet-1k with ResNet-50, PFB reaches 77.0% at 30% pruning (vs. 76.4% full data) and 75.3% at 70% pruning. In terms of efficiency, PFB achieves a 33.2% total training time reduction on ImageNet-1k at 40% pruning while simultaneously gaining 0.5% accuracy over the full-data baseline, outperforming the next-best method (InfoBatch, 26.8% reduction) by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PFB consistently outperforms all 20 baselines across every dataset and pruning ratio tested. On CIFAR-100, PFB surpasses the strongest baselines DivBS (78.5/78.2/77.2%) and InfoBatch (78.2/78.1/76.5%) by 0.6–1.4 points at 30/50/70% pruning, respectively. On ImageNet-1k with ResNet-50, PFB (77.0/76.1/75.3%) beats InfoBatch (76.5/75.8/74.9%) and MoSo (76.5/73.5/70.0%) at 30/50/70% pruning; with Swin-T, it edges out Dyn-Unc (79.1/78.5/77.6%) by 0.5–0.6 points. Gradient-based methods (EL2N, GraNd, DivBS) and proxy-based methods (SVP, MoSo) degrade sharply at high pruning ratios (e.g., EL2N drops to 54.8% on ImageNet at 70%), while PFB remains within 1.1 points of full-data performance. In terms of training efficiency on ImageNet, PFB achieves 33.2% total time reduction versus 26.8% for InfoBatch, 26.7% for UCB, and only 14.4% for DivBS, while EL2N actually increases total cost by >72% due to its expensive scoring phase. On segmentation tasks, PFB achieves 79.8% mIoU on Cityscapes (30% pruned) vs. 79.2% for InfoBatch and 79.0% for DivBS, with a 24.1% time saving versus 15.8% and 7.8% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of data pruning and online batch selection by introducing a fundamentally different pruning mechanism: rather than scoring samples via gradients, loss values, or proxy-model predictions (as in EL2N, InfoBatch, DivBS, or SVP), PFB computes importance from shallow-layer features and physically blocks the deep forward pass for pruned samples. The importance criterion repurposes kernel density estimation—a classical statistical tool—as a per-sample redundancy measure, which is a conceptual departure from the gradient-norm, loss-based, and uncertainty-based metrics that dominate the literature. The Adaptive Distribution Estimation module, combining online clustering with EMA centroid updates, extends the static KDE framework into an adaptive, training-aware setting that no prior data-pruning method has employed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 33% training-time reduction with a 0.5% accuracy gain on ImageNet-1k is practically significant for large-scale training pipelines, and the architecture-agnostic design (validated on both ResNet and Swin Transformer, plus segmentation) broadens its applicability. The finding that pruning 40% of data can *improve* accuracy suggests the probability-density diversity criterion is selecting a genuinely more informative subset, raising the question of whether this effect persists at even higher pruning ratios or in non-vision domains. Open questions include how the KDE-based scoring scales to very high-dimensional feature spaces, whether the method transfers to generative or language-model training, and whether the modest overhead of the ADE module (0.06h on ImageNet) remains negligible as batch sizes or dataset sizes grow further.
