## Problem

Existing human pose estimation datasets and methods assume all subjects have complete limbs, causing models to produce incorrect or biased predictions for the 35–40 million people worldwide with limb deficiencies. No dataset, loss function, or evaluation metric has been designed to handle the case where standard joint keypoints are absent and must be replaced by residual-limb endpoint annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252509/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 2D RGB images of variable resolution (200×143 to 8100×4752) as input and outputs 25 keypoints per person: the 17 standard MSCOCO body joints plus 8 additional residual-limb endpoints (above/below elbow and above/below knee, left and right). A critical structural constraint is that for each limb, the standard downstream joint keypoints and the corresponding residual-limb endpoint are mutually exclusive—the model must decide which limb type is present and suppress the other, rather than predicting all 25 points simultaneously.

## Method

The paper contributes three interlocking components. First, the LDPose dataset (28,065 images, 72,716 instances) is annotated under the guidance of internationally accredited para-athletics classifiers, with a cross-evaluation protocol (40 batches, 20% double-review, full-batch re-review if error rate exceeds 10%). Second, LDLoss is a cross-entropy-based contrastive loss applied to 8 mutually exclusive keypoint pairs (intact joint vs. residual endpoint); it pushes the confidence of the absent keypoint down while pulling the present one up, explicitly teaching the model to distinguish limb types. Third, LD Metrics extend the standard COCO OKS with an adaptive weight γ_i that zeros the contribution of any keypoint whose prediction is anatomically inconsistent with its associated keypoints (e.g., predicting both a residual endpoint and the natural joint on the same limb), thereby penalizing the contradictory predictions that standard COCO metrics silently ignore. The authors benchmark YOLO-Pose, RTMPose, and ViTPose (multiple backbone scales) with and without LDPose fine-tuning and LDLoss, showing that LDLoss consistently improves LD Metrics while COCO Metrics remain misleadingly high.

## Results

On the LDPose test set, ViTPose with a ViT-l backbone achieves the highest COCO AP of 82.6 after fine-tuning, while RTMPose (CSPNeXt-l) reaches 80.8 and YOLO-Pose (Darknet_csp-d53-l) reaches 51.9. After applying the proposed LDLoss, LD Metrics AP improves substantially: ViTPose ViT-l rises from 75.5 to 78.4, RTMPose CSPNeXt-l from 69.1 to 72.6, and YOLO-Pose from 44.4 to 45.5, while COCO AP drops (e.g., ViTPose ViT-l from 82.6 to 80.9), confirming that COCO Metrics overestimate performance on limb-deficient individuals. The best overall LD Metrics result is ViTPose ViT-l + fine-tuning + LDLoss with LD AP 78.4, LD AP50 93.1, and LD AR 81.6.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViTPose consistently outperforms RTMPose and YOLO-Pose across all configurations: its best COCO AP (82.6) exceeds RTMPose (80.8) by 1.8 points and YOLO-Pose (51.9) by 30.7 points. On the proposed LD Metrics, ViTPose ViT-l + LDLoss achieves LD AP 78.4, beating RTMPose CSPNeXt-l + LDLoss (72.6) by 5.8 points and YOLO-Pose + LDLoss (45.5) by 32.9 points. Fine-tuning on LDPose improves COCO AP for all three methods (e.g., YOLO-Pose +7.2, RTMPose +8.0, ViTPose +2.6), but the gains are modest for the already-strong ViTPose. Applying LDLoss uniformly reduces COCO AP (by 1.7–5.1 points) while increasing LD AP (by 1.1–3.5 points), demonstrating that COCO Metrics reward false-positive predictions on missing limbs. The paper's central finding is that the gap between COCO and LD scores (e.g., ViTPose fine-tuned: COCO AP 82.6 vs. LD AP 75.5) exposes a critical evaluation blind spot that LD Metrics correct.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the Limb-Deficient Pose Estimation task, which extends the standard 17-keypoint MSCOCO formulation with 8 additional residual-limb endpoint keypoints, requiring models to determine whether a limb is absent rather than assuming all joints exist. The LDLoss draws conceptually from CLIP-style contrastive learning, repurposing the pairwise alignment mechanism to enforce mutual exclusivity between intact and residual keypoint predictions. The LD Metrics extend the COCO OKS framework with an adaptive anatomical-consistency weight, and the benchmark positions the work against established SOTA methods (YOLO-Pose, RTMPose, ViTPose) that were never designed for or trained on limb-deficient individuals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LDPose is the first large-scale dataset (28k images, 72k instances) for this underrepresented population, and the demonstration that COCO Metrics assign deceptively high scores (e.g., AP 82.6 for ViTPose ViT-l) while the model simultaneously predicts anatomically impossible co-occurring keypoints exposes a fundamental evaluation gap for inclusive pose estimation. Key limitations include a demographic skew (67% Caucasian, predominantly young adults, >50% para-athletic settings) that may limit generalization to everyday scenarios, and the 2D-only annotation precludes 3D pose or prosthetic-aware modeling. Open questions include whether the contrastive LDLoss generalizes to more complex multi-limb deficiencies, how to incorporate prosthetic geometry into keypoint definitions, and whether privacy-preserving collection can scale the dataset beyond its current size.
