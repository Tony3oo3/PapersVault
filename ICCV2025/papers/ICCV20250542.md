## Problem

Moment retrieval models trained on scarce annotated data fall into shallow keyword-feature associations, struggle with ambiguous boundaries between adjacent events, and lack fine-grained semantic discrimination (e.g., 'kicking' vs. 'throwing'). Existing remedies—external pre-trained models, large-scale data synthesis, or additional datasets—introduce costly dependencies, while naively training on augmented data causes overfitting to synthetic distributions and degrades real-world performance.

## Input / Output

The model consumes pre-extracted video features (L×C from CLIP + SlowFast) and text features (K×C from CLIP text encoder), where L is the number of video frames and K the number of text tokens. It produces N predicted temporal segments, each with start/end boundary coordinates and a confidence score, resolved via Hungarian matching against ground-truth segments. The framework is backbone-agnostic, supporting alternative encoders (VGG, I3D) and text representations (GloVe) for secondary benchmarks.

## Method

AMR combines a Splice-and-Boost augmentation strategy with a two-stage cold-start/distillation training pipeline. Splice inserts ground-truth segments into blank intervals of unrelated background videos to create samples with sharp, unambiguous boundaries; Boost injects semantically similar hard negatives (mined via cross-validation false positives) to force the model to discriminate between confusable events. In the cold-start stage, the model is trained exclusively on augmented data with curriculum learning to build foundational boundary and semantic awareness. In the distillation stage, a dual-path decoder introduces original queries (anchored to the frozen cold-start model via a cosine-similarity distillation loss) alongside active queries with dedicated FFNs that adapt to real-data distributions, while a discriminative contrastive loss enforces relative ranking—higher matching scores within ground-truth segments than within ambiguous regions—without suppressing legitimate background correlations.

## Results

On QVHighlights (primary benchmark), AMR achieves 70.13% R1@0.5 and 56.65% R1@0.7 on the validation set, surpassing the previous SOTA BAM-DETR by +5.03% and +5.04% respectively, with a +4.05% gain in average mAP (51.66 vs. 47.61). On the test set, the improvement over BAM-DETR reaches +5.51% in R1@0.5 (68.22 vs. 62.71). On Charades-STA, AMR scores 62.02% R1@0.5 and 52.58 mIoU, outperforming UVCOM by an average of +3.40%; on TACoS it achieves 40.91% R1@0.5 and 38.22 mIoU, a +3.64% average gain over UVCOM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMR outperforms all 12 compared baselines across every reported metric on all three datasets. On QVHighlights, the largest gap is against the previous SOTA BAM-DETR [27]: +5.03% R1@0.5 and +4.05% mAP (avg) on validation, and +5.51% R1@0.5 on test. Against TR-DETR [59], the gain is +3.03% R1@0.5 and +6.57% mAP (avg) on validation. On Charades-STA, AMR beats UVCOM [69] by +2.77% R1@0.5 and +2.58 mIoU, and surpasses TR-DETR [59] (VGG backbone) by +2.09% R1@0.5 and +6.93% R1@0.7. On TACoS, the improvement over UVCOM [69] is +4.52% R1@0.5 and +4.90 mIoU. The ablation further confirms that no single component (augmentation alone or two-stage training alone) suffices; the full Splice-and-Boost + dual-path distillation + discriminative contrastive loss pipeline is required to achieve the reported gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DETR-based moment retrieval lineage (M-DETR, TR-DETR, BAM-DETR, UVCOM, TaskWeave), adopting the standard query-based transformer decoder with Hungarian matching. It departs from prior work primarily in two dimensions: (1) a zero-external-dependency Splice-and-Boost data augmentation strategy that replaces the reliance on external datasets (VIDEOLIGHTS/BLIP-2) or large pre-trained vision-language models (SG-DETR) with structured recombination of existing training samples; and (2) a two-stage cold-start-to-distillation training pipeline with dual-path queries, which addresses the distribution gap between augmented and real data that single-stage frameworks cannot resolve. The paper explicitly positions against three existing research paths—feature alignment optimization, pre-training-based transfer, and external model-based synthesis—arguing all fail to mine discriminative spatiotemporal patterns from limited data without external dependencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in providing a resource-efficient pathway to improve moment retrieval without the deployment costs of billion-parameter external models or the annotation costs of additional data collection. The dual-path distillation mechanism (original queries anchoring cold-start knowledge while active queries adapt to real distributions) is a generalizable technique potentially applicable to other synthetic-to-real transfer problems in temporal grounding. Limitations include the dependence on sufficient video diversity within the training set for meaningful splicing, the added overhead of cross-validation-based hard negative mining, and the fact that 'zero-external-dependency' still presupposes standard pre-trained feature extractors (CLIP, SlowFast). Open questions include whether the Splice-and-Boost strategy generalizes to very long videos with many overlapping events, and whether the dual-path distillation can be extended to multi-query or streaming settings.
