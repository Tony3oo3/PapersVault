## Problem

Existing video grounding methods assume full offline access to the video and rely exclusively on text queries, making them unsuitable for streaming applications where predictions must be made in real time and where users may naturally provide visual cues (images, short clips) alongside or instead of text. This paper introduces Online Video Grounding with Hybrid-modal Queries (OVG-HQ), which requires irrevocable, online moment localization from a sliding window of frames under arbitrary combinations of text, image, and video-segment queries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250881/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each timestamp t the model receives only a sliding window of L frames (advancing every M seconds, with overlapping segments cached) and a hybrid-modal query drawn from {text, image, video segment} or any pair thereof; all modalities are encoded via CLIP into a unified feature dimension. The model outputs, per anchor, a foreground/background score and a length/offset regression that yield a (start, end) boundary; once a prediction is emitted it cannot be revised, so no NMS or future-frame correction is permitted. The model's PMB parameters are reset per video and dynamically updated frame-by-frame during inference via a self-supervised reconstruction loss.

## Method

OVG-HQ-Unify centers on a Parametric Memory Block (PMB) built from a Test-Time Training layer: a self-supervised reconstruction loss compresses the current input into the layer's weights (W^m), and the updated weights then produce a memory-augmented representation that fuses historical context with the current frame without storing raw history. A Transformer decoder performs cross-modal fusion (video snippet features as queries, padded multi-modal query features as keys/values), and a second Transformer decoder over learnable anchor queries produces classification and regression heads. A Prediction Refinement Module concatenates the current prediction and anchor features, feeds them through a second PMB instance, and outputs refined scores so that past predictions inform the current one. To counter modality imbalance during training, a hybrid distillation strategy trains an expert teacher on text+segment queries and distills its classification, regression, and anchor-feature outputs into the unified student via KL-divergence and MSE losses, while the student is also trained with focal classification and L1 regression losses on all three query types in alternating batches.

## Results

On the new QVHighlights-Unify dataset, OVG-HQ-Unify achieves oR@1, IoU=0.5 of 23.26 and omAP@0.5 of 23.09 with text queries, outperforming the best online baseline TwinNet (20.78 / 19.73) by +2.48 and +3.36 points respectively. On ANet-Captions, the method reaches R@1, IoU=0.7 of 14.36 (vs. TwinNet's 12.56, +1.80) and R@1, IoU=0.5 of 26.57 (vs. 25.48). On TACoS and MAD, it achieves R@1, IoU=0.7 of 21.17 and R@5, IoU=0.5 of 3.27, respectively, consistently surpassing all offline-VG-modified and action-detection baselines by 1–12 points depending on the dataset and metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The strongest direct competitor is TwinNet [9], the only other dedicated online VG method; OVG-HQ-Unify beats it on every dataset and metric, with the largest gap on MAD (R@5, IoU=0.5: 3.27 vs. 2.00, +1.27) and ANet-Captions R@1, IoU=0.7 (14.36 vs. 12.56, +1.80). Among offline VG methods adapted to the online setting (TaskWeave, TR-DETR, R2-Tuning, VSLNet, 2DTAN, SeqPAN, SMIN), the best performer is R2-Tuning on TACoS (R@1, IoU=0.5: 21.69), still 9.29 points below OVG-HQ-Unify (30.98); on ANet-Captions the gap is even wider (26.57 vs. 12.89 for VSLNet, the best offline method there). Action-detection baselines modified for VG (OadTR, LSTR, GateHUB) are competitive on ANet-Captions R@1, IoU=0.5 (24.05 for LSTR vs. 26.57 for Ours) but fall behind on the stricter IoU=0.7 threshold and on TACoS/MAD. On the authors' own QVHighlights-Unify benchmark (Table 1), OVG-HQ-Unify (oR@1, IoU=0.5 = 23.26) surpasses TwinNet (20.78) by 2.48 points and the best offline method R2-Tuning (9.30) by 13.96 points, confirming that purpose-built online strategies are essential. Overall, the method wins every head-to-head comparison across all four datasets and all reported metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online video grounding line initiated by Gan et al. [10] (streaming temporal sentence grounding) by adding hybrid-modal queries (text, image, video segment, and combinations), and draws on the Test-Time Training (TTT) framework of Sun et al. [42] to implement a Parametric Memory Block that compresses historical context into network parameters rather than relying on fixed-size hidden states (LSTM) or growing memory banks (self-attention). The sliding-window anchor-based prediction head follows the online temporal action localization scheme of Kim et al. [17]. The hybrid distillation strategy for mitigating modality imbalance is motivated by findings in multi-modal learning [71] but applied here specifically to the video grounding setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real-world video grounding (surveillance, live media) requires both streaming inference and flexible query types, yet prior work treats these as separate problems. The new QVHighlights-Unify dataset and timeliness-aware metrics (oR@n, IoU=m, omAP) provide the first benchmark for this combined setting, which will likely become a standard evaluation target. Open questions include whether the PMB approach scales to much longer streams (hours of video), whether the distillation strategy generalizes to more than three modalities, and whether the modest PMB-over-LSTM gap (~1%) widens with longer temporal contexts. The 46 FPS runtime on a single GPU is encouraging for deployment but has not been validated on edge hardware.
