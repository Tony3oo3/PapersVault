## Problem

Existing video understanding MLLMs treat all visual tokens with equal priority across spatial and temporal dimensions, causing redundant tokens from irrelevant regions to dilute the sparse anomaly signal. Since abnormal events typically occupy only small regions in a few frames, this uniform token processing leads to suboptimal anomaly detection and temporal localization. The paper asks how multimodal architectures can dynamically prioritize anomaly-salient tokens while preserving comprehensive scene understanding.

## Input / Output

The model takes a video of T frames plus a natural language user query as input. Frames are encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT encoder into N patch tokens per frame, while DINOv2 extracts patch embeddings for inter-frame difference computation. The output is a natural language response that identifies whether an anomaly exists, describes the event type and spatial context, and provides temporal localization as a time interval. Evaluation uses multiple-choice QA format where each option pairs an anomaly category with a specific time interval, scored on both overall accuracy and temporal localization accuracy.

## Method

VA-GPT introduces two token-alignment modules on top of a LLaMA-VID-style pipeline (frozen CLIP ViT + Q-Former projector + Vicuna-7B). Spatial Effective Token Selection (SETS) computes per-patch Manhattan distances between DINOv2 embeddings of consecutive frames, then retains only the top-K% patches with the largest inter-frame change via a binary mask, discarding redundant tokens before projection into the LLM space. Temporal Effective Token Generation (TETG) trains a lightweight MLP classifier on CLIP class embeddings to score each frame's anomaly likelihood, then converts the predicted start/end timestamps into natural-language text tokens (e.g., 'There is one of the crime types occurring from <a-start> to <a-end>') that are prepended to the LLM input as temporal priors. Training proceeds in two stages: first, instruction-tuning on 4,077 UCF-Crime videos and 7,730 images with mixed text/image/video QA pairs; second, a brief fine-tuning pass (<150 iterations) using only SETS-filtered tokens to refine spatial alignment. The combination of spatially filtered visual tokens and text-space temporal priors achieves state-of-the-art results with fewer tokens than competing methods.

## Results

On the in-domain UCF-Crime benchmark, VA-GPT achieves 30.69% Total Accuracy and 35.00% Temporal Accuracy, surpassing the strongest competitor Hawkeye (28.60% / 30.00%) and more than doubling the direct baseline LLaMA-VID (14.83% / 26.70%). On the proposed cross-domain XD-Violence benchmark, VA-GPT reaches 26.20% Total Accuracy and 31.02% Temporal Accuracy, again leading all compared methods. On the MMEval benchmark, VA-GPT outperforms A-Guardian on all three sub-metrics (Description 80.83 vs. 79.65, Causes 59.55 vs. 58.92, Effect 51.08 vs. 50.64).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VA-GPT outperforms all nine compared methods on both in-domain and cross-domain benchmarks. Against the closest anomaly-specific competitor Hawkeye [79], VA-GPT gains +2.09% Total Acc. and +5.00% Temporal Acc. in-domain, and +0.90% / +2.52% cross-domain. Over the direct architectural baseline LLaMA-VID [31], the improvement is dramatic: +15.86% Total Acc. and +8.30% Temporal Acc. in-domain, and +7.40% / +7.42% cross-domain. Among general video MLLMs, Video-ChatGPT [42] is the strongest (24.13% in-domain Total), yet VA-GPT still leads by +6.56%. Otter [26], Valley [39], mPLUG [27], and Video-LLaMA2 [7] all trail by 5–10+ points in Total Accuracy. On the MMEval benchmark, VA-GPT narrowly beats A-Guardian [12] on all three sub-metrics (Description, Causes, Effect), confirming consistent gains across evaluation axes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LLaMA-VID architecture (frozen CLIP ViT encoder + Q-Former + Vicuna-7B) as its backbone, but departs from it by replacing the uniform feeding of all visual tokens with a selective spatial filtering mechanism (SETS) based on inter-frame DINOv2 patch differences. Conceptually, it extends the line of anomaly-aware video MLLMs (Hawkeye, A-Guardian) by addressing the spatial and temporal redundancy problem that prior methods ignore: instead of treating all tokens equally, it assigns different roles to spatially salient patches and injects temporal anomaly priors as natural-language tokens. The approach also draws on the broader video understanding MLLM literature (Video-Chat, Otter, Valley, mPLUG) for its general pipeline, while the token-selection idea is novel to the anomaly domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic treatment of token efficiency specifically for video anomaly understanding in MLLMs, and its cross-domain benchmark (XD-Violence-based) fills a gap in evaluating generalization beyond UCF-Crime's surveillance style. However, the anomaly-aware classifier is a lightweight MLP trained on a single dataset, raising questions about whether the temporal prior generalizes to unseen anomaly types or domains. The spatial filtering relies on inter-frame difference, which may miss anomalies that are static or involve subtle changes. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer videos, more diverse anomaly categories, and whether the two-stage training strategy is necessary or could be replaced by end-to-end learning.
