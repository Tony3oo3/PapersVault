## Problem

Existing image stylization methods face a persistent trade-off: collection-based fine-tuning approaches (e.g., style-specific LoRAs) capture rich stylistic patterns but overemphasize style at the expense of content preservation, while score distillation methods applied to image editing overemphasize style and fail to maintain source identity. The paper addresses this by reframing stylization as a style distribution matching problem that explicitly balances style alignment with content fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251293/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image, a text description of its content, and a pretrained style-specific LoRA module (integrated into Stable Diffusion 1.5) as inputs, and produces a single stylized image in the target artistic style. In single-image mode, optimization runs for 500 iterations in the VAE latent space; in feed-forward mode, a lightweight ~43MB generator network is trained at 512×512 resolution to enable one-step inference. The text prompt serves dual roles: conditioning the diffusion models during score computation and defining the editing instruction for the relevance map.

## Method

SMS minimizes the KL divergence between the generated image distribution and the target style distribution by computing a gradient from the difference between two noise predictions: a fixed style-LoRA-integrated model (ε_style) and a dynamically trained fake model (ε_fake^φ). Two key regularizers balance style and content: (1) Progressive Spectrum Regularization applies a timestep-dependent low-pass filter in the DCT domain, preserving more low-frequency structure at high-noise steps and allowing high-frequency stylization at low-noise steps; (2) Semantic-Aware Gradient Refinement computes a relevance map from the disagreement between noise predictions with and without style instructions, using it as an element-wise weight to emphasize stylization in semantically important regions. An adaptive narrowing sampling strategy gradually reduces the timestep upper bound over iterations to prevent excessive high-frequency editing. The formulation extends naturally from pixel-space optimization to parameter-space training of a feed-forward generator, enabling efficient one-step stylization.

## Results

On the PIE-benchmark with Ghibli style, SMS achieves the best content preservation (LPIPS 0.326, CFSD 0.102) and the best overall quality (ArtFID 18.686), while attaining competitive style fidelity (FID 13.089, second only to Style-LoRA's 12.267). For Oil Painting style, SMS again leads in LPIPS (0.431), CFSD (0.128), and ArtFID (27.055), and achieves the best FID (17.905). In the feed-forward setting on LHQ, SMS attains the lowest LPIPS (0.268) and ArtFID (17.079) among all compared methods, with a competitive FID of 12.472.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMS outperforms all five single-image baselines on content preservation metrics (LPIPS and CFSD) for both Ghibli and Oil Painting styles, with the largest margins over FreeStyle (LPIPS 0.326 vs. 0.690) and StyleID (LPIPS 0.326 vs. 0.608). Against Style-LoRA, SMS trades a small FID gap (13.089 vs. 12.267) and PickScore (1.487 vs. 2.067) for substantially better content preservation (LPIPS 0.326 vs. 0.438) and ArtFID (18.686 vs. 19.077); the authors note Style-LoRA serves as an upper bound since it generates directly from the target style distribution. DDS and InstantStyle-Plus both suffer from content distortion and oversaturation, yielding LPIPS values 0.19–0.21 higher than SMS. In the feed-forward setting, SMS beats Scenimefy on LPIPS (0.268 vs. 0.422) and ArtFID (17.079 vs. 18.561) while Scenimefy retains a slight FID edge (12.054 vs. 12.472) due to direct access to real anime training data; PDS is clearly outperformed on all three metrics (LPIPS 0.427, FID 16.229, ArtFID 24.590).

## Relation to Prior Work

SMS builds directly on the score distillation lineage (SDS, VSD, DMD), adopting DMD's KL-divergence formulation between two score-function-estimated distributions, but departs in two key ways: it operates on real images rather than noise-to-image generation, and it replaces the generic pretrained DM with a style-specific LoRA-integrated model to define the target distribution. For content preservation, it moves beyond the spatial-domain identity losses used by DDS, PDS, and CDS by introducing a frequency-domain progressive spectrum regularization (DCT-based low-pass filtering with timestep-dependent thresholds) and a semantic-aware gradient weighting derived from diffusion relevance maps [18]. The method also extends the optimization target from a single image to network parameters, enabling distillation into a lightweight feed-forward generator—a direction not explored by prior score-distillation editing methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the persistent style-vs-content trade-off in diffusion-based stylization with a principled distribution-matching framework, and the progressive frequency-domain regularization offers a reusable technique for any task requiring gradual structural-to-detail editing. The feed-forward distillation (43 MB model, one-step inference) makes the approach practically deployable beyond per-image optimization. Open questions include whether the method generalizes to 3D representations (NeRF, 3D Gaussian Splatting) as the authors suggest, whether it works without a pre-trained style LoRA (i.e., from a single exemplar), and how it scales to styles with very different frequency characteristics. A limitation is that style quality is bounded by the LoRA's own distribution, as acknowledged by the Style-LoRA baseline outperforming SMS on pure style metrics.
