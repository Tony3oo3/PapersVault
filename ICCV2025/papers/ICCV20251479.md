## Problem

Updating 3D scene representations as environments evolve over time remains an open challenge: existing continual learning methods for novel view synthesis suffer from catastrophic forgetting, require re-capturing the entire scene, or are too slow for practical use. No prior approach simultaneously achieves photorealistic reconstruction, efficient local updates without forgetting, and the ability to recover or merge past scene states.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251479/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an existing 3DGS reconstruction G^{t-1} and a small set of new RGB images (960×540) capturing only the locally changed region, along with COLMAP-estimated camera poses aligned to the existing coordinate system. Crucially, it does not require re-capturing unchanged areas or knowing poses for static regions. The output is an updated 3DGS scene G^t, a 3D set of changed Gaussians O^t, and—uniquely—support for recovering any prior scene state from stored localized deltas (only ~36 MB per step vs. ~1173 MB for full storage).

## Method

CL-Splats operates in three stages. First, it detects 2D changes by rendering the existing reconstruction from new poses and thresholding per-patch DINOv2 cosine similarity against the real image, then dilating the binary mask. Second, it lifts changes to 3D via majority voting: Gaussians projected into the 2D mask in more than K views are marked as changed, and new points are sampled in the region to handle newly appearing objects. Third, it performs a locally constrained optimization: HDBSCAN clusters the changed Gaussians, spheres are fitted around each cluster as bounding primitives, and a custom kernel renders and backpropagates only within the dynamic 2D mask of those Gaussians—yielding identical gradients to full 3DGS while reducing optimization time by 60%. Gaussians whose centers drift outside the sphere union are pruned, enforcing strict spatial locality and preventing boundary artifacts.

## Results

On the CL-Splats dataset, CL-Splats achieves 40.13 dB PSNR and 0.015 LPIPS on synthetic scenes, and 28.25 dB PSNR and 0.065 LPIPS on real-world scenes, while maintaining real-time rendering at 223 FPS (synthetic) and 135 FPS (real). On the CL-NeRF dataset, CL-Splats reaches 29.98 dB PSNR, outperforming CL-NeRF (27.30 dB) and 3DGS (11.07 dB). The dense-capture upper bound for the synthetic scenes is ~42 dB, meaning CL-Splats recovers 95% of the maximum achievable quality from sparse views of only the changed region.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CL-Splats dominates all four baselines on every metric across both synthetic and real-world splits. Against the strongest continual-learning baseline CL-NeRF [42], CL-Splats gains +10.1 dB PSNR on synthetic scenes (40.13 vs. 30.06) and +5.0 dB on real scenes (28.25 vs. 23.27), while rendering at 223 FPS versus <1 FPS for CL-NeRF. CLNeRF [4] trails further behind at 26.76 dB synthetic PSNR and suffers from oversmoothed renderings. The vanilla 3DGS baseline (21.99 dB synthetic) degrades severely because re-optimizing from sparse views destroys unconstrained regions, and the 3DGS+M variant (15.13 dB) confirms that naively restricting photometric loss to 2D masks without 3D-aware constraints is harmful. GaussianEditor [8] performs best among editing methods on real scenes (24.13 dB) but still lags CL-Splats by 4.1 dB and was not designed for observation-driven updates. On the more challenging CL-NeRF dataset (complex reflectances, textureless walls), CL-Splats still leads CL-NeRF by +2.7 dB PSNR (29.98 vs. 27.30) and 3DGS by +18.9 dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the continual scene reconstruction line initiated by CL-NeRF and CLNeRF, which update implicit NeRF representations over time, and on 3D Gaussian Splatting as an explicit, real-time-renderable scene representation. It departs from prior work by introducing a localized optimization pipeline—DINOv2-based 2D change detection, mask-majority-voting to identify affected 3D Gaussians, and sphere-pruning to constrain updates spatially—rather than re-optimizing the full scene. The explicit 3DGS representation also enables applications (history recovery, concurrent non-overlapping updates) that are intractable for implicit NeRF-based continual learners, positioning CL-Splats as a bridge between the photorealism of NeRF continual learning and the efficiency and editability of Gaussian Splatting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 75× speedup over CL-NeRF combined with a 10+ dB PSNR improvement makes continual scene updates practical for robotics and mixed-reality deployment where re-optimizing entire scenes is infeasible. The explicit representation unlocks history recovery (36 MB per step vs. 1173 MB for naive storage) and seamless merging of concurrent changes, opening applications in long-term environmental monitoring and embodied AI. Key limitations remain: the method assumes localized changes and cannot handle global illumination shifts, and it inherits 3DGS's difficulty with high-frequency detail in large-scale outdoor scenes. Open questions include whether the sphere-pruning strategy generalizes to non-rigid deformations and whether the approach scales to driving-scale environments.
