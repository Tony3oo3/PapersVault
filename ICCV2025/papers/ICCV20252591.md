## Problem

Generating long-duration, multi-view consistent driving videos with fine-grained controllability remains unsolved: existing methods either produce short clips with degrading temporal consistency, lack multi-view spatial coherence, or rely on coarse scene-level descriptions that limit diversity and editing precision. No prior framework simultaneously supports 20-second surround-view generation, diverse condition-based editing (weather, time-of-day, object placement), and stable autoregressive rollout without reference frames.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a flexible combination of optional reference frames (3 frames from n viewpoints), per-frame per-view text descriptions (generated at 2 Hz via DriveMLM), 3D bounding boxes, HD maps, and camera intrinsics/extrinsics. 3D conditions are projected into sparse color-coded images matching the original resolution and fed through a lightweight adapter, while text is processed by three encoders (CLIP, T5, mT5) with joint attention. The output is a 20-second multi-view driving video (6 viewpoints, 16 frames per viewpoint in evaluation) generated autoregressively in chunks, with the model also supporting generation from conditions alone without any reference frames.

## Method

UniMLVG extends SD3's MM-DiT backbone with a temporal module (attention over T×(VHW)×C) and a cross-view module (attention over V×(THW)×C), both using absolute sine-cosine positional encodings for frames and viewpoints. The key architectural novelty is explicit perspective modeling: camera rays (center and direction) are encoded via multi-frequency sine-cosine positional encoding in a unified coordinate system and injected as spatial features into the video latent, outperforming implicit camera-ID encoding by 12.5% FVD and 22.4% FID. Training employs four tasks (video prediction, image prediction, video generation, image generation) sampled via a mask to prevent over-reliance on reference frames and mitigate autoregressive error accumulation. A three-stage curriculum first trains the temporal module on 1,486 hours of single-view OpenDV-Youtube data, then trains temporal and cross-view modules on multi-view nuScenes/Waymo/Argoverse2 data with 3D conditions, and finally performs full fine-tuning for 1–2 epochs.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set (150 scenes, 6 viewpoints, 16 frames each), UniMLVG achieves FID 5.8 and FVD 36.1, representing 48.2% and 35.2% improvements over the second-best method (DriveDreamer-2: FID 11.2, FVD 55.7). For condition adherence, UniMLVG attains mIoU_road 70.81 and mIoU_vehicle 29.12, surpassing the next best by 8.8% and 7.8% respectively, while achieving mAP_obj 22.50 (second only to DiVE's 24.55). The model generates 20-second multi-view videos, nearly three times longer than DriveDreamer-2's 7-second output.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniMLVG outperforms all seven baselines on FID and FVD, with the most notable margins over DriveDreamer-2 (FID 5.8 vs 11.2; FVD 36.1 vs 55.7) and DiVE (FVD 36.1 vs 94.6, a 61.8% improvement). On condition adherence, UniMLVG achieves the best mIoU_road (70.81) and mIoU_vehicle (29.12), beating Drive-WM (65.07) and MagicDrive (61.05) on road segmentation. The sole metric where UniMLVG trails is mAP_obj (22.50 vs DiVE's 24.55), though the paper notes DiVE requires distinct classifier-free guidance scales per condition, increasing inference time. Compared to DriveDreamer-2, UniMLVG also generates videos nearly three times longer (20s vs 7s) without stitching views into a single image, reducing memory usage. Against MagicDrive and DreamForge, UniMLVG improves FVD by roughly 6× and 6.2× respectively, while matching or exceeding their 20-second duration capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniMLVG extends the DiT-based multi-view driving video generation line established by DriveDreamer-2, DiVE, and DreamForge, adopting the SD3/MM-DiT backbone with rectified flow training. It departs from prior work in three key ways: (1) replacing implicit camera parameter encoding (as in MagicDrive) with explicit camera-ray spatial encoding, (2) introducing a four-task training objective (VP, IP, VG, IG) to combat autoregressive error accumulation, and (3) employing a multi-stage curriculum that first trains on large-scale single-view data before multi-view fine-tuning. The lightweight T2I-Adapter-style condition injection replaces the heavier ControlNet used by DreamForge, and image-level text descriptions replace the scene-level prompts common in prior driving video generators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical bottleneck in autonomous driving: the need for diverse, temporally consistent, multi-view driving videos that can be generated under precise 3D and textual control to augment scarce real-world data. The multi-task training strategy and explicit perspective modeling offer transferable techniques for any multi-view video generation setting beyond driving. However, evaluation is limited to nuScenes (150 scenes, 16 frames), the 20-second generation length remains modest for full driving scenarios, and the paper does not explore real-time inference or integration with downstream planning modules. Open questions include whether the explicit ray encoding generalizes to arbitrary camera rigs and whether the multi-task ratios found here transfer to other domains.
