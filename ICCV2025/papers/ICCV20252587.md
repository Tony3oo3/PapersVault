## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models are computationally expensive, and while prior work has focused on reducing per-inference cost through distillation or faster solvers, the common workflow of generating large sets of images from correlated prompts remains unoptimized. This paper asks whether early-stage denoising computations can be reused across semantically similar prompts, exploiting the coarse-to-fine generation property of diffusion models where initial steps encode shared structural content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252587/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of N text prompts, encodes them via an off-the-shelf text encoder (e.g., CLIP), and produces N generated images through a modified latent diffusion pipeline. The compute budget is controlled by a single hyperparameter τ that governs how quickly embeddings specialize from shared to prompt-specific; at τ=0 the method reduces to standard per-prompt diffusion, while higher τ values increase sharing. The approach is training-free and operates on pre-trained latent diffusion models such as Kandinsky 2.2, Karlo, and Stable Diffusion.

## Method

The method constructs a hierarchical embedding tree via agglomerative clustering on prompt embeddings (using cosine distance between cluster means), where each internal node stores the average embedding of its children and a heterogeneity score reflecting the distance between its two child clusters. During denoising, a linearly decreasing threshold φ(k) = τ(1 − k/K) determines which tree level to query at each step: early steps use high-level shared embeddings (redundant computation is cached and reused), while later steps descend toward leaf-level prompt-specific embeddings. This reduces the number of unique denoiser evaluations from O(N·K) toward O(log N · K) for balanced trees. The approach is most effective on models trained with a text-to-image prior (e.g., Kandinsky, Karlo), which defer fine-grained detail generation to later steps, leaving more early steps available for sharing; on models like Stable Diffusion that establish structure early, savings are limited.

## Results

Across four datasets (GenAI Bench, Prompt Template, Style Variations, Animals), the method saves 50.43%–73.93% of total diffusion compute relative to standard per-prompt denoising while achieving a higher VQA Score on 50.75%–52.80% of images. At a fixed compute budget of approximately 31 steps per image, the method produces higher VQA scores than standard diffusion using the full 40-step budget, indicating that shared early-step computation yields a more stable denoising trajectory. The largest savings (73.93%) occur on the most homogeneous set (Style Variations, 100 prompts), while the most diverse set (GenAI Bench, 1600 prompts) still achieves 50.43% savings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is standard diffusion, where each prompt is denoised independently for K=40 steps. The method consistently outperforms this baseline on VQA Score at every fixed compute budget below 40 steps (Figure 5), with the gap widening as the budget shrinks. At matched quality (tuned to match 40-step standard VQA), the method saves 50–74% of compute depending on dataset homogeneity, and the win rate stays above 50% on all four datasets, confirming no quality degradation. The clustering ablation (Table 2) shows that replacing semantic agglomerative clustering with random clustering drops savings to under 6%, confirming that the hierarchical structure is essential. The method also outperforms standard diffusion qualitatively at 18 steps, producing sharper fine-grained details (e.g., shirt wrinkles, cloud contours) that standard diffusion over-smooths at that budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as orthogonal to the dominant line of per-inference diffusion acceleration (distillation, fast ODE solvers) by targeting a different axis of redundancy: correlation across multiple prompts in a single generation batch. It builds on the well-known coarse-to-fine generation property of diffusion models and the latent diffusion framework of Rombach et al. [28], but repurposes agglomerative clustering on CLIP text embeddings to construct a hierarchical tree that enables shared early denoising steps. The paper also connects to prompt-expansion tools (DreamSheets, Promptify) that generate structured prompt sets, but focuses on the downstream generation efficiency rather than prompt construction itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50–74% compute savings for image-set generation have direct practical and environmental implications for large-scale creative workflows, and the method is training-free and trivially integrable into existing pipelines. A notable limitation is that the approach is strongly model-dependent: it works well for Kandinsky and Karlo (trained with UnCLIP-style text-to-image priors) but degrades significantly for Stable Diffusion, which establishes structure too early in denoising. Open questions include whether the method extends to autoregressive coarse-to-fine models (e.g., VAR), whether the quality-improvement effect generalizes beyond the Prompt Template dataset, and how performance scales when prompt sets are small but highly diverse.
