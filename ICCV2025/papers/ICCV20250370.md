## Problem

Existing multimodal RGB-X trackers use rigid, modality-specific branches that are activated based on a priori knowledge of the input sensor, preventing any cross-modal knowledge transfer during training. Since comprehensive datasets containing all modalities simultaneously do not exist—only paired RGB-X data is available at a time—this strict isolation wastes the opportunity for shared feature learning across sensing domains. The paper asks: how can a tracker trained on one RGB-X pair (e.g., RGB-Depth) benefit from knowledge learned on other pairs (e.g., RGB-Event, RGB-Thermal) at inference?

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB template and search region paired with an auxiliary modality (depth, thermal, or event) template and search region, all processed through a frozen RGB foundation tracker (OSTrack for XTrack-B, DropMAE for XTrack-L). Only the MeME module parameters are trainable, keeping the backbone frozen. At inference, only a single modality is available, and the model must select the appropriate expert without requiring explicit modality labels beyond the input itself, producing a standard object bounding box per frame.

## Method

The core contribution is the Mixture of Modal Experts (MeME) module, inserted after each attention and FFN block of the frozen transformer backbone. MeME routes tokens through modality-specific experts (2 per modality) and a shared expert, using a soft router supervised by a deliberately weak classification loss (targeting ~80% accuracy) so that when features from different modalities are sufficiently similar, the router 'confuses' them and activates cross-modal experts, enabling knowledge transfer. The shared expert incorporates an edge-gated mechanism with Laplacian-initialized convolution to capture high-frequency commonalities across modalities. All experts operate in a low-dimensional latent space (k << c) for efficiency, and their fused output is used as a gating signal to prompt the RGB tokens back to the full embedding dimension, making the frozen RGB features modality-aware without modifying the backbone.

## Results

On the RGB-Depth benchmark (DepthTrack), XTrack-L achieves a F-score of 64.8, surpassing the previous best SDSTrack (61.4) by +3.4 points; on VOT-RGBD2022, XTrack-B reaches EAO 74.0 vs. SDSTrack's 72.8. On RGB-Thermal (LasHeR), XTrack-L attains 73.1% Precision, outperforming OneTracker (67.2%) by +5.9 points. On RGB-Event (VisEvent), XTrack-L achieves 80.5% Precision, exceeding OneTracker (76.7%) by +3.8 points. Overall, the paper reports an average +3% precision improvement over the current SOTA across all three RGB-X tracking scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

XTrack outperforms all 23 baselines across every benchmark. Among unified multi-modal trackers, the closest competitors are SDSTrack, OneTracker, UnTrack, and ViPT; XTrack-B edges out SDSTrack by +0.1 F-score on DepthTrack and +1.2 EAO on VOT-RGBD2022, while XTrack-L extends the lead to +3.4 F-score. On RGB-Thermal (LasHeR), XTrack-L achieves 73.1% Precision, beating OneTracker (67.2%) by +5.9 points and SDSTrack (66.5%) by +6.6 points; on RGBT234, XTrack-L reaches 87.8% MPR vs. OneTracker's 85.7%. On RGB-Event (VisEvent), XTrack-L attains 80.5% Precision, surpassing OneTracker and SDSTrack (both 76.7%) by +3.8 points. Modality-specific trackers (e.g., DeT, SPT, SiamRCNN_E, PrDiMP_E) are consistently outperformed by a wider margin, confirming that the cross-modal knowledge sharing strategy generalizes beyond the training modality. Notably, XTrack requires no parameter switching between modalities at inference, unlike all unified baselines that activate modality-specific branches based on a priori input type.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on frozen RGB foundation trackers (OSTrack, DropMAE) and adapts the Mixture-of-Experts architecture from DeepSeekMoE (balance loss, shared experts) to the multimodal tracking domain. It departs fundamentally from prior unified multimodal trackers (ViPT, UnTrack, OneTracker, SDSTrack) that use rigid, a priori modality-specific branches with no cross-modal interaction during training. The key conceptual departure is repurposing MoE routing as a soft classifier with a deliberately weakened loss, so that 'confusion' between modalities becomes a signal for knowledge transfer rather than a failure mode — a design not present in prior MoE-for-vision or multimodal tracking work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical deployment gap: in the field, only one auxiliary modality is available at a time, yet training data is only available as RGB-X pairs. By enabling cross-modal knowledge sharing during training while preserving single-modality inference, XTrack offers a practical path toward generalist RGB-X trackers without requiring all sensors simultaneously. Limitations include evaluation on only three modalities (depth, thermal, event) and the assumption that paired data is available for each; whether the approach scales to more modalities or works with unpaired/mismatched data remains open. The finding that an 80% classification probability (not full separation) is optimal suggests a broader principle about controlled ambiguity in multi-domain learning that could inform other tasks.
