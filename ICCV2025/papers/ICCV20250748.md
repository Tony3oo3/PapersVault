## Problem

DUSt3R's viewpoint-invariant point maps cannot represent dynamic scenes because 3D points move over time, breaking the invariance property that enables direct correspondence and reconstruction. MonST3R partially addressed this by freezing time, but lost the ability to predict cross-time correspondences directly, forcing reliance on a separate optical flow module. The paper asks whether a minimal point map formulation can be both viewpoint- and time-invariant, enabling a single feed-forward network to solve 4D tasks without auxiliary modules.

## Input / Output

The network takes a pair of RGB images at resolutions (512,288) or (512,336) and outputs four point maps—two per image, each expressing the 3D point at both timestamps t₁ and t₂—all defined in the reference frame of the first camera π₁. Each point map is accompanied by a per-pixel confidence map in [0,1]. From these four maps, scene flow (difference of same-image maps at different times), motion segmentation (thresholding point displacement), camera pose (Procrustes alignment of same-time maps), and rigid object pose (Umeyama alignment of masked points) are all derived in closed form without additional networks.

## Method

The key insight is that invariance in dynamic scenes requires fixing both viewpoint and time simultaneously: for each image Iᵢ, the network predicts a pair of point maps Pᵢ(t₁,π₁) and Pᵢ(t₂,π₁), so that maps sharing the same (t,π) argument are directly comparable. Architecturally, this is a minimal extension of DUSt3R's transformer encoder-decoder backbone: the single regression head per image is replaced by two heads (four total), each outputting 3D coordinates plus confidence, initialized from the original DUSt3R head. Training uses a confidence-calibrated per-pixel regression loss with scale normalization, supervised on a mixture of synthetic data (MOVi-G, a new Kubric-based dataset with spline-interpolated camera trajectories and randomized intrinsics) and real data (Waymo LiDAR, PointOdyssey), where cross-time supervision is omitted for static scenes. The dual-map design means scene flow is simply P₁(t₂,π₁)−P₁(t₁,π₁) and cross-view correspondences are direct 3D point matches, eliminating the optical flow dependency that MonST3R requires.

## Results

On dynamic point cloud reconstruction (Table 3), DPM achieves Lrel errors of 0.041–0.049 on Kubric-F and 0.057–0.079 on Kubric-G, substantially outperforming MonST3R (0.047–0.122 and 0.061–0.188 respectively), with the largest gap on cross-time predictions. For object pose tracking on Kubric-F, DPM reduces rotation error from 56.1° to 33.7° and translation error from 0.504 to 0.053 compared to MonST3R. On scene flow (Table 4), DPM achieves 3D EPE of 0.051 on Waymo and 0.104 on Kubric-G using RGB-only input, surpassing RAFT-3D (which uses ground-truth depth) on both datasets. On TUM-dynamics camera pose (Table 5), DPM achieves ATE 0.056, RPE trans 0.014, and RPE rot 0.836°, beating MonST3R (0.074/0.019/0.905°) and matching CasualSAM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MonST3R (the primary baseline), DPM wins decisively on all dynamic reconstruction metrics: on Waymo, Lrel drops from ~0.20–0.25 to ~0.065 across all four point maps, and on Kubric-F the cross-time predictions improve from 0.095/0.122 to 0.047/0.049. Object pose tracking error is reduced by ~40% in rotation (56.1° → 33.7°) and ~10× in translation (0.504 → 0.053). Against RAFT-3D (which has the advantage of ground-truth depth input), DPM is slightly worse on Kubric-F scene flow (0.081 vs 0.051 EPE) but substantially better on Kubric-G (0.104 vs 4.067) and Waymo (0.051 vs 0.150), and uniquely supports Object Flow estimation which RAFT-3D cannot perform. Against Depth Anything V2 (single-frame depth), DPM is competitive on Sintel (0.311 vs 0.367 Abs Rel) and Bonn (0.064 vs 0.106) in the video depth setting. Against DUSt3R with masking on TUM-dynamics, DPM improves ATE from 0.127 to 0.056 and RPE rotation from 3.099° to 0.836°, confirming that explicit dynamic modeling outperforms static reconstruction with occlusion handling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends DUSt3R's viewpoint-invariant point maps by adding a second invariance axis—time—so that each image in a pair is mapped to two point clouds (one per timestamp), yielding four point maps total. It departs from MonST3R, which attempted a similar dynamic extension but only predicted two of the four maps and relied on an external optical flow network to recover cross-time correspondences, breaking the invariance property that makes DUSt3R's representation so powerful. The architectural change is minimal (two additional regression heads on the DUSt3R transformer backbone), and the core contribution is the representation itself: the observation that fixing both viewpoint and time is the minimal condition for invariance in dynamic scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that a single feed-forward network can simultaneously recover camera pose, depth, scene flow, motion segmentation, and rigid object tracking without test-time optimization, DPM offers a unifying alternative to the patchwork of specialized modules (depth estimators, matchers, segmenters) that current dynamic reconstruction pipelines require. The clean invariance formulation also makes downstream task reductions (e.g., scene flow as a simple difference of two point maps) trivial, which could accelerate future work on 4D understanding. Open questions include scaling beyond image pairs to longer video sequences, handling non-rigid deformations more explicitly, and validating the approach on more diverse real-world dynamic scenes beyond the synthetic-heavy training mix.
