## Problem

Generating realistic interactions among three or more characters remains difficult: existing conditional diffusion methods (InterControl, FreeMotion) rely on a single shared prompt for the entire group, producing uniform and overly simplified interactions, while supervised multi-person models require dedicated training data that is intractable to collect for arbitrary group sizes. Additionally, these approaches lack fine-grained per-character control and suffer from physical artifacts such as body overlap and penetration as group size grows.

## Input / Output

The user specifies an ordered list of pairwise interactions, each pairing a reference character with a target character and a semantically specific text prompt (e.g., 'one person is posing while the other is taking a picture'), rather than a single group-level description. The method consumes the frozen weights of a pre-trained two-person diffusion model (InterGen) and, for long-duration generation, the final n frames of existing motion sequences as inpainting context. Output is a set of M multi-person 3D skeletal motion sequences in non-canonical global coordinates (joint positions, velocities, local rotations, and foot-ground contact), preserving spatial relationships essential for interaction modeling.

## Method

PINO decomposes arbitrary-sized group interactions into sequential pairwise generations, where each new character is synthesized by conditioning a masked variant of a pre-trained two-person diffusion model on a reference character's motion and a pair-specific prompt. The key technical contribution is post-hoc noise optimization: the initial latent noise is refined by backpropagating through the DDIM denoising process to minimize a differentiable loss combining overlap-avoidance penalties (root-position distance thresholds) and spatio-temporal control penalties (root position, movement region, orientation, and relative positioning constraints). For long-duration sequences, motion inpainting integrates the final frames of prior segments at every denoising step with a boundary acceleration penalty for smooth transitions, and prompt switching between pairs enables role changes mid-sequence (e.g., alternating handshakes). The entire pipeline is training-free, requiring only the frozen two-person model weights and user-specified pairwise prompts.

On the multi-person interaction generation task (Table 4), PINO-InterGen dramatically reduces overlap compared to baselines: 0.000 for pair (1,2), 0.002 for (1,3), 0.019 for (1,4), and 0.069 for (1,5), versus InterGen (0.119, 0.799, 0.937, 0.977) and FreeMotion (0.255, 0.806, 0.964, 0.991). PINO also achieves the lowest FID across all pairs (12.92–16.91 vs. 13.10–19.50 for InterGen and 19.38–25.81 for FreeMotion). ## Baselines & Comparisons

PINO-InterGen is compared against two baselines: InterGen (the underlying two-person diffusion model) and FreeMotion (a ControlNet-based sequential multi-person generator). Against InterGen, PINO achieves a near-zero overlap (0.000–0.069) across all five-person pairs, whereas InterGen's overlap escalates from 0.119 to 0.977 as more characters are added, indicating severe body penetration. PINO also improves FID by 1.5–2.6 points over InterGen at each pair (e.g., 16.911 vs. 19.501 for pair (1,5)), while Foot Skate and Max Acc. remain comparable (within ~0.005–0.011). Against FreeMotion, PINO's advantage is even more pronounced: FreeMotion's overlap reaches 0.991 for pair (1,5) and its FID degrades to 25.671, while PINO maintains 0.069 overlap and 16.911 FID. The trade-off is a slight increase in Foot Skate and Max Acc. for PINO at larger group sizes (0.142/0.045 vs. 0.130/0.042 for FreeMotion at pair (1,5)), reflecting the cost of enforcing spatial constraints through noise optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

PINO builds directly on InterGen, a pretrained two-person interaction diffusion model, repurposing it as a motion prior for arbitrary-sized group generation. It departs from prior multi-person approaches (InterControl, FreeMotion, Shan et al.) that rely on a single shared prompt and ControlNet-style sequential conditioning, instead decomposing group dynamics into semantically distinct pairwise interactions each guided by its own text prompt. The noise-optimization component draws from image-generation techniques (Attend-and-Excite, InitNo) and single-person motion work (ProgMoGen, DNO), but is the first to apply differentiable physics-based penalties to the initial noise of a multi-person diffusion model in a training-free setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PINO offers a practical, training-free path to generating physically plausible, user-controllable group interactions of arbitrary size without dedicated multi-person datasets—a significant barrier for animation, gaming, and robotics pipelines. Key limitations remain: the framework inherits the expressiveness ceiling of the two-person base model (higher-order interactions like A pushing B into C are unmodeled), hand-region penetrations persist due to missing hand data in training, and computational cost from iterative noise optimization limits real-time use. Open questions include whether the pairwise decomposition generalizes to truly coordinated group behaviors (e.g., synchronized dance) and whether more expressive base models (multi-person diffusion) can be plugged in without architectural changes.
