## Problem

Existing text-guided image editing techniques rely on cross-attention manipulation or latent inversion designed for diffusion models, but these approaches fail when transferred to autoregressive (AR) image generators. AR models suffer from spatial poverty of attention maps (cross-attention carries minimal structural information) and sequential accumulation of structural errors, so naive token modification or attention injection disrupts global layout and content coherence. No training-free editing strategy existed for AR visual generative models.

## Input / Output

The method requires three inputs: an AR-generated image at 512×512 resolution, the original text prompt used to generate that image, and a new editing text prompt. The original image is re-encoded into its discrete VQ token sequence, which serves as the anchor reference during decoding. The output is a single edited RGB image that preserves the structural layout of the original while reflecting the semantic changes specified by the editing prompt.

## Method

ISLock introduces Anchor Token Matching (ATM): at each autoregressive decoding step, K candidate tokens are sampled from the conditional distribution under the editing prompt, and the candidate with the smallest Euclidean distance to the corresponding anchor token (from the original prompt's generation) in latent space is selected. A dynamic window mechanism progressively shrinks the candidate pool from 100% to 40% of K over the generation sequence, enforcing strict structural alignment early and relaxing constraints later. An Adaptive Constraint Relaxation (AdaCR) threshold τ allows the model to fall back to standard argmax sampling when no candidate is sufficiently close, preserving generative autonomy for regions that genuinely need to change. This implicitly aligns self-attention patterns with the reference image's structural blueprint without any explicit attention injection or parameter modification.

## Results

On the PIE-Bench subset (5 editing categories, 512×512 resolution), ISLock achieves a Structure Distance of 31.79, second only to Null-text Inversion (18.42) and PnPInversion (24.81) among all compared methods. For semantic alignment, ISLock attains CLIP Similarity scores of 24.19 (whole image) and 21.33 (edited region), ranking second to PnPInversion (25.56 / 21.50) and Prompt-to-Prompt (26.70 / 21.43). Background preservation metrics (PSNR 19.75, SSIM 76.71) are competitive with inversion-free instruction-based methods like InstructPix2Pix (PSNR 19.69, SSIM 76.98) but trail top inversion-based methods such as Null-text Inversion (PSNR 25.68, SSIM 85.71).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ISLock dramatically outperforms both AR baselines: compared to Naive Prompt Modification (NPM), it reduces Structure Distance from 113.95 to 31.79 (3.6× improvement) and improves PSNR from 12.14 to 19.75; against PnP-AR (direct attention map injection), it cuts Structure Distance from 103.94 to 31.79 and raises SSIM from 58.25 to 76.71, confirming that explicit attention transplantation is incompatible with AR sequential generation. Against diffusion-based methods, ISLock achieves the second-best Structure Distance (31.79) behind Null-text Inversion (18.42) and PnPInversion (24.81), and the second-best CLIP Edited score (21.33) behind PnPInversion (21.50), while trailing on background fidelity metrics (PSNR, LPIPS, SSIM) where Null-text Inversion and PnPInversion lead. Among inversion-free instruction-based methods (InstructPix2Pix, MGIE), ISLock matches or exceeds them on Structure Distance (31.79 vs. 67.49 and 53.46) and CLIP Edited (21.33 vs. 20.48 and 19.58), though InstructPix2Pix edges it slightly on PSNR (19.69 vs. 19.75) and SSIM (76.98 vs. 76.71). Overall, ISLock is the strongest AR-based editing method and is competitive with the best diffusion-based approaches on structural and semantic metrics, while the gap in pixel-level background preservation reflects the inherent difference between AR token-level generation and diffusion latent-space optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of autoregressive image generation (LlamaGen, Lumina-mGPT) and text-guided image editing, a space previously dominated by diffusion-based methods like Prompt-to-Prompt and Null-text Inversion that rely on explicit cross-attention manipulation. It departs from prior AR editing attempts (ControlAR, EditAR) that require fine-tuning on large-scale paired datasets, instead introducing a training-free paradigm. The key conceptual departure is the finding that diffusion-style attention injection fails in AR models due to 'spatial poverty' of cross-attention maps and sequential error accumulation, motivating the implicit token-matching approach rather than explicit attention transplantation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ISLock establishes the first training-free editing framework for AR visual models, a practical gap as AR architectures gain traction as alternatives to diffusion. The implicit structure locking via nearest-neighbor token selection is simple and model-agnostic (demonstrated on both LlamaGen and Lumina-mGPT), making it a potentially adoptable technique as AR image models proliferate. Open questions include scalability to higher resolutions and more complex multi-object edits, whether the dynamic window and threshold heuristics generalize across architectures, and how the method performs on real (non-AR-generated) images. The evaluation is limited to 512×512 and five editing categories, leaving broader applicability untested.
