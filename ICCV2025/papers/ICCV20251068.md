## Problem

Transformer-based vertex approaches for 3D articulated mesh generation and reconstruction are limited to coarse meshes of ~500 vertices due to quadratic sequence-length complexity, requiring lossy upsampling that destroys local geometry such as hand poses and facial expressions. This paper addresses the gap by adapting Mamba State Space Models, which process sequences in near-linear time, to enable dense mesh modeling with over 10,000 vertices.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251068/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

For generation (MambaDiff3D), the model takes noisy 3D vertex coordinates (N×3, where N is 6890 for SMPL or 10475 for SMPL-X) plus a diffusion timestep and optional clothing-type class condition, and predicts the noise to denoise into a full-resolution articulated mesh. For reconstruction (Mamba-HMR), a 384×288 RGB image is fed through an HRNet-w48 CNN backbone, and the resulting features are combined with joint and vertex queries to directly output full-resolution mesh vertices without any upsampling stage. In both cases, vertices are serialized into 1D sequences using body-part UV maps or template-mesh 3D coordinates before being processed by Mamba blocks.

## Method

The core contribution is a vertex serialization technique that orders mesh vertices into 1D sequences using DensePose IUV body-part annotations and 3D coordinates of a template mesh, making the spatial structure of articulated shapes accessible to the sequential Mamba architecture. MambaDiff3D adopts a U-ViT-style diffusion architecture with shallow/mid/deep Mamba block groups and skip connections, trained with v-prediction and sampled via DDIM; it generates both vertex positions and surface normals, which are combined through a Poisson system to produce smooth, globally consistent meshes. Mamba-HMR follows the Mesh Transformer architecture but replaces transformer blocks with MeshMamba blocks, adding local geometric regularization losses (edge length, Laplacian, face normal) to preserve fine surface detail at full resolution. A key design choice is combining two serialization strategies across layers (one strategy in all layers except one, which uses the other) to balance the quality gains of multiple orderings against the computational cost of token re-indexing.

## Results

For unconditional 3D human mesh generation (Table 1), MambaDiff3D achieves the best 1-NNA score of 53.1% (trained on SURREAL), outperforming the previous best DiffSurf (54.4%) and all parametric baselines (VPoser 60.7%, NRDF 81.6%). For whole-body 3D human mesh recovery on UBody (Table 2), Mamba-HMR achieves the lowest PA-MVE of 26.3 mm (All) and 2.4 mm (Face), and the lowest MVE of 54.4 mm (All) and 17.7 mm (Face), while running at 22 FPS. Inference speed is a key advantage: MambaDiff3D generates a 10,475-vertex mesh in ~4.5 s (250 DDIM steps) on an A100, which is 6× faster than a transformer-based equivalent (28.1 s), and ~9× faster on a V100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MambaDiff3D outperforms all seven generation baselines on the 1-NNA metric: it beats the closest competitor DiffSurf (53.1% vs. 54.4%), VPoser (53.1% vs. 60.7%), and substantially outperforms NRDF (53.1% vs. 81.6%), Pose-NDF (53.1% vs. 92.0%), GDVAE (53.1% vs. 93.8%), and LIMP (53.1% vs. 81.3%). On FID, MambaDiff3D (0.32) is competitive with NRDF (0.64) and far better than Pose-NDF (3.92), though VPoser achieves a lower FID (0.05) at the cost of much worse 1-NNA. For whole-body HMR on UBody, Mamba-HMR achieves the best PA-MVE-All (26.3 mm) by a clear margin over Multi-HMR-B (31.4 mm) and SMPLer-X-L† (31.9 mm), and the best MVE-Face (17.7 mm) over AiOS (19.6 mm). The one area where Mamba-HMR trails is PA-MVE-Hands (10.7 mm vs. AiOS's 7.3 mm), though after fine-tuning on UBody it narrows to 9.7 mm. Mamba-HMR also substantially outperforms NLF-L (66.8 mm PA-MVE-All) and the transformer-based METRO [39] in qualitative comparisons, while maintaining a competitive 22 FPS inference speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Mamba State Space Models to 3D articulated mesh generation and reconstruction, building on the U-ViT diffusion architecture for the generative model and the Mesh Transformer (METRO) architecture for the recovery model, while replacing transformer self-attention blocks with Mamba blocks. The key conceptual departure from prior vertex-based approaches (limited to ~500 vertices due to quadratic attention complexity) is a vertex serialization technique that orders mesh vertices using DensePose body-part UV maps and template mesh 3D coordinates, making the unordered mesh data compatible with SSMs' sequential processing. The Poisson-based combination of generated vertex positions and surface normals, inspired by Nehab et al., addresses local noise in dense mesh generation without end-to-end training through the reconstruction step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MeshMamba demonstrates that SSMs can process 10,000+ vertex tokens at near real-time speed (22 FPS for whole-body recovery), breaking the ~500-vertex ceiling that has constrained vertex-based approaches to body-only reconstruction without hands or face. The 6–9× inference speedup over transformer-based diffusion models makes dense mesh generation practical for interactive applications. However, the method is limited to fixed-topology meshes with tight clothing, and generalization to unseen datasets remains weaker than large-scale pre-trained parametric approaches like SMPLer-X, leaving open whether SSM-based mesh models can match the data efficiency of parametric priors trained on diverse corpora.
