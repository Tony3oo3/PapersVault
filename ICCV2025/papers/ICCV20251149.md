## Problem

Existing anthropometric measurement methods require complete 3D body scans of undressed or minimally clothed subjects in canonical poses (A-pose or T-pose), with registered views, which is impractical in real-world settings. Clothing occlusion and arbitrary pose variations further complicate accurate shape extraction, and no prior deep learning method could handle two unregistered, partial, dressed scans in independent poses simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251149/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two unregistered partial point clouds (front and back views) of a clothed individual in arbitrary, potentially different poses, captured by a single commodity depth camera with no prior alignment required. It outputs 21 anthropometric measurement values (perimeters of convex hulls at distinct body levels), a refined T-posed body mesh (6890 vertices), two posed body meshes matching the input postures, and 21 groups of measurement landmarks. The two input scans need not be in the same pose or captured at the same time, and may contain occlusions from loose clothing.

## Method

MeasureXpert comprises two components: TrioNet and OR-Net. TrioNet uses two MLPs with max pooling to extract global features from each partial scan, then employs a 'one-to-N' multi-decoder architecture (six sub-decoders for torso, head, arms, and legs) to reconstruct posed bodies, while a shape branch extracts a pose-independent shape vector (USV) via a weighted combination of front and back shape features, where weights are computed by a Pose-Independent Function applied to pose vectors. A key self-supervised shape loss enforces consistency between the front and back shape features, encouraging the network to separate shape from pose information. OR-Net then refines the coarse T-posed mesh and landmarks by predicting per-point offsets through fully connected layers, and regresses the 21 measurement values from the refined landmarks using an encoder-decoder module. The entire system is trained on a synthetic BWM dataset of 300K samples with L2, Chamfer Distance, and L1 losses for mesh, landmark, and value supervision respectively.

## Results

On the FAUST dataset (unclothed bodies), MeasureXpert achieves a mean torso measurement error of 2.44 cm (std 1.44 cm) and a limb error of 1.04 cm (std 0.52 cm). On the 4D-Dress dataset (clothed bodies), it reduces the torso error to 0.98 cm (std 0.62 cm) and limb error to 0.49 cm (std 0.30 cm). For unseen clothing styles, the torso error is 1.43 cm (std 0.68 cm) and limb error is 0.68 cm (std 0.31 cm), all measured as mean absolute error in centimeters across 21 anthropometric dimensions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeasureXpert decisively outperforms POCO on clothed and unseen-clothing torso measurements (0.98 cm vs 4.94 cm; 1.43 cm vs 4.61 cm) and limb measurements (0.49 cm vs 1.66 cm; 0.68 cm vs 1.51 cm), reflecting its training on clothed subjects. Against ArtEq, MeasureXpert reduces unclothed torso error from 4.20 cm to 2.44 cm and achieves lower limb standard deviation (0.30 cm vs 0.67 cm on clothed; 0.31 cm vs 0.63 cm on unseen clothing). Compared to 3DBodyNet, MeasureXpert is substantially more stable, with torso std of 1.44 cm vs 3.01 cm and limb std of 0.52 cm vs 0.85 cm on unclothed bodies. The only case where MeasureXpert does not achieve the lowest mean is unclothed limb measurement, where NLF reports 0.87 cm vs MeasureXpert's 1.04 cm, though MeasureXpert still leads in stability (std 0.52 cm). PyMAF-X is included in the box-plot comparisons but no specific numerical values are reported in the text for this baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own PoseNormNet [66] architecture and the broader line of SMPL-based body shape estimation under clothing (ArtEq, 3DBodyNet, GALA) by relaxing the requirement for complete, registered, canonical-pose scans to just two unregistered partial point clouds in arbitrary poses. The multi-decoder segmentation strategy is adapted from the same group's Point2PartVolume [23], while the self-supervised shape-consistency loss (Eq. 6) is a novel mechanism to disentangle pose-conditional shape from pose information without explicit pose labels. The OR-Net offset refinement module departs from prior parametric fitting approaches by learning per-point residuals on a coarse T-posed mesh rather than optimizing SMPL parameters directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling accurate anthropometric measurement from two commodity depth-camera partial scans of a clothed, posed individual, this work removes the practical barriers (undressing, canonical posture, full-body capture) that have limited real-world deployment of 3D body measurement. The 300K-sample BWM synthetic dataset and the extended FAUST/4D-Dress evaluation protocol provide a reusable benchmark for the partial-scan measurement setting. Key open questions include generalization to diverse populations (the authors note missing hair representations like Afro styles), robustness to large occlusions, and validation on a real-world benchmark with independently verified measurements, which the authors flag as future work.
