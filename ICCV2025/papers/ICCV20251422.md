## Problem

Existing approaches to infinite anime life simulation, such as Unbounded, rely on LLMs to translate text-only dialogues into captions for static image generation, neglecting historical visual context and producing no motion. This results in visually inconsistent gameplay and a fundamentally static experience that lacks the dynamics essential for an engaging game.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251422/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes open-ended player language instructions alongside historical action-aware multimodal representations (N=226 tokens per shot, each fusing CLIP visual embeddings of the first frame with T5 text embeddings of the action description) and current character state values. It produces a 16-frame animation video clip at 480×720 resolution, updated character state values (stamina, social, entertainment), and a motion scope signal controlling action intensity. A sliding-window scheme over the multimodal context enables theoretically unbounded multi-turn generation.

## Method

AnimeGamer introduces an action-aware multimodal representation that decomposes each animation shot into a visual reference (CLIP embedding of the first frame), an action description (T5 text embedding), and a motion scope (optical flow magnitude), with the first two concatenated via MLP+LN to form the shot token and the third injected as a conditioning signal. A CogVideoX-based decoder is trained to reconstruct video from this representation, while a Mistral-7B MLLM is fine-tuned to predict the next shot's 226-token representation (MSE loss) and discrete character-state/motion-scope tokens (cross-entropy loss) from the full multimodal history. Training proceeds in three phases: encoder-decoder alignment, MLLM game-state prediction, and a decoder adaptation stage that fine-tunes the decoder on MLLM outputs to close the latent-space gap. A data pipeline extracts ~20,000 clips from 10 anime films, using InternVL to auto-annotate character movements, backgrounds, and states for supervised training.

## Results

On automatic metrics (Table 1), AnimeGamer achieves the best character consistency (CLIP-I 0.8132, DreamSim 0.7403), semantic consistency (CLIP-T 0.4161, CLIP-TE 0.4012), and motion quality (ACC-F 0.6744, MAE-F 0.4238) across all baselines, while matching them on character state update (ACC-S 0.6773, MAE-S 0.5872). On MLLM judgement and human evaluation (Table 2), AnimeGamer scores 8.36/10.00 on Overall, 9.14/9.95 on Instruction Following, and 8.41/9.95 on Contextual Consistency, substantially outperforming the best baseline GC (6.42/7.38, 7.29/7.37, 6.58/6.89 respectively). Inference time is 24 s/turn, the fastest among all methods (vs. 50 s for GSC and 63 s for GFC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All three baselines (GC, GFC, GSC) use Gemini-1.5 as a router LLM to translate dialogues into text instructions, then rely on separate generation models. AnimeGamer wins decisively on every visual and motion metric: CLIP-I improves by 1.7–4.7 points over baselines, DreamSim by 9.9–23.8 points, and ACC-F by 25–38 points, confirming that the action-aware multimodal representation provides substantially better controllability than text-only conditioning. On contextual and style consistency (Table 2), AnimeGamer scores 8.41/9.95 and 7.52/9.95 (GPT-4V/Human) versus 6.58/6.89 and 6.57/6.10 for the best baseline GC, a gap of roughly 2 points on GPT-4V and 3 points on human ratings. Character state update (ACC-S, MAE-S) is essentially tied across all methods (~0.677 ACC, ~0.588 MAE), indicating that the MLLM-based state prediction matches the Gemini-1.5 router. AnimeGamer also runs fastest at 24 s/turn, compared to 25 s (GC), 50 s (GSC), and 63 s (GFC), since it avoids the extra LLM API call and the two-stage image-to-video pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the infinite anime life simulation task introduced by Unbounded [39], which used an LLM to translate text dialogues into captions for static image generation. The key departure is replacing the text-only LLM pipeline with an MLLM (Mistral-7B) that directly predicts action-aware multimodal representations, which are then decoded into video clips via a fine-tuned CogVideoX diffusion model. The action-aware representation—combining CLIP visual features, T5 motion text embeddings, and optical-flow-based motion scope—serves as a novel bridge between the MLLM's latent space and the video diffusion decoder, addressing the information loss inherent in text-only or image-only intermediate representations. The sliding-window inference strategy for unbounded generation is borrowed from TimeChat [50].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to produce dynamic video (rather than static images) for infinite anime life simulation, making the generated game world feel alive and interactive. The action-aware multimodal representation offers a reusable pattern for coupling MLLMs with video diffusion models in interactive generation settings. However, the evaluation is confined to a closed domain with custom-trained characters, and the authors explicitly acknowledge that generalization to unseen characters remains unexplored. Open questions include whether the approach scales to open-domain settings, whether real-time inference is achievable, and how the method handles long-horizon drift over hundreds of turns.
