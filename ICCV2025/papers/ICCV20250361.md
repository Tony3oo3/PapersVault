## Problem

Existing interactive 3D scene generation methods, such as WonderWorld, require approximately 10 seconds per viewpoint update due to iterative 3DGS optimization and multi-step diffusion inpainting, making true real-time interaction infeasible. The core challenge is simultaneously accelerating both geometric representation updates and appearance refinement while maintaining spatial consistency across dynamically changing viewpoints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250361/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system is initialized with a single RGB image and then operates iteratively: at each step the user provides a target camera pose and a text prompt describing desired scene content. The pipeline consumes the current scene's rendered image and depth map alongside the text prompt, and produces an updated global 3D Gaussian Splatting representation (with new local Gaussians fused in), a completed depth map for the new view, and an inpainted appearance image. Each full interaction cycle completes in 0.72 seconds on an H20 GPU, with geometry (0.50s) and appearance (0.22s) modeled in parallel.

## Method

WonderTurbo decomposes the pipeline into three specialized modules. StepSplat replaces iterative 3DGS optimization with a feed-forward network (RepVGG backbone) that maintains a feature memory of prior views' matching features; it constructs a depth-guided cost volume by warping neighboring views' features onto candidate depth planes (constrained by QuickDepth's output), computes normalized dot-product correlations, and decodes Gaussian parameters via a 2D U-Net, then merges local Gaussians into the global scene using a depth-tolerance-based incremental fusion that prunes conflicting Gaussians. QuickDepth is a lightweight depth completion network (initialized from a pre-trained depth estimator) that fills missing depth regions in the rendered depth map using the new appearance image, trained on simulated camera trajectories to match interactive generation conditions. FastPaint reduces Stable Diffusion inpainting to 2 inference steps via ODE trajectory distillation (following Hyper-SD) and fine-tunes on a purpose-built dataset of interactive 3D generation masks. All three modules are trained on a 6M+ frame dataset constructed by rendering simulated interactive trajectories (rotational, linear, hybrid) through scenes generated by multiple existing 3D generation methods, covering indoor, urban, natural, and stylized categories.

## Results

WonderTurbo achieves a total scene generation time of 0.72 seconds (0.50 s geometry + 0.22 s appearance) on an H20 GPU, representing a 15× speedup over the fastest prior online method WonderWorld (11.05 s). On quality metrics, WonderTurbo attains the best CLIP Score (28.65), CLIP Consistency (0.9732), CLIP-IQA+ (0.6812), and CLIP Aesthetic (7.3243) among all compared methods, while WonderWorld leads only on Q-Align (3.7823 vs. 3.7253). In user studies, WonderTurbo wins 69.43% of comparisons against WonderWorld and over 94% against all other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WonderTurbo outperforms all six baselines on CLIP Score, CLIP Consistency, and CLIP Aesthetic, and matches or exceeds WonderWorld (the strongest prior online method) on 4 of 5 quality metrics, losing only narrowly on Q-Align (3.7253 vs. 3.7823). Against offline methods (LucidDreamer, Text2Room, Pano2Room, DreamScene360), WonderTurbo leads by a wide margin on all metrics—for example, CLIP Score 28.65 vs. 27.72 (LucidDreamer) and 24.50 (Text2Room/DreamScene360)—while being 40–110× faster. Against the closest competitor WonderWorld, WonderTurbo achieves a 15× speedup (0.72 s vs. 11.05 s) while maintaining competitive quality, winning 69.43% of user-study comparisons. Against WonderJourney, the other online baseline, WonderTurbo is over 110× faster (0.72 s vs. 79.57 s) and wins 96.54% of user preferences. The ablation confirms that StepSplat (vs. FreeSplat/DepthSplat) and FastPaint (vs. the base SD inpainting model) each contribute measurable gains, with the depth-guided cost volume and incremental fusion being the most impactful components within StepSplat.

## Relation to Prior Work

WonderTurbo extends the feed-forward 3DGS paradigm (MVSplat, FreeSplat, DepthSplat) to the interactive setting by introducing a feature memory that adaptively constructs depth-guided cost volumes as viewpoints change, replacing the per-scene iterative optimization used by WonderWorld's FLAGS. For appearance, it distills a Stable Diffusion inpainting model to 2 steps via Hyper-SD's ODE trajectory reformulation, departing from the dozens-of-steps diffusion pipelines in prior interactive methods. The overall framework positions itself as a direct acceleration of WonderWorld, the fastest prior online 3D generation system (~10s per view), by attacking both the geometry and appearance bottlenecks simultaneously with a purpose-built interactive dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 0.72s per-view updates makes interactive 3D scene generation practically viable for real-time applications (virtual walkthroughs, game prototyping, collaborative design), a regime previously inaccessible. However, the approach depends on a 6M-frame dataset synthesized from other 3D generation methods, raising questions about circularity and whether quality degrades over very long interactive sessions. The 69.43% user-study win rate against WonderWorld (despite slightly better CLIP metrics) suggests that metric-based evaluation may understate perceptual quality gaps, an open question for the field. Scaling to more complex interactions (object manipulation, physics) or longer unbounded scenes remains unaddressed.
