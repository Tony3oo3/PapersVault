## Problem

Diffusion models produce vast visual variation from a single prompt, but this structure remains opaque to users who must pre-specify every edit direction through prompts, reference images, or attribute vectors. Unlike GANs whose compact latent spaces exhibited emergent disentanglement, diffusion models' higher-dimensional latents lack interpretable, composable control axes. The paper asks whether the principal modes of visual variation within a model's knowledge of a concept can be discovered automatically, without any user-specified attributes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt and a pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model (U-Net-based like SDXL/SDXL-DMD or transformer-based like Flux Schnell) as input, requiring no reference images, attribute labels, or manual curation. It produces a set of n lightweight LoRA adapters (typically 32–64) attached to the model's cross-attention layers, each encoding one orthogonal semantic direction. At inference time, users scale and compose any sparse subset of these adapters to generate images with controlled, interpretable variation, with the full discovery pipeline running in under 2 hours on a single A100 GPU with <24 GB VRAM.

## Method

SliderSpace operates in three stages. First, it samples ~5000 images from the model for the prompt and, at each diffusion timestep, computes an estimated final image via 'Final Image Extrapolation' (applying the predicted noise direction through the remaining schedule in closed form), avoiding full denoising passes. Second, it maps these estimated images into CLIP embedding space and performs PCA to extract n mutually orthogonal principal directions of maximal semantic variation. Third, for each principal direction v_i, it trains a dedicated LoRA adapter by minimizing 1 − cos(Δφ_i, v_i), where Δφ_i is the CLIP embedding shift induced by the adapter, ensuring each slider's effect is semantically aligned with its assigned direction and orthogonal to all others. The key design choice is performing the spectral decomposition in a semantic embedding space rather than pixel or latent space, which guarantees that discovered directions are semantically distinct and interpretable rather than capturing low-level texture or noise artifacts.

## Results

On the diversity enhancement task (COCO-30k), DMD-SliderSpace reduces FID from 15.52 (SDXL-DMD) to 12.12, nearly matching undistilled SDXL (11.72), while maintaining comparable CLIP alignment (29.13 vs 29.41). In the concept decomposition user study, SliderSpace achieves win rates of 72.4% (diverse), 66.0% (useful), and 68.1% (creative) against SDXL-DMD. For art style exploration, SliderSpace achieves a lower FID than both LLM-generated prompts (38.84) and generic prompts, and outperforms supervised Concept Sliders in matching the ParrotZone reference distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SDXL-DMD (the 4-step distilled model), SliderSpace substantially improves FID from 15.52 to 12.12 on COCO-30k, closing most of the gap to undistilled SDXL (11.72), and wins the user study with 72.4% preference for diversity. Against undistilled SDXL, SliderSpace nearly matches FID (12.12 vs 11.72) while retaining the 4-step speed advantage, and still wins the user study (65.3% diverse, 61.2% useful). In art style discovery, SliderSpace achieves a lower FID than supervised Concept Sliders [16] and LLM-generated style prompts, indicating broader style coverage without per-artist training. Against the manually curated ParrotZone artist prompts [25], SliderSpace achieves comparable diversity (54% win rate on 'Diverse') but is significantly preferred for utility (63% on 'Useful'), suggesting the discovered directions are more practically actionable than real artist names.

## Relation to Prior Work

SliderSpace extends the Concept Sliders framework [16], which used LoRA adapters for user-specified attributes, by replacing the supervised attribute specification with an unsupervised discovery process: PCA in CLIP embedding space identifies principal directions of variation, and each direction is then trained as a LoRA adapter. Conceptually, the paper draws an analogy to GAN latent space disentanglement (StyleGAN, GANSpace), where low-dimensional latent spaces naturally exhibit interpretable axes, and asks whether similar structure can be extracted from the much higher-dimensional diffusion model latent space. The key departure from prior control methods (ControlNet, Prompt-to-Prompt, DreamBooth) is that no user-specified edit direction, reference image, or attribute vector is required—directions emerge purely from the model's own distribution over a single prompt.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a simple PCA-plus-LoRA pipeline (~65 min on one A100) yields composable, interpretable control over diffusion model outputs without any manual curation, and can reverse mode collapse in distilled models (SDXL-DMD FID improves from 15.52 to 12.12, nearly matching undistilled SDXL at 11.72). Open questions include whether the discovered directions truly reflect the model's internal concept structure or merely statistical variation in CLIP space, how the results depend on the choice of semantic encoder, and whether the approach scales to more complex multi-concept prompts. The limitation that art-style directions do not map one-to-one to real artists suggests the discovered axes are a lossy compression of the model's stylistic knowledge.
