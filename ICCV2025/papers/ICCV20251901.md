## Problem

Existing pixel grounding benchmarks (RefCOCO series, gRefCOCO, RefCOCOm) are constrained to 80 COCO object categories, short referring phrases (3–8 words), and lack coverage of stuff-class, part-level, and multi-object segmentation. Automatic annotation pipelines like GLaMM and MRES mitigate the human-labeling bottleneck but introduce textual ambiguity, rely on fixed vocabularies or pre-existing box annotations, and require expensive multi-step GPU pipelines. The paper addresses both the data-quality gap and the evaluation gap by proposing a streamlined VLM-based annotation framework and a multi-granularity benchmark.

## Input / Output

The system takes an RGB image and a natural-language referring expression (averaging 16 words in GSTrain-10M, substantially longer than the 3–8 word phrases in RefCOCO) and produces a pixel-level segmentation mask. A bounding-box variant (GSEval-BBox) converts masks to boxes for evaluating MLLM grounding accuracy at IoU thresholds. The evaluation benchmark spans four granularity levels—stuff, part, multi-object, and single-object—each with distinct annotation requirements (e.g., amorphous background regions vs. fine-grained object components).

## Method

GSSculpt is a three-stage VLM-based annotation pipeline: (1) Entity Spatial Localization generates scene captions with InternVL2.5, grounds phrases to bounding boxes via Florence-2, and extracts pixel masks with SAM2; (2) Grounding Text Generation uses multimodal LLMs with prompts emphasizing spatial relationships and distinctive visual features to produce unambiguous 16-word descriptions; (3) Noise Filtering prompts EVF-SAM to re-segment from the generated text and discards text-mask pairs whose IoU falls below 0.5. Applied to 2M SA-1B images, this yields GSTrain-10M (9.56M text-mask pairs). The GSEval benchmark (3,800 COCO unlabeled images) adds VLM-based pre-categorization, matting-based boundary refinement, and human verification across the four segmentation categories, reducing pipeline steps by 78% compared to GLaMM.

## Results

On the proposed GSEval benchmark (zero-shot), EVF-SAM achieves the best overall gIoU of 62.6, followed by LISA-7B (57.6) and GLaMM (57.2), while specialist methods LAVT and ReLA drop to 22.5 and 19.7 respectively. Part-level segmentation is the most challenging subset, with the best score being only 24.2 gIoU (InstructSeg). Training EVF-SAM on GSTrain-10M yields a +14.7 gIoU gain on GSEval (62.6→77.3), +2.9 on gRefCOCO (63.5→66.4), and +4.0 on RefCOCOm (51.3→55.3); LISA-7B gains +16.0, +4.1, and +5.1 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Specialist RES methods (LAVT, ReLA) that score 65.8–68.3 cIoU on RefCOCO collapse to 19.7–22.5 gIoU on GSEval, revealing severe generalization failure on stuff and part categories absent from their training data. Among MLLMs, EVF-SAM leads GSEval overall (62.6 gIoU) and on multi-object (72.1) and single-object (54.5) subsets, while InstructSeg achieves the best part-level score (24.2) and the highest RefCOCO cIoU (81.9) yet trails EVF-SAM by 10.1 points on GSEval overall. LISA-7B, despite a lower RefCOCO score (67.9), outperforms InstructSeg on GSEval (57.6 vs. 52.5), which the authors attribute to LISA's broader multi-task training data. On GSEval-BBox, Gemini-1.5-Pro leads proprietary models (74.3 Acc) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B leads open-source models (66.7 Acc), but part-level grounding remains weak across all MLLMs (best open-source: 31.2 for Qwen2.5-VL-72B). Training on GSTrain-10M consistently outperforms training on GranD (GLaMM's auto-annotated dataset) across all three evaluated models, with the largest gain being +8.0 points for LISA-7B on RefCOCOm (37.6 vs. 29.6).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a direct response to the limitations of existing automatic annotation pipelines (GLaMM's 23-step process, MRES's fixed vocabulary) and closed-set benchmarks (RefCOCO series restricted to 80 COCO categories). GSSculpt repurposes off-the-shelf VLMs (InternVL2.5, Florence-2, SAM2) into a streamlined three-phase annotation pipeline, while GSEval extends the RES task from single-object, closed-set segmentation to a multi-granular, open-vocabulary setting covering stuff, part-level, multi-object, and single-object references. The training corpus GSTrain-10M builds on SA-1B's image pool but replaces its geometric masks with semantically grounded, linguistically rich annotations averaging 16 words—substantially longer than the 3–8 word phrases in manually annotated RefCOCO datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GSEval is the first benchmark to simultaneously evaluate all four granularity levels (stuff, part, multi-object, single-object) under an open-vocabulary setting, filling a critical gap that RefCOCO, gRefCOCO, and RefCOCOm each only partially address. The finding that RefCOCO scores poorly predict GSEval performance (e.g., InstructSeg: 81.9 cIoU on RefCOCO vs. 52.5 gIoU on GSEval, while LISA shows the inverse pattern) challenges the field's reliance on RefCOCO as the primary evaluation standard and suggests existing benchmarks systematically overestimate generalization. Open questions include whether the automatic annotation quality at 9.56M scale truly matches human annotation, whether the 0.5 IoU noise-filtering threshold is optimal, and whether the benchmark's difficulty (even Gemini-1.5-Pro scores only 74.3 on GSEval-BBox) reflects genuine model limitations or annotation artifacts.
