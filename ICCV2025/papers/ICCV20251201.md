## Problem

Reconstructing 3D part poses of articulated objects from casually captured monocular video is hampered by depth ambiguity, self-occlusion, and hand-object occlusion. Pure optimization methods (e.g., RSRD) fall into irrecoverable local minima and drift on longer videos, while purely predictive approaches lack the large-scale 4D datasets needed to cover the diversity of man-made articulations. No existing system interleaves prediction and optimization in a mutually reinforcing cycle that improves with additional observation time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251201/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

POD takes a multi-view object scan (processed into a 3D Gaussian Splat with DINOv2 features and part segmentation via GARField) and a 10–30 second monocular video of a person manipulating the object, typically with a mostly static camera. It outputs per-frame SE(3) part-to-object transforms (one-level kinematic hierarchy supporting revolute, prismatic, and detachable joints) plus a camera-to-object transform, yielding a full 4D reconstruction. The part parameterization is relative to an object frame at the centroid, so outputs are local poses that can be composed with the global object pose for world-space placement.

## Method

POD runs a three-stage self-improving cycle: (1) Predict — a lightweight transformer-decoder on frozen DINOv2 features predicts per-frame part poses and camera-to-object transform from a single RGB image; (2) Optimize — these predictions initialize a global inverse-rendering optimization over 3DGS that minimizes DINO feature loss, pairwise depth ranking loss, mask loss, a static prior on connected parts, temporal smoothing, and a quasi-multiview supervision term that jointly optimizes frame pairs with similar predicted part configurations to resolve depth ambiguity; (3) Distill — the optimized trajectory is rendered from ~18,000 novel hemisphere viewpoints and near-camera viewpoints to generate synthetic image-pose pairs that fine-tune the predictor. Each iteration feeds better initializations into optimization and cleaner labels into the predictor, creating a virtuous cycle. The quasi-multiview mining strategy is itself data-driven: the improving predictor identifies better matching frame pairs over successive loops, tightening the supervision signal.

## Results

On 5 synthetic objects with ground-truth poses, POD achieves MSE 0.0422 and PCP 0.778 (α=0.05), substantially outperforming the RSRD optimization-only baseline (MSE 0.0952, PCP 0.454). POD also demonstrates greater robustness to tighter accuracy thresholds, maintaining PCP 0.622 at α=0.03 versus RSRD's 0.266. Performance improves with longer input videos (up to 6% PCP gain from 1s to 6s) and with successive self-improvement iterations (up to 14% PCP gain over 5 loops on the longest video).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole quantitative baseline is RSRD [13], a pure optimization-based method that refines part poses via inverse rendering without a predictive model. POD wins decisively across all metrics: it reduces MSE by 56% (0.0422 vs. 0.0952) and more than doubles PCP at every threshold (e.g., 0.778 vs. 0.454 at α=0.05; 0.622 vs. 0.266 at the strictest α=0.03). The gap widens under tighter accuracy tolerances, indicating that RSRD's errors are not merely small perturbations but structurally incoherent poses (e.g., parts intersecting or disconnecting from novel viewpoints). Qualitatively, RSRD frequently fails irrecoverably under heavy hand-object occlusion or on longer videos due to drift, whereas POD's feed-forward predictor recovers plausible 3D-consistent configurations even from a single occluded frame. The paper notes that RSRD was augmented with a mask loss (borrowed from 3DGS tracking literature) to give it the best possible chance, yet the performance gap remains large.

## Relation to Prior Work

POD builds directly on RSRD (Kerr et al., CoRL 2024) for its optimization backbone—DINOv2 feature matching, inverse rendering, and SE(3) part parameterization—and on GARField (Kim et al., CVPR 2024) for decomposing a 3D Gaussian Splat into articulated parts. It departs from the pure-optimization lineage (RSRD, PARIS, Art-GS) by interleaving a feed-forward pose predictor and a synthetic-data distillation step, creating a self-improving loop inspired by SPIN and Agent-to-Sim but extended to general man-made articulated objects without predefined templates. The quasi-multiview mining strategy, which exploits repeated motions in long video to create pseudo-multiview supervision, is a novel mechanism not present in prior monocular 4D reconstruction work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-improving predict-optimize-distill cycle offers a practical path to 4D articulated object understanding that scales with observation length—a property pure optimization methods lack, as RSRD degrades on longer videos. The quasi-multiview supervision is a lightweight, general-purpose technique for resolving depth ambiguity that could transfer to other monocular reconstruction settings. Key limitations remain: per-object retraining, dependence on repeated motions for quasi-multiview pairing, and sensitivity to thin, small, or rotationally symmetric parts. Open questions include whether a unified cross-object predictor can replace per-instance training, and whether generative models (e.g., conditional diffusion) could subsume the distillation step and enable simulator compatibility.
