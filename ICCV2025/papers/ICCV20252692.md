## Problem

Existing blind video super-resolution methods assume blur kernels are spatially invariant within each frame or across the entire video, and their temporal alignment modules assume all frames share identical degradations. These assumptions fail under realistic conditions where motion and focus produce spatially varying, temporally inconsistent degradations, leading to visible artifacts and suboptimal reconstruction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252692/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a group of 5 degraded LR video frames (temporal radius M=2) at resolution H×W×3 and produces 4× super-resolved HR frames at sH×sW×3. Uniquely, the degradation is applied per-frame (one randomly generated kernel per frame) rather than per-video, better simulating real-world spatially and temporally varying blur. The model is trained on REDS with 256×256 patches and evaluated on REDS4, Vid4, and UDM10 under both Gaussian blur (σ∈[0.4,2.0]) and realistic motion blur (13×13 kernels) scenarios.

## Method

BVSR-IK constructs a per-frame multi-scale kernel dictionary (R=7 scales, N=8 atoms per scale, sizes 1×1 to 13×13) parameterized by implicit neural representations (compact MLPs mapping spatial coordinates to kernel values), enabling spatially varying per-pixel blur kernels expressed as linear combinations of dictionary atoms. A novel recurrent Transformer with recurrent optimization attention (ROA) operates in a multi-scale U-Net structure to predict the coefficient weights (ω for kernel shape, μ for scale selection) by capturing both short-term and long-term scale-aware information through a hidden state branch. The predicted weights drive two filtering modules: Implicit Spatial Correction (ISC) applies spatially-varying deconvolution to each LR frame, and Implicit Temporal Alignment (ITA) uses bidirectional propagation with Spynet optical flow warping to align features across frames. Training uses a combined Charbonnier loss on the final SR output and an auxiliary correction loss on intermediate corrected frames, optimized with Adam and cosine annealing over 400 epochs.

## Results

On REDS4, BVSR-IK achieves 29.50 dB PSNR / 0.8412 SSIM under Gaussian blur and 29.48 dB / 0.8303 SSIM under realistic motion blur, outperforming the second-best method FMA-Net by 0.39 dB and 0.59 dB respectively. On Vid4 and UDM10, BVSR-IK also leads all baselines in both scenarios (e.g., 24.48 dB on Vid4 Gaussian, 30.27 dB on UDM10 motion blur). The model is also more efficient than FMA-Net, with fewer parameters (9.30M vs. 9.94M) and lower FLOPs (459.23G vs. 564.42G) while delivering superior quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BVSR-IK consistently outperforms all eight baselines (four BISR and four BVSR methods) across all three datasets and both degradation scenarios. The closest competitor is FMA-Net, which BVSR-IK surpasses by 0.39 dB PSNR on REDS4 (Gaussian blur), 0.59 dB on REDS4 (motion blur), 0.15 dB on Vid4 (Gaussian), 0.36 dB on Vid4 (motion blur), 0.02 dB on UDM10 (Gaussian), and 0.15 dB on UDM10 (motion blur). BVSR-IK also leads in SSIM and tOF (temporal consistency) across nearly all settings, with particularly notable tOF improvements on Vid4 (0.48 vs. 0.65–0.68 for competitors). Among the BVSR baselines, Self-BVSR and BSVSR trail FMA-Net by 0.03–0.77 dB depending on scenario, while DBVSR lags the most. In terms of efficiency, BVSR-IK uses fewer parameters (9.30M) and FLOPs (459.23G) than FMA-Net (9.94M, 564.42G) and is faster at runtime (1.58s vs. 2.07s), making it both more accurate and more efficient than the prior SOTA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spatially-varying kernel dictionary formulation from Quan et al. (ICCV 2023) and Guo et al. (ECCV 2024) into the blind video super-resolution setting, where prior BVSR methods (DBVSR, BSVSR, Self-BVSR, FMA-Net) assumed spatially invariant kernels per frame or per video. The key architectural departure is the use of implicit neural representations to parameterize multi-scale kernel atoms, replacing fixed or learned convolutional filters with a continuous coordinate-based MLP that can interpolate kernels at arbitrary scales. Additionally, the paper introduces a recurrent Transformer (building on Restormer-style attention) to predict per-pixel coefficient weights, shifting from the aggregation-based filtering paradigm in prior BVSR to a correction-alignment framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By modeling spatially varying degradations through INR-based kernel dictionaries, this work addresses a practical limitation in BVSR where real-world videos often exhibit non-uniform blur due to motion or focus changes. The approach is also more parameter-efficient (9.30M vs. 9.94M for FMA-Net) while achieving consistent gains across three datasets and two degradation types. However, the evaluation is limited to synthetic Gaussian and motion blur at a fixed 4× scale with M=2 temporal radius, leaving open how well the INR-based kernel representation generalizes to more complex real-world degradations (e.g., mixed blur-noise, defocus with depth variation) or to higher scale factors and longer temporal contexts.
