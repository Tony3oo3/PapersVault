## Problem

World models that simulate future states under different actions have been widely explored in robotics and autonomous driving but remain entirely unexplored in medicine. Existing medical GPTs and vision-language models make less informed treatment decisions than clinicians because they cannot visually simulate how a tumor will evolve under a specific treatment protocol, leaving physicians without a mechanism to preview and compare intervention outcomes before committing to a plan.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252659/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a 3D pre-treatment CT volume (H×W×D), its corresponding tumor mask, and a free-form language treatment goal (e.g., 'What TACE protocols are recommended for this HCC patient?'). It produces a simulated post-treatment 3D CT volume, a ranked optimal TACE protocol consisting of a specific drug and embolism combination, and a survival risk score. The action space is structured as D candidate drugs crossed with E candidate embolisms, constrained by clinical contraindication rules, and the exploration is bounded by drug horizon H_d and embolism horizon H_e to prevent cumulative toxicity.

## Method

MeWM integrates three components: a VLM-based Policy Model (GPT-4o + DeepSeek-R1) that extracts tumor context from pre-treatment CT and proposes candidate drug/embolism action combos; a 3D Latent Diffusion Dynamics Model that generates post-treatment CT conditioned on a Text-driven Morpho-Gaussian Attenuated pre-treatment latent (simulating occlusion, lipiodol deposition, and necrosis) and fused CLIP text + learnable concept embeddings for the action combo, trained with a Combo Contrastive Learning loss that pulls together tumors from similar protocols and pushes apart those from dissimilar ones; and an Inverse Dynamics Model (3D ResNet MC3 + Cross-Attention Transformer) that scores survival risk from paired pre/post CT with nnUNet-segmented masks. A beam-search tree exploration algorithm iteratively extends B parallel protocol beams over drug and embolism steps, synthesizing T tumor replicas per candidate action and pruning by lowest average survival risk, with clinical rules eliminating contraindicated combinations.

## Results

On the TACE Protocol Exploration task, MeWM achieves an F1-score of 52.38 on the in-house dataset and 64.08 on the public HCC-TACE-Seg dataset, outperforming the best multimodal GPT baseline (HuatuoGPT-Vision: 40.13 / 52.62) by over 10% in F1-score. When used as a clinical decision-support tool, MeWM boosts interventional physicians' F1-score from 48.81 to 61.51 (+13%) on the in-house dataset and from 71.43 to 80.00 (+9%) on the public dataset. In the Visual Turing Test, MeWM achieves the lowest specificity across all three radiologists (79.17%, 70.83%, 75.00%), indicating the highest realism, along with the best FID (0.71) and LPIPS (0.6120) among all tumor synthesis methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeWM outperforms all six multimodal GPT baselines (Qwen2.5-VL, GPT-4o, Claude-3.7-sonnet, CT2Rep, MedGPT, HuatuoGPT-Vision) on every metric in TACE Protocol Exploration, with the largest F1-score gap over HuatuoGPT-Vision (52.38 vs. 40.13 in-house; 64.08 vs. 52.62 public). In the Visual Turing Test, MeWM achieves the lowest specificity (79.17/70.83/75.00%) compared to SynTumor (95.83/95.83/95.83%), Pixel2Cancer (100/100/100%), DiffTumor (91.67/87.50/87.50%), and TextoMorph (91.67/83.33/87.50%), meaning radiologists most frequently mistake MeWM's synthetic tumors for real ones. MeWM also achieves the best FID (0.71 vs. 1.03–3.34) and LPIPS (0.6120 vs. 0.6831–0.9111) among all tumor synthesis methods. In survival analysis, MeWM's heuristic function reduces prediction MSE from 0.3550 (Cox model) to 0.2142 and achieves a c-index of 0.752 with log-rank p-value of 6.74×10⁻⁵, surpassing the radiomics-based Cox model. Notably, MeWM as a decision-support tool elevates physician performance by 13% F1-score (in-house) and 9% (public), surpassing the physician's standalone performance on all four metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the world-model paradigm (policy → dynamics → inverse dynamics) from robotics and autonomous driving into oncology, building directly on DiffTumor's 3D latent diffusion architecture for tumor synthesis but repurposing it from a data-augmentation tool into an action-conditioned treatment simulator. It departs from prior tumor-synthesis work (SynTumor, Pixel2Cancer, TextoMorph) by conditioning generation on specific drug/embolism combos via CLIP text embeddings and a novel Combo Contrastive Learning loss, and from prior clinical-decision LLM work by closing the loop with a survival-analysis heuristic that prunes a beam-search over treatment protocols. The Text-driven Morpho-Gaussian Attenuation for simulating occlusion effects during training is a new preprocessing step not present in any prior tumor-synthesis pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MeWM is the first system to frame medical treatment planning as a world-model problem, offering a visually trackable, simulation-in-the-loop alternative to black-box LLM recommendations that radiologists can inspect. The 13% F1 boost for interventional physicians selecting TACE protocols is clinically meaningful, yet the evidence base is narrow: a single disease (HCC), a single treatment modality (TACE), and a modest 338-pair in-house dataset limit confidence in generalizability. Open questions include whether the beam-search scales to multi-cycle or multi-modality regimens, whether the survival heuristic transfers to other cancer types, and whether the synthetic post-treatment CTs remain realistic under distribution shift (different scanners, tumor stages, or treatment combinations).
