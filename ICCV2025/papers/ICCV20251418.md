## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation methods built on 3D Gaussian Splatting or NeRF fail to maintain consistent language embeddings across different views, because the same object exhibits varying appearances that cause CLIP to extract inconsistent features. This multi-view inconsistency leads to fragmented and mis-labeled semantic masks in novel views, where different language embeddings are assigned to the same part of an object.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251418/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D scene represented as a learned Gaussian field (initialized from RGB images captured by Polycam on iPhone for LERF, or multi-pose captures for 3D-OVS) and a free-form text query at inference time. During training, SAM generates 2D segmentation masks and a zero-shot tracker (Track Anything) provides coherent identity labels across views as pseudo ground truth. At test time, the output is a per-Gaussian 3D semantic segmentation mask aligned with the query, which can also drive 3D scene editing operations (object removal, color modification via SH coefficient updates, and geometric resizing).

## Method

Each 3D Gaussian is augmented with a 3-dimensional language embedding and a 16-dimensional identity embedding, both optimized end-to-end through the differentiable splatting process. The core contribution is an identity-aware semantic consistency loss that, for randomly sampled Gaussian pairs, maximizes cosine similarity of language embeddings when identity labels match and minimizes it when they differ, thereby forcing same-object Gaussians to share consistent language features across all views. An outlier filtering mechanism excludes pixels where the rendered identity feature map has high cross-entropy relative to the tracker-provided labels, compensating for tracker inaccuracies. At inference, a progressive mask expanding scheme replaces fixed thresholds: starting from the identity segment with the highest average cosine similarity to the query embedding, it iteratively absorbs neighboring segments whose similarity gap from the current seed is below 10% of the seed's value, yielding precise boundaries without manual tuning. Training uses a warm-up schedule where the consistency loss is withheld for the first 15,000 iterations (letting CLIP loss establish initial features) before the CLIP loss is dropped and the consistency loss takes over.

## Results

On the LERF dataset, the proposed method achieves 80.5 mIoU and 76.0 mBIoU on average across three scenes (figurines, teatime, ramen), outperforming the strongest baseline (Gaussian Grouping) by 7.7 mIoU and 8.4 mBIoU. On the 3D-OVS dataset, it reaches 94.4 mIoU on average, surpassing the next-best method (LangSplat) by 1.0 mIoU. For novel view rendering quality, the method also slightly exceeds vanilla 3DGS (26.108 vs. 25.870 PSNR), confirming that the added identity and language embeddings do not degrade reconstruction fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five segmentation baselines on both benchmarks. On LERF, the largest margin is over LangSplat (+22.9 mIoU) and Feature-3DGS (+32.8 mIoU), while the closest competitor is Gaussian Grouping, which trails by 7.7 mIoU and 8.4 mBIoU. On 3D-OVS, the gap narrows: the method leads LangSplat by 1.0 mIoU and Gaussian Grouping by 6.7 mIoU, with GOI at 90.6 mIoU in third place. LEGaussian [24] is compared only qualitatively (Figs. 5–6), where the paper notes it suffers from boundary inaccuracies due to fixed-threshold masking. For rendering quality (Table 3), the method marginally beats vanilla 3DGS (26.108 vs. 25.870 PSNR) and clearly surpasses Gaussian Grouping (25.710 PSNR), showing that the identity/language augmentation does not compromise novel-view synthesis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the 3DGS-based open-vocabulary segmentation line initiated by LangSplat [20], which first rasterized CLIP features through differentiable splatting, and on Gaussian Grouping [31], which introduced per-Gaussian identity embeddings for segmentation and editing. The key departure is the identity-aware semantic consistency loss, which explicitly enforces that Gaussians sharing the same (tracker-assigned) identity have aligned language embeddings in latent space while pushing different identities apart — a constraint absent from prior 3DGS language-field methods. The progressive mask expanding scheme also departs from the fixed-threshold binarization used by LangSplat, LEGaussian, and Feature-3DGS, replacing it with an iterative, similarity-relative expansion from a seed segment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identity-aware consistency mechanism offers a simple, general-purpose remedy for the multi-view language-embedding inconsistency that plagues all current 3DGS-based open-vocabulary segmentation, and the progressive mask expanding provides a threshold-free boundary extraction that adapts to arbitrary query distributions. The method also demonstrates practical 3D scene editing (removal, recoloring, resizing) as a downstream application, broadening its utility beyond segmentation. Limitations include reliance on a zero-shot tracker for pseudo-identity labels (imperfect under occlusion or rapid appearance change), evaluation only on small-scale datasets (LERF, 3D-OVS), and a very low-dimensional (3-D) language embedding whose capacity for fine-grained or compositional queries remains untested. Open questions include scalability to large, cluttered scenes, compatibility with VLMs beyond CLIP, and whether the consistency loss generalizes to dynamic or multi-object interactions.
