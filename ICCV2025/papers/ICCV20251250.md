## Problem

Most knowledge distillation methods treat all samples uniformly, implicitly assuming equal learning value across the dataset. The paper reveals that this uniform scheme causes KLD to be dominated by low-entropy (easier) samples, overshadowing high-entropy samples that carry critical knowledge near decision boundaries. The gap is a principled, low-cost mechanism to identify and prioritize the most valuable samples during distillation.

## Input / Output

EA-KD is a plug-and-play reweighting layer applied on top of existing KD frameworks, so it inherits their input/output specifications: RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet/MS-COCO) or text prompts for LLM distillation, producing class labels, bounding boxes, or generated text respectively. The only additional computation is per-sample entropy from the teacher and student softmax outputs (using an auxiliary temperature T′=3), which yields a scalar reweighting factor w_EA,n ∈ [0, log C] multiplied onto each sample's distillation loss.

## Method

EA-KD constructs a per-sample reweighting factor w_EA,n = ½(H_n^T + H_n^T·H_n^S / H_ub), where H_n^T and H_n^S are the temperature-softened entropies of the teacher and student outputs, and H_ub = log C. This decomposes into a base term (teacher entropy, capturing inherent sample value) and an interaction term (normalized product of both entropies, capturing the student's evolving perspective). Reformulated as w_EA,n = ½·H_n^T·(1 + H_n^S/H_ub), the factor acts as the teacher's assessment of sample value dynamically scaled by the student's current uncertainty: when both agree a sample is hard, weight is maximized; when the student is already confident, weight is halved; when the teacher deems a sample trivial, weight stays near zero regardless. The final loss is simply L_EA-KD = Σ_n w_EA,n · L_KD,n, making the method a drop-in replacement for any logit- or feature-based KD loss with negligible overhead.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, EA-KD improves vanilla KD by +2.13% top-1 accuracy (73.33→75.46) for the ResNet32×4→ResNet8×4 pair, with an average gain of +0.56% across all seven teacher-student configurations and six KD frameworks. On Tiny-ImageNet, EA-KD boosts KD by +3.39% (56.00→59.39), and on ImageNet it surpasses both KD+LS and PAD (71.79 vs. 71.42 and 71.71). For LLM distillation (GPT-2-XL→GPT-2-S), EA-KD achieves 18.34 average ROUGE-L, outperforming KD (17.63), RKLD (17.82), and JSD (16.98).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EA-KD consistently outperforms vanilla KD [11] across all datasets: +2.13% on CIFAR-100, +3.39% on Tiny-ImageNet, +0.76% on ImageNet, and +0.81/+1.68 AP on MS-COCO. Against logit-level adaptive methods, EA-CTKD [20] improves over CTKD by +1.27% on CIFAR-100, and EA-DKD [47] gains +0.48% over DKD; on ImageNet, EA-DKD+LS (71.99) also surpasses PAD [46] (71.71) and KD+LS [38] (71.42). For feature-based distillation, EA-FCFD [26] improves FCFD by +0.88% and EA-ReviewKD [2] gains +0.47% on CIFAR-100. In LLM distillation, EA-KD (18.34 avg ROUGE-L) beats both RKLD (17.82) and JSD (16.98) from MiniLLM [9], with the largest margin on UnNI (+1.18 over KD). The only minor exception is EA-MLD+LS on the VGG13→MN-V2 pair (−0.27%), where the already-strong MLD+LS baseline leaves little room for improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

EA-KD builds directly on the standard KD loss (Hinton et al., 2015) by introducing a per-sample entropy-based reweighting factor, positioning itself as a plug-and-play enhancement rather than a new distillation framework. It departs from prior adaptive distillation methods (CTKD, LS, PAD, RW-KD) that adjust temperature at the logit level or rely on auxiliary estimation branches, instead operating at the sample level with a single interpretable scalar. The paper also distinguishes itself from earlier entropy-in-KD work (AKD, DynamicKD) by combining both teacher and student entropy into a unified factor, rather than relying on teacher entropy alone or being restricted to logit-based settings. The key conceptual departure is the demonstration that KLD inherently biases learning toward low-entropy (easier) samples, motivating the reweighting as a correction to this imbalance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play simplicity of EA-KD (a single reweighting factor with one hyperparameter T') makes it immediately applicable to virtually any existing KD pipeline, which is practically valuable for practitioners already using DKD, MLD, FCFD, or other SOTA frameworks. The consistent but modest gains (typically 0.3–1.5%, up to ~3.4% on Tiny-ImageNet) across image classification, object detection, and LLM distillation suggest the entropy-reweighting principle is broadly applicable rather than task-specific. Open questions include whether the approach extends to generative or sequence-to-sequence distillation beyond the sequence-level application shown here, and whether the T' hyperparameter can be made fully adaptive rather than fixed per architecture. The method's reliance on softmax entropy also limits its direct applicability to non-classification outputs (e.g., continuous regression or token-level language modeling) without further adaptation.
