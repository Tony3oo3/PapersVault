## Problem

Existing LiDAR-based 3D object detection datasets and methods are almost exclusively designed for vehicle-mounted sensors, leaving drone and quadruped platforms underexplored despite their growing importance in robotics. The fundamental challenge is that different platforms exhibit intrinsic geometric discrepancies—ego-motion jitter up to 20° in roll/pitch, divergent point-cloud elevation distributions, and shifted bounding-box orientation profiles—that standard cross-dataset domain adaptation techniques do not address.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252483/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw LiDAR point clouds (1×64-beam, 10 Hz) along with the ego pose as an SE(3) transformation (roll, pitch, yaw, translation), and outputs 3D bounding boxes parameterized by center (c^x, c^y, c^z), dimensions (l, w, h), and heading angle φ, with class labels for Vehicle and Pedestrian. A key operational detail is that during Knowledge-Adaptation, the target platform's point cloud and boxes are re-expressed in a virtual coordinate frame that zeroes roll/pitch and fixes height to the vehicle reference, so the detector always sees a vehicle-like geometric layout regardless of the actual platform.

## Method

Pi3DET-Net is a two-stage cross-platform adaptation framework. In Pre-Adaptation (PA), the source-platform detector is trained with Random Platform Jitter (uniformly sampled roll/pitch rotations applied to both points and box centers) to build robustness to ego-motion noise, while a Geometry-Aware Transformation Descriptor (GTD) is learned via global max-pooling and a regression MLP to predict the applied jitter angles, encoding platform-specific geometric invariance. In Knowledge-Adaptation (KA), the target platform's data is geometrically aligned via Virtual Platform Pose (zeroing roll/pitch, preserving yaw and planar coordinates, fixing height to the vehicle reference), and features are aligned through KL Probabilistic Feature Alignment (PFA), which encodes RoI features as Gaussian distributions via a reparameterization trick and minimizes KL divergence between source and target latent samples. The GTD descriptor is injected into the target backbone as a channel-attention residual offset to correct global spatial offsets, and pseudo-labels generated by the source model supervise target detection jointly with the KL alignment loss.

## Results

On the Vehicle→Drone cross-platform task (nuScenes source, PV-RCNN backbone), Pi3DET-Net achieves 65.43/45.94 AP@0.7 (BEV/3D), a +11.84% mAP gain over the source-only baseline (50.91/35.26). On Vehicle→Quadruped, it reaches 56.80/46.36 AP@0.7, a +12.03% improvement over source-only (43.40/33.55). In the cross-dataset nuScenes→KITTI setting, Pi3DET-Net attains 57.51/46.45 average AP, a +25.27% gain over source-only (36.48/21.18), and its Car AP@0.7 (82.86/70.20) is within 3.25% of the target-only upper bound (83.29/73.45).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pi3DET-Net consistently outperforms all cross-domain adaptation baselines (ST3D, ST3D++, ST3D+++, MS3D++, REDB) on both Vehicle→Drone and Vehicle→Quadruped tasks, achieving the highest average AP@0.7 of 60.68/44.83 (BEV/3D) with PV-RCNN, surpassing the strongest baseline MS3D++ (59.35/41.34) by 1.33/3.49 points. Notably, Pi3DET-Net even exceeds target-only training on the Vehicle→Quadruped task (56.80 vs. 54.15 BEV AP@0.7), attributed to the smaller target dataset size. In the cross-dataset nuScenes→KITTI experiment, Pi3DET-Net (57.51/46.45 avg AP) outperforms ST3D++ (52.86/43.43) by 4.65/3.02 points and PLR (44.57/39.42) by 12.94/7.03 points. In the multi-platform benchmark (Table 6), applying RPJ augmentation to the top grid-point detector PV-RCNN++ boosts quadruped AP@0.7 from 47.54 to 57.08 and drone AP@0.7 from 60.54 to 68.52, while slightly reducing vehicle performance, confirming the architecture-agnostic nature of the geometric alignment strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cross-dataset 3D detection adaptation line (ST3D, ST3D++, MS3D++) from same-platform dataset shifts to a genuinely new cross-platform setting where geometric discrepancies (ego-motion jitter, sensor height, point-cloud elevation) are intrinsic rather than statistical. The two-stage pre-adaptation/knowledge-adaptation pipeline with pseudo-labeling is directly inherited from ST3D++ [98], but the authors introduce geometric alignment (Random Platform Jitter, Virtual Platform Pose) and a variational feature-alignment module (KL Probabilistic Feature Alignment + Geometry-Aware Transformation Descriptor) that have no counterpart in prior cross-dataset work. The dataset builds on M3ED [9] by adding the 3D bounding box annotations that M3ED lacked, making it directly usable for detection training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first multi-platform LiDAR 3D detection dataset with 250K+ annotated boxes fills a critical gap for robotics perception beyond autonomous vehicles, and the 18-detector benchmark reveals that grid-point architectures (which dominate vehicle benchmarks) degrade substantially on quadruped and drone platforms while point-based detectors maintain more balanced performance — a practical insight for system designers. Open questions include whether the geometric alignment strategies generalize to platforms with more extreme viewpoint differences (e.g., underwater or indoor robots), whether the approach scales to more than two object categories, and whether the 51K-frame dataset is sufficient for robust training or whether larger multi-platform collections are needed. The method's reliance on a source platform with abundant labeled data (vehicles) also limits its applicability in settings where no dominant source domain exists.
