## Problem

Large-kernel ConvNets (e.g., SLaK, UniRepLKNet) expand the effective receptive field (ERF) but disrupt its asymptotically Gaussian distribution (AGD), producing either discriminative impact at odd positions or uniform impact across scales. Small-kernel ConvNets preserve AGD but are constrained by limited ERF. The paper asks whether ERF can be expanded while maintaining AGD through proper combinations of moderate-sized kernels rather than extremely large ones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250225/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard RGB images at 224×224 (lightweight and mid-scale variants) or 384×384 (large-scale variants). Internally, the input is split along the channel dimension into N+1 heads (N=3 for 224×224), which are processed through a pyramidal channel-increment structure where each successive layer increases its channel count. Outputs are class labels for ImageNet classification, and when fine-tuned with detection/segmentation heads, bounding boxes, instance masks, and per-pixel semantic masks.

## Method

The core module is a Three-layer Receptive Field Aggregator (RFA) built from a Layer Operator (LO) containing two components: an Amplifier (Amp) that expands the receptive field via element-wise multiplication between a depth-wise large-kernel convolution (progressive sizes 7×7, 9×9, 11×11) and a GELU-activated feature, and a Discriminator (Dis) that injects small-scale (3×3) discriminative impacts from new pixels into the large receptive field. Each LO produces a two-layer AGD receptive field; stacking three LOs yields a four-layer AGD within a single shadow module. The pyramidal channel increment (channels grow from C/(N+1) to C across layers) reduces parameters and FLOPs compared to direct-in/direct-out designs. The RFA is integrated into the InternImage backbone, replacing the standard 3×3 residual convolution with a modified DCNV3, and the full UniConvNet is trained supervised on ImageNet-1K (300 epochs) or pre-trained on ImageNet-22K then fine-tuned for large variants.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, UniConvNet-T achieves 84.2% top-1 accuracy with 30.3M parameters and 5.1G FLOPs, surpassing InternImage-T (83.5%) and FlashInternImage-T (83.6%) by 0.6–0.7 points. At the large scale, UniConvNet-XL (pre-trained on ImageNet-22K) reaches 88.4% top-1 accuracy with 226.7M parameters, outperforming InternImage-XL (88.0%) and MOAT3 (88.2%). On downstream tasks, UniConvNet-L achieves 55.7% AP^b on COCO (Cascade Mask R-CNN, 1× schedule) and 55.1% mIoU on ADE20K (UperNet, single-scale), both setting new state-of-the-art among pure CNNs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniConvNet consistently outperforms all compared CNN and ViT baselines at every scale. At the Tiny scale, UniConvNet-T (84.2%) beats InternImage-T (83.5%), FlashInternImage-T (83.6%), UniRepLKNet-T (83.2%), and SLaK-T (82.5%) by 0.6–1.7 points with comparable parameters (~30M) and FLOPs (~5G). At the Small and Base scales, UniConvNet-S (84.5%) and UniConvNet-B (85.0%) surpass InternImage-S/B (84.2%/84.9%) and FlashInternImage-S/B (84.4%/84.9%) while using fewer FLOPs (8.48G vs. 8.0G for S; 15.9G vs. 16.0G for B). At the Large/XL scale, UniConvNet-XL (88.4%) achieves the best accuracy with 226.7M parameters, beating InternImage-XL (88.0%, 335M params) by 0.4 points with 32% fewer parameters, and outperforming MOAT3 (88.2%) and FlashInternImage-L (88.1%). On COCO detection, UniConvNet-L (55.7% AP^b) exceeds InternImage-XL (55.3%) and FlashInternImage-L (55.6%), and on ADE20K segmentation, UniConvNet-L (55.1% mIoU) edges out InternImage-XL (55.0%) with fewer parameters (234M vs. 368M). The paper also shows that replacing the proposed Three-layer RFA with a simple depth-wise 7×7 convolution drops accuracy by 1.2–1.7 points (e.g., 84.2% → 77.0% at P0 scale), confirming the design's contribution over generic large-kernel convolutions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on InternImage's DCNV3 deformable convolution as its residual component, but departs fundamentally from the dominant large-kernel ConvNet paradigm (RepLKNet's 31×31, SLaK's 51×51, PeLK's 101×101) by arguing that extremely large kernels disrupt the asymptotically Gaussian distribution (AGD) of the effective receptive field. Rather than scaling kernel size, it introduces a Receptive Field Aggregator that combines moderate progressive kernels (7×7, 9×9, 11×11) through a Layer Operator with Amplifier and Discriminator sub-modules, achieving comparable ERF scale while preserving the multi-scale impact pattern that small-kernel stacks naturally produce. This positions the work as a conceptual counterpoint to the 'bigger kernel is better' trend, reframing the design question from ERF magnitude to ERF distribution shape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The AGD principle offers a potentially useful diagnostic for ConvNet design: it explains why ResNet's 3×3 stacks work (natural Gaussian ERF) and why large-kernel networks can underperform despite larger ERF (disrupted AGD). Practically, UniConvNet provides a competitive pure-CNN backbone across the full scale range (3.4M to 226.7M parameters) with consistent gains over both CNN and ViT baselines on classification, detection, and segmentation. Open questions include whether the AGD framing generalizes beyond 224×224 resolution (the paper only ablates at this scale), whether the specific (7,9,11) kernel progression is truly optimal or merely locally optimal, and whether the conceptual advantage over large-kernel methods persists as model scale and data scale increase further.
