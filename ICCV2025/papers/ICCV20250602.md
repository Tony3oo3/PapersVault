## Problem

Existing voxel-based 3D detectors rely on simple MLP followed by global pooling (average or max) to encode voxel features, which discards fine-grained spatial structure information from raw point clouds. This information loss is particularly severe with larger voxel sizes and constrains the upper bound of detection accuracy, as no prior method explicitly models the geometric relationships between individual points within a voxel.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250602/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes LiDAR point clouds voxelized into a 3D grid (e.g., 0.3×0.3×0.25 m for nuScenes) and processes each non-empty voxel by padding its points to a fixed maximum of M=32 nodes. The Geometry Point Encoder outputs a single feature vector per voxel (taken from the first token position, analogous to ViT's [CLS]), which is then fed into any existing voxel-based detection head (TransFusion, CenterPoint, or heatmap-based sparse center) to produce 3D bounding boxes with class labels and confidence scores. The module is designed as a drop-in replacement for the voxel encoder, requiring no changes to the downstream backbone or head.

## Method

GeoFormer represents points within each voxel as a graph whose edge weights are computed from pairwise relative distances via a piecewise linear function bounded by thresholds θ_min and θ_max, with all coordinates shifted to a voxel-centered relative frame. A geometry-guided multi-head attention mechanism then re-weights the standard QK^T/√d similarity scores by element-wise multiplication with the edge matrix E_i (inspired by Graph Transformer), explicitly suppressing attention between distant point pairs that lack geometric connectivity. No positional encoding is added, following PCT's assumption that raw coordinates already encode position. The encoder uses 2 transformer layers with 8 heads and 128-dim features, and a two-GPE partitioning variant splits voxels by internal point count to avoid wasting computation on padded tokens. The resulting voxel features are plug-and-play compatible with CNN, Transformer, and SSM-based backbones, yielding consistent mAP gains across all tested architectures.

## Results

On nuScenes validation, GeoFormer achieves 68.2 mAP and 72.4 NDS, outperforming the previous best VoxelMamba (67.5 mAP, 71.9 NDS) by +0.7 mAP and +0.5 NDS. On the nuScenes test set, it reaches 69.8 mAP and 73.7 NDS. On Waymo Open (L2), GeoFormer attains 72.21/71.75 mAP/mAPH for Vehicle, 78.32/74.03 for Pedestrian, and 76.42/75.61 for Cyclist, setting new SOTA. On Argoverse 2, it achieves 41.7 mAP, the highest among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On nuScenes val, GeoFormer (68.2 mAP / 72.4 NDS) surpasses TransFusion-L (65.5 / 70.1) by +2.7 mAP and +2.3 NDS, SAFDNet (66.3 / 71.0) by +1.9 mAP, and VoxelMamba (67.5 / 71.9) by +0.7 mAP and +0.5 NDS. On the nuScenes test set, it leads VoxelMamba (69.0 / 73.0) by +0.8 mAP and +0.7 NDS, and SAFDNet (68.3 / 72.3) by +1.5 mAP. On Waymo Open L2, GeoFormer outperforms VoxelMamba on Pedestrian (78.32 vs 77.70 mAP) and Cyclist (76.42 vs 75.70 mAP), though VoxelMamba retains a slight edge on Vehicle L1 (80.80 vs 80.48 mAP). On Argoverse 2, GeoFormer (41.7 mAP) edges out LION-Mamba (41.5) by +0.2 and SAFDNet (39.7) by +2.0 mAP. The plug-and-play GPE module also yields consistent gains when inserted into existing backbones: +3.6 mAP on PillarNet, +1.0 mAP on VoxelMamba, and +0.5 mAP on SAFDNet (Table 5), confirming broad generalizability across Transformer-, CNN-, and SSM-based architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoFormer builds directly on the Graph Transformer [Dwivedi & Bresson, 2021] by incorporating edge-weighted attention into a standard multi-head self-attention mechanism, but repurposes it for the specific problem of encoding intra-voxel point geometry in LiDAR 3D detection. It departs from the dominant voxel-encoding paradigm (MLP + global pooling in PointPillars/CenterPoint, or PointNet-style encoding) by explicitly constructing a distance-based graph over points within each voxel and using that graph structure to modulate attention, rather than relying on implicit feature similarity alone. The design also draws on PCT's observation that raw coordinates suffice as positional information, and positions itself as a drop-in replacement for the voxel encoder stage of any existing voxel-based detector, orthogonal to the choice of 3D backbone (CNN, Transformer, or SSM-based).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of GPE—showing consistent mAP gains across PillarNet, DSVT, HEDNet, SAFDNet, and VoxelMamba without architectural changes—makes it a practical upgrade path for existing LiDAR detection pipelines. The robustness to voxel size (e.g., +16.09 AP for Cyclists at 0.64 m voxels vs. baseline) addresses a real deployment concern where larger voxels are used for efficiency. Open questions include whether the fixed distance thresholds (θ_min, θ_max) generalize across sensor configurations and driving scenarios, whether the 2-layer depth is truly sufficient for denser point clouds, and whether the graph construction overhead becomes prohibitive at higher point densities or in indoor/robotic settings with tighter spatial structures.
