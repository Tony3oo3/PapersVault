## Problem

Existing monocular semantic scene completion methods adopt a single-stage framework that must simultaneously perform visible-region segmentation and occluded-region hallucination, while being further degraded by noisy estimated depth. This coupling leads to suboptimal performance, particularly in complex scenes where geometric restoration and semantic consistency are both challenging. The paper asks whether decomposing MSSC into a coarse estimation followed by iterative masked refinement can decouple these difficulties and improve both accuracy and robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250397/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a volumetric voxel grid with per-voxel occupancy (binary) and semantic class labels. For NYUv2 the output grid is 60×36×60 voxels at 0.08 m resolution; for SemanticKITTI it is 128×128×16 (upsampled to 256×256×32 at 0.2 m). Depth is not provided as input but estimated off-the-shelf via AdaBins and used solely for 2D-to-3D feature projection in the coarse stage.

## Method

MonoMRN decomposes MSSC into a Coarse MSSC stage (ResNet-50 2D encoder → depth-based surface projection → AIC 3D modules) and a Masked Recurrent Network that iteratively refines the result over two steps. The core refinement unit is the MS-GRU, which applies a dynamic binary occupancy mask to gate both the hidden state and the input, and uses a mixture of submanifold and sparse convolutions to exploit 3D sparsity, reducing MACs from 172 G (dense GRU) to 52 G. A Distance Attention Projection replaces naive surface or sight projection by assigning per-voxel attention weights inversely proportional to distance from the estimated observation surface, enabling feature propagation into occluded regions while suppressing spurious assignments. A Mask Updating Module iteratively adds and removes top-K voxels from the occupancy mask based on the current MSSC head output, and the whole pipeline is trained with a sequential cross-entropy loss (γ=0.8), a sequential binary mask loss (γ=0.6), and a Scene-Class Affinity loss.

## Results

On NYUv2 (indoor), MonoMRN achieves 53.16% SC IoU and 30.73% SSC mIoU, surpassing the previous best (NDC-Scene) by 8.99% in SC IoU and 1.60% in SSC mIoU. On SemanticKITTI (outdoor), MonoMRN achieves 42.0% SC IoU and 13.8% SSC mIoU, outperforming the best monocular baseline (NDC-Scene) by 3.35% in SC IoU and 1.07% in SSC mIoU. The method runs at 2.56 FPS on NYUv2, comparable to MonoScene (1.96 FPS) while delivering substantially higher accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MonoMRN outperforms all monocular baselines on both datasets. On NYUv2, it beats NDC-Scene (44.17/29.03) by 8.99/1.60 points in SC IoU/SSC mIoU, MonoScene (42.51/26.94) by 10.65/3.79, and 3DSketch (38.64/22.91) by 14.52/7.82. On SemanticKITTI, MonoMRN (42.0/13.8) surpasses NDC-Scene (37.2/12.7) by 4.8/1.1, OccFormer (34.5/12.3) by 7.5/1.5, TPVFormer (34.3/11.3) by 7.7/2.5, and MonoScene (34.2/11.1) by 7.8/2.7. Against stereo-based methods on SemanticKITTI, MonoMRN trails HASSC-S (44.8/13.5) and HTCL-M (44.2/17.1) in SC IoU but matches or exceeds them in SSC mIoU (13.8 vs. 13.5 and 17.1 respectively, with HTCL-M using temporal input). MonoMRN also outperforms the monocular VoxFormer (38.7/10.7) by 3.3/3.1 points, demonstrating that the recurrent refinement stage compensates for the lack of stereo geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

MonoMRN departs from the single-stage MSSC paradigm established by MonoScene and NDC-Scene by decomposing the problem into a coarse estimation stage followed by iterative recurrent refinement. The MS-GRU repurposes the gated recurrent unit concept from sequence modeling for 3D voxel feature refinement, combined with submanifold and sparse convolutions borrowed from 3D point cloud processing. The Distance Attention Projection carves a middle ground between the surface projection used in VoxFormer/AICNet (which leaves occluded regions unassigned) and the sight projection used in MonoScene (which introduces incorrect features into occluded regions), weighting features by their distance to the observed surface.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-stage decomposition with masked recurrent refinement offers a practical path to improving MSSC in complex scenes where single-stage methods struggle, and the robustness analysis under darkness, motion blur, fog, and brightness shifts demonstrates that the iterative refinement stage genuinely aids information recovery under out-of-distribution conditions. However, the method remains heavily dependent on estimated depth (0.364 m RMS vs. 0.08 m voxel size on NYUv2), which the authors acknowledge as a significant bottleneck. Open questions include whether depth correction can be integrated into the recurrent loop, whether more iterations or alternative recurrent architectures (e.g., transformers) could further improve refinement, and whether the approach generalizes to higher-resolution outdoor scenes beyond SemanticKITTI's 256×256×32 grid.
