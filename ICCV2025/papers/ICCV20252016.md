## Problem

Holistic aerial scene reconstruction—jointly addressing semantic and geometric understanding—lacks a comprehensive benchmark: real-world UAV datasets are small, fragmented, and missing key modalities, while synthetic alternatives suffer from low resolution (≤2K), absent panoptic annotations, rendering artifacts, and restricted single-viewpoint capture. Additionally, no principled metric exists to quantify scene complexity from 2D imagery alone in a way that predicts downstream reconstruction difficulty, leaving practitioners reliant on heuristic assessments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252016/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ClaraVid provides 16,917 RGB frames at 4032×3024 (75° horizontal FoV) captured simultaneously from 3 viewpoints at varying altitudes (45–75 m) and pitch angles (45°, 55°, 90°) with 80–90% overlap and 2–3 s inter-frame intervals. Per-frame outputs include metric depth maps, panoptic segmentation masks over 18 semantic classes, dynamic object masks isolating moving entities, and scene-level sparse point clouds (RGB + semantic + instance) sampled at three LiDAR-like densities (30, 100, 200 cm). The DSP module consumes grayscale frames with dynamic regions masked out and emits a truncated Beta distribution parameterized by (α, β, a, b) over the empirical delentropy range [a, b] ⊂ [0, 9.83].

## Method

The paper introduces two coupled contributions. First, ClaraVid is rendered in Unreal Engine 4 leveraging the CARLA ecosystem for dynamic agents, spanning 5 environments (urban, urban-high, rural, highway, natural) across 8 grid-based UAV missions covering 1.8 km², with photorealistic asset refinement and procedural foliage to mitigate the uniform compositions of prior synthetic datasets. Second, the Delentropic Scene Profile (DSP) computes per-image delentropy by estimating the joint PDF of Sobel gradient components (f_x, f_y) via a 2D histogram, then taking the negative log-entropy of that distribution; across all N images in a scene, the resulting delentropy values are modeled as a truncated Beta distribution fitted by maximum likelihood, yielding four interpretable descriptors (μ, σ, α, β) that characterize overall complexity, heterogeneity, and skew. The benchmarking framework then evaluates five neural reconstruction methods (Nerfacto, TensoRF, Instant-NGP, ZipNeRF, Gaussian Splatting) on novel view synthesis, semantic segmentation, and depth estimation, demonstrating a consistent negative Pearson correlation between DSP mean μ and PSNR/SSIM and a positive correlation with LPIPS, validating delentropy as a reliable a priori complexity prior.

## Results

Across 8 scenes with varying Delentropic Scene Profiles, Gaussian Splatting achieves the best average reconstruction quality (PSNR 23.67, SSIM 0.6057, LPIPS 0.4987), closely followed by ZipNeRF (PSNR 23.75, SSIM 0.5966, LPIPS 0.5622), while TensoRF performs worst (PSNR 21.16, SSIM 0.4144, LPIPS 0.7189). A clear trend emerges: higher DSP mean (μ) consistently correlates with lower PSNR/SSIM and higher LPIPS, confirming that increased scene complexity degrades reconstruction fidelity. For semantic segmentation, ClaraVid achieves an oracle mIoU of 82.51% and a smaller synthetic-to-real domain gap (average 17.82%) compared to Syndrone (22.34%), despite containing 4.5× fewer samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the five reconstruction methods benchmarked, ZipNeRF achieves the highest average PSNR (23.75) while Gaussian Splatting achieves the best SSIM (0.6057) and lowest LPIPS (0.4987), making them the two strongest performers; TensoRF is consistently the weakest (PSNR 21.16, LPIPS 0.7189), and Instant-NGP is second-worst (PSNR 21.38, LPIPS 0.6648). Nerfacto sits in the middle (PSNR 21.84, LPIPS 0.7138) and generalizes better to unseen viewpoints in Table 3, while Gaussian Splatting degrades markedly under viewpoint shifts. For the synthetic-to-real semantic segmentation comparison, ClaraVid outperforms Syndrone across all four real test sets (e.g., 57.50 vs 52.49 mIoU on UAVid; 58.22 vs 54.43 on UDD), with a smaller average domain gap of 17.82% versus 22.34%, despite having 4.5× fewer training samples. Delentropy also outperforms Shannon pixel entropy and GLCM texture entropy as a complexity proxy, yielding stronger Pearson correlations with reconstruction error and tighter predictive intervals across both Gaussian Splatting and Nerfacto.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Larkin's delentropy (2016), originally formulated as a pixel-level image complexity measure, into a scene-level descriptor (the Delentropic Scene Profile) that characterizes reconstruction difficulty from 2D aerial imagery alone. It positions itself against a fragmented landscape of aerial datasets—real ones (UAVid, UDD, SkyScapes, Dronescapes) that lack multi-modal ground truth or mapping-oriented acquisition, and synthetic ones (Syndrone, Matrix City, UrbanSyn) that suffer from low resolution, missing semantic annotations, or rendering artifacts. The neural reconstruction methods benchmarked (NeRF variants, Gaussian Splatting) are well-established for urban scenes but have largely been evaluated without semantic supervision or under restricted single-viewpoint protocols, which ClaraVid addresses through its multi-view, multi-altitude, panoptic-annotated design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ClaraVid fills a concrete gap by providing the first synthetic aerial dataset combining 4K multi-view imagery, panoptic segmentation, dense depth, dynamic object masks, and scene-level point clouds under a structured mapping protocol, making it a practical benchmark for holistic (semantic + geometric) aerial reconstruction. The DSP offers a lightweight, model-agnostic complexity prior that could inform adaptive resource allocation in reconstruction pipelines, though its utility is constrained to structured, bounded acquisition settings and is sensitive to imaging artifacts. Open questions include whether the delentropy–error correlation holds under unstructured or unbounded flight patterns, and whether the synthetic-to-real gap (still ~18% mIoU) can be further narrowed without substantially increasing dataset scale.
