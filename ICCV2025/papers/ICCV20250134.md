## Problem

Existing OCSR methods rely heavily on synthetic training data and standard SMILES, which cannot represent Markush structures, connection points, abstract rings, and other complex molecular entities prevalent in real patents and scientific literature. This results in poor performance on in-the-wild molecular images that contain noise, varied drawing styles, and structurally complex Markush compounds, limiting the use of LLMs for large-scale chemical document understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250134/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB molecular structure image (224×224 for Tiny/Small variants, 384×384 for Base) cropped from a real PDF document and autoregressively generates an E-SMILES string. The output format is SMILES<sep>EXTENSION, where the first segment is standard RDKit-compatible SMILES and the optional second segment uses XML-like tokens (<a>, <r>, <c>, <dum>) to encode Markush R-groups, uncertain ring attachments, abstract rings, and connection points that standard SMILES cannot express.

## Method

MolParser is an end-to-end image captioning architecture comprising an ImageNet-pretrained Swin-Transformer vision encoder, a two-layer MLP feature compressor, and a BART decoder that autoregressively generates E-SMILES tokens. Training follows a two-stage curriculum: pretraining on 7.7M synthetic image–E-SMILES pairs with progressively increasing data augmentation and molecular complexity, followed by fine-tuning on 400K in-the-wild samples collected via an active learning data engine. The data engine uses a 5-fold model ensemble to compute Tanimoto-similarity-based confidence scores, selectively routing low-confidence (0.6–0.9) samples for human annotation with model pre-annotations, reducing per-sample annotation time from ~3 minutes to ~30 seconds. This combination of extended SMILES vocabulary, large-scale synthetic pretraining with curriculum scheduling, and active-learning-driven real-data fine-tuning is the core mechanism that distinguishes MolParser from prior end-to-end OCSR systems.

## Results

On the newly proposed WildMol-10K benchmark (molecules cropped from real patents and literature), MolParser-Base achieves 76.9% accuracy, substantially outperforming the previous state-of-the-art MolScribe (66.4%) and MolGrapher (45.5%). On the in-the-wild ColoredBG benchmark, MolParser-Base reaches 57.0% accuracy versus 21.0% for MolScribe and 7.5% for MolGrapher. In terms of speed, MolParser-Tiny processes 131.6 molecular images per second (vs. 2.2 for MolGrapher and 16.5 for MolScribe) while retaining 73.1% accuracy on WildMol-10K. On classical benchmarks (USPTO, UoB, CLEF, JPO), MolParser-Base achieves 93.0%, 91.8%, 90.7%, and 78.9% respectively, matching or exceeding all prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MolParser-Base decisively outperforms all baselines on the in-the-wild benchmarks: WildMol-10K (76.9% vs. 66.4% MolScribe, 45.5% MolGrapher, 56.0% DECIMER 2.7) and ColoredBG (57.0% vs. 21.0% MolScribe, 7.5% MolGrapher), confirming that real-data fine-tuning via the active learning data engine is critical. On the USPTO-10K benchmark, MolScribe narrowly edges out MolParser-Base (96.0% vs. 94.5%), while MolParser-Base leads on JPO (78.9% vs. 76.2%). On classical benchmarks (USPTO, UoB, CLEF), all top methods cluster within 1–3% of each other, with MolParser-Base competitive but not dominant. In speed-accuracy tradeoff (Table 3), MolParser-Tiny achieves 131.6 FPS at 73.1% WildMol accuracy, far surpassing MolGrapher (2.2 FPS, 45.5%) and MolScribe (16.5 FPS, 66.4%), making it the only method combining high throughput with strong in-the-wild accuracy. For molecular property prediction (Table 6), the MolParser-pretrained Swin-T backbone achieves 73.7 average ROC-AUC, matching 3D graph-based methods (Mol-AE, UniCorn at 74.5) and substantially outperforming the same architecture with ImageNet pretraining (68.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

MolParser sits at the intersection of two OCSR lineages: end-to-end image captioning models (Img2Mol, DECIMER, SwinOCSR) and graph-based reconstruction methods (MolGrapher, MolScribe). Architecturally it follows the image-captioning paradigm (Swin encoder + BART decoder), but its key departure is the data strategy: rather than relying solely on synthetic data like prior end-to-end methods, it introduces an active-learning data engine that extracts and annotates ~400k real-world molecular images from patents and literature. The E-SMILES representation extends the limited FG-SMILES attempt in Image2SMILES to handle Markush structures, abstract rings, polymers, and connection points while maintaining RDKit compatibility, filling a representation gap that no prior encoding addressed comprehensively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7.7M paired dataset and WildMol benchmark address a critical bottleneck in OCSR: prior methods were trained and evaluated almost exclusively on clean synthetic images, leaving a large performance gap on real patent and literature scans. The active-learning data engine (YOLO11 detection → p-hash deduplication → ensemble-confidence sampling → human annotation) provides a replicable pipeline for building domain-specific vision datasets at scale. Open questions remain: Markush accuracy is still low (38.1%), molecular chirality is unhandled, and the property-prediction side result (2D image features matching 3D graph methods) is promising but underexplored. Scaling the real-data fine-tuning set beyond 400k samples is the most obvious path to further gains.
