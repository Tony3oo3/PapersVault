## Problem

Reconstructing persistent 3D geometry and full-length 3D motion trajectories from a single casually captured monocular video remains an open challenge. Prior dynamic reconstruction methods either require synchronized multi-view captures or LIDAR, model motion only as short-range scene flow between consecutive frames, or use deformation fields that entangle object and camera motion, failing to produce globally consistent 3D trajectories in world coordinates.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video sequence with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics, plus three off-the-shelf priors: per-frame relative monocular depth maps (Depth Anything, aligned via per-frame scale/shift), long-range 2D point tracks (TAPIR), and user-clicked moving object masks (Track-Anything). It outputs a set of persistent 3D Gaussians parameterized by shared SE(3) motion bases, enabling both real-time novel view synthesis (~140 fps) and queryable full-length 3D trajectories for any surface point at any time. The pipeline requires per-scene test-time optimization (~2 hours on an A100 for 300 frames at 960×720) and manual mask annotation.

## Method

The scene is represented as N canonical 3D Gaussians whose positions and orientations evolve via rigid SE(3) transformations, with the key architectural choice of expressing each Gaussian's motion as a weighted linear combination of B=10 globally shared SE(3) motion bases, enforcing a low-dimensional structure that soft-decomposes the scene into rigidly-moving groups. Optimization proceeds in two phases: an initialization stage that fits the motion bases via weighted Procrustes alignment on k-means-clustered noisy 3D tracks, followed by joint training with losses on rendered RGB/depth/mask reconstruction, long-range 2D track consistency, reprojected depth consistency, and a distance-preserving rigidity regularizer between k-nearest Gaussian neighbors. The approach consolidates complementary but noisy depth and 2D-track signals into a single globally coherent 4D representation, allowing occluded motion to be inferred from nearby visible regions through the shared motion basis structure.

## Results

On the iPhone dataset, the method achieves EPE of 0.082 m for long-range 3D tracking (vs. 0.114 m for the best baseline TAPIR+DA), 43.0% of points within 5 cm and 73.3% within 10 cm of ground truth. For 2D tracking, it reaches AJ of 34.4 and occlusion accuracy of 86.6%, substantially outperforming all baselines (best: TAPIR+DA at AJ 27.8, OA 67.4). For novel view synthesis, it achieves PSNR 16.72, SSIM 0.63, and LPIPS 0.45, surpassing the best dynamic NeRF/GS baselines (DynMF: PSNR 16.54; D-3DGS: PSNR 11.92). On the synthetic Kubric MOVi-F dataset, it also leads with EPE 0.16 vs. 0.19–0.20 for CoTracker+DA and TAPIR+DA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 10 baselines across every evaluated metric. In 3D tracking, it reduces EPE from 0.114 m (TAPIR+DA, the strongest tracking baseline) to 0.082 m, and from 0.125 m (SpatialTracker) to 0.082 m, while also improving δ₃D⁰·¹⁰ from 63.9% to 73.3%. For 2D tracking, it surpasses TAPIR+DA (AJ 27.8, OA 67.4) by a wide margin (AJ 34.4, OA 86.6), and similarly beats CoTracker+DA (AJ 24.1) and DELTA (AJ 24.7). Among view-synthesis baselines, it edges out DynMF (PSNR 16.54) by 0.18 dB and substantially outperforms D-3DGS (PSNR 11.92), HyperNeRF (PSNR 15.99), and DynIBaR (PSNR 13.41); T-NeRF does not support 3D/2D tracking evaluation. On the NVIDIA dataset, the method is comparable to Dynamic Gaussian Marbles (DGM) [91], with slightly higher PSNR on Balloon1, Balloon2, and Truck scenes but slightly lower SSIM on Playground and Umbrella, yielding a near-tie in mean PSNR (23.37 vs. 23.46). The paper notes that tracking-only baselines (TAPIR+DA, CoTracker+DA, DELTA, SpatialTracker) cannot produce novel views, while view-synthesis baselines (D-3DGS, HyperNeRF, DynIBaR) either fail to produce accurate 3D tracks or degrade significantly on high-motion scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) as its rendering and optimization backbone, and on DynMF (Kratimenos et al., 2024) for the concept of shared SE(3) motion bases that decompose scene motion into a compact set of rigid trajectories. It departs from prior dynamic NeRF/GS methods (HyperNeRF, Deformable-3DGS, DynIBaR) that model motion implicitly via deformation fields or time-dependent radiance, instead recovering explicit persistent 3D trajectories in world coordinates. A key conceptual departure is the consolidation of noisy off-the-shelf priors (TAPIR 2D tracks, Depth Anything depth, Track-Anything masks) into a single globally coherent 4D representation, rather than treating them as independent signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to jointly achieve state-of-the-art long-range 3D tracking, 2D tracking, and novel view synthesis from a single casually-captured monocular video, unifying tasks that prior work addressed in isolation. The 'Shape of Motion' visualization—revealing geometric patterns in 3D trajectories—offers an intuitive diagnostic for scene understanding. Key limitations include per-scene test-time optimization (hindering real-time/streamable use), reliance on user-provided motion masks, and sensitivity to the quality of off-the-shelf depth and tracking priors in textureless or high-motion regions. Open questions include whether feed-forward variants can eliminate per-scene optimization and whether automatic motion segmentation can replace manual masking.
