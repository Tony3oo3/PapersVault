## Problem

Visual tracking benchmarks rely on precisely annotated bounding boxes for initialization, which is labor-intensive and introduces seconds of delay in real-world use. Click-based initialization is far more practical but suffers from a severe information gap—lacking scale, boundary, and appearance cues—that existing modular solutions (detector or interactive model + tracker) address only partially, with added parameters, post-processing uncertainty, and a persistent performance gap versus box-initialized trackers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252006/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single click point (simulated by sampling within a W_gt/10 × H_gt/10 rectangle centered on the ground-truth target) and a sequence of video frames. During CBL, the full search frame is downsampled by a factor of 16 and the click coordinate is encoded by a two-layer MLP into a continuous spatial vector; during JSVR, 4× and 2× cropped regions around the CBL output provide search and visual prompts. The output is an initialized bounding box followed by per-frame target locations, with CTMoE active only during initialization and the frozen foundation tracker handling subsequent frames.

## Method

CAT freezes a one-stream transformer tracker (OSTrack or ODTrack) and inserts a CTMoE module—comprising an asymmetric Mixture-of-Experts (shared A matrix across experts, task-specific B matrices) and a Sequential Task Router that inverts gating weights when the second task is introduced—adding only 10.6% parameters. A two-stage initialization bridges the click-to-box gap: CBL performs global localization on the full frame using coordinate embeddings, then JSVR refines the result in a tracking-isomorphic format by jointly matching spatial prompts (center, size) with visual prompts (cropped template) against a cropped search region. Training is parameter-efficient and sequential: 400 epochs on CBL alone, then 600 epochs with JSVR added, optimizing only CTMoE and the spatial encoder under L_cls + L_iou + L_1 losses with α=1, β=3.

## Results

On LaSOT, CAT_od384 achieves a state-of-the-art 70.5% AUC for click-based tracking, surpassing the previous best click-based method (SAM2L_os384 at 63.8%) by 6.7% and even matching SimTrack (70.5%) which uses precise bounding-box initialization. On TrackingNet, CAT_od384 reaches 83.9% AUC, outperforming the DETA-initialized tracker by 1.4%. On GOT-10k, CAT_os384 and CAT_os256 both achieve 72.1% AO, matching the bounding-box-initialized LoRAT-B224. The framework adds only 10.6% parameters (9.8M) over the frozen foundation tracker while unifying initialization and tracking in a single model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAT decisively outperforms all click-based baselines: on LaSOT, CAT_od384 (70.5% AUC) exceeds SAM2L_os384 (63.8%) by 6.7%, SAM1H_os384 (59.8%) by 10.7%, and detector-based methods like DETA (55.6%) and YOLOv7 (55.7%) by ~15%. Against bounding-box-initialized trackers, CAT_od384 narrows the gap to just 0.6% behind OSTrack-B384 (71.1%) and ties SimTrack (70.5%), though it trails the strongest box-based trackers ODTrack-B384 (73.2%) and ARTrackV2-B384 (73.0%) by 2.5–2.7%. On TrackingNet, CAT_od384 (83.9%) surpasses the DETA-initialized tracker (82.5%) by 1.4% and beats SAM2L_os256 by 8.1% under aligned settings. On GOT-10k, CAT_os384 and CAT_os256 both reach 72.1% AO, matching LoRAT-B224 exactly. On UAV benchmarks (Table 2), CAT_od384 leads all click-based methods by 4.3% over SAM2L_os256 on UAV123 and by 7.4% over the dedicated UAV tracker TCTrack.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAT builds directly on one-stream transformer trackers (OSTrack, ODTrack) as frozen foundation models, extending the PEFT paradigm (LoRA, adapters) into the tracking domain via a novel asymmetric Mixture-of-Experts module (CTMoE) inspired by HydraLoRA. It departs from the dominant modular paradigm for click-based tracking—where a separate detector or interactive segmentation model (SAM, SAM2, YOLOv7) generates an initial box that is then handed to a tracker—by unifying initialization and tracking within a single architecture. The two-stage initialization (CBL followed by JSVR) is a new formulation that explicitly addresses the geometric information loss inherent in point-only prompts, a problem previously handled only implicitly by auxiliary models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CAT narrows the performance gap between click-based and bounding-box-initialized tracking to roughly 2–3% AUC on LaSOT while adding only 10.6% parameters, making realistic, low-latency tracking initialization practical without the seconds-long annotation delay of bounding boxes. The JSVR component generalizes to refine outputs from unrelated interactive models (SAM1, SAM2), suggesting it could serve as a plug-in refinement stage more broadly. Key limitations remain: the framework handles only single-click prompts (no language, scribble, or multi-click), and the sequential training protocol (CBL must converge before JSVR is introduced) adds complexity to the training pipeline. Extending CAT to richer interaction modalities and reducing the residual gap to box-initialized trackers are natural next steps.
