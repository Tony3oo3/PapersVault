## Problem

Deep neural networks frequently exploit spurious features that correlate with labels in training but lack causal relevance, leading to poor out-of-distribution generalization and uninterpretable decisions. Prior approaches to suppress spurious features rely on expensive additional annotations (bounding boxes, segmentation masks) or external attribution methods. The paper asks how to promote genuine feature usage and inherent interpretability using only standard image labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251315/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (e.g., 224×224) and requires no annotations beyond class labels. In addition to the final class prediction, the model produces binary spatial masks at each encoding stage of the backbone (resolutions 56², 28², 14², 7² for ConvNeXt-tiny), which are element-wise multiplied with the corresponding feature maps to yield spatially sparse representations. These masks are the direct, interpretable basis of the classification decision rather than post-hoc explanations.

## Method

AIM augments a standard CNN backbone (ConvNeXt-tiny, ResNet-50/101) with a top-down masking pathway inspired by the Feature Pyramid Network. At each stage, a lightweight mask estimator (3×3 conv → three residual blocks → global average pooling → 1×1 conv) predicts a soft attention map, which is binarized via Gumbel-softmax to produce a binary mask that sparsifies the stage's feature maps. The key architectural insight is that the top-down direction (propagating semantic information from high-level to low-level stages) naturally yields sparse, focused masks without auxiliary sparsity losses, unlike bottom-up masking which tends to produce fully active masks. Mask estimators are supervised indirectly: each stage's masked features are passed through a stage-specific classifier, and the resulting classification loss provides the only training signal. An optional mask annealing schedule progressively reduces the target active-area ratio (e.g., from 100% to 25–35%) to further sharpen mask focus on challenging OOD datasets.

## Results

On the most challenging out-of-distribution benchmark, Waterbirds-100%, ConvNeXt-tiny+AIM[2,35%] improves worst-group accuracy from 39.6% (baseline) to 78.1%, a gain of nearly 39 points, while EPG rises from 57.2 to 68.5. On Waterbirds-95%, worst-group accuracy jumps from 81.6% to 92.7% (AIM[2,25%]), and on TravelingBirds, accuracy increases from 59.5% to 77.4% with EPG improving from 74.4 to 85.0. On in-domain benchmarks (ImageNet100, Hard-ImageNet), AIM maintains or slightly improves accuracy while boosting EPG by 1–4 points, confirming that interpretability gains do not come at a performance cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is each backbone (ConvNeXt-tiny, ResNet-50, ResNet-101) against itself augmented with AIM, using only image labels as supervision. On Waterbirds-100%, the best AIM configuration (ConvNeXt-t+AIM[2,35%]) more than doubles worst-group accuracy over the vanilla ConvNeXt-tiny baseline (78.1% vs. 39.6%), and on TravelingBirds it gains 17.9 points (77.4% vs. 59.5%). EPG scores improve by roughly 6–30% across all OOD datasets, confirming better localization of genuine features. On in-domain benchmarks (ImageNet100, Hard-ImageNet, CUB-200), AIM matches or slightly exceeds baseline accuracy (e.g., 90.7% vs. 89.2% on ImageNet100) while improving EPG by 1–4 points. A user study further showed human raters preferred AIM's attribution maps over the baseline in 70.7% of cases (p<0.00001), and a bottom-up masking ablation (Table 2) confirms the top-down design is critical, as bottom-up masking degrades CUB-200 accuracy to 72.8% versus 88.8% for the top-down AIM.

## Relation to Prior Work

AIM adapts the Feature Pyramid Network's top-down pathway structure [Lin et al., 2017] and the Gumbel-softmax binary mask estimation mechanism from Verelst et al. [2020] and Hesse et al. [2023], but critically repositions masking from a bottom-up to a top-down direction. This departure is the paper's central architectural insight: prior bottom-up masking approaches (Verelst, Hesse) produce fully active masks requiring auxiliary sparsity losses, whereas the top-down formulation naturally yields sparse, focused masks without extra supervision. The method also contrasts with model-guidance approaches that require bounding boxes or segmentation annotations, and with post-hoc attribution methods (Grad-CAM, etc.), by embedding interpretability directly into the forward pass through self-supervised spatial selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AIM offers a lightweight, annotation-free mechanism that simultaneously improves OOD generalization and provides inherent (not post-hoc) interpretability, which is practically valuable for deploying trustworthy vision models. The consistent gains across six datasets and three backbone architectures strengthen the claim that the top-down masking principle is domain-agnostic. However, the method is currently limited to CNN backbones; the authors acknowledge that extending to Vision Transformers remains open. The mask annealing schedule introduces additional hyperparameters (threshold τ, annealing duration) that may complicate practical adoption, and the large accuracy gains on Waterbirds-100% (40%) should be contextualized against the extremely low baseline performance on that synthetic benchmark.
