## Problem

Learned video codecs propagate temporal information via either output-recurrence (buffering decoded frames) or hidden-to-hidden connections (buffering latent features), but each has a fundamental limitation: the former imposes a dual constraint on the decoded frame to both approximate the input and summarize past information, while the latter requires large implicit buffers (48+ high-resolution feature maps) with non-compact, non-explainable content. No prior work systematically combines both mechanisms in a unified framework to study their rate-distortion-complexity trade-offs.

## Input / Output

The codec processes RGB video frames (converted from YUV420 via BT.601, cropped to multiples of 64) frame-by-frame with an IPPP structure, encoding the first 96 frames with an intra period of 32. At each time step, the input is the current RGB frame plus the previously decoded frame and decoded optical flow map from the prior step; the output is a compressed bitstream and a reconstructed frame. The hybrid buffer stores only 7.875 full-resolution-equivalent feature maps per frame (one decoded RGB frame plus a small number of latent features), compared to 48–67 maps in state-of-the-art implicit-buffering codecs.

## Method

HyTIP integrates output-recurrence and hidden-to-hidden connections in both motion coding and inter-frame coding within a masked conditional residual coding framework. For inter-frame coding, the previously decoded frame serves as the primary explicit temporal reference (motion-compensated to produce the pixel-domain predictor x_c), while a small set of latent features F_{t-1} provides complementary implicit information as multi-scale condition signals {C_1, C_2, C_3} to the encoder and decoder. A pixel-wise soft mask m (generated from the decoded flow and predictor) blends conditional and conditional residual coding, so the codec input is x_t − m⊙x_c. For motion coding, the same hybrid strategy buffers both the decoded flow map f̂_{t-1} and latent features F^f_{t-1} as temporal references without motion compensation. The key design insight is that leveraging the high correlation between the previous decoded frame and the current frame reduces reliance on implicit features, enabling a 14× smaller buffer than purely implicit methods while achieving comparable BD-rate performance.

## Results

HyTIP achieves an average BD-rate of −22.1% (PSNR-RGB) and −56.7% (MS-SSIM-RGB) relative to VTM 17.0 (Low-delay B) across UVG, MCL-JCV, HEVC B–E, and HEVC-RGB. In terms of complexity, HyTIP requires only 7.875 full-resolution feature maps of buffer size—just 14% of the 55.75 used by DCVC-DC/DCVC-FM—while maintaining 16.6M parameters and 1293/873 kMACs/pixel for encoding/decoding. The hybrid buffering ablation (Table 2) shows that combining explicit and implicit buffers yields −21.5% average BD-rate with only 2 additional latent channels, versus −14.7% for explicit-only and −15.0% for implicit-only at matched buffer sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest learned codec DCVC-DC (−23.6% avg PSNR-RGB), HyTIP (−22.1%) is within 1.5% on average but trails on HEVC-E (−18.6% vs −28.1%), a dataset of static-background video-conferencing sequences; on all other datasets HyTIP matches or exceeds DCVC-DC. HyTIP clearly outperforms DCVC-FM (−18.5%), DCVC-HEM (−5.8%), DCVC-TCM (+33.9%), and MaskCRT (+4.1%) in PSNR-RGB, and surpasses all of them in MS-SSIM-RGB (−56.7% vs −55.2% for DCVC-DC). Compared to the traditional VTM 17.0 anchor, HyTIP saves 22.1% bits at equal PSNR-RGB and 56.7% at equal MS-SSIM-RGB. The critical differentiator is buffer size: HyTIP needs only 7.875 full-resolution feature maps versus 55.75 for DCVC-DC/DCVC-FM and 67 for DCVC-TCM, a 7–8× reduction, while its decoding kMACs/pixel (873) is comparable to DCVC-FM (871) and lower than DCVC-DC (918). The ablation in Table 2 confirms that the hybrid strategy (explicit decoded frame + 2 latent channels) outperforms both explicit-only (−14.7%) and implicit-only (−15.0%) at matched buffer sizes, reaching −21.5% average BD-rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lineages in learned video compression—output-recurrence (explicit buffering of decoded frames, as in MaskCRT, DVC, and traditional codecs) and hidden-to-hidden connections (implicit buffering of latent features, as in DCVC-DC, DCVC-FM, DCVC-TCM)—under a single RNN perspective, then proposes a hybrid buffering strategy that combines both. It builds directly on the masked conditional residual coding framework of MaskCRT [9] and the CNN-based inter-frame codec architecture of DCVC-FM [22], extending them with a small number of implicit latent features alongside the explicitly buffered decoded frame. The conceptual departure is framing the dual-constraint problem of output-recurrence and the non-compactness problem of implicit buffering as complementary limitations that a hybrid RNN connection can jointly address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the 14% buffer size relative to SOTA implicit-buffering methods while maintaining comparable coding performance, which directly reduces off-chip memory bandwidth demands and improves deployability on hardware with constrained memory. The RNN-based taxonomy provides a useful lens for future work to reason about temporal propagation design choices. Open questions include whether the hybrid strategy generalizes to bidirectional prediction structures, YUV color spaces, and whether the small implicit buffer can be further reduced or made adaptive per content. The paper acknowledges it is validated in one specific coding framework (masked conditional residual) and one prediction structure (IPPP), leaving broader applicability to be confirmed.
