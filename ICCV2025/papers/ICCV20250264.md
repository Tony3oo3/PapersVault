## Problem

Existing medical VQA datasets lack both visual and textual explanations for their answers, hindering comprehension for patients and junior doctors, and they offer a narrow range of question formats (typically only open- and closed-ended), failing to reflect the diverse inquiry types encountered in clinical practice. These two gaps limit the practical deployability of Med-VQA systems in real healthcare settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250264/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a chest X-ray image and a text question in one of four formats (open-ended, closed-ended, single-choice, or multi-choice) and produces a structured output comprising a text answer, a textual reasoning explanation, and a visual bounding box annotation indicating the relevant anatomical region. The visual grounding is generated as an anatomical region label (e.g., 'left lower lung zone') that is post-processed into bounding box coordinates, and multi-choice questions may involve multiple bounding boxes matched via the Hungarian algorithm.

## Method

GEMeX is constructed via a two-stage pipeline: Stage I re-grounds reports from Chest ImaGenome using OpenBioLLM-70B with iteratively refined prompts (guided by radiologist feedback) to ensure each sentence maps to exactly one of 30 defined anatomical regions, resolving the original dataset's one-to-many region ambiguity. Stage II leverages GPT-4o with carefully crafted demonstrations covering 7 question content categories to generate 11 questions per image (3 open-ended, 2 closed-ended, 3 single-choice, 3 multi-choice), each enriched with an answer, reasoning text, and visual location. For evaluation, the authors fine-tune LLaVA-Med-v1-7B using question-type-aware instruction tuning—appending a type-specific prompt that instructs the model to output the answer, a detailed reason, and the corresponding visual location—producing LLaVA-Med-GEMeX as a strong baseline. Twelve LVLMs are benchmarked using three metrics: GPTScore-based AR-score for semantic answer-reason quality, exact-match A-score for choice/yes-no accuracy, and mIoU V-score for visual grounding precision.

## Results

On GEMeX, the proposed LLaVA-Med-GEMeX (fine-tuned LLaVA-Med-v1-7B) achieves the best average AR-score of 86.04%, surpassing the strongest zero-shot model GPT-4o-mini (82.30%) by ~3.7 points. Among zero-shot medical models, LLaVA-Med-v1.5 leads with 76.07% average AR-score, while general-domain models like LLaVA-v1.5 (64.21%) and DeepSeek-VL (67.89%) trail significantly. Visual grounding (V-score/mIoU) remains the weakest dimension across all models: LLaVA-Med-GEMeX reaches 51.47–54.57% mIoU, while GPT-4o-mini manages only 18.05–28.64%. On the transfer test (SLAKE-CXR), LLaVA-Med-GEMeX improves over LLaVA-Med-v1 by ~9.5 points in open-ended AR-score (82.78 vs 73.31) and ~12.7 points in closed-ended AR-score (75.06 vs 62.35).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLaVA-Med-GEMeX outperforms all 12 zero-shot LVLMs on average AR-score (86.04% vs. best zero-shot GPT-4o-mini at 82.30%), with the largest gains on closed-ended (+9.6 pts over GPT-4o-mini) and single-choice (+3.9 pts) questions. Among zero-shot medical models, LLaVA-Med-v1.5 (76.07%) and LLaVA-Med-v1 (72.53%) lead, while XrayGPT (63.19%) and MiniGPT-Med (67.83%) lag; general-domain models (LLaVA-v1.5 at 64.21%, DeepSeek-VL at 67.89%) are consistently weaker than their medical counterparts. GPT-4o-mini is the only zero-shot model to report V-scores across all four question types, yet its mIoU (18–29%) is far below LLaVA-Med-GEMeX (48–55%), confirming that powerful general LVLMs rely on shortcut reasoning rather than true visual grounding. Most models (especially medical ones) fail to produce a definitive A-score on choice-based questions, indicating they can analyze options but cannot commit to an answer. On the SLAKE-CXR transfer test, LLaVA-Med-GEMeX beats LLaVA-Med-v1 by 9.5 pts (open-ended) and 12.7 pts (closed-ended) in AR-score, demonstrating that GEMeX training transfers to external chest X-ray VQA benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the medical VQA task by building on Chest ImaGenome for base X-ray images and reports, and on LLaVA-Med as the fine-tuning backbone. It departs from all prior Med-VQA datasets (VQA-RAD, SLAKE, MIMIC-CXR-VQA, OmniMedVQA, etc.) by introducing two dimensions absent from existing work: multimodal explainability (simultaneous visual bounding-box grounding and textual reasoning per answer) and four question types including single- and multi-choice formats. The re-grounding pipeline using OpenBioLLM-70B with radiologist-iterated prompts represents a methodological contribution to the data-construction lineage, while the GPT-4o-based generation with quality control follows the broader trend of LLM-augmented dataset creation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the largest chest X-ray VQA dataset (151K images, 1.6M questions) and the first to require both visual and textual explanations, GEMeX establishes a new evaluation standard that exposes the gap between current LVLMs and clinically useful explainable systems. The finding that even GPT-4o-mini achieves only ~18–28% mIoU on visual grounding, while a 7B fine-tuned model surpasses it on most metrics, suggests that domain-specific instruction tuning with grounded data is a practical path forward. Key open questions include whether the task-specific fine-tuning generalizes to multi-task clinical settings, whether the GPT-4o-generated explanations faithfully reflect radiological reasoning, and how to scale the explainability requirement to other imaging modalities beyond chest X-ray.
