## Problem

Linear Blend Skinning (LBS), the dominant method for character animation, produces volume loss, candy-wrapper artifacts, and cannot model elastic materials such as soft tissues, fur, or flexible appendages. Existing physics-based skinning alternatives better capture volumetric and material behavior but are non-differentiable, preventing integration with gradient-based learning pipelines for inverse skinning and optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252607/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a 3D object (mesh, point cloud, or Gaussian representation) that is first converted to a volumetric point cloud of N points, along with a sequence of SE(3) skeleton transformations for M virtual joints and coarse skinning weights (from Pinocchio or RigNet) used to initialize driving point positions. It outputs per-frame deformed point positions, learned material parameters (Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio for P material prototypes), and per-joint driving point velocities, which together produce physically plausible multi-view animation sequences.

## Method

PhysRig embeds the rigid skeleton into a deformable soft-body volume simulated via the Material Point Method (MPM), where particles are transferred to an Eulerian grid using B-spline interpolation (P2G/G2P) with a Fixed Corotated hyperelastic constitutive model for stress computation. To reduce the high-dimensional material parameter space, the method introduces material prototypes—Gaussian ellipsoids parameterized by center, orientation, and diagonal scale—whose weighted contributions (via Mahalanobis distance) assign per-point Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio. Driving points are placed at joint locations identified through spectral clustering of coarse skinning weights and variance-based boundary detection, encoding skeletal motion as velocities that propagate deformation through the volume. The inverse skinning problem is solved by an iterative alternating optimization: material parameters are updated using all frames as a single batch, while driving point velocities are optimized sequentially per frame, ensuring temporal consistency and stable convergence.

## Results

On the inverse skinning task across 17 diverse objects (humanoids, quadrupeds, and other entities) with 120 motion sequences, PhysRig achieves the highest User Study Rate (UR) and lowest Chamfer Distance (CD) across all categories. Headline numbers include UR 4.7 / CD 0.139 on Michelle, UR 4.8 / CD 0.228 on Kaya, UR 4.45 / CD 0.212 on Leopard, and UR 4.32 / CD 0.021 on Angelfish. The best LBS baseline (LBS-3, ground-truth skinning weights) reaches at most UR 3.83 and CD 0.031, while PhysRig improves UR by roughly 1–2 points and reduces CD by up to an order of magnitude on most objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhysRig is compared against three LBS-based baselines: LBS-1 (RigNet [41] for skinning-weight initialization), LBS-2 (Pinocchio [3] for initialization), and LBS-3 (ground-truth skinning weights), all of which jointly optimize skinning weights and bone transformations. PhysRig wins on both UR and CD across all 17 evaluated objects and all three categories (humanoid, quadruped, other entities). Against the strongest baseline LBS-3, PhysRig improves UR by approximately 1.0–1.4 points (e.g., 4.70 vs. 3.32 on Michelle, 4.80 vs. 3.43 on Kaya) and reduces CD by 2–4× on most objects (e.g., 0.139 vs. 0.372 on Michelle, 0.021 vs. 0.209 on Angelfish). The gap is even larger against LBS-1 and LBS-2, which suffer from suboptimal initialization. Notably, even before optimization (Ours-init), PhysRig already matches or exceeds LBS-3 on UR for several objects, indicating that the physics-based formulation provides a stronger inductive bias than LBS from the start.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhysRig builds directly on the Material Point Method (MPM) for continuum mechanics simulation, adapting its P2G/G2P transfer and deformation gradient updates into a fully differentiable pipeline for articulated object skinning. It departs from the dominant Linear Blend Skinning paradigm (RigNet, Pinocchio, SMPL) by replacing linear vertex blending with volumetric soft-body simulation driven by embedded driving points, and from prior differentiable physics works (DreamPhysics, Physics3D, PhysGaussian) by targeting the specific inverse skinning problem rather than general material property estimation. The material prototype formulation—a small set of Gaussian-ellipsoid parameterized prototypes whose Mahalanobis-distance-weighted combination yields per-point Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio—is a novel compact representation not seen in prior skinning or MPM-learning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PhysRig provides the first differentiable physics-based alternative to LBS that can be jointly optimized with deep learning pipelines, opening the door to physically consistent 4D generation, motion retargeting, and avatar modeling without hand-tuned skinning weights. Key limitations include evaluation exclusively on synthetic data (no real-world video or scan validation), the computational cost of iterative MPM simulation that precludes real-time use, and the reliance on coarse skinning weights from external tools (Pinocchio) for initialization. Open questions include whether the framework scales to complex multi-body interactions, how it performs on real captured motion with noise, and whether the material prototype count can be learned automatically rather than set heuristically.
