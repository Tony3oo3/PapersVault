## Problem

Existing diffusion-based video editing models predominantly handle attribute-level changes (style, appearance, background) while largely overlooking motion manipulation. The only prior motion editing model, MotionEditor, relies on a heavy ControlNet and attention injection that cause high computational cost, temporal inconsistency, and degradation under large camera movements or complex backgrounds. MotionFollower addresses this gap by introducing a score-guided diffusion framework that edits motion while faithfully preserving the source's appearance, background, and camera dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video (providing protagonist appearance and background) and a target video (from which a pose sequence is extracted via DWPose) as input. It produces a motion-edited video in which the source protagonist performs the target motion while retaining the original background, clothing details, and camera movement. The model processes 24-frame clips in ~50 seconds on a single A100 and can handle up to 56 frames per pass, compared to 16 for MotionEditor, at roughly 80% lower GPU memory.

## Method

MotionFollower builds on a 3D U-Net (SD1.5 + AnimateDiff temporal layers) augmented with two lightweight convolution-only controllers: a Pose Controller (PoCtr) that encodes target poses into latent-space additions, and a Reference Controller (ReCtr) that injects multi-scale source appearance features into the U-Net encoder, both avoiding the costly attention operations of ControlNet. The core novelty is a score-guided inference principle using a two-branch architecture (reconstruction and editing branches sharing the same controllers), where a composite score function decomposes into a foreground term S_fg (enforcing protagonist appearance consistency) and a background term S_bg (with sub-terms for overlapping background, body misalignment, and complementary inpainting). These terms are computed from intermediate decoder features of both branches, masked by predicted foreground regions, and their gradients are used to update the editing-branch latents during the first 30 of 50 denoising steps without modifying any model weights. Training proceeds in two stages—single-frame image editing followed by video editing with a 40/60 hybrid sampling scheme—to balance per-frame fidelity and temporal coherence.

## Results

On 100 in-the-wild video cases, MotionFollower achieves the best scores across all metrics: PSNR 20.85, SSIM 0.75, LPIPS 0.22, FID 26.30, FID-VID 12.42, and FVD 276.04. Compared to the strongest baseline MotionEditor, MotionFollower improves FID-VID by 8.15 (12.42 vs. 20.57) and FVD by 119.39 (276.04 vs. 395.43) while reducing GPU memory from 42.6G to 9.8G (~80% reduction). In a consistency user study, MotionFollower achieves the lowest optical flow L2 distance (3.21 vs. 5.87 for MotionEditor) and wins user preference in all three categories (Motion, Temporal, Appearance Consistency).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MotionFollower outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric. The closest competitor, MotionEditor (the prior state-of-the-art for video motion editing), is surpassed by 3.51 PSNR, 0.07 SSIM, 0.12 LPIPS, 5.68 FID, 8.15 FID-VID, and 119.39 FVD, while using roughly 77% less GPU memory (9.8G vs. 42.6G). Among human motion transfer models adapted for video editing, MagicAnimate and AnimateAnyone are the strongest (FID-VID 26.59 and 28.31 respectively), but both trail MotionFollower by over 14 FID-VID points and struggle with body distortion and ghosting artifacts. Tune-A-Video and Disco, which were not originally designed for motion editing, show substantially weaker results (FID-VID 84.58 and 83.69), with Tune-A-Video exhibiting severe overlapping and appearance loss. In the consistency user study (Table 3), MotionFollower achieves the lowest optical flow L2 (3.21) and wins user preference over all competitors in Motion, Temporal, and Appearance Consistency categories, confirming its advantage in preserving source details during motion transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

MotionFollower builds on the latent diffusion model (LDM/Stable Diffusion) backbone and AnimateDiff's temporal motion modules, but departs sharply from the most direct predecessor, MotionEditor, by replacing its heavy ControlNet and attention-injection mechanism with two lightweight pure-convolution controllers (Pose Controller and Reference Controller). The key conceptual departure is the introduction of score regularization as an inference-time consistency mechanism: rather than relying on cross-attention or key/value interaction with source features (as in MagicAnimate, AnimateAnyone, or MotionEditor), the paper formulates a two-branch (reconstruction + editing) architecture where regional consistency is enforced through gradients of mask-weighted similarity functions added to the denoising score. This positions the work at the intersection of score-based generative modeling and video editing, a combination not previously explored in the video editing literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: existing motion editing methods degrade on videos with large camera movements or complex backgrounds, which are common in real-world content. The 80% GPU memory reduction (9.8G vs. 42.6G for MotionEditor) while simultaneously improving all quality metrics makes the task accessible on a single A100, broadening its practical applicability. Open questions include whether the score regularization principle generalizes beyond human-centric motion editing to other editing tasks (e.g., object motion, scene-level dynamics), whether it can be extended to longer videos beyond the 24-frame clips tested, and whether the reliance on pose extraction (DWPose) and a lightweight segmentation model limits robustness to non-human subjects or occluded scenes.
