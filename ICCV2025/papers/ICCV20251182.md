## Problem

Self-supervised blind denoising for Poisson-Gaussian noise using the re-visible framework (Blind2Unblind) achieves lossless signal retention but relies on greedy pixel-level MSE fitting that cannot adapt to dynamic noise levels, resulting in noticeable residual noise artifacts. The core gap is the absence of explicit noise-level sensing within the loss function, preventing personalized denoising intensity adjustment across varying Poisson-Gaussian noise conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251182/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single noisy image corrupted by Poisson-Gaussian noise (y = x + N(0, αx + σ²)) in either grayscale or sRGB format. During training, the input is split into a masked volume Ω_y (blindspot) and the full raw noisy image y, with 128×128 patches randomly cropped. During inference, the auxiliary noise estimator and masked branch are discarded, and the denoiser directly produces a clean image from the raw noisy input alone, requiring no noise parameter specification at test time.

## Method

Blind2Sound introduces an adaptive re-visible loss that models the masked and visible branches as independent Gaussian processes and constructs a Gaussian mixture marginal likelihood whose negative log-likelihood serves as the training objective. The noise parameters (α, σ) are embedded in the covariance term diag(αμ_y) + σI, making the loss inherently noise-aware: the masked branch acts as a gradient-update medium while the visible branch provides a constant reference, and a growing visible factor λ progressively shifts the optimal solution toward the visible estimate. A Cramer Gaussian loss regularizes the joint noise estimator by imposing fine-grained sub-block constraints (single-channel) or cross-channel variance-matching constraints (multi-channel) on GAT-transformed images, reducing the solution space for accurate noise parameter recovery. Theoretical analysis shows that disabling the gradient through diag(αμ_y) is necessary to avoid training instability from a complex second-order term, and the IID assumption between branches prevents masked-branch suppression of visible denoising.

## Results

On the SIDD Benchmark (raw-RGB), Blind2Sound achieves 50.92 dB PSNR / 0.991 SSIM, outperforming the closest self-supervised method Blind2Unblind (50.79 dB) by 0.13 dB and the supervised N2C baseline (50.61 dB) by 0.31 dB. On SIDD sRGB Benchmark, it reaches 38.21 dB / 0.945 SSIM, surpassing all self-supervised and supervised baselines. On the FMD fluorescence microscopy dataset, Blind2Sound attains 38.46 dB / 0.965 SSIM (Confocal Mice) and 34.11 dB / 0.918 SSIM (Two-Photon Mice), leading all self-supervised methods. On synthetic grayscale denoising (BSD68, α=0.01, σ=0.0002), it achieves 30.83 dB, marginally exceeding the supervised N2C baseline (30.82 dB) and outperforming Blind2Unblind (30.61 dB) by 0.22 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Blind2Sound outperforms all self-supervised baselines across every real-world benchmark. On SIDD RAW Benchmark, it leads Blind2Unblind by 0.13 dB, FBI-D by 0.35 dB, NBR2NBR by 0.45 dB, and N2V by 2.91 dB. It also surpasses both supervised baselines (N2C and N2N) on SIDD RAW by ~0.3 dB and on SIDD sRGB by ~0.13 dB, demonstrating that noise-sensed personalized denoising generalizes better than paired-data supervision under dynamic real-world noise. On FMD, the advantage over Blind2Unblind narrows to 0.02–0.08 dB, and the method slightly trails N2C on Two-Photon Mice SSIM (0.918 vs. 0.925) while winning on PSNR (34.11 vs. 34.02). On synthetic grayscale denoising, Blind2Sound beats N2C and N2N on Set12 and Urban100 by up to 0.4 dB but is essentially tied on BSD68. On synthetic sRGB denoising, it outperforms all self-supervised methods (e.g., +0.35 dB over Blind2Unblind on Kodak at low noise) but does not surpass the supervised N2C/N2N baselines, which retain a ~0.4 dB edge, reflecting the added difficulty of cross-channel noise removal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the re-visible denoising framework of Blind2Unblind (CVPR 2022), which achieves lossless self-supervised denoising but relies on greedy pixel-level MSE that cannot adapt to varying noise levels, leaving residual noise. It also builds on FBI-D's (CVPR 2021) GAT-based Gaussian loss for noise estimation, augmenting it with fine-grained sub-block and cross-channel constraints (Cramer Gaussian loss) to improve parameter accuracy. The key conceptual departure is formulating the re-visible objective as a Bayesian marginal likelihood with explicit Poisson-Gaussian noise parameters, replacing MSE with an adaptive loss that personalizes denoising intensity to the sensed noise level while preserving the lossless property.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical limitation of self-supervised denoising—residual noise under dynamic Poisson-Gaussian conditions—by coupling noise sensing directly into the denoising objective, which is a clean and interpretable design choice. However, the performance gains over the closest baseline (Blind2Unblind) are modest (~0.1 dB on SIDD), and the method does not surpass supervised baselines on sRGB images, suggesting the approach's advantage is most pronounced for single-channel or high-noise scenarios. Open questions include whether the Bayesian re-visible formulation generalizes to other noise models (e.g., mixed or spatially-varying noise) and whether the joint noise-estimation-and-denoising training can be decoupled for greater flexibility.
