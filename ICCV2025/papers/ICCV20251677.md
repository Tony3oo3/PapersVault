## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting training is bottlenecked by the exponentially growing number of Gaussian primitives, and the standard cyclic pruning strategy faces a fundamental trade-off: conservative opacity thresholds yield minimal speedup while aggressive ones degrade rendering quality. The paper asks whether training can be accelerated without sacrificing—and ideally while improving—reconstruction fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251677/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the same multi-view RGB images and camera poses as standard 3DGS, with no changes to input format or resolution. The output is a set of 3D Gaussian primitives (position, scale, rotation, opacity, SH color coefficients) that are rendered via α-blending into novel views; notably, the resulting model is 10–40% smaller than the baseline 3DGS model while achieving equal or better PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS, and Group Training is implemented as a drop-in plug-in requiring no architectural modification to the underlying splatting framework.

## Method

Group Training periodically partitions all Gaussians into an Under-training Group (active in rendering and gradient computation) and a Cached Group (temporarily excluded), with groups merged and resampled every 500 iterations. The key sampling strategy is Opacity-based Prioritized Sampling (OPS), which assigns each Gaussian a selection probability proportional to its opacity, ensuring high-opacity primitives remain in the active set. The paper proves two properties: (1) high-opacity Gaussians dominate the gradient signal that triggers densification, so caching low-opacity ones reduces redundant splitting; and (2) high-opacity Gaussians reach α-saturation faster during α-blending, reducing the number of primitives that must be composited per pixel. Group Training is applied during both the densification phase (0–15K iterations) and the optimization phase (15K–30K), with a final global densification and optimization step at the end of each phase to ensure all cached Gaussians are reintegrated before the model is finalized.

## Results

Group Training with Opacity-based Prioritized Sampling (OPS) applied over 0–30K iterations achieves up to ~30% faster reconstruction on complex scenes (e.g., Mip-NeRF360: 26.7→19.6 min; Deep Blending: 23.9→16.9 min) while simultaneously improving PSNR (e.g., 27.445→27.564 on Mip-NeRF360, 23.697→23.853 on Tanks & Temples, 29.586→29.746 on Deep Blending) and SSIM across all four benchmarks. Model size is reduced by 10–40% (e.g., 792→679 MB on Mip-NeRF360, 666→487 MB on Deep Blending) with lower GPU peak memory. The same gains transfer to Mip-Splatting, where OPS 0–30K reduces training time from 23.0→18.2 min (Tanks & Temples) and 35.1→24.0 min (Deep Blending) while matching or exceeding baseline PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the vanilla 3DGS baseline [Kerbl et al., 2023], Group Training with OPS (0–30K) wins on both speed and quality: it reduces training time by 26–30% on complex scenes (Mip-NeRF360, Tanks & Temples, Deep Blending) while improving PSNR by 0.06–0.16 dB and SSIM by 0.001–0.003 across all four datasets. Model size shrinks by 10–40% (e.g., 792→679 MB on Mip-NeRF360, 666→487 MB on Deep Blending), and GPU peak memory drops correspondingly. Against Mip-Splatting [Yu et al., 2024], the same OPS strategy reduces training time by 21–32% (e.g., 35.1→24.0 min on Deep Blending, 23.0→18.2 min on Tanks & Temples) while matching or slightly exceeding baseline PSNR (e.g., 29.358→29.788 on Deep Blending, 23.749→24.156 on Tanks & Temples). The Random Sampling variant (RS) also accelerates training but produces larger, more redundant models (e.g., 907 MB vs. 679 MB on Mip-NeRF360), making OPS the clearly superior sampling strategy. Notably, the speedup from Group Training is attributed to reduced per-iteration rendering cost rather than smaller final models, as even the RS variant (which yields larger models) still trains faster than the baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) and Mip-Splatting (Yu et al., 2024), positioning Group Training as a plug-in strategy that modifies the training pipeline rather than the rendering or representation. It departs from prior pruning-based efficiency methods (which permanently remove low-opacity Gaussians) by introducing a caching mechanism that temporarily excludes primitives from computation while retaining them for later resampling. The Opacity-based Prioritized Sampling strategy is grounded in two formal propositions linking opacity to densification gradients and α-saturation speed, distinguishing it from random or importance-score-based sampling approaches explored in prior 3DGS compression and acceleration work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in a simple, universally compatible training strategy that simultaneously reduces training time by ~30% and improves rendering quality across multiple 3DGS variants and datasets, without requiring architectural changes. The counterintuitive finding that model size does not determine training speed (RS produces larger models yet still accelerates training) challenges the common assumption that fewer primitives always mean faster convergence. Open questions include whether adaptive grouping intervals or dynamic sampling ratios could further improve results, and whether the approach extends to dynamic or large-scale scenes where the Gaussian count is even more extreme.
