## Problem

LVLMs produce more hallucinations in longer free-form responses, a phenomenon typically attributed to accumulated autoregressive errors. This paper challenges that explanation, asking whether the increased hallucination rate is caused by length itself or by a deeper mechanism. The authors identify context—specifically the pressure for coherence (avoiding repetition) and completeness (covering comprehensive content)—as the underlying driver that shifts hallucination positions earlier when context is enriched or reduced.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text prompt, first generating a standard LVLM response (up to 512 tokens). It then consumes that response as context: the initial caption is extended with a 'There is also' cue to induce a reference object, and separate imagination prompts are issued to elicit external-expansion hallucinations. The final output is a hallucination-suppressed text response produced via modified contrastive decoding, with intermediate detection scores (IGScore, EEScore) available per generated object for downstream use.

## Method

HalTrapper is a training-free 'induce-detect-suppress' pipeline built on two hypotheses about context. For induction, Internal Grounding appends 'There is also' to the initial response to force the model to generate an additional object (likely hallucinated due to completeness pressure), while External Expansion prompts the model to imagine objects outside the frame using a reason-then-imagine strategy. For detection, IGScore computes cosine similarity between the induced reference object's image attention map and each preceding object's attention map (hallucinated objects share dispersed, similar attention patterns), and EEScore counts how often an object appears in imagination sets minus reason sets across multiple prompts (hallucinations recur consistently for the same image). For suppression, Contrastive Contextual Decoding (CCD) encodes detected hallucinated objects as Contrastive Contextual Tokens, concatenates them with the image to form a contrastive branch, and subtracts the contrastive branch logits from the original branch logits—thereby reducing the probability of hallucinated objects in the final generation without any parameter updates.

## Results

On CHAIR (COCO) with LLaVA v1.5 7B under Beam Search, HalTrapper reduces CHAIR_S from 55.6 to 45.2 (−10.4) and CHAIR_I from 15.8 to 12.1 (−3.7) while improving F1 from 76.7 to 80.0, outperforming OPERA (C_S=53.6) and all contrastive decoding baselines. On AMBER, HalTrapper achieves the best CHAIR across all three models tested (LLaVA v1.5: 8.0, Qwen2 VL: 5.6, Janus Pro 7B: 5.4), reducing the Hal metric by 11.6, 4.2, and 4.8 points respectively. For hallucination detection, the IG Score achieves AUROC of 82.3 on LLaVA (vs. 70.7 for PoScore) and TPR@5%FPR of 43.3 (vs. 4.3 for PoScore), demonstrating substantially stronger discriminative power.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HalTrapper consistently outperforms all four contrastive decoding baselines (VCD, ICD, CODE, OPERA) across every decoding strategy and model tested. On LLaVA v1.5 (Beam Search), it reduces CHAIR_S by 10.4 points over vanilla and 8.4 over OPERA, while also improving F1 by 5.1 points over OPERA. On MiniGPT-4 (Greedy), HalTrapper achieves C_S=28.6 vs. CODE's 32.8 and vanilla's 39.6, and on Qwen VL (Beam Search) it reaches C_S=34.2 vs. vanilla's 41.4. On AMBER, HalTrapper is the only method that improves CHAIR on all three models simultaneously (LLaVA v1.5, Qwen2 VL, Janus Pro 7B), whereas VCD and ICD actually worsen CHAIR on Qwen2 VL (7.3 and 8.2 vs. baseline 6.6). For detection, IG Score surpasses PoScore by 5–12% AUROC and by at least 10 points in TPR@5%FPR across all three models, confirming that attention-similarity-based detection is far more effective than position- or uncertainty-based heuristics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contrastive decoding (CD) lineage for LVLM hallucination mitigation (VCD, ICD, CODE) by introducing Contrastive Contextual Decoding (CCD), which uses detected hallucinated objects as contrastive context tokens rather than relying on a separate amateur model or modified visual input. It departs from the dominant explanation in prior work (Zhou et al. [97]) that longer responses accumulate more hallucinations due to autoregressive uncertainty, instead arguing that the underlying driver is contextual coherence and completeness. The detection component (Internal Grounding via attention similarity, External Expansion via cross-prompt consistency) is novel and builds on the observation that hallucinated tokens share diffuse, similar attention patterns, a property not previously exploited for detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reframing of length-correlated hallucinations as context-driven (coherence + completeness) offers a more actionable mechanistic explanation that could guide future training and decoding strategies beyond the current contrastive-decoding paradigm. The training-free, zero-shot HalTrapper framework is practical and achieves consistent improvements across five LVLMs and two benchmarks, but its scope is limited to object-level hallucinations in captioning-style generation. Open questions include whether the context hypothesis generalizes to attribute or spatial hallucinations, whether the extra forward passes for induction scale efficiently, and whether the findings hold for non-autoregressive or iterative decoding settings.
