## Problem

Existing autonomous driving benchmarks emphasize spatial perception and basic planning (lane keeping, collision avoidance) but rarely assess whether models can reason over the full spectrum of traffic regulations, right-of-way rules, and rare edge-case scenarios that human drivers must master before licensing. This paper asks whether current LLMs and MLLMs can pass a comprehensive driving knowledge test covering all traffic rules, signage, and complex intersection decision-making.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250448/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DriveQA-T presents text-only multiple-choice questions (4 options each) drawn from 51 US state driver's handbooks, while DriveQA-V pairs each question with a synthetic or real-world image of a traffic sign or intersection scene, with controlled metadata (camera perspective, weather, time of day, distance). Each QA pair includes a ground-truth explanation for the correct answer, enabling evaluation of reasoning quality via BLEU-4 and ROUGE-L in addition to accuracy. For downstream transfer, the fine-tuned model additionally outputs trajectory waypoints (evaluated by L2 error at 1s/2s/3s horizons) and driving action labels with explanations (evaluated by F1).

## Method

The core contribution is the DriveQA benchmark (26K text + 448K visual QA pairs, 19 categories, 220 sign types, 474K samples) built via three pipelines: GPT-4o-generated questions from state handbooks with manual verification, procedural CARLA simulation augmented with 220 new 3D US traffic sign assets and automatic right-of-way spawning scripts, and manually annotated Mapillary real-world images. Evaluation employs four prompt structures (basic, CoT, CoT+RAG, RAG-only) where RAG retrieves relevant handbook passages per question, and question categorization uses BERT embeddings with hierarchical clustering and KeyBERT keyword extraction. Models are fine-tuned via LoRA on DriveQA, and the paper demonstrates that this pretraining transfers to downstream real-world tasks (nuScenes trajectory prediction via OpenEMMA, BDD-OIA action/explanation classification), showing that text and synthetic traffic knowledge internalized during fine-tuning generalizes to unseen driving scenarios.

## Results

On DriveQA-V (multimodal), GPT-4o achieves the highest off-the-shelf average accuracy of 75.29%, with ~94% on traffic sign categories but only 50–60% on intersection right-of-way questions. After LoRA fine-tuning, Mini-InternVL (2B) reaches 86.59% average, surpassing GPT-4o's zero-shot score, while LLaVA-1.6-mistral (7B) reaches 83.66%. On DriveQA-T (text-only), GPT-4o scores 91.96% average accuracy, and fine-tuned Llama-3.1 (8B) reaches 87.62%. On downstream tasks, DQA pretraining reduces nuScenes trajectory L2 error from 2.98 m to 2.91 m (LLaVA-1.6-mistral) and improves BDD-OIA action mF1 from 0.2951 to 0.5285 (InternVL-2.5-8B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o dominates all off-the-shelf comparisons, scoring 91.96% on DriveQA-T and 75.29% on DriveQA-V, far ahead of the best open-source MLLM (Mini-InternVL at 41.82% on DriveQA-V). However, after LoRA fine-tuning on DriveQA, Mini-InternVL (2B) surpasses GPT-4o's zero-shot average (86.59% vs. 75.29%), and LLaVA-1.6-mistral (7B) reaches 83.66%, indicating that the benchmark's knowledge is learnable from synthetic data. LLaVA-1.5 and VILA-1.5 remain the weakest even after fine-tuning, particularly on first-person intersection views (47–64% vs. 86% for Mini-InternVL), suggesting architectural limitations in spatial reasoning. On sim-to-real transfer (Mapillary), fine-tuned VILA-1.5 jumps from 34.38% to 60.86%, a +26.5 pp gain, while GPT-4o's off-the-shelf 84.73% still leads. On downstream nuScenes trajectory planning, DQA pretraining reduces average L2 error by 0.07 m for LLaVA-1.6-mistral (2.98→2.91 m) and 0.35 m for InternVL-2.5-8B (3.06→2.71 m), and on BDD-OIA it lifts action mF1 from 0.2951 to 0.5285, confirming cross-domain transferability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing a driving knowledge benchmark that explicitly targets traffic rule compliance, right-of-way reasoning, and regulatory sign recognition—dimensions that existing driving VQA benchmarks (NuScenes-QA, DriveLM, DriveBench, LingoQA) largely neglect in favor of spatial perception and basic planning. It repurposes the CARLA simulator by integrating 220 US traffic sign 3D assets and procedural rule scripts, and augments Mapillary with VQA annotations, combining synthetic controllability with real-world diversity. Methodologically, it applies standard evaluation techniques (LoRA fine-tuning, CoT prompting, RAG from driver's handbooks, hierarchical clustering for question taxonomy) to this novel evaluation domain rather than proposing new model architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DriveQA fills a critical evaluation gap: current autonomous driving benchmarks test whether models can perceive and plan, but not whether they understand and comply with the full spectrum of traffic regulations that human drivers must master before licensing. The finding that most open-source MLLMs perform at or below random chance on intersection right-of-way questions, and that even GPT-4o plateaus around 60%, challenges the assumption that pretraining has endowed models with sufficient traffic rule knowledge. Open questions include how to make models internalize explicit textual regulations (as humans do), how to extend evaluation to dynamic/video scenarios, and whether the synthetic-to-real transfer demonstrated on nuScenes and BDD-OIA generalizes to geographically diverse driving environments with different road layouts and regulations.
