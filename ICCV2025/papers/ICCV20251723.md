## Problem

Identifying animal species from indirect biological evidence such as footprints, feces, eggs, bones, and feathers is critical for wildlife monitoring but remains largely unexplored in computer vision. Existing datasets for animal traces are small-scale, cover only a single trace type, or include very few species, and none support the full suite of classification, detection, and instance segmentation tasks needed for practical deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251723/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input consists of RGB images of animal traces sourced from iNaturalist (research-grade, Creative Commons licensed), with 159,605 bounding boxes spanning 968 species across five trace categories. For classification, traces are cropped to their bounding boxes to minimize background; for detection and segmentation, full scene images are used. Outputs include species/family/order labels, bounding boxes, pixel-level segmentation masks (applied to feces, eggs, bones, and feathers but not footprints, which are ambiguous traces), and 22 ecological/behavioral trait labels per species.

## Method

AnimalClue is a dataset and benchmark contribution rather than a novel architecture. The annotation pipeline combines manual labeling (footprints, feces, bones), third-party annotation services (half of footprints), and SAM-assisted initialization with human correction (eggs, feathers), with all labels verified by multiple citizen scientists. Four benchmarks are established: image classification (VGG-16, ResNet-50, EFNet-B1, ViT-B, Swin-B), object detection (YOLOv8, YOLOv11, Faster-RCNN, DINO, RT-DETR), instance segmentation (YOLOv8, YOLOv11, Mask-RCNN, MaskDINO), and trait classification (Swin-B). A feature-space analysis using CLIP, BioCLIP, and a contrastively fine-tuned CLIP shows that domain-specific trace data is necessary even for large pretrained models. Key findings: Swin-B leads classification (up to 65.3% on feathers), RT-DETR leads detection (0.57 mAP at order level), and rare-species performance drops dramatically (e.g., 1.0% on rare bone species), highlighting the long-tail challenge.

## Results

On the AnimalClue classification benchmark, Swin-B achieves the highest top-1 accuracy across all trace types and taxonomy levels, reaching 65.3% (species-level feathers), 49.4% (species-level eggs), and 84.0% (order-level eggs). For object detection, RT-DETR attains the best mAP@50-95, with a peak of 0.57 on order-level feathers and 0.43 on species-level feathers. Instance segmentation peaks at 0.48 mAP (order-level feathers) with MaskDINO. Rare-category performance drops dramatically: species-level bone classification falls from 20.5% (all) to 4.91% (rare) for Swin-B, and rare-category detection mAP is near zero for most trace types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For classification, Swin-B consistently outperforms all other baselines (VGG-16, ResNet-50, EFNet-B1, ViT-B) across nearly all trace types and taxonomy levels, with the largest margins on species-level feces (38.6% vs. 32.2% for ViT-B) and species-level bones (20.5% vs. 18.3% for ResNet-50). For object detection, RT-DETR dominates, achieving 0.57 mAP on order-level feathers versus 0.43 for YOLOv8/YOLOv11 and 0.22 for Faster-RCNN; however, RT-DETR collapses on rare categories (e.g., 0.00 mAP on rare species-level eggs) while YOLOv11 retains slightly higher rare-category scores. In instance segmentation, MaskDINO leads with 0.48 mAP on order-level feathers, outperforming YOLOv11 (0.41) and Mask-RCNN (0.24), though all models struggle with rare species (mAP < 0.06). In the feature-space analysis, fine-tuned CLIP achieves the best t-SNE separation, outperforming both standard CLIP (ViT-B-32) and the domain-specific BioCLIP, confirming that trace-specific training is necessary even for large pretrained models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying five previously fragmented trace-identification efforts (OpenAnimalTracks for footprints, DFML for dog feces, Automated Egg Classification, Skull2Animal for bones, and FeathersV1 for feathers) into a single multi-trace, multi-task dataset spanning 968 species. Rather than proposing a new architecture, it repurposes standard classification (Swin-B, ResNet-50), detection (YOLOv11, RT-DETR, DINO), and segmentation (MaskDINO, Mask-RCNN) backbones as baselines to establish benchmarks. The t-SNE analysis with CLIP and BioCLIP positions the work within the vision-language pretraining literature, showing that even domain-adapted foundation models do not transfer effectively to trace identification without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AnimalClue addresses a practical bottleneck in wildlife monitoring: the heavy reliance on manual expert inspection of indirect biological evidence. The key open question highlighted by the results is how to handle the extreme long-tail of rare species, where performance collapses (e.g., bone rare-species classification at 1.0% for ResNet-50) and detection mAP drops below 0.1. The finding that BioCLIP, a biology-specific foundation model, still fails to separate trace categories in feature space without fine-tuning suggests that trace identification may require fundamentally different visual features than direct animal appearance recognition. Future work could explore trace-specific pretraining, cross-trace transfer (e.g., using feather features to inform bone identification), and data augmentation strategies for rare species.
