## Problem

Free energy-based OOD detection methods, despite strong empirical performance, contain previously unexplored theoretical vulnerabilities: when the feature dimension exceeds the number of classes, the null space of the classifier's last linear layer creates 'blind spots' where in-distribution and OOD samples with distinct feature representations produce identical free energy scores. Additionally, displacements along the least singular vector direction yield minimal energy changes, further degrading OOD discrimination.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252470/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-100, variable resolution for PASCAL VOC object detection) and produces a scalar free energy score that is fed into a small MLP ψ: R→(0,1) to yield a continuous uncertainty score, thresholded for binary in-distribution/OOD classification. For object-level detection, the same pipeline operates per-object on Faster R-CNN feature vectors (originally 1024-dim, reduced to 768/512/256 via NSR). The architectural modification is minimal: one extra linear layer is inserted before the classification head, adding only 10k–105k parameters to a 21.5M-parameter ResNet-34.

## Method

FEVER-OOD introduces two complementary mechanisms to eliminate free energy vulnerabilities. First, Null Space Reduction (NSR) inserts an additional linear layer g′: R^{d′}→R^r with r < d′ before the classification head, shrinking the nullity from d′−rank(W_cls) to r−rank(W_cls) and eliminating the blind-spot condition entirely when r equals the number of classes. Second, a Least Singular Value Regulariser (LSVR) adds λ_LSV·σ_min^{-1}(W_cls) to the training loss, maximizing the smallest singular value so that even the weakest feature-space direction produces a detectable energy change; a Condition Number Regulariser (CNR) optionally adds λ_CN·κ(W_cls) to equalize energy variation across all directions. These are applied on top of existing free energy baselines (VOS, FFS, Dream-OOD) without altering their outlier synthesis or training protocols, and the combined approach achieves state-of-the-art OOD detection on ImageNet-100 (36.50% FPR95, 92.74 AUROC with Dream-OOD).

## Results

On CIFAR-10, FEVER-OOD (VOS + 96-NSR + LSVR) reduces average FPR95 from 33.66% to 28.22% and increases AUROC from 92.15 to 94.78. On CIFAR-100, Dream-OOD + 100-NSR achieves 42.77% FPR95 and 89.98 AUROC versus 50.96% and 88.06 for the baseline. On ImageNet-100, Dream-OOD + 100-NSR + LSVR reaches a state-of-the-art 36.50% FPR95 and 92.74 AUROC, improving over the 39.33% / 91.84 baseline. In-distribution accuracy is preserved across all settings (e.g., 94.74% vs 94.83% on CIFAR-10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FEVER-OOD consistently outperforms all three energy-based baselines (VOS, FFS, Dream-OOD) across every dataset tested. On CIFAR-10, VOS + 96-NSR + LSVR improves FPR95 by 5.44 points (33.66→28.22) and AUROC by 2.63 points (92.15→94.78); FFS + 96-NSR + LSVR improves FPR95 by 3.78 points (34.82→31.04) and AUROC by 3.51 points (90.52→94.03). On CIFAR-100, Dream-OOD + 100-NSR yields the largest single-method gain, reducing FPR95 by 8.19 points (50.96→42.77) and increasing AUROC by 1.92 points (88.06→89.98). On ImageNet-100, Dream-OOD + 100-NSR + LSVR achieves state-of-the-art 36.50% FPR95 and 92.74 AUROC, beating the baseline by 2.83 and 0.90 points respectively. For object detection (PASCAL VOC → MS-COCO/OpenImages), VOS + 768-NSR + CNR reduces FPR95 by ~3–4 points over the VOS baseline while maintaining comparable mAP (~0.49). The LSV regulariser generally outperforms the Condition Number regulariser, and the latter can prevent convergence when combined with aggressive null-space reduction (e.g., 100-NSR on CIFAR-100).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the energy-based OOD detection lineage initiated by Liu et al. (NeurIPS 2020) and advanced by VOS (Du et al., ICLR 2022), FFS (Kumar et al., CVPR 2023), and Dream-OOD (Du et al., NeurIPS 2023), all of which use the free energy score as the core OOD discriminator. The key departure is a theoretical analysis revealing that the free energy formulation has inherent 'blind spots': when the feature-space displacement between an in-distribution and OOD sample lies in the null space of the classifier's final linear layer, both produce identical energy scores (NSV), and even outside the null space, the least singular value direction yields minimal energy change (LSVV). The proposed mitigations—null space reduction via an inserted linear layer and LSV/condition-number regularizers—are architectural and loss-level modifications applied on top of these existing baselines rather than a new scoring function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The null space and LSV vulnerability analysis provides a principled explanation for why free energy-based OOD detectors can fail on specific OOD directions, which is valuable for understanding the limits of energy-based scoring in general. The practical fixes are lightweight (one extra linear layer plus a regularizer term) and broadly applicable to any classifier with a linear head, making them easy to adopt. However, the empirical gains are modest (2–8% FPR95 reduction depending on dataset), the CNR regularizer causes non-convergence in some settings, and the theoretical analysis assumes a strictly linear final layer, limiting its direct applicability to architectures with non-linear classification heads. Open questions include whether similar vulnerabilities exist in non-linear scoring functions and whether the null space reduction strategy interacts adversely with very deep or transformer-based backbones.
