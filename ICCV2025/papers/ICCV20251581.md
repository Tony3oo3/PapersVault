## Problem

Inserting 3D objects into videos requires simultaneously maintaining temporal consistency (no flickering across frames) and photorealistic lighting (realistic shadows, highlights, and illumination interaction). 2D diffusion models produce visually compelling edits but suffer from temporal incoherence under motion and perspective change, while 3D rendering methods guarantee geometric and temporal consistency but cannot model complex real-world lighting. No prior approach has effectively bridged this gap for dynamic scenes with complex object motion.

## Input / Output

The method takes a wrist video clip, a 3D bracelet model reconstructed as 3D Gaussian Splatting from smartphone photos, and optional user pose adjustments via a lightweight GUI. It additionally consumes monocular depth maps (UniDepth), surface normal maps, and 2D keypoint tracks (CoTracker) for occlusion handling and motion alignment. The output is a temporally consistent video with the bracelet inserted, featuring realistic scene-adaptive lighting and cast shadows, produced at the original video resolution with per-frame 3DGS re-rendering and diffusion-based enhancement.

## Method

The pipeline first renders the bracelet via 3DGS with CoTracker-based pose tracking and UniDepth-based occlusion masking to guarantee geometric and temporal consistency. For realism, a shading-driven enhancement decomposes each frame into albedo, shading, and reflectance residual using a pretrained intrinsic decomposition model, then applies two sequential single-step diffusion networks (fine-tuned from Stable Diffusion) — one for bracelet relighting conditioned on background shading and surface normals, and one for shadow generation — before a final sRGB refinement stage. Each diffusion network uses a single-step formulation (zero-latent concatenation) with an enhanced VAE decoder that injects multi-scale features from an auxiliary encoder to prevent content drift. Temporal coherence is restored by optimizing 3DGS color attributes over a Gaussian-weighted sliding window of refined frames, while shadows are smoothed via EbSynth frame interpolation, and the two are composited using the bracelet mask.

## Results

On 56 bracelet-insertion cases (8 bracelets × 7 Pexels videos), the method achieves a DeQA Score of 3.66, Temporal Consistency of 0.984, and CLIP Score of 0.662, outperforming all three baselines on every metric. In a user study with 36 participants, the method is preferred 81.7–84.1% of the time for realism, temporal consistency, and fidelity, compared to 3.6–8.7% for the strongest baseline (AnyV2V). The largest margin is in visual quality (DeQA), where the method leads the closest competitor AnyV2V by 0.25 points and ConsistI2V by 1.22 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is compared against three baselines: AnyV2V (video-to-video editing), ReVideo (zero-shot video editing), and ConsistI2V (image-to-video generation), all using AnyDoor for first-frame insertion. The proposed method wins on all three quantitative metrics against every baseline. Against AnyV2V (the strongest competitor), it gains +0.25 DeQA, +0.013 Temporal Consistency, and +0.003 CLIP Score. Against ReVideo, the margins are larger: +0.49 DeQA, +0.007 TC, and +0.001 CLIP. Against ConsistI2V, the gap is most pronounced in visual quality (+1.22 DeQA) and fidelity (+0.024 CLIP). In the user study, the method is overwhelmingly preferred (81.7–84.1%) over all baselines (3.6–8.7%), with AnyV2V being the closest competitor at 8.7% for realism and consistency, while ReVideo and ConsistI2V trail further behind at 3.6–5.6%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two well-studied but traditionally separate paradigms: 3D rendering (via 3D Gaussian Splatting) for geometric and temporal consistency, and 2D diffusion models (fine-tuned from Stable Diffusion) for photorealistic appearance. It builds on intrinsic image decomposition (albedo/shading/reflectance separation) from recent work [3,4] but departs by applying it as a two-stage pipeline—first enhancing shading in a physically-motivated domain, then refining in sRGB—rather than performing harmonization in a single space. The single-step diffusion formulation with an enhanced VAE decoder borrows from deterministic sampling [22] and decoder control [36], while the 3DGS-based temporal smoothing (multi-frame weighted color optimization) is a novel use of the 3D representation as a temporal regularizer rather than just a renderer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first pipeline to explicitly combine 3D rendering for temporal coherence with 2D diffusion for photorealistic lighting in video object insertion, offering a practical template for hybrid 3D+2D video editing. The shading-driven decomposition (separating albedo from shading before enhancement) is a transferable technique that could apply to other compositing or relighting tasks. However, the method is currently limited to the bracelet-on-wrist scenario, struggles with poor-quality inputs (motion blur, artifacts), and the baselines compared against (AnyV2V, ReVideo, ConsistI2V) are general-purpose video editing tools rather than purpose-built 3D insertion systems, leaving open whether the large user-study margins would hold against a dedicated 3D compositing baseline. Generalization to arbitrary objects and longer sequences remains a key open question.
