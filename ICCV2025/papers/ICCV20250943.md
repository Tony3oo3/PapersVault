## Problem

High-quality autonomous driving scene rendering typically depends on expensive manual 3D object annotations, while existing self-supervised methods struggle to capture fine-grained local object motions and decompose scenes robustly under noisy pseudo labels, leading to rendering artifacts. The core gap is that trigonometric-function-based motion models provide global fitting but cannot represent local motion details, and neural-network-based models are computationally expensive and similarly miss local structure.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-camera RGB images (2 cameras on KITTI at 375×1242, 1 on Waymo at 1280×1920, 3 on nuScenes at 900×1600) and a single LiDAR point cloud per sequence, plus pseudo supervision signals from SAM (binary object/background segmentation and sky mask), CoTracker3 (point flow), and DPTv2 (monocular inverse depth). It produces novel-viewpoint rendered images of the dynamic driving scene at arbitrary camera poses and timestamps, with the scene internally decomposed into a static background and a set of moving objects represented by dynamic Gaussians.

## Method

AD-GS represents the scene as 3D Gaussians split into object and background subsets via simplified binary pseudo 2D segmentation from SAM. Object Gaussians are deformed over time using learnable uniform B-spline curves (order k=6) for position and B-spline quaternion curves for rotation, combined with trigonometric functions to provide both local and global motion fitting; background Gaussians remain static. A bidirectional temporal visibility mask modulates object Gaussian opacity with asymmetric Gaussian decay around the LiDAR acquisition timestamp to handle sudden object appearance/disappearance. Training is fully self-supervised, combining photometric loss, flow supervision (CoTracker3), inverse-depth supervision (DPTv2 with scale-and-shift loss), object/sky mask BCE losses, and a physically rigid regularization that penalizes variance in deformation parameters among KNN-neighboring object Gaussians to enforce local structural coherence.

## Results

On KITTI-75%, AD-GS achieves PSNR 29.16, SSIM 0.920, and LPIPS 0.033, outperforming the best self-supervised baseline PVG (27.13/0.895/0.049) by 2.03 dB in PSNR while remaining competitive with the annotation-assisted 4DGF (31.34/0.945/0.026). On Waymo, AD-GS reaches PSNR 33.91 and PSNR* 27.41 (moving objects), surpassing Grid4D (32.19/22.77) and approaching 4DGF (34.64/29.77). On nuScenes, AD-GS attains PSNR 31.06, SSIM 0.925, and LPIPS 0.164, leading all self-supervised methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AD-GS outperforms all self-supervised baselines across all three datasets. On KITTI-75%, it beats PVG by 2.03 dB PSNR, Grid4D by 5.37 dB, and SUDS by 6.39 dB; on Waymo, it surpasses Grid4D by 1.72 dB PSNR and 4.64 dB PSNR* (moving objects), and EmerNeRF by 2.59 dB PSNR; on nuScenes, it leads Grid4D by 0.77 dB PSNR. Against annotation-assisted methods, AD-GS narrows the gap substantially: on KITTI-75% it trails 4DGF by only 2.18 dB PSNR and ML-NSG by 0.78 dB, while on Waymo it is within 0.73 dB of 4DGF and 0.06 dB of StreetGS in overall PSNR. The largest margin over self-supervised competitors appears in the moving-object metric (PSNR*) on Waymo, where AD-GS (27.41) exceeds Grid4D (22.77) by 4.64 dB, demonstrating the benefit of B-spline local fitting and temporal visibility masks for dynamic objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

AD-GS builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting as its rendering backbone and extends the self-supervised driving scene rendering line (SUDS, EmerNeRF, PVG, HUGS) by replacing their motion representations—either neural networks or pure trigonometric functions—with a hybrid of learnable B-spline curves and trigonometric terms, exploiting B-splines' local support for fine-grained motion while retaining trigonometric functions for global fitting under noisy supervision. It also departs from prior scene-decomposition strategies (instance-level segmentation, feature-based self-supervision) by collapsing the problem to a binary object/background split guided by SAM pseudo-labels, and introduces an asymmetric bidirectional temporal visibility mask that fixes the LiDAR acquisition timestamp as the center rather than learning it. The physically rigid regularization (KNN-based variance penalty on nearby Gaussians) is a new stabilization mechanism not present in prior Gaussian-splatting driving methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for manual 3D object tracklet annotations, AD-GS lowers the practical barrier to high-quality driving-scene simulation, which is critical for scalable autonomous-vehicle testing. The B-spline-plus-trigonometric motion model and the simplified binary decomposition are modular ideas that other Gaussian-splatting or NeRF-based driving pipelines could adopt. However, a notable gap remains versus annotation-assisted methods (e.g., 29.16 vs. 31.34 PSNR on KITTI-75%), and the authors acknowledge that extremely sparse training views still challenge self-supervised motion fitting. Open questions include whether the B-spline formulation scales to longer sequences or more complex multi-object interactions, and whether the binary object/background split is sufficient for scenes with many overlapping dynamic actors.
