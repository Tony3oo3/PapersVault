## Problem

Monocular 3D object detection has traditionally required fully-supervised training with LiDAR-captured data and laborious human 3D annotations, limiting data diversity and scalability. Prior weakly-supervised methods (e.g., VSRD) still require 2D human masks and are prohibitively slow (~15 min/frame), making large-scale auto-labelling infeasible. No existing method could train a competitive monocular 3D detector with zero human annotations while remaining fast enough to process large datasets and handle heterogeneous camera setups.

## Input / Output

The auto-labelling pipeline consumes RGB video sequences (100-frame windows) from a moving vehicle along with ego-motion data (GPS/IMU) and known camera intrinsics/extrinsics; no LiDAR or human labels are required. It outputs 7-DOF 3D bounding boxes (x, y, z position, length, width, height, yaw) for vehicles, which are transformed into Canonical Object Space (canonical focal length f_c=750) to enable cross-dataset training. The resulting pseudo-labels train a MonoDETR detector that, at inference, takes a single RGB image and camera parameters and predicts 3D boxes in world coordinates by inverting the COS transform.

## Method

The pipeline first generates per-frame pseudo-LiDAR point clouds by back-projecting Metric3D depth maps masked by MViT2-Huge 2D instance segmentation, then tracks objects across frames using a physics-aware motion model. The key novelty is the Local Object Motion Model (LOMM), which classifies each tracked instance as stationary or moving by comparing the norm of mean inter-frame displacement to its standard deviation (z-ratio threshold), enabling temporal aggregation for both classes. For moving objects, yaw is trivially derived from trajectory direction; for stationary objects, points are aggregated across frames and fitted with a Saturated Closeness Criterion (sigmoid-saturated distances to 10th/90th percentiles instead of min/max) that improves robustness over prior L-shape fitting. Position is refined via Template Fitting Loss with small perturbations, and Canonical Object Space (scaling x,y,z by f_c/f_i) makes the trained MonoDETR invariant to focal length differences, enabling multi-dataset training and zero-shot transfer to unseen camera setups.

## Results

On KITTI-360 test set, MonoSOWA achieves APBEV/AP3D@0.5 of 38.41/29.98 (Easy) and 35.26/27.56 (Hard) using no human annotations, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art weakly-supervised method VSRD (29.07/21.77 Easy) by over 9 points in BEV and 8 points in 3D at 0.5 IoU. At the looser 0.3 IoU threshold, MonoSOWA reaches 50.84/42.72 (Easy), competitive with VSRD (58.40/50.86) while requiring no 2D masks. On Waymo validation, MonoSOWA achieves APBEV@0.5 of 18.98 (All) and AP3D@0.5 of 13.46 (All), surpassing the fully-supervised DEVIANT (10.29 BEV, 10.29 3D) despite using zero human labels. The auto-labelling pipeline runs at ~1.3 s/frame, approximately 700× faster than VSRD's 15 min/frame.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MonoSOWA decisively outperforms the prior weakly-supervised SOTA VSRD [18] at the stricter 0.5 IoU threshold (38.41 vs 29.07 APBEV Easy; 29.98 vs 21.77 AP3D Easy), despite VSRD requiring 2D instance masks while MonoSOWA requires no human annotations at all; at 0.3 IoU the gap narrows (50.84 vs 58.40 APBEV Easy), which the authors attribute to VSRD benefiting from amodal human labels. Against Autolabels [47] and WeakM3D [27], both of which require LiDAR and 2D masks, MonoSOWA wins by a very large margin (e.g., 38.41 vs 20.18 and 8.10 APBEV@0.5 Easy). On Waymo, MonoSOWA surpasses the fully-supervised DEVIANT [11] (18.98 vs 10.29 APBEV@0.5 All) and is competitive with fully-supervised MonoDETR [49] (18.98 vs 23.63 APBEV@0.5 All) while using zero human labels. In the pre-training setting on KITTI, MonoSOWA pseudo-labels plus 25% human fine-tuning (39.99/32.64 APBEV/AP3D@0.7 Easy) outperform training from scratch with 100% human labels (37.99/29.36), and with 15% human labels the accuracy nearly matches full supervision. The auto-labelling quality itself (Table 2) also surpasses VSRD and Autolabels on KITTI-360 training set (61.17/47.07 vs 47.12/35.25 and 51.85/4.65 APBEV/AP3D@0.5 Easy).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the auto-labelling line of work (VSRD [18], Autolabels [47]) by eliminating all human annotations—including the 2D instance masks that prior weakly-supervised methods like VSRD and WeakMono3D still require—relying instead on off-the-shelf 2D detection and monocular depth estimation. It improves upon the L-shape fitting algorithm of Zhang et al. [50] with a Saturated Closeness Criterion and reuses the Template Fitting Loss from the authors' prior TCC-Det [34] for position refinement. The key conceptual departure is the Local Object Motion Model, which disentangles ego-motion from object motion to aggregate temporal information for both stationary and moving vehicles, and the adoption of Canonical Object Space to unify training across heterogeneous camera setups—neither of which existed in prior monocular 3D auto-labelling work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing both LiDAR and human annotation requirements, this method unlocks training data from the vast majority of currently manufactured vehicles (single-camera), dramatically expanding the diversity of geographies and traffic scenarios available for monocular 3D detection. The 700× speedup over VSRD makes large-scale auto-labelling (e.g., 150k-frame Waymo) practically feasible for the first time, and the COS mechanism enables cross-dataset training that matches single-dataset human-label accuracy. The main limitation is performance on distant objects (a few pixels high), which is inherent to monocular depth inference rather than the auto-labelling pipeline. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond rigid vehicles to pedestrians or cyclists, and whether the reliance on off-the-shelf depth estimators (Metric3D) introduces systematic biases that cap achievable accuracy.
