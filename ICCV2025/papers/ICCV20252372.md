## Problem

Existing unsupervised 3D gaze estimation methods that rely on learning-by-synthesis suffer from two critical shortcomings: generated facial images lack visual realism (especially in the eye region), and the paired pseudo gaze ground-truths are inaccurate due to the parallel-gaze assumption or implicit 3D representations that make precise eyeball rotation difficult. This limits their ability to match the performance of supervised or cross-dataset approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252372/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires only a set of RGB facial images of a single static individual along with corresponding camera parameters—no gaze annotations or 3D scans are needed. It first produces a per-subject 3D head representation (FLAME mesh with bound 3D Gaussians and a learnable lighting model), then generates diverse synthetic facial images with accurate pseudo 3D gaze directions by rotating eyeballs toward randomly sampled targets on a virtual screen. The final output is a ResNet18 gaze estimator that maps a single facial image to a 3D gaze direction in the camera coordinate frame.

## Method

The core representation is 3D Gaussian Splatting with mesh binding (following GaussianAvatars), where each Gaussian is associated with a parent triangle on a FLAME head mesh, enabling explicit deformation control. A learnable MLP replaces spherical harmonics to map position and lighting features to Gaussian colors, providing explicit lighting controllability. For eye-region fidelity, the training introduces a Random Symmetric Rotation (RSR) protocol that adds uniform random roll angles to eyeball Euler angles during rendering, forcing the model to learn occluded eyeball appearance under a rotational-symmetry assumption, combined with a geometry loss (binary mask overlap between rendered and detected eye regions) and an appearance loss (L1 + D-SSIM + LPIPS restricted to the eye area). For accurate pseudo labels, a virtual 2D screen is placed in front of the 3D model; gaze targets are randomly sampled on this screen, and eyeballs are rotated to fixate on the target, replacing the physically inaccurate parallel-gaze assumption with realistic convergence geometry. The resulting synthetic image–gaze pairs train a ResNet18 estimator, achieving state-of-the-art unsupervised performance on EVE, Columbia, and MPII.

## Results

On the three gaze estimation benchmarks, the method achieves 3D angular errors of 9.00° (EVE), 6.62° (Columbia), and 7.39° (MPII), outperforming all unsupervised baselines by margins of 18.92%, 26.44%, and 2.78% respectively. Notably, it surpasses the best cross-dataset baseline (ETH-XGaze) on Columbia (6.62° vs. 8.23°) and MPII (7.39° vs. 7.50°), though it trails on EVE (9.00° vs. 6.61°). For image quality, the method achieves FID 12.18 and KID 0.011 on MPII, roughly half the FID of the next-best method (GaussianAvatars, 32.04), and receives 46.4% of user votes for realism versus 24.5% for the runner-up.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among unsupervised methods, the paper's approach beats the strongest prior baselines on all three benchmarks: it outperforms FLAME tracking (11.10°) by 2.10° on EVE, Kuang et al. (9.0°) by 2.38° on Columbia, and HGSM (7.6°) by 0.21° on MPII. Against cross-dataset methods, it surpasses ETH-XGaze on Columbia (6.62° vs. 8.23°, a 1.61° margin) and MPII (7.39° vs. 7.50°), but falls short on EVE (9.00° vs. 6.61°). It also outperforms MPIIFaceGaze on Columbia (6.62° vs. 7.75°) and Gaze360 on both Columbia (6.62° vs. 9.0°) and MPII (7.39° vs. 12.1°). For image quality, the method nearly halves the FID of GaussianAvatars (12.18 vs. 32.04) and SimGAN (12.18 vs. 36.44), and doubles the user-study voting rate of the next-best method (46.4% vs. 24.5%). The gap to supervised methods remains substantial (e.g., 9.00° vs. 2.49° on EVE), confirming that unsupervised synthesis still lags behind direct annotation but narrows the gap considerably.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) for scene representation and GaussianAvatars' mesh-binding technique for associating Gaussians with deformable FLAME mesh triangles, extending both toward gaze-specific controllability. It departs from prior learning-by-synthesis gaze methods (SynthesEyes, UnityEyes, SimGAN, HGSM) that either rely on expensive 3D eye scans or GAN-based refinement that risks label corruption, and from implicit NeRF-based approaches (GazeNeRF, EyeNeRF) where precise eyeball rotation is difficult. The key conceptual departure is replacing spherical-harmonic lighting with a learnable MLP-based lighting model, introducing a Random Symmetric Rotation protocol to handle occluded eyeball regions, and using a virtual 2D screen to generate physiologically accurate (non-parallel) gaze directions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that a fully unsupervised pipeline—requiring only a handful of facial images per subject—can produce gaze estimators that match or exceed cross-dataset methods on two of three benchmarks, removing the need for expensive human-annotated gaze data. The explicit controllability framework (lighting, eye geometry, gaze targets) is modular and could transfer to other face-conditioned generation tasks such as fatigue detection or expression synthesis. However, the method still trails supervised approaches by a meaningful margin (e.g., 9.00° vs 2.49° on EVE), and the RSR symmetry assumption, while effective, does not always hold perfectly as evidenced by the FID degradation when RSR is removed. Open questions include scalability to highly diverse subject pools, video-based gaze estimation, and whether the virtual screen paradigm generalizes to unconstrained in-the-wild settings.
