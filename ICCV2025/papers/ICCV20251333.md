## Problem

Standard 3D Morphable Models represent facial expressions as identity-independent vertex displacements, so applying the same expression parameters to different faces produces identical deformations regardless of facial structure, causing identity leakage during retargeting. Additionally, the field lacks a geometric benchmark for evaluating expression capture accuracy in 3D space, with existing evaluations relying on image-space metrics or neutral-mesh reconstruction only.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251333/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB image and a neutral 3D mesh in FLAME topology (13k vertices) as input, and produces a semantic expression code (a latent vector) that, combined with the identity mesh, yields an expressive 3D mesh via the decoder. The expression code is domain-agnostic and can be applied to any target identity mesh, enabling retargeting. While trained on individual frames, the model produces temporally consistent results when applied to video sequences.

## Method

SEREP is a three-stage pipeline. First, a semantic expression model (spiral-convolution encoder-decoder) is trained on unpaired 3D meshes to disentangle identity from expression, using a cycle-consistency loss to align expression codes across subjects and a delta loss to prevent decoder collapse. Second, this trained model generates a synthetic dataset of 2D renders with known expression codes by sampling identities and codes, adding procedural teeth/eyes and random textures, requiring no manual 3D modeling. Third, a ConvNeXt-B-based capture model is trained semi-supervised on both synthetic and in-the-wild images (WFLW + CelebV-HQ), with three heads predicting the expression code, 2D landmarks, and a domain label; a gradient-reversal layer on the domain classifier aligns synthetic and real feature spaces, while the landmark head (shared across domains) anchors the encoder to face geometry.

## Results

On the MultiREX benchmark, SEREP achieves an average per-vertex reconstruction error of 2.19 mm, outperforming DECA (2.59 mm), EMICA (2.36 mm), and SMIRK (2.74 mm). The advantage is most pronounced in profile views (60° yaw), where SEREP reaches 2.30 mm versus 3.11 mm for SMIRK and 2.61 mm for DECA, demonstrating superior viewpoint robustness. For expression representation quality, SEREP reduces reconstruction error to 1.15 mm (vs. FLAME's 1.36 mm) and improves identity preservation (CSIM 0.791 vs. 0.766) on the 56-subject test set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEREP outperforms all three quantitative baselines on MultiREX average error: DECA (2.59 mm), EMICA (2.36 mm), and SMIRK (2.74 mm) vs. SEREP's 2.19 mm. The gap widens substantially in profile views, where SMIRK degrades to 3.11 mm and DECA to 2.61 mm, while SEREP holds at 2.30 mm, confirming the benefit of semantic-level expression representation over image-space optimization. SEREP loses only on the forehead region to EMICA (1.89 vs. 1.65 mm), likely due to the domain loss trade-off noted by the authors. For expression representation (Table 1), SEREP beats FLAME on both reconstruction error (1.15 vs. 1.36 mm) and identity preservation CSIM (0.791 vs. 0.766), validating the semantic disentanglement approach. Against 3DDFA-V3 and TEASER, the comparison is qualitative (Fig. 7), where SEREP produces more plausible asymmetrical expressions (e.g., winks, funnel mouths) and better side-view results, though no geometric metric is reported for these two methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 3DMM paradigm (FLAME, DECA, EMICA, SMIRK) that represents expressions as identity-independent additive vertex displacements, arguing instead that expressions must be encoded at a semantic level to avoid identity leakage during retargeting. It also diverges from prior semantic disentanglement work (Facescape, NPHM) that requires paired multi-subject expression data, learning its expression space from unpaired 3D meshes with a cycle-consistency objective. For the capture model, it borrows the gradient-reversal domain adaptation mechanism from Ganin & Lempitsky [20] to bridge a synthetic-to-real gap, but replaces the expensive photorealistic synthetic pipelines of Wood et al. with a simple procedural rendering of retargeted meshes, and avoids the differentiable-rendering self-supervision used by DECA/EMICA/SMIRK by training directly in 3D space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identity-leakage problem in 3DMM-based retargeting is a concrete barrier for game and VR applications where captured performances must transfer across diverse character morphologies; SEREP's semantic representation addresses this directly. MultiREX fills a notable gap as the first video-based geometric 3D expression benchmark, enabling fair comparison beyond image-space proxies. However, the method assumes a known neutral mesh (identity), struggles with external occluders like glasses, and the benchmark currently supports only FLAME topology. Open questions include whether temporal modeling and camera-position conditioning can further improve in-the-wild robustness, and whether the semantic expression space generalizes to topologies beyond FLAME.
