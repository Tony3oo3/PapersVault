## Problem

Transformer-based monocular depth estimation methods rely on window-based CRFs that restrict attention to fixed-size local windows, causing over-fitting to local textures and neglecting global context. Additionally, multi-scale visual features—critical for accurate depth regression—have not been explicitly and effectively fused within transformer-based MDE pipelines due to their stage-wise optimization design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251184/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces a dense per-pixel depth map. A Swin-Large encoder extracts four levels of patch features (from 4×4 to 32×32 pixel patches), which are channel-wise concatenated into a unified multi-scale representation. The framework is trained supervised on KITTI (outdoor, max depth 80 m) and NYU-Depth-v2 (indoor, max depth 10 m), with zero-shot evaluation on nuScenes and SUN RGB-D.

## Method

Hyper-Depth introduces three coupled components. First, a Semantic Consistency Enhancement (SCE) module replaces the standard PPM by constructing a cluster-free hypergraph (via linear projection of multi-scale patch features followed by a sigmoid of pairwise dot products to approximate a KNN incidence matrix) and applying spatial-domain hypergraph convolution to aggregate cross-position, cross-scale information in semantic space. Second, a Geometric Consistency Constraint (GCC) module computes binary ground-truth consistency scores between each patch and its K=8 spatial neighbors based on depth thresholds, then supervises the learned correlation matrix with a cross-entropy loss to prevent over-reliance on local texture. Third, a Correlation-based CRF (C-CRFs) decoder uses the trained hypergraph incidence matrix to gate the query-key attention computation, restricting it to semantically correlated patches rather than a fixed spatial window. The total loss combines a Scaled Scale-Invariant (SSI) regression loss with the GCC cross-entropy term (γ=5).

## Results

On the KITTI Eigen split, Hyper-Depth achieves an RMSE of 1.886, reducing the main metric by 6.21% over the previous best (DiffusionDepth, 2.011) and 4.35% on Abs. Rel. On NYU-Depth-v2, it reaches an RMSE of 0.211, a 3.32% improvement over ECoDepth (0.218). On the KITTI official online benchmark, the method lowers the primary SILog metric to 9.16, a 4.80% reduction over DCDepth (9.60). Zero-shot evaluations confirm generalizability: RMSE of 2.891 on nuScenes (trained on KITTI) and 0.372 on SUN-RGBD (trained on NYU-Depth-v2), both outperforming all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hyper-Depth outperforms all 14 compared baselines on every reported metric across both KITTI and NYU-Depth-v2. On KITTI Eigen split, the largest gains are over DiffusionDepth (RMSE 2.011→1.886, −6.21%) and DCDepth (2.032→1.886, −7.19%), while the closest competitor ECoDepth (ViT-Large backbone) is surpassed by 7.4% in RMSE. On NYU-Depth-v2, the method beats ECoDepth (RMSE 0.218→0.211, −3.32%) and DCDepth (0.304→0.211, −30.6%), with the gap widening substantially against older baselines like BTS (0.391) and Eigen (0.641). On the KITTI official online benchmark, Hyper-Depth reduces SILog from 9.60 (DCDepth) to 9.16 (−4.80%) and iRMSE from 10.58 (DiffusionDepth) to 10.15 (−4.07%). In zero-shot transfer, the method leads on both nuScenes (RMSE 2.891 vs. IEBins 3.028) and SUN-RGBD (RMSE 0.372 vs. iDisc 0.402), indicating that the hypergraph-based fusion generalizes beyond the training domain. Model size (262 M params) and inference time (0.276 s) remain comparable to NewCRFs and IEBins, confirming that the added hypergraph modules do not introduce prohibitive overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the window-based CRF decoder from NewCRFs [51] and the spatial-domain hypergraph convolution from HGNN+ [13], repurposing the latter to replace the Pyramid Pooling Module (PPM) that is standard in transformer-based MDE pipelines. It departs from the prevailing local-window attention paradigm by introducing a cluster-free hypergraph construction that models high-order, cross-position patch correlations in a differentiable, end-to-end manner. The geometric consistency constraint (GCC) module is a novel addition that leverages ground-truth depth to regularize the learned correlation structure, a supervision signal not previously exploited in this form for MDE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that explicitly modeling high-order patch correlations via hypergraphs can meaningfully reduce the over-fitting to local textures that plagues window-based transformer MDE methods, with the largest gains appearing at greater distances where local cues are weakest. The zero-shot transfer results on nuScenes and SUN-RGBD suggest the learned geometric-semantic representations generalize beyond the training domain, though the GCC loss still requires ground-truth depth, limiting applicability to unsupervised or self-supervised settings. An open question is whether the approximate hypergraph incidence matrix (via sigmoid of dot products) remains effective at higher resolutions or with larger patch counts, and whether the approach can be adapted to metric depth estimation without per-dataset fine-tuning.
