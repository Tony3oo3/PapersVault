## Problem

Existing machine learning approaches treat action understanding (recognizing what an action means) and embodied execution (physically performing it) as independent tasks, despite neuroscience showing that mirror neurons create shared neural representations for both. This paper asks whether explicitly aligning the intermediate representations of these two tasks can improve both, and whether such alignment already emerges spontaneously in separately trained models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251075/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The action understanding branch (ViCLIP) consumes front-facing RGB video clips paired with language instructions and outputs a top-1 action label via video-text similarity. The embodied execution branch (ARP with MVT backbone) consumes multi-view RGBD images at 128×128 (converted to point clouds) plus a language instruction and outputs the next target end-effector pose and gripper state. For alignment, intermediate features from both branches are projected via separate linear layers into a shared 1024-dimensional latent space, where positive pairs are constructed by matching samples that share the same language instruction (not necessarily the same episode).

## Method

The core contribution is a bidirectional InfoNCE contrastive loss that aligns intermediate representations of a ViCLIP video-language model and an ARP manipulation policy during joint end-to-end training. Two lightweight linear projections map each model's internal features into a common 1024-d latent space, and the alignment loss pulls together representations of the same semantic action while pushing apart different ones, effectively maximizing mutual information between the two representation streams. The final objective is L_AU + λ_EE·L_EE + λ_align·L_align, requiring only the addition of two linear layers and one loss term to the original architectures. The paper first demonstrates via probing that independently trained models already achieve >60% retrieval alignment and that task-successful samples show significantly higher alignment, motivating the explicit alignment. Ablations show that aligning 'by instruction' (same language command, different episodes) with τ=0.1 yields the best trade-off between semantic consistency and variation, outperforming both stricter (by episode) and looser (by task class) criteria.

## Results

On the RLBench multi-task manipulation benchmark (18 tasks), the proposed MN method achieves an average success rate of 88.8%, outperforming the direct baseline ARP (85.3%) by 3.5% and the previous best RVT-2 (81.4%) by 7.4%. On the action recognition task, MN achieves 74.9% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the fine-tuned ViCLIP baseline (71.6%) by 3.3% and the zero-shot ViCLIP (10.4%) by 64.5%. The largest gains in manipulation appear on fine-grained affordance tasks such as Sort Shape (66.7% vs. 49.3% for ARP) and Stack Cup (93.3% vs. 82.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The MN method outperforms all 10 compared baselines on the RLBench manipulation benchmark. Against the most directly comparable baseline ARP [44], MN improves average success from 85.3% to 88.8% (+3.5%), with the largest per-task gains on Sort Shape (+17.4%) and Stack Cup (+10.6%). Over RVT-2 [13] (81.4%), the previous state-of-the-art, MN leads by 7.4% on average. The gap widens substantially against earlier methods: +23.8% over Act3D [11], +25.9% over RVT [12], +39.4% over PerAct [37], +42.4% over PolarNet [2], +43.5% over HiveFormer [14], +68.7% over C2F-ARM [19], and +87.5% over BC-Z [20]. On the action understanding side (Table 1), MN (74.9%) beats the fine-tuned ViCLIP [41] baseline (71.6%) by 3.3% and the zero-shot ViCLIP† (10.4%) by 64.5%, confirming that the alignment objective benefits both tasks simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws on two distinct research threads: the neuroscience literature on mirror neurons (Rizzolatti, Gallese, and colleagues) and the ML literature on cross-model representation alignment (Platonic Representation Hypothesis, CLIP-style contrastive learning). It builds directly on ARP (autoregressive policy with MVT backbone) for embodied execution and ViCLIP (video-language model pretrained on InternVid) for action understanding, adding a bidirectional InfoNCE alignment loss between their intermediate representations. Conceptually, it departs from prior work that treats action understanding and embodied execution as independent tasks by proposing they should be jointly regularized through a shared latent space, mirroring the biological mechanism where observation and execution activate overlapping neural circuits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that independently trained AU and EE models spontaneously develop >60% representation alignment (measured by linear-probe retrieval) and that task-successful samples show significantly higher alignment than failures provides empirical grounding for the mirror neuron analogy in ML. However, the practical impact is currently limited: improvements are modest (~3% on both tasks), evaluation is confined to simulated RLBench with a single robot platform, and the alignment mechanism is a simple contrastive loss between two linear projections. Open questions include whether this approach transfers to real-robot settings, scales to more complex multi-step tasks, or benefits from hierarchical/multisensory alignment strategies as the authors themselves suggest.
