## Problem

Under low-light or fast-motion capture, RGB frames suffer severe motion blur that causes structure-from-motion tools like COLMAP to fail, leaving no reliable initial camera poses for downstream 3D reconstruction. Existing Gaussian Splatting and NeRF-based deblurring methods all presuppose accurate poses and sparse point clouds, creating a chicken-and-egg dependency: deblurring needs poses, but pose estimation needs sharp images. This paper tackles the joint problem of pose estimation, geometry refinement, and deblurring from scratch when no SfM solution exists.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251369/cropped_page0_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes undistorted RGB-D image sequences (640×480 synthetic; 1280×720 real Azure Kinect with 33.3 ms fixed exposure) where depth maps are eroded three times to remove edge outliers. It produces per-frame camera-to-world matrices, a dense set of 3D Gaussian splats with fixed scale s/2 initialized one-per-pixel from depth, and deblurred novel-view renderings. Deblurring requires 15–17 virtual camera poses interpolated across each frame's exposure window, and the final output includes both color and depth renderings evaluated against ground truth.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages. First, local relative poses between consecutive frames are estimated by jointly minimizing an optical-flow reprojection loss (using Unimatch), a photometric L1 loss, and a novel depth-consistency loss that compares vertical components of backprojected points relative to the inter-camera baseline; optimization proceeds in two phases (flow-only, then adding photometric and depth terms), followed by point-to-plane ICP on the globally aligned point clouds. Second, all frames are merged into a world-coordinate point cloud, and dense 3D Gaussians (one per depth pixel, scale fixed at s/2) are initialized; camera poses, Gaussian positions, and opacities are then jointly refined by minimizing a depth-alignment L1 loss between rendered and input depth maps plus an opacity regularization that prunes ambiguous splats, eliminating drift and loop-closure artifacts. Third, motion blur is modeled as the average of k sharp renders from virtual poses interpolated between the start and end of exposure; the deblur loss combines L1 alignment with the input blurry image, a depth-aware structural similarity term (D-SSIM), and the depth consistency loss to produce geometrically faithful, sharp reconstructions.

## Results

On the synthetic RGB-D dataset with extreme motion blur, the proposed method achieves the best deblurring quality across all three scenes: PSNR of 26.75 (Bedroom), 27.65 (Living Room), and 25.65 (Office), with SSIM up to 0.900 and LPIPS as low as 0.150. For camera pose estimation, the method achieves the best RPE (translational and rotational) across all scenes (e.g., RPE(trans) of 0.002 m in the Living Room), while its ATE (0.005–0.102 m) is comparable to DROID-SLAM (0.011–0.059 m). The method also produces the lowest depth RMSE (0.005–0.014 m⁻¹ in inverse depth), confirming geometrically consistent reconstructions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines on every deblurring metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, RMSE) across all three synthetic scenes. Against the strongest baseline MonoGS [21], the gains are 2.0–3.2 dB in PSNR and 0.08–0.10 in SSIM, with LPIPS reduced by 0.03–0.13, indicating substantially sharper novel-view renderings. DROID-SLAM [42] achieves the best ATE (0.011–0.059 m) among all methods, but its RPE is 3–40× worse than the proposed method (e.g., RPE(rot) of 1.212° vs. 0.092° in the Bedroom), and its deblurring PSNR trails by 4–6 dB. ElasticFusion [47] and FPFH-ICP [29] perform poorly under extreme motion blur, with ElasticFusion achieving only 13.8–19.1 dB PSNR and FPFH-ICP showing high depth RMSE (0.033–0.450 m⁻¹), confirming that traditional ICP-based and surfel-based methods struggle when point cloud overlap is limited by fast camera motion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) as its rendering and optimization backbone, and adapts the virtual-camera-pose deblurring formulation from BADGaussians (Zhao et al., 2024) into a depth-aware setting. It departs from prior GS-based deblurring and SLAM methods (MonoGS, SplaTAM, DROID-SLAM) by eliminating the dependency on COLMAP for initial poses—a critical departure, since SfM fails under extreme motion blur. The pipeline instead chains optical flow (Unimatch) and point-to-plane ICP for initial alignment, then performs joint pose-geometry refinement within the GS framework, a combination not previously explored for the no-SfM-initialization regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical gap: low-light or fast-motion capture produces extreme motion blur that breaks COLMAP, leaving RGB-D reconstruction pipelines without a viable initialization. By unifying pose estimation, geometry refinement, and deblurring in a single GS pipeline, the method removes the chicken-and-egg dependency between sharp images and accurate poses. Limitations include the requirement for RGB-D input (not monocular), high per-frame computational cost (~11 s for initial pose), and evaluation on only three synthetic and a small number of real scenes. Open questions include scalability to long sequences, real-time operation, and whether the depth-consistency loss generalizes to scenes with significant dynamic content or non-rigid geometry.
