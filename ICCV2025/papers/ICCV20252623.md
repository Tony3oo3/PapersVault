## Problem

Existing 3D driving scene generation methods face a fundamental trade-off: voxel- and neural-field-based approaches produce valid 3D geometry but lack photorealistic appearance, while video diffusion models generate rich visuals but are limited to short clips without true 3D representation. Neither class of method simultaneously delivers large-scale 3D consistency, flexible controllability (HD maps, vehicle poses, text), and a renderable 3D format suitable for physics simulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252623/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an HD map (road edges and lane lines as 3D polylines), 3D vehicle bounding boxes with heading angles, and a free-form text prompt describing weather and time of day. It produces a dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting scene spanning up to ~100,000 m² (~300 m × 400 m), an intermediate semantic voxel world, and a 200-frame driving video at 10 Hz. The 3DGS output supports unlimited free driving, novel view synthesis, and per-object trajectory editing, while the voxel world provides the geometric scaffold for collision and LiDAR simulation.

## Method

InfiniCube is a three-stage pipeline. First, a map-conditioned sparse voxel LDM (built on XCube) generates a semantic voxel world chunk-by-chunk using a Repaint-style outpainting strategy that fixes overlapping latents to ensure cross-chunk consistency; conditions include rasterized HD-map polylines, fitted road-surface planes, and heading-angle-encoded bounding boxes. Second, a finetuned SVD-XT video model is conditioned on pixel-aligned guidance buffers (a semantic color buffer and a 3D-coordinate buffer) rendered from the voxel world, which provide 3D grounding that suppresses auto-regressive drift and extends generation from 25 to 200 frames; a ControlNet-on-FLUX module produces the initial frame from the text prompt. Third, a dual-branch feed-forward network lifts the video and voxels into dynamic 3DGS in seconds: a sparse 3D convolution U-Net (voxel branch) reconstructs static geometry, while a 2D UNet (pixel branch) handles mid-ground and dynamic objects using self-supervised depth supervision from masked voxel depth and Depth Anything V2 features, with an implicit sky MLP completing the scene.

## Results

On novel view synthesis (Table 3), InfiniCube achieves the best PSNR of 20.80 dB (T+5) and 19.93 dB (T+10), SSIM of 0.73/0.72, and LPIPS of 0.42/0.45, outperforming the closest competitor SCube (19.90/18.78 PSNR) by ~0.9 dB. In the HD map alignment user study (Table 2), InfiniCube maintains a 84.6–84.8% positive rate across frames 40–120, while Panacea degrades sharply from 76.8% to 53.4%. For long video generation, InfiniCube sustains lower FID scores over 200 frames compared to Panacea and Vista, which show significant quality degradation after ~100 frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfiniCube outperforms all six 3D reconstruction baselines (PixelNeRF, PixelSplat, DUSt3R, MVSplat, MVSGaussian, SCube) on every metric in the novel view synthesis evaluation, with the largest gains over PixelNeRF (+5.6 dB PSNR) and the most competitive margin over SCube (+0.9 dB PSNR, −0.05 LPIPS at T+5), confirming the benefit of the dual-branch design. In the video generation comparison, InfiniCube's 3D-grounded guidance buffers maintain a stable ~84% HD map alignment positive rate across 120 frames, whereas Panacea drops from 76.8% to 53.4% over the same span, and Vista (used without trajectory control) shows comparable FID degradation after ~100 frames. The ablation in Fig. 9b further shows that the semantic buffer is the most critical component for long-video quality, while the coordinate buffer resolves motion-induced ambiguities. Overall, InfiniCube is the only method that simultaneously produces a renderable 3D representation with detailed geometry, 200-frame video output, and full controllability over dynamic objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

InfiniCube sits at the intersection of three research threads: sparse-voxel 3D generation (XCube, InfiniCity), controllable driving video generation (Vista, Panacea, MagicDrive3D), and feed-forward 3DGS reconstruction (SCube, GS-LRM). It departs from prior 3D scene generators by adding appearance fidelity through a video model, and from prior driving video models by grounding them in a 3D voxel world via rendered guidance buffers (semantic + coordinate), enabling 8× longer video generation (200 vs. 25 frames) with reduced auto-regressive drift. The dual-branch reconstruction (voxel-space for static geometry, pixel-space for mid-ground and dynamic objects) is a novel combination of the SCube and GS-LRM paradigms tailored to scenes with moving vehicles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to generate ~100,000 m² dynamic 3D driving scenes with HD-map, bounding-box, and text controllability directly addresses a critical bottleneck in autonomous-vehicle simulation and testing, where hand-crafted or captured scenes are expensive and limited in diversity. The guidance-buffer mechanism offers a general recipe for grounding video diffusion models in 3D structure, potentially transferable beyond driving. Key open questions include maintaining global consistency across very large outpainted scenes (the authors acknowledge long-distance chunk drift), scaling beyond the single Waymo training distribution, and whether the 200-frame video length is sufficient for truly open-world driving at scale.
