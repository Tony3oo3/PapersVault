## Problem

Vision Transformer self-attention maps offer inherent spatial interpretability, but two obstacles prevent their direct use for weakly supervised semantic segmentation: (1) there is no mechanism to assign individual attention heads or [CLS] tokens to specific classes, and (2) redundant attention heads introduce noise that degrades mask quality. Traditional WSSS methods sidestep this by relying on external modules like Class Activation Maps, which produce only coarse localization and add architectural complexity.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (256×256 for DFC2020, variable resolution for other datasets) and produces two outputs: per-class binary predictions from the multi-[CLS] ViT, and pseudo segmentation masks generated by reshaping, thresholding, and combining the self-attention maps of the [CLS] tokens corresponding to predicted classes. These pseudo-masks then serve as training labels for a downstream UNet segmentation model, which produces the final pixel-level predictions.

## Method

The architecture replaces the single [CLS] token with C class-specific [CLS] tokens plus a [REG] token that absorbs background context. During training, a random masking strategy zeros out the output embeddings of [CLS] tokens whose classes are absent from the current image (50% masking ratio), forcing each token to specialize in a single class. Simultaneously, scalar gating units on each attention head are trained with a Hard Concrete distribution to approximate L0 sparsity, pruning roughly two-thirds of heads and sharpening the remaining attention maps. At inference, the self-attention maps of [CLS] tokens for predicted classes are reshaped to spatial dimensions, binarized via thresholding, and combined from lowest to highest logit probability, with unassigned pixels filled by majority-vote of neighbors.

## Results

On Pascal VOC 2012, the method achieves 72.7% mIoU (val) and 73.5% mIoU (test) for final segmentation, and 73.7% mIoU for pseudo-mask quality on the val set. On MS COCO val, it reaches 43.2% mIoU. On the specialized DFC2020 remote sensing dataset, it achieves 67.2% mIoU, surpassing even fully supervised baselines (best supervised: 53.1% mIoU). On EndoTect (medical) and ADE20K (general scene), it achieves 69.8% and 38.2% mIoU respectively, outperforming all weakly supervised baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among single-stage WSSS methods, the proposed approach outperforms DuPL [32] on VOC val (72.7% vs 72.2%) and VOC test (73.5% vs 71.6%), though DuPL edges ahead on COCO val (43.6% vs 43.2%). It also clearly surpasses ToCo [23] (72.7% vs 69.8% VOC val) and AFA [22] (72.7% vs 66.0% VOC val). Against multi-stage methods, I/C-CTI [34] remains stronger on VOC val (74.1% vs 72.7%) and COCO val (45.4% vs 43.2%), but the proposed method wins on VOC test (73.5% vs 73.2%) with a simpler single-stage pipeline. On the specialized DFC2020 dataset, the method achieves 67.2% mIoU, beating the best weakly supervised baseline Sparse ViT [9] (52.2%) by 15 points and even surpassing the best supervised UNet (53.1%). On EndoTect, it reaches 69.8% mIoU vs 68.0% for Sparse ViT [9], and on ADE20K it achieves 38.2% vs 34.5% for Sparse ViT [9].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Vision Transformer architecture (Dosovitskiy et al.) by replacing the single [CLS] token with C class-specific [CLS] tokens, and adopts the register token concept from Darcet et al. to prevent attention artifacts. It builds directly on the authors' prior Sparse ViT work (Hanna et al., CVPRW 2023) for attention head pruning via Hard Concrete distributions, but departs from the broader WSSS literature by eliminating the reliance on Class Activation Maps or external localization modules entirely. Unlike multi-stage methods (I/C-CTI, ReCAM) that iteratively refine pseudo-masks, this is a single-stage approach that reads class-specific segmentation directly from the self-attention of designated [CLS] tokens, with a novel random masking strategy to enforce one-to-one class-token assignment during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a clean, end-to-end pipeline for WSSS that avoids the complexity of CAM-based or iterative refinement approaches, and demonstrates that native ViT attention—properly structured—can serve as a direct segmentation signal. A notable practical finding is that on the small, imbalanced DFC2020 remote sensing dataset, the weakly supervised method (67.2% mIoU) substantially outperforms fully supervised baselines (53.1% for UNet), suggesting particular value in data-scarce specialized domains. However, the scalability concern is real: with 150 classes in ADE20K, the number of [CLS] tokens and associated attention computations grows linearly, and the paper acknowledges this as an open challenge. The modest gains on standard benchmarks (0.2–1.5% over DuPL) and the dependence on a downstream UNet for final segmentation limit the method's immediate impact on the broader WSSS landscape.
