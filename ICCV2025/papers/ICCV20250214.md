## Problem

Standard DETR enhancements such as increasing the number of queries or decoder layers, which reliably improve object detection, paradoxically degrade performance in temporal sentence grounding. The paper identifies two root causes: (1) queries corresponding to semantically similar target segments become indistinguishable, causing random one-to-one matching across layers, and (2) each query faces an internal tension between encoding global segment semantics and precisely localizing boundary frames.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250214/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an untrimmed video (224×224 resolution) and a natural language sentence as input. Video features are extracted by concatenating CLIP [CLS] tokens with SlowFast features, while text is encoded via the CLIP text encoder into token-level features. The decoder uses M queries (default 10) to produce a set of temporal spans (start, end), per-query classification confidence scores, and IoU predictions, with a total of 6 transformer layers matching recent DETR-based TSG baselines.

## Method

Sim-DETR extends the standard DETR-based TSG decoder with two targeted modifications. First, Query Grouping and Ranking (QGR) modulates inter-query self-attention using a learned mask derived from L2 span distances between queries and a ranking score (classification confidence × IoU prediction), encouraging indistinguishable queries to attend to different contexts while allowing high-quality queries to aggregate information from similar ones. Second, Global-Local Bridging (GLB) introduces a query-to-frame matching loss that computes cosine similarity between each query and all N frame features, pushing the query to align with every frame inside its ground-truth segment while suppressing similarity to out-of-segment frames, thereby bridging global semantics to local boundary prediction. The overall training objective combines the standard DETR losses (L1, gIoU, classification, saliency) with the bridge loss and an auxiliary IoU-head loss used for query ranking.

## Results

On QVHighlights (the primary benchmark), Sim-DETR achieves 69.48% R1@0.5 and 49.50% mAP (avg) on validation, and 67.64% R1@0.5 and 46.93% mAP (avg) on test, outperforming all prior methods. On Charades-STA, it reaches 61.34% R1@0.5 and 52.56% mIoU; on TACoS, 42.79% R1@0.5 and 39.44% mIoU. Notably, Sim-DETR also eliminates the anomaly where increasing queries or decoder layers degrades performance, and achieves faster convergence than prior DETR-based TSG methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sim-DETR outperforms all 17 compared baselines across every metric on all three benchmarks. On QVHighlights validation, it surpasses the strongest prior DETR-based method BAM-DETR [25] by +1.89% R1@0.5 and +1.89% mAP avg, and the Mamba-based SpikeMba [31] by +4.16% R1@0.5 and +4.66% mAP avg. Compared to its direct baseline TR-DETR [57] (AAAI'24), Sim-DETR gains +2.38% R1@0.5 and +4.41% mAP avg on validation. On Charades-STA, it beats the prior SOTA SpikeMba [31] by +1.69% R1@0.5 and +0.82% mIoU; on TACoS, it exceeds CG-DETR [46] by +3.18% R1@0.5 and +2.96% mIoU. The paper also shows that with VGG and I3D backbones (Table 3), Sim-DETR still achieves SOTA, surpassing the best prior method by +4.26% R1@0.5 with I3D, confirming generalizability across feature extractors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DETR-based temporal sentence grounding line (Moment-DETR, QD-DETR, CG-DETR, TR-DETR, TaskWeave) by diagnosing why standard DETR scaling strategies—more queries, more decoder layers—fail or degrade in TSG, unlike in object detection. It borrows the query-ranking idea from EASE-DETR (CVPR 2024) but adapts it to use IoU predictions rather than confidence scores, and introduces a novel query-to-frame bridging loss. The key conceptual departure from prior DETR-TSG work is the systematic identification of two distinct conflict sources (inter-query semantic similarity and intra-query global-vs-local tension) as the root cause of the abnormal behavior, rather than treating the decoder as a black box to be augmented with richer features or multi-task objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that increasing queries or decoder layers degrades TSG performance—opposite to object detection—challenges the assumption that DETR improvements transfer across detection-style tasks and provides a concrete diagnostic framework (query similarity analysis, cross-layer matching consistency, global-vs-local attention decomposition) that future TSG work should account for. The two modifications are minimal in implementation cost yet yield consistent gains across three benchmarks and multiple backbones, establishing a strong, simple baseline. Open questions include whether the identified conflicts persist in longer-video or multi-modal settings, whether the query-grouping mechanism generalizes to other query-based frameworks beyond DETR, and whether the global-local bridging loss can be extended to tasks requiring finer-grained temporal structure (e.g., action segmentation).
