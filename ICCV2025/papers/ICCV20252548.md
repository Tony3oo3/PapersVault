## Problem

Existing differentially private dataset distillation methods couple signal sampling with synthetic image optimization, forcing equal iteration counts that either under-converge the images or inject excessive DP noise. Additionally, these methods extract training signals from randomly initialized networks that capture uninformative details, yielding low signal-to-noise ratios that amplify the impact of added Gaussian noise. The paper asks how to better exploit limited private signals under a fixed (ε,δ)-DP budget to produce compact synthetic datasets with higher downstream accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a private labeled image dataset (28×28 grayscale for MNIST/FashionMNIST, 32×32 RGB for CIFAR-10) and an auxiliary dataset of 1,000 images per class generated by a generative model (Stable Diffusion v1-4 for CIFAR-10, or a DP-trained diffusion model consuming 0.2ε of the budget for MNIST/FashionMNIST). The output is a synthetic dataset of M images per class (M=10 or 50), initialized as random Gaussian noise and optimized to match DP-protected feature signals, all within a total (ε=1, δ=10⁻⁵) privacy budget. The auxiliary dataset is used only to compute a PCA projection matrix and incurs no additional privacy cost when sourced from a pre-trained model.

## Method

Dosser combines two mechanisms: (1) Decoupled Optimization and Sampling (DOS) separates the I₁=10,000 sampling iterations (where Poisson-sampled batches from the private data are passed through randomly initialized ConvNets, augmented, clipped, averaged, and perturbed with Gaussian noise) from the I₂=200,000 optimization iterations (where synthetic images are updated via gradient descent to match the stored noisy signals), allowing convergence without extra noise; (2) Subspace Discovery for Error Reduction (SER) projects training signals into a d=500-dimensional informative subspace identified by PCA on auxiliary-data features before noise injection, concentrating signal power on high-variance dimensions and improving SNR. Theorem 1 formally decomposes the MSE gain into projection residual, dimensional reduction effect, and projection error terms, showing that norm reduction and dimensionality reduction post-projection lower the required noise scale for the same (ε,δ) budget. The overall loss is the squared ℓ₂ distance between the noisy real aggregated signal and the projected synthetic signal, optimized with PEA augmentation for enhanced diversity.

## Results

Under (ε=1, δ=10⁻⁵)-DP, Dosser with PEA achieves 52.3% accuracy on CIFAR-10 at IPC=50, a 10.0-point improvement over the prior state-of-the-art NDPDC (42.3%), and 50.6% at IPC=10 (vs. 39.4% for NDPDC). On MNIST, Dosser w/ PEA reaches 96.7% at IPC=50, narrowing the gap to the non-private DM upper bound (99.2%) to just 2.5 points. On CIFAR-10 IPC=10, Dosser w/ PEA (50.6%) is only 1.5 points below the non-private DM baseline (52.1%), compared to NDPDC's 11.7-point gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dosser w/ PEA outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and IPC setting where comparisons are available. Against the closest competitor NDPDC [39], Dosser w/ PEA gains +10.0 points on CIFAR-10 IPC=50 (52.3 vs. 42.3), +11.2 points on CIFAR-10 IPC=10 (50.6 vs. 39.4), +4.3 points on FashionMNIST IPC=50 (83.1 vs. 78.8), and +2.6 points on MNIST IPC=50 (96.7 vs. 94.1). Against PSG [4], Dosser w/ PEA leads by +17.0 points on CIFAR-10 IPC=10 (50.6 vs. 33.6) and +17.8 points on MNIST IPC=10 (96.4 vs. 78.6). The kernel-based DP-KIP-ScatterNet [29] and the embedding-based DP-MERF [12] and DP-Sinkhorn [2] are substantially outperformed, with gaps exceeding 30 points on CIFAR-10 (52.3 vs. 9.5 for DP-KIP) and 12+ points on MNIST (96.7 vs. 84.4 for DP-MERF). Notably, Dosser w/ PEA on CIFAR-10 IPC=10 (50.6%) nearly matches the non-private DM upper bound (52.1%), whereas NDPDC lags by 11.7 points, demonstrating that the decoupled sampling and subspace projection effectively recover most of the utility lost to DP noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the matching-based differentially private dataset distillation line initiated by NDPDC (Zheng & Li, 2022) and PSG (Chen et al., 2022), which add calibrated Gaussian noise to features or gradients extracted from randomly initialized networks. The key conceptual departure is twofold: (1) decoupling the private-data sampling stage from the synthetic-image optimization stage (DOS), breaking the synchronization that forces equal iteration counts and thus excessive cumulative noise in prior methods; and (2) projecting signals into an informative subspace identified via PCA on auxiliary data from generative models (SER), which raises the signal-to-noise ratio before noise is injected. A clean MSE decomposition (Theorem 1) formalizes the trade-off between projection residual, dimensional reduction benefit, and projection error, providing theoretical grounding absent in prior DP-DD work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10% accuracy gain on CIFAR-10 (IPC=50) and the narrowing of the gap to non-private distribution matching from 11.7% to 1.5% (IPC=10) demonstrate that noise efficiency, not just budget allocation, is a critical bottleneck in private DD. However, the method is constrained to randomly initialized networks and small-scale datasets (MNIST, FashionMNIST, CIFAR-10), and the SER component requires an auxiliary dataset whose distribution closely matches the private data—limiting applicability to specialized or data-scarce domains. Open questions include adapting the decoupling and subspace ideas to pre-trained-model-based DD methods (e.g., trajectory matching, kernel methods) that scale to ImageNet, and whether the SNR gains from SER transfer to gradient-matching or other signal types beyond distribution matching.
