## Problem

Existing vision-language-motion models (VLMMs) lack practical controllability: they fail to handle arbitrary user commands, random initial poses, long-term or unseen motions, and fine-grained part-level control over individual body parts. This is compounded by the scarcity of large-scale, multi-task motion data and the absence of part-level annotations, making it difficult to train models that generalize beyond narrow text-to-motion settings while maintaining real-time inference speed.

## Input / Output

MotionCtrl accepts text instructions (arbitrary natural-language commands), initial 3D poses, video clips at 224×224 resolution (up to 64 frames), and partial motion sequences as input. It outputs 3D human motion as discrete tokens decoded frame-by-frame via PRQ (4 quantization layers × 5 body parts × 1024 codebook entries, temporal downsampling of 4), requiring at least 100 tokens/second to sustain 20 FPS. The motion representation uses HuMo263, which preserves original rotation information and avoids the slow inverse kinematics step required by HM3D263-format features.

## Method

MotionCtrl is a 7B-parameter VLMM built on LLaVA-video-7B (SigLIP visual encoder + 2-layer MLP projection + LLaMA-2-7B backbone with a slow-fast strategy for visual token reduction), treating motion as a foreign language via discrete tokens. The core novelty is Part-Aware Residual Quantization (PRQ), which decomposes each motion frame into five body-part features (left arm, right arm, left leg, right leg, torso) with shared joints, then independently quantizes each part through 4 residual layers against a single shared 1024-entry codebook—expanding representable motion space to ∏uⱼ without enlarging the codebook. Trained on HuMo100M (5M motions, 100M multi-task instructions with part-level, long-form, and text-aligned visual annotations) via three stages (motion-text alignment, vision-text-motion alignment, motion instruction tuning), the model supports five controllability tasks: Instruct-to-Motion, Motion Prediction & In-between, Instruct-to-LongMotion, Instruct-to-Unseen, and Instruct-to-PartMotion. Unlike prior RQ-based decoders that generate all layers before outputting any frame, MotionCtrl decodes frame-by-frame across all quantization layers simultaneously, enabling streaming real-time motion output at 20–29 FPS on modern GPUs.

## Results

On the HumanML3D text-to-motion benchmark, MotionCtrl-PRQ4 (LLaMA2-7B, 4 quantization layers) achieves FID 0.056, R-Precision@1 0.535, R-Precision@3 0.821, and MMDist 2.865, outperforming all prior LLM-based motion generators. On the Instruct-to-Motion (I2M) task using the HuMo-I2M testbed, MotionCtrl-PRQ4 reaches FID 0.148 and R@1 0.428, substantially surpassing MoMask-RQ6 (FID 0.324, R@1 0.325) and MotionGPT-2 (FID 0.314, R@1 0.336). For the Instruct-to-Unseen (I2U) task, scaling training data from HumanML3D to HuMo100M reduces FID from 65.04 to 8.65, demonstrating the critical role of data scale for generalization to unseen motions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MotionCtrl-PRQ4 outperforms all LLM-based baselines (LMM, MotionGPT-v2, MotionGPT-1/2, MotionLLM, AvatarGPT) on every metric in Table 1, with the largest gaps over MotionGPT-2 (FID 0.056 vs 0.542) and AvatarGPT (FID 0.056 vs 0.567). Against the strongest non-LLM baselines, MotionCtrl-PRQ4 beats ScaMo-FSQ on all four metrics (FID 0.056 vs 0.101, R@1 0.535 vs 0.512) and MoMask-RQ6 on R@1 (0.535 vs 0.521), R@3 (0.821 vs 0.807), and MMDist (2.865 vs 2.958), though MoMask achieves a marginally lower FID (0.045 vs 0.056) using 6 quantization layers versus MotionCtrl's 4. On the I2M task (Table 2), MotionCtrl-PRQ4 achieves FID 0.148 and R@1 0.428, roughly doubling the R@1 of MoMask-RQ6 (0.325) and MotionGPT-2 (0.336), confirming that the 7B LLM backbone is essential for interpreting arbitrary human instructions. On the I2U generalization task, training on HuMo100M reduces FID from 65.04 (HumanML3D) to 8.65, a 7.5× improvement, while Motion-X training yields an intermediate FID of 43.28. In the tokenizer ablation (Table 4), PRQ4 with only 1024 codebook entries achieves FID 0.007 and MPJPE 14.06 mm on HumanML3D, surpassing RQ-VAE8 (FID 0.009, MPJPE 20.42) and FSQ with 65,536 entries (FID 0.051, MPJPE 35.04) by a wide margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

MotionCtrl builds on the LLaVA-video-7B framework for its vision-language backbone and extends residual vector quantization (RQ) from MoMask into a part-aware variant (PRQ) that decomposes whole-body motion into five limb-specific code sequences. It departs from prior VLMMs (LMM, MotionGPT, MotionGPT-2) by reframing motion generation as a multi-task, controllable instruction-following problem rather than a single text-to-motion mapping, and by switching from layer-by-layer to frame-by-frame decoding to enable real-time streaming. The HuMo100M dataset addresses a data-scaling gap that prior web-video motion datasets (MotionBank, Wang et al.) left open by adding part-level descriptions, concatenated long-term sequences, and text-aligned visual clips.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combination of a 5M-motion / 100M-instruction dataset, part-level controllability, and real-time inference (≥20 FPS on consumer GPUs) makes this one of the first VLMMs that could plausibly be deployed in interactive animation or humanoid-control pipelines. Open questions include whether the web-video-derived motions (extracted via WHAM + PHC) are reliable enough for high-fidelity downstream use, whether the 7B backbone is truly the optimal size for motion tasks, and how far the visual modality can be leveraged beyond weak supervision. The finding that deeper quantization layers improve reconstruction but degrade generation performance suggests a fundamental tension in autoregressive motion tokenization that the field has not yet resolved.
