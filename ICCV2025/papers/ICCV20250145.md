## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods for vision-language models like CLIP require large batch sizes, extensive data augmentations, and gradient-based optimization, making them impractical for resource-constrained online deployment. The paper asks whether token condensation—originally designed purely for ViT inference efficiency—can be repurposed as a training-free adaptation strategy that improves visual-text alignment on unseen datasets while simultaneously reducing computational cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250145/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

TCA operates in a strict online setting with batch size 1 and no data augmentation, processing RGB images sequentially as ViT patch tokens (e.g., 196 patches for 224×224 input with ViT-B/16) alongside precomputed text class prompt embeddings. The output is a refined probability vector over C classes, where the standard CLIP cosine-similarity logits are augmented by a token-level correction term derived from cross-layer cosine similarities between the current sample's <cls> tokens and stored domain anchor tokens. As a byproduct, the number of tokens processed in later ViT layers is reduced by a factor R (e.g., 0.9 or 0.7), lowering GFLOPs by 12–49%.

## Method

TCA introduces three coupled mechanisms that transform standard token pruning into a domain-adaptive procedure. First, a Domain-aware Token Reservoir (DTR) maintains a per-class priority queue of <cls> tokens (or pooled vectors for SigLIP) from the lowest-entropy test samples, providing stable domain anchor tokens that implicitly distill semantic information from text prompts. Second, Domain-aware Cross-head Token Reduction replaces EViT's single averaged attention score with a refined attention map that concatenates the current <cls> token with the retrieved domain anchor as an additional query/key, then computes per-head ranking positions and averages them to achieve robust cross-head consensus for pruning class-irrelevant tokens and merging class-ambiguous ones via coreset selection. Third, Logits Self-correction uses the stored domain anchor tokens as a token-level classifier, adding a layer-weighted cosine-similarity term to the predicted probabilities to pull visual embeddings toward text embeddings without modifying any model parameters. The entire pipeline is training-free, requires only a handful of hyperparameters (reservoir size M, retention ratio R, pruning fraction α, correction weight λ, and layer-scaling β), and generalizes to SigLIP and SigLIP v2 by substituting pooled attention weights for the <cls> token.

## Results

On the cross-dataset benchmark (10 fine-grained datasets) with CLIP ViT-B/16, TCA (R=0.9) achieves 68.69% average accuracy, outperforming the strongest training-free baseline TDA (67.53%) and the base CLIP (64.59%) while reducing GFLOPs by 12.2% (15.45 vs. 17.59). The largest single-dataset gain is on EuroSAT, where TCA reaches 70.43% versus TDA's 58.00% (+21.4% relative). On CIFAR-100-Corrupted, TCA consistently improves over CLIP across all five severity levels of three corruption types (e.g., +18.59% at severity 5 for contrast), whereas EViT degrades. TCA also generalizes to SigLIP (+2.21% avg) and SigLIP v2 (+1.32% avg) without any parameter updates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TCA outperforms all 15 baselines on average. Against the strongest training-free method TDA (67.53%), TCA gains +1.16% average accuracy while using fewer hyperparameters and 12.2% fewer GFLOPs; the gap widens to +12.43% on EuroSAT. Conventional TTA methods (Tent 59.06%, SAR 62.32%) and prompt-tuning methods (CoOp 63.88%, CoCoOp 64.63%) all underperform TCA by 4–10% on average, with Tent suffering the largest drop due to BN-layer tuning instability on fine-grained data. Test-time prompting methods (TPT 65.10%, C-TPT 65.47%, Diff-TPT 65.47%) require 60× more GFLOPs (1108.61) from heavy augmentation yet still trail TCA by 3.2–3.6%. Among token-reduction baselines, EViT (65.17%) and ToME (64.88%) at R=0.9 improve slightly over CLIP but fall 3.5–3.8% short of TCA, while ATS (61.63%) degrades significantly. On SigLIP and SigLIP v2, TCA adds +2.21% and +1.32% average respectively, with EuroSAT showing the largest gains (+21.12% and +11.17%), confirming cross-architecture generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes token condensation techniques originally designed for ViT inference efficiency (EViT, ToME, ATS) as a test-time adaptation mechanism for vision-language models. While prior TTA methods for CLIP/SigLIP (Tent, SAR, TPT, C-TPT, TDA) either require gradient-based tuning, large batch sizes, or extensive data augmentation, TCA operates entirely training-free with batch size 1 by selectively pruning and merging low-attentiveness tokens guided by a domain-aware token reservoir. The key conceptual departure from both lines of work is the observation that removing class-irrelevant and class-ambiguous tokens improves visual-text alignment on unseen distributions, rather than merely preserving in-distribution performance at lower compute cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TCA offers a practically attractive deployment story: no training, no augmentation, batch size 1, and reduced GFLOPs while improving accuracy, making it suitable for resource-constrained online inference. However, the average accuracy gains on the cross-dataset benchmark are modest (~1–2% over CLIP), and the method's effectiveness depends on sequential test data arriving to populate the reservoir, limiting its utility in truly single-sample or offline settings. The paper acknowledges the absence of a rigorous theoretical explanation for why token condensation aids alignment, and performance degrades sharply under aggressive pruning budgets (R < 0.8), leaving open questions about the fundamental relationship between token sparsity and cross-modal alignment in VLMs.
