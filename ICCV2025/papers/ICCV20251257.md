## Problem

Existing layout-to-image generation methods require millions of semantic layout annotations (bounding boxes paired with regional prompts), which are expensive to produce and suffer from conflicting descriptions when regions overlap. This paper asks whether the latest diffusion transformers (SD3, FLUX) truly need such extensive regional prompt annotations, and shows they do not.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251257/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a global text prompt plus a set of bounding boxes (anonymous layout) or bounding boxes with per-region prompts (semantic layout), and produces a 1024×1024 RGB image with objects placed according to the specified layout. Regional prompts are optional: the model can operate with just boxes and a global prompt, or with full per-region descriptions. At inference, the layout is encoded as downsampled cropped regional tokens concatenated with global visual tokens, incurring only minimal additional computational cost.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-stage hybrid training pipeline that decomposes layout control into: (1) anonymous layout control (learning to place objects within designated regions using only bounding boxes, no regional prompts), (2) semantic layout control (fine-tuning on a much smaller set of regionally-prompted samples), and (3) quality-tuning on a small high-aesthetic dataset. Architecturally, the method introduces a regional diffusion transformer that crops regions from the latent feature map, applies bilinear downsampling to the regional tokens, and concatenates them with global tokens using shared positional encodings so that denoised regional content propagates back to the global image. A shared Gaussian noise map is used for both global and regional tokens, and a logit-normal sampling schedule with a smaller mean emphasizes high-noise steps for better layout adherence. This design achieves >10× data efficiency (200K vs. 2.7M samples) while outperforming SiamLayout on layout fidelity and aesthetics.

## Results

On LayoutSAM-Eval, the proposed method with FLUX and only 10% of the training data (200K samples vs. SiamLayout's 2.7M) achieves the best layout quality scores: Spatial 95.81, Color 89.95, Texture 91.81, and Shape 91.61, surpassing SiamLayout-FLUX (95.67/80.71/83.53/82.80) by 9+ points on Color, Texture, and Shape. With quality-tuning, the method further improves Image Reward from 65.21 to 87.96, exceeding SiamLayout-FLUX (80.48). On COCO, the FLUX-based method achieves AP/AP50/AR of 55.2/79.1/51.8, outperforming SiamLayout-FLUX (46.9/67.1/40.9) by a large margin. The user study confirms a 63.17% win rate for aesthetics, 50.93% for layout following, and 58.51% for prompt following over SiamLayout.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SiamLayout-FLUX (the primary SOTA), the proposed method with only 10% of the training data wins decisively on regional prompt-following metrics: Color (89.95 vs. 80.71), Texture (91.81 vs. 83.53), and Shape (91.61 vs. 82.80), while matching Spatial (95.81 vs. 95.67). Without quality-tuning, the method trails on Image Reward (65.21 vs. 80.48) and FID (20.97 vs. 16.12), but quality-tuning closes the IR gap (87.96 vs. 80.48) at the cost of slightly worse FID (19.98 vs. 16.12). On COCO, the FLUX-based method dominates SiamLayout-FLUX on detection-style metrics (AP 55.2 vs. 46.9, AP50 79.1 vs. 67.1, AR 51.8 vs. 40.9). On T2I-CompBench, the method wins on Color (79.60 vs. 75.03), Shape (58.70 vs. 53.04), Texture (68.82 vs. 60.35), and Numeracy (62.74 vs. 54.67) but loses on Spatial (32.08 vs. 38.35). Compared to earlier baselines like GLIGEN, InstanceDiff, and HiCo, the proposed method leads on all four layout quality dimensions by 8–40 points. The training-free Regional-Prompting-FLUX baseline is outperformed on all layout metrics and matches on image quality, confirming that the fine-tuned hybrid approach provides superior spatial control.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the anonymous regional diffusion transformer design from ART and Prismlayers, repurposing cropped regional tokens (originally for multi-layer transparent image generation) as a lightweight layout encoding mechanism for single-image layout-to-image generation. It departs from the dominant paradigm of layout control methods (GLIGEN, SiamLayout, InstanceDiff) that require millions of semantic layout annotations with per-region prompts, instead decomposing the task into a three-stage pipeline: anonymous layout learning, semantic refinement, and quality tuning. The key conceptual departure is the argument that modern diffusion transformers (SD3, FLUX) do not fundamentally need regional prompts for layout adherence, a claim supported by ablations showing only marginal degradation when regional prompts are removed from a well-trained SiamLayout model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× data efficiency (200K vs. 2.7M semantic layout samples) and reduced annotation burden have direct practical implications for production systems (the paper is co-authored with Canva), making layout-controlled generation more accessible. The finding that a dominant anonymous-layout stage (80% compute) plus a small semantic stage (20%) matches or exceeds full semantic training challenges the field's assumption that fine-grained regional descriptions are essential for layout control in modern DiTs. Open questions include whether this decomposition generalizes to more complex multi-object scenes, video generation, or 3D layout control, and whether the quality-tuning stage can be further automated to reduce the remaining human annotation effort.
