## Problem

CLIP's contrastive image-text training objective produces incoherent patch embeddings, causing inter-class patch correlations that aggregate information from unrelated categories and degrade segmentation discriminability. Existing methods modify self-attention to improve OVSS but do so without explicitly identifying which patch correlations are harmful or confining the scope of patch interactions, leaving a systematic gap in how inter-class correlations are regulated.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252124/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 336 px shorter side for VOC/Context/COCO, 448 px for Cityscapes/ADE20K) and a set of category names combined with standard ImageNet prompts as text input. It produces a pixel-wise segmentation map via slide inference with a 336×336 window and 112×112 stride. SAM2 generates region masks from a 32×32 grid of prompt points, which are downsampled to match the CLIP feature map resolution (e.g., 42×42 for ViT-B/16) and used throughout the pipeline for scope reconstruction and map correction.

## Method

CorrCLIP builds on ClearCLIP's query-query attention (with residual connections and FFN removed in the last layer) and introduces four components. Scope reconstruction uses SAM-generated region masks, merged via DBSCAN clustering on mask-averaged features, to build a binary interaction matrix that restricts patch attention to within-region pairs via masked softmax, directly eliminating most inter-class correlations. Value reconstruction replaces CLIP's incoherent similarity with a normalized similarity computed from DINO's combined query+key embeddings, sharpened by a temperature coefficient (τ=0.25) to further suppress residual inter-class weights. Feature refinement adds a spatial branch (injecting lower-layer value features) and a semantic branch (mask-specific class tokens that attend only to their region's patches), combined with coefficients α=1 and β=0.5. Finally, map correction applies a majority-vote relabeling within each region mask to enforce spatial consistency.

## Results

CorrCLIP achieves an averaged mIoU of 53.6% on ViT-L/14 across eight OVSS benchmarks, surpassing the previous best training-free method (SC-CLIP at 45.2%) by 8.4 percentage points. On ViT-B/16, it reaches 51.0% avg mIoU (+5.2% over Trident's 45.8%), and on ViT-H/14 it reaches 52.3% (+3.7% over Trident's 48.6%). The largest per-benchmark gains are on Cityscapes (+9.8% to 51.1%) and ADE20K (+6.3% to 30.7%) with ViT-L/14, while VOC21 reaches 76.7% mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CorrCLIP (ViT-L/14) outperforms all 13 training-free baselines across all eight benchmarks, with the largest margins over SC-CLIP (+8.4% avg), FreeDA (+9.7%), and ProxyCLIP (+10.6%). Among methods that also leverage external foundation models, Trident (ViT-H/14, 48.6% avg) is the closest competitor, yet CorrCLIP still leads by 3.7–5.0 points on most benchmarks. Against training-based methods, CorrCLIP surpasses weakly-supervised approaches (TCL 37.2%, CLIP-DINOiser 40.3%, CoDe) and narrows the gap to fully-supervised models (CAT-Seg, ESC-Net), which report only partial benchmark coverage. Notably, CorrCLIP's advantage is most pronounced on fine-grained benchmarks: +9.8% on Cityscapes, +6.3% on ADE20K, and +6.6% on Pascal Context, suggesting that scope reconstruction is particularly effective where inter-class confusion is highest. The paper also shows that CorrCLIP's gains scale positively with CLIP's zero-shot classification accuracy (Table 7), unlike ClearCLIP or raw CLIP, indicating the method fully exploits stronger backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ClearCLIP's decomposition of CLIP's final layer (query-query attention, removal of FFN and residual connections) as its baseline, and extends the line of work that modifies self-attention for dense prediction (SCLIP, ProxyCLIP, SC-CLIP, ResCLIP). Its key departure is an explicit empirical diagnosis that inter-class correlations—not merely incoherent features—are the primary bottleneck, followed by a structured two-part solution: scope reconstruction (confining patch interactions within SAM-generated region masks) and value reconstruction (computing similarity from DINO's query+key embeddings for more coherent attention weights). Unlike prior methods that implicitly reduce inter-class correlations through architectural tweaks or thresholding, CorrCLIP makes the scope of interactions an explicit, mask-defined constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear mechanistic explanation for why CLIP underperforms in segmentation (inter-class correlations in self-attention) and shows that explicitly regulating the interaction scope yields large, consistent gains (5–8% mIoU) across three CLIP scales and eight benchmarks without any training. A notable open question is the computational cost of SAM2 mask generation and the quantization error from downsampling masks to patch resolution; the authors show performance improves with higher mask resolution but stop at 42×42 due to memory constraints. The finding that CorrCLIP's gains scale with CLIP's zero-shot accuracy (unlike ClearCLIP) suggests the method unlocks latent capability in stronger CLIP variants, pointing toward future work combining better pre-trained backbones with structured attention.
