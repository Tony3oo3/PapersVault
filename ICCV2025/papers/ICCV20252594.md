## Problem

Aligning text-to-image models with human preferences via DPO requires large-scale, human-annotated preference datasets (e.g., Pick-a-Picv2 costs ~$50K) that become outdated as T2I models rapidly improve. Moreover, existing DPO formulations only exploit pairwise preferences, discarding the richer ranking signal available when multiple images are generated per prompt. This paper addresses both the cost/scalability bottleneck and the underutilized ranking structure in preference optimization for diffusion-based T2I models.

## Input / Output

At inference, the fine-tuned model (SDXL or SD3-Medium) takes a text prompt and generates a 1024×1024 RGB image. For training, the Syn-Pic dataset provides 58K prompts, each with 4 images generated by different T2I models (SDXL, SD3-Medium, PixArt-Σ, Stable Cascade), yielding 240K images total. Each image carries an aggregate preference score computed by averaging win-rates across 5 reward models, producing a ranked list per prompt rather than a single winning/losing pair.

## Method

The method has two components. First, Syn-Pic is a fully synthetic preference dataset where images from 4 T2I models are scored by an ensemble of 5 reward models (HPSv2.1, MPS, PickScore, VQAScore, ImageReward); the aggregate score φ(x) for each image is the fraction of pairwise wins across all reward models, and images are sorted by φ to form a ranked preference list per prompt. Second, RankDPO extends the Diffusion-DPO objective by weighting each pairwise term with a learning-to-rank coefficient Δ_{i,j} = |G_i − G_j| · |1/D(τ(i)) − 1/D(τ(j))|, where G_i = 2^{φ(i)} − 1 is the gain and D(τ(i)) = log(1+τ(i)) is a logarithmic discount on the predicted rank. The score s_i for each image is the difference between the current model's and reference model's denoising errors at a random timestep, and the weighted sum over all i > j pairs encourages the model to respect the full ranking rather than only the top pairwise comparison, with higher-ranked images receiving larger penalties when misranked.

## Results

On GenEval, RankDPO improves the mean score from 0.55 to 0.61 for SDXL and from 0.70 to 0.74 for SD3-Medium, with the largest gains in 'two objects' (+0.12 for SDXL), 'counting' (+0.09 for SD3-Medium), and 'color attribution' (+0.06 for SD3-Medium). On T2I-CompBench, SDXL gains over 10 points on Color (58.79→72.33) and Texture (52.99→69.67). On DPG-Bench with long prompts, RankDPO-SDXL achieves the best prompt alignment (DSG 79.26, VQA 87.52) and visual quality (Q-Align 0.81) among all compared methods, outperforming DPO-SDXL (DSG 76.74) and SPO (DSG 74.73).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RankDPO outperforms Diffusion-DPO (Li et al.) on GenEval mean (0.61 vs 0.59 for SDXL; 0.74 vs 0.72 for SD3-Medium) and on DPG-Bench DSG score (79.26 vs 76.74), while using 3× fewer training images. Against CaPO (Hong et al.), which was trained on 1.6M images (8× Syn-Pic), RankDPO still achieves higher GenEval mean (0.61 vs 0.59 for SDXL; 0.74 vs 0.71 for SD3-Medium) and substantially better DPG-Bench DSG (79.26 vs 74.53). SPO (Liang et al.) improves visual quality (Q-Align 0.82) but lags on prompt alignment (DSG 74.73 vs 79.26). Among non-preference-optimized models, DALL-E 3 remains the strongest on GenEval mean (0.67) and DPG-Bench DSG (83.50), but RankDPO-SDXL surpasses it on VQA score (87.52 vs not reported) and Q-Align (0.81 vs not reported). For SD3-Medium, which was already DPO-optimized with 3M human preferences, RankDPO still yields further gains (0.70→0.74 GenEval mean; 85.54→86.78 DSG), demonstrating that synthetic ranked preferences complement existing human-annotated alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., 2024) by replacing its pairwise Bradley-Terry objective with a ranking-aware loss that borrows DCG-weighted LambdaLoss from the learning-to-rank literature (Burges et al., 2006; Liu et al., 2024). It simultaneously departs from the standard practice of human-annotated preference datasets (e.g., Pick-a-Picv2) by introducing Syn-Pic, a fully synthetic dataset where preferences are generated by ensembling five off-the-shelf reward models. The conceptual lineage traces from LLM preference optimization (DPO, LIPO) through diffusion-model alignment, positioning the work at the intersection of scalable data collection and richer preference signals for generative model fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a ~$200 synthetic dataset outperforms the ~$50K human-annotated Pick-a-Picv2, and a 240K-image synthetic set further improves SD3-Medium beyond its original 3M human-preference DPO training, suggesting that data quality and signal richness (rankings, multi-model diversity) can compensate for scale. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to video generation, whether the reward-model ensemble introduces systematic biases that compound over iterative re-training, and whether the ranking loss gains (modest, ~0.4 DSG over plain DPO) persist at larger dataset scales. The method is likely to become a standard post-training recipe given its low cost and plug-and-play nature.
