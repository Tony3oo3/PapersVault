## Problem

Existing sketch-to-image diffusion adapters (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, IP-Adapter) condition on a single global text prompt, causing 'attribute confusion' where fine-grained properties of one garment (e.g., a floral pattern) incorrectly leak to another garment in the generated outfit. No prior method supports multiple localized sketch-text pairs as conditioning inputs while preserving per-item attribute fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252095/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a variable number N of localized sketch-text pairs, where each pair consists of a binary sketch mask S_i ∈ {0,1}^{H×W} and a natural-language description T_i, plus a single global text prompt T_g specifying style/background. The local sketches must be spatially coherent (their union forms the global outfit sketch). The output is a 512×512 RGB fashion image in which each garment's attributes are rendered only in the spatial region defined by its associated sketch, without leaking to other garments.

## Method

LOTS processes each sketch-text pair independently through a Pair-Former module: sketches are encoded by a frozen DINOv2 ViT-S/14, text by a dual OpenCLIP ViT-bigG + CLIP ViT-L encoder, and a self-attention layer with k learnable pooling tokens compresses each pair into a fixed-size token sequence in a shared latent space. These per-pair token sequences are concatenated (never merged) and injected into a frozen SDXL denoising network via additional learnable cross-attention layers placed alongside the existing text cross-attention at every denoising step, so that multi-condition fusion is deferred across the iterative diffusion process rather than performed in a single pooling operation. This modular, step-based merging strategy prevents inter-pair interference and is the key mechanism that mitigates attribute confusion. The adapter is lightweight and trained end-to-end on the newly introduced Sketchy dataset (47k images, 79k garment-level annotations built on Fashionpedia), while the base diffusion model and modality encoders remain frozen.

## Results

On the Sketchy test set, LOTS achieves state-of-the-art performance across most metrics: GlobalCLIP 0.679 (+3.4% over fine-tuned ControlNet at 0.645), LocalCLIP 0.813, VQAScore 0.749 (tied with T2I-Adapter), and SSIM 0.678, while attaining the second-lowest FID of 0.79 (behind only IP-Adapter's 0.69). In the human evaluation study, LOTS achieves the highest F1 score of 0.722 for attribute localization (vs. 0.690 for SDXL and 0.614 for Multi-T2I-Adapter) and the highest Precision of 0.813, indicating superior resistance to attribute confusion. The method uses localized sketch-text pairs (L/L conditioning) rather than the global sketch + global text (G/G) or local sketch + global text (L/G) used by all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LOTS outperforms all fine-tuned baselines on GlobalCLIP (0.679 vs. 0.645 for ControlNet, 0.621 for IP-Adapter) and LocalCLIP (0.813 vs. 0.801 for ControlNet, 0.787 for IP-Adapter), confirming superior semantic alignment at both global and garment-level granularity. On VQAScore, LOTS ties T2I-Adapter at 0.749 and surpasses Multi-T2I-Adapter (0.734) and ControlNet (0.717), while on SSIM it leads all baselines (0.678 vs. 0.631 for IP-Adapter, 0.612 for T2I-Adapter). The only metric where LOTS is not best is FID, where fine-tuned IP-Adapter achieves a lower 0.69 vs. LOTS's 0.79, though IP-Adapter sacrifices compositional alignment (VQAScore 0.714, LocalCLIP 0.787). In the human evaluation, LOTS achieves the highest F1 (0.722) and Precision (0.813), beating SDXL (F1 0.690) by +3.1% and Multi-T2I-Adapter (F1 0.614) by +10.8%, with the gap driven primarily by Precision—indicating LOTS localizes attributes to the correct garment far more reliably than baselines that suffer from attribute leakage. Zero-shot baselines (SD, SDXL, GLIGEN) are consistently weaker across all metrics, with GLIGEN's VQAScore dropping to 0.419 due to its inability to handle sketch conditioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

LOTS extends the adapter-based conditioning paradigm established by IP-Adapter and T2I-Adapter, which inject external guidance into frozen diffusion models via cross-attention, but departs fundamentally from their single-global-sketchn-plus-single-global-text formulation. Prior controllable generation methods (ControlNet, AnyControl, Multi-T2I-Adapter) treat multiple spatial conditions as independent channels or pooled features, while LOTS introduces a pair-centric architecture where each sketch is tightly coupled with its own textual description through a self-attention Pair-Former, and the multi-condition merge is deferred to the iterative denoising process rather than performed in a single pooling step. The Sketchy dataset, built on Fashionpedia, is the first to provide multiple localized sketch-text pairs per image, filling a gap that prior fashion generation benchmarks (which offer only global sketches or global text) cannot support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and practically important failure mode in fashion design workflows—attribute confusion, where a floral pattern meant for a top leaks onto the shorts—by showing that pair-level isolation plus deferred merging in the diffusion loop substantially reduces this problem (F1 +3.1% over SDXL in human evaluation). The Sketchy dataset provides a reusable benchmark for localized multi-condition generation in fashion, a domain where fine-grained per-garment control is essential. However, the method remains limited to visually grounded attributes and struggles with abstract stylistic concepts (e.g., 'summer-vibe') due to CLIP's encoding limitations, pointing to a need for more expressive textual encoders. The approach is also domain-specific (fashion), and whether the pair-centric deferral strategy generalizes to other multi-object compositional generation tasks remains an open question.
