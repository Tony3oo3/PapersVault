## Problem

Existing memorization detection for diffusion models relies on exhaustive sampling, nearest-neighbor search in training sets, or prompt-based detection, all of which are incomplete, unreliable, or restricted to specific conditions. There is no instance-level measure that is commensurable across samples, works for both unconditional and conditional models, and captures worst-case memorization from an adversarial standpoint rather than only the training-caption-triggered case.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252514/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single target image (and, for text-to-image models, a prompt tokenized into at most M tokens) and produces a scalar memorization score defined as the per-dimension KL divergence between the inverted noise distribution and the standard Gaussian prior. The inverted noise is parameterized as a multivariate Gaussian with learnable mean and diagonal variance, yielding a closed-form score; if the sensitivity constraint (all generated samples replicate the target) cannot be met, the score is reported as +infinity. For text-to-image models, the prompt is simultaneously inverted as a per-token categorical distribution over the vocabulary.

## Method

InvMM inverts a target image into a sensitive latent noise distribution q_φ(ε₀|x₀) such that any noise drawn from it, when fed through the diffusion sampler, reproduces the image. The memorization score is the minimum KL divergence of q_φ to the standard Gaussian prior, subject to the constraint that sensitivity S(q_φ)=1 (all samples replicate the target). To solve this constrained optimization, the authors formulate a Lagrangian combining an x₀-prediction denoising loss (proxy for sensitivity) and the KL normality term, then adaptively tune the Lagrange multiplier λ using an additive-increase/multiplicative-decrease schedule inspired by TCP congestion control: λ is halved when denoising error stalls and incremented by a small δ otherwise. For text-to-image models, the prompt is jointly inverted via Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization of per-token categorical distributions, enabling the measure to discover adversarial prompts that trigger replication even when the original training caption does not.

## Results

On DDPM (CIFAR-10), InvMM achieves AUC 0.997 and TPR@1%FPR 0.899 for detecting highly memorized images, substantially outperforming the ε₀-loss baseline (AUC 0.981, TPR@1%FPR 0.414) and the leave-one-out differential metric M^LOO (AUC 0.418, TPR@1%FPR 0.000). On SD v1.4 (LAION), InvMM reaches AUC 0.974 and TPR@1%FPR 0.908, matching or exceeding state-of-the-art prompt-based detectors Wen (AUC 0.999, TPR@1%FPR 0.868) and Ren (AUC 0.975, TPR@1%FPR 0.355) while operating without requiring training captions. The adaptive algorithm successfully inverts all images, whereas fixing λ=1 fails on 92.4% (CelebAHQ) and 99.7% (FFHQ) of images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the leave-one-out differential metric M^LOO [55], InvMM dominates on DDPM (AUC 0.997 vs. 0.418; TPR@1%FPR 0.899 vs. 0.000), confirming that likelihood-based differential measures are not commensurable across samples. On SD v1.4, InvMM slightly trails Wen [61] on AUC (0.974 vs. 0.999) but surpasses it on TPR@1%FPR (0.908 vs. 0.868), and substantially outperforms Ren [42] on TPR@1%FPR (0.908 vs. 0.355) while matching on AUC (0.974 vs. 0.975). The paper argues that Wen and Ren's prompt-based formulation is overly optimistic because it only tests whether training captions trigger replication, whereas InvMM searches over all potential prompts and reveals that images in the 'suspicious' set (not triggered by their captions) are also heavily memorized. Compared to the membership inference metric SecMI [13] on FFHQ-6k, InvMM shows a positive correlation but a consistent performance gap, demonstrating that membership does not deterministically imply high memorization—some hold-out images are more memorized than some training images due to the gap between model loss and perceptual similarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDPM/LDM denoising framework and the Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization, but departs fundamentally from prior memorization detection approaches (Somepalli et al., Webster, Wen et al., Ren et al.) that rely on random sampling, nearest-neighbor search, or fixed training captions. Instead of inverting to a single latent vector or detecting replication under a specific prompt, InvMM inverts to a *distribution* of sensitive noise vectors and measures memorization as the minimum KL divergence to the standard Gaussian prior, making the score commensurable across samples. The adaptive weight-tuning algorithm, inspired by TCP congestion control, is a novel optimization strategy for balancing the sensitivity constraint against normality, addressing a practical difficulty that fixed-weight approaches cannot resolve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is that InvMM reveals a more adversarial and complete picture of memorization than prompt-based detection: images not triggered by their training captions can still be replicated by other (adversarial) prompts, meaning current safety audits based on training captions substantially underestimate risk. The paper also clarifies a conceptual distinction between memorization and membership inference that had been conflated in prior work. The main limitation is the assumption that the sensitive noise distribution is multivariate Gaussian, which the authors acknowledge is likely an oversimplification; future work could employ generative networks to model the true (potentially multimodal) sensitive noise distribution. As an auditing tool, it has direct applicability for developers seeking to validate safety guarantees before deployment.
