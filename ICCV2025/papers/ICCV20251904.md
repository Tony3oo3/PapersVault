## Problem

Existing low-light image enhancement methods predominantly adjust global brightness distribution, which causes overexposure in already-normal regions when the input image has uneven illumination. This leads to loss of original usable information in the enhanced output. The paper addresses this by seeking a mechanism to precisely regulate local brightness smoothness during enhancement without sacrificing detail recovery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251904/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-light RGB image, converts it to HSB color space, and decomposes it into a brightness channel (B) and a two-channel color map (HS) that serve as conditioning inputs to the diffusion model. Training uses randomly cropped 192×288 patches from paired low-light/high-light images, and inference employs DDIM with 8 denoising steps (T=2000 forward steps) to produce the enhanced high-light RGB image. The two-channel decomposition is key: it avoids the color-shifting artifact that arises when diffusion models predict biased means across three RGB channels independently.

## Method

BSCNet is a diffusion model with a one-encoder, two-decoder branch architecture that decouples brightness enhancement (B channel) from color restoration (HS channel) to prevent cross-task interference. A Bezier surface is constructed from the ground-truth brightness map by dividing it into n×n patches and using patch-mean brightness values as control points; the L1 distance between the predicted brightness map and this surface serves as a smoothness constraint (weighted by λ=0.01) that prevents overexposure in unevenly lit regions. To compensate for high-frequency detail loss inherent in the Bezier surface constraint, a Spatial-Frequency Reconstruction Module (SFRM) applies a Fourier transform to shift features into the frequency domain, uses cross-attention to fuse spatial and frequency features (preserving phase-driven detail while maintaining magnitude-driven structure), and outputs a PReLU-activated convolution. The total loss combines the standard diffusion noise-prediction L1 loss with the Bezier surface brightness loss, and the network is trained with Adam at 1e-4 learning rate for 320k iterations.

## Results

BSCNet achieves PSNR of 28.07, 28.33, 30.06, 31.49, 33.34, and 27.95 on LOLv1, LOLv2-real, LOLv2-synthetic, SDSD-indoor, SDSD-outdoor, and FiveK respectively, with corresponding SSIM values of 0.923, 0.904, 0.962, 0.910, 0.900, and 0.937. These represent improvements of 2.91 dB, 5.38 dB, 3.87 dB, 1.37 dB, 3.34 dB, and 2.14 dB over the strongest prior methods on each dataset. On the no-reference generalization datasets (DICM, LIME, MEF, NPE, VV), BSCNet also achieves the best or near-best NIQE and BRISQUE scores, confirming strong zero-shot transfer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BSCNet outperforms all 21 compared baselines on every evaluated dataset. On LOLv1, it surpasses the previous best (RetinexFormer, 25.16 PSNR) by 2.91 dB; on LOLv2-real, it beats MambaLLIE (22.95) by 5.38 dB; on LOLv2-synthetic, it exceeds DPEC (26.19) by 3.87 dB; on SDSD-indoor, it leads MambaLLIE (30.12) by 1.37 dB; and on SDSD-outdoor, it surpasses MambaLLIE (30.00) by 3.34 dB. On FiveK, BSCNet (27.95 PSNR) improves over the strongest baseline PyDiff (25.81) by 2.14 dB. The SSIM gains are equally consistent, with BSCNet reaching 0.923 on LOLv1 versus 0.845 for RetinexFormer and 0.962 on LOLv2-synthetic versus 0.940 for MambaLLIE. On the no-reference generalization benchmarks (DICM, LIME, MEF, NPE, VV), BSCNet achieves the best NIQE on four of five datasets and the best BRISQUE on all five, confirming that the Bezier surface constraint and task decoupling generalize well beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the diffusion model framework for image-to-image translation (DDPM/DDIM) and applies it to low-light image enhancement, a task previously dominated by Retinex-based networks, GANs, and CNNs. It departs from prior diffusion-based LLIE work (e.g., PyDiff, Diff-Retinex) by operating in HSB color space rather than RGB, decoupling brightness enhancement from color restoration into separate decoder branches to avoid the color-shifting artifact documented in RGB diffusion pipelines. The Bezier surface smoothness loss is a novel constraint borrowed from classical computer-aided design, repurposed here to regularize the brightness channel against overexposure in unevenly illuminated scenes—a problem that prior LLIE methods address only implicitly through global brightness mapping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper targets a concrete and visually obvious failure mode (overexposure in originally well-lit regions of unevenly lit images) that many SOTA methods still exhibit, and the HSB decoupling plus Bezier constraint offer a principled mechanism to suppress it. However, the ablation reveals that the Bezier surface alone actually degrades PSNR, and the full model's gain over the branch-only variant is modest (~0.2 dB), suggesting the diffusion backbone carries most of the performance. Open questions include whether the Bezier constraint generalizes to video LLIE, how the control-point grid size (n) should be chosen adaptively per scene, and whether the approach extends to more extreme underexposure where the brightness distribution is highly non-Gaussian.
