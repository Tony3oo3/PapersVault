## Problem

Existing motion retargeting methods treat the task frame-by-frame and prioritize either geometric plausibility or temporal consistency, but not both. Correcting penetration in isolation truncates motion paths and introduces temporal jitter, while ignoring temporal structure when handling penetration further disrupts trajectories. The paper addresses this by jointly enforcing full-body geometric correction and multi-level trajectory smoothness across the entire sequence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251931/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives the source skeletal motion as rotation quaternions (Q ∈ R^{T×K×4}) and global movement (D ∈ R^{T×4}), along with T-posed meshes of both source and target characters. At each forward pass, 1024 vertices are randomly sampled from each mesh to form dense point-set inputs, and the network predicts a residual rotation adjustment ΔQ over the full T-frame sequence (sequence-to-sequence), which is composed with the source motion to yield the retargeted skeleton Q_B. Global translation is handled separately via a normalization scheme to avoid overfitting on the limited Mixamo dataset.

## Method

STaR is a spatio-temporal network that predicts a residual motion correction in an end-to-end, unsupervised manner. The spatial module concatenates motion features (from a motion encoder) with dense shape features (from a frozen PCT point cloud transformer applied to randomly sampled mesh vertices) and processes them through a spatial transformer to capture inter-joint geometric relationships. The temporal module then permutes dimensions and applies a temporal transformer per joint to model motion evolution across the full sequence. Two novel losses drive training: a limb penetration loss that computes a normal-based SDF via Chamfer Distance between limb vertices and all other body parts (handling both limb-body and limb-limb interpenetration, implemented in CUDA), and a multi-level temporal consistency loss that constrains pairwise motion vectors of self-normalized joint trajectories to match the source. The overall objective combines these with self-reconstruction, constraint, and joint orientation losses, trained for 50 epochs with Adam.

## Results

On the Mixamo dataset, STaR (Curv) achieves a penetration rate of 8.70% with a curvature of 7.76 (close to the GT value of 7.64), while maintaining a low MSE of 0.0353. The final STaR configuration achieves the lowest penetration rate of 7.99%—16.4% lower than R²ET—though at the cost of slightly higher curvature (10.66). Among skinned motion retargeting methods, STaR is the only one that simultaneously achieves both low penetration and low curvature, whereas R²ET suffers from severe motion jitter (Curv 35.67) and MeshRet shows moderate jitter (Curv 17.67).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against R²ET [20], STaR (Curv) reduces penetration from 9.55% to 8.70% and dramatically lowers curvature from 35.67 to 7.76, at only a marginal MSE increase (0.0353 vs. 0.0335). Compared to the debugged R²ET† [20], STaR achieves lower penetration (8.70% vs. 9.92%) with comparable curvature (7.76 vs. 7.87), while R²ET† retains a slightly lower MSE. Against MeshRet [18], STaR dominates on all four metrics: lower MSE (0.0353 vs. 0.0691), lower penetration (8.70% vs. 9.39%), and substantially lower curvature (7.76 vs. 17.67). Versus skeletal-only baselines SAN [1] and PM-Net [11], STaR achieves far lower penetration (8.70% vs. 12.43% and 11.46%) and much lower MSE (0.0353 vs. 0.1664 and 0.2469), confirming that dense shape representations and the limb penetration constraint are essential for geometric plausibility. The 'Copy' baseline achieves the lowest MSE but has the highest penetration among non-skeletal methods (11.23%), illustrating the trade-off between semantic fidelity and geometric correctness.

## Relation to Prior Work

STaR extends the line of skinned motion retargeting (R²E, MeshRet, SAN) by shifting from frame-by-frame processing to a sequence-to-sequence spatio-temporal architecture. It departs from prior geometric-correction methods in two key ways: (1) a Chamfer-Distance-based limb penetration loss that penalizes all interpenetration types (limb-body and limb-limb) rather than only limb-body, and (2) a multi-level temporal consistency loss using pairwise motion vectors that enforces smoothness across the full trajectory, not just between consecutive frames. The dense shape representation via a frozen point-cloud transformer aligns the sparse skeletal input with the dense geometric supervision, a design choice not present in prior skeletal-only or SDF-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper makes concrete the tension between geometric plausibility and temporal coherence that prior work treated as separate concerns: R²E achieves low penetration but introduces 5× the source motion's curvature, while skeletal-only methods preserve smoothness but ignore geometry entirely. STaR's sequence-to-sequence design with balanced losses shows both can be addressed simultaneously, yielding 7.99% penetration (below GT's 11.26%) with curvature near source levels. Open questions remain: LBS skinning itself is a major penetration source that the method cannot fix, missing contact is unaddressed due to false positives, and generalization to longer sequences or more extremereme body-shape differences is untested.
