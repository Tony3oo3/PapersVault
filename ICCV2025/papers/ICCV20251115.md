## Problem

Existing inpainting-based virtual try-on methods rely on simple textual prompts (e.g., 'short sleeve t-shirt') and fixed clothing-agnostic masks, leaving rich text-driven editability of wearing styles (tucking, fit, sleeve rolling) largely unexplored. Conventional masks constrain generation to the original clothing shape, causing unnatural results when the new garment differs in length or silhouette, while naive LMM captioning introduces textual conflicts between the person's existing outfit and the target clothing.

## Input / Output

The method takes a person image, a reference clothing image, and an adaptively generated inpainting mask as spatial inputs, plus two text prompts (a main prompt combining person and clothing attributes, and a reference prompt with clothing-only attributes) encoded by CLIP's 77-token text encoder. The LMM-generated captions are structured around pre-defined attribute sets (body shape, gender, hand pose, tucking style for the person; category, material for clothing) rather than free-form descriptions, and the inpainting mask is produced at inference by a prompt-aware coarse-to-fine pipeline rather than being fixed to the original garment boundary.

## Method

PromptDresser builds on an SDXL inpainting U-Net augmented with a frozen reference U-Net whose self-attention key-value pairs are concatenated into the main U-Net to preserve clothing details. Text prompts are generated by an LMM via in-context learning over few-shot exemplar images with human-labeled attribute captions, separating person-specific and clothing-specific descriptions to avoid textual conflicts in unpaired person-clothing scenarios. During training, a random dilation mask augmentation interpolates between a coarse mask (clothing-agnostic region) and a fine mask (original garment boundary), teaching the model to handle diverse mask extents. At inference, Prompt-aware Mask Generation (PMG) performs a partial denoising pass with the coarse mask, segments the predicted clothing region via an off-the-shelf human parser, and unions it with the fine mask to produce a refined inpainting mask that preserves non-target regions while allowing text-driven shape changes.

## Results

On VITON-HD, PromptDresser achieves the best unpaired metrics: FID 8.54 and KID 0.67, outperforming the strongest baseline IDM-VTON (FID 9.20, KID 1.27) by 7.2% and 47.2% respectively. On the generalization test set SHHQ-1.0, it reaches FID 23.46 and KID 6.18, again the best among all methods. On DressCode, PromptDresser leads in FID and KID across all three clothing categories (upper body, lower body, dresses), e.g., FID 11.00 vs. 11.91 for IDM-VTON on upper body and KID 0.74 vs. 1.75.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PromptDresser consistently outperforms all baselines on unpaired metrics (FID, KID) across VITON-HD, SHHQ-1.0, and DressCode. Against LADI-VTON, it improves FID from 9.95 to 8.54 (14.2% reduction) and KID from 2.20 to 0.67 (69.5% reduction) on VITON-HD. Against StableVITON, the gains are FID 9.84→8.54 and KID 2.07→0.67. Against OOTDiffusion, which has the second-best KID (1.04), PromptDresser still leads with KID 0.67 and FID 8.54 vs. 9.36. On the harder generalization set SHHQ-1.0, the advantage over StableVITON narrows (FID 23.46 vs. 23.97) but KID improves substantially (6.18 vs. 7.30). On paired metrics (SSIM, LPIPS), the pose-conditioned variant (Ours_pose) achieves the best scores, while the final model without DensePose is slightly behind IDM-VTON on LPIPS (0.1119 vs. 0.1023), a trade-off the authors accept for better generalization and text editability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the inpainting-based virtual try-on lineage (VITON-HD, StableVITON, LADI-VTON, IDM-VTON) that fine-tunes SDXL for clothing transfer, and reuses StableVITON's frozen reference U-Net for clothing feature preservation. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) replacing simple attribute prompts with LMM-generated structured captions via in-context learning, separating person and clothing descriptions to avoid textual conflicts in unpaired settings, and (2) introducing Prompt-aware Mask Generation (PMG) with random dilation augmentation, which replaces the conventional clothing-agnostic mask that constrains new clothing to the original garment's shape. The LMM in-context learning strategy is borrowed from broader multimodal prompting literature but is the first application to virtual try-on captioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The text-editable virtual try-on capability (controlling tucking style, fit, sleeve rolling via natural language) opens a practical path toward user-customizable fashion previews that go beyond simple garment swapping. The finding that adding DensePose spatial conditioning actually degrades text-based editing accuracy (44% vs 67% for 'tight fit') challenges the assumption that more spatial information always helps, suggesting a tension between spatial fidelity and text-driven flexibility. Key limitations include the 77-token CLIP constraint limiting prompt richness, the manually constructed in-context exemplar set, and evaluation on only three datasets. Future directions include automated in-context data construction and leveraging SD3/T5 for longer contexts.
