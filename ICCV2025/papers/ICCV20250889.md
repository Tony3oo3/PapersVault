## Problem

Deformable 3D Gaussian Splatting is limited by the low temporal resolution of RGB cameras, which miss intermediate motion and suffer from motion blur in dynamic scenes. While event cameras provide microsecond-level continuous motion data, integrating them into 3D-GS is hindered by complex threshold variations across polarity, space, and time that degrade event supervision quality. Additionally, existing dynamic 3D-GS methods waste deformation field capacity on static regions, reducing both rendering speed and reconstruction fidelity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sparse RGB frames (30 FPS sampled from high-temporal-resolution sequences) and a continuous event stream (microsecond-level timestamps with binary polarity) as joint supervision. For synthetic scenes, events are generated via ESIM from Blender-rendered 360° monocular rotations; for real-world scenes, a hybrid system pairs a Prophesee Gen4 event camera with a Basler ace frame camera on a motorized turntable. The output is a deformable 3D Gaussian scene with explicitly separated dynamic and static Gaussians, rendered via differentiable tile-based rasterization at up to 241 FPS on an RTX 3090.

## Method

The core contribution is a GS-Threshold Joint Modeling (GTJM) strategy that creates a mutually reinforcing loop: RGB brightness changes supervise learnable per-pixel, per-polarity, per-time-bin threshold parameters, and the trained 3D-GS then renders pseudo-intermediate frames to further refine thresholds via geometric consistency, with both jointly optimized in a final stage. A second key component is Dynamic-Static Decomposition (DSD), which exploits the inherent inability of static Gaussians to represent motion: after 3k iterations of static-only reconstruction, VGG19 multi-scale feature similarity between rendered and ground-truth images produces a bimodal histogram, and Otsu's thresholding yields a 2D dynamic mask that is unprojected via depth to classify 3D Gaussians. A buffer-based soft decomposition with two radii (r₁, r₂) prunes boundary Gaussians, letting adaptive density control optimize the decomposition boundary. The deformation field (MLP with positional encoding) is applied exclusively to dynamic Gaussians, while static Gaussians bypass it entirely, yielding both faster rendering and higher dynamic-region fidelity.

## Results

On the 8-scene synthetic benchmark, the proposed method achieves an average PSNR of 31.56 dB, outperforming Event-4DGS (28.83 dB) by 2.73 dB and Deformable-3DGS (25.55 dB) by 6.01 dB, while rendering at 156 FPS on average—1.71× faster than 4D-GS (91 FPS). On the 4-scene real-world benchmark, the method reaches an average PSNR of 30.39 dB, surpassing Event-4DGS (28.96 dB) by 1.43 dB and 4D-GS (27.78 dB) by 2.61 dB, with an average rendering speed of 152 FPS. The method also achieves the best LPIPS scores across all scenes (e.g., 0.003 on Materials, 0.020 on Celestial globe) and the highest SSIM values, confirming superior perceptual quality alongside speed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five baselines on every scene in both synthetic and real-world evaluations. Against the static 3D-GS [17], the method gains 5–12 dB PSNR on dynamic scenes (e.g., 31.85 vs. 23.60 on Lego), confirming that event-assisted deformation is essential for motion capture. The NeRF-based baselines TiNeuVox [7] and K-Planes [9] are outperformed by 6–15 dB in PSNR while being 60–280× slower in rendering (0.5–2.4 FPS vs. 156 FPS). Among dynamic Gaussian methods, 4D-GS [42] achieves competitive speed (91 FPS) but lags by 3–8 dB PSNR due to its reliance on sparse RGB frames alone; Deformable-3DGS [46] shows similar quality gaps (e.g., 23.79 vs. 31.85 on Lego) and is also slower (64 FPS). The closest competitor, Event-4DGS (Deformable-3DGS extended with a constant-threshold event loss), is consistently surpassed by 2.7–4.5 dB PSNR, with the gap attributed to the proposed GS-threshold joint modeling that handles spatially and temporally varying thresholds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deformable 3D Gaussian Splatting line (building directly on 3D-GS [17] for scene representation and Deformable-3DGS [46] for the deformation field) by introducing event cameras as a high-temporal-resolution supervision signal. It departs from prior event-based neural rendering work (EventNeRF, E-NeRF, DE-NeRF), which focused on static scenes or deblurring, by being the first to integrate events into a deformable 3D-GS framework for dynamic scenes. The GS-threshold joint modeling strategy is a novel departure from the constant-threshold assumption used in all prior event-based reconstruction methods, and the dynamic-static decomposition contrasts with prior dynamic 3D-GS approaches that uniformly apply deformation to all Gaussians.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first event-inclusive 4D benchmark (8 synthetic + 4 real-world scenes) fills a critical gap, as prior event-based dynamic datasets were unpublished or used static cameras precluding novel-view evaluation. The GTJM mutual-reinforcement insight—that 3D-GS geometric consistency can correct unreliable event supervision and vice versa—offers a generalizable principle for multi-sensor fusion. However, the benchmark remains small in scale, real-world scenes rely on a rotating turntable rather than true camera motion, and the method requires synchronized event+RGB hardware, limiting immediate adoption. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-view event setups and whether the VGG19-based 2D decomposition holds for more complex occlusion patterns.
