## Problem

Human inertial localization suffers from trajectory drift due to IMU sensor noise accumulating over time, and existing methods are further limited by their focus on displacement-centric activities like walking, ignoring the rich motion patterns from diverse human actions. The paper identifies that egocentric actions (e.g., bending to look in an oven, washing dishes) correlate with specific spatial structures in the environment, yet no prior method exploits these action-environment correlations as spatial anchors for drift-free localization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251011/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a sequence of 6-DoF head-mounted IMU readings (3-axis acceleration and angular velocity, downsampled to 400 Hz) over T seconds and a pre-built 3D point cloud of the environment (uniformly partitioned into S local 1 m² segments, each sub-sampled to 8192 points). It outputs a per-timestep location as an S-class classification over point cloud segments (yielding 2D ground-plane coordinates) and, as a by-product, a 35-class action label at each timestep. During training only, synchronized egocentric RGB frames and free-text action captions from the EgoExo4D dataset are additionally consumed to guide contrastive alignment.

## Method

EAIL is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 trains a short-term IMU encoder (ResNet18-1D) and a local point cloud encoder (PointNet++) via four-way multimodal contrastive learning, aligning their features with pretrained CLIP image and text encoders so that action semantics bridge the IMU and point cloud representations. Stage 2 freezes both encoders and computes per-timestep correspondence heatmaps between IMU features and all point cloud segment features; a temporal reasoning module (3D convolutional network) refines these heatmaps across the T-second sequence, and a spatial reasoning module (dilated 3D convolutional network) reasons over the 2D spatial layout to produce the final trajectory. A location-aware action recognition head then uses the predicted location probabilities as spatial attention over point cloud features, fused additively with IMU features through an MLP, so that localization and action recognition mutually reinforce each other.

## Results

On the EgoExo4D cooking subset, EAIL achieves 43.86% success rate at 0.2 m error and 89.60% at 0.6 m in seen rooms, and 26.86% / 90.79% in unseen rooms, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline NILoc+ (17.03% / 74.15% seen; 13.32% / 69.21% unseen). The Relative Score reaches 96.01% (seen) and 89.55% (unseen), indicating strong ranking of the true location. As a by-product, EAIL also achieves 21.48% top-1 and 53.62% top-5 action recognition accuracy in seen rooms, and 15.03% / 43.34% in unseen rooms, surpassing all inertial action recognition baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EAIL outperforms all velocity-accumulation baselines (MnasNet, EfficientNet, IMUNet, RoNIN) by a wide margin: at 0.2 m in seen rooms, EAIL reaches 43.86% versus RoNIN's 4.86% and IMUNet's 3.74%, a roughly 9× improvement, because velocity-accumulation methods suffer severe drift over the long recordings (avg. 525 s). Against the closest direct-prediction baseline NILoc+, EAIL gains +26.83 points at 0.2 m (43.86 vs. 17.03) and +15.45 points at 0.6 m (89.60 vs. 74.15) in seen rooms; the gap widens in unseen rooms where EAIL achieves 26.86% vs. NILoc+'s 13.32% at 0.2 m, demonstrating that incorporating 3D point cloud structure enables cross-environment generalization that NILoc's scene-specific training cannot match. For the secondary action-recognition task, EAIL surpasses IMU2CLIP (the strongest action baseline) by +2.52 top-1 and +3.19 top-5 in seen rooms, and by +2.76 top-1 and +6.30 top-5 in unseen rooms, attributed to the spatial attention over point cloud features that provides environmental context beyond what IMU signals alone can offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant velocity-accumulation paradigm in inertial localization (RoNIN, IMUNet, PDR) and extends the direct-location-prediction approach of NILoc by incorporating explicit 3D environmental structure. Conceptually, it repurposes the vision-language contrastive learning framework (CLIP, EgoVLP, IMU2CLIP) to align IMU action cues with point cloud geometry, drawing on the affordance literature (SceneFun3D, ConceptGraphs) that established action-environment correlations in 3D scenes. The key reframing is treating human actions not as noise to be filtered from IMU signals but as spatial anchors that constrain where the user can be, a perspective absent from prior inertial navigation work that focused almost exclusively on displacement-centric activities like walking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The action-as-anchor insight offers a principled way to combat trajectory drift without external infrastructure (GPS, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth), which is significant for privacy-preserving and energy-efficient personal navigation. However, the method's applicability is constrained by its dependence on a pre-built, up-to-date 3D point cloud and its sensitivity to low-motion periods where action cues are absent. Open questions include whether the framework extends to different sensor placements (wrist, ankle), whether online/incremental map updates can replace the static point cloud assumption, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the cooking activities subset of EgoExo4D to more diverse activity domains.
