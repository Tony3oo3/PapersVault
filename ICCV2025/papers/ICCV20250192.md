## Problem

Diffusion model inference is computationally expensive due to its iterative sampling process, limiting deployment in latency-sensitive applications. Existing acceleration techniques either require extensive retraining (distillation) or sacrifice sample quality, while prior multi-core parallel methods are architecture-specific, training-based, or constrained to fixed resource allocations. The paper addresses the gap of a general, training-free, model-agnostic multi-core acceleration strategy that scales flexibly with the number of available compute cores without quality degradation.

## Input / Output

CHORDS takes a pretrained diffusion model (any architecture or noise schedule), a text prompt, and a user-specified number of computation cores K (4, 6, or 8 in experiments), and produces a generated image or video. A distinctive feature is its streaming output structure: each core in the hierarchy returns an intermediate result at progressively later wall-clock times with increasing quality, and the final output from the slowest core is guaranteed identical to the sequential solver's output. The method operates with N=50 diffusion steps by default and is measured in terms of sequential network forward calls rather than wall-clock time for the speedup metric.

## Method

CHORDS formulates multi-core diffusion sampling as a hierarchical ODE solver pipeline where K cores are initialized at staggered times (determined by a theoretically optimal sequence from Theorem 2.5) and solve the probability-flow ODE simultaneously. The key mechanism is a multi-core rectification update (Eq. 3–4): whenever a slower, more accurate core 'reaches' a faster core's time point, it computes a correction term based on the difference in vector field evaluations and the latent gap, which is added to the fast core's trajectory to eliminate accumulated error. This rectification is fully pipelined across the hierarchy—each core is updated by its predecessor while itself updating its successor—ensuring continuous information flow without idle bubbles. The initialization sequence is derived by decomposing the optimization into triple-core subproblems with a greedy fast-to-slow recursion, allocating shorter time intervals to slower solvers for more frequent early-stage rectification. The entire framework is training-free and model-agnostic, compatible with arbitrary diffusion solvers (DDIM, Euler, flow matching) and architectures, and can be combined with orthogonal parallelism techniques like tensor-parallel model splitting.

## Results

On video diffusion models (HunyuanVideo, Wan2.1, CogVideoX1.5), CHORDS achieves 2.1×–2.9× speedup with 4–8 GPU cores while maintaining VBench quality within 0.1–0.3% of sequential sampling (e.g., 84.1% vs. 84.4% on HunyuanVideo). On image diffusion models (SD-3.5-Large, Flux), CHORDS reaches 2.0×–2.6× speedup with 4–8 cores with negligible CLIP Score change (e.g., 31.0 vs. 31.0 on Flux). Latent RMSE remains at or below baseline levels (e.g., 0.066–0.068 on HunyuanVideo vs. 0.068 for SRDS), confirming no measurable quality degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CHORDS consistently outperforms both baselines across all five models and all core counts (4, 6, 8). Against SRDS [29], the closest competitor, CHORDS achieves up to 67% higher speedup with 4 cores (e.g., 2.0× vs. 1.2× on CogVideoX1.5) and up to ~14% higher speedup with 8 cores (e.g., 2.9× vs. 2.6× on HunyuanVideo). Against ParaDIGMS [30], the gap is even larger: CHORDS delivers roughly 2× the speedup of ParaDIGMS at 4 cores (2.1× vs. 1.3× on HunyuanVideo) and ~2× at 8 cores (2.9× vs. 1.4×). In terms of quality, CHORDS matches or slightly exceeds sequential VBench/CLIP scores, while ParaDIGMS shows substantially higher latent RMSE (e.g., 0.202 vs. 0.068 on HunyuanVideo at 8 cores), indicating visible quality degradation. SRDS maintains quality comparable to CHORDS but at significantly lower speedup, particularly at 4 cores where its fixed √N core constraint limits parallelism.

## Relation to Prior Work

CHORDS unifies and generalizes existing parallel diffusion sampling methods—specifically SRDS (Parareal-based self-refining) and ParaDIGMS (Picard-iteration-based)—into a single multi-core hierarchical ODE solver framework with a theoretically justified initialization sequence. The core rectification operation is directly inherited from classical multi-grid ODE solvers (Brandt 1977, Greenbaum 1986), repurposed for the diffusion PF-ODE setting. The key departure from prior work is the principled, reward-function-driven selection of the initialization sequence (Theorem 2.5), which replaces the ad-hoc pipeline designs and fixed core-count constraints (e.g., ⌊√N⌋ cores) that limited SRDS and ParaDIGMS to specific resource budgets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a training-free, model-agnostic accelerator, CHORDS can be dropped onto any existing diffusion model (image or video) without retraining, making it immediately deployable for latency-sensitive applications like real-time editing and streaming generation. The streaming output structure—where progressively more accurate results are available as slower cores finish—enables users to trade off quality for speed on the fly, a capability absent from prior parallel samplers. Open questions include how CHORDS composes with tensor-parallel model sharding, whether the theoretical initialization transfers cleanly to very large core counts (beyond 8), and whether the speedup ceiling can be pushed further by combining with distillation or fast single-core solvers.
