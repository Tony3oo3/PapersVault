## Problem

Despite the rapid commercialization of image-to-video diffusion models, no dedicated dataset exists that captures real user-provided text and image prompts for this task. Existing prompt-gallery datasets (VidProM for text-to-video, DiffusionDB for text-to-image) differ fundamentally in both structure (text-only vs. text+image) and semantics (scene descriptions vs. motion instructions for static objects), leaving researchers without a resource to study user preferences, build practical benchmarks, or address safety concerns like misinformation in image-to-video generation.

## Input / Output

Each data point pairs a text prompt (motion/action instructions, up to 8192 tokens) with an image prompt (the first frame of a 3-second Pika video, parsed from scraped outputs) to produce generated videos from five models (Pika, Stable Video Diffusion, Open-Sora, I2VGen-XL, CogVideoX-5B). Beyond the media, each entry carries UUIDs, anonymous UserIDs, timestamps, GPT-4o-inferred subjects and directions, text-embedding-3-large and CLIP embeddings, and Detoxify/nsfw_image_detection NSFW scores. For the downstream detection and tracing tasks, inputs are individual video clips or single frames, and outputs are three-class labels (real, text-generated, image-generated) or a retrieved source image from a 1,010,000-image reference database.

## Method

The dataset is curated by scraping Pika Discord channel messages (Jul 2023–Oct 2024) via DiscordChatExporter, extracting 1,701,935 unique text prompts with regex, scraping corresponding 3-second Pika videos, and parsing image prompts from video first frames. Metadata is assigned using GPT-4o (subjects/directions), text-embedding-3-large and CLIP (embeddings), and Detoxify/nsfw_image_detection (NSFW scores). To diversify outputs, 100,000 randomly selected prompts are additionally fed to four open-source models (SVD, Open-Sora, I2VGen-XL, CogVideoX-5B), constrained by compute (e.g., CogVideoX-5B requires 294 s per video on an A100). For empirical studies, the authors fine-tune VideoMAE as a three-class video detector (real vs. text-generated vs. image-generated) and train a deep metric learning model for source-image tracing, while constructing TIP-Eval (1,000 subjects × 10 real-user prompts) to benchmark five models across 10 evaluation dimensions.

## Results

For source image tracing (TIP-Trace), the proposed deep metric learning baseline achieves 89.1% μAP in the same-domain setting and 84.7% μAP in the cross-domain setting, outperforming the best existing method SSCD (77.5% μAP) by +7.2% in cross-domain. For video classification (TIP-ID), the fine-tuned VideoMAE detector reaches 96.3% accuracy and 99.4% mAP (same domain) and 87.2% accuracy and 94.8% mAP (cross domain), while all existing fake-image detectors remain near random (47–53% accuracy). On the TIP-Eval benchmark, Pika outperforms CogVideoX-5B on 8 out of 10 dimensions, and no model exceeds 0.26 on video-text alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed metric learning baseline dominates all 19 baselines on source image tracing: in cross-domain, it achieves 84.7% μAP vs. SSCD (77.5%, best copy-detection), CNNCL (59.5%), EfficientNet (61.2%, best supervised), SimCLR (60.8%, best self-supervised), and SLIP (56.2%, best vision-language). The gap is most pronounced on Open-Sora and I2VGen-XL, where existing methods drop to 20–50% μAP while the proposed method retains 57.0% and 73.3% respectively. For video classification, the fine-tuned VideoMAE detector (87.2% cross-domain accuracy) massively outperforms all existing fake-image detectors, whose best is UnivFD at 53.1% accuracy and 55.5% mAP — most methods (CNNSpot, Fusing, DIRE, LGrad, LNP) hover near the 50% random-guess baseline. A notable weakness is the cross-domain generalization gap: I2VGen-XL drops from 90.3% to 73.3% μAP (−17.0%) and Open-Sora from 61.5% to 57.0%, indicating the baseline still struggles with unseen diffusion architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first prompt-gallery dataset for image-to-video generation, extending the conceptual lineage of VidProM (text-to-video) and DiffusionDB (text-to-image) to a domain where both a text instruction and a source image jointly condition generation. Unlike those prior datasets, TIP-I2V captures the distinct semantic structure of image-to-video prompts—short motion directives applied to specific objects in a given image—rather than free-form scene descriptions. For the safety components, the paper repurposes VideoMAE for a novel 3-way video classification task (real vs. text-generated vs. image-generated) and adapts metric learning for source-image retrieval, showing that existing fake-image detectors and copy-detection models fail to transfer to this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TIP-I2V fills a critical gap by providing 1.7M real user prompts with paired images and multi-model generated videos, enabling user-oriented training data curation, practical evaluation (TIP-Eval), and safety research that were previously infeasible. The finding that all existing fake-image detectors perform at near-random chance on image-to-video generated content, while a simple fine-tuned VideoMAE achieves 96.3% same-domain accuracy, reveals a fundamental blind spot in current deepfake detection and motivates new detection paradigms. Open questions include the 9.1% cross-domain accuracy gap, whether the metric-learning source-tracing baseline generalizes to future unseen models, and how to handle the 11% of prompts involving potentially copyrighted cartoon content.
