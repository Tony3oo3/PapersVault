## Problem

Adapting 2D large multimodal models to 3D scene understanding has relied primarily on input-level modifications (fusing point cloud features, voxel aggregation, or treating multi-view images as video), but these alone are insufficient because the LMM's inherent 2D inductive bias impedes genuine 3D integration. The absence of large-scale 3D vision-language datasets further limits supervised training, leaving a gap in how to inject true 3D spatial awareness into LMMs during the training procedure itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250074/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 32 multi-view video frames at 384×384 resolution, each paired with a depth map to produce position-aware video representations, alongside a text prompt. For global-view reconstruction, a 432×432 Bird's-Eye-View image rendered from scene point clouds serves as the reconstruction target. Outputs include free-form text (answers, captions) and 3D bounding boxes for grounding tasks, while the LMM's intermediate visual token outputs (x_{i≤N}) are additionally used as conditioning signals for a separate denoising network.

## Method

Ross3D augments standard visual instruction tuning with two 3D-aware reconstructive visual pretext tasks that supervise the LMM's visual outputs directly. Cross-view reconstruction randomly masks a subset of views (25% ratio) and requires the model to recover their latent tokens by aggregating overlapping information from unmasked views; global-view reconstruction requires recovering a BEV image latent from all available views. A small DiT-based denoiser, conditioned on the LMM's visual outputs via learnable queries and timestep embeddings, predicts the noise in a diffusion process over FLUX-VAE latents. The key distinction from prior work is that 3D awareness is injected through output-level visual supervision rather than input-level feature engineering, and the two pretext tasks are complementary: cross-view improves fine-grained spatial grounding while global-view improves holistic scene comprehension. The approach also enables semi-supervised learning, where the 3D visual objective can be applied to unlabeled 3D video data without any text annotations.

## Results

Ross3D achieves 63.0 EM on SQA3D and 107.0 CIDEr on ScanQA, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art Video-3D-LLM by +4.4 EM and +4.9 CIDEr respectively. On 3D dense captioning (Scan2Cap), it reaches 66.9 ROUGE and 43.4 BLEU-4. For 3D visual grounding, Ross3D obtains 61.1 Acc@0.25 on ScanRefer and 59.6 F1@0.25 on Multi3DRefer, surpassing Video-3D-LLM by +3.0 and +1.6 points respectively. Notably, in a semi-supervised setting (50% labeled + 50% unlabeled 3D data), Ross3D even exceeds the 100% text-supervised baseline on ScanQA (103.2 vs 102.1 CIDEr).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ross3D outperforms the previous state-of-the-art Video-3D-LLM [88] across all five benchmarks: +4.4 EM on SQA3D (63.0 vs 58.6), +4.9 CIDEr on ScanQA (107.0 vs 102.1), +4.6 ROUGE on Scan2Cap (66.9 vs 62.3), +3.0 Acc@0.25 on ScanRefer (61.1 vs 58.1), and +1.6 F1@0.25 on Multi3DRefer (59.6 vs 58.0). Against GPT4Scene-HDM [58], which uses a larger input resolution (512×490) and extra BEV inputs, Ross3D wins on SQA3D (63.0 vs 59.4 EM) and ScanQA (107.0 vs 96.3 CIDEr) but trails on ScanRefer (61.1 vs 62.6 Acc@0.25) and Multi3DRefer (59.6 vs 64.5 F1@0.25). Among point-cloud-based 3D LMMs, Ross3D substantially surpasses 3D-LLM [32] (69.4 CIDEr), LLaVA-3D [89] (91.7 CIDEr), and LEO [33] (80.0 CIDEr) on ScanQA, confirming that output-level 3D-aware visual supervision is more effective than input-level 3D feature aggregation. Zero-shot 2D LMMs (InternVL2-8B, Qwen2-VL-7B, LLaVA-Video-7B) lag significantly behind all 3D-adapted methods, highlighting the necessity of 3D-specific training. The semi-supervised experiment further shows that applying the 3D visual objective to unlabeled data (50% labeled + 50% unlabeled) surpasses the fully supervised 100% baseline on ScanQA (103.2 vs 102.1 CIDEr), demonstrating the value of the self-supervised 3D pretext tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reconstructive visual instruction tuning paradigm (Wang et al., ICLR 2025) from 2D images to 3D scenes, building on the LLaVA-Video-7B base model and the multi-view-as-video input representation from Video-3D-LLM. The key conceptual departure is from prior 3D LMMs (3D-LLM, LLaVA-3D, GPT4Scene) that focus on input-level modifications—fusing point cloud features, aggregating 2D features into voxel spaces, or treating multi-view images as video sequences. Ross3D argues these input-level changes are insufficient due to the LMM's inherent 2D inductive bias, and instead introduces 3D-aware output-level supervision via cross-view and global-view (BEV) reconstruction tasks, using a DiT-based denoiser conditioned on the LMM's visual token outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The semi-supervised finding—that applying the 3D visual reconstruction objective to 50% unlabeled 3D data alongside 50% text-labeled data surpasses the 100% text-supervised baseline on ScanQA—demonstrates a practical pathway to scale 3D LMMs beyond the severe data bottleneck that plagues the field. However, all evaluations are confined to ScanNet-derived indoor scenes, the BEV rendering pipeline depends on 3D reconstruction quality, and the base model is fixed to LLaVA-Video-7B, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to outdoor scenes, larger models, or different video encoders. The work also raises the broader question of what other 3D-aware visual supervision signals (beyond cross-view and BEV reconstruction) could further enhance spatial reasoning in LMMs.
