## Problem

Existing referring image segmentation methods face a fundamental trade-off: perception-centric architectures preserve fine-grained spatial detail but lack multi-modal cognitive capacity, while cognition-centric architectures (vision-language pre-trained models) achieve strong image-text understanding but lose fine-grained spatial information due to quadratic transformer complexity. The paper further identifies that the primary bottleneck in current RIS is insufficient multi-modal cognition rather than perceptual deficiency, and that non-referent samples (~9% in gRefCOCO) create a long-tail distribution problem that destabilizes training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a high-resolution RGB image (384×384) for the perception branch and a low-resolution version (224×224) for the cognition branch, alongside a free-form text description that may be dynamically replaced during training via the NSC augmentation. Outputs include a per-instance segmentation mask, a per-query referring confidence score (filtered at threshold 0.7 during inference), and a global non-referent judgment computed by average-pooling a linear projection of the final-round cognition queries. The dual-resolution design is deliberate: the cognition branch only needs semantic understanding for referent classification, while the perception branch requires high resolution for precise contour delineation.

## Method

DeRIS explicitly decouples RIS into a perception branch (Mask2Former-style Swin hierarchical encoder + FPN at 384×384) and a cognition branch (BEiT3 vision-language pre-trained model at 224×224), then couples them via a Loopback Synergy mechanism consisting of 3 iterative rounds. In each round, the perception layer generates object queries Q_p and masks M_p via deformable cross-attention; the cognition layer uses M_p as a spatial prior to compute instance-instance relations (masked average pooling + self-attention) and instance-text relations (cross-attention with text features), yielding cognition queries Q_c and per-query referring scores S_r; fused queries Q_f = MLP(Concat(Q_p, Q_c)) feed the next round. A Non-referent Sample Conversion (NSC) strategy dynamically replaces text descriptions with sentences from mismatched images (filtered by image identity, length, and Jaccard+Cosine similarity thresholds) to expand non-referent diversity, with a conversion rate R_c=15%. Training uses Hungarian matching with combined BCE+Dice mask loss, BCE referring loss, and BCE non-referent loss, with auxiliary supervision at each loopback round (weight 0.2).

## Results

On the RIS task, DeRIS-L (Swin-B + BEiT3-L) achieves 85.72 mIoU on RefCOCO val, 81.28 on RefCOCO+ val, and 80.01 on RefCOCOg val, surpassing the previous best specialist method OneRef-L by 4.46%, 4.68%, and 4.33% respectively. The MLLM variant DeRIS-7B (Swin-B + Qwen2-OV-7B) reaches 84.05 cIoU on RefCOCO val, outperforming SAM4MLLM-8B by 4.25% cIoU. On the GRES task (gRefCOCO), DeRIS-L achieves 77.67 gIoU, 72.00 cIoU, and 82.22 N-acc on val, with the N-acc improvement being particularly notable (+19.42 over the prior best HieA2G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeRIS-L dominates all specialist baselines across every RefCOCO/+/g split, with the largest margins over OneRef-L (+4.46% RefCOCO val, +4.68% RefCOCO+ val, +4.33% RefCOCOg val) and C3VG (+4.35%, +4.23%, +3.67% on val). Among MLLM-based methods, DeRIS-7B outperforms SAM4MLLM-8B by 4.25% cIoU on RefCOCO val and 5.70% on RefCOCO+ val, and surpasses GLaMM-7B by 4.55% and 7.70% on the same splits. On the GRES task, DeRIS-L leads HieA2G (the strongest prior specialist) by 9.27 gIoU and 19.42 N-acc on val, while also exceeding SAM4MLLM-8B by 5.81 gIoU and 16.14 N-acc. The N-acc gains are the most striking: DeRIS-L reaches 82.22 N-acc on gRefCOCO val versus 62.80 for HieA2G and 66.08 for SAM4MLLM-8B, demonstrating that the non-referent sample conversion strategy effectively addresses the long-tail distribution problem. DeRIS-B, despite using a smaller Swin-S backbone, still outperforms all specialist baselines except OneRef-L and C3VG on RefCOCO val, confirming the framework's efficiency at lower model scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeRIS explicitly decouples the two dominant RIS paradigms—perception-centric methods built on hierarchical backbones (Mask2Former, Swin) and cognition-centric methods built on vision-language pre-trained models (BEiT3, CLIP)—into separate branches connected by a novel Loopback Synergy mechanism that iteratively exchanges object queries. Conceptually, it departs from prior work by treating the perception-cognition trade-off as a structural design problem rather than an architectural compromise, and by providing the first systematic quantitative decomposition of their respective contributions. The non-referent sample conversion (NSC) strategy extends the GRES formulation introduced by ReLA [42] with a practical data augmentation to address the long-tail distribution of non-referent samples, while the MLLM variant (DeRIS-7B with Qwen2-OV-7B) positions the framework within the emerging wave of large-model-based segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that cognition, not perception, is the primary bottleneck in RIS (+12.9% vs. +1.2% cIoU) provides a clear diagnostic that should redirect community investment toward stronger multi-modal understanding rather than ever-larger perceptual backbones. The decoupled architecture offers a reusable design principle for other multi-modal tasks where fine-grained spatial output must coexist with rich semantic reasoning. Open questions include whether the loopback synergy scales effectively with larger MLLMs beyond 7B parameters, whether the NSC strategy generalizes to other long-tail classification sub-problems, and whether the 224×224 cognition resolution is a fundamental limitation or merely a current engineering trade-off.
