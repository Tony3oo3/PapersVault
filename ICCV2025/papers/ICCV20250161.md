## Problem

Differentiable PnP, the mainstream technique for enforcing projective constraints in I2P registration, is highly sensitive to noise and outliers in predicted correspondences, making the estimated camera pose unreliable and hindering correspondence learning. Blind PnP is robust to such noise but is computationally intractable for end-to-end training. The paper addresses how to retain blind PnP's robustness while making it tractable for supervised correspondence learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250161/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image augmented with surface normals (predicted by the pre-trained DSINE model) and an RGB point cloud with surface normals. 2D keypoints are pre-detected offline using the Shi-Tomasi detector and held fixed, while 3D keypoints are learned end-to-end. The output is a set of 2D-3D correspondences and a 6-DoF camera pose estimated via a Point Transformer encoder followed by MLPs, with the Chamfer distance between projected 3D keypoints and 2D keypoints serving as the geometric supervision signal.

## Method

MinCD-PnP reformulates blind PnP's inlier-maximization objective into a tractable Chamfer-distance minimization between 2D and 3D keypoints via a triple approximation: (1) replacing the boolean correspondence matrix with a nearest-neighbor Chamfer term, (2) reducing the pixel/point sets to ~10³ keypoints to cut the matrix from 10¹¹ to 10⁵ entries, and (3) fixing 2D keypoints (Shi-Tomasi) and learning 3D keypoints guided by 2D feature matching with a confidence threshold. MinCD-Net implements this as a lightweight multi-task module: a Point Transformer encodes 2D and 3D keypoint features into global descriptors, concatenated MLPs predict the pose T, and the Chamfer loss L_Chamfer(T|K_I, K_P) plus a keypoint loss L_key supervise the network jointly with the standard circle correspondence loss. The module plugs into the MATR backbone (ResNet + KPConv extractors) without modifying its main matching branch, and is trained for 40 epochs with the Chamfer and keypoint losses activated after the first 20 epochs.

## Results

On the 7-Scenes cross-scene benchmark, MATR+MinCD-Net achieves the highest average IR of 0.568 and RR of 0.647 when trained on Office, outperforming MATR+BPnPNet (IR 0.486, RR 0.578) and MATR+Diff.PnP (IR 0.463, RR 0.501). In the cross-dataset setting, MinCD-Net attains the best average IR of 0.371 and RR@0.1 of 0.870 on RGBD-V2, and the best RR@0.05 of 0.898 on ScanNet. In the standard comparison on RGBD-V2 (Table 3), MinCD-Net achieves IR 0.472 and RR@0.1 0.823, surpassing all baselines except Diff-Reg on RR@0.1 (0.862).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MinCD-Net consistently outperforms all differentiable PnP baselines: it beats MATR+Diff.PnP (EPro-PnP [6]) by 8.5–10.5% in IR and 14.6–14.6% in RR across the three 7-Scenes training splits, and surpasses MATR+BPnPNet [4] by 2.5–8.2% in IR and 8.0–12.9% in RR. Against the feature-matching baselines, MinCD-Net improves IR over P2-Net [35] by 7.2–17.5% and over MATR [25] by 5.2–11.2% in the cross-scene setting. In the standard RGBD-V2 comparison (Table 3), MinCD-Net (IR 0.472) outperforms FCGF [7] (0.081), Predator [18] (0.157), FreeReg+PnP [36] (0.309), and MATR+SN (0.451), but Diff-Reg [38] achieves a higher RR@0.1 (0.862 vs. 0.823) despite a lower IR (0.377). On ScanNet (Table 4), MinCD-Net (IR 0.468, RR@0.3 0.898) beats FreeReg [36] (IR 0.568, RR@0.3 0.780) on RR but trails on IR, while decisively outperforming LCD [27] (IR 0.307) and SuperGlue [30] (IR 0.184, RR@0.3 0.065). Overall, MinCD-Net wins on the majority of metrics across all four datasets, with the sole exception being RR@0.1 on RGBD-V2 where Diff-Reg leads.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the I2P registration line of work (P2-Net, MATR) that relies on pixel-to-point feature matching, and specifically departs from the differentiable PnP paradigm (EPro-PnP, BPnPNet) that enforces projective constraints but is fragile to correspondence outliers. The key conceptual move is borrowing the robustness of blind PnP (Campbell et al., TPAMI 2020) and reformulating its inlier-maximization objective into a tractable Chamfer distance minimization over learned keypoints via a triple approximation strategy. The resulting MinCD-Net module is positioned as a lightweight, plug-and-play addition to existing I2P architectures (demonstrated on MATR), rather than a full pipeline redesign.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-identified weakness of differentiable PnP—sensitivity to noisy/outlier correspondences—by showing that a simple Chamfer distance on a small set of keypoints captures the essential robustness of blind PnP at a fraction of the computational cost. The practical impact is that MinCD-Net can be dropped into any existing I2P registration backbone with minimal architectural change, making it broadly applicable. Open questions include whether the reliance on a fixed 2D keypoint detector (Shi-Tomasi) limits performance in textureless or highly dynamic scenes, whether the approach extends to outdoor or large-scale environments, and whether jointly learning 2D and 3D keypoints (rather than fixing one) could yield further gains.
