## Problem

Existing EQA benchmarks suffer from three critical gaps: datasets embed prior knowledge (e.g., naming specific rooms) that enables disembodied reasoning without actual exploration, frontier-based exploration strategies fail in cluttered or constrained environments and cannot perform fine-grained investigation of task-relevant areas, and evaluation metrics based solely on answer similarity cannot detect hallucinated responses that appear plausible but lack supporting visual evidence. This paper addresses all three by constructing a large-scale exploration-aware benchmark, a hybrid exploration method, and a grounding-aware evaluation metric.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural-language question and operates within a 3D HM3D scene on the Habitat simulator, gathering first-person RGB observations at each step of its exploration trajectory (average 39.8 steps, 6.6 m geodesic distance per question). The output is an open-ended text answer grounded in the final visual observation, scored by the EAC metric which multiplies a correctness score σ_i (1–5, assessed by a VLM against the question, ground-truth answer, and final image) by a grounding score δ_i (0, 0.5, or 1, assessing whether the agent's observation actually supports the answer). The benchmark comprises 777 trajectories and 2,044 question-trajectory pairs across seven question categories (state, knowledge, location attribute, counting, existence, object, and one more).

## Method

Fine-EQA is a two-stage exploration framework that alternates between Frontier-Based Exploration (FBE) and Goal-Oriented Exploration (GOE). FBE maintains a global semantic map M_sem by fusing local semantic values (VLM-assigned exploration priority of farthest-sampled points back-projected onto the RGB image) with global semantic values (VLM scene-level confidence), then selects frontier points weighted by semantic value, unexplored rate, unoccupied rate, and distance to the current position. GOE constructs a functional region semantic map M_reg by classifying observed scenes into named regions (kitchen, bedroom, etc.) via a VLM, ranks regions by task relevance using an LLM, and applies a masking operation to M_sem to focus exploration exclusively on the highest-priority region while decaying previously visited points. The agent switches strategies when a region-specific exploration limit is reached, and GPT-4o-mini decides when sufficient information has been gathered to produce the final answer. The EAC evaluation metric penalizes answers that are correct in content but unsupported by the agent's actual observations, directly targeting the hallucination problem in prior EQA evaluation.

## Results

On EXPRESS-Bench, Fine-EQA achieves the best overall correctness C = 40.55, correctness without grounding C* = 63.95, path efficiency E_path = 16.22, and target distance d_T = 6.43 m among all exploring agents. The gap between C* and C (e.g., RE: 62.75 vs. 36.95) exposes severe hallucination in weaker explorers, while Fine-EQA narrows this gap to 23.4 points. All methods remain well below the human agent's C* of 83.99, highlighting the difficulty of exploration-aware EQA. The ablation confirms both FBE and GOE contribute: removing FBE drops C to 38.54 and E_path to 12.74, while removing GOE drops C* to 60.74.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fine-EQA (C* = 63.95, C = 40.55) outperforms all non-exploratory baselines on C*, with the closest competitors being the Socratic models: DeepSeek-V3 + GPT-4o-mini (C* = 62.60) and GPT-4 + GPT-4o-mini (C* = 62.56), both trailing by roughly 1.4 points. Blind LLMs (DeepSeek-V3 59.15, GPT-4 58.96, LLaMA-3-8b 57.25) and Multi-Frame VLMs (GPT-4o-mini 58.37, LLaVA-v1.5-7b 57.66) lag further behind, confirming that passive frame sampling cannot substitute for active exploration. Among exploring agents, Fine-EQA beats RE by 3.6 points on C and 4.16 on E_path, while also achieving the lowest d_T (6.43 m vs. 7.26 m for RE), indicating more efficient navigation to task-relevant regions. Notably, RE's C* (62.75) is competitive with Fine-EQA's, but its C collapses to 36.95, revealing that random exploration produces many hallucinated answers that the EAC grounding score (δ_i) correctly penalizes. The paper also reports ambiguity rates of 35.6% (HM-EQA) and 62.3% (OpenEQA) versus only 9.7% in EXPRESS-Bench, underscoring that the benchmark's design reduces spurious-correct answers that inflate scores on prior datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the EQA benchmark lineage (EQA-v1, MP3D-EQA, HM-EQA, OpenEQA) by making exploration a first-class, explicitly evaluated component rather than an implicit prerequisite. It builds most directly on OpenEQA's GPT-4-based scoring and exploration-track design, but departs from it by adding a grounding score (δ) to form the EAC metric and by scaling trajectories from 152 to 777. On the method side, Fine-EQA extends the frontier-based exploration paradigm (Yamauchi 1997; Ren et al. 2024 'Explore until Confident') by integrating a goal-oriented phase guided by functional region semantic maps, addressing the known weakness of pure frontier strategies in cluttered or occluded indoor scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The EAC metric addresses a genuine evaluation gap: prior EQA benchmarks reward answer similarity without verifying that the agent actually observed the relevant evidence, enabling 'unfaithful' correct answers. The finding that random exploration achieves C* ≈ 63% but C ≈ 37% concretely demonstrates this failure mode and validates the need for grounding-aware scoring. However, the benchmark's reliance on GPT-4o-mini for both question generation and evaluation introduces a circularity risk, and the modest method gains (Fine-EQA C = 40.55 vs. FBE 38.60) suggest the exploration bottleneck is not yet cracked. Open questions include whether the EAC metric generalizes to outdoor or multi-floor settings, and whether the 9.7% ambiguity rate holds under different VLM evaluators.
