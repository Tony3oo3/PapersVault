## Problem

Existing low-light RAW image enhancement methods and datasets are limited to relatively brighter illuminance levels (0.03–5.0 lux), leaving the extremely dark regime (as low as 0.0001 lux) unexplored. In such conditions, obtaining well-exposed reference images via long exposure is unattainable due to residual noise and motion blur, and no paired dataset or dedicated enhancement framework exists for this regime.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250473/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-channel low-light RAW image (Bayer format, 3840×2160) at one of three calibrated illuminance levels (0.01–0.1, 0.001–0.01, or 0.0001–0.001 lux) and produces a three-channel enhanced sRGB image. Both the RAW and reference sRGB images are first encoded into a shared latent space before processing, and the final sRGB output is decoded from the reconstructed latent feature. The SIED dataset provides 1,680 paired images per illuminance level for each of the Sony and Canon camera subsets.

## Method

The framework encodes the low-light RAW and reference sRGB images into latent space via separate encoders, then applies a proposed Adaptive Illumination Correction Module (AICM) that estimates per-channel amplification coefficients from the RAW feature itself (rather than relying on reference exposure) to correct contrast in latent space and avoid exposure bias during diffusion. The encoded sRGB feature is progressively noised via forward diffusion, and a U-Net-based denoiser performs reverse diffusion conditioned on the AICM-corrected RAW feature to reconstruct the clean sRGB latent. Training uses a two-stage strategy: first optimizing encoders, decoders, and AICM with content and Retinex-based illumination correction losses, then fine-tuning the diffusion model with a content diffusion loss (L1 on predicted clean feature) and a novel color consistency loss that minimizes KL divergence between generated and reference color histograms. Inference uses DDIM sampling with 20 steps.

## Results

On the proposed SIED dataset (Canon subset), the method achieves PSNR of 24.85 / 24.02 / 22.52 dB and LPIPS of 0.360 / 0.379 / 0.435 at illuminance levels of 0.01–0.1, 0.001–0.01, and 0.0001–0.001 lux respectively, outperforming the strongest multi-stage baseline DNF by 0.82 / 0.55 / 0.89 dB in PSNR. On the Sony subset, gains over DNF are 0.74 / 0.60 / 0.68 dB in PSNR across the three illuminance levels. On the public SID benchmark, the method reaches 31.20 dB PSNR (Sony) and 28.90 dB PSNR (Fuji), surpassing DNF (30.62 / 28.71 dB) while estimating amplification adaptively rather than relying on ground-truth exposure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all nine baselines across every metric and illuminance level on both SIED subsets. Among single-stage methods (SID, DID, SGN, LLPackNet, RRT), the best performer SGN trails the proposed method by roughly 3.1 dB PSNR on the Canon 0.01–0.1 lux setting, and the gap widens to over 4 dB at the darkest level (0.0001–0.001 lux). Among multi-stage methods, DNF is the closest competitor, achieving 24.03 dB PSNR on Canon 0.01–0.1 lux versus 24.85 dB for the proposed method (a 0.82 dB margin), while RAWMamba lags DNF by about 1.4 dB in the same setting. On the public SID benchmark, the proposed method leads DNF by 0.58 dB PSNR on Sony and 0.19 dB on Fuji, and notably does so without using ground-truth exposure for pre-amplification, instead estimating the factor adaptively via AICM. The LPIPS advantage is particularly pronounced at the darkest illuminance (0.435 vs. 0.522 for DNF on Canon), indicating substantially better perceptual quality in extreme low-light conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-stage RAW-to-sRGB enhancement paradigm (DNF, MCR, RAWMamba) by replacing the deterministic reconstruction backbone with a conditional diffusion model, leveraging its generative prior and intrinsic denoising capability. It departs from prior methods that rely on ground-truth exposure values for pre-amplification by introducing the Adaptive Illumination Correction Module (AICM), which learns per-channel amplification coefficients directly from the low-light RAW feature in latent space. The paired-to-paired data synthesis strategy is conceptually distinct from prior dataset construction (e.g., SID, SDSD), which typically captures dark images by shortening exposure; here, dark images are synthesized from other dark images with calibrated sensor noise, enabling precise illuminance control down to 0.0001 lux.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SIED dataset fills a critical gap by providing the first well-calibrated paired benchmark at illuminance levels as low as 0.0001 lux, a regime where prior datasets (SID: 0.03–5.0 lux) simply cannot operate. The AICM addresses a practical deployment limitation—eliminating the need for reference exposure information—making the pipeline more applicable to real-world single-shot scenarios. Open questions include the computational cost of 20-step DDIM sampling for real-time or mobile deployment, generalization beyond the two camera models (Sony, Canon) tested, and whether the diffusion-based approach scales to video or higher-resolution RAW data.
