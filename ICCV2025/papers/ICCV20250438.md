## Problem

Current trajectory prediction methods model randomness in an undifferentiated, all-in-one manner (via GANs or VAEs), making it impossible to distinguish and separately describe the randomness triggered by different trajectory determinants such as intention changes versus social interactions. This limits both the explainability of predictions and the causal attribution of stochastic behavior to specific causes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250438/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes the ego agent's t_h-step 2D position history and all neighbors' t_h-step trajectories as input, and produces K future trajectory predictions (each t_f steps of 2D positions) by superposing three components: a linear least-squares base, a self-bias, and a resonance-bias. All spectral computations use the discrete Haar transform, and the self-bias is decoded at t_way equally-spaced waypoints then linearly interpolated to t_f steps, while the resonance-bias is decoded directly at all t_f future steps.

## Method

The Resonance (Re) model decomposes trajectory randomness into two independent vibrations—self-sourced and social-sourced—each parameterized by its own noise variable z, so that randomness attributable to different causes is learned and sampled separately. The self-sourced vibration is computed via a differential structure that compares Haar-spectral embeddings of the observed trajectory against its linear fit, encoded by a 4-layer Transformer T_s and decoded to waypoint biases. The social-sourced vibration (resonance-bias) compares spectral properties of the ego and each neighbor via element-wise multiplication, gathers these resonance features using angle-based partitioning into N_θ sectors, and encodes them with a 2-layer Transformer T_r. Both vibrations are trained end-to-end with L2 loss under best-of-K sampling, and the paper observes that the two learned vibration directions are nearly orthogonal, with self-biases capturing orientation/multipath changes and resonance-biases capturing velocity modifications due to social interactions.

## Results

On ETH-UCY, Re achieves an average ADE/FDE of 0.17/0.28 m (best-of-20), outperforming the strongest baseline SocialCircle+ (0.18/0.29) by approximately 8.0% ADE and 10.2% FDE on the eth scene, and 5.6%/3.7% on the overall average. On SDD, Re obtains 6.27/10.02 px, improving over SocialCircle+ (6.44/10.22) by 2.6%/1.9% and over the LLM-based LG-Traj (7.80/12.79) by 19.6%/20.2%. On NBA, Re reaches 0.60/0.78 m (t_f=5) and 1.12/1.38 m (t_f=10), achieving a 9.3% FDE improvement over SocialCircle+ at t_f=5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Re outperforms all 20 compared baselines on the ETH-UCY average ADE (0.17 m), tying with UPDD (0.17) but achieving a substantially lower FDE (0.28 vs. 0.32). Against the closest competitor SocialCircle+ (0.18/0.29), Re gains ~8% ADE and ~10% FDE on the eth scene specifically. On SDD, Re (6.27/10.02) beats SocialCircle+ (6.44/10.22) by 2.6%/1.9% and the LLM-driven LG-Traj (7.80/12.79) by a large margin of 19.6%/20.2%, while also surpassing the diffusion-based UPDD (6.59/13.90) by 4.9%/27.8% in FDE. On NBA, Re achieves the best ADE at both prediction horizons (0.60 at t_f=5, 1.12 at t_f=10), with a notable 9.3% FDE improvement over SocialCircle+ at t_f=5 (0.78 vs. 0.86), though the FDE gap narrows to 0.7% at t_f=10 (1.38 vs. 1.37, essentially a tie). The ablation studies further confirm that both the self-sourced vibration and the resonance-based social vibration contribute meaningfully, with the full model (a0) consistently outperforming any single-component variant across all three datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own line of spectral-based trajectory prediction (V2-Net, E-V2-Net, SocialCircle/SocialCircle+) by reframing the problem through the lens of vibration superposition and resonance. It departs from the dominant GAN/VAE paradigm for modeling trajectory randomness, which treats all stochasticity in a single latent space, by decomposing it into self-sourced and social-sourced vibrations with independent noise variables. The angle-based social interaction representation from SocialCircle is retained but reinterpreted as a 'resonance' mechanism that compares spectral properties of agents' observed trajectories, and the Haar transform (replacing Fourier in prior work) provides the spectral backbone for both vibration encoding and resonance computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled vibration framework offers a principled way to attribute trajectory changes to specific causes (intention vs. social interaction), improving interpretability beyond what monolithic latent-space models provide. The emergent near-orthogonality between self and social vibrations—without any explicit regularization—suggests the decomposition is capturing genuinely distinct behavioral axes, though this finding is currently qualitative and limited to visualized examples. A notable limitation is that the linear-base reference assumption degrades performance on dynamic sports scenarios (NBA), indicating the framework may not generalize to highly non-linear, fast-changing agent dynamics. Open questions include whether the vibration decomposition scales to more than two components (e.g., separating environmental, goal-directed, and social randomness) and whether the resonance analogy holds for non-pedestrian agents with fundamentally different interaction physics.
