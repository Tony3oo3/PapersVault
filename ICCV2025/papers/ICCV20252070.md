## Problem

Existing industrial anomaly generation methods either handle only one task (anomalies or normal products) or require a separate model per anomaly type, making it impossible to use a single unified model for diverse anomaly synthesis, authentic normal product generation, and pixel-accurate mask prediction simultaneously. This fragmentation limits the ability to flexibly support downstream synthesis-based AD, unsupervised AD, and supervised segmentation tasks with one generative pipeline.

## Input / Output

At inference, SeaS takes a randomly selected normal product image (512×512 RGB) as the starting point, adds noise to form an initial latent, and conditions the U-Net on a UA text prompt containing one normal token and N anomaly tokens. It produces three outputs: anomaly images with corresponding 512×512 binary masks (generated by the RMP branch during the final three denoising steps), and normal product images (using only the normal token as conditioning). Training uses only 60 normal images and one-third of masked anomaly images per anomaly type per product.

## Method

SeaS fine-tunes Stable Diffusion v1-4 with a Separation and Sharing strategy: a Decoupled Anomaly Alignment (DA) loss binds distinct anomaly attributes to separate anomaly tokens in the UA prompt (trained on abnormal images), while a Normal-image Alignment (NA) loss aligns the single normal token to globally consistent product surfaces (trained on normal images), both operating on a shared U-Net. The unbalanced token design—one normal token versus multiple anomaly tokens—exploits U-Net's differential learning to model low-variation normal surfaces and high-variation anomalies simultaneously, and recombining decoupled anomaly attributes at inference yields previously unseen anomalies. A Refined Mask Prediction (RMP) branch grafted onto the U-Net fuses discriminative U-Net decoder features with high-resolution VAE decoder features through three cascaded Mask Refinement Modules, progressively upsampling from 64×64 to 512×512 to produce pixel-accurate anomaly masks.

## Results

On supervised anomaly segmentation (Table 4), SeaS-generated image-mask pairs train BiSeNet V2, UPerNet, and LFD to average IoU of 57.66% (MVTec AD), 29.40% (VisA), and 35.37% (MVTec 3D AD), outperforming DFMGAN by +11.67%, +18.88%, and +18.95% IoU respectively. For generation quality (Table 1), SeaS achieves IS of 1.88/1.27/1.95 and IC-LPIPS of 0.34/0.26/0.30 on MVTec AD/VisA/MVTec 3D AD, the best fidelity and diversity among all compared methods. Downstream, SeaS-generated anomalies boost synthesis-based AD by +8.66% average pixel-level AP, and SeaS-generated normal images improve unsupervised AD by +1.10% average image-level AP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeaS outperforms all five baselines across every metric where they overlap. Against DFMGAN [11], SeaS achieves substantially higher IoU (57.66 vs 45.99 on MVTec AD; 29.40 vs 10.52 on VisA; 35.37 vs 16.42 on MVTec 3D AD) and better generation fidelity (IS 1.88 vs 1.72, IC-LPIPS 0.34 vs 0.20 on MVTec AD), while also producing accurate masks that DFMGAN cannot. Against AnomalyDiffusion [17], SeaS wins on IoU by +11.17% (MVTec AD), +11.71% (VisA), and +15.49% (MVTec 3D AD), and on generation diversity (IC-LPIPS 0.34 vs 0.32, IC-LPIPS(a) 0.18 vs 0.12 on MVTec AD), while AnomalyDiffusion cannot generate normal images at all. The three GAN-based baselines (Crop&Paste [23], SDGAN [28], Defect-GAN [42]) are only evaluated on MVTec AD generation quality and are clearly outperformed by SeaS on both IS (1.88 vs 1.51/1.71/1.69) and IC-LPIPS (0.34 vs 0.14/0.13/0.15), though they do not produce masks or normal images.

## Relation to Prior Work

SeaS builds directly on Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion architecture (VAE + U-Net + CLIP text encoder) and extends the line of few-shot industrial anomaly generation initiated by AnomalyDiffusion and DFMGAN. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it unifies anomaly image generation, normal product generation, and pixel-accurate mask prediction into a single model, whereas AnomalyDiffusion only generates anomalies and DFMGAN requires separate models per anomaly type; and (2) it introduces a 'Separation and Sharing' fine-tuning strategy where a shared U-Net is trained with decoupled objectives (DA loss for anomalies, NA loss for normals) using an unbalanced text prompt with one normal token and multiple anomaly tokens. The RMP branch for mask prediction also departs from prior diffusion-based mask methods (DiffuMask, DatasetDM) by fusing U-Net discriminative features with high-resolution VAE decoder features rather than relying solely on cross-attention maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified single-model approach eliminates the need for maintaining separate generators per anomaly type, which is a practical simplification for industrial deployment. The substantial improvements in downstream tasks (+8.66% pixel-level AP for synthesis-based AD, +12.79% IoU for supervised segmentation) demonstrate that generation quality directly translates to detection performance. Limitations include the per-product training requirement (one model per product category), restriction to 2D RGB images (MVTec 3D AD evaluated only on RGB), and dependence on a small number of masked anomaly images per type. Open questions include whether the separation-and-sharing strategy generalizes to 3D generation, whether a single model could handle multiple product categories simultaneously, and whether the approach extends to other domains with similar 'low-variation normal / high-variation anomaly' structure.
