## Problem

Existing diffusion-based detectors for domain generalization and adaptation (DDT, GDD) rely on computationally expensive multi-step denoising processes, incurring large inference costs that limit practical deployment. Moreover, these frameworks fail to exploit the multimodal (text-conditioned) capabilities of diffusion models and lack specialized mechanisms to balance source-domain fitness with cross-domain generalization. The paper addresses all three gaps: efficiency, underutilized multimodality, and the transfer of diffusion-derived robustness to standard detectors.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single RGB image and produces bounding boxes with class labels via a standard Faster R-CNN head, requiring no text prompts or target-domain data. During training, the auxiliary branch additionally consumes binary bounding-box masks (element-wise multiplication with the image) and class-name text prompts fed through the diffusion model's text encoder, enabling object-centered feature extraction. The frozen Stable Diffusion UNet (SD-1.5 or SD-2.1) operates at a single diffusion timestep rather than the multi-step schedule used by prior work, and the final feature pyramid is structurally aligned with standard ResNet outputs (256×2^(l-1) channels at four scales) for plug-and-play compatibility with detection heads.

## Method

The core contribution is a dual-branch diffusion detector that extracts features from a single-step forward diffusion process through a frozen Stable Diffusion UNet. Feature collection gathers 12 ResNet-block and 9 cross-attention feature groups from the UNet upsampling path, which are fused via channel-wise concatenation at each scale, bottleneck projection, and skip connections to form a four-level feature pyramid at 75% less inference time than GDD. The Object-centered Auxiliary Branch feeds box-masked images and class text prompts into the same diffusion backbone, producing domain-invariant, object-focused features; a consistency loss (MSE on features plus cross-head box and class alignment inspired by CrossKD) distills this invariance into the Ordinary Branch without affecting inference speed. A unified transfer framework then guides a standard Faster R-CNN (R101) detector through feature-level and object-level alignment with the diffusion detector, applicable to both DG (source-only alignment) and DA (pseudo-label generation on unlabeled target data).

## Results

On 5 DG benchmarks (BDD100K, FoggyCityscapes, Clipart/Comic/Watercolor, Diverse Weather, Cityscapes-C), the Diff. Detector (SD-1.5) achieves mAP improvements of {2.7, 0.6, 3.8, 4.8, 3.3}% over GDD while reducing inference time by 75% (164 ms vs. 789 ms). The Diff. Guided detector (Faster R-CNN R101) gains {0.4, 1.6, 0.8, 1.8, 1.6}% mAP over GDD on DG and {7.9, 6.6, 1.7}% mAP over DDT on 3 DA benchmarks. On the COCO Generalization Benchmark (11 target datasets), the method achieves 34.1% average mAP with only 1% COCO training data (vs. 24.9% for ConvNeXt-Base) and 51.3% with 100% COCO (vs. 44.2% for ConvNeXt-Base).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Diff. Detector (SD-1.5) outperforms GDD (SD-1.5) on all 5 DG benchmarks, with the largest gains on Diverse Weather (+4.8% mAP, 45.0 vs. 40.2) and Clipart/Comic/Watercolor (+3.8% mAP, 64.1/55.2/69.7 vs. 58.3/51.9/68.4), while achieving 75% lower inference time (164 ms vs. 789 ms). For the Diff. Guided detector, the method beats GDD (R101) on all 5 DG benchmarks (e.g., 54.1 vs. 52.5 on FoggyCityscapes, 39.7 vs. 38.1 on Diverse Weather) and DDT (R101) on all 3 DA benchmarks (51.3 vs. 43.4 on BDD100K, 56.6 vs. 50.0 on FoggyCityscapes, 58.2/50.5/68.0 vs. 55.6/50.2/63.7 on Clipart/Comic/Watercolor). On the COCO Generalization Benchmark, the method surpasses ConvNeXt-Base, Swin-Base, ViT-Base, and GLIP by 9.2–19.5% average mAP under 1% training data and 7.1–15.4% under 100% data, with the largest advantage on extreme domain shifts (Clipart: 44.0 vs. 18.8 for ConvNeXt at 1%). The one notable weakness is the Diff. Guided detector on Real-to-Artistic DG, where it slightly underperforms GDD (−0.3% on Clipart) and DivAlign (−3.2% on Comic, −0.8% on Watercolor), attributed to the enormous domain gap without target data access.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own prior work on diffusion-based detection (DDT for domain adaptation and GDD for domain generalization) by replacing computationally expensive multi-step denoising with single-step feature extraction from the UNet, reducing inference time by 75%. It departs from both DDT and GDD by introducing an object-centered auxiliary branch that exploits the text-conditioning capability of diffusion models (via box-masked images and class prompts), a dimension neither prior work leveraged. The consistency loss design draws on CrossKD's cross-head distillation idea, and the unified transfer framework consolidates the separate DA and DG alignment strategies from DDT and GDD into a single formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 75% inference speedup with simultaneous performance gains makes diffusion-based detectors more practical for deployment, addressing a key barrier to adoption. The COCO Generalization Benchmark (11 target domains, 1% and 100% training scales) provides a useful large-scale evaluation protocol that reveals diffusion detectors' particular strength in data-scarce and extreme-domain-shift settings. However, the paper acknowledges that transferring generalization from diffusion to standard detectors remains limited in Real-to-Artistic DG scenarios, and the method has only been validated on official Stable Diffusion weights without exploring instance-specialized diffusion models or engineering optimizations like TensorRT, leaving open whether the approach's advantages persist under more realistic deployment constraints.
