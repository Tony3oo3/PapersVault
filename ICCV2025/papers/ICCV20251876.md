## Problem

Scaling visual tokenizers for autoregressive image generation creates a reconstruction vs. generation dilemma: larger tokenizers improve reconstruction fidelity but produce increasingly complex latent spaces that degrade downstream AR generation quality. This trade-off has been observed but not adequately addressed in prior work, and no existing method simultaneously improves reconstruction, generation, and representation quality when scaling tokenizers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251876/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tokenizer takes 256×256 RGB images and produces 256 discrete visual tokens (1D sequence) via a hybrid CNN-Transformer encoder with vector quantization. The downstream LlamaGen AR model (up to 1.4B parameters) consumes these tokens with 1D absolute positional embeddings and a class label for conditional generation, producing a 256×256 image through iterative next-token prediction. The tokenizer decoder reconstructs images from the discrete tokens in a single forward pass, taking only ~10% of total inference time for the 2.9B tokenizer paired with a 1.4B AR model.

## Method

GigaTok uses a hybrid CNN-Transformer backbone where CNN blocks handle local detail capture and Transformer layers (Q-Former for 1D, ViT for 2D) provide scalable global modeling, with asymmetric scaling that prioritizes decoder depth/width over encoder. The key innovation is semantic regularization: a cosine-similarity loss aligns intermediate Transformer decoder features with frozen DINOv2-B representations, constraining latent space complexity and preventing the tokenizer from encoding overly fine-grained dependencies that hinder AR learnability. For billion-scale training, an entropy penalty on the VQ codebook is added to ensure high codebook utilization and stable convergence. The total training objective combines standard VQGAN losses (reconstruction, perceptual, PatchGAN, VQ codebook) with the semantic regularization term (λ=0.5) and entropy loss, scaling the tokenizer to 2.9B parameters while maintaining consistent improvements in rFID, gFID, and linear probing accuracy.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, GigaTok-XL-XXL (2.9B tokenizer) paired with a 1.4B LlamaGen-XXL AR model achieves a gFID of 1.98 (without CFG), setting a new state-of-the-art among LLM-style next-token-prediction autoregressive image generators. The same system attains a linear probing accuracy of 74.0%, the highest reported for visual generative pretraining. For reconstruction, GigaTok-B-L (622M) with a DINO discriminator reaches rFID 0.51, while the 2.9B model achieves rFID 0.79 without the discriminator. The 111M AR Probing model trained on GigaTok-XL-XXL already reaches gFID 3.15 and Lin Acc 72.0, demonstrating that the tokenizer's quality transfers even to small downstream models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among LLM-style next-token-prediction AR generators, GigaTok-XL-XXL + LlamaGen-XXL (gFID 1.98) outperforms IBQ-XXL (2.05), Open-MAGVIT2-XL (2.53), LlamaGen-XXL with its original tokenizer (2.34), and ViT-VQGAN + VIM-Large (4.17*). Against the next-scale-prediction VAR-d30 (gFID 1.92), GigaTok is within 0.06 gFID while using a simpler NTP paradigm more compatible with language models. For masked-generation baselines, GigaTok substantially outperforms TiTok-S + MaskGIT-UViT-L (1.97) and VQGAN + MaskGIT (6.18*). Among continuous-token diffusion baselines, GigaTok's 1.98 gFID trails VA-VAE + LightningDiT (1.35) and SD-VAE + SiT + REPA (1.42), but those methods use diffusion rather than autoregressive next-token prediction. On representation quality, GigaTok-XL-XXL + LlamaGen-XXL achieves 74.0% linear probing accuracy, matching SD-VAE + SiT + REPA (74.6%) and surpassing BiGR-XL (69.8%) and LlamaGen-L (40.5%). The 622M GigaTok-B-L already beats the 2.1B IBQ-XXL and 1.5B Open-MAGVIT2-XL on gFID, demonstrating that tokenizer quality matters more than raw generator size.

## Relation to Prior Work

GigaTok builds directly on the VQGAN training framework (reconstruction, perceptual, GAN, and codebook losses) and extends it with two key additions: semantic regularization via alignment to frozen DINOv2 features, and an entropy penalty for codebook utilization borrowed from MAGVIT. The semantic regularization idea connects to a broader trend of leveraging pre-trained visual representations for generative modeling (REPA, VA-VAE), but GigaTok is the first to identify it as the specific mechanism that resolves the reconstruction-vs-generation dilemma in tokenizer scaling—a problem observed anecdotally in prior work (ViT-VQGAN, TiTok) but never diagnosed or solved. The hybrid CNN-Transformer backbone with Q-Former for 1D tokenization departs from the purely CNN-based VQGAN lineage and the purely ViT-based TiTok/ViT-VQGAN, positioning itself as a scalable middle ground.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By resolving the reconstruction-vs-generation dilemma, GigaTok provides the first principled recipe for scaling visual tokenizers to billion-parameter scale, which is a prerequisite for competitive AR image generation and future unified multimodal models. The practical findings (1D tokenizers outscale 2D, decoder should be larger than encoder, entropy loss is essential at billion scale) give the community concrete design guidance that was previously absent. Open questions include whether the semantic regularization approach generalizes to video tokenization, whether the 1D advantage persists at even larger resolutions or with different downstream architectures, and whether the DINOv2 alignment can be replaced by self-supervised alternatives to reduce dependency on a specific pre-trained model.
