## Problem

Existing AVSE methods fuse audio and visual modalities primarily along the temporal dimension, overlooking the well-documented correlation between visual appearance (gender, body shape) and vocal frequency characteristics (pitch, spectral shape). Moreover, prior work does not explicitly leverage high-level semantic context to guide low-level signal reconstruction, despite cognitive science evidence (phonemic restoration, McGurk effect) showing that humans rely on semantic priors to recover corrupted speech segments.

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy speech waveform (converted via STFT into magnitude, real, and imaginary channels concatenated into a 3-channel time-frequency tensor) and grayscale facial video frames (H×W×T_v at ~25 fps) as input, and produces an enhanced speech waveform via ISTFT of a reconstructed complex spectrogram. Notably, the audio encoder preserves fine-grained time-frequency resolution (only 2× frequency downsampling) rather than compressing the spectrogram as in prior AVSE methods, and the visual features must be upsampled in both time and frequency to align with the audio embedding.

## Method

CogCM is a GAN-based framework whose generator contains three cognition-inspired modules: a Semantic Context Module (SeCM) that extracts high-level semantic embeddings from audio-visual inputs (using a VSR encoder or pre-trained AVHuBERT), a Semantic-to-Signal Guidance Module (SSGM) that uses channel-swapping blocks and context-gating blocks to let semantic context calibrate signal features along both temporal and frequency dimensions, and Signal Context Modules (SiCM) built on bidirectional Mamba plus Conformer-style convolutions for sequential dependency modeling. A key design choice is the TF-Upsampler, which simultaneously upsamples semantic features in time and frequency so that visual cues (e.g., speaker gender implying pitch range) directly inform spectral recovery—a contribution absent in prior AVSE work. The SSGM guides signal modeling sequentially (time first, then frequency), and the final output combines an IRM-predicted magnitude with a complex decoder that compensates spectral details using the noisy phase. Training uses magnitude MSE, complex MSE, and a PESQ-based adversarial loss with a CMGAN-style discriminator.

## Results

On the primary LRS3+DNS benchmark, CogCM achieves SDR of 7.28 dB, PESQ of 2.269, and STOI of 0.842 at -15 dB SNR, surpassing the previous SoTA (DualAVSE) by 63.6% in SDR, 58.1% in PESQ, and 20.3% in STOI. At 0 dB SNR, CogCM reaches SDR 15.34, PESQ 3.235, and STOI 0.939. On the 3rd AVSEC dataset, CogCM attains PESQ 2.633, STOI 0.895, and SI-SDR 10.646, outperforming AVDPRNN 2024 (PESQ 2.132, STOI 0.818, SI-SDR 8.867). On {GRID, TCD-TIMIT}+Nonspeech, CogCM improves over EANet by 21.2% in PESQi, 35.7% in STOIi, and 28.7% in SI-SDRi.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CogCM outperforms all 10 compared baselines across every metric and SNR level tested. Against the strongest AVSE baseline DualAVSE 2023 [69], CogCM gains +2.83 dB SDR, +0.834 PESQ, and +0.142 STOI at -15 dB, with the gap widening at higher SNRs (e.g., +3.02 dB SDR, +0.994 PESQ at 0 dB). Compared to VisualVoice 2021 [24], CogCM improves SDR by +4.76 dB and PESQ by +0.952 at -15 dB. Against the audio-only DEMUCS 2020 [14], the improvement is even larger: +4.95 dB SDR and +1.059 PESQ at -15 dB, confirming the substantial benefit of visual cues. On the {GRID, TCD-TIMIT}+Nonspeech benchmark, CogCM surpasses EANet [78] (the prior SoTA) by 21.2% PESQi, 35.7% STOIi, and 28.7% SI-SDRi, and also outperforms Percepnet+ [25], Cochleanet [26], DCCRN+ [47], Dual-stream [43], and Balasubramanian et al. [4]. On the 3rd AVSEC dataset, CogCM leads AVDPRNN 2024 by +0.501 PESQ and +0.077 STOI, and A-V DEMUCS 2024 by +1.382 PESQ and +0.251 STOI. No baseline matches or exceeds CogCM on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

CogCM builds on the CMGAN architecture (Cao et al., 2022) for its GAN-based training framework and the Mamba state-space model (Gu & Dao, 2023) for sequence modeling in its Signal Context Module. It departs from prior AVSE methods (DualAVSE, VisualVoice, MuSE) in two key ways: (1) it introduces a hierarchical separation of semantic context modeling (SeCM) from signal context modeling (SiCM), with an explicit top-down guidance mechanism (SSGM) inspired by cognitive science findings on phonemic restoration and the McGurk effect; and (2) it leverages visual cues for frequency-domain spectral recovery, an aspect largely overlooked by existing AVSE work that focuses on temporal fusion. The channel-swapping mechanism in SSGM is directly adapted from CEN (Wang et al., NeurIPS 2020), and the semantic context is extracted using frozen pre-trained AV speech recognition models (AVHuBERT, VATLM), repurposing representation learning for enhancement rather than recognition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly modeling the interplay between high-level semantic context and low-level signal context—guided by visual cues in both temporal and frequency dimensions—yields substantially better AVSE than prior temporal-only fusion approaches, with particularly large gains at very low SNR (-15 dB). The finding that visual appearance cues (gender, body shape) provide useful priors for frequency-domain spectral recovery opens a new research direction for multimodal signal processing beyond speech. Open questions include whether the cognitive framing is truly necessary or merely a convenient architectural justification, whether the frozen pre-trained semantic encoders limit adaptability to out-of-distribution speakers or noise types, and whether the approach scales to real-time deployment given the multi-module pipeline. The reliance on synthetic noise mixing for evaluation also leaves open how well the method generalizes to truly unpredictable real-world noise.
