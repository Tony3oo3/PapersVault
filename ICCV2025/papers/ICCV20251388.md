## Problem

MLLMs incur substantial computational overhead from processing hundreds to thousands of visual tokens through deep LLM backbones. While token-wise redundancy (e.g., FastV) has been exploited to prune unimportant visual tokens, layer-wise redundancy specific to visual tokens remains unquantified and unexploited. The paper asks: to what extent are MLLM layers ineffective for visual tokens, and can this be leveraged for efficiency without retraining?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251388/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ShortV operates on standard MLLM pipelines: an RGB image is encoded by CLIP-ViT and projected into visual tokens (576 for LLaVA-1.5 at 336×336, up to 2880 for LLaVA-NeXT), which are concatenated with text prompt tokens and fed into the LLM backbone. The output is a standard text response. The method modifies only the internal computation of selected LLM layers at inference time—no changes to input format, output structure, or model parameters are required.

## Method

ShortV introduces a Layer Contribution (LC) metric that quantifies a layer's importance for a given token type by computing the KL divergence between the model's output logits when that layer's transformations on those tokens are frozen versus the original model. Using LC, the paper reveals that MLLM layers are far more ineffective for visual tokens than for text tokens, with the last layer contributing zero to visual token processing. The method then replaces the N layers with the lowest LC scores for visual tokens with sparse ShortV layers, in which visual tokens are frozen: they do not attend to other tokens, do not pass through the FFN, and only text tokens function as queries through the attention projections. This is entirely training-free with no parameter updates, and is orthogonal to token pruning methods like FastV, allowing both to be applied simultaneously for compounded efficiency gains.

## Results

ShortV achieves a 50% FLOPs reduction on LLaVA-NeXT-13B (N=24, 41.0 TFLOPs vs. 81.8 vanilla) while maintaining or slightly exceeding vanilla performance across all benchmarks (e.g., 70.2 MMBench vs. 69.3 vanilla, 39.9 MMStar vs. 39.9 vanilla). On LLaVA-1.5-7B, freezing 19 of 32 layers (55% FLOPs ratio) yields 75.7 VQAv2, 60.9 GQA, and 64.8 MMBench, matching or exceeding the vanilla model. The method can freeze visual tokens in approximately 60% of MLLM layers with nearly no performance degradation, and even at ~80% frozen layers, models retain over 90% of vanilla performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ShortV consistently outperforms both FastV and VTW at comparable or lower FLOPs ratios across all four model variants. Against FastV (K=2, R=50%), ShortV achieves higher scores on VQAv2 (e.g., 79.7 vs. 76.8 on LLaVA-NeXT-13B), GQA (63.6 vs. 62.9), and Flickr30K CIDEr (67.5 vs. 66.0) while using a lower FLOPs ratio (50% vs. 51%). Against VTW, ShortV shows a particularly large advantage on VQAv2 (79.7 vs. 77.0) and Flickr30K CIDEr (67.5 vs. 56.6 on LLaVA-NeXT-13B), where VTW's aggressive token withdrawal causes substantial captioning degradation. On MMBench, ShortV edges out both baselines (70.2 vs. 68.5 FastV, 69.1 VTW on LLaVA-NeXT-13B). The key distinction is that ShortV reduces per-token computation rather than token count, making it orthogonal to and stackable with FastV for further efficiency gains (29% FLOPs ratio when combined).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the layer-redundancy analysis of ShortGPT (Men et al., 2024) from text-only LLMs to multimodal LLMs, introducing the Layer Contribution (LC) metric to measure per-modality layer importance via KL divergence on output logits. It departs from the dominant token-pruning paradigm (FastV, VTW) by targeting layer-wise rather than token-wise redundancy, and its ShortV layer design draws inspiration from the NAAViT self-attention blocks in SAISA. The key conceptual departure is the discovery that MLLM layers are far more ineffective for visual tokens (~60%) than for text tokens (~25%), a modality-asymmetric redundancy pattern not present in text-only LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ShortV offers a practical, training-free 50% FLOPs reduction on LLaVA-NeXT-13B with negligible performance loss, and its orthogonality to token pruning (demonstrated with FastV) enables compounding efficiency gains. The empirical finding that most MLLM layers contribute minimally to visual token processing provides a new lens for understanding cross-modal computation and could inform future architectural design. Limitations include evaluation restricted to the LLaVA family, dependence on a small calibration set for LC scoring, and untested generalization to other MLLM architectures (e.g., those with cross-attention or different projector designs).
