## Problem

Existing 3D human pose and shape estimators are subject-agnostic, estimating shape, pose, and pelvis position simultaneously per frame, which causes shape inconsistencies over time and entangles pose errors with shape errors. Additionally, heavy reliance on 2D reprojection objectives during fitting degrades 3D accuracy, particularly for uncommon poses and absolute pelvis position. The paper asks whether explicitly calibrating a user's body shape once and then using it as a fixed condition in a personalized pose fitting loop can resolve both issues.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a monocular RGB video with known camera focal length and optionally the user's height and weight. Shape is calibrated once from a single rest-pose frame using 2D keypoints from an off-the-shelf detector; the resulting SMPL shape vector β ∈ ℝ¹⁰ is then frozen for all subsequent frames. Per-frame output consists of 23 axis-angle joint rotations, pelvis orientation, and pelvis position in camera coordinates, with the internal representation being a point cloud of 238 mesh vertices plus 45 joints (283 × 3 coordinates) that is converted back to SMPL parameters via a differentiable Point Fitter.

## Method

PHD decouples body fitting into two stages: SHAPify, an optimization-based shape calibrator that minimizes 2D keypoint reprojection plus height/weight regularization on a single rest-pose image, and PointDiT, a shape-conditioned point diffusion transformer that serves as a 3D pose prior during fitting. PointDiT adapts the DiT architecture with rectified flow (enabling sampling in as few as 5 steps), conditions on ViTPose image tokens and 2D heatmaps via self-attention, and injects the calibrated shape β through adaLN-Zero. During fitting, a Point Distillation Sampling loop iteratively denoises a perturbed point cloud with PointDiT and applies two losses—pelvis-aligned L2 between sampled and fitted points, and a parameter-space penalty via the Point Fitter—alongside the standard 2D keypoint reprojection term. The key insight is that representing pose as 3D body surface points (rather than joint angles) creates a stronger correlation with both the image and shape conditions, enabling the prior to correct implausible poses that 2D-only fitting cannot resolve.

## Results

On EMDB1, PHD with sampled initialization achieves MPJPE-PA of 49.2 mm and MVE-PA of 59.1 mm, substantially outperforming ScoreHMR (82.3 / 101.9 mm). When refining HMR2.0b initializations, PHD reduces MPJPE-PA from 77.9 to 47.4 mm (−30.5), while ScoreHMR only achieves 70.0 mm. With the SOTA CameraHMR initialization, PHD further improves MPJPE from 70.3 to 62.5 mm and MVE from 81.7 to 74.6 mm, whereas ScoreHMR degrades performance (MPJPE 74.9, MVE 89.0). For absolute pose, PHD achieves Pelvis Error of 91.5 mm and C-MPJPE of 115.8 mm with sampled init, compared to ScoreHMR's 154.3 / 154.4 mm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PHD decisively outperforms ScoreHMR [67] across all initialization strategies: with sampled init, MPJPE-PA drops from 82.3 to 49.2 mm; with HMR2.0b init, from 70.0 to 47.4 mm; and with CameraHMR init, from 45.0 to 42.4 mm. Against the HMR2.0b [15] regressor alone, PHD's fitting loop reduces MPJPE-PA by 30.5 mm (77.9→47.4) and MVE-PA by 35.4 mm (93.9→58.5), demonstrating strong correction of implausible bending poses. Against the SOTA CameraHMR [54] regressor, PHD improves MPJPE by 7.8 mm and MVE by 7.1 mm, while ScoreHMR actually degrades both metrics. For shape estimation, SHAPify achieves 11.29 mm mean joint error and 9.18 mm mean vertex error, outperforming NLF [61] (19.36/20.61), SHAPY [7] (22.94/21.38), and TokenHMR [12] (25.15/27.46). The 6D angular representation from Cho et al. [5] yields MPJPE of 177.9 mm versus PHD's point-based 75.6 mm, confirming the superiority of the point cloud pose representation. On absolute pose (Table 2), PHD reduces Pelvis Error from 154.3 to 91.5 mm and C-MPJPE from 154.4 to 115.8 mm over ScoreHMR with sampled init.

## Relation to Prior Work

PHD builds on the Diffusion Transformer architecture (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023) and the rectified flow formulation (Liu et al., ICLR 2023; Esser et al., ICML 2024) to create a shape-conditioned point diffusion prior for 3D body pose. It departs from the closest prior work, ScoreHMR (Stathopoulos et al., CVPR 2024), in two key ways: (1) it introduces a personalization stage (SHAPify) that calibrates user-specific shape before pose fitting, breaking the entanglement of shape and pose that plagues generalized regressors, and (2) it represents pose as body surface points rather than joint angles, which the authors show correlates more strongly with image and shape conditioning signals. The Point Distillation Sampling loss, inspired by Score Distillation Sampling, is a novel mechanism for iteratively injecting 3D prior guidance into a 2D-keypoint-driven fitting loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling of shape calibration from pose fitting addresses a practical bottleneck in video-based body tracking for AR/VR and avatar applications, where consistent identity over time is essential. The finding that per-subject mean shape performs worse than zero shape challenges a widely adopted practice in the field and suggests that shape averaging introduces systematic errors that compound in pose fitting. Open questions include whether the BEDLAM-trained prior generalizes to diverse populations and clothing, whether the calibration step can be made fully automatic without a rest-pose frame, and whether the iterative sampling-fitting loop can be accelerated for real-time use. The plug-and-play design (compatible with HMR2.0b, CameraHMR, etc.) makes the contribution broadly applicable beyond the specific pipeline.
