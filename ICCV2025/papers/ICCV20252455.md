## Problem

Existing lidar generative models either produce unconditional point clouds without semantic control or rely on latent diffusion architectures (e.g., LiDM) that introduce substantial compression loss and fail to generalize across domains. The only prior semantic-to-lidar method, LiDM, generates point clouds with coarse geometry, structural inconsistencies, and significant noise, limiting its utility for data augmentation in driving perception. This paper addresses the gap by enabling high-fidelity, semantically-guided lidar synthesis that operates directly in the native lidar space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252455/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 2D semantic segmentation map (projected into a range-image representation following RangeNet++) as conditioning input and produces a corresponding lidar point cloud in the same projected range-image format. For the lidar translation task, the input is a synthetic lidar range image with per-point semantic labels, and the output is a real-domain range image that preserves the original semantic layout. The range-image representation relaxes the problem from 3D conditional generation to 2D, and circular convolution is used in the backbone to respect the panoramic geometry of the projection.

## Method

SG-LDM discards the VAE-based latent diffusion architecture entirely, performing DDPM directly on the 2D range-image representation with a circular-convolution backbone. The key architectural contribution is a Semantic Alignment module: a three-layer CNN that projects the diffusion encoder's latent features into the dimensionality of a downscaled semantic map, enforcing a cosine-similarity loss that is applied only during unconditional training steps with a dynamic weight λ = 1 − t/T that fades as noise increases. This prevents the unconditional branch from degrading when classifier-free guidance is used, which otherwise causes a marked performance drop. Training alternates between conditional (80%) and unconditional (20%) modes, and inference uses DDIM with CFG at scale 2 for 50-step sampling. The paper further introduces a lidar translation framework that applies forward diffusion to synthetic data up to an intermediate timestep (optimal at 50%) and then reverse diffusion with SG-LDM to reconstruct real-like point clouds while preserving per-point semantic labels.

## Results

On SemanticKITTI (same-domain), SG-LDM achieves a FRID of 4.4, FSVD of 10.5, FPVD of 7.9, and MMD of 1.31×10⁻⁴, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art LiDM (FRID 22.9, MMD 3.16×10⁻⁴) by 81% in FRID and 59% in MMD. On the cross-domain SynLiDAR test set, SG-LDM reduces FRID from 184.1 (LiDM) to 118.7 (36% improvement) and FSVD from 147.9 to 78.0 (47% improvement). For data augmentation, SG-LDM + Lidar Translation achieves 63.7% mIoU on SemanticKITTI segmentation (+3.4 over source-only), outperforming DGT (63.3%) but trailing PCT (64.7%) which also translates appearance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LiDM [45], the primary semantic-to-lidar diffusion baseline, SG-LDM wins decisively on all perceptual metrics (FRID, FSVD, FPVD) in both same-domain and cross-domain settings, with an 81% FRID reduction on SemanticKITTI and 36–47% reductions on SynLiDAR; however, LiDM retains a slight edge on JSD (0.072 vs 0.084) and MMD on SynLiDAR (1.48 vs 3.63×10⁻⁴), which the authors attribute to LiDM's noisy points not being fully captured by statistical metrics. Compared to the older GAN-based LiDARGen [77] and the generic Latent Diffusion [46] baseline, SG-LDM improves FRID by roughly 10× and 5× respectively. In the data augmentation setting (Table 2), SG-LDM-generated data yields 62.1% mIoU (+1.8), while LiDM-generated data actually degrades performance to 59.7% (−0.6). The proposed SG-LDM + Lidar Translation framework achieves 63.7% mIoU, surpassing DGT [71] (63.3%) and all Group A augmentation methods (Jittering 61.2%, Dropout 61.4%, PointAug 62.6%), but falls 1.0% short of PCT [67] (64.7%), which additionally translates point cloud appearance beyond density.

## Relation to Prior Work

SG-LDM builds directly on the DDPM training objective, DDIM sampling, and classifier-free guidance, but deliberately departs from the latent diffusion architecture (VAE + diffusion in compressed space) that LiDM and prior lidar diffusion works adopt. The key architectural departure is operating the diffusion process directly in the native range-image space, eliminating the VAE's compression loss and domain-specificity. The semantic alignment module is a novel loss that addresses a previously unaddressed failure mode of CFG in conditional generation, where the unconditional branch degrades when trained alongside the conditional one. The lidar translation framework repurposes the forward/reverse diffusion process as a domain-bridging mechanism, positioning it as a diffusion-based alternative to GAN-based translators like PCT and DGT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that for lidar point cloud generation, the standard latent diffusion recipe inherited from image generation is actively harmful, and that a simpler native-space diffusion model with semantic alignment substantially outperforms it. This has practical implications for autonomous driving data pipelines where semantic-to-lidar synthesis can augment rare scenarios (accidents, pedestrians) without manual annotation. However, the translation framework only transfers point density, not appearance, leaving a gap versus GAN-based methods that handle both. Open questions include whether domain-robust VAEs could recover the efficiency of latent diffusion without sacrificing fidelity, and whether the semantic alignment strategy generalizes beyond lidar to other structured 3D generation tasks.
