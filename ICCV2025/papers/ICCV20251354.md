## Problem

Vision-based 3D occupancy prediction faces a critical trade-off in 2D-to-3D view transformation: depth-based LSS suffers from premature convergence, poor occlusion handling, and sparse distant features, while cross-attention lacks explicit geometric grounding. Extending these frameworks to jointly predict motion flow further introduces a conflicting representational demand, as features must simultaneously encode static semantics and dynamic motion, and severe class imbalance from long-tail distributions and empty voxels compounds training difficulty.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251354/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes N_I surround-camera RGB images (default 256×704) and cached historical frame BEV features for temporal fusion, optionally augmented with a sparse depth signal mapped from LiDAR points. It produces a dense 3D semantic occupancy grid of 200×200×16 voxels (0.4 m resolution, ±40 m in X/Y, −1 to 5.4 m in Z) with 17–18 semantic classes, and optionally a co-located flow grid storing per-voxel 2D displacement vectors in the X-Y plane. The architecture is purely convolutional, with variants ranging from a real-time 2D-mini model (30.5 FPS) to a full 3D model (6.0 FPS) on an RTX 4090.

## Method

ALOcc introduces three targeted mechanisms. First, an occlusion-aware adaptive lifting replaces hard rounding with trilinear soft filling and propagates depth probabilities into occluded regions via a Bayesian conditional probability (intra-object) and MLP-estimated spatial offsets (inter-object), stabilized by a depth denoising schedule that cosine-anneals ground-truth depth weight from 1 to 0. Second, a semantic prototype-based occupancy head uses shared per-class prototypes to compute segmentation logits for both 2D and 3D features, with conditional loss (only for classes present in the scene) and uncertainty-guided voxel sampling to focus training on hard, under-represented regions. Third, a BEV cost volume for flow prediction collapses volumetric features into a downsampled BEV map, warps the prior frame's BEV via ego-motion, and computes cosine similarity within a local search window; the final flow is a weighted sum of bin centers from a predicted probability distribution, supervised by a hybrid L2 + cosine regression loss plus a bin classification loss, decoupling motion estimation from the semantic feature pathway.

## Results

On Occ3D (camera-only, ResNet-50, 256×704), ALOcc-3D achieves mIoU_D^m = 39.3 and mIoU^m = 45.5, surpassing the best prior camera-only method COTR (44.5) by 1.0 points. With depth input (C+D), ALOcc-3D reaches mIoU^m = 54.5, outperforming LiDAR-fusion methods like EFFOcc (52.8) and SDGOcc (51.7). On OpenOcc for joint occupancy-flow prediction, ALOcc-Flow-3D (Intern-T) achieves Occ Score = 45.3, mAVE_TP = 0.469, and RayIoU = 44.4, exceeding the best prior method F-Occ (Occ Score 41.0) by 4.3 points. The lightweight ALOcc-2D-mini variant runs at 30.5 FPS while maintaining mIoU^m = 41.4, outperforming all existing real-time methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among camera-only methods at 256×704 with ResNet-50, ALOcc-3D (mIoU^m = 45.5) outperforms COTR (44.5), SurroundSDF (42.4), BEVDetOcc-SF (41.9), and FB-Occ (39.8) by 1.0–5.7 points. The lightweight ALOcc-2D-mini (30.5 FPS) beats FlashOCC (29.6 FPS, mIoU^m = 32.0) by 9.4 points while matching its speed, and surpasses OPUS (8.2 FPS, 36.2) by 5.2 points. With depth input, ALOcc-3D (54.5) exceeds LiDAR-fusion baselines EFFOcc (52.8) and SDGOcc (51.7) despite using only a sparser depth signal rather than full LiDAR. At the Swin-Base 512×1408 scale, ALOcc-3D (60.0) outperforms FusionOcc (56.6) and BEVFusion (54.0) by 3.4 and 6.0 points respectively. On the joint flow prediction benchmark (OpenOcc), ALOcc-Flow-3D with Intern-T achieves Occ Score 45.3 and RayIoU 44.4, beating F-Occ (41.0, 39.9), CascadeFlow (40.9, 39.6), and FB-Occ (39.2, 39.0) by 4.3–6.1 Occ Score points, while also improving mAVE_TP from 0.493 (F-Occ) to 0.469 (lower is better). The paper does not report any setting where ALOcc loses to a directly comparable baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

ALOcc extends the depth-based Lift-Splat-Shoot (LSS) pipeline [44, 14] by replacing its hard rounding-based projection with a differentiable trilinear soft-filling and adding occlusion-aware probability transfer (both intra-object via Bayesian conditional depth-to-length conversion and inter-object via learned offsets). For flow prediction, it adapts the BEV cost volume construction from stereo matching [22] to the occupancy setting, decoupling motion estimation from the semantic feature stream. The semantic prototype head draws on the shared-prototype idea from Mask2Former [8] and uncertainty-guided sampling from PointRend [17], but applies them jointly across 2D and 3D domains for the first time in occupancy prediction. Overall, the paper is a methodological refinement of the standard LSS-based occupancy pipeline rather than a departure from it, and it remains purely convolutional in a field increasingly adopting transformer-based view transformation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing SOTA on both semantic occupancy (Occ3D) and joint occupancy-flow (OpenOcc) benchmarks with a purely convolutional architecture, ALOcc demonstrates that transformer-based view transformation is not a prerequisite for top performance, which is practically significant for deployment on resource-constrained hardware. The family of variants (ALOcc-3D, ALOcc-2D, ALOcc-2D-mini) spanning 5.5–30.5 FPS provides a concrete efficiency-accuracy spectrum that prior work has not offered in this form. Open questions include whether the depth denoising module (which relies on ground-truth depth during training) translates to unsupervised or self-supervised settings, and whether the BEV cost volume approach generalizes to 3D flow (including Z-axis motion) or longer temporal horizons beyond the single prior frame.
