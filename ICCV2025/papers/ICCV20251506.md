## Problem

Existing feature-based knowledge distillation methods for dense prediction tasks rely on static, teacher-driven or heuristic-based feature selection that remains fixed throughout training. This ignores the student's evolving learning state, provides the same knowledge regardless of the student's progress, and typically neglects channel-wise feature importance, leading to inefficient or even misleading distillation as the student's needs change across epochs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251506/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (1088×800 for detection, 512×1024 for segmentation) and produces task-specific outputs: bounding boxes with class labels for object detection or pixel-level class masks for semantic segmentation. Distillation operates on intermediate feature maps extracted at the FPN neck (detection) or predicted segmentation maps (segmentation), with M=6 adaptive masks for detection and M=19 masks (one per Cityscapes class) for segmentation. The student and teacher feature maps are aligned via a 1×1 convolutional adaptation layer before masked distillation.

## Method

ACAM-KD introduces two coupled modules. First, Student-Teacher Cross-Attention Feature Fusion (STCAFF) computes cross-attention where the teacher's feature map provides the query and the student's feature map provides both key and value, yielding fused features that dynamically evolve as the student learns. Second, Adaptive Spatial-Channel Masking (ASCM) applies two sets of learnable selection units—channel-wise (m^c) and spatial (m^s)—to the fused features via sigmoid gating, producing M importance masks that are continuously updated during training rather than fixed offline. A Dice-coefficient-based diversity loss prevents mask collapse, and the total objective combines the task loss, spatial and channel-wise masked distillation losses, and the diversity term. The key distinction from prior work is that both the feature fusion and the masking are driven by the joint, evolving teacher-student interaction, so the regions and channels emphasized for distillation shift adaptively as the student's representations improve.

## Results

On COCO2017 with a ResNet-101 teacher and ResNet-50 student, ACAM-KD achieves 41.2 mAP (RetinaNet), 41.4 mAP (Faster R-CNN), and 42.5 mAP (RepPoints), surpassing the best prior KD methods (MasKD, FreeKD) by 1.3, 0.6, and 1.4 mAP respectively. With a ResNeXt-101 teacher, gains increase to 4.1–4.2 mAP over the second-best method across all three detector families. On Cityscapes semantic segmentation, ACAM-KD improves mIoU by 3.09 over the DeepLabV3-MobileNetV2 baseline (76.21 vs. 73.12) and by 0.79 over the best competing KD method (CIRKD, 75.42).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACAM-KD outperforms all 17 compared baselines across every detector and segmentation configuration. The closest competitors are MasKD [9] and FreeKD [29], which also use learnable masking or prompting but keep masks static throughout training; ACAM-KD surpasses them by 1.3–1.4 mAP on COCO (ResNet teacher) and by 0.5–0.6 mAP with a ResNeXt teacher. Earlier heuristic or teacher-driven methods (FGD [26], GID [6], FRS [7], FKD [28], COFD [8]) trail by 1.5–2.0 mAP, confirming that static teacher-defined attention is a significant bottleneck. FitNet [17], the simplest feature-matching baseline, is outperformed by 3.8–4.8 mAP, highlighting the value of adaptive masking. For segmentation, ACAM-KD beats the strongest prior methods (CIRKD [24], MasKD [9], LAD [14]) by 0.5–0.8 mIoU on Cityscapes, with the largest absolute gain (3.09 mIoU) over the un-distilled MobileNetV2 student. No baseline matches or exceeds ACAM-KD on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in feature-based KD for dense prediction, where distillation masks are either derived from teacher activations (FKD, FGD), predefined heuristics (FGFI, GID), or learned offline in a single shot from a frozen teacher (MasKD, FreeKD). By introducing student-teacher cross-attention (teacher as query, student as key/value) to generate fused features that evolve during training, ACAM-KD repurposes the transformer attention mechanism as a dynamic interaction channel rather than a static feature selector. The joint spatial-channel adaptive masking with learnable selection units that update throughout distillation extends prior spatial-only masking (MasKD) and channel-only alignment (CWD) into a unified, co-adaptive framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that making masks merely learnable from the teacher (39.9 mAP) barely improves over fixed teacher masks (39.8 mAP), while adding student-teacher cross-attention yields a 1.3 mAP jump, suggests that the student's evolving feature space is the critical missing ingredient in adaptive KD — a useful diagnostic for the field. The approach is validated across three detector families and three segmentation architectures on two benchmarks, lending practical credibility. Open questions include whether the cross-attention overhead is acceptable for very large feature maps, whether the method transfers to transformer-based backbones (ViT, Swin), and whether the gains persist when the teacher-student capacity gap is small.
