## Problem

Mainstream few-shot segmentation methods follow an image-to-mask decoding paradigm that decodes masks from support-query feature matching, making them heavily dependent on support sample quality and vulnerable to class bias from intra-class variation and inter-class similarity. Existing diffusion-based approaches either start from pure Gaussian noise (losing the prior information of the query image) or remain stuck in the matching-based decoding framework. The paper asks whether mask decoding can be reformulated as a denoising process that starts from a noisy version of the query image, thereby combining the prior advantage of image-to-mask decoding with the distributional robustness of denoising diffusion.

## Input / Output

The method takes a query RGB image and K support images with binary foreground masks (K=1 or 5). Before denoising, the query image is adaptively corrupted with region-modulated Gaussian noise: a FG-BG-specific prior mask (computed via intra-query propagation for FG and superpixel clustering for BG) controls the noise level so that foreground pixels receive less perturbation than background pixels. The output is a pixel-level binary foreground segmentation mask for the query image, produced in a single forward pass of the denoising network rather than through iterative reverse diffusion steps.

## Method

DeFSS formulates few-shot segmentation as a two-stage image-to-mask denoising process. First, an Adaptive Image Corruption (AIC) module generates a FG-BG-specific prior mask (using intra-query propagation to handle FG intra-class variation and superpixel clustering with max-selection to handle BG diversity) and uses it to modulate Gaussian noise, corrupting the query image more in background regions than foreground regions at a fixed corruption timestamp τ=400. Second, a Discriminative Mask Denoising (DMD) network denoises the corrupted image embedding in a single forward pass: a Condition ViT builds hierarchical support features, and a Denoising ViT with self-attention, cross-attention, and adaptive layer normalization (adaLN) propagates support-to-query semantics while denoising. To counter inter-class similarity, a Discriminative Attribute Learning (DAL) scheme inserts a class token into the denoising sequence and trains it against learnable label surrogates via attribute-level (not one-hot classification) supervision, with a stochastic cross-surrogate regularization loss that enforces surrogate divergence at O(d²) cost, all without requiring any annotations on novel classes.

## Results

On PASCAL-5^i with ResNet50, DeFSS achieves 71.7 mIoU (1-shot) and 75.1 mIoU (5-shot), outperforming the strongest baseline HMNet (70.4 / 74.1) by 1.3 and 1.0 points respectively. On the more challenging COCO-20^i, DeFSS reaches 53.7 mIoU (1-shot) and 60.0 mIoU (5-shot), surpassing HMNet (52.1 / 58.9) by 1.6 and 1.1 points. The gap is notably larger in the 1-shot setting, indicating the denoising framework better handles intra-class variation when support samples are scarce. With VGG backbone, DeFSS also leads on both datasets (68.6 / 72.0 on PASCAL-5^i; 50.3 / 55.5 on COCO-20^i).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeFSS outperforms all seven baselines on both PASCAL-5^i and COCO-20^i across both 1-shot and 5-shot settings. The closest competitor is HMNet (NeurIPS'24), which DeFSS beats by 1.3 mIoU (1-shot) and 1.0 mIoU (5-shot) on PASCAL-5^i, and by 1.6 and 1.1 on COCO-20^i. AENet (ECCV'24) and ABCB (CVPR'24) are the next strongest baselines, trailing DeFSS by 1.1–1.4 mIoU on PASCAL-5^i and 2.4–3.7 mIoU on COCO-20^i. The gap widens substantially on COCO-20^i compared to PASCAL-5^i, suggesting the discriminative attribute learning component is particularly effective under complex multi-object scenes. Older methods (HSNet, DCAMA, SCCAN) are outperformed by 5–14 mIoU, reflecting the cumulative gains from the denoising paradigm, adaptive corruption, and attribute learning over purely correlation-matching approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeFSS repurposes the denoising diffusion framework (DDPM) for few-shot segmentation by formulating mask prediction as an image-to-mask denoising process, departing from the dominant image-to-mask decoding paradigm (HSNet, HDMNet, SCCAN, AENet) that relies on support-query feature matching. It adopts the in-model single-pass denoising paradigm from Detrack (Zhou et al., NeurIPS 2024) rather than iterative reverse diffusion, and borrows the learnable label surrogate and EMA update mechanism from He et al. (2022) for its discriminative attribute learning module. Unlike prior diffusion-based FSS work (DifFSS, AlignDiff) that uses diffusion models for data augmentation, DeFSS positions the denoising network as the primary mask decoder, starting from a corrupted query image rather than pure Gaussian noise to retain input prior information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled reframing of FSS as a denoising regression problem, which naturally addresses the intra-class variation issue by learning a distribution over masks rather than performing point-estimate matching. The discriminative attribute learning with surrogates avoids the need for novel-class annotations, aligning with the core FSS constraint, and the cross-surrogate regularization provides a lightweight mechanism for class discrimination. Open questions include whether the adaptive corruption strategy generalizes to video or 3D segmentation, whether the in-model denoising approach can be extended to multi-step iterative refinement for harder cases, and whether the framework's gains persist at larger scale (e.g., 16-shot or open-vocabulary settings).
