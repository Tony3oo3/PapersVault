## Problem

Text-to-image masked generative models are difficult to replicate because they depend on large-scale private datasets and complex multi-stage training pipelines. The recent 1D tokenizer TiTok, while efficient, is restricted to a two-stage training procedure, supports only discrete VQ tokens, and focuses on low-level reconstruction without leveraging textual semantics. This paper asks whether a compact, text-aware 1D tokenizer combined with a masked generative model can achieve state-of-the-art text-to-image quality using exclusively open data.

## Input / Output

The system takes a text prompt (encoded by CLIP's text encoder into 77 context tokens) and an optional aesthetic score (projected into sinusoidal embeddings) as conditioning signals, and produces 256×256 RGB images. Images are first tokenized into compact 1D sequences of 32, 64, or 128 tokens (either 8192-entry VQ codebook vectors of 64 channels, or 16-channel continuous Gaussian embeddings), which are far fewer than the 256 tokens typical of 2D grid-based tokenizers. Sampling uses 16 iterative steps for the discrete variant and 32 for the continuous variant, with classifier-free guidance applied at inference.

## Method

TA-TiTok extends TiTok with three key changes: (1) a simplified one-stage training recipe using ResNet-50 perceptual loss and a blur-kernel PatchGAN discriminator with LeCAM regularization, eliminating the original two-stage distillation; (2) support for continuous KL-regularized 1D tokens alongside discrete VQ tokens; and (3) text-aware de-tokenization, where CLIP text embeddings are concatenated with the latent tokens and mask tokens before the ViT decoder, injecting high-level semantic guidance into reconstruction. MaskGen builds on TA-TiTok by concatenating CLIP text embeddings with image tokens and feeding the combined sequence into MM-DiT blocks with separate adaLN layers for text and image modalities, plus aesthetic score conditioning via adaLN. For discrete tokens, MaskGen is trained with cross-entropy loss over the codebook; for continuous tokens, it uses a per-token diffusion loss with a small MLP denoiser. The entire pipeline is trained exclusively on open datasets (DataComp-1B, LAION subsets, CC12M, JourneyDB, DALL-E 3) with Molmo recaptioning, and uses CLIP rather than T5-XXL for text encoding to reduce computational and storage costs.

## Results

On MJHQ-30K, MaskGen-XL with continuous (KL) tokens achieves the best FID of 6.53, surpassing SDXL (8.76) despite using only 1.1B parameters vs. 2.6B and being trained exclusively on open data. The lighter MaskGen-L (568M, VQ) achieves FID 7.74 with 30.3× faster inference throughput than Show-o, while requiring only 20.0 8-A100 days of training (vs. 94.1 for PixArt-α). On GenEval, MaskGen-XL (VQ) attains the highest overall score of 0.57, outperforming SDXL (0.55) and Show-o (0.53), while MaskGen-XL (KL) reaches 0.55 at a fraction of the training cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaskGen decisively outperforms both autoregressive baselines: MaskGen-XL (VQ) achieves FID 7.51 vs. LlamaGen's 25.59 and Show-o's 14.99 on MJHQ-30K, while also being 18.5× faster in inference than Show-o. Against diffusion models, MaskGen-XL (KL) achieves FID 6.53, beating SDXL (8.76) and PixArt-α (9.85) despite using only 1.1B parameters (vs. 2.6B for SDXL) and being trained on open data alone in 30.5 8-A100 days (vs. 94.1 for PixArt-α). On GenEval, MaskGen-XL (VQ) scores 0.57 overall, surpassing SDXL (0.55) and Show-o (0.53), while MaskGen-L (VQ) ties Show-o at 0.53 with fewer parameters (568M vs. 1.3B). The continuous-token variant (KL) consistently outperforms the discrete variant (VQ) on FID (6.53 vs. 7.51 for XL), aligning with the reduced quantization loss, though the VQ variant edges ahead on GenEval (0.57 vs. 0.55). All MaskGen variants are trained exclusively on open data, whereas SDXL and PixArt-α rely on proprietary datasets, making the performance gap particularly notable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends TiTok (Yu et al., NeurIPS 2024), which introduced compact 1D image tokenization, in three directions: replacing its complex two-stage training with a simpler one-stage recipe, extending from VQ-only to continuous (KL/VAE) tokens, and incorporating CLIP text embeddings during de-tokenization to improve semantic alignment. The MaskGen generator builds on the MM-DiT architecture from SD3 (Esser et al., ICML 2024) and the diffusion loss for continuous tokens from MAR (Li et al., NeurIPS 2024), while deliberately substituting the heavier T5-XXL text encoder with CLIP for efficiency. The masked generative framework itself follows MaskGIT (Chang et al., CVPR 2022) and Muse (Chang et al., ICML 2023), but the combination of 1D tokens, text-aware tokenization, and open-data training is novel in positioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MaskGen is the first open-weight, open-data masked generative model for text-to-image synthesis to reach SOTA-comparable quality (FID 6.53–7.74 on MJHQ-30K, GenEval 0.55–0.57), trained in as little as 20 8-A100 days—roughly 2% of SD-2.1's training cost—with 30× faster inference than Show-o. This substantially lowers the barrier for research groups to experiment with masked generative T2I models. Open questions include whether the 1D tokenization advantage persists at higher resolutions (the paper evaluates at 256×256), whether the CLIP text encoder becomes a bottleneck for complex compositional prompts compared to T5-XXL, and whether the text-aware de-tokenization strategy transfers to video or other modalities.
