## Problem

Contrastive pre-training (e.g., CLIP, MoCo) is heavily data-driven, yet its data efficiency has remained under-explored. Existing approaches rely on static coreset selection that pre-identifies important samples before training, making them unable to dynamically track how data utility evolves throughout pre-training, leading to subpar pre-trained models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251705/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard image-text pairs (for CLIP) or single images (for MoCo) at their native resolutions, with no additional annotation or feature extraction required beyond the in-batch InfoNCE loss already computed during training. It produces two outputs: (1) a dynamically pruned training schedule that reduces average per-epoch data size by 30–35% while maintaining the same total number of epochs, and (2) a static coreset (the intersection of pruned sets across multiple architectures) that can be directly reused for from-scratch pre-training of new models.

## Method

SCAN identifies prunable samples using per-sample InfoNCE loss values (avoiding expensive gradient computation) and partitions them into two categories: redundant samples (lowest losses, indicating effective memorization) and ill-matched samples (highest losses, indicating poor image-text semantic alignment). Rather than applying a fixed pruning ratio, SCAN uses a bootstrapping mutation strategy where the pruning ratio follows a cosine annealing schedule that gradually increases from 0 to 2ρ within each round, then the dataset is grown back to full size for a new round of candidate identification. A warm-up phase delays pruning until epoch-wise loss changes fall below a threshold, ensuring the model has stabilized before data is removed. This iterative prune-and-regrow cycle is applied to both CLIP (vision-language) and MoCo (vision-centric) frameworks across ResNet, ViT, and Swin Transformer architectures, achieving <1% average performance degradation at 30–35% data reduction.

## Results

On the CC12M+ dataset (10.1M image-text pairs), SCAN with 30% data pruning achieves 23.10% IN Zero-Shot Top-1 for RN101, 26.12% for ViT-B/32, and 24.71% for ViT-B/16, matching or exceeding the full-data CLIP baselines (18.78%, 24.62%, 23.43% respectively). On the smaller CC3M+ dataset (4.1M pairs), SCAN preserves ~99% of CLIP's performance (e.g., 74.61% vs 75.39% IN Top-1 for RN101). For MoCo on ImageNet with 35% pruning, SCAN achieves 78.58% Top-1 (ViT-S/16) and 79.15% Top-1 (ViT-B/16), closely matching the full-data MoCo baselines (78.48% and 79.53%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCAN consistently outperforms all four baselines across every architecture and downstream task. Against the static coreset methods, the margin is substantial: on RN101 IN Zero-Shot Top-1, SCAN (23.10%) exceeds SemDeDup (13.26%) by ~10 points and D-Pruning (12.59%) by ~10.5 points. Against the dynamic baseline Info-Batch, SCAN still leads by 1.5 points (RN101), 4.3 points (ViT-B/16), and is the only method that does not collapse on ViT-B/32 (where Info-Batch fails entirely). Notably, SCAN with 30% less data often matches or slightly exceeds the full-data CLIP model (e.g., 23.10% vs 18.78% for RN101, 26.12% vs 24.62% for ViT-B/32), suggesting the pruning removes genuinely uninformative samples. On MoCo (Table 2), SCAN also edges out Info-Batch by 0.59% (ViT-S/16) and 0.69% (ViT-B/16) on ImageNet Top-1 while using 35% less data.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCAN draws a direct conceptual analogy from dynamic sparse training (DST), where weights are pruned and regrown during training, to the data domain: instead of a fixed weight mask, it applies a bootstrapping prune-and-regrow cycle to training samples. Prior coreset selection methods for vision-language pre-training (SemDeDup, D-Pruning, SIEVE) are static—they select a subset once before training begins—while Info-Batch introduces dynamic pruning but is not designed for contrastive objectives. The paper repurposes the InfoNCE per-sample loss as a lightweight, dynamically-updated importance metric, avoiding the gradient or influence-function costs that make per-sample scoring infeasible at the multi-million-pair scale of CLIP/MoCo pre-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 30–35% reduction in pre-training data with under 1% average downstream degradation is a practically significant compute and carbon-footprint saving for teams pre-training CLIP or MoCo models, and the method's negligible overhead (loss values already computed) makes it easy to adopt. The coreset byproduct—obtained by intersecting pruned sets from two architectures—outperforms static coreset baselines by a wide margin, offering a reusable smaller dataset for future experiments. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to truly web-scale datasets (LAION-400M+), whether it transfers to language-centric contrastive pre-training or LLM pre-training (where only 1–3 epochs are typical), and whether the iterative bootstrapping schedule can be further optimized or made architecture-agnostic.
