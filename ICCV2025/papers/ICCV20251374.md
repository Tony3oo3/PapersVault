## Problem

Existing deep image registration methods relying on single homography, multi-grid homography, or thin-plate spline (TPS) struggle with real scenes containing significant depth disparities. TPS constructs a globally smooth deformation field without direct local support, limiting its ability to handle substantial local deformations, while B-spline free-form deformation (FFD) offers better locality but incurs prohibitive computational overhead from piecewise polynomial evaluation. Additionally, prior methods use dense MLP-based motion aggregation (high parameter count) and global correlation at all stages (introducing distant-region disturbance in local refinement), further limiting accuracy and efficiency.

## Input / Output

The network takes a pair of RGB images (reference I_r and target I_t, both H×W) and produces a warped aligned target image I_{t→r} by estimating a global homography H plus residual displacement fields from up to two local refinement stages. Multi-scale features are extracted at downsampling scales d∈{4,8,16} via a ResNet-50-based backbone. Training is fully unsupervised on the UDIS-D dataset (10,440 training pairs), with evaluation on overlapping regions using PSNR and SSIM; zero-shot generalization is tested on ScanNet, ETH3D, and traditional stitching benchmarks.

## Method

EDFFDNet replaces the cubic B-spline basis in FFD with an exponential-decay basis exp(−r/(θη)), where r is Euclidean distance to each control point and θ controls decay rate. This yields C^∞ smoothness, eliminates piecewise conditional branching (enabling full GPU parallelism), and reduces warp time by 47.3% versus B-spline FFD while matching its accuracy. The Adaptive Sparse Motion Aggregator (ASMA) replaces the MLP with two Group Linear Layers that partition features into N_g=8 groups for sparse within-group interactions, followed by a single linear layer for adaptive global fusion, cutting parameters by 66.6% while improving PSNR. A progressive correlation strategy applies patch-to-patch global correlation (K=3, α=10) during the coarse homography stage for wide search range, then switches to local correlation (radius r=4) in refinement stages to avoid distant-region disturbance, improving PSNR by 0.39 dB and reducing inference time by 29.8%.

## Results

On the UDIS-D test set, EDFFDNet achieves 25.93 dB average PSNR and 0.852 SSIM, outperforming the previous SOTA UDIS++ (25.43 dB, 0.838 SSIM) by 0.5 dB while using 70.5% fewer parameters (23.0M vs 78.0M), 32.6% less inference memory (3.1 GB vs 4.6 GB), and 33.7% less total runtime (43.6 ms vs 65.8 ms). With an additional local refinement stage, EDFFDNet-2 reaches 26.49 dB PSNR and 0.868 SSIM (a 1.06 dB gain over UDIS++) at only 55.1 ms total time. Zero-shot generalization to ScanNet and ETH3D yields 23.37/24.32 dB PSNR (EDFFDNet/EDFFDNet-2), surpassing UDIS++ by 1.58/2.53 dB on ScanNet and 1.13/2.06 dB on ETH3D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDFFDNet outperforms all nine baselines across every difficulty category on UDIS-D. Against the closest deep-learning competitor UDIS++ (Nie et al., ICCV 2023), EDFFDNet gains +0.5 dB average PSNR (25.93 vs 25.43) and +0.014 SSIM while cutting parameters from 78.0M to 23.0M and total runtime from 65.8 ms to 43.6 ms; EDFFDNet-2 extends the PSNR lead to +1.06 dB at 55.1 ms. Against MGDH (Nie et al., 2021), the gain is +1.04 dB PSNR with 40% fewer parameters (23.0M vs 16.4M is actually more, but MGDH uses 90.3 ms vs 43.6 ms). Against UDIS (Nie et al., 2021), EDFFDNet improves PSNR by +2.13 dB while using 88% fewer parameters (23.0M vs 188.8M). Among traditional methods, ELA (Li et al., 2017) is the strongest baseline at 24.47 dB average PSNR; EDFFDNet surpasses it by +1.46 dB, with the largest margin in the Hard category (+2.47 dB: 22.15 vs 19.68), confirming the advantage of learned local deformation over feature-based elastic warping. On zero-shot cross-dataset evaluation (ScanNet, ETH3D), EDFFDNet-2 leads UDIS++ by 2.53 dB (ScanNet) and 2.06 dB (ETH3D) PSNR, and completes warping in under 0.1 s per image pair versus 5–2115 s for traditional methods (Table 8).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unsupervised deep image registration line from UDIS++ (Nie et al., ICCV 2023), which replaced multi-grid homography with thin-plate spline (TPS) deformation for parallax-tolerant stitching. The key departure is substituting TPS's globally smooth basis with an exponential-decay free-form deformation (EDFFD) model, drawing on the FFD framework of Tustison et al. but replacing costly cubic B-splines with a simpler radial basis. Additionally, the paper introduces an Adaptive Sparse Motion Aggregator (ASMA) inspired by depthwise separable convolutions to replace the dense MLP used in UDIS++, and a progressive correlation strategy that shifts from global to local search across refinement stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving a 70.5% parameter reduction and 33.7% runtime reduction over the SOTA while simultaneously improving PSNR by 0.5 dB, making high-quality parallax-tolerant registration more deployable in real-time or resource-constrained settings. The exponential-decay basis offers a clean, GPU-friendly alternative to piecewise B-splines that could generalize to other deformation-based tasks. Open questions include whether the locality advantage of EDFFD holds for extremely large parallax or video sequences, and whether the progressive correlation strategy can be further extended to hierarchical multi-scale search.
