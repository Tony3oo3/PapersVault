## Problem

While data poisoning methods for diffusion models have been proposed, no systematic study has identified the temporal and spatial mechanisms by which adversarial signals disrupt Textual Inversion (TI) training. Specifically, it was unknown at which timesteps poison signals are most effective and how they alter the spatial regions from which TI learns, leaving defenses to be designed without understanding the underlying vulnerabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252226/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SZT consumes 512×512 RGB training images (already poisoned with adversarial perturbations), a text prompt containing a novel token R*, and a binary pixel-space mask delineating the concept region (generated via SAM). The poisoned images are first JPEG-compressed (quality 25) before being encoded to latent space. The output is a single learned text embedding for R* that, when used with a frozen Stable Diffusion 1.5 model, generates images of the novel concept in new contexts.

## Method

Safe-Zone Training (SZT) modifies the TI noise-prediction objective with three targeted interventions derived from mechanistic analysis. First, JPEG compression (quality 25) is applied to poisoned images, which converts bimodal adversarial perturbations into unimodal Gaussian-like noise in pixel space and centralizes the latent frequency spectrum toward natural-image distributions. Second, timestep sampling is restricted to t ≥ 600 (out of 1000), exploiting the finding that the noise-prediction gradient with respect to the text embedding is near zero at high timesteps and that adversarial signals are concentrated at lower-middle timesteps (t ∈ [0, 600]) due to the same gradient structure. Third, a binary loss mask (dilated by 16 pixels) is element-wise multiplied with the noise-prediction error in latent space, forcing backpropagation to only the concept region and counteracting the l²/l∞ adversarial objective that spreads poison signals uniformly across all spatial coordinates. The combined objective (Eq. 7) requires no additional networks or regeneration steps, making it a lightweight drop-in modification to standard TI training.

## Results

On CustomConcept101, SZT achieves an average DINOv2 similarity of 0.46 across all six poisons (ADM+, ADM−, SDS+, SDS−, EA, DA), matching the 0.46 score of nominal TI on clean data and effectively nullifying all poisoning attacks. The strongest individual component, JPEG compression alone, reaches 0.37 average, while the full SZT pipeline (JPEG + T600 + loss masking) closes the remaining gap. On the most damaging poisons (ADM+, SDS+, EA), where nominal TI drops to 0.10, SZT recovers to 0.44–0.46, a 4–5× improvement over the unmodified baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SZT outperforms all four established defenses across every poison. Against Regen [84] (0.19 avg), SZT improves by +0.27; against PDMPure [77] (0.24 avg), by +0.22; against AdvClean [65] (0.26 avg), by +0.20; and against JPEG alone [45] (0.37 avg), by +0.09. The gap is most dramatic on loss-maximizing poisons (ADM+, SDS+, EA), where Regen and AdvClean remain near 0.10–0.19 while SZT reaches 0.44–0.46. Notably, Regen actually degrades below nominal TI on EA (0.09 vs 0.10), and PDMPure is the weakest defense on clean data (0.25 vs 0.46), suggesting its regeneration step can damage already-clean images. SZT's advantage over JPEG alone comes from the complementary timestep restriction and loss masking, which address the temporal and spatial biases that JPEG cannot fix.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Textual Inversion [15] as the personalization target and on the family of diffusion-model adversarial poisoning methods (ADM+, SDS+, EA, DA) that maximize noise-prediction loss or manipulate encoder outputs. It departs from prior poisoning work, which has focused on proposing new attack objectives, by providing the first systematic mechanistic analysis of *when* (which timesteps) and *where* (which spatial regions) adversarial signals are learned during TI. On the defense side, it contrasts with regeneration-based approaches (Regen, PDMPure) and augmentation-based methods (AdvClean) by proposing a training-time strategy (SZT) that requires no additional networks or dataset preprocessing, positioning itself as a lightweight alternative grounded in the noise-prediction objective's inherent timestep and spatial biases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mechanistic finding that the noise-prediction objective creates an inherent learning bias toward lower-middle timesteps (where gradients are non-trivial) explains why all diffusion-based AEs concentrate their signals there, and why a single hyperparameter change (restricting TI to t≥600) yields substantial robustness gains. This insight generalizes beyond TI to any diffusion-based fine-tuning task and suggests that timestep-aware training is a broadly applicable robustness lever. Open questions include whether more adaptive poisons could learn to distribute signals across timesteps to defeat SZT, whether the loss-masking component generalizes to multi-concept or style-based personalization, and whether the JPEG compression step remains effective against poisons designed in latent space rather than pixel space.
