## Problem

Regression models trained on imbalanced data exhibit degraded generalization for rare target values, as evidenced by sharply increased loss landscape sensitivity in low-density regions. Existing Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) methods guarantee generalization only to the training distribution, which is inadequate when the test distribution is balanced but the training distribution is imbalanced. The paper identifies this distribution-shift gap in SAM's sharpness estimation as the root cause of inconsistent generalization across the target value range.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (H×W×C) as input and produces a single continuous scalar output (e.g., age in years or depth in meters). The continuous label space [L, U] is partitioned into K equal-width bins to compute per-bin importance weights, which modulate the perturbation direction during training. For age estimation, bins are 1 year wide; for depth estimation, bins are 0.1 meters wide, consistent with prior imbalanced regression benchmarks.

## Method

BSAM extends SAM by replacing the standard training loss in the worst-case perturbation computation with an importance-weighted loss, where each sample's weight is the ratio of its bin's test-distribution probability to its training-distribution probability (e.g., square-root-inverse frequency). Specifically, the perturbation ε* is computed as the dual-norm solution to maximizing the weighted loss gradient over a ρ-ball, while the actual parameter update still uses the unweighted loss gradient at the perturbed point. This targeted reweighting is applied exclusively in the perturbation step, making BSAM agnostic to the choice of regression loss (l1, l2, etc.) used for parameter updates. The approach guarantees a generalization bound with respect to the balanced test distribution rather than the imbalanced training distribution, and introduces no additional computational overhead beyond a single weighted gradient computation per step.

## Results

On AgeDB-DIR, BSAM achieves the best overall MAE of 6.067 and GM of 3.895, reducing few-shot MAE from 10.688 (Vanilla) to 7.928. On IMDB-WIKI-DIR, BSAM attains 6.811 MAE (All) and 3.765 GM, outperforming the next-best SQINV (7.040 MAE) and LDS (7.214 MAE). On NYUD2-DIR, BSAM achieves the lowest RMSE of 1.272 (All) and 1.705 (Few-shot), along with the highest δ₁ of 0.727 (All) and 0.724 (Few-shot), surpassing Balanced MSE (1.283 RMSE) and ConR+FDS (1.299 RMSE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BSAM outperforms all compared baselines across all three benchmarks. On AgeDB-DIR, BSAM (6.067 MAE) beats the strongest reproduced baselines LDS (6.350) and SQINV (6.391) by 0.28 and 0.32 MAE respectively, with the largest gap in the few-shot region (7.928 vs. 8.355 for LDS and 10.688 for Vanilla). On IMDB-WIKI-DIR, BSAM (6.811 MAE) surpasses SQINV (7.040) and LDS (7.214), while also improving over RNC (7.466) and Ordinal Entropy (7.322). On NYUD2-DIR, BSAM achieves the best RMSE (1.272) over Balanced MSE (1.283) and ConR+FDS (1.299), with a notable few-shot RMSE improvement (1.705 vs. 1.736 for Balanced MSE). Compared to SAM variants, BSAM (6.067) outperforms standard SAM (6.230) and ImbSAM (6.184) on AgeDB-DIR, confirming that targeted reweighting in the perturbation step is more effective than either uniform or minority-only perturbation strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) from Foret et al. by identifying that SAM's uniform perturbation step is inherently biased toward high-density regions under imbalanced training distributions, and correcting this through importance reweighting applied specifically to the perturbation computation. It departs from the broader imbalanced regression literature (LDS/FDS distribution smoothing, RankSim, ConR, RNC, Ordinal Entropy, Balanced MSE), which primarily addresses imbalance through loss function redesign, feature-space constraints, or probabilistic modeling. The key conceptual distinction is that BSAM operates at the optimization level rather than the objective level, making it orthogonal to and combinable with existing loss-based reweighting strategies like SQINV.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reframing imbalanced regression as a generalization problem and showing that targeted reweighting in the perturbation step alone suffices to homogenize loss landscape flatness across the observation space, the paper offers a lightweight, loss-agnostic optimization trick that practitioners can drop into existing regression pipelines without architectural changes. The dramatic Hessian trace reduction in few-shot regions (653→91) provides mechanistic evidence for why the method works. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to higher-dimensional regression outputs (pose, multi-point depth), whether the square-root-inverse weighting is optimal, and whether the benefits persist at scale with larger backbones or more extreme imbalance ratios.
