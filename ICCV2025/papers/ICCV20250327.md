## Problem

Generative replay for continual learning relies on a separate generative model to produce rehearsal samples for a classifier, creating a knowledge-transfer bottleneck: the classifier's performance degrades when retrained on imperfectly generated data, and even state-of-the-art diffusion models cannot precisely capture the data distribution. This imbalance motivates the question of whether the generative and discriminative components can be unified into a single model to eliminate the transfer gap entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250327/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images at 32×32 (CIFAR-10/100) or 64×64 (ImageNet-100) resolution. During training, each image is noised to a random diffusion timestep for the denoising objective while the clean image is fed to the classifier head; at inference, the model outputs a class label from pooled UNet features and can also generate synthetic rehearsal images via the diffusion sampling process. In the semi-supervised variant, unlabeled images are additionally consumed for the generative loss and pseudo-labeling without requiring class annotations.

## Method

JDCL unifies a UNet denoising diffusion model and a two-layer feed-forward classifier into a single jointly optimized network, where features from all UNet depth levels are average-pooled into one vector that feeds the classifier. Training minimizes a combined loss of the DDPM denoising objective and cross-entropy classification over shared encoder parameters, so the generative and discriminative objectives co-adapt without any explicit knowledge transfer. For continual learning, a two-stage local-to-global scheme is used: a local copy is fine-tuned on the new task for plasticity, then the global model is updated on a synthetic rehearsal set (generated by both the old global and the local model) with an additional knowledge-distillation loss that matches both noise predictions and classifier probability vectors from the frozen previous model. In the semi-supervised extension, pseudo-labeling and consistency regularization on weakly/strongly augmented unlabeled data are added to the local training stage, leveraging the shared parametrization to align labeled and unlabeled representations.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (5 tasks), JDCL achieves 83.69% average accuracy, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art GUIDE (64.47%) by 19.2 points and approaching the soft upper bound of a joint model with infinite replay buffer (86.41%). On ImageNet-100 (5 tasks), JDCL reaches 54.53%, outperforming GUIDE (39.07%) by 15.5 points. On CIFAR-100 (5 tasks), JDCL scores 47.95% vs. GUIDE's 41.66%, and on CIFAR-100 (10 tasks) it achieves 29.04%, marginally exceeding the best non-generative method TKD/SS-IL (28.96%). In the semi-supervised continual setting (CIFAR-10, 0.8% labeled), JDCL reaches 78.93% without any memory buffer, outperforming the buffer-based SOTA NNCSL (73.2% with 500-sample buffer).

## Baselines & Comparisons

JDCL decisively outperforms all generative replay baselines: on CIFAR-10 it surpasses GUIDE (64.47%) by 19.2 points, DDGR (43.69%) by 40 points, DGR Diffusion (59.00%) by 24.7 points, and DGR VAE (28.23%) by 55.5 points. Against regularization-based methods (EWC at 20.08%, LWF at 49.60%) and feature-replay methods (GFR at 26.70%), the gap is even larger. On CIFAR-100 with 10 tasks, JDCL (29.04%) narrowly edges out the best non-generative distillation method TKD/SS-IL (28.96%) and clearly beats GUIDE (26.13%). In the semi-supervised continual setting, JDCL (78.93% on CIFAR-10, 0.8% labeled) outperforms the buffer-based SOTA NNCSL (73.2%) by 5.7 points without using any memory buffer, and also beats CCIC (54.0%), CSL (64.5%), and ORDisCo (41.7%). For self-supervised representation quality (logistic regression on learned features, Fig. 6), JDCL outperforms both supervised continual methods [39] and self-supervised continual methods [14] on CIFAR-100 with 5 tasks, confirming that the joint diffusion architecture learns informative and stable representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the joint diffusion model architecture of Deja et al. (2023), which pools UNet encoder features for classification, into a continual learning framework by combining it with generative replay, two-stage local-to-global training, and knowledge distillation. It departs from the standard generative replay paradigm (separate GAN/VAE/diffusion model + classifier, as in DGR, DDGR, GUIDE) by unifying both components into a single shared parametrization, thereby eliminating the knowledge-transfer bottleneck between generator and classifier. For the semi-supervised extension, it adapts FixMatch-style consistency regularization and pseudo-labeling to the joint model, building on the continual semi-supervised learning line (ORDisCo, CCIC, NNCSL) but replacing buffer-based replay with diffusion-based generative replay.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the in-distribution property of jointly-trained rehearsal samples (Fig. 4) is a key mechanism explaining why joint modeling outperforms separate generator-classifier pipelines, offering a clear conceptual explanation beyond empirical gains. However, the CIFAR-100 results (47.95 supervised, 22.19 semi-supervised) remain notably below both the upper bound and the strongest baselines, suggesting the approach struggles when the classifier signal is weak relative to the generative objective. Open questions include whether the method scales to higher-resolution or larger-scale datasets, whether the two-stage training overhead can be further reduced, and whether the shared-parametrization advantage persists in non-image modalities or with transformer-based backbones.
