## Problem

Most prevalent image understanding models focus on preserving visual similarity, leaving the learning of visual hierarchies (e.g., scene→object→part) largely unexplored. While hyperbolic spaces naturally accommodate tree-like structures, prior work has not encoded user-defined multi-level complex visual hierarchies without requiring explicit hierarchical labels or additional modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250728/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB images in two forms—full scene images and cropped bounding boxes—as input, with the hierarchy defined by spatial containment (larger boxes entail smaller ones) and cross-image class matching. Output is a d-dimensional embedding in the Lorentz model of hyperbolic space, where the radial axis encodes hierarchical level: high-level scene embeddings cluster near the origin while part-level embeddings are pushed toward the boundary. Retrieval rankings are produced by minimizing hyperbolic exterior angles between query and candidate embeddings.

## Method

The paper defines a part-based image hierarchy from bounding box annotations (if B⊂A then A entails B) and enforces it via a bidirectional contrastive loss in hyperbolic space. The key mechanism replaces the standard similarity function in InfoNCE with exterior angles β₁ and α₂ computed in the Lorentz model, creating an asymmetric distance that provides an extra degree of freedom along the radial axis to form emergent tree structures. A shared image encoder (CLIP ViT-B/16 or MoCo-v2 ResNet-50) is fine-tuned for one epoch on pairwise entailment relationships, with embeddings projected onto the hyperboloid via the exponential map from the tangent space at the origin. Cross-image entailment is added by sampling K same-class bounding boxes from other images and enforcing scene-to-object relationships. For evaluation, the paper introduces an optimal transport (1-D Wasserstein) distance between the retrieved label distribution and the ground-truth hierarchy tree distribution, complementing standard Recall@k metrics.

## Results

On HierOpenImages same-class retrieval (Table 1), CLIP-hyp† achieves 73.37% top-5 child-to-parent accuracy and 66.02% top-5 parent-to-child accuracy, substantially outperforming the unfine-tuned CLIP baseline (26.73% and 47.52%) and the Euclidean fine-tuned variant CLIP-euc† (67.83% and 65.58%). For MoCo-v2, the hyperbolic model reaches 55.53% top-5 child-to-parent accuracy versus 17.23% for unfine-tuned MoCo and 15.11% for HCL. On hierarchical retrieval (Table 2), CLIP-hyp† achieves 74.06% recall at top-150k with an OT distance of 16.36, compared to 51.33% recall and 23.81 OT distance for the CLIP baseline, demonstrating superior alignment with the ground-truth hierarchical label distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed hyperbolic angle-based entailment loss consistently outperforms all baselines. Against the unfine-tuned CLIP baseline, CLIP-hyp† gains +46.64 points on child-to-parent top-5 (73.37 vs 26.73) and +18.50 on parent-to-child top-5 (66.02 vs 47.52), confirming that the angle-based loss in hyperbolic space captures hierarchical structure far beyond visual similarity. Compared to the Euclidean fine-tuned variant (CLIP-euc†), the hyperbolic model wins on all child-to-parent metrics (e.g., 73.37 vs 67.83 at top-5) and narrowly on parent-to-child (66.02 vs 65.58 at top-5), indicating hyperbolic geometry provides a meaningful but moderate advantage over Euclidean angle-based learning. Against HCL [11], the state-of-the-art hyperbolic hierarchical model, the proposed method achieves a dramatic improvement of +40.42 points on child-to-parent top-5 (55.53 vs 15.11) and +35.93 on parent-to-child top-5 (52.01 vs 16.08) with MoCo-v2; notably, fine-tuning HCL† on the same dataset actually degrades its hierarchical retrieval performance (Table 2), suggesting HCL's simpler scene-to-object formulation cannot capture multi-level part-based hierarchies. On the out-of-domain LVIS dataset, CLIP-hyp† maintains its lead over CLIP-euc† (22.33 vs 19.36 child-to-parent top-5; 22.41 vs 20.24 parent-to-child top-5), demonstrating generalization to unseen fine-grained object classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hyperbolic angle-based entailment loss from Ramasinghe et al. [32] (originally developed for vision-language contrastive learning) and the entailment cone formulation of Ganea et al. [8] to the pure image domain, adapting it to handle multiple positive entailment pairs via InfoNCE. It departs from prior hyperbolic image embedding work (HCL [11], Khrulkov et al. [20]) which was limited to simple scene-to-object hierarchies or single-class images with predefined label taxonomies, by encoding multi-level part-based hierarchies derived purely from bounding box containment without explicit hierarchical labels. The construction of HierOpenImages and the optimal-transport-based retrieval metric represent new dataset and evaluation contributions not present in prior hyperbolic retrieval literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that pairwise entailment relationships are sufficient to learn complex multi-level visual hierarchies in hyperbolic space, opening a path toward hierarchical reasoning in vision models without requiring costly hierarchical annotations. The finding that the hyperbolic model implicitly captures cross-image structure even when trained only on within-image entailment pairs suggests hyperbolic geometry provides an inductive bias for hierarchical organization that Euclidean space lacks. Limitations include evaluation on only two backbones with single-epoch fine-tuning, and the bounding-box-containment hierarchy, while natural, may not capture all semantically meaningful part-whole relationships. Open questions include whether this paradigm extends to other modalities, scales to deeper hierarchies, and whether the OT-based metric generalizes beyond 1-D Wasserstein formulations.
