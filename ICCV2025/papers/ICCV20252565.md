## Problem

INR-based arbitrary-scale super-resolution methods (e.g., LIIF, CiaoSR) query an MLP per pixel, yielding limited representation capacity and high computational cost, especially at large scaling factors. While 2D/3D Gaussian Splatting offers stronger explicit representation and faster rendering, the original GS is an optimization-based per-scene method that cannot generalize to unseen images or arbitrary scales, making direct application to ASR non-trivial.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252565/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts an arbitrary-size low-resolution RGB image (e.g., 48×48 patches in training) and a continuous scaling factor s (trained on [1.0, 4.0], tested up to ×30) as input, and outputs a super-resolved image of size sH × sW. The number of Gaussians is set to N = m·(H×W), proportional to the LR image dimensions, so the model adapts its representation density to input size without requiring retraining. The rasterization step is parameter-free with respect to scale: the scaling factor simply controls the sampling interval over the continuous Gaussian field.

## Method

GSASR replaces per-scene Gaussian optimization with a feed-forward, image-conditioned Gaussian decoder. An off-the-shelf CNN encoder (EDSR or RDN) extracts LR features, which are injected into learnable Gaussian embeddings (inspired by DETR) via window-based cross-attention; the embeddings then interact through stacked Swin-Transformer-style self-attention blocks to become scale-aware. Five disentangled MLP heads predict each Gaussian's opacity, position offset (added to a fixed reference position), standard deviations, correlation coefficient, and peak RGB color. Rendering is performed by a custom differentiable 2D GPU/CUDA rasterizer that, for each Gaussian, only accumulates contributions within a rasterization ratio r=0.1 of its center, reducing complexity from O(s²HWN) to O(r²s²HWN) and enabling end-to-end training with L1 loss against the HR ground truth.

## Results

GSASR achieves the best PSNR across all scaling factors (×2 to ×30) on both DIV2K and LSDIR with the EDSR-baseline encoder, e.g., 36.65 dB at ×2, 30.89 dB at ×4, 27.22 dB at ×8, and 22.19 dB at ×30 on DIV2K. It also leads on all perceptual metrics (LPIPS, DISTS) at every scale. In terms of speed, GSASR renders a 720×720 image in 91 ms at ×12, compared to 540 ms for CiaoSR and 688 ms for GaussianSR, while at ×4 it takes 543 ms versus 1165 ms for CiaoSR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GSASR outperforms all eight baselines on every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, DISTS) at every tested scale (×2 through ×30) on both DIV2K and LSDIR. Against the strongest INR-based method CiaoSR, GSASR gains +0.23 dB PSNR at ×2 (36.65 vs 36.42), +0.22 dB at ×4 (30.89 vs 30.67), and +0.01 dB at ×30 (22.19 vs 22.18), with the gap being most pronounced in the mid-range (×8: +0.18 dB). Against the closest GS-based competitor GaussianSR, GSASR leads by +0.55 dB at ×2, +0.43 dB at ×4, and +0.44 dB at ×16, with the advantage widening at higher scales because GaussianSR fixes its Gaussian count at 100 regardless of scale. In terms of inference speed at 720×720 output, GSASR is fastest at ×6 (265 ms), ×8 (195 ms), and ×12 (91 ms), beating CiaoSR by 2.7×, 3.2×, and 5.9× respectively, though at lower scales (×2, ×3, ×4) lighter INR methods like Meta-SR and LINF are faster in absolute time while GSASR still delivers superior quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

GSASR adapts the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework (Kerbl et al., 2023) to the 2D image domain, replacing the optimization-based per-scene fitting with a feed-forward, image-conditioned architecture inspired by DETR's learnable object queries. It positions itself as an explicit, region-level alternative to the dominant INR-based ASR line (LIIF, CiaoSR, LINF), arguing that pixel-by-pixel MLP queries lack sufficient representation capacity. Compared to the concurrent GaussianSR (Hu et al., 2025), GSASR departs by predicting all Gaussian attributes (position, shape, color) freely rather than anchoring centers to fixed LR pixel locations, and by introducing a CUDA-accelerated scale-aware rasterizer that avoids GaussianSR's fixed-Gaussian-count bottleneck.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit Gaussian representations can match or exceed implicit neural functions for ASR while being substantially faster (e.g., 91 ms vs. 540 ms at ×12), suggesting a viable alternative paradigm for continuous image representation. The generalization to unseen scaling factors (trained on [1,4], tested up to ×30) without architectural changes is practically valuable. However, the authors acknowledge that performance at ultra-high scales is bottlenecked by the number of Gaussians (a speed-quality tradeoff), and the large parameter count of the Gaussian decoder remains an open efficiency concern. Future work could explore adaptive Gaussian counts, distillation, or integration with generative priors to push quality further.
