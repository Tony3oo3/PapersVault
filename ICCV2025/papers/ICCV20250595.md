## Problem

Existing text-guided sketch animation methods (Live-Sketch, FlipSketch) perform well on single-object sketches but fail in multi-object scenarios due to two key gaps: they lack object-aware motion modeling that accounts for relative motions, interactions, and physical constraints among objects, and they cannot effectively optimize complex multi-object motions through their existing optimization pipelines. No training datasets for multi-object sketch animation exist, making a training-data-free approach essential.

## Input / Output

The method takes a vector sketch parameterized by n 2D control points (each stroke is a cubic Bézier curve) and a free-form text instruction describing the desired motion. It produces a 16-frame animated vector sketch as control point displacements ΔZ ∈ R^{n×f×2}. The pipeline is entirely training-data-free: GPT-4 handles scene decomposition and motion planning, Grounding DINO localizes objects, and a small Transformer-based network is iteratively optimized via SDS for ~500 steps (~1 hour on a single RTX 3090 Ti).

## Method

MoSketch tackles multi-object sketch animation with four modules in a divide-and-conquer strategy. First, GPT-4-based scene decomposition identifies m objects (≤7), decomposes the complex text instruction into r simpler sub-instructions (≤5), and assigns control points to objects via Grounding DINO bounding boxes. Second, GPT-4-based motion planning generates a coarse object-level trajectory (bounding boxes across all frames) that encodes physical constraints and inter-object interactions. Third, a multi-grained motion refinement network replaces Live-Sketch's single sketch-level motion with per-object holistic transformations (ΔZ_o) and per-object point-level translations (ΔZ_p), using a 2-layer Transformer with self-attention to model inter-object relationships, and adds the coarse motion ΔZ_c as a residual. Fourth, a compositional SDS loss augments the standard SDS objective with per-sub-instruction SDS terms computed on extracted object subsets, allowing the T2V diffusion model to guide each simpler motion it can effectively feature. The total loss L_CSDS = L_SDS + Σ L_SDS-i is minimized iteratively with Adam (lr=5e-3, weight decay=1e-2).

## Results

On 60 multi-object sketches, MoSketch achieves the best scores across all four VBench metrics: Text-to-Video Alignment (0.218), Sketch-to-Video Alignment (0.914), Motion Smoothing (0.977), and Dynamic Degree (0.283). The closest competitor, Live-Sketch, scores 0.207 / 0.897 / 0.956 / 0.266 respectively, while the I2V baselines CogVideoX and DynamiCrafter fall substantially behind on text alignment (0.141 and 0.184) and sketch preservation (0.610 and 0.771). The Dynamic Degree metric is only reported for Live-Sketch and MoSketch, as the other methods fail to preserve visual appearance, making the metric meaningless for them.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoSketch outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest sketch-animation baseline Live-Sketch (CVPR 2024), MoSketch gains +0.011 in Text-to-Video Alignment, +0.017 in Sketch-to-Video Alignment, +0.021 in Motion Smoothing, and +0.017 in Dynamic Degree, reflecting its ability to model complex multi-object motions while preserving vector sketch appearance. FlipSketch (CVPR 2025) trails MoSketch by 0.019 in text alignment and 0.210 in sketch alignment, primarily because its raster representation and DDIM-inversion-based appearance capture degrade on multi-object scenes. The I2V baselines CogVideoX (arXiv 2024) and DynamiCrafter (ECCV 2024) perform worst overall, with CogVideoX scoring only 0.141 in text alignment and 0.610 in sketch alignment, as the domain gap between natural images and sketches causes them to lose visual appearance entirely. Notably, only Live-Sketch and MoSketch produce meaningful Dynamic Degree scores, since the other three methods generate outputs that no longer resemble the input sketch.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Live-Sketch (Gal et al., CVPR 2024), which animates single-object vector sketches via iterative SDS optimization guided by a T2V diffusion model, to the multi-object setting. The key departure is a divide-and-conquer strategy that inserts LLM-based scene decomposition (GPT-4 + Grounding DINO) and LLM-based motion planning upstream of the generative network, replacing Live-Sketch's monolithic MLP with an object-aware Transformer-based multi-grained motion refinement module. The compositional SDS loss, which decomposes the text instruction into simpler per-object sub-instructions and computes separate SDS losses on extracted point subsets, is a novel loss formulation that addresses the T2V model's known weakness in modeling complex inter-object motion. Unlike FlipSketch, which relies on raster representation and fine-tuning on Live-Sketch-synthesized data, MoSketch remains training-data free and vector-based.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoSketch is the first training-data-free method explicitly targeting multi-object sketch animation, filling a gap where prior SOTA methods (Live-Sketch, FlipSketch) produce shaky, static, or physically implausible results. The practical impact is tempered by the ~1-hour optimization per animation on a single GPU and the dependence on GPT-4's motion-planning quality, which cannot be corrected downstream when the plan is grossly wrong. Open questions include scalability beyond 7 objects, handling hierarchical or nested object relationships, and whether the LLM planning bottleneck can be replaced by learned surrogates to enable real-time or near-real-time animation.
