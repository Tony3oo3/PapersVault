## Problem

Medical Vision-Language Models (MedVLMs) frequently produce factually incorrect or clinically inconsistent responses due to inherent probabilistic uncertainties and domain knowledge gaps. Existing remedies—architecture modifications, large-scale fine-tuning, or preference alignment—are computationally expensive and still fail to achieve sufficient alignment with clinical expertise. The paper tackles two sub-problems: identifying which MedVLM outputs are unreliable, and incorporating expert corrections without imposing heavy annotation burdens on clinicians.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252413/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a medical image (X-ray, CT, MRI, or pathology slide) paired with a natural-language question, and optionally a set of expert-highlighted key terms extracted from retrieved knowledge references. Uncertainty estimation gates the pipeline: only the top 5% highest-entropy predictions trigger the expert-in-the-loop branch, while low-entropy answers are returned directly. The output is a refined free-text medical VQA answer whose token-level generation is steered toward the expert-highlighted content, evaluated via accuracy (closed-set) and token recall (open-set).

## Method

Expert-CFG is a training-free, three-stage framework built on a 4.2B Phi-3/Phi-3.5-Vision baseline fine-tuned with LoRA on PubMedVision. First, normalized predictive entropy over the autoregressive output distribution flags unreliable predictions. Second, BioMedCLIP + FAISS retrieves relevant medical image-text references, and experts (or GPT-4-assisted entity extraction) highlight critical terms within those references. Third, a token-level classifier-free guidance mechanism steers generation: a binary mask marks highlighted tokens, a two-branch condition (full context vs. a scaled-down unconditional context where highlighted embeddings are attenuated by α=0.01) is combined with a guidance strength γ=1.5, and attention scores in the normal branch are re-weighted by β^{m_i} to amplify highlighted tokens. This allows the model to follow expert annotations at inference time without any parameter updates, outperforming 13B-parameter baselines on three medical VQA benchmarks.

## Results

On the three medical VQA benchmarks, Expert-CFG (Phi3.5V-Med 4.2B, 5% expert annotations) achieves overall accuracy of 84.70 on VQA-RAD, 87.75 on SLAKE, and 69.56 on PathVQA. This 4.2B model surpasses LLaVA-Med 13B (72.66 / 85.21 / 65.69) on all three datasets and outperforms BioMed-VITAL 13B (150K) on VQA-RAD (84.70 vs. 78.85) and PathVQA (69.56 vs. 66.24), though it trails on SLAKE (87.75 vs. 91.30). The uncertainty-guided selection of only the top 5% high-entropy samples for expert annotation yields a +2.66 / +2.55 / +4.32 overall accuracy gain over the unassisted baseline, while full RAG (100% retrieval) provides inconsistent or negative gains on open-ended questions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Expert-CFG (Phi3.5V, 4.2B) outperforms LLaVA-Med 13B by a wide margin on all three benchmarks: +12.04 on VQA-RAD (84.70 vs. 72.66), +2.54 on SLAKE (87.75 vs. 85.21), and +3.87 on PathVQA (69.56 vs. 65.69). Against the strongest autoregressive baseline, BioMed-VITAL (13B, 150K), Expert-CFG wins on VQA-RAD (+5.85) and PathVQA (+3.32) but trails on SLAKE (87.75 vs. 91.30, −3.55), where the larger 13B model with 150K preference-aligned samples retains an edge. Compared to classification-based methods (MUMC, M3AE, BioMedCLIP), Expert-CFG is competitive on closed-set accuracy but uniquely supports open-ended generation, which the paper argues is essential for practical clinical deployment. The ablation further shows that plain RAG (100% retrieval) actually degrades open-ended performance on VQA-RAD (−3.91 for Phi3V), while the 5% expert-annotated Expert-CFG consistently improves both open and closed scores, confirming that targeted expert guidance via classifier-free guidance is more effective than indiscriminate retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts classifier-free guidance from diffusion models (Ho 2022) through its autoregressive extension (LLM-CFG, Sanchez 2023) and the token-level highlight mechanism of Prompt-Highlight (Zhang et al. 2023) to the medical VQA setting, but departs from prior work by making the guidance conditional on an uncertainty gate (predictive entropy) and on lightweight expert highlights rather than full annotations. It positions itself against the dominant training-dependent paradigm for improving MedVLMs (instruction fine-tuning with HuatuoGPT-Vision, preference alignment with BioMed-VITAL, architecture modifications) and against retrieval-augmented approaches (RULE, MMed-RAG), arguing that both are costly and that RAG alone cannot enforce alignment because retrieved knowledge may conflict with the model's internal representations. The key conceptual contribution is treating expert alignment as an inference-time, training-free control problem rather than a data or architecture problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant for resource-limited clinical deployments: a 4.2B model with only 5% expert-annotated samples outperforms 13B baselines, suggesting that targeted inference-time intervention can substitute for scale. However, the framework's reliance on expert involvement (even if reduced to highlighting) and its demonstrated retrieval limitations (top-1 hit rate below 56%) raise open questions about full automation and scalability to long-form report generation or multi-turn clinical dialogue. The finding that RAG can degrade open-ended performance while Expert-CFG consistently improves it points to a broader open question about how to make retrieved knowledge actually constrain generation rather than merely inform it.
