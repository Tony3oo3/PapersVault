## Problem

Existing autonomous driving evaluation platforms suffer from a fundamental trade-off: simulators like CARLA provide interactivity but have a significant visual domain gap with the real world, while replayed datasets (nuScenes, Waymo) offer realistic sensor inputs but are static, biased toward simple straight-ahead scenarios, and cannot model reactive traffic. No prior system combines high-fidelity surround-view image generation with true closed-loop interactivity where background vehicles react to the ego agent's actions in real time.

## Input / Output

The Traffic Manager consumes OpenStreetMap road network data (any city worldwide) and ego trajectories (6 path points over 3 s at 2 Hz, interpolated to 10 Hz internally) to produce vehicle layouts and collision states. The World Dreamer takes these layouts plus camera intrinsics/extrinsics, 3D bounding boxes, BEV map grids, text prompts, and a reference frame from the past n frames to generate 224×400 surround-view images at 2 Hz. The full system outputs multi-view camera images for the driving agent, which in turn produces ego trajectories and evaluation scores (PDMS in [0,1] and ADS = RC × PDMS for closed-loop).

## Method

DRIVEARENA couples a LimSim-based Traffic Manager (hierarchical multi-vehicle planning with explicit traffic-flow algorithms, not diffusion-based) with a diffusion-based World Dreamer built on Stable Diffusion v1.5 and a randomly initialized ControlNet. The World Dreamer's condition encoder fuses CLIP text embeddings, Fourier-embedded camera parameters and 3D box vertices, BEV map grids, and—critically—2D layout canvases obtained by projecting HD map categories and 3D boxes onto each camera view, providing denser geometric guidance than prior BEV-only conditioning. A cross-view attention module enforces spatial coherence across surround cameras, while an auto-regressive generation pipeline uses the previously generated frame (and its relative ego pose) as a reference condition to maintain temporal consistency over arbitrarily long video streams. The three components (Traffic Manager, World Dreamer, driving agent) communicate over standardized network interfaces, enabling modular replacement of any component; the closed loop iterates as: agent outputs trajectory → Traffic Manager updates all vehicle states and detects collisions → new layout fed to World Dreamer → new surround images fed back to agent. Trained on nuScenes (8×A100, batch 4×8, 200K iterations), the system supports both open-loop (agent trajectory recorded but ego controlled by Traffic Manager) and closed-loop (agent trajectory directly controls ego) evaluation modes.

## Results

On generation fidelity (Table 1), DRIVEARENA's World Dreamer outperforms MagicDrive across all metrics: 3DOD mAP 16.06 vs. 12.92, BEV Drivable mIoU 59.37% vs. 51.46%, and average collision rate 0.24% vs. 0.35%. In open-loop driving evaluation, UniAD achieves a PDMS of 0.902 on World Dreamer-generated images, a drop of less than 1% from 0.910 on original nuScenes, confirming high visual fidelity. In closed-loop mode, UniAD attains an average PDMS of 0.667 with 13.7% route completion, while VAD drops to 0.534 PDMS and 4.59% route completion, revealing limitations that open-loop evaluation masks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole direct baseline is MagicDrive [Gao et al., 2023], a BEV-map-conditioned diffusion model for street view generation. DRIVEARENA's World Dreamer wins on every fidelity metric: 3DOD mAP improves from 12.92 to 16.06 (+24%), BEV Drivable mIoU from 51.46% to 59.37% (+7.9 pp), and average collision rate drops from 0.35% to 0.24% (−31%). The advantage is most pronounced on BEV Crossing (8.92 vs. 5.25, +70%) and BEV Divider (20.79 vs. 17.10, +22%), suggesting the layout-canvas projection and cross-view attention modules provide stronger geometric guidance. On the CARLA Town05 map (out-of-distribution for nuScenes-trained models), MagicDrive visibly fails to match road curvature and width, while DRIVEARENA produces structurally accurate images. In driving-agent evaluation, the paper does not compare against other simulators (e.g., CARLA, UniSim) quantitatively, but notes that open-loop PDMS on generated images (0.902 for UniAD) is within 1% of original nuScenes (0.910), whereas closed-loop route completion remains low (≤13.7%), highlighting that no existing platform fully resolves the closed-loop evaluation gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRIVEARENA builds on the Stable Diffusion + ControlNet pipeline for conditional image generation, extending it with a cross-view attention module (inspired by MagicDrive) and an auto-regressive generation paradigm for temporal consistency in surround-view driving scenes. For traffic simulation, it repurposes LimSim's multi-vehicle decision-making framework as the backend physical engine, departing from game-engine simulators (CARLA, MetaDrive) and 3D-reconstruction-based simulators (MARS, OASIM, UniSim) that rely on pre-built digital assets or logged-video reconstruction. The key architectural departure is decoupling traffic flow generation (explicit, rule-based) from visual rendering (generative, diffusion-based), enabling closed-loop interactivity without the domain gap of game engines or the scene-reconstruction overhead of NeRF/3D-GS methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DRIVEARENA addresses a critical gap: vision-based driving agents have been evaluated almost exclusively in open-loop on static datasets or in low-fidelity game-engine simulators, neither of which captures the interactive, cumulative-error dynamics of real driving. The platform's OpenStreetMap-based scalability and modular design make it a potentially widely adopted benchmark for closed-loop evaluation. However, significant limitations remain: the 2Hz generation rate, the acknowledged geometric/semantic consistency gaps without explicit 3D constraints, and the fact that current agents still achieve only 4–14% route completion in closed-loop mode, suggesting the evaluation challenge is far from solved. Open questions include whether higher-resolution generation, multi-frame auto-regression, and 3D-consistency constraints can close the sim-to-real gap sufficiently for meaningful agent differentiation.
