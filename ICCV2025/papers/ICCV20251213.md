## Problem

Current geospatial foundation models rely on contrastive learning or masked image modeling for self-supervised pretraining, but these paradigms struggle with the multi-scale, spatially heterogeneous nature of satellite imagery—contrastive methods overlook fine-grained spatial details, while MIM produces overly coarse representations in homogeneous regions. Generative diffusion models, which naturally capture multi-grained semantics during iterative denoising, remain largely unexplored as discriminative feature extractors for remote sensing, leaving open whether they can rival or exceed established SSL objectives as geospatial foundation models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB satellite images (resized to 512×512 or 256×256 depending on the DiffusionSat variant), which are encoded into latent space by a pretrained autoencoder before feature extraction; on multi-spectral datasets, only RGB bands are matched and missing channels are zero-filled. The frozen diffusion backbone produces a feature pyramid at 4 spatial scales, and the trained fusion module plus a task-specific decoder (UPerNet for segmentation, linear head for classification) yields per-pixel class maps or image-level labels. Notably, the diffusion model is run unconditionally with a generic text prompt ('A satellite image') to avoid information leakage from class-specific conditioning.

## Method

SatDiFuser repurposes the frozen DiffusionSat latent diffusion model as a self-supervised feature extractor by performing DDIM inversion from clean latents to trace a deterministic noise path, then running the denoising U-Net on the resulting noisy latents to capture multi-scale (4 resolutions) and multi-timestep (selected from {1, 100, 200}) features from ResNet and self-attention blocks. Three fusion strategies aggregate these diverse features: (i) global weighted fusion applies learnable scalar weights per block-timestep pair for broad aggregation; (ii) localized weighted fusion uses a lightweight convolutional gating network to produce pixel-wise importance maps that adaptively emphasize features at each spatial location; (iii) a mixture-of-experts joint fusion (E=8 experts, top-k=2 routing) concatenates module outputs per timestep and routes through specialized sub-networks to model complex inter-timestep and inter-module interactions. Only the fusion parameters and task decoder are trained, while the entire diffusion backbone remains frozen, making the approach a lightweight transfer-learning pipeline. Ablations show that early timesteps (first 20% of the diffusion schedule) and ResNet/self-attention features carry the most discriminative signal, while cross-attention features contribute minimally.

## Results

On semantic segmentation (mIoU), SatDiFuser with MoE fusion achieves 95.3% on PV4GER, 83.7% on NZ Cattle, 63.4% on NeonTree, 66.1% on Cashew Plantation, 31.9% on SA Crop Type, and 71.6% on Chesapeake Landcover, outperforming the strongest baseline (DOFA) by up to +5.9% mIoU. On classification, SatDiFuser with Global fusion reaches 66.2% F1 on BigEarthNet, 98.3% on Brick Kiln, 97.7% on EuroSAT, 58.4% on ForestNet, 97.8% on PV4GER, and 59.3% on SO2Sat, surpassing the best baseline by up to +7.9% F1. Notably, SatDiFuser uses only RGB bands yet outperforms multi-spectral baselines on datasets like Cashew Plantation and SA Crop Type, and even exceeds fully supervised results on ForestNet and SO2Sat.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SatDiFuser outperforms all seven baselines on the majority of tasks. Against the strongest MIM-based baseline DOFA [68], SatDiFuser gains +5.7% mIoU on NeonTree, +4.3% on Cashew Plantation, and +5.9% on Chesapeake Landcover, while narrowly losing by 0.4% on NZ Cattle (83.7 vs. 84.1 for ScaleMAE). Against the contrastive baselines CROMA [14] and RemoteCLIP [35], SatDiFuser leads by 4–12% mIoU on segmentation and 3–13% on classification, with the largest gap on BigEarthNet (+7.9% F1 over CROMA). The supervised Satlas [6] baseline is consistently outperformed, confirming that self-supervised diffusion features surpass even large-scale supervised pretraining. ScaleMAE [44] is the closest competitor on NZ Cattle (84.1 vs. 83.7) but trails SatDiFuser by 5–16% on other segmentation tasks. GFM [38] (continual pretraining) and SSL4EO-MAE/DINO [62] are clearly outperformed across nearly all datasets, with gaps exceeding 5% mIoU on most tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes DiffusionSat, a latent diffusion model pretrained for satellite image generation, as a frozen feature extractor for discriminative remote sensing tasks, drawing on DDIM inversion to obtain multi-timestep, multi-scale features from the U-Net backbone. It extends the CV line of work on mining diffusion model internals for representation learning (DiffSeg, DiffCut, Diffusion Hyperfeatures) into the geospatial domain, where prior SSL efforts have relied on contrastive learning, masked image modeling, or self-distillation. The three proposed fusion strategies—global weighted, localized weighted, and mixture-of-experts—go beyond the simple concatenation or uniform-resolution aggregation used in prior diffusion-feature work, preserving the native multi-scale structure of RS imagery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a purely generative diffusion model, with no discriminative training signal, yields features that outperform purpose-built discriminative GFMs by up to 5.7% mIoU and 7.9% F1 challenges the assumption that SSL objectives must be explicitly discriminative to produce strong representations. Key limitations include the restriction to RGB bands (the backbone was not pretrained on multi-spectral data) and the use of an unconditional diffusion model; extending to multi-modal RS data and conditioned image-to-image diffusion could unlock further gains. The framework is backbone-agnostic in principle, but validation beyond DiffusionSat remains limited, leaving open whether the findings generalize to other diffusion architectures or to tasks beyond segmentation and classification.
