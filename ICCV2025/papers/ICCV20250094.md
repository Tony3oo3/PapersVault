## Problem

All-in-One (AiO) adverse weather removal models struggle with highly variable and localized degradations because global frequency-domain filtering (Fourier, wavelet) modifies the entire image uniformly, which is effective for repetitive patterns like blur but ineffective for random, spatially heterogeneous distortions such as rain streaks, snow particles, and raindrops. The paper identifies a gap between extracting useful spectral information and preserving spatial detail: wavelet-based methods halve resolution requiring distortion-prone resizing, while direct Fourier operations lack spatial selectivity.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single weather-degraded RGB image (rain, snow, raindrop, or fog) as input and produces a single restored clean image, operating without any external knowledge, language prompts, or multi-frame information. It is a 4-stage encoder-decoder network where the stage index increases from 1 to 4 in the encoder and decreases back to 1 in the decoder, with the number of blocks per stage controlled by L_p. The same grouping-mask generated at a given encoder stage is reused at the corresponding decoder stage to ensure coherent feature manipulation.

## Method

SSGformer decomposes the input via a Spectral-based Decomposition Prompt (SDP) that extracts high-frequency edges with the Sobel operator and low-frequency degradation textures via SVD, then fuses them through multi-head linear attention to produce degradation-aware features. A Mask Generator converts these features into a single-channel grouping-mask that partitions the feature map into g_p spatially similar groups. The Spatial Grouping Transformer Block (SGTB) applies Feature-Grouped Attention (FGA) within each group (in-group attention) and between the most relevant pair of groups (cross-group attention, selected via cosine similarity on channel-pooled representative features), with learnable weights balancing the two. Half the SGTB blocks per stage use channel attention (SGTB-C) and the other half use spatial attention (SGTB-S), balancing feature-wise and spatial dependencies without increasing model capacity. Training uses a combined L1 and Pearson correlation loss to capture both pixel-level and patch-level fidelity.

## Results

On the All-weather dataset, SSGformer achieves an average PSNR of 32.63 dB and SSIM of 0.939, outperforming the strongest baseline Histoformer (32.43 dB / 0.936) by +0.20 dB in PSNR. Per-weather, SSGformer leads on rain (32.43 dB, +0.35 dB over Histoformer) and raindrop (33.24 dB, +0.18 dB), while achieving the best PSNR on snow (32.22 dB, +0.06 dB). On the real-world WeatherStream dataset, SSGformer attains the best average PSNR (24.77 dB) and SSIM (0.774), surpassing Histoformer (24.64 / 0.773) and TransWeather (24.56 / 0.742).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSGformer outperforms all 21 baselines on the All-weather dataset average, beating the closest competitor Histoformer [47] by +0.20 dB PSNR and +0.003 SSIM. Against the general All-in-One restoration models (Restormer, PromptIR, AdaIR), SSGformer gains +1.77 to +2.05 dB in average PSNR, demonstrating the advantage of weather-specific spectral grouping over generic restoration. Among dedicated adverse-weather AiO models, SSGformer surpasses AWRCP [63] by +0.88 dB and WGWS [78] by +2.01 dB in average PSNR. On the real-world WeatherStream dataset, SSGformer achieves the best average PSNR (24.77 dB) and SSIM (0.774), edging out Histoformer (24.64 / 0.773) and clearly outperforming TransWeather (24.56 / 0.742) and WGWS (24.43 / 0.735). The only metric where SSGformer trails a baseline is fog SSIM on WeatherStream (0.7335 vs. Histoformer's 0.7348), a negligible 0.0013 gap. Single-task methods (e.g., MPRNet for rain at 28.03 dB, DDMSNet for snow at 28.85 dB) are substantially outperformed, confirming the benefit of a unified model with spectral grouping over task-specific approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work in All-in-One (AiO) adverse weather removal: frequency-domain restoration (Fourmer, AdaIR, AIRFormer) and prompt/grouping-based restoration (Histoformer, AWRCP). It departs from global frequency filtering (Fourier, wavelet) by instead extracting local spectral features via classical Sobel and SVD filters, preserving spatial resolution while still capturing degradation structure. The grouping mechanism extends Histoformer's intensity-based pixel-sorting by replacing it with a spectral-similarity-driven mask, and the SGTB introduces spatial attention alongside the channel attention used by prior AiO transformers. Multi-head linear attention is repurposed from the NLP literature to fuse heterogeneous spectral features before mask generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete limitation of global frequency-domain methods—poor handling of localized, random weather degradations—by grounding grouping in spectral similarity rather than intensity or learned codebooks. The modest but consistent gains over Histoformer (+0.20 dB PSNR, +0.003 SSNR on average) and the clean ablations (Sobel+SVD > either alone; spatial+channel > channel-only; cross-group > in-group-only) provide practical guidance for AiO architecture design. A key open question is whether the reliance on classical filters (Sobel, SVD) generalizes to scenes with high background complexity where weather-specific spectral cues are absent; the authors themselves flag this and point toward LLM/VLM-augmented strategies as future work.
