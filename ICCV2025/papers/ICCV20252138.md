## Problem

Long-video VLMs face a fundamental sampling dilemma: low-density uniform sampling risks missing critical visual cues concentrated in brief segments, while high-density sampling floods the context with redundant frames, exceeding model limits and degrading accuracy. No existing benchmark systematically evaluates how well models handle questions that genuinely require high sampling density, making it impossible to measure whether a model's sampling strategy is adequate for a given task.

## Input / Output

The method takes hour-scale egocentric videos (20–115 min, from EGO4D) and multiple-choice text questions containing explicit temporal referring expressions that point to a specific short segment (average 3 min) within the video. The output is the selected answer option (A–D) along with the set of sampled keyframes used for inference. Questions target dense, short-duration action sequences (each step lasting ~1 second), ensuring a high Necessary Sampling Density that cannot be resolved by sparse global sampling.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage, training-free Reasoning-Driven Hierarchical Sampling (RHS) framework. In Stage 1, the video is sparsely sampled (e.g., 4 frames/min) and the VLM reasons over spatio-temporal context to localize segments containing question-relevant visual cues. In Stage 2, only those identified segments are densely sampled (e.g., 1 FPS) and fed to the VLM for final answer inference. A lightweight Semantic-Guided Frame Selector (SGFS) augments Stage 1 by using SigLIP2 embeddings to compute pairwise cosine similarities between candidate frames, adding a temporal distance penalty, and solving a dynamic-programming optimization to select a diverse subset of k frames that maximizes information coverage. This plug-and-play module requires no question prior and adds negligible computational overhead relative to VLM inference.

## Results

On LSDBench (hour-scale videos, ~45 min average), the proposed RHS method applied to Qwen2.5-VL achieves 52.2% accuracy with only 225 sampled frames, matching the 768-frame global uniform sampling result (52.5%) and surpassing the 256-frame setting (50.1%) by 2.1%. Gemini-2.0-Flash, the strongest baseline, reaches 56.2% at 1-FPS (2,700 frames) but still lags 8.6% behind its Oracle upper bound of 64.8%, demonstrating that even massive frame budgets under global uniform sampling fail to close the gap. Under the Oracle setting (target segment only, ~3 min), Qwen2.5-VL peaks at 56.4% (1-FPS, 180 frames) while InternVideo2.5 reaches 59.1% (256 frames), establishing the performance ceiling for each model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed RHS method on Qwen2.5-VL (52.2%, 225 frames) outperforms all single-stage baselines at comparable or lower frame budgets: it beats Qwen2.5-VL global uniform at 256 frames (50.1%) by 2.1%, matches Qwen2.5-VL at 768 frames (52.5%) with 3.4× fewer frames, and surpasses LongVila (49.8%), InternVideo2.5 (50.1%), and Qwen2-VL (48.0%) at 256 frames. LongVA is the weakest baseline, peaking at only 33.0% even at 1024 frames, indicating severe degradation under high-NSD conditions. Gemini-2.0-Flash achieves the highest absolute accuracy (56.2%) but requires 2,700 frames at 1-FPS and still trails its Oracle by 8.6%, confirming that brute-force sampling cannot resolve the dilemma. The Oracle experiments further reveal that the gap between ideal and global sampling is largest for Qwen2.5-VL (56.4% Oracle vs. 52.5% full video), suggesting the most room for sampling-strategy improvement in that model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by formalizing the 'Sampling Dilemma' through the concept of Necessary Sampling Density (NSD), a metric absent from prior long-video benchmarks like EgoSchema, LongVideoBench, and VideoMME, which focus on segment retrieval or rely heavily on audio. While existing long-video VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL, Gemini, LongVila) employ global uniform or dynamic sampling, and RAG-based methods (Video-RAG, VITA) sidestep the dilemma at the cost of real-time applicability, none explicitly evaluate how well a model's sampling strategy matches the information density required by a question. The RHS framework repurposes the general idea of coarse-to-fine localization (analogous to retrieval-then-refine in RAG) into a training-free, two-stage inference pipeline, while the SGFS module applies a dynamic-programming frame-selection objective inspired by diversity-maximizing subset selection, but without any question-conditioned signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By isolating sampling efficiency as the variable under test, LSDBench gives the community a diagnostic tool to measure whether a video VLM's frame budget is being spent where it matters, a question that prior benchmarks conflate with model capacity or context length. The finding that even Gemini-2.0-Flash at 1-FPS over a full hour-long video lags its own Oracle performance by 8.6% underscores that simply scaling context windows does not solve the sampling problem. Open questions include whether the two-stage RHS approach generalizes to non-egocentric or multi-camera settings, whether the NSD concept can be extended to streaming/real-time scenarios, and whether the modest accuracy gains (≈2% over uniform sampling) scale to more complex or open-ended video tasks beyond multiple-choice QA.
