## Problem

Existing human motion generation models (diffusion, autoregressive, masked) capture statistical distributions of motion but lack explicit physical constraints, producing artifacts like floating, foot sliding, and ground penetration. Prior physics-based approaches either require expensive real motion data or embed physics optimization into multiple diffusion steps, inflating inference cost. The paper tackles the unexplored question of whether an effective, model-agnostic physics optimizer can be trained entirely on synthetic noisy motion data without any real-world motion capture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252564/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework accepts text descriptions or music signals as conditioning and outputs a 3D human motion sequence represented as per-frame joint rotations and positions (20 FPS on HumanML3D, 60 FPS on AIST++). The motion generator is model-agnostic, compatible with diffusion-based (MDM, MotionDiffuse), autoregressive (T2M-GPT, Bailando), and masked (MoMask) architectures. An imitation selection operation filters out non-grounded motions (sitting, swimming, climbing) that the physics simulator cannot replicate, using an MPJPE threshold to decide whether to accept the refined or original motion.

## Method

Morph employs a two-stage collaborative training paradigm. In Stage 1, a pretrained motion generator produces large-scale noisy synthetic motions, which are used to train a Motion Physics Refinement (MPR) module: an MLP-based motion imitator trained with PPO inside Isaac Gym to mimic the noisy motions under enforced physical constraints, guided by a mimic reward, an energy penalty, and a Motion VAE prior reward (trained on synthetic data to provide smooth distributional constraints without adversarial instability). Hard negative mining upweights sequences where imitation fails. In Stage 2, the physics-refined motions (filtered by the imitation selection operation) fine-tune the motion generator via MSE loss, creating a mutual enhancement loop that can be iterated over multiple rounds. At inference, the fine-tuned generator and MPR module operate in tandem, with the imitation selection as a post-processing step.

## Results

On HumanML3D (text-to-motion), Morph-MoMask achieves the best overall performance with RTOP-3 of 0.816, FID of 0.041, and dramatically reduced physical artifacts: Penetrate drops to 0.000 (vs. 23.152 for MoMask), Float to 2.141 (vs. 10.660), and Skate to 0.010 (vs. 5.262). On AIST++ (music-to-dance), Morph-Bailando achieves FIDk of 26.25, BAS of 0.2412, and reduces Penetrate to 0.000 (vs. 31.183 for Bailando) and Float to 2.057 (vs. 12.650). Across all four text-to-motion generators (MDM, MotionDiffuse, T2M-GPT, MoMask), Morph consistently eliminates ground penetration (Penetrate = 0.000) and reduces foot sliding by >99% while maintaining or improving generation quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Morph consistently outperforms all physical-plausibility baselines (PhysDiff, Reindiffuse) on the majority of physical metrics: Morph-MDM achieves PFC 0.697 vs. PhysDiff-w/MDM's 0.998, and Morph-MoMask achieves Skate 0.010 vs. Reindiffuse's 0.058, though Reindiffuse reports a lower Float (0.058 vs. 2.141) and PFC (0.000 vs. 0.645). Against the four underlying generators (MDM, MotionDiffuse, T2M-GPT, MoMask), Morph eliminates ground penetration entirely (Penetrate 0.000 vs. 17.4–72.3) and reduces foot sliding by 99%+ (Skate 0.010–0.039 vs. 3.4–7.8) while matching or improving FID and RTOP-3. On music-to-dance (AIST++), Morph-Bailando beats Bailando on FIDk (26.25 vs. 28.16), BAS (0.2412 vs. 0.2332), and all physical metrics (Penetrate 0 vs. 31.2, Float 2.06 vs. 12.65, Skate 0.021 vs. 4.47); Morph-EDGE similarly dominates EDGE on physical metrics (Penetrate 0 vs. 76.5, Float 3.52 vs. 61.0) and FIDg (19.82 vs. 22.12). For generation-only baselines in Table 3 (MLD, AttT2M, MMM, BAMM), Morph-MoMask achieves the best RTOP-3 (0.816) and FID (0.041), slightly surpassing BAMM (0.814, 0.055) and MoMask (0.807, 0.045). The key trade-off is that Morph's physical refinement introduces a small IFR (~1.5–2%), meaning a small fraction of motions (e.g., non-grounded actions) are passed through unrefined rather than degraded.

## Relation to Prior Work

Morph departs from prior physics-aware motion generation work (PhysDiff, Reindiffuse) that embeds physics constraints iteratively within the diffusion denoising process, instead decoupling physics refinement into a separate module trained via reinforcement learning in a physics simulator. It also diverges from physics-based imitation learning approaches (DeepMimic, AMP, Perpetual Humanoid Control) that require large-scale real 3D motion data as priors, by training the motion imitator exclusively on synthetic noisy data produced by the motion generator itself. The prior reward module (a lightweight Motion VAE) replaces the adversarial motion priors used in AMP-style methods, trading training stability for comparable naturalness. The collaborative two-stage training loop—where the generator supplies data to the physics refiner, which in turn fine-tunes the generator—represents a new training paradigm not present in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The model-agnostic, motion-free design makes physical plausibility enhancement broadly applicable to any existing motion generator without requiring expensive real motion capture data, lowering the barrier for physically plausible synthesis in animation and VR. However, the framework cannot handle non-grounded or interactive motions (sitting, swimming, climbing), which limits its coverage of real-world scenarios, and the IFR metric reveals a non-trivial failure rate. Open questions include whether the multi-round iterative optimization converges to a stable fixed point, whether the approach extends to multi-agent or scene-interaction settings, and whether the Isaac Gym dependency limits deployment to real-time or embedded applications.
