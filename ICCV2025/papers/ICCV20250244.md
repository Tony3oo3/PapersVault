## Problem

Large-scale 3D Gaussian Splatting scenes are not static—new objects are constantly added—but full reconstruction after every change is prohibitively expensive. Simply inserting new Gaussians from a source field into a target field produces visible stitching artifacts because the two fields were captured under different environmental lighting. This paper addresses the under-explored problem of partially updating a 3D Gaussian field with new objects while maintaining consistent global illumination.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250244/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two pre-reconstructed 3D Gaussian fields (a source field containing the new object and a target field without it), each built from multiview posed images captured at different times under different lighting, plus optional 2D prompt points for segmentation. It produces an updated target Gaussian field where the new object is seamlessly integrated with relit appearance matching the target's illumination. Registration uses rendered depth maps and image correspondences (GIM) to align the fields in 3D, and segmentation leverages depth-difference prompts fed into SAGA to isolate the new object's Gaussians.

## Method

GauUpdate is a four-stage pipeline: (1) coarse-to-fine registration via GeoTransformer point-cloud alignment refined by GIM image correspondences projected into 3D through depth maps, followed by RANSAC+ICP; (2) new-object segmentation using SAGA with automatically generated depth-difference prompts; (3) shared material decomposition where a single multi-scale hash grid stores albedo, roughness, and metallic parameters for both fields while each field retains its own environment light, trained alternately with L1+D-SSIM rendering loss and TV regularization; (4) appearance blending that relights the inserted object under the target's estimated environment using a simplified Disney BRDF with split-sum approximation, plus re-baked occlusion/indirect illumination and shadow mapping. The key insight is that sharing the material hash grid across the two fields provides a strong cross-scene consistency constraint that alleviates the ill-posedness of inverse rendering, since identical geometry must yield identical BRDF under different lighting.

## Results

On the GauUpdate-Synthetic benchmark (8 scenes), GauUpdate achieves an average PSNR of 27.51, SSIM of 0.893, and LPIPS of 0.105, substantially outperforming all baselines. On the GauUpdate-Real benchmark (3 real-world scenes), the method reaches PSNR 25.27, SSIM 0.875, and LPIPS 0.099. In the relighting comparison against R3DG, GauUpdate improves PSNR by 6.6–8.2 dB (e.g., 28.34 vs. 21.68 on Bear) while running 3–4× faster (29.4 min vs. 136.2 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GauUpdate outperforms all four baselines across every metric on both synthetic and real datasets. Against mix-3DGS (PSNR 20.88) and GOF (PSNR 19.01) on GauUpdate-Synthetic, the proposed method gains +6.6 and +8.5 dB respectively, as these baselines simply merge or retrain fields without addressing illumination inconsistency. The Composite baseline (PSNR 22.85) is the closest competitor on PSNR but still trails by 4.7 dB, because direct Gaussian composition leaves visible stitching artifacts at object boundaries. GS-IR (PSNR 21.38) performs better than naive merging thanks to its material-lighting decomposition, but lacks cross-scene material consistency constraints, leading to inaccurate environment light estimation and residual appearance mismatches. Against R3DG (Table 3), GauUpdate achieves 6.6–8.2 dB higher PSNR (e.g., 28.34 vs. 21.68 on Bear) while training 3–4× faster (29.4 min vs. 136.2 min), confirming that the shared material hash grid provides a stronger inductive bias than R3DG's per-Gaussian BRDF attributes with ray tracing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representation and the inverse rendering line of work (GS-IR, GIR, R3DG) that decomposes material and lighting from multiview images, but departs from them by introducing a cross-scene shared material hash grid constraint that leverages the fact that two fields of the same scene (differing only by a new object) must share identical BRDF parameters. For registration and segmentation, it repurposes off-the-shelf tools (GeoTransformer, GIM, SAGA) in a straightforward two-phase pipeline rather than proposing new algorithms, positioning the core contribution squarely in the inverse-rendering-with-constraints space. The paper also connects to Gaussian registration work (GaussReg, LoopSplat) but demonstrates that simple image-correspondence-based refinement suffices for the narrower alignment problem here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As 3DGS is adopted for large-scale scene mapping (rooms, city blocks), the inability to incrementally update a reconstructed field without full recapture is a practical bottleneck that this paper directly addresses. The shared-material constraint is a clean and generalizable idea for resolving ill-posed inverse rendering whenever multiple observations of the same geometry under different lighting are available, potentially applicable beyond object insertion. However, the method is limited to additive changes (new objects), relies on GOF-style geometry that struggles with fine details, and the inverse rendering remains fundamentally ill-posed without stronger priors; scaling to simultaneous multi-object edits, object removal, or very large scenes remains open.
