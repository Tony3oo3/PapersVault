## Problem

Inversion-based image editing pipelines (inversion + denoising) incur roughly double the computation of generation alone, yet prior acceleration methods ignore the fact that only a small spatial region actually changes and that the inversion phase is far more redundant than denoising. This paper identifies and exploits both spatial and temporal redundancy to make editing practical on resource-constrained devices.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251626/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a source image, a binary editing mask delineating the region to modify, and a task-specific conditioning signal (text prompt, drag point pairs, or a reference image). It operates entirely in the latent space of a flow-matching DiT (FLUX-Dev 12B or SD-series), with 28 inversion and 28 denoising steps, and outputs a single edited RGB image via the VQ-decoder. The mask is applied in latent space at every denoising step to enforce background preservation.

## Method

EEdit introduces three complementary mechanisms. (1) Spatial Locality Caching (SLoC) assigns a decaying score bonus to tokens in the edited region and its L1-neighborhood, then at each timestep only recomputes the top-R% highest-scoring tokens while reusing cached SA/CA/MLP outputs for the rest; a frequency counter further prioritizes tokens that have been cached the longest to limit error accumulation. (2) Token Index Preprocessing (TIP) proves that the score-update-and-selection logic is mathematically equivalent when computed offline, so all index selection is precomputed before inference, reducing per-step cache overhead to a single scatter/read. (3) Inversion Step Skipping (ISS) reuses the noise latent from the previous timestep at every m-th inversion step (m=3 in practice), cutting inversion cost to ~33% of full computation with negligible quality loss, since the paper shows inversion is far more temporally redundant than denoising.

## Results

On the PIE-Bench prompt-guided editing task using FLUX-Dev (12B), EEdit (SLoC + ISS) achieves the best background consistency among all compared methods: PSNR 31.97 dB, LPIPS 0.0195, and SSIM 0.94, substantially outperforming the next-best InfEdit (PSNR 28.11, LPIPS 0.0561). In terms of efficiency, EEdit completes editing in 4.60 s with 264.5 T FLOPs, compared to 26.23 s / 2223.2 T for RF-Edit and 13.56 s / 1111.6 T for RF-inversion, yielding a 5.7× speedup over RF-Edit. The CLIP-T prompt-adherence score of 25.38 is competitive with PnP (25.42–25.48) and P2P (25.13), though slightly below RF-Edit (26.64) and RF-inversion (26.31). The paper reports an average 2.46× acceleration across all editing tasks with near-lossless quality relative to the unaccelerated pipeline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EEdit dominates on background-consistency metrics across all baselines: PSNR improves from 17.74 (RF-inv) / 20.17 (RF-Edit) / 22.20 (Flow-Edit) to 31.97, and LPIPS drops from 24.40 / 18.50 / 10.49 to 1.95, reflecting the spatial locality caching that skips unedited regions. On prompt adherence (CLIP-T), EEdit (25.38) is competitive with PnP (25.42–25.48) and P2P (25.13) but trails RF-Edit (26.64) and RF-inversion (26.31) by roughly 1–1.3 points, a trade-off the authors attribute to reduced computation in the edited region. In speed, EEdit (4.60 s) is 5.7× faster than RF-Edit (26.23 s), 2.9× faster than RF-inv (13.56 s), and 2.6× faster than Flow-Edit (11.84 s), while remaining only 1.6× slower than the inversion-free InfEdit (2.90 s), which however achieves lower background fidelity (PSNR 28.11 vs 31.97). For drag-guided editing, EEdit is compared qualitatively against DragDiffusion, RegionDrag, and FastDrag, showing superior background preservation; quantitatively (Table 3), enabling TIP+ISS reduces inference from 7.12 s to 5.66 s while slightly improving FID (20.61→22.07) and PSNR (33.47→33.68). For reference-guided composition, EEdit is compared against TF-ICON, PrimeComposer, and DCCF, with qualitative results in Figure 5 demonstrating better structural consistency; the quantitative ablation shows TIP+ISS cuts inference from 7.25 s to 5.66 s with negligible metric change.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the token-wise feature caching line of work (Toca, Duca) by repurposing it for mask-guided editing, where the editing mask provides a strong spatial prior that general caching methods ignore. It builds directly on RF-inversion (Rout et al.) for the inversion stage and draws on DDIM's deterministic sampling to motivate skipping inversion steps. The key departure from prior caching approaches is the exploitation of editing-specific structure: unedited regions are provably redundant because their latents are directly replaced, and the score bonus map encodes spatial locality around the edit boundary rather than relying on learned or attention-based importance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.46× average speedup with near-lossless quality makes inversion-based editing viable on resource-constrained devices, addressing a practical bottleneck that has limited real-time interactive editing. The observation that inversion is far more redundant than denoising (33% of steps suffice) is a broadly applicable insight for any inversion-based pipeline, not just this framework. Limitations include the requirement for an explicit editing mask (restricting applicability to mask-guided tasks) and evaluation primarily on FLUX and SD-series models; extending to mask-free editing or video editing remains open.
