## Problem

Existing multi-turn conversation benchmarks for VLMs (MMDU, ConvBench) cover a narrow range of tasks and lack advanced reasoning requirements such as mathematics, coding, and equation verification, leaving it unclear whether VLMs that excel on single-turn benchmarks can sustain coherent, contextually-aware responses across multiple turns of increasingly complex dialogue.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250876/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each conversation pairs a single RGB image (sourced from 12 diverse benchmarks spanning charts, diagrams, nature, and science) with four user queries of increasing complexity, yielding eight utterances total. The evaluated model produces free-form text responses at each turn, which are scored by GPT-4o against an instance-specific checklist of binary questions covering 37 key aspects (perceptual accuracy, factual correctness, linguistic clarity, etc.). The final per-turn score is the product of the checklist completion ratio and a quality assessment score (integer 1–10, rescaled to 10–100).

## Method

MULTIVERSE is constructed through a five-step pipeline: (1) collecting and filtering ~50K images from 12 VLM benchmarks via pHash deduplication, GPT-4o quality scoring, and weighted category sampling down to 1K images; (2) generating fictional user personas with scenario-specific goals using GPT-4o; (3) producing four-turn conversations that follow principles of increasing complexity, diverse linguistic style, and detailed expert-level responses; (4) manual review removing samples for unnatural flow, factual errors, or blindness (solvability without the image); and (5) generating per-turn checklists validated by both GPT-4o and Claude-3.5-Sonnet plus manual review. For evaluation, GPT-4o-2024-11-20 acts as the judge VLM, answering each binary checklist item and assigning a 1–10 quality score per turn, with the final metric being the product of checklist completion ratio and rescaled quality score. Two evaluation settings are compared—Oracle (ground-truth dialogue history) and Self-Prediction (model-generated history)—revealing that in-context learning from golden dialogue significantly boosts weaker models while exposing a maximum 44.64% performance gap for Qwen2.5-VL-72B.

## Results

Under the Oracle setting (ground-truth dialogue history), GPT-4o achieves the highest average score of 49.63% on MULTIVERSE, with Qwen2.5-VL-7B (48.15%) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (47.95%) following closely. Even the strongest model remains below 50%, confirming the benchmark's difficulty. Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL-7B leads at 48.15%, while the weakest models (InternVL2.5-1B at 21.22% and LLaMA-3.2-11B-Vision at 21.61%) score less than half of GPT-4o's performance. The slope metric (r) shows that smaller/weaker models benefit most from multi-turn context (e.g., LLaVA-1.5-7B: r=7.08), whereas Qwen2.5-VL-72B (r=-1.95) and Claude-3.5-Sonnet (r=-0.37) show no improvement across turns.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o leads all 18 evaluated VLMs with an average score of 49.63%, narrowly ahead of Qwen2.5-VL-7B (48.15%) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (47.95%), with Gemini-2.0-Flash close behind at 47.76%. Notably, the Qwen2.5-VL series outperforms all proprietary models except GPT-4o, with Qwen2.5-VL-7B surpassing Claude-3.5-Sonnet (46.76%) and Claude-3.7-Sonnet (44.06%) by 1.4 and 4.1 points respectively. Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL-7B dominates, while LLaMA-3.2-11B-Vision (21.61%) underperforms even the older LLaVA-1.5-7B (24.12%). Under the Self-Prediction setting (model generates its own dialogue history), all models degrade substantially, with Qwen2.5-VL-72B suffering the largest drop of -44.64 points, suggesting linguistic style mismatch with the GPT-4o-generated reference dialogues. Across interaction goals (Table 3), all models perform best on Analysis and Understanding but struggle with Optimization and Research tasks, with GPT-4o scoring 56.41 on Analysis but only 43.50 on Research.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-turn VLM evaluation line initiated by MMDU and ConvBench by dramatically broadening task coverage (484 tasks vs. <15 and 219 respectively) and incorporating advanced reasoning domains (mathematics, coding, chart interpretation) that prior benchmarks largely omit. For dialogue generation, it adopts the personal background-to-conversation approach from Jang et al. (2023), Kim et al. (2022), and Lee et al. (2024) to ensure lexical diversity and realistic user personas. For evaluation, it departs from the common 1–10 LLM-as-judge scoring in favor of instance-specific checklist-based assessment inspired by CheckEval (Lee et al., 2024) and WildBench (Lin et al., 2024), arguing that binary checklist items yield more interpretable and robust scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MULTIVERSE fills a critical gap by providing the first multi-turn VLM benchmark that spans diverse tasks, interaction goals, and image domains at scale, making it a likely standard reference for evaluating conversational VLM capabilities. The finding that even GPT-4o achieves under 50% success rate underscores a substantial and underappreciated gap between single-turn and multi-turn performance. Open questions include whether the in-context learning effect (ground-truth dialogue history boosting weaker models) reflects a genuine capability or an artifact of linguistic alignment with the GPT-4o-generated reference, and how to close the multi-turn reasoning gap without simply scaling model size. A limitation is the reliance on GPT-4o as both the dialogue generator and the evaluator, which may introduce systematic biases.
