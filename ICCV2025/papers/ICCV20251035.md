## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting faces two critical barriers when scaled to urban environments: explicit Gaussian representations grow without bound, causing out-of-memory failures beyond ~11 million Gaussians on a 24 GB GPU, and uncontrolled appearance variations across multi-view captures (seasonal, weather, lighting) cause 3DGS to overfit with redundant primitives that manifest as floating artifacts from novel viewpoints. Existing large-scale methods either sacrifice real-time rendering or rely on post-training compression that degrades quality in scenes requiring high compression ratios.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view aerial RGB images (up to ~10,000 images spanning ~5 km²), SfM-derived camera poses, and SfM 3D point clouds with associated 2D feature points. It produces a partitioned 3D Gaussian scene with three bottom-up LOD levels, each governed by a user-defined Gaussian budget, enabling real-time rendering (63–100 FPS on an A100) within 24 GB VRAM. The appearance transform module additionally outputs per-Gaussian color and opacity offsets that can be re-applied at render time for flexible appearance editing at zero additional cost.

## Method

The framework partitions the scene horizontally and assigns training images via a point-based visibility metric (convex hull area ratio of partition-relevant feature points), then applies a distance-dependent gradient threshold for in-partition prioritized densification to avoid wasting Gaussians outside each partition. A controllable bottom-up LOD strategy progressively generates detail levels by increasing the Taming3DGS budget, shortening densification intervals, and raising image resolution at each level, eliminating the need for post-training compression. A fine-grained appearance transform module assigns independent embeddings to both each image and each 3D Gaussian, feeding them through a lightweight MLP to predict per-Gaussian color and opacity offsets, with cosine-similarity and opacity-offset regularizations to prevent overfitting. Scale regularization (maximum-scale and anisotropy-ratio penalties) and depth regularization (alternating hard/soft depth losses from Depth Anything V2) suppress floaters and ordering-inversion artifacts, while Mip-Splatting anti-aliasing and AbsGS detail enhancement further improve fidelity.

## Results

On the three main urban-scale scenes (Rubble, JNU-ZH, BigCity), the proposed method with LOD enabled achieves PSNR of 27.03, 25.71, and 26.41 dB respectively, while using only 3.60M, 6.65M, and 6.84M Gaussians and rendering at 99.7, 63.9, and 73.0 FPS. Without LOD, the method reaches PSNR 27.29, 25.85, and 26.62 dB, outperforming all baselines on SSIM, PSNR, and LPIPS across all scenes. The LOD mode reduces Gaussian count by 73–91% (e.g., 13.52M → 3.60M on Rubble) while incurring only a 0.26 dB PSNR drop, enabling real-time rendering within 24 GB VRAM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Switch-NeRF, the proposed method wins decisively on all quality metrics (e.g., PSNR 27.03 vs. 23.05 on Rubble, 26.41 vs. 20.39 on BigCity) and is orders of magnitude faster (99.7 vs. <0.1 FPS). Against CityGaussian with LOD, Ours achieves 2.13 dB higher PSNR on Rubble (27.03 vs. 24.90), 3.38 dB on JNU-ZH (25.71 vs. 22.33), and 4.17 dB on BigCity (26.41 vs. 22.24), while using comparable or fewer Gaussians. Against Hierarchical-3DGS with LOD, Ours leads by 3.65 dB PSNR on Rubble and 3.24 dB on BigCity, with substantially better LPIPS (0.245 vs. 0.300 on Rubble). Against vanilla 3DGS, Ours (LOD) achieves higher PSNR on all scenes (27.03 vs. 25.72 on Rubble, 26.41 vs. 24.52 on BigCity) while using fewer Gaussians (3.60M vs. 7.10M on Rubble), though at a slight FPS cost (99.7 vs. 108.9). The only near-tie is LPIPS on Rubble without LOD, where Ours (0.228) matches CityGaussian (0.228).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3DGS (Kerbl et al., 2023) as its core representation and extends Block-NeRF's scene partitioning with a novel visibility-based image selection mechanism. It adopts Taming3DGS's steerable densification to generate controllable LOD levels in a bottom-up manner during training (rather than via post-training compression as in CityGaussian or Hierarchical-3DGS), and integrates CityGaussian's partition-level LOD selection, Mip-Splatting's anti-aliasing, DNGaussian's depth regularization, and StopThePop's tile-based culling. The key conceptual departure is the fine-grained appearance transform module that assigns independent embeddings to both images and individual Gaussians, going beyond image-level embeddings used in NeRF-W or SWAG, and the distance-dependent densification threshold that prioritizes in-partition computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides a practical, end-to-end pipeline for reconstructing truly urban-scale scenes (up to 5 km²) with real-time rendering under strict memory budgets, addressing a gap where prior 3DGS-based large-scale methods either required post-training compression or multi-GPU setups. The controllable LOD generation during training eliminates the quality degradation associated with aggressive post-hoc compression, which is particularly important for scenes with complex geometry. Open questions remain: the method's reliance on accurate SfM poses limits robustness in challenging capture conditions, the LOD switching is non-incremental (causing storage overhead and potential visual popping), and it is unclear how well the approach generalizes to dynamic urban scenes with heavy transient content beyond simple mask-based removal.
