## Problem

Continual VQA methods that mitigate catastrophic forgetting typically store full image-question pairs in a replay buffer, incurring high memory costs and raising serious privacy concerns (e.g., storing faces, license plates). Memory-free alternatives avoid these issues but perform poorly in multimodal settings. The paper asks whether storing only past questions—without any visual data—can suffice to prevent forgetting, and identifies the 'out-of-answer-set' problem where models overfit to the current task's answer vocabulary and misanswer prior-task questions.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives an image (or video clip for NExT-QA) and a natural-language question, and generates a text answer autoregressively. During training, the distinctive input construction pairs current-task images with questions sampled from a question-only memory buffer (5000 questions for VQAv2, 500 for NExT-QA), so the model never sees the original images those questions were associated with. Visual features are extracted via Faster R-CNN (36 region proposals per image) or I3D ResNeXt-101 (16 clip-level regions), and the T5-based transformer (12 encoder + 12 decoder blocks, 12 heads each) processes the unified image-text token sequence.

## Method

QUAD introduces a two-part stability loss that replaces the need for stored images. First, Question-only Replay (L_QR) pairs each current-task image with an object-matched question from the memory buffer and uses the previous task's model to generate soft pseudo-labels (full output distribution, no argmax), which the current model is trained to match via cross-entropy—this directly counteracts the out-of-answer-set problem. Second, Attention Consistency Distillation (L_ACD) applies cross-entropy on post-softmax self-attention maps across all heads and layers, aligning the current model's attention with the previous model's to preserve intra-modal (text-text, image-image) and inter-modal (text-image) focus without the rigidity of L1 or ReLU+L1 feature distillation. The total objective is L = (1−λ)·L_CE + λ·(L_QR + L_ACD) with λ=0.5, and the object-matched question selection strategy ensures replayed question-image pairs remain semantically coherent by prioritizing questions relevant to the current sub-task's visual categories.

## Results

On VQAv2 (standard test), QUAD achieves a Final Average Performance (AP) of 39.25% with 4.91% average forgetting, outperforming the best rehearsal-based baseline VQACL (37.46% AP, 6.96% forgetting) by 1.79% AP. On NExT-QA, QUAD reaches 31.70% AP with 2.91% forgetting, exceeding VQACL (30.86% AP, 4.12% forgetting) by 0.84% AP. In novel composition testing, QUAD attains 40.00% AP on VQAv2 and 33.21% AP on NExT-QA, with a minimal gap (<1%) between standard and novel settings, indicating strong compositional generalization. Notably, QUAD stores only questions (no images), yet surpasses all methods that store full image-question pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QUAD outperforms all six baselines on both datasets in both AP and forgetting. Against the strongest rehearsal-based competitor VQACL [95] (which stores both images and questions), QUAD gains +1.79% AP on VQAv2 (39.25 vs. 37.46) and +0.84% on NExT-QA (31.70 vs. 30.86), while also reducing forgetting from 6.96% to 4.91% (VQAv2) and 4.12% to 2.91% (NExT-QA). Compared to other rehearsal methods, QUAD leads ER by +2.26% AP on VQAv2 and +1.15% on NExT-QA, DER by +3.90% and +5.53%, and VS by +5.22% and +3.57%. The regularization-only baselines (EWC, MAS) are far behind, with EWC achieving only 15.77% AP on VQAv2 and MAS reaching 20.56%, confirming that some form of replay is essential in this multimodal continual setting. Critically, QUAD achieves these gains while storing only questions (no visual data), making it the only method in the comparison that is both privacy-preserving and state-of-the-art.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the VQACL benchmark [95], which established the continual VQA task with a hierarchical macro-task/sub-task structure, and on knowledge distillation-based continual learning methods (Learning without Forgetting [47], LwM [14]) that use a previous model's outputs as soft targets. It departs from standard rehearsal methods (ER, DER, VS, VQACL) that store full image-question pairs by introducing a constrained 'question-only replay' setting (VQACL-QR), positioning itself as an intermediate between memory-free regularization (EWC, MAS) and full exemplar-based replay. The attention consistency distillation component extends prior attention-based regularization for transformers [63] by replacing L1/ReLU+L1 penalties on raw scores with cross-entropy on softmax-normalized attention maps, preserving the probabilistic structure of attention distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in eliminating the need to store visual data, which addresses both memory overhead and privacy concerns (GDPR, sensitive imagery in healthcare/surveillance) that make full rehearsal infeasible in many real-world deployments. The finding that question-only replay can match or exceed full image+question storage challenges the assumption that visual exemplars are necessary for effective multimodal replay, though the margins are modest (1.79% AP on VQAv2, 0.84% on NExT-QA) and narrow further on visually complex video tasks. Open questions include whether this strategy generalizes to other multimodal continual learning tasks (e.g., video captioning, embodied QA), whether it scales to larger VLM backbones, and whether the object-matched question selection can be automated without ground-truth category labels.
