## Problem

Existing text-to-video alignment metrics (CLIPScore, VideoScore, BLIP-BLEU) produce only coarse-grained scores that fail to capture fine-grained semantic details, resulting in poor correlation with human judgment (Spearman's ρ ≈ 31 vs. ETVA's 58.5). Two specific challenges prevent direct transfer of text-to-image QG/QA evaluation to video: (1) complex multi-element prompts make vanilla in-context question generation produce overly intricate or unanswerable questions, and (2) video LLMs suffer from severe hallucination when questions require commonsense knowledge (e.g., microgravity physics) that humans access intuitively.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt and a corresponding generated video (from any DiT-based T2V model) as input. It first decomposes the prompt into a set of atomic binary yes/no verification questions (e.g., 'Is the cup made of glass?'), then answers each question by analyzing the video, and finally aggregates all binary answers into a single alignment score S ∈ [0,1] via simple averaging. The system is entirely zero-shot with no training: Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct handles question generation and commonsense knowledge retrieval, while Qwen2-VL-72B performs video understanding and multi-stage reasoning.

## Method

ETVA operates as a two-stage pipeline. In Question Generation, a three-agent system (Element Extractor → Graph Builder → Graph Traverser) parses the prompt into a hierarchical scene graph with entity, attribute, and relation nodes, then traverses the graph in dependency order to produce atomic yes/no questions that cover all semantic requirements without redundancy. In Question Answering, an auxiliary LLM first retrieves relevant commonsense knowledge (e.g., 'in microgravity, liquids form floating spheres'), which is then fed alongside the video and question into a multi-stage reasoning process in the video LLM: (1) Video Understanding — frame-by-frame visual description without text input, (2) General Reflection — cross-modal analysis combining observations with retrieved knowledge, and (3) Conclusion — a grounded yes/no answer with explicit visual-linguistic alignment checks. This brain-inspired architecture emulates how human annotators recall physics before inspecting video content, reducing hallucination by 26.2% over direct video LLM answering.

## Results

ETVA achieves a Spearman's ρ of 58.47 and Kendall's τ of 47.16 in correlation with human judgment on text-to-video alignment, substantially outperforming the best existing metric VideoScore (Spearman's ρ = 31.0, Kendall's τ = 23.7) by 27.5 points in Spearman's correlation. Across all 10 semantic categories (existence, action, material, spatial, number, shape, color, camera, physics, other), ETVA consistently leads, with the largest margins in shape (64.1/75.1) and material (55.5/66.1). On ETVA-Bench-105, Vidu-1.5 achieves the highest overall T2V alignment score (0.761) among all 15 evaluated models, while all models struggle most with camera control (max 0.474) and physics (max 0.600).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ETVA decisively outperforms all five baseline metric families across every semantic category. Against the strongest CLIP-based metric ViCLIPScore (19.4/25.9 overall), ETVA more than doubles the Spearman's ρ (58.5 vs. 25.9). Against the strongest MLLM-based metric VideoScore (23.7/31.0), ETVA gains +23.5 in Kendall's τ and +27.5 in Spearman's ρ, a gap the authors attribute to VideoScore's coarse-grained scoring failing to capture fine-grained details in color, camera, and physics. Caption-based metrics (BLIP-BLEU at 8.5/12.1, BLIP-ROUGE at 6.3/8.8) and CLIPScore (10.3/13.8) are far behind, with BLIP-ROUGE even showing negative correlation in the color category (-4.7/-5.2). UMTScore (17.6/23.5) sits in the middle of the CLIP-based group but still trails ETVA by over 29 points in Spearman's ρ. The consistent dominance of ETVA across all 10 categories—rather than selective wins—suggests the multi-agent QG plus knowledge-augmented reasoning paradigm captures alignment dimensions that embedding-similarity and single-score approaches fundamentally cannot.

## Relation to Prior Work

ETVA extends the question-generation-and-answering (QG/QA) evaluation paradigm pioneered for text-to-image alignment (TIFA, DreamSync) to the text-to-video setting, where it had not previously been applied due to the added complexity of temporal and physical reasoning. It departs from the dominant coarse-grained scoring approaches (CLIPScore, VideoScore, BLIP-BLEU) by decomposing alignment into atomic yes/no questions derived from scene-graph traversal, and introduces a knowledge-augmented multi-stage reasoning pipeline for the answering stage that explicitly injects commonsense physics via an auxiliary LLM before the video LLM reasons. The multi-agent scene-graph construction for question generation is a novel structural contribution not present in prior QG-based evaluators, which relied on flat in-context prompting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ETVA-Bench (2K prompts, 12K questions, 10 categories) and the ETVA metric together provide the first fine-grained, human-aligned evaluation framework for T2V text alignment, with a Spearman correlation (58.47) roughly double that of the best prior metric (VideoScore, 31.0). The systematic evaluation of 15 models reveals that even the strongest closed-source systems (Sora, Vidu-1.5) remain weak on physics simulation (≤0.600) and camera control (≤0.474), pointing to concrete open problems in spatiotemporal and physical reasoning for diffusion-transformer video generators. Open questions include whether the heavy reliance on 72B-parameter LLMs for both QG and QA limits practical deployment, whether the benchmark's 10-category taxonomy captures all meaningful alignment dimensions, and whether the approach generalizes to longer or more complex multi-shot prompts.
