## Problem

Identifying diverse protein compositions and conformations simultaneously from 3D cryo-ET subtomograms in a fully unsupervised manner remains an open problem. Existing content-transformation disentanglement methods require an explicit parametric form for the transformation (e.g., affine, diffeomorphic), which is unavailable for protein conformational changes, and standard self-supervised contrastive methods with geometric augmentation fail to separate conformation-like transformations from semantic content in shape-focused images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251451/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D cryo-ET subtomograms of size 32³ voxels at very low signal-to-noise ratio, with proteins in random orientations and shifts, as well as 2D RGB images (MNIST digits, LineMod objects) for validation. It produces two disentangled latent codes—a content code (c) encoding protein composition and a transformation code (z) encoding conformation—alongside reconstructed images. Downstream, GMM clustering on the c codes followed by RELION subtomogram averaging yields identified protein structures, while clustering on z codes recovers distinct conformational states.

## Method

DualContrast builds on a VAE with a CNN encoder and FCN decoder that jointly infers content (c) and transformation (z) latent codes, but its key novelty is a dual contrastive loss that structures both latent spaces simultaneously. For content, positive pairs are created by rotating the input (preserving identity) and negative pairs by batch permutation; for transformation, the rotated image serves as the negative pair, while positive pairs are generated by sampling a shared z from N(0,1) and decoding it with two different content codes from permuted batch members. This latent-space generation of transformation positives is the critical design choice, since conformational transformations cannot be explicitly parameterized in input space. The contrastive losses use mean absolute cosine distance (for positives) and mean absolute cosine similarity (for negatives), and the total objective is L = L_VAE + L_con(c) + L_con(z), trained end-to-end with small KL weights (≈0.01) to prevent one latent code from collapsing.

## Results

On the simulated protein subtomogram dataset, DualContrast achieves perfect content predictivity (D(c|c)=1.00) with SAP(c)=0.44, and strong transformation informativeness (D(z|z)=0.86) with SAP(z)=0.22, outperforming all baselines on the joint content-transformation disentanglement objective. On LineMod, DualContrast attains D(c|c)=0.95, SAP(c)=0.47, and S(z)=0.77, indicating the z code captures conformation-like viewpoint changes beyond simple rotation. For downstream protein identification, DualContrast achieves ARI=0.99 for composition clustering and average AUC-FSC=0.56 for refined structures, substantially exceeding Harmony (ARI=0.72, AUC-FSC=0.43) and RELION (ARI=0.75, AUC-FSC=0.40).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DualContrast outperforms all nine baselines on the joint content-transformation disentanglement objective. Among explicit-parameterization methods, SpatialVAE [3] and Harmony [51] are limited to in-plane rotation (S(z)=1.0 on LineMod) and fail to capture conformation-like transformations; on protein subtomograms, Harmony achieves high SAP(c)=0.94 but poor transformation informativeness (D(z|z)=0.52), while SpatialVAE scores D(c|c)=0.69. Among self-supervised baselines, SimCLR [6], SimSiam [8], MoCo [17], and Barlow Twins [55] all show near-zero SAP(z) (≤0.04) on protein subtomograms, indicating their z codes are uninformative of transformation. VITAE [47] achieves D(c|c)=0.92 on LineMod but SAP(c)=0.02, meaning content and transformation are not separated. For the downstream protein identification task (Table 2), DualContrast achieves ARI=0.99 for composition clustering versus 0.72 for Harmony and 0.75 for RELION [46], and average AUC-FSC=0.56 versus 0.43 (Harmony) and 0.40 (RELION). Hydra [34], the most recent composition-conformation method, fails entirely on 3D subtomograms (AUC-FSC avg=0.07, ARI=0.01), producing hybrid structures rather than correct protein identities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the explicit content-transformation disentanglement line (SpatialVAE, Harmony, VITAE) by removing the requirement for a parametric transformation form, which is unavailable for protein conformational changes. It also diverges from standard self-supervised contrastive learning (SimCLR, SimSiam, Barlow Twins, MoCo) by showing that augmentation-based SSL alone fails to disentangle conformation-like transformations, and by introducing a novel strategy that creates contrastive pairs for the transformation code in latent space rather than input space. The VAE-based architecture and the content-transformation disentanglement framework are inherited from prior work, but the dual contrastive loss design—simultaneously constraining both content and transformation codes with distinct positive/negative pair constructions—is the paper's core methodological contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that protein compositions and conformations can be simultaneously identified in a fully unsupervised manner from 3D cryo-ET subtomograms, a capability that prior methods (Harmony for composition, HEMNMA3D/TomoFlow for conformation, Hydra for 2D) addressed only separately or in lower dimensions. The key limitation is that all validation is on simulated data with known ground truth; the authors acknowledge that experimental cryo-ET data introduces additional complexities (noise, heterogeneity, missing density) that remain untested. Open questions include whether the implicit transformation parameterization generalizes to real subtomograms, how the method scales to larger numbers of compositions and conformations, and whether the rotation-based contrastive pair strategy remains sufficient for more complex conformational changes.
