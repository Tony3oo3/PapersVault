## Problem

Text-to-video diffusion models struggle to accurately align generated frames with text prompts, especially for spatial relationships and temporal dynamics. Existing RL-based alignment methods require differentiable reward functions trained specifically for video, which are scarce and computationally expensive to backpropagate through. This excludes powerful black-box LVLMs (e.g., GPT-4o) as reward models and limits scalability to larger video models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and operates on an existing video diffusion model (LaVie or VideoCrafter2) to produce a 16-frame video at 320×512 resolution. During the reverse denoising process, key frames are decoded from the latent, stitched into a labeled grid image, and sent to an LVLM API for alignment scoring. The output is the guided video latent decoded to pixel space, with no modification to the underlying diffusion model's parameters.

## Method

Free²Guide frames the reverse SDE as an entropy-regularized MDP and applies path integral control to approximate the optimal guidance term without computing reward gradients. At each denoising step, it samples n candidate latents via stochastic DDIM, decodes key frames, and selects the candidate whose stitched frame grid receives the highest LVLM alignment score (argmax over the reward). To adapt image-trained LVLMs for temporal understanding, key frames are arranged in a structured grid with index labels, and a system prompt with zero-shot chain-of-thought instructs the model to evaluate sequential frame order and score alignment on a 1–9 scale. The framework further supports ensembling multiple reward models (e.g., GPT-4o + CLIP or ImageReward) via weighted sum, normalized sum, or Borda-count consensus, enabling synergistic guidance without backpropagation.

## Results

On VBench text alignment, Free²Guide with GPT-4o improves the average score from 0.3722 to 0.3827 for LaVie (+2.8%) and from 0.4129 to 0.4425 for VideoCrafter2 (+7.2%). The largest gains appear in Multiple Objects (+25.6% for LaVie, +25.7% for VC2) and Spatial Relationship (+12.4% for VC2). The best ensemble configuration (LaVie+CLIP+GPT: 0.3868; VC2+ImageReward+GPT: 0.4483) further boosts overall consistency and human action scores. On T2V-CompBench Dynamic Attribution Binding, GPT guidance improves LaVie from 0.01242 to 0.01360 and VC2 from 0.00663 to 0.00770.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Free²Guide against two open-source T2V diffusion baselines: LaVie and VideoCrafter2. Free²Guide with GPT-4o alone consistently outperforms both baselines on average text alignment (LaVie: 0.3827 vs 0.3722; VC2: 0.4425 vs 0.4129), with the most dramatic gains in Multiple Objects (+25.6%/+25.7%) and Spatial Relationship for VC2 (+12.4%). Ensembling GPT with CLIP or ImageReward further improves results for VC2 (best: +IR+GPT at 0.4483, +8.6% over baseline) but yields mixed results for LaVie, where CLIP alone actually degrades Spatial Relationship by 12.1% and ImageReward alone drops it by 25.2%. The GPT ensemble partially recovers these spatial losses, suggesting LVLMs compensate for the limited spatial reasoning of CLIP. On general video quality (Table 3), Free²Guide maintains or slightly improves most metrics (Subject Consistency, Motion Smoothness, Aesthetic Quality) while Dynamic Degree sometimes decreases, though the IR+GPT ensemble for LaVie recovers it (0.5165 vs 0.4799 baseline). On T2V-CompBench Dynamic Attribution Binding, GPT guidance improves both baselines (LaVie: 0.01242→0.01360; VC2: 0.00663→0.00770), confirming better handling of temporal state changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

Free² Guide extends the path integral control framework from SCG (Huang et al., 2024), which used zeroth-order gradient approximation to guide image diffusion with non-differentiable rewards, into the video domain. It also borrows the DPS posterior-mean approximation (Chung et al., 2023) to avoid solving full PF-ODEs. The key departure from prior gradient-based alignment methods (DDPO, VADER, AlignProp) is the elimination of differentiability requirements, enabling black-box LVLM APIs as reward models. The frame-stitching technique for adapting image-trained LVLMs to temporal assessment is a novel practical contribution not present in prior video alignment work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to plug in arbitrary non-differentiable reward models (including proprietary LVLM APIs) without any training or gradient computation is practically significant, as it removes the need for video-specific reward model training and large-scale video-text datasets. However, the approach is currently limited to short (16-frame, 320×512) videos, applies guidance only in the first 5 of 50 sampling steps, and depends on API access to GPT-4o. Open questions include whether the method scales to longer/higher-resolution videos, whether open-source LVLMs can substitute for GPT-4o, and whether the modest per-metric gains (typically 1–3%) compound meaningfully at scale. The trade-off observed in Dynamic Degree (often decreasing) suggests that reward-guided alignment may suppress motion diversity, an unresolved tension.
