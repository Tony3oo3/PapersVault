## Problem

The streaming complexity of underdetermined ℓp linear regression—minimizing ‖x‖p subject to Ax=b with n≪d—was essentially unexplored. While p=2 admits a simple O(n²)-space algorithm via maintaining AAᵀ, no streaming algorithms or space lower bounds were known for general p∈(1,∞], and it was unclear whether sublinear-in-d space suffices for either estimating the optimal cost or outputting an approximate solution vector.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives d+1 updates in a single column-arrival stream: each update is either a column aʲ of A paired with its weight cⱼ, or the vector b, in arbitrary order. For cost estimation, the output is a diagonal sparsifier matrix S with Õ(ε⁻²n) nonzeros (for p≥2) such that the ℓp flow cost on the sparsified instance approximates the original within (1±ε). For solution output, the algorithm stores O(sd/β^q) columns and produces a sparse vector x̂ supported on those columns satisfying Ax̂=b with ‖x̂‖p ≤ β·OPT (or n^{1/2}·OPT for p=1). In the graph setting, the stream is an edge-insertion sequence and the sparsifier is a weighted subgraph.

## Method

For cost estimation, the key insight is a duality reduction: constructing a sparse diagonal S such that ‖SCAᵀy‖q = (1±ε)‖CAᵀy‖q for all y (an ℓq subspace embedding of the column space of Aᵀ) yields a (1±ε)-approximate ℓp flow sparsifier via strong duality. This embedding is built using online ℓp Lewis weight sampling, which selects columns irrevocably in one pass with a polylogarithmic overhead in the online condition number κ^OL. For solution output, the method uses streaming well-conditioned spanning subsets—constructed via an online Löwner-John ellipsoid algorithm—to identify O(n log d) columns that represent all others with ℓ2-bounded coefficients, then applies a blocking technique over O(d/β^q) blocks to trade space for distortion, yielding the first sublinear-space algorithms that output an actual solution vector for p>1.

## Results

For cost estimation, the paper achieves Õ(ε⁻²n) columns (i.e., Õ(ε⁻²n²) bits) for p∈[2,∞], matching the Ω(n²) lower bound for p=2, and Õ(ε⁻²n^{q/2}) columns for p∈(1,2) where q is the Hölder conjugate. For p=1, no poly(n)-space algorithm can estimate the cost to an o(√n) factor, and for p=0, Ω(d) space is required even for a 2-approximation with n=2. For outputting a solution vector, the paper gives the first sublinear-space upper bounds: a β-approximation using poly(n)·Õ(d/β^q) space for p>1, and a √n-approximation using poly(n, log d) space for p=1, while proving Ω(d) space is necessary for (1+ε)-approximations and Ω̃(d/β^{2q}) for β-approximations when p>1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; the 'comparisons' are between upper and lower bounds and against prior theoretical results. The main algorithmic contribution (Theorem 1) generalizes the p=2 spectral sparsification results of Kelner & Levin (2013), Kapralov et al. (2014), and Cohen et al. (2016) to all p∈(1,∞], using online ℓ_p Lewis weight sampling of Woodruff & Yasuda (2023a) as the core subroutine. The p=2 case recovers the known Õ(n²) space bound, and the paper proves this is tight via a new Ω(n²) lower bound (Theorem 2), settling the streaming complexity for least-squares regression. For the harder problem of outputting a solution vector, the paper provides the first sublinear-space upper bounds (Theorem 7), building on the online Löwner–John ellipsoid algorithm of Woodruff & Yasuda (2022) to construct well-conditioned spanning subsets in a streaming fashion. The gap between the upper bound Õ(d/β^q) and lower bound Ω̃(d/β^{2q}) for solution output remains open (Question 1.4), and the p=1 case is notably easier, achieving √n distortion in poly(n, log d) space, circumventing the Ω(d) lower bound that applies for p>1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper initiates the unified study of one-pass streaming algorithms for underdetermined ℓ_p regression, generalizing the p=2 spectral sparsification results of Kelner & Levin (2013), Kapralov et al. (2014), and Cohen et al. (2016) to all p ∈ (1,∞]. The core algorithmic machinery draws on online ℓ_p Lewis weight sampling (Woodruff & Yasuda, 2023a) for cost estimation and the online Löwner–John ellipsoid algorithm (Woodruff & Yasuda, 2022) for constructing well-conditioned spanning subsets used in solution output. While individual special cases (transshipment for p=1, electrical flows for p=2, max flow for p=∞) had prior streaming results, no prior work treated the full ℓ_p spectrum or the harder problem of outputting an approximate solution vector in sublinear space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a clean complexity landscape: for p=2, O(n²) space is both sufficient and necessary for cost estimation, while for p=1, no poly(n)-space algorithm can even estimate the cost to an o(√n) factor—a dramatic breakdown of the p=2 picture. The lower bound showing Ω(d) space is required for (1+ε)-approximate solution output for any p>1 rules out a natural sublinear-space approach, and the gap between the upper bound poly(n)·Õ(d/β^q) and lower bound Õ(d/β^{2q}) for β-approximate solutions (Question 1.4) is a major open problem. The separation between undirected and directed graph streaming complexity (semi-streaming for undirected max flow vs. near-quadratic lower bounds for directed reachability) has broader implications for graph streaming theory.
