## Problem

Existing few-shot OOD detection methods built on vision-language models optimize only global prompts, which fail when OOD samples are globally similar to known classes but differ in subtle local regions (e.g., a cat vs. tiger differing only in the forehead). No prior work explicitly exploits regional outlier knowledge through dedicated local prompts, leaving a gap in detecting hard OOD cases that require fine-grained spatial discrimination.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253126/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (processed by CLIP-Base/16 into 16×16 patches) and produces a binary OOD/ID decision via a Regional-MCM score thresholded at γ, along with an ID class label from a local-aware score that multiplies global cosine similarity by the mean of the top-k local patch similarities. During training, each image is randomly cropped m=24 times to generate hard negative samples; the regional number k is set to 50 for training and 10 for inference, controlling how many top-scoring patches contribute to the score.

## Method

Local-Prompt introduces a coarse-to-fine tuning paradigm that freezes hand-crafted global prompts and trains only learnable local prompts (one per class plus N_neg=300 local negative prompts) via two components. First, global prompt guided negative augmentation randomly crops each training image and uses frozen global prompts to select the least-similar crop as a hard negative, simulating local outlier regions without real OOD data. Second, local prompt enhanced regional regularization optimizes local prompts with a contrastive loss (pulling ID-related patches toward class prompts and pushing outlier patches toward negative prompts, both using top-k regional selection) plus a diversity regularization on the negative prompts. At inference, the proposed Regional-MCM score sums the global MCM score with the mean of the top-k local softmax scores (including negative prompts in the denominator), and the approach is orthogonal to global prompt optimization, allowing seamless combination with trained global prompts like LoCoOp for further gains.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k with 4-shot tuning, Local-Prompt achieves an average FPR95 of 27.66% and AUROC of 93.53%, outperforming CoOp (33.82% FPR95) and LoCoOp (31.46% FPR95) by 6.16% and 3.80% respectively. When extended with trained global prompts (Local-Prompt+LoCoOp), the 4-shot result reaches 26.29% FPR95 and 94.03% AUROC, surpassing the 16-shot LoCoOp result (28.66% FPR95) by 2.37%. On the near-OOD task (ImageNet-10 vs. ImageNet-20), Local-Prompt achieves 5.05% average FPR95, substantially outperforming MCM (8.75%), GL-MCM (9.55%), and LoCoOp (11.60%). ID classification accuracy also improves, reaching 70.52% (4-shot) and 74.24% (16-shot) versus LoCoOp's 69.50% and 71.86%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary few-shot baseline CoOp, Local-Prompt reduces average FPR95 by 6.16% in 4-shot (33.82→27.66) and 12.15% in 16-shot (30.67→24.52 with LoCoOp extension), while improving AUROC by 2.55% and 2.66% respectively. Against the closest competitor LoCoOp, Local-Prompt wins by 3.80% FPR95 in 4-shot and, when combined with LoCoOp's trained global prompts, achieves a 5.17% FPR95 reduction over LoCoOp's own 16-shot result (28.66→24.52), demonstrating that local prompt enhancement is orthogonal to and complementary with global prompt optimization. On the near-OOD task (semantically similar classes), Local-Prompt achieves 5.05% average FPR95 versus LoCoOp's 11.60%, a 5.55% gap that the authors attribute to local prompts capturing subtle regional differences that global prompts miss. Against NegLabel, which leverages real outlier samples from external corpora, Local-Prompt (without any outlier data) achieves a lower average FPR95 (24.52 vs. 25.40) and higher AUROC (94.48 vs. 94.21), while also showing better balance across OOD datasets. Against ID-like (CVPR 2024), Local-Prompt+LoCoOp achieves 26.29% FPR95 versus 30.07%, a 3.78% improvement, though ID-like performs better on iNaturalist specifically (8.98 vs. 12.81 FPR95), suggesting the methods excel on different OOD types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CLIP-based OOD detection line (MCM, GL-MCM) and few-shot prompt learning for vision-language models (CoOp, LoCoOp), but departs from both by decomposing the prompt space into frozen global prompts and trainable local prompts. Prior methods like GL-MCM and LoCoOp apply the same prompt to both global and local features, which the authors argue conflates overall similarity with regional outlier signals. The key conceptual departure is treating local prompts as a separate, orthogonal optimization target that learns regional-related outlier knowledge through negative augmentation and contrastive regularization, rather than jointly optimizing a single prompt for all feature levels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The extensibility property—seamlessly combining local prompts with any trained global prompt at inference—makes this a practical drop-in enhancement for existing CLIP-based OOD pipelines, and the 4-shot results surpassing 16-shot baselines suggest efficient local information exploitation. However, the method is limited to classification, requires additional training time for negative augmentation, and the authors acknowledge that joint global-local prompt training remains unexplored. Open questions include whether the regional regularization generalizes to dense prediction tasks (segmentation, detection) and whether the hand-crafted global prompt assumption holds when the ID class space is very large or open-ended.
