## Problem

Existing scalable manipulation policies either perform naive behavior cloning from large-scale robotic data (action-centric) or use decoupled two-stage pipelines where a pre-trained visual world model sets goals and a separate inverse dynamics policy executes actions (vision-centric). Neither approach closes the loop between vision and action in an end-to-end manner, leaving the rich temporal and visual information in robot demonstrations underutilized. The paper asks whether unifying visual prediction and action execution in a single end-to-end model can yield a more scalable and generalizable manipulation policy.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a history of m timesteps (7 for LIBERO/real-world, 10 for CALVIN), each containing two 224×224 RGB images (eye-on-hand and eye-on-base), a 7-dimensional robot state (6D end-effector pose + binary gripper), and a language instruction. It outputs a 3-step action chunk (7D per step: 6D arm displacement + 1D gripper) and a predicted future RGB image at timestep t+n. During pre-training, missing language annotations are handled by substituting the future robot state as the goal signal, and random exploration behaviors are excluded from the [INV]/[FRS] attention to prevent overfitting to noise.

## Method

Seer is a GPT-2-style transformer (24 layers, 384 hidden dim) that processes tokenized language (CLIP), images (MAE ViT + Perceiver Resampler), and robot states (MLP) with two special readout tokens per timestep: [FRS] for conditional visual foresight and [INV] for inverse dynamics prediction. The critical architectural choice is a unidirectional attention mask that allows [INV] to attend to [FRS] but not vice versa, so the action token integrates both past observations and the predicted future visual state within the multi-layer network. Training jointly optimizes an image reconstruction loss (MSE, following MAE) and an action prediction loss (Smooth-L1 for arm + BCE for gripper) with a weighted sum (α=0.5), making the predicted future latent directly condition the action output in a differentiable, end-to-end fashion. The model is pre-trained on large-scale robot datasets (DROID with 76K trajectories, LIBERO-90, or CALVIN play data) and fine-tuned on downstream tasks with as few as 100 demonstrations, with the visual and text encoders kept frozen (251M parameters) while 65M (or 315M for Seer-Large) backbone parameters are trainable.

## Results

On LIBERO-LONG (10 long-horizon tasks), Seer achieves 87.7% average success rate after pre-training, a 9-point gain over its scratch version (78.7%) and 10.4 points over the best baseline MPI (77.3%). On CALVIN ABC-D (34 tasks, zero-shot evaluation in Env D), Seer-Large sets a new state-of-the-art with an average task completion length of 4.28, surpassing CLOVER (3.53) by 0.75. In real-world experiments across six tasks, pre-training on DROID improves average success rate from 60.0% to 78.4% and accumulated score from 32.8 to 39.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Seer outperforms all baselines on both simulation benchmarks. On CALVIN ABC-D, Seer-Large (4.28 Avg. Len.) surpasses the closest two-stage PIDM baseline CLOVER (3.53) by 0.75, the video-generative method GR-1 (3.06) by 1.22, and the VLM-based Roboflamingo (2.47) by 1.81. On LIBERO-LONG, Seer (87.7%) beats MPI (77.3%), MVP (68.2%), OpenVLA (54.0%), and MTACT (41.0%), with the gap over OpenVLA being particularly notable given Seer uses only 65M trainable parameters versus OpenVLA's 7B. In real-world tasks, Seer (78.4% SR) dominates MVP (55.0%), MPI (48.4%), and OpenVLA (16.7%), with OpenVLA's poor performance attributed to overfitting from its large 3B fine-tuned model and reliance on a single camera view. The paper also shows that even Seer trained from scratch (no pre-training) already matches or exceeds several baselines, indicating the architectural design itself is effective independent of the pre-training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work in scalable robotic manipulation: action-centric behavior cloning (RT-1, Octo, OpenVLA) and vision-centric pre-training (MVP, MPI, CLOVER, Susie). Prior PIDM approaches (Susie, CLOVER) decouple visual prediction and inverse dynamics into a two-stage pipeline, where a generative model predicts sub-goals and a separate policy maps them to actions. Seer's key departure is making this loop end-to-end within a single GPT-2-style transformer, using a unidirectional attention mask that lets the action token attend to the foresight token, so both modules are jointly optimized. The visual reconstruction component directly adopts MAE's pixel-level MSE loss and ViT decoder, while the multi-modal tokenization leverages CLIP text embeddings and a Perceiver Resampler for image compression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The end-to-end PIDM paradigm offers a clean, scalable recipe for robot learning that simultaneously exploits visual, temporal, and action information from large-scale datasets, and the strong data-efficiency results (187% relative improvement with only 10% fine-tuning data) make it practically attractive for settings where collecting robot demonstrations is expensive. However, the cross-embodiment experiments reveal that pre-training on OXE (a multi-robot dataset) provides only marginal gains and even degrades high-precision tasks, suggesting that embodiment-specific action distributions and wrist-camera information remain critical bottlenecks. Open questions include whether the end-to-end PIDM formulation scales to more diverse embodiments, longer horizons, and contact-rich tasks beyond the six evaluated here.
