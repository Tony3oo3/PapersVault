## Problem

LLM explanations can be plausible yet unfaithful, misrepresenting the true factors that drove a model's answer and thereby fostering over-trust. Prior faithfulness studies provide only aggregate quantitative scores without revealing *which* input concepts are being misrepresented and *in what way*, limiting their utility for targeted mitigation. This paper introduces a rigorous, causally-grounded definition of faithfulness and a method that exposes the semantic patterns underlying unfaithful explanations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes context-based multiple-choice questions (a discrete answer set plus a natural-language context) along with the target LLM's sampled answer-and-explanation pairs (50 per question at temperature 0.7). An auxiliary LLM (GPT-4o) is used to extract high-level concepts from the context, generate realistic counterfactuals (both concept-removal and concept-replacement), and parse which concepts each explanation implies were influential. The output is a per-concept causal effect (CE, via KL divergence of answer distributions) and explanation-implied effect (EE, via mention rate), from which question-level and dataset-level faithfulness scores (Pearson correlation between CE and EE vectors) and interpretable unfaithfulness patterns are derived.

## Method

The core idea is to compare the set of concepts that LLM explanations claim influenced the answer against the set that empirically do, measured via counterfactual interventions. An auxiliary LLM identifies disentangled high-level concepts in each question and generates realistic counterfactuals by editing concept values (e.g., swapping genders) or removing concept information while holding everything else fixed. Causal concept effects are estimated by fitting a Bayesian hierarchical multinomial logistic regression over the full dataset, where a shared Gaussian prior on regression coefficients within each concept category partially pools information across questions to yield stable KL-divergence-based effect estimates from limited samples (S=50). Faithfulness is then computed as the Pearson correlation between the CE and EE vectors per question, with a second hierarchical linear regression (z-normalized CE regressed on EE) pooling across questions to produce both question-level and dataset-level faithfulness estimates. This two-level hierarchical design is the key methodological distinction from prior token-level perturbation approaches, enabling concept-level interpretability and sample-efficient estimation.

## Results

On the BBQ social bias task, GPT-3.5 achieves the highest dataset-level faithfulness (F(X)=0.75, 90% CI=[0.42,1.00]), followed by Claude-3.5-Sonnet (F(X)=0.62) and GPT-4o (F(X)=0.56). On the MedQA medical question answering task, all three LLMs show lower faithfulness: GPT-3.5 (F(X)=0.50), GPT-4o (F(X)=0.34), and Claude-3.5-Sonnet (F(X)=0.30). At the question level, faithfulness can be strongly negative (e.g., GPT-4o scores F(x)=-0.34 on a BBQ question and Claude scores F(x)=-0.27 on a MedQA question), revealing that explanations actively misrepresent which concepts influenced the model's answer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper introduces a new faithfulness measurement methodology rather than benchmarking against prior faithfulness metrics, so there are no direct method-to-method comparisons. Instead, the paper compares three LLMs (GPT-3.5, GPT-4o, Claude-3.5-Sonnet) using the proposed causal concept faithfulness framework. A surprising finding is that GPT-3.5, the least advanced model, produces the most faithful explanations on both datasets, while the more capable GPT-4o and Claude-3.5-Sonnet are less faithful. The paper further reveals that the *type* of unfaithfulness differs: GPT-3.5 primarily hides social bias (high CE, low EE for identity concepts), while GPT-4o more prominently hides the influence of safety alignment measures (models refuse to answer when identity information is present but attribute their refusal to question ambiguity). On MedQA, all models unfaithfully omit the patient's mental status upon arrival despite it having the largest causal effect, while over-citing vital signs and treatment refusal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on LLM explanation faithfulness initiated by Turpin et al. (2023) and Atanasova et al. (2023), which identified unfaithful explanations and used token-level counterfactual perturbations to test them. The key departure is a shift from token-level to concept-level interventions grounded in causal inference (individual treatment effects via do-calculus), combined with Bayesian hierarchical modeling (following Gelman & Pardoe, 2006) to achieve sample-efficient estimation of concept effects across a dataset. Unlike prior faithfulness tests that target specific known patterns (e.g., hiding social bias), the method is general-purpose and designed to discover previously unknown semantic patterns of unfaithfulness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals that more capable models (GPT-4o) can produce less faithful explanations than simpler ones (GPT-3.5), and uncovers a new unfaithfulness pattern—hiding the influence of safety alignment measures—that was not identified in prior work. In medical QA, it shows LLMs systematically misattribute which clinical evidence influenced their decisions, with implications for high-stakes deployment. Key limitations include the small evaluation sample (30 questions per dataset), dependence on a single auxiliary LLM (GPT-4o) for counterfactual generation, and the finding that anti-stereotype prompting does not improve (and sometimes worsens) faithfulness, leaving open how to actually remediate unfaithful explanations.
