## Problem

Existing LLM-based reasoning methods (prompting, semantic parsing, inference-rule emulation) cannot simultaneously leverage the LLM's internal commonsense knowledge and an explicit axiomatic knowledge base, lack a mechanism to reliably repair erroneous inference steps, and are restricted to small KBs that fit within the LLM context window. This paper addresses the gap of scalable, verifiable, and repairable logical commonsense reasoning over large, potentially incomplete knowledge bases with arbitrary predicate arities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251497/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a natural language query, a knowledge base of axioms and ground facts expressed in clausal form with arbitrary predicate arities (up to ~32k rules), and a small NLI entailment model (BART-large 406M or RoBERTa-large 365M tuned on MNLI) that scores predicate entailment probabilities. It outputs a ranked set of proofs, each annotated with a priority tuple (type consistency score, predicate entailment score, proof length score), enabling both binary True/False answers and multi-choice ranking. Each proof is a transparent sequence of atomic resolution steps with explicit unifications, making the reasoning process fully inspectable and repairable.

## Method

LLM-TH extends classical theory resolution by using a small NLI model as the 'theory' that identifies entailment between natural language predicates, relaxing the standard resolution requirement for identical complementary literals. Two efficiency mechanisms make it scalable to tens of thousands of rules: (1) typed inference, where variables carry ontological types and resolution is restricted to type-consistent unifications, pruning semantically invalid branches early; and (2) hyperresolution, which combines multiple resolution steps into a single inference. A three-tier priority queue (type consistency → predicate entailment → proof length) orders the backward-chaining search from the negated query. A repair mechanism with theoretical guarantees allows inserting intended axioms (priority 1) to fix missed inferences and forbidden axioms (priority 0) to suppress incorrect LLM entailments, provably correcting errors without re-running the full search.

## Results

On complete knowledge bases, LLM-TH (using only a BART 406M NLI model) achieves 100% accuracy and 100% reasoning score (RS) on both the multi-domain deductive reasoning (32k rules) and geographical QA (12k rules) datasets, while the best LLM baselines (Llama3-70B, GPT-3.5, Gemini-1.5-Flash, Mixtral-46.7B) achieve at most 80% and 91% accuracy respectively. On the preference reasoning task (Recipe-MPR), LLM-TH achieves 84% accuracy with 0.94 micro-RS, slightly below Llama3-70B few-shot CoT (91% accuracy) but with substantially higher reasoning faithfulness. On incomplete KBs (one axiom ablated), LLM-TH maintains 97% and 95% accuracy on deductive reasoning and geographical QA, outperforming the best RAG-based baseline (Llama3-70B) by 20 and 29 percentage points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLM-TH decisively outperforms all LLM-based baselines on the two large-KB tasks: on deductive reasoning (32k rules), LLM-TH achieves 100% accuracy vs. 80% for Llama3-70B zero-shot CoT and 86% for Gemini-1.5-Flash RAG+few-shot CoT; on geographical QA (12k rules), LLM-TH reaches 100% vs. 91% for Llama3-70B RAG+few-shot CoT. On preference reasoning (small implicit KB), LLM-TH (84%) is slightly below Llama3-70B few-shot CoT (91%) but exceeds Mixtral-46.7B (79%) and matches GPT-3.5/Gemini zero-shot CoT, while achieving the highest reasoning score (0.94 micro-RS vs. 0.84 for Llama3). Against semantic parsing baselines (Logic-LM, LINC), LLM-TH is dramatically superior: Prover9-based methods achieve only 5% accuracy on geographical QA and crash entirely on the 32k-rule deductive reasoning KB. On incomplete KBs, LLM-TH (97%/95%) outperforms the strongest RAG baseline (Llama3-70B at 77%/66%) by 20–29 points, demonstrating that theory resolution effectively compensates for missing axioms using the NLI model's commonsense knowledge. The reasoning score (RS) metric further highlights LLM-TH's advantage: it achieves perfect 1.00 RS on both large-KB tasks, while all LLM baselines score between 0.43 and 0.93, reflecting their hallucinated or incorrect intermediate steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends classical theory resolution (Stickel, 1985; Baumgartner, 1992) by replacing the specialized theorem prover with an LLM-based NLI entailment model, enabling resolution over natural language predicates without requiring explicit symbolic translation. It departs from prior LLM reasoning approaches—CoT prompting, RAG, semantic parsing (Logic-LM, LINC), and LLM-emulated inference rules (LAMBADA, SYMBA)—by restricting the LLM's role to providing entailment judgments between predicates while the logical structure is handled by a typed hyperresolution search. The combination of theory resolution with typed inference and hyperresolution for efficiency over large KBs with arbitrary predicate arities is novel; prior LLM-based logical reasoning work was limited to small KBs fitting within context windows and often restricted to unary predicates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a critical gap in deploying LLMs for high-stakes reasoning: it provides verifiable, repairable inference with theoretical guarantees, and scales to KBs with tens of thousands of rules—far beyond what prior LLM-based methods handle. The finding that a 406M-parameter NLI model within this structured framework matches or exceeds 70B+ parameter LLMs on logical reasoning tasks challenges the assumption that reasoning quality scales monotonically with model size. Key limitations include the requirement for KBs in clausal form with type annotations, and the NLI model's inability to perform multi-step entailment reasoning (requiring larger LLMs with CoT for highly incomplete KBs). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to KBs without clean type hierarchies and how it performs on truly open-ended commonsense reasoning beyond structured predicate logic.
