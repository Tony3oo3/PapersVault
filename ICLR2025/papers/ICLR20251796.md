## Problem

Existing video anomaly detection methods rely on global patterns with redundant visual details that are inconsistent across data variations such as occlusions, viewpoint changes, and low resolutions, limiting their ability to generalize to novel, unseen anomalies. The paper asks whether identifying compact, semantically meaningful local patterns (e.g., body joints) that remain consistent across domains can yield representations that generalize to previously unseen anomalous events.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251796/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Input video frames are first segmented into per-object bounding box regions via an object detector (YOLOv7 or Qwen-VL-7B) with 50% box expansion, and each region is processed independently through the pipeline. The method is fully unsupervised: the reconstruction module is trained exclusively on normal videos from each benchmark. Output is a per-region anomaly score computed as the reconstruction error of a 7-layer fully-connected network that jointly reconstructs a 768-dim spatial local pattern vector and an 8-bin directional motion histogram.

## Method

The framework identifies spatial local patterns in two stages: Stage 1 (ITAM) uses the frozen Q-Former image transformer from BLIP-2 to extract 32 text-aligned image tokens from ViT-L/14 features; Stage 2 (CMAM) generates a caption via a Temporal Sentence Generation Module (TSGM) and uses the caption's [CLS] embedding as a query to weightedly sum image tokens by cosine similarity, yielding a compact cross-modal representation. TSGM incorporates a State Machine Module (SMM) built on Hippo-style Legendre-polynomial state machines that determines whether events described in earlier high-resolution captions persist in later low-resolution frames, enabling coherent multi-frame captioning. Temporal dynamics are captured by encoding frames as HEVC video and extracting motion vectors quantized into an 8-directional histogram. A 7-layer reconstruction network trained on normal data jointly reconstructs the concatenated spatial and temporal features, with reconstruction error serving as the anomaly score.

## Results

On the five primary benchmarks (ShanghaiTech, CUHK Avenue, Ubnormal, UCSD Ped2, NWPU Campus), the proposed method achieves micro-AUC of 88.9%, 94.5%, 81.5%, 99.1%, and 71.6% respectively, setting new state-of-the-art on ShanghaiTech, Ubnormal, and NWPU Campus. On UCF-Crime (micro-AUC) and XD-Violence (AP), the method scores 88.83% and 86.96%, outperforming all prior baselines on UCF-Crime but trailing Chen et al. (2024) by 1.25 AP points on XD-Violence. The method also runs at 83.95 ms/frame, roughly 8× faster than the LLM-based AnomalyRuler (697.35 ms/frame) while achieving higher AUC on both ShanghaiTech and Avenue.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms the strongest recent baselines on most benchmarks. On ShanghaiTech micro-AUC, it leads Zhang et al. (2024) by 1.4 points (88.9 vs 87.5) and Micorek et al. (2024) by 2.2 points (88.9 vs 86.7), though it trails Zhang et al. by 0.3 on macro-AUC (92.7 vs 93.0). On Ubnormal, the improvement is most dramatic: 81.5 micro-AUC vs 72.8 for Micorek et al. (2024) and 71.9 for AnomalyRuler (Yang et al., 2024a), a gap of 8.7 and 9.6 points respectively. On NWPU Campus, the method achieves 71.6 micro-AUC, surpassing Zhang et al. (2024) by 1.5 points. On CUHK Avenue, the method is competitive but not dominant: it matches Micorek et al. on micro-AUC (94.5) and trails Reiss & Hoshen (2022) on macro-AUC (94.9 vs 96.2). On UCF-Crime, the method achieves 88.83 micro-AUC, beating the best prior result (Wu et al., 2024b at 88.02), but on XD-Violence it scores 86.96 AP, slightly below Chen et al. (2024) at 88.21 AP. Overall, the local-pattern framework delivers consistent gains across human-centric benchmarks while remaining competitive on non-human object detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on BLIP-2's Q-Former, repurposing its image transformer as a frozen feature extractor (ITAM) that identifies text-informative local regions rather than producing holistic image representations. The State Machine Module (SMM) adapts Hippo's Legendre polynomial projection framework from sequence modeling to a binary yes/no prediction task for temporal caption coherence. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant VAD paradigms—reconstruction of global features, prediction-based temporal modeling, and MIL-based segment selection—by arguing that semantically localized patterns (e.g., body joints) extracted through image-text alignment are more consistent across visual data variations and thus generalize better to novel anomalies than global representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine limitation in VAD: most methods struggle to generalize to anomalies unseen during training because global features are inconsistent across visual data variations. However, the framework's heavy reliance on an external object detector (YOLOv7 or Qwen-VL) to crop regions before processing is a significant practical constraint that limits scalability to complex scenes with many objects, a limitation the authors acknowledge. The use of HEVC motion vectors as a temporal signal is an interesting but somewhat ad hoc choice that may not capture all relevant dynamics. Open questions include whether the two-stage local pattern extraction can be made end-to-end without object detection, and whether the modest gains from SMM (≈1 AUC point) justify its architectural complexity.
