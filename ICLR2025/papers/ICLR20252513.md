## Problem

Existing counterfactual explanation (CE) methods that incorporate plausibility (data likelihood) either cannot handle categorical features, provide only approximate likelihood bounds (e.g., VAEs), or produce CEs that are distant and non-sparse from the factual. No prior method simultaneously optimizes plausibility, similarity, sparsity, and actionability within a single globally optimal formulation, leaving a gap between 'plausible' and 'realizable' explanations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252513/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a factual tabular sample with mixed feature types (continuous, categorical, ordinal, binary), a fixed trained neural network classifier, a fixed trained Sum-Product Network fitted on the training data, and user-specified data constraints (immutable features, monotonicity directions, causal implications). It outputs a counterfactual explanation as a modified input vector guaranteed to be valid (different classification) and actionable, plus a set of up to M closest counterfactuals from which the most likely is selected. All continuous values are normalized to [0,1], and the mixed polytope encoding (Russell, 2019) unifies categorical one-hot and continuous variables into a single MIO-compatible representation.

## Method

The core contribution is a Mixed-Integer Optimization (MIO) formulation of a trained Sum-Product Network's log-likelihood, embedded within a larger MIO that also encodes the neural network classifier (via OMLT big-M linearization of ReLUs), validity, MAD-weighted L1 similarity, sparsity, and actionability constraints. In log-space, SPN product nodes reduce to linear sums of child outputs, while sum nodes are approximated by the max of weighted child log-outputs, linearized via binary slack indicators and a big-M constant, yielding a lower bound with error at most log(number of predecessors). The SPN likelihood enters the objective with a tunable weight α or as a hard lower-bound constraint δ^SPN, allowing the user to trade off plausibility against proximity. This formulation is novel in combining tractable probabilistic models with MIO, and the number of variables and constraints scales with the SPN size rather than the dataset size, unlike LOF-based approaches.

## Results

On all three datasets (GMSC, Adult, Credit), LiCE (median) achieves the best plausibility (NLL) at 18.3±2.2, 12.9±1.0, and 29.9±3.1 respectively, while LiCE (optimize) and MIO(+spn) achieve the best similarity (‖·‖₁,ₘₐₐ) at ~5.6–5.9 and sparsity at ~2.1–2.6 changed features. All baseline methods show substantially worse similarity (12.9–27.7) and sparsity (2.8–10.8). The MIO approximation of the SPN has a worst-case error of only 0.53 (2.92%) on average, confirming the formulation's fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LiCE dominates all five baselines on similarity and sparsity across every dataset. On GMSC, LiCE (optimize) achieves a similarity of 5.9 vs. 12.9 (PROPLACE, the best baseline) and 27.3 (DiCE), with sparsity of 2.6 vs. 6.4 and 6.5 respectively. On Credit, the gap is even larger: LiCE (optimize) reaches 4.4 similarity and 2.3 sparsity, while the best baseline (C-CHVAE) achieves 12.9 and 6.6. On plausibility (NLL), LiCE (median) is the clear winner (18.3 on GMSC, 12.9 on Adult, 29.9 on Credit), outperforming the next-best methods by 7–10 NLL points. C-CHVAE is the strongest baseline on similarity for Adult (8.2) but still trails LiCE by ~3 points. Notably, merely adding SPN-based post-hoc selection to DiCE or VAE does not close the gap, confirming that integrating plausibility directly into the MIO objective is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of tractable probabilistic modeling and mixed-integer optimization for counterfactual explanation generation. It builds on Poon & Domingos's Sum-Product Networks as a likelihood estimator, Russell's mixed polytope formulation for encoding heterogeneous (categorical + continuous) features in MIO, and the DACE line of work that first embedded plausibility into MIO-based CE search. The key departure is formulating a trained SPN directly as MIO constraints, replacing dataset-size-dependent plausibility proxies (1-LOF in DACE, KDE in FACE, convex hulls in PROPLACE) with a model-size-dependent likelihood term that naturally handles categorical features and provides globally optimal solutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MIO formulation of an SPN is of independent interest beyond CE generation, enabling any MIO-based pipeline to incorporate tractable probabilistic likelihood as a constraint or objective. For the CE community, LiCE demonstrates that plausibility, similarity, and sparsity can be jointly optimized with certified global optimality, addressing a gap where prior methods (VAE-based, GMM-based) either sacrifice optimality guarantees or cannot handle mixed data types. Key limitations include the NP-hardness of MIO (2-minute time limits in experiments), the approximation error in sum-node log-sum-exp linearization, and the reliance on SPN quality for plausibility. Open questions include whether approximation algorithms or tighter big-M constants can improve scalability, and whether other probabilistic circuit architectures (e.g., Trapp et al.'s Bayesian SPNs) yield better plausibility estimates.
