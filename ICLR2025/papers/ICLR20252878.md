## Problem

High-performing LLM web agents depend on expensive proprietary APIs, while open LLMs lack the decision-making capability for multi-step web tasks. Existing training approaches (imitation learning, fixed-task RL) fail to exploit the inherently online nature of web interaction and suffer from three compounding challenges: scarcity of training tasks, sparse binary-only feedback over long horizons (~10 steps), and policy distribution drift that causes catastrophic forgetting during iterative online learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252878/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a user instruction, the simplified HTML of the current webpage (with distinct element IDs assigned to clickable elements), and the history of previously taken actions. It outputs a single web action (click, type, search, scroll, select_dropdown_option, goto, etc.) targeting a specific element ID, augmented with '# Element:' and 'Note:' comments that ground the action in the page context. The ORM separately consumes the instruction, action history, and final-state HTML to emit a binary YES/NO success judgment, avoiding the need for task-specific reward functions on newly generated tasks.

## Method

WEBRL is a self-evolving online curriculum RL framework with three coupled mechanisms. (1) A self-evolving curriculum generates ~500 new instructions per phase from previously failed tasks via GPT-4o, filtered by a trained critic (retaining scores in [0.05, 0.75]) and an automatic feasibility check, so task difficulty adapts to the agent's evolving competence. (2) A KL-constrained off-policy policy update, derived from the maximum-entropy RL optimal policy (Rafailov et al., 2024a), minimizes the MSE between β·log(π_θ/π_ref) and the estimated advantage A*, where the value network is trained with a cross-entropy objective and advantages use a λ=0.5 blend of next-step and final-step estimators; this explicitly penalizes deviation from the previous phase's policy to prevent drift. (3) An experience replay buffer stores only successful trajectories and, at each phase, selects replayed actions whose perplexity under the current actor falls in [1/0.95, 1/0.5], excluding both over-familiar and too-difficult data. Together these components enable consistent, phase-over-phase improvement: Llama-3.1-8B rises from 4.8% to 42.4% SR on WebArena-Lite, and Llama-3.1-70B reaches 47.3%, surpassing GPT-4-Turbo (17.6%) by over 160% relatively.

## Results

On WebArena-Lite (165 test cases across 5 websites), Llama-3.1-8B trained with WEBRL achieves a 42.4% average success rate, up from 4.8% for the untrained Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct baseline. The Llama-3.1-70B variant reaches 49.1% average SR, surpassing GPT-4-Turbo (17.6%) by over 160% relatively and the previous best open-LLM web agent AutoWebGLM (18.2%) by more than 2x. WEBRL also outperforms the strongest RL baseline DigiRL (30.3% for 8B) by 12.1 points and SFT (20.6%) by 21.8 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WEBRL (Llama-3.1-8B, 42.4%) outperforms all prompting-based proprietary LLMs: GPT-4-Turbo (17.6%) and GPT-4o (13.9%), with a relative improvement of over 160% over GPT-4-Turbo. Against the previous state-of-the-art open-LLM web agent AutoWebGLM (18.2%), WEBRL more than doubles the success rate. Among training-based baselines on Llama-3.1-8B, WEBRL beats SFT/BC (20.6%), Filtered BC (23.0%), AWR (28.5%), and DigiRL (30.3%) by 12–22 points, with the largest gap over DigiRL attributable to the self-evolving curriculum enabling broader task coverage versus DigiRL's fixed task set. On the 70B scale, WEBRL (49.1%) improves over SFT (23.0%) by 26.1 points, showing that the RL gains scale with model size. The ORM (8B) also outperforms GPT-4 (71.9%), GPT-4V (71.2%), and Captioner+GPT-4 (72.6%) on trajectory evaluation, reaching 80.8% accuracy on the test set.

## Relation to Prior Work

WEBRL builds directly on the maximum-entropy RL formulation from Rafailov et al. (2024a), adapting the Q*-to-policy derivation to an off-policy, KL-constrained setting for sequential web interaction. It inherits the actor-critic architecture and sparse-reward advantage estimation from DigiRL (Bai et al., 2024) and the classification-based value training from Farebrother et al. (2024), but departs from both by introducing a self-evolving curriculum that generates new tasks from the agent's own failures (rather than operating on a fixed task set) and by replacing on-policy PPO-style updates with an off-policy MSE loss on the log-policy ratio. The KL-divergence constraint on policy updates is borrowed from RLHF (Ouyang et al., 2022) but repurposed here to prevent catastrophic forgetting across curriculum phases rather than to align with a human preference distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WEBRL demonstrates that a small open LLM (8B) can be trained to substantially outperform prompted proprietary APIs on web-agent tasks, which is practically significant for cost-sensitive deployments. However, the framework's reliance on GPT-4o for task generation and infeasibility filtering introduces a proprietary dependency that limits the 'fully open' claim, and evaluation is confined to a single 165-task benchmark (WebArena-Lite), leaving open whether the self-evolving curriculum generalizes to dynamic, real-world web environments. Key open questions include whether the ORM can be replaced by environment-specific reward functions at scale, whether the curriculum can be made fully autonomous without LLM-based task synthesis, and whether the KL-constrained off-policy update transfers to other long-horizon agent domains beyond web browsing.
