## Problem

Fourier Neural Operators (FNOs) and other neural operators excel at forward simulation of parametric differential equations but fail to capture parameter sensitivities ∂u/∂p, which are essential for inverse problems, sensitivity analysis, and robustness under concept drift. No prior neural operator training had incorporated sensitivity (Jacobian) information, leaving a gap where accurate solution prediction coexisted with poor gradient estimation and degraded inversion performance.

## Input / Output

The model takes initial conditions u₀, spatial coordinates x, time t, and physical parameters p (which can be high-dimensional, up to 82 in their zoned Burgers test) as function-space inputs processed through a lifting layer that reshapes and concatenates parameters to match spatiotemporal dimensions. It outputs the full solution path u(x,t) across all time and space in a single forward pass (no time-stepping), and the parameter sensitivities ∂u/∂p are obtained as a byproduct via automatic differentiation through the trained network. The architecture is meshless and discretization-invariant, so outputs can be queried at arbitrary spatiotemporal resolutions.

## Method

SC-FNO uses an identical FNO architecture but augments the standard data loss L_u with a sensitivity loss L_s that penalizes the discrepancy between the network's predicted Jacobian (computed via AD through the FNO) and true Jacobians pre-computed from a differentiable numerical solver (torchdiffeq) or finite differences. During training, only a random subset of spatial-temporal points is sampled per epoch for gradient evaluation, keeping overhead to 30–130% extra time per epoch while eventually covering the full domain. An optional PINN equation loss L_Eq can be added (SC-FNO-PINN), but the paper shows it provides minimal additional benefit because standard PDE formulations do not contain ∂u/∂p terms, leaving parameter sensitivities unconstrained by equation-based regularization. The key insight is that explicit sensitivity supervision governs how input parameters influence the output, building a more robust internal representation that improves inversion accuracy, reduces training data requirements, and maintains performance under parameter perturbations beyond the training range.

## Results

On PDE1 (5 parameters, 2000 training samples), SC-FNO achieves R²=0.924–0.932 for parameter sensitivities (∂u/∂p) versus R²=0.723–0.781 for FNO, while maintaining comparable solution accuracy (R²=0.983 vs 0.986). Under 40% parameter perturbation, SC-FNO retains R²=0.912 for u(t) while FNO drops to R²=0.529. For single-parameter inversion, SC-FNO achieves R²=0.998 with less than 1/5 the relative L² error of FNO (R²=0.905). In the high-dimensional 82-parameter setting, SC-FNO with only 100 samples achieves relative L²=0.0087, less than 1/3 of FNO's error with 500 samples (0.039).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SC-FNO dramatically outperforms vanilla FNO on sensitivity accuracy: for PDE2, FNO's R² for ∂u/∂α is only 0.206 while SC-FNO reaches 0.987, and for PDE4 (Allen-Cahn) FNO's Jacobian R² is −3.11 versus SC-FNO's 0.998. FNO-PINN (PINO) provides only marginal improvement over FNO for sensitivities (R² remains below 0.52 for most PDE2 gradients) and still incurs 3–5× the relative L² inversion error of SC-FNO. SC-FNO-PINN offers a slight additional benefit over SC-FNO (e.g., R²=0.945 vs 0.925 for ∂u/∂α on PDE1), but the paper notes the equation loss's impact is surprisingly minimal. Across alternative neural operators (WNO, MWNO, DeepONet), adding the sensitivity constraint yields uniform improvements that are larger than the differences between the operators themselves (Table D.11). Under 40% parameter perturbation, SC-FNO maintains R²=0.912–0.933 for u(t) while FNO collapses to 0.529–0.734, and in the 82-parameter zoned setting SC-FNO with 100 samples beats FNO with 500 samples by a factor of 3 in relative L² error.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) framework by Li et al. (2021) and the Physics-Informed Neural Operator (PINO/FNO-PINN) by Li et al. (2023), which added PDE-residual regularization to FNO training. The key departure is introducing a sensitivity loss that supervises parameter Jacobians (∂u/∂p) computed via automatic differentiation or finite differences, rather than the spatial-temporal derivatives (∂u/∂x, ∂u/∂t) that PINN-style losses constrain. The authors connect to gradient-enhanced neural networks (Liu & Batill, 2000) and Sobolev training (Czarnecki et al., 2017) but note those prior works targeted low-dimensional derivative approximation, whereas SC-FNO applies the principle to infinite-dimensional operator learning. The framework is shown to generalize beyond FNO to Wavelet Neural Operators, Multiwavelet Neural Operators, and DeepONets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Neural operators are increasingly deployed as surrogates for PDEs in inverse problems, data assimilation, and optimization, yet their inability to capture parameter sensitivities has been a recognized but unaddressed gap. SC-FNO's sensitivity loss is a minimal architectural change (one additional loss term) that dramatically improves inversion accuracy, robustness to concept drift, and data efficiency—particularly in high-dimensional parameter spaces where FNO's performance ceiling is otherwise low. A notable open question is why the PINN equation loss provides almost no benefit for sensitivity estimation (since PDEs do not contain ∂u/∂p terms), suggesting that equation-based and sensitivity-based regularization address fundamentally different aspects of model quality. Future work could explore incorporating boundary condition sensitivities, scaling to even higher parameter dimensions, and applying the framework to real-world geophysical or engineering inverse problems.
