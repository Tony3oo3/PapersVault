## Problem

Existing methods for mitigating spurious correlations in neural networks rely on sample-wise information about spurious features (environment labels, inferred group membership, or human-annotated spuriosity), which is only attainable when the spurious signal is significantly stronger than the core invariant signal. In realistic settings where the spurious feature appears in only a small fraction of samples (e.g., 10% of a class), these identification-based approaches fail entirely, yet the resulting spurious correlations still cause catastrophic test-time failures under distribution shift.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard supervised training dataset (RGB images at 32×32 for CIFAR-10S, 224×224 for CelebA/Hard ImageNet/Waterbirds, or sentence pairs for MultiNLI) and produces a pruned training subset by removing a small fraction of the hardest samples per class. The pruned dataset is then used for standard supervised training, yielding a classifier that outputs class labels. No auxiliary inputs such as environment labels, validation splits mimicking test distribution, or human annotations of spurious features are required in the unidentifiable setting.

## Method

The paper discovers that spurious correlations are learned almost entirely from a handful of training samples that simultaneously possess hard core/invariant features and the spurious feature, with reliance growing super-linearly with sample difficulty. The proposed data pruning technique first trains a network briefly (1–10 epochs depending on the dataset) to compute per-sample difficulty scores via training error, then prunes the hardest samples per class (equal proportion per class to account for inter-class difficulty differences). In unidentifiable settings where spurious samples are uniformly distributed across the difficulty spectrum, pruning the global hardest samples suffices to remove the critical spurious+hard-core samples without needing to know which samples contain spurious features. In identifiable settings where strong spurious signals concentrate spurious samples in the easy half of the distribution, the method instead uses group labels to prune the hardest samples within each spurious group. No hyperparameter tuning is performed, and the approach attains state-of-the-art worst-group accuracy on Waterbirds and MultiNLI while requiring only a small fraction of training data to be removed.

## Results

On the standard Waterbirds benchmark, the proposed data pruning method achieves 90.93% Worst-Group Accuracy (WGA), outperforming all baselines including PDE (90.3%), DFRTr (90.2%), and CnC (88.5%), while maintaining a competitive 92.48% Mean accuracy. On MultiNLI, the method reaches 75.88% WGA and 81.07% Mean accuracy, slightly trailing gDRO (77.7% WGA) but surpassing JTT (72.6%) and DFRTr (71.5%). In unidentifiable settings (CelebA, Hard ImageNet), pruning only ~1% of the hardest training samples (10% of spurious samples) yields substantial WGA improvements without significant drops in overall test accuracy, demonstrating that spurious correlations are driven by a small subset of hard samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Waterbirds WGA, the proposed method (90.93%) achieves the best result, narrowly beating PDE (90.3%) and DFRTr (90.2%), and clearly outperforming CnC (88.5%), JTT (86.7%), and gDRO (86.0%). On Waterbirds Mean accuracy, DFRTr (97.0%) and ERM (98.10%) remain higher, with the proposed method at 92.48%, though it still exceeds CnC (90.9%). On MultiNLI WGA, gDRO (77.7%) edges out the proposed method (75.88%), while the method clearly surpasses JTT (72.6%) and DFRTr (71.5%); on MultiNLI Mean, ERM (82.8%) and DFRTr (82.5%) are marginally ahead of the proposed method (81.07%). Notably, the proposed method requires no hyperparameter tuning and does not rely on inferred sample-wise spurious information, unlike JTT and CnC which need validation-set group labels, and unlike gDRO and DFRTr which require training group labels. The method also demonstrates effectiveness in unidentifiable settings (CelebA, Hard ImageNet) where all prior baselines are inapplicable, as spurious features cannot be detected at the sample level.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the sample-difficulty estimation framework from data pruning (Paul et al., 2021) for a fundamentally different goal: severing spurious correlations rather than reducing computational cost. It positions itself against the spurious-correlation mitigation literature (gDRO, JTT, CnC, DFRTr, PDE) by identifying a critical shared assumption—access to sample-wise spurious or group information—that breaks down when the spurious signal is weak relative to the invariant signal. The key conceptual departure is the empirical discovery that spurious correlation learning is driven by a tiny subset of hard samples, enabling a pruning-based solution that bypasses the need to identify which samples carry spurious features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a realistic deployment gap: most prior robustness methods assume the spurious signal dominates, but in practice spurious cues are often weak and undetectable, rendering those methods ineffective. The practical implication—that pruning a small fraction of the hardest training samples per class (without knowing which contain spurious features) mitigates spurious reliance—is attractive for practitioners who lack group labels or domain knowledge. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-label or open-set settings, how it interacts with other failure modes (e.g., label noise, adversarial examples), and whether the 'hard sample' signal computed early in training remains stable across architectures and training regimes.
