## Problem

Score identity Distillation (SiD) achieves state-of-the-art one-step generation by distilling pretrained diffusion models in a data-free manner, but its ultimate quality is bounded by how accurately the teacher score network approximates the true data score. When the teacher is undertrained or has limited capacity, this approximation error becomes a hard performance ceiling for the distilled generator. The paper asks whether incorporating real images and adversarial training can break through this teacher bottleneck while preserving SiD's efficiency.

## Input / Output

The generator takes a random Gaussian noise vector (and optionally a class label for conditional generation) and produces a single RGB image in exactly one forward pass, with no iterative denoising. Experiments span 32×32 (CIFAR-10), 64×64 (ImageNet, FFHQ, AFHQ-v2), and 512×512 (ImageNet) resolutions. The generator is initialized either from the pretrained teacher score network (SiDA) or from a pre-distilled SiD generator (SiD²A), and the fake score network is always initialized from the teacher, with its encoder repurposed as a discriminator via a return-flag mechanism that requires no additional parameters.

## Method

SiDA integrates a Diffusion GAN-based adversarial loss into SiD's model-based Fisher divergence objective by repurposing the fake score network's U-Net encoder as a time-conditioned discriminator that outputs a 2D spatial logit map. The adversarial loss is computed per spatial location in the latent feature map, then averaged over the per-GPU batch to yield a scalar 'fakeness' value that is multiplied into each pixel's SiD loss before applying the standard diffusion weighting, ensuring the two losses remain balanced without dataset-specific tuning. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) stabilize the fake score network for 100k images, (2) train both networks with the SiD loss for 100k images, and (3) jointly optimize with the combined SiD + adversarial loss. The SiD²A variant further initializes the generator from a pre-distilled SiD checkpoint, enabling rapid fine-tuning that surpasses the teacher within a fraction of the iterations SiD requires.

## Results

SiDA and SiD²A (SiD-initialized SiDA) achieve state-of-the-art one-step generation FID scores across all tested benchmarks: 1.499 on CIFAR-10 unconditional, 1.396 on CIFAR-10 conditional, 1.110 on ImageNet 64×64, and 1.366 on ImageNet 512×512 (EDM2-XXL). Critically, on ImageNet 512×512, SiD²A surpasses the EDM2-XXL teacher (FID 1.81 with CFG and 63 NFEs) by a large margin using only a single generation step and no classifier-free guidance. Convergence is approximately 10× faster than SiD: while SiD required ~100M generator-synthesized images to match the teacher, SiDA needed only ~10M to exceed it.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SiDA/SiD²A consistently outperforms SiD (the direct data-free predecessor) across all datasets and model sizes, with FID improvements of 0.2–1.2 depending on the setting (e.g., 1.366 vs. 1.969 on EDM2-XXL). On CIFAR-10 unconditional, SiD²A (FID 1.499) beats GDD-I (1.54), CTM (1.98), DMD (3.77), and the VP-EDM teacher (1.97 at 35 NFEs). On ImageNet 64×64, SiD²A (FID 1.110) surpasses GDD-I (1.16), DMD2+longer training (1.28), DMD2 (1.51), and CTM (1.92), all in a single step. On ImageNet 512×512, SiD²A-EDM2-S (280M params, FID 1.669) already outperforms the much larger EDM2-XXL teacher (1.5B params, FID 1.81 with CFG) and sCD at 2 NFEs (FID 1.88), while SiD²A-EDM2-XXL (FID 1.366) sets a new record among all reported one-step and multi-step methods. The key advantage over GDD-I is that SiDA introduces no external pretrained encoder (e.g., VGG16 or EfficientNet) as a discriminator, avoiding potential feature leakage concerns that affect FID evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SiDA directly extends SiD (Zhou et al., ICML 2024), a data-free Fisher divergence-based distillation method, by incorporating a Diffusion GAN (Wang et al., ICLR 2023) adversarial loss at noisy diffusion stages. The key architectural departure from prior adversarial distillation methods (DMD2, CTM, GDD-I) is that SiDA reuses the fake score network's U-Net encoder as the discriminator via a return-flag mechanism, introducing zero additional parameters. This unifies the score-matching objective of SiD with the distribution-matching objective of Diffusion GAN in a single joint loss, where the adversarial component is batch-normalized per GPU and folded into the pixel-level SiD loss to respect the existing diffusion weighting scheme.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SiDA demonstrates that a single-step generator can surpass its multi-step teacher (EDM2-XXL with CFG and 63 NFEs) by a substantial margin (FID 1.366 vs. 1.81) without using classifier-free guidance, while converging roughly 10× faster than SiD. The no-new-parameters design and GPU-batch normalization strategy make the method easy to adopt on top of existing SiD or EDM/EDM2 codebases. Limitations include the requirement for real training data (unlike SiD's data-free setting), a ~10% per-iteration slowdown, and the absence of CFG integration, which the authors note could yield further gains. Open questions include whether the approach extends to text-to-image generation, video, or other modalities, and whether the adversarial component's benefit persists when the teacher is already near-optimal.
