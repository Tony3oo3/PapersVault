## Problem

Outliers in LLMs have been widely observed and algorithmically mitigated (via quantization, pruning, etc.), but their formation mechanisms and functional roles remain unexplored. Existing work treats activation, weight, and attention outliers in isolation, lacking a unified framework that explains why they co-occur, how they propagate through layers, and what purpose they serve within the attention mechanism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251131/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis consumes pretrained LLMs (LLaMA2-7B/13B, GPT-2, Phi-2, Mistral-7B, OPT-6.7B, MPT-7B, Falcon-7B) and probes their internal representations—layer outputs h_ℓ, down-projection inputs x_ℓ^down, down-projection weight matrices W_ℓ^down, and cumulative attention score matrices A_ℓ^i—using 100 sequences of length 2,048 from RedPajama. Outputs include a three-way outlier taxonomy with mathematical definitions (threshold τ=1000× mean absolute value), cross-dimensional consistency metrics, and a proposed explicit context-aware scaling factor S_c(x) that can be inserted into the attention mechanism. Compression experiments evaluate perplexity on WikiText2 under 8-bit AbsMax quantization and 50% unstructured magnitude pruning.

## Method

The paper first formalizes three outlier types (activation, weight, attention) and demonstrates they are simultaneously present and interlocked: weight outliers in W_ℓ^down align 100% with activation outliers in feature dimensions, and activation outliers overlap 95% with attention outliers in sequence dimensions, both concentrated at start tokens and weak-semantic tokens. Tracing the lifecycle reveals a causal chain—weight outliers in up/gate projections amplify through SiLU-GLU to produce activation outliers, which then align query-key dot products to generate attention outliers. The theoretical core argues that softmax's constraint (scores sum to 1) forces extreme dynamic range in QK^T to achieve near-zero updates for low-information tokens; this demand propagates backward through LayerNorm and residual connections, compelling MLPs to generate high-magnitude activations that become the observed outliers. To validate, the authors train five GPT-2 variants with different attention formulations and show that only explicit context-aware scaling (multiplying softmax output by a learnable S_c(x)) eliminates outliers, while fixed or context-aware biases do not. This architectural fix reduces quantization PPL from 93.44 to 29.22 and pruning PPL from 7235.68 to 39.47 with <0.1% parameter overhead, and accelerates early-training convergence.

## Results

The headline quantitative result (Table 3) shows that adding a context-aware scaling factor to GPT-2 dramatically improves compression robustness: under 8-bit AbsMax quantization, PPL drops from 93.44 to 29.22, and under 50% unstructured pruning, PPL drops from 7235.68 to 39.47, while FP16 PPL remains comparable (27.24 vs. 26.95). The parameter overhead is negligible, increasing from 123.59M to 123.70M (<0.1%). On the analysis side, the paper reports 100% consistency between weight and activation outliers in feature dimensions, and 95% consistency between activation and attention outliers in sequence dimensions (Table 1), confirming their systematic interconnection.

**Table 3: Comparison of GPT-2 Default and GPT-2 with Context-aware Scaling Factor under pruning and quantization methods (WikiText2 PPL).**

| Model | PPL (FP16) | PPL (AbsMax W8) | PPL (50% Sparse) |
|---|---|---|---|
| GPT-2 Default | 27.24 | 93.44 | 7235.68 |
| GPT-2 + Context-aware Scaling | **26.95** | **29.22** | **39.47** |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external outlier-mitigation methods; instead, it isolates the causal mechanism through five attention variants trained from scratch on GPT-2. Only the attention bias variant (d) and the explicit context-aware scaling factor variant (e) successfully eliminate systematic outliers, while the default attention (a), explicit fixed bias (b), and explicit context-aware bias (c) all still produce outliers. This comparison confirms that the scaling factor—not the bias—is the critical component. In the compression comparison (Table 3), GPT-2 + Context-aware Scaling outperforms GPT-2 Default by a factor of ~3.2× under W8 quantization (29.22 vs. 93.44 PPL) and ~183× under 50% pruning (39.47 vs. 7235.68 PPL), while maintaining comparable FP16 performance. The paper also validates generalizability by showing sigmoid attention (which avoids softmax normalization) and the context-aware scaling factor in TinyLLaMA-120M both eliminate outliers, reinforcing that softmax is the root cause.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously studied but treated-in-isolation phenomena—activation outliers (Dettmers et al., 2022), massive activations as attention biases (Sun et al., 2024), and attention sinks (Xiao et al., 2023b)—into a single causal framework rooted in the softmax operation. Prior work predominantly approached outliers as nuisances to be mitigated via quantization-aware training, smoothing, or pruning heuristics, without investigating their functional role. The key departure here is the hypothesis that these outliers are not bugs but implicit context-aware scaling factors that the model learns to exploit, supported by ablation experiments with five attention variants trained from scratch on GPT-2.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of activation, weight, and attention outliers under a single softmax-driven mechanism provides a coherent theoretical lens for a phenomenon that was previously understood only in fragments, potentially guiding more principled compression and architecture design. The compression results (PPL 93.44→29.22 under W8 quantization, 7235→39 under 50% pruning) are striking but validated only on GPT-2-scale models, leaving open whether the context-aware scaling factor generalizes to 7B+ architectures or whether the <0.1% parameter overhead remains negligible at scale. A key open question is whether non-softmax attention mechanisms (e.g., sigmoid attention, linear attention) naturally avoid these outliers, which would confirm the causal claim and suggest a cleaner architectural fix.
