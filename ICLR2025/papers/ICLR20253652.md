## Problem

Existing LLM-based methods for formulating and solving optimization problems from natural language suffer from limited optimization generalization: they achieve low solving accuracy and can only handle a narrow range of problem types. Prompt-based approaches (e.g., Chain-of-Experts, OptiMUS) rely on fixed reasoning chains, while learning-based methods (e.g., ORLM, LLaMoCo) lack structured problem definitions and hallucination mitigation, restricting their deployment across diverse real-world optimization scenarios.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language description of an optimization problem as input and produces a structured five-element formulation (Sets, Parameters, Variables, Objective, Constraints) as an intermediate step, followed by executable Pyomo solver code and the optimal solution. The auto-testing loop feeds execution logs and errors back to the model for self-correction, iterating up to 12 times. The five-element formulation explicitly captures implicit constraints (e.g., integer positivity) that are often lost in direct code generation, serving as a bridge between free-form text and formal mathematical structure.

## Method

LLMOPT is a unified learning-based framework built on Qwen1.5-14B with three stages: (1) a five-element universal formulation that decomposes any optimization problem into Sets, Parameters, Variables, Objective, and Constraints, enabling consistent modeling of LP, MILP, nonlinear, and combinatorial problems; (2) multi-instruction supervised fine-tuning via LoRA on ~30K instances covering both formulation generation and Pyomo code generation, followed by KTO (Kahneman-Tversky Optimization) alignment using expert-validated desirability labels to suppress hallucinations without requiring pairwise preference data; (3) an auto-testing pipeline that executes generated code, parses output/errors, and invokes a self-correction LLM to decide whether to revise the formulation or the code. Data augmentation applies seven diverse transformation instructions to 1,763 seed problems, with all labels verified by 12 domain experts across four review stages.

## Results

LLMOPT (based on Qwen1.5-14B) achieves state-of-the-art Solving Accuracy (SA) across all six datasets: 93.0% on NL4Opt, 97.0% on Mamo Easy, 68.0% on Mamo Complex, 46.0% on IndustryOR, 83.8% on NLP4LP, and 72.7% on ComplexOR. This represents an average 11.08% SA improvement over the previous best methods. The largest gains appear on Mamo Complex (+30.1% over ORLM) and NL4Opt (+14.2% over OptiMUS), while the smallest is on ComplexOR (+6.0% over OptiMUS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLMOPT outperforms all five baseline families across every dataset it is evaluated on. Against the strongest learning-based baseline ORLM (best variant: Deepseek-Math-7B-Base at 86.5% on NL4Opt), LLMOPT gains +6.5% on NL4Opt, +14.7% on Mamo Easy, +30.1% on Mamo Complex, and +8.0% on IndustryOR. Against the strongest prompt-based baseline OptiMUS, LLMOPT improves by +14.2% on NL4Opt, +11.8% on NLP4LP, and +6.0% on ComplexOR. GPT-4 used directly (without fine-tuning) is substantially outperformed, with LLMOPT achieving 93.0% vs. 47.3% on NL4Opt and 68.0% vs. 14.6% on Mamo Complex. The weaker prompt-based methods Reflexion and Chain-of-Experts are also clearly surpassed, with LLMOPT exceeding them by 40+ percentage points on NL4Opt. Notably, LLMOPT also achieves SOTA on NLP4LP and ComplexOR despite these datasets being entirely excluded from training, demonstrating strong generalization to unseen problem distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the KTO alignment framework (Ethayarajh et al., 2024) and positions itself within the emerging LLM-for-optimization literature that spans prompt-based methods (Chain-of-Experts, OptiMUS) and learning-based methods (LLaMoCo, ORLM). It departs from prior work by introducing the five-element formulation as a universal structured intermediate representation between natural language and solver code, and by combining multi-instruction SFT, KTO-based model alignment, and a self-correction loop into a single unified pipeline. Unlike ORLM, which focuses on code generation alone, LLMOPT explicitly targets both problem formalization accuracy and solver code generation, framing the challenge as 'optimization generalization' encompassing both accuracy and generality across problem types.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical, reproducible pipeline for automating the full workflow from natural language to optimal solution, with the five-element formulation serving as a reusable structured interface that could benefit other LLM-based optimization systems. Key limitations include the labor-intensive expert labeling process, the scarcity of high-quality optimization training data, and the unaddressed challenge of large-scale problems where data resides in databases rather than natural language. Open questions include whether the approach scales to problems with thousands of variables and complex constraint structures, and how to efficiently generate diverse, well-labeled training data without extensive expert involvement.
