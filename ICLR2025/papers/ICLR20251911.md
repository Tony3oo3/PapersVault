## Problem

RAG systems comprise multiple modules (retriever, generator, decision maker, query rewriter) that are typically hand-designed and separately optimized, yet the golden answers for intermediate modules are often model-dependent or retriever-dependent, making independent optimization inherently suboptimal. This paper argues that multi-module RAG systems should be jointly optimized end-to-end so that all components can adapt to each other's behavior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251911/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a single question as input and produces a structured action whose first token is either [ANSWER] (followed by the final answer text) or [RETRIEVE] (followed by a rewritten search query). When retrieval is triggered, the Bing search engine returns top-K=4 concatenated snippets as an observation, which is appended to the state for the next policy call. A retrieval quota N (set to 1 in experiments) caps the number of retrieval steps, after which the policy is forced to produce an answer.

## Method

SmartRAG uses a single policy network (Flan-T5-large or LLaMA-2-7B) that simultaneously acts as decision maker, query rewriter, and answer generator, paired with a fixed Bing search engine retriever. Training is two-phase: first, an SFT warm-up constructs three types of (state, action) pairs per question (direct answer, retrieve with GPT-3.5-rewritten query, answer conditioned on retrieved observation) to establish the output format; optionally, data points are pruned based on whether the base model already answers correctly to encode self-knowledge. Second, PPO jointly optimizes the full pipeline using a sparse reward that gives +1 (exact match) + F1 score for correct answers and −α (α=0.2) for each retrieval action, with a discount factor γ. The key design choice is that supervision comes solely from environment feedback (the final answer correctness and retrieval cost) rather than from proxy golden labels for intermediate modules, enabling the policy to learn when to retrieve, what query to issue, and how to extract the answer from observations as a coordinated whole.

## Results

On the three main open-domain QA datasets (PopQA, AmbigNQ, HotpotQA), SmartRAG achieves average EM of 33.29 and F1 of 41.25 with Flan-T5-large, and average EM of 36.27 and F1 of 43.93 with LLaMA-2-7B, outperforming all separately-optimized baselines. The largest gains are on PopQA, where SmartRAG reaches 44.32 EM / 47.60 F1 (LLaMA-2-7B), surpassing GPT4 Rewriter + SFT RAG (42.22 EM / 46.04 F1) by over 2 EM points. SmartRAG also demonstrates adaptive retrieval behavior: on datasets where the Bing search engine provides little useful knowledge (OpenBookQA, MedQA-cn, ARC-c), it learns to skip retrieval entirely (0% retrieval rate) while maintaining competitive accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SmartRAG outperforms all separately-optimized baselines across both Flan-T5-large and LLaMA-2-7B on all three main datasets. Against the strongest baseline GPT4 Rewriter + SFT RAG, SmartRAG gains +1.03 EM and +1.45 F1 on average (Flan-T5) and +1.73 EM and +1.44 F1 (LLaMA-2-7B), with the largest margin on PopQA (+2.10 EM for LLaMA-2). Against SKR (which adds a learned decision-maker but still trains modules separately), SmartRAG improves by +4.58 EM / +4.67 F1 on average (LLaMA-2-7B). Notably, GPT4 Rewriter + SFT RAG sometimes performs *worse* than SFT RAG alone, confirming that GPT-4's rewrites are reasonable but not optimal for the specific retriever. Self-RAG and ReAct/IRCoT perform substantially worse (e.g., Self-RAG averages only 4.66 EM on LLaMA-2-7B), as they rely on model-dependent proxy labels or unadapted chain-of-thought without joint optimization. On datasets where the search engine provides little useful information (OpenBookQA, MedQA-cn, ARC-c), SmartRAG learns to skip retrieval entirely (0% retrieval rate) while matching or exceeding SFT accuracy, whereas SKR still retrieves 5.6–84.3% of the time, wasting retrieval budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

SmartRAG departs from the dominant paradigm of training RAG modules (decision maker, query rewriter, answer generator) separately with proxy golden answers, arguing that such answers are inherently model-dependent and retriever-dependent. It builds on the adaptive retrieval line (Self-RAG, SKR, Adaptive-RAG) and the PPO-for-LLMs line (Ma et al. 2023 for query rewriting, Ouyang et al. 2022 for instruction following), but unifies all three sub-tasks into a single policy network optimized end-to-end via RL with environment feedback (answer correctness + retrieval cost) rather than per-module supervision. Unlike ReAct and IRCoT, which rely on the base model's inherent reasoning without task-specific adaptation, SmartRAG explicitly learns retrieval decisions and query formulation through reward-driven training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean argument and empirical evidence that joint optimization of multi-module RAG systems outperforms separate optimization, with the practical implication that practitioners should not treat query rewriting, retrieval gating, and answer generation as independent sub-problems. A notable finding is that GPT-4-rewritten queries can underperform original questions, suggesting that query optimization must be grounded in the specific retriever's behavior rather than relying on a general-purpose LLM. Limitations include reliance on a web search engine (Bing) rather than a standard dense retriever, small model scales (Flan-T5 large, LLaMA-2 7B), and a retrieval quota of N=1, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger models, multi-hop retrieval, or domain-specific knowledge bases.
