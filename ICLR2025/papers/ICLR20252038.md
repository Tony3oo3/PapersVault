## Problem

While fair submodular maximization has been studied under cardinality and matroid constraints, no prior work has addressed fairness in the submodular cover problem, despite its direct relevance to applications like data summarization and clustering where both a coverage threshold and balanced representation across protected groups are required. This paper initiates the study of the Fair Submodular Cover (FSC) problem and develops the first bicriteria approximation algorithms for it.

## Input / Output

The method takes a ground set U partitioned into N disjoint groups U_1,...,U_N, oracle access to a monotone submodular function f, a threshold τ, and per-group proportion bounds p_c ≤ q_c (with Σp_c ≤ 1 ≤ Σq_c). It returns a subset S ⊆ U of minimum cardinality satisfying f(S) ≥ τ and the fairness constraint p_c|S| ≤ |S ∩ U_c| ≤ q_c|S| for all groups c. In the bicriteria setting, the solution is guaranteed to satisfy the fairness constraint exactly but may violate the threshold by a factor β, yielding f(S) ≥ βτ with β arbitrarily close to 1.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a two-stage framework: (1) conversion algorithms (convert-fair for discrete, convert-continuous for fractional) that exploit the duality between FSC and FSM by iteratively guessing |OPT| and invoking a bicriteria FSM subroutine on a fairness matroid, followed by rounding to enforce proportion constraints; and (2) three new bicriteria FSM algorithms—greedy-fairness-bi, threshold-fairness-bi, and cont-thresh-greedy-bi—that operate on a novel β-extension of the fairness matroid M_fair, allowing the solution to exceed the original budget by a factor β while maintaining feasibility within the extended matroid. A central technical lemma (Lemma 2) guarantees the existence of a sequence containing β copies of OPT such that each prefix of the solution plus the next element remains feasible in the β-extension, enabling submodularity-based analysis. The continuous algorithm uses the multilinear extension of f with pipage rounding, achieving a (1−O(ε), ln(1/ε)+1) bicriteria ratio that matches the best known guarantee for unconstrained submodular cover. Combined with the conversion theorems, the discrete algorithms yield ((1+α)/ε, 1−O(ε)) and the continuous algorithm yields ((1+α)(ln(1/ε)+1), 1−O(ε)) bicriteria guarantees for FSC.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical contribution is bicriteria approximation algorithms for Fair Submodular Cover (FSC). The discrete algorithms (greedy-fairness-bi and threshold-fairness-bi) achieve a (1−O(ε), 1/ε) bicriteria ratio for FSM, which after conversion yields ((1+α)/ε, 1−O(ε)) for FSC. The continuous algorithm (cont-thresh-greedy-bi) improves the cardinality factor to ln(1/ε)+1, giving ((1+α)(ln(1/ε)+1), 1−O(ε)) for FSC, matching the best known guarantee for submodular cover without fairness. Experimentally, on the Twitch Gamers maximum coverage instance (5,000 nodes, 6 languages, τ=2400), the fair algorithms produce balanced language distributions while the standard greedy-bi baseline allocates over 80% of its solution to English speakers, at the cost of a larger solution size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its fair algorithms (greedy-fairness-bi and threshold-fairness-bi) against the standard greedy algorithm for submodular cover without fairness (greedy-bi, based on Krause et al. 2008) on the Twitch Gamers maximum coverage dataset. On the fairness metric (measured as (max_c |S∩U_c| − min_c |S∩U_c|)/|S|), both fair algorithms substantially outperform greedy-bi, which produces heavily skewed solutions (over 80% English speakers at τ=2400), while the fair algorithms distribute selections more evenly across all six languages. On the objective function value f(S), all three algorithms scale nearly linearly with τ, indicating comparable coverage quality. However, on cost (solution cardinality), the fair algorithms return strictly larger solutions than greedy-bi, reflecting the expected trade-off of enforcing balance constraints. The fairness gap between the fair and unfair algorithms widens as τ increases, suggesting the fairness constraint becomes more critical for larger summaries. The paper also notes that threshold-fairness-bi requires fewer oracle queries to f than greedy-fairness-bi while achieving the same approximation guarantee.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper initiates the study of fair submodular cover (FSC), a problem that had no prior treatment despite the extensive literature on both fair submodular maximization (FSM) and standard submodular cover. The key technical building blocks come from El Halabi et al. (2020), who defined the fairness matroid and Fair-Greedy algorithm for FSM, and from Iyer & Bilmes (2013), who established the conversion paradigm from submodular maximization to submodular cover. The paper's central departure is the introduction of the β-extension of the fairness matroid, which allows the solution to exceed the original fairness budget by a factor β, thereby breaking the 1−1/e approximation barrier that is tight for matroid-constrained submodular maximization. The discrete and continuous bicriteria algorithms adapt the threshold greedy (Badanidiyuru & Vondrák, 2014) and standard greedy (Fisher et al., 1978) frameworks to operate on this extended matroid.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing the first bicriteria approximation algorithms for FSC, this paper provides a principled framework for fairness-aware submodular optimization in cover-type settings (data summarization, training data selection, influence maximization) where prior methods either ignored fairness or produced solutions far from the feasibility threshold. The continuous algorithm's ln(1/ε) guarantee matching the best known bound for standard submodular cover suggests that fairness constraints need not fundamentally degrade approximation quality in the continuous regime. Open questions include whether the β-extension idea generalizes beyond the fairness matroid to other matroid structures, whether the discrete gap between 1/ε and ln(1/ε) can be closed, and whether the assumption of sufficiently large group sizes (|U_c| ≥ q_c(1+α)ln(1/ε)|OPT|) holds in practical settings with small protected groups.
