## Problem

Existing GNN-based methods for ground-state molecular conformation prediction rely on covalent bonds and monotonic pairwise distances to model inter-atomic forces, which inadequately captures the non-linear, atom-type-specific nature of non-bonded van der Waals interactions. This leads to significantly higher prediction errors for low-degree atoms whose conformations are dominated by non-bonded forces rather than bonded interactions, as empirically demonstrated by up to 0.488 Å RMSD gaps between low- and high-degree atom groups.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D molecular graph with node features (atom type, chirality) and edge features (bond type), augmented with Laplacian positional encoding, and produces a 3D ground-state conformation as an N×3 coordinate matrix. The encoder first predicts an initial conformation from which inter-atomic distances are derived to compute Lennard-Jones forces for graph rewiring; the decoder then generates residual adjustments to refine this into the final conformation. Training uses a rotation/translation-invariant loss on pairwise Euclidean distances, and evaluation includes a novel Energy-weighted RMSD (E-RMSD) metric that penalizes both energetically unstable conformations via a Boltzmann factor and atoms off-equilibrium with respect to net force.

## Method

REBIND is an encoder-decoder graph transformer that rewires the molecular graph based on Lennard-Jones forces computed from the encoder's initial conformation prediction. Using UFF parameters with Lorentz-Berthelot mixing rules, it calculates the derivative of the LJ potential for all non-bonded atomic pairs, then adds edges to the top-K non-bonded neighbors with the largest force magnitudes in a degree-compensating fashion (each atom receives edges up to the maximum degree in the graph, prioritizing low-degree atoms). The augmented edges are decomposed into separate attractive and repulsive adjacency matrices with positive and negative weights, respectively, to preserve force directionality. In the decoder, these force-based matrices are incorporated as learnable-scaled residuals in the multi-head self-attention scores alongside the bond adjacency matrix, enabling the model to distinguish between attractive and repulsive non-bonded interactions during conformation refinement. The framework is architecture-agnostic and can be plugged into existing GNN backbones (GINE, GATv2, GraphGPS) by splitting layers into encoder and decoder halves.

## Results

On QM9 (test), REBIND achieves C-RMSD of 0.321 and E-RMSD of 0.610, reducing these metrics by 22.5% and 23.8% respectively over the best baseline (GTMGC: 0.414 C-RMSD, 0.800 E-RMSD). On Molecule3D scaffold split, REBIND attains C-RMSD 0.667 and E-RMSD 1.182, a 3.6% and 7.3% improvement over GTMGC. On the large-molecule GEOM-DRUGS benchmark, REBIND reaches C-RMSD 1.396 and E-RMSD 2.602, outperforming GTMGC by 4.3% and 8.1%, and surpassing the diffusion-based Torsional Diffusion (C-RMSD 1.751, E-RMSD 2.992) by a wider margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REBIND outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest GNN baseline GTMGC (which already incorporates bond and distance-based attention residuals), REBIND achieves the largest gains on QM9 (22.5% C-RMSD, 23.8% E-RMSD reduction) and GEOM-DRUGS (4.3% C-RMSD, 8.1% E-RMSD), while the improvement on Molecule3D scaffold is more modest (3.6% C-RMSD, 7.3% E-RMSD). Traditional cheminformatics methods (RDKit-DG, RDKit-ETKDG) are consistently outperformed by a wide margin on all deep-learning metrics. Among GNN baselines, GraphGPS(LP) is the closest competitor to GTMGC, but REBIND still surpasses it by 5.1% C-RMSD and 8.7% E-RMSD on QM9. On GEOM-DRUGS, REBIND also beats the diffusion-based Torsional Diffusion by 20.2% in C-RMSD and 12.8% in E-RMSD, while requiring only a single forward pass rather than iterative denoising steps. The consistent E-RMSD advantage over C-RMSD across all comparisons indicates that REBIND produces not only geometrically accurate but also energetically more stable conformations.

## Relation to Prior Work

REBIND extends the GTMGC encoder-decoder graph transformer (Xu et al., 2024), which was the first to incorporate both bond adjacency and row-subtracted pairwise distances as attention residuals for ground-state conformation prediction. The key departure is replacing the monotonic distance-based proximity signal with explicit Lennard-Jones force magnitudes, motivated by the observation that non-bonded interactions are non-monotonic in distance and atom-type-dependent. The paper also positions itself against traditional message-passing GNNs (GINE, GATv2, GraphGPS) that model only bonded interactions, and against diffusion-based conformer generators (Torsional Diffusion) that target multi-conformer generation rather than single ground-state prediction. The degree-compensating edge augmentation strategy is novel in that it prioritizes low-coordination atoms, which prior methods treat uniformly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic identification of low-degree atoms as a failure mode provides a useful diagnostic for the molecular conformation prediction community, and the force-aware rewiring technique is a lightweight, parameter-free plug-in that improves any encoder-decoder GNN backbone by 5–25%. The introduction of E-RMSD (energy-weighted RMSD) as an evaluation metric is a practical contribution that better reflects physical plausibility beyond geometric accuracy. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to biomolecular systems (proteins, nucleic acids) where force-field parameters are less standardized, whether more accurate force fields (beyond UFF) would yield further gains, and whether the technique transfers to property prediction tasks where ground-truth 3D coordinates are unavailable.
