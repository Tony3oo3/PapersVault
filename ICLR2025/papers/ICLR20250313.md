## Problem

LLMs augmented with external contexts (e.g., in RAG) tend to over-rely on that context regardless of its factual accuracy, making them vulnerable to misinformation and knowledge conflicts. While prior work has identified this problem, it has largely focused on filtering irrelevant contexts or benchmarking with only synthetically generated errors, without providing effective methods for the model to dynamically calibrate trust between its internal knowledge and external evidence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250313/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each QA instance consists of a question paired with a single external context (document) that is deliberately either correct or incorrect, and the model must produce the correct answer in both cases. Every question is evaluated twice—once with a correct context and once with an incorrect one—and situated faithfulness is measured as the average of accuracy on true contexts (Acc_t) and accuracy on false contexts (Acc_f). Contexts range from human-written Reddit posts containing real factual errors (the newly introduced RedditQA) to LLM-synthesized perturbations of correct passages across TriviaQA, NaturalQA, PopQA, FreshQA, and ClashEval.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary approaches for resolving knowledge conflicts. Self-Guided Confidence Reasoning (SCR) prompts the model to assess the reliability of the external context relative to its own internal knowledge; Explicit SCR generates separate internal and context-based answers before performing chain-of-thought confidence reasoning to select the final answer, while Implicit SCR embeds this reasoning implicitly. Rule-Based Confidence Reasoning (RCR) instead extracts numerical confidence signals (token probabilities, self-evaluation scores) and applies predefined decision rules such as thresholding or percentile comparison to choose between the two answers. To further enhance SCR, the authors introduce Confidence Reasoning DPO (CR-DPO), which trains the model via direct preference optimization on self-sampled pairs of correct and incorrect confidence reasoning paths, using dual sampling with different in-context examples to encourage reasoning diversity and improve generalization to unseen datasets.

## Results

The paper evaluates situated faithfulness using Overall (OV) accuracy, defined as the average of accuracy given true contexts (TR) and accuracy given false contexts (FA). For GPT-4o, ExplicitSCR achieves 76.4% OV and ImplicitSCR achieves 78.1% OV, outperforming Direct Input Augmentation (53.9%) by +24.2% and the best rule-based method InternalConf (74.3%) by +3.8%. For GPT-4o mini, ExplicitSCR reaches 69.5% OV versus 51.7% for DIA (+17.8%). For the smaller Llama-3-8B, rule-based InternalConf (58.7% OV) outperforms ExplicitSCR (53.5%), but CR-DPO fine-tuning boosts ExplicitSCR to 62.4% OV (+8.9% over untrained SCR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TACS(LR) (Yu et al., 2024), both SCR methods substantially outperform: GPT-4o's ImplicitSCR achieves 78.1% OV versus TACS's 55.1%, a gap of +23.0%, because LLMs struggle to effectively filter untruthful sentences without in-distribution training. Against TPC/Token Probability Correction (Wu et al., 2024, ClashEval), SCR also wins clearly: GPT-4o's ImplicitSCR (78.1%) beats TPC (73.0%) by +5.1%, and GPT-4o mini's ExplicitSCR (69.5%) beats TPC (58.4%) by +11.1%. Against ActiveRAG/InternalConf (Jiang et al., 2023), the gap is smaller but consistent: GPT-4o's ImplicitSCR (78.1%) edges out InternalConf (74.3%) by +3.8%, while for Llama-3-8B the relationship inverts—InternalConf (58.7%) beats untrained ExplicitSCR (53.5%) by +5.2%, though CR-DPO fine-tuning reverses this to 62.4% vs. 58.7%. Overall, SCR dominates for strong-reasoning models (GPT-4o series) while RCR remains competitive for smaller models, and no single method wins across all model sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) by extending it to confidence reasoning paths with chain-of-thought, and on iterative reasoning preference optimization (Pang et al., 2024) for the loss formulation. Conceptually, it departs from prior knowledge-conflict work (Xie et al., 2023; Longpre et al., 2021) and concurrent benchmarks (ClashEval, Wu et al., 2024; FaithEval, Ming et al., 2024) by formalizing 'situated faithfulness' with explicit metrics (Acc_t, Acc_f, SF) and proposing a two-class taxonomy of solutions—self-guided (SCR) versus rule-based (RCR) confidence reasoning—rather than focusing solely on measurement. The CR-DPO method also differentiates from KnowPO (Zhang et al., 2024), which applies DPO to final answers only, by incorporating full CoT reasoning paths into the preference optimization objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first comprehensive benchmark for situated faithfulness across six diverse QA datasets, including the novel RedditQA dataset with human-written incorrect contexts, filling a gap where prior work relied exclusively on synthetically generated errors. The finding that the optimal strategy (SCR vs. RCR) depends on model capability, and that self-sampled reasoning paths outperform those from stronger models for fine-tuning smaller LLMs, offers practical guidance for practitioners deploying RAG systems. Open questions remain around long-form QA with partial conflicts, multi-context RAG settings, and the computational overhead of CoT-based confidence reasoning at inference time.
