## Problem

No prior work addressed fair clustering in the sliding window model, despite efficient sublinear-space algorithms existing for both standard clustering in sliding windows and fair clustering in the insertion-only streaming model. This paper reveals a striking separation: any algorithm for fair (k,z)-clustering in the sliding window model that achieves any finite multiplicative approximation (or additive Δ/2−1 error) requires Ω(n) space, meaning fairness and recency are fundamentally incompatible in sublinear space. The paper then shows that relaxing the fairness constraint by a multiplicative (1+ε) factor restores the existence of a (1+ε)-approximate algorithm using poly(kε^{-1} log n) space.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a stream of points in [Δ]^d, each tagged with a unique time step and a subset of group IDs from ℓ possible groups, along with a pre-specified window size m, fairness bounds (α,β)∈[0,1]^ℓ, and cluster count k. After each insertion it outputs a k-center set that is a (1+ε)-approximation to the optimal ((1−ε)α,(1+ε)β)-fair clustering on the last m points, and simultaneously maintains a weighted coreset of the window whose size is L·poly(ε^{-1}kd log Δ) where L≤2^ℓ is the number of distinct group combinations present. Points are distinguished by time step, so two geometrically identical points arriving at different times are treated as distinct elements.

## Method

The algorithm builds on a merge-and-reduce framework that processes the stream in reverse time order, maintaining a hierarchy of blocks B_0,…,B_ℓ where each block is an online assignment-preserving coreset of a doubling-sized prefix. The key subroutine RINGCORESET (Algorithm 2) samples points from annular rings around bicriteria approximate centers (obtained via the Meyerson sketch) with probability proportional to 1/i, yielding an online coreset whose prefix property tolerates a 1±ε relative weight perturbation—a relaxation necessary to overcome the lower bound. The final fair clustering solution is obtained by partitioning the window coreset by group-combination, taking the union of per-partition coresets, and solving the relaxed ((1−ε)α,(1+ε)β)-fair clustering problem on the resulting set. The lower bound (Theorem 1.1) is proved by reducing the Augmented Index communication problem to fair clustering, showing that distinguishing whether a window contains n or n+1 points at a particular location encodes the index bit.

## Results

The paper evaluates fair k-MEDIAN in the sliding window model on five real datasets (Adult, Bank, Diabetes, Athlete, Census) with k=10. The proposed algorithm uses substantially smaller coresets than the accuracy benchmark (e.g., 150 vs. 231 on Adult, 1500 vs. 1988 on Census) while achieving comparable clustering cost. Compared to uniform sampling at the same coreset size, the method achieves lower variance in cost. Against the Borassi sliding-window baseline (which ignores fairness), the proposed method delivers better accuracy and faster running time, particularly on larger datasets like Census (2.5M points).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Three baselines are compared: (1) Benchmark, a large coreset serving as an accuracy upper bound; (2) Uniform, uniform sampling at the same coreset size as the proposed method; and (3) Borassi, a prior sliding-window k-clustering algorithm that does not enforce fairness constraints. Against Benchmark, the proposed method achieves comparable fair k-MEDIAN cost while using 35–40% fewer points in the coreset (e.g., 150 vs. 231 on Adult, 1500 vs. 1988 on Census). Against Uniform sampling, the proposed method achieves comparable mean cost but with noticeably lower variance across time steps, as expected since uniform sampling can have unbounded variance. Against Borassi, the proposed method wins on both accuracy (lower fair clustering cost) and running time, with the running-time advantage becoming more pronounced on larger datasets such as Athlete (200k) and Census (2.5M), as shown in Figure 2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: fair clustering (Chierichetti et al. 2017; Braverman et al. 2022) and sliding window clustering (Woodruff et al. 2023; Borassi et al. 2020). It builds directly on the assignment-preserving coreset framework of Braverman et al. (2022) for fair clustering and the online coreset / merge-and-reduce framework of Woodruff et al. (2023) for sliding window clustering, combining them via a Meyerson sketch (Borassi et al. 2020) for bicriteria approximation. The key departure is the discovery that these two lines of work are fundamentally incompatible: while each achieves near-optimal (1+ε) approximations in polylog space in their respective settings, their combination in the sliding window model is impossible in sublinear space, requiring a relaxation of the fairness constraint by a (1+ε) factor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The impossibility result (Theorem 1.1) establishes a clean separation between insertion-only streaming and sliding window models for fair clustering, with the informal message that 'fairness and recency are not compatible in sublinear space.' This has direct practical implications for GDPR-compliant data processing where organizations must forget old data while maintaining fair clustering. The positive result shows that a minimal (1+ε) relaxation of fairness constraints restores tractability, providing a principled trade-off for practitioners. Open questions include whether the (1+ε) relaxation is tight, whether other fairness notions (individual fairness, social fairness) exhibit similar separations, and whether the coreset-based approach can be made more efficient in practice beyond the APX-hardness barrier.
