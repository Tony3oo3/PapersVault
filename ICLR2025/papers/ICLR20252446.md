## Problem

Pure Transformer language models suffer from quadratic KV-cache memory growth and slow per-token inference at long contexts, while pure state-space models like Mamba are more efficient but struggle with in-context learning and format adherence. No production-grade hybrid Attention-SSM architecture had been demonstrated at scale (tens of billions of active parameters) with competitive benchmark performance, and the interaction between such hybrids and mixture-of-experts capacity scaling was unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252446/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Jamba is a decoder-only model that consumes text sequences up to 256K tokens and autoregressively generates text tokens. Because only 1 out of every 8 layers is an attention layer (1:7 ratio), the KV cache is roughly 8× smaller than a vanilla Transformer of equivalent depth, enabling 256K-token contexts on a single 8×80GB GPU machine. At each token, the MoE router selects top-2 of 16 experts per applicable layer, so only ~94B of 398B total parameters are active per forward pass in the Large variant.

## Method

Jamba interleaves Transformer attention layers and Mamba SSM layers within repeated Jamba blocks (8 layers per block, 1:7 attention-to-Mamba ratio), with MoE replacing the MLP every 2nd layer (16 experts, top-2 routing, load-balancing and z-loss regularization). Key architectural findings from ablations: (i) the hybrid outperforms both pure Attention and pure Mamba at matched scale, with 1:7 ratio matching 1:3 in quality while being more compute-efficient; (ii) Mamba layers supply implicit positional information, making RoPE unnecessary; (iii) RMSNorm inside Mamba layers is essential to prevent loss spikes at 7B+ scale; (iv) Mamba-1 outperforms Mamba-2 in the hybrid setting because full attention layers already pool long-range information. For efficient serving, the authors introduce ExpertsInt8, which quantizes MoE/MLP weights to INT8 and dequantizes inside the fused vLLM kernel, matching FP8 latency without calibration and enabling 256K-context inference on 8×A100 GPUs.

## Results

On the RULER long-context benchmark, Jamba-1.5-Large achieves a 95.7 average score with a confirmed effective context length of 256K tokens, and Jamba-1.5-Mini achieves 92.6 at the same 256K effective length — the only publicly available models to reach this. On standard academic benchmarks, Jamba-1.5-Large scores 80.0 on MMLU, 93.0 on ARC-Challenge, 87.0 on GSM8K, and 71.3 on HumanEval, while Jamba-1.5-Mini scores 69.7 on MMLU and 62.8 on HumanEval. Both models reduce KV cache memory by roughly an order of magnitude at 256K context (9 GB and 4 GB respectively, versus 80–252 GB for comparable Transformer models). On chatbot evaluations, Jamba-1.5-Large scores 65.4 on Arena-Hard and 48.5 on WildBench.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Llama-3.1, Jamba-1.5-Large clearly wins on long-context: it achieves a 256K effective RULER length (95.7 avg) versus Llama-3.1-70B's 64K (89.6 avg), and Jamba-1.5-Mini (92.6) outperforms Llama-3.1-8B (88.3). On chatbot benchmarks, Jamba-1.5-Large beats Llama-3.1-70B on Arena-Hard (65.4 vs 55.7) and Jamba-1.5-Mini beats Llama-3.1-8B (46.1 vs 21.3), though Jamba trails on some academic benchmarks (MMLU 80.0 vs 83.6, HumanEval 71.3 vs 80.5). Against Mixtral, Jamba-1.5-Mini has comparable active parameters to Mixtral 8x7B (12B vs 12.9B) but a dramatically smaller KV cache (4 GB vs 32 GB at 256K) and a higher RULER score (92.6 vs 80.4); Jamba-1.5-Large (95.7) also far exceeds Mixtral 8x22B (81.9) on RULER. Against Mistral-Large-2 123B, Jamba-1.5-Large trails on several academic benchmarks (MMLU 80.0 vs 82.5, GPQA 36.9 vs 40.7, HumanEval 71.3 vs 92.0, Arena-Hard 65.4 vs 70.4) but dominates on long-context (RULER 95.7 vs 80.5, effective length 256K vs 32K) and KV cache memory (9 GB vs 88 GB). Overall, Jamba trades a small gap in peak academic benchmark scores for a large advantage in long-context capability, memory efficiency, and throughput.

## Relation to Prior Work

Jamba combines three well-established architectural components—Transformer attention (Vaswani et al., 2017), Mamba state-space layers (Gu & Dao, 2023), and mixture-of-experts (Shazeer et al., 2017; Fedus et al., 2022)—into a single hybrid decoder. Prior attempts to interleave attention and SSM layers (Zuo et al., 2022; Pilault et al., 2023; H3/Fu et al., 2022; StripedHyena/Poli et al., 2023b) were limited to models under 7B parameters and did not incorporate MoE. Jamba departs from this line by scaling the hybrid to production grade (94B active parameters), adding MoE to a subset of MLP layers, and demonstrating that Mamba-1 outperforms Mamba-2 in the hybrid setting—a finding that contrasts with Mamba-2's reported standalone superiority.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first open-weight production-grade Attention-SSM hybrid, Jamba provides a concrete, reproducible blueprint for building efficient long-context models, with 256K effective context and an 8× smaller KV cache than comparable Transformers. The ExpertsInt8 quantization technique and the architectural ablations (e.g., 1:7 attention-to-Mamba ratio, no positional encoding needed, MoE every other layer) offer practical guidance for future hybrid model design. Open questions remain about whether in-context learning is truly emergent in SSMs or merely rescued by the attention layers, whether the Mamba-1-over-Mamba-2 finding persists at even larger scales, and how the architecture behaves under continued pre-training or domain adaptation.
