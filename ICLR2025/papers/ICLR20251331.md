## Problem

Existing antibody design methods generate CDR sequences de novo without template constraints, which demands large training data and produces unnatural sequences due to the limited SAbDab dataset (<10K structures). This paper addresses the gap by introducing structural homologous motifs as retrieval-augmented conditions to guide a diffusion model toward functionally plausible CDR sequences, reducing reliance on exhaustive training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251331/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a fixed antibody-antigen complex structure (backbone Cα coordinates and orientations) as the geometric scaffold, along with a set of top-15 CDR-like fragments retrieved from the full PDB via the MASTER algorithm (matched by backbone RMSD with length-dependent thresholds from 0.4 Å to 1.0 Å). The output is a 20-category amino acid sequence for the target CDR region, generated iteratively over 100 diffusion timesteps while the backbone structure remains frozen. ESM2 650M embeddings of the partially-noised antibody sequence provide evolutionary context at each denoising step.

## Method

RADAb employs a dual-branch denoising architecture within a DDPM framework. The global branch uses six Invariant Point Attention layers conditioned on ESM2 sequence embeddings to capture antigen-antibody geometric context, producing a per-residue probability vector r_global. The local branch constructs a pseudo-MSA matrix (antibody framework + noisy CDR in row 1, retrieved CDR-like sequences in rows 2–16) and processes it with CDR-focused Axial Attention using a tied-row mechanism from MSA Transformer to extract homologous evolutionary signals, yielding r_local. A skip connection adds the two probability representations before softmax, fusing global structural context with local template information. Training minimizes KL divergence between the predicted and true posterior at each timestep, with separate training for CDR-H3 (100K iterations) and the five conserved CDRs (250K iterations). The framework is generative-model agnostic, demonstrated with DiffAb as the backbone.

## Results

On the SAbDab test set (63 antibody-antigen complexes), RADAb achieves 57.02% AAR on CDR-H3 inverse folding, outperforming the best baseline Diffab-fix (49.17%) by 7.85 percentage points and AbMPNN (52.99%) by 4.03 points. On the more challenging long CDR-H3 subset (length > 14), RADAb reaches 51.35% AAR vs. 43.27% for AbMPNN (the next best), an 8.08% absolute gain. For functionality optimization, RADAb achieves a ΔΔG-seq of 7.06 cal/mol (vs. 14.05 for Diffab-fix) and 37.30% IMP-seq, indicating the highest fraction of designed sequences with improved binding energy over the native antibody.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RADAb outperforms all four deep learning baselines across every CDR region and every metric in Table 1. Against Diffab-fix (the strongest diffusion-based baseline), RADAb gains +7.85% AAR on CDR-H3 (57.02 vs. 49.17) and +8.08% on long CDR-H3 (51.35 vs. 42.26), while also achieving the lowest scRMSD (2.23 vs. 2.24) and highest plausibility (−0.530 vs. −0.541). Against AbMPNN (the best antibody-specific inverse folding model), RADAb leads by +4.03% AAR on CDR-H3 and substantially reduces scRMSD (2.23 vs. 2.80) and improves plausibility (−0.530 vs. −0.675). ProteinMPNN and ESM-IF1 are consistently the weakest baselines, with ESM-IF1 trailing RADAb by over 27% AAR on CDR-H3. In the functionality optimization task (Table 3), RADAb halves the ΔΔG-seq of the next-best method Diffab-fix (7.06 vs. 14.06 cal/mol) and achieves the highest IMP-seq (37.30% vs. 34.52%), confirming that retrieval-augmented generation produces sequences with measurably better binding affinity.

## Relation to Prior Work

RADAb builds directly on DiffAb (Luo et al., 2022) as its diffusion backbone for antibody CDR inverse folding, and on MASTER (Zhou & Grigoryan, 2015) for structural fragment retrieval from the PDB. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of retrieval-augmented generation into antibody design: rather than generating sequences purely from the antigen-antibody context, the model conditions on a set of structurally homologous CDR-like fragments retrieved from non-antibody proteins, injecting evolutionary information via a dual-branch denoising architecture (global IPA + local tied-row axial attention inspired by MSA Transformer). This positions the work at the intersection of template-based rational design (fragment grafting) and modern generative modeling, explicitly targeting the data-scarcity limitation of SAbDab-trained models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The retrieval-augmented framework offers a practical path around the fundamental data bottleneck in antibody design (fewer than 10K SAbDab structures), by leveraging the much larger PDB for evolutionary signal without requiring additional training data. The generative-model-agnostic design means the retrieval module can be plugged into any diffusion-based protein generator, broadening its applicability. However, the method has not been validated in wet-lab experiments, and the reliance on known CDR backbone structure precludes its use in de novo or co-design settings without risking data leakage. Extending the retrieval mechanism to protein-protein interaction (PPI) retrieval for full co-design is identified as a key open direction.
