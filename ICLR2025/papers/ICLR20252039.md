## Problem

Existing pixel-aligned implicit models for 3D clothed human reconstruction (e.g., PIFuHD, ICON, ECON) degrade severely when input images contain occlusions—whether external (inter-person, object-caused) or internal (self-occlusion, non-frontal pose). Prior robustness efforts (ECON, Wang et al.) assume a groundtruth SMPL-X mesh is available, which is impractical in real-world settings, and none address internal occlusions. The paper tackles the open problem of reconstructing accurate clothed human meshes from single images with arbitrary occlusions, without requiring a pre-supplied body model.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (512×512) that may contain up to three human subjects with external or internal occlusions, plus an optional SMPL-X mesh (used to derive multi-layered normal maps N_S for conditioning). It produces a 3D clothed human mesh extracted via Marching Cubes from a 128³ occupancy grid, refined by a differential optimizer against 8 predicted normal maps. The intermediate outputs include a disentangled image (one per subject) and 8 multi-layered normal maps (N_C) covering front/back/left/right camera directions at two depth layers (outer and inner). An optional Refining Diffuser can super-resolve N_C to 1024×1024 for finer mesh detail.

## Method

Robust-PIFu is a four-stage pipeline built on fine-tuned latent diffusion models (initialized from the Stable Diffusion / Zero-1-to-3 pretrained weights on LAION-5B). Stage 1, the Disentangling Diffuser (D), uses a binary Human Subject Selection Map and CLIP cross-attention to isolate individual subjects from external occlusions. Stage 2, the Penetrating Diffuser (P), is run twice to generate 8 multi-layered normal maps (4 front/back, then 4 left/right conditioned on the first 4), bypassing self-occlusion and non-frontal pose. Stage 3, the Layered-Normals Pixel-aligned Implicit Model, incorporates these maps through two streams: (a) channel-concatenating N_S, N_C, and the disentangled image into a stacked hourglass encoder, and (b) constructing a 24×128×128×128 Multi-dimensional Layered Normals Grid (each cell holding 8×3 normal vectors) processed by a 3D-CNN with a skip connection into the occupancy-prediction MLP. Stage 4 (optional), the Refining Diffuser (R), super-resolves each of the 8 normal maps using a noised normal map and masked SMPL-X normal maps as conditioning. All diffusion components are fine-tuned with AdamW (lr=1e-4, batch 72, 200 DDIM steps at inference); the implicit model is trained with RMSprop (lr=1e-3) minimizing occupancy MSE.

## Results

Robust-PIFu achieves the best Chamfer Distance (CD) and Point-to-Surface (P2S) scores across all three evaluation datasets. On THuman2.0, it reaches CD 8.426 and P2S 8.000 (×10⁻⁵); on BUFF, CD 6.127 and P2S 6.525 (×10²); and on MultiHuman, CD 13.38 and P2S 11.66 (×10⁻⁵). The closest competitor, SeSDF, trails by approximately 0.5 in CD and 0.4 in P2S on THuman2.0, and by about 0.45 in CD on BUFF. Notably, Wang et al. degrades severely on MultiHuman (CD 21.01, P2S 21.64), highlighting its dependence on a groundtruth SMPL-X mesh that is unavailable in multi-person scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Robust-PIFu outperforms all five baselines on every metric across all three datasets. The strongest competitor is SeSDF, which is closest on THuman2.0 (CD 8.941 vs. 8.426) and BUFF (CD 6.582 vs. 6.127), but still trails Robust-PIFu by roughly 6–7% in CD. ECON, despite its explicit shape-completion module for inter-person occlusion, is the weakest on THuman2.0 (CD 11.17) and BUFF (CD 9.064), confirming the paper's critique that its reliance on a groundtruth SMPL-X mesh limits practical robustness. Wang et al. performs acceptably on THuman2.0 (CD 8.944) but collapses on MultiHuman (CD 21.01, P2S 21.64), nearly doubling the error of Robust-PIFu, because its pipeline cannot function without an accurate SMPL-X prior in multi-person settings. D-IF and ICON show moderate performance but are consistently outperformed by Robust-PIFu by 1.5–3.3 in CD on THuman2.0 and by 2.4–3.3 on MultiHuman.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pixel-aligned implicit model lineage (PIFu → PIFuHD → ICON → ECON) by repurposing the Zero-1-to-3 insight—that pretrained latent diffusion models acquire 3D conceptual understanding—to solve occlusion rather than novel-view synthesis. It departs from prior occlusion-robust methods (ECON, Wang et al.) by eliminating their reliance on a groundtruth SMPL-X mesh, instead using a Disentangling Diffuser to remove external occlusions and a Penetrating Diffuser to generate multi-layered normal maps that bypass self-occlusion. The Layered-Normals Pixel-aligned Implicit Model then integrates these layered maps via a novel Multi-dimensional Layered Normals Grid (a 3D visual-hull-like structure) alongside a standard concatenation stream, extending the PIFu/ICON architecture in a principled way.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real-world single images frequently contain occlusions that cause existing SOTA methods to produce broken or structurally inaccurate meshes, and prior robustness solutions required an unrealistic groundtruth SMPL-X assumption. The multi-layered normal map representation is a reusable intermediate that could benefit other 3D human reconstruction pipelines. Key open questions include the hallucination risk inherent to generative occlusion filling (e.g., inventing wrinkles on unseen body parts), the absence of a large-scale dedicated occlusion dataset for training, and whether finer-grained control over how occlusions are resolved (e.g., specifying the pose of a reconstructed hand) is feasible. The modest but consistent quantitative gains across three datasets suggest the approach is sound but not yet transformative.
