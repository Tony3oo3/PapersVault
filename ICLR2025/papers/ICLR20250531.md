## Problem

Fully homomorphic encrypted neural networks (FHENNs) face prohibitive latency when scaling to large images and deep architectures, primarily due to the computational cost of non-linear activations and homomorphic bootstrapping operations in the encrypted domain. Prior TFHE-based methods like SHE required 2.5 days for ResNet-18 inference on ImageNet, and CKKS-based methods struggle to scale beyond small networks due to error accumulation from polynomial activation approximations. The paper asks whether operating in the frequency domain can reduce these dominant costs while maintaining or improving classification accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images (or JPEG-encoded images already in DCT form) and converts them to YCrCb color space before applying 2D-DCT with 8×8 blocks (yielding 64 frequency components per block) for ImageNet-sized inputs or 4×4 blocks for smaller inputs like CIFAR-10. A fixed subset of low-frequency channels is retained (6, 24, 48, 64, or 192), with luma (Y) components emphasized over chroma. The encrypted model outputs a class label; in the MLaaS deployment, the user decrypts the penultimate layer output locally and applies a lightweight classifier to obtain the final prediction.

## Method

DCT-CryptoNets replaces spatial-domain RGB processing with frequency-domain DCT coefficients, reducing spatial dimensions by a factor of 8 (e.g., 224×224 → 56×56) while increasing channel count, which disproportionately reduces ReLU operations concentrated in early high-spatial layers. The ResNet architecture is minimally modified: the first convolution uses stride-1 instead of stride-2, and the ReLU after the first channel-widening layer is pruned (removing ~35% of ReLUs with <0.5% accuracy loss). The network is trained via quantization-aware training (4-bit for CIFAR-10/mini-ImageNet/Imagenette, 5-bit for ImageNet) and then encrypted into TFHE using programmable bootstrapping (PBS) to implement exact non-linear activations without polynomial approximation. This reduces both ReLU count and the number of PBS operations needed to reset ciphertext noise, yielding up to 5.3× latency reduction on ImageNet (8,562s vs. 45,000s normalized) while narrowing encrypted-accuracy confidence intervals from ±2.5% to ±1.0%.

## Results

On ImageNet (ResNet-18), DCT-CryptoNets with 64×56² DCT input achieves 66.3% top-1 accuracy with 8,562s inference latency, a 5.3× speedup over SHE (45,000s normalized to 96 threads) at comparable accuracy (66.8%). On CIFAR-10, the 24×16² DCT variant reaches 90.5% accuracy in 565s. The method also narrows the encrypted-accuracy confidence interval from ±2.5% (RGB) to ±1.0% (DCT) on ImageNet, and reduces total homomorphic operations by 33.1% at 224×224 and 44.1% at 448×448 resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SHE (Lou & Jiang, 2019), the only other published TFHE method scaling to ImageNet, DCT-CryptoNets achieves a 5.3× latency reduction (8,562s vs. 45,000s normalized) at nearly identical accuracy (66.3% vs. 66.8%); on CIFAR-10 ResNet-18, DCT-CryptoNets is 1.4× faster (1,746s vs. 2,509s) with a 2.3% accuracy trade-off. Compared to CKKS-based methods (Kim & Guyot 2023, Ran et al. 2023, Rovida & Leporati 2024), DCT-CryptoNets has higher per-image latency on CIFAR-10 (565–1,339s vs. 336–392s) but is the only method that scales to full ImageNet inference, whereas CKKS methods are limited to small networks due to polynomial approximation error accumulation. Against other TFHE baselines, DCT-CryptoNets substantially outperforms Stoian et al. (2023) in both speed (565s vs. 3,000s normalized) and accuracy (90.5% vs. 87.5%), and Benamira et al. (2023) in accuracy (90.5% vs. 74.0%). Older BGV/FV-RNS methods (Hesamifard 2017, Chou 2018) are 10–20× slower and less accurate. The DCT-specific speedup over the RGB variant of DCT-CryptoNets alone is 1.7×–2.4× across model sizes, isolating the frequency-domain contribution from the TFHE/PBS architectural improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DCT-Net frequency-domain learning framework (Xu et al., 2020) and the TFHE/programmable bootstrapping cryptographic stack (Chillotti et al., 2019, 2021; Bergerat et al., 2023), but departs from prior FHENN work in a key conceptual way: rather than optimizing convolutions in the frequency domain (as in Falcon or Kim & Guyot's HDFT) or approximating activations with polynomials (CKKS-based methods), it learns features directly from DCT coefficients, reducing spatial dimensionality and thereby cutting the number of ReLUs and bootstrapping operations. This positions DCT-CryptoNets as an architectural intervention for FHENNs rather than a cryptographic or convolutional optimization, and it is the first to retain encrypted data in the frequency domain throughout the entire CNN inference pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5.3× ImageNet speedup (2.5 h vs. 12.5 h) and the narrower encrypted-accuracy confidence intervals (±1.0% vs. ±2.5%) make private inference on high-resolution images substantially more practical, and the scalability argument—that DCT's HOP reduction grows with image size (33% → 44%)—suggests the approach becomes more valuable as real-world image resolutions increase. Key limitations remain: cryptographic hyperparameter selection (precision rounding, PBS error probability) is still manual and task-dependent, and the accuracy benefits of DCT are less pronounced on small images where high-frequency content is already sparse. Open questions include whether the frequency-domain formulation extends to other modalities (video, medical imaging) and whether automated hyperparameter search can replace the current empirical tuning.
