## Problem

Current LLM-based agents require explicit task specifications—such as user instructions or reward functions—to generate behavior, which constrains their autonomy and limits behavioral diversity. This paper asks whether an agent can instead be motivated by a multi-dimensional system of intrinsic desires (inspired by Maslow's Theory of Needs) to autonomously propose and select daily activities, producing sequences that are more natural, coherent, and plausible than those from goal-reasoning or characteristic-driven baselines.

## Input / Output

At each 20-minute time step, the agent receives its current qualitative desire descriptions (11 dimensions for indoor, 14 for outdoor, each on a 0–10 Likert scale), the environment description, its personality profile, and all past activities and observations. It outputs a single activity for that step; after T steps, an LLM rewrites the full sequence into a coherent narrative for fair cross-agent comparison. The desire values are initialized randomly in [0,10] while expected values are derived deterministically from the profile's trait–degree pairs via a predefined mapping (e.g., 'extremely sociable' → social connectivity expected value of 9).

## Method

The D2A framework comprises two modules: a Value System and a Desire-driven Planner. The Value System maintains numerical desire values per dimension, applies a probability-based decay at each step to model natural desire fluctuation, converts values into qualitative text descriptions via LLM prompts (critical for the agent to interpret its state), and updates values after each action using a reflection-and-correction loop. The Desire-driven Planner, inspired by Tree of Thoughts, proposes N candidate activities (default N=3) conditioned on the current desire descriptions, then evaluates each by prompting the LLM to imagine the resulting desire states, and finally selects the activity whose forecasted state best aligns with the agent's expected values. Notably, the framework deliberately omits a multi-step planning module, as ablation shows that pre-scheduled plans reduce responsiveness to real-time desire changes. The entire system is prompt-based (no fine-tuning), built on Concordia for environment interaction, and validated with LLaMA3.1-70B and Qwen2.5-72B backbones.

## Results

The paper evaluates D2A using two primary metrics: Likelihood (pairwise win rate judged by GPT-4o on naturalness, coherence, and plausibility) and Dissatisfaction (sum of positive gaps between current and expected desire values). In the indoor environment, D2A achieves the highest win rate against all three baselines (ReAct, BabyAGI, LLMob) in the pairwise comparison heatmap (Figure 3). In the Random-8-Steps experiment, D2A exhibits significantly lower mean dissatisfaction and smaller standard deviations than all baselines over 8 time steps (Figure 4a). In the Fixed-12-Steps experiment, D2A produces dissatisfaction trajectories closest to human-annotated sequences, though the gap to baselines narrows compared to the random initialization setting (Figure 4b). D2A also generalizes to the outdoor multi-agent environment, maintaining lower dissatisfaction and higher win rates (Figures 5 and 13).

## Baselines & Comparisons

D2A outperforms all three baselines on both Likelihood (win rate) and Dissatisfaction metrics across indoor and outdoor environments. Against ReAct (goal-reasoning), D2A wins because ReAct's lack of structured planning diminishes coherence and naturalness, despite reasonable plausibility from its reasoning step. Against BabyAGI (characteristic-driven task list), D2A wins because BabyAGI's sequential task generation leads to repetitive, low-diversity behaviors (e.g., repeatedly enhancing hunger-related activities), reducing overall human-likeness. Against LLMob (profile + motivation planning), D2A wins because LLMob's characteristic-oriented approach produces activities with weak inter-activity connections, hurting coherence. On Dissatisfaction, D2A shows the lowest mean and variance in the Random-8-Steps experiment, indicating more rational desire-satisfying behavior. In the Fixed-12-Steps experiment (uniform initialization at 5), D2A does not significantly outperform baselines but remains closest to human-annotated sequences. The advantage is consistent in the outdoor multi-agent setting, where D2A also autonomously engages NPCs to satisfy higher-level social desires.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Concordia as its simulation backbone and departs from the dominant paradigm in LLM-based agents (ReAct, BabyAGI, AutoGPT, Voyager) that rely on explicit goal decomposition or characteristic-driven action selection. Its core conceptual contribution is reframing agent motivation through Maslow's Theory of Needs, replacing external task instructions with an intrinsic multi-dimensional desire system that drives activity generation. The activity proposal-evaluation-selection mechanism borrows the Tree of Thoughts paradigm, but applies it to desire-state forecasting rather than reasoning chains. It also extends the human daily activity simulation literature (OpenSHS, Generative Agents, LMA3) by providing a flexible, multi-agent-capable text-based simulator with a principled motivational framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: most LLM agents require explicit task specifications, limiting their autonomy and behavioral diversity in open-ended settings. The desire-driven framework offers a psychologically grounded alternative that could improve virtual agents, intelligent assistants, and game NPCs by producing more natural and varied behavior. However, the desire dynamics are modeled with a simplistic linear decay, the dimension set is heuristic and environment-specific, and the entire pipeline depends on LLMs for value interpretation—raising questions about scalability, robustness, and whether the framework captures genuine human motivational complexity. Open questions include whether richer causal structures among desires, adaptive planning, or model-agnostic value representations could close the remaining gap to human behavior.
