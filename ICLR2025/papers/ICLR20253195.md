## Problem

Existing cellular solid design methods are restricted to translational symmetry of a single pore unit cell, leaving the much larger space of crystallographically symmetric pore arrangements (17 wallpaper groups in 2D) unexplored. Representing and optimizing over this expanded design space is challenging because space groups are countably infinite, making standard symmetrization tricks inapplicable, and because shape optimization must simultaneously preserve both symmetry and volume fraction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253195/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a G-invariant reference binary shape s₀ (a 2D cross-section tiled by a space group G over a 7×7 domain at 50% volume fraction), a chosen wallpaper group G, and a target mechanical property (e.g., a target stress-strain curve or a target effective Poisson's ratio at ε=0.1). It produces optimized neural network parameters θ that define a divergence-free, G-equivariant flow deforming s₀ into a new cellular solid geometry, represented as a triangular mesh with ≥25,000 degrees of freedom. The volume fraction is strictly preserved throughout optimization, and boundary conditions are enforced via an envelope function that drives the flow to zero at the domain edges.

## Method

The core construction defines the geometry implicitly as s_θ(x) = s₀(F_θ(x, t_max)), where F_θ is the solution to an ODE driven by H(x,t) = Γ(Λ(h_θ(ρ(x),t))). Here h_θ is a small fully-connected network (2 hidden layers of size 10, tanh), ρ maps R² onto a 2D torus via cos/sin of lattice-normalized coordinates (a maximal invariant for the translation subgroup T_G), Λ converts the network output into a divergence-free vector field via a curl-like operator, and Γ symmetrizes over the finite quotient group Ĝ = G/T_G (at most a handful of elements per wallpaper group). Theorem 1 proves that this two-step decomposition—torus invariance for translations plus finite averaging over Ĝ—yields full G-equivariance despite G being infinite. The resulting flow is coupled end-to-end with a differentiable isogeometric-analysis (IGA) solver for nearly incompressible neo-Hookean elasticity (E=50 MPa, ν=0.46), implemented in JAX with adjoint-based backpropagation, so that gradients of a mechanical loss (e.g., mismatch to a target stress-strain curve or Poisson's ratio) flow through both the ODE integration and the nonlinear FEM solve. Optimization uses ADAM (lr=0.001) with multiple random initializations, taking ~60 s per step on a single RTX 2080 Ti GPU.

## Results

When targeting a negative Poisson's ratio of ν_ef = −0.5 under uniaxial tension, the p4 symmetry group achieves the best result of ν_ef = −0.45, followed by p2mg at −0.40, while the standard translational symmetry (p1) reaches only −0.05. For linear force-displacement responses under tension, the p2gg group successfully learns linear responses for all target stiffness ratios β from 0.1 to 1.4, whereas p1 is limited to β ≤ 1.1. Real-world fabrication validates the simulations: the pg design yields ν_ex = −0.14 (simulation: −0.12) and the p4 design yields ν_ex = −0.49 (simulation: −0.45) under pulling, confirming strong agreement between predicted and measured auxetic behavior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external baseline methods in the traditional sense; instead, it compares performance across the 17 two-dimensional wallpaper (space) groups, with p1 (pure translational symmetry) serving as the de facto baseline representing all existing cellular solid design approaches. For negative Poisson's ratio under tension, six of the eight best-performing groups (p2mg, p4, p3, p6, p2gg, pg) outperform p1, with p4 achieving ν_ef = −0.45 versus p1's −0.05 — a nearly 9× improvement toward the target of −0.5. For linear stress-strain responses under tension, p2gg extends the achievable range to β = 1.4 compared to p1's limit of β = 1.1. Interestingly, for the more challenging uniaxial compression task (plateau force with tunable critical strain), p1 is the only symmetry group that successfully matches the target, suggesting that higher-order symmetries are not universally advantageous. The real-world experiments confirm that the simulation-predicted auxetic behavior transfers to fabricated prototypes with less than 0.05 absolute error in Poisson's ratio.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cellular solids inverse-design literature (Overvelde & Bertoldi 2014; Wang et al. 2014; Xue & Mao 2022; Medina et al. 2023; Du et al. 2024), which has been restricted to translational symmetry of a single pore unit cell, by generalizing the design space to all 17 two-dimensional wallpaper groups. The technical machinery draws on three recent threads: crystallographically equivariant neural network constructions (Adams & Orbanz 2023), divergence-free vector field parameterizations (Richter-Powell et al. 2022), and end-to-end differentiable isogeometric mechanics solvers (Oktay 2024). The key conceptual departure is using a symmetry-preserving, volume-preserving neural flow to deform a reference shape, rather than directly optimizing pore geometry via level sets or moving-mesh methods, which avoids disconnected-region pathologies and guarantees volume conservation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is demonstrated by the fact that p4 symmetry achieves an effective Poisson's ratio of −0.45 under tension (validated experimentally at −0.49), whereas the best translational-symmetry (p1) design reaches only −0.05 — a nine-fold improvement purely from expanding the symmetry constraint. This suggests that the design space of mechanically functional cellular solids has been substantially underexplored when restricted to translations. Key limitations include the restriction to 2D, the ~60-second per-step optimization cost on a single GPU, and the reliance on a single nearly-incompressible neo-Hookean material model; scaling to 3D crystallographic groups will require learned surrogates or order-reduction techniques. An open question is whether the observed symmetry-performance hierarchy (higher-order groups enabling more extreme behaviors) generalizes to 3D space groups and to other material models.
