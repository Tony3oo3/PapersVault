## Problem

Solving high wavenumber Helmholtz equations in heterogeneous media remains a major challenge: traditional solvers have not achieved satisfactory results, and existing neural operator methods struggle to accurately handle wavenumbers beyond a few hundred. No prior AI solver could match the wavenumber scope (k≈2000) of advanced parallel traditional solvers while offering significantly faster solve times, and specialized iterative AI solvers like encoder-solvers or Wave-ADR-NS require extensive domain-specific knowledge or rely on existing traditional solvers for data generation.

## Input / Output

The solver takes a 2D slowness field κ(x) (interpolated from CIFAR-10 images or real ultrasonic CT data), a source term f(x) (white noise covering all frequencies), and a normalized problem coefficient (ωκh, γ) as input, and produces the complex-valued wave field u(x) on the same uniform grid. Grid sizes range from 256×256 (supervised comparisons) up to 4095×4095 (scalability tests), with at least 10 grid points per wavelength required. The solve phase is designed for iterative application: it can be run once as a single inference or repeatedly within a stationary or GMRES framework until a relative residual tolerance (rtol=1E-7) is met, with the setup phase executed only once per coefficient configuration.

## Method

MGCFNN adapts the MGCNN multigrid-hierarchical architecture and introduces a novel Fourier Neural Network (FNN) at coarse levels of the solve phase. FNN modifies FNO's spectral convolution to handle full Fourier modes (not truncated), uses a small smooth weight tensor interpolated across the frequency domain, and supports larger problem sizes via an IFFT→pad→FFT procedure, making it mathematically equivalent to a CNN with an extended kernel that facilitates global wave propagation. The architecture separates a one-time setup phase (processing slowness and damping coefficients through nonlinear ResCNN blocks with downsampling) from a solve phase where ResFNN blocks at the two coarsest levels and ResCNN blocks at finer levels process the residual iteratively. FNN is mathematically connected to the inverse of the damped Helmholtz operator and analogous to a learnable Born series iteration. The solver is trained unsupervised via a residual-based loss (or supervised via L2 error for fair comparison) and achieves optimal O(k) convergence up to k≈2000, with at least 4.8× speedup over the best prior AI iterative solvers.

## Results

On the scalability benchmark (γ=0, up to 4095×4095 grid, ω=640π≈2000), MGCFNN achieves 4.8×–6.8× speedup over the encoder-solver (Lerer et al., 2024) and 33.5×–116.2× speedup over Wave-ADR-NS (Cui et al., 2024), while also outperforming the sparse direct solver CHOLMOD by 3.1×–33.8×. In supervised learning on a 256×256 grid (ω=80π, κ_min=0.75), MGCFNN achieves the best test error of 0.070 with only 5.3 MB parameters, compared to 0.079 for MgNO and 0.561 for FNO2D. Both MGCFNN and MGCNN exhibit optimal O(k) convergence up to k≈2000, with MGCFNN requiring roughly half the iterations of MGCNN (e.g., 83 vs. 231 iterations at ω=640π as a standalone solver).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against operator-learning baselines in supervised learning (Table 1, κ_min=0.75), MGCFNN (test error 0.070) and MGFNN (0.061) outperform all seven competitors: MgNO (0.079), FNO2D (0.561), MWT2D (0.527), U-NO (0.880), U-Net (0.803), Dil-ResNet (0.606), and LSM (0.783), while also using fewer parameters (5.3 MB vs. 4.6–86.7 MB) and less training time. The gap widens substantially at higher heterogeneity (κ_min=0.25), where MGCFNN reaches 0.458 test error versus 0.633 for MgNO and 0.851 for FNO2D. In the scalability comparison (Table 3), MGCFNN dominates the encoder-solver (Lerer et al., 2024) by 4.8×–6.8× in solve time and the AI-enhanced Wave-ADR-NS (Cui et al., 2024) by 33.5×–116.2×, with the advantage growing at higher wavenumbers. Against the sparse direct solver CHOLMOD, MGCFNN is 3.1× faster at the smallest problem but 33.8× faster at 2047×2047, confirming superior scalability. In the ablation (Table 5), MGCFNN requires roughly half the iterations of MGCNN (e.g., 83 vs. 231 at ω=640π standalone), validating the benefit of the FNN component at coarse levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MGCNN multigrid-hierarchical solver architecture (Xie et al., 2023) to the Helmholtz equation setting, where the original CNN-based design is insufficient for high-frequency wave propagation. The key architectural departure is the replacement of FNO's truncated spectral convolution with a novel Fourier Neural Network (FNN) that retains full-mode information and is mathematically equivalent to a CNN with an extended kernel size, drawing a conceptual bridge to the classical Born series iterative method. The work also positions itself against recent specialized iterative AI solvers (encoder-solver, Wave-ADR-NS) that require domain-specific construction, arguing that the multigrid-hierarchical design with FNN achieves comparable or better convergence without such specialized knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MGCFNN achieves the theoretically optimal O(k) convergence up to k≈2000 while delivering 4.8–33.5× speedups over the most recent AI and AI-enhanced traditional solvers, closing the gap between AI solver speed and traditional solver wavenumber scope. Open questions include handling reflection boundary conditions (which conflict with the periodic assumption of FFT and padding), extending to 3D problems, improving the simplistic frequency-domain interpolation, and resolving the unexpected computational inefficiency of the per-pixel linear map in the frequency domain. The method's reliance on unsupervised learning and its generality across wave equations (demonstrated on Marmousi and ultrasonic CT data) suggest broader applicability beyond the tested Helmholtz setting.
