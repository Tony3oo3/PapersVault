## Problem

Current generative protein design methods operate on fixed backbones and cannot account for protein flexibility, a property critical for enzyme catalysis, substrate specificity, and molecular transport. Learning to predict and engineer flexibility is further hampered by scarce, heterogeneous, and costly data from both experimental and computational sources, with no systematic comparison of available quantification methods or integration into state-of-the-art inverse folding pipelines.

## Input / Output

Flexpert-Seq takes a protein sequence (20 amino acid alphabet) and outputs per-residue RMSF values; Flexpert-3D additionally accepts a backbone structure (4 atoms × 3 coordinates per residue) and ANM-derived flexibility as auxiliary input. Flexpert-Design takes a backbone structure plus a per-residue flexibility instruction vector (real-valued, initialized from native Flexpert-3D predictions and optionally perturbed by a scalar τ over a contiguous segment) and outputs a full protein sequence of matching length. The flexibility instructions are the key control signal: at inference, non-native values steer the generated sequence toward increased or decreased local flexibility.

## Method

Flexpert-Seq fine-tunes the ProtTrans protein language model via LoRA adapters plus a linear regression head to predict per-residue RMSF from sequence alone, trained on the ATLAS MD dataset (1390 proteins, 100 ns × 3 replicas). Flexpert-3D augments this with a CNN adaptor that maps ANM-predicted flexibility into the ProtTrans embedding space, effectively learning to correct ANM's coarse estimates toward MD ground truth. Flexpert-Design modifies ProteinMPNN by adding flexibility instructions to zero-initialized per-residue node features, then fine-tunes with a combined loss L_Flexpert = θ·L_Flex + (1−θ)·L_Seq (θ=0.8), where L_Flex is a regression loss between Flexpert-3D's prediction on the generated sequence and the input flexibility instructions. Differentiability through the discrete amino acid output is preserved via straight-through Gumbel-Softmax sampling, enabling end-to-end training of the inverse folding model conditioned on the flexibility predictor. The key training insight is to first learn flexibility preservation on native pseudolabels, which makes the model sensitive to the flexibility input channel, so that at inference non-native instructions can steer the output toward desired flexibility changes.

## Results

For flexibility prediction, Flexpert-Seq achieves a Pearson correlation coefficient (PCC) of 0.78 with MD-simulated RMSF (upper bound 0.88), outperforming AlphaFold2 pLDDT (0.72) and ESMFold pLDDT (0.58). Flexpert-3D further improves to PCC 0.83 by incorporating backbone structure and ANM features, surpassing ANM (0.76) and GNM (0.76). For flexibility engineering, Flexpert-Design achieves a median enrichment ratio of 1.52 and 83% flexibility-increasing mutations in the targeted segment, compared to 1.07 and 61% for vanilla ProteinMPNN, with only a 1-percentage-point drop in sequence recovery.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Flexpert-Seq outperforms both AlphaFold2 pLDDT (PCC 0.78 vs 0.72) and ESMFold pLDDT (0.78 vs 0.58) in correlating with MD-simulated RMSF on the ATLAS test set, with the gap over ESMFold being particularly large. Flexpert-3D improves further to PCC 0.83, beating both ANM (0.76) and GNM (0.76) while only partially relying on ANM input (PCC 0.78 between Flexpert-3D and ANM, indicating it learns complementary information from sequence). For the engineering task, Flexpert-Design substantially outperforms vanilla ProteinMPNN: the median enrichment ratio rises from 1.07 to 1.52 and the proportion of flexibility-increasing mutations from 61% to 83%. The ablation without L_Flex (1.43, 80%) shows that the flexibility loss provides modest but consistent gains over simply adding flexibility as input. On the mdCATH generalization test, both predictors degrade gracefully with increasing simulation temperature (from 320 K to 450 K), with Flexpert-Seq remaining more robust than Flexpert-3D at higher temperatures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established lines of work: protein language models (ProtTrans) for sequence-level representations, Elastic Network Models (ANM/GNM) for structure-based flexibility estimation, and graph-based inverse folding (ProteinMPNN) for sequence design. It departs from prior flexibility engineering approaches (LoopGrafter, chimeric transplants, MD-based optimization) by reframing the problem as a learnable prediction task followed by differentiable steering of an inverse folding model. The key conceptual departure is using a fast ML flexibility predictor as a differentiable reward signal during inverse folding fine-tuning, rather than relying on slow physics-based simulations or manual loop transplantation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Protein flexibility is a critical determinant of enzyme activity, specificity, and substrate transport, yet current ML-based protein design tools largely ignore it. This paper provides the first systematic comparison of flexibility quantification methods (identifying MD RMSF as the best learning target with a 0.88 PCC upper bound) and demonstrates that inverse folding models can be steered toward increased flexibility in specified regions. However, the acknowledged inability to reliably decrease flexibility (Table 10 shows near-baseline performance) limits practical utility, and the modest gain from the L_Flex loss over simply adding flexibility as input suggests the current training signal is not yet the bottleneck. Future work on tighter bidirectional flexibility control and integration with discrete flow matching models is needed before this becomes a broadly deployable design tool.
