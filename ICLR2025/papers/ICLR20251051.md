## Problem

While factuality hallucination (deviation from world knowledge) has been extensively benchmarked, faithfulness hallucination—where a model's response misaligns with the provided context—remains underexplored, particularly when contexts are noisy, contradictory, or fabricated. Existing benchmarks either conflate factuality with faithfulness, lack multi-paragraph contextual structure, or do not simulate the full range of retrieval failures (missing evidence, conflicting sources, counterfactual claims) that RAG systems encounter in practice.

## Input / Output

Each FaithEval sample pairs a question with a multi-paragraph context passage (3–5 paragraphs with distractor content) and a ground-truth answer. For Unanswerable and Inconsistent Context tasks, the expected output is a specific abstention phrase ('unknown' or 'conflict'), evaluated via strict or non-strict keyword matching against a predefined list of semantically equivalent expressions. For Counterfactual Context, the output is a multiple-choice selection (3–5 options) where the correct answer is the counterfactual one supported by the context rather than the model's parametric knowledge. All models are evaluated with deterministic decoding (temperature=0) and a fixed retrieval-based QA system prompt.

## Method

FaithEval is constructed via a four-stage pipeline: (1) an LLM (GPT-4o) generates modified multi-paragraph contexts from 10 source QA datasets (SQuAD, HotpotQA, DROP, etc.) or ARC-Challenge, tailored to each task's criterion—removing supporting evidence (Unanswerable), fabricating conflicting evidence (Inconsistent), or generating counterfactual scientific claims (Counterfactual); (2) task-specific instructions are appended to form the evaluation prompt; (3) a separate LLM judge (GPT-4o-mini) verifies both the 'if' condition (new answer is supported by context) and 'only-if' condition (no alternative answer is supported); (4) professional human annotators on Mechanical Turk perform majority-vote validation, yielding 4.9K high-quality samples (2.4K Unanswerable, 1.5K Inconsistent, 1K Counterfactual). The benchmark is then used to evaluate 18 open-source and proprietary instruction-tuned LLMs, with additional analyses on chain-of-thought prompting, decoding strategies, sycophancy from task-specific instructions, and per-dataset performance decomposition.

## Results

Across 18 instruction-tuned models, faithfulness degrades sharply when contexts are noisy. On Unanswerable Context, the performance gap between original and modified contexts ranges from 13.6% to 68.4% (e.g., Phi-3-medium drops from 75.8% to 7.4%). On Inconsistent Context, the GPT-4 series averages 89.35% while the Phi-3 series averages only 5.8%. On Counterfactual Context, GPT-4o drops from 96.3% (closed-book) to 47.5% when counterfactual evidence is provided, versus 95% human accuracy on a held-out subset. Claude 3.5 Sonnet is the most consistently faithful model, ranking #1 on both Unanswerable (62.3%) and Inconsistent (94.4%) Context, and only dropping one rank on Counterfactual.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 18 models from six families (Phi-3, Llama-3/3.1, Mistral, Gemma-2, GPT, Command R, Claude) as baselines on the three FaithEval tasks. Proprietary models (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, GPT-4 Turbo) dominate the Inconsistent Context task (89–94% avg), while open-source models lag significantly (Llama-3.1-70B at 42.9%, Gemma-2-27B at 24.9%). On Counterfactual Context, a striking inversion occurs: GPT-4o drops 16 ranks (from #1 to #17) and GPT-4 Turbo drops 16 ranks (from #2 to #18), while smaller models like Mistral-7B (rank 18→3) and Llama-3.1-8B (rank 17→6) rise substantially, suggesting that smaller models are less anchored to parametric knowledge and thus more faithful to contradictory context. Within the same family, larger models generally improve on Unanswerable Context (e.g., Llama-3.1-70B improves 10.3% over the 8B variant) but do not consistently outperform smaller counterparts on Counterfactual Context. Claude 3.5 Sonnet is the only model that maintains a top-3 rank across all three tasks, making it the most robustly faithful model in the study.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of hallucination evaluation and RAG robustness testing. Prior faithfulness work has been confined to summarization (Laban et al., 2023; Jia et al., 2023) or small-scale QA settings, while RAG robustness benchmarks like RGB (Chen et al., 2024b) focus narrowly on news articles and lack the inconsistent-context dimension. Adversarial context generation studies (Shi et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2024a; Xie et al., 2024) modify contexts but at much smaller scale and without the systematic multi-stage validation pipeline used here. FaithEval unifies three distinct context failure modes—unanswerable, inconsistent, and counterfactual—into a single 4.9K-sample benchmark with LLM-judge and human-annotated verification, positioning itself as the first fine-grained, comprehensive benchmark specifically targeting contextual faithfulness hallucination in RAG scenarios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a critical gap for RAG deployment in high-stakes domains: even GPT-4o drops from 96.3% (closed-book) to 47.5% when given counterfactual context, and the finding that larger models do not reliably improve faithfulness challenges the scaling narrative. The sycophancy tradeoff—where task-specific instructions that help on noisy contexts degrade performance on normal ones—highlights an unresolved tension in prompt design. Open questions include whether training-time interventions (beyond prompting) can close the faithfulness gap, whether the three-task decomposition captures the full spectrum of real-world retrieval failures, and how results generalize to longer, more heterogeneous contexts than those used here.
