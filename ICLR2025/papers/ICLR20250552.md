## Problem

Existing audio-driven portrait video diffusion models rely on auxiliary spatial conditions (face locators, speed layers, movement regions) to stabilize motion, which restricts the range and velocity of portrait movements and prevents the model from learning natural motion patterns from data. Without these constraints, the weak audio-to-motion correlation and short temporal receptive fields (<0.2 s of motion frames) cause inconsistent, static, or random motion. The paper asks whether an audio-only conditioned diffusion model can generate natural, expressive portrait videos without any spatial movement constraints.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single 512×512 reference portrait image and an audio clip as input, producing a 512×512 talking portrait video at 25 FPS in 12-frame clips. Unlike prior methods, no face bounding boxes, speed layers, or landmark conditions are required at inference. During generation, 124 preceding motion frames (abstracted to 20 latents via the temporal segment module) provide temporal context, and the reference image is randomly sampled from the video during training. The model is trained on 174 hours of filtered talking-head video at 512×512 resolution.

## Method

Loopy is built on Stable Diffusion with a dual U-Net (denoising + reference network) and introduces two key modules. First, an inter/intra-clip temporal module separates cross-clip and within-clip temporal attention, with a temporal segment module (TSM) that geometrically segments 124 motion frames into 5 segments (stride s=4, expand ratio r=2) and uniformly samples representative frames, extending the effective receptive field to ~100 frames (~5 s, 30× the original). Second, an audio-to-latents module maps audio features (wav2vec multi-scale, 5-frame context) and facial motion variance features (head movement variance, expression variance from DWPose keypoints) into a shared 1280-dim motion latent space via cross-attention over 128 learnable embeddings; during training the condition is randomly sampled from audio, head variance, or expression variance, while at inference only audio drives the latent. Training uses a 2-stage schedule (image-level pose variation, then full temporal+audio training) with condition-specific dropout/masking, and inference employs multi-condition classifier-free guidance (audio ratio 5, reference ratio 3) with 25 DDIM steps.

## Results

On CelebV-HQ, Loopy achieves the best scores on IQA (3.780), Sync-C (4.849), Sync-D (8.196), FVD-R (49.153), FVD-I (680.634), FID (33.204), and DExp (0.309), outperforming all baselines. On RAVDESS, Loopy leads in IQA (4.506), Sync-C (4.814), FVD-R (16.134), FVD-I (394.288), FID (17.017), global motion (Glo 2.962), expression dynamics (Exp 0.343), and E-FID (3.132). The model also achieves the highest user-vote win rate across diverse openset scenarios (real people, anime, humanoid crafts, side faces; speech, singing, rap, emotional audio).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Loopy outperforms all four baselines on the majority of metrics across both CelebV-HQ and RAVDESS. Against Hallo, Loopy improves FVD-R by ~9% (49.2 vs 54.0) and FID by ~8% (33.2 vs 36.0) on CelebV-HQ, while also achieving substantially higher motion diversity (Glo 2.233 vs 0.499). Against VExpress, Loopy dominates on nearly every metric, with FID dropping from 65.1 to 33.2 and FVD-R from 117.9 to 49.2 on CelebV-HQ; VExpress's only advantage is slightly better lip-sync (Sync-C 5.001 vs 4.814 on RAVDESS), which the authors attribute to VExpress's near-static outputs (Glo 0.007). Against EchoMimic, Loopy wins on image quality (IQA 3.780 vs 3.307), lip-sync (Sync-C 4.849 vs 3.136), and FVD/FID, while EchoMimic edges ahead on raw expression amplitude (Exp 0.640 vs 0.452) and E-FID on CelebV-HQ (3.018 vs 2.307 — actually Loopy wins here). Against SadTalker (GAN-based), Loopy improves FVD-R by ~72% (49.2 vs 171.8) and FID by ~9% (33.2 vs 36.6) on CelebV-HQ. The paper also notes that retraining Hallo on Loopy's dataset (Hallo*) does not close the gap, confirming that architectural design rather than data volume drives the improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

Loopy builds directly on the Stable Diffusion / Latent Diffusion Model architecture and the dual U-Net design from AnimateAnyone and TryOnDiffusion for reference-image conditioning, while adopting the wav2vec multi-scale audio feature extraction and two-stage training protocol from EMO. It departs from the dominant paradigm in recent audio-driven portrait diffusion methods (Hallo, EchoMimic, VExpress) by eliminating auxiliary spatial conditions—face locators, speed layers, and landmark encoders—that those methods require to stabilize motion. The core architectural departure is the separation of temporal modeling into inter-clip and intra-clip layers with a temporal segment module that extends the motion-frame receptive field from ~4 frames to over 100 frames, combined with an audio-to-latents module that bridges weakly-correlated audio to strongly-correlated motion statistics during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that natural, diverse portrait motion can be generated from audio alone without spatial constraints, Loopy removes a practical bottleneck that limited prior methods to template-like or near-static movements. The temporal segment module's ability to extend temporal coverage 30× with only a 3% RTF increase is a practically useful technique for any video diffusion model requiring long-range temporal context. However, the paper acknowledges significant limitations: the model struggles with inputs containing large upper limbs, occluded figures, animals, or multiple people, and degrades with silent or noisy audio. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond talking-head scenarios to full-body or multi-character animation, and whether the 43× real-time factor can be reduced to practical levels through distillation and hardware acceleration.
