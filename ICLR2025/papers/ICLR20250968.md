## Problem

Diffusion-based image enhancement methods produce high-quality results but require lengthy iterative sampling (dozens to hundreds of steps), making them impractical for real-time use. Existing distillation approaches that compress this to one step suffer from over-smoothing and reduced diversity, and directly applying score-based distillation to enhancement tasks yields inaccurate score estimates because the broad noise schedule pushes noisy samples too far from the ground truth, corrupting the gradient direction.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a low-quality RGB image (degraded by a composite of blur, Gaussian noise, downsampling, and JPEG compression), a natural-language text prompt (generated by BLIP after a lightweight SwinIR coarse restoration pass), and a random noise vector z, all processed in the VQGAN latent space at 512×512 resolution. It outputs a single high-quality enhanced image in one forward pass of the DiT-based generator, with the text prompt enabling controllable editing (e.g., changing hair color or adding accessories in face cartoonization). The generator also accepts a random noise input concatenated with the LQ latent to encourage output diversity across runs.

## Method

InstaRevive distills a pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model (PixArt-Alpha DiT) into a one-step generator via score-based distillation, using two score estimators: a frozen teacher ε_ψ that scores the HQ data distribution and a trainable student ε_φ (updated with LoRA) that scores the generated distribution. The key novelty is a dynamic noise control strategy that sets T_max = σ⁻¹[κ·d(x_HQ, x)] based on the current batch-level distance between HQ and generated images, so the diffusion noise level adapts as training progresses—starting with moderate noise for coarse alignment and shrinking to fine-grained refinement. A complementary dynamic loss control linearly maps T_max to a weight α that shifts emphasis from regression (LPIPS) loss early in training to score-matching loss later, preventing the inaccurate student score from destabilizing early optimization. Textual prompts extracted by BLIP condition both score estimators and the generator, providing explicit semantic guidance that improves restoration of severely degraded content and enables controllable editing.

## Results

On the BFR task, InstaRevive achieves 22.33 PSNR, 19.78 FID, and 0.4025 LPIPS on CelebA-Test, and sets new state-of-the-art FID scores of 38.73 (LFW-Test) and 35.29 (WIDER-Test) on real-world datasets, all in a single inference step at 7.97 FPS. On BSR, it attains the best MANIQA (0.4571/0.4722), MUSIQ (65.85/64.54), and NIQE (4.20/4.28) on RealSet65 and RealSR respectively, outperforming both multi-step diffusion methods and prior one-step distilled methods. The framework also generalizes to face cartoonization, denoising, deblurring, deraining, and image-to-image transition tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On BFR, InstaRevive outperforms all nine baselines on PSNR, FID (CelebA), LFW FID, and WIDER FID simultaneously. The closest competitor is DifFace-100 (FID 19.95 vs. 19.78 on CelebA), but InstaRevive is 35× faster (7.97 vs. 0.23 FPS) and substantially better on real-world LFW (38.73 vs. 46.17 FID). Among GAN-based methods, CodeFormer is the strongest (FID 22.19), yet InstaRevive still leads by 2.4 FID points while matching its PSNR. On BSR, InstaRevive achieves the best MANIQA, MUSIQ, and NIQE on both RealSet65 and RealSR, beating the prior best one-step method SinSR by 0.02 MANIQA and 3.2 MUSIQ on RealSet65. The only metric where a baseline edges ahead is CLIPIQA, where SinSR scores 0.7138 vs. InstaRevive's 0.6755 on RealSet65. Multi-step diffusion methods (StableSR, GDP, LDM-15, ResShift-15) are consistently outperformed across all four no-reference quality metrics, confirming that the one-step distilled generator retains the generative quality of iterative sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

InstaRevive adapts the Variational Score Distillation (VSD) framework from ProlificDreamer—originally designed for text-to-3D generation—to the image enhancement domain, repurposing the two-score-estimator KL divergence objective for aligning generated and HQ image distributions. The core departure from prior score-based distillation is the dynamic noise control strategy: unlike text-to-image generation where the generated distribution starts far from the target, image enhancement begins with distributions already in close proximity, so the paper dynamically shrinks the diffusion noise scope (T_max) based on the current HQ–generated distance to maintain accurate score estimates. This builds on Score-SDE's theoretical foundation of optimizing KL divergences between diffused distributions, but introduces a task-specific practical mechanism absent from prior one-step distillation methods like SinSR or OSEDiff, which apply fixed noise schedules and suffer from over-smoothing on complex degradations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-step inference at ~8 FPS while matching or exceeding multi-step diffusion methods (e.g., 100-step DifFace) on FID and PSNR makes high-quality image enhancement practical for real-time applications, and the framework's versatility across BFR, BSR, cartoonization, inpainting, low-light enhancement, denoising, deblurring, and deraining with only 10K fine-tuning steps suggests a broadly applicable recipe. The dynamic control insight—that enhancement tasks require fundamentally different noise scheduling than generative tasks—could inform future distillation work beyond image enhancement. However, the paper acknowledges that extreme degradations and complex content remain challenging, and the one-step paradigm cannot surpass larger multi-step models like SUPIR on the hardest cases, leaving open how to better exploit generative capacity under severe information loss.
