## Problem

Pairwise prompting of LLMs or human experts to infer causal graphs is fundamentally unreliable because, given only two variables, no expert can distinguish a direct edge from an indirect (mediated) one, leading to spurious edges and, in practice, many cycles in the aggregated graph. The paper argues that causal order (topological ordering) is a more stable and always-correct output interface for imperfect experts, and that the standard practice of evaluating expert quality via graph metrics like SHD is misleading.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of variable names (defining the node set of a causal graph) and queries an LLM or human annotator using triplet prompts—each prompt presents three variables and asks the expert to orient the causal relationships among them while respecting acyclicity. The output is a topological ordering (causal order) over the variables, which is then fed as a constraint into downstream discovery algorithms (PC, CaMML) or used directly to identify a backdoor adjustment set for average causal effect estimation via DoWhy. For large graphs, a variant limits the number of auxiliary variables per pair to a constant k, yielding O(k|V|²) LLM calls instead of O(|V|³).

## Method

The core contribution is a triplet prompting strategy that, for every pair (A, B), introduces a third auxiliary variable C and instructs the LLM to orient the three-node subgraph as a DAG. Each pair appears in |V|−1 different triplets, enabling a majority-vote ensemble over orientations; ties are broken by a high-cost expert (GPT-4), and residual cycles are removed via an entropy-based edge-pruning step (edges with above-mean entropy are pruned to minimize D_top). Theoretically, the paper proves that for an ε-expert, the triplet strategy yields per-pair error probability strictly less than ε (Proposition 4.1), because the acyclicity constraint within each triplet eliminates one of the two wrong options and renormalizes probability. The causal order is then integrated into constraint-based methods (orienting CPDAG undirected edges per the order, à la Meek 1995) or score-based methods (as a level-order prior in CaMML), and used to construct a valid backdoor adjustment set (all variables preceding the treatment in the order) for effect estimation.

## Results

On the Child dataset (20 nodes), the triplet method using GPT-3.5-turbo achieves D_top = 1 and SHD = 28 with zero cycles, compared to the base pairwise method's >3k cycles (D_top undefined) and SHD = 177. On the Neuropathic dataset (22 nodes), the triplet method yields D_top = 3 and SHD = 24 versus the pairwise base's >5k cycles and SHD = 212. Remarkably, the triplet method with Phi-3 (3.8B) achieves D_top = 17 and SHD = 69 on Child, outperforming GPT-4's pairwise method which produces >10k cycles (SHD = 148). For downstream causal discovery at N=250, Ours(LLM+PC) achieves D_top = 0 on Earthquake, Cancer, and Survey, matching or exceeding standalone PC (0.16, 0.00, 0.50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The triplet method consistently outperforms both the base pairwise prompt and the best pairwise variant (Chain-of-Thought) across all datasets and metrics. On the large Child dataset, the triplet method reduces SHD from 177 (base pairwise) to 28 and eliminates all cycles (>3k → 0), while CoT only reduces SHD to 138 with >500 cycles remaining. On Neuropathic, the triplet method achieves SHD = 24 versus 212 (base) and 64 (CoT), with zero cycles. A striking result is that the triplet method with small models (Phi-3, Llama-3 8B) outperforms GPT-4's pairwise method: on Child, Triplet+Llama-3 achieves D_top = 12 and SHD = 129 with 0 cycles, while Pairwise+GPT-4 produces >10k cycles (D_top undefined, SHD = 148). For downstream causal discovery, Ours(LLM+PC) and Ours(LLM+CaMML) improve D_top over standalone PC, SCORE, ICA-LiNGAM, Direct-LiNGAM, NOTEARS, and CaMML at low sample sizes (N=250), with the gap narrowing at N=10000. The BFS and BFS+Stats baselines from Jiralerspong et al. (2024) also underperform the triplet method on both SHD and D_top across tested datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of LLM-as-expert causal discovery work (Kiciman et al. 2023; Long et al. 2022) by arguing that the full causal graph is the wrong output interface for imperfect experts, and proposing causal order (topological ordering) instead. It builds on the ε-expert framework from Long et al. (2023) to provide a theoretical guarantee that triplet prompting reduces error below pairwise prompting, and integrates the resulting order into constraint-based (PC/Meek) and score-based (CaMML) discovery algorithms. The triplet method departs from all prior LLM causal discovery work, which relies on pairwise 'does A cause B?' queries, by introducing an auxiliary variable per pair and enforcing acyclicity within each triplet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that even a perfect expert's pairwise answers yield an incorrect graph but always a correct causal order—reframes how expert knowledge should be extracted and evaluated in causal inference, with practical implications for reducing error in downstream effect estimation and graph discovery. The finding that small open-source models (Phi-3, Llama-3 8B) with triplet prompting outperform GPT-4 with pairwise prompting on small-to-medium graphs suggests cost-efficient alternatives for practitioners. Limitations include reliance on small graphs (≤22 nodes), inability to handle novel variables or counterfactual tasks, and the assumption of no latent confounders for the backdoor set guarantee.
