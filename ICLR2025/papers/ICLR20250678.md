## Problem

Diffusion models have shown strong potential for single-task dense prediction by reformulating tasks as label-denoising, but extending them to multi-task dense prediction remains unexplored. The key challenges are that per-task denoising prevents the model from capturing cross-task relations, heterogeneous labels (discrete categories vs. continuous values) require cumbersome task-specific encodings, and running separate diffusion decoders per task multiplies inference cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250678/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (512×512 on PASCAL-Context, 448×576 on NYUD-v2) and simultaneously produces dense prediction maps for 4–5 tasks (e.g., semantic segmentation, human parsing, saliency, surface normal, boundary, or depth). Rather than generating each task's prediction through an independent denoising loop, all task labels are encoded into one joint cross-task map that is denoised in a single iterative process, so the output for all tasks emerges from one shared diffusion trajectory.

## Method

TaskDiffusion couples the denoising of all tasks into a joint diffusion process in the decoder. A shared ViT-Large backbone feeds task-specific branches that produce multi-level features; a cross-task label encoder maps heterogeneous labels (one-hot for discrete, raw values for continuous) through 1×1 convolutions and concatenation into a single normalized cross-task map. The cross-task diffusion decoder conditions on these multi-level features and performs two explicit interaction stages: a task-interaction module generates S×S pixel-wise task-relation maps at each level to fuse features across tasks, and a level-interaction module generates level-fusing maps to aggregate across scales. Training uses task-specific losses (cross-entropy or L1) on the predicted logits rather than a standard L2 denoising loss, and inference employs DDIM with asymmetric time intervals over 3 steps, yielding a coarse-to-fine refinement of the task-relation maps.

## Results

On PASCAL-Context (5 tasks), TaskDiffusion achieves 81.21 mIoU (semantic segmentation), 69.62 mIoU (human parsing), 84.94 maxF (saliency), 13.55 mErr (surface normal), and 74.89 odsF (boundary), with an MTL gain (Δm) of −1.11%. Combined with MLoRE, the method reaches Δm = −0.04%, surpassing the previous best (MLoRE at −0.62%) by +0.58%. On NYUD-v2 (4 tasks), TaskDiffusion/w MLoRE achieves Δm = +1.04%, the only method to exceed the single-task baseline, compared to MLoRE's +0.11% and TSP-Transformer's −0.02%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TaskDiffusion (standalone) outperforms all prior multi-task methods on the MTL gain metric Δm on PASCAL-Context (−1.11% vs. TaskExpert's −1.74%, TaskPrompter's −2.03%, and DiffusionMTL's −3.16%), while using fewer FLOPs than TaskExpert (610G vs. 622G) and TSP-Transformer (610G vs. 1991G). When combined with MLoRE, the method achieves the best Δm of −0.04%, beating MLoRE alone (−0.62%) by +0.58% and TSP-Transformer (−1.01%) by +0.97%. On NYUD-v2, TaskDiffusion/w MLoRE is the only method to achieve a positive Δm (+1.04%), surpassing MLoRE (+0.11%), TSP-Transformer (−0.02%), and TaskExpert (−0.84%). Against the other diffusion-based multi-task method DiffusionMTL, TaskDiffusion wins on every individual task metric and reduces FLOPs from 732G to 610G. The paper notes that per-task, TaskDiffusion (standalone) is slightly behind MLoRE on semantic segmentation (81.21 vs. 81.41) and human parsing (69.62 vs. 70.52), but leads on saliency (84.94 vs. 84.90) and surface normal (13.55 vs. 13.51), with the combined version closing all gaps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based dense prediction line (DDP, DFormer, DepthGen) from single-task to multi-task settings, and simultaneously extends discriminative multi-task architectures (TaskPrompter, TaskExpert, MTI-Net) by replacing their task-specific decoders with a joint denoising diffusion decoder. It builds directly on DDP's encoder-decoder decoupling and DDIM sampling, while adopting the shared ViT backbone and task-specific branch design from TaskPrompter/MLoRE. The key conceptual departure is the joint denoising process: instead of running separate diffusion decoders per task (as DDP does for single tasks), all heterogeneous task labels are encoded into a single cross-task map and denoised jointly, explicitly modeling inter-task and inter-level relations during the iterative denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first diffusion-based method for fully-labeled multi-task dense prediction, opening a new direction for leveraging generative models to capture cross-task dependencies. However, the MTL gain remains negative (−0.84% to −1.11% on PASCAL-Context), meaning multi-task learning still degrades performance relative to single-task baselines, and the gains over strong discriminative baselines like TaskExpert and MLoRE are modest. The authors acknowledge limitations: the method is only validated on 4–5 tasks with shared spatial structure, and its behavior on highly heterogeneous task combinations (e.g., instance segmentation + classification) remains unexplored. Whether the joint denoising paradigm scales to larger task suites or more diverse task types is an open question.
