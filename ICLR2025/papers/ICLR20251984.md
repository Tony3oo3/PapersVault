## Problem

Existing methods for constrained diffusion models are restricted to specific constraint types—convex sets, Riemannian manifolds, or symmetry groups—because analytical mirror maps only exist for convex constraints. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a general framework that handles arbitrary, possibly non-convex constraints (e.g., PDE satisfaction, object counting) without requiring closed-form mirror maps.

## Input / Output

The method takes Gaussian noise as input for generation and noisy state observations for inverse problems (data assimilation). It produces images that satisfy a user-specified constraint defined by a differentiable distance function, with demonstrated constraints on 64×64, 128×128, and 128×256 images (e.g., 2-channel velocity fields for divergence-free flow). The forward mirror map transforms constrained images into an unconstrained mirror space, and the inverse map restores generated samples to the constraint set; for inverse problems, the output is a full state trajectory reconstructed from partial noisy observations.

## Method

A NAMM jointly learns a forward mirror map g_φ (parameterized as the gradient of an input-convex neural network to encourage invertibility) and an inverse map f_ψ (a ResNet-based CNN) via a cycle-consistency loss, a constraint loss evaluated over a sequence of Gaussian-noise-perturbed mirror distributions (σ ∈ [0, σ_max]), and an identity-regularization term. A standard score-based diffusion model (NCSN++ with VP SDE) is then trained in the learned mirror space on the pushforward of the data distribution through g_φ, and generated samples are mapped back to the constraint set via f_ψ. An optional finetuning step adapts f_ψ to the actual MDM sample distribution to reduce distribution shift. For constrained inverse problems, diffusion posterior sampling is adapted by evaluating the measurement likelihood on f_ψ-inverted mirror-space samples, enforcing the constraint at each denoising step.

## Results

Across five constraints (total brightness, 1D Burgers', divergence-free, periodic, and count), the finetuned NAMM-based MDM achieves 38%–96% improvement in constraint distance (CD) over a vanilla diffusion model trained on the same data. The largest gains are for periodic tiling (CD 0.04 vs. 1.04, 96% improvement) and 1D Burgers' (CD 0.04 vs. 0.45, 91% improvement), while the smallest is for the count constraint (CD 0.35 vs. 0.56, 38%). Distribution-matching accuracy is preserved: KID is equal or better than the baseline in 4 of 5 cases, and MMD is comparable. The method also achieves these constraint distances in less total compute time than the baseline DM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is a vanilla diffusion model (DM) trained on the same constrained dataset. The NAMM-based MDM outperforms the DM on constraint distance for all five constraints, with improvements ranging from 38% (count) to 96% (periodic). On distribution matching, the DM achieves slightly lower MMD in some cases (e.g., 1D Burgers': 0.062 vs. 0.129), but the NAMM approach yields substantially lower KID (e.g., 1D Burgers': 0.0022 vs. 0.2308), indicating better perceptual fidelity. For the data assimilation inverse problem, the paper compares against vanilla DPS (Chung et al., 2022) and constraint-guided DPS (CG-DPS). Mirror DPS with the NAMM-based MDM produces solutions with notably smaller PDE residual (1D Burgers') and lower divergence (Kolmogorov flow) than both DPS and CG-DPS, and this advantage holds across different measurement-likelihood weights ζ and constraint-guidance strengths. The count constraint is the weakest case, where the MDM without finetuning performs on par with the DM, and finetuning is essential to achieve the 38% improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends mirror diffusion models (Liu et al., 2024), which required analytical mirror maps available only for convex constraint sets, by replacing them with jointly learned neural networks (NAMMs) that handle general, possibly non-convex constraints. The forward map is parameterized as the gradient of an input-convex neural network (ICNN), repurposing the learned mirror map framework from convex optimization (Tan et al., 2023) into a generative modeling context, while the inverse map uses a CycleGAN-style ResNet trained with cycle-consistency and constraint-distance losses. The approach also adapts diffusion posterior sampling (Chung et al., 2022) to the learned mirror space for constrained inverse problems, and is positioned against other constrained diffusion methods (equivariant, Riemannian, reflected, log-barrier) that are each restricted to specific constraint geometries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NAMMs provide a practical, general-purpose recipe for enforcing visually-subtle constraints (PDEs, incompressibility, object counts) on generative models by construction rather than through guidance or penalties, and the framework is model-agnostic (demonstrated with both diffusion models and VAEs). Key limitations include the requirement of a differentiable distance function to the constraint set, the fact that constraint satisfaction is approximate (soft) rather than guaranteed (hard), and the lack of theoretical invertibility guarantees for the ICNN parameterization when the constraint set is non-convex. Open questions include scalability to high-dimensional or composite constraints, handling constraints without differentiable distance functions, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D data where the constraint structure is more complex.
