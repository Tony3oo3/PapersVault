## Problem

It remains unclear whether language models genuinely learn mathematical reasoning skills or merely memorize solution templates, because existing GSM8K-based studies are confounded by data contamination and limited solution diversity (at most ~8K templates). This paper asks: can a model trained from scratch on fully controlled synthetic math data truly generalize to unseen reasoning lengths, what hidden mental processes underlie its solutions, and how do depth and width independently affect reasoning capability?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253522/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a synthetic grade-school math problem in natural language, structured over a 4-layer hierarchical categorization (e.g., School → Classroom → Backpack) with a randomly permuted set of sentences describing parameter dependencies, followed by a query. It generates a fixed-format chain-of-thought solution where each necessary parameter is assigned a letter (a–z/A–Z), all arithmetic is broken into binary operations separated by semicolons, and all computations are performed in integers mod 23 to isolate logical reasoning from arithmetic difficulty. The final output is a single numerical answer mod 23.

## Method

The paper introduces iGSM, a synthetic data generation framework that constructs math problems from random structure graphs (instance parameters) and dependency graphs (direct, instance, and implicit dependencies) over hierarchical categorizations, with difficulty controlled by ip (instance parameters) and op (solution operations), yielding >90 trillion distinct solution templates. A GPT2-small (12-layer, 768-dim, RoPE) is trained from scratch via supervised learning on iGSM-med (op≤15) or iGSM-hard (op≤21), with template-level hash separation between train and test to certify zero contamination. The key finding is that the model achieves ~99% in-distribution accuracy and generalizes to OOD problems with longer reasoning chains (up to op=32), producing shortest solutions that skip unnecessary parameters. Probing experiments reveal the model mentally preprocesses all relevant parameters before generation and learns an all-pair dependency skill not required by the training data, while ablations show that depth (16 layers, 576-dim) outperforms width (4 layers, 1920-dim) for harder reasoning tasks.

## Results

After pretraining from scratch on synthetic iGSM data, the 12-layer GPT2-small (768-dim, RoPE) achieves 99% accuracy on in-distribution problems (op≤15 for iGSM-med, op≤21 for iGSM-hard) and generalizes to out-of-distribution problems with longer reasoning chains (up to op=23 for med, op=32 for hard) that were never seen during training. The model predominantly generates shortest solutions (level-1 reasoning), computing only necessary parameters rather than brute-forcing all quantities. A key architectural finding is that depth matters more than width: a 16-layer, 576-dim model solves harder problems (longer reasoning length) than a 4-layer, 1920-dim model that is twice as large in parameter count.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is a controlled empirical study of a single model (GPT2-small) rather than a method-vs-method comparison, so there are no external baselines in the traditional sense. The key internal comparisons are: (1) In-distribution vs. out-of-distribution evaluation, where the model trained on op≤15 (med) or op≤21 (hard) generalizes to unseen reasoning lengths up to op=23 or op=32, demonstrating true reasoning skill acquisition rather than template memorization (supported by >90 trillion solution templates, far exceeding model capacity). (2) GPT-4 few-shot serves as a sanity-check reference: GPT-4/4o cannot solve iGSM-med at op=11 (Figure 2), confirming the synthetic task is genuinely difficult and not trivially solvable by prompting. (3) Depth vs. width ablation (Results 7–8): a 16-layer/576-dim model outperforms a 4-layer/1920-dim model on harder reasoning-length problems, even with Chain-of-Thought, challenging the 'only size matters' scaling narrative. (4) Level-0 vs. level-1 reasoning: the model avoids computing unnecessary parameters (Figure 4), indicating it formulates a plan before generating rather than brute-forcing all quantities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of fine-tuning pre-trained LLMs on GSM8K or GPT-4-augmented variants (Liu et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024), which the authors argue is confounded by data contamination and limited solution diversity. Instead, it introduces a fully synthetic, controllable data generation pipeline (iGSM) and trains GPT2 from scratch, drawing on the length-generalization-in-arithmetic line of work (Anil et al., 2022; Jelassi et al., 2023) but extending it to multi-step dependency reasoning rather than single-operation arithmetic. The probing methodology builds on structural probes (Hewitt & Manning, 2019) but applies them to intermediate reasoning states in math problem-solving, and the depth-vs-width analysis directly challenges the 'only size matters' scaling narrative of Kaplan et al. (2020).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a model trained purely on iGSM learns all-pair dependency computation—a skill not required to fit any training example—is, to the authors' knowledge, the first evidence of a language model acquiring useful capabilities beyond its training distribution, offering a potential early signal toward emergent general intelligence. The depth-over-width result provides a principled, controlled-data counterpoint to internet-scale scaling laws and has practical implications for architecture design. Key limitations include the synthetic setting (mod-23 arithmetic, no common-sense reasoning), the single architecture family (GPT2), and the relatively small model scale, leaving open whether these findings transfer to frontier models or natural-language math. The iGSM framework itself is a reusable tool for future controlled studies of LLM reasoning.
