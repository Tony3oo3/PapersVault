## Problem

Current volumetric biomedical foundation models struggle to generalize across new populations, procedures, and imaging devices because public 3D annotated datasets are small (dozens to a few thousand volumes) and cover narrow anatomical regions and modalities. No existing biomedical vision foundation model has been demonstrated to handle multiple disparate 3D tasks (e.g., both registration and segmentation) without pretraining on real images, and prior approaches either operate on 2D slices, require dataset-specific self-supervised pretraining, or are limited to a single task.

## Input / Output

The pretrained 4-level 3D U-Net (5.9M parameters) takes a single 3D volume of arbitrary size as input and produces a 16-channel feature map at the same spatial resolution, computed via sliding-window inference with 128³ windows and 0.8 overlap. For registration, a fixed and moving volume pair are each passed through the network and the resulting 16-channel feature maps replace the raw intensities in existing solvers (ConvexAdam, ANTs) to produce a deformation field. For segmentation, the pretrained weights initialize the U-Net, which is finetuned with an additional softmax head to output an N-channel multi-label mask on 1–3 annotated volumes.

## Method

The core contribution is a synthetic data engine that generates highly diverse 3D training volumes without any real images: it composes randomly deformed binary shape templates (sampled from ~45,000 TotalSegmentator labels) into 128³ label ensembles, optionally applies foreground sphere masks and envelope labels, then synthesizes two contrastive volume views by sampling per-label intensities from independent Gaussian mixture models and corrupting them with Perlin noise plus biomedical augmentations (bias fields, Fourier spikes, gamma shifts, blurring, Gibbs ringing, motion, affine warps). Pretraining uses a spatial extension of multi-positive supervised contrastive learning: for each anchor voxel, all voxels sharing the same label across both views are positives while all others are negatives, with the loss applied at six decoder layers to enforce multiscale supervision. The key inductive bias is that features within a 3D shape should be approximately invariant to appearance and pose nuisance variation, yielding representations that are simultaneously appearance-stable (for registration) and semantically discriminative (for segmentation). The temperature τ=0.33 is deliberately relaxed relative to natural-vision contrastive learning because all negative pairs are inherently difficult when appearances vary widely within the same label.

## Results

On few-shot 3D segmentation across six diverse datasets (1–3 annotated volumes), the proposed 5.9M-parameter U-Net achieves the best or second-best Dice on all datasets: .89 on MSD-Heart, .85 on PROMISE12, .86 on L2RAb-MRI, .80 on FeTA, .61 on AMOS-CT, and .76 on WUFetal, consistently outperforming larger foundation models (17–67M params) that were specifically pretrained for segmentation. For unsupervised multi-modality 3D registration, using the network's 16-channel features with ConvexAdam yields .74 Dice on L2RAb (vs. .68 for base ConvexAdam) and .66 on MM-WHS (vs. .65), while ANTs-Ours improves 26 and 5 Dice points over ANTs-MI on L2RAb and MM-WHS respectively. In the multitask feature-extraction comparison (Table 2), Ours is the only method whose features improve upon the default ANTs MutualInfo baseline for registration (.70 vs. .48 on L2RAb; .63 vs. .58 on MM-WHS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest segmentation baseline PrimGeoSeg (67.2M params, SwinUNETR), Ours (5.9M params) wins on 5 of 6 datasets (MSD-Heart .89 vs .87, PROMISE12 .85 vs .79, L2RAb-MRI .86 vs .84, FeTA .80 vs .79, WUFetal .76 vs .76) and loses narrowly on AMOS-CT (.61 vs .63); when PrimGeoSeg is retrained at the matched 5.9M parameter count, its AMOS-CT score drops to .56, below Ours. Ours dominates SMIT (67.2M) on all six datasets, with the largest gap on PROMISE12 (.85 vs .72) and AMOS-CT (.61 vs .58). Against Models Genesis, MedicalNet, and Disruptive AE, Ours wins on every dataset, with MedicalNet showing the largest deficits (e.g., .53 vs .85 on PROMISE12, .50 vs .76 on WUFetal). For registration, ConvexAdam-Ours outperforms base ConvexAdam by 11 Dice on L2RAb and 6 on MM-WHS, while ANTs-Ours gains 26 and 5 Dice over ANTs-MI. In the multitask feature comparison (Table 2), all existing 3D biomedical foundation models (PrimGeoSeg, Models Genesis, SMIT, Disruptive AE) fail to beat the default ANTs MutualInfo baseline for registration, whereas Ours improves it by 22 Dice on L2RAb and 5 on MM-WHS. The 2D DINOv2 + LoRA + FPN baseline (Table 8) is outperformed by Ours on all six segmentation datasets, with the largest gap on AMOS-CT (.61 vs .25), and even a randomly initialized 3D U-Net generally beats the slice-wise 2D foundation model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the domain-randomization paradigm from brain-specific tools (SynthSeg, SynthMorph, SynthStrip) to a task-agnostic, multi-region biomedical setting by composing randomly deformed shape templates from TotalSegmentator with a Gaussian-mixture appearance model, drawing loose inspiration from the Dead Leaves model for natural images. The representation learning objective is a spatial extension of multi-positive supervised contrastive learning (SupCon), adapted to 3D volumes with the key inductive bias that features within a semantic label should be stable across nuisance imaging variation. Unlike prior biomedical foundation models (Models Genesis, SMIT, DAE, MedicalNet, PrimGeoSeg) that aggregate real annotated volumes and focus on segmentation, this work trains entirely on synthetic data and targets both registration and segmentation, filling a gap the authors identify: no prior 3D biomedical model has been demonstrated across multiple disparate voxel-level tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical impact is that a single 5.9M-parameter U-Net, pretrained without any real images, provides a dataset-agnostic initialization that consistently matches or beats much larger (17–67M parameter) foundation models on few-shot segmentation across six diverse radiological datasets, while also yielding state-of-the-art training-free multimodality registration when its features drive existing solvers. This challenges the prevailing assumption that biomedical foundation models must be trained on aggregated real datasets to generalize. Key limitations remain: the intensity-invariance inductive bias is suboptimal for tasks relying on relative intensities or texture, MRI reconstruction requires domain-specific architectural changes, and segmentation still requires dataset-specific finetuning. An open direction the authors note is using the data engine to train promptable 3D segmentation models that eliminate finetuning entirely.
