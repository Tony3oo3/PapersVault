## Problem

Existing diffusion-based data augmentation methods for offline RL synthesize forward trajectories (from known to unknown states), which produce risky In2Out transitions that can overestimate values and mislead the agent into out-of-distribution regions. This paper identifies that the direction of synthesis matters: reverse trajectories (from unknown to known states) naturally eliminate the overestimation risk because the agent never plans from unknown states during deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251010/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an offline dataset D of (s, a, r, s') tuples and repeatedly samples initial states s_0 from D as generation conditions. It produces variable-length reverse trajectories structured as 2D arrays of [states; actions; rewards] indexed from 0 (current) to -L (oldest), which are truncated by an OOD detector before being appended to D. For visual RL (Meta-World), the pipeline extends to pixel-based generation via Stable Diffusion, where actions and rewards are predicted from generated trajectory images using diffusion-layer feature maps.

## Method

RTDiff trains a conditional diffusion model (MLP with skip connections, width 4096, EDM sampling with 128 steps) to generate reverse trajectories conditioned on an initial state s_0 from the offline dataset. The key architectural insight is that by generating trajectories in reverse (s_0 → s_{-1} → ... → s_{-L}), the synthesized transitions are predominantly Out2In (from OOD to in-distribution states), which are safe for the agent since it never makes decisions from OOD states during planning. An SSD-based OOD detector (Mahalanobis distance over clustered features) automatically truncates each trajectory at the first state exceeding a normalized distance threshold, enabling flexible length control without fixed-length constraints. A noise control strategy generates multiple initial noise vectors simultaneously by minimizing their pairwise dot products, ensuring generated trajectories are maximally diverse and reducing the total number of samples needed for effective augmentation.

## Results

RTDiff reports performance *increase* (normalized score gain) over the unaugmented baseline across D4RL and Meta-World environments. Headline gains include +14.3 for IQL on maze2d-large, +17.2 for IQL on hopper-mixed, and +14.2 for TD3+BC on kitchen-partial. On Meta-World visual tasks, RTDiff improves success rates by 0.05–0.11 over the original offline RL scores (e.g., Coffee Push IQL: 0.61→0.72). Across all 12 D4RL Locomotion tasks and 9 Maze/AntMaze/Kitchen tasks, RTDiff achieves the highest or near-highest gain in the majority of settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RTDiff is compared against four baselines: SynthER (Lu et al., 2023, transition-level diffusion synthesis), ATraDiff (Yang & Wang, 2024, forward trajectory diffusion), MOPO (Yu et al., 2020, model-based offline RL), and ROMI (Wang et al., 2021, reverse model-based imagination). Against SynthER, RTDiff wins in 11 of 12 Locomotion tasks and 8 of 9 Maze/AntMaze/Kitchen tasks; the sole exception is hopper-mixed with IQL where SynthER edges ahead (18.4 vs 17.2). Against ATraDiff, RTDiff wins in 10 of 12 Locomotion tasks and 8 of 9 Maze/AntMaze/Kitchen tasks, with the largest margin on maze2d-large IQL (+14.3 vs +12.4) and kitchen-partial TD3+BC (+14.2 vs +12.3). Against the model-based baselines MOPO and ROMI (Table 6, Maze2D), RTDiff outperforms both across all three maze layouts for both IQL and TD3+BC, e.g., maze2d-large IQL: RTDiff +14.3 vs MOPO +5.9 vs ROMI +8.1. On Meta-World visual tasks, RTDiff consistently exceeds ATraDiff by 0.02–0.09 success rate across all five tasks and three algorithms.

## Relation to Prior Work

RTDiff builds on the growing line of work applying diffusion models to reinforcement learning, particularly trajectory generation for data augmentation (SynthER by Lu et al., 2023; ATraDiff by Yang & Wang, 2024) and planning (Janner et al., 2022; Ajay et al., 2023). Its key conceptual departure is the direction of synthesis: rather than generating forward trajectories from known to unknown states (as prior methods do), RTDiff generates reverse trajectories from unknown to known states, which naturally avoids the overestimation risk that plagues forward synthesis. The method also draws on OOD detection (SSD, Sehwag et al., 2021) for adaptive trajectory length control and introduces a noise-repulsion strategy for improving sample efficiency, both of which are novel components not present in prior diffusion-based RL augmentation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reverse-synthesis insight offers a simple yet principled way to mitigate distribution shift in offline RL without the conservative biases that restrict generalization, and it is broadly applicable as a plug-in augmentation step for existing offline RL algorithms (IQL, TD3+BC, CQL, Decision Transformer). However, the method still requires generating millions of samples, leading to substantial inference time, and the paper acknowledges this as a key limitation. Open questions include whether the noise-control and OOD-detector components can be further refined to reduce sample requirements, and whether the reverse-synthesis principle extends to more complex settings such as multi-agent or hierarchical RL.
