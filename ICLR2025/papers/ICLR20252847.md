## Problem

Diffusion model-based inverse problem solvers have achieved state-of-the-art results on image tasks like super-resolution and deblurring, but their extension to video inverse problems with spatio-temporal degradation remains largely unexplored. The central obstacle is that solving video inverse problems naively requires a video diffusion model, which demands large-scale video training data and computational resources far beyond what is practical for diverse applications.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 16-frame degraded video clip at 256×256 resolution, a known spatio-temporal degradation operator (e.g., temporal PSF convolution optionally combined with spatial deblurring, 4× super-resolution, or 50% random inpainting), and a pre-trained unconditional 256×256 ADM image diffusion model used zero-shot without fine-tuning. It outputs 16 reconstructed frames at the same resolution. The temporal dimension is treated as the batch dimension of the image diffusion model, so VRAM scales linearly with frame count (e.g., 8 frames use ~7.3 GB, 32 frames use ~23.7 GB on an RTX 4090).

## Method

The key insight is to treat the video's time axis as the batch dimension of a pre-trained image diffusion model, eliminating the need for a video diffusion model entirely. At each reverse DDIM step, Tweedie's formula is applied per-frame via the image diffusion model to produce a denoised spatio-temporal batch, which is then optimized as a whole using multi-step conjugate gradient (CG) in a Krylov subspace to enforce data consistency with the spatio-temporal degradation operator. To prevent the batch-consistent noise initialization from collapsing all frames to identical images, the method introduces a batch-consistent sampling strategy: the same stochastic noise is shared across all frames during both initialization and re-noising, while the frame-dependent CG perturbation in the spatio-temporal volume gradually diverges the frames to satisfy the measurement constraint. This synergistic combination of synchronized stochasticity and spatio-temporal optimization yields temporally coherent yet frame-distinct reconstructions, achieving 10×–50× speedup over prior diffusion-based solvers while outperforming them on PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and FVD.

## Results

On the DAVIS dataset for temporal degradation, the proposed method (20 NFE) achieves PSNR of 43.16 dB (uniform PSF k=7), 39.69 dB (uniform PSF k=13), and 34.25 dB (Gaussian PSF σ=1.0), with FVD values of 0.008, 0.035, and 0.068 (×10⁻³) respectively. For spatio-temporal degradations (100 NFE), it reaches PSNR of 27.77 dB (+deblur), 25.71 dB (+4× SR), and 29.45 dB (+inpainting), consistently outperforming all baselines by large margins. The method achieves a 10×–50× speedup over diffusion-based baselines (12 s vs. 611–1244 s), exceeding 1 FPS for temporal degradation with 20 NFE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method dominates all four baselines across every metric and degradation setting. Against DiffusionMBIR (Chung et al., 2023b), it gains +14.03 dB PSNR on uniform PSF k=7 (43.16 vs. 29.13) and reduces FVD by 54× (0.008 vs. 0.430), while running 51× faster (12 s vs. 611 s). Against DPS (Chung et al., 2022a), the advantage is even more pronounced under complex PSFs: on Gaussian PSF σ=1.0, the proposed method achieves 34.25 dB PSNR versus DPS's 12.21 dB, and FVD of 0.068 vs. 2.210, with a 104× speedup. Against the classical ADMM-TV baseline, the method improves PSNR by +18.7 dB (k=7) and FVD by 190×, confirming that diffusion priors are essential for natural video reconstruction. For blind video deblurring on GoPro, the method (blind) achieves 38.98 dB PSNR and 0.058 FVD, substantially outperforming the DeepDeblur pre-restoration module (30.93 dB, 0.119 FVD). In the additional comparison with DiffIR2VR (Yeh et al., 2024) on 4× video SR, the proposed method achieves 32.88 dB PSNR vs. 30.51 dB and 0.166 FVD vs. 0.212 FVD on DAVIS, and 33.79 dB vs. 31.31 dB on the Pexels high-frame-rate dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Decomposed Diffusion Sampler (DDS) framework of Chung et al. (2024), which reformulates diffusion-based inverse problem solving as Krylov-subspace optimization on the Tweedie-denoised manifold, to the video domain. The key departure is that instead of requiring an expensive video diffusion model, the authors treat the temporal dimension as the batch dimension of pre-trained 2D image diffusion models, introducing a batch-consistent sampling strategy that synchronizes stochastic noise across frames to enforce temporal coherence. This is conceptually related to DiffusionMBIR (Chung et al., 2023b), which used 2D diffusion models for 3D MRI inverse problems, but the batch-consistency mechanism and the multi-step CG perturbation in video space are novel contributions specific to the temporal setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: eliminating the need for video diffusion model training removes a major computational barrier, and the 10×–50× speedup over prior DIS methods (achieving >1 FPS at 20 NFE) makes diffusion-based video restoration feasible in near-real-time settings. However, the current implementation is limited to 256×256 pixel-space ADM, and the blind deblurring extension requires two full sampling passes, doubling compute. Extending the framework to latent diffusion models (e.g., SD-based) to support higher resolutions, and reducing the sampling overhead for blind problems, are the most pressing open questions for broader adoption.
