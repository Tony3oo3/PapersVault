## Problem

Existing Bayesian meta-learning methods for causal discovery (AVICI, CSIvA) only approximate the MAP causal graph rather than the full posterior, and fail to encode essential posterior properties such as edge dependencies and permutation equivariance with respect to nodes. This limits their ability to produce valid acyclic samples from the correct posterior distribution, which is necessary for downstream tasks like effect estimation and active data collection.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an observational dataset of N samples across D variables (tabular data) and produces multiple samples from the posterior over DAGs, where each sample is guaranteed acyclic by construction. Rather than outputting marginal edge probabilities, the decoder generates a permutation matrix Q and a lower triangular binary matrix A per sample, combined as G = QAQ^T to yield a valid DAG. This allows the model to represent correlated edge structures and produce diverse causal hypotheses consistent with the data.

## Method

BCNP is an encoder-decoder transformer trained via KL-divergence between the true Bayesian causal model posterior and the model's approximate posterior. The encoder alternates attention over samples and nodes, using a learned query vector with cross-attention to produce a summary representation that is permutation-invariant over samples and permutation-equivariant over nodes. The key architectural novelty is the decoder, which parametrizes a distribution over DAGs by combining a Gumbel-Sinkhorn distribution (for sampling permutation matrices, ensuring acyclicity) with a multi-head parameter attention mechanism (for sampling lower triangular Bernoulli matrices, ensuring edge dependencies and permutation equivariance). The loss approximates the log-probability of a DAG by Monte Carlo averaging over S permutation samples, with the Hungarian algorithm used for discrete permutations in the forward pass and a straight-through estimator for backpropagation.

## Results

On the Syntren semi-synthetic gene expression dataset (20 nodes, 500 samples), BCNP achieves the best AUC of 0.84±0.02 and log probability of −76.35±3.61, outperforming DiBS (AUC 0.81, log prob −83.31), AVICI (AUC 0.66, log prob −90.82), BayesDAG (AUC 0.52, log prob −195.53), and CSIvA (AUC 0.37, log prob −100.59). On the hardest synthetic setting (20-node Linear ER60), BCNP reaches AUC 0.85–0.86 and expected edge F1 of 0.36, compared to AVICI's 0.83 AUC and 0.31 F1, while explicit Bayesian methods (DiBS, BayesDAG) drop to AUC 0.40–0.51. In the two-variable unidentifiable case, BCNP correctly samples X→Y and Y→X with ~50% probability each, whereas AVICI produces cyclic samples 19% of the time and no-edge samples 32% of the time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BCNP outperforms all four baselines on the Syntren dataset across AUC, log probability, and expected edge F1, with the largest margins over BayesDAG (AUC 0.84 vs 0.52) and CSIvA (AUC 0.84 vs 0.37). Against DiBS, BCNP gains ~0.03 AUC and ~7 log-probability points on Syntren, and the gap widens dramatically on dense synthetic graphs (e.g., AUC 0.85 vs 0.40 on Linear ER60). Against AVICI, BCNP achieves comparable AUC on synthetic data (0.85 vs 0.83 on Linear ER60) but substantially better log probability (−121.87 vs −126.31) and edge F1 (0.36 vs 0.31); critically, AVICI cannot sample valid DAGs from the posterior (producing cyclic and no-edge samples in the 2-variable case), a failure mode invisible to AUC but reflected in the lower F1. Against CSIvA, BCNP wins decisively on every metric and dataset, with CSIvA's autoregressive decoder and lack of permutation equivariance causing it to collapse to near-random performance (AUC ~0.49–0.51) as the number of variables increases. A single BCNP model trained on a mixture of all distributions (BCNP All Data) matches or exceeds distribution-specific models, suggesting robustness to data-distribution mismatch.

## Relation to Prior Work

BCNP extends the Bayesian meta-learning framework for causal discovery established by AVICI (Lorch et al., 2022) and CSIvA (Ke et al., 2022), which reformulate posterior estimation as a supervised learning task over datasets. The encoder architecture is directly inherited from these works (alternating sample/node attention transformers), while the decoder is the key departure: it combines the Gumbel-Sinkhorn permutation sampling of Mena et al. (2018) with the permutation-plus-lower-triangular-matrix DAG parameterization of Charpentier et al. (2021) and Annadani et al. (2024) to produce acyclic samples by construction. Unlike AVICI (which only estimates marginal edge probabilities) and CSIvA (which captures edge dependencies but lacks permutation equivariance and acyclicity guarantees), BCNP simultaneously encodes all three posterior properties—acyclicity, edge correlation, and node permutation equivariance—within a single differentiable decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to sample directly from the posterior over DAGs (rather than only estimating the MAP) is essential for downstream tasks like active causal inference and effect estimation, where the full uncertainty distribution is needed. A practically useful finding is that training on a mixture of function types and graph densities (BCNP All Data) performs comparably to or better than distribution-specific training, suggesting robustness to model misspecification. However, performance degrades notably on GPCDE (Gaussian process) data, and the paper does not incorporate interventional data or provide a rigorous analysis of out-of-distribution generalization. Open questions include scaling to graphs with hundreds of nodes, integrating interventional data, and whether the meta-learning approach can match explicit Bayesian models when the latter are well-specified.
