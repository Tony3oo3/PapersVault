## Problem

Existing large reconstruction models (LRMs) that generate 3D assets from multi-view images suffer from grid-shaped artifacts due to deconvolutional upsampling, loss of high-frequency details from pre-trained DINO encoders, and significant quality degradation when extracting meshes via Marching Cubes. Additionally, these feed-forward pipelines lack a mechanism to faithfully reconstruct complex textures such as text and portraits on object surfaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250739/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 4 multi-view RGB images at 512×512 resolution, augmented with binary foreground masks and Plücker coordinates encoding camera rays, and produces a textured 3D mesh at 256 grid resolution. The mesh can be rasterized to render novel-view RGB images and normal maps. A per-instance texture refinement step reuses the same 4 input views to fine-tune surface colors in approximately 4 seconds on an A100 GPU (20 optimization steps at 5 iterations/second).

## Method

GTR modifies the standard LRM architecture in three ways: replacing the DINO ViT encoder with a convolutional encoder (trained from scratch, accepting multi-channel input of images + masks + Plücker rays), substituting deconvolutional upsampling with a linear layer followed by PixelShuffle to eliminate grid artifacts, and splitting the NeRF decoder into separate shallow MLPs for color (f_c, 4 layers) and density (f_d, 3 layers). Training proceeds in two stages: first optimizing the full pipeline with NeRF volume rendering (MSE + LPIPS loss), then fine-tuning through a differentiable mesh representation extracted via DiffMC (density-to-SDF conversion at level set s=10), supervised with RGB, LPIPS, depth, mask, and normal losses. A lightweight per-instance texture refinement step then freezes the triplane generator and density MLP while fine-tuning only the triplane features and color MLP f_c on the extracted mesh surface using an L2 loss against the input views, enabling rapid (4-second) recovery of fine texture details without re-optimizing the heavy generator.

## Results

On the GSO dataset (4-view input), GTR with texture refinement achieves PSNR 29.79, SSIM 0.960, LPIPS 0.047, Chamfer Distance 0.740, and IoU 0.708, outperforming all baselines on every metric. On OmniObject3D, the same configuration reaches PSNR 25.40, SSIM 0.937, LPIPS 0.059, CD 0.504, and IoU 0.728. The feed-forward model alone (without per-instance texture refinement) already achieves PSNR 28.67 and IoU 0.708 on GSO, demonstrating that the architecture and geometry refinement stages contribute the bulk of the improvement. The full pipeline (feed-forward + 4-second texture refinement) adds approximately 1.1 dB PSNR and 0.014 SSIM on GSO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GTR outperforms all six baselines on every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, CD, IoU) on both GSO and OmniObject3D. The strongest competitor is LGM (Tang et al., 2024), which uses Gaussian Splatting; GTR surpasses it by 4.56 dB PSNR (29.79 vs. 25.23) and 0.107 IoU (0.708 vs. 0.601) on GSO, while also producing explicit meshes in ~5 seconds versus LGM's ~1 minute for mesh extraction. InstantMesh (Xu et al., 2024a) is the second-closest baseline, with GTR leading by 5.05 dB PSNR and 0.063 IoU on GSO; notably, GTR's feed-forward model alone already exceeds InstantMesh's mesh variant by 4.23 dB PSNR. LRM (Hong et al., 2024) is the weakest baseline, trailing GTR by 9.34 dB PSNR and 0.356 IoU on GSO, largely because it takes only a single front-view image. On OmniObject3D, GTR leads LGM (GS) by 2.57 dB PSNR and 0.102 IoU, and InstantMesh (Mesh) by 3.26 dB PSNR and 0.053 IoU. The paper also notes that baselines without explicit geometry representation during training (e.g., LGM's Gaussian Splatting) typically degrade when converted to meshes, whereas GTR's mesh-native training avoids this gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the LRM (Hong et al., 2024) architecture, which pairs a transformer-based triplane generator with a NeRF decoder, by replacing the DINO ViT image encoder with a convolutional encoder, swapping deconvolution upsampling for PixelShuffle, and splitting the NeRF decoder into separate density and color MLPs. For geometry refinement, it adopts Differentiable Marching Cubes (DiffMC, Wei et al., 2023) to enable end-to-end mesh rendering supervision, building on the differentiable mesh literature (DMTet, Flexicubes). The per-instance texture refinement stage draws a conceptual parallel to Gaussian Splatting's color memorization property, but applies it to a triplane+MLP pipeline rather than explicit Gaussians.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the end-to-end pipeline producing high-quality textured meshes in under 5 seconds (1s feed-forward + 4s texture refinement), which is competitive with or faster than concurrent methods like LGM that require ~1 minute for mesh extraction. The two-stage NeRF-to-mesh training recipe and the lightweight per-instance fine-tuning are straightforward to adopt in existing LRM pipelines. Open questions remain: the method requires posed multi-view input (no unposed handling), is limited to object-centric assets, and the 256-resolution triplane may still be insufficient for very fine geometric details. Extending to complex scenes and unposed inputs are natural next steps.
