## Problem

The accuracy of density functional theory is limited by the approximation of the exchange-correlation (XC) functional, particularly its non-local contributions. Existing machine-learned XC functionals are either semi-local (unable to capture molecular-range interactions like Van der Waals forces) or non-local but suffer from poor computational scaling or dependence on costly reference electron densities. The critical open challenge is efficiently capturing non-local molecular-range interactions in a scalable, data-efficient manner within the DFT framework.

## Input / Output

EG-XC takes the electron density (represented as a density matrix in a finite atom-centered basis set) and nuclear positions as input, and produces a scalar exchange-correlation energy. Uniquely, the continuous electron density is compressed into a finite nuclei-centered SO(3)-equivariant point cloud via equivariant convolution with radial and spherical-harmonic filters, making the representation basis-set independent. Training requires only energy targets (no reference densities) because gradients are backpropagated through a differentiable self-consistent field solver, with the XC functional needing to be at least twice differentiable (achieved via SiLU activations for C^∞ smoothness).

## Method

EG-XC consists of four components: (1) nuclei-centered equivariant embeddings obtained by convolving a soft-partitioned electron density with SO(3)-equivariant filters (products of spherical harmonics and sin/cos radial filters) at each nuclear position, yielding a finite point cloud; (2) equivariant message passing (NequIP-style with SO(3)-equivariant convolutions) over T layers to propagate molecular-range information between nuclei; (3) a non-local reweighted meta-GGA where a non-local feature density g_NL is constructed from the equivariant embeddings and used by an MLP to reweight a semi-local mGGA energy density (Dick & Fernandez-Serra 2021); and (4) a graph readout MLP on the invariant (l=0) embeddings for additional global corrections. The total XC energy is the integral of the reweighted mGGA density over the electron density plus the graph readout sum. The key insight is that the equivariant point cloud representation is derived purely from the electron density (not nuclear charges), enabling proper differentiation through the SCF procedure while still capturing non-local molecular-range effects.

## Results

On the MD17 CCSD(T) dataset, EG-XC achieves the lowest MAE on 3 of 5 molecules (Aspirin: 0.69 mEh, Benzene: 0.10 mEh, Ethanol: 0.21 mEh), reducing errors of the semi-local baseline (Dick) by a factor of 2–4. On the 3BPA structural extrapolation task, EG-XC reduces relative MAE by 35–50% compared to the next-best method across all test sets, and is the only method achieving chemical accuracy (1.6 mEh) on the far OOD 1200K samples (1.39 mEh). On QM9 size extrapolation, EG-XC trained on QM9(6) outperforms the best alternative trained on QM9(7) with 5× more data, and is at least 33% more accurate on 9-heavy-atom test molecules.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EG-XC outperforms all force field baselines (SchNet, PaiNN, NequIP) by a wide margin on MD17, with MAEs roughly 3–10× lower, confirming that pure force fields struggle to reconstruct CCSD(T) energy surfaces from energy labels alone. Against the Δ-ML variants (force fields + DFT), EG-XC still wins on all five MD17 molecules, with the closest competitor being Δ-NequIP (1.04 mEh on Aspirin vs. EG-XC's 0.69). Compared to the semi-local learnable XC functional (Dick & Fernandez-Serra 2021), EG-XC reduces MAE by a factor of 2–4 across all molecules, demonstrating the value of the non-local equivariant GNN component. On 3BPA extrapolation, EG-XC achieves 35–50% lower relative MAE than the next-best method (Δ-NequIP) on every test set, and is the only method reaching chemical accuracy on the 1200K OOD samples. On QM9, EG-XC surpasses all baselines from QM9(5) onward, achieving at least 2× lower MAE than Dick on QM9(6) and QM9(7), and matching force fields trained on 5× more and larger molecules. The one exception is QM9(4), where Dick's physical constraints give it a slight edge, suggesting constraints matter most for the smallest systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

EG-XC sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: learnable semi-local (meta-GGA) XC functionals (Dick & Fernandez-Serra 2021, Nagai et al. 2022) and SO(3)-equivariant GNN force fields (NequIP, PaiNN, SchNet). The key departure is that prior non-local ML functionals (Margraf & Reuter 2021, Bystrom & Kozinsky 2022) either scale poorly or require costly reference electron densities, while force fields bypass the quantum-mechanical SCF procedure entirely. EG-XC instead compresses the continuous electron density into a finite SO(3)-equivariant nuclei-centered point cloud via spherical-harmonic convolutions, then applies equivariant message passing (NequIP-style) to capture molecular-range interactions, all while remaining differentiable through the SCF solver (Li et al. 2021) so training requires only energy targets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that equivariant GNNs can be embedded *within* the DFT framework to capture non-local effects (van der Waals, long-range correlation) that semi-local functionals fundamentally cannot, achieving 35–50% lower OOD errors and 5× data efficiency over the best force-field baselines. This opens a path toward accurate, scalable DFT that retains physical structure (unlike pure force fields) while gaining the expressivity of graph neural networks. Key open questions include: enforcing physical constraints in the non-local component, extending to open-shell/spin systems and nucleus-free systems (e.g., homogeneous electron gas), transferring to plane-wave basis sets for periodic systems, and whether multimodal training (densities, forces) can further improve generalization.
