## Problem

Existing SVD-based LLM compression methods (ASVD, FWSVD, SVD-LLM) decompose each layer's weight matrix independently, ignoring the potential for cross-layer parameter sharing. Prior parameter-sharing approaches like Dynamic Tying require training from scratch and force identical weights across layers, causing significant performance degradation. This paper asks whether pretrained LLMs can be compressed more effectively by sharing a common set of basis vectors across layers while retaining layer-specific coefficients, without any retraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes pretrained LLM weight matrices (specifically W_K, W_Q, W_V, W_Up, W_Gate types) and 256 calibration samples from WikiText-2 (2048 tokens each) to compute an activation-aware scaling matrix S via Cholesky decomposition of X^T X over vertically concatenated layer inputs. The output is a compressed model where n consecutive layers share a single basis matrix B'' (d₁×k) while each layer retains its own coefficient matrix C' (k×d₂), reducing total parameters from n·d₁·d₂ to d₁·k + k·d₂·n. The number of basis vectors k is determined by the target compression ratio through a closed-form formula that accounts for the shared structure.

## Method

Basis Sharing horizontally concatenates weight matrices from n consecutive layers into a single matrix, scales it by an activation-aware matrix S (derived from Cholesky decomposition of concatenated input statistics), applies SVD to the scaled product, and decomposes the result into a shared basis matrix B'' = S⁻¹U_k'Σ_k' and per-layer coefficient matrices C'. The key insight is that for certain weight matrix types (W_K, W_Q, W_V, W_Up, W_Gate), the Frobenius loss from SVD on the concatenated matrix is smaller than the sum of individual-layer losses, meaning sharing actually improves approximation quality. A theoretical analysis (Appendix A.3) identifies when this holds based on matrix dimensions: sharing reduces loss when d₁ ≤ d₂ (Case 1) but increases it when d₁ ≥ n·d₂ (Case 2), explaining why W_O and W_Down are excluded. Layers are grouped as adjacent pairs from first to last, with 2-layer grouping optimal for compression ratios above 30%, and the method requires no fine-tuning or gradient computation.

## Results

On LLaMA-7B at 20% compression ratio, Basis Sharing achieves a WikiText-2 PPL of 7.74 and an average zero-shot reasoning accuracy of 0.48, outperforming SVD-LLM (PPL 7.94, accuracy 0.44) and ASVD (PPL 11.14, accuracy 0.43). At 50% compression, Basis Sharing maintains a PPL of 19.99 versus 23.97 for SVD-LLM and 15,358 for ASVD, with average reasoning accuracy of 0.36 vs. 0.33. Across model families at 20% compression, Basis Sharing reduces PPL by up to 26% compared to SVD-LLM (e.g., OPT-6.7B: 11.79 vs. 16.04; Mistral-7B: 7.57 vs. 10.21). After LoRA fine-tuning, Basis Sharing with 2-layer grouping achieves a PPL of 7.14 at 20% and 10.56 at 50% compression on LLaMA-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Basis Sharing consistently outperforms SVD-LLM (the strongest per-layer SVD baseline) across all compression ratios: at 20% it reduces WikiText-2 PPL from 7.94 to 7.74 and improves average reasoning accuracy from 0.44 to 0.48; at 50% the gap widens to PPL 19.99 vs. 23.97 and accuracy 0.36 vs. 0.33. Against ASVD, the advantage is dramatic at higher compression ratios (PPL 19.99 vs. 15,358 at 50%), while at 20% the gap is moderate (7.74 vs. 11.14). FWSVD and vanilla SVD are effectively non-functional beyond 20% compression, producing PPL values in the thousands or tens of thousands. Compared to Dynamic Tying (Hay & Wolf, 2024) on GPT-2 at 20% compression, Basis Sharing achieves lower PPL (43.15 vs. 49.37) with far fewer parameters (94M vs. 264M) and completes compression in 26 seconds versus 13.75 hours of training. The paper also shows that Basis Sharing generalizes across model families, achieving up to 26% PPL reduction over SVD-LLM on OPT-6.7B and Mistral-7B at 20% compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SVD-based LLM compression line (FWSVD, ASVD, SVD-LLM) by moving from per-layer weight decomposition to cross-layer decomposition, where weight matrices in multiple layers are horizontally concatenated before SVD, yielding a shared basis matrix and layer-specific coefficient matrices. It directly builds on SVD-LLM's activation-aware scaling matrix S (via Cholesky decomposition of X^T X) but generalizes it to multiple layers simultaneously. Conceptually, it departs from prior parameter-sharing work (Universal Transformers, SubFormer, Dynamic Tying) that enforced identical weights across layers and required training from scratch; instead, Basis Sharing operates on pretrained models and preserves layer-specific functionality through unique coefficients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, training-free compression path that reduces memory footprint while maintaining competitive perplexity and reasoning accuracy up to 50% compression, which is relevant for deploying LLMs on resource-constrained hardware. The structural finding that W_K, W_Q, W_V, W_Up, and W_Gate benefit from cross-layer sharing while W_O and W_Down do not provides actionable guidance for which matrices to compress jointly. Open questions include whether vertical concatenation (proposed for W_Down) can close the remaining gap, how the method scales beyond 50% compression, and whether the calibration-data requirement (256 WikiText-2 samples) generalizes well to domain-specific deployments.
