## Problem

Popular LLM sampling methods like top-p (nucleus sampling) use fixed truncation thresholds that do not adapt to the model's confidence at each decoding step. At higher temperatures, this causes low-probability tokens to enter the sampling pool, producing incoherent or repetitive outputs, while conservative settings sacrifice creativity. The paper addresses this quality-diversity tradeoff by proposing a dynamic threshold that responds to per-step model confidence.

## Input / Output

At each decoding step, the method consumes the temperature-scaled token probability distribution over the vocabulary and a single scalar hyperparameter p_base (typically 0.05–0.1). It outputs a filtered candidate pool from which the next token is sampled via standard categorical sampling. Unlike top-p, which truncates by cumulative probability mass, or top-k, which truncates by a fixed token count, min-p's effective threshold varies per step: in high-confidence contexts it can truncate a much larger fraction of the distribution than its p_base value suggests, while in low-confidence contexts it retains a broader pool.

## Method

Min-p sampling computes the maximum token probability p_max at each step and sets the truncation threshold to p_scaled = p_base × p_max. All tokens with probability below p_scaled are removed, and the next token is sampled from the renormalized remaining distribution. The key insight is that this makes the threshold relative to model confidence: when the model is highly confident (p_max near 1), the threshold is high and the pool shrinks to a few tokens, preserving coherence; when the model is uncertain (p_max low), the threshold drops and the pool expands, allowing diverse continuations. This dynamic behavior means min-p automatically tightens filtering in factual or high-certainty contexts (e.g., completing 'dispersion of ___' → 'light') while loosening it in open-ended creative contexts, all with a single hyperparameter and negligible computational overhead compared to entropy-based methods like η-sampling or Mirostat.

## Results

On GPQA Main (Mistral 7B, 5-shot), min-p achieves 29.18% accuracy at τ=0.7 and maintains 24.55% at τ=3.0, where top-p drops to 0.46% and temperature-only to 0.89%. On GSM8K CoT (8-shot), min-p reaches 35.18% at τ=0.7 and 18.42% at τ=1.5, compared to top-p's 36.09% and 0.68% respectively. On AlpacaEval Creative Writing, min-p (τ=1.5, p_base=0.1) achieves a 56.54% win rate versus top-p's 50.43% at τ=1.0. On Mistral Large (123B), min-p sustains 22.77% GPQA accuracy at τ=3.0 where top-p collapses to 2.01%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Min-p outperforms top-p (Holtzman et al., 2020) at all temperatures ≥1.5 on both GPQA and GSM8K, with the gap widening sharply at high temperature (e.g., 24.55% vs 0.46% on GPQA at τ=3.0). Against top-k (Fan et al., 2018), min-p wins at every temperature tested, most dramatically at τ=2.0–3.0 where top-k retains 5.88–16.52% GPQA accuracy versus min-p's 24.55–26.34%. Min-p also surpasses η-sampling and ε-sampling (Hewitt et al., 2022) at all shared temperature points, and critically remains functional at τ≥2 where both entropy-based methods fail entirely due to exponential runtime blowup. On AlpacaEval Creative Writing, min-p (56.54% win rate at τ=1.5) substantially outperforms Mirostat (Basu et al., 2021), which scores only 14.23–16.69%, and also beats ε-sampling (45.51%) and temperature-only (53.18%). At low temperatures (τ≤0.5), min-p and top-p perform comparably within error margins, confirming that min-p's primary advantage is robustness at elevated temperatures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the established line of work on stochastic sampling strategies for autoregressive LLMs, building directly on temperature scaling (Ackley et al., 1985), top-k (Fan et al., 2018), and top-p/nucleus sampling (Holtzman et al., 2020). Its key conceptual departure is replacing the fixed truncation threshold with a dynamic one scaled by the top token's probability (p_max), making the threshold context-sensitive without requiring the entropy estimation or auxiliary computations used by η-sampling, ε-sampling, or Mirostat. The method is positioned as a lightweight, drop-in alternative that achieves the adaptive behavior of entropy-based methods with negligible computational overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: min-p has been integrated into Hugging Face Transformers, vLLM, SGLang, and dozens of other inference frameworks, making it a default option for a large portion of the open-source LLM ecosystem. The finding that a simple p_max-scaled threshold outperforms both fixed-threshold methods and more complex entropy-based approaches at high temperatures provides practitioners with a reliable tool for creative generation without coherence loss. Limitations include the small human-evaluation sample size (3 outputs per configuration), marginal gains at low temperatures, and the absence of a formal theoretical analysis of why the dynamic threshold is effective. Open questions include optimal interaction with other decoding techniques (repetition penalties, speculative decoding) and whether the approach generalizes to non-autoregressive or diffusion-based language models.
