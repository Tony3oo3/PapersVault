## Problem

Existing offline RL methods that mitigate OOD Q-value overestimation (via policy constraints, value penalization, or in-sample-only learning) introduce an over-constraint issue: they become overly conservative in OOD regions, suppressing Q-function generalization and producing inaccurate Q-value estimates that hinder policy improvement. The paper asks whether better Q-value estimation can be achieved by actively enhancing Q-function generalization in OOD regions, and identifies the Convex Hull and its Neighborhood (CHN) as the safe boundary within which such generalization is theoretically justified.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard offline RL dataset of (state, action, reward, next_state, done) tuples collected by a behavior policy, and at inference takes a state to produce an action via the learned policy. During training, OOD actions are generated on-the-fly by adding clipped Gaussian noise (scale 0.6, clip ±0.5) to in-sample actions from the dataset, ensuring they remain within the CHN boundary. The critic outputs Q-values for both in-sample and these perturbed OOD state-action pairs, while the actor outputs a deterministic action given a state.

## Method

The core contribution is the Smooth Bellman Operator (SBO), which decomposes the Bellman update into a base empirical Bellman backup (for in-sample actions) and a smooth generalization operator G1 (for OOD actions within the CHN). G1 replaces the standard Bellman target for OOD actions with the Q-value of a neighboring in-sample action, effectively smoothing OOD Q-values toward nearby well-estimated in-sample values. The CHN provides two safety guarantees under the NTK regime: bounded Q-value difference from in-sample projections and uniform continuity, ensuring that generalization within this region cannot produce uncontrolled overestimation. The practical algorithm SQOG implements this by adding a single OOD generalization loss term (weighted by β) to the standard TD3+BC twin-critic loss, where OOD actions are cheaply sampled by perturbing in-sample actions with clipped Gaussian noise—avoiding the expensive CVAE generative model used by MCQ. The actor loss remains identical to TD3+BC, and the overall training procedure matches TD3+BC's computational cost while achieving SOTA performance on D4RL benchmarks.

## Results

On the D4RL Mujoco benchmarks (12 datasets), SQOG achieves the highest average normalized score of 70.7, outperforming the closest competitor MCQ (68.4), DOGE (64.1), CQL (61.8), IQL (59.9), and TD3+BC (58.2). On Maze2d tasks, SQOG reaches 124.7 average score versus MCQ's 102.2, and on Adroit tasks it achieves a total of 149.6 versus MCQ's 123.3. Critically, SQOG matches TD3+BC's runtime of 0.4 hours while MCQ requires 8.0 hours and CQL requires 10.8 hours, making SQOG 20× faster than MCQ with superior performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SQOG outperforms all six baselines on the Mujoco average (70.7 vs. MCQ 68.4, DOGE 64.1, CQL 61.8, IQL 59.9, TD3+BC 58.2, BC 36.5) and dominates on Maze2d (124.7 vs. MCQ 102.2) and Adroit (149.6 vs. MCQ 123.3). The closest competitor is MCQ, which SQOG beats by 2.3 points on Mujoco average, 22.5 on Maze2d, and 26.3 on Adroit, while being 20× faster (0.4h vs. 8.0h) due to avoiding MCQ's CVAE generative model. SQOG wins on 9 of 12 individual Mujoco datasets but loses to MCQ on hopper-random (15.6 vs. 31.0), walker2d-medium (82.9 vs. 88.4), and halfcheetah-medium-replay (46.4 vs. 51.5). Compared to TD3+BC, SQOG achieves a 70% improvement on hopper-medium (100.6 vs. 59.3) at the same 0.4h runtime. CQL and IQL are consistently outperformed across all three task families, with CQL also being 27× slower. DOGE is competitive on some Mujoco datasets (e.g., hopper-medium 98.6) but is not evaluated on Maze2d or Adroit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DOGE's (Li et al., 2022) convex hull analysis by introducing the Convex Hull and its Neighborhood (CHN), broadening the safe generalization boundary beyond the convex hull itself. It builds on the TD3+BC actor-critic framework (Fujimoto & Gu, 2021) but departs conceptually from the dominant paradigm of constraining Q-values in OOD regions (as in CQL, IQL, MCQ) by instead actively generalizing Q-values within the CHN using neighboring in-sample Q-values via the Smooth Bellman Operator. The NTK-based theoretical analysis follows DOGE's approach but is extended to cover the neighborhood region and the new SBO operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses the 'over-constraint issue'—a recognized but underexplored limitation where existing offline RL methods become overly conservative in OOD regions, degrading Q-value estimation and policy improvement. The practical algorithm SQOG achieves SOTA results with 20× lower computational cost than MCQ by avoiding generative models, making it attractive for practitioners. However, the approach is limited to OOD regions within the CHN; extending safe generalization to regions outside the CHN remains an open challenge, and the paper acknowledges that precisely identifying CHN boundaries is impractical in high-dimensional settings.
