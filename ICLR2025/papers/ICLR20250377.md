## Problem

Task arithmetic merges fine-tuned models via weighted combination of task vectors, but its effectiveness depends on the linearity of the model (a linear relationship between weight differences and output feature differences). Conventional fine-tuned models typically lack this linearity at the full-model level, and existing remedies require retraining with original data to enforce global linearity. This paper asks whether the needed linearity already exists at a finer granularity within the model, and whether it can be exploited directly without any retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250377/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes T fine-tuned models sharing a common pre-trained backbone (e.g., Llama-2-7B/13B), the pre-trained model itself, and a very small set of task-related samples (as few as 1–30 per task) used solely to compute per-module merging weights via a single forward pass through the pre-trained model. The output is a merged model with the identical architecture to the input models, where each submodule (layer, attention block, or MLP) carries its own independently computed merging weight rather than a single global scalar.

## Method

The paper first demonstrates via a Non-linearity Score and a Projection Distance metric that submodules (individual layers, self-attention blocks, and MLPs) exhibit near-perfect linearity even when the full model does not, and that this property persists when merging two or three models. Building on this, it decomposes each model into submodules and independently merges them using task arithmetic with per-module weights α_t^i. Under the linearity assumption, the objective of minimizing the squared distance between the merged module's output and each task's module output reduces to a linear system Aα = b, where A is a T×T matrix of inner products of feature-difference vectors and b is a T-vector of self-inner products, yielding a closed-form solution α = A⁻¹b. A practical normalization term is added to balance samples of differing magnitudes across tasks. The entire procedure is training-free, requires no hyperparameter search, and needs only ~30 samples per task and a handful of forward passes through the pre-trained model.

## Results

On Llama-2-13B, the proposed Attn/MLP-level merging achieves 35.11 (Math&Coding), 66.11 (Math&Translate), 52.51 (Coding&Translate), and 51.05 (Math&Coding&Translate) average accuracy, outperforming Task Arithmetic (34.60, 64.72, 53.22, 48.24) in 3 of 4 merging settings. The largest gain appears in the three-model merging scenario, where the method improves from 48.24 to 51.05 (+2.8%). On Llama-2-7B, the method similarly surpasses Task Arithmetic in the three-model setting (42.88 vs. 39.59, +3.3%) and Math&Translate (55.86 vs. 54.65), while showing slight degradation in Coding&Translate (49.80 vs. 51.11).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four baselines: Weight Average, DARE, Task Arithmetic, and TIES (in the appendix). The proposed method consistently outperforms Weight Average and DARE across all merging settings on both Llama-2-7B and Llama-2-13B, with margins of 2–10 points depending on the configuration. Against Task Arithmetic (the strongest baseline), the method wins in 3 of 4 two-model settings on Llama-2-13B (e.g., Math&Translate: 66.11 vs. 64.72) and in the critical three-model setting (51.05 vs. 48.24, a +2.8% gain), but slightly underperforms in Coding&Translate (52.51 vs. 53.22). On Llama-2-7B, the advantage over Task Arithmetic is more pronounced in the three-model case (42.88 vs. 39.59, +3.3%) but the method trails in Coding&Translate (49.80 vs. 51.11). In the appendix comparison (Table 9), the method also outperforms TIES (42.88 vs. 42.12) and Consensus TA (42.88 vs. 42.14) in the three-model Llama-2-7B setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends task arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2023) by moving the merging operation from the global model level to individual submodules (layers, attention blocks, MLPs), and adopts the linearity framework from Ortiz-Jiménez et al. (2023) to justify this decomposition. The key conceptual departure is from prior work that seeks to *impose* global linearity through constrained retraining (Ortiz-Jiménez et al., 2023; Tang et al., 2024; Jin et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024): here, the authors argue that linearity already exists at the submodule level and can be exploited directly without any retraining. The closed-form solution for per-module merging weights builds on the merging objective from Jin et al. (2023), but the linearity assumption at the module level (rather than the model level) is what makes the linearization step valid and the solution tractable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that submodules exhibit near-zero non-linearity scores even when the full model does not provides a structural explanation for why task arithmetic partially works and suggests a principled path to improve it without retraining. Practically, the method is attractive because it is training-free, requires only ~30 samples per task, and has no tunable hyperparameters. However, the performance gains over standard task arithmetic are modest (1–3% in most settings), the method fails entirely at the model level and produces unstable results at the head level, and it requires all models to share the same backbone. Open questions include why head-level merging collapses despite good linearity (possibly due to functional specialization of individual heads) and whether a head-aware merging objective could recover performance at that granularity.
