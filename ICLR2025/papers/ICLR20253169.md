## Problem

Existing customized concept swapping (CCS) methods suffer from two key limitations: inconsistency, where attention-based approaches fail to handle large shape differences between source and target concepts while score distillation methods alter the background, and inefficiency, where score distillation methods perform redundant forward passes at every timestep. No dedicated benchmark for evaluating CCS also existed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253169/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source image with a descriptive prompt, a small set (typically fewer than 5) of target concept images used for one-time DreamBooth customization (~8.5 min per concept), and a target prompt containing a rare token (e.g., 'sks teapot'). It produces a single swapped image in the latent space of Stable Diffusion v2.1-base, optimized over 550 SGD steps on a single GPU. The source concept's bounding box is automatically extracted from cross-attention maps during the first three denoising steps, requiring no external segmentation module.

## Method

INSTANTSWAP is a training-free framework extending Delta Denoising Score (DDS) with three components. First, it automatically generates a bounding box for the source concept by combining self-attention and cross-attention maps (Â^c = A^s · (A^c)^α) with a threshold, then applies Background Gradient Masking (BGM) to zero out gradients outside the box, confining edits to the foreground. Second, it introduces Semantic-Enhanced Concept Representation (SECR), which crops the image feature within the bbox, computes a partial cross-attention with the concept's CLIP embedding, and injects the result back—effectively turning the target branch into a concept adder and the source branch into a concept remover. Third, Step-Skipping Gradient Updating (SSGU) computes gradients only at every λ-th timestep (λ=5 by default) and reuses the last anchor gradient for intervening steps, reducing forward passes by 1/λ and cutting inference time to ~20 s while preserving swapping quality.

## Results

On ConSwapBench (1,600 generated images), INSTANTSWAP achieves the best score on all seven evaluation metrics: CLIP-I 75.00 (foreground consistency), PSNR 27.39, LPIPS 47.68×10⁻³, MSE 27.87×10⁻⁴, SSIM 86.58×10⁻² (background consistency), CLIP-T 25.74 (overall prompt alignment), and 19.83 s inference time (efficiency). The method is the fastest by a wide margin, running 2× faster than SDS (40.37 s), 3.4× faster than DDS (66.89 s), and ~7× faster than CDS and PhotoSwap (~140 s each).

## Baselines & Comparisons

INSTANTSWAP outperforms all six baselines on every single metric, making it a clear-cut win across the board. Against score-distillation methods (SDS, DDS, CDS), it improves foreground CLIP-I by 1.3–3.95 points and background PSNR by 3.3–6.6 dB, while also being 2–7× faster. Against attention-based methods (PhotoSwap, PnPInv+P2P), it gains 4.26–4.86 CLIP-I points and 2.75–5.32 dB PSNR, with a 1.9–7× speedup. The largest absolute gaps appear in background preservation (LPIPS 47.68 vs. 339.51 for SDS) and inference speed (19.83 s vs. 140.26 s for CDS), reflecting the paper's core contributions of BGM for background consistency and SSGU for efficiency. The closest competitor is PnPInv+P2P on CLIP-T (24.25 vs. 25.74), but INSTANTSWAP still leads by 1.49 points while being nearly 2× faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

INSTANTSWAP extends the score distillation optimization framework (SDS, DDS) for image editing into the customized concept swapping (CCS) setting, where a user-specified target concept (via DreamBooth or Textual Inversion) replaces a source concept in an image. It departs from prior CCS methods like PhotoSwap and SwapAnything by replacing per-image training or external mask modules with an automatic bounding-box extraction from attention maps, and by injecting semantic information via a cross-attention mechanism (SECR) to handle sharp shape differences. The Step-Skipping Gradient Updating (SSGU) strategy is a novel efficiency contribution that generalizes beyond this paper's method to other score distillation approaches (SDS, DDS, CoSD).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of ConSwapBench fills a clear gap as the first dedicated benchmark for CCS, enabling systematic evaluation of a task that previously lacked standardized metrics. SSGU's transferability to other score distillation methods (reducing inference time by ~5×) offers a broadly applicable efficiency gain for the entire score distillation editing family. Key limitations include the base model's inability to perceive relative object sizes (e.g., generating a cup larger than a table), and the method's current dependence on U-Net cross-attention layers, making direct integration with DiT-based models (SD3) an open problem. Future directions include extending to video, developing task-specific evaluation metrics, and achieving more lightweight swapping.
