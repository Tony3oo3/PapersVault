## Problem

Existing text-to-texture synthesis methods that progressively inpaint across predefined viewpoints suffer from a gradual shift in texture properties (color, pattern, material) as the camera moves, because previously synthesized textures are merged only once per diffusion timestep and the autoregressive pipeline progressively obscures early-viewpoint information. Additionally, fixed camera positions that ignore object geometry lead to suboptimal texture conditioning for complex shapes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251639/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a textureless 3D mesh (e.g., from Objaverse or ShapeNet) and a short text prompt (e.g., 'a hippopotamus') as input, and produces a fully textured mesh with view-consistent appearance. Internally, the mesh is rendered at 512×512 from multiple viewpoints with depth maps extracted as conditioning; fine-tuning requires only five rendered images from viewpoints near the initial camera position, eliminating the need for an external 3D texture dataset.

## Method

TexTailor extends the Repaint resampling scheme into the DDIM non-Markovian process, performing R=3 noise-add/denoise/merge cycles at each of 30 timesteps to thoroughly harmonize known and unknown latent regions within a single view. To counter the autoregressive gradual shift, it fine-tunes only the ControlNet component of a Stable Diffusion model on five resampled images using a combined loss (L_Fine + λ·L_pre), where the performance preservation loss L_pre constrains the fine-tuned noise predictor to remain close to the frozen pretrained network's output, preventing catastrophic forgetting of depth- and text-conditioned generation. An adaptive viewpoint refinement step monitors the pixel ratio of already-textured ('keep') to untextured ('new') regions at each candidate camera; when this ratio drops below a threshold β, an intermediate viewpoint is automatically inserted by interpolating azimuth and elevation angles, ensuring sufficient texture conditioning for complex geometries without manual camera placement.

## Results

On a 410-mesh subset of Objaverse (225 categories), TexTailor achieves the best LPIPS of 37.889 and FID of 29.998, outperforming all four baselines on both metrics. The closest competitor on LPIPS is Paint-it (38.316), while on FID the gap is largest against Paint-it (39.823) and TEXTure (43.337). Qualitative results on ShapeNet cars and RenderPeople clothed humans further demonstrate superior view-consistency, particularly for fine-grained categories where SDS-based methods struggle.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TexTailor outperforms all four baselines on both LPIPS and FID. Against the closest inpainting-based competitor Text2Tex (Chen et al., 2023), TexTailor improves LPIPS by ~1.0 points (37.889 vs. 38.931) and FID by ~3.5 points (29.998 vs. 33.487), primarily by reducing the gradual texture shift across viewpoints. Against Paint-it (Youwang et al., 2024), which uses SDS-based texture map optimization, TexTailor achieves a substantially lower FID (29.998 vs. 39.823), indicating better texture quality and diversity without the oversaturation artifacts inherent to SDS. The SDS-based methods Latent-Paint (Metzer et al., 2023) and TEXTure (Richardson et al., 2023) are significantly outperformed on both metrics (LPIPS gaps of ~16–17 points), reflecting their susceptibility to oversaturation, over-smoothing, and low diversity. On ShapeNet cars, where SDS-based baselines fail due to differential renderer optimization issues, TexTailor produces coherent textures while Text2Tex still shows visible inconsistencies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-viewpoint inpainting pipeline established by Text2Tex (Chen et al., 2023a) and TEXTure (Richardson et al., 2023), which progressively texture a mesh by segmenting each view into keep/new/update/ignore regions and applying depth-conditioned inpainting. The key departure is twofold: (1) it ports the iterative resampling scheme from 2D inpainting (Repaint, Lugmayr et al., 2022) into the DDIM non-Markovian framework, enabling multiple known/unknown merges per timestep with far fewer total steps (30 vs. 250), and (2) it fine-tunes the ControlNet component on a handful of resampled images with a novel performance preservation loss to counteract the autoregressive drift in texture properties across viewpoints. The adaptive viewpoint refinement is a practical addition that has no direct analogue in prior text-to-texture work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and visually obvious failure mode—gradual texture drift across viewpoints—that plagues all autoregressive multi-view inpainting approaches, and offers a clean, dataset-free remedy (fine-tuning on 5 self-generated images). However, the method's practical utility is tempered by its reliance on the quality of those 5 training images, the ~1.5-hour fine-tuning cost per mesh, and the tendency for patterns learned near the initial viewpoint to repeat unrealistically across the object. Open questions include whether lighter-weight adaptation (e.g., LoRA) can reduce the fine-tuning burden, whether better multi-view consistency metrics than LPIPS can capture spatial misalignment in overlapping regions, and how to handle non-diffuse materials (glossy, metallic, translucent) where baked-in lighting artifacts limit real-world usability.
