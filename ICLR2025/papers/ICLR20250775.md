## Problem

Existing LVLM evaluation benchmarks suffer from two critical flaws: severe data contamination (up to 84% image-only overlap with pre-training corpora like LAION-100M) and a static, fixed-complexity design that cannot keep pace with rapidly improving models. This raises the fundamental question of whether models genuinely perceive and reason about inputs or merely recall memorized training examples, and prevents meaningful assessment of model capability boundaries as they evolve.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an original VQA sample consisting of an RGB image, a natural-language question, and a ground-truth answer (e.g., from SEEDBench, MMBench, or MME). It produces a dynamic variant where the image is modified via object insertion/removal or outpainting, the question is rewritten at word, sentence, or context level, and the ground-truth answer is preserved unchanged. The output is a new VQA pair that is visually and semantically consistent with the original but presents a different level of difficulty, enabling complexity-controlled evaluation without manual annotation.

## Method

Vision-Language Bootstrapping (VLB) combines a multimodal bootstrapping module with a judge module. The bootstrapping module applies three image strategies—adding objects (GPT-4V proposes insertable objects with bounding boxes, PowerPaint renders them), removing objects (SAM provides semantic masks, GPT-4V selects removable objects, PowerPaint erases them), and outpainting (extending the image boundary by a configurable ratio)—and four language strategies spanning word substitution, role-play sentence rephrasing, adding relevant image context, and adding irrelevant distracting context. A judge module (InternVL-2) then adversarially verifies that the modified image-question pair still admits the original answer, regenerating samples up to five times if consistency fails. Because each strategy is atomic and tagged with a difficulty level, they can be composed in paired multimodal (12 V×L combinations) or multi-strategy stacks to produce a continuum of benchmark variants from easier to harder than the original, enabling flexible complexity control and significantly reducing data contamination.

## Results

Under the hardest dynamic variant (V1+L4: adding objects + irrelevant context), all evaluated LVLMs show significant performance drops. On MMBench_DEV_Full, GPT-4o drops from 85.27% (vanilla) to 79.55%, InternVL-2 from 86.78% to 78.00%, and DeepSeek-VL from 78.30% to 73.82%. Conversely, the easiest variant (V2+L3: removing objects + relevant context) boosts performance, with GPT-4o reaching 86.32% and InternVL-2 reaching 88.17% on MMBench. On MMvet, the gap between hardest and easiest variants is even more pronounced, e.g., GPT-4o drops from 73.94% to 43.66% (hardest) while rising to 77.15% (easiest).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against competing evaluation methods but instead compares each LVLM's performance under vanilla (static) benchmarks versus dynamically bootstrapped variants. GPT-4o is the strongest model overall, achieving 85.27% on vanilla MMBench and 73.94% on vanilla MMvet, but it still drops 5.72 points on MMBench and 30.28 points on MMvet under the hardest variant (V1+L4). InternVL-2 is the strongest open-source model, leading on MMBench (86.78% vanilla) but showing a larger relative drop (10.78 points) under the hardest variant compared to GPT-4o. Claude3-5V-Sonnet shows an anomalous pattern: it scores 70.77% on vanilla MMvet (second only to GPT-4o) but drops to just 26.10% under the hardest variant, a 44.67-point collapse, suggesting extreme sensitivity to visual distraction. Across all models, the easiest variant (V2+L3) consistently outperforms vanilla, confirming that removing irrelevant objects and adding relevant context helps LVLMs focus on key visual content. The paper also demonstrates that VLB reduces image-text data contamination rates substantially (e.g., from 84.46% to much lower values on the hardest variant), validating its utility as a contamination-resistant evaluation protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dynamic evaluation paradigm established for LLMs (DyVal, NPHardEval, MPA) to the multimodal setting, which the authors identify as entirely unexplored. While prior work on VQA robustness (Shah et al., Gokhale et al., Selvaraju et al.) attempted question rephrasing, none provided an automated, composable framework that simultaneously transforms both images and language while preserving answer correctness. The method repurposes existing tools—GPT-4V for planning, PowerPaint/BrushNet for image editing, SAM for segmentation, and Set-of-Mark prompting for visual grounding—into a unified bootstrapping pipeline with a judge module for consistency enforcement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a pressing and growing concern: static LVLM benchmarks are increasingly contaminated by web-scraped pre-training data, undermining the validity of reported scores. By enabling on-the-fly generation of dynamic variants with controllable complexity, VLB provides a practical path toward evaluation that co-evolves with model capabilities. Key limitations include reliance on GPT-4V and image-editing tools (which introduce their own biases and costs), the VQA-centric design that may not generalize to all multimodal tasks without adaptation, and the fact that the judge module (InternVL-2) adds inference overhead. Open questions include whether the strategies capture the full diversity of real user interactions and how the framework scales to video or 3D modalities.
