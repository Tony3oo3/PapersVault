## Problem

Fine-tuning continuous flow-based generative models (flow matching) to align with arbitrary user-defined reward functions via RL remains largely unexplored. Existing RLHF methods such as DPO and DDPO depend on likelihood calculations or filtered datasets that are computationally intractable or inapplicable to continuous normalizing flows, and no prior work provides a theoretical characterization of convergence behavior or addresses the policy collapse that arises in online reward-weighted fine-tuning of flow matching models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes Gaussian noise as the initial distribution for the flow ODE and, in text-image alignment tasks, a text prompt as conditioning, producing RGB images at the target resolution of the base model. It is demonstrated on both small-scale U-Net flow matching models (MNIST, CIFAR-10) and the large-scale Stable Diffusion 3 transformer (with LoRA adapters and fp16 precision). The reward signal is an arbitrary scalar function evaluated on each generated sample—CLIP logit, image compression rate, or a binary classifier score—requiring no gradient access, no filtered datasets, and no differentiability of the reward.

## Method

ORW-CFM-W2 modifies the standard Conditional Flow Matching loss in two coupled ways. First, an online reward-weighting mechanism multiplies the per-sample CFM loss by an exponential reward weight w(x₁)=exp(τ·r(x₁)) and samples training targets from the model's own current distribution p^θ(x₁), turning each training epoch into an online policy iteration that progressively concentrates mass on high-reward regions. Second, a Wasserstein-2 regularization term penalizes the squared L2 difference between the fine-tuned and frozen reference vector fields, whose tractable upper bound (Theorem 3) is an integral over time of the expected squared vector-field gap, computable via Monte Carlo sampling without any divergence or likelihood calculation. The paper proves that the unregularized online method inevitably collapses to a Dirac delta at the maximum-reward point as epochs grow (Lemma 1), while the W2 term induces a closed-form reward-diversity trade-off in the induced distribution q^N(x₁) ∝ exp(τNr(x₁) − βΣₙDⁿ(x₁))·q(x₁) (Theorem 5), where the hyperparameters τ and α provide explicit, controllable knobs between exploitation and diversity.

## Results

On the SD3 text-image alignment task (prompt: 'a cat in the sky'), ORW-CFM-W2 achieves the highest CLIP score of 35.46 while maintaining a diversity score of 4.21, compared to the un-fine-tuned SD3 baseline (CLIP 28.69, diversity 4.78). The method outperforms RAFT (CLIP 29.30, diversity 2.05) and ReFT (CLIP 29.32, diversity 3.26) by a wide margin on alignment quality, and also surpasses their W2-regularized variants (RAFT+W2: 30.88; ReFT+W2: 32.03). On MNIST target image generation, the method reaches near-optimal expected return of 1 (exclusively generating even digits), and on CIFAR-10 image compression, varying the W2 coefficient α provides a smooth, controllable reward-diversity trade-off curve.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ORW-CFM-W2 outperforms all baselines on the primary CLIP alignment metric: it achieves 35.46 vs. RAFT (29.30), ReFT (29.32), RAFT+W2 (30.88), and ReFT+W2 (32.03), representing a 6.1–14.4 point improvement over the strongest baseline. On diversity, ORW-CFM-W2 (4.21) is closest to the un-fine-tuned SD3 baseline (4.78), while RAFT without W2 collapses to 2.05 — a 57% diversity loss — empirically confirming the policy collapse predicted by Lemma 1. Adding W2 regularization to RAFT and ReFT recovers some diversity (2.81 and 3.63 respectively) but still trails ORW-CFM-W2 by 1.4 and 0.58 points. The paper also demonstrates that ORW-CFM without W2 (33.63 CLIP, 3.47 diversity) already beats RAFT and ReFT on alignment, validating the online reward-weighting mechanism independently of the W2 term. Across multiple reward models (HPS-V2, Pick Score, Alpha-CLIP) and compositional prompts, ORW-CFM-W2 consistently maintains the best reward-diversity balance, confirming the reward-agnostic nature of the approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reward-weighted regression (RWR) framework from Peters & Schaal (2007) and the online RL fine-tuning paradigm of DDPO (Black et al., 2024) from diffusion models to continuous flow matching (CFM) models. It departs from prior RLHF methods (DPO, DPOK, DDPO) that rely on tractable likelihoods or KL divergence—both intractable for continuous normalizing flows—by replacing KL with a Wasserstein-2 distance regularizer for which a computable upper bound is derived directly from vector field differences. The online reward-weighting mechanism builds on RAFT (Dong et al., 2023) and ReFT (Huguet et al., 2024), but the paper's key conceptual departure is proving that unregularized online reward-weighting in the flow matching setting provably collapses to a Dirac delta (Lemma 1), and showing that W2 regularization yields a controllable reward-diversity trade-off (Theorem 5).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretically-grounded online RL framework specifically for fine-tuning flow matching models, which are increasingly used in production systems (e.g., Stable Diffusion 3). The tractable W2 upper bound (Theorem 3) provides a practical, low-cost regularization that avoids the expensive divergence-integration required for exact likelihoods in CNFs, making RL fine-tuning of flow models feasible at scale. Open questions include whether the W2 bound is tight enough for very large architectural divergences, how reward model quality interacts with the regularization strength in practice, and whether the approach extends to multi-step or hierarchical flow architectures. The paper also leaves open the question of optimal hyperparameter scheduling for τ and α during training.
