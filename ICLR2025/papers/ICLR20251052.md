## Problem

Probabilistic time series forecasting struggles to simultaneously capture global temporal dynamics and local nonlinear variations while maintaining stable convergence. Existing Koopman-based models suffer from spectrum pollution in highly nonlinear systems, and Dynamic Mode Decomposition's reliance on discrete point-spectra limits its ability to represent continuous or chaotic dynamics. No prior framework unifies point-spectra, continuous-spectra, and pseudo-spectra within a single probabilistic forecasting architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251052/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a delay-embedded multivariate time series z_{1:T} ∈ R^{T×d×k} (where d is feature dimension and k is the delay-embedded length) and, at test time, requires only the initial step z_1 ∈ R^{1×d×k} to generate forecasts of arbitrary horizon length. Output is a full Gaussian predictive distribution (mean and variance) at each future time step, from which prediction intervals (50%, 90%) are derived. The input history length is decoupled from the training window T, so a single trained model can forecast any future length without retraining.

## Method

KooNPro synergistically integrates a Neural Process (NP) with a probabilistic deep Koopman model under a unified variational inference framework. The NP encodes DMD point-spectra from a context set into a latent variable S (via a network τ that computes eigenvalues of x†y), capturing global temporal dynamics; the Koopman component then refines local dynamics using an encoder φ, decoder φ^{-1}, and auxiliary network κ. The Koopman operator A evolves latent states via pure-imaginary eigenvalues (λ_t = jω_t), where ω_t is modeled as a Gaussian distribution parameterized by κ([h_t; S])—this variance-aware continuous-spectra, inspired by pseudo-spectra theory, replaces point eigenvalue estimates to capture uncertainty in the neighborhood of each spectral component. Training maximizes a tailored ELBO combining the NP's KL divergence between S_D and S_C with the Koopman prediction loss (decoder Gaussian NLL) and linear loss (Koopman transition Gaussian NLL), optimized via the reparameterization trick with Adam at lr=1e-5.

## Results

KooNPro achieves the best CRPS_sum on 8 out of 9 datasets and the best NRMSE_sum on 7 out of 9, with particularly strong margins on ETTm2 (CRPS 0.081 vs. next-best 0.129) and Solar (CRPS 0.211 vs. 0.298). On Electricity, TimeGrad retains a slight edge in both CRPS_sum (0.044 vs. 0.057) and NRMSE_sum (0.072 vs. 0.095), while MG-TSD narrowly beats KooNPro on ETTh2 NRMSE_sum (0.220 vs. 0.224). Across all nine datasets, KooNPro's average CRPS_sum is approximately 0.174 and average NRMSE_sum is approximately 0.247, substantially lower than the next-best competitor MG-TSD (avg. CRPS ≈ 0.254, avg. NRMSE ≈ 0.394).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KooNPro outperforms all seven baselines on CRPS_sum for 8 of 9 datasets, with the sole exception being Electricity where TimeGrad achieves a lower CRPS_sum (0.044 vs. 0.057). The largest margins are against DPK (which degrades severely on high-dimensional data, e.g., CRPS 0.827 on Traffic vs. KooNPro's 0.184) and TACTiS (CRPS 1.871 on Solar vs. 0.211). Against the strongest competitor MG-TSD, KooNPro wins on CRPS_sum by 0.03–0.11 across most datasets (e.g., 0.081 vs. 0.129 on ETTm2, 0.211 vs. 0.298 on Solar), though MG-TSD edges ahead on Electricity CRPS (0.107 vs. 0.057 is actually KooNPro winning; TimeGrad wins at 0.044) and ETTh2 NRMSE (0.220 vs. 0.224). On NRMSE_sum, KooNPro leads on 7 of 9 datasets, losing only to TimeGrad on Electricity (0.072 vs. 0.095) and to MG-TSD on ETTh2 (0.220 vs. 0.224). GP-Copula and Trans-MAF are consistently outperformed across all datasets, with Trans-MAF showing particularly poor performance on Solar (CRPS 0.964) and high-dimensional datasets like Taxi (CRPS 0.403). D³VAE is competitive on low-dimensional ETT datasets but degrades sharply on high-dimensional ones (e.g., NRMSE 0.926 on Traffic vs. KooNPro's 0.289).

## Relation to Prior Work

KooNPro unifies three previously separate threads: the probabilistic deep Koopman architecture of Lusch et al. (2018) and Morton et al. (2019), the variance-aware pseudo-spectra concept of Colbrook et al. (2024), and Neural Process (Garnelo et al., 2018) for global dynamics. Prior Koopman-based forecasters (Liu et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2023) model continuous-spectra but suffer from spectrum pollution, while existing probabilistic Koopman models do not incorporate a separate global dynamics prior. The key departure is the joint ELBO that couples NP's point-spectra (global) with a variance-aware Gaussian continuous-spectra (local), a combination not present in either lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The variance-aware continuous-spectra offers a principled remedy for spectrum pollution that plagues Koopman-based forecasting, and the NP component provides a learnable global prior that the ablation shows is critical (removing it degrades performance substantially, while replacing it with a GP makes things worse). Limitations include the pure-imaginary eigenvalue assumption, the independence assumption on latent states, and reliance on MLPs that may constrain expressiveness for highly non-stationary or very high-dimensional series. Open questions include whether the Gaussian parameterization of ω_t is sufficient for heavy-tailed or multimodal dynamics, and how the framework scales beyond the 1214-dimensional Taxi dataset.
