## Problem

Current AI search engines (e.g., SearchGPT, Perplexity) are limited to text-only queries and cannot process multimodal user inputs or the text-image interleaved nature of modern websites. Whether Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) can function as effective multimodal AI search engines remains an open question, with no existing pipeline or benchmark to evaluate this capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250065/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline accepts a text query (optionally accompanied by a query image) and produces three sequential outputs: a reformulated search query (requery), a single website index selected from 8 candidates (rerank), and a concise factual answer (summarization). Webpage content is consumed as both parsed text (retrieved via BGE-M3 embeddings, capped at 2K tokens) and visual screenshots — a 1024×1024 top-section crop for reranking and a full-page screenshot at 512px width, segmented into 512px-height tiles (max 10 segments) after Sobel-gradient-based blank-area removal. Image queries additionally receive a Google Lens screenshot as auxiliary input at every pipeline stage.

## Method

MMSEARCH-ENGINE is a three-stage zero-shot pipeline that wraps any LMM into a multimodal search agent: (i) Requery — the LMM translates the user's question (and image content identified via Google Lens) into a search-engine-friendly text query; (ii) Rerank — the LMM selects the single most informative website from 8 candidates using title, snippet, and a top-section screenshot; (iii) Summarization — the LMM extracts the answer from the selected website's slimmed full-page screenshot and embedding-retrieved text. The key design insight is preserving the interleaved text-image structure of webpages through screenshots rather than relying solely on parsed HTML, and using Google Lens to bridge the visual-to-textual gap for image queries. The companion benchmark MMSEARCH contains 300 manually curated queries across 14 subfields in two areas (time-sensitive News from May–Aug 2024 and rare Knowledge verified beyond SoTA LMMs' capabilities), with a step-wise evaluation protocol weighting end-to-end F1 at 75%, rerank and summarization at 10% each, and requery at 5%.

## Results

On MMSEARCH, GPT-4o with MMSEARCH-ENGINE achieves the best overall score of 62.3, with an end-to-end score of 60.4, surpassing the commercial Perplexity Pro (47.8 end-to-end) by 12.6 points. Among open-source models, Qwen2-VL-72B leads with 52.7 overall and 49.1 end-to-end, still trailing GPT-4o by 9.6 points. The human baseline scores 69.2 overall and 68.2 end-to-end, indicating a remaining gap of ~7 points even for the best model. The summarization task consistently outperforms end-to-end by 2.7–23.9 points across models, revealing that requery and rerank are the primary bottlenecks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o with MMSEARCH-ENGINE (60.4 end-to-end) decisively outperforms Perplexity Pro (47.8) by 12.6 points, and even Qwen2-VL-72B (49.1) edges out Perplexity Pro by 1.3 points, demonstrating that the pipeline design itself adds substantial value over the commercial product. Among closed-source LMMs, GPT-4o leads Claude 3.5 Sonnet (53.5) and GPT-4V (55.0) by 8.8 and 7.3 points respectively on overall score. The best open-source model, Qwen2-VL-72B (52.7), trails GPT-4o by 9.6 points overall and 11.3 points on end-to-end, while 7B open-source models fall 20+ points behind. On the rerank task, GPT-4o (83.0) and Claude 3.5 Sonnet (80.2) approach the human level (85.7), whereas most 7B open-source models score below 80, with InternLM-XC2.5 scoring 0.0 due to format failures. The summarization gap between end-to-end and standalone summarization is smallest for GPT-4o (2.7 pts) and largest for LLaVA-OneVision-7B (23.9 pts), confirming that weaker models suffer disproportionately from poor requery/rerank cascading into the final answer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of AI search engines (SearchGPT, Perplexity) and Large Multimodal Models, which have previously been evaluated only on static understanding benchmarks (MMBench, MME, MathVista) rather than as active search agents. It departs from text-only RAG and AI search frameworks (MindSearch, RQ-RAG, Self-RAG) by introducing a multimodal pipeline that integrates visual search (Google Lens) with text-based retrieval, and by formulating the search process as a three-step LMM task (requery, rerank, summarization). The step-wise evaluation strategy is novel for search tasks, borrowing the diagnostic philosophy of decomposed evaluation from other LMM benchmarks but applying it to a multi-round, interactive setting that had no prior evaluation protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first benchmark to systematically measure whether LMMs can function as multimodal search engines, revealing that even GPT-4o (62.3%) falls short of human performance (69.2%) and that the primary bottleneck is not summarization but requery and rerank—capabilities largely absent from current LMM training data. The finding that a simple pipeline with GPT-4o surpasses Perplexity Pro suggests commercial products may be underutilizing their underlying models, while the test-time computation scaling result (7B+TTC beating 72B) points toward a practical path for improving search without larger models. Open questions include whether interactive user feedback loops can close the remaining gap, how the benchmark evolves as model knowledge cutoffs advance, and whether the requery/rerank deficiencies can be addressed through targeted training data.
