## Problem

In multi-armed bandits with cost subsidy (MAB-CS), the goal is to minimize cumulative sampling cost while ensuring cumulative reward meets a feasibility threshold, rather than simply maximizing reward. Prior work (Sinha et al., 2021) addressed only the subsidized best reward variant and provided a single algorithm (ETC-CS) with a suboptimal T^{2/3} regret guarantee. This paper extends MAB-CS to two new settings (fixed threshold and known reference arm), establishes instance-dependent lower bounds for all three variants, and introduces the first algorithms with order-optimal O(log T) instance-dependent regret guarantees.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a bandit instance with K arms pre-sorted by ascending known cost, a subsidy factor α ∈ [0,1], a horizon T, and either a known reference arm index ℓ (for the known reference arm setting) or no reference (for the subsidized best reward setting). At each time step it outputs a single arm to pull, and the performance is measured via two zero-clipped regret quantities: cost regret (penalizing sampling arms more expensive than the optimal feasible arm) and quality regret (penalizing sampling arms whose reward falls below the feasibility threshold). The zero-clipping is critical—it prevents an algorithm from offsetting infeasible decisions with over-performing ones, which would be meaningless in the target applications.

## Method

The core algorithm, Pairwise Elimination (PE), evaluates candidate arms against a reference arm in ascending cost order, one arm per 'episode,' using Improved-UCB's round-based sampling cadence to determine when an arm can be confidently eliminated as infeasible or declared as the optimal feasible arm. A key sample-efficiency feature is that reference arm samples are reused across episodes, so the reference arm need only be sampled when the current candidate arm's sample count exceeds the reference arm's accumulated count. For the subsidized best reward setting where the best arm is unknown, PE-CS prepends a Best-Arm-Identification (BAI) stage that uses the same round-based elimination to identify the highest-reward arm before handing control to the PE stage, with round numbers carrying over so the PE stage resumes exactly where BAI left off. The instance-dependent analysis shows PE achieves O(log T) cost and quality regret matching the lower bound up to constants, establishing order-optimality for all known reference arm instances.

## Results

The paper establishes that both PE and PE-CS achieve O(log T) instance-dependent upper bounds on expected cumulative cost and quality regret, making them the first algorithms with such guarantees in the MAB-CS setting. PE is shown to be order-optimal for all known reference arm instances, matching the lower bound up to constant factors. Empirically, on MovieLens 25M (20 arms) and Goodreads (8 arms) at T=5M with 25–50 independent runs, PE consistently outperforms UCB-CS Known ℓ in summed cost+quality regret, particularly for smaller subsidy factors α and when the reference arm is in the cheaper half of arms. PE-CS outperforms ETC-CS (the only other algorithm with a regret guarantee) and substantially outperforms UCB-CS, which exhibits linear regret due to persistent mis-identification of the best action.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines from Sinha et al. (2021): ETC-CS, UCB-CS, and TS-CS. PE (for the known reference arm setting) outperforms UCB-CS Known ℓ on both MovieLens and Goodreads, especially for smaller α values and when the reference arm is among cheaper arms; UCB-CS is shown to be unreliable and susceptible to linear regret. PE-CS (for the subsidized best reward setting) outperforms ETC-CS, the only other algorithm with a theoretical regret guarantee, achieving lower terminal summed regret at T=5M. PE-CS also substantially outperforms UCB-CS, which shows a linear regret trend from persistent mis-identification of the optimal arm. Against TS-CS, PE-CS has slightly higher average regret but offers superior reliability: while TS-CS initially shows lower regret due to interleaved exploration, it exhibits a distinct slow-but-steady upward regret trend, whereas PE-CS locks onto the optimal arm consistently and then incurs no further incremental regret. The authors conclude that PE-CS offers the best balance between performance and reliability among all tested methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the MAB-CS framework of Sinha et al. (2021), which introduced the subsidized best reward setting with algorithms ETC-CS, UCB-CS, and TS-CS, and on the Improved-UCB successive elimination algorithm of Auer & Ortner (2010). The key departure is methodological: rather than interleaving exploration and exploitation to maintain an empirical feasible set (as UCB-CS and TS-CS do), the authors adopt a pairwise elimination strategy that evaluates candidate arms in ascending cost order against a reference arm, inheriting the round-based cadence from Improved-UCB. The paper also extends the MAB-CS task itself to two new settings (fixed threshold and known reference arm) that were absent from the original formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main impact is providing the first instance-dependent O(log T) regret bounds for MAB-CS variants, with PE proven order-optimal for the known reference arm setting. A notable limitation is that PE-CS is not order-optimal for all instances in the subsidized best reward setting—the BAI stage introduces an irreducible overhead that creates a gap between upper and lower bounds, and the authors characterize but do not close this gap. The zero-clipped regret structure, while seemingly technical, is shown to be essential for the problem's practical relevance: without it, sub-linear regret could be achieved by algorithms that are useless in real applications. Open questions include whether the PE-CS gap can be closed with a more efficient BAI-PE interface, and whether the pairwise elimination paradigm extends to non-stationary or contextual variants of cost-subsidized bandits.
