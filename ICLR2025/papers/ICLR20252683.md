## Problem

Nonlinear network models such as the latent space model (LSM) and β-model contain nonlinear factors that make parameter estimation a long-standing open problem, and the degree-corrected block model (DCBM), while low-rank and amenable to spectral methods, imposes restrictive probability constraints. The paper addresses the gap by proposing the logit-DCBM (which unifies all three models without constraints) and resolving the core difficulty: how to estimate the nonlinear factors that destroy the low-rank structure needed by spectral community detection algorithms like SCORE.

## Input / Output

The method takes a binary adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{n×n} (no self-loops) and the number of communities K as input. Beyond the final community label vector, the algorithm produces intermediate estimates of per-node degree parameters θ_i, the K×K community probability matrix P, and the n×n matrix of nonlinear factors N̂. The algorithm is recursive with a fixed number of iterations (M=10 in practice, though it typically converges in 1–2), and each iteration's refitting step has computational cost O(n²d) where d is the maximum node degree.

## Method

The key insight is a cancellation trick: by constructing two carefully designed cycle-count statistics (one alternating edges and non-edges around an odd-length cycle), the nonlinear factors N_ij cancel in their ratio, yielding a closed-form expression for θ_i² or for the community probability P_kℓ. This solves the open β-model estimation problem and, more generally, enables estimation of N when community labels are known. R-SCORE then iterates: (1) given current community estimates Π̂, use the cancellation trick to estimate θ̂ and P̂, then form N̂_ij = (1 + θ̂_i θ̂_j P̂_kℓ)^{-1}; (2) apply SCORE (eigendecomposition + k-means on entry-wise ratios of the first K eigenvectors) to the renormalized matrix A⊘N̂, which approximately recovers the low-rank structure of the DCBM. Theoretically, the Hamming error rate of R-SCORE is C(1/(nθ̄²) + θ̄⁶ + log n/n) versus C(1/(nθ̄²) + θ̄⁴) for SCORE, giving a strictly faster rate in the sparse regime β < 1/6.

## Results

Theoretically, R-SCORE achieves a Hamming error rate of $Cn^{-a_1(\beta)}$ versus $Cn^{-a_0(\beta)}$ for SCORE, where $a_1(\beta)=\min\{1-2\beta,\,6\beta\}$ and $a_0(\beta)=\min\{1-2\beta,\,4\beta\}$; R-SCORE is strictly faster when $\beta<1/6$ (the sparse regime). In simulations across four settings (n=2400–2500, K=3–5, SNR 14–31.5), R-SCORE reduces the Hamming error rate substantially compared to SCORE (m=0), typically converging in 1–2 iterations. Against npMLE on a larger network (n=5400, K=6, SNR=28), R-SCORE achieves a slightly lower error rate while running ~50× faster (6 s vs. >300 s per repetition).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SCORE (Jin 2015), R-SCORE wins decisively: in all four simulation settings (A–D) the Hamming error drops sharply after just one refitting iteration, and the theoretical rate is strictly faster in the sparse regime $\beta<1/6$ (e.g., at $\beta=1/10$, R-SCORE gives $n^{-0.6}$ vs. SCORE's $n^{-0.4}$). Against npMLE (Ma et al. 2020), R-SCORE converges in far fewer iterations (visible in Figure 3 left), achieves a slightly lower steady-state error rate, and is approximately 50× faster per run (6 s vs. >300 s). In Experiment 3, where the community dissimilarity parameter $\beta_2$ is swept from 0.58 to 0.7, R-SCORE outperforms npMLE across the entire range, confirming robustness to varying signal strength. The paper does not report a setting where R-SCORE loses to either baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SCORE spectral algorithm (Jin, 2015) from the degree-corrected block model (DCBM) to a new nonlinear model, the logit-DCBM, which unifies the DCBM, latent space model (Hoff, 2005), and β-model (Chatterjee et al., 2011) under a logit-link. The central technical departure is that SCORE's success for DCBM relies on the model being low-rank, but the logit-DCBM is not low-rank due to nonlinear factors N; the paper's cancellation trick—taking ratios of carefully constructed cycle-count statistics—cancels these nonlinear terms and restores an approximately low-rank structure. This trick also resolves a long-standing open problem for the β-model (equivalently, the p₁ model) regarding consistent parameter estimation, which prior MLE-based approaches could not handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cancellation trick is a general-purpose tool for any nonlinear latent variable model where E[A] = N ∘ Ω̃ with low-rank Ω̃, and the authors note it extends to directed networks, tensors, and text analysis. However, the theoretical improvement of R-SCORE over SCORE is confined to a narrow sparse regime (β < 1/6), and the proof requires K fixed, balanced communities, and bounded degree heterogeneity—conditions that may not hold in practice. The computational advantage over MLE-based approaches (6 s vs. 300+ s) is substantial, but the practical gains over SCORE are most visible in moderately sparse networks; for dense or highly heterogeneous networks the benefit may be smaller. Open questions include whether the conservative error bound for R-SCORE can be tightened, whether the trick extends to directed or multi-layer networks, and whether the approach scales to growing K.
