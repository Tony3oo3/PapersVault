## Problem

Existing RL-based acquisition functions for single-objective Bayesian optimization cannot be directly extended to the multi-objective setting because hypervolume improvement is inherently history-dependent (non-Markovian), creating a fundamental identifiability issue where identical AF inputs can yield different hypervolume gains depending on past queries. All current MOBO acquisition functions are handcrafted and myopic, greedily optimizing one-step surrogate metrics without long-term planning capability.

## Input / Output

At each step, BOFormer consumes a Q-augmented per-point observation (GP posterior mean, variance, best observed value, and time ratio t/T for each of K objectives) concatenated over the queried history, with each past observation-action pair additionally augmented by the Q-value from a frozen target network. This representation is domain-agnostic and memory-efficient: its dimension depends only on the number of past queries, not on the total domain size. The output is a Q-value assigned to each candidate point in the domain, from which the next sample is selected via argmax (approximated with Sobol grids for continuous domains).

## Method

BOFormer instantiates a Generalized DQN framework for non-Markovian environments, where the Q-function is defined over the full history of observations and actions rather than a fixed-dimension state, and is trained by minimizing a generalized temporal-difference Bellman residual. The architecture uses a GPT-2-style Transformer with two networks (policy and target) that process the historical sequence of Q-augmented observation-action pairs, with the target network's Q-values recursively embedded into the history to break the non-Markovian dependency. Training employs off-policy learning with a Prioritized Trajectory Replay Buffer (prioritizing entire trajectories by summed TD-error), a demo policy (EHVI) used with probability r_demo to guide exploration, and a normalized hypervolume-improvement reward that prevents signal decay in later steps. The window size w controls how many past steps are included, and ablations confirm that w>1 (non-Markovian) is necessary to resolve the identifiability issue.

## Results

On 7 synthetic multi-objective functions (100-step budget), BOFormer achieves the highest hypervolume on 3 of 7 tasks (AR: 0.5900, ARa: 0.7377, RBF: 0.8751) and remains within 1% of the best on all others. On the introduced HPO-3DGS dataset (30-step budget, 5 real-world 3D reconstruction scenes), BOFormer attains the top hypervolume on 4 of 5 scenes (e.g., Lego: 0.9508, Ship: 0.9745, Mic: 0.8816), outperforming the strongest rule-based baseline qNEHVI by up to 5.4% (Mic: 0.8816 vs 0.8353). Per-step inference time is ~14.2 ms, nearly constant across 2–4 objectives, versus 224 ms for qNEHVI at 4 objectives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BOFormer clearly outperforms all learning-based baselines on the majority of tasks: it beats FSAF by 14.8–25.8% hypervolume on AR/ARa, OptFormer by 14.5–26.4% on AR/ARa, and QT by 15.5–26.4% on AR/ARa, confirming that the Q-augmented representation and non-Markovian formulation are critical (QT, which lacks Q-augmentation, underperforms BOFormer by ~20% on most tasks). Against rule-based methods, BOFormer wins on AR (+2.0% over qHVKG), ARa (+5.3% over NSGA-II), and RBF (+0.2% over qNEHVI), but trails qNEHVI on BC (−6.2%), Matern (−0.6%), and BCD (−2.2%), which the authors attribute to the larger length scales of those functions. On HPO-3DGS, BOFormer dominates qNEHVI on 4/5 scenes (e.g., Mic: 0.8816 vs 0.8353, +5.5%) and edges out FSAF on Lego (0.9508 vs 0.9504) and Ship (0.9745 vs 0.9737), though FSAF slightly leads on Mic (0.8871 vs 0.8816). OptFormer, designed for single-objective HPO, degrades substantially on multi-objective tasks (e.g., 0.7875 on Lego vs 0.9508 for BOFormer), highlighting the difficulty of adapting text-to-text HPO models to the Pareto-front recovery setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of RL-based acquisition functions for single-objective BO (FSAF, MetaBO, RTDK-BO) to the multi-objective setting, but identifies a fundamental obstacle: the hypervolume identifiability issue arising from the non-Markovian nature of MOBO, which prevents direct transfer of Markovian DQN formulations. To resolve this, the authors draw on the general non-Markovian RL framework of Dong et al. (2022) to define a Generalized DQN whose Q-function operates over full observation-action histories, and instantiate it with a Transformer architecture inspired by Decision Transformer and Q-Transformer. A key practical departure from both the direct history-based implementation and from QT is the Q-augmented observation representation, which makes the model domain-agnostic and memory-efficient, enabling zero-shot cross-domain transfer that neither OptFormer (task-specific HPO) nor FSAF (requires metadata) can offer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BOFormer is the first learning-based (RL) acquisition function for MOBO, and its identification of the hypervolume identifiability issue provides a clear theoretical explanation for why prior SOBO RL-based AFs cannot be naively extended to multiple objectives. The zero-shot and cross-domain transfer capability (different domain sizes and dimensionalities between training and deployment) is practically significant for real-world HPO pipelines where retraining per task is infeasible. Limitations include reliance on GP surrogates, the use of Sobol grids for continuous-domain maximization (which may struggle in very high dimensions), and the need for the optimal hypervolume HV(X*) during training for reward normalization. Open questions include whether the approach scales to many-objective settings (K > 4), whether it can be combined with learned neural process surrogates beyond GPs, and whether the Q-augmented representation generalizes to other non-Markovian sequential decision problems.
