## Problem

Coordinate-based MLPs suffer from spectral bias, learning high-frequency details far more slowly than low-frequency ones. While Fourier feature encodings (FFEs) have been analyzed through the neural tangent kernel (NTK) to explain their spectral improvements, no analogous NTK analysis existed for multigrid parametric encodings (MPEs), which empirically outperform FFEs in graphics applications. The paper asks: what mechanism in the MPE raises the NTK eigenvalue spectrum, and how does it differ fundamentally from the FFE's mechanism?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251951/cropped_page8_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

For 2D image regression, the network takes normalized pixel coordinates in [0,1]² and predicts RGB values; for 3D implicit surface regression, it takes 3D point coordinates and predicts a scalar occupancy value. The NTK is computed as an N×N Gram matrix over all pairwise training samples using finite-width automatic differentiation, and its eigenvalue spectrum (sorted descending, plotted on a log scale) is the primary analytical output. The encoding maps the low-dimensional input into a higher-dimensional embedding space (2dL dimensions for FFE, or a concatenation of bilinearly interpolated grid values plus the original coordinates for MPE) before the MLP processes it.

## Method

The paper derives the closed-form NTK for a single-layer MLP composed with an MPE, showing it decomposes into three additive components: the baseline MLP kernel, a kernel from the MPE's embedding space, and a new kernel induced by the learnable grid parameters (K_MPE). Using Weyl's inequality on the sum of positive semi-definite Gram matrices, the authors prove that each eigenvalue of the MPE-composed kernel is bounded below by the corresponding baseline eigenvalue plus the smallest eigenvalue of the grid contribution matrix (Theorem 1). Critically, they isolate the grid's contribution by computing the spectrum with and without K_MPE, showing that without the learnable grid the MPE's spectrum is barely above baseline—proving the performance gain comes from the grid parameters, not the higher-dimensional embedding. This contrasts with FFEs, whose NTK (Equation 14) improves solely through the embedding space with no additional parameter-driven term. Empirical validation on 100 ImageNet synonym sets and three Stanford 3D meshes confirms the theory: the finest MPE grid raises the minimum eigenvalue by 8 orders of magnitude over baseline and 2 orders over the highest-frequency FFE, corresponding to 15 dB PSNR / 0.65 MS-SSIM and 12 dB / 0.33 improvements respectively.

## Results

On ImageNet 2D image regression (100 synonym sets), the MPE achieves 45.28 dB PSNR and 0.79 MS-SSIM, compared to 33.47 dB / 0.39 for the FFE and 29.94 dB / 0.32 for the baseline MLP. In the controlled scaling experiment, the fine-grid MPE reaches 41.5 dB PSNR and 0.73 MS-SSIM versus 36.5 dB / 0.40 for the highest-frequency FFE and 20 dB / 0.08 for the baseline. The MPE increases the minimum NTK eigenvalue by 8 orders of magnitude over the baseline and 2 orders of magnitude over the FFE, with the improvement attributed to the learnable grid rather than the embedding space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the multigrid parametric encoding (MPE) against the axis-aligned logarithmic Fourier feature encoding (FFE, Tancik et al., 2020) and a baseline coordinate-based MLP with no encoding. The MPE consistently outperforms the FFE on both PSNR and MS-SSIM across all experimental settings: on ImageNet, the MPE achieves 45.28 dB PSNR versus 33.47 dB for the FFE (a 12 dB gap) and 0.79 versus 0.39 MS-SSIM (a 0.40 gap). In the scaling experiment, the fine-grid MPE reaches 41.5 dB PSNR compared to 36.5 dB for the highest-frequency FFE (L=16), while the coarse MPE (34.5 dB) is slightly below the mid-frequency FFE (35 dB) but still well above the low-frequency FFE (25 dB). Both encodings substantially outperform the baseline, with the MPE providing a 15 dB PSNR / 0.65 MS-SSIM improvement and the FFE a 3.5 dB / 0.07 improvement over the unencoded network. A key finding is that the MPE's advantage is isolated to its learnable grid parameters: when the grid contribution is removed from the NTK, the MPE's eigenvalue spectrum drops to near-baseline levels, whereas the FFE's performance depends entirely on its fixed sinusoidal embedding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NTK-based spectral bias analysis, previously applied only to Fourier feature encodings (Tancik et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2020), to multigrid parametric encodings (MPEs) originating from Hadadan et al. (2021) and Müller et al. (2022). Building on the infinite-width kernel regression framework of Jacot et al. (2018) and the spectral decomposition tools of Yang & Salman (2019), the authors derive a closed-form NTK for MPEs and prove a Weyl-inequality lower bound on eigenvalues. The key conceptual departure from prior FFE analysis is showing that MPEs improve the kernel through two distinct mechanisms—embedding space and learnable grid parameters—whereas FFEs rely solely on the embedding, a distinction no prior NTK study had isolated.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical explanation for why MPEs empirically outperform FFEs in graphics applications, and the ablation cleanly showing that the learnable grid (not the embedding) is responsible for the 8-orders-of-magnitude eigenvalue lift gives practitioners a principled basis for choosing and tuning encodings. Limitations include the analysis being restricted to ReLU activations and single-layer derivations (though multi-layer extension is argued), and the NTK computation being intractable for arbitrary problem sizes. Open questions include whether the grid-vs-embedding decomposition holds for other interpolation schemes (e.g., spherical harmonics), whether the results transfer to non-MLP backbones, and how to exploit the eigenvalue lower bound to design encodings with targeted spectral profiles for specific applications.
