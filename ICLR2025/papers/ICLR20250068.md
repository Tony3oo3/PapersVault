## Problem

Finding rare, desirable designs (e.g., functional proteins or DNA sequences) in combinatorially vast discrete spaces requires a limited budget of expensive black-box evaluations, yet existing methods either target a single optimum (BBO) or use fixed-sample variational approaches that collapse to point masses in batch settings. The paper formalizes 'active generation'—sequentially learning a generative model of a rare feasible class—and provides the first variational inference solution with asymptotic convergence guarantees for this task.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a discrete sequence design space (e.g., 20 amino acids over M positions, or 4 nucleotides over M positions), a prior distribution p(x|D₀) over designs, a black-box fitness function f_•, an initial dataset D_N of evaluated designs, and a threshold τ (or adaptive threshold schedule τ_t). Each round outputs a batch of B unique candidate sequences sampled from the learned variational distribution q(x|φ_t*), which are then evaluated and appended to D_N. The final output is a trained generative model of the super level-set distribution p(x|y>τ), not a single design, enabling diverse and task-specific generation of fit sequences.

## Method

VSD casts estimation of the super level-set distribution p(x|y>τ) as minimizing the reverse KL divergence between a parameterized variational distribution q(x|φ) and the target, yielding an ELBO objective: E_q[log π_θ(x)] − D_KL[q(x|φ)‖p(x|D₀)], where π_θ is a class probability estimator (CPE) trained via log-loss on binary labels z=1[y>τ]. Because the design space is discrete, the reparameterization trick is unavailable, so gradients are estimated via REINFORCE (score function) with control variates. The KL term to the prior is critical: it prevents the variational distribution from collapsing to a point mass, which is essential for generating diverse batches of O(1000) candidates per round. The algorithm is sequential—each round fits the CPE, optimizes φ via Adam, samples a batch, evaluates it, and updates the dataset—requiring only O(1000) Monte Carlo samples per round even for M=O(100) sequences. Theoretical analysis proves convergence of the variational distribution to the true level-set distribution at rate O_P(t^{-1/2}) under GP-PI and Õ_P(t^{-1/(2(M+1))}) under infinitely wide ReLU CPEs via the neural tangent kernel.

## Results

On the MNIST conditional generation task, VSD refines prior distributions (LSTM and transformer) to generate digits 3 and 5, raising the sample mean CPE score from ~0.13–0.14 (priors) to 0.189 (posteriors), with the CPE achieving ~99% balanced accuracy. On the fitness landscape tasks (DHFR, TrpB, TFBIND8), VSD achieves the highest precision, recall, and cumulative performance among all methods across all three datasets. On the higher-dimensional BBO tasks (AAV, GFP, Ehrlich functions at M=15/32/64), VSD achieves the lowest simple regret among all compared methods, outperforming CbAS on the Ehrlich benchmarks and matching or exceeding LaMBO-2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VSD outperforms all six baselines on the fitness landscape experiments (DHFR, TrpB, TFBIND8) by precision, recall, and cumulative performance. Against CbAS and DbAS, VSD's advantage comes from its ability to sample from the variational distribution while simultaneously learning it, whereas CbAS/DbAS use fixed samples from the previous round. BORE (adapted with REINFORCE) performs worst of all methods, with its variational distribution collapsing to a point mass, demonstrating the critical role of the KL regularization term in VSD's ELBO. AdaLead and PEX (evolutionary-search methods) are competitive on the lowest-dimensional task (TFBIND8, M=8) but degrade sharply as sequence length increases, while VSD's learned generative models scale better. On the Ehrlich function BBO benchmarks, VSD outperforms CbAS across all sequence lengths (M=15, 32, 64) and is competitive with LaMBO-2 (guided discrete diffusion), which uses its own surrogate and generative models. On AAV and GFP, VSD achieves the lowest simple regret, with the gap over CbAS widening at higher dimensions where informative priors (LSTM/transformer) become crucial.

## Relation to Prior Work

VSD unifies black-box variational inference (Ranganath et al., 2014), the variational bounding principle from optimization (Staines & Barber, 2013), and density-ratio estimation from BORE (Tiao et al., 2021) into a single ELBO-maximization framework for learning a generative model of a rare design class. It departs from CbAS/DbAS, which use fixed importance-weighted samples from a previous round's distribution, by employing REINFORCE-style score function gradients with fresh samples each round, and from BORE by retaining the KL divergence term that prevents batch collapse. The paper also positions itself against latent-space optimization (LaMBO-2, LSO) by operating directly in the discrete sequence space without an encoder, and against evolutionary heuristics (AdaLead, PEX) by learning a parametric generative model rather than relying on random mutation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first asymptotic convergence guarantees for a variational generative model of a super level-set in discrete combinatorial spaces, covering both GP-based and NTK-based (infinite-width ReLU) class probability estimators. Empirically, VSD outperforms existing baselines on near-complete biological fitness landscapes (DHFR, TrpB, TFBIND8) and is competitive with LaMBO-2 on high-dimensional BBO benchmarks, suggesting the framework is practical for real sequence-design pipelines. Open questions include extending to multi-objective Pareto-set generation, graph-structured designs, and mixed discrete-continuous spaces, as well as addressing the known limitation that CNN oracles used for intractable datasets (AAV, GFP) do not generalize well out-of-distribution.
