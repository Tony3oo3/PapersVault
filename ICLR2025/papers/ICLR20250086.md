## Problem

Despite being trained on identical data with the same contrastive objective, CLIP vision backbones (ResNets and ViTs) exhibit substantially different representations, classification accuracies across datasets, and robustness to image perturbations. Prior work focused on which single backbone generalizes best, but the complementarity across backbones—where different architectures excel on different subsets of images—remained unexploited. Traditional ensembling techniques (logit averaging, voting) fail to leverage this diversity, improving accuracy by only ~1% over the best single backbone.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images and produces a single class label per image. At inference, each of the 9 CLIP backbones (RN50, RN101, RN50x4, RN50x16, RN50x64, ViT-B-32, ViT-B-16, ViT-L-14, ViT-L-14-336) independently computes zero-shot classification logits via dot-product similarity between the image embedding and text prompt embeddings. The NLC then takes the concatenated visual feature vectors from all backbones as input and outputs a vector of B temperature scalars that reweight each backbone's logits before a final weighted-sum combination. Training requires as few as one labeled example per class from the target dataset's training split.

## Method

The Neural Logit Controller (NLC) is a one-layer MLP (hidden width 128) that maps concatenated visual features from B CLIP backbones to B input-adaptive temperature values t ∈ ℝ^B. Each backbone's logits z_i^b are divided by its corresponding temperature, and the resulting scaled logits are combined via a weighted sum to produce the final prediction. The MLP is trained end-to-end with cross-entropy loss on a small holdout set, learning to calibrate each backbone's confidence conditionally on the input image. Unlike SuperLearner, which learns fixed temperature scalars for the entire validation set, NLC's temperatures vary per test example, enabling it to exploit the observed diversity in which backbone is correct for which image. The approach achieves +9.1% average accuracy over the best single backbone (up to +39.1% on EUROSAT) and complements existing adapters like Tip-Adapter, boosting its performance by over 15%.

## Results

On 21 zero-shot image classification datasets, NLC achieves a mean improvement of +9.3% over the best single CLIP backbone, with a maximum gain of +39.1% on EUROSAT and a minimum of +0.1% (never degrading performance). In the few-shot setting on ImageNet-1K, NLC reaches 78.2–78.4% accuracy across 1–16 shots, substantially outperforming the best adapter baseline (Tip-Adapter-F at 65.5% at 16-shot). Combining only the top-4 most efficient backbones, NLC surpasses the best single backbone while using approximately 300 fewer GFLOPs per image.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NLC decisively outperforms all non-parametric ensembling baselines: LOG-AVG yields only +0.5% mean improvement (and degrades on 4 datasets), while VOTE T-1 actually underperforms the best backbone by −0.3% on average. Among parametric methods, SuperLearner (SL) achieves +3.3% mean improvement but cannot adapt its temperature scaling to individual inputs, capping its max gain at +8.8% versus NLC's +39.1%. In the few-shot ImageNet-1K setting, NLC (78.2–78.4%) surpasses Tip-Adapter-F (65.5% at 16-shot) by over 12 points, CLIP-Adapter (63.6%) by ~15 points, CoOP (63.0%) by ~15 points, and Linear-probe CLIP (56.1%) by over 22 points. When combined with Tip-Adapter, NLC provides an additional 10–15% boost over the best Tip-Adapter backbone (L-14-336), confirming that backbone ensembling is complementary to adapter-based fine-tuning. The Mixture-of-Experts baseline (Zoph et al.) degrades on several datasets (down to −20.9%), whereas NLC never reduces performance below the best single backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP framework (Radford et al., 2021) by treating its diverse vision backbones not as interchangeable alternatives but as complementary experts whose strengths can be adaptively combined. Methodologically, it builds on temperature scaling (Guo et al., 2017) and the SuperLearner ensemble (Cheng Ju and van der Laan, 2018), but departs from both by making the temperature coefficients input-dependent via a small MLP (the Neural Logit Controller), rather than using a single global scalar or fixed per-model weights. Conceptually, it contrasts with prior backbone-comparison studies (Goldblum et al., 2023) that concluded larger models generally dominate, by showing that within the same architecture family, different models exhibit distinct and non-nested patterns of correct predictions across datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that CLIP backbones trained on identical data and objectives exhibit up to 43.5% oracle complementarity challenges the implicit assumption that a single 'best' backbone suffices, and provides a principled justification for maintaining diverse model portfolios in production CLIP pipelines. The practical recipe—train a 1-layer MLP on one labeled example per class to learn input-adaptive temperature weights—is lightweight enough to be adopted alongside existing few-shot adapters like Tip-Adapter, where it yields over 15% additional gains. Key limitations include the requirement for multiple forward passes per image (mitigated but not eliminated by the Cascade integration), the restriction to late-fusion logit combination rather than representation-level fusion, and evaluation limited to image classification; extending the approach to retrieval, segmentation, or other CLIP downstream tasks remains open.
