## Problem

Existing indoor human trajectory datasets are limited in scene diversity (GIMO: 19 scenes, THOR-MAGNI: 4 scenes), lack multi-person social motion behaviors such as proxemics-based yielding and personal-space maintenance, or are collected in outdoor public spaces that do not transfer to confined home geometries. No large-scale dataset captures two-person socially-aware locomotion across diverse indoor home environments, hindering the development of AI agents that navigate domestic spaces while respecting implicit social norms.

## Input / Output

All spatial data is encoded as multi-channel 256×256 pixel images (representing a 10m×10m physical area), where trajectory positions are plotted as Gaussian-distributed 2D coordinates across time channels. The scene map is a binary walkability mask (1 for the −0.3m to 0.3m height band above floor, 0 elsewhere), and heading directions are similarly encoded as per-timestep images. For global path prediction the goal position is an additional single-channel image; for trajectory prediction the output is a 45-timestep probabilistic distribution per person; for goal prediction the output is a single-channel probability map over the scene.

## Method

The core contribution is a VR-based data collection system using HTC VIVE outside-in tracking (90 Hz, sub-centimeter accuracy) in a 10m×10m physical lab, where two participants wearing headsets and six body trackers each explore virtual rooms rendered from 131 HM3D scenes. Participants see synchronized SMPL-mesh avatars of each other and perform sequential goal-reaching tasks (~5 min per scene), eliciting natural social proxemics behaviors like yielding in narrow passages and maintaining personal distance. For evaluation, U-Net and YNet architectures are trained with BCE loss under 8× spatial augmentation (flipping + 90° rotations) and temporal sliding-window augmentation, then tested on LocoReal (a real-world motion-capture dataset in 4 physical room layouts). The ablation study isolates the contributions of dataset scale, multi-person input, and heading direction, showing that scene diversity is the dominant factor for generalization to unseen indoor geometries.

## Results

On global path prediction (evaluated on LocoReal), A*+U-Net trained on LocoVR achieves a mean Chamfer distance of 0.06 m (0–3 m range) and 0.19 m (6 m+ range), substantially outperforming the same model trained on GIMO (0.09 m / 0.36 m) and THOR-MAGNI (0.07 m / 0.30 m). On trajectory prediction, U-Net(LocoVR) reaches 0.11 m ADE at 0–1 s and 0.44 m at 2–3 s, versus 0.19 m / 0.55 m for GIMO and 0.59 m / 1.14 m for THOR-MAGNI. On goal prediction, U-Net(LocoVR) attains 0.83 m goal position error and 72.2% object prediction accuracy in the 0–3 m range, compared to 1.58 m / 49.2% (GIMO) and 1.82 m / 40.1% (THOR-MAGNI).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LocoVR-trained models consistently outperform both GIMO- and THOR-MAGNI-trained models across all three tasks and all distance/time ranges. On global path prediction, A*+U-Net(LocoVR) reduces mean Chamfer distance by ~33% over GIMO and ~37% over THOR-MAGNI in the 6 m+ range (0.19 m vs 0.36 m and 0.30 m), with the gap widening for longer distances. On trajectory prediction, U-Net(LocoVR) achieves 0.44 m ADE at 2–3 s, nearly halving the error of THOR-MAGNI (1.14 m) and improving 20% over GIMO (0.55 m). On goal prediction, U-Net(LocoVR) reaches 72.2% object accuracy (0–3 m) versus 49.2% for GIMO and 40.1% for THOR-MAGNI. Among model architectures, A*+U-Net(LocoVR) outperforms Ynet(LocoVR) on global path prediction (0.19 m vs 0.42 m mean at 6 m+), while Ynet is not applicable to the two-person trajectory prediction task. The non-learning baselines (A*+MAP, A*+DISTMAP) are outperformed by all learning-based methods, with A*+DISTMAP being the strongest non-learning baseline at 0.26 m mean (6 m+) versus 0.19 m for A*+U-Net(LocoVR).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of VR-based motion capture and indoor social navigation, rather than extending a single prior method. It departs from existing indoor trajectory datasets (GIMO with 19 scenes, THOR-MAGNI with 4) by using VR to rapidly collect two-person locomotion data across 131 diverse home environments, and from outdoor social navigation datasets (SIT, SACSON, JRDB) by focusing on confined indoor geometry where proxemics and obstacle avoidance dominate. The validation models (U-Net, YNet, A* pathfinding) are standard baselines repurposed to demonstrate dataset utility rather than methodological novelty. Conceptually, the work draws on proxemics and Interpersonal Adaptation Theory to frame social motion behaviors as a first-class signal for trajectory modeling, a perspective largely absent from prior indoor trajectory datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LocoVR fills a clear gap: no prior dataset combines large-scale scene diversity (131 scenes), multi-person social motion, and precise 3D geometry for indoor home environments, making it a practical resource for training socially-aware home robots. The ablation showing that scene diversity (not just trajectory count) drives generalization to unseen scenes is a useful design principle for future dataset construction. Key open questions include the VR-to-real transfer gap (mitigated but not eliminated by the LocoReal test set), the limited demographic diversity (ages 18–42, able-bodied adults), and whether two-person interactions adequately capture the social dynamics of larger household groups. The paper also raises the interesting but underexplored finding that semantic and height maps can improve path prediction over binary obstacle maps, suggesting object attributes influence human trajectory planning in ways not yet formally modeled.
