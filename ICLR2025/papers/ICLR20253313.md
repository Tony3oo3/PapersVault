## Problem

Existing rule-based TKG extrapolation methods treat fact truth values as binary (present/absent), ignoring the temporal validity and recurrence frequency of historical facts, and apply rules through CPU-based path matching that cannot infer missing facts. This makes rule application brittle to dataset incompleteness and computationally expensive, especially for longer rules.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253313/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a future-timestamp query (s, r_q, ?, t') with t' > t_n, the model consumes historical TKG facts, a pre-trained ComplEx static KG embedding model (trained on the non-temporal triples), and a library of mined cyclic temporal rules with confidence scores. It outputs a ranked score vector over all |E| candidate entities, where scores are aggregated across multiple applicable rules via Noisy-OR. The temporal weight matrices are |E|×|E| per relation, sparsified by a threshold of 0.0005, and updated incrementally at each new timestamp.

## Method

INFER introduces a Temporal Validity Function V(s,r,o,t_c) = 1/√(t_c − t_last) + λ·√F·M_r(s,o) that maps the time span since a fact's last occurrence and its historical frequency into a continuous [0,1] truth value, replacing the binary 0/1 encoding used by prior symbolic methods. These values update per-relation Temporal Weight Matrices initialized from softmax-normalized ComplEx scores, which assign approximate probabilities even to unseen facts, enabling inference over missing edges. Rule application is reformulated as iterative GPU matrix operations: for a rule of length n, the first hop selects row s of M_{R1}, and each subsequent hop transposes the score vector, tiles it to |E|×|E|, takes the Hadamard product with M_{Ri}, and applies column-wise max—effectively performing multi-hop traversal on weighted adjacency matrices rather than explicit graph search. Variable constraints in rules are handled by additional indexing operations that enforce entity identity at specified positions. Multiple rule outputs are combined via Noisy-OR weighted by rule confidence, and a 'generated rule' of length 1 (the query itself) is always included to capture pure temporal-embedding signal.

## Results

INFER-60(with vc) achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three main TKG datasets: MRR of 44.46 on ICEWS14, 48.73 on ICEWS05-15, and 32.22 on ICEWS18. Compared to the strongest rule-based baseline TR-Rules, INFER gains an average of 1.35% in MRR and 1.55% in Hits@10 across the three datasets. On the incomplete-dataset experiments (30% and 50% facts removed), INFER's performance degrades less sharply than TLogic or TR-Rules, demonstrating superior robustness to missing facts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

INFER-60(with vc) outperforms all 16 baselines on every metric across ICEWS14, ICEWS05-15, and ICEWS18. Against the strongest embedding-based method TECHS, INFER gains +0.58 MRR on ICEWS14 (44.46 vs 43.88), +0.35 MRR on ICEWS05-15 (48.73 vs 48.38), and +1.37 MRR on ICEWS18 (32.22 vs 30.85). Against the best rule-based baseline TR-Rules, INFER improves MRR by +1.14 (ICEWS14), +1.09 (ICEWS05-15), and +1.81 (ICEWS18), with Hits@10 gains of +1.14, +0.91, and +2.60 respectively. Static embedding baselines (Dismult, ComplEx) and early temporal embedding models (TA-Dismult, DE-SimplE, TNTComplEx) are substantially outperformed, with gaps exceeding 10 MRR points on ICEWS14. On the incomplete-dataset robustness test (30%/50% facts removed), INFER's performance slope is noticeably flatter than TLogic and TR-Rules, confirming that the Temporal Weight Matrices and static embedding model effectively compensate for missing facts during rule application.

## Relation to Prior Work

INFER sits at the intersection of two lines of work: rule-based TKG extrapolation (TLogic, TR-Rules) and matrix-based multi-hop reasoning over knowledge graphs (QTO). It adopts TR-Rules' rule mining and confidence calculation pipeline but fundamentally reimagines the rule application step, replacing CPU-based path matching with GPU-accelerated matrix operations inspired by QTO's query computation tree. The key conceptual departure from prior rule-based methods is the Temporal Validity Function, which extends fact truth values from binary to continuous real numbers by jointly modeling recency and frequency, and the use of a pre-trained static KG embedding (ComplEx) to assign non-zero probabilities to missing facts, thereby decoupling rule applicability from dataset completeness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in rule-based TKG reasoning: the inability to handle missing facts and the oversimplification of treating all historical facts as equally valid regardless of recency or repetition. The demonstrated robustness on datasets with 30–50% of facts removed is practically significant for real-world TKGs that are inherently incomplete. However, open questions remain: the model cannot explicitly capture the exact temporal ordering of facts (limiting longer rules with variable constraints), cannot leverage acyclic rules, and the Temporal Validity Function is acknowledged as an empirical design rather than a principled one. The GPU matrix paradigm also opens a path toward hybrid neural-symbolic TKG models that were previously infeasible due to computational cost.
