## Problem

Existing 4D human-object interaction (HOI) generation methods rely on SMPL-based motion generation, which cannot capture realistic full-body appearance, while zero-shot diffusion-based approaches lack understanding of where objects contact the body and how to maintain spatial correspondence during animation. This paper is the first to synthesize animatable 4D avatars with object interactions directly from text in a zero-shot setting, addressing both the contact-area identification and penetration-robust animation challenges.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252207/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt describing the interaction (e.g., 'Iron Man holding an axe in his hand'), individually pre-generated 3D Gaussian Splatting models of a human avatar and an object (via DreamGaussian), and a SMPL-X motion sequence as driving input. It outputs a 4D animatable scene represented as 3D Gaussians with HexPlane-based non-rigid deformation, where the object is rigidly attached to the human at the correct contact region. Training takes approximately 10 minutes for the 3D composition stage and 20 minutes for the 4D animation stage on a single A100 GPU.

## Method

AvatarGO introduces two key mechanisms. First, LLM-guided contact retargeting renders the 3D human from a frontal view, uses Lang-SAM to segment the target body part (e.g., 'hand') from the text prompt, back-projects the 2D mask onto 3D Gaussians via inverse rendering, and initializes the object's translation to the centroid of the segmented region; this is combined with Spatial-aware SDS (SSDS) that amplifies attention maps for interaction tokens to enforce spatial correlation. Second, correspondence-aware motion optimization constructs deformation fields for both human and object using SMPL-X linear blend skinning, then introduces a novel loss L_CA that minimizes the MSE between canonical and observed deformation matrices (computed via nearest-vertex lookup in SMPL-X), effectively using SMPL-X as an intermediary to preserve the static contact correspondence during animation. The overall training objective combines L_CA with texture-structure joint SDS and SSDS, optimizing the object's rotation, translation, and Gaussian attributes jointly.

## Results

On CLIP-based metrics, AvatarGO achieves the best CLIP-Score of 32.84 and CLIP-Directional Similarity of 28.03, substantially outperforming all baselines (next best: Ours Var-B at 30.57 CLIP-S and 25.90 CLIP-DS). In user studies across 7 criteria (penetration, scale accuracy, contact accuracy, motion quality, motion amount, text alignment, overall performance), AvatarGO scores 4.87–4.93/5, far exceeding the next-best method TC4D (4.03–4.52/5) and DreamGaussian4D (1.27–3.47/5). The method also maintains strong human identity preservation (CLIP-Image 92.20), though GraphDreamer scores slightly higher (98.44) due to its failure to properly composite objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AvatarGO decisively outperforms all four external baselines on the key interaction metrics. Against GraphDreamer, AvatarGO achieves 32.84 vs. 8.09 CLIP-S and 28.03 vs. 1.71 CLIP-DS, a >4× improvement, while GraphDreamer's higher CLIP-Image (98.44 vs. 92.20) is attributed to its failure to properly composite objects rather than better identity preservation. Against TC4D, AvatarGO leads by 13.0 points on CLIP-S (32.84 vs. 19.84) and 12.75 on CLIP-DS (28.03 vs. 15.28), and in user studies wins on all 7 criteria (e.g., 4.87 vs. 4.24 on penetration, 4.59 vs. 1.95 on motion quality). Against HumanGaussian, the gap is even wider on CLIP-DS (28.03 vs. 4.71, ~6×), reflecting HumanGaussian's inability to model object interactions. DreamGaussian4D, evaluated only in user studies, scores lowest on most criteria (1.27 penetration, 1.32 motion quality), confirming that video-diffusion-based 4D animation struggles with composited human-object scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

AvatarGO sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) 3D Gaussian Splatting–based generative methods (DreamGaussian, HumanGaussian) for realistic 3D content, (2) spatial-aware score distillation sampling (ComboVerse's SSDS) and LLM-guided scene composition (GraphDreamer) for multi-object 3D generation, and (3) 4D animation pipelines (DreamGaussian4D, TC4D, Comp4D) that extend static 3D scenes into dynamic ones. The key departure is from SMPL-based 4D HOI generation (InterDreamer, InterFusion), which is constrained by training data and cannot produce realistic full-body avatars, and from monolithic 4D scene animation (DreamGaussian4D, TC4D), which treats the entire scene as a single entity and cannot model per-object contact. By repurposing SMPL-X's linear blend skinning as a correspondence intermediary rather than a generative target, the paper bridges the gap between parametric human motion and free-form Gaussian-based avatars.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first zero-shot framework to produce animatable 4D human-object interaction scenes with realistic (non-SMPL) appearance directly from text, opening a path toward human-centric 4D content creation for AR/VR and gaming without per-scene training. However, several limitations remain: objects are treated as rigid bodies, only single-object interactions are handled, and the pipeline depends on separately generated 3D models and motion sequences, making end-to-end generation from text alone an open problem. The reliance on Lang-SAM for contact identification also raises questions about robustness to ambiguous or multi-contact prompts. Scaling to multi-object, non-rigid, or physically grounded interactions would be a natural next step.
