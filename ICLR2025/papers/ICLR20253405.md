## Problem

Existing temporal reasoning benchmarks rely on real-world knowledge-graph facts that LLMs may have memorized during pre-training, or on anonymization strategies that introduce factual inconsistencies, making it impossible to distinguish genuine temporal reasoning from parametric knowledge exploitation. Additionally, prior benchmarks conflate two distinct skills—temporal semantics/logic and temporal arithmetic—preventing fine-grained diagnosis of where models actually fail.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253405/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For ToT-Semantic, the model receives a set of anonymized temporal facts (e.g., 'E92 was the R17 of E69 from 1980 to 1988') presented in one of five controlled orderings, followed by a question drawn from eight types (EventAtTimeT, BeforeAfter, Timeline, etc.), and must return a JSON object with 'explanation' and 'answer' fields. For ToT-Arithmetic, the model receives a natural-language time-arithmetic question (e.g., timezone conversion, duration calculation) and returns a JSON with step-by-step reasoning and a structured answer. Evaluation uses exact-match accuracy, with precision and recall additionally reported for multi-entity Timeline questions.

## Method

The benchmark decouples temporal reasoning into two independent axes. ToT-Semantic is fully synthetic: it generates random graph backbones using seven generators (Erdős-Rényi, scale-free, Barabási-Albert, stochastic block model, star, complete, and anonymized WikiData ego-nets), assigns generic entity/relation names, attaches valid time intervals to edges, and templates eight question types over the resulting facts, yielding 2,800 questions with controlled variation in graph structure, size, and fact ordering. ToT-Arithmetic is crowd-sourced: 15 annotators expanded a seed set of time-arithmetic problems, which were then filtered, categorized into seven operation types (AddSubtract, Compare, Duration, Schedule, Timezone, Trick, MultiOp), functionalized for parameter sampling, and instantiated into 1,850 questions. The hermetic design—using only synthetic or novel real-world content—ensures LLMs cannot shortcut via memorized facts, forcing genuine reasoning over the presented temporal structure.

## Results

On ToT-Semantic, GPT-4o achieves the highest average rank (1.12) across graph structures, with peak accuracy of 94.00% on AWE graphs and 86.00% on SFN graphs, while the hardest setting (Complete graphs) drops all models below 53%. On ToT-Arithmetic, GPT-4o again leads (average rank 1.57), with the easiest category being Timezone (86.20% average accuracy) and the hardest being Duration (15.40% average accuracy), where all models struggle severely. Across both tasks, the gap between best and worst graph structures exceeds 40 percentage points for most models, and single-fact questions (e.g., EventAtWhatTime at 91.94%) are dramatically easier than multi-fact questions (e.g., Timeline at 31.66%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks five frontier LLMs rather than proposing a new method, so the 'baselines' are the competing models. GPT-4o achieves the best average rank on both ToT-Semantic (1.12) and ToT-Arithmetic (1.57), outperforming Gemini 1.5 Pro (1.43 on Semantic) and GPT-4 (2.75 on Semantic, 2.43 on Arithmetic). Gemini 1.5 Pro is the strongest on ToT-Semantic overall and notably excels on Timeline questions (36.29% vs. 24.00% for Claude-3-Sonnet) and large graphs (>1000 edges). GPT-4 is competitive on ToT-Arithmetic (rank 2.43) but lags on ToT-Semantic, particularly on Complete graphs (40.25%) and Timeline questions (28.29%). Claude-3-Sonnet consistently ranks last or near-last across both tasks (rank 4.75 on Semantic, 4.71 on Arithmetic), with its worst performance on Duration questions (15.00%) and Timeline questions (24.00%). Mistral Large occupies the middle tier, showing the largest variance across graph structures (32.75% on Complete to 88.50% on AWE).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the synthetic-probe methodology for LLM reasoning evaluation (Fatemi et al., 2024, which targeted graph-based reasoning) to the temporal domain, using parametric graph generators (ER, SFN, BA, SBM, star, complete) to construct hermetic temporal fact collections. It also adopts the functional-benchmark paradigm from Srivastava et al. (2024) to enable systematic sampling of ToT-Arithmetic problems. The key departure from prior temporal reasoning benchmarks (TimeSensitiveQA, TempReason, TGQA, TIQ, etc.) is the explicit rejection of real-world KG data and GPT-generated anonymization in favor of fully synthetic or crowd-sourced content, eliminating training-data leakage and spurious entity-name correlations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a striking gap: frontier LLMs achieve only ~15% average accuracy on Duration questions (computing days between dates), with a recurring one-day-off error pattern, suggesting that even basic temporal arithmetic remains a fundamental weakness. The finding that fact ordering (target-then-start-time) can swing accuracy by 10+ percentage points over shuffled order has direct practical implications for how temporal context should be structured in production LLM pipelines. Open questions include whether these findings generalize to multi-sentence or document-level temporal anchoring (acknowledged as a limitation), and whether the graph-structure sensitivity persists as models scale or are fine-tuned specifically for temporal tasks.
