## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models frequently fail on compositional prompts involving object counts (e.g., 'four unicorns') and spatial relations (e.g., 'a penguin on the right of a bowl'), yet the underlying cause of these inconsistencies remained unclear. This paper identifies that the initial random seed plays a critical, previously underexplored role: certain seeds systematically guide the model toward object arrangements that are more or less conducive to correct compositional rendering.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt in a structured compositional format (e.g., 'four apples, on a snowy mountain' for numerical or 'an apple on top of a hamburger, in a misty jungle forest' for spatial) and a random seed as input, producing a single RGB image (768×768 for Stable Diffusion 2.1, 512×512 for PixArt-α). The seed is not uniformly random but drawn from a small set of top-performing 'reliable seeds' identified through a VLM-based mining pipeline. For fine-tuning, the model consumes self-generated image-prompt pairs where images were produced exclusively with these reliable seeds, and optionally CogVLM2-rectified captions that correct mismatches between the prompt and the generated content.

## Method

The approach has two complementary components. First, a seed mining strategy evaluates 100 candidate seeds by generating images on a small prompt subset and using CogVLM2 to score compositional accuracy (object count or spatial relation correctness), then selecting the top-k seeds per quantity or relation. The key empirical finding is that reliable seeds correspond to specific object arrangement patterns (e.g., a 'four-grid' layout for four objects, vertical stacking for 'on top of') that consistently yield correct compositions. Second, a targeted fine-tuning procedure generates a curated dataset (2,400 numerical + 2,560 spatial image-prompt pairs) using only the top-performing seeds, then updates only the Q and K projection layers in all attention modules (excluding the first down-sampling and last up-sampling blocks in the U-Net) to transfer the seed-associated arrangement behaviors to arbitrary seeds without degrading overall image quality or diversity. An optional CogVLM2 rectification step rewrites mismatched prompts to match the generated image content before fine-tuning, further amplifying gains.

## Results

On the Comp90 benchmark, fine-tuning Stable Diffusion 2.1 with reliable-seed-generated data and CogVLM2 rectification achieves 51.3% accuracy and 0.80 MAE on numerical composition (vs. 37.5%/1.39 baseline, a 29.3% relative gain) and 36.6% accuracy on spatial composition (vs. 17.8% baseline, a 60.7% relative gain). For PixArt-α, the corresponding improvements are 19.5% (34.3%→41.2%) on numerical and 21.1% (22.7%→27.2%) on spatial composition. Aesthetic scores remain near the baseline (5.13 vs. 5.19) and recall drops only modestly (71.3 vs. 76.7), whereas external layout-based methods suffer much larger quality and diversity losses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LMD (Lian et al., 2023), the proposed method achieves substantially higher numerical accuracy (51.3% vs. 35.8%), aesthetic score (5.13 vs. 4.65), and recall (71.3% vs. 49.4%), but LMD leads on spatial accuracy (51.9% vs. 36.6%) at the cost of much lower aesthetic and recall. MultiDiffusion (Bar-Tal et al., 2023) shows a similar pattern: it edges out the proposed method on spatial accuracy (51.4% vs. 36.6%) but falls far behind on numerical accuracy (29.2% vs. 51.3%), aesthetic (4.40 vs. 5.13), and recall (36.2% vs. 71.3%). Ranni (Feng et al., 2024b) is the closest competitor on numerical accuracy (50.7% vs. 51.3%) but is outperformed on spatial accuracy (35.5% vs. 36.6%), aesthetic score (4.43 vs. 5.13), and recall (46.6% vs. 71.3%). Overall, the proposed fine-tuning strategy dominates on numerical composition, aesthetic quality, and output diversity across all three baselines, while LMD and MultiDiffusion retain an advantage on spatial composition accuracy—albeit with significant visual artifact and diversity penalties.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on prior observations that initial noise in diffusion models carries low-frequency structural information (Guttenberg 2023; Lin et al. 2024) and that noise patterns in high-density latent regions yield better samples (Xu et al. 2024; Guo et al. 2024a), but it is the first to systematically link specific seeds to recurring object-arrangement patterns (e.g., four-grid layouts) and to exploit this for compositional generation. It departs from the dominant inference-time layout-control paradigm (LMD, MultiDiffusion, Attend-and-Excite) by requiring no external layout inputs or attention manipulation, and from prior fine-tuning-on-curated-data approaches (GORS, SDXL, EMU) by restricting updates to Q,K attention projections and sourcing training data from the model's own reliable-seed generations rather than external datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practically attractive two-tier solution: a zero-cost plug-and-play sampling strategy (use top-k reliable seeds) and a lightweight fine-tuning recipe that preserves aesthetic quality and diversity while substantially boosting compositional accuracy, making it accessible to practitioners without layout-annotation pipelines. The Comp90 benchmark (90 object categories, 12 backgrounds, numerical + spatial splits) provides a reproducible evaluation resource for compositional T2I. However, absolute accuracies remain moderate (≈51% numerical, ≈37% spatial for SD 2.1), and the seed-to-layout mapping may not transfer across model versions, architectures, or prompt distributions—open questions the authors flag for future work on generalizing seed mining as a broader inference-time scaling technique.
