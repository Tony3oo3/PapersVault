## Problem

Existing camera control methods for video diffusion models (e.g., MotionCtrl, CameraCtrl) rely on U-Net architectures with decomposed spatial and temporal layers, making them inapplicable to the newer transformer-based video diffusion models (Sora, SnapVideo, Lumina-T2X) that process spatial and temporal information jointly. No prior work enables fine-grained 3D camera pose control for these spatiotemporal transformers, leaving a critical gap as the community shifts toward this architecture for higher-quality video generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, a per-frame sequence of camera extrinsics [R;t] and intrinsics K_f (normalized relative to the first frame), and optionally a reference image for image-to-video or multi-view synthesis. Camera parameters are converted into per-pixel Plücker coordinates (6D vectors), patchified at 4×4 resolution, and fed alongside the noisy video latents. The base model generates 16-frame videos at 36×64 resolution in a compressed latent space, which are then upscaled to 288×512 by a frozen upsampler that inherits camera motion from the low-resolution output.

## Method

VD3D introduces a ControlNet-like conditioning mechanism tailored to FIT (Far-reaching Interleaved Transformer) blocks. Camera extrinsics are normalized to the first frame, then converted to per-pixel Plücker coordinates that provide fine-grained spatiotemporal positional information; these are patchified and projected via a 2-layer MLP into camera tokens matching the video token sequence length and dimensionality. In each FIT block, a new cross-attention layer reads from the concatenated camera and video tokens, followed by a feed-forward layer and a zero-initialized 1D convolution whose output is added as a residual to the standard read cross-attention path. Only the newly added parameters (~230M, ~5% of the 4B model) are trained on RealEstate10K (~65K clips) with the base model frozen, while the camera cross-attention and FF layers are initialized from the original network weights to preserve visual quality at initialization. Ablations confirm that pixel-wise Plücker embeddings (vs. raw camera matrices), the ControlNet residual structure (vs. simple feature addition), and injection into spatiotemporal patches (vs. the text context vector) are each essential for achieving strong camera controllability without degrading generation quality.

## Results

On the RealEstate10K test set, VD3D achieves a translation error of 0.409 and rotation error of 0.043 (measured via ParticleSfM), substantially outperforming MotionCtrl (0.518/0.161) and CameraCtrl (0.532/0.165) adapted to the same transformer backbone. On the out-of-distribution MSR-VTT prompts, VD3D achieves 0.504/0.050 vs. 0.627/0.148 (MotionCtrl) and 0.578/0.220 (CameraCtrl). In a 20-participant user study, 82% of participants prefer VD3D's camera alignment over MotionCtrl and 78% over CameraCtrl, with overall preference of 84% and 66% respectively (all p < 0.001).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines—MotionCtrl and CameraCtrl—both originally designed for U-Net-based video diffusion models and adapted here to the same SnapVideo FIT transformer backbone. VD3D decisively outperforms both on camera pose accuracy: on RealEstate10K, it reduces rotation error from 0.161 (MotionCtrl) and 0.165 (CameraCtrl) to 0.043, and translation error from 0.518/0.532 to 0.409. The advantage is even more pronounced on out-of-distribution MSR-VTT prompts, where VD3D's rotation error (0.050) is roughly 3× lower than MotionCtrl (0.148) and 4× lower than CameraCtrl (0.220). In the human preference study, 82% of participants prefer VD3D's camera alignment over MotionCtrl and 78% over CameraCtrl, with overall preference of 84% and 66% respectively. The U-Net-based versions of both baselines (MotionCtrl U-Net, CameraCtrl U-Net) achieve intermediate accuracy (e.g., 0.465–0.477 translation error on RE10K) but are architecturally incompatible with the transformer setting. The key qualitative finding is that MotionCtrl degrades visual quality when adapted to the transformer (due to fine-tuning base weights), while CameraCtrl preserves quality but fails to follow camera poses—VD3D's ControlNet-style zero-initialized conditioning avoids both failure modes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the camera-controlled text-to-video generation line (MotionCtrl, CameraCtrl) from U-Net-based architectures with decomposed spatial/temporal layers to large spatiotemporal transformers (FIT-based SnapVideo, vanilla DiT) that process all tokens jointly. The key architectural departure is replacing frame-level camera conditioning on temporal layers with pixel-wise Plücker coordinate embeddings injected through a ControlNet-like cross-attention mechanism into the FIT read-attention blocks. The approach inherits the zero-initialized residual conditioning paradigm from ControlNet but adapts it to the entangled spatiotemporal computation of transformers, where naive adaptation of prior methods either fails to provide control or degrades visual quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the video generation community shifts from U-Net to transformer backbones (Sora, SnapVideo, CogVideoX, Lumina-T2X), this work provides the first viable recipe for 3D camera control in that new paradigm, unblocking downstream applications like single-image 3D reconstruction and multi-view synthesis from photorealistic video models. Key limitations include the 16-frame generation length, lack of control over scene-internal motion (only camera), and the frozen upsampler. The demonstrated architecture-agnosticism (working on both FIT and vanilla DiT) suggests the Plücker-in-patches + ControlNet conditioning pattern will be a reusable building block for future controllable video transformers.
