## Problem

CLIP-style VLMs are trained with an inter-modal contrastive loss that aligns paired image-text embeddings but imposes no constraints on intra-modal distances, leading to what the authors term intra-modal misalignment: image-image and text-text similarity scores in the shared embedding space do not faithfully reflect true semantic similarity. This makes the common practice of using CLIP encoders for intra-modal tasks (e.g., image-to-image retrieval) inherently suboptimal, a gap that prior work has only partially addressed in narrow settings like zero-shot classification.

## Input / Output

OTI takes a single RGB image and produces a text-space feature vector by iteratively optimizing one or more pseudo-tokens through the frozen text encoder; OVI takes a single text prompt and produces an image-space feature vector by optimizing pseudo-patches through the frozen ViT image encoder (requiring nearest-neighbor interpolation to match the fixed patch count U). The resulting inverted features are then used for inter-modal cosine similarity comparisons against a gallery of native features (images for OTI, texts for OVI) to produce retrieval rankings or class predictions. OVI is restricted to ViT-based backbones due to its reliance on the patch embedding space, and the optimal number of pseudo-patches (1–4) depends on model size.

## Method

The paper introduces two single-feature-level, optimization-based modality inversion techniques that map representations from their native modality to the complementary one while keeping all encoder weights frozen. OTI (adapted from Baldrati et al.) optimizes R pseudo-tokens in the token embedding space, concatenates them with a fixed template ('a photo of'), and minimizes cosine distance to the image features through the text encoder. OVI (new) optimizes P pseudo-patches in the patch embedding space, applies nearest-neighbor interpolation to fill the ViT's fixed U-patch input, and minimizes cosine distance to the text features through the image encoder. The key insight is that this transforms intra-modal tasks into inter-modal ones, exploiting CLIP's well-calibrated cross-modal alignment; the paper validates this by showing the reverse transformation (making inter-modal tasks intra-modal) degrades performance. Additionally, the authors demonstrate that incorporating an intra-modal SimCLR loss during pre-training (as in SLIP) or increasing the contrastive temperature to close the modality gap both reduce the misalignment, confirming the causal link between the training objective and the phenomenon.

## Results

On image-to-image retrieval across 15 datasets, approaching the task inter-modally via OTI-inverted features consistently outperforms the intra-modal baseline, with average mAP improvements of +2.0% (CLIP B/32: 39.2→41.2), +3.3% (OpenCLIP B/32: 52.0→55.3), and up to +10.9% (SigLIP B/32: 49.5→60.4). On text-to-text retrieval, OVI-inverted features yield 1–5% mAP gains over the intra-modal baseline (e.g., CLIP B/32: 37.7→40.6 average). Conversely, transforming the natively inter-modal zero-shot image classification task into an intra-modal one via modality inversion degrades accuracy (e.g., CLIP B/32: 61.9→56.4 average), confirming that the gains stem from exploiting inter-modal alignment rather than the inversion process itself.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares OTI/OVI against two families of alternative inter-modal mapping strategies: captioning-based approaches (DeCap and CoCa) and a trained linear adapter. On image-to-image retrieval (CLIP ViT-B/32, 5-dataset average mAP), OTI achieves 40.2, substantially outperforming DeCap (2.0), CoCa fine-tuned on COCO (1.4), and CoCa trained on LAION (16.3); all three captioning methods actually fall below the intra-modal baseline (38.5), indicating that generated captions lack the discriminative detail needed for fine-grained retrieval. The adapter-based approach (trained on 595K LLaVA-CC3M pairs) reaches 39.6 mAP on image retrieval and 39.3 on text retrieval, improving over the intra-modal baselines (38.5 / 37.7) but still trailing OTI (40.2) and OVI (40.6). This confirms that the performance gain comes from exploiting CLIP's inter-modal alignment at the single-feature level without external training data, rather than from the modality inversion mechanism per se.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the modality gap literature (Liang et al., 2022) and the limited observations of intra-modal misalignment in zero/few-shot classification (Udandarao et al., 2023; Yi et al., 2024), but generalizes the analysis to a broad range of intra-modal retrieval tasks across 15+ datasets. It repurposes Optimization-based Textual Inversion (OTI) from Baldrati et al. (2023) — originally designed for composed image retrieval — as a pure feature-mapping tool, and introduces the complementary Optimization-based Visual Inversion (OVI) to map text features into the image embedding space. The work also connects the modality gap phenomenon directly to intra-modal misalignment, showing that narrowing the gap (via higher temperature or intra-modal losses as in SLIP) reduces the problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that CLIP's intra-modal similarities are systematically misaligned challenges a widespread assumption in the VLM community, where image-image and text-text comparisons are routinely used for KNN classification, video consistency scoring, and retrieval. However, the proposed modality inversion workaround is computationally expensive (150–1000 optimization steps per sample), limiting immediate practical adoption. The key open question is whether efficient, training-free or lightweight methods can compensate for intra-modal misalignment without iterative per-sample optimization, and whether future VLM pre-training objectives should routinely include intra-modal constraints to prevent the problem at its source.
