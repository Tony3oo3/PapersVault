## Problem

Monolithic VLA models that directly fine-tune VLMs to predict robot actions require large, expensive on-robot datasets and cannot effectively leverage cheaper off-domain data such as action-free videos, simulation, or data from different embodiments. Meanwhile, small 3D policy models achieve dexterous control but are brittle to semantic and visual variations. The paper asks how to combine VLM-level generalization with small-policy dexterity while training the high-level component on abundant, inexpensive off-domain data.

## Input / Output

The high-level VLM takes a single monocular RGB image and a natural-language task instruction, and outputs a 2D path: a short sequence (typically 2–5 waypoints after Ramer-Douglas-Peucker simplification) of normalized pixel coordinates (x, y ∈ [0,1]) interleaved with gripper open/close tokens. The low-level policy receives the RGB image, a 3D point cloud, proprioceptive state, the language instruction, and the VLM's 2D path rendered as a color-gradient trajectory overlaid on the image (or concatenated as extra channels), and outputs continuous robot end-effector actions at high control frequency. The VLM is queried only once per episode, decoupling its inference cost from the action rate.

## Method

HAMSTER decouples a large fine-tuned VLM (VILA-1.5-13b) from a small 3D policy (RVT-2 or 3D-DA) via a 2D end-effector path as the intermediate representation. The VLM is fine-tuned on a multi-domain off-domain dataset—770k RoboPoint pixel-location tasks, 320k simulated RLBench trajectories, and 110k real-robot trajectories from Bridge and DROID—none of which come from the deployment environment, so the VLM never sees in-domain data. The low-level policy is trained via standard supervised imitation learning on a small in-domain dataset (320 teleoperated episodes) where oracle 2D paths are projected from proprioception and drawn onto the image as colored trajectories with gripper-state markers. The key architectural insight is that the 2D path is embodiment-agnostic, easily extractable from videos or simulation via point tracking or forward kinematics, and sufficiently abstract to bridge large domain gaps, allowing the VLM to transfer knowledge from simulation and other robots to the real deployment platform while the 3D policy handles precise spatial control.

## Results

In real-world experiments across 74 tasks and 7 generalization axes (object/goal, visual, language, spatial, novel object, multiple), HAMSTER achieves an average 20% absolute improvement in success rate over OpenVLA, representing a 50% relative gain. In the Colosseum simulation benchmark, HAMSTER+3D-DA achieves 0.46 average success across 16 visual variation conditions versus 0.35 for vanilla 3D-DA (31% relative improvement). HAMSTER also demonstrates strong data efficiency, achieving 0.36 success with only 50% of demonstrations versus 0.18 for standard 3D-DA (2× improvement), and robust camera-view invariance (0.98 success on a novel camera angle vs. 0.23 for OpenVLA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HAMSTER consistently outperforms all four baselines. Against OpenVLA (monolithic VLA), HAMSTER achieves a 20% absolute success-rate improvement across all seven real-world generalization axes (e.g., 0.98 vs. 0.23 on novel camera views; 1.00 vs. 0.60 on original camera pick-and-place), with the gap widening under semantic and visual perturbations. Against RVT-2 (3D policy without path conditioning), HAMSTER+RVT2 more than doubles success rates on most task categories in Table 4 (e.g., 1.00 vs. 0.50 on basic pick-and-place; 1.00 vs. 0.00 on spatial generalization). Against 3D-DA (3D Diffuser Actor), HAMSTER+3D-DA improves average Colosseum success from 0.35 to 0.46 (31% relative gain) and achieves 2× the success rate with only 50% of the training demonstrations (0.36 vs. 0.18). Against RT-Trajectory with GPT-4o (zero-shot path generation), HAMSTER's fine-tuned VLM achieves a significantly better human-evaluation rank (1.40 vs. 3.47), confirming that off-domain fine-tuning on simulation and video data produces more accurate and generalizable 2D paths than prompting a closed-source VLM directly.

## Relation to Prior Work

HAMSTER sits at the intersection of monolithic VLA models (RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA) that finetune VLMs to directly predict robot actions, and small 3D policy models (RVT-2, 3D-DA) that achieve dexterous manipulation but lack semantic generalization. The closest prior work is RT-Trajectory (Gu et al., 2023), which conditions low-level policies on trajectory sketches, but HAMSTER departs by (1) finetuning the VLM on cheap off-domain data (simulation, videos, point predictions) rather than relying on zero-shot VLMs or human annotation for path generation, and (2) enabling the low-level policy to consume rich 3D and proprioceptive inputs that monolithic VLMs cannot process. The 2D path interface is more expressive than keypoint-affordance approaches (RoboPoint, KITE) while remaining embodiment-agnostic and cheaply extractable from diverse data sources.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core bottleneck in robot learning: the cost and scarcity of on-robot observation-action data. By showing that a VLM finetuned on simulation and video data can produce useful 2D path guidance that transfers to real-robot manipulation, HAMSTER opens a path toward training VLA models on abundant, inexpensive data sources. Key limitations include the bandwidth-limited 2D path interface (cannot encode force, rotation, or 3D geometry), the restriction to tabletop manipulation on a single Franka Panda arm, and the fact that the low-level policy still requires in-domain teleoperation data. Open questions include whether learnable intermediate representations could replace the fixed 2D path format, whether the approach extends to dexterous multi-finger manipulation, and whether training the VLM directly on large-scale human video (without any robot data) would suffice.
