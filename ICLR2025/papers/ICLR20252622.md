## Problem

Post-hoc attribution methods are assumed to produce explanations independent of how a model was trained, yet this paper reveals that the training objective of the classification probe (BCE vs. CE loss) and probe complexity have a far greater impact on explanation quality than the pre-training paradigm itself. This overlooked dependency means that two models differing only in a single linear layer (<10% of parameters) can yield dramatically different attribution maps, undermining the reliability of post-hoc explanations in practice.

## Input / Output

The method consumes frozen pre-trained backbone features (ResNet-50 or ViT-B/16) from diverse pre-training paradigms (supervised, MoCo v2, BYOL, DINO, CLIP) and trains only the lightweight classification head on top. The output is a class prediction paired with an attribution heatmap, generated either by post-hoc methods (LRP, GradCAM, IntGrad, LIME, ScoreCAM, CausalX, CGW1, Rollout) or by the inherent B-cos explanation mechanism. The unusual aspect is that the backbone is entirely frozen, isolating the probe's training details as the sole variable, and evaluation uses synthetic grid images (GridPG) and bounding-box annotations (EPG) rather than standard accuracy metrics.

## Method

The paper identifies that CE loss's softmax shift-invariance admits infinitely many functionally equivalent probes that produce identical predictions but vastly different attribution maps (e.g., GridPG scores of 65.7% vs. 11.9%), making CE-trained explanations poorly calibrated. To resolve this, probes are trained with BCE loss, which penalizes constant positive shifts to non-target classes and biases the probe toward class-specific features, yielding significantly more localized, compact, and stable attributions. Additionally, the authors propose 2-3 layer B-cos MLP probes—where each layer applies a cosine-similarity-weighted transformation (|c(a;W)|^{B-1} ⊙ Wa) that induces weight-input alignment during optimization—instead of linear probes, better distilling class-specific features from frozen representations that may not be linearly separable. This combination of BCE loss and B-cos MLP probing consistently improves localization, compactness, and pixel-deletion stability across all pre-training paradigms, both CNN and ViT architectures, and a wide range of attribution methods.

## Results

The paper's central finding is that training linear probes with BCE loss instead of the conventional CE loss yields large, consistent gains in explanation localization across all pre-training paradigms (supervised, DINO, MoCo v2/v3, BYOL, CLIP) and architectures (ResNet-50, ViT-B/16). For ResNet-50 with LRP on conventional models, DINO GridPG improves from 52% to 92% (+40 p.p.), MoCo v2 from 50% to 80% (+30 p.p.), and CLIP from 19% to 51% (+32 p.p.). For ViT-B/16 probed with CGW1, DINO GridPG improves by +11.7 p.p. and supervised by +15.7 p.p., while B-cos ViTs show even larger gains (e.g., B-ViTc-S/16 DINO: +32.7 p.p.). Accuracy remains comparable (within ~1 p.p.), confirming that the localization gains come at no classification cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an empirical study rather than a method-vs-method comparison, so the primary 'baseline' is the standard CE-trained linear probe, and the proposed alternative is the BCE-trained probe (and optionally a B-cos MLP probe). BCE probes consistently outperform CE probes on every localization metric (GridPG, EPG) across all pre-training frameworks and attribution methods tested (LRP, IntGrad, LIME, B-cos, CGW1, CausalX, Rollout, GradCAM, I×G, ScoreCAM), with the largest gains seen for DINO (+37–40 p.p. GridPG on ResNet-50) and for inherently interpretable B-cos models (+20–33 p.p. on ViTs). The only exceptions are I×G and GradCAM on conventional (non-B-cos) backbones, where improvements are inconsistent, which the authors attribute to known 'shattered gradient' issues. On the secondary axis of probe complexity, 3-layer B-cos MLP probes further boost both accuracy and localization over linear probes (e.g., DINO IntGrad: 70.6% acc / 70.0% GridPG → 73.1% / 89.0%), while conventional MLPs do not show the same consistent GridPG gains. The paper also demonstrates that the choice of pre-training paradigm (DINO vs. BYOL vs. MoCo v2 vs. CLIP vs. supervised) has a comparatively limited effect on explanation quality once the probe loss is fixed, reinforcing that the probe training objective is the dominant factor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the B-cos family of inherently interpretable networks (Bohle et al., 2022; 2024), extending the B-cos layer transformation into multi-layer MLP probes applied on top of frozen pre-trained backbones. It departs from the standard linear-probing paradigm (Caron et al., 2021; Grill et al., 2020) by showing that the probe's training objective—specifically BCE versus CE—matters far more for explanation quality than the pre-training scheme itself, a dependency that prior post-hoc attribution work has implicitly assumed away. The non-linear probing angle connects to He et al. (2022) and Li et al. (2023b) on readout complexity, but the paper's distinctive contribution is framing probe design through the lens of explanation quality rather than classification accuracy alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single classification layer trained with BCE (rather than the conventional CE) yields dramatically better localizations across all tested attribution methods, architectures, and pre-training paradigms provides an immediately actionable recipe for practitioners deploying post-hoc XAI on pre-trained models. It also exposes a previously unexamined assumption in the attribution literature—that explanations are independent of how the final layer was trained—which has implications for how explanations are interpreted and trusted in safety-critical applications. Open questions include whether the effect persists under full fine-tuning (where backbone and probe co-adapt), whether it generalizes to non-visual modalities, and whether the shift-invariance argument extends to other loss formulations beyond the softmax family.
