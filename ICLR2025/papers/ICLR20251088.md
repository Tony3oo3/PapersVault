## Problem

Wasserstein Distributionally Robust Optimization (WDRO) defends against adversarial perturbations at the distribution level, offering better generalization than pointwise adversarial training. However, WDRO still suffers from robust overfitting because it ignores the statistical error inherent in finite-sample training. This paper identifies that incorporating statistical error into the WDRO ambiguity set remains an unexplored avenue and addresses this gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251088/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100) and outputs class labels. Adversarial perturbations are restricted to the input feature space only, with a large penalty constant M in the Wasserstein cost to prevent label flipping. The training procedure operates on mini-batches of (x, y) pairs, where the empirical distribution D_n over these pairs defines the center of the ambiguity set, and the method jointly optimizes model parameters θ and a dual variable λ for the Wasserstein constraint.

## Method

SR-WDRO introduces a novel ambiguity set defined by a two-stage constraint: first, an intermediate distribution D'' must lie within Wasserstein distance ε of the empirical distribution D_n (controlling adversarial noise), and second, the final adversarial distribution D' must lie within KL divergence γ of D'' (controlling statistical error). This formulation is cast as a zero-sum game between a learner choosing model parameters and an adversary choosing a distribution, for which the paper proves existence of both Stackelberg and Nash equilibria under natural assumptions. Theoretically, the paper establishes high-probability generalization bounds showing the adversarial test loss is upper-bounded by the statistically robust training loss, with failure probability decaying exponentially in sample size n·γ. Practically, the method uses the UDR algorithm to generate adversarial examples via the Wasserstein dual, then solves a KL-constrained reweighting problem (an exponential cone program) to compute the statistically robust loss, incurring only ~12.7% additional training time over standard adversarial training.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18, ε=8/255, PGD-10 evaluation), SR-WDRO achieves 48.58% final robust accuracy with the smallest overfitting gap (Diff=3.36) among all methods, compared to PGD-AT (45.16%, Diff=7.75), UDR-AT (46.60%, Diff=6.63), and HR (47.32%, Diff=3.90). On CIFAR-100, SR-WDRO reaches 23.09% final robust accuracy with Diff=5.86, again the smallest gap versus PGD-AT (21.87%, Diff=7.50), UDR-AT (22.07%, Diff=7.28), and HR (21.15%, Diff=7.20). The method also achieves the best final robust accuracy under stronger attacks (PGD-200, Auto-Attack) and with larger training budgets (10/255, 12/255), confirming superior robustness generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SR-WDRO outperforms all four baselines on the key overfitting metric (Diff between best and final robust accuracy) on both CIFAR-10 (3.36 vs. 7.75/6.63/3.90) and CIFAR-100 (5.86 vs. 7.50/7.28/7.20), confirming it most effectively mitigates robust overfitting. It also achieves the highest final robust accuracy on both datasets: 48.58% on CIFAR-10 (vs. 45.16% PGD-AT, 46.60% UDR-AT, 47.32% HR) and 23.09% on CIFAR-100 (vs. 21.87%, 22.07%, 21.15%). The trade-off is a modest drop in natural accuracy (83.34% vs. 84.80% for PGD-AT on CIFAR-10) and a slightly lower best robust accuracy than UDR-AT on CIFAR-10 (51.95% vs. 53.23%). Under stronger attacks (PGD-200, Auto-Attack) and when training with larger budgets (10/255, 12/255) while testing at 8/255, SR-WDRO consistently achieves the best robust accuracy across all settings, e.g., 46.79% PGD-200 and 44.06% AA at ε=8/255, surpassing all baselines by 1–2%. On the larger WRN28-10 architecture, SR-WDRO further extends its lead with 51.26% final robust accuracy and the smallest Diff of 2.61.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Wasserstein Distributionally Robust Optimization (WDRO) by augmenting its Wasserstein-ball ambiguity set with a KL-divergence constraint, creating a two-stage uncertainty set that simultaneously accounts for adversarial perturbations (via Wasserstein distance) and finite-sample statistical error (via KL divergence). It builds directly on UDR (Bui et al., 2022) for the Wasserstein-based adversarial component and on the KL-reweighting machinery of Bennouna et al. (2023), but departs from the latter by retaining Wasserstein distance rather than the Levy-Prokhorov metric, preserving WDRO's distributional-perturbation advantage over pointwise adversarial training. The game-theoretic analysis (Stackelberg and Nash equilibrium existence) is novel for WDRO-type formulations and extends prior minimax results that required stronger assumptions on the loss function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete theoretical gap—WDRO's blind spot to statistical error—as the root cause of robust overfitting, providing a principled fix with high-probability generalization certificates that do not depend on parameter-space dimensionality. Practically, the training algorithm adds only ~12.7% overhead over standard adversarial training, making it accessible. Key limitations include a modest natural-accuracy drop (e.g., 83.34% vs. 84.80% on CIFAR-10), the intractability of the exact SR-WDRO objective requiring approximate solvers, and the current restriction to supervised classification. Open questions include whether the framework extends to unsupervised or regression settings, whether better approximations can close the accuracy gap, and whether the KL budget γ can be adapted automatically rather than set manually.
