## Problem

Long-context training for LLMs suffers from a severe shortage of naturally long documents (less than 0.1% of RefinedWeb exceeds 64K tokens), and existing remedies—mechanical concatenation of short documents or upsampling of relatively long ones—fail to preserve the critical quality attribute of long-distance referrals (semantically identical phrases separated by large distances). The paper identifies that referral density, not merely document length, governs training effectiveness, and that previous fixed-threshold upsampling is suboptimal because it does not account for this density.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes root web pages sampled from a pretraining dataset (RefinedWeb) and the Common Crawl HTML database as inputs. It extracts all hyperlinks from each root page's original HTML via regex on <a> tags, yielding (anchor_text, URL) pairs, then retrieves and cleans the linked page content. The output is a single long document per root page, structured by prepending each referenced page's content (prefixed with its anchor text on a separate line) before the root document, ordered by hyperlink appearance. These packed documents are then chunked into fixed 128K-token sequences for continued pretraining.

## Method

LongPack constructs long documents by exploiting hyperlinks as native referral signals in web pages. For each root page, it looks up the original HTML from Common Crawl, extracts all (key, URL) hyperlink pairs, retrieves and cleans the linked content, and packs everything into a single document where the anchor text explicitly bridges the root content to the referenced material—creating dense, structurally meaningful long-distance referrals. The paper first validates the importance of these referrals through controlled experiments: breaking long-distance referrals in 16K–64K documents drops RULER score by 37.6%, while artificially elongating them via intra-chunk round-robin reordering improves it by 33.9%. Training uses GLM-4-9B with RoPE position interpolation (16× frequency scaling for 128K context), 1B tokens, RingAttention for memory-efficient long-sequence attention, and DeepSpeed-ZeRO for multi-GPU parallelism. With only 0.5% of RefinedWeb as root pages, LongPack generates a corpus matching the full pretraining dataset in size, achieving 73.0 RULER score versus 55.1 for the previous state-of-the-art upsampling baseline.

## Results

On the RULER benchmark at 128K context length, LongPack achieves an overall score of 73.0, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art upsampling baseline (55.1) by 32.7%. The method also surpasses domain-specific baselines: Books (60.61) and Dialogues (62.15). LongPack reaches 86.2% of the model's upper-bound capacity (Ideal = 89.3) on all tasks except common-word-extraction, where all methods perform poorly. In terms of scalability, LongPack generates a corpus equivalent in size to the entire pretraining dataset using only 0.5% root documents, with packed documents growing 13× larger on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LongPack (73.0) decisively outperforms the upsampling baseline from Fu et al. (2024) (55.1) by 17.9 points overall, with particularly large gains on multi-pos retrieval (+31.9) and QA (+12.8). Against the Books baseline following Peng et al. (2023) (60.61), LongPack leads by 12.4 points overall, winning on every subtask except aggregation where it is only marginally behind (37.8 vs. 31.3). Compared to the Dialogues baseline from Li et al. (2023) (62.15), LongPack gains 10.9 points overall, dominating on multi-pos retrieval (+27.9) and single-pos retrieval (+0.5), while trailing slightly on multi-hop tracing (87.2 vs. 91.0) and aggregation (37.8 vs. 38.6). The paper attributes LongPack's multi-hop and aggregation gap to the fact that its packed documents, while rich in long-distance referrals, are assembled from multiple shorter pages rather than being single coherent long texts. Validation loss on SlimPajama domains confirms that LongPack training does not degrade general model quality, with losses dropping or staying flat across all seven domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant long-context data engineering paradigm of mechanical concatenation and upsampling (Xiong et al., 2024; Fu et al., 2024) by identifying a specific structural property—long-distance referral density—as the critical quality attribute of training data. Rather than treating document length as the sole quality signal, it repurposes hyperlinks in web pages as a native referral signal to construct documents that preserve or enhance these long-range semantic connections. The approach is conceptually adjacent to LongAlign's reordering strategy (Yushi et al., 2024) and builds on the mechanistic observation that attention heads develop long-distance retrieval capabilities (Wu et al., 2024), but shifts the focus from model-side phenomena to a data-side construction principle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, scalable recipe for generating long-context training data that addresses the fundamental scarcity of naturally long documents (less than 0.1% of RefinedWeb exceeds 64K tokens). The 32.7% improvement over the upsampling baseline and the demonstration that 0.5% of root documents can generate a corpus matching the full pretraining dataset in long-document count are practically significant. However, the method is limited to web-page data with a ~10% hyperlink hit rate, validated on a single 9B model with only 1B training tokens, and the hyperlink signal does not transfer to non-web domains (code, math, books). Open questions include whether multi-round packing, domain-specific referral signals (imports, citations), and filtering weak hyperlinks can close the remaining gap to the 'Ideal' upper bound.
