## Problem

Existing personalized text-to-image methods (Textual Inversion, DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion) associate a single fixed token with a visual concept, which works for same-instance personalization but fails when reference images represent a distribution of different instances sharing common visual attributes. The fixed token either overfits to a specific feature combination or underfits to generic category information, producing limited diversity and inconsistent visual attributes in generated images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250410/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of 5–20 reference images (different instances sharing visual attributes) and optionally a text prompt at inference for guided editing. It produces diverse 768×768 images (256×256 for synthetic dataset experiments) that follow the reference set's visual distribution. The learned artifact is a Gaussian distribution over K=32 soft prompt embeddings in CLIP text feature space, which is directly transferable to other text-guided pipelines (e.g., MVDream for 3D) without retraining the generation model.

## Method

DreamDistribution replaces the single fixed token of prior personalization methods with a set of K learnable soft prompts whose CLIP text-encoder features define a Gaussian distribution (mean μ_c and std σ_c). During training, the reparameterization trick (sampling ω ~ N(0,I) and computing c̃ = μ_c + ω·σ_c) makes the distribution differentiable through the frozen Stable Diffusion 2.1 denoiser, with the standard diffusion noise-prediction loss as the primary objective. An orthogonal loss penalizes pairwise cosine similarity among the K prompt features to prevent collapse into a degenerate low-variance distribution. At inference, sampling from the learned distribution yields diverse prompts that guide generation of novel in-distribution images, and the distribution can be manipulated (variance scaling, linear interpolation between distributions, text concatenation) for controllability.

## Results

On the diverse personalized generation task (12 scenarios, 40 images each), DreamDistribution achieves the best CLIP-I (0.84), DINO (0.50), Density (1.59), Coverage (0.93), and FID (215.15) among all methods, outperforming DreamBooth, Textual Inversion, and Custom Diffusion on 5 of 6 metrics. On the synthetic ImageNet-100 classification task, training a ResNet-50 on DreamDistribution-generated images yields 56.7% Top-1 accuracy on ImageNet validation, surpassing ImageNet-SD (55.4%), DreamBooth (44.1%), and Custom Diffusion (34.0%), with the best FID (131.60) and Coverage (0.56) among all generation methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamDistribution outperforms all three instance-level personalization baselines (DreamBooth, Textual Inversion, Custom Diffusion) on 5 of 6 automatic metrics for diverse generation, with the largest margins on Density (+0.14 to +0.68) and Coverage (+0.06 to +0.19), confirming superior diversity. The only metric where it trails is CLIP-T (text-image alignment at 0.29 vs. 0.33 for Custom Diffusion), which the authors attribute to the inherent randomness of sampling from a distribution. On the synthetic ImageNet task, DreamDistribution beats ImageNet-SD by 1.3% Top-1 on IN and 6.2% on IN-V2, and dramatically outperforms DreamBooth (56.7% vs. 44.1%) and Custom Diffusion (56.7% vs. 34.0%) on IN Top-1, while also achieving the best FID (131.60 vs. 139.49 for DreamBooth). Against non-learning baselines (Class Names, CLIP Prompts), the gap is even larger (56.7% vs. ~45.5%), demonstrating that learning a prompt distribution from reference images is essential for capturing real image distributions. Human evaluation (20 annotators, 240 samples) confirms the automatic metrics, with DreamDistribution receiving the highest preference for diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends prompt tuning (Zhou et al., 2022) from a single learnable soft prompt to a learnable *distribution* of K prompts in CLIP text feature space, adapting the Gaussian-distribution-over-prompts idea from ProDA (Lu et al., 2022) from image classification to text-to-image diffusion generation. It departs from instance-level personalization methods (Textual Inversion, DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion) that associate a fixed token with one specific subject, instead modeling category- or attribute-level commonalities across a set of reference images. The reparameterization trick from VAEs is repurposed to make sampling from the prompt distribution differentiable through the frozen diffusion denoiser, and an orthogonal loss (also from ProDA) prevents prompt collapse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical gap where users want diverse images following a shared visual style or category rather than replicating a single instance, and the learned distribution's residence in CLIP text feature space enables zero-cost transfer to other text-guided pipelines (e.g., text-to-3D via MVDream) and flexible manipulation (variance scaling, distribution interpolation). Key open questions include whether the Gaussian assumption is sufficient for highly multimodal or sparse reference sets, and whether the approach scales to very large or very heterogeneous image collections. The synthetic ImageNet experiment (100–500 real images per class suffice) suggests the method could be useful for data augmentation in low-resource classification settings.
