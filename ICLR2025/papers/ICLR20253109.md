## Problem

Current multimodal misinformation detection methods assume each sample has a single, known forgery source (text, image, or cross-modal inconsistency), which is overly simplistic for real-world scenarios where multiple forgery sources coexist. No benchmark or general-purpose detector exists for this mixed-source setting, leaving a critical gap between controlled single-source evaluations and the complexity of actual social media misinformation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253109/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consists of 11,000 image-text pairs (1,000 validation, 10,000 test) where each pair is labeled into one of four classes: real, textual veracity distortion, visual veracity distortion, or cross-modal consistency distortion, further subdivided into 12 forgery sub-categories. The MMD-Agent framework consumes a text-image pair and produces both a four-class misinformation source label and a structured sequence of multi-perspective reasoning traces (Thought/Observation pairs) that explain the detection decision. All evaluations are conducted zero-shot, with the model's free-form response parsed via regular expressions to extract the classification answer.

## Method

MMFakeBench is constructed by collaboratively leveraging diffusion generators (Stable Diffusion XL, DALL-E 3, Midjourney V6) and ChatGPT to synthesize realistic mixed-source misinformation across three distortion types: textual veracity distortion (natural, artificial, and GPT-generated rumors paired with AI-generated or repurposed supporting images), visual veracity distortion (PS-edited and AI-generated fact-conflicting images with veracious text), and cross-modal consistency distortion (repurposed and edited inconsistencies). The proposed MMD-Agent framework decomposes mixed-source detection into three sequential subtasks—textual veracity check, visual veracity check, and cross-modal consistency reasoning—where each subtask is solved through an interleaved sequence of reasoning and action. At each stage, the LVLM generates multi-perspective reasoning traces by integrating internal knowledge (identifying key entities, factual analysis, commonsense reasoning) with external knowledge retrieved via the Wikipedia API, producing a final decision d_t = M(R_t, O_t, a_t). This hierarchical decomposition with tool-augmented reasoning significantly outperforms standard prompting, raising LLaVA-1.6-34B's F1 from 25.7% to 49.9% and GPT-4V's from 51.0% to 61.6% on the multiclass task.

## Results

On the MMFakeBench test set (10,000 samples), GPT-4V with standard prompting achieves a macro-F1 of 48.8%, while the proposed MMD-Agent framework boosts it to 61.5%. Among open-source models, LLaVA-1.6-34B with MMD-Agent reaches 47.7% macro-F1, approaching GPT-4V's standard prompting score. The best 7B model (LLaVA-1.6-7B) achieves only 19.0% macro-F1, and human evaluators score 35.9% macro-F1 on the validation set, confirming the benchmark's difficulty. In binary classification, GPT-4V with MMD-Agent reaches 72.8% F1, while LLaVA-1.6-34B with MMD-Agent achieves 68.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMD-Agent consistently outperforms standard prompting across all evaluated models: GPT-4V improves from 48.8% to 61.5% macro-F1 (+12.7), LLaVA-1.6-34B from 25.4% to 47.7% (+22.3), and BLIP2-13B from 16.3% to 28.8% (+12.5). Against single-source detectors, LLaVA-1.6-34B with MMD-Agent (49.9% multiclass F1) substantially outperforms the best single-source detector HAMMER (43.0% binary) and the combined mixed-detection pipeline (22.5% multiclass F1). GPT-4V with MMD-Agent (61.6% multiclass F1) leads all baselines by a wide margin. Among open-source 7B models, LLaVA-1.6-7B (19.0% F1) is the strongest, but all 7B models remain far below the 34B model, indicating that smaller LVLMs lack sufficient multimodal understanding for mixed-source detection. The paper also shows that fine-tuning specialized detectors (HAMMER, FKA-Owl) on MMFakeBench improves their scores (e.g., HAMMER from 44.1% to 72.4%) but causes catastrophic forgetting on their original training data (DGM4 drops from 83.2% to 63.2%), whereas LVLMs with MMD-Agent generalize without such degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant single-source assumption in multimodal misinformation detection, where prior benchmarks (FEVER, Fakeddit, NewsClippings, DGM4, etc.) each isolate one forgery modality—textual, visual, or cross-modal—and evaluate detectors trained on that specific source. By constructing MMFakeBench with all three distortion types coexisting in a unified 12-subcategory taxonomy, the authors reframe misinformation detection as a mixed-source problem, drawing on generative models (Stable Diffusion XL, DALL-E 3, Midjourney) and LLMs (ChatGPT) to synthesize realistic, multi-source samples that no prior dataset covers. The proposed MMD-Agent framework extends the ReAct-style agent paradigm (reasoning + action + tool-use) to LVLMs, applying hierarchical task decomposition and external knowledge retrieval (Wikipedia API) in a way that has not been systematically explored for misinformation detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even GPT-4V achieves only ~51% macro-F1 on the 4-class mixed-source task, and a human study shows 62.1% error rate, confirming that mixed-source misinformation is a genuinely hard and underexplored problem. This opens several questions: whether more advanced RAG or multi-source knowledge retrieval can close the gap for natural and GPT-generated rumors, whether the catastrophic forgetting observed when fine-tuning specialized detectors limits their practical utility, and how the framework scales to additional forgery modalities (video, audio). As generative AI tools make multi-source misinformation increasingly accessible, a realistic benchmark of this kind is likely to become a standard reference for the field.
