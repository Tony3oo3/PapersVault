## Problem

Despite PPG's widespread use in clinical and consumer health monitoring, no open, general-purpose foundation model exists for PPG signals. Existing approaches are task-specific, trained on single-device proprietary datasets, and fail to demonstrate out-of-domain generalization, limiting reproducibility and broader adoption.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251729/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 10-second single-channel PPG segments that are bandpass-filtered (0.5–12 Hz), Z-score normalized, and resampled to 125 Hz (the lowest rate among pre-training sources). It produces a 512-dimensional embedding via a single FC projection layer, which is then used as a fixed feature for downstream linear probes (logistic regression for binary tasks, ridge regression for regression, random forest for multi-class). At inference, only the 5M-parameter encoder is used; the MoE expert heads and projection head are discarded.

## Method

PAPAGEI uses a ResNet-style 1D CNN backbone (18 convolutional blocks, 32→512 filters) with two self-supervised training objectives. PAPAGEI-P maximizes NT-Xent agreement between augmented segments from the same participant. PAPAGEI-S introduces a morphology-aware objective: it computes three PPG waveform metrics (sVRI, IPA, SQI) on augmented inputs, then trains a contrastive head on sVRI-discretized positive pairs (8 bins) alongside two Mixture-of-Experts heads (three parallel FCNNs each, softmax-weighted) that predict IPA and SQI via MAE loss. The key insight is that defining positive pairs by similar vascular morphology rather than by patient identity forces the encoder to capture physiologically meaningful waveform features (systolic peak shape, dicrotic notch timing) that transfer across devices and populations. The full objective is a weighted combination (α=0.6) of the sVRI contrastive loss and the IPA+SQI regression losses.

## Results

PAPAGEI-S (5M params) achieves the best average AUROC of 0.67 across 9 binary classification tasks and the lowest average MAE of 10.12 across 9 regression tasks, outperforming Chronos (200M; 0.62 AUROC, 11.60 MAE) and Moment (385M; 0.63 AUROC, 10.43 MAE) by 6.3% and 2.9% respectively. Notable task-level wins include ICU Admission (0.79 AUROC), Sleep Disordered Breathing (0.70 AUROC), and Apnea/Hypopnea Index >3% (12.97 MAE). The model is 70× smaller than Moment yet consistently outperforms it, and improves over the statistical-features baseline by 17.5% in average AUROC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against pre-trained foundation models, PAPAGEI-S (5M) outperforms Chronos (200M) and Moment (385M) on average AUROC (0.67 vs. 0.62/0.63) and average MAE (10.12 vs. 11.60/10.43), while being 40–77× smaller. PAPAGEI-S wins on 7 of 9 classification tasks (notably ICU Admission 0.79 vs. 0.73, SDB 0.70 vs. 0.58/0.45) and 7 of 9 regression tasks (AHI>3% 12.97 vs. 14.06/14.23; Diastolic BP-VV 8.29 vs. 10.53/10.53), but loses to Moment on Gestation Age (6.05 vs. 5.69) and Heart Rate (11.53 vs. 8.82). Against SSL baselines trained from scratch (Table 4), PAPAGEI-S beats TF-C (0.64 AUROC, 10.65 MAE), BYOL (0.62, 10.89), SimCLR (0.61, 10.79), and the statistical-features baseline (0.57, 11.60) on both average metrics. PAPAGEI-P is competitive with TF-C on classification (0.63 vs. 0.64) and beats it on regression (10.92 vs. 10.65 MAE is actually TF-C winning; PAPAGEI-P wins on Average HR 3.47 vs. 3.58). REGLE (0.07M, trained on UK Biobank) consistently underperforms all other methods, suggesting its extremely small size limits representational capacity despite large-scale PPG pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAPAGEI extends the patient-level contrastive SSL strategy demonstrated by Abbaspourazad et al. (2023) and Kiyasseh et al. (2021) to PPG signals at foundation-model scale, but departs from prior work in two key ways: it uses exclusively public datasets (whereas prior PPG SSL relied on proprietary Apple Watch data) and introduces a morphology-aware objective (PAPAGEI-S) that leverages domain-specific PPG metrics (sVRI, IPA, SQI) as supervision signals rather than relying solely on participant-level positive pairs. The architecture is a standard ResNet-style CNN following Ding et al. (2024), so the novelty lies in the training objective and the multi-task MoE heads rather than the backbone. The paper positions itself against both generic time-series FMs (Chronos, Moment) that lack physiological specificity and PPG-specific but closed models (REGLE), carving out the niche of an open, PPG-specific foundation model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first open foundation model for PPG, PAPAGEI provides a reproducible baseline and feature extractor that the community can build upon, addressing a critical gap where prior PPG SSL models were proprietary. The 5M-parameter efficiency (outperforming 200M+ generic FMs) makes it practical for on-device medical applications where large models are infeasible. However, the skin tone analysis reveals that no single model dominates across all skin tones, and the non-monotonic scaling (5M > 35M > 139M) suggests the current pre-training data volume may be the bottleneck rather than model capacity. Open questions include whether more diverse training data (different devices, populations, sampling rates) would unlock the benefits of larger architectures, and how PAPAGEI's embeddings integrate into multimodal health monitoring pipelines.
