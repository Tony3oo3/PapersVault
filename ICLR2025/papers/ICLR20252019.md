## Problem

Continual learning of neural fields (CL-NF) suffers from catastrophic forgetting and slow convergence when processing sequential data streams, while meta-learning approaches for neural fields (ML-NF) assume batch access to all data and cannot handle sequential, memory-constrained settings. No prior work has combined meta-learning with continual learning specifically for neural fields, leaving a gap for applications like drone/satellite capture where large-scale 3D environments must be learned rapidly from streaming, resource-limited data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes coordinate-based inputs that are partitioned into spatial or temporal sub-regions: a 180×180 image is split into four 180×45 patches, a video into four temporal segments, and a NeRF scene into four 3D spatial grids (with resolution reduced to 480×270 for city-scale scenes). Each sub-module is a coordinate-based MLP (5-layer with Sine activations for 2D/3D signals, 10-layer with ReLU for NeRF) that maps its local coordinates to field values. At inference, the final output is a weighted sum over all modules whose spatial regions contain the query coordinate, enabling seamless reconstruction across the full domain without a single monolithic network.

## Method

The approach combines a modular Mixture-of-Experts architecture with MAML-based optimization: each task (spatial/temporal region) receives its own sub-module initialized from a shared parameter set, and the outer meta-learning loop optimizes this shared initialization so that new modules can be instantiated and rapidly adapted with few gradient steps. To replace replay-based forgetting mitigation, the paper introduces a Fisher Information Maximization (FIM) loss that re-weights individual training samples by w(θ) = 1 + λ·g(θ)ᵀF(θ)⁻¹g(θ), where g is the score function and F is the Fisher Information Matrix, thereby prioritizing samples that cause large changes in important parameters. This sample-level weighting is shown to approximate mutual information maximization between model parameters and data via a local quadratic KL-divergence bound, and the paper proves convergence of FIM-SGD and a VC-dimension-based generalization bound. The modular design allows the number of sub-modules to grow dynamically with task count while sharing initializations, decoupling model capacity from the number of seen tasks.

## Results

On CelebA, the proposed method (Ours) achieves 45.40 PSNR at step 4096, surpassing the meta-offline upper bound MOL (45.28) and ER (43.90). On LibriSpeech1, Ours reaches 67.58 PSNR, far exceeding MOL (55.76) and MAML+CL (43.40). On the city-scale NeRF benchmark MatrixCity-B5, Ours (MIM) attains 32.80 PSNR at step 1024, outperforming MOL (30.57) and MAML+CL (25.79). Across all six datasets and four modalities (image, audio, video, NeRF), the method consistently achieves the highest or near-highest PSNR while using the least GPU memory (787 MiB vs. 968–1068 MiB for baselines).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the meta-offline upper bound MOL (LearnedInit, Tancik et al. 2021), Ours wins decisively on audio (67.58 vs. 55.76 PSNR on LibriSpeech1; 51.09 vs. 41.97 on LibriSpeech3) and video (47.80 vs. 41.75 on VoxCeleb2), and narrowly on CelebA (45.40 vs. 45.28) and FFHQ (35.89 vs. 35.21), while MOL retains a small edge on ImageNette (45.68 vs. 44.83). Against MAML+CL (Finn et al. 2017), Ours shows a dramatic advantage at later steps due to MAML+CL's catastrophic forgetting: on CelebA, MAML+CL drops to 28.06 PSNR at step 4096 versus Ours' 45.40, and on MatrixCity-B5 the gap is 32.80 vs. 25.79. Against OML (Javed & White 2019), Ours consistently outperforms, with OML degrading severely over steps (e.g., 23.06 vs. 45.40 on CelebA at step 4096; 21.59 vs. 47.80 on VoxCeleb2). Against ER (Rolnick et al. 2019), Ours wins on most datasets (e.g., 45.40 vs. 43.90 on CelebA; 67.58 vs. 49.83 on LibriSpeech1) and on MatrixCity-B5 (32.80 vs. 30.21), though ER requires storing and replaying all past data. Against EWC (Kirkpatrick et al. 2016) and MER (Riemer et al. 2019), Ours achieves substantially higher PSNR across all modalities (e.g., 45.40 vs. 20.08 for EWC and 21.97 for MER on CelebA), confirming that regularization-based and bi-level CL approaches are insufficient for the rapid-adaptation demands of neural fields.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established lines of work: continual learning of neural fields (CL-NF, e.g., CNM, MeIL-NeRF), meta-learning of neural fields (ML-NF, e.g., LearnedInit, MetaSDF), and meta-continual learning for classification (OML, ANML). It departs from prior CL-NF methods that rely on replay or knowledge distillation by adopting a modular architecture inspired by large-scale NeRF systems (MegaNeRF, SwitchNeRF), and from prior MCL methods by operating at the sample level via Fisher Information Maximization rather than the parameter level (EWC). The meta-learning loop is built directly on MAML's inner/outer optimization structure, while the FIM loss derivation relies on the KL-divergence-to-Fisher-Information theorem of Kunstner et al. (2019).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MCL-NF addresses a practical bottleneck in deploying neural fields on resource-constrained edge devices (drones, satellites) where replay-based continual learning incurs ever-growing storage costs and convergence is too slow for real-time rendering. The demonstrated city-scale NeRF reconstruction on MatrixCity with fewer parameters than monolithic approaches suggests a viable path toward scalable, incremental 3D scene modeling. Open questions remain around performance over very long, highly dissimilar task sequences, memory growth as the number of modules increases, and robustness to abrupt distribution shifts—limitations the authors acknowledge but do not fully resolve.
