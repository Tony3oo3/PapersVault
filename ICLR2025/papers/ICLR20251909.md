## Problem

Existing RL generalization methods assume that source and target domains share the same state and action space, differing only in distribution. In practice, however, environments can undergo structural changes—new state variables or actions may appear entirely (e.g., new enemies in higher-difficulty CoinRun levels). This paper addresses the broader challenge of generalizing RL agents across tasks where both distributional shifts and state/action space expansions occur, without knowing in advance which type of change is present.

## Input / Output

The method consumes high-dimensional RGB observations, discrete actions, and scalar rewards from POMDP environments (CartPole, CoinRun, Atari 100K), processed through convolutional encoders into latent representations. Beyond the standard policy action output, the system produces a set of latent causal state variables s_t, binary structural matrices D encoding which variables causally influence observations, rewards, and transitions, and a compact domain-specific change factor θ_i that parameterizes distributional differences. The policy network takes only the pruned compact state s_t^c and compact change factor θ_i^c as input, discarding irrelevant causal variables identified by the structural masks.

## Method

CSR augments a Dreamer-style RSSM world model with two key additions: (1) binary structural masks D that gate which latent causal variables feed into the observation, reward, and transition models, and (2) a task-specific change factor θ_i that captures distributional shifts in observations, rewards, and transitions. A three-step self-adaptive strategy governs adaptation to each new task: first, only θ_i is re-estimated while all other parameters are frozen, and a forward prediction error L_pred is compared against a threshold τ* (set to the source task's final prediction loss) to detect whether the change is purely distributional; second, if L_pred exceeds τ*, new causal variables are added to the state space via a Random, Deterministic, or Self-Adaptive (RL-based) expansion strategy that searches for the optimal number of variables; third, an L1 sparsity regularization J_reg on D and θ_i prunes irrelevant causal edges concurrently with training, yielding a minimal sufficient representation. The paper further provides identifiability guarantees (Theorems 1–3, Corollary 1) showing that the latent states, structural matrices, and change factors are component-wise identifiable under conditional independence, linear independence, and faithfulness assumptions.

## Results

On CartPole (Table 1), CSR achieves a perfect score of 500.0 on all four tasks (distribution shift, space expansion, and combined), while Dreamer drops to 311.6–397.6 and AdaRL to 407.5–468.0; CSR also requires the fewest adaptation steps (2k–10k) versus 50k for Dreamer/AdaRL on Task 1 and failure (X) on Tasks 3–4. On Atari 100K (Table 2), CSR attains the highest mean score across all five games, e.g., 1586.9 on Alien (vs. 1147.5 for AdaRL), 88306.5 on Crazy Climber (vs. 68026.5 for Dreamer), and 6718.6 on Gopher (vs. 5607.3 for Dreamer). On CoinRun, CSR consistently outperforms all baselines in learning curves when transferring from low to high difficulty levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSR outperforms all five baselines across every evaluated environment. Against Dreamer (Hafner et al., 2023), CSR gains the largest margins on space-expansion tasks: on CartPole Task 3 (friction introduced), CSR scores 500.0 vs. Dreamer's 311.6, and on Atari Crazy Climber CSR reaches 88306.5 vs. Dreamer's 68026.5 (+30%). Against AdaRL (Huang et al., 2021), which handles distribution shifts but not space expansions, CSR leads on all four CartPole tasks (500.0 vs. 407.5–468.0) and on Atari Alien (1586.9 vs. 1147.5, +38%). Against DQN (Mnih et al., 2015), the model-free baseline, CSR's advantage is most dramatic on CartPole (500.0 vs. 65.6–107.4) and DQN fails to adapt at all beyond Task 1. Against SPR (Schwarzer et al., 2020), CSR dominates on Atari, e.g., 1454.1 vs. 110.1 on Bank Heist and 6718.6 vs. 294.0 on Gopher. Against EfficientZero (Ye et al., 2021), the state-of-the-art model-based method, CSR wins on all five Atari games, with the largest gap on Gopher (6718.6 vs. 1083.2, +520%). Notably, CSR also requires the fewest adaptation steps (2k–10k) compared to 50k for Dreamer/AdaRL on Task 1, and is the only method that successfully adapts to Tasks 3 and 4 within limited training budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Dreamer world-model framework (RSSM-based) and the causal representation learning line (ASRs, AdaRL) that uses structural masks to identify task-relevant latent variables. The key departure is that prior transfer RL methods (AdaRL, CSP, reward shaping) assume a fixed state/action space across tasks, whereas CSR explicitly handles scenarios where the environment space itself expands with new causal variables. The identifiability theory extends Yao et al. (2021) and Kong et al. (2023) to accommodate a domain-specific change factor θ_i and newly added state dimensions, providing theoretical grounding for the self-adaptive mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying distribution-shift detection and state-space expansion under a single causal framework, CSR addresses a more realistic generalization setting than prior work, where new enemies or physics parameters can appear in previously unseen forms. The three-step strategy (detect via prediction error → expand via self-adaptive search → prune via edge-minimality) offers a practical, interpretable protocol for low-cost policy transfer. A key limitation is that the method only handles cross-domain generalization and does not account for nonstationary changes within a single task over time, which the authors identify as future work. The self-adaptive expansion, while effective, is computationally expensive due to the search over expansion sizes, limiting its practicality in time-constrained settings.
