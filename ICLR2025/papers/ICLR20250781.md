## Problem

Dynamic Workflow Scheduling (DWS) in cloud computing involves assigning tasks from unpredictably arriving workflows to heterogeneous machines, yet existing methods either assume homogeneous/static settings or rely on vector-based state representations that cannot capture the complex, evolving interactions between tasks and machines. GNN-based approaches further suffer from fixed graph structures and shared feature extractors that limit scalability to large, highly dynamic scheduling problems with real-time workflow arrivals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250781/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each decision step, the actor consumes a task-specific graph G^a(s_t^a, a_t) that is constructed separately for every candidate machine, encoding the focused task's topological connection to that machine's last assigned task plus real-time updated feature vectors (execution time, completion time, machine speed, utilization) specific to that machine. The critic consumes a single system-oriented graph G^c(s_t^c) containing all uncompleted tasks across all active workflows, with three edge types (task precedence, machine FIFO order, and eligible-action links) processed bi-directionally. The actor outputs a softmax probability distribution over |M| eligible machines; the critic outputs a scalar expected future return.

## Method

GOODRL introduces two distinct graph representations paired with separate GAT-based networks: a task-specific graph with a GAT actor that processes each (state, action) pair independently to extract a focused-task embedding (avoiding mean-pooling dilution), and a system-oriented graph with a GAT + self-attention critic that models long-range dependencies across all workflows including newly arrived ones. Training follows a two-stage offline-online scheme: offline, the actor is first pre-trained via imitation learning on HEFT-generated trajectories (preventing task accumulation from random initialization), then refined with standard PPO; online, an enhanced PPO variant applies gradient control (zeroing the policy gradient when its L2 norm exceeds a dynamically tracked threshold μ_prev + σ_prev or a hard cap τ_0) and decouples the critic into independent high-frequency updates (20 epochs vs. 5 for the actor per iteration) to provide stable value estimates during real-time scheduling on a single long trajectory.

## Results

GOODRL achieves an average rank of 1.17 across 12 offline scenarios (varying machine configurations, arrival rates, and workflow counts from 1k to 5k), consistently producing the lowest mean flowtime. Compared to the best expert-designed PDR (HEFT), GOODRL reduces mean flowtime by up to 289.98% (e.g., 277.65 vs. 391.61 in ⟨6×4, 5.4, 1k⟩). In online scenarios with up to 20k workflows, Ours-Online further improves over Ours-Offline by up to 1.24%, demonstrating effective real-time adaptation. ERL-DWS degrades severely at scale, reaching gaps as high as 1128.92% relative to GOODRL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three expert-designed PDRs (EST, PEFT, HEFT from Topcuoglu et al., 2002), GOODRL dominates across all 12 offline scenarios, with mean flowtime gaps ranging from 24.91% (vs. HEFT in ⟨5×5, 5.4, 1k⟩) to 289.98% (vs. EST in ⟨6×4, 5.4, 3k⟩). Against GPHH (Xu et al., 2023), GOODRL wins on 10 of 12 scenarios; GPHH edges out GOODRL by only 1.24% and 0.15% on two ⟨5×5⟩ scenarios, but GPHH exhibits extreme instability with gaps up to 39.49% against GOODRL on ⟨6×4⟩ scenarios and requires ~200 CPU hours of evolutionary search. Against ERL-DWS (Shen et al., 2024), GOODRL is substantially superior in every scenario, with ERL-DWS gaps reaching 1128.92% in ⟨6×4, 9, 5k⟩, and ERL-DWS performance degrades dramatically as workflow count increases while GOODRL remains stable. In online settings (Table 2), Ours-Online achieves the best rank (1.17) across all six scenarios, consistently outperforming Ours-Offline and all baselines, with the largest online improvement (1.24%) observed in ⟨6×4, 9, 20k⟩.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the actor-critic RL framework for scheduling (building on PPO and imitation learning) by introducing two distinct graph representations: a task-specific graph for the actor that processes (state, action) pairs individually, and a system-oriented graph for the critic that captures global workflow-machine interactions via bi-directional edges and self-attention. It departs from prior DRL scheduling work (e.g., Song et al. 2022, Zhu et al. 2024) that uses shared graph encoders for both actor and critic, and from GPHH-based hyper-heuristics (Xu et al. 2023) that rely on evolutionary search over hand-crafted dispatching rules. The offline-online training paradigm—combining HEFT-based imitation pre-training with an enhanced PPO featuring gradient control and decoupled high-frequency critic updates—is a novel contribution to the scheduling RL literature, addressing the gap between offline-trained agents and real-time deployment in dynamic cloud environments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GOODRL tackles DWS at scales (5k–20k workflows) far beyond what prior methods handle reliably, and demonstrates that the state-of-the-art GPHH approach becomes highly unstable at these scales, while the proposed method remains robust. The offline-online training scheme with gradient control and decoupled critic training offers a practical recipe for deploying RL scheduling agents in production cloud systems where the environment changes continuously. Open questions remain around handling unlimited machine configurations, explicit constraint satisfaction, and multi-objective optimization (e.g., cost-flowtime trade-offs), which the authors identify as future directions. The techniques—separate actor/critic graph representations, pairwise action evaluation, and decoupled high-frequency critic updates—could transfer to other dynamic combinatorial optimization problems beyond workflow scheduling.
