## Problem

Existing long-context benchmarks have average token lengths of only ~60K, far below the 200K+ context windows of current LLMs, leaving a critical evaluation gap. No prior benchmark combines manually crafted questions with golden answers and textual evidences on documents exceeding 200K tokens, making it impossible to rigorously assess whether models truly comprehend extremely long narratives or merely pattern-match on shorter inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250843/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs the full text of an English novel (all exceeding 50K words, with average context windows over 200K tokens) with a question; in the multichoice variant, four answer options (one golden, three GPT-4-generated distractors) are appended. The model must produce either a short free-form answer (1–several words) or a single choice index. Questions are stratified by complexity (multi-hop, single-hop, detail) and aspect (times, meaning, span, setting, relation, character, plot), and each carries annotated textual evidences from the novel for ground-truth verification.

## Method

NovelQA is constructed from 89 English novels (65 public-domain from Project Gutenberg, 24 copyrighted), with 2,305 QA tuples annotated by English Literature experts through a two-phase process: a template-based phase using 10+ pre-designed question templates targeting multi-hop and detail information, followed by a free-form phase for natural question diversity. Each tuple includes a golden answer, four multichoice options, and textual evidences (original excerpts or annotator-written reasoning steps), with quality control via triple manual review (retaining 79.4% of collected data) and 94.6% Cohen's Kappa inter-annotator agreement. Evaluation uses zero-shot prompting with a uniform 'literature professor' persona across all models, and generative answers are scored by GPT-4-as-judge (validated at 89.25% Cohen's Kappa against human evaluators). The benchmark is tested on commercial (GPT-4-128K, Claude-2.1/3/3.5-200K) and open-source (InternLM2-Chat-7b/20b, Llama-3.1-8b/70b) models, revealing that accuracy drops sharply when evidence lies beyond the 100K-token mark and that multi-hop, meaning, and detail questions remain the hardest categories even for the best model (Claude-3.5-Sonnet at 62.30% generative).

## Results

On NovelQA, the top-performing model Claude-3.5-Sonnet achieves 77.92% accuracy in the multichoice setting and 62.30% in the generative setting, while the best open-source model Llama-3.1-70B reaches 69.39% and 51.50% respectively. All models fall substantially short of the human baseline (97.00% multichoice, 90.00% generative), with a gap of 28–35 points in the generative setting. Performance drops sharply for evidence located beyond the 100K-token mark: the weighted average accuracy falls from 46.84% (pre-100K) to 25.36% (post-100K) in the generative setting, and from 66.24% to 48.08% in multichoice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among commercial models, Claude-3.5-Sonnet leads both settings (77.92% multichoice, 62.30% generative), outperforming GPT-4o-mini (71.85%/53.32%) by 6.07 and 8.98 points respectively, and GPT-4 (71.80%/46.88%) by 6.12 and 15.42 points. Claude-2.1 trails its successors, scoring 66.84%/46.04%, roughly 11 points below Claude-3.5 in multichoice. Among open-source models, Llama-3.1-70B (69.39%/51.50%) substantially outperforms InternLM2-Chat-20b (49.18%/32.37%) by 20.21 and 19.13 points, while Llama-3.1-8B (62.31%/42.65%) still exceeds InternLM2-Chat-7b (43.51%/30.90%) by 18.80 and 11.75 points. All open-source models lag behind the best commercial model by 8–34 points, with the gap widening in the generative setting. Notably, even models with 200K context windows (Claude-2.1, InternLM2) show degraded performance on evidence beyond 100K tokens, suggesting that nominal context window size does not guarantee effective long-range comprehension.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the long-context QA evaluation lineage (NarrativeQA, LongBench, ZeroSCROLLS, LooGLE, L-Eval, BAMBOO) by pushing context lengths to an average exceeding 200K tokens, far beyond the ~60K maximum in prior benchmarks. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) all questions, golden answers, and evidences are manually crafted by English Literature experts rather than AI-generated or crowd-sourced, and (2) the source material consists of full novels with narrative coherence from start to end, rather than synthetic or fragmented documents. The paper does not propose a new modeling method but rather a new evaluation instrument calibrated to the current generation of 200K+ context window LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NovelQA provides the first rigorous, human-annotated benchmark for the 200K+ token regime, revealing that even top commercial models (Claude-3.5-Sonnet at 62.30% generative) fall far short of human performance (90%), with particularly severe degradation on multi-hop reasoning and detail-oriented questions. The finding that performance drops sharply beyond the 100K-token mark—diverging from the previously documented lost-in-middle pattern—suggests a distinct failure mode for extremely long contexts that current architectures do not handle gracefully. Limitations include English-only coverage, novel-genre restriction, and truncation of content exceeding model context windows. Open questions include whether the 100K cliff is architectural or a data-coverage artifact, and how to design models that maintain coherent comprehension across truly novel-length inputs.
