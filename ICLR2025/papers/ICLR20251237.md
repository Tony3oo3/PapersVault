## Problem

Existing time series foundation models are limited in scale (typically under 700M parameters) and rely on dense architectures where every input token activates all parameters, making further scaling prohibitively expensive. Sparse mixture-of-experts designs that decouple model capacity from per-token computation have not been explored for time series forecasting, and current FMs also suffer from rigid context lengths, fixed horizons, or hardcoded heuristics that limit their universality.

## Input / Output

The model accepts univariate time series of arbitrary length (up to 4096 tokens) via point-wise tokenization, with multivariate inputs handled through channel independence. At inference, a greedy scheduling algorithm combines outputs from four fixed-horizon projection heads ({1, 8, 32, 64}) to produce forecasts of any target length H, enabling flexible multi-horizon prediction without retraining.

## Method

TIME-MOE is a decoder-only transformer that replaces each FFN layer with a sparse mixture-of-experts module containing 8 experts (7 isolated + 1 shared), where top-2 routing activates only a subset per token, scaling total parameters to 2.4B while keeping activated parameters at ~1.1B. Inputs are embedded via SwiGLU point-wise projections, processed through stacked blocks of RMSNorm, causal multi-head self-attention with rotary positional embeddings, and the MoE layer. Training uses a composite loss combining Huber loss across four multi-resolution forecasting heads (horizons 1, 8, 32, 64) with an auxiliary load-balancing term to prevent expert routing collapse. Pre-trained on Time-300B (309B time points across 9 domains) for 100K steps with sequence packing and BF16 precision, the model achieves 78% lower training cost and 39% lower inference cost compared to dense equivalents while consistently outperforming them in both zero-shot and fine-tuned settings.

## Results

In zero-shot forecasting across six benchmarks (ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm1, ETTm2, Weather, Global Temp), TIME-MOE_base and TIME-MOE_large achieve an average MSE of 0.336 and MAE of 0.384/0.380, outperforming the strongest baselines Moirai_base (MSE 0.347), Chronos_large (MSE 0.429), and Moment (MSE 0.461) by 23–30% in average MSE. In in-distribution (one-epoch fine-tuning) forecasting, TIME-MOE_base achieves an average MSE of 0.306, surpassing the best dense baselines TimeMixer (0.337) and iTransformer (0.349) by roughly 9–12%. The paper reports an overall 20% (zero-shot) and 24% (in-distribution) average MSE reduction over the most competitive baselines, and demonstrates that scaling from 50M to 1.1B activated parameters (2.4B total) yields consistent gains, validating scaling laws for time series.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIME-MOE outperforms all zero-shot foundation model baselines on average: it beats Moirai_large (311M params) by 11% in MSE, Chronos_large (710M params) by 23%, and Moment (385M params) by 30%, despite having fewer total parameters than Chronos and Moment. In in-distribution forecasting, TIME-MOE_base (50M activated) surpasses every dense baseline including TimeMixer (MSE 0.337), iTransformer (0.349), TimesNet (0.356), DLinear (0.316), and FEDformer (0.375), with the largest margins over Crossformer (0.560) and TiDE (0.421). The paper also shows that replacing MoE with an equivalent dense FFN increases training cost by 78% and inference cost by 39% while yielding worse zero-shot MSE, confirming the efficiency advantage of the sparse design. Among the TIME-MOE variants, the base and large models achieve the lowest average MSE (0.336) in zero-shot, while the ultra model achieves the best MAE (0.322), suggesting the larger model is more robust to outliers but occasionally produces larger individual errors. No baseline is reported to outperform TIME-MOE on any of the six benchmarks in either the zero-shot or in-distribution setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

TIME-MOE extends the decoder-only transformer paradigm (Vaswani 2017) with sparse mixture-of-experts routing inherited from Switch Transformers (Fedus 2022) and GShard (Lepikhin 2020), adapting these NLP techniques to time series for the first time at billion-parameter scale. It departs from prior time series foundation models (Moirai, TimesFM, Moment, Chronos, Timer) in three key ways: replacing dense FFNs with sparsely-activated expert pools, using point-wise tokenization instead of patch-based tokenization, and introducing a multi-resolution forecasting head with greedy scheduling to support arbitrary horizons. The Time-300B dataset (309B time points across 9 domains) substantially exceeds prior pre-training corpora (Moirai: 27B, TimesFM: 100B, Chronos: 84B) and includes a dedicated data-cleaning pipeline addressing missing values and invalid observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work validates that scaling laws transfer to time series forecasting and demonstrates that sparse MoE architectures can scale time series FMs to 2.4B parameters while reducing training costs by 78% and inference costs by 39% relative to dense equivalents — a practical efficiency gain likely to influence future time series model design. The open-sourcing of both the models and Time-300B provides a significant resource for the community. Open questions include whether the 90.5% dominance of the Nature domain in training data biases generalization, whether scaling continues to yield gains beyond 2.4B parameters, and whether the MoE design extends to other time series tasks (anomaly detection, classification, imputation) beyond point forecasting.
