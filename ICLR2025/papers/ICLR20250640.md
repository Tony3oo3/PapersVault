## Problem

Contrastive Learning (CL) for sequential recommendation incurs high computational costs from negative sample generation and is sensitive to ad-hoc data augmentations that may introduce preference inconsistency in positive samples. Non-Contrastive Learning (NCL), which avoids negative samples entirely, has not been explored for sequential recommendation, and the sparsity of recommendation data makes NCL particularly vulnerable to representation collapse without carefully designed positive samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250640/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes user interaction sequences as item textual descriptions (titles, categories) rather than discrete item IDs, encoded via the E5 (e5-base-v2) text encoder. During training, the DP augmentation perturbs each sequence by replacing up to 3 randomly selected items with their top-k cosine-similar synonym items, producing an expected augmented profile as a weighted sum of candidate embeddings. The model outputs a softmax probability distribution over the full item vocabulary for ranking, with evaluation via Recall@N and NDCG@N (N∈{10,20}).

## Method

NCL-SR introduces a preference-preserving augmentation built on the Differential Privacy exponential mechanism: for each item in a user's history, synonym candidates are identified via top-k cosine similarity in embedding space, and the exponential mechanism selects replacements with probability proportional to e^(ε·u/2Δu), providing a provable guarantee (Theorem 1) that the augmented profile retains the same top-1 recommendation. To avoid O(k^l) combinatorial explosion, the mechanism is applied independently per item, reducing complexity to O(k·l). Training uses Matrix Cross Entropy (MCE) to define two NCL losses: a uniformity loss that aligns the centered covariance matrix of representations with the identity matrix, and an alignment loss that enforces similarity between original and augmented representations while regularizing their individual covariance structures. The total objective combines the standard ranking task loss with weighted uniformity and alignment terms, eliminating all negative samples from the training pipeline.

## Results

NCL-SR achieves an average improvement of 11.93% over the best baseline (RecFmr.) across all six datasets on Recall@10, with the largest gain of 33.2% on the sparsest dataset (Sports). Against CL-based methods, NCL-SR yields an average 12.48% improvement, reaching 31.7% on NDCG@10 for Sports and 24.0% on NDCG@10 for Auto. NCL-SR also shows stronger retrieval (Recall) gains than ranking (NDCG) gains, with 16.7% average Recall@10 improvement versus 8.78% NDCG@10 improvement, reflecting the semantic matching strength of the text-based E5 backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NCL-SR wins on every metric across all six datasets against all ten baselines. Among ID-based models, LRURec is the strongest (e.g., 0.1220 R@10 on Auto), yet NCL-SR still surpasses it by 11.0% on Auto R@10 and by 33.2% on Sports R@10. Among text-based baselines, RecFmr. is the closest competitor (e.g., 0.1085 R@10 on Auto, 0.0729 on Beauty), and NCL-SR outperforms it by 11.0% and 8.5% respectively. Against CL-based methods (Table 2), NCL-SR leads by 12.48% on average; the gap is largest on Sports (30.9–31.7% across metrics) and Auto (15.6–24.0%), while smallest on Toys (2.96–5.09%), suggesting CL-based methods are more competitive when data is less sparse. Notably, NCL-SR's advantage over CL-based methods (CoSeRec, DUORec, SCL, CLS4Rec, EC4Rec) is consistent but more moderate than over ID-based baselines, indicating that the primary benefit of NCL-SR is eliminating negative-sample overhead while matching or exceeding CL performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: non-contrastive self-supervised learning (BYOL, SimSiam, Barlow Twins, Matrix Information Theory) and sequential recommendation (CLS4Rec, CoSeRec, DUORec). It departs from the dominant contrastive learning paradigm in SR by eliminating negative samples entirely, and from prior NCL work (which was confined to CV and NLP) by being the first to apply NCL to sequential recommendation. The key conceptual novelty is repurposing the Differential Privacy exponential mechanism—not for privacy, but as a principled, provably preference-preserving data augmentation strategy—replacing the ad-hoc random or heuristic augmentations (random crop, swap, mask, correlation-based substitution) used in existing CL-based SR methods. The non-contrastive losses (uniformity and alignment) are drawn directly from Matrix Cross Entropy (Zhang et al., 2023), which generalizes SimSiam, Barlow Twins, and MEC into a unified framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the need for negative samples, NCL-SR addresses a practical bottleneck in CL-based recommenders (large batch sizes, hard-negative retrieval) while maintaining competitive or superior performance, making it attractive for resource-constrained deployment. The finding that alignment is more critical than uniformity in data-sparse settings offers a useful diagnostic for practitioners tuning self-supervised recommenders. However, the DP guarantee is conditional on a score-gap assumption (Theorem 1), the exponential mechanism is applied at item-level rather than user-level for tractability (weakening the theoretical guarantee), and the entire pipeline depends on a fixed E5 text encoder for synonym retrieval—raising open questions about scalability to very large item catalogs and sensitivity to the synonym set size k. Whether NCL can consistently outperform well-tuned CL methods in data-rich (non-sparse) settings remains to be established.
