## Problem

Traditional boosting algorithms require fully observed responses, but interval-censored data—where event times are known only to fall within observed intervals—are prevalent in survival analysis and reliability engineering. While boosting has been extended to right-censored and missing data, no principled boosting framework existed for interval-censored data, where the response is completely unobserved yet constrained to a known interval.

## Input / Output

The method takes p-dimensional feature vectors X and interval-censored observations consisting of pairs (L_i, R_i] with associated censoring indicators Δ_{i,j} for each monitoring time. The output is a function f(X) that predicts a user-defined transformation g(Y): the raw survival time (g(Y)=Y), log-survival time (g(Y)=log Y), or binary survival status at a prespecified threshold s (g(Y)=2I(Y>s)−1). For classification, the output is constrained to [−1,1] via a sign-and-clip operation, and the predicted survival probability is recovered as (f(X)+1)/2.

## Method

The paper proposes two boosting algorithms—L2Boost-CUT and L2Boost-IMP—that adapt L2 Boost to interval-censored data via the censoring unbiased transformation (CUT). L2Boost-CUT replaces the unobserved {g(Y_i)}^k terms in the L2 loss with conditional expectations E[{g(Y_i)}^k | Δ_{i,j}=1, X_i] computed from the conditional survivor function S(y|X_i), yielding an adjusted loss whose expectation equals the original risk. L2Boost-IMP instead imputes the transformed response Ỹ_1(O_i) and plugs it into the standard L2 loss. Both methods use functional gradient descent with smoothing splines as base learners, estimating S(y|X_i) via interval-censored recursive forests (ICRF). The key theoretical result is that with smoothing spline base learners, both methods achieve minimax-optimal MSE rates O(n^{−2v/(2v+1)}) for v-th order smooth functions, and the boosting operator takes the closed form B^(t) = I − (I−Ψ)^{t+1} where Ψ is the smoother matrix.

## Results

On synthetic data (n=500, p=1, m=3 monitoring times, τ=6), the proposed L2Boost-CUT and L2Boost-IMP methods produce SMaxAE, SMSqE, and SKDT values that are fairly comparable to the Reference (R) method using complete data, while the Naive (N) method (midpoint imputation) yields the largest SMaxAE and SMSqE and the smallest SKDT across both lognormal and loglogistic AFT models. For classification (predicting survival status at time s), CUT and IMP produce identical sensitivity and specificity curves, with the N method showing similar specificity but significantly lower sensitivity. On the real-world Signal Tandmobiel and Bangkok HIV datasets, CUT and IMP yield comparable survival time estimates, while the N method produces systematically smaller estimates and the COX-based procedure appears closer to the proposed methods than to N.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares L2Boost-CUT and L2Boost-IMP against three baselines: the Naive (N) method (midpoint of the censoring interval as surrogate response), the Reference (R) method (complete uncensored data, serving as an upper bound), and the Oracle (O) method (true φ(Xi) values, a theoretical upper bound). On synthetic regression tasks, both CUT and IMP substantially outperform N in SMaxAE, SMSqE, and SKDT, with performance approaching that of R, which itself is bounded above by O. For classification, CUT and IMP match each other exactly and clearly dominate N on sensitivity (while specificity is similar), indicating that the censoring-unbiased transformation meaningfully recovers discriminative information lost by naive midpoint imputation. On the two real-world datasets, CUT and IMP produce nearly identical survival time estimates that are larger than N's, and the COX model-based procedure (not directly comparable) also appears closer to the proposed methods than to N, suggesting the naive approach systematically underestimates survival times. The paper does not report a single numerical comparison table; results are presented as boxplots over 300 replications, so exact per-method metric values are not tabulated.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the L2 Boost framework of Bühlmann & Yu (2003) to handle interval-censored data by incorporating the censoring unbiased transformation (CUT) of Fan & Gijbels (1994) into the loss function, and using interval censored recursive forests (ICRF, Cho et al., 2022) for conditional survivor function estimation. Prior boosting extensions addressed right-censored or missing data, but interval censoring—where the event time is known only to fall within an interval—remained unaddressed. The paper also builds on Yang et al. (2024), who applied CUT to tree-based methods for interval-censored data but did not leverage the iterative refinement and theoretical structure of boosting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a concrete gap in the boosting literature by providing the first boosting methods with rigorous minimax-optimality guarantees for interval-censored data, a setting common in medical and reliability studies where exact event times are unobserved. A key limitation is the dependence on ICRF for survivor function estimation, which adds computational overhead (O(n^γ) with γ up to 2) and introduces an additional source of estimation error. Open questions include extending the framework to Lq losses (q≥3) and general loss functions, where the elegant boosting operator structure breaks down, and adapting the approach to deep learning or ensemble frameworks beyond smoothing splines.
