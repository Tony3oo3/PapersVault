## Problem

3D diffusion models for molecule generation produce high-quality continuous conformers but occasionally generate chemically invalid molecules, while 1D SELFIES-based language models guarantee 100% validity yet cannot produce 3D coordinates. Prior work has not thoroughly explored combining these complementary strengths into a unified 3D molecule generation pipeline, leaving a gap in both validity and conformer accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250916/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For de novo generation, the system takes no explicit input and produces a complete 3D molecule (2D graph + 3D coordinates) via a two-step process: MoLlama autoregressively generates a SELFIES token sequence, then DMT denoises Gaussian noise into 3D atomic coordinates conditioned on the atom and bond features. For conditional generation, desired quantum chemical property values (e.g., HOMO-LUMO gap, dipole moment) are encoded as soft-prompt embeddings prepended to the SELFIES sequence and fed into DMT's adaLN conditioning. For conformer prediction, the input is a fixed 2D molecular graph (atom types + bond matrix) and the output is an ensemble of 3D conformers sampled via ancestral sampling over 100 diffusion steps.

## Method

NExT-Mol is a two-step foundation model: (1) MoLlama, a 960M-parameter Llama-2 decoder-only LM pretrained from scratch on 1.8B SELFIES sequences (90B tokens) from ZINC-15, generates 1D molecules with guaranteed validity; (2) DMT, a 55M/150M-parameter diffusion model, predicts 3D conformers by denoising continuous-time Gaussian perturbations on atomic coordinates using a noise-prediction MSE objective. DMT's key architectural innovation is Relational Multi-Head Self-Attention (RMHA), which maintains full 2D graph information through explicit pair representations E ∈ R^{N×N×d} that modulate atom-level queries and values via element-wise multiplication, avoiding the lossy top-k Laplacian eigenvector encoding used by MCF. A cross-modal projector (single-layer bidirectional self-attention + MLP) bridges MoLlama's causal SELFIES token representations to DMT's atom representations, with a three-stage training strategy (pretrain DMT → warmup projector with frozen DMT → joint fine-tuning) and LoRA on MoLlama to manage the 1D-to-3D modality gap. Randomized SELFIES augmentation during fine-tuning improves sample diversity by exploiting the multiple valid traversals of a molecular graph.

## Results

On GEOM-DRUGS for de novo 3D molecule generation, NExT-Mol achieves a 2D FCD of 0.334 (49% relative improvement over the best baseline MolGen at 0.655) and a 3D FCD of 14.69 (26% relative improvement over JODO at 19.99), while maintaining 100% atom stability and 99.9% molecule stability. On QM9-2014, NExT-Mol achieves the best 13 out of 14 metrics, with a 2D FCD of 0.070 and 3D FCD of 0.879. For conditional 3D generation on QM9-2014, NExT-Mol achieves a 13% average relative gain in MAE over JODO across six quantum chemical properties. For 3D conformer prediction on GEOM-DRUGS, DMT-B (55M params) achieves 85.4% COV-R, surpassing MCF-B (64M params) at 84.0% and Torsional Diffusion with Particle Guidance at 77.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NExT-Mol decisively outperforms all 3D diffusion baselines (JODO, MiDi, EQGAT-diff, EDM, CDGS) on both 2D and 3D metrics for de novo generation on GEOM-DRUGS, with the 2D FCD gap being particularly large (0.334 vs. 2.523 for JODO and 22.051 for CDGS). Among 1D LM-based baselines, NExT-Mol (via MoLlama) improves 2D FCD from 0.655 (MolGen) to 0.334 and from 0.888 (MolGPT) to 0.334, while maintaining 100% validity that MolGPT fails to achieve (95.7% V&C). For 3D conformer prediction, DMT-B (55M) surpasses the larger MCF-B (64M) by 1.4% COV-R and DMT-L (150M) beats MCF-L (242M) by 1.1% COV-R, demonstrating that DMT's RMHA architecture better leverages 2D graph information than MCF's lossy Laplacian eigenvector representation. On conditional generation, NExT-Mol outperforms JODO (the strongest baseline) by 13% average relative MAE reduction across all six quantum properties, with the largest gain on HOMO-LUMO gap (11.3%). The only metric where NExT-Mol does not lead is novelty (V&U&N) on QM9-2014, where CDGS scores 0.860 vs. NExT-Mol's 0.802, though CDGS significantly underperforms on all other metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper decomposes 3D molecule generation into a two-step pipeline: a large autoregressive SELFIES-based LM (MoLlama, built on Llama-2) for 1D molecule generation, followed by a diffusion model (DMT) for 3D conformer prediction. The DMT architecture builds on MCF's 'bitter lesson' philosophy of scalable transformers over equivariant inductive biases, but addresses MCF's lossy graph representation by incorporating Relational Multi-Head Self-Attention (RMHA) to retain full 2D bond information. The 1D-to-3D transfer learning component, which injects MoLlama's pretrained representations into DMT via a cross-modal projector, draws on the protein structure prediction literature (ESMFold, RoseTTAFold) but is, to the authors' knowledge, the first application of this paradigm to small molecules. The cross-modal projector design follows MolCA (Liu et al., 2023a), adapted here for the SELFIES-to-atom mapping challenge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that combining a validity-guaranteeing 1D LM with a 3D diffusion model can substantially outperform end-to-end 3D diffusion baselines, addressing the persistent invalid-molecule problem in 3D generation. The finding that 1D-to-3D transfer learning improves conformer prediction (especially for unseen scaffolds) opens a path to leveraging the vastly larger 1D molecular corpora (54.9B in ZINC22 vs. 37M in GEOM) to mitigate 3D data scarcity. Key limitations include the O(N²) memory cost of DMT's pair representations, the computational overhead of incorporating the 960M-parameter MoLlama, and the lack of exploration into advanced diffusion guidance methods. Future work on structure-based generation, bidirectional molecular LMs, and scaling to larger molecules remains open.
