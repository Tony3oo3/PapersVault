## Problem

Existing methods for estimating heterogeneous treatment effects over time are almost entirely model-based, proposing specific architectures and losses tied to particular adjustment mechanisms, while model-agnostic meta-learners remain largely unexplored for the time-varying setting. Doubly robust adjustment, which offers favorable theoretical properties in the static setting, has been overlooked for time-varying HTEs, and the resulting DR-learner suffers from high variance due to products of inverse-propensity scores that grow with the prediction horizon.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253088/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes patient trajectories consisting of time-varying covariates, discrete treatments, and continuous outcomes observed over T periods, along with a specified treatment intervention sequence of length τ+1. It outputs CAPO and CATE as functions of the history at time t, predicting τ-step-ahead potential outcomes. A key design choice is that all nuisance functions (response functions, propensity scores) and second-stage regressions are estimated by arbitrary ML models, making the framework model-agnostic; in experiments, encoder-only transformers with causal masks are used as the backbone.

## Method

The paper proposes a family of meta-learners (PI-HA, PI-RA, RA, IPW, DR, IVW-DR) that leverage different time-varying adjustment mechanisms—history adjustment, G-computation, inverse-propensity weighting, and doubly robust adjustment—via pseudo-outcomes constructed from estimated nuisance functions. The central novelty is the IVW-DR-learner, which minimizes a loss weighted by inverse-variance weights derived in Theorem 2; these weights take the form of sums of products of squared inverse-propensities and downweight trajectories with extreme propensity products, reducing estimation variance under low overlap. The DR-learner's pseudo-outcomes are estimators of the efficient influence function, yielding Neyman-orthogonality and double robustness. Theorem 1 provides point-wise risk bounds showing that the DR-learner converges at a doubly robust rate (fast if either propensity scores or response functions are well-estimated at each time step), while the PI-HA-learner remains asymptotically biased. In experiments using transformer instantiations on simulated and MIMIC III data, the IVW-DR-learner achieves the best RMSE, particularly for long horizons and low-overlap regimes where unweighted IPW and DR learners become unstable.

## Results

On simulated dataset D1, the IVW-DR-learner achieves the lowest RMSE across all prediction horizons (τ=0 to 4), with values of 0.57, 1.29, 2.40, 2.36, and 1.28 (×10), while the DR-learner becomes highly unstable at τ≥2 (RMSE 14.60, 6.82, 5.70). On D2, IVW-DR-learner similarly leads with RMSE 0.11–0.80 (×10) versus DR-learner's 0.14–2.69. On the real-world MIMIC III dataset (n=3000, T≤10), IVW-DR-learner achieves factual RMSE of 0.39–0.71 (×10) across τ=0–3, outperforming all other learners, while IPW and DR-learners degrade sharply at τ=3 (2.03 and 1.00 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The IVW-DR-learner consistently outperforms the standard DR-learner, with the gap widening dramatically at longer horizons: on D1 at τ=2, IVW-DR achieves RMSE 2.40 versus DR's 14.60 (a ~6× improvement), and at τ=4, 1.28 versus 5.70. The IPW-learner suffers the same instability as DR at τ≥2 on D1 (RMSE 14.77 vs. 5.03 at τ=4), confirming that the variance issue stems from products of inverse-propensity scores rather than the doubly robust correction itself. The RA-learner is the strongest non-DR baseline, achieving RMSE 2.13–2.29 at τ=2–3 on D1, but IVW-DR still edges it out (2.40, 2.36) with much lower variance (±0.43, ±1.12 vs. ±0.83, ±0.80). On D2, where the treatment assignment is simpler, the IPW-learner performs competitively at small τ (0.20 at τ=0) but degrades at τ=3–4 (1.46, 2.62), while IVW-DR remains stable (0.80, 0.50). On MIMIC III, IVW-DR-learner achieves the best factual RMSE at every horizon (0.39–0.71), with the advantage over DR-learner growing from 0.13 at τ=0 to 0.29 at τ=3.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the static-setting meta-learner framework (DR-learner from Kennedy 2023, IVW-DR-learner from Fisher 2024) to the time-varying setting, where adjustment must account for post-treatment confounders via G-computation (van der Laan & Gruber 2012) or products of propensity scores (Robins et al. 2000). It fills a notable gap: prior work on time-varying HTEs was almost entirely model-based (CRN, Causal Transformer, G-Net, RMSNs), and the only model-agnostic learner was the R-learner (Lewis & Syrgkanis 2021), which imposes parametric SNMM assumptions. The paper further unifies the landscape by showing that several existing model-based learners are specific instantiations of the proposed meta-learners, while doubly robust adjustments had been entirely overlooked in the time-varying literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IVW-DR-learner addresses a practically critical problem: in time-varying settings, products of inverse-propensity scores cause severe variance inflation for long horizons and low overlap, rendering standard DR- and IPW-learners unstable. The theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) provides actionable guidance on which learner to prefer based on the relative complexity of response functions versus propensity scores. Limitations include the restriction to discrete time, the lack of architecture-specific optimization (transformers used only as proof-of-concept), and the inherent difficulty of evaluating causal models on real-world data. Open directions include extending to continuous time and developing task-specific backbone architectures.
