## Problem

Speculative inference (SI) accelerates language model generation by drafting tokens with a faster model and verifying them with the target, but it is fundamentally sequential: each verification must complete before the next draft begins. This means SI can be slower than standard autoregressive inference when drafters are too slow or inaccurate, a limitation unaddressed in prior work. The paper asks whether SI can be parallelized to eliminate this failure mode entirely.

## Input / Output

DSI takes a text prompt, a frozen target LM (e.g., StarCoder-15B, Phi3-14B, Vicuna-13B), and one or more frozen drafter LMs that are faster than the target, and produces N generated tokens drawn from the target model's distribution (lossless in expectation). The method requires at least two processors (GPUs) and a lookahead hyperparameter that must satisfy a latency-ratio inequality to ensure verification tasks never queue. Each server maintains its own KV cache, and the token tree is managed via SpecInfer-style shared-prefix caching.

## Method

DSI introduces speculation parallelism (SP), a new form of task parallelism that overlaps target verification with drafter computation by running multiple concurrent instances of both models. Algorithm 1 initiates m threads (one per model) simultaneously; when any thread finishes, it spawns m new threads, and the current verifier thread (always the target model) triggers synchronization by terminating threads that produced rejected tokens and relabeling the next verifier. The lookahead hyperparameter batches multiple draft tokens per verification task, allowing DSI to be deployed on any fixed number of GPUs (≥2) by tuning lookahead to the target-to-drafter latency ratio. The paper proves DSI is always at least as fast as non-SI and strictly faster than both SI and non-SI in expectation for any drafter, with empirical speedups of 1.29–1.92× over SI across off-the-shelf model pairs on code, summarization, and instruction-following tasks.

## Results

DSI achieves 1.29x–1.92x speedup over speculative inference (SI) across all tested target/drafter pairs and datasets in single-node, multi-GPU setups. The largest speedup (1.92x) is observed for StarCoder-15B with StarCoder-168M drafter on HumanEval (93% acceptance rate, 32.3% drafter latency), while the smallest (1.29x) is for Vicuna-7B with Vicuna-68M on CNN-DM (67% acceptance rate, 8.4% drafter latency). Offline simulations (Figure 2) confirm that DSI is faster than both SI and non-SI for all configurations of non-zero acceptance rate, with up to 1.6x speedup over the faster of SI or non-SI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSI is compared against two baselines: standard speculative inference (SI, as in Leviathan et al. 2023 and Chen et al. 2023) and non-speculative autoregressive inference (non-SI). DSI wins against SI in every configuration tested, achieving 1.29x–1.92x speedup (Table 2), with the advantage growing as drafter latency increases relative to the target. Against non-SI, DSI is provably at least as fast (Theorem 1) and strictly faster in expectation (Theorem 2); the offline simulation (Figure 2) confirms DSI is faster than non-SI for all configurations where non-SI itself is faster than SI. Notably, DSI also outperforms non-SI in configurations where SI actually *slows down* inference (i.e., when the drafter is too slow or inaccurate), a regime where SI fails entirely. The paper also contrasts DSI with PEARL (Liu et al. 2025), noting that PEARL remains a sequential algorithm with no theoretical guarantees and is slower than non-SI for slow/inaccurate drafters, whereas DSI provides provable speedups in all cases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends speculative inference (SI) by introducing speculation parallelism (SP), a new form of task parallelism that overlaps target-model verification with drafter computation, breaking SI's sequential draft-then-verify bottleneck. It builds directly on the lossless verification frameworks of Leviathan et al. (2023) and Chen et al. (2023), and is conceptually distinct from model parallelism (tensor/pipeline parallelism) which accelerates individual forwards rather than reducing their count. The closest prior work, PEARL (Liu et al., 2025), also overlaps drafting and verification but remains a sequential algorithm limited to one target and one drafter instance, whereas DSI orchestrates an arbitrary number of target servers and provides provable speedup guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that DSI provably eliminates the regime where SI is slower than plain autoregressive inference (i.e., when drafters are too slow or inaccurate), making speculative decoding universally beneficial rather than conditional on drafter quality. However, the empirical validation is limited to single-GPU simulations with synthetic wait times rather than actual multi-GPU hardware, and the demonstrated speedups (1.29–1.92×) are modest relative to the theoretical upper bounds (4×–50×). Open questions include whether the gains hold under real multi-GPU contention, communication latencies in multi-node settings, and for models requiring model parallelism degree > 1 to avoid memory offloading.
