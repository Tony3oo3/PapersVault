## Problem

LLM-based data quality rating systems are widely used for instruction-tuning data selection, but their scores contain systematic inaccuracies and model-specific biases that degrade selection quality. Prior work relies on raw LLM scores without accounting for these errors, and no principled mechanism exists to detect and correct score-level noise in the absence of ground-truth quality labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253633/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pool of instruction-tuning samples (300K across five datasets in TULU format), LLM-generated quality scores initially on a 1–10 integer scale that are rescaled to a 0–5 discrete space by merging low scores {1,2,3,4}→4 and high scores {9,10}→9 then shifting, and embedding features from BGE (or SentenceBERT). It produces a curated subset (e.g., 10K or 1K samples) for fine-tuning, along with corrected per-sample quality scores and long-tail diversity scores. The selection is performed via dual sorting: first by curated score (descending), then by long-tail score within each score group.

## Method

DS² models LLM rating errors as a score transition matrix T (analogous to a label-noise matrix), estimated without ground truth by solving a linear program over first-, second-, and third-order k-NN consensus vectors under a 2-NN clusterability assumption. For instance-level correction, it computes a cosine-similarity agreement score between each sample's raw score and its 2-NN neighbors' soft score distribution, then applies a Bayesian error threshold derived from T and the estimated ground-truth score distribution p to flag misrated samples, which are reassigned to the k-NN-suggested candidate score. A confidence-probability mechanism (default 0.5) mitigates the tendency of imbalanced score distributions to over-correct high-rated samples toward the mode. Finally, a long-tail diversity score—computed as the average cosine distance to k-NN neighbors in embedding space—is used as a secondary sort key within each curated-score group, ensuring the selected subset balances quality with coverage of rare data regions.

## Results

Using only 10K samples (3.3% of the 300K data pool), DS² with GPT-4o-mini rating achieves an average of 61.4 across five OpenLLM Leaderboard tasks (MMLU, TruthfulQA, GSM, BBH, TydiQA) on LLaMA-3.1-8B, outperforming the full 300K dataset (57.7) by 3.7 points. Even with weaker rating models, DS² achieves 60.2 (LLaMA) and 61.1 (Mistral), both surpassing the full-data baseline. At the 1K sample size, DS² with GPT-4o-mini matches or exceeds the human-curated LIMA dataset (54.8 vs. 50.2 average on LLaMA-3.1-8B), and at 10K it clearly surpasses LIMA on Vicuna-Bench and MT-Bench LLM-judge evaluations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DS² consistently outperforms all nine baselines across all three rating models. Against the statistical-metric baselines, DS² (GPT) achieves 61.4 average vs. Completion Length (50.4), Perplexity (56.3), k-NN-10 (57.3), and Random Selection (57.4), with the largest gap over Completion Length (+11.0). Against the score-aware baselines, DS² (GPT) beats AlpaGasus (58.1) by +3.3 and DEITA (59.7) by +1.7; notably, DS² with the weaker LLaMA rating model (60.2) still surpasses AlpaGasus with GPT (58.1). Against LESS (58.0), DS² gains +3.4. Most strikingly, DS² with only 10K samples (3.3% of data) outperforms the Full Data 300K baseline (57.7) by +3.7, and at 1K samples DS² with GPT (54.8 average) surpasses the human-curated LIMA dataset (50.2) by +4.6 on LLaMA-3.1-8B, while at 10K it further extends the lead on Vicuna-Bench and MT-Bench LLM-judge evaluations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the noisy-label transition matrix framework from Zhu et al. (2021) and Liu et al. (2023b)—originally developed for image classification with ground-truth labels—to the setting of LLM-generated quality scores where ground truth is unavailable. It adapts the k-NN clusterability condition to text embedding spaces and uses consensus vectors to estimate the score transition matrix without any human annotations. In the LLM-based data selection lineage (AlpaGasus, DEITA, LESS), the paper's key departure is treating raw LLM scores as noisy observations requiring correction rather than as reliable signals, and adding a diversity-aware long-tail scoring step that prior score-based methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 3.3% of a 300k dataset outperforms the full dataset, and that weaker open-source models with score curation can match GPT-4o-mini without curation, offers a practical cost-saving path for instruction tuning pipelines. However, the method's reliance on the sample-independent transition matrix assumption and k-NN clusterability in text (where subtle token changes can flip correctness) limits its generality, and experiments are confined to 7B/8B models with GPT-4o-mini rather than GPT-4. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger base models, whether more powerful rating models (GPT-4, o1) still benefit from curation, and whether group-dependent or instance-dependent transition matrices could tighten the sample-independent assumption.
