## Problem

Standard IRL methods frame imitation as an adversarial min-max game between a reward discriminator and an RL policy, making them computationally expensive, unstable to train, and dependent on expert action labels. This paper asks whether occupancy matching can be achieved through direct policy search without any adversarial component, and whether the resulting method can operate on state-only demonstrations where action labels are entirely absent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251391/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single expert trajectory of raw state vectors (no action annotations required) and online environment transitions collected during training. It produces a deterministic or stochastic imitation policy parameterized by a neural network, along with learned successor feature representations that encode expected discounted state-feature visitation. The state-only requirement means the method can ingest video, motion-capture, or any demonstration format where the demonstrator's control inputs are unavailable or incompatible with the agent's action space.

## Method

SFM exploits the linear factorization of the return as an inner product of successor features and a reward vector to compute the imitation-gap-maximizing witness w* in closed form as the normalized difference between expert and agent successor features (ψ^E − ψ^π), eliminating the adversarial reward-learning loop entirely. A key theoretical result (Proposition 1) shows that the agent's initial-state successor features can be estimated from arbitrary replay-buffer transitions via a bootstrapped TD expression, avoiding high-variance Monte Carlo rollouts. The policy gradient (Proposition 2) then uses the learned SF network directly as the Q-function under the witness reward, yielding a drop-in replacement for the actor loss in standard actor-critic algorithms like TD3 or TD7. Base features φ are learned jointly via self-supervised objectives (forward dynamics model, inverse dynamics model, autoencoder, or Hilbert representations), with FDM found to perform best across DMC tasks. The entire pipeline requires no gradient penalties, bespoke optimizers, or learning-rate schedules typically needed to stabilize adversarial IRL.

## Results

On 10 tasks from the DeepMind Control Suite with a single expert demonstration, SFM achieves the highest mean normalized return, outperforming all baselines by 16% on average. SFM wins on 6 out of 10 tasks (Cheetah Walk: 945.1, Quadruped Jump: 799.1, Quadruped Run: 671.7, Quadruped Stand: 941.6, Walker Stand: 909.4, Walker Walk: 916.5) while trailing on Cheetah Run (648.8 vs. MM's 781.6) and Walker Flip (856.9 vs. OPOLO's 913.6). Notably, SFM achieves this without expert action labels, whereas BC and IQ-Learn require them, and it does so without adversarial training tricks like gradient penalties or bespoke optimizers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFM (state-only, non-adversarial) outperforms all five baselines on mean normalized return by 16%. Against the non-adversarial IQ-Learn (which requires expert actions), SFM dominates on nearly every task (e.g., Quadruped Jump: 799.1 vs. 260.7; Walker Walk: 916.5 vs. 25.3). Against behavior cloning (BC), SFM is substantially better, with BC failing in the single-demonstration regime due to distribution shift. Among state-only adversarial methods, SFM beats MM on 7/10 tasks and GAIfO on 6/10 tasks, with the largest margins on quadruped tasks (e.g., Quadruped Run: 671.7 vs. MM's 433.9 and GAIfO's 289.4). SFM trails OPOLO on Cheetah Run (648.8 vs. 747.5) and Walker Flip (856.9 vs. 913.6), where OPOLO's inverse dynamics model provides an advantage. A key robustness finding is that SFM with the weaker TD3 optimizer maintains performance close to TD7, while adversarial baselines (MM, GAIfO) degrade significantly under TD3.

## Relation to Prior Work

SFM builds on the successor feature framework (Barreto et al., 2017) and the feature-matching formulation of IRL (Abbeel & Ng, 2004; Ziebart et al., 2008), but departs from the dominant adversarial min-max paradigm (GAIL, MM, GAIfO, OPOLO) by computing the imitation-gap-maximizing witness in closed form from the difference in learned successor features. The closest prior method is IQ-Learn (Garg et al., 2021), which also reduces IRL to a single RL problem via an inverse Bellman operator, but SFM additionally eliminates the need for expert action labels by using action-independent base features. For representation learning, SFM draws on recent RL representation techniques (Hilbert Representations, Forward Dynamics Models, Proto-Value Networks) to jointly learn the base feature function rather than assuming it is given.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SFM provides the first non-adversarial, state-only, interactive IRL method, opening the door to imitation from video or motion-capture data where action labels are naturally absent. The unexpected robustness to the policy optimizer (TD3 vs. TD7) suggests that the non-adversarial formulation is inherently more stable than game-solving approaches, which may simplify deployment in resource-constrained robotics settings. Key limitations remain: SFM does not theoretically address the exploration problem inherent to IRL, and evaluation is confined to 10 DMC tasks with a single demonstration, leaving open questions about scalability to more complex, high-dimensional, or multi-task settings. The paper also notes a conceptual parallel to DPO in RLHF, suggesting a broader trend toward direct policy search over explicit reward modeling.
