## Problem

Releasing a differentially private average 3D human face is challenging because the high dimensionality (7150 points × 3 coordinates) makes point-wise noise injection prohibitively expensive in privacy budget and distorts facial structure. No prior work had extended Gaussian DP to functional data or proposed a shape-preserving curve representation of 3D faces that is amenable to functional-data privacy mechanisms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250002/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a collection of n 3D face point clouds (7150 quasi-landmarks each, genus-0 surfaces) and produces a μ-GDP private average face represented as J=23 face radial curves, each with three coordinate functions defined on the unit circle. Input faces are consumed only after disk conformal parameterization and elastic registration to a shared template, so the mechanism operates on aligned, unit-area, origin-centered surfaces rather than raw point clouds. The output is a set of smooth functions (not a point cloud) that can be discretized back to a 3D surface for visualization.

## Method

The framework has two components: a novel face radial curve representation and an extension of Gaussian DP to functional data. Each face is disk-parameterized via conformal mapping, scaled to unit surface area, centered at the origin, and elastically registered to a template using the square-root normal field (SRNF) from elastic shape analysis; radial curves are then extracted as concentric circles on the disk so that corresponding curves across faces capture the same anatomical features. The key theoretical contribution proves that the RKHS Gaussian process noise mechanism satisfies μ-GDP with σ ≥ Δ/μ, where Δ is bounded via the smoothness penalty φ in the regularized mean. Each of the J×3 coordinate curves is sanitized independently with a periodic kernel, allocating tighter smoothing to x,y coordinates (which are near-sinusoidal by construction) and looser smoothing to z (which encodes facial features), and tight GDP composition (√(Σμᵢ²)) is used across all mechanisms to stay within a total budget μ_T.

## Results

On the Sero et al. (2019) dataset of 1,000 3D faces (7,150 points each), the proposed face radial curve GDP method with J=23 curves achieves an MSE of 5.2989×10⁻⁴ against the point-wise mean at a total privacy budget of μ_T=2.9961, outperforming the point-wise Gaussian noise baseline at μ_T=3 (MSE=7.5807×10⁻⁴) and substantially outperforming it at μ_T=2 (MSE=14×10⁻⁴). The MSE between the private face radial curve mean and the non-private RKHS mean is 3.6365×10⁻⁴, less than half the point-wise sanitization error. The method reduces the number of required independent mechanisms from 21,450 (7,150 points × 3 coordinates) to just 69 (23 curves × 3 coordinates).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed face radial curve GDP mechanism against a point-wise Gaussian noise baseline that independently sanitizes each of the 7,150×3 coordinates of the average face. At a comparable privacy budget (μ_T ≈ 3), the face radial curve method achieves an MSE of 5.2989×10⁻⁴ versus 7.5807×10⁻⁴ for the point-wise method, a ~30% reduction in error. At a lower budget (μ_T=2), the gap widens dramatically: 5.2989×10⁻⁴ vs. 14×10⁻⁴, a ~62% reduction. The authors also report the MSE between their private mean and the non-private RKHS mean (3.6365×10⁻⁴), which is less than half the point-wise sanitization error at μ_T=3, further confirming the advantage. Visually, the point-wise method produces a rough, fuzzy face with distorted structures, while the face radial curve method preserves smooth facial features (lips, eyes) with only subtle smoothing. The key structural advantage is that the radial curve representation requires only 69 mechanisms (23 curves × 3 coordinates) versus 21,450 for the point-wise approach, enabling a much more favorable noise-to-signal ratio under GDP's tight composition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) differential privacy for functional data, building directly on Mirshani et al.'s (2017) infinite-dimensional density framework for (ε,δ)-DP; (2) Gaussian DP (Dong et al., 2019), whose tight composition property is essential when sanitizing many curves simultaneously; and (3) elastic shape analysis of 3D surfaces (Jermyn et al., 2017), which provides the disk parameterization and registration machinery. The key departure from prior radial-curve face representations (Drira et al., 2010; Samir et al., 2006) is the use of a global disk parameterization to ensure all curves capture the same anatomical features across faces, rather than extracting curves independently. The authors explicitly note that parameterized surfaces in the DP context had not been previously explored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: 3D faces encode identity and even genomic information (Venkatesaramani et al., 2021), and anthropological studies increasingly need to release data with formal privacy guarantees. The method's structural advantage—reducing thousands of point-wise noise injections to a few dozen functional estimates with GDP's tight composition—makes it more practical than naive point-wise approaches. However, the empirical evaluation is preliminary: a single clean dataset of 1000 neutral-expression faces, partially data-driven sensitivity estimation, and a genus-0 (no holes) assumption limit generalizability. Open questions include handling expression variability, missing data, private selection of the number of curves J, and whether the approach extends to other disk-diffeomorphic surfaces (terrain, organs) as the authors suggest.
