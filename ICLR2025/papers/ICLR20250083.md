## Problem

Existing Lipschitz bandit algorithms (Zooming, HOO, A-BLiN) must store reward statistics for all visited arms, requiring O(T) or poly(T) space that becomes impractical for large time horizons. This paper asks: what is the minimum memory cost to achieve the optimal regret rate of Õ(T^{(d_z+1)/(d_z+2)}) for the Lipschitz bandit problem, and proves that O(log T) bits are both sufficient and necessary.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250083/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates on a continuous arm space [0,1]^d with a 1-Lipschitz expected reward function and subGaussian (parameter 1) reward feedback over a time horizon T. At each time step it selects one arm and receives a scalar reward. The critical constraint is that the algorithm may store information about only a constant number of arms (specifically 3: the best arm from the previous round, the best arm in the current round, and the current running arm), each encoded in O(log T) bits, forcing it to re-explore regions it has forgotten.

## Method

Log-Li performs iterative deepening over a doubling edge-length sequence r_m = 2^{-m+1}, restarting from depth 1 each round since it cannot remember which regions were previously eliminated. Within each round, it recursively partitions the arm space into subcubes, pulling n_h = 16 log(T/δ)/r_h^2 times per cube at depth h, and eliminates a subcube if its empirical mean falls more than 4r_h below the previous round's best reward (tilde_mu^{m-1}). The key mechanism is that only the empirically best arm from the prior round is retained as a comparison anchor, enabling arm elimination without storing per-arm statistics. The total number of rounds B is chosen as 1 + log(T/log(T/δ))/(d_z+2) to balance cumulative exploration regret against the final exploitation gap, yielding the optimal Õ(T^{(d_z+1)/(d_z+2)}) regret while consuming only O(log T) bits.

## Results

Log-Li achieves the optimal regret rate of Õ(T^{(d_z+1)/(d_z+2)}) using only O(log T) bits of memory, matching the regret guarantees of Zooming and A-BLiN while reducing space from O(T) (Zooming) or O(T^{(d_z+1)/(d_z+2)}(log T)^{-(d_z+1)/(d_z+2)}) (A-BLiN) to logarithmic. A matching lower bound of Ω(log T) bits is proven, establishing that Log-Li is simultaneously optimal in regret and space. In experiments with T=100,000 on [0,1]², Log-Li achieves sublinear regret comparable to A-BLiN while consuming orders of magnitude less memory, and runs in O(T) time versus O(T²) for Zooming.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Log-Li matches the optimal regret rate Õ(T^{(d_z+1)/(d_z+2)}) of both Zooming (Kleinberg et al., 2008) and A-BLiN (Feng et al., 2022), so there is no regret degradation from the space reduction. On space, Log-Li's O(log T) bits is a dramatic improvement over Zooming's O(T) and A-BLiN's O(T^{(d_z+1)/(d_z+2)}(log T)^{-(d_z+1)/(d_z+2)}), reducing memory by orders of magnitude as T grows (Log-Li stores only 3 arms regardless of T). In the empirical comparison (T=100,000, [0,1]²), Log-Li's cumulative regret is slightly higher than A-BLiN and Zooming in the mid-range of t due to restarting exploration from depth 1 each round, but it converges to a sublinear curve with a growth trend similar to A-BLiN. On time complexity, Log-Li runs in O(T), improving over Zooming's O(T²) and matching A-BLiN's O(T). The paper also proves that no algorithm can achieve the optimal regret with less than Ω(log T) bits, confirming Log-Li's space usage is information-theoretically necessary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Lipschitz bandit framework established by Kleinberg et al. (2008, Zooming) and Bubeck et al. (2011, HOO), which both rely on storing reward statistics for all visited arms, requiring poly(T) space. The state-of-the-art A-BLiN (Feng et al., 2022) reduces space to O(T^{(d_z+1)/(d_z+2)}(log T)^{-(d_z+1)/(d_z+2)}) but still grows with T. Log-Li departs fundamentally by abandoning persistent memory of explored regions: it restarts exploration from depth 1 each round and uses only the empirically best arm from the previous round for elimination, achieving O(log T) bits while preserving the optimal regret rate. The paper also positions itself against the broader constrained-memory learning literature (Raz 2017, streaming MAB), noting those results do not transfer to the infinite-arm, non-streaming Lipschitz setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that optimal regret and O(log T) space are simultaneously achievable—challenges the intuition that memory of past exploration is essential for avoiding redundant visits to suboptimal regions. The matching lower bound (Ω(log T) bits necessary) closes the gap, making Log-Li provably optimal in both dimensions. Practically, this makes Lipschitz bandits feasible in settings with very large time horizons where prior algorithms' poly(T) memory becomes infeasible. Limitations include the algorithm's tendency to restart from depth 1 each round, causing higher constant factors and instability in finite-horizon experiments, and the analysis is restricted to the [0,1]^d cube (though generalization to doubling spaces is noted as straightforward). Open questions include whether the iterative-deepening-with-restart principle transfers to other continuous-arm bandit variants (e.g., adversarial, contextual).
