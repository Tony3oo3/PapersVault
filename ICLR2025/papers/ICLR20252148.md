## Problem

While scaling neural decoding models within homogeneous populations (same brain region, cell type, task) has been shown to improve performance, it remains unknown whether transformer-based decoders can be pretrained on heterogeneous data spanning distinct brain regions, genetically defined cell types, and multiple stimulus contexts, and whether such models can transfer across these boundaries. This paper asks whether shared computational principles exist across diverse neural circuits that a sufficiently large and appropriately designed model can extract.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252148/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw calcium imaging time-series (ΔF/F) from populations of up to ~100,000 neurons across 1,335 sessions, tokenized as P×T pairs of (neuron-identity embedding, signal amplitude) with continuous sinusoidal time encodings. It simultaneously produces 12 different outputs per context window: continuous regression targets (running speed at 30 Hz, 2-D pupil location), discrete classification targets (8-class orientation, 5-class temporal frequency, 6-class spatial frequency), and high-dimensional segmentation targets (900–8000-dim movie frame and noise pattern predictions), with task-specific linear heads projecting from a shared 128-dim latent space to each target's native dimensionality.

## Method

POYO+ is a multi-task, multi-modal transformer built on the POYO encoder-decoder architecture. The encoder compresses P×T neural activity tokens into M×N learned latent tokens via cross-attention with rotary position embeddings (RoPE) over continuous time, followed by L rounds of self-attention. The key architectural innovation is the multi-task decoder: a single shared cross-attention layer is queried by composite tokens o_ℓk = φ_k + s_ℓ, where φ_k is a learned task embedding (accessed via lookup table) and s_ℓ is a learned session embedding, allowing the same latent space to be flexibly interrogated for any number of regression, classification, or segmentation targets without architectural modification. The entire network is trained end-to-end on all 12 tasks simultaneously with weighted MSE and cross-entropy losses, using the LAMB optimizer over 300 epochs on 8 H100 GPUs. This design enables the model to capture shared structure across 6 visual cortical areas and 13 Cre-defined cell types while preserving biologically meaningful distinctions in its latent representations.

## Results

On the Allen Brain Observatory drifting gratings orientation task, POYO+ (All) achieves 55.96% average accuracy across 30 held-out sessions, outperforming the single-session MLP baseline (43.36%) by 12.6 percentage points. Transfer to the OpenScope dendritic/somatic dataset yields 81.91% accuracy (vs. 72.94% MLP), and transfer to a hippocampal open-maze dataset reduces position decoding error from 10.33 cm (trained from scratch) to 9.58 cm (pre-trained). The model's latent embeddings enable 67.93% Cre-line classification and 59.81% brain-area classification via a linear probe, substantially exceeding hand-crafted feature baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CEBRA (Schneider et al., 2023b), POYO+ achieves 48.96% accuracy on the multi-session drifting gratings task versus CEBRA's 18.66%, a 30-point gap. In the pseudomouse natural-movie frame prediction benchmark, POYO+ outperforms CEBRA at every population size tested (10–1000 neurons), with margins exceeding 10% at 30 neurons and approaching 20% at 200 neurons. Against the transcriptomic feature baseline from Schneider et al. (2023a), POYO+ latents yield 67.93% Cre-line classification accuracy (vs. 56.00%), 40.88% Cre-line+Layer (vs. 38.48%), and 59.81% brain-area classification (vs. 36.47%), demonstrating that the learned multi-task representations capture biologically meaningful structure far beyond hand-crafted firing-rate statistics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the POYO framework (Azabou et al., 2024), which introduced a scalable Perceiver-style transformer for multi-session neural decoding, by adding a multi-task, multi-modal decoder with learned task and session embeddings that enables simultaneous decoding of heterogeneous behavioral and stimulus targets. It departs from prior multi-session approaches (POYO, NDT2, CEBRA) that were restricted to recordings from the same brain region and cell type, by demonstrating that a single model can be trained across 6 cortical areas and 13 genetically defined Cre-lines simultaneously. The rotary position embedding scheme is adapted from RoFormer (Su et al., 2021) to handle continuous timestamps in calcium imaging data rather than discrete token positions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that neural circuits across distinct brain regions and cellular sub-types share common computational structure that a sufficiently large multi-task model can extract, opening the door to 'algorithmically stitching together' neural populations that are otherwise studied in isolation. The emergent organization of latent representations by region and cell type—without any explicit anatomical or genetic labels—suggests that functional differences are encoded in activity patterns in a way that is learnable at scale. Key limitations include testing only in mouse visual cortex (plus a small hippocampus transfer), absence of neuron activity prediction, and the relatively small number of held-out sessions (30 animals) for transfer evaluation. Future work expanding to additional species, non-visual regions, and larger held-out sets would strengthen the generalizability claims.
