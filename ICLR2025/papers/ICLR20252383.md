## Problem

Designing effective neural network architectures for unseen datasets remains computationally expensive, requiring iterative training and hyperparameter tuning. While benchmark data recording model performance across datasets already exists, it is stored in flat tabular formats that do not support structured, relationship-aware retrieval, and LLMs lack the detailed, structured knowledge needed to produce customized rather than commonplace designs. The paper asks how to transform benchmark records into a queryable knowledge structure that enables training-free model recommendation for new datasets.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unseen dataset characterized by 14+ graph statistics (node/edge counts, density, clustering coefficients, eigenvector/betweenness/closeness centrality, diameter) plus a semantic description, and queries a pre-built KBG containing 26,206 GNN architectures (4-node DAGs over 9 layer types) with 211,669 performance records stored as hyper-relational facts. It outputs a recommended architecture (topology + per-node layer operations), per-dataset similarity scores in [0,1], and per-model relevance scores, all computed without any model training. The KBG additionally stores categorical entities (domain, task, modality, optimizer, GPU, layer operators) as separate nodes to reduce redundancy and enable flexible subgraph queries.

## Method

The core contribution is a Knowledge Benchmark Graph (KBG) schema that restructures tabular benchmark data into a graph with Data and Model entities (carrying numerical attributes like learning rate, dropout, hidden dimension) and shared categorical entities (domain, task, architecture components, hardware), connected by hyper-relational hasPerformance facts (storing accuracy, loss, latency, parameters per data-model pair) and binary hasFeature relations. Dataset similarity uses a capped power-difference formula S = 1 − (1/d)Σ min{(v^b − v^u)^p, α} with p=1/3 and α=0.8 to suppress outlier feature gaps, while model relevance R(F;D^u) = Σ_{D^b∈D^c} M(F;D^b)·S(D^b,D^u) aggregates weighted historical performance across all benchmark datasets exceeding a similarity threshold δ. A novel Relevance Linearity Score (RLS) evaluates similarity metrics by measuring R² alignment between similarity rankings and the area under the ε-Δ transferability curve, providing a training-free ground-truth proxy. For LLM integration, an iterative loop retrieves candidate models from KBG, prompts GPT-4 to infer or select a model given dataset info and candidate details, then retrieves the next candidate set using a combined score β·R(F;D^u)+(1−β)·W(F,F′) (β=0.8) where W is binary-vector architecture similarity, repeating until the computation budget is exhausted.

## Results

On the NAS-Bench-Graph benchmark (9 graph datasets, 26,206 GNN architectures), the paper's naive KBG retrieval method achieves the best or second-best accuracy on 4 out of 8 datasets without any training. Specifically, it attains 82.53% on Cora (best overall), 76.53% on PubMed, 89.32% on CS, and 71.68% on arXiv, while remaining competitive on the remaining four. When combined with GPT-4 for iterative refinement (KBG+LLM Select), the method reaches 92.61% on Physics and 82.34% on Computers, surpassing the DesiGNN-init baseline on 6 of 8 datasets. The method requires no model training, unlike all competing AutoML and NAS baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest LLM-based baseline DesiGNN-init, the paper's KBG retrieval wins on Cora (82.53 vs. 80.31, +2.22) and arXiv (71.68 vs. 71.50), ties on Citeseer (69.20), but trails on Physics (90.34 vs. 92.10, −1.76) and Computers (76.61 vs. 82.20, −5.59). Compared to the other similarity-based baselines Kendall and Overlap, the proposed method dominates on Cora (82.53 vs. 67.73/79.36), PubMed (76.53 vs. 71.80/71.80), and CS (89.32 vs. 88.56/88.56), while roughly matching on Photo and arXiv. Against all classic NAS methods (Random, EA, RL) and AutoML methods (GNAS, Auto-GNN, GPT4GNAS, GHGNAS), the KBG retrieval is competitive or superior on most datasets without requiring any training. When GPT-4 is integrated with the KBG for iterative selection (KBG+LLM Select), the combined method surpasses DesiGNN-init on 6 of 8 datasets, reaching 92.61% on Physics and 82.34% on Computers. The key advantage is that all competing methods require repeated model training (GPU-hours to days), whereas the KBG retrieval is training-free.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of AutoML/NAS, knowledge graphs, and LLMs, departing from both traditional NAS (which searches from scratch) and LLM-based NAS methods (GPT4GNAS, GHGNAS, DesiGNN) that rely on the LLM's parametric knowledge. It builds on the hyper-relational knowledge graph schema (Rosso et al., 2020) to structure benchmark data into a Knowledge Benchmark Graph (KBG), and extends the LLM-assisted GNN design loop from DesiGNN (Wang et al., 2024) by grounding the LLM in retrieved structured benchmark knowledge rather than free-form generation. The key conceptual departure is treating 'data-model-performance' records as a queryable knowledge base for retrieval-based model recommendation, eliminating the need for any training during the design phase.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The KBG framework offers a principled way to accumulate and reuse design knowledge across benchmarks, potentially reducing the computational cost of model design from GPU-days to near-zero. However, the paper is candid that the dataset similarity metric is 'flawed' (RLS scores are low, often below 0.5), and the evaluation is confined to a single benchmark (NAS-Bench-Graph) with only 9 graph datasets, limiting generalizability claims. Open questions include designing more accurate similarity functions, combining multiple benchmarks into a larger KBG, and whether the LLM integration provides meaningful gains over retrieval alone (the improvements are modest). The small-scale CV experiment on NATS-Bench is a promising but underdeveloped validation of cross-domain applicability.
