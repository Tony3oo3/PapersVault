## Problem

Existing million-scale text-to-video datasets (WebVid-10M, Panda-70M) overemphasize scale at the cost of quality, containing watermarked, flickering, blurry, or static videos paired with short, imprecise captions. Simultaneously, DiT-based T2V models rely on a simple cross-attention module that fails to fully exploit the semantic information carried by text tokens, limiting generation fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250042/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For T2V generation, the model takes a free-form text prompt and produces a 16-frame video clip (sampled at 3-frame intervals) at resolutions from 256×256 up to 1024×1024. The OpenVid-1M dataset provides 1,019,957 clips (≥512×512, averaging 7.2 s) with long expressive captions from LLaVA-v1.6-34b, while OpenVidHD-0.4M supplies 433K clips at 1920×1080 for HD generation. For the secondary video restoration task, a low-resolution video is upscaled to arbitrary resolution using a model trained on OpenVidHD-0.4M.

## Method

The paper contributes two main components. First, a multi-stage curation pipeline filters source videos (primarily Panda-50M) through aesthetics scoring (LAION Aesthetics Predictor, top 20%), temporal consistency (CLIP cosine similarity between adjacent frames to remove static/flickering clips), motion difference (UniMatch optical flow to exclude overly fast or static motion), clarity assessment (DOVER-Technical, top 30%), and cascaded cut detection for single-scene clip extraction, followed by recaptioning with LLaVA-v1.6-34b. Second, the proposed MVDiT architecture introduces a parallel visual-text structure where each layer contains three modules: a Multi-Modal Self-Attention (MMSA) that concatenates visual and text tokens for spatial interaction within each frame, a Multi-Modal Temporal-Attention (MMTA) that performs temporal attention jointly over both modalities to capture structural and semantic temporal consistency, and a Multi-Head Cross-Attention (MHCA) that explicitly injects text semantics into visual tokens. The model is initialized from PixArt-α, uses T5 for text encoding and a VAE for video latent encoding, and is trained in a staged 256→512→1024 resolution schedule.

## Results

On the EvalCrafter benchmark (700 prompts), MVDiT trained on OpenVid-1M at 1024×1024 resolution achieves the highest aesthetic score (VQA_A = 73.46) and second-best technical score (VQA_T = 68.58) among all compared T2V models, while also attaining strong temporal consistency (Clip_temp_score = 99.87, Warping_error = 0.0052). In the controlled dataset comparison (Table 4, same STDiT architecture, 50K iterations), OpenVid-1M outperforms WebVid-10M and Panda-50M across nearly all metrics at both 256×256 and 1024×1024 resolutions. For video restoration on UDM10, a model trained on OpenVidHD-0.4M surpasses Upscale-A-Video on all five metrics (PSNR 23.49 vs 23.43, SSIM 0.7165 vs 0.6195, LPIPS 0.2015 vs 0.2731, DOVER 0.5351 vs 0.4863, E*warp 0.00283 vs 0.00532).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVDiT+OpenVid-1M achieves the best VQA_A (73.46) over all nine T2V baselines, with the closest competitor being VideoCrafter (66.18) and Pika (59.09). On VQA_T, CogVideoX-5B leads (76.86) ahead of Ours (68.58), OpenSoraPlan-V1.2 (65.86), and Pika (64.96); Ours still substantially outperforms UNet-based methods like Modelscope (32.93) and Show-1 (44.24). On temporal consistency, Pika and OpenSoraPlan-V1.2 achieve the lowest warping error (0.0006 and 0.001) and highest Clip_temp_score (99.97), while Ours is competitive at 0.0052 / 99.87. In the controlled dataset ablation (Table 4, same STDiT model), OpenVid-1M beats WebVid-10M and Panda-50M on VQA_A, Blip_bleu, SD_score, and Clip_temp_score at 256×256, and OpenVidHD-0.4M dominates at 1024×1024 (VQA_A 73.46 vs 69.26 for WebVid+SR and 63.25 for Panda+SR). For video restoration, OpenVidHD-0.4M-trained I2VGen-XL outperforms Upscale-A-Video on all five metrics despite using fewer training samples (~130K vs ~370K), with the largest gap in SSIM (0.7165 vs 0.6195) and LPIPS (0.2015 vs 0.2731).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DiT paradigm (Peebles & Xie, 2023) to video generation by adapting MMDiT's (Esser et al., 2024) parallel multi-modal self-attention to a video setting, adding a novel Multi-Modal Temporal-Attention module and a cross-attention layer to capture both structural and semantic temporal information. For dataset construction, it draws on the SVD pipeline (Blattmann et al., 2023) but departs in key ways: using DOVER for clarity assessment, UniMatch for motion scoring, and a quality-first-then-clip ordering for efficiency. Conceptually, it positions itself against prior large-scale T2V datasets (WebVid-10M, Panda-70M) that prioritized volume over quality, and against prior DiT-based T2V methods (OpenSora, Latte) that relied on a simple cross-attention module for text conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a publicly available 1M+ high-quality open-scenario dataset with expressive LLaVA-generated captions, this work addresses a practical bottleneck in T2V research where prior datasets suffered from watermarks, flickering, blur, and imprecise short captions. The demonstration that 1M curated clips outperform 50M–70M unfiltered datasets across multiple metrics validates a quality-over-scale philosophy that could reshape how the community approaches T2V data collection. However, the authors acknowledge limitations in modeling complex physical dynamics, and the MVDiT architecture, while effective, remains a relatively incremental extension of MMDiT. Open questions include whether the quality-first curation strategy scales to tens of millions of clips without losing the diversity needed for robust generalization, and whether the parallel visual-text attention design generalizes to longer video sequences or higher resolutions beyond 1024×1024.
