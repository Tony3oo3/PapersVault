## Problem

Existing diffusion-based inverse problem solvers face a fundamental trade-off: guidance-based methods (DPS, IIGDM) require expensive and unstable backpropagation through the score network, while the optimization-based RED-diff suffers from mode collapse and blurry reconstructions due to gradient inaccuracy in its Denoising Regularization (DR) term. The root cause is that DR relies on a single-step denoising estimate as a stochastic bootstrap target, which is both noisy and detail-poor, leading to feature-averaged outputs and slow convergence requiring thousands of optimization steps.

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded measurement y ∈ R^n, a known measurement operator A(·): R^m → R^n (linear or nonlinear), and a pretrained score-based diffusion model ε_θ. It initializes the reconstruction variable μ as A^{-1}(y) (the degraded image itself, not Gaussian noise) and iteratively refines it via stochastic gradient descent with the Adam optimizer. Experiments use 256×256 RGB images on ImageNet and FFHQ, with 100–500 optimization steps depending on task difficulty, and the method requires only forward passes through the score network (no backpropagation), keeping memory at ~4.3 GB on a single RTX 3090.

## Method

The paper identifies that DR's gradient inaccuracy stems from using the single-step denoising estimate μ̂_{0|t} as a noisy, detail-poor bootstrap target. To fix this, it introduces Consistency Regularization (CR), which replaces the single estimate with the difference between two adjacent denoising estimates (at t and t+ε), inspired by the self-consistency property in consistency distillation—this yields more stable gradients without requiring ODE simulation. The key unification is Hybrid Regularization (HR), which blends DR and CR through a hybrid noise ε_hybrid = √(1−β)·ε_θ(μ_{t+ε}, t+ε) + √β·ε, where β=0.2 interpolates between fully deterministic (CR, β=0) and fully random (DR, β=1) noise while preserving constant total variance. The resulting gradient ∇_μ L_HR = ∇_μ‖y−A(μ)‖² + E[ω_t(ε_θ(μ_t,t) − ε_hybrid)] requires only two forward passes per step (one for the current noise prediction, one reused from the previous step), and the small stochastic component in the hybrid noise contracts accumulated inversion errors while the deterministic component provides consistent directional guidance.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, HRDIS achieves the best LPIPS, FID, and classifier accuracy across all four linear inverse problems: Inpaint(10-20%) (LPIPS 0.054, FID 10.94, CA 74.7%), Inpaint(20-30%) (LPIPS 0.096, FID 20.10, CA 71.3%), SR×4 (LPIPS 0.137, FID 33.01, CA 68.8%), and CS 25% (LPIPS 0.059, FID 22.04, CA 72.3%). On FFHQ, HRDIS leads on Inpaint(30-40%) and CS(10%) but trails IIGDM on SR×16 (FID 40.97 vs. 39.33). For nonlinear tasks on FFHQ, HRDIS dramatically outperforms both DPS and RED-diff, e.g., phase retrieval LPIPS drops from 0.387 (DPS) to 0.089, and nonlinear deblurring FID improves from 80.13 (RED-diff) to 52.36. In terms of efficiency, HRDIS requires only 14 s/image on ImageNet (vs. 82 s for RED-diff and 274 s for DPS) with 4.3 GB memory, matching the lightest baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HRDIS outperforms all eight baselines on every metric for all four linear tasks on ImageNet, with the largest margins over RED-diff (its direct predecessor) and the projection-based methods (DDRM, DDNM). On FFHQ, HRDIS wins on Inpaint(30-40%) and CS(10%) but IIGDM retains a slight edge on SR×16 (FID 39.33 vs. 40.97), the only task where HRDIS does not lead. For nonlinear problems, HRDIS achieves a 4.3× LPIPS reduction over DPS on phase retrieval (0.089 vs. 0.387) and a 34% FID reduction over RED-diff on nonlinear deblurring (52.36 vs. 80.13). In terms of wall-clock time, HRDIS (14 s/image on ImageNet) is ~6× faster than RED-diff (82 s) and ~20× faster than DPS (274 s), while using the same 4.3 GB memory as RED-diff and DDRM, compared to 8.7–8.8 GB for DPS and IIGDM. FPS is the closest competitor on linear tasks (e.g., Inpaint 10-20%: LPIPS 0.065 vs. 0.054) but still trails HRDIS by a clear margin across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RED-diff variational optimization framework (Mardani et al., 2024) by diagnosing its gradient inaccuracy as stemming from the stochasticity of single-step denoising bootstrap targets. It introduces Consistency Regularization (CR) by transplanting the self-consistency principle from consistency distillation (Song et al., 2023b) into the inverse-problem setting, then unifies DR and CR through the DDIM noise decomposition (Song et al., 2020a) into a single hybrid-noise parameter β. The result positions HRDIS as a principled interpolation between fully stochastic (DR) and fully deterministic (CR) regularization, rather than a fundamentally new sampling paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HRDIS offers a practical ~6× wall-clock speedup over RED-diff (14s vs 82s on ImageNet inpainting) with comparable memory, while fixing the mode-collapse/blur that limited RED-diff's utility. The single hyperparameter β=0.2 transfers across linear and nonlinear tasks without tuning, lowering the barrier to adoption. Open questions remain: convergence guarantees for the non-convex stochastic optimization are absent, the method requires a known measurement operator (blind inverse problems are only preliminarily explored), and extension to latent diffusion models is left for future work.
