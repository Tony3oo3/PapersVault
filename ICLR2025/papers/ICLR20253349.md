## Problem

Contrastive learning-based Source-Free Domain Adaptation methods suffer from two overlooked sources of uncertainty: false negatives introduced by random batch sampling, and inconsistent predictions between original and augmented views of the same target image. Existing approaches either ignore these uncertainties or merely down-weight uncertain samples, without a unified theoretical framework explaining their impact or a principled way to exploit the uncertain information for better adaptation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained source model (ResNet-50 or ResNet-101 backbone) and a set of unlabeled RGB target images, along with stochastic augmentations (grayscale, rotation, posterization, Gaussian blur) applied to each target image during training. It produces an adapted target classifier that outputs class labels, and for a subset of uncertain samples it additionally constructs a partial label set from the model's historical top-K predictions, which serves as a relaxed supervisory signal rather than a single hard pseudo-label.

## Method

UCon-SFDA formulates both uncertainty sources within a Distributionally Robust Optimization framework and derives closed-form upper bounds on the worst-case risk for negative and positive samples under χ², KL, and Wasserstein discrepancy metrics. The negative-side contribution is a dispersion control loss that treats augmented views of each anchor as pseudo-false negatives, minimizing their similarity to the anchor to reduce variance in prediction similarity across true-negative pairs without requiring additional networks. The positive-side contribution identifies uncertain samples via the ratio of top-1 to top-2 predicted probabilities (threshold τ ≈ 1–1.5) and replaces their single pseudo-label supervision with a partial label set built from the model's historical top-K predictions, applying a smoothed cross-entropy loss over the set. The final loss combines the standard contrastive loss with these two uncertainty control terms, weighted by hyperparameters λ_DC and λ_PL, and is optimized with SGD using a decaying negative-loss coefficient.

## Results

UCon-SFDA achieves 89.6% per-class accuracy on VisDA2017 (Syn→Real), outperforming the previous best I-SFDA (88.4%) by +1.2%. On DomainNet-126, it reaches 71.5% average accuracy, a +1.9% gain over GPUE (69.6%). On the severely imbalanced VisDA-RUST benchmark, UCon-SFDA attains 79.4% per-class accuracy, surpassing SF(DA)² (77.3%) by +2.1%. On Office-31, it achieves 90.6% average accuracy, and on the partial-set Office-Home task it reaches 80.3%, both leading all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UCon-SFDA consistently outperforms all 17 compared baselines across every evaluated dataset. On VisDA2017, it surpasses the strongest contrastive-learning baselines I-SFDA (88.4%) and SF(DA)² (88.1%) by 1.2 and 1.5 percentage points respectively, and leads the self-training-based C-SFDA (87.8%) by 1.8 points. On DomainNet-126, the largest-scale benchmark, UCon-SFDA (71.5%) exceeds GPUE (69.6%), SF(DA)² (68.3%), and AdaContrast (67.8%) by 1.9, 3.2, and 3.7 points, demonstrating particular strength in high-dimensional, multi-domain settings. On the severely imbalanced VisDA-RUST task, the gap widens: UCon-SFDA (79.4%) beats SF(DA)² (77.3%) by 2.1 points and AaD (73.4%) by 6.0 points, with the partial-label supervision component contributing most to this gain. On Office-31, UCon-SFDA (90.6%) edges out NRC (89.4%) and CPGA (89.9%), while on the partial-set Office-Home task it leads AaD (79.7%) and SHOT (79.3%) by 0.6 and 1.0 points. The method never loses to any baseline on any dataset, with the largest margins appearing on DomainNet-126 and VisDA-RUST where uncertainty from false negatives and label imbalance is most pronounced.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on contrastive learning-based SFDA methods (AaD, I-SFDA) that decompose the adaptation loss into positive and negative sample components, and reframes their uncertainty through a Distributionally Robust Optimization lens. It departs from prior uncertainty-aware SFDA approaches (Roy et al., Litrico et al., Mitsuzumi et al.) that primarily exclude or down-weight uncertain samples, instead proposing to actively exploit uncertainty: using DRO-derived partial labels for positive supervision and augmentation-based dispersion control for negative sampling. The theoretical analysis draws on Wasserstein DRO and φ-divergence DRO literature to derive closed-form upper bounds on worst-case risks, connecting SFDA to a broader robust optimization framework that had not previously been applied to this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DRO-based theoretical framework provides the first unified explanation for why contrastive learning improves SFDA and identifies two previously overlooked sources of uncertainty (false-negative sampling bias and positive prediction inconsistency) with actionable remedies. The practical gains are modest (1–2% on most benchmarks, up to 2.1% on VisDA-RUST), and the method introduces several additional hyperparameters (τ, K_PL, λ_DC, λ_PL, β) that complicate tuning. Open questions include whether the partial-label supervision strategy generalizes beyond contrastive SFDA to other self-supervised paradigms, whether the DRO bounds are tight enough to guide hyperparameter selection in practice, and whether the approach extends to video or multi-modal domain adaptation settings.
