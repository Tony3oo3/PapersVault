## Problem

LLMs struggle with symbolic music generation because the dominant MIDI format produces long, structurally opaque sequences that resist next-token prediction, while the more LLM-friendly ABC notation suffers from a multi-track alignment problem: tracks are written sequentially, so bars at the same musical position across different tracks are separated by hundreds of tokens, making it difficult for autoregressive models to learn cross-track coherence. Additionally, no principled scaling law exists for the data-constrained regime typical of symbolic music, where repeated epochs over limited corpora are unavoidable.

## Input / Output

The model takes the first bar (or header + first bar) of an ABC notation piece as a conditioning prompt and autoregressively generates the remainder of the composition as plain-text ABC notation, supporting both single-track and multi-track outputs. The 8192-token context window covers over 90% of the training corpus, and multi-track pieces can span up to 11+ tracks. Generation is evaluated under first-measure-conditioned completion, and outputs are validated for correct ABC syntax, measure consistency across tracks, and musical structure (repetition, intra-texture similarity).

## Method

MuPT is a decoder-only Transformer (LLaMA2-style with SwiGLU, RMSNorm, and RoPE) trained with next-token prediction on 33.6B tokens of ABC notation using a 50K-vocabulary BPE tokenizer. The key structural contribution is SMT-ABC notation, which reorganizes multi-track ABC by concatenating all bars sharing the same index across tracks and enclosing each group in a new <|> delimiter, so that temporally simultaneous bars become adjacent tokens rather than being separated by entire track lengths. For scaling, the authors derive the SMS Law by augmenting the Chinchilla formula with a d/(N^α·D^β) interaction term to correct under/over-estimation at small data sizes, and a GELU-gated overfitting term L_overfit = GELU(k_d·D + k_n·log N − k_u·log U_D − k_in) to model loss beyond the early-stopping point in data-constrained training. Models of 190M–4.23B parameters are trained with the L-BFGS-fitted SMS Law to select compute-optimal configurations under a fixed FLOP budget.

## Results

On the full test set, MuPT-SMT achieves an intra-texture similarity (ITS) of 0.4193 and repetition rate (RR) of 43.7%, closely matching ground truth (0.3729 / 43.5%) while substantially outperforming GPT-4 (0.3614 / 16.9%) and MMT (0.1767 / N/A). On the single-track subset, MuPT-SMT reaches RR of 52.6% versus ChatMusician's 40.1%, though ChatMusician edges ahead on ITS (0.5260 vs. 0.4507). In subjective A/B listening tests (N=150), listeners preferred MuPT over GPT-4 in 79% of cases (p=6.66×10⁻⁸) and over MMT in 73% of cases (p=4.22×10⁻⁶).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-4, MuPT-SMT wins decisively on both repetition metrics: ITS improves from 0.3614 to 0.4193 (+16%) and RR jumps from 16.9% to 43.7% on the full test set, and from 23.0% to 52.6% on single-track. Against MMT (a MIDI-based autoregressive model), MuPT-SMT achieves more than double the ITS (0.4193 vs. 0.1767) and a 43.7% repetition rate where MMT produces no repeat signs at all. Against ChatMusician (the prior SOTA ABC-notation LLM), MuPT-SMT wins on repetition rate (52.6% vs. 40.1%) but trails on intra-texture similarity (0.4507 vs. 0.5260) in the single-track setting; the paper reports a net 6% advantage when averaging both metrics. Subjectively, MuPT is preferred over GPT-4 in 79% of pairwise comparisons and over MMT in 73%, with Fleiss' kappa of 0.5807 confirming moderate inter-annotator agreement across 15 listeners.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ABC-notation-based symbolic music pre-training paradigm established by ChatMusician (Yuan et al., 2024) by introducing SMT-ABC notation to solve multi-track bar alignment, a problem that prior single-track-focused approaches did not address. For scaling analysis, it builds directly on the Chinchilla Law (Hoffmann et al., 2022) and the Data-Constrained Law (Muennighoff et al., 2024), extending the latter's discrete effective-data formulation to a continuous version and adding an explicit overfitting term to derive the SMS Law. Architecturally, it follows the standard LLaMA2-style decoder-only transformer (RoPE, SwiGLU, RMSNorm) without novel architectural changes, positioning its contributions squarely in data representation and scaling theory rather than model design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SMS Law provides the first domain-specific scaling framework for symbolic music, offering practitioners a principled way to allocate compute between model size and training epochs in a data-constrained regime where open-source music corpora are limited. The SMT-ABC notation is a lightweight, practical fix (8 CPU hours for 33.6B tokens) that meaningfully improves multi-track coherence, and the open-sourcing of models up to 4.23B parameters with 8192-token context gives the community a strong baseline. Key limitations include the absence of instruction-following or text-to-music capability, reliance on ABC notation (excluding performance-level MIDI expressiveness), and the fact that the scaling law was validated on a single corpus family. Open questions include whether SMS Law generalizes to other data-constrained domains, whether ABC notation's structural advantages persist at much larger scales, and how to integrate ABC leadsheets with audio rendering for full music production pipelines.
