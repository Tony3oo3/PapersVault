## Problem

Existing learning-based planners for autonomous driving struggle to model the multi-modal nature of human driving behavior, often produce low-quality trajectories that require rule-based post-processing for safety, and lack a mechanism to flexibly adapt driving style (e.g., speed, comfort, collision avoidance) at inference time without retraining. This paper addresses all three gaps simultaneously by leveraging diffusion models' distributional expressiveness and their native classifier guidance mechanism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251332/cropped_page5_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes vectorized structured inputs: ego vehicle current state (position and heading only, deliberately excluding velocity/acceleration to prevent learning shortcuts), up to 32 neighboring vehicles' 2-second history, 70 lane polylines with traffic-light and speed-limit attributes, 25 navigation route lanes, and static object descriptors. It jointly outputs future trajectories (coordinates + sin/cos heading) for the ego vehicle and the nearest M=10 neighboring vehicles over τ=80 timesteps at 10 Hz, sufficient for a downstream LQR controller. All coordinates undergo ego-centric transformation followed by z-score normalization (μ=10, σ=20 on x-axis) to stabilize training.

## Method

Diffusion Planner is built on the DiT architecture, where noised future trajectories for all vehicles are denoised via a transformer decoder that fuses conditional information through MLP-Mixer encoders (for sparse lane/neighbor vectors) and multi-head cross-attention. A single diffusion loss jointly trains prediction and planning, enabling cooperative multi-agent modeling without auxiliary losses. The key differentiator is a training-free classifier guidance mechanism based on diffusion posterior sampling: at inference, differentiable energy functions (collision distance, longitudinal jerk, target speed deviation, drivable-area signed distance) are evaluated on the model's denoised estimate, and their gradients are injected into the score function to steer trajectories toward safe or user-preferred behaviors. These energy functions can be freely combined at runtime without any additional training, enabling flexible behavior alignment (e.g., collision + drivable guidance simultaneously) that rule-based refinement cannot achieve.

## Results

On the nuPlan benchmark, Diffusion Planner achieves 89.19 (Test14 NR) and 82.93 (Test14 R) as a pure learning-based method without any rule-based refinement, outperforming PlanTF (85.62/79.58) and PLUTO w/o refine (89.90/78.62) on most sub-benchmarks, with the largest margin on Test14-hard (75.99 vs. 70.03 for PLUTO w/o refine). With a lightweight post-processing module, Diffusion Planner w/ refine. reaches 94.80 (Test14 NR) and 91.75 (Test14 R), surpassing all rule-based and hybrid baselines including PDM-Closed (90.05/91.63) and PLUTO (92.23/90.29). On the 200-hour delivery-vehicle dataset, Diffusion Planner scores 92.08 overall with 100.0 on Drivable and Progress metrics, outperforming PlanTF (90.89) and PLUTO w/o refine (87.77), demonstrating strong cross-domain transferability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among pure learning-based methods (no refinement), Diffusion Planner outperforms PlanTF by 3.57 points on Test14-hard NR (75.99 vs. 69.70) and by 3.35 on Test14 R (82.93 vs. 79.58), while PLUTO w/o refine. edges it slightly on Test14 NR (89.90 vs. 89.19) but trails by 5.96 on Test14-hard NR (70.03 vs. 75.99). GameFormer w/o refine. and UrbanDriver are far behind, scoring below 12 and 52 respectively on Test14 NR, confirming that game-theoretic modeling and policy-gradient approaches struggle without heavy rule-based post-processing. With the refinement module added, Diffusion Planner w/ refine. surpasses PDM-Closed (94.80 vs. 90.05 on Test14 NR), PDM-Hybrid (94.80 vs. 90.10), and PLUTO (94.80 vs. 92.23), and also exceeds the human log-replay expert on Test14 NR (94.80 vs. 94.03). On the delivery-vehicle dataset, Diffusion Planner (92.08) beats PlanTF (90.89) and PLUTO w/o refine. (87.77), while PDM-Closed drops to 80.95 and GameFormer to 51.35, highlighting the transferability advantage of the diffusion approach over methods tuned to nuPlan-specific designs. Against other diffusion-based planners (Table 4), Diffusion Planner achieves 89.19 on Test14 NR versus 68.74 for STR2-CPKS-800M w/o refine. and 50 for Diffusion-es w/o LLM, while running at 0.04 s inference time compared to >11 s for the 800M-parameter STR2 model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the diffusion model framework (score-based generative modeling, classifier guidance) and the DiT transformer architecture, repurposing them for closed-loop autonomous driving planning. It departs from prior learning-based planners (PlanTF, PLUTO, GameFormer) that rely on rule-based post-processing or refinement to ensure trajectory quality, arguing instead that a properly designed diffusion architecture can generate drivable trajectories directly. The joint modeling of ego planning and neighbor prediction as a single 'future trajectory generation' task, trained with a unified diffusion loss, is a conceptual departure from prior work that treats prediction and planning as separate sub-modules with dedicated losses. The training-free classifier guidance mechanism (via diffusion posterior sampling) for runtime behavior adaptation extends the standard classifier guidance literature into the driving domain with differentiable energy functions for collision, comfort, speed, and drivability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that diffusion models can achieve closed-loop planning performance comparable to rule-based methods (PDM-Closed) without any rule-based refinement, which is a meaningful step toward truly learning-based autonomous driving. The flexible guidance mechanism—enabling runtime adjustment of safety, comfort, and speed preferences without retraining—addresses a practical gap in current learning-based planners that lack post-training controllability. Key limitations remain: lateral flexibility (lane changes, avoidance maneuvers) is still weak due to dataset composition, sample efficiency is reduced by multi-step denoising, and the method relies on vectorized map/detection inputs rather than end-to-end perception. The 200-hour delivery-vehicle dataset and its transferability results open questions about generalization across vehicle types and driving styles.
