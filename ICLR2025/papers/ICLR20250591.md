## Problem

Cryo-ET tomograms suffer from anisotropic resolution due to the missing-wedge effect, and the state-of-the-art deep learning correction method IsoNet relies on recursive prediction updates that cause training instability, error accumulation, and model divergence. The paper identifies a fundamental one-to-many mapping failure mode in IsoNet's formulation—where the network averages over multiple valid reconstructions, producing suboptimal outputs in the low-probability region between modes—and seeks a more stable, direct reconstruction approach.

## Input / Output

The method takes WBP-reconstructed 3D subtomograms (typically 64×64×64 pixels) corrupted by the missing wedge as input and produces a missing-wedge-restored, isotropic 3D volume as output. During training, inputs are augmented with Gaussian noise (ε_g) to resolve the one-to-many ambiguity, and the energy model is trained on the observation space Y (missing-wedged projections) rather than the full tomogram space X, since ground-truth complete tomograms are unavailable. At inference, the full tomogram is split into overlapping subtomograms, processed independently, and reassembled with weighted averaging to minimize edge effects.

## Method

CryoGEN frames reconstruction as a Bayesian MAP estimation problem and models the unknown prior p(x) via an energy-based model E_φ implemented as a GAN discriminator, trained adversarially alongside the prediction network g_θ. The training objective combines two terms: (1) a consistency loss that enforces g_θ as an approximate inverse of the missing-wedge operator T_M (modified from IsoNet by removing the epoch-wise recursive refinement step and injecting noise ε_h), and (2) a posterior maximization term that minimizes the energy of rotated, missing-wedged predictions while penalizing deviation from the observed data. The key insight is that adding Gaussian noise ε_g to the input resolves the one-to-many mapping ambiguity, steering the model toward specific low-energy states rather than averaging over multiple minima. The energy model is trained via a min-max adversarial objective (Eq. 9) that reaches Nash equilibrium when the generated distribution matches the noise-convolved data distribution, yielding a ~10× training speedup over IsoNet by eliminating recursive subtomogram averaging.

## Results

On the C13 Vipp1 stacked rings dataset, CryoGEN achieves a PSNR of 30.65 and SSIM of 0.9199, outperforming IsoNet (27.12 / 0.8191) and DeepDeWedge (28.75 / 0.8758). On the synthetic prism shape, the improvement is most dramatic: CryoGEN reaches 32.69 PSNR and 0.9949 SSIM versus 19.11 / 0.8857 for IsoNet and 21.10 / 0.9278 for DeepDeWedge. CryoGEN also delivers an approximate 10× training speedup over IsoNet (≈2 hours vs. ≈20 hours on an NVIDIA V100 for the real-world ribosome experiment).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CryoGEN is compared against two baselines: IsoNet (Liu et al., 2022), the prior state-of-the-art missing-wedge correction method, and DeepDeWedge (Wiedemann & Heckel, 2024), a recent method that jointly performs denoising and missing-wedge reconstruction. CryoGEN wins on every metric across all three evaluated datasets. Against IsoNet, the PSNR gains range from +2.03 dB (Vipp1: 30.65 vs. 27.12) to +13.58 dB (prism: 32.69 vs. 19.11), with SSIM improvements from +0.101 (Vipp1) to +0.109 (prism). Against DeepDeWedge, CryoGEN leads by +1.90 dB PSNR on Vipp1 (30.65 vs. 28.75), +6.02 dB on the prism (32.69 vs. 21.10), and +6.02 dB on the sphere (29.19 vs. 23.17). Qualitatively, CryoGEN eliminates the streak artifacts, synthetic artifacts, and ringing artifacts that persist in both baselines, and produces more symmetric and consistent Fourier-domain reconstructions. The paper also reports that CryoGEN trains approximately 10× faster than IsoNet, removing the need for recursive subtomogram averaging during training.

## Relation to Prior Work

CryoGEN builds directly on IsoNet (Liu et al., 2022), the current state-of-the-art for missing wedge correction in Cryo-ET, but fundamentally departs from its recursive training paradigm. Where IsoNet iteratively refines predictions through self-supervised inpainting loops, CryoGEN reframes the problem as a Bayesian MAP estimation task solved via an energy-based model trained adversarially (using a GAN as the energy function). The noise-injection strategy to resolve the one-to-many mapping ambiguity draws on ideas from generative modeling (Arjovsky & Bottou, 2017), while the overall probabilistic formulation connects to the EBM literature (Lecun et al., 2006). The paper also positions itself against DeepDeWedge (Wiedemann & Heckel, 2024), a concurrent method that unifies denoising and wedge correction, showing that CryoGEN's energy-based approach avoids the artifacts that plague both IsoNet and DeepDeWedge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~10× training speedup (2 hours vs. 20 hours on V100) is practically significant for Cryo-ET labs that must iterate on reconstruction parameters, and the identification of IsoNet's mode-averaging failure mode provides a clear theoretical explanation for previously observed training instabilities. The energy-based formulation offers a more principled probabilistic interpretation of the reconstruction task, potentially enabling future extensions such as uncertainty quantification or integration with other generative priors. Limitations include reliance on the assumption that tomogram distributions are rotationally uniform (beyond the missing wedge), GAN training instability at convergence, and evaluation on a relatively small number of datasets. Open questions include whether the EBM framework scales to full-cell tomograms, whether it can be combined with learned denoising priors, and whether the rotation-invariance assumption holds for anisotropic biological structures.
