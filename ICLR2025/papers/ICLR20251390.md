## Problem

Neural PDE solvers (autoregressive surrogate models) require large training datasets generated by expensive numerical simulators, yet active learning has not been systematically studied for this setting. Unlike PINNs where AL selects collocation points based on residuals, autoregressive PDE solvers face high-dimensional input/output spaces and time-series rollouts that make standard AL methods non-trivial to apply. This paper introduces AL4PDE, the first modular benchmark for evaluating and developing AL methods specifically for neural PDE solvers.

## Input / Output

The AL framework operates on a pool of 100,000 candidate inputs (30,000 for 3D CNS), where each input is a tuple of an initial condition (superposition of sinusoidal waves with random amplitudes, phases, and wave numbers) and PDE parameters (e.g., viscosity, domain length, Mach number) drawn from a test distribution. The surrogate models consume the current spatial field state (at resolutions from 256×1 to 32×32×32) plus PDE parameters and predict the next timestep autoregressively. The AL loop outputs a selected batch of inputs to simulate, with the training set growing exponentially (each iteration doubles the data), and the final output is a trained surrogate that predicts full PDE trajectories.

## Method

AL4PDE is a modular, extensible benchmark combining three components: AL algorithms, surrogate models (U-Net, FNO, SineNet), and differentiable numerical simulators for six PDEs (Burgers, Kuramoto-Sivashinsky, Combined Equation, and 1D/2D/3D Compressible Navier-Stokes). The AL methods fall into two classes: uncertainty-based (query-by-committee ensemble variance over autoregressive rollouts) and feature-based (last-layer features concatenated across timesteps, reduced via Gaussian sketching to 512 dimensions, with spatial averaging to enforce translation invariance). Batch selection strategies include SBAL (power-law sampling over uncertainty scores), LCMD (k-medoids-inspired clustering that selects the furthest point from the largest cluster), Core-Set (greedy farthest-point), Top-K, and BAIT (Bayesian linear regression on features minimizing posterior predictive variance). A key design choice is the exponential data schedule where each AL iteration adds data equal to the current training set size, and the framework supports both pool-based and query-synthesis AL paradigms.

## Results

On the Combined Equation (CE), SBAL reduces RMSE from 1.108×10⁻² (random) to 0.318×10⁻² at the final AL iteration, a 71% reduction. On Burgers' equation, SBAL achieves 0.259×10⁻² vs. 0.552×10⁻² for random (53% reduction), and LCMD reaches 0.269×10⁻². AL methods also substantially reduce worst-case errors: on CE, the 99th-percentile error drops from 2.373×10⁻² (random) to 0.313×10⁻² (SBAL). On Kuramoto-Sivashinsky, only SBAL (0.200) and BAIT (0.205) improve over random (0.229), while on CNS the differences are not statistically significant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SBAL (Kirsch et al., 2023) is the strongest method overall, achieving the lowest final RMSE on Burgers (0.259×10⁻²), CE (0.318×10⁻²), and KS (0.200), and it is the only method that reliably improves over random on KS. LCMD (Holzmüller et al., 2023) is competitive with SBAL on Burgers and CE and excels at reducing higher error quantiles (e.g., 99th-percentile on Burgers: 0.573×10⁻² vs. 0.649×10⁻² for random). BAIT (Ash et al., 2021) matches SBAL/LCMD on Burgers (0.266×10⁻²) and KS (0.205) but lags on CE (0.424×10⁻²). Core-Set (Sener & Savarese, 2018) consistently underperforms the top three methods and on KS is actually worse than random sampling (0.235 vs. 0.229). LHS (McKay et al., 1979) provides no meaningful improvement over random sampling on any PDE, confirming that static space-filling designs do not capture the informative regions that adaptive AL identifies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts general deep batch active learning methods—SBAL (Kirsch et al., 2023), LCMD and Gaussian sketching (Holzmüller et al., 2023), and BAIT (Ash et al., 2021)—to the specific challenge of autoregressive neural PDE solvers with high-dimensional spatiotemporal outputs. Prior AL work on PDEs was confined to PINN collocation point selection, narrow PDE domains, or single-shot prediction models, leaving the broader class of iterative surrogate solvers (U-Net, FNO, SineNet) unexplored. The key conceptual departure is handling the autoregressive rollout structure, where uncertainty and features must be aggregated across timesteps, and adapting distance-based selection to account for translation equivariance in periodic domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AL4PDE is the first extensible benchmark for AL in neural PDE solvers, filling a clear gap between general deep AL benchmarks and PDE-specific surrogate modeling. The finding that AL-generated datasets are reusable across different surrogate architectures (e.g., FNO-selected data improves U-Net) has significant practical implications, as it decouples data acquisition from model development. Open questions remain around hyperparameter adaptation during iterative AL, extension to complex geometries and irregular grids, and the reliability of surrogate training when the model is retrained repeatedly with shifting data distributions.
