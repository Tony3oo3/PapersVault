## Problem

Adaptive optimizers like Adam require optimizer states (first- and second-moment estimates) that double the model's memory footprint, a critical bottleneck for training large language models. While low-rank gradient projection methods such as GaLore reduce this cost, they accumulate gradients from different subspaces into shared momentum buffers without correcting for the resulting projection errors, and their convergence is only guaranteed under a restrictive stable-rank assumption. The paper asks whether one can design a memory-efficient full-parameter optimizer that performs adaptive optimization in dynamically changing low-dimensional subspaces while maintaining provable convergence under standard assumptions.

## Input / Output

At each step, LDAdam consumes the stochastic gradient matrix g_t ∈ R^{n×m} (viewed as a 2D tensor for each weight layer) and the current model parameters θ_t, along with a user-specified projection rank r (typically r ≪ min(n,m)). It produces updated parameters θ_{t+1} and low-rank optimizer states m_t, v_t of shape r×m, plus a projection matrix U_t of shape n×r. The generalized error feedback buffer ξ_t is stored in-place within the gradient accumulation variable, making the error correction mechanism memory-neutral relative to standard Adam. Low-rank compression is applied only to 2D weight matrices (e.g., attention and MLP layers); 1D parameters such as embeddings and output layers are updated with standard Adam.

## Method

LDAdam performs adaptive optimization within a rank-r subspace that is updated at every step via warm-started block power iteration on a barycenter B_t = ρ·U_{t-1}·m̂_{t-1} + (1−ρ)·A_t, balancing gradient fidelity with preservation of optimizer-state information. The key technical contribution is a projection-aware update rule: before the Adam-type momentum and variance updates, the previous optimizer states are projected into the new subspace via the r×r change-of-basis matrix U_t^T·U_{t-1}, and the second-moment transition additionally accounts for the nonlinear (coordinate-wise squaring) structure of Adam by approximating cross-coordinate covariances as products of first-moment estimates. A generalized error feedback mechanism then reintroduces both the gradient projection residual (A_t − U_t·a_t) and the optimizer-state projection loss (U_{t-1}·m_{t-1} − U_t·m_{t-1/2}) into the next iteration's accumulator, with loading factors β₁/(1−β₁) and a consistency condition on β₂ to preserve unbiasedness of both moment estimates. Under standard smoothness, bounded-gradient, and bounded-variance assumptions, LDAdam achieves the O(1/√T) non-convex rate of AMSGrad and an O(log T/T) rate under the Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition, with compression-dependent constants scaling as O(d/r) (or O((d/r)²) under PL), matching the best-known rates for the uncompressed algorithm up to these factors.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark (RoBERTa-base, r=8), LDAdam achieves 86.48 average accuracy, slightly exceeding Adam (86.28) and substantially outperforming GaLore (84.73). On GSM8K (Llama-2 7B, r=512), LDAdam reaches 35.86% accuracy versus Adam's 34.72% and GaLore's 35.18%. On C4 pre-training, LDAdam (r=256) achieves validation perplexity of 22.65 on Llama 130M and 17.30 on Llama 350M, beating Adam (24.64 and 18.08 respectively) while using only a fraction of the optimizer memory (e.g., 1.22 GB vs 25.1 GB for Llama-27B at r=32).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Adam, LDAdam matches or slightly exceeds performance across all three experimental settings: on GLUE it gains +0.20 average accuracy (86.48 vs 86.28), on GSM8K at r=512 it gains +1.14% (35.86 vs 34.72), and on C4 pre-training it achieves lower perplexity at every model scale (e.g., 22.65 vs 24.64 for Llama 130M, 14.09 vs 14.86 for Llama 1.3B) while reducing optimizer state memory by up to 95% (1.22 GB vs 25.1 GB for Llama-27B at r=32). Against GaLore, LDAdam consistently wins: on GLUE the gap is +1.75 average accuracy (86.48 vs 84.73), on GSM8K at r=32 the gap is +5.46% (32.53 vs 27.07), and on C4 pre-training at r=8 for Llama 130M, GaLore fails to converge (perplexity 67.78) while LDAdam reaches 23.82. The ablation (LDAdam no-EF) confirms that the generalized error feedback mechanism is the key differentiator: without it, LDAdam drops to 85.54 on GLUE and fails to converge on C4 at r=8, though it still outperforms GaLore, indicating that the projection-aware update rule alone also provides a meaningful advantage over GaLore's periodic subspace updates.

## Relation to Prior Work

LDAdam extends the low-rank gradient projection framework introduced by GaLore (Zhao et al., 2024) with two key departures: a projection-aware update rule that adapts optimizer states to dynamically changing subspaces at every step (rather than GaLore's periodic SVD-based updates), and a generalized error feedback mechanism that corrects for compression errors in both gradients and optimizer states. The block power iteration for efficient subspace computation follows PowerSGD (Vogels et al., 2020), and the error feedback concept is adapted from distributed optimization (Seide et al., 2014). Unlike GaLore, which requires a strong 'stable-rank' assumption for convergence, LDAdam provides convergence guarantees under standard smoothness and bounded-gradient assumptions, and is the first method to compress both gradient and optimizer state memory simultaneously with provable convergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LDAdam addresses a critical practical bottleneck: Adam's optimizer states consume twice the model's parameter memory, which is prohibitive for training large language models on limited hardware. By reducing optimizer state memory to a fraction (nr + 2rm vs. 2nm) while maintaining competitive accuracy, the method makes full-parameter training accessible on consumer GPUs. Key limitations include the inability to support gradient clipping or per-layer weight updates (due to the error buffer reusing gradient storage), and experiments are limited to models up to 1.3B parameters. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 7B+ parameter pre-training, whether it extends to other adaptive optimizers beyond Adam, and whether the slight accuracy gains over Adam (attributed to implicit regularization from sparse updates) persist at larger scale.
