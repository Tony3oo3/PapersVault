## Problem

Current Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) are limited to processing visual content from a single scenario and lack the ability to memorize and recognize instance identities (e.g., character names and appearances) across different scenes. This 'ID awareness' is a fundamental competency for understanding complex multi-identity visual narratives such as movies, yet no existing model or benchmark has systematically addressed it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251894/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an interleaved sequence of ID reference images (single-character portraits paired with names), one or more test images (keyframes containing multiple characters from different scenes), and a text instruction, all fed simultaneously in an image-text-image-text format. Outputs are task-dependent: a selected image index for matching, bounding box coordinates for localization, a free-form text answer for Q&A, or a comprehensive scene description for captioning. The critical constraint is that ID images and test images depict the same characters from different scenes, requiring cross-image identity association rather than within-image recognition.

## Method

IDA-VLM builds on Qwen-VL-Chat and introduces ID-Former, a two-module cross-attention projector: the first module compresses visual features into a fixed-length encoding via learnable queries, and the second uses ID-image queries to modulate test-image embeddings, explicitly activating identity-relevant information. Training follows a dual-stage instruction-tuning strategy: Stage 1 (~80K samples) uses VCR, RefCOCO, and Flickr30k with ID images cropped from the same test image to teach basic cross-image association; Stage 2 (~60K samples) uses MovieNet with GPT-4V-generated Q&A and captions where ID images are independent single-character shots, making recognition genuinely cross-scene. LLaVA and ShareGPT4V instruction data are mixed in to preserve general capabilities. The paper also introduces MM-ID, a 585-sample benchmark spanning four progressively complex sub-tasks (matching, location, Q&A, captioning) evaluated with GPT-4 scoring and IoU metrics.

## Results

On the proposed MM-ID benchmark, IDA-VLM achieves the best performance across all four sub-tasks: Matching (0.716), Location (IoU 0.821), Q&A (5.71/10), and Caption (5.15/10). The most dramatic margin is on Location, where IDA-VLM scores 0.821 IoU versus 0.504 for Qwen-VL-Chat and 0.244 for GPT-4V. On Q&A, IDA-VLM leads the strongest competitor Gemini-pro (4.97) by 0.74 points, and on Caption it surpasses GPT-4V (4.67) by 0.48 points. On standard benchmarks (MME, SEED-Bench), IDA-VLM preserves or slightly improves the base Qwen-VL-Chat's perception and video understanding scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDA-VLM outperforms all nine baselines on every sub-task it is evaluated on. Against the strongest closed-source API, GPT-4V, IDA-VLM gains +0.089 on Matching, +0.577 on Location IoU, +0.94 on Q&A, and +0.48 on Caption. Gemini-pro is the closest competitor on Matching (0.687 vs 0.716) and Q&A (4.97 vs 5.71), but its Location score (0.081) is dramatically lower than IDA-VLM's (0.821), indicating that even frontier models struggle with spatial grounding of identified characters. Among open-source models, Qwen-VL-Chat (the base model) achieves the best Location score (0.504) but still trails IDA-VLM by 0.317 IoU, while its Q&A and Caption scores (3.63, 2.65) are substantially lower. The relative scoring (Table 2) confirms IDA-VLM's dominance: all ratios (GPT-4V/IDA-VLM, Gemini-pro/IDA-VLM, etc.) are below 1, with the largest gap on Caption where Qwen-VL-Chat scores only 0.396 relative to IDA-VLM. On traditional caption metrics, IDA-VLM achieves METEOR 0.191 and CIDEr 0.515, substantially exceeding GPT-4V (0.144, 0.078) and Gemini-pro (0.121, 0.185).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual instruction tuning paradigm established by LLaVA, Otter, and GPT4RoI by introducing a new modality of reference: character ID images paired with names, rather than spatial RoI coordinates or visual prompts within a single image. The base architecture builds directly on Qwen-VL-Chat, with the novel ID-Former (two cross-attention modules) inserted between the visual encoder and LLM to explicitly modulate test-image features with ID-image queries. The dual-stage training strategy repurposes existing datasets (VCR, RefCOCO, Flickr30k) in a novel configuration for stage one, then leverages MovieNet with GPT-4V-generated instructions for stage two, bridging the gap between generic visual grounding and character-specific identity recognition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and previously untested capability gap in LVLMs: the inability to memorize and recognize instance identities across disparate scenes, which is fundamental to understanding movies and complex visual narratives. The finding that even GPT-4V achieves only 0.244 IoU on the location sub-task and 0.627 on matching suggests that current frontier models lack reliable cross-scene identity binding, a limitation that prompt engineering alone cannot overcome. However, the MM-ID benchmark is small (585 samples), relies on GPT-4 for open-ended scoring (introducing evaluator bias), and evaluates only single-frame scenes rather than actual video, leaving open whether the approach scales to longer temporal sequences, larger cast sizes, or dynamic identity changes. The practical impact is moderate: the ID-reference formulation and ID-Former design offer a reusable recipe, but the narrow scope (single keyframes, ≤3 characters) limits immediate applicability to full movie understanding.
