## Problem

Existing RAG evaluation metrics assess end-to-end system performance, conflating retriever quality with LLM groundedness, and fail to penalize models that rely on parametric knowledge rather than the provided documents. There is no holistic metric that simultaneously measures an LLM's refusal accuracy, answer correctness calibrated to document availability, and citation groundedness in isolation from retrieval quality. This paper addresses both the measurement gap (introducing TRUST-SCORE) and the alignment gap (showing that prompting methods like ICL are insufficient to make LLMs ground their responses in documents).

## Input / Output

The system takes a question and a set of top-5 retrieved documents as input. The LLM must produce either a response consisting of citation-grounded statements with inline document references (e.g., 'statement1 [1][2] statement2 [3]') or a generic refusal statement when the documents lack sufficient information. TRUST-SCORE is computed as the unweighted mean of three F1 sub-scores: F1_GR (grounded refusals, measuring correct answer/refusal decisions), F1_AC (answer-calibrated correctness, measuring claim coverage relative to claims obtainable from the documents), and F1_GC (grounded citations, measuring whether cited documents entail the statements they support).

## Method

TRUST-ALIGN constructs a 19K-sample preference dataset where each sample pairs a positive response (GPT-4-generated, stitching gold claims with correct citations, or a refusal for unanswerable questions) with a hard negative response (drawn from a fine-tuned LLaMA-2-7b model's hallucinations, ranked by a severity score combining five error types: Inaccurate Answer, Over-Responsiveness, Excessive Refusal, Over-Citation, and Improper Citation). The model is then aligned via Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) on these paired responses. The TRUST-SCORE metric introduces answer calibration—computing per-sample recall against only the gold claims entailed by the provided documents (|A_G ∩ A_D|) rather than all gold claims—which prevents parametric knowledge from artificially inflating scores and sets a uniform maximum recall of 1 for all models. Citation groundedness is verified using the TRUE NLI model (T5-11B) to check whether cited documents entail the statements they support, with both statement-wise recall and per-citation precision computed. The alignment dataset is built by collecting ~10K seed questions from ASQA/QAMPARI/ELI5 training splits, retrieving oracle documents, augmenting (q, D) pairs to trigger diverse hallucination types, and selecting the top 50% most severe negative responses for DPO training.

## Results

On the flagship LLaMA-3-8b model, TRUST-ALIGN achieves TRUST-SCOREs of 69.23 (ASQA), 58.77 (QAMPARI), and 50.24 (ELI5), outperforming the strongest baseline FRONT by +12.56, +36.04, and +17.69 points respectively. Across all 27 model-family × dataset configurations (LLaMA 1b–8b, Qwen-2.5 0.5b–7b, Phi3.5-mini), TRUST-ALIGN wins on 26/27. The method also substantially improves Grounded Refusals F1 (F1_GR) in all 27 configurations (e.g., +23.87 on ASQA for LLaMA-3-8b) and Grounded Citations F1 (F1_GC) in 24/27 (e.g., +38.35 on QAMPARI). On the out-of-domain ExpertQA benchmark, TRUST-ALIGNed LLaMA-3-8b reaches a TRUST-SCORE of 54.85, slightly surpassing GPT-4 ICL (54.69).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRUST-ALIGN (DPO) outperforms FRONT (Huang et al., 2024b) on TRUST-SCORE in 26/27 configurations, with the largest gap on QAMPARI (+36.04 for LLaMA-3-8b) where FRONT's over-responsiveness (AR% = 100%) severely hurts its F1_GR. Against Self-RAG (Asai et al., 2024), TRUST-ALIGN wins by a wide margin on all three datasets for LLaMA-2-7b (e.g., 67.51 vs. 49.28 on ASQA), primarily due to superior refusal and citation grounding. The prompting-based baselines from Gao et al. (2023b) — ICL, PostCite, and PostAttr — are consistently the weakest, with TRUST-SCOREs often below 35, as they fail to learn grounded refusal behavior. On the out-of-domain ExpertQA set, TRUST-ALIGNed LLaMA-3-8b (54.85) marginally beats GPT-4 ICL (54.69) and substantially outperforms GPT-3.5 (52.32) and Claude-3.5-Sonnet (24.76), though GPT-4 retains a higher F1_AC (41.32 vs. 27.36) by leveraging parametric knowledge that TRUST-SCORE penalizes. DPO consistently outperforms SFT-only training on TRUST-SCORE in 26/27 configurations, with the gap most pronounced on F1_GC (citation quality), confirming that preference optimization is critical for citation grounding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024b) as its alignment mechanism but departs from prior RAG evaluation work (Gao et al., 2023b) by isolating LLM performance from retriever quality through answer calibration—scoring claims only against what is actually present in the provided documents rather than the full gold answer set. It extends the attribution-focused frameworks of Huang et al. (2024b, FRONT) and Ye et al. (2024) by incorporating refusal behavior as a first-class evaluation dimension, and contrasts with Thakur et al. (2024) by jointly measuring refusal, answer correctness, and citation groundedness in a single holistic score. The TRUST-ALIGN dataset construction, which pairs positive responses (stitched from gold claims) with hard negatives (hallucinations observed from a fine-tuned model), is a novel data curation strategy that targets five specific hallucination types simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that even GPT-4 and Claude-3.5-Sonnet heavily rely on parametric knowledge in RAG settings—resulting in low TRUST-SCORE—challenges the assumption that frontier models are naturally suited for document-grounded generation, and suggests that explicit alignment is necessary rather than optional. TRUST-SCORE addresses a real evaluation gap by preventing the 'recall gamification' where models that ignore documents and use internal knowledge score higher than properly grounded models. However, the mixed results on F1_AC (answer correctness) reveal a tension: models that correctly refuse unanswerable questions lose recall credit, and the metric's design may still partially reward over-responsiveness. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-document or multi-turn RAG settings, and whether the DPO-based alignment can be replaced by more sample-efficient methods.
