## Problem

The prevailing theoretical justification for Diffusion Posterior Sampling (DPS) is that it approximates the conditional score ∇log p(X_t|y) to achieve posterior sampling. This paper demonstrates through empirical analysis on 512×512 ImageNet images that DPS's conditional score estimation is actually poor—exhibiting large score error (worse than the unconditional score), a significantly non-zero mean, and markedly low sample diversity—arguing instead that DPS more closely resembles Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) estimation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded 512×512 RGB image (produced by ×8 bicubic downsampling, Gaussian blurring with kernel size 61, or non-linear blurring), the corresponding degradation operator f(·), and a pre-trained Stable Diffusion 2.0 model operating in latent space with a 500-step DDPM ancestral sampling solver. It outputs a single restored 512×512 image. The CSE-enhanced variant additionally requires a small training set (as few as 100 ImageNet images or self-generated Stable Diffusion images) for an 8-GPU-hour ControlNet training step, amortized to ~28 seconds per evaluation image.

## Method

The core contribution is DMAP (Diffusion Maximize a Posterior), which reformulates the DPS update as a constrained MAP optimization: maximize log p(y|X_{t-1}) subject to X_{t-1} ~ p(X_{t-1}|X_t). Exploiting the high-dimensional concentration of isotropic Gaussians on a sphere surface of radius √d·σ_t, DMAP performs K steps of gradient ascent on the data-fidelity term followed by explicit spherical projection, replacing DPS's single-step offset. The second improvement introduces a lightweight Conditional Score Estimator (ControlNet) trained on only 100 images in 8 GPU hours, which provides a better initialization distribution q(X_{t-1}|X_t,y) for the MAP optimization, provably yielding a higher expected log-likelihood than the unconditional initialization. Together, DMAP (same complexity, K=2 with halved diffusion steps) and DMAP (full, K=3) outperform all training-free baselines, while DPS+CSE and DMAP+CSE further reduce FID by 20–30% over DPS alone.

## Results

On 512×512 ImageNet, DMAP (full) achieves PSNR 23.52 / LPIPS 0.3494 / FID 39.56 for SR×8, outperforming DPS (22.33 / 0.4137 / 58.48) and DSG (23.15 / 0.3912 / 51.15). Adding a lightweight CSE trained on only 1000 images (8 GPU hours) further boosts DPS to PSNR 23.35 / LPIPS 0.3142 / FID 36.23 for SR×8 and PSNR 25.47 / LPIPS 0.2150 / FID 18.96 for Gaussian Deblur. DMAP+CSE(Self-gen) achieves the best overall FID of 32.04 for Non-linear Deblur and 21.17 for Gaussian Deblur, with only ~28s amortized training overhead per image.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMAP (same complexity) outperforms all training-free baselines across all three tasks: it beats DSG (the strongest prior method) by +0.22 PSNR and −6.78 FID on SR×8, and by +0.72 PSNR and −7.86 FID on Gaussian Deblur, while running in comparable time (172s vs. 165s). DMAP (full) extends these gains to +0.37 PSNR and −11.59 FID over DSG on SR×8. The CSE-enhanced variants (DPS+CSE, DMAP+CSE) substantially outperform all training-free methods on LPIPS and FID; for example, DPS+CSE(n=1000) achieves FID 18.96 on Gaussian Deblur versus 52.01 for DSG, a 64% reduction. Against the trained conditional model StableSR (FID 29.12, LPIPS 0.2855 on SR×8), DMAP+CSE(Self-gen) is competitive on FID (38.29) but still trails on LPIPS (0.3107 vs. 0.2855). ControlNet alone (FID 67.38) is outperformed by DPS+CSE(n=100) (FID 44.59), confirming that the CSE's value lies in providing a better initialization for the MAP optimization rather than standalone generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly challenges the dominant theoretical interpretation of Diffusion Posterior Sampling (DPS, Chung et al. 2022a), which has been explained as a conditional score estimator since its introduction and reinforced by subsequent works (Song et al. 2023c, Yu et al. 2023, Boys et al. 2023). It builds on the spherical concentration observation from DSG (Yang et al. 2024), which already showed a large lower bound on DPS's score estimation error, and repurposes that geometric insight to reframe DPS as a MAP procedure rather than a score-based sampler. The proposed DMAP algorithm combines multi-step gradient ascent with spherical projection (from Yang et al. 2024), while the lightweight CSE leverages ControlNet (Zhang et al. 2023) as a cheap initialization for the constrained optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reframe from conditional score estimation to MAP explains several previously puzzling observations in the DPS literature—why Adam helps despite being an adaptive optimizer, why sample diversity is low, and why a large score error lower bound does not prevent good performance. The practical contribution of a CSE trainable in 8 GPU hours on 100 images (or even self-generated data) is notable for reducing the barrier to conditional diffusion-based restoration. However, the strict theoretical derivation of MAP from original DPS remains unresolved (the authors acknowledge this explicitly), and the CSE's reliance on ControlNet limits its efficiency. Open questions include whether the MAP interpretation extends to PF-ODE solvers and whether the approach generalizes to flow-matching models.
