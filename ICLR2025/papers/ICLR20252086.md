## Problem

Existing graph representation learning methods face a fundamental trade-off: Riemannian GNNs (e.g., κGCN, QGCN) capture geometric curvature but are restricted to low-pass spectral filtering, degrading on heterophilic graphs, while spectral GNNs (e.g., GPRGNN, BernNet) access the full eigenspectrum but assume flat Euclidean geometry and ignore curvature. No prior method unifies both spectral and geometric signals, and none adapts the relevance of different curvature components to the downstream task.

## Input / Output

CUSP takes a weighted graph adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{n×n} and node feature matrix F ∈ R^{n×d_f} as input. The Ollivier-Ricci curvature is precomputed per-edge using a lazy random walk (δ=0.5) with the Sinkhorn algorithm to approximate the Wasserstein-1 distance, and the product manifold signature (e.g., H^{16}×S^{16}×E^{16}) is heuristically determined from the ORC distribution. The output is a set of node embeddings ζ ∈ P^{n×(d_M+d_C)} in a mixed-curvature product manifold (total dimension d_M=48 plus curvature encoding dimension d_C), which are fed to a classifier for node classification (F1) or a bilinear scorer for link prediction (AUC-ROC).

## Method

CUSP introduces three coupled components. First, the Cusp Laplacian replaces standard edge weights with curvature-based weights w̄_xy = exp(−1/(1−κ̃(x,y))) derived from a heat-flow argument where Ollivier-Ricci curvature encodes transport resistance between neighborhoods, yielding a positive-semidefinite operator whose eigenvalues remain in [0,2]. Second, Cusp Filtering extends GPRGNN to a product manifold of multiple constant-curvature spaces (hyperbolic, spherical, Euclidean) using κ-stereographic operations (Möbius addition, κ-matrix multiplication), and constructs a filter bank of L+1 GPR-based polynomial filters (including an identity filter) to capture distinct bands of the eigenspectrum—low-pass for homophilic and high-pass for heterophilic graphs. Third, Cusp Pooling performs hierarchical attention over component manifolds by lifting embeddings to a common tangent space, computing a centroid, and weighting each component by its relative position, while fusing a Bochner-theorem-based curvature positional encoding Φ_P(κ̃(x)) as a per-node signal that encodes local ORC into the product manifold via exponential maps.

## Results

CUSP achieves state-of-the-art performance on all eight benchmark datasets for both Node Classification (F1 Score) and Link Prediction (AUC-ROC). For NC, CUSP records F1 scores of 83.45 (Cora), 74.21 (Citeseer), 87.99 (PubMed), 70.23 (Chameleon), 43.91 (Actor), 52.98 (Squirrel), 94.03 (Texas), and 92.31 (Cornell), improving over the second-best baseline by 2.81%–5.32% per dataset. For LP, CUSP achieves up to a 5.11% increase in ROC-AUC over the best baseline. The average gain over the strongest spectral baseline (GPRGNN) is 5.58% and over the strongest mixed-curvature baseline (SelfMGNN) is 4.62%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CUSP outperforms all 12 baselines across all 8 datasets for both NC and LP. The strongest competing baselines are GPRGNN (spectral) and SelfMGNN (mixed-curvature Riemannian), with CUSP improving over them by 5.58% and 4.62% average F1 respectively. Riemannian baselines (HGCN, HGAT, κGCN, QGCN) perform competitively on homophilic datasets (e.g., HGCN reaches 88.11 on Texas) but degrade sharply on heterophilic ones (e.g., HGCN drops to 39.93 on Squirrel vs. CUSP's 52.98), confirming the low-pass filtering limitation (L1). Fixed-filter spectral methods (ChebyNet, BernNet) struggle most on heterophilic graphs, with BernNet scoring only 62.12 on Citeseer and 33.55 on Actor. FiGURe, which also uses a filter bank, is the closest spectral competitor (e.g., 67.18 on Chameleon vs. CUSP's 70.23), but still trails CUSP by 3–5% on most datasets, underscoring the benefit of combining spectral filtering with mixed-curvature geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

CUSP sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: mixed-curvature Riemannian GNNs (κGCN, QGCN, SelfMGNN) that capture geometric structure but are constrained to low-pass spectral behavior, and spectral GNNs (GPRGNN, ChebyNet, BernNet, FiGURe) that flexibly model the eigenspectrum but assume a flat Euclidean manifold. The paper departs from both by using GPRGNN as its spectral backbone, the κ-stereographic model for unified Riemannian operations across hyperbolic/spherical/Euclidean components, and Ollivier-Ricci curvature to define a curvature-aware Laplacian that modulates message-passing resistance. The product manifold framework from Gu et al. (2019) and the Bochner-theorem-based positional encoding from Xu et al. (2020) are repurposed as the geometric substrate and curvature encoding, respectively, but the key conceptual departure is treating geometry and spectrum as jointly parameterized rather than as independent inductive biases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first architecture to simultaneously learn spectral filters on a mixed-curvature product manifold, addressing three concrete limitations of prior work (low-pass bias, task-agnostic curvature, and lack of geometry-equivariance in spectral GNNs). The consistent 3–5% gains across both homophilic and heterophilic benchmarks, with the filter bank automatically adapting to low-pass or high-pass regimes, suggest the unification is not merely additive. However, open questions remain: the manifold signature is determined heuristically rather than learned end-to-end, ORC computation (even via Sinkhorn) is quadratic and may bottleneck on million-node graphs, and the Cusp Laplacian's contribution (1.57% ablation drop) is modest relative to the filter bank (3.08%), raising the question of whether the curvature-aware Laplacian is the right mechanism for injecting geometric signal into the spectral domain.
