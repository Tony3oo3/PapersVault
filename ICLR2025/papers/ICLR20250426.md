## Problem

Existing generalization bounds for deep neural networks trained by gradient descent rely on Rademacher complexity and scale unfavorably with network width (e.g., √(m/n) terms), require polynomial overparameterization, and fail to capture the role of initialization on sample complexity. Additionally, the NTK regime's small-step-size constraint leads to poor computational and sample complexities for structured distributions like XOR, where feature learning with large steps could yield dramatic improvements.

## Input / Output

The method takes n training pairs (x_i, y_i) with ||x_i|| ≤ 1 and binary labels y_i ∈ {±1}, a network with L hidden layers of width m and a 1-smooth, 1-Lipschitz activation σ (with σ(0)=0), and standard Gaussian initialization. It produces a trained classifier outputting ±1 predictions, along with explicit theoretical guarantees: a test loss bound of Õ(e^{O(L)}/γ²n + 1/γ²T) under NTK-separability with margin γ, and for the XOR task, a one-hidden-layer quadratic-activation network where only first-layer weights are trained (a_i ∈ {±1} fixed, Σa_i = 0) achieves near-perfect accuracy in log(d) SGD steps.

## Method

The core contribution is an algorithmic stability analysis for deep networks that exploits the Hessian structure of the objective along the gradient descent trajectory, yielding the first initialization-dependent, width-independent generalization bounds for multi-layer networks. The key mechanism is an induction argument showing that both ||w_t − w_0|| and ||w_t − w*|| remain O(||w* − w_0||) throughout GD iterates, which keeps the Hessian norm bounded and enables bounding the generalization gap by the cumulative training loss (Eq. 5) rather than by Rademacher complexity. Under NTK-separability with margin γ, this gives test error Õ(e^{O(L)}/γ²n + 1/γ²T) with only poly-logarithmic width m = Ω(poly(log(n)/γ)). For the XOR distribution, the paper escapes the NTK regime entirely by using a large step-size η = m with a constant-width quadratic-activation network, where signal grows as 2^t/√d while noise grows sub-exponentially, yielding perfect test accuracy after exactly ⌈log(d)⌉ SGD steps with Õ(d) samples.

## Results

Under NTK separability with margin γ, the paper achieves a test error rate of Õ(e^{O(L)}/γ²n + 1/γ²T) with width m = Ω(poly(log(n)/γ)), improving upon the prior bound of Õ(e^{O(L)}/γ²·√(m/n) + 1/γ²T) by eliminating the unfavorable √(m/n) width dependence. For the d-dimensional XOR distribution, a one-hidden-layer network with quadratic activation and constant width m reaches near-perfect test accuracy after only ⌈log(d)⌉ SGD steps with step-size η = m, using Õ(d) samples. On FashionMNIST and MNIST (n = 2000–12000, m = 250–600), the algorithmic-stability generalization bound (Eq. 11) closely tracks the empirical generalization gap across iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Rademacher-complexity-based bounds of Bartlett et al. (2017) and Chen et al. (2021), this paper's algorithmic-stability bound removes the √(m/n) width dependence, yielding a test loss of Õ(e^{O(L)}/γ²n) instead of Õ(e^{O(L)}/γ²·√(m/n)), while maintaining the same poly-logarithmic width condition. For the XOR distribution, the paper's large-step-size SGD (Theorem 2.4) achieves log(d) iterations with constant width m, substantially outperforming the NTK-regime results of Telgarsky (2023) and Taheri & Thrampoulidis (2024) which require d² iterations and Ω(poly(d)) width. Compared to Glasgow (2024), which uses ReLU activation and requires poly(log(d)) neurons and poly(log(d)) SGD steps, this paper's quadratic-activation network needs only constant width and log(d) steps while matching the linear sample complexity Õ(d). The empirical validation on FashionMNIST and MNIST confirms that the derived stability bound (Eq. 11) provides non-vacuous approximations of the actual generalization gap, whereas the Rademacher-based bounds would grow with √m.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the algorithmic stability framework for generalization (previously applied to two-layer networks by Taheri & Thrampoulidis 2024 and Richards & Rabbat 2021) to deep networks, leveraging Hessian structure results from Liu et al. (2020) and Banerjee et al. (2022) to control the network's behavior throughout gradient descent iterates. It departs from the dominant Rademacher-complexity approach (Bartlett et al. 2017; Chen et al. 2021) by deriving width-independent generalization bounds for multi-layer networks, a gap explicitly identified as open in Chen et al. (2021). The XOR result builds on the feature-learning line of work (Glasgow 2024; Telgarsky 2023) but achieves substantially better iteration and width complexities by using a large step-size with quadratic activation rather than ReLU.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The width-independent generalization bound for deep networks trained by GD is a first in the literature and provides a tighter, algorithm-dependent characterization of test error that captures the role of initialization. The XOR result demonstrates that the NTK regime's polynomial width and d² iteration requirements are not fundamental—constant-width networks with aggressive step-sizes can learn in log(d) steps—suggesting the kernel regime is a conservative lens. Open questions include whether the poly-logarithmic width conditions can be further tightened, whether the XOR analysis extends to noisy labels or multi-index tasks, and whether depth provides genuine sample-complexity benefits beyond what single-layer networks achieve.
