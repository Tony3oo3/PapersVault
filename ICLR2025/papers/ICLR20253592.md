## Problem

Traditional search data structures like skip lists and KD trees promote or split elements uniformly, ignoring the query distribution and thus missing large speedups on skewed (e.g., Zipfian) workloads. While learning-augmented binary search trees have been studied, they inherit BST weaknesses such as complex rebalancing under dynamic updates and inability to handle partially-ordered or high-dimensional data. This paper extends learning augmentation to skip lists and KD trees, providing near-optimal expected search time guarantees with robustness to erroneous predictions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of n dataset items (1D ordered keys for skip lists, d-dimensional points for KD trees) and a predicted frequency vector p = (p_1, …, p_n) from an oracle, where p_i ∈ [0,1] and Σp_i = 1. For KD trees, the oracle also covers the full search space [Δ]^d, and high-frequency query points not present in the dataset are explicitly incorporated into the tree to enable fast negative-query rejection. The output is a fully constructed skip list or KD tree whose expected query time is provably bounded by O(min(H(p), log n)) under a perfect oracle, where H(p) is the entropy of the predicted distribution.

## Method

For skip lists, the promotion rule replaces the traditional fixed-probability random promotion: item i is deterministically promoted to level ℓ if its predicted frequency satisfies p_i ≥ (2^ℓ − 1)/n, and otherwise is promoted with probability 1/2, ensuring high-frequency items occupy higher levels while preserving O(log n) total depth with high probability. For KD trees, the splitting rule searches across all dimensions for the axis and threshold that most closely balances the predicted query probability between the two child subtrees, rather than using median or variance-based splits; items with predicted probability below 1/n² are inserted via standard balanced KD tree construction to cap their depth at O(log n). The key theoretical result is that the expected search time is at most 2C + 2Σ f_i · min(log(1/p_i), log n), which is within a factor of two of the entropy lower bound H(f) for any skip list or KD tree, yielding O(1) expected search time under Zipfian distributions with exponent s > 1. Robustness is established by showing that under (α,β)-noisy oracles (p_i ≥ α·f_i − β), performance degrades by at most an additive constant, and under arbitrarily bad predictions the structure still achieves O(log n) matching oblivious baselines.

## Results

On synthetic Zipfian datasets, the learning-augmented skip list achieves average speed-up factors ranging from 1.33× (uniform) to 7.76× (α=1.75) over classic skip lists, with the improvement scaling with distribution skewness. On the real-world CAIDA internet traffic dataset (α≈1.37), the augmented skip list achieves a 1.86× query-time speed-up, closely matching the theoretical prediction of 1.81×. For KD trees, the learning-augmented construction reduces average query depth from ~13.3–13.4 (traditional) to ~10.8–11.0 (learned) across dimensions 1–40, and on the Stanford Lucy 3D point cloud achieves depth 15.1 vs. 17.6 for the traditional tree.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its learning-augmented skip lists and KD trees against their traditional (oblivious) counterparts, which use random promotion (skip lists) or standard balanced splitting (KD trees). The augmented skip list consistently outperforms the classic skip list across all tested distributions, with speed-ups increasing monotonically with Zipfian skewness (1.33× uniform → 7.76× at α=1.75). On real-world data, the speed-up is 1.86× on CAIDA (α≈1.37) and 1.45× on AOL (α≈0.75), with the smaller AOL gain explained by its near-uniform distribution. For KD trees, the learned construction reduces average query depth by roughly 18–20% over traditional KD trees across all dimensions (1–40) and all tested Zipfian parameters, and achieves a 14.4% depth reduction on the Stanford Lucy point cloud (15.1 vs. 17.6) and a 37% reduction on the neuron-activation dataset (14.9 vs. 23.7). Under noisy predictions, the augmented structures degrade gracefully, remaining on par with or slightly above the traditional baselines, confirming the theoretical robustness guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the learning-augmented data structure paradigm initiated by Lin et al. (2022) for binary search trees to two structurally different settings: skip lists (which avoid the rebalancing complexity of BSTs) and KD trees (which handle high-dimensional, partially-ordered data where BSTs are inapplicable). The construction for skip lists directly modifies Pugh's (1990) random promotion mechanism, replacing it with a deterministic frequency-threshold promotion plus a random fallback, while the KD tree construction introduces a probability-balanced splitting criterion across dimensions. The near-optimality lower bound is established via a reduction to Shannon's source coding theorem, connecting the search-time analysis to Huffman coding in a way that Lin et al.'s pivot-based BST analysis did not require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first provably near-optimal (within a factor of 2) learning-augmented search structures that are simultaneously robust to arbitrarily erroneous predictions, which is critical for practical deployment where ML oracles can degrade under distribution shift. For the extremely common Zipfian query distributions, the O(1) expected search time (vs. O(log n) for traditional structures) represents a fundamental improvement that scales with dataset size. Open questions include extending the analysis to fully dynamic settings with interleaved insertions and deletions, handling range queries and nearest-neighbor queries on KD trees with tighter guarantees, and developing principled oracle training procedures beyond the simple history-based frequency estimation used empirically.
