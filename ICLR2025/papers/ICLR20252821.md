## Problem

Existing unsupervised domain adaptation methods for semantic segmentation assume identical class labels across source and target domains, ignoring the label-level taxonomy gap (differences in granularity, naming, or novel categories) that is common in real-world deployment. While open-set DA can flag unknown classes and taxonomy-adaptive DA can handle differing label spaces, the former provides no detailed classification of new classes and the latter requires annotated target samples. The fully unsupervised setting where both image-level and label-level domain gaps must be bridged simultaneously remains largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled source domain (e.g., GTA with 19 classes), an unlabeled target domain (e.g., Mapillary Vistas with 45 classes or IDD with 24 classes), and a manually defined taxonomy mapping linking each source label to a subset of target labels. The taxonomy mapping is defined once per dataset pair and requires no pixel-level annotation. Output is a pixel-wise segmentation mask in the target label space, which can be used directly for inference or as pseudo-labels to train a standalone segmentation model (e.g., Mask2Former) that achieves faster inference (0.3s vs. 64.1s per image) while maintaining or improving accuracy.

## Method

DynAlign operates in three stages: (1) a UDA model (HRDA) is trained on the labeled source and unlabeled target domains via self-training to produce coarse predictions in the source label space; (2) GPT-4 constructs the taxonomy mapping and generates context-aware textual descriptions for each target label (e.g., 'bridge' → {'road bridge', 'footbridge', 'pedestrian bridge'}), while SAM produces fine-grained mask proposals with precise boundaries; (3) a knowledge fusion mechanism reassigns labels to each SAM mask by computing CLIP similarity between multi-scale visual features (local bounding-box features combined with class-dependent padded global features, weighted by cosine similarity) and context-aware text features of the candidate target classes retrieved via the taxonomy mapping. The initial source label for each mask is determined by majority vote of the UDA prediction within that region, constraining the candidate target class set. A confidence threshold of 0.5 gates whether a reassignment is applied, preserving UDA predictions where foundation model evidence is weak.

## Results

On Mapillary Vistas (GTA→Mapillary, 19→45 classes), DynAlign achieves 53.0% mIoU and 36.7% mACC overall, with 72.6% mIoU on known classes and 39.9% mIoU on unknown classes. On IDD (GTA→IDD, 19→24 classes), it reaches 57.7% mIoU and 41.7% mACC overall, with 66.8% mIoU on known and 34.3% mIoU on unknown classes. When pseudo-labels from DynAlign are used to train a Mask2Former on the target domain, performance further improves to 56.2% mACC and 38.9% mIoU on Mapillary Vistas, while reducing inference time from 64.1 s to 0.3 s per image.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynAlign outperforms all baselines on both datasets across every metric. Against the strongest naive combination baseline (HRDA + OwlVIT-SAM), DynAlign gains +12.8 mIoU on Mapillary Vistas (53.0 vs. 40.2) and +1.8 mIoU on IDD (57.7 vs. 55.9), with the largest margin on unknown classes: +10.4 mIoU on Mapillary (39.9 vs. 29.5) and +7.0 mIoU on IDD (34.3 vs. 27.3). Compared to pure open-vocabulary methods (Grounded-SAM, OwlVIT-SAM), DynAlign improves overall mIoU by 19.9 and 23.4 points on Mapillary respectively, demonstrating that domain-specific UDA knowledge is essential even when strong foundation models are available. On known classes, DynAlign (72.6% mIoU on Mapillary) approaches but does not fully match standalone HRDA (75.8%), indicating a small trade-off from the taxonomy reassignment process, while simultaneously achieving 39.9% mIoU on unknown classes that HRDA cannot predict at all.

## Relation to Prior Work

DynAlign sits at the intersection of three research threads: UDA for semantic segmentation (HRDA, DAFormer), open-vocabulary segmentation via foundation models (Grounded-SAM, OWL-ViT), and taxonomy-adaptive domain adaptation (TACS, Fan et al.). It departs from prior taxonomy-adaptive DA methods, which require few-shot labeled target samples to learn novel classes, by instead leveraging LLM-generated taxonomy mappings and CLIP-based knowledge fusion to operate fully unsupervised. The framework also departs from open-vocabulary segmentation, which relies solely on foundation model priors and lacks domain-specific precision, by first establishing in-domain predictions through a UDA model before refining them with SAM mask proposals and CLIP feature matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formulation of fully unsupervised taxonomy-adaptive domain adaptation, addressing a practical gap where target taxonomies evolve (finer granularity, new categories, renamed labels) without any target annotations. The key practical advantage is that when the target label set changes, only the taxonomy mapping needs updating—no retraining—enabling rapid adaptation to new taxonomies. Limitations include the high inference cost (64.1s per image vs. 1.9s for HRDA alone), the current focus on street scene understanding, and the residual need for human intervention in refining GPT-4-generated taxonomy mappings. Open questions include whether the framework extends to other domains (medical, satellite), whether taxonomy mapping can be fully automated, and whether the computational overhead can be reduced through distillation or lighter foundation models.
