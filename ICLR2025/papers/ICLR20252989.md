## Problem

Context-augmented generation (RAG, ICL) requires encoding multiple retrieved contexts into KV states, but sequential encoding of all contexts as one long sequence creates a severe prefilling bottleneck (e.g., 17s for 128K context vs. 6s for 256-token generation). Parallel encoding—encoding each context independently and reusing positional ranges—eliminates this bottleneck but causes significant accuracy drops (up to 49% on reasoning tasks) due to misalignments in attention weight distributions between independently encoded and sequentially encoded contexts. Prior trainable fixes (CEPE, Block-Attention) recover performance only on easy tasks and require expensive instruction-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a user query and N independently pre-computed KV state tensors (one per context chunk), each encoded separately with the same positional range, plus a short shared prefix (system prompt or newline characters). Contexts are loaded from cache at inference time without re-encoding, enabling cost-free insertion, deletion, or swapping of contexts. The output is standard autoregressive text generation, where the query attends to all cached context KV states simultaneously within a single attention computation. The approach is compatible with Flash Attention by splitting context and non-context KV states into separate attention calls and merging outputs.

## Method

APE is a training-free, three-step inference-time modification to the attention mechanism that aligns parallel encoding's attention distribution with sequential encoding's. (1) A shared prefix is prepended to all contexts so that the anomalous initial-token KV states (caused by the attention sink) appear only once rather than being duplicated across every context. (2) The attention temperature T < 1 is applied exclusively to context tokens, sharpening the softmax distribution to counteract the uniform spreading of attention that occurs when neighboring KV states are duplicated across parallel contexts. (3) A scaling factor S < 1 is applied to the LogSumExp of context attention scores to offset the magnitude inflation introduced by the lower temperature. The final attention output is computed hierarchically: each context's KV states are attended with temperature T, the non-context (prefix + query) states are attended normally, and the two groups are merged via a softmax over their respective LogSumExp values (with S scaling the context group). Hyperparameters T and S are tuned via greedy search on a validation set, and the entire procedure adds less than 10% overhead to inference time.

## Results

On ChatRAG-Bench (RAG), APE retains 98% of sequential encoding performance with only 0.51%–1.14% drops across three retrievers, while on LongBench it achieves a 5.6% average gain over base models and 3.3% over sequential RAG by processing more and longer contexts. On ICL tasks, APE outperforms parallel encoding by 15.4% on GSM8K, 4.7% on TriviaQA, and 3.5% on MMLU, while preserving 93% of full-shot sequential encoding accuracy. In efficiency, APE achieves up to 4.5× end-to-end speedup and 28× reduction in prefilling time for 128K-token contexts compared to sequential encoding on an H100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PCW (Parallel Context Windows), APE consistently wins across all four models on LongBench RAG (e.g., 39.62 vs. 36.73 avg on LLaMA-3-8B) and on all ICL tasks (15.4% on GSM8K, 4.7% on TriviaQA, 3.5% on MMLU), confirming that the three alignment steps recover the performance gap of naive parallel encoding. Against CEPED (CEPE-Distilled), APE outperforms on reasoning-heavy tasks like GSM8K and MMLU where CEPED degrades significantly, while CEPED only marginally beats sequential encoding on 2/3 RAG QA tasks. Against long-context LLM methods (LLMLingua2, StreamingLLM, Self-Extend, Long-context FT), APE achieves the highest average on LongBench (38.11 vs. 35.96 for Self-Extend, 27.21 for Long-context FT, 26.29 for LLMLingua2, 23.76 for StreamingLLM). Against sequential encoding with the same inputs, APE incurs only a 0.51%–1.14% drop on ChatRAG-Bench and preserves 93% accuracy on ICL, while gaining 3.3% on LongBench by enabling retrieval of more and longer contexts. Against MInference, APE achieves 4.5× end-to-end speedup versus MInference's 2.2×, and reduces 128K prefilling time by 28× versus MInference, with linear scaling overhead under 10% of inference time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the parallel encoding framework introduced by Parallel Context Windows (PCW, Ratner et al., 2022), which proposed encoding contexts independently and reusing positional slots to enable pre-caching and position reuse. Prior trainable fixes—Block-Attention (Sun et al., 2024), CEPE (Yen et al., 2024), and FocusLLM (Li et al., 2024)—attempted to recover performance through fine-tuning or new encoder architectures, but APE departs from this line by being entirely training-free, instead applying three inference-time attention modifications (shared prefix, temperature, scaling factor) grounded in the attention sink phenomenon identified by StreamingLLM (Xiao et al., 2023). The hierarchical attention formulation also draws on Flash Attention's LogSumExp-based merging to enable efficient implementation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

APE addresses a critical practical bottleneck in deployed CAG systems: the quadratic prefilling cost of long concatenated contexts. The 4.5× end-to-end speedup and 28× prefilling reduction for 128K contexts, combined with near-sequential accuracy (98% RAG, 93% ICL) and compatibility with Flash Attention/VLLM, make this immediately deployable without retraining. Key limitations include sensitivity to the temperature and scaling hyperparameters (requiring per-task tuning) and an inability to model inter-context relationships, which caps performance on complex multi-hop reasoning. An open question is whether the alignment can be automated at runtime for varying context lengths and quantities without a validation set.
