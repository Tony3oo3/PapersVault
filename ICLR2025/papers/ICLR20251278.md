## Problem

Existing self-play RLHF methods face a fundamental trade-off: Mirror Descent-based approaches (e.g., SPO) only guarantee average-iterate convergence, requiring storage of multiple historical policies for inference, while methods that achieve last-iterate convergence (e.g., NLHF) converge to the Nash equilibrium of a KL-regularized game rather than the original preference game, misaligning with true human preferences. No prior method achieves last-iterate convergence to the NE of the original game using a single model.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt x and a trained preference model P(y1 > y2 | x) that scores pairwise response quality without assuming transitivity. It produces a single aligned LLM policy π* that is the Nash equilibrium of the two-player constant-sum preference game, eliminating the need to store or average over multiple historical policies. The preference model is trained via cross-entropy on annotated preference pairs, and the policy is optimized autoregressively at the token level using sequential KL divergence between policies.

## Method

MPO adapts Magnetic Mirror Descent (MMD) to RLHF by introducing a periodically updated 'magnetic' reference policy that attracts the current policy toward the Nash equilibrium. The key theoretical insight is a two-stage convergence: MMD achieves linear last-iterate convergence (rate O((1/(1+ηα))^k)) to the NE of each KL-regularized sub-game, and by iteratively updating the magnet to the most recent regularized NE, the sequence of regularized NEs provably converges to the NE of the original game (Theorem 3.4). In practice, the algorithm leverages game symmetry so a single player plays against its own iterates, updates the reference policy every T_k iterations, uses REINFORCE with a ReMax baseline for advantage estimation, and enforces trust-region constraints via multiple SGD steps with an annealed stepsize. The KL regularization is applied as a soft loss term (MPO) or as a hard reward transformation (MPO-RT), with the former empirically outperforming the latter.

## Results

On safety alignment (cost model, lower is better), MPO reduces the PKU-SafeRLHF cost from 6.37 (SFT) to -11.38 after three self-play iterations, and HH-Harmless from 11.33 to -3.86. On GPT-4o evaluation, MPO Iter.3 achieves a 79.5% win rate versus 30.1% for MPO without self-play. On general capability (Open LLM Leaderboard v2), MPO Iter.4 improves the average score from 33.16 (SFT) to 33.95, with gains in IFEval (41.63→42.97), BBH (48.54→51.38), and GPQA (28.95→30.54).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MPO against PPO and Iterative DPO (Best-of-8 and Best-of-4) on the MixEval-Hard benchmark using Llama-3-8B. MPO significantly outperforms all three baselines, with the gap widening across self-play iterations. Iterative DPO generally performs better than PPO, which the authors attribute to complementary effects of on-policy sampling and negative gradients, but both are surpassed by MPO. The Iterative DPO results are highly sensitive to the Best-of-N sampling strategy: larger N improves scores but increases computational overhead. On safety alignment, MPO without self-play (a single-round RLHF-like setting) achieves only -4.18 on PKU-SafeRLHF, far below MPO Iter.3 (-11.38), demonstrating that self-play is essential rather than merely beneficial. The ablation also shows that fixing the reference policy (converging only to the regularized NE) performs substantially worse than periodically updating it, confirming the importance of the magnetic mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

MPO adapts Magnetic Mirror Descent (MMD, Sokota et al. 2022) to the RLHF setting, introducing a periodically updated 'magnetic' reference policy that guides convergence to the Nash equilibrium of the original preference game rather than a KL-regularized variant. It departs from SPO (Swamy et al. 2024), which uses standard Mirror Descent and only guarantees average-iterate convergence requiring storage of all historical policies, and from NLHF (Munos et al. 2023), which achieves last-iterate convergence but only to the NE of a regularized game via a first-order approximation of the reference policy. The game-theoretic formulation of RLHF as a two-player constant-sum game is inherited from Munos et al. (2023) and Swamy et al. (2024); MPO's contribution is the convergence mechanism and its practical adaptation to token-level LLM training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MPO eliminates the need to store and average over multiple historical policies during inference—a significant practical cost reduction for LLM deployment—while providing a linear convergence rate to the true NE of the original game. The empirical finding that omitting self-play causes severe performance degradation (30.1% win rate vs. 51.8% with one self-play iteration) reframes self-play from a performance booster to a necessity for preventing overfitting to the opponent, which has broader implications for how alignment pipelines are designed. Open questions include whether the fixed update interval T_k can be made adaptive, how the method scales to much larger models (experiments use 2B and 8B parameters), and whether the modest gains on specialized tasks like Math Hard indicate a fundamental limitation of preference-based self-play for reasoning capabilities.
