## Problem

Existing long-context benchmarks suffer from performance saturation on simple retrieval tasks, evaluation limited to sub-100k contexts (an order of magnitude below frontier model limits), and a lack of granular decomposition of which variables drive performance degradation. This paper asks whether LLMs can follow multi-step chains of linked information ('threads') through near-million-token haystacks, and whether they can track multiple such threads simultaneously without significant performance loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252496/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each experiment presents a JSON object of random UUID key-value pairs (the 'haystack') at context sizes from 1k to 630k LLaMA 3.1 tokens, where all distractor pairs are drawn from the same distribution as the relevant ones. The query specifies either a single key, a set of keys, a character-based condition, or a thread start key; for threading tasks, the model must iteratively resolve a value that equals another key until reaching a terminal value. Outputs are exact-match evaluated 32-character UUID strings (or lists thereof), parsed via an LLM reformatting step before scoring.

## Method

The paper introduces a hierarchy of six synthetic retrieval tasks of increasing difficulty: Single Needle, Multiple Needles, Conditional Needles, Threading (following a chain where each value equals the next key, up to 25 steps in forward, backward, or random directions), Multi-Threading (simultaneously resolving 2–25 concurrent threads), and Branched Threading (threads with decoy branches at each step). By using synthetic UUID pairs, the authors gain fine-grained control over context length, thread length, direction, and needle placement, enabling clean ablations. They evaluate 17 LLMs (closed- and open-source) in a zero-shot, greedy-decoding setting and introduce a task-specific effective context length metric: for each task, they interpolate accuracy on a dense 2D grid of the two key experimental variables, extract the 75%-accuracy contour, and report the maximum context size (measured in characters to be tokenizer-agnostic) at which reliable retrieval is still possible. A natural-language ablation using sentences from Gibbon's *Decline and Fall of the Roman Empire* confirms that threading and multi-threading findings transfer beyond the synthetic UUID setting.

## Results

Across 17 LLMs evaluated on five long-context retrieval tasks (Single Needle, Multiple Needles, Conditional Needles, Threading, Multi-threading), performance degrades substantially as context length increases from 1.2k to 630k LLaMA 3.1 tokens. At the shortest context (1.2k), GPT-4o leads with 93.2% overall accuracy, followed by Claude 3.5 Sonnet (91.5%) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (87.7%). At 128k tokens, Gemini 1.5 Pro is best at 65.2%, while GPT-4o drops to 60.9% and Claude 3.5 Sonnet to 50.6%. The proposed effective context length metric reveals that most models' usable context is far below their advertised limit—for example, Gemini 1.5 Pro's effective limit for threading is 0% of its 2M-token context, and GPT-4o's is only 3% (7k chars out of 214k).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks 17 LLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-4o mini, Gemini 1.5 Pro/Flash, Gemini 1.0 Pro, Claude 3.5/3 Sonnet/Haiku, Reka Core/Flash, Jamba 1.5 Large/Mini, LLaMA 3.1 8b/70b/405b, Mistral Large/Nemo) on identical synthetic retrieval tasks. GPT-4o dominates at short contexts (93.2% at 1.2k) but Gemini 1.5 Pro overtakes it at 64k+ (69.2% vs. 64.9%), while Claude 3.5 Sonnet is the strongest performer in the 2.5k–32k range (88.7% at 2.5k, 75.9% at 32k). All closed-source models consistently outperform open-source counterparts; LLaMA 3.1 405b (the strongest open model) trails GPT-4o by 16.5 points at 1.2k and by 41.3 points at 128k. A key finding is that models are remarkably thread-safe: increasing concurrent threads from 2 to 5 causes negligible performance drop, with Claude 3.5 Sonnet showing no degradation even at 25 threads. However, in natural-language multi-threading (using Gibbon's *Decline and Fall of the Roman Empire*), performance degrades much more sharply than in the synthetic setting, suggesting the thread-safety advantage is partly an artifact of the uniform UUID distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the needle-in-a-haystack paradigm (Kamradt, 2023) and the position-bias findings of Liu et al. (2024) by introducing multi-step threading tasks—following chains of linked key-value pairs through the context—and multi-threading variants that require tracking several such chains simultaneously. It departs from prior long-context benchmarks (LongBench, RULER, Counting-stars) in three ways: using synthetic UUID-based data for fine-grained control over independent variables, pushing evaluations to 630k tokens (beyond the sub-100k range of most prior work), and decomposing performance along specific axes (thread direction, thread length, number of concurrent threads) rather than reporting only aggregate scores. The effective context length metric builds on the contour-based analysis approach but is explicitly tokenization-agnostic, addressing a gap the authors identify in cross-model comparisons.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that most models' effective context length is a small fraction of their advertised limit (e.g., Gemini 1.5 Pro at ~13% for single-needle retrieval) has direct practical implications for system design and model selection in long-context applications. The thread-safety result—that models can follow multiple threads concurrently without significant degradation—suggests that the bottleneck in long-context reasoning is not parallel tracking but rather the depth and position of individual retrieval steps. Key limitations include the reliance on synthetic data (which may not capture domain-specific behavior), limited experimental repeats for expensive models, and the fact that natural-language multi-threading proved substantially harder than the synthetic version, leaving open the question of how well these findings transfer to real-world document retrieval.
