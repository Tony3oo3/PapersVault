## Problem

Sampling from pre-trained diffusion models requires many neural function evaluations (NFEs), and while ODE solvers can reduce this count, the choice of time discretization critically affects sample quality. No universal discretization schedule works well across all dataset-model-solver combinations, and existing optimization-based approaches either require expensive network retraining (distillation) or rely on handcrafted heuristics that are suboptimal, particularly in the low-NFE regime (≤10 steps).

## Input / Output

LD3 takes a frozen pre-trained denoising network, a high-NFE teacher ODE solver, and a small set (~100) of random Gaussian noise samples as input, and produces a compact vector of learned time discretization parameters (≤20 values) plus optional decoupled time-step offsets. These parameters are then plugged into any differentiable ODE solver (DPM-Solver++, Uni_PC, iPNDM, Euler) to generate images (32×32 to 512×512), point clouds, or molecular docking predictions with far fewer NFEs. The method requires no retraining of the denoising network and trains in 5–40 minutes on a single GPU.

## Method

LD3 frames discretization learning as minimizing the global truncation error by matching a student ODE solver's output to a high-NFE teacher's output (hard teacher forcing). Because the student has too few parameters to exactly replicate the teacher for every initial condition, the paper introduces a surrogate 'soft teacher forcing' objective that allows the student to start from a nearby point x'_T within an r-ball of the teacher's input x_T, making the problem tractable. A theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) upper-bounds the KL divergence between teacher and student distributions in terms of r, dimensionality, and a Jacobian-determinant term. In practice, time steps are parameterized via cumulative softmax to enforce monotonicity, LPIPS serves as the distance metric, projected SGD enforces the ball constraint on x'_T, and a rematerialization trick keeps memory nearly constant with respect to NFE. Decoupled time-step inputs to the denoising network (following Li et al., 2023) further improve performance by mitigating exposure bias.

## Results

On pixel-space DPMs (EDM backbone), LD3 with iPNDM achieves FID 2.38 at 10 NFE on CIFAR-10, 2.27 on AFHQv2, and 3.25 on FFHQ, approaching the teacher FID of 2.08–2.54. At the challenging 4-NFE regime, iPNDM[LD3] reduces CIFAR-10 FID from 35.04 (Time LogSNR baseline) to 9.31, a 3.7× improvement. On latent-space models, Uni_PC[LD3] achieves FID 9.89 at 4 NFE on ImageNet-256 (vs. 20.01 for the baseline), and on text-to-image, LD3 improves InstaFlow's FID from 22.56 to 15.49 at just 2 NFE. Training requires only 5–40 minutes on a single GPU with ~100 noise-sample pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two closest discretization-optimization baselines, LD3 dominates at low NFE: on CIFAR-10 at 4 NFE, iPNDM[LD3] achieves FID 9.31 vs. 15.63 (GITS) and 28.09 (DMN); on AFHQv2 at 4 NFE, LD3 scores 9.96 vs. 12.89 (GITS) and 33.15 (DMN). At 10 NFE, LD3 still leads on CIFAR-10 (2.38 vs. 2.78 GITS, 3.31 DMN) and AFHQv2 (2.27 vs. 3.26 GITS, 3.22 DMN), though DMN edges out LD3 on CIFAR-10 with Uni_PC (2.45 vs. 2.87). Against AYS (Sabour et al., 2024), LD3 is competitive: AYS achieves FID 2.98 vs. LD3's 3.38 on CIFAR-10 at 10 NFE, but LD3 wins decisively on FFHQ (2.89 vs. 5.43) and on ImageNet-64 across all NFE settings (e.g., 19.81 vs. 21.42 at 25 NFE). Compared to distillation methods, LD3 matches Consistency Distillation's FID 2.93 at NFE=2 with FID 2.90 at NFE=8, but trains in <6 minutes on 1 GPU versus 1 day on 8 A100s (240× faster). Against Rectified Flow (FID 2.58 at 127 NFE), LD3 achieves FID 2.38 at 10 NFE with the same DDPM++ backbone. On text-to-image, LD3 improves InstaFlow from FID 22.56 to 15.49 at 2 NFE, and on Stable Diffusion v1.5, LD3 outperforms the default Time Uniform discretization at all tested NFEs (e.g., 13.07 vs. 14.41 at 5 NFE). Against handcrafted schedules (Time Uniform, Quadratic, EDM, LogSNR), LD3 is dramatically better at low NFE: on CIFAR-10 at 4 NFE, LD3 scores 9.31 vs. 35.04 (LogSNR), 29.78 (EDM), 139.72 (Quadratic), and 266.26 (Uniform).

## Relation to Prior Work

LD3 builds on the ODE formulation of diffusion models (Song et al., 2020b) and the differentiable ODE-solver framework of Watson et al. (2022), from which it adopts the rematerialization trick for memory-efficient backpropagation. It departs from prior time-discretization optimization methods (AYS, GITS, DMN) by directly targeting the global truncation error rather than local errors or derived upper bounds, and from distillation-based approaches (Consistency Models, Progressive Distillation, Rectified Flow) by requiring no retraining of the denoising network—only ~20 scalar parameters are learned. The soft teacher forcing surrogate, which relaxes the requirement that student and teacher share the same initial condition, is the paper's key conceptual departure from the standard teacher-student distillation paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LD3 offers a plug-and-play, minutes-long training procedure on a single GPU that consistently improves FID scores across 7 pre-trained models and multiple solvers, making it immediately practical for users who cannot afford distillation retraining. The theoretical KL-divergence bound for the soft teacher forcing objective provides a principled guarantee that the relaxed optimization remains close to the true distributional matching objective. Key limitations include the need to retrain for each NFE value, the requirement of a differentiable solver, and the finding that optimized time steps are solver-specific (Table 8), which limits portability. Open questions include whether a single learned schedule could generalize across solvers or NFE values, and whether the approach extends to non-differentiable or stochastic (SDE) samplers.
