## Problem

Personalizing pretrained text-to-image diffusion models with only a few target images requires balancing alignment to the new concept against preservation of text editability. Existing fine-tuning methods (LoRA, DreamBooth, SVDiff) directly modify the full generation space, inevitably causing either poor concept alignment or overfitting to the small training set. No prior approach explicitly controls the rank of model parameters to constrain the denoising trajectory into a smaller, well-balanced target space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253485/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For single-subject generation, PaRa takes a text prompt containing a placeholder token [V] plus 5–6 target images of the subject and produces 500+ personalized images at SDXL 1.0 resolution using the DDIM sampler (η=0). For single-image editing, it takes one training image and a modified text prompt, generating edited images without requiring DDIM noise inversion. The hyperparameter r (typically 2–8) controls the trade-off: larger r increases reconstruction fidelity while smaller r preserves text editability. A rank boundary γ (set to 1/40) caps the per-layer reduction to prevent model collapse.

## Method

PaRa introduces a single learnable matrix B ∈ R^{d×r} per linear layer (r ≪ d), initialized to zero and finetuned on the target images. A QR decomposition B = QR yields an orthonormal basis Q, and the effective weight becomes W_reduce = W₀ − QQ^T W₀, which projects out the components of W₀ along Q, provably reducing the output column space from d to (d−r) dimensions. Unlike LoRA, which adds a low-rank update W₀ + αBA to expand or shift the generation space, PaRa subtracts a projection to shrink it, making the operation idempotent and enabling stable combination of multiple individually trained PaRa weights via sequential application. For convolutional layers, the kernel is reshaped to a 2D matrix before applying the same rank-reduction procedure. PaRa is also compatible with existing LoRA weights by applying the rank reduction first and then adding the LoRA update, preserving both the restricted generation space and the LoRA-learned style.

## Results

On the Dreambooth dataset (8 subjects, 500 generated images each), PaRa achieves the highest CLIP image similarity scores in 7 out of 8 subjects, with PaRa r=8 reaching 0.9467 on CAT and 0.8971 on DUCKTOY, compared to LoRA r=8's best of 0.8583 (CAT) and 0.8361 (DUCKTOY). PaRa requires only 2× fewer learnable parameters than LoRA (e.g., 13 MB vs. 33 MB at r=16 for the ExCA subset) and converges in ~200 steps versus 1000 steps for baselines. In a 209-participant human preference survey, PaRa is preferred over LoRA in 91–97% of single-subject comparisons and 78–90% of multi-subject comparisons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PaRa consistently outperforms LoRA (the primary baseline) across all 8 Dreambooth subjects at both r=4 and r=8, with average CLIP image similarity gains of roughly 0.05–0.10 over LoRA r=8 (e.g., 0.8971 vs. 0.8361 on DUCKTOY, 0.8916 vs. 0.7873 on WOLF_PLUSHIE), while using half the parameters. Against SVDiff, PaRa wins in 7 of 8 subjects, with the largest margin on WOLF_PLUSHIE (0.8916 vs. 0.7035). PaRa also surpasses DreamBooth in 7 of 8 cases and Textual Inversion in all 8, with the gap over Textual Inversion being the most pronounced (up to 0.32 on MONSTER_TOY). The closest competitors are OFT and LyCoris: PaRa r=8 edges out OFT on 6 of 8 subjects and LyCoris on 5 of 8, though OFT slightly leads on CAT (0.9024 vs. 0.9467 is actually PaRa winning; OFT leads on no subject at r=8). In multi-subject generation, PaRa's combination of individually trained weights produces coherent multi-concept images, whereas LoRA tends to generate only the primary concept or a hybrid entity, and in single-image editing PaRa achieves significantly higher average SSIM than SVDiff without requiring DDIM inversion.

## Relation to Prior Work

PaRa departs from the dominant parameter-efficient fine-tuning paradigm for T2I diffusion models, which typically adds low-rank updates (LoRA) or adjusts singular value scales (SVDiff) to the pretrained weights. Instead, PaRa uses QR decomposition of a learnable matrix to explicitly subtract components from the original weight matrix, reducing the rank of the layer output space rather than expanding or rescaling it. This reframing is motivated by the observation that personalization toward a specific concept (e.g., a particular art style) implies a restricted generation space, not an enlarged one. The approach also borrows the FSGAN-style reshaping trick for applying rank reduction to convolutional kernels, and is designed to be algebraically compatible with existing LoRA weights in the public model zoo.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PaRa offers a practically attractive alternative to LoRA for T2I personalization: half the storage cost, faster convergence (~200 steps vs. 1000), and the ability to combine individually trained PaRa weights for multi-subject generation without auxiliary data augmentation or additional training. The single-image editing capability without noise inversion is a notable practical simplification. However, the authors acknowledge a fundamental limitation: PaRa only reduces the output space, so personalization tasks that require expanding beyond the pretrained distribution (e.g., generating entirely novel object categories) may still fail. Whether this limitation diminishes as base models grow more universal remains an open question, and the algebraic treatment of simultaneous space extension and reduction is flagged as future work.
