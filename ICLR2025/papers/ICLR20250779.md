## Problem

Video object counting suffers from a dynamic fore-background imbalance caused by the spatial sparsity of target objects, which existing methods address inadequately—single-frame approaches discard temporal information, while video methods still struggle to focus on sparse foreground regions during optimization. Additionally, no large-scale video bird counting dataset existed for natural-scenario applications such as migratory bird protection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250779/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of temporally adjacent video frames (I_t, I_{t-1}) along with their corresponding ground-truth density maps (D_t, D_{t-1}) during training, where both modalities are patchified into tokens and fed jointly into the encoder. During inference, density map tokens are fully masked (N_D^ret = 0), so only image tokens are input and the model must reconstruct the complete density map from video alone. Input resolutions vary by dataset (320×320 on FDST, 448×640 on Mall), and the final output is a fused density map D_fuse whose pixel-wise sum yields the object count estimate.

## Method

E-MAC integrates three components around a pre-trained MultiMAE ViT-B encoder. (1) Density-Embedded Masked Modeling (DEMO) treats the density map as an auxiliary modality alongside the image, jointly tokenizing both and using a Transformer autoencoder to reconstruct the masked density map from the retained multi-modal tokens, thereby driving density regression implicitly through self-representation learning. (2) Spatial Adaptive Masking (SAM) replaces random masking with a density-guided strategy: image tokens are sorted by the target count in their corresponding density-map patches, and the top N_I^ret tokens are retained with probability (1−P) to focus on foreground, while with probability P the sort is reversed to retain background, balancing the fore-background optimization. (3) Temporal Collaborative Fusion (TCF) uses a pre-trained PWC-Net optical flow to warp the previous frame's predicted density map into the current frame's coordinate system, then applies cross-attention between the warped and current density maps to extract temporal residuals that are added to the current prediction. Training minimizes a combined loss of MSE on the fused and current density maps, MSE on the optical-flow-warped image, and a total-variation regularizer on the fused output.

## Results

E-MAC achieves state-of-the-art or near-state-of-the-art performance across all four video object counting benchmarks. On FDST, it attains MAE 1.29 and RMSE 1.69, outperforming the previous best video method STGN (1.38/1.82) by 7%. On the newly introduced DroneBird dataset, E-MAC achieves MAE 38.72 and RMSE 42.92, surpassing the best prior video method STGN (92.38/124.67) by 58% in MAE and 66% in RMSE. On VSCrowd, it records the best MAE (6.0) and competitive RMSE (10.3) among all video methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E-MAC outperforms all 23 compared baselines (7 image-based and 6 video-based methods with full results) on every dataset where it is evaluated. Against the strongest video baseline STGN, E-MAC improves MAE by 4% on Mall (1.35 vs. 1.53), 7% on FDST (1.29 vs. 1.38), 38% on VSCrowd (6.0 vs. 9.6), and 58% on DroneBird (38.72 vs. 92.38). Against the best image-based method PET, E-MAC achieves lower MAE on all four datasets (e.g., 1.29 vs. 1.73 on FDST, 38.72 vs. 45.10 on DroneBird), confirming that temporal modeling provides substantial gains over single-frame approaches. On VSCrowd, E-MAC achieves the best MAE (6.0) but a slightly higher RMSE (10.3) than GNANet (10.2), making it the only metric where it does not lead. The largest absolute gains appear on DroneBird, where the drone-perspective bird counting scenario with small, sparse, fast-moving targets amplifies the benefits of E-MAC's spatial adaptive masking and temporal fusion over prior methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the multi-modal masked autoencoder paradigm from MultiMAE (Bachmann et al., 2022) to video object counting by treating density maps as an auxiliary modality alongside images, creating a density-embedded self-representation learning framework (DEMO). It departs from prior video counting methods that rely on LSTM, 3D convolutions, or simple optical flow warping by introducing a Transformer-based autoencoder with spatial adaptive masking guided by density distributions. The temporal collaborative fusion module builds on the optical flow alignment idea common in video counting (PWC-Net) but applies it to predicted density maps with cross-attention residuals rather than raw features, and the overall framework is the first to transfer a pre-trained vision foundation model (ViT-B from MultiMAE) into a video counting pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DroneBird dataset fills a genuine gap as the first video bird counting benchmark from a drone perspective, providing both point and trajectory annotations for migratory bird protection research. The finding that task-specific architectural design (E-MAC) yields 43–47% improvement over simply swapping pre-training weights (6%) suggests that generic foundation model pre-training is insufficient for counting tasks without task-aware modifications. Open questions include whether the density-embedded masking strategy generalizes to other sparse-object counting domains (e.g., vehicles, animals in the wild), how the method scales to longer video sequences beyond two-frame fusion, and whether the reliance on ground-truth density maps during training limits applicability to scenarios where only point annotations are available.
