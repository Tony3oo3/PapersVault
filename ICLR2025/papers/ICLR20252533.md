## Problem

While TV distance computation has been studied for product distributions and Bayes nets, two natural questions remained open: (1) whether equivalence (TV distance = 0) between mixtures of product distributions can be decided efficiently, and (2) whether TV distance between Ising models admits any efficient multiplicative approximation. The paper addresses both, providing a polynomial-time algorithm for the former and a conditional hardness result for the latter.

## Input / Output

For equivalence checking, the input consists of two mixtures each specified by k weights and k product distributions over an alphabet Σ on n variables (input size Ω(kn|Σ|)); the algorithm runs in O(nk⁴|Σ|⁴) time and outputs either a certificate of equality or a witness assignment x ∈ Σⁱ (i ≤ n) where the i-th marginals differ. For the hardness result, the input is a pair of Ising models given by interaction weights {w_{i,j}} and field parameters {h_i}, and the output is the conditional statement that no FPRAS exists for estimating d_TV(P, Q) unless NP ⊆ RP.

## Method

The equivalence algorithm (Theorem 1) reduces the problem to maintaining a basis B_j of coefficient vectors for linear equations encoding marginal equality at each coordinate j. By induction, it shows that checking the (j+1)-th marginal only requires evaluating equations on the at-most-2k basis vectors from B_j (rather than all |Σ|^j assignments), then constructs B_{j+1} by scaling each basis vector's coordinates by the next variable's probabilities and extracting independent vectors via Gaussian elimination. The hardness proof (Theorem 2) establishes an approximation-preserving reduction chain: estimating d_TV between two Ising models reduces to estimating an atomic marginal Pr_P[x_k = ±1], which in turn reduces to estimating the partition function. The key construction introduces a dummy variable x_0 with field h_0 → −∞ (making x_0 independent of all other spins) and a strong coupling δ·x_0·x_k in a second model Q, so that d_TV(P_0, Q) multiplicatively approximates Pr_P[x_k = 1] when h_0 and δ are chosen as poly(n, 1/ε). This, combined with Jerrum & Sinclair's result that no FPRAS exists for the Ising partition function unless NP ⊆ RP, yields the hardness of TV distance estimation.

## Results

The paper establishes two main theoretical results. First, Theorem 1 provides a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm for checking equivalence between two mixtures of k product distributions over n variables with alphabet Σ, with running time O(nk⁴|Σ|⁴). The algorithm uses an inductive basis-construction argument over a vector space of dimension at most 2k, and if the distributions are not equivalent, it outputs a witness assignment where the marginals differ. Second, Theorem 2 proves that unless NP ⊆ RP, no FPRAS exists for estimating the TV distance between arbitrary Ising models, via an approximation-preserving reduction from the partition function (leveraging Jerrum & Sinclair's 1993 hardness result).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The algorithmic result (Theorem 1) is novel: no prior polynomial-time equivalence-checking algorithm existed for mixtures of product distributions, and the paper's contribution is the connection between equivalence checking and basis construction over a vector space. The hardness result (Theorem 2) builds directly on Jerrum & Sinclair (1993), who showed no FPRAS exists for the Ising partition function unless NP ⊆ RP; the paper's contribution is the intermediate reduction chain showing that TV distance estimation is at least as hard as partition function estimation (via atomic marginals). The paper positions itself relative to prior work on TV distance approximation (Bhattacharyya et al. 2020 for additive approximation of ferromagnetic Ising models, Feng et al. 2023/2024 for product distributions) by establishing the first multiplicative hardness result for Ising models, complementing the existing positive algorithmic results for restricted settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

The paper extends the line of work on computational aspects of total variation distance (Sahai & Vadhan 2003; Bhattacharyya et al. 2020, 2023, 2024) by addressing two previously unexplored special cases. For the equivalence-checking result, it repurposes classical Gaussian elimination and basis construction over vector spaces to handle the non-unique parameterization of mixtures of product distributions—a gap left by prior work that could only handle single product distributions trivially. For the hardness result, it builds directly on Jerrum & Sinclair's (1993) no-FPRAS theorem for Ising partition functions, constructing an approximation-preserving reduction through atomic marginals to show that multiplicative TV distance estimation inherits the same intractability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The equivalence algorithm is notable for its simplicity (accessible to undergraduates) and polynomial running time O(nk⁴|Σ|⁴), providing a clean tool for a problem that is #P-complete in the general product-distribution setting. The hardness result closes a gap by establishing that even bounded-error randomized multiplicative approximation of TV distance between Ising models is intractable, complementing prior additive-approximation hardness. Open questions include whether the equivalence technique extends to mixtures of Bayes nets (where even single-net equivalence is NP-hard) and whether the Ising hardness generalizes to other undirected graphical models like factor graphs.
