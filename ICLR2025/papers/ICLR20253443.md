## Problem

Transformer-based LLMs face a fundamental bottleneck in length extrapolation: positional embeddings become out-of-distribution beyond the pre-training window, self-attention entropy grows logarithmically with context length, and models lose effective awareness of critical contextual information. Existing solutions like StreamingLLM sacrifice global context awareness, InfLLM introduces bias from chunk-wise representation, and RoPE-scaling methods (NTK, PI) impose explicit extrapolation upper bounds, leaving no training-free approach that simultaneously satisfies all three conditions for truly infinite context.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a long text token sequence (arbitrarily long, up to 4M tokens demonstrated) and the accumulated KV cache from all prior tokens, which is stored without positional encoding. At each decoding step, it produces a single generated token along with a selected subset of KV cache segments (global + top-k middle + local) whose total length is bounded by the model's pre-training attention window (e.g., 8192 for LLaMA3-8B-8K). Unlike standard implementations where RoPE is baked into the cached K vectors, ReAttention defers position embedding to after selection, so the cached KV is purely semantic.

## Method

ReAttention inserts a position-agnostic top-k attention step before the standard self-attention at every inference step. The KV cache is partitioned into a global prefix (32 tokens), a middle region, and a local suffix (4096 tokens); the current query is dotted against the middle K vectors without any positional encoding, and the top-k=4 positions (each expanded to a span of 32 neighboring tokens, yielding k'=127 selected positions) are concatenated between the global and local segments. Positional embedding (RoPE) is then applied sequentially to the concatenated cache, guaranteeing it never exceeds the pre-training window and thus never goes OOD. The key insight, validated via attention-distribution visualization, is that the position-agnostic dot product actually localizes critical information more sharply than the position-aware one, filtering out irrelevant noise. A custom Triton kernel fuses the score computation and top-k selection into a single GPU pass (analogous to FlashAttention's IO-aware design), keeping the added overhead negligible while reducing peak memory compared to the PyTorch baseline.

## Results

On LongBench, ReAttention achieves average scores of 35.03 (LLaMA3-8B-8K), 38.63 (LLaMA3.1-8B-128K), and 39.43 (LLaMA3.2-3B-128K), matching or nearly matching full-attention baselines (35.48, 38.79, 40.76 respectively) while outperforming StreamingLLM and InfLLM across all models. On InfiniteBench at 128K context, ReAttention achieves an average of 22.98 with LLaMA3.1-8B-Instruct-128K, surpassing full attention (18.09), StreamingLLM (20.68), and InfLLM (20.91). In Needle-In-A-Haystack tests, ReAttention extends LLaMA3.1-8B to at least 1M tokens and expands LLaMA3.2-3B-chat context by 128× to 4M tokens without any additional training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReAttention consistently outperforms StreamingLLM across all 9 evaluated models on LongBench, with the largest gap on LLaMA3.2-3B-128K (39.43 vs. 35.08 avg), confirming that full-context top-k selection recovers information that the fixed Lambda-shaped window discards. Against InfLLM, ReAttention also wins on all models, with particularly large margins on LLaMA3.2-3B-128K (39.43 vs. 28.21), attributed to InfLLM's chunk-wise representation bias and ReRoPE-style position embedding mismatch. On InfiniteBench at 128K, ReAttention surpasses both full attention and InfLLM (22.98 vs. 18.09 and 20.91 for LLaMA3.1-8B-Instruct-128K), while Dynamic NTK fails to produce stable outputs beyond its extrapolation upper bound. On the RULER benchmark, however, ReAttention (90.10 at 8K, 66.57 at 16K) underperforms Dynamic NTK (91.47 at 8K, 88.13 at 16K), particularly on the MultiKey3 subtask involving chaotic synthetic text where the linear manifold structure of natural language is absent. Overall, ReAttention matches or exceeds full attention on natural-language long-context tasks while enabling unbounded context length, but struggles on synthetic tasks requiring precise positional disambiguation across multiple overlapping manifolds.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReAttention sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the Lambda-shaped attention window of StreamingLLM/LM-Infinite, which retains only global and local KV segments; (2) the pre-attention retrieval paradigm of InfLLM and LongHeads, which extracts critical middle-context information before self-attention; and (3) the IO-aware kernel fusion strategy of FlashAttention, adapted here via a custom Triton kernel for top-k selection. The key conceptual departure is making the cache selection step position-agnostic (no RoPE applied to cached K/V), which the paper argues both avoids OOD position embeddings and, counterintuitively, yields sharper attention localization than position-aware scoring. Unlike RoPE-scaling methods (NTK, PI, YARN) that impose an explicit extrapolation ceiling, ReAttention keeps the per-step attention scope finite and within the pre-training window, thereby sidestepping the length-extrapolation problem entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a training-free, plug-and-play method that works across LLaMA, Mistral, Qwen, and InternLM families, ReAttention offers a practical path to deploying long-context LLMs without retraining, with the 128× expansion (32K→4M) on Qwen2-1B being the largest reported amplification without additional training. The honest negative result on RULER MultiKey3—where the linear-manifold structure of natural text breaks down for synthetic chaotic patterns—highlights a fundamental limitation: position-agnostic top-k selection relies on the assumption that critical information lies along a coherent semantic manifold, an assumption that fails for non-linguistic, repetitive key-value stacks. Open questions include whether hybrid strategies (e.g., position-aware selection for structured/synthetic content) can close this gap, and whether the method's reliance on a finite attention scope constrains tasks requiring global aggregation over the full context.
