## Problem

Pretrained discrete diffusion models can generate natural-like biological sequences, but practical design tasks (e.g., protein stability, enhancer activity) require optimizing downstream reward objectives while preserving naturalness. While RL-based fine-tuning has been developed for continuous diffusion models, discrete diffusion models are built on continuous-time Markov chains (CTMCs) rather than Brownian motion, making trajectories non-differentiable and introducing unique algorithmic and theoretical challenges that prior work has not addressed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained masked discrete diffusion model (e.g., a ProteinMPNN-architecture inverse folding model conditioned on a 3D backbone structure, or a CNN-based enhancer sequence model), a differentiable reward oracle (Enformer for HepG2 enhancer activity, or a ProteinMPNN-based stability predictor), and a fully masked initial state. It produces generated sequences—200-bp regulatory DNA or amino acid sequences—by running the fine-tuned CTMC from t=0 to t=T. For protein design, generation is always conditioned on a specific backbone conformation, and outputs are evaluated via ESMFold-predicted structures (scRMSD) and predicted ΔΔG stability.

## Method

DRAKES formulates reward maximization as an RL problem over CTMC trajectories, with a KL divergence penalty against the pretrained generator to prevent over-optimization. The core technical contribution is making the originally non-differentiable categorical transitions in the CTMC differentiable via the Gumbel-Softmax trick: at each time step, the discrete state is replaced by a relaxed one-hot vector obtained by applying softmax to log-probabilities plus Gumbel noise, preserving gradient flow through the entire trajectory. The algorithm alternates between sampling relaxed trajectories and performing gradient ascent on the empirical reward minus the KL penalty, with practical modifications including straight-through Gumbel-Softmax at the final step and truncated backpropagation from intermediate time steps. A theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) shows the optimal fine-tuned distribution is proportional to exp(r/α)·p_pre, and the paper rederives Nisonoff et al.'s classifier guidance formula as a special case via the Doob transform, unifying RL fine-tuning and guidance under the CTMC framework.

## Results

On regulatory DNA enhancer design, DRAKES achieves 92.5% ATAC accessibility accuracy and 0.911 JASPAR motif correlation while maintaining a pretrained-model log-likelihood of -264, outperforming all guidance-based baselines on naturalness metrics. On protein inverse folding, DRAKES achieves a 78.6% overall success rate (Pred-ΔΔG > 0 AND scRMSD < 2 Å), nearly doubling the best baseline (SMC at 63.6%) and dramatically outperforming classifier-free guidance (1.3%). The KL regularization term is critical: without it, DRAKES achieves 100% positive predicted stability but only 34.1% successful folding (scRMSD < 2), confirming over-optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRAKES outperforms all four baselines on the composite success rate for protein inverse folding (78.6% vs. 63.6% SMC, 62.9% TDS, 34.7% CG, 1.3% CFG), primarily by achieving the highest predicted stability (1.095 median ΔΔG) while maintaining a low scRMSD (0.918 Å) comparable to the pretrained model. For DNA enhancer design, DRAKES matches CFG on ATAC accessibility (92.5% vs. 92.1%) but substantially surpasses it on 3-mer correlation (0.887 vs. 0.746) and JASPAR motif correlation (0.911 vs. 0.864), indicating more natural-like sequences. Classifier guidance (CG) performs nearly identically to the pretrained model on both tasks, suggesting its Taylor-approximation-based guidance is ineffective for discrete state spaces. SMC and TDS improve stability over the pretrained model but sacrifice some folding accuracy relative to DRAKES. CFG fails catastrophically on protein design (1.3% success) due to the limited number of distinct backbone structures in Megascale (~300), confirming the paper's argument that conditioning on reward labels is suboptimal when labeled data is scarce.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-based fine-tuning paradigm established for continuous diffusion models (DPOK, DDPO, Uehara et al. 2024b) to the discrete setting, where trajectories are governed by continuous-time Markov chains rather than Brownian motion. The key algorithmic innovation is adapting the Gumbel-Softmax trick (Jang et al., 2017) to make CTMC trajectories differentiable, enabling direct reward backpropagation through the full generation process. The theoretical framework (HJB equations for CTMC, Feynman-Kac formula) also rederives the classifier guidance formula of Nisonoff et al. (2024) as a special case, establishing a unifying Doob-transform perspective that connects RL fine-tuning and guidance-based inference for discrete diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first RL-based fine-tuning method for discrete diffusion models, filling a clear gap between the well-developed continuous-space literature and the rapidly growing discrete diffusion community in biology and NLP. The KL regularization is shown to be essential (not merely helpful) for preventing reward over-optimization, a practical concern that limits naive reward maximization. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer sequences, whether non-differentiable reward models can be handled (the authors suggest PPO as a fallback), and whether wet-lab validation will confirm the in silico gains. The theoretical connection to classifier guidance also suggests that fine-tuning and guidance may be interchangeable in ideal settings, though practical differences (inference cost, data requirements) remain significant.
