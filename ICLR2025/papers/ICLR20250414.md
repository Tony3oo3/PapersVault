## Problem

Standard Mixup samples interpolation coefficients from a fixed distribution regardless of how similar or dissimilar the paired data points are, which can produce synthetic samples that fall outside the original class manifolds (manifold mismatch) and degrade calibration. The paper shows that the probability of assigning a noisy label to a mixed sample is lower-bounded by the distance between the source manifolds, yet prior work either restricts which pairs to mix (losing diversity) or applies uniform interpolation to all pairs (risking manifold intrusion).

## Input / Output

For classification, the method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 ImageNet) and outputs a class label with a calibrated confidence score; for regression, it takes tabular or time-series features and outputs a predicted value with an MC-Dropout uncertainty estimate. The key operational detail is that pairwise distances are computed within each mini-batch: in embedding space (encoder outputs) for classification, or directly between target labels for regression, so no external distance oracle or precomputed kernel matrix is required.

## Method

Similarity Kernel Mixup (SK Mixup) replaces the fixed Beta(α,α) sampling of interpolation coefficients with a two-step warping procedure. First, a base coefficient λ is drawn from Beta(1,1) (uniform); second, a similarity kernel τ(x, i, σ; τ_max, τ_std) = τ_max · exp(−(d̄_n − 1)/(2τ_std²)) is computed from the batch-normalized L2 distance between the pair, and λ is warped through ω_τ(λ) = I^{-1}_{λ}(τ,τ) to yield the effective coefficient. By Proposition 3.3, the warped coefficient follows Beta(τ,τ), so similar points (small d̄_n) receive τ > 1 and thus stronger interpolation, while dissimilar points receive τ < 1 and weaker mixing. This preserves full diversity (every pair in the batch can still be mixed) while dynamically controlling interpolation strength, and the framework composes with RegMixup to further boost accuracy. The approach requires only one batch in memory and a single forward pass for distance computation, making it ~1.5× faster than MIT-A and ~3× faster than RankMixup.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet101, SK Mixup achieves 95.04% accuracy with 2.20 ECE, while SK RegMixup reaches 96.02% accuracy with 2.36 ECE, outperforming all calibration-driven Mixup baselines. On CIFAR-100, SK Mixup attains 78.20% accuracy with 2.23 ECE (vs. 5.77 for Mixup and 6.72 for RegMixup), and on Tiny-ImageNet, 67.60% accuracy with 3.04 ECE. Under covariate shift (CIFAR-100-C), SK RegMixup achieves 57.98% accuracy with 6.89 ECE, roughly 7 points better ECE than RegMixup. For regression, SK Mixup achieves the best MAPE across all three datasets (e.g., 1.609 on Airfoil vs. 1.706 for C-Mixup) while also improving UCE and ENCE calibration metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SK Mixup consistently outperforms standard Mixup (Zhang et al., 2018) on calibration: on CIFAR-100, ECE drops from 5.77 to 2.23 while accuracy improves from 77.50% to 78.20%. Against RegMixup (Pinto et al., 2022), SK Mixup achieves substantially better ECE (2.23 vs. 6.72 on CIFAR-100), while SK RegMixup further boosts accuracy to 79.57% (vs. 78.85%) at the cost of slightly higher ECE (4.32 vs. 6.72). Compared to MIT-A (Wang et al., 2023), SK Mixup reduces ECE from 9.55 to 2.23 on CIFAR-100 with higher accuracy (78.20% vs. 77.23%), and is ~1.5× faster in training time. Against RankMixup (Noh et al., 2023), SK Mixup wins on both accuracy and calibration across all three datasets (e.g., 78.20% vs. 77.27% accuracy, 2.23 vs. 11.69 ECE on CIFAR-100) while being ~3× faster. Among explicit calibration methods (MMCE, ECP, CRL, CPC), SK Mixup achieves the best joint accuracy-calibration trade-off, e.g., 95.04% accuracy with 2.20 ECE on CIFAR-10 vs. 95.04%/3.74 for CRL and 95.36%/4.78 for CPC. For regression, SK Mixup outperforms C-Mixup (Yao et al., 2022a) on all three datasets in both MAPE (e.g., 1.609 vs. 1.706 on Airfoil) and UCE (93.8 vs. 111.2), confirming the framework's flexibility beyond classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mixup framework (Zhang et al., 2018) by introducing a similarity kernel that dynamically warps the Beta distribution of interpolation coefficients based on pairwise distance, building on the warping-function formalism. It departs from prior calibration-driven Mixup methods (MIT-A, RankMixup, RegMixup) that either restrict which pairs to mix or apply fixed interpolation strengths, by instead preserving full pair diversity while modulating interpolation strength per-pair. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 3.2) provides a Wasserstein-distance lower bound on noisy-label probability, connecting the manifold mismatch phenomenon to a formal guarantee that motivates the distance-dependent design. The framework is also shown to be composable with RegMixup's regularization formulation, yielding SK RegMixup.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled and computationally efficient mechanism to resolve the well-known accuracy-calibration trade-off in Mixup, with the practical advantage of requiring only a single batch in memory and being 1.5–3× faster than competing calibration-driven methods. The theoretical result cleanly formalizes why careless mixing hurts calibration, giving practitioners a concrete criterion (distance-dependent interpolation) rather than ad-hoc heuristics. Open questions include the interaction with temperature scaling (which can reorder calibration results), sensitivity to the two additional hyperparameters (τ_max, τ_std), and extension to structured prediction tasks like semantic segmentation or depth estimation where the notion of 'distance' is less straightforward.
