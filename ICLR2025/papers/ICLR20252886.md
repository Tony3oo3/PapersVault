## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models struggle to accurately compose multiple semantic concepts, especially in the presence of negations and large conjunctions, making fine-grained control over generated content unreliable. Existing conditioning approaches (text prompts, spatial maps, latent codes) either lack interpretability or do not provide a principled measure of which semantic factors are most relevant to a given task. The paper asks whether a compressed, interpretable representation built from user-defined queries can both enable reliable multi-concept generation and give users an intuitive interface for editing.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference image (or a user-crafted sequence of answers) and a predefined set of binary task-specific queries (e.g., 58 for bedrooms, 44 for churches, 40 facial attributes for CelebA) as input, and produces a compressed representation D of length L ≤ |Q| consisting of the L most informative query-answer pairs, plus generated images at the dataset resolution (512×512 for LSUN, 64×64 for CLEVR/CelebA). At inference time, the user can flip individual answers in D to edit specific attributes while preserving the rest of the scene. For location-based queries (MNIST, CLEVR patches), the input is a partial image with only some patches visible, and the output is a full reconstructed image.

## Method

InCoDe comprises three modules: a Querier that greedily selects the next most informative query via Variational Information Pursuit (VIP), a QuerySet Answerer that predicts the full query-answer set from a partial history, and a diffusion-based Generator that synthesizes images conditioned on the query-answer representation. The Querier and QuerySet Answerer are trained jointly by minimizing the KL divergence between the true posterior p(Q(X)|X) and the model's prediction given the selected query and history, with query sampling strategies (random then biased) to handle the combinatorial space. The Generator is a pre-trained Stable Diffusion V1-4 UNet fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 32) and a lightweight query embedder that maps each query-answer pair into a single null-text token dimension; during training, a random mask determines which queries are active, and cross-attention layers inject the embedded conditions at multiple UNet hierarchies. This structured conditioning bypasses the compositional failures of free-form text prompts, and the information-theoretic ordering of queries provides a natural k-similarity metric between images.

## Results

On the compositional generation task (Table 2), InCoDe achieves 85% accuracy and 84% F1 on LSUN Bedroom, and 75% accuracy and 72% F1 on LSUN Churches, substantially outperforming all text-based baselines. For the information acquisition strategy (Figure 8), InCoDe's Querier consistently selects queries that yield higher prediction accuracy and F1 than Random, DT-IC, and TopK-IC baselines, with the largest gains on the more complex LSUN datasets. In location-based reconstruction experiments (Figure 9), InCoDe outperforms Spiral and Cross baselines on MNIST and CLEVR in both MSE and LPIPS, though the Random baseline slightly surpasses InCoDe for fewer than 20 queries on CelebA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InCoDe decisively outperforms all three text-to-image baselines on compositional generation. Against Stable Diffusion V1 (Rombach et al., 2022), InCoDe gains +24 points in accuracy on LSUN Bedroom (0.85 vs. 0.61) and +18 on LSUN Churches (0.75 vs. 0.57). Against the larger SD XL (Podell et al., 2023), the margin is even wider: +27 accuracy on Bedroom (0.85 vs. 0.58) and +16 on Churches (0.75 vs. 0.59), showing that simply scaling the base model does not resolve multi-concept composition failures. Structured Diffusion (Feng et al., 2023), which explicitly targets attribute binding, actually performs worst among the baselines (0.52/0.51 accuracy), suggesting its training-free guidance degrades under large conjunctions. For location-based queries, InCoDe beats the Spiral and Cross baselines from Rangrej et al. (2022) on MNIST and CLEVR in both MSE and LPIPS, but the Random baseline slightly surpasses InCoDe for fewer than 20 queries on CelebA, which the authors attribute to the Generator being pre-trained on random histories and CelebA's information being spread across the full image.

## Relation to Prior Work

InCoDe builds directly on Variational Information Pursuit (VIP) from Chattopadhyay et al. (2022, 2023), repurposing the IP framework—which was originally designed for interpretable classification—into a generative setting where the 'target variable' becomes the full query-answer set Q(X) rather than a class label. For the generative module, it adapts Stable Diffusion (Rombach et al., 2022) via LoRA fine-tuning (Hu et al., 2022), introducing a novel query-embedder that maps discrete query-answer pairs into the text-token conditioning space of the UNet. The paper departs from the dominant text-prompt conditioning paradigm (and spatial control signals like depth maps or bounding boxes) by conditioning on a compressed, interpretable-by-design set of binary questions, positioning itself against both text-based compositional failures (Feng et al., 2023) and post-hoc interpretability approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InCoDe offers a principled, information-theoretic mechanism for compressing image semantics into a small number of human-readable queries, enabling targeted semantic editing by flipping individual answers—a capability that text-prompt-based systems handle poorly. The key limitation is the requirement for a task-specific, human-defined query set, which trades generality for controllability and interpretability; the authors acknowledge this and raise the open question of automatically discovering query sets without direct supervision. The introduced LSUN Bedroom and Churches datasets with binary query annotations fill a practical gap for domain-specific generation research. Broader implications include whether the IP-based conditioning paradigm can be extended to open-vocabulary or continuous-valued queries without the combinatorial explosion that motivates the current binary-query design.
