## Problem

Polysemanticity in LLMs—where individual neurons encode multiple unrelated concepts—hinders mechanistic interpretability and safe knowledge manipulation. Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) attempt to disentangle these features but introduce post-hoc reconstruction errors that degrade model performance, while conventional Mixture-of-Experts architectures are limited to small expert counts, specific layers, and linear parameter growth that prevents scaling to the granularity needed for monosemanticity.

## Input / Output

MONET takes standard text token sequences (or image tokens in the VisionMONET variant) and produces generated text, but its distinguishing output is the set of interpretable expert routing scores (g_hij) that reveal which of 262,144 monosemantic experts per layer are activated for each token. These routing scores enable direct knowledge manipulation: selectively zeroing out experts associated with a target domain, programming language, or toxicity correlation produces modified text with the targeted knowledge surgically removed while preserving general performance.

## Method

MONET replaces standalone expert networks with product key composition: each expert is decomposed into two complementary components (bottom/top linear layers in Horizontal Decomposition, or left/right segments in Vertical Decomposition), so N = 262,144 unique experts are dynamically composed from only √N = 512 weight choices per group, reducing parameter scaling from O(N) to O(√N). Routing factorizes into two independent softmax operations over each group (g_hij = g_hi¹ · g_hj²), and dense routing with precomputed einsum operations enables efficient batched computation across all experts. Training integrates sparse dictionary learning directly into end-to-end pretraining with two auxiliary losses—a KL-divergence uniformity loss for balanced expert utilization and an ambiguity loss that encourages high-confidence single-expert assignment per token—promoting the monosemantic specialization that post-hoc SAEs cannot achieve.

## Results

MONET-VD 4.1B achieves an average 0-shot score of 0.511 and 5-shot score of 0.550 across 8 open-ended benchmarks, approaching the LLAMA 3.8B baseline (0.520 / 0.567) with comparable total parameters. At the 1.4B scale, MONET-VD reaches 0.478 (0-shot) and 0.510 (5-shot), within 0.6–0.8 points of LLAMA 1.3B (0.484 / 0.518). In knowledge unlearning experiments, MONET achieves targeted domain removal with minimal collateral damage (e.g., −30.6% pass@100 on Python while other languages drop only −1.1%), and purging just 4.1% of toxic experts reduces expected maximum toxicity from 0.795 to 0.657 on RealToxicityPrompts with no measurable performance loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the dense LLAMA baseline trained under identical conditions, MONET-VD trails by 0.6–1.7 points in average 0-shot accuracy (e.g., 0.511 vs. 0.520 at ~4B scale), while MONET-HD lags slightly more; vertical decomposition consistently outperforms horizontal across all scales. Compared to OLMoE (1.3B active / 6.9B total params), MONET-VD 4.1B matches or exceeds its 0-shot average (0.511 vs. 0.502) with fewer total parameters, and critically, MONET's experts exhibit clear domain specialization (2.2% of experts per domain) whereas OLMoE shows no detectable expert specialization in any MMLU domain due to evenly distributed routing scores. Against Gemma Scope SAEs (65K features on Gemma 2 2B), MONET demonstrates a decisive advantage in knowledge manipulation: SAE-based feature suppression causes catastrophic performance collapse (0.571 → 0.473 for MLP SAE, → 0.337 for residual SAE in 0-shot average), while MONET's expert purging achieves targeted unlearning with <2% collateral damage on unrelated domains. In toxicity mitigation, MONET's expert purging reduces expected maximum toxicity from 0.795 to 0.657 (threshold 0.1, 4.1% experts removed) with zero measurable drop in helpfulness, a capability the SAE baselines cannot replicate without severe performance degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

MONET extends the product key retrieval mechanism from Lample et al. (2019) and the PEER architecture (He, 2024) by replacing standalone expert storage with a compositional decomposition (horizontal or vertical) of expert weight matrices, reducing parameter complexity from O(N) to O(√N). It departs from the post-hoc Sparse Autoencoder paradigm (Bricken et al., 2023; Lieberum et al., 2024) by integrating sparse dictionary learning directly into end-to-end SMoE pretraining, following the dense-routing computational strategy of Pan et al. (2024) and Puigcerver et al. (2023). The architecture is positioned as a solution to the polysemanticity problem that limits both standard MoE (OLMoE, Switch Transformers) and SAE-based interpretability, scaling to 262,144 experts per layer where prior approaches were confined to specific layers or suffered linear parameter growth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MONET demonstrates that architectural monosemanticity (achieved through massive expert counts with O(√N) parameter scaling) enables surgical knowledge manipulation—domain unlearning, language-specific code removal, and toxicity mitigation—without the collateral performance degradation that plagues SAE-based or neuron-suppression approaches. The finding that standard MoE (OLMoE) exhibits no detectable domain specialization while MONET cleanly separates knowledge across 262K experts validates the superposition hypothesis at scale and suggests a practical path toward transparent, editable LLMs. Open questions include whether the basic expert-selection criteria (routing skewness, Pearson correlation) generalize to more nuanced knowledge types, whether quantitative evaluation of the self-explained expert framework is feasible, and whether the approach scales beyond 4.1B parameters and 100B training tokens without the small performance gap to dense LLaMA baselines widening.
