## Problem

Pretrained GNNs are typically locked to a specific task level (graph, node, or edge) and domain, making knowledge transfer across heterogeneous tasks and domains impractical. Existing transfer methods either assume task-level consistency or suffer severe negative transfer when the source and target domains differ substantially in structure, feature space, or topology. No unified framework existed to enable arbitrary cross-task, cross-domain transfer in GNNs without reconfiguring the model architecture or graph structure.

## Input / Output

The framework accepts node features and adjacency matrices of arbitrary dimensionality (ranging from 2-dim molecular atom features to 1433-dim text embeddings) or 3D point clouds, and produces task-appropriate outputs: binary/multi-class graph labels, per-node class labels, or point cloud class labels. An Input Bridge (learnable or non-learnable linear adapters) projects heterogeneous input dimensions into the backbone's expected feature space, while an Output Bridge (pooling + linear head for graph tasks, direct linear head for node tasks) reshapes the tuned embeddings into the correct output format for each downstream task.

## Method

GraphBridge is a two-stage framework: a pre-training stage (using existing methods like GraphCL or SimGRACE) produces a frozen GNN backbone, and a tuning stage attaches an Input Bridge, a side-tuning module, and an Output Bridge. The key innovation is two graph side-tuning architectures: GSST (Graph Scaff Side-Tuning) fuses a randomly initialized MLP side network layer-wise with the frozen backbone via learnable alpha blending, avoiding expensive graph convolutions during tuning; GMST (Graph Merge Side-Tuning) further introduces a backup model with identical architecture but random initialization, merging it with the pretrained backbone layer-wise via learnable scaling before the base-side fusion, thereby diluting source-domain bias that causes negative transfer in large domain gaps. Only 5–20% of total parameters are tunable, and backpropagation is restricted to the side network, yielding both parameter and memory efficiency.

## Results

On the hard Graph2Node transfer task, GMST achieves 74.8% test accuracy on Cora (GCN, GraphCL pre-training), outperforming fine-tuning (69.2%) by 5.6% and MetaFP (64.3%) by 10.5%. On the medium Node2Node task, GMST reaches 72.6% on PubMed (GIN, GraphCL), surpassing scratch training (65.8%) by 6.8%. On the easy Graph2Graph task, GSST achieves 70.9% average ROC-AUC across 8 molecular datasets with GraphCL pre-training, exceeding fine-tuning (70.3%) by 0.6% while using only 5–20% of tunable parameters. In the extension Graph2PtCld task, GSST reaches 84.7% on ModelNet10 (GIN, graph-level pre-training), outperforming fine-tuning (83.9%) and scratch training (87.9% is the upper bound).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MetaGP and MetaFP (Jing et al., 2023, CVPR 2023), which are prompt-based tuning methods, GSST/GMST consistently outperform across all four task difficulties. On the hard Graph2Node task, GMST exceeds MetaFP by 10.5% on Cora (74.8% vs. 64.3%, GraphCL/GCN) and by 16.6% on CiteSeer (61.9% vs. 50.4%), while MetaFP suffers severe negative transfer (e.g., 42.6% on Flickr vs. 53.07% scratch). Against Adapter-GNN (Li et al., 2023), which is limited to GIN backbones, GMST achieves substantially higher accuracy on hard tasks (e.g., 74.8% vs. 57.8% on Cora under GraphCL pre-training) and also extends to GCN and GAT backbones where Adapter-GNN is not applicable. On the easy Graph2Graph task, GSST (70.9% avg ROC-AUC) narrowly beats Adapter (70.7%) and fine-tuning (70.3%), while MetaGP and MetaFP underperform fine-tuning by 1.2% and 0.4% respectively. Overall, the proposed methods win or tie on the majority of dataset-backbone combinations, with the largest margins appearing in the hard and medium transfer scenarios where negative transfer is most severe.

## Relation to Prior Work

GraphBridge extends the side-tuning paradigm from CV (Zhang et al., 2020; LST by Sung et al., 2022a) to the graph domain, adapting the layer-wise base-side fusion architecture to GNN backbones. It departs from prior GNN transfer work (Adapter-GNN, MetaGP/MetaFP, graph domain adaptation methods) by addressing arbitrary cross-level transfer (graph→node, node→graph, graph→pointcloud) rather than same-level adaptation, and introduces the GMST mechanism of merging a frozen pre-trained model with a randomly-initialized backup to dilute source-domain bias. The framework is agnostic to the pre-training method (GraphCL, SimGRACE) and backbone (GCN, GAT, GIN), positioning itself as a universal tuning layer atop existing GNN pre-training pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Task Pyramid taxonomy (Easy/Medium/Hard/Extension) provides the community with a structured way to evaluate and compare graph transfer methods across difficulty levels, filling a gap where prior work only addressed same-level transfer. The finding that 5–20% tunable parameters suffice for competitive performance has practical value for resource-constrained settings. However, the framework does not consistently surpass full fine-tuning, the point-cloud extension is preliminary, and the evaluation is limited to 16 datasets with relatively small molecular graphs. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger, more heterogeneous domains, whether the GMST random-merge strategy generalizes beyond the tested architectures, and how it interacts with emerging foundation-model-style GNN pre-training.
