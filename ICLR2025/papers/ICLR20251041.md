## Problem

The standard composition-based privacy analysis of DP-SGD assumes the adversary has access to all intermediate iterates, yet in practice only the final model is released. This creates a large and unexplained gap between theoretical upper bounds and empirical privacy auditing lower bounds for the last-iterate-only setting. The paper asks whether sharper, more realistic privacy guarantees can be obtained when the adversary sees only the final model.

## Input / Output

The heuristic takes standard DP-SGD hyperparameters (number of iterations T, Poisson subsampling probability q, noise multiplier σ) and a target privacy failure probability δ, and outputs a privacy loss bound ε for the last-iterate-only release. Unlike the composition-based analysis, the resulting ε converges to a constant as T grows rather than increasing without bound. The computation reduces to evaluating the hockey-stick divergence between a Binomial-Gaussian mixture P = Binomial(T,q) + N(0, σ²T) and a pure Gaussian Q = N(0, σ²T), which is efficiently computable via binary search over the privacy loss function.

## Method

The core insight is that under the assumption of linear loss functions, DP-SGD's final iterate reduces to a 1D problem where the worst-case output distributions are P = Binomial(T,q) + N(0, σ²T) (canary included) versus Q = N(0, σ²T) (canary excluded), yielding an exact (ε, δ)-DP guarantee via the hockey-stick divergence. The heuristic is to apply this linear-loss guarantee to non-linear deep learning losses, justified by the observation that current auditing attacks achieve their tightest bounds when canary gradients are constant across iterations and independent of other gradients — precisely the linear case. The paper validates the heuristic empirically via privacy auditing on CIFAR-10 (WideResNet) and PersonaChat (Gemma 2), showing it consistently upper-bounds empirical leakage across gradient-space and input-space attacks. It also probes limitations through counterexamples: a quadratic regularizer that partially cancels noise (small violation ≤3%), a pathological regularizer that encodes the full training trajectory into the final iterate, and a malicious dataset construction that matches the standard composition-based bound even in the last-iterate-only setting.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with WideResNet, the heuristic ε ranges from 6.34 to 7.0 across batch sizes 256–4096, while the standard composition-based analysis gives ε = 8–14 and empirical privacy auditing yields ε = 1.6–1.8. Model accuracy remains stable at 78.2–79.9% regardless of batch size. On Gemma 2 fine-tuned on PersonaChat, the heuristic ε is 6.0–6.1 versus standard ε of 8–26, with empirical ε at 2.0–2.1 and perplexity around 24.2–24.8.

**Table 2: CIFAR-10 image classification (WideResNet) comparing heuristic, standard, and empirical privacy bounds across batch sizes.**

| Batch Size | Heuristic ε | Standard ε | Accuracy | Empirical ε |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4096 | 6.34 | 8 | 79.5% | 1.7 |
| 512 | 7.0 | 12 | 79.9% | 1.8 |
| 256 | 6.7 | 14 | 78.2% | 1.6 |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The heuristic ε consistently falls between the empirical auditing lower bound and the standard composition-based upper bound across all settings. On CIFAR-10 (batch 4096), the heuristic gives ε = 6.34, which is 4.3× tighter than the standard analysis (ε = 8) yet still 3.7× above the empirical auditing bound (ε = 1.7). The gap between the standard analysis and the heuristic widens dramatically at smaller batch sizes: at batch 256, the standard ε reaches 14 while the heuristic stays at 6.7. For the language model setting (Gemma 2 on PersonaChat), the standard analysis inflates to ε = 26 at batch 512, whereas the heuristic remains at 6.0 and empirical auditing confirms ε ≈ 2.0. The full-batch DP-GD approximation (q=1 baseline) generally gives smaller ε than the heuristic, confirming that subsampling amplification is the primary source of the gap between the two analyses. Notably, batch size has negligible effect on model accuracy or empirical privacy but significantly affects the standard analysis, challenging the current best practice of using large batch sizes for privacy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a 'third angle' between two existing lines of work on DP-SGD privacy: theoretical upper bounds (Feldman et al., 2018; Altschuler & Talwar, 2022; Ye & Shokri, 2022) that require strong convexity/smoothness assumptions, and privacy auditing lower bounds (Nasr et al., 2021, 2023; Jagielski et al., 2020) that are expensive and attack-specific. The key conceptual departure is a principled heuristic based on linear losses that is rigorous under that assumption yet predictive for general deep learning losses, avoiding both the restrictive assumptions of prior theory and the computational cost of auditing. It builds directly on DP-SGD (Abadi et al., 2016) and the Poisson subsampling framework, but reframes the analysis around the last-iterate-only threat model rather than the standard all-iterates composition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The heuristic provides a computable, pre-training estimate of privacy leakage that bridges the large gap between composition-based upper bounds and auditing lower bounds, potentially enabling hyperparameter selection without expensive auditing runs. It challenges the prevailing best practice of using large batch sizes for privacy, showing that smaller batches achieve comparable empirical privacy and accuracy on CIFAR-10. However, the counterexamples (regularizer-based, pathological, malicious dataset) demonstrate the heuristic is not a rigorous guarantee, and the central open question remains whether a fully general theoretical analysis can match the heuristic's predictions without linearity assumptions. The paper explicitly sets a target: future theory should match the heuristic, and future auditing should exceed it.
