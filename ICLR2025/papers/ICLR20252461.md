## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation methods over-trust SAM or DINOv2 to regularize 2D VLM features without further refinement, producing incomplete or imprecise boundaries. Additionally, methods that reduce feature dimensionality for efficiency perform compression in 2D space before 3D fusion, inevitably introducing multi-view inconsistency that degrades segmentation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252461/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes posed multi-view RGB images (640×480 for ScanNet/Replica, 4032×3024 for 3DOVS) along with Colmap-derived depth maps and SfM point clouds, plus free-form text prompts for open-vocabulary queries. It produces per-pixel semantic segmentation masks rendered from novel views via 3DGS alpha-blending, with the underlying 3D semantic fields stored in a compact 6–32 dimensional latent space rather than the original 512-dim CLIP/OpenSeg feature space. At inference, any text or image query is encoded by the frozen autoencoder and matched against the rendered low-dimensional semantic fields via cosine similarity.

## Method

econSG introduces two key components. First, Confidence-region Guided Regularization (CRR) mutually refines OpenSeg and SAM: high-confidence OpenSeg pixels are back-projected to 3D using Colmap depth and average-pooled for multi-view consistency, then reprojected with majority voting to produce clean 2D masks; bounding boxes fitted to these masks prompt SAM for improved regional proposals, which in turn refine the semantic features toward precise boundaries. Second, a low-dimensional 3D contextual space is built by first fusing backprojected multi-view 2D features into 3D (unlike prior work that compresses each 2D view independently), then pre-training an MLP autoencoder to map the fused 3D features into a 6–32 dimensional latent code. This latent space initializes the per-Gaussian semantic fields in 3DGS, and all subsequent training and inference operate entirely in this low-dimensional space, supervised by cross-entropy loss against CRR-refined masks and an optional L2 feature loss, yielding both multi-view consistency and significant speedups over high-dimensional baselines.

## Results

On the 3DOVS benchmark, econSG achieves 94.3% overall mIoU and 98.3% overall mAcc, outperforming the strongest 3DGS baseline LangSplat (82.3% mIoU) and the NeRF-based 3DOVS (86.8% mIoU). On LERF localization, econSG reaches 90.5% overall accuracy versus 85.2% for SemanticGaussian and 84.3% for LangSplat. On ScanNet and Replica, econSG achieves 48.2% mIoU (multi-view) and 33.9% mIoU (multi-view) respectively, surpassing the next-best method (Feature3DGS at 18.1% on ScanNet) by over 30 points. In terms of efficiency, econSG renders at 156 FPS and trains in 29 minutes, compared to 0.2–2.5 FPS and 66–87 minutes for competing 3D methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

econSG outperforms all seven baselines across every evaluated benchmark. Against the 2D baseline LSeg, econSG gains +73.7 mIoU on 3DOVS (94.3 vs. 20.6), confirming the critical benefit of 3D multi-view consistency. Among NeRF-based methods, econSG surpasses 3DOVS by +7.5 mIoU and LERF by +39.5 mIoU on 3DOVS, while also being orders of magnitude faster (156 FPS vs. 0.2–0.3 FPS). Among 3DGS-based competitors, econSG beats LangSplat by +12.0 mIoU on 3DOVS and +5.2 on LERF, and dominates Feature3DGS (+68.8 mIoU) and LEGaussians (+47.2 mIoU) by large margins, which suffer from high-dimensional feature noise and 2D-space dimensionality reduction that breaks multi-view consistency. On ScanNet and Replica, where all baselines struggle, econSG achieves 48.2% mIoU (multi-view) versus 18.1% for Feature3DGS and 17.8% for 3DOVS, a gap of over 30 points attributable to the CRR module and low-dimensional 3D contextual space. In training efficiency, econSG (29 min) is 2.3× faster than LangSplat (66 min) and 3× faster than Feature3DGS (87 min), with inference at 4.9 s versus 401.9 s for LangSplat.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3DGS-based open-vocabulary semantic segmentation line (LeRF, 3DOVS, LangSplat, LEGaussians, Feature3DGS, Gaussian Grouping) by addressing two specific weaknesses in prior methods: (1) over-trusting SAM or DINOv2 masks without refinement, and (2) performing feature dimensionality reduction in 2D space before 3D fusion, which introduces multi-view inconsistency. The key departure is the Confidence-region Guided Regularization (CRR), which mutually refines OpenSeg pixel features and SAM region masks through 3D back-projection and majority voting, and the decision to build the low-dimensional latent space from fused 3D features rather than compressed 2D features. The autoencoder-based 3D contextual space draws on the efficiency motivation of LangSplat and LEGaussians but relocates the compression step to after multi-view fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~80× inference speedup over LangSplat (4.9s vs 401.9s) and 156 FPS rendering make open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation practical for interactive applications like language-guided scene editing and 3D object retrieval, which were previously constrained by NeRF-based or high-dimensional 3DGS methods. The conceptual insight that dimensionality reduction should occur in 3D space after multi-view fusion rather than in 2D space before fusion offers a generalizable principle for other multi-view feature consolidation tasks. Limitations include reliance on Colmap SfM for depth and point initialization, the out-of-scope nature of part-level segmentation, and the fact that the method requires per-scene training rather than generalization to unseen scenes. Open questions include whether the CRR approach transfers to dynamic scenes or whether the autoencoder can be replaced by a learned generalizable mapping.
