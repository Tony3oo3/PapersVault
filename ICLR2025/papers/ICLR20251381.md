## Problem

While pre-trained LLMs have demonstrated zero-shot in-context learning for univariate time series forecasting, their application to continuous state-space Markov Decision Processes in reinforcement learning remains largely unexplored. Two critical barriers limit direct deployment: (1) incorporating the control signal (actions) into the LLM's forecasting context, and (2) handling the interdependence among multivariate state and action dimensions, which prior ICL approaches treat as independent.

## Input / Output

The method consumes trajectories of states (and optionally actions) from RL environments, which are rescaled to a fixed range and tokenized as comma-separated digit strings (e.g., "150, 516, 850") using a numeric sub-vocabulary of the LLM. After a forward pass through the frozen LLM, the logits over numeric tokens yield a full categorical distribution over the next value, enabling both point predictions and calibrated uncertainty estimates. In the DICL-SAC application, the output is synthetic transitions (state, sampled action, reward, predicted next state) injected into a replay buffer alongside real transitions.

## Method

DICL (Disentangled In-Context Learning) applies PCA to the joint state-action feature space to produce linearly uncorrelated principal components, then runs vanilla ICL independently on each component using a frozen LLM (Llama 3 series) in a zero-shot manner, and finally maps predictions back via the inverse PCA transform. This disentangling step simultaneously resolves the multivariate interdependence problem and allows actions to be naturally incorporated into the projected space. The paper provides a theoretical multi-branch return bound generalizing MBPO (Janner et al., 2019) that accounts for overlapping LLM-based rollout branches, with the error scaling as k²·p·ε_llm(T) where k is branch length, p is branching probability, and ε_llm(T) is the ICL generalization error. Two proof-of-concept applications are demonstrated: DICL-SAC, which augments SAC's replay buffer with LLM-generated transitions to improve early sample efficiency, and a hybrid policy evaluation method that uses LLM-predicted reward trajectories to reduce online evaluation time. The LLM's output logits are shown to produce well-calibrated quantile uncertainty estimates, a desirable property for model-based RL.

## Results

On 7 D4RL tasks (HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2d variants), DICL-(s) with Llama 3.1-70B achieves the lowest multi-step prediction MSE of 359×10⁻², outperforming vICL (392×10⁻²) and the MLP baseline (406×10⁻²). DICL-(s,a) achieves the best calibration (KS statistic of 45×10⁻² with Llama 3.1-8B) compared to vICL (53×10⁻²) and MLP (55×10⁻²), indicating well-calibrated uncertainty estimates. In the data-augmented off-policy RL setting (DICL-SAC), the method improves early-training sample efficiency over SAC on HalfCheetah and Pendulum, though gains diminish as the LLM data proportion α increases, consistent with the theoretical multi-branch return bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DICL against two baselines: vICL (vanilla in-context learning treating state dimensions independently) and a 4-layer MLP trained on the same context data. On prediction accuracy (MSE), DICL-(s) with Llama 3-8B (370×10⁻²) and 3.1-70B (359×10⁻²) both outperform vICL with the same models (375×10⁻² and 392×10⁻²) and the MLP baseline (406×10⁻²), with the 70B model achieving the best overall MSE. On calibration (KS statistic), DICL-(s,a) substantially outperforms both vICL (45–48×10⁻² vs. 52–55×10⁻²) and the MLP (55×10⁻²), confirming that PCA-based disentangling improves uncertainty calibration. However, DICL-(s,a) has higher MSE than DICL-(s) and vICL because it must also predict actions, introducing additional error. In the data-augmented RL setting, DICL-SAC improves early-training returns over SAC on HalfCheetah and Pendulum, but the advantage shrinks as the LLM data proportion α increases (consistent with the k²p factor in the theoretical bound), and Hopper results are noisier.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on LLM in-context learning for time series forecasting (Gruver et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2024c) from univariate signals to multivariate state-action trajectories in continuous MDPs, addressing two key gaps: incorporating action information and handling interdependencies between state dimensions. It builds on the dynamical-systems formulation of ICL (Li et al., 2023) and the branched-rollout theoretical framework of MBPO (Janner et al., 2019), generalizing the single-branch return bound to multi-branch rollouts. The closest prior work, Wang et al. (2023), uses LLMs for direct policy generation in control tasks, whereas this paper positions LLMs as zero-shot dynamics predictors within an MBRL pipeline, requiring no fine-tuning or gradient updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a principled bridge between MBRL and pre-trained LLMs, showing that ICL can serve as a plug-and-play dynamics model with calibrated uncertainty estimates—a property critical for safe model-based planning. However, the practical impact is currently limited by computational cost (d_s LLM calls per transition) and modest performance gains over simple MLP baselines in some settings. Open questions include whether non-linear disentangling (autoencoders, ICA) can close the remaining gap, how to integrate textual context to reduce the context length requirement, and how to make full MBRL with LLM dynamics computationally feasible beyond the data-augmentation and policy-evaluation use cases demonstrated here.
