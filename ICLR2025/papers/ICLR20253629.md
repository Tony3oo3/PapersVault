## Problem

Prior theoretical work attributes CoT's reasoning benefits to increased representational capacity of transformers, but this explanation is insufficient since large models still fail on simple tasks without CoT. The paper identifies the missing mechanism: CoT's sparse sequential structure dramatically reduces sample complexity from exponential to polynomial, and the key enabler is the induction of sparse attention patterns during training.

## Input / Output

For the synthetic parity task, the model receives an autoregressive binary sequence of length n+2 (no CoT) or n+k+1 (with CoT), where k intermediate XOR steps are appended before the final answer token, and it predicts the next token at each position. For real-world evaluation, GSM8K math word problems are fed into pretrained Qwen2-7B models with or without ground-truth reasoning chains. Beyond the binary parity prediction or math answer, the critical output is the attention pattern itself, which becomes one-hot and interpretable (each CoT token attending to its corresponding secret index) when trained with CoT.

## Method

The paper provides a three-phase optimization dynamics analysis of a simplified 1-layer transformer (Densenet-style concatenation, frozen random hypercube embeddings, square attention matrix) trained with mini-batch SGD on parity data. Theoretically, they prove that without CoT the sample complexity is 2^Ω(k) (via online-learning lower bounds under bounded memory), while with CoT's sparse sequential dependence—where each intermediate token depends on exactly one prior token—only Õ(n) samples suffice and the attention converges to a one-hot pattern on the secret indices. The three training phases are: (1) FFN weights acquire correlation with secret-index embeddings, (2) this correlation drives the attention matrix to amplify weights on secret indices until the pattern is one-hot, and (3) the FFN learns the final output mapping using both the current embedding and the retrieved secret-index embedding. Empirically, they validate with standard GPT-2 architecture transformers on parity (confirming exponential vs. linear sample complexity across layers and heads) and measure normalized attention entropy on Qwen2-7B and Qwen2-Math-7B over GSM8K, showing that CoT data induces sparser attention in pretrained models and that CoT fine-tuning further reduces entropy.

## Results

Theoretically, the paper proves that without CoT, learning parity (n,k) requires 2^Ω(k) samples (Theorem 2), while with CoT, a 1-layer transformer achieves perfect accuracy with Õ(n) samples (Theorem 3). Empirically, on the (n=20, k=6) parity problem, CoT training reaches 100% evaluation accuracy in just 5 epochs, whereas multi-pass training without CoT requires 1,000 epochs on 10,000 samples. On GSM8K, the pre-trained Qwen2-7B model exhibits lower normalized attention entropy on CoT data than on non-CoT data, and the CoT-fine-tuned Qwen2-Math-7B shows further reduced entropy, confirming that CoT induces sparser attention on real-world data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between training with and without Chain-of-Thought on the parity problem. Without CoT, sample complexity grows exponentially with the hardness parameter k (e.g., k=4 already exceeds the 10⁷ sample budget for n=30), while with CoT it grows approximately linearly in k. On the (n=20, k=6) problem, multi-pass training without CoT (10,000 samples, 1,000 epochs) eventually achieves 100% accuracy across most layer-head configurations, but CoT training reaches the same accuracy in only 5 epochs—a 200× reduction in training steps. A surprising negative result shows that training without CoT on a larger dataset (1M samples) actually fails to learn, with both loss and attention entropy remaining elevated, whereas 50K samples suffice—suggesting that sparsity development, not data volume, is the critical factor. On GSM8K, the CoT-fine-tuned Qwen2-Math-7B model achieves lower normalized attention entropy than the base Qwen2-7B on the same CoT data, indicating that fine-tuning on CoT data further promotes sparse attention patterns.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant theoretical narrative that CoT improves transformers by expanding their representational capacity (Feng et al., 2023; Li et al., 2024c; Merrill & Sabharwal, 2024), arguing instead that expressiveness is not the bottleneck in the LLM regime. It builds on the simplified transformer architecture used in Wang et al. (2024b) and Nichani et al. (2024) for optimization dynamics analysis, and leverages time-space lower bounds from online learning (Lyu et al., 2023) to establish exponential sample complexity without CoT. The closest prior work is Wies et al. (2023), which showed subtask decomposition enables polynomial-sample parity learning in RNNs, and Kim & Suzuki (2024a), which proved CoT reduces gradient steps for parity; this paper extends the analysis to mini-batch SGD with finite-sample guarantees and explicitly identifies attention sparsity as the mechanistic link.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, testable mechanism—sparse sequential dependencies inducing sparse attention—for why CoT improves sample efficiency, shifting the explanatory focus from expressiveness to optimization landscape. The real-world validation on Qwen2-7B/GSM8K showing lower normalized attention entropy on CoT data adds practical credibility beyond the synthetic parity setting. Key limitations include the restriction to parity functions where each token depends on exactly one prior token, the use of a simplified (Densenet-style, no layer-norm) architecture for theory, and the observational (rather than causal) nature of the real-world sparsity measurements. Extending the analysis to multi-dependency structures, standard transformer architectures, and broader function classes remains an open and important direction.
