## Problem

Localizing concept-selective regions in the visual cortex traditionally relies on expensive fLoc experiments with manually constructed, biased stimulus sets, limiting coverage to a handful of concept categories and raising concerns about generalizability. Real fMRI data are scarce, and existing stimuli often depict isolated objects in unnatural contexts, making it difficult to explore novel or fine-grained concept-selective regions at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252768/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes natural-scene RGB images (e.g., from MSCOCO or CIFAR-10/100) as input, encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 into 257×768 token representations. It outputs synthetic BOLD fMRI voxels restricted to the visual cortex ROI (12,682–15,724 voxels per subject, flattened to 1-D sequences), generated via multi-trial averaging of N correlated-noise samples. Downstream, the synthetic fMRI feeds a standard one-sample t-test with Bonferroni correction to produce voxel-wise significance maps for concept-selective region localization.

## Method

MindSimulator is a per-subject generative fMRI encoder with three components: (1) an fMRI autoencoder that maps raw BOLD voxels through ridge regression and residual MLP blocks into a 257×768 latent space, trained jointly with MSE reconstruction and SoftCLIP contrastive alignment to a frozen CLIP image embedding; (2) a 6-block Transformer diffusion estimator (100 timesteps, cosine schedule) that performs x-prediction—directly predicting the target fMRI latent rather than noise—conditioned on image tokens via cross-attention; and (3) an inference sampler that generates N fMRI from N correlated Gaussian noises (ε_n = √β_n·ε₁ + √(1−β_n)·ε₂) and averages them for multi-trial enhancement. The key architectural insight is that fMRI encoding is inherently one-to-many (the same stimulus elicits variable neural responses), so a generative model capturing the conditional distribution is more faithful than a deterministic regression. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end on ~9,000 paired image-fMRI samples per subject from NSD, requiring only ~32 GPU-hours on a single V100.

## Results

On the NSD dataset (4 subjects, MSCOCO images), MindSimulator (Trials=5) achieves Pearson 0.355 and MSE 0.385 at the voxel level, and CLIP 91.2%, Incep 93.2%, and Alex(5) 96.8% at the semantic level, approaching the ground-truth fMRI upper bound (CLIP 94.5%, Incep 96.4%, Alex(5) 99.0%). For out-of-distribution generalization on CIFAR-10/100, MindSimulator retains strong semantic-level performance (e.g., CLIP 90.5% on CIFAR-10, 86.3% on CIFAR-100) with only minimal degradation compared to in-distribution MSCOCO. For concept-selective region localization, MindSimulator achieves 91.7% accuracy and 0.674 F1 for bodies-selective regions (vs. 45.8% / 0.570 for linear regression) using only 100 top-ranked images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MindSimulator outperforms both baselines across all voxel-level and semantic-level metrics. Against the linear regression baseline (Wang et al., 2023), MindSimulator (Trials=5) improves Pearson from 0.334 to 0.355, CLIP from 87.2% to 91.2%, and Incep from 90.1% to 93.2%, while reducing Eff from 0.728 to 0.688. Against the Transformer encoding baseline (Adeli et al., 2023), the gains are even larger on semantic metrics: CLIP improves from 85.5% to 91.2% (+5.7 pp) and Incep from 89.8% to 93.2% (+3.4 pp), while MSE is slightly lower (0.385 vs. 0.387). For region localization (Table 4), MindSimulator achieves 91.7% accuracy on bodies-selective regions versus 45.8% for linear regression, and 52.6% on places-selective regions versus 32.0%. The concurrent Grad-CAM-based localization method (Shen et al., 2025) is qualitatively compared in Figure 20, where MindSimulator's data-driven approach localizes concept-selective regions more focused on the higher-level visual cortex, whereas Grad-CAM produces more diffuse activations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper inverts the fMRI-to-image decoding pipeline (MindEye2, Scotti et al. 2024b) into an image-to-fMRI encoding model, but departs fundamentally from all prior encoding work by replacing deterministic regression (linear or transformer) with a conditional diffusion model in a joint fMRI-image latent space. The architecture borrows the autoencoder-plus-latent-diffusion structure from Stable Diffusion (Rombach et al., 2022) and the SoftCLIP alignment loss (Gao et al., 2024) for cross-modal space construction, while the diffusion estimator itself is a direct adaptation of DDPM (Ho et al., 2020) that predicts fMRI representations rather than noise. The key conceptual shift is treating the image-to-fMRI mapping as a one-to-many conditional distribution (motivated by trial-to-trial variability in BOLD signals) rather than a single deterministic output, which regression models cannot capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By synthesizing large-scale fMRI from arbitrary images, this work removes the primary bottleneck (costly fLoc experiments with manually curated stimuli) that has limited concept-selective region discovery to a handful of categories, opening the possibility of a data-driven concept atlas of the visual cortex. However, the novel-region exploration remains preliminary—only four concepts are tested, validation relies on partial voxel ablation rather than independent fMRI confirmation, and all results are confined to the visual cortex of four NSD subjects. Open questions include whether the localized regions generalize across subjects and modalities, whether the approach extends to non-visual cortex, and whether the synthetic fMRI's statistical properties (beyond semantic content) are sufficient for the full range of neuroscience analyses.
