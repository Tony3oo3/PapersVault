## Problem

Pre-processing-based backdoor defenses face a fundamental trade-off: transformation-based methods must modify all input features indiscriminately, so stronger transformations that remove triggers also degrade benign accuracy, while BTI-based methods require accurately inverting unknown trigger patterns, which is inherently unreliable without prior knowledge. The paper asks whether this trade-off can be broken without relying on trigger inversion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251568/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 224×224 for ImageNet) and produces class labels with remapped confidence scores. The defender only requires an unlabeled dataset i.i.d. with the original training data; pseudo-labels are obtained by running the fixed backdoored model on the raw inputs. The original model's weights remain entirely frozen—only the lightweight UNet input transformation module is trained, making the approach applicable to third-party models where parameter access is unavailable.

## Method

REFINE reprograms a fixed backdoored model via two modules: a trainable UNet autoencoder that encodes and decodes input images to disrupt both benign and backdoor patterns, and a hard-coded one-to-one label remapping function that shuffles the output domain. The key theoretical insight (Theorem 1) shows that the prediction change induced by preprocessing is bounded by the Wasserstein-1 distance between pre- and post-transformation feature representations; by remapping the output domain, the input transformation is freed from the constraint of preserving original feature geometry, enabling more aggressive trigger removal without sacrificing accuracy. Training optimizes the UNet using a combined loss of cross-entropy (maintaining utility in the new output domain) and supervised contrastive loss (pulling same-class transformed samples together and pushing different-class samples apart), guided by pseudo-labels from the original model. At inference, the pipeline is simply T → F → M with all parameters fixed.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, REFINE reduces the attack success rate (ASR) to below 2% across all 7 backdoor attacks (e.g., 0.78% for BadNets, 1.21% for BATT) while maintaining benign accuracy (BA) within 3% of the undefended model (e.g., 90.43% vs. 91.24% for BadNets). On ImageNet, REFINE achieves ASR below 3% in all cases (e.g., 1.81% for BadNets, 0% for LC) and even improves BA over the undefended model (e.g., 66.27% vs. 65.42% for BadNets), attributed to the added depth from the input transformation module. Across all 14 attack-dataset combinations, REFINE is the only method that simultaneously keeps ASR < 3% and BA drop < 3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REFINE dominates all seven baselines across every attack and dataset. Against transformation-based defenses, ShrinkPad (Li et al., 2021c) suffers large BA drops (e.g., 84.51% vs. 90.43% on CIFAR-10 BadNets) and often fails to reduce ASR below 10%; BDMAE (Sun et al., 2023) is competitive on some attacks (e.g., 3.18% ASR on BadNets) but catastrophically fails on BATT (100% ASR) and Blended (84.80% ASR); ZIP (Shi et al., 2023) shows the weakest overall performance with BA drops exceeding 10% and ASR above 70% on several attacks. Against BTI-based defenses, Neural Cleanse (Wang et al., 2019) and UNICORN (Wang et al., 2023) both exhibit high ASR on Blended and WaNet attacks (e.g., NC: 89.47% ASR on Blended; UNICORN: 98.00% on ImageNet Blended), while BTI-DBF(P) (Xu et al., 2024) is the strongest baseline but still trails REFINE by 3–5% ASR on most attacks (e.g., 5.60% vs. 0.78% on CIFAR-10 BadNets). Traditional model reprogramming (T-MR, Elsayed et al., 2019) drops BA by over 15% (e.g., 75.51% vs. 90.50% on BadNets) and fails to reduce ASR below 25% on WaNet, confirming that the autoencoder-based input transformation in REFINE is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

REFINE repurposes the model reprogramming framework (input transformation module + output mapping module) from cross-domain adaptation (Chen, 2024; Elsayed et al., 2019; Tsai et al., 2020) for backdoor defense, replacing the traditional adversarial-perturbation-based input transformation with a trainable UNet autoencoder. It departs from both transformation-based defenses (ShrinkPad, BDMAE, ZIP) and BTI-based defenses (Neural Cleanse, UNICORN, BTI-DBF) by identifying a shared root cause of their failure: the fixed output domain constrains how aggressively inputs can be transformed. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 1) formalizes this by bounding defense effectiveness via the Wasserstein-1 distance between pre- and post-transformation feature representations, motivating the output-domain shift as the key mechanism to enlarge that bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, model-agnostic defense that consistently achieves ASR < 3% across 7 attack types, 2 datasets, and 4 architectures without modifying the backdoored model's weights, which is valuable in settings where only frozen third-party models are accessible. The conceptual insight that a simple hard-coded output permutation can decouple the utility-defense trade-off is elegant and could inform future defense designs beyond backdoors (e.g., adversarial robustness). Open questions include: how the method behaves when the pseudo-labeling step is corrupted by a heavily backdoored model, whether the i.i.d. unlabeled data assumption holds in practice, and whether the approach extends to multi-modal or language-model backdoors where output remapping is less straightforward.
