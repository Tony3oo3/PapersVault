## Problem

Existing medical multimodal datasets lack multigranular annotations that capture region-wise correlations (e.g., how a local lesion affects surrounding tissue), and their construction relies on human-written paired text descriptions, severely limiting scalability. This paper addresses both gaps by proposing the first automated pipeline that generates multigranular visual and textual annotations from unpaired medical images at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250220/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes unpaired medical images across 10 modalities (X-ray, CT, MRI, histopathology, ultrasound, etc.) with no requirement for pre-existing text descriptions, supplemented by dataset metadata (modality, organ, disease labels) and a vector-indexed medical knowledge base (PubMed, StatPearls, textbooks). It outputs image-ROI-description triplets where ROIs are bounding boxes or segmentation masks localized by domain-specific expert models, and descriptions are structured text spanning five attributes: modality, organ detection, ROI analysis, lesion texture, and region-wise correlations. 3D volumetric inputs (DICOM/NIfTI) are converted to 2D PNG slices before processing.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages. Stage 1 (Data Processing) integrates dataset metadata into coarse captions, locates ROIs using an ensemble of modality-specific expert models (HoverNet for histopathology, SAT for CT, CheXmask for X-ray, DINO for general images, BA-Transformer for skin lesions), and retrieves top-8 semantically relevant medical knowledge snippets via Faiss vector search over Med-CPT-encoded corpora. Stage 2 (Text Generation) prompts LLaVA-Medcap—a LLaVA variant with LLaMA3 and multiscale visual features, fine-tuned on 200K GPT-4V-generated descriptions—using a three-level hierarchical prompt that elicits global image description, local ROI-specific analysis, and region-wise correlation inference. The resulting 25M triplets are then used to pretrain LLaVA-Tri (first on 600K PMC-15M pairs, then on MedTrinity-25M for multigranular alignment), achieving SOTA on VQA-RAD (81.6%), SLAKE (87.8%), and PathVQA (82.8%).

## Results

LLaVA-Tri, pretrained on MedTrinity-25M, achieves state-of-the-art average accuracy on all three medical VQA benchmarks: 81.6% on VQA-RAD, 87.8% on SLAKE, and 82.8% on PathVQA. Ablation studies (Table 4) confirm that multigranular alignment with MedTrinity-25M yields consistent gains across diverse architectures, with LLaVA-Tri improving by +10.8%, +6.1%, and +8.3% on VQA-RAD, SLAKE, and PathVQA respectively, and even smaller models like InternVL2-8B gaining +3.2%, +2.8%, and +3.9%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLaVA-Tri outperforms all 11 compared baselines on every benchmark where they overlap. On VQA-RAD, it surpasses the closest competitor Q2ATransformer (80.2%) by 1.4 points and LLaVA-Med (72.8%) by 8.8 points. On SLAKE, it beats BiomedCLIP (85.9%) by 1.9 points and LLaVA-Med (84.1%) by 3.7 points. On PathVQA, the margin is largest: LLaVA-Tri (82.8%) exceeds VL Encoder-Decoder (78.6%) by 4.2 points and LLaVA-Med (64.5%) by 18.3 points. Notably, GPT-4V, while strong on closed questions (78.9% on VQA-RAD), scores only 59.2% average, well below LLaVA-Tri's 81.6%. The ablation in Table 4 further shows that MedTrinity-25M alignment benefits not only LLaVA-Tri but also MiniCPM-V-2.6-8B (+1.6–4.4%), InternVL2-8B (+2.8–3.9%), and PubMedCLIP (+2.7%), confirming the dataset's generalizability as a pretraining resource.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLaVA/LLaVA-Med lineage of medical vision-language models by introducing a large-scale pretraining dataset (MedTrinity-25M) and an automated annotation pipeline that departs fundamentally from prior medical datasets reliant on expert-written paired reports. Where MIMIC-CXR, CheXpert, and RadGenome-Chest CT are bottlenecked by the availability of human-authored text, this work uses retrieval-augmented generation (following MedRAG) combined with domain-specific segmentation/grounding models (SAT, BA-Transformer, CheXmask) to synthesize multigranular image-ROI-description triplets from unpaired images. The architectural base is LLaVA with LLaMA3 and multiscale visual features, and the captioning model (LLaVA-Medcap) is itself fine-tuned following the two-stage strategy of LLaVA-Med, creating a self-reinforcing pipeline where GPT-4V seeds the initial training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

At 25 million triplets across 10 modalities and 65+ diseases, MedTrinity-25M is by a wide margin the largest open-source multimodal medical dataset, and the automated pipeline removes the primary scalability bottleneck (expert annotation) that has constrained the field. The consistent performance gains across four architecturally distinct models (LLaVA-Tri, MiniCPM-V, InternVL2, PubMedCLIP) when pretraining on the dataset suggest it can serve as a general-purpose medical pretraining resource rather than a model-specific asset. Open questions include whether auto-generated annotations introduce systematic biases that expert reports would not, how well the pipeline generalizes to truly unannotated clinical images without any metadata, and whether the VQA benchmark improvements translate to clinically meaningful diagnostic accuracy.
