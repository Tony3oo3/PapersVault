## Problem

Training a 3D object detector for a new agent (e.g., ego car) typically requires expensive, large-scale human annotation. This paper investigates an unexplored alternative: learning from the 3D bounding box predictions of a nearby reference agent equipped with an accurate detector. Naively using these predictions as labels fails due to two fundamental challenges—viewpoint mismatch (occlusion/field-of-view differences between agents) and mislocalization (GPS errors and synchronization delays of ~0.1 s causing ~2.7 m displacement at 60 mph)—which produce noisy, incomplete pseudo labels.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the ego car's LiDAR point cloud (e.g., 32-beam Velodyne VLP-32) together with the reference car's predicted 3D bounding boxes expressed in a global coordinate frame, which are transformed into the ego car's local frame. The ego and reference agents may use different sensor configurations (8/16/32-beam LiDAR) and different detector architectures (PointPillars, SECOND), making the pipeline sensor- and model-agnostic. The output is a trained 3D detector for the ego car plus a set of refined pseudo labels; only 40 annotated frames (<1% of training data) are needed to train the auxiliary box ranker, and the entire offline pipeline runs in ~2.5 hours on 8 GPUs.

## Method

R&B-POP combines two complementary modules. First, a lightweight PointNet-based box ranker, trained on as few as 40 annotated frames, scores candidate boxes by predicting their IoU with ground truth and a 3D offset; at inference it uses a coarse-to-fine (C2F) sampling strategy—256 boxes from a wide uniform translation range, select top-3, then 256 boxes from a narrow Gaussian range around them—to correct mislocalization and filter false positives. Second, a distance-based curriculum for self-training exploits the observation that pseudo-label quality degrades with ego-reference distance: the detector is first trained only on frames within 40 m (high-quality labels), then fine-tuned on all frames using its own predictions, with a distance-adaptive confidence threshold (T_c + λ/d) to prevent false-positive accumulation. Together these components raise AP@IoU 0.5 from 22.0% (naive use of reference predictions) to 56.5%, approaching the 58.4% upper bound from ego ground-truth labels.

## Results

On V2V4Real (32-beam LiDAR, PointPillars), naively using the reference car's predictions as labels yields only 22.0% AP@IoU 0.5 (0–80 m), far below the 58.4% upper bound from the ego car's own ground-truth labels. The full R&B-POP pipeline (box ranker + coarse-to-fine refinement + distance-based curriculum self-training) closes most of this gap, reaching 56.5% AP@IoU 0.5 and 32.6% AP@IoU 0.7, using only 40 annotated frames (<1% of training data) for ranker training. On the OPV2V simulation dataset, R&B-POP similarly improves AP@IoU 0.5 from 71.7% (naive) to 83.2% (full pipeline) under 1-frame time delay.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the heuristic box refinement of Luo et al. (NeurIPS 2023), the learned box ranker more than doubles AP@IoU 0.7 (22.9 vs. 10.3 without self-training; 32.6 vs. 11.0 with distance-based curriculum), showing that learning to rank noisy candidates is far more effective than edge-alignment heuristics. Against the naive self-training of You et al. (CVPR 2022), the distance-based curriculum consistently improves AP@IoU 0.5 by 10+ points (56.5 vs. 45.0 with ranker; 42.5 vs. 32.4 without ranker), confirming that ordering training by ego-reference distance is critical when pseudo labels are inherently noisy. In the supplementary comparison with 3DIoUMatch (Wang et al., CVPR 2021) using PV-RCNN, R&B-POP achieves 41.7% AP@IoU 0.7 (0–80 m) versus 37.1% for 3DIoUMatch alone, and the two methods are complementary: combining 3DIoUMatch with R&B-POP's ranker-refined labels and distance-based curriculum pushes performance to 44.4%. Overall, R&B-POP approaches the ground-truth upper bound (56.5 vs. 58.4 AP@IoU 0.5) while requiring only 40 human-annotated frames, demonstrating that the learning-from-others' predictions scenario is viable with minimal labeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a distinct niche between collaborative perception (which fuses predictions at inference time) and standard label-efficient learning (which relies on unlabeled data from the same agent). It builds on heuristic box refinement from Luo et al. (2023) by replacing hand-crafted alignment criteria with a learned PointNet-based ranker, and extends self-training from You et al. (2022b) with a distance-based curriculum that exploits the correlation between inter-vehicle distance and pseudo-label quality. The paper explicitly positions itself as orthogonal to domain adaptation and semi-supervised methods, noting that generic DA techniques can be layered on top of its pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: a self-driving car entering a new area or adopting a new sensor can bootstrap its 3D detector using only 40 human-annotated frames (<1% of training data) plus predictions from nearby 'expert' agents, approaching the performance of a fully supervised detector (56.5 vs. 58.4 AP@IoU 0.5). The approach is sensor-agnostic and detector-agnostic, validated across different LiDAR beam counts, architectures (PointPillars, SECOND), and domains (simulation-to-real). Open questions include handling malicious or unreliable reference agents, extending to multi-modality (camera + LiDAR), and scaling to more diverse urban scenarios with roadside units and heterogeneous sensor suites.
