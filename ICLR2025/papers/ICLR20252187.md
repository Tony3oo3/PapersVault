## Problem

Existing temporal latent representation learning methods assume all latent factors are active at every time step, failing to capture real-world dynamics where certain latent influences become intermittently inactive or irrelevant. This paper introduces intermittent temporal latent processes, where any subset of latent factors may be missing during nonlinear data generation at any time step and the active set is unknown, and provides the first general identifiability guarantees for recovering such latent variables.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252187/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a temporal sequence of K-dimensional observations (x_1, ..., x_T) in the undercomplete regime (K ≥ N) and produces N-dimensional latent variable estimates at each time step. In the real-world volleyball experiment, input consists of ResNet-18 features (1024-dim) extracted from the top 20 person detections per frame via Faster R-CNN, and the output includes both the learned latent representations and a group activity class label predicted by a downstream MLP classifier over the concatenated latent sequence.

## Method

InterLatent extends the Sequential Variational Autoencoder with three L1 sparsity regularization terms applied to the Jacobians of the mixing function (column norms) and transition function (row and column norms), enforcing the intermittent support structure without requiring ground-truth active sets. The temporal prior is modeled via a normalizing flow that maps each latent component through an invertible transition function to Gaussian noise, enabling efficient KL computation while enforcing the conditional independence assumption. Theoretically, the paper proves block-wise identifiability (Theorem 1) under sufficient variability of transitions and support sparsity, and further component-wise identifiability (Theorem 2) within the support given conditional independence of latent variables given the previous time step. The ELBO objective combines reconstruction loss, KL divergence against the flow-based prior, and the three Jacobian sparsity penalties, trained end-to-end in an unsupervised manner (or weakly supervised with a classifier head for the GAR task).

## Results

On the Volleyball group activity recognition benchmark, InterLatent achieves 95.7% Multi-class Classification Accuracy (MCA), surpassing the previous best result of 94.0% by CaRiNG by 1.7 points and outperforming state-of-the-art GAR methods Dual-AI (93.2%) and BiCausal (93.4%) by 2.5 and 2.3 points respectively. On synthetic experiments, InterLatent consistently achieves the highest Mean Correlation Coefficient (MCC) across all six nonstationary and stationary scenarios, exceeding the next-best method by more than 0.1 MCC in the most challenging nonstationary setting with d^c=1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InterLatent outperforms all 15 baselines on the Volleyball dataset. Against the closest temporal causal representation learning competitors, it beats CaRiNG (94.0%) by 1.7 points and TDRL (92.9%) by 2.8 points, both of which do not model missingness in both transition and mixing functions simultaneously. It also surpasses the strongest dedicated GAR methods—Dual-AI (93.2%) and BiCausal (93.4%)—by 2.5 and 2.3 points respectively. On synthetic experiments, InterLatent achieves substantially higher MCC than LEAP, TDRL, CaRiNG, BetaVAE, i-VAE, and SlowVAE across all six nonstationary/stationary scenarios, with the largest gap (>0.1 MCC) in the nonstationary d^c=1 setting. Ablation studies confirm that removing sparsity regularization on either the transition or mixing function significantly degrades performance, while a weakly supervised variant with known support achieves comparable scores, validating the effectiveness of the unsupervised sparsity terms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the temporal latent representation learning line (LEAP, TDRL, CaRiNG) by introducing intermittent temporal latent processes, where latent factors can be missing from both the transition and mixing functions at any time step. It builds architecturally on Sequential Variational Autoencoders (Li & Mandt, 2018) but departs conceptually from prior work: CaRiNG handles missingness only in the mixing function, while linear/piecewise-linear methods (Lachapelle et al., Fumero et al., Xu et al.) cannot capture the nonlinear intermittent dynamics considered here. The key theoretical departure is proving block-wise and component-wise identifiability under support sparsity and conditional independence, generalizing Hessian-span arguments from prior nonlinear ICA and temporal identification results to the intermittent setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is, to the authors' knowledge, the first general identifiability framework for temporal latent processes with intermittent missingness, providing theoretical guarantees that hold for both stationary and nonstationary support patterns. The practical impact is currently limited by validation on a single real-world dataset (Volleyball) and synthetic experiments, and the authors acknowledge the lack of broader application domains. Open questions include whether the identifiability assumptions (sufficient variability, conditional independence) hold in more complex real-world systems, how the approach scales to longer sequences or larger latent dimensions, and whether the Gumbel-Softmax-based support estimation (Proposition 1) remains stable in high-dimensional settings.
