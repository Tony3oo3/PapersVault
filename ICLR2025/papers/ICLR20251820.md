## Problem

Aligning text-to-image diffusion models with user intent remains challenging, and existing solutions rely on auxiliary vision-language models, human annotation, or costly inference-time optimization. No prior work investigates a fully self-supervised alignment strategy that uses only the pre-trained T2I model itself to identify and correct misalignment, without external supervision or linguistic prompt analysis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251820/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained latent diffusion model (SD-2.1-base or SDXL) and a set of text prompts (e.g., 700 prompts per category from T2I-CompBench) as input. For each prompt, it generates M=50 candidate 512×512 images in latent space, computes a point-wise MI score for each, and retains the top-k=1 most aligned pair. The output is a fine-tuned model with DoRA adapters injected into the UNET, which produces better-aligned images at standard inference time with no additional computational overhead.

## Method

MI-TUNE introduces a point-wise mutual information estimator (Eq. 2) derived via the Girsanov Theorem for discrete-time diffusion: I(z,p) = E_{t,ε}[κ_t ‖ε_θ(z_t,p,t) − ε_θ(z_t,∅,t)‖²], where κ_t = β_t·T / (2α_t(1−ᾱ_t)). This quantity measures how much the prompt-conditioned denoiser diverges from the unconditional one, providing a self-contained alignment signal that requires no auxiliary models. The self-supervised fine-tuning loop generates M images per prompt, ranks them by point-wise MI, and selects the top-k pairs to form a synthetic fine-tuning set. The pre-trained UNET is then fine-tuned using DoRA adapters (rank 32, α=32) on attention and fully-connected layers only, with the text encoder frozen. The approach is validated across six T2I-CompBench categories, SDXL, and DiffusionDB, achieving state-of-the-art alignment while studying the alignment–image-quality trade-off via CFG tuning.

## Results

On T2I-CompBench (SD-2.1-base), MI-TUNE achieves the best average alignment across all three metrics: BLIP-VQA 53.56% (+7.75 over base), HPS 27.21 (+1.03), and Human preference 48.35% (+12.64). The largest gains appear in attribute binding (e.g., Color BLIP-VQA: 65.04 vs. 49.65 base) and 2D-Spatial (18.51 vs. 15.77). On SDXL, MI-TUNE further improves BLIP-VQA from 60.78 to 69.66 (Color) and HPS from 23.99 to 25.32 (DiffusionDB). Image quality metrics (FID, FD-DINO, CMMD on MS-COCO-2014) show modest degradation (e.g., FID 17.1→18.7 avg) that can be mitigated by lowering CFG at generation time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MI-TUNE outperforms all six baselines on every category and metric. Against inference-time methods, MI-TUNE leads by +2.39 BLIP-VQA (avg) over the best (A&E, 51.17) and by +10.29 Human preference (48.35 vs. 38.06), while also avoiding the extra sampling-time optimization cost. Against fine-tuning methods, the margin is larger: MI-TUNE beats DPOK (47.98) and GORS (47.88) by ~5.6 BLIP-VQA points, and HN-ITM (44.28) by ~9.3 points. On HPS, where inter-method variation is naturally small (~0.5–1.0 range per Appendix D), MI-TUNE's +0.64 over the best competitor (DPOK, 26.57) is still meaningful. Notably, GORS uses BLIP-VQA as its selection criterion (creating an evaluation bias acknowledged by its authors), yet MI-TUNE still surpasses it by 5.68 points on BLIP-VQA, confirming the gain is not an artifact of metric overlap. On the more realistic DiffusionDB benchmark, MI-TUNE achieves HPS 25.32 vs. 24.35 for DiffusionDB-paired fine-tuning and 23.99 for the base model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MI estimation framework of Franzese et al. (2024, MINDE) from continuous-time to discrete-time diffusion, deriving a point-wise MI estimator (Eq. 2) that measures the scaled squared difference between conditional and unconditional denoising scores. It departs from the dominant T2I alignment literature—which splits into inference-time attention manipulation (A&E, SDG, SCG) and fine-tuning methods requiring auxiliary reward models (DPOK), VQA-based selection (GORS), or human-annotated contrastive pairs (HN-ITM)—by being the first fully self-supervised approach that uses only the pre-trained denoising network itself to both generate and select fine-tuning data. The parameter-efficient fine-tuning stage leverages DoRA adapters (Liu et al., 2024), and the overall pipeline is conceptually closest to GORS but replaces the biased BLIP-VQA selection criterion with a model-internal MI signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: MI-TUNE eliminates the need for auxiliary VQA/LLM models, human annotation, or inference-time optimization overhead, making T2I alignment accessible with only the base model and modest compute (~32 hours on one A100). The alignment/image-quality trade-off analysis (showing that lowering CFG after fine-tuning recovers a 'sweet spot') addresses a gap the authors note has been overlooked in prior work. Key limitations include the computational cost of generating M=50 images per prompt for selection, the finding that MI can strengthen but not fully induce alignment (2D-Spatial still benefits from pre-aligned SPRIGHT), and the small-scale user studies (46 and 5 participants respectively). Open questions include whether the self-supervised paradigm generalizes to video or 3D generation, and whether MI-based selection can be made more efficient than exhaustive sampling.
