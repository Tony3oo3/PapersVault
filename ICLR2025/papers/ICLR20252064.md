## Problem

Deterministic vision-language models like CLIP map images and texts to fixed points in Euclidean space, implicitly assuming a one-to-one correspondence that fails to capture the inherent many-to-many nature of image-text matching. This oversimplification prevents the model from representing aleatoric uncertainty (e.g., a general caption like 'a train' matching many images), and prior probabilistic VLMs were either limited in scale, required heavy dedicated modules for uncertainty estimation, or suffered from unstable training at billion-sample scale.

## Input / Output

The model takes 224×224 RGB images and text captions (up to 64 tokens) and outputs a 768-dimensional Gaussian distribution (mean μ from the [CLS] token, log-variance σ² from a dedicated [UNC] token) for each modality, rather than a single embedding vector. For zero-shot classification, the closed-form sampled distance (CSD) between image and text Gaussians replaces cosine similarity, and multiple prompt embeddings are combined as a mixture of Gaussians. The uncertainty values (tr(Σ)) are also directly usable for downstream tasks like prompt re-weighting and hierarchical dataset traversal.

## Method

ProLIP uses the same ViT + Transformer architecture as CLIP/SigLIP but adds a single [UNC] token (placed alongside [CLS]) whose output, after a linear projection with bias initialized to −10, parameterizes log σ² — requiring negligible extra parameters compared to prior PrVLMs that used dedicated attention modules. Training combines three objectives: (1) a Probabilistic Pairwise Contrastive Loss (PPCL) that replaces the unstable BCE-based loss of PCME++ with a log-sigmoid formulation over the CSD distance for stable large-scale convergence; (2) a novel asymmetric Inclusion Loss that enforces distributional inclusion (text ⊃ image, original ⊃ masked) via a squared-pdf overlap measure, making learned uncertainty align with human intuitions about generality; and (3) a VIB regularization to prevent variance collapse. The model is trained from scratch on DataComp 1B (1.28B–12.8B samples) with 32 H100 GPUs, achieving 74.6% ImageNet zero-shot accuracy with ViT-B/16 and improving to 75.8% via Bayesian Prompt Re-Weighting that exploits the learned text uncertainties.

## Results

With ViT-B/16 and 1.28B seen samples, ProLIP achieves 67.8% ImageNet zero-shot accuracy, outperforming CLIP (67.2%) and SigLIP (67.4%), and attains the best average across 38 DataComp evaluation tasks (57.9% vs. 57.1% for CLIP and 56.7% for SigLIP). Scaling to 12.8B seen samples, ProLIP reaches 74.6% ImageNet zero-shot accuracy and 63.3% average across all 38 tasks. With the proposed Bayesian Prompt Re-Weighting (BPRW) under a 9-shot setting, ImageNet accuracy further improves to 75.8% (+1.21 pp over the 80-prompt baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

At the matched 1.28B-sample scale with ViT-B/16, ProLIP outperforms CLIP on ImageNet zero-shot accuracy (67.8% vs. 67.2%), VTAB (58.5% vs. 56.9%), and the 38-task average (57.9% vs. 57.1%), while slightly trailing on retrieval (53.0% vs. 53.4%). Against SigLIP, ProLIP leads on ImageNet (67.8% vs. 67.4%), VTAB (58.5% vs. 55.7%), and average (57.9% vs. 56.7%), but is marginally behind on ImageNet distribution shifts (55.3% vs. 55.4%) and retrieval (53.0% vs. 53.4%). The largest gap over both baselines appears on VTAB, where ProLIP's probabilistic representation provides a 1.6–2.8 pp advantage, suggesting the uncertainty-aware embeddings generalize better to diverse visual tasks. When scaled to 12.8B samples, ProLIP achieves 74.6% ImageNet accuracy, surpassing the CLIP model trained on the same number of samples (73.5% as reported by Ilharco et al., 2021). The uncertainty-based BPRW further extends the lead, reaching 75.8% ImageNet accuracy with only 9 labeled images per class, a +1.21 pp gain over the standard 80-prompt ensemble.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProLIP extends the line of probabilistic vision-language models (PrVLMs) initiated by Chun et al. (2021) and PCME++ (Chun, 2024), which demonstrated that aleatoric uncertainty can be captured in image-text embeddings but were limited to small-scale datasets (CUB, COCO) and required dedicated multi-head modules for uncertainty estimation. The key departures are: (1) replacing the dedicated uncertainty module with a single [UNC] token that requires negligible extra parameters, (2) substituting the binary cross-entropy probabilistic matching loss of PCME++ with a log-sigmoid-based probabilistic pairwise contrastive loss (PPCL) borrowed from SigLIP for stable large-scale training, and (3) introducing a novel inclusion loss that enforces distributional containment relationships. Unlike prior PrVLMs that were trained on frozen CLIP adapters or small caption datasets, ProLIP is the first to train a fully probabilistic VLM from scratch on billion-scale image-text pairs using only probabilistic objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that fully probabilistic pre-training objectives can be scaled to billion-sample datasets without auxiliary deterministic losses, resolving a key scalability barrier that limited prior PrVLMs. The [UNC] token design and inclusion loss offer a practical, parameter-efficient path toward interpretable uncertainty in VLMs, with concrete downstream applications (BPRW prompt re-weighting, uncertainty-based dataset traversal) that go beyond mere classification accuracy. Limitations include the diagonal covariance assumption (discussed in Appendix D), the modest zero-shot gains over deterministic CLIP/SigLIP at matched scale (~0.4–0.6pp), and the fact that BPRW's largest gains require a few-shot setting that partially violates the zero-shot assumption. Open questions include whether full covariance or non-Gaussian distributions would yield meaningfully better uncertainty estimates, and whether the inclusion loss's hyperparameters (ε, c) can be made more principled rather than empirically tuned.
