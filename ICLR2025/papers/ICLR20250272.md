## Problem

Visual imitation learning policies are brittle to appearance perturbations such as lighting changes and object texture variations, because the high-dimensional observation space contains virtually infinite task-irrelevant visual variations. Existing augmentation methods (e.g., SRM, Mix-Spectrum) remove image details indiscriminately without distinguishing low-level photometric features from high-level structural semantics, limiting their generalization in complex real-world settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250272/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images from two RealSense D435I cameras (real-world) or a single rendered image (simulation) and produces low-level robot arm actions via a Diffusion Policy. During training, each observation is preprocessed through t steps of DDPM inversion (typically 15 out of 50 total steps, ~0.2s per image) before being fed to the policy's ResNet18 encoder. Crucially, at test time the policy operates directly on original, un-inverted observations, requiring zero additional inference-time computation.

## Method

Stem-OB applies partial DDPM inversion to training observations, mapping them into a shared 'stem observation' space where low-level appearance differences (lighting, texture, color) are suppressed while high-level scene structure (object silhouettes, spatial arrangement) is preserved. The theoretical justification comes from an attribute loss analysis: under Gaussian diffusion transitions, the overlap between two images' posterior distributions increases monotonically with inversion step, and pairs with smaller (low-level) perturbations become indistinguishable at earlier steps than pairs with structural changes. Practically, the method uses the simplified DDPM forward process with independent per-step noise (no error-reduction correction), making it a single-pass operation that avoids full inversion or reverse-order corrections. The downstream Diffusion Policy is trained entirely in this inverted latent space, yet exhibits zero-shot transfer back to the original observation space at test time, yielding a 22.2% average success-rate improvement over the best baseline on real-world tasks with challenging lighting and appearance changes.

## Results

On real-world robotic tasks (C2P, D2B, OD, ToF), Stem-OB achieves an average success rate of 83.3% across all settings, outperforming the best baseline (Random Overlay at 61.1%) by an average of 22.2%. The improvement is most dramatic under generalization conditions: on D2B, Stem-OB reaches 44.4% vs. 13.3% (Org) and 8.9% (SRM); on OD, it achieves 100% vs. 13.3% (Org). On SAPIEN 3 simulation, Stem-OB reaches 99.1% on PushCube and 27.2% on StackCube, compared to 61.8% and 4.3% for the unmodified baseline. On MimicGen, gains are more modest due to the less photorealistic environment, with Stem-OB achieving 50.5% on Coffee vs. 43.3% (Org).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Stem-OB consistently outperforms all five baselines on the four real-world tasks. Against SRM (Huang et al., 2022), Stem-OB gains 22.2% on C2P (92.6 vs. 70.4), 35.2% on D2B (50.0 vs. 14.8), 72.2% on OD (100.0 vs. 27.8), and 50.0% on ToF (90.7 vs. 40.7). Against Mix-Spectrum (Lee & Hwang, 2024), the advantage is 31.5% on C2P, 24.1% on D2B, 64.8% on OD, and 16.6% on ToF. Against Random Overlay (Yuan et al., 2024b), Stem-OB leads on C2P (92.6 vs. 53.7), D2B (50.0 vs. 20.4), and ToF (90.7 vs. 81.5), while RO is competitive on OD (88.9 vs. 100.0). Against SSL-based encoders R3M (Nair et al., 2022) and MVP (Xiao et al., 2022) on SAPIEN 3, Stem-OB achieves 99.1% on PushCube vs. 19.5% (R3M frozen) and 17.4% (MVP frozen), indicating that diffusion inversion provides a far more effective observation transformation than pretrained visual representations for this task. On MimicGen, Stem-OB does not uniformly dominate (e.g., Mix-Spectrum leads on Threading at 25.8% vs. 16.4%), which the authors attribute to the low-resolution, texture-free nature of those environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

Stem-OB builds on the DDPM inversion framework of Huberman-Spiegelglas et al. (2024) and the pretrained Stable Diffusion model of Rombach et al. (2022), repurposing the forward noising process as a semantic abstraction operator for visual imitation learning. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant data augmentation paradigm (SRM, Mix-Spectrum, Random Overlay) that perturbs observations to encourage invariance, instead applying a deterministic, semantically-aware transformation that selectively erases low-level appearance while preserving high-level structure. The visual IL backbone is Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2023), and the theoretical grounding draws on the attribute loss analysis of Yue et al. (2024) to formalize why similar images converge faster in the inversion process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature (no inference-time inversion, no architectural changes) and the 22.2% average improvement in real-world robotic tasks make this a practically attractive preprocessing step for deployment. However, the method is only validated with Diffusion Policy as the IL backbone, underperforms in less photorealistic environments (MimicGen), and the optimal inversion step must be tuned empirically per task. Open questions include compatibility with other IL algorithms (ACT, GAIL), cross-category generalization (only preliminary PushCube results with YCB objects), and whether the approach transfers to video-based or multi-modal observation settings.
