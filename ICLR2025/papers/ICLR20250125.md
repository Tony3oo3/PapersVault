## Problem

Existing keyframe interpolation methods built on image-to-video diffusion models (e.g., TRF, Generative Inbetweening) fuse temporally forward and backward denoising paths via linear interpolation on the noisy manifold, causing off-manifold deviations that produce artifacts. Mitigating these artifacts requires multiple iterative re-noising steps or model fine-tuning, substantially increasing computational cost. No training-free approach existed that could perform bounded two-frame interpolation while keeping the sampling trajectory on-manifold.

## Input / Output

The method takes two RGB keyframes (start and end) as input, which are encoded into CLIP image embeddings to condition the Stable Video Diffusion model (replacing its text-embedding conditioning). It outputs a 25-frame interpolated video at 1024×576 resolution, generated in 195 seconds on a single RTX 3090 GPU. The method also supports static bounds (identical start and end frames) and dynamic bounds with varying end frames, and the micro-conditioning fps parameter is adjusted based on motion magnitude.

## Method

The core contribution is a bidirectional sampling strategy that replaces parallel path fusion with sequential sampling: at each timestep, the method first denoises along the temporally forward path conditioned on the start frame, re-noises the result back to the current noise level using stochastic noise, then denoises along the temporally backward path (time-flipped) conditioned on the end frame. This sequential chaining keeps the sampling trajectory within the SVD manifold, eliminating the off-manifold artifacts inherent in linear interpolation of two distinct sample points. Two additional manifold guidance techniques are integrated: CFG++ replaces standard CFG in the Euler-step update (using the unconditional score to avoid CFG's own off-manifold bias), and DDS guidance applies Krylov-subspace optimization to the denoised estimates to enforce alignment of the last generated frame with the target boundary condition. The entire pipeline is training-free, requiring no fine-tuning of SVD or multiple re-noising iterations.

## Results

On the DAVIS dataset (large, varied motions), ViBiDSampler (Full) achieves the best LPIPS (0.2355), FID (35.659), and FVD (399.15) among all methods, outperforming the strongest diffusion-based baseline Generative Inbetweening by 16% in LPIPS and 19% in FVD. On the Pexels dataset (natural/sports motions), ViBiDSampler (Full) achieves the best FVD (452.34) and FID (37.341), though FILM retains an advantage on pixel-level LPIPS (0.0821 vs. 0.1366) due to its PSNR-oriented training. The method is entirely training-free, requiring only 50 NFE and 195 seconds to generate 25 frames at 1024×576 on a single RTX 3090 GPU—roughly 2.3× faster than TRF (443 s) and 6.3× faster than Generative Inbetweening (1222 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FILM (flow-based, ICCV 2019), ViBiDSampler (Full) wins decisively on DAVIS across all three metrics (LPIPS 0.2355 vs. 0.2697, FID 35.659 vs. 40.241, FVD 399.15 vs. 833.80) and on Pexels FVD (452.34 vs. 559.16), but loses on Pexels LPIPS and FID where FILM's PSNR-oriented training gives it a pixel-level edge. Against TRF (time-reversal fusion, arXiv 2024), ViBiDSampler (Full) dominates on both datasets—e.g., DAVIS FVD 399.15 vs. 622.16 and Pexels FVD 452.34 vs. 880.97—confirming that the sequential bidirectional sampling avoids the off-manifold artifacts of TRF's parallel fusion. Against DynamiCrafter (arXiv 2023), ViBiDSampler (Full) achieves better LPIPS (0.2355 vs. 0.3274) and FVD (399.15 vs. 538.36) on DAVIS without any fine-tuning, while DynamiCrafter only generates 16 frames at 512×320 versus 25 frames at 1024×576. Against Generative Inbetweening (arXiv 2024), ViBiDSampler (Full) wins on DAVIS FVD (399.15 vs. 490.34) and Pexels FVD (452.34 vs. 746.26) while being training-free and 6.3× faster at inference (195 s vs. 1222 s), whereas Generative Inbetweening requires SVD fine-tuning and 300 NFE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the time-reversal fusion strategy used by TRF and Generative Inbetweening, which compute forward and backward denoising paths in parallel and linearly interpolate between them. The authors identify this parallel fusion as the source of off-manifold artifacts and instead propose sequential bidirectional sampling with a single stochastic re-noising step between the two paths, keeping the trajectory on the SVD manifold. The method further repurposes CFG++ (originally for text-to-image alignment) and DDS (originally for image inverse problems) as manifold-constrained guidance for bounded video generation, combining them with the bidirectional sampling in a training-free pipeline built on Stable Video Diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature and 2.3× speedup over TRF (195s vs 443s for 25 frames at 1024×576) make this a practical option for keyframe interpolation without the fine-tuning overhead of competing methods. The conceptual insight that sequential sampling inherently avoids the off-manifold problem that plagues parallel fusion is a useful diagnostic for the broader video diffusion community. Limitations include evaluation only on SVD with 25-frame clips at a single resolution, and the approach has not been tested on longer sequences or alternative video diffusion backbones, leaving open whether the sequential strategy scales to minute-long interpolations or models with different temporal architectures.
