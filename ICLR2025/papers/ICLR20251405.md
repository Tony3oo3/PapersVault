## Problem

While DPO has achieved empirical success in language model alignment, the optimization-theoretic impact of different sampling strategies on its convergence rates remained under-explored. Prior theoretical work analyzed samplers from a data/sample-complexity perspective with impractical assumptions (e.g., MLE oracles), leaving open whether specific online samplers could provably accelerate gradient descent convergence. This paper fills that gap by establishing a sharp separation: uniform sampling yields only linear convergence, while appropriately designed mixed samplers achieve quadratic convergence.

## Input / Output

The method consumes human preference pairs (prompt, winning response, losing response) annotated by a gold reward model, a fixed reference policy π_ref, and the current policy parameters θ. The key design choice is the sampling distribution over response pairs: rather than drawing both responses from a single policy, the algorithm draws them from a mixture of an on-policy component (π_θ for both) and a reward/policy-guided component (π_θ^{3/2}π_ref^{-1/2} for the winner, π_θ^{1/2}π_ref^{1/2} for the loser), with a mixing ratio determined by the reward margin. The output is the updated policy parameters θ after T gradient descent steps, with the convergence guarantee expressed as a bound on the reward-policy gap δ(y,y';θ).

## Method

The core insight is that by choosing the sampling distribution proportional to 1/σ'(r(y)−r(y')), the linear term in the Taylor expansion of the DPO gradient (Δ) in terms of the reward-policy gap (δ) is exactly cancelled by the gradient update, leaving only quadratic remainders and thus achieving quadratic convergence. The paper formalizes this as two samplers: DPO-Mix-R (reward-guided, requiring access to the true reward) and DPO-Mix-P (policy-difference-guided, using β log(π_θ/π_ref) as a reward surrogate), both mixed with a uniform component. For practical implementation, the hybrid sampling distributions are approximated via logit mixing (weighted sum of per-token logits of π_θ and π_ref), and a posterior distribution π_θ^{2β} is set over the action space to align sampling with the performance difference lemma. Under noisy gradients, the method achieves linear convergence to the noise scale (14σ) in O(log(1/σ)) iterations, with a rejection sampling mechanism to handle policy deviation in DPO-Mix-P.

## Results

On Safe-RLHF (iteration 3), the proposed method achieves a test win-rate of 80.7% and test reward of -0.903, outperforming vanilla DPO (73.3% win-rate, -1.372 reward) by over 7.4% in win-rate. On Iterative-Prompt (iteration 3), it reaches 84.4% test win-rate and 3.321 test reward, a 5.4% win-rate improvement over vanilla DPO (79.0%, 2.101). The method also dominates On-policy DPO and Hybrid GSHF across all metrics on both datasets at the final iteration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three baselines: Vanilla DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), On-policy DPO (Guo et al., 2024), and Hybrid GSHF (Xiong et al., 2024). On Safe-RLHF at iteration 3, the proposed method wins on all four metrics: it achieves 80.7% test win-rate vs. 73.3% (Vanilla DPO), 78.3% (On-policy DPO), and 78.7% (Hybrid GSHF), and -0.903 test reward vs. -1.372, -0.989, and -1.039 respectively. On Iterative-Prompt at iteration 3, it similarly leads with 84.4% test win-rate and 3.321 test reward, beating Vanilla DPO (79.0%, 2.101), On-policy DPO (83.5%, 3.132), and Hybrid GSHF (82.1%, 2.490). The advantage is most pronounced against Vanilla DPO (7.4% and 5.4% win-rate gains) and Hybrid GSHF (2.0% and 2.3% gains), while the margin over On-policy DPO is smaller (2.4% and 0.9%). The paper also shows via KL-divergence curves that the proposed method does not overfit or deviate excessively from the reference policy, maintaining a better reward-KL trade-off than baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant sample-complexity perspective on DPO samplers (Xiong et al., 2024; Xie et al., 2024; Song et al., 2024), which typically assumes access to an MLE oracle, by instead analyzing convergence rates of gradient descent under different sampling strategies. It draws on the policy-gradient convergence literature (Mei et al., 2020; Agarwal et al., 2021) for its tabular softmax analysis framework, and positions its mixed samplers as a unifying lens: vanilla DPO and on-policy DPO both reduce to DPO-Unif, while Hybrid GSHF and Online GSHF approximate DPO-Mix-P and DPO-Mix-R respectively. The practical algorithm further borrows logit mixing (Shi et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024a) to implement the theoretically-motivated hybrid distributions in a way compatible with autoregressive decoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean separation between linear (uniform) and quadratic (mixed) convergence provides a principled explanation for why on-policy and reward-guided sampling outperform uniform sampling in DPO, and offers a template for designing future samplers by choosing posterior distributions and mixing ratios. However, the analysis is restricted to tabular softmax parametrization, which is far from practical LM settings (log-linear, function approximation), and the empirical validation is limited to two datasets with a gold reward model rather than open benchmarks. Open questions include whether the quadratic convergence advantage persists under realistic gradient noise and function approximation, and whether the posterior choice (π_θ^{2β}) is optimal or merely one of many useful options.
