## Problem

CLIP ViTs fine-tuned with Region-Language Alignment (RLA) project dense visual embeddings into a text-oriented domain, causing a notable degradation in spatial awareness that is critical for dense prediction tasks. Prior RLA work focuses exclusively on language alignment and overlooks the visual-centric quality of dense features, leaving open-vocabulary detection and segmentation suboptimal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250521/cropped_page1_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images along with region proposals (from a trained RPN or ground-truth COCO boxes/masks) and language supervision signals (either CLIP text-encoder embeddings or the image encoder's [CLS] tokens of cropped regions). It produces a fine-tuned CLIP image encoder whose dense feature maps retain spatial structure, which downstream detectors (F-ViT) and segmenters (Cat-Seg, MaskCLIP) then use to generate bounding boxes, segmentation masks, and class predictions. The Refiner operates on RoIAlign'd features from intermediate CLIP layers (first N−K blocks frozen, last K blocks cloned), producing refined L×D token sequences per region.

## Method

The framework has two key components. First, Spatial Correlation Distillation (SCD) computes intra-region spatial correlation matrices (Z·Zᵀ) from both the student and frozen teacher CLIP, applies softmax normalization with a temperature parameter, and minimizes cross-entropy between them—preserving spatial structure without requiring cross-domain feature consistency, so it integrates seamlessly with RLA. Second, a lightweight Refiner (initialized from the last K attention blocks of CLIP) is trained with a global-to-local InfoNCE objective: it takes RoIAlign'd features from the frozen early CLIP layers and aligns them token-by-token against context-free local crops processed by the full frozen CLIP, effectively filtering out semantic contamination from surrounding context. In the final R-SC-RLA pipeline, the Refiner's output replaces the raw teacher features in the SCD correlation matrix, while the Refiner itself does not participate in the RLA branch, preserving language supervision integrity.

## Results

On OV-COCO, R-SC-CLIPSelf with ViT-L/14 achieves 48.1 AP₅₀ⁿᵒᵛᵉˡ, surpassing the previous SOTA DITO (46.1) and CORA+ (43.1), while improving over the CLIPSelf baseline (44.3) by 3.8 points. On OV-LVIS, R-SC-CLIPSelf (ViT-L/14) reaches 37.2 mAPᵣ, outperforming CoDet (37.0) and CLIPSelf (34.9) by 2.3 points. For open-vocabulary segmentation, Cat-Seg+R-SC-V (ViT-B/16) achieves 32.7 mIoU on ADE-150 and 58.0 mIoU on PASCAL Context, consistently exceeding both vanilla Cat-Seg and Cat-Seg+CLIPSelf. The method also improves off-the-shelf zero-shot segmentation (MaskCLIP) by 4.2–4.8 mIoU across multiple CLIP variants (EVA-CLIP, Meta-CLIP, DFN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against CLIPSelf (Wu et al., 2023b), the strongest RLA baseline: R-SC-CLIPSelf improves AP₅₀ⁿᵒᵛᵉˡ by +3.3 (ViT-B/16: 40.9 vs 37.6) and +3.8 (ViT-L/14: 48.1 vs 44.3) on OV-COCO, and by +2.2 (ViT-B/16: 27.5 vs 25.3) and +2.3 (ViT-L/14: 37.2 vs 34.9) on OV-LVIS. Against the prior SOTA DITO (Kim et al., 2023c), R-SC-CLIPSelf (ViT-L/14) wins on OV-COCO (48.1 vs 46.1) but trails slightly on OV-LVIS (37.2 vs 38.4). On segmentation, Cat-Seg+R-SC-V (ViT-B/16) achieves 32.7 mIoU on ADE-150, beating Cat-Seg (31.8) and Cat-Seg+CLIPSelf (30.8), while Cat-Seg+R-SC-V (ViT-L/14) reaches 38.4 mIoU vs Cat-Seg's 37.9. The method also outperforms RegionCLIP/RegionText by 3.5–6.6 AP₅₀ⁿᵒᵛᵉˡ on OV-COCO, and improves DINOv2's unsupervised segmentation by +1.9 mIoU on Cityscapes (31.8 vs 29.9) and +1.1 mIoU on COCO-Stuff (44.1 vs 43.0). Notably, the visual-centric R-SC-V variant (without any RLA branch) still improves Cat-Seg segmentation, confirming that spatial awareness gains transfer independently of language alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Region-Language Alignment (RLA) line of work (RegionCLIP, CLIPSelf) by identifying and addressing a critical blind spot: RLA fine-tuning degrades the spatial awareness of CLIP ViTs that is essential for dense prediction. It repurposes correlation distillation (Li et al., 2020; Zhang & Ma, 2023) from its traditional single-modal knowledge-transfer setting into a cross-modal spatial-structure preservation mechanism (SCD), and introduces a lightweight Refiner module that exploits the observation that CLIP's dense features contain high-quality spatial signals masked by semantic contamination from context. The approach is deliberately complementary to existing RLA methods rather than replacing them, positioning itself as a visual-centric add-on to the vision-language alignment pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a useful diagnostic (RLA degrades spatial structure) and a practical, plug-in remedy that improves open-vocabulary detection and segmentation by 2–4% without architectural changes to downstream detectors. The finding that standard visual-centric self-supervised objectives (DenseCL, iBOT) actively conflict with RLA supervision is a non-trivial negative result that cautions against naively combining the two paradigms. Open questions include whether the Refiner's global-to-local training strategy generalizes beyond ViT backbones, whether the two-stage training requirement can be eliminated without performance loss, and whether the semantic-contamination framing extends to other frozen encoders beyond CLIP variants.
