## Problem

Binarizing or ternarizing the recurrent weights of vanilla RNNs has never been achieved: prior work (Ott et al., 2016) explicitly reported that BinaryConnect on recurrent weights 'never worked,' and while LSTMs/GRUs can be quantized, they fail on long-term dependency tasks like the 1000-timestep copy problem. This paper addresses the gap by constructing orthogonal RNNs whose recurrent weights are exactly binary or sparse ternary, enabling kilobyte-sized models that still handle long-range dependencies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251790/cropped_page20_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes variable-length sequences (one-hot characters of length 1020, 784 pixel values, 500 word embeddings of dimension 512, or 125–128 sensor readings) and produces either a single class label (digit, sentiment, activity) or a per-timestep character sequence. The hidden dimension d_h must be a power of 2 (HadamRNN) or of the form q·2^k (Block-HadamRNN), and the input/output matrices U and V are quantized to p ≥ 2 bits via uniform quantization with a scaling parameter. Notably, the recurrent unit is linear (no activation applied to the hidden state), which the authors show is essential for memorization over long delays.

## Method

The recurrent weight matrix is parameterized as W(u) = (1/√d_h)·diag(u)·S_{2^k}, where S_{2^k} is the Sylvester Hadamard matrix and u ∈ {−1,1}^{d_h} is a trainable binary vector optimized via the straight-through estimator. This yields exactly 2^{d_h} distinct orthogonal matrices from just d_h bits of storage, since flipping any subset of rows of a Hadamard matrix preserves orthogonality. For the sparse ternary variant (Block-HadamRNN), the weight becomes W(u) = (1/√2^k)·diag(u)·(I_q ⊗ S_{2^k}), introducing zeros so that only 1/q of entries are non-zero and the recurrent multiplication cost drops by a factor of q. The architecture uses a linear recurrent unit h_t = Wh_{t−1} + Ux_t + b_i (no ReLU in the recurrent step), which the authors prove theoretically and verify empirically preserves the norm of input perturbations regardless of temporal distance, unlike ReLU-ORNNs where the diagonal Jacobian D_s progressively attenuates early inputs.

## Results

On the Copy task (T=1020), HadamRNN (d_h=128, 1-bit W, 4-bit U&V, 12-bit activations) achieves a cross-entropy of 2.3×10⁻⁷ at just 1.40 kB, making it the first binary recurrent-weights model to solve the 1000+ timestep copy task. On pMNIST, the fully quantized HadamRNN (d_h=512) reaches 94.90% accuracy at 3.58 kB, compared to 97.00% for a full-precision ORNN at 1046 kB. On sMNIST, HadamRNN achieves 96.34% at 3.58 kB (vs. 98.6% for a binary LSTM at 5.11 kB). On IMDB, HadamRNN (d_h=512) reaches 87.13% accuracy at 129 kB, outperforming the 2-bit LSTM of He et al. (88.12% at 514 kB) while being 4× smaller.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the Copy task, HadamRNN decisively outperforms QORNN (Foucault et al., 2024): 2.3×10⁻⁷ vs. 2.5×10⁻³ cross-entropy at 1.40 kB vs. 50.6 kB (36× smaller), while full-precision ORNN (Kiani et al., 2022) achieves 1.1×10⁻¹² but requires 275 kB; LSTMs (Jing et al., 2017) fail entirely. On pMNIST, HadamRNN (94.90% at 3.58 kB) matches or exceeds QORNN (94.76% at 35 kB) and outperforms the 170-dim full-precision ORNN (94.30% at 120 kB), though it trails the 512-dim full-precision ORNN (97.00% at 1046 kB) by ~2%. On sMNIST, HadamRNN (96.34%) is outperformed by the binary LSTM of Ardakani et al. (98.6%) and FastGRNN (98.2%) by ~2%, but it surpasses QORNN (96.2% at 35 kB) at 10× smaller size. On IMDB, HadamRNN (87.13% at 129 kB) beats the binary LSTM of Alom et al. (76.25–79.64% at 40–80 kB) by 7–11%, while the 2-bit LSTM of He et al. (88.12%) edges it out by ~1% at 4× the size. On GLUE, Block-HadamRNN (82.1% at 33 kB on QQP) surpasses the smallest BiBERT (78.2% at 4400 kB) by ~4% at 133× smaller size, though on SST-2 it trails the smallest BiBERT (85.4% at 4400 kB) by 3.5%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the ORNN architecture (Arjovsky et al., 2016), which constrains the recurrent weight matrix to be orthogonal to prevent gradient vanishing and improve long-term memory. It departs from prior quantization work on recurrent networks (Ott et al., 2016; Ardakani et al., 2019; He et al., 2016), which focused exclusively on gated architectures (LSTM/GRU) and explicitly reported that binary recurrent weights 'never worked' for vanilla RNNs. The key conceptual departure is using Hadamard matrix theory—specifically Sylvester matrices combined with row-sign switching—to parameterize a learnable subset of binary orthogonal matrices, a structural constraint that makes 1-bit recurrent weights viable where unconstrained binarization fails. The linear recurrent unit (rather than ReLU) is motivated by SSM literature (Gu et al., 2022) and is shown to be critical for memorization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that vanilla RNNs can operate with 1-bit recurrent weights, breaking a long-standing assumption in the quantization literature and enabling model sizes in the kilobyte range (vs. tens of megabytes for quantized transformers) while solving long-term dependency tasks like the 1000-timestep copy problem. The Block-HadamRNN variant offers a tunable sparsity-complexity trade-off via the Kronecker product parameter q, making it practical for edge deployment. Open questions include whether the power-of-2 constraint on hidden dimension limits applicability, whether the approach extends to Structured State Space Models for even longer sequences (Long Range Arena), and whether the method generalizes beyond the specific Hadamard/Sylvester family to other structured orthogonal parameterizations.
