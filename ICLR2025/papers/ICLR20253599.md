## Problem

First-order optimizers like Adam approximate curvature with simple diagonal second-moment statistics, leading to suboptimal convergence and poor generalization, while existing second-order methods (K-FAC, Shampoo, AdaHessian) incur prohibitive per-iteration costs, struggle to generalize across architectures (especially Transformers), and demand extensive hyperparameter tuning. The paper addresses the gap by asking whether a principled, computationally tractable approximation of the Fisher Information Matrix can deliver second-order convergence benefits at first-order computational cost.

## Input / Output

At each training step, AdaFisher consumes per-layer activations h_{i-1} and pre-activation derivatives s_i (obtained during the forward and backward passes) to compute diagonal Kronecker factors H_{D_{i-1}} and S_{D_i}, which are smoothed via an EMA scheme (decay γ=0.8) and Min-Max normalized. The output is a per-parameter update Δθ = -α(F̃_D^{-1} m) that replaces Adam's √v second-moment denominator with the inverse of the diagonal FIM approximation, yielding updated parameters θ^{(t+1)}. Unlike Adam, no square root is applied to the second moment, and the FIM naturally incorporates its own EMA, eliminating the need for a separate second-moment decay.

## Method

AdaFisher's key insight is that the Kronecker factors (KFs) of the FIM are empirically diagonally concentrated, validated via Gershgorin circle theorem analysis and eigenvalue perturbation under Gaussian noise on ResNet-18 layers. Building on this, the paper proposes a diagonal block-Kronecker FIM approximation F̃_{D_i} = H'_{D_{i-1}} ⊗ S'_{D_i} + λI (with λ=0.001 Tikhonov damping), where H and S retain only their diagonal elements after Min-Max normalization, guaranteeing eigenvalues in [λ, 1+λ] for stable inversion. This approximation is integrated into the Adam framework by substituting the conventional second moment v^{(t)} with F̃_D^{(t)}, while preserving Adam's momentum term and removing the square root to stay faithful to second-order Taylor expansion. The method also introduces a novel KF computation for normalization layers (BatchNorm/LayerNorm) via Proposition 3.1, and provides convergence proofs for both convex (O(1/k)) and non-convex stochastic (O(log T/√T)) settings, matching Adam-type regret bounds.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k with ResNet-50 (batch size 256, single GPU), AdaFisher achieves 76.95% Top-1 and 93.39% Top-5 accuracy, outperforming Shampoo (72.82%), K-FAC (70.96%), and Adam (67.78%) by 4.1, 6.0, and 9.2 points respectively. On CIFAR-10, AdaFisher reaches 96.25–96.72% across ResNet/DenseNet architectures, and 88.74% on Tiny Swin where K-FAC and Shampoo collapse to ~64%. For language modeling, AdaFisherW achieves 152.72 PPL on WikiText-2 and 41.15 on PTB, beating AdamW (175.06 / 44.70) by 22.3 and 3.55 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaFisher wins against all nine baselines on every dataset and architecture tested. On ImageNet-1k (ResNet-50, batch 256), AdaFisher's 76.95% Top-1 exceeds Shampoo (72.82%) by 4.1 points, K-FAC (70.96%) by 6.0 points, and Adam (67.78%) by 9.2 points, and even surpasses LAMB at 16K batch size (76.66%) while using 64× fewer samples per step. On CIFAR-10/100, the advantage is most dramatic for ViT architectures: on Tiny Swin (CIFAR-10), AdaFisher reaches 88.74% while K-FAC and Shampoo collapse to 64.79% and 63.91% respectively, and on FocalNet (CIFAR-100) AdaFisher achieves 53.69% versus 9.98% (K-FAC) and 9.18% (Shampoo). For language modeling, AdaFisherW achieves 152.72 PPL on WikiText-2, beating AdamW (175.06) by 22.3 points; Shampoo fails to converge (1727.75 PPL) and AdaHessian degrades to 407.69. The only baseline that comes close on standard CNNs is SGD (e.g., 95.98% vs 96.39% on ResNet-101/CIFAR-10), but AdaFisher consistently leads by 0.3–3.7 points across all CNN architectures on both CIFAR datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaFisher sits at the intersection of two well-established lines of work: the adaptive first-order framework of Adam (which uses a diagonal second moment of squared gradients) and the Kronecker-factored approximate curvature (K-FAC) approach to the Fisher Information Matrix. The key departure is replacing Adam's simple diagonal second moment with a diagonal block-Kronecker approximation of the FIM, justified by an empirical Gershgorin-based analysis showing that Kronecker factors are diagonally concentrated. This positions AdaFisher as a 'lightweight second-order' method that retains Adam's momentum structure and computational footprint while incorporating richer curvature information, and it extends the K-FAC framework to normalization layers where prior methods were silent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant result is achieving 76.95% Top-1 on ImageNet-1k with a single GPU and batch size 256, surpassing methods like LAMB that require 16K batch sizes, which democratizes competitive training for resource-constrained settings. The optimizer also demonstrates notably better hyperparameter stability than Adam across learning rates and batch sizes, reducing tuning burden. A key limitation is that AdaFisher is designed for negative log-likelihood losses (exponential family outputs), so its FIM-based preconditioning may not capture true curvature for other loss functions. Open questions include applicability to generative models, graph neural networks, and whether CUDA-optimized Kronecker factor computation can further close the per-iteration gap with pure first-order methods.
