## Problem

Existing methods for characterizing neuronal invariance either approximate the underlying continuous invariance manifold with a finite set of discrete stimuli or learn continuous manifolds but lack a mechanism to spatially align and compare them across neurons with differing receptive field attributes (position, size, orientation). This makes systematic, population-level analysis of invariance types and functional clustering of neurons based on shared invariances under-explored.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen response-predicting DNN and generates grayscale images via an INR that maps 2D pixel coordinates (x, y) and a 1D periodic latent variable z ∈ [0, 2π) to pixel values in [-1, 1], with images linearly scaled to a fixed norm before being fed to the DNN. The output is a continuous family of invariance manifold images per neuron, a learned affine transformation per neuron pair, an asymmetric N×N activation matrix of relative activations (normalized so 0 = baseline, 1 = MEI response), and functional clusters obtained via agglomerative hierarchical clustering with cosine dissimilarity on the matrix rows.

## Method

The approach has two stages. In Template Learning, an INR (4-layer fully connected network, 50 units per layer, Tanh activations, with random Fourier positional encoding on both pixel coordinates and the latent variable) is trained to generate a diverse family of images that all maximally excite a target neuron, using a combined objective of normalized activation maximization plus a SimCLR-style contrastive loss that encourages smoothness among nearby latent points and diversity among distant ones. In Template Matching, the INR parameters are frozen and an affine transformation on pixel coordinates is learned to spatially align the template neuron's manifold to a target neuron, maximizing the target's response to the transformed manifold. Pairwise alignment across all neurons yields an activation matrix whose asymmetry encodes subset relationships between invariance manifolds (e.g., a simple cell's Gabor pattern activates a complex cell but not vice versa), and agglomerative hierarchical clustering on this matrix recovers functional clusters invariant to nuisance RF differences.

## Results

On 36 simulated Gabor-based neurons (6 types × 6 instances with varying affine RF properties), the method perfectly recovers ground truth functional clusters via hierarchical clustering on the activation matrix, with within-cluster aligned manifolds activating neurons near their MEI response and cross-cluster alignments consistently lower. On 100 macaque V1 neurons, the method identifies 5 functional clusters including canonical simple cells (Cluster 1, Gabor-selective with no invariance) and complex cells (Cluster 2, phase-invariant), with cluster consistency across 5 seeds yielding an Adjusted Rand Index of 0.75. Cross-model generalization confirms robustness: invariance manifolds learned from Model 1 activate the same neurons in an architecturally different Model 2 at 89% of Model 2's own manifold strength.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two model-based clustering methods: Klindt et al. (2017), a translation-equivariant CNN that clusters neurons by readout feature weights, and Ustyuzhaninov et al. (2019), a rotation-equivariant CNN extending invariance to orientation. On a controlled 40-neuron simulation (odd/even simple cells × 2 sizes × 2 orientations × 5 positions), Klindt et al. correctly clusters neurons only when size and orientation are held fixed, while Ustyuzhaninov et al. additionally handles orientation but still fails when RF size varies. The proposed method, by learning a full affine transformation during template matching, correctly clusters neurons by type regardless of position, orientation, scale, and shear, and is model-agnostic (it works identically on both the CNN-based and rotation-equivariant CNN-based response-predicting models). The baselines also require a specific model architecture with a factorized readout, whereas the proposed method applies to any response-predicting DNN without architectural constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Baroni et al. (2023), which first used Implicit Neural Representations (INRs) to learn continuous invariance manifolds for individual visual neurons, by adding a template matching stage that learns affine transformations to spatially align one neuron's manifold onto another. Prior invariance characterization methods (Cadena et al. 2018; Ding et al. 2023) relied on discrete sets of maximally exciting inputs, and model-based clustering approaches (Klindt et al. 2017; Ustyuzhaninov et al. 2019) handled only translation or rotation invariance without accounting for RF size. The paper positions itself at the intersection of single-neuron invariance characterization and population-level functional clustering, filling the gap of comparing invariance properties across neurons with differing receptive field attributes in a model-agnostic framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a principled, interpretable, and model-agnostic tool for systematically exploring the neural invariance landscape at the population level, potentially revealing functional cell types beyond the canonical simple/complex dichotomy. The identification of clusters with checkerboard-like texture selectivity and multi-directional phase invariance in macaque V1 is intriguing but remains unvalidated by closed-loop neurophysiology, and the quadratic scaling limits applicability to large populations. Open questions include whether higher-dimensional invariance manifolds are needed for other cortical areas, whether the discovered clusters reflect biological reality or model/preprocessing artifacts, and how the approach generalizes across species to reveal conserved or divergent computational principles.
