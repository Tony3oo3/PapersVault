## Problem

Error analysis for discrete diffusion models remains underdeveloped compared to continuous counterparts, largely due to the absence of a discrete analogue of Girsanov's theorem. Existing theoretical results (e.g., Campbell et al. 2022 for τ-leaping in TV distance, Chen & Ying 2024 for uniformization on {0,1}^d) are algorithm-specific and difficult to generalize, leaving no unified framework to decompose and bound inference error across different discretization schemes.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a target distribution p_0 on a finite state space X, a (time-homogeneous, symmetric) rate matrix Q governing the forward continuous-time Markov chain, and a neural network score estimator ŝ_t^θ trained via score entropy. The primary output is not a sample but a set of KL divergence error bounds D_KL(p_δ ‖ q̂_{T−δ}) decomposed into truncation (exp(−ρT) log|X|), approximation (ε), and discretization (D̄²κT) terms, with explicit parameter choices (T, κ, N) yielding D_KL ≤ ε. The bounds apply to both τ-leaping (N = Õ(d²) steps) and uniformization (N = Õ(d) steps) implementations, with the dimensionality d entering through the rate matrix bound D̄ and state space size log|X|.

## Method

The paper generalizes the Poisson random measure to one with a time-inhomogeneous, state-dependent (evolving) intensity λ_t(y), rigorously establishing well-definedness via an augmented measure-space construction. It then defines Lévy-type stochastic integrals w.r.t. this measure and proves a change-of-measure theorem (Theorem 3.3) that is the discrete analogue of Girsanov's theorem: under a Radon-Nikodym derivative Z_t[h] built from the intensity ratio, the Poisson random measure's intensity transforms multiplicatively. Both τ-leaping and uniformization are recast as stochastic integrals with piecewise-constant or augmented intensities (Propositions 4.1–4.2), enabling a unified error decomposition into truncation, approximation, and discretization components that mirrors the continuous diffusion analysis of Benton et al. (2023a). The key technical advance is obtaining the first KL divergence convergence bound for τ-leaping (stronger than prior TV-distance results) under relaxed assumptions on the score function, and showing that the quadratic dimensionality of τ-leaping stems from the discretization error term absent in uniformization.

## Results

The paper establishes the first KL divergence error bound for the τ-leaping scheme: D_KL(p_δ ‖ q̂_{T−δ}) ≲ exp(−ρT) log|X| + ε + D̄²κT, requiring N = Õ(d²) total steps. For the uniformization scheme, the bound is D_KL(p_δ ‖ q_{T−δ}) ≲ exp(−ρT) log|X| + ε with E[N] = Õ(d) steps, matching the linear dimensionality dependence of state-of-the-art continuous diffusion model theory (Benton et al., 2023a). The truncation error is governed by the modified log-Sobolev constant ρ of the rate matrix, and the discretization error for τ-leaping scales quadratically in D̄ (the maximum row sum of the rate matrix), explaining its Õ(d²) complexity versus the Õ(d) for uniformization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's τ-leaping result improves upon Campbell et al. (2022), which provided only a total variation distance bound via Markov chain analysis, by establishing a strictly stronger KL divergence guarantee using the new stochastic integral framework. The uniformization result generalizes Chen & Ying (2024), which was restricted to the binary state space X = {0,1}^d, to arbitrary finite state spaces with symmetric rate matrices, while maintaining the same Õ(d) step complexity. Compared to the continuous diffusion model theory of Benton et al. (2023a), the discrete τ-leaping scheme incurs an additional quadratic dimensionality factor (Õ(d²) vs. Õ(d)) due to the discretization error term D̄²κT, whereas uniformization achieves the same linear rate as the continuous case. The paper also relaxes assumptions relative to prior work: it does not require a uniform L^∞ bound on the true score function, and the score estimation assumption (Assumption 4.6) is tied more closely to the practical training loss rather than pointwise accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continuous diffusion model error analysis paradigm (truncation + approximation + discretization, as in Benton et al. 2023a) to the discrete setting by constructing the missing stochastic calculus infrastructure: a Poisson random measure with time-inhomogeneous, state-dependent evolving intensity, a corresponding Lévy-type stochastic integral, and a change-of-measure theorem analogous to Girsanov's theorem. It builds on Protter's (1983) and Glasserman & Merener's (2004) state-dependent intensity Poisson measures, Gillespie's (2001) τ-leaping, and Van Dijk's (1992) uniformization, but departs from prior discrete diffusion error analyses (Campbell et al. 2022 in TV distance; Chen & Ying 2024 for {0,1}^d) by providing a unified, algorithm-agnostic framework that yields the first KL divergence bound for τ-leaping and relaxes assumptions on the rate matrix and score estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first principled decomposition of inference error in discrete diffusion models into truncation, approximation, and discretization components, directly mirroring the continuous case and enabling cross-domain transfer of techniques. The quadratic dimensionality scaling of τ-leaping versus linear scaling of uniformization (both Õ(d²) vs Õ(d)) gives concrete guidance for algorithm selection, and the conjecture that Õ(d) is optimal in the discrete case opens a clear research direction. Limitations include the restriction to time-homogeneous, symmetric rate matrices and the absence of empirical validation; extending to asymmetric and time-inhomogeneous settings, as well as exploring the continuum limit, are identified as important next steps.
