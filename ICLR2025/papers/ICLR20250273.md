## Problem

Consistency models must learn to map every intermediate point along the PF ODE trajectory to its data endpoint, forcing a single network to simultaneously handle denoising at small times and generation at large times. This dual-task requirement limits one-step generation quality, as the model implicitly sacrifices denoising capacity for generation in an uncontrolled manner. The paper asks whether explicitly restricting the training time range can free network capacity for generation and improve sample quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250273/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a sample from a Gaussian prior N(0, T²I) at the terminal time T (and optionally a class label for ImageNet 64×64) and produces a synthetic image in 1 or 2 network function evaluations. Experiments use 32×32 CIFAR-10 and 64×64 ImageNet images, with EDM/EDM2 architectures (55.7M–1.1B parameters). For text-to-image, the method is applied to Stable Diffusion 1.5 with classifier-free guidance, producing 512×512 images from text prompts.

## Method

Truncated Consistency Models (TCM) generalize the consistency training objective from the full time range [0, T] to a truncated range [t′, T], where t′ (set to 1) separates denoising from generation tasks. A two-stage procedure prevents collapse: Stage 1 trains a standard consistency model to convergence, and Stage 2 initializes a new model with those weights and trains only on [t′, T], using the Stage 1 model as a frozen boundary condition at t′. A new parameterization (Eq. 5) routes inputs below t′ to the pretrained model and above t′ to the trainable model, while a boundary loss term (weighted by w_b) enforces agreement at the dividing time. The time sampling distribution is a mixture of a Dirac delta at t′ and a log-Student-t distribution over (t′, T], with the heavy-tailed Student-t component allocating more probability mass to larger times to emphasize generation. This explicit capacity reallocation yields better one-step and two-step FIDs than iCT and iCT-deep using 2× smaller networks, along with improved training stability.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, TCM achieves a one-step FID of 2.46 and a two-step FID of 2.05 using a 55.7M-parameter network, outperforming the previous best consistency model iCT (2.83 / 2.46) and iCT-deep (2.51 / 2.24, which uses a 2× larger 112M network). On ImageNet 64×64 with EDM2-S (280M params), TCM reaches a one-step FID of 2.88 and two-step FID of 2.31, surpassing iCT (4.02 / 3.55) and iCT-deep (3.25 / 2.77). The two-step TCM FID of 2.05 on CIFAR-10 is close to the 35-step EDM diffusion model (1.97), demonstrating near-diffusion quality at 1/17 the sampling cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TCM outperforms all consistency model baselines on both datasets and both step counts. On CIFAR-10, TCM improves one-step FID from 2.83 (iCT) and 2.51 (iCT-deep, 2× larger network) to 2.46, and two-step FID from 2.46 (iCT) and 2.24 (iCT-deep) to 2.05. On ImageNet 64×64, TCM (EDM2-S, 280M) achieves one-step FID 2.88 vs. iCT's 4.02 and iCT-deep's 3.25 (592M), and two-step FID 2.31 vs. iCT-deep's 2.77. TCM also beats all knowledge distillation methods (KD 9.36, DSNO 3.78, TRACT 3.78, PD 9.12) without requiring offline simulation or additional networks. Among variational score distillation methods, SiD achieves a slightly better one-step FID of 1.92 vs. TCM's 2.46, but TCM does not require training an additional score network or running PF-ODE simulations. TCM also demonstrates improved training stability: standard consistency training diverges on ImageNet after 150K iterations, while TCM continues to improve FID from 2.83 to 2.46.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the consistency model framework (Song et al., 2023) and its improved training variants (iCT, Song & Dhariwal 2023; ECM, Geng et al. 2024) by identifying an implicit denoising-vs-generation capacity trade-off within the standard consistency training objective. The key conceptual departure is restricting the consistency training to a truncated time range [t', T], which requires a new parameterization (Eq. 5) and a two-stage procedure where a pre-trained standard consistency model provides the boundary condition at t'. The diffusion model formulation and parameterization follow EDM (Karras et al., 2022), and the training schedule follows iCT/ECM conventions, but the core contribution is the explicit control over which time steps the model must handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that consistency models implicitly sacrifice denoising capability at small t to improve generation at large t provides a useful diagnostic for understanding training dynamics in this model family, and the truncated training approach offers a simple, practical lever to improve one-step generation quality without architectural changes. The method achieves state-of-the-art FID among consistency models using smaller networks than iCT-deep and eliminates the training divergence observed on ImageNet, making it a strong default choice for fast diffusion sampling. However, a notable gap remains relative to multi-step diffusion models (e.g., 2.46 vs. 1.97 FID on CIFAR-10), and the two-stage procedure adds ~18% training time and 15% memory overhead. Open questions include whether the truncated training principle transfers to other generative frameworks (e.g., flow matching, adversarial distillation) and whether the optimal dividing time t' can be learned rather than fixed.
