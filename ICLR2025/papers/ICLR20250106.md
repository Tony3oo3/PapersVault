## Problem

Neural population dynamics are typically modeled as autonomous systems, conflating intrinsic recurrent dynamics with the effects of external inputs (sensory stimuli, neurostimulation), and linear methods cannot capture nonlinear dynamical phenomena like multi-stable fixed points. Additionally, most approaches do not prioritize behaviorally relevant neural dynamics over the majority of neural variance that is irrelevant to the behavior of interest. No prior method simultaneously addresses all three challenges: explicit input-driven modeling, prioritization of shared neural-behavioral dynamics, and full nonlinearity.

## Input / Output

BRAID takes neural activity time series (smoothed spike counts or raw LFP at 20 Hz), measured external inputs (e.g., 2D target location as sensory stimulus), and behavior time series (fingertip position and velocity) as inputs. Critically, behavior is used only as a training-time supervision signal and is never available during inference; the model predicts behavior solely from neural activity and inputs. The model outputs two complementary representations of dynamics (predictor form for recursive state estimation and generative form for intrinsic dynamics), multi-step-ahead neural-behavioral forecasts, and a dissociated latent state split into behaviorally relevant, neural-specific, and optionally behavior-specific components.

## Method

BRAID's core architecture pairs two RNNs per latent state section: a predictor RNN (parameterized by A, K) that recursively infers latent states from neural observations and inputs, and a generative RNN (parameterized by A_fw, K_fw) that propagates the inferred state forward using only intrinsic dynamics and future inputs to produce m-step-ahead predictions. The m-step-ahead prediction loss (m > 1) is the key mechanism that forces the model to learn the generative form separately, since multi-step forecasting requires evolving the state purely by intrinsic dynamics without new neural observations. A multi-stage optimization prioritizes behaviorally relevant dynamics: Stage 1 learns shared neural-behavioral dynamics (x^(1)) by first minimizing behavior prediction loss, then neural prediction; Stage 2 learns residual neural-specific dynamics (x^(2)); an optional Stage 3 captures input-driven behavior-specific dynamics not encoded in neural activity. Each of the six transformations (A, A_fw, K, K_fw, C_y, C_z) can be independently set to linear or nonlinear MLPs, and an automatic nonlinearity selection procedure identifies the optimal configuration via inner cross-validation. A preprocessing step fits a high-dimensional unsupervised RNN to extract only neurally-encoded behavior, ensuring the learned intrinsic states are grounded in the recorded neural activity rather than confounded by input-driven behavior dynamics.

## Results

On the NHP motor cortical reaching dataset, BRAID achieves 4-step-ahead behavior forecasting CC of 0.8042±0.0085 (n_x=16) and 0.7970±0.0086 (n_x=64), outperforming all baselines. In nonlinear simulations with a sinusoidal behavior mapping, BRAID with nonlinear decoder reaches behavior decoding CC of 0.8696±0.0487, nearly matching the true model's ideal accuracy of 0.8737±0.0486, while linear baselines (IPSID, linear BRAID) achieve only ~0.457. BRAID also achieves near-ideal neural prediction CC of 0.8913±0.0305 in simulations and 0.4123±0.0077 (n_x=64) on real data, demonstrating accurate learning of both intrinsic dynamics and input-driven contributions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BRAID outperforms all six baselines on the primary metric of multi-step-ahead behavior forecasting on the NHP dataset. Against IPSID (linear, input-driven), BRAID's advantage is dramatic in simulations: behavior decoding CC jumps from 0.4567 to 0.8696, confirming that nonlinearity is essential when the true system is nonlinear. Against DPAD (nonlinear, no input), BRAID improves 4-step-ahead behavior forecasting from 0.6706 to 0.8042 (n_x=16), demonstrating the critical importance of explicitly modeling sensory inputs. Against mmPLRNN (piecewise-linear, multi-modal, input-capable), BRAID wins on both behavior (0.8042 vs 0.6851) and neural (0.3274 vs 0.3162) forecasting at n_x=16, despite mmPLRNN having the advantage of using behavior as an input during inference. Against U-BRAID (unsupervised ablation), BRAID wins on behavior forecasting (0.8042 vs 0.7663) but U-BRAID achieves higher neural forecasting at low dimensions (0.4089 vs 0.3274), converging at n_x=64 (0.4185 vs 0.4123), confirming that prioritization benefits low-dimensional behaviorally relevant representations. Against TNDM and LFADS (sequential autoencoders), BRAID significantly outperforms in behavior decoding while achieving comparable or better neural prediction, and against CEBRA (contrastive, non-dynamic), BRAID outperforms in both modalities, validating the benefit of explicit dynamical modeling over static embedding approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

BRAID sits at the intersection of three prior lines of work: (1) linear input-driven dynamical modeling (IPSID, Vahidi et al. 2024), which explicitly incorporates measured external inputs but is strictly linear; (2) nonlinear autonomous dynamical models (DPAD, Sani et al. 2024; TNDM, Hurwitz et al. 2021), which capture nonlinearities but treat the system as input-free; and (3) multi-modal dynamical models (mmPLRNN, Kramer et al. 2022), which can in principle model inputs but use a single combined loss that does not prioritize shared dynamics. BRAID's key architectural departure is the dual predictor/generative-form RNN pair, which for nonlinear systems (where no analytical Kalman-filter relationship exists between the two forms) enables m-step-ahead forecasting to explicitly validate learned intrinsic dynamics, combined with a multi-stage optimization that sequentially prioritizes behaviorally relevant, neural-specific, and behavior-specific dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete interpretability gap in neural dynamics modeling: when measured inputs (sensory stimuli, stimulation) are ignored, their dynamics get conflated with intrinsic recurrent dynamics, misleading downstream neuroscience conclusions. The ablation showing that the separate generative RNN is essential for correct eigenvalue recovery (Table A.3) provides a clean mechanistic explanation for why forecasting-based training is needed beyond 1-step-ahead prediction. Limitations include single-session modeling, dependence on having measured inputs available, and the fact that nonlinearity placement (decoder vs. recursion vs. encoder) is dataset-dependent and must be selected empirically. Open questions include cross-session generalization, variational extensions of the multi-stage architecture, and how to handle unmeasured inputs alongside measured ones.
