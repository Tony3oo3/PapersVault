## Problem

Generative diffusion models are increasingly repurposed for discriminative perception tasks, but the fundamental gap between the generative denoising process (which tolerates intermediate deviations as long as the final distribution is reasonable) and discriminative objectives (which demand precise matches to rigorous ground truth) has been largely unexplored. This gap causes noticeable underperformance on multi-modal reasoning tasks like referring image segmentation, where perception quality unexpectedly drops at later denoising steps and timestep contributions are highly uneven.

## Input / Output

The method operates in the latent space of Stable Diffusion (SD1.5/SD2.0/SDXL), taking an RGB image (256×256 during training, 512×512 during evaluation) and a text prompt (referring description for RIS, or task instruction like 'Detect %Category%' for generalist perception) as conditioning. For RIS, the output is an edited image where the target object is painted with a solid red mask, which is then thresholded (δ_c=50 from pure red) into a binary mask for IoU computation. The interactive extension additionally accepts a correctional prompt D⁻ (a language description of what the model should NOT segment) to progressively refine predictions via classifier-free guidance.

## Method

ADDP introduces three plug-and-play alignments between the diffusion denoising process and perception objectives without architectural changes. (1) Contribution-aware timestep sampling: the paper derives contribution factors c_t² showing that earlier denoising steps (closer to t=T) disproportionately determine final perception quality, and uses these as multinomial sampling weights during training so critical steps receive more gradient updates; c_t² can be estimated from the diffusion formulation (√(1-ᾱ_t)/√ᾱ_t) or empirically via iterative R² regression on intermediate-step perception metrics. (2) Diffusion-tailored data augmentation: to combat the training-denoising distribution shift (exposure bias), the ground truth x₀ is purposefully corrupted before adding noise, with corruption intensity scaling linearly with timestep (e.g., Gaussian blur for depth; color/shape/location perturbations for RIS masks), and the model is trained with x₀-prediction rather than ε-prediction for better consistency. (3) Correctional guidance: classifier-free guidance is extended with a negative condition D⁻ so that user or LLM-generated correctional prompts can nudge the denoising trajectory away from incorrect predictions, enabling multi-round interactive refinement in an agentic workflow with GPT-4o and LLaVA.

## Results

On depth estimation, ADDP consistently improves Marigold across all five zero-shot benchmarks, reducing average AbsRel from 12.3 to 11.6 and improving average δ₁ from 90.6 to 91.2 (e.g., ETH3D: 7.1→6.3 AbsRel, 95.1→96.1 δ₁). On referring image segmentation (RIS), InstructPix2Pix-SD2.0+ADDP achieves 66.86 oIoU on RefCOCO val, 55.35 on RefCOCO+ val, and 55.85 on G-Ref val, substantially outperforming the prior generative baseline InstructDiffusion (61.74/46.57/51.17) and enabling a diffusion model to match mid-tier discriminative methods for the first time. On generalist perception, ADDP improves InstructCV across all three tasks: RMSE 0.302→0.288 (NYUv2 depth), mIoU 46.67→48.40 (ADE20K segmentation), and mAP@0.5 46.6→48.1 (COCO detection).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On depth estimation (Table 1), ADDP+Marigold achieves the best average rank (1.4) among all methods, outperforming Marigold (2.4), DPT (3.8), HDN (3.1), Omnidata (4.7), LeReS (5.1), MiDaS (7.1), and DiverseDepth (7.4) across all five benchmarks. On RIS, ADDP significantly outperforms the prior generative baselines: InstructDiffusion (61.74 oIoU on RefCOCO val) and Unified-IO (46.42), with gains of +5.1 and +20.4 points respectively. However, ADDP still trails the strongest discriminative methods—UNINEXT (77.90), PVD (74.82), and ReLA (73.82) on RefCOCO val—by 11–11 points, though it surpasses mid-tier discriminative baselines like MCN (62.44) and VLT (65.65). With correctional guidance added, the gap to CRIS (70.47) narrows to ~1.3 points on RefCOCO val. In ablations against generative-task sampling strategies (Table G), ADDP's perception-estimated c_t² (64.00 oIoU) outperforms P2 (57.90), Min-SNR (26.13), MLT (55.60), SpeedD (57.89), and Soft Truncation (57.49), confirming that task-specific weights are critical. For augmentation, ADDP's diffusion-tailored strategy (66.19) beats TADA (65.76) and Epsilon Scaling (64.13) from generative studies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the InstructPix2Pix image-editing framework and the DDPM training formulation, extending them to align generative denoising with discriminative perception objectives. It departs from prior diffusion-for-perception work (Marigold, InstructCV, InstructDiffusion) that focused on designing generative formats or using diffusion models as feature extractors, by instead investigating and correcting the fundamental mismatch between the iterative denoising process and the precision demands of tasks like referring image segmentation. The contribution-aware timestep sampling connects to rectified flow re-weighting (Kingma & Gao, 2024) and generative loss-scaling strategies (Min-SNR, P2), but derives weights from perception-specific statistics rather than generative objectives. The correctional guidance extends classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022) with a negative-prompt-like term grounded in vision-language reasoning, repurposing it as an interactive user interface rather than a generation quality tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides plug-and-play training improvements (contribution-aware sampling, diffusion-tailored augmentation) that consistently enhance multiple diffusion-based perception models without architectural changes, and notably enables a common InstructPix2Pix baseline to approach discriminative RIS methods for the first time. The interactive correctional guidance perspective opens a new direction for agentic perception workflows where diffusion models serve as controllable interfaces rather than one-shot predictors. Key limitations remain: generative perception still underperforms strong discriminative baselines on multi-modal reasoning tasks, and the underlying diffusion models are pre-trained for aesthetic image generation rather than perception, suggesting that pre-training alignment is a critical open problem. The authors also note the methods could extend to other tasks with well-defined ground truth (super-resolution, 3D reconstruction), but this remains unexplored.
