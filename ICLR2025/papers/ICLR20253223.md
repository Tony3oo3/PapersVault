## Problem

Standard LLM pretraining treats all samples equally via uniform averaging, wasting compute on redundant or uninformative data. Existing reweighting methods operate at the domain/group level and lack the fine-grained, dynamic instance-level adaptation needed for web-scale corpora where each sample is seen only once, making multi-epoch or history-dependent approaches infeasible.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-sample NLL losses already computed during the standard forward pass (no extra forward or backward passes required) and produces a set of importance weights w_i that sum to 1 per minibatch, which scale the per-sample gradients in the parameter update. Losses are first linearly normalized into [-α, α] using batch min/max, then transformed by a strategy function and softmaxed with an annealed temperature r, making the entire reweighting pipeline a lightweight post-processing step on existing loss tensors.

## Method

The paper proposes a family of fully online, instance-level loss-based reweighting strategies (LinUpper, Quadratic, Extremes) that map normalized per-sample losses to importance weights via simple analytical functions, combined with a curriculum-based temperature annealing that starts near-uniform and progressively sharpens the weighting. The key theoretical contribution is a convergence analysis (Theorems 1–2) showing that down-weighting low-loss samples yields a tighter convergence bound (δ_t ≤ 0) for both full-gradient and minibatch SGD with momentum under convex, L-smooth, interpolation-regime assumptions, with a critical constraint that max weight ≤ 2/b to prevent outlier overfitting. Proposition 1 further proves that LinUpper followed by the softmax temperature schedule corresponds to the optimal solution of a KL-divergence-regularized reweighting objective, providing a principled justification for the empirical finding that deprioritizing low-loss (redundant) samples consistently accelerates convergence and improves downstream performance across 124M–7B parameter models.

## Results

On the GPT2-medium (300M) model trained on SlimPajama, LinUpper achieves a mean 5-shot accuracy of 40.9% across LogiQA, LogiQA2, SciQ, and PiQA, compared to 39.4% for uniform sampling. When combined with domain-level reweighting, LinUpper+DoGE reaches 41.5% mean accuracy (vs. 40.8% for DoGE alone) and LinUpper+DoReMi reaches 41.5% (vs. 41.0% for DoReMi alone). On the larger 7B Llama model trained on FineWeb 15T, LinUpper improves average performance by 1.44% on 13 LUR tasks and 1.16% on 6 QA tasks, winning on 14 out of 19 benchmarks overall.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares LinUpper against two domain-level reweighting baselines: DoGE (Fan et al., 2023) and DoReMi (Xie et al., 2023). LinUpper consistently improves upon both: when stacked on DoGE, it boosts mean 5-shot accuracy from 40.8% to 41.5% (+0.7%), with the largest gain on LogiQA (27.2% → 28.6%, +1.4%) and SciQ (52.8% → 53.2%). When stacked on DoReMi, it lifts mean accuracy from 41.0% to 41.5% (+0.5%), with the most notable improvement on SciQ (53.3% → 54.5%, +1.2%). LinUpper wins on all four benchmarks in both combinations, never losing to either baseline. The paper also compares against uniform sampling (the standard training paradigm), where LinUpper outperforms it on 3 of 4 tasks for GPT2-medium and on 14 of 19 tasks for the 7B Llama model, confirming that down-weighting low-loss samples provides consistent gains across model scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the well-known concept of instance-level sample reweighting (established in computer vision for multi-epoch training by Loshchilov & Hutter, Jiang et al., Katharopoulos & Fleuret) for the fundamentally different setting of LLM pretraining, where each sample is seen only once and storing per-sample history is infeasible. It departs from prior LLM-specific reweighting work (DoReMi, DoGE) by moving from static, domain-level importance to dynamic, instance-level importance computed purely from the current forward-pass loss, requiring no auxiliary models, validation sets, or historical statistics. The theoretical contribution—providing the first convergence-bound characterization of how loss-based reweighting affects gradient descent under the interpolation regime—has no direct predecessor in the LLM pretraining literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a near-zero-overhead, plug-and-play modification to existing LLM training pipelines that consistently improves downstream benchmark performance (up to ~1.4% on 7B models) and creates synergies with domain-level reweighting, suggesting a path toward reducing reliance on expensive manual data curation. However, the theoretical guarantees rely on the interpolation regime and convexity assumptions that may not strictly hold for overparameterized transformers, and the empirical gains, while consistent, are modest in absolute terms. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to instruction-tuning or RLHF stages, how it interacts with other training-time interventions (e.g., learning-rate scheduling, data shuffling strategies), and whether the benefits persist at even larger scales (100B+ parameters) where the signal-to-noise ratio of individual sample losses may differ.
