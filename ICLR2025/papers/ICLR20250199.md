## Problem

DETR-based one-stage scene graph generation models suffer from slow convergence due to sparse relation supervision: Hungarian matching assigns each ground-truth relation to only one query, and suboptimally matched queries (with correct entity classes and IoU > 0.6) are treated as false negatives. With only ~5.5 GT relations per image and 200–300 queries, fewer than 3% of queries receive positive supervision per step, forcing 150+ training epochs versus 32 for two-stage methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250199/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image and outputs a set of N=300 predicted relation triplets, each comprising a subject entity (class label + bounding box), a relation class label, and an object entity (class label + bounding box). The i-th subject query is paired exclusively with the i-th object query (no permutation), and predictions are post-processed by ranking on relation probability and removing self-referential triplets. At inference, the Hydra Branch is discarded entirely, so the model introduces no extra parameters or latency over the baseline RelTR architecture.

## Method

Hydra-SGG introduces a Hybrid Relation Assignment that augments standard One-to-One Hungarian matching with an IoU-based One-to-Many assignment, where the matching score combines subject/object class probabilities and box IoUs, retaining the top-6 queries per ground-truth triplet above a threshold T=0.4. This increases positive training samples by ~65% over the baseline. To resolve the conflict between self-attention (which promotes diverse, non-duplicate predictions) and One-to-Many assignment (which benefits from multiple queries matching the same GT), the paper adds Hydra Branch: an auxiliary decoder that shares all parameters with the main RelDecoder but removes self-attention layers, thereby encouraging duplicate relation predictions that synergize with One-to-Many assignment. Hydra Branch is used only during training and discarded at inference, and the combined loss weights One-to-One and One-to-Many terms equally (1:1 ratio).

## Results

On VG150 test (SGDet), Hydra-SGG achieves 16.0 mR@50 and 28.6 R@50 in only 12 training epochs with a ResNet50 backbone (67.6M params), setting a new state-of-the-art. On Open Images V6, it reaches 50.0 weighted score in 7 epochs, and on GQA it achieves 12.5 mR@50 in 12 epochs. Training time is 12.0 hours on 8× RTX 4090 GPUs, compared to 50.0 hours for RelTR, representing a ~4× speedup while delivering substantially higher accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hydra-SGG outperforms all compared one-stage and two-stage methods on mR@50 across all three benchmarks. On VG150, it surpasses the best one-stage competitor DSGG by +3.0 (16.0 vs 13.0), SGTR by +4.0 (16.0 vs 12.0), and the best two-stage method PE-Net by +3.6 (16.0 vs 12.4), while requiring only 12 epochs versus 60, 123, and 32 respectively. On Open Images V6, Hydra-SGG achieves 50.0 weighted score in 7 epochs, beating EGTR (48.6, 275 epochs) and PE-Net (44.9). On GQA, it leads CFA (10.8 mR@50) by +1.7 and VETO (7.0) by +5.5. The training efficiency gain is dramatic: Hydra-SGG converges in 12 epochs (12.0 h) versus 150 epochs (50.0 h) for RelTR, a ~4× wall-clock speedup while improving mR@50 from 10.8 to 16.0.

## Relation to Prior Work

Hydra-SGG extends the DETR-based one-stage SGG lineage initiated by SGTR and RelTR, which use Hungarian (One-to-One) matching to assign ground truth relations to queries. The key departure is the introduction of a Hybrid Relation Assignment that augments One-to-One matching with an IoU-based One-to-Many assignment, directly addressing the sparse supervision problem that causes slow convergence in prior DETR-SGG models. Architecturally, it adopts training techniques from DN-DETR and DINO (denoising queries, anchor boxes) and deformable attention from Deformable DETR, but the novel Hydra Branch—an auxiliary decoder with self-attention removed—represents a deliberate departure from standard transformer decoder design, motivated by the finding that self-attention suppresses the duplicate predictions that One-to-Many assignment requires.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× convergence speedup (12 epochs vs. 150 for RelTR) while simultaneously achieving SOTA mR@50 on VG150, Open Images V6, and GQA makes Hydra-SGG a practically important efficiency gain for the SGG community. The conceptual insight that self-attention in the decoder is counterproductive for One-to-Many assignment challenges the default assumption that inter-query interaction is always beneficial in transformer decoders. However, the method remains limited to closed-set prediction, the One-to-Many assignment rules are manually designed (top-6 queries per GT with a fixed threshold), and the Hydra Branch adds training-time compute even though it is discarded at inference. Open questions include whether the hybrid assignment generalizes to open-vocabulary SGG and whether learned (rather than hand-crafted) assignment criteria could further reduce label noise.
