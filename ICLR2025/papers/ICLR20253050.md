## Problem

Existing latent dynamics models (e.g., LFADS, seqVAE) are designed to infer dynamics from a single recording session and cannot account for statistical heterogeneities across recordings—such as differing neuron counts, tuning properties, and stimulus statistics—when integrating data from multiple animals or tasks. While meta-learning in machine learning has shown that shared structure across environments enables rapid adaptation, no prior approach learns a family of related dynamical systems directly from heterogeneous neural recordings in a unified latent space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253050/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes M neural time series recordings (each with potentially different numbers of recorded neurons, e.g., 30–100 dimensions in synthetic data or varying unit counts in motor cortex recordings) from sessions/subjects performing related tasks, along with a small number of trials (n_s = 1–64) from a novel recording for few-shot adaptation. Dataset-specific read-in networks project each recording into a common intermediate space before joint inference. Outputs include per-dataset latent state trajectories, a low-dimensional dynamical embedding vector (d_e = 2) that indexes the recording's position within the learned family of dynamics, reconstructed and k-step forecasted neural observations, and decoded hand velocity for motor cortex data.

## Method

The paper proposes a hierarchical nonlinear state-space model in which a shared latent dynamics function f_θ is adapted per dataset via low-rank perturbations (rank d_r) generated by a hypernetwork h_ϑ conditioned on a low-dimensional dynamical embedding e^i. This embedding, drawn from a standard Gaussian prior, encodes inter-dataset variability in dynamical structure (e.g., oscillation speed, bifurcation regime) while the shared parameters capture global topology. Inference is performed with a Deep Kalman Filter: a bidirectional RNN encoder infers the embedding distribution (aggregated across trials within a session), and a forward RNN infers the latent state trajectory conditioned on both the projected observations and the sampled embedding. Training jointly maximizes the ELBO across all M datasets, with dataset-specific likelihood functions and read-in networks handling heterogeneous observation dimensions, while the embedding encoder, state encoder, shared dynamics, and hypernetwork parameters are shared across all recordings.

## Results

On the synthetic bifurcating systems (Hopf + Duffing), the proposed method achieves 0.87±0.037 k=30-step forecasting r² with n_s=16 training trials on held-out datasets, outperforming all baselines. In the few-shot regime (n_s=1), it reaches 0.69±0.072, substantially exceeding Single Session (0.47±0.119) and Embedding-Input (0.59±0.084). On motor cortex recordings (CO and Maze tasks), the method outperforms all multi-session baselines on forecasting, especially in low-sample regimes, while matching or exceeding single-session reconstruction performance. The learned dynamical embedding displays distinct clustering by task and subject, with continuous interpolation across the embedding space producing smoothly varying predicted behaviors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Shared Dynamics baseline (LFADS-style shared dynamics with dataset-specific likelihoods), the proposed method achieves dramatically better forecasting (0.69–0.87 vs. −0.31 to −0.13 r²), confirming that a single shared dynamics model cannot capture velocity/topology variations across datasets. Against the Linear-Adapter (CoDA) baseline, the proposed method is comparable at n_s=1 (0.69 vs. 0.68) and n_s=8 (0.78 vs. 0.79), but clearly wins at n_s=16 (0.87 vs. 0.74), suggesting the low-rank hypernetwork parametrization scales better with more data. Against Embedding-Input (CAVIA/DYNAMO), the proposed method wins at n_s=1 (0.69 vs. 0.59) and n_s=16 (0.87 vs. 0.74), indicating that conditioning via low-rank parameter changes is more effective than concatenating the embedding as an input. Against Single Session (seqVAE/DKF), the proposed method wins in the few-shot regime (n_s=1: 0.69 vs. 0.47) but is matched at n_s=8 (0.78 vs. 0.79), showing the meta-learned structure provides the greatest benefit when data is most limited. On motor cortex recordings, Multi-Session CEBRA fails to capture Maze task variability (negative forecasting r²), while the proposed method balances both CO and Maze tasks and outperforms all baselines on forecasting, particularly for a new subject (Sub T) where performance improves steadily with n_s from 8 to 64 trials.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three previously separate threads: hierarchical Bayesian state-space models for multi-dataset neural inference (Linderman et al., 2019), hypernetwork-based parameter generation (Ha et al., 2016), and meta-learning via low-dimensional task embeddings (Rusu et al., 2019). The key departure from prior multi-session approaches (LFADS, Herrero-Vidal et al.) is replacing dataset-specific full parameter vectors with a low-dimensional 'dynamical embedding' that drives a hypernetwork to make low-rank modifications to shared dynamics parameters. This is conceptually closest to CoDA (Kirchmeyer et al., 2022) and DYNAMO (Cotler et al., 2023), but those operate in supervised settings on known low-dimensional systems or on pre-trained RNNs, whereas this work performs unsupervised joint inference of latent states and dynamics directly from raw neural recordings with heterogeneous dimensionalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical bottleneck in computational neuroscience: single-session models overfit with limited trials, while naive multi-session sharing fails when dynamics vary across recordings. The learned embedding manifold offers an interpretable, continuous map over dynamical regimes that could support studying inter-subject variability or learning-induced changes in dynamics. Key limitations include the assumption of shared structure across related tasks, dependence on event-aligned data, and sensitivity of few-shot performance to the amount of pretraining data. Open questions include extending to unstructured tasks, recordings without expected shared structure, and establishing theoretical guarantees for why the hypernetwork low-rank parameterization outperforms alternatives.
