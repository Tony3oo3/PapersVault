## Problem

Prior work on neural PDE solvers largely assumed a Markovian dynamics where the next state depends only on the current one, with ablations suggesting this is optimal. However, no prior work identified *when* this assumption breaks down or provided theoretical justification for when explicitly modeling memory of past states is necessary. This paper shows that under lossy observations (low spatial resolution or observation noise) combined with significant high-frequency Fourier content in the solution, a memory-augmented solution can be arbitrarily better than any Markovian one.

## Input / Output

The model receives a discretized PDE initial condition on an equispaced spatial grid of resolution f (ranging from 16 to 1024 in 1D, or 64×64 in 2D) and autoregressively predicts the solution at each subsequent timestep on the same grid. During training, teacher forcing is used so the model sees ground-truth past states; during inference it consumes its own compressed memory of all previous predicted timesteps via the S4 state-space model. The S4 memory layer operates independently at each spatial point, maintaining a fixed-size (state dimension 64) compressed representation of the entire temporal history rather than requiring raw past timesteps as input.

## Method

The core architecture, MemNO (instantiated as S4FFNO), interleaves a causal S4 state-space layer between the spatial layers of a Factorized Fourier Neural Operator, motivated by the Mori-Zwanzig decomposition of projected PDE dynamics into a Markovian term, a memory (convolutional) term, and an unobserved residual. The S4 layer is applied independently along the temporal axis at each spatial grid point, compressing the sequence of hidden representations from all previous timesteps into a fixed-size state that is added residually to the spatial feature map. Theoretically, the paper proves (Theorem 1) that for a linear PDE whose operator mixes observed and unobserved Fourier subspaces, the memory term in the Generalized Langevin Equation yields a solution whose error relative to the Markovian approximation grows as exp(√2·Bt), making the gap arbitrarily large. Empirically, S4FFNO achieves up to 6× lower nRMSE than FFNO at resolution 32 for low-viscosity Kuramoto-Sivashinsky, and outperforms FFNO-2D by ~50% under observation noise on Navier-Stokes, while matching FFNO at high resolutions where memory is unnecessary.

## Results

On the Kuramoto-Sivashinsky (KS) equation at resolution 32, S4FFNO achieves nRMSE of 0.031 (ν=0.125), 0.108 (ν=0.1), and 0.139 (ν=0.075), representing up to 6× lower error than its memoryless counterpart FFNO (0.187, 0.446, 0.500 respectively). On Burgers' equation (ν=0.001) at resolution 32, S4FFNO achieves 0.053 nRMSE versus FFNO's 0.207 (~4× improvement). On 2D Navier-Stokes with observation noise, S4FFNO-2D outperforms FFNO-2D by approximately 50% for noise standard deviations above 0.128 (ν=10⁻³) and above 0.032 (ν=10⁻⁵). At high resolution (128), FFNO matches or slightly outperforms S4FFNO, confirming that memory is most beneficial when observations are lossy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S4FFNO dominates all four Markovian baselines (Factformer, GKT, U-Net, FFNO) at low resolutions (32 and 64) across both KS and Burgers' equations, with the largest gap against FFNO (up to 6× lower nRMSE at resolution 32, KS ν=0.125). Against the memory-augmented Multi Input FFNO baseline (which uses 4 raw past timesteps as input), S4FFNO still achieves roughly 3× lower nRMSE at resolution 32, demonstrating that compressed sequential memory (S4) is more effective than simply concatenating recent observations. At resolution 128, FFNO slightly outperforms S4FFNO on KS (0.006 vs 0.008 for ν=0.075), consistent with prior findings that the Markovian assumption suffices when observations are high-fidelity. On 2D Navier-Stokes with added Gaussian noise, S4FFNO-2D consistently outperforms FFNO-2D by ~50% nRMSE for moderate-to-high noise levels, while both perform comparably at zero noise. The paper also shows S4FFNO retains its advantage even when FFNO is scaled to 7× more parameters, confirming the gain comes from memory modeling rather than model capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Factorized Fourier Neural Operator (FFNO, Tran et al. 2023) as its Markovian spatial backbone and the S4 state space model (Gu et al. 2022) as its temporal memory component, unifying them into the modular MemNO framework. It departs from the recent consensus (Tran et al. 2023; Lippe et al. 2023) that the Markovian assumption is optimal for neural PDE solvers, showing that this conclusion holds only at high observation resolution. The theoretical grounding draws on the Mori-Zwanzig formalism from statistical mechanics, which had been applied to PDE model reduction (Ma et al. 2018; Parish & Duraisamy 2017) but not previously connected to the design of data-driven neural operators with memory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the 'Markovian is optimal' result is resolution-dependent has direct practical implications: real-world PDE observations are frequently low-resolution or noisy, precisely the regime where memory architectures deliver up to 6× error reduction. The ω_f metric provides a practical diagnostic for practitioners to decide whether memory is warranted, and the low-viscosity KS benchmark addresses the community concern (McGreivy & Hakim 2024) that current PDE benchmarks are too easy. Open questions include extending to non-i.i.d. noise, aliasing effects, 3D PDEs, and systematic trade-offs among memory architectures (S4 vs. LSTM vs. Mamba-type models) under different observation conditions.
