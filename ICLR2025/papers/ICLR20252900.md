## Problem

Existing multi-task RL methods that share behaviors between tasks (e.g., policy distillation in DnC, Distral) do so uniformly via regularization, which introduces bias into each task's RL objective and causes negative interference when tasks require conflicting optimal actions from the same state. The paper asks whether behaviors can be shared selectively and without modifying the training objective, so that each task only incorporates genuinely helpful behaviors from other tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252900/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each environment step, QMP receives the current state observation and the task identity, then evaluates all N task policies' proposed actions against the current task's Q-function to select which policy's action to execute for data collection. The output is a single action drawn from the selected policy, which is used only to populate the off-policy replay buffer; the actual policy update still uses the task's own SAC training procedure unchanged. In practice, the expectation in the Q-switch is approximated by evaluating Q at the mean action of each policy's distribution, and the entropy term is dropped, making the selection effectively argmax over Q_i(s, a_j) where a_j is the mean action of policy j.

## Method

QMP replaces the behavioral (data-collection) policy in any off-policy MTRL framework with a Q-switch mixture of all N task policies. At each state s in task T_i, the mixture selects the policy π' from {π_1,...,π_N} that maximizes E_{a~π'(·|s)}[Q_i(s,a)] + αH[π'(·|s)], which is derived by minimizing the KL divergence between candidate policies and the SAC optimal policy exp(Q_i/α)/Z. This guarantees the selected policy is at least as good as π_i for the current state (Theorem 5.1), and the resulting mixture soft policy iteration converges to the optimal policy at least as sample-efficiently as SAC alone (Theorem C.2). Because QMP only modifies which policy collects data and leaves the SAC training objective untouched, it introduces no bias and is complementary to parameter sharing, data sharing, and gradient-surgery methods like PCGrad. The Q-switch naturally learns to reduce cross-task sharing as the task-specific policy improves, and to avoid sharing from policies whose behaviors conflict with the current task.

## Results

In Multistage Reacher, QMP reaches >90% success rate at 0.5M environment steps, while the next-best behavior-sharing baseline (DnC with regularization) requires 3× the steps to converge. QMP is complementary to other MTRL frameworks: adding QMP to PCGrad improves final success rate in 3 of 4 environments (Maze: 0.90→1.00, Kitchen: 0.55→0.60, MT50: 0.35→0.42). In Maze Navigation, QMP achieves 72.9% success vs. 57.9% for No-QMP, and in Walker2D it reaches 3205±218 reward vs. 3110±220. QMP is most sample-efficient in Walker2D and Meta-World CDS, and scales better with task set size (10→50 tasks in Meta-World).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DnC (Ghosh et al., 2018), QMP clearly outperforms in tasks with conflicting behaviors: in Multistage Reacher, QMP reaches >90% success at 0.5M steps while DnC (reg.) takes 3× longer, and in Maze Navigation QMP scales better from 3 to 10 tasks. Against Data-Sharing/UDS (Yu et al., 2022), QMP provides complementary gains when added on top, though data-sharing alone can be competitive when ground-truth reward relabeling is available (CDS setting). Against PCGrad (Yu et al., 2020), QMP+PCGrad improves final success rate in 3 of 4 environments (Maze 0.90→1.00, Kitchen 0.55→0.60, MT50 0.35→0.42) with no degradation in Reacher. Against MCAL (Mysore et al., 2022), adding QMP yields approximately 20% final success rate gain in both MCAL variants on Multistage Reacher. Against DECAF (Glatt et al., 2020), QMP outperforms by more than 20% final success rate in both Multistage Reacher and Meta-World CDS, demonstrating that using the current task's Q-function to evaluate other policies is more sample-efficient than learning to combine Q-functions across tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior behavior-sharing MTRL methods (Teh et al. 2017, Ghosh et al. 2018) that enforce sharing through policy distillation or regularization to a mean policy, which introduces bias when tasks have conflicting optimal behaviors. Instead, QMP modifies only the off-policy data collection process by selecting, at each state, the policy from a mixture that best optimizes the current task's soft Q-value, leaving the RL training objective untouched. The theoretical grounding builds directly on SAC's generalized policy iteration framework, where the Q-switch mixture is derived as the argmin of KL divergence to the exponential Q-function over a finite set of candidate policies, guaranteeing monotonic policy improvement. This positions QMP as orthogonal to and complementary with parameter-sharing and data-sharing MTRL approaches rather than a replacement for them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QMP provides a simple, plug-and-play mechanism for MTRL that is theoretically guaranteed to be at least as sample-efficient as the underlying off-policy algorithm while being unbiased, making it broadly applicable across task families without requiring hand-crafted task similarity measures. A key open question is whether the per-step policy selection can be extended to temporally-extended (H-step) rollouts while preserving convergence guarantees, which the paper shows empirically helps in some environments but lacks theory for. The method is limited to off-policy algorithms (not PPO), and its gains are bounded by the degree of shareable behaviors and the suboptimality gap, so it offers no benefit in task sets with no behavioral overlap. Future directions include incorporating language embeddings for instruction-following tasks and exploring skill-based temporally-extended sharing.
