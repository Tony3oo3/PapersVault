## Problem

While multi-agent learning (MAL) algorithms for strongly monotone games provably converge to the unique Nash equilibrium at polynomial rates, their robustness to adversarial corruption of even a single agent's utility observations has been entirely unexplored. Prior work addressed either single-agent attacks in isolated online learning or many-agent attacks on MAL systems, leaving open whether one compromised agent can disrupt the entire system. This paper shows that the answer is yes: a sublinear corruption budget suffices to steer any converging MAL dynamics away from the true equilibrium.

## Input / Output

The adversary operates on a strongly monotone game with continuous action spaces and bandit feedback, requiring only knowledge of the victim agent's utility function and a chosen deviation vector δ (bounded by β/L to preserve monotonicity). The attack produces a per-round corruption c_t applied after observing the current joint action (a 'strong' corruption), and the total expected budget scales as O(T^{1-pα/(p+1)}), which is sublinear in the time horizon T. The output is a shifted Nash equilibrium x̃* whose distance from the original x* is lower-bounded by β‖δ‖/sup ρ(H_G), along with a full characterization of how convergence rate α and norm p affect attack cost.

## Method

The core attack, SUSA (Single-agent Utility Shifting Attack), constructs a corrupted utility ũ_k(x_k, x_{-k}) = u_k(x_k + δ, x_{-k}) + Δ(x_{-k}, δ) where the offset Δ is specifically designed so that the victim's best response under ũ_k equals BR_k(x_{-k}) − δ, causing the per-round corruption to vanish as the system converges to the new equilibrium. This design ensures the total budget is sublinear while the corrupted game remains strongly monotone (guaranteed when ‖δ‖ < β/L via second-order smoothness). The paper further establishes an intrinsic efficiency-robustness trade-off: by adjusting MD-SCB's learning rate to η_t ∝ t^{-φ} with φ = (2/3)(ρ + 1/2), the algorithm recovers last-iterate convergence under O(T^ρ) corruption for any ρ < 1, but at the cost of slower clean convergence. Corollary 1 formalizes this trade-off via the quantities ρ_-(α) and ρ_+(α), showing that faster-converging dynamics (larger α) require smaller attack budgets to be disrupted.

## Results

The paper's central theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows that a single-agent utility poisoning attack (SUSA) can steer any (α,p)-MAL dynamics in a β-strongly monotone game away from the true NE by at least β‖δ‖₂/sup{ρ(H_G)}, while requiring only a sublinear total budget of O(T^{1−pα/(p+1)}). For n-person Cournot competition, SUSA induces an NE shift of Ω(n⁻¹) with budget O(√n · T^{1−pα/(p+1)}); for Tullock contests, the shift is Ω(n^{-5/2}) under the same budget. Theorem 2 establishes that MD-SCB can be made resilient to any sublinear corruption O(T^ρ) by choosing learning rate φ = (2/3)(ρ+1/2), recovering last-iterate convergence at rate O(dT^{2(ρ−1)/3}). Corollary 1 formalizes the efficiency-robustness trade-off: 1−α ≤ ρ(MD-SCB(α)) ≤ ρ₊(α) ≤ ρ₋(α) ≤ ρ(α;SUSA) ≤ 1−2α/3, meaning faster-converging algorithms (larger α) tolerate less corruption.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without traditional algorithmic baselines; the 'comparisons' are between game structures and convergence rates. Cournot competition is shown to be significantly more vulnerable than Tullock contests: the NE shift is Ω(n⁻¹) vs. Ω(n^{-5/2}), and Cournot allows arbitrary δ while Tullock restricts ‖δ‖ < O(n⁻¹) as n grows. Among MAL algorithms, MD-SCB (α=1/4, the fastest known) is the most vulnerable, requiring the smallest attack budget, while slower dynamics (e.g., MAMD with α=1/6) tolerate more corruption. The paper's key comparison is the efficiency-robustness trade-off: Corollary 1 shows that the maximum tolerable corruption level ρ decreases linearly with convergence rate α (bounded between 1−α and 1−2α/3), confirming that no MAL algorithm can simultaneously achieve fast convergence and high robustness. Empirically, on n-person Cournot games with MD-SCB, the cumulative SUSA budget is visibly smaller for α=0.25 than for α=0.15 or α=0.05, and the induced NE deviation decreases with n, matching theoretical predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial attack literature from two adjacent areas—single-agent bandit poisoning (Jun et al., 2018; Bogunovic et al., 2021) and many-agent attacks on multi-agent reinforcement learning (Liu & Lai, 2023; Wu et al., 2023)—to the specific and previously unexplored setting of single-agent utility poisoning in multi-agent learning for monotone games with bandit feedback. It builds directly on the MD-SCB algorithm (Ba et al., 2024) and MAMD (Bravo et al., 2018) as the target dynamics, modifying their learning rate schedules to derive robustness guarantees. The efficiency-robustness trade-off identified here mirrors one previously documented in single-agent online learning (Cheng et al., 2024), but this is the first formalization in the multi-agent context. Unlike equilibrium steering work (Zhang et al., 2023) that can only redirect to existing equilibria, SUSA can induce arbitrary non-equilibrium outcomes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a single compromised agent with a sublinear corruption budget can permanently derail an entire multi-agent learning system toward a wrong Nash equilibrium has direct security implications for deployed systems like autonomous vehicle coordination and algorithmic trading. The formalized efficiency-robustness trade-off (Corollary 1) gives algorithm designers a concrete knob—learning rate decay—to tune resilience, but the bounds are not tight, leaving three open problems: the optimal attack strategy, whether the adversary gains a strict advantage by observing the dynamics first, and the identification of the most robust MAL algorithm. A key limitation is the assumption that the adversary knows the victim agent's utility function, and the potential dual use of SUSA for 'steering for social good' by a benevolent planner remains an unexplored direction.
