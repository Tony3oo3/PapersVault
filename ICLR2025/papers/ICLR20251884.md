## Problem

Existing watermarking and tamper localization methods are designed for images and degrade video quality when applied frame-by-frame via post-processing. Moreover, image-based tamper localization cannot detect temporal tampering such as frame reordering, insertion, or dropping, and passive detection methods suffer from poor generalization across diverse data distributions. No prior method provides a unified, training-free solution for both watermarking and tamper localization in AI-generated videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251884/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For generation, the method takes a text prompt (T2V) or conditional image (I2V) plus 512 watermark bits, which are encrypted into template bits and converted to Gaussian noise that seeds the diffusion model's denoising process (16 frames at 256 or 512 resolution). For detection, the possibly tampered video is VAE-encoded and inverted via DDIM Inversion to recover the initial noise, from which watermark bits are extracted and a pixel-level spatial tamper mask plus per-frame temporal position labels are produced. The entire pipeline is training-free and requires no modification to the underlying diffusion model.

## Method

VIDEOSHIELD establishes a reversible chain m ↔ TP ↔ Z^T ↔ FR where watermark bits are encrypted via ChaCha20 into template bits (one-to-one with pixels, locally fragile), then converted to Gaussian noise via truncated sampling (bit 0 → negative half, bit 1 → positive half of N(0,1)), preserving the standard Gaussian distribution so the diffusion model denoises normally. Extraction reverses the chain: the video is VAE-encoded, DDIM-inverted to recover the initial noise, thresholded into inverted bits, and decrypted to recover the watermark. Tamper localization compares inverted bits against the original template bits: a temporal module scores each tampered frame against all original positions to detect frame swaps/insertions/drops, and a spatial module applies Hierarchical Spatial-Temporal Refinement (HSTR) with Partial Threshold Binarization (PTB) across multiple aggregation levels to balance localization accuracy and granularity, producing a refined pixel-level mask.

## Results

VIDEOSHIELD embeds 512 watermark bits (vs. 30–256 for baselines) while maintaining the highest video quality (0.806 on ModelScope, 0.836 on SVD) and near-perfect bit accuracy (1.000/0.999). For spatial tamper localization, VIDEOSHIELD achieves average scores of 0.906 (ModelScope) and 0.761 (SVD) across three tamper types, dramatically outperforming the best baseline PSCC-Net (0.529/0.518). Temporal tamper localization reaches 100% accuracy on ModelScope and 93.6% on SVD for frame swap, insert, and drop operations. On images generated by Stable Diffusion (1.4/1.5/2.1), VIDEOSHIELD achieves 0.928–0.939 average spatial localization, outperforming EditGuard (0.885) on all three versions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On watermarking (Table 1), VIDEOSHIELD embeds 512 bits—2× more than MBRS (256) and 4× more than SepMark (128)—while matching or exceeding all baselines in bit accuracy (1.000 on MS, 0.999 on SVD) and achieving the highest video quality (0.806/0.836), since it requires no post-processing. RivaGAN, MBRS, CIN, PIMoG, and SepMark all degrade video quality through frame-by-frame post-processing, with CIN and PIMoG showing the most severe degradation (0.756/0.753 on MS). For spatial tamper localization (Table 3), VIDEOSHIELD outperforms all four passive baselines by a wide margin: PSCC-Net is the strongest baseline (0.561/0.466 average on MS/SVD for STTN) yet still trails VIDEOSHIELD by 0.325/0.298. MVSS-Net and OSN perform near-random (0.24–0.28), and HiFi-Net is unreliable (0.125–0.143 on STTN, with degenerate behavior on ProPainter). Against EditGuard (Table 15), VIDEOSHIELD wins on ModelScope (0.906 vs. 0.890), ZeroScope (0.900 vs. 0.880), I2VGen (0.867 vs. 0.882—narrow loss), and all three Stable Diffusion image versions (0.928–0.939 vs. 0.885), but loses on SVD (0.761 vs. 0.886) due to lower video generation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

VIDEOSHIELD extends the in-generation watermarking paradigm established by Gaussian Shading (Yang et al., 2024) and Tree-Ring (Wen et al., 2023) from images to video, leveraging DDIM Inversion (Song et al., 2020) as the extraction mechanism. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of template bits — a one-to-one pixel correspondence derived from watermark bits via ChaCha20 encryption — which enables tamper localization without any separate template image or training, unlike EditGuard or V2A-Mark. The Hierarchical Spatial-Temporal Refinement (HSTR) and Partial Threshold Binarization (PTB) modules are novel video-specific components that address the unique challenges of multi-frame content, including the entirely new sub-task of temporal tamper localization (frame swap, insert, drop).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first training-free framework that unifies watermark embedding, extraction, and both temporal and spatial tamper localization for diffusion-based video generation, making it immediately deployable on any compatible model without retraining. The finding that passive tamper detection methods (MVSS-Net, PSCC-Net, etc.) are essentially non-functional on AI-generated video (average scores ~0.25–0.56 vs. VIDEOSHIELD's ~0.76–0.91) highlights a fundamental gap: methods trained on natural-image manipulations do not transfer to synthetic content. Open questions include how the approach scales to longer videos and 3D-VAE-based models (post-Sora architecture), whether DDIM inversion fidelity degrades with increasing video length, and how the method handles adversarial attacks specifically targeting the inversion process.
