## Problem

Injective flows for density estimation on lower-dimensional manifolds require computing a rectangular Jacobian determinant, which is O(d³) and cannot be decomposed across stacked layers as in bijective normalizing flows. Existing work either restricts to trivial manifolds (spheres, tori) or relies on high-variance approximations like the Hutchinson trace estimator, which is particularly problematic in variational inference settings where no samples are available and exact density evaluation is essential for correct posterior approximation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a base distribution defined on d−1 spherical angles, a radius function r(θ) that parametrizes the specific star-like manifold (e.g., an ℓ_p-norm ball or the probability simplex), and an unnormalized target density for variational inference. It produces a density expressed in Cartesian coordinates in the ambient ℝ^d space, with all samples and log-density evaluations constrained to the (d−1)-dimensional manifold by construction. The only manifold-specific component the user must supply is the analytic radius function r(θ) and its gradient ∇_θ r(θ).

## Method

The key insight is that any (d−1)-dimensional star-like manifold (boundary of a star domain) admits a unique generalized spherical coordinate parametrization with d−1 angles and a radius function r(θ). The flow composes three transformations: a bijective layer T_θ (circular spline layers) mapping base samples to spherical angles, an injective dimensionality-inflation step T_r that appends r(θ) to the angles, and the spherical-to-Cartesian map T_{s→c}. The central theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows the full Jacobian determinant factorizes as det(J_{T_θ}) · det(J_{T_{s→c}}) · ‖(J_{T_{s→c}}^T)^{−1} y‖₂ where y = [−∇_θ r(θ), 1]^T, reducing computation from O(d³) to O(d²) by exploiting the almost-triangular structure of J_{T_{s→c}}^T. The proof relies on pseudo-determinant and adjugate-matrix identities for rank-(d−1) matrices. Training minimizes reverse KL divergence against the unnormalized target, making the approach directly applicable to variational inference on the simplex (mixing models) and on penalty level-sets (objective Bayesian penalized likelihood).

## Results

The proposed O(d²) Jacobian determinant computation achieves a significant speedup over the explicit O(d³) computation, with fitted scaling exponents of O(d^1.81) vs. O(d^2.96) on the hypersphere. For learning the uniform distribution on an l_p (pseudo-)norm ball (p=0.5) via variational inference, the exact Jacobian yields substantially lower MSE in log-density reconstruction compared to the Hutchinson trace estimator (n=1,10,50,100 Gaussian samples), and even the highest sample count fails to match the exact method. On 3D targets, the flow achieves MSE of 0.013 (von Mises-Fisher, κ=5) and 0.011 (mixture of 50 VMF, κ=50) over 10,000 samples. In the Bayesian mixing model comparison, the injective flow outperforms a Metropolis-Hastings MCMC sampler (1000 chains × 100,000 samples) in posterior credibility intervals at dimensions d=30 and d=50.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines. Against brute-force Jacobian computation (Eq. 2), the proposed analytical formula (Eq. 4) reduces complexity from approximately cubic to quadratic, yielding a clear speedup that grows with dimension. Against the Hutchinson trace estimator (the standard approximation in injective flow literature, e.g. Caterini et al. 2021, Flouris and Konukoglu 2023), the exact Jacobian consistently produces better log-density reconstruction and sample quality; increasing the number of Hutchinson samples from 1 to 100 improves the approximation but never closes the gap, and the approximate method incurs additional runtime overhead. Against a Metropolis-Hastings MCMC sampler for the conjugate Dirichlet-multinomial mixing model, the injective flow achieves tighter 95% posterior credibility intervals at d=30 and d=50, while the MCMC sampler struggles with sparse, multi-modal posteriors due to difficulty designing proposals near the simplex boundary. The MCMC gap persists even when the sampler is run 5× longer, suggesting a fundamental limitation of the proposal strategy rather than insufficient mixing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the injective flow framework for manifold density estimation, building on the change-of-variables formula for rectangular Jacobians (Ben-Israel, 1999) and the circular spline bijective layers from Rezende et al. (2020) for spherical coordinate boundaries. It departs from prior injective flow work (Caterini et al., 2021; Kumar et al., 2020; Sorrenson et al., 2024) that either approximates the Jacobian determinant via Hutchinson's trace estimator or ignores it entirely, and from the exact-computation results limited to trivial manifolds like spheres and tori (Gemici et al., 2016; Rezende et al., 2020). The key conceptual advance is showing that the pseudo-determinant/adjugate-matrix structure of the zero-padded Jacobian for star-like manifolds yields a closed-form O(d²) evaluation, generalizing the exact-computation result from spheres/tori to an entire class of codimension-1 manifolds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exact O(d²) Jacobian computation removes the primary computational barrier to injective flows on star-like manifolds and is shown to be essential for variational inference, where approximate Jacobians (even with 100 Hutchinson samples) produce poor density reconstructions. The two applications—objective Bayesian penalized likelihood via level-set manifolds and variational inference on the probability simplex for mixing models—demonstrate practical value in settings where the posterior is constrained to a known manifold by construction. The main limitation is the restriction to codimension-1 star-like manifolds (intrinsic dimension d−1 in R^d), which excludes lower-dimensional manifolds common in maximum-likelihood manifold learning; extending the adjugate-matrix trick to higher codimension remains an open question.
