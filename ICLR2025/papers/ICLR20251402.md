## Problem

Offline safe RL policies trained on static datasets suffer from severe distribution shift when deployed in unseen safety-constrained environments, leading to constraint violations that make them impractical for real-world robotic deployment. While model-based RL offers data-efficient fine-tuning, naively applying online safe RL after offline pretraining causes performance degradation and safety failures due to mixed soft/hard constraints and world-model prediction errors. No prior work has addressed the offline-to-online safe generalization problem—adapting a pretrained safe policy to novel safety regions with minimal interaction while maintaining near-zero constraint violations.

## Input / Output

The agent receives 64×64 RGB images (first-person view in simulation; third-person Intel RealSense camera in the real-world Franka Panda setup) concatenated with a 6-dimensional end-effector pose vector, producing a 6-dimensional action (joint-space pose delta for the robot; forward/backward/rotation commands for simulation agents). The world model encodes the image into a stochastic latent state via an RSSM, and all downstream planning, Q-value estimation, and policy selection operate in this compressed latent space rather than pixel space. Rewards and costs are sparse binary signals (goal reached / obstacle collision).

## Method

FOSP is a three-stage framework built on DreamerV3's RSSM world model with an added cost decoder. (1) Offline pretraining uses in-sample optimization inspired by IQL: a separate state-conditioned value network trained via expectile regression prevents the critic from overestimating out-of-distribution actions generated by the policy during latent rollouts, while a regularization term anchors early training to real dataset rewards. (2) Safety is enforced by replacing soft Lagrangian constraints with a feasibility-guided hard-constraint formulation, where a reachability estimation function (REF) captures the probability of future constraint violations and unifies the feasible/infeasible sub-problems into a single objective solved via Augmented Lagrangian with a closed-form policy update weighted by reward and cost advantages. (3) For online fine-tuning, safe policy expansion (PEX) freezes the offline policy and trains a new policy that probabilistically selects actions from both policies using a softmax over normalized Q-values (reward minus cost), ensuring the initial fine-tuning stage retains offline safety while gradually exploring toward higher performance without constraint violations.

## Results

On the Safety-Gymnasium benchmark (5 vision-only tasks, 1M offline + 0.5M online fine-tuning steps), FOSP achieves an average reward of 16.6 with an average cost of 0.54, substantially outperforming SafeDreamer (reward 12.5, cost 1.01) and Recovery-RL (reward 6.7, cost 0.91) while maintaining near-zero constraint violations. Compared to DreamerV3, which achieves a higher average reward of 20.8, FOSP trades a modest reward gap for a dramatic cost reduction (0.54 vs. 171.1). Against purely online baselines (CPO, PPO-Lagrangian trained for 10M steps), FOSP achieves comparable or better reward (16.6 vs. 14.5 and 7.5) with orders-of-magnitude lower cost (0.54 vs. 51.8 and 27.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FOSP outperforms SafeDreamer (the strongest safe model-based baseline) on average reward (16.6 vs. 12.5) while achieving lower cost (0.54 vs. 1.01), and the gap widens on harder tasks like PointPush1 (13.2 vs. 10.3 reward) and PointGoal2 (13.5 vs. 12.7). Against DreamerV3, FOSP sacrifices some reward (16.6 vs. 20.8) but reduces cost by over 300× (0.54 vs. 171.1), confirming that DreamerV3's lack of safety awareness leads to severe constraint violations. Recovery-RL, which learns constraint violation zones offline before online training, is consistently outperformed by FOSP on both reward and cost across all five tasks. In the online-only comparison (Table 2), FOSP (0.5M fine-tuning steps) beats CPO and PPO-Lagrangian (10M training steps) on cost by 2–3 orders of magnitude (0.54 vs. 51.8 and 27.7) while matching or exceeding their reward, and also outperforms SafeDreamer with online planning (12.9 reward, 1.77 cost) on both metrics. The real-world Franka robot experiments further show FOSP improving success rate from 20–40% to 50–65% after only 40 fine-tuning trials on unseen obstacle configurations, while reducing constraint violation rates from 50–60% to 20–30%.

## Relation to Prior Work

FOSP sits at the intersection of three research threads: model-based offline RL (building on DreamerV3's RSSM world model and IQL's in-sample optimization to combat value overestimation), safe model-based RL (extending SafeDreamer's Lagrangian planning with the reachability estimation function from RESPO to handle hard constraints), and offline-to-online RL (adopting policy expansion from PEX to bridge the offline-online gap). The key departure from prior work is the unification of these threads into a single framework specifically designed for safe generalization—adapting a pretrained safe policy to unseen safety constraints—rather than treating offline safe RL and online safe RL as separate problems. The feasibility-guided approach from Zheng et al. (2023) is repurposed to split the optimization into feasible/infeasible regions, with the reachability function serving as the connecting mechanism between them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practically critical gap: deploying safe robot policies in environments with safety constraints not present in the training data, where unsafe exploration is unacceptable. The safe policy expansion mechanism directly tackles the known pathology that stronger offline methods lead to worse online fine-tuning, offering a principled way to leverage offline safety knowledge during adaptation. Limitations include evaluation on relatively simple 2D navigation tasks and a single-robot reach task, with 'unseen' scenarios still sharing substantial structure with training data; scalability to complex multi-constraint manipulation or long-horizon tasks remains open. The finding that dataset composition (safe vs. unsafe ratio) has a non-trivial, counterintuitive effect on model-based safe RL performance is a useful practical insight for practitioners collecting offline data.
