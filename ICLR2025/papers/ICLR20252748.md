## Problem

Neuron-adaptive methods for accelerating Transformer inference suffer from three critical limitations in modern architectures: they are restricted to MLP layers and cannot be applied to QKV/attention linear layers, they depend on activation-induced sparsity (e.g., ReLU) making them ineffective with non-sparse activations like SwiGLU used in Llama, Gemma, and Mistral, and they require costly full activation computations before thresholding. These constraints lead to rapid performance degradation at moderate compression rates in state-of-the-art Transformers.

## Input / Output

The method consumes hidden-state token representations (vectors of dimension d_in) and the pretrained weight matrices of linear layers (W ∈ R^{o×i}), using 32,000 observed hidden states to compute the SVD-based A and B decomposition offline. At inference, the adapted layer takes the same hidden state x and produces a low-rank approximation of the original layer output, where the effective rank is determined dynamically per-token by a binary masker, reducing FLOPs proportionally to the number of active ranks. The final model output remains standard next-token predictions, with the compression transparent to downstream task evaluation.

## Method

RaNA introduces the Adaptive Rank Allocation framework, which replaces any linear layer Wx with A(r(x) ⊙ Bx), where A and B are derived from the SVD of WX (A = U_r, B = U_r^T W) and r(x) is an input-dependent binary masker that selects which ranks to activate. The B-Masker computes (Bx)² to identify the most important ranks per input, exploiting the observed heavy-tailed, near-zero-concentrated distribution of rank contributions. For SwiGLU MLPs, RaNA applies rank adapters to the Up and Gate projections while using neuron thresholding on the Down projection, combined with a FLOP allocation procedure (line search at the rank-adapter level, grid search at the MLP level) that balances compute across components. This design generalizes neuron adapters (proven in Proposition 1), eliminates reliance on activation sparsity, and enables direct application to QKV layers, achieving up to 7 perplexity-point and 8 percentage-point accuracy improvements over CATS at ~44% FLOP compression.

## Results

On Llama2-7b at 42% FLOP compression, RaNA achieves 56.37% average accuracy across six NLP benchmarks and 8.04 perplexity, compared to 47.51% and 12.36 for CATS and 43.21% and 13.69 for SliceGPT. On Gemma-2b at 44% compression, RaNA attains 48.16% accuracy and 13.83 perplexity versus 43.01% and 21.02 for CATS. In layer-level reconstruction error at ~50% FLOP reduction, RaNA achieves 10.5% average error on Llama2-7b MLPs and 2.18% on Gemma-2b MLPs, substantially outperforming CATS (17.22% and 20.31% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CATS (the state-of-the-art neuron adapter for SwiGLU models), RaNA wins decisively at higher compression rates: at 42% FLOP reduction on Llama2-7b, RaNA improves accuracy by 8.9 percentage-points (56.37% vs. 47.51%) and reduces perplexity by 4.3 points (8.04 vs. 12.36); on Gemma-2b at 44% compression, the gap widens to 5.2 accuracy points and 7.2 perplexity points. At lower compression (~15–20%), CATS slightly edges out RaNA in both accuracy and perplexity, suggesting RaNA's advantage is most pronounced under aggressive compression. Against SliceGPT (static structured pruning), RaNA dominates across all compression levels, achieving 8–13 perplexity-point improvements and 8–13 accuracy-point gains, which the authors attribute to the adaptive nature of RaNA versus SliceGPT's fixed row/column deletion. Against the standard neuron-adaptive baseline (Deja Vu-style MLP masker), RaNA consistently outperforms on Pythia models (GeLU-based) across multiple sizes, with 6.7–18.1 percentage-point improvements in layer reconstruction error, confirming the benefit of rank-based adaptation over neuron masking even for non-SwiGLU architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the neuron-adaptive compute framework (CATS, ReLUfication, Deja Vu) into a broader Adaptive Rank Allocation framework, showing via Proposition 1 that neuron adapters are a strict special case of rank adapters. The key conceptual departure is shifting the unit of adaptation from individual neurons (only present in MLPs) to matrix ranks (present in any linear layer), enabling direct application to QKV projections and eliminating reliance on activation-function sparsity. The A and B matrices are derived from an empirical SVD of the weight-input product (Eckart-Young), and the B-Masker selects ranks by thresholding (Bx)², drawing on the same thresholding philosophy as CATS but operating in a lower-dimensional rank space rather than the full neuron space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RaNA offers a practically significant efficiency win—44% FLOP reduction with only ~4 perplexity points lost on Llama2-7b versus ~12 for CATS—making it a strong candidate for deployment in latency-sensitive LLM serving. The framework's generality (any linear layer, any activation function) and the clean theoretical grounding (SVD optimality, rank-sparsity) position it as a more principled foundation for future adaptive-compute research. Open questions include whether model-level (rather than per-layer) FLOP allocation could yield further gains, whether the data-dependent A/B calibration transfers across domains, and whether the approach extends to architectures like Mamba or mixture-of-experts models where the linear-layer assumption may not hold as cleanly.
