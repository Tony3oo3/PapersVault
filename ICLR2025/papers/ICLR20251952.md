## Problem

Score-based causal discovery from observational data is NP-hard and computationally expensive, yet existing methods (gradient-based, RL-based) do not exploit past evaluation data to prioritize the most promising DAGs. Bayesian Optimization offers sample-efficient black-box optimization but has not been applied to purely observational causal discovery, and its standard GP surrogate scales cubically with the number of evaluations, making it impractical for the thousands of trials DAG learning requires.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an observational dataset of n i.i.d. samples over d variables, a decomposable score function (e.g., BIC for additive noise models), and a low-rank parameter k ≪ d that controls the search space dimensionality d(1+k). It outputs a binary adjacency matrix representing a DAG that maximizes the score, optionally pruned via linear thresholding, GAM significance testing, or conditional independence testing. The search operates over a continuous vector z ∈ [-1,1]^{d(1+k)} (node potentials concatenated with a flattened d×k embedding matrix), mapped to a DAG via a deterministic, acyclicity-guaranteeing operator.

## Method

DrBO replaces the GP surrogate in Bayesian Optimization with single-layer dropout neural networks (BatchNorm + ReLU + dropout) trained continually via a reservoir-sampling replay buffer, enabling scalable uncertainty quantification through stochastic forward passes for Thompson sampling. The search space is a low-rank DAG representation τ(p,R) = H(grad(p)) ⊙ H(R·R^T) with dimensionality d(1+k) instead of O(d²), where the node potential p enforces acyclicity and the embedding matrix R controls connectivity. Rather than modeling a direct DAG→score map, DrBO trains d independent local dropout networks predicting node-wise scores (e.g., ln MSE_i from the parent set), then combines them via the BIC formula to form the acquisition function. Each BO iteration generates C preliminary candidates in an adaptively-sized trust region around the current best solution, evaluates the Thompson-sampling acquisition function on all C, and evaluates the top B candidates on the true score before updating the surrogate networks.

## Results

On real-world BnLearn structures (Table 1), DrBO achieves zero SHD on four of five datasets (Asia, Cancer, Child, Earthquake) and a SHD of 1.0±2.2 on the challenging 37-node Alarm network, compared to 4.8±6.2 for the next-best method (GOLEM) and 7.0±7.9 for NOTEARS+TMPI. On dense synthetic graphs (30 nodes, ~240 edges), DrBO surpasses the second-best methods (ALIAS and CORL) by margins exceeding 20% across all five summary metrics (TPR, Precision, Recall, F1, FDR). On nonlinear GP data (10 nodes, ~40 edges), DrBO reaches a final SHD of 0.4, and on the Sachs protein-signaling dataset it achieves SHD of 9 with fewer evaluations than all competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DrBO outperforms all nine baselines across every experimental setting. Among gradient-based methods, DAGMA and COSMO struggle most on dense and large graphs, with SHDs of 25.0 and 26.8 respectively on the 37-node Alarm network versus DrBO's 1.0; NOTEARS+TMPI and GOLEM are competitive on small structures (achieving zero SHD on Asia, Cancer, Child, Earthquake) but still trail DrBO on Alarm (7.0 and 4.8 vs. 1.0). Among RL-based methods, ALIAS and CORL are the closest competitors on dense synthetic graphs (30 nodes, ~240 edges), yet DrBO surpasses them by more than 20% in each of TPR, Precision, Recall, F1, and FDR, and reaches higher BIC scores and lower SHDs with fewer evaluations. On nonlinear GP data, DrBO achieves a final SHD of 0.4 versus visibly higher values for all baselines, and on the Sachs dataset it reaches SHD 9 with fewer evaluations than any competitor. For the BGe score comparison on Markov equivalence class discovery, DrBO finds DAGs with scores closely matching ground truth, while PC and GES produce substantially lower scores and higher structural errors.

## Relation to Prior Work

DrBO sits at the intersection of score-based causal discovery (NOTEARS, DAGMA, COSMO, GOLEM, CORL, ALIAS) and Bayesian optimization, but departs from prior BO applications to causal discovery that were limited to the active/interventional setting (Toth et al., Zhang et al.) or to optimizing variables within a known causal system (Aglietti et al.). The method repurposes the low-rank DAG representation from Vec2DAG (Duong et al., 2025) and its predecessors (Yu et al., Massidda et al.) to create an unconstrained search space amenable to BO, and replaces the standard GP surrogate with dropout neural networks (Gal & Ghahramani, Guo et al.) to overcome the cubic scaling bottleneck. The indirect surrogate modeling—learning node-wise local scores rather than a direct DAG-to-score map—is a design choice specific to the decomposable structure of BIC-type scores, distinguishing it from generic BO applications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first score-based causal discovery method to harness BO for purely observational data, and the consistent large-margin improvements over strong RL and gradient-based baselines (often achieving SHD ≈ 0 where competitors reach 20–120) demonstrate that sample-efficient sequential optimization is a viable and superior paradigm for DAG learning. The scalability solution (dropout NNs + continual training + low-rank representation) makes BO practical for the thousands of evaluations that DAG learning typically requires, a regime where conventional GP-based BO fails. Open questions include whether the fixed surrogate architecture can be adapted via neural architecture search, whether the approach extends to hidden confounders (where outputs are no longer DAGs), and how it combines with active causal discovery for even greater efficiency.
