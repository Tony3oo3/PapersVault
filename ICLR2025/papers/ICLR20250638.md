## Problem

Supervised graph anomaly detection (GAD) methods trained on a small set of labelled anomalies tend to overfit those seen anomalies, causing unseen anomaly classes to be misclassified as normal. Existing open-set anomaly detection approaches were designed for Euclidean data (images, tabular) and fail to exploit the discriminative structural information inherent in graph data. This paper tackles the under-explored problem of open-set GAD, where the model must generalise to anomaly classes not represented in training while retaining detection of seen anomalies.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an attributed graph (node feature matrix X and edge set E) together with a small set of labelled nodes: 50 seen anomaly nodes and 5% of normal nodes by default (reduced to 100 anomalies and 1% normal nodes for large-scale datasets like ogbn-arxiv). During training, the NSR module additionally samples batches of normal-node-oriented relation pairs (up to 512 per batch, balanced across three relation types). At inference, the NSR module is fully disconnected; only the shared GraphSAGE representation learner and a two-layer anomaly scoring network produce a scalar anomaly score per node, introducing no additional runtime overhead.

## Method

NSReg introduces a Normal Structure Regularisation (NSR) module as a plug-and-play regularisation term alongside any supervised GAD loss. The NSR module models three types of normal-node-oriented relations—connected normal-normal pairs (label 1), unconnected normal-normal pairs (label α=0.8), and unconnected normal-to-unlabelled pairs (label 0)—using a relation encoder that computes element-wise products of sigmoid-scaled node representations followed by a linear normality predictor. A key theoretical result (Proposition 1) proves that a well-trained relation discriminator forces all anomaly nodes (seen or unseen) outside the connected normal subspace in the shared representation space, effectively tightening the normal decision boundary. The representation learner (two-layer GraphSAGE + two-layer projection) is jointly optimised with both the GAD loss and the NSR loss via backpropagation, while the NSR module itself is discarded at inference. The approach is validated as a drop-in plugin that improves DevNet, DCI, and BWGNN by 5–10% AUC-ROC and 10–30% AUC-PR without modifying their core architectures.

## Results

NSReg achieves an average AUC-ROC of 0.849 and AUC-PR of 0.622 on all anomalies, and 0.808 AUC-ROC and 0.425 AUC-PR on unseen anomalies across Photo, Computers, CS, and Yelp. On unseen anomalies specifically, NSReg improves AUC-ROC by 14% over the best competing method (BCE, 0.709) and by 17.4% over the second-best (PMP, 0.688), while improving AUC-PR by 35% and 39.4% respectively. On large-scale datasets (ogbn-arxiv, ogbn-proteins, T-Finance), NSReg achieves 0.810 average AUC-ROC on all anomalies, outperforming the best baseline (BWGNN at 0.745) by 6.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NSReg outperforms all 19 competing baselines on both AUC-ROC and AUC-PR across all four medium-scale datasets. Among supervised graph methods, the closest competitors are BCE (0.774 avg AUC-ROC on all anomalies) and PMP (0.761), which NSReg surpasses by 9.7% and 11.6% respectively; on unseen anomalies the gap widens to 14% and 17.4%. Supervised Euclidean methods (PReNet, DevNet) degrade substantially on unseen anomalies (e.g., PReNet drops from 0.642 to 0.532 AUC-ROC), confirming their inability to generalise beyond seen anomaly patterns. Unsupervised GAD methods (DOMINANT, COLA, TAM, etc.) consistently underperform, with average AUC-ROC below 0.56 on all anomalies, highlighting the value of anomaly-informed supervision. Imbalanced classification methods (G.ENS, G.SMOTE) also fail to generalise to unseen anomalies, with G.SMOTE achieving only 0.665 AUC-ROC on unseen anomalies versus NSReg's 0.808. On the large-scale T-Finance dataset, NSReg (0.929 AUC-ROC) edges out BWGNN (0.922) and GHRN (0.923), while on ogbn-proteins it achieves 0.843 versus DCI's 0.815, demonstrating consistent gains at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of supervised graph anomaly detection and open-set anomaly detection, two lines of work that have developed largely independently. Supervised GAD methods (BWGNN, DCI, AMNet, PMP) overfit to seen anomalies and lack structural regularisation, while open-set AD methods (DevNet, PReNet) are designed for Euclidean data and ignore graph topology. NSReg introduces a normal-node-oriented relation prediction regulariser with a three-level normality hierarchy, repurposing the contrastive-learning-style relation modelling idea but specifically targeting the normal class boundary rather than anomaly separation. The theoretical Proposition 1 provides a formal guarantee that this structural normality enforcement displaces all anomalies (seen and unseen) from the normal subspace, a result not available in prior supervised GAD work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Open-set GAD is a practically critical but under-explored setting where real-world anomaly types inevitably evolve beyond training labels, and the 14% AUC-ROC gap on unseen anomalies over the best baselines is substantial. The plug-and-play design (validated on DevNet, DCI, BWGNN) means the NSR module can immediately benefit a wide range of existing supervised GAD pipelines without architectural changes. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that normality is modelled only within the immediate neighbourhood of labelled normal nodes, which may become insufficient as real-world graphs grow in scale and interaction complexity. Open questions include whether higher-order structural relations or cross-domain normality signals could further strengthen the regularisation, and whether the approach extends to other open-set structured-data tasks beyond node-level GAD.
