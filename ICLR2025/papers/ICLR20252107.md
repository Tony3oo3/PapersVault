## Problem

The quantum implementation of k-means++ given by Kerenidis et al. (2019) lacked a proof that its O(log k) approximation guarantee survives the small D²-sampling errors introduced by quantum distance estimation. Additionally, it was unclear whether the polylogarithmic-in-N speedup of quantum D²-sampling could be replicated classically, and no quantum approximation scheme for k-means with polylogarithmic dependence on N existed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252107/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a dataset V ∈ R^{N×d} (N points in d dimensions), a number of clusters k, and optionally an error parameter ε for the approximation scheme. In the quantum setting, data is accessed via a QRAM structure; in the classical (quantum-inspired) setting, a sample-query (SQ) tree data structure must be built in O(Nd) preprocessing time, after which each D²-sampling round is sublinear in N. The output is a set of k cluster centers C ∈ R^{k×d} achieving O(log k) approximation, or for the approximation scheme, a center set with cost within (1+ε)·OPT at the cost of exponential dependence on k/ε.

## Method

The core quantum D²-sampling procedure prepares an approximate distance state |i⟩|j⟩|ψ_{i,j}⟩ via swap test, coherent amplitude estimation, and median estimation (following Kerenidis et al. 2019), then uses controlled rotations and rejection sampling to draw a point with probability proportional to its squared distance to the nearest center. The key dequantization insight is that Tang's sample-query (SQ) access model natively supports sampling by squared ℓ₂ norms—exactly the D²-sampling distribution—so the quantum steps map directly to classical operations on tree-based data structures that support O(log n) sampling and O(1) norm queries. The resulting QI-k-means++ algorithm runs in O(Nd) + Õ(ζ²k²d) time, where ζ is the aspect ratio, and the O(Nd) preprocessing is parallelizable. For the approximation scheme, the paper quantizes the D²-sampling-based scheme of Bhattacharya et al. (2020b), showing robustness of the (1+ε) guarantee against the small distance-estimation errors inherent in the quantum procedure, yielding the first quantum k-means approximation scheme with polylogarithmic dependence on N.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical contribution is a quantum-inspired classical algorithm (QI-k-means++) with running time O(Nd) + Õ(ζ²k²d) that preserves the O(log k) approximation guarantee of k-means++, where ζ is the aspect ratio. A quantum version achieves Õ(ζ²k²) running time (polylogarithmic in N), and a quantum approximation scheme achieves Õ(2^(Õ(k/ε))dζ^O(1)) for (1+ε)-approximation. Empirically, on binarized MNIST (70,000 points, d=784), QI-k-means++ achieves slightly lower clustering costs than classical k-means++ (e.g., 6.66×10⁻⁶ vs 6.83×10⁻⁶ at k=10), while on IRIS (150 points, d=4) results are mixed due to the large aspect ratio regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against K-MC² (Bachem et al. 2016b), QI-k-means++ has a better dependence on k (k² vs k³) and avoids the need to compute the parameter γ, which itself requires solving the k-means problem. Against AFK-MC² (Bachem et al. 2016a), QI-k-means++ provides a clean O(log k) multiplicative approximation without the additive ε·Φ_OPT^1 term, and again has better k-dependence (k² vs k³). Against RejectionSampling (Cohen-Addad et al. 2020), there is a tradeoff: QI-k-means++ has a worse ζ-dependence (ζ² vs (log ζ)^(1+O(1/c²))) but a much better d-dependence (d vs d³) and a simpler O(log k) approximation guarantee (vs O(c⁶log k)). Against classical k-means++ (Arthur & Vassilvitskii 2007), QI-k-means++ is sublinear in N after O(Nd) preprocessing, making it advantageous for large N with small aspect ratio (e.g., binarized MNIST), but can be slower for small N with large aspect ratio (e.g., IRIS) due to the ζ² factor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the quantum k-means work of Kerenidis et al. (2019), which provided a quantum D²-sampling procedure but lacked a rigorous approximation guarantee for the resulting quantum k-means++. The authors close this gap by invoking the noisy k-means++ analysis of Grunau et al. (2023) and Bhattacharya et al. (2020a), showing that small relative errors in D²-sampling probabilities preserve the O(log k) approximation. A second major thread is the dequantization framework of Tang (2023), which the authors apply to their quantum D²-sampling to obtain a classical quantum-inspired algorithm (QI-k-means++) with sublinear dependence on N after linear preprocessing. Finally, the quantum approximation scheme (Theorem 4) quantizes the highly parallel D²-sampling-based scheme of Bhattacharya et al. (2020b), yielding the first quantum algorithm for k-means with polylogarithmic dependence on dataset size N.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first quantum approximation scheme for k-means with polylogarithmic N-dependence and a practical dequantized classical algorithm (QI-k-means++) that is sublinear in N after O(Nd) preprocessing, adding a new entry to the family of fast k-means++ implementations. However, the running time depends on the aspect ratio ζ (quadratically or worse), which can be large even for simple clustered data, limiting practical speedups. The experimental evaluation is limited to two small datasets (binarized MNIST, IRIS) and does not demonstrate clear advantages over existing fast implementations like AFK-MC² on large-scale data. Open questions include improving the dependence on ζ, understanding the tradeoff between ζ and dataset size, and whether the quantum approximation scheme's exponential dependence on k/ε can be reduced.
