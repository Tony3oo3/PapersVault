## Problem

Existing convergence results for Riemannian min-max algorithms rely on global convexity/concavity assumptions that do not hold in practical applications such as GANs, while local convergence to differential equilibria (DSE/DNE)—which are generic in non-convex non-concave problems—remains unanalyzed in the Riemannian setting. The paper fills this gap by establishing local linear convergence guarantees for simultaneous gradient-based algorithms on Riemannian manifolds and identifying when a gradient-adjustment variant outperforms standard gradient descent-ascent.

## Input / Output

The method takes a C² objective f on a product manifold M₁ × M₂ (e.g., Euclidean × Stiefel), a base learning rate γ, and a two-time-scale ratio τ, and produces a sequence converging locally to a differential Stackelberg or Nash equilibrium. In the GAN application, the generator maps low-dimensional noise to 28×28 grayscale images (MNIST/FashionMNIST) or 5-dimensional Gaussian samples, while the discriminator is constrained to the Stiefel manifold to enforce 1-Lipschitz continuity. The trained generator's quality is measured by FID scores (images) or EMD distance (Gaussian), with the discriminator parameterized by orthogonal matrices on St(k,d).

## Method

The core technique applies the Ostrowski fixed-point theorem on manifolds to reduce local convergence analysis to computing the spectral radius of a Jacobian matrix in a local coordinate chart, yielding explicit sufficient conditions on τ and γ for linear convergence of τ-GDA to DSE and DNE. To counteract slow convergence under strong rotational dynamics, the paper extends symplectic gradient-adjustment to Riemannian manifolds as τ-SGA, where the correction term is built from Riemannian cross-gradients and provably orthogonal to the base gradient direction. An asymptotic analysis for large τ shows that τ-SGA can admit a broader valid range of τ and achieve a faster linear rate than τ-GDA when the x-Hessian is not positive definite (DSE but not DNE). The approach is validated on orthogonal Wasserstein GANs with Stiefel-constrained discriminators, where stochastic τ-GDA and τ-SGA exhibit the predicted convergence behavior on both synthetic Gaussian and real image benchmarks.

## Results

On MNIST with orthogonal Wasserstein GAN, τ-SGA at τ=5 with T=5×10⁵ iterations achieves FID(train)=5.76 and FID(val)=7.6, outperforming τ-GDA at τ=20 (FID(train)=7.0, FID(val)=9.1) which is the best stable τ-GDA configuration. τ-GDA at τ=5 is unstable (FID(train)=13.38, FID(val)=16), while τ-SGA at the same τ=5 converges steadily as T increases. On the Gaussian distribution benchmark, τ-GDA at τ=1 exhibits large oscillations and increasing EMD distance, whereas τ=10 and τ=100 maintain stable convergence with EMD close to 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares τ-GDA and τ-SGA (its proposed extension of symplectic gradient-adjustment to Riemannian manifolds) on orthogonal Wasserstein GANs. τ-GDA at τ=5 fails to converge on MNIST (FID degrades from initial values), while τ-SGA at the same τ=5 converges steadily, achieving FID(val)=7.6 after 5×10⁵ iterations versus τ-GDA's best stable result of FID(val)=9.1 at τ=20. The correction term in τ-SGA is identified as the key factor enabling convergence at smaller τ values where τ-GDA exhibits rotational instability. On the Gaussian distribution benchmark, τ-GDA at τ=1 shows oscillating angle and increasing EMD, confirming the theoretical prediction that τ must exceed a threshold (≈36 in the toy example) for convergence, while τ-SGA can converge at much smaller τ. In terms of computational cost, τ-SGA takes roughly 3–4× the per-iteration time of τ-GDA, but reaches comparable or better FID in fewer iterations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Euclidean local convergence analysis of gradient-based min-max algorithms (Daskalakis & Panageas 2018; Li et al. 2022; Jin et al. 2020) to Riemannian manifolds by leveraging the Ostrowski fixed-point theorem in local coordinates, reducing the spectral analysis to intrinsic quantities (Riemannian Hessian, cross-gradients). It also generalizes the symplectic gradient-adjustment method (Balduzzi et al. 2018; Letcher et al. 2019) from Euclidean space to manifolds, introducing a two-time-scale variant τ-SGA. The application to orthogonal Wasserstein GANs (Müller et al. 2019) connects the theory to a practical Riemannian min-max problem where the discriminator is constrained to the Stiefel manifold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear theoretical explanation for the 'convergence dilemma' of τ-GDA (requiring large τ for stability but suffering slow rates) and shows that τ-SGA can break this trade-off by permitting smaller τ with faster linear convergence. However, the analysis is strictly local and deterministic; the stochastic GAN experiments are validated empirically without theoretical guarantees, and the benchmarks (Gaussian, MNIST, FashionMNIST) are relatively simple. Open questions include extending the framework to global convergence, analyzing stochastic variants rigorously, and whether the insights transfer to more complex GAN architectures or non-zero-sum games.
