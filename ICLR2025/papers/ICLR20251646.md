## Problem

Self-interested independent learning agents typically fail to cooperate in general-sum games, converging to sub-optimal equilibria like mutual defection. While prior learning-aware methods (LOLA, M-FOS, Shaper) show that modeling co-player learning dynamics can improve cooperation, they suffer from biased gradients, reliance on higher-order derivatives, or inability to handle the minibatched nature of practical RL updates. This paper asks: can we derive an unbiased, higher-derivative-free policy gradient for learning-aware RL that correctly accounts for minibatched co-player updates, and under what conditions does learning awareness actually produce cooperation among equally capable agents?

## Input / Output

The policy is a Hawk recurrent sequence model that consumes long observation histories h_l^{i,b} spanning M inner episodes of length T steps each, across B parallel trajectories (a full meta-episode of MT steps per trajectory). Unlike standard RL policies that condition only on the current episode, the input here explicitly contains inter-episode traces of co-player learning dynamics. The output is a factorized joint action distribution over the B batch elements with shared parameters, producing one action per trajectory per timestep. The same architecture serves both meta agents (full multi-episode context) and naive agents (context truncated to the current inner episode), enabling direct comparison and mixed-group training.

## Method

COALA-PG is derived by casting co-player shaping as a single-agent expected-return maximization over a batched POMDP where B parallel inner episodes form the environment dynamics and co-player policy updates occur at episode boundaries. The resulting policy gradient has three critical properties distinguishing it from prior methods: (i) gradients are summed (not averaged) across the batch to compensate for the O(1/B) effect of a single action on a naive learner's minibatch update, (ii) current-episode returns are rescaled by 1/B to correctly balance against future-episode returns, and (iii) each action's credit assignment uses future returns averaged over the entire minibatch rather than the individual trajectory. The algorithm is implemented with PPO/A2C using a custom GAE variant that reuses a batched lambda-returns procedure. The paper further proves that LOLA-DICE is a special case of COALA-PG, and provides an analytical IPD analysis showing that cooperation emerges only in heterogeneous groups mixing naive and learning-aware agents, where naive learners provide shaping gradients away from defection while learning-aware peers provide gradients away from extortion.

## Results

On the Iterated Prisoner's Dilemma, COALA-PG-trained agents extort naive learners into unfair cooperation (reaching considerably higher reward than M-FOS or batch-unaware baselines) and, when trained in mixed pools (p_naive=0.75), cooperate with other meta agents, while M-FOS and batch-unaware COALA-PG both collapse into mutual defection. On CleanUp-lite (a sequential social dilemma from Melting Pot), COALA-PG achieves significantly higher average returns than all baselines, establishing a mutual cooperation protocol with lower zapping rates and fairer division of cleaning efforts. The key quantitative distinction is that COALA-PG's minibatch-aware gradient correctly balances current-episode and future-episode return contributions, whereas M-FOS overemphasizes current-episode returns (missing the 1/B rescaling), causing it to lose the co-player shaping signal entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against M-FOS (Lu et al., 2022), COALA-PG wins decisively on both environments: on IPD, M-FOS agents defect from the beginning of each meta-episode and achieve lower reward, failing to optimize the shaping objective, while COALA-PG shapes naive co-players effectively; on CleanUp-lite, M-FOS produces higher cleaning discrepancy and more frequent zapping. Against LOLA-DICE (Foerster et al., 2018b), COALA-PG matches the cooperation behavior on IPD but without requiring higher-order derivatives or access to opponent parameters; moreover, the paper shows LOLA-DICE with many look-ahead steps (≥10) degrades into mutual extortion rather than cooperation, whereas COALA-PG remains stable. Against the batch-unaware COALA-PG ablation (which ignores cross-trajectory credit assignment), the full COALA-PG rule is essential: the batch-unaware variant performs identically to M-FOS, confirming that the 1/B rescaling and cross-batch return averaging are critical for shaping to succeed. The paper also notes that when COALA-PG agents play against M-FOS agents, they successfully shape M-FOS agents into cooperative behavior, demonstrating asymmetric dominance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the learning-aware multi-agent RL line initiated by LOLA (Foerster et al., 2018a) and M-FOS (Lu et al., 2022), and adopts the single-level long-context sequence policy architecture from Shaper (Khan et al., 2024). Its key departure is deriving an unbiased, higher-derivative-free policy gradient (COALA-PG) that explicitly accounts for the minibatched nature of co-player updates, whereas prior methods either require backpropagating through opponent parameter updates (LOLA-DICE) or use biased bilevel formulations (M-FOS). The use of an efficient Hawk recurrent sequence model to condition on multi-episode observation histories is a practical innovation that makes the approach scalable in context length without quadratic attention costs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first unbiased, higher-derivative-free policy gradient for learning-aware RL, removing the need for privileged access to opponent parameters or learning rules, which is a meaningful practical simplification. The conceptual finding that cooperation among learning-aware agents requires the presence of naive agents in the training pool (a pure learning-aware group cannot escape mutual defection) is a non-obvious mechanism that reframes how heterogeneous agent populations might be designed. Limitations include evaluation only on two-agent settings and relatively small environments (IPD, CleanUp-lite); whether the heterogeneity requirement and minibatch-aware gradient advantage persist at larger scale, with more agents, or in continuous-action domains remains open.
