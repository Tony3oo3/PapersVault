## Problem

Hot diffusion (pure Gaussian noise) ignores the strong spectral dependency between high-frequency image detail and low-frequency structures, causing random behavior in early generation steps. Cold diffusion (pure blurring) exploits this dependency but neglects noise's role in maintaining data manifold diversity, leading to out-of-manifold sampling and degraded quality. No prior work provides a principled framework for jointly controlling blur and noise levels to balance these two failure modes.

## Input / Output

The model takes a random Gaussian noise sample (and optionally a class label for conditional generation) and iteratively produces an RGB image at 32×32, 64×64, or 128×128 resolution. During the reverse process, the network receives a blurry-noisy observation x_{α,β} = VM_αV^T x_0 + βε along with two continuous conditioning signals (blur level α and noise level β), and outputs two predictions in a single forward pass: a denoised blurry signal and a residual high-frequency component. The blur is applied as a diagonal Gaussian mask in the DCT domain, making the corruption frequency-selective rather than spatially uniform.

## Method

Warm Diffusion extends the DDIM-style diffusion process to a two-dimensional state space controlled by blur level α and noise level β. The key architectural insight is a divide-and-conquer decomposition of the reverse-step mean function into two sub-tasks: a denoiser D_θ that recovers the blurry signal VM_αV^T x_0 from the noisy observation, and a deblurrer R_θ that predicts the residual high-frequency detail x_res = V(I−M_α)V^T x_0. Both networks share most parameters (output channels are doubled and split), adding <0.15% model size over the EDM baseline. The Blur-to-Noise Ratio (BNR = α/β) is set to 0.5 based on spectral analysis: natural images follow a 1/f² power law while white noise is flat, so BNR=0.5 is the threshold where noise begins to dominate high-frequency bands, preserving data manifold integrity while still simplifying the denoising task. Sampling uses Heun's 2nd-order solver with a modified noise schedule (higher ρ) that allocates more steps to the low-noise regime where high-frequency detail is generated.

## Results

On unconditional CIFAR-10 (32×32), Warm Diffusion achieves FID 1.85 and IS 10.02 with only 35 NFE, outperforming EDM (FID 1.97) and PFGM++ (FID 1.91). On class-conditional CIFAR-10, it reaches FID 1.68 and IS 10.19, and on FFHQ 64×64 it achieves FID 2.29 with 79 NFE. On the higher-resolution LSUN-church 128×128 benchmark, the method attains FID 3.47 (10k samples) and FID 2.56 (50k samples) with 511 NFE, substantially outperforming Blurring Diffusion (FID 3.88) and IHDM (FID 45.06).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Warm Diffusion outperforms all compared baselines across every dataset and setting. Against the strongest hot-diffusion baselines (EDM, EDM-ES, STF, PFGM++), it improves FID by 0.06–0.11 on CIFAR-10 and by 0.14–0.24 on FFHQ 64×64, while using the same number of sampling steps (35 or 79 NFE). Against cold/blur-based methods, the gains are dramatic: FID drops from 80.08 (Cold Diffusion) and 18.96 (IHDM) to 1.85 on unconditional CIFAR-10, and from 45.06 (IHDM) to 2.56 on LSUN-church 128×128. Compared to Blurring Diffusion, which requires 1000 NFE for FID 3.17 on CIFAR-10, Warm Diffusion achieves FID 1.85 with only 35 NFE—a roughly 28× reduction in sampling cost. On LSUN-church, the method also needs fewer steps (511 vs. 1000 NFE) while achieving a lower FID (3.47 vs. 3.88). The ablation in Table 4 further shows that re-implementing Blurring Diffusion's BNR schedule under the same parameterization yields FID 12.97 at 35 NFE, confirming that the BNR scheduling strategy is a key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate diffusion paradigms—hot diffusion (pure Gaussian noise, as in DDPM/EDM) and cold diffusion (deterministic blurring, as in Cold Diffusion, IHDM, and Blurring Diffusion)—into a single blur-noise mixture framework. It builds directly on DDIM's forward/reverse process formulation and EDM's network architecture and training recipe, while introducing the Blur-to-Noise Ratio (BNR) as a new design parameter that interpolates between the two extremes. The divide-and-conquer parameterization, which splits the reverse-step prediction into a dedicated denoiser and deblurrer operating in the DCT domain, departs from prior blur-based methods (e.g., Blurring Diffusion) that use a single network to handle both degradation types jointly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The BNR framework provides a principled, spectrally-motivated design knob for diffusion model practitioners, explaining why purely cold (blur-only) methods underperform: excessive blurring shifts the data manifold and causes out-of-manifold sampling failures. The finding that BNR=0.5, derived from the 1/f² power-law of natural images versus the flat spectrum of white noise, offers a concrete recipe for balancing learning simplicity against manifold integrity. Limitations include evaluation only on small-to-medium resolutions (32×32 to 128×128), a fixed BNR rather than a learned schedule, and no exploration beyond RGB image generation. Open questions include whether the spectral-dependency argument generalizes to video, 3D, or other modalities, and whether adaptive or per-frequency-band BNR schedules could further improve quality.
