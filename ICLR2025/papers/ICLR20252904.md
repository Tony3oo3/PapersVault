## Problem

Existing training data detection methods for LLMs frame the task as binary classification and optimize for accuracy, but provide no controllable guarantee on the false discovery rate (FDR). This is a critical gap in high-stakes settings such as copyright enforcement, where falsely identifying non-training samples as training data could trigger unwarranted legal action. The paper asks whether rigorous FDR control can be achieved for training data detection without relying on validation sets or heuristic thresholds.

## Input / Output

The method requires gradient access to the target LLM (not just logits or output text), a T5-based paraphraser for generating knockoff texts, and a user-specified FDR bound q. It processes n text samples and outputs a subset Ŝ of indices identified as training samples, with the guarantee that E[|Ŝ ∩ D_non-train| / |Ŝ|] ≤ q. Unlike baseline MIA methods that need a labeled validation set to calibrate thresholds, KTD determines its detection threshold self-containedly from the knockoff statistics, making it applicable in settings where ground-truth membership labels are unavailable.

## Method

KTD reformulates training data detection as a variable selection problem: training samples are 'relevant variables' and the model parameter θ is the 'output' of the training algorithm. It generates knockoffs by paraphrasing each text sample (preserving semantics while altering surface form), then computes importance scores as the L2 norm of the model's gradient with respect to each sample. The key innovation over vanilla knockoff inference is aggregating m independent knockoff draws into a single statistic W_j^KTD = Z_j − (1/m)Σᵢ Z̃_j⁽ⁱ⁾, which reduces the variance of the statistic and improves separability between training and non-training samples. The paper proves that W_j^KTD retains the sign-flip symmetry needed for FDR control (Lemma 1) and establishes asymptotic optimality: as m → ∞, power → 1 and FDR → 0 (Theorems 1–2). A data-driven threshold τ is selected from the observed statistics to guarantee FDR ≤ q without any external calibration set.

## Results

At FDR bound q=0.1, KTD achieves FDR ≤ 0.1 on XSum (Pythia: 0.109, GPT-Neo: 0.101) and BBC Real Time (Pythia: 0.071, GPT-Neo: 0.067) while maintaining high power (0.97–1.000). On WikiMIA-GPT-2, KTD dramatically improves power over Vanilla KI from 0.476 to 0.869 at comparable FDR (~0.20). KTD is the only method that provides a formal FDR control guarantee without requiring a validation set or reference model, and it achieves asymptotically optimal power (→1) and FDR (→0) as the number of knockoff draws m→∞.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KTD's most direct comparison is against Vanilla KI (m=1): KTD substantially outperforms it in power while maintaining similar or better FDR — e.g., on WikiMIA-GPT-2, power jumps from 0.476 to 0.869, and on BBC Real Time-GPT-Neo, power improves from 0.873 to 0.973 at lower FDR (0.067 vs 0.083). Among the MIA baselines, LOSS achieves high power (0.93–1.00) but its FDR (0.12–0.18) exceeds the q=0.1 target and it requires a validation set to set its threshold. MinK% is highly unstable, swinging between FDR 0.10 and 0.31 with power as low as 0.024 on XSum-GPT-2. Zlib occasionally achieves very low FDR (0.000 on WikiMIA-GPT-2) but at the cost of near-zero power (0.060), and Lowercase consistently fails FDR control (FDR 0.48–0.81). The Ref baseline (Mireshghallah et al.) achieves competitive FDR on some settings but requires a reference model and provides no formal FDR guarantee. Critically, KTD is the only method in the comparison that offers a provable FDR ≤ q guarantee without any validation set, reference model, or heuristic threshold.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the knockoff inference framework from high-dimensional variable selection (Barber & Candès, 2015; Candes et al., 2018) into the LLM training data detection setting, reformulating membership inference as relevant variable selection. The core methodological departure from vanilla KI is the use of multiple knockoff draws to compute the statistic, drawing on the aggregation principle from Nguyen et al. (2020) to reduce variance and improve power. It positions itself against MIA baselines (LOSS, MinK%, Zlib, Lowercase, Ref) that optimize accuracy without statistical guarantees, and against dataset-level contamination tests (Oren et al., Dekoninck et al.) that do not operate at the individual-sample level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FDR control guarantee is practically critical in copyright and privacy enforcement scenarios where false positives carry legal consequences, and the asymptotic optimality results (power → 1, FDR → 0 as m → ∞) provide a clean theoretical foundation absent from prior MIA work. Key limitations include the requirement for gradient access (ruling out proprietary black-box models) and dependence on paraphraser quality, which the authors acknowledge limits performance in the low-FDR regime. Open questions include whether the framework can be adapted to use only logits or generated text, and how to generate 'good knockoffs' that satisfy the swap-invariance property more faithfully for natural language.
