## Problem

Existing gradient-free model merging methods (Task Arithmetic, TIES, DARE) achieve strong results when merging full-rank finetuned models but fail to transfer to LoRA finetuned models, even on the same tasks. The paper identifies the root cause as low centered kernel alignment (CKA) between LoRA task-updates: the low-rank constraint forces each model to operate in a different subspace, making their updates functionally misaligned. Additionally, prior benchmarks only evaluate merged models per-task, failing to test whether the merged model is truly general.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full-matrix LoRA task-updates (ΔW = BA) for each attention layer (query, key, value, output) across all models to be merged, along with the shared pretrained model weights. It is entirely data-free and gradient-free, requiring no validation data or gradient computation. The output is a single merged model whose weights equal the pretrained weights plus the SVD-reconstructed merged update at each layer, capable of solving all source tasks simultaneously. The paper also introduces a joint-task evaluation where the merged model must classify over the union of all 748 labels across eight vision datasets without knowing the source dataset.

## Method

KnOTS (Knowledge Orientation Through SVD) aligns LoRA task-updates by concatenating them column-wise per layer and decomposing the result via SVD into UΣV^T, where U provides a shared orthonormal basis, Σ encodes per-basis scales, and V^T decomposes into task-specific matrices [V^(1); ...; V^(n)]^T that are all aligned to the common U. Existing merging methods (TIES, DARE-TIES) are then applied directly on the aligned V^(i) matrices to produce a merged V^(merged), and the final merged update is reconstructed as UΣV^(merged) and added to the pretrained weights. Column-wise concatenation is critical: row-wise concatenation shifts the shared component to ΣV with task-specific U^(i), reducing alignment and hurting performance by 2.6%. For magnitude-based methods like TIES, pruning is applied on ΣV^(i) (which retains scale information) while merging operates on the pruned V^(i). KnOTS on Task Arithmetic reduces exactly to standard TA, making it a strict generalization that adds no new hyperparameters.

## Results

On the eight-vision-task per-task benchmark (ViT-B/32, LoRA rank 16), KnOTS-TIES achieves 68.0% average normalized accuracy, improving over TIES, TA, and DARE-TIES (all at 63.7%) by 4.3%. On the new joint-task benchmark evaluating generality across 748 labels, KnOTS-TIES reaches 46.8% Hits@1, outperforming the best baseline DARE-TIES (44.0%) by 2.8%. On the NLI setting with Llama-3-8B, KnOTS-TIES achieves 92.9% average normalized accuracy versus 90.9% for DARE-TIES, a 2.0% gain. The method also scales to ViT-L/14 (78.2% vs. 75.2% for TIES) and remains robust across LoRA ranks from 4 to 768.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KnOTS-TIES consistently outperforms all four gradient-free baselines on the primary vision benchmark: it beats RegMean (Jin et al., 2023) by 7.1% (68.0% vs. 60.9%), and beats TA (Ilharco et al., 2023), TIES (Yadav et al., 2023), and DARE-TIES (Yu et al., 2024) each by 4.3% (68.0% vs. 63.7%). Against the gradient-based Fisher Weight Averaging (Matena & Raffel, 2022), KnOTS-TIES also wins by 4.1% (68.0% vs. 63.9%) while requiring no training data or gradient computation. On the joint-task generality benchmark, KnOTS-TIES leads all baselines at every Hits@k level, achieving 46.8% Hits@1 versus 44.0% for DARE-TIES and 43.6% for both TA and TIES. On the NLI setting with Llama-3-8B, KnOTS-TIES (92.9%) surpasses DARE-TIES (90.9%) by 2.0% and TA (90.2%) by 2.7%, confirming the method's robustness across modalities and model scales. Notably, KnOTS-DARE-TIES provides only marginal gains over DARE-TIES (63.9% vs. 63.7%), suggesting the SVD alignment is most beneficial when combined with sign-resolution-based merging (TIES) rather than random pruning (DARE).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient-free model merging literature (Task Arithmetic, TIES, DARE, RegMean) to the LoRA setting, where prior methods are known to underperform. The key conceptual departure is diagnosing the failure mode via CKA analysis—showing that LoRA task-updates operate in misaligned subspaces due to the low-rank constraint—and proposing SVD-based subspace alignment (KnOTS) as a preprocessing step before applying existing merging operators. This is distinct from prior SVD-based LoRA tools (e.g., HuggingFace's '-svd' suffix methods), which use SVD to re-factorize already-merged parameters back into LoRA form rather than to align them pre-merging.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since LoRA is the dominant PEFT method in practice, a data-free, gradient-free alignment step that consistently improves merging by 3–4% across ViT and Llama-3-8B models has direct practical value for practitioners who accumulate task-specific LoRA adapters. The new joint-task benchmark (748-label union evaluation) is a meaningful step toward measuring whether merged models are truly general rather than merely preserving per-task skills, though it remains a proxy for open-ended generality. Open questions include whether the SVD alignment generalizes to other PEFT methods (adapters, QLoRA), to models from different pretrained checkpoints, and to settings where the number of tasks grows very large.
