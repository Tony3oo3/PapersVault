## Problem

Existing discrete diffusion models for Neural Probabilistic Optimization suffer from linear memory scaling with the number of diffusion steps, because the reverse-KL loss requires backpropagation through the entire generative trajectory. This limits the number of usable diffusion steps and hence model expressivity. Additionally, unbiased sampling (e.g., computing expectation values of physical observables) with approximate-likelihood models such as diffusion models on discrete domains had not been explored, leaving a gap for scientific applications in statistical physics.

## Input / Output

For combinatorial optimization, the model takes a QUBO problem instance encoded as a graph with an associated Q matrix and produces a binary solution vector X ∈ {0,1}^N; the GNN backbone conditions on the graph structure at every diffusion step. For unbiased sampling on the Ising model, the input is a 2D periodic lattice of size L×L with nearest-neighbor coupling J=1 and inverse temperature β, and the output is unbiased estimates of free energy, internal energy, and entropy computed via importance-weighted or MCMC-corrected samples. The diffusion model itself is a time-conditioned network (GNN for CO, U-Net for Ising) that outputs a product of Bernoulli distributions for each reverse step, with an exponential noise schedule.

## Method

The paper introduces two memory-efficient training objectives that decouple the number of diffusion steps from the memory budget. SDDS: rKL w/RL reformulates the reverse-KL objective as an episodic RL problem (via the policy gradient theorem) and optimizes it with PPO, so that gradients are computed on mini-batches of uniformly sampled diffusion time steps rather than the full trajectory. SDDS: fKL w/MC rewrites the forward-KL gradient using Self-Normalized Neural Importance Sampling over full diffusion paths and then applies a Monte Carlo estimate over the sum of per-step log-likelihoods, again avoiding full-trajectory backpropagation. For unbiased sampling, the authors extend SN-NIS and NMCMC to operate on joint diffusion-path likelihoods q_θ(X_{0:T}) = q(X_T)∏_t q_θ(X_{t-1}|X_t), which are exactly computable for diffusion models, enabling importance-weighted or Metropolis-Hastings-corrected estimates of target-distribution expectations without requiring the intractable marginal q_θ(X_0).

## Results

On UCO benchmarks, SDDS:rKLw/RL achieves the best average solution quality on 6 out of 7 problem instances, e.g. 19.62 independent set size on MIS RB-small (vs. 19.42 for DiffUCO and 19.18 for LTFT) and 18.89 clique size on MaxCl RB-small (vs. 17.40 for DiffUCO and 16.24 for LTFT). When sampling 150 solutions per instance, SDDS:fKLw/MC and SDDS:rKLw/RL jointly achieve the best best-of-N quality on 6 of 7 benchmarks due to fKL's mass-covering property. For unbiased sampling on a 24×24 Ising model at critical temperature, SDDS:fKLw/MC yields free energy −2.11209 (optimal: −2.11215), internal energy −1.4410 (optimal: −1.44025), and entropy 0.29573 (optimal: 0.29611), outperforming both the AR baseline from Nicoli et al. (2020) and a reimplementation under identical compute.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiffUCO (Sanokowski et al., 2024), the prior diffusion-based UCO method, SDDS:rKLw/RL improves average solution quality on 5 of 7 benchmarks (e.g. +0.20 on MIS RB-small, +1.49 on MaxCl RB-small) while using twice as many diffusion steps within the same memory budget; DiffUCO wins only on MaxCut BA-large (2974.60 vs. 2971.62). Against LTFT (Zhang et al., 2023), a GFlowNet-based autoregressive method, SDDS:rKLw/RL achieves substantially larger gains, e.g. +2.65 on MaxCl RB-small (18.89 vs. 16.24) and +27.93 on MaxCut BA-small (731.93 vs. 704). For unbiased Ising sampling, SDDS:fKLw/MC outperforms the AR baseline from Nicoli et al. (2020) on free energy (−2.11209 vs. −2.1128) and internal energy (−1.4410 vs. −1.43), and dramatically outperforms a same-architecture AR reimplementation (free energy −2.09344, internal energy −1.65420) under identical compute. The AR reimplementation's effective sample size per sample is 0.00006, versus 0.0102 for SDDS:fKLw/MC, indicating far better coverage of the target distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DiffUCO framework (Sanokowski et al., 2024) for discrete diffusion in unsupervised combinatorial optimization by replacing its memory-inefficient reverse-KL loss with two alternatives: a PPO-based policy gradient objective (rKL w/RL) and a self-normalized importance sampling objective (fKL w/MC), both of which decouple memory cost from the number of diffusion steps. It also extends the unbiased sampling toolkit of Nicoli et al. (2020)—originally designed for exact-likelihood autoregressive models—to approximate-likelihood diffusion models by reformulating SN-NIS and NMCMC over full diffusion paths. The RL formulation draws directly on PPO (Schulman et al., 2017), treating each reverse diffusion step as an MDP transition with a temperature-scaled log-likelihood ratio as reward.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling 2× more diffusion steps under the same memory budget and achieving SOTA on 6 of 7 UCO benchmarks, the paper makes discrete diffusion models practically competitive with autoregressive approaches for combinatorial optimization. More broadly, it is the first work to demonstrate unbiased sampling with diffusion models on discrete domains, opening a path for applying these expressive generative models to statistical physics and other scientific tasks previously restricted to exact-likelihood models. Open questions include whether the fKL objective's mass-covering advantage persists at larger system sizes, how the PPO hyperparameters scale with diffusion depth, and whether recent advances in discrete score matching (Lou et al., 2024) can further improve the SNIS-based training signal.
