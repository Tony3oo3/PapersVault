## Problem

Frontier LLMs are predominantly trained on English and lack fluency in Arabic, risking cultural homogenization and leaving the Arabic-speaking world underserved. The core technical challenge is adapting a strong English pretrained model to Arabic without catastrophic forgetting, compounded by poor Arabic tokenization in existing models, scarce high-quality Arabic pretraining data, and the difficulty of aligning knowledge across two typologically distinct languages.

## Input / Output

The model accepts multi-turn conversational prompts in either Arabic or English and generates autoregressive text responses in the corresponding language. The tokenizer was expanded from Llama-2's 32,000 tokens to 61,568 to handle Arabic morphology efficiently, reducing fertility scores and preserving effective context length. Outputs are structured as natural-language chat responses, with the model expected to maintain language consistency across conversation turns and adhere to cultural and ethical guardrails specific to the Arabic-speaking context.

## Method

ALLaM employs a two-stage pretraining recipe: (1) tokenizer augmentation by training an Arabic-specific BPE tokenizer and merging it with Llama-2's, followed by initializing new token embeddings as averages of their sub-token representations (rather than random initialization, which dramatically accelerates second-language acquisition); and (2) continued pretraining on 1.2T tokens at a 45/55 Arabic/English ratio, where 270B of the Arabic tokens are machine-translated from English to stabilize training and mitigate catastrophic forgetting. For from-scratch models, 4T English tokens precede the mixed phase. Alignment proceeds via supervised fine-tuning on the quality-filtered Ultra-Instinct dataset (~6M instruction-response pairs) followed by DPO preference optimization on 245K human-verified triplets, with rejected samples generated by varying temperature and nucleus sampling to improve robustness across decoding strategies.

## Results

ALLaM-70B achieves state-of-the-art Arabic performance, scoring 75.92 on MMLU Arabic (natural), 78.38 on Arabic Exams, and 56.8 on araTruthfulQA, outperforming all compared models on five of eight Arabic benchmark sets. On English, ALLaM-70B reaches 75.43 on MMLU and 84.97 on HellaSwag, trailing only Llama-3-Instruct-70B (78.38 MMLU) while substantially surpassing its Llama-2 base (61.15 MMLU). On MT-Bench Arabic, ALLaM-70B scores 8.19, the highest among all evaluated models, edging out GPT-3.5 Turbo (8.12) and Command-R+ (7.97). The 34B model achieves 81.00 on ACVA, the best score in that category across all model sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its Llama-2 base, ALLaM-70B improves MMLU Arabic from 40.77 to 75.92 (+35 points) and Arabic Exams from 30.6 to 78.38 (+48 points), demonstrating that continued pretraining with tokenizer expansion enables dramatic Arabic capability gains without English degradation (MMLU English rises from 61.15 to 75.43). Compared to Llama-3-Instruct-70B, ALLaM-70B leads on MMLU Arabic natural (75.92 vs. 36.27) and Arabic Exams (78.38 vs. 71.41), but trails on English MMLU (75.43 vs. 78.38) and MMLU-Pro (48.61 vs. 59.52). Against Jais-Chat-v3-30B, ALLaM-70B wins on MMLU Arabic natural (75.92 vs. 62.37), Exams (78.38 vs. 38.53), and araTruthfulQA (56.8 vs. 32.5), while Jais leads on araSwag (88.37 vs. 57.91). On MT-Bench Arabic, ALLaM-70B (8.19) outperforms Command-R+ (7.97) and GPT-3.5 Turbo (8.12), while on MT-Bench English it is competitive at 7.44 versus GPT-3.5's 7.55. The 13B model is particularly notable in human evaluation, where it wins pairwise comparisons against Jais-30B, Command-R+, and Command-R, and achieves the second-highest ELO score behind only GPT-4.

## Relation to Prior Work

ALLaM extends the second-language-acquisition paradigm for LLMs, building directly on Llama-2 weights and the DPO alignment framework. It departs from prior Arabic LLMs (Jais, AceGPT) by introducing a systematic tokenizer-augmentation and vocabulary-expansion recipe that enables continued pretraining in a new language without catastrophic forgetting, and by incorporating machine-translated parallel data into the pretraining mixture to explicitly align cross-lingual representations. The two-stage from-scratch recipe (English-only pretraining followed by mixed Arabic/English) draws on compute-optimal scaling principles from Hoffmann et al. and Touvron et al., while the SFT and preference-tuning pipeline follows the standard instruction-tuning + DPO paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ALLaM provides a concrete, reproducible recipe for adapting strong English LLMs to low-resource languages, which is practically important as most frontier models remain English-centric and risk cultural homogenization. The finding that translated data stabilizes training and mitigates catastrophic forgetting, and that halving SFT data with stricter quality filtering yields equivalent performance, offers actionable guidance for resource-constrained multilingual training. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond Arabic to other low-resource languages, how well the model handles dialectal Arabic (acknowledged as unaddressed), and whether the evaluation robustness concerns (multiple-choice sensitivity, MT-Bench leakage) limit the reliability of the reported SOTA claims.
