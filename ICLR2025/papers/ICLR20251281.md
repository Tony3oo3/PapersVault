## Problem

3DGS-based inverse rendering methods treat opacity as a standalone parameter independent of material properties, violating the Bouguer-Beer-Lambert law from radiative transfer which dictates that light attenuation depends on the material (cross section) of the absorbing medium. This disentangled representation leaves material properties under-constrained, as they receive gradients only from the color term in PBR rather than also from the alpha-blending term, leading to sub-optimal albedo, roughness, and relighting estimates.

## Input / Output

The method is a plug-and-play module that takes multi-view RGB images and inserts into existing 3DGS-based inverse rendering pipelines (GaussianShader, GS-IR, R3DG) without modifying their overall architecture. It produces per-Gaussian material properties (albedo, roughness, metalness/specular tint), surface normals, and a learned cross-section value, which together enable novel view synthesis, relighting under arbitrary lighting, and albedo/roughness map extraction. The cross-section network is a small MLP (2 hidden layers, 128 dimensions, ReLU activations, sigmoid output) whose input is the per-Gaussian material vector, optionally augmented with first-stage SH coefficients for two-stage pipelines.

## Method

The core contribution re-derives the per-Gaussian alpha value to strictly follow the Bouguer-Beer-Lambert law: each Gaussian blob is treated as an absorbing body where the number density n corresponds to the original opacity weighted by the 2D Gaussian distribution, the path length s is set to 1 (since splatting marginalizes depth), and the cross section σ is predicted by a neural network f(m) conditioned on material properties. The resulting alpha becomes α_i(x) = 1 − exp(−o_i · G_i(x) · f(m_i)), replacing the original linear opacity formulation. This causes material properties to receive gradients from both the PBR color term and the alpha-blending term, providing a physics-inspired regularization that was absent in prior 3DGS inverse rendering. A Taylor expansion analysis shows the original opacity is a first-order approximation of this formulation, and the derived form exactly matches NeRF's volume-rendering alpha, unifying the two representations.

## Results

On Synthetic4Relight, OMG (plugged into R3DG) improves novel view synthesis PSNR from 34.07 to 34.47 dB, relighting PSNR from 32.87 to 33.21 dB, albedo PSNR from 28.70 to 29.31 dB, and reduces roughness MSE from 0.011 to 0.007. On Shiny Blender and Glossy Synthetic (plugged into GaussianShader), PSNR improves by ~0.3 dB and ~0.4 dB respectively. On real-world MIP-NeRF 360 (plugged into GS-IR), average PSNR improves from 25.38 to 25.85 dB (~0.5 dB gain) with consistent SSIM and LPIPS improvements across most of the 8 scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OMG is evaluated as a plug-and-play module on three 3DGS-based inverse rendering baselines. Against R3DG on Synthetic4Relight, OMG wins on every reported metric: +0.4 dB PSNR on novel view synthesis, +0.34 dB on relighting, +0.61 dB on albedo, and a 36% reduction in roughness MSE (0.011→0.007). Against GaussianShader on Shiny Blender and Glossy Synthetic, OMG achieves +0.30 dB and +0.43 dB PSNR respectively, with consistent SSIM and LPIPS gains, and also produces visibly better normal estimation without any additional normal supervision. Against GS-IR on MIP-NeRF 360, OMG improves average PSNR by ~0.5 dB and LPIPS by ~0.011, though it slightly underperforms on SSIM and LPIPS for the Flowers scene (SSIM 0.510 vs 0.542; LPIPS 0.401 vs 0.368), likely due to the challenging geometry in that scene. Overall, the method delivers universal improvements across all three baselines and all four datasets, confirming the plug-and-play nature of the opacity reformulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3DGS-based inverse rendering methods (GaussianShader, GS-IR, R3DG) by identifying a missing physical constraint: opacity in the alpha-blending formulation should depend on material properties, as dictated by the Bouguer-Beer-Lambert law in radiative transfer. Prior 3DGS-based methods treat opacity as a standalone parameter decoupled from albedo, roughness, and metalness, whereas NeRF-based methods implicitly capture this dependency through shared MLP outputs. The authors introduce a plug-and-play neural network that maps material properties to a cross-section term, modifying the opacity computation to follow the exponential decay form from the Bouguer-Beer-Lambert law, and show via Taylor expansion that the original 3DGS opacity is a first-order approximation of this physically correct form.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that incorporating a physically grounded prior into the opacity computation yields consistent improvements in material estimation (albedo, roughness) and novel view synthesis across three independent baselines and four datasets, validating the importance of correct physical modeling in disentangled representations. The plug-and-play design makes the contribution broadly applicable to any 3DGS-based inverse rendering pipeline. However, the improvements are modest (~0.3–0.5 dB PSNR), and the frequency dependence of cross section is not modeled, leaving room for more complete radiative transfer modeling. The paper also highlights an open question about how to better regularize cross-section learning in specular regions with strong lighting.
