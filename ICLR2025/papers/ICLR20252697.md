## Problem

All existing 3D Gaussian Splatting compression methods require per-scene optimization (finetuning) to compress a given 3DGS, making the compression process slow (minutes per scene) and impractical for time-sensitive applications. No generalizable, optimization-free compression pipeline existed that could rapidly compress arbitrary 3DGS representations in a single feed-forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252697/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an existing 3DGS scene (~1M Gaussians, each with 56 attributes: 8 geometry parameters and 48 spherical-harmonic color coefficients, plus 3D coordinates) and produces a compressed bitstream. Gaussian coordinates are 16-bit quantized and losslessly encoded via GPCC, while attributes and binary masks are encoded with arithmetic coding. The decoded 3DGS preserves the exact number and spatial structure of Gaussians, so rendering speed remains unchanged. FCGS generalizes zero-shot to 3DGS from both optimization-based training and feed-forward reconstruction/generation models (MVSplat, LGM).

## Method

FCGS adopts an autoencoder-with-hyperprior architecture (inspired by image compression) but introduces a Multi-path Entropy Module (MEM) that routes each Gaussian's color attributes through either an MLP autoencoder (m=1, for redundancy elimination) or direct scalar quantization (m=0, for fidelity preservation), while geometry attributes always bypass the MLP since rasterization dependencies make them too deviation-sensitive. For probability estimation, FCGS combines three context sources into a Gaussian Mixture Model: a hyperprior branch, an inter-Gaussian context model that builds multi-resolution 3D/2D grids from previously decoded Gaussians and interpolates features at new positions, and an intra-Gaussian context model that autoregressively predicts distribution parameters from already-decoded channel chunks. The mask rate is integrated directly into the bit-consumption term of the rate-distortion loss rather than using a separate regularization hyperparameter. Trained end-to-end on 6770 DL3DV-GS scenes, FCGS achieves >20× compression in ~1 second per 100K Gaussians on a single GPU.

## Results

FCGS achieves a compression ratio exceeding 20× on DL3DV-GS, Mip-NeRF 360, and Tanks and Temples while maintaining high rendering fidelity, surpassing most per-scene optimization-based methods despite requiring no finetuning. Compression time is reduced from minutes (optimization-based) to approximately 1 second per 100K Gaussians on a single GPU. When combined with pruning (Mini-Splatting), FCGS reaches a 100× compression ratio over vanilla 3DGS, with the compressed file at the highest rate being only 40% the size of Mini-Splatting's MSC at equivalent fidelity. Rendering speed is preserved at 91 FPS post-compression vs. 102 FPS pre-compression on MipNeRF360 (λ=1e-4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FCGS is compared against five baselines across RD curves (PSNR vs. size in MB) on DL3DV-GS, Mip-NeRF 360, and Tanks and Temples. Against LightGaussian (Fan et al., 2024), FCGS achieves improved RD performance while requiring dramatically less execution time, as highlighted in Figure 1. Against train-from-scratch methods EAGLES (Girish et al., 2024) and Compact 3DGS (Lee et al., 2024), FCGS surpasses most of their RD curves despite lacking per-scene adaptation, which the authors note is an inherently unfair comparison. Against Mini-Splatting (Fang et al., 2024), FCGS at its highest compression rate produces files only 40% the size of Mini-Splatting's built-in MSC at the same fidelity level, and when stacked on top of Mini-Splatting's pruning, achieves a combined 100× ratio. Against Trimming (Ali et al., 2024), FCGS similarly boosts the compression performance of the pruned representation. All baselines require per-scene optimization (minutes to hours), whereas FCGS completes compression in a single feed-forward pass (seconds), making it the first generalizable optimization-free 3DGS compression pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

FCGS repurposes the variational image compression architecture (analysis/synthesis transforms, hyperprior, entropy coding from Ballé et al., 2018) for the 3DGS domain, adapting it to the unstructured, sparse nature of Gaussian primitives. It departs from all prior 3DGS compression methods—which fall into value-based (pruning, VQ) or structure-based (grid/anchor organization) principles and universally require per-scene finetuning—by introducing the first optimization-free, single-pass feed-forward pipeline. The key architectural innovations are the Multi-path Entropy Module, which routes geometry attributes directly (bypassing the autoencoder) while adaptively choosing between autoencoder and direct quantization for color attributes, and the inter-/intra-Gaussian context models that construct spatial grids from the Gaussians themselves to capture structural redundancy without altering their positions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: compression time drops from minutes (per-scene optimization) to seconds (single forward pass), making 3DGS compression viable for time-sensitive applications like streaming, mobile deployment, and server-side encoding at scale. The pipeline is source-agnostic, compressing 3DGS from both optimization-based training and feed-forward reconstruction models (MVSplat, LGM) in a zero-shot manner. However, the authors acknowledge that the absence of per-scene adaptation inherently limits RD performance compared to optimization-based methods, and the comparison is 'inherently unfair.' Open questions include whether the quality gap can be closed with better context modeling or larger training data, and whether the optimization-free paradigm can be extended to other explicit 3D representations beyond 3DGS.
