## Problem

Transfer-based adversarial attacks on skeletal action recognition (S-HAR) suffer from consistently low and unreliable transferability, with success rates highly dependent on the specific surrogate model chosen. The underlying cause of this failure remained unexplained, and no comprehensive benchmark existed to systematically evaluate robustness of S-HAR models against diverse attack methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252199/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean 60-frame skeletal action sequence and a frozen pre-trained surrogate (GCN or Transformer-based, e.g., ST-GCN, MS-G3D, CTR-GCN, SkateFormer) as input, and produces a perturbed skeleton sequence bounded by an l∞ norm of 0.01. The surrogate is augmented with a lightweight two-layer fully-connected MLP appended via a skip connection, with K=3 appended models and M=20 dual MCMC samples drawn at inference time. The attack is evaluated across 7 target models, 3 datasets (NTU-60, NTU-120, HDM05), and 2 defense models (BEAT, TRADES) within the newly constructed RobustBenchHAR benchmark.

## Method

TASAR identifies loss landscape smoothness as the key determinant of adversarial transferability in S-HAR and proposes a post-train Dual Bayesian strategy to smooth the surrogate's loss without retraining. A tiny Bayesian MLP is appended behind the frozen pre-trained surrogate via a skip connection, and a novel dual Bayesian optimization adds Gaussian noise around each sampled posterior to ensure uniformly low loss in the neighborhood, solved analytically via Taylor expansion and SGAHMC with SWAG for posterior approximation. Crucially, unlike prior transfer attacks that treat each frame independently, TASAR augments the position gradient with first-order (velocity) and second-order (acceleration) motion gradients computed via time-varying autoregressive (TV-AR) models, disrupting the spatial-temporal coherence that S-HAR models rely on. This combination of smoothed posterior sampling and temporal motion dynamics yields the first transfer-based attack on S-HAR with consistently high transferability across diverse surrogate and target architectures.

## Results

On NTU-60 and NTU-120, TASAR achieves the highest average untargeted transfer success rate of 35.5% across all surrogate-target model pairs, surpassing the SOTA S-HAR attack SMART by 23.4% and the SOTA transfer-based attack MIG by 8.1%. With MS-G3D as surrogate, TASAR reaches 41.11% average transfer success on NTU-60 and 39.65% on NTU-120, consistently outperforming all baselines across every target model. Against defense models (BEAT and TRADES) on HDM05 and NTU-60, TASAR also achieves the highest transfer success rates, e.g., 60.16% against BEAT-MS-G3D on HDM05 versus 3.34% for SMART.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TASAR consistently outperforms all eight baselines across every surrogate-target combination. Against the SOTA S-HAR white-box attacks adapted for transfer (SMART, CIASA), TASAR achieves 3–7× higher average transfer success rates (e.g., 38.82% vs. 6.39% for SMART with ST-GCN surrogate on NTU-60). Against the SOTA general transfer-based attacks, TASAR surpasses MIG (the strongest gradient-based baseline) by 8.1% on average and MI-FGSM by ~19%, while also outperforming the input-transformation method DIM and the ensemble-based SVRE. Compared to the Bayesian Attack (BA, Li et al. 2023), which re-trains the full surrogate as a BNN (255.57M params), TASAR achieves higher transfer success with only 0.012M additional parameters (3.54M total vs. 12.78M for the base surrogate), and a significantly flatter loss landscape. Against defense models, TASAR also leads: on HDM05 with BEAT defense, TASAR reaches 60.16% transfer success on MS-G3D versus 3.34% for SMART and 22.29% for MI-FGSM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bayesian adversarial attack line (Li et al., 2023) and the post-train Bayesian defense strategy (Wang et al., 2023, BEAT) into the S-HAR attack setting, but departs from both by avoiding full BNN re-training of the surrogate. Instead of re-parameterizing millions of weights, TASAR appends a lightweight two-layer MLP with a dual Bayesian optimization that smooths the posterior via Gaussian noise injection, drawing on SWAG and SGAHMC for efficient sampling. The temporal motion gradient component, modeled via time-varying autoregressive (TV-AR) processes, is a novel addition not present in prior transfer-based attacks (which treat frames independently) or in existing S-HAR white-box attacks (which use perceptual or GAN-based constraints for naturalness rather than transferability).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of loss landscape smoothness as the dominant predictor of adversarial transferability in S-HAR reframes how practitioners should select and design surrogate models, potentially outweighing the significance of specific gradient-based optimization tricks. The RobustBenchHAR benchmark (7 models, 10 attacks, 3 datasets, 2 defenses) fills a clear gap and will likely become the standard evaluation suite for future S-HAR robustness work. Open questions include whether the TV-AR motion modeling is optimal compared to learned motion manifolds, whether the approach extends to other sequential modalities (e.g., IMU, video), and whether the post-train dual Bayesian strategy can be applied to other domains where full BNN re-training is infeasible.
