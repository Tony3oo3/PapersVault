## Problem

Existing learning-based heuristics for combinatorial optimization are limited in their search capabilities, typically either iteratively improving a single solution or naively restarting and discarding previously generated solutions. This paper addresses the gap of effectively balancing exploration (generating diverse candidate solutions) and exploitation (refining promising partial solutions) within a unified neural framework for NP-hard graph optimization problems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253363/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected unweighted graph G=(V,E) and internally constructs a multilayer graph with K copies of G connected by cross-edges between corresponding nodes, with node features representing soft solution probabilities in [0,1]. The GNN outputs a K-coupled soft solution that is decoded into discrete feasible solutions via problem-specific rules (thresholding for Max Cut, greedy constraint-satisfying selection for Max Clique and Max Independent Set). At inference, the model generates C independent K-coupled solution groups and applies T-1 stochastic refinement iterations, yielding C×K×T total candidate solutions from which the best is selected.

## Method

X²GNN combines two mechanisms: (i) exploration via K-coupled solutions, where a multilayer graph with cross-edges enables a GNN to simultaneously generate K diverse solutions that share information through alternating GIN layers (operating on original edges) and GAT layers (operating on cross-edges); and (ii) exploitation via stochastic iterative refinement, where the previous iteration's output is randomly zeroed with probability φ before being fed back as input, controlling the exploration-exploitation trade-off. Training uses a two-stage procedure (construction, then one refinement step) with an unsupervised loss combining Lagrangian-relaxed objective, constraint satisfaction penalty, and a diversity loss that penalizes overlap between coupled solutions. The same model parameters are shared across construction and refinement, and the recurrent output-space iteration (rather than hidden-state iteration) avoids overthinking while enabling arbitrarily long inference-time refinement.

## Results

On Maximum Clique (RB1000), X2GNN achieves a 1.19% drop from optimality, while the best learning-based baseline (GFlow) has a 22.71% drop — a 19× improvement. On Maximum Independent Set (RB1000), X2GNN(1024×32) reaches 0.94% drop versus 10.06% for DiffUCO, and on ER750 it achieves 0.72% versus 3.55% for DiffUCO. On Maximum Cut (BA1000), X2GNN(256×32) matches the virtual best (0% drop) in 7.3s, outperforming Gurobi given 30 minutes (0.76% drop, 1800s). X2GNN also generalizes: a model trained only on RB250 still outperforms in-distribution learning-based methods on RB1000 and ER750.

## Baselines & Comparisons

X2GNN decisively outperforms all learning-based baselines across all three problems. On MC (RB1000), X2GNN achieves 1.19% drop versus 22.71% (GFlow), 24.97% (DiffUCO), and 37.44% (Erdos) — a 14–33× improvement over the best neural method. On MIS (RB1000), X2GNN reaches 0.94% drop versus 10.06% (DiffUCO), 13.28% (GFlow), and 25.22% (PPO). On MCut (BA1000), X2GNN matches the virtual best (0% drop) while ANYCSP achieves 0.02% and DiffUCO 0.27%. Against traditional solvers, X2GNN outperforms Gurobi on large instances (e.g., MC RB1000: 1.19% vs 10.88% at similar runtime; MCut BA1000: 0% vs 0.76% with 30-min budget) and is competitive with KaMIS, which remains slightly better on small MIS instances but is 50–190× slower on large ones. On the DIMACS MC benchmark, X2GNN (fine-tuned) achieves a 6.68% average gap versus 3.87% for KaMIS, and on the Coding Theory MIS dataset it finds best-known solutions on 20/32 instances (vs 28/32 for KaMIS) with a 3.37% average gap. On the SNAP MCut dataset, X2GNN achieves a 0.12% average gap from the best, outperforming ANYCSP (0.69%) and the BMZ heuristic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unsupervised learning-based combinatorial optimization line that includes single-step non-autoregressive methods (Erdos, GFlow), autoregressive MDP-based approaches (PPO), and diffusion-based solvers (DiffUSCO, T2T, DiffUCO). It specifically adopts the Lagrangian relaxation loss formulations from Sanokowski et al. (2024) but departs from all prior work by introducing K-coupled solutions via a multilayer graph with cross edges and a stochastic iterative refinement mechanism. The key conceptual departure is that existing methods either construct a single solution or naively restart, whereas X2GNN simultaneously generates and refines a small pool of coupled solutions, explicitly balancing exploration and exploitation through the perturbation parameter φ and the C/T tradeoff.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic gap over all learning-based baselines (up to 20× on large MC instances) combined with strong out-of-distribution generalization (trained on 250-node graphs, tested on 1000-node and different-distribution graphs) suggests that the K-coupling + stochastic refinement mechanism addresses a fundamental limitation in neural CO: insufficient search diversity. Open questions include whether the approach extends to problems with more complex constraint structures (e.g., TSP, scheduling), whether K=2 remains optimal at larger scales, and whether the technique can be integrated into hybrid solvers that combine neural heuristics with exact methods. The DIMACS results show the method still struggles on some adversarially hard instances, indicating room for improvement.
