## Problem

Multilingual LLMs lag significantly behind specialized NMT systems like NLLB on low-resource language translations, primarily due to scarce pre-training data in these languages. This performance gap persists despite growing efforts to support long-tail languages, and existing prompting or finetuning strategies do not adequately exploit the multilingual context that could help disambiguate and align semantics for under-resourced targets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252609/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an English source sentence, N auxiliary translations in closely related languages (selected via URIEL geographic and genealogical distance, arranged farthest-to-closest), and a draft translation in the target language as a postediting target. The auxiliary translations are generated by a teacher model (PaLM2 S) using 5-shot prompting in a first iteration. The output is a single corrected translation in the target language, with the prompt structured as an instruction listing all auxiliary languages, labeled 'Automatic' candidates, and a 'Corrected' target line for the model to complete.

## Method

Mufu (Multilingual Fused Learning) combines in-context learning with supervised finetuning by constructing prompts that include multiple auxiliary translations from closely related languages plus a postediting target, turning translation from scratch into a correction task. The model must assess input quality across the multilingual candidates, align semantics cross-lingually, copy from high-quality inputs, and override incorrect instances—inducing multi-step reasoning akin to chain-of-thought without explicitly predicting the reasoning chain. Student models (PaLM2 XXS/XS/S, Gemma 2B/7B) are finetuned jointly over 201 En-XX language pairs using only ~787 parallel sentences per pair against Mufu-style prompts with reference outputs. Auxiliary languages are selected per target using URIEL typological, geographical, and phylogenetic vectors, and the approach is robust to noisy auxiliary candidates. A knowledge distillation step (sequence-level, following Kim & Rush 2016) on the best Mufu models reduces inference cost to baseline level while retaining most of the accuracy gains.

## Results

On FLORES-200 devtest (201 En-XX language pairs), the best Mufu model (PaLM2 XXS-NTL finetuned with mufu20) achieves 48.4 mean chrF, outperforming the PaLM2 S teacher (43.3) and NLLB 1.3B distilled (46.0), and approaching NLLB 54B MoE (48.9). On NTREX (112 languages), the same model scores 48.8 chrF, maintaining a 61.6% win rate over NLLB 1.3B. In low- and very-low-resource languages specifically, Mufu models outperform NLLB 1.3B in 64% of language pairs and beat the teacher in 78–85% of pairs. Distilled Mufu models retain an average 3.1 chrF improvement over finetune-only baselines in low-resource translations while reducing inference cost to baseline level.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the PaLM2 S teacher model, the best Mufu model (PaLM2 XXS-NTL mufu20) wins in 54.2% of language pairs on FLORES-200 and 20.5% on NTREX, with the advantage concentrated in low- and very-low-resource languages (78–85% win rate). Against NLLB 1.3B distilled, PaLM2 XXS-NTL mufu20 achieves a 76.8% win rate on FLORES-200 and 61.6% on NTREX, with a 2.7 chrF average improvement; in low- and very-low-resource languages specifically, the win rate reaches 64%. Against the much larger NLLB 54B MoE, Mufu models are competitive in up to ~40% of translation pairs despite being an order of magnitude smaller. The Gemma 7B mufu20 model also outperforms NLLB 1.3B in 63.6% of pairs on FLORES-200, confirming that Mufu generalizes across model families. Distilled Mufu models (PaLM2 XXS-NTL and Gemma 7B) retain competitive performance against both baselines while reducing inference cost to the baseline level, though with a modest 3.1 chrF average gain over finetune-only in low-resource settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

Mufu directly extends the FIAT paradigm (Wang et al., 2023) of fusing in-context learning with supervised finetuning, but repurposes the in-context component from few-shot exemplars to multilingual auxiliary translations from closely related languages, combined with a postediting target. This departs from prior multilingual CoT work (Shi et al., 2023; Puduppully et al., 2023) that uses reasoning chains or decomposed chunks, and from dictionary-based prompting (Ghazvininejad et al., 2023) that requires bilingual resources unavailable for very-low-resource languages. The distillation component follows the standard sequence-level knowledge distillation recipe of Kim & Rush (2016), applied here to reduce the inference overhead of generating auxiliary translations at test time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a small LLM finetuned on only ~787 parallel examples per language pair can match or exceed a specialized NMT model (NLLB 1.3B) in the majority of low- and very-low-resource En-XX directions, offering a practical data-efficient alternative to large-scale NMT training. Key open questions include whether stronger auxiliary generators (e.g., NLLB 54B) would further close the gap, whether the approach extends to XX-En or non-English source directions, and how to handle the minimum quality threshold below which noisy auxiliary translations become harmful. The inference cost tradeoff (6× latency for mufu5) and the limited gains from distillation remain practical barriers to deployment.
