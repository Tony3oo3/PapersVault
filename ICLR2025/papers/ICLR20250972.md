## Problem

Fine-tuning pre-trained models causes catastrophic forgetting, degrading zero-shot generalization on other tasks, and merging fine-tuned checkpoints from disparate tasks introduces task interference that further reduces performance. Existing model merging methods treat all layers uniformly, overlooking the well-established insight that shallow layers encode general, transferable features while deeper layers specialize for specific tasks. This paper asks whether this layer-depth prior can be exploited post-training to simultaneously preserve generalization and retain task-specific knowledge.

## Input / Output

The method requires only the pre-trained checkpoint θ₀ and one or more fine-tuned checkpoints θ_t (no training data or gradient access needed). It computes the task vector τ = θ_t − θ₀, applies per-layer-block linear scaling to this residual, and outputs a single edited checkpoint θ = θ₀ + τ_LiNeS. For encoder-decoder architectures like T5, the scaling is applied exclusively to the decoder blocks. In multi-task merging, the input is a merged multi-task vector produced by an existing merging algorithm (Task Arithmetic, TIES, Consensus), and the output is the same merged vector with layer-wise rescaling applied before addition to the pre-trained weights.

## Method

LiNeS (Layer-increasing Network Scaling) rescales the task vector linearly by layer depth using λ^(ℓ) = α + β·(ℓ−1)/(L−1), so that the first layer is scaled by α and the last by α+β, with intermediate layers following a linear schedule. The key insight is that downscaling shallow-layer updates preserves the general features learned during pre-training (restoring zero-shot control-task accuracy) while deep layers retain task-specific adaptations (preserving target-task accuracy). For multi-task merging, α is set by a heuristic α = (1/N_models)·‖τ_sum‖/‖τ_MTL‖ to normalize across merging methods, and only β is tuned over the same search range as the baseline's constant scaling factor, introducing no additional computational cost. The method is plug-and-play and orthogonal to existing merging algorithms, and it generalizes to single-task editing, OOD generalization via WiSE-FT, Model Soups, and RLHF-aligned LLM policy merging (Rewarded Soups).

## Results

On the 8-task vision benchmark with CLIP ViT-B/32, LiNeS preserves 99.8% of fine-tuned target-task accuracy (90.3% vs. 90.5%) while restoring control-task accuracy from 38.0% to 48.0% (97.9% of pre-trained zero-shot performance). For multi-task merging on the 20-task benchmark with ViT-L/14, LiNeS improves Task Arithmetic by 3.1% (74.0→77.1), TIES-Merging by 4.0% (75.6→79.6), and Consensus Merging by 2.3% (78.7→81.0). In NLP with T5-large on 7 tasks, LiNeS boosts Task Arithmetic by 4.5 points (71.9→76.4). For Model Soups on ImageNet, LiNeS adds +0.48% over uniform soup and +0.15% over greedy soup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LiNeS consistently outperforms all six baselines it is compared against, with no setting where it degrades performance. Against Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2023), LiNeS adds +2.0 to +4.5 points across vision benchmarks and +2.6 to +4.5 points on NLP tasks. Against TIES-Merging (Yadav et al., 2023), gains range from +0.4 to +4.5 points in vision and +0.4 to +3.0 in NLP. Against Consensus Merging (Wang et al., 2024), improvements are +0.0 to +3.5 points, with the smallest gain (0.0) on the 11-task NLP benchmark. For Model Soups (Wortsman et al., 2022a), LiNeS improves uniform soup by +0.48% and greedy soup by +0.15% on ImageNet, surpassing both the best individual model (80.36%) and task-arithmetic-enhanced soups. Against WiSE-FT (Wortsman et al., 2022b), LiNeS produces a Pareto-dominating front across all five OOD datasets (ImageNet-Sketch, ImageNet-A, ImageNet-R, ObjectNet, ImageNet-V2). For Rewarded Soups (Ramé et al., 2024a) on LLaMA-2 7B, LiNeS Pareto-dominates the vanilla interpolation across the full preference space between two RLHF-aligned policies.

## Relation to Prior Work

LiNeS builds directly on the task vector formulation from Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2023) and the well-established observation that shallow layers encode general features while deeper layers specialize (Yosinski et al., 2014; Neyshabur et al., 2020). Prior work applied layer-wise learning rate decay during fine-tuning to preserve shallow-layer representations (Howard & Ruder, 2018; Dong et al., 2022), but LiNeS repurposes this insight as a post-training weight-space edit, requiring no retraining. It is positioned as orthogonal to and composable with existing multi-task merging methods (TIES-Merging, Consensus Merging) and weight interpolation (WiSE-FT), rather than replacing them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LiNeS offers a simple, zero-training-cost post-processing step that consistently improves a wide range of merging and fine-tuning pipelines across vision and NLP, making it an attractive plug-in for practitioners who already have fine-tuned checkpoints. The fact that a fixed linear schedule approximates the per-layer coefficients learned by Ada-merging and aTLAS without any gradient-based optimization is practically significant for scaling to large models. Open questions include whether non-linear or architecture-aware schedules could yield further gains, how the method behaves with very deep or heterogeneous architectures, and whether the heuristic for α in multi-task merging generalizes beyond the tested settings.
