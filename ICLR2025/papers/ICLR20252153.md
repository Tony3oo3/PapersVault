## Problem

Conditional Neural Fields (CNFs) encode each data sample as a single global latent vector that conditions a shared neural field backbone, but this geometry-free representation discards spatial structure such as locality and orientation. As a result, the latent space is poorly suited for downstream tasks requiring fine-grained geometric reasoning (classification, segmentation), and the architecture lacks the weight-sharing inductive bias that makes convolutional networks data-efficient. The paper asks: can we ground neural field latents in explicit geometry so that transformations in the field are mirrored in the latent, enabling both geometric interpretability and equivariant weight sharing?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a continuous signal field f: R^d → R^c (e.g., a 32×32 RGB image, a 3D occupancy grid, a 46×90 temperature map, or 256×256 satellite imagery) and produces a latent point cloud z = {(p_i, c_i)}_{i=1}^N, where each element pairs a pose p_i ∈ G (a group element encoding position and optionally orientation) with a context vector c_i ∈ R^d. The latent is decoded back to a field via the shared ENF backbone f_θ(x; z), or fed directly into an equivariant GNN (PΘNITA) for classification/segmentation. A key structural detail is that each latent point is spatially localized via a Gaussian window, so individual latents are responsible for local neighborhoods of the input domain rather than the entire field globally.

## Method

ENF replaces the single global conditioning vector of standard CNFs with a latent point cloud and imposes a steerability property (f_θ(g⁻¹x; z) = f_θ(x; gz)) by conditioning a cross-attention operator on bi-invariant attributes a(x, p_i) = p_i⁻¹x, which are the unique descriptors of coordinate-pose equivalence classes under the group G (e.g., R², SE(2), or SE(3)). The query is a linear projection of the Gaussian RFF embedding of a(x, p_i), the key is a linear projection of the context vector c_i, and the value uses FiLM modulation of c_i by the bi-invariant, giving a depth-wise separable convolutional form. Locality is enforced by adding a Gaussian spatial window μ_σ(x, p_i) = −σ_att‖p_i − x‖² to the attention logits, and computational cost is reduced via a k-nearest-neighbours truncation of the attention. Latents are obtained per sample through MAML (few inner-loop gradient steps) or autodecoding, and downstream tasks (classification, segmentation, forecasting) are performed by training a PΘNITA equivariant message-passing network on the latent point clouds, conditioned on the same bi-invariant attributes.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, ENF with translational equivariance (a^R2) achieves 82.1% classification accuracy, a 13.8-point improvement over Functa (68.3%), and 42.2 dB reconstruction PSNR vs. Functa's 38.1 dB. On OMBRIA flood segmentation, ENF reaches 74.00 IoU at full observation and maintains 71.58 IoU at only 10% observation, where the convolutional OmbriaNet baseline collapses to 0.0. For ERA5 climate forecasting, ENF reduces 1-hour prediction MSE from 3.45E-03 (Functa) to 9.44E-06, a ~365× improvement. On generative modelling, ENF achieves 23.5 FID on CIFAR-10 vs. Functa's 78.2, though dedicated generative frameworks (DPF: 15.1) still lead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ENF consistently outperforms Functa (Dupont et al., 2022) across nearly all tasks: +13.8 pp on CIFAR-10 classification (82.1% vs. 68.3%), +6.6 dB on CelebA reconstruction (34.6 vs. 28.0), +20.3 dB on ImageNet reconstruction (27.5 vs. 7.2), +31.25 IoU on OMBRIA segmentation at 100% observation (74.00 vs. 42.75), and a 365× reduction in ERA5 forecasting MSE (9.44E-06 vs. 3.45E-03). Against the modality-specific OmbriaNet (Drakonakis et al., 2022), ENF slightly wins at full observation (74.00 vs. 72.36 IoU) and dramatically outperforms it under data scarcity (71.58 vs. 0.0 IoU at 10% observation). On generative modelling, ENF (23.5 FID on CIFAR-10) marginally beats GEM (Dupont et al., 2021; 23.8 FID) but is outperformed by dedicated field-generative frameworks DPF (Zhuang et al., 2023; 15.1 FID) and GASP (Du et al., 2021; 13.5 FID on CelebA), which are trained directly on generative objectives rather than self-supervised pre-training. On ShapeNet-Part segmentation, ENF (82.2/75.4 mIoU) is competitive with but slightly below modality-specific baselines like PointNet++ (84.9/82.7), while substantially outperforming Functa (82.8/74.8) on class mIoU.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Conditional Neural Field (CNF) framework introduced by Functa (Dupont et al., 2022), which represents each data sample as a single global latent vector conditioning a shared neural field backbone. The key departure is replacing this geometry-free global latent with an attributed point cloud (poses + context vectors), imposing bi-invariance constraints derived from equivariant CNN theory (Cohen et al., 2019; Bekkers, 2019) to guarantee a steerability property. The cross-attention mechanism draws on PΘNITA's bi-invariant attributes for weight sharing (Bekkers et al., 2023) and on Gaussian spatial windowing from Cordonnier et al. (2019) to enforce locality, while the downstream analysis leverages equivariant graph neural networks (PΘNITA) to process the geometric latent representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ENFs address a fundamental limitation of CNFs—the loss of spatial structure in the latent space—by grounding representations in explicit geometry, which enables provable equivariance, weight sharing over spatially similar patterns, and unique editing capabilities (e.g., stitching latents from different samples). The dramatic improvement in ERA5 forecasting (~300× MSE reduction over Functa) and the 13.8-point CIFAR-10 classification gain demonstrate that geometric inductive biases are critical for fine-grained reasoning in continuous representations. Open questions include whether latent-specific spatial extents (σ) and noise on poses in diffusion training can further improve generative quality, and whether the approach scales to very large or unaligned datasets where the kNN approximation may become a bottleneck. The ShapeNet segmentation result, where ENF matches Functa due to global dataset alignment, highlights that the benefits are most pronounced when data lacks consistent spatial registration.
