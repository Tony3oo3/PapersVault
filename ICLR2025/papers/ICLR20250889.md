## Problem

TABPFN, the state-of-the-art transformer for tabular in-context learning, suffers from two bottlenecks on larger datasets: (1) quadratic time and linear space complexity in context size forces random subsampling of training data, discarding useful information, and (2) distribution shift between the handcrafted pretraining prior and real-world inference-time data degrades effectiveness. The paper demonstrates that PFN performance does not scale with dataset size and proposes a framework to close both gaps.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a tabular training dataset D_train (with numerical, ordinal, or categorical columns) and a set of test samples X_test. The training set is partitioned into K K-Means clusters, each forming a fixed-size prompt of B=3000 samples; test samples are routed to the nearest cluster center via ball-tree nearest-neighbor search. Output is a class label obtained by ensembling predictions across N_ensemble feature-order shufflings and power-law scalings, following TABPFN's column-invariance protocol. The routing step has O(1) memory and O(log N_train) time per test sample, while PFN inference is O(B² + B·N_batch) per batch.

## Method

MIXTUREPFN combines two components. First, MICP (Mixture of In-Context Prompters) replaces TABPFN*'s random global sampling with a Sparse Mixture of Experts-style routing: K-Means partitions D_train into K clusters, each cluster's B samples form a specialized 'prompter,' and a non-learnable router assigns each test point to its nearest cluster center, so each test sample attends only to its locally relevant context. The number of prompters is controlled by a single hyperparameter γ via K = ⌈γ·N_train/B⌉, trading efficiency for effectiveness. Second, CAPFN (Context-Aware PFN) addresses distribution shift by finetuning the frozen TABPFN transformer with lightweight adapter layers, trained via the PFN prior-data negative log-likelihood loss on bootstrapped prompts that simulate the MICP inference mechanism (for large datasets: sample a random point, retrieve its B nearest neighbors, split into sub-train/sub-test; for small datasets: 90/10 random split). This parameter-efficient finetuning aligns the pretrained handcrafted prior p(D|φ)p(φ) with the inference-time data prior p(D|D_train) without destroying the pretraining knowledge.

## Results

On the TABZILLA benchmark (36 tabular classification datasets, 19 baselines), MIXTUREPFN achieves the Condorcet winner with 524 pairwise votes, 19 wins, 0 ties, and 0 losses. Its mean rank is 2.350±1.824 across all 20 shared datasets and 2.273±1.710 across the 22 Top-10 shared datasets, substantially ahead of the next-best baselines (CatBoost at 4.900±4.158 and XGBoost at 5.500±4.621 mean rank). The Wilcoxon Signed-Rank test (p < 0.05) confirms MIXTUREPFN's superiority over all Top-10 baselines is statistically significant across 10 cross-validation splits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIXTUREPFN is the Condorcet winner, beating all 19 baselines in pairwise comparisons with zero losses. The closest competitors are GBDTs: XGBoost (500 votes, 1 loss to MIXTUREPFN) and CatBoost (474 votes, 2 losses), followed by TabPFN* (381 votes, 5 losses), which is the direct predecessor that randomly samples B=3000 training samples. Among deep learning baselines, SAINT (408 votes) and FTTransformer (294 votes) rank highest but still trail MIXTUREPFN by 116 and 230 Condorcet votes respectively. On the mean-rank metric, MIXTUREPFN's 2.350 (All) / 2.273 (Top-10) is roughly 2× better than the next-best (CatBoost 4.900 / 3.955), and its performance does not degrade with increasing dataset size, unlike TabPFN* whose rank worsens as N_train grows. Ablations confirm both components matter: removing CAPFN (finetuning) drops mean rank to 5.10, and removing MICP (routing) drops it to 5.30 (equivalent to TabPFN*). Against the recent retrieval-based LoCALPFN on three large datasets (Electricity, Phoneme, Airplane), MIXTUREPFN with 1024 experts achieves 89.7%/90.2%/85.7% accuracy versus LoCALPFN's 85.4%/88.7%/64.0%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends TABPFN (Hollmann et al., 2022), the state-of-the-art Prior-Fitted Network for tabular data, by combining it with the Sparse Mixture of Experts routing paradigm (Shazeer et al., 2017) in a novel way: rather than routing to different sub-networks, it routes test samples to different *contexts* (prompts) constructed from K-Means clusters of the training data. The bootstrapping-based finetuning (CAPFN) departs from standard PFN usage, which is purely zero-shot inference, by introducing a parameter-efficient adapter finetuning step that simulates the inference-time prompting distribution. The paper also positions itself against KNN-Prompting (Xu et al., 2023) and LoCALPFN (Thomas et al., 2025), showing that PFN-style batching constraints make naive KNN prompting suboptimal and that multiple specialized experts outperform a single shared one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that In-Context Learning via PFNs can now match or exceed GBDTs (XGBoost, CatBoost) on general tabular data at scale, addressing a long-standing gap where deep learning methods consistently underperformed tree-based models on medium-to-large datasets. The practical impact is significant: practitioners who value the probabilistic outputs and inductive bias of PFNs can now apply them to datasets with tens of thousands of samples. Key limitations remain: the method does not scale well with the number of features (due to TABPFN's column-permutation ensembling), cannot handle more than 10 classes with the current pretrained model, and has not been tested on billion-scale datasets. Open questions include whether the bootstrapping finetuning strategy generalizes to other ICL-based models and whether the K-Means routing can be replaced by learned routing for further gains.
