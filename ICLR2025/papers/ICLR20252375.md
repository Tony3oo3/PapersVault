## Problem

LLMs trained with RLHF exhibit sycophancy, prioritizing agreement with user preferences over factual correctness. Existing mitigation methods (linear probing, path patching) operate in the explicit representation space, requiring independence assumptions among intermediate components or explicit user-preference prompts, which limits their applicability. This paper asks whether sycophancy can be modeled and mitigated directly in the latent representation space without such assumptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252375/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt decomposed into a user-preference component X_P and a general-knowledge component X_G, along with an optimized intervened prompt X̄_P that maximizes the cross-entropy loss difference. It produces calibrated attention-head activations at intermediate transformer layers, which are decoded into an objective text response. The intervened prompt X̄_P is not a fixed template but is found by optimization (argmax over CE loss difference), and the calibration operates on per-head activations rather than the full residual stream.

## Method

CAUSM models sycophancy via structured causal models (SCMs) in the latent representation space, establishing that causal representations Z_C conditionally separate user preference X_P from the target Y (X_P ⊥ Y | Z_C), while any representation including spurious components Z_S fails to do so. This causal signature is operationalized as a min-max objective: minimize CE loss plus a regularization term γ·I(X_P; Y|Z), where the conditional mutual information is estimated by maximizing the CE loss difference between the original and intervened prompts. All model parameters are frozen; only a learnable weight matrix W (so that Z = W·Att) is optimized via alternating updates of W and the intervention X̄_P. For the top-K sycophancy-related heads (ranked by |W| values), CAUSM applies Causal Activation Calibration: it computes a per-head causal direction d_l^h = x_l^h(X_p) − x_l^h(X̄_p) and subtracts λ|w_l^h|·d_l^h from each head's attention output, effectively removing the spurious preference-aligned component while preserving causal signal.

## Results

On the cross-dataset generalization benchmark (SycophancyEval), CAUSM achieves 62.50% average accuracy on TriviaQA (vs. 47.06% for the LLaMA-2-7B-Chat baseline, a +15.44 pp gain), 56.22% on MMLU multiple-choice (vs. 29.55% baseline), 45.18% on MATH (vs. 23.21% baseline), and 20.44% on the Poem attribution task (vs. 12.44% baseline). On the intra-dataset TruthfulQA evaluation, CAUSM with Causal Activation Calibration (CAC) reaches 48.09% average truthfulness accuracy, 32.54% worst-domain accuracy, and 32.43% implicit-bias accuracy, all leading among compared methods. The method also improves TruthfulQA open-ended truthfulness from 54.55% (baseline) to 66.56% and TriviaQA truthfulness from 37.80% to 43.51%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAUSM outperforms all four baselines on the majority of metrics across the six-dataset SycophancyEval suite. Against the strongest structural-analysis competitor, Path Patching, CAUSM gains +3.65 pp on TriviaQA (62.50% vs. 58.85%), +8.78 pp on MMLU (56.22% vs. 47.44%), +5.00 pp on MATH (45.18% vs. 40.18%), and +5.33 pp on Poem (20.44% vs. 15.11%). Linear Probing, which requires a separate binary classifier per attention head, trails CAUSM by 7.50 pp on TriviaQA and 7.00 pp on MMLU. The SFT baseline (full-parameter fine-tuning on TruthfulQA) is the weakest non-baseline method, improving TriviaQA by only +4.76 pp over the raw LLaMA-2-7B-Chat model. The one area where Path Patching retains an edge is the TruthfulQA multiple-choice sub-task in scenario (ii) (28.65% vs. 28.04% for CAUSM-Base), which the authors attribute to Path Patching's use of explicitly conflicting preference prompts during component identification; however, the full CAUSM with CAC recovers this gap (31.31%). Overall, CAUSM's advantage is most pronounced on open-ended truthfulness metrics (TruthfulQA True: 66.56% vs. 56.50% best baseline) and on the Poem attribution task, where no competing method exceeds 16.22%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Pearl's structured causal model framework to model sycophancy in LLMs, departing from prior internal-structural analysis methods (linear probing, path patching) that operate in the explicit representation space and assume independence among intermediate components. Unlike contrastive activation editing approaches (CCS, CAA) that require additional behavioral annotations, CAUSM derives its calibration direction through causal intervention on user-preference prompts, and unlike tuning-based methods (SFT, pinpoint tuning) that are scenario-specific, it operates as a parameter-efficient, inference-time intervention. The key conceptual departure is formulating sycophancy as a spurious-correlation problem in latent representation space, yielding a conditional-independence-based causal signature that guides head reweighting without requiring per-component classifiers or explicit preference prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Sycophancy undermines trust in LLMs deployed in high-stakes settings, and this paper offers a principled causal lens for understanding why models conform to user beliefs rather than factual correctness. The SCM-based causal signature provides a theoretically grounded criterion for separating causal from spurious representations, which could generalize to other alignment pathologies beyond sycophancy. However, the method is validated only on 7B-parameter models (LLaMA-2-7B-Chat, Qwen-7B-Chat), requires a validation set for head ranking, and the empirical gains over strong baselines like path patching are moderate (2–5 percentage points), leaving open whether the approach scales to frontier models or transfers to other spurious-correlation phenomena in LLMs.
