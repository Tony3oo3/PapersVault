## Problem

Existing knowledge editing methods for LLMs face a fundamental editing-locality trade-off: parameter-based updates (ROME, MEMIT, AdaLoRA) affect all inputs indiscriminately, while the more recent representation-based ReFT applies a fixed linear subspace update to all selected representations. The paper proves that this subspace-level linearity inherently prevents ReFT from simultaneously achieving high editing reliability and preserving irrelevant knowledge, motivating a more fine-grained, input-dependent update mechanism.

## Input / Output

BaFT takes a textual knowledge pair (subject-relation prompt x, target object y) and intervenes on intermediate token representations at pre-specified positions (e.g., last 3 input/output tokens) and layers within a frozen LLM. The output is the modified model's generated text response, which should reflect the new knowledge while preserving all unrelated knowledge. The intervention operates on d-dimensional hidden states at selected positions, applying a rank-r (r=12) update in a learned subspace rather than modifying model parameters.

## Method

BaFT generalizes ReFT by replacing the fixed subspace update with a basis-level, input-dependent weighting: for each representation h, it computes a scalar weight w_k(h)∈[0,1] via logistic regression for each of r orthogonal basis vectors, then applies a linear update along each basis direction scaled by its weight. This makes the overall update non-linear and allows different knowledge types to activate different subsets of bases. Training combines three losses: a teacher-forcing cross-entropy on the target knowledge, an incremental load-balancing regularizer (inspired by sparse MoE) that prevents collapse to a fixed subspace, and an optional locality margin loss that pushes weights for irrelevant knowledge below a threshold while keeping editing-knowledge weights above another. The paper also provides a theoretical proof (Theorem 2.3) showing that any linear representation update satisfying generality must break locality for sufficiently fragile irrelevant representations, establishing the inherent limitation that BaFT's non-linearity overcomes.

## Results

On Single Editing (LLaMA 2-7b-chat), BaFT achieves average scores of 0.78 (WikiData_recent), 0.84 (WikiData_counterfact), 0.97 (WikiBio), and 1.00 (ConvSent reliability), consistently matching or exceeding ReFT while improving locality (e.g., 0.73 vs 0.71 on WikiData_recent Loc.). In Continual Editing on ZsRE at T=1000, BaFT maintains 0.98 locality versus 0.73 for ReFT and 0.00 for AdaLoRA/ROME, while using only 606K learnable parameters (vs. 6.3M for AdaLoRA and 45M for FT-L). BaFT achieves comparable average performance to WISE_light (0.74 vs 0.65 at T=1000 on LLaMA 2) with 10× fewer parameters and faster per-edit time (13.5s vs 58s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BaFT consistently outperforms all parameter-based baselines (AdaLoRA, ROME, MEMIT, FT-L) on locality and portability while matching their reliability. On LLaMA 2-7b-chat, BaFT achieves 0.73 locality on WikiData_recent versus 0.56 (AdaLoRA), 0.55 (ROME), and 0.52 (MEMIT), and reaches 1.00 ConvSent reliability versus 0.45–0.50 for all parameter-based methods. Against its direct predecessor ReFT, BaFT matches reliability (1.00 across benchmarks) while improving locality by 1–3 points (e.g., 0.81 vs 0.78 on WikiData_counterfact Loc.) and portability. In Continual Editing at T=1000, BaFT (Avg 0.74) substantially outperforms ReFT (0.62) and WISE_light (0.65) on LLaMA 2, while WISE_full (0.83) retains a small edge on reliability/generality but uses 10× more parameters and 4.3× more time per edit. BaFT also preserves downstream task performance (PIQA accuracy 0.77, identical to the unedited base model), whereas AdaLoRA drops to 0.48 and ROME to 0.50 after 100 edits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ReFT (Wu et al., 2024), which applies low-rank linear updates to a few token representations in a learned subspace, and extends it to the basis level by assigning input-dependent weights to each basis vector. It departs from the dominant parameter-based editing paradigm (ROME, MEMIT, FT-L, AdaLoRA) by arguing that their global effect on all inputs is a fundamental source of the editing-locality trade-off, and from external-memory methods (GRACE, MELO, WISE) by achieving comparable performance with 10–20× fewer parameters. The theoretical analysis (Theorem 2.3) formalizes why any linear subspace update, including ReFT, inherently cannot maintain both generality and locality simultaneously, positioning BaFT as the minimal non-linear extension that breaks this constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result that linear representation updates impose an irreducible editing-locality tension is broadly applicable beyond knowledge editing to continual learning and machine unlearning, giving those communities a formal reason to prefer input-dependent (non-linear) updates. Practically, BaFT's ~600K trainable parameters matching WISE's 5–9M parameters makes frequent knowledge updates far more affordable in deployment. Open questions remain: whether representation-based editing can capture all knowledge that parameter-based methods can, whether the approach scales beyond 7B models, and how to automate the selection of intervention positions and layers without manual hyperparameter tuning.
