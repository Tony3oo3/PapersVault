## Problem

Existing state-space models for long-sequence learning rely on restrictive parameterizations (HiPPO matrices, carefully constrained diagonal entries) to guarantee stable dynamics and the ability to learn long-range interactions, which heavily constrains the latent feature space and limits expressiveness. This paper asks whether a fundamentally different structural principle—harmonic oscillators—can yield stable, expressive state-space dynamics with far fewer parameterization constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250805/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multivariate time-series sequences of length up to ~50k timesteps (e.g., 6-channel physiological signals, 400–18k-step UEA classification sequences, 720-step weather records) as a time-varying input signal u(t) ∈ R^p. Depending on the task, it produces a class label (classification), a continuous scalar (heart-rate regression), or a future sequence of L2 timesteps (forecasting, via a masked sequence-to-sequence formulation where the last L2 input entries are zeroed and only the corresponding output entries are scored).

## Method

LinOSS models the hidden state as a system of forced linear second-order ODEs (uncoupled harmonic oscillators) with a nonnegative diagonal stiffness matrix A, discretized either fully implicitly (LinOSS-IM, dissipative) or via a symplectic implicit-explicit scheme (LinOSS-IMEX, volume-preserving and time-reversibility-symmetric). Both discretizations reduce to a linear recurrence x_n = M x_{n-1} + F_n whose 2×2 block-diagonal structure allows O(m) per-step updates, solved in O(log N) time via associative parallel scans. The paper proves that stability (spectral radius ≤ 1) and long-range learnability require only A_kk ≥ 0, in contrast to prior models needing HiPPO or other constrained structures, and further establishes universality: LinOSS can approximate any continuous causal operator between time-series to arbitrary accuracy. Each LinOSS layer is followed by a GELU activation and a GLU nonlinearity with a skip connection, stacked into multi-block architectures.

## Results

On the UEA long-range classification benchmark (6 datasets, sequences up to 17,984 timesteps), LinOSS-IM achieves 67.8% average test accuracy, outperforming the next-best Log-NCDE (64.3%) and S6 (62.0%). On the PPG-DaLiA very-long-sequence regression task (sequences of length ~50k), LinOSS-IM achieves 6.4×10⁻² MSE, outperforming Mamba (10.65×10⁻²) and LRU (12.17×10⁻²) by nearly a factor of 2. On the weather forecasting task (720-step prediction), LinOSS-IMEX achieves 0.508 MAE, beating S4 (0.578) and Informer (0.731).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LinOSS-IM achieves the best average accuracy (67.8%) across all six UEA datasets, outperforming Mamba (58.6%) by 9.2 points, LRU (61.7%) by 6.1 points, S5 (61.8%) by 6.0 points, and S6 (62.0%) by 5.8 points. On the two longest-sequence datasets (EigenWorms at 17,984 and MotorImagery at 3,000 timesteps), LinOSS-IM achieves state-of-the-art results of 95.0% and 60.0% respectively, beating the next-best LRU (87.8%) and Log-NCDE (53.7%). On the PPG-DaLiA regression task with ~50k-length sequences, LinOSS-IM (6.4×10⁻² MSE) outperforms Mamba (10.65×10⁻²) and LRU (12.17×10⁻²) by nearly 2×, and beats the previous best Log-NCDE (9.56×10⁻²) by 33%. On weather forecasting, LinOSS-IMEX (0.508 MAE) surpasses S4 (0.578), Informer (0.731), LSTnet (0.757), and all Transformer-based baselines. LinOSS-IMEX, the conservative variant, trails LinOSS-IM on dissipative tasks but excels on energy-conserving dynamics, where its error remains constant over time while LinOSS-IM's error grows (by a factor of >8 over 1000 steps on harmonic motion prediction).

## Relation to Prior Work

LinOSS extends the oscillatory RNN lineage (CoRNN, UniCoRNN) by recasting nonlinear oscillator dynamics as a linear state-space model with second-order ODEs, bridging the gap between the author's earlier nonlinear oscillator work and the modern SSM framework (S4, S5, Mamba, LRU). The key conceptual departure from prior SSMs is the use of forced harmonic oscillators (second-order ODEs) rather than first-order systems, which yields stability under the simple constraint of a nonnegative diagonal state matrix—avoiding the HiPPO or complex parameterizations required by S4/S5/Mamba. The universality proof directly builds on the fundamental lemma from Lanthaler et al. (2024) on neural oscillator universality, while the symplectic IMEX discretization introduces Hamiltonian structure and time-reversibility preservation as a novel theoretical lens for SSMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a physically grounded, theoretically clean alternative to SSMs with a remarkably simple stability condition (nonnegative diagonal A) and a useful dissipative-vs-conservative design axis (IM vs. IMEX) that practitioners can exploit depending on whether their target system is energy-conserving or dissipative. The ~2× MSE improvement over Mamba and LRU on 50k-length PPG sequences is the most striking empirical result, though it rests on a single dataset. Open questions include whether LinOSS scales to foundation-model regimes (language, genomics), how higher-order symplectic schemes (e.g., velocity Verlet) affect practical performance, and whether the oscillatory inductive bias helps or hurts on non-oscillatory data. The connection to computational neuroscience (cortical oscillations) suggests potential cross-pollination, though the paper explicitly defers this to future work.
