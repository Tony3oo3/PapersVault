## Problem

Existing Gaussian-splatting-based head avatar methods rely on similarity transformations (rigid rotation + isotropic scaling) for mesh-bound deformation, which cannot represent the stretch and shear that occur in extreme facial expressions, leading to over/under-occupying artifacts and geometric distortion. No prior method within the Gaussian Splatting framework achieves geometrically accurate, riggable head avatars with well-defined depth and normals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250282/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single tracked RGB video (monocular, e.g., FaceTalk at 512×512, or multi-view, e.g., NeRSemble with 16 cameras) as input and produces a fully controllable riggable head avatar represented by 2D Gaussian surfels bound to a FLAME parametric mesh. Outputs include photorealistic rendered images, high-fidelity surface normals, depth maps via ray-surfel intersection, and extractable 3D meshes (via TSDF fusion), all supporting arbitrary pose, expression, and viewpoint control at real-time rendering speeds (~90 FPS).

## Method

SurFhead replaces the similarity transformation used in GaussianAvatars with a full affine Jacobian deformation gradient (J = ẼE⁻¹) applied to 2D surfel covariance, enabling accurate stretch and shear representation. Normals are deformed via the inverse-transpose of the Jacobian (n_d = J⁻ᵀ n_c), derived from the orthogonality constraint between tangents and normals. To resolve discontinuities at triangle boundaries, the proposed Jacobian Blend Skinning (JBS) applies polar decomposition to split each Jacobian into rotation (U) and stretch/shear (P) components, blending rotations in matrix-logarithm space (SO(3)) and stretch/shear in linear matrix space, with learnable convex weights. For the cornea, the method regularizes opacity to prevent concave 'hollow-illusion' geometry and employs Anisotropic Spherical Gaussians (ASGs) with a compact frontal-hemisphere sampling to capture sharp specular reflections that Spherical Harmonics cannot represent.

## Results

On the NeRSemble real multi-view dataset, SurFhead achieves the best Normal Cosine Similarity (NCS) across both tasks: 0.896 for novel-view synthesis and 0.832 for self-reenactment, substantially outperforming the closest competitor GaussianAvatars (0.797 and 0.716 respectively). On the FaceTalk synthetic dataset with ground-truth normals, SurFhead reaches PSNR 40.15, SSIM 0.992, LPIPS 0.020, and NCS 0.983, surpassing the best baseline (PointAvatar) by over 5 dB in PSNR and 0.22 in NCS. In rendering quality (PSNR) on NeRSemble NVS, GaussianAvatars edges out SurFhead slightly (30.29 vs. 30.07), but SurFhead with LPIPS loss recovers the best LPIPS (0.062) while maintaining competitive PSNR (29.94).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SurFhead decisively outperforms all baselines on the geometry-fidelity metric (NCS) by large margins: on NeRSemble self-reenactment, it achieves NCS 0.832 vs. GaussianAvatars 0.716 (+0.116), SplattingAvatars 0.688, FLARE 0.737, and PointAvatar 0.611. On FaceTalk (synthetic, with GT normals), SurFhead dominates all four metrics, reaching PSNR 40.15 vs. PointAvatar 34.93, IMAvatar 32.23, and FLARE 31.64, with NCS 0.983 vs. FLARE 0.943 and IMAvatar 0.931. The closest competitor is GaussianAvatars, which slightly wins PSNR on NeRSemble NVS (30.29 vs. 30.07) due to 3DGS's higher representational capacity, but SurFhead surpasses it on NCS (0.896 vs. 0.797) and LPIPS (0.079 vs. 0.067, or 0.062 with LPIPS loss), and the paper notes SurFhead is more robust on extreme expressions. SplattingAvatars and PointAvatar suffer from representation-level limitations (drifting Gaussians and inflexible point sizes respectively), yielding substantially lower NCS. In the appendix comparison with GHA (Xu et al., 2024), GHA wins PSNR (27.25 vs. 26.20) thanks to a screen-space super-resolution module, but SurFhead wins SSIM (0.932 vs. 0.909), LPIPS (0.052 vs. 0.153), and NCS (0.837 vs. 0.505), with GHA exhibiting over-saturation artifacts on extreme poses.

## Relation to Prior Work

SurFhead sits at the intersection of 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) for geometrically well-defined surfels and the mesh-binding rigging paradigm established by GaussianAvatars and SplattingAvatar. Its key departure is replacing the similarity transformation (rotation + isotropic scaling) used by all prior Gaussian-based avatar methods with a full affine Jacobian deformation gradient, inherited from classical deformation transfer (Sumner & Popović, 2004), and introducing Jacobian Blend Skinning (JBS) that uses polar decomposition to interpolate adjacent-triangle deformations in geometrically valid spaces rather than naive element-wise matrix blending. The corneal specularity modeling via Anisotropic Spherical Gaussians (Xu et al., 2013) is borrowed from relighting literature but repurposed here to resolve a geometry-appearance conflict unique to the Gaussian splatting regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving state-of-the-art normal cosine similarity (NCS 0.896 vs. 0.797 for GaussianAvatars on NeRSemble) while maintaining competitive rendering quality, SurFhead demonstrates that geometrically accurate and photorealistic head avatars are not mutually exclusive within the Gaussian Splatting framework, opening the door to downstream tasks like relighting and dynamic mesh extraction that require reliable normals and depth. The JBS algorithm is a general-purpose technique for blending affine deformations that could transfer to other mesh-rigged primitive systems. Open questions include the bounded expression space of FLAME (which limits extreme expressions), the absence of hair and tongue in 3DMFM, and the computational cost of per-Gaussian polar decomposition at scale, which the authors acknowledge as a bottleneck for real-time deployment.
