## Problem

Pathology-specific vision-language models are constrained by the limited scale and quality of available image-text pairs: existing datasets from PubMed, YouTube, and Twitter do not exceed one million samples, suffer from compression artifacts and image-text misalignment, and are inherently unscalable. Meanwhile, large WSI repositories like TCGA contain high-resolution tissue images with rich morphological detail but lack patch-level textual annotations, leaving this data untapped for contrastive vision-language pretraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253413/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes ~7,500 TCGA whole-slide images (cropped to 672×672 patches at highest magnification) along with their clinical reports and tissue-origin metadata, and produces 1.6 million image-caption pairs where each caption is constrained to ≤77 tokens for CLIP compatibility. A derivative 200K instruction-tuning set (95K multiple-choice QAs + 105K multi-round dialogues) is also generated for LMM training. The final outputs feed two downstream models: PathGen-CLIP (contrastive encoder producing 512/768-dim embeddings for zero-shot/few-shot/WSI classification) and PathGen-LLaVA (a 13B LMM producing free-text pathology descriptions and answers).

## Method

The core contribution is a five-step multi-agent pipeline that converts unannotated WSIs into high-quality image-text pairs. First, a pathology-specific CLIP (PathGen-CLIP-L_init, trained on 700K curated pairs) performs prompt-based retrieval using cleaned WSI reports and GPT-4-generated tissue-attribute prompts, combined with k-means clustering on patch embeddings to yield 384 representative patches per WSI. Second, a trained pathology LMM (PathGen-LLaVA_desp, built on LLaVA-v1.5-13B with the pathology CLIP as vision encoder and fine-tuned on 30K GPT-4V-enhanced captions) generates detailed morphological descriptions. Third, a Revise LMM Agent—trained on GPT-4-synthesized (image, erroneous caption, correction operation) triplets—corrects hallucinations and errors. Fourth, a Llama-2-based summarizer compresses descriptions to fit the 77-token CLIP limit. Finally, PathGen-CLIP is trained in two stages: first on PathGen-1.6M to learn morphological features, then on PathGen_init to acquire diagnostic comprehension, avoiding the performance degradation observed when morphological and diagnostic data are mixed at equal scale.

## Results

PathGen-CLIP-L achieves 79.7% average zero-shot accuracy across nine pathology image classification datasets, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art CONCH (68.7%) by 11.0 percentage points. On WSI classification with ACMIL, PathGen-CLIP-L reaches 92.6% average AUC, surpassing CONCH (91.1%) and the much larger vision-only GigaPath-G (90.9%). For pathology LMM evaluation on PathMMU, PathGen-LLaVA achieves 58.4% overall accuracy, exceeding GPT-4V (49.8%) by 8.6 points and the previous best pathology LMM Quilt-LLaVA (41.5%) by 16.9 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PathGen-CLIP-L decisively outperforms all eight pathology-specific CLIP baselines on average zero-shot accuracy: CONCH (68.7%), PathCLIP (66.2%), QuiltNet (58.9%), PLIP (58.6%), BiomedCLIP (52.9%), OpenAI-CLIP-L (51.3%), PubMedCLIP (39.8%), and PMC-CLIP (34.0%). The largest individual margins are on Pcam (+10.5% over CONCH), BACH (+13.2% over CONCH), and SICAPv2 (+30.5% over CONCH), indicating particularly strong gains on harder classification tasks. On WSI classification (Table 3), PathGen-CLIP-L with ACMIL achieves 92.6% average AUC, beating CONCH (91.1%) and the 4×-larger vision-only GigaPath-G (90.9%), confirming that the vision-language pretraining on PathGen-1.6M is more data-efficient than pure vision pretraining. For the LMM comparison on PathMMU, PathGen-LLaVA (58.4%) surpasses GPT-4V (49.8%), Gemini Pro Vision (42.7%), Qwen-VL-Max (45.9%), and both pathology-specific LMMs Quilt-LLaVA (41.5%) and LLaVA-Med (26.2%). The only notable exception is on WSSSLUAD zero-shot, where PathCLIP (85.1%) slightly edges out PathGen-CLIP-L (82.2%), though PathGen-CLIP-L still leads on the other eight datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP contrastive learning paradigm (Radford et al., 2021) and the LLaVA visual instruction tuning framework (Liu et al., 2024) to the pathology domain, but its core departure is in data generation: rather than scraping image-text pairs from PubMed, Twitter, or YouTube (as in PathCap, OpenPath, Quilt-1M), it uses a cascaded multi-agent pipeline—CLIP-based patch retrieval, LMM description generation, LMM revision, and LLM summarization—to synthesize captions from TCGA whole-slide images. The two-stage CLIP training strategy (morphological features first via PathGen-1.6M, then diagnostic comprehension via PathGen_init) is a novel training recipe not present in prior pathology CLIP work. The approach also repurposes GPT-4 as a data-augmentation tool for constructing revision training triplets and instruction-tuning samples, positioning LLMs as infrastructure for dataset construction rather than end-task solvers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PathGen-1.6M addresses the fundamental data bottleneck in pathology VLMs by providing a scalable, high-quality generation pipeline that can be extended to additional TCGA WSIs without manual annotation, and the resulting PathGen-CLIP-L outperforms CONCH (trained on proprietary data) and even the much larger vision-only GigaPath-G on WSI classification, suggesting that language supervision at moderate scale can rival massive vision-only pretraining. A key open question is whether the morphological (non-diagnostic) nature of the generated captions—imposed by LLM value alignment—limits the model's diagnostic reasoning, and whether the two-stage training recipe generalizes beyond the specific CLIP architecture. The pipeline's reliance on GPT-4 for multiple steps (report cleaning, prompt generation, revision data construction, instruction-tuning data) introduces a dependency on a single proprietary model, raising reproducibility and cost concerns for the broader community.
