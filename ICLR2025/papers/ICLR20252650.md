## Problem

Tuning step-size and momentum parameters for Stochastic Heavy Ball (SHB) is prohibitively expensive in practice, and while Polyak-type adaptive step-sizes have proven effective for SGD, naively applying them to SHB leads to divergence for typical momentum values (e.g., β=0.9). This paper addresses the missing convergence theory and practical instability by proposing momentum-aware Polyak step-sizes with rigorous guarantees in the non-interpolated stochastic setting.

## Input / Output

The method requires a finite-sum objective with convex, smooth component functions, a user-chosen momentum parameter β ∈ [0,1), and lower bounds ℓ*_S_t on each mini-batch loss (settable to 0 in practice). It produces the optimized parameter vector x* and, at each iteration, an adaptive step-size γ_t computed from the current function value, gradient norm, and accumulated loss history—requiring no knowledge of smoothness constants, strong convexity parameters, or other problem-specific quantities. The decreasing variants additionally assume bounded iterates (D² < ∞) for their convergence guarantees.

## Method

The core insight is leveraging the Iterate Moving Average (IMA) reformulation of SHB, which reveals that the SHB step-size must be scaled by a correcting factor (1−β) relative to the corresponding SGD Polyak step-size to maintain stability. This yields three variants: MomSPS_max (non-decreasing, converging to a neighborhood at O(1/T)), MomDecSPS (decreasing, achieving O(1/√T) convergence to the exact solution for any β ∈ [0,1)), and MomAdaSPS (robust decreasing, automatically adapting between O(1/T) under interpolation and O(1/√T) otherwise). The (1−β) factor is placed outside the minimum for MomSPS_max but inside the first term for the decreasing variants, a distinction that follows from the IMA derivation and is critical for preventing divergence at large β. All three reduce to their SGD counterparts (SPS_max, DecSPS, AdaSPS) when β=0, and the convergence analysis is tight, recovering the best-known SGD Polyak rates as special cases.

## Results

The paper provides the first convergence guarantees for adaptive SHB with Polyak-type step-sizes in the non-interpolated convex setting. MomSPS_max achieves O(1/T + σ²) convergence to a neighborhood, MomDecSPS achieves O(1/√T) convergence to the exact solution, and MomAdaSPS achieves the robust rate O(1/T + σ/√T) that automatically adapts to whether interpolation holds. Empirically, MomSPS_max significantly outperforms SPS_max with naive momentum (which diverges for β ≥ 0.5 on logistic regression) and outperforms constant-step-size SHB, ADAM, and ALR-SMAG on LIBSVM datasets in both training loss and accuracy. On CIFAR-10/100 with ResNet-34, MomSPS_max consistently outperforms its no-momentum counterpart SPS_max and achieves competitive generalization compared to standard optimizers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SPS_max with naive momentum (the most direct comparison), MomSPS_max is strictly more stable: the naive rule diverges for β = 0.5 on a simple logistic regression problem, while MomSPS_max converges for all tested β ∈ {0, 0.2, 0.5, 0.9}. Against constant-step-size SHB (Liu et al., 2020), Corollary 3.5 allows a larger step-size γ ≤ (1−β)/(2L_max) versus Liu et al.'s γ ≤ (1−β)²/L · min{1/(4−β+β²), 1/(2√(2β+2β²))}, which is strictly larger for β ≥ √5−2 ≈ 0.236, and does not require the bounded variance assumption. Against ALR-SMAG (Wang et al., 2023), the proposed methods target the standard SHB update rather than a moving-averaged-gradient variant, and when β = 0 the guarantees reduce exactly to the original SPS_max/DecSPS/AdaSPS rates, confirming tightness. Against IMA (Sebbouh et al., 2021), MomDecSPS achieves O(1/√T) versus O(log T/√T) and does not require knowledge of L_max or a specific β_t schedule. In the deterministic least-squares experiment, MomPS_max (Corollary 3.3) matches the performance of AGM (Barré et al., 2020) and ALR-MAG (Wang et al., 2023), both of which are faster than vanilla Polyak step-size after initial iterations, while the step-size converges from below to the optimal HB value. For the decreasing variants, MomDecSPS and MomAdaSPS outperform their no-momentum counterparts (SGD with DecSPS and AdaSPS) in both training loss and test accuracy on LIBSVM datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of Polyak-type adaptive step-sizes (SPS_max, DecSPS, AdaSPS) from SGD to the Stochastic Heavy Ball (SHB) method by leveraging the Iterate Moving Average (IMA) equivalence from Sebbouh et al. (2021). The key insight is that naively applying SPS_max to SHB diverges for typical momentum values, and the fix is a simple (1−β) scaling factor derived from the IMA parameter mapping. Prior adaptive SHB work either targets moving-averaged gradient momentum (Wang et al., 2023), assumes interpolation (Schaipp et al., 2023b), or requires knowledge of the smoothness constant (Sebbouh et al., 2021); this paper is the first to provide Polyak-type adaptive step-sizes for SHB in the fully stochastic, non-interpolated regime with no a priori problem parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: practitioners using momentum-based optimizers (the default in most deep learning pipelines) can now use fully adaptive step-sizes without tuning, with convergence guarantees to the exact solution (MomDecSPS, MomAdaSPS) or to a neighborhood (MomSPS_max). MomAdaSPS is particularly notable as the first 'robust' adaptive step-size for SHB that automatically adapts to whether the problem is interpolated or not. Limitations include the bounded-iterates assumption for the decreasing variants, the restriction on β for MomSPS_max (though empirically not binding), and the analysis being confined to convex objectives. Open questions include extending these guarantees to non-convex settings (where the DNN experiments suggest promise but lack theory) and removing the bounded-iterates assumption.
