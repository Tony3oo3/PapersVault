## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods for Large Multimodal Models require a substantial number of tunable parameters yet still trail full fine-tuning in performance, and the parameters they introduce are abstract and difficult to interpret or control. The paper asks how to simultaneously achieve near-full-fine-tuning performance with extreme parameter efficiency and how to make the fine-tuning process interpretable and controllable at the representation level.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253204/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a free-form text instruction as input. The image is encoded by a frozen CLIP-L vision encoder (24 Transformer layers) into a fixed-length sequence of visual patch tokens, and the text is tokenized into a sequence of textual tokens; these are concatenated into a joint multimodal representation consumed by a frozen Vicuna-7B LLM that produces a natural-language response. In the controllability experiments, the input is constrained to a fixed question template (e.g., 'Is the object an [indicator] in the image?') paired with a CIFAR-10 image, and the output is a binary Yes/No classification answer, enabling precise counterfactual control over the predicted class.

## Method

MRT introduces a lightweight representation editor ψ(x) = x + Uᵀ(Wx + b − Ux) that projects the hidden state into a low-rank subspace spanned by an orthonormal matrix U, applies a learnable linear transformation Wx + b within that subspace, and projects the residual intervention back into the full space, leaving all orthogonal components untouched. This editor is instantiated at three decoupled levels: (i) every layer of the CLIP-L vision encoder to edit visual representations, (ii) the output of the cross-modality projector to refine visual-to-textual alignment, and (iii) the prefix and suffix segments of textual tokens at every layer of the Vicuna-7B LLM to edit multimodal representations. During instruction tuning on Vision-Flan, the entire LLaVA backbone remains completely frozen and only these editors (totaling 0.03% of model parameters) are optimized, yet the method reaches 99.56% of full fine-tuning performance on MME (1580.40 vs. 1587.26) and surpasses all PEFT baselines including MixLoRA and M²PT. Because the edits operate in interpretable linear subspaces, the same mechanism enables token-level controllability: training a single editor on a targeted class indicator token or visual RoI patch can flip the model's classification output with 100% success rate while leaving all other classes unaffected.

## Results

MRT achieves a MME score of 1580.40 and MMAvg of 64.93 using only 0.03% tunable parameters, reaching 99.56% of full fine-tuning LLaVA performance (1587.26 MME) while using 65× fewer parameters than the next most efficient PEFT method. MRT surpasses the strongest PEFT baselines MixLoRA (1509.61) and M²PT (1503.98) by 4.70% and 5.08% on MME, respectively. Notably, MRT also exceeds full fine-tuning on Text-VQA (40.62 vs. 37.26), CIFAR-10 (96.96 vs. 92.97), and MNIST (95.63 vs. 94.27). On the controllability experiments, MRT achieves 100% counterfactual control rate for both misclassification and misalignment scenarios on CIFAR-10 classes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MRT outperforms all seven PEFT baselines on MME, with the largest margins over PTUM (+16.6%), LoRA (+13.1%), and VPT (+12.7%). Against the two strongest PEFT competitors, MRT beats MixLoRA by 70.79 points (1580.40 vs. 1509.61) and M²PT by 76.42 points (1580.40 vs. 1503.98) on MME, while using 28× and 65× fewer parameters, respectively. Compared to ReFT (the closest representation-tuning baseline at the same 0.03% parameter budget), MRT gains 107.15 MME points, demonstrating the benefit of extending representation editing to visual and cross-modality components beyond text-only editing. MRT also surpasses full fine-tuning LLaVA on Text-VQA (+3.36), CIFAR-10 (+3.99), and MNIST (+1.36), though it trails on VSR (−2.29), SNLI-VE (−10.01), and CIFAR-100 (−6.53). On MMAvg, MRT (64.93) narrows the gap to full fine-tuning (66.59) to within 1.66 points, the closest among all PEFT methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

MRT extends the representation engineering line of work (ReFT, RED, activation addition) from single-modality NLP to the multimodal setting, applying low-rank subspace interventions simultaneously to visual encoder layers, the cross-modality projector, and textual prefix/suffix tokens within the LLM. It departs from mainstream multimodal PEFT (LoRA, MixLoRA, M2PT, VPT) which modify attention parameters or insert prompt tokens in a black-box fashion, instead directly editing semantically meaningful internal representations. The method is grounded in the linear representation hypothesis and causal interchange interventions (Geiger et al., 2021), but is the first to structure these interventions across multiple modalities and the cross-modality alignment layer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MRT demonstrates that 0.03% of parameters (roughly 21× fewer than LoRA) can recover 99.56% of full fine-tuning performance on MME, offering a practical, low-overhead alternative for adapting large multimodal models on resource-constrained hardware. The token-level controllability experiments—achieving 100% counterfactual control by editing a handful of visual and textual tokens—open a new axis of multimodal interpretability that prior PEFT methods cannot provide. Key limitations include the manual rank hyperparameter, weak performance on logical-reasoning tasks (SNLI-VE), and controllability demonstrated only on simple classification/VQA templates; open questions include automating rank selection, extending control to compositional or multi-step reasoning, and understanding the security implications of representation-level manipulation.
