## Problem

Existing automated prompt optimization methods (APE, APO, PE2, TextGrad) rely exclusively on large, expensive proprietary LLMs like GPT-4 to generate textual feedback for refining prompts of smaller models, since smaller models cannot produce reliable optimization judgments on their own. Moreover, these text-space methods discard finer-grained numerical signals such as loss gradients, making them less direct and more computationally costly than necessary.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250318/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

GREATER takes a small representative task dataset (e.g., 50 train / 100 dev / 100 test splits from GSM8K, BBH, or FOLIO), an initial prompt string, and an open-source language model (Llama-3-8B-Instruct or Gemma-2-9B-it) as input, and produces a single optimized discrete text prompt. The prompt is optimized position-by-position over a fixed or dynamically extended token sequence, with each position restricted to a small set of top-k candidate tokens proposed by the model's own forward probabilities, keeping the search space tractable and the resulting prompt interpretable.

## Method

GREATER introduces 'gradient over reasoning': for each prompt token position, it (1) proposes top-k candidate tokens using the LM's conditional probabilities over task inputs, (2) generates a full reasoning chain r ~ f_LLM(x ⊙ p) with the current prompt, (3) appends a formatted extractor prompt to obtain final-answer logits, and (4) computes a cross-entropy loss (plus a perplexity regularization term) and backpropagates through the entire reasoning chain to obtain gradients with respect to a one-hot token indicator over the candidates. The token with the most negative gradient is selected (with a top-3 forward-pass verification for robustness), and the process sweeps sequentially over all positions for T=105 iterations. Because the gradient flows through the generated reasoning rather than just the final answer token, the optimization signal captures the model's actual problem-solving trajectory, enabling self-optimization without any external large LLM.

## Results

On BBH (21 tasks), GREATER achieves 76.6% with Gemma-2-9B and 68.7% with Llama-3-8B, outperforming the best textual-feedback baseline (TextGrad at 72.9% and 58.5% respectively) by 3.7 and 10.2 points. On GSM8K, GREATER reaches 89.4% (Gemma-2-9B) and 82.6% (Llama-3-8B), surpassing the strongest baselines (APO at 88.6% and 81.1%). On FOLIO, GREATER attains 69.1% (Gemma-2-9B) and 62.6% (Llama-3-8B), matching or exceeding all baselines. When compared against prompts optimized by GPT-4 or PaLM-2-L, GREATER-optimized prompts achieve 71.6% average on five BBH tasks with Llama-3-8B versus 61.0% for the best large-model-optimized baseline (OPRO/PaLM-2-L).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GREATER consistently outperforms all four textual-feedback baselines (APE, APO, PE2, TextGrad) across all three benchmarks and both models. The largest margin is on Llama-3-8B BBH, where GREATER (68.7%) surpasses TextGrad (58.5%) by 10.2 points and APO (62.7%) by 6.0 points. On Gemma-2-9B BBH, GREATER (76.6%) beats the best baseline TextGrad (72.9%) by 3.7 points. Against prompts optimized by larger proprietary models (GPT-4, PaLM-2-L), GREATER-optimized prompts for Llama-3-8B achieve 71.6% average on five BBH tasks, exceeding OPRO/PaLM-2-L (61.0%), PE2/GPT-4 (60.6%), and APO/GPT-4 (59.8%) by 10+ points; for Gemma-2-9B, GREATER reaches 75.4% versus APO/GPT-4's 69.8%. On GSM8K, GREATER matches or slightly exceeds GPT-4-optimized prompts (82.6% vs 81.5% for PE2/GPT-4 on Llama-3-8B; 89.4% vs 89.6% for PE2/GPT-4 on Gemma-2-9B). On FOLIO with Llama-3-8B, GREATER ties PE2 at 62.6%, suggesting the model has reached its performance ceiling on that task.

## Relation to Prior Work

GREATER sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: (1) textual-feedback prompt optimizers (APE, APO, PE2, TextGrad, OPRO, EvoPrompt) that rely on a larger proprietary LLM to generate natural-language 'textual gradients' guiding prompt refinement, and (2) gradient-based discrete prompt search methods (AutoPrompt, PEZ) that use numerical gradients but are restricted to simple classification or fixed-template tasks. GREATER departs from both by computing true loss gradients through a generated reasoning chain ('gradient over reasoning') while restricting the search space to LM-proposed top-k token candidates, thereby combining the directness of numerical optimization with the task-appropriateness of chain-of-thought reasoning, all without requiring an external optimizer LLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: practitioners deploying lightweight open-source models (Llama-3-8B, Gemma-2-9B) can now self-optimize prompts without API access to GPT-4 or PaLM-2-L, reducing both cost and dependency on proprietary systems. The transferability results (prompts optimized on one small model improving another, and even transferring to a 27B model) suggest the optimized prompts capture task-agnostic reasoning strategies rather than model-specific quirks. Limitations include occasional grammatical awkwardness in generated prompts, dependence on being able to extract answer logits via a fixed extractor prompt, and the observation that gains narrow for already-strong models. Open questions include whether integrating gradient-over-reasoning with textual feedback yields complementary benefits, and whether dynamic top-k or adaptive candidate proposal could further improve prompt naturalness.
