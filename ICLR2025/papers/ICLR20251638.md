## Problem

Generating realistic 3D two-person human interactions from text remains challenging because each individual's motion depends on both their own state and their partner's, requiring precise spatial positioning and temporal coordination. Existing diffusion-based approaches (InterGen, in2IN, ComMDM) produce interactions that lack fidelity, exhibit implicit biases, and cannot seamlessly support complementary tasks like reaction generation without architectural redesign.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251638/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text description (encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14@336px model) and produces synchronized 3D motion sequences for two individuals, each represented as N poses × J joints × d features (up to 10 seconds). It supports two skeleton formats: AMASS (22 joints, 12-dim features) for InterHuman and SMPL-X (56 joints, 6-dim features) for InterX. For reaction generation, a reference motion sequence is additionally provided and tokenized, with its tokens kept unmasked while the other individual's motion is generated.

## Method

InterMask is a two-stage framework: first, a shared VQ-VAE with 2D convolutions encodes each individual's motion into a 2D discrete token map (n×j grid over a 1024-entry codebook), preserving both spatial (body-part) and temporal context within each token—unlike prior 1D VQ approaches that collapse spatial structure. Second, an Inter-M Transformer collaboratively models both individuals' tokens via generative masked modeling, using three attention types per block: global self-attention, a shared spatio-temporal attention (separate spatial and temporal attention within each person), and a shared cross-attention (each person attends to the other). Training employs a two-stage masking scheme—random or interaction masking (one person fully unmasked) followed by step-unroll remasking of low-confidence predictions—with a cosine schedule controlling mask ratio. Inference starts from fully masked sequences and progressively fills tokens over 20 iterations, retaining only the most confident predictions at each step, with classifier-free guidance for text alignment.

## Results

InterMask achieves state-of-the-art FID of 5.154 on InterHuman (vs. 5.535 for in2IN) and 0.399 on InterX (vs. 5.207 for InterGen), indicating superior generation fidelity. It also leads in R-Precision Top-1 (0.449 on InterHuman, 0.403 on InterX) and MM Dist (3.790 on InterHuman, 3.705 on InterX), demonstrating strong text alignment. On InterX, the performance gap over InterGen is particularly large (FID 0.399 vs. 5.207), suggesting InterMask scales effectively with more data. InterMask uses only 74M inference parameters and 0.77s inference time, compared to InterGen's 182M parameters and 1.63s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InterMask outperforms all six baselines on FID across both datasets, with the most dramatic margin on InterX where it achieves 0.399 versus InterGen's 5.207 (a 13× improvement). On InterHuman, InterMask edges out in2IN (FID 5.154 vs. 5.535) and MoMat-MoGen (5.154 vs. 5.674), while also matching MoMat-MoGen's R-Precision Top-1 (0.449) and MM Dist (3.790). Against InterGen, InterMask wins on FID, R-Precision, and MM Dist on both datasets but trails on MModality (1.737 vs. 2.141 on InterHuman; 2.261 vs. 3.686 on InterX), indicating a trade-off favoring text adherence over extreme multimodality. The single-person baselines (T2M, MDM) and the bridging approach (ComMDM) are substantially outperformed, with ComMDM particularly struggling on InterX (FID 29.266). In a user study, 69.14% of participants preferred InterMask over InterGen, with higher scores on both interaction quality (4.089 vs. 3.296) and text adherence (3.938 vs. 3.198).

## Relation to Prior Work

InterMask adapts the MaskGIT paradigm of progressive masked token generation (Chang et al., 2022) from images to the discrete motion domain, extending the single-person masked motion models (MoMask, MMM) to the two-person interaction setting. It departs from the dominant diffusion-based interaction generation line (InterGen, in2IN, ComMDM) by operating in a learned discrete token space rather than continuous latent space. A key architectural departure is the 2D VQ-VAE token map, which preserves spatial (joint) and temporal dimensions simultaneously, in contrast to prior 1D motion tokenizers (TM2T, T2M-GPT, MoMask) that collapse spatial structure into each token.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic FID improvement on InterX (0.399 vs. 5.207) and the ability to perform reaction generation without any fine-tuning or architectural redesign make InterMask a practically attractive alternative to diffusion-based interaction models. The 2D token map and spatio-temporal attention design offer a reusable recipe for any task where fine-grained spatial positioning of body parts across two agents matters. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond two individuals, whether the 10-second sequence limit can be lifted, and whether the residual body-penetration and implicit-dancing biases can be addressed without sacrificing the discrete-token efficiency.
