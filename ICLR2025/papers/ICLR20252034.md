## Problem

Gaussian processes are the standard surrogate for Bayesian optimization but require bespoke kernels for complex, non-Euclidean, or high-dimensional correlation structures, and scale poorly with data. Bayesian neural networks offer the expressivity to handle such structures but have underperformed GPs on many BO benchmarks due to under-fitting, lack of efficient online conditioning, and numerical instability. This paper bridges the gap by showing that variational Bayesian last layer (VBLL) networks can match well-tuned GPs on standard benchmarks while significantly outperforming them on high-dimensional and non-stationary problems.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequentially sampled noisy observations (x_t, y_t) from a black-box objective f: X → R^K with X ⊆ R^D, initialized with a Sobol-sequence design. At each iteration it produces a parametric Gaussian predictive distribution (mean w̄ᵀφ_θ(x) and variance φ_θ(x)ᵀSφ_θ(x)+σ²) at any candidate point, which feeds acquisition functions (logEI, Thompson sampling, or logEHVI for multi-objective). Because the predictive is a generalized linear model in the features, Thompson samples can be optimized analytically via L-BFGS-B rather than requiring non-parametric GP sampling, and multi-objective predictions are multivariate normals amenable to NSGA-II-based Pareto front construction.

## Method

The core insight (Theorem 1) is that, for fixed network features θ, the variational posterior over the last layer that maximizes the VBLL ELBO is exactly the posterior obtained by recursive Bayesian linear regression (conjugate conditioning), establishing an equivalence between optimization-based training and GP-style conditioning. Exploiting this, the paper proposes a continual learning loop (Algorithm 1) that interleaves two steps: (i) full model retraining via mini-batch AdamW on the variational objective (updating backbone θ, variational posterior η, and noise σ²), and (ii) cheap recursive last-layer updates via rank-1 Cholesky factorization of the precision matrix, which have quadratic complexity in feature dimension. A re-initialization criterion (event-triggered log-likelihood threshold or sigmoid schedule) decides when to perform full retraining versus recursive updates, balancing exploration (fresh network initialization) with computational efficiency. The parametric architecture enables efficient Thompson sampling by drawing w̃ ~ q_η(w) and analytically maximizing the resulting linear function, and for multi-objective problems the multivariate regression formulation yields a joint predictive that is optimized with NSGA-II followed by greedy hypervolume-improvement selection.

## Results

On classic low-dimensional benchmarks (Branin, Ackley2D/5D, Hartmann), VBLLs match or slightly exceed GPs, with Thompson sampling (TS) on Ackley2D and Ackley5D surpassing all baselines including GPs. On high-dimensional and non-stationary problems (200D NNdraw, 25D Pestcontrol, 12D Lunarlander), VBLLs and LLLA are consistently the best-performing surrogates for TS, while GPs without tailored kernels struggle significantly. In multi-objective optimization, VBLL+TS is the only method that avoids numerical crashes on the 3-objective OilSorbent benchmark, enabling accurate approximation of its Pareto front. The continual learning variant (event-triggered re-initialization with recursive last-layer updates) achieves near-full-VBLL performance while substantially reducing surrogate fit time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPs (Matérn-2.5 with ARD), VBLLs match performance on stationary classic benchmarks but significantly outperform on high-dimensional and non-stationary tasks (NNdraw, Pestcontrol, Lunarlander), where GP kernels fail to capture complex input correlations even with D-scaled hyperpriors. Against I-BNNs, VBLLs outperform across most benchmarks, particularly with TS where the parametric form enables analytic optimization of Thompson samples. Against DKL, VBLLs are superior especially under TS, since DKL's non-parametric nature makes high-dimensional sampling intractable. Against LLLA, VBLLs show comparable or slightly better performance, but LLLA is more sensitive to observation noise (Appendix C.3). Against Deep Ensembles, VBLLs outperform or match, with the key advantage being calibrated Gaussian predictive distributions that pair naturally with acquisition functions. The continual learning variant (VBLL ET-CL) achieves near-identical performance to full retraining VBLLs while reducing runtime by 30–60% depending on the benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Variational Bayesian Last Layers (VBLLs) from Harrison et al. (ICLR 2024), which provide a lightweight Bayesian formulation by placing a variational posterior only over the output layer of a neural network. The key conceptual departure is Theorem 1, which establishes an exact equivalence between optimizing the VBLL variational objective and performing recursive Bayesian linear regression (the same conditioning mechanism used in GPs), thereby bridging optimization-based NN training with GP-style online conditioning. This connection enables a continual learning loop that interleaves full model retraining with cheap rank-1 Cholesky recursive updates, a design not present in prior VBLL or BLL work. The paper positions itself against the full spectrum of BO surrogates—GPs, I-BNNs, DKL, LLLA, and Deep Ensembles—arguing that VBLLs inherit GP-like uncertainty calibration while gaining the expressivity of learned features for non-Euclidean correlation structures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is strongest in multi-objective BO, where the parametric form of VBLLs enables exact Thompson sampling that avoids the numerical instabilities (vanishing gradients in EHVI) that crash all logEHVI-based baselines on OilSorbent. The continual learning scheme offers a meaningful runtime reduction (up to ~50% in some settings) while maintaining near-full-retraining performance, making VBLLs more deployable in compute-constrained BO loops. Open questions include: whether more sophisticated model-selection criteria can replace the simple log-likelihood threshold for re-initialization, how the approach scales to very high-dimensional or batched BO, and whether the event-triggered stagnation observed in high-dimensional logEI settings can be resolved. The paper also notes that tuning the Wishart scale (noise prior) per problem can further improve performance, suggesting room for adaptive hyperparameter strategies.
