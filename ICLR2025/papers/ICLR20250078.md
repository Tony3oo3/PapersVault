## Problem

Existing neural methods for estimating conditional average potential outcomes (CAPOs) over time either assume fixed, discrete observation and treatment schedules (unrealistic in clinical practice) or, in the rare continuous-time case (TE-CDE), rely on balancing heuristics that do not properly adjust for time-varying confounding, leading to biased estimates. No prior neural method performs proper time-varying confounding adjustment in continuous time with arbitrary, irregular timestamps for both measurements and treatments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250078/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes patient trajectories (covariates, binary treatments, outcomes) recorded at arbitrary, irregular timestamps, together with a future interventional treatment sequence that specifies both the treatment assignments (a_{*,s}) and the treatment times ({t_{*,j}^a}). It outputs the expected outcome Y_τ at a chosen future horizon under hard interventions on both treatment propensity and treatment frequency, conditional on the observed history up to time t. Unlike discrete-time methods, both the observation process and the treatment process are modeled as counting processes with data-dependent intensities, so no fixed grid is assumed.

## Method

SCIP-Net builds four neural CDE components: a stability network that learns marginal treatment intensity and propensity (conditioned only on past treatment history) to compute scaling factors, a weight network that learns full-history-conditioned treatment intensity and propensity for the inverse propensity weights, an encoder that maps the observed trajectory to a latent representation, and a decoder that integrates the future treatment path to predict the CAPO. The key theoretical contribution is a tractable finite-product decomposition of the continuous-time IPW (Proposition 2), replacing the intractable product integral with a product over treatment jumping times involving exp(∫λ ds) in the numerator and λ·π in the denominator. Stabilized weights (Propositions 3–4) multiply the IPW by the ratio of marginal to conditional treatment transition probabilities, provably preserving the optimal parameters while reducing variance from extreme weights. The decoder is trained end-to-end with a stabilized-weight-weighted MSE loss, making SCIP-Net the first neural architecture to explicitly adjust for time-varying confounding in continuous time.

## Results

On MIMIC-III, SCIP-Net achieves the lowest RMSE across all prediction windows (1, 2, and 3 hours), with relative improvements of +4.1%, +19.1%, and +12.2% over the best baseline (TE-CDE) respectively. On the tumor growth model, SCIP-Net consistently outperforms all baselines across varying prediction horizons (up to 3 days) and confounding strengths, with gains becoming more pronounced under stronger time-varying confounding. The CIP-Net ablation (without stabilized weights) remains competitive but shows higher variance, particularly at longer horizons and strong confounding, confirming the benefit of stabilization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCIP-Net outperforms all five baselines on both datasets. Against TE-CDE (Seedat et al., 2022), the only other continuous-time method, SCIP-Net achieves relative RMSE improvements of 4.1% (1 hr), 19.1% (2 hr), and 12.2% (3 hr) on MIMIC-III; TE-CDE relies on balancing heuristics that do not properly adjust for time-varying confounding. Against the discrete-time baselines—CT (Melnychuk et al., 2022), CRN (Bica et al., 2020), RMSNs (Lim et al., 2018), and G-Net (Li et al., 2021)—SCIP-Net shows clear and consistent advantages, with the gap widening at longer prediction horizons where discrete-time assumptions break down most severely. On the tumor growth model, SCIP-Net's advantage over all baselines grows with increasing confounding strength, confirming that proper continuous-time IPW adjustment is the key differentiator. The CIP-Net ablation (unstabilized weights) is competitive but less robust than SCIP-Net, especially at 3-hour horizons on MIMIC-III (1.291±0.400 vs. the stabilized version), demonstrating the practical importance of the stabilized weighting scheme.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continuous-time causal inference framework of Rytgaard et al. (2022, 2023) from nonparametric targeted maximum likelihood estimation to a neural setting, deriving a tractable product-integral-to-finite-product reduction for inverse propensity weights in continuous time. It also adapts the stabilized IPW concept from discrete-time marginal structural networks (Lim et al., 2018) to the continuous-time setting, where the scaling factors involve treatment intensity functions rather than simple transition probabilities. The key departure from the closest prior neural method, TE-CDE (Seedat et al., 2022), is replacing the balancing heuristic—which the paper argues targets an improper estimand—with proper stabilized IPW that explicitly adjusts for time-varying confounding at arbitrary timestamps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first neural method to properly adjust for time-varying confounding in continuous time, filling a clear gap between discrete-time causal ML methods (which assume unrealistic fixed schedules) and the single continuous-time baseline TE-CDE (which uses a heuristic that can be biased). The practical relevance is direct: EHR data inherently has irregular measurement and treatment timestamps, so methods that cannot handle this are limited in clinical deployment. Open questions include scalability to high-dimensional covariates, extension beyond binary treatments, validation on fully real-world data without simulated ground truth, and whether the positivity assumption (which can be severely violated in long treatment sequences) can be relaxed.
