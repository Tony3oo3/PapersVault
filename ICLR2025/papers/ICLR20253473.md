## Problem

Existing text-guided image inpainting models based on diffusion suffer from 'prompt neglect,' where the model fills masked regions with background or continues nearby objects from the known region instead of generating the object specified by the text prompt. This occurs because self-attention layers are purely spatial and reinforce similarity between the masked region and surrounding context, effectively overriding the textual condition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253473/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, a binary inpainting mask, and a free-form text prompt, and produces a completed image where the masked region contains the prompted object while the unmasked region remains unchanged. It operates as a training-free plug-in on pre-trained Stable Inpainting models (SD 1.5, SD 2.0, DreamShaper-8) at 512px base resolution, with an optional inpainting-specialized conditional super-resolution pipeline extending output to 2048×2048px. The mask can consist of multiple disconnected regions for multi-object generation.

## Method

HD-Painter introduces two training-free, plug-and-play components. First, PAIntA (Prompt-Aware Introverted Attention) replaces self-attention layers at H/32 and H/16 resolutions by scaling attention scores from masked pixels to known-region pixels using a coefficient derived from cross-attention similarity with the prompt's CLIP token embeddings, thereby suppressing prompt-irrelevant context. Second, RASG (Reweighting Attention Score Guidance) integrates post-hoc guidance into the general DDIM sampling formula by replacing the stochastic noise term ε_t with the standard-normalized gradient of a binary cross-entropy loss between averaged cross-attention maps and the inpainting mask, preserving the latent distribution while steering generation toward prompt-aligned content. The combination of both components is applied to the first half of diffusion steps, adding only ~10% runtime overhead from PAIntA and roughly doubling runtime from RASG's backward pass.

## Results

On MSCOCO 2017 (10,000 image-mask-prompt triplets), DreamShaper 8 + HD-Painter achieves the best CLIP score (26.32), generation class accuracy (68.05%), and LAION aesthetic score (4.980) among all compared methods. The method improves its own baseline (DreamShaper Inpainting) by +0.71 CLIP, +9.12% accuracy, and +0.015 aesthetic score. Stable 2.0 + HD-Painter reaches the highest CLIP score overall (26.48) with 59.74% accuracy, while Stable 1.5 + HD-Painter gains +0.73 CLIP and +4.32% accuracy over its baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HD-Painter (DreamShaper 8) outperforms all seven baselines on at least two of the three metrics. Against SmartBrush (reprod.), the closest competitor on accuracy (66.88%), HD-Painter leads by +1.17% accuracy, +0.46 CLIP, and +0.124 aesthetic score, indicating better prompt alignment and visual quality. Against DreamShaper-ControlNet Inpainting, HD-Painter gains +0.59 CLIP and +9.31% accuracy while also improving aesthetic score (4.980 vs 4.946). Against the blended-latent approach (BLD), HD-Painter achieves +0.68 CLIP, +12.41% accuracy, and +0.158 aesthetic score. Against GLIDE and SDXL Inpainting, the margins are even larger (e.g., +25% accuracy over GLIDE). Notably, SmartBrush improves accuracy over its DreamShaper baseline but degrades aesthetic score (4.856 vs 4.965), whereas HD-Painter improves all three metrics simultaneously over the same baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Stable Diffusion / Stable Inpainting architecture (Rombach et al., 2022) and the DDIM sampling framework (Song et al., 2021), positioning itself as a training-free plug-in that modifies attention mechanisms rather than fine-tuning the model. It departs from prior post-hoc guidance methods (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021; Chefer et al., 2023; Epstein et al., 2023) that add a gradient term outside the DDIM update rule, instead integrating the gradient into DDIM's stochastic component to preserve the latent domain. Conceptually, it also contrasts with SmartBrush and Imagen Editor, which address prompt neglect through object-aware masking strategies, by operating purely at the attention-score level within the existing UNet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature of PAIntA and RASG makes them immediately applicable to any Stable Diffusion-based inpainting model without retraining, which is practically significant for practitioners who lack training infrastructure. The RASG mechanism is general-purpose (works with any objective function) and could potentially extend beyond inpainting to other diffusion-based editing tasks. However, the method inherits backbone limitations (anatomical errors, illogical appearances), RASG roughly doubles inference time, and the approach has only been validated on MSCOCO 2017 with a single evaluation protocol, leaving open questions about robustness on more diverse or adversarial prompts.
