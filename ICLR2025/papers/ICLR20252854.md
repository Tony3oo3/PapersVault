## Problem

Existing causal discovery methods with latent variables predominantly rely on the pure children assumption, requiring each latent variable to have observed variables whose sole parent is that latent variable. This assumption is restrictive in practice (e.g., a stock price is influenced by both intrinsic value and macroeconomic environment, where the latter also affects the former) and has been shown to be unnecessary for identifiability in theory. The paper asks whether causal graph recovery with latent variables is possible under weaker structural conditions that do not require pure children.

## Input / Output

The method takes i.i.d. samples of observed variables (tabular data) generated from a LiNGAM with latent variables, where all exogenous noises are non-Gaussian and independent. It outputs the full adjacency matrix A (direct causal strengths between all variables), the set of latent variables, and the complete ancestral/parental structure. Under the weaker Assumption 1 (each latent variable has at least one homologous surrogate and at least two children), only ancestral relationships are recovered; under the stronger Assumption 2 (additional generalized homologous surrogate conditions), the full parent-child structure is identified. The algorithm has O(|O|⁴) complexity for partial recovery and O(|O|²|L|²) for full recovery.

## Method

The paper introduces the concept of a homologous surrogate: an observed variable O that is a child of latent variable L, has no children, whose latent ancestors are exactly An(L)∪{L}, and whose observed ancestors do not intersect De_O(L). Unlike a pure child (which must have L as its only parent), a homologous surrogate may have additional latent parents provided they are all ancestors of L. The algorithm (Alg. 1) works iteratively from roots to leaves: it identifies observed root variables via pseudo-residual independence tests (Thm. 1), then locates latent root variables by finding candidate homologous surrogates (Thm. 2), verifying shared latent parents (Prop. 1), and confirming true surrogates via sparsity of mixing submatrices (Prop. 2). Effects are estimated using covariances and 4th-order cumulants (Cor. 3), exploiting the non-Gaussianity of exogenous noise. For full recovery (Alg. 2), the method removes observed-to-observed edges, locates generalized homologous surrogates (Lem. 1), and progressively estimates M_L^L through n-hop descendants using a ratio-of-mixing-coefficients identity (Thm. 4), finally recovering A from M = (I−A)⁻¹.

## Results

On synthetic data (20k samples), under Assumption 2 (Case 2), the proposed method achieves 0.2 error in latent variables, 0.97 correct-ordering rate, and 0.94 F1-score, dramatically outperforming GIN (4.1 error, 0.05 CO, 0.13 F1), LaHME (2.1 error, 0.33 CO, 0.39 F1), and PO-LiNGAM (3.9 error, 0.12 CO, 0.17 F1). Under the weaker Assumption 1 only (Case 3), it still leads with 0.5 error, 0.91 CO rate, and 0.81 F1-score. Running time is 1–2 orders of magnitude faster than LaHME (~116s) and PO-LiNGAM (~117s) at 20k samples, with the proposed method completing in ~4s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: GIN (Xie et al., 2020), LaHME (Xie et al., 2022), and PO-LiNGAM (Jin et al., 2024). In Case 1 where the pure children assumption holds, GIN and the proposed method both achieve near-perfect results (F1 ≈ 1.00), while LaHME and PO-LiNGAM lag behind (F1 0.76 and 0.98 respectively). In Case 2 where Assumption 2 holds but pure children do not, the proposed method dominates all baselines by a wide margin: 0.94 F1 vs. 0.13 (GIN), 0.39 (LaHME), and 0.17 (PO-LiNGAM), with a correct-ordering rate of 0.97 vs. 0.05/0.33/0.12. In Case 3 (only Assumption 1 holds), the method still achieves the best F1 (0.81) and correct-ordering rate (0.91), outperforming PO-LiNGAM (0.54 F1, 0.60 CO) which is the strongest baseline here. In Case 4 where Assumption 1 is violated, performance degrades but the method still substantially outperforms all baselines (0.50 F1 vs. 0.00–0.27). On efficiency, the proposed method is ~30× faster than LaHME and ~30–250× faster than PO-LiNGAM across all cases, due to its polynomial O(|O|⁴) complexity versus factorial/exponential worst-case complexity of the baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant pure-children assumption in latent-variable causal discovery (GIN, LaHME, PO-LiNGAM) by introducing the homologous surrogate, which permits an observed variable to have additional latent parents as long as they are ancestors of the target latent variable. It builds on the LiNGAM framework and the pseudo-residual / high-order cumulant toolkit from Cai et al. (2019) and the Darmois-Skitovitch theorem, while responding directly to Adams et al. (2021)'s theoretical argument that pure children are unnecessary for identifiability but their proposed algorithm was impractical. The work also contrasts with Li et al. (2024), who transform pseudo-pure pairs into pure children under additional assumptions, by instead relaxing the parent structure of the surrogate itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the pure-children requirement, the paper broadens the class of causal graphs for which latent variables and their causal relations can be recovered from observational data alone, addressing a practical limitation acknowledged across the field. Two open limitations remain: the method cannot handle latent hierarchical structures where some latent variables lack observed children, and it does not extend to non-stationary or cyclic causal relations. Additionally, the progressive estimation via high-order cumulants makes the algorithm sensitive to small sample sizes and error propagation, which limits its practical utility in low-data regimes.
