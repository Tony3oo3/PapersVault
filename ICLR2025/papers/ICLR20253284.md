## Problem

Existing multi-modal multi-task medical imaging models assume fixed information distributions across modalities and tasks, ignoring that modality quality varies per patient (e.g., FFDM vs. 2DS reliability shifts with breast density) and that different diagnostic tasks require different modality-feature combinations. This leads to 'modality competition,' where one modality dominates the joint latent space and suppresses domain-specific features, and to static shared representations that fail to capture task-specific modality relevance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253284/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired multi-modal medical images per patient—up to four mammography views (two FFDM, two 2DS) or two ophthalmology views (color fundus photo + OCT)—which are tokenized via ViT-style patch embedders into sequences of shape l×d. Outputs include per-task classification labels (cancer risk, breast density, BI-RADS, glaucoma grade), a segmentation mask (optical cup), and interpretable modality-contribution scores at both the individual-patient and population level, derived directly from the MToE routing weights.

## Method

M⁴oE comprises two Soft-MoE modules: Modality-Specific MoE (MSoE) blocks, one per modality, that retain input-dependent modality-specific features to counteract modality competition, and a Modality-Shared Modality-Task MoE (MToE) that routes mixed multi-modal tokens through task-specific slots with learnable task embeddings, creating data-dependent pathways linking modalities → experts → tasks. The MToE's 3D routing matrix Φ ∈ ℝ^{d×n×p} induces a joint probability model P(M,E|T), and a conditional mutual information loss I(M;E|T) = H(M|T) − H(M|E,T) is maximized to encourage experts to learn diverse, task-specific modality combinations while preserving modality diversity. Task-specific fused features from MToE are concatenated with modality-specific features from MSoE, passed through a final Soft-MoE fusion block, and fed to per-task prediction heads. The framework is trained end-to-end with standard task losses plus the MI regularization term (α = 0.05), using 128 MToE experts and 32 MSoE experts per modality.

## Results

In the multi-task setting, M⁴oE achieves 85.9% accuracy on EMBED 1-year risk, 84.1% on density, and 75.1% on BI-RADS; 77.8% density and 66.7% BI-RADS on RSNA; 89.6% density and 71.8% BI-RADS on VinDr; and 90.4% glaucoma detection with 89.7% DICE on GAMMA. These represent consistent improvements of 0.3–3.5 points over the strongest natural-domain baselines (MODN, EVIF, AIDE, Fuller) across all four datasets and all tasks. In the single-task setting, M⁴oE also outperforms medical-specific baselines (MIRAI, Asymmirai, EyeMost, EyeStar) by 1.1–8.8 points, with the largest gains over Asymmirai on VinDr density (+8.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

M⁴oE outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and task in the multi-task setting. Against the strongest natural-domain multi-task baselines, it leads MODN by +1.1 (EMBED risk), +1.2 (EMBED BI-RADS), +1.4 (VinDr BI-RADS), +1.2 (GAMMA glaucoma), and +2.5 (GAMMA segmentation); it surpasses EVIF by up to +2.7 on GAMMA segmentation and +1.5 on EMBED BI-RADS; and it beats Fuller by the largest margins, up to +3.5 on GAMMA segmentation and +3.1 on VinDr BI-RADS. In the single-task setting, M⁴oE outperforms medical-specific baselines MIRAI and Asymmirai on all mammography tasks (e.g., +6.5 on EMBED risk vs. Asymmirai, +8.8 on VinDr density vs. Asymmirai) and EyeMost/EyeStar on retinal tasks (+1.6 glaucoma, +1.1 DICE vs. EyeMost). The paper also shows that adding MToE to existing medical backbones (MIRAI+MToE, Asymmirai+MToE, EyeMost+MToE, EyeStar+MToE) yields significant gains (e.g., MIRAI+MToE reaches 84.6% EMBED risk vs. 83.1% standalone), demonstrating MToE's plug-and-play generalizability. No baseline outperforms M⁴oE on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Soft Mixture-of-Experts architecture (Puigcerver et al., 2023) and repurposes it for a dual purpose in medical imaging: modality-specific feature retention (MSoE) and joint modality-task-expert routing (MToE). It departs from prior multi-modal fusion work (early/late/intermediate fusion, information-theoretic disentanglement) by rejecting the assumption of fixed modality information distribution across patients, and from prior multi-task learning by rejecting fixed modality-task associations. The conditional mutual information loss that encourages task-dependent modality-expert coupling is a new objective not present in prior MoE or multi-modal fusion literature, and the explicit probabilistic modeling of P(M,E|T) via dispatch weights is a novel interpretability mechanism for multi-modal multi-task settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MToE module's demonstrated plug-in compatibility with existing medical AI backbones (MIRAI, Asymmirai, Eyemost, Eyestar) makes it a potentially reusable component for the broader medical multi-modal community, not just a monolithic framework. The sample-level and population-level modality contribution interpretability addresses a real clinical need for understanding which imaging modality drives a specific prediction. Open questions include scalability to 10+ modalities or tasks, handling of missing modalities at inference, and whether the conditional MI loss generalizes beyond the 2–4 modality, 2–7 task settings tested. The evaluation is limited to two medical domains (mammography, ophthalmology) and four datasets, leaving generalizability to other imaging modalities (CT, MRI, PET) unverified.
