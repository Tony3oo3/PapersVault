## Problem

Existing parameter nowcasting methods (WNNs, IntrospectionMLP) predict future weights coordinate-wise with simple MLPs, ignoring the structural connectivity between neurons and layers that is critical for accurate parameter prediction. Additionally, the neural graph representations proposed for Transformers do not correctly model the neuron permutation symmetry of multi-head self-attention, making them unnecessarily restrictive and less generalizable. This paper addresses both limitations to enable a single model to accelerate Adam training across diverse architectures and tasks.

## Input / Output

NiNo takes c=5 past parameter checkpoints (collected every 200 Adam steps) along with the target network's architecture to construct a single neural graph whose edge features encode the stacked parameter history. It outputs K future parameter deltas simultaneously via direct multi-step forecasting, which are un-scaled and added to the current parameters to produce the nowcasted weights. The prediction horizon k is not fixed but decays during optimization as k ≈ K((T−t)/T)^p, using large k early in training and small k near convergence, so a single trained model handles the full optimization trajectory without retraining.

## Method

NiNo represents the target network's parameters as a neural graph where nodes are neurons and edges are weight connections, then processes this graph with a GNN (3 layers, D=128 hidden units, mean aggregation) to predict future edge features. The key architectural improvement over prior neural graphs is a corrected MSA representation that (1) restricts neuron permutations across attention heads and (2) keeps W^q, W^k, W^v as separate 1-dimensional directed edge features rather than stacking them, accurately capturing the relaxed permutation symmetry of self-attention. Node features use 8-dimensional Laplacian Positional Encoding plus a word-position embedding for embedding layers, while edge features include integer edge-type labels distinguishing trainable parameters from auxiliary connections. The model is meta-trained with a supervised L1 loss on ~1.6M parameter trajectories from 1000 small models across four in-distribution tasks, and at inference it is applied only once per 1000 Adam steps, adding <0.5% wall-clock overhead while reducing total training steps by up to 50%.

## Results

NiNo achieves an average 48.9% reduction in the number of optimization steps to reach target validation performance across nine vision and language tasks, roughly halving training time compared to Adam/AdamW. On out-of-distribution tasks (different architectures and/or datasets from meta-training), NiNo still achieves 46.8–49.8% speedup, outperforming WNN+ (31.6–44.0%) and the naive-graph variant of NiNo (23.4–49.3%). On the largest evaluated model (111M-parameter Llama3-style Transformer), NiNo reaches the AdamW baseline perplexity of 24.4 in ~30% fewer steps, with a wall-clock overhead of only 1.47%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NiNo consistently outperforms all baselines across all nine tasks. Compared to Linefit (Sinha et al., 2017), which achieves 27.3% average speedup via simple per-parameter linear extrapolation, NiNo improves by 21.6 percentage points (48.9% vs. 27.3%), with the gap widening on out-of-distribution language tasks (e.g., 48.9% vs. 29.8% on LM1B/3-64). Against WNN (Jang et al., 2023), which predicts parameters coordinate-wise with an MLP, NiNo gains 22.4 points on average (48.9% vs. 26.5%), and the advantage is most pronounced on Transformer tasks where the naive per-parameter approach fails to capture neuron connectivity (e.g., 50.0% vs. 26.1% on LM1B/3-24). Against L2O/FM16 (Kofinas et al., 2024), a learned optimizer applied at every step, NiNo generalizes far better to unseen tasks: while L2O/FM16 overfits on out-of-distribution tasks and fails to reach target validation scores in most settings, NiNo/FM16 (trained on the same single task) still achieves strong speedups on unseen architectures, and the full NiNo model (trained on 4 tasks) achieves 48.9% average speedup with only 0.37–1.47% wall-clock overhead versus ~5% for L2O.

## Relation to Prior Work

NiNo extends the periodic parameter nowcasting framework of WNNs (Jang et al., 2023) by replacing coordinate-wise MLPs with graph neural networks operating on neural graphs (Kofinas et al., 2024; Lim et al., 2024), thereby leveraging neuron connectivity and permutation symmetry. A key departure is the corrected neural graph construction for multi-head self-attention, which the paper shows the prior naive graph gets wrong by conflating cross-head and within-head permutations. Unlike learning-to-optimize (L2O) methods that apply a learned update at every step, NiNo is invoked only once per ~1,000 Adam steps, keeping overhead below 0.5% while still achieving substantial speedups.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A ~50% reduction in optimization steps with negligible wall-clock overhead is a practically significant efficiency gain for training both vision and language models, and the approach generalizes across architectures (GPT2, Llama3-style) and tasks without retraining. The corrected neural graph for Transformers is a useful contribution to the broader literature on equivariant representations of network parameters. Open questions include whether the approach scales to billion-parameter models (the largest tested is 111M), whether NiNo's underfitting regime can be resolved with more meta-training data, and how to reduce inference memory for very large graphs. The paper also notes that the graph embedding space provides a useful byproduct for analyzing training dynamics across architectures.
