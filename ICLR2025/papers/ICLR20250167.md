## Problem

Action chunking in robot imitation learning has inconsistent effects across studies—some find it crucial for strong performance while others observe degradation under practical conditions. The paper identifies the root cause as a fundamental tradeoff: longer action horizons improve long-term consistency by implicitly conditioning on past actions but reduce short-term reactivity by limiting access to recent state observations. The key question is how to preserve the temporal consistency benefits of action chunking without sacrificing the ability to react to unexpected dynamics.

## Input / Output

At each timestep, BID consumes the current robot state (RGB images at 256×256 from wrist and third-person cameras plus proprioception), the previously selected action chunk stored in decision memory, and N=16 candidate samples drawn independently from both a strong (well-trained) and a weak (underfitting) policy checkpoint. It outputs a single selected action chunk of length l=16 that minimizes the combined backward coherence and forward contrast losses. The method operates entirely at test time without modifying trained policy weights, and all N samples are generated in parallel on a GPU, adding roughly 2× computational overhead over vanilla single-sample inference.

## Method

BID is a test-time decoding algorithm that frames closed-loop action chunking as a search over a batch of N candidate samples, selecting the one that best balances two criteria. Backward coherence computes a weighted L2 distance between each candidate and the previously selected chunk with exponential decay ρ^τ over overlapping steps, encouraging commitment to a consistent latent strategy while permitting gradual adaptation to unforeseen changes. Forward contrast, inspired by LLM contrastive decoding, minimizes the average distance to K positive samples from a strong policy while maximizing distance to K negative samples from a weak (underfitting) policy, effectively identifying high-likelihood plans under the target distribution. Before computing forward losses, each sample set is trimmed to the K candidates with the smallest backward distance to the previous decision, preventing cross-strategy contamination. The paper also provides a theoretical analysis (Proposition 1) formalizing the consistency-reactivity tradeoff, proving that longer action horizons reduce expected loss in deterministic environments with diverse strategies but increase it in stochastic settings where forward inference is unreliable. BID is model-agnostic, fully parallelizable, and complementary to existing methods—combining it with temporal ensembling (EMA) yields a 46% relative improvement over the vanilla baseline.

## Results

On seven simulation tasks (Push-T, Lift, Can, Square, Transport, Tool Hang, Kitchen) with Diffusion Policy, BID achieves a 32% relative improvement over the vanilla receding-horizon baseline, and 46% when combined with EMA. On VQ-BeT (Push-T, closed-loop), BID attains 54.4% / 45.3% / 31.7% success at noise levels 0.0 / 1.0 / 1.5, versus 48.9% / 38.3% / 29.5% for vanilla sampling. In real-world dynamic picking, BID achieves a 2× higher success rate than all other baselines. Inference overhead is modest: ~26 ms per step on an A5000 GPU at batch size 16 (vs. 13 ms for vanilla).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BID is compared against three existing inference methods for action chunking: (1) Vanilla receding horizon (Chi et al., 2023), which executes the first action of a freshly sampled chunk each step; (2) Warmstart (Janner et al., 2022), which warm-starts diffusion noise from the previous decision; and (3) EMA / temporal ensembling (Zhao et al., 2023b), which linearly blends the new chunk with the previous one. BID outperforms all three across all seven Diffusion Policy simulation tasks, with the largest gains on Push-T, Lift, Square, and Tool Hang. Warmstart degrades performance on 3 out of 7 tasks, and EMA drops significantly on 2 tasks, while BID shows consistent gains everywhere. On VQ-BeT (Push-T, closed-loop), BID improves over vanilla by 5.5 / 7.0 / 2.2 percentage points at noise 0.0 / 1.0 / 1.5, and over EMA by 1.8 / 9.6 / 13.3 points respectively. Combining BID with EMA further boosts the relative gain from 32% to 46%, confirming the methods are complementary rather than competing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the action chunking paradigm introduced by Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2023) and ALOHA's temporal ensembling (Zhao et al., 2023b), which are the dominant inference strategies for generative robot policies. Its key conceptual departure is borrowing contrastive decoding from the LLM literature (Li et al., 2023) and adapting it to low-level robotic action sequences, framing the selection of an action chunk as a bidirectional search problem rather than a single-sample execution. The theoretical analysis in §3 is novel in explicitly characterizing the consistency-reactivity tradeoff of action chunking through observation advantage and inference disadvantage, providing the first formal explanation for the conflicting empirical reports in the field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Action chunking is a core component of virtually all modern generative robot policies (RT-2, π0, ALOHA, UMI), yet its optimal configuration has been chosen heuristically with inconsistent outcomes across settings. BID offers a plug-and-play, model-agnostic test-time improvement that resolves the consistency-reactivity tension without retraining, which is practically valuable given the cost of collecting robot demonstrations. Key limitations include the doubled computational overhead (constraining high-frequency control on low-cost hardware) and the restriction to short-context policies; open questions include whether the approach scales to variable-horizon chunking, long-context policies, and whether the forward contrast criterion can be replaced by learned value functions to reduce the need for a separate weak checkpoint.
