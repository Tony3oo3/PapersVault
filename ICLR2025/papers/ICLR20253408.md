## Problem

While synthetic captions improve image-text alignment over noisy web-crawled AltText, it remains unclear whether they can fully replace AltText in pretraining, and which caption format each foundation model (CLIP, MLLMs, diffusion models) actually prefers. Prior work has not systematically studied the interaction between synthetic captions and original AltText, nor identified model-specific optimal captioning strategies at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253408/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes RGB images (224×224 for CLIP, 256×256 for diffusion, 336×336 for MLLM) along with their original web-crawled AltText and produces four controllable caption formats: Short Synthetic Captions (10–15 tokens), Descriptive Synthetic Captions (40–60 tokens), Dense Synthetic Captions (>100 tokens), and AltText Fusion Captions (DSC-length with AltText knowledge integrated). For CLIP and diffusion models, captions are constrained to the 77-token text encoder limit, while MLLM experiments additionally use the longer DSC+ and AFC formats. The captioner itself is a 3B-parameter MM1 model fine-tuned to strictly follow format instructions, achieving lower hallucination rates (CHAIR_i=5.9) than LLaVA-1.5 or GPT-4V-based captioners.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage fine-tuning procedure that converts a pre-trained MLLM (MM1-3B) into a controllable, human-aligned image captioner: Stage 1 fine-tunes on a curated 1M human-annotated dataset (Stage-1-1M) augmented with OCR-extracted text and LLM-controlled rewriting to enforce format compliance and suppress hallucination; Stage 2 further fine-tunes on a human-annotated descriptive dataset (Stage-2-HA) to support richer caption formats. The resulting captioner generates captions at scale (7B images re-captioned) across four formats, which are then used to systematically pretrain three foundation models—CLIP (ViT-B/16, contrastive learning on VeCap-300M), MM1 (1.2B, supervised pretraining), and Stable Diffusion 3 (DiT, text-to-image)—varying caption type and synthetic-to-AltText mixing ratio. The key empirical finding is that a hybrid recipe combining synthetic captions with original AltText consistently outperforms either alone, with CLIP favoring short captions (~40–50% SSC ratio) for zero-shot classification, MLLMs favoring dense captions (DSC+) for post-SFT performance, and diffusion models benefiting most from DSC+AltText for prompt-following (GenEval).

## Results

For CLIP (ViT-B/16), the hybrid SSC+AltText recipe achieves the best overall performance: 66.67/48.13 R@1 on COCO I-T/T-I, 91.81/76.54 R@1 on Flickr30k, and 66.63% zero-shot ImageNet accuracy, outperforming AltText alone (65.70%) and DSC alone (27.30%). For MM1 (1.2B), DSC+ alone yields the best SFT average score of 58.7%, surpassing LLaVA captions (56.5%) and AltText (57.6%). For the diffusion model (Stable Diffusion 3), DSC+AltText achieves the highest GenEval average of 67.4 (vs. 62.3 for AltText alone) and DSG of 74.2, while SSC+AltText achieves the best FID of 13.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's controllable captioner outperforms all four MLLM baselines on hallucination metrics: it achieves CHAIR_i=5.9 and CHAIR_s=19.6, beating LLaVA-1.5 (6.2/20.6), Shikra (7.0/22.0), MiniGPT-4 (8.2/24.2), and InstructBLIP (14.5/30.0). In the MM1 SFT evaluation, the paper's DSC+ captions achieve a 58.7% average score, outperforming LLaVA-generated captions (56.5%) by 2.2 points, while also surpassing AltText (57.6%) and DSC (56.3%). For CLIP, the key finding is that no single synthetic caption format replaces AltText: DSC alone collapses ImageNet accuracy to 27.30% (vs. 65.70% for AltText), but mixing SSC with AltText recovers and exceeds AltText performance (66.63%). In the diffusion model setting, DSC+AltText improves GenEval average from 62.3 (AltText) to 67.4, while SSC+AltText achieves the best FID (13.1 vs. 13.6 for AltText), confirming that synthetic captions enhance prompt-following without sacrificing generation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on improving image-text pretraining data through synthetic captions (VeCLIP, ShareGPT4V, LaCLIP, Recap-DataComp-1B) by introducing a controllable, two-stage fine-tuning pipeline that converts an MLLM (MM1-3B) into a format-specific captioner, moving beyond the GPT-4V-dependent or single-format approaches of prior work. It departs from prior studies that treated synthetic captions as a drop-in replacement for AltText by systematically studying their interaction and optimal mixing ratio across three distinct model families (CLIP, MLLMs, diffusion models). The captioning pipeline itself builds on MM1's architecture and VeCLIP's recaptioning recipe, but adds human-aligned fine-tuning stages and format controllability (SSC, DSC, DSC+, AFC) that prior captioning methods lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that synthetic captions alone can severely degrade CLIP's zero-shot classification (DSC: 27.3% vs. 65.7% ImageNet) while a hybrid with AltText recovers and exceeds baseline performance provides critical, actionable guidance for anyone pre-training multimodal foundation models at scale. The model-specific caption preferences (CLIP favors short captions, MLLMs favor dense captions, diffusion models favor descriptive captions) suggest that a one-size-fits-all captioning strategy is suboptimal, motivating the controllable pipeline as a practical tool. Open questions include whether the optimal mixing ratio (~40-50% for CLIP) is stable across model scales and dataset sizes, whether the hallucination tolerance observed in MLLM pretraining (DSC+ outperforming cleaner LLaVA captions) holds at larger scales, and whether the distribution-mismatch explanation for DSC's CLIP degradation is the full story.
