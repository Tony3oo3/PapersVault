## Problem

Existing coreset selection methods for task-specific LLM fine-tuning face two critical limitations: they struggle to balance data importance and diversity at high pruning rates, causing catastrophic performance degradation (up to 39.4% drop), and they require multiple epochs of expensive fine-tuning on the target LLM to compute data scores, incurring selection overheads exceeding 20 GPU-hours. There is no method that simultaneously achieves robust selection across all pruning rates and keeps the selection process tractable for large models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250934/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a downstream task dataset D of N (input, target) pairs, a target LLM θ_t and a smaller same-family LLM θ_s (both unfine-tuned), a pruning rate p, and hyperparameters (fine-tuning budget T=3 epochs, K=50 stratified regions, verification budget b_v=10 samples per region). It outputs a coreset D' with |D'|/|D| ≤ 1−p. All fine-tuning uses LoRA parameter-efficient adaptation, and the scoring function is the effort score (L2 gradient norm) computed on the fine-tuned small model, with verification scores computed on the unfine-tuned target LLM.

## Method

STAFF applies the speculative execution paradigm from computer architecture to coreset selection in two stages. First, it fine-tunes the small same-family model θ_s on the full dataset for T epochs and computes the effort score (gradient norm) for every sample as a cheap proxy for data importance. Second, it stratifies the dataset into K equal-width score regions, then for each region (processed from sparsest to densest) samples b_v points and computes a verification ratio V_i = ΣS_d^t / ΣS_d^s on the target LLM θ_t, which quantifies how much more (or less) important that region is to the target versus the small model. The selection budget for each region is scaled by V_i relative to the remaining average budget, so regions where the target model finds data more important receive proportionally more samples while all regions retain at least some coverage. This design ensures that at low pruning rates easy samples are preferentially dropped, while at high pruning rates diverse regions are still represented, avoiding the importance-only collapse seen in baselines.

## Results

On three LLMs (Gemma-7b, Llama-2-13b, Mistral-Nemo) and three downstream tasks (BioInstruct, DialogSum, WMT-19), STAFF achieves the best Rouge-L across all pruning rates (20%–90%), improving over the best SOTA baseline by up to 54.3% (e.g., Llama-2-13b on WMT-19 at 90%: STAFF 54.8 vs. GraNd 35.5). STAFF also reduces coreset selection time overhead by up to 70.5% (e.g., Gemma-7b on WMT-19: 3.5 h vs. D2 Pruning's 11.8 h). Notably, at 20% pruning on DialogSum, STAFF-selected coresets (Rouge-L 50.0 for Gemma-7b) outperform fine-tuning on the full dataset (49.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STAFF consistently outperforms all three SOTA baselines across all models, datasets, and pruning rates. Against GraNd (Paul et al., 2021), the gap is most dramatic at high pruning rates: on Llama-2-13b/WMT-19 at 90%, GraNd collapses to 35.5 Rouge-L while STAFF maintains 54.8, a 54.3% relative improvement. Against CCS (Zheng et al., 2023), which already balances importance and diversity via uniform budget allocation, STAFF still leads by 1.2–1.9 points at 90% pruning (e.g., 55.9 vs. 54.1 on Gemma-7b/WMT-19), demonstrating that its verification-guided budget allocation is more effective than uniform allocation. Against D2 Pruning (Maharana et al., 2024), STAFF wins on both quality and efficiency: on Gemma-7b/WMT-19 at 90%, STAFF achieves 55.9 vs. 51.4 Rouge-L while completing selection in 3.5 h versus D2 Pruning's 11.8 h (a 70.5% time reduction). EL2N, while competitive at low pruning rates (e.g., matching STAFF at 20% on Mistral-Nemo/BioInstruct), degrades sharply at 90% (44.9 vs. 60.3 on WMT-19), confirming that importance-only selection fails without diversity coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

STAFF sits at the intersection of two research threads: coreset selection for data-efficient training (GraNd/EL2N from Paul et al. 2021, CCS from Zheng et al. 2023, D2 Pruning from Maharana et al. 2024) and speculative execution in LLMs (SpecInfer, speculative decoding). It repurposes the speculative-execution paradigm—originally a computer-architecture optimization for speeding up computation—into the data-selection pipeline: a small same-family model acts as the 'speculative' scorer, and the target LLM performs lightweight verification rather than full fine-tuning. The stratified region-based selection directly inherits from Zheng et al. (2023), but STAFF departs from prior coreset methods by decoupling score computation (done on the small model) from score verification (done on the target model), thereby eliminating the need to fine-tune the target LLM during selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 70.5% reduction in selection overhead is practically significant for teams that must repeatedly select coresets across multiple downstream tasks, as it removes the most expensive step (multi-epoch fine-tuning of the target LLM). However, the performance gains over the strongest baselines (CCS, D2 Pruning) are modest—typically 1–3 Rouge-L points—and the headline 54.3% improvement is measured against a catastrophically failing method (GraNd at 90% pruning on Llama-2-13b/WMT-19), not against the best competitor. Open questions include whether the same-family requirement generalizes to rapidly evolving model lineages, whether the approach extends to pretraining data selection or preference fine-tuning, and whether the verification budget can be further reduced without sacrificing the region-identification accuracy that drives the method's advantage at high pruning rates.
