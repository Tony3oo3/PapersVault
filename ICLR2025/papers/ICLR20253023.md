## Problem

Existing RL-based formulations for computing circuit optimization (e.g., multipliers, adders) converge to local optima rather than global optima due to two fundamental issues: (1) deceptive reward signals, where maximizing cumulative discounted performance gains misaligns with the true objective of finding the single best-performing design, and (2) incrementally localized actions that severely constrain exploration in a highly oscillatory objective landscape. The paper provides a theoretical proof (Theorem 3.1) that the optimal RL policy necessarily terminates at a local optimum, and empirically demonstrates a significant objective gap between the RL proxy task and the original circuit design task.

## Input / Output

The method takes a circuit specification (type: multiplier/adder/MAC; bit width: 8–64; PPG encoding: AND-gate or Booth) and represents each design solution as a 2×N_C grid where the two rows encode the total number of 3:2 (full) and 2:2 (half) compressors at each column. A learned Q-network from the RL initialization phase guides mutation, and a ResNet-18-based surrogate model (with a two-MLP multi-head decoder predicting area and delay) pre-ranks offspring before the top-5 are evaluated by the real OpenROAD/NanGate 45nm synthesis flow. The output is a set of Pareto-optimal compressor/prefix tree configurations characterized by their post-synthesis area and delay, which can be directly instantiated into large-scale PE arrays for DNN accelerators.

## Method

MUTE reformulates circuit optimization as a genetic evolution process over grid-based genetic individuals, where each iteration evaluates solutions using the true post-synthesis objective rather than proxy rewards, thereby bridging the objective gap identified in RL formulations. The framework comprises three key components: (1) a learning-based population initialization that runs DQN to collect an elite pool of high-performing designs, (2) two genetic variation operators—a sequential mutation operator that chains Q-network-guided local modifications for efficient long-term exploration, and a multi-granularity crossover operator that recombines substructures at single-column or cross-column ranges between two parents to enable globally diverse exploration, and (3) a model-based cascade ranking selection that uses a learned ResNet-18 surrogate to rapidly pre-rank 100+ offspring per generation, sending only the top-5 to the expensive real synthesis tool. The multi-granularity crossover is the critical mechanism for escaping local optima, expanding design variation from single-gene (one adder type at one column) to cross-column (entire substructure spanning multiple columns) recombination.

## Results

On multiplier design across eight tasks (8/16/32/64-bit, AND and Booth encoding), MUTE consistently Pareto-dominates all five baselines, improving hypervolume by up to 38% over the previous SOTA (HAVE). On adder design, MUTE achieves hypervolumes of 116.94 (16-bit), 281.66 (32-bit), and 582.73 (64-bit), improving over RL-MUL by 32.84%, 33.01%, and 15.75% respectively. On MAC design, MUTE achieves hypervolumes of 414.80, 5843.00, and 11487.37 for 16/32/64-bit, improving over RL-MUL by up to 85.74%. When deployed in PE arrays for large-scale circuits, MUTE outperforms the previous SOTA by up to 48.18% in hypervolume.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MUTE outperforms all five baselines across every circuit type and bit width tested. Against the classical Wallace Tree (human-designed heuristic), MUTE achieves 87% higher hypervolume on 16-bit AND multipliers. Against GOMIL (integer programming), MUTE consistently Pareto-dominates in both area and delay. Among the three RL-based baselines, MUTE surpasses RL-MUL by up to 32.84% (adders) and 42.03% (MACs) in hypervolume, outperforms AdaReset by up to 35.28%, and beats the strongest baseline HAVE by up to 38.46% on multipliers and 15.75% on 64-bit adders. The advantage is most pronounced on MAC circuits at 64-bit width, where MUTE achieves 85.74% hypervolume improvement over RL-MUL. On PE array generalization, MUTE's optimized multipliers yield up to 48.18% hypervolume improvement over HAVE, confirming that the gains transfer to large-scale computation-intensive circuits.

## Relation to Prior Work

MUTE builds directly on the RL-based circuit optimization line (RL-MUL, AdaReset, HAVE, PrefixRL), reusing their grid-based representation of compressor/prefix tree structures and their DQN-based policy learning. However, it fundamentally departs from this lineage by proving that the RL proxy task's cumulative discounted reward objective is misaligned with the true 'find the single best solution' objective, and by reformulating the problem as a genetic evolution process with multi-granularity crossover and model-based cascade ranking. The learning component (Q-network-guided sequential mutation and population initialization) is repurposed from the RL methods, but the overall search paradigm shifts from sequential local modification to population-based global exploration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result that the RL formulation's optimal policy necessarily converges to a local optimum (Theorem 3.1), combined with the empirical demonstration that ~54% of policies outperform the one reaching the global optimum, challenges a core assumption in the ML-for-EDA community that RL is a sound formulation for circuit optimization. The 38% hypervolume improvement and 48% gain on PE arrays demonstrate practical relevance for AI chip design. Open questions include whether the genetic evolution formulation generalizes beyond compressor/prefix trees to other circuit topologies, whether the model-based cascade ranking can reduce the remaining dependence on expensive synthesis evaluations, and whether the approach scales to 128-bit or larger circuits where the design space grows combinatorially.
