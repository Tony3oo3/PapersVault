## Problem

The inner-loop global optimization of Bayesian optimization acquisition functions is catastrophically difficult when the acquisition function is a posterior sample path (as in GP-TS or entropy search variants), because such paths are highly non-smooth with exponentially many local optima in the input dimension. Existing approaches rely on random or grid-based multistart points that become ineffective in higher dimensions, and no efficient strategy existed for judiciously selecting starting points that are specific to each posterior sample realization.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a separable GP prior sample (approximated as a product of univariate spectral samples), a noise draw, the separable prior covariance, the observation dataset, and three set-size hyperparameters (n_o for candidate prior minima, n_e for exploration starts, n_x for exploitation starts). It outputs the global minimum location and value of the posterior sample path. A key constraint is that the domain must be a hypercube and the kernel must be separable; the number of gradient-based optimizer calls is only n_e + n_x (as small as 2 in practice), while n_o can be moderate (e.g., 500) to ensure coverage without exponential cost.

## Method

TS-roots decomposes each posterior sample via pathwise conditioning into a prior sample plus a smoother data-adjustment term, then exploits the fact that the rougher prior sample's local minima are near the posterior sample's local minima. It finds all univariate critical points of each separable prior component via Chebyshev polynomial approximation and colleague-matrix eigenvalue solving (univariate global rootfinding), then uses a min-heap-based maxk_sum algorithm to select the n_o best prior-sample local minima in time linear in the sum of univariate critical points rather than their product. These are filtered by posterior-sample value to form an exploration set, which is combined with a small exploitation set drawn from observed locations to seed a gradient-based multistart optimizer. The overall cost scales linearly in the input dimension d, breaking the curse of dimensionality, and even a single point from each set recovers the global optimum most of the time.

## Results

TS-roots achieves the best outer-loop optimization performance on the 2D Schwefel, 16D Ackley, and 16D Powell functions, and gives competitive results on 4D Rosenbrock and 10D Levy, across 20 independent runs. In inner-loop optimization, rootfinding-based starting points yield the highest-quality GP-TS acquisition function solutions and the lowest CPU times, with the advantage growing substantially in high dimensions (10D–16D) where random multistart degrades. Remarkably, even with just one exploration point and one exploitation point, the global optimum of the posterior sample is discovered most of the time, and the algorithm scales linearly in input dimension d.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against **TS-DSRF** (decoupled sampling with random Fourier features, Wilson et al. 2020) and **TS-RF** (GP-TS with random Fourier features, Rahimi & Recht 2007), TS-roots recommends better solutions in high-dimensional problems (16D Ackley, 16D Powell) and offers competitive performance in low dimensions, with the gap widening as dimension increases. Against **EI** (Jones et al. 1998) and **LCB/GP-UCB** (Srinivas et al. 2010), EI and LCB tend to perform better in the initial optimization stages, but TS-roots shows faster improvement in later stages and ultimately achieves the best final solutions on 2D Schwefel, 16D Ackley, and 16D Powell. Against **LogEI** (Ament et al. 2023), TS-roots outperforms on 2D Schwefel, 4D Rosenbrock, 10D Levy, and 16D Ackley. Against **MES-R** (MES with TS-RF sampling, Wang & Jegelka 2017), MES-TS-roots demonstrates superior optimization performance and faster convergence on 4D Rosenbrock and 6D Hartmann, especially with 50 posterior samples, while on 10D Levy the advantage is clear with 10 samples but comparable with 50.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the pathwise conditioning (decoupled representation) of GP posteriors from Wilson et al. (2020/2021) for efficient posterior sampling, and on classical univariate global rootfinding via Chebyshev polynomial approximation and colleague matrix eigenvalue solvers (Trefethen, 2019). The key departure is from the standard practice of using random or grid-based starting points for gradient-based multistart optimization of acquisition functions: instead, TS-roots exploits the separability of GP prior samples to analytically enumerate and order the prior sample's local minima via univariate rootfinding, then uses a small subset of those (plus observed data points) as adaptive starting points for the posterior sample. This reframes the inner-loop optimization problem from a generic non-convex search into a structured, dimension-scalable procedure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The linear scaling in input dimension breaks the curse of dimensionality that has made posterior-sample-based acquisition functions (GP-TS, entropy search variants) impractical in high dimensions, potentially enabling their use in problems up to 16+ dimensions where they previously underperformed. The sample-average posterior function (α_aTS) offers a simple, single-parameter control over exploration-exploitation that is computationally free (cost of one posterior sample). Open questions include: the probability and conditions under which TS-roots fails to find the global optimum, the optimal value of N_c for α_aTS on a given problem, and extension beyond separable kernels to generalized additive or compositional kernels.
