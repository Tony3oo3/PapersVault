## Problem

Bayesian learning with modern deep learning models is computationally challenging due to the need to approximate high-dimensional posteriors, and existing software (Stan, Pyro, BlackJAX) is either not minibatch-first, not easily composable with the PyTorch/Hugging Face ecosystem, or not easily extensible. Additionally, there is no unified theoretical framework connecting stochastic gradient optimization and SGMCMC that would make Bayesian inference accessible to practitioners already working with standard optimizers, and deep ensembles lack a principled guarantee of convergence to the Bayesian posterior.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a PyTorch model (passed as a functional forward map), minibatches of training data, a prior distribution, and an arbitrary likelihood/loss function, with the log posterior normalized by N to keep the drift term scale-invariant. Outputs include Gaussian posterior approximations (Laplace, VI), Monte Carlo sample ensembles (serial or parallel SGMCMC), and a decomposition of predictive uncertainty into aleatoric and epistemic components. The functional API means the model is treated as a pure function of parameters and inputs, enabling easy parallelization across ensemble members and composition with PEFT/LoRA adapters for large language models.

## Method

The paper introduces a tempered SGMCMC framework where a temperature parameter T interpolates between optimization (T=0, yielding standard SGD variants) and exact Bayesian posterior sampling (T=N^{-1}), formalized through the Ma et al. (2015) SDE with symmetric positive-semidefinite diffusion D and skew-symmetric Q matrices. This yields a key corollary: adding the noise term √(2N^{-1}D)dw to the gradient descent ODE used in deep ensemble training produces parallel SGMCMC (Bayesian deep ensembles) that is asymptotically unbiased for the posterior, whereas standard deep ensembles only concentrate on posterior modes. The posteriors library implements this as a functional, minibatch-first PyTorch package with a swappable transform framework supporting Laplace (empirical/conditional Fisher via GGN), variational inference, SGLD, SGHMC, SGNHT, and their parallel variants, all sharing a unified optimizer-style API compatible with torchopt and the Hugging Face ecosystem.

## Results

For out-of-distribution detection with Llama 3 (8B) fine-tuned on TQA, Parallel SGHMC (Bayesian deep ensemble) achieves 0.95 AUROC using epistemic uncertainty, substantially outperforming SGD with total uncertainty (0.80 AUROC) and base Llama 3 (0.62 AUROC). In the cold posterior experiment on IMDB (CNN-LSTM, 2.7M params), VI and SGHMC significantly outperform MAP optimization on test loss, with a strong cold posterior effect (T<1) observed for Gaussian approximations but only a mild effect for SGMCMC. For continual learning with Llama 2 + LoRA on PG19, the Laplace posterior-as-prior approach maintains low loss on early episodes while online SGD exhibits catastrophic forgetting. On the Pima Indians logistic regression benchmark, posteriors' parallel SGHMC matches Pyro NUTS in posterior fidelity (KSD 2.690 vs 2.680) while running ~19× faster per chain (0.79s vs 15.38s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SGD (the standard optimization baseline), Parallel SGHMC with epistemic uncertainty achieves 0.95 AUROC for OOD detection versus SGD's 0.80 AUROC with total uncertainty, a 15-point improvement that the paper attributes to the Bayesian decomposition of uncertainty. Against base Llama 3 without fine-tuning, the Bayesian ensemble improves AUROC from 0.62 to 0.95 while incurring only a small loss penalty (4.40 vs 4.68 on English). Against serial SGHMC, the parallel (Bayesian deep ensemble) approach dramatically outperforms on epistemic uncertainty (0.95 vs 0.17 AUROC), which the paper attributes to serial chains getting trapped in single posterior modes. Against Pyro NUTS on the Pima Indians benchmark, posteriors' parallel SGHMC matches posterior fidelity (KSD 2.690 vs 2.680) at a fraction of the per-chain runtime (0.79s vs 15.38s), while posteriors' VI actually achieves a lower KSD (2.516) than NUTS. Against BlackJAX's full-batch SGHMC, posteriors' minibatched VI achieves a lower KSD (2.516 vs 2.689), demonstrating the advantage of minibatch-first design. In the cold posterior experiment, SGMCMC methods show only a mild cold posterior effect compared to the strong effect in Laplace and VI, supporting the hypothesis that the cold posterior is an artifact of Gaussian approximation crudeness rather than a fundamental Bayesian property.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies stochastic gradient optimization and SGMCMC under a single tempered SDE framework, building on the general SGMCMC recipe of Ma et al. (2015) and the specific algorithms of Welling & Teh (2011), Chen et al. (2014), and Ding et al. (2014). The key theoretical step is showing that setting temperature T=0 recovers standard SGD variants (including momentum-based methods), while T=N^{-1} recovers the Bayesian posterior target, yielding a simple modification to deep ensembles (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017) that makes them asymptotically unbiased for the posterior. On the software side, the paper positions posteriors against Stan, Pyro, and BlackJAX, differentiating itself as minibatch-first, PyTorch-native, and composable with the Hugging Face ecosystem. The cold posterior effect investigation extends the empirical findings of Wenzel et al. (2020) and Izmailov et al. (2021) by systematically comparing Gaussian and non-Gaussian approximations under a unified API.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: by reframing SGMCMC within a familiar optimization API, the paper lowers the barrier to Bayesian learning for the broader PyTorch/Hugging Face community, and the Llama 2/3 experiments demonstrate that these techniques are viable at the scale of modern LLMs. The finding that the cold posterior effect is largely an artifact of Gaussian approximations (VI, Laplace) rather than Bayesian learning per se is a useful clarification, though it was already anticipated by Izmailov et al. (2021). Open questions include whether the parallel SGMCMC approach scales to even larger models, how to integrate K-FAC for non-diagonal curvature, and whether the serial SGHMC failure in high-dimensional settings (AUROC 0.17 for epistemic uncertainty in the Llama 3 experiment) reflects a fundamental limitation of single-trajectory MCMC or a hyperparameter issue.
