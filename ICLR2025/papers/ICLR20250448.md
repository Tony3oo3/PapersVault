## Problem

While inference scaling laws have been extensively explored for LLMs and image diffusion models, video diffusion models (VDMs) remain constrained by their training data quality, producing low-fidelity videos with weak semantic faithfulness. Existing VDM-based training-free algorithms (e.g., FreeInit) have their performance ceiling bounded by the VDM itself, and prior attempts to leverage image diffusion models (IDMs) for video synthesis have yielded only minimal perceptible gains. The paper asks whether IDMs, trained on large-scale high-quality datasets, can be systematically integrated into the VDM sampling process to surpass the VDM's intrinsic quality limits.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, a pre-trained VDM (e.g., Animatediff at 512×512×16 frames, ModelScope-T2V at 256×256×16, VideoCrafterV2 at 512×320×16), and a corresponding IDM that shares the same VAE/latent space (e.g., SD V1.5 for Animatediff, Realistic Vision V6.0 B1 for VideoCrafterV2). During inference, the video latent of shape b×c×t×h×w is reshaped to (b×t)×c×h×w when passed through the IDM so each frame is processed independently. The output is a synthesized video with improved visual quality and prompt alignment while preserving temporal coherence; the method is entirely training-free and plug-and-play, requiring no architectural modifications to either model.

## Method

IV-mixed Sampler defines a base operator I that performs DDIM-Inversion (forward) with one model followed by DDIM (reverse) with another, injecting semantic information via classifier-free guidance scale differences (ω_back − ω_go > 0). The empirically optimal 'IV-IV' configuration applies this operator with the IDM for both the inversion and denoising steps at every timestep of the standard VDM sampling loop, effectively using the IDM to enhance per-frame quality while the VDM's temporal blocks maintain inter-frame coherence. Theoretically, the paper proves (Theorem 3.1) that IV-mixed Sampler reduces to a standard ODE whose score function is a weighted sum of the IDM and VDM score estimates, enabling principled trade-offs via dynamic CFG scales parameterized by a Karras-style noise schedule. A recursive multi-step formulation (Eq. 8) generalizes the single-step injection to N-step cascades, and ablations over C(4,2) model combinations confirm that using the IDM for both forward and reverse steps ('IV-IV') dominates all alternatives across UMT-FVD, UMTScore, and GPT4o-MTScore.

## Results

On Chronomagic-Bench-150, IV-mixed Sampler achieves UMT-FVD of 228.60 on Animatediff (SD V1.5, Motion Adapter V3), reducing the standard DDIM score of 275.18 by 46.58 points and approaching the closed-source Pika-2.0 score of 223.1. On the more comprehensive Chronomagic-Bench-1649, IV-mixed Sampler achieves the best UMT-FVD (192.72), UMTScore (3.39), and GPT4o-MTScore (3.35) across all three VDMs tested. On UCF-101-FVD, ModelScope-T2V with IV-mixed Sampler reduces FVD from 1492.16 to 841.65 (StyleGAN), a 650.51-point improvement. On VBench, IV-mixed Sampler improves Animatediff's Multiple Objects score from 36.88% to 58.46% and its average score from 60.19% to 66.69%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FreeInit, IV-mixed Sampler achieves a substantially lower UMT-FVD on Animatediff (228.60 vs. 268.31) and VideoCrafterV2 (210.58 vs. no FreeInit entry, but 3.48 below standard), while on ModelScope-T2V FreeInit retains a UMT-FVD advantage (220.96 vs. 234.90) due to the lack of a high-quality matching IDM for that model's low 224×224 resolution; however, IV-mixed Sampler still wins on UMTScore and GPT4o-MTScore. Against I4VGen, IV-mixed Sampler is marginally worse on UMT-FVD for Animatediff on Chronomagic-Bench-150 (228.60 vs. 227.22) but decisively outperforms it on UMTScore (3.32 vs. 2.68) and GPT4o-MTScore (3.55 vs. 3.01); on the larger Chronomagic-Bench-1649, IV-mixed Sampler beats I4VGen on all three metrics (192.72 vs. 206.22 UMT-FVD, 3.39 vs. 3.21 UMTScore, 3.35 vs. 3.09 GPT4o-MTScore). Against UniCtrl on UCF-101-FVD, IV-mixed Sampler achieves 800.47 (StyleGAN) versus UniCtrl's 1859.11, a more than 2× improvement. Overall, IV-mixed Sampler achieves state-of-the-art results on 4 of 5 benchmarks and is competitive on the fifth, demonstrating strong plug-and-play generalization across VideoCrafterV2, ModelScope-T2V, and Animatediff.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the inference-scaling paradigm from LLMs and image diffusion models to video diffusion, but departs from prior VDM-specific inference methods (FreeInit, I4VGen) by explicitly introducing a separate, higher-quality IDM as a score-function assistant rather than relying solely on the VDM's own capacity. The technical mechanism builds on SDEdit's add-noise-then-denoise idea and DDIM/DDIM-Inversion as a deterministic bridge, but replaces the stochastic noise injection of SDEdit with DDIM-Inversion to avoid the temporal incoherence that random noise introduces. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 3.1) positions the entire multi-step IV-mixed procedure as equivalent to a single ODE with a weighted combination of IDM and VDM score functions, grounding the heuristic in the score-based generative modeling framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a fundamental asymmetry: VDMs are bottlenecked by the scarcity of high-quality video training data, while IDMs benefit from the mature image dataset ecosystem, and provides a training-free mechanism to transfer IDM quality into VDM sampling. The 5× NFE overhead (50→250 steps, 21s→92s on a single GPU) and the shared-VAE requirement are significant practical constraints that limit immediate deployment, though the authors outline distillation and VAE-agnostic workarounds (e.g., the Mochi-1 adaptation). An open question is whether the ODE formulation enables more efficient sampling schedules or whether the gains can be distilled into the base VDM at training time, potentially making the approach a stepping stone rather than a final solution.
