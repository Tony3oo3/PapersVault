## Problem

Current multimodal large language models (MLLMs) act as visual agents that generate direct answers without recognizing when a query requires more deliberate visual reasoning, leading to hallucinations and overconfident errors on complex or ambiguous questions. The absence of an explicit mechanism to distinguish between simple (intuitive) and complex (analytical) cognitive tasks means these agents cannot acknowledge their own uncertainty or seek additional visual evidence before responding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250258/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes an RGB image (resized or padded to 336×336 pixels) and a free-form text question or instruction. In fast mode it returns a direct textual answer; in slow mode it additionally produces a structured chain of evidence comprising identified missing objects, contextual clues, a region-of-interest bounding box, and a pixel-level segmentation mask, before synthesizing the final answer. The chain of evidence serves as an interpretable, neuro-symbolic intermediate output that humans can visually inspect.

## Method

FAST builds on LLaVA-v1.5 (CLIP-ViT-L-336px encoder + Vicuna-7B LLM) and introduces three lightweight LoRA adapters that implement a dynamic System 1/System 2 switch. The Switch Adapter, fine-tuned on a 100K negative dataset where target objects are deliberately absent or smaller than 20×20 pixels, learns to detect uncertainty and output missing objects plus contextual clues that trigger slow thinking. In System 2 mode, a Proposal Adapter narrows the search from image-level to a bounding-box-level region using the context clues, and a Seg Adapter (SAM-based decoder fine-tuned on segmentation datasets) produces a pixel-level mask for the target, forming a hierarchical image→box→pixel chain of evidence. The final answer is generated by feeding the original image, question, and the full chain of evidence back through the LLM, with a cross-attention visual resampler compressing 256 image tokens to 32 to fit the context window.

## Results

On VQA and multimodal benchmarks, FAST (built on LLaVA-v1.5/Vicuna-7B) achieves 80.8% accuracy on VQA-v2, 63.8% on GQA, 60.7% on TextVQA, 68.9% on ScienceQA, 86.4 F1 on POPE, 1517.4 on MME, 60.1 on SEED, and 31.0 on MM-Vet, outperforming the LLaVA-v1.5 baseline by +2.3, +1.8, +2.5, +2.1, +0.4, +6.7, +1.5, and +0.5 respectively. On reasoning segmentation (ReasonSeg), FAST achieves 47.6 CIoU and 48.7 GIoU, surpassing LISA-7B by +3.2 CIoU and +2.7 GIoU. In runtime analysis, FAST runs 31% faster than System 2-only on MME and 50% faster on GQA while maintaining comparable accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline LLaVA-v1.5 (same Vicuna-7B backbone), FAST wins on all eight benchmarks, with the largest gains on MME (+6.7) and TextVQA (+2.5%). Compared to Chain of Spot (the closest competitor using the same LLM), FAST edges ahead on VQA-v2 (80.8 vs 80.7), GQA (63.8 vs 63.7), ScienceQA (68.9 vs 68.2), MME (1517.4 vs 1501.1), SEED (60.1 vs 59.7), and MM-Vet (31.0 vs 30.8), while slightly trailing on TextVQA (60.7 vs 60.9) and tying on POPE (86.4). Against mPLUG-Owl2, FAST wins on VQA-v2, GQA, TextVQA, and MME but loses on SEED (60.1 vs 61.6) and MM-Vet (31.0 vs 36.2). On segmentation, FAST outperforms LISA-7B on refCOCO+ (64.4 vs 62.4 CIoU), refCOCOg (67.0 vs 66.4), and ReasonSeg (47.6 vs 44.4 CIoU; 48.7 vs 46.0 GIoU), but trails slightly on refCOCO (73.3 vs 74.1). In the EVA2-CLIP-E encoder comparison (Fig. 4), FAST with the stronger encoder further narrows the gap with CoVLM and CogCoM, confirming that the framework's gains are not solely attributable to the vision encoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

FAST builds directly on LLaVA-v1.5 as its base visual agent, LISA for its segmentation adapter, and V* for negative training data that triggers the slow-thinking mode. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant end-to-end instruction-tuning paradigm (which the authors characterize as purely 'System 1') and from tool-using agents that invoke external perception tools, instead proposing an internal, adaptive switch between fast and slow cognitive modes. The 'chain of evidence' mechanism extends chain-of-thought reasoning from purely textual intermediate steps to visual-symbolic ones (bounding boxes, segmentation masks), positioning the work at the intersection of neuro-symbolic AI and multimodal LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dynamic System 1/System 2 switching offers a practical path to reducing hallucination in MLLMs without incurring the full computational cost of always-on deliberative reasoning (31–50% faster than System 2-only), while the neuro-symbolic intermediate outputs provide a degree of interpretability that black-box MLLMs lack. However, the framework's reliance on a hand-constructed negative dataset for the switch adapter limits its coverage of real-world uncertainty, and the pixel-level decoder still struggles with highly textured or occluded objects. Open questions include whether the switch mechanism can be learned end-to-end without curated negative data, whether the chain-of-evidence approach generalizes to video or 3D inputs, and how the framework scales to open-vocabulary or compositional reasoning beyond the COCO-centric training data.
