## Problem

While general-purpose text-to-image models have been extensively benchmarked, their ability to generate scientifically accurate visualizations—requiring precise spatial composition, exact numeric representations, and domain-specific object conventions—remains largely unexplored. Existing benchmarks target real-world images or use automated metrics (CLIPScore, FID) that show poor correlation with human judgment in the scientific domain, leaving no structured evaluation of how well multimodal LLMs handle the unique precision demands of scientific figures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250255/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each prompt consists of an auxiliary instruction (constraining output to direct image, Python code, or TikZ code) followed by a generation query specifying objects, attributes, spatial relations, and numeric quantities. Prompts are provided in four languages (English, German, Chinese, Farsi), and code outputs must be compiled to produce the final image—compilation failures are penalized with a score of 0. The 404 prompts span 9 object categories (2D/3D shapes, charts, graph theory, matrices, real-life objects, tables, annotations, functions) and 7 understanding configurations (single and combined dimensions).

## Method

ScImage constructs prompts via a structured dictionary D (objects, colors, spatial relations, numeric values) populated from DaTikZ and manually curated, combined with 101 query templates T to yield 404 diverse, traceable generation queries. Seven models are evaluated in two output modes: direct raster generation (DALL-E, Stable Diffusion) and code-mediated generation (GPT-4o, Llama 3.1 8B, AutoTikZ, OpenAI o1, Qwen2.5-Coder producing Python or TikZ). Evaluation is conducted by a panel of 11 scientists (Ph.D. students, postdocs, faculty) who rate each image on a 1–5 scale across correctness, relevance, and scientific style, with at least two annotators per instance and a calibration session to ensure inter-annotator agreement (weighted kappa ≥ 0.41). The key finding is that code-based outputs substantially outperform direct image generation in scientific style, spatial understanding is the weakest dimension for code-based models, numeric understanding is weakest for image-based models, and all models degrade significantly when multiple understanding dimensions must be combined simultaneously.

## Results

On the ScImage benchmark (404 generation queries, 11 human evaluators), GPT-4o in text-code-image mode achieves the highest scores across all three criteria: Correctness 3.51 (Python) / 3.50 (TikZ), Relevance 3.67 (TikZ) / 3.40 (Python), and Scientific Style 3.93 (Python) / 3.75 (TikZ), all on a 1–5 scale. No model exceeds 4.0 on any dimension, and all models struggle most with combined understanding types (Attribute & Numerical & Spatial averaging only 2.21 across all models). Code-based outputs (Python/TikZ) substantially outperform direct raster generation on Scientific Style (>2.5 vs. <2.0), while direct image models (DALL-E, Stable Diffusion) are weakest on Numeric understanding (scores below 1.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o outperforms all other models by a large margin on Correctness (>1.3 points above the second-best, Llama_python at 2.10) and Relevance (>1.1 points above Llama_python at 2.54). Among code-based models, GPT-4o_python edges out GPT-4o_tikz on Correctness (3.51 vs. 3.50) and Scientific Style (3.93 vs. 3.75), while GPT-4o_tikz leads on Relevance (3.67 vs. 3.40). Llama 3.1 8B performs considerably better with Python than TikZ output (Correctness 2.10 vs. 1.78), and its low scores are largely driven by a ~28% compile error rate; ignoring compile errors, Llama_python improves to 2.92 Correctness. AutoTikZ, fine-tuned specifically for TikZ, achieves the lowest compile error rate (4%) but still trails GPT-4o by over 1.4 points on Correctness. Direct image models (DALL-E, Stable Diffusion) are consistently the weakest on Scientific Style (1.55 and 1.96 respectively), confirming that code-as-intermediate offers a significant advantage for scientific figure generation. In the multilingual subset (Table 5), OpenAI o1-preview surpasses GPT-4o in English (e.g., 4.28 vs. 3.38 Correctness with Python) but degrades substantially in German and Chinese, while GPT-4o often performs better in non-English languages.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of text-to-image generation and scientific visualization. While prior benchmarks like T2I-CompBench evaluate compositional text-to-image generation for general images, and narrower works like DaTikZ, VGBench, and ChartMimic target specific output formats (TikZ, SVG, Python chart code), none provide a structured, multi-dimensional evaluation of scientific figure generation. ScImage departs from these by unifying code-based (Python, TikZ) and direct raster image generation under a single evaluation framework, and by explicitly decomposing performance along spatial, numeric, and attribute understanding dimensions — a structured taxonomy absent from prior scientific figure benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even the strongest model (GPT-4o) scores below 4/5 on correctness, and that all models struggle substantially with combined understanding dimensions, highlighting a significant gap between current capabilities and the precision required for scientific communication. The finding that code-based outputs consistently outperform direct image generation in scientific style, while image models struggle most with numeric understanding and code models with spatial understanding, provides actionable direction for future model development. The ~3k human-annotated images serve as a valuable ground truth for developing automated evaluation metrics, and the multilingual analysis (showing open-source models like Llama degrade sharply in non-English settings) raises important accessibility concerns for global scientific communities.
