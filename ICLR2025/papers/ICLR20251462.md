## Problem

Standard speculative decoding verifies draft tokens solely by their probability alignment with the target model, rejecting many objectively correct continuations that are merely phrased differently. The authors demonstrate that even GPT-4o outputs and human-written text achieve acceptance lengths of only ~2 tokens under greedy verification, capping the number of useful draft tokens at M≤7 and severely limiting achievable speedups.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251462/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of M candidate tokens from a draft model (e.g., Llama-8B) and the corresponding last-hidden-layer embeddings (pre-RMSNorm) produced by the target model (e.g., Llama-405B) during its parallel forward pass. A 16.4k-parameter linear head maps each embedding to a scalar correctness score, which is combined via logical OR with the standard SD accept/reject mask to yield the final per-token decision. The output is the accepted token sequence appended to the context, with a bonus token sampled from the target distribution when all M candidates are accepted.

## Method

The key insight is that a target LLM's hidden-state embedding of an erroneous token carries a detectable 'error signal'—the model immediately attempts to correct the mistake in subsequent tokens. The authors exploit this by training a single linear classifier on top of the target's last-layer embeddings using a 500-example dataset of question/correct-answer/wrong-answer triples (sourced from Alpaca, ARC, and newly created prompts), where every token up to the first error is labeled positive and every subsequent token is labeled negative. Training uses weighted cross-entropy (20× upweighting on negatives) with AdamW for under 1.5 hours, freezing all target parameters. At inference, a candidate token is accepted if either standard SD verification passes OR the judge head outputs σ(f_judge(e_i)) > 0.5, making the judge a strict relaxation of the original criterion. This allows drafting M=25 tokens (vs. M≤7 in prior work) while preserving target-model accuracy on GSM8K, HumanEval, MT-Bench, ARC, and MMLU.

## Results

The 8B/405B-Judge achieves an average acceptance length (m*) of 19.7 tokens, a 9.7× speedup over standard decoding in HuggingFace, and 129.3 tokens/s in the optimized gpt-fast framework on 8 H100s. For the 8B/70B pair, judge decoding reaches m*=18.8, a 3× speedup in gpt-fast, and 141.8 tokens/s on 2 H100s. Critically, judge decoding preserves target-model accuracy across GSM8K, MT-Bench, HumanEval, ARC, and MMLU, while the top-K heuristic baseline degrades significantly. The method requires training only 16.4k parameters (a single linear head) on 500 annotated examples in under 1.5 hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard speculative decoding (Leviathan et al., 2023), judge decoding more than triples the acceptance length (from ~6 to ~19–20 tokens) and nearly doubles the gpt-fast speedup (1.7×→3× for 70B; 1.78×→3.9× for 405B), while preserving target-model accuracy on all benchmarks. Compared to Eagle-2 (Li et al., 2024b), judge decoding achieves a substantially higher acceptance length (18.8 vs. 4.5 for 70B) and a larger gpt-fast speedup (3× vs. 1.9×), though Eagle-2 retains an edge in HuggingFace (3.3× vs. 2×) due to its lightweight draft head. Against Medusa (Cai et al., 2024), judge decoding wins on acceptance length (19.7 vs. <6) and HuggingFace speedup (9.7× vs. <6×) for the 405B target; in gpt-fast, judge decoding reaches 129.3 tokens/s versus Medusa's 108 tokens/s, though Medusa benefits from faster H200 hardware with NVLink. The paper also shows that a simple top-K verification heuristic (training-free) fails to improve over the draft model even at K=5, underscoring that the learned judge head is necessary for meaningful gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the speculative decoding framework (Leviathan et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2023) but departs from its core verification mechanism: rather than improving draft-target alignment (as in DistillSpec, EAGLE, Medusa) or increasing the number of parallel guesses (tree-based methods), it replaces the probability-ratio acceptance test with a learned judge head over target-model token embeddings. The conceptual inspiration comes from the LLM-as-a-judge paradigm (Zheng et al., 2023), repurposed into a 16.4k-parameter linear classifier that predicts per-token correctness, and from prior work showing that hidden-layer embeddings encode latent knowledge and error signals (Burns et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2023a). The paper explicitly positions itself as changing the verification scheme itself, in contrast to all prior SD work that keeps the acceptance criterion fixed and optimizes the drafter or the candidate set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 9× speedup on Llama-405B (129 tokens/s on gpt-fast) is a substantial practical win for deploying frontier-scale models, and the method is remarkably cheap to train (500 annotated examples, <1.5 hours, frozen target). However, the loss of the mathematical guarantee that output matches the target distribution is a fundamental trade-off, and the out-of-distribution experiment (86.6→80.4% on HumanEval when coding data is excluded) shows the judge does not fully transfer across task domains without re-annotation. Open questions include whether the judge can be made distribution-free, whether the safety concern of accepting tokens the target would never generate is real in practice, and whether the approach composes with other SD optimizations (quantization, tree attention) without further quality loss.
