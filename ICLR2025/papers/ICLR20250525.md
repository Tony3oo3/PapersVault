## Problem

Existing molecular docking methods face a fundamental trade-off: rigid-protein approaches (FABind, DiffDock) ignore conformational changes that are critical in real binding, while flexible docking methods (DynamicBind, ReDock) rely on diffusion-based sampling that is computationally expensive. No regression-based method had been explored for the blind flexible docking setting, where both the binding pocket location is unknown and the protein must adapt its structure, leaving a gap for a solution that is simultaneously fast and accurate.

## Input / Output

The method takes an apo ligand (3D coordinates randomly initialized via RDKit's ETKDG algorithm, positioned at the protein center) and an apo protein (AlphaFold2-predicted Cα coordinates with ESM-2 residue features) as input, and produces holo ligand atom coordinates, holo pocket residue coordinates, and a binary per-residue pocket classification as output. The protein graph connects residues within 8 Å and ligand-protein interface edges within 10 Å, while the pocket is delineated by a 20 Å radius. The iterative update mechanism (default K=6) refines only coordinates while keeping node features fixed, and gradients are recorded only at the final iteration to reduce memory overhead.

## Method

FABFlex decomposes blind flexible docking into three subtasks handled by specialized modules—pocket prediction (binary classification), ligand docking (coordinate regression), and pocket docking (coordinate regression)—all built from stacked FABind layers, which are E(3)-equivariant GNNs tailored for heterogeneous ligand-protein graphs. A key architectural innovation is the iterative update mechanism that feeds predicted ligand and pocket coordinates back into their respective docking modules across K iterations, enabling mutual structural refinement that mimics the physical docking process. Training uses a multi-task loss combining weighted BCE for pocket classification, Huber losses for ligand and pocket coordinates, and an MSE distance-map auxiliary loss, with a two-stage pretraining curriculum (rigid ligand prediction, then rigid pocket prediction) followed by joint flexible training. A partial teacher-forcing strategy (probability p=0.5) gradually transitions the model from relying on ground-truth pocket sites to its own predictions. By directly regressing bound structures in a single forward pass without sampling or external pocket-detection tools, FABFlex achieves 208× speedup over DynamicBind while improving ligand RMSD < 2 Å from 33.00% to 40.59%.

## Results

On PDBBind v2020 (303 test complexes), FABFlex achieves a ligand RMSD < 2 Å rate of 40.59% (all cases) and 32.46% (unseen protein receptors), outperforming all baselines. The pocket RMSD mean is 1.10 Å with a median of 0.63 Å. Inference takes only 0.49 seconds per complex, approximately 208× faster than the state-of-the-art flexible docking method DynamicBind (102.12 s). On the more rigorous unseen-protein subset, FABFlex's 32.46% < 2 Å rate exceeds the next-best method (DiffDock-L at 29.82%) by over 2 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary flexible docking competitor DynamicBind, FABFlex wins decisively on ligand accuracy (40.59% vs 33.00% < 2 Å) and runtime (0.49 s vs 102.12 s, a 208× speedup), but loses on pocket RMSD (1.10 Å vs 0.84 Å mean), as DynamicBind's diffusion model adjusts the entire protein rather than just the pocket. Among rigid deep-learning methods, FABFlex outperforms the strongest baseline FABind+ (35.64% < 2 Å) by nearly 5 percentage points and DiffDock-L (36.75%) by over 3.8 points, while also being ~120× faster than DiffDock-L. On the unseen-protein generalization subset, FABFlex leads all methods at 32.46% < 2 Å, with every competitor falling below 30%. Against traditional docking software (Vina, Glide, Gnina), FABFlex dominates on both accuracy and speed, achieving 40.59% < 2 Å versus at most 19.32% for Gnina, while running in 0.49 s versus 146–1405 s. In the pocket-based flexible docking setting (Appendix C.10), FABFlex matches ReDock(40) at 42.24% vs 42.90% < 2 Å while being over 100× faster (0.47 s vs 58 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

FABFlex extends the FABind/FABind+ series (Pei et al., 2024; Gao et al., 2024) by replacing their rigid-protein assumption with a flexible docking formulation, while retaining the FABind layer (an improved EGNN) as the core architectural primitive. It departs from the dominant diffusion-based flexible docking paradigm (DynamicBind, ReDock, PackDock) by demonstrating that a regression-based, single-pass approach can handle protein conformational changes, and it integrates pocket prediction end-to-end rather than relying on external tools like P2Rank. The iterative update mechanism between ligand and pocket docking modules is conceptually inspired by AlphaFold2's iterative refinement, but applied to coordinate exchange between two separate subtask modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 208× speed advantage over DynamicBind while achieving superior ligand RMSD < 2Å (40.59% vs. 33.00%) makes large-scale virtual screening of molecule-protein pairs practically feasible, which is critical for drug discovery pipelines. However, FABFlex's pocket RMSD (1.10 Å) remains worse than DynamicBind (0.84 Å), and its predicted structures exhibit higher steric clash scores and lower PoseBuster physical validity than diffusion-based methods, indicating that regression-based flexible docking has not yet fully captured the physical realism of generative approaches. Open questions include whether sampling-based refinements can be hybridized with the regression framework, and whether the approach extends to multi-site docking scenarios.
