## Problem

Point-based 3D object completion methods achieve high quality but assume canonical coordinate alignment (fixed scale, pose, and center), making them unsuitable for objects embedded in scenes where pose and scale are arbitrary. Existing instance scene completion methods (RfD-Net, DIMR) handle arbitrary pose but produce lower-fidelity completions and, critically, do not incorporate known scene constraints such as other observed surfaces, free space, or occluded space, leading to collisions and physically implausible completions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a partial scene point cloud at 2 cm resolution (generated by backprojecting depth maps from 10 camera viewpoints) along with estimated surface normals oriented toward the camera. A Mask3D instance segmentation network decomposes the scene into individual object instances, each of which is independently completed. The output is a set of watertight 3D meshes (reconstructed via NKSR from 2048-point completions and predicted surface normals) placed back in world coordinates, forming a labeled instance scene with complete geometry. Scene constraints are derived as two sparse bounding shells (free space and occluded space) at 10 cm resolution offset ±2 cm from the partial scan surface along its normals.

## Method

The core architecture replaces SeedFormer's local-attention seed generator with a global-attention design that first predicts an object center via a learnable token and then regresses patch seed coordinates as offsets from that center, making completion robust to arbitrary pose and scale. The partial encoder uses VI-PointConv layers (rotation- and scale-invariant position embeddings) plus surface normals as input to improve robustness. Scene constraints—sparse point clouds encoding known free and occluded space—are integrated into the seed generator via multi-head cross-attention, where partial object features act as queries and constraint points (with learned free/occluded embeddings) act as key-value pairs, enabling the model to avoid generating geometry in occupied or visible-free regions. A coarse-to-fine decoder applies three upsampling layers (256→512→1024→2048 points), each augmented with global self-attention alongside local attention to encourage coherent fine-grained geometry. The model is pre-trained on 34 ShapeNet categories under canonical alignment, then fine-tuned on the new ScanWCF dataset (1202 watertight, collision-free indoor scenes) with the full scene-aware pipeline.

## Results

On the instance scene completion task (Table 1), the proposed method achieves 62.77 mAP on IoU@0.25 and 47.55 mAP on IoU@0.5, substantially outperforming RfD-Net (38.78 / 7.25) and DIMR (34.18 / 8.67). On the isolated scene completion task with ground-truth instances (Table 2), the method achieves a Chamfer Distance of 20.74 (vs. 38.36 for RfD-Net and 39.04 for DIMR) and reduces average collision distance (COL) to 2.67, a 3–4× improvement over both baselines. The advantage is most pronounced at harder thresholds: at IoU@0.5 the gap exceeds 40 points, and at CD@0.047 the method scores 56.07 versus 31.32 (RfD-Net) and 23.30 (DIMR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior instance scene completion methods: RfD-Net (Nie et al., CVPR 2021) and DIMR (Tang et al., ECCV 2022). The proposed method wins on virtually every metric in both the joint instance scene completion task and the isolated scene completion task. The most dramatic gap appears at harder thresholds: on IoU@0.5 the method scores 47.55 versus 7.25 (RfD-Net) and 8.67 (DIMR), a >40-point margin, indicating that baselines can only produce coarse shape approximations while the proposed method captures fine geometric detail. On the scene completion task with ground-truth instances, the method reduces Chamfer Distance from ~38–39 to 20.74 and cuts average collision distance (COL) from 8.58–10.42 to 2.67, a 3–4× improvement in plausibility. The only near-tie is CD@0.1 in the instance task (61.57 vs. RfD-Net's 61.68), where both methods perform similarly at the easier threshold. Pre-training the object completion model on ShapeNet provides a modest but consistent boost at harder thresholds (e.g., LFD@2500: 29.03 vs. 24.57 without pre-training), confirming that shape priors help hallucinate missing structures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: point-based object completion (SeedFormer, Khademi & Fuxin 2024) which achieves high-quality completions but assumes canonical coordinates, and instance scene completion (RfD-Net, DIMR, DDIT) which handles objects in scenes but relies on voxel-based or template-based methods with lower geometric fidelity. The key architectural departure is replacing SeedFormer's local-attention seed generator with a global-attention design that predicts an object center and seed offsets, combined with VI-PointConv for rotation/scale invariance, enabling the point-based pipeline to operate at arbitrary pose and scale. The introduction of sparse scene constraints (free/occluded space shells) integrated via cross-attention is a novel mechanism for incorporating scene context into point-based completion, filling a gap the authors note no prior work has addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The large performance gap over prior instance scene completion methods (e.g., ~6× improvement in IoU@0.5) highlights that the field has been limited by the architectural mismatch between high-quality point-based completion and scene-level reasoning. The ScanWCF dataset addresses a practical evaluation problem—prior datasets (Scan2CAD, ScanARCW) have misaligned or colliding ground truth that makes metrics unreliable—making it a useful resource for the community. Key limitations include the deterministic nature of the model (single completion per input), dependence on upstream instance segmentation quality, and inability to reason about collisions in regions that will be filled by other objects' completions. The authors identify incorporating generative models for diverse completions as a natural next step.
