## Problem

Training-free guided generation with pretrained diffusion models requires accurate estimates of the denoiser covariance Cov[x₀|xₜ] to compute the conditional score, but existing methods either demand expensive Hessian or Jacobian computations, require retraining or architectural changes, or rely on crude diagonal/identity approximations that introduce significant bias. The paper shows analytically that these simplistic covariance choices cause the guidance magnitude to scale with data dimensionality, producing implausibly large updates that force ad-hoc clipping and degrade reconstruction quality, especially at low step counts.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a degraded measurement y, a linear measurement operator A (Gaussian blur, motion blur, random mask, or 4× downsample), a pretrained unconditional denoiser (Dhariwal & Nichol on ImageNet 256×256), and a data covariance estimated from the training set and projected onto a DCT-diagonal basis. It produces a restored clean image via standard ODE/SDE sampling (Euler or Heun) with 15–100 steps, while internally maintaining the denoiser covariance in a memory-efficient diagonal-plus-low-rank form (D + UUᵀ − VVᵀ) that is updated at every denoiser call without any architectural modification to the pretrained model.

## Method

Free Hunch (FH) integrates two free sources of covariance information: the data covariance from training and the implicit curvature along the generative trajectory, linked through the second-order Tweedie formula. A *time update* propagates the covariance across noise levels using a locally Gaussian (second-order Taylor) approximation of the forward diffusion, yielding the simple inverse-covariance rule Σ⁻¹ ← Σ⁻¹ + Δ(σ⁻²)I. A *space update* then refines the covariance at each new spatial location via a BFGS-style low-rank correction, exploiting the identity that Cov[x₀|xₜ] equals the Jacobian of σ²·E[x₀|xₜ] with respect to xₜ, so consecutive denoiser evaluations provide the secant information needed. For high-dimensional images the covariance is stored as a diagonal matrix plus two N×k low-rank outer-product terms, and all matrix inversions reduce to k×k operations via the Woodbury identity. The covariance is initialized from the training-data covariance in the DCT basis, and during reconstruction guidance the full Jacobian ∇μ₀|ₜ is used when affordable, with a fallback to Σ₀|ₜ/σ² when the resulting guidance norm exceeds the data range, preventing trajectory blow-up without post-hoc clipping.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 with a 15-step Heun solver, FH+online achieves the best PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across all four inverse problems (Gaussian deblurring, random inpainting, motion deblurring, 4× super-resolution). The largest gains appear on inpainting, where FH+online reaches 25.25 PSNR / 0.728 SSIM / 0.317 LPIPS versus 22.23 / 0.579 / 0.489 for the next-best baseline (Peng Convert). At 30 steps, FH+online maintains its lead (e.g., 26.18 PSNR on inpainting vs. 24.84 for DPS), and the method requires no post-hoc guidance scaling (optimal scale = 1.0) unlike identity-covariance baselines that need tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FH and FH+online outperform all six baselines (DPS, IIGDM, TMPD, Peng Convert, Peng Analytic, and the identity-covariance ablations) on every metric across all four inverse problems at both 15 and 30 Heun steps. The advantage is most pronounced at 15 steps: on random inpainting, FH+online achieves 25.25 PSNR versus 22.23 for Peng Convert (+3.0 dB) and 20.68 for DPS (+4.6 dB); on motion deblurring, FH+online reaches 21.84 PSNR versus 17.02 for DPS (+4.8 dB). At 30 steps the gap narrows slightly on Gaussian deblurring (FH+online 23.62 vs. Peng Convert 23.61 PSNR, essentially tied) but remains large on inpainting (26.18 vs. 24.84 for DPS) and motion deblurring (21.88 vs. 21.99 for Peng, a near-tie). In the extended Euler-solver comparison (Table 2), FH also beats DDNM+ on Gaussian blur (23.57 vs. 7.21 PSNR at 15 steps) and DiffPIR on most tasks, though DDNM+ retains an edge on inpainting/super-resolution PSNR due to its null-space projection design. A key practical advantage is that FH requires no post-hoc guidance scaling (optimal scale = 1.0), whereas DPS and identity-covariance methods need task-specific hyperparameter tuning to avoid trajectory blow-up.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits within the training-free conditional generation framework for diffusion models (DPS, IIGDM, TMPD, Peng et al.), where all methods share the same reconstruction guidance formula (Eq. 23) but differ in their denoiser covariance approximations. FH departs from prior work by repurposing two ideas from unrelated fields: (i) the Fokker-Planck equation to derive closed-form time updates for the denoiser covariance, and (ii) BFGS quasi-Newton updates from optimization to incorporate curvature information from consecutive denoiser evaluations during sampling. Unlike training-based methods (Peng Convert) or expensive Jacobian-based methods (TMPD), FH requires no additional training, no architectural changes, and no score Jacobian computations, while still adapting to the specific input and noise level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear theoretical diagnosis of why heuristic diagonal covariances (used in DPS and IIGDM) cause guidance to scale with pixel count N, leading to implausibly large corrections that are then masked by clipping—introducing systematic bias. This insight generalizes beyond the specific baselines and applies to any method using simplified covariance in the reconstruction guidance formula. Limitations include the focus on linear inverse problems, the DCT-diagonal initialization that may not transfer to non-image data, and the lack of formal error bounds on the estimated covariance. Future work on nonlinear inverse problems, PCA-based initialization for other modalities, and rigorous convergence guarantees would strengthen the framework's applicability.
