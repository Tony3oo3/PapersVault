## Problem

Existing One-Shot Federated Learning (OFL) methods either depend on Knowledge Distillation with server-side training and public datasets, or rely on neuron matching and model fusion that degrade under heterogeneous data distributions. No prior OFL approach combines data-free operation, elimination of server-side training, and robustness to non-IID data while remaining computationally feasible for resource-constrained edge devices.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251879/cropped_page5_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

Inputs are booleanized images (binary literals from pixel thresholds or adaptive Gaussian thresholding; CIFAR-10 additionally uses 3×3 and 4×4 color thermometer encodings) processed by Convolutional Tsetlin Machines with patch-based convolution. The server produces φ aggregated TM models (φ < number of clients J), each containing class-specific clause weights and TopK-combined TA states, rather than a single fused model. Inference is a bitwise OR/AND operation over clause literals followed by argmax over class sums, making the output structure fundamentally different from a single DNN weight vector.

## Method

FedTMOS replaces DNNs with Convolutional Tsetlin Machines locally, then performs a two-stage data-free server aggregation: (1) each client's class-specific clause weights are scaled by the mean normalized Gini index to correct for class imbalance, then grouped via k-means clustering; (2) a greedy reassignment algorithm distributes class weights from clusters into φ server models by maximizing the average squared distance between class centroids within each model (inter-class separation). TA states are aggregated independently using a TopK=2 bitwise OR over the two clients with the most samples per class, preventing overly inclusive state representations. The entire pipeline requires no synthetic data, no public dataset, and no server-side training, reducing upload costs by ≥2.3× and server aggregation latency by ≥75× compared to KD-based OFL methods.

## Results

FedTMOS achieves an average accuracy improvement of 7.22% over the leading state-of-the-art OFL baselines and 12.79% over the best data-free OFL methods across MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, SVHN, and CIFAR-10 under both Dirichlet (α=0.05, 0.1, 0.3) and subset (S(2), S(3), S(4)) non-IID partitions. Specifically, FedTMOS outperforms the strongest baseline (Distilled-FedOV) by 13.3%, 3.67%, 3.13%, and 8.82% on MNIST, F-MNIST, SVHN, and CIFAR-10 respectively. The method also reduces upload communication costs by at least 2.3× and server aggregation latency by up to 75× compared to methods requiring server-side training (e.g., 0.48 s vs. 647 s on MNIST).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedTMOS outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and partition setting. Against the strongest KD-based baseline, Distilled-FedOV (Diao et al., ICLR 2023), FedTMOS leads by 13.3% on MNIST, 3.67% on F-MNIST, 3.13% on SVHN, and 8.82% on CIFAR-10, while requiring no public dataset or server-side training. Among data-free KD methods, DENSE (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2022) and Co-Boosting (Dai et al., ICLR 2024) show high variance under non-IID splits (e.g., DENSE drops to 15.73% on CIFAR-10 S(2)), whereas FedTMOS maintains stable performance (39.95%). Among server-training-free methods, OT-Fusion (Singh & Jaggi, NeurIPS 2020) is the closest competitor on CIFAR-10 Dir(0.3) (49.43% vs. 57.03% for FedTMOS) and even achieves slightly lower server latency on CIFAR-10 (2.04 s vs. 3.24 s), but FedTMOS still leads by ~20% accuracy on that dataset. RegMean (Jin et al., ICLR 2023) and FedFisher (Jhunjhunwala et al.) trail FedTMOS by 10–20+ points on most settings. FedAvg (McMahan et al., AISTATS 2016) and Fed-OneShot (Guha et al., 2019) serve as simple iterative/ensemble baselines and are consistently the weakest, with FedTMOS exceeding them by 20–70 points depending on the partition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Tsetlin Machine (TM) line of work from iterative federated learning (FedTM, How et al. 2023) to the one-shot FL setting, replacing the multi-round aggregation with a single-round, data-free procedure. It departs from the dominant OFL paradigm of knowledge distillation (Guha et al. 2019; Diao et al. 2023; Dai et al. 2024) and from neuron-matching/fusion methods (Singh & Jaggi 2020; Jin et al. 2023) by exploiting the TM's class-specific clause weights: rather than aligning or distilling, it clusters scaled weights via k-means and greedily reassigns them to a fixed number of server models to maximize inter-class separation. The TM Composites framework (Granmo 2023) provides the ensemble baseline (FedTMOS-ensemble) that the proposed reassignment strategy improves upon, while the Weighted TM (Phoulady 2020) and Convolutional TM (Granmo 2019) supply the underlying model architecture and image-processing pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedTMOS offers a practically significant efficiency win for OFL: eliminating server-side training yields a 75× reduction in aggregation latency and a 2.3× reduction in upload costs, making one-shot aggregation feasible on bandwidth-constrained edge servers. However, the approach is constrained by the TM's booleanization, which limits absolute accuracy on harder datasets (e.g., ~57% on CIFAR-10 vs. ~82% for a centralized CNN), and performance degrades as the number of clients grows. Open questions include whether pre-trained TM weights (analogous to ImageNet pre-training for DNNs) can close the centralized-accuracy gap, whether φ and k can be adapted dynamically to the number of clients, and whether the inter-class-separation principle transfers to other lightweight architectures beyond TMs.
