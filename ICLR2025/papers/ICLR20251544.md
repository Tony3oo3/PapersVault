## Problem

Existing multi-condition video generation methods suffer from mutual interference between conditioning signals: camera control is distorted by intermediate features (preventing out-of-distribution motions), and textual guidance intrudes into already-observed scene regions, causing disruptive transitions from realistic to hallucinated content. No prior method can simultaneously accept camera poses, a context frame, and text as coherent, non-conflicting inputs for egocentric video generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251544/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a single reference RGB frame (context frame), a free-form text prompt, and a sequence of camera poses (intrinsic + extrinsic parameters) as joint conditions, producing a 14- to 16-frame video at 256×384 (AnimateDiff) or 256×512 (SVD) resolution. The generated video begins at the context frame and must faithfully follow the specified camera trajectory while filling in unobserved regions guided by the text prompt. Evaluation uses Colmap-based pose estimation to compute TransErr/RotErr against ground-truth or random trajectories, alongside FVD and SSIM.

## Method

EgoSim introduces two key modules on top of frozen video diffusion backbones (SVD or AnimateDiff). First, the Spacetime Epipolar Attention Layer (SEAL) is a plug-in DiT block that rearranges latents into spacetime patches and computes sparse cross-frame attention restricted to patches lying on or near the epipolar line of each query patch, thereby enforcing rigid geometric constraints that override interference from other features. Second, the CI2V-adapter upgrades I2V-adapter by injecting camera-embedding-conditioned patch-wise sigmoid gates that route each spatial location to either text cross-attention or image cross-attention, making the two information sources mutually exclusive and preventing text from eroding observed content. Camera embeddings are encoded via Plücker coordinates with positional encodings, resized via pixel unshuffle, and injected into the temporal transformer. Training freezes all base-model parameters and optimizes only the new layers (SEAL, CI2V-adapter, and subsequent layers) with AdamW at lr 2×10⁻⁴ for 300k iterations on 8 A100 GPUs.

## Results

On the full multi-condition setting (camera + image + text) over RealEstate, EgoSim achieves TransErr 6.75, RotErr 0.77, FVD 293.7, and SSIM 0.808, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline CameraCtrl+I2V (TransErr 13.95, RotErr 1.68, FVD 472.9, SSIM 0.645). On the more dynamic Epic-Field dataset, EgoSim records TransErr 12.41, RotErr 1.27, FVD 663.2, and SSIM 0.755, again leading all baselines by a wide margin. The ablation confirms that SEAL contributes the largest single drop in TransErr (from 13.96 to 8.12) and CI2V-adapter further improves SSIM from 0.739 to 0.808.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EgoSim dominates all four baselines across every reported metric on both RealEstate and Epic-Field. Against MotionCtrl+I2V-Adapter, EgoSim reduces TransErr by roughly 52% (6.75 vs 14.10) and FVD by 40% (293.7 vs 488.3) on RealEstate, while also improving SSIM from 0.637 to 0.808. Against CameraCtrl+I2V-Adapter, the gains are similar in magnitude: TransErr drops from 13.95 to 6.75 and FVD from 472.9 to 293.7. CameraCtrl+SparseCtrl is the weakest baseline, failing to produce valid camera-pose estimates (TransErr/RotErr unavailable) and yielding the worst FVD (1360.8) and SSIM (0.353), indicating severe visual degradation when sparse image conditioning is naively combined with camera control. On the more challenging Epic-Field dataset, EgoSim still leads by a clear margin (FVD 663.2 vs 1172.1 for CameraCtrl+I2V), confirming that the advantage generalizes to dynamic, interaction-rich egocentric scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video diffusion model line (SVD, AnimateDiff) by addressing a specific failure mode in multi-modal conditioning: naive fusion of camera, image, and text embeddings causes them to interfere with or negate each other. For camera control, it departs from MotionCtrl and CameraCtrl (which concatenate or add camera embeddings to intermediate features) by introducing the Spacetime Epipolar Attention Layer (SEAL), which enforces rigid geometric constraints via epipolar geometry within a DiT-style spacetime patch structure. For the image-text conflict, it upgrades I2V-adapter into CI2V-adapter, using camera motion to gate between textual and visual cross-attention in a mutually exclusive manner. The paper also repurposes the Epic-Field egocentric dataset (originally for action recognition) as a new benchmark for interactive video generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe for building a controllable egocentric world simulator that simultaneously respects camera trajectories, observed context frames, and textual imagination — a capability no prior method achieves in a single pipeline. The diagnostic that conditioning modalities actively undermine each other (camera overridden by feature distribution, text eroding observed regions) is a useful warning for anyone combining multiple control signals in diffusion models. Key limitations include reliance on UNet-based backbones (incompatible with 3D-VAE DiT models like Sora), short clip lengths (14–16 frames), and evaluation on only two datasets with somewhat weak baselines (no direct multi-modal competitor exists). The authors identify inverse dynamics for robotics and DiT compatibility as the most impactful next steps.
