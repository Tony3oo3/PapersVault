## Problem

Current object-centric learning methods either maintain monolithic, entangled representations per object (Slot Attention, STEVE) or only operate on static images (SysBinder), leaving open the question of whether hierarchical compositional representations—capturing both static attributes (color, shape) and dynamic attributes (direction, speed, dance patterns)—can emerge purely from unsupervised video learning. No prior method has demonstrated the ability to discover such composable concepts from raw pixels and then recombine them to generate novel, unseen video futures without language, masks, or bounding boxes.

## Input / Output

The model takes a temporal context window of T RGB frames (64×64 for most datasets, 128×128 for CLEVRTex) and predicts K future frames (typically 2–3) via a Discrete VAE token sequence (4×4 patches, vocabulary of 4096). Internally, the encoder produces a block-slot state of N=5 slots, each a concatenation of M=8 blocks of dimension 96, which serves as the manipulable world model representation. For compositional imagination, individual blocks within slots can be swapped or replaced across objects or videos before feeding the modified state to the decoder for autoregressive rollout.

## Method

Dreamweaver's core component is the Recurrent Block-Slot Unit (RBSU), a recurrent module that processes a sequence of per-frame feature sets. Each RBSU step performs: (1) inverted attention from N slot states to L input features, (2) per-block GRU + residual MLP updates with separate weights per block to enforce modularity, (3) per-block attractor dynamics where each block performs dot-product attention over a dedicated learned prototype library (N_prototypes vectors), pulling its state toward reusable concept attractors, and (4) a single-layer transformer for inter-block interaction. The encoder applies a CNN per frame followed by RBSU over T frames; the decoder is an autoregressive transformer over Discrete VAE tokens, using distinct BOS tokens as step indicators to condition on which future frame to predict. The critical design choice is the multi-future-frame predictive cross-entropy objective (rather than reconstruction), which the authors show is necessary for dynamic concepts (direction, speed, dance patterns) to emerge in the block representations—ablations confirm that replacing prediction with reconstruction causes a sharp drop in dynamic informativeness (I-D) scores.

## Results

Dreamweaver consistently achieves the highest DCI scores (Disentanglement, Completeness, Informativeness) across all six evaluated datasets (Moving-Sprites, Moving-CLEVR, Moving-CLEVRTex, Dancing-Sprites, Dancing-CLEVR, and CLEVR-Hard). Most notably, its Informativeness-Dynamic (I-D) score—measuring how well the representation captures dynamic factors like motion direction and dance patterns—exceeds all baselines by more than a factor of two. On the downstream compositional scene prediction task, Dreamweaver outperforms all baselines at every frame offset, and is the only model that succeeds on Dancing-CLEVR even at frame offset zero. For compositional imagination, Dreamweaver achieves PSNR of 30.42 (Moving-Sprites) and 33.83 (Dancing-CLEVR) with LPIPS of 0.288 and 0.395 respectively when generating novel block-slot reconfigurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dreamweaver outperforms all three baselines—RSSM, STEVE, and SysBinder—on every dataset and every DCI metric (D, C, I, and I-D). Against RSSM (a single-vector world model) and STEVE (slot-based object representation), the gap is largest on dynamic informativeness (I-D), where Dreamweaver's predictive objective enables it to capture motion direction and dance patterns that reconstruction-trained baselines largely miss. Against SysBinder (the closest architectural competitor, which also uses block-slot representations but is trained with a reconstruction objective), Dreamweaver wins on all metrics, with the most pronounced advantage on I-D and on the downstream prediction task at non-zero frame offsets. On Dancing-CLEVR, SysBinder, STEVE, and RSSM all fail even at frame offset zero on the downstream task because they do not adequately capture dynamic movement types, while Dreamweaver succeeds. For out-of-distribution generalization (Dancing-Sprites-OOD), Dreamweaver and SysBinder both generalize well, whereas STEVE and RSSM degrade significantly, confirming that the block-slot structure itself aids OOD transfer but the predictive objective is needed for dynamic reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

Dreamweaver extends the block-slot representation introduced by SysBinder (Singh et al., 2023) and Neural Language of Thought Models (Wu et al., 2024) from static images to video by wrapping it in a recurrent unit (RBSU) with per-block attractor dynamics over a learned concept memory. The bottom-up attention mechanism is inherited from Slot Attention (Locatello et al., 2020), while the autoregressive transformer decoder with discrete VAE tokenization follows the generative video transformer lineage (Wu et al., 2021; Singh et al., 2022a). The key departure from prior object-centric video models (STEVE, SAVi++) is the use of a multi-future-frame predictive objective rather than reconstruction, which the authors show is essential for dynamic concept emergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first unsupervised model to learn both static (color, shape) and dynamic (direction, speed, dance pattern) compositional concepts from raw video and use them for out-of-distribution compositional imagination without language or auxiliary supervision. The clean ablation showing that the predictive objective—not just the block-slot architecture—is what enables dynamic concept abstraction provides a useful design principle for future world models. However, the method remains limited to simple synthetic scenes (sprites, CLEVR) and cannot handle complex real-world imagery, a shared limitation of current object-centric learning. Open questions include whether the approach scales to probabilistic modeling, richer abstract concepts (e.g., numbers), and realistic video.
