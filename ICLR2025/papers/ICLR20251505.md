## Problem

While automated conjecture generation has accelerated the discovery of formulas for individual mathematical constants, the systematic discovery of connections *between* constants has remained unaddressed. No central repository organizes known constants and their interrelations, and existing integer relation algorithms focus on linear relations rather than the nonlinear (polynomial) structures that capture most known formulas linking constants like π, e, and ζ(3).

## Input / Output

The method consumes high-precision numeric values of mathematical constants and C-transforms (a canonical continued fraction form that eliminates redundancy among equivalent generalized continued fractions) as input, along with user-defined polynomial degree and order parameters that bound the monomial search space. Output is structured as a hypergraph where vertices are constants and edges are integer polynomial relations, each annotated with its binary precision and a Return on Investment (RoI) score quantifying confidence. The system additionally produces convergence diagnostics for C-transforms, predicting error rates from the asymptotic behavior of the underlying sequence without requiring a closed-form limit.

## Method

The core contribution is a hypergraph representation of mathematical constants and their relations, where edges encode integer polynomial relations (generalizing linear integer relations) discovered via PSLQ. The authors modify PSLQ's standard stopping conditions to run only until tolerance is exhausted, then introduce a novel Return on Investment (RoI) heuristic—defined as the ratio of relation precision to the total binary digit count of the integer coefficients—to filter false positives, with an empirically determined threshold of RoI ≥ 2. Two enrichment strategies populate the hypergraph: an automated search over subsets of constants with polynomial degree/order constraints, and an 'identify' tool that matches newly introduced constants against the existing database and uploads new relations. The algorithm is embarrassingly parallel across independent PSLQ runs, and discovered relations are cached to skip redundant computations in subsequent runs, making the search progressively more efficient as the hypergraph grows.

## Results

The automated search algorithm discovered 118 integer relations between mathematical constants (43 known, 75 novel) after approximately 16 compute months on an 8-core AWS machine. Notable discoveries include a new formula for the first continued fraction constant C₁, novel formulas connecting π and e that generalize Ramanujan's century-old relation into a family of 8 formulas (Table 3), and a conjectured closed form C[n²/(k²(1−4n²))] = (2/k)/(ln(k+1)−ln(k−1)) for all k≥1. The Return on Investment (RoI) heuristic with a cutoff of 2 effectively separates true relations from false positives, with verified relations achieving RoI values ranging from ~4 to over 10,000 (e.g., C[−2n⁴/(9n⁴−3n²+1)] = 1/(2G) at RoI 10630.2, precision 53151 bits).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against other automated conjecture-generation systems in a head-to-head benchmark, but does compare its identify tool against the commercial Wolfram Alpha numerical identification service. The identify method successfully identified the limit of C[−(2n+3)²/(18n(n+1))] as (5A+6B)/(5A−9B) (where A, B are the first and second Lemniscate constants) to 50+ digits, in cases where Wolfram Alpha failed to identify the constant. Additionally, the RoI heuristic is validated across two integer relation algorithms: PSLQ and LLL. Both algorithms show similar RoI behavior (average RoI approaching an upper limit below 1.5 for random inputs), but PSLQ exhibits greater standard deviation than LLL, suggesting PSLQ is more sensitive to the structure of the input vector. The paper also demonstrates that its polynomial (nonlinear) relation search goes beyond what linear-only approaches can find, as evidenced by the discovery of relations involving squared and higher-order terms (e.g., the πe/2 formulas involving C-transforms squared).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Ramanujan Machine project (Raayoni et al., 2021; Elimelech et al., 2024), which automated conjecture generation for individual mathematical constants, by shifting focus to the systematic discovery of interrelations *between* constants. It repurposes the PSLQ integer relation algorithm (Ferguson and Bailey, 1992) but departs from prior usage in two key ways: extending from linear to polynomial (nonlinear) relations, and introducing the Return on Investment (RoI) heuristic to filter false positives. The hypergraph representation for organizing formulas is novel, and the C-transform canonical form for continued fractions builds directly on the Ramanujan Machine's framework while adding a complete convergence analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The library provides the first centralized, open-source repository of mathematical constants and their interrelations, with 75 previously unknown connections discovered—including a generalization of Ramanujan's century-old π-e formula into a family, suggesting it was never truly singular. However, all results remain conjectures awaiting formal proofs, and the convergence conjecture for C-transforms lacks rigorous treatment for many special cases. Open questions include whether the RoI threshold generalizes across algorithms and scales, whether automated addition of 'interesting' constants can bootstrap further discoveries, and whether the hypergraph structure reveals deeper algebraic patterns amenable to proof.
