## Problem

Value-based offline RL (Q-learning) has proven difficult to scale to large language and vision-language models for multi-turn tasks, because the regression-based value learning objective is unstable at scale and requires reinitializing weights or adding new value heads, thereby discarding the knowledge acquired during maximum-likelihood pretraining. As a result, offline RL fine-tuning of LLMs and VLMs does not reliably outperform plain supervised fine-tuning on sequential decision-making tasks like dialogue, navigation, and robotic control.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the same inputs as the underlying pretrained model: token sequences for LLM tasks (chess moves, word guesses, dialogue utterances, web actions) or image-plus-text observations for VLM tasks (ALFWorld navigation, robotic manipulation). The output is a standard token probability distribution over the vocabulary, but these probabilities are trained to simultaneously serve as conservative estimates of Q-values. At inference time, the final policy is computed in closed form as π̂(a|s) ∝ π_φ(a|s)·exp(β·p_θ(a|s)), combining a separately trained behavior-policy estimate with the learned value probabilities, requiring no additional training step.

## Method

Q-SFT replaces the traditional TD regression loss for Q-learning with a weighted cross-entropy loss over the model's existing token logits, so that the learned probabilities directly encode Q-values without any architectural modification. The key mechanism is a modified Bellman probability operator B* p(a|s) = r + γ·max_{a'} p(a'|s')/π_β(a'|s'), where the backup is divided by the behavior policy to convert Q-values into probability-scale weights; the residual weight (1 − B* target) is spread uniformly across all other actions, acting as label smoothing. Theoretically, the fixed-point probabilities satisfy Q*(s,a) ≥ p̂(a|s) ≥ π_β(a|s)·Q*(s,a), guaranteeing conservative value estimation with bounds comparable to state-of-the-art offline RL. In practice, two sets of parameters are trained from the same pretrained initialization: one for the behavior policy via standard cross-entropy, and one for the value probabilities via the weighted cross-entropy objective with a slowly-updated target network, after which the policy is extracted at inference time via the entropy-regularized formula without further optimization.

## Results

On language games, Q-SFT achieves the best or near-best scores: Chess 0.15 (vs. SFT 0.11, ILQL 0.09), Wordle −2.11 (vs. SFT −3.81, ILQL −2.08), and 20Q −13.1 (vs. SFT −17.3, ILQL −14.2). On WebShop, Q-SFT scores 0.63, outperforming ReAct prompting (0.60), SFT (0.55), and Offline ArCHer (0.57). On robotic manipulation, Q-SFT reaches 94% success on Pick Object (vs. QT 92%, CQL 78%) and 64% on Place Object (vs. QT 68%, CQL 57%). On ALFWorld, Q-SFT leads on 5 of 6 subtasks (e.g., Clean 19% vs. SFT 0%, Heat 14% vs. ILQL 5%) while remaining competitive with GPT-4V prompting on the remaining two.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SFT, Q-SFT consistently wins across all language game tasks (e.g., 20Q: −13.1 vs. −17.3, a 25% improvement in return) and WebShop (0.63 vs. 0.55), confirming that incorporating multi-step reward signals beyond imitation is beneficial. Against ILQL, Q-SFT outperforms on Chess (0.15 vs. 0.09) and 20Q (−13.1 vs. −14.2), and dominates on ALFWorld subtasks with low data success rates (Clean: 19% vs. 0%, Cool: 18% vs. 2%), while ILQL edges out Q-SFT marginally on Wordle (−2.08 vs. −2.11). Against ReAct prompting with GPT-3.5/GPT-4V, Q-SFT wins on Chess, Wordle, and WebShop but trails on ALFWorld Pick (39 vs. 45) and Pick2 (21 vs. 24), though the prompting baseline uses a model ~30× larger. In robotic manipulation, Q-SFT slightly beats Q-Transformer on Pick Object (94% vs. 92%) but falls short on Place Object (64% vs. 68%), while substantially outperforming CQL on both tasks (94/64 vs. 78/57). Against Offline ArCHer on WebShop, Q-SFT achieves 0.63 vs. 0.57, a 10% relative improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes offline Q-learning as a weighted cross-entropy (SFT-like) objective, departing from the standard TD-regression formulation used by ILQL, CQL, and Q-Transformer. It is most closely related to Farebrother et al. (2024), which also replaces regression with classification, but unlike that work, Q-SFT initializes its probability estimates directly from pretrained model logits rather than a randomly-initialized value head. The policy extraction step borrows the entropy-regularized form π̂(a|s) ∝ π_β(a|s) exp(βQ*(s,a)) from Peng et al. (2019) and Brandfonbrener et al. (2021), applied here at inference time without additional training. The approach also positions itself against return-conditioned supervised learning (Decision Transformer), arguing that value-based methods retain the ability to stitch suboptimal trajectories into better policies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Q-SFT is the first value-based RL fine-tuning algorithm that requires no architectural modification (no new value heads, no weight reinitialization), making it directly compatible with the standard LLM/VLM training pipeline and enabling full transfer of pretrained knowledge. The empirical finding that Q-SFT's advantage over ILQL grows with model size (Figure 4) suggests the approach may scale better to frontier models, though current experiments use only 345M-parameter GPT-2. Open questions include whether the bounded-reward assumption (Assumption 4.1) limits applicability to dense-reward tasks, whether the method extends to online RL, and whether it can be applied to vision-language-action models where the action space is continuous. The modest empirical margins over strong baselines (e.g., 0.63 vs 0.60 on WebShop) leave room for further improvement.
