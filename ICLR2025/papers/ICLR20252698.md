## Problem

Traditional evolutionary algorithms like CMA-ES and PEPG tend to collapse onto a single solution, while quality-diversity methods like MAP-Elite sacrifice fitness for diversity. The paper asks whether diffusion models and evolutionary algorithms share a deeper mathematical duality beyond superficial analogy, and whether this equivalence can yield an evolutionary method that naturally balances solution quality and diversity without explicit design for either.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252698/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm requires only a black-box fitness function f: R^D → R, a population size N, and schedules α_t and σ_t; no gradients or analytical structure of f are needed. The output is a full population of N parameter vectors rather than a single optimum, with diversity emerging as a structural byproduct of the decreasing Gaussian neighborhood radius over evolution steps. For reinforcement learning tasks, the output is a trained neural network controller (e.g., 58-parameter or 17,410-parameter networks) whose weights are evolved directly.

## Method

The paper proves that the DDIM denoising step is mathematically equivalent to an evolutionary update by mapping fitness to probability density via g[f(x)] and applying Bayes' theorem to estimate x_0 from x_t. This yields a weighted-average estimator where each individual's target is a fitness-weighted, Gaussian-localized average of its neighbors—encoding selection (fitness weights), mutation (the σ_t w noise term), and reproductive isolation (the shrinking Gaussian kernel that restricts each individual to local neighbors as α_t increases). The resulting Diffusion Evolution algorithm requires no neural network; the 'denoiser' is replaced by an explicit Bayesian computation over the current population. For high-dimensional problems, Latent Space Diffusion Evolution projects individuals into a low-dimensional space via a random matrix (Johnson-Lindenstrauss) solely to compute pairwise distances in the Gaussian kernel, while updates still occur in the full parameter space, enabling effective evolution of 17,410-parameter networks in as few as 10 generations.

## Results

On 2D multi-optimum benchmarks (Himmelblau, Ackley, Rastrigin2), Diffusion Evolution achieves entropy of 5.23–5.79 with fitness of 0.64–0.96, simultaneously matching or exceeding the fitness of CMA-ES, PEPG, and OpenES while maintaining near-maximal diversity. On high-dimensional Rastrigin functions (32D, 256D), Latent Diffusion Evolution substantially outperforms all baselines in fitness (0.19 and 0.15 vs. ≤0.02 for all others) while retaining entropy of 6.00. On the cart-pole reinforcement learning task, Latent Diffusion Evolution solves the task in 10 generations with a 512-individual population, whereas CMA-ES fails within the same budget (success rate 1.5–6.4% vs. 80–96% for Latent Diffusion Evolution).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMA-ES achieves perfect fitness (1.00) on single-optimum functions (Rosenbrock, Beale, Himmelblau) but collapses to zero entropy, confirming it converges to a single solution; on multi-optimum Ackley and Rastrigin-2 it finds lower fitness (0.66, 0.57) than Diffusion Evolution (0.78, 0.64) while showing moderate entropy. PEPG consistently exhibits near-zero entropy (0.00–0.85) and underperforms Diffusion Evolution on fitness for all multi-optimum benchmarks. OpenES achieves the highest fitness on Rastrigin-2 (1.00) but with near-zero entropy (0.01), and degrades sharply in higher dimensions (0.00 fitness at 32D and 256D). MAP-Elite attains the maximum possible entropy (6.00) across all benchmarks but sacrifices fitness substantially (e.g., 0.28 on Himmelblau vs. 0.96 for Diffusion Evolution; 0.50 on Ackley vs. 0.78), and its QD-score drops to 0.14 on Rastrigin-256 versus 70.2 for Latent Diffusion Evolution. Overall, Diffusion Evolution uniquely balances high fitness and high diversity simultaneously without explicit quality-diversity design, and Latent Diffusion Evolution dominates all baselines in high-dimensional settings (≥4D Rastrigin) by a wide margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the DDIM sampling equation (Song et al., 2020a) and the DDPM forward diffusion process (Ho et al., 2020) by replacing the learned neural-network denoiser with a Bayesian estimation of x₀ computed directly from the fitness function, eliminating the need for any trained model. It departs from prior diffusion-for-optimization work (Krishnamoorthy et al., 2023; Yan & Jin, 2024) that still required a neural network to predict the noise, and from the broader qualitative literature on evolution–learning parallels (Watson & Levin, 2023; Vanchurin et al., 2022) by providing an explicit, implementable mathematical equivalence. The latent-space extension draws on Rombach et al. (2022) and the Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma, while accelerated sampling via cosine scheduling follows Nichol & Dhariwal (2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a concrete mathematical identity between diffusion sampling and evolutionary search, showing that selection, mutation, hybridization, and reproductive isolation emerge naturally from the denoising dynamics rather than being imposed by design. This opens a bidirectional pipeline: diffusion-model tools (latent spaces, accelerated sampling, classifier-free guidance, non-Gaussian noise) can be imported into evolutionary optimization, and evolutionary concepts (open-endedness, neutral drift) can inform diffusion theory. Key open questions include whether non-Gaussian or discrete diffusion variants yield qualitatively different evolutionary dynamics, how the fitness-to-density mapping g should be chosen for complex landscapes, and whether the approach scales to genuinely hard, high-dimensional optimization beyond the 17K-parameter cart-pole demonstration. The empirical validation, while thorough on 2D benchmarks and classic RL tasks, does not yet test the method on the large-scale, rugged landscapes where CMA-ES and its variants are the standard.
