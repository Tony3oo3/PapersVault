## Problem

Existing label differential privacy methods (randomized response, RRWithPrior, ALIBI) protect labels by flipping each scalar label to another scalar, but this inevitably causes performance to degrade sharply as the number of classes K grows, since the probability of retaining the original label decays inversely with K. The paper asks whether a richer privatization mechanism can preserve more label information while still satisfying ε-label LDP, thereby stabilizing accuracy for large-K classification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252559/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard feature vectors (grayscale 28×28 for MNIST/Fashion-MNIST, RGB 32×32 for CIFAR-10/100) paired with a K-dimensional binary vector Z ∈ {0,1}^K as the training target, where multiple components of Z can simultaneously equal 1 (unlike one-hot encoding). The model's last layer uses sigmoid activation per class (not softmax) with binary cross-entropy loss, and the final prediction is argmax_j g_j(x), yielding a single class label. Label transformation costs O(NK) time, and the overall architecture is a single shared network with K independent sigmoid outputs.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces scalar label flipping with a RAPPOR-style vector randomization: given true label Y=j′, each component Z(j) is set to 1 with probability e^{ε/2}/(1+e^{ε/2}) if j=j′ and 1/(1+e^{ε/2}) otherwise, with components conditionally independent given Y. This satisfies ε-label LDP (Theorem 1) while preserving more information than any scalar mechanism, since the expected vector E[Z|X=x] is an affine transform of the true conditional class probabilities η(x). A standard classifier (CNN, ResNet-18, or kNN) is trained to regress the K-dimensional vector using binary cross-entropy with sigmoid outputs. The theoretical analysis (Theorem 2) shows the excess risk is bounded by an integral involving Δ_ε(x), the maximum per-class approximation error scaled by (e^{ε/2}+1)/(e^{ε/2}−1), and that Δ(x) grows only as O(√(ln K)) for kNN, implying the accuracy degrades only mildly with K.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (K=100), the proposed vector approximation method achieves 67.7% accuracy at ε=3, substantially outperforming LP-2ST (28.7%) and ALIBI (55.0%). On CIFAR-10 (K=10), the method reaches 75.1% at ε=1 versus 63.7% (LP-2ST) and 71.0% (ALIBI). On MNIST (K=10), the method achieves 63.9% at ε=0.2, beating RR (58.0%), LP-2ST (47.5%), and ALIBI (50.9%). The advantage is most pronounced at small ε and large K, converging to near-parity with baselines at large ε where all methods approach the non-private baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three families of label LDP methods: Randomized Response (RR, Warner 1965), LP-2ST/RRWithPrior (Ghazi et al. 2021), and ALIBI (Malek Esmaeili et al. 2021). The proposed vector approximation method consistently outperforms all three baselines across MNIST, Fashion MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100, with the largest margins at small ε and large K. On CIFAR-100 at ε=3, the method achieves 67.7% versus 28.7% for LP-2ST (a 39-point gap) and 55.0% for ALIBI (a 12.7-point gap). On CIFAR-10 at ε=1, the method reaches 75.1% without pre-training, surpassing even the pre-trained LP-2ST★ (70.2%) and ALIBI (71.0%). At large ε (e.g., ε=8 on CIFAR-100), all methods converge to similar accuracy (~74%), indicating the privacy budget becomes the bottleneck rather than the randomization mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the label differential privacy (label DP) framework, where only labels are privatized while features remain public. It departs from the dominant scalar-approximation paradigm (randomized response, RRWithPrior/LP-2ST, ALIBI) that flips each label to a single noisy class, and instead repurposes the RAPPOR mechanism (originally designed for private frequency estimation) to generate a K-dimensional binary vector per label. The key conceptual shift is that a vector preserves more statistical information than a scalar under the same ε-budget, which the authors formalize through an excess-risk bound showing performance decays only as O(√ln K) for kNN rather than inversely with K.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental limitation of all existing local label DP methods: utility collapses as the number of classes grows, making them impractical for fine-grained classification (e.g., CIFAR-100, medical diagnosis with many conditions). The practical recipe—sigmoid activation with binary cross-entropy instead of softmax with cross-entropy, plus O(NK) label transformation overhead—is simple enough to drop into existing training pipelines. Open questions include whether the theoretical guarantees extend rigorously to deep networks (the paper provides only an intuitive argument in Appendix C.2), how the method interacts with feature-level DP, and whether the advantage persists under even stricter privacy budgets (ε < 0.5) where all methods struggle.
