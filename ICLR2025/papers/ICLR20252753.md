## Problem

Implicit Neural Representations rely on embedding hyperparameters (e.g., ω₀ for Siren, σ for Fourier features) that are typically set to default values performing well on average but suboptimal for specific target signals. Finding optimal configurations requires training multiple models in a grid search, which is computationally prohibitive in practice. No prior method efficiently selects embedding frequencies without this repeated-training overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252753/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target signal (a single image of 380–6720 px, a video, or a set of posed 3D scene images) and a discrete set of candidate embedding configurations (e.g., ω₀ ∈ {10, 20, …, 200}). It produces a single selected hyperparameter value that is then used to initialize the INR for standard supervised training. Notably, the comparison is performed on untrained model outputs at initialization, and for multi-image tasks (video, NeRF) a random frame or view is sampled per measurement, with 10 measurements averaged to reduce noise. The spectrum is reduced to a 1D vector of length n=64 by summing DFT diagonals, removing direction dependence.

## Method

FreSh exploits the observation that the frequency spectrum of an untrained model's output strongly correlates with its eventual reconstruction quality on a given target. For each candidate embedding configuration, it computes the Discrete Fourier Transform of the model's initialization output, collapses the 2D DFT into a 1D spectrum vector by summing along diagonals (making it invariant to transposition), normalizes it to a probability distribution, and measures the Wasserstein distance (equivalently, the L1 norm of the CDF difference) to the target signal's spectrum. The configuration minimizing this distance is selected for training. This replaces the costly grid search of training M models with M forward passes plus Fourier transforms, achieving roughly 20× speedup while matching grid-search performance. A cropped spectrum size (n=64) avoids mismatch between synthetic low-frequency model outputs and high-frequency noise in real signals, and the method is model-agnostic, applicable to Siren, Fourier features, Finer, and Hashgrid embeddings.

## Results

On image representation (15k steps, 5 datasets), Siren+FreSh achieves 34.62 dB average PSNR vs. 33.85 dB for default Siren, and Fourier+FreSh reaches 33.45 dB vs. 32.12 dB for default Fourier features. On synthetic NeRF reconstruction, Fourier+FreSh improves from 32.04 to 32.63 dB average PSNR, and Hashgrid+FreSh improves from 31.09 to 31.22 dB. Critically, FreSh achieves these gains with only ~1× the training time of a single baseline model (e.g., 30.3 h vs. 29.1 h for Siren), compared to ~20× for a full grid search (584.2 h), making it an order of magnitude cheaper than conventional hyperparameter sweeps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreSh consistently improves Siren (Sitzmann et al., 2020) by +0.77 dB average PSNR (34.62 vs. 33.85) and Fourier features (Tancik et al., 2020) by +1.33 dB (33.45 vs. 32.12) on image representation, with the largest gains on high-frequency datasets like FFHQ-1024 (+1.57 dB for Siren). On NeRF reconstruction, Fourier+FreSh gains +0.59 dB (32.63 vs. 32.04) and Hashgrid+FreSh gains +0.13 dB (31.22 vs. 31.09), though positional encoding (NeRF, Mildenhall et al., 2021) remains the strongest reference at 32.22 dB and is incompatible with FreSh due to its excessively high frequencies. Finer (Liu et al., 2024) is the one architecture where FreSh does not clearly help (35.03 vs. 35.11 dB), likely because its dual hyperparameters already provide sufficient frequency coverage; however, removing Finer's bias (k=0) and applying FreSh recovers most of the gap (34.88 vs. 34.81 dB). Wire (Saragadam et al., 2023) is incompatible with FreSh due to its layer-wise frequency amplification and is reported only as a reference. FreSh is also complementary to other initialization methods: combining it with FAI (Saratchandran et al., 2024) yields 34.87 dB (vs. 34.32 for FAI alone and 34.62 for FreSh alone), and with Fourier Reparameterization (Shi et al., 2024) yields 34.28 dB (vs. 33.79 for FR alone). Overall, FreSh matches or approaches grid-search-optimal configurations (e.g., 31.81 vs. 32.02 dB for Siren on the 5-dataset average in Table 1) while being ~20× faster than a full parameter sweep.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreSh sits at the intersection of the spectral bias literature (Rahaman et al., 2019) and the large family of embedding/activation architectures designed to overcome it (Siren, Fourier features, NeRF positional encoding, Finer, Wire, Hashgrid), but deliberately does not introduce a new architecture. Instead, it repurposes the Discrete Fourier Transform and Wasserstein distance—tools previously applied to INR regularization (Ramasinghe et al., 2024)—as a lightweight proxy for model performance, replacing costly multi-model grid search with a single forward pass per candidate configuration. The method is positioned as complementary to initialization schemes (IGR, meta-learning) and regularization strategies, addressing the under-studied practical problem of embedding hyperparameter selection that prior INR papers largely sidestep by fixing default values.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FreSh offers a practical, model-agnostic recipe that cuts embedding hyperparameter search cost by roughly an order of magnitude while matching grid-search quality, which is immediately useful for practitioners deploying INRs across diverse signals. However, the method is limited to embeddings whose frequencies are comparable to (not excessively higher than) the target signal, excluding NeRF-style positional encodings and Wire, and it does not account for direction-dependent frequency magnitudes—a gap the authors highlight through their video experiments where temporal and spatial axes require different frequency treatments. Open questions include whether extending FreSh to direction-aware spectra could unlock improvements for video and 3D tasks, and whether the spectrum-size hyperparameter (n) can be further simplified or made fully automatic.
