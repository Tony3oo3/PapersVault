## Problem

Existing flexible docking methods either severely limit protein degrees of freedom to sidechain torsions or implicitly force the model to learn full protein folding, resulting in inaccurate conformational predictions. Additionally, ML docking models frequently generate non-physical poses with steric clashes and strained bonds. The paper identifies that directly applying Flow Matching to transport between apo and holo protein distributions is ineffective because the single-sample nature of holo structures and the need to shift protein conformations create complex, long conditional flows that are difficult to learn.

## Input / Output

The method takes a ligand seed conformation (generated by RDKit), an ESMFold-predicted apo protein structure, and identified pocket residues as input, and produces a predicted ligand pose, a predicted protein pocket structure (backbone frames and sidechain torsions), a confidence score, and a relaxed pose. The docking stage operates on a reduced manifold space of SE(3) residue frames and SO(2) sidechain torsion angles rather than full 3D coordinates, while the relaxation stage operates on full Euclidean atomic coordinates. A key constraint is that only a single holo structure (crystal structure) is available per complex, which necessitates the unbalanced coupling formulation rather than standard optimal transport.

## Method

The core contribution is Unbalanced Flow Matching (UFM), which relaxes the marginal-preservation constraint of standard Flow Matching by using a coupling distribution q(x₀,x₁) ∝ q₀(x₀)q₁(x₁)·𝕀_{c(x₀,x₁)<c_task}, trading some sample efficiency (measured via Rényi divergence) for significantly simpler transport maps. FLEXDOCK chains two unbalanced flows: a manifold docking flow on SE(3)ⁿ × SO(2)^mp that predicts approximate ligand pose and protein conformational change using geodesic interpolation on SO(3) and the torus, followed by a relaxation flow on full Euclidean space with shorter maps (smaller cutoff c_relax < c_dock) that refines the structure. The relaxation flow incorporates a flat-bottom potential energy loss that penalizes violations of bond lengths, bond angles, and steric clashes, and at inference time uses this potential combined with a learned confidence discriminator to filter and select physically valid poses. Theoretically, the paper proves that composing short unbalanced flows in the limit ε→0 corresponds to local likelihood gradient steps pushing the current distribution toward the target, and that the UFM objective provides a tractable upper bound on the joint approximation-error vs. sample-efficiency trade-off.

## Results

On the PDBBind flexible pocket-based docking benchmark, FLEXDOCK achieves 39.7% ligand RMSD < 2Å, 41.7% all-atom RMSD < 1Å, and 72.9% PoseBusters-valid poses, with a runtime of 11s per complex. Compared to DiffDock-Pocket (same architecture and training data), FLEXDOCK improves all-atom RMSD < 1Å from 32.4% to 41.7% and PoseBusters validity from 30.1% to 72.9%. On the PoseBusters v2 benchmark, FLEXDOCK outperforms fast ML-based rigid docking methods (DeepDock, UniMol) and achieves similar RMSD to UMol while being ~19× faster (11s vs 206s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against search-based methods SMINA and GNINA, FLEXDOCK achieves dramatically better ligand accuracy (39.7% vs 3.6%/8.4% for RMSD < 2Å) and all-atom accuracy (41.7% vs 5.2%/4.5% for RMSD < 1Å), while being ~100× faster (11s vs 1914s/1575s). Compared to the closest ML baseline DiffDock-Pocket (same architecture and training data), FLEXDOCK improves all-atom RMSD < 1Å by 9.3 percentage points (41.7% vs 32.4%) and PoseBusters validity by 42.8 percentage points (72.9% vs 30.1%), though it slightly trails on ligand %<2Å (39.7% vs 41.8%). Against ReDock, FLEXDOCK wins on all-atom accuracy (41.7% vs 39.8%) and reports PoseBusters validity (72.9%) where ReDock does not. On the PoseBusters v2 benchmark, FLEXDOCK significantly outperforms rigid ML methods DeepDock and UniMol in validity, and matches UMol on RMSD while being ~19× faster; however, AlphaFold3 (trained on ~10× more data) still achieves superior overall performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2022; Albergo et al., 2023) by relaxing the marginal-preservation constraint on the coupling distribution, connecting the resulting objective to Unbalanced Optimal Transport (UOT) and providing a formal efficiency-vs-approximation trade-off. It builds directly on the DiffDock/DiffDock-Pocket architecture (Corso et al., 2022; Plainer et al., 2023) for the manifold docking component, and departs from prior flexible docking methods (Re-Dock, DynamicBind) that either restrict flexibility to sidechain torsions or rely on hardcoded interpolation rules. The first exploration of unbalanced couplings in flow matching appeared in Eyring et al. (2024) for domain translation; here the authors generalize the framework to arbitrary couplings, prove the UOT upper bound, and show that composing short unbalanced flows yields local likelihood gradient steps—a theoretical result not present in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 30%→73% improvement in PoseBusters physical validity and the 32%→42% gain in all-atom RMSD < 1Å (with identical architecture and training data) address two persistent failure modes of ML docking that have limited practical adoption. The Unbalanced FM framework is general and could benefit any generative task where the source and target distributions have mismatched support or where the optimal transport mapping is too complex to learn. Open questions include whether the approach extends to blind docking (unknown pocket), whether the single-holo-sample limitation can be alleviated with better conformational sampling of apo states, and whether the flat-bottom energy loss can be replaced by learned force fields without sacrificing training stability.
