## Problem

Pretraining low-rank parameterized language models from scratch (where each weight W is represented as the product BA of two rank-r factors) is significantly harder than low-rank fine-tuning, as standard gradient descent on the factors separately introduces redundant gradient information and fails to follow the steepest descent direction on the low-rank matrix manifold. Existing memory-efficient pretraining methods (GaLore, ReLoRA, SLTrain) use low-rank or sparse updates but still produce full-size models, offering no inference-time memory reduction or speedup. The paper asks whether a low-rank parameterized LM can be trained from scratch to match or exceed full-size baseline performance while genuinely reducing both training and inference costs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a large-scale unsupervised text corpus (C4) for next-token prediction pretraining and produces a decoder-only transformer (LLaMA architecture) where every attention projection and MLP linear layer is stored as a pair of rank-r factors (B ∈ R^{d×r}, A ∈ R^{r×d}) rather than a full d×d weight matrix, while the token embedding and output LM head remain full-size. With r = d/4, each layer's parameter count drops from d² to 2rd (roughly 50% reduction) and per-layer FLOPS drop from O(d³) to O(rd²), yielding a ~×2 inference speedup. The trained model outputs standard next-token logits for autoregressive generation.

## Method

LORO performs retraction-based Riemannian optimization on the rank-r matrix manifold, jointly updating the factor pair (B, A) so their product moves along the Riemannian gradient rather than optimizing each factor independently in ambient space. Two key propositions make this practical under low-rank parameterization: (1) the Riemannian gradient can be computed entirely from the backpropagated factor gradients ∇_B L and ∇_A L via the formula B(BᵀB)⁻¹∇_A L + ∇_B L(AAᵀ)⁻¹A − B(BᵀB)⁻¹∇_A L·Aᵀ(AAᵀ)⁻¹A, eliminating any need for the full-size d×d gradient; (2) the SVD-based retraction step can be performed in factorized form by decomposing the d×d tangent vector through QR decompositions of B and Aᵀ, reducing the SVD to a 2r×2r matrix. In practice, LORO alternates between a cheap approximate update (scaling factor gradients by r/d, equivalent to O(rd) element-wise operations) and an exact Riemannian update every K steps (involving QR of d×r matrices, inversion of r×r matrices, and SVD of a 2r×2r matrix), with Adam-style momentum accumulated on the factor gradients. This design avoids all full-size d×d matrix multiplications or SVDs, keeping the per-step complexity at O(r²+rd) while ensuring the product BA tracks the steepest descent direction on the manifold.

## Results

On LLaMA-1B pretrained on C4, LORO achieves a validation perplexity of 15.19, which is 2% better than the full-size baseline (15.56), while using only 609M parameters (vs. 1339M) and 3.66 GB memory (vs. 8.04 GB). LORO delivers a ×1.82 training speedup and a ×2.08–×4.04 inference speedup over the full-size model. Across all four LLaMA sizes (60M–1B), LORO consistently achieves the lowest perplexity among all low-rank and memory-efficient baselines, matching or exceeding full-size performance in most settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LORO decisively outperforms the standard low-rank baseline (Kamalakara et al., 2022) by a large margin — on LLaMA-1B, LORO achieves PPL 15.19 versus 142.53 for vanilla low-rank training, confirming that separate factor optimization is fundamentally inadequate for pretraining. Against LoRA (Hu et al., 2022), which relies on a frozen full-size weight plus low-rank delta, LORO achieves 15.19 vs. 19.21 PPL on LLaMA-1B while using half the parameters (609M vs. 1339M). LORO also beats ReLoRA (Lialin et al., 2024) by 3.14 PPL on LLaMA-1B (15.19 vs. 18.33) and by 4.49 PPL on LLaMA-130M (24.59 vs. 29.37). Compared to GaLore (Zhao et al., 2024), which uses low-rank gradients to update full-size weights, LORO achieves 15.19 vs. 15.64 PPL on LLaMA-1B with 54% less memory and a ×1.82 training speedup (GaLore shows no practical speedup). Finally, LORO outperforms SLTrain (Han et al., 2024) by 0.95 PPL on LLaMA-1B (15.19 vs. 16.14) and by 2.58 PPL on LLaMA-130M (24.59 vs. 26.04), while using fewer parameters (609M vs. 646M). Notably, LORO also surpasses the full-size baseline on LLaMA-1B (15.19 vs. 15.56) and LLaMA-60M (33.96 vs. 34.06), suggesting that Riemannian optimization on the low-rank manifold can find flatter, more effective minima.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: Riemannian optimization on low-rank matrix manifolds (Absil et al., 2009; Vandereycken, 2013; Bian et al., 2023) and low-rank parameterization of neural networks (LoRA, Hu et al., 2022). Prior Riemannian approaches for training low-rank models (Savostianova et al., 2023; Schotthofer et al., 2022) required either full-size gradient evaluation or SVD-based parameterization with O(r²) overhead, making them impractical for language model pretraining. Concurrent low-rank pretraining methods (GaLore, ReLoRA, SLTrain) use low-rank updates but still maintain full-size weights, yielding no inference-time savings. LORO departs from both lines by performing Riemannian gradient descent entirely within the low-rank factorization, eliminating all d×d matrix operations while preserving the geometric structure of the rank-r manifold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a rank-d/4 model pretrained from scratch can match or slightly exceed full-size perplexity while halving memory and doubling inference speed is practically significant for resource-constrained pretraining. The finding that LORO discovers flatter minima than full-size Adam (evidenced by asymmetric loss barriers in linear interpolation) suggests the low-rank manifold constraint may act as an implicit regularizer, an insight with broader implications for representation learning. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to 7B+ parameter models where the r/d ratio and exact-update frequency K may require re-tuning, whether it generalizes to vision or multimodal architectures, and whether the modest 2% perplexity advantage over full-size baselines persists with larger training budgets.
