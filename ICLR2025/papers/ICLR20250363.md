## Problem

Computational RNA design tools either focus on secondary structure without 3D geometry or rely on slow, non-learnt physics-based optimisation (e.g., Rosetta) that cannot handle conformational flexibility. No existing deep learning pipeline explicitly models RNA as a multi-state conformational ensemble, making it impossible to design sequences that simultaneously satisfy multiple distinct 3D backbone structures required by dynamic RNAs such as riboswitches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250363/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is one or more 3D RNA backbone structures (a conformational ensemble) specified as PDB coordinates, featurised into a 3-bead coarse-grained geometric graph per nucleotide (P, C4', N1/N9) with edges to the 32 nearest neighbours in 3D space. The output is a set of candidate RNA sequences (A/G/C/U per nucleotide) sampled autoregressively from the 5' to 3' end, along with a perplexity score per sequence that estimates the model's confidence and can be used for zero-shot ranking of mutants without ground-truth labels.

## Method

gRNAde introduces a multi-state GNN encoder built from rotation-equivariant GVP-GNN layers that processes a 'multi-graph' tensor of shape (n_nodes × k_states × feature_channels), performing message passing independently per conformational state while maintaining permutation equivariance along both the node and conformation axes. After the final encoder layer, a Deep Set (average) pooling over the conformation axis yields order-invariant per-nucleotide representations, which are fed to an autoregressive GVP-GNN decoder that predicts conditional base-identity probabilities from 5' to 3'. The model is trained self-supervised with cross-entropy loss (label smoothing 0.05) and autoregressive teacher forcing on 4,223 unique RNA sequences (12,011 structures) from RNASolo. The key architectural insight is that stacking conformations along a dedicated tensor axis and generalising PyG message passing to operate only on the feature-channel axis provides a plug-and-play multi-state inductive bias, avoiding the need to batch conformations or average single-state logits (which the paper shows can produce logically incorrect base assignments for multi-state design).

## Results

On the single-state fixed-backbone benchmark of 14 PDB RNA structures (Das et al. 2010), gRNAde achieves 56.8% average native sequence recovery, outperforming Rosetta (45%), RDesign (43%), FARNA (32%), and ViennaRNA (27%). In wet lab validation via Eterna OpenKnot Round 6 (10 pseudoknotted backbones, 200 designs), gRNAde achieves a 50% success rate (OpenKnot score >80) versus 35% for Rosetta. For multi-state design on structurally flexible RNAs, the multi-state GNN improves sequence recovery by 3–5% over an equivalent single-state model, with the best performance at 3 conformational states. Inference speed is ~1 second on GPU for 100+ designs of a 60-nt RNA, compared to hours for Rosetta.

## Baselines & Comparisons

gRNAde outperforms Rosetta (the state-of-the-art physics-based 3D RNA design tool) on all three evaluation axes: native sequence recovery (56.8% vs. 45% average on the 14-structure benchmark), wet lab success rate (50% vs. 35% in OpenKnot Round 6), and inference speed (~1 s on GPU vs. hours on CPU). Against RDesign (Tan et al. 2023), the concurrent deep learning baseline, gRNAde achieves higher average recovery (56.8% vs. 43%) and wins on all 14 individual structures; the gap is attributed to gRNAde's autoregressive decoder (vs. RDesign's one-shot NAR) and rotation-equivariant GNN layers (vs. RDesign's invariant layers), as confirmed by ablations. In the multi-state setting, gRNAde's multi-state GNN improves sequence recovery by 3–5% over its own single-state variant, a capability neither Rosetta nor RDesign supports. For zero-shot fitness ranking on the McRae et al. (2024) ribozyme landscape, gRNAde's perplexity outperforms random selection and single-site saturation mutagenesis at design budgets up to ~500 sequences, identifying a 4-fold fitness improvement in the top 10 mutants.

## Relation to Prior Work

gRNAde adapts the ProteinMPNN paradigm (autoregressive GNN-based inverse folding) from proteins to RNA, using GVP-GNN layers as the geometric backbone. Its key architectural departure is the multi-state GNN encoder, which generalises message passing to handle a conformational ensemble axis while maintaining permutation equivariance across both nodes and states — a capability absent from both Rosetta's physics-based MCMC optimisation and the concurrent RDesign model. The paper also extends the featurisation and evaluation framework from protein design (Ingraham et al., 2019; Dauparas et al., 2022) to the RNA-specific setting of 3-bead pseudotorsional representations and 2D/3D self-consistency scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

gRNAde is the first pipeline to enable explicit multi-state RNA inverse design, addressing a fundamental limitation of prior tools that could only condition on a single backbone conformation. The 50% wet-lab success rate on pseudoknotted structures (vs. 35% for Rosetta) and the zero-shot perplexity-based fitness ranking demonstrate practical utility beyond in-silico benchmarks. Key open questions include supporting multi-chain complexes, partner molecules, and negative design, as well as how the approach scales as RNA 3D structure prediction tools (currently a major bottleneck for self-consistency evaluation) mature. The multi-state GNN architecture is generic and could transfer to multi-state protein design or other conformational ensemble tasks.
