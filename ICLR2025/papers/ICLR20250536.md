## Problem

Zeroth-order (ZO) optimization eliminates backpropagation memory (gradients and activations) for LLM fine-tuning, but still requires full-precision perturbation and updates across all parameters, making it incompatible with the weight quantization needed for memory-constrained devices like smartphones. Existing sparse ZO methods use dynamic sparsity patterns that are expensive to recompute each step and cannot be combined with quantization without frequent dequantization/quantization cycles. The paper asks whether an extreme static sparsity pattern can be identified that is both transferable across tasks and compatible with quantization, enabling on-device ZO fine-tuning under 8 GB memory.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained 7B LLM (Llama2-7B, Mistral-7B, or OPT-6.7B), a small batch of C4 text used once to compute the sensitive parameter mask via empirical Fisher diagonal, and user-specific fine-tuning data processed entirely on-device. The output is a model where only 0.1% of parameters (the sensitive subset) are updated in 16-bit precision via ZO-SGD, while the remaining 99.9% are frozen and stored as 4-bit SqueezeLLM quantized weights. The entire fine-tuning pipeline fits within 8 GB of CUDA memory without CPU offloading, and the resulting model can be deployed directly on edge hardware for inference.

## Method

The core insight is that only 0.1% of LLM parameters—those with the largest coordinate-wise gradient magnitudes, identified as the top-k entries of the empirical Fisher information matrix diagonal—need to be updated during ZO fine-tuning, and this sparsity pattern is stable across tasks and transferable from pre-training data (C4) without modification. The sensitive parameters are extracted once before fine-tuning, kept in 16-bit, and optimized using SPSA-based ZO-SGD (Simultaneous Perturbation Stochastic Approximation with a single random Gaussian perturbation per step), while the remaining dense weights are 4-bit quantized and frozen. This static decomposition makes the method compatible with quantization because no dequantization/quantization is needed during training—only the small sparse subset is perturbed and updated. Theoretically, the convergence rate improves from O(d/T) for full ZO to O((k/c)·L/T) where k is the number of sensitive parameters and c is the fraction of gradient norm they capture, yielding a significant speedup when c ≫ k/d. Empirically, SensZOQ outperforms ZO full fine-tuning, in-context learning, and ZO-LoRA on 4-bit quantized models across SuperGLUE, commonsense reasoning, math, and MMLU benchmarks.

## Results

On Mistral-7B across 8 SuperGLUE tasks, SensZOQ (4-bit quantized, 0.1% sparse ZO fine-tuning) achieves 74.8% average accuracy, outperforming ZO Full FT (74.1%) and in-context learning (52.6%) while fitting in under 8 GB GPU memory. On OPT-6.7B, SensZOQ reaches 73.3% average accuracy, surpassing FO-Adam full fine-tuning on a 1.3B model (72.3%) that already exceeds the 8 GB budget (12.5 GB on RTE vs. 5.6 GB for SensZOQ). The method also outperforms ZO LoRA on the same 4-bit quantized base model (74.8% vs. 71.3% on Mistral-7B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SensZOQ outperforms ZO Full FT (MeZO) on both Mistral-7B (74.8% vs. 74.1% avg) and OPT-6.7B (73.3% vs. 72.9% avg), despite updating only 0.1% of parameters on a 4-bit quantized model versus 100% of parameters in FP16. Against in-context learning, SensZOQ achieves a substantial margin (74.8% vs. 52.6% on Mistral-7B; 78.0% vs. 61.6% on Llama2-13B). Compared to ZO LoRA on the same 4-bit quantized base, SensZOQ wins by 3.5 points on Mistral-7B (74.8% vs. 71.3%), confirming that gradient-derived sensitive parameters are more effective than low-rank adapters for ZO fine-tuning. In the cross-size comparison (Table 2), SensZOQ on 6.7B OPT (73.3%) beats FO-Adam full FT on 1.3B OPT (72.3%), demonstrating that the ZO performance gap versus first-order methods is acceptable when the model size difference is considered. Against SparseMeZO's smallest-magnitude sparsity mask, SensZOQ's C4-gradient-derived sensitive parameters maintain near-flat performance from 10% down to 0.1% sparsity, whereas smallest-magnitude degrades significantly in the extreme sparsity regime (<1%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ZO fine-tuning line initiated by MeZO (Malladi et al., 2023a) and the dynamic sparsity approach of SparseMeZO (Liu et al., 2024c), but departs from both by replacing dynamic, weight-magnitude-based sparsity with a static, gradient-sensitivity-based mask derived once from pre-training data (C4). The sensitive-parameter concept is borrowed from the quantization literature (SqueezeLLM, LQ-LoRA) and fixed-mask training (Sung et al., 2021), but is repurposed here as the selection criterion for which parameters to perturb and update in ZO optimization. The combination of this static sparse ZO with 4-bit quantization of the remaining weights is novel, creating an on-device personalization workflow that neither QLoRA nor prior ZO methods achieve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SensZOQ demonstrates that 7B LLMs can be fine-tuned under 8 GB GPU memory without CPU offloading, opening a practical path for on-device personalization where privacy constraints prevent cloud-based fine-tuning. The key open question is whether the C4-derived static mask remains effective for highly domain-specific tasks (the paper shows degradation on MMLU) and whether the 0.1% sparsity level generalizes to larger models or longer fine-tuning horizons. The theoretical convergence analysis is standard non-convex ZO with a sparsity assumption, so the practical value rests heavily on the empirical transferability evidence, which is well-supported across three model families and multiple task categories.
