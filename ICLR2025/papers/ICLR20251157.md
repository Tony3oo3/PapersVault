## Problem

Standard conditional diffusion models trained with classifier-free guidance violate the conditional independence between attribute marginals, causing them to fail at generating images for arbitrary logical compositions (AND, OR, NOT) of attributes. This failure occurs even when all attribute compositions are uniformly observed during training, and is severely exacerbated under non-uniform or partial support where only a subset of compositions appears in the training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251157/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a logical expression over discrete attribute values (e.g., 'digit=4 ∧ ¬[color=Green ∨ color=Pink]') and produces an RGB image satisfying that composition. A single monolithic U-Net diffusion model handles all attribute subsets via classifier-free guidance, with each attribute independently set to ∅ during training. At inference, the logical expression is decomposed into primitive AND and NOT operations, each expressed as a linear combination of conditional marginal scores (e.g., ∇log p(X|C₁) + ∇log p(X|C₂) − ∇log p(X) for AND), and a hyperparameter γ allows fine-grained control over the relative strength of individual attributes.

## Method

COIND augments the standard score-matching loss with a conditional independence loss L_CI that enforces the causal factorization p(X|C) = [p(X)/p(C)] ∏ᵢ [p(X|Cᵢ)p(Cᵢ)/p(X)] by minimizing the Fisher divergence between the joint score and the product-of-marginals score. The theoretical derivation shows, via the Wasserstein triangle inequality, that minimizing L_score + L_CI upper-bounds the 2-Wasserstein distance between the true conditional and the causal factorization. For scalability, mutual conditional independence is approximated by pairwise independence: at each training step, two random attributes (i,j) are selected and the loss enforces ∇log p(X|Cᵢ,Cⱼ) = ∇log p(X|Cᵢ) + ∇log p(X|Cⱼ) − ∇log p(X), reducing model evaluations from O(n) to O(1) in expectation. The final training objective is L_final = L_score + λ·L_CI, where λ balances the two terms, and the entire procedure requires only a few additional lines of code on top of standard classifier-free diffusion training.

## Results

On Colored MNIST under partial support, COIND (λ=1.0) achieves a Conformity Score of 52.38% for AND composition and 53.28% for NOT-color, compared to 7.40% and 5.09% for Composed GLIDE and 10.85% and 9.03% for LACE — a 2–10× improvement. Under non-uniform support, COIND reaches 99.95% AND CS and 84.98% NOT-digit CS, matching or exceeding uniform-support baselines (Composed GLIDE: 86.10% AND CS, 70.44% NOT-digit CS). On Shapes3D with orthogonal partial support, COIND achieves 91.10% AND CS and 53.90% NOT CS versus 51.56% and 34.63% for Composed GLIDE. On CelebA, COIND reduces FID from 61.21 to 43.97 for unseen 'smiling male' generation and from 95.41 to 43.76 for the AND composition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COIND consistently outperforms both baselines across all three support settings and all composition tasks. Against Composed GLIDE (Liu et al., 2022), COIND reduces JSD by 33–58% (e.g., 0.15 vs 0.30 non-uniform; 1.17 vs 2.75 partial) and improves AND Conformity Score by up to 7× under partial support (52.38% vs 7.40%). Against LACE (Nie et al., 2021), which trains separate score-based models per attribute, COIND achieves substantially higher CS under non-uniform (99.95% vs 82.61% AND) and partial (52.38% vs 10.85% AND) support, while also matching or exceeding LACE on NOT compositions. On Shapes3D with six attributes, COIND maintains 91.10% AND CS under orthogonal partial support while both baselines drop to 51–62%, demonstrating scalability. On CelebA, COIND achieves the lowest FID (43.76–43.97) for unseen 'smiling male' generation, outperforming Composed GLIDE (61.21–95.41) by 28–54%, though LACE reports a higher AND CS (24.20 vs 8.79) at the cost of a much worse FID (80.40 vs 43.76).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the classifier-free guidance training framework (Ho & Salimans, 2022) and the logical composition sampling equations of Composed GLIDE (Liu et al., 2022), which decompose AND/NOT/OR operations into sums of conditional marginal scores. The key departure is identifying that these methods implicitly assume the learned conditional marginals are statistically independent—a property the paper proves is violated by standard diffusion training even under ideal uniform support. COIND introduces a Fisher-divergence-based conditional independence loss (L_CI) derived from a causal factorization of the joint likelihood, positioning the contribution as a principled fix to the training objective rather than a new sampling strategy. The causal modeling framework (Peters et al., 2017) and the Wasserstein-to-score-matching bound (Kwon et al., 2022) provide the theoretical scaffolding for the loss derivation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard diffusion models violate conditional independence even when all attribute compositions are uniformly observed challenges a foundational assumption underlying multiple compositional generation methods (Composed GLIDE, LACE, and related EBMs). COIND's practical simplicity—a few lines added to the CFG training loop—makes it broadly applicable, and the 2–10× improvement under partial support addresses a critical real-world limitation where training data rarely covers all compositions uniformly. Open questions include whether the approach extends to causally related attributes, open-set compositions beyond the support-cover assumption, and whether the pairwise independence approximation degrades with many highly correlated attributes. The method also requires knowing the correct causal structure a priori, limiting its applicability to settings where attribute independence is a reasonable domain assumption.
