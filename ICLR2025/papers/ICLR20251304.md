## Problem

Positional encodings for directed graphs remain underexplored: existing methods (symmetrized Laplacian PE, SVD-PE, single-q Magnetic Laplacian PE) provably fail to capture bidirectional structural relations such as common successors/predecessors, feed-forward loops, and directed shortest/longest path distances. The paper formalizes this gap through the notion of Walk Profile, which counts length-ℓ bidirectional walks by the exact number of forward vs. backward edges, and shows that no single-q spectral PE can recover it.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a directed graph's adjacency matrix (binary or weighted) and optionally node features, producing per-node positional encodings in the complex domain that are then processed into real-valued stable representations via the SPE framework. The PE dimension scales linearly with the number of q values Q (each contributing n complex eigenvector entries), and the number of q's is chosen as ⌈L/2⌉+1 where L is the maximum walk length of interest. Downstream, these PEs feed into GIN, bidirectional GIN, or SAT backbones to predict pairwise distances, walk profile vectors, sorting-network satisfiability labels, or circuit properties (gain, bandwidth, phase margin, DSP/LUT usage).

## Method

The core contribution is Multi-q Magnetic Laplacian PE: for Q distinct potential constants q_i ∈ [0, 1/4), the method computes eigendecompositions of Q Hermitian Magnetic Laplacians L_{q_i} and concatenates the resulting eigenvectors per node. The key theoretical insight is that each q acts as a frequency encoding the accumulated phase shift over bidirectional walks, and the walk profile Φ_{u,v}(ℓ,k) can be exactly recovered by solving a Fourier-type linear system across the Q frequencies (requiring Q = ⌈L/2⌉+1 for walk length ≤ L). To address the basis ambiguity and instability of complex eigenvectors (unitary transformations and per-eigenvector phase rotations), the paper extends the Stable PE (SPE) framework from the real to the complex domain, processing real and imaginary parts of V·diag(φ(λ))·V† through permutation-equivariant functions to yield basis-invariant node and edge features. This stable architecture is shown to be critical: naive concatenation or SignNet processing of the same PEs yields substantially worse performance, while SPE reduces test RMSE by ~66% on distance prediction.

## Results

On directed acyclic graph distance prediction, Multi-q Mag-PE with SPE achieves RMSE of 0.016 (shortest path), 0.185 (longest path), and 0.002 (walk profile of length 4), reducing error by 66% over naive concatenation and substantially outperforming single-q Mag-PE (0.124/0.432/0.040) and symmetrized Laplacian (0.355/0.655/0.326). On sorting network satisfiability, Multi-q Mag-PE + SPE reaches 91.27% F1 versus 86.86% for the best single-q Mag-PE and 56.35% for Laplacian PE. On the Open Circuit Benchmark and High-level Synthesis, Multi-q Mag-PE with SPE achieves the best or near-best RMSE/MSE across all five circuit targets (Gain, BW, PM, DSP, LUT) for every backbone model tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Multi-q Mag-PE with SPE dominates all baselines across every task. Against symmetrized Laplacian PE (Dwivedi et al., 2023), the improvement is dramatic: on DAG walk profile prediction, RMSE drops from 0.326 to 0.002, and on sorting network F1 from 56.35% to 91.27%. SVD-PE (Hussain et al., 2022) performs worst among spectral methods, with SPE actually degrading its performance (e.g., DAG spd RMSE worsens from 0.649 to 0.727), confirming the theoretical limitation that SVD cannot express walk profiles. Single-q Magnetic Laplacian (Geisler et al., 2023) is the closest competitor, but Multi-q consistently outperforms even the best-tuned single q: on DAG walk profile, 0.002 vs. 0.040 RMSE, and on sorting network, 91.27% vs. 86.86% F1, with the gap widening on longer sorting networks. As PE processing, SignNet (Lim et al., 2022) is consistently inferior to the proposed SPE framework and sometimes worse than naive concatenation due to node ambiguity. As backbone models, both GIN (Xu et al., 2018) and SAT (Chen et al., 2022) benefit from Multi-q Mag-PE over all other PE choices, with the largest gains appearing on direction-sensitive targets like circuit gain and bandwidth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Magnetic Laplacian PE (Mag-PE) of Geisler et al. (2023) from a single potential q to multiple q's, and generalizes the stable PE framework (SPE) of Huang et al. (2023) from real to complex eigenvectors. Conceptually, it departs from prior directed-graph PE work (symmetrized Laplacian, SVD-PE, single-q Mag-PE) by introducing the Walk Profile as a formal expressiveness criterion and proving that all existing methods are provably insufficient for it. The Fourier interpretation of q as a phase-shift frequency, requiring ⌈L/2⌉+1 distinct q's for exact recovery of length-L walk profiles, is the key theoretical insight that unifies the design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The walk profile provides a principled, fine-grained measure of what a directed PE can express, moving beyond ad-hoc distance metrics and giving the field a clear target for future PE design. The provable gap between single-q and multi-q Mag-PE resolves an open question about whether the Magnetic Laplacian's phase information is fundamentally limited. Practical limitations include the O(Q) eigendecomposition overhead (roughly 2× runtime for Q=5) and the requirement that Q scales with desired walk length, which may be prohibitive for very long-range relations. Open questions include whether learned or adaptive q-selection can reduce the number of required frequencies, and whether the complex-domain stable PE framework extends to other spectral operators beyond the Magnetic Laplacian.
