## Problem

Modern time series forecasting models predominantly use the Direct Forecast (DF) paradigm, which computes per-step losses independently and implicitly assumes conditional independence among future labels. The paper proves that this assumption is violated by the inherent label autocorrelation in time series, introducing a measurable bias in the learning objective relative to the true negative log-likelihood. While input autocorrelation has been extensively addressed through architecture design, label autocorrelation remains an unexplored source of estimation bias in the training paradigm itself.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series input sequence of fixed length H=96 with D covariates and produces a multi-step forecast of length T ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720} via any standard forecasting model g. The key structural detail is that FreDF is a loss-level modification rather than an architectural change: the model's forward pass and output format remain identical to vanilla DF, but the training signal is augmented with a frequency-domain component. The method is model-agnostic, compatible with Transformers, MLPs, and other architectures, and requires only tuning a single scalar weight α ∈ [0,1] and a conservative learning-rate adjustment.

## Method

FreDF augments the standard temporal MSE loss with a frequency-domain loss computed by applying FFT to both the forecast and label sequences, then taking the element-wise L1 norm of their difference: L^(freq) = |F(Ŷ) − F(Y)|₁. The total training objective is a convex combination L^α = α·L^(freq) + (1−α)·L^(tmp), where α controls the relative emphasis. The theoretical justification rests on two results: (1) Theorem 3.1 shows that label autocorrelation ρ_ij biases the DF loss against the practical NLL, and (2) Theorem 3.3 proves that DFT coefficients of a wide-sense stationary process become asymptotically uncorrelated at different frequencies, so the frequency-domain loss operates on a representation where the independence assumption of DF is approximately satisfied. The L1 norm is used in the frequency domain (rather than L2) because Fourier coefficients exhibit vastly different magnitudes across frequencies, making squared losses numerically unstable. Empirically, the method also supports alternative orthogonal bases (Legendre, Chebyshev) and 2D FFT over both time and variable axes, and demonstrates improved sample efficiency in the frequency domain.

## Results

Using iTransformer as the backbone, FreDF achieves the best average MSE across 8 out of 9 long-term forecasting datasets (e.g., 0.392 on ETTm1, 0.278 on ETTm2, 0.170 on ECL, 0.254 on Weather), outperforming the vanilla iTransformer by 0.019–0.027 MSE. FreDF also secures the lowest MAE on all 9 datasets, with the largest gains on ETTm2 (0.319 vs. 0.335 for iTransformer) and Weather (0.274 vs. 0.302). On the short-term M4 benchmark, FreDF-enhanced FreTS achieves the best average SMAPE (12.112), MASE (1.648), and OWA (0.877) across yearly, quarterly, and monthly horizons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreDF (iTransformer backbone) outperforms all 10 baselines on 8 of 9 datasets for both MSE and MAE, with the closest competitor being the vanilla iTransformer (the backbone without FreDF), which it beats by 0.019–0.027 MSE. The sole exception is PEMS03, where MICN achieves a lower MSE (0.099 vs. 0.113) and MAE (0.214 vs. 0.219), though the gap is small. Against Transformer-based baselines (Autoformer, FEDformer, Transformer), FreDF shows the largest margins—e.g., on ETTm1, FreDF's 0.392 MSE beats Autoformer's 0.596 by 0.204. Against MLP-based baselines (DLinear, TiDE, FreTS), FreDF consistently leads, with DLinear being the strongest MLP competitor (e.g., 0.404 MSE on ETTm1 vs. FreDF's 0.392). The paper further demonstrates that FreDF is model-agnostic: when applied to DLinear, Autoformer, and Transformer backbones, it improves each by 2–6% MSE, and notably, FreDF-enhanced Autoformer (2021) surpasses DLinear (2023) on ECL, highlighting that the loss-function refinement can close or reverse architecture-driven gaps.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreDF carves new ground by shifting frequency-domain analysis from the model architecture (as in FEDformer, FourierGNN, FreTS) to the loss function, targeting a previously overlooked source of bias: label autocorrelation in the Direct Forecast paradigm. Theoretically, it connects to the NLL framework for multivariate Gaussian processes, showing that the standard MSE loss used in DF is biased whenever future steps are conditionally dependent given the input. Empirically, it draws on double machine learning (DML) from the causal inference literature to quantify partial correlations between label steps, providing evidence that Fourier transform effectively decorrelates them. The method is explicitly positioned as a model-agnostic refinement of DF rather than a new architecture, distinguishing it from the large body of work on input-side autocorrelation modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) that DF's MSE loss is biased under label autocorrelation provides a principled justification for a simple, broadly applicable training modification that can be plugged into any forecasting model. The sample-efficiency finding—that 30% of training data in the frequency domain matches 100% in the time domain—suggests practical value for data-limited settings. However, the empirical gains over strong baselines are modest (typically 1–5% MSE reduction), and the reliance on predefined Fourier bases limits adaptability to non-stationary or non-periodic data. Open questions include whether data-adaptive orthogonal bases (e.g., ICA) could further reduce residual correlation, and whether the label-autocorrelation framing generalizes to other structured prediction tasks (3D point clouds, speech, images) as the authors speculate.
