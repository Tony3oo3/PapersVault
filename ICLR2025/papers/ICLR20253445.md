## Problem

Despite widespread empirical success of adaptive optimizers (SignSGD, RMSprop, Adam, AdamW), their theoretical understanding remains incomplete: no formal SDE existed for SignSGD, and existing SDEs for Adam/RMSprop (Malladi et al., 2022) were derived under restrictive assumptions and only accurate near minima. The precise role of gradient noise—especially heavy-tailed noise—in shaping convergence speed, stationary distributions, and asymptotic loss for these optimizers was uncharacterized, leaving a gap between worst-case convergence rates and practical behavior.

## Input / Output

The method takes discrete-time optimizer update rules (SignSGD, AdamW, RMSpropW) together with regularity assumptions on the loss function (C^8 smoothness, Lipschitz gradients, polynomial growth) and gradient noise (bounded smooth density, covering both Gaussian and Student's t distributions). It produces order-1 weak approximations in the form of continuous-time SDEs, from which convergence rate bounds, stationary distributions around minima, asymptotic loss values, and a novel batch size scaling rule for AdamW are extracted. Validation is performed by numerically integrating the SDEs via Euler-Maruyama and comparing trajectories against the actual optimizers on MLPs, CNNs, ResNets, and Transformers.

## Method

The paper derives the first formal SDE for SignSGD under general noise assumptions (not restricted to Gaussian), revealing three distinct dynamical phases governed by the signal-to-noise ratio: a deterministic SignGD-like phase, a transitional phase, and a noise-dominated phase where the inverse noise scale premultiplies the gradient, inversely affecting convergence speed while linearly (rather than quadratically, as in SGD) affecting asymptotic loss. For AdamW and RMSpropW, improved SDEs are derived under weaker assumptions than prior work, showing that decoupled weight decay provides a crucial stabilization mechanism at high noise levels by bounding the asymptotic loss in the noise level. The technical framework matches first and second moments of SDE increments to those of the discrete optimizer (Mil'shtein weak approximation), applies Itô's lemma to derive loss and covariance dynamics, and extends to infinite-variance heavy-tailed noise (Student's t with ν=2), where SignSGD's performance degrades by only ~10% compared to the Gaussian case while SGD diverges.

## Results

The paper derives the first formal SDE for SignSGD, revealing three distinct dynamical phases governed by the signal-to-noise ratio. The key quantitative finding is that for SignSGD, the asymptotic expected loss scales linearly in σ_max (the noise level), whereas for SGD it scales quadratically; additionally, noise inversely affects SignSGD's convergence rate but not SGD's. For AdamW, the asymptotic loss is upper-bounded in σ (unlike vanilla Adam, which scales linearly), demonstrating that decoupled weight decay provides crucial stabilization at high noise levels. Empirically, the derived SDEs (integrated via Euler-Maruyama) faithfully track the respective optimizers on an MLP (Breast Cancer), CNN (MNIST), Transformer (MNIST), and ResNet (CIFAR-10), with superior modeling accuracy over prior SDEs for Adam and RMSprop from Malladi et al. (2022).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between SGD, SignSGD, Adam, and AdamW in terms of noise sensitivity. SignSGD is shown to be more resilient than SGD to high noise: its asymptotic loss grows only linearly in σ (vs. quadratically for SGD), and under infinite-variance Student's t noise (ν=2), SignSGD's dynamics shift by only ~10% in the drift constant while SGD diverges entirely. AdamW outperforms vanilla Adam at high noise levels: while Adam's asymptotic loss scales linearly in σ, AdamW's is upper-bounded due to the decoupled weight decay term, which acts as a stabilizer near the minimizer. The novel batch size scaling rule (κ=√δ, ξ=κ, β̃_i=1−κ(1−β_i)) is validated on a Pythia-like 160M LLM trained on 2.5B/10B SlimPajama tokens, showing that failing to rescale θ leads to loss of the asymptotic loss level. For the SDE modeling quality, the paper's SDEs for Adam and RMSprop track the actual optimizers more faithfully than the prior SDEs of Malladi et al. (2022), which the authors argue are accurate primarily near minima rather than globally.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SDE framework for modeling optimizers (Li et al., 2017; Jastrzebski et al., 2018) to SignSGD for the first time and provides improved SDEs for AdamW and RMSpropW under weaker assumptions than prior work (Malladi et al., 2022; Zhou et al., 2020a). It departs from the convergence-rate-focused literature (Defossez et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2020b) by explicitly analyzing how gradient noise shapes the dynamics, stationary distribution, and asymptotic loss of adaptive methods rather than merely bounding worst-case convergence. The paper also contrasts with Malladi et al. (2022), whose SDEs require σ ≫ ‖∇f(x)‖ and σ ~ 1/η, by deriving models valid without these restrictive conditions, and with Zhou et al. (2020a), whose Lévy SDE relies on random coefficients that are theoretically justified only under very specific conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous theoretical explanation for why adaptive optimizers are more resilient to heavy-tailed and high-variance gradient noise than SGD: SignSGD's asymptotic loss scales linearly in noise level while SGD's scales quadratically, and AdamW's decoupled weight decay actively bounds the loss even as noise diverges. The novel batch size scaling rule for AdamW (κ = √δ with ξ = κ for weight decay) offers a practical alternative to the one in Malladi et al. (2022), validated on a 160M LLM. Open questions include extending the analysis to non-convex landscapes beyond local quadratics, other optimizers (Signum, AdaGrad, Nadam), and whether the SDE-derived insights can guide the design of hybrid optimizers that adaptively switch strategies across training phases.
