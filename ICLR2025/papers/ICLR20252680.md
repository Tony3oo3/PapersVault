## Problem

Standard object detection pipelines have two sub-optimal steps: proposals regress to the entire ground truth box (requiring extrapolation beyond their visible scope), and non-maximum suppression discards all but the top-scoring proposal per region, ignoring complementary information from other candidates. This paper asks whether redefining both the regression target and the candidate selection strategy can improve localization without architectural changes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252680/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (evaluated at 666×400 and 2000×1200 scales) and produces the same outputs as the underlying detector: class-labeled bounding boxes for detection and pixel-level segmentation masks for instance segmentation. The key structural difference is internal: during training, regression targets are the intersection regions between proposals and ground truth rather than full ground truth boxes, and during inference, final boxes are formed by merging regressed intersections from up to 5 grouped proposals before a refinement regression pass.

## Method

UoI makes two minimal modifications to the detection pipeline. First, intersection-based regression: each proposal's regression target is changed from the full ground truth box to the intersection region between the proposal and ground truth (computed via coordinate-wise max/min), so the network only predicts what is visible within the proposal's scope. Second, union-over-intersections grouping: instead of NMS selecting a single winner, proposals are grouped using the same IoU-based clustering as NMS, and the final box is formed by taking the element-wise minimum of top-left and maximum of bottom-right coordinates across all regressed intersections in the group, followed by a second regression stage that refines the combined box toward the ground truth. The total loss combines the intersection regression loss with a weighted refinement loss (λ=0.5). The approach is plug-and-play, requiring only target coordinate changes in existing regression heads and a swap of the NMS selection step, and integrates into proposal-based (Faster R-CNN, Cascade R-CNN), grid-based (YOLOv3), and query-based (Deformable DETR) architectures with minimal modifications.

## Results

On MS-COCO, UoI consistently improves mAP across all five tested architectures: Faster R-CNN (R-50: 37.4→38.1, R-101: 39.4→40.3), Mask R-CNN (R-50: 38.2→38.8, R-101: 39.8→40.9), Cascade R-CNN (R-101: 42.5→43.1), YOLOv3 (33.7→34.5), and Deformable DETR (44.3→44.8). Gains are most pronounced at stricter IoU thresholds (AP75) and for large objects (APL), e.g., Mask R-CNN R-101 improves APL from 52.5 to 54.6. For instance segmentation, UoI boosts Mask R-CNN mAP by 0.7 (R-50) and 0.9 (R-101). On PASCAL VOC, improvements are larger at high overlap thresholds, e.g., Faster R-CNN AP90 jumps from 6.8 to 11.3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UoI is applied as a plug-and-play modification to five baseline detectors, and in every case it improves mAP without architectural changes: Faster R-CNN gains +0.7 mAP (R-50) and +0.9 mAP (R-101), Mask R-CNN gains +0.6 (R-50) and +1.1 (R-101), Cascade R-CNN gains +0.6, YOLOv3 gains +0.8, and Deformable DETR gains +0.5. The largest relative improvements appear at high IoU thresholds (AP75) and for large objects (APL), confirming that UoI primarily improves localization rather than classification (classification accuracy stays at ~76.4–76.5% while localization mIoU jumps from 53.7% to 64.4%). Compared to box voting (Roman et al., 2019), UoI outperforms across all test-time augmentation settings (e.g., 38.1 vs. 37.5 mAP without TTA; 39.3 vs. 38.8 with flip+multi-scaling), and unlike box voting, UoI does not rely on TTA to be effective. On PASCAL VOC, the advantage widens at stricter thresholds: Faster R-CNN AP90 improves from 6.8 to 11.3, and Deformable DETR AP90 from 22.1 to 26.5. The method also composes with IoU-based losses (GIoU, DIoU, Alpha-IoU) and NMS variants (Soft-NMS, Cluster-NMS), consistently adding ~0.5–0.8 mAP on top of each.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes two elementary geometric operations—intersection and union—within the standard object detection pipeline, departing from the long-standing convention of regressing each proposal to the full ground truth box and then applying winner-takes-all NMS. It sits alongside prior post-processing refinements (Soft-NMS, IoU-aware NMS, box voting) but reframes the problem: rather than improving which single box survives, it asks how to combine all surviving boxes cooperatively. The intersection-based regression target is conceptually related to part-based detection and IoU-aware losses (GIoU, DIoU, Alpha-IoU), but the paper's key departure is treating the regression target as a dynamic, per-proposal quantity rather than a fixed full-object box, and replacing the selection step with a union operator over grouped proposals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of UoI—requiring only a target-coordinate change in the regression head and a grouping modification in NMS—makes it an attractive drop-in for existing pipelines across two-stage, single-stage, and transformer-based detectors. The oracle experiment suggesting that UoI's advantage grows as classification accuracy improves positions it as a formulation that could compound with future gains in classification. However, the consistent but modest gains (roughly 0.5–1.1 mAP) and the acknowledged failure mode on crowded same-class objects (merging distinct instances) limit its immediate impact, and it remains open whether the approach generalizes to keypoint-based or anchor-free detectors like FCOS without substantial architectural changes.
