## Problem

Existing multiset and distribution embeddings (sum-pooling, max-pooling, sort-based methods) either fail to be bi-Lipschitz with respect to Wasserstein distance or are restricted to fixed-size inputs. The paper asks whether one can construct a single embedding that is bi-Lipschitz on multisets of varying size, injective on general weighted distributions, and computationally efficient—while also establishing that bi-Lipschitz embedding of arbitrary distributions is fundamentally impossible.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a multiset of up to N vectors in R^d or a weighted distribution with up to N support points (points x^(i) and non-negative weights w_i summing to 1), and outputs a vector in R^m where m ≈ 2Nd suffices for bi-Lipschitzness on multisets. The embedding is parameterized by m random projection directions v^(k) ∈ S^{d-1} and m random frequencies ξ^(k) ≥ 0 drawn from a heavy-tailed distribution, and is computed in O(mNd + mN log N) time. The map is piecewise smooth and differentiable in both the input points/weights and the embedding parameters, making it directly usable inside gradient-based pipelines.

## Method

The FSW embedding computes m independent one-dimensional projections of the input distribution, extracts the quantile function Q_{v^T μ}(t) of each projected 1D distribution, and samples the cosine transform of Q at a single random frequency ξ drawn from the PDF f_ξ(ξ) = (1+ξ)^{-2}. The key insight is that the cosine transform is a linear L²-isometry, so the expected squared Euclidean distance between two FSW embeddings equals the squared sliced Wasserstein distance exactly, with standard error O(1/√m) independent of N and d. This Fourier-domain sampling (rather than direct t-domain sampling of the quantile function) is what guarantees injectivity and differentiability. The resulting embedding is bi-Lipschitz on multisets for m ≥ 2Nd+1 and injective on distributions for m ≥ 2Nd+2N−1, with a matching lower bound showing m < Nd precludes injectivity for any continuous map.

## Results

On the 1-Wasserstein distance approximation task, the FSW embedding combined with an MLP achieves the lowest relative error on 5 out of 6 datasets: 1.4% (noisy-sphere-3), 1.3% (noisy-sphere-6), 2.9% (ModelNet-small), 2.6% (ModelNet-large), and 1.1% (RNAseq), while being competitive on uniform (2.4% vs. PSWE's 2.1%). Training times are dramatically shorter, ranging from 2.2 to 14.2 minutes versus 33 minutes to 14 hours 26 minutes for competing methods. On ModelNet40 classification, the FSW-based architecture achieves 83.5% accuracy with only 31k parameters, compared to PointNet's 54.1% at 35k parameters, demonstrating superior robustness to parameter reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PSWE (Naderializadeh et al., 2021), FSW achieves lower relative error on 4 of 6 datasets (e.g., 1.4% vs. 2.2% on noisy-sphere-3, 2.9% vs. 5.7% on ModelNet-small) and is competitive on the remaining two (2.4% vs. 2.1% on uniform; 2.6% vs. 2.4% on ModelNet-large). Against N_ProductNet and N_SDeepSets (Chen & Wang, 2023), FSW wins decisively on most datasets, with the largest gaps on noisy-sphere-3 (1.4% vs. 4.6% and 36.2%) and ModelNet-large (2.6% vs. 14.0% and 16.6%). Against WPCE (Kawano et al., 2020), FSW outperforms by an order of magnitude on most datasets (e.g., 1.4% vs. 34.1% on noisy-sphere-3, 1.1% vs. 47.7% on RNAseq). Against the Sinkhorn algorithm (Cuturi, 2013), FSW achieves lower error on 5 of 6 datasets (e.g., 1.4% vs. 18.7% on noisy-sphere-3, 2.6% vs. 14.8% on ModelNet-large). On ModelNet40 classification, the FSW-based architecture is far more robust to parameter reduction than PointNet (Qi et al., 2017): at ~31k parameters, FSW achieves 83.5% accuracy while PointNet drops to 54.1% at 35k parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

The FSW embedding is explicitly positioned as a non-trivial generalization of the sort embedding (Balan et al., 2022), which handles only fixed-size multisets via project-and-sort operations. The method repurposes the sliced Wasserstein distance framework (Bonneel et al., 2015) by interpreting the Euclidean distance between sort embeddings as a Monte Carlo sample of the sliced Wasserstein distance, then extending this to variable-size multisets and weighted distributions. The key conceptual departure from prior work is sampling the quantile function in the Fourier (cosine transform) domain rather than the t-domain, which simultaneously resolves injectivity, differentiability, and distance-preservation issues that plague direct sampling approaches. This contrasts with PSWE (Naderializadeh et al., 2021), which embeds into an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space and loses injectivity under finite discretization, and with max-filtering (Cahill et al., 2022), which is computationally expensive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provably bi-Lipschitz embedding for variable-size multisets with near-optimal dimension (~2Nd), filling a gap that sort-based methods could not address. The impossibility result (Theorem 4.4) showing that no bi-Lipschitz Euclidean embedding exists for the full distribution space establishes that the achieved metric properties are essentially best possible, providing a clean theoretical boundary. Practically, the embedding's robustness to parameter reduction (40-fold with minor degradation) and its SOTA performance on Wasserstein distance learning suggest it could become a standard building block for set/multiset architectures. Open questions include extension to unbalanced or partial optimal transport, and whether the embedding's advantages persist in deeper hierarchical architectures like PointNet++.
