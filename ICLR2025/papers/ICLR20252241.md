## Problem

Visual whole-body humanoid control is extremely challenging due to the high-dimensional observation and action spaces combined with bipedal instability, yet prior work relies on simplifying assumptions such as privileged state observations, hand-designed skill primitives, or thousands of individually trained tracking policies. No existing method performs complex visual whole-body control tasks in a fully data-driven manner without reward engineering while also producing motions that humans perceive as natural. The paper also notes that no benchmark exists for evaluating visual whole-body humanoid control with a high-DoF embodiment.

## Input / Output

The high-level puppeteer agent consumes a 212-d proprioceptive state vector (joint positions/velocities, velocimeter, accelerometer, gyro, joint torques, binary contact sensors, and world-frame orientation) together with raw 64×64 RGB images from a third-person camera, and outputs 15-d reference end-effector commands (5 points in 3D space, normalized to [-1,1]) specifying target positions for the head, both hands, and both feet. The low-level tracking agent receives the same proprioceptive state plus these reference commands and produces 56-DoF joint-level torque actions. Both agents operate at the same control frequency (k=1), and the tracking agent's command interface is deliberately low-dimensional to keep it task-agnostic and reusable.

## Method

Puppeteer is a two-level hierarchical world model where both agents are algorithmically identical TD-MPC2 instances (JEPA-style latent world models with MPPI planning), differing only in their input/output spaces. The low-level tracking agent is pretrained once on 836 CMU MoCap clips retargeted to a 56-DoF CMU Humanoid, using a 50/50 mixture of offline rollouts from MoCapAct and online interaction to learn a single world model that converts any reference end-effector trajectory into physically executable joint actions. The high-level puppeteer agent is then trained from scratch via online interaction on each downstream task, learning to synthesize reference end-effector commands from visual and proprioceptive observations while the tracking agent remains frozen. A key design choice is that the hierarchy operates over data sources and input modalities rather than skill primitives or temporal abstractions, and a novel termination prediction head with soft-truncated rollouts handles episodic MDPs with contact-based termination conditions.

## Results

On the proposed 8-task visual whole-body humanoid control suite (56-DoF CMU Humanoid), Puppeteer achieves episode returns comparable to TD-MPC2 across 7 of 8 tasks within 3M environment steps, while SAC and DreamerV3 fail to reach meaningful performance. In a user study (n=51), 97.8% of participants rated Puppeteer's motions as more natural than TD-MPC2's (0.4% preferred TD-MPC2, 1.8% rated equal). On the gaps task, Puppeteer achieves 115.9±5.2 steps survival at 1M steps (vs. 66.9±9.8 for TD-MPC2) and a torso height of 96.0±0.2 cm (vs. 85.9±4.7 cm), indicating more human-like upright gait.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TD-MPC2 (the strongest baseline), Puppeteer matches or slightly trails in raw episode return on 7 of 8 tasks, with TD-MPC2 achieving higher return only on the stairs task (where it learns to 'roll' rather than walk, a form of reward hacking). However, Puppeteer is overwhelmingly preferred in naturalness: 97.8% of 51 human participants rated its motions as more human-like, and quantitative proxies (survival time, torso height) confirm more stable, upright behavior. Against SAC (model-free), Puppeteer wins decisively—SAC fails to achieve any meaningful performance on the 56-DoF tasks within the 3M-step budget, even after extensive hyperparameter tuning. Against DreamerV3 (model-based, no planning), the outcome is similar: vanilla DreamerV3 does not reach meaningful performance, and a hierarchical variant (DreamerV3 high-level + TD-MPC2 low-level) achieves only non-trivial but still poor results. The paper attributes these gaps to the critical role of planning (MPPI) in high-dimensional continuous control, which both SAC and DreamerV3 lack.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TD-MPC2 (Hansen et al., 2024) as its algorithmic backbone, applying its decoder-free JEPA-style world model and MPPI planning to a two-level hierarchical architecture for humanoid control. It departs from prior hierarchical humanoid work (MoCapAct, COMIC, DeepMimic) in three key ways: (1) a single tracking world model replaces ~2600 per-clip policies or learned skill primitives, (2) the high-level agent outputs geometric end-effector positions rather than goal embeddings, and (3) visual RGB observations are incorporated, which prior whole-body humanoid methods did not support. The concurrent HumanoidBench (Sferrazza et al., 2024) benchmarks whole-body control but uses a less expressive embodiment, privileged state only, and does not evaluate motion naturalness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to combine visual observations with data-driven whole-body humanoid control and to explicitly evaluate the naturalness of learned policies via a user study, filling a gap where prior methods optimized reward without regard for human-like motion quality. The 8-task benchmark and the finding that a single pretrained tracking model generalizes across diverse downstream tasks (including zero-shot to 3× gap lengths) provide a practical foundation for future visual humanoid research. Key limitations include the two-level hierarchy (only one level pretrained in most experiments), the focus on locomotion tasks rather than manipulation, and the absence of real-robot validation, leaving open questions about scaling the hierarchy, broadening task diversity, and sim-to-real transfer.
