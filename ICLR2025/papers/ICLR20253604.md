## Problem

Most generative models of virtual cells and tissues in spatial transcriptomics lack causal identifiability, meaning they can conflate correlation with causation and produce non-robust, non-unique explanations of gene regulation. No existing method provides a mathematically identifiable framework for disentangling intra-cellular from inter-cellular gene-gene interactions in spatial data, nor one that supports spatially resolved counterfactual perturbation predictions with guaranteed uniqueness of the learned causal structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253604/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes spatial transcriptomics data organized as a graph (nodes = cells or spots, edges = nearest-neighbor adjacency matrix J), optionally augmented with dissociated scRNA-seq data, and a perturbation specification identifying a target gene and spatial location for knockout. It produces two disentangled N×N gene-gene interaction matrices — g (inter-cellular, enforced across 1st-neighbor pairs) and g' (intra-cellular, enforced within each cell/spot) — whose entries are the Lagrange multipliers and simultaneously the GNN weights. The generative module (SCE) then takes these learned matrices plus a perturbation intervention to output a full spatial counterfactual transcriptome. At multi-cell resolution (e.g., Visium), the intra-spot matrix captures a mixture of intracellular, juxtacrine, and short-range paracrine regulation rather than purely intracellular effects.

## Method

Celcomen formulates spatial gene-gene correlation matching as a maximum-entropy optimization over the joint distribution P of all spatial transcriptomics samples, with Lagrange multipliers g and g' enforcing that expected inter- and intra-cellular correlations match observed ones. Theorem 1 (via functional calculus) shows that extremizing over P reduces the problem to minimizing an empirical log-likelihood over g and g' alone, revealing that the optimal architecture is a k-hop graph convolutional network whose weight matrices are exactly the Lagrange multipliers — making the network parameters physically meaningful 'forces' rather than arbitrary learned parameters. A mean-field approximation (s_k^α = m^α + δs_k^α) renders the partition function tractable, reducing computation from ~10^6,000,000 summations to ~10^6 gene-pair operations. Theorem 2 proves identifiability: if two parameter settings (g, g') and (h, h') yield the same distribution over all spatial samples, then g = h and g' = h, guaranteeing a unique causal structure. The inference module (CCE) trains these matrices unsupervised from data, while the generative module (SCE) performs counterfactuals by fixing the learned parameters and optimizing the likelihood of a perturbed sample, requiring no prior annotation of secreted versus cytoplasmic genes.

## Results

On the Perturb-map in-vivo validation (mouse KP lung cancer with Tgfbr2 or Jak2 knockouts), Celcomen achieves Spearman correlations of 0.28–0.47 between predicted and experimentally measured gene rankings across all lesions, significantly outperforming a random-shuffling baseline (Mann-Whitney U-test p = 0.0079). For identifiability on real human fetal spleen data, sample-specific and leave-one-out models yield Spearman correlations of 0.5–0.6 on gene-gene interaction matrices despite sharing no training samples. On synthetic data, Celcomen recovers ground-truth gene-gene interaction forces with high self-consistency, confirming its theoretical identifiability guarantee (Theorem 2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare Celcomen against other spatial transcriptomics or causal inference methods; the only baseline is a random-shuffling control on the Perturb-map dataset. Celcomen's Spearman correlations (0.28–0.47) are significantly higher than the random baseline (p = 0.0079, Mann-Whitney U-test), confirming that the learned gene-gene forces capture genuine perturbation effects rather than noise. The authors note this is the first learning method to utilize the Perturb-map dataset, so no prior method benchmarks exist for direct comparison. The identifiability experiments (fetal spleen, synthetic) serve as internal consistency checks rather than head-to-head comparisons with competing approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

Celcomen extends the Virtual Cell paradigm (Bunne et al., 2024; Roohani et al., 2024) to a Virtual Tissue setting by jointly modeling intra- and inter-cellular gene regulation, and grounds this in the causal disentanglement framework of Squires et al. (2023) and the identifiability theory of Khemakhem et al. (2020). Architecturally, it is a k-hop GNN (Nikolentzos et al., 2020; Feng et al., 2023) whose weights are provably the Lagrange multipliers of a maximum-entropy Hamiltonian, a connection not previously exploited in spatial transcriptomics. The paper departs from prior cell-communication methods (NicheNet, CellComm, Dialogue Maps) by providing the first identifiability guarantee in the spatial setting, whereas those methods remain black-box and lack mathematical uniqueness of the learned causal structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identifiability guarantee means the learned intra- and inter-cellular gene-gene interaction matrices are the unique explanation of observed spatial correlations, enabling trustworthy spatial counterfactuals (e.g., in-silico gene knockouts at specific tissue locations) that prior black-box models cannot provide. The Lagrange-multiplier-as-weight construction also opens a route to mechanistic interpretability: network parameters are physically meaningful forces recoverable from input-output behavior. Key open questions include whether the causal-sufficiency assumption (no unmeasured confounders) can be relaxed, how to handle cyclic gene regulation, and whether cell-type-aware extensions preserve identifiability. Empirically, the Perturb-map validation (Spearman 0.28–0.47) is encouraging but modest, and the method has not yet been stress-tested on the largest-scale spatial atlases.
