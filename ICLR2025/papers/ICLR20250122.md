## Problem

Denoising diffusion models trained on purely data-driven objectives do not strictly enforce the governing PDEs that generated samples must satisfy, leading to physically inconsistent outputs in scientific applications. Existing remedies such as physics-guided conditioning (PG-Diffusion) or post-hoc residual corrections (CoCoGen) lack a first-principles derivation and fail to reduce PDE residuals to physically meaningful levels. The paper closes this gap by providing a rigorous probabilistic framework that embeds PDE constraints directly into the diffusion training objective.

## Input / Output

For the Darcy flow benchmark, the U-Net takes a noisy 64×64 permeability field as input and generates the corresponding 64×64 pressure field; residuals are evaluated via second-order central finite differences matching the dataset construction. For topology optimization, the model receives 10 input channels (noisy displacement/density fields, von Mises stress, strain energy, boundary conditions, and volume fraction) at 64×64 resolution and outputs 3 channels (two displacement components and a sigmoid-constrained density field); residuals are computed by applying the precomputed FEM stiffness matrix to the predicted displacements, treating pixels as FE nodes. Inference is identical to standard diffusion sampling with no additional surrogate models or correction steps.

## Method

The core contribution is a physics-informed loss (Eq. 14) that augments the standard denoising diffusion objective with a residual penalty term under the same expectation over t and x_{0:T}. The PDE residual R(x_0^*) is evaluated on an estimate x_0^* of the clean signal obtained either as the conditional mean E[x_0|x_t] (mean estimation) or via a two-step DDIM sample (sample estimation). The residual is treated as a 'virtual observable' drawn from a Gaussian q_R centered at R(x_0^*) with variance coupled to the denoising schedule Σ_t/c, so that constraint enforcement tightens as t→0 and relaxes at noisier timesteps where estimates are less reliable. This yields a single hyperparameter c controlling the residual penalty strength, and the resulting loss is a straightforward extension of the standard training loop requiring only one additional forward pass (for sample estimation) or none (for mean estimation). A consistency proof (Proposition 1) shows that in the ideal score-matching setting, the optimal score model still recovers the true data distribution despite the added residual term, and empirically the physics loss acts as a natural regularizer against overfitting.

## Results

On the Darcy flow benchmark, PIDM reduces the PDE residual error by approximately two orders of magnitude compared to the standard diffusion model, with mean estimation (PIDM-ME) outperforming sample estimation (PIDM-SE). On the topology optimization benchmark, PIDM achieves a median R_MAE of 1.24e-3 (in-distribution) and 1.29e-3 (out-of-distribution), a median compliance error of 0.06% (in-dist) and 0.56% (out-dist), and a volume fraction error of 2.25% (in-dist) and 1.91% (out-dist). Notably, PIDM also acts as a natural regularizer: the test data loss trajectory matches or improves upon the vanilla diffusion model, indicating reduced overfitting without sacrificing generative quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PIDM outperforms all four baselines on the PDE residual (R_MAE) in both Darcy flow and topology optimization: it reduces R_MAE by ~33% (in-dist) and ~35% (out-dist) over the standard diffusion model, and by ~18% over CoCoGen on topology optimization. On compliance error (MDN %CE), PIDM achieves 0.06% in-distribution, beating PG-Diffusion (0.09%), CoCoGen (0.14%), TopoDiff-G (0.83%), and DOM (0.74%); out-of-distribution, PIDM (0.56%) beats all except the standard diffusion model (0.3%), which benefits from higher volume usage. On volume fraction error, PIDM (2.25% in-dist, 1.91% out-dist) is competitive with TopoDiff-G (1.49%, 1.80%) and DOM (1.52%, 1.59%) but slightly worse, while substantially outperforming PG-Diffusion (3.59%, 3.23%) and CoCoGen (4.00%, 3.64%). In the Darcy flow study, PG-Diffusion shows no significant residual reduction and CoCoGen only a moderate one, whereas PIDM achieves the two-orders-of-magnitude improvement. The topology-optimization-specific baselines (TopoDiff-G, DOM) require auxiliary datasets and surrogate models, whereas PIDM needs no additional data or models beyond the standard training protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two established lines of work—denoising diffusion models (Ho et al., 2020) and physics-informed neural networks (Raissi et al., 2019)—by introducing a first-principles training objective that embeds PDE residuals directly into the diffusion loss via a 'virtual observable' formulation borrowed from Rixner & Koutsourelakis (2021). It departs from prior physics-aware diffusion approaches (PG-Diffusion by Shu et al., 2023; CoCoGen by Jacobsen et al., 2024) that rely on gradient guidance or inference-time corrections, instead enforcing constraints during training with a provably consistent score-matching objective (Appendix A.1). The DDIM-based sample estimation variant (Song et al., 2021a) provides a practical mechanism to evaluate residuals at intermediate timesteps without full multi-step sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a simple, drop-in modification to standard diffusion training that yields two-orders-of-magnitude PDE residual reduction and, notably, acts as a natural regularizer against overfitting—a side benefit that suggests physics constraints improve the model's internal representation of the data-generating mechanism. Limitations include restriction to regular pixel grids (64×64) and geometrically simple domains, a single hyperparameter c requiring manual tuning, and validation on only two 2D problems. Open questions include extending to 3D and irregular meshes (via graph-based or coordinate-based architectures), more sophisticated variance schedulers for the virtual likelihood, and whether the regularization effect persists at larger scales or with more complex PDE systems.
