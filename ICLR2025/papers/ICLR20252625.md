## Problem

Decoder-only Transformers struggle with multi-step arithmetic reasoning, and this paper identifies representation collapse in intermediate layers as a key limiting factor that prevents the model from tracking successive carryovers and storing intermediate results. While chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting mitigates the issue, it requires costly human supervision and substantially increases inference time. The paper asks whether directly regularizing intermediate representations can restore reasoning capacity without explicit CoT tokens.

## Input / Output

The model consumes arithmetic problem tokens (e.g., "12345 x 67890") with two dummy pause tokens inserted between the question and answer segments, formatted as <question> <pause_start> <pause> <pause> <pause_end> <answer>. The output is the predicted answer token sequence (e.g., "838304050"). Evaluated tasks span multi-digit multiplication (4×4 and 5×5 digits), arithmetic expressions with 4–6 operators, and longest increasing subsequence with input sequence lengths of 50, 80, and 100.

## Method

Seq-VCR is a regularization loss adapted from VICReg for sequential data, applied to a linearly projected version of intermediate layer representations (projected to 2048 dimensions) to avoid the prohibitively large vocabulary dimension of the classification head. It comprises a variance term encouraging unit variance per dimension and a covariance term penalizing inter-dimension correlations, with the covariance matrix computed across the batch dimension at each sequence position. The total training objective is the standard next-token cross-entropy plus the Seq-VCR loss, trained end-to-end with the projection layer. Combined with two dummy pause tokens that provide extra computation steps without explicit CoT supervision, the approach achieves 99.5% exact-match accuracy on 5×5 digit multiplication—outperforming GPT-4 with 5-shot CoT (44%)—while running approximately 5× faster than CoT at inference.

## Results

On the 5×5 digit multiplication task, GPT-2 Small with Seq-VCR+Pause achieves 99.5% exact match accuracy, compared to 0% for Vanilla, Pause, and Seq-VCR alone, and 44% for GPT-4 with 5-shot CoT prompting. On the 4×4 multiplication task, Seq-VCR+Pause reaches 99.2% accuracy versus 25% (Vanilla), 52% (Seq-VCR alone), and 77% (GPT-4 with CoT). The method also achieves near-CoT-level accuracy on Arithmetic Expression and LIS tasks while being approximately 5× faster at inference than CoT-based approaches (0.91 vs. 0.14 normalized throughput on 5×5 multiplication).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 using 5-shot prompting (with and without CoT), as reported in Deng et al. (2024). On the 5×5 multiplication task, Seq-VCR+Pause (99.5%) dramatically outperforms GPT-4 with CoT (44%) and GPT-3.5 with CoT (5%), while all no-CoT configurations of GPT-2 Small (Vanilla, Pause, Seq-VCR alone) achieve 0%. On 4×4 multiplication, Seq-VCR+Pause (99.2%) also surpasses GPT-4 with CoT (77%) and GPT-3.5 with CoT (43%). The method matches the performance of GPT-2 Small trained with explicit CoT supervision (100%) while requiring no CoT labels and running ~5× faster at inference. On the Arithmetic Expression and LIS tasks, Seq-VCR+Pause achieves accuracy comparable to CoT-based methods, with Seq-VCR alone also showing substantial gains over Vanilla baselines as task complexity increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the variance-covariance regularization framework from VICReg (Bardes et al., 2021) and VCReg (Zhu et al., 2023)—originally developed for vision self-supervised learning—to sequential transformer data, formulating Seq-VCR as a per-position covariance regularizer across the batch dimension. It combines this with the pause-token mechanism from Goyal et al. (2023), which provides additional computation steps without explicit CoT supervision. The key conceptual departure is identifying intermediate-layer representation collapse (quantified via matrix-based entropy) as the mechanistic bottleneck for arithmetic reasoning, and showing that a lightweight regularizer targeting this collapse, paired with just two dummy tokens, can replace the need for explicit CoT training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, testable hypothesis for why small transformers fail at multi-step arithmetic (intermediate-layer collapse) and a simple remedy that achieves near-perfect accuracy on 5×5 multiplication without CoT supervision, at 5× lower inference cost than CoT. However, generalization evidence is limited: GSM8K results (20.2% vs. 43.7% for CoT) suggest the method does not transfer well to open-ended mathematical reasoning, and experiments are confined to small models (GPT-2 Small, Llama 3.2-1B). Open questions include whether Seq-VCR helps during pre-training at scale, whether it generalizes to non-arithmetic reasoning tasks, and whether the layer-specific application (best on first/last layers) reflects a deeper architectural principle.
