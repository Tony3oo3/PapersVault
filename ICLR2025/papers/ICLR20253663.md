## Problem

Existing mode connectivity research is restricted to curve-based (1D) paths between pairs of trained models, limiting exploration to narrow regions of parameter space and preventing the integration of information from more than two models simultaneously. This paper asks whether mode connectivity can be extended to 2D surfaces, enabling a broader and more comprehensive search for low-loss, high-accuracy regions that connect multiple independently trained networks.

## Input / Output

The method takes four independently trained neural networks of identical architecture (e.g., VGG16, ResNet18, ViT) as fixed corner control points, along with the original training dataset for surface optimization. The four corners are never updated; the remaining interior control points (5 for the default 3×3 grid) are initialized via bilinear interpolation and then optimized. The output is a parametric Bezier surface over (u,v)∈[0,1]² where every point corresponds to a valid model; from this surface the authors extract the single best-performing model (for merging) or uniformly sampled model outputs averaged together (for ensembling).

## Method

The core contribution is a two-phase optimization over a bivariate Bezier surface defined by Bernstein polynomial basis functions, where the four corner control points are fixed trained models and interior control points are learnable. Phase 1 optimizes only the edge curves (u=0 and u=1) to establish low-loss boundaries; Phase 2 then refines all interior control points by uniformly sampling (u,v) pairs and minimizing the expected cross-entropy loss over the surface, yielding an unbiased gradient estimate of the surface integral. A key empirical finding is that simple linear combinations of independently trained models generally produce high-loss surfaces, but the nonlinear Bezier parameterization with optimized interior points consistently recovers low-loss, high-accuracy surfaces. The authors further identify a special case—selecting corner models from a single training trajectory where pairwise linear mode connectivity holds—under which even a purely linear surface (bilinear interpolation of corners) exhibits mode connectivity without any optimization.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with VGG16, the Bezier surface model merging achieves 90.7% accuracy (vs. 89.6% average corner accuracy), and surface-based ensembling reaches 92.0% (vs. 90.4% four-corner ensembling). On CIFAR-10 with ResNet18, model merging yields 90.9% and surface ensembling reaches 92.7% (vs. 90.1% four-corner). Across CIFAR-100 (VGG16: 70.8%) and Tiny-ImageNet (VGG16: 51.1%), the method consistently identifies low-loss surfaces, though the merging gain over corner models is smaller on these harder datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external competing methods but instead compares its Bezier surface approach against three internal baselines. First, model merging via the optimized Bezier surface outperforms the average of the four corner models by 0.3–1.1% on CIFAR-10 (e.g., 90.7% vs. 89.6% for VGG16; 90.9% vs. 90.1% for ResNet18), though the gain is marginal on CIFAR-100 (70.8% vs. 70.7%). Second, surface-based ensembling (sampling 100 points on the surface and averaging outputs) beats four-corner ensembling by 1.6% for VGG16 (92.0% vs. 90.4%) and 2.6% for ResNet18 (92.7% vs. 90.1%) on CIFAR-10. Third, the paper shows that simple linear combinations of three independently trained models fail to produce a low-loss surface (Figure 3), whereas the nonlinear Bezier surface optimization succeeds, and that linear surface mode connectivity only works when corner models are selected from the same training trajectory (epochs 160–220) satisfying pairwise linear mode connectivity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mode connectivity framework from Garipov et al. (2018) and Frankle et al. (2020), which study 1D low-loss curves between pairs of trained models, to 2D Bezier surfaces connecting up to four models simultaneously. It departs from prior surface-related work (Benton et al.'s loss surface simplexes, Fort et al.'s high-dimensional wedges) by using a globally smooth nonlinear parameterization (Bernstein-polynomial-based Bezier surfaces) with a dedicated two-phase optimization algorithm, rather than piecewise-linear approximations. The model merging application builds directly on Wortsman et al.'s model soups, but replaces linear weight averaging across a single basin with surface-based exploration that can bridge across basins.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The surface-based formulation opens a higher-dimensional search space for identifying high-performing merged or ensembled models, and the practical ensembling gains (e.g., 92% vs. 90.7% best individual on CIFAR-10/VGG16) demonstrate tangible utility. However, the evaluation is limited to relatively small datasets (CIFAR-10/100, Tiny-ImageNet) and modest architectures, and the ViT result on CIFAR-10 (70.2% merging accuracy) is substantially lower than the CNN results, raising questions about generalizability to larger-scale or transformer-heavy settings. Open questions include whether the approach scales to ImageNet-scale models or LLMs, whether theoretical guarantees exist for the existence of low-loss Bezier surfaces, and how the method interacts with permutation symmetries in deeper networks.
