## Problem

Structure-based drug design (SBDD) methods are evaluated under inconsistent protocols—different train/test splits, different docking evaluators (Gnina vs. AutoDock Vina), different network architectures, and different task definitions—making fair comparison and systematic understanding impossible. Additionally, the field has focused almost exclusively on de novo molecule generation, neglecting other critical lead-optimization tasks such as linker design, fragment growing, side-chain decoration, and scaffold hopping that are essential in real drug discovery pipelines.

## Input / Output

For de novo generation, the model receives a 3D protein pocket (atom types, 3D coordinates, amino acid types, and intra-protein bonds) and generates a complete 3D ligand with atom types, continuous 3D positions, and bond connectivity. For the four lead-optimization subtasks, the input additionally includes a partial molecule context (known atoms and bonds), and the model generates only the missing substructure: a linker (≥2 atoms on the shortest path between two fragments each >5 atoms), a grown fragment, terminal non-cyclic side chains (via Bemis-Murko decomposition), or a scaffold. The binding system is formally modeled as a heterogeneous graph with protein subgraph, molecule subgraph, and cross-edges, and the generative target is p(G | C, P) where G is the to-be-generated subgraph.

## Method

CBGBench reformulates all SBDD tasks as fill-in-the-blank of a 3D complex binding graph and unifies 12 state-of-the-art methods (LiGAN, 3DSBDD, GraphBP, Pocket2Mol, TargetDiff, DiffBP, DiffSBDD, FLAG, D3FG, DecompDiff, MolCraft, VoxBind) into a single modular codebase. Methods are categorized along three axes—continuous vs. voxelized positions, one-shot vs. autoregressive generation, and domain-knowledge vs. fully data-driven—and implemented with fixed GNN backbones (GVP for autoregressive, EGNN for diffusion) to isolate architectural contributions. Evaluation spans four aspects with 20+ metrics: substructure (JSD/MAE on atom types, ring types, and 25 functional groups), chemical properties (QED, SA, LogP, LPSK), interaction (AutoDock Vina in Score/Minimize/Dock modes, IMP%, MPBG, LBE, and PLIP-based 7-type interaction pattern distributions), and geometry (bond length/angle JSD, atom-level and molecule-level clash ratios). Fair ranking uses weighted Friedman rank across all metrics, and the framework extends naturally to four lead-optimization subtasks by conditioning on partial molecular context, with case studies on real GPCR targets (ADRB1, DRD3) validating generalizability.

## Results

On the unified CBGBench protocol (CrossDocked2020, 100 test pockets, 100 molecules per pocket), MOLCRAFT (Bayesian Flow Network) achieves the best overall rank (#1) with weighted scores of 1.97 (substructure), 1.60 (chemical property), 3.30 (interaction), and 2.00 (geometry). VOXBIND (CNN-based voxel diffusion) ranks #2, while TARGETDIFF (EGNN diffusion) ranks #3. In the highest-weighted interaction category (weight 0.4), LIGAN achieves the best Vina Dock energy (−7.70 kcal/mol, 72.71% IMP), while MOLCRAFT leads in MPBG (8.38%) and VOXBIND in LBE (0.3588). GRAPHBP ranks last (#11) with near-zero scores in substructure (0.13) and geometry (0.10), and only POCKET2MOL among autoregressive methods achieves a competitive overall rank (#7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOLCRAFT (BFN, EGNN) outperforms all 11 baselines in overall ranking, leading in substructure (JSD_fg = 0.2469, JSD_rt = 0.1196) and geometry (JSD_BL = 0.2250, Ratio_cm = 0.2691), and achieving the best Vina Dock MPBG (8.38%) among GNN-based methods. VOXBIND (CNN voxel diffusion) ranks #2 overall and is particularly strong in interaction (Vina Dock E = −7.68, MPBG = 9.89) and geometry (Ratio_cc = 0.0103), demonstrating that CNN-based voxelized methods remain highly competitive. LIGAN (VAE + CNN) achieves the best interaction score (3.64) with the lowest Vina Dock energy (−7.70) and highest IMP% (72.71%), but trails in substructure and geometry. Among diffusion-based GNN methods, TARGETDIFF (EGNN) ranks #3, while DIFFBP and DIFFSBDD rank #8 and #9 respectively, with DIFFSBDD showing the weakest interaction (Vina Dock E = −5.53, MPBG = −23.51%). Domain-knowledge methods (D3FG, DECOMPDIFF, FLAG) do not clearly surpass their non-prior counterparts: D3FG ranks #6 (vs. TARGETDIFF #3), DECOMPDIFF ranks #4 (competitive but not superior), and FLAG ranks #10. GRAPHBP (EGNN autoregressive) is the worst performer overall (#11), with near-zero substructure and geometry scores and a Vina Dock MPBG of −30.03%, while POCKET2MOL (GVP autoregressive) is the only autoregressive method to achieve a competitive rank (#7), benefiting from bond-aware contrastive learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

CBGBench does not build on a single prior method but rather unifies 12 existing SBDD approaches (spanning VAE/CNN, autoregressive GNN, diffusion, and Bayesian Flow Network families) under a single 'fill-in-the-blank' graph completion formulation. It departs from prior work primarily at the evaluation and task-framing level: previous papers used inconsistent training/test splits, different docking evaluators (Gnina vs. AutoDock Vina), and partial evaluation protocols, while this paper standardizes all of these and extends the scope from de novo generation alone to four lead-optimization subtasks (linker, fragment, side chain, scaffold) under one unified probabilistic model p(G|C, P). The three-axis taxonomy (voxelized vs. continuous, one-shot vs. autoregressive, domain-knowledge vs. data-driven) provides a systematic positioning that prior individual papers lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This benchmark is likely to become the standard reference for SBDD evaluation, providing a single codebase that lowers the barrier to entry and enables fair, reproducible comparisons across method families. Two findings carry practical weight: (1) CNN-based voxelized methods remain highly competitive despite the field's pivot to GNNs and diffusion, suggesting GNN architectures for 3D point clouds still lag in expressivity; (2) incorporating physicochemical domain knowledge (fragment motifs, arm-scaffold priors) has shown limited or even negative impact on generation quality, indicating the field's assumption that more prior knowledge automatically helps is not yet validated. Open questions include effective integration of domain knowledge into training (not just sampling), whether edge-cutting techniques (DPO, ITA) can close the gap in lead optimization, and how to validate computationally-generated molecules without wet-lab experiments.
