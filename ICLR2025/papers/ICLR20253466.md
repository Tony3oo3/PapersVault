## Problem

DNNs deployed on energy-harvesting wireless sensor networks face intermittent and unpredictable power supply, yet existing methods (iNAS, Stateful, ePerceptive, DynBal) either assume fixed resource bounds or optimize only architecture/inference without training the network to expect and adapt to real-time energy fluctuations. This gap means that during energy shortfalls, systems either halt inference entirely or apply untrained approximations that propagate errors and violate service-level objectives.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes standard classification inputs (28×28 or 32×32 images, 1D time-series sensor windows, audio spectrograms, or 3-axis accelerometer vibration spectra) alongside a real-time energy availability signal E_b(t) sampled from the device's supercapacitor. The energy signal does not alter the input data but directly modulates computational behavior: it determines per-layer dropout rates, quantization bit-widths, and which QuantaTasks (atomic compute units) can be executed before a potential power loss. Output is a single class label, with the constraint that only inferences completing within the latency SLO (e.g., <500 ms) are counted as correct.

## Method

NExUME integrates three components: DynNAS (architecture search via iNAS), DynFit (intermittency-aware training), and DynInfer (intermittency-aware inference scheduling). DynFit's core mechanism samples energy traces at each training iteration and dynamically adjusts per-layer dropout rates (d_i = d_max(1 − E_b/E_max)) and quantization levels (q_j = q_min + (q_max − q_min)·E_b/E_max), so the network learns to function correctly at varying computational budgets. An adaptive regularization strategy monitors per-weight update frequencies over a sliding window to counteract uneven training caused by stochastic dropout. DynInfer decomposes inference into QuantaTasks—atomic units guaranteed to complete within the current energy budget—then applies task fusion (merging small tasks to minimize checkpointing overhead) and an O(N log N) energy-aware priority scheduler that maximizes weighted task completion under the constraint ΣE_i ≤ E_b(t). The key distinction from prior work is that energy variability is embedded in the training objective itself (Eq. 3), so the DNN is explicitly trained to degrade gracefully under low energy rather than being merely compressed to fit a fixed budget.

## Results

On the TI MSP430FR5994 board with piezoelectric energy harvesting, NExUME achieves 88.90% on Fashion-MNIST, 76.29% on CIFAR-10, 80.75% on MHEALTH, 75.16% on PAMAP2, and 80.01% on AudioMNIST, consistently outperforming all baselines. Across multiple hardware platforms and energy sources (piezoelectric, thermal, RF), NExUME delivers 6%–22% accuracy improvement over the iNAS+PT baseline with ≤5% additional computational overhead. Energy efficiency (MOps/Joule) also improves across all tested platforms, with NExUME achieving the highest values in every configuration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NExUME outperforms all six baselines across every dataset. Against the strongest SOTA baseline DynBal (Yen et al., 2023), NExUME gains 1.40 pp on FMNIST (88.90 vs 87.50), 4.54 pp on CIFAR-10 (76.29 vs 71.75), 4.65 pp on MHEALTH (80.75 vs 76.10), 0.66 pp on PAMAP2 (75.16 vs 74.50), and 1.41 pp on AudioMNIST (80.01 vs 78.60). Against the iNAS+PT baseline (Mendis et al., 2021 combined with EAP/NetAdapt from Yang et al., 2017/2018), improvements range from 5.25 pp (FMNIST) to 9.31 pp (CIFAR-10) under piezoelectric harvesting, and up to 22.44 pp on CIFAR-10 under RF harvesting on Arduino. Stateful (Yen et al., 2022) and ePerceptive (Montanari et al., 2020) are consistently outperformed by 2–10 pp depending on dataset and platform. The largest relative gains appear on CIFAR-10 and MHEALTH, where the intermittent energy profile most severely degrades fixed-architecture baselines, while the smallest gaps (PAMAP2, AudioMNIST) suggest the benefit is more modest when the task is inherently simpler.

## Relation to Prior Work

NExUME builds directly on iNAS (Mendis et al., 2021) for intermittent-aware neural architecture search, but departs from the broader line of intermittent DNN inference work (Stateful NNs, ePerceptive, DynBal, Zygarde) by integrating energy variability awareness into the training process itself rather than only at inference time. Prior methods assume a static resource budget during training and then apply runtime adaptations (checkpointing, early exit, multi-resolution inference) at deployment; NExUME instead trains the network with dynamic dropout and quantization driven by sampled energy traces, so the model learns to be robust to fluctuating energy. The QuantaTask abstraction and task-fusion scheduling extend the checkpointing paradigm from Maeng & Lucia (2018) by minimizing the number of save/restore cycles through atomic task grouping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap for battery-free IoT and EH-WSN deployments where SLO violations from power interruptions are common, and the 6–22% accuracy gains over strong baselines with <5% overhead are meaningful in that context. However, the authors acknowledge significant limitations: DynFit struggles to converge on larger architectures (MobileNetV2, ResNet) and datasets (ImageNet), the energy model relies on approximate microprofiling, and the stochastic dropout can introduce overfitting requiring careful fine-tuning. Open questions include whether the training-time energy-awareness paradigm scales to transformer-based models, whether the approach generalizes beyond the specific MSP430/Arduino/ESP32 platforms tested, and how the method performs under truly unpredictable (non-stationary) energy profiles versus the periodic traces used in evaluation.
