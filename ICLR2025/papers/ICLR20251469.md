## Problem

Existing Gaussian Splatting-based avatar methods either reconstruct only the head (discarding the body) or fail to capture high-frequency cloth textures on the upper body, because each Gaussian can only encode a single directional radiance without spatial variance. This fundamental limitation forces an impractically large number of Gaussians to represent even simple clothed geometry, producing blurry textures and floating artifacts under novel poses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251469/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a casual monocular video (smartphone or internet-sourced) preprocessed with DECA for per-frame FLAME pose/expression parameters and DWPose for 2D body landmarks (nose, neck, shoulders). It produces an animatable 3D upper-body avatar comprising FLAME-driven head Gaussians and a neural-textured chest/shoulder region, renderable at novel poses and expressions. The accelerated inference variant eliminates all MLP queries at test time, replacing the neural warping field with a homography estimated from anchor correspondences, achieving ~130 FPS on an RTX 4080 Ti.

## Method

The core contribution is a hybrid representation that splits the avatar into FLAME-driven regular Gaussians for the head and a sparse set of 1024 anchor Gaussians plus a learnable neural texture (coarse RGB + latent fine channels) for the clothed body. Instead of requiring UV mapping or accurate mesh geometry, anchor Gaussians establish fixed 3D-to-2D correspondences with a canonical texture space, which constrain a coordinate-based MLP warping field that maps image-plane pixels to texture coordinates per pose. Training proceeds in three stages: a warm-up phase optimizing only regular Gaussians, a joint optimization phase with anchor constraints and VGG perceptual loss, and a texture refinement phase that removes anchors and sharpens the body texture. For accelerated inference, all MLP outputs are cached and the neural warping field is replaced by a homography estimated via RANSAC from anchor correspondences, doubling rendering speed to ~130 FPS while preserving visual quality.

## Results

On the self-reenactment task (Table 1), the full method achieves 26.80 PSNR / 0.875 SSIM / 0.070 LPIPS on the full upper-body region, outperforming the next-best baseline GS* (25.94 / 0.854 / 0.095) and PointAvatar (25.21 / 0.851 / 0.102). On the head-only crop, the method reaches 33.06 PSNR / 0.959 SSIM / 0.028 LPIPS, again the best across all methods. The accelerated No-MLP variant retains competitive quality (25.47 PSNR full, 0.074 LPIPS) while doubling rendering speed from ~70 FPS to ~130 FPS, surpassing pure Gaussian implementations for subjects with high-frequency cloth textures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against INSTA (Zielonka et al., CVPR 2022), the proposed method wins decisively on every metric: 26.80 vs 20.59 PSNR and 0.070 vs 0.236 LPIPS on the full body, reflecting INSTA's NeRF-based representation struggling with high-frequency cloth detail. Compared to PointAvatar (Zheng et al., CVPR 2023), the advantage is more moderate but consistent—26.80 vs 25.21 PSNR full and 33.06 vs 30.64 PSNR head—showing that the anchor-Gaussian-guided texture warping captures sharper cloth patterns than isotropic point clouds. Against GaussianAvatars (Hu et al., CVPR 2024), the gap is the largest among the three listed baselines (26.80 vs 20.97 PSNR full, 0.070 vs 0.193 LPIPS), confirming that mesh-embedded Gaussians fail on intricate textures. The No-MLP variant, while slightly lower in PSNR (25.47 vs 26.80), still beats all baselines on LPIPS for the full body (0.074), indicating perceptually sharper results despite losing pose-dependent appearance. Notably, the method also improves head-region quality over all baselines, suggesting that freeing Gaussians from modeling the body yields more accurate FLAME-driven head reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Gaussian Splatting-based head avatar line (PointAvatar, SplattingAvatar, GaussianAvatars) by identifying a fundamental representation mismatch: Gaussians excel at the head (where FLAME 3DMM provides strong geometric priors) but fail on clothed upper bodies with complex textures because each Gaussian carries only a single directional radiance. The key departure is a hybrid representation that replaces body Gaussians with a neural texture warped by a coordinate-based MLP, constrained by a sparse set of 'anchor' Gaussians that provide 3D-to-2D correspondences without requiring UV mapping or accurate body geometry. The LBS skinning formulation follows GauHuman and PointAvatar, while the anchor-guided warping and the MLP-free accelerated inference (replacing the neural warping field with a RANSAC-estimated homography) are novel contributions not present in prior Gaussian avatar work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This addresses a practical gap that limits the deployment of neural avatars in video conferencing and immersive applications: most high-fidelity methods reconstruct only the head, while full-body methods sacrifice texture detail. The hybrid Gaussian-plus-texture approach with anchor constraints offers a principled way to get sharp cloth detail at 130 FPS without the Gaussian count explosion that plagues pure-splatting methods on patterned clothing. The limitation is clear — arms and hands are excluded, requiring SMPLX-style priors — and the method still depends on FLAME tracking quality for the head. An open question is whether the anchor-Gaussian warping paradigm generalizes to full-body reconstruction with articulated limbs and loose garments where even the 'simple geometry' assumption breaks down.
