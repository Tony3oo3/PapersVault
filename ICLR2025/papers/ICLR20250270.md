## Problem

Existing UAV vision-language navigation benchmarks adopt ground-based discrete action spaces (e.g., fixed forward/turn commands) that fail to capture the continuous 6-DoF flight dynamics of real UAVs, and rely solely on target descriptions that are insufficient in complex aerial environments with obstructed views and shifting perspectives. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a realistic simulation platform, a 6-DoF trajectory dataset, and an assistant-guided benchmark that acknowledges the need for external guidance in realistic UAV object search.

## Input / Output

The model consumes five-view RGB and depth images (front, rear, left, right, downward), a structured task description comprising target direction, object visual features, and surrounding environmental context, assistant instructions at one of three guidance levels (L1 high-frequency path correction, L2 low-frequency deviation correction, L3 obstacle-avoidance only), and the current UAV state (position, posture, velocity). The output is hierarchical: a high-level 6-DoF target pose (x, y, z, pitch, roll, yaw) followed by a fine-grained sequence of N intermediate 6-DoF poses that the UAV executes via the AirSim flight API, with success defined as landing within 20 meters of the target.

## Method

The UAV Navigation LLM builds on Vicuna-7B with EVA-CLIP and a Q-former to encode multi-view images into 1 context token plus 16 content tokens per view, concatenated with tokenized task and instruction text. A hierarchical trajectory decoder operates in two stages: a high-level MLLM decoder uses a learnable trajectory query token (Q_wp) to extract trajectory-specific features decoded by an MLP into the target pose, while a fine-grained path decoder combines front-view ViT features with the aligned target pose to produce the refined trajectory sequence. Training freezes most parameters and fine-tunes only the visual projector, trajectory prediction head, and LoRA layers (4% of total parameters), using cosine similarity loss for 3D angles and L1 loss for waypoints. A backtracking sampling-based DAgger strategy augments training data by reverting the UAV to its state two frames before a collision and following the teacher's trajectory, yielding more complete obstacle-avoidance trajectories. Grounding DINO is invoked for final target detection to trigger the landing sequence.

## Results

On the UAV-Need-Help Test Seen set with L1 assistant, the proposed UAV Navigation LLM with backtracking data aggregation (Ours-DA) achieves a success rate (SR) of 17.45%, oracle success rate (OSR) of 48.87%, SPL of 15.76, and navigation error (NE) of 98.66 m. Without data aggregation, the base model achieves SR=16.10%, OSR=44.26%, SPL=14.30, NE=106.28 m. Human operators with the same L1 assistant achieve SR=94.51% and SPL=77.84, revealing a substantial gap between current methods and human-level performance. On the harder subset (trajectories >250 m), Ours-DA reaches SR=14.02% versus only 4.57% for CMA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: Random (unstructured trajectory selection), Fixed Action (mapping assistant instructions to predefined discrete moves), and CMA (Cross-Modal Attention, Anderson et al. 2018, adapted to output continuous trajectories). The proposed method outperforms CMA by an average of ~5% SR across all assistant levels on the Test Seen set; with L1 assistant, Ours-DA achieves SR=17.45% vs. CMA's 8.37% (more than 2× improvement), and OSR=48.87% vs. 18.72%. On the Hard subset, the gap widens further: Ours-DA reaches SR=14.02% while CMA manages only 4.57%. The Random and Fixed baselines confirm that without learned visual-language understanding, the task is nearly intractable (SR < 3%). Notably, even the best model remains far below human performance (SR=94.51%), underscoring the difficulty of realistic 6-DoF UAV navigation in complex outdoor environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLN line of work from ground-based agents (R2R, TouchDown) and early UAV VLN efforts (AerialVLN, CityNav, AVDN) by replacing their discrete 2–4 DoF action spaces with continuous 6-DoF flight trajectories, arguing that the kinematic mismatch makes direct transfer of ground-based formulations inadequate. Methodologically, it builds on DAgger (Ross et al., 2011) for imitation learning and extends it with a backtracking sampling mechanism for collision recovery, while leveraging the MLLM paradigm (Vicuna-7B + EVA-CLIP + Q-former) recently popularized by LLaMA-VID and Navid for multimodal navigation. The assistant-guided formulation (UAV-Need-Help) departs from prior UAV VLN benchmarks that rely solely on target descriptions, introducing a tiered guidance mechanism that has no direct analogue in the ground-based VLN literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TRAVEL platform and 12k-trajectory 6-DoF dataset fill a critical infrastructure gap: prior UAV VLN datasets collected trajectories by simply modifying positions rather than simulating realistic flight dynamics, limiting their transferability to real-world deployment. The enormous performance gap between the best model (17.45% SR) and human operators (94.51% SR) under the same L1 assistant setting underscores that realistic continuous-trajectory UAV VLN remains far from solved, and the assistant mechanism serves as a practical bridge while autonomous capabilities mature. Key open questions include whether the model can operate effectively without any assistant (no-assist SR drops to 6.25%), how well sim-to-real transfer works given the UE4/AirSim rendering, and whether the hierarchical MLLM + fine-grained decoder architecture scales to longer-horizon or multi-object tasks.
