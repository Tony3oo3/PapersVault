## Problem

Despite the rapid advancement of large multi-modal models (LMMs), their capability to handle the full pipeline of oracle bone inscription (OBI) processing—from character recognition on eroded original bones to deciphering undeciphered ancient characters—remains entirely unexplored. No prior benchmark systematically evaluates LMMs on OBI-specific tasks that demand fine-grained visual perception combined with domain expertise in ancient Chinese paleography.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253697/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of the 5,523 test instances pairs an OBI image (spanning five visual forms: original bone at ~1529×1192 px, inked rubbings at ~435×538 px, fragments at ~265×313 px, cropped characters at ~50–135 px, and handprinted characters at ~400×400 px) with a natural-language question in one of four types (What, Yes-or-No, How, Where). Outputs are task-specific: pixel-level bounding boxes (mIoU), character counts (MRE), binary adjacency judgments or rejoining probabilities (Acc@k), class labels (Acc@1/Acc@5), ranked retrieval lists (Recall@k, mAP), and free-text interpretations scored via BERTScore against expert golden descriptions extracted from an authoritative Chinese character database.

## Method

OBI-Bench is a VQA-style benchmark organized along two evaluation axes: perception granularity (coarse global content to fine pixel-level localization) and processing stage (from excavated original bones to synthetic handprinted characters). The authors introduce two new datasets to fill critical gaps: O2BR (800 original oracle bone images with 4,2,11 character-level bounding boxes, annotated by domain experts using LabelImg) and OBI-rejoin (200 complete OBI pieces manually fractured into 483 rejoinable fragments by three OBI experts and one senior scholar). Four question types—What (open-ended description), Yes-or-No (binary judgment), How (quantitative counting or probability), and Where (bounding-box localization)—probe progressively finer visual perception. Twenty-three LMMs (6 proprietary including GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro, Qwen-VL-Max; 17 open-source including InternVL2, LLaVA-NeXT, mPLUG-Owl3) are evaluated zero-shot, with a 5-round averaging strategy to mitigate output variability. For deciphering, rather than mapping to modern Chinese characters, the benchmark requires free-text interpretation scored by BERTScore against expert-written golden descriptions, capturing the full semantic and cultural context of each character.

## Results

On the deciphering task, GPT-4o (0.3876) and GPT-4V (0.3867) match or slightly exceed public-level human performance (0.3872 BERTScore), while Gemini 1.5 Pro reaches 0.3810. On recognition, even the best LMM (GPT-4o, 0.6114 cosine similarity on O2BR 'What' queries) remains far below the human baseline (0.9364), and all LMMs underperform on pixel-level locating (mIoU ≤ 0.1126 vs. human 0.9272). On rejoining, GPT-4o achieves 78.47% Acc@10 and Gemini 1.5 Pro reaches 76.67%, indicating potential for narrowing candidate fragments, while open-source LMMs average only 18.65% Acc@10. On classification, GPT-4o attains 72.75% accuracy on HWOBC (handprinted), dropping to 62.50% on the noisier OBI125 dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o and GPT-4V are the top proprietary LMMs on deciphering (0.3876 and 0.3867 BERTScore), matching or marginally exceeding the public-level human baseline (0.3872), while Gemini 1.5 Pro trails slightly at 0.3810. Proprietary LMMs outperform open-source LMMs by an average of 145.9% across all deciphering sub-tasks; the best open-source model, InternLM-XComposer2-VL (7B), reaches only 0.2304, well below the human level. On recognition 'Where' queries (mIoU), Gemini 1.5 Pro (0.1126 on O2BR) surpasses GPT-4o (0.0038) by two orders of magnitude due to its native coordinate-return capability, though both remain far below the human score of 0.9272. On rejoining, GPT-4o leads proprietary models (32.21% Yes-or-No, 78.47% Acc@10) while InternVL2-Llama3-76B is the strongest open-source model (20.13%, 31.68% Acc@10), yet all open-source models average only 3.85% Acc@1. GLM-4V consistently underperforms across all tasks (e.g., 39.17% on recognition Yes-or-No vs. ~99% for GPT-4o), attributed to answer-preference bias. On classification, GPT-4o (72.75% on HWOBC) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (68.75%) lead proprietary models, while open-source models cluster around 40–47%, with the gap widening on noisier datasets like OBI125.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the LMM evaluation benchmark paradigm (in the tradition of MMBench, MMMU, and SEED-Bench) for the highly specialized domain of oracle bone inscription (OBI) research, where prior work has focused on training task-specific deep learning models (CNNs, GANs, diffusion models) rather than evaluating general-purpose multi-modal models. It also fills two critical gaps in the OBI data landscape by introducing the O2BR dataset (original oracle bone recognition) and the OBI-rejoin dataset (fragment rejoining), neither of which had publicly available counterparts. Conceptually, the paper departs from the OBI-specific modeling literature by treating LMMs as zero-shot, prompt-driven tools rather than architectures requiring domain-specific training, and from the general LMM benchmark literature by grounding evaluation in expert-annotated, domain-specific tasks spanning five distinct processing stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OBI-Bench is the first systematic evaluation of whether general-purpose LMMs can assist in the full pipeline of ancient script research, from raw fragment perception to high-level cultural interpretation. The key open question it surfaces is whether LMMs' creative reasoning in deciphering (rivaling untrained humans) can be harnessed to generate genuinely new hypotheses for the >50% of OBI characters that remain undeciphered, or whether these outputs are merely plausible-sounding but unverified. Limitations include the reliance on BERTScore as a proxy for deciphering quality (no dedicated OBI decipherment metric exists), zero-shot-only evaluation, and small sample sizes for some sub-tasks (e.g., 40–50 images for deciphering). The finding that LMMs excel at creative interpretation but fail at fine-grained perception suggests that domain-specific fine-tuning or hybrid pipelines (LMM + specialized perception modules) may be the most promising path forward.
