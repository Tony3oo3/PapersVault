## Problem

Existing unified vision-language models either assemble separate expert models for understanding and generation (e.g., NExT-GPT pairs a language model with a diffusion model) or force all modalities through a single autoregressive paradigm (e.g., Chameleon), which is inefficient for high-resolution image generation due to the large number of sequential sampling steps. The paper asks whether one single transformer can natively handle both multimodal understanding and generation by adaptively applying the most suitable modeling paradigm—autoregressive for text, diffusion for images—within a unified architecture.

## Input / Output

All inputs are tokenized into a unified discrete sequence: images are encoded into 16×16 (256×256) or 32×32 (512×512) discrete tokens via a MAGVIT-v2 lookup-free quantizer with codebook size 8,192, while text uses the standard LLM tokenizer. A unified prompting format with special task tokens ([MMU], [T2I]) and boundary tokens ([SOT]/[EOT], [SOI]/[EOI]) structures the sequence so the same model handles VQA, text-to-image, inpainting (image + mask + prompt), and mixed-modality video keyframe generation. For generation, the model starts from all-[MASK] image tokens and iteratively denoises them in ~25–50 steps (vs. ~1024 for autoregressive baselines), producing a 256×256 or 512×512 image decoded by the image tokenizer.

## Method

Show-o is built on Phi-1.5 (1.3B) with an expanded embedding layer for 8,192 discrete image tokens and QK-Norm added to each attention layer. The key architectural innovation is the omni-attention mechanism, which applies causal attention to text tokens and full (bidirectional) attention to image tokens within the same sequence, with the attention mask adaptively determined by the input format (e.g., in VQA, text tokens attend to all preceding image tokens; in text-to-image, image tokens attend to all preceding text tokens). Training combines two objectives: standard next-token prediction (NTP) for text and mask-token prediction (MTP) for images, where image tokens are randomly replaced with [MASK] at a time-step-dependent ratio and the model reconstructs them conditioned on unmasked tokens and text—effectively a simplified absorbing discrete diffusion (MaskGIT-style) that shares the same cross-entropy loss form as NTP. Notably, Show-o eliminates the separate text encoder used by diffusion models, relying on the LLM backbone itself to encode text conditional information, and employs classifier-free guidance at inference by randomly dropping text conditioning during training.

## Results

On multimodal understanding, Show-o (1.3B) achieves POPE 80.0, MME 1097.2, Flickr30k 62.5, VQAv2 69.4, GQA 58.0, and MMMU 26.7, with the improved variant Show-o† (using CLIP-ViT continuous representations) reaching POPE 84.5, MME 1232.9, GQA 61.0. On text-to-image generation, Show-o achieves a GenEval overall score of 0.68 with only 1.3B parameters, surpassing SD3 (2B, 0.62) and SDXL (2.6B, 0.55), and attains a zero-shot FID of 9.24 on MSCOCO 30K with just 35M training images, outperforming GLIDE (5B, 12.24) and DALL-E 2 (6.5B, 10.39).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the apple-to-apple understanding-only baseline LLaVA-v1.5-Phi-1.5 (1.3B), Show-o† matches or exceeds it on POPE (84.5 vs 84.1), MME (1232.9 vs 1128.0), and GQA (61.0 vs 56.5), while trailing slightly on Flickr30k (67.6 vs 69.6), VQAv2 (74.7 vs 75.3), and MMMU (27.4 vs 30.7). Among unified models, Show-o (1.3B) outperforms NExT-GPT (13B) on VQAv2 (69.4 vs 66.7) and Chameleon (34B) on VQAv2 (69.4 vs 66.0), despite being 10–26× smaller. On GenEval, Show-o (1.3B) achieves 0.68 overall, beating SD3 (2B, 0.62), SDXL (2.6B, 0.55), DALL-E 2 (6.5B, 0.52), and all unified baselines including SEED-X (17B, 0.49) and Chameleon (7B, 0.39). On MSCOCO FID, Show-o (1.3B, 35M images) achieves 9.24, outperforming GLIDE (5B, 250M, 12.24) and DALL-E 2 (6.5B, 650M, 10.39), though trailing larger generation-only models like Imagen (3B, 7.27) and RAPHAEL (3B, 6.61) that use substantially more training data. Show-o also surpasses unified generation baselines SEED-X (17B, FID 14.99) and DreamLLM (7B, FID 8.76) on FID, while matching DreamLLM's GenEval score (0.68 vs 0.66 for Emu3).

## Relation to Prior Work

Show-o departs from the fully autoregressive paradigm of Chameleon and Emu3 by introducing a hybrid modeling strategy within a single transformer: text tokens are processed with causal (autoregressive) attention while image tokens use full attention with mask token prediction (a simplified discrete diffusion à la MaskGIT). It also diverges from assembly-based unified systems like NExT-GPT and SEED-X, which bolt a separate diffusion model onto a language model, by eliminating the external text encoder and handling both modalities in one network. The architecture inherits its backbone from Phi-1.5, its image tokenizer from MAGVIT-v2, and its inference sampling strategy from MaskGIT/Muse, but the omni-attention mechanism and unified prompting format are novel contributions that tie these components together.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Show-o demonstrates that a single 1.3B transformer can be competitive with or surpass much larger unified models (13B NExT-GPT, 34B Chameleon) on both understanding and generation benchmarks, suggesting that architectural design (mixed AR + diffusion) may matter more than raw parameter count for unified multimodal systems. The ~20× reduction in sampling steps over autoregressive image generation and the zero-fine-tuning support for inpainting/extrapolation are practically significant for deployment. Key open questions include whether the discrete-token approach scales favorably to higher resolutions and video, whether the understanding gap versus CLIP-based MLLMs closes with more data, and whether the omni-attention design generalizes to additional modalities (audio, action).
