## Problem

Cross-attention (CA) layers in text-to-image diffusion models are widely leveraged for visual generative tasks, yet their internal structure remains poorly understood at the individual head level. Existing methods such as Prompt-to-Prompt and Attend-and-Excite manipulate entire CA layers without fine-grained control over specific attention heads, leaving no principled way to identify which heads encode particular human-specified visual concepts or to steer those concepts during generation.

## Input / Output

The HRV construction phase consumes 2,100 random generation prompts (1,000 ImageNet class names + 1,100 from PromptHero) and 34 human-specified visual concepts, each paired with 10 GPT-4o-generated concept-words, to produce a 128-dimensional relevance vector per concept (1300-dimensional for SDXL). At inference time, the method takes a text prompt (for generation), a source image plus target prompt (for editing via P2P-HRV), or a multi-concept prompt (for A&E-HRV), and outputs a generated or edited image at 512×512 resolution using 50 PNDM timesteps with classifier-free guidance at scale 7.5. The rescaling is applied only to semantic-token CA maps, leaving special tokens (SOT/EOT) unchanged.

## Method

The core contribution is a training-free procedure to construct Head Relevance Vectors (HRVs): during random image generation, at every timestep and CA head position, the image query is cross-attended against concatenated key-projected embeddings of one sampled concept-word per visual concept; an argmax over the spatially-averaged CA maps identifies the best-matching concept, and that concept's HRV is incremented at the corresponding head index. The argmax step is critical because CA heads exhibit up to 8.1× variation in pre-softmax L1-norm scale, which would otherwise bias accumulation toward high-scale heads. HRVs are validated via an ordered weakening analysis that sequentially multiplies target heads' CA maps by −2 from most-to-least relevant (MoRHF) versus least-to-most (LeRHF), showing the target concept disappears significantly faster under MoRHF. Two steering operations are then defined: concept strengthening (rescale the h-th CA map by the h-th HRV element) and concept adjusting (rescale by 2·HRV_desired − 1·HRV_undesired), which are plugged into P2P for image editing and Attend-and-Excite for multi-concept generation without any model fine-tuning.

## Results

On polysemous word disambiguation, SD-HRV reduces the human-perceived misinterpretation rate from 63.0% (Stable Diffusion) to 15.9%. For image editing, P2P-HRV achieves Pareto-optimal CLIP and BG-DINO scores across three object attributes (Color, Material, Geometric Patterns), and improves CLIP scores by 4.20% (Image Style) and 9.91% (Weather Conditions) over the previous SOTA (PnP), while receiving 2.39× and 2.53× more human preference votes respectively. For multi-concept generation, A&E-HRV outperforms Attend-and-Excite by 2.3%–6.3% across Full Prompt, Min. Object, and BLIP-score metrics on both Type 1 and Type 2 prompts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against eight baselines across three tasks. In image editing, P2P-HRV dominates all five methods (SDEdit, P2P, PnP, MasaCtrl, FPE) on both CLIP alignment and BG-DINO structure preservation for object attributes, achieving Pareto-optimal performance. For image attributes, P2P-HRV outperforms PnP (the previous SOTA) by 4.20% on Image Style CLIP (0.3424 vs 0.3286) and 9.91% on Weather Conditions CLIP (0.3348 vs 0.3046), and receives 2.39× and 2.53× more human preference votes than the second-best methods. In multi-concept generation, A&E-HRV consistently beats A&E (the prior SOTA) by 2.3%–6.3% on all six metrics, while both substantially outperform Stable Diffusion and Structured Diffusion. In polysemous word disambiguation, SD-HRV reduces misinterpretation from 63.0% to 15.9%, a 47.1-point absolute improvement over vanilla Stable Diffusion. The method never loses to any baseline on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of mechanistic interpretability (Olah et al., 2018; Templeton et al., 2024) and practical control of text-to-image diffusion models. Prior work on cross-attention in T2I models—P2P for image editing and Attend-and-Excite for multi-concept generation—treats CA layers as monolithic units, swapping or rescaling entire CA maps without distinguishing individual heads. The paper departs from this by introducing Head Relevance Vectors (HRVs), a training-free construction that assigns per-head importance scores for human-specified visual concepts, drawing conceptually from Gandelsman et al.'s (2023) text-based decomposition of CLIP self-attention but extending it to the generative U-Net's cross-attention heads and making the result directly actionable for steering generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HRV framework provides the first head-level interpretability tool for T2I diffusion models that is simultaneously validated (via ordered weakening analysis) and practically useful (reducing polysemous misinterpretation from 63% to 15.9%, achieving Pareto-optimal image editing, and improving multi-concept generation). The finding that head-concept relevance patterns are stable across generation timesteps (t-SNE separates concepts but not timesteps) suggests these are architectural properties rather than transient artifacts, opening the door to pre-computed, model-specific 'concept maps' for CA heads. Limitations include the need for manual specification of desired/undesired concepts, evaluation on only 34 concepts, and some residual failure cases; open questions include whether HRVs can be constructed automatically via LLMs, whether they transfer across model families beyond the Stable Diffusion lineage, and whether head-level control can be leveraged for safety alignment in generative models.
