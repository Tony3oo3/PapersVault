## Problem

Neural ODEs trained on data from multiple physical environments struggle to generalize to unseen parameter regimes, particularly when the governing parameters are unobserved during data collection. Existing meta-learning approaches either require retraining per environment (OPE), fail to extrapolate (OFA, LEADS), or rely on hypernetworks that hinder parallelism and scalability (CoDA). The paper addresses how to learn a single, generalizable vector field that adapts rapidly to new environments with minimal data while providing interpretability and uncertainty estimates.

## Input / Output

The method consumes trajectories from m related environments (S trajectories each, N time steps over horizon T) along with their initial conditions, and learns a shared network weight vector θ plus m environment-specific latent context vectors ξ^e ∈ R^{d_ξ}. At prediction time, it produces p candidate trajectories (one per context in the pool P) from which the main prediction (j=e) is extracted for evaluation, while the ensemble spread yields uncertainty estimates (mean and standard deviation). During few-shot adaptation, the network is frozen and only a single new context vector ξ^{e'} is optimized from S'≪S trajectories, converging in seconds.

## Method

NCF parameterizes a single environment-agnostic vector field f_θ(x, ξ) and, for each environment e, Taylor-expands f_θ around p other environments' context vectors ξ^j to generate p candidate Neural ODEs whose trajectories are all supervised against the ground truth. This Taylor expansion creates 'contextual self-modulation': contexts are pulled together (to keep the approximation valid) while being pushed apart by data diversity, a friction mechanism absent in CAVIA and CoDA. The second-order expansion is expressed entirely via Jacobian-Vector Products (Proposition 1), avoiding explicit Hessian materialization. Training uses proximal alternating minimization (NCF-t2) or ordinary alternating minimization (NCF-t1), alternating updates of θ and all ξ^{1:m}. A 3-network architecture (state encoder, context encoder, main network) projects both inputs into a shared representational space before interaction, and the framework is massively parallelizable across environments, trajectories, and pool members.

## Results

NCF-t2 achieves state-of-the-art Out-of-Distribution (OoD) MSE on 5 out of 6 benchmark problems (LV, GO, SM, BT, NS), with particularly large margins on the non-linear Sel'kov Model (2.03×10⁻³ vs. CoDA's 8.28×10⁻³) and Brusselator (3.77×10⁻¹ vs. CoDA's 19.47×10⁻¹). CoDA retains its advantage only on the Gray-Scott problem (OoD MSE 0.75×10⁻³ vs. NCF-t2's 3.40×10⁻³). CAVIA is consistently the weakest baseline across all six problems, with OoD MSEs an order of magnitude or more worse than NCF-t2 on most tasks. The first-order variant NCF-t1 also fails on highly non-linear problems (e.g., SM OoD MSE 677.2×10⁻³), confirming the necessity of second-order Taylor expansion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CAVIA (Zintgraf et al., 2019), NCF-t2 dominates on all six problems by a wide margin — e.g., OoD MSE of 2.03×10⁻³ vs. 859.1×10⁻³ on SM and 3.77×10⁻¹ vs. 22.6×10⁻¹ on BT — with CAVIA exhibiting clear overfitting to its few-shot trajectories. Against CoDA (Kirchmeyer et al., 2022), NCF-t2 wins on five of six OoD problems, with the largest gaps on the non-linear Sel'kov Model (4.1× improvement) and Brusselator (5.2× improvement), while CoDA retains its edge on the Gray-Scott system (0.75×10⁻³ vs. 3.40×10⁻³), suggesting CoDA's linear hypernetwork decoder is better suited to that particular reaction-diffusion structure. The first-order NCF-t1 variant, which uses ordinary alternating minimization instead of proximal, fails to resolve the Hopf bifurcation in SM (OoD MSE 677.2×10⁻³) and underperforms NCF-t2 on all non-linear problems, validating the role of second-order Taylor expansion in preventing spurious associations. On the Simple Pendulum interpolation task (Table 1), NCF-t1 reduces adaptation MSE by ~4 orders of magnitude over OFA (0.0356×10⁻³ vs. 115000×10⁻³) and by ~4 orders over OPE (0.0356×10⁻³ vs. 459.0×10⁻³), while also being ~11× faster to adapt than OPE.

## Relation to Prior Work

NCF builds directly on the context-informed dynamics paradigm introduced by CoDA (Kirchmeyer et al., 2022), which uses a linear hypernetwork to generate environment-specific vector field weights from latent context vectors. The key departure is replacing this hypernetwork with a Taylor expansion of the shared vector field around neighboring context vectors, enabling 'contextual self-modulation' where contexts influence each other's predictions — a mechanism absent from CoDA, CAVIA, and other contextual meta-learning approaches. The paper also extends the identifiability theory of Blanke & Lelarge (2024) from linearly parameterized systems to nonlinear settings via higher-order Taylor expansion, and grounds its training procedure in proximal alternating minimization with convergence guarantees. The Gen-Dynamics benchmark initiative addresses the lack of standardized evaluation protocols in the generalizable dynamical systems literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NCF provides a practical path toward foundational models for physical sciences by enabling fast, few-shot adaptation to unseen parameter regimes without retraining the shared vector field, while simultaneously offering interpretability (recovering physical parameters up to a linear transform) and built-in uncertainty estimation. The main limitations are the regularity assumption (NCF breaks down for fundamentally discontinuous vector fields) and the proliferation of hyperparameters (context dimension, pool size, pool-filling strategy, proximal coefficient). Open questions include whether the Taylor-based self-modulation mechanism generalizes to higher-dimensional parameter spaces, whether the convergence guarantees hold under less restrictive assumptions, and how NCF scales to truly large-scale PDE problems beyond the current benchmarks.
