## Problem

Visual robotic manipulation policies trained on fixed camera viewpoints suffer severe performance degradation when the camera position shifts during deployment, because learned representations entangle task-relevant state with viewpoint-specific details like lighting and perspective. Existing approaches (MVWM, RoboUniView, RT-X) either rely on a single encoder that cannot fully disentangle these components, require 3D calibration, or depend on massive data scale, leaving the complete separation of view-invariant state from viewpoint information unresolved.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 128×128 RGB images from fixed-position third-person cameras. During autoencoder training, it requires multi-view image tuples with known view labels (20 viewpoints spanning 180° azimuth in Meta-world, 20 viewpoints over 270° in PandaGym) plus unlabeled multi-view data from Open X-Embodiment. The VIE outputs a quantized sequence of M×64 patch-level features (M=64 patches of 16×16) serving as the view-invariant state, while the VDE outputs a continuous view-dependent encoding. The world model and policy operate entirely in VIR space, and the final output is a robot action (end-effector displacement + gripper torque for Meta-world; 3D position + gripper aperture for PandaGym).

## Method

ReViWo decomposes each visual observation into a view-invariant representation (VIR) and a view-dependent representation (VDR) using two parallel ViT-based encoders: the VIE applies VQ-VAE quantization (codebook size 512, embedding dim 64) to produce discrete, task-relevant features, while the VDE uses a linear projection to capture continuous viewpoint-specific features. A transformer decoder (Genie-style parallel patch output) reconstructs a target image by fusing the VIR from one image with the VDR from a different image, forcing the model to disentangle state from viewpoint. The training objective combines pixel reconstruction loss, VQ commitment/quantization losses, and InfoNCE contrastive losses that enforce VIR consistency across viewpoints for the same state and VDR consistency for the same viewpoint; a theoretical analysis via mutual information bounds proves that minimizing these contrastive terms upper-bounds the mutual information between VIR and VDR, guaranteeing disentanglement. The learned VIR then serves as the state space for a COMBO-based offline model-based RL pipeline: an ensemble of MLP world models predicts next-state VIRs, and the policy is optimized using both offline data and model-generated rollouts.

## Results

On Meta-world, ReViWo maintains 30.6% success rate on Drawer Open and 71.4% on Window Close under a 10° camera offset (CIP), while COMBO collapses to 0% and 22.8% respectively. Under continuous camera shaking (CSH), ReViWo achieves 25.6% (Drawer Open) and 23.3% (Window Close), whereas COMBO drops to near-zero. On the real-world ALOHA robot, ReViWo-BC retains 60% success on Stage 2 and 50% on Stage 3 under a +15° azimuth / −15° elevation viewpoint shift, while ACT (Action Chunking Transformer) drops to 0% on both stages.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReViWo consistently outperforms all three baselines under viewpoint disturbance. Against COMBO (offline model-based RL with VAE representation), the gap is stark: COMBO drops from 94.4% to 0% on Drawer Open and from 98.9% to 22.8% on Window Close under CIP, while ReViWo sustains 30.6% and 71.4% respectively; under camera shaking, COMBO is near-zero whereas ReViWo retains 25.6% and 23.3%. Against MVWM (multi-view masked autoencoder + world model), ReViWo achieves higher success rates on both CIP and CSH conditions across all four evaluated tasks (Door Open, Drawer Open, Window Close, Reach). On the real-world ALOHA benchmark, ReViWo-BC (behavior cloning with the learned view-invariant representation) matches ACT on the training viewpoint (100%/80%/60% across three stages) but dramatically outperforms it under the viewpoint shift, maintaining 60%/50% on Stages 2/3 versus ACT's 0%/0%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the offline model-based RL framework of COMBO for policy learning and incorporates VQ-VAE quantization losses into its autoencoder training objective, while adopting the simultaneous patch-output decoder design from Genie. Conceptually, it departs from prior multi-view manipulation approaches (MVWM, RoboUniView) that learn a single unified representation by explicitly decomposing observations into view-invariant and view-dependent components via dual encoders, and provides a mutual-information-based theoretical justification for why the contrastive training objective enforces this disentanglement. The RT-X line of work addresses viewpoint robustness through data scale rather than architectural disentanglement, which the authors position as complementary rather than competing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Viewpoint robustness is a practical deployment bottleneck for vision-based robotic manipulation, and the explicit VIR/VDR decomposition offers a more interpretable and data-efficient path than scaling data volume. Key limitations include the reliance on labeled viewpoints (mitigated but not eliminated by Open X-Embodiment integration), the use of a simple MLP world model, and the relatively small experimental scale. Open questions include whether the view-label dependency can be removed via information bottleneck or masking, whether the framework scales to more complex dynamics with recurrent state-space world models, and whether pre-trained vision backbones (e.g., CLIP) can replace the from-scratch ViT encoders.
