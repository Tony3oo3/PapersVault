## Problem

Sampling from the posterior in Bayesian inverse problems with diffusion model priors requires estimating an intractable guidance term at every denoising step, and existing approximations (DPS, PGDM, DDNM) introduce significant errors when the posterior is multimodal, the forward operator is nonlinear, or the observation is heavily noisy. No prior method offers a principled mechanism to trade off the accuracy of the guidance approximation against the fidelity of the prior backward transition, leaving a gap in handling challenging inverse problems with high fidelity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a noisy or incomplete observation y (e.g., a downsampled image, a blurred image, a single ECG lead, or a partial ECG block), a pre-trained diffusion model (pixel-space DDPM or latent diffusion model with VQ autoencoder), and a differentiable likelihood p(y|x). It outputs posterior samples x at the original resolution: 256×256 RGB images for FFHQ/ImageNet tasks, or 12-lead 10-second ECG waveforms for the PTB-XL experiments. For latent diffusion models, the observation y is evaluated in pixel space via the decoder while all diffusion steps operate in the compressed latent space, and the final sample is decoded back to pixel space.

## Method

MGPS introduces a midpoint decomposition of each backward transition π_{k|k+1}: instead of directly denoising from step k+1 to k, it first denoises to an intermediate midpoint X_{ℓ_k} (with ℓ_k < k) using a Gaussian variational distribution fitted by M stochastic gradient steps on a KL upper bound, then re-noises from the midpoint to X_k via a closed-form Gaussian bridge kernel. The free parameter ℓ_k (typically set to ⌊k/2⌋) balances two approximation errors: the guidance term p(y|m_{0|ℓ_k}^θ(x_{ℓ_k})) is more accurate at smaller ℓ_k, while the DDPM prior transition is more accurate when ℓ_k is close to k. The variational parameters (mean and diagonal log-variance) are initialized from the DDPM transition and refined with a reparameterized Monte Carlo gradient estimator that involves one vector-Jacobian product per step. A warm-start strategy further refines early diffusion steps by introducing a second intermediate transition through step 1, improving stability for challenging tasks.

## Results

On the Gaussian mixture toy problem (d=20, d_y=1), MGPS with η=0.75 achieves a sliced Wasserstein distance of 2.11±0.30, outperforming DPS (8.93), PGDM (2.44), DDNM (4.24), DIFFPIR (4.14), and REDDIFF (6.70). On image reconstruction tasks (FFHQ, 256×256, σ_y=0.05), MGPS achieves the best LPIPS on 9 out of 10 tasks, with particularly large gains on nonlinear problems: JPEG dequantization (0.16 vs. 0.39 for DPS), phase retrieval (0.11 vs. 0.46 for DPS), and high dynamic range (0.07 vs. 0.49 for DPS). On ECG reconstruction from lead I (PTB-XL), MGPS with only 50 diffusion steps achieves balanced diagnosis accuracy of 0.94 for Atrial Fibrillation and 0.92 for LBBB, surpassing all baselines and approaching ground-truth levels (0.94 and 0.98).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MGPS consistently outperforms DPS (Chung et al., 2023) across nearly all tasks, with the largest margins on nonlinear problems: JPEG dequantization (0.16 vs. 0.39 LPIPS), phase retrieval (0.11 vs. 0.46), and high dynamic range (0.07 vs. 0.49) on FFHQ. Against PGDM (Song et al., 2023a), MGPS wins on 8 of 10 FFHQ tasks; PGDM is particularly weak on deblurring (0.87 vs. 0.15 for Gaussian deblur) and JPEG (1.10 vs. 0.16). DDNM (Wang et al., 2023) and DIFFPIR (Zhu et al., 2023) are competitive on linear tasks (e.g., DIFFPIR achieves 0.12 on Gaussian deblur vs. MGPS's 0.15) but are not applicable to nonlinear problems, where MGPS dominates. REDDIFF (Mardani et al., 2024) is the strongest nonlinear baseline but still trails MGPS by 2–4× on LPIPS for JPEG, phase retrieval, and HDR. For latent diffusion models on FFHQ, MGPS outperforms both RESAMPLE (Song et al., 2024) and PSLD (Rout et al., 2024) on 8 of 10 tasks (e.g., SR×4: 0.11 vs. 0.20/0.22; box inpainting: 0.16 vs. 0.22/0.26). On the ECG diagnosis task, MGPS with 50 steps achieves balanced accuracy of 0.94 (AF) and 0.92 (LBBB), beating DPS (0.79/0.84), PGDM (0.88/0.87), DDNM (0.86/0.83), DIFFPIR (0.77/0.80), and REDDIFF (0.88/0.86), while approaching ground-truth accuracy (0.94/0.98).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) for the forward/backward diffusion process and on the DPS guidance approximation (Chung et al., 2023) for the intractable likelihood term. Its core conceptual departure is the midpoint decomposition: rather than requiring the guidance score at the current diffusion step k+1 (as DPS does), it splits each backward transition into a denoising phase to an intermediate step ℓ_k and a re-noising phase back to k, introducing a tunable trade-off between guidance accuracy (improved at smaller ℓ_k) and prior-transition fidelity (improved at larger ℓ_k). A Gaussian variational approximation is then fitted at the midpoint via a KL upper bound, yielding a principled surrogate posterior that subsumes DPS as the special case ℓ_k = k.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The midpoint decomposition provides a clean, theoretically grounded knob (the sequence ℓ_k) that practitioners can tune to balance two competing approximation errors, and the Gaussian toy example confirms the optimal midpoint is near η=0.5 rather than the DPS-like η=1. The method's consistent gains across linear and nonlinear tasks, pixel-space and latent diffusion models, and its extension to ECG reconstruction demonstrate broad applicability. Open questions remain: the choice of ℓ_k is still heuristic (adaptive sequences help but lack theory), the vector-Jacobian product cost persists, and the warm-start strategy introduces instabilities at small k that are not yet resolved.
