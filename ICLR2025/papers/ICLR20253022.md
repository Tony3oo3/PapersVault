## Problem

Current LLM rating systems (e.g., per-task Elo/Glicko fits) suffer from three critical limitations: they cannot model or quantify judge biases (length, position, readability, etc.) that distort model comparisons, they are sample-inefficient and require large expensive preference datasets from scratch for each new task or model, and their shift-invariance makes ratings on different tasks incomparable, preventing meaningful cross-task leaderboards.

## Input / Output

POLYRATING consumes a single unified preference dataset of games (query, two model responses, judge, binary/draw judgment) spanning multiple tasks simultaneously, augmented with continuous features extracted per response (log-length, Flesch readability, formality/sentiment classifier scores, repetitiveness) and optional existing benchmark scores or LLM-judge preferences. It outputs a single base rating per model plus task-specific modifier coefficients (β), shared bias coefficients (α) with their average rating-point influence, and 95% pivotal confidence intervals computed via bootstrapping. Unlike per-task fits, all parameters are estimated jointly in one convex optimization, so the base rating is anchored across tasks and modifiers are directly interpretable as rating-point gains/losses relative to that anchor.

## Method

POLYRATING models each model's rating in a given game as a linear function: R^m(g) = R_base^m + Σ_j α_j f_j(g, i) + Σ_j β_j^m f'_j(g, i), where shared coefficients α capture model-independent judge biases (e.g., length, position) and model-specific coefficients β capture task modifiers. It optimizes this via MAP estimation—maximizing the Bradley-Terry logistic likelihood plus Gaussian priors (mean 0, task-specific standard deviations) on all α and β parameters—which simultaneously regularizes against overfitting, breaks the shift-invariance of pure MLE (enabling cross-task comparison), and allows existing benchmark or LLM-judge data to be injected as additional 'tasks' to improve sample efficiency. The objective is proven convex and twice differentiable, optimized with Newton's method for model-specific parameters and L-BFGS for shared parameters, and shown to converge to the same optimal ratings as the univariate MLE in the infinite-data limit. A rescaling hyperparameter W is introduced to align the compressed win-rate distributions of easy benchmarks with the spread of human preference data before combining them.

## Results

POLYRATING quantifies judge biases on the Chatbot Arena and Wildbench datasets: length bias inflates model ratings by 41 points for human judges and 48 points for LLM-based judges, while position bias adds ~38 points for LLM-based judges. In terms of sample efficiency, POLYRATING reduces the cost of human evaluation by up to 77% for new tasks (hard questions), 58% for Chinese questions, and 38% for code-based questions when collecting 10,000 annotations. Leveraging LLM-based evaluations or traditional benchmarks further reduces costs by 38% and 41% respectively, and evaluating new model versions requires 41% fewer samples than the univariate baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the standard univariate per-task MLE fitting of the Bradley-Terry model, which fits independent ratings for each task without shared parameters or priors. POLYRATING dominates this baseline on every axis the paper evaluates: (1) sample efficiency — at 10,000 annotations, POLYRATING requires 58%, 38%, and 77% fewer samples to reach the same logistic loss on Chinese, code-based, and hard questions respectively, and 41% fewer for new model versions; (2) bias quantification — the univariate system has no mechanism to capture or report judge biases, whereas POLYRATING estimates interpretable coefficients (e.g., length bias = +41 rating points for humans); (3) cross-task comparability — the univariate approach is shift-invariant, so fixing a reference model (Mixtral at 1114) produces arbitrary task-level offsets (e.g., 97/114 models gain Chinese rating in the univariate fit vs. only 67 in POLYRATING), rendering cross-task comparisons meaningless. POLYRATING does not lose on any reported metric; the univariate baseline is strictly inferior in every comparison the paper makes.

## Relation to Prior Work

POLYRATING extends the Bradley-Terry model and Elo-style exponential rating parametrization from univariate, per-task MLE fitting to a multivariate MAP framework that simultaneously fits all tasks with shared bias features and model-specific task modifiers. It departs from AlpacaEval's length-control approach by making bias coefficients directly interpretable in rating points and generalizing to multiple bias dimensions (position, formality, sentiment, repetitiveness, readability). The multivariate structure draws conceptual inspiration from multidimensional rating systems in recommendation systems, but adapts them to LLM evaluation by incorporating priors for sample efficiency and eliminating the shift-invariance that plagues standard Elo/Glicko systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rating system that quantifies judge biases in interpretable rating-point units (e.g., length bias ≈ 41 points, comparable to the gap between the 1st and 10th best models), enabling practitioners to understand and correct for systematic evaluation distortions. The 77% cost reduction for new tasks and 41% for new models makes human evaluation more accessible to resource-constrained teams. Key limitations remain: ratings are relative rather than absolute, feature selection still requires manual specification, and the framework assumes the linear model form is sufficient. Open questions include automatic discovery of relevant bias dimensions and whether the approach generalizes to non-pairwise evaluation paradigms.
