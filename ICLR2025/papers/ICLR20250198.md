## Problem

Existing multimodal contrastive learning methods (e.g., CLIP) align unimodal representations across modalities, which under the multiview redundancy assumption can only capture shared/redundant information between modalities. They fundamentally fail to learn uniqueness (task-relevant information present in only one modality) and synergy (information requiring simultaneous access to all modalities), limiting their ability to generalize across diverse multimodal tasks where different interaction types dominate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250198/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CoMM accepts any number of modalities (2–3 in experiments) including raw images, text, audio, time series, tabular data, proprioception, and force-torque signals, each processed by a modality-specific encoder. During training, two stochastic multimodal augmentations are applied to the joint input, and n projection operators mask all but one modality, yielding n+2 network passes per sample. The output is a single fused multimodal embedding (via a [CLS] token from a 1-layer, 8-head transformer) that is transferred to downstream tasks through linear probing or full fine-tuning, producing class labels or regression values.

## Method

CoMM replaces cross-modal alignment with multimodal self-alignment: a shared transformer fusion block (with modality-specific latent converters) produces a single multimodal embedding Z from all modalities, and the contrastive objective maximizes mutual information between two augmented versions of Z (capturing R+S+ΣUi) and between single-modality projections Zi and the augmented multimodal views (capturing R+Ui). The theoretical analysis via Partial Information Decomposition proves that redundancy, uniqueness, and synergy terms naturally emerge from this formulation without requiring task-specific augmentations or conditional augmentation strategies. The key architectural difference from prior work is that fusion happens before contrastive alignment (multimodal features are aligned to each other) rather than after (unimodal features are aligned across modalities), and the attention-based fusion module is essential for capturing synergistic interactions that linear fusion cannot model.

## Results

On the MultiBench benchmark (Table 1), CoMM achieves an average classification accuracy of 65.22% under linear probing, outperforming FactorCL (58.7%), Cross+Self (57.07%), and Cross (54.52%). The largest gains are on MOSI (+16.3% over FactorCL), MUSTARD (+8.1%), and UR-FUNNY (+2.6%), while on the V&T end-effector regression task CoMM achieves 4.55×10⁻⁴ MSE, roughly 2.4× lower than the second-best method. In the fine-tuning setting, CoMM reaches 69.88% average accuracy, matching FactorCL-SUP (69.82%) overall. On MM-IMDb (Table 2), CoMM with a BLIP-2 backbone achieves 64.75% weighted-F1 and 58.44% macro-F1 in the self-supervised setting, surpassing CLIP (54.49/44.94), SLIP (56.54/47.35), and BLIP-2 (57.4/49.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the self-supervised linear-probing setting, CoMM decisively outperforms FactorCL (the closest competitor) on three of four classification tasks: MOSI (67.5% vs 51.2%, +16.3 pp), MUSTARD (63.9% vs 55.8%, +8.1 pp), and UR-FUNNY (63.1% vs 60.5%, +2.6 pp), while FactorCL narrowly leads on MIMIC (67.3% vs 66.4%). CoMM also substantially beats the cross-modal baselines Cross/CLIP and Cross+Self on all classification tasks (e.g., MOSI: 67.5% vs 47.8% and 49.0%) and dominates the V&T regression task (4.55 vs 10.82 and 7.56 MSE×10⁻⁴). In the fine-tuning setting, CoMM matches FactorCL-SUP on average (69.88% vs 69.82%) but FactorCL-SUP retains a large lead on MIMIC (76.8% vs 68.18%), while CoMM wins on MOSI (74.98% vs 69.1%), UR-FUNNY (65.96% vs 63.5%), and MUSTARD (70.42% vs 69.9%). On MM-IMDb, CoMM with a BLIP-2 backbone surpasses all self-supervised baselines by 5.8–10 pp in macro-F1 (58.44% vs 49.9% for BLIP-2, 47.35% for SLIP, 44.94% for CLIP) and, when fine-tuned, outperforms fully supervised methods MFAS (55.6% macro-F1) and LLaVA-NeXT (56.51%). The controlled Trifeature experiments further confirm that CoMM is the only method capturing all three PID terms (redundancy, uniqueness, synergy), whereas Cross captures only redundancy, Cross+Self captures uniqueness but not synergy, and FactorCL also fails on synergy.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoMM extends multiview contrastive learning (CMC, Tian et al. 2020) from unimodal to multimodal settings, but departs fundamentally from the dominant cross-modal contrastive paradigm (CLIP, SLIP) that aligns separate unimodal representations. Instead of imposing cross-modal constraints, CoMM first fuses modalities via a transformer-based attention module and then applies contrastive objectives on the resulting multimodal embeddings. The theoretical grounding draws on Partial Information Decomposition (Williams & Beer 2010; Bertschinger et al. 2014) to formalize what information terms (redundancy, uniqueness, synergy) different contrastive strategies capture, positioning CoMM as a response to the limitations identified in FactorCL (Liang et al. 2023b), which modeled uniqueness and redundancy but relied on unrealistic conditional augmentation assumptions and ignored synergy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that cross-modal contrastive methods are provably limited to redundancy (Lemma 1) and empirically confirming this with chance-level performance on uniqueness and synergy tasks, the paper reframes a core assumption in multimodal representation learning. The practical impact is significant: CoMM achieves SOTA on seven tasks across healthcare, robotics, and affective computing with a simple, versatile architecture that scales to any number of modalities. Key open questions remain: PID theory is only well-defined for n=2 modalities (the n>2 case is empirically validated but theoretically uncharacterized), disentangling the contributions of R, U, and S in the learned representation space is unresolved, and the assumption of label-preserving multimodal augmentations, while empirically supported, lacks a general constructive guarantee.
