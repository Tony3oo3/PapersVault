## Problem

Despite the proliferation of DPO variants (xPOs) claiming improvements over the original DPO, there is no comprehensive study decomposing these methods into their constituent components or identifying which elements genuinely contribute to downstream performance. Fair comparisons are scarce due to inconsistent base models, datasets, and evaluation setups, making it impossible to determine whether proposed modifications are truly beneficial or merely redundant. The paper asks two questions: what components in xPOs actually improve over DPO, and can the effective ones be combined into a single unified objective?

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts from UltraFeedback, generates 5 candidate responses per prompt from Llama3-8B-Instruct, and ranks them using ArmoRM to form (prompt, winning response, losing response) preference pairs. A reward model is used to compute percentile-based scores for the modified rejection sampling (RS+) step, and the SFT model serves as the reference policy. The output is a fine-tuned Llama3-8B-Instruct model (8B parameters) evaluated on AlpacaEval2's 805 instructions using both GPT-4-turbo and Llama3-70B as judges, reporting Length-Controlled Win Rate, raw Win Rate, and average generation length.

## Method

RAINBOWPO decomposes 10+ DPO variants into seven mathematically orthogonal components (length normalization, link function, margin/home advantage, reference policy, contextual scaling, rejection sampling, SFT loss) and empirically validates each under a unified Llama3-8B-Instruct setup. Three components prove effective: length normalization (dividing log-likelihoods by sequence length to counter verbosity), a novel mixing reference policy π_{α,γ} that exponentially blends the SFT policy with a 'good policy' π_γ (generalizing both DPO at α=1 and SimPO at α=0), and contextual scaling φ(x) from MallowsPO that weights each preference pair by its predictive entropy. The final RAINBOWPO objective combines these three elements with the standard logistic link function, trained via supervised learning on the preference dataset. A greedy hyperparameter search strategy is used to avoid combinatorial explosion, tuning β, α, γ, and the scaling factor sequentially as components are added.

## Results

On AlpacaEval2 (GPT4-turbo judge) with Llama3-8B-Instruct as the base model, RainbowPO achieves 51.66% Length-Controlled Win Rate (LC WR) after 3 training epochs, improving over the base model's 41.88% and surpassing SimPO (48.40%) and DPO (43.65%). Under a single training epoch, RainbowPO already reaches 48.08% LC WR, matching SimPO (47.96%) while producing shorter outputs (1,683 vs. 1,730 average tokens). The method also reduces average generation length from 2,169 (base) to 1,878 tokens, confirming that length normalization effectively mitigates verbosity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RainbowPO outperforms all six baselines on LC WR in both the 1-epoch and 3-epoch settings. Against the strongest baseline SimPO, RainbowPO gains +3.26 LC WR points at 3 epochs (51.66% vs. 48.40%) and +0.12 at 1 epoch (48.08% vs. 47.96%), while also achieving a lower average output length. Against DPO, the improvement is +8.01 LC WR points at 3 epochs (51.66% vs. 43.65%) and +10.13 at 1 epoch (48.08% vs. 37.95%). The remaining baselines trail substantially: IPO (34.80%), KTO (35.61%), CPO (31.89%), and ORPO (22.91%) at 1 epoch. Notably, RainbowPO also benefits from additional training epochs (46.36% → 50.88% → 51.66% at 1/2/3 epochs), whereas SimPO shows diminishing returns beyond 1 epoch, making RainbowPO more data-efficient when the preference dataset is limited or expensive to collect.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) and its extensive family of variants (SimPO, ORPO, IPO, CPO, MallowsPO, RSO, etc.), drawing a conceptual parallel to RAINBOW (Hessel et al., 2018) in RL, which combined multiple orthogonal improvements to DQN. Rather than proposing a single new loss, it systematically decomposes 10+ existing DPO variants into seven mathematically orthogonal components and identifies which are empirically effective. The key conceptual departure is reinterpreting SimPO's margin term not as a 'home advantage' but as an implicit reference policy, motivating a novel exponential mixing policy that interpolates between the SFT reference and the margin-based policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework gives practitioners a principled recipe for combining DPO improvements rather than relying on ad-hoc comparisons across incompatible experimental setups, and the 22.92%→51.66% LC WR gain on Llama3-8B-Instruct demonstrates substantial practical value. However, validation is limited to a single model size and the AlpacaEval2 benchmark, and the paper acknowledges that mathematically orthogonal components are not empirically independent (e.g., RSO helps DPO but degrades LN-DPO), leaving open questions about which combinations generalize. Future work on other model families, online/iterative settings, and broader evaluation benchmarks would strengthen the conclusions.
