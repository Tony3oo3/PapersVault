## Problem

Existing generative models—diffusion, flow matching, discrete diffusion—are all Markov processes, yet they are developed in isolation with no unified framework connecting them. The design space of Markov processes is far broader than what has been explored: jump processes on continuous spaces, state-dependent diffusion coefficients, and principled combinations of different Markov model classes remain unexplored. There is also no rigorous, general recipe for constructing multimodal generative models across heterogeneous state spaces.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sample from a simple prior (e.g., Gaussian) and iteratively simulates a Markov process to produce a data sample. For image generation, outputs are 32×32 RGB images on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet32. For protein generation, the state space is the product R^d × SO(3)^d × {1,…,20}^d, jointly producing 3D backbone coordinates, rotation frames, and amino acid sequences. The neural network parameterizes the generator components (velocity field, diffusion coefficient, jump measure) and can operate on arbitrary state spaces including discrete, continuous, and manifold-valued data.

## Method

Generator Matching (GM) parameterizes a Markov process via its generator—the linear operator describing infinitesimal distributional change—and trains it by minimizing a Bregman-divergence loss between a known conditional generator (solving the KFE for a single data point's probability path) and the learned marginal generator. A universal characterization (Theorem 1) shows that on R^d, any Markov generator decomposes into flow, diffusion, and jump components, exhaustively defining the design space. Training uses the Conditional Generator Matching (CGM) loss, which is tractable because the conditional generator is known in closed form for common probability paths (geometric average, mixture). The framework enables Markov superpositions (convex combinations of different generator classes, e.g., flow + jump) and rigorous multimodal construction by combining component-wise generators on product spaces, with the loss simply summing across modalities.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the Markov superposition of a flow and jump model (Jump+Flow MS) achieves FID 2.49 with Euler sampling and 2.36 with mixed sampling (2nd-order ODE for flow, Euler for jump), outperforming the flow-only baseline (2.94 Euler, 2.48 2nd-order) and all diffusion baselines (DDPM 3.17, VP-SDE 3.01). On ImageNet32, Jump+Flow MS (mixed) reaches FID 3.33, beating the flow-only 2nd-order model (3.59) and all diffusion baselines. For protein generation, adding SO(3) jumps to MultiFlow improves multimodal diversity from 0.38 to 0.48 and unimodal diversity from 0.52 to 0.63, achieving state-of-the-art diversity while maintaining competitive novelty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Jump+Flow Markov superposition outperforms all diffusion baselines (DDPM, VP-SDE) by a wide margin on both CIFAR-10 and ImageNet32, though it trails the heavily optimized EDM (1.98 FID on CIFAR-10). Against the flow-only baseline, the superposition improves FID from 2.94 to 2.49 (Euler) and from 2.48 to 2.36 (mixed sampling), demonstrating that the jump component adds complementary generative capacity. The jump model alone (FID 4.23 CIFAR-10, 7.66 ImageNet) underperforms the flow model, confirming that jumps are most effective as a complement rather than a standalone generator. For protein generation, the SO(3) jump augmentation of MultiFlow improves multimodal diversity from 0.38 to 0.48 and unimodal diversity from 0.52 to 0.63, surpassing all baselines including RFdiffusion (0.4/0.37), FrameFlow (0.39/0.39), FoldFlow (0.24/0.32), Protpardelle (0.12/0.41), and ProteinGenerator (0.19/0.35) on diversity while matching or exceeding their novelty scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

Generator Matching unifies denoising diffusion models, flow matching, stochastic interpolants, and discrete diffusion models under a single Markov-process framework by learning the infinitesimal generator of a probability path via a conditional training objective (CGM loss). It builds directly on the flow matching and score-based SDE formulations, recovering them as special cases of the Bregman-divergence CGM loss, and subsumes stochastic interpolants as a Markov superposition with a divergence-free Langevin component. The key departure is Theorem 1, which exhaustively characterizes all Markov generators on R^d (flow + diffusion + jump) and on finite discrete spaces, identifying jump processes on Euclidean space as a previously unexplored model class, and introducing Markov superpositions as a principled way to combine heterogeneous Markov process families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a rigorous, modality-agnostic design space for generative modeling that goes beyond the diffusion/flow dichotomy, enabling new model classes (state-dependent diffusion coefficients, jump processes on R^d) and principled multimodal construction without ad-hoc coupling. Empirically, the results are promising but preliminary: jump models alone trail flow baselines by a wide margin (FID 4.23 vs 2.94 on CIFAR-10), and the Markov superposition improvement (2.36 vs 2.48) is modest and relies on Euler sampling without classifier-free guidance. Open questions include developing higher-order samplers for jump processes, extending guidance to jump models, exploring the full Bregman-divergence loss landscape, and scaling the framework to larger datasets and more complex multimodal settings.
