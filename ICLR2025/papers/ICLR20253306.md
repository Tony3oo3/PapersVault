## Problem

Conformal prediction provides distribution-free coverage guarantees but has been largely limited to one-dimensional outputs due to its reliance on scalar nonconformity scores. For multi-dimensional outputs, existing methods either restrict prediction regions to boxes or ellipsoids (which inflate for multimodal or asymmetric conditional densities) or construct regions as unions of many small balls (PCP, ST-DQR), yielding irregular, disconnected boundaries that hinder interpretability.

## Input / Output

The method takes a p-dimensional feature vector x and a miscoverage level α (e.g., 0.1 for 90% coverage), along with a dataset of (x, y) pairs where y ∈ R^q. It produces a connected, smooth prediction region in R^q with guaranteed marginal coverage of at least 1−α. The region is the image of a single ball in q-dimensional latent space under the learned CNF bijection, so its shape adapts to the conditional density. A ResCONTRA variant additionally accepts a user-chosen point predictor f̂(x) and applies the conformal procedure to residuals y − f̂(x).

## Method

CONTRA trains a conditional normalizing flow (RealNVP with 6–10 coupling layers) on a training split to learn a differentiable bijection t_θ mapping a standard q-dimensional Gaussian latent space to the output space. For calibration, each calibration point (x_i, y_i) is mapped to its latent representation ẑ_i = t_θ⁻¹(y_i, x_i), and the conformal radius r_{1−α} is set as the ⌈(1−α)(n₂+1)⌉-th smallest Euclidean norm among these latent points. The prediction region for a new x_{n+1} is simply t_θ({z : ‖z‖ ≤ r_{1−α}}, x_{n+1}), a bijection of a single ball that is guaranteed connected and closed (Proposition 1) with marginal coverage ≥ 1−α (Proposition 2). ResCONTRA extends this to arbitrary point predictors by first fitting f̂ on D₁, then training a simpler NF on residuals from D₂ and calibrating on D₃, yielding the region f̂(x_{n+1}) + t*(Ê*, x_{n+1}).

## Results

Across six real datasets (Bio, NYC Taxi, Energy, 2D River Flow, SCM20D, 4D River Flow), CONTRA achieves the smallest prediction region volume in 4 out of 6 cases, with coverage consistently at or near the nominal 90% level. On the challenging 4D River Flow task, CONTRA's volume is 0.59×10², compared to 26.10×10² for NLE and 52.93×10² for RCP — a 44–90× reduction. On NYC Taxi (2D output), CONTRA achieves volume 8.71×10⁻³ versus 10.77×10⁻³ (NLE) and 12.21×10⁻³ (RCP). PCP is the closest competitor, matching or slightly beating CONTRA on Bio (12.54 vs 13.23) and Energy (16.40 vs 18.24), but its regions are unions of K balls with irregular, disconnected boundaries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CONTRA outperforms all shape-restricted baselines (NLE, RCP, MCQR, Dist-split$_{\text{bon}}$, CQR$_{\text{bon}}$) by a wide margin on volume: on 4D River Flow, CONTRA's volume (0.59×10²) is 44× smaller than NLE (26.10×10²) and 90× smaller than RCP (52.93×10²), while on 2D River Flow it is 3× smaller than NLE and 4.5× smaller than RCP. The closest competitor is PCP (Wang et al., 2022), which matches or slightly beats CONTRA on volume for Bio (12.54 vs 13.23) and Energy (16.40 vs 18.24), but PCP regions are unions of K balls producing highly irregular, disconnected boundaries that hinder interpretability. NLE (Messoudi et al., 2022) and RCP (Johnstone & Cox, 2021) are consistently the weakest, producing inflated elliptical regions that encompass large low-density areas. Dist-split$_{\text{bon}}$ (Izbicki et al., 2020) and CQR$_{\text{bon}}$ (Romano et al., 2019), applied via Bonferroni correction, produce rectangular regions that are 1.5–2× larger than CONTRA on most datasets and show degraded coverage when input dimensionality is high (e.g., Bio with 9 inputs). Overall, CONTRA wins on volume in 4 of 6 real datasets and ties or narrowly loses to PCP in the remaining 2, while offering smoother, connected, and more interpretable prediction regions across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

CONTRA sits at the intersection of conditional normalizing flows (Winkler et al., 2019) and split conformal prediction (Papadopoulos et al., 2002), using the NF's latent-space bijection to define a natural non-conformity score (distance from the Gaussian center) and map a single conformal ball back to the output space. The closest prior methods—PCP (Wang et al., 2022) and ST-DQR (Feldman et al., 2023)—also leverage generative models for multi-dimensional conformal regions but construct them as unions of many balls, yielding disconnected and irregular boundaries. ResCONTRA extends Colombo's (2024) one-dimensional residual-based conformal regression to multi-dimensional outputs, while MCQR generalizes CQR (Romano et al., 2019) to produce optimally-sized rectangular regions for multi-target settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-dimensional conformal prediction remains under-explored, and CONTRA provides the first method that simultaneously guarantees coverage, produces a single connected region with smooth boundaries, and adapts its shape to the true conditional density—addressing a practical interpretability gap that shape-restricted (ellipse/rectangle) and union-of-balls (PCP/ST-DQR) methods leave open. Key limitations include the requirement of a well-trained bijection (challenging for high-dimensional outputs or small datasets), restriction to continuous outcomes via the Gaussian base, and only marginal (not fully conditional) coverage guarantees. Open questions include extending the framework to discrete or mixed-type outcomes, deriving practical conditional coverage bounds, and developing adaptive strategies when the latent distribution deviates from Gaussian due to underfitting.
