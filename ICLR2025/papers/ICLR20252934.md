## Problem

Existing time series anomaly detection methods require training a dedicated model per dataset, limiting generalization when data or resources are scarce. Two fundamental challenges prevent a single general model: (1) multi-domain data exhibit vastly different information densities, demanding different internal bottleneck sizes for faithful reconstruction, and (2) diverse anomaly manifestations across domains make it hard to learn a robust decision boundary between normal and abnormal patterns without overfitting to domain-specific anomalies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252934/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a multivariate time series window (size 100) which is decomposed via channel independence into univariate series, then split into patches of size 5 and randomly masked. At inference, the anomaly decoder is discarded and 5 pairs of complementary masks are applied; the per-timestep anomaly score is the variance of reconstructed values across these pairs, thresholded via the SPOT algorithm to yield a binary anomaly label per time point. The model operates zero-shot: after multi-domain pre-training (~400M time points), it is applied directly to unseen downstream datasets without any fine-tuning.

## Method

DADA combines three key mechanisms within a mask-based reconstruction architecture. First, complementary mask modeling generates two masked series with mutually complementary mask positions that reconstruct each other, capturing bidirectional temporal dependencies. Second, Adaptive Bottlenecks (AdaBN) maintains a pool of six bottlenecks with latent dimensions [16, 32, 64, 128, 192, 256]; a learned router with stochastic noise selects the top-3 bottlenecks per input and fuses their outputs via softmax-weighted averaging, dynamically matching information capacity to each data sample's reconstruction needs. Third, Dual Adversarial Decoders employ a normal decoder that minimizes reconstruction error on clean series and an anomaly decoder that reconstructs noise-injected abnormal series; a Gradient Reversal Layer between the shared encoder and the anomaly decoder forces the encoder to maximize abnormal reconstruction error while the anomaly decoder minimizes it, explicitly widening the decision boundary between normal and common anomaly patterns. The model is pre-trained on multi-domain data (Anomaly Detection Data + Monash+) using anomaly injection to generate diverse abnormal samples, then deployed zero-shot on downstream tasks.

## Results

As a zero-shot detector (no training on target data), DADA achieves state-of-the-art Affiliated F1 scores on all five main benchmarks: 84.57% on SMD, 78.48% on MSL, 75.42% on SMAP, 74.60% on SWaT, and 82.26% on PSM, outperforming the strongest trained baselines (GPT4TS and ModernTCN) by 0.7–1.4 F1 points. On the NeurIPS-TS benchmark, DADA achieves the best F1 on all four sub-datasets (CICIDS 73.49, Creditcard 75.12, GECCO 90.20, SWAN 71.93) and the best AUC on three of four. Notably, all competing baselines are trained on each target dataset's training split, whereas DADA requires no target-specific training at all.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DADA outperforms all 19 baselines on every one of the five main datasets in Affiliated F1, despite being evaluated zero-shot while all baselines are trained on the target dataset. The closest competitors are GPT4TS (NeurIPS 2023) and ModernTCN (ICLR 2024), both general time series models; DADA leads GPT4TS by 1.43 F1 on SMD, 1.25 on MSL, 0.75 on SMAP, 4.49 on SWaT, and 0.82 on PSM, and leads ModernTCN by 1.41, 1.31, 8.01, 3.47, and 2.67 respectively. On the NeurIPS-TS benchmark, DADA achieves the best F1 on all four sub-datasets (CICIDS, Creditcard, GECCO, SWAN), with the largest margin on CICIDS (+9.5 F1 over D3R) and SWAN (+25.5 F1 over ModernTCN). Traditional methods (OCSVM, PCA, IForest, LOF) and reconstruction-based deep models (AE, DAGMM, LSTM, BeatGAN) trail DADA by 5–30 F1 points, confirming that the gap is not merely a matter of model capacity but of generalization strategy. The ablation further shows that removing the adversarial mechanism drops average F1 by 16.26 points and removing adaptive bottlenecks drops it by 5.04 points, validating both core design choices.

## Relation to Prior Work

DADA synthesizes ideas from three distinct research threads: mask-based time series reconstruction (PatchTST, SimMTM), mixture-of-experts routing (Shazeer et al., Jacobs et al.), and gradient reversal for adversarial domain adaptation (Ganin & Lempitsky). It departs from prior time series anomaly detection methods (AE, OmniAnomaly, Anomaly Transformer, D3R) that train a dedicated model per dataset, and from time series pre-training models (GPT4TS, Timer, MOMENT) that target forecasting rather than anomaly detection. The adaptive bottleneck module repurposes MoE-style routing to select among differently-sized latent spaces rather than identically-structured experts, while the dual adversarial decoder applies GRL to explicitly push the encoder away from encoding anomaly patterns. The paper carves new ground by being the first to pre-train a model specifically for zero-shot time series anomaly detection across domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: in scenarios with scarce labeled data, privacy constraints, or rapid deployment needs, a single pre-trained model that performs competitively without domain-specific training removes a major bottleneck in deploying anomaly detection. The finding that multi-domain pre-training alone (without DADA's specific modules) fails to yield generalization (Figure 5 right) underscores that task-specific architectural choices—adaptive information compression and explicit adversarial boundary learning—are necessary, not just data scale. Open questions include how well the model handles truly novel anomaly types absent from the injected patterns, whether channel independence (treating multivariate series as independent univariate streams) limits performance on strongly correlated multivariate data, and whether the approach extends to streaming or online anomaly detection settings.
