## Problem

Topological masking modulates transformer attention by a function of the underlying graph structure, but this is straightforward only for full-rank (O(N²)) attention where the N×N matrix is materialised. For linear/low-rank attention, which avoids instantiating the attention matrix to achieve O(N) complexity, no general algorithm existed for flexible graph-based masking: prior methods were restricted to specific topologies (grids, trees) and achieved at best O(N log N) time. The paper asks how to implement efficient, flexible topological masking of linear attention on general graphs.

## Input / Output

The method takes token embeddings (from image patches, video frames, or 3D point cloud particles) together with a weighted adjacency matrix W defining the graph topology. For images the graph is a fixed 2D grid; for point clouds (32,768 particles) the graph is constructed on-the-fly via approximate k=3 nearest neighbours that change each timestep. Outputs are class labels for image/video classification, or predicted point cloud displacements (Δx, Δf) rendered to video frames via a conditional NeRF. The graph structure itself acts as a learned inductive bias rather than a fixed architectural constraint.

## Method

The paper parameterises topological masks as learnable power series M_α(G) = Σ α_k W^k (graph node kernels) of the weighted adjacency matrix, then shows via a deconvolution identity that this equals Φ_G Φ_G^T where Φ_G = Σ f_k W^k. Masked linear attention is then computed implicitly as vec(φ(q_i) ⊗ φ_G(v_i))^T vec(φ(k_j) ⊗ φ_G(v_j)), avoiding materialisation of the N×N attention or mask matrices. To achieve O(N) complexity, dense graph features are replaced by sparse Graph Random Features (GRFs): Monte Carlo estimates obtained by importance-sampling terminating random walks from each node, where each walk deposits weighted contributions at visited coordinates. The paper proves the first known exponential concentration bounds (via McDiarmid's inequality) showing GRFs have O(1) nonzero entries per node with high probability independent of graph size, guaranteeing O(N) time and space. Intuitively, two nodes attend to each other only if their respective ensembles of random walks 'hit' common nodes, upweighting local interactions while sampling longer-range attention with lower probability.

## Results

On ImageNet, GRF-masked linear attention achieves 73.0% top-1 accuracy, a +3.7% absolute improvement over unmasked linear attention (69.3%) while maintaining O(N) time complexity. On iNaturalist2021 and Places365, the gains are +2.2% (68.9% vs 66.7%) and +0.5% (54.8% vs 54.3%) respectively. For the HD-VPD robotics task with 32,768 particles, the GRF Interlacer outperforms both the message-passing Interlacer and the vanilla transformer on SSIM across rollout timesteps, while training 6% faster than the MP baseline (0.982 vs 0.925 steps/sec). On Kinetics-400 video classification, GRF-masked softmax attention improves accuracy by +0.4% over unmasked softmax (75.8% vs 75.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against unmasked linear attention (Katharopoulos et al., 2020), GRF-masked linear wins on all three image benchmarks by +3.7%, +2.2%, and +0.5% respectively, confirming that the topological inductive bias from graph random features provides substantial gains over no masking at the same O(N) complexity. Against Toeplitz-masked linear (Choromanski et al., 2022), GRFs match or beat the baseline on ImageNet (73.0% vs 73.3%, within 0.3%) and iNaturalist (68.9% vs 67.4%, +1.5% win), while being strictly faster at O(N) vs O(N log N); on Places365 the gap is negligible (54.8% vs 55.0%). Against unmasked softmax (Vaswani et al., 2017), GRFs substantially narrow the accuracy gap on ImageNet (73.0% vs 74.1%, only 1.1% behind) and iNaturalist (68.9% vs 69.9%, 1.0% behind) while being orders of magnitude faster. On HD-VPD, the GRF Interlacer outperforms the message-passing Interlacer (Whitney et al., 2024) on SSIM across all rollout timesteps and trains 6% faster, demonstrating that GRF-masked attention can replace expensive GNN-style message passing while improving prediction quality on 32k-particle point clouds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three threads: linear (low-rank) attention (Katharopoulos et al. 2020; Choromanski et al. 2020), topological masking of transformers with graph structure (Choromanski et al. 2022), and graph random features for approximating graph node kernels (Choromanski 2023; Reid et al. 2024b). Prior work on efficient topological masking for linear attention was restricted to O(N log N) algorithms for highly structured graphs (grids, trees) or O(N²) for general graphs. The key departure here is parameterising the mask as a learnable power series in the weighted adjacency matrix and approximating it with importance-sampled random walks (GRFs), yielding the first O(N) algorithm for general graphs, backed by novel exponential concentration bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(N) guarantee for general graphs removes the last scalability barrier for incorporating topological inductive bias into linear-attention transformers, enabling applications like 30k+ particle point cloud dynamics modelling in robotics where O(N²) or O(N log N) is infeasible. The concentration bounds (Thm. 3.2) and sparsity guarantees (Lemma 3.3) are the first of their kind for GRFs and may benefit other scalable graph-kernel applications (e.g., geometric Gaussian processes). Open questions include whether hardware-level optimisation of sparse linear algebra can close the constant-factor gap with unmasked linear attention, whether dimensionality reduction (e.g., Johnson-Lindenstrauss) can further compress GRFs, and whether the assumption that the constant c remains fixed as N grows holds for realistic growing graphs.
