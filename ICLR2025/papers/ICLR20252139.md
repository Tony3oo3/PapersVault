## Problem

Prior theoretical work on continual linear classification (Evron et al., 2023) analyzed a projection-based Sequential Max-Margin (SMM) scheme derived from weakly regularized training to convergence, which does not match the practical setting of running a fixed number of gradient descent steps per task. It remained unclear whether standard unregularized gradient descent with a fixed iteration budget per task would converge to the joint max-margin solution, how catastrophic forgetting evolves over repeated cycles, and what happens when tasks are jointly non-separable.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives M binary classification tasks, each consisting of a fixed set of labeled d-dimensional points {(x_i, y_i)} with y_i ∈ {-1,+1}, presented sequentially in either a cyclic order (m_t = t mod M) or a uniformly random order. At each stage t, only the current task's data is available, and the algorithm runs exactly K steps of gradient descent on the unregularized logistic loss before switching to the next task. The output is a linear weight vector w ∈ R^d whose direction converges to the joint ℓ₂ max-margin solution (in the separable case) or whose value converges to the unique global minimizer of the joint loss (in the non-separable case).

## Method

The paper analyzes sequential GD—running K steps of gradient descent on the unregularized logistic loss of each task before moving to the next—and proves that, under joint separability, the iterates satisfy w_k^(t) = ln(t)·ŵ + ρ_k^(t) with bounded residual ρ, meaning the direction converges to the joint max-margin solution ŵ even though each individual task's GD is biased toward its own max-margin direction. The key technical mechanism is a descent-lemma argument over full cycles that yields a non-asymptotic loss bound of O(ln²J/J), from which cycle-averaged forgetting is bounded at O(ln⁴J/J²) with constants governed by positive/negative cross-task data alignment (A_{p,q}^+ and A_{p,q}^-). For the jointly non-separable case, the paper leverages local strong convexity of the joint logistic loss on a compact set (via the Freund et al. condition) to obtain a faster O(ln²J/J²) convergence rate to the unique global minimum. The results extend to random task ordering (almost sure convergence) and are validated empirically on synthetic 2D/3D data, wide two-layer ReLU networks, and CIFAR-10.

## Results

The paper establishes that sequential GD on jointly separable cyclic tasks converges in direction to the joint (offline) ℓ₂ max-margin solution at rate O(ln t) in magnitude (Theorem 3.2), with the joint training loss decaying at a non-asymptotic rate of O(ln²J/J) (Theorem 3.3). Cycle-averaged forgetting vanishes at the faster rate O(ln⁴J/J²) (Theorem 3.4), and the non-separable case achieves O(ln²J/J²) convergence to the unique global minimum (Theorem 5.2). On CIFAR-10 (2-class, 3-task linear setup, K=50, J=1350 cycles), the distance between sequential GD iterates and the jointly trained solution converges close to zero, confirming the theoretical predictions in a real-data regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with the Sequential Max-Margin (SMM) projection algorithm of Evron et al. (2023). On a 3D two-task toy example, SMM converges to (12/11, 1/11, 1/11), which is NOT the joint max-margin direction (1, 0, 0), whereas sequential GD aligns with the joint max-margin direction as predicted by Theorem 3.2. This reveals a fundamental algorithmic bias difference: SMM's projection-based updates do not guarantee convergence to the offline max-margin solution, while sequential GD's implicit bias does. Additionally, SMM requires training each task to convergence with a vanishing regularization coefficient (λ→0), making the equivalence between the regularized training and the projection scheme questionable in magnitude, whereas sequential GD operates with a fixed finite budget K per task and no regularization. The paper also contrasts with the single-task implicit bias result of Soudry et al. (2018), showing that the joint max-margin direction can lie outside the subspace spanned by individual task max-margin directions, making the continual learning result non-trivial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continual linear classification framework of Evron et al. (2023), who analyzed a projection-based Sequential Max-Margin (SMM) scheme derived from regularized exponential loss trained to convergence. The key departure is the use of unregularized logistic loss with a fixed iteration budget per task, which is far more realistic in practice. The directional convergence proof technique is adapted from Soudry et al. (2018) on single-task implicit bias, and the non-separable case leverages the local strong convexity framework of Freund et al. (2018). Critically, the paper reveals an algorithmic bias difference: SMM converges to some offline solution (not necessarily max-margin), while sequential GD provably converges to the joint max-margin solution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that sequential GD overcomes individual-task max-margin biases to converge to the joint max-margin direction provides a theoretical explanation for why repeated task presentation mitigates catastrophic forgetting without any explicit regularization or replay. The non-asymptotic forgetting bound explicitly quantifies how positive/negative task alignment drives forgetting vs. backward knowledge transfer, offering a concrete diagnostic for task design. Limitations include the restriction to linear models, small learning rates, and fixed datasets; extending to deep networks, larger learning rates (edge of stability), and streaming data remains open. The authors note that analogous behavior was observed empirically for wide ReLU networks, suggesting the theory may generalize, but a rigorous proof is lacking.
