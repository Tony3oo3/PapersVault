## Problem

Fine-tuning LLMs is resource-intensive because a large fraction of training data is redundant or uninformative, yet existing data selection methods either rely on prohibitively expensive gradient-based influence estimates or static embeddings that fail to track the model's evolving knowledge. No prior framework provides a unified, computationally efficient selection strategy that adapts across the three distinct fine-tuning stages—instruction tuning, task-specific adaptation, and continual fine-tuning—while maintaining strong theoretical grounding.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a training corpus of (instruction, input, output) triples along with a target LLM, and optionally a target benchmark dataset (for task-specific alignment) or a previously used dataset (for continual learning). It produces a pruned subset (default budget 30%) of the original corpus, selected via greedy submodular maximization. The pairwise utility matrix is computed once in O(n²) forward passes under teacher forcing and is amortized across all three fine-tuning stages, so the selection cost is incurred only once regardless of how many stages or models are subsequently fine-tuned.

## Method

DELIFT defines a pairwise utility metric UF_ij that quantifies how much sample j improves the model's prediction of sample i by measuring the reduction in distance between the ground-truth one-hot distribution and the model's predicted distribution, with and without j provided as an in-context example. Theoretically, under KL divergence this equals the conditional pointwise mutual information between y_i and (x_j, y_j) given x_i; in practice a length-normalized L2 norm is used for numerical stability. These utilities form a kernel matrix that feeds into three tailored submodular objectives—Facility Location (FL) for diverse instruction coverage, FLMI for alignment with a target benchmark, and FLCG for novelty relative to previously seen data—each solved via greedy maximization with a (1−1/e) approximation guarantee. The key design choice is that the same model-aware utility kernel underlies all three stages, making the framework a single-stop solution rather than a collection of stage-specific heuristics.

## Results

DELIFT prunes up to 70% of training data while maintaining or exceeding full-data performance across instruction tuning, task-specific fine-tuning, and continual fine-tuning. On Mix-Instruct with Qwen2-72B (QLoRA), DELIFT achieves ROUGE 52.79, BGE 88.04, and LAJ 3.37 using only 30% of data, versus Full Data's 65.51/92.24/3.51. On HotpotQA→MMLU (5-shot classification), DELIFT actually surpasses Full Data by 3.34% for Qwen2 (81.70 vs. 78.36) and 4.20% for Phi-3 (68.70 vs. 64.50), demonstrating that removing redundant samples can improve generalization. Across all settings, DELIFT outperforms the next-best baseline by up to 26% in LAJ score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DELIFT consistently outperforms both SelectIT (self-reflection-based quality filtering) and LESS (gradient-based influence estimation via LoRA) across all three fine-tuning stages and both model scales. On Mix-Instruct (Qwen2, QLoRA), DELIFT achieves LAJ 3.37 vs. SelectIT's 3.21 and LESS's 3.28, a gap of 0.16–0.16 points; on the more challenging P3 dataset, the gap widens to 0.68 over SelectIT and 0.04 over LESS in LAJ. In task-specific fine-tuning (HotpotQA→MMLU), DELIFT beats Full Data outright (81.70 vs. 78.36 for Qwen2), while SelectIT and LESS both fall below Full Data (79.13 and 80.35 respectively). The static-embedding ablation DELIFT(SE) trails the full DELIFT by 0.05–0.09 LAJ points, confirming that the ICL-based utility kernel provides a meaningful advantage over fixed sentence-embedding similarities. In continual fine-tuning (SQuAD→HotpotQA), DELIFT narrows the gap to Full Data to just 1.94% LAJ, whereas LESS and SelectIT show 3–4% gaps, highlighting DELIFT's superior handling of redundancy with previously seen data.

## Relation to Prior Work

DELIFT builds on the submodular optimization framework for subset selection (Fujishige, 2005; Iyer et al., 2021) and repurposes the in-context learning paradigm as a data-utility signal, connecting it formally to conditional pointwise mutual information. It departs from two dominant prior paradigms: static embedding-based selection (which ignores the model's evolving state) and gradient/influence-based methods like LESS (which incur prohibitive computational cost at scale). The key conceptual departure is unifying three distinct fine-tuning stages—instruction tuning, task-specific adaptation, and continual learning—under a single utility metric with tailored submodular objectives (FL, FLMI, FLCG), whereas prior methods typically target only one stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstrated 70% data reduction with minimal (and sometimes zero) performance loss is practically significant for resource-constrained fine-tuning, and the unified framework removes the need to choose different selection strategies per stage. However, the O(n²) pairwise utility computation remains a scalability bottleneck for very large corpora, and the method's sensitivity to initial data quality and potential bias amplification are acknowledged but not fully addressed. Open questions include whether adaptive, online selection during training (rather than one-shot pre-selection) could further improve results, and whether the approach extends naturally to multimodal or non-textual data.
