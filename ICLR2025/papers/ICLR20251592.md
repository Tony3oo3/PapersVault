## Problem

Pre-trained 3D geometric foundation models like DUSt3R degrade significantly under challenging conditions such as limited view overlap or low lighting, due to the inherent difficulty of 3D geometric inference and the scarcity of high-quality in-the-wild 3D training data. Existing specialization methods for 3D foundation models still depend on large amounts of labeled data, leaving a gap for label-free, scene-specific adaptation that can run efficiently on a single GPU.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251592/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes 10 uncalibrated sparse RGB images (no camera intrinsics, extrinsics, or depth required) from a target scene as input and produces a compact rank-16 LoRA adapter (~18 MB) that plugs into the frozen pre-trained DUSt3R backbone. Intermediate outputs include globally aligned 3D point maps, per-pixel calibrated confidence scores, and recovered camera parameters (focal lengths and relative poses). Downstream, the calibrated model yields improved pairwise/multi-view 3D reconstructions, multi-view camera pose estimates, and novel-view renders via InstantSplat, all evaluated on held-out test images from the same scene.

## Method

The core contribution is a self-calibration pipeline that leverages multi-view consistency to refine DUSt3R's own predictions without any ground truth. The key technical step is a robust global optimization that re-parameterizes the fixed prediction confidence as optimizable per-pixel weights, regularized toward the original confidence via a Geman-McClure-inspired term; a closed-form iterative re-weighted least-squares update (w = C / (1 + ‖e‖)²) down-weights overconfident outlier predictions while preserving accurate ones. The calibrated confidence then drives pseudo-labeling: high-confidence refined point maps are back-projected into local image-pair frames and thresholded (cutoff = 1.5) to generate training targets. Finally, rank-16 LoRA is applied to all attention layers of DUSt3R, reducing trainable parameters by over 99% while matching full fine-tuning performance, and the adapter is trained for 10 epochs in under 3.5 minutes on a single 3090 GPU.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Dataset, LoRA3D reduces absolute trajectory error (ATE) by up to 88% and average focal length error (AFE) by up to 79% compared to pre-trained DUSt3R, improving on 116 out of 150 test segments. On Replica, the method reduces pairwise point prediction error by up to 38% (e.g., 8.84 cm vs. 14.29 cm on office0) and improves multi-view reconstruction accuracy by up to 61% and completeness by up to 41%. For novel view rendering via InstantSplat, self-calibration yields gains of up to 0.97 dB PSNR, 0.09 SSIM, and 0.04 LPIPS over the pre-trained baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRA3D consistently outperforms the pre-trained DUSt3R baseline across all three datasets and all evaluated tasks, with the largest gains on Waymo (up to 88% ATE reduction) where the pre-trained model struggles with dynamic objects and large camera baselines. Against the ground-truth fine-tuned oracle (DUSt3R-GT-FT), the self-calibrated model closes most of the gap without any labeled data, confirming that calibrated-confidence pseudo-labels are a strong proxy for ground truth. COLMAP fails entirely on Waymo due to dynamic objects and large baselines, while MASt3R degrades significantly on Waymo (e.g., ATE of 2.85 m vs. 0.37 m for LoRA3R on seg-10084) but remains competitive on inward-facing Replica and TUM scenes. FlowMap, PoseDiffusion, RayDiffusion, and RelPose++ all fail to produce reasonable estimates on the out-of-distribution Waymo data, with ATE values often exceeding 10–65 m, whereas LoRA3D maintains sub-meter accuracy. On Replica multi-view reconstruction, MASt3R achieves comparable or slightly better accuracy than LoRA3D on some scenes (e.g., office1: 3.92 vs. 3.56 cm), but LoRA3D is more consistent across scenes and does not require explicit feature matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-supervised pseudo-labeling strategy from Yang et al. (2021) — which used robust optimization to generate geometric pseudo-labels for smaller-scale models — to the setting of large 3D geometric foundation models like DUSt3R. It combines this with LoRA (Hu et al., 2021), a parameter-efficient adaptation technique well-established in LLMs and vision transformers but previously unexplored for 3D geometric foundation models. The key conceptual departure from prior specialization work (MASt3R, Spanner3D, Real3D) is the elimination of all ground-truth labels and external priors: the model calibrates its own predictions via multi-view consistency and a Geman-McClure-inspired confidence re-weighting, then fine-tunes on the resulting pseudo-labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical efficiency (5 minutes on a single GPU, 18 MB per adapter) makes scene-specific 3D foundation model adaptation feasible in deployment settings where retraining is infeasible. The finding that DUSt3R's native confidence is systematically overconfident under limited view overlap — and that un-calibrated confidence actively harms pseudo-label quality — is a useful diagnostic for anyone building on these models. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond DUSt3R/MASt3R to other 3D foundation models, whether the 10-image saturation point holds for more complex scenes, and whether the remaining gap to ground-truth fine-tuning can be closed with better pseudo-label selection or more sophisticated confidence modeling.
