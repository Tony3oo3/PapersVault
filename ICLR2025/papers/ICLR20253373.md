## Problem

Bidirectional attention has been considered essential for high-quality text embeddings, and prior methods to adapt autoregressive LMs into embedders required either casting the architecture to bidirectional attention or large-scale synthetic data. This paper challenges that premise by asking whether causal attention alone can produce competitive embeddings without any architectural modification or additional training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253373/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text sentence or paragraph and produces a fixed-dimensional embedding vector via pooling (mean or last-token) of final-layer hidden states. The key unusual aspect is that the input is consumed twice: the prompt instructs the model to repeat/rewrite the input, and the input text is appended both as the instruction target and as the 'output' to be encoded, doubling the sequence length. Embeddings are extracted exclusively from the second occurrence of the input, not the first.

## Method

Echo embeddings prompt an autoregressive LM to act as an autoencoder (e.g., 'Rewrite the following paragraph: S. The rewritten paragraph: S') and pool contextualized representations from the second occurrence of S. Because causal attention allows the second occurrence to attend to all tokens in the first occurrence, the second-occurrence representations effectively encode bidirectional information without any architectural change. In the zero-shot setting, this yields ~5% improvement over classical embeddings and matches LLM2Vec-unsupervised without any fine-tuning. For supervised fine-tuning, the method uses SimCSE contrastive loss with in-batch and mined hard negatives, LoRA (r=16, α=16), and a batch size of 2048 via GradCache, matching or outperforming bidirectionally-cast models at identical compute budgets.

## Results

In the zero-shot setting on MTEB (56 datasets), echo embeddings with Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.1 achieve an average score of 48.64, outperforming classical embeddings (42.38) by 6.26 points and PromptEOL (43.69) by 4.95 points. Compute-matched echo embeddings (halving input length to equalize FLOPs) achieve 49.02, slightly exceeding full-compute echo. In the fine-tuning setting, echo embeddings reach 64.68 average MTEB, outperforming classical causal embeddings (63.98) and classical bidirectional embeddings (64.23), with compute-matched echo at 64.66.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Echo embeddings outperform PromptEOL (Jiang et al., 2023b) by ~5 points on average MTEB (48.64 vs. 43.69) and are substantially less sensitive to prompt wording. Against bidirectional encoder baselines, echo embeddings surpass BERT-large (48.64 vs. 37.87) and RoBERTa-large (48.64 vs. 37.86) by over 10 points in zero-shot. In the fine-tuning setting, compute-matched echo (64.66) approximately matches LLM2Vec-supervised (64.80) and GritLM-7B trained on public data (64.70), despite not requiring bidirectional attention or multi-stage training. Echo also outperforms UAE-Large-V1 (64.68 vs. 64.64), multilingual-e5-large (64.68 vs. 64.41), bge-large-en-v1.5 (64.68 vs. 64.23), udever-bloom-7b (64.68 vs. 60.63), and sgpt-5.8b (64.68 vs. 58.93). The only baseline that outperforms echo is e5-mistral-7b (66.63), which uses closed-source training data not available to the authors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of bidirectional encoder models (BERT, RoBERTa) and recent autoregressive-to-encoder conversion methods (LLM2Vec, GritLM) that require architectural modification to bidirectional attention and/or additional unsupervised MLM fine-tuning. It also contrasts with PromptEOL, which extracts embeddings from a single predicted summary token. The key conceptual departure is borrowing the repetition idea from the reasoning literature (Xu et al., 2023; Arora et al., 2024) and repurposing it as a zero-shot inference-time trick for embeddings: by prompting the model to repeat the input and pooling from the second occurrence, causal attention effectively becomes bidirectional for those tokens without any weight changes or architectural modifications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a trivial input-repetition prompt nearly matches bidirectionally-converted LMs with additional MLM training (48.64 vs. 49.43 zero-shot on MTEB) challenges the field's working assumption that bidirectional attention is a prerequisite for strong embeddings, and opens the door to truly unified architectures that generate text and produce embeddings without switching modes. The main practical limitation is the 2× inference compute cost, though compute-matched results still show consistent gains. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multimodal or very long-context settings, whether the gains persist as base models scale beyond 7B, and whether the repetition trick can be combined with other inference-time interventions (e.g., chain-of-thought) for further improvement.
