## Problem

Convergence guarantees for incremental gradient and proximal methods on finite-sum convex problems were previously established only for the ergodic (average) iterate, leaving the practically relevant last iterate without nonasymptotic bounds in general convex settings. This paper closes that gap and further uses the incremental proximal method as a mathematical abstraction of catastrophic forgetting in continual learning with cyclic task replay, revealing fundamental limitations of regularization as a forgetting-control mechanism.

## Input / Output

The methods take T convex component functions (each L-smooth or G-Lipschitz), an initial point x_0, a fixed step size η (which in the proximal case equals the inverse of the ridge regularization strength), and a number of epochs K. The output is the last iterate x_K with a nonasymptotic optimality gap bound f(x_K) − f(x_*) ≤ ε, measured in individual gradient or proximal oracle queries. In the continual learning interpretation, the step size directly controls the regularization parameter, and the optimality gap at the last iterate corresponds to the excess forgetting after K cyclic replays of T tasks.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a reference-point analysis: a sequence z_k is defined recursively as z_k = (1−λ_k)x_k + λ_k z_{k−1} with z_{−1} = x_*, and carefully chosen weights w_k ensure that the distance terms telescope while the gap f(x_k) − f(z_{k−1}) can be related to the optimality gap f(x_k) − f(x_*) via a convexity-based decomposition (Lemma 2). By selecting λ_k and w_k so that intermediate optimality gap terms cancel, the analysis isolates the last iterate gap, yielding oracle complexity bounds that match the best known average-iterate rates up to a square-root-log factor. For the incremental proximal method, the proximal step is equivalently a backward gradient step on the Moreau envelope, enabling the same reference-point machinery in both smooth and Lipschitz settings. A key negative result (Theorem 3) constructs univariate quadratic examples showing that for any constant regularization parameter, forgetting is catastrophic (the optimality gap does not vanish), and that controlling forgetting to level ε requires regularization scaling polynomially in 1/ε, T, and σ_*.

## Results

The paper establishes the first nonasymptotic last-iterate oracle complexity bounds for incremental gradient descent (IGD) and the incremental proximal method (IPM). For IGD on convex L-smooth objectives, the last iterate achieves Õ(TL‖x₀−x*‖²/ε + TL^{1/2}σ*‖x₀−x*‖²/ε^{3/2}), matching the best known average-iterate rate up to a √log(1/ε) factor. For IPM in the smooth convex setting, the same Õ(TL‖x₀−x*‖²/ε + TL^{1/2}σ*‖x₀−x*‖²/ε^{3/2}) bound is obtained (a first for any IPM variant), while in the Lipschitz setting the bound is Õ(G²T‖x₀−x*‖²/ε²), matching prior average-iterate results up to a log factor. A key negative result (Theorem 3) proves that for any constant regularization parameter, forgetting is always catastrophic (non-zero asymptotic optimality gap), and that regularization must scale polynomially in 1/ε, T, and σ* to achieve ε-level forgetting control.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against prior average-iterate oracle complexity bounds for the same algorithms. For IGD, the last-iterate rate matches the best known average-iterate bound (Mishchenko et al., 2020; Cai et al., 2024) up to a √log(1/ε) factor, which is negligible in practice (≈4.3 for ε=10⁻⁸). For shuffled SGD (RR/SO), the last-iterate rate matches the average-iterate bound and the lower bound of Cha et al. (2023) up to the same √log factor, while achieving an order-√T improvement over the deterministic IGD rate. For IPM in the smooth convex setting, this is the first convergence result of any kind (average or last iterate) for component-wise proximal updates, so there is no direct prior bound to compare against. In the Lipschitz setting, the last-iterate rate matches Bertsekas (2011) and Li et al. (2019) up to a log factor. Compared to prior continual learning theory (Evron et al., 2022; Swartworth et al., 2024), which only handles convex quadratics with shared zero-valued minima (σ*=0), this paper covers general convex smooth/Lipschitz functions without shared-minima assumptions, at the cost of requiring sufficiently large explicit regularization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws on two distinct technical lineages: (1) the recent reference-point construction for last-iterate convergence of subgradient methods and SGD (Zamani & Glineur, 2023; Liu & Zhou, 2024a), and (2) the oracle-complexity analysis of incremental gradient methods and shuffled SGD (Mishchenko et al., 2020; Nguyen et al., 2021; Cai et al., 2024), which previously only yielded average-iterate guarantees. The key technical departure is adapting the reference-point technique to the incremental setting, where using a reference point other than x_* introduces nontrivial error terms (of the form (α/β)T(f(z)−f(x_*))) that must be carefully controlled without imposing unrealistic assumptions on component gradients. On the CL side, the paper departs from prior work (Evron et al., 2022; 2023; Swartworth et al., 2024) that required overparameterized linear models with shared minima and exact minimization, instead using the incremental proximal method with explicit ℓ₂-regularization to model a much broader class of convex tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first nonasymptotic last-iterate convergence guarantees for incremental gradient and proximal methods, closing a long-standing gap (open since Bottou, 2009) where only average-iterate bounds existed. The CL implications are significant: Theorem 3 proves that any constant regularization parameter leads to catastrophic forgetting (non-zero asymptotic optimality gap) even for simple quadratic tasks, and that controlling forgetting to level ε requires regularization scaling polynomially in 1/ε, T, and σ_*. This directly challenges the common CL practice of using fixed ridge regularization as a forgetting mitigation. Open questions include whether task-similarity assumptions can relax the regularization requirement, whether results extend to non-convex or overparameterized settings, and how the simplified cyclic-replay model translates to more realistic CL protocols with memory-based or expansion-based approaches.
