## Problem

Developing foundation models for bimanual manipulation faces a dual challenge: the doubled action space induces multi-modal action distributions that deterministic or discretized policies cannot represent, while the high cost of dual-arm hardware limits available data to fewer than 10K trajectories per robot. Existing approaches either rely on task-specific primitives with narrow generalization or scale to small models (≤93M parameters) on limited data, failing on complex, dexterous bimanual tasks.

## Input / Output

The model consumes two timesteps of three RGB views (exterior, left-wrist, right-wrist) resized to 384×384, a variable-length language instruction, the current proprioception vector, and the control frequency. It outputs a 64-step action chunk in a 128-dimensional Physically Interpretable Unified Action Space, where each slot has a fixed physical meaning (e.g., right arm joint positions at indices 0–9, left gripper velocities at 75–79, base linear velocities at 100–101). Unavailable dimensions for a given robot are padded with a 0-1 availability indicator appended to the vector (yielding 256 dims before encoding), and inference runs at 6 Hz chunk frequency (381 Hz per-action) via DPM-Solver++ with 5 denoising steps on an RTX 4090.

## Method

RDT-1B is a 1.2B-parameter Diffusion Transformer that models the conditional action distribution p(a_{t:t+64} | ℓ, o_t) via DDPM denoising, with three key architectural modifications from standard DiT: (1) QKNorm and RMSNorm replace LayerNorm to handle the unstable numerical range and time-series symmetry of robotic signals; (2) a nonlinear MLP decoder replaces the final linear projection to capture nonlinear robot dynamics; (3) Alternating Condition Injection (ACI) alternates image and text tokens across successive cross-attention layers to prevent the larger image token set from overshadowing language instructions. Multi-modal inputs are encoded by frozen SigLIP (images), frozen T5-XXL (language), and MLPs with Fourier features (proprioception, noisy actions, control frequency), then concatenated into a unified token sequence. A 128-dimensional Physically Interpretable Unified Action Space maps heterogeneous robot actions into shared physically meaningful slots, enabling pre-training on 46 multi-robot datasets (1M+ trajectories, 21 TB) before fine-tuning on a self-collected 6K+ trajectory bimanual dataset.

## Results

RDT-1B achieves 58.3% average success rate on Wash Cup (unseen objects), 100% on Pour Water-L-1/3 and 87.5% on Pour Water-R-2/3 (instruction following), 75% on Pour Water in unseen scenes, 68% on Fold Shorts (1-shot), 40% on Handover (5-shot), and 48% on Robot Dog (dexterity). The paper reports an overall 56% improvement in success rates over the strongest baselines across the seven challenging tasks. RDT also achieves 100% correct-hand and 100% correct-amount sub-task success on Pour Water-L-1/3, demonstrating precise language instruction following for quantities never seen in training (e.g., 'one-third').

## Baselines & Comparisons

RDT-1B outperforms all three baselines (ACT, OpenVLA, Octo) on every task where they are evaluated. ACT (Action Chunking with Transformers, VAE-based) achieves only 32% on Robot Dog and 12.5% on Wash Cup, and 0% on all instruction-following and few-shot tasks, as it lacks language conditioning and cannot model multi-modal action distributions. OpenVLA (7B, discretized VLA) and Octo (93M, diffusion-based) both score 0% on nearly all tasks, with Octo marginally achieving 4% on Fold Shorts and 4.2% on Pour Water (unseen scene). RDT's advantage is most dramatic on instruction-following tasks (100% vs. 0% for Pour Water-L-1/3) and few-shot learning (68% vs. 0–4% for Fold Shorts, 40% vs. 0% for Handover). Notably, even RDT trained from scratch (no pre-training) surpasses all baselines on Fold Shorts (40% vs. ≤4%) and Pour Water unseen scenes (41.7% vs. ≤16.7%), indicating the diffusion-Transformer architecture itself provides substantial gains, while large-scale multi-robot pre-training further amplifies zero-shot generalization to unseen objects and scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

RDT extends the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone with three targeted modifications (QKNorm+RMSNorm, MLP decoder, Alternating Condition Injection) to handle the nonlinear, high-frequency, and numerically unstable characteristics of robotic physics data, departing from DiT's image-domain assumptions. It builds on the diffusion-policy paradigm of Chi et al. (2023) for continuous action modeling and action chunking, while scaling the approach from Octo's 93M parameters to 1.2B. The Physically Interpretable Unified Action Space is a novel contribution that departs from prior cross-robot approaches (RT-1, Octo, OpenVLA) which either restrict to structurally similar robots or discard heterogeneous dimensions, instead preserving physical meaning across all gripper-arm embodiments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RDT-1B establishes the largest diffusion-based foundation model for robotic manipulation and one of the largest open-source bimanual datasets (6K+ episodes, 300+ tasks), providing a strong baseline and practical tool for the community. The Physically Interpretable Unified Action Space offers a principled recipe for multi-robot pre-training that could be adopted by subsequent work. Key limitations include evaluation on a single robot platform (ALOHA), heavy compute requirements (48×H100 for one month of pre-training), and the fact that several baselines (OpenVLA, Octo) score 0% on many tasks, suggesting they were not properly adapted to bimanual settings, which weakens the comparative evidence. Open questions include whether the unified action space generalizes to non-gripper or dexterous-hand robots, and whether the 1.2B scale is truly necessary or whether smaller models with better data could match performance.
