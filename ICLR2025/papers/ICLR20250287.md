## Problem

Existing convergence analyses for Adam and SGD both achieve the minimax-optimal rate Õ(T^{-1/4}) under standard ℓ₂-smoothness, making them theoretically indistinguishable in the worst case. This cannot explain Adam's consistent empirical advantage over SGD for training language models. The paper identifies that Adam is only permutation-equivariant (not rotation-equivariant like SGD), suggesting it exploits non-rotation-invariant geometric properties of the loss landscape that are invisible to standard smoothness assumptions.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a (possibly stochastic) loss function L: ℝᵈ → ℝ, its Hessian (or Hessian-vector products for tractable estimation via random Cauchy vectors), and Adam hyperparameters (η, β₁, β₂, ε, v₀). It produces convergence rate bounds on the ℓ₁ norm of the gradient, where the smoothness constant is the (1,1)-norm of the Hessian ‖∇²L(x)‖₁,₁ for coordinate-wise Adam, or the more general Φ-smoothness H(L,Φ) for blockwise variants. The (1,1)-norm is shown to be much smaller than d·‖∇²L(x)‖₂ empirically (e.g., 2.35 vs 4.24 for GPT-2), yielding tighter bounds.

## Method

The key insight is that SignGD (Adam with β₁=β₂=0) is normalized steepest descent w.r.t. the ℓ∞ norm, making ℓ∞-smoothness the natural geometric assumption rather than the conventional ℓ₂-smoothness. The paper introduces coordinate-wise ℓ∞-smoothness (Definition 3.3) where each coordinate i has its own Lipschitz constant Hᵢ, with the (1,1)-norm of the Hessian providing a tractable upper bound on ΣHᵢ. For the stochastic case, RMSProp (β₁=0) achieves O((log T/T)^{1/4}) rate via a first-order approximation lemma (Lemma 3.13) that handles the stochastic denominator and a second-order bounding lemma (Lemma 3.12) that controls the sum via a log-ratio. The analysis is unified under blockwise Adam (Algorithm 3) with a novel Φ-smoothness measure (Definition 3.9) that recovers Adam (per-coordinate blocks, smallest H) and AdaSGD (single block, largest H = d·‖∇²L‖₂) as special cases, explaining why Adam's coordinate-wise adaptivity yields a strictly smaller smoothness constant and thus faster convergence when the (1,1)-norm is well below the spectral norm.

## Results

The paper proves a convergence rate of O(1/√T) for Adam in the deterministic case and O((log T/T)^(1/4)) in the stochastic case under ℓ∞-smoothness, with the smoothness constant given by the (1,1)-norm of the Hessian rather than the spectral norm. Empirically, on GPT-2 the (1,1)-norm of the Hessian is 2.35 for Adam, 4.24 for AdaSGD, and 14.37 for rotated Adam, directly correlating with observed optimization speed. On ResNet-18/CIFAR-10, the corresponding values are 0.0036 (Adam), 1.54 (AdaSGD), and 0.99 (rotated Adam), confirming the same pattern across architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is AdaSGD (Wang & Wiens, 2020), a rotation-equivariant variant of SGD with coordinate-wise normalization. Adam consistently outperforms AdaSGD on both GPT-2/OpenWebText and ResNet-18/CIFAR-10 in terms of training loss convergence speed, with the gap explained by the (1,1)-norm of the Hessian being much smaller for Adam's partition (2.35 vs 4.24 on GPT-2; 0.0036 vs 1.54 on ResNet-18). When the loss is rotated, Adam's performance degrades dramatically (Hessian (1,1)-norm jumps to 14.37 on GPT-2), becoming even worse than AdaSGD, while AdaSGD's performance remains unchanged due to its rotation-equivariance. This confirms that Adam's advantage over AdaSGD is specifically tied to the non-rotation-invariant ℓ∞-geometry of the loss landscape, which AdaSGD cannot exploit. The paper also compares against vanilla SGD, which is dominated by both Adam and AdaSGD in optimization speed on these tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard ℓ₂-smoothness framework used in prior Adam convergence analyses (Defossez et al., 2022; Li & Lin, 2024) by introducing ℓ∞-smoothness, motivated by the observation (from Xie & Li, 2024) that SignGD is normalized steepest descent under the ℓ∞ norm. While Li & Lin (2024) improved the dimension dependence using ℓ₁ gradient norms, they retained rotation-invariant ℓ₂ smoothness, which yields identical rates for Adam and AdaSGD. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of the (1,1)-norm of the Hessian as a non-rotation-invariant complexity metric, and the unification of Adam and AdaSGD under a blockwise Adam framework with a corresponding Φ-smoothness measure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first theoretical explanation for why Adam empirically outperforms SGD on language models despite identical worst-case convergence rates: Adam exploits non-rotation-invariant ℓ∞-geometry of the loss landscape that SGD cannot access. The (1,1)-norm of the Hessian is empirically much smaller than d × spectral norm for GPT-2 and ResNet-18, making the bound practically meaningful. A significant limitation is that the main analysis requires β₁=0 (no momentum), so it does not explain the benefit of the β₁=0.9 used in practice; the authors acknowledge this gap and leave it for future work. The blockwise framework also offers a principled lens for understanding recent practical variants like Adam-mini and Adalayer.
