## Problem

Prior MI-based diversity objectives in heterogeneous imitation learning (e.g., InfoGAIL) fail to produce novel behaviors that generalize to unseen latent factor values: without regularization, the decoder assigns arbitrary latent vectors to nearby states, and with uniform Lipschitz constraints, behaviors deviate from demonstrations in task-irrelevant regions. The paper asks how to formulate diversity so that learned behaviors interpolate and extrapolate along the specific latent dimensions that actually vary across demonstrations, while still accomplishing the task.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of diverse expert demonstration trajectories from a continuous state-action space, where each trajectory is implicitly governed by a 1D continuous factor value (e.g., running speed, placement location, overtaking headway). At test time, the agent receives a state and a 2D latent vector sampled from a Gaussian prior; the policy outputs an action, and different latent samples yield distinct behaviors corresponding to different factor values. The factor values are not provided during training or at inference—the latent space must be inferred entirely from the demonstration heterogeneity.

## Method

GSD introduces two synergistic components on top of InfoGAIL. First, *conditioned distillation* (ConDist) decomposes the latent-conditioned discriminator D(s,a,z) in logit space into a latent-independent energy function f(s,a) plus a latent-dependent residual g(s,a,z), with a distillation loss driving g toward zero so that f captures both common and demonstration-specific occupancy regions as a task-relevance measure. Second, *f-relevant diversity* replaces uniform Lipschitz constraints on the decoder with a constraint scaled by f-energy of both the source and target state-action pairs, so the decoder is only incentivized to assign distinct latent vectors in high-energy (task-relevant) regions while collapsing low-energy regions to a shared code. Training alternates between updating f/g/b via the combined GAIL + distillation objective, updating the decoder with the f-scaled constraint enforced through a Lagrange multiplier, and updating the policy with PPO using rewards from both the discriminator (imitation) and the decoder (diversity).

## Results

GSD achieves a ~21% average reduction in recovery error (MAE between desired and measured latent factor values) over the next-best baseline across three continuous control domains (HalfCheetah, DriveLaneshift, FetchPickPlace) and two generalization splits (interpolation and extrapolation). Specifically, at K=50 test-time samples, GSD improves recovery by 18.3% for interpolation and 24.6% for extrapolation over the next-best method. In DriveLaneshift interpolation, GSD reduces average recovery error from 1.277 (InfoGAIL) to 0.530, a 58% reduction. GSD also maintains or improves task performance (environment return) while achieving these recovery gains, as shown in the task-vs-recovery tradeoff plots.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against InfoGAIL (Li et al., 2017), GSD consistently achieves lower recovery error across all domains and splits, with the largest gap in DriveLaneshift (0.530 vs. 1.277 for interpolation, a 58% reduction) and HalfCheetah extrapolation (0.472 vs. 0.697, a 32% reduction). Against offline IL baselines (BC-VAE, IBC, BeT, Diffusion Policy), GSD outperforms all methods in both task performance and recovery error for domains with complex dynamics (HalfCheetah, FetchPickPlace), where environment interaction is necessary for task completion. In the simpler DriveLaneshift domain, IBC, BeT, and Diffusion Policy are competitive with GSD for interpolation, but GSD still outperforms all offline methods for extrapolation. The paper also shows that adding Lipschitz constraints to InfoGAIL (IG+Lipz) helps only in FetchPickPlace where the relevant factor aligns with uniform state-space diversity, while GSD's task-relevant diversity generalizes across all domains without domain-specific tuning of the diversity structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on InfoGAIL (Li et al., 2017), which couples GAIL with a mutual-information diversity objective to learn latent-conditioned policies from heterogeneous demonstrations. It departs from this lineage by showing that both the vanilla MI objective and standard Lipschitz-constrained regularizations (borrowed from unsupervised skill discovery, Park et al. 2022) produce misaligned or insufficient diversity for generalization to unseen factor values. The key new ingredient is a 'task-relevant diversity' constraint that scales latent-assignment freedom by a learned energy function f, derived via a conditioned distillation procedure (ConDist) inspired by MSRD (Chen et al., 2020) reward distillation. The overall framework sits at the intersection of adversarial imitation learning, variational representation learning, and network distillation, but the selective-diversity formulation is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in heterogeneous imitation learning: prior methods can reproduce training modes but fail to interpolate or extrapolate to latent factor values never seen in demonstrations, limiting their use in personalized HRI and few-shot adaptation. The ~21% average recovery-error reduction and the real-world table-tennis demonstration show the approach is not purely theoretical. However, the evaluation is restricted to 1D continuous factors and synthetic expert policies for the main benchmarks, and the real-world demo covers only one domain with a single factor (ball height). Open questions include whether the formulation extends to higher-dimensional or non-Markovian factors, visual observation spaces, and non-expert demonstrators, as well as whether the approximate Lagrange-multiplier constraint enforcement can be replaced with parametric alternatives for stronger guarantees.
