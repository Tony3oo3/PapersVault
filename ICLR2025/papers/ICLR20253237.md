## Problem

Existing masked motion generation models rely on random masking that treats all tokens equally, failing to target the most informative and challenging regions of motion sequences. Additionally, prior text-to-motion methods condition generation on a single sentence embedding (e.g., CLIP), which cannot capture the dynamic, multi-level structure of human motion described in natural language, leading to a lack of fine-grained control over complex sequential actions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253237/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language description of human motion (e.g., 'a person walks forward, is pushed backward, then resumes walking forward') and produces a 3D human motion sequence at 20 FPS with durations of 2–10 seconds. The text is decomposed via semantic role parsing into a hierarchical graph of motion, action, and specific-level nodes, while the motion is represented as discrete tokens from a residual VQ-VAE with V+1 quantization layers. At inference, the output is generated hierarchically over L iterations, progressively refining from coarse global structure to fine-grained action details.

## Method

HGM³ integrates two key components into a shared-weight masked transformer framework. First, Hard Token Mining (HTM) employs a teacher-student scheme where an auxiliary loss-predictor transformer (G_φ) ranks tokens by reconstruction difficulty, and a curriculum (easy-to-hard) linearly increases the proportion of hard-masked tokens from α₀ to α_T over training, replacing indiscriminate random masking. Second, a hierarchical semantic graph built from semantic role parsing is processed by a Graph Attention Network to produce three levels of text conditioning (motion, action, specific), all fed simultaneously to the same masked transformer to balance global and fine-grained semantics. During inference, the model iteratively unmaskes tokens over L steps, switching conditioning levels from motion-only to motion+action to full hierarchy, before a residual transformer predicts the remaining V layers of quantization residuals for final decoding.

## Results

On HumanML3D, HGM³ achieves the best FID of 0.036, R-Precision Top-1 of 0.535, and MM-Distance of 2.904, outperforming all 14 baselines on these primary metrics. On KIT-ML, HGM³ similarly leads with FID 0.176, R-Precision Top-1 0.664, and MM-Distance 2.710. The method also reports competitive Diversity (9.545) and MModality (1.206) on HumanML3D, though MoMask slightly edges it on MModality (1.241) and M2DM/BAMM lead on Diversity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HGM³ outperforms all 14 baselines on the three primary metrics (FID, R-Precision, MM-Distance) on both HumanML3D and KIT-ML. The closest competitors among masked-motion models are BAMM (FID 0.055, R-P1 0.525) and MoMask (FID 0.045, R-P1 0.521); HGM³ improves FID by 35% over BAMM and 20% over MoMask, while gaining 0.010 and 0.014 in R-Precision Top-1 respectively. Among diffusion-based methods, the gap is larger: HGM³'s FID of 0.036 is roughly 3× lower than MLD (0.473) and 7× lower than MDM (0.544). On secondary metrics, MoMask slightly leads on MModality (1.241 vs. 1.206) and M2DM/BAMM lead on Diversity (9.926/9.717 vs. 9.545), but the authors note these are secondary to FID and R-Precision. The ablation in Table 4 confirms that the Hard Token Mining strategy (α₀=0, α_T=0.5) is critical: removing it (pure random masking) degrades FID from 0.036 to 0.044 and R-P1 from 0.535 to 0.529.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: (1) the masked motion model paradigm established by MoMask and MMM, which tokenize motion via residual VQ-VAE and reconstruct masked tokens with a transformer, and (2) the Hard Patch Mining (HPM) strategy from the image domain, which uses a teacher-student scheme to selectively mask high-loss regions. The paper adapts HPM to motion tokens (HTM) and combines it with hierarchical semantic graph conditioning borrowed from GraphMotion (Jin et al., 2024), which itself draws on semantic role parsing and GAT-based message passing. The key departure from prior masked motion models is the replacement of random masking with difficulty-aware masking and the introduction of a hierarchical inference schedule where text conditioning granularity increases across generation iterations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combination of difficulty-aware token masking with multi-granularity text conditioning offers a principled way to address two persistent weaknesses in text-to-motion generation: the model's inability to focus learning on temporally complex segments, and the loss of fine-grained semantic detail when compressing text into a single CLIP embedding. The ablation showing that pure hard masking (α_T=1) degrades performance relative to a mixed random+hard schedule is a useful practical insight for anyone designing curriculum-based masking strategies. Open questions include whether the semantic role parsing pipeline generalizes to non-English descriptions or highly compositional/long prompts, and whether HTM's teacher-student overhead is justified at scale compared to simpler difficulty heuristics.
