## Problem

Existing Video-LLMs and Audio-Visual LLMs consistently neglect the audio modality, producing responses derived solely from visual content and exhibiting severe audio hallucinations. This stems from three gaps: (1) most architectures simply concatenate audio and visual tokens without fine-grained cross-modal alignment, (2) large-scale audio-visual instruction-following datasets are scarce, and (3) the higher information density of video causes models to disregard audio as unnecessary.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250152/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 8 video frames at 224×224 resolution (encoded by CLIP ViT-L/14) and 8 corresponding audio clips converted to 128×204 spectrograms (encoded by ImageBind's AST-based audio encoder), alongside a text instruction. Each frame's audio-visual tokens are condensed into two tokens before being fed to the Vicuna-v1.5 LLM, which generates free-form text responses including captions, multi-turn Q&A, and task-specific answers (e.g., source localization coordinates, event timestamps).

## Method

Dolphin introduces two complementary alignment mechanisms. For spatial alignment, an audio-visual multi-scale adapter (inspired by ViT-Adapter) extracts pyramid features at 1/8, 1/16, and 1/32 resolutions from both modalities, then injects the other modality's global features into each modality's multi-scale features via cross-attention across 4 ViT blocks, followed by intra-modality fusion back into the global pathway. For temporal alignment, audio-visual interleaved merging performs bidirectional cross-attention within each frame so that visual tokens become audio-contextualized and vice versa, before a joint projector maps each AV group into LLM input space. Training proceeds in two stages: pretraining on high-consistency AVU-Pretrain samples (freezing encoders and LLM, training only projectors and the adapter) followed by instruction tuning on mixed subsets (AVU-MultiQA, AVU-Specific, AVU-Negatives, AVU-Tasks) that include modality-specific questions and negative samples to teach rejection and mitigate hallucination.

## Results

On zero-shot video QA, Dolphin-7B achieves 72.7% accuracy on MSVD-QA, 62.6% on MSR-VTT-QA, 49.1% on ActivityNet-QA, and 85.9 on POPE, outperforming VideoLLaMA 2 (71.7/57.4/49.9/85.4) and Video-LLaVA (70.7/59.2/45.3/84.4). On the proposed AVU-Bench, Dolphin attains 78.2% accuracy on MUSIC (AVQA), 52.1% on AVE, and 59.1% on AVSD, surpassing video-SALMONN (74.7/51.5/51.6) and VideoLLaMA 2 (73.2/48.2/57.2). On audio tasks, Dolphin reaches 83.1% on ESC-50, 17.8 SPICE on AudioCaps, and 63.2 on the audio hallucination benchmark, exceeding VideoLLaMA 2 (74.8/15.8/56.8) and video-SALMONN (77.6/16.6/58.2), though it trails video-SALMONN on Librispeech WER (8.3 vs. 3.9) due to using ImageBind rather than Whisper as the audio encoder.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dolphin-7B outperforms VideoLLaMA 2 by +1.0 on MSVD-QA (72.7 vs. 71.7), +5.2 on MSR-VTT-QA (62.6 vs. 57.4), and +0.5 on POPE (85.9 vs. 85.4), while trailing slightly on ActivityNet-QA (49.1 vs. 49.9). Against video-SALMONN, Dolphin leads on MSVD-QA (+4.8) and MSR-VTT-QA (+3.1). On the AV-specific benchmark (Table 4), Dolphin achieves consistent gains over all baselines: +3.5 over video-SALMONN on MUSIC, +0.6 on AVE, +3.0 on LLP, and +7.5 on AVSD. On audio tasks (Table 3), Dolphin surpasses VideoLLaMA 2 on ESC-50 (+8.3), AudioCaps (+2.0 SPICE), IEMOCAP (+5.3), and audio hallucination (+6.4), and beats video-SALMONN on ESC-50 (+5.5), AudioCaps (+1.2), and hallucination (+5.0), but loses on Librispeech WER (8.3 vs. 3.9) because it uses ImageBind-audio rather than Whisper. The 13B variant further extends the lead, reaching 74.8 on MSVD-QA and 86.2 on POPE, confirming that the architecture and dataset scale effectively with model size.

## Relation to Prior Work

Dolphin extends the AV-LLM lineage (Video-LLaMA, AV-LLM, FAVOR, AVicuna, video-SALMONN) by addressing a specific architectural gap: prior models either simply concatenate audio and visual tokens or perform only coarse temporal alignment, neglecting fine-grained spatial interaction. The spatial alignment module directly adapts the dual-pathway cross-attention design of ViT-Adapter (Chen et al., 2023) to a cross-modal setting, while the temporal interleaved merging generalizes the token-interleaving idea from AVicuna with bidirectional contextual attention. On the data side, the paper departs from the common practice of training vision-language and audio-language branches separately by introducing AVU, the first large-scale dataset with a consistency-based partitioning strategy that explicitly targets modality-specific and negative samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The white-noise diagnostic cleanly demonstrates that widely-used AV-LLMs effectively ignore audio, a finding that should prompt the community to re-examine how audio is integrated into video LLMs. The AVU dataset (5.2M Q&A pairs) and AVU-Bench fill a concrete gap in the ecosystem and provide a reusable resource for future AV-LLM research. Limitations include reliance on frozen encoders (ImageBind audio encoder underperforms Whisper on speech tasks), 7B/13B model scale, and the fact that the multi-scale adapter adds significant computational overhead. Open questions include whether the fine-grained alignment strategy scales to larger backbones, whether the consistency-based dataset partitioning generalizes beyond the curated source datasets, and whether the approach transfers to streaming or long-form video settings.
