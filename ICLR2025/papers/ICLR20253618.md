## Problem

Offline evaluation and alignment of chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning in LLMs remains underexplored: human feedback for sequential CoT is expensive, scales exponentially with reasoning depth, and annotators often lack domain expertise. Existing KG-based CoT evaluation methods require accurate entity linking and grounding of free-form LLM reasoning in specific KG structures, creating a heterogeneity gap that limits generalizability across models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253618/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes question prompts (from the CWQ dataset), the Wikidata5M knowledge graph, and offline logged CoT trajectories. KG trajectories are verbalized into natural language via GPT-4 and used to train a GPT2-Medium behavior policy μ_φ. The output is an optimized LLM policy whose CoT generation is aligned with KG reasoning preferences, evaluated via a token-level KG-IPS value estimate V̂(θ) that separately weights entity tokens (by μ_φ) and non-entity tokens (by the base policy π_0).

## Method

OCEAN formulates CoT generation as an MDP where each reasoning step is a token-sequence action, then bridges the LLM–KG heterogeneity by training a verbalized KG preference policy μ_φ (GPT2-Medium) via policy gradient on self-consistency QA accuracy over sampled Wikidata5M trajectories. The core estimator, KG-IPS, applies two-folded inverse propensity weights: entity tokens are reweighted by π_θ/μ_φ while non-entity tokens are reweighted by π_θ/π_0, preventing variance accumulation over long reasoning chains and avoiding model degeneration on surrounding text. The paper proves the estimator is unbiased and derives a variance lower bound of Ω(M²/n) with sub-Gaussian concentration confidence intervals. Policy optimization proceeds by directly maximizing V̂(θ) via policy gradient, requiring no online interaction or ground-truth answer supervision during alignment.

## Results

OCEAN consistently improves the estimated policy value V̂(θ) across all four backbone LLMs and all three multi-hop QA tasks. For example, on HotpotQA (w/o ctx), Mistral-0.2's V̂(θ) improves from −19.08 (Base) to −3.12 (OCEAN), while SFT degrades it to −18.53; on MuSiQue, Gemma-2's V̂(θ) improves from −26.41 to −22.15 under OCEAN versus −40.25 under SFT. On commonsense reasoning, OCEAN maintains or improves average accuracy (e.g., Gemma-2: 57.10→62.22; Mistral-0.2: 60.98→61.82), whereas SFT causes catastrophic forgetting (Gemma-2: 57.10→30.58; Mistral-0.2: 60.98→31.05). On knowledge-intensive tasks, OCEAN achieves the best or near-best accuracy on ARC, PubMedQA, and SciQA across all backbones, while SFT suffers large drops (e.g., Gemma-2 ARC: 65.89→18.06; Mistral-0.2 ARC: 73.91→43.48).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares OCEAN against two baselines: the unmodified Base LLM and Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on the CWQ dataset. OCEAN wins decisively on the policy value metric V̂(θ) across all 12 model-task combinations in Table 1, with the largest gains on Mistral-0.2 HotpotQA (−19.08→−3.12) and Phi-3.5 HotpotQA (−19.49→−14.84). SFT, in contrast, consistently degrades V̂(θ) (e.g., Phi-3.5 HotpotQA drops to −44.57, Gemma-2 MuSiQue to −40.25), indicating that direct supervised alignment with KG-derived answers harms the model's reasoning policy. On downstream accuracy, OCEAN matches or slightly exceeds Base LLMs (e.g., Phi-3.5 HotpotQA w/ctx: 32.13→35.13; Gemma-2 StrategyQA w/o ctx: 60.99→66.23), while SFT often causes large drops (Llama-3 HotpotQA w/ctx: 32.78→8.22). On commonsense reasoning (Table 3), OCEAN preserves or improves average accuracy for all four backbones, whereas SFT triggers catastrophic forgetting for Gemma-2 (57.10→30.58) and Mistral-0.2 (60.98→31.05). The paper does not compare against other alignment methods such as DPO, RLHF, or RLAIF, limiting the scope of the baseline comparison to SFT only.

## Relation to Prior Work

OCEAN extends the offline policy evaluation (OPE) literature—originally developed for recommender systems and healthcare—to the setting of LLM chain-of-thought reasoning, formulating CoT generation as an MDP and proposing a KG-IPS estimator that combines inverse propensity scoring with knowledge-graph preference feedback. It departs from prior CoT evaluation work (Nguyen et al., 2024b) by verbalizing KG trajectories into natural language rather than requiring exact entity grounding, and from RLHF/DPO by replacing human-annotated preferences with automatic KG-derived rewards. The reward function follows Zhang et al. (2024) (log-likelihood as reward), and the optimal-policy equivalence argument borrows Lemma 2 from DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024) to show that the non-entity regularization term does not alter the optimal policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OCEAN offers a theoretically grounded, fully offline alternative to RLHF for aligning CoT reasoning, which is significant given the cost and scalability challenges of collecting human feedback on multi-step reasoning. The unbiasedness proof and variance lower bound provide rare theoretical guarantees in the LLM alignment literature. However, empirical gains over base models are modest (typically 0.5–3% accuracy), and the framework is validated only on small-to-mid-size LLMs (2B–8B) with a single KG (Wikidata5M); open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models, more diverse KGs, and multi-agent or agentic settings where CoT evaluation becomes even more critical.
