## Problem

Neural PDE solvers have become highly capable but also highly specialized, requiring problem-specific meshing, discretization, and solver expertise that limits practical adoption by engineers. Existing autoregressive neural solvers suffer from error accumulation over long rollouts, and no prior work has explored natural language as a conditioning modality for generating physics simulations. This paper asks whether latent diffusion models can produce accurate, full spatio-temporal PDE solutions from either physical initial conditions or a compact text prompt, while handling arbitrary mesh discretizations.

## Input / Output

The model accepts either the first frame of a PDE solution (velocity, pressure, or density fields defined on ~2000 mesh points or a 128×128 grid) or a natural language prompt (e.g., describing cylinder geometry, Reynolds number, and flow regime) as conditioning. It outputs a complete spatio-temporal solution trajectory (25–48 timesteps for 2D problems, 48 timesteps for 3D turbulence) decoded onto arbitrary query points via a kernel integral, enabling discretization invariance. The text prompt is encoded by a fine-tuned RoBERTa transformer, while the first frame is encoded by the same mesh autoencoder used for the full solution, with the downsampled latent flattened as a conditioning sequence.

## Method

The framework has three components: (1) a mesh autoencoder that uses a learned kernel integral to aggregate arbitrarily discretized PDE data onto a uniform latent grid, compresses it via a CNN backbone into a low-dimensional latent vector, and decodes back to arbitrary mesh points via a reversed kernel integral; (2) a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) that denoises in this latent space to generate the entire spatio-temporal trajectory at once, avoiding autoregressive error accumulation; and (3) a conditioning mechanism that injects either the first-frame latent (via cross-attention) or a RoBERTa-encoded text prompt (via adaLN-Zero plus cross-attention) into the DiT. The autoencoder is trained with a small KL penalty to balance latent regularity against reconstruction fidelity, and the diffusion model is trained with a standard DDPM objective using a linear noise schedule and ε-parameterization. The DiT backbone outperforms a 3D U-Net, and the model scales to ~3B parameters with consistent accuracy gains.

## Results

On the Cylinder Flow benchmark (mesh-based), the LDM_M-FF model achieves a relative L2 loss of 0.1309 with only 1.16 Tflops, outperforming GINO (0.2445, 0.73 Tflops), MGN (0.2617, 32.16 Tflops), and OFormer (0.3386, 17.34 Tflops). On the Buoyancy-Driven Flow benchmark, the LDM_L-FF (1.55B params) achieves L2 loss 0.2728 at 2.19 Tflops, beating FNO (0.5126), Unet (0.3050), Dil-Resnet (0.4466), and ACDM (0.4766). On 3D Turbulence, LDM-FF (2.72B) achieves L2 loss 0.602 and log-TKE distance 5.630, outperforming FNO (0.862/6.524), FactFormer (0.795/6.022), and Dil-Resnet (0.707/6.153) while using far fewer FLOPs (9.78 vs. 249 Tflops for Dil-Resnet).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The LDM framework consistently outperforms all nine baselines across all three benchmarks. On Cylinder Flow, LDM_M-FF (L2=0.1309) beats GINO (0.2445) by 46% while using comparable FLOPs (1.16 vs. 0.73), and dramatically outperforms MGN (0.2617) and OFormer (0.3386) which require 32 and 17 Tflops respectively. On Buoyancy-Driven Flow, LDM_L-FF (0.2728) surpasses the strongest baseline Unet (0.3050) and the autoregressive diffusion model ACDM (0.4766) by 18% and 43% respectively, while using 10–24× fewer FLOPs. On 3D Turbulence, LDM-FF (L2=0.602, D_TKE=5.630) outperforms FNO (0.862/6.524), FactFormer (0.795/6.022), and Dil-Resnet (0.707/6.153) on both metrics, with Dil-Resnet requiring 25× more FLOPs (249 vs. 9.78 Tflops). Text-conditioned LDM models remain competitive: LDM_M-Text on Cylinder Flow (0.1476) approaches first-frame conditioning (0.1309) despite conditioning on ~1000× less data, and LDM_L-Text on Buoyancy (0.2944*) nearly matches LDM_L-FF (0.2728). The key efficiency advantage is that LDM generates the full spatio-temporal trajectory in a single forward pass, avoiding the autoregressive error accumulation that degrades GINO, MGN, FNO, and ACDM over long rollouts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of latent diffusion models (Rombach et al., 2022; Ho et al., 2020) and neural PDE solvers, departing from the dominant autoregressive paradigm (GINO, MeshGraphNet, OFormer, FNO) by generating full spatio-temporal trajectories in a single denoising pass. The mesh autoencoder repurposes the kernel integral from geometry-informed neural operators (Li et al., 2020, 2023c) as a discretization-invariant compression mechanism, while the DiT backbone (Peebles & Xie, 2023) replaces the conventional Unet. Prior diffusion-for-PDE work (Kohl et al., 2024; Lienen et al., 2024) operated without latent spaces and on individual timesteps; this paper's combination of a learned latent space, full-trajectory generation, and text conditioning is the key conceptual departure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The text2PDE framing opens a genuinely new interface for physics simulation—engineers could prompt a simulator with natural language rather than specifying discretizations and initial fields—though the current evidence is strongest on simple geometries (cylinder flow) and weaker on complex initial conditions (smoke buoyancy). Scaling to ~3B parameters with continued accuracy gains suggests the approach could unify diverse PDE phenomena given larger datasets. Key open questions include: inference speed (DDIM helps but 1000-step DDPM is still slow), fixed temporal resolution limiting long-horizon rollouts, LLM captioning hallucinations degrading text quality, and whether the single-mode distribution argument for fast DDIM convergence generalizes beyond the tested benchmarks.
