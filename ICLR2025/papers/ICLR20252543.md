## Problem

State-of-the-art motion correction (MoCo) methods for radial MRI, such as Score-MoCo, rely on pre-trained neural networks that require large-scale high-quality MR datasets, increasing reconstruction costs and causing severe out-of-domain generalization failures. The potential of unsupervised implicit neural representations (INR) for the challenging joint problem of motion estimation and artifact-free reconstruction from undersampled, motion-corrupted k-space data remains unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252543/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes undersampled radial k-space spokes (360 or 180 views at 2×/4× acceleration, each of length 511) corrupted by rigid motion across 18 quasi-static motion stages, and produces a complex-valued artifact-free MR image (e.g., 320×320 or 256×256) along with per-spoke rigid motion parameters (rotation angle and 2D shift). The k-space data is first converted to projection data via 1D inverse Fourier transform before optimization, and the entire pipeline is fully unsupervised—no paired training data is accessed at any point.

## Method

Moner represents the MR image as a continuous function parameterized by an MLP with multiresolution hash encoding, and integrates a quasi-static rigid motion model by assigning each spoke a learnable motion triplet (rotation, x-shift, y-shift) that warps canonical-space coordinates into the physical world before INR evaluation. A key reformulation uses the Fourier-slice theorem to convert the radial k→f problem into a back-projection (g→f) problem, where the INR is optimized against projection data rather than raw k-space, fundamentally avoiding the high dynamic range issue that destabilizes optimization. A novel coarse-to-fine hash encoding strategy progressively increases the encoding resolution (L=4→16) during training, allowing early iterations to focus on low-frequency global structures for accurate motion estimation before recovering high-frequency image details. The INR parameters and all motion triplets are jointly optimized via Adam by minimizing an L1 loss between predicted and measured projections over randomly sampled rays, requiring no external training data.

## Results

On the in-domain fastMRI dataset (AF=2×, MR=±5), Moner achieves 32.62±2.61 dB PSNR and 0.914±0.067 SSIM, slightly below the SOTA Score-MoCo (33.64±2.22 dB, 0.849±0.038 SSIM) but well above TV (29.53 dB), DRN-DCMB (25.63 dB), and NuIFFT (22.93 dB). On the out-of-domain MoDL dataset, Moner substantially outperforms Score-MoCo, reaching 34.54±0.90 dB PSNR vs. 32.45±4.27 dB at AF=2× and 33.24±0.88 dB vs. 30.09±2.55 dB at AF=4× (MR=±5). For motion parameter estimation, Moner achieves σ_θ ≈ 0.009–0.010° and σ_τ < 1 mm across all fastMRI settings, compared to σ_θ ≈ 0.038–0.044° for Score-MoCo and σ_θ up to 2.5° for TV at AF=4×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Moner is compared against four baselines: NuIFFT (analytical), TV (iterative optimization), DRN-DCMB (supervised CNN), and Score-MoCo (supervised diffusion, current SOTA). On the in-domain fastMRI dataset, Moner is within ~1 dB PSNR of Score-MoCo (e.g., 32.62 vs. 33.64 at AF=2×, MR=±5) while requiring no training data, and substantially outperforms TV (~3 dB), DRN-DCMB (~7 dB), and NuIFFT (~10 dB). On the out-of-domain MoDL dataset, Moner decisively wins: it surpasses Score-MoCo by 2.1 dB at AF=2× and 3.2 dB at AF=4× (MR=±5), while Score-MoCo degrades to near-TV levels due to OOD failure. For motion parameter estimation, Moner achieves σ_θ ≈ 0.009° and σ_τ < 1 mm across all fastMRI settings, compared to σ_θ ≈ 0.038° for Score-MoCo and σ_θ up to 2.5° for TV at AF=4×. Moner also runs >5× faster than Score-MoCo and >6× faster than TV on a single GPU.

## Relation to Prior Work

Moner extends the implicit neural representation (INR) framework for MRI reconstruction (Shen et al. 2022; Spieker et al. 2023b; Feng et al. 2022) by integrating a quasi-static rigid motion model, a capability entirely absent from prior INR-based MRI methods. It departs from the dominant supervised paradigm (DRN-DCMB, Score-MoCo) that requires large-scale paired training data, instead leveraging the INR's inherent low-frequency learning bias as a data-free prior. The reformulation of radial MRI via the Fourier-slice theorem into a back-projection problem builds on Catalan et al. (2023) but targets a distinct goal—stabilizing optimization against k-space dynamic range rather than enabling direct neural-field optimization from raw k-space. The coarse-to-fine hash encoding strategy draws on BARF-style piecewise resolution scheduling (Lin et al. 2021) adapted to the MRI MoCo context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for any training data, Moner addresses a critical practical barrier in clinical MRI MoCo: the cost and availability of large, high-quality paired datasets, and the resulting out-of-domain fragility that plagues supervised methods like Score-MoCo (which degrades to below TV performance on unseen MoDL data at 4× acceleration). The 5–6× speed advantage over diffusion-based SOTA makes it more deployable in time-sensitive clinical workflows. Key limitations remain: the method is currently 2D (though a 3D extension is demonstrated), assumes rigid quasi-static motion, and is restricted to radial sampling patterns. Extending to non-rigid motion, 3D volumetric acquisition, and Cartesian/spiral trajectories are natural but non-trivial next steps.
