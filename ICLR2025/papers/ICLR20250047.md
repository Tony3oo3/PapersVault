limitations: The model is trained at 128×128 resolution and evaluated at 480p, leaving a gap in resolution generalization; video generation experiments are limited to unconditional generation on small datasets (SkyTimelapse, UCF-101) at 256×256, and the flow regularization requires on-the-fly RAFT inference during training, adding significant computational overhead.

surprise_factor: The ablation showing that purely learnable down/upsampling kernels significantly overfit to training sequence length (1.62 dB PSNR drop at T=32 vs. 0.38 dB for non-learnable) is a useful practical insight that challenges the assumption that learned kernels are always superior. Overall, the contributions are a well-executed combination of known ideas rather than a paradigm shift.

## Problem

Existing video autoencoders for generative modeling rely on pretrained image autoencoders with added 3D convolutions, which overlook temporal redundancy and produce temporally incoherent decodings (e.g., flickering). This limits the ability to encode longer-duration videos and exploit the well-documented temporal redundancy in video data. The paper addresses this by proposing a causal video VAE that jointly compresses images and videos in both spatial and temporal dimensions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250047/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts video clips of (1+T)×H×W×3 or single images, with the causal 3D convolution design ensuring the first frame is independent of subsequent frames, enabling seamless joint image and video compression. Trained on 128×128 crops with T∈{8,16} frames, it produces a continuous latent of dimension (1+t)×h×w×c at compression rates ranging from 4×8×8 (256×) to 16×16×16 (4096×) with channel sizes of 4, 8, or 16. The decoder reconstructs the input in pixel space, and the latent can be plugged into diffusion-based video generation frameworks.

## Method

The architecture is a causal 3D VAE built from Causal3DResBlocks, dual-path spatio-temporal down/upsampling blocks, and spatio-temporal attention blocks (spatial self-attention + causal temporal attention). Three key innovations distinguish it: (1) a FILM-style weight-shared encoder that processes an input pyramid (k=3 levels) and channel-concatenates features from different depths to preserve large-motion information lost at deeper pyramid levels; (2) a dual-path STDownBlock/STUpBlock that sums outputs of a strided causal 3D convolution (learnable) and 3D average pooling or nearest interpolation (non-learnable), achieving both high reconstruction quality and temporal adaptability across arbitrary sequence lengths; (3) a flow regularization loss computing bidirectional MSE between RAFT optical flows of input and decoded frames, enabling up to 16× temporal compression without significant quality loss. Training combines L1 reconstruction, frame-wise LPIPS, KL divergence, flow regularization, and a 3D PatchGAN discriminator.

## Results

On video autoencoding at 4×8×8 compression (channel size 4), the proposed causal video VAE achieves 32.12 dB PSNR and 0.107 LPIPS on Xiph-2K, and 30.31 dB PSNR and 0.159 LPIPS on DAVIS, outperforming all competing methods at the same compression rate. At 1×8×8 (no temporal compression), it reaches 34.08 dB PSNR on Xiph-2K, surpassing Video LDM (30.32 dB) and SVD (30.40 dB) by ~3.7 dB. For video generation via Open-Sora, the proposed VAE yields FVD₁₆ of 49.50 on SkyTimelapse and 248.7 on UCF-101, improving over Open-Sora's own VAE (55.12 / 283.4) and LDM (53.38 / 266.8) while providing 4× greater temporal compression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At the same 1×8×8 compression rate, the proposed VAE outperforms LDM (29.72→34.08 dB PSNR on Xiph-2K), Video LDM (30.32→34.08), SVD (30.40→34.08), and HVDM (31.16→34.08) by 2.8–4.4 dB PSNR. At 4×8×8, it beats Open-Sora (28.20→32.12), Open-Sora-Plan (29.33→32.12), CV-VAE (29.31→32.12), and OmniTokenizer (28.61→32.12) by 2.8–3.5 dB PSNR on Xiph-2K, and also surpasses CogVideoX (32.78→34.51) at channel size 16. Notably, at 8×8×8 (512× total compression), the model achieves 31.28 dB PSNR on Xiph-2K, exceeding VideoGPT's 31.04 dB at only 64× compression (4×4×4). For video generation, Ours+Open-Sora achieves FVD₁₆ of 49.50 on SkyTimelapse, outperforming DIGAN (83.11), StyleGAN-V (79.52), PVDM (71.46), and Open-Sora's own VAE (55.12), while also beating LDM+Open-Sora (53.38) despite 4× greater temporal compression. On UCF-101, the advantage is even larger: 248.7 vs. 283.4 (Open-Sora) and 266.8 (LDM+Open-Sora) FVD₁₆.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VAE-based video compression lineage (LDM, Video LDM, SVD) by replacing the common reliance on pretrained image autoencoders with a fully causal 3D convolutional architecture that jointly handles images and videos. It adapts the FILM weight-shared encoder from frame interpolation to video VAEs for large-motion robustness, and introduces a dual-path (learnable + non-learnable) spatio-temporal down/upsampling module to address the sequence-length overfitting observed in MAGVIT-v2's purely learnable kernels. A flow regularization loss using RAFT optical flow is a novel training objective not previously applied to video VAEs, complementing the standard L1 + LPIPS + KL + GAN training recipe inherited from LDM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstrated 16× temporal compression with minimal quality degradation, combined with robustness to arbitrary sequence lengths and latent corruption, makes this a practically important backbone for video generation pipelines that need to handle variable-length clips. The finding that a video-only-trained causal VAE matches a dedicated image VAE (LDM) on image compression validates the causal formulation for unified image/video tokenization. Open questions include scalability to higher resolutions (training was at 128×128, evaluation at 480p), the computational cost of on-the-fly RAFT inference during training, and whether the FILM encoder's multi-scale aggregation generalizes to even more aggressive compression rates beyond 16×16×16.
