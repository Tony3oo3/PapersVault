## Problem

Deep neural networks suffer from two intertwined limitations: they produce overconfident, deterministic predictions that fail to capture aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty, and their entangled, multifaceted neurons respond to multiple unrelated features, undermining interpretability. Existing uncertainty methods (ensembles, MC dropout) require multiple full-network forward passes at test time, while sparse linear decision layers that improve interpretability often degrade accuracy and lack theoretical grounding for their disentanglement claims.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-1k and Places-10) and produces three outputs: a class label, a calibrated uncertainty estimate in the form of a p-value (obtained by sampling 20 logits from the variational posterior and performing a two-sample t-test on the top-2 classes), and a K-dimensional non-negative factor score vector θ that serves as a disentangled, interpretable representation of the input. Unlike ensemble or dropout-based uncertainty, BNDL requires only a single forward pass through the backbone followed by lightweight sampling from the learned Weibull posterior.

## Method

BNDL replaces the final fully-connected + softmax layer of any DNN with a Bayesian non-negative factor analysis: the one-hot label y_j is generated from a categorical likelihood parameterized by the product of a per-sample gamma-distributed factor score θ_j and a global gamma-distributed factor loading matrix Φ. Because the exact posterior is intractable, a Weibull variational inference network approximates both posteriors — the local θ_j via a deep network outputting Weibull shape (Softplus) and scale (ReLU/exp) parameters, and the global Φ via randomly initialized matrices with the same parameterization. Training maximizes the ELBO (expected log-likelihood minus two KL terms) with standard SGD, and the gamma prior plus an adaptive ReLU(x−α) activation enforce sparsity without the performance degradation of L1/L2 regularization. The paper proves partial identifiability of the learned factors by showing BNDL satisfies the selective-window and sparsity conditions of Gillis & Rajkó (2023) for NMF, and demonstrates that in the limit of infinite Weibull shape the model reduces exactly to standard non-negative matrix factorization.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, ResNet-BNDL achieves 95.54% top-1 accuracy and 95.58% PAvPU, outperforming the ResNet baseline (94.98%) and all uncertainty baselines. On ImageNet-1k, ResNet-BNDL reaches 77.01% accuracy and 77.66% PAvPU, while ViT-BNDL achieves 81.29% accuracy and 82.50% PAvPU. For disentanglement, BNDL features score 2.59 on SEPIN@1 versus 1.50 for ResNet-50 on ImageNet-1k, confirming substantially more identifiable representations. The sparsity-accuracy tradeoff shows BNDL maintains 75.7% ImageNet accuracy even at 0.0024 non-sparse weight ratio, outperforming the Debuggable Network at equivalent sparsity levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the dense ResNet baseline (He et al., 2016), BNDL improves accuracy by 0.56% on CIFAR-10, 5.20% on CIFAR-100, and 1.68% on ImageNet-1k, while also providing uncertainty estimates the baseline lacks. Compared to MC Dropout (Gal et al., 2016), ResNet-BNDL gains 1.00% accuracy and 16.75% PAvPU on CIFAR-10, and 1.03% accuracy and 1.16% PAvPU on ImageNet-1k. Against BM (Joo et al., 2020), BNDL achieves 1.47% higher accuracy and 1.60% higher PAvPU on CIFAR-10, and 4.01% higher accuracy and 3.97% higher PAvPU on CIFAR-100. Versus CARD (Han et al., 2022), BNDL leads by 4.61% accuracy and 4.47% PAvPU on CIFAR-10, and 0.81% accuracy and 1.37% PAvPU on ImageNet-1k. Against ViT-Base (Dosovitskiy et al., 2021), ViT-BNDL improves accuracy by 0.83% on CIFAR-10, 1.01% on CIFAR-100, and 0.96% on ImageNet-1k. In the sparsity-accuracy tradeoff (Fig. 3), BNDL consistently outperforms the Debuggable Network (Wong et al., 2021) at matched sparsity levels across all four datasets, achieving up to two orders of magnitude sparser decision layers with minimal accuracy loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

BNDL synthesizes three previously separate research threads: Bayesian Last Layer (BLL) methods that place stochasticity only on the output layer for efficient uncertainty estimation (Ober & Rasmussen, 2019; Kristiadi et al., 2020; Daxberger et al., 2021), sparse linear decision layers for interpretability (Wong et al., 2021), and non-negative factor analysis for sparse concept disentanglement (Lee & Seung, 1999; Gillis & Rajkó, 2023). The key architectural departure is replacing the standard softmax with a Bayesian non-negative factor analysis parameterized by gamma-distributed latent variables, with a Weibull variational inference network (Zhang et al., 2018) providing a tractable reparameterization. This positions BNDL as a principled bridge between the shallow NFA paradigm and deep non-linear feature extractors, a connection motivated by prior evidence that the decision layer alone suffices for capturing uncertainty (Kristiadi et al., 2020; Dhuliawala et al., 2023b).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BNDL offers a single, lightweight module that simultaneously addresses two problems typically tackled by separate toolkits—calibrated uncertainty estimation and feature disentanglement—without requiring ensemble inference or full Bayesian network training. The theoretical partial-identifiability guarantee (via the selective window and sparsity conditions of Gillis & Rajkó, 2023) provides a rare formal grounding for the interpretability claims that prior sparse-layer methods lacked. Open questions include whether the selective window assumption holds reliably in highly overlapping class spaces, whether the Weibull-to-gamma approximation degrades in high-dimensional latent spaces, and whether the approach extends naturally to regression or multi-label settings beyond single-label classification.
