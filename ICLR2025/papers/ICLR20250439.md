## Problem

When adapting CLIP to domain-specific tasks with limited image-text pairs, the model struggles to bridge the domain gap between pre-training and target tasks. Existing semi-supervised CLIP methods like S-CLIP can only exploit relational information within a single mini-batch to construct pseudo-labels, failing to leverage the full labeled dataset and yielding unsatisfactory performance on large-scale or diverse benchmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250439/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of labeled image-text pairs (default 10% of training data) alongside a large pool of unlabeled RGB images, plus class-name templates for zero-shot evaluation. During training, NLTK extracts noun concepts from labeled captions, a linear classifier predicts top-k concepts per unlabeled image, and learnable prompts assemble these into surrogate captions fed through the CLIP text encoder. Outputs are aligned image-text embeddings used for zero-shot classification (highest-similarity class template) and bidirectional image-text retrieval (R@k rankings).

## Method

SEMICLIP follows a two-stage 'supervised pre-training, semi-supervised fine-tuning' paradigm. In pre-training, a Semantic Concepts Mining module extracts frequency-filtered noun concepts from labeled captions, initializes a linear classifier ψ with CLIP text-encoder features of concept templates, and jointly optimizes the CLIP contrastive loss with a soft cross-entropy concept-matching loss. In fine-tuning, two consistency regularizers exploit unlabeled images: (1) concept-level semantic consistency applies FixMatch-style strong/weak augmentation agreement on ψ's concept predictions, and (2) caption-level trapezoidal consistency constructs surrogate captions from top-k predicted concepts via learnable prompt vectors in a [V1][V2][V3][Concept] template, then enforces a geometric constraint where labeled pairs form the upper base of a trapezoid and unlabeled image–surrogate-caption pairs form the lower base, with equal-length legs (intra-modal similarity consistency) and equal-length diagonals (cross-modal similarity consistency) but no fixed lower-base length, thereby avoiding direct alignment to noisy surrogate captions while still promoting structured interaction across both modalities.

## Results

On remote sensing zero-shot classification (L=U setting), SEMICLIP achieves 83.8% on RSICD-CLS, 85.6% on UCM-CLS, 96.6% on WHU-RS19, 75.3% on RSSCN7, and 87.4% on AID, outperforming S-CLIP by an average of 1.72% (L=U) and 2.94% (L≠U). On fashion datasets, SEMICLIP achieves 44.7% on Fashion200k sub-class and 47.6% on FashionGen sub-class, surpassing S-CLIP by an average of 12.15% on sub-class. For image-text retrieval, SEMICLIP gains 4.48% over fine-tuned CLIP on remote sensing R@5 and 6.35% over S-CLIP on fashion R@5. Overall, SEMICLIP improves zero-shot classification by 1.72%–6.58% and image-text retrieval by 2.32%–3.23% over supervised fine-tuned CLIP across all benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEMICLIP consistently outperforms all four baselines across nearly all datasets and settings. Against S-CLIP (the closest competitor), SEMICLIP gains 1.72% on average for remote sensing zero-shot (L=U) and 2.94% for the harder L≠U domain-shift setting, with the largest single-dataset margin being +4.4% on WHU-RS19. On fashion sub-class, the advantage over S-CLIP is dramatic: +6.3% on Fashion200k (44.7% vs. 38.4%) and +18.0% on FashionGen (47.6% vs. 29.6%), which the authors attribute to S-CLIP's inability to find suitable neighbors within a single mini-batch for large, diverse datasets. Hard-PL and Soft-PL (nearest-neighbor pseudo-labeling baselines) perform substantially worse, with Hard-PL even degrading below fine-tuned CLIP on several fashion sub-class settings (e.g., 23.9% vs. 35.1% on Fashion200k). Notably, SEMICLIP occasionally surpasses the fully supervised oracle in zero-shot classification (e.g., 85.6% vs. 67.9% on UCM-CLS), which the authors explain as a consequence of sub-dataset imbalance in the training mixture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the semi-supervised CLIP adaptation paradigm introduced by S-CLIP (Mo et al., 2023), which reconstructs pseudo-captions for unlabeled images via batch-level nearest-neighbor caption combination. SEMICLIP departs from this by mining a global concept vocabulary from all labeled captions and training a linear classifier to predict concepts for any unlabeled image, decoupling pseudo-label generation from mini-batch composition. The trapezoidal consistency draws on the cross-modal symmetry constraints of CyCLIP (Goel et al., 2022) but repurposes them for a semi-supervised setting where one vertex of the quadrilateral is a noisy surrogate caption, and the lower base is deliberately left unconstrained. The concept-level semantic consistency directly adapts FixMatch's strong/weak augmentation consistency to the vision-language domain, with the justification that noun-level concepts are robust to augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The trapezoidal consistency offers a principled geometric mechanism for incorporating noisy pseudo-labels into contrastive training without forcing direct alignment, which is a broadly applicable insight for any setting where surrogate labels are imperfect. The concept mining approach scales better than batch-level methods and is less sensitive to batch size, making it more practical for resource-constrained settings. However, the approach is limited to noun-level concepts extracted via NLTK, which may miss relational or attribute-level semantics; the paper also acknowledges that retrieval performance still benefits substantially from more labeled data (oracle outperforms SEMICLIP on retrieval). Open questions include whether the geometric constraints generalize to other multi-modal architectures beyond CLIP-style dual encoders, and whether richer concept representations (beyond flat noun lists) could further close the gap to fully supervised performance.
