## Problem

Existing NODE training methods rely on numerical ODE solvers that require evaluating the DNN-based vector field at many additional points beyond the observed data, making training 10–1000× slower than necessary. The autoregressive nature of these solvers also causes error accumulation that degrades prediction accuracy, and the adjoint-based backward pass introduces further discretization error. Prior acceleration techniques (regularization, architecture design, modified training loops) still depend on ODE solvers and fail to eliminate this fundamental bottleneck.

## Input / Output

The method consumes irregularly sampled trajectories {(t_k, x(t_k))} where observations may contain Gaussian noise and missing entries (denoted by *), with no requirement for uniform spacing. The DNN vector field is evaluated only at the K observed time points per trajectory (exactly M evaluations), in contrast to ODE-solver-based methods that require >>M×J evaluations. At inference, the trained NODE is integrated forward with a standard ODE solver (e.g., Dopri5) to produce interpolated predictions within [0,T] and extrapolated predictions beyond T.

## Method

VF-NODE replaces the ODE-solver-based training loop with a variational formulation (VF) loss that enforces the ODE constraint through a series of global integrals against a sine Fourier basis {φ_ℓ(t)=√(2/T)sin(πℓt/T)}. Because these integrals are computed over the entire time domain simultaneously, the DNN vector field is evaluated only at the observed data points and in parallel, reducing NFEs from >>M×J to exactly M and eliminating autoregressive error accumulation. To handle the oscillatory integrals introduced by the Fourier basis, Filon's method is used, which approximates only the non-oscillatory component h(t) with a polynomial while integrating the sine/cosine factor analytically. Natural cubic spline regression (with smoothing parameter λ) constructs closed-form cubic polynomial approximations of both the noisy trajectory and the vector field values, simultaneously denoising the data and filling in missing entries. Theorem 2 guarantees that minimizing the VF loss over an increasing number of basis functions converges to the L² distance between the learned and true vector fields, providing a principled training objective without any ODE solver in the loop.

## Results

On four dynamical systems (Glycolytic Oscillator, Genetic Toggle Switch, Repressilator, Age-structured SIR), VF-NODE achieves the best or near-best interpolation MSE across all systems: 6.35e-05 (Glycolytic), 1.69e-04 (Toggle), 1.92e-02 (Repressilator), and 7.39e-03 (AgeSIR) at 80% observed data. On extrapolation, VF-NODE achieves 1.63e-04, 4.79e-07, 1.23e-01, and 2.37e-02 respectively. Training is accelerated by 10 to 1000× per epoch compared to ODE-solver-based baselines (Dis-Opt, Opt-Dis, Seminorm), while maintaining higher or comparable prediction accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VF-NODE outperforms all NODE-based baselines on interpolation for Glycolytic (6.35e-05 vs. ODE-RNN's 8.80e-05, the closest competitor), Toggle (1.69e-04 vs. Vanilla NODE's 8.00e-04), and Repressilator (1.92e-02 vs. Vanilla NODE's 2.25e-02). On AgeSIR, VF-NODE (7.39e-03) is essentially tied with Vanilla NODE (7.54e-03), both far outperforming ODE-RNN (6.75e+00) and NCDE (1.22e+01). On extrapolation, VF-NODE wins on Toggle (4.79e-07 vs. Vanilla NODE's 8.14e-07) and Repressilator (1.23e-01 vs. 1.25e-01), but is slightly outperformed by ODE-RNN on Glycolytic (4.68e-05 vs. 1.63e-04) and by Vanilla NODE on AgeSIR (1.99e-02 vs. 2.37e-02). Neural Flows (ResNet/GRU) are consistently the weakest baselines, with MSEs orders of magnitude higher. In terms of training speed, VF-NODE is 10–1000× faster per epoch than Dis-Opt, Opt-Dis, and Seminorm, as the VF loss requires only M vector-field evaluations (at observed points) versus ≫M×J evaluations for ODE-solver-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the variational formulation (VF) of ODEs—previously used for parameter estimation of known ODEs (Brunel et al., 2014) and symbolic regression (D-CODE, Qian et al., 2022)—as a training loss for Neural ODEs, replacing the numerical ODE solver entirely. The key departure from all prior NODE training methods (discretize-then-optimize, adjoint/optimise-then-discretize, seminorm, Taylor-based) is that VF-NODEs compute a series of global integrals over the trajectory rather than stepping through the ODE iteratively, reducing function evaluations from ≫M×J to exactly M. Two technical gaps left open by D-CODE are addressed: oscillatory integrals arising from the Fourier basis (handled via Filon's method) and noisy/incomplete observations (handled via natural cubic spline regression).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10–1000× training speedup, combined with equal or better accuracy, makes NODEs far more practical for iterative model development and large-scale dynamical-systems applications. The elimination of autoregressive stepping also removes a known source of error accumulation, which is particularly relevant for long-horizon extrapolation. Limitations remain: the method relies on polynomial (cubic spline) approximation, which may degrade for chaotic systems or extremely sparse sampling; it requires trajectory points to be available (restricting it to time-series tasks); and the standard NODE architecture cannot natively handle time-varying parameters without external segmentation. Open questions include whether the VF-loss paradigm extends to PDEs, stochastic differential equations, or very high-dimensional state spaces where the number of basis functions L becomes prohibitive.
