## Problem

Bounding box regression in DETR-based detectors treats edges as fixed coordinates (Dirac delta distributions), which limits uncertainty modeling and makes optimization sensitive to small coordinate shifts, leading to slow convergence and suboptimal localization. Additionally, existing knowledge distillation methods for detection are either ineffective (logit/feature imitation) or incur substantial training overhead and are incompatible with anchor-free architectures, leaving a gap in how to efficiently transfer localization knowledge within a single model.

## Input / Output

The detector takes standard RGB images (resized to 640×640 during pretraining) and produces 300 object predictions end-to-end without NMS, each consisting of a bounding box (x, y, w, h) and a class label. Rather than regressing fixed coordinates directly, the model outputs probability distributions over 32 discrete bins for each of the four edge offsets (top, bottom, left, right), which are then converted to pixel-space offsets scaled by the predicted box dimensions. The architecture uses an HGNetV2 CNN backbone with a transformer decoder of 3–6 layers depending on model size (S/M/L/X).

## Method

D-FINE introduces two core components. Fine-grained Distribution Refinement (FDR) replaces fixed-coordinate regression with iterative residual refinement of probability distributions: the first decoder layer predicts initial boxes, and subsequent layers predict residual logits that are added to previous logits and passed through softmax to produce refined edge-offset distributions, weighted by a non-uniform function W(n) that enables fine adjustments near accurate predictions while allowing large corrections when far off. Global Optimal Localization Self-Distillation (GO-LSD) distills the final layer's refined distributions into shallower layers via KL divergence, using a union set of Hungarian-matched predictions across all layers for global optimization and a Decoupled Distillation Focal (DDF) loss that decouples IoU-based weighting for matched predictions from confidence-based weighting for unmatched ones. A new Fine-Grained Localization (FGL) loss, inspired by DFL, applies IoU-weighted cross-entropy to the distributions to encourage concentration. The architecture further incorporates lightweight modifications (removing decoder projection layers, adding a Target Gating Layer, replacing CSP with GELAN blocks, and uneven multi-scale sampling) to reduce latency and FLOPs while FDR and GO-LSD recover the resulting accuracy loss.

## Results

On COCO val2017, D-FINE-L achieves 54.0% AP with 31M parameters, 91 GFLOPs, and 8.07 ms latency (124 FPS on NVIDIA T4), while D-FINE-X reaches 55.8% AP with 62M parameters, 202 GFLOPs, and 12.89 ms latency (78 FPS). After pretraining on Objects365, D-FINE-L and D-FINE-X attain 57.1% and 59.3% AP respectively, surpassing all existing real-time detectors. On CrowdHuman, D-FINE-S achieves 55.5% AP with only 10M parameters and 3.49 ms latency, outperforming RT-DETRv2-S (54.2%) and YOLOv10-S (50.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

D-FINE-L (54.0% AP) outperforms all end-to-end real-time detectors: YOLOv10-L (53.2%), RT-DETR-R50 (53.1%), RT-DETR-HG-L (53.0%), RT-DETRv2-L (53.4%), and LW-DETR-X (53.0%), while using fewer GFLOPs (91 vs. 120–234) and lower latency (8.07 ms vs. 8.21–14.01 ms). D-FINE-X (55.8% AP) surpasses YOLOv10-X (54.4%), RT-DETR-R101 (54.3%), RT-DETR-HG-X (54.8%), RT-DETRv2-X (54.3%), and YOLO11-X (54.7%), and even edges out the non-end-to-end YOLOv9-E (55.6%) with fewer parameters (62M vs. 57M) and lower latency. With Objects365 pretraining, D-FINE-X (59.3%) leads all real-time detectors by a clear margin over LW-DETR-X (58.3%) and RT-DETR-R101 (56.2%), requiring only 21 pretraining epochs versus 300 for YOLOv10. On CrowdHuman, D-FINE-S (55.5% AP) beats RT-DETRv2-S (54.2%) and YOLOv10-S (50.6%) with fewer parameters (10M vs. 20M/7M) and lower latency (3.49 ms vs. 4.59/2.65 ms). As a plug-in module, FDR+GO-LSD improves Deformable-DETR by +3.4 AP, DAB-DETR by +5.3 AP, DN-DETR by +3.7 AP, and DINO by +2.6 AP (12 epochs) or +2.0 AP (24 epochs), with no additional parameters or computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

D-FINE builds directly on RT-DETR's real-time DETR architecture, streamlining its computationally heavy modules while introducing two novel components. Fine-grained Distribution Refinement (FDR) extends GFocal's distribution-based regression from an anchor-based, single-shot formulation to an anchor-free, iteratively refined residual process, addressing GFocal's limitations of anchor dependency and coarse localization. GO-LSD adapts Localization Distillation (Zheng et al., 2022) from a teacher-student paradigm to a self-distillation setting, transferring the final decoder layer's refined distributions back to shallower layers via KL divergence with decoupled weighting. The overall framework operates within the DETR family's Hungarian matching and end-to-end prediction paradigm (Carion et al., 2020), positioning D-FINE as a regression-formulation and distillation-strategy upgrade rather than an architectural overhaul.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

D-FINE's FDR and GO-LSD modules are plug-and-play, improving diverse DETR variants (Deformable DETR, DAB-DETR, DN-DETR, DINO) by 2.0–5.3% AP with negligible extra parameters, making them broadly applicable beyond the D-FINE architecture itself. The paper confirms and quantifies that DETR-based detectors benefit substantially more from large-scale pretraining than YOLO variants (21 epochs vs. 300 epochs for comparable gains), reinforcing the strategic value of the DETR route for real-time detection. A key limitation acknowledged is that lighter models (fewer decoder layers) show smaller gains, since shallow final-layer predictions provide weaker distillation targets; the authors suggest training with extra decoder layers and discarding them at inference as a future direction. Open questions include whether the distribution-refinement paradigm can be extended to other regression tasks (e.g., keypoint detection, segmentation) and whether the self-distillation strategy generalizes to non-DETR architectures.
