## Problem

Even state-of-the-art multimodal models like GPT-4o exhibit significant performance drops on ambiguous natural language instructions (ellipsis, colloquialism, subjectivity, relativity) that are common in real-world interaction. Existing chain-of-thought methods require high model intelligence to process long reasoning chains, making them inapplicable to general-intelligent models like LLaVA or LISA, and no dedicated benchmark or framework exists for handling multimodal ambiguity across tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252341/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes an RGB image and an ambiguous text instruction as input and produces task-specific outputs: a segmentation mask for referring image segmentation or a free-form text answer for VQA. For general-intelligent models, the ambiguous instruction is first transformed into a clear instruction via a learned disambiguation prompt, then both the original and clear instructions are jointly fed to the task model for final answer synthesis. The method operates as a plug-in wrapper around existing task models without modifying their parameters, adding only ~0.14s inference overhead for LLaVA.

## Method

VISUAL-O1 introduces an iterative reasoning-reflection loop where the model alternates between reasoning about the ambiguous instruction given visual context and reflecting on its own reasoning to update a disambiguation experience prompt. For high-intelligent models, this produces 'instantial experience' during inference: all reasoning and reflection steps are concatenated into a growing context (Eq. 5), and the final answer is synthesized from the complete reasoning trace. For general-intelligent models, a one-time optimization phase builds 'empirical experience' by iterating across multiple samples, retaining only the latest reflection as the updated prompt (Eq. 6) to ensure generalizability; at inference, this prompt transforms the ambiguous instruction into a clear one, which is then combined with the original instruction for answer synthesis. The key design choice is that the empirical experience is learned once per task and reused, avoiding per-sample complex reasoning that would overwhelm weaker models.

## Results

On the ambiguous VQA dataset (VizWiz), VISUAL-O1(GPT-4o) achieves 22.78% accuracy and 0.6640 BLEU-1, outperforming GPT-4o alone (14.10% ACC) by 61% relatively, while VISUAL-O1(LLaVA) reaches 22.13% ACC versus LLaVA's 8.58% (a 158% relative gain). On the ambiguous RIS dataset (RefCOCO+), VISUAL-O1(SOM) achieves 0.1304 gIOU / 0.1756 cIOU versus SOM's 0.0752 / 0.1158, and VISUAL-O1(LISA) reaches 0.1131 / 0.1215 versus LISA's 0.0237 / 0.0272. On general (non-ambiguous) instructions, VISUAL-O1 also improves performance: 63.14% ACC for GPT-4o (vs. 61.25% baseline) and 57.58% ACC for LLaVA (vs. 54.19%), while traditional CoT and FUDD actually degrade general performance (e.g., CoT drops GPT-4o to 39.78% ACC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VISUAL-O1 consistently outperforms all 12 baselines across tasks. On ambiguous VQA, it beats FUDD (19.18% ACC) by 3.6 points and CoT (15.03%) by 7.7 points with GPT-4o, while also surpassing the raw GPT-4o baseline (14.10%) by 8.7 points. On ambiguous RIS, VISUAL-O1(SOM) achieves 0.1304 gIOU versus SOM (0.0752), CoT (0.0746), and FUDD (0.0718), and VISUAL-O1(LISA) reaches 0.1131 gIOU versus LISA's 0.0237. On general RIS (Table 4), VISUAL-O1 improves over OVSeg (0.0418→0.3686 gIOU with SOM), Ref-Diff (0.3301→0.3686), Unified-IO (cIOU 0.4015→0.4508), InstructDiffusion (cIOU 0.3904→0.4508), Qwen2-VL (0.4417→0.5161 gIOU), and GPT-4o (0.5728→0.5777 gIOU). Notably, traditional CoT and FUDD degrade general-instruction performance (e.g., CoT drops LLaVA from 54.19% to 39.78% ACC on general VQA), whereas VISUAL-O1 maintains or improves general performance while fixing ambiguity. On VLN (Room-to-Room), VISUAL-O1(VLN-SIG) achieves 26.42% SR versus VLN-SIG's 4.72%, a 5.6× improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends chain-of-thought reasoning (Wei et al., 2022) from single-turn text-based CoT to a multi-modal, multi-turn reasoning-reflection loop that iteratively refines disambiguation experience. It departs from prior ambiguity-handling methods (FUDD, WAFFLECLIP, REPHRASE) that required extensive predefined interactions or were task-specific, by proposing a unified framework that adapts to model intelligence level: instantial experience accumulated during inference for high-intelligent models, or empirical experience from one-time optimization for general-intelligent models. The key conceptual departure from standard CoT is that the reasoning process is not a single forward pass but an iterative loop where reflection updates a disambiguation prompt, and the final answer is synthesized from this accumulated experience rather than from the raw reasoning trace.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals a striking vulnerability: even GPT-4o drops from 61.25% to 14.10% accuracy when instructions shift from general to ambiguous, suggesting that current multi-modal models lack robust disambiguation capabilities that humans take for granted. The practical significance is that VISUAL-O1 enables general-intelligent models (LLaVA, LISA) to approach or exceed high-intelligent model performance on ambiguous instructions without additional models at inference time, with minimal computational overhead. However, the absolute performance remains low (22.13% ACC for LLaVA+VISUAL-O1 on ambiguous VQA), and the one-time optimization still requires a high-intelligent model, limiting deployment in resource-constrained settings. Open questions include whether the empirical experience can be learned without any high-intelligent model, whether the approach scales to more complex multi-step ambiguous instructions, and whether the overfitting-like degradation at higher budgets can be systematically addressed.
