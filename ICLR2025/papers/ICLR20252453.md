## Problem

Existing zero-cost proxies for neural architecture search are often restricted to ReLU activations, require output labels, or fail to consistently outperform the trivial parameter-count baseline across diverse search spaces. Moreover, no prior proxy has been applied to selecting activation functions or weight initialization schemes, and most require a predefined architecture search space, limiting their use in domains like machine learning potentials where no such space exists.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only an untrained network architecture and a set of unlabeled sample inputs (32 random samples for NAS benchmarks, 400 for molecular datasets), performing a single forward pass per sample. For CNNs, the 3D feature map tensors are flattened and a random contiguous submatrix of C' rows is extracted to keep the effective-rank computation tractable. The output is a single scalar NEAR score per architecture, which can additionally be fed into a power-law fit to predict optimal per-layer neuron counts or compared across activation/initialization combinations to rank hyperparameter choices.

## Method

NEAR computes the effective rank (exponential of the Shannon entropy of normalized singular values) of both the pre-activation matrix Z_l and post-activation matrix H_l for every layer l, then sums these values across all layers to produce a single expressivity score. The key design choice is relaxing the classical binary activation-pattern definition (which is ReLU-specific) to the continuous post-activation vector h_l = sigma(z_l), making the proxy applicable to any activation function. For convolutional layers, feature maps are flattened into a matrix and a random contiguous row submatrix is used as a computationally efficient proxy for the full matrix, preserving sensitivity to kernel size and channel count. For layer-size estimation, the relative NEAR score (score divided by neuron count) is fitted to a power function f(s) = alpha + beta*s^gamma, and the optimal size is taken where the fitted slope drops below a user-defined threshold (0.5% of the slope at s=1 in the paper's experiments).

## Results

NEAR achieves the best average rank (1.71) across all three NAS search spaces and seven dataset combinations, outperforming the second-best proxy MeCo_opt (2.57) and ZiCo (3.57). On NAS-Bench-101, NEAR attains the highest Spearman's ρ of 0.70 (Kendall's τ = 0.52), substantially exceeding the #Params baseline (ρ = 0.43) and MeCo_opt (ρ = 0.49). On NATS-Bench-TSS, NEAR reaches ρ = 0.88/0.87/0.84 on CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100/IN16-120, and on NATS-Bench-SSS it reaches ρ = 0.91/0.82/0.92. NEAR is one of only two proxies (with ZiCo) that consistently surpasses the #Params baseline on every search-space/dataset combination, and it outperforms ZiCo in every individual case.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MeCo_opt (Jiang et al., 2023), NEAR wins decisively on NAS-Bench-101 (ρ = 0.70 vs. 0.49) and NATS-Bench-SSS CIFAR-100 (ρ = 0.82 vs. 0.81), but trails slightly on NATS-Bench-TSS CIFAR-10 (ρ = 0.88 vs. 0.90) and CIFAR-100 (ρ = 0.87 vs. 0.90). Against SynFlow (Abdelfattah et al., 2021), NEAR dominates on NAS-Bench-101 (ρ = 0.70 vs. 0.37) and NATS-Bench-SSS CIFAR-100 (ρ = 0.82 vs. 0.78), but SynFlow edges ahead on NATS-Bench-SSS CIFAR-10 (ρ = 0.94 vs. 0.91) and IN16-120 (ρ = 0.95 vs. 0.92). Against ZiCo (Li et al., 2023), NEAR outperforms on every individual benchmark/dataset pair, most notably on NAS-Bench-101 (ρ = 0.70 vs. 0.63) and NATS-Bench-TSS CIFAR-10 (ρ = 0.88 vs. 0.78). Against swap/reg_swap (Peng et al., 2024), NEAR is far superior on NAS-Bench-101 (ρ = 0.70 vs. 0.43/0.43) and NATS-Bench-SSS (ρ = 0.91/0.82/0.92 vs. 0.67/0.34/0.60 for swap), while reg_swap is competitive on NATS-Bench-TSS (ρ = 0.88 vs. 0.88 on CIFAR-10). Against Zen-Score (Lin et al., 2021), NEAR wins on NATS-Bench-TSS (ρ ≈ 0.88 vs. 0.43) and NAS-Bench-101 (ρ = 0.70 vs. 0.64), though Zen-Score is slightly ahead on NATS-Bench-SSS CIFAR-10 (ρ = 0.92 vs. 0.91). Overall, NEAR is the only proxy that is either best or second-best on every search space, and it is the only one that consistently beats the trivial #Params baseline across all seven dataset/search-space combinations.

## Relation to Prior Work

NEAR builds on the effective rank concept from signal processing (Roy & Vetterli, 2007) and repurposes it as a measure of neural network expressivity by computing it on pre- and post-activation matrices. It departs from prior zero-cost NAS proxies (NASWOT, Zen-Score, swap, reg_swap, TE-NAS) by relaxing the activation pattern definition from binary ReLU-specific indicators to continuous post-activation values, thereby removing the ReLU restriction that limits all those methods. It also extends the zero-cost proxy paradigm beyond architecture ranking to two new applications: estimating optimal layer sizes without a predefined search space and selecting activation functions and weight initialization schemes—tasks for which no prior zero-cost proxy has been applied.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NEAR is the only zero-cost proxy that consistently outperforms the trivial #Params baseline across every combination of search space and dataset tested, and it achieves the best average rank (1.71) among 13 proxies. Its practical value is amplified by requiring only unlabeled input samples, working with any activation function, and not needing a predefined architecture search space—making it applicable to domains like machine learning potentials where such search spaces do not exist. Open questions include whether the power-law fitting heuristic for layer size generalizes beyond the tested architectures, whether NEAR can resolve fine-grained differences between closely performing activation functions, and whether the expressivity-only signal (ignoring training dynamics) limits its predictive power for very deep or irregularly structured networks.
