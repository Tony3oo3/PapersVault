## Problem

LLM-based biomedical QA systems suffer from hallucination and lack of grounded verification, while RAG-based approaches depend on retrieval quality without post-retrieval fact-checking. Existing KG-based methods either retrieve only direct edges (missing implicit relationships) or operate end-to-end on fixed graphs that cannot handle unseen entities. The paper addresses the need for a system that generates knowledge from LLMs, verifies it against a structured KG, and iteratively refines errors before producing an answer.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a biomedical question stem and, for multi-choice settings, a set of candidate answers (typically 2–4 options); for open-ended settings, only the stem is provided. A biomedical knowledge graph (PrimeKG or OGB-biokg) supplies structural embeddings used exclusively in the verification step, not as a retrieval source. The output is either a selected option letter or a free-text answer, with the intermediate artifact being a set of verified (head, relation, tail) triplets that ground the final response.

## Method

KGAREVION is a four-action LLM agent: Generate prompts the LLM to produce 1–3 undirected triplets per answer candidate (or per question stem for yes/no questions); Review uses a LoRA-fine-tuned LLM that has been trained on KG completion with TransE structural embeddings aligned into the LLM's token space via an attention block and two-layer FFN, prepended as prefix tokens to the instruction; Revise iteratively corrects triplets flagged as False (up to k rounds); and Answer prompts the LLM to produce the final response from the verified triplet set. A soft-constraint rule in Review distinguishes factually wrong triplets (both entities mappable to the KG and judged False) from incomplete-knowledge cases (an entity unmappable), retaining the latter to avoid over-pruning. The agent adaptively switches between choice-aware and non-choice-aware generation strategies, and the KG is used only for verification rather than retrieval, making the system robust to KG incompleteness.

## Results

On four established medical QA benchmarks (MMLU-Med, MedQA-US, PubMedQA, BioASQ-Y/N), KGAREVION with LLaMA3.1-8B (k=1) achieves 73.4%, 61.8%, 61.9%, and 76.3% accuracy respectively, improving over the best baseline by 5.3%, 5.7%, 3.8%, and 4.4%. On the three newly curated MedDDx datasets (Basic, Intermediate, Expert), KGAREVION (LLaMA3.1, k=1) reaches 48.3%, 45.7%, and 40.9% accuracy, with improvements of 4.4%, 4.4%, and 5.6% over the best baseline. In the open-ended reasoning setting, KGAREVION (LLaMA3, k=2) achieves the largest gains, improving over the best baseline by up to 18.4% on MedDDx-Basic and 18.0% on MedQA-US. On AfriMed-QA (zero-shot, African healthcare), KGAREVION improves accuracy by 5.2% (LLaMA3.1-8B) and 4.6% (GPT-4-Turbo) over the respective backbone LLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KGAREVION outperforms all 15 baselines across all seven datasets in the multi-choice setting. Against the strongest LLM baseline (LLaMA3.1-8B CoT), KGAREVION (LLaMA3.1, k=1) gains +5.3% on MMLU-Med (73.4% vs. 68.1%), +5.7% on MedQA-US (61.8% vs. 54.9%), and +5.6% on MedDDx-Expert (40.9% vs. 32.2%). Among RAG-based methods, KGAREVION surpasses MedRAG (70B) by +15.5% on MMLU-Med and +13.2% on MedDDx-Basic, and outperforms KG-RAG (8B) by +21.8% on MedQA-US. KG-based end-to-end models (QAGNN, JointLK, Dragon) are consistently the weakest, with KGAREVION exceeding them by 20–40+ percentage points on most datasets. In the open-ended setting, the advantage widens substantially: KGAREVION (LLaMA3, k=2) improves over the best baseline by +18.0% on MedQA-US and +18.4% on MedDDx-Basic, indicating that the verification pipeline is especially critical when no candidate answers are provided. On the zero-shot AfriMed-QA benchmark, KGAREVION beats GPT-4-Turbo by 4.6% and LLaMA3.1-8B by 5.2% on expert-level questions, confirming generalization to underrepresented medical contexts.

## Relation to Prior Work

KGAREVION sits at the intersection of three research threads: LLM-based reasoning (CoT, MedAlpaca), retrieval-augmented generation (Self-RAG, KG-RAG, MedRAG), and knowledge-graph-based QA (QAGNN, JointLK, Dragon). It departs from RAG models by inverting the knowledge flow—rather than retrieving from a KG and feeding it to the LLM, it first generates triplets from the LLM's latent knowledge and then verifies them against a KG, using the KG as a validator rather than a primary source. The fine-tuning of the LLM on a KG completion task with TransE structural embeddings as prefix tokens (via LoRA) is a novel integration that bridges graph representation learning and LLM inference. The iterative Generate–Review–Revise–Answer agent loop draws on recent agentic frameworks (AutoGen, CAMEL) but applies them specifically to biomedical triplet verification, a use case not previously explored in this form.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete gap in medical AI: RAG-based systems lack post-retrieval verification, and pure KG models cannot handle unseen or incomplete entities. By grounding LLM-generated knowledge in KG verification, KGAREVION offers a practical path toward more reliable medical QA, and the MedDDx benchmark (1,769 semantically complex differential-diagnosis questions) fills a notable evaluation gap for distinguishing near-synonymous biomedical entities. The finding that KGAREVION is substantially more robust to answer reordering and relabeling than pure LLMs (reducing accuracy shifts from 8–16% to 2.5–3.9%) has direct implications for clinical deployment where question formats vary. Open questions include scalability to much larger or multi-hop KGs, the reliance on UMLS mapping for entity alignment, and whether the verification-based approach generalizes beyond the two KGs tested (PrimeKG, OGB-biokg) to truly novel or rapidly evolving biomedical ontologies.
