## Problem

Reconstructing an empty street from in-car camera videos is challenging because temporarily static objects (stopped vehicles, pedestrians) are observed from limited angles over brief periods along long trajectories, and no ground-truth 'empty street' data exists for training. Unlike object-centric 3D inpainting on small scenes with thorough multi-view coverage, street scenes involve camera-centric movement where objects appear and disappear at different time points, making consistent multi-view inpainting and mask generation significantly harder.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250434/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes front-view in-car camera video sequences (Waymo at 484×320, Pandaset at 480×270) and corresponding Lidar point clouds for 2DGS initialization. It produces a hard-label semantic 2D Gaussian Splatting representation of the empty street supporting real-time free-viewpoint rendering, from which a colored mesh can be extracted via TSDF fusion with Open3D. Semantic labels come from a pre-trained SegFormer, and the inpainting backbone is the diffusion-based LeftRefill model.

## Method

StreetUnveiler represents the street as a hard-label semantic 2DGS where each Gaussian carries a non-trainable one-hot semantic label, augmented with a semantic depth distortion loss (enforcing depth consistency within each semantic class) and a shrinking loss (penalizing opacity to eliminate unobserved Gaussians). After removing object Gaussians by semantic label, the method renders an alpha map to automatically identify completely unobservable regions (alpha < 0.99) as the inpainting mask, avoiding the blurring caused by naive object masks. A novel time-reversal inpainting framework then processes frames in reverse chronological order, using later (nearer) frames as references to inpaint earlier (farther) frames via LeftRefill, exploiting the high-to-low-resolution nature of the reversed trajectory for better pixel matching and temporal consistency. Finally, the inpainted pixels serve as pseudo-labels to re-optimize the 2DGS with L1, depth distortion, and normal consistency losses restricted to Gaussians spatially near the removed objects.

## Results

On the Waymo dataset, StreetUnveiler achieves the best LPIPS of 0.216 and FID of 127.581 among all temporally consistent methods, outperforming the closest 3D inpainting baseline SPIn-NeRF (0.221 LPIPS, 140.831 FID) and the video inpainting baseline ProPainter (0.233 LPIPS, 141.906 FID). On Pandaset, the method achieves 0.261 LPIPS and 155.527 FID, again leading all baselines. The only method with a lower FID on Waymo is SDXL (116.634), but SDXL does not maintain temporal consistency across video frames, making it unsuitable for 3D re-optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StreetUnveiler outperforms all single-image inpainting baselines (LaMa, SDXL) on LPIPS for both datasets, and outperforms both on FID for Pandaset; SDXL achieves a lower FID on Waymo (116.6 vs. 127.6) but fails to maintain temporal consistency across frames, which is critical for 3D re-optimization. Against the video inpainting baseline ProPainter, the method wins on both LPIPS and FID across both datasets (e.g., 0.216 vs. 0.233 LPIPS on Waymo). Among 3D inpainting methods, StreetUnveiler substantially outperforms Infusion (0.216 vs. 0.307 LPIPS on Waymo) and the re-implemented SPIn-NeRF (0.216 vs. 0.221 LPIPS, 127.6 vs. 140.8 FID on Waymo). In the direct comparison with LeftRefill (Table 4), the full time-reversal pipeline with 2DGS re-optimization improves over naive reverse-order LeftRefill inpainting by 0.011 LPIPS and 7.8 FID on Waymo. For geometry evaluation against StreetSurf on the 'before unveiled' scene, the method achieves a slightly higher Chamfer Distance (0.55 vs. 0.52) but a notably higher F-Score (61.54 vs. 56.70), while using only Lidar input without the monocular geometry prior that StreetSurf relies on.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D inpainting line of work (SPIn-NeRF, Infusion, NerFiller) from small, object-centric scenes to long-trajectory street scenes captured by in-car cameras. It builds on 2D Gaussian Splatting as the scene representation, augmenting it with non-trainable hard semantic labels and a semantic depth distortion loss to enable clean instance-level removal, and adopts LeftRefill as the reference-based inpainting backbone. The key conceptual departure is the time-reversal inpainting framework, which exploits the forward-moving camera trajectory to convert a low-to-high-resolution inpainting problem into a high-to-low-resolution one, and the use of rendered alpha maps (rather than user-provided or SAM-based masks) to automatically identify completely unobservable regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reconstructing empty street representations is a practical prerequisite for autonomous driving simulation and urban planning, yet the task has been largely unaddressed due to the absence of ground-truth 'unveiled' data and the difficulty of maintaining temporal consistency over long trajectories. The paper's main limitations are its restriction to forward-facing cameras (360° coverage is left open), the heavy reconstruction bottleneck (~10,000 s per scene), and reliance on a pre-trained 2D segmenter whose boundary errors propagate into the 3D semantic field. Open questions include extending the time-reversal strategy to surround-view cameras, handling dynamic objects more robustly, and reducing the reconstruction cost to make the pipeline practical for large-scale deployment.
