## Problem

Existing diffusion-based sampling methods assume non-degenerate (full-rank) noise, excluding underdamped Langevin dynamics where noise acts only in velocity dimensions. Although underdamped dynamics are known to offer superior convergence rates and compatibility with sophisticated numerical integrators, no rigorous framework existed for learning diffusion bridges with degenerate noise or for connecting underdamped score matching to likelihood optimization. This paper fills that gap by providing a unified path-space framework for diffusion bridges with degenerate diffusion matrices.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unnormalized target density ρ_target: R^d → R_{≥0} that can be evaluated pointwise (with intractable normalization constant Z) and a prior distribution over the position variable. The target is extended to the joint (x,y) space by coupling with a standard Gaussian in the velocity variable y, so the extended target is τ(x,y) = p_target(x)·N(y;0,Id). The method produces samples from p_target, importance-weighted estimates of log Z with guaranteed lower bounds, and distributional metrics (ESS, Sinkhorn distance) for evaluation.

## Method

The framework learns forward and backward control functions u and v for SDEs with degenerate noise coefficient η = (0,σ)^T such that the two processes become time reversals of each other (Nelson's relation), minimizing a KL divergence between path-space measures. The Radon-Nikodym derivative between path measures is computed via the pseudoinverse η⁺ of the degenerate diffusion matrix, and its discretization using splitting schemes (OBAB, BAOAB, OBABO) yields a guaranteed lower bound on log Z. For the underdamped case, the Fokker-Planck generator is decomposed into free transport (A), acceleration (B), and damping (O) sub-operators, each integrated exactly or semi-implicitly within a Strang-splitting composition. The method also learns hyperparameters—mass matrix M, diffusion matrix σ, terminal time T, and prior π—end-to-end, eliminating manual tuning. A key theoretical result (Proposition 2.5) shows that underdamped score matching is equivalent to maximizing the ELBO, extending the non-degenerate result of Chen et al. (2021) to degenerate noise.

## Results

The underdamped Diffusion Bridge Sampler (DBS) achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three benchmark problems. On Funnel (d=10), underdamped DBS attains ΔlogZ = 0.010, ESS = 0.779, and W₂^γ = 101.4, outperforming all other methods. On the high-dimensional LGCP (d=1600), underdamped DBS achieves a log-normalizing constant lower bound of 497.5 and ESS×10 = 0.174, dramatically surpassing the next-best method (overdamped DBS at 486.4 and 0.032). Notably, the underdamped DIS diverges entirely on LGCP, while underdamped DBS requires only N=8 discretization steps to match or exceed methods using significantly more steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The underdamped DBS dominates all seven baselines across every benchmark. On Funnel (d=10), it achieves ΔlogZ = 0.010 vs. 0.021 for overdamped DBS, 0.023 for CMCD (OD), and 0.047 for DIS (OD), while its ESS of 0.779 exceeds the next-best (CMCD OD at 0.567) by 37%. On ManyWell (d=50), underdamped DBS matches the best ΔlogZ (0.005) and achieves the highest ESS (0.898) among all methods. On the high-dimensional LGCP (d=1600), the advantage is most dramatic: underdamped DBS achieves logZ(LB) = 497.5 and ESS×10 = 0.174, compared to 486.4 and 0.032 for overdamped DBS, 484.9 for MCD (UD), and a complete divergence for DIS (UD). Underdamped variants consistently outperform their overdamped counterparts for ULA, MCD, and DBS, though CMCD (UD) paradoxically degrades on Funnel (ΔlogZ worsens from 0.023 to 0.268), suggesting the controlled forward process in CMCD does not benefit from the additional velocity variable in the same way.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends the diffusion-bridge sampling framework of Richter & Berner (2024) and the controlled Monte Carlo diffusion framework of Vargas et al. (2024) to processes with degenerate (underdamped) noise, where Brownian motion acts only in a subset of dimensions. It draws on the underdamped Langevin splitting literature (Geffner & Domke, 2023; Monmarché, 2021) for numerical integrators and on the Schrödinger bridge ELBO of Chen et al. (2021) to rigorously establish that underdamped score matching maximizes a likelihood lower bound. The key conceptual departure is treating the density evolution as learned rather than prescribed, combined with novel splitting schemes (OBABO, BAOAB) adapted to the controlled setting, yielding a single framework that subsumes ULA, MCD, CMCD, DIS, and DBS as special cases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The underdamped diffusion bridge sampler achieves state-of-the-art sampling accuracy with as few as 8 discretization steps and eliminates manual hyperparameter tuning through end-to-end learning of the terminal time, prior, and diffusion coefficient—practical gains that make the method broadly accessible. A notable open question is the theoretical convergence rate: while uncontrolled underdamped Langevin enjoys √d scaling, the learned control depends on both position and velocity, which should negate this advantage; the paper offers three heuristic reasons for the empirical success but leaves a rigorous analysis for future work. The framework also naturally extends to generative modeling (underdamped stochastic bridges via the derived ELBO) and to higher-order Langevin dynamics, suggesting a productive direction for subsequent research.
