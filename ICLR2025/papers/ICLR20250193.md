## Problem

Margin-based preference optimization losses (DPO, SimPO, IPO, CPO, etc.) specify ideal LM behavior only through the margin between chosen and rejected log-probabilities, leaving the individual probabilities underspecified. This leads to two unintended consequences as the margin grows: rejected (e.g., unsafe) response probabilities may increase, and chosen (ideal) response probabilities may decrease. The paper identifies the root cause as 'gradient entanglement'—an inherent coupling between chosen and rejected gradients that prevents them from changing independently.

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on standard preference optimization triplets (prompt x, chosen response y_w, rejected response y_l) and the language model's parameters θ, with no architectural modifications required. The key output is a gradient condition—an inequality bounding the inner product ⟨∇log π_w, ∇log π_l⟩ relative to individual gradient norms—that determines whether the ideal behavior (chosen probability increases, rejected decreases) is achieved. The framework also yields per-algorithm characterizations via scalars d_w and d_l that explain why different margin-based methods (DPO, SPPO, SimPO, CPO, etc.) exhibit distinct training dynamics.

## Method

The paper derives the DPO gradient and shows via first-order Taylor expansion that the change in chosen log-probability depends on the rejected gradient and vice versa, formalizing this coupling as 'gradient entanglement.' It then derives a general gradient condition: the inner product between chosen and rejected gradients must be small relative to their individual norms for the ideal behavior to hold, and generalizes this to all margin-based losses through algorithm-specific scalars d_w and d_l that encode regularization, length normalization, and loss curvature. Theoretically, the paper proves the inner product grows as chosen and rejected responses share more similar tokens (Theorem 1, Corollary 2, Theorem 3), showing that shared suffix tokens drive entanglement while the contrasting token itself produces negative correlation. Empirically, the framework is validated on TL;DR with Mistral-7B and Llama-3-8B across ten preference optimization algorithms, and on a sentiment classification task with GPT-2 where token-level gradient cosine similarities confirm the theoretical predictions.

## Results

The paper's central result is a general gradient condition: for margin-based preference optimization, the chosen log-probability increases and the rejected log-probability decreases if and only if the inner product ⟨∇log π_w, ∇log π_l⟩ is sufficiently small relative to the individual gradient norms (specifically, ≤ min(‖∇log π_w‖², ‖∇log π_l‖²) for DPO). Empirically, on the TL;DR dataset with Mistral 7B and Llama-3 8B, DPO and R-DPO exhibit Case 2 behavior (both chosen and rejected log-probabilities decrease, with rejected decreasing more), while SPPO achieves the ideal Case 1 (chosen increases, rejected decreases). Theoretical analysis further shows that the gradient inner product grows as chosen and rejected responses share more similar tokens, explaining why the condition is frequently violated in practice.

**Table 1: Three possible cases of chosen and rejected log-probability changes in DPO, categorized by the gradient inner product.**

| Case | Δlog π_w, Δlog π_l | Behavior | Gradient Condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (Ideal) | Δlog π_w ≥ 0 ≥ Δlog π_l | log π_w ↑, log π_l ↓ | ⟨∇log π_w, ∇log π_l⟩ ≤ min(‖∇log π_w‖², ‖∇log π_l‖²) |
| 2 | 0 ≥ Δlog π_w ≥ Δlog π_l | log π_w ↓, log π_l ↓ | ‖∇log π_w‖² ≤ ⟨∇log π_w, ∇log π_l⟩ ≤ ‖∇log π_l‖² |
| 3 | Δlog π_w ≥ Δlog π_l ≥ 0 | log π_w ↑, log π_l ↑ | ‖∇log π_l‖² ≤ ⟨∇log π_w, ∇log π_l⟩ ≤ ‖∇log π_w‖² |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper analyzes ten margin-based preference optimization methods through the unified gradient entanglement framework rather than comparing them on a single benchmark metric. DPO and R-DPO both fall into Case 2 (both probabilities decrease) on TL;DR, confirming that pure margin-based objectives without regularization tend to suppress the chosen response. SPPO uniquely achieves Case 1 (ideal behavior) because its ratio d_w/d_l > 1 makes the condition for chosen increase more lenient while making rejected decrease harder. CPO, DPOP, RRHF, and SlicHF all show consistent chosen log-probability increases due to their explicit NLL regularizer on the chosen response, which enlarges d_w and relaxes the gradient condition. SimPO and IPO, through length normalization, rewrite the condition in terms of per-token gradients, making it more lenient when preference data is length-biased. KTO's behavior is hyperparameter-dependent (ratio λ_w/λ_l), so no universal conclusion can be drawn. The key finding is that no single margin-based method universally achieves ideal behavior; the appropriate choice depends on whether the task prioritizes increasing chosen probability (use explicit regularization) or decreasing rejected probability (use SPPO-style objectives).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024) as its analytical starting point and extends the partial diagnosis in Pal et al. (2024) (Smaug/DPOP), which identified decreasing chosen probability as a DPO-specific failure mode. The key departure is a unified gradient-entanglement framework that generalizes from DPO to all margin-based preference optimization losses (IPO, SimPO, CPO, RRHF, SlicHF, KTO, SPPO, R-DPO), deriving a single inner-product condition that characterizes when synchronized probability changes occur. Rather than proposing a new loss, the paper provides a mechanistic explanation—through the coupling of chosen and rejected gradients—that accounts for the differing training dynamics observed across these methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The gradient condition offers practitioners a diagnostic lens for understanding why a given preference optimization method may fail in safety-critical or distillation settings, and explains design choices (length normalization, explicit NLL regularization) in terms of how they relax or tighten the entanglement constraint. However, the paper is purely analytical and does not propose a fix; the theoretical results rely on simplified model assumptions (learnable last layer, learnable logits) that may not fully capture dynamics in large transformer architectures, and empirical validation is limited to 7B/8B models on a single summarization dataset. Open questions include whether token-level advantage-based methods can directly address gradient entanglement, and whether the findings hold at the scale of frontier models where gradient geometry may differ substantially.
