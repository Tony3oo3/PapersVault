## Problem

GTD, the canonical solution to the deadly triad in off-policy RL with function approximation, requires two sets of parameters and two learning rates that are notoriously difficult to tune. During the original GTD derivation, an intermediate algorithm A^T TD was invented but abandoned due to O(K²) per-step cost. This paper revisits that abandoned design choice and shows a refined variant, A_t^T TD, achieves the same convergence guarantees with only one parameter set and one learning rate, at O(K) per-step computation.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a Markovian transition stream (s_t, a_t, r_{t+1}, s_{t+1}) generated by a behavior policy μ, along with a K-dimensional state feature function x(s). It produces a single weight vector w ∈ ℝ^K approximating the target policy's value function. Unusually, the update at time t requires access to a second sample at time t+f(t) (where f is an increasing gap function), so the algorithm must maintain a rolling buffer of O(f(t)) past transitions; with the recommended f(t)=⌊ln²(t+1)⌋, this buffer grows only logarithmically and is negligible in practice.

## Method

A_t^T TD estimates the gradient of the NEU objective ∇L(w)=A^T(Aw+b) by using a single sample at time t+f(t) to estimate A^T and a single sample at time t to estimate Aw_t+b, rather than maintaining a moving-average estimate of A^T as in the original A^T TD. The increasing gap function f(t) (e.g., ⌊ln²(t+1)⌋) separates the two samples to mitigate the double-sampling bias that arises from Markovian correlation. The per-step update is w_{t+1} = w_t + α_t · ρ_{t+f(t)}(x_{t+f(t)}−γx_{t+f(t)+1})x_{t+f(t)}^T · ρ_t δ_t x_t, requiring only O(K) computation and one learning rate. The paper proves almost-sure convergence to the TD fixed point using a skeleton-iterates technique that coordinates interval lengths with both the learning rate and the gap function, and establishes a finite-sample rate of O(f(t)ln(t)/t) for a projected variant, matching on-policy TD up to logarithmic factors.

## Results

The paper evaluates A_t^T TD on two standard benchmarks using RMSPBE (root mean squared projected Bellman error) as the metric. On Boyan's chain (on-policy), A_t^T TD achieves the lowest final RMSPBE among all convergent methods, outperforming GTD2 and TDC. On Baird's counterexample (off-policy), A_t^T TD remains convergent and reaches the same final RMSPBE as GTD2, TDC, and TDRC, while naive off-policy TD, Vtrace, and HTD all diverge. The convergence rate of the projected variant is O(f(t)·ln(t)/t), which with the recommended gap function f(t)=⌊ln²(t+1)⌋ is on par with canonical on-policy linear TD up to logarithmic factors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Boyan's chain (on-policy), A_t^T TD outperforms GTD2 and TDC in final RMSPBE, while Vtrace and HTD reduce to plain TD in this setting and are not competitive. On Baird's counterexample (off-policy), A_t^T TD matches the final performance of GTD2, TDC, and TDRC, all of which converge. In stark contrast, naive off-policy TD, Vtrace, and HTD all diverge rapidly on Baird's counterexample, illustrating the deadly triad. The key practical advantage over GTD2/TDC is that A_t^T TD requires only one set of parameters and one learning rate, whereas GTD-family methods require two of each, making them harder to tune. The paper also shows that even a suboptimal gap function f(t)=O(log t) (violating Assumption 4.4) still yields convergence, suggesting the theoretical conditions may be conservative.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly revisits A^T TD, an intermediate algorithm from Sutton et al. (2008) that was discarded during the derivation of GTD due to its O(K²) per-step cost. The key departure is the introduction of a gap function f(t) that separates the two samples used to estimate A^T and Aw_t+b, reducing computation to O(K) while addressing the double-sampling issue inherent in Markovian data streams. The asymptotic convergence proof relies on the skeleton iterates technique from Qian et al. (2024), extended here to handle the increasing gap, and the finite-sample analysis follows the projected-TD framework of Bhandari et al. (2018).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that A_t^T TD eliminates GTD's two learning rates and two parameter sets, which Sutton et al. (2008) themselves noted were 'hard to tune in practice.' The theoretical contribution—a skeleton-iterates analysis that coordinates interval lengths with both the learning rate and an increasing gap function—is a reusable technical tool for analyzing stochastic approximation with Markovian noise and non-constant delays. Open questions include whether Assumption 4.4 is overly conservative (experiments suggest f(t)=O(log t) may suffice), whether incorporating MDP transition knowledge can further reduce the gap, and whether the analysis extends to the unprojected algorithm without the ball constraint.
