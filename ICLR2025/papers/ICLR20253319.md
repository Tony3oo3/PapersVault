## Problem

Existing robustness verification methods for text classifiers cannot practically handle Levenshtein distance constraints, which include insertions and deletions that change input length. Interval Bound Propagation requires multiple forward passes and assumes fixed-length inputs, while randomized smoothing only provides probabilistic certificates. This paper is the first to compute deterministic Levenshtein distance robustness certificates for convolutional classifiers in a single forward pass.

## Input / Output

The method takes variable-length character sequences (tokenized as one-hot vectors over the alphabet) as input and produces both a class prediction and a certified radius k, meaning the prediction is guaranteed correct for all sentences within Levenshtein distance k. Unlike image-domain verification that operates on fixed-dimension ℓp balls, the input space here is the set of all finite character sequences, and the certified radius is an integer representing the maximum number of character insertions, deletions, or substitutions tolerated.

## Method

The key insight is to use the ERP (Edit Reference Point) distance, a natural extension of Levenshtein distance to sequences of real-valued vectors, as the metric for computing Lipschitz constants at intermediate layers where Levenshtein distance is undefined. The global Lipschitz constant of the margin function is upper-bounded by multiplying per-layer constants: the embedding layer bound M(E), each convolutional layer bound M(K) (sum of ℓp norms of kernel slices), and the classification layer bound ‖wŷ − wy‖r. During training, each layer's output is divided by its Lipschitz constant to enforce 1-Lipschitzness, yielding a modified classifier whose certified radius is simply floor(margin / local_Lipschitz_constant) computed in one forward pass. This avoids the exponential blowup of brute-force enumeration and the impractical multi-pass requirement of IBP, achieving 4–7 orders of magnitude speedup while being the only method that can verify at distances k > 1.

## Results

LipsLev achieves 38.80% verified accuracy at k=1 and 13.93% at k=2 on AG-News (p=2), 91.33% and 75.33% on Fake-News (p=1), 18.69% and 1.83% on SST-2 (p=1), and 31.37% and 5.90% on IMDB (p=∞). Verification requires a single forward pass with runtimes of 0.001–0.017 seconds per sample, making it 4–7 orders of magnitude faster than both brute-force and IBP baselines. LipsLev is the only method capable of providing non-trivial verified accuracy at Levenshtein distances greater than 1, where IBP and brute-force either fail or are computationally infeasible.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against IBP (Huang et al., 2019), LipsLev achieves higher verified accuracy on AG-News (38.80% vs. 29.10% at k=1, p=2) and matches it exactly on Fake-News (91.33% at k=1, p=1), while being 4–7 orders of magnitude faster (0.01s vs. 31.5s per sample). Critically, IBP cannot verify at k > 1 due to the combinatorial explosion of insertion/deletion perturbations, whereas LipsLev provides non-trivial certificates at k=2 across all datasets. Against RS-Del (Huang et al., 2023), LipsLev maintains significantly higher clean accuracy (e.g., 74.80% vs. 48.20% on AG-News p=2) and provides deterministic certificates in a single forward pass, while RS-Del offers probabilistic guarantees requiring ~5,000 forward passes. RS-Del does outperform LipsLev on verified accuracy for SST-2 (e.g., 21.98% vs. 14.68% at k=1, p=∞) and IMDB p=2 (33.67% vs. 8.67% at k=1), but at the cost of a large clean accuracy drop and probabilistic (not deterministic) certification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the Lipschitz-constant-based certification paradigm from the image domain (Hein & Andriushchenko 2017; Tsuzuku et al. 2018; Xu et al. 2022) into NLP by introducing the ERP distance (Chen & Ng 2004) as a bridge metric between the discrete, variable-length Levenshtein space and the continuous intermediate representations of convolutional layers. It departs from prior NLP verification work (Huang et al. 2019; Jia et al. 2019), which relied on Interval Bound Propagation and could only handle fixed-length substitution constraints, by showing that layer-wise Lipschitz bounds under ERP compose multiplicatively to yield a global Levenshtein-distance certificate. The 1-Lipschitz training formulation (Eq. 7) directly adapts the layer-wise normalization strategy of LOT (Xu et al. 2022) to the ERP-distance setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LipsLev is the first method to provide deterministic Levenshtein-distance certificates (including insertions and deletions) in a single forward pass, and the only existing method that can practically verify for k > 1, making it a foundational step toward scalable text verification. However, verified accuracy remains modest (e.g., 13.93% at k=2 on AG-News) and the gap to adversarial accuracy is large, indicating the Lipschitz bound is still loose. The paper explicitly identifies three barriers to extending the approach to modern Transformer architectures: non-differentiable tokenizers, poor character-level performance of transformers, and the unbounded Lipschitz constant of self-attention, leaving the most practically relevant models out of scope.
