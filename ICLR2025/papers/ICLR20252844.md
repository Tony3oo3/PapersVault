## Problem

Standard Langevin Monte Carlo requires log-concavity or isoperimetric inequalities (e.g., log-Sobolev) for convergence guarantees, but these conditions fail for multimodal distributions where the sampler gets trapped in a single mode. Annealing-based MCMC is widely used empirically to overcome this limitation, yet no non-asymptotic complexity bound existed for annealed LMC that avoids such isoperimetric assumptions. This paper provides the first such bound, showing that annealed LMC achieves polynomial complexity in problem parameters for a class of mixtures of Gaussians where all prior isoperimetry-based guarantees are exponential.

## Input / Output

The algorithm requires oracle access to a β-smooth potential V and its gradient ∇V, an annealing schedule (η(θ), λ(θ)) interpolating from a Gaussian π₀ to the target π, and a total time T with M discretization points. It outputs a single approximate sample from π ∝ e^{-V} with ε² accuracy in KL(π‖·). The complexity depends on the action A of the interpolation curve in Wasserstein-2 geometry rather than on LSI or Poincaré constants, and the initial sample from π₀ is obtained via rejection sampling in Õ(1) oracle calls.

## Method

The key technical tool is the Girsanov theorem, which bounds the KL divergence between the path measure of the annealed Langevin diffusion and a reference path measure that exactly tracks the interpolation curve (π_θ ∝ exp(−η(θ)V − λ(θ)/2‖·‖²)). This yields an error decomposition into two terms: the action A of the curve divided by total time T (governing continuous-dynamics tracking error), and a discretization error from the exponential-integrator scheme that exploits the linear structure of the score ∇log π̃_t = −η(t/T)∇V − λ(t/T)x. By setting T ∝ A/ε² and M ∝ dβ²A²/ε⁶ uniform steps, the algorithm achieves ε² KL accuracy in Õ(dβ²A²/ε⁶) oracle calls. For a mixture of N Gaussians with means at distance r, the action is polynomial in r while the LSI constant is exponential in βr², demonstrating a fundamental advantage of the annealing approach over isoperimetry-based analyses.

## Results

The paper establishes the first non-asymptotic oracle complexity bound for annealed Langevin Monte Carlo (ALMC): Õ(dβ²A²/ε⁶) gradient evaluations to achieve ε² accuracy in KL divergence, where A is the action of the interpolation curve in Wasserstein geometry. For a mixture of N Gaussians with means at distance r, the action A = O(d(r²β+1)(r²+d/β)), yielding polynomial complexity Õ(d³β²r⁴(r⁴β²∨d²)ε⁻⁶) in TV distance, in contrast to the exponential Õ(e^{Θ(βr²)}dε⁻²) bound available from LMC under LSI. Experiments on 6-component Gaussian mixtures with r ∈ {2,...,30} confirm polynomial scaling (log-log slope ≈ 2.84–2.89, R² > 0.995) in the number of iterations required to reach KL < 0.1 or 0.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among isoperimetry-free samplers, ALMC achieves the most favorable ε-dependence (ε⁻⁶) compared to RDMC (e^{poly(ε⁻¹)}), RS-DMC (exp(O(log³d ε⁻²))), and ZOD-MC (exp(Õ(d)·O(log ε⁻¹))), all of which exhibit exponential or super-polynomial dependence on 1/ε. ALMC also requires the least stringent assumptions—only β-smoothness of the potential—whereas RDMC additionally requires near-convexity at infinity and ZOD-MC requires the potential to grow at most quadratically. Compared to STLMC, which also avoids isoperimetric assumptions, ALMC applies to general non-log-concave distributions rather than being restricted to translated mixtures of a well-conditioned distribution. Against LMC and PS, which achieve better ε-dependence (ε⁻² and log ε⁻¹ respectively), ALMC trades off a worse ε-rate in exchange for eliminating the C-LSI requirement entirely, making it applicable to multimodal distributions where the LSI constant is exponential in the mode separation. The key empirical validation (Example 2 and Fig. 2) demonstrates that for 2-component Gaussian mixtures with r² ≫ β⁻¹, ALMC's polynomial complexity in r contrasts sharply with the exponential e^{Θ(βr²)} dependence of LMC-based bounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Langevin Monte Carlo framework (Vempala & Wibisono, 2019) by incorporating an annealing schedule and analyzing the resulting process through the lens of Girsanov's theorem and optimal transport, rather than the traditional warm-start decomposition that requires log-concavity or isoperimetric assumptions at each intermediate step. The discretization leverages the exponential integrator scheme (Zhang & Chen, 2023) to exploit the linear structure of the annealing potential. Conceptually, the paper departs from the score-based diffusion sampling literature (Huang et al., 2024a,b; He et al., 2024) by operating on a known interpolation of distributions, thereby avoiding the need for score estimation or neural network training, while also connecting to stochastic thermodynamics where the action-to-duration ratio mirrors irreversible entropy production.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first non-asymptotic complexity bound for annealed MCMC that does not require log-concavity or isoperimetric inequalities, with the action functional in Wasserstein geometry serving as the key problem-dependent quantity. For highly multimodal distributions like mixtures of Gaussians with well-separated modes, this yields polynomial complexity where LSI-based methods give exponential bounds. However, the ε^{-6} dependence (versus ε^{-2} for LSI-based LMC) is a significant limitation that the authors acknowledge stems from the Girsanov-based analysis; sharpening this dependence and extending the framework to non-smooth potentials or optimal annealing schedules remain open problems. The empirical validation is limited to a single 2D mixture-of-Gaussians experiment, leaving the practical competitiveness of the method at scale untested.
