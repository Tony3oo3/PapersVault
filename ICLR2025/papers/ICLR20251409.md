## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting optimization lacks explicit geometric constraints, causing overfitting and corrupted geometry in sparsely observed regions (e.g., indoor scenes). While monocular depth and normal priors have been explored, they suffer from scale ambiguity. The paper identifies that pairwise matching priors from pre-trained optical flow models can provide absolute scale information, but this capability has not been leveraged to regularize Gaussian radiance field optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251409/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB training images with known camera poses (from COLMAP) and a frozen pre-trained optical flow model (Sea-Raft or RAFT). During training, it renders novel views at sampled camera positions and feeds the ground-truth input image alongside the rendered sampled view into the flow model. The output is an optimized 3D Gaussian radiance field (3DGS or 2DGS) that supports depth rendering, novel view synthesis, and mesh extraction, with the FDS loss applied after 15,000 of 35,000 total iterations.

## Method

FDS introduces a depth-adaptive camera sampling scheme that translates the input camera by a radius ε_t = σ·D̄_i/f in a random in-plane direction, ensuring a consistent radiance flow magnitude σ across views regardless of scene depth. For each sampled view, the method computes a Radiance Flow analytically from rendered depth and relative camera poses, and a Prior Flow from the pre-trained optical flow model operating on the ground-truth input image and the rendered sampled view. The FDS loss is the L2 distance between these two flows, with the Prior Flow detached from Gaussian parameters to prevent gradient backpropagation through the flow network. This creates a mutual refinement loop: the Prior Flow corrects inaccurate Radiance Flow, which repositions Gaussian points, improving rendered views and in turn yielding more accurate Prior Flow in subsequent iterations.

## Results

On the Mushroom dataset, 3DGS+FDS achieves an Abs Rel of 0.0568 (a 53% reduction over 3DGS's 0.1214) and an F-Score of 0.6958 (vs. 0.3739 for 3DGS), while 2DGS+FDS reaches Abs Rel 0.0561 and F-Score 0.6974. On ScanNet (V2), 2DGS+FDS further improves Abs Rel to 0.0432 (vs. 0.0831 for 2DGS) and F-Score to 0.5459 (vs. 0.4464). Rendering quality also improves slightly: PSNR gains 0.58 dB for 3DGS (24.76 vs. 24.18) and 0.50 dB for 2DGS (24.06 vs. 23.56) on Mushroom, with training time increasing by only ~0.5 hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FDS consistently outperforms all four baselines (GOF, PGSR, 3DGS, 2DGS) on geometry metrics across both Mushroom and ScanNet. On Mushroom, 3DGS+FDS reduces Abs Rel by 53% over 3DGS (0.0568 vs. 0.1214) and nearly doubles F-Score (0.6958 vs. 0.3739), while also beating GOF (Abs Rel 0.2380) and PGSR (Abs Rel 0.1723) by large margins. On ScanNet, 2DGS+FDS achieves the best Abs Rel (0.0432) and F-Score (0.5459), outperforming 2DGS by 48% in Abs Rel and 22% in F-Score. In rendering quality, FDS provides modest but consistent PSNR gains (+0.58 dB for 3DGS, +0.50 dB for 2DGS on Mushroom) while slightly improving SSIM and LPIPS. The only trade-off is training time: FDS adds roughly 0.5 hours to the ~0.8h baseline, bringing total to ~1.3h, which remains competitive with GOF (≈1.4h) and PGSR (≈1.2h).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3DGS and 2DGS as the underlying radiance field representations, adding a new regularization loss (FDS) during training. It departs from prior geometric-prior approaches (DN-Splatter, monocular depth/normal supervision) by leveraging pairwise matching priors from a pre-trained optical flow model, which inherently provides absolute scale information that monocular cues lack. The depth-adaptive camera sampling scheme and the mutual-refinement mechanism between Prior Flow and Radiance Flow are novel contributions not present in prior flow-supervision work, which has been largely confined to dynamic scene reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FDS offers a plug-and-play regularization that addresses the well-known overfitting problem in sparse-view 3DGS without requiring additional sensor data or architectural changes, making it broadly applicable to existing Gaussian splatting pipelines. The finding that multi-view depth priors (Unimatch) actually degrade performance due to limited feature overlap is a useful cautionary result for practitioners. Limitations remain for scenes with significant lighting variation, reflective surfaces, and motion blur, and the method's dependence on a pre-trained optical flow model introduces a quality ceiling tied to that model's accuracy. Future exploration of FDS in monocular video reconstruction is an open direction.
