## Problem

It was known that ReLU-based GNNs can uniformly express all GC2 queries (with parameter count independent of graph size), but whether other widely-used activations like sigmoid and tanh share this property remained an open question posed by Grohe (2021). This paper resolves that question negatively, showing that uniform expressivity is impossible for a broad class of Pfaffian activations, while simultaneously demonstrating that a near-uniform alternative with only logarithmic parameter growth in graph degree is achievable.

## Input / Output

The method takes a finite, undirected, simple vertex-colored graph (with a fixed number of colors ℓ) and a GC2 query (a guarded two-variable first-order formula of depth d) as input, and produces a per-vertex real-valued embedding that must be ≥ 1−ε when the query is true and ≤ ε when false. The key design constraint is on the number of trainable parameters: the negative result shows no fixed-size GNN suffices for Pfaffian activations, while the positive result bounds the size by O(d log Δ) or O(d log log Δ) where Δ is the maximum graph degree, making the parameter count grow only logarithmically with graph size.

## Method

The paper contributes two complementary results. First, a negative result proving that GNNs with bounded superpolynomial Pfaffian activations (including sigmoid and tanh) cannot uniformly express all GC2 queries: the proof constructs families of rooted trees T[x,k,m] whose root vertices are distinguished by a fixed GC2 query, then shows via induction that the GNN's output signals at corresponding vertices converge to the same limit as the tree order grows, exploiting the superpolynomial decay of error terms inherent to Pfaffian functions. Second, a positive result showing that for 'step-like' activation functions (characterized by fast convergence of iterated composition σ^ℓ to the linear threshold function σ*), GC2 queries of depth d can be expressed over graphs of degree ≤ Δ with O(d log Δ) parameters, or O(d log log Δ) when the convergence is quadratic (η=0). The construction adapts the Barcelo et al. (2020) CReLU GNN by replacing the exact threshold with σ^ℓ, and the error analysis shows that the approximation error per layer is bounded by the convergence rate of σ^ℓ to σ*, which compounds favorably across d iterations. Propositions 5.2–5.4 instantiate step-like activations from arctan, tanh, and sigmoid via small 3- or 4-layer neural networks, yielding concrete O(d log log Δ) bounds for sigmoid and tanh GNNs.

## Results

The paper proves that GNNs with bounded superpolynomial Pfaffian activations (sigmoid, tanh) cannot uniformly express all GC2 queries (Theorem 3.2), in contrast to CReLU GNNs which achieve uniform expressivity with size at most 4d. However, the authors show that step-like activations (including sigmoid and tanh) can express GC2 queries of depth d over graphs of degree ≤ Δ with GNN size O(d log log Δ) (Corollary 3.5), specifically at most (9 + 4 log(2 + log(Δ+2)))d parameters. In experiments, the CReLU GNN maintains a constant output separation of 1 regardless of graph size, while the Pfaffian GNN requires ℓ=10 (for Q₁) or ℓ=12 (for Q₂) compositions to reach near-1 separation on graphs up to 4000 vertices. In the learning experiment on 3750 random graphs (50–2000 nodes, avg. degree 5), both Sigmoid and CReLU GNNs achieve similar test MSE, with a slight advantage for the Pfaffian variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the CReLU (or ReLU) Σ-AC-GNN construction from Barceló et al. (2020), which achieves uniform expressivity of all GC2 queries with a fixed size of 4d independent of graph size. The paper's central negative result shows that sigmoid and tanh GNNs strictly lose to this baseline: there exists a GC2 query (of depth 4) that no sigmoid/tanh GNN can express uniformly, regardless of the number of parameters. In the non-uniform regime, however, the Pfaffian GNNs recover expressivity with only a log-log dependence on the maximum degree Δ, which the authors argue is effectively constant for practical graph sizes. In the empirical separation experiment (Figure 3), the CReLU GNN maintains a perfect output gap of 1 at all graph sizes, while the Pfaffian GNN with ℓ≤8 compositions fails to separate on graphs exceeding ~1500 vertices; increasing ℓ to 10–12 restores near-perfect separation up to 4000 vertices. In the supervised learning experiment (Figure 4), both GNN architectures achieve comparable test MSE on 1250 held-out graphs, with the Pfaffian (Sigmoid) GNN showing a slight edge, suggesting that the theoretical expressivity gap does not translate into a clear learning advantage under the same training protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the logical expressivity framework for GNNs established by Barcelo et al. (2020) and Grohe (2021, 2023), which characterized the GC2 fragment as the exact uniform expressivity of ReLU Σ-AC-GNNs and introduced GFO+C for general message-passing GNNs. It departs from prior work in two directions: first, it generalizes the negative result for rational GNNs (Khalife, 2023) to the broader class of bounded superpolynomial Pfaffian activations (including sigmoid and tanh), answering an explicit open question from Grohe (2021); second, it provides the first constructive non-uniform expressivity upper-bounds with explicit parameter-size guarantees (log and log-log in the maximum degree) for these activation functions, building on the descriptive complexity framework of Grohe (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The negative result resolves a long-standing conjecture that sigmoid activations might match ReLU in uniform GC2 expressivity, clarifying that the ReLU's piecewise-linear structure is genuinely essential for uniform expressivity. The positive 'almost-uniform' result with log-log parameter growth is practically reassuring: for any realistic graph degree, the additional parameters needed are negligible, meaning sigmoid/tanh GNNs remain viable for GC2-level queries. Open questions include a complete characterization of which activation functions enable uniform GC2 expressivity, and bridging the gap between provable expressivity and actual learnability, as the paper's experiments suggest that higher expressivity does not trivially translate to easier training.
