## Problem

Existing safe RL methods that guarantee constraint satisfaction during learning are limited to low-dimensional policies (e.g., PID controllers) and cannot scale to the high-dimensional policies required by deep RL. Conversely, scalable deep RL methods for constrained MDPs do not enforce safety throughout the learning process. No prior model-based RL algorithm for continuous state-action spaces provides both provable safety during exploration and finite sample-complexity bounds for reaching a near-optimal policy.

## Input / Output

The method takes either continuous state vectors or RGB visual observations (via an RSSM encoder) along with a scalar safety budget d and an initial nonempty safe set S_0 (e.g., from a simulator or offline data). It outputs a policy (a 750K-parameter neural network in visual control experiments) that maximizes cumulative reward over a horizon T while keeping expected accumulated cost below d. In the visual control setting, the agent receives partial observations o_t ~ p(·|s_t) rather than full states, and the dynamics model is an ensemble of M RSSMs whose disagreement serves as the epistemic uncertainty estimate.

## Method

ACTSAFE operates in two stages: an expansion phase where it selects policies from a pessimistic safe set that maximize model epistemic uncertainty (intrinsic reward ‖σ_n(s,a)‖) to reduce uncertainty and grow the safe set, followed by an exploitation phase that greedily maximizes the extrinsic reward subject to the safety constraint. The safe set is defined pessimistically as policies whose worst-case cost over all plausible dynamics (within a well-calibrated confidence region) stays below the budget d, and is expanded by adding policies close (via a Lipschitz-based distance D) to already-safe policies. Theoretically, under RKHS dynamics with GP models, ACTSAFE proves safety in all episodes and ε-optimality within a finite number of episodes. Practically, the algorithm replaces explicit safe-set maintenance with a constrained optimization (Eq. 7) solved by Log-Barrier SGD (LBSGD), uses RSSM ensembles for uncertainty in visual control, and approximates pessimistic cost via ensemble-disagreement-weighted penalties (à la MOPO).

## Results

In the GP-based PENDULUM and CARTPOLE experiments, ACTSAFE incurs zero accumulated cost during all learning episodes, while the non-pessimistic variant and OPAX both violate safety constraints. On SAFETY-GYM vision control tasks, ACTSAFE significantly reduces constraint violations across all environments compared to CPO, LAMBDA, and BSRP-LAG, though it slightly underperforms LAMBDA in reward at the cost of much smaller constraint violations. In hard exploration tasks with sparse rewards (SAFETY-GYM and RWRL CARTPOLESWINGUPSPARSE), ACTSAFE substantially outperforms the GREEDY baseline and is the only method (alongside OPTIMISTIC) to find non-trivial policies, while UNIFORM fails entirely. On the HUMANOIDBENCH POLE task, ACTSAFE significantly reduces constraint violations compared to OPAX while maintaining competitive forward-velocity performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CPO (Achiam et al., 2017), ACTSAFE outperforms on both safety and performance across SAFETY-GYM tasks and the DOGGO robot, with CPO incurring substantially more constraint violations. Against LAMBDA (As et al., 2022), ACTSAFE slightly underperforms in reward but incurs much smaller costs, reflecting the conservatism needed to maintain safety during learning; on the DOGGO robot, ACTSAFE moderately underperforms LAMBDA in performance while maintaining safety. Against BSRP-LAG (Huang et al., 2024), ACTSAFE outperforms in both safety and performance on SAFETY-GYM, and outperforms in performance on the DOGGO robot. Against OPAX (Sukhija et al., 2024), ACTSAFE maintains strict safety during learning while OPAX violates constraints; in the CARTPOLESWINGUPSPARSE task, ACTSAFE's confined safe exploration allows it to visit reward-relevant states faster than OPAX's undirected exploration, finding a good policy within 1M steps where OPAX requires 1.25M steps and still fails to satisfy constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

ACTSAFE synthesizes two previously separate lines of work: (1) provably safe exploration methods from safe Bayesian optimization (Berkenkamp et al., 2021; Sukhija et al., 2023) that maintain and expand a safe set of policies but are limited to low-dimensional, model-free settings, and (2) model-based RL with optimistic planning (Curi et al., 2020; Chua et al., 2018) that achieves sample efficiency but lacks safety guarantees during learning. The key conceptual departure is using the model's epistemic uncertainty to *implicitly* define and expand the safe set (rather than explicitly maintaining it), enabling scalability to high-dimensional policies while preserving the pessimism-for-safety / optimism-for-exploration duality. For practical implementation, ACTSAFE builds on RSSM ensembles (Hafner et al., 2019) for uncertainty quantification and LBSGD (Usmanova et al., 2024) for constrained policy optimization, integrating these into a two-stage (expansion → exploitation) algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first model-based safe RL algorithm to provide both safety and finite sample-complexity guarantees for continuous state-action spaces, closing a significant theoretical gap between provably safe (but low-dimensional) methods and scalable (but unsafe) deep RL approaches. The practical finding that a small warm-up period (<5% of total data) suffices for safe visual control makes the method deployable in realistic settings where some prior data is available. Key limitations include the requirement for an initial safe set (Assumption 4.3), the gap between the RKHS/GP theory and the RSSM ensemble practice (no formal calibration guarantee for the latter), and the conservatism that trades off some reward performance for safety. Open questions include extending the theoretical guarantees beyond RKHS to general well-calibrated models, reducing or eliminating the warm-up requirement, and whether the two-stage expansion-then-exploitation structure is optimal or whether a more adaptive transition would improve sample efficiency.
