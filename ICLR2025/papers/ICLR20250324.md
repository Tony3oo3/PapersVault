## Problem

LLMs are inaccurate and overconfident when directly estimating numerical probabilities, and they lack an interpretable, controllable process for how estimates are derived. This prevents their use in large-scale decision-making tasks (e.g., infrastructure planning, medical triage) that require reliable probability estimation under incomplete information, where simple ranking or argmax over LLM outputs yields suboptimal solutions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a general scenario S (defining the decision context and two binary outcomes) and a downstream condition U (partial information about a specific instance), forming context C = S + U. It produces calibrated probability estimates P(O_i | C) for each outcome, a hard decision via argmax, and optionally binary yes/no follow-up questions targeting the most informative unobserved factor. The Bayesian network is trained per-scenario using 128 randomly sampled complete-information instances, and the factor set is generated from S alone so it remains neutral and reusable across all conditions U in that scenario.

## Method

BIRD approximates a Bayesian network whose nodes are LLM-generated real-world factors (with discrete values) and whose CPT is parameterized by individual factor-outcome probabilities P(O_i | f_j). The joint probability P(O_i | f) is computed via Bordley's logarithmic opinion pool formula, which combines individual factor odds multiplicatively under a conditional-independence assumption. These parameters are optimized by gradient descent (SGD, lr=1e-2, 20 epochs) minimizing a combined MSE loss against LLM coarse verbalized probabilities (mapped from a 7-level Likert scale) and a margin ranking loss that preserves the directional support of each factor value, using 128 sampled complete-information instances per scenario as supervision. At inference, an LLM entailment step maps the condition U to specific factor values (observed factors get P=1, unobserved factors get uniform), and the final probability is obtained by marginalizing over the product space of all factor values. The key architectural insight is separating abduction (factor generation from scenario only, ensuring neutrality) from deduction (condition-to-factor mapping), which makes the trained network reusable and interpretable while tolerating LLM estimation errors through the Bayesian constraint structure.

## Results

On the preference-based pairwise probability estimation task (Table 1), BIRD with Llama-3.1-70b achieves an average F1 of 0.588, more than 30% higher than GPT-4 CoT (0.289) and Llama-3.1 CoT (0.309). Even under the unfair Explicit Comparison (EC*) setting where both conditions are shown simultaneously, BIRD (0.588) outperforms GPT-4 EC* (0.540) by 5%. On decision-making benchmarks (Table 2), BIRD with Llama-3.1 outperforms CoT with self-consistency on all three datasets: TODAY (74.3 vs. 72.6), PLASMA (73.0 vs. 71.5), and COMMON2SENSE (92.3 vs. 90.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Chain-of-Thought with self-consistency (Wei et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2023), BIRD achieves a decisive win on the core probability estimation task: 0.588–0.592 average F1 versus 0.289–0.309 for GPT-4/Llama-3.1 CoT, a gap of roughly 30 percentage points. On the decision-making task (Table 2), BIRD with Llama-3.1 beats CoT on all three benchmarks (TODAY +1.7, PLASMA +1.5, COMMON2SENSE +1.5), while with Llama-2 the comparison is mixed (BIRD wins on TODAY +2.4 and COMMON2SENSE −4.8, but loses on PLASMA −2.8). The Explicit Comparison (EC*) baseline, which sees both conditions simultaneously and is thus not directly comparable, still trails BIRD by 5% (GPT-4 EC* 0.540 vs. BIRD 0.588), confirming that BIRD's per-condition probability decomposition is more reliable than joint comparison. The vanilla verbalization baseline (Wang et al., 2023) with self-consistency scores only 0.303–0.311 F1, showing that simply asking LLMs to output probabilities is far less effective than BIRD's Bayesian alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

BIRD builds on Bordley's (1982) logarithmic opinion pool formula for aggregating probability assessments, repurposing it as the structural backbone of a Bayesian network whose conditional probability table is learned from LLM coarse predictions rather than observed data. It departs from the dominant paradigm of direct LLM inference (vanilla verbalization, CoT, logit-based scoring) by treating the LLM as a component oracle within an explicit probabilistic model, rather than as the entire inference engine. This positions BIRD between decomposition-based reasoning (Wolfson et al., 2020; Tafjord et al., 2022) and uncertainty estimation in LLMs (Xiong et al., 2024; Tian et al., 2023), adding a rigorous Bayesian constraint layer that prior verbalization-based approaches lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a well-documented failure mode of LLMs—overconfident and inconsistent probability estimation—by externalizing the probabilistic structure into an interpretable Bayesian network with natural-language variables, which is critical for mission-critical applications like medical decision support or infrastructure planning. However, the approach is limited to binary outcomes, relies on a conditional independence assumption that may not hold for correlated real-world factors, and requires multiple LLM calls (factor generation, entailment, coarse probability sampling) that limit scalability. Open questions include whether the framework extends to multi-class or continuous outcomes, how it behaves when the generated factor set is incomplete or biased, and whether the 30% improvement generalizes beyond the specific pairwise-preference evaluation protocol used here.
