## Problem

Existing adversarial defenses (robust training, regularization, purification) require expensive retraining or domain-specific heuristics and inevitably alter a model's learned feature-matching behavior, often degrading clean accuracy. The paper tackles the open question of whether a well-trained model can be reprogrammed to improve robustness against adversarial or noisy perturbations without modifying its parameters, offering an orthogonal and efficient complement to existing defenses.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard classification images (MNIST 28×28, CIFAR-10 32×32, SVHN 32×32, ImageNet-10) and produces class labels, identical to the original pre-trained model's I/O. The key distinction is that the method is applied post-hoc to an already-trained backbone (MLP, LeNet, ResNet, MLP-Mixer): the input image passes through the same architecture, but each linear feature-transformation layer is replaced or blended with a robust nonlinear counterpart, so no architectural change is visible to the user. Three paradigms control how much of the original parameters are touched, from zero-cost plug-and-play to light fine-tuning.

## Method

The paper reformulates the standard linear feature transformation z = aᵀx as the closed-form solution of a least-squares problem (Linear Pattern Matching, LPM), then replaces the quadratic penalty with an L1 (least-absolute-deviation) objective to obtain Nonlinear Robust Pattern Matching (NRPM), which down-weights outlier features via adaptive instance-wise reweighting. NRPM is solved by unrolling K steps of a Newton-IRLS algorithm into differentiable layers, yielding a robust nonlinear layer that can replace or blend with the original linear layer. A hybrid architecture computes λ·z_LPM + (1−λ)·z_NRPM per layer, and three progressive paradigms control the trade-off: Paradigm 1 freezes all parameters and treats λ as a fixed hyperparameter (zero training cost), Paradigm 2 freezes parameters but learns layer-wise λ via light fine-tuning, and Paradigm 3 fine-tunes both model parameters and λ. An influence-function analysis (Theorem 3.2) proves that NRPM's sensitivity to a perturbation Δx is bounded by a weight w_Δx = 1/|Δx − z_LPM/D|, which shrinks for large deviations, whereas LPM's influence is unbounded and grows linearly with the perturbation magnitude.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet18, Paradigm 3 (full fine-tuning) achieves 80.43% natural accuracy and 57.23% PGD-20 robust accuracy, outperforming all baselines on average robustness (58.03% vs. 52.03% for DYNAT, the strongest competitor). Under AutoAttack, the method reaches 52.60% robust accuracy, surpassing AWP (46.90%) and PGD-AT (42.14%) by 5.7 and 10.5 points respectively. On MNIST with a 3-layer MLP, Paradigm 3 achieves 86.1% natural accuracy and 39.8% robust accuracy at ε=0.3, compared to only 9.1% for standard adversarial training. The method also demonstrates certified robustness advantages via randomized smoothing across multiple σ levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed Paradigm 3 outperforms all five baselines on average robustness (58.03% vs. 44.89% for PGD-AT, 44.54% for TRADES-2.0, 46.96% for MART, 48.24% for AWP, and 52.03% for DYNAT). Against the strongest competitor DYNAT, the method gains +4.98 points on PGD, +4.27 on FGSM, +11.88 on C&W, +7.50 on AutoAttack, and +7.78 on DeepFool, while trailing by only 0.41 points on SPSA. Compared to PGD-AT (Madry, 2017), the improvement is most dramatic on DeepFool (+21.69 points) and C&W (+17.35 points). TRADES-2.0 (Zhang et al., 2019) achieves the highest natural accuracy (82.80%) but is outperformed on every adversarial metric by the proposed method. AWP (Wu et al., 2020) and MART (Wang et al., 2019) are consistently outperformed across all attack types, with gaps of 6–12 points on average. The method's natural accuracy (80.43%) is slightly below TRADES-2.0 and DYNAT but competitive with PGD-AT and AWP, indicating a favorable clean-robustness trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant adversarial defense paradigms (robust training, regularization, purification) by reframing the fundamental linear feature transformation in DNNs as a least-squares optimization problem and replacing it with a robust LAD-based alternative solved via Newton-IRLS. Conceptually, it draws from robust statistics (influence functions, LAD estimation) and optimization-induced deep learning architectures where iterative algorithms are unrolled into network layers, but applies this to the core pattern-matching operation rather than to training objectives or input preprocessing. The authors explicitly position their approach as orthogonal and complementary to existing defenses, with the key distinction that Paradigm 1 requires no parameter modification at all—only a plug-and-play architectural swap controlled by a single hyperparameter per layer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'reprogramming without training' paradigm is practically significant in the era of large pre-trained models where retraining is prohibitively expensive, and the three paradigms offer a useful spectrum from zero-cost to light fine-tuning. However, the clean-accuracy degradation in deeper models (ResNet18) and the strongest results being concentrated on smaller datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10) limit immediate practical impact. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large models (ViTs, LLMs), how it interacts with other defenses in combination, and whether the L∞-norm weakness (where all pixels are perturbed uniformly, making median-based robustness less effective) can be addressed. The influence-function analysis provides a clean theoretical foundation that could inspire further robust architecture design beyond adversarial settings.
