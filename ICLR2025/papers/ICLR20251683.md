## Problem

Existing variational diffusion-based samplers for multi-modal distributions rely on a single Gaussian reference distribution whose variance hyperparameter must be tuned using ground truth samples, making them impractical in settings where such samples are unavailable. This hyperparameter sensitivity causes severe mode collapse and inaccurate recovery of relative mode weights, particularly in high dimensions or when energy barriers between modes are large.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251683/cropped_page5_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a pointwise-evaluable unnormalized target density γ(x) and prior knowledge of the target mode locations (no ground truth samples or target score/gradient required). It produces approximate samples from the target distribution by simulating a learned denoising SDE. A key distinction from competing methods is that LRDS only requires density evaluations of π, whereas baselines like PIS, DDS, and iDEM require the target score during training or sampling.

## Method

LRDS proceeds in three stages: (a) collecting reference samples by running local MALA chains initialized at each known mode location, (b) learning a multi-modal reference distribution π^ref that mirrors the target's mode structure—either as a Gaussian Mixture Model fitted via EM (GMM-LRDS) or as a multi-level Energy-Based Model trained with annealed MCMC for negative sampling (EBM-LRDS)—and (c) running the RDS variational optimization with the Log-Variance divergence, where the learned reference scores replace the intractable target scores in the denoising drift. The core insight is that a reference distribution sharing the target's multi-modality eliminates the need for a manually tuned isotropic variance and prevents mode collapse, since the reference process already encodes the correct number and location of modes. GMM-LRDS provides analytical reference scores at low computational cost, while EBM-LRDS handles non-Gaussian geometries (e.g., ring-shaped or compactly supported modes) at higher cost.

## Results

On the 16/32/64-dimensional bimodal Gaussian mixture, GMM-LRDS achieves absolute mode weight estimation errors of 1.7%±0.6%, 2.7%±0.8%, and 4.1%±0.6% respectively, outperforming all competing methods. On the 1D φ⁴ field system (d=32), GMM-LRDS is the only method that avoids mode collapse and recovers the correct relative mode weights across varying local-field parameters. On the 2D Checkerboard distribution (8 unbalanced modes), LRDS is the only method producing samples close to the exact target, while all other approaches fail.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GMM-LRDS decisively outperforms all baselines on the high-dimensional bimodal Gaussian mixture: at d=32 and d=64, LV-PIS (33.2%), LV-DDS (31.5%), and iDEM (66.7%) all suffer mode collapse, while GMM-LRDS maintains errors below 5%. PDDS is competitive at d=16 (0.8%) but collapses at d=32 (66.7%) and fails numerically at d=64. Among annealed MCMC methods, SMC (11–16%) and RE (16–17%) are stable but consistently 4–6× worse than GMM-LRDS. LV-DIS is the most robust among variational diffusion baselines (~16% error) but still 4× worse than GMM-LRDS. LV-CMCD is highly unstable (16–50% error depending on dimension). On the φ⁴ model, all competing methods suffer mode collapse while GMM-LRDS recovers correct weights. On the Checkerboard distribution, LRDS is the sole method producing accurate samples, with all others failing to capture the 8-mode structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the variational reference-based diffusion sampling framework of Richter et al. (2023), which itself generalizes the Path Integral Sampler (Zhang & Chen, 2022) and Denoising Diffusion Sampler (Vargas et al., 2023a) by allowing arbitrary reference distributions rather than fixed Gaussians. The key departure is that all prior methods in this lineage (PIS, DDS, DIS, CMCD) use a fixed isotropic Gaussian reference whose variance hyperparameter σ must be tuned with ground truth samples, whereas LRDS learns the reference distribution from mode-location priors using either GMMs (lightweight) or multi-level EBMs (expressive, drawing on cooperative training strategies from Gao et al., 2021 and Zhu et al., 2024). The paper also positions itself against adaptive diffusion-based samplers (iDEM, PDDS) and annealed MCMC (SMC, Replica Exchange), showing that the learned-reference approach avoids their respective failure modes of high-variance losses and mode switching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for anyone using variational diffusion-based samplers on multimodal targets: the paper demonstrates that the σ hyperparameter in PIS/DDS is not a minor tuning detail but a critical one whose mis-specification causes mode collapse, and that learning the reference from mode locations is a principled fix that eliminates this dependency. The main limitation is the computational overhead of pre-training the reference process (EM for GMMs, annealed MCMC for EBMs), which adds a non-trivial cost before the variational optimization even begins. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-Euclidean spaces (the authors suggest protein Boltzmann distributions as a test case) and whether the mode-location prior can be relaxed when modes are not known a priori.
