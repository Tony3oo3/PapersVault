## Problem

Offline alignment methods like DPO and RLHF suffer from overoptimization, where the learned policy degrades as it overfits to an imperfect reward model and drifts off the data manifold. KL-regularization, the standard remedy, is provably too weak to prevent this distribution shift, and existing pessimistic alternatives either require impractical convexity assumptions on the policy class or are restricted to linearly parameterized policies. The paper asks whether a simple, general-purpose offline alignment algorithm can be provably robust to overoptimization with sample complexity based on single-policy concentrability.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a reference policy π_ref (typically an SFT language model), an offline preference dataset of n tuples (x, a_+, a_-) generated by sampling pairs from π_ref and labeling via the Bradley-Terry model, and a regularization parameter β > 0. It outputs a single aligned policy π̂ by solving a modified DPO objective. The key structural detail is that the objective clips the link-function argument to [-2R_max, 2R_max] to handle unbounded density ratios, and the policy class Π must satisfy realizability of the optimal mixed-χ²-regularized policy and bounded implicit reward range V_max.

## Method

χPO is a one-line modification to DPO: the logarithmic link function log(π(a|x)/π_ref(a|x)) is replaced with φ(z) = z + log(z), which is the derivative of the mixed f-divergence f_χmix(z) = ½(z-1)² + z log z (i.e., χ² + KL). The χ² component enforces pessimism by penalizing off-manifold drift more aggressively than KL, while the KL component provides the barrier property (0 ∉ dom(f′)) needed for the DPO reparameterization trick to apply. This yields a purely policy-based objective that implicitly optimizes a χ²-regularized reward, achieving single-policy concentrability guarantees—the gold standard in offline RL—under only policy realizability and bounded implicit rewards. The optimal policy under this regularization has a heavy-tailed density ratio bounded by 1 + R_max/β (vs. exp(R_max/β) for KL), which is the mechanism that decouples overoptimization from bias and enables a 1/√n regret rate where DPO is stuck at 1/log n.

## Results

On the TL;DR summarization task, χPO achieves a higher average winrate than DPO across all tested regularization parameters β and training epochs. At the standard DPO setting (β=0.05, 1 epoch), χPO reaches 56.5% winrate versus DPO's 55.8%. The performance gap widens dramatically in the overoptimization regime: at β=0.005 with 4 epochs, χPO maintains 51.6% winrate while DPO collapses to 0.5%. Theoretically, χPO achieves single-policy concentrability guarantees (sample complexity scaling with C^{π*} rather than max_π C^π), making it the first simple offline alignment algorithm provably robust to overoptimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares χPO against DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) as the sole empirical baseline on the TL;DR summarization task. χPO wins in every tested configuration, with the advantage growing as the overoptimization regime intensifies (lower β, more epochs). At the standard setting (β=0.05, 1 epoch), the gap is modest (56.5% vs. 55.8%), but at β=0.005 with 4 epochs, χPO retains 51.6% winrate while DPO degrades to 0.5%—a 51-point gap. This empirical result aligns with the paper's theoretical analysis (Figure 1), which shows that DPO suffers an exponentially slower convergence rate (1/log n) compared to χPO's 1/√n rate, because DPO's KL-regularization forces a tradeoff where small β causes overoptimization and large β causes high bias, while χPO's χ²-regularization allows small β to simultaneously prevent overoptimization and keep bias low.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO framework (Rafailov et al., 2023) by replacing the logarithmic link function with a new one (z + log z) that implicitly implements χ²-regularization, drawing on the f-divergence reparameterization machinery of Wang et al. (2023a). It is positioned against the offline RL pessimism literature (Liu et al., 2020; Jin et al., 2021; Xie et al., 2021), which provides the theoretical template of single-policy concentrability but has not been efficiently implementable for language models. A key departure is the demonstration that the recently proposed DPO+SFT objective (Liu et al., 2024; Cen et al., 2024; Fisch et al., 2024) fails to achieve single-policy concentrability without restrictive convexity assumptions on the policy class, and that KL-regularization alone is provably too weak to prevent overoptimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-line modification to DPO that yields provable robustness to overoptimization is practically appealing and could be widely adopted if it scales, potentially replacing the current default of KL-regularized DPO in offline alignment pipelines. However, the empirical validation is preliminary—limited to a single summarization dataset (TL;DR), a single baseline (DPO), and small models—so the practical impact at LLM scale remains unproven. Open questions include extending the guarantees to general (non-Bradley-Terry) preference models, where the paper shows single-policy concentrability is impossible and a weaker local coverage condition is needed, and adapting the χ²-regularization framework to multi-step MDPs beyond the contextual bandit formulation.
