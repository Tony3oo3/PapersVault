## Problem

Existing RAG indexing methods organize corpus data based on either semantic similarity (e.g., RAPTOR) or entity-based relatedness (e.g., GraphRAG, HippoRAG), but never both simultaneously. The paper quantifies this gap by showing that similarity-only and relatedness-only clustering each cover only ~14–19% of gold pairwise connections in multihop QA corpora, and that 50% of correct similarity connections are absent from relatedness predictions (and vice versa), indicating that neither perspective alone yields sufficient knowledge synthesis for multihop reasoning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a corpus of text chunks and a user query. It produces a unified retrieval pool that flattens all nodes from two independent trees (similarity and relatedness), including original text chunks, entity-grouped proposition aggregates, and recursive summaries at multiple hierarchy levels. The pool is roughly 3× the size of RAPTOR's, and top-20 candidates are retrieved via an embedding model (text-embedding-3-small) or a reranker (BM25, ColBERTv2) before being passed to GPT-4o for answer generation. Proposition and entity extraction is performed by a fine-tuned Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 model, keeping inference-time LLM calls limited to summarization and final QA.

## Method

SIRERAG builds two independent trees over the same corpus. The similarity tree follows RAPTOR: GMM-based soft clustering on chunk embeddings with recursive LLM summarization at each upper level (4 levels total). The relatedness tree first extracts fine-grained propositions and their associated named entities from each chunk using a Mistral-7B model fine-tuned on Distill-SynthKG-generated data (originally produced by Llama-3-70B-Instruct); propositions sharing an entity are concatenated into 'proposition aggregates' that act as pseudo-documents, and RAPTOR's GMM clustering plus recursive summarization pipeline is then applied on top of these aggregates. Both trees are flattened into a single retrieval pool so that any node—regardless of origin tree or hierarchy level—competes uniformly during retrieval. The key design choice is using entity-grouped proposition aggregates rather than raw chunks to carry relatedness, which reduces noise and redundancy while naturally inducing soft clustering (a proposition can appear in multiple aggregates). Cross-tree interaction was explored but rejected because it did not improve performance while increasing complexity.

## Results

SIRERAG achieves an average F1 of 65.83 (GPT-4o) and 65.88 (GPT-3.5-Turbo) across MuSiQue, 2WikiMultiHopQA, and HotpotQA, outperforming the strongest baseline RAPTOR (61.21 avg F1) by approximately 4.6% and HippoRAG (64.03 avg F1) by 1.8%. On MuSiQue, SIRERAG reaches 53.08 F1, surpassing RAPTOR (49.09) and HippoRAG (43.78). The method also improves reranking-based retrieval: SIRERAG+ColBERTv2 achieves 63.93 average F1 versus 59.90 for ColBERTv2 alone, and SIRERAG+BM25 reaches 63.09 versus 55.26 for BM25 alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RAPTOR (similarity-only indexing), SIRERAG wins on all three datasets in average F1 (65.83 vs. 61.21), with the largest gap on MuSiQue (+3.99 F1) and HotpotQA (+3.40 F1), confirming that adding a relatedness tree over a similarity tree yields consistent gains. Against HippoRAG (relatedness-only via knowledge graph), SIRERAG wins on MuSiQue (+9.30 F1) and HotpotQA (+2.19 F1) but loses on 2Wiki (−6.07 F1), where HippoRAG's entity-centric design is particularly well-suited; overall SIRERAG still leads by 1.8% average F1. Against GraphRAG, SIRERAG dominates by a wide margin (65.83 vs. 30.15 average F1), as GraphRAG is designed for corpus-level summarization rather than fine-grained multihop QA. When paired with rerankers, SIRERAG's retrieval pool improves ColBERTv2 by +4.03 average F1 (63.93 vs. 59.90) and BM25 by +7.83 average F1 (63.09 vs. 55.26), while RAPTOR's pool only helps BM25 on MuSiQue and degrades performance elsewhere. Against the iterative self-ask method, SIRERAG's pool adds +3.62 average F1 (62.77 vs. 59.15), demonstrating broad applicability beyond single-pass retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIRERAG builds directly on RAPTOR's recursive summarization tree for similarity-based indexing and on the entity/proposition extraction pipelines from Distill-SynthKG and Dense X Retrieval for relatedness-based indexing. It departs from prior RAG indexing methods (RAPTOR, GraphRAG, HippoRAG) by explicitly constructing and unifying two separate trees—one organized by semantic similarity and one by entity-linked proposition aggregates—whereas each prior method addresses only one of these perspectives. The proposition-aggregate mechanism, which groups fine-grained factual statements via shared entities before applying RAPTOR-style recursive summarization, is the paper's core architectural novelty relative to both the similarity-only and relatedness-only baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear quantitative argument (Section 3 coverage analysis) that neither similarity nor relatedness alone captures more than ~19% of the pairwise connections needed for multihop reasoning, motivating the dual-tree design. Practically, SIRERAG serves as a drop-in upstream indexing step that improves existing rerankers (BM25, ColBERTv2) by up to 7.8% F1, making it broadly applicable without requiring changes to downstream retrieval or generation. Limitations include the dependence on LLM-based proposition and entity extraction (a potential quality bottleneck), the roughly 3× larger retrieval pool compared to RAPTOR, and the modest absolute gains (~1.9% average F1) that may not justify the added pipeline complexity in all deployment settings. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-multihop or long-form generation tasks and whether the proposition extraction step can be made more robust without expensive LLM calls.
