## Problem

Go-Explore, a powerful archive-based exploration algorithm, requires carefully hand-designed heuristics at all three stages (state selection, action selection, and archive filtering) to guide exploration, making it infeasible to apply to new domains without extensive domain expertise. Meanwhile, foundation model agents struggle in environments requiring deep exploration over extended time horizons, as they lack a mechanism to reliably return to promising previously-discovered states. This paper addresses both gaps by replacing all handcrafted heuristics with the intelligence of pretrained foundation models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes environment observations (text descriptions or 6×6 grid images for BabyAI, free-form room descriptions for TextWorld, or numeric tuples for Game of 24), a task/goal description, the current state archive, and a state-conditional action history tracking previously attempted actions from each archived state. At each of the three Go-Explore stages, the FM is queried with a structured prompt and returns a specific decision: a numerical index selecting a state from the archive, a concrete action (parsed from JSON or free-form text), or a binary interestingness judgment for archive updates. The final output is the best discovered solution trajectory, which can be used for downstream imitation learning or in-context learning.

## Method

INTELLIGENT GO-EXPLORE (IGE) reimagines the classic Go-Explore loop by querying a foundation model at all three stages instead of using handcrafted heuristics. For state selection, the FM is shown the full archive and asked to pick the most promising state to return to, replacing visitation-frequency or progress-based sampling. For action selection, the FM is given the current state, the state-conditional action history (to prevent repeating previously tested actions and avoid recency bias), and prompted to output the next action, replacing random sampling. For archive filtering, the FM judges whether each newly discovered state is 'interestingly new'—novel and relevant to the task—before adding it to the archive, replacing domain-specific cell representations. The approach is agnostic to the underlying FM agent strategy (zero-shot, few-shot, or chain-of-thought prompting) and leverages the FM's internalized notions of interestingness to recognize serendipitous discoveries that could not have been anticipated by a human designer. Unlike Tree of Thoughts, IGE's archive is grounded in real environment states rather than abstract hallucinated thoughts, and unlike classic Go-Explore, it requires no domain-specific heuristic design.

## Results

On Game of 24, IGE achieves 100% success rate within 150 operations, 70.8% faster than the next-best baseline (DFS). On BabyAI's hardest task (Put Next To), IGE reaches 84% success with only 250 environment steps, outperforming the RL-trained GLAM baseline (which required 1.5M steps) by a wide margin. On TextWorld's Cooking Game, IGE achieves 92% success, and on Coin Collector it is the only method that finds any solution at all, where all baselines completely fail. Across all three environments, IGE consistently outperforms Naive LLM, ReAct, and Reflexion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IGE decisively outperforms all three LLM agent baselines (Naive LLM, ReAct, Reflexion) across every environment. On Game of 24, IGE reaches 100% while Reflexion plateaus at 83% and Naive LLM at 70%; notably, all LLM baselines are eventually beaten by the unintelligent DFS graph search, highlighting the need for diverse action selection that IGE provides. On BabyAI's Put Next To task, IGE (84%) more than doubles ReAct (48%) and vastly exceeds Naive LLM (12%), with the gap growing as task difficulty increases. On TextWorld's Cooking Game, IGE (92%) leads ReAct (56%) by 36 percentage points, and Reflexion actually underperforms ReAct, likely due to context-length issues in long-horizon episodes. On Coin Collector, IGE is the sole method to find the solution, exhibiting emergent BFS-like search behavior, while all other methods exhaust the environment horizon. Compared to Classic Go-Explore with hand-designed heuristics, IGE achieves 100% vs. 38% on Game of 24, 84% vs. 4% on BabyAI, and 92% vs. 0% on TextWorld, demonstrating that FM intelligence at all three stages is critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reimagines the Go-Explore framework (Ecoffet et al., 2021a,b), which achieved superhuman performance on hard-exploration problems but required carefully hand-designed heuristics at each stage (state selection, action sampling, archive filtering), by replacing all three heuristic components with queries to a giant pretrained foundation model. It is complementary to existing FM agent strategies like ReAct and Reflexion, which the paper uses as baselines, and departs from Tree of Thoughts by maintaining a grounded state archive rather than an abstract thought tree, enabling the agent to jump around the search space rather than following a fixed tree structure. The approach also contrasts with Latent Go-Explore, which learns a latent representation to avoid hand-crafted cell definitions, by instead leveraging the FM's internalized notions of interestingness directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IGE removes the primary practical barrier to applying Go-Explore—the need for domain-specific heuristic design—making the framework applicable to virtually any problem representable in an FM's context, including serendipitous discoveries that could not be anticipated ahead of time. Key limitations include the restriction to deterministic environments, linear archive growth (mitigated but not solved by filtering), and dependence on the underlying FM's capabilities and potential biases. Open questions include extension to stochastic environments, scaling to much larger state spaces (e.g., NetHack), integration with MCTS or imitation learning on discovered trajectories, and whether the FM's interestingness judgments can be formalized or fine-tuned for specific domains.
