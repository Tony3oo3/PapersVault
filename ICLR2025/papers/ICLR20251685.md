## Problem

Existing large-scale zero-shot TTS systems face a fundamental trade-off: autoregressive models offer diverse prosody but suffer from poor robustness and lack duration controllability, while non-autoregressive models require explicit text-speech alignment supervision and phone-level duration prediction, resulting in complex pipelines and less natural, more standardized speech. No prior system achieves fully non-autoregressive generation without alignment or duration prediction while maintaining human-level quality.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a text sequence (tokenized via G2P or BPE), a prompt speech clip (used to extract both semantic and acoustic tokens as in-context conditioning), and a target total duration (predicted by a flow-matching duration model or set manually). It produces a 24 kHz speech waveform reconstructed from 12-layer RVQ acoustic tokens via a Vocos-based decoder. Notably, the output length is explicitly controllable at inference time, allowing the user to specify speech tempo without any per-phoneme duration model.

## Method

MaskGCT is a two-stage fully non-autoregressive system using the mask-and-predict paradigm. The first stage (T2S) is a Llama-style bidirectional transformer (SwiGLU, RoPE, adaptive RMSNorm) that predicts masked semantic tokens—extracted from W2v-BERT 2.0 hidden states via a VQ-VAE with an 8,192-entry codebook—conditioned on text tokens and prompt semantic tokens prepended as an in-context prefix. The second stage (S2A) is a SoundStorm-style masked generative model that predicts 12-layer RVQ acoustic tokens layer-by-layer (coarse-to-fine), conditioned on the semantic tokens and prompt acoustic tokens. A key design choice is training a VQ-VAE semantic codec rather than using k-means clustering, which preserves prosodic information critical for tonal languages (e.g., Chinese) and improves downstream acoustic reconstruction. Both stages are trained with classifier-free guidance (prompt dropped at 15% probability) and decoded via iterative parallel remasking with confidence-based scheduling, requiring only 25–50 inference steps regardless of speech length.

## Results

MaskGCT achieves SIM-O of 0.687, 0.717, and 0.774 on LibriSpeech test-clean, SeedTTS test-en, and SeedTTS test-zh respectively, reaching human-level speaker similarity (within ±0.03 of ground truth). It attains WER of 2.634, 2.623, and 2.273 on the three benchmarks and CMOS of +0.10, +0.03, and +0.05, indicating human-level naturalness. MaskGCT also achieves the best FSD (0.886, 0.188, 0.106) and SMOS (4.27, 4.24, 4.09) among all compared systems, with the advantage being most pronounced on the SeedTTS benchmarks where it outperforms the best baseline by a large margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On LibriSpeech test-clean, MaskGCT outperforms NaturalSpeech 3 (the strongest baseline) on SIM-O (0.687 vs. 0.67) and SMOS (4.27 vs. 4.26), but trails it on WER (2.634 vs. 1.94), FSD (0.886 vs. 0.786), and CMOS (0.10 vs. 0.16). On SeedTTS test-en and test-zh, MaskGCT dominates all baselines across every metric: it beats CosyVoice by +0.074 and +0.024 in SIM-O, by 1.46 and 1.82 in WER, and by +0.72 and +0.55 in SMOS, respectively. Against AR-based systems (VALL-E, VoiceCraft, XTTS-v2), MaskGCT consistently achieves substantially higher SIM-O (e.g., 0.717 vs. 0.470 for VoiceCraft on SeedTTS test-en) and lower WER, confirming the robustness advantage of the masked generative paradigm. The paper also shows that replacing MaskGCT's T2S with an AR model (AR+SoundStorm) degrades CMOS by 0.12, 0.08, and 0.37 on the three benchmarks, further validating the fully non-autoregressive design.

## Relation to Prior Work

MaskGCT extends the masked generative transformer paradigm (MaskGIT, MAGVIT-v2) from vision and audio to zero-shot TTS, building directly on SoundStorm's masked generative approach for acoustic token prediction and the two-stage semantic-to-acoustic decomposition popularized by VALL-E. Its key departure is making both stages fully non-autoregressive: the text-to-semantic stage replaces the AR language model used in VALL-E, SoundStorm, and CosyVoice with a bidirectional masked generative transformer using in-context learning, while the semantic-to-acoustic stage follows SoundStorm's layer-by-layer masked prediction. Additionally, it introduces a VQ-VAE trained on W2v-BERT 2.0 SSL features as a semantic codec, departing from the k-means discretization used in prior two-stage systems, motivated by reduced information loss particularly for tonal languages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MaskGCT demonstrates that a fully non-autoregressive TTS system can achieve human-level naturalness (positive CMOS), similarity, and intelligibility without text-speech alignment or phone-level duration prediction, while also offering explicit total-duration controllability — a capability AR systems inherently lack. The VQ-VAE semantic codec finding (WER dropping from 11.4 to 2.1 on Chinese vs. k-means) highlights a practical concern for tonal-language TTS that prior work overlooked. Open questions include the model's limited robustness in speech editing tasks (the authors note a fill-in-mask training paradigm may be needed), degraded cross-lingual translation quality for non-English target languages, and whether the masked generative advantage over AR models persists at even larger scales or with different SSL backbones.
