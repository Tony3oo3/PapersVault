## Problem

Existing singular subspace perturbation bounds for rectangular matrices perturbed by Gaussian noise (via Davis-Kahan-Wedin or O'Rourke et al.) scale with √m, the number of rows, when bounding the perturbation to the top-k right-singular vectors of an m×d matrix with m > d. This paper asks whether one can obtain bounds that depend on the smaller dimension d instead, which is critical in applications where the number of datapoints m far exceeds the number of features d.

## Input / Output

The method takes a rectangular matrix A ∈ R^{m×d} (d ≤ m) with singular values σ₁ ≥ … ≥ σ_d, a noise scale T > 0, and a rank parameter k, under the constraint that the top-k+1 singular value gaps satisfy σ_i − σ_{i+1} ≥ 8√T√m·log(1/δ) for i ∈ [k]. It produces an expected Frobenius-norm bound on the perturbation to the matrix VΓᵀΓVᵀ (a generalization of the projection onto the top-k right-singular subspace), specifically E[‖V̂ΓᵀΓV̂ᵀ − VΓᵀΓVᵀ‖_F²] ≤ O(Σ_{i≤k} Σ_{j>i} (γ_i²−γ_j²)²/(σ_i−σ_j)²)·T, which for the subspace recovery case (γ_i = 1 for i ≤ k) simplifies to O(√d/(σ_k−σ_{k+1})·√T) in expectation.

## Method

The core technique models the Gaussian perturbation as a matrix-valued Brownian motion Φ(t) = A + B(t), whose entries evolve as independent standard Brownian motions, inducing the Dyson-Bessel process on the singular values and right-singular vectors of Φ(t). The authors define a stochastic process Ψ(t) = V(t)ΓᵀΓV(t)ᵀ tracking the projection onto the orthogonal orbit O_{Γ²}, then apply Itô's lemma to derive the stochastic differential dΨ(t) in terms of the SDEs governing the right-singular vectors. The key insight is that in the stochastic setting, higher-order matrix derivative terms that appear in deterministic Taylor expansions vanish because Brownian increments at each infinitesimal time step are independent, eliminating the dependence on m that arises from those terms. They bound the inverse singular-value-gap coefficients c_{ij}(t) using Weyl's inequality combined with Gaussian spectral-norm concentration, then integrate the stochastic derivative over [0,T] and apply Cauchy-Schwarz to obtain the final sum-of-squares bound. This yields an improvement of √m/√d over prior deterministic and random-matrix bounds for the right-singular subspace perturbation.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 2.2) bounds the expected squared Frobenius distance between the orbit projection VΓᵀΓVᵀ and its perturbed counterpart as O(ΣᵢΣⱼ (γᵢ²−γⱼ²)²/(σᵢ−σⱼ)²)·T. For subspace recovery (Corollary 2.3), when top-k singular value gaps are Ω(σₖ−σₖ₊₁), the expected Frobenius distance satisfies E[‖V̂ₖV̂ₖᵀ − VₖVₖᵀ‖_F] ≤ O(√d/(σₖ−σₖ₊₁))·√T. For rank-k covariance approximation (Corollary 2.4), the expected squared Frobenius error is bounded by O(d‖Σₖ‖_F² + k(d−k)(σₖ²/(σₖ−σₖ₊₁))²)·T. The key improvement over all prior bounds is replacing the dependence on √m (number of rows/datapoints) with √d (number of columns/features).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's bounds strictly improve upon all three prior Gaussian-perturbation results by a factor of √m/√d (or √k·√m/√d for the Davis–Kahan–Wedin and O'Rourke 2018 bounds). Specifically, the Davis–Kahan–Wedin sine-Theta theorem gives O(√(km)/(σₖ−σₖ₊₁))√T w.h.p., which the paper improves to O(√d/(σₖ−σₖ₊₁))√T in expectation — a factor of √k·√m/√d better. O'Rourke et al. (2018) give O(√(mk)/σₖ)√T w.h.p., improved by a factor of √k·√m/√d. O'Rourke et al. (2023) give O(√(m√k)/σₖ)√T w.h.p., improved by a factor of √m/√d. The improvement is most significant in the common regime where m ≫ d (many datapoints, few features), where the factor √m/√d can be very large. The paper also notes that its bounds hold in expectation rather than w.h.p., and require the singular value gaps to be Ω(√m·√T·log(1/δ)), which is satisfied for matrices with exponentially decaying singular values.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Dyson-Bessel process framework—originally developed for symmetric matrices by Dyson (1962), Norris et al. (1986), and Bru (1989), and applied to symmetric-matrix differential privacy by Mangoubi & Vishnoi (2022, 2023)—to the rectangular matrix setting. The key conceptual departure from prior work is that the stochastic calculus approach (Itô's lemma applied to the SDEs governing singular vector evolution) eliminates higher-order Taylor expansion terms that plague deterministic perturbation analysis, yielding a √d dependence instead of the √m dependence inherent in Davis-Kahan-Wedin and O'Rourke et al. bounds. The paper positions itself as a strict improvement over both the classical deterministic sine-Theta theorem and the more recent random-matrix perturbation results of O'Rourke et al. (2018, 2023), which still carry √m factors when applied to right-singular-vector subspaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The √m/√d improvement is practically significant in the common regime where the number of datapoints m far exceeds the number of features d, directly tightening utility guarantees for the Gaussian mechanism in differential privacy and for subspace recovery in high-dimensional statistics. However, the results require singular value gaps of Ω(√m), which may fail for matrices with slowly decaying spectra, and the analysis is restricted to Gaussian perturbations—both are acknowledged as important open questions. The empirical simulations confirm tightness in m and d but suggest the k-dependence may be loose, pointing to room for further refinement.
