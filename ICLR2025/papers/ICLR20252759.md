## Problem

Pre-trained cross-modal retrieval models (e.g., CLIP, BLIP) assume queries follow the source-domain distribution, but real-world queries arrive from shifted distributions due to diverse user preferences, cultural backgrounds, or corruptions. This 'query shift' simultaneously diminishes intra-modality uniformity and amplifies the inter-modality gap, undermining the common space. Existing test-time adaptation methods are designed for unimodal recognition and cannot handle the compounded intra- and inter-modality distortions or the heavy prediction noise inherent to retrieval (1/N vs. 1/K random-correct probability).

## Input / Output

The method consumes an online stream of mini-batches (size 64) of queries in one modality (image or text) alongside a fixed gallery set in the other modality, plus a frozen pre-trained source model (CLIP or BLIP). Only the Layer Normalization parameters within the query-specific encoder are updated via AdamW; the gallery encoder and all other weights remain frozen. The output is a refined retrieval ranking (query-to-gallery association scores) computed after each adaptation step, with a fixed temperature τ=0.02 and uniformity parameter t=10 across all experiments.

## Method

TCR introduces a query prediction refinement module that replaces the full-gallery softmax with predictions computed only against the nearest-neighbor gallery candidate per query, avoiding under/overfitting caused by variable gallery sizes. From the resulting query-candidate pairs, it selects the 30% with the smallest SI criterion (a composite of modality gap and intra-modality compactness) as pseudo source-domain-like data, from which it estimates the source modality gap Δ_S and a self-adaptive entropy threshold E_m. The joint objective then combines three losses: (1) an intra-modality uniformity loss that contrasts each query against the batch center to enlarge scatter, (2) an inter-modality gap loss that rectifies the target-domain gap to the estimated source gap in a non-monotonic manner (preserving the well-aligned common space rather than eliminating the gap entirely), and (3) a noise-robust adaptation loss that applies entropy minimization weighted by a self-adaptive confidence score, excluding high-entropy predictions from the gradient. This design explicitly addresses both the uniformity collapse and gap amplification caused by query shift while preventing overfitting to noisy retrieval predictions.

## Results

On the COCO-C benchmark under image-modality query shift (maximum severity), TCR achieves 59.1% Recall@1 (BLIP ViT-B/16) and 62.0% Recall@1 (BLIP ViT-L/16), outperforming the best baseline EATA by 9.8 and 7.6 points respectively. Under text-modality query shift, TCR reaches 42.3% and 44.4% Recall@1, a more modest but consistent gain of ~1–1.5 points over EATA. On the Query-Gallery Shift setting (Table 3), TCR achieves 67.0% average Recall@1 with BLIP ViT-B/16 across six cross-domain benchmarks, surpassing the strongest baseline DeYO (64.2%) by 2.8 points, with the largest gains on out-of-domain transfers (e.g., Base2Fashion: 23.6% vs. 12.2% TR@1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TCR outperforms all five TTA baselines (Tent, EATA, SAR, READ, DeYO) across every dataset and evaluation setting reported. The margin is largest under image-modality query shift: on COCO-C (max severity), TCR gains +9.8 R@1 over EATA (59.1 vs. 49.3, BLIP ViT-B/16) and +11.0 over Tent (59.1 vs. 43.0), with the gap widening on severe corruptions like Zoom (+25.3 over EATA) and Pixel (+36.2 over EATA). Under text-modality query shift the advantage narrows to ~1–1.5 R@1 (42.3 vs. 41.5 for ViT-B/16), reflecting that character/word-level perturbations are less disruptive to the common space. On the Query-Gallery Shift setting, TCR consistently improves over the base model across all six cross-domain benchmarks, whereas Tent and READ often degrade below the base model as gallery size grows (e.g., Base2Fashion TR@1: Tent 5.6 vs. base 8.5). On the ReID benchmarks (CUHK-PEDES, ICFG-PEDES), TCR achieves 39.9% average R@1 versus 37.8% for the best baseline (EATA/SAR/DeYO), a +2.1 point gain. Notably, Tent frequently underperforms the unadapted base model, confirming the paper's claim that vanilla entropy-minimization TTA is ill-suited to the retrieval task's large candidate set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time adaptation (TTA) paradigm—originally developed for unimodal recognition tasks (Tent, EATA, SAR, DeYO)—to the cross-modal retrieval setting, where the challenge is fundamentally different due to the large gallery set and the dual nature of distribution shift affecting both intra-modality uniformity and inter-modality gap. It builds on the modality gap analysis of Liang et al. (2022) and the entropy-minimization TTA framework of Wang et al. (2021), but departs from both by introducing a query prediction refinement module (to handle the retrieval-specific noise from large candidate sets) and a joint objective that explicitly manipulates uniformity and gap rather than relying solely on entropy. The paper also positions itself against offline domain adaptation methods for cross-modal retrieval (pseudo-labeling, domain alignment, prototype-based), which require full target-domain access and cannot handle online query streams.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is one of the first systematic studies of query shift in cross-modal retrieval, providing both a new problem formulation and benchmarks (COCO-C, Flickr-C) that the field can use to evaluate robustness under realistic query distributions. The finding that existing TTA methods can actually degrade performance on large gallery sets (worse than the unadapted base model) is practically important for search-engine and retrieval applications. The untrained TCR variant (Appendix C), which achieves gains over EATA without any parameter updates by simply scaling uniformity and rectifying the modality gap, suggests the core geometric insights may be more transferable than the adaptation mechanism itself. Open questions include whether the approach extends to streaming/gallery-growing settings, additional modalities beyond image-text, and whether the normalization-layer-only updates are sufficient for deeper distribution shifts.
