## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary 3D instance segmentation methods either overfit to base categories during training or generate class-agnostic masks and rely on 2D projection for classification, both of which disregard semantic information during mask generation. This leads to poor generalization to novel categories and sub-optimal mask quality, as the 3D mask generator is never directly aligned with language instructions.

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes a 3D point cloud (voxelized at 2 cm), corresponding multi-view 2D images (for projecting CLIP features via camera pose), and instance-level mask annotations—critically without any class labels. At inference, a free-form text prompt (category name, visual question, attribute description, or functional description) is encoded by CLIP's text encoder and used as a binary classifier. The output is a set of 3D instance masks with per-instance class probabilities, where 150 object queries are initialized via farthest point sampling and refined through a 4-layer transformer decoder.

## Method

SOLE builds on Mask3D's transformer mask-prediction paradigm and introduces two architectural components: a backbone feature ensemble that concatenates projected 2D CLIP features (from OpenSeg) with 3D MinkowskiUNet features at multiple resolutions, and a Cross-Modality Decoder (CMD) that injects CLIP textual features into instance queries via cross-attention. The key training innovation is three hierarchical multimodal associations used as semantic supervision without class labels: Mask-Visual Association (pooled per-point CLIP features per mask), Mask-Caption Association (captions generated by DeCap from CLIP visual features, then encoded by CLIP text encoder), and Mask-Entity Association (noun phrases extracted from captions, aggregated via cosine-similarity-based multimodal attention). Training uses Hungarian matching with a composite cost incorporating all three associations plus dice and BCE mask losses, while inference combines mask-feature and CLIP-feature classification probabilities via a soft geometric mean (τ=0.667) to balance geometric and semantic confidence.

## Results

On ScanNetv2 (closed-set, 2cm voxel), SOLE achieves 44.4 AP, 62.2 AP50, and 71.4 AP25, surpassing the best mask-training baseline Open3DIS (31.3/42.4/47.8) by +13.1/+19.8/+23.6 points respectively. On ScanNet200 (closed-set), SOLE reaches 20.1 AP, outperforming Open3DIS (18.6) and OpenMask3D (15.4). In the cross-domain open-set setting (ScanNet200 → Replica), SOLE achieves 24.7 AP, a +9.8 improvement over Open3DIS. On the ScanRefer 3D visual grounding benchmark, SOLE (zero-shot, trained only on ScanNetv2 masks) attains 25.2 ACC@25 and 22.6 ACC@50, outperforming the generalist 3D-LLM model (21.2 ACC@25).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOLE decisively outperforms all three mask-training baselines on ScanNetv2: OpenIns3D (Huang et al., 2023) reports only 28.7 AP50/38.9 AP25, OpenMask3D (Takmaz et al., 2023) reaches 31.0 AP/39.5 AP50, and Open3DIS (Nguyen et al., 2024) achieves 31.3 AP/42.4 AP50/47.8 AP25, while SOLE attains 44.4/62.2/71.4 — a gap of 13+ AP points over the strongest baseline. Against the fully-supervised Mask3D upper bound (Schult et al., 2022; 55.2 AP), SOLE closes the gap to ~11 AP points despite using no class labels during training. On the ScanRefer 3D visual grounding task, SOLE (zero-shot) beats the specialist baselines VoteRand (10.0 ACC@25), SCRC (18.7), and One-stage (20.4), and surpasses the generalist 3D-LLM (Hong et al., 2023; 21.2 ACC@25), though it remains below the fully-supervised ScanRefer specialist (Chen et al., 2020; 41.2 ACC@25). On ScanNet200, SOLE wins on 5 of 6 metrics versus Open3DIS and OpenMask3D, with a slight 0.8-point deficit on APtail. In the cross-domain Replica evaluation, SOLE's 24.7 AP represents a +9.8 gain over Open3DIS (14.9), confirming superior robustness under both domain and category shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

SOLE builds directly on the Mask3D transformer-based mask-prediction paradigm for 3D instance segmentation, augmenting it with projected 2D CLIP features (via OpenSeg) in the backbone and a Cross-Modality Decoder that injects textual features during decoding. It departs from prior OV-3DIS methods (OpenMask3D, OpenIns3D, Open3DIS) that generate class-agnostic masks and defer semantic classification to 2D projection, instead supervising mask generation itself with three hierarchical multimodal associations (mask-visual, mask-caption, mask-entity) derived from CLIP-space captioning (DeCap) and NLP noun-phrase extraction. The soft geometric mean at inference, combining 3D mask features with pooled CLIP features, is a lightweight but effective fusion strategy not present in prior mask-training baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SOLE demonstrates that semantic-aware mask generation from 3D point clouds—without any class labels—can approach fully-supervised performance (44.4% vs. 55.2% AP on ScanNetv2) while additionally responding to free-form language instructions (visual questions, functional descriptions) that category-name-only methods cannot handle. The ablation revealing that mask-visual and mask-caption associations slightly degrade benchmark AP yet are essential for free-form language understanding highlights an unresolved tension between precision on closed-set queries and generalization to open-ended instructions. Open questions include whether 3D-native multimodal foundation models could eliminate the 2D-projection dependency, whether better caption generation (e.g., LLM-based as in SceneVerse) closes the remaining gap, and how the framework scales to larger or outdoor scenes.
