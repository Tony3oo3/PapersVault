## Problem

White-box LLM-generated text detection methods (e.g., Fast-DetectGPT, LogRank) are more efficient and robust than black-box alternatives, but they require full access to model predictive distributions. Proprietary LLMs like GPT-4, Claude-3, and Gemini-1.5 only expose partial observations (top-K token probabilities) through their APIs, making it seemingly impossible to apply white-box methods to the most powerful models. This paper addresses the gap by estimating full distributions from these partial observations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251864/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text passage and the top-K (typically 3–5) token probabilities returned by a proprietary model's Completion API as input. It produces a scalar detection score—conditional probability curvature for Fast-DetectGPT, or entropy/rank/log-rank for other metrics—which is compared against a threshold for binary human-vs-machine classification. A key design choice is that the estimated distribution is parameterized by rank position rather than token identity, since the detection metrics depend only on probability values, not on which specific tokens occupy each rank. The rank list size M (100–1000) is much smaller than the full vocabulary, as low-probability tokens contribute negligibly to the metric.

## Method

Glimpse estimates the full probability distribution over ranks from the observed top-K probabilities using three constrained estimation algorithms. The Geometric approach models the tail as exponential decay p(k) = p_K · λ^(k−K), solving for the decay factor λ under the total-probability constraint (sum equals 1) and monotonic decrease. The Zipfian approach uses p(k) = p_K · (β/(k−K+β))^α, finding α and β by minimizing a loss that balances the probability-mass constraint against regularization toward typical Zipfian values (α≈1, β≈2.7). The MLP approach trains a single-hidden-layer network on open-source model distributions to map top-K log-probabilities to the remaining rank distribution via softmax. The estimated distribution is then substituted into existing white-box metrics (e.g., the conditional probability curvature in Fast-DetectGPT), requiring no changes to the detection framework itself. This enables methods like Fast-DetectGPT, Entropy, Rank, and LogRank to operate on proprietary models, achieving ~0.95 average AUROC across five source models with GPT-3.5 as the scoring model.

## Results

On the Mix3 dataset (XSum + Writing + PubMed), Glimpse with Fast-DetectGPT and GPT-4 (MLP estimation) achieves the best average AUROC of 0.9583 across five source models (ChatGPT, GPT-4, Claude-3 Sonnet, Claude-3 Opus, Gemini-1.5 Pro). Glimpse with Fast-DetectGPT and GPT-3.5 (Geometric) achieves 0.9537, a 51% relative improvement over the best open-source baseline Fast-DetectGPT (GPT-J/Neo-2.7B) at 0.9057. On individual source models, the best Glimpse configurations reach AUROC 0.98 (ChatGPT), 0.97 (GPT-4), 0.98 (Claude-3 Opus), 0.96 (Claude-3 Sonnet), and 0.90 (Gemini-1.5 Pro). Glimpse is also 4.1× faster than DNA-GPT (462s vs. 1911s) and ~10× cheaper in API cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Glimpse with Fast-DetectGPT (GPT-3.5, Geometric) achieves 0.9537 Avg. Mix3 AUROC, outperforming the best open-source baseline Fast-DetectGPT (GPT-J/Neo-2.7B) at 0.9057 by 51% relative to remaining space, and the commercial GPTZero (0.9143 on ChatGPT, 0.9009 on GPT-4) by a wide margin. Against black-box proprietary methods, Glimpse (Fast-Detect, GPT-3.5) surpasses DNA-GPT (GPT-3.5) by 16.4 points (0.9537 vs. 0.7896) and Likelihood (GPT-3.5) by 8.1 points (0.9537 vs. 0.8727) on Avg. Mix3. The simpler LogRank with Glimpse (GPT-3.5) at 0.8683 still beats LogRank (Neo-2.7B) at 0.8347 and DNA-GPT (GPT-3.5) at 0.7896, confirming Glimpse's universality across white-box metrics. Notably, even the smallest proprietary scoring model Babbage (1.3B) with Glimpse (0.9191) outperforms the larger open-source Neo-2.7B (0.9057), suggesting model recency matters more than raw parameter count for detection. Glimpse methods also run 4.1× faster than DNA-GPT and cost ~10× less in API fees, since they only echo input probabilities rather than generating new text.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the white-box zero-shot detection lineage (Fast-DetectGPT's conditional probability curvature, GLTR's Rank/LogRank metrics) by solving a previously intractable access problem: proprietary models only expose top-K token probabilities via API, not full distributions or inner embeddings. Rather than developing new black-box detection algorithms (as DetectGPT or DNA-GPT do), Glimpse estimates the missing portion of the predictive distribution using parametric models (Geometric, Zipfian) or a small MLP, thereby unlocking the superior statistical properties of white-box methods under API-level access. The approach is orthogonal to the detection metric itself, making it a plug-in layer between the API and any distribution-based detector.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the most powerful proprietary LLMs (GPT-3.5, GPT-4) outperform smaller open-source models as universal detectors by 40–51% relative AUROC directly contradicts prior evidence (Mireshghallah et al., 2023) that smaller models are better black-box detectors, suggesting that distribution quality matters more than model size when full distributions are available. Glimpse's simplicity (top-3 probabilities + a geometric fit) makes it immediately deployable, and the 4× speedup over DNA-GPT with 10× lower cost is practically significant. Open questions include whether the approach extends to methods requiring inner embeddings (e.g., PHD), its behavior under adversarial paraphrasing (where performance degrades notably), and whether it generalizes to models lacking Completion API access. The paper also flags potential bias against non-native writers as a broader concern.
