## Problem

Classifier-free guidance (CFG) is essential for improving generation quality and condition alignment in diffusion models, but high guidance scales cause oversaturation, unrealistic color extremes, and simplified compositions. Prior work has not identified the specific mechanism within the CFG update that drives saturation versus quality improvement, leaving practitioners to accept oversaturation as an unavoidable trade-off for using high guidance scales.

## Input / Output

APG is a plug-and-play inference-time modification that operates on the denoised predictions D_θ(z_t, t, y) and D_θ(z_t, t, y_null) at each sampling step of any conditional diffusion model. It takes the same inputs as standard CFG (a text prompt or class label plus a noisy latent z_t) and produces a single guided prediction per step, with no architectural changes or retraining. The method requires converting model outputs (noise, velocity, or EDM-style predictions) to the denoised prediction space before applying the projection, then converting back to the model's native output type for the sampler.

## Method

APG decomposes the CFG update direction ΔD_t into a component parallel to the conditional prediction D_θ(z_t, t, y) and an orthogonal component, showing that the parallel component acts as a gain that pushes pixel values toward extremes (causing saturation) while the orthogonal component drives quality improvements. It down-weights the parallel component via a hyperparameter η (default 0), then applies two additional modifications motivated by interpreting CFG as one step of gradient ascent on the ℓ₂ distance between conditional and unconditional predictions: (1) rescaling that constrains the update norm to a sphere of radius r to prevent large drifts, and (2) reverse momentum with negative coefficient β that repels the current update from previous update directions, further limiting cumulative drift. The three components are applied sequentially—momentum update, norm rescaling, then orthogonal projection—and introduce negligible computational overhead (~0.45 ms per step on an RTX 3090 for SDXL).

## Results

APG consistently outperforms CFG across all five tested models. On EDM2-S (w=4), APG reduces FID from 10.42 to 6.49 while improving recall from 0.48 to 0.62 and lowering saturation from 0.46 to 0.33. On DiT-XL/2 (w=4), FID drops from 19.14 to 9.34 with recall improving from 0.35 to 0.56. On Stable Diffusion XL (w=15), APG achieves FID 25.35 vs 26.29 for CFG, with saturation dropping from 0.28 to 0.18 and contrast from 0.24 to 0.17, while precision remains comparable (0.64 vs 0.62).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard CFG (Ho & Salimans, 2022), APG wins on FID, recall, saturation, and contrast across all five models while maintaining comparable precision (e.g., FID 6.49 vs 10.42 on EDM2-S; saturation 0.18 vs 0.28 on SDXL). Against CFG Rescale (Lin et al., 2024a), the paper demonstrates via visual comparison (Figure 11) that APG produces significantly less oversaturation and more realistic outputs at high guidance scales. Against CADS (Sadat et al., 2024a), combining APG with CADS achieves FID 5.56 versus 8.65 for CADS alone, showing APG is complementary to diversity-focused methods. Against Interval Guidance (Kynkänniemi et al., 2024), APG+IG reaches FID 5.29 versus 7.49 for IG alone. Against ICG and TSG (Sadat et al., 2024b), APG dramatically improves FID from 17.63 to 5.73 (ICG) and from 14.00 to 5.84 (TSG), while preserving precision at 0.85 and 0.81 respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on classifier-free guidance (CFG) by Ho & Salimans (2022), decomposing the CFG update direction into components parallel and orthogonal to the conditional denoised prediction. The key conceptual departure is the observation that the parallel component acts as a gain (causing oversaturation) while the orthogonal component drives quality improvements, motivating a projection-based down-weighting. The authors further connect CFG to a single step of gradient ascent on the ℓ₂ distance between conditional and unconditional predictions, which motivates rescaling and a reverse-momentum term borrowed from adaptive optimization. This positions APG as complementary to diversity-focused methods like CADS and interval guidance, and as a more effective alternative to prior saturation mitigations such as dynamic thresholding and CFG Rescale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

APG offers a near-zero-overhead, plug-and-drop replacement for CFG that expands the practically usable guidance range, which is significant given that high guidance scales are standard practice in production text-to-image systems. The method's compatibility across diverse architectures (EDM2, DiT, SDXL, SD3, distilled models) and samplers makes it broadly applicable. A key open question the authors identify is whether the double network query (conditional + unconditional) inherent to CFG/APG can be eliminated, which would bring inference cost closer to unguided sampling. The finding that a simple geometric projection onto the denoised prediction (rather than the noise prediction) is critical for effectiveness is a practical detail that may inform future guidance modifications.
