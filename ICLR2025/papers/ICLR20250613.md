## Problem

Reconstruction-based multivariate time series anomaly detection methods struggle with two intertwined limitations: (1) coarse-grained frequency-domain modeling neglects high-frequency band details, causing missed detections of heterogeneous subsequence anomalies (seasonal, shapelet, trend); and (2) existing channel strategies are polarized—Channel-Independent (CI) lacks capacity to capture inter-channel dependencies, while Channel-Dependent (CD) is vulnerable to noise from irrelevant channels. Neither strategy can flexibly adapt channel correlations that vary across different frequency bands.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series window X ∈ R^{N×T} (N channels, length T) and applies FFT to obtain real and imaginary frequency components, which are then partitioned into L frequency-band patches of size p with stride s. The output is a per-timestamp anomaly score computed as a weighted sum of a time-domain reconstruction error (L2 norm) and a frequency-domain reconstruction error (L1 norm), where the frequency score for each timestamp is the average reconstruction error over all patches that contain that timestamp, achieving point-granularity alignment with subsequence-level sensitivity.

## Method

CATCH consists of three modules: a Forward Module (Instance Normalization → FFT → frequency patching → linear projection), a Channel Fusion Module (CFM), and a Time-Frequency Reconstruction Module. The CFM is the core novelty: a linear-based Mask Generator produces a per-patch binary mask matrix via Gumbel-Softmax Bernoulli resampling, which gates a Channel-Masked Transformer Layer's attention to cluster relevant channels and isolate irrelevant ones within each frequency band. Training uses a bi-level optimization where the outer loop updates the Mask Generator and the inner loop updates all other model parameters, guided by four objectives: time-domain reconstruction loss (L2), frequency-domain reconstruction loss (L1), an InfoNCE-style ClusteringLoss that encourages high attention scores among mask-defined 'positive' channel pairs, and a RegularLoss that constrains the number of relevant channels to prevent degeneration to an all-ones mask. This bi-level scheme iteratively refines channel correlations between the CI and CD extremes, providing both capacity and robustness.

## Results

CATCH achieves state-of-the-art Affiliated-F1 (Aff-F) on 10 of 12 real-world datasets and all 6 synthetic anomaly types, with notable margins such as 0.994 on NYC (vs. 0.862 for DCdetector) and 0.997 on Seasonal synthetic (vs. 0.992 for TimesNet). On AUC-ROC, CATCH leads on 10 of 12 real-world datasets, e.g., 0.970 on GECCO (vs. 0.952 for ModernTCN) and 0.974 on Genesis (vs. 0.947 for Anomaly Transformer). The two exceptions are SWAT (A-R 0.345 vs. 0.831 for PCA) and SMAP (A-R 0.504 vs. 0.585 for PCA), which the authors attribute to continuous abnormal segments exceeding the reconstruction window. Across the 6 synthetic TODS anomaly categories, CATCH achieves Aff-F scores of 0.823–0.997, consistently outperforming all 15 baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CATCH outperforms all 15 baselines on Aff-F for 10 of 12 real-world datasets and all 6 synthetic anomaly types. The largest margins appear on NYC (Aff-F 0.994 vs. 0.862 for DCdetector, +13.2 pts) and CICIDS (0.787 vs. 0.708 for iTransformer, +7.9 pts). On the two real-world datasets where CATCH does not lead in Aff-F—SWAT (0.755 vs. 0.793 for TimesNet) and SMAP (0.699 vs. 0.703 for Anomaly Transformer)—the gaps are small (≤0.4 pts). On AUC-ROC, CATCH leads on 10 of 12 real-world datasets; the notable exception is SWAT (0.345 vs. 0.831 for PCA), which the authors explain is caused by continuous abnormal segments exceeding the reconstruction look-back window. Among the strongest deep-learning baselines, CATCH consistently beats iTransformer, ModernTCN, and DualTF across nearly all datasets, with DualTF showing the largest overall gap (e.g., A-R 0.603 vs. 0.795 on CICIDS). On synthetic TODS data, CATCH achieves near-perfect detection for Seasonal (Aff-F 0.997, A-R 0.998) and Global (Aff-F 0.949, A-R 0.997) anomalies, while the closest competitor (iTransformer or TimesNet) trails by 0.5–3.0 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

CATCH builds on the reconstruction-based MTSAD paradigm (Anomaly Transformer, DCdetector) and the frequency-domain analysis line (SR-CNN, PFT, TFAD, Dual-TF), but departs from both in key ways. Rather than patching the time domain as in PatchTST, it patches the frequency domain into fine-grained bands to capture high-frequency subsequence anomalies that coarse-grained methods miss. Its Channel Fusion Module carves new ground between the Channel-Independent and Channel-Dependent strategies by using a Gumbel-Softmax-based mask generator and masked attention, guided by a bi-level multi-objective optimization that iteratively discovers patch-wise channel correlations—repurposing ideas from selective state-space models (Mamba) and contrastive learning (InfoNCE) in a novel combination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frequency-patching mechanism and the adaptive channel correlation discovery address two persistent weaknesses in MTSAD: loss of high-frequency detail and the CI/CD polarization trade-off. The patch-wise point-aligned scoring is a practical contribution that resolves the time-frequency granularity mismatch without requiring window-level labels. However, the bi-level optimization yields only a marginal gain over joint training (0.816 vs 0.811 avg AUC-ROC), raising the question of whether the added complexity is justified. Open questions include scalability to very high-dimensional series (beyond 72 channels), whether the discovered channel correlations generalize across domains, and whether the approach extends to streaming or online settings.
