## Problem

Diffusion-based ensemble weather forecasting models are computationally expensive due to sequential forward passes and accumulate errors when rolled out autoregressively at high temporal resolution, forcing existing methods to resort to coarse 12–24h timesteps. Naively forecasting each future timestep independently from initial conditions produces temporally inconsistent trajectories, since samples from marginal distributions do not form a coherent joint trajectory. The paper addresses how to generate temporally consistent ensemble forecasts in parallel, at arbitrary fine temporal resolution, without sacrificing accuracy.

## Input / Output

The model takes two previous weather states (at 5.625° global resolution), a scalar lead time t, and static fields (land-sea mask, orography) as conditioning inputs, along with a noise sample Z. It outputs a full ensemble of temporally consistent forecast trajectories for five atmospheric variables (z500, t850, t2m, u10, v10) at any set of lead times up to 10 days, including non-equidistant and 1-hour resolution timesteps. Ensemble members are generated by varying only the initial noise sample while keeping the ODE solver deterministic, enabling fully parallel sampling across both ensemble members and lead times.

## Method

The core mechanism is a conditional diffusion model (U-Net, 3.5M params) trained with denoising score matching to predict the score function ∇_z log p(X(t)|X(Ω), t), where the lead time t is fed via Fourier embedding alongside the noise level σ. Sampling solves the probability flow ODE from pure noise to a forecast using Heun's 2nd-order method (20 steps). The key insight for temporal consistency is that freezing the driving noise Z makes the ODE solver a deterministic map f_θ^Z, so evaluating it at multiple lead times yields a continuous trajectory; stochasticity across ensemble members comes from varying Z. To avoid conditional determinism, an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process can replace fixed Z with a temporally autocorrelated noise process, preserving marginal distributions while introducing inter-timestep stochasticity. The ARCI framework combines 24h autoregressive rollouts with continuous interpolation at 6h or 1h resolution within each step, limiting error accumulation while retaining fine temporal resolution and enabling parallelism across the interpolated timesteps.

## Results

On WeatherBench ERA5 data at 5.625° resolution, ARCI-24/6h achieves the best RMSE and CRPS among all 6h-resolution models for both z500 and t850 at 5 and 10-day lead times. For z500 at 10 days, ARCI-24/6h reaches RMSE 765.6 and CRPS 355.2, outperforming AR-6h (RMSE 811.8, CRPS 391.9) and Graph-EFM (RMSE 817.1, CRPS 373.6), while nearly matching the 24h-resolution AR-24h (RMSE 750.6, CRPS 335.2). For t850 at 10 days, ARCI-24/6h achieves RMSE 3.29 and CRPS 1.63 with SSR 0.95, compared to AR-6h (RMSE 3.39, CRPS 1.69, SSR 0.92). At 1h resolution, ARCI-24/1h outperforms both the autoregressive AR-1h model and DYffusion across all variables and lead times.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the GenCast reimplementation (AR-6h, Price et al. 2025), ARCI-24/6h consistently wins on both RMSE and CRPS at 6h resolution: for z500 at 10 days, RMSE improves from 811.8 to 765.6 (−5.7%) and CRPS from 391.9 to 355.2 (−9.4%), while SSR moves closer to the ideal value of 1 (0.88→0.93). Against Graph-EFM (Oskarsson et al. 2024), ARCI-24/6h achieves lower RMSE and CRPS (e.g., z500 10-day: 765.6 vs. 817.1 RMSE; 355.2 vs. 373.6 CRPS), though Graph-EFM has a slightly higher SSR (1.10 vs. 0.93), indicating mild overdispersion in Graph-EFM. Against DYffusion (Ruhling Cachay et al. 2023) at 1h resolution, ARCI-24/1h outperforms DYffusion on all variables and lead times, while the purely autoregressive AR-1h model degrades substantially compared to its 6h/24h counterparts. The key advantage of ARCI-24/6h over AR-24h is matching its accuracy at 4× the temporal resolution (6h vs. 24h), and over AR-6h it reduces error accumulation by limiting autoregressive steps to 24h intervals while filling intermediate timesteps via parallel continuous forecasting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of diffusion-based ensemble weather forecasting initiated by GenCast (Price et al., 2025), which trains a conditional diffusion model on a single fixed timestep and rolls out autoregressively. The key departure is the adoption of continuous forecasting—conditioning on lead time as an input—which has previously been applied only to deterministic models (Nguyen et al., 2023; 2025) and not to diffusion models. The paper further departs from both GenCast and DYffusion (Ruhling Cachay et al., 2023) by introducing correlated driving noise (via an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process) across lead times to ensure temporal consistency of ensemble trajectories without autoregressive iteration, and by combining autoregressive rollouts with continuous interpolation (ARCI) to retain long-horizon accuracy while enabling fine temporal resolution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method enables 10-day global ensemble forecasts at 1-hour resolution with accuracy matching 24-hour-step autoregressive models, while parallelizing the sampling of individual timesteps (reducing a 10-day 6h forecast from 32s to 8s per member). This is practically important for decision support in extreme weather situations where high temporal resolution is critical. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher spatial resolutions (beyond 5.625°), how to handle the weakening correlation between initial and future states at very long lead times, and whether the sequential nature of diffusion ODE solving can be further accelerated. The framework is also general and applicable to other spatio-temporal forecasting problems beyond weather.
