## Problem

Designing Organic Structure Directing Agents (OSDAs) for zeolite synthesis has traditionally relied on empirical trial-and-error or computationally expensive DFT simulations, while existing ML-based generators lack interactivity and human-in-the-loop feedback. Pure LLMs, though capable of generating molecular structures, produce uncontrollable outputs that frequently violate chemical validity rules and lack first-principle computation, making them unreliable for chemistry-critical design tasks. The paper addresses the gap of an interactive, feedback-driven framework that combines LLM generative reasoning with rigorous computational chemistry validation for de novo OSDA design.

## Input / Output

The system takes a target zeolite framework (parsed from CIF into a crystal graph with atom types X, positions P, and lattice vectors L) and a natural-language design prompt specifying the desired OSDA properties; for optimization tasks, an existing OSDA SMILES is additionally provided. The Actor outputs candidate OSDA molecules as SMILES strings, which are then scored by the Evaluator on three axes: chemical validity (RDKit empirical rules such as rotatable bonds < 5, C:N ratio 4–20, absence of unfavorable atoms), synthetic complexity (SCScore on a 1–5 scale), and estimated binding energy (kJ/mol Si) computed via a neural network over VOID-generated docking poses. The Self-reflector produces a natural-language feedback summary that is appended to the agent's memory and fed back into the next generation round.

## Method

The OSDA Agent is a three-component iterative loop (Actor → Evaluator → Self-reflector) built on the Reflexion paradigm. The Actor (GPT-4) generates OSDA SMILES using few-shot Chain-of-Thought prompting with role-play as a zeolite expert, in-context examples drawn from the OSDB database, and a persistent memory of past evaluations. The Evaluator applies three chemical tools in sequence: RDKit filters for chemical validity against OSDA-specific empirical rules, SCScore assesses synthetic feasibility, and a novel binding-energy estimation model—combining SMILES-BERT molecular embeddings, CGCNN crystal-graph encoders for both the zeolite and the OSDA-zeolite complex, and a Transformer fusion layer followed by fully connected heads—predicts complex energy from VOID-docked poses, with binding energy computed as E_complex − E_zeolite − E_osda. The Self-reflector (GPT-4o) reads the Evaluator scores and memory, then produces targeted verbal critique (e.g., flagging high SCScore or poor binding energy) that conditions the Actor's next generation, enabling iterative refinement over multiple rounds without any gradient-based fine-tuning of the LLMs.

## Results

On the AFI framework, the OSDA Agent achieves 100% validity (vs. 44.0% for GPT-4, 0.8% for BioT5, and 0% for MolT5), BLEU of 0.577, MACCS of 0.781, RDK of 0.608, Energy Distance of 2.567, and KL divergence of 0.588. On the LTA framework, it similarly achieves 100% validity, BLEU of 0.499, MACCS of 0.737, RDK of 0.412, ED of 2.084, and KL of 0.744. In the optimization task, the OSDA Agent reduces the average SCScore from 3.45 to 2.46 while maintaining binding energies within the experimentally validated range (−3.38 to −9.00 kcal/mol).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The OSDA Agent decisively outperforms both text-to-molecule baselines (MolT5 and BioT5) across all seven metrics on both AFI and LTA frameworks; MolT5 and BioT5 produce essentially zero valid OSDAs (0.0% and 0.5–0.8% validity) and exhibit ED values 25–100× higher than the OSDA Agent, indicating their generated molecules bear little resemblance to real OSDAs. Against the strongest baseline, pure GPT-4 (with the same OSDB context but no chemical tools or reflection), the OSDA Agent achieves perfect validity (1.000 vs. 0.440/0.471), lower ED (2.567 vs. 4.061 on AFI; 2.084 vs. 12.80 on LTA), and lower KL divergence (0.588 vs. 0.653; 0.744 vs. 0.976), while also winning on BLEU, MACCS, and RDK. The sole metric where GPT-4 edges out the OSDA Agent is Morgan fingerprint similarity on AFI (0.332 vs. 0.308), a marginal 0.024 difference. In the ablation (Appendix G), removing the reflection mechanism drops validity from 1.000 to 0.702 and raises ED from 0.934 to 1.359, confirming that the iterative feedback loop is the primary driver of the quality gain over the raw LLM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Reflexion framework (Shinn et al., 2024) — an Actor-Evaluator-Self-reflector loop for verbal reinforcement — to the specific domain of zeolite OSDA design, coupling GPT-4-based generation with domain-specific chemical tools (RDKit validation, SCScore, and a novel CGCNN-based binding energy estimator). It departs from prior LLM-based OSDA work (Ito et al., 2024a,b), which relied on pure GPT-4 prompting without external chemical verification, and from traditional ML/generative approaches (Jensen et al., 2021; Xu et al., 2023; Muraoka et al., 2020) that lack interactivity and human-in-the-loop feedback. The binding energy estimation module, which combines SMILES-BERT, CGCNN, and a transformer fusion layer trained on the OSDB database, is a new component not present in prior OSDA design pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that an LLM agent augmented with computational chemistry tools and iterative self-reflection can achieve 100% chemical validity and distributional alignment with experimentally validated OSDAs, outperforming both pure LLM generation and text-to-molecule baselines (MolT5, BioT5) across all seven evaluation metrics. However, the approach remains unvalidated experimentally — generated OSDAs have not been synthesized and tested in actual zeolite crystallization — and the binding energy model, while fast, is an approximation that may not capture all supramolecular interactions. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to other template-directed materials beyond zeolites, how it scales to the full chemical space of potential OSDAs, and whether the reliance on proprietary LLMs (GPT-4) limits reproducibility and accessibility.
