## Problem

State-of-the-art DDPMs require 100+ function evaluations (NFEs) for high-fidelity sampling, making them impractical as surrogates for long-horizon fluid dynamics simulations where inference speed is critical. Existing accelerated sampling methods (DDIM, EDM, distillation) either sacrifice accuracy, require extensive retraining, or introduce additional architectural complexity. The paper asks whether single-step or few-step diffusion sampling can match or exceed the accuracy of full ancestral sampling without retraining the base model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252598/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 2D fluid state (velocity components, pressure, and optionally density on grids of 32×32 to 128×64), a prediction stride j ∈ {0,…,10} indicating how many physical timesteps ahead to predict, and flow parameters (Re, Ma, or AoA) as conditioning channels. The output is the predicted fluid state at τ + j·δτ, obtained either in a single network evaluation (TSM with s=1) or in 1–10 refinement steps (IR). Unlike standard autoregressive solvers that must step through every intermediate state to satisfy CFL conditions, the model directly maps to the target timestep, and the same network can also recover intermediate states by querying with smaller j values.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary approaches built on Tweedie's formula E[x̂₀|x_t;θ] = (x_t − √(1−ᾱ_t)·ε_θ(x_t,t))/√ᾱ_t. First, Truncated Sampling Models (TSMs) modify DDPM training by restricting the noise-step sampling to t ∈ {⌊sT⌋,…,T} and, at inference, stop ancestral sampling at t_s and directly estimate x̂₀ via Tweedie's formula, enabling single-step inference when s=1. Second, Iterative Refinement (IR) is a sampling algorithm for pre-trained DDPMs that starts from an arbitrary initial state x_init (Gaussian noise, a low-fidelity prediction, or a partially noisy output) and iteratively applies forward diffusion at a chosen noise level t followed by a Tweedie posterior-mean estimate, using a greedily optimized refinement schedule γ. The key insight is that fluid dynamics data are predominantly unimodal and at coarse resolutions lack high-frequency content, so Tweedie's formula introduces negligible bias and truncating the reverse process loses no information—properties that make single-step and few-step sampling not merely faster but often more accurate than full ancestral sampling.

## Results

On the Transonic flow (Tra) case, single-step TSM (1 NFE) achieves TA-MSE of 1.2×10⁻³ (extrapolation) and 1.5×10⁻³ (interpolation), outperforming DDPM T100 (3.0×10⁻³ / 4.1×10⁻³) with 100 NFEs and the ACDM benchmark (2.3×10⁻³ / 2.7×10⁻³) with 20 NFEs. On Forced turbulence (Fturb), IR with 10 NFEs achieves TA-MSE of 2.93×10⁻² (ext) and 1.70×10⁻² (int), surpassing the deterministic single-step baseline (3.95×10⁻² / 4.82×10⁻²) and DDPM T80 (6.16×10⁻² / 5.27×10⁻²). On the Airfoil uncertainty case, TSM with 10 NFEs matches the 200-NFE DDPM benchmark on Air_One (MSE_μ: 3.30 vs 3.79 ×10⁻⁴) while achieving ×20 speedup, and TSM with 25 NFEs outperforms the benchmark on Air_Multi (MSE_μ: 1.66 vs 1.76 ×10⁻³).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDPM (Ho et al., 2020), both TSM and IR achieve substantially lower TA-MSE with 10–100× fewer NFEs: on Tra, single-step TSM (1.2×10⁻³ ext) beats DDPM T100 (3.0×10⁻³ ext) by 2.5×, and on Fturb, IR with 10 NFEs (2.93×10⁻² ext) beats DDPM T80 (6.16×10⁻² ext) by 2.1×. Against DDIM (Song et al., 2021), IR consistently outperforms across all NFE budgets and base models; on Fturb, IR-10 (2.93×10⁻² ext) beats DDIM-40 (4.31×10⁻² ext) despite using 4× fewer steps. Against EDM (Karras et al., 2022), single-step TSM matches or slightly beats the best EDM solver (deterministic Euler, 4 NFEs) on Tra (1.2 vs 1.3 ×10⁻³ ext) while being 4× faster; on Fturb, EDM-10 is on par with single-step TSM but 10× slower. Against the ACDM benchmark (Kohl et al., 2024), TSM single-step outperforms ACDM T20 by ×20 speedup (1.2 vs 2.3 ×10⁻³ ext on Tra), and IR achieves ×4 speedup with comparable accuracy. Against the airfoil uncertainty benchmark (Liu & Thuerey, 2024), TSM with 10 NFEs achieves comparable MSE_μ (3.30 vs 3.79 ×10⁻⁴) and better MSE_σ (5.89 vs 8.40 ×10⁻⁴) than the 200-NFE DDPM on Air_One, a ×20 speedup. PDE-Refiner (Lippe et al., 2023) performs suboptimally on Tra (5.4×10⁻³ ext) with a significant gap to all other methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DDPMs (Ho et al., 2020) and Tweedie's formula (Efron, 2011), repositioning the standard ancestral sampling procedure as either a truncated process (TSM) or an iterative refinement loop (IR). It departs from TDPMs (Zheng et al., 2023) by truncating the *first* steps of the reverse chain rather than the last, and by eliminating the need for an auxiliary GAN-based generator. In the fluid dynamics surrogate literature, it extends the autoregressive conditional diffusion framework of Kohl et al. (2024) by introducing a prediction-stride conditioning that enables flexible, parallelizable sampling, and it contrasts with PDE-Refiner (Lippe et al., 2023) by operating as a sampling algorithm on pre-trained models rather than a training procedure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing NFEs from 100+ to 1–10 while matching or exceeding DDPM accuracy, TSMs and IR make diffusion-model surrogates practically viable for long-horizon fluid dynamics rollouts where inference cost was previously prohibitive. The finding that aggressive truncation can *improve* accuracy (rather than merely degrade it) is enabled by the low-frequency character of coarse-resolution fluid data, but this limits generalizability to higher-resolution or multimodal settings. Key open questions include whether TSMs can be trained via transfer learning to avoid retraining from scratch, whether TSM and IR can be composed for further gains, and whether the stochastic advantage of IR over DDIMs generalizes beyond the predominantly unimodal distributions typical of fluid dynamics.
