## Problem

While upper bounds for the oracle complexity of minimizing functions that are simultaneously high-order Hölder smooth (order p, degree ν) and uniformly convex (degree q) were established by Song et al. (2021) for all combinations of p, q, and ν, no matching lower bounds existed in this general asymmetric setting. In particular, the cases where the convexity degree q exceeds or falls below the smoothness order p+ν had never been addressed with tight lower bounds, leaving a gap between known upper and lower complexity guarantees.

## Input / Output

The analysis considers a composite objective F(x) = f(x) + (σ/q)‖x‖^q where f has p-th order Hölder-continuous derivatives (degree ν, modulus H) and the regularization enforces uniform convexity of degree q with modulus σ. The algorithm is assumed to have access to function values and derivatives up to order p at queried points. The output is a worst-case lower bound on the number of oracle calls T required to achieve an ε-approximate solution (F(x_T) − F(x*) ≤ ε), expressed as a function of H, σ, ε, p, q, and ν, with the bound taking different functional forms depending on whether q > p+ν or q < p+ν.

## Method

For the q > p+ν case, the paper introduces a novel ℓ∞-ball-truncated multivariate Gaussian smoothing operator applied p times to a max-of-linear-functions hard function (following the Guzmán–Nemirovski framework). The ℓ∞-ball truncation is critical: unlike ℓ2-ball truncation, it yields a marginal distribution that is a standard truncated normal on [−1,1], ensuring dimension-free smoothness constants while preserving locality (the smoothed function at a point depends only on values within a bounded neighborhood). This locality is essential for the indistinguishability argument that equates the trajectory generated on a sequence of intermediate functions with that on the final hard function. For the q < p+ν case, the paper generalizes Nesterov's hard function construction to Hölder smooth and uniformly convex settings by building a chain of |x_i − x_{i+1}|^{p+ν} terms with an orthogonal basis that adaptively hides coordinates from the algorithm, then bounding the optimality gap via per-coordinate decay properties of the optimal solution and the uniform convexity modulus.

## Results

The paper establishes two tight lower bounds for oracle complexity of minimizing high-order Hölder smooth and uniformly convex functions. For the q > p+ν case, the lower bound is Ω((H/σ)^(2/(3(p+ν)−2)) · (σ/ε)^(2(q−p−ν)/(q(3(p+ν)−2)))), and for the q < p+ν case, it is Ω((H/σ)^(2/(3(p+ν)−2)) + log log((σ^(p+ν)/H^q)^(1/(p+ν−q)) · 1/ε)). Both bounds exactly match the corresponding upper bounds from Song et al. (2021), closing the gap between known upper and lower bounds in this general asymmetric setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's lower bounds match the upper bounds of Song et al. (2021) exactly in both asymmetric regimes, confirming optimality of the unified acceleration framework. For the q > p+ν case, the construction generalizes and improves upon Doikov (2022) and Thomsen & Doikov (2024), which were limited to first-order smoothness (p=1); the new ℓ∞-ball-truncated-Gaussian smoothing removes the dimension-dependent factor that made Agarwal & Hazan (2018) sub-optimal (their rate was O(T^(−2/(5p+1))) vs. the tight O(T^(−2/(3p+1)))). For the q < p+ν case, the Nesterov hard-function construction generalizes Arjevani et al. (2019) and Kornowski & Shamir (2020), which only covered the p=2, q=2 (strongly convex) setting, to arbitrary p, q, and Hölder degree ν. The q = p+ν boundary case remains open, and the Nesterov-based lower bound applies only to deterministic/zero-respecting algorithms rather than randomized ones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper closes the lower-bound gap for the asymmetric regime of high-order Hölder smoothness combined with uniform convexity, where upper bounds were established by Song et al. (2021) but no matching lower bounds existed. For the q > p+ν case, it extends the Guzmán–Nemirovski max-function framework (previously applied only to first-order smoothness by Doikov 2022 and Thomsen & Doikov 2024) by introducing a novel ℓ∞-ball-truncated Gaussian smoothing operator that is simultaneously local, dimension-free, and scales correctly under repeated application—properties that no prior smoothing operator (uniform ℓ2-ball, full Gaussian, softmax, or Moreau) satisfies. For the q < p+ν case, it generalizes Nesterov's hard-function construction from Arjevani et al. (2019) and Kornowski & Shamir (2020), which were limited to Lipschitz smoothness and strong convexity (q=2), to arbitrary Hölder degree ν and uniform convexity degree q.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

These tight lower bounds confirm that the unified acceleration framework of Song et al. (2021) is optimal for both asymmetric regimes, providing a complete complexity characterization for the general (p, q, ν) setting. The ℓ∞-ball-truncated Gaussian smoothing operator is a reusable technical tool that resolves a specific structural tension (locality vs. dimension-free smoothness vs. correct p-th power scaling) that blocked prior attempts at high-order lower bounds. The q = p+ν boundary case remains open, and the Nesterov-based lower bound for q < p+ν applies only to deterministic/zero-respecting algorithms rather than randomized ones, leaving a gap for the randomized setting in that regime.
