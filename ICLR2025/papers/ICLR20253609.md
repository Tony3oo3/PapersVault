## Problem

Training large-scale Transformers without learning rate warmup or architectural modifications (e.g., ReZero, QKNorm, DeepNet) remains extremely challenging, as training typically diverges in the initial phase. The paper asks two questions: what are the training dynamics that distinguish successful from failed Transformer training, and can one stabilize training purely through an optimizer change without altering the network structure or relying on warmup?

## Input / Output

The method is an optimizer (AdamW²) applied to standard, unmodified Transformer architectures (ViT, Swin-Transformer, GPT-2, Flatten-Swin). For vision tasks, inputs are 224×224 RGB images from ImageNet producing class labels; for language modeling, inputs are text sequences of length 1024 from OpenWebText producing next-token predictions. At each optimization step, the optimizer additionally computes the spectral norm of the current weight matrix and the update quantity via 3-iteration power iteration, introducing negligible overhead.

## Method

The paper identifies Spectral Energy Concentration (SEC) of W_q^T W_k as the root cause of model crash: when the top few singular values of W_q^T W_k grow disproportionately, the attention map becomes simultaneously sparse and low-rank (malignant entropy collapse), triggering divergence. The theoretical analysis shows the Jacobian ∂vec(P)/∂vec(W_q^T W_k) = X^T ⊗ X^T, so low-rank inputs produce low-rank gradients that further concentrate spectral energy. Motivated by Weyl's Inequality (σ_1(W_t) ≤ σ_1(W_{t-1}) + α_t·σ_1(∇W_t)), the proposed AdamW² optimizer enforces a steady-updating rule: if α_t·σ_1(∇W_t)/σ_1(W_{t-1}) exceeds a small threshold τ (e.g., 0.004), the learning rate is truncated to τ·σ_1(W_{t-1})/σ_1(∇W_t), preventing rapid singular-value growth. This requires no architectural changes, no warmup schedule, and only 3 power-iteration steps per parameter group per update, yet achieves competitive or superior accuracy on ViT, Swin-Transformer, and GPT compared to AdamW with warmup.

## Results

On ImageNet, AdamW² without warmup achieves 80.58% top-1 accuracy on ViT-B/16 and 81.82% on ViT-L/16, outperforming AdamW with warmup (80.22% and 81.65% respectively). On OpenWebText, GPT-Small trained with AdamW² reaches a validation loss of 2.840, slightly better than AdamW with warmup (2.848). On Swin-Transformer, AdamW² achieves 83.14% (Swin-S) and 83.44% (Swin-B) top-1 accuracy, matching or exceeding the warmup baseline. The method also stably trains a 1B-parameter ViT-g and a 774M nanoGPT-large without warmup, where standard AdamW crashes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is standard AdamW with a learning rate warmup schedule (60 epochs for ViT, 20 epochs for Swin, 2000 steps for GPT). AdamW² without any warmup outperforms this baseline on 4 out of 5 configurations: ViT-B by +0.36% accuracy, ViT-L by +0.17%, GPT-S by 0.008 lower loss, and Swin-S by +0.12%. On Swin-B, AdamW² is marginally lower by 0.04% (83.44 vs 83.48), essentially a tie. Critically, without warmup, standard AdamW crashes entirely on all tested architectures, making AdamW² the only method that trains stably from step zero. The paper further validates at scale: a 1B ViT-g crashes with AdamW without warmup but trains stably with AdamW², and a 774M nanoGPT-large achieves comparable validation loss to GPT-2-large without warmup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on AdamW as its base optimizer, adding a spectral-norm-based learning rate truncation rule derived from Weyl's Inequality. It departs from prior training-stability approaches (ReZero, LipsFormer, DeepNet, QKNorm) that require architectural modifications by instead operating purely at the optimizer level, requiring no changes to the network. The theoretical analysis connects to the entropy collapse (Zhai et al., 2023) and rank collapse (Dong et al., 2021) literature, but goes further by identifying spectral energy concentration of W_q^T W_k as the specific root cause of malignant collapse, and by providing a matrix-calculus derivation of the attention Jacobian via Kronecker products. The approach is conceptually related to Kosson et al. (2023, 2024), who also argue for controlling update magnitude relative to weight magnitude, but this paper provides a cleaner theoretical justification via Weyl's Inequality and a simpler implementation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Learning rate warmup is treated as a near-universal requirement for stable large-scale Transformer training; demonstrating that it can be entirely eliminated by a simple per-step spectral norm check (3 power iterations) has direct practical value for training pipelines. The identification of spectral energy concentration as the mechanistic cause of model crash provides a more precise diagnostic than the previously recognized entropy or rank collapse, potentially guiding future stabilization techniques. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-billion-parameter models with different parallelism strategies, whether the per-parameter spectral norm check introduces meaningful overhead at very large batch sizes, and whether the same principle applies to non-Transformer architectures or diffusion models.
