## Problem

Most deep state-space model networks follow a uniform block architecture with little variation in intermediate feature shapes, restricting SSM layers to depthwise-separable connectivity that may limit network capacity. This paper asks whether the heterogeneous design principles from classical CNNs—group convolutions, full convolutions, and bottleneck blocks—can be transplanted into SSM architectures, and whether the resulting flexible tensor shapes can be trained efficiently via optimal contraction ordering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251102/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

All tasks consume raw 16 kHz audio waveforms directly (no STFT or spectral preprocessing). For keyword spotting, the network outputs a 35-class label from 1-second clips; for speech denoising, it produces a clean waveform in [-1,+1] from 8.192-second segments; for ASR, it streams BERT token logits at 20 ms periods (total downsampling factor of 320) from ~20.5-second segments, with a separate character head producing soft logits distilled from Wav2Vec2. The ASR model is fully online-capable with no attention or nonlinear recurrence.

## Method

The paper expresses SSM blocks as einsum tensor networks, revealing that standard SSM layers are depthwise-separable and enabling new block variants (full, bottleneck, group) by varying which channel indices appear in the basis kernel tensor. During training, SSM operations are performed in the frequency domain where the convolution becomes a pointwise product, and the optimal contraction order is determined dynamically based on tensor operand shapes (batch, channels, states, sequence length) using an augmented opt-einsum framework that accounts for FFT operator placement and real-vs-complex tensor preferences. The Centaurus network is a heterogeneous mixture of these blocks—full SSM blocks in shallow layers, bottleneck blocks in mid layers, and pointwise bottleneck blocks in deep layers—mirroring classical CNN design philosophy of increasing sparsity with depth. The SSM uses complex diagonal A matrices with real B and C projections, ZOH discretization, and basis kernels parameterized by A and E weighting factors, with the D term absorbed into the state.

## Results

On LibriSpeech ASR (streaming, no attention), Centaurus with a Mamba macro-block achieves 4.4/10.2 WER (clean/other) with 29.9M parameters and 46.9G FLOPs, competitive with Conformer (4.6/9.9, 30.7M, 45.2G) while being fully SSM-based. On real-time speech denoising (VB-DMD), the Centaurus hybrid achieves PESQ 3.12 (3.25 with a causal conv) using only 0.51M parameters and 0.29G FLOPs/sec. On keyword spotting (SC10), Centaurus hybrid reaches 98.53% accuracy with 0.378M parameters and 0.134G FLOPs/sec, outperforming S4 (98.32%, 11.8G) and LiquidS4 (98.51%) by orders of magnitude in inference FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On speech denoising (VB-DMD), Centaurus hybrid (PESQ 3.12) is slightly below FRCRN (3.21) and DeepFilterNet3 (3.16) but with 13.5× fewer parameters and 263× fewer FLOPs/sec than FRCRN; adding a causal conv pushes Centaurus to 3.25, surpassing both. Centaurus clearly outperforms SEMamba (2.76), PercepNet (2.73), and DEMUCS (2.65) by 0.36–0.47 PESQ while using fewer parameters. On streaming ASR, Centaurus with a Mamba macro-block (4.4/10.2 WER) marginally beats Conformer online (4.6/9.9) on clean speech and ties on other, while remaining fully SSM-based without attention; it trails offline Wav2Vec2 (2.1/4.8) and Conformer (2.0/4.3) as expected for a streaming model. On keyword spotting (SC10), Centaurus hybrid (98.53%) edges out LiquidS4 (98.51%) and S4 (98.32%) while using ~88× fewer inference FLOPs than S4 (0.134G vs 11.8G), and substantially outperforms S4D-Lin (96.25%). Compared to S5 (0.280M params, 13.5G FLOPs), Centaurus uses more parameters (0.378M) but achieves 100× lower inference FLOPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

Centaurus extends the structured state-space model lineage (S4, S4D, S5, Mamba) by reframing SSM blocks as tensor networks expressible via einsum, which reveals that existing SSM layers are essentially depthwise-separable convolutions. This tensor-network formalism enables the design of new SSM block types—full, grouped, and bottleneck—directly inspired by classical CNN architectures (MobileNet, ResNet, AlexNet), and the optimal contraction order for training is determined systematically using an augmented opt_einsum framework that accounts for FFT operator placement. The paper departs from the homogeneous, uniform-block design that characterizes most deep SSM networks by adopting the heterogeneous CNN philosophy of gradually increasing channel width, decreasing temporal resolution, and increasing connective sparsity with depth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Centaurus demonstrates that a fully state-space-based network (no attention, no LSTM, no explicit CNN) can achieve competitive streaming ASR performance, opening a path toward lightweight, online-inference-friendly sequence models. The tensor contraction optimization provides a general, systematic speedup for training any SSM block, and the heterogeneous block design offers practitioners a principled way to balance capacity, memory, and compute. Open questions include whether the data-gating mechanism (Mamba-style) can be efficiently integrated without sacrificing contraction-order flexibility, whether the approach scales to language modeling, and whether higher-dimensional SSM convolutions (stride, dilation) can extend Centaurus to vision tasks. A notable limitation is that the audio-focused evaluation, while thorough, does not yet validate the architectural principles on larger-scale or cross-modal tasks.
