## Problem

Multi-view classification suffers from two intertwined issues: feature heterogeneity (views originate from different distributions, making fusion difficult) and information redundancy (ambiguous samples blur decision boundaries and impair generalization). Existing methods either mine common subspaces while ignoring inter-class differences, or assign static view weights that fail to adapt to sample-specific view reliability, leaving ambiguous boundary regions unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N instances each described by V heterogeneous feature vectors of varying dimensions (e.g., 240/76/216/47/64/6 for HandWritten, 64/64/512/647/838 for YoutubeFace) along with class labels during training. View-specific autoencoders first project all views into a shared 512-dimensional representation space. At inference, the model outputs a fused prediction vector obtained by averaging the softmax outputs of a single parameter-shared logistic classifier applied to each view's representation independently.

## Method

MAMC combines three components: (1) view-specific autoencoders that normalize heterogeneous features into a common representation space via reconstruction loss; (2) a multi-scale alignment module with an instance-scale contrastive loss that pulls together all view representations of the same instance while pushing apart different instances, and a prototype-scale alignment loss that exploits inter-class differences; (3) a parameter-shared joint classifier. The key novelty is the expanded boundary constructed via fuzzy set theory: after centralizing representations around class prototypes, L trainable Gaussian membership functions produce fuzzy representations, and the boundary elements are computed as (2 − R̃) ⊙ Z, where R̃ is the Min-Pooled membership degree matrix. A theoretical result (Theorem 1) proves that Min Pooling maximizes within-class variance, preserving margins for unseen data. The prototype-scale loss then performs contrastive alignment between restored boundary elements and a fused prototype (linear combination of the crisp prototype and the boundary-element prototype), effectively tightening representations inside the expanded boundary while widening inter-class separation.

## Results

MAMC achieves the best accuracy on all 8 multi-view datasets, with particularly strong gains on challenging benchmarks: 81.5% on Scene15 (vs. 80.7% for the next-best MV-HFMD), 93.1% on PIE, 54.0% on CCV, and 87.1% on YoutubeFace. On easier datasets where baselines already perform well, MAMC still leads: 98.9% on HandWritten, 98.5% on 100Leaves, and 99.8% on Hdigit. The method also reports the lowest standard deviations across most datasets (e.g., ±0.7 on Scene15 vs. ±1.2 for MV-HFMD), indicating more stable performance across 10 random splits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAMC outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset. The closest competitor is MV-HFMD (Black & Souvenir, WACV 2024), which leads on no dataset but comes within 0.8% on Scene15 (80.7% vs. 81.5%) and 0.6% on CCV (53.4% vs. 54.0%); MAMC's advantage widens on PIE (93.1% vs. 86.8%) and Hdigit (99.8% vs. 84.1%), suggesting MV-HFMD's feature-fusion approach struggles when feature heterogeneity is severe. RCML (Xu et al., AAAI 2024) is the second-strongest baseline, reaching 91.8% on PIE and 98.3% on Hdigit, yet MAMC still leads by 1.3% and 1.5% respectively. PDMF (Xu et al., AAAI 2023) performs well on YoutubeFace (85.6%) but is surpassed by MAMC by 1.5% there and by larger margins elsewhere. ETMC (Han et al., TPAMI 2022) and mmdynamics (Han et al., CVPR 2022) trail by 5–20% on most datasets, with ETMC particularly weak on Hdigit (90.8% vs. 99.8%). UMDL (Xu et al., ACM MM 2023) suffers OOM on YoutubeFace and achieves only 34.0% on Animal, while IPMVSC (Hu et al., TKDE 2023) degrades sharply on large-scale data (28.2% on YoutubeFace, 66.1% on 100Leaves), confirming the paper's claim that fuzzy-rule-based interpretable classifiers do not scale well.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAMC synthesizes ideas from contrastive learning (for inter-view commonality mining), prototype-based representation learning (for inter-class separation), and fuzzy set theory (for adaptive boundary expansion) into a unified multi-view classification framework. It departs from subspace-based methods (CCA, D2CCA) that focus solely on feature commonality while ignoring class-level structure, and from view-weighted approaches (ETMC, RCML) that assume stable view importance across samples. The expanded boundary concept repurposes fuzzy set membership functions not for interpretable rule-based classification (as in IPMVSC) but as a mechanism to adaptively widen class boundaries in the representation space, treating crisp representations as a special case of fuzzy ones to generate learnable boundary elements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two persistent and underexplored problems in multi-view learning—feature heterogeneity and information redundancy—simultaneously, which prior methods have typically tackled in isolation. The theoretical result that Min Pooling maximizes within-class variance (and thus generalization margin) provides a principled justification for the combination operator choice. However, the method introduces considerable complexity (four loss terms, multiple temperature and penalty coefficients, learnable Gaussian membership functions), and the performance gains over strong baselines like MV-HFMD are modest (1–2% on most datasets). Open questions include whether the O(N²) complexity and the multi-scale alignment strategy scale effectively to very high-dimensional or many-view settings, and whether the expanded boundary concept transfers to other representation learning tasks beyond classification.
