## Problem

Existing waveform generators face a fundamental trade-off: GAN-based vocoders are fast but vulnerable to train-inference mismatch in two-stage TTS and require complex multi-discriminator training, while diffusion-based models produce high quality but are too slow for practical waveform synthesis. Critically, no prior generator architecture explicitly disentangles the natural multi-periodic structure of high-resolution waveform signals, and flow matching had never been applied to waveform generation.

## Input / Output

The model accepts either a Mel-spectrogram (80 bins at 22.05 kHz or 100 bins at 24 kHz, hop size 256) or post-quantized latent tokens from the Mimi neural audio codec (12.5 Hz, Q=8) as conditioning, and produces a high-resolution waveform signal. The Mel-spectrogram is encoded by a ConvNeXt V2 encoder and upsampled 4× before being downsampled by period-specific strides to match each period path's spatial dimensions. For codec tokens, the energy-based prior is removed since energy cannot be extracted from discrete representations, and an additional upsampling layer bridges the 12.5 Hz token rate to 24 kHz waveform.

## Method

PeriodWave introduces a period-aware flow matching estimator that reshapes the 1D waveform into 2D tensors of shape (T/p × p) for prime periods [1,2,3,5,7] (the 'Periodify' operation), then processes all periods through a single UNet conditioned on a learned period embedding, summing the per-period outputs before estimating the vector field. Training uses conditional flow matching with optimal transport paths (OT-CFM), regressing the network's vector field to the linear OT target with a data-dependent energy prior extracted from the Mel-spectrogram. To address high-frequency modeling limitations, the paper applies FreeU (scaling skip connections by α=0.9 and backbone features by β=1.1) and, in the multi-band variant (PeriodWave-MB), decomposes the signal into four frequency bands via discrete wavelet transform with sequential low-to-high band generation. A period-conditional universal estimator enables parallel batch inference across periods, and the Midpoint ODE solver with 16 steps achieves quality comparable to GAN baselines trained for 5–10× more steps using only a single vector-field regression loss.

## Results

On LibriTTS (24 kHz, multi-speaker), PeriodWave-MB (16 steps) achieves a MOS of 3.95±0.03, matching the ground-truth MOS of 3.94±0.03, while outperforming BigVGAN (3.92±0.03) and Vocos (3.89±0.03). PeriodWave-MB attains the best Periodicity (0.0704) and Pitch error (16.829 Hz) among all methods, compared to BigVGAN's 0.1018 and 25.651 Hz respectively. On LJSpeech (22.05 kHz), PeriodWave+FreeU (16 steps) achieves UTMOS 4.3578, surpassing BigVGAN (4.2172) and HiFi-GAN (4.2691), with the best Periodicity of 0.0749. Notably, PeriodWave trains in only 1M steps (~3 days) versus 5M steps (~3+ weeks) for BigVGAN, while using a single loss function instead of multiple adversarial losses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PeriodWave-MB outperforms BigVGAN (112M params, 5M steps) on PESQ (4.262 vs 4.027), Periodicity (0.0704 vs 0.1018), V/UV F1 (0.9678 vs 0.9598), Pitch error (16.829 vs 25.651 Hz), and MOS (3.95 vs 3.92), while using fewer parameters and 5× fewer training steps; however, it trails BigVGAN on M-STFT (0.9729 vs 0.7997), reflecting the absence of spectral-domain losses. Against Vocos (13.53M params), PeriodWave-MB wins on PESQ (4.262 vs 3.615), Periodicity (0.0704 vs 0.1113), Pitch (16.829 vs 24.075), and MOS (3.95 vs 3.89), but again loses on M-STFT (0.9729 vs 0.8544). Compared to UnivNet-c32, PeriodWave-MB dominates on all metrics except M-STFT, with a particularly large gap in Pitch error (16.829 vs 53.021 Hz). In the zero-shot TTS setting (Table 9), PeriodWave+FreeU achieves UTMOS 4.2621 and MOS 4.07, surpassing BigVGAN (4.0424 / 4.03) and BigVSAN (3.9732 / 3.99), demonstrating that iterative flow-matching sampling mitigates the train-inference mismatch that plagues one-step GAN vocoders. On out-of-distribution MUSDB18-HQ music samples, PeriodWave-MB achieves the best average SMOS (3.63) and Periodicity (0.0945), outperforming BigVGAN (3.56 / 0.0959) and Vocos (3.29 / 0.1305).

## Relation to Prior Work

PeriodWave is the first work to apply conditional flow matching with optimal transport paths to waveform generation, repurposing a framework previously established for image and audio generation in other domains. Its period-aware UNet architecture directly adapts the multi-period concept from HiFi-GAN's discriminator (Kong et al., 2020) into the generator side, while the data-dependent prior follows PriorGrad (Lee et al., 2022b) and the FreeU skip-connection scaling is transferred from image diffusion (Si et al., 2024) to the waveform domain. The multi-band variant with discrete wavelet transforms extends the frequency-disentanglement idea from Multi-Band Diffusion (Roman et al., 2023) but replaces STFT-based band splitting with lossless DWT decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3-day training time (vs. 3+ weeks for GAN-based vocoders) and single-loss training paradigm offer a practical efficiency win for the vocoder community, while the iterative sampling naturally mitigates the train-inference mismatch problem that plagues one-step GAN vocoders in two-stage TTS pipelines. The demonstration on Mimi codec decoding and streaming generation positions PeriodWave as a viable alternative decoder for speech language models. However, the consistently worse M-STFT metric compared to GAN baselines, the slower inference speed (7.5× vs. 38–167× for GANs at 16 steps), and the acknowledged high-frequency limitations suggest that flow matching vocoders are not yet a drop-in replacement for GAN vocoders in latency-sensitive or spectral-fidelity-critical applications.
