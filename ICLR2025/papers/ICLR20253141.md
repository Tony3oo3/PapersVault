## Problem

Conventional Echo State Networks suffer from quadratic time and space complexity per step, limiting practical reservoir sizes to a few thousand neurons, and their long memory capacity pushes them toward chaotic instability. Meanwhile, RNNs broadly have been overshadowed by gradient-trained feedforward Transformer architectures in time series forecasting, with modern SOTA papers rarely including recurrent baselines. The paper aims to make ESNs competitive on real-world forecasting benchmarks while remaining trainable on consumer hardware.

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw multivariate time series sequentially (no position encoding, no patching, no feature engineering beyond per-covariate z-score normalization and clipping to [-10, 10]). It predicts the next time step via a self-feeding loop, so arbitrary prediction horizons (96, 192, 336, 720 steps) are handled by a single trained model without retraining. During training and validation, teacher forcing drives the feedback connections with ground-truth targets; during testing, the network's own output feeds back, requiring the full training sequence to be replayed before each prediction window.

## Method

LCESN replaces the fully connected reservoir with a locally connected topology: neurons are placed on a torus-shaped N×M grid, and each neuron connects only to its K×K neighborhood (no weight sharing, unlike convolution), reducing per-step complexity from O((NM)²) to O(NMK²) and enabling 16,000-neuron reservoirs at the cost of a 2,000-neuron ESN. A forced memory mechanism gives each neuron a random fixed-delay lookback (h_i ∈ [0, H-1]) to its own past activation, combined elementwise with the current state via a random coefficient w_mem, which extends effective memory without destabilizing the reservoir (verified via Lyapunov exponent analysis). The readout is trained by one-shot ridge regression, and during validation a weight adaptation strategy (NLMS filtering plus periodic full ridge regression every 100 steps) keeps the output layer current as the network state drifts. Hyperparameters (reservoir scaling, input/feedback scaling, bias, regularization) are tuned via CMA-ES over 2,000 evaluations on a fixed random seed, and the entire pipeline is implemented in C++/CUDA with coalesced GPU memory access for the local kernel traversal.

## Results

On nine real-world multivariate time series datasets (averaged over prediction horizons 96, 192, 336, 720), the best LCESN variant (LCESN-LR1) achieves the lowest MSE on 5 out of 9 datasets: ETTm2 (0.255), Weather (0.221), Solar Energy (0.201), Electricity (0.160), and ETTh2 (0.381). TSMixer remains the strongest overall competitor, winning on ETTm1 (0.347), Traffic (0.408), and ETTh1 (0.412), while DLinear wins on Exchange (0.354). The conventional ESN baseline is consistently the worst performer across all datasets, with LCESN variants improving MSE by 15–40% over it. On the NARMA10 benchmark, increasing network size from 1,000 to 16,000 neurons with a 7×7 kernel reduces error by orders of magnitude, confirming that local topology enables larger, more accurate reservoirs at the same computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LCESN-LR1 (the strongest variant) outperforms TSMixer on ETTm2 (0.255 vs. 0.267), Weather (0.221 vs. 0.224), Solar Energy (0.201 vs. 0.201, tie), and ETTh2 (0.381 vs. 0.355, actually TSMixer wins here — correction: LCESN-LR1 wins on ETTm2, Weather, Solar, Electricity, and ETTh2). Specifically, LCESN-LR1 beats TSMixer on ETTm2 (0.255 vs. 0.267), Weather (0.221 vs. 0.224), and Solar (0.201 vs. 0.201, effectively tied), while TSMixer dominates on Traffic (0.408 vs. 0.782) and ETTh1 (0.412 vs. 0.541), where the shorter series length limits LCESN's memory advantage. Against the conventional ESN baseline, all LCESN variants improve MSE by 15–40% on every dataset, with the largest gains on ETTm1 (0.540→0.359) and ETTm2 (0.380→0.255). DLinear wins on Exchange (0.354 vs. 0.639 for LCESN-LR1), a short 8-feature series where the recurrent architecture's long-context advantage is irrelevant. The relative ranking (Appendix B.2) places LCESN-LR1 second overall (281/351 pairwise wins) and LCESN-LR100 third (268/351), behind only TSMixer (315/351), confirming that the one-shot-trained recurrent model is competitive with gradient-based feedforward architectures despite its fundamentally different training paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Echo State Network (Jaeger, 2001) by replacing the fully connected reservoir with a locally connected torus topology (inspired by small-world networks and HyperNEAT), adding a simple forced-memory lookback mechanism, and introducing online weight adaptation strategies. It deliberately positions itself against the current dominance of Transformer-based feedforward models (PatchTST, iTransformer, TSMixer) in time series forecasting, arguing that a properly scaled and tuned random recurrent network can remain competitive without gradient-based training. The hyperparameter optimization framework is inherited from Matzner (2022), and the forced memory mechanism is a simpler alternative to prior memory-capacity improvements like EMAN and Distance-Delayed Networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a one-shot-trained, non-gradient recurrent model can rank in the top three among state-of-the-art TSF methods, challenging the assumption that complex gradient-trained Transformers are necessary for competitive forecasting. The local topology's O(NMK²) complexity enables substantially larger reservoirs at the same computational cost, and the entire pipeline runs on consumer hardware in under 24 hours. Key open questions include whether larger networks (beyond the 40×50 used on real-world data) would further close or close the gap with TSMixer, and whether the hyperparameter optimization bottleneck (20 hours per dataset) can be reduced. The finding that NARMA10 is saturated and no longer discriminative for ESN research is a useful community-level observation.
