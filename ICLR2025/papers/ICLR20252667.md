## Problem

Video LLMs consistently fail at basic temporal reasoning tasks (e.g., ordering events, tracking attribute changes), yet prior work attributed this to inadequate temporal encoding in vision modules. This paper's diagnostic probing reveals the true bottleneck lies in the LLM backbone's inherent inability to process temporal concepts, even when given explicit textual descriptions of video frames — a finding that redirects the solution from improving visual encoders to strengthening the language model's temporal reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252667/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives 8 uniformly sampled video frames plus a multiple-choice temporal question and outputs a single letter answer. The key methodological twist is in training: T3 constructs QA pairs entirely from sequences of image captions (sourced from LLaVA-ReCap-558K) interleaved with distractor captions, so the LLM is fine-tuned on pure text with no video or image tokens. For holistic benchmark evaluation, the text-only T3 samples are mixed with original LLaVA-Next image-text instruction data at a 1:2 ratio to preserve visual perception.

## Method

The paper first diagnoses the bottleneck via three controlled probes: (i) a small LSTM classifier on visual features in the LLM embedding space achieves >90% accuracy on temporal discrimination, (ii) standalone LLMs given GPT-4o frame captions still perform near-random on temporal questions, and (iii) full Video LLMs score below 70% — isolating the LLM decoder as the limiting component. T3 then synthesizes temporal reasoning QA pairs across four aspects (Order, Attribute, Temporal Referring, Temporal Grounding) using GPT-4-turbo to generate questions and modified captions, with distractor captions inserted to increase robustness. The resulting text-only dataset (22k–200k samples) is used to fine-tune LongVA-7B with no video data, yielding a 5.3-point gain on TempCompass and 12.4-point gain on Video-MME temporal reasoning. Ablations show self-attention modules (not MLP layers) are the critical components for temporal transfer, and the enhanced model better leverages increased frame counts (32→128).

## Results

On TempCompass, T3-enhanced LongVA-7B achieves 61.2 average accuracy (up from 55.9 baseline), outperforming ShareGPT4Video-8B (59.4) which was trained on 28,000 video samples, using no video data at all. On Video-MME, the T3 model reaches 49.7 on Temporal Reasoning (vs. 37.3 baseline), surpassing VILA1.5-40B (40.7) and InternVL-Chat-V1.5-20B (33.3), with an overall score of 55.0. On MLVU, the macro average improves from 56.4 to 58.1, with Action Order gaining +12.7 points (41.7→54.4) and Action Count gaining +3.9 points (25.2→29.1). GPT-4o remains the strongest reference at 71.0 (TempCompass) and 71.9 (Video-MME overall).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The headline comparison is against ShareGPT4Video-8B (Chen et al., 2024), which was trained on 28,000 video samples annotated by GPT-4V: T3-enhanced LongVA-7B achieves 61.2 vs. 59.4 on TempCompass average, winning by 1.8 points without any video training data. On Video-MME Temporal Reasoning, T3-enhanced LongVA-7B (49.7) decisively outperforms both VILA1.5-40B (40.7) and InternVL-Chat-V1.5-20B (33.3), despite being a 7B model. Against Llama-3-VILA1.5-8B, the T3 model leads on TempCompass (61.2 vs. 56.4) and Video-MME overall (55.0 vs. 45.9). The method also surpasses LLaVA-NeXT-Video-7B-DPO (61.2 vs. 50.6 on TempCompass) and Video-LLaVA-7B (61.2 vs. 45.6). However, GPT-4o remains substantially ahead across all benchmarks (71.0 TempCompass, 71.9 Video-MME, 64.6 MLVU), indicating a remaining gap to frontier commercial models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of work that attributes Video LLM temporal reasoning failures to ineffective visual encoding, which has motivated a proliferation of temporal aggregation modules (TimeChat, Koala, spatial-temporal Q-Formers). Using classifier probes (Alain & Bengio, 2017) on synthesized videos, the authors show that visual representations already contain sufficient temporal information, and the true bottleneck lies in the LLM decoder's inability to process temporal concepts even in pure text. Building on LongVA as the backbone, the paper introduces T3, a text-only temporal reasoning transfer framework that synthesizes QA pairs from image captions (sourced from LLaVA-ReCap-558K) to enhance the LLM's temporal reasoning without any video training data, contrasting with prior approaches like ShareGPT4Video that rely on large-scale video instruction tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnostic finding that the LLM backbone—not the vision encoder—is the primary bottleneck in Video LLM temporal reasoning redirects the field's improvement efforts from architectural changes to the language model side, with practical implications for training efficiency (text-only data is far cheaper to generate than video instruction data). The approach's limitations are acknowledged: it struggles with continuous motion concepts like precise rotation counts, and gains vary across model scales. Open questions include whether more complex temporal scenarios (multi-step causal chains, nested temporal hierarchies) can be captured through textual augmentation, and whether the self-attention-centric mechanism identified here generalizes to other reasoning domains beyond temporal understanding.
