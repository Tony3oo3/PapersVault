## Problem

Existing multi-task RL methods for LTL specifications are limited to finite-horizon fragments, produce myopic (globally suboptimal) solutions by decomposing tasks into independent subtasks, and fail to incorporate safety constraints during high-level planning. This paper addresses all three shortcomings simultaneously for the first time.

## Input / Output

At test time, the method takes an arbitrary LTL formula and the current environment observation (a 7×7 grid image in LetterWorld, or a high-dimensional state vector with lidar data in ZoneEnv/FlatWorld). The LTL formula is converted to an LDBA, from which all paths to accepting cycles are extracted as reach-avoid sequences of truth assignments; the optimal sequence is selected via the learned value function and fed to the policy. The output is an action distribution (categorical over 4 directions in discrete spaces, Gaussian mean/std in continuous spaces), with an additional ε-action dimension when the LDBA has jump transitions.

## Method

The core insight is to represent the current LDBA state as a set of reach-avoid sequences—ordered pairs of positive (must-achieve) and negative (must-avoid) truth-assignment sets extracted from all paths to accepting cycles in the Büchi automaton. The policy is a modular network: DeepSets encode each set of assignments in a permutation-invariant way, a GRU (applied backwards) encodes the truncated sequence, and an actor module combines this with environment features to output actions. Training uses goal-conditioned PPO with curriculum learning over randomly sampled reach-avoid sequences of increasing length and complexity, requiring no access to a distribution over LTL formulas. At test time, the method is non-myopic because it selects the optimal sequence by maximizing the value function over the full accepting-cycle structure, and safety constraints are enforced during this planning step via negative assignments rather than only during execution.

## Results

On complex finite-horizon LTL specifications, DeepLTL achieves near-perfect success rates (SR ≥ 0.90) across all three environments while requiring substantially fewer steps than baselines (e.g., 9.66 steps vs. 29.48 for LTL2Action and 15.29 for GCRL-LTL on φ₁ in LetterWorld; 303 steps vs. 424 and 452 on φ₆ in ZoneEnv). On infinite-horizon (ω-regular) recurrence tasks, DeepLTL completes 17.84 cycles vs. 6.74 for GCRL-LTL on ψ₁ (LetterWorld) and 12.12 vs. 7.20 on ψ₅ (FlatWorld). On an adversarial safety task requiring planning-level safety reasoning, DeepLTL achieves 79.6% SR compared to 9.2% for GCRL-LTL and 50.2% for LTL2Action.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LTL2Action (Vaezipoor et al., 2021), DeepLTL wins on 15 of 16 finite-horizon tasks in both SR and μ, with the largest gaps on complex nested specifications (e.g., 1.00 vs. 0.41 SR on φ₃; 1.00 vs. 0.07 on φ₁₅). LTL2Action is restricted to the finite-horizon LTL fragment and cannot handle ω-regular tasks at all. Against GCRL-LTL (Qiu et al., 2023), DeepLTL achieves higher SR on 13 of 16 finite-horizon tasks and fewer steps on all 16, with the efficiency gap most pronounced in ZoneEnv (e.g., 187 vs. 291 steps on φ₁₀). On infinite-horizon recurrence tasks, DeepLTL completes 2.6× more cycles than GCRL-LTL on average (e.g., 17.84 vs. 6.74 on ψ₁), confirming the advantage of non-myopic planning over subtask composition. On the adversarial safety task, DeepLTL's 79.6% SR dramatically exceeds GCRL-LTL's 9.2%, demonstrating that considering safety constraints during high-level sequence selection (rather than only during execution) is critical. The paper also compares to RAD-embeddings (Yalcinkaya et al., 2024) in Appendix G.8, finding DeepLTL superior due to its separation of high-level planning from low-level action selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on LTL-conditioned multi-task RL (Vaezipoor et al., 2021; Qiu et al., 2023) by replacing formula-syntax encodings (GNNs over syntax trees) and myopic subtask composition with a representation extracted directly from the structure of limit-deterministic Büchi automata (Sickert et al., 2016). The key conceptual departure is the use of reach-avoid sequences of truth assignments as the conditioning signal, which encodes all possible ways of satisfying a specification from the current automaton state, rather than encoding the formula itself or decomposing it into independent subtasks. The training paradigm follows standard goal-conditioned RL (Liu et al., 2022) with PPO, and the theoretical grounding for probability-optimal policies draws on eventual discounting (Voloshin et al., 2023), though the paper's practical objective (Problem 1) uses standard discounting to incorporate efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DeepLTL is the first approach that simultaneously handles infinite-horizon (ω-regular) LTL specifications, reasons non-myopically about the full specification structure, and incorporates safety constraints during high-level planning rather than only during execution. This addresses three concrete limitations that have prevented prior methods from producing efficient, safe policies for complex temporal tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to environments with many atomic propositions (where LDBA construction and the set of reach-avoid sequences can grow), the formal relationship to discounted LTL semantics (noted but deferred), and whether the strict-negative-assignment threshold can be learned rather than fixed. The method's reliance on constructing LDBAs also means it inherits the worst-case exponential blowup of automata construction, though in practice LDBAs are compact for common specifications.
