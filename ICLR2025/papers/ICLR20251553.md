## Problem

Despite the widespread practical use of spectral invariants in GNN design, the theoretical understanding of their precise expressive power remains incomplete. Prior analyses relied on Weisfeiler-Lehman tests, which have inherent limitations in capturing fine-grained expressivity differences. This paper asks: what is the exact homomorphism expressivity of spectral invariant GNNs, and how does it quantitatively relate to other GNN architectures?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251553/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The spectral invariant GNN consumes a graph's adjacency matrix to compute eigenvalues and orthogonal projection matrices, which together form a node-pair feature P(u,v) = {(λ, P_λ(u,v))} used as edge features in a color refinement process. The output is a multiset of stable node colors (a graph invariant) after d iterations, where each color encodes the full walk-counting profile between the node and all others. The key structural detail is that the refinement treats the entire spectrum-projection tuple as a single edge feature, making the architecture invariant to eigenvector sign choices.

## Method

The paper proves that the homomorphism expressivity of a d-iteration spectral invariant GNN is exactly the family of parallel trees with depth at most d, where a parallel tree is a tree skeleton with each edge replaced by a parallel edge (multiple paths sharing the same endpoints). The proof proceeds in three steps: (1) establishing equivalence between the spectral invariant GNN and a walk-encoding GNN via the Cayley-Hamilton theorem, showing that projection matrices are linear combinations of walk counts; (2) characterizing the homomorphism expressivity through unfolding trees and a strong homomorphism counting formula; and (3) proving maximality via a novel pebble game adapted for spectral invariant GNNs, where counterexamples are constructed using Fürer graphs and their twisted variants. This characterization yields a quantitative hierarchy (spectra < 1-iteration spectral GNN < 2-iteration < ... < 2-FWL < Local 2-GNN), tight iteration lower bounds of Θ(n), and precise subgraph counting capabilities (e.g., all cycles up to 7 vertices within 2 iterations).

## Results

On the homomorphism counting benchmark, the Spectral Invariant GNN successfully encodes 2 out of 5 substructures (MAE ≈ 0.045–0.053), compared to 0 for MPNN, 3 for Subgraph GNN, and 4 for Local 2-GNN. On ZINC-subset, the Spectral Invariant GNN achieves MAE 0.103±0.006, outperforming MPNN (0.138±0.006) but trailing Local 2-GNN (0.069±0.001). For substructure (cycle) counting, the Spectral Invariant GNN accurately counts 3-, 4-, 5-, and 6-cycles (MAE ≤ 0.099) but fails on the 7-cycle, consistent with the theoretical bound of Corollary 3.14.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MPNN, the Spectral Invariant GNN clearly wins: it encodes 2 homomorphism substructures (vs. 0 for MPNN), achieves lower ZINC-subset MAE (0.103 vs. 0.138), and successfully counts 3- through 6-cycles where MPNN fails on all. Against Subgraph GNN, the Spectral Invariant GNN is strictly weaker on homomorphism counting (2 vs. 3 substructures encoded) and substructure counting (higher MAE on all cycle lengths), but achieves a slightly lower ZINC-subset MAE (0.103 vs. 0.110), consistent with the theoretical finding that 1-iteration spectral invariants are strictly weaker than Subgraph GNN while multi-iteration variants become incomparable. Against Local 2-GNN, the Spectral Invariant GNN is uniformly outperformed across all three task categories (homomorphism, ZINC, and substructure counting), aligning with the theoretical result that spectral invariant GNNs are strictly weaker than Local 2-GNN for any depth d.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the homomorphism expressivity framework introduced by Zhang et al. (2024a) — which previously characterized MPNNs, Subgraph GNNs, Local GNNs, and Folklore GNNs — to the class of spectral invariant GNNs, bridging two previously orthogonal branches of graph theory (homomorphism theory and spectral invariants). It builds directly on the WL-based expressivity analyses of spectral invariants by Fürer (2010), Rattan & Seppelt (2023), Arvind et al. (2024), and Zhang et al. (2024b), but replaces the WL lens with the finer-grained homomorphism-counting perspective. The proof technique draws on the pebble game (Cai et al., 1992) and Fürer graphs (Fürer, 2001), adapted to the spectral setting, and on the walk-encoding equivalence that connects spectral projections to walk counts via the Cayley-Hamilton theorem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper settles a key open question from Arvind et al. (2024) by showing spectral invariant GNNs require Ω(n) iterations for maximal expressivity, disproving the conjecture of constant-iteration convergence. The parallel-tree characterization provides a clean, quantitative hierarchy across GNN architectures (spectra → projection → refinement → 2-FWL → Local 2-GNN) that was previously only partially understood through WL comparisons. Open questions remain about whether the parallel-tree characterization extends to other spectral-based architectures (e.g., those using Laplacian or signless Laplacian spectra), and whether the O(n) iteration lower bound is tight for all graph families or only for the specific counterexample constructions. The higher-order and symmetric-power extensions suggest the framework is broadly applicable, but full characterizations for k > 2 remain in the appendix and less developed.
