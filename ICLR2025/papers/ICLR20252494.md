## Problem

Existing preference optimization methods like DPO and its variants fundamentally adhere to the Bradley-Terry reward maximization objective, making them prone to overfitting and causing them to degrade reasoning abilities (e.g., on GSM8K, MATH). The paper asks whether preference alignment can be understood and redesigned from an imitation learning perspective, bypassing the BT assumption entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252494/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes offline preference triplets (prompt x, chosen response y_w, rejected response y_l) from datasets like UltraFeedback, Anthropic-HH, and Reddit TL;DR. Rejected responses serve as samples from the reference policy π_ref while chosen responses approximate the target distribution π_chosen. The output is a single aligned LLM policy π_θ with no auxiliary reward model or discriminator at inference time, and the loss contains no tunable hyperparameters.

## Method

The paper proves that RLHF's reward-model training is equivalent to forward-KL behavior cloning on chosen responses via an energy-based policy, and that the PPO step is reverse-KL distillation of that EBM into an analytical policy. Building on this, DIL directly minimizes the reverse KL divergence between π_θ and the unknown π_chosen by reformulating the objective as maximizing an expected log density ratio r(x,y) = π_chosen/π_ref minus a KL penalty to π_ref. The density ratio is estimated via Bregman divergence (LSIF by default, with UKL and BCE as alternatives), and the closed-form optimal policy allows reparameterizing the density ratio as log(π_θ/π_ref), yielding a simple two-term loss with no hyperparameters. DPO is shown to be a special case of DIL that uses contrastive predictive coding (InfoNCE) for density ratio estimation instead of Bregman divergence.

## Results

On the Open LLM Leaderboard benchmarks, DIL w/ LSIF achieves the best or near-best scores across both Mistral-7B-Base and Llama3-8B-Base models. For Llama3-8B, DIL scores 32.22 on MMLU-PRO, 48.78 on BBH, 42.75 on MUSR, 4.68 on MATH, 48.98 on GSM8K, and 65.37 on ARC, outperforming the strongest baselines (SimPO, DPO) on all six benchmarks. On GPT-4 pairwise evaluation, DIL achieves a 69.17% average win rate on Anthropic-HH and 67.86% on TL;DR summarization, surpassing the best baseline (CPO at 67.86% and 66.01% respectively). Notably, DIL preserves reasoning ability, with a 7.5%+ relative gain over SimPO on MATH for Llama3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIL w/ LSIF outperforms all seven baselines (DPO, SLiC, f-DPO, IPO, KTO, CPO, SimPO) on the majority of benchmarks for both Mistral-7B and Llama3-8B. The most striking gains are on reasoning-heavy tasks: DIL achieves 4.68 on MATH vs. 4.23 (SimPO) and 4.53 (DPO) for Llama3, and 48.98 on GSM8K vs. 31.54 (SimPO) and 38.67 (DPO), indicating that DPO and SimPO degrade reasoning ability while DIL preserves it. DIL also leads on MMLU-PRO (32.22 vs. 31.61 SimPO) and BBH (48.78 vs. 48.38 SimPO) for Llama3. The one notable exception is GSM8K on Mistral-7B, where KTO (36.58) edges out DIL (32.19). On GPT-4 pairwise evaluation, DIL achieves 69.17% average win rate on Anthropic-HH, beating the best baseline CPO (67.86%) and DPO (64.34%), and 67.86% on TL;DR vs. CPO's 66.01%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes RLHF as a two-step imitation learning problem: reward modeling as forward-KL behavior cloning on chosen responses (via energy-based policies), and the RL step as reverse-KL knowledge distillation into an analytical policy. It departs from the DPO line of work (Rafailov et al., 2024) by showing DPO is a special case of a more general density-ratio-based imitation learning objective, where DPO corresponds to using contrastive predictive coding (InfoNCE) for density ratio estimation. The core technical machinery is borrowed from the Bregman divergence framework for density ratio estimation (Sugiyama et al., 2012), instantiated with LSIF, UKL, and BCE, and applied to the preference alignment setting for the first time in this unified form.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical unification provides a principled lens for designing alignment losses: any valid Bregman divergence yields a valid DIL variant, and the framework naturally avoids the Bradley-Terry assumption that underpins DPO and its variants. The training dynamics analysis revealing that DPO and SimPO progressively decrease chosen-response likelihood (explaining their degradation on reasoning tasks) while DIL preserves it offers a concrete diagnostic for practitioners. Open questions include whether the offline-only formulation extends to on-policy settings, which density-ratio estimator is theoretically optimal for alignment, and whether the connection to adversarial IL (GAIL/AIRL) can be exploited for more robust training.
