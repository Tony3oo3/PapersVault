## Problem

Existing metrics for comparing neural representations—Procrustes shape distance, Stochastic Shape Distance (SSD), and Dynamical Similarity Analysis (DSA)—each capture only a subset of the structure in noisy dynamic neural responses: mean trajectories, marginal noise correlations, or recurrent flow fields, respectively. The paper demonstrates that these metrics can fail to distinguish systems with identical marginal statistics but fundamentally different across-time statistical dependencies, such as systems where information is revealed early versus late in a trajectory.

## Input / Output

The method takes two neural processes, each specified by a sequence of N-dimensional mean vectors over T time steps and a full NT×NT across-time covariance matrix (not just per-time-point marginals). If the two processes have different dimensions, the lower-dimensional one is zero-padded to match. The output is a single scalar Causal OT distance; when applied to a collection of processes (e.g., 60 conditional diffusion trajectories), it yields a pairwise distance matrix that can be visualized or embedded via MDS.

## Method

The Causal OT distance is derived as the minimal L² transport cost between two Gaussian processes coupled through a shared white noise source, where the admissible couplings must satisfy a causality (adapted) condition: the past of one process is independent of the future of the other given the past of the other. Concretely, the distance decomposes into a Procrustes term on mean trajectories plus an adapted Bures term on the full covariance matrices, where the adapted Bures distance restricts the alignment rotations to block-diagonal N×N matrices (one per time step) rather than arbitrary NT×NT rotations, thereby preserving temporal ordering. This restriction is what makes the metric sensitive to when information becomes available in a trajectory, unlike the standard Bures/Wasserstein distance which allows arbitrary temporal mixing. Computationally, the distance is evaluated via an alternating minimization algorithm that iterates over the global nuisance rotation Q and the per-time-step noise rotations R_t, each step solved in closed form via Procrustes alignment on stacked Cholesky factor blocks.

## Results

In the scalar example (Sec. 4.1), Causal OT distance equals √(σ²+ε²) ≈ 1.58 (with σ=1.5, ε=0.5), while Procrustes distance is 0 and both 1-SSD and Wasserstein distance equal ε=0.5; critically, as ε→0, Causal OT converges to σ while all other metrics converge to 0. In the motor cortex model (Sec. 4.2), as preparatory activity scale γ→0, Procrustes and SSD distances converge to zero while Causal OT and DSA distances plateau, demonstrating that only the latter capture the across-time statistical structure. In the latent diffusion model experiment (Sec. 4.3), SSD and Causal OT distance matrices exhibit a clear 3×3 block structure reflecting within-category vs. between-category distances and are approximately invariant to model choice (v1-1 vs. v1-2), whereas Procrustes and DSA distance matrices lack this structure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Procrustes shape distance (Williams et al., 2021) fails in every experimental setting involving stochastic or dynamic differences: it yields zero distance for adversarially tuned flow fields with identical mean trajectories, converges to zero as preparatory activity vanishes in the motor model, and produces unstructured distance matrices for diffusion model trajectories. Stochastic Shape Distance (Duong et al., 2023) captures marginal noise correlations and successfully identifies block structure in diffusion model distances, but still fails to distinguish systems with identical marginal statistics yet different across-time dependencies (Fig. 2, motor model with small preparatory activity). Dynamical Similarity Analysis (Ostrow et al., 2023) captures recurrent dynamics and plateaus in the motor model, but is sensitive to two hyperparameters (number of delays and regression rank) and cannot distinguish systems with different noise levels but identical dynamics (Appendix F). The 2-Wasserstein distance between Gaussian processes (Mallasto & Feragen, 2017) treats trajectories as unstructured vectors and loses temporal causality: in the scalar example it converges to zero as ε→0, failing to capture that one process reveals its future immediately while the other does not. Causal OT outperforms all four baselines by simultaneously capturing stochastic structure, dynamic structure, and temporal causality, at the cost of requiring estimation of the full NT×NT covariance matrix.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate lines of work on comparing neural representations: Procrustes shape distance (comparing mean trajectories), Stochastic Shape Distance (comparing marginal noise statistics), and Dynamical Similarity Analysis (comparing recurrent flow fields via Koopman operators). The key conceptual departure is deriving a single metric from causal optimal transport theory between Gaussian processes, where the adapted Bures distance replaces the standard Bures distance to enforce temporal causality in the coupling. This positions the Causal OT distance as a strict generalization of both Procrustes and SSD (recovering them when across-time correlations vanish) while also capturing the dynamic information that DSA targets, without DSA's sensitivity to hyperparameter choices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that no single existing metric captures both stochastic and dynamic structure of neural trajectories, and provides a principled metric that does—important for distinguishing recurrent from input-driven dynamics in motor cortex and for analyzing stochastic generative processes like diffusion models. A notable finding is that mean latent trajectories in Stable Diffusion contain no decodable visual information, yet the stochastic structure does, suggesting that representation-similarity analyses of generative models must account for noise. Key limitations include the Gaussian assumption (only first and second moments), the need for many samples to estimate the full NT×NT covariance matrix, and the fact that the diffusion model experiments use PCA to 10 dimensions, leaving open whether the metric's conclusions hold in the full 16384-dimensional latent space.
