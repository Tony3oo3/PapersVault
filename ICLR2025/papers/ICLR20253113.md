## Problem

Novel view synthesis from sparse visual inputs (single image, few views, or monocular video) remains challenging because existing methods either require per-scene optimization (NeRF, 3D Gaussian Splatting) or produce views with significant pose errors and limited generalization. The paper asks whether a pre-trained large video diffusion model can be repurposed as a zero-shot NVS engine without any additional training, while maintaining both visual quality and geometric (pose) accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253113/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts one or more RGB images with known camera poses (or a monocular video of 25 frames), an estimated depth map per input view (from Depth Anything), and a set of target camera poses, and produces a temporally coherent sequence of 24 novel-view images rendered simultaneously in the SVD latent space over 100 reverse diffusion steps. Because monocular depth estimation yields only relative scale, pose metrics (ATE, RPE) are evaluated via Particle-SFM on the synthesized sequence rather than direct pixel comparison, and paired metrics like LPIPS are reported separately.

## Method

NVS-SOLVER reformulates NVS as a score-modulated reverse diffusion process on a pre-trained Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) model. At each denoising step, the clean-image estimate μ_{t,p_i} from the U-Net is blended with a depth-warped version of the input view at the target pose, producing a modulated score  μ̃_{t,p_i} = (1/(1+λ))·μ_{t,p_i} + (λ/(1+λ))·X̂_{0,p_i}. The blending weight λ(t, p_i) is derived in closed form by minimizing a theoretical upper bound on the estimation error that decomposes into a diffusion error term (proportional to σ(t)) and a warping/truncation error term (proportional to ‖Δp‖), yielding λ̂ = (−(2v₁+Q)+√(Q²+4v₁Q))/(2v₁) with Q = v₃‖Δp‖−v₂σ(t). Two sampling strategies are provided: Directly Guided Sampling (DGS), which substitutes μ̃ directly into the VE-SDE update for speed, and Posterior Sampling, which back-propagates the gradient of ‖μ_t − μ̃_t‖₂ through the U-Net to nudge the noised latent toward the data manifold with a small step size 2e^{−2}√σ(t). The paper proves that the modulated score achieves lower error than inpainting-style guidance and that the posterior update direction lies in the tangent space of the data manifold, ensuring safe on-manifold sampling.

## Results

On single-view NVS (Tanks and Temples), Ours (Post) achieves the best FID of 165.12 and the lowest pose errors (ATE 0.767, RPE-T 0.156, RPE-R 0.170), substantially outperforming MotionCtrl (FID 179.24, ATE 3.851) and 3D-aware (FID 217.19, ATE 2.836). On multi-view NVS, Ours (DGS) achieves the best FID of 124.31, while Ours (Post) achieves the best pose accuracy (ATE 4.052, RPE-T 2.030, RPE-R 0.330). On dynamic scene NVS from monocular video, Ours (DGS) achieves the best FID of 37.97, nearly halving the FID of MotionCtrl (70.35) and 4D-Gaussian (74.34), while also achieving the best RPE-R of 0.446.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against eight baselines spanning Gaussian splatting, NeRF, and diffusion-based methods. Against the strongest zero-shot diffusion baseline MotionCtrl, Ours (Post) reduces single-view FID from 179.24 to 165.12 and ATE from 3.851 to 0.767 (5× improvement), while on dynamic scenes it nearly halves FID (37.97 vs. 70.35). Against 3D-aware, the method achieves substantially lower FID (165.12 vs. 217.19 single-view; 37.97 vs. 159.03 dynamic) and lower pose errors across all metrics. Against training-based methods (Sparse Gaussian, Sparse Nerf, Text2Nerf, Deformable-Gaussian, 4D-Gaussian), the zero-shot method matches or exceeds their FID while requiring no per-scene optimization. The only scenario where a baseline slightly outperforms is multi-view ATE, where 3D-aware reports 2.159* (incomplete evaluation) vs. Ours (Post) 4.052, though the asterisk indicates unreliable pose estimation. On dynamic scenes, 4D-Gaussian achieves a marginally lower ATE (2.087 vs. 2.236) but with significantly worse FID (74.34 vs. 37.97) and RPE-R (0.825 vs. 0.446).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) as a pre-trained video diffusion backbone, repurposing its reverse sampling process for novel view synthesis without any scene-specific training. It departs from prior diffusion-based NVS methods (MotionCtrl, 3D-aware, Text2Nerf) that rely on learned geometric structures or text prompts, instead formulating NVS as a score modulation problem where depth-warped input views serve as scene priors injected adaptively into the diffusion trajectory. The theoretical derivation of the adaptive weight λ(t, p_i) via error-bound minimization distinguishes it from both inpainting-style guidance (which the paper proves is less accurate) and fixed-weight blending approaches, positioning it as a principled bridge between geometric warping and generative diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free paradigm eliminates per-scene optimization (a major bottleneck for NeRF and 3DGS methods), making NVS accessible from a single image or short video with no fitting. However, the method's practical utility is constrained by its speed (1 hour for 25 views with posterior sampling vs. 6 minutes for DGS), and its quality depends on off-the-shelf depth estimation accuracy. The authors suggest the approach could serve as a distillation target for pose-controllable video diffusion models, pointing toward a future where generative models natively handle view synthesis. Open questions include whether the adaptive modulation framework generalizes to more complex dynamics (deformable objects, lighting changes) and whether the speed gap can be closed with faster solvers or distillation.
