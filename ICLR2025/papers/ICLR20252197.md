## Problem

Fine-tuning LLMs on data containing knowledge the model has not seen during pre-training incentivizes the model to fabricate plausible but incorrect answers, a phenomenon known as knowledge inconsistency. No existing fine-tuning strategy effectively disentangles the learning of task skills from the absorption of new knowledge, making it impossible to safely use synthetic or unfamiliar data for fine-tuning without degrading factuality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (e.g., Llama-3-8B) and a task dataset of instruction-response pairs (QA questions with answers, or biography/summary generation requests with ground-truth texts). It produces a fine-tuned model parameterized by a single skill LoRA that, at inference time, generates factual responses grounded in the base model's pre-trained knowledge. The knowledge LoRA used during training is discarded, so the deployed model has no additional parameters beyond the skill LoRA, and the same skill LoRA can be paired with different knowledge LoRAs at inference to control which knowledge the model accesses.

## Method

PREREQ-TUNE introduces a two-stage fine-tuning procedure that explicitly separates knowledge from skill learning. In the prerequisite learning stage, a knowledge LoRA is trained via next-token prediction on a dataset of paraphrased factual statements covering all entities in the task data. In the SFT stage, the knowledge LoRA is frozen and a skill LoRA is trained on top of it to minimize the task loss, so the skill LoRA never needs to absorb knowledge and focuses purely on output format and task structure. During inference, the knowledge LoRA is removed and only the skill LoRA is retained, and the model generalizes its knowledge-grounding behavior to the base model's original pre-trained knowledge. A multi-version variant leverages fictitious synthetic data to create K different knowledge LoRAs with conflicting facts about the same entities, forcing the single skill LoRA to condition its answers on whichever knowledge LoRA is plugged in, thereby enforcing groundedness and enabling a plug-and-play modular architecture.

## Results

PREREQ-TUNE achieves the best performance across all four evaluation tasks: 45.30% FActScore accuracy on biography generation (Persons), 74.35% on medical entity generation, 47.91% on PopQA, and 65.02%/26.22% on HotpotQA comparison/bridging accuracy. Notably, for long-form generation tasks, PREREQ-TUNE was trained entirely on fictitious synthetic data yet outperforms all baselines trained on real data. The method also reduces the number of generated claims (16.0 vs. 20.8 for SFT on Persons), indicating more concise and focused outputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PREREQ-TUNE outperforms all four primary baselines on every task where they overlap. On biography generation (Persons), it beats POPULAR (Ghosal et al., 2024) by +4.14 points (45.30 vs. 41.16) and FACTTUNE (Tian et al., 2024) by +13.37 points (45.30 vs. 31.93). On medical entity generation, it surpasses RL (Kang et al., 2024) by +4.32 points (74.35 vs. 70.03) and FLAME (Lin et al., 2024) by +6.43 points (74.35 vs. 67.92). On PopQA, it edges out SFT by +1.49 points (47.91 vs. 46.42) and POPULAR by +2.60 points (47.91 vs. 45.31). On HotpotQA, it leads SFT by +3.27 on comparison and +0.23 on bridging accuracy. A critical finding is that PREREQ-TUNE was trained on entirely fictitious synthetic data for long-form generation, yet still outperforms all baselines trained on real data, while the ablation SFT^fictitious (same fictitious data without prerequisite learning) performs worst of all, confirming that the prerequisite learning stage is what transforms harmful fictitious data into a beneficial training signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the recently identified knowledge inconsistency problem (Gekhman et al., 2024; Kang et al., 2024; Lin et al., 2024), where fine-tuning on unfamiliar knowledge incentivizes hallucination. Prior solutions addressed this by filtering training data to known entities (POPULAR), using self-generated summaries (FLAME), or applying DPO/RL on top of SFT (FACTTUNE, RL). PREREQ-TUNE departs from these by explicitly disentangling knowledge and skill learning through a two-stage LoRA architecture (knowledge LoRA trained first, then frozen during skill LoRA training), and by repurposing fictitious synthetic data—which prior work (Jones et al., 2024) used for consistency with provided evidence—into a tool for enforcing grounding to internal knowledge without any external evidence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular plug-and-play design (knowledge LoRAs as swappable 'knowledge flash drives' with a generic skill LoRA) opens avenues for novel RAG paradigms, privacy-preserving fine-tuning, and knowledge-controlled generation that go well beyond hallucination reduction. A key limitation is that the method does not eliminate hallucinations entirely, and the training-inference gap (knowledge LoRA present during training but absent at inference) remains a theoretical concern even though empirically it generalizes well. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large knowledge bases, whether the disentanglement holds for more complex multi-turn or agentic tasks, and how to handle cases where pre-trained knowledge is genuinely wrong rather than absent.
