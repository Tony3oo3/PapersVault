## Problem

In cooperative multi-agent systems without explicit communication channels, agents must coordinate through implicit signals. Existing approaches rely on Theory of Mind (ToM) to infer others' intentions from observed actions, but ToM-based methods suffer from high computational complexity and inaccurate inferences, especially in complex or dynamic environments. This paper asks whether agents can establish reliable, explicit-like communication protocols purely through their actions in the environment, without requiring costly intention inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251985/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent receives its local partial observation, the joint action-observation history τ_{t,i}, and decoded messages m_{-i} from other agents' scouting actions. The modified action space U' = {U \ U^s, send_info} means the agent first decides whether to communicate; if so, it outputs a discrete one-hot message vector (size |U^s|) that is mapped via the centralized protocol P to a specific scouting action, which is then broadcast to all agents. The message space is discrete and non-binary, and the Gumbel-Softmax relaxation makes the discrete message differentiable for end-to-end gradient training.

## Method

ICP designates a subset of actions (scouting actions) that have no or uniform effects on environment dynamics as information carriers, and establishes a centralized one-to-one mapping P between a discrete message space and these actions, creating an implicit channel analogous to explicit communication. Two training strategies are proposed: (1) a random initial map where the mapping is fixed at initialization and both the action policy (VDN with parameter sharing) and message strategy (Q-networks trained via Gumbel-Softmax communication gradients) are learned jointly in an on-policy fashion; and (2) a delayed map where agents first learn communication strategies on a direct channel (e.g., via RGMComm), then the learned messages are mapped to scouting actions (optionally using hat mapping for efficient multi-receiver broadcast), followed by fine-tuning in the implicit channel. The key insight is that because all agents share the same scouting-action selection strategy, the inverse of P directly decodes the intended message, eliminating the need for ToM-based state estimation or intention inference while preserving the broadcast nature of observable actions.

## Results

On the Guessing Numbers task (N=3, l=11), ICN-DIAL-RM achieves an average episode length of ~8.63 steps, compared to ~12.4 for both VDN-on-policy and VDN-off-policy, demonstrating more efficient information transfer. On Revealing Goals (N=4, H=5, T=50), ICN-DIAL-RM achieves an average score 2.17× that of the VDN baselines. On 4-player Hanabi, ICN-RGMComm-DM-hat-mapping achieves a score of 24.91/25 with a 91% win rate, surpassing SAD (23.81/25, 41.45% win rate) and approaching the search-based state-of-the-art WTFWThat+search (24.96/25).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VDN (Sunehag et al., 2018), ICP wins decisively on all three tasks: on Guessing Numbers, ICN-DIAL-RM reduces the average episode length from ~12.4 to ~8.63 steps (a ~30% reduction), and on Revealing Goals it achieves 2.17× the baseline score, confirming that the implicit channel transmits information more efficiently than relying solely on environmental feedback. Against DIAL (Foerster et al., 2016), the paper uses DIAL-Cheat (a direct communication channel) as an upper-bound reference; ICP's implicit channel approaches this ceiling without requiring an explicit channel, validating the protocol's design. Against SAD (Hu & Foerster, 2019) on Hanabi, ICP with hat mapping (ICN-RGMComm-DM-hat-mapping) achieves 24.91/25 versus SAD's 23.81/25, and more importantly, the win rate jumps from 41.45% to 91%, indicating ICP solves a far larger fraction of deals to the maximum score. ICP also substantially outperforms ToM-based methods (19.13) and average human play (17.0) on Hanabi, while remaining within 0.05 points of the search-based state-of-the-art (24.96).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the explicit communication literature (DIAL's communication gradient, RGMComm's return-gap minimization) and repurposes their message-encoding machinery to operate through the environment's action space rather than a dedicated channel. It departs from the dominant ToM-based implicit communication paradigm by replacing intention inference with a shared, centrally-defined mapping between discrete messages and a subset of 'scouting actions' that have no or uniform environmental effect. The hat-mapping broadcast technique draws on combinatorial hat-guessing games to enable one transmission to carry individualized information to multiple receivers, a mechanism not previously exploited in MARL communication.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ICP offers a practical middle ground between explicit channels (which may be unavailable) and ToM inference (which is computationally expensive and error-prone in complex settings), and its compatibility with diverse action-policy and communication algorithms makes it a plug-in framework. The near-optimal Hanabi result (24.91/25, 91% win rate) demonstrates that learned implicit conventions can approach the theoretical ceiling, but the framework's applicability is limited to environments that naturally contain scouting actions; extending ICP to settings where no such actions exist remains an open challenge. The paper also leaves open how to jointly optimize the environment-reflected information and the choice-encoded information carried by scouting actions.
