## Problem

Model-based offline RL methods struggle with inaccurate value estimation from model rollouts, particularly in long-horizon sparse-reward tasks where they achieve near-zero scores (e.g., D4RL AntMaze). Prior approaches penalize Q-values using heuristically estimated uncertainties, which prevents exploitation of erroneous estimates but also prevents the policy from maximizing true value, leading to suboptimal behavior. The paper asks whether a simpler, more principled conservative value estimation can replace uncertainty-based penalization while remaining computationally efficient.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed offline trajectory dataset of (state, action, reward, next-state) tuples and a ground-truth termination function, with no online environment interactions. It supports both state-vector inputs (4-layer MLP world models, 3-layer MLP policy/critic) and RGB image inputs (via DreamerV3 12M-parameter world model). The output is a deterministic policy π_θ(s) producing a single action per state, trained jointly with a single Q-network Q_φ(s,a) that provides conservative value estimates. The world model ensemble (5 of 7 best-validated models) is sampled independently at each rollout step to diversify imaginary trajectories.

## Method

LEQ replaces uncertainty-based penalization with lower expectile regression (τ ≤ 0.5) on target Q-values from model rollouts, learning a Q-function that systematically underestimates rather than relying on heuristic uncertainty estimates. Both the critic and policy are trained using λ-returns (TD(λ) targets with λ=0.95) computed over 10-step imaginary rollouts, reducing bias from single-step bootstrapping. The critic loss combines the expectile loss on model-generated data (weighted by β=0.25), a standard Bellman loss on offline transitions, and an EMA regularization term. The policy is updated using a differentiable surrogate loss that applies asymmetric weighting |τ − 1(Q_φ > Q_t^λ)| to λ-returns, giving higher weight to conservative estimates and lower weight to optimistic ones, which the paper proves provides a lower-bias approximation of the true expectile objective than direct Q-value maximization.

## Results

On the long-horizon D4RL AntMaze benchmark, LEQ achieves a total success rate of 470.4, dramatically outperforming the previous best model-based method IQL-TD-MPC (348.5) and surpassing model-free IQL (378.0), while also beating sequence-modeling baselines TT (494.8) and TAP (517.0) on the most challenging ultra mazes (55.8 vs 26.0 on ultra-diverse). On NeoRL locomotion tasks, LEQ scores 572.4, exceeding MOBILE (546.5) and CQL (504.6). On D4RL MuJoCo Gym, LEQ achieves 923.2, competitive with MOBILE (959.5) and CBOP (953.4), and on pixel-based V-D4RL, LEQ reaches 307.6, on par with ROSMO (310.9) and DrQ+BC (321.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEQ decisively outperforms all prior model-based offline RL methods on AntMaze: MOBILE and CBOP score 0.0 across all AntMaze tasks, COMBO and RAMBO score near zero on large/ultra mazes, and IQL-TD-MPC (the strongest prior model-based method) reaches only 4.0 on large-diverse and 3.6 on ultra-diverse versus LEQ's 60.2 and 55.8. Against model-free baselines, LEQ surpasses CQL and IQL on umaze, large, and ultra mazes, though IQL remains competitive on medium mazes. On NeoRL, LEQ (572.4) beats MOBILE (546.5), CQL (504.6), TD3+BC (490.1), EDAC (456.6), and IQL (385.0), with particularly large gains on Hopper-M (104.3 vs 51.1 for MOBILE) and Walker2d-L (65.1 vs 37.6). On D4RL MuJoCo, LEQ (923.2) is competitive with MOBILE (959.5) and CBOP (953.4) but trails EDAC (911.4) slightly; LEQ wins on walker2d-r (21.5 vs 17.9) and walker2d-mr (98.7 vs 92.7) but shows high variance on walker2d-m (74.9±26.9). On V-D4RL pixel tasks, LEQ (307.6) is on par with ROSMO (310.9) and DrQ+BC (321.6), outperforming OfflineDV2 (284.0), MOPO (203.3), and ACRO (309.0), with the best model-based score on walker_walk-medium_expert (87.2 vs 82.9 for ROSMO). Sequence-modeling baselines TT and TAP remain strong on medium AntMaze (100.0 and 85.0 on medium-diverse) but LEQ surpasses them on ultra-diverse (55.8 vs 26.0 for TAP), demonstrating the advantage of low-bias multi-step returns in the most challenging long-horizon setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

LEQ builds on the model-based offline RL framework of MOBILE (Sun et al., 2023) for its training scheme, world model ensemble, and implementation details, but replaces MOBILE's uncertainty-based Q-value penalization with lower expectile regression. The expectile regression technique itself is borrowed from IQL (Kostrikov et al., 2022), which uses upper expectiles to approximate max over actions; LEQ inverts this to use lower expectiles for conservative return estimation from model rollouts. The use of λ-returns (TD(λ) targets) for both critic and policy updates on multi-step model rollouts is a departure from prior methods that rely on 1-step Bellman targets (MOBILE, MOPO) or expensive exhaustive multi-step distributions (CBOP).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LEQ is the first model-based offline RL method to substantially outperform model-free baselines on the notoriously difficult long-horizon AntMaze tasks (60.2 vs. 4.0 on large-diverse), suggesting that conservative value estimation via expectile regression is more effective than uncertainty penalization for handling model errors in sparse-reward settings. The ablation finding that reducing the model-to-env loss ratio β from 0.95 to 0.25 is the single most critical factor for long-horizon success challenges the prior assumption that model-generated data should dominate training. Key limitations include the reliance on ground-truth termination functions (performance drops from 461.8 to 232.6 without them) and high variance in medium mazes and MuJoCo tasks due to optimistic model predictions near failure states.
