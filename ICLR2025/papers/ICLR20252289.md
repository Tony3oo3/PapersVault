## Problem

Vision-language models persistently hallucinate objects not present in images, and this problem does not resolve with scale. Existing mitigation strategies rely on external detectors, additional fine-tuning, or new model parameters, making them costly and difficult to apply to subtle hallucinations. This paper asks whether the VLM's own internal image representations can be directly interpreted and linearly edited to remove hallucinated content without any retraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and a text prompt (e.g., 'Please describe this image in detail') and feeds them through a 7B VLM (LLaVA 1.5 or InstructBLIP) whose CLIP vision encoder produces a 24×24 grid of image embeddings. It produces three outputs: (1) a revised caption with hallucinated objects removed, (2) a per-object internal confidence score c_o used for presence/hallucination classification, and (3) a 336×336 binary segmentation mask derived by thresholding spatially-resolved confidence values. All editing and detection operate on intermediate decoder-layer latent representations, requiring no access to the vision encoder or any external model.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary techniques. First, it applies the logit lens to intermediate image embeddings in the VLM's language model decoder, projecting them through the unembedding matrix to obtain per-object probability distributions over the text vocabulary at each layer; the maximum probability across all layers and patches defines an internal confidence c_o that is empirically higher for objects truly present than for hallucinated ones, and is spatially localized to the correct image patches. Second, it proposes PROJECTAWAY, a linear editing algorithm that, for a target object, extracts its text embedding t at a chosen decoder layer l^T and, for each image embedding k_i at layer l^I, computes the dot product p = k_i · t and subtracts α·(p/‖t‖²)·t from k_i only when p > 0, effectively orthogonalizing the image representation away from the object's text direction. Hallucinated objects are identified by thresholding c_o (e.g., c_o < 0.1 for LLaVA) and mass-removed in a single pass. The key empirical finding is that hallucinated objects manifest more weakly in image embeddings than correctly detected objects, making them substantially easier to erase via this linear projection while preserving caption accuracy.

## Results

On the COCO2014 Karpathy validation set, the proposed method reduces hallucinations by 25.7% (InstructBLIP) and 23.8% (LLaVA) compared to beam search, achieving CHAIR_S of 12.5 and 12.2 respectively, while preserving recall of correctly detected objects. For hallucination detection via internal confidence, the method improves mAP by 47.17% over the baseline in InstructBLIP (0.78 vs 0.53) and 22.45% in LLaVA (0.60 vs 0.49). For zero-shot segmentation on ImageNet, the logit-lens-based spatial mapping achieves 79.90 mAP, outperforming raw VLM attention (73.96 mAP) by 8.03% and matching TextSpan (80.22 mAP) on mIoU (54.26 vs 53.60).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OPERA (Huang et al., 2024), the proposed method wins on all three metrics for both models: on InstructBLIP it achieves CHAIR_S of 12.5 vs OPERA's 13.9 and reduces hallucinated objects from 472 to 419; on LLaVA it achieves CHAIR_S of 12.2 vs 12.8 and hallucinated objects of 444 vs 462. The method also substantially outperforms standard decoding strategies (greedy, nucleus, beam search) on both CHAIR scores and raw hallucination counts. For zero-shot segmentation, the method slightly trails TextSpan (Gandelsman et al., 2024b) on mAP (79.90 vs 80.22) but wins on Pixel Accuracy (76.16 vs 75.57) and mIoU (54.26 vs 53.60), while decisively beating raw VLM attention (73.96 mAP). Notably, the method requires no additional training or external models, unlike OPERA which adds an over-trust penalty during decoding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes two established LLM techniques—the logit lens for interpreting intermediate representations and linear knowledge editing—for the VLM setting, specifically targeting the *image* embeddings rather than text token representations. It departs from prior hallucination-reduction methods (OPERA, LURE, Woodpecker) that rely on external detectors, fine-tuning, or attention-weight manipulation by instead operating directly on the VLM's internal image features with no parameter updates. The conceptual bridge from LLM interpretability (nostalgebraist, 2020; Meng et al., 2023) to VLM image representations is the paper's core positioning, and the PROJECTAWAY orthogonalization algorithm draws on the knowledge-erasure literature (Ravfogel et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024b) while adapting it to the multimodal embedding space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that VLM image representations carry a measurable confidence signal distinguishing real from hallucinated objects—readable via the model's own text unembedding—provides a training-free, model-agnostic diagnostic tool that could be integrated into any VLM pipeline. The zero-shot segmentation result, achieved without any segmentation training, suggests that current VLMs encode richer spatial-semantic structure than previously exploited. Key limitations include reliance on single-token or simple multi-token object names, sensitivity to hyperparameter selection (layer, weight factor) that may not transfer across architectures, and evaluation confined primarily to COCO's 80 object classes. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to attribute-level or relational hallucinations, whether it composes with other decoding strategies, and whether the confidence gap persists in more open-ended, non-COCO visual domains.
