## Problem

MaskGIT's token unmasking scheduler, a critical component of the iterative generation process, has received little attention despite directly impacting image quality and diversity. The original Confidence scheduler greedily selects the highest-confidence tokens first, which causes spatially clustered selections and low information gain per step, ultimately degrading both diversity and detail in generated images. The paper formalizes this failure through a mutual information analysis showing that the scheduler neglects inter-token correlations within each sampling step.

## Input / Output

The model takes either a class label (ImageNet, 1000 classes) or a free-form text prompt (processed by a frozen T5-XL encoder into 120 text tokens) as conditioning, and produces a 256×256 or 512×512 RGB image. Generation operates in a discrete token space: a frozen VQGAN tokenizer (codebook size 16,384, 8× downscaling) maps the image to a 32×32 grid of tokens, which is further patchified by a factor of 2 to a 16×16 grid. The scheduler determines the unmasking order across S steps (12 or 32), after which a deterministic decoder reconstructs the full image from the complete token sequence.

## Method

The paper introduces a Halton scheduler that replaces MaskGIT's Confidence scheduler by selecting token positions according to a 2D Halton low-discrepancy sequence (bases 2 and 3), which quasi-randomly spreads tokens across the image so that consecutive points are spatially distant. A mutual information decomposition shows that MaskGIT's product-distribution sampling incurs an error equal to the KL divergence between the true joint and the factored marginals, which decomposes into per-token entropy (Eq. 5.a) and inter-token mutual information (Eq. 5.b); the Confidence scheduler minimizes only the former greedily while the Halton sequence controls both by ensuring uniform spatial coverage at every step. The scheduler is fully deterministic, requires no retraining, no Gumbel noise injection, and no architectural changes, making it a direct drop-in replacement. The Halton sequence is precomputed, discretized onto the token grid with duplicates removed, and used as a fixed unmasking order, so the overall image composition is established in early steps while later steps refine high-frequency details.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation (32 steps, 705M params), the Halton scheduler achieves FID 3.74, IS 279.5, Precision 0.81, and Recall 0.60, reducing FID by ~40% and improving IS by over 50% compared to the original MaskGIT (FID 6.18, IS 182.1). On ImageNet 512×512, Halton achieves FID 6.11 versus 8.38 for the Confidence scheduler at 32 steps. On zero-shot COCO text-to-image (256×256, 480M params, 17M training images), the Halton scheduler yields FID 28.8 and CLIP-Score 25.7, outperforming the Confidence scheduler (FID 31.5) and aMused (FID 38.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Within the MIM family, the Halton scheduler substantially outperforms the original MaskGIT (FID 3.74 vs. 6.18), Token-Critics (3.74 vs. 4.69), FSQ (3.74 vs. 4.53), and MAGE (3.74 vs. 7.04), while AutoNAT-L retains a FID advantage (2.68 vs. 3.74) though with comparable IS (278.8 vs. 279.5). Against the best GAN (StyleGAN-XL, FID 2.30), diffusion (DiT-XL/2, FID 2.27), and autoregressive (LlamaGen-3B, FID 2.18) methods, the Halton scheduler still trails by 1.5–1.6 FID points, but narrows the gap considerably compared to vanilla MaskGIT. On text-to-image COCO, the Halton scheduler beats aMused by 10.1 FID (28.8 vs. 38.9) and the Confidence scheduler by 2.7 FID (28.8 vs. 31.5), while remaining competitive with diffusion models trained on far larger datasets (e.g., PixArt-α at FID 7.3 with 25M+ images vs. 17M here). The Halton scheduler also demonstrates better scaling: performance continues to improve with more steps (up to 32), whereas the Confidence scheduler's FID degrades beyond 12 steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MaskGIT (Chang et al., 2022), replacing its Confidence scheduler with a deterministic Halton-sequence-based scheduler, and draws on the classical numerical-analysis literature on low-discrepancy sequences (Halton, 1964; Sobol, 1967). Conceptually, it departs from the greedy, confidence-driven unmasking strategy by introducing a mutual information analysis that formalizes why spatially clustered token selection is suboptimal, then proposes a quasi-random spatial distribution as the fix. It is related to M2T (Mentzer et al., 2023a), which introduced a deterministic pseudo-random scheduler for image compression, but this work targets generative quality rather than compression and provides the information-theoretic justification for the scheduling choice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Halton scheduler is a zero-cost, drop-in replacement that consistently improves FID by roughly 2 points across resolutions, model sizes, and step counts without any retraining or architectural change, making it immediately adoptable by the MaskGIT/MUSE community. The mutual information framework it introduces offers a principled lens for evaluating any future scheduler design. A key open question the paper identifies is the inability of the current scheme (and MaskGIT in general) to handle long-range dependencies, which limits coherence for complex scenes; resolving this while preserving MaskGIT's fast inference would require rethinking encoding, scheduling, and decoding as a unified system.
