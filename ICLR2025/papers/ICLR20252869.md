## Problem

Existing diffusion-based generative models for physical dynamics frequently produce samples that violate fundamental physical laws because they do not incorporate physical priors expressed as explicit equations. The core difficulty is that generative models output a distribution (score function or conditional expectation E[x₀|xₜ]) rather than a single ground-truth value, so constraints cannot be naively imposed on the model output due to Jensen's gap (R(E[x₀|xₜ]) ≠ E[R(x₀)|xₜ]). This paper addresses how to systematically embed both distributional invariances and physical feasibility constraints into the diffusion training objective.

## Input / Output

For PDE datasets, the model takes an initial condition on a spatial grid (1D for advection/Burgers, 2D for Darcy flow and shallow water) or pure noise for unconditional generation, and outputs a full spatiotemporal trajectory of the velocity/pressure field. For particle dynamics, input is random noise (three-body, 3 particles in 3D over 10 timesteps) or noise conditioned on spring connectivity (five-spring, 5 particles in 2D over 50 timesteps), and output is a sequence of particle positions and velocities structured as L×K×2D tensors. The generated samples are drawn from the learned distribution rather than being single-point predictions, and are evaluated by simulating forward dynamics from the generated initial state and comparing against ground-truth trajectories.

## Method

The framework integrates two categories of priors into score-based diffusion training. For distributional priors, the paper proves that the score function of a G-invariant distribution is (G, ∇⁻¹)-equivariant, guiding the choice of equivariant architectures (SO(n)-equivariant + translation-invariant for SE(n) data, permutation-equivariant for particle systems) paired with noise matching, or data matching for smooth PDE-constrained fields. For physical feasibility priors, a penalty loss J_R is added to the diffusion objective, with the treatment depending on constraint structure: linear constraints are applied directly to the model output; multilinear constraints leverage a reweighted-optimizer equivalence (Theorem 3); convex constraints exploit Jensen's inequality to bound the gap; reducible nonlinear constraints (e.g., energy conservation) are decomposed into compositions of elementary functions; and general nonlinear constraints are handled by reparameterizing through an auxiliary model sharing hidden states with the main diffusion network. This avoids the need for explicit physics constants and requires only determining whether the model output is elementary with respect to the constraint.

## Results

On the three-body dataset, incorporating physical priors reduces trajectory error from 2.4132 to 1.9880, velocity error from 2.5745 to 0.8328, and energy error from 4.3292 to 0.5465 (an ~87% reduction in energy error). On the five-spring dataset, momentum error drops from 5.3699 to 0.3898 (~93% reduction) and energy error from 1.0618 to 0.7418. For PDE prediction (Table 1), RMSE improves from 1.7263 to 1.6536 (×10⁻²) on advection and from 2.0648 to 1.9678 (×10⁻²) on Darcy flow. PDE constraint feasibility (Table 2) also improves across all datasets, e.g., shallow water RMSE drops from 8.0153 to 7.7618.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the same diffusion model trained without physical feasibility priors (w/o prior). The proposed method consistently outperforms this baseline across all six datasets and all metrics, with the most dramatic gains on conservation-law violations: energy error on three-body drops by ~87% and momentum error on five-spring by ~93%. The only external baseline comparison is against NRI (Neural Relational Inference, Kipf et al. 2018) in Appendix Table 10 on the five-spring dataset. With priors, the diffusion model achieves dynamic error of 2.4329×10⁻³ (vs. NRI's 2.4471×10⁻³), momentum error of 0.3687 (vs. NRI's 5.2234), and energy error of 0.7448 (vs. NRI's 1.4577), outperforming NRI on all three metrics. The paper also ablates against a 'prior by PINN' approach that directly applies nonlinear energy constraints to the model output (ignoring Jensen's gap), showing this naive approach actually degrades performance (energy error 4.2219 vs. 0.5062 for the proposed method), validating the need for the constraint decomposition strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends score-based diffusion models (Song et al., 2020) by systematically incorporating physical priors into the generative training objective, adapting the penalty-loss paradigm from PINNs (Raissi et al., 2019) to the generative setting. It departs from prior diffusion-based physics generation work (Bastek et al., 2024; Shu et al., 2023; Lienen et al., 2023) that either ignores physical constraints entirely or handles them through expensive iterative sampling during training. The key conceptual departure is a formal analysis of the Jensen's gap that prevents direct constraint application to the conditional expectation E[x₀|xₜ], yielding a taxonomy of constraint types (linear, multilinear, convex, reducible nonlinear, general nonlinear) with corresponding handling strategies. The paper also connects equivariant network theory to the choice between noise-matching and data-matching objectives, grounding distributional priors (SE(n)-invariance, permutation-invariance) in the score function's equivariance properties.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Jensen's gap analysis provides a principled explanation for why naive PINN-style constraint injection degrades generative model performance, and the resulting constraint taxonomy offers practitioners a clear decision framework for incorporating physics laws into diffusion models without the computational overhead of iterative sampling. Limitations include reliance on data augmentation rather than true equivariant architectures, evaluation on relatively simple PDEs and small particle systems (≤5 particles), and the finding that the reducible nonlinear approach fails when the backbone cannot accurately predict inter-particle distances (five-spring case). Open questions include whether the framework scales to high-dimensional stochastic PDEs, whether true equivariant diffusion architectures would outperform the augmentation-based approach, and how the method performs on long-horizon generation where constraint violations may accumulate.
