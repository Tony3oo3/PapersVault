## Problem

Personalized federated learning (PFL) achieves high accuracy by assuming clients only test on their own local data, but in real-world GFL scenarios clients encounter test data from other distributions where PFL models collapse in performance. The paper asks whether personalized models (PMs) trained under PFL can be selectively deployed to enhance GFL generalization, and formalizes this as a new Selective FL (SFL) problem that bridges the two paradigms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253690/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, a test image (e.g., 32×32 CIFAR-10 or 64×64 Tiny-ImageNet) is passed through a frozen shared backbone to produce intermediate features, which are then noised (Gaussian noise scaled by coefficient κ) to form a task marker. This task marker is compared via MMD distance against stored plug-in markers (noised features from each client's training data) to select the appropriate plug-in head, which then produces the final class label. The unusual aspect is that selection operates on noised intermediate representations rather than raw data or full model parameters, enabling privacy-preserving retrieval from a server-side modular store.

## Method

HPFL decomposes the model into a shared backbone g (trained via any GFL algorithm, e.g., FedAvg) and lightweight personalized plug-in heads ρ_m (fine-tuned locally on each client's data with g frozen). Each client uploads its plug-in module along with a plug-in marker (noised feature statistics) to a server modular store. At inference, the backbone extracts features from test data, and an MMD-based selection algorithm retrieves the plug-in whose marker is closest to the noised test features, completing inference as ρ_i ∘ g(ξ). The paper proves (Theorem 2.3) that accurate selection recovers PFL-level performance in the GFL setting, and provides (ε,δ)-differential privacy guarantees on the noised markers with theoretical bounds on the noise coefficient κ. The framework is architecture-agnostic (validated on ResNet-18, MobileNet, Simple-CNN) and naturally extends to federated continual learning by preserving prior knowledge in stored plug-ins.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (10 clients, Dir(0.1)), HPFL achieves 95.4% (1 epoch) and 95.7% (10 epochs) PM-on-GFL accuracy, dramatically outperforming the best baseline FedTHE (64.4%) by over 30 percentage points. This pattern holds across all four datasets: HPFL reaches 98.3% on Fashion-MNIST, 74.8% on CIFAR-100, and 51.9% on Tiny-ImageNet-200 in the GFL-PM setting, while the strongest baselines (FedTHE, FedRep) achieve only 69.4%, 24.8%, and 19.0% respectively. In the standard PFL setting, HPFL matches or slightly trails the best PFL baselines (e.g., 95.4% vs. FedPer's 95.8% on CIFAR-10), confirming that the plug-in selection mechanism does not sacrifice local personalization. HPFL's GFL-GM accuracy is identical to FedAvg (81.5% on CIFAR-10) since it reuses the same backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HPFL decisively outperforms all seven baselines in the GFL-PM setting (personalized models evaluated on global test data), which is the paper's primary evaluation. On CIFAR-10 (10 clients, Dir(0.1)), HPFL (95.4–95.7%) surpasses FedTHE (64.4%), FedRep (51.3%), FedSAM (47.8%), FedRoD (41.6%), FedPer (40.9%), and PerFedMask (23.4%) by 31–72 percentage points. The gap is even more extreme on CIFAR-100, where HPFL (74.8%) exceeds FedTHE (24.8%) by 50 points, and on Tiny-ImageNet where HPFL (51.9%) beats FedTHE (19.0%) by 33 points. In the standard PFL setting, HPFL is competitive but not dominant: it matches FedPer (95.4% vs. 95.8%) and FedSAM (95.4% vs. 96.0%) on CIFAR-10, confirming that plug-in selection does not degrade local personalization. For GFL-GM accuracy, HPFL is identical to FedAvg by construction (same backbone), while FedRoD (85.3%) and FedTHE (86.2%) achieve slightly higher GM scores due to their joint optimization strategies. The advantage of HPFL is most pronounced under high heterogeneity (Dir(0.05)) and with 100 clients, where baselines collapse (e.g., FedPer drops to 20.6% PM-on-GFL) while HPFL retains 88.6%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the shared-backbone-plus-personalized-head decomposition introduced by FedRep (Collins et al., 2021) and the two-predictor GFL/PFL bridging framework of FedRoD (Chen & Chao, 2021), but departs from both by introducing a dynamic selection mechanism at inference time. While prior PFL methods (FedPer, FedRep, FedRoD) assume clients only encounter their own test distribution, and prior GFL methods (FedAvg, FedSAM) train a single monolithic model, HPFL formulates Selective FL (SFL) as a new problem that selects among personalized plug-in modules based on input features. The selection mechanism draws conceptual inspiration from model-component editing in LLMs (Shah et al., 2024; Lai et al., 2025), but is the first to apply such a plug-in retrieval paradigm to federated learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HPFL provides a practical, modular architecture for FL systems where clients encounter heterogeneous test data, with demonstrated applications to federated continual learning, one-shot FL, and anarchic FL. The plug-in store design naturally supports dynamic client participation and avoids the catastrophic performance collapse that PFL methods suffer under GFL-PM evaluation. Key open questions include scalability of plug-in selection to thousands of clients (the authors suggest clustering/merging but leave this unresolved), the sub-optimality of current distance-based selection methods, and whether the framework extends to foundation models with PEFT techniques like LoRA. The finding that differential privacy noise (κ up to 1000) has zero measurable impact on selection accuracy is a practically encouraging result for deployment.
