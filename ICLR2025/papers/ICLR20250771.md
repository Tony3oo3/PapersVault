## Problem

The vast majority of MS/MS spectra in metabolomics remain unannotated because reference databases are too small to cover the chemical diversity in biological samples, leaving molecules in 'dark chemical space.' Existing de novo generation methods attempt to predict full molecular structures directly from spectra, which is an extremely high-complexity search problem. MADGEN addresses this by decomposing the task into scaffold retrieval followed by scaffold-conditioned generation, arguing that predicting the core backbone is substantially easier than predicting the complete structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250771/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an MS/MS spectrum (a variable-length set of m/z–intensity peak pairs), the molecular chemical formula, and a candidate scaffold pool as input. The atom set is fully determined by the chemical formula, so the generation task reduces to predicting the edge set that connects scaffold atoms to the remaining free atoms. The output is a complete molecular graph (scaffold edges fixed, new edges added), with the spectrum tokenized as individual peak embeddings rather than binned, preserving per-peak resolution for cross-attention during generation.

## Method

MADGEN is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 (scaffold retrieval) uses a CLIP-style contrastive learning objective to align an MLP-encoded spectrum embedding with a GNN-encoded scaffold graph embedding in a shared latent space, ranking candidate scaffolds by cosine similarity with a frequency-based aggregation over top-k results. Stage 2 (scaffold-conditioned generation) adapts the RetroBridge Markov bridge process to progressively add edges from free atoms to the scaffold over T diffusion steps, where each edge is a D-dimensional one-hot vector over bond types and the forward process uses absorbing transition matrices. A Graph Transformer with FC-GNN layers predicts the endpoint edge configuration at each step, and classifier-free guidance (CFG) integrates the mass spectrum via cross-attention at every generation step, with the spectrum encoded as self-attended peak tokens that nodes and edges query. Training optimizes the ELBO of the Markov bridge, and during inference the CFG scale λ_t amplifies spectrum-conditioned logits to sharpen generation toward the target structure.

## Results

With the oracle scaffold retriever, MADGEN achieves top-1 accuracy of 49.0% (NIST23), 18.7% (CANOPUS), and 10.5% (MassSpecGym), with corresponding top-10 accuracies of 65.5%, 22.2%, and 12.4%. The predictive scaffold retriever yields substantially lower top-1 accuracies of 4.6%, 2.10%, and 1.31% respectively, reflecting the difficulty of scaffold prediction (SPA ranges from 13.2% to 40.3%). All baseline methods (Spec2Mol, MSNovelist, random generation, SMILES/SELFIES Transformers) achieve 0.0% top-1 accuracy across all three datasets, making MADGEN the first method to produce any exact matches on the MassSpecGym benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MADGEN decisively outperforms all baselines on every dataset and metric. Spec2Mol and MSNovelist both achieve 0.0% top-1 and top-10 accuracy across NIST23, CANOPUS, and MassSpecGym, while MADGEN's predictive retriever alone reaches 1.31–4.6% top-1 accuracy. On MassSpecGym specifically, MADGEN (Pred.) surpasses the best previously reported benchmark results (Rand. Gen., SMILES Transformer, SELFIES Transformer — all at 0.0% top-1) by a wide margin, and the oracle retriever further improves to 10.5% top-1. The gap between predictive and oracle retrieval is the paper's central finding: correct scaffold knowledge boosts top-1 accuracy by roughly 10× on NIST23 (4.6% → 49.0%) and ~9× on CANOPUS (2.10% → 18.7%), indicating that scaffold prediction accuracy is the primary bottleneck. In terms of structural similarity, MADGEN (Oracle) achieves Tanimoto scores of 0.43–0.63 and MCES distances of 3.88–18.48, far exceeding the 0.03–0.14 Tanimoto and 3.88–79.39 MCES of baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

MADGEN adapts the Markov bridge framework from RetroBridge (Igashov et al., 2023) — originally designed for retrosynthesis — to scaffold-conditioned molecular generation, and builds on the discrete absorbing transition matrix formulation of Austin et al. (2021) for the diffusion-like edge generation process. It departs from prior de novo mass-spectra-to-molecule methods (MSNovelist, Spec2Mol, MassGenie, MS2Mol) that generate the full structure in one pass, instead decomposing the problem into a contrastive-learning scaffold retrieval stage (inspired by JESTR and CLIP-style multimodal alignment) followed by a scaffold-conditioned completion stage. The use of Murcko scaffolds as a structural anchor, combined with classifier-free guidance from the mass spectrum via cross-attention, is the key architectural departure from both the retrosynthesis and direct-generation literatures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'dark chemical space' problem in metabolomics — where the vast majority of MS/MS spectra lack structural annotations — remains a major bottleneck for drug discovery, biomarker identification, and environmental analysis. MADGEN's scaffold-based decomposition offers a principled way to reduce the combinatorial search space, and the oracle results (up to 49% top-1 on NIST23) demonstrate that the generation stage is effective when the scaffold is known. However, the predictive scaffold retrieval accuracy (13.2%–40.3%) is the clear bottleneck, limiting end-to-end practical utility; the authors themselves suggest that end-to-end training, larger/more distinct scaffold definitions, and incorporating peak-level chemical formulae or molecular properties could close this gap. An open question is whether the two-stage error compounding can be mitigated without sacrificing the interpretability benefit of scaffold anchoring.
