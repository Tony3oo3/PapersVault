## Problem

Atom-level protein-ligand binding affinity models suffer from excessive computational complexity (many atom pairs contribute negligibly), noise from irrelevant atoms, and an inability to capture hierarchical, group-level interactions such as aromatic rings binding collectively to protein pockets. Existing cluster-level approaches rely on predefined geometric constraints or fixed residue groupings, failing to dynamically identify which atom groups are actually relevant to a given binding interaction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253208/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a protein-ligand complex as a molecular graph where each atom node carries feature vectors (one-hot atom type, degree, hybridization, valence electrons for GIGN; co-crystallized metal indicators for Atom3D) and 3D coordinates, with intra-molecular covalent bonds and inter-molecular non-covalent edges defined at a 5 Å cutoff. The number of protein and ligand clusters is a fixed hyperparameter (e.g., 156 protein / 28 ligand clusters for cross-dataset evaluation), chosen as the median node count in the training set. For the LBA task the output is a scalar binding affinity (−log Kd or −log Ki); for LEP it is a binary active/inactive label produced by an MLP head trained with BCE loss.

## Method

CheapNet first encodes atom-level embeddings using a geometric interaction GNN (GIGN by default, but swappable with GCN or EGNN). A differentiable pooling layer then learns soft cluster assignment matrices via a GNN followed by row-wise softmax, aggregating atom embeddings into a fixed number of cluster-level representations and constructing cluster adjacency matrices from the original graph structure. A second GNN refines these cluster embeddings using the cluster adjacency. The core novelty is a bidirectional cross-attention mechanism (ligand-to-protein and protein-to-ligand) applied at the cluster level, which dynamically weights inter-molecular cluster interactions and filters out irrelevant clusters. The pooled cross-attention outputs are combined with residual cluster embeddings through an MLP to produce the final prediction. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with MSE loss (or BCE for LEP), requiring no auxiliary clustering losses, and is permutation-invariant with respect to cluster ordering.

## Results

On the cross-dataset evaluation (PDBbind), CheapNet achieves RMSE of 1.262 / 1.107 / 1.343 and Pearson of 0.857 / 0.870 / 0.665 on the v2013 core, v2016 core, and v2019 holdout sets respectively, outperforming the best atom-level baseline GIGN (RMSE 1.380 / 1.190 / 1.393) with only 1.33M parameters. On the more challenging diverse protein evaluation (LBA 30%), CheapNet achieves RMSE 1.311 and Pearson 0.642, beating the best cluster-level baseline GET (RMSE 1.327, Pearson 0.620) and all pre-training models. On the Ligand Efficacy Prediction task, CheapNet reaches AUROC 0.935 and AUPRC 0.924, surpassing the previous best BindNet (AUROC 0.882, AUPRC 0.870) by a substantial margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CheapNet outperforms all interaction-free baselines (DeepDTA, GraphDTA, MGraphDTA) by a wide margin on every test set, with the largest gap on the v2019 holdout set (RMSE 1.343 vs. 1.485–1.705). Among interaction-based atom-level models, CheapNet beats GIGN (the strongest baseline) by 0.118 RMSE on v2013 core and 0.083 RMSE on v2016 core, while using roughly 2× more parameters (1.33M vs. 0.62M). Against attention-based interaction models (CAPLA, GAABind, DEAttentionDTA, AttentionSiteDTI), CheapNet achieves the best RMSE on both v2013 and v2016 core sets and is the only method evaluated on the v2019 holdout set among this group. On the diverse protein evaluation (LBA 30%), CheapNet edges out GET (RMSE 1.311 vs. 1.327) and all pre-training models (BindNet 1.340, ProFSA 1.377); on LBA 60% it slightly trails BindNet on RMSE (1.238 vs. 1.230) but matches on Pearson (0.794 vs. 0.793). On the LEP classification task, CheapNet's AUROC of 0.935 exceeds BindNet (0.882) by 5.3 points and Uni-Mol (0.823) by 11.2 points, with AUPRC of 0.924 vs. 0.870 for BindNet.

## Relation to Prior Work

CheapNet builds on the differentiable pooling framework of Ying et al. (2018) for hierarchical graph representation, repurposing it to dynamically group atoms into soft clusters within protein-ligand complexes. It departs from atom-level interaction-based models (GIGN, IGN, EGNN) that model all pairwise atom interactions, and from existing cluster-level models (LEFTNet, GET, GemNet, Equiformer) that rely on predefined geometric or residue-based clusters. The key architectural departure is the bidirectional cross-attention between learned protein and ligand clusters, which filters irrelevant clusters and focuses on biologically meaningful inter-molecular interactions without requiring 3D geometric constraints at the cluster level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CheapNet demonstrates that a 1.33M-parameter model without pre-training can match or exceed 47M+ parameter pre-trained models (BindNet, ProFSA) on binding affinity prediction, offering a practical and interpretable alternative for resource-constrained drug discovery pipelines. The modularity—showing consistent gains when paired with GCN, EGNN, or GIGN encoders—makes the cluster-attention module a drop-in enhancement for existing pipelines. Open questions include whether integrating SE(3)-equivariant encoders at the cluster level, handling noisy AlphaFold3-predicted structures, and extending 3D geometric information into the cross-attention mechanism itself could further close the gap with larger pre-trained systems.
