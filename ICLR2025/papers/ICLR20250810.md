## Problem

Joint-embedding predictive architectures (JEPAs) have excelled in self-supervised representation learning but remain unexplored for generative modeling, while diffusion models dominate generation without leveraging representation learning. This paper asks whether JEPA's masked-prediction objective can be reinterpreted as a generalized next-token prediction strategy to drive autoregressive data generation in continuous space, and whether the two objectives (representation and generation) can coexist without architectural conflict.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250810/cropped_page9_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

For ImageNet conditional generation, the input is a class label plus a VAE-encoded latent tensor of shape 16×32×32, which is patchified (patch size 1) into 256 continuous-valued tokens. Generation proceeds via iterative next-set-of-token prediction over a cosine schedule (typically 64 autoregressive steps), where each step denoises a random subset of tokens using a per-token diffusion or flow-matching MLP conditioned on the JEPA-predicted embedding. The final token sequence is decoded by the VAE to a 256×256 (or 512×512) RGB image; the same pipeline extends to video frames and audio waveforms with text-prompt conditioning.

## Method

D-JEPA uses three identical ViT backbones—a context encoder φ, an EMA-updated target encoder φ̄, and a feature predictor γ—trained jointly with two losses: a per-token diffusion (or flow-matching) loss that models p(x_i | z_i) via a compact 6–12 residual-block MLP, and a smooth-L1 prediction loss that regresses the predicted embedding z_i toward the target embedding g_i. The key architectural insight is that the diffusion loss operates in a separate energy landscape from the JEPA prediction loss, which eliminates representation collapse and allows both encoders to be identical with a fixed EMA coefficient (α=0.9999) rather than requiring asymmetric designs or warmup schedules. Masked image modeling is reframed as generalized next-token prediction: at each autoregressive step, the context encoder processes already-sampled tokens, the feature predictor estimates embeddings for the remaining masked tokens, and the denoising MLP samples concrete token values from the learned conditional distribution. This yields a model that scales favorably—larger variants require fewer training epochs and fewer autoregressive steps to reach peak FID—while remaining extensible to video, audio, and multi-modal generation.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation without classifier-free guidance, D-JEPA achieves state-of-the-art FID and IS across all model scales: D-JEPA-B (212M) reaches FID 3.40 / IS 197.1, D-JEPA-L (687M) reaches FID 2.32 / IS 233.5, and D-JEPA-H (1.4B) reaches FID 2.04 / IS 239.3. A key efficiency finding is that larger models require fewer training epochs: D-JEPA-H achieves its best FID in only 320 epochs versus 800 for MAR-H, and D-JEPA-L in 480 epochs. With classifier-free guidance, D-JEPA-L and D-JEPA-H further improve to FID 1.58 and 1.54 respectively, approaching the VAE upper bound of FID 1.199.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D-JEPA-L (687M) outperforms the previous state-of-the-art MAR-H (943M) on both FID (2.32 vs. 2.35) and IS (233.5 vs. 227.8) while using 40% fewer training epochs (480 vs. 800) and 27% fewer parameters. D-JEPA-H (1.4B) achieves FID 2.04, substantially beating VDM++ (2.0B, FID 2.40) and MAR-H (943M, FID 2.35). At the same scale, D-JEPA-L dramatically outperforms DiT-XL (675M, FID 9.62), SiT-XL (675M, FID 8.60), and MDTv2-XL (676M, FID 5.06), indicating that the JEPA-based representation learning provides a significant advantage over pure diffusion or masked-diffusion approaches. D-JEPA-B (212M) edges out MAR-B (208M) with FID 3.40 vs. 3.48 and higher recall (0.61 vs. 0.58). With classifier-free guidance, D-JEPA-H (FID 1.54, IS 324.2) matches MAR-H's FID (1.55) but achieves substantially higher IS (324.2 vs. 303.7), and D-JEPA-L (FID 1.58, IS 303.1) ties MDTv2-XL on FID while surpassing it on IS (303.1 vs. 314.7 at cfg=3.0, or 327.2 at cfg=3.9). The paper also demonstrates that D-JEPA's advantage grows with scale: the gap over MAR widens from 0.08 FID at base to 0.31 FID at huge, suggesting the JEPA representation learning component becomes increasingly beneficial as model capacity increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

D-JEPA repurposes the joint-embedding predictive architecture (JEPA) from Assran et al. (2023), originally designed for self-supervised representation learning, as the backbone of a generative model. It draws on MAR (Li et al., 2024) for the per-token diffusion/flow-matching denoising MLP and the generalized next-set-of-token sampling strategy, and on MaskGIT (Chang et al., 2022) for the iterative unmasking schedule. The key conceptual departure is from both the representation-learning side (where JEPA was considered incapable of generation) and the generative-modeling side (where MAR and Transfusion integrate diffusion into autoregressive frameworks without leveraging representation-learning objectives). By adding a per-token diffusion loss alongside the JEPA prediction loss, D-JEPA bridges these two threads: the prediction loss supplies high-level semantic features z_i that condition the diffusion, while the diffusion loss prevents the representation collapse that plagues standard JEPA, enabling identical context and target encoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

D-JEPA demonstrates that representation learning and generative modeling are not competing objectives but can be jointly optimized within a single architecture, with each loss reinforcing the other. The finding that optimal autoregressive steps decrease as model scale grows (64 steps suffice for the 1.4B-parameter model) has direct practical implications for scaling to ultra-high-resolution and video generation. The framework extends naturally to video, audio, and text-conditioned generation, positioning it as a candidate architecture for unified multimodal continuous-data generation. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond ~1.4B parameters, how it compares to pure diffusion or pure autoregressive baselines at much larger scales, and whether the per-token diffusion formulation remains efficient for very long sequences (e.g., high-resolution video).
