## Problem

Existing VOS methods rely on pixel-level spatial correlation that neglects instance-level semantic information, producing incomplete masks for objects with complex or occluded parts. Additionally, query-based VOS methods update target queries using the entire predicted sample, accumulating noise that causes identity confusion and tracking failures in long-term videos with dramatic appearance changes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250263/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video frame sequence where the initial frame carries binary mask annotations for one or more target objects; frames are resized to 480×480 (or 600/720 for higher-resolution evaluation) and padded to multiples of 32 for ViT patch embedding. During inference, a dual-level memory (pixel-level features and object-level queries) is updated every 5th frame using online predicted masks, and a long-term fusion strategy handles sequences longer than the memory window. The output is a per-frame, per-object segmentation mask predicted by a decoder operating on multi-scale correlated features.

## Method

S3 introduces a spatial-semantic block that fuses global semantic priors from a frozen pre-trained ViT (via CLS token and global-pooled features used as Key/Value in cross-attention) with multi-scale CNN features (as Query), followed by multi-scale deformable cross-attention to model spatial dependencies between object parts. A discriminative query propagation module then selects the single most representative feature channel within the target mask (via argmax of query–feature similarity) to update the object query, rather than aggregating over the entire predicted region, thereby reducing noise accumulation during long-term propagation. The ViT backbone remains frozen throughout training, and the network is trained end-to-end on video datasets (YouTubeVOS, DAVIS, and optionally the MEGA set) with 8-frame sampling, point-supervision loss, and a 125K–195K iteration schedule using AdamW at lr 5e-5.

## Results

Trained on the MEGA dataset, S3 achieves state-of-the-art J&F scores of 74.0% on MOSE-val, 73.0% on LVOS test, 87.8% on DAVIS 2017 test, 88.0% (G) on YouTube-VOS 2018 val, and 88.1% (G) on YouTube-VOS 2019 val. Even without MEGA (trained only on YouTubeVOS + DAVIS), S3 reaches 68.5% on MOSE-val and 66.5% on LVOS test, substantially outperforming the previous best (Cutie at 64.0% and 56.2% respectively). Against SAM 2 (Hiera-L), S3 (ViT-B) gains +7.9 J&F on LVOS V2 val (83.7 vs 78.1) while using far less training data (~11.4K vs ~120.6K videos).

## Baselines & Comparisons

S3 outperforms all matching-based VOS baselines across every benchmark. On MOSE-val (MEGA training), S3 achieves 74.0 J&F, beating Cutie (69.9) by +4.1 and DEVA (66.5) by +7.5; the gap widens dramatically on LVOS test (73.0 vs 66.7 for Cutie, +6.3), confirming the benefit of discriminative query propagation in long-term videos. On DAVIS 2017 test, S3 (87.8) surpasses Cutie (86.1) and DEVA (83.2), though JointFormer with static-image pre-training (87.6) is competitive in the YT+DAVIS-only setting. Against SAM 2 (Hiera-L), S3 (ViT-B) wins on LVOS V2 val by +5.6 J&F (83.7 vs 78.1) and DAVIS 2017 by +0.8 (89.8 vs 89.0), but trails slightly on MOSE-val (75.9 vs 77.2) and YouTube-VOS 2019 (88.4 vs 89.1), likely due to SAM 2's much larger and more diverse training corpus. The ablation in Table 1 shows the spatial-semantic block contributes the largest single gain (+4.3 J&F on MOSE over Cutie+ViT), while the discriminative query adds a further +1.0–1.2 J&F, with the combined effect most pronounced on long-term benchmarks (LVOS: +10.3 over Cutie baseline).

## Relation to Prior Work

S3 extends the query-based VOS framework introduced by Cutie (Cheng et al., 2023a) and the memory-based architecture of XMem (Cheng & Schwing, 2022), departing from both in key ways. Rather than updating object queries from the entire predicted sample (as in Cutie), S3 proposes discriminative query propagation that selects only the most representative target features, reducing noise accumulation over long sequences. For feature extraction, S3 introduces a spatial-semantic block that integrates CLS-token semantic information from a frozen pre-trained ViT with multi-scale CNN features via cross-attention and deformable spatial modeling, distinguishing it from adapter-based approaches (ViT-Adapter, ViTCoMer) that require full fine-tuning and lack explicit semantic-spatial fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method achieves state-of-the-art results across five VOS benchmarks and notably outperforms SAM 2 (Hiera-B+) by +7.9% on LVOSV2 val while training on roughly 10× fewer videos (~11.4K vs. ~120K), suggesting that task-specific architectural design can compensate for data scale in VOS. A key open limitation is that the semantic understanding pushes the model toward whole-object segmentation, failing on part-level targets (e.g., a person's head), which the authors acknowledge requires new part-aware benchmarks. Inference speed (13.1 FPS) remains slower than Cutie (36.4 FPS), and whether the spatial-semantic integration generalizes beyond VOS to other dense video tasks (e.g., video instance segmentation) remains unexplored.
