## Problem

Previous text-to-video diffusion models produced short (2–3 s), low-motion clips and struggled to generate coherent narratives with large-scale dynamic events (e.g., a person leaping from a shattered rock). The paper tackles how to achieve long-duration (10 s), high-resolution video generation with temporally consistent, rich motion semantics from a single text prompt.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252994/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a natural-language text prompt (upsampled at inference by a fine-tuned LLM to increase detail) and, for the image-to-video variant, a reference RGB image that is encoded through the same 3D VAE and concatenated in the channel dimension. It outputs a continuous 10-second video at 16 fps with a maximum resolution of 768×1360 pixels, supporting multiple aspect ratios. During training, images are treated as single-frame videos and mixed with multi-second clips in the same batch via the Frame Pack strategy.

## Method

CogVideoX combines three key architectural components: (1) a 3D causal VAE that compresses video 8×8×4 (spatial×temporal) into a 16-channel latent using temporally causal convolutions, reducing sequence length while preventing inter-frame flicker; (2) an Expert Transformer that concatenates T5 text embeddings and patchified video latents into a single sequence, applying modality-specific Expert Adaptive LayerNorm (separate AdaLN for vision and text) to reconcile their differing feature spaces without duplicating transformer parameters; and (3) 3D full attention over the joint text-video sequence (rather than separated 2D spatial + 1D temporal attention), which enables direct cross-frame information flow for large motions. Training uses progressive resolution scaling (256→512→768 px), Multi-Resolution Frame Pack to batch variable-length/aspect-ratio clips together, 3D-RoPE positional encoding, v-prediction with zero-SNR noise schedule, and Explicit Uniform Sampling to stabilize the diffusion loss across data-parallel ranks.

## Results

On VBench, CogVideoX-5B achieves the best scores in 5 out of 7 metrics: Human Action (96.8), Dynamic Degree (62.22), Multiple Objects (70.95), Dynamic Quality (69.5), and GPT4o-MT Score (3.36). CogVideoX-2B is also highly competitive, leading on Human Action (96.6), Scene (55.35), and Dynamic Degree (66.39). In human evaluation against the closed-source Kling model, CogVideoX-5B wins on all four aspects, achieving a total score of 2.74 versus Kling's 2.17. The 3D VAE also outperforms Open-Sora and Open-Sora-Plan on reconstruction quality (PSNR 29.1 vs. 28.5/27.6) and flickering (85.5 vs. 92.4/90.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CogVideoX-5B decisively outperforms all open-source baselines on dynamic and multi-object metrics: Dynamic Degree (62.22 vs. 49.17 for T2V-Turbo, 42.50 for VideoCrafter-2.0, 31.11 for LaVie-2) and Multiple Objects (70.95 vs. 54.65 for T2V-Turbo, 40.66 for VideoCrafter-2.0, 64.88 for LaVie-2). On GPT4o-MT Score, CogVideoX-5B (3.36) leads all competitors by a wide margin over the next-best CogVideoX-2B (3.09) and VideoCrafter-2.0 (2.68). The only metrics where CogVideoX-5B does not lead are Scene (T2V-Turbo edges ahead by 0.14: 55.58 vs. 55.44) and Appearance Style (VideoCrafter-2.0 at 25.13 and LaVie-2 at 25.09 slightly exceed 24.44). Against the closed-source Kling model in human evaluation, CogVideoX-5B wins on every dimension—Sensory Quality (0.722 vs. 0.638), Instruction Following (0.495 vs. 0.367), Physics Simulation (0.667 vs. 0.561), and Cover Quality (0.712 vs. 0.668)—for a total of 2.74 vs. 2.17. The 2B variant is particularly competitive, matching or exceeding 5B on Scene and Dynamic Degree while remaining within 1–2 points on most other metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

CogVideoX extends the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) paradigm from image generation (Peebles & Xie, 2023) to video, building on the latent diffusion framework of LDM (Rombach et al., 2022) and the noise schedule of v-prediction. It departs from prior video diffusion models (SVD, Make-A-Video, CogVideo) in three key architectural choices: replacing 2D VAE fine-tuning with a purpose-built 3D causal VAE, substituting MMDiT's dual-transformer design with a single transformer using Expert Adaptive LayerNorm for modality-specific normalization, and adopting 3D full attention over the separated 2D+1D attention common in earlier work. The Multi-Resolution Frame Pack training strategy draws on Patch'n Pack (Dehghani et al., 2024) for handling variable-length inputs, while the video captioning pipeline repurposes GPT-4 as a teacher to distill dense captions into a lightweight LLaMA2 model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first open-source, commercial-grade text-to-video model (5B and 2B parameters), CogVideoX provides the research community with a practical, reproducible recipe for long-duration, high-resolution video generation that was previously locked behind closed APIs. The finding that a single transformer with Expert AdaLN matches or exceeds MMDiT's dual-transformer architecture at equal parameter count challenges the prevailing assumption that modality separation requires architectural duplication, potentially simplifying future multi-modal diffusion designs. Open questions remain around the VAE compression ceiling (16×16×8 proved too aggressive), scaling to videos longer than 10 seconds, and whether the 3D full attention advantage over 2D+1D persists at substantially larger model sizes where memory constraints force approximations.
