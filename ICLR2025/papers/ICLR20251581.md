## Problem

Classic uniform generalization bounds based on VC-dimension or Rademacher complexity cannot explain why over-parameterized neural networks generalize well, since these complexity measures scale with the number of parameters. Algorithm-dependent bounds (stability, NTK) require strong assumptions like convexity or the NTK condition that are unrealistic for deep networks. This paper asks whether generalization can be established for networks that minimize (or approximately minimize) empirical risk under only mild assumptions on the data distribution.

## Input / Output

The theory operates on binary classification distributions D over [0,1]^n × {-1,1} with a positive separation bound (distinct-class points are separated by a positive distance). The key input parameter is the 'expressive width' W_0 — the minimum width of a two-layer network needed to classify D with a given confidence margin c — rather than the VC-dimension of the full hypothesis space. The output is a lower bound on population accuracy A_D(F) for any network F of width W that minimizes cross-entropy empirical risk on N i.i.d. samples, with the bound decaying in both 1/W and 1/√N independently.

## Method

The core result (Theorem 4.4) shows that if distribution D can be expressed by a width-W_0 network with confidence c, then any width-W ≥ W_0+1 network minimizing empirical risk on N samples achieves population accuracy ≥ 1 − O(W_0/(cW) + nL_p(W_0+c)√log(4n)/(c√N) + √(ln(2/δ)/N)). The proof proceeds in four steps: (1) construct a width-W network by replicating the expressing network's neurons to bound the minimum empirical risk; (2) use this bound to show most training points are correctly classified with large margin; (3) apply a Rademacher complexity bound on the clamped network class to control the training-to-population gap; (4) convert the margin bound into an accuracy bound. The critical insight is that both N and W appear in the denominator independently, so increasing either alone improves generalization — directly explaining over-parameterization. The result extends to q-approximate minimizers (Theorem 4.8), and complementary lower bounds (Section 5) show that for some distributions the required N must exceed the network size W_0 needed to express D.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 4.4) establishes that for a two-layer network of width W minimizing empirical risk on N samples from a distribution expressible by width W₀ with confidence c, the population accuracy satisfies A_D(F) ≥ 1 − O(W₀/(cW) + nL_p(W₀+c)√log(4n)/(c√N) + √(ln(2/δ)/N)). Crucially, both N and W appear independently in the denominator, meaning generalization is guaranteed when N ≥ Ω(W₀²) and W ≥ Ω(W₀) (Corollary 4.5), explaining why over-parameterized models generalize well. A matching lower bound (Theorem 5.2, Corollary 5.3) shows that for ReLU networks, at least Ω(nW₀) training samples are necessary in the worst case. The bound extends to q-approximate minimizers (Theorem 4.8) with only a q-factor degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its expressive-ability-based bound against three families of existing generalization bounds. Against algorithm-independent VC-dimension bounds (Mohri et al. 2018, Theorem 4.9), the new bound avoids the requirement that N exceed the VC-dimension (which scales as O(nW) for ReLU networks), thereby explaining over-parameterized generalization that VC bounds cannot. Against the NTK-based bound of Ji & Telgarsky (2020, Theorem 4.10), the new result requires only that the distribution be expressible by a network of width W₀, rather than the strong NTK condition that reduces training to convex optimization. Against the stability bound of Hardt et al. (2016, Theorem 4.11), the new bound does not assume convexity or smoothness of the loss, and the network width W appears in the denominator rather than being absorbed into the Lipschitz constant. Compared to PAC-Bayes-KL bounds (Tolstikhin & Seldin 2013), the new result provides guarantees for individual ERM-minimizing networks rather than in expectation over a distribution of networks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in generalization theory by shifting the focus from hypothesis-space complexity (VC-dimension, Rademacher complexity) or algorithmic assumptions (NTK, convexity, smoothness) to the *expressive cost* of the data distribution itself. It builds conceptually on the universal approximation theorem (Cybenko, 1989) and the Rademacher-complexity machinery of Mohri et al. (2018), but departs from both algorithm-independent bounds (which fail for over-parameterized models, as shown by Nagarajan & Kolter, 2019) and algorithm-dependent bounds (which require unrealistic convexity/NTK assumptions). The key novelty is studying networks that minimize or approximately minimize empirical risk and deriving bounds where both the number of samples N and the network width W appear independently in the denominator, with the constants governed by the minimum width W₀ needed to express the target distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical explanation for why over-parameterized models generalize well: the bound improves independently with more data and a wider network, and the required thresholds depend only on how hard the distribution is to express. It also yields a necessary condition (more data than network size for some distributions) and interprets practical phenomena—robustness failure, the role of over-parameterization, and loss-function choice—through the expressive-ability lens. The main limitation is the restriction to two-layer networks; preliminary deep-network results are sketched but not fully developed, and the bounds use loose O(·) notation that makes tight quantitative predictions difficult. Extending the framework to deep architectures and obtaining sharper, distribution-specific expressiveness estimates are the natural next steps.
