## Problem

Existing image evaluation metrics such as Aesthetics, CLIPScore, PickScore, ImageReward, and HPS show near-zero correlation (0.02–0.08) with actual viewer engagement (likes, clicks, purchases) in real-world marketing contexts, and no large-scale dataset of in-the-wild user engagement over images exists. This paper addresses the absence of both a reliable engagement metric and a pathway to optimize text-to-image generation for viewer engagement rather than merely aesthetics or realism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251373/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

EngageNet consumes an RGB image alongside rich contextual metadata—company handle, posting date, image resolution, color/tonal coverage, LLaVA-generated caption, and tags—and outputs a single scalar engagement score on a 0–100 scale representing brand-normalized likes. The generation pipeline takes a text prompt (optionally retrieved from a FAISS index of high-engagement captions) and produces a 512×512 image via Stable Diffusion or other T2I models. The Engagement Arena outputs Elo ratings comparing models head-to-head on shared prompts, with ground-truth EngagingImageNet images serving as a topline benchmark.

## Method

The paper introduces three complementary components. First, EngageNet is built by visual instruction fine-tuning LLaVA-1.5 13B on 957K samples from EngagingImageNet (168M enterprise tweets, 2007–2023), using a combined cross-entropy + MSE loss (λ=0.1) and synthetic negative samples to penalize image–context misalignment, achieving 0.62 Pearson correlation with ground-truth likes (0.87 as an oracle trained on all data). Second, three engagement-optimization strategies are applied to Stable Diffusion: (a) run-time prompt retrieval via FAISS over high-engagement captions, (b) Preferred Finetuning (PFT) of the U-Net on high-liked images using standard denoising score matching, and (c) DDPO reinforcement learning with EngageNet as the reward model in two variants—Engagement Simulation (ES) scoring generated images directly, and Design Specification Generation (DSG) rewarding alignment between verbalized image content and engagement-conditioned design specs. Third, the Engagement Arena uses EngageNet as an automated judge to compute Elo ratings across open- and closed-source T2I models, establishing the first engagement-based benchmark for text-to-image generation.

## Results

EngageNet achieves a Pearson correlation of 0.62 with ground-truth user engagement (account-normalized likes), substantially outperforming all existing metrics: PickScore (0.07), ImageReward (0.03), HPS (0.07), Aesthetic Score (0.07), CLIP Score (0.04), and even GPT-4V with 3-shot in-context learning (0.15). At run-time, prompt retrieval from high-engagement captions yields an average 12.41% engagement increase across 12 text-to-image models. At train-time, Preferred Finetuning (PFT) on high-engagement images improves SD 1.4's engagement reward by ~11% (e.g., 38.77→43.12 on the Low bucket), while RLHF-ES and RLHF-DSG yield smaller gains of ~3.7% and ~2.5% respectively. In the Engagement Arena, SD 3 Medium ranks highest with a 46% win rate over ground-truth images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EngageNet (0.62 correlation) decisively outperforms all existing image evaluation metrics, which cluster near random chance: PickScore (0.07), HPS (0.07), Aesthetic Score (0.07), CLIP Score (0.04), and ImageReward (0.03). Even GPT-4V with 3-shot in-context learning reaches only 0.15 correlation, less than a quarter of EngageNet's performance. Among text-to-image models in the Engagement Arena, SD 3 Medium (Elo 1041) leads, followed by SDXL-DPO and Flux.1-dev, while smaller models like PixArt (600M params) and EOIG-SD PFT (860M params) surprisingly surpass the much larger DALL-E-2 (6.5B params). For train-time optimization on SD 1.4, Preferred Finetuning (PFT) yields the largest engagement gains (~11% across buckets), outperforming RLHF-ES (~3.7%) and RLHF-DSG (~2.5%), though all three improve over the untrained baseline. On the FlickrUser generalization test, EngageNet achieves 0.53 correlation with only 15K fine-tuning samples, versus 0.23 for a from-scratch LLaVA-1.5 baseline, confirming cross-platform transferability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes LLaVA-1.5's visual instruction tuning paradigm to build EngageNet, an engagement-prediction VLM, and adapts the DDPO reinforcement learning framework (Black et al., 2023) to align Stable Diffusion's U-Net with engagement-based rewards. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant line of work on aligning text-to-image models with human preferences (PickScore, HPS, ImageReward, DPO), which the authors argue captures creator-side aesthetic biases rather than viewer-side engagement. The paper positions itself as filling a data gap: unlike MS-COCO or LAION, which contain no engagement signals, EngagingImageNet provides 168 million enterprise tweets with real-world like counts, enabling the first systematic study of engagement-optimized generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all widely-used image quality metrics (aesthetics, CLIP, PickScore, HPS, ImageReward) exhibit near-zero Pearson correlation (0.02–0.08) with actual user engagement is a significant negative result that challenges the field's reliance on these proxies. The EngagingImageNet dataset and Engagement Arena benchmark address a genuine gap in the literature, providing infrastructure for future engagement-driven research. However, limitations remain: engagement is measured solely via Twitter likes from enterprise accounts, EngageNet's 0.62 correlation is moderate, RL-based improvements (2–4%) are modest compared to supervised fine-tuning (8–11%), and external confounders (viral trends, timing effects) are not explicitly modeled. Open questions include generalization to other platforms and metrics, handling of unobserved confounders, and whether the engagement signal is stable enough to serve as a reliable training reward at scale.
