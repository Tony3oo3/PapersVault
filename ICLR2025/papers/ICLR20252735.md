## Problem

Energy-based decoding methods for controlled text generation define a target distribution via a weighted energy function and sample using gradient-based MCMC in continuous space (logit or embedding space). This continuous-space sampling produces only incremental sequence updates, leading to a persistent and difficult-to-tune trade-off between fluency and constraint satisfaction. The paper identifies this domain mismatch—sampling in continuous space rather than the natural discrete token space—as the root cause of the suboptimal balance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, a pre-trained auto-regressive LM (GPT-2 Large), its embedding table, and an external constraint function (e.g., a fine-tuned RoBERTa sentiment/toxicity classifier or differentiable BLEU score) as inputs, and produces a discrete token sequence of fixed length that satisfies the constraint while remaining fluent. No fine-tuning of the base LM is required; all control is applied at inference time. The constraint function must be differentiable with respect to one-hot token representations to enable gradient-based discrete sampling.

## Method

DAB introduces a joint distribution P(Y,B|X) over the response sequence Y and an auxiliary bias sequence B of discrete vocabulary tokens, then samples from it via a Langevin-within-Gibbs algorithm. In the B-update step, it applies the Discrete Langevin Proposal (DLP) to sample bias tokens by computing gradients of the constraint function f with respect to one-hot token vectors, using a globally balanced proposal that enables large, directional token changes. In the Y-update step, it performs biased auto-regressive generation where each position's logits are penalized by the squared L2 embedding distance between the candidate token and the corresponding bias token, scaled by a normalizing factor that matches the logit norm. Because the gradient is computed directly with respect to the one-hot representation of Y (rather than backpropagating through the auto-regressive chain), the computation is substantially cheaper—yielding over 2× the tokens-per-second of BOLT—while discrete sampling also eliminates the perplexity divergence observed in continuous methods.

## Results

DAB achieves the highest control scores across all three tasks: 0.992 internal classifier accuracy for sentiment (vs. 0.903 for BOLT), 0.211 average max toxicity for detoxification (vs. 0.264 for BOLT), and 0.8303 BertScore for keyword generation (vs. 0.8291 for BOLT). Fluency remains competitive, with DAB achieving 11.77 perplexity and 0.860 CoLA on sentiment (close to BOLT's 9.92 PPL and 0.874 CoLA), and substantially better fluency on keyword generation (23.4 PPL vs. BOLT's 32.0). DAB also decodes at 23.2 tokens/sec versus BOLT's 9.5 tokens/sec, a ~2.4× speedup, due to simpler gradient computation that avoids backpropagation through auto-regressive generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BOLT (the strongest continuous-bias baseline), DAB wins decisively on control: 0.992 vs. 0.903 internal sentiment classifier, 0.211 vs. 0.264 average max toxicity, and 0.8303 vs. 0.8291 BertScore for keyword relevance. On fluency, DAB is slightly behind BOLT on sentiment perplexity (11.77 vs. 9.92) but ahead on keyword PPL (23.4 vs. 32.0) and CoLA (0.726 vs. 0.705); DAB also decodes 2.4× faster (23.2 vs. 9.5 tokens/sec). Against COLD and MuCOLA, DAB dominates on both control and fluency across all three tasks—for example, on keyword generation, DAB achieves 23.4 PPL versus COLD's 242.0 and MuCOLA's 475.3. Against LM-Steer, DAB achieves comparable or better sentiment control (0.975 vs. 0.948 SST-2) while dramatically outperforming on fluency (11.8 vs. 72.2 PPL; 0.860 vs. 0.564 CoLA), and also wins on toxicity (0.211 vs. 0.265 avg max toxicity). The only metric where a baseline edges out DAB is sentiment REP-3gram (BOLT: 0.0008 vs. DAB: 0.004), a negligible difference in repeated trigrams.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAB builds directly on the energy-based decoding lineage (COLD, MuCOLA, BOLT) that frames controlled generation as sampling from a joint LM-plus-constraint distribution, but departs fundamentally by replacing continuous-space Langevin dynamics with the Discrete Langevin Proposal (DLP) from Zhang et al. (2022). The key conceptual move is introducing an auxiliary discrete bias sequence B and a Langevin-within-Gibbs scheme that alternates between discrete gradient-based sampling of B and biased auto-regressive generation of Y, thereby keeping the entire sampling loop in the token space. This is positioned as the first explicit connection between discrete gradient-based MCMC and controlled LLM decoding, distinguishing it from BOLT's continuous embedding-space biasing and from earlier encoder-based Gibbs samplers (Wang & Cho, 2019; Mireshghallah et al., 2022) that update one token at a time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a 2× decoding speedup over BOLT with comparable or better fluency and stronger constraint satisfaction across sentiment control, detoxification, and keyword generation, making discrete-space controlled decoding a viable default for inference-time steering. The paper's diagnosis—that the fluency-vs-control trade-off in prior EBM decoders stems from the continuous-to-discrete domain mismatch rather than from insufficient tuning of energy coefficients—reframes how practitioners should think about decoding-time control. Open questions include scalability to multiple simultaneous constraints, compositional generation, and whether the per-query gradient cost becomes prohibitive for very long sequences or high-frequency deployment.
