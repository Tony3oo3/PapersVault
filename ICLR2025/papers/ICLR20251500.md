## Problem

Data-driven carbon flux modelling (DDCFM) lacks a standardized, machine-learning-ready dataset, forcing research teams to prepare data ad-hoc and hindering reproducibility and cross-model comparison. Additionally, the current state-of-the-art relies on off-the-shelf tabular methods (random forests, gradient boosting) that fail to exploit the inherently multimodal nature of the biophysical predictors (meteorological time series + satellite imagery).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251500/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model ingests 21 hourly meteorological variables (temperature, radiation, wind, humidity, etc.) alongside 8×8-pixel MODIS images with 9 channels (7 spectral bands from MCD43A4 plus water/snow cover from MCD43A2), processed over a 32-hour context window. Meteorological values are Fourier-encoded into 40-dimensional vectors while spectral bands are linearly projected, and a modality mask flags missing timesteps or absent geospatial data. The output is a single scalar carbon flux estimate (NEE, GPP, or RECO) in μmol CO₂ m⁻² s⁻¹ per timestep.

## Method

EcoPerceiver is a multimodal transformer built on the Perceiver architecture that cross-attends a variable number of encoded inputs onto a compact latent space of shape (T, H_l), where each latent token represents the ecosystem state at one timestep. Meteorological inputs use NeRF-style Fourier encoding with learned per-variable embeddings, while spectral bands are flattened and linearly projected; a modality mask handles missing data and doubles as observational dropout. The key architectural innovation is Windowed Cross Attention (WCA), which pushes the time dimension into the batch dimension (inspired by SWin Transformer) to achieve O(T·V_t·H_a) runtime, followed by Causal Self Attention so each timestep is conditioned only on past and present observations. The full model stacks 8 WCA blocks with weight sharing and 4 CSA blocks, totaling ~989K parameters, and is trained with AdamW and cosine annealing over 20 epochs on 4 A100 GPUs. It is benchmarked against an XGBoost baseline configured to mimic the FLUXCOM-X SOTA, with evaluation via Nash-Sutcliffe Efficiency across 15 IGBP ecosystem types including zero-shot and one-shot generalization tests.

## Results

On the CarbonSense test set (385 EC sites, 15 IGBP ecosystem types), EcoPerceiver achieves a mean NSE of 0.62 across all types, outperforming the XGBoost baseline (mean NSE 0.55) in 12 of 15 IGBP types. The largest gains appear in open savannas (SAV: NSE 0.737 vs 0.580), snow/ice (SNO: 0.290 vs −0.037), and deciduous needleleaf forests (DNF: 0.434 vs 0.280). XGBoost retains a narrow edge in boreal ecosystems (ENF: +0.007 NSE, WET: +0.039 NSE), and both models fail on water bodies (WAT: NSE −11.1 and −14.4 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is XGBoost, configured to mimic the FLUXCOM-X SOTA pipeline [4]. EcoPerceiver wins in 12 of 15 IGBP types on NSE, with the most dramatic improvements in zero-shot (SNO: +0.327 NSE) and one-shot (DNF: +0.153 NSE) generalization settings. XGBoost edges out EcoPerceiver in three boreal-associated types (ENF by +0.007, WET by +0.039, WAT by +3.35 NSE), though the WAT comparison is between two models that both perform far worse than the mean predictor. A secondary comparison against a vanilla Transformer encoder [225] (Tables 8–9) shows EcoPerceiver winning in 13 of 15 types, with the vanilla Transformer only prevailing on WAT (NSE −9.68 vs −14.40), confirming that the WCA and CSA architectural components provide meaningful gains over standard self-attention for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of the long-standing DDCFM literature (dating to 2003) and recent advances in multimodal deep learning. Architecturally, EcoPerceiver is a direct descendant of the Perceiver [26], adopting its cross-attention-to-latent-space paradigm, while borrowing Fourier positional encoding from NeRF [27] for continuous meteorological inputs and the windowed-attention efficiency trick from SWin Transformer [28] to make per-timestep cross-attention tractable. The key conceptual departure from prior DDCFM work is treating carbon dynamics as non-Markovian via a 32-hour context window and ingesting satellite imagery as a first-class modality alongside tabular predictors, rather than reducing geospatial data to a handful of scalar features as in XGBoost-based SOTA [4].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CarbonSense is the first standardized, ML-ready dataset for DDCFM (385 sites, 27M+ hourly observations), removing the ad-hoc data preparation barrier that has kept deep learning researchers out of this domain. The demonstration that a compact (~1M parameter) multimodal transformer outperforms gradient boosting in 12 of 15 ecosystem types—especially on zero-shot and one-shot generalization—suggests that richer architectures can meaningfully improve global carbon flux inference. Open questions include closing the boreal-ecosystem gap where XGBoost still leads, addressing the severe geographic imbalance (Africa, Central Asia, South America underrepresented), and whether the WAT ecosystem's persistent failure signals a fundamental data-quantity limitation rather than an architectural one.
