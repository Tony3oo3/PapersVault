## Problem

Previous work on exact network embeddings using Logistic PCA (LPCA) provided only coarse dimension estimates in multiples of 16 and was restricted to networks under 20,000 nodes, leaving the true intrinsic dimensionality of complex networks unclear. This paper asks whether metric embedding formulations can provably achieve lower dimensions than LPCA, and how to efficiently identify and verify exact embedding dimensions for large-scale networks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252872/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a binary adjacency matrix (directed or undirected, self-links excluded) and produces paired embedding matrices X, Y ∈ R^{N×D} plus a scalar radius β that define a unit disk graph structure where connected nodes fall within each other's β-radius. The exact embedding dimension D* is identified via binary search, and reconstruction is verified through KD-tree fixed-radius nearest-neighbor queries against sparse edge lists rather than dense Frobenius-norm comparison, enabling scalability to million-node graphs without materializing the full adjacency matrix.

## Method

The core contribution is proving that the Euclidean Latent Distance Model reconstruction R_L2(X,Y,β) = β − ‖x_i − y_j‖₂ can achieve embedding dimensions up to 2 lower than LPCA (Theorem 2.1), by showing the metric model is expressible as an LPCA model augmented with two extra dimensions. An efficient binary search (Algorithm 1) identifies an upper bound on D*, using truncated SVD of higher-rank solutions to hot-start lower-rank embeddings. For large-scale networks, a two-stage optimization first uses the Hierarchical Block Distance Model (HBDM) for O(N log N) likelihood approximation via Euclidean divisive clustering, then refines with zero-margin hinge loss over only the misclassified dyads identified by KD-tree radius queries. This achieves exact reconstruction of networks up to a million nodes (com-amazon, roadNet-PA) with overall linearithmic complexity.

## Results

The proposed L2 metric embedding (LDM) achieves exact embedding dimensions D* of 6–41 across eight benchmark networks, consistently 1–4 dimensions lower than both LPCA and the latent eigenmodel. Compared to the previously reported dimensions by Chanpuriya et al. (2020), the improvement is substantial: e.g., Cora drops from 16 to 6, PubMed from 48 to 14, and ca-HepPh from 32 to 16. For the first time, exact embeddings are demonstrated on large-scale networks: com-amazon (334K nodes) achieves D*=13 and roadNet-PA (1.09M nodes) achieves D*=16, using the HBDM + hinge-loss two-stage procedure with linearithmic O(N log N) scaling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the LPCA baseline (Chanpuriya et al., 2020), the L2 metric embedding consistently achieves 1–4 fewer dimensions: e.g., Cora 6 vs. 9, ca-HepPh 16 vs. 19, PubMed 14 vs. 17, and Wiki-Vote 41 vs. 45. Against the latent eigenmodel (Hoff, 2007), the L2 model similarly wins by 1–4 dimensions on every undirected network (Cora 6 vs. 9, ca-GrQc 8 vs. 10, ca-HepPh 16 vs. 19, PubMed 14 vs. 17). The most striking comparison is against the coarse dimensions previously reported by Chanpuriya et al. (2020), which used multiples of 16: the L2 model finds dimensions 2.7×–3.4× lower (e.g., 6 vs. 16 for Cora, 14 vs. 48 for PubMed, 16 vs. 32 for ca-HepPh). The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 2.1: D*_LPCA − 2 ≤ D*_L2 ≤ D*_LPCA) is empirically confirmed, with the L2 model achieving the lower bound in most cases. The hinge-loss variant of L2 matches or slightly exceeds the plain L2 dimension (e.g., 7 vs. 6 on Cora), confirming that the two-stage HBDM + hinge procedure does not degrade the embedding quality while enabling scalable inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent distance model (LDM) of Hoff et al. (2002) and the Hierarchical Block Distance Model (HBDM) of Nakis et al. (2022) from Poisson to Bernoulli likelihood, establishing a provable dimensional advantage over the LPCA-based exact embeddings of Chanpuriya et al. (2020). The key departure is replacing the inner-product reconstruction with a metric (Euclidean distance) formulation, which Theorem 2.1 shows can require up to two fewer dimensions, and introducing a binary-search procedure with SVD-based warm starts to efficiently locate the exact embedding dimension. For scalability, the paper repurposes KD-tree nearest-neighbor queries and a two-stage HBDM-then-hinge-loss optimization to achieve linearithmic reconstruction checking, something infeasible with the O(N²) Frobenius-norm checks used in prior LPCA work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that even million-node networks (roadNet-PA, com-amazon) admit exact reconstruction in 13–16 dimensions substantially revises the previously reported intrinsic dimensionality of complex networks and opens the door to lossless low-dimensional representations for community detection, visualization, and motif discovery. However, the method identifies upper bounds rather than true minima, is susceptible to local minima (demonstrated on synthetic graphs), and the large-network results leave the search for tighter bounds to future work. Open questions include whether alternative geometries (hyperbolic, L∞) yield even lower dimensions, and whether exact embeddings remain useful for link prediction given that they explicitly encode non-edges as zero.
