## Problem

VQ-VAE-style visual tokenizers suffer from two fundamental limitations: CNN-based encoders require non-trivial architectural changes to handle video, and vector quantization scales poorly with codebook size (linear runtime, overfitting on smaller datasets). Additionally, treating videos as sequences of independently tokenized frames ignores temporal correlations and leads to suboptimal compression of dynamic motion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253269/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tokenizer accepts RGB images (256×256 or 128×128) or variable-length video clips, which are divided into 1×p×p non-overlapping patches and flattened into a 1D token sequence. Each patch is encoded into an L-bit binary code (effective vocabulary 2^L), and the decoder reconstructs the visual content. For compression, an autoregressive Transformer predicts conditional token probabilities consumed by an arithmetic coder to produce a near-optimal bitstream; for generation, a MaskGIT-style masked language model produces tokens that are decoded into images.

## Method

BSQ-ViT uses a Vision Transformer encoder-decoder with a block-wise causal attention mask that restricts each temporal position to attend only to current and past frames, unifying image and video tokenization and supporting variable-length inputs at inference. The core quantization step (BSQ) linearly projects the high-dimensional latent to L dimensions, normalizes onto the unit hypersphere, applies per-axis binary quantization (sign), and back-projects to the decoder space. A key theoretical result is that the soft quantization distribution factorizes into a product of L independent Bernoulli terms, reducing entropy computation from O(2^L × L) to O(L) and enabling efficient entropy regularization. Unlike LFQ, BSQ has a bounded quantization error (√(2−2/√L) < √2), which eliminates the need for a commitment loss and yields faster, more stable convergence. The full system is trained end-to-end within the VQ-GAN framework using MSE, LPIPS, adversarial (StyleGAN discriminator), and entropy losses, with the video tokenizer fine-tuned from a pre-trained image tokenizer.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k val (256×256), BSQ-ViT with L=36 achieves a reconstruction FID (rFID) of 0.41, a 43% reduction over the runner-up SDXL-VAE (0.72), while running 2.4× faster (45.1 vs. 18.9 images/sec). On UCF-101 video reconstruction, the best BSQ-ViT (BC ViT, L=36) reduces rFVD from 8.62 (MAGVIT-v2) to 4.10 on the training split. For image compression on Kodak, BSQ achieves MS-SSIM of 12.852 dB at 0.2812 bpp, outperforming JPEG2000 (11.574 dB) and WebP (12.193 dB). For image generation via Masked LM, BSQ reaches FID 5.44 (32 steps), competitive with BigGAN (6.02) and ADM (5.91).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BSQ-ViT outperforms all prior image tokenizers on every reconstruction metric. Against SDXL-VAE (the strongest prior method), BSQ-ViT with L=36 improves rFID from 0.72 to 0.41 (43% reduction), PSNR from 25.38 to 27.88 dB, and SSIM from 0.7276 to 0.8410 on ImageNet-1k, while being 2.4× faster in throughput. On video reconstruction (UCF-101), BSQ-ViT with L=36 achieves rFVD of 4.10 (train) / 6.21 (val), halving the 8.62 rFVD of the best MAGVIT-v2 configuration and substantially outperforming MAGVIT (25), TATS (162), and MaskGIT (216). For image compression on Kodak, BSQ beats both JPEG2000 and WebP on MS-SSIM at comparable bpp (12.852 vs. 11.574 and 12.193 dB). For video compression on MCL-JCV, BSQ with arithmetic coding matches H.264/H.265 at 0.1373 bpp (MS-SSIM 0.9818 vs. 0.9796/0.9807) while achieving a much lower LPIPS (0.0501 vs. 0.0949/0.0908). For image generation, BSQ with Masked LM (FID 5.44, 32 steps) surpasses BigGAN (FID 6.02) and is competitive with ADM (FID 5.91), while also outperforming VQ (FID 9.4) and FSQ (FID 8.5) under the same masked LM framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VQ-GAN framework (Esser et al., 2021) and the transformer-based ViT-VQGAN tokenizer (Yu et al., 2022) by replacing the learned codebook with Binary Spherical Quantization (BSQ), a parameter-free implicit codebook that projects embeddings onto a unit hypersphere before binarization. BSQ is a direct modification of Lookup-Free Quantization (LFQ, Yu et al., 2024), adding ℓ₂ normalization to the binary quantizer, which yields bounded quantization error and a factorized soft-quantization distribution enabling O(L) entropy computation instead of O(2^L × L). The architecture also departs from CNN-based video tokenizers (MAGVIT, TATS) by using a Vision Transformer with block-wise causal attention to natively handle variable-length videos, and integrates with MaskGIT-style masked language modeling for generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BSQ offers a clean, parameter-free quantization that scales to arbitrarily large effective vocabularies (2^L) without codebook overfitting, making it attractive for video tokenization where large codebooks are needed for motion representation. The competitive video compression results without explicit motion modeling (comparable to H.264/HEVC on MCL-JCV) suggest that learned tokenizers plus autoregressive entropy coding may eventually replace hybrid codecs, though the gap with HEVC on UVG and the reliance on a ~300M autoregressive model temper this claim. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 4K video and larger, more diverse training sets, and whether the spherical constraint's advantage over LFQ persists at much higher dimensions or with different encoder architectures.
