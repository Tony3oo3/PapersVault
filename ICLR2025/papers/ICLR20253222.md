## Problem

Existing EEG-based models trained on fixed datasets fail to generalize to the continual flow of new unseen subjects in real-world clinical settings, where significant individual discrepancies cause domain shifts. Prior continual learning and domain adaptation methods assume small, gradual domain changes at the sample level, but practical EEG scenarios involve a long-term, unordered stream of numerous individuals with large inter-subject variability, requiring adaptation at the individual level without ground-truth labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253222/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes one unlabeled EEG subject at a time from a continual flow (8-channel at 100 Hz for sleep staging, 32-channel at 250 Hz for emotion recognition, 64-channel at 160 Hz for motor imagery), along with a pre-trained model and a mixed buffer of true-labeled and pseudo-labeled samples drawn in an 8:2 ratio. It outputs per-epoch class predictions (5 sleep stages, 9 emotions, or 4 motor imagery classes) and an updated incremental model whose generalization is evaluated on a held-out set of unseen subjects after each adaptation round.

## Method

BrainUICL introduces two novel modules to balance the stability-plasticity dilemma in unsupervised individual continual learning. The Dynamic Confident Buffer (DCB) employs a selective replay strategy that stores true-labeled training samples and high-confidence pseudo-labeled incremental samples separately, replaying them in an 8:2 ratio to ensure label reliability while preserving diversity. The Cross Epoch Alignment (CEA) module applies KL-divergence loss every two fine-tuning epochs to align the current model's output distribution with its state two epochs earlier, preventing overfitting to outlier individuals without resetting critical parameters. Pseudo-labels are generated via Contrastive Predictive Coding (CPC) self-supervised fine-tuning of a guiding model, filtered by a confidence threshold (ξ₁=0.9). A schedule-controlled hyperparameter α gradually decreases the weight of incremental individual loss over the continual flow, progressively stabilizing the model as it accumulates knowledge. The backbone is a CNN feature extractor followed by a Transformer temporal encoder and a classifier, kept identical across all three EEG tasks.

## Results

On the three EEG tasks, BrainUICL achieves the best plasticity and stability simultaneously. On ISRUC (sleep staging), it reaches 74.9% ACC and 74.0% AAA; on FACED (emotion recognition), 40.3% ACC and 36.0% AAA; and on Physionet-MI (motor imagery), 48.4% ACC and 48.7% AAA. Compared to the initial pre-trained model M₀, BrainUICL improves average MF1 by +13.4% on ISRUC, +19.5% on FACED, and +2.8% on Physionet, while also improving stability (AAA) by +2.1%, +12.5%, and +1.9% respectively. The method demonstrates the lowest variance across five random input orders (e.g., ±0.1 on ISRUC AAA), indicating strong robustness to the ordering of the continual individual flow.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BrainUICL outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across all three datasets. The UDA methods (MMD, TSTCC) perform worst overall: while they achieve moderate plasticity on ISRUC and FACED, they severely degrade stability (e.g., TSTCC drops ISRUC AAA from 72.0 to 61.3 and FACED AAA from 24.0 to 33.5). The CL-based methods (EWC, LwF) improve slightly over UDA but still fall 2–5 points behind BrainUICL on AAA/AAF1, confirming that regularization alone is insufficient for the large individual domain shifts in EEG. Among UCDA methods, CoTTA is the strongest baseline (e.g., 72.2% ACC on ISRUC, 47.4% on Physionet), yet BrainUICL still leads by 2.7 and 1.0 ACC points respectively, and by a larger margin on stability (ISRUC AAA: 74.0 vs. 69.2; Physionet AAF1: 48.3 vs. 44.6). ReSNT is competitive on ISRUC stability (AAA 71.3) but lags significantly on plasticity (ACC 70.7 vs. 74.9) and on FACED/Physionet across all metrics. Notably, BrainUICL also exhibits the smallest standard deviations (±0.1–0.6) compared to baselines (±0.1–1.8), indicating superior robustness to the random ordering of the continual individual flow.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends rehearsal-based continual learning and unsupervised continual domain adaptation (UCDA) to a new EEG-specific formulation where each subject constitutes a distinct individual domain. It builds on CPC (Oord et al., 2018) for self-supervised pseudo-label generation and draws on replay-based CL methods (iCaRL, EWC, LwF) and UCDA approaches (UCL-GV, ConDA, CoTTA), but departs from them by addressing significant individual-level domain shifts rather than the minimal batch-level shifts assumed in prior UCDA work. The two novel modules—Dynamic Confident Buffer (selective real-pseudo mixed replay) and Cross Epoch Alignment (KL divergence between model states at different epochs)—are designed specifically for the long-term, unordered, unsupervised individual flow that characterizes real-world EEG deployment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap: EEG models deployed in clinical or BCI settings must handle a continuous stream of new patients with individual physiological discrepancies, yet prior models are trained on fixed datasets and cannot adapt. BrainUICL's plug-and-play design (no architecture modification) and its ability to simultaneously improve both plasticity on new subjects and stability on all unseen subjects make it directly applicable to real-world EEG pipelines. Limitations include evaluation on only three tasks constrained by publicly available dataset sizes, and the reliance on a small source domain for pretraining. Open questions include whether the approach extends to tasks with very few subjects, how it behaves under truly adversarial outlier distributions, and whether the 8:2 real-pseudo replay ratio generalizes across domains.
