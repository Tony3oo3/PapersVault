## Problem

Weight space learning aims to predict properties of neural networks (e.g., training dataset, generalization error) from their weights, but existing probing methods that represent models by their responses to learned inputs produce probes no better than random samples. The paper identifies that current latent-optimization-based probe learning is suboptimal and overfits, and asks how to generate structured, generalizable probes that actually improve upon unlearned baselines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250603/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target neural network (INR, small CNN, or ResNet18) and generates a set of k probe inputs (32×32 images for CNNs, 2D coordinate pairs for INRs) via a shared generator. These probes are forwarded through the target model, and the concatenated output vectors (pixel values or class logits) are fed to a 6-layer MLP classifier that predicts either a discrete class label (dataset identity) or a continuous generalization error. The probe count is a tunable budget (16–2048), and the method requires only forward passes through the target model at inference, making it applicable to black-box APIs.

## Method

ProbeGen factorizes each probe p_i into a per-probe latent code z_i (dimension 32) and a shared deep linear generator G, so p_i = G(z_i). The generator is a stack of linear layers with no non-linear activations between them, which the authors show provides implicit regularization under SGD (following Arora et al., 2019) and reduces overfitting compared to non-linear generators. For image probes, G uses transposed convolutional layers (DCGAN-style, 5–6 layers, width multiplier 16) to impose local structure and multi-scale hierarchy as a data-specific inductive bias; for INR coordinate probes, G uses 2 fully-connected layers. Training jointly optimizes the latent codes via latent optimization and the generator/classifier via cross-entropy or MSE, with the key finding that removing activations and sharing parameters across probes yields probes with far more structure and significantly lower generalization gap than vanilla latent-optimized probes.

## Results

On the four standard weight-space learning benchmarks (128 probes), ProbeGen achieves 98.4% accuracy on MNIST INRs, 87.7% on Fashion-MNIST INRs, 0.957 Kendall's τ on CIFAR10-GS, and 0.932 on CIFAR10 Wild Park, outperforming all baselines by a large margin. On the newly introduced ResNet18 Zoo (6,000+ models trained on Tiny-ImageNet subsets), ProbeGen reaches 0.910 Kendall's τ, surpassing vanilla probing (0.843) and StatNN (0.856). In terms of efficiency, ProbeGen requires only 0.02B FLOPs on MNIST and 3.41B on CIFAR10-GS, compared to 63.40B and 94.56B for Neural Graphs — a 30× to 1000× reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProbeGen outperforms all five baselines across every benchmark where they overlap. Against the strongest graph-based method, Neural Graphs (Kofinas et al., 2024), ProbeGen gains +1.1% on MNIST (0.984 vs 0.976), +13.2% on FMNIST (0.877 vs 0.745), +0.019 on CIFAR10-GS (0.957 vs 0.938), and +0.047 on CIFAR10 Wild Park (0.932 vs 0.885) at 128 probes. ScaleGMN (Kalogeropoulos et al., 2024), the best 0-probe mechanistic baseline, is surpassed by ProbeGen on all four tasks (e.g., 0.877 vs 0.808 on FMNIST). StatNN (Unterthiner et al., 2020) is the weakest baseline, trailing ProbeGen by 58.6% on MNIST and 21.3% on CIFAR10 Wild Park. DWS (Navon et al., 2023) and NFN (Zhou et al., 2024) are not applicable to CNNs (CIFAR10 tasks) and are outperformed on the INR tasks. Notably, ProbeGen also requires 30–1000× fewer FLOPs than Neural Graphs, making it the only method that scales to the 6,000+ ResNet18 models in the new ResNet18 Zoo, where it achieves 0.910 Kendall's τ versus 0.843 for vanilla probing and 0.856 for StatNN.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions probing as a viable alternative to the dominant mechanistic and graph-based approaches for weight space learning, drawing an explicit analogy to dynamic vs. static binary code analysis. It builds directly on the probing framework of Herrmann et al. (2024) and Kofinas et al. (2024), but departs from their use of direct latent optimization by introducing a shared deep linear generator module. The architectural choice of stacked linear layers without activations is grounded in Arora et al. (2019)'s analysis of implicit regularization in deep linear networks, repurposing that regularization effect to constrain probe expressivity rather than to model data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 30–1000× FLOPs reduction over graph-based methods makes weight space learning practical at scales (e.g., ResNet18) where GNN/Transformer approaches become infeasible, and the black-box compatibility of probing opens a path to analyzing models accessible only via API. Key limitations remain: probing requires consistent input/output semantics across models (ruling out heterogeneous class sets), cannot produce layer-level or weight-level predictions, and scaling to very large models (CLIP, Stable Diffusion) under limited compute is unresolved. The finding that current learned probes are no better than random synthetic inputs suggests the broader probing literature may be underexploited, and adaptive probe selection is identified as a promising next direction.
