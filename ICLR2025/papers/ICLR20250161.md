## Problem

MLLMs excel at single-page document understanding but struggle with long, multi-page visually-rich documents: classical document parsers lose layout information and degrade performance, while feeding all pages to MLLMs causes severe memory overhead and distraction from irrelevant content, often triggering out-of-memory errors on documents with hundreds of pages. The paper asks whether MLLMs themselves can serve as effective multimodal retrievers to select only the relevant evidence pages before answering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250161/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-page document (each page rendered as a single RGB image, e.g., 1024×768) and a free-form text question. The retrieval module processes each page independently as a single image, producing a ranked list of top-k evidence pages (k=1 for single-page backbones like PaliGemma and Phi-3-v, k=5 for multi-image backbones like InternVL2-4B). The QA module then consumes the retrieved page images plus the question and generates a free-form text answer autoregressively. Documents in evaluation range from 20 pages (SlideVQA) to over 468 pages (MMLongBench-Doc).

## Method

SV-RAG shares a single MLLM backbone (vision encoder + LLM) across two modules differentiated by dual LoRA adapters (~2% extra parameters): a retrieval adapter and a QA adapter. The retrieval module follows the ColPali architecture, projecting LLM hidden states through a Col-projection layer and scoring relevance via contextualized late interaction (max-over-dot-product between question token embeddings and page visual token embeddings), trained with contrastive learning using hardest in-batch negatives. A key ablation finding is that hidden states from an intermediate layer (layer 21 of Phi-3-vision) yield better retrieval accuracy than the final layer, reducing both computational cost and improving top-1 accuracy. The QA module uses a standard LLaVA-style decoder that autoregressively generates answers from the retrieved evidence pages. This design keeps retrieval memory constant regardless of document length (each page is encoded independently) while limiting QA memory to only the top-k pages, enabling 4B-parameter models to handle documents that would OOM 8B models processing all pages.

## Results

SV-RAG-InternVL2† (4B parameters, top-5 retrieval) achieves 23.0 ACC and 24.2 F1 on MMLongBench-Doc, outperforming all open-source MLLMs (best prior: InternVL-Chat-v1.5 at 14.6 ACC) and rivaling proprietary models like Claude-3 Opus (17.4 ACC) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (28.2 ACC). On retrieval, the Col-Phi-3-vision module reaches 90.6% top-1 accuracy on SlideVQA and 64.8% on MMLongBench-Doc, surpassing all text-based (BM25, SBERT, BGE-large) and encoder (CLIP, SigLip) baselines. On cross-page QA, SV-RAG-InternVL2† achieves 31.98 EM on MP-SlideVQA and 0.71 ANLS on MP-DocVQA, consistently exceeding InternVL2-8B processing all pages (17.04 EM, 0.68 ANLS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On retrieval (Table 1), SV-RAG's Col-retrieval modules decisively outperform all baselines: Col-Phi-3-vision achieves 90.6% top-1 on SlideVQA vs. 82.2% for the best text-based method (NV-Embed-v2) and 66.2% for SigLip; on MMLongBench-Doc it reaches 64.8% vs. 47.4% for BGE-large and 44.9% for SigLip. On multi-page QA (Table 2), SV-RAG-InternVL2† (4B, top-5) surpasses InternVL2-8B processing all pages on every metric across SP-SlideVQA (45.07 vs. 12.62 EM), MMLongBench (34.0 vs. 14.1 G-Acc), and MP-SlideVQA (31.98 vs. 17.04 EM), demonstrating that retrieval-based page selection is far more effective than brute-force multi-page input. On MMLongBench-Doc (Table 3), SV-RAG-InternVL2† (23.0 ACC) outperforms all open-source models by a wide margin—nearly doubling InternVL-Chat-v1.5 (14.6 ACC) and exceeding 26B-parameter models—and approaches Claude-3 Opus (17.4 ACC) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (28.2 ACC), though GPT-4o (42.8 ACC) remains substantially ahead. Fine-tuning the QA adapter with LoRA improves EM on SlideVQA (e.g., 45.07 vs. 16.40 for InternVL2-4B) but can slightly degrade generalization on MMLongBench-Doc (34.0 vs. 33.2 G-Acc) and DUDE, suggesting a trade-off between domain-specific and general performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

SV-RAG builds directly on ColPali's use of MLLMs as document retrievers with late-interaction scoring and on ColBERT's max-over-dot-product relevance formulation, but departs from both by unifying retrieval and generation within a single MLLM backbone via dual LoRA adapters rather than deploying separate encoder and generator models. It also moves beyond ColPali's single-page retrieval focus to the multi-page document QA setting, where the retrieved evidence may span several pages. The dual-adapter parameter-sharing design (adding only ~2% parameters) is the paper's primary architectural departure from prior RAG pipelines that treat retrieval and generation as independent components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a 4B-parameter model with a retrieval-then-answer pipeline can rival proprietary systems like Gemini-1.5-Pro on MMLongBench-Doc is practically significant for resource-constrained and on-device deployment of document intelligence. The VisR-Bench dataset (226 documents, 471 QA pairs across nine visual domains) fills a gap in evaluation for visually-rich, multi-page document QA beyond slides and financial reports. Open questions include whether the approach scales to documents with thousands of pages, whether the performance drop observed when fine-tuning the QA adapter on one domain harms generalization (noted on MMLongBench-Doc and DUDE), and whether true on-device inference (e.g., Apple Intelligence routing) can match the A100 results reported here.
