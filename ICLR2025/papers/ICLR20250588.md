## Problem

Real-time instruction-based portrait editing requires simultaneously preserving facial identity, faithfully following text instructions, and achieving fast inference, yet these three objectives are in inherent tension. Existing image-editing models (InstructPix2Pix, MagicBrush) prioritize text fidelity but cause severe identity distortion, while identity-preserving models (IP-Adapter, InstantID) maintain identity at the cost of facial style and text alignment, and all require 30–50 diffusion sampling steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250588/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 1024×576 RGB portrait image and a free-form text editing instruction (e.g., 'smiling, in bright clothes, pop art style') and produces a single edited portrait at the same resolution in one forward pass. The input image is encoded to latent space and concatenated with the noisy latent in the U-Net's first convolutional layer (InstructPix2Pix-style conditioning), while the text instruction is injected via cross-attention. The 859M-parameter model requires only 1 inference step versus 30–50 for all baselines.

## Method

Training proceeds in two stages. First, an Identity Enhancement Network (IDE-Net) is built on InstructPix2Pix and trained with an Annealing Identity Loss that computes L2 distance between FaceNet embeddings of grayscale face crops, weighted by a linear decay t/T_max so identity supervision dominates early denoising steps while text alignment takes over later. Second, IPNet is distilled from IDE-Net via a Diffusion Multi-Objective Distillation scheme combining three losses: an unconditional Adversarial Loss (hinge-loss discriminator) for one-step generation, an Identity Distillation Loss that replaces stochastic SDS noise with DDIM inversion for t ≤ 200 (fine-grained detail transfer) while retaining stochastic sampling for t > 200 (coarse structure/pose), and a Face-Style Enhancing Triplet Loss using DINOv2 embeddings to pull the output closer to the target's style while keeping it near the input's identity. Distillation is staged across three time-step ranges ([400,800], [200,400], [150,200]) with progressively shifting loss weights, culminating in a large-batch (2048) low-time-step phase that activates the triplet loss for style refinement.

## Results

IPNet achieves the best scores across all evaluated metrics while requiring only 1 inference step (vs. 30–50 for all baselines) and a compact 859M parameter model. On identity preservation, IPNet scores 0.867 (FaceNet) and 0.782 (InsightFace), surpassing the next-best IP-Control-1.5 (0.794/0.670) and InstantID (0.751/0.689). On text fidelity, IPNet reaches 0.271 (CLIP-Vit-g) and 0.309 (CLIP-Vit-H), outperforming InstantID (0.257/0.296). On image quality, IPNet attains 4.960 (Q-Align-Q) and 3.629 (Q-Align-A), leading all baselines by a clear margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IPNet outperforms all five baselines on every single metric. Against the instruction-editing models (MagicBrush, InstructPix2Pix), IPNet dramatically improves identity preservation (FaceNet 0.867 vs. 0.461 and 0.263) while also matching or exceeding their text fidelity, confirming that the Annealing Identity Loss and distillation resolve the identity distortion these methods suffer from. Against the identity-preserving models (IP-Control-1.5, IP-Control-XL, InstantID), IPNet wins on both identity (FaceNet 0.867 vs. 0.794/0.662/0.751) and text fidelity (CLIP-Vit-g 0.271 vs. 0.235/0.228/0.257), showing that the Face-Style Enhancing Triplet Loss and DDIM-inversion-based distillation overcome the style degradation that plagues embedding-based identity methods. Notably, IPNet achieves all of this with 1 inference step and an 859M model, compared to 30–50 steps and up to 4150M parameters for the baselines, making it 30–50× faster at inference with a smaller footprint.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on InstructPix2Pix as its architectural base for instruction-conditioned image editing, and on the IP-Adapter + ControlNet and InstantID line of work for identity-preserving generation. It departs from prior editing models (MagicBrush, InstructPix2Pix) by introducing a dedicated portrait-editing training pipeline with an Annealing Identity Loss, and from prior distillation methods (SDS, adversarial diffusion distillation) by combining adversarial loss, DDIM-inversion-based identity distillation, and a DINOv2-based face-style triplet loss into a unified multi-objective distillation framework. The two-stage teacher-student design (IDE-Net → IPNet) is inspired by progressive distillation and adversarial diffusion distillation but is specifically structured to decouple identity learning from style refinement, addressing a trade-off that prior single-stage approaches could not resolve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving one-step inference (vs. 30–50 steps) while simultaneously improving identity preservation, text fidelity, and image quality over all compared SOTA models is a practically significant efficiency win for real-time applications like AR filters and video communication. The ablation revealing that adversarial loss alone catastrophically destroys identity (FaceNet 0.89 → 0.048) without distillation supervision is a useful cautionary result for anyone attempting GAN-like distillation of diffusion models. A key limitation is that facial expression editing remains weak due to dataset composition, and the method is confined to portrait editing rather than general image editing, leaving open whether the multi-objective distillation recipe generalizes to other domains.
