## Problem

Existing dynamic scene video methods rely on Layered Depth Images (LDIs) to represent pseudo-3D space, which discretizes continuous landscapes into separate layers, causing diminished depth perception, distortions under camera movement, and restricting output to the 2D domain. No prior work has represented a complete 3D space with fluid animation (i.e., 4D) from a single landscape image using an explicit volumetric representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252901/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single landscape RGB image and a manually annotated motion mask (created via LabelMe) that designates regions to animate (e.g., water, clouds). Depth is estimated with ZoeDepth to lift pixels into a 3D point cloud, and ~30 camera viewpoints are defined along a trajectory. The output is a set of 4D Gaussians—an explicit 3D volumetric representation with per-Gaussian temporal deformation of position, rotation, and scale—that can be rendered from arbitrary novel viewpoints to produce dynamic scene video, unlike prior LDI-based methods whose output is confined to 2D image sequences.

## Method

The framework operates in three stages: (1) multi-view RGB images are rendered from a depth-lifted point cloud and used to optimize 3D Gaussians via L1+SSIM loss; (2) 2D Eulerian flow is estimated independently for each multi-view image using an off-the-shelf animation model, then unprojected into 3D via the depth map, and the proposed 3D Motion Optimization Module (3D-MOM) enforces cross-view consistency by minimizing the L1 loss between the projected 3D motion and each view's estimated 2D motion over all N viewpoints using SGD; (3) the consistent 3D motion initializes the position deformation of 4D Gaussians (using the 4D-GS HexPlane+MLP architecture), and a two-stage training scheme—first fixing time at 0 to refine 3D Gaussians across all viewpoints, then training temporal deformation across sampled viewpoints with animated video frames—yields the final 4D Gaussians.

## Results

On the Holynski et al. (2021) validation set (162 samples, 27 scenes, 4 camera trajectories), the proposed method achieves PSNR 20.57, SSIM 0.90, LPIPS 0.14, and PIQE 7.80, outperforming all baselines on every metric. In the user study, the method receives the highest preference across all four criteria: Immersion (56.25%), Realism (57.81%), Structural Consistency (62.81%), and Quality (60.25%). The two-stage training strategy (Appendix E) reduces 4D Gaussian training time from 51m34s to 39m45s while maintaining comparable metrics (PSNR 17.93 vs. 18.17).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five baselines on every quantitative metric. Against the closest LDI-based competitor, 3D-Cinemagraphy (Li et al., 2023), it gains +3.27 dB PSNR (20.57 vs. 17.30) and +0.07 SSIM, while reducing LPIPS from 0.17 to 0.14 and PIQE from 8.93 to 7.80. Make-It-4D (Shen et al., 2023) trails by +3.59 dB PSNR and +0.09 SSIM, with the gap widening in the user study where the proposed method earns 56–63% preference versus Make-It-4D's 8–12%. The diffusion-based methods DynamiCrafter (Xing et al., 2025) and Motion-I2V (Shi et al., 2024) lag substantially, with PSNR gaps of +5.59 and +6.19 dB respectively, and DynamiCrafter shows a particularly poor PIQE of 24.58 versus 7.80 for the proposed method. In the user study, the proposed method more than doubles 3D-Cinemagraphy's preference on every criterion (e.g., 62.81% vs. 28.75% for Structural Consistency), confirming that the explicit 4D Gaussian representation yields visibly superior depth perception and motion consistency compared to LDI-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dynamic scene video line of work (3D Cinemagraphy, Make-It-4D) by replacing their pseudo-3D Layered Depth Image representations with explicit 4D Gaussian Splatting, enabling a complete volumetric 3D space with animation from a single image. It builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) for spatial representation, 4D-GS (Wu et al., 2024) for temporal deformation via HexPlane and MLP, and Eulerian motion fields (Holynski et al., 2021) for 2D motion estimation. The key conceptual departure is the 3D Motion Optimization Module (3D-MOM), which addresses motion ambiguity arising from independently estimated 2D flows by jointly optimizing a parametric 3D motion field against multi-view 2D motion targets — a problem not addressed in prior LDI-based or 4D Gaussian approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit Gaussian output provides practical versatility beyond 2D video (editable 3D assets, arbitrary camera paths, long-duration rendering up to 330 frames), and the two-stage training strategy achieves a 30× speedup in the 3D Gaussian fitting stage with negligible quality loss. However, the method still requires manually annotated motion masks, depends on the quality of off-the-shelf 2D animation models, and is validated primarily on landscape scenes with fluid motion (water, clouds). Open questions include whether 3D-MOM generalizes to non-fluid dynamic scenes (e.g., moving people, vehicles), whether automatic motion mask generation is feasible, and how the approach scales to larger or more complex scenes beyond the ~30-viewpoint trajectories tested.
