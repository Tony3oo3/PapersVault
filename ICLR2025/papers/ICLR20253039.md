## Problem

Scaling 2D Gaussian Splatting to large-scale scenes (0.3–2.7 km²) is hindered by three coupled failures: blurry surfels that slow convergence, Gaussian count explosion during parallel block tuning that triggers OOM errors, and the absence of a reliable geometry evaluation protocol that accounts for boundary under-observation. Existing large-scale pipelines like CityGaussian also incur prohibitive training time (~4 hours) and memory (~31.5 GB) due to post-pruning and distillation stages.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 4,000+ multi-view RGB images per scene (aerial views downsampled to 1600 px on the long side, street views at 1000×1000), COLMAP-estimated camera poses, and a sparse point cloud for initialization. It produces a compressed 2D Gaussian surfel representation (as small as 290 MB with vectree quantization), novel-view renderings at real-time FPS, a TSDF-fused 3D mesh (1 m voxel, 4 m SDF truncation), and per-pixel depth maps. Ground-truth LiDAR point clouds are used exclusively for geometry evaluation, not training.

## Method

CityGaussianV2 builds on 2DGS surfels and introduces three optimization components: (1) Decomposed-Gradient-based Densification (DGD), which reformulates the densification gradient to use only the SSIM-loss component (scaled to match the original threshold), eliminating blurry surfels that L1 loss cannot penalize; (2) an Elongation Filter that excludes surfels with aspect ratio below a threshold from cloning/splitting, preventing the exponential Gaussian count growth that causes OOM in parallel tuning; and (3) depth supervision from Depth Anything V2 with exponentially decaying weight. For scalability, the pipeline replaces CityGaussian's post-pruning and distillation with SH degree 2 from scratch (reducing feature dimension from 48 to 27) and contribution-based trimming integrated into per-block fine-tuning, followed by vectree quantization achieving ~10× storage compression. A TnT-style evaluation protocol with visibility-based crop volume estimation (using a GS rasterizer to count per-point visible frequency) is introduced to produce stable F1 scores with error bars reduced from 0.1 to 0.003.

## Results

On GauU-Scene, CityGaussianV2 achieves F1 0.501 (P 0.576, R 0.450) and PSNR 24.51, outperforming 2DGS (F1 0.491) and CityGaussian (F1 0.453) in geometry while matching rendering quality. On MatrixCity-Aerial, the method reaches F1 0.556 and PSNR 27.23, doubling 2DGS's F1 (0.270) and surpassing CityGaussian (F1 0.462) by a wide margin. On MatrixCity-Street, it achieves F1 0.503 and PSNR 22.24, again leading all baselines in geometric accuracy. The small compressed version (ours-s) reduces training time by 25% and memory by 50% versus CityGaussian while maintaining superior F1 scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CityGaussianV2 achieves the best F1 score across all three dataset splits, outperforming the closest competitor 2DGS by 0.010 (GauU-Scene), 0.286 (MatrixCity-Aerial), and 0.060 (MatrixCity-Street). Against CityGaussian, the method gains approximately 11% relative F1 improvement (0.501 vs 0.453 on GauU-Scene) at the cost of only 0.24 PSNR, and achieves a 20% F1 gain on MatrixCity-Aerial (0.556 vs 0.462). NeRF-based baselines (NeuS, Neuralangelo) fail entirely on geometry for most scenes due to NaN errors or poor convergence under sparse supervision in large-scale settings. GOF fails to produce meaningful meshes on MatrixCity-Aerial and produces shell-like artifacts on other scenes, while SuGaR suffers from severely low recall (0.292 on GauU-Scene) due to inability to recover complex geometry details. The paper also shows that directly applying CityGaussian's parallel training to 2DGS (without the proposed optimization) triggers OOM errors on 4 out of 5 scenes, underscoring the necessity of the Elongation Filter and DGD components.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) as its geometric primitive, departing from the 3DGS-based approaches (SuGaR, GOF) that struggle with surface ambiguity and scalability. It inherits CityGaussian's block-partitioned parallel training strategy but streamlines the pipeline by removing post-pruning and distillation, replacing them with contribution-based trimming and a lower SH degree from the start. The optimization mechanism draws on Depth Anything V2 for depth supervision and introduces a decomposed-gradient densification strategy that prioritizes SSIM loss gradients over L1 RGB gradients, a design choice motivated by the observation that 2DGS is more susceptible to early-stage blurriness than 3DGS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CityGaussianV2 is among the first to deliver geometrically accurate large-scale surface reconstruction with Gaussian primitives at practical training costs (25% less time, 50% less memory than CityGaussian), making it relevant for real-world city-scale applications. The TnT-style evaluation protocol with visibility-based crop volume estimation addresses a genuine gap in the field—prior geometry benchmarks for large-scale scenes were unreliable due to boundary effects and inconsistent point extraction. Open questions remain around handling illumination variance (current datasets have compensated lighting), reconstructing thin structures like spires, and closing the rendering-speed gap with CityGS, which still achieves higher FPS at similar Gaussian counts.
