## Problem

Current mechanistic interpretability methods, particularly sparse dictionary learning on activations, can identify which features are present in a model but cannot explain how MLP weights construct those features or what their downstream computational role is. Element-wise nonlinearities in standard MLPs introduce higher-order interactions that make it impossible to trace computations directly through the weight matrices, leaving MLPs as undecomposable black boxes in circuit analysis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251691/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the raw weight matrices of a bilinear MLP layer (W, V of shape d_hidden × d_input, and downprojection P of shape d_output × d_hidden) along with a set of output feature directions (e.g., unembedding vectors for classification or SAE decoder directions for language models). It produces symmetric interaction matrices of shape (d_input, d_input) per output direction, which are then eigendecomposed into orthonormal eigenvectors and real-valued eigenvalues. The eigenvectors are quadratic feature detectors in input space, and their eigenvalues provide importance scores that enable truncation, adversarial mask construction via pseudoinverse, and low-rank circuit extraction—all without any forward passes or input data.

## Method

The paper shows that a bilinear MLP (a GLU with no element-wise nonlinearity, g(x) = (Wx) ⊙ (Vx)) can be fully expressed as a third-order tensor B with entries b_ijk = w_ik · v_jk, where the downprojection P is absorbed into B. Because each interaction matrix B_a is evaluated with two copies of the same input x, only its symmetric part contributes, yielding real eigenvalues and orthogonal eigenvectors by the spectral theorem. For a chosen output direction u, the interaction matrix Q = u·_out B is eigendecomposed, and the layer's output along u becomes a sum of quadratic terms λ_i(v_i^T x)², making each eigenvector an interpretable feature detector with no cross-interactions. This weight-only decomposition reveals low-rank structure across MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and language models, enables construction of adversarial masks from pseudoinverse rows of the eigenvector matrix, and identifies nonlinear feature circuits (e.g., a sentiment-negation circuit) directly from the bilinear tensor without gradient-based attribution or input datasets.

## Results

On TinyStories (6-layer, ~30M params), bilinear MLPs achieve a loss of 1.337 under both constant-epoch and constant-time training, compared to 1.321/1.336 for SwiGLU and 1.332/1.337 for ReGLU — a 6% data-efficiency gap that vanishes when compute is held fixed. For interpretability, eigenvector decomposition of bilinear layers on MNIST/Fashion-MNIST reveals low-rank structure where truncating to the top few eigenvectors causes only a 0.01% accuracy drop. In language models, SAE output features are well-approximated by low-rank interaction matrices: the average correlation with a single eigenvector is ~0.65, and most features exceed 0.75 correlation with just two eigenvectors (conditioned on the feature being active). The sentiment negation circuit is captured by two eigenvectors with a 0.66 correlation (0.76 at large activations) with the not-good feature.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between bilinear MLPs and standard GLU variants (ReGLU, SwiGLU) as drop-in replacements in transformers. On TinyStories, bilinear layers are 6% less data-efficient than SwiGLU (requiring ~6% more epochs to reach the same loss of 1.337 vs. 1.321) but are equally compute-efficient when training time is held constant (1.337 vs. 1.336). The authors note this gap likely narrows with longer training. Beyond performance, the key comparison is methodological: bilinear layers enable weight-based eigendecomposition that is fully equivalent to the layer's computation, whereas prior circuit-discovery methods (gradient-based attribution in Marks et al. 2024, transcoders in Dunefsky et al. 2024) rely on input datasets and linear approximations that may not capture nonlinear interactions. The paper demonstrates this advantage by extracting a sentiment negation circuit directly from weights with no forward passes or gradient computations, something not achievable with the prior approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes Sharkey's (2023) suggestion that bilinear layers are intrinsically interpretable by developing concrete eigendecomposition and HOSVD methods to extract features directly from weights, without relying on input data. It departs from the dominant activation-based interpretability paradigm (sparse dictionary learning, attribution patching) that treats MLPs as undecomposable black boxes, instead reviving the older weight-based approach (Olah et al., 2017; Voss et al., 2021) in a form that is fully equivalent to the layer's computation. The bilinear architecture itself draws on prior GLU work (Shazeer, 2020; Dauphin et al., 2017) and bilinear CNNs (Lin et al., 2015; Li et al., 2017), but repurposes it specifically as an interpretable drop-in replacement rather than a performance optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that weight-based interpretability can identify nonlinear circuits (e.g., the sentiment negation circuit) grounded entirely in model weights, offering a potential alternative to gradient-based or dataset-dependent circuit discovery methods that may not generalize out-of-distribution. The finding that SAE output features in language models are surprisingly low-rank (correlation >0.75 with just two eigenvectors) suggests that the interactions producing large activations have a simple geometric structure, making them more tractable for analysis. Key limitations include reliance on pre-existing output feature dictionaries (SAEs), no guarantee of monosemanticity in eigenvectors, and evidence primarily on models up to ~335M parameters; whether the low-rank structure and interpretability gains persist at frontier scale remains an open question.
