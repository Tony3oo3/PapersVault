## Problem

Predicting the change in binding free energy (ΔΔG) upon mutation is critical for modulating protein-protein interactions, but experimental data is scarce and existing pre-training-based methods rely on simple supervised fine-tuning that risks catastrophic forgetting. More fundamentally, previous inverse folding–based approaches neglect the unbound state in the thermodynamic cycle, lacking the physical inductive bias needed for energy-related predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250657/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the backbone structure of the full protein complex (bound state) and the individual backbone structures of each chain (unbound state), together with the wild-type and mutant amino acid sequences. For the unbound state, chains are evaluated independently by the inverse folding model rather than as a joint complex, reflecting the physical assumption of minimal inter-chain interaction. The output is a scalar ΔΔG value (or ΔG for absolute binding energy), computed as a log-likelihood ratio scaled by k_B T, with the assumption that backbone geometry is unchanged between wild-type and mutant.

## Method

Boltzmann Alignment derives a closed-form expression linking ΔΔG to protein sequence likelihoods p(S|X) by combining the Boltzmann distribution with Bayes' theorem, thereby circumventing the intractable conformational distribution p(X|S). The key physical insight is explicitly including the unbound state probability p_unbnd in the thermodynamic cycle (Eq. 8), which previous inverse folding methods omitted. BA-Cycle performs unsupervised ΔΔG estimation using a pretrained ProteinMPNN, evaluating the bound state as the full complex and the unbound state as the product of independent per-chain probabilities. BA-DDG then fine-tunes ProteinMPNN with a Boltzmann loss (Eq. 12) that minimizes the ΔΔG prediction error while penalizing KL divergence from the reference model distribution, preserving pre-trained knowledge while injecting thermodynamic structure. This physics-informed alignment outperforms both SFT and DPO baselines, achieving Spearman correlations of 0.3201 (unsupervised) and 0.5134 (supervised) on SKEMPI v2.

## Results

On SKEMPI v2 (3-fold cross-validation), BA-Cycle (unsupervised) achieves a per-structure Spearman correlation of 0.3201 and overall Spearman of 0.4097, surpassing the previous unsupervised SoTA (RDE-Linear, 0.2632 per-structure Spearman). BA-DDG (supervised) achieves per-structure Spearman of 0.5134 and overall Spearman of 0.6346, outperforming the previous supervised SoTA (Surface-VQMAE, 0.4324 per-structure Spearman). BA-DDG also leads on all other metrics: overall Pearson 0.7118, RMSE 1.4516, MAE 1.0151, and AUROC 0.7726.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BA-Cycle (unsupervised) outperforms all 10 unsupervised baselines across every metric, with the largest gap over RDE-Linear (previous SoTA): per-structure Spearman 0.3201 vs. 0.2632 (+0.057) and overall Spearman 0.4097 vs. 0.3514 (+0.058). It also surpasses empirical energy functions Rosetta (0.2988) and FoldX (0.3693) on per-structure Spearman, and dominates all sequence/evolution-based methods (ESM-1v, PSSM, MSA Transformer, Tranception) by a wide margin. BA-DDG (supervised) beats all 8 supervised baselines on all 7 metrics; the closest competitor is Prompt-DDG (per-structure Spearman 0.4257, overall 0.5910), which BA-DDG exceeds by +0.088 and +0.044 respectively. The ablation in Table 2 confirms that the thermodynamic cycle (accounting for the unbound state) is the key differentiator: BA-Cycle (0.3201 per-structure Spearman) substantially outperforms raw ProteinMPNN log-likelihood (0.2741) and ESM-IF (0.2019), which ignore the unbound state. In the binding energy prediction task on SAbDab, BA-DDG achieves Spearman 0.385, outperforming DSMBind and other pre-trained methods. For rigid protein-protein docking (DIPS test set), BA-DDG as a selection method approaches 'Perfect Selection' performance, surpassing DiffDock-PP's native confidence model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ProteinMPNN as the inverse folding backbone and departs from prior inverse-folding-based ΔΔG predictors (Bennett et al., 2023; Widatalla et al., 2024; Shanker et al., 2024) by explicitly incorporating the unbound state into the thermodynamic cycle, rather than relying solely on bound-state log-likelihoods. It also draws on the LLM alignment literature (DPO, RLHF) but argues these techniques lack the physical inductive bias needed for energy-related biological tasks, motivating a Boltzmann-distribution-based loss instead. The key conceptual contribution is a Bayes' theorem derivation that provides a thermodynamic justification for the empirically observed correlation between inverse folding log-likelihoods and binding free energy, framing the alignment as a physically grounded knowledge transfer rather than an ad-hoc heuristic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The thermodynamic cycle formulation offers a principled, physics-informed alternative to supervised fine-tuning for ΔΔG prediction, reducing the risk of catastrophic forgetting while introducing a meaningful inductive bias. The demonstrated applicability beyond ΔΔG (binding energy prediction, docking selection, antibody sequence design) suggests the Boltzmann Alignment framework could generalize to other energy-scoring tasks. Key limitations remain: the method requires crystal or predicted structures as input, assumes backbone invariance upon mutation, and does not model side-chain conformations, all of which constrain practical deployment in structure-agnostic settings.
