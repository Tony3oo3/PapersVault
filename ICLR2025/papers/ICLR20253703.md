## Problem

Vision-language contrastive models like CLIP achieve strong zero-shot classification but perform poorly on dense tasks such as open-vocabulary segmentation, due to the absence of localization cues in captions and the entangled learning of visual representations and cross-modality alignment. The paper asks whether a frozen, spatially-aware self-supervised vision encoder is already 'one text encoder away' from becoming an open-vocabulary segmenter, and whether decoupling visual representation learning from concept-level alignment can close this gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253703/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes image-caption pairs (COCO Captions at 224×224 or LAION-400M) where captions are parsed into noun phrases via a POS tagger and filtered against a 574-concept bank. At inference, an RGB image is processed at 448×448 (or 896×896) with a sliding window, and pixel-wise predictions are obtained by projecting patch-level visual representations onto pre-computed text embeddings of class names (contextualized with CLIP templates), then upsampled via bilinear interpolation. Background is assigned by thresholding the maximum foreground confidence rather than by a textual class description.

## Method

SimZSS freezes a self-supervised vision transformer (e.g., DINOv2 ViT-B/14) and trains only a randomly initialized text encoder (CLIP architecture) plus a linear projection from text to visual space. The key mechanism is concept-level consistency: noun phrases extracted from captions are averaged into text concept vectors, projected into the visual space, and used to query the dense patch representations via softmax-weighted similarity pooling, yielding visual concept vectors. A cross-entropy loss is then applied using a linear classifier whose weights are the sum of text concept representations for each unique concept in the batch (a semantic-level rather than instance-level objective, since concepts repeat across samples). This local loss is combined with the standard CLIP-style global contrastive loss between [CLS] and [EOS] tokens, weighted by λ=0.05. The entire pipeline requires only 15 minutes on 8 GPUs for COCO Captions and adds less than 1% runtime overhead over LiT.

## Results

Trained on COCO Captions with a ViT-B/14 DINOv2 backbone, SimZSS achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot foreground segmentation mIoU on 7 out of 8 benchmark datasets: 90.3 on Pascal VOC, 43.1 on Pascal Context, 29.0 on COCO-Stuff, 33.0 on Cityscapes, and 21.8 on ADE20K (without background). With background included, SimZSS reaches 37.2 (Pascal Context), 43.5 (COCO-Object), and 58.4 (Pascal VOC) mIoU. On zero-shot classification, SimZSS trained on LAION-400M achieves 64.1% ImageNet-1k top-1 accuracy and 38.9% average across 38 datasets, slightly outperforming LiT (63.6% / 37.5%) while using 32× fewer training samples than CLIP (12.8B pairs). Training on COCO Captions takes less than 15 minutes on 8 GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimZSS (ViT-B, COCO Captions) outperforms LiT† by 4.2 mIoU on Pascal VOC (90.3 vs 86.1), 7.6 on Pascal Context (43.1 vs 35.5), 3.4 on COCO-Stuff (29.0 vs 25.6), 7.2 on Cityscapes (33.0 vs 25.8), and 3.7 on ADE20K (21.8 vs 18.1), confirming that the gains come from the concept-level consistency objective rather than simply freezing the vision tower. Against the strongest miscellaneous baseline CLIP-DINOiser, SimZSS leads on all five foreground datasets (e.g., +9.4 on Pascal VOC, +7.2 on Pascal Context). Compared to OVDiff (1.2B params), SimZSS achieves higher mIoU on Pascal VOC (+8.6), Pascal Context (+9.4), and ADE20K (+6.9) with far fewer parameters. On the with-background setting (Table 2), SimZSS wins on Pascal Context (37.2 vs 32.4 for CLIP-DINOiser) and COCO-Object (43.5 vs 34.8) but trails OVDiff on Pascal VOC (58.4 vs 67.1), which the authors attribute to OVDiff's per-class background prototypes versus SimZSS's simpler confidence-threshold approach. For zero-shot classification, SimZSS matches or slightly exceeds LiT (64.1% vs 63.6% ImageNet-1k on LAION-400M) while remaining well below CLIP trained on 12.8B pairs (67.0%), indicating that the local consistency objective does not degrade global alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

SimZSS directly extends LiT (Zhai et al., 2022), which established the paradigm of aligning a trainable text encoder to a frozen self-supervised vision encoder for zero-shot classification. The key departure is the addition of a concept-level consistency objective that leverages linguistic structure (POS tagging to extract noun phrases) and similarity-based pooling to enforce localized cross-modal alignment, rather than relying solely on global image-caption contrastive loss. This builds on the broader observation that self-supervised ViTs (DINOv2) possess strong spatial awareness, and positions itself against CLIP-style methods that jointly train both encoders, arguing that the vision encoder's representations are already sufficient and only the text side needs alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: SOTA zero-shot segmentation on 7/8 benchmarks with 15 minutes of training on 8 GPUs using only 600K image-caption pairs, making open-vocabulary segmentation accessible without large-scale compute. The finding that curation (COCO Captions) outperforms scale (LAION-400M) for segmentation—opposite to classification—challenges assumptions about data requirements for dense tasks. Open questions include whether the crude confidence-threshold background handling can be improved, whether the concept bank can be eliminated entirely without performance loss, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D segmentation. The paper also reveals that ViT-L/14 does not outperform ViT-B/14, suggesting diminishing returns from larger frozen backbones in this setting.
