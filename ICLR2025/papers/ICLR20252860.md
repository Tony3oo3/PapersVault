## Problem

Cross-attention-based interpretability methods like DAAM and prompt-to-prompt have been well-established for text-to-image diffusion models, but their direct application to image-to-image (I2I) models fails because the spatial continuity between reference and generated images makes token-wise separation infeasible. This leaves a significant gap in understanding how I2I latent diffusion models transfer visual information between input and output domains, hindering model debugging and performance refinement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252860/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes internal cross-attention scores (HW×HW matrices) extracted from the U-Net decoder's cross-attention layers at each diffusion time step, attention head, and layer, along with the reference and generated images (typically 512×384 or 1024×768) and optional inpainting masks. Attention scores are first column-averaged from (HW, HW) to (HW,) vectors, then reshaped to (H, W) spatial maps and thresholded at δ=0.4. The outputs are bidirectional attribution maps at five granularities (unified, time, head, layer, and specific-reference levels) plus the scalar IMACS consistency score that quantifies mask-attention alignment.

## Method

I²AM computes bidirectional attention scores by reversing the softmax direction relative to T2I methods: for R2G, the reference image embedding serves as the query and generated patches as keys, while for G2R the roles are swapped, enabling interpretable maps that DAAM's standard direction cannot produce for I2I models. These scores are aggregated via summation over any subset of three axes—diffusion time steps, attention heads, and network layers—yielding unified-level (ULAM), time-level (TLAM), head-level (HLAM), and layer-level (LLAM) attribution maps. A specific-reference attribution map (SRAM) further isolates which reference patches inform a particular generated patch by selecting the corresponding row from the raw G2R attention matrix. For inpainting tasks, the paper introduces IMACS, a metric that measures the ratio of attention mass inside versus outside the inpainting mask with a penalty factor λ=3, providing a quantitative proxy for information-transfer fidelity that correlates with FID, KID, LPIPS, and SSIM. The method is training-free for visualization but also demonstrates how SRAM-derived insights can be incorporated into auxiliary loss terms (distance-centering maximization, total variation, and center-weighted Gaussian losses) to refine model training.

## Results

On the VITON-HD virtual try-on benchmark, StableVITON achieves the best downstream metrics (FID 10.68, KID 0.0064, LPIPS 0.0817, SSIM 0.8634) and the highest proposed IMACS scores (IMACS_g 0.3083, IMACS_r 0.3388), while DCI-VTON shows the weakest alignment (IMACS_g 0.0785) and worst FID (13.10). For object detection on PBE-generated COCO images, I²AM achieves mIoU>0.5_gen of 0.2416, outperforming DAAM (0.1807), random 10–30% boxes (0.2028), and even YOLOv3 (0.1978) on generated content. The refined custom model, guided by SRAM-based debugging, improves IMACS_g from 0.0833 to 0.2215 and SSIM from 0.8396 to 0.8644 over the original custom model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the object detection experiment (Table 1), I²AM achieves mIoU>0.5_gen of 0.2416 on PBE-generated COCO images, surpassing DAAM (0.1807), the 'overall' baseline (0.1807), and random 10–30% bounding boxes (0.2028) by 33%, 33%, and 19% respectively. It also exceeds YOLOv3's 0.1978 on generated images, though supervised detectors like MaskFormer (0.2932) and Mask R-CNN (0.2706) still lead on generated content. In the VITON-HD inpainting comparison (Table 2), StableVITON dominates both downstream quality (best FID 10.68, best LPIPS 0.0817) and the proposed IMACS alignment metric (0.3083/0.3388), while DCI-VTON trails significantly on IMACS_g (0.0785) and FID (13.10), confirming that scattered attention scores correlate with degraded output. The refined custom model, improved via SRAM-guided loss functions, closes the gap with StableVITON on SSIM (0.8644 vs. 0.8634) and KID (0.0022 vs. 0.0064) but remains below on IMACS, indicating residual attention misalignment. The paper demonstrates that IMACS scores rank models consistently with traditional metrics, validating its utility as an XAI evaluation signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cross-attention interpretability paradigm established by DAAM (Tang et al., 2022) and prompt-to-prompt (Hertz et al., 2022) from text-to-image models to image-to-image latent diffusion models. The key conceptual departure is the reversal of softmax direction: where DAAM treats the generated image as Query and text tokens as Keys, I²AM treats the reference image as Query and generated patches as Keys (R2G), and vice versa (G2R), exploiting the shared spatial domain between reference and generated images. The method operates within the LDM framework (Rombach et al., 2022), aggregating cross-attention scores across time steps, heads, and layers, and introduces SRAM to resolve ambiguities inherent in the G2R direction that have no T2I analogue.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

I²AM provides the first systematic bidirectional interpretability toolkit for I2I diffusion models, filling a gap where prior XAI methods were limited to single-directional token attribution in T2I settings. The IMACS metric offers a new evaluation axis that correlates with standard quality metrics (FID, KID, LPIPS, SSIM), and the SRAM-guided debugging workflow demonstrates a practical loop for identifying and fixing layer-specific attention failures. Limitations include testing only on paired settings, reliance on models that use cross-attention with all patch embeddings (excluding CLS-only models like PBE for G2R), and preliminary (not deeply analyzed) unpaired results. Extending to colorization, style transfer, and depth estimation remains open, as does formalizing the debugging workflow into a general training procedure.
