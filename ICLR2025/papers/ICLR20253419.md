## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks on Test-Time Adaptation (TTA), such as DIA, assume access to true labels of test samples to craft perturbations, an assumption that is fundamentally incompatible with the label-free nature of TTA deployment. This paper asks whether an adversary can still generate strong attacks that degrade TTA performance on benign samples without any access to ground-truth labels, and whether existing defenses can mitigate such label-free threats.

## Input / Output

The attack takes an unlabeled test batch (e.g., 200 RGB images at 32×32 for CIFAR or 224×224 for ImageNet) in which a small fraction (e.g., 20%) is designated as compromised samples, along with white-box or grey-box access to the DNN parameters and the TTA algorithm configuration. It outputs an l∞-bounded perturbation vector (ε = 8/255) applied only to the compromised samples, generated over 100 projected gradient descent iterations with adversarial learning rate α = 2/255, such that pixel values remain in [0,1]. The perturbation is designed to maximize the error rate on the remaining benign (unperturbed) samples after the TTA update.

## Method

The Feature Collapse Attack (FCA) is grounded in a new decomposition of robust risk (Theorem 1) that separates natural risk from boundary risk in a form amenable to label-free optimization. Three loss terms are derived from Neural Collapse assumptions: a Nearest Centroid Loss (L_ncc) that pushes benign sample features away from their predicted class centroids to increase natural risk, a Feature Collapse Loss (L_col) that pulls features toward the batch global mean to exploit the Equinorm property as a regularizer against unreliable centroid estimates, and a Feature Scattering Loss (L_sc) that maximizes the activation difference before and after the TTA update to increase boundary risk, weighted by the predicted class probability. The combined loss L = L_ncc − L_col + L_sc · p(Ĉ = F_θ(x_i)) is optimized via iterative projected gradient descent with l∞ clipping, requiring no true labels at any point. Across five TTA methods (TeBN, TENT, EATA, SAR, SoTTA) and three corrupted benchmark datasets, FCA matches or exceeds the label-dependent DIA attack (e.g., >4% higher error on CIFAR-10-C) while rendering existing defenses—sharpness-aware minimization, entropy-based filtering, robust BN statistics, and MedBN—largely ineffective.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-C, FCA increases the error rate of TENT from 19.72% (no attack) to 37.95%, exceeding the DIA benchmark (33.62%) by over 4% and the label-free baselines DIA(PL) (20.67%) and TePA (20.81%) by more than 17%. On CIFAR-100-C and ImageNet-C, FCA achieves error rates within 0.5% of the DIA benchmark (e.g., 64.03% vs 64.21% on CIFAR-100-C TeBN; 51.06% vs 51.54% on ImageNet-C TeBN), despite not requiring access to true labels. Across all five TTA methods and three datasets, FCA consistently produces the strongest or near-strongest attacks, while DIA(PL) and TePA remain close to the no-attack baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FCA is compared against two prior attack methods: DIA (Wu et al., 2023), which assumes access to true test labels, and TePA (Cong et al., 2024), a test-time poisoning attack. On CIFAR-10-C, FCA substantially outperforms DIA by 4–5% error (e.g., 38.87% vs 34.11% for TeBN; 40.15% vs 36.80% for EATA), while on CIFAR-100-C and ImageNet-C it matches DIA within 0.5% (64.03% vs 64.21% and 51.06% vs 51.54% for TeBN, respectively). In stark contrast, the label-free baselines DIA(PL) and TePA are nearly ineffective, producing error rates only 1–2% above the no-attack baseline across all datasets (e.g., DIA(PL) yields 21.04% vs FCA's 38.87% on CIFAR-10-C TeBN). This demonstrates that FCA achieves the attack efficacy of the label-dependent DIA benchmark without requiring true labels, while existing label-free attacks fail to meaningfully degrade TTA performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the TTA adversarial security line initiated by DIA (Wu et al., 2023) and TePA (Cong et al., 2024) by removing the impractical assumption of access to true test labels. The attack design is grounded in Neural Collapse theory (Papyan et al., 2020), leveraging its three properties (variability collapse, equinorm, NNC simplification) to construct label-free loss functions that estimate robust risk. The theoretical foundation draws on the natural/boundary risk decomposition from Yang et al. (2023b), which the authors reformulate (Theorem 1) to expose actionable attack design directives that are invisible in the original formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals that the field's prior understanding of TTA vulnerability was built on an unrealistic threat model: when true labels are unavailable (as in any real deployment), the standard DIA attack becomes nearly ineffective, yet a well-designed label-free attack (FCA) achieves comparable or even greater damage. This reframes the security landscape for TTA and exposes that existing defenses (sharpness-aware minimization, entropy-based filtering, MedBN) are largely ineffective against the practical threat. The open question is whether any defense can simultaneously resist FCA without degrading TTA's core utility on benign distribution shifts, a trade-off the paper shows is currently unresolved.
