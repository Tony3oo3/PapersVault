## Problem

Wasserstein gradient flows on GAN discriminators offer a principled way to generate samples by transporting particles toward the target distribution, but existing approaches either rely on fixed kernel classes unsuited to high-dimensional images or require adversarial training to learn the discriminator, introducing instability and mode collapse. The paper asks whether a discriminator can be trained without adversarial training while still supporting a gradient flow that produces competitive image samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251644/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At sampling time, the method starts from a batch of random Gaussian particles (N(0, Id)) and iteratively transports them through a sequence of noise-adaptive MMD gradient flow steps, producing generated images at the target resolution (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 28×28 for MNIST, 64×64 for CELEB-A and LSUN Church). A scalable variant using a linear base kernel reduces the witness function to an inner product with precomputed feature means, enabling single-particle generation at cost independent of the number of training particles, with only O(TK) storage for T noise levels and K-dimensional features.

## Method

The discriminator is a noise-conditional MMD with learned U-Net features φ(x, t; θ) and a linear base kernel, trained by maximizing the squared MMD between clean data and data corrupted by a forward diffusion process at each noise level t ~ U[0,1], with gradient and ℓ₂ penalties—no generator or min-max game is involved. At sampling, particles follow a noise-adaptive MMD gradient flow: starting from t_max (pure noise), they take N_s gradient steps of the witness function at each noise level before stepping to t − Δt, gradually decreasing noise until t_min, where an optional high-learning-rate denoising step refines the output. A theoretical result (Proposition 3.1) shows that for Gaussian sources and targets, the optimal RBF kernel width depends on the distance between distributions, justifying the adaptive schedule and revealing a phase transition where adaptivity becomes unnecessary near the target. The linear-kernel approximation replaces the particle interaction term with a precomputed dataset feature mean, decoupling sampling cost from particle count.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, DMMD achieves FID 8.31 (IS 9.09) at 100 NFE and FID 7.74 (IS 9.12) at 250 NFE, substantially outperforming all discriminator-flow baselines (JKO-Flow: FID 23.10; DGGF-KL: FID 28.80; GS-MMD-RK: FID 55.00) and MMD-GAN (FID 13.62). On additional datasets, DMMD achieves FID 3.0 on MNIST, 8.3 on CELEB-A (64×64), and 6.1 on LSUN Church (64×64), consistently beating MMD-GAN and the discriminator-flow baseline from Franceschi et al. (2023). DMMD underperforms DDPM (FID 5.19 at 100 NFE on CIFAR-10), which the authors attribute to DDPM's architecture and forward process being specifically optimized for that objective.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMMD decisively outperforms all discriminator-flow baselines: it achieves FID 8.31 versus 23.10 for JKO-Flow (Fan et al., 2022), 28.80 for DGGF-KL (Heng et al., 2023), and 55.00 for GS-MMD-RK (Hertrich et al., 2024) on CIFAR-10, with the gap attributed to the absence of adversarial training and the use of adaptive noise-conditional discriminators. Against MMD-GAN (Binkowski et al., 2018), DMMD improves FID from 13.62 to 8.31, and the MMD-GAN-Flow variant (applying gradient flow to a converged MMD-GAN discriminator) catastrophically fails (FID 450), confirming the necessity of noise-adaptive discriminators. On CELEB-A, DMMD (FID 8.3) dramatically outperforms the adversarial discriminator-flow method of Franceschi et al. (2023) (FID 41.0), while on MNIST it achieves FID 3.0 versus 4.0. The only baseline DMMD does not beat is DDPM (Ho et al., 2020), which achieves FID 5.19 at the same 100 NFE on CIFAR-10; the authors note this is expected since the U-Net architecture and forward diffusion schedule were co-optimized for DDPM.

## Relation to Prior Work

DMMD synthesizes three existing threads: MMD gradient flow (Arbel et al., 2019) provides the particle-transport sampling mechanism; MMD GAN discriminators (Binkowski et al., 2018) supply the learned-feature kernel architecture; and the forward diffusion process (Ho et al., 2020) replaces the adversarial generator with a controlled noise curriculum. The key departure from prior discriminator-flow methods (Fan et al., 2022; Franceschi et al., 2023; Heng et al., 2023) is that all of those still require adversarial training to learn their discriminators, whereas DMMD trains a single noise-conditional discriminator on clean-vs-noisy pairs, eliminating the min-max game entirely. The paper also extends MMD gradient flow from fixed kernels to noise-adaptive kernels, with a theoretical result (Proposition 3.1) showing that adaptivity yields faster convergence and a phase transition in optimal bandwidth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of adversarial training removes a major practical barrier (instability, mode collapse, hyperparameter sensitivity) for discriminator-based generative methods, and the explicit noise-level control gives practitioners a more predictable training trajectory than GANs offer. The scalable linear-kernel approximation (Section 5) enables single-particle sampling at cost independent of particle count, which is important for deployment. However, DMMD still underperforms DDPM on every tested dataset (e.g., FID 8.31 vs. 5.19 on CIFAR-10), and the paper acknowledges open questions about scaling to ImageNet, Riemannian manifolds, and establishing convergence guarantees for the general noise-adaptive flow. The extension to f-divergences (D-KALE-Flow) works but underperforms the corresponding GAN, suggesting the MMD/IPM structure is particularly well-suited to this framework.
