## Problem

Existing temporal GNN benchmarks (Wikipedia, Reddit, Enron, etc.) contain 56–99% repeated edges, causing state-of-the-art methods to achieve high scores by memorizing historical edges rather than learning sequential dynamics. The paper demonstrates that even simple sequential patterns (e.g., 'a user who followed OpenAI and Anthropic will follow Meta AI next') are unlearnable by current temporal GNNs, which rely on aggregating historical neighbors and thus fail to generalize to unseen edges in real-world recommendation and social-network scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253175/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes a continuous-time temporal graph as a stream of timestamped edges (s_i, d_i, t_i) with no node or edge features, and for each test query (s, d, t) the model must rank the positive destination d among k=100 randomly sampled negative destinations drawn from all possible nodes (all items for bipartite datasets, all nodes for non-bipartite). Evaluation uses Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR) rather than AUROC/AP, and datasets are split chronologically 70/15/15 with cold-start nodes (degree < 3 in training) removed. The key distinction from prior benchmarks is that negative samples are drawn uniformly from the full node set rather than from historical edges, since the TGB-Seq datasets have repeat ratios of 0–25% versus 56–99% in existing benchmarks.

## Method

TGB-Seq is a benchmark of eight large-scale temporal graph datasets (1.9M–34M edges) spanning four bipartite recommendation domains (ML-20M, Taobao, Yelp, GoogleLocal) and four non-bipartite domains (Flickr, YouTube, Patent, WikiLink), all curated to minimize repeated edges so that models must learn sequential dynamics rather than edge memorization. The paper first constructs a synthetic toy example proving that both memory modules (RNN-based state updates) and aggregation modules (Transformer/MLP-mixer over one-hop neighbors) in existing temporal GNNs produce indistinguishable representations for items that appear at identical timestamps in mirrored interaction sequences, yielding ~50% AP (chance level) for all eight evaluated methods while SGNN-HN achieves 100%. Comprehensive experiments on the eight real datasets with eight temporal GNNs (JODIE, DyRep, TGAT, TGN, CAWN, TCL, GraphMixer, DyGFormer) plus SGNN-HN reveal substantial MRR degradation compared to prior benchmarks, with no single method dominating across all datasets, and significant training-cost overhead (some methods exceeding 24 hours per epoch) on the larger TGB-Seq datasets.

## Results

On the TGB-Seq recommendation datasets, all eight temporal GNNs suffer severe performance degradation compared to existing benchmarks: e.g., CAWN drops from 88.23 MRR on Wikipedia to 12.31 on ML-20M, and DyGFormer drops from 84.64 on Wikipedia to 21.68 on Yelp. The sequential recommendation method SGNN-HN consistently outperforms temporal GNNs on recommendation datasets (e.g., 68.58 MRR on Taobao vs. 60.28 for the best temporal GNN TGN). On non-bipartite TGB-Seq datasets, no single temporal GNN dominates: DyGFormer leads on Flickr (49.58), GraphMixer on YouTube (58.87), TGN on WikiLink (62.94), and JODIE on Patent (24.60). Many methods (TGAT, CAWN, DyGFormer) fail to complete one training epoch within 24 hours on larger TGB-Seq datasets, incurring prohibitive training costs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks eight temporal GNNs (JODIE, DyRep, TGAT, TGN, CAWN, TCL, GraphMixer, DyGFormer) plus EdgeBank (toy example only) against SGNN-HN, a sequential recommendation baseline. CAWN and DyGFormer, which lead on existing benchmarks (88.23 and 84.64 MRR on Wikipedia), collapse to 12–26 MRR on TGB-Seq recommendation datasets, confirming their inability to capture sequential dynamics. Memory-based methods JODIE and TGN are the most competitive temporal GNNs on TGB-Seq, with TGN reaching 60.28 MRR on Taobao and 62.94 on WikiLink, yet still trailing SGNN-HN by 8–12 points on recommendation tasks. GraphMixer achieves the best non-bipartite result on YouTube (58.87 MRR) but remains mid-pack elsewhere. TGAT and CAWN are the most computationally expensive, frequently exceeding the 24-hour training limit on larger datasets. The toy example (Table 1) shows all temporal GNNs scoring ~50% AP (random chance) on a simple sequential pattern, while SGNN-HN achieves 100%, starkly illustrating the gap in sequential dynamics capture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of temporal graph benchmarking work (TGB, BenchTeMP, Poursafaei et al. 2022) by identifying a critical blind spot: existing benchmarks contain excessive repeated edges (up to 99.77% repeat ratio in Social Evo.), allowing temporal GNNs to succeed via memorization rather than genuine sequential reasoning. It departs from prior benchmarks by curating eight datasets specifically to minimize repeated edges and by introducing a toy example that formally demonstrates the inability of both memory-based (TGN, JODIE) and aggregation-based (DyGFormer, GraphMixer, CAWN) temporal GNN architectures to capture even trivial sequential patterns. The evaluation protocol also shifts from historical-edge negative sampling to random negative sampling, aligning with the reality that future links in recommendation and social systems are typically novel rather than repeated.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that state-of-the-art temporal GNNs suffer dramatic performance degradation (e.g., CAWN drops from 88% MRR on Wikipedia to 12% on ML-20M) and often cannot complete training within 24 hours on medium-scale datasets, exposing both a capability gap and an efficiency gap that prior benchmarks masked. The finding that a sequential recommendation model (SGNN-HN) outperforms all temporal GNNs on recommendation datasets challenges the assumption that temporal graph architectures are the natural tool for these applications. Open questions include how to design architectures that capture sequential dynamics without the computational cost of high-order neighbor aggregation, and whether the efficiency-effectiveness tradeoff observed here is fundamental or an artifact of current design choices.
