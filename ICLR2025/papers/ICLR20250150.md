## Problem

Existing LLM-based knowledge graph reasoning methods (e.g., ToG, KG-Agent) suffer from a high non-retrieval rate because greedy forward search can be misdirected by initially selecting plausible but incorrect relations, and the maximum path length limits depth. The paper identifies misdirection and depth limitation as the dominant causes of retrieval failure, accounting for the majority of non-retrieval instances on benchmarks like GrailQA.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a natural language question and a knowledge graph (Freebase or Wikidata) as input, with the query entity pre-extracted from the question. It produces a single answer entity via exact match. The reasoning is bounded by a maximum path length L (default 3) and a search width N (default 3), and the LLM is queried at each step to score super-relations and decide whether to continue searching or produce the final answer.

## Method

ReKnoS introduces super-relations—groups of fine-grained relations under a shared semantic field (e.g., 'music.featured_artist' encompassing 'recordings', 'albums', etc.)—to represent multiple relation paths simultaneously during reasoning. At each of up to L steps, the framework (1) collects all candidate super-relations connected to the previous step's selection, (2) prompts the LLM to score and retain the top N candidates, and (3) extracts terminal entities to let the LLM decide whether to answer or extend the path. Because super-relations abstract over many individual relations, the number of LLM calls per question is 2L*+1 (independent of N), versus 2LN+1 for ToG, while the search space expands by ~87% on average. The framework also enables both forward reasoning (exploring future paths) and backward reasoning (revisiting alternative paths) through the summarized super-relation structure.

## Results

ReKnoS achieves an average Hits@1 accuracy gain of 2.92% over the best baseline across nine datasets. With GPT-3.5, ReKnoS reaches 81.1% on WebQSP, 71.9% on GrailQA, and 58.5% on CWQ, outperforming KG-Agent (79.2%, 68.9%, 56.1%) and ToG (76.2%, 68.7%, 57.1%). With GPT-4o-mini, the gains persist: 83.8% on WebQSP and 80.5% on GrailQA versus KG-Agent's 81.2% and 77.5%. ReKnoS also requires fewer LLM calls (7.2 vs. 15.3 for ToG on WebQSP), demonstrating both higher accuracy and greater efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReKnoS consistently outperforms the strongest LLM-based baseline KG-Agent on the core multi-hop KGQA datasets: +1.9% on WebQSP, +3.0% on GrailQA, and +2.4% on CWQ (GPT-3.5), with similar margins under GPT-4o-mini (+2.6%, +3.0%). Against ToG, the gains are larger: +4.9% on WebQSP and +3.2% on GrailQA (GPT-3.5). ReKnoS also beats StructGPT by 5.9% on WebQSP and 5.5% on GrailQA. However, ReKnoS does not uniformly dominate: KG-Agent edges out ReKnoS on T-REx (79.8 vs. 78.9) and SimpleQA (50.7 vs. 52.9 with GPT-3.5, but 67.9 vs. 67.2 with GPT-4o-mini), likely benefiting from its specialized toolbox for single-hop or slot-filling tasks. Against subgraph-based methods (SR, UniKGQA), ReKnoS leads by 6.0–7.8% on the four KGQA benchmarks. In efficiency, ReKnoS uses roughly half the LLM calls of ToG (7.2 vs. 15.3 on WebQSP) while achieving higher accuracy, and its search space is ~42–55% larger than KG-Agent and ToG respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Think-on-Graph (ToG) framework (Sun et al., ICLR 2023), which iteratively queries an LLM to select individual relations and entities for multi-hop KG reasoning. The key architectural departure is the introduction of super-relations—grouping fine-grained relations under a shared semantic field (e.g., 'music.featured_artist' encompassing 'recordings', 'albums', etc.)—to replace ToG's greedy single-relation selection with a broader, multi-path search. This positions ReKnoS alongside other LLM-based KG reasoning methods (StructGPT, KG-Agent) but distinguishes itself by explicitly targeting the non-retrieval failure mode through expanded search width and depth, rather than through tool-use or subgraph retrieval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The super-relation abstraction offers a practical, plug-and-play mechanism for improving retrieval success in LLM-based KG reasoning, with the notable efficiency benefit of keeping LLM call counts independent of search width N. However, the accuracy gains are modest (~3% average), and the approach relies on KGs with natural hierarchical relation structures (Wikidata, Freebase); for KGs lacking such structure, the paper falls back to semantic clustering, which is less well-characterized. Open questions include whether super-relations generalize to dense or dynamically evolving KGs, and whether the modest accuracy ceiling reflects a fundamental limitation of the abstraction or simply the quality of the underlying LLM scoring.
