## Problem

Sparse attention methods for long-sequence LLM prefilling rely on fixed patterns or limited offline search spaces, failing to adapt to the diverse and dynamic attention structures that vary across attention heads and input complexities. This rigidity leads to suboptimal sparsity ratios—over-computing for simple inputs while under-computing for complex ones—resulting in either unnecessary latency or degraded accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the prefill phase only, consuming the full query, key, and value matrices (each of shape L×d) along with two scalar hyperparameters: a pattern threshold τ (set to 0.1) and a cumulative attention threshold γ (0.85–0.95 depending on model). It produces a sparse attention output computed only over a dynamically selected subset of query-key pairs, with the constraint that each head computes at least 1024 tokens and retains the first and last key blocks per query block. During decoding, the system reverts to dense attention, so the sparsification applies exclusively to the computationally expensive prefill stage.

## Method

FlexPrefill introduces two adaptive components that jointly determine both the sparse pattern and the sparsity ratio per attention head at inference time. First, Query-Aware Sparse Pattern Determination computes a Jensen-Shannon divergence between a block-pooled estimated attention distribution and the true (sum-pooled) distribution over a representative query subset; if the divergence falls below τ, the head is classified as Query-Aware (diverse pattern) and uses per-query block-level index selection, otherwise it falls back to a Vertical-Slash structured pattern. Second, Cumulative-Attention Based Index Selection minimizes the number of computed query-key pairs subject to the constraint that the sum of normalized attention scores in the selected subset exceeds γ, solved by sorting candidate blocks (for Query-Aware heads) or vertical/slash lines (for Vertical-Slash heads) in descending score order and greedily selecting until the threshold is met. This dual adaptivity—choosing both the structural pattern and the computational budget per head per input—distinguishes FlexPrefill from prior methods that fix either the pattern or the ratio globally.

## Results

On the RULER benchmark, FlexPrefill (γ=0.95) achieves average scores of 89.18 (LLaMA), 89.34 (GLM), 76.32 (Yi), and 70.75 (Qwen), matching or exceeding full attention on three of four models while delivering 2.4–3.5× speedup at 128k context. On InfiniteBench, FlexPrefill preserves most full-attention performance (e.g., 47.14 vs. 48.06 for LLaMA, 44.83 vs. 41.98 for GLM) while Streaming LLM collapses to 14.72 and 27.16 respectively. At 128k tokens, FlexPrefill reduces prefill latency from 658.83 ms (full attention, LLaMA) to 271.07 ms (γ=0.95) or 185.75 ms (γ=0.9), a 2.4×–3.5× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlexPrefill decisively outperforms Streaming LLM across all four models on RULER (e.g., 89.18 vs. 61.62 for LLaMA, 70.75 vs. 52.96 for Qwen), as Streaming LLM's fixed sink+window pattern degrades severely beyond 16k tokens. Against MInference, FlexPrefill wins on LLaMA (89.18 vs. 85.42), GLM (89.34 vs. 89.18), and Qwen (70.75 vs. 66.07), with the gap widening at 128k (79.09 vs. 58.17 for LLaMA); on Yi the two are nearly tied (76.32 vs. 76.00). Compared to full attention, FlexPrefill matches or exceeds performance on LLaMA (+0.48), GLM (+0.28), and Qwen (+1.92) while sacrificing only 3.17 points on Yi, all at 2.4–3.5× lower 128k prefill latency. In the extended comparison (Table 4), FlexPrefill also surpasses MoA (84.78 vs. 84.58 avg on LLaMA-3), InfLLM (84.78 vs. 62.9), LM-Infinite (84.78 vs. 35.82), and HiP (88.38 vs. 85.4 on LLaMA-3.1), achieving the highest 128k speedup of 3.47×–3.49×.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlexPrefill extends the line of training-free sparse attention methods for LLM prefill, building directly on MInference's observation that attention exhibits dynamic sparse patterns and StreamingLLM's attention-sink insight. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) instead of using a fixed set of predefined patterns (as in MInference's offline-searched patterns or StreamingLLM's sink+window), it dynamically classifies each attention head per input as either Query-Aware or Vertical-Slash using Jensen-Shannon divergence between pooled and true attention distributions; and (2) instead of a fixed sparsity ratio, it allocates a per-head computational budget via a cumulative-attention threshold γ, grounded in a dual optimization formulation. This makes it the first method to jointly adapt both the pattern type and the sparsity ratio at the granularity of individual attention heads and individual inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: FlexPrefill achieves 2–4× prefill speedup while matching or slightly exceeding full-attention accuracy across four model families (LLaMA, GLM, Yi, Qwen) on RULER and InfiniteBench, without any retraining or per-model pattern search. This makes long-context inference substantially more accessible for deployment. Open questions include whether the approach extends to the decoding phase (where KV-cache management introduces different constraints), whether the per-head pattern determination overhead becomes prohibitive at extreme sequence lengths (>1M tokens), and whether the γ parameter can be learned or auto-calibrated rather than manually tuned per model. The method's reliance on a representative query subset (last block) for pattern determination also raises questions about robustness for highly non-stationary inputs.
