## Problem

Existing concept erasure methods for text-to-image diffusion models either require computationally expensive fine-tuning or rely on token remapping and blacklisting, making them vulnerable to adversarial jailbreaks that restore erased concepts. No prior approach simultaneously achieves training-free operation, lightweight erasure suitable for real-time deployment, and robustness against both white-box and black-box adversarial attacks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250486/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model along with two small calibration prompt sets: target concept prompts (e.g., 'a cat in Van Gogh style') and reference prompts (e.g., 'a cat'), each containing M common objects. A single forward pass per prompt at each of the first t̂ denoising timesteps (typically 10 out of 50) suffices to compute all required neuron activations. The output is a permanently modified model where approximately 0.12% of total weights (less than 3% of FFN second-layer parameters) are zeroed out, producing images that omit the target concept while preserving object identity and overall generation quality.

## Method

ConceptPrune identifies 'skilled neurons' in the second linear layer (W^{l,2}) of FFN blocks within the UNet by adapting the Wanda importance score — the element-wise product of weight magnitude and input feature L2 norm — to the diffusion setting. For each row of W^{l,2}, a weight neuron is deemed skilled if it ranks in the top-k% Wanda scores under target concept prompts AND its score exceeds the corresponding score under reference prompts, thereby disentangling concept-specific neurons from general object-generating neurons. Time-dependent binary masks are aggregated via logical OR over the first t̂ denoising timesteps (typically 10, reflecting that style and object identity form early in the denoising trajectory), and the resulting union mask is applied by zeroing the corresponding weights. The entire procedure is training-free: no backpropagation or parameter updates are needed, only a calibration forward pass and weight masking, enabling rapid multi-concept erasure by simply taking the union of skilled neuron masks across concepts.

## Results

On artist style erasure (Table 2), ConceptPrune achieves 26.9 CLIP Similarity and 94.0 CLIP Score (lower/higher is better), outperforming all lightweight baselines (best: MACE at 28.2/85.4) and training-intensive methods (best: SA at 27.1/86.9), while retaining 29.9 CLIP Similarity on COCO30k. For nudity erasure on the I2P dataset, ConceptPrune reduces explicit content by 94.1% (47/4703 prompts), surpassing ESD (88%) and UCE (85.6%). On object erasure (ImageNette), it achieves 1.7% average top-1 accuracy on erased classes while maintaining 85.9% on preserved classes. Under adversarial attacks, ConceptPrune achieves 95.6% MMA reduction, 85.2% Ring-A-Bell reduction, and 64.8% UnlearnDiffAtk ASR, substantially outperforming lightweight competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConceptPrune outperforms all lightweight baselines on artist style erasure: it beats MACE (28.2/85.4), FMN (28.4/82.4), Receler (28.4/82.0), and UCE (32.8/44.0) on both CLIP Similarity and CLIP Score, with the largest margin over UCE (94.0 vs 44.0 CLIP Score). Against training-intensive methods, it surpasses SA (27.1/86.9) and AdvUnlearn (27.2/82.0) on CLIP Score while matching on CLIP Similarity, though SA achieves a slightly lower FID (14.7 vs 16.9). On object erasure, ConceptPrune achieves the best average erased-class accuracy (1.7%) and preserved-class accuracy (85.9%), outperforming UCE (2.7%/80.2%), FMN (4.1%/77.5%), and ESD (12.5%/66.9%). Under adversarial attacks, ConceptPrune dramatically outperforms lightweight methods: MMA reduction of 95.6% vs 53.6% (FMN), 63.3% (UCE), 65.7% (Receler), and 78.4% (SPM); Ring-A-Bell of 85.2% vs 5.6% (FMN) and 67.6% (UCE). However, on white-box UnlearnDiffAtk, training-intensive methods like SA (11.3% ASR) and SH (22.3% ASR) still outperform ConceptPrune (64.8% ASR), though ConceptPrune far exceeds all lightweight baselines (UCE 93.2%, FMN 97.9%, SPM 91.6%). Against safety-tuned models SLD and SDv2, ConceptPrune achieves substantially higher adversarial robustness (MMA 95.6% vs 25.5%/26.8%; Ring-A-Bell 85.2% vs 2.8%/1.8%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Wanda pruning heuristic (Sun et al., 2024), originally designed for LLM compression, as a concept-identification mechanism in diffusion models, combined with the timestep-aggregation strategy from DiffPrune (Fang et al., 2023). It departs fundamentally from the dominant concept-erasure paradigm (ESD, UCE, FMN, CA), which relies on fine-tuning cross-attention weights or token remapping, by instead identifying 'skilled neurons' in FFN layers and permanently zeroing them out in a single forward pass. The conceptual bridge to NLP skill discovery (Wang et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2023e) — where specific neurons in transformer FFNs specialize to particular tasks — is the key insight that makes this transfer from LLM pruning to diffusion-model concept editing natural.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, permanent nature of pruning offers a practical advantage over token-remapping methods: the pruned model can be distributed without the risk of adversaries simply bypassing a prompt-level safeguard, and the ~0.12% weight reduction is negligible for deployment. However, the method still requires per-concept hyperparameter tuning (sparsity k% and t̂), and erasing many objects simultaneously risks degrading overall generation quality. Open questions include whether the skilled-neuron localization generalizes to more abstract or compositional concepts, whether the approach extends to video diffusion or autoregressive image models, and how to automate the sparsity selection without manual grid search.
