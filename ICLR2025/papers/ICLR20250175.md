## Problem

Selecting the optimal low-rank adapter rank per layer in parameter-efficient fine-tuning remains a key challenge: static ranks waste budget on unimportant layers, while existing dynamic methods (AdaLoRA, DyLoRA) rely on heuristic pruning or expensive sensitivity computations. Prior bilevel approaches like BiLoRA address this but require implicit differentiation for the hypergradient, making them computationally prohibitive at scale.

## Input / Output

The method consumes frozen pre-trained weights (DeBERTaV3-base, ResNet50, or Stable Diffusion UNet/text encoder) and a training dataset randomly split into two equal subsets—one for the upper-level loss f₁ and one for the lower-level loss f₂. It produces per-layer low-rank adapters parameterized by basis matrices B (optionally constrained to Stiefel or oblique manifolds) and a nonnegative singular-value vector s with an L1 budget constraint ‖s‖₁ ≤ τ, yielding an adapted model with dynamically reduced rank per layer. For convolutional layers, the CP tensor factorization scales parameters as O(r(2k+C+F)) rather than the O(r(F+Ck²)) of standard LoRA matrix factorization.

## Method

dEBORA formulates fine-tuning as a bilevel problem: the lower level optimizes basis matrices B on dataset D₂ for a fixed singular-value vector s, while the upper level optimizes s over a perturbed L1 simplex on dataset D₁. The key technical contribution is a closed-form, Hessian-free hypergradient approximation G(s) = ∂_s f₁* − diag[U*ᵀ ∂_U f₁*] ⊙ s⁻¹ that exploits the multilinear structure of the CP/SVD parameterization, eliminating the need to solve the implicit gradient system. The upper-level problem is solved with a stochastic away-step Frank-Wolfe algorithm, which is projection-free, provides sublinear convergence to a stationary point, and guarantees identification of the optimal sparse face (i.e., the correct active set of singular values) in finite time. Once the optimal face is identified, entries of s below a threshold ε are truncated and corresponding columns of the basis matrices are removed, adaptively reducing the effective rank per layer.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark with DeBERTaV3-base, dEBORA (τ=16) achieves competitive accuracy across all tasks using only 0.4M trainable parameters, compared to 1.27M for AdaLoRA and 1.33M for LoRA (r=8). For example, dEBORA scores 90.01 on MNLI, 95.29 on SST-2, and 91.88 on QQP accuracy, within 0.4–1.3 points of AdaLoRA while using roughly 3× fewer parameters. On CIFAR-10 with ResNet50, dEBORA (τ=8) reaches 93.74% validation accuracy with just 64K parameters, outperforming AdaLoRA (93.28%, 316K) and LoRA r=16 (89.66%, 1.17M). On Stable Diffusion fine-tuning, dEBORA (Oblique, τ=16) achieves the lowest loss (0.231) with 0.4M parameters versus 4.7M for AdaLoRA and 11.3M for LoRA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AdaLoRA (Zhang et al., 2023), dEBORA uses roughly 3× fewer parameters (0.4M vs 1.27M on GLUE; 64K vs 316K on CIFAR-10) while remaining within ~0.4 points on most GLUE tasks; on CIFAR-10, dEBORA actually outperforms AdaLoRA by 0.46% accuracy (93.74% vs 93.28%). Compared to LoRA (Hu et al., 2022), dEBORA achieves comparable or better accuracy with far fewer parameters (0.4M vs 1.33M), and on CIFAR-10 the gap is larger: 93.74% vs 89.66% for LoRA r=16. Against the Houlsby adapter (HAdapter) and Pfeiffer adapter (PA adapter), dEBORA matches or exceeds accuracy on most GLUE tasks (e.g., QQP 91.88 vs 89.27/89.40; MRPC 90.16 vs 89.95/89.46) while using less than half the parameters. On Stable Diffusion, dEBORA (Oblique) achieves the best loss (0.231) with 0.4M parameters, outperforming AdaLoRA (0.245, 4.7M) and LoRA (0.241, 11.3M) by a wide margin in both loss and parameter count.

## Relation to Prior Work

dEBORA extends the bilevel optimization framework for low-rank adaptation introduced by BiLoRA (Qiang et al., 2024) by replacing its implicit-differentiation-based hypergradient with a closed-form, Hessian-free approximation derived from the multilinear CP/SVD parameterization structure. It also departs from AdaLoRA's SVD-based iterative singular-value pruning by casting rank selection as a constrained optimization over a perturbed L1 simplex, solved via a stochastic away-step Frank-Wolfe algorithm that provides finite-step sparsity (identifiability) guarantees. The method further generalizes the matrix-only LoRA setting to higher-order tensor layers via CP decomposition, yielding a more parameter-efficient factorization for convolutional layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Hessian-free hypergradient approximation (with error O(Kβ)) and the projection-free Frank-Wolfe solver together make bilevel rank adaptation computationally tractable at scale, removing the main bottleneck that limited BiLoRA's practicality. The tensor (CP) extension for conv layers reduces parameter scaling from O(r(F+Ck²)) to O(r(2k+C+F)), which is practically significant for vision models. Open questions include: how the bilevel data-split requirement (D1/D2) affects performance when data is scarce, whether the Kβ approximation error bound holds reliably in deep networks with large condition numbers, and whether the constrained (Stiefel/Oblique) variants can be made as effective as the unconstrained one. The per-iteration cost remains T_max times that of AdaLoRA, so the method's advantage is most pronounced when the final rank is much smaller than the initial rank.
