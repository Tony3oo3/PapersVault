## Problem

Many PDEs admit non-compact Lie point symmetry groups (e.g., SL(2,ℝ)⋉H(1,ℝ) for the heat equation) that do not act via linear representations on the underlying jet space, rendering most existing equivariant neural network architectures inapplicable. Furthermore, pre-trained foundation models for PDE solving and image classification are typically not equivariant, and no practical method existed to retrofit equivariance onto such models without full retraining, especially when only infinitesimal generators (not full group parameterizations) are available.

## Input / Output

For PDE tasks, the method takes an initial condition u₀ on a spatial domain Ω, a target evaluation point (x_f, t_f), and a set of Lie algebra generators defining the symmetry group; it outputs the PDE solution u(t,x) at the target point. For image classification, it takes a potentially affine- or homography-transformed grayscale image and outputs a digit class label. In both settings, the input is first canonicalized via Lie group descent (minimizing an energy over the orbit) to produce a canonical representative g⁻¹·x, which is then fed to the frozen pre-trained model. The energy function is constructed from a VAE's ELBO plus an adversarial regularizer trained to distinguish real data from group-transformed samples, ensuring level sets are transverse to orbits.

## Method

LieLAC (Lie aLgebrA Canonicalization) achieves equivariance by transforming inputs to a canonical form before inference, requiring only the action of Lie algebra basis vectors rather than a full group parameterization. Theoretically, the paper introduces weighted closed canonicalizations—probability measures supported on orbit closures—which resolve the non-existence of minima on non-closed orbits of non-compact groups, and establishes their equivalence to weakly-equivariant weighted frames. Practically, canonicalization is performed via Lie group descent: gradient descent on the Lie algebra with a retraction map (Algorithm 1) when a global parameterization exists (e.g., solvable algebras decomposed as trains of exponentials), or coordinate descent over individual generator actions when only infinitesimal flows are available. The energy combines a VAE's ELBO (approximating negative log-likelihood of the training distribution) with an adversarial regularizer whose level sets are transverse to group orbits, ensuring unique and well-behaved minimizers. This plug-and-play approach makes any pre-trained model equivariant without architectural modification, as demonstrated on CNNs for homography/affine-invariant MNIST classification and on POSEIDON/DeepONet for Lie point symmetry equivariant PDE evolution.

## Results

On the Allen-Cahn equation (primary PDE task), LieLAC applied to the pre-trained POSEIDON foundation model reduces out-of-distribution error from 75.76×10⁻⁴ to 29.19×10⁻⁴ without any finetuning, and to 11.34×10⁻⁴ with light finetuning on only 100 trajectories. On invariant image classification, LieLAC [CNN] achieves 97.2% accuracy on affNIST and 96.0% on homNIST, substantially outperforming the plain CNN (62.9% and 64.4% respectively). The method requires only knowledge of Lie algebra generators, not the full group structure, and operates as a drop-in preprocessing step for any pre-trained model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MacDonald et al. (2022) equivariant convolutions (affConv, homConv), LieLAC [CNN] wins decisively on both perturbed datasets: 97.2% vs 94.3% on affNIST and 96.0% vs 92.7% on homNIST, while maintaining near-parity on clean MNIST (97.9% vs 98.2%/98.0%). Against the unmodified POSEIDON foundation model, LieLAC trades a modest increase in in-distribution error (16.69 vs 6.93) for a dramatic 2.6× reduction in out-of-distribution error (29.19 vs 75.76), yielding a 45% lower average error without any finetuning. Against the data-augmentation baseline of Brandstetter et al. (2022), LieLAC [POSEIDON+ft] achieves 10.79 average error versus 8.46 for data augmentation, a gap of ~2.3×10⁻⁴; however, the data-augmentation approach requires retraining on the full augmented dataset, whereas LieLAC finetuning uses only 100 trajectories. The paper notes that the remaining gap to data augmentation stems from the SE(2) orbits being under-sampled in the original training data, and that with sufficient orbit coverage (as in the MNIST experiments), finetuning becomes unnecessary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the energy-based canonicalization framework of Kaba et al. (2023) and the weighted frame theory of Dym et al. (2024) to handle non-compact Lie groups, introducing weighted closed canonicalizations as the natural generalization when orbits are non-closed. It departs from standard equivariant architectures (Cohen & Welling 2016; MacDonald et al. 2022) that require full group parameterization and compactness, instead operating solely on Lie algebra generators via descent schemes. The approach also subsumes and unifies the frame averaging and canonicalization perspectives from Ma et al. (2024) and Puny et al. (2022) under a single theoretical diagram, while connecting to the PDE symmetry augmentation literature (Brandstetter et al. 2022; Akhound-Sadegh et al. 2023) by offering an architecture-level alternative to data/loss augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Many physically relevant PDEs (heat, Burgers, Allen-Cahn) admit non-compact symmetry groups like SL(2,R) ⋊ H(1,R) that are incompatible with all existing equivariant architectures, and pre-trained foundation models like POSEIDON are not equivariant by design. LieLAC provides a model-agnostic, plug-in solution requiring only Lie algebra information, opening the door to making arbitrary pre-trained models symmetry-equivariant without retraining from scratch. Key limitations include a 5–30× inference slowdown from non-convex Lie group optimization, difficulty with hard-coded boundary conditions in existing operators, and the open question of extending to infinite-dimensional or local (gauge) symmetry groups. The finding that fine-tuning on as few as 100 canonicalized trajectories can match data-augmentation baselines suggests a practical path for low-data scientific ML settings.
