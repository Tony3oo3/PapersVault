## Problem

Mamba models, despite their sub-quadratic complexity and theoretical capacity for long sequences, suffer severe performance degradation when evaluated on contexts longer than their pre-training length. The paper identifies the root cause as out-of-distribution accumulation of discretization steps (Δt) across tokens, which causes excessive information decay in later layers. Existing remedies like DeciMamba require memory-intensive full fine-tuning of all model parameters, limiting scalability to larger models and resource-constrained settings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen pre-trained Mamba or Mamba2 checkpoint and a small calibration set (20 samples for PPL evaluation, 10 samples per LongBench task, or 4k-context passkey retrieval samples). It outputs a vector of per-layer scaling factors S = [s₁, …, s_L] applied multiplicatively to each layer's Δt tensor at inference time. The granularity is task-dependent: a single scalar per layer (m=1) for perplexity and LongBench tasks, or one factor per channel (m=D) for passkey retrieval, yielding L or L×D learnable parameters respectively—orders of magnitude fewer than the full model.

## Method

MambaExtend introduces a learnable scaling factor s_i for each layer's discretization step (Δt_i' = s_i · Δt_i) while keeping all model weights frozen, then calibrates these factors via a dedicated calibration function (CF) on a small sample set. Two CF variants are provided: CFBP uses standard backpropagation with Adam to update only the scaling factors, and CFZO employs two-sided SPSA zeroth-order optimization that approximates gradients from just two forward passes per iteration (positive and negative Rademacher perturbations), eliminating backward-pass memory overhead entirely. The key insight driving the design is that the accumulated sum ΣΔt grows with context length, and since all entries of A are negative, exp(−ΣΔt) acts as a decay gate on earlier tokens; layer-specific down-scaling of Δt counteracts this OOD accumulation without uniform scaling, which the authors show is suboptimal. For PPL and LongBench tasks, CFZO with m=1 per layer achieves near-identical results to CFBP while requiring up to ~5.42×10⁶ fewer parameter updates and 3.87× lower peak memory compared to DeciMamba's full fine-tuning.

## Results

On the Pile dataset, MambaExtend achieves a training-free context extension of up to 32× (2k→64k tokens) with minimal perplexity degradation: Mamba-130M drops from PPL 46,592 (pre-trained at 64k) to 5.72, a ~8,145× improvement; Mamba-1.4B improves from 6,304 to 6.93; and Mamba2-780M from 378 to 5.00. On PG-19 at 70k context, MambaExtend-130M yields PPL 30.62 versus 995,328 for the unmodified baseline (~32,506× improvement). On LongBench, MambaExtend-1.4B improves average accuracy from 12.88 to 18.91 (+6.03 points), and MambaExtend2-780M from 10.89 to 16.86 (+5.97 points).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the pre-trained Mamba and Mamba2 baselines, MambaExtend dramatically reduces perplexity at extended contexts: at 64k on Pile, Mamba-130M improves from 46,592 to 5.72 PPL and Mamba2-780M from 378 to 5.00, while at the original 2k context the scores remain essentially unchanged. Compared to DeciMamba (the primary fine-tuning-based competitor), MambaExtend achieves up to 40.6% lower PPL on PG-19 and 5.72 vs. 10.62 PPL at 64k on Pile, while requiring ~5.42×10⁶ fewer parameter updates and 3.87× lower peak memory; on LongBench, MambaExtend-1.4B also edges out DeciMamba-1.4B on HotpotQA (14.29 vs. 13.88) and Qasper (16.67 vs. 14.24). Against the transformer-based TinyLLaMA-1.1B with positional interpolation (PI), MambaExtend-130M is substantially better at long contexts: 5.72 vs. 229.46 PPL at 64k on Pile, and 25 vs. 375.49 at 64k on PG-19, despite being a smaller model. On passkey retrieval, MambaExtend-130M (91.4% with per-channel scaling) matches or exceeds both full fine-tuning and DeciMamba (93.1% with more epochs) while calibrating ~3,500× fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

MambaExtend draws a direct conceptual parallel between positional interpolation (PI) for transformers and rescaling of the discretization step Δt in Mamba's S6 layer, identifying accumulated Δt as the OOD bottleneck analogous to out-of-range positional indices. It departs sharply from DeciMamba (Ben-Kish et al., 2024), which achieves context extension via memory-intensive full fine-tuning, by freezing all model weights and calibrating only L (or L×D) per-layer scaling factors. The calibration procedure borrows zeroth-order optimization (two-sided SPSA) from the derivative-free optimization literature, enabling forward-pass-only updates that further reduce memory overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 32× context extension (2k→64k) with minimal PPL degradation, using ~5.42×10⁶ fewer parameter updates and 3.87× lower peak memory than fine-tuning baselines, makes long-context Mamba deployment feasible on resource-constrained hardware. The clean diagnostic that accumulated Δt governs the effective receptive field provides a mechanistic understanding that could inform future SSM design. Open questions include the theoretical characterization of why per-layer (rather than uniform) scaling is necessary, whether the approach generalizes to other SSM variants beyond Mamba/Mamba2, and whether the ZO calibration can be made to converge reliably for high-dimensional parameter settings (e.g., per-channel scaling).
