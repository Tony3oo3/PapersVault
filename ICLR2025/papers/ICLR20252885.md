## Problem

Transformers fail to length-generalize on arithmetic tasks, and two sub-problems remain unsolved: (1) no prior work has demonstrated generalization over the number of operands in addition (e.g., training on ≤10 operands and extending to 30), and (2) no prior work has achieved simultaneous length generalization for both operands in multiplication. These tasks are considered notoriously difficult because the number of tokens the model must attend to grows with operand count or length.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252885/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a token sequence where each digit is a single token, formatted as '[query]=[response]' (e.g., '057+048+096=000→750→501→102'). The response portion includes a scratchpad of intermediate results (cumulative sums for addition, partial products and shifted sums for multiplication) that the model must generate token-by-token via next-token prediction with greedy decoding. All numbers in the scratchpad are reversed (least-significant-digit first) and zero-padded to a uniform length determined by the maximum possible answer length.

## Method

The paper combines task-specific scratchpads with multi-level Position Coupling to reduce the attention burden to a constant number of tokens per prediction step. For multi-operand addition, a scratchpad stores intermediate cumulative sums so the model only attends to two tokens (the current operand digit and the previous cumulative sum digit) rather than all m operands; bi-level Position Coupling assigns level-1 IDs coupling digits of the same significance and level-2 IDs distinguishing operand numbers. For multiplication, a two-stage scratchpad decomposes the problem into M-digit×1-digit multiplications followed by a shifted multi-operand addition, with tri-level Position Coupling encoding digit significance, operand identity, and the left-shift in stage 2. Theoretically, the authors prove a 1-layer 4-head Transformer with this construction solves multi-operand addition for operands of length and count exponential in the embedding dimension d = O(log(m+1) + log(n+1)).

## Results

On multi-operand addition (trained on S_A(10,10)), the proposed bi-level Position Coupling + scratchpad achieves ≥90% exact-match accuracy for up to 30 operands of 30 digits each, a 3× extension in both dimensions. On integer multiplication (trained on S_M(10,10)), tri-level Position Coupling + scratchpad achieves ≥78.55% exact-match accuracy for 20-digit × 15-digit products, a 2× extension in both operand lengths. On the parity task, the combination achieves near-perfect accuracy on 100-bit queries versus ~35 bits for NoPE/FIRE with scratchpad. Theoretically, a 1-layer 4-head Transformer with the proposed method solves addition with operands and operand counts exponential in embedding dimension.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (multi-level Position Coupling + scratchpad) decisively outperforms all baselines on every task. On multi-operand addition, NoPE and FIRE with scratchpad barely solve 13 operands of 12 digits, while the proposed bi-level PC + scratchpad extends to 30×30 with ≥90% accuracy — a 2.3× improvement in both dimensions. On multiplication, NoPE/FIRE + scratchpad reach only ~12×12 digits, whereas tri-level PC + scratchpad generalizes to 20×15 digits (≥78.55%), a 1.7–2× extension. On the parity task, NoPE and FIRE with scratchpad generalize to ~35 bits, while PC + scratchpad reaches ~100 bits with near-perfect accuracy. Single-level Position Coupling without scratchpad (the prior state-of-the-art from Cho et al., 2024) fails to generalize beyond the training distribution on all three tasks, confirming that the scratchpad is essential for handling variable operand counts and both-operand length variation in multiplication.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two independently developed techniques—scratchpads (Nye et al., 2021) and Position Coupling (Cho et al., 2024; McLeish et al., 2024)—in a complementary way that neither prior work achieved. It extends single-level Position Coupling to bi-level (addition) and tri-level (multiplication) versions, and designs task-specific scratchpad formats that reduce each next-token prediction to attending to a constant number of tokens. The theoretical construction extends Theorem 5.1 of Cho et al. (2024), which handled only a fixed number of operands, to variable operand counts and exponentially long operands.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to achieve simultaneous length generalization in both operand length and operand count for addition, and in both operand lengths for multiplication—two tasks previously considered intractable for Transformers. The theoretical result (a 1-layer 4-head Transformer suffices) provides a clean explanation for why the approach works and is validated by attention-pattern visualizations. The main limitation is that the method requires well-defined task structure that can be encoded by scratchpads and position coupling, leaving open the question of how to apply these ideas to tasks with implicit or unknown structure.
