## Problem

Existing explainability methods for vision Transformers either attribute importance to input regions (saliency maps, Grad-CAM), analyze individual neurons in isolation (knowledge attribution), or rely on attention visualizations, but none capture the joint, layer-by-layer transmission of critical information through a connected path of neurons. This gap means that the coherent flow of visual information from input to output across all Transformer layers remains unexplained, and individual-neuron attribution can be dominated by a few abnormally prominent neurons while ignoring the collective role of intermediate layers.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained vision Transformer (ViT-B-16, ViT-B-32, ViT-L-32, or MAE-B-16, all fine-tuned on ImageNet-1k) and a single input image with its ground-truth label. It produces a neuron path: exactly one neuron index per Transformer layer (drawn from the 3072 or 4096 activations of the first linear layer in each FFN block), forming a sequence of length L from the first to the last layer. The path is accompanied by a Joint Attribution Score quantifying the collective contribution of the selected neurons to the model's classification output.

## Method

The paper introduces a Joint Attribution Score (JAS) that extends Integrated Gradients from a single neuron to a set of neurons spanning multiple layers: it integrates the partial derivative of the model output with respect to each selected neuron, weighted by that neuron's original activation, along a linear interpolation path from zero to full effect (α ∈ [0,1]), using Riemann approximation with m=20 steps. To find the path that maximizes JAS, a layer-progressive greedy algorithm iterates from layer 1 to layer L, and at each layer selects the single neuron whose addition to the partially constructed path yields the highest JAS, thereby ensuring that each layer contributes at least one neuron critical to the information flow. The analysis is restricted to FFN neurons (first linear layer activations), motivated by prior work showing FFNs function as key-value memories encoding high-level visual concepts. This joint, path-level formulation differs from prior single-neuron attribution (Dai et al., 2022) and from Influence Pattern (Lu et al., 2021), which computes a product of per-layer gradients rather than a sum of joint partial derivatives, and from simple max-activation tracing, which ignores cross-layer interactions entirely.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k validation, the proposed Neuron Path method achieves Joint Attribution Scores of 0.4078 (ViT-B-16), 0.6610 (ViT-B-32), and 1.0086 (ViT-L-32), substantially exceeding the Influence Pattern baseline (0.0412–0.1227) and the Activation baseline (near-zero or negative). Manipulating the discovered neurons causes a removal accuracy deviation of −2.40% to −26.50% and an enhancement accuracy deviation of +2.04% to +7.28% across the four target models, confirming that the identified neuron paths carry the most critical information flow. In a pruning experiment, retaining only the top-5 JAS-selected neurons per layer while zeroing 100% of remaining neurons preserves 67.7% accuracy, far outperforming the ViT-Slim baseline at 9.96%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines. Against the Activation method (Dhamdhere et al., 2019), which simply selects the max-activation neuron per layer, Neuron Path wins decisively on all three metrics: JAS is roughly 10–100× larger, removal causes 5–10× greater accuracy drops, and enhancement yields 4–7× greater accuracy gains. Against Influence Pattern (Lu et al., 2021), which traces the largest integrated gradient layer-by-layer, Neuron Path again dominates: JAS is approximately 10× higher (e.g., 1.0086 vs. 0.1227 on ViT-L-32), removal deviation is 2–5× more negative, and enhancement deviation is 2–4× more positive. In the pruning application, JAS-Prune retains 67.7% accuracy after zeroing 100% of non-selected neurons, while the ViT-Slim baseline (Chavan et al., 2022) drops to only 9.96% under the same protocol, demonstrating that the joint cross-layer neuron selection captures far more essential model capacity than module-level weight pruning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of neuron-level explainability in vision Transformers by moving from individual neuron attribution (Knowledge Attribution, Dai et al. 2022) and single-neuron integrated gradients (Sundararajan et al. 2017) to a joint, cross-layer formulation. The Joint Attribution Score (JAS) is a direct multi-neuron generalization of the single-neuron integrated gradient, and the layer-progressive locating algorithm builds on the greedy path-search idea from Influence Patterns (Lu et al., 2021) but replaces the per-neuron gradient product with a collective influence measure. The focus on FFN neurons is motivated by the key-value memory interpretation of Transformer FFNs (Geva et al., 2021), and the method deliberately departs from input-attribution and visualization-based approaches by targeting the model's internal information flow rather than input saliency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, quantitative tool for identifying the sparse set of neurons that carry critical information through a ViT, with immediate practical implications for model pruning (retaining only 5 neurons per layer preserves most accuracy while zeroing out the rest). The class-level clustering and semantic-similarity findings offer a new lens on how ViTs organize visual concepts internally, complementing attention-map visualizations. Key limitations include the restriction to FFN neurons (ignoring attention modules), evaluation on a single dataset (ImageNet-1k validation), and a pruning comparison against ViT-Slim that is not fully apples-to-apples. Extending the analysis to attention layers, generative or segmentation tasks, and larger-scale validation remain open.
