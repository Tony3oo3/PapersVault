## Problem

Existing interactive world models for robot manipulation either use language as the action representation (requiring task-specific labeling or extra datasets) or use low-level robot actions (which cannot describe subtle, diverse movements across objects and robots). There is no scalable, general-purpose action representation that enables model-based planning across diverse manipulation tasks while being extractable from pure video data without manual annotation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250793/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial RGB image (128×128×3 or 96×128×3), a 16-frame observation history, and a natural-language goal instruction. It produces a long-horizon plan consisting of per-timestep image flows (delta scale and 2D direction for Nq uniformly sampled grid query points over 16 future steps), corresponding generated video frames (≥200 frames total), and a 16-step robot action chunk (7-dim: 6-DOF pose + gripper) executed in a receding-horizon fashion with replanning every 8 steps. The world model itself is trained exclusively on language-annotated video with no action labels.

## Method

FLIP decomposes the world model into three modules: (1) a Conditional VAE with transformer architecture that generates flow actions by predicting per-point delta scale and direction (rather than absolute coordinates) conditioned on image history, query points, and Llama 3.1 language embeddings; (2) a DiT-based latent video diffusion model with a mixed conditioning mechanism—cross-attention for flow tokens, direct concatenation for clean image history, and AdaLN-Zero for language/timestep—that generates short-horizon future frames conditioned on the proposed flow; (3) a fine-tuned LIV value module that computes γ-weighted cosine similarity between frame embeddings and the language goal, with a key modification of treating short video clips (not individual frames) as states in time-contrastive learning to produce smooth value curves on long-horizon imperfect videos. Planning proceeds via hill-climbing beam search: at each step, A flow candidates are generated per beam, corresponding videos are synthesized, the highest-value video is selected, and the lowest-value beam is periodically replaced by the best to prevent exploitation of irregular states.

## Results

On model-based planning (Table 1), FLIP achieves 100% success on LIBERO-LONG, 86% on FMB-S, 78% on FMB-M, 100% on cloth folding, and 90% on cloth unfolding, substantially outperforming UniPi (2%, 0%, 0%, 20%, 10%) and its own no-value ablation FLIP-NV (78%, 52%, 40%, 100%, 70%). On long-horizon video generation (Table 2), FLIP achieves FVD of 35.62 on LIBERO-LONG, 43.71 on FMB, and 36.15 on Bridge-V2, compared to IRASim (206.28, 172.45, 138.97) and LVDM (610.98, 358.22, 153.41), with PSNR gains of ~14 dB over LVDM. On real-world tasks (Table 8), FLIP achieves 55% on tea scooping and 50% on cloth unfolding with only 40–50 demonstrations, versus 15%/0% for Diffusion Policy and 25%/0% for ATM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLIP dominates UniPi (Du et al., 2024) across all five planning benchmarks, with the largest gap on FMB-S (86% vs. 0%) and FMB-M (78% vs. 0%), confirming that direct text-to-video synthesis cannot handle long-horizon manipulation. Against IRASim (Zhu et al., 2024) on video generation, FLIP reduces FVD by 4–8× (e.g., 35.62 vs. 206.28 on LIBERO-LONG) and improves PSNR by ~14 dB, showing that flow-conditioned planning-based generation far outperforms autoregressive trajectory-conditioned generation. Against LVDM (He et al., 2022b), FLIP achieves 5–17× lower FVD and ~15 dB higher PSNR, demonstrating the advantage of structured flow guidance over pure text conditioning. On the action module, FLIP's CVAE with delta-scale/direction prediction reduces ADE from 19.6 (ATM, Wen et al., 2023) to 12.7 on LIBERO-10 and from 18.4 to 11.9 on Bridge-V2, while improving LTDR by ~23 and ~14 percentage points respectively. For low-level policy, FLIP's flow+video-guided policy outperforms ATM-DP and OpenVLA (Kim et al., 2024) on LIBERO-LONG, with the flow+video variant (Ours-FV) achieving the best average success rate and lowest variance, while video-only guidance shows high variance due to sensitivity to trajectory deviation.

## Relation to Prior Work

FLIP sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) interactive video-generation world models for manipulation (UniPi, IRASim, Genie), which typically use language or low-level robot actions as the conditioning signal; (2) flow-based manipulation policies (ATM, Track2Act, Flow-as-interface), which use point trajectories as a dense action representation but do not perform generative planning; and (3) vision-language value functions (LIV, VIP) for goal-conditioned reward. The paper's key architectural departure is using image flow as the *action* representation in a model-based planning loop, combining a CVAE flow generator, a DiT-based flow-conditioned video generator with a mixed cross-attention/AdaLN-Zero conditioning scheme, and a clip-level time-contrastive value function into a unified hill-climbing planner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FLIP demonstrates that a purely video-and-language-trained world model can perform interactive, goal-conditioned planning across diverse robots, objects, and tasks without task-specific action labeling, and that the generated flow/video plans can bootstrap low-level policy learning from very few demonstrations (40–50). The main limitations are the slow planning speed (constrained to quasi-static tasks) and the absence of 3D/physical grounding, which the authors flag as the primary directions for future work. An open question is whether the flow-centric action space can be extended to 3D point clouds or physics-aware representations to handle dynamic and contact-rich manipulation at scale.
