## Problem

The Forward-Forward (FF) algorithm offers biologically plausible, fully parallel local updates but is confined to shallow networks because its squared-goodness design suffers from feature scaling inconsistencies and deactivated neurons that prevent deeper layers from learning new features. This paper addresses how to redesign the goodness and feature-extraction mechanism so that FF can effectively train 17-layer CNNs without backpropagation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251812/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 32×32 images (RGB for CIFAR-10/100, grayscale for MNIST/F-MNIST) in a single forward pass with no backward pass. Unusually, every convolutional layer independently produces a G-dimensional classification score via channel-wise convolution, and the final prediction is a sum of scores from a selected contiguous range of layers (determined by the SIP module on a held-out validation split), rather than a single final-layer output.

## Method

DeeperForward replaces FF's squared goodness with mean goodness (sum of ReLU outputs) and replaces vector-length normalization with layer normalization, so that the output features have zero mean and unit variance while the mean itself serves as the goodness score that is decoupled from the features. In CNNs, this is implemented as channel-wise convolution (CW-Conv) where outputs are grouped by class, each group's mean yields a per-class score, and group normalization extracts features—effectively performing local classification at every layer. Parameter-free residual connections (addition or concatenation with average-pooled shortcuts) integrate multi-level features, and a Signal Integrating and Pruning (SIP) module selects the optimal layer range to sum for the final prediction. Training uses a local cross-entropy loss at each layer with standard optimizers (Adam), requiring no intermediate-state storage, which enables a pipeline-based model-parallel strategy across GPUs and a memory-saving layer-by-layer update scheme.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, DeeperForward with a 17-layer ResNet achieves 86.22±0.17% accuracy, an 8.11% improvement over the best prior FF-based method (CwComp at 4 layers, 78.11%) and a 2.71% gain over Trifecta (12 layers, 83.51%). On MNIST and Fashion-MNIST, the same model reaches 99.63±0.04% and 93.13±0.13% respectively. On the more challenging CIFAR-100, the channel-tripled variant (ResNet-CHx3-ours) achieves 60.28±1.02%, surpassing BP-trained ResNet (58.01±0.48%), while the standard-width ResNet-ours (53.09%) trails BP, indicating sensitivity to per-class channel allocation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeeperForward (ResNet-ours, 17 layers) outperforms all pure FF-based baselines on CIFAR-10: it beats CwComp at 4 layers (78.11%) by 8.11 points, CwComp extended to 14 layers (75.28%) by 10.94 points, Trifecta at 12 layers (83.51%) by 2.71 points, and the original FF (59.00%), SymBa (59.09%), and CaFo (67.43%) by wide margins. On MNIST and Fashion-MNIST, ResNet-ours also leads all FF-based methods (99.63% and 93.13% respectively). However, it trails BP-trained ResNet18 (94.03% with data augmentation) by ~7.8 points on CIFAR-10, and the gap narrows but persists even without augmentation. Among non-FF brain-inspired methods, DTP (89.38%) and recLRA (93.58%) still exceed DeeperForward on CIFAR-10, while block-wise BP methods (BWBPF at 95.52%) remain stronger. On CIFAR-100, the channel-tripled variant (60.28%) surpasses BP ResNet (58.01%), but the standard-width model (53.09%) underperforms BP, revealing a sensitivity to per-class channel allocation in multi-class settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Hinton's Forward-Forward (FF) algorithm and CwComp's channel-wise convolution structure by replacing squared goodness with mean goodness and vector-length normalization with layer normalization, addressing feature scaling and deactivated neuron issues that confined prior FF work to shallow (≤4-layer) networks. Conceptually, it departs from the FF lineage by exploiting layer normalization's zero-mean property to decouple goodness from features, and from CwComp by avoiding batch normalization's goodness leakage. The model parallel and memory-saving strategies are novel contributions that leverage the local-update property of FF, which prior FF implementations did not exploit for multi-GPU training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DeeperForward demonstrates that FF-based training can reach 17-layer CNNs with competitive (though still inferior to BP) accuracy, keeping the bio-plausible, parallelizable training paradigm alive for deeper architectures. However, the method still lags BP by ~8% on CIFAR-10 without augmentation and shows no improvement beyond 17 layers (ResNet33 and ResNet100 underperform), suggesting a fundamental ceiling for greedy layer-wise learning without direct representation learning. Open questions include whether the gap to BP can be closed with data augmentation, whether the approach scales to ImageNet-scale datasets, and whether the deactivated-neuron sparsity in deep layers can be mitigated to enable truly deep FF networks.
