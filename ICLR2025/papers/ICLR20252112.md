## Problem

While large models trained on vast offline datasets achieve remarkable generalization in text and image domains, training general agents for sequential decision-making remains an open challenge. Most RL environments represent narrow, homogeneous sets of scenarios, limiting the generalization ability of trained agents. This paper addresses this by asking whether large-scale pre-training on a procedurally generated, open-ended space of physics-based control tasks can produce a general RL agent that transfers to unseen environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252112/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a symbolic, permutation-invariant observation: a set of entity vectors (polygons, circles, joints, thrusters) each encoding physical properties such as position, velocity, rotation, mass, friction, restitution, and role (green/blue/red). This makes the environment fully observable, eliminating the need for recurrent memory. The agent outputs multi-discrete actions (off/forward/backward for motors, off/on for thrusters) or continuous actions in [-1,1] and [0,1] respectively. The reward is a fixed sparse signal (+1 for green-blue collision, -1 for green-red collision) augmented with a dense auxiliary term proportional to the reduction in green-blue distance per timestep.

## Method

The core contribution is Jax2D, a deterministic impulse-based 2D rigid-body physics engine written entirely in JAX whose scenes are dynamically specified, meaning the same computation graph executes for every environment regardless of morphology. This enables JAX vmap parallelization across thousands of heterogeneous tasks on a single GPU, achieving 30× speedup over Box2D in an RL training loop. On top of Jax2D, Kinetix provides a random level generator (with rejection sampling to filter trivial levels), 74 hand-designed holdout levels, and a size taxonomy (S/M/L). The agent uses a gated transformer with multi-headed attention employing four distinct masks (fully-connected, joint-adjacent, n-step-connected, and currently-colliding) to process the entity set in a permutation-invariant manner, followed by message-passing layers for joints and thrusters. Training uses PPO with SFL (a learnability-based UED algorithm) over 5 billion environment interactions on procedurally generated levels, and the resulting general agent is evaluated zero-shot on hand-designed environments and fine-tuned on difficult tasks to demonstrate sample-efficiency gains and new capabilities.

## Results

The general agent trained on 5 billion random Kinetix timesteps achieves increasing zero-shot solve rates on hand-designed holdout levels across all three environment sizes (S, M, L), with the strongest results on S where most holdout levels are solved. In fine-tuning experiments on the L holdout set, the pre-trained general agent drastically outperforms tabula rasa PPO on 3 of 4 selected environments, including MuJoCo-Hopper-Hard and MuJoCo-Walker-Hard where the tabula rasa agent fails entirely. On the deceptive Car-Ramp task, tabula rasa PPO at 1B timesteps reaches the goal 0% of the time, while the general agent solves it zero-shot ~5% of the time and becomes reliable after minimal fine-tuning. Jax2D achieves 824K steps/sec in an RL loop (30× faster than Box2D's 24K) and 9,049K steps/sec engine-only (4.5× faster than Box2D's 1,982K).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Box2D, Jax2D wins decisively on speed: 4.5× faster in raw engine benchmarks (9,049K vs 1,982K SPS) and over 30× faster inside an RL training pipeline (824K vs 24K SPS), with the gap widening as parallel environment count exceeds physical CPU cores. Against the UED baselines PLR (Jiang et al., 2021) and ACCEL (Parker-Holder et al., 2022), the paper reports that neither method provided improvements over simple domain randomization (DR) in preliminary experiments, so SFL (Rutherford et al., 2024) was used as the level-selection strategy instead. The most striking internal comparison is fine-tuned general agent vs. tabula rasa PPO: on the L holdout set, fine-tuning the pre-trained agent substantially reduces sample complexity and enables solving environments (MuJoCo-Hopper-Hard, MuJoCo-Walker-Hard, Car-Ramp) where tabula rasa training makes no progress even at 1B timesteps, though one environment (Thruster-Large-Obstacles) shows slower fine-tuning convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Box2D/ImpulseEngine lineage of impulse-based 2D rigid-body physics, reimplementing it in JAX as Jax2D with the critical design choice of dynamic scene specification (unlike Brax's static specification) to enable vmap parallelisation across heterogeneous morphologies. Conceptually, it draws an explicit analogy to LLM pretraining on mixed-quality data, applying this to online RL via domain randomisation over a vast space of procedurally generated physics tasks. The most closely related prior work is XLand (Team et al., 2021; 2023), which also trains on procedurally generated 3D physics levels and evaluates on hand-designed holdout tasks, but Kinetix differentiates through 2D expressivity (arbitrary joint/thruster configurations vs. homogeneous agents), a unified green-blue-red reward, and open-source availability. The transformer-based permutation-invariant architecture extends prior work on set-based representations in RL (StarCraft II, graph attention masks) to a fully dynamic physics scene.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Kinetix provides the first open, fast, and sufficiently diverse 2D physics benchmark to study large-scale online pretraining for general RL agents, filling a gap left by closed-source XLand and gridworld-constrained UED experiments. The demonstration that a general agent pretrained on mixed-quality random levels can zero-shot solve unseen hand-designed environments and, more strikingly, overcome deceptive problems (Car-Ramp) that tabula rasa PPO fails at even with 1B timesteps, validates the LLM-pretraining analogy for RL and opens a concrete research direction. Key limitations include the 2D restriction, the need for billions of timesteps (impractical for real-world transfer without world models or meta-learning), and the fact that the 'pretraining beats target training' effect only manifests on 2 of 74 levels. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 3D, whether more sophisticated UED methods (beyond SFL) can further improve the training distribution, and whether the general agent's capabilities transfer to real robotic systems.
