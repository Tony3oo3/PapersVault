## Problem

Model-based RL world models depend on sequence models to simulate environment dynamics, but RNN-based models (e.g., DreamerV3's GRU) suffer from vanishing gradients and poor long-range dependency capture, while Transformer-based models (e.g., STORM, IRIS) incur O(n²) memory and compute that limits sequence length. No prior work has applied Mamba/SSM architectures to model-based RL, and the suboptimality problem—where an inaccurate early world model leads the policy to converge to poor behaviour—remains inadequately addressed.

## Input / Output

The agent observes 64×64×3 RGB game frames and selects from a discrete action set; the world model encodes each frame into a discrete latent vector via a VQ-VAE, concatenates it with the action, and feeds the result into Mamba-2 blocks. Outputs include the next discrete action (from an actor-critic policy trained on imagined rollouts), predicted scalar reward, predicted termination flag, and a reconstructed frame decoded from the latent embedding. The imagination process starts from the last real transition in a sampled sequence and autoregressively generates h additional steps, with Mamba's recurrent state automatically resetting at episode boundaries without manual intervention.

## Method

Drama replaces the RNN or Transformer sequence model in a DreamerV3-style world model with Mamba-2, which reformulates the SSM recurrence as multiplication by a semiseparable lower-triangular matrix decomposable into q×q blocks, achieving O(n) complexity while capturing long-term dependencies. The world model comprises a discrete VAE (5-layer encoder/decoder with SiLU activations), two Mamba-2 layers (512-dim hidden state, 16-dim latent state, 4 heads of 128 dims each), and single-layer MLP heads for reward and termination prediction, totalling only 7M trainable parameters. A key design choice eliminates sequential dependence in training: the target latent z_{t+1} depends solely on the next observation O_{t+1} rather than on the predicted state, enabling fully parallel GPU computation. The paper also introduces Dynamic Frequency-based Sampling (DFS), which maintains per-transition counters for world-model and policy-training usage and biases imagination sampling toward transitions the world model has learned well (v_i ≥ b_i), thereby mitigating early-training suboptimality without additional hyperparameters.

## Results

On the Atari100k benchmark (26 games, 100k environment steps), Drama XS (7M-parameter world model) achieves a normalised mean score of 105% and a normalised median of 27%, matching IRIS (105% mean) and outperforming TWM (96% mean), SPR (62%), SimPLe (33%), and PPO (11%). In a like-for-like parameter comparison, Drama XS (7M) dramatically outperforms DreamerV3 XS (12M) with a normalised mean of 105% vs. 37% and a median of 27% vs. 7%. Drama excels in games requiring long-sequence memory (Krull: 9693, MsPacman: 2270, Asterix: 1632) and opponent-based games (Boxing: 78, Pong: 15), but struggles in sparse-reward games (PrivateEye: 90, Jamesbond: 372) and visually demanding games (Breakout: 7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Drama XS (7M params) ties IRIS on normalised mean (105% vs. 105%) but trails slightly on median (27% vs. 29%), while substantially outperforming TWM on mean (105% vs. 96%) despite losing on median (27% vs. 51%). Against the larger STORM (127% mean, 58% median) and DreamerV3 (125% mean, 49% median), Drama lags on both metrics, but DreamerV3 uses 200M parameters—roughly 28× more than Drama's 7M model. In the critical like-for-like comparison (Table 3), Drama XS (7M) achieves 105% mean vs. DreamerV3 XS (12M) at only 37% mean, a 2.8× improvement, and 27% vs. 7% median. Drama outperforms all model-free and early model-based baselines (PPO 11%, SimPLe 33%, SPR 62%) by wide margins. In the Mamba vs. Mamba-2 ablation, Mamba-2 (used in Drama) wins in 3 of 4 tested games (Krull, Boxing, Freeway) over Mamba, confirming the SSD-based architecture's advantage. In the grid-world long-sequence task, Mamba-2 achieves the lowest training time (214 ms at l=1664) with competitive error (14.2%), while the Transformer runs out of memory entirely at that sequence length.

## Relation to Prior Work

Drama extends the Dreamer lineage of latent world models (VAE + sequence model + linear heads) by replacing the GRU backbone (DreamerV3) and the transformer backbone (STORM, IRIS, TWM) with the Mamba-2 selective state space model, achieving O(n) complexity in sequence length. It is implemented on the STORM infrastructure and adopts DreamerV3's robustness techniques for the actor-critic policy. The novel Dynamic Frequency-based Sampling (DFS) method builds on the frequency-based sampling idea in TWM but introduces a dual-vector mechanism that explicitly balances world-model training frequency against behaviour-policy sampling frequency to mitigate early-training suboptimality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a 7M-parameter world model achieves a normalised mean of 105% on Atari100k, competitive with 200M-parameter DreamerV3 (125%) and STORM (127%), while being trainable on a standard laptop — a meaningful accessibility win for the MBRL community. However, the paper itself acknowledges that Drama does not decisively outperform other SOTA world models, and the normalised median (27%) lags behind STORM (58%) and DreamerV3 (49%), suggesting the efficiency gains come with some performance trade-off on harder games. Open questions include whether the O(n) scaling advantage translates to genuinely superior performance in tasks requiring very long-horizon planning, and whether Mamba-based world models can address persistent MBRL challenges like informed exploration and credit assignment over long sequences.
